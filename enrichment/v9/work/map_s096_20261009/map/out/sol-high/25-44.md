# 25:44: 21 questions

## 25:44/q01 What does the disjunctive am do here?  [أَمْ · grammar]
- 25:44/q01/p1 It means 'rather, do you suppose?' and shifts to a stronger question or reproach. — Al-Razi calls the shift harsher because stubbornness prevents listening and thought; al-Baydawi and al-Nasafi say it intensifies the preceding rebuke; Abu Hayyan parses it as bal plus interrogative hamza; Ibn Ashur says it coordinates an implied question after 25:42; al-Alusi says it turns from the preceding objection despite the Prophet's earnest preaching. Al-Tabatabai calls it disjunctive. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]

## 25:44/q02 How is tahsab read and what does it mean?  [تَحْسَبُ · readings]
- 25:44/q02/p1 Hamzah and al-Kisai read taḥsibu; the other readers read taḥsabu, with no difference of meaning. — Qiraat-ER attributes the vowel difference to Najdi and Hijazi usage. Al-Tabatabai glosses ḥisāb here as supposition. [2 notes: QIRAAT-ER; TABATABAI]

## 25:44/q03 Whom does the question address and what expectation does it challenge?  [تَحْسَبُ · interpretation]
- 25:44/q03/p1 It addresses Muhammad and challenges his hope that continued guidance will make these people understand and believe. — Al-Baydawi asks whether signs and arguments could benefit them; al-Biqai denies hearing or reasoning that might lead them to return voluntarily; al-Alusi mentions the Prophet's earnest preaching and care for their faith; Islahi says he was exerting himself to reform them. Tabatabai says the question's answer is that most neither hear truth nor reason it out. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 25:44/q04 Who are 'most of them' and how do the pronouns work?  [أَكْثَرَهُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun follows the previous relative expression grammatically or names the Meccan disbelievers directly; al-Alusi appeals to earlier singular عليه.
- 25:44/q04/p1 The majority is taken from the preceding relative expression, with plural agreement by meaning. — Al-Alusi contrasts the earlier singular عليه, which follows grammatical form, with the plural here, which follows meaning; their common desire allowed an earlier singular. Ibn Ashur says the plural pronouns follow the majority's meaning and cites Abu al-Tayyib's verse to illustrate a person surpassing members of his own kind. Al-Tabatabai likewise derives the plural from the prior relative clause. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 25:44/q04/p2 They are the Meccan polytheists or disbelievers being addressed. — Yahya ibn Sallam identifies the polytheists; al-Wahidi and Ibn al-Jawzi specify Meccans; al-Qurtubi reports the Meccan identification. Al-Alusi rejects taking 'most' as unbelievers independently of the preceding relative expression because singular عليه rules it out. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 25:44/q05 Why does the verse say 'most' rather than 'all'?  [أَكْثَرَهُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes give two compatible reasons for the qualification: eventual believers and resistant people who already recognized the truth.
- 25:44/q05/p1 Some of the group did or would believe. — Al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi say some were known to believe; al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, al-Alusi, and Bursevi also mention believers or those destined for felicity. [7 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 25:44/q05/p2 Some understood or recognized the truth but resisted it from arrogance, leadership, or status. — Al-Razi and al-Nasafi stress love of leadership; al-Baydawi and Bursevi also mention fear of its loss; al-Alusi adds arrogance. Ibn Ashur contrasts ordinary followers with intelligent people who read cosmic signs but would not join poor and enslaved believers such as Ammar and Bilal. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 25:44/q06 What is the hearing denied here?  [يَسْمَعُونَ · interpretation]
- 25:44/q06/p1 It is attentive, receptive hearing of the recited message that seeks understanding and benefit, not mere detection of sound. — Al-Tabari says they fail to grasp recitation and God's proofs; Ibn Abbas says they neither hear nor see guidance. Al-Nahhas, al-Qurtubi, Jishumi, al-Baghawi, al-Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi, and Bursevi describe receptive listening; al-Razi says their faculty exists but is unused. Ibn Ashur compares doubters to those never hearing, citing 27:80. Al-Tabatabai distinguishes understanding sound from merely hearing it. Al-Biqai, al-Baydawi, and al-Alusi emphasize no benefit from warnings. [27 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 25:44/q07 What is the reasoning denied here?  [يَعْقِلُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'reason' is directed especially to visible and cosmic signs or to the admonitions within recitation; al-Alusi prefers the former contextual pairing.
- 25:44/q07/p1 It is reflection on proofs, signs, or miracles and distinguishing guidance from error. — Al-Wahidi defines it as distinguishing right from error; Jishumi, al-Baghawi, al-Tabrisi, and Ibn al-Jawzi name visible signs and miracles; al-Razi says intellect exists but is unused. Al-Alusi prefers signs in the world and selves, and Ibn Ashur specifies nonverbal created signs, citing 10:101. Elmalili contrasts rational evidence with transmitted evidence. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 25:44/q07/p2 It is understanding the Prophet's recited words and their admonitions. — Al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi say to reflect on the Prophet's words; Bursevi says to grasp admonitions and calls to virtue; al-Alusi reports the reading that both hearing and reasoning concern recited verses and their moral counsel. [4 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 25:44/q08 How do hearing and reasoning offer paths to guidance?  [يَسْمَعُونَ يَعْقِلُونَ · interpretation]
- 25:44/q08/p1 One may reason independently to truth or, lacking that ability, listen to a knowledgeable adviser and follow truth. — Al-Tabatabai cites 67:10. Al-Alusi puts hearing first because it suits the context of recitation. Elmalili describes failure to heed transmitted evidence and to act on rational evidence; Jishumi says listening without seeking benefit is no true hearing. [4 notes: TABATABAI; ELMALILI; JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 25:44/q09 In what respect are they like cattle?  [كَٱلْأَنْعَٰمِ · interpretation]
turns on: The comparison is explained by uncomprehending reception, appetite, or failure to use human faculties; the notes often combine these.
- 25:44/q09/p1 They receive sounds or signs but do not understand or benefit from them. — Ibn Abbas says cattle hear a command or admonition as mere sound; al-Zajjaj stresses failure to discern signs and proofs; Ibn Ashur says sound senses can receive a call without attentive understanding. Al-Nasafi adds that abandoning inference lets Satan master them. Al-Tabatabai says animals cannot grasp meanings beyond sound; al-Wahidi, al-Baydawi, and al-Alusi stress unheeded evidence. [17 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI]
- 25:44/q09/p2 They are absorbed in food, drink, appetite, and enjoyment, neglecting the Hereafter. — Muqatil and al-Wahidi explicitly mention neglect of the afterlife; al-Kalbi says nothing beyond food and drink is understood; Jishumi adds enjoyment; the Najmi Interpretations add ego desire; Study Quran says cattle are not condemned for these concerns. [8 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,مقاتل); JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); STUDYQURAN]
- 25:44/q09/p3 They leave their faculties unused, following sensation or desire rather than reason. — Ibn Atiyya says they neglect benefit and consequences despite possessing understanding; al-Biqai says misguidance veils reason; Elmalili says they follow sensations; Islahi calls their capacity squandered; Kuranyolu ties the condition to deifying base desire. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 25:44/q10 Why are they further astray than cattle?  [بَلْ هُمْ أَضَلُّ سَبِيلًا · interpretation]
turns on: The notes offer several cumulative grounds: practical guidance, unused capacity, knowledge of God, accountability, chosen falsehood, wider harm, and the rule of appetite.
- 25:44/q10/p1 Cattle find food and water, obey caretakers, recognize kindness and harm, and seek benefit; these people fail to obey God or pursue reward and avoid punishment. — Al-Tabari contrasts pasture and masters with rejection and ingratitude toward the Creator; al-Wahidi reports obedience to owners who feed them; al-Razi and al-Baydawi add mistaking Satan's harm for good; al-Nasafi and Abu Hayyan add failure to find truth; al-Alusi adds shelter. Al-Tabrisi says livestock instinctively avoid harm while people choose ruin despite knowing salvation. Ibn Kathir says animals fulfill their purpose, whereas humans were made to worship God alone despite messengers and proofs. [25 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(قول غير مسمى); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 25:44/q10/p2 They possess a capacity for knowledge and higher excellence but leave it unused; animals lack that capacity or naturally follow their created instincts. — Jishumi and al-Tabrisi contrast possible human knowledge with animals' incapacity; Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan say humans abandon understanding; al-Razi and al-Baydawi say attainable perfection makes refusal blameworthy; al-Alusi contrasts proper animal use of innate faculties with disabled reason. Islahi says animals follow God-given instinct without deviation, while desire-driven humans exceed natural limits. [13 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 25:44/q10/p3 Animals recognize, glorify, or prostrate to God, while these unbelievers deny or fail to recognize Him. — Muqatil specifies recognition and remembrance of their Lord versus Meccan failure to affirm oneness; al-Zajjaj cites 2:74 for hard hearts; al-Razi cites 17:44, 22:18, and 24:41 for animal glorification; al-Alusi reports innate animal knowledge and glorification. [10 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI]
- 25:44/q10/p4 People face commands, promises, reckoning, and punishment, whereas cattle do not. — Ibn Abbas and al-Kalbi say livestock receive no reward, punishment, or burden of proof; Jishumi adds commands, promises, and threats; al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, and Bursevi stress deserved punishment for human neglect. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الكلبي); JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN]
- 25:44/q10/p5 Animals lack false belief and wrongdoing, while these people positively choose unbelief and hold harmful falsehood. — Jishumi says animals do not profess unbelief; al-Qurtubi says they cannot reason monotheism and prophethood but do not believe these false. Al-Razi says humans compound ignorance with confidence in their knowledge; al-Baydawi and al-Alusi say humans affirm falsehood and derive evil actions or rules from false premises; Bursevi says animals acquire neither good nor evil. [8 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 25:44/q10/p6 Animal ignorance harms no one, but these people's ignorance spreads corruption and obstructs truth. — Al-Razi says they obstruct God's path and seek to make it crooked; al-Baydawi and Bursevi mention strife; al-Alusi adds social disorder. Elmalili lists oppression, deception, seduction, and betrayal of even homeland. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 25:44/q10/p7 In a nature combining reason and appetite, allowing appetite to rule makes humans lower than beasts. — Al-Nasafi reports the scheme of angels as spirit and reason, beasts as soul and desire, humans as both; Bursevi says reason's rule raises humans toward angels and cites 98:7 for mastery of desire surpassing angels. The Mathnawi depicts the upward and downward pull. Islahi describes excess beyond animal instinct. [5 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(المثنوي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 25:44/q11 Does the cattle comparison imply that animals lack knowledge of God?  [كَٱلْأَنْعَٰمِ · theology]
turns on: Whether likeness in failed understanding entails lack of all God-knowledge, or only lack of inferential response; al-Tabatabai disputes the stronger inference and the unsupported outward-only answer.
- 25:44/q11/p1 Some infer that animals do not know their Lord. — Al-Alusi and al-Tabatabai both report this inference from the comparison. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الصوفية وجماعة من الناس); TABATABAI(بعضهم); TABATABAI-]
- 25:44/q11/p2 Animals have innate knowledge of God and glorify Him; the comparison concerns noninferential or outward perception. — Al-Alusi attributes knowledge and glorification to Sufis and others, and reports the distinction between innate animal knowledge and inference from signs. Al-Tabatabai rejects the denial of basic God-knowledge in either livestock or disbelievers: desire veils humans' following of innate rational truth. He says an outward-appearance-only explanation cannot be established by argument. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الصوفية وجماعة من الناس,قيل); TABATABAI]
- 25:44/q11/p3 The comparison may speak only according to outward appearance. — Al-Tabatabai reports this as another answer but says argument does not establish it. [1 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم)]

## 25:44/q12 What does 'further astray in path' mean?  [أَضَلُّ سَبِيلًا · meaning]
- 25:44/q12/p1 They have missed or gone farther from the right path of truth. — Muqatil ibn Hayyan says they missed the right path; al-Durr transmits the same from Muqatil; Asad glosses it as farther astray from truth and links 7:179. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); DURR-FULL(مقاتل); ASAD-NOTES]

## 25:44/q13 How does the verse connect with the preceding discussion of desire and the Prophet's mission?  [* · links]
turns on: The cited connections emphasize either the immediate desire-driven exchange or the broader messenger narratives.
- 25:44/q13/p1 It deepens the rebuke of desire-driven rejecters and reassures the Prophet that conveying truth does not force their acceptance. — Al-Tabari describes idol worship governed by preference for a better stone and says Muhammad is no guardian over such acts. Ibn Ashur connects the disjunctive question with 25:42 and prior despair over desire-dominated minds. Islahi says verses 35-44 cite earlier messengers and rejecters to encourage patience; intellect guides but desire enslaves, so the Prophet should convey rather than compel. Kuranyolu says deifying base desire prevents understanding the call. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 25:44/q13/p2 The earlier Moses, Noah, and intervening peoples' stories warn that mockery of messengers ended in ruin; the next surah details them. — Islahi says the narratives both reassure the Prophet and warn his nation. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 25:44/q14 Which other verses are cited as parallels or illustrations?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources cite different verses for parallels and for evidence supporting their explanations.
- 25:44/q14/p1 7:179 and other animal comparisons parallel this verse. — Al-Wahidi cites 7:179 as a parallel; Asad also links it. Study Quran lists 2:65, 7:176, 29:41, 31:19, and 62:5 as further comparisons of people to animals. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 25:44/q14/p2 Related cited verses illustrate deafness, signs, faith's light, animal praise, or the moral hierarchy. — Ibn Ashur cites 27:80 for one who hears without understanding and 10:101 for created signs; al-Biqai cites 10:9 for faith's illumination; al-Zajjaj cites 2:74 for hard hearts; al-Razi cites 17:44, 22:18, 24:41 for creatures' praise; Bursevi cites 98:7 for the best of creation. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ZAJJAJ; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 25:44/q15 What inward accounts explain who hears or understands the call?  [يَسْمَعُونَ يَعْقِلُونَ · inward]
turns on: The notes present distinct inward explanations: primordial response, faith's illumination, and recollection of blessings.
- 25:44/q15/p1 Response to a human caller flows from hearing and answering God's pre-eternal call. — Ibn Ata, as reported by al-Sulami and Bursevi, says receptive response is a blessing of the primordial answer, while heedlessness signals distance from its source. [2 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); BURSEVI(ابن عطاء)]
- 25:44/q15/p2 Faith illuminates spiritual insight, letting reason perceive meanings that misguidance veils. — Al-Biqai compares faith's light to sunlight needed by bodily sight, calls spiritual insight the soul's eye, and cites 10:9. He says God's inward illumination shows why people can be deprived of benefit from given faculties. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 25:44/q15/p3 Recalling God's many blessings establishes signs of divine unity. — Jishumi gives this as the way signs of oneness are made clear. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 25:44/q16 What report about the disbeliever's heart is attached to this verse?  [* · reports]
- 25:44/q16/p1 A report of four kinds of hearts identifies the covered heart as the disbeliever's. — Ibn Abi Hatim refers back to a previously cited report from Salman al-Farisi. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سلمان الفارسي)]

## 25:44/q17 Is this verse said to be abrogated?  [* · sciences]
- 25:44/q17/p1 Some commentators say it was abrogated by the sword verse. — Bursevi reports the view without naming those commentators here. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعض المفسرين)]

## 25:44/q18 What revelation classification is reported for the surah?  [* · sciences]
- 25:44/q18/p1 A minority calls most of the surah Madinan but verses 68–70 Makkan. — Study Quran reports this minority view; the note gives no verse-specific argument. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(A minority)]

## 25:44/q19 What wider moral lesson is drawn from this verse?  [* · interpretation]
- 25:44/q19/p1 Across history, prioritizing bodily desire, material gain, or status over reason and justice makes people unable to heed truth. — Kuranyolu identifies the Meccan polytheists as the immediate target and generalizes the lesson; Islahi says desire enslaves while intellect guides; al-Razi notes some knew truth but preferred leadership. [4 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; RAZI-FULL]

## 25:44/q20 What unattached imala examples appear among the notes?  [* · readings]
- 25:44/q20/p1 Sibawayh describes imala near kasra or ya, sometimes across a transparent enclitic ha, with strength and distance affecting it. — He says a closer or firmer kasra strengthens imala; ya can trigger it in بيني وبينها, فينا, علينا, and رأيت يدا; عندها treats ha as absent and imala spreads leftward. A damma blocks it in raised يكيلها; يعلمها, يخفها, دما, and دمها lack a trigger; strong intervening consonants may keep عبدا and عنبا open while عمادا and معزانا take analogical imala. He also describes variation in يدا versus يدها, رمى versus حبلى and معزى, and pause versus connected speech. He says dialects vary without defective mixing; some speakers avoid the category, rare alifs have no regular trigger, and Banu Tamim with some Qays and Asad use رأيت عدا and هو منا. These examples are not expressly applied to a word of this verse. [18 notes: SIBAWAYH(بنو تميم)]

## 25:44/q21 What unattached ellipsis and curse examples appear among the notes?  [* · grammar]
- 25:44/q21/p1 Sibawayh explains accusative curses and fa-ha la-fik as expressions with an omitted verb, alongside a nominative curse construction. — تربا وجندلا means a divine affliction of dust and stones, replacing تربت يداك and جندلت; some Arabs raise ترب and جندل as subject with predicate, while retaining the curse. فاها لفيك means calamity for you in place of دهاك الله; Abu Sidrah al-Hujaymi's trusted transmitted poetry and a line of Amir ibn al-Ahwas illustrate it. The notes make no explicit connection to this verse. [5 notes: SIBAWAYH(أبو سدرة الهجمي)]
