# 78:21: 19 questions

## 78:21/q01 What does the name Jahannam designate, and what is its origin?  [جَهَنَّمَ · meaning]
- 78:21/q01/p1 It names the abode of afterlife punishment; its name may be a declined borrowing from Hebrew or another Semitic language. — Ibn Ashur offers the borrowing as a possibility and cites 2:206. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 78:21/q02 How is the hamza of إن read, and what does the variant imply?  [إِنَّ · readings]
turns on: Whether the hamza begins an independent emphatic clause or a causal clause; Alusi disputes the causal explanation.
- 78:21/q02/p1 With kasra, as the majority read it. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan explicitly contrast the majority's kasra with the reported fatḥa. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو معمر المنقري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عمر، المنقري، ابن يعمر)]
- 78:21/q02/p2 With fatḥa, making the clause causal for the Hour and its recompense. — Al-Kashshaf attributes it to Ibn Yaʿmur; Ibn Atiyya to Abu Maʿmar; Abu Hayyan to Abu Umar, al-Minqari and Ibn Yaʿmur; Baydawi and Alusi explain its causal force. Alusi objects that the pious clause would then need coordinating fatḥa and that separate rewards do not explain establishing recompense itself. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن يعمر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو معمر المنقري); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عمر، المنقري، ابن يعمر); ALUSI-FULL(أبو عمر); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 78:21/q03 What does كانت suggest about Hell's role?  [كَانَتْ · interpretation]
- 78:21/q03/p1 Its role as a watch post was already decreed. — Ibn Ashur compares the preordination of Hell's punishment with the appointment of the Day of Decision in 78:17 and sees a hint of God's eternal preparation. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 78:21/q04 What does مِرْصَادًا mean?  [مِرْصَادًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the expression denotes a place of surveillance, a route, a prison, preparedness, an active watcher, or the reported 'became' gloss.
- 78:21/q04/p1 A watch post or ambush place where one waits and watches. — Al-Azhari compares an enemy lookout to a horse-training place (Wahidi, Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi); al-Mubarrad calls it an observation post (Wahidi, Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi). Jishumi says it is prepared to observe an expected event. Al-Raghib distinguishes the specially appointed mirsad from marsad; al-Mufradat traces rasd to readiness to watch. Kashshaf calls it a boundary. Ibn Atiyya cites 89:14; Elmalili likens it to a hunter's lookout. Al-Asmaʿi and al-Kisaʾi distinguish waiting from preparing (Qurtubi). [34 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق، الحسن,الأزهري,المبرد); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الأزهري,المبرد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأزهري,المبرد); QURTUBI-FULL(الأصمعي,القشيري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأزهري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Ebû Hayyân); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(المفردات)]
- 78:21/q04/p2 A road or passage across Hell. — Al-Sihah attests marsad as road (Qurtubi). Al-Hasan and Qatada describe crossing it before Paradise (Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Alusi); Tabatabai links the watch post to 19:71. Bursawi allows this extended application but prefers the sense concerning the wretched. [17 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن بن أبي الحسن,قتادة); TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(الصحاح,قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); ELMALILI(Mukātil); TABATABAI]
- 78:21/q04/p3 A prison in which the enemies are held. — Muqatil is cited by Thalabi, Tabrisi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Elmalili; some formulations pair the enemies' prison with the friends' passage. [5 notes: THALABI; TABRISI(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل); ELMALILI(Mukātil)]
- 78:21/q04/p4 Something prepared for its occupants. — Baghawi derives this from arsadtu, 'I prepared it'; al-Mubarrad's explanation includes its being prepared, and Qurtubi concludes it is prepared and awaiting arrivals. Ibn Kathir likewise calls Hell prepared. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 78:21/q04/p5 A watcher or sentry. — Abu Sinan says it watches and requites deeds (Mawardi, Qurtubi); Ibn Atiyya reports an active-noun construal. Biqai and Ibn Ashur personify Hell as a relentless watcher; Alusi allows the intensive watcher. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبو سنان); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المتأولين); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سنان); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 78:21/q04/p6 It means 'became'. — Al-Durr reports this unusual gloss from Abu al-Jawzaʾ without further explanation. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو الجوزاء)]

## 78:21/q05 What grammatical form can مِرْصَادًا have?  [مِرْصَادًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the form denotes a place or instrument, an intensive watcher, possession of watchers, or surveillance itself.
- 78:21/q05/p1 A noun of place, with an intensive pattern that can also name an instrument. — Qurtubi calls it mifaʿal from rasada and says the pattern conveys frequent waiting; Abu Hayyan calls it an intensive place noun. Alusi and Elmalili allow a place or instrument, and Ibn Ashur notes the pattern commonly forms both. Nahhas says it needs no feminine ending because it is not directly a verbal form. [7 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 78:21/q05/p2 An intensive adjective or watcher ascribed figuratively to Hell. — Wahidi compares miʿṭar and miʿmar; Qurtubi invokes repeated waiting. Biqai compares a frequent stabber; Alusi permits watching unbelievers, believers or both and calls attribution to Hell figurative. Ibn Ashur compares a relentless male guard, explaining the missing feminine ending. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 78:21/q05/p3 A relational adjective: Hell possesses a watch or guards. — Qurtubi reports the parse as possessing watchers; Alusi attributes it to al-Bahr; Elmalili reports Abu Hayyan's view that relational predicates imply abundance and permanence. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(البحر); ELMALILI(Ebû Hayyân)]
- 78:21/q05/p4 A verbal noun meaning surveillance. — Ibn Ashur says predicating surveillance itself of Hell intensifies the image, as though it originated the watching and let none destined for it escape. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 78:21/q06 Who watches for whom at Hell?  [جَهَنَّمَ مِرْصَادًۭا · referent]
turns on: The proposed watcher and the people being watched: Hell or its keepers for the condemned, Paradise's keepers for believers, or angels for both.
- 78:21/q06/p1 Hell itself watches or ambushes the condemned. — Zajjaj includes disbelievers and all decreed punishment; Nahhas says disobedient people; Wahidi transmits Abu Ishaq and al-Hasan. Baydawi and Alusi emphasize that none escapes, with Alusi treating the attribution figuratively. Study Quran places the ambush at death. [16 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق، الحسن); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 78:21/q06/p2 Hell's keepers watch for unbelievers or God's enemies and seize them. — Al-Mubarrad supplies keepers (Wahidi, Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi); al-Qushayri compares waiting for enemies and says angels watch until the descent to Hell (Qurtubi). Biqai describes capture on arrival; Ibn Ashur likens it to guards at an outpost. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI(المبرد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المبرد); QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Ebû Hayyân); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 78:21/q06/p3 Angels or keepers of Paradise watch for believers and protect them as they cross. — Kashshaf and Nasafi place their reception at Hell; Baydawi and Alusi specify shielding them from its heat, and Biqai describes rescue by Paradise's wardens. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 78:21/q06/p4 Angels watch both groups, protecting one and punishing the other. — Alusi reports this alternative and says the intensive sense can apply to watching both groups. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 78:21/q07 Must people cross Hell before reaching Paradise?  [جَهَنَّمَ مِرْصَادًۭا · interpretation]
- 78:21/q07/p1 Yes; the way to Paradise passes over Hell. — Al-Hasan and Qatada repeatedly state this (Tabari, Nahhas, Mawardi, Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, Durr, Alusi). Ibn Atiyya calls the bridge hadith sound; Durr's Qatada cites 19:71. Nasafi says believers pass and unbelievers enter. Tabatabai also invokes 19:71. Qashani cites 19:71–72 in an inward reading. [33 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); NAHHAS(الحسن); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقسم عن ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن بن أبي الحسن,قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن,قيل); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); QASHANI(الصادق عليه السلام); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأزهري,مقاتل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن,سفيان,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); ELMALILI(Mukātil); TABATABAI]

## 78:21/q08 What determines a person's fate at the crossing?  [جَهَنَّمَ مِرْصَادًۭا · reports]
- 78:21/q08/p1 Those with a pass or means of rescue cross; others are held or fall into Hell. — Al-Hasan's pass image appears in Tabari, Mawardi, Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir. Abu Hayyan's version identifies good deeds; Ibn Atiyya says those with means of rescue survive and cites a sound bridge hadith. Nasafi distinguishes believers who pass from disbelievers who enter. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن بن أبي الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأزهري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن وقتادة)]

## 78:21/q09 How many bridges span Hell?  [جَهَنَّمَ مِرْصَادًۭا · reports]
- 78:21/q09/p1 Three bridges. — Sufyan's report appears in Tabari and Qurtubi; Ibn Kathir names Sufyan al-Thawri; Durr says everyone must cross them before Paradise. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(سفيان); QURTUBI-FULL(سفيان); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سفيان الثوري); DURR-FULL(سفيان)]

## 78:21/q10 What checkpoints are reported on the crossing?  [جَهَنَّمَ مِرْصَادًۭا · reports]
- 78:21/q10/p1 Seven: confession, prayer, alms, fasting, Hajj, umrah and wrongs. — Baghawi transmits Muqsim from Ibn Abbas; Biqai also recounts them. Both say voluntary worship fills shortfalls in deeds before entry to Paradise; Biqai specifies outstanding wrongs. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(مقسم عن ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL]

## 78:21/q11 What is reported about the Ahl al-Bayt crossing Hell?  [جَهَنَّمَ · reports]
- 78:21/q11/p1 They had already crossed it while it was quiescent. — Qashani reports al-Sadiq's answer to a question about whether the Ahl al-Bayt also enter Hell, in connection with 19:71. [1 notes: QASHANI(الصادق عليه السلام)]

## 78:21/q12 Whose appointed destination is Hell?  [جَهَنَّمَ · interpretation]
- 78:21/q12/p1 It is the destination of unbelievers, rebellious people and those decreed punishment. — Muqatil calls the transgressors unbelievers and says this was revealed concerning al-Walid ibn al-Mughira. Tabari includes deniers of Hell and resurrection; Zajjaj says those sentenced cannot pass beyond it. Kashshaf and Nasafi call it tyrants' limit. Tabrisi says sinners cannot escape; Bursawi prefers application to the wretched because such a terrifying surveillance place serves punishment. Study Quran calls them rebels against truth. [15 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 78:21/q13 When does Hell's ambush confront the rebellious?  [جَهَنَّمَ مِرْصَادًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the image is applied at death or at the resurrection scene.
- 78:21/q13/p1 At the Day of Decision, immediately after the world's upheaval. — Islahi says Hell appears after the destruction and raising of people, leaving rebels no time to prepare; Ibn Ashur locates the threat in the resurrection scene; Kuran Yolu says the preceding signs establish resurrection. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 78:21/q13/p2 At death. — Study Quran says the rebels are ambushed then because they had been oblivious to truth in worldly life. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 78:21/q14 What warning or moral point does the watch-post image convey?  [جَهَنَّمَ مِرْصَادًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: The notes present both an inescapable threat and recompense according to deeds.
- 78:21/q14/p1 Punishment is inescapable for those singled out by the threat. — Mawardi transmits Qatada that it is God's threat to disbelievers; Zajjaj and Wahidi say the condemned cannot pass beyond Hell; Baydawi likens its vigilance to a fierce attacker. Kuran Yolu pictures a waiting warrior and cites 67:8. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 78:21/q14/p2 People are repaid for their own deeds. — Abu Sinan says the watcher requites deeds (Mawardi, Qurtubi); Islahi says every deed is registered and punishment is the natural outcome of what the rebellious did. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبو سنان); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سنان); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 78:21/q15 How does the sentence attach to the preceding description of the Day?  [إِنَّ جَهَنَّمَ كَانَتْ مِرْصَادًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause continues the syntax of 78:17 or independently answers what follows the Day's terrors.
- 78:21/q15/p1 It is a second predicate of the initial إن in 78:17, with an implied 'on that day'. — Ibn Ashur relates a fixed appointment to a watch post as temporal marks for punishing rebels; the internal إن doubles the emphasis, as in a line by Jarir and 22:17, and completes what was said of people arriving in companies in 78:18. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 78:21/q15/p2 It is an independent answer to the audience's implied question after the Day's horrors. — Ibn Ashur says this reading explains what follows those horrors and does not make al-taghin an unexpected turn in wording. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 78:21/q16 How is مِرْصَادًا related to لِلطَّاغِينَ مَآبًا in the next verse?  [مِرْصَادًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Where the phrase 'for the rebellious' attaches, and whether the complement of mirsad or maʾab is inferred.
- 78:21/q16/p1 The next verse supplies the omitted complement: a watch post for the rebellious. — Ibn Ashur prefers this because the short verse cola permit a pause after mirsad. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 78:21/q16/p2 لِلطَّاغِينَ attaches to مِرْصَادًا, or to both it and مَآبًا. — Ibn Ashur reports both alternatives and says neither creates a semantic obstacle. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 78:21/q17 Why does the passage present Hell first?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes give different rhetorical effects of the sequence: priority of harm, warning the deniers, and clarifying later rewards.
- 78:21/q17/p1 The fearful outcome is presented before the benefit because averting harm has priority. — Biqai states this ordering principle. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 78:21/q17/p2 The audience's denial of resurrection calls for a threat first. — Ibn Ashur cites the occasion and the order of later statements; Kuran Yolu says the prior signs of resurrection lead into the deniers' afterlife. Islahi says the abrupt appearance after upheaval leaves rebels no time to prepare. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 78:21/q17/p3 Opening with Hell helps identify the later مَفَازًا as Paradise. — Ibn Ashur says the exact order of Hell, mafazan, the overflowing cup and the no-speech clause makes several connections possible; he notes Quranic sentence placement can carry cause, correction, answer or allusion beyond the narrower aims of poetry and oratory. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 78:21/q18 What inward meaning is given to Hell and its crossing?  [جَهَنَّمَ · inward]
- 78:21/q18/p1 Hell signifies nature, a boundary watched by all; the blessed pass over it and the God-fearing are rescued. — Qashani cites 19:71–72 for the passage and rescue; he also reports al-Sadiq's account of the Ahl al-Bayt crossing it while quiescent. [2 notes: QASHANI(الصادق عليه السلام)]

## 78:21/q19 What transition does this verse make in the account of judgment?  [* · links]
- 78:21/q19/p1 It begins the detailed account of judgment after the preceding description of the Day's terror. — Alusi identifies this change in the passage's focus. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
