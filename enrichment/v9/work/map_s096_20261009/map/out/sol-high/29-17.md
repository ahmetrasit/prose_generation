# 29:17: 31 questions

## 29:17/q01 What are the awthān worshipped here?  [أَوْثَٰنًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the terms wathan and ṣanam are synonyms or distinguished by material, bodily form, or the breadth of objects worshipped.
- 29:17/q01/p1 They are idols, images or statues worshipped besides God. — Ibn Qutayba calls wathan a stone or plaster idol; al-Ṭabarī calls them likenesses, Qatāda statues, and Bursevī manufactured images. Al-Jawharī equates wathan and ṣanam; Biqāʿī reports lexicographers who agree. Qatāda calls them an idol or stone. Abū Ḥātim notes Christian images of Christ and Mary as objects of prostration. [17 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(الجوهري); NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو حاتم أحمد بن حمدان الرازي,بعضهم); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; TABATABAI]
- 29:17/q01/p2 A wathan is specifically a carving of wood or stone, unlike a metal ṣanam. — Abū ʿUbayda contrasts wooden or stone awthān with gold, silver or copper aṣnām; Qurtubī transmits a variant assigning plaster or stone to wathan. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة)]
- 29:17/q01/p3 A wathan is a bodily object set up for worship, unlike a bodiless ṣanam image. — Biqāʿī reports Abū Manṣūr through al-Qazzāz. Ibn ʿArafa instead includes any plaster, stone or similar image; Abū Ḥātim calls a wall image wathan but a stone statue ṣanam, showing the distinction varies. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو حاتم أحمد بن حمدان الرازي,أبو منصور,ابن عرفة)]
- 29:17/q01/p4 A wathan is a nonhuman stone image, unlike a human shaped ṣanam of wood or metal. — Bursevī reports this distinction from some authorities. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
- 29:17/q01/p5 Awthān covers any worshipped object, more broadly than aṣnām. — Elmalılı includes stone images, fetishes, standing stones and crosses. Islahi includes shrines among the baseless objects worshipped. [2 notes: ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 29:17/q02 What does the root of awthān evoke in this rebuke?  [أَوْثَٰنًۭا · meaning]
- 29:17/q02/p1 Its associated senses of increase, dispersal and weakness expose idols as unfit for divinity. — Biqāʿī traces permutations of وثن through abundance, scattered reports, weakness and fixed immobility, and takes the plural as scattered objects dividing attention. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 29:17/q03 How does إنما govern the clause ending in أوثانا?  [إِنَّمَا أَوْثَٰنًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā merely blocks inna or is its relative noun; the actual accusative awthānan bears on that choice.
- 29:17/q03/p1 Mā blocks inna, making innamā one restrictive particle; awthānan is the object of taʿbudūna. — Naḥḥās says inna cannot govern a verb and that mā still blocks it before a noun. ʿAlī ibn ʿĪsā objects that a relative mā would require nominative awthān. Al-Farrāʾ treats innamā as one particle. Jishumī transmits the restrictive analysis. [5 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); NAHHAS+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 29:17/q03/p2 Mā can be relative, yielding nominative awthān as inna's predicate in a hypothetical construction. — Some grammarians allow the relative analysis before a noun. Naḥḥās and Qurtubī allow it outside the Qurʾānic reading, with taʿbudūna the relative clause and an omitted return pronoun; Jishumī also reports the analysis. ʿAlī ibn ʿĪsā rejects applying it to the actual accusative reading. [4 notes: NAHHAS(بعض النحويين); JISHUMI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; JISHUMI(علي بن عيسى)-]

## 29:17/q04 What force does calling their objects only awthān have?  [إِنَّمَا تَعْبُدُونَ أَوْثَٰنًۭا · rhetoric]
- 29:17/q04/p1 It strips their objects of divine standing and confines their worship to mere manufactured idols. — Biqāʿī says restriction reverses the claim to divinity, while plurality signals division and weakness; Ālūsī stresses that statues have no quality beyond manufacture. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the indefinite plural belittles empty claims and sets true worship with God. Biqāʿī contrasts God, without equal or minister, with the idols. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 29:17/q05 What does khalq or تخلقون mean in Arabic usage?  [وَتَخْلُقُونَ · meaning]
turns on: The root ranges from measuring and shaping to fabrication and divine origination; context determines its force here.
- 29:17/q05/p1 Here it can mean inventing or fabricating a lie, especially in speech. — Ibn Qutayba compares 26:137 and 38:7 and links ikhtilāq to invention. Abū ʿUbayda groups khalaqa, ikhtalaqa, kharaqa, ikhtaraqa and iftarā. Ibn Jawzī cites exegetes and 26:137. Al-Rāghib, reported by Bursevī and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ, distinguishes human fabrication from God's origination; al-Qāḍī Abū Bakr invokes God's sole creation at 6:102 and 39:62 to read this as lying. [8 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); BURSEVI(الراغب); BURHAN(القاضي أبو بكر); ITQAN(القاضي أبو بكر); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب)]
- 29:17/q05/p2 It can mean forming, shaping or fashioning an object. — Ibn Qutayba cites the clay bird in 5:110; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ traces the sense to measuring and shaping. Al-Rāghib permits carving by hand. The Itqān glosses the verse's verb as making or fashioning. [4 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; BURSEVI(الراغب); ITQAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 29:17/q05/p3 Its underlying sense is measuring or determining. — Ibn Qutayba illustrates the leather cutter's measured pattern with Zuhayr's verse; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ also begins from measuring or shaping. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 29:17/q05/p4 It can mean originating creation, a sense assigned to God. — Ibn Qutayba cites 7:189; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says divine usage is origination without precedent; al-Rāghib reserves this sense to God. [3 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب)]
- 29:17/q05/p5 Khalq can mean religion in other verses. — Ibn Qutayba reads God's khalq in 30:30 as religion, and reports that changing it in 4:119 can mean changing religion or bodily alterations such as castration and slitting ears. [3 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 29:17/q06 How is وتخلقون read?  [وَتَخْلُقُونَ · readings]
turns on: Vocalization, gemination and an added or elided tāʾ distinguish the transmitted verb forms.
- 29:17/q06/p1 The common reading is the simple form تخلقون, meaning make or fabricate. — Al-Ṭabarī accepts the regional readers' light khāʾ and ḍamma on lām because the authoritative readers agree; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it the majority reading. Al-Samīn describes it as from خلق. Al-Zajjāj and Qurtubī record a form spelled like the verse. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبد الرحمن); TAB-FULL+]
- 29:17/q06/p2 A doubled-lām form from تخلّق or تتخلّق is read, conveying emphatic invention or lying. — Al-Sulamī, Zayd, ʿAlī, Qatāda, ʿAwn, Ibn Abī Laylā, ʿUbāda and Ibn al-Zubayr appear in varying transmitter lists. Ibn Mujāhid says one tāʾ drops from تتخلقون; Ibn Jinnī compares تكذبون. Some allow the basic فعل sense. The reports describe the initial tāʾ as open. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(السلمي وزيد بن علي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الرحمن السلمي وعون العقيلي وقتادة وابن أبي ليلى); QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(علي بن أبي طالب وزيد بن علي والسلمي وقتادة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مجاهد,علي والسلمي وعون العقيلي وعبادة وابن أبي ليلى وزيد بن علي)]
- 29:17/q06/p3 An intensive doubled-lām form from خلق is read, suggesting repeated or abundant making or lying. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and Ālūsī give the intensive implication; al-Nasafī names Abū Ḥudhayfa and al-Sulamī, and Ālūsī reports Zayd ibn ʿAlī, with lying and slander as its sense. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(أبو حذيفة والسلمى); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن علي)]
- 29:17/q06/p4 The verb is read تختلقون with an added tāʾ. — Ibn al-Sumayfiʿ and Abū al-Mutawakkil are named by Ibn al-Jawzī. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السميفع وأبو المتوكل)]
- 29:17/q06/p5 A doubled-lām reading also has ḍamma on the initial tāʾ. — Al-Samīn reports this separately from Zayd ibn ʿAlī. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(زيد بن علي)]

## 29:17/q07 What does إفكا mean here?  [إِفْكًا · meaning]
- 29:17/q07/p1 It means a lie, falsehood or perversion of truth. — Jishumī calls it a lie; Bursevī calls it the worst kind of lie. Biqāʿī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī trace it to being turned from proper truth, for a statement or deed. Mujāhid gives the same basic sense; Asad renders the phrase literally as creating a lie. [8 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 29:17/q08 What are they doing when they 'create falsehood'?  [وَتَخْلُقُونَ إِفْكًا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the falsehood is primarily their spoken divine claims, the manufactured idols themselves, or a sarcastic characterization; some notes combine carving with the claim.
- 29:17/q08/p1 They invent lies by calling idols gods, partners or intercessors. — Al-Suddī specifies spoken claims, while reports from Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid say uttering or making lies; Mujāhid also extends lies beyond idols. Muqātil joins hand manufacture to the claim of divinity, citing 37:96. Al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī, Bursevī and others mention partners or intercession. Ibn Kathīr reports invented divine names; Elmalılı compares a portrait to a living person and calls worship of an imagined image slander. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the true God is one. Islahi says their claim that God made these objects His partners has no divine statement or proof. [32 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي,مجاهد); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس، السدي,ابن عباس، قتادة,ابن عباس، مجاهد، الحسن، عكرمة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,السدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(العوفي عن ابن عباس، مجاهد، السدي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(IJ); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TAB-FULL+]
- 29:17/q08/p2 They carve or manufacture idols, which are themselves called falsehood. — Al-Ḥasan, Qatāda, Ibn Zayd and Ibn ʿAbbās are variously reported; Wahidī also gives Mujāhid and al-Kalbī. The things are handmade yet falsely called gods; Ibn ʿAbbās explains the name falsehood by this false claim. Yaḥyā cites 37:95 and Muqātil 37:96. Biqāʿī notes that the present tense evokes continual remaking and the inversion of created artifact into creator. Al-Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī describe making them for falsehood, with Ālūsī's lām of outcome rather than intention. Al-Ṭabarī prefers speech fabrication over merely carving; Ibn Kathīr's report says Ibn Jarīr chooses carving, an attribution that differs from the extant Tabari note. [34 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,قتادة); ZAJJAJ(قيل); IBNABIHATIM(أبوه,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,الحسن,الكلبي,قتادة,مجاهد,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,قتادة,مجاهد); ITQAN; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأفاضل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس من رواية الوالبي، مجاهد، عكرمة، الحسن، قتادة)+; TAB-FULL-]
- 29:17/q08/p3 The phrase is a sarcastic description of their supposed creating. — Ālūsī reports this as a further possibility without specifying its target beyond the phrase. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 29:17/q09 How is إفكا analyzed grammatically and read?  [إِفْكًا · readings]
turns on: Whether the form is a verbal noun, an adjective, or a shortened participle, and whether the initial hamza is read with fatḥa.
- 29:17/q09/p1 The written ifkan is a verbal noun meaning lying or a false act. — Al-Kashshāf compares kadhib and laʿib. Ibn Jinnī allows a verbal noun and al-Nasafī treats ifk as a shortened form of afk; al-Samīn says the verbal noun analysis may require an omitted noun. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)]
- 29:17/q09/p2 The written ifkan is an adjective describing a false creation. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Samīn report this analysis; al-Samīn says an omitted noun is unnecessary for the adjective, opposing the proposed ellipsis. Ibn Jinnī allows an adjective replacing an omitted noun. [6 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(فضيل بن مرزوق وابن الزبير); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR+]
- 29:17/q09/p3 Afkan with open hamza is read as a verbal noun or adjective. — Fuḍayl and Ibn al-Zubayr are named; Ibn Jinnī also permits a shortened active participle of āfik. Qurtubī and Nasafī give its relation to lighter ifk. Ālūsī specifies fatḥa on hamza and kasra on fā. ʿĀṣim is reported to read its letters lightly in a connected recitation. [7 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(فضيل بن مرزوق وابن الزبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن الزبير وفضيل); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن الزبير وفضيل بن زرقان); ALUSI-FULL(ابن الزبير وفضيل بن زرقان); DURR-FULL(عاصم بن أبي النجود)]

## 29:17/q10 What governs the accusative إفكا?  [إِفْكًا · grammar]
turns on: Whether its accusative is simply the verb's object or part of the broader coordinated restrictive construction.
- 29:17/q10/p1 The verb تخلقون itself makes إفكا accusative as its object. — Naḥḥās and Qurtubī state direct verbal government. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 29:17/q10/p2 إفكا is coordinated back to إنما, as though the opening restriction were repeated. — Al-Ṭabarī describes the sense as 'you only do this'; al-Farrāʾ, reported by Ibn al-Jawzī, uses a cited idiom for the coordination. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء)]

## 29:17/q11 What does the idols' lack of ملك رزقا show?  [لَا يَمْلِكُونَ لَكُمْ رِزْقًۭا · interpretation]
- 29:17/q11/p1 The idols have no power to give any provision or benefit, so their worship is futile. — Rāzī distinguishes merit, origin of existence, sustenance and fear as reasons for worship; the idols fail to create or sustain. Bayḍāwī calls provision a second argument. Biqāʿī points to human need and God's sole good; Ṭabāṭabāʾī reasons that the creator of people and provision owns it. Bursevī glosses malaka through 5:25; Elmalılı says idols cannot even aid digestion. Study Quran cites 16:73, 39:3, 5:76 and 25:3 against treating them as intermediaries. Islahi says God alone controls sustenance. [24 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 29:17/q12 What does رزقا denote in the clause?  [رِزْقًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether rizq names the act of giving or what is given, with different accusative analyses.
- 29:17/q12/p1 It is the act of providing, an accusative verbal noun. — Bayḍāwī and Abū Ḥayyān allow an infinitive sense; al-Samīn makes it a cognate accusative governed by لا يملكون. Kufan analysis supplies 'they do not have power to provide you provision' with the infinitive clause as object. Ālūsī allows an absolute object by meaning or an omitted verb, but calls the latter strained. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أصول الكوفيين); ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 29:17/q12/p2 It is the provision itself, the thing provided or one's share. — Bayḍāwī and Abū Ḥayyān allow this sense; al-Samīn parses it as direct object. A Burhān note says an earlier verse's rizq can mean one's allotted share, avoiding ellipsis; the note cites 29:17. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL]

## 29:17/q13 Why is رزقا indefinite while الرزق is definite?  [رِزْقًۭا ٱلرِّزْقَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the article expresses totality, known promised provision, or deliberate repetition beyond the usual definite noun rule.
- 29:17/q13/p1 Negated indefinite رزقا excludes even the least provision; definite الرزق encompasses all provision from God. — Al-Kashshāf, cited also by Rāzī, states the contrast; Bayḍāwī says the indefinite is general. An eminent scholar cited by Ālūsī takes it as even a little being beyond idols. Ālūsī says the repeated definite need not simply identify the earlier indefinite. Burhān calls the verse a counterexample to an unrestricted rule equating a later definite with an earlier indefinite. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الزمخشري); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة)]
- 29:17/q13/p2 God's provision is known by His promise, whereas any supposed provision from idols is unknown. — Rāzī cites God's promise in 11:6 to explain the definite and indefinite contrast. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 29:17/q13/p3 The definite repetition is deliberate and need not obey a simple identity rule. — Burhān compares the preceding repeated 'Lord' and 21:22; another reported analysis says a derived predicate such as 'god' means worshipped, so repeating such predicates with different objects does not repeat their substance. [3 notes: BURHAN(غيره)]

## 29:17/q14 Why is provision singled out in the appeal?  [رِزْقًۭا ٱلرِّزْقَ · rhetoric]
- 29:17/q14/p1 Provision is a universal, essential benefit that stands for all good and makes the idols' impotence clear. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says every creature knows this need and one part can stand for the whole kind. Rāzī uses sustenance as the benefit that preserves life. Ālūsī's cited scholar notes its importance in creation; Biqāʿī connects it to perpetual human need. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة)]

## 29:17/q15 Where and how should provision be sought?  [فَٱبْتَغُوا۟ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرِّزْقَ · interpretation]
turns on: How reliance on God, obedience, worship and ordinary earning relate to the command to seek His provision.
- 29:17/q15/p1 Seek all provision from God alone, the only one able to provide it. — Al-Ṭabarī says this obtains what is sought; Ibn Kathīr finds emphatic restriction in seeking at God's presence, comparing 1:5 and 66:11. Biqāʿī says all provision is with God in an interconnected created order; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says ownership follows creation. Islahi explicitly directs people to ask God for provision. [20 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 29:17/q15/p2 Seek provision through trust in God instead of earning. — Sahl presents earning as the way of ordinary people; Tustarī and Sulamī transmit the same guidance. [3 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل); BURSEVI(سهل)]
- 29:17/q15/p3 Seek provision through obedience and turning toward worship. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ is reported by Sulamī and Bursevī; Bursevī adds that worship brings requests to fulfillment. [3 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); BURSEVI(ابن عطاء)]
- 29:17/q15/p4 Seek it with effort, whether in worship and reliance or ordinary means. — Biqāʿī reads exertion in ابتغاء, says God usually grants provision through effort and rebukes idle wishfulness; he warns that outward earning should not distract from worship and that sin may cost provision. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 29:17/q16 What does the command to worship God require and accomplish?  [وَٱعْبُدُوهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the commands emphasize present needs or preparation for the final meeting, and what motive prompts worship.
- 29:17/q16/p1 Worship God alone, sincerely recognizing His worth and provision. — Muqātil specifies affirming oneness; al-Ṭabarī adds humility and recognition of blessing. Rāzī says worship responds to God's intrinsic worth and benefit; Biqāʿī says sincerity excludes association and that God rewards or punishes. Ibn Kathīr says it brings good and repels harm in both lives. Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts God with idols. [14 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 29:17/q16/p2 Worship and gratitude prepare people to meet God after death. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī give this as an alternative to treating the commands as means to obtain worldly provision; Nasafī also connects them to the return. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 29:17/q16/p3 Worship may arise from habit, hope for mercy, desire, or love. — Yaḥyā ibn Muʿādh enumerates the four motives. [1 notes: SULAMI(يحيى بن معاذ)]

## 29:17/q17 Why and how should they thank God?  [وَٱشْكُرُوا۟ لَهُۥٓ · interpretation]
- 29:17/q17/p1 Thank God alone for provision and all His blessings, through devotion and deeds. — Muqātil links gratitude to the living return after death; al-Ṭabarī to provision and blessing. Qatāda calls it a divine honor and Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb counts every deed for God as gratitude. Rāzī includes creation and ongoing sustenance; Bursevī and Ālūsī say gratitude preserves blessings and draws more bounty; Ibn Kathīr calls God sole giver. Islahi says associating partners with God is ingratitude to the giver of everything. [18 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة,محمد بن كعب القرظي); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 29:17/q18 What is the force of شكروا له rather than شكروا إياه?  [وَٱشْكُرُوا۟ لَهُۥٓ · grammar]
- 29:17/q18/p1 Both constructions mean to thank someone; the version with له is more eloquent. — Al-Ṭabarī compares shakartuhu and shakartu lahu and prefers the eloquence of the latter. Ibn ʿAṭiyya likewise says the two Arabic expressions are equivalent. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 29:17/q19 How is ترجعون read?  [تُرْجَعُونَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the verb makes the people agents of returning or recipients of being returned.
- 29:17/q19/p1 The verse has the passive 'you are returned' form. — The active form is expressly presented as an alternative by al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Ālūsī and the Qirāʾāt note. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(يعقوب); ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]
- 29:17/q19/p2 An active reading has fatḥa on tāʾ and kasra on jīm: 'you return.' — Al-Nasafī attributes it to Yaʿqūb; al-Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī mention it. The Qirāʾāt note says it gives the addressees agency. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(يعقوب); ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]

## 29:17/q20 What does returning to God mean here?  [إِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ · interpretation]
- 29:17/q20/p1 After death they return to God at resurrection for judgment and recompense. — Yaḥyā specifies Judgment Day; al-Ṭabarī says they are questioned for worshipping others despite living on His bounty and cites 2:28. Biqāʿī says bodily return and effortless resurrection, with reward or punishment in both abodes. Ibn Kathīr and Study Quran cite judgment and parallel return verses. Ālūsī includes death then resurrection; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this shifts motive from provision to afterlife happiness. Islahi adds that the idols will be useless to people when they are raised and returned to God. [14 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 29:17/q21 How does the return clause relate to Abraham's preceding speech?  [إِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ · links]
- 29:17/q21/p1 It concludes the call to worship, seek provision and give thanks, urging preparation for judgment. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it Abraham's conclusion; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the return shifts motive from worldly provision to afterlife happiness, where faith and worship matter though natural causes govern worldly provision. Ālūsī allows it to conclude all the speech or connect to its opening, making the intervening case against idols parenthetical. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 29:17/q22 How does this verse advance Abraham's argument and connect to its context?  [* · links]
- 29:17/q22/p1 Abraham moves from the call to worship one God to exposing idols' false status and inability to provide, then to return and accountability. — Bāqillānī calls the interruption and return a rhetorical figure, noting disagreement whether it is a distinct badīʿ type and citing poems and Qurʾānic passages including 29:16, 18 and 24. Rāzī tests inherent worth, benefit and fear; Ibn Kathīr calls Abraham the model ḥanīf. Kur'an Yolu says all prophets share the monotheist principle and Abraham calls his people to reflect and heed earlier nations. Study Quran notes his father and people invoke ancestral practice, citing 6:74, 19:42, 21:52-53 and 26:74. [9 notes: BAQILLANI; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 29:17/q23 Why does the speech use direct 'you worship' and 'you fabricate'?  [تَعْبُدُونَ وَتَخْلُقُونَ · rhetoric]
- 29:17/q23/p1 The second-person verbs turn narration into Abraham's direct rebuke of his people. — Burhān calls it a shift from third-person narration to address, citing 29:16-17; Bāqillānī describes the speech moving from a call to worship and fear to direct exposure before narrating the reply. [2 notes: BURHAN; BAQILLANI]

## 29:17/q24 What grammatical role can the command sequence have?  [فَٱبْتَغُوا۟ وَٱعْبُدُوهُ وَٱشْكُرُوا۟ · grammar]
- 29:17/q24/p1 A present-tense verb functioning like a noun can take noun-like inflection in the sequence. — Burhān cites the commands to seek provision, worship and thank as its example. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 29:17/q25 How should reports of an occasion of revelation for a verse be assessed?  [* · reports]
turns on: Reliability of transmitted occasions and whether a specific triggering event limits general Qurʾānic wording.
- 29:17/q25/p1 Claims require transmitted eyewitness evidence, since invented occasions and ignorant assertions mislead. — Al-Wāḥidī condemns invented reports and says his book preserves heard and studied transmitted occasions, starting with revelation's beginnings and then treating verses with a transmitted cause. Ibn ʿĀshūr quotes his warning and agrees that weak unmarked reports create false impressions. [5 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB; IBNASHUR-FULL(الواحدي)]
- 29:17/q25/p2 Occasions can clarify a verse, but should be scrutinized; general wording normally governs beyond its occasion. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says verses may indicate their own occasion, transmission can establish others, and ignoring the topic risks error; he excuses early compilers' breadth but criticizes later acceptance of weak reports. He says revelation guides society without awaiting events. Most scholars privilege general wording; he rejects a minority restriction to the occasion and warns against reports that distort general or unrestricted language. [9 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 29:17/q25/p3 An occasion confines a verse's general wording to that specific event. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this as a deviant minority claim and rejects its tendency to restrict general wording. [0 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 29:17/q26 How is apparent conflict between human 'creating' and God's sole creation resolved?  [وَتَخْلُقُونَ · theology]
- 29:17/q26/p1 God alone originates; human خلق here is fabrication of a lie, while Jesus's خلق is shaping clay. — Al-Qāḍī Abū Bakr, reported in Burhān and Itqān, cites God's sole creative power (6:102; 39:62) and the clay bird of 5:110. Ibn Qutayba and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ likewise distinguish divine origination from human shaping and false speech; al-Rāghib's reported distinction reflects objections to calling the Qurʾān created. [7 notes: BURHAN(القاضي أبو بكر); ITQAN(القاضي أبو بكر); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب)]

## 29:17/q27 What principles address alleged contradictions or abrogation when this verse is cited?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the apparent opposition can be reconciled, and if not how chronology or consensus establishes abrogation.
- 29:17/q27/p1 Revelation and sound reason do not genuinely conflict; apparent differences should be reconciled by meaning and context. — Al-Qāḍī Abū Bakr gives خلق here versus God's creation and Jesus's shaping as an example. Al-Ṣayrafī says genuine contradiction would require opposite meanings in every respect, which Qurʾān and Sunna do not have; abrogation concerns different times. [3 notes: BURHAN(القاضي أبو بكر); ITQAN(الصيرفي,القاضي أبو بكر)]
- 29:17/q27/p2 If two verses cannot be reconciled, chronology or consensus identifies the later abrogating rule. — Al-Isfarāyīnī says known chronology settles it, otherwise consensus on one practice identifies the abrogating verse, and says no unresolved case falls outside these conditions. [1 notes: ITQAN(أبو إسحاق الإسفراييني)]

## 29:17/q28 What neighboring textual or recitational details are cited with this verse?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes cite distinct neighboring reading details rather than competing analyses of one word.
- 29:17/q28/p1 A neighboring verb يبدا is read with softened rather than fully realized hamza. — Ibn Jinnī reports al-Zuhrī and argues true replacement would lead to yāʾ then alif, comparing Abū Zayd's poetic ملا for ملأ. [1 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(الزهري)]
- 29:17/q28/p2 A connected recitation reads أوثانا مودةً with accusative tanwīn and بينكم accusative. — Al-Durr transmits this from ʿĀṣim together with his light reading of afkan. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(عاصم بن أبي النجود)]

## 29:17/q29 What practical ruling is associated with avoiding an idol?  [أَوْثَٰنًۭا · law]
- 29:17/q29/p1 Do not treat a prayer staff as an idol; pray standing if able, otherwise sitting, otherwise lying. — Ibn ʿUmar gives this response when asked about a sick person's praying on a staff. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عمر)]

## 29:17/q30 What material are the idols referred to here made from?  [أَوْثَٰنًۭا · referent]
- 29:17/q30/p1 They are idols made of stone. — Al-Wāḥidī reports this identification from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)]

## 29:17/q31 What does the reported saying of Jesus imply about seeking provision and the two worlds?  [فَٱبْتَغُوا۟ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرِّزْقَ · inward]
- 29:17/q31/p1 People truly desire neither this world nor the next if they do not obey the Lord of either. — Tustarī reports Jesus saying obedience to the Lord of either world would bring it, yet people obey neither. [1 notes: TUSTARI(عيسى بن مريم)]
