# 4:28: 17 questions

## 4:28/q01 What does God intend to lighten for people?  [يُرِيدُ ٱللَّهُ أَن يُخَفِّفَ عَنكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the immediate marriage concession exhausts the wording or exemplifies a general principle; other readings identify repentance, temporary marriage, inward relief, or guidance as the intended relief.
- 4:28/q01/p1 He eases religious duties and burdens generally, with marriage to enslaved women among the concessions. — Mujahid includes every concession (MUJAHID, TAB-FULL, IBNABIHATIM). Ibn Abbas and others compare this community with the Children of Israel (WAHIDI-BASIT, RAZI-FULL); Razi cites 7:157, 2:185, 22:78. Baghawi cites 7:157 and the Prophet's description of an easy religion. Ibn Kathir includes commands, prohibitions and decreed circumstances. Biqai mentions lifted earlier restrictions. Ibn Ashur describes a law that weighs benefit and harm, cites 22:78, 2:185, 7:157 and reports on easy religion, easy instruction to envoys, good news, and the rebuke of prolonged prayer. Study Quran adds eased obligations (8:66), Ramadan nights (2:187), the five prayers after the Night Journey, and earlier communities' self-imposed obligations (7:157, 3:93). Elmalili relates the prayer of 2:286 and 7:157, 2:185, 22:78. Tabatabai cites 2:286 and 22:78. Asad calls guidance a reconciliation of bodily and spiritual impulses. [42 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الباقون); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ELMALILI(Peygamber); IBNASHUR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 4:28/q01/p2 He permits men who cannot afford free wives to marry enslaved women when needed, relieving sexual pressure and avoiding fornication. — Muqatil and Tabari explicitly cite inability to afford a free wife and fear of fornication. Ibn Zayd says without necessity the original rule remains (TAB-FULL). Ibn Atiyya treats this as the immediate context; Alusi supports it from context. Tabatabai objects that slave marriage already existed before Islam, though disliked; he says the passage instead removes aversion to enslaved women's equal humanity and companionship. [27 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد ومقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(مجاهد وطاوس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد وابن زيد وطاوس); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(طاوس ومجاهد); TABATABAI(قيل); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); TABATABAI-]
- 4:28/q01/p3 He lightens people's burden by accepting repentance and enabling them to repent. — Tabrisi and Alusi report this as another interpretation of the phrase. [2 notes: TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 4:28/q01/p4 He legislates temporary marriage to relieve sexual pressure. — Tabatabai says it avoids ordinary marriage burdens such as dowry and maintenance, addresses the believing community specifically, and follows the easing of desire through marriage and ownership of female slaves. He argues slave marriage predated Islam and was already available, although disliked. [4 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 4:28/q01/p5 He lightens sins through repeated litanies and their lights entering the heart. — Qushayri reports this inward explanation. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 4:28/q01/p6 He lightens spiritual struggle through the lights of witnessing entering the heart. — Qushayri reports this inward explanation. Alusi says God eases the burdens of servanthood at the station of witnessing. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 4:28/q01/p7 He lightens service through the sweetness of obedience. — Qushayri reports this inward explanation. Alusi alternatively says God eases the self's burden by opening delight in worship after initial patience. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 4:28/q01/p8 He lightens the burden of the trust by carrying it for people. — Qushayri reports this inward explanation; Sulami reports a gloss of lightening the immense trust people assumed in ignorance. [2 notes: SULAMI(قيل); QUSHAYRI]
- 4:28/q01/p9 He prescribes servanthood while knowing people's weakness and ignorance. — Sulami reports this gloss. [1 notes: SULAMI(قيل)]
- 4:28/q01/p10 He gives guidance that reconciles bodily and spiritual impulses so both elements of human nature can flourish. — Asad describes this as the way God lightens the human burden. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]

## 4:28/q02 What does takhfīf mean here?  [يُخَفِّفَ · meaning]
- 4:28/q02/p1 It means making a burden easier or gentler, including reducing or removing a legal duty. — Damghani gives ease or gentleness; Jishumi compares easing to reducing weight; Ibn al-Jawzi specifies easing or removing part of a duty. [3 notes: DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 4:28/q03 What omitted object is understood after 'lighten'?  [يُخَفِّفَ عَنكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Which unspoken thing the transitive verb 'lighten' governs.
- 4:28/q03/p1 The omitted object is a burden or duty, including concessions such as slave marriage and removal of older restrictions. — Abu Hayyan lists concessions and prior burdens among proposed supplements; Samīn supplies a burden of reasoning and dispelling confusion. Burhan illustrates the clause as 'lighten [the burden] for you.' [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN]
- 4:28/q03/p2 The omitted object is clarified rules. — Abu Hayyan lists this among the proposed senses. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 4:28/q03/p3 The omitted object is reward for obligations. — Abu Hayyan lists this among the proposed senses. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 4:28/q03/p4 The omitted object is sin, including what people commit ignorantly. — Abu Hayyan mentions pardon of ignorant sins; Samīn reports the sin of what people commit. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 4:28/q04 What syntactic role does 'an yukhaffifa' have after 'God wants'?  [يُرِيدُ ٱللَّهُ أَن يُخَفِّفَ · grammar]
- 4:28/q04/p1 The an-clause is the accusative object or complement of yurīdu. — Qurtubi calls it an accusative complement; Burhan says an with its clause occupies the object position. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN]

## 4:28/q05 How does 'God wants to lighten for you' connect syntactically with the preceding clause?  [يُرِيدُ ٱللَّهُ أَن يُخَفِّفَ عَنكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether a circumstantial clause can attach across the intervening coordinated sentence with an overt divine name rather than a pronoun.
- 4:28/q05/p1 It is an independent sentence with no syntactic role in the preceding sentence. — Abu Hayyan parallels the promise that God wants ease. He and Samīn object that a coordinated sentence intervenes before the proposed circumstantial governor and an overt noun replaces the pronoun expected in such a clause. Alusi likewise calls it independent. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 4:28/q05/p2 It is circumstantial under the earlier 'God wants to turn to you.' — Samīn reports this alternative; Abu Hayyan and Samīn challenge the separation by an unrelated coordinated sentence and the overt subject in place of a pronoun. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 4:28/q06 What is meant by 'the human' in the statement of weakness?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the marriage context limits the generic word 'human' or supplies one example of it.
- 4:28/q06/p1 Humanity generally is meant, although weakness concerning women is one included instance. — Ibn Atiyya extends the immediate marriage case to general human susceptibility; Ibn Ashur calls the phrase a generalization, includes Tawus's specific example, and says it does not exhaust the verse. Kuran Yolu describes vulnerabilities alongside will and power as part of humanity's test. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(طاووس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 4:28/q06/p2 The relevant human weakness is especially that of men in relation to women and marriage. — Tawus and other commentators focus on the immediate marriage ruling; Ibn Ashur accepts this as an instance but says it is not exhaustive. [10 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(طاوس); TAB-FULL(طاوس); THALABI(طاوس والكلبي وأكثر المفسرين); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(طاوس,مجاهد وابن زيد وطاوس); QURTUBI-FULL(طاوس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(طاووس,مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL(طاووس)]

## 4:28/q07 What weakness was the human created with?  [وَخُلِقَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ ضَعِيفًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether weakness is chiefly sexual impatience, wider moral vulnerability, material origin or bodily frailty, dependence, impaired judgment, or an inward capacity.
- 4:28/q07/p1 People have little patience to resist sexual desire, especially for women. — Muqatil and Tabari connect this with the slave-marriage concession preventing fornication; Salama singles out intercourse itself as the weakest moment, and Waki says reason departs around women. Tawus says no sphere makes a person weaker. Saʿid ibn al-Musayyib fears temptation even at eighty with failing sight, and ʿUbada fears Satan if alone with an unlawful woman despite wealth and frailty (Thalabi, Qurtubi); Alusi reports a saying that even a noble man is overcome by women. Tabatabai describes implanted sexual powers continually pressing toward their objects; Kuran Yolu treats sexual desire as a vulnerability directed into lawful satisfaction. [37 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(سلمة,طاوس); TAB-FULL(طاوس); IBNABIHATIM(طاوس,وكيع); THALABI(سعيد بن المسيب,طاوس والكلبي وأكثر المفسرين,عبادة بن الصامت); KASHSHAF-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(طاووس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(طاوس,مجاهد وابن زيد وطاوس); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(طاووس، مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن المسيب,ابن عباس,طاوس,عبادة بن الصامت); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن المسيب,طاووس,مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن طاوس); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(طاوس); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 4:28/q07/p2 People are broadly vulnerable to desire, appetite, passion, anger, fear and grief, and struggle with demanding obedience. — Zajjaj, Ibn Kaysan, Nahhas and Qurtubi mention desire and appetite, with Ibn Kaysan adding fear and grief; Kashshaf and Nasafi add hardship of worship. Al-Qadi (in Razi) stresses strong motives toward pleasure rather than weak bodily capacity; Razi accepts that account while disputing its Muʿtazilite implication. Alusi reports these variants; Bursevi adds unreliable control of appetites and powers. Kuran Yolu adds love of wealth, warning of its corruption while encouraging lawful earning. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI(ابن كيسان); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن كيسان); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج، ابن كيسان); RAZI-FULL(القاضي); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 4:28/q07/p3 Human weakness lies in being created from lowly fluid or another frail beginning. — Al-Hasan cites 30:54; Ibn al-Jawzi also identifies daʿf in 30:54 as semen. Raghib relates weakness to humanity's beginning and end. Alusi rejects the weak-substance explanation as poorly fitted to the marriage context. [9 notes: THALABI(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن,الراغب); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 4:28/q07/p4 People are weak in judgment and reason unless God grants certainty and light. — Sulami says strength comes through certainty, not the self; Alusi reports that divine light alone discloses hidden wisdom, but calls this poorly supported by the marriage context. [2 notes: SULAMI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)-]
- 4:28/q07/p5 Weakness means poverty, need and dependence on God and one another. — Qushayri treats need as an excuse. Biqai says people cannot abstain from marriage and other appetites or act without divine support. Raghib relates weakness to comparison with the celestial realm, the human apart from God's aid, or mutual dependence; he balances this with human intellect, vicegerency, nearness to God, and God's favor in 17:70. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الراغب); BIQAI-FULL]
- 4:28/q07/p6 It is physical frailty, shown by pain from even a small event. — Alusi reports this from al-Hasan but rejects it as ill suited to the marriage context. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)-]
- 4:28/q07/p7 People cannot endure a moment's separation from God; this weakness reflects divine and human mutual love. — Bursevi praises this spiritual weakness as a mark peculiar to humans. Alusi says humans cannot endure a moment's veil from the beloved or separation from the desired. He holds that patience with God is blameworthy because of perfect longing, though patience otherwise is praiseworthy. He reports al-Shibli's prayer that there is no rest with God and no escape from Him, seeking refuge through God in God. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الشبلي); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 4:28/q07/p8 Weakness is human mutability between animal appetite and angelic obedience, with potential for either misery or perfection. — Bursevi says this capacity belongs uniquely to humans, permits perfection through adopting divine attributes, and makes the perfected human best of creation or the beastly human worst. He cites a divine hadith promising the obedient a living kingship without death. [4 notes: BURSEVI(الله تعالى)]
- 4:28/q07/p9 Humans cannot bear unseen disclosures or the force of witnessing without divine aid. — Alusi gives this as an inward account of human weakness. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]

## 4:28/q08 What is the lexical sense and range of ḍaʿīf?  [ضَعِيفًۭا · meaning]
- 4:28/q08/p1 It denotes weakness, the opposite or absence of strength; here it can mean little patience or inability to restrain desire. — Jishumi treats weakness and lightness as absences of strength and heaviness rather than independent qualities. Ibn al-Jawzi derives ḍaʿīf from ḍaʿf and gives the noun's two vocalizations, ḍaʿf and ḍuʿf; he glosses this verse as little patience and catalogues other contexts: incapacitated (4:76, 8:66), blind (11:91), chronically infirm (9:91), oppressed (28:5), socially lowly (34:31), and semen (30:54). [12 notes: DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]

## 4:28/q09 How is ḍaʿīfan parsed in 'the human was created weak'?  [وَخُلِقَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ ضَعِيفًۭا · grammar]
turns on: The function of the final accusative and whether khalaqa can take a second object or an omitted 'from' phrase.
- 4:28/q09/p1 It is an accusative circumstance describing the human, emphatic on the usual passive reading and also possible on the active reading. — Nahhas, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Samīn and Alusi give the circumstantial parse. Abu Hayyan and Samīn reject a proposed tamyīz or specification parse, though they differ in how they discuss supplying min. [8 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 4:28/q09/p2 It is understood through an omitted preposition and qualified noun: created from a weak substance. — Abu Hayyan mentions clay or embryonic stages; Samīn gives lowly water or a drop. Alusi says this does not fit the context. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 4:28/q09/p3 On the active reading, it is a second object if khalaqa is taken like jaʿala. — Ibn Atiyya proposes this; Abu Hayyan objects that no grammarian makes khalaqa govern two objects this way, and Samīn calls it unusual because jaʿala meaning 'create' takes one object. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية)-]
- 4:28/q09/p4 It is a tamyīz or specification accusative. — Abu Hayyan and Samīn report and reject this proposal; the possibility of inserting min is discussed. [0 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 4:28/q10 How is khuliqa read?  [وَخُلِقَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ ضَعِيفًۭا · readings]
turns on: Whether the verb is passive or active, changing the grammatical subject and case of al-insān.
- 4:28/q10/p1 The majority read passive khuliqa, with al-insān as the passive subject. — Nahhas calls the verb passive; Ibn Atiyya explicitly contrasts the majority reading with the active variant. [2 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 4:28/q10/p2 Ibn Abbas, and in some transmissions Mujahid, read active khalaqa: God created the human weak. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan include Mujahid; Kashshaf, Qurtubi and Alusi name Ibn Abbas. Al-insān is accusative and God's name is the understood subject; Ibn Atiyya keeps ḍaʿīfan circumstantial or proposes a second object. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 4:28/q11 How does human weakness explain God's easing?  [يُرِيدُ ٱللَّهُ أَن يُخَفِّفَ عَنكُمْ وَخُلِقَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ ضَعِيفًۭا · interpretation]
- 4:28/q11/p1 The coordinating clause gives the reason for concessions: weak people struggle under severe obligations, especially marriage restrictions. — Nahhas and Qurtubi connect easily swayed desire with necessary concessions. Biqai makes the waw causal. Ibn Ashur calls the second clause a concluding generalization explaining the first; Islam is suited to all times and places whereas earlier religions addressed particular conditions, and 8:66 similarly cites known weakness before relief. Elmalili says legislation must avoid both indulgence of lust and oppressive severity; easier marriage and dealings avert fornication and misuse. Kuran Yolu says obligations have concessions in normal and exceptional circumstances. Tabatabai says the cause is general but impediments kept its effects from earlier peoples until repentance and mercy reached this community. [13 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 4:28/q11/p2 God eases servanthood through witnessing or delight in worship because humans cannot bear spiritual disclosure unaided or separation from God. — Alusi gives witnessing and delight after patience as ways God eases the burden (r8), and describes human inability to bear disclosure or separation (r9). [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]

## 4:28/q12 Does the verse rule out obligations beyond human capacity or support a view of who creates human acts?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the need for divine easing is compatible with God creating acts and the motives behind them.
- 4:28/q12/p1 God's stated wish to ease rules out duties beyond capacity and human acts created by God. — Al-Qadi argues that creating unbelief then forbidding it, or withholding faith while commanding it, would impose the greatest burden. Razi rejects the argument by invoking prior replies from divine knowledge and motivating causes. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); RAZI-FULL-]
- 4:28/q12/p2 The motives that move people to act ultimately come from God. — Razi accepts al-Qadi's analysis of motives but argues that their causes must either regress infinitely or originate with God; he calls the latter correct and says it defeats al-Qadi's Muʿtazilite position. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+]

## 4:28/q13 What legal guidance follows from the verse's promise of ease?  [* · law]
- 4:28/q13/p1 Religious obligations should be manageable, with concessions for hardship while lawful routes channel desire and protect benefits. — Ibn Ashur calls ease foundational and says the law can accept a lesser harm to prevent hardship or secure benefit, including slave marriage; he says two kinds of concessions branch from it. Elmalili rules out excessive severity and permissive surrender to lust, and urges easier marriage and dealings to prevent fornication or misuse. Kuran Yolu says sex and wealth should be directed lawfully, with concessions in ordinary and exceptional circumstances; human vulnerability does not erase the capacity to meet the test. Study Quran supplies Ramadan nights and reduction of prayers; Ibn Kathir transmits Moses' counsel on the Miʿraj: fifty daily prayers became five with tenfold reward after repeated requests. [10 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL(موسى الكليم عليه الصلاة والسلام)]

## 4:28/q14 How does this verse connect to its surrounding passage and other verses about ease?  [* · links]
turns on: Which part of the surrounding passage supplies the link: prior divine intentions, the marriage rule, or the subsequent financial address.
- 4:28/q14/p1 It follows God's clarification and repentance in 4:26–27 and states enduring care and ease for the community. — Ibn Ashur links it to 4:26 and sees its human-weakness statement as a concluding rationale. He compares 8:66, where God lightens a burden knowing believers' weakness. Elmalili and Tabatabai connect the prayer against heavy burdens in 2:286 with 7:157, 2:185 and 22:78. Study Quran also cites 2:185; Asad describes integrated spiritual and bodily guidance. [9 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]
- 4:28/q14/p2 It leads from marriage and inheritance to financial conduct, because marriage depends on means and wealth management protects lineage. — Biqai says human weakness undermines denying women and minors inheritance and explains the move toward rules for money. Elmalili links the next address, 4:29, to the financial ability needed for marriage and the handling of wealth. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 4:28/q14/p3 Nearby wording about human weakness signals why the conditional slave-marriage rule addresses sexual desire. — Abu Hayyan notes a series of implied links: reluctance suggests consensual inheritance transfer, restrictions on coercing wives can serve their benefit, the prohibition on fathers' wives recalls pre-Islamic custom, a permission implies an earlier ban, and slave-marriage conditions and weakness signal desire. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 4:28/q15 What is reported about the value of this verse among verses of al-Nisāʾ?  [* · reports]
turns on: The transmitted enumeration of the eight verses, whose last entries differ across sources.
- 4:28/q15/p1 Ibn Abbas counted it among eight verses better for this community than everything under the sun, usually alongside 4:26, 4:27, 4:31, 4:40, 4:48, 4:110 and 4:147. — Thalabi gives this list; Kashshaf gives the first three as clarification, repentance and relief and names 4:31 and 4:48; Durr gives only an excerpt; Study Quran names the same eight and calls them signs of mercy. Elmalili's listed references include 4:116 as well as 4:147, producing a variant enumeration. [5 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI(İbn Abbas); STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAbbās)]
- 4:28/q15/p2 A transmitted list substitutes 4:116 and 3:147 for 4:48 and 4:147. — Razi gives this enumeration and then prays to be worthy of the blessings of these verses by grace and mercy. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس,محمد الرازي)]
- 4:28/q15/p3 A transmitted list substitutes 4:152 for 4:147. — Alusi attributes this list to al-Bayhaqi from Ibn Abbas. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 4:28/q16 What rhetorical qualities does the verse exemplify?  [* · rhetoric]
- 4:28/q16/p1 Its wording exemplifies the Qur'an's gentle register, suited to its context. — Ibn Ashur contrasts this register with a forceful warning in 41:13 and also cites 39:53 for gentleness. In his wider discussion he describes taḍmīn as a dependent showing a verb's additional sense; unusually effective Qur'anic proverbial expression (39:39, 4:81, 41:34, surpassing Zuhayr); expansive description that instills fear, illustrated by Ibn Ziyaba's poetry (75:26–29, 56:83–84, 14:43); and concise simultaneous literal and figurative senses where context permits. These are general rhetorical comparisons, not claims that each device occurs in this verse. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 4:28/q17 What does the related phrase 'do not kill yourselves' mean?  [* · links]
- 4:28/q17/p1 It means do not destroy yourselves. — Abu Ubayda (Majaz) glosses the phrase lā taqtulū anfusakum this way. [1 notes: MAJAZ]
