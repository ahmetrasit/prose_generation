# 55:4: 12 questions

## 55:4/q01 Who is the human taught in “taught him”?  [عَلَّمَهُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun resumes the human species, Adam, or Muhammad in the preceding verse.
- 55:4/q01/p1 Humankind collectively, the human species. — Zajjaj, Qushayri, Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ṭabrisī, Bursevī, and Ālūsī read the preceding الإنسان generically. Qushayri and Ṭabrisī say the capacity distinguishes the species from animals; Ṭabrisī calls this the broadest and most apparent interpretation, and Ālūsī the immediately apparent one. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العالية ومرة وابن زيد والحسن والقرظي والسدي ويمان بن رباب); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو العالية وابن زيد والحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد والجمهور); BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+; TABRISI(الحسن، أبو العالية، ابن زيد، السدي)+]
- 55:4/q01/p2 Adam. — Muqātil and Tustarī speak of Adam. Wāḥidī attributes this to al-Kalbī, Qatāda, and Muqātil; Baghawī and Ibn al-Jawzī to Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatāda. The ensuing instruction is variously names, languages, comprehensive explanation, worldly and afterlife knowledge, or the Greatest Name. Ṭabāṭabāʾī considers the Adam-and-names reading remote. [19 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي وقتادة ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وقتادة,غير مسمى); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(بحر العلوم); ALUSI-FULL(جعفر الصادق,قتادة); STUDYQURAN(some); TABATABAI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 55:4/q01/p3 Muhammad, the Prophet. — Zajjaj reports a Qurʾān explanation; ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās calls the gift eloquent Qurʾānic speech; Ibn Kaysān describes knowledge and disclosure of past and future events, Judgment, or explanation of revelation. Ālūsī cites 16:44. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the specialized reading remote. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ(قيل); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن كيسان,عطاء عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن كيسان); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كيسان); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(ابن كسيان); TABATABAI-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 55:4/q02 What is the bayān God taught?  [ٱلْبَيَانَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether bayān denotes the unrestricted capacity to disclose and grasp meaning, verbal expression, a particular taught knowledge or medium, or a special lesson to Adam or Muhammad.
- 55:4/q02/p1 Articulate or eloquent speech, pronunciation, and verbal communication. — Ibn Qutayba, Ibn Zayd, Abū al-ʿĀliya, Murra, al-Ḥasan, and others give speech. Zamakhsharī, Nasafī, Qāshānī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Ālūsī stress expression of the inward and human distinction from animals; Abū Ḥayyān notes a mute person cannot receive verbally conveyed learning. Rāzī contrasts the formed body with knowledge expressed in speech. Ibn Kathīr prefers pronunciation because Qurʾānic recitation requires articulated letters; Ālūsī prefers this ordinary means of Qurʾān learning. Qāshānī speaks of expressing the Qurʾānic intellect within. Rāzī takes bayān here as articulate speech and calls it rational speech in explaining human creation. [25 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI(أبو العالية ومرة الهمذاني وابن زيد,الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد والجمهور); TABRISI(الجبائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد والجمهور); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 55:4/q02/p2 The broad human faculty of making and grasping meaning, including speech, thought, and other expression. — Ṭabarī prefers unrestricted scope: religion and worldly life, law, livelihoods, and speech. Māwardī says reason apprehends what expression discloses; Qushayrī says bayān clarifies meanings. Bāqillānī lists speech, condition, gesture, and sign, with degrees of clarity; the story of Bāqil losing a gazelle while indicating eleven illustrates failed communication. Bīqāʿī includes particulars, universals, inference, action, writing, and gesture. Rāghib, via Bursevī, defines it as disclosure broader than speech. Bursevī, Ālūsī, Elmalılı, Asad, and Kuranyolu include understanding others. Asad cites Rāghib and Tāj al-ʿArūs and recalls the names in 2:31; Study Quran includes articulation, comprehension, and clear evidence. Kuranyolu extends expression to tears, laughter, feelings, art, and action. [23 notes: BAQILLANI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العالية ومرة وابن زيد والحسن والقرظي والسدي ويمان بن رباب); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو العالية وابن زيد والحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد والجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد والجمهور); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; TAB-FULL+; TABRISI(الحسن، أبو العالية، ابن زيد، السدي)+]
- 55:4/q02/p3 Recognition of good and evil, benefit and harm, or truth and falsehood. — Qatāda says people learn what to do or leave; al-Ḍaḥḥāk and al-Rabīʿ give the moral distinction, while Qurtubī reports benefit and harm. Ibn Kathīr reports al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Qatāda, and others; Study Quran adds truth and falsehood through revelation. Qatāda's version makes such discrimination evidence against people. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك والربيع بن أنس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(الربيع بن أنس,الضحاك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(محمد بن كعب والضحاك وابن جريج ويمان); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الضحاك وقتادة وغيرهما); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك); STUDYQURAN(Q, Sh); TABATABAI-]
- 55:4/q02/p4 The names of all things taught to Adam. — Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Qurtubī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī's Bahr al-ʿUlūm, Ālūsī, and Study Quran report this. Study Quran and Ṭabāṭabāʾī relate it to 2:31; Ṭabāṭabāʾī considers it remote. [10 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي وقتادة ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وقتادة,غير مسمى); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(بحر العلوم); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some); TABATABAI-]
- 55:4/q02/p5 Knowledge of the lawful and unlawful and of religious obligations. — Qatāda says this includes worldly and afterlife matters and gives grounds for accountability; al-Ṭabarī includes law within the unrestricted general sense. Thaʿlabī adds good and evil and what to do or avoid. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān expressly treat the legal gloss as one part of broad bayān. Study Quran links moral and legal discrimination to revelation. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); STUDYQURAN(Q, Sh)]
- 55:4/q02/p6 All languages taught to Adam. — Thaʿlabī, Baghawī, and Bahr al-ʿUlūm give 700,000 languages with Arabic finest; Ibn al-Jawzī, Qurtubī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Ālūsī report languages without that number. Ibn al-Jawzī sets this beside Adam's names and explanation of all things. [9 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وقتادة,غير مسمى); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(بحر العلوم); ALUSI-FULL]
- 55:4/q02/p7 Writing or script with the pen. — Yamān is cited by Thaʿlabī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Qurtubī, and Ālūsī; Mawardī reports the view. Thaʿlabī cites 96:4, and Qurtubī 96:4–5. Ālūsī dismisses it as an alternative gloss. [6 notes: THALABI(يمان); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(يمان); QURTUBI-FULL(يمان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(محمد بن كعب والضحاك وابن جريج ويمان); ALUSI-FULL(يمان)]
- 55:4/q02/p8 The Qurʾān as clarification or the Prophet's Qurʾānic explanation. — Zajjaj says it clarifies everything; Wāḥidī says the Qurʾān is bayān. ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās calls Muhammad's eloquent speech the Qurʾān. Rāzī reads it as expansion of the earlier Qurʾān teaching and cites 3:138, where it separates truth and falsehood. Study Quran also cites 3:138. Ālūsī reports Ibn Kaysān's explanation of revelation, including general or ambiguous passages and 16:44, but calls identifying bayān with the Qurʾān itself very remote. Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise calls the Muhammad-and-Qurʾān reading remote. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن كسيان); STUDYQURAN(Q, R); ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 55:4/q02/p9 Guidance or the paths of guidance and error. — Māwardī transmits Ibn Jurayj's simple guidance gloss; Ibn al-Jawzī calls it paths of guidance; Ālūsī specifies the path of guidance and the path of error; Abū Ḥayyān reports the guidance view among alternatives. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جريج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(محمد بن كعب والضحاك وابن جريج ويمان); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جريج)]
- 55:4/q02/p10 The history of past and future events disclosed to Muhammad. — Ibn Kaysān, in Wāḥidī, Baghawī, and Ibn al-Jawzī, includes earlier and later peoples and Judgment Day; Qurtubī likewise says the Prophet told of what was and will be. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن كيسان); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن كيسان); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كيسان); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 55:4/q02/p11 The language given to each people. — Al-Suddī is reported by Thaʿlabī, Baghawī, and Qurtubī; Qushayrī gives the same explanation while treating the human as a species. [4 notes: THALABI(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); QUSHAYRI]
- 55:4/q02/p12 What one says and what is said to one. — Muhammad ibn Kaʿb's two-way formulation is reported by Thaʿlabī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and Abū Ḥayyān; it includes receiving speech as well as speaking. [3 notes: THALABI(محمد بن كعب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(محمد بن كعب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(محمد بن كعب والضحاك وابن جريج ويمان)]
- 55:4/q02/p13 The Greatest Name through which all things were taught. — Ṭabrisī attributes this to al-Ṣādiq; Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī report its attribution to Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq. [3 notes: TABRISI(الصادق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(جعفر الصادق)]
- 55:4/q02/p14 Knowledge of this world and the next. — Abū Ḥayyān lists this among Adam interpretations; Ālūsī attributes it to Qatāda. Qatāda's formulation in Ṭabarī likewise speaks of distinguishing worldly and afterlife matters, including law. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 55:4/q02/p15 Comprehensive explanation of all things taught to Adam. — Muqātil says Adam learned speech as explanation of everything. Ibn al-Jawzī reports explaining everything as distinct from learning all names or languages. [3 notes: MUQATIL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وقتادة,غير مسمى)]
- 55:4/q02/p16 The way to God taught to a human created without knowledge of Him. — Junayd, as transmitted by Sulamī, and the anonymous companions of thoughts, as transmitted by Māwardī, give this inward meaning. [2 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد); MAWARDI-FULL(بعض أصحاب الخواطر)]

## 55:4/q03 How are language and expression acquired through God's teaching?  [عَلَّمَهُ ٱلْبَيَانَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether teaching means direct bestowal of languages or creation of the nature and social conditions in which people form them.
- 55:4/q03/p1 God directly gives or institutes particular languages. — Rāzī says the verse may support divine institution of languages. Al-Suddī, as reported by Thaʿlabī, Baghawī, and Qurtubī, says God taught each people its language; the Adam traditions instead give him every language. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; THALABI(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(بحر العلوم)]
- 55:4/q03/p2 God creates human nature for socially developed, conventional language. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī denies revelation or inspiration of ready-made languages: social need leads people through signs, sounds, and speech; human nature supports conventional links of words to meanings, and writing subsequently represents words and completes speech. He cites 30:22. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 55:4/q04 How is teaching bayān related to teaching the Qurʾān?  [عَلَّمَهُ ٱلْبَيَانَ · links]
turns on: Whether bayān is the medium that makes Qurʾān instruction possible, the Qurʾān itself, or the mode specified by the subsequent clauses.
- 55:4/q04/p1 Expression and understanding equip humans to learn, recite, convey, and interpret the Qurʾān. — Ibn Kathīr connects pronunciation to reciting its letters; Bīqāʿī calls the faculty the means of learning. Bursevī, Ālūsī, Elmalılı, and Ebussuud emphasize understanding others as well as speaking. Elmalılı invokes Adam's names, prophetic communication, and communities' access to scripture. Iṣlāḥī says the capacity permits mutual teaching and learning by revelation instead of punishment. Study Quran links 2–4 to learning and reciting God's Word, citing 44:58 and 54:17, 22, 32, 40. [12 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Ebussuud); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Q); IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 55:4/q04/p2 Bayān is the Qurʾān itself, so the later clause unpacks the first teaching. — Rāzī makes it an elaboration of the concise initial reference and cites 3:138; Wāḥidī calls the Qurʾān a clarification of everything. Ālūsī formulates this as a possible repetition but rejects it as very remote; Study Quran reports the Qurʾān as a name of bayān with 3:138. [3 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q, R); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 55:4/q04/p3 Human creation identifies the learner and bayān explains how teaching occurs. — Ālūsī transmits al-Irshād's account of how the clauses relate; Rāzī compares 96:4–5, where teaching with the pen leaves the recipient unnamed before teaching humanity what it did not know. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الإرشاد); RAZI-FULL]

## 55:4/q05 How do the clauses about creating the human and teaching bayān work grammatically and rhetorically?  [عَلَّمَهُ ٱلْبَيَانَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the asyndetic clauses function as independent predicates, a confirming assertion, or an almost substitutive pairing.
- 55:4/q05/p1 They are successive predicates of al-Raḥmān without conjunction, enumerating distinct favors. — Zamakhsharī and Nasafī treat al-Raḥmān as subject and each verb with pronoun as predicate. Zamakhsharī says each unconnected clause independently rebukes deniers; later conjunctions join suitable neighbors, illustrated by favors to Zayd followed by a challenge to denial. Ālūsī also treats each clause as a predicate. Ebussuud, via Elmalılı, calls them synonymous clauses in an enumeration. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Ebussuud)]
- 55:4/q05/p2 Human creation is a separate assertion confirming the preceding clause. — Wāḥidī describes خلق الإنسان as coordinated by sense but without an overt conjunction, affirming the prior statement. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 55:4/q05/p3 Teaching bayān is placed alongside creation almost as its substitute. — Burhān says the omitted conjunction makes the gift nearly a substitute for creating humanity, since a human is a living speaker. Rāzī says the absence of a conjunction makes teaching articulate speech explain the preceding creation of the human: rational speech defines that creation. [2 notes: BURHAN; RAZI-FULL]

## 55:4/q06 What does fronting the subject in this construction convey?  [عَلَّمَهُ · rhetoric]
- 55:4/q06/p1 Restriction. — Elmalılı says placing the subject first in the construction conveys restriction. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 55:4/q07 Why do Qurʾān teaching, human creation, and teaching bayān appear in this order?  [عَلَّمَهُ ٱلْبَيَانَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the order follows rank and purpose, pre-eternal origin, a heaven-and-earth pairing, temporal creation before human instruction, or the sūra's sequence of favors.
- 55:4/q07/p1 The greatest religious favor and its purpose take precedence over creation and the human faculty. — Zamakhsharī calls revelation and teaching the summit and standard of heavenly books; Nasafī says humanity was created for religion and knowledge of revelation; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Zamakhsharī and says religion reforms worldly life and secures the next. Sāmarrāʾī cites 51:56: worship is creation's purpose, so the book of worship comes first; Qurʾānic order can follow purpose, merit, or context rather than time. Rāzī says the order of al-ʿAlaq differs only because it lacks explicit Qurʾān teaching; its creation-before-teaching sequence parallels this one. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الكشاف); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; RAZI-FULL]
- 55:4/q07/p2 The sequence pairs heavenly instruction with earthly creation and expression. — Ālūsī says the arrangement is elegant whether the omitted first recipient is angels or Gabriel; Rāzī distinguishes humanity taught the Qurʾān, with the second object omitted to exalt the favor, from angels taught first, requiring the human recipient to be named here. Ālūsī cites 56:77–79 for angels before human creation. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 55:4/q07/p3 The opening lists divine favors while showing mercy and perfected creation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the sūra highlights God's perfected creation, humanity's dependence on mercy, and worldly and afterlife favors amid punishment of sinners and honor of the pious. Iṣlāḥī connects the opening to God's merciful choice to teach by revelation rather than punish. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 55:4/q07/p4 Qurʾān teaching precedes creation because its origin is pre-eternal; human expression requires creation. — Bursevī reports that receptive people were readied in pre-eternity; Qurʾān names speech at its unified origin and Furqān its differentiated end, citing 25:1. He therefore places expression after creation, while Qurʾān teaching can precede it. [2 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
- 55:4/q07/p5 Bayān comes after creation because teaching the human expression follows forming the human. — Rāzī compares the creation-before-teaching order with 96:1, 3, 4 and 96:4–5; on the speech reading, bodily formation precedes the distinguishing knowledge of expression. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 55:4/q08 What does the gift of bayān enable or teach about humanity?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Which consequence of expression the sources foreground: human distinction, religious learning, reasoning and society, eloquence, or the favor's addressees.
- 55:4/q08/p1 It distinguishes humans from animals through speech, thought, and learning. — Zajjaj, Qushayrī, Wāḥidī, Zamakhsharī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ṭabrisī, Nasafī, Qāshānī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ālūsī, and Study Quran stress this distinction. Rāzī contrasts bodily form and acquired knowledge. Bīqāʿī adds inference of unseen from signs or calculation and Adam's knowledge before angels. Iṣlāḥī reports Aristotle on intellectual distinction and says refusing to reason despite this gift lowers people to animals; Kuranyolu says comprehension is intellect's practical face. Rāzī treats rational speech as defining human creation. [23 notes: ZAJJAJ; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العالية ومرة وابن زيد والحسن والقرظي والسدي ويمان بن رباب); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد والجمهور); TABRISI(الجبائي,الحسن، أبو العالية، ابن زيد، السدي); RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد والجمهور); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Aristotle); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 55:4/q08/p2 It makes religious instruction, worship, and human accountability possible. — Qatāda says law and moral discrimination establish proof against people. Iṣlāḥī says speech supports learning from the merciful Qurʾān rather than punishment and should be used for teaching. Kuranyolu says thought and expression allow people to recognize worship and articulate duties and rights. Elmalılı says prophets communicate revelation and communities benefit from scripture. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 55:4/q08/p3 It supports reasoning, self-perfection, shared knowledge, culture, and civilization. — Bīqāʿī says it grasps particulars and universals, infers absent matters, and lets people perfect themselves and teach others; familiarity hides the extraordinary divine gift. Iṣlāḥī describes movement between particulars and principles, reasoning, inference, and opinions. Kuranyolu links it to culture, civilization, meaningful action, art, and diverse modes from tears to visual arts; Elmalılı calls it essential to Qurʾānic understanding. [9 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]
- 55:4/q08/p4 It is a great blessing that preserves human standing and guides people toward good. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls language one of God's greatest blessings and a condition of human standing; Ibn ʿĀshūr places knowledge and expression among merciful created favors, with the sūra contrasting sinners' punishment and the pious' honor. [3 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 55:4/q08/p5 It honors eloquence and equips understanding and persuasion. — Burhān says speech with Qurʾān teaching suffices to show eloquence's honor; he cites 3:138 and 16:89 as scriptural praise of bayān, and says rhetorical forms strengthen interpretation, argument, and a hearer's assent. [3 notes: BURHAN]
- 55:4/q08/p6 The human is emphasized because he receives and explains revelation to both groups. — Sāmarrāʾī says the human is singled out as recipient of the book and its interpreter for both groups; expression reveals what is inward. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 55:4/q09 What inward or spiritual senses are given to bayān?  [ٱلْبَيَانَ · inward]
turns on: Which inner process is identified with bayān: faculties of the person, prior disclosure, guidance toward God, address to God, or purified remembrance.
- 55:4/q09/p1 Speech arises from spirit and unfolds through reason, heart, thought, and nature. — Tustarī describes Adam's speech from spirit, rational understanding, heart discernment, human thought, and natural disposition, subsequently clarified by God. Sulamī transmits Sahl's parallel account: speech is creatures' mind and spirit's essence, with intellect understanding, heart discernment, and self nature. [2 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل)]
- 55:4/q09/p2 Bayān teaches the way to God after humanity was created ignorant of Him. — Sulamī attributes this to Junayd; Māwardī transmits the same from unnamed companions of thoughts. [2 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد); MAWARDI-FULL(بعض أصحاب الخواطر)]
- 55:4/q09/p3 Bayān discloses a teaching already present in pre-eternity. — Sulamī transmits al-Wāsiṭī: the spirit had been disclosed before time, and bayān reveals an earlier teaching. [1 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي)]
- 55:4/q09/p4 For believers and knowers it is addressing the Lord through speech, breath, tears, longing, and sighs. — Qushayrī contrasts ordinary address by likeness and form with the knowers' modes of addressing God. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 55:4/q09/p5 Remembrance and reflection become clear spiritual perception when directed through God. — Al-Wāsiṭī, via Sulamī, says self-directed remembrance and reflection cut one off; for and through God they connect and purify the senses, letting the clear heart behold divine dominion. Increasing both brings nearness and knowledge. [1 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي)]

## 55:4/q10 Can unqualified bayān name ugly speech?  [ٱلْبَيَانَ · meaning]
- 55:4/q10/p1 No; God's praise reserves the unqualified term for worthy speech, though an ugly utterance can be called clarification with a qualifier. — Rummānī distinguishes the praised absolute name from the qualified sense of merely making one's intended meaning understood. [1 notes: RUMMANI]

## 55:4/q11 How does divine or Qurʾānic bayān compare with other speech?  [ٱلْبَيَانَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the point is the Qurʾān's superior expressive rank or recognition of genuinely divine speech.
- 55:4/q11/p1 Qurʾānic expression is the highest and most guiding form. — Bāqillānī calls it noblest, most complete, elevated, eloquent, radiant, and rightly guiding. Burhān cites 3:138 and 16:89 as praise of divine clarification. Zamakhsharī and Nasafī call Qurʾānic teaching the chief religious favor and standard for scriptures. [4 notes: BAQILLANI; BURHAN; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 55:4/q11/p2 Speech from divine sovereignty can be recognized as different from human pretension. — Bāqillānī reports Abū Bakr judging Musaylima's utterance not to be from God; the episode illustrates distinguishing divine speech from other speech. [1 notes: BAQILLANI(أبو بكر الصديق)]

## 55:4/q12 What doctrinal inference is drawn from the pairing of Qurʾān and human creation?  [* · theology]
- 55:4/q12/p1 The Qurʾān is uncreated. — Bursevī reports Abū al-Suʿūd's count: the Qurʾān is mentioned in 54 places without being called created, while humanity is called created in 18; this passage pairs their different descriptions. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أبو السعود)]
