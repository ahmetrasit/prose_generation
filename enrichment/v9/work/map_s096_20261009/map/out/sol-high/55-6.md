# 55:6: 12 questions

## 55:6/q01 What does al-najm refer to here?  [ٱلنَّجْمُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the tree pairing privileges a ground plant, the preceding sun and moon suggest stars, or the shared word is intentionally inclusive; inward readers assign the terms to faculties.
- 55:6/q01/p1 Stemless or low-growing earthly vegetation. — Al-Farrāʾ names grass and herbage; Ibn Qutayba, al-Zajjāj, Ibn ʿAbbās and others distinguish plants without a stem from trunked trees. Al-Suddī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya derive the name from emerging from earth; Ibn al-Aʿrābī notes najma is a small plant and najama describes a horn, star or tooth appearing. Abū ʿUbayda cites al-Ḥārith b. Ẓālim's donkey pulling a plant; al-Azharī explains its strained body; al-Layth and Zuhayr supply plant imagery. Ṣafwān b. Asad's line uses anjama for plants emerging, though al-Wāḥidī says he found no linguist using anjama for growing. Al-Qurṭubī derives najm from appearance. Some define the plant as winter-perishing, al-Muntaqā as growth ending within a year; Bursawī includes vine and pumpkin, Kuran Yolu grain and grass. Ibn Jarīr prefers this sense according to Ibn Kathīr; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and al-Ālūsī favor it for its pairing with shajar. Abū Ḥayyān calls it the apparent reading. Islahi calls the proposed small plants, thorny trees or creepers far-fetched. [49 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(الكلبي); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ(أهل اللغة وأكثر أهل التفسير); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); THALABI(السدي,قيل); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة عن الحارث بن ظالم,ابن الأعرابي,الأزهري,الليث,المفسرون,عامة أهل اللغة والمفسرين); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI(غريب القرآن); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس والسدي وسفيان); TABRISI(ابن عباس، سعيد بن جبير، سفيان الثوري); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس والسدي ومقاتل واللغويون); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره,زهير بن أبي سلمى,صفوان بن أسد التميمي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة والحسن); BURHAN; ITQAN; BURSEVI(المنتقى); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس، ابن جبير، أبو رزين); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Aj, Ṭ); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)+; TABATABAI+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some people)-]
- 55:6/q01/p2 The heavenly stars, taken collectively. — Mujāhid, Qatāda, al-Ḥasan and others give this sense; Ibn ʿĀshūr and the Study Quran explain singular najm as collective, while Ibn ʿĀshūr also allows a singular with plural nujūm, citing 53:1 and 52:49. Al-Zajjāj invokes 22:18's celestial prostration; Abū Isḥāq invokes 16:12; Muḥammad b. Yazīd cites poetry for celestial najm. Ibn Kathīr prefers it on 22:18, and Islahi regards it as the conventional sense. Al-Qurṭubī reports al-Ṭabarī choosing the star reading with its moving shadow. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says stars appear in their heavenly realm as trees do on earth. [27 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); ZAJJAJ(قيل); THALABI(مجاهد وقتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,قتادة,مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة والحسن); TABRISI(مجاهد، قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن يزيد); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة والحسن); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد، قتادة، الحسن); IBNASHUR-FULL(مجاهد); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Mujāhid, Qatādah and Ḥasan); STUDYQURAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد والحسن وقتادة)+; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن ومجاهد)+]
- 55:6/q01/p3 Both earthly vegetation and celestial stars fall within the word. — Al-Zajjāj derives the breadth from najama, to appear or rise. Al-Biqāʿī includes both sorts of stars and their appointed courses and benefits. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the shared word bridges heavenly and earthly pairs; Qushayrī sets out both referents. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; QUSHAYRI]
- 55:6/q01/p4 Inwardly, it is the sentient animal soul. — Al-Qāshānī places that soul in the body's night; with the nourishing vegetative soul as tree it turns toward the body's earth to nurture and perfect it. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 55:6/q01/p5 Inwardly, it is the intellect. — Bursawī says intellect guides knowledge but fades before divine realities without unveiling; thought, its tree counterpart, remains confined to natural, imaginative and bodily powers. Both are bounded creatures unable to comprehend the eternal Creator; he cites 39:67. [2 notes: BURSEVI]
- 55:6/q01/p6 Inwardly, it is the lower powers. — Al-Ālūsī reports the counterpart tree as higher predispositions; both humble themselves on returning to God. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 55:6/q02 What does al-shajar refer to here?  [ٱلشَّجَرُ · referent]
turns on: Whether shajar names its botanical referent or an inward counterpart to najm.
- 55:6/q02/p1 Plants or trees with a lasting stem or trunk. — Al-Farrāʾ, Ibn ʿAbbās and many others oppose this to stemless najm; al-Wāḥidī and al-Baghawī say its stem endures winter. Al-Muntaqā instead contrasts growth that continues beyond its year with a plant whose growth ends. Ibn ʿAṭiyya relates shajar to interwoven branches. Ibn al-Jawzī calls shajar plural of shajara and notes shajir for a tree-filled valley or land and shajara for human dispute. Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies rooted growth with an elevated stem beneficial to people and animals. [37 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(الكلبي); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ(أهل اللغة وأكثر أهل التفسير); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(عامة أهل اللغة والمفسرين); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI(غريب القرآن); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس، سعيد بن جبير، سفيان الثوري); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI(المنتقى); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس، ابن جبير، أبو رزين); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Aj, Ṭ)]
- 55:6/q02/p2 Inwardly, it is the nourishing vegetative soul. — Al-Qāshānī says it turns to the body's earth with the animal soul to nourish and perfect the body. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 55:6/q02/p3 Inwardly, it is thought. — Bursawī confines it to natural, imaginative and bodily powers; reason and thought circle the created world but cannot encompass God, citing 39:67. [2 notes: BURSEVI]
- 55:6/q02/p4 Inwardly, it is the higher predispositions. — Al-Ālūsī reports their humbling themselves before God on their return to Him. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 55:6/q03 How do the star and tree prostrate?  [ٱلنَّجْمُ ٱلشَّجَرُ يَسْجُدَانِ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes variously explain sujūd as submission, observable motion or shadow, a literal unseen act, an evidentiary sign, or inward humbling.
- 55:6/q03/p1 They submit to God's will and appointed functions. — Ibn Qutayba describes inanimate compliance under subjugation; al-Naḥḥās calls submission the broad sense of sujūd, extending to animals and prayer. Ibn al-Jawzī says Arabic sujūd involves lowering the head without necessarily touching the ground and can denote any submission. Al-Jubbāʾī says divine direction meets no resistance. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Bursawī and al-Ālūsī compare natural obedience to willing worship; al-Ālūsī calls it an explicit derivative metaphor. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī specifies obedience in growth and, more precisely, roots penetrating soil for nourishment as dependence. Ibn ʿAṭiyya transmits Mujāhid's figurative submission, illustrated by a line about hills prostrating to hooves. Qushayrī says created things' submission precludes their worship. [26 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(الجبائي); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري,النحاس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد والحسن); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 55:6/q03/p2 They prostrate through shadows that move and turn. — Abū Razīn and Saʿīd, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Zajjāj and others identify the shadow; al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī cite 16:48, as do al-Baghawī and Bursawī. Al-Farrāʾ makes the bodies face the rising sun and incline with it until the shadow recedes; al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Qutayba, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī transmit that detail. Ibn ʿAṭiyya applies vegetation's shadow and turning to either meaning of najm. Al-Qurṭubī reports Mujāhid's moving shadow for the star and al-Ṭabarī's choice. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds their approaching earthly shadows and cites 13:15. Al-Ṭabrisī says shadows reveal a directing Creator. [24 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(الفراء); TAB-FULL(أبو رزين وسعيد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(السدي,قيل); MAWARDI-FULL(الزجاج,الضحاك,الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل التفسير); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(الضحاك، سعيد بن جبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج,الضحاك,الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد والحسن); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد، الحسن); IBNASHUR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن ومجاهد)+]
- 55:6/q03/p3 The star prostrates by rising. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports Mujāhid and Qatāda; al-Baghawī reports Mujāhid. [2 notes: THALABI(مجاهد وقتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 55:6/q03/p4 The star prostrates by setting, while the tree makes fruit available. — Al-Māwardī gives this alternative, repeated by al-Qurṭubī. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān transmit star setting with the tree or vegetation's shadow. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds the descending star, ground-hugging plants, and trees bending in wind or lowering branches for fruit and leaves; he cites Imruʾ al-Qays's face-first leaning image for this figurative descent. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد والحسن); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد، قتادة، الحسن); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 55:6/q03/p5 Their form and activity disclose the Creator's workmanship and call for submission. — Ibn Baḥr says their form and power show workmanship requiring submission; al-Ṭabrisī says origination and artistry are visible in them, while their shadows also disclose a directing Creator. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes rational signs of divine making and subjection from symbolic or imaginative signs accessible to differing minds. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); TABRISI(الضحاك، سعيد بن جبير); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 55:6/q03/p6 They prostrate morning and evening. — Mujāhid gives this timing for trees, star and tree, or the stars together with trees, in the several transmissions. [4 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); TABRISI(مجاهد، قتادة)]
- 55:6/q03/p7 The stars can prostrate literally. — Abū Isḥāq permits literal celestial prostration and cites 16:12. Al-Zajjāj finds the celestial reading possible because 22:18 names the sun, moon and stars among prostrating beings. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); ZAJJAJ]
- 55:6/q03/p8 Inwardly, souls or faculties turn toward their appointed end. — Al-Qāshānī's animal and vegetative souls turn to the body's earth for nurture; al-Ālūsī's lower powers and higher predispositions humble themselves upon return to God; Bursawī says reason and thought cannot encompass the Creator. [4 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 55:6/q04 Why is yasjudān dual?  [يَسْجُدَانِ · grammar]
- 55:6/q04/p1 It agrees with the two named kinds, even though each noun is collective. — Abū ʿUbayda says the two subjects are dual in wording; Ibn ʿAṭiyya illustrates collective nouns with dual verbs in poetry; al-Majāz likewise matches the two nouns. Al-Farrāʾ and al-Kisāʾī observe that two nonhuman collectives often take one singular verb, although a dual is valid; al-Wāḥidī adds that the dual suits the line endings. Al-Kisāʾī heard both sūdān and sūd of two black sheep; al-Farrāʾ favored sūd. Al-Farrāʾ says masculine sheep with feminine camels usually take singular feminine but either-gender dual is valid; in 'your people and their camels came', a human plural may mean the handlers or both mixed groups. [9 notes: FARRA(الكسائي); MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة)]

## 55:6/q05 What does the present verb convey?  [يَسْجُدَانِ · grammar]
- 55:6/q05/p1 Their prostration continually recurs. — Ibn ʿĀshūr points to the imperfect and cites 13:15. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 55:6/q06 Why is al-najm placed after the sun and moon and before al-shajar?  [ٱلنَّجْمُ ٱلشَّجَرُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the word's two senses act as misdirection, a bridge between realms, or an ordering of foods.
- 55:6/q06/p1 It invites a celestial reading while intending the more remote plant sense. — Al-Zarkashī calls this tawriya, a shared word whose near sense misdirects toward a distant intended sense; al-Suyūṭī calls it a Qurʾanic double entendre suggested by 55:5. Al-Ālūsī likewise says the prior sun and moon evoke stars even though the paired tree favors plants. [3 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL]
- 55:6/q06/p2 It bridges celestial and earthly pairs through a word that can name either. — Ibn ʿĀshūr uses the ambiguity to make a transition from heavenly to earthly favors. Al-Zajjāj's derivation from appearing or rising supports both senses. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ZAJJAJ]
- 55:6/q06/p3 The staple food plant precedes the tree whose fruit is often a treat. — Abū Ḥayyān makes the earthly pair traces and sustenance corresponding to the sun and moon. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 55:6/q07 How is this clause built and connected syntactically to what precedes?  [* · grammar]
turns on: The notes distinguish formal coordination, implicit semantic reference, and the significance of nominal form.
- 55:6/q07/p1 It is a coordinated predicate linked to the preceding clause. — Al-Ālūsī calls the plant prostration sentence a predicate coordinated with the prior predicate; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it begins with its subject as the preceding clause does. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 55:6/q07/p2 The connection to the Merciful is understood from meaning. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī say the sun and moon's calculation is God's, and the prostration is to Him, without an explicit pronoun or verbal connective. Al-Bayḍāwī says the expected parallel would explicitly state celestial movement and plants' prostration to God, but the omitted material is clear. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(الزمخشري)]
- 55:6/q07/p3 Its nominal construction distinguishes the four nonhuman forms from the human addressee. — Bursawī says their essence and attributes differ from those of the human addressee. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 55:6/q08 How does the verse fit the sequence of nearby signs?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes emphasize ordered pairs, the pattern of omitted and resumed conjunctions, or a shift toward reflection.
- 55:6/q08/p1 Heavenly bodies are paired with earthly plants under one divine order. — Al-Zamakhsharī pairs sun and moon above with plants below, heaven with earth, and celestial reckoning with plant submission; al-Rāzī calls them celestial and earthly signs and cites 75:9. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Bursawī, al-Ālūsī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Islahi develop the paired symmetry, motion and stillness, food from grains, fruit and fodder, or harmony under one Creator. Al-Suyūṭī calls it combining multiple things under one judgment. [14 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 55:6/q08/p2 Separate blessings are first enumerated without conjunction, then related clauses are joined. — Al-Zamakhsharī says omission makes each earlier blessing an independent rebuke to deniers; al-Nasafī, Bursawī, al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī repeat this. Al-Ālūsī says joining the first clauses could make their blessings seem one, while the later conjunction unites suitable celestial and terrestrial clauses. Al-Bayḍāwī notes the omitted connective is evident. Al-Thaʿlabī reports alternatives linking the arrangement backward to the Merciful's teaching and calculation or to the explanation. [9 notes: THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); TABATABAI(الزمخشري)]
- 55:6/q08/p3 The clause moves from a list of favors to a symbolic sign that awakens reflection. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says its coordinated terms contrast and reveal divine power; Islahi urges opponents to reflect on familiar heavenly and earthly signs instead of demanding a new sign, and on the recurrent challenge to humans and jinn. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 55:6/q09 What do the verse's prostrating beings teach people?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw ethical, theological and cosmic lessons from the same obedience.
- 55:6/q09/p1 They call people to obey and worship God gratefully. — Elmalılı says natural obedience should lead humans to willing prostration and gratitude; Kuran Yolu contrasts involuntary plant obedience with the dignity of conscious human reverence. Islahi says the star and tree call people to worship; their ordered courses and the cosmic laws warn humans against anarchy and punishment. Muqātil links the surrounding balance to justice and honest measures. [8 notes: ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; MUQATIL]
- 55:6/q09/p2 They reveal the Creator's unity, power and rule over all beings. — Al-Biqāʿī says their submission refutes independent natural causes. Qushayrī says created stars and trees must not be worshiped. Al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn ʿĀshūr point to divine artistry and signs of subjection. The Study Quran cites 22:18 and 16:49 for universal submission; Bursawī cites 17:44 to say creatures' praise exceeds people's comprehension. Qatāda says every created thing sent from heaven worships willingly or unwillingly. [8 notes: BIQAI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري); TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; BURSEVI; TAB-FULL(قتادة)]
- 55:6/q09/p3 The sun and moon's punctual paths model obedience to divine limits. — Islahi cites 36:40: the two paths do not intrude on each other, their disciplined obedience is taqwā in religious terms, and human lawbreaking brings punishment. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 55:6/q10 What does the preceding sun-and-moon clause contribute to this verse?  [* · links]
turns on: The contextual notes discuss the meaning and syntax of 55:5, its cosmic example, and a transmitted inscription.
- 55:6/q10/p1 Its ḥusbān means reckoning, either a verbal noun or a plural of ḥisāb. — Al-Thaʿlabī compares ghufrān and kufrān for the verbal noun and ordinary broken plurals for the plural; al-Ṭabrisī records both explanations. [2 notes: THALABI; TABRISI]
- 55:6/q10/p2 Its sun and moon may be raised by an implied verb or be a subject awaiting a predicate. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports both analyses. Al-Ālūsī denies that the sun-and-moon phrase is a predicate of the implicit subject; he says al-Zamakhsharī discussed semantic linkage. He also says a further interpretation goes too far while conceding al-Zamakhsharī's surface wording might suggest such predication and leaves reflection open. [2 notes: THALABI; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 55:6/q10/p3 It gives the ordered heavenly counterpart to the plants' submission. — Islahi says sun and moon stay in punctual, distinct ordained paths, citing 36:40; the plant-and-tree pair shares divine governance and supplies earthly sustenance. Al-Ṭabrisī also notes ālāʾ are blessings and cites Abū ʿUbayda for singular forms. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI]
- 55:6/q10/p4 A transmitted account places inscriptions on the sun and moon. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports writing on their faces and interiors affirming divine unity, creation and power, and the moral consequences of good and evil. [1 notes: THALABI]

## 55:6/q11 What rhetorical matching is seen at the transition to the following verse?  [* · rhetoric]
- 55:6/q11/p1 The wording of adjacent expressions can be matched despite differences in force or tense. — Al-Zarkashī says a weaker passive participle may be selected over a stronger past tense for parallelism, and a present participle may match a future form although 51:6's participle itself denotes present. Matching may pull a second expression toward the first, as in a neighboring-word ablution reading at 5:6 and the shift from trees' prostration to heaven's raising; or pull the first toward the second, as in Ibrāhīm b. Abī ʿUbayla's kasra reading of al-ḥamdu lillāh. He also says heavy wording can suit a weighty sense, and lowly wording a diminishing comparison. [5 notes: BURHAN]

## 55:6/q12 Does this verse itself establish an issue about prostration by beings without reason?  [يَسْجُدَانِ · links]
- 55:6/q12/p1 Its fuller explanation is deferred to the discussion of 16:49. — Ibn al-Jawzī directs the reader there for how beings without reason prostrate. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
