# 47:17: 12 questions

## 47:17/q01 Who are those who have been guided?  [ٱلَّذِينَ ٱهْتَدَوْا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the description refers generally to believers or specifically to earlier scriptural believers who accepted Muhammad.
- 47:17/q01/p1 The believers or Muslims who receive the truth and follow it. — Al-Kalbi (Wahidi), the majority (Ibn al-Jawzi), and others identify them as Muslims or believers. Nahhas stresses acceptance and action; Biqai describes sincere faith, surrender, listening, and spiritual effort; Razi and Kuranyolu stress understanding and acting on what is heard; Tabatabai calls it following sound innate nature and truth. Ibn Kathir says they seek guidance and remain firm; Ibn Atiyya says the wording credits their effort. Jishumi contrasts heedless and mindful listeners through Qatada's report. [19 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجمهور); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; JISHUMI(قتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 47:17/q01/p2 People of Scripture who followed their earlier prophets and then believed in Muhammad. — Ibn Atiyya's group specifies Christians guided by belief in Jesus before accepting Muhammad. Ikrima identifies People of Scripture; Durr contrasts those who accepted Muhammad after his mission with people who had earlier believed but then rejected him, linking the latter to 3:106. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عكرمة); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة); DURR-FULL(أهل الكتاب,عكرمة)]

## 47:17/q02 Who or what increases their guidance?  [زَادَهُمْ · referent]
turns on: The implicit subject of زادهم and whether the preceding speech, revelation, or hypocritical conduct functions as its cause.
- 47:17/q02/p1 God increases it, through His grace, inspiration, proofs, or revelation. — Zajjaj prefers God as the pronoun's referent. Ibn Atiyya lists understanding, evidence, laws, prohibitions, and reports as means; Abu Hayyan points to God's sealing in the preceding verse and His giving in the next clause. Razi also pairs God's increase with His sealing of hypocritical hearts. Wahidi reports Ibn Abbas and Muqatil on God granting the increase; Baydawi and Alusi mention enabling grace and inspiration. Islahi links the increase to preserving innate light and cites 9:124–125. [28 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس,الضحاك,عطاء عن ابن عباس,مقاتل بن حيان,مقاتل بن سليمان); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); ZAJJAJ+]
- 47:17/q02/p2 The Qur'an or its recitation increases it. — Ibn Jurayj names the Qur'an; Wahidi's Dahhak says believers rejoice at each revelation; Qurtubi says the heard Qur'an multiplies certainty. Tabrisi explicitly offers Qur'an recitation as the implicit agent; Razi includes God's words among what they heard. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج); WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 47:17/q02/p3 The Messenger or his words increase it. — Zajjaj says what the Messenger said; Wahidi links it to his immediately preceding words. Razi contrasts the believers' understanding of the Prophet's and God's words with the hypocrites' failure in 47:16. Ibn Atiyya's special reading makes Muhammad the cause of further guidance for Christians. Alusi calls a proposed prophetic subject contrary to the apparent sense and argues for alignment with God's sealing of hearts. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء وأبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 47:17/q02/p4 The hypocrites' mockery, words, or turning away increase it by provoking believers' insight. — Al-Farra and Zajjaj identify mockery or turning away. Ibn Atiyya says believers marvel at the hypocrites' confusion, thank God for faith, and gain insight, with the cause expressed as agent. Razi says they abhor and avoid that conduct; Abu Hayyan says they praise God and strengthen their support of religion. Alusi calls this suggestion very remote and scarcely worth considering. [10 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء وأبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 47:17/q03 What is the increase in guidance?  [هُدًۭى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether added guidance is chiefly understanding, greater faith, new revelation and rulings, sustained practice, or the capacity to guide others.
- 47:17/q03/p1 New understanding, knowledge, insight, and evidence. — Nahhas includes revealed verses, proofs, signs, and arguments. Al-Rabi calls it added knowledge; Dahhak says they understand what they hear and act on it; al-Kalbi adds religious insight and affirmation of the Prophet. Ibn Atiyya mentions growing knowledge and belief from laws and reports. Ibn al-Jawzi reports knowledge or insight; Razi cites following the best of what is heard (39:17–18) and affirming ambiguous revelation (3:7). Abu Hayyan mentions understanding and proofs; Tabatabai says the increase perfects knowledge. Study Quran reports faith, knowledge, and insight. [16 notes: NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(الربيع بن أنس,الضحاك,الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الربيع بن أنس,الضحاك,الكلبي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ)]
- 47:17/q03/p2 Further faith, certainty, conviction, or openness of heart. — Mawardi and Qurtubi say God opens hearts further to existing faith. Qurtubi says hearing the Qur'an multiplies certainty. Ibn Atiyya says their knowledge and belief grow; Nasafi allows an opening of hearts. Tabatabai says faith and guidance have degrees. Razi contrasts this with sealed hypocritical hearts; Islahi links it to preserved innate light. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ)]
- 47:17/q03/p3 Knowledge and obedience to successive abrogating rulings. — Muqatil says decisive verses abrogated an earlier command. Abu Salih says an abrogator itself may be the additional guidance; Muqatil ibn Hayyan adds reward for obedience to the earlier command before following the new one. Atiyya names knowledge of abrogating and abrogated rulings; Ibn Abbas says the Qur'an guided first, then such knowledge increased guidance. [7 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(عطية); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس,مقاتل بن حيان,مقاتل بن سليمان); QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 47:17/q03/p4 Deeper practice, steadfastness, and guidance toward truth. — Mawardi says truth leads to further truth. Dahhak joins understanding to acting. Qushayri says recognition without dispute yields steadfastness on truth's paths. Ibn Kathir says seekers find guidance and remain firm. Kuranyolu connects sound reasoning and the Prophet's explanations to continuing obedience; Islahi says God nurtures virtue according to capacity and desire. Razi invokes 6:90 as a command to follow earlier people's guidance and identifies hudā as the verbal noun of هدَى; Biqai links 10:9 to God's guidance of believers by their faith. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 47:17/q03/p5 A move from being guided listeners to guides who explain the Qur'an with proof. — Razi proposes that listeners understand and teach others; they guard against unsupported or merely personal interpretation. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 47:17/q04 How is وَٱلَّذِينَ ٱهْتَدَوْا۟ parsed?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ ٱهْتَدَوْا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the relative expression starts as a nominative subject or depends on an implied verb.
- 47:17/q04/p1 It is nominative as the sentence's subject. — Samin gives this parsing; Alusi calls it the apparent reading. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 47:17/q04/p2 It is accusative by preoccupation, governed by an omitted verb explained by the following verb. — Both Samin and Alusi allow this construction. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 47:17/q05 How is هُدًۭى construed and why is it indefinite?  [هُدًۭى · grammar]
turns on: Whether hudan fills a second object slot or specifies the increase.
- 47:17/q05/p1 It is the second object of زاد, with indefiniteness magnifying the added guidance. — Alusi calls this the more apparent construction and says the indefinite form magnifies the gift. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]
- 47:17/q05/p2 It is a specification of the increase; its indefiniteness magnifies it. — Alusi records this alternative while still explaining the indefinite form as magnifying the increase. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 47:17/q06 What does God give them as their taqwa?  [وَءَاتَىٰهُمْ تَقْوَىٰهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether taqwa is the disposition itself, ability to practice it, knowledge of what to avoid, its reward, or a particular form of obedience.
- 47:17/q06/p1 He inspires or creates God-fearing piety in them. — Zajjaj cites 48:26, the word of piety; al-Rabi names fear of God. Ibn Atiyya says آتاهم means gave and that God made them fear Him. Abu Hayyan and Samin analyze تقواهم as a verbal noun annexed to its subject. Ibn Kathir calls it inspired right judgment; Ibn al-Jawzi says fear of punishment leads them to avoid sin. Asad glosses it God-consciousness. Bursevi and Alusi mention creating piety; Alusi gives Ashʿari and effective-capacity accounts separately. Biqai's Ibn Barjan calls piety the action of faith, as limbs' deeds are the action of Islam. [19 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الربيع); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي); RAZI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(الربيع); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(ابن برجان); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 47:17/q06/p2 He enables or helps them act on what piety requires. — Muqatil specifies acting on decisive revelation until understood; Mawardi and Qurtubi cite doing obligatory deeds. Wahidi's Ibn Hayyan specifies God's commands and obligations. Al-Tibi prefers help because it pairs taqwa with resistance to desire and guidance with unsealed hearts, and cites 3:102 for perfected taqwa. Study Quran says God helps attain reverence. Tabatabai makes giving taqwa the perfection of action. [16 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن حيان); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البيضاوي,الزمخشري); STUDYQURAN(Z); TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي)+]
- 47:17/q06/p3 He gives them the reward of their piety, especially in the afterlife. — Al-Suddi specifies the afterlife; Wahidi's Ibn Hayyan calls it reward for deeds there. Sa'id ibn Jubayr and al-Jubba'i are named by Tabrisi; Wahidi's Sa'id wording speaks of inspiring the reward. Alusi says reward may be literal because piety causes it, or involve an omitted possessive; he calls it more informative without requiring maximal piety. Alusi says Baydawi's options do not conflict with orthodox doctrine. [18 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(سعيد بن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن حيان,سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); TABRISI(سعيد بن جبير وأبو علي الجبائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); RAZI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البيضاوي,الزمخشري); STUDYQURAN(Z)]
- 47:17/q06/p4 He explains what they must guard against and avoid. — Ibn Ziyad, and in Qurtubi also al-Suddi, give this explanation. Razi contrasts this direct gift of knowledge of piety with an implied reward word. Biqai adds help resisting desire and links the recipients to those on clear proof in 47:14. Alusi says the figurative wording can express showing what to avoid, and that the contrast with desire fits this reading. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زياد); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زياد، السدي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(البيضاوي)]
- 47:17/q06/p5 He enables them to leave abrogated rulings and follow abrogating ones. — Al-Farra speaks of receiving piety from an abrogated rule when an abrogator came. Atiyya is reported by Mawardi, Ibn al-Jawzi, and Qurtubi; Muqatil says they learned to act on the decisive replacement text. [5 notes: FARRA; MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(عطية); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطية); QURTUBI-FULL(عطية)]
- 47:17/q06/p6 He directs them to leave dispensations and undertake stricter duties. — Tabrisi gives this as a specification of what to avoid; Qurtubi attributes a sixth possibility to Mawardi. [2 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي)]

## 47:17/q07 Who is the agent of وَءَاتَىٰهُمْ?  [وَءَاتَىٰهُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the second clause retains a divine agent or follows another proposed agent from the preceding context.
- 47:17/q07/p1 God gives them their taqwa. — Ibn Atiyya prefers this as strongest despite possible variation with interpretations of the preceding clause. Samin allows God; Abu Hayyan explicitly says God makes them God-fearing. Alusi argues the apparent subject aligns with God's sealing of hearts in 47:16. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 47:17/q07/p2 The Messenger is its subject by figurative attribution. — Samin lists the Messenger as possible. Alusi reports the proposal from the preceding address but says it opposes the apparent sense and should align with God's sealing of hearts. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 47:17/q07/p3 The hypocrites' statement is its subject and increases believers' piety. — Samin records this as a possible antecedent for the pronoun. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 47:17/q08 How is تَقْوَىٰهُمْ grammatically formed?  [تَقْوَىٰهُمْ · grammar]
- 47:17/q08/p1 It is a verbal noun annexed to its subject, those who are guided. — Samin gives this analysis; Abu Hayyan uses it when explaining that God made them God-fearing. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 47:17/q09 How was وَءَاتَىٰهُمْ read differently?  [وَءَاتَىٰهُمْ · readings]
- 47:17/q09/p1 A variant reads 'and bestowed/gave them' in place of آتاهم. — Tha'labi attributes a bestowed/gave wording to Ibn Masud and al-A'mash. Ibn Atiyya gives al-A'mash's وأنطاهم, equates it with أعطاهم, and notes Muhammad ibn Talha's transmission from his father and its occurrence in Abd Allah's codex. Qurtubi reports أعطاهم. [3 notes: THALABI(ابن مسعود والأعمش); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعمش); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 47:17/q10 How do inward readings describe growth in guidance and piety?  [زَادَهُمْ هُدًۭى تَقْوَىٰهُمْ · inward]
turns on: Which stage or practice the spiritual reading takes as the verse's increase.
- 47:17/q10/p1 Sincere seekers reach guidance's station and grow by reaching the Guide. — Ibn Ata gives this account, transmitted by Sulami and Bursevi. [2 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); BURSEVI(ابن عطاء قدس سره)]
- 47:17/q10/p2 Reflection and spiritual striving deepen into disclosure, witnessing, and certainty. — Qushayri says striving gains lights of witnessing, reflection on proof gains the spirit of disclosure, and knowledge of certainty is followed by truth of certainty. Biqai describes illuminated hearts as vessels of wisdom. Qushayri adds that the manners of intimate prayer lead to salvation and elevated ranks. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 47:17/q10/p3 Taqwa is turning to God and healing desire by leaving worldly craving. — Al-Tibi calls following desire a spiritual illness, abiding by taqwa divine medicine, and cites 26:80; he also interprets perfected taqwa as turning wholly to God and cites 3:102. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي)]

## 47:17/q11 How does the verse contrast the surrounding account of hypocrites?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the relevant link is the immediate contrast with hypocrites or the move onward to the Hour.
- 47:17/q11/p1 It contrasts receptive believers with hypocrites who hear without benefit and follow desire. — Ibn Atiyya points to 47:16 and says the contrast credits believers' effort. Jishumi says the earlier hearers were hypocrites who did not seek guidance; Qatada contrasts heedless and mindful listeners. Razi says the believers' understanding removes any claim that revelation is obscure: the hypocrites' failure is blindness of heart. Al-Tibi pairs the guided with desire-followers and increased guidance with sealed hearts, though Alusi says this pattern is not decisive. Tabatabai says guidance counters sealed-heart misguidance and taqwa counters desire: increased guidance perfects knowledge and taqwa perfects action, while sealing and desire remove those perfections; he allows the second clause to explain the first. Islahi calls hypocrites the Muslims' hidden enemy whose hypocrisy grows from the same hearing that strengthens believers. Tabatabai situates the passage after diseased hearts and apostasy. [13 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; JISHUMI(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 47:17/q11/p2 The contrast leads onward to a warning that the Hour is near. — Wahidi says the commentary then warns Meccan disbelievers that nothing from them will be accepted when the Hour comes; Elmali says believers' increased guidance and taqwa prepare them to meet God at the appointed hour. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; ELMALILI]

## 47:17/q12 How is God's increase of guidance and gift of taqwa related to human action?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether divine help creates faith or piety directly, gives an effective capacity, or facilitates a person's own response.
- 47:17/q12/p1 Guidance's increase is divine facilitating grace, while people themselves accept, reflect, and act. — Jishumi rejects the Mujabbira's claim that the increase means God creates faith and instead calls it لطف. Ibn Atiyya emphasizes believers' own effort in seeking guidance; Islahi says God nurtures virtue according to retained innate capacity and desire. [3 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 47:17/q12/p2 God creates piety in people, as Ash'aris say of human acts. — Alusi explicitly gives the Ash'ari account and says making them God-fearing fits it; Bursevi also says God creates piety. Jishumi rejects analogous creation of faith as the meaning of increased guidance. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; JISHUMI-]
- 47:17/q12/p3 God creates an effective capacity by His permission through which they practice piety. — Alusi attributes this account to al-Kurani's presentation of al-Ash'ari and other investigators and says making them God-fearing accommodates it. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
