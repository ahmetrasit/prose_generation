# 78:22: 13 questions

## 78:22/q01 Who are the ṭāghīn, and what makes someone one of them?  [لِّلطَّٰغِينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the expression identifies the passage's polytheist disbelievers specifically or describes everyone who exceeds the proper limit; one lexical report emphasizes deviation or error.
- 78:22/q01/p1 The transgressors here are disbelievers, especially the polytheists addressed by the passage. — Muqātil calls them unbelievers; Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil, as reported by al-Wāḥidī, call them misguided polytheists, while Ibn ʿAbbās in Ibn al-Jawzī calls them polytheists. Bursawī cites the following verses. Ibn ʿĀshūr links the definite expression to the polytheists arriving in companies (78:18), and says their shirk, rejection of worship, and arrogant refusal of the Messenger explain their punishment (78:27–28). [10 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 78:22/q01/p2 They are those who exceed God's bounds in rebellion or disobedience, including unbelief and worldly injustice. — Al-Ṭabarī specifies arrogant violation of God's limits in worldly life; al-Māwardī, al-Qurṭubī, and Bursawī distinguish disbelief in religion from oppression in worldly affairs. Al-Jishumī relates ṭāghī to ʿātī and ʿādī as exceeding the limit in disobedience. Ibn Kathīr specifies rebellious sinners opposing the messengers; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds disregard of others' rights and arrogance. He says Muslims who slight God's rights or contemptuously wrong people share the warning in proportion to their resemblance to disbelievers. The Study Quran describes rebellion against the truth. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 78:22/q01/p3 In one Quranic usage, transgression means deviation or error. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this as a Quranic sense, adducing the Companion's denial that he led his associate astray and this verse's destination for the transgressors. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]

## 78:22/q02 Why name the rebellious explicitly instead of using a pronoun?  [لِّلطَّٰغِينَ · rhetoric]
- 78:22/q02/p1 The explicit description brands them with their defining rebellion and explains why they are punished. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the description replaces a pronoun, marks their transgression, and makes the punishment intelligible: shirk is the greatest transgression, rejecting God's worship and the Messenger's call; he cites 78:27–28. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 78:22/q03 What does li-l-ṭāghīn attach to in the construction?  [لِّلطَّٰغِينَ · grammar]
turns on: The prepositional phrase can attach to mirṣād, maʾāban, an omitted modifier, or both; fronting also permits a circumstantial parse. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the pause after mirṣād for his preference.
- 78:22/q03/p1 It attaches directly to mirṣād, making Hell a watch-post for the rebellious. — Al-Samīn, al-Ālūsī, and Elmalılı give this parse. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports it as possible, with the complement of maʾāban inferred, and says it poses no semantic obstacle; his preferred analysis differs. Al-Ālūsī also relates mirṣād to arṣadtu, 'I prepared for him.' [6 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 78:22/q03/p2 It attaches directly to maʾāban, making Hell the rebellious one's place of return. — Al-Samīn notes this works because maʾāban means a place of return; al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı also allow it. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers its attachment to maʾāban and says the short verse cola support pausing after mirṣād, with maʾāban supplying the understood complement of mirṣād. He reports attachment to mirṣād as semantically possible. [6 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 78:22/q03/p3 It modifies an omitted element, understood as a description of mirṣād. — Al-Samīn describes it as an adjective of mirṣād through an omitted element; Bursawī makes it an adjective of an omitted noun with maʾāban in apposition. Al-Ālūsī also gives an omitted description of mirṣād. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 78:22/q03/p4 It is a fronted circumstantial expression from maʾāban, originally its adjective. — Al-Samīn says the fronting turns what would have been an adjective of maʾāban into a circumstantial accusative and requires an omitted attachment. Bursawī calls it a fronted circumstantial predicate; al-Ālūsī also allows the circumstantial analysis. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 78:22/q03/p5 It can depend on both mirṣād and maʾāban, or function as a second predicate. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports shared attachment to both terms without a semantic obstacle. Al-Ālūsī allows the phrase itself as a second predicate as well as attachment to either noun. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 78:22/q04 Why is li-l-ṭāghīn placed before maʾāban?  [لِّلطَّٰغِينَ مَـَٔابًۭا · rhetoric]
- 78:22/q04/p1 Fronting the phrase confronts the polytheists with their frightening destination. — Ibn ʿĀshūr ties this effect to his preferred attachment of li-l-ṭāghīn to maʾāban; he acknowledges other attachments as possible. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 78:22/q05 What does maʾāban mean here?  [مَـَٔابًۭا · meaning]
turns on: The reports gloss the word as the place one returns to, as a dwelling or refuge, or as both; Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the latter through metonymic usage.
- 78:22/q05/p1 A place of return, destination, or final outcome: Hell is where they come back. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī derive it from āba yaʾūbu, 'to return'; al-Naḥḥās cites a poem saying the dead do not return. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī derives it from awb, 'return.' Al-Suddī also calls it an outcome; Ibn Kathīr adds final turning place and resting place. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the original sense is a place of return. Al-Qāshānī cites 19:72 for the wicked's destination. Bursawī stresses its inevitability. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(سفيان); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 78:22/q05/p2 A dwelling, abode, or refuge. — Qatāda supplies 'dwelling and refuge' in al-Ṭabarī and 'refuge or dwelling' in al-Qurṭubī; al-Ḥasan is not named in these lexical reports. Sufyān, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī, and al-Durr's Qatāda report combine return and abode. Ibn ʿĀshūr explains that the original place-of-return sense was transferred by metonymy to a dwelling and became common usage for residence. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(سفيان,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 78:22/q06 What grammatical role does maʾāban have beside mirṣād?  [مَـَٔابًۭا · grammar]
turns on: The noun can further specify mirṣād, act as another predicate, describe Hell's state, or fill the complement implied by mirṣād.
- 78:22/q06/p1 It is an apposition to, substitute for, or specification of mirṣād. — Al-Qurṭubī calls the expression appositive to mirṣād; Bursawī puts maʾāban in apposition on one parse. Al-Ālūsī gives substitution; Ibn ʿĀshūr gives appositional specification because surveillance includes the destination of those watched. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 78:22/q06/p2 It serves as a second predicate of kāna. — Al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr give this syntactic possibility. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 78:22/q06/p3 It is a circumstantial predicate of Hell. — Ibn ʿĀshūr lists this as one of the possible grammatical roles. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 78:22/q06/p4 It describes mirṣād, or supplies its understood complement. — Al-Ālūsī allows maʾāban as a description of mirṣād. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers maʾāban supplying the omitted complement of mirṣād, citing the short verse cola and their pause after mirṣād. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 78:22/q07 Why is Hell called their return or destination?  [مَـَٔابًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: The notes explain the 'return' through the transgressors' deeds and preparation in life, or through al-Biqāʿī's account of creation, guidance, denial, and return.
- 78:22/q07/p1 Their transgression and recorded deeds have made Hell their earned destination. — Al-Ṭabarī says they arrogantly crossed God's limits in worldly life; al-Ṭabrisī says their crimes are as though they had already placed them there. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says they prepared Hell as their dwelling in the world and return to it after leaving it. Iṣlāḥī says every deed is registered and punishment naturally follows what they did. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies shirk, rejection of worship, and refusal of the Messenger as the cause, citing 78:27–28. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 78:22/q07/p2 The return recalls that people were created for an abode, instructed toward it, then brought back by their denial. — Al-Biqāʿī says their primordial nature, revealed books, and messengers prepared them to depart, before denial brought them back to the abode; he calls maʾāb both return and abode. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 78:22/q08 What does this warning imply for sinful believers?  [* · theology]
- 78:22/q08/p1 Sinful believers can share the warning in proportion to their resemblance to disbelievers, but their punishment is finite. — Ibn ʿĀshūr applies the warning proportionally to Muslims who contemptuously violate God's or other people's rights. Bursawī contrasts the transgressors' unending punishment with the finite punishment of a sinful believer. [2 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 78:22/q09 About whom was this passage revealed?  [* · reports]
- 78:22/q09/p1 It was revealed concerning al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra. — Muqātil both states this identification in his explanation of the transgressors and separately reports it as the occasion for the passage. [2 notes: MUQATIL]

## 78:22/q10 What is reported about entering Paradise in relation to the Fire?  [* · reports]
- 78:22/q10/p1 Everyone crosses the Fire before entering Paradise; those with a pass escape and the others are detained. — Ibn Kathīr reports this from al-Ḥasan and Qatāda. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن وقتادة)]

## 78:22/q11 What follows from the reported open hamza in an jahannama?  [* · readings]
- 78:22/q11/p1 The open hamza gives a causal reading: the Hour is established for requital, with Hell a watch-post for transgressors. — Al-Samīn attributes the reading to Ibn Yaʿmur and Abū ʿAmr al-Minqārī and reports al-Zamakhsharī's explanation. Al-Ālūsī names Abū ʿAmr, al-Minqārī, and Ibn Yaʿmar and supplies an implied causal preposition. Al-Ālūsī objects that the corresponding phrase about the pious would then also require an open hamza and coordination, while independent need for each reward does not explain establishing recompense itself. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(ابن يعمر وأبو عمرو المنقري,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو عمر); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 78:22/q12 What reading of fī l-ṣūr is reported in the surrounding passage?  [* · readings]
- 78:22/q12/p1 Abū ʿIyāḍ reads the wāw of al-ṣūr with a fatḥa. — Al-Samīn says a similar reading was mentioned earlier. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو عياض)]

## 78:22/q13 Why does the passage present Hell before the reward of the God-fearing?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Al-Biqāʿī appeals to priority of averting harm; Ibn ʿĀshūr appeals to how the actual sequence and its audience establish later meanings.
- 78:22/q13/p1 It presents the feared destination first because averting harm takes priority over gaining benefit. — Al-Biqāʿī gives this ordering principle. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 78:22/q13/p2 Naming Hell first clarifies that the later mafāzan means Paradise and lets the passage's ordered details convey their meanings. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the order of Hell, mafāzan, the overflowing cup, and the no-speech clause enables meanings unavailable in a different sequence. He says rhetorical explanation must attend to the occasion and audience of revelation, since attention only to nearby words can force strained explanations. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
