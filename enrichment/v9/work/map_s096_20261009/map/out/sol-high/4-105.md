# 4:105: 27 questions

## 4:105/q01 What is the Book sent down to the Prophet?  [ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the Book is read as the Qurʾan in its outward sense or as knowledge of divine manifestations.
- 4:105/q01/p1 The Qurʾan, containing commands, prohibitions, rulings and guidance. — Ibn al-Jawzī identifies al-kitāb as the Qurʾan; al-Baghawī specifies commands, prohibitions and decisive judgment; Ibn Kathīr says its reports and commands are true; Elmalılı describes rights before God and people. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; BAGHAWI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 4:105/q01/p2 In an inward reading, the Book is detailed knowledge of divine attributes, their manifestations, and their rulings. — Al-Qāshānī grounds this knowledge in truth and justice and makes it the basis of judgment. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 4:105/q02 What does sending the Book 'in truth' mean?  [بِٱلْحَقِّ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether truth describes the Book's content and mode of revelation, its just purpose, or the Prophet's entitlement.
- 4:105/q02/p1 The Book is true, contains truth, and is revealed rightly, without falsehood or deviation. — Muqātil contrasts truth with vain revelation and cites 34:6. Al-Wāḥidī cites 22:62 for possessing truth. Al-Jishumī defines truth as putting things where wisdom requires and calls the Book ordered by wisdom. Al-Māturīdī includes protection from alteration and true reports, commands, rights and resurrection. Ibn Kathīr includes reports and commands. [13 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الماتريدي); ELMALILI]
- 4:105/q02/p2 It means revelation for just judgment and the fulfillment of rights. — Ibn al-Jawzī glosses al-ḥaqq as judgment with justice. Al-Ṭabrisī says God's due from servants; al-Māturīdī includes duties and mutual rights; Elmalılı describes fair settlement of doctrinal and practical disputes. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الماتريدي); ELMALILI]
- 4:105/q02/p3 It may mean the Prophet is most entitled to the Book. — Al-Māwardī and al-Ṭabrisī record this gloss. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI]

## 4:105/q03 How does 'in truth' attach grammatically?  [بِٱلْحَقِّ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the preposition qualifies the Book as a state or the act of sending.
- 4:105/q03/p1 It is a circumstantial qualification: the Book was sent down clothed in truth. — Al-Samīn calls it emphatic; al-Ālūsī likewise makes it a state of the Qurʾan. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 4:105/q03/p2 The preposition attaches to 'We sent down'; alternatively the Book descended upon and clarified truth. — Al-Jishumī gives both parsings. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 4:105/q04 Why is the opening expressed emphatically and with 'to you'?  [إِنَّآ أَنزَلْنَآ إِلَيْكَ · rhetoric]
- 4:105/q04/p1 The emphasis marks the ruling's importance, the majestic plural magnifies it, and placing the recipient early draws attention. — Al-Ālūsī gives these rhetorical functions. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 4:105/q04/p2 The preposition ilā accompanies rational recipients in Qurʾanic usage, whereas ʿalā can accompany rational and inanimate recipients. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī draws this usage distinction. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 4:105/q05 For what purpose was the Book sent down here?  [لِتَحْكُمَ بَيْنَ ٱلنَّاسِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause stresses the Book's provision of law or the Prophet's authority to apply it.
- 4:105/q05/p1 It enables the Prophet to judge disputes among all people by divine truth, rights, and rulings. — Al-Naḥḥās calls the lām purposive; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares the general statement in 2:213. Al-Biqāʿī ties 'all people' to the universality of the Prophet's call; al-Ālūsī includes upright and wicked alike; Kur'an Yolu lists facts, norms, methods and general and specific rulings. Abū Ḥayyān says the verse honors the Messenger by entrusting judgment to him under divine direction. [10 notes: NAHHAS; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 4:105/q05/p2 The Prophet's particular judicial decisions themselves have binding authority. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes this verse from 2:213: a concrete case requires determining which general rule applies, and this verse grants authority to the Prophet's judgment. [3 notes: TABATABAI]

## 4:105/q06 What governs the purposive lām in 'so that you judge'?  [أَنزَلْنَآ لِتَحْكُمَ · grammar]
- 4:105/q06/p1 It depends on 'We sent down' and states the purpose of revelation. — Al-Naḥḥās calls it purposive; al-Samīn names anzalnā as governor. [2 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR]

## 4:105/q07 What does 'what God showed you' mean?  [بِمَآ أَرَىٰكَ ٱللَّهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether God's showing denotes revealed instruction, divinely guided reasoning, a particular judicial determination, evidentiary proof, or inward disclosure.
- 4:105/q07/p1 What God taught or disclosed through the Book and revelation. — Ibn Qutayba and al-Wāḥidī compare 34:6; al-Dāmghānī adds 2:128, 71:14 and 21:30. Ibn ʿAbbās and ʿAṭiyya specify the revealed Book. Al-Burūsawī calls this certain recognition 'seeing' for its clarity and force; al-Rāzī likens its certainty to vision. Al-Biqāʿī says the Prophet should act once God clarifies a matter and wait where it remains unclear, rather than follow whim. [23 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TUSTARI; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,عطية); ZAJJAJ; SULAMI(سهل); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); ELMALILI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 4:105/q07/p2 What God inspired through reasoning from revealed principles, including the Prophet's own guided ijtihād. — Al-Māwardī lists the Prophet's ijtihād; Ibn ʿAṭiyya combines explicit revelation and reasoning along its path; Abū Manṣūr and al-Māturīdī say God does not show him error; al-Rāzī says sound analogy remains obedience to scripture. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); NASAFI-FULL(أبو منصور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الماتريدي,قيل); RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); STUDYQURAN(Some scholars); IBNKATHIR-FULL(علماء الأصول)]
- 4:105/q07/p3 The divine determination of the right judgment in a concrete dispute, beyond knowledge of general laws. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God formed the Prophet's judgment and added it to the Book's rules and wisdom. [3 notes: TABATABAI]
- 4:105/q07/p4 Clear proofs and witnesses, or sound outward evidence used in adjudication. — Maṭar names proof and witnesses; Ibn al-Jawzī says the Prophet was taught to reject unproved claims; al-Biqāʿī says the judge need not inspect hidden states. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مطر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BIQAI-FULL]
- 4:105/q07/p5 Direct unveiling or knowledge cast into the Prophet's heart, reaching past ordinary outward appearances. — Al-Sulamī reports unveiling and direct witnessing; Ibn ʿAṭā says the Prophet sees through God; Abū Ḥayyān records illuminated inner perception; al-Qāshānī calls it divine justice shown apart from the self. [4 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,بعضهم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); QASHANI]

## 4:105/q08 What are the lexical and grammatical grounds for 'showed you'?  [أَرَىٰكَ · grammar]
turns on: Which sense and transitivity of raʾā underlie the causative form.
- 4:105/q08/p1 Arāka is a causative cognitive verb with two objects: the suffix and an omitted 'what'; it is not ocular seeing. — Abū ʿAlī says judgment cannot be seen by the eye and causativizing a two-object verb would require three objects. Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī give the omitted relative pronoun. Al-Bayḍāwī rejects a three-object 'made you know' analysis, while al-Ālūsī calls derivation from opinion remote. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); RAZI-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 4:105/q08/p2 The root's senses extend from visual perception to knowledge, consideration, hearing, wonder, and informing. — Ibn al-Jawzī contrasts visual examples (39:60, 63:4, 76:20), reflection (16:79), hearing (6:68), wonder (4:49, 4:60), informing (2:258, 105:1), and related forms for opinion, fullness, report transmission, ostentation and poetic rhyme. Here he selects knowledge/instruction. [10 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 4:105/q08/p3 Deriving arāka from raʾā 'to hold an opinion' is possible, with one object before causativization and two after. — Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī offers this parsing; al-Samīn also permits opinion or knowledge. Al-Ālūsī objects that an opinion verb should take three objects here, with two suppressed. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); RAZI-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 4:105/q09 May the Prophet judge by ijtihād?  [بِمَآ أَرَىٰكَ ٱللَّهُ · law]
turns on: Whether divine showing confines judgment to direct revelation or includes guided reasoning from it.
- 4:105/q09/p1 Yes: his ijtihād applies and extends revealed principles under God's guidance. — Al-Māwardī, Abū Manṣūr, al-Māturīdī and some legal theorists cite this verse. Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows reasoned judgment following revelation. Ibn Kathīr cites the inheritance dispute and Abū Dāwūd's wording that the Prophet judged by opinion where no revelation had arrived. Kur'an Yolu says divine oversight corrects mistakes; al-Rāzī defends text-grounded analogy against its denial. [17 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ربيعة,مالك); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); NASAFI-FULL(أبو منصور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الماتريدي,قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو داود,أم سلمة,علماء الأصول); RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); STUDYQURAN(Some scholars); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 4:105/q09/p2 His judgments are wholly based on revelation or explicit text, not independent opinion. — Al-Wāḥidī reports al-Ḥasan's assertion that prophets' judgments are revelation; Ibn ʿAbbās warns against personal opinion. ʿUmar says only the Prophet can call a judgment what God showed him, ordinary opinions being conjecture. Al-Rāzī records an inference against analogy from 7:158 but answers that authorized analogy itself follows scripture; Kur'an Yolu explicitly rejects using this verse to deny prophetic ijtihād. [15 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن,عمر); KASHSHAF-FULL(عمر); RAZI-FULL(عمر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عمر); ELMALILI(Hazret-i Ömer); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الشافعي); ITQAN(الشافعي); RAZI-FULL-; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]

## 4:105/q10 How should judges decide when the facts are disputed?  [لِتَحْكُمَ بَيْنَ ٱلنَّاسِ · law]
turns on: Whether the case is governed by conclusive knowledge, ordinary proof, or reasoned judgment where text is silent.
- 4:105/q10/p1 They should use proof, witnesses, and what they hear, without accepting an unsupported accusation or favoring a persuasive wrongdoer. — Maṭar names clear proofs and witnesses; Ibn al-Jawzī says no claim against another without proof; al-Biqāʿī says outward evidence is sufficient. Umm Salama's report warns that a skilled claimant may win another's right, a piece of fire. Kur'an Yolu says a proper judge may err through deceptive testimony and the deceivers bear the responsibility. Elmalılı requires investigation and forbids a ruling against certain known facts. [14 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مالك,مطر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(القاضي أبو يعلى); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أم سلمة); BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hz. Peygamber)]
- 4:105/q10/p2 When no explicit text settles the case, qualified reasoning is available, but presumptuous judgment without knowledge is unreliable. — Mālik distinguishes binding Qurʾan and established Sunna, potentially correct qualified reasoning, and unsupported presumption. The Prophet's inheritance dispute ended with division, a lot, and mutual release; Abū Dāwūd says he judged by opinion where revelation had not come. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مالك); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو داود,أم سلمة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 4:105/q11 What does 'traitors' mean lexically?  [لِّلْخَآئِنِينَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether khiyāna is limited here to breach of deposit, encompasses wrongful taking, or signals an inward trust.
- 4:105/q11/p1 Those who fail a trust; betrayal is the opposite of trustworthiness and has a root sense of diminution. — Al-Dāmghānī applies breach of trust to the armor; al-Jishumī gives deficiency or taking away; Ibn al-Jawzī adds takhawwan for shortchanging and Thaʿlab's possible derivation of khuwān. Ibn al-Jawzī catalogs other Qurʾanic uses: disobedience (2:187, 8:27, 40:19), covenant breaking (5:13, 8:58), religious disloyalty (4:107, 8:71, 66:10), adultery (12:52). [9 notes: DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,ثعلب)]
- 4:105/q11/p2 In this context, betrayal includes theft and any wrongful violation of another's rights, including the community's shared trust. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues that the context points to theft and similar violations, since believers form one self; he prefers this to narrowing it to deposits. Al-Ṭabarī prefers the deposit account on the lexical ground that theft is not betrayal in the same proper sense. [2 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+; TABRISI(الطبري)-]
- 4:105/q11/p3 In an inward reading, they betray the primordial trust of knowing God and misuse rights entrusted to their capacities. — Al-Qāshānī extends betrayal of self and others to failure of that trust. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 4:105/q12 Who are 'the traitors' in the verse?  [لِّلْخَآئِنِينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural names the accused household and accomplices, all people acting similarly, or hypocrites as a class.
- 4:105/q12/p1 Ṭuʿma or Abū Ṭuʿma of Banū Ubayriq/Ẓafar and those who defended him. — Muqātil and al-Baghawī name him; al-Wāḥidī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Burūsawī, al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı include his helpers. Abū Ḥayyān explains the plural by three brothers and those who knowingly vouched for the thief; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī's Qatāda report calls Banū Ubayriq the traitors. [12 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(قتادة بن النعمان)]
- 4:105/q12/p2 The warning extends to anyone who commits such treachery or injustice. — Abū Ḥayyān makes the plural general; al-Ṭabrisī includes betrayals against Muslims or protected non-Muslims in person or property; Elmalılı and Asad likewise generalize the admonition. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]
- 4:105/q12/p3 The traitors can be read as hypocrites in the larger passage, without confining the point to one incident. — Ibn Baḥr links it to 4:88; Elmalılı says this can accord with the reported Ṭuʿma incident, since he was a hypocrite. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكرماني); ELMALILI(İbn Bahr)]

## 4:105/q13 What does 'be a disputant for the traitors' forbid?  [وَلَا تَكُن لِّلْخَآئِنِينَ خَصِيمًۭا · law]
turns on: Whether the prohibition concerns human advocacy in a dispute or an inward plea against divine justice.
- 4:105/q13/p1 Do not advocate a traitor's false claim against an innocent or rightful party, even across religious lines. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī gloss it as litigating or defending; al-Kashshāf specifies Banū Ẓafar against the Jew. Al-Ṭabrisī includes protected non-Muslims. Ibn al-Jawzī says advocacy requires knowing the facts. Elmalılı and Kur'an Yolu extend the rule to judges, witnesses and lawyers, against bias from rank, wealth, fame, gain or collusion; legitimate advocacy protects rightful claims, settlement and fair penalties. [25 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج,القاضي أبو يعلى); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABRISI; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 4:105/q13/p2 In the inward sense, do not plead for traitors against divine punishment or say that God wronged them. — Al-Qāshānī says wrongdoers have no defense against the consequences of their acts. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 4:105/q14 What does khaṣīm mean and what is its form?  [خَصِيمًۭا · meaning]
- 4:105/q14/p1 A disputant or advocate who takes a side in litigation. — Al-Wāḥidī derives the sense from an edge or side and cites al-Akhṭal's cloud whose sides answer in thunder; al-Jishumī says khaṣm serves singular/plural and masculine/feminine. Al-Zajjāj and al-Fārisī compare jalīs, though an intensive form is also possible. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says one who defends a claim. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخطل); JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI]

## 4:105/q15 How are the lām and omitted complement in the prohibition construed?  [لِّلْخَآئِنِينَ خَصِيمًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the preposition retains its ordinary benefactive sense or is read by substitution.
- 4:105/q15/p1 The lām means 'on behalf of' the traitors; the omitted complement is the innocent person or pleading the accused's innocence. — Al-Rāghib compares 4:107 and distinguishes 'adversary of God'; al-Samīn says the normal lām suffices without replacing it with ʿan. Abū Ḥayyān supplies pleading innocence; al-Samīn supplies 'the innocent.' [5 notes: BURHAN(الراغب); SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 4:105/q15/p2 The preposition may instead mean 'away from' or 'concerning' them. — Al-Ālūsī records these alternatives without preferring them. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:105/q16 How is 'do not be' connected to the preceding sentence?  [وَلَا تَكُن · grammar]
turns on: Whether the prohibition continues an implied command or needs another syntactic coordination.
- 4:105/q16/p1 It follows an implicit command drawn from the declaration: judge by the Book, and do not advocate for traitors. — Al-Ālūsī prefers an omitted command; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī treats the declarative clause itself as commanding judgment. [2 notes: TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 4:105/q16/p2 It can be coordinated with 'We sent down' by supplying 'We said', or with 'the Book' as another revealed injunction. — Al-Ālūsī records these possibilities and calls the latter remote. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:105/q17 What happened in the theft case behind the revelation?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the underlying charge was theft of armor, betrayal of a deposited item, the Rifāʿa storeroom theft, or a general hypocrite context.
- 4:105/q17/p1 Ṭuʿma stole armor from Qatāda or his household, hid it with the Jew Zayd, and his clan urged the Prophet to blame the Jew. — Ibn ʿAbbās versions give a leaking flour or bran sack leading to Zayd ibn al-Samīn; some say the Prophet nearly ordered the Jew's hand cut off. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī mention Ṭuʿma's oath and Jewish witnesses. Al-Burūsawī says the clan knowingly conspired overnight; Elmalılı prefers this majority account. Names and minor details vary. Elmalılı reports consensus on the Banū Ubayriq occasion and al-Kirmānī's identification of Ṭuʿma as of Banū Ẓafar ibn al-Ḥārith. Elmalılı transmits al-Rāzī's statement that most of the verses concern Ṭuʿma, while case details differ. Ibn Kathīr reports Ibn ʿAbbās's version in which Ṭuʿma hid a stolen shield in an innocent man's house and his clan sought public defense. Ibn Kathīr says Mujāhid, ʿIkrima, Qatāda, al-Suddī and Ibn Zayd relate the thief's occasion with variations. Al-Ḥasan and Ibn Zayd say Ṭuʿma stole armor and relatives argued for him. [38 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-ASBAB(جماعة من المفسرين); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، الضحاك، السدي، الحسن، ابن زيد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عكرمة); TABRISI(عكرمة وابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة وغيره); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI(Râzî,المفسرون); STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI(ابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد، وعكرمة، وقتادة، والسدي، وابن زيد); JISHUMI(الحسن وابن زيد); ELMALILI+]
- 4:105/q17/p2 Armor had been entrusted to Ṭuʿma, who denied the deposit, concealed it in another house, and implicated an innocent party. — Muqātil says Zayd entrusted it and Ṭuʿma hid it with Abū Malik; al-Suddī's reports name Abū Malil/Mulayl. Other versions have an Anṣārī depositor, a Jew as owner or party, and a false charge against that Jew. Al-Ṭabarī prefers this account because betrayal properly describes violation of a deposit. [16 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد ومقاتل); JISHUMI(السدي والضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي); TABRISI(الضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي، مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(رواية أخرى,رواية ثالثة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(الضحاك,عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(السدي); ELMALILI(Râzî); TABRISI(الطبري)+]
- 4:105/q17/p3 Banū Ubayriq broke into Rifāʿa's store and stole food and weapons, then falsely blamed the upright Muslim Labīd and attacked Qatāda's accusation. — Qatāda names three brothers, Bishr, Bashīr and Mubashshir; reports variously name Rifāʿa ibn Zayd and his nephew Qatāda, two armors and swords, or food and a single armor. Bashīr was a hypocritical poet who falsely attributed anti-Companion verse. Usayr ibn ʿUrwa rallied neighbors; the Prophet rebuked Qatāda for accusing a pious household without proof; revelation exposed them and the weapons were returned. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says commentators agree that the Banū Ubayriq case prompted the warning and command to seek forgiveness. Qatāda and other interpreters identify the culprit as Taʿma, also called Tuʿayma. [40 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة,قتادة بن النعمان); JISHUMI(قتادة بن النعمان); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة بن النعمان,قتادة وغير واحد من المتأولين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة بن النعمان); TABRISI(أسير بن عروة,قتادة بن النعمان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة بن النعمان); DURR-FULL(قتادة); SUYUTI-LUBAB(قتادة بن النعمان,محمود بن لبيد); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة بن النعمان); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Tirmizî); TIRMIDHI(قتادة بن النعمان); TABATABAI(قتادة بن النعمان)]
- 4:105/q17/p4 The passage concerns hypocrites generally, continuing the earlier division over them. — Ibn Baḥr links it to 4:88; Elmalılı says the named culprit's hypocrisy can reconcile the accounts. Asad objects to treating the ethical teaching as exclusively historical. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكرماني); ELMALILI(İbn Bahr); ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]

## 4:105/q18 Who was the innocent person accused in the reported case?  [لِّلْخَآئِنِينَ · reports]
turns on: The accused person's identity follows the differing occasion reports.
- 4:105/q18/p1 A Jew, usually named Zayd ibn al-Samīn, with reported variants of his name. — The flour-sack accounts identify him as the recipient of planted armor; al-Ṭabrisī has Zayd ibn al-Suhaym, while al-Māwardī records Yazīd ibn al-Samak. [6 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(عكرمة وابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
- 4:105/q18/p2 Abū Malik or Abū Malil al-Anṣārī, in the entrusted-armor version. — Al-Suddī says Ṭuʿma hid the deposited armor in his house and tried to frame him. [4 notes: MUQATIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(السدي)]
- 4:105/q18/p3 The upright Anṣārī Labīd ibn Sahl, in the Rifāʿa storeroom version. — Banū Ubayriq named him as thief; al-Māwardī lists him among accounts of the person involved. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 4:105/q19 Did the Prophet actually defend the accused traitor before revelation?  [وَلَا تَكُن لِّلْخَآئِنِينَ خَصِيمًۭا · reports]
turns on: Whether the Prophet acted, merely intended to act, or rebuked an accuser on the apparent evidence.
- 4:105/q19/p1 He defended him or publicly argued for him on the apparent evidence. — Al-ʿAwfī from Ibn ʿAbbās says he rose to defend him; al-Ḍaḥḥāk's account says he defended the apparently innocent depositor after relatives pleaded. Al-Māwardī's account says he argued or was about to argue. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(العوفي عن ابن عباس); TABRISI(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 4:105/q19/p2 He intended or inclined to defend him, but revelation intervened before any defense or judgment. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and Qatāda say intention only; al-Ṭabrisī says the reproach addressed intention. Many flour-sack reports say he nearly supported Ṭuʿma or punished the Jew. Al-Rāzī says he waited for revelation; al-Burūsawī says he withheld judgment. Ibn ʿAṭiyya criticizes his quick acceptance of the report. [13 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير، قتادة); TABRISI(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(Some,al-Rāzī); TABATABAI]
- 4:105/q19/p3 In the Qatāda report, he reproached Qatāda for an unsupported accusation before revelation exposed the household. — Qatāda says the Prophet rebuked him on the clan's representation; the later command to seek forgiveness is related to that rebuke in his gloss. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة بن النعمان); TABATABAI(قتادة بن النعمان); TIRMIDHI(قتادة بن النعمان)]

## 4:105/q20 What does the correction imply about prophetic protection from error?  [وَلَا تَكُن لِّلْخَآئِنِينَ خَصِيمًۭا · theology]
turns on: Whether the warning proves a completed wrong act, an excused intention, or a lesson to the community.
- 4:105/q20/p1 The warning does not establish a sinful defense: God protected or corrected the Prophet before a wrong judgment. — Al-Rāzī says a prohibition is no proof the act occurred; possible forgiveness concerns inclination toward an apparent Muslim, an excused intent based on false testimony, or seeking forgiveness for the defenders. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī affirm protection; Abū Ḥayyān says his protection explains why the address instructs others. Kur'an Yolu distinguishes infallible conveyance from fallible fact-finding corrected by God. [15 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)]
- 4:105/q20/p2 Some take his intended defense and the forgiveness command as a possible challenge to protection from major sins. — Study Quran reports this inference from the warnings; al-Rāzī rejects the claim that the prohibition proves a forbidden act. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Some); RAZI-FULL-; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)-]

## 4:105/q21 What became of the accused thief after the revelation?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports give different endings for the fugitive.
- 4:105/q21/p1 He fled to Mecca, apostatized, and died when a wall or house collapsed during burglary. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī and Ibn Zayd transmit this ending; other accounts mention his lodging with Sulāfa and expulsion after Ḥassān's satire. The associated warning is linked to 4:115. Al-Ālūsī reports Ḥassān's satire of Sulāfa and her casting the fugitive's possessions at al-Abṭaḥ. Al-Māwardī records Ṭuʿma's apostasy and flight to Meccan polytheists, connected to 4:115. Al-Ṭabrisī reports the woman carrying his load to al-Abṭaḥ, throwing it down, and complaining in connection with Ḥassān's poem. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(روي); TABRISI(مجاهد، قتادة بن النعمان، عكرمة، ابن جريج); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد,حسان بن ثابت); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL]
- 4:105/q21/p2 After fleeing and theft, he was expelled or killed by people he robbed, variously at Banū Sulaym or after a Qudāʿa caravan robbery. — Al-Suddī places his death at a lava field after al-Ḥajjāj expelled him; ʿIkrima says a stone first struck him and a caravan later stoned him; Ibn ʿAṭiyya lists killing by Arabs. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(السدي,عكرمة); ELMALILI]
- 4:105/q21/p3 He sailed toward Jeddah, stole a purse aboard ship, and was thrown into the sea. — Al-Ālūsī records this alternative. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:105/q22 How is the Banū Ubayriq report graded?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the connected route's rarity or an authenticity grading bears more weight.
- 4:105/q22/p1 It is gharīb as a connected report; other transmissions are mursal. — Al-Tirmidhī says Muḥammad ibn Salamah alone transmits a connected chain while Yūnus ibn Bukayr and others omit Qatāda's father and grandfather. Ibn Kathīr calls an Ibn ʿAbbās story unusual. [3 notes: TIRMIDHI(أبو عيسى); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الترمذي)]
- 4:105/q22/p2 Al-Ḥākim grades Qatāda's report sound by Muslim's criteria, though neither major Ṣaḥīḥ includes it. — Ibn Kathīr and al-Suyūṭī transmit al-Ḥākim's assessment; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes it and says the basic story has many routes with slight variations, though he cautions that occasion reports may apply remembered events to similar verses. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحاكم); SUYUTI-LUBAB(قتادة بن النعمان); TABATABAI(قتادة بن النعمان)]

## 4:105/q23 How does the passage relate to its context and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Which surrounding theme supplies the immediate transition and how the local case illustrates broader scriptural principles.
- 4:105/q23/p1 It returns from discussion of hypocrites and fighting to their attempt to corrupt judgment; even unbelief cannot justify injustice. — Al-Rāzī gives this sequence and the connection to fighting disbelievers; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ṭabrisī similarly describe the return to hypocrites. Al-Biqāʿī ties it to the People of the Book and hypocrites seeking another judgment and to exposing hidden opponents. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 4:105/q23/p2 After many laws, it affirms their divine truth and requires judgment by them rather than pleasing a group. — Al-Rāzī and al-Ṭabrisī give this connection; Abū Ḥayyān emphasizes that even an unbeliever receives revealed justice. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 4:105/q23/p3 It applies the general principle of 2:213 to a particular case and gives the Prophet authoritative judgment. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the whole passage forms a unit requiring judicial justice and lived religion. [3 notes: TABATABAI]
- 4:105/q23/p4 Its command against defense of traitors is reinforced later in the passage by 4:107–113 and 4:109. — Al-Rāghib compares 4:107; Kur'an Yolu cites 4:109; Study Quran connects betrayal of others with betrayal of oneself, and with verses saying people wrong themselves (2:57, 3:117, 7:160). [5 notes: BURHAN(الراغب); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL(العلماء)]

## 4:105/q24 Who is instructed by the warning beyond its immediate addressee?  [وَلَا تَكُن لِّلْخَآئِنِينَ خَصِيمًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the prohibition is confined to the narrated event or governs later people generally.
- 4:105/q24/p1 The Prophet is addressed, and the community and later judges also learn a general rule. — Al-Ṭabrisī and Abū Ḥayyān say the address teaches others because the Prophet is protected. Asad cites the plural address in 4:109; Kur'an Yolu extends the ten-verse passage to Muslims through time. Elmalılı applies it to judges, witnesses, and advocates. [7 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI]
- 4:105/q24/p2 The historical case supplies an occasion but should not be made the exclusive meaning. — Asad calls an exclusively historical reading too narrow for the self-explanatory ethical teaching; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes that occasions may attach remembered stories to resembling verses. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad); TABATABAI]

## 4:105/q25 What broader purposes and methods of revelation are drawn from this verse?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Which wider scriptural functions the cited authors use the verse to illustrate.
- 4:105/q25/p1 Revelation supplies law and moral guidance, and the Sunna explains its principles. — Ibn Kathīr urges Muslims to learn and teach scripture rather than neglect it like earlier People of the Book, and ranks Qurʾan-by-Qurʾan then Sunna interpretation, citing 16:44 and 16:64. Al-Shāfiʿī says the Prophet's rulings were understood from the Qurʾan; a prophetic report calls the Sunna a non-recited revelation. Al-Suyūṭī adds eyewitness Companion reports, reconciliation of transmitted differences, and sound interpretive commitments. [10 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(الشافعي,النبي); ITQAN(أبو طالب الطبري,الحاكم,الشافعي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 4:105/q25/p2 The Qurʾan's purposes also include moral refinement, general and detailed legislation, communal order, sound reasoning, knowledge, exhortation, and narrative instruction. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 68:4 and ʿĀʾisha on the Prophet's Qurʾanic character; a report on perfecting noble character; Abū Khirāsh's poem on Islamic restraint; 5:48, 16:89 and 5:3 for legislation and complete principles; al-Shāṭibī on concise comprehensiveness; 3:103, 6:159, 8:46 and 42:38 on communal order; 2:269, 29:43, 39:9 and 68:1 on reasoning and learning; 12:3, 6:90 and 14:45 on exemplary stories; and 10:38 on the challenge of inimitability. He notes occasions clarify rhetoric and credits al-Ghazālī's survey. Ibn Kathīr links reviving dead earth to softening hardened hearts through faith, citing 57:16–17 as warning and encouragement. [18 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو خراش الهذلي,الشاطبي,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عائشة); IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 4:105/q25/p3 Inwardly, revelation and judgment disclose divine justice and the trust of knowing God. — Al-Qāshānī reads the Book as knowledge of attributes and their manifestations; the Prophet judges by divine justice; betrayers fail the primordial trust. [3 notes: QASHANI]
- 4:105/q25/p4 Al-Khiḍr was a prophet, and Muhammad received all prophets' miracles plus miracles unique to him. — Al-Biqāʿī calls the majority view that al-Khiḍr was a prophet sound and cites Ibn Ḥajar. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الجمهور)+]

## 4:105/q26 What rhetorical or figurative features are noted around this verse?  [* · rhetoric]
- 4:105/q26/p1 The passage uses unspecified referents, ellipsis, word correspondence, and figurative attribution. — Abū Ḥayyān finds the referents of 'what God showed' and 'what you did not know' left unspecified, plus ellipses, repetitions with changed complements, similar forms such as yuḍillūka/y uḍillūna and ʿallamaka/taʿlam, and metaphors for travel and battle. Al-Jurjānī distinguishes rational attribution metaphor from transferred predicate metaphor: poetry assigns life to wealth and weapons and destruction to coins; 35:9 assigns life to rain-revived earth. Reason tests agency, whereas language supplies lexical likeness. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; JURJANI-ASRAR]

## 4:105/q27 Is assimilation of mīm into bāʾ in the verse accepted?  [بِمَآ · readings]
- 4:105/q27/p1 Al-Ḥasan and Abū ʿAmr are reported to assimilate mīm into bāʾ, but grammarians disallow it because mīm is nasalized. — Al-Naḥḥās records both the reading and grammatical objection. [1 notes: NAHHAS(الحسن وأبو عمرو)]
