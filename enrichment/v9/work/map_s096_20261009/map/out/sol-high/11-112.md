# 11:112: 31 questions

## 11:112/q01 What does istiqāma mean as a word?  [فَٱسْتَقِمْ · meaning]
- 11:112/q01/p1 Continuing straight on one course without turning aside. — Al-Wāḥidī contrasts a single direction with turning right or left; Jishumī derives it from q-w-m and compares aqāma; Ibn Qutayba glosses it as continuing; Biqāʿī and Qurṭubī stress constancy. Ṭabāṭabāʾ explains standing as stable capacity to bring about intended effects, with examples of justice, prayer, testimony, law, and religion; iqāma makes that functioning available and istiqāma sustains it. [7 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 11:112/q02 What does the command to stand firm require?  [فَٱسْتَقِمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the command is taken comprehensively or identified with a particular part of religious constancy.
- 11:112/q02/p1 Steady observance of the whole commanded religion, in belief, conduct, worship, and conveying it. — Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Biqāʿī, Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Ṭabāṭabāʾ, and Kuryolu give overlapping comprehensive accounts. Al-Ṭabrisī specifies performing commands and avoiding prohibitions; Rāzī includes creed, practice, and preaching after promise and threat, with belief balanced between anthropomorphism and negation and anger and desire between extremes. Bayḍāwī includes faithful transmission and worship without excess or neglect. Biqāʿī adds endurance of mockery and demands to conceal revelation. Ibn ʿĀshūr says complete law after faith prevents factional alteration and cites the hadith on earlier peoples ruined by excessive questions and disagreement. Ṭabāṭabāʾ invokes 41:6, 41:30, and 30:30 for inner and outward monotheism; he rejects reducing the command merely to moderation, because full servitude is required. Al-Ālūsī prefers this breadth over narrower glosses and likens the difficult balance to the line between sun and shade, citing 17:74. Rāzī uses the similarly imperceptible edge of shadow and light. ʿUmar compares constancy in commands and prohibitions to avoiding a fox's twisting; Sufyān ibn ʿAbd Allāh reports the Prophet's concise instruction, believe in God, then stand firm. Ibn ʿAbbās advises God-consciousness, precedent, and avoidance of innovation. Jishumī makes obligations and avoiding prohibitions necessary for valid repentance; Qushayrī adds avoiding unlawful wealth and approval or companionship of the unjust, forbidding wrong, and valuing each moment of worship. Qushayrī says true obedience continually fulfills its due without neglect or deviation from concord, and worshippers withhold nothing from worship, carrying out easy and difficult obligations alike. He also reports that the steadfast person stays on the path, proceeds in piety, and persistently abandons desire. [35 notes: TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI(ويقال); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(سفيان بن عبد الله الثقفي,عمر بن الخطاب); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+; QUSHAYRI+]
- 11:112/q02/p2 Remain firm upon monotheism without mixing it with disbelief or doubt. — Muqātil frames the command for Muhammad and those who left shirk; Abū Ḥayyān reports his gloss. Bursevī ties steadfast monotheism and its call to the earlier prophets' endurance; Ṭabāṭabāʾ joins monotheism with servitude. [4 notes: MUQATIL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل); BURSEVI; TABATABAI]
- 11:112/q02/p3 Remain firm upon the Qur'an. — Sufyān ibn ʿUyayna gives the gloss in Ṭabarī and Abū Ḥayyān; the Study Quran reports it. Ālūsī reports Ibn ʿAṭiyya's related construal of kāf as upon. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(سفيان بن عيينة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عيينة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية); STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 11:112/q02/p4 Persevere in jihad. — Abū Ḥayyān attributes this gloss to al-Ḍaḥḥāk. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 11:112/q02/p5 Report truthfully about God with firm resolve. — Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī attribute this to Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(جعفر الصادق); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية)]

## 11:112/q03 Why command the already upright Prophet to stand firm?  [فَٱسْتَقِمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether istifʿāl here asks for divine establishment or commands continued practice.
- 11:112/q03/p1 He must continue and preserve the uprightness he already has. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares telling a person already walking or eating to continue; Abū Ḥayyān transmits him, and al-Zamakhsharī's gloss in Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn calls for maintained straightness. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the comparison effectively means remain as you are. Qushayrī reports that the sīn of istiqāmah can mean remaining firm in a command. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; QUSHAYRI]
- 11:112/q03/p2 He is to ask God to establish him firmly in religion. — Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān take the istifʿāl prefix as seeking, like asking forgiveness; Ālūsī reports this as a more remote reading with kāf meaning upon. Qushayrī reports that the sīn of istiqāmah can express asking God for steadfastness on truth. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; QUSHAYRI]

## 11:112/q04 How does this command relate to its surrounding passage and sūra?  [فَٱسْتَقِمْ · links]
turns on: Which part of the preceding discourse explains the command's force.
- 11:112/q04/p1 It draws a practical conclusion from the preceding disputes, promises, threats, and reassurance. — Rāzī and Bayḍāwī connect it to the preceding promise and threat and disputes over unity and prophecy. Ālūsī likewise sees a conclusion after those matters. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it follows consolation and reassurance in 11:109-110; Ṭabāṭabāʾ says preceding accounts of destroyed nations intensify the address, citing 17:74-75. Bursevī sees continuity with earlier prophets and believers who bore harm. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 11:112/q04/p2 It sums up Sūrat Hūd's sustained guidance through the hardships of calling people. — Al-Shihāb says the sūra guides prophetic methods and trials from beginning to end and that this concluding command concentrates its burden of accountability. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الشهاب)]

## 11:112/q05 Who is addressed by the singular command?  [فَٱسْتَقِمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the Prophet is an intended recipient as well as the direct addressee.
- 11:112/q05/p1 It directly addresses the Prophet and also extends to his companions and community. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya takes the companions who left disbelief and the whole community by implication; Qurṭubī includes others, and Abū Ḥayyān reports Ibn ʿAṭiyya. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the direct address honors the first recipient and makes him a model. Ṭabāṭabāʾ ties this honor to 2:285 and 66:8. The Study Quran notes the direct prophetic address and companions' inclusion. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 11:112/q05/p2 The Prophet is addressed in form, but his community is intended. — Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Suddī say this in al-Wāḥidī; Qurṭubī transmits al-Suddī. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي)]

## 11:112/q06 How is kāmā umirta construed?  [كَمَآ أُمِرْتَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether kāf expresses comparison or the sense upon.
- 11:112/q06/p1 It compares the commanded uprightness with the manner prescribed to the Prophet. — Samīn reports kāmā as accusative, either qualifying an omitted verbal noun or circumstantial from its pronoun. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats the comparison as a circumstantial phrase equating uprightness with other commands. Ālūsī reports a comparison likened to the idiom one like you is not miserly, but objects that self-comparison is needless; Abū Ḥayyān supplies the like of what the command seeks. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:112/q06/p2 Kāf means upon, so the phrase says to remain upon what was commanded. — Ālūsī attributes this to Ibn ʿAṭiyya and others, with the Qur'an as its object; he reports a further version combining this kāf with an istifʿāl of seeking establishment. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية)]

## 11:112/q07 What command does umirta recall?  [أُمِرْتَ · referent]
turns on: Whether an earlier Qur'anic command supplies the antecedent or another revelation must be inferred.
- 11:112/q07/p1 It recalls prior Qur'anic commands to uphold religion. — Ṭabāṭabāʾ points to the earlier command to face religion in 30:30 and rejects positing an unmentioned revelation, since Qur'anic instructions already suffice. [3 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 11:112/q07/p2 It implies another command, perhaps revelation outside the recited Qur'an. — Ālūsī suggests this from the wording after the discussion of monotheism and prophecy; Ṭabāṭabāʾ objects that an earlier Qur'anic command already exists. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(صاحب روح المعاني); TABATABAI-]

## 11:112/q08 Who are those who repented with the Prophet?  [وَمَن تَابَ مَعَكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether repentance is specified as leaving shirk, left general for believers, or read as surrendering selfhood.
- 11:112/q08/p1 Believers who turned from shirk or disbelief and joined him, including his companions. — Ibn ʿAbbās specifies the companions in al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī; Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Bursevī, Ālūsī, Elmalılı, and Ṭabāṭabāʾ identify faith as turning from shirk. Qurṭubī includes later followers; Ibn ʿĀshūr says faith itself is repentance from associating partners and treats with you as circumstantial, not attached to repent. Bursevī says they share faith with the Prophet without implying he shared their unbelief; Ālūsī says shared repentance need not have the same object. Biqāʿī says these believers should not lose heart over delayed help. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 11:112/q08/p2 All believers who turn back to God and His Messenger. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports this broader alternative and a distribution in which Muhammad continues conveying while believers continue receiving. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 11:112/q08/p3 In the inward sense, people who leave self-assertion and independent selfhood. — Qāshānī describes multiplicity within unity and abiding after annihilation; Ālūsī calls them monotheists reaching that station. [2 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 11:112/q09 How is wa-man tāba attached grammatically?  [وَمَن تَابَ مَعَكَ · grammar]
turns on: Which preceding pronoun or implied construction governs man, and whether the separator permits conjunction with a hidden subject.
- 11:112/q09/p1 It is nominative, coordinated with the hidden subject of istāqim. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān say the intervening kāmā umirta licenses conjunction without an explicit pronoun; Samīn prefers this and cites 2:35, though he describes an implied clause, you and your followers be upright. Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Bursevī, Ṭabāṭabāʾ, and Ālūsī also give the coordination. Burhān says the separator must precede the conjunction; it rejects using mā ashraknā wa-lā ābāʾunā as a counterexample. Burhān notes that singular address can prevail over the absent persons joined to the Prophet. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); TABATABAI; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 11:112/q09/p2 It coordinates with the subject pronoun in umirta. — Al-Wāḥidī and Rāzī list this analysis; Jishumī calls the tāʾ the nominative passive subject. Ibn ʿĀshūr argues that the intervening prepositional phrase licenses the conjunction. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 11:112/q09/p3 It begins a new or implied clause commanding the repentant to stand firm. — Al-Wāḥidī and Rāzī give an implied command; al-Zamakhsharī in Burhān and Samīn supplies and let those who repent stand firm. Ālūsī reports that some favor the omitted verb since an imperative cannot take an overt subject; he also reports a nominal clause with omitted predicate. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(الكشاف); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء,أبو حيان)]
- 11:112/q09/p4 It is a comitative object: stand firm alongside those who repent. — Abū al-Baqāʾ gives this parse in Samīn and Ālūsī; Samīn finds the resulting sense awkward. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]

## 11:112/q10 Does shared repentance imply the Prophet had disbelieved?  [وَمَن تَابَ مَعَكَ · theology]
- 11:112/q10/p1 No; sharing the act of turning need not mean sharing the same past sin. — Bursevī says prophets are protected from unbelief and deliberate major sins before and after revelation; he can call repentance a leaving of a former state even without actual unbelief. He reports that the Prophet said ʿAlī never disbelieved, and an invitation to reject al-Lāt and al-ʿUzzā does not show otherwise. Ālūsī notes the Prophet's practice of seeking forgiveness more than seventy times a day, while shared repentance may have distinct objects. [6 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 11:112/q11 Must the Prophet and his companions have equal degrees of steadfastness?  [وَمَن تَابَ مَعَكَ · interpretation]
- 11:112/q11/p1 No; conjunction shares the general duty without equal rank. — Ālūsī reports that the Prophet's commanded degree exceeds the companions', with 4:18 cited. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 11:112/q12 What does ṭughyān mean as a word and form?  [تَطْغَوْا۟ · meaning]
turns on: The notes give a shared limit sense and a separate morphological observation.
- 11:112/q12/p1 Crossing or rising beyond a proper limit. — Al-Wāḥidī, Jishumī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, Qurṭubī, and Biqāʿī give the limit sense. Jishumī cites floodwater by analogy and variant root forms; Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites the flood and Pharaoh in 69:11, 20:24, 20:43, and 79:17. Qurṭubī cites 69:11. Biqāʿī connects the root's rising and covering to night, flood, sea, fertile land, and Ghūṭa. Ibn ʿĀshūr says its older sense is arrogance and audacity, here daring to violate commands, citing 2:15 and 20:81. Ṭabāṭabāʾ recalls water passing its banks. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 11:112/q12/p2 The verb's form follows ṭaghawtu ṭaghā, like maḥawtu maḥā. — Al-Akhfash gives this morphological analogy. [1 notes: AKHFASH]

## 11:112/q13 What conduct does do not transgress forbid?  [وَلَا تَطْغَوْا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ṭughyān is read as the inclusive crossing of all limits or a specified kind of excess.
- 11:112/q13/p1 Every departure from God's limits, including excess and neglect, disobedience, oppression, and distortion of commands. — Ṭabarī and Ibn Zayd identify disobedience; al-Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, Qurṭubī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, and Ālūsī state the general limit. Al-Ṭabrisī specifies additions and omissions; Rāzī prefers the inclusive reading that embraces unlawful rulings, excess, and abandonment of humility. Biqāʿī says religion disciplines people rather than meeting a divine need, that modest sound practice suffices, and that barring excess also bars deficiency a fortiori. Bursevī says both extremes are blameworthy and the word highlights common excess. Ālūsī says crossing sacred law is heresy. Ibn Kathīr emphasizes oppression even of polytheists as ruinous; Asad attributes a universal conduct rule toward believers and unbelievers to Ibn Kathīr following Ibn ʿAbbās and reports the equity reading from several commentators. Ṭabāṭabāʾ frames the boundary as human servitude and warns against proud refusal like earlier nations. [26 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Ibn Kathir,Tabari, Zamakhshari, Baghawi, Razi); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 11:112/q13/p2 It specifically forbids arrogance toward anyone and requires humility before God. — Ibn ʿAbbās gives this gloss in al-Wāḥidī and Rāzī; al-Wāḥidī's Wajīz and Asad repeat it. Qurṭubī reports it as an alternative, with oppression also mentioned. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ASAD-NOTES(Ibn 'Abbas)]
- 11:112/q13/p3 It forbids declaring lawful or unlawful beyond what God commanded. — Al-Kalbī says this in al-Wāḥidī; Abū Ḥayyān ascribes it to Ibn ʿAbbās, and Rāzī and Ālūsī include it among the interpretations. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:112/q13/p4 It warns that prosperity and blessings may produce pride or leave the straight path. — Al-Jubbāʾī gives the warning in al-Ṭabrisī; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Biqāʿī, and Rāzī mention this danger. Rāzī's inclusive reading also includes abandoning gratitude. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 11:112/q13/p5 It forbids mixing monotheism with doubt or idolatry. — Abū Ḥayyān attributes this to Muqātil; Ālūsī reports the same gloss. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL]

## 11:112/q14 Why does the prohibition use plural address?  [وَلَا تَطْغَوْا۟ · rhetoric]
- 11:112/q14/p1 It addresses the believers included with the Prophet, while the singular positive command honors him. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the plural recipients; Abū Ḥayyān calls the alternation eloquent. Elmalılı says the positive command grammatically addresses the Prophet but applies to all, whereas the prohibition addresses the community because their transgression is possible and the Prophet's inconceivable; he links this to the Prophet's burden in Hūd. Ālūsī says this account need not conflict with prophetic infallibility. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الشهاب)]

## 11:112/q15 How does the ban on transgression connect with other commands?  [وَلَا تَطْغَوْا۟ · links]
- 11:112/q15/p1 It parallels the command in 42:15 against following unbelievers' desires. — Bursevī says resisting those desires preserves full steadfastness, while following them constitutes transgression. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 11:112/q16 How is the final verb read?  [تَعْمَلُونَ · readings]
turns on: Direct address in the preceding clauses or a shift to third-person reference.
- 11:112/q16/p1 The common reading has second-person tāʾ, matching the direct address. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the majority; Samīn calls it the general reading. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور)]
- 11:112/q16/p2 Al-Ḥasan, al-Aʿmash, and ʿĪsā al-Thaqafī read third-person yaʾ. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya names al-Ḥasan and al-Aʿmash; Abū Ḥayyān adds attribution to ʿĪsā. Samīn says the shift resembles 11:111; Ālūsī also notes the third-person reading. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن، والأعمش); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن والأعمش وعيسى الثقفي); ALUSI-FULL]

## 11:112/q17 What does God being baṣīr of deeds convey?  [إِنَّهُۥ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرٌۭ · interpretation]
- 11:112/q17/p1 He sees and knows every deed, including hidden obedience and disobedience, and recompenses them. — Ṭabarī says nothing escapes God; Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Wāḥidī adds that He knew acts before they occurred. Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Wāḥidī's Wajīz, and Ibn Kathīr stress nothing is hidden. Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ālūsī, and Elmalılı add recompense or punishment; Ālūsī prefers this as the explanation of both command and prohibition. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن، والأعمش); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 11:112/q18 Why close with God's sight of deeds?  [بَصِيرٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 11:112/q18/p1 It warns against concealed disobedience and gives God's knowledge vivid force. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says seeing conveys clear, powerful knowledge even of transgression people hide; Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī connect it causally with the command, prohibition, and recompense. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 11:112/q19 What legal method does the command to stand firm support?  [فَٱسْتَقِمْ كَمَآ أُمِرْتَ · law]
turns on: Whether text-grounded analogy can count as obedience to the command and whether conjunction imposes legal sequence.
- 11:112/q19/p1 Follow the explicit order of divine texts, without specifying general wording by analogy or juristic preference. — Rāzī uses commanded steadfastness to require the stated sequence in ablution and like livestock for zakāt; he objects to qualifying a general text by analogy. Bayḍāwī likewise bars alteration by analogy or juristic preference. Ālūsī reports the inference but distinguishes analogy based on textual causes as authorized ijtihād; he rejects the ablution-order proof because it would impose order on every pair joined by wa, such as prayer and alms, and unfairly condemn Ḥanafīs who do not require that order. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 11:112/q19/p2 Fidelity to texts also permits analogy grounded in textual causes. — Ālūsī reports this qualification as authorized ijtihād and objects to Rāzī's blanket rejection of specifying general texts by analogy, particularly its ablution-order and Ḥanafī implications. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 11:112/q20 What inward steadfastness does the verse describe?  [فَٱسْتَقِمْ · inward]
- 11:112/q20/p1 Fulfill divine and human rights through God while witnessing unity without egoic appropriation or neglect of duties. — Qāshānī says the Prophet returns to creation and upholds commands while witnessing unity, and cites his swollen feet and reply that he should be a grateful servant despite forgiveness in 48:2. Ālūsī similarly names God's and creatures' rights, guidance of creation, and seeing multiplicity in unity and unity in multiplicity without losing reverence. Junayd distinguishes steadfastness with fear and hope for worshipers, awe and hope for the near ones, and no self-regard for gnostics. [4 notes: QASHANI(عليه السلام); ALUSI-FULL(الجنيد)]
- 11:112/q20/p2 Remain on the path, continue the journey in piety, and persistently relinquish desire. — Qushayrī reports this description of the steadfast person. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال)]
- 11:112/q20/p3 Keep each inward faculty from its particular lapse: the soul from slips, the heart from heedlessness, the spirit from attachment, and the secret from observation. — Qushayrī reports these four corresponding forms of restraint. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال)]
- 11:112/q20/p4 Worshippers are steadfast by withholding nothing from worship and fulfilling its difficult and easy obligations. — Qushayrī assigns this form of steadfastness to worshippers. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI+]
- 11:112/q20/p5 Ascetics are steadfast by hoping for no share of worldly life, whether little or much. — Qushayrī assigns this form of steadfastness to ascetics. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI+]
- 11:112/q20/p6 Repentants are steadfast by avoiding the dwelling place of sin and leaving minor and major offenses. — Qushayrī assigns this form of steadfastness to repentants. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI+]

## 11:112/q21 What inward transgression does the verse warn against?  [وَلَا تَطْغَوْا۟ بَصِيرٌۭ · inward]
- 11:112/q21/p1 Assigning unqualified divine perfection to the limited ego veils God's reality. — Qāshānī says God sees whether acts are through Him or through the self. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 11:112/q22 What is reported about the Prophet's burden on receiving this command?  [فَٱسْتَقِمْ · reports]
turns on: The reports describe different effects of the verse, with varying sura names in the graying account.
- 11:112/q22/p1 Ibn ʿAbbās called it the hardest verse for the Prophet, connecting it to his graying. — Jishumī, Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, Rāzī, Qurṭubī, Biqāʿī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Ṭabāṭabāʾ transmit this. The reported sura wording varies: Hūd and its sisters in several, Hūd and al-Wāqiʿa in al-Ṭabrisī and Ṭabāṭabāʾ; al-Ālūsī reports Ibn ʿAbbās also called it hardest. [9 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(ابن عباس)]
- 11:112/q22/p2 After its revelation, the Prophet urged earnestness and was not seen laughing. — Ālūsī transmits al-Ḥasan's report; Ṭabāṭabāʾ says Ibn Abī Ḥātim and Abū al-Shaykh transmit from al-Ḥasan that believers girded themselves and the Prophet ceased laughing. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); TABATABAI(الحسن)]

## 11:112/q23 Which suras did the Prophet say made him gray?  [* · reports]
turns on: The transmitted wording names Hūd alone, Hūd with sister suras, Hūd with al-Wāqiʿa, or those suras together with earlier nations' fates.
- 11:112/q23/p1 Hūd and its sister suras. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Rāzī, Qurṭubī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Ṭabāṭabāʾ, and Kuryolu report this wording or describe it as familiar. Qurṭubī says companions noticed his rapid graying; al-Ālūsī notes other wordings are compatible. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عساكر); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Resûlullah)]
- 11:112/q23/p2 Hūd alone. — Bayḍāwī and Nasafī cite the shortened saying; al-Ālūsī says this wording need not conflict with fuller reports, while al-Kashf questions singling Hūd out when sister suras lack an explicit uprightness command. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:112/q23/p3 Hūd and al-Wāqiʿa. — Al-Ṭabrisī and Ṭabāṭabāʾ transmit this wording with Ibn ʿAbbās; Ṭabāṭabāʾ says al-Wāqiʿa lacks a parallel command and may share the theme of Resurrection instead. [2 notes: TABRISI(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(ابن عباس)]
- 11:112/q23/p4 Hūd, its sister suras, and the fate of earlier nations. — Ālūsī cites an Ibn ʿAsākir transmission through Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad from his father and explains that fuller and shorter versions can coexist. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عساكر)]

## 11:112/q24 What part of Hūd was said to have made him gray, and why?  [فَٱسْتَقِمْ · reports]
turns on: Whether the report points to the command's difficulty, previous nations' fates, the community's risk, or shared eschatological material.
- 11:112/q24/p1 The command to stand firm as ordered, because fulfilling it is especially demanding. — A dream in Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Rāzī, Qurṭubī, and Qāshānī has the Prophet identify this verse rather than the destroyed peoples' stories; Qāshānī says an ʿārif saw the dream, while Qurṭubī names Abū ʿAlī al-Sarrī in al-Sulamī's account. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kuryolu likewise cite the identification; Bayḍāwī links Hūd's effect to the difficulty of uprightness. Al-Shihāb says the verse summarizes Hūd's guidance and raises fear of falling short of its demands without impugning infallibility. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو علي السري); BAYDAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QASHANI(عليه السلام); ALUSI-FULL(الشهاب); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Resûlullah)]
- 11:112/q24/p2 Fear that earlier peoples' punishment might overtake his community. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls this the well-known explanation; al-Ālūsī's expanded report includes what befell earlier nations, while Ṭabāṭabāʾ says the grave address after their destruction heightens the warning. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 11:112/q24/p3 Concern for his community because their steadfastness is vulnerable. — Elmalılı connects the singular positive command and plural prohibitions to risk among the community and says this rhetorical feature aged the Prophet. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 11:112/q24/p4 The Resurrection scenes common to the named suras may explain the effect. — Ṭabāṭabāʾ proposes this because al-Wāqiʿa has no equivalent command to stand firm; al-Kashf as reported by Ālūsī likewise questions singling out uprightness when the sister suras do not mention it. [2 notes: TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 11:112/q25 What other reports illustrate steadfastness and moderation?  [فَٱسْتَقِمْ وَلَا تَطْغَوْا۟ · reports]
turns on: The reports illustrate distinct settings and outcomes rather than competing accounts of one event.
- 11:112/q25/p1 A report from ʿUmar says extreme prayer and fasting do not attain steadfastness if people prefer two over one. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports it under the Prophet's authority through ʿUmar; the note does not explain the final phrase further. [1 notes: TABRISI(رسول الله)]
- 11:112/q25/p2 The Prophet taught that religion is easy and urged moderate practice without overburdening oneself. — Abū Hurayra's report in Baghawī adds rejoicing and seeking help at times of day and night. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة)]
- 11:112/q25/p3 Earlier believers held firm under torture, and the Prophet promised Islam's eventual completion. — Khabbāb's report in Biqāʿī describes saws and iron combs, safety on the road from Ṣanʿāʾ to Ḥaḍramawt, and the Prophet's rebuke of impatience. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(خباب بن الأرت)]

## 11:112/q26 What readings and analyses of lammā in the neighboring verse are reported?  [* · readings]
turns on: The vocalization of in and kull, the doubled mīm, whether lammā is a noun, particle, contraction, or ellipsis, and the limits of Arabic usage.
- 11:112/q26/p1 The transmitted tanwīn reading can treat lammā as a verbal noun or adjective describing comprehensive fulfillment. — Abū ʿAlī compares aklan lammā; al-Ṭabrisī says an adjectival analysis needs kull annexed to an indefinite noun and has no governing element for a circumstantial reading. Ibn Jinnī likens emphatic fulfillment to qiyāman; a proposal derives it from gathering on the pattern faʿlā, like tatrā, with al-Zuhrī's tanwīn supporting it. [4 notes: TABRISI(أبو علي,ابن جني)]
- 11:112/q26/p2 Lammā may contract li-man mā, dropping one of three mīms. — Al-Farrāʾ cites a poem for this. [1 notes: TABRISI(الفراء)]
- 11:112/q26/p3 Lammā may mean illā, an exception particle. — Al-Zajjāj proposes it; al-Farrāʾ and Abū ʿAlī object that this use belongs to oaths. Al-Ṭabrisī reports a related reading wa-in kulla illā la-yuwaffiyannahum, allowing illā as extra with lightened in, and explains a poem's extra illā as saying time confuses its people and frustrates petitioners. [3 notes: TABRISI(الزجاج)]
- 11:112/q26/p4 Its doubled form may be an emphatically doubled light form. — Al-Māzinī proposes this; al-Zajjāj objects that Arabic only weakens a doubled form under poetic necessity, rather than doubling a light form. Al-Kisāʾī says he cannot explain the doubling, and Abū ʿAlī accepts the difficulty. [1 notes: TABRISI(الكسائي); TABRISI(المازني)-]
- 11:112/q26/p5 An omitted phrase may explain it: each will be repaid for what they did. — ʿAlī ibn Abī al-Ṭayyib supplies wa-in kullan lammā ʿamilū la-yuwaffiyannahum rabbuka aʿmālahum and cites poetry illustrating ellipsis. [1 notes: TABRISI(علي ابن أبي الطيب)]
- 11:112/q26/p6 With light in and nominative kull, it can be an adjective of indefinite kull or amount to each certainly being repaid. — Abū ʿAlī says this would ease the analysis, parallel to 43:35; al-Ṭabrisī says a definite annexation blocks it. Al-Ṭabrisī rejects proposals using inserted wa-in hum illā or explanatory kullan, and says a doubled mīm with either form of in yields none of the known senses of lam, ḥīn, or illā. [2 notes: TABRISI(أبو علي); TABRISI-]

## 11:112/q27 What other neighboring word forms and meanings are noted?  [* · meaning]
turns on: The notes concern separate neighboring words and give no dispute beyond dialect variation.
- 11:112/q27/p1 The readings of zulan are dialect variants, and the word is accusative as a time adverb. — Jishumī says the common zulafan has ḍamma on zāy and fatḥa on lām, while Abū Jaʿfar, Ibn Muḥayṣin, and Mujāhid vocalize it differently; he also gives its adverbial case. [2 notes: JISHUMI]
- 11:112/q27/p2 Mirya is doubt despite clear evidence; a share is an allotted portion; disagreement can be contradictory or validly varied. — Al-Ṭabrisī illustrates valid difference by judgments about facing the qibla and includes istiqāma as staying straight and transgression as excess in corruption. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 11:112/q27/p3 Readers vary the kāf vowel in tarkunū, with rounded kāf a dialect form for settling or being content. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this alongside the final verb's readings. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور)]

## 11:112/q28 How does Abū ʿAmr pronounce tāʾ in wa-man tāba maʿak and comparable phrases?  [وَمَن تَابَ مَعَكَ · readings]
turns on: Whether consonants are adjacent in connected speech and how the transmitted routes treat the following thāʾ.
- 11:112/q28/p1 He pronounces the tāʾ clearly in wa-man tāba maʿak. — Ibn al-Jazarī contrasts it with his assimilation of wa-man tāba min baʿdi ẓulmihi in al-Māʾida, arguing that immediate adjacency determines assimilation; he lists ten following letters and illustrates with bil-bayyināt thumma. [2 notes: NASHR]
- 11:112/q28/p2 Assimilation is transmitted in al-zakāt thumma and al-tawrāt thumma. — Ibn Ḥabash transmits this through al-Dūrī and al-Sūsī, and al-Dānī read both routes thus. Ibn Mujāhid prefers clear pronunciation because fatḥa after a quiescent consonant is light; his students transmit that too. [1 notes: NASHR(ابن حبش); NASHR(ابن مجاهد)-]
- 11:112/q28/p3 Clear pronunciation is preferable in those two thumma phrases. — Ibn Mujāhid cites ease after a quiescent consonant; his pupils transmit it. The rival assimilated routes are reported by Ibn Ḥabash and al-Dānī. [1 notes: NASHR(ابن مجاهد)+]
- 11:112/q28/p4 Assimilating the pronoun tāʾ in wa-idhā raʾayta thumma raʾayta is exceptional and rejected. — Ibn Shanabūdh alone transmits it; Ibn al-Jazarī rejects it as contrary to Abū ʿAmr's principles and favors clear pronunciation. [1 notes: NASHR(ابن شنبوذ)]

## 11:112/q29 How are quiescent hamzas like the one in fastāqim eased?  [فَٱسْتَقِمْ · readings]
turns on: Whether connected quiescent word-initial hamza is treated like other medial hamzas and what the transmission records.
- 11:112/q29/p1 They are replaced by the homorganic long vowel and count as medial when joined to the preceding word. — Ibn al-Jazarī gives wāw after ḍamma, yāʾ after kasra, and alif after fatḥa; Abū ʿAmr, Abū Jaʿfar, and Warsh are reported to ease these cases without disagreement. He rejects Maghrebi distinctions that treat a word-initial hamza differently, since a quiescent hamza here occurs only in connected speech. He also rejects Ibn al-Bādhish's attribution of full articulation to Ibn Ghalbūn, his father, and Ibn Sahl, citing al-Tadhkira for agreed replacement. [3 notes: NASHR(أبو عمرو وأبو جعفر وورش)]
- 11:112/q29/p2 Full pronunciation is attributed to some transmitters. — Ibn al-Bādhish attributes it to Ibn Ghalbūn, his father, and Ibn Sahl; Ibn al-Jazarī rejects that attribution against al-Tadhkira. [0 notes: NASHR(ابن الباذش)-]

## 11:112/q30 What happens to the hāʾ after easing hamza in cited forms?  [* · readings]
turns on: Whether the incidental yāʾ after hamza easing affects the preceding hāʾ.
- 11:112/q30/p1 Some give the hāʾ a kasra by analogy with forms followed by yāʾ. — Abū Bakr ibn Mujāhid, Abū al-Ṭayyib ibn Ghalbūn, and his son apply this to anbihim and nabbiʾhum, comparing fīhim and yuʾtīhim. [1 notes: NASHR(أبو بكر بن مجاهد وأبو الطيب ابن غلبون وابنه أبو الحسن)]
- 11:112/q30/p2 Most retain its ḍamma because the resulting yāʾ is incidental. — Ibn Mihrān, Makkī, al-Mahdawī, Ibn Sufyān, and most readers take this line; Ibn Ghalbūn accepts either pronunciation. [1 notes: NASHR(الجمهور)]

## 11:112/q31 What does repentance make possible and require of those with the Prophet?  [* · interpretation]
- 11:112/q31/p1 It opens the straight path and obliges continued upright practice. — Rāzī says a person still in unbelief or sin cannot yet follow the straight path, but can serve God after repentance; Qushayrī says those who repent with the Prophet must remain upright. Qushayrī says repentants remain steadfast by avoiding the dwelling place of sin and leaving both minor and major offenses. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; QUSHAYRI+]
