# 20:49: 23 questions

## 20:49/q01 What encounter is understood before Pharaoh says this?  [قَالَ · interpretation]
- 20:49/q01/p1 Moses and Aaron came to Pharaoh and delivered the message they had been commanded to deliver; the narrative omits this understood step. — Abū Isḥāq in al-Wāḥidī infers their arrival and speech from Pharaoh's reply. Al-Zajjāj in Ibn al-Jawzī supplies 'they went and delivered the message.' Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī say their obedience is understood; al-Ālūsī calls the compression brevity and the question an explanatory resumption. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the omitted steps a standard dialogue style (cf. 2:30). Ṭabāṭabāʾī says their invitation to monotheism and request to release Israel are implied; Bursevī/Kāshifī mentions their call to worship God. [13 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:49/q02 What does the prefixed فَ connect Pharaoh's question to?  [فَمَن · grammar]
- 20:49/q02/p1 It follows the brothers' assertion that they are messengers of their Lord, prompting Pharaoh to ask who sent them. — Al-Ālūsī explicitly grounds this in the prefixed fāʾ; Bursevī's reported explanation likewise treats the question as following their claim to be messengers. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI(غيره)]

## 20:49/q03 What is the function of مَن here?  [مَن · grammar]
- 20:49/q03/p1 It is an interrogative meaning 'who.' [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 20:49/q04 What is Pharaoh asking about their Lord, and with what intent?  [فَمَن رَّبُّكُمَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether Pharaoh seeks the sender's identity or God's essence, and whether his challenge is sincere, evasive, or mocking.
- 20:49/q04/p1 He asks for the identity of the deity who sent and commissioned Moses and Aaron. — Al-Jishumī reports this as 'who sent them'; al-Baghawī and Ibn Kathīr identify the sought deity as their sender. Al-Ālūsī relates it to the brothers' claim to be messengers. Iṣlāḥī says Pharaoh challenges whose messengers they can be. [5 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:49/q04/p2 He asks what sort of being their Lord is, raising the question of divine essence or modality. — Al-Ṭabrisī says Moses answers through God's acts because God has no genus. Al-Rāzī's reported argument distinguishes asking essence from disputing existence, citing Pharaoh's response to Moses' description in 26:23-27. Study Quran says that, if sincere, the question concerns how God is. [3 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN]
- 20:49/q04/p3 He asks in denial, mockery, or confrontation rather than as a sincere inquiry. — Al-Jishumī reports denial of the message. Ibn Kathīr connects the question to Pharaoh's pretense that he knows no other deity. Iṣlāḥī says it is sarcastic because Pharaoh controlled Israel while their God appeared absent, despite their presentation of a living God who commands and warns. Kuranyolu calls the question mocking; Study Quran reports other commentators' confrontational reading of 26:23-24. [6 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(other commentators); STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)-]
- 20:49/q04/p4 The initial 'who' can begin a serious theological inquiry that later moves to 'what' in 26:23. — Study Quran attributes this to al-Rāzī. Al-Rāzī prefers a sequence in which Pharaoh first asks identity and, after God's existence becomes undeniable, turns to the harder question of essence. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī); RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+; STUDYQURAN(other commentators)-]

## 20:49/q05 Why does Pharaoh say 'Lord' rather than 'God'?  [رَّبُّكُمَا · rhetoric]
- 20:49/q05/p1 He exploits the two senses of lordship: he had raised Moses and so claims to be his lord, while Moses speaks of divine Lordship. — Al-Rāzī and Bursevī's Imam cite Pharaoh's boast about raising Moses (26:18); al-Ālūsī calls it a possible taunt at Moses as his dependent. Al-Rāzī compares the equivocation to Nimrod's verbal counterclaim to Abraham about giving life and death (2:258). [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإمام); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 20:49/q06 Why does Pharaoh ask about 'your Lord' rather than acknowledge Him as his own?  [رَّبُّكُمَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the wording is explained by Pharaoh's calculated refusal, the prior message's scope, or pagan conventions for naming divine beings.
- 20:49/q06/p1 He avoids conceding that God is his Lord and turns the claim back against the brothers. — Al-Biqāʿī says he deflects fair consideration to humiliate them; Bursevī links it to arrogance. Al-Ālūsī cites extreme tyranny; Ibn ʿĀshūr says he feared his followers might think he doubted his deity or admitted a lord. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says he feigns not to hear their calling God Pharaoh's Lord. Iṣlāḥī says he implies his own lordship; Ibn ʿĀshūr ties the dual pronoun to their statement in 20:47. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(غيره); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:49/q06/p2 Their prior declaration already included universal or Pharaoh's lordship, so referring only to the brothers' Lord served the immediate exchange. — Al-Ālūsī reports this explanation and cites 26:16. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 20:49/q06/p3 In pagan usage, 'my lord' would name an intermediary deity, whereas the creator was understood as Lord of lords. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says pagans worshipped created intermediaries for nearness and intercession because the creator was too transcendent for direct worship; their idiom did not use 'god' or 'lord' for the supreme creator in the same way. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:49/q07 What did Pharaoh believe or claim about divine lordship?  [رَّبُّكُمَا · theology]
turns on: How to reconcile Pharaoh's god-king claim with his knowledge of a creator and the force of his question.
- 20:49/q07/p1 He knew of the creator but denied Him publicly through arrogance or opposition. — Al-Rāzī reports six arguments: 17:102 and 27:14 say he knew or inwardly recognized the signs; a rational accountable person knows his own dependence; 28:39 is read as denial of return rather than creation; his power stopped at Egypt and Moses escaped to Midian (28:25); his question in 26:23-27 concerns essence, not existence. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the question denies neither the creator nor Pharaoh's having a deity. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); TABATABAI]
- 20:49/q07/p2 He was ignorant of God, possibly a materialist who denied an effective cause. — Al-Rāzī reports this view but says its advocates still concede a rational person could not believe himself creator of the cosmos or of himself; materialism is one proposed form of ignorance. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(من الناس)]
- 20:49/q07/p3 He may have been a philosopher affirming a necessary causal principle. — Al-Rāzī reports this as one proposed form of his belief. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(من الناس)]
- 20:49/q07/p4 He may have worshipped the stars. — Al-Rāzī reports this possibility. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(من الناس)]
- 20:49/q07/p5 He may have belonged to an incarnationist corporealist sect. — Al-Rāzī reports this possibility. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(من الناس)]
- 20:49/q07/p6 His claim to lordship demanded his subjects' obedience and submission. — Al-Rāzī explains his claim as political obedience. Ṭabāṭabāʾī points to Pharaoh's boasts and threats (79:24). Iṣlāḥī describes the Egyptian god-king as a solar avatar with statues and idols worshipped throughout the land. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:49/q08 How does the dual address to Moses and Aaron become a vocative to Moses alone?  [رَّبُّكُمَا يَٰمُوسَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the singular vocative follows Moses' leadership or personal tie, poetic endings, Pharaoh's desire to exploit his speech, ordinary representative address, or an omitted conjunction.
- 20:49/q08/p1 Moses is singled out as the principal messenger and bearer of the message and signs, with Aaron his supporter or minister. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds the distinguished signs; Ibn ʿĀshūr says Moses had palace access and likely spoke while Aaron affirmed him. Al-Qurṭubī says Moses alone carried the speech and sign. Al-Samīn says leading with Moses matters more than a possible Aaron-first alternative. Al-Qushayrī calls this Moses' precedence. [18 notes: QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(ابن عطية); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(ابن عطية); BURSEVI(الإمام); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:49/q08/p2 Aaron's name is omitted for brevity or to preserve the verse endings. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Qushayrī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī mention rhyme; al-Wāḥidī also says Moses' name suffices for both. Al-Samīn concedes rhyme could suit a proposed ellipsis but denies that ellipsis is needed. [6 notes: THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 20:49/q08/p3 Pharaoh targets Moses' speech impediment and avoids Aaron's greater eloquence. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī cite Pharaoh's jibe in 43:52; al-Samīn also cites Aaron's eloquence in 28:34. Al-Burhān says Pharaoh feared Aaron's sharp answer; al-Ālūsī notes the explanation assumes Moses' impediment persisted. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(الزمخشري,صاحب الكشاف); ITQAN(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 20:49/q08/p4 The dual refers to both brothers, while Moses alone is the immediate addressee and can answer for them. — Ibn Qutayba cites comparable shifts of addressee and the mention of Adam as humanity's father. Al-Ṭabarī compares one person's answering for a group to the lost-fish story (18:61, 18:63). Al-Wāḥidī explicitly distinguishes the dual 'your Lord' from the singular address. Al-Ṭabrisī calls it grammatical dominance of the dual. Al-Burhān calls it al-mutalawwin or iltifāt (also 33:1), and al-Itqān surveys singular, dual and plural address shifts (26:16; 65:1; 20:117; 10:87; 9:112). [11 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(الثعلبي); ITQAN(التنوخي وابن الأثير)]
- 20:49/q08/p5 The singular vocative reflects Moses' special relationship to Pharaoh through his upbringing. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, as reported in al-Itqān, connects the direct address to their upbringing relationship; Ibn ʿĀshūr also notes Moses' earlier palace access. [2 notes: ITQAN(ابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:49/q08/p6 An omitted conjunction makes the vocative mean 'O Moses and Aaron.' — Abū al-Baqāʾ proposes ellipsis of the conjunction. Al-Samīn replies that the ellipsis is unnecessary, though it could serve the verse ending; an Aaron-first wording is grammatically possible but Moses' precedence counts more. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR-]

## 20:49/q09 What does the dual pronoun show about Aaron's part in the mission?  [رَّبُّكُمَا · interpretation]
- 20:49/q09/p1 Both brothers took part in delivering the commission, with Moses speaking and Aaron present or supporting him. — Al-Qurṭubī says one speaker can discharge a jointly entrusted task while the other supports him, both meriting reward; commands to both and Pharaoh's dual address show both performed it. Al-Ālūsī says Aaron was apparently present despite Wahb's omission. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Aaron likely affirmed Moses. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls him Moses' minister. Al-Sāmarrāʾī cites their joint sending, fear, reassurance, delivery and address (20:29, 20:42, 20:63, 20:90, 20:92). [7 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 20:49/q10 How does Moses answer this question, and why?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:49/q10/p1 He answers with a concise proof from God's acts of creating, forming, and guiding creatures, rather than defining a divine genus. — Al-Ṭabrisī says God has no genus and is known through acts. Kuranyolu says every creature owes its nature, form, guidance and existence to God. Al-Rāzī says this question calls for reasoning, whereas the later question about past generations calls for unseen transmitted knowledge that Moses lacks. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Moses answers because Pharaoh named him. [4 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 20:49/q11 How does this question relate to Pharaoh's question in 26:23?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the two formulations mark successive stages of inquiry or both serve Pharaoh's confrontation.
- 20:49/q11/p1 The 'who' question comes first; after Moses establishes God's existence, Pharaoh turns to the difficult 'what' question about essence. — Al-Rāzī says the who-question naturally precedes the what-question; his preferred reconstruction begins with Pharaoh's lordship claim, then an undeniable proof, then the essence question that people cannot answer. Study Quran attributes this deeper theological sequence to him. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī); RAZI-FULL+]
- 20:49/q11/p2 Pharaoh's questions in both passages are confrontational challenges. — Study Quran reports other commentators rejecting the serious theological reconstruction and referring to 26:23-24. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(other commentators); STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)-]

## 20:49/q12 What does the exchange teach about establishing knowledge of God?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether a messenger or scripture can be the first ground for knowing God before their own authority is established.
- 20:49/q12/p1 Reasoned proof of the Creator precedes proof of a messenger; blind imitation cannot establish knowledge of God. — Al-Rāzī says Moses establishes the Creator before his own prophethood. He rejects the Taʿlīmiyya appeal to a messenger's statement and the Ḥashwiyya appeal to scripture and Sunnah for the first knowledge of God. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 20:49/q12/p2 Knowledge of God first comes from a messenger's statement. — Al-Rāzī reports this Taʿlīmiyya claim only to reject it from the order of Moses' argument. [0 notes: RAZI-FULL-]
- 20:49/q12/p3 Knowledge of God and religion first comes from scripture and Sunnah. — Al-Rāzī reports this Ḥashwiyya claim only to reject it from the order of Moses' argument. [0 notes: RAZI-FULL-]

## 20:49/q13 How should a believer handle a hostile question such as Pharaoh's?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:49/q13/p1 Hear the opponent, answer with proof and good admonition, and avoid force or alienation. — Al-Rāzī says Moses debated despite Pharaoh's power and Pharaoh first avoided violence lest he appear ignorant; even Pharaoh demanded proof. He says a question must be answered so doubt does not remain, cites 16:125 for wise invitation and 9:6 for protection while a polytheist hears God's word. Al-Biqāʿī likewise says Pharaoh chose debate to avoid being called foolish. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 20:49/q14 May the words of a false opponent be quoted?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:49/q14/p1 Yes; the Qur'an quotes Pharaoh and deniers of prophethood and resurrection. — Al-Rāzī draws the permission from this verse and comparable Qur'anic quotations. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 20:49/q15 What do accounts say happened during the brothers' audience with Pharaoh?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in added events, especially Wahb's forty-day delay, which al-Ālūsī compares with 20:59.
- 20:49/q15/p1 They waited for admission, declared their mission, showed the staff and white hand, and Pharaoh's chiefs urged a contest with magicians. — Al-Durr's account says Pharaoh fled a huge open-mouthed serpent, begged Moses to stop it, and the white hand's leprous appearance vanished in the sleeve. It says the chiefs called the brothers magicians seeking to displace Egyptians and take their kingdom and ease, rejected Moses' request, and sought numerous magicians (26:54). [6 notes: DURR-FULL]
- 20:49/q15/p2 They waited at Pharaoh's gate, then entered after a severe screen was raised. — Al-Ālūsī attributes this admission account to Ibn ʿAbbās; it adds the screen to the more general waiting in al-Durr. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 20:49/q15/p3 Moses entered fearlessly through trained lions; his serpent killed many, and Pharaoh requested forty days before lions escorted Moses out. — Al-Ālūsī reports this from Wahb. He objects that a forty-day reprieve conflicts with Moses' later festival-day appointment (20:59), and says there was apparently only one encounter; he also notes Wahb's focus on Moses could omit Aaron. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(وهب بن منبه); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:49/q15/p4 Asiya urged Pharaoh to accept the brothers' call, while Haman opposed losing mastery and lordship. — Al-Ālūsī transmits this as a reported account. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 20:49/q15/p5 Accounts beyond revelation should be used only when their soundness is established. — Al-Ālūsī gives this caution about the encounter stories. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:49/q16 How does this exchange fit the sūra's account of Moses?  [* · links]
- 20:49/q16/p1 It continues the account of the brothers' joint mission, signs, the magicians, Israel's escape, and Pharaoh's drowning. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says this stretch had not appeared in earlier sūras and the speech is God's instruction, as Pharaoh's reply confirms. Ṭabāṭabāʾī outlines the mission, magicians' defeat and conversion, escape and drowning. Al-Sāmarrāʾī stresses Aaron's partnership across 20:29, 20:42, 20:63, 20:90 and 20:92. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 20:49/q17 What lesson does the passage offer those calling to divine unity?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:49/q17/p1 They should keep trusting God in every circumstance, as the episodes of Moses' life teach, especially to the Prophet. — Kuranyolu makes this the passage's lesson. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 20:49/q18 What inward state does Pharaoh's question reveal?  [* · inward]
- 20:49/q18/p1 It reveals the self veiled from its Lord. — Al-Qāshānī gives this inward reading. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:49/q19 May the sūras be read in an order different from the familiar codex order?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the sūra sequence binds recitation as strictly as the order of verses and letters.
- 20:49/q19/p1 Yes; reading or beginning with any sūra is permissible. — Ibn Kathīr takes the unordered reading report as predating ʿUthmān's provincial codices and prescribed order. ʿĀʾisha says beginning with any sūra does no harm. A sound report from Ḥudhayfa and Ibn Masʿūd says the Prophet recited al-Baqara, al-Nisāʾ, then Āl ʿImrān at night. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عائشة)]
- 20:49/q19/p2 No; changing sūra order corrupts an established arrangement like changing verses or letters. — Ibn Kathīr reports Ibn al-Anbārī's argument. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو بكر بن الأنباري)]

## 20:49/q20 Who fixed the order of sūras and verses in the codex?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the sūra sequence follows revelation, a fixed inherited order, or ʿUthmān's judgment; verse order is treated separately.
- 20:49/q20/p1 Verse order within each sūra is fixed by the Prophet, while sūra order could vary and the Uthmanic order partly reflects ʿUthmān's judgment. — Ibn Kathīr says ʿĀʾisha dictated verse order instead of allowing rearrangement. He cites Ibn ʿAbbās' question about the missing basmala before Barāʾa and al-Anfāl's classification among long sūras as evidence of ʿUthmān's judgment, calling its chain good and strong. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 20:49/q20/p2 ʿAlī intended to arrange the Qur'an by revelation order. — Ibn Kathīr reports ʿAlī's resolve; this is a proposed arrangement, not an account of the familiar codex's final form. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(علي)]

## 20:49/q21 Which first revelation mentioning Paradise and Hell did ʿĀʾisha mean?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether ʿĀʾisha named one first sūra or a broader early group.
- 20:49/q21/p1 She meant Sūrat Iqraʾ, possibly. — Ibn Kathīr treats this as a possible referent of ʿĀʾisha's remark. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عائشة)]
- 20:49/q21/p2 She may instead have meant the category of Mufaṣṣal sūras containing promise and warning. — Ibn Kathīr offers this if her remark did not mean Sūrat Iqraʾ. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 20:49/q22 Why did commands and prohibitions come after the first calls to faith?  [* · sciences]
- 20:49/q22/p1 People first embraced faith and then received law gradually through divine wisdom and mercy. — Ibn Kathīr gives this explanation of the revelation sequence. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 20:49/q23 What implied question does قَالَ answer here?  [قَالَ · rhetoric]
- 20:49/q23/p1 It answers an implied question asking what Pharaoh said in reply; a question is best answered with a verb. — Burhān explains Qur'anic 'he said' clauses as answers to omitted questions, citing Pharaoh's question about God and comparing other passages such as 11:69, 15:57, and 36:13. [1 notes: BURHAN]
