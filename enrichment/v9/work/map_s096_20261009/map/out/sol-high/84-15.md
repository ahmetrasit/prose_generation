# 84:15: 14 questions

## 84:15/q01 What does balā affirm against the preceding denial of return?  [بَلَىٰٓ · interpretation]
- 84:15/q01/p1 The denier certainly will return to God and be resurrected for judgment and recompense. — Al-Farrāʾ says balā begins the affirmative answer to the thought in 84:14; al-Naḥḥās says the person sinned boldly because he thought he would not be raised. Al-Jishumī reports a contrast between the human's belief and God's decisive reply, a return to the afterlife, and a return to the Lord who has always seen him. Al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Qāshānī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, and Study Quran likewise explain the rebuttal as actual return; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it a threat, and Ibn Kathīr says God recreates him as He first created him and repays good and evil. Al-Biqāʿī adds that he returns humbled, diminished, and ruined; Elmalılı describes death and resurrection as reversal and links the return to 84:6. Al-Wāḥidī attributes the return and resurrection explanation to Muqātil and al-Farrāʾ. Ibn ʿĀshūr says God's message through the Messenger establishes the resurrection denied by the person. [20 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل والفراء); JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs)]

## 84:15/q02 How does balā function grammatically here?  [بَلَىٰٓ · grammar]
- 84:15/q02/p1 It answers the preceding negation as an independent affirmative response; the following clause begins a new sentence. — Al-Samīn says it answers the negation expressed by lan. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it cancels a negative assertion, may follow a declarative denial as well as a negative question, cites 7:172 and 64:7, and says answer particles begin new sentences. Al-Qurṭubī reports a parsing in which balā affirms the return and the statement of God's sight starts separately. Al-Farrāʾ places the affirmative answer before that statement. [5 notes: FARRA; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 84:15/q03 What is the force of inna in this reply?  [إِنَّ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes give a rhetorical account of emphasis and a grammatical account of an implied oath; neither disputes the other.
- 84:15/q03/p1 It reinforces the rebuttal of the denier's disbelief in resurrection. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the resurrection was taught by the Messenger and contrasts God's knowledge of the person's state with the person's ignorance, citing 2:216. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 84:15/q03/p2 It introduces the answer to an implicit oath. — Al-Samīn identifies an unspoken oath whose answer begins with inna. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 84:15/q04 Why is God called his Lord here?  [رَبَّهُۥ · interpretation]
- 84:15/q04/p1 He created and governs the person, knows his deeds, and will hold him accountable. — Muqātil identifies the Lord as the person's creator and witness to his deeds. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reasons from divine lordship and governance to knowledge of acts and their assigned recompense, alongside the human obligation to return. Al-Biqāʿī invokes God's wisdom, justice, and sovereignty; Iṣlāḥī adds divine power and mercy. [5 notes: MUQATIL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 84:15/q05 Who is meant by the pronoun in bihi?  [بِهِۦ · referent]
- 84:15/q05/p1 The person who thought he would never return. — Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies that God knows this person's circumstances, not merely his essence. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 84:15/q06 Why is bihi placed before basiran?  [بِهِۦ بَصِيرًۭا · rhetoric]
- 84:15/q06/p1 Its early placement stresses God's sure knowledge of him and suits the verse endings. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives both emphasis and the rhyme of the verse endings as reasons. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 84:15/q07 What grammatical work does the preposition in bihi do?  [بِهِۦ بَصِيرًۭا · grammar]
- 84:15/q07/p1 It connects basiran to its object and conveys attachment or contact. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the intransitive verb baṣira can mean seeing someone clearly and takes the preposition bāʾ for this connection. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 84:15/q08 What does God's being basiran of him encompass?  [بَصِيرًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: The reported explanations specify different objects and temporal ranges for God's sight: deeds and circumstances, lifelong observation, prior knowledge of return, decreed fate, or the time of resurrection.
- 84:15/q08/p1 Complete awareness of his deeds, states, and moral worth for recompense. — Muqātil calls God witness to the person's deeds; al-Naḥḥās says He knows the deeds and destination. Al-Jishumī reports knowledge of the person's states and appropriate recompense and himself says God knows and repays deeds. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says nothing is hidden from God's sight. Al-Qāshānī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Kathīr, al-Biqāʿī, Bursevī, Elmalılı, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, and Iṣlāḥī relate divine observation to repayment. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls sight a metaphor for complete knowledge, including differing moral worth and the person's circumstances rather than merely his essence. [17 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; JISHUMI(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 84:15/q08/p2 He watched the person continuously from creation until resurrection. — Al-Wāḥidī attributes this temporal span to al-Kalbī. Al-Baghawī says God watched him from creation onward. Al-Qurṭubī reports it as another parsing of the clause. Study Quran also reports observation of all actions over this span. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs)]
- 84:15/q08/p3 He knew the person even before creating him and knew he would return to Him. — Al-Zajjāj states both prior knowledge and the eventual return. Al-Wāḥidī reports Abū Isḥāq's same explanation. Al-Qurṭubī gives it as his own explanation. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 84:15/q08/p4 He knows the person's decreed fate, including wretchedness or felicity. — Al-Wāḥidī transmits ʿAṭāʾ's specification of the Preserved Record and its decree of wretchedness and disgrace. Al-Qurṭubī reports a broader alternative of wretchedness or felicity. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 84:15/q08/p5 He knows when He will resurrect the person. — Al-Wāḥidī reports this explanation from Muqātil. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)]

## 84:15/q09 How does God's sight support return and recompense?  [* · theology]
turns on: The notes give both a moral argument from just governance and an argument from God's superior knowledge and revealed statement; they do not expressly set them against one another.
- 84:15/q09/p1 God's knowledge and just governance require resurrection, accounting, and repayment for deeds. — Al-Jishumī says an afterlife is required by the obligations imposed on humans. Abū Ḥayyān, al-Qāshānī, and Elmalılı connect observation of deeds with return and requital. Ibn Kathīr specifies good and evil deeds. Al-Biqāʿī says leaving the person unaccountable would contradict wisdom, justice, and sovereignty. Bursevī says divine wisdom requires accounting and warns all accountable people against sin. Ibn ʿĀshūr says differing moral worth requires reward and punishment in eternal life. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds God's lordship and charge to humans; Iṣlāḥī cites divine power, wisdom, justice, and mercy and says this argument for Judgment occurs elsewhere. [13 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 84:15/q09/p2 God knows the denier's state and has informed him of resurrection through the Messenger. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the clause explains balā because the Lord had already informed the denier of resurrection; inna strengthens that rebuttal and 2:216 illustrates God's superior knowledge. Al-Jishumī reports the contrast between human disbelief and God's decisive assertion. [3 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 84:15/q10 How does this reply connect to the surrounding passages?  [* · links]
- 84:15/q10/p1 It completes this sura's account of humanity's return and its divergent outcomes. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says 84:7–15 detail the return announced in 84:6: right-hand recipients are believers and recipients of books behind their backs are polytheists. Elmalılı connects death and resurrection here to 84:6; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also cites 84:6 in arguing for return. [3 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 84:15/q10/p2 The sura continues al-Muṭaffifīn's closing contrast of rewarded allies and punished enemies. — Al-Biqāʿī says the enemies denied resurrection and judgment before their Creator-King and cites 83:1 in the connection. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 84:15/q10/p3 The denied return echoes those who claim that worldly life alone exists and time destroys them. — Al-Biqāʿī cites 45:24 while discussing the denial of ḥawr. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 84:15/q11 What senses of ḥawr in the preceding denial bear on this reply?  [* · links]
turns on: The Arabic usage cited for ḥawr supports return, diminution, and destruction; al-Qurṭubī and al-Biqāʿī allow more than one sense.
- 84:15/q11/p1 Return, including returning to one's Lord or returning something. — Al-Qurṭubī says aḥāra means to return something and gives the example of a mill that yields no flour, saying it has returned nothing. Al-Biqāʿī includes return to the Lord among the possible senses. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 84:15/q11/p2 Diminution, decline, or one loss after another. — Al-Qurṭubī gives the phrase 'ḥawr in a maḥārah' for successive loss and cites a poet's line in which a group's increase turns to decline while blame remains. Al-Biqāʿī includes diminution and describes the returning denier as humbled and diminished. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الشاعر); BIQAI-FULL]
- 84:15/q11/p3 Perishing or destruction. — Al-Qurṭubī says ḥawr can mean destruction; he reports Abū ʿUbayd's explanation of a rajaz line about a 'well with no ḥawr' as a well of destruction, taking lā as extra. Al-Biqāʿī includes perishing and says the denier returns ruined. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); BIQAI-FULL]

## 84:15/q12 What wording of the Prophet's supplication is cited beside this reply?  [* · reports]
turns on: The transmitted wording differs between al-kawr and al-kawn, producing the respective images of decline after increase and dispersion after completion.
- 84:15/q12/p1 He sought refuge from al-ḥawr after al-kawr: decline after increase. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this wording and gloss, alongside a distinct transmission with al-kawn. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي)]
- 84:15/q12/p2 He sought refuge from al-ḥawr after al-kawn: dispersion after completion. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this transmission and gloss; his report from Maʿmar and ʿAbd al-Razzāq further describes affairs spreading out after completion. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي,معمر وعبد الرزاق)]

## 84:15/q13 How is al-kunti explained in discussion of that supplication?  [* · meaning]
turns on: Maʿmar uses al-kunti for moral deterioration, whereas Abū ʿAmr and Ibn al-Aʿrābī describe nostalgic boasts about the past.
- 84:15/q13/p1 A formerly good person who becomes bad. — Al-Qurṭubī reports Maʿmar's explanation of the expression al-kunti. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(معمر وعبد الرزاق)]
- 84:15/q13/p2 An older man who boasts of what he used to be or do. — Al-Qurṭubī reports Abū ʿAmr's example of an old man recalling his youth and a poet's 'I became a kunti.' Ibn al-Aʿrābī says a kunti boasts of former youth or bravery, while a kāni recalls former wealth, horses, giving, or riding. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو,ابن الأعرابي)]

## 84:15/q14 Which readings of yaṣlawhā are reported in this passage?  [* · readings]
- 84:15/q14/p1 Yaṣlawhā and yuṣlāhā are both valid readings. — Al-Farrāʾ allows both forms with broad latitude. [1 notes: FARRA]
