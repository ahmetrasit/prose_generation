# 96:12: 11 questions

## 96:12/q01 Who is envisaged as commanding piety in this alternative?  [أَمَرَ بِٱلتَّقْوَىٰٓ · referent]
turns on: Whether the implied subject of 'was guided' and 'commanded' is the praying servant or the forbider; if the forbider, whether his supposed piety is his mistaken claim or a better course he failed to take.
- 96:12/q01/p1 The praying servant, Muhammad, is guided and commands piety; the description can also embrace any worshipper who calls others to right conduct. — Muqātil identifies Muhammad and understands his command as sincerity; al-Ṭabarī and Qatāda identify his call as warning Abū Jahl of God and punishment. Al-Māwardī adds obedience to his Lord. Al-Farrāʾ contrasts the servant's guidance and command with the prohibiter's denial; Ibn ʿĀshūr says context restores the servant as the hidden subject despite nearby pronouns referring to the prohibiter, citing a verse of ʿAbbās ibn Mirdās for contextual shifts of pronouns. Jishumī allows either the Prophet specifically or every worshipper who calls to right. Ibn Kathīr distinguishes the servant's guided conduct from his spoken exhortation. Qatāda's report identifies Muhammad as guided and Abū Jahl as the denier. The question makes obstructing such a servant especially outrageous, according to Quṭb, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Iṣlāḥī. [43 notes: MUQATIL; TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الفراء); IBNKATHIR; DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI(الفراء); ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 96:12/q01/p2 The prohibiter is imagined as calling to what he himself considers piety, namely idol worship, although he actually denies the truth. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Nasafī and the majority as reported by al-Ālūsī make this an ironic concession to the prohibiter's claimed righteousness. Al-Qāshānī calls it the arrogant denier's own misguided claim and invitation to associationism. Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī's contrast between this claim and the Muslim judgment that he turns from true religion. Al-Ālūsī says the next condition exposes his actual denial. [13 notes: KASHSHAF; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ALUSI(الجمهور)]
- 96:12/q01/p3 The prohibiter is imagined instead choosing guidance and commanding piety; this better course exposes the good he forfeits by opposing worship. — Al-Rāzī says a rational, prosperous man should have chosen faith and reform instead of disbelief; al-Ālūsī reports the same reading from the Imām and its reproach for forfeited higher ranks. Elmalılı says the address then stays with the worshipper. ʿIṣām al-Dīn treats the question as rebuke for the benefit the man missed; al-Ālūsī distances himself from his further elaboration. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports al-Rāzī's generic application to people who transgress despite creation and blessings. [12 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI(الإمام,عصام الدين); ELMALILI; BINTSHATI(الفخر الرازي)]

## 96:12/q02 To whom is the repeated challenge addressed?  [* · referent]
turns on: Who receives the second question, and which person the hidden subjects of 'was' and 'commanded' denote.
- 96:12/q02/p1 It addresses the prohibiter, asking whether he would still oppose a servant whose prayer guides and whose call commands piety. — Al-Rāzī and al-Bayḍāwī compare God to a judge or master who turns between opposing parties. Al-Qurṭubī says Abū Jahl is directly confronted with the ruin entailed by opposing Muhammad. Abū Ḥayyān reports this as another view; al-Ālūsī's split-address version then returns to the Prophet for the question about God's sight. Elmalılı says this address follows if the hidden subjects are the servant. [12 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ALUSI(بعضهم); ELMALILI]
- 96:12/q02/p2 It continues to address the Prophet, asking him to consider the prohibiter's claimed piety or the better course he could have chosen. — Al-Rāzī says keeping the first and third challenges addressed to the Prophet preserves rhetorical order. Elmalılı says this follows if the hidden subjects are the prohibiter. Al-Ālūsī reports the Imām's reading of the whole address as directed to the Prophet. [4 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI(الإمام); ELMALILI]
- 96:12/q02/p3 The address may be to the Prophet alone or generally to him and his community. — Al-Māwardī presents both scopes of address while keeping the inquiry about the Prophet's guidance. [2 notes: MAWARDI]

## 96:12/q03 What does taqwā mean here?  [بِٱلتَّقْوَىٰٓ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term is glossed as general God-fearing conduct, specifically sincere monotheism, a claimed idol-worshipping piety, or its inward and outward effects.
- 96:12/q03/p1 Reverence or consciousness of God that entails fearing Him, avoiding disobedience and acting responsibly. — ʿAṭāʾ explicitly joins fear of God to abstention from disobedience. Al-Ṭabarī specifies fear of God and His punishment; al-Wāḥidī calls it reverence. Asad renders it God-consciousness throughout, calls this its earliest occurrence in revelation, and refers to 2:2. Kur'an Yolu adds responsible conduct. [6 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 96:12/q03/p2 Sincerity and God's oneness, expressed by avoiding association of partners with Him. — Muqātil supplies sincerity; al-Wāḥidī reports his fuller gloss as sincerity and monotheism, and al-Baghawī likewise pairs sincerity with God's oneness. [4 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 96:12/q03/p3 In the ironic reading, it is the forbider's supposed piety: worship of idols. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī qualify the description as what the forbider himself believes; al-Ālūsī's majority reading contrasts that supposition with his actual denial. [5 notes: KASHSHAF; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI(الجمهور)]
- 96:12/q03/p4 It is inward illumination through guidance whose outward effects protect the worshipper from divine anger and destruction. — Al-Biqāʿī calls taqwā the religion's pillar and connects inner light, outward devotion and safety from the sovereign Lord's displeasure. [3 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 96:12/q04 Why is commanding piety mentioned beside guidance and prayer?  [أَمَرَ بِٱلتَّقْوَىٰٓ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the command is paired with prayer to show self-reform and reform of others, prayer's exemplary force, the two provocations to Abū Jahl, or the full scope of the prohibition and rebuke.
- 96:12/q04/p1 Guidance or prayer cultivates the person himself, while commanding piety reforms and guides others. — Al-Rāzī says the Prophet alternated between self-reform in prayer and reform of others by command. Al-Farrāʾ, as reported by Abū Ḥayyān, makes the same distinction; al-Biqāʿī describes an advance from inner illumination to outward help. Al-Bayḍāwī describes worship and invitation as the two ways human states are perfected. [10 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; ABUHAYYAN(الفراء); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 96:12/q04/p2 Prayer itself is an active call to guidance and piety, stronger than verbal exhortation. — Al-Rāzī says witnessing the Prophet pray softened hearts and moved people toward faith. Al-Bayḍāwī says mentioning prayer suffices for both practice and invitation. Al-Ālūsī says an exemplar's action effectively says 'Do this,' and Ibn ʿĀshūr says obstructing prayer thus obstructs guidance. [9 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 96:12/q04/p3 Prayer and calling people to God are named because both aspects of Muhammad's conduct troubled Abū Jahl. — Al-Rāzī identifies these as the two activities that provoked Abū Jahl; Abū Ḥayyān attributes the same pairing to al-Farrāʾ. [6 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الفراء)]
- 96:12/q04/p4 The added command heightens the rebuke: the opponent blocked prayer and perhaps its call to piety, while prayer alone was enough to name in the earlier prohibition. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī invoke brevity and the comprehensive force of prayer; al-Ālūsī says the command may cover the prohibiter's verbal call in the ironic reading. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the opponent might even forbid counsel to piety. A further explanation from al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī says that forbidding someone during prayer need not target prayer alone and could obstruct both worship and invitation. [12 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI(بعضهم,قيل); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 96:12/q05 How is the repeated 'Have you seen?' construction parsed?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Whether raʾayta here requires two objects, what supplies them, and whether an interrogative may answer a condition without fāʾ.
- 96:12/q05/p1 It is cognitive seeing or 'tell me': the first object is the prohibiter or preceding relative clause, while later objects or pronouns are supplied from context. — Abū Ḥayyān argues the first object is the relative clause and the second an interrogative clause, citing 53:33–35, 19:77–78 and 56:58–59; later instances have omitted objects, whose pronouns can denote prohibiter or servant. He denies syntactic competition among the three verbs because clauses cannot be pronominalized. Al-Bursawī makes the first object the forbidding man. Al-Ālūsī supplies a pronoun or demonstrative in later clauses. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls raʾayta cognitive and links its understood object to the earlier 'forbids' or later reminder of God's sight. [17 notes: ABUHAYYAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN+]
- 96:12/q05/p2 The repeated conditional is the second object of raʾayta; its omitted answer is supplied by the later condition or by 'Did he not know that God sees?'. — Al-Bayḍāwī treats the repetitions as grammatically parallel, with the condition as second object. Al-Bursawī says an interrogative or oath clause must complete that object. Al-Zamakhsharī, reported by Abū Ḥayyān, treats the later question about God's sight as the answer and cites two interrogative analogies to allow it without fāʾ. Abū Ḥayyān rejects that omission of fāʾ outside poetic necessity. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ABUHAYYAN-]
- 96:12/q05/p3 The interrogative arayta serves as a challenge or stylistic prompt and need not have a second object supplied. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes it from knowledge-based vision, which takes two objects. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites Meccan examples at 107:1, 19:77, 25:43, 45:23 and 53:33 to argue that Qurʾānic usage often leaves the second object absent to direct attention to a lesson; she cautions against forcing such patterns into school grammar by conjecture. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA; BINTSHATI]
- 96:12/q05/p4 The first object is the relative clause, but the second must be an interrogative clause rather than al-Zamakhsharī's proposed object analysis. — Abū Ḥayyān specifically invokes Qurʾānic parallels at 53:33–35, 19:77–78 and 56:58–59 for his preferred grammatical analysis. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN+]

## 96:12/q06 What consequence is left implicit after the condition?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Which earlier or later clause supplies the unspoken answer, whether it is an exhortation, warning or expression of wonder, and whether the next statement is independent.
- 96:12/q06/p1 Would the forbider still forbid the guided servant and his call to piety? — Ibn ʿĀshūr understands the answer from the earlier prohibition or the later reminder in 96:14; the particle in leaves the condition uncertain in keeping with the forbider's standpoint. Al-Ālūsī reports that, on the split-address reading, 'would you forbid him?' can be either the question's object or its conditional answer. [5 notes: ALUSI(قيل); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 96:12/q06/p2 Would guidance and commanding piety not be better for the forbider than his present course? — Al-Biqāʿī explicitly supplies 'would this not be better for him?'; al-Rāzī and the Imām reported by al-Ālūsī expand on the rational, wealthy man's forfeited higher station. [5 notes: BIQAI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI(الإمام)]
- 96:12/q06/p3 The opponent is ruined or liable to God's judgment for opposing a rightly guided worshipper. — Al-Qurṭubī says the question to Abū Jahl implies his doom. Jishumī says the mistaken tyrant destroys himself; Iṣlāḥī says ignoring the worshipper's possible guidance invites wrath. [5 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 96:12/q06/p4 How astonishing that a guided servant is opposed by a man who denies and turns away. — Al-Farrāʾ's reading, reported by al-Ālūsī, supplies astonishment as the implied conclusion; al-Baghawī also stresses the contrast. [4 notes: BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; ALUSI(الفراء)]
- 96:12/q06/p5 Did the denier not know that God sees him? — Al-Ṭabarī says the repeated conditions work by substitution and culminate in God's sight. Al-Zamakhsharī makes this the answer supplied by the second condition, but Abū Ḥayyān objects to its lack of fāʾ. Ibn Kathīr adds that God hears and will repay the opponent fully. [6 notes: TAB; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-]
- 96:12/q06/p6 This is how the prohibiter treated the guided servant. — Al-Māwardī supplies this omitted consequence for the construction. [2 notes: MAWARDI]
- 96:12/q06/p7 The conditional consequences are omitted, and the following statement begins independently. — Elmalılı prefers this to al-Kashshāf's allowance that both conditionals have expressed consequences. [1 notes: ELMALILI(Keşşâf)+]
- 96:12/q06/p8 Both conditionals can have expressed consequences. — Elmalılı reports this allowance from al-Kashshāf but favors omitted consequences and an independent following statement. [0 notes: ELMALILI(Keşşâf)-]

## 96:12/q07 What rhetorical force does this renewed question have?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the repetition chiefly expresses astonishment, counterfactual reproach, or a prompt to reflect and judge one's course.
- 96:12/q07/p1 It expresses wonder and rebukes the perversity of forbidding a guided servant who commands piety. — Al-Wāḥidī calls the repeated 'have you seen?' an expression of amazement; al-Baghawī contrasts the guided servant and denier. Al-Bayḍāwī says it repeats the first question. Ibn ʿĀshūr describes a second, detached hypothetical that renews wonder about the same person's possible states; al-Qushayrī asks whether the worshipper's guidance or piety would not be better. [10 notes: QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-QT; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 96:12/q07/p2 It asks the hearer to imagine the forbider's better course and reproaches him for losing its benefit. — Al-Rāzī says the sensible, prosperous man could have gained a higher station through guidance and piety; ʿIṣām al-Dīn frames it as rebuke for a missed victory, though al-Ālūsī distances himself from his elaborate sequence. [8 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI(الإمام,عصام الدين); ALUSI-]
- 96:12/q07/p3 It prompts reflection on the warning and on whether actions have evidence of guidance and piety. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ describes repeated arayta as a stylistic call to notice the lesson; al-Biqāʿī urges testing each action by evidence before proceeding. [3 notes: BIQAI; BINTSHATI]

## 96:12/q08 What happened in the confrontation over prayer connected with this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in details of Abū Jahl's threat and whether his retreat before the angels is included; al-Baghawī notes uncertainty in the report's attribution of the revelation statement.
- 96:12/q08/p1 Abū Jahl found or threatened Muhammad at prayer, forbade him, and the passage was revealed about his opposition. — Ibn ʿAbbās reports the prohibition and Abū Jahl's boast about his council; he says angels would seize Abū Jahl if he summoned it. Al-Suyūṭī transmits Ibn ʿAbbās's account extending to the lying, sinful forelock. Qatāda says Abū Jahl threatened to trample Muhammad, identifies his assembly as his clan and glosses zabāniya as police in Arabic. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ accepts that a specific occasion can coexist with general wording. [10 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(ابن عباس); DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); BINTSHATI]
- 96:12/q08/p2 When Abū Jahl threatened to trample the praying Prophet, he recoiled from a trench of fire, terror and wings; angels would have seized him limb by limb. — Al-Baghawī transmits this from Abū Hurayra but notes uncertainty whether the reference to revelation belonged to that narration or another source. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة)]

## 96:12/q09 What general warning or lesson does the verse convey?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the emphasized lesson is the injustice of obstruction, accountability before God, or the duty to assess one's own acts.
- 96:12/q09/p1 Obstructing a rightly guided worshipper who urges piety is unjust and invites divine anger; the warning extends to tyrants beyond Abū Jahl. — Jishumī says the mistaken tyrant destroys himself. Iṣlāḥī calls this obstruction satanic; Kur'an Yolu prefers the universal reading, applying it to tyrants in every age who obstruct religion's work through goodness, truth and justice. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says a specific occasion does not restrict the general wording. [8 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 96:12/q09/p2 God sees, hears and will repay the prohibiter for his conduct. — Al-Ṭabarī makes God's sight the culmination of the repeated challenges; Ibn Kathīr adds hearing and full recompense. Al-Qurṭubī says opposing Muhammad brings ruin. [5 notes: TAB; IBNKATHIR-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 96:12/q09/p3 A person should examine each act for evidence that it follows guidance and piety before acting. — Al-Biqāʿī draws this practical test from the omitted response and the contrast between guidance and obstruction. [1 notes: BIQAI]

## 96:12/q10 How does this question fit the surrounding passage and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the relation under discussion is the local argumentative sequence, the surah's revelatory arrangement, or a cross-verse link involving taqwā.
- 96:12/q10/p1 The passage proceeds from tyranny born of felt self-sufficiency to lost guidance and piety, then denial and divine warning. — ʿIṣām al-Dīn maps 96:9–10 to tyranny, 96:11–12 to lost guidance, and 96:13–14 to deserved punishment and distance from God; al-Ālūsī explicitly distances himself from that elaboration. Al-Rāzī's general reading, reported by Bint al-Shāṭiʾ, similarly links human creation, blessings, transgression and the failure to enjoin piety. [4 notes: ALUSI(عصام الدين); BINTSHATI(الفخر الرازي); ALUSI-]
- 96:12/q10/p2 The later passage about Abū Jahl was placed after the opening revelation, and its sequence suggests public preaching and prayer. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports al-Rāzī's first solution that the remaining surah came later and the Prophet was told to place it at the beginning; she considers both his explanations possible and infers public preaching and prayer from the sequence. [2 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 96:12/q10/p3 Taqwā here connects with its discussion at 92:5 and with istaġnā, self-sufficiency, in 96:7. — Corpus Coranicum points to the verb ittaqā and noun taqwā at 92:5 and their contrast here with istaġnā in 96:7; Asad also notes taqwā's early revelatory occurrence. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; ASAD-NOTES]

## 96:12/q11 What reward is reported for reciting the sūra?  [* · sciences]
- 96:12/q11/p1 The reciter receives a reward as though he had recited the entire collection of shorter sūras. — Al-Bayḍāwī transmits this as a report from the Prophet. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
