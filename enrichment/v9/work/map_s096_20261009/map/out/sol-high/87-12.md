# 87:12: 12 questions

## 87:12/q01 Who is the one destined for the Fire?  [ٱلَّذِى · referent]
turns on: Whether the description is general, tied to a reported occasion, grounded in prior knowledge, or read as an inward spiritual condition.
- 87:12/q01/p1 The person who evades the reminder through contempt, obstinacy, or disbelief; the wording applies generally. — Qatāda (TAB-FULL) contrasts God-fearing rememberers with one who disdains both the reminder and its people. Rāzī says the wording remains general even if an occasion involving named men is reported, and the sequence is rationally established. Qutb and Iṣlāḥī say the messenger reminds while people's reception determines their outcome; Iṣlāḥī says evaders take worldly life as all there is and do not fear the Hereafter, and their rejection reflects their own condition rather than any flaw in the message or preacher. Bursawī cites a sage's signs of wretchedness: gluttony, excessive sleep, persistent sin, hardness of heart, forgetting God, and facing the Almighty. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(بعض الحكماء); QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNKATHIR; RAZI+]
- 87:12/q01/p2 It was revealed concerning al-Walīd, ʿUtba, and Abī. — Rāzī reports this attribution, while insisting that the wording has general application. [1 notes: RAZI]
- 87:12/q01/p3 The person God already knew to be wretched. — Muqātil grounds the fate in God's prior knowledge. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 87:12/q01/p4 Inwardly, the most wretched is veiled from the Lord, unprepared, and spiritually distant. — Qāshānī contrasts this person with one who has readiness but whose readiness has lapsed and who is veiled by the self's dark qualities. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 87:12/q02 Does the most wretched person imply a less wretched group?  [ٱلَّذِى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the superlative entails a real comparison, or can function without a lesser counterpart.
- 87:12/q02/p1 Yes: the obstinate are most wretched, while the hesitant have some misery and the knowledgeable are happy. — Rāzī raises the question posed by contrasting this person with those who remember and fear, then prefers a threefold distinction between knowers, the hesitant, and the obstinate who ignore the call (87:11). [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI+]
- 87:12/q02/p2 No: the superlative can name the wretched without implying an actual lesser rival. — Rāzī reports an explanation from 25:24, where Paradise's people are called better settled and rested without a required rival; another takes 'most wretched' simply as 'wretched,' as 'easier' means 'easy' in 30:27. An unnamed poet's 'more honored' and 'taller' likewise show superlatives without implied rivals. [3 notes: RAZI(شاعر غير مسمى)]

## 87:12/q03 What does his burning in the Fire involve?  [يَصْلَى ٱلنَّارَ · interpretation]
- 87:12/q03/p1 He enters and endures the Fire, suffering fully in both body and heart. — Al-Ṭabarī calls it entry into Hell and emphasizes its heat and pain; al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Qutb describe the condemned one's entry or afterlife outcome. Al-Biqāʿī says the fire is endured directly and completely, by heart and body; his full account connects this to hard-hearted ignorance, blind imitation, or arrogant defiance. [11 notes: TAB; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNKATHIR]

## 87:12/q04 How is the construction 'yasla al-nar' used?  [يَصْلَى ٱلنَّارَ · grammar]
- 87:12/q04/p1 The verb takes the Fire as its object in an early Quranic formula for afterlife punishment. — Corpus Coranicum compares 111:3, 92:15, 88:4, 84:12, and 82:15. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 87:12/q05 How is the lām in 'yasla' pronounced here?  [يَصْلَى · readings]
- 87:12/q05/p1 It is pronounced emphatically here, while some readers soften lām at verse endings. — Ibn al-Jazarī cites the preferred distinction: a preceding trigger calls for emphatic lām away from verse endings, while final lām is softened for euphony. He lists this verse's yasla al-nar among non-final examples and contrasts final examples in 75:31, 87:15, and 96:10; the cited set also includes 2:125, 17:18, 92:15, 84:12, 88:4, and 111:3. [2 notes: NASHR; NASHR(أصحاب التفريق بين رؤوس الآي وغيرها)+]

## 87:12/q06 What is the greatest Fire, and what makes it greatest?  [ٱلنَّارَ ٱلْكُبْرَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'greatest' compares the Hereafter with worldly fire, selects Hell's lowest layer or graded torments within Hell, or denotes spiritual estrangement.
- 87:12/q06/p1 It is Hell or the Hereafter's Fire, greater than the fire of this world. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām and al-Ḥasan explicitly contrast Hell or the afterlife fire with worldly fire; al-Zamakhsharī likewise calls worldly fire the lesser fire. ʿAṭāʾ calls it immense and terrifying; Muqātil, al-Baghawī, and al-Ṭabarī emphasize greater heat, severity, and pain. Bayḍāwī, Bursawī, and Ālūsī cite the report making earthly fire one seventieth of Hellfire, while Ālūsī also cites an Aḥmad version of one hundredth and suggests that seventy may express abundance. Al-Biqāʿī says the afterlife fire is seventy degrees greater than the intermediate realm's and worldly fire; Bursawī prefers the greater punishment of the Hereafter over worldly and barzakh punishments. Qutb adds intensity, duration, vastness, and a long stay. Ibn ʿĀshūr says 'greatest' magnifies terror and threat; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this apparent meaning. Al-Nasafī identifies Hell explicitly. Full and abbreviated sources repeat these grounds. [36 notes: TAB; NAHHAS; MAWARDI(يحيى بن سلام); MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء,مقاتل); BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI(الحسن); RAZI(الحسن); QURTUBI(الحسن ويحيى بن سلام); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن ويحيى بن سلام); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI(أبو هريرة,الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); BURSEVI+; TABATABAI+]
- 87:12/q06/p2 It is Hell's lowest and most severe level, assigned to unbelievers. — Al-Farrāʾ identifies the lowest layer; al-Māwardī's two versions contrast its disbelievers' fire with the upper layer for sinful believers. Al-Kalbī says this level is especially great and hot. Rāzī invokes 4:145 on hypocrites in the lowest depth. Al-Biqāʿī links it to hard-hearted ignorance, imitation, and arrogant defiance. Bursawī places Pharaoh's people, hypocrites, and rejecters of Jesus' table there, citing 40:46. Ālūsī allows levels to differ in size and heat. Other sources transmit or offer the same interpretation; Bayḍāwī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī mention it as an alternative. [24 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS; MAWARDI(الفراء); MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفراء); TABRISI(الفراء); RAZI; QURTUBI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); TABATABAI(قيل); KASHSHAF+]
- 87:12/q06/p3 It indicates graded fires within the Hereafter, with the worst fire for the worst sinners. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān report that parts of the afterlife fire differ in severity. Rāzī relates the gradation to the gravity of sins and says the worst unbelievers enter the greatest fire. Ālūsī sees no difficulty in differing sizes and heat, and Study Quran reports the worst fire for the most wretched. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA(بعض المفسرين); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); STUDYQURAN(others)]
- 87:12/q06/p4 Inwardly, the greater fire is estrangement and veiling from God with enduring spiritual punishment. — Qāshānī describes veiling by association and attachment, then coercion, wrath, and the effects of Hell across four stations from dominion to divinity; the less wretched person knows only the fire of effects. Bursawī transmits this and the Najmiyya account: worldly desire is the lesser fire, while the afterlife's greater fire is abandonment, loss, expulsion, and estrangement, with 17:72 cited. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(القاشاني,النجمي)]

## 87:12/q07 What numerical comparison between earthly fire and Hellfire is reported?  [ٱلنَّارَ ٱلْكُبْرَىٰ · reports]
turns on: The transmitted numbers differ; Ālūsī proposes that seventy could be an expression of abundance.
- 87:12/q07/p1 Earthly fire is one seventieth of Hellfire. — Bayḍāwī quotes the Prophet; Bursawī repeats the comparison; Ālūsī cites Abū Hurayra's marfūʿ report and notes that seventy may express abundance because another version gives one hundred. Al-Biqāʿī instead describes the afterlife fire as seventy degrees greater than the intermediate realm's and worldly fires. [8 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI(أبو هريرة); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 87:12/q07/p2 Earthly fire is one hundredth of Hellfire. — Ālūsī cites an Aḥmad transmission from Abū Hurayra and suggests that the seventy in the other version may be a conventional large number. [2 notes: ALUSI(أبو هريرة); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]

## 87:12/q08 What fate in the Fire is described with this verse and the next?  [يَصْلَى ٱلنَّارَ · interpretation]
turns on: The Prophetic report distinguishes permanent inhabitants from those God later removes.
- 87:12/q08/p1 Those destined to remain suffer without death, relief, or a life worth calling life. — Al-Thaʿlabī says there is neither death for relief nor beneficial life. Ibn Kathīr reports the Prophet's distinction and connects enduring torment to 43:77 and 35:36, which say its inhabitants are not killed and punishment is not eased. Iṣlāḥī says evaders who deny the Hereafter face eternal fire and will wish vainly for death; Kur'an Yolu says continued existence amid horrific suffering is no positive life. Ibn Kathīr adds that the person remains conscious of painful punishment. [7 notes: THALABI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNKATHIR]
- 87:12/q08/p2 Those whom God removes from Hell become charcoal, emerge in groups, and sprout by Paradise's rivers. — Ibn Kathīr reports the Prophet's distinction between these people and those destined to remain in Hell. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 87:12/q09 How does the fire warning connect with the surrounding verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes emphasize different links: contrasting responses and fates, reciprocal ellipsis, or clarification of the earlier label.
- 87:12/q09/p1 It gives the bad outcome of evading the reminder, balanced by the good outcome promised to those who heed it. — Qutb says the Prophet's duty is to remind, after which people diverge by their reception. Iṣlāḥī says the fearful heed the warning while the obdurate evade it; the messenger should not force them. Corpus Coranicum sees verses 10–11's responses matched with fates in verses 12–15, a chiasm comparable to 92:5–11 and 92:14–21, with the warning balanced by the promise in 87:14–15. [7 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNKATHIR]
- 87:12/q09/p2 The passage uses paired omissions: the causes and outcomes of the two groups imply their unspoken opposites. — Al-Biqāʿī says fear and happiness imply the other's omitted hardness and misery; the misery named as cause implies omitted happiness. Al-Malawī says Quranic concision pairs cause and consequence across passages, likening judgment, decree, and ordainment to designing a waterwheel, constructing it, and measuring its water. Al-Biqāʿī also pairs the fire with its omitted opposite and later purification with its omitted opposite; purification, remembrance, and prayer form a therapy sequence, reprise 87:1's removal of defects and acquisition of perfections, and link prayer with escape from the fire. [7 notes: BIQAI(الملوي); BIQAI-FULL(الملوي)]
- 87:12/q09/p3 The explicit burning explains the previously vague description 'most wretched.' — Ibn ʿĀshūr relates this clarification to the early Meccan setting. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 87:12/q10 How does this warning fit the surah's broader message?  [* · links]
- 87:12/q10/p1 It makes revelation's public admonition part of a surah about divine praise, the messenger's mission, and afterlife recompense. — Qutb describes the cosmos praising God and the Prophet receiving good news of preservation and ease; he names oneness in creation, revelation, and afterlife recompense as foundations rooted in earlier scriptures (87:18–19), alongside the ease of the message, messenger, and community. Corpus Coranicum says the second stanza addresses the messenger again: after praising God, perhaps with close followers, he speaks to a wider audience, making the revelation of verse 6 public in purpose. [3 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 87:12/q11 How does the next surah relate to this fire warning?  [* · links]
- 87:12/q11/p1 Al-Ghāshiyah elaborates the same warning and the same great punishment. — Iṣlāḥī identifies this passage's great Fire with al-Ghāshiyah's great torment, citing 88:21–26. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 87:12/q12 What are the surah's setting, length, and recitational associations?  [* · sciences]
- 87:12/q12/p1 It is Meccan, has nineteen verses, and was beloved to the Prophet, who recited it with al-Ghāshiyah at Eid and Friday prayers. — Qutb says the two surahs were sometimes recited on the same day and cites 88:1. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]
