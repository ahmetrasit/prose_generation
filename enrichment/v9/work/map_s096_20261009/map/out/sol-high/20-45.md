# 20:45: 22 questions

## 20:45/q01 Who spoke the dual words قالا, and when?  [قَالَا · referent]
turns on: Whether Aaron had already joined Moses when the fear was expressed, and whether the dual reports a single shared utterance.
- 20:45/q01/p1 Moses and Aaron spoke together after they met and learned of the mission. — Ibn al-Jawzi reports Aaron was in Egypt, summoned to meet Moses; Qaffal says Moses may have paused until their meeting (20:36). Kashifi says Aaron heard Moses's story and warned of Pharaoh's power to kill or crucify. Islahi takes joint supplication as evidence that these directions followed their reunion in Egypt. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL(القفال); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 20:45/q01/p2 Moses spoke while Aaron was absent, and the dual includes Aaron as his partner. — Ibn al-Anbari notes Arabic dual address may include one person; Qaffal says Moses's leadership included Aaron and cites collective attribution in 2:72 and 63:8. Al-Irshad calls Moses primary and Aaron subordinate by grammatical predominance. Razi refers back to this explanation. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL(القفال); BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:45/q01/p3 Both spoke together at Sinai. — Al-Alusi compares the collective attribution to the plural address to messengers in 23:51, which did not require each messenger to be addressed individually. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:45/q01/p4 The verse combines Moses's earlier fear with Aaron's later fear, without requiring one occasion. — Tabatabai says Moses's fear could precede Aaron's receipt of the command. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:45/q02 Is قالا transmitted in another number?  [قَالَا · readings]
turns on: The transmitted dual wording versus the singular codex reading.
- 20:45/q02/p1 The verse has dual 'they said'. — The ordinary dual wording underlies the explanations of both brothers speaking or of Aaron's inclusion in Moses's speech. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,المفسرون); RAZI-FULL(القفال); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:45/q02/p2 The codices of Ibn Masʿud and Hafsa have singular 'he said'. — Razi reports this reading and explains that Moses spoke for himself and his brother. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن مسعود وحفصة)]

## 20:45/q03 Why does إننا emphatically state their fear?  [إِنَّنَا · rhetoric]
- 20:45/q03/p1 The added nūn intensifies the fear and complaint, preparing proportionate reassurance. — Al-Biqaʿi relates the intensified expression to the severity of their distress. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 20:45/q04 What does نخاف denote in this setting?  [نَخَافُ · meaning]
- 20:45/q04/p1 Fear is expectation of harm from signs that make it likely or known. — Bursevi contrasts fear with hope, expectation of a desired thing, and security; fear covers worldly and otherworldly matters (17:57). Fear of God means restraint from sin and obedience, rather than bodily terror. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 20:45/q05 Why could Moses and Aaron fear Pharaoh despite their prophetic mission and assurances?  [نَخَافُ · theology]
turns on: Whether fear concerns their safety, completion of the message, or desire for reassurance, and how the timing and scope of the prior promise in 28:35 are understood.
- 20:45/q05/p1 They feared Pharaoh's physical harm or death as a real danger. — Jishumi says prophets fear enemies until reassured. Al-Biqaʿi stresses Pharaoh's great power and their need for divine protection. Asad says banishment or killing could cut off the message. Tabatabai says the earlier fear concerned Moses's life (26:14); al-Alusi notes early authorities support a physical-harm reading despite 28:35. [7 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:45/q05/p2 Their chief fear was interruption of the mission, rather than death for its own sake. — Razi says they trusted Pharaoh would not kill them but feared harm before or after delivery. Al-Taʾwilat al-Najmiyya says human fear persists in prophets but they feared failure in servitude. Al-Alusi reports that removal of this obstacle elaborates Moses's earlier request; Tabatabai distinguishes this verse from Moses's earlier personal fear. Asad names banishment or death before full delivery. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:45/q05/p3 Fear can coexist with an expanded breast and patient resolve. — Razi explains expansion as capacity to preserve commands and laws without forgetfulness or distortion, not absence of fear. Al-Alusi reports that view but notes many take it as ease and patience in hardship; he says one may seek protection and still endure an affliction steadfastly. Kuranyolu favors responsibility's psychological burden over a physical speech defect and cites Moses's wish that his words be understood in 20:28. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 20:45/q05/p4 They sought a further revealed assurance to reinforce their confidence. — Razi says rational grounds for confidence were strengthened by revelation, comparing Abraham's request in 2:260. Al-Alusi reports this explanation but rejects its claim that they already knew with certainty that nothing could interrupt delivery. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)-]
- 20:45/q05/p5 Their fear appears difficult to reconcile with a prior promise of protection. — The objectors cite the promise to strengthen Moses with Aaron and shield both (28:35). Al-Alusi says the promise may concern defeat in argument and that its timing is uncertain, while early authorities favor physical harm. Tabatabai treats their fear as unobjectionable. [1 notes: TABATABAI(المعترضون); ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 20:45/q05/p6 The two fears may have arisen on separate occasions. — Tabatabai allows Moses's earlier private fear and Aaron's fear after receiving the command to be combined in one verse. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:45/q06 What does their pause to express fear imply about a command's timing?  [نَخَافُ · law]
- 20:45/q06/p1 An imperative alone does not require immediate performance. — Razi argues that, if immediacy were required, their delay after the command would strongly imply prophetic disobedience, which is excluded. Al-Alusi reports the Imam's same inference from the repeated commands. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)]

## 20:45/q07 What action is feared in يَفْرُطَ عَلَيْنَا?  [يَفْرُطَ عَلَيْنَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the verb denotes haste, excess, or induced haste, and whether Pharaoh or an elided act or statement is its subject.
- 20:45/q07/p1 Pharaoh may hasten to punish, attack, or kill them, especially before they finish presenting the message and signs. — Mujahid, Ibn Abbas, Ibn Zayd and others give punishment or killing; Ibn Zayd says it could follow immediately on conveying God's command (20:46). Tabari, Zajjaj, Ibn Qutayba, al-Asmaʿi, Ibn al-Aʿrabi, Zamakhshari, Abu Hayyan and others derive haste from farṭ, going ahead; fāriṭ precedes a party to water, a leading horse outruns others, rajaz and al-Qutami's verse illustrate it, and the Prophet says 'I precede you at the Pond.' Al-Kisaʾi lists verb forms; Kaʿb ibn Zuhayr uses afraṭa for arriving ahead of time. Islahi says ʿalaynā gives the force of sudden assault and notes Pharaoh's existing anger; Study Quran cites his swift execution of the sorcerers (20:70–73; 7:124). Wahidi refers to discussion at 16:62. [56 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأعرابي,الأصمعي,الكسائي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور,القطامي عمير بن شييم); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL(غير مسمى); QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور,الراجز,الضحاك,الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشاعر,النبي); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,كعب بن زهير); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد,مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(JJ,Ṭs)]
- 20:45/q07/p2 Pharaoh may exceed bounds in injuring or punishing them. — Tabari, al-Mubarrad, al-Farraʾ and Jishumi distinguish ifrāṭ as excess from tafrīṭ as shortcoming; Jishumi connects the root to advancing out of place or failing to advance. Al-Mubarrad compares punishment to one excessive in sin; Qurtubi also reports his view that Pharaoh punishes for supposed prior wrongdoing. The rajaz cited by Qurtubi uses afraṭa for overstepping and hurrying. This sense accompanies readings from ifrāṭ. [20 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(المبرد); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(غير مسمى); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وعكرمة وابن محيصن,الراجز,الفراء,المبرد); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فرقة والزعفراني عن ابن محيصن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(فرقة والزعفرانى عن ابن محيصن)]
- 20:45/q07/p3 Something may impel Pharaoh to rush against them. — Zamakhshari and Razi list Satan, Pharaoh's divine claim and arrogance, love of rule, or rebellious Egyptian courtiers; Baydawi also mentions fear for rule and a human or jinn devil. Abu Hayyan cites 7:109 and 7:127. The passive or causative readings support the idea. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن محيصن); RAZI-FULL(غير مسمى); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن محيصن); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(يحيى، أبو نوفل، وابن محيصن); SAMIN-DURR(ابن محيصن والزعفراني,يحيى بن وثاب وابن محيصن وأبو نوفل); ALUSI-FULL(يحيى وأبو نوفل وابن محيصن)]
- 20:45/q07/p4 An act or statement from Pharaoh may issue against them ahead of time. — Al-Nahhas supplies an omitted 'act'; Abu al-Baqa supplies 'statement.' Al-Alusi regards this ellipsis as contrary to the apparent meaning, and al-Samin prefers Pharaoh himself as subject. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)-]

## 20:45/q08 What is the grammatical object of نخاف?  [نَخَافُ أَن يَفْرُطَ · grammar]
- 20:45/q08/p1 The clause أن يفرط is the object of نخاف. — Al-Samin states the syntactic relation directly. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 20:45/q09 Who is the subject of يَفْرُطَ?  [يَفْرُطَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether an unspoken noun is supplied or Pharaoh remains the common subject of both verbs.
- 20:45/q09/p1 Pharaoh himself is the subject. — Abu al-Baqa allows this parallel to يطغى, and al-Samin prefers it as the evident construction; al-Alusi likewise calls the ellipsis contrary to the apparent sense. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR+]
- 20:45/q09/p2 An omitted act or statement from Pharaoh is the subject. — Al-Nahhas proposes an act; Abu al-Baqa proposes a statement. Al-Samin prefers Pharaoh as subject, and al-Alusi objects that an omitted subject departs from the apparent reading. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)-]

## 20:45/q10 How is يَفْرُطَ read?  [يَفْرُطَ · readings]
turns on: Differences in vowel pattern and verbal form yield haste, excessive harm, or an agent causing haste.
- 20:45/q10/p1 The majority read yafruṭ with fatḥa on yāʾ and ḍamma on rāʾ. — Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi call this the majority reading and explain it as Pharaoh hastening to harm; the forerunner to water illustrates the sense. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور); KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 20:45/q10/p2 Some read yufriṭ with ḍamma on yāʾ and kasra on rāʾ. — Ibn Atiyya assigns it to a group; Ibn al-Jawzi names Abd Allah ibn Amr, Ibn al-Sumayfi, Ibn Yaʿmar and Abu al-Aliya; Qurtubi names Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrima and Ibn Muhaysin; Abu Hayyan, al-Samin and al-Alusi give further group or al-Zaʿfarani transmissions. The sense is excessive harm, with some explanations allowing improper speech; Qurtubi cites rajaz. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عبد الله بن عمرو وابن السميفع وابن يعمر وأبو العالية); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وعكرمة وابن محيصن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فرقة والزعفراني عن ابن محيصن); SAMIN-DURR(ابن محيصن والزعفراني); ALUSI-FULL(فرقة والزعفرانى عن ابن محيصن); BURSEVI]
- 20:45/q10/p3 Some read yufarraṭ or yufraṭ with ḍamma on yāʾ and fatḥa on rāʾ. — Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi report Ibn Muhaysin; Ibn al-Jawzi also names Abu Rajaʾ. Abu Hayyan names Yahya, Abu Nawfal and Ibn Muhaysin; al-Samin and al-Alusi report the same readers. The passive or causative explanation is that an agent drives Pharaoh to haste; Abu Hayyan also allows excessive punishment. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن محيصن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو رجاء العطاردي وابن محيصن); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن محيصن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(يحيى، أبو نوفل، وابن محيصن); SAMIN-DURR(يحيى بن وثاب وابن محيصن وأبو نوفل); ALUSI-FULL(يحيى وأبو نوفل وابن محيصن); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 20:45/q10/p4 Some read yafraṭ with fatḥa on both yāʾ and rāʾ. — Ibn al-Jawzi names Ikrima and Ibrahim al-Nakhaʿi; Qurtubi reports Ibn Muhaysin and al-Mahdawi's suggestion that the form is dialectal. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عكرمة وإبراهيم النخعي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن محيصن)]

## 20:45/q11 What may Pharaoh's يَطْغَىٰ involve?  [يَطْغَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the feared transgression is violence toward the messengers, increased defiance or unbelief, oppression of Israelites, or offense against God.
- 20:45/q11/p1 He may exceed bounds in attacking, mistreating, or killing Moses and Aaron. — Wahidi says tyranny exceeds limits in mistreating them, and al-Kalbi and al-Mawardi specify killing. Al-Dahhak glosses aggression or transgression. Bursevi allows tyranny against the brothers with the preposition omitted for the verse ending, comparing 93:3. Islahi includes killing and plotting. [19 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs)]
- 20:45/q11/p2 He may become more arrogant, defiant, and uncontrollable. — Al-Thaʿlabi reports arrogance and defiance; Ibn al-Jawzi attributes defiance to Muqatil. Al-Biqaʿi says Pharaoh may surpass his existing arrogance; Islahi says he may become more rebellious before the brothers can speak. [6 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:45/q11/p3 He may commit a graver offense against God or the sacred. — Razi says he may utter what is improper about God; Baydawi and Bursevi connect it with audacious, harsh speech. Al-Taʾwilat al-Najmiyya fears insults to God's commands. Tabatabai includes unprecedented daring against the sacred. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فرقة والزعفراني عن ابن محيصن); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:45/q11/p4 His disbelief may increase as he rejects or attempts to refute their proofs. — Tabrisi says he might kill before weighing the proof and reject in increased disbelief. Study Quran reports increased disbelief through attempted refutation. [2 notes: TABRISI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs)]
- 20:45/q11/p5 He may intensify oppression of the Israelites. — Tabatabai includes worsening their punishment as part of the feared tyranny. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:45/q12 Why is the second fear left open and placed last?  [أَوْ أَن يَطْغَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 20:45/q12/p1 The wording alludes discreetly to Pharaoh's potential offense against God, and saves the graver concern for last. — Zamakhshari describes the unqualified phrase as courteous restraint. Razi compares the hoopoe ending with the people's sun worship (27:24); Bursevi says the wording politely avoids saying 'against God' and ends with the greater offense. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فرقة والزعفراني عن ابن محيصن); BURSEVI]

## 20:45/q13 What does the repeated أن after أو convey?  [أَوْ أَن · rhetoric]
- 20:45/q13/p1 It marks both feared alternatives as live possibilities and gives them special weight. — Al-Alusi says أو keeps either alternative from being excluded and the explicit second أن, though unnecessary to basic sense, signals special concern. Al-Burhan records the hamza-before-waw form in Hud, Bani Israil, Ta Ha and al-Muʾmin, citing examples in Hud and al-Muʾmin. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURHAN]

## 20:45/q14 How does قالا introduce the response to the preceding command?  [قَالَا · links]
- 20:45/q14/p1 It is an explanatory resumption stating what the brothers said after receiving the command. — Al-Alusi reads قالا as answering the implied question created by the preceding instruction. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:45/q15 How does this fear fit the surrounding mission narrative?  [* · links]
- 20:45/q15/p1 The difficult command to confront Pharaoh leads to the brothers' fear and God's reassurance. — Ibn Ashur says God showed Moses two signs, then assigned confrontation with the mightiest ruler; 20:45–46 recount fear and reassurance. Samarrai notes that Ta-Ha presents the joint mission throughout: both are sent, fear, reassured, deliver the message, and face Pharaoh together (20:29, 20:42, 20:63, 20:90, 20:92). [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 20:45/q16 What is known of Moses and Aaron's journey and meeting before the mission?  [* · reports]
- 20:45/q16/p1 Moses traveled alone from the mountain to Egypt in severe want; Aaron then met him after revelation directed him to do so. — Bursevi's travel report says Moses lacked route knowledge, provisions, mount and companion, living on sparse fruit and game. Kashifi says Aaron met him on the road, heard the story and warned of Pharaoh's power. Ibn al-Jawzi reports Aaron was in Egypt when summoned. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الكاشفي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون)]

## 20:45/q17 Why must the mission present proof completely?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:45/q17/p1 Complete presentation allows the proof to take effect and establishes accountability. — Tabatabai says omitting complete evidence for the wretched would defeat the testing account (17:82). Kuranyolu says Moses had to persuade people steeped in magic through rational proofs; the public signs expose magic's baselessness, while rational proofs carry monotheism. Tabrisi and others relate their fear to interruption before proof or miracle. [5 notes: TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs)]

## 20:45/q18 How may the following verse's أسمع be parsed?  [* · grammar]
turns on: The syntactic function of أسمع.
- 20:45/q18/p1 It may be a second predicate or an accusative circumstantial clause. — Tabrisi gives both grammatical analyses of the following reassurance. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 20:45/q19 Why is hearing mentioned before sight in God's reassurance?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the order conveys divine nearness or hearing's role in receiving revelation.
- 20:45/q19/p1 Hearing has shorter reach and so signals nearness more strongly than sight. — The two Samarrai works and Lamasat al-Halaqat all give this ordering rationale; seeing may operate at a greater distance. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 20:45/q19/p2 Hearing is especially important for receiving a revealed message. — Samarrai favors this: a blind person can understand a message, while informing a deaf person is harder; prophets were not deaf though one could be blind. He cites 76:2 and 25:73. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH+]

## 20:45/q20 What does annexing a verbal noun to its agent imply?  [* · grammar]
- 20:45/q20/p1 It presents the agent's action as occurring or characteristic, even under a negation. — Jurjani says al-Mutanabbi's transmitted line attributes preservation to the fingers; annexation to the object would deny that they preserve anything. His idiom 'do not be angry with Zayd's anger' presents Zayd's anger as actual or habitual, unlike a verbal construction denying one's habit. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 20:45/q21 How should melodic recitation be judged?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether the melody improves the voice while preserving the words or distorts their pronunciation.
- 20:45/q21/p1 A beautiful voice and appropriate melody are commended or permitted. — Al-Itqan cites reports from Ibn Hibban and others that a good voice adorns the Qur'an; those without one should improve their voice. Al-Shafiʿi's Mukhtasar allows melody, and a report attributed to al-Tabarani and al-Bayhaqi commands Arab melodies while warning against styles of scriptural communities and immoral people. [4 notes: ITQAN(الشافعي)]
- 20:45/q21/p2 Melody that distorts recitation by excessive lengthening or misplaced assimilation is disliked or forbidden. — A report from al-Rabiʿ al-Jizi says al-Shafiʿi disliked melody; al-Rafiʿi reconciles the jurists by limiting dislike to distorted elongation, overfilled vowels or misplaced assimilation. Al-Nawawi calls the excess forbidden and sinful for reader and listener, and reads al-Shafiʿi's dislike accordingly. [4 notes: ITQAN(الرافعي,الشافعي,النووي)]

## 20:45/q22 May listeners invite a skilled reciter, and may a group recite in turns?  [* · law]
- 20:45/q22/p1 It is recommended to request and listen to a good-voiced reader. — Al-Nawawi bases the recommendation on a sound hadith. [1 notes: ITQAN(النووي)]
- 20:45/q22/p2 Group recitation in which readers take successive portions is permitted. — Al-Nawawi allows both collective recitation and taking turns. [1 notes: ITQAN(النووي)]
