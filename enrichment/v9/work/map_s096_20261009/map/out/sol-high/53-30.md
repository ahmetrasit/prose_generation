# 53:30: 22 questions

## 53:30/q01 What reaches the limit of their knowledge?  [ذَٰلِكَ مَبْلَغُهُم مِّنَ ٱلْعِلْمِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'that' points to worldly life or preference, angel claims, conjecture, rejection, or turning away; and whether ʿilm denotes genuine knowledge, conjecture, or the known object.
- 53:30/q01/p1 Worldly livelihood, affairs, and pleasures exhaust what they know; they do not know religion or the afterlife. — Al-Zajjāj links their worldly livelihood to 53:29 and says they cast aside the afterlife. Ibn ʿAṭiyya names farming, crafts, and deceptive status seeking. Abū Ḥayyān adds trades and cites 30:7. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī depicts a metaphorical endpoint: knowledge stops at the world, which then absorbs desire, concern, and hope. Islahi says they see outward appearances rather than deeper reality; the evident Creator makes a merely playful universe implausible, and 30:7 supplies the contrast. Al-Ṭabrisī calls immediate pleasure an animal-like aim unworthy of reason. The Study Quran contrasts religion. Bursevī and Ālūsī include false conjectural perception within ʿilm. Asad glosses the total extent or goal of knowledge. [21 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق والكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 53:30/q01/p2 Preferring transient worldly life over the lasting Hereafter is their intellectual limit. — Al-Farrāʾ links the angel claim with belittling their choice; al-Naḥḥās mentions fading adornment and status versus lasting reward. Jishumī says worldly benefit displaces lasting reward in ignorance. Rāzī relates this possibility to 53:29. Bursevī cites Bahr al-ʿUlūm and al-Irshād for choosing the world and restricting desire to it. Ālūsī prefers the contextual world over turning away or conjecture. [16 notes: NAHHAS(غيره); THALABI(الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد,بحر العلوم); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 53:30/q01/p3 Their claims that angels are God's daughters and intercessors mark their limit. — Muqātil says they rely on this and ignore the Qur'an. Al-Ṭabarī calls it shirk and uncertain conjecture. Al-Qurṭubī's alternative includes idols as alleged daughters. The Study Quran adds caprice and desire. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(غير مسمى); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; STUDYQURAN; THALABI(الفراء)]
- 53:30/q01/p4 Their furthest intellectual reach is conjecture or opinion itself. — Mujāhid calls it their limit of opinion. Rāzī prefers conjecture as the clearest antecedent; Abū Ḥayyān reports it. Al-Ṭabarī and the Study Quran relate conjecture to the angel claims. [5 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TAB-FULL; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 53:30/q01/p5 Their limit is rejecting the Messenger and God's revelation. — Ibn Zayd calls them people of shirk plotting against revelation; al-Naḥḥās adds obstinacy. Rāzī says ignorance followed their own withdrawal from remembrance, making punishment just despite the objection that God does not burden a child or mad person. He ranks responses to the Qur'an: wholehearted acceptance, accepting its miracle and following the Messenger, hesitation like Abū Ṭālib's, and blameful rejection. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); NAHHAS(ابن زيد); RAZI-FULL]
- 53:30/q01/p6 The demonstrative points to turning away; 'knowledge' may mean the known object, the Qur'an. — Rāzī reports this third possibility from 53:29. Ālūsī rejects turning away as the antecedent. Al-Burhān explains that a verbal noun can carry passive force: ʿilm is the known thing, as ṣunʿ is the made thing. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 53:30/q01/p7 The phrase names the extent of knowledge without specifying its subject. — Bursevī calls mablagh a place or extent noun. Asad gives 'total extent or goal.' Al-Burhān explains the passive sense of ʿilm. Al-Biqāʿī calls the assertion sarcastic because blindness to the afterlife leaves no real knowledge. [5 notes: BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL]

## 53:30/q02 Why describe their knowledge in these terms?  [ذَٰلِكَ مَبْلَغُهُم مِّنَ ٱلْعِلْمِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the emphasis is on belittling ignorance or confirming confined ambition.
- 53:30/q02/p1 The wording belittles narrow minds blind to the afterlife. — Al-Farrāʾ calls their judgment small and foolish; al-Biqāʿī calls the claim sarcastic because the afterlife is the root of knowledge. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the parenthesis belittles understanding. Islahi says temporary pleasure, timidity, and deprivation divert them from the eternal kingdom, citing 30:7. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 53:30/q02/p2 The wording confirms that their ambitions are confined to worldly life. — Al-Bayḍāwī, Bursevī, and Ālūsī say the inserted statement confirms their low aspirations. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says limited knowledge narrows desire, concern, and hope. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 53:30/q03 Why is 'their' plural?  [مَبْلَغُهُم · grammar]
- 53:30/q03/p1 The earlier relative expression is singular in form but denotes a group, so the suffix follows its plural sense. — Bursevī and Ālūsī contrast the earlier singular following man in 53:29 with this plural. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the singular verb agrees with man while the plural suffix confirms its group referent. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:30/q04 Is the limited-knowledge clause parenthetical or the command's direct explanation?  [ذَٰلِكَ مَبْلَغُهُم مِّنَ ٱلْعِلْمِ إِنَّ رَبَّكَ · links]
turns on: Whether the clause interrupts a command and its cause or itself supplies that cause.
- 53:30/q04/p1 It is an interjection or explanatory parenthesis between 'turn away' and the cause given by God's knowledge. — Al-Zamakhsharī's view is reported by Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Samīn. Al-Samīn argues that an interjection between cause and result is clear, even within a narrative. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it explains ignorance of the afterlife; Bursevī calls it an interruption. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 53:30/q04/p2 It directly explains the command: leave them because this is all they attain. — Rāzī prefers this, saying their turning away and preference for the immediate explain ignorance. Abū Ḥayyān rejects the aside analysis for lack of basis; al-Samīn reports that objection and answers it. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); RAZI-FULL+]

## 53:30/q05 Why should the Prophet turn away from these people?  [ذَٰلِكَ مَبْلَغُهُم مِّنَ ٱلْعِلْمِ إِنَّ رَبَّكَ · links]
turns on: Whether their resistance, God's judgment, or His knowledge of receptive hearers supplies the immediate emphasis.
- 53:30/q05/p1 Their worldly aims and resistance make further admonition fruitless; his duty of conveying is complete. — Al-Bayḍāwī says invitations deepen their obstinacy. Al-Kashshāf cites 28:56: the Prophet cannot guide whom he loves. Bursevī and Ālūsī say the persistently astray will not return and he need not exhaust himself. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies Qur'an rejecters through 53:2, 19, 27, 28 and links their withdrawal from remembrance to the command. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 53:30/q05/p2 God knows and will judge both groups, consoling the Prophet and settling their cases. — Rāzī offers three contexts: failed reminders should give way to fighting without uncertainty or despair; opponents claimed guidance despite proof, as in 34:24 and 7:87, so judgment belongs to God; and harm the Prophet endured will be recompensed, as 53:31 indicates. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān call it consolation, warning, and promise. Ibn ʿĀshūr says God's superior knowledge removes grief. Islahi promises no escape for the astray and aid and mercy for the faithful. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 53:30/q05/p3 God alone knows which counsel will work and can bless effective preaching. — Al-Biqāʿī says outward worldly knowledge and forceful admonition cannot establish ultimate states. He gives Muṣʿab's preaching: Usayd ibn Ḥuḍayr and Saʿd ibn Muʿādh accepted Islam in an hour. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(مصعب بن عمير)]

## 53:30/q06 What does calling God 'your Lord' suggest?  [إِنَّ رَبَّكَ · rhetoric]
- 53:30/q06/p1 God's exclusion of an astray person may benefit the Prophet. — Al-Biqāʿī says the person might otherwise corrupt more than he improved. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 53:30/q07 Does aʿlam mean knowing or knowing better?  [أَعْلَمُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the form is absolute or retains an explicit or implied comparison.
- 53:30/q07/p1 It may simply mean 'knowing.' — Al-Naḥḥās, Rāzī, and Makkī as reported by al-Samīn allow this because God has no equal knower. [3 notes: NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(مكي)]
- 53:30/q07/p2 It may retain the comparative sense: God knows better than any other knower. — Al-Naḥḥās posits an omitted comparison, Rāzī a hypothetical knower, and Makkī allows the comparative. Ibn ʿĀshūr says God knows the people's inner conditions better than the Prophet. [4 notes: NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(مكي); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:30/q08 What is the function of 'He'?  [هُوَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the pronoun primarily clarifies syntax or asserts exclusive divine knowledge.
- 53:30/q08/p1 It is a separating pronoun clarifying that aʿlam is the predicate. — Rāzī calls it ʿimād or faṣl: the sentence works without it, but a hearer might otherwise take 'most knowing' as part of a following clause. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:30/q08/p2 It emphatically restricts hidden knowledge and reckoning to God. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says inna and the pronoun emphasize implied reckoning rather than bare omniscience; real restriction excludes the Prophet's knowledge of inner states and relieves his grief. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:30/q09 Why does aʿlam take bi before its object?  [أَعْلَمُ بِمَن · grammar]
turns on: Whether bi follows the adjective's government or a broader alternation in constructions of knowing.
- 53:30/q09/p1 Its weaker verbal force permits bi, which associates knowledge with each person's state. — Rāzī calls aʿlam a noun denoting an action with weak government. Ibn ʿĀshūr says bi governs both occurrences and marks association with the astray or guided person's condition. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:30/q09/p2 Knowing can govern directly or through bi, with direct government sometimes stronger. — Rāzī cites 73:20, 96:14, 39:52, 47:19, and 9:2 and considers clarity of object, required certainty, or force of action as reasons for direct attachment. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:30/q10 What is God's way here?  [سَبِيلِهِۦ · referent]
- 53:30/q10/p1 It is His religion, the true path of Islam. — Al-Naḥḥās locates the guided on this path in God's prior knowledge. Jishumī and al-Qurṭubī likewise gloss it as God's religion. [3 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 53:30/q11 Who are the astray and guided?  [ضَلَّ ٱهْتَدَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether the contrast is described as lasting receptivity or as faith communities.
- 53:30/q11/p1 The astray persist without returning, while the guided can accept guidance. — Bursevī and Ālūsī put the Prophet's opponents in the first class. Elmalılı contrasts persistent straying with accepting guidance; al-Biqāʿī cautions that outward signs cannot disclose ultimate states. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 53:30/q11/p2 They are the groups of unbelief and faith known to God. — Ibn ʿAbbās says both were known before creation. The Study Quran reads the contextual difference as Muslim and pagan beliefs; Ibn Zayd calls the opponents people of shirk. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد)]

## 53:30/q12 What does God's knowledge mean for the two groups?  [إِنَّ رَبَّكَ ضَلَّ ٱهْتَدَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the emphasis is recompense, discernment of response, foreknowledge, sovereign guidance, or the difference between beliefs.
- 53:30/q12/p1 He knows their states and will repay evil and good as deserved. — Al-Wāḥidī, Jishumī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Nasafī state recompense. Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds consolation, warning, and promise. Elmalılı says the knowledge orders and implements punishment or reward. Ibn ʿĀshūr hears implied reckoning; Islahi says the astray cannot escape and hardship endured for God gains aid and mercy. Muqātil says God needs no worship, and angels and all others are His servants. [19 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 53:30/q12/p2 He knows who will respond; the Messenger can only convey. — Al-Kashshāf cites 28:56 and al-Bayḍāwī says the duty is discharged. Al-Biqāʿī says outward signs cannot decide final states and gives Muṣʿab's successful preaching as God's blessing. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(مصعب بن عمير)]
- 53:30/q12/p3 His knowledge precedes creation and embraces each ultimate end. — Ibn ʿAbbās says neither group's final reward can evade it. Rāzī says eternal knowledge relates to each event as it occurs without delay; no atom escapes it, comparing 6:117. The Study Quran calls it knowledge of all final ends. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls this assertion God's testimony to the preceding characterization. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 53:30/q12/p4 God guides and misguides whom He wills with knowledge, power, wisdom, and justice. — Ibn Kathīr grounds this in His creation of all creatures, knowledge of their welfare, and justice in law and decree. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 53:30/q12/p5 He distinguishes Muslim and pagan beliefs. — The Study Quran gives this contextual explanation within the sūra's reproach of Quraysh's idol worship and conjecture. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 53:30/q13 How are guidance and misguidance attributed to God and people?  [ضَلَّ ٱهْتَدَىٰ · theology]
turns on: Whether the account emphasizes human acquisition or divine determination.
- 53:30/q13/p1 People acquire them, while God creates and originates both. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes human acquisition from divine creation. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 53:30/q13/p2 God guides and misguides according to His will, knowledge, power, and wisdom, always justly. — Ibn Kathīr cites creation and knowledge of what benefits His servants. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 53:30/q14 Why repeat 'He knows best'?  [أَعْلَمُ · rhetoric]
- 53:30/q14/p1 It reinforces certainty and the groups' complete divergence. — Bursevī says the repetition marks separation; Ālūsī says it intensifies confirmation and warning; Elmalılı says it marks the contrast. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 53:30/q15 Why mention the astray before the guided?  [ضَلَّ ٱهْتَدَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 53:30/q15/p1 The opponents are the immediate concern; their mention threatens them, while the guided complete consolation and promise. — Rāzī compares the order in 6:117 and 68:7, where the Prophet likewise faces stubborn opponents. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the guided complete the statement and promise believers. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:30/q16 How does the clause compare with parallel passages?  [ضَلَّ ٱهْتَدَىٰ · links]
turns on: Whether the comparison concerns forms, narrative time, or the grammar of a related construction.
- 53:30/q16/p1 The parallels vary in preposition and verb form while preserving the two groups. — Al-Burhān compares 6:117's man yuḍill and bi-l-muhtadīn, 68:7's bi and past ḍalla, and this verse's bi-man ḍalla and bi-man ihtadā. The Study Quran also points to 28:85. [2 notes: BURHAN; STUDYQURAN]
- 53:30/q16/p2 Past ḍalla here fits a completed turning away; 6:117 has a prospective warning. — Rāzī contrasts this completed rejection and withdrawal with 6:116–17, where most people's misleading still lies ahead. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:30/q16/p3 The participle in a related construction governs verbally for future, but not past, time. — Rāzī gives 'I will strike Zayd tomorrow' as a future example; past action needs a finite verb or genitive attachment. He discusses this with 6:117. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:30/q17 What did Ibn Masʿūd say about the limits of knowledge?  [ٱلْعِلْمِ · reports]
- 53:30/q17/p1 Speak only of what one knows; admitting ignorance is itself knowledge. — He cites the Prophet's refusal to be among the pretentious in 38:86. [1 notes: IBNHIBBAN(عبد الله بن مسعود)]

## 53:30/q18 How did Ibn Masʿūd identify the smoke and threatened punishments?  [* · reports]
- 53:30/q18/p1 The smoke was Quraysh's seven-year famine; the great seizure and binding punishment were Badr. — He says the Prophet prayed for lean years, Quraysh saw smoke, Abū Sufyān asked relief, the punishment lifted, and disbelief resumed (44:11, 44:15). He says the Roman episode and four events had passed (44:16, 25:77, 30:1). [2 notes: IBNHIBBAN(عبد الله بن مسعود)]

## 53:30/q19 What counts as worldly life in the inward reading?  [ذَٰلِكَ مَبْلَغُهُم مِّنَ ٱلْعِلْمِ · inward]
turns on: Whether the world is a distraction, a temporary means, an object of divine disfavor, or the limiting concern.
- 53:30/q19/p1 Whatever distracts one from God is one's world, even outwardly religious pursuits. — Bursevī names asceticism, piety, unveilings, and miracles pursued for acclaim, status, or wealth; such motives waste deeds and spiritual states. Apparent unveilings can be immediate returns, as Iblīs's nine thousand years of worship preceded his respite after unbelief (7:14). People of spiritual realization define one's world as whatever distracts from the Master. [3 notes: BURSEVI(بعض أهل الحقيقة)]
- 53:30/q19/p2 The world is a passage and useful mount, not a final destination. — Bursevī cites a prophetic bridge saying: cross, do not build. Another saying forbids reviling the world because it carries believers to good and from evil; when cursed, it curses the most disobedient. [3 notes: BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام)]
- 53:30/q19/p3 The world is hateful to God. — Bursevī reports Abū Hurayra that God created nothing more hateful and has not looked toward it since creation. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أبو هريرة)]
- 53:30/q19/p4 Making the world one's greatest concern is the warned-of limit. — Ibn Kathīr transmits ʿĀʾisha's saying that the world is a home for the homeless and wealth for the penniless, gathered by one without intelligence; a supplication asks God not to make it one's greatest concern or limit of knowledge. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(الدعاء المأثور,عائشة)]

## 53:30/q20 How is the contrast between the groups read inwardly?  [ضَلَّ ٱهْتَدَىٰ · inward]
turns on: Whether the reading stresses the ego's fixed readiness or the soul's natural goodness under coercive influence.
- 53:30/q20/p1 The unbelieving ego lacks readiness for the call; pre-eternal severity does not become eternal gentleness. — Bursevī associates the ego with Jewish traits and cites a sacred report of people created for Paradise and Fire, blessing the former and warning the latter. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الحديث القدسي)]
- 53:30/q20/p2 The soul is good by nature; evil comes through a companion's obstinate urging. — Bursevī reports Sufi teachers comparing inability to resist to coercion, which law and reason do not blame. He takes the Prophet's saying that goodness is habitual and evil obstinate as glad tidings. [2 notes: BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام,بعض الكبار)]

## 53:30/q21 How do the angel claims relate to God's judgment?  [* · links]
- 53:30/q21/p1 Calling angels female and trusting their intercession cannot evade God. — Islahi says angels speak or intercede only by His permission; He knows the righteous and wrongdoers. Muqātil says angels and all others are God's servants and possessions. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; MUQATIL]

## 53:30/q22 How does the verse serve the sūra's argument?  [* · links]
- 53:30/q22/p1 It exposes ignorance and conjecture beneath Quraysh's idolatry and hope in angels' intercession. — The Study Quran locates it in the second section's reproach. Islahi says the universe's evident Creator makes a merely playful purpose implausible; the Qur'an calls to the eternal kingdom. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
