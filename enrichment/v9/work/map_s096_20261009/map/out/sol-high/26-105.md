# 26:105: 13 questions

## 26:105/q01 What does the perfect verb كَذَّبَتْ convey about the duration of the denial?  [كَذَّبَتْ · grammar]
- 26:105/q01/p1 The denial continued from the beginning of Noah's preaching to its end. — Bursevī reads the perfect form as expressing sustained denial throughout Noah's mission. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 26:105/q02 Why does كَذَّبَتْ have feminine agreement with قَوْمُ?  [كَذَّبَتْ قَوْمُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether qawm itself licenses feminine agreement, an understood feminine collective supplies it, or the choice carries a rhetorical effect.
- 26:105/q02/p1 Qawm itself permits feminine agreement. — Majāz cites both masculine and feminine usage; Kashshāf, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī and Samīn cite the feminine diminutive quwayma. Nasafī and Ālūsī say the collective admits both genders; Abū Ḥayyān notes masculine pronouns later refer to its rational male members, and Samīn explains that by predominance. Bursevī also cites the feminine diminutive. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this as one analysis. [10 notes: MAJAZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 26:105/q02/p2 The verb agrees with an understood feminine sense, such as Noah's community, group or nation, while qawm is masculine. — Zajjāj, Abū Isḥāq as transmitted by Wāḥidī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī's report from Zajjāj, Abū Ḥayyān's alternative, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī's reported analysis explicitly retain masculine qawm. Thaʿlabī compares the Qur'anic expression 'the Bedouins said.' Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī invoke community or congregation; Samīn offers umma as an alternative, and Ālūsī offers umma or jamāʿa. Burhān posits an understood feminine construct and says qawm otherwise takes a masculine verb. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 26:105/q02/p3 The marked feminine choice belittles the people's vile act or their standing despite their power. — Biqāʿī says masculine agreement with qawm is more common, making the feminine form a deliberate rhetorical choice. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 26:105/q03 Whom can قَوْمُ include?  [قَوْمُ · meaning]
turns on: The narrower original sense versus broader Qur'anic or contextual use.
- 26:105/q03/p1 In its original or narrower sense, qawm denotes men. — Al-Mufradāt, reported by Ṭabāṭabāʾī, cites 49:11 and a poet's contrast between men and women; the cited line asks whether al-Ḥiṣn's qawm are men or women. Bursevī allows this narrower sense with women included by subordination. [3 notes: TABATABAI(الشاعر,المفردات); BURSEVI]
- 26:105/q03/p2 In broad usage, qawm includes men and women. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says most Qur'anic usage includes both despite the original sense; Bursevī gives a mixed group as one possible meaning. [2 notes: TABATABAI; BURSEVI]

## 26:105/q04 Who are Noah's قَوْمُ in this account?  [قَوْمُ نُوحٍ · referent]
- 26:105/q04/p1 They are all humanity on earth before peoples divided through the separation of languages. — Biqāʿī ties the scope of Noah's people to humanity before the linguistic division of peoples. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 26:105/q05 How is Noah characterized as an early prophet or messenger?  [نُوحٍ · reports]
turns on: The notes give different descriptions of Noah's priority and the setting of his mission.
- 26:105/q05/p1 Noah was the first prophet sent. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports this from Anas from the Prophet; the note gives no grading. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 26:105/q05/p2 Noah was the first messenger sent to earth after people began worshiping idols. — Ibn Kathīr says God sent him to forbid idolatry and warn of its punishment. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 26:105/q05/p3 Noah was the first of the foremost prophets. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī uses this description when introducing the account after Moses and Abraham. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 26:105/q06 Why was Noah named نُوحٍ?  [نُوحٍ · reports]
- 26:105/q06/p1 He was named for his long lament over himself. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports this from Yazīd al-Raqāshī. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(يزيد الرقاشي)]

## 26:105/q07 What chronology is reported for Noah's life?  [نُوحٍ · reports]
turns on: The reports concern different milestones in Noah's life.
- 26:105/q07/p1 He was sent at forty, preached for 950 years, and lived sixty years after the Flood while people multiplied. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits this from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس)]
- 26:105/q07/p2 He was born during Adam's lifetime. — Nasafī reports this without a named transmitter. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 26:105/q08 Why are Noah's people said to deny ٱلْمُرْسَلِينَ in the plural?  [ٱلْمُرْسَلِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the plural expresses the shared message or proof of all messengers, a generic designation for Noah, literal rejection of multiple prophets or messengership, or Noah's report of later messengers.
- 26:105/q08/p1 Their denial of Noah counted as denial of every messenger because the messengers share one call, proof or command to believe in all. — Ṭabarī says Noah's people denied God's messengers when Noah called them to fear punishment for disbelief and denial. Zajjāj says each messenger commands belief in all; Wāḥidī attributes this to Ibn ʿAbbās, Muqātil and the exegetes, and reports al-Ḥasan that later messengers bring what earlier ones brought. Thaʿlabī also reports al-Ḥasan. Ibn ʿAṭiyya invokes their single statement and call; Ṭabrisī and Qurṭubī cite the command to affirm all. Rāzī says the way prophethood is known is the same; Biqāʿī transmits Baghawī's point that rejecting one miracle rejects what all brought. Ibn Kathīr, Bursevī and Ālūsī invoke a shared message, with Bursevī and Ālūsī specifying monotheism and basic laws. Nasafī extends the rule across this sūra. Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this explanation, citing 4:150; Study Quran's R combines universal recognition with the command to affirm all. Iṣlāḥī adds a common mentality and fate among rejecters. [19 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل والمفسرون,الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(البغوي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(R); TABATABAI(قيل)+]
- 26:105/q08/p2 The plural names the class of messengers generically while referring specifically to Noah alone. — Muqātil says they denied Noah alone and compares 54:9; Thaʿlabī compares the address 'O messengers.' Kashshāf, Nasafī, Zamakhsharī as reported in Burhān, Ālūsī, Ṭabāṭabāʾī's reported alternative and Study Quran's Q compare a plural category used for someone who owns only one mount or cloak. Qurṭubī calls this a generic class intending Noah. Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes the alternative recurs for ʿĀd and Thamūd. [9 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURHAN(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 26:105/q08/p3 They rejected all messengers or messengership outright. — Zajjāj allows a literal denial of all, as Wāḥidī also reports. Rāzī suggests they may have been unbelieving materialists or Brahmins. Nasafī says they rejected messengership altogether. Elmalılı speaks of the messengers sent to them. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 26:105/q08/p4 The plural includes Noah and prophets between him and Adam. — Ṭabrisī reports this from Abū Jaʿfar. [1 notes: TABRISI(أبو جعفر)]
- 26:105/q08/p5 They denied Noah's prophethood and his announcement of later messengers. — Wāḥidī reports al-Kalbī referring to Noah and what he told them about later messengers; Qurṭubī and Study Quran's Q report the same future-oriented explanation. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 26:105/q08/p6 A reported question asks why peoples with one messenger are described as denying messengers, but the excerpt gives no answer. — Baghawī records a question put to al-Ḥasan about Noah, ʿĀd and Thamūd; the surviving note ends before his answer. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن البصري)]

## 26:105/q09 How is the later phrase about Noah's lowly followers read?  [* · readings]
turns on: Whether the later wording is read as the separated noun form or the connected past verb.
- 26:105/q09/p1 It is read أتباعك الأرذلون, with a separated initial alif, tāʾ, and nominative ʿayn. — Jishumī reports Yaʿqūb's reading and aligns it with Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: JISHUMI(يعقوب)]
- 26:105/q09/p2 It is read واتبعك, a connected past verb with doubled open tāʾ and no alif. — Jishumī associates this with Ibn Masʿūd and says Abū ʿUbayd and Abū Ḥātim chose it. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو عبيد وأبو حاتم)+]

## 26:105/q10 Why does Noah's story follow Moses and Abraham here?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes emphasize consolation, the age and outcome of trial, or the repeated pattern of prophetic messages and responses.
- 26:105/q10/p1 It consoles Muhammad amid rejection by recalling Noah's 950 years of preaching and his people's denial. — Rāzī identifies consolation for Muhammad after the Moses and Abraham accounts. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 26:105/q10/p2 The story moves back to Noah as the second father to show ancient trials, long respite and eventual universal punishment. — Biqāʿī emphasizes Noah's earlier time after Abraham in the narrative order. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 26:105/q10/p3 The sequence shows that the prophets' essential message and their peoples' responses recur. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes the turn from Moses and Abraham to Noah, most of whose people disbelieved and were drowned while Noah and believers were saved. Kuran Yolu says Noah, Moses and Abraham share essential principles and that their peoples' beliefs and responses are alike, citing 11:25 and 11:49. Iṣlāḥī says Abraham's story warns Muhammad's opponents. [4 notes: TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:105/q11 How does this verse's formula frame the surrounding accounts?  [* · links]
turns on: One note describes the opening formula across successive stories; another describes this section through its refrain.
- 26:105/q11/p1 The denial formula introduces parallel accounts of ʿĀd, Thamūd, Lot's people and the Thicket. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 26:123, 26:141, 26:160 and 26:176. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 26:105/q11/p2 The section recounts Noah's message, rejection and punishment, then closes with the repeated refrain. — Iṣlāḥī describes the section's arc and recurring close. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:105/q12 What does the Noah account teach about rejection and its consequences?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw attention to the rejecters' recurring disposition, their social aversion, or the narrated outcome.
- 26:105/q12/p1 Rejecters of prophetic truth show a recurring mentality and meet a recurring fate. — Iṣlāḥī connects rejection of Noah with rejection of every messenger and applies Abraham's warning to Muhammad's opponents. Kuran Yolu sees the peoples of Noah, Moses and Abraham responding similarly to the same essential religion. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 26:105/q12/p2 Noah's people resisted followers of low social standing. — Study Quran points to 26:111 as the story's emphasis. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 26:105/q12/p3 The people disbelieved and were drowned, while Noah and the believers were saved. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī summarizes the outcome of Noah's account. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 26:105/q13 Which other Qur'anic passages are connected with Noah's account here?  [* · links]
- 26:105/q13/p1 The account has parallels or earlier mentions across several Qur'anic passages. — Muqātil compares the denial in 54:9. Rāzī points to detailed prior accounts at 7:59 and 11:25. Abū Ḥayyān connects the one-messenger plural wording with 25:37. Ibn ʿĀshūr refers back to Noah among God's chosen at 3:33. Study Quran lists 7:59–64, 10:71–73, 11:25–48, 23:23–30, 37:75–82, 54:9–15 and 71:1–28. [5 notes: MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
