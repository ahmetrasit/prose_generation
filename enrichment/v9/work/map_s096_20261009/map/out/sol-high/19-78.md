# 19:78: 16 questions

## 19:78/q01 What does aṭṭalaʿa convey?  [أَطَّلَعَ · meaning]
turns on: Which usage of the root is being explained: ascent to a vantage point or its wider senses of appearing and extent.
- 19:78/q01/p1 It evokes climbing or looking over a height, extended to gaining access to or mastery of a matter. — Al-Kashshāf cites Jarīr's line on ascending a mountain and allows mastery of a matter; al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Samīn relay the mountain image. Al-Nasafī and al-Bursawī mention a summit or pass. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls iṭṭalaʿa an intensified form of ṭalaʿa and compares 37:54–55 and 18:18. Al-Ālūsī says the ascent image implies possession of hidden knowledge, more than ordinary knowing. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 19:78/q01/p2 The root can describe a sudden appearance or the visible extent or capacity of something. — Al-Jishumī says ṭalaʿa ʿalayya describes someone suddenly appearing and relates the root to what is seen or where an affair comes to; he quotes a Prophetic use of ṭilāʿ al-arḍ for all the gold the earth could contain. [2 notes: JISHUMI]

## 19:78/q02 How is the initial hamza of aṭṭalaʿa formed and read?  [أَطَّلَعَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the interrogative hamza is pronounced or understood from am in connected speech.
- 19:78/q02/p1 The opening hamza asks a question; the original connecting hamza drops before it. — Al-Naḥḥās calls the question a rebuke. Al-Jishumī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, al-Samīn, and al-Bursawī describe the omitted hamzat al-waṣl; al-Qurṭubī cites following am and explains that fatḥa versus declarative kasra removes the need for lengthening, unlike 6:143. Abū Ḥayyān also links the interrogative to am. [9 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]
- 19:78/q02/p2 A connected reading omits the question hamza and begins with kasra; am still signals the question. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn cite a poetic question with the omitted hamza; al-Ālūsī likewise says am indicates its presence. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:78/q03 How does the aṭṭalaʿa question fit the preceding sentence?  [أَطَّلَعَ ٱلْغَيْبَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the question completes raʾayta or begins an independent rebuttal.
- 19:78/q03/p1 The interrogative clause is the second object of the preceding raʾayta. — Al-Ṭabrisī makes the relative clause its first object and this question its second. [1 notes: TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 19:78/q03/p2 It is a new sentence that rebukes the boast. — Al-Ālūsī judges the second-object analysis unlikely and treats the question as fresh speech. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]

## 19:78/q04 What is the grammatical object of iṭṭalaʿa here?  [أَطَّلَعَ ٱلْغَيْبَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the verb itself licenses the direct object or an omitted preposition must be supplied.
- 19:78/q04/p1 Al-ghayb is a direct object; the verb can be directly transitive. — Al-Samīn rejects an implied preposition. Al-Ālūsī says ṭalaʿa can take a direct object though it also occurs with ʿalā. Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise rejects the missing-preposition account, while observing that the sense 'look over' often takes ʿalā in 37:54–55 and 18:18. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 19:78/q04/p2 Al-ghayb is governed by an unspoken preposition. — Al-Samīn and Ibn ʿĀshūr mention this analysis only to reject it; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes the regular use of ʿalā with 'look over.' [0 notes: SAMIN-DURR-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 19:78/q05 What unseen reality does the question challenge him to know?  [ٱلْغَيْبَ · referent]
turns on: Whether ghayb names the general hidden realm, its alleged written source, or the particular future outcome of his boast.
- 19:78/q05/p1 His future fate, especially whether Paradise or afterlife wealth and children await him. — Muqātil and al-Ṭabarī connect it to the boast of wealth and children. Mujāhid and reports from Ibn ʿAbbās ask whether he knows he will be in Paradise; al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Nasafī transmit that focus. Ibn Kathīr says he cannot swear to his afterlife allotment. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes seeing new wealth and children there from seeing his present family accompany him. Asad calls it the unknowable future, and Study Quran specifies next-world realities. [17 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; THALABI(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 19:78/q05/p2 The Preserved Tablet, which would disclose the hidden outcome. — Ibn ʿAbbās is cited by al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Ālūsī; al-Wāḥidī attributes this to al-Kalbī. Al-Ṭabrisī says al-Kalbī has him learn of future wealth and children there. Al-Dāmghānī gives the Tablet as the sense of al-ghayb; Ibn al-Jawzī's lexical work compares 52:41. Al-Nasafī couples the Tablet with seeing one's destiny. [10 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 19:78/q05/p3 What is hidden from human beings or lies beyond their ordinary knowledge. — Ibn al-Jawzī defines ghayb as what is concealed and notes related usages for sunset, an absent husband, a hollow, and a thicket. Study Quran includes what exceeds sensory knowledge and conjecture, citing 6:59; it says God alone has the keys and knowledge of it, though He may disclose some to prophets or others, citing 27:65. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; STUDYQURAN]
- 19:78/q05/p4 Knowledge that God will grant him wealth despite unbelief, or that He has already granted it despite unbelief. — Al-Māwardī offers these two temporal ways of construing the boast; Qatāda's report in al-Durr describes God showing the man his coming wealth. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 19:78/q06 What does it mean to take a covenant with the Merciful?  [ٱتَّخَذَ عِندَ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ عَهْدًا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether covenant denotes earned religious standing, a specific practice, or an actual promise about Paradise or the boasted gifts.
- 19:78/q06/p1 Professing God's oneness or the testimony that there is no god but God. — Muqātil, Ibn ʿAbbās, and others explain the testimony as grounds for hoping for mercy or reward. Al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī mention it as an alternative; al-Nasafī reports it. The reports range from simple monotheism to the full testimony. [16 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(بعضهم); THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة والثوري); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,محمد بن كعب القرظي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 19:78/q06/p2 A righteous deed already performed, giving him reason to hope for reward. — Qatāda repeatedly uses a deed sent ahead; al-Ṭabarī says it would be evidence from prior righteous conduct. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Durr, and al-Ālūsī relate this to expected reward. [16 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); RAZI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة والثوري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 19:78/q06/p3 A direct divine promise or binding guarantee of the wealth and children he claimed. — Ibn ʿĪsā speaks of a statement God promised; al-Zajjāj selects a received covenant. Al-Kashshāf, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Kathīr, al-Bukhārī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr frame it as a guarantee of the claimed gifts. Al-Ālūsī says the literal formulation rhetorically grants the sinner's wording for the sake of challenge. [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكلبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BUKHARI; ALUSI-FULL(الكلبي,قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج)+]
- 19:78/q06/p4 God's promise to admit him to Paradise. — Al-Kalbī or Ibn al-Sāʾib is cited for admission to the Garden; Study Quran reports the same. This differs from a pledge of wealth and children there. [7 notes: THALABI(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); TABRISI(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة والثوري); STUDYQURAN(Ṭs)]
- 19:78/q06/p5 Faith joined with righteous action or proper obedience to God. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya joins faith and good works. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Bursawī say the promised reward for testimony and action resembles a covenant. Al-Wāḥidī reports al-Suddī's 'obedience'; Study Quran spells it out as belief, obeying commands, and avoiding prohibitions. Al-Biqāʿī says proper obedience earns a covenant. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 19:78/q06/p6 Completion of the five prescribed prayers creates a covenant for Paradise. — A Prophetic report in Yaḥyā says full performance brings a covenant to enter Paradise while deficient performance leaves punishment or forgiveness to God. Al-Wāḥidī's version stresses prayer on time without neglect and explicitly applies it to this question. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(النبي)]

## 19:78/q07 Why does the verse say the covenant is 'with the Merciful'?  [عِندَ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the wording's force lies in the spatial picture of a stored pact or the appeal to mercy.
- 19:78/q07/p1 It pictures the pledge as a document securely kept with God. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares written pacts deposited in a known safe place and mentions the pact against Banū Hāshim. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 19:78/q07/p2 The divine name invokes mercy as the alleged ground for the promised grant. — Al-Ālūsī says the literal-covenant wording concedes the sinner's claim rhetorically and presents divine mercy as its supposed basis. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الكلبي)]

## 19:78/q08 What is the force of the two questions together?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the speaker's claim is treated as an asserted expectation requiring proof or merely a taunt.
- 19:78/q08/p1 They deny the boast: certainty about future gifts would require unseen knowledge or a covenant from its Knower, and he has neither. — Al-Zajjāj compares the same boast in 18:36. Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursawī, al-Biqāʿī, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī make these the two possible grounds. Ibn ʿĀshūr says taking the speaker's words literally ridicules his claim; al-Ālūsī stresses its outrageousness. Yaḥyā states the negative answers, and Study Quran compares unfounded expectations in 2:80 and 53:35. [16 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI-]

## 19:78/q09 What occasion or speaker is reported for this challenge?  [* · reports]
turns on: The transmitted identity and wording of the debtor, and whether mockery coheres with the passage's argument.
- 19:78/q09/p1 Al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil owed Khabbāb money and, when asked to pay, demanded disbelief in Muhammad before promising payment from afterlife wealth and children. — Khabbāb's report appears in Aḥmad, Bukhārī, Ibn Kathīr, Yaḥyā, and al-Qurṭubī; Yaḥyā says Khabbāb informed the Prophet. Al-Qurṭubī cites Muslim and Bukhārī and prefers this sound-collection account. Al-Kalbī and Muqātil add that Khabbāb was a blacksmith who made jewelry; al-Nasafī mentions Paradise's gold and silver. Bukhārī 4735 also mentions punishment and solitary return. Al-Bayḍāwī reports the same setting. [12 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(خباب بن الأرت); MUSNAD-AHMAD(خباب); QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل,خباب); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(خباب بن الأرت); BUKHARI(خباب); TABATABAI(الأكثر); NASAFI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن)+; TABATABAI-]
- 19:78/q09/p2 A polytheist debtor promised a Companion payment in Paradise from the wealth and children he expected there. — Qatāda's report in Yaḥyā leaves the debtor and Companion unnamed and describes the challenge as asking whether he had seen the unseen. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة)]
- 19:78/q09/p3 The verses were revealed about al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra. — Al-Ḥasan is cited by al-Qurṭubī and al-Nasafī; both sources favor or identify the better-known al-ʿĀṣ occasion instead. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports both names as majority attributions. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL(الحسن); TABATABAI(الأكثر); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن)-; NASAFI-FULL-]
- 19:78/q09/p4 The reported mockery setting does not fit the passage's serious argument. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says a resurrection denier's taunt needs no belief in its premise, making an appeal to unseen knowledge irrelevant; he rejects the report's fit with the context. [2 notes: TABATABAI+]

## 19:78/q10 What does the challenge teach beyond this one boast?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the emphasis is the claimant's unwarranted certainty or God's exclusive knowledge.
- 19:78/q10/p1 One must not claim knowledge or assured afterlife reward without grounds. — Al-Jishumī condemns claims about what one does not know. Study Quran extends the challenge to confident expectations of bliss or escape from punishment and cites 2:80 and 53:35. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says unbelief supplies no basis for certainty of reward. [3 notes: JISHUMI; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI]
- 19:78/q10/p2 God uniquely knows the unseen, though He may disclose some of it to chosen people. — Study Quran cites 6:59 and 27:65 for God's keys and exclusive knowledge, while allowing disclosure to prophets or others; al-Biqāʿī says access exceeds creatures, and Abū Ḥayyān describes God's unique unseen knowledge. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 19:78/q11 What readings of walad are noted in the surrounding boast?  [* · readings]
turns on: The vowels of the word and which occurrences receive the rounded form.
- 19:78/q11/p1 Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī read wuld with rounded wāw and still lām at six places. — Al-Ṭabrisī counts four in Maryam plus al-Zukhruf and Nūḥ; al-Bayḍāwī describes it as plural or dialect variation. [2 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 19:78/q11/p2 Basrans, Ibn Kathīr, and Khalaf use the rounded form only in Sūrat Nūḥ. — Al-Ṭabrisī distinguishes this distribution from the six-place reading. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 19:78/q11/p3 The remaining readers use fatḥa on both wāw and lām throughout the Qurʾān. — Al-Ṭabrisī gives this distribution. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 19:78/q12 Can walad in the surrounding boast denote one child or several?  [* · meaning]
turns on: Whether the proverb and poetry establish singular usage.
- 19:78/q12/p1 It may be singular or plural. — Al-Farrāʾ cites a Banū Asad proverb for singular use. Abū ʿAlī rejects the plural-only restriction, using the poetic walad ḥimār and comparing fulk as another singular/plural form. [3 notes: TABRISI(أبو علي,الفراء); TABRISI(أبو علي)+]
- 19:78/q12/p2 It is only plural. — Muʿādh al-Khurashī holds this restriction, although his cited poetic line appears to use the term for one child; Abū ʿAlī rejects the restriction. [1 notes: TABRISI(معاذ الخرشي); TABRISI(أبو علي)-]

## 19:78/q13 How does kallā following this challenge work?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether kallā looks back to deny the claim or forward to alert and affirm the next statement.
- 19:78/q13/p1 It rejects and rebukes the preceding boast, with force like 'no' or 'that is not so.' — Al-Zajjāj calls it rebuke, restraint, and alert. Al-Aʿshā's verse shows a rebuking reply. Al-Burhān contrasts it with affirmative ī and cites 19:81 and 96:19; al-Khalīl, Sībawayh, al-Akhfash, al-Mubarrad, and al-Zajjāj analyze an implied 'the matter is not so.' Al-Itqān counts thirty-three kallā occurrences and seven unanimously rebuking ones, citing 19:82 and 26:15. [9 notes: TABRISI(أبو حاتم,الأعشى,الزجاج); BURHAN(الخليل); ITQAN]
- 19:78/q13/p2 It can introduce an alert or emphatic confirmation attached to what follows. — Al-Farrāʾ compares kallā wa-rabbi al-kaʿbah. Abū Ḥātim gives an attention-marker sense akin to illā and a new-statement example at 96:6 after 96:5. Al-Burhān says other contexts use it for ḥaqqan, an oath adjunct, or confirmation about judgment and records, citing 74:32, 89:21, and 83:7,15,18; it says 104:3 also admits either sense. Al-Ṭabrisī says some listed examples allow both analyses. [8 notes: TABRISI(أبو حاتم,الفراء); BURHAN]

## 19:78/q14 Where may a reciter pause around the following kallā?  [* · readings]
turns on: The backward rebuke versus forward confirmation analysis of kallā.
- 19:78/q14/p1 Pause on kallā when it rebukes the claim, then restart after it. — Al-Ṭabrisī permits a stop in the rebuke analysis and lists this among a first class of difficult stops, alongside examples of return, partners, gardens, scripture, and wealth preserving life. Al-Burhān prefers pausing at a rebuke and says this first class can permit a stop before and a restart after kallā. [5 notes: TABRISI; BURHAN; BURHAN+]
- 19:78/q14/p2 Do not pause on kallā; begin with it or connect it to what follows when it alerts or confirms. — Abū al-ʿAbbās says its answer is completed by what follows. Al-Ṭabrisī says restart at it under the alert or affirmation sense; al-Burhān prefers beginning with it for confirmation. Al-Farrāʾ's oath comparison supports forward connection. [6 notes: TABRISI(أبو العباس,الفراء); BURHAN; BURHAN+]

## 19:78/q15 What does the nearby use of kallā indicate about the sūra's setting?  [* · sciences]
- 19:78/q15/p1 A sūra containing kallā is Meccan. — Al-Burhān says kallā carries threat and warning and therefore occurs only in Meccan sūras. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 19:78/q16 What other adjacent lexical comparisons are recorded?  [* · meaning]
turns on: The two supplied lexical notes concern different expressions and neither gives a direct explanation of 19:78.
- 19:78/q16/p1 A truncated lexical entry glosses fitnah as associationism but supplies no verse example in the excerpt. — Al-Dāmghānī's cited entry cuts off before its example, so its connection to this verse cannot be determined from the supplied note. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]
- 19:78/q16/p2 A separate discussion treats dhālika and hādhā as counterparts to negation, and kadhālika as a counterpart to kallā. — Al-Burhān cites a following conjunction as evidence for an implied prior clause and points to 25:32 and 35:43; the supplied note does not tie this point directly to 19:78.
