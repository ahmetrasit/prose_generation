# 31:27: 31 questions

## 31:27/q01 What does law imply in this supposition?  [وَلَوْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether an unreal condition entails that the consequent fails without it.
- 31:27/q01/p1 It links an immense hypothetical to non-exhaustion without implying exhaustion otherwise. — Sībawayh, Ibn Mālik, Burhān and Ibn ʿĀshūr separate the conditional link from independent truth; Burhān says fewer supplies strengthen the denial. Al-Samīn rejects the simple counterfactual slogan. [18 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(سيبويه); ITQAN(ابن مالك,سيبويه); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 31:27/q01/p2 It only joins past condition and result without asserting either's failure. — Al-Itqān reports this first view and Ibn Hishām's objection; al-Zamakhsharī links verbal propositions. [2 notes: BURHAN(الزمخشري); ITQAN(أصحاب القول الأول)]
- 31:27/q01/p3 It conventionally negates the result when its condition fails. — Al-Itqān reports the widespread account but notes this verse as a difficulty; Burhān tests it against Suhayb's obedience and 8:23. [1 notes: ITQAN(جمهور النحاة)]

## 31:27/q02 How does annamā function after law?  [أَنَّمَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether law licenses a nominal predicate and whether an existential predicate is supplied.
- 31:27/q02/p1 The anna clause has mā as noun and aqlām as nominal predicate. — Sībawayh says annamā can stand where anna can, unlike independent innamā; al-Mubarrad makes the clause a subject of implied existence, while others move an omitted predicate before or after. Al-Ṣaffār and poems support the construction. [20 notes: SIBAWAYH; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المبرد,غيره); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(الصفار); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عصفور,المبرد,سيبويه,قيل)]
- 31:27/q02/p2 A nominal predicate after law anna is valid and refutes a strict verbal requirement. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn cite common Arabic and poems; Ibn al-Ḥājib concurs. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); BURHAN(أبو حيان); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]
- 31:27/q02/p3 A verbal predicate is required only with a derived predicate; aqlām is outside that rule. — Al-Ālūsī narrows al-Zamakhsharī's rule and excludes a wish construction at 33:20. [3 notes: BURHAN(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]
- 31:27/q02/p4 The noun after law is subject to an omitted existential verb. — Burhān reports the Kufan estimate 'if it were established that.' [1 notes: BURHAN(الكوفيون)]

## 31:27/q03 What does min shajaratin specify?  [مِن شَجَرَةٍ · grammar]
turns on: Whether specification attaches to mā or its locative pronoun.
- 31:27/q03/p1 It specifies mā or its locative pronoun and encompasses the whole class of earthly trees. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī give these grammatical analyses; al-Ālūsī compares an indefinite in a conditional and Ibn ʿAbbās's date-and-locust analogy. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 31:27/q04 Why is shajarah singular?  [مِن شَجَرَةٍ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the singular distributes each tree or names the whole class.
- 31:27/q04/p1 It distributes the picture tree by tree until none is omitted. — Al-Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Rāzī, Biqāʿī and Bursevī stress exhaustive individual treatment. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 31:27/q04/p2 It denotes the class; each tree can yield many pens from its branches. — Abū Ḥayyān cites indefinite singular parallels at 2:106 and 35:2; al-Ālūsī objects that a lone singular does not formally mean 'tree by tree' and one pen per tree would weaken the image; Ibn ʿĀshūr describes suitable branches. [9 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,إبن عباس)]

## 31:27/q05 What is aqlām?  [أَقْلَٰمٌۭ · meaning]
- 31:27/q05/p1 Pens are cut and sharpened writing tools, potentially many from each tree. — Al-Thaʿlabī describes trimmed branches; Bursevī derives qalam from cutting and distinguishes kinds of cutting; Ibn ʿĀshūr describes a split reed. Al-Ālūsī calls aqlām formally a paucity plural because qilām is rare. [7 notes: THALABI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 31:27/q05/p2 No parallel supply of pens is needed, because one pen writes many books. — Al-Rāzī contrasts quickly consumed ink with a pen's long use; al-Biqāʿī infers omitted ink and more trees. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 31:27/q06 Which sea is intended?  [وَٱلْبَحْرُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the article points to the known ocean, all seas, or fresh water.
- 31:27/q06/p1 The sea generically, including Earth's seas. — Al-Rāzī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī make the first sea generic; al-Ṭībī takes its article as replacing Earth's possessive pronoun. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); TABATABAI]
- 31:27/q06/p2 The great encircling ocean. — Bursevī describes its inlets, depth, islands, jinn cities and Iblīs's throne; al-Ālūsī prefers the known greatest sea. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 31:27/q06/p3 Fresh water that grows trees for pens. — Abū ʿUbaydah argues salt water cannot grow the pens; Majāz notes inland country may also be called a sea. [2 notes: MAJAZ; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة)]
- 31:27/q06/p4 Bahr lexically denotes broad or abundant water and can have figurative uses. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites a broad-striding horse, a prophetic use for abundance, briny water poetry, bāḥir for a fool and baḥrah for a locality. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(الأموي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 31:27/q07 How is al-baḥr read?  [وَٱلْبَحْرُ · readings]
turns on: Which transmitted case or wording is followed.
- 31:27/q07/p1 Accusative al-baḥra, attributed chiefly to Abū ʿAmr and Yaʿqūb. — Ibn Mujāhid says Abū ʿAmr alone in his list; al-Nashr names two Basrans, while other accounts add Ibn Abī Isḥāq and ʿĪsā. Al-Ṭabarī accepts both cases. [14 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(بعضهم); IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو); THALABI(ابن أبي إسحاق وأبو عمرو ويعقوب); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عمرو، يعقوب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو وابن أبي إسحاق وعيسى); TABRISI(أبو عمرو ويعقوب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمرو); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو وابن إسحاق); NASAFI-FULL(أبو عمرو ويعقوب); NASHR(البصريان); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البصريان); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو عمرو ويعقوب)]
- 31:27/q07/p2 Nominative al-baḥru, the majority reading. — Ibn Mujāhid and al-Fārisī name the majority; Ibn ʿUmar reports prophetic nominative recitation. Al-Ṭabarī accepts both cases. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNMUJAHID(الباقون); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وعاصم وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي); JISHUMI(الباقون); BAGHAWI-FULL(الباقون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير ونافع وعاصم وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي); DURR-FULL(ابن عمر)]
- 31:27/q07/p3 Indefinite nominative wa-baḥrun is read by Ibn Masʿūd and Ṭalḥah. — Ibn Jinnī supplies 'there is a sea'; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn allow a circumstantial wāw; al-Ālūsī prefers the stated verb as predicate. [8 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(ابن مسعود وطلحة بن مصرف); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود); TABRISI(ابن مسعود وطلحة بن مصرف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله); SAMIN-DURR(عبد الله); ALUSI-FULL(عبدالله)]
- 31:27/q07/p4 Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad reads 'the sea is its ink' (madāduhu). — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah calls madād a verbal noun; al-Ṭabrisī rejects taking seawater merely as addition to other ink. [7 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(جعفر بن محمد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جعفر بن محمد); TABRISI(جعفر بن محمد); QURTUBI-FULL(جعفر بن محمد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(جعفر بن محمد); ALUSI-FULL(جعفر بن محمد)]

## 31:27/q08 How is the sea clause parsed?  [وَٱلْبَحْرُ يَمُدُّهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether it is circumstantial or coordinated within the law clause.
- 31:27/q08/p1 It is a new circumstantial nominal clause with al-baḥr as subject and yamudduhu as predicate. — Sībawayh estimates 'the sea is in this state'; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī prefer wāw al-ḥāl, with no linking pronoun required. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it supplies the simultaneous ink state. [20 notes: SIBAWAYH; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(سيبويه); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 31:27/q08/p2 Accusative al-baḥr coordinates with mā, the noun of anna. — Sībawayh gives the verse as an example. Ibn al-Ḥājib says yamudduhu must predicate the sea if no other predicate is given; al-Ṭabrisī prefers elided 'ink' as predicate and makes the verb circumstantial. [11 notes: SIBAWAYH; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(ابن الحاجب)]
- 31:27/q08/p3 Nominative al-baḥr coordinates with the syntactic position or sense of anna. — Ibn Jinnī cites 9:3; al-Zamakhsharī permits it. Abū Ḥayyān says it depends on a disputed subject parse after law, while al-Ālūsī questions crossing an omitted predicate. [16 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض النحويين); TABRISI(ابن جني); QURTUBI-FULL(بعض النحويين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ,سيبويه); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,الزمخشري)]
- 31:27/q08/p4 Accusative al-baḥr depends on an omitted verb explained by yamudduhu. — Bayḍāwī, al-Samīn and Qiraat-ER report this; al-Ālūsī also mentions an omitted thābit. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR(المبرد); ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]
- 31:27/q08/p5 The sea clause implicitly means 'earth's sea' to provide a possessive link. — Al-Zamakhsharī proposes this; Abū Ḥayyān objects that the nominal circumstantial clause needs no such link and the article-for-pronoun parse is Kufan. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]

## 31:27/q09 How is yamudduhu read?  [يَمُدُّهُۥ · readings]
turns on: Whether the verb is from madda or amadda and whether the subject is treated as feminine.
- 31:27/q09/p1 Some read the causative form from amadda with ḍamma on the yāʾ. — Attributed variously to al-Aʿraj, al-Ḥasan, Ibn Hurmuz, Ibn Muṣarrif and Ibn Masʿūd; Ibn Jinnī compares army reinforcements and rivers, citing 8:9. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it loses the inkwell nuance. [10 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(الأعرج والحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن بن أبي الحسن); TABRISI(الحسن والأعرج); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن هرمز والحسن); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الحسن وابن هرمز وابن مصرف); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود والحسن وابن مصرف وابن هرمز)]
- 31:27/q09/p2 Some read feminine tamudduhu. — Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿAbbās or Ubayy are named; the seven seas are construed as feminine subject. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(عبد الله وأبي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود وأبي,الحسن)]
- 31:27/q09/p3 The majority reads masculine yamudduhu from madda. — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah calls it the common reading; Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies its vowels. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس)]

## 31:27/q10 What does yamudduhu mean here?  [يَمُدُّهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb evokes ink supply or a general extension.
- 31:27/q10/p1 Seven seas continually replenish the first as ink replenishes an inkpot. — Al-Yazīdī compares adding water to a pot; al-Zamakhsharī, Nasafī and al-Ālūsī use an inkwell image; al-Ālūsī notes the imperfect form, and Ibn ʿĀshūr says ink is implicit. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(اليزيدي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 31:27/q10/p2 Madda extends or adds gradually, while amadda denotes external assistance or supply. — Al-Māwardī, Abū Zayd, Ibn ʿAṭiyyah and al-Qurṭubī report distinctions, comparing rivers, wells, money and troops; Ibn Qutaybah connects this use to midād ink. [7 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو زيد); MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(أبو زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL(فرقة)]
- 31:27/q10/p3 Madda and amadda can be synonymous dialect variants. — Groups reported by Ibn ʿAṭiyyah and al-Qurṭubī, and Ibn al-Shaykh, see no material difference. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); QURTUBI-FULL(فرقة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن الشيخ)]

## 31:27/q11 What does the pronoun in yamudduhu refer to?  [يَمُدُّهُۥ · referent]
- 31:27/q11/p1 The first sea. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Ṭabrisī make the antecedent explicit. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI]

## 31:27/q12 What does min baʿdihi mean?  [مِنۢ بَعْدِهِۦ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the relation is spatial/additive or temporal.
- 31:27/q12/p1 From behind or in addition to the first sea. — Majāz pictures seven seas flowing into the first; al-Baghawī says behind and additional; al-Ālūsī allows this. [3 notes: MAJAZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 31:27/q12/p2 After the first sea's ink has been used up. — Al-Ālūsī presents this as an alternative. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 31:27/q13 What are the seven added seas?  [سَبْعَةُ أَبْحُرٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether they are imagined equivalents or particular physical waters.
- 31:27/q13/p1 Actual seas or rivers identified in reports. — Ibn ʿAmr reports alternating seven fire and water seas below the sea; Bursevī cites China, Tibet, India, Sind, Persia, East and West, and separately suggests seven named rivers; al-Irshād speaks of mountain-fed waterways. No grading is stated. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(عبد الله بن عمرو); BURSEVI(أسئلة الحكم,الإرشاد,القاموس)]
- 31:27/q13/p2 Imagined seas comparable to the first. — Al-Rāzī places them beyond existing seas; al-Ālūsī makes each match the ocean in extent and water; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī agrees. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 31:27/q13/p3 A literal seven seas surrounding the world is unconfirmed Israelite material. — Ibn Kathīr rejects deriving a fixed cosmography from the number. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 31:27/q14 Why say seven and abḥur?  [سَبْعَةُ أَبْحُرٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether seven is a limit or a sign of abundance.
- 31:27/q14/p1 Seven means great abundance and does not limit further supply. — Al-Rāzī says even a thousand would not change the point; Ibn Kathīr invokes 18:109's repeated supply. Al-Ālūsī cites heavens, planets, regions and days and the believer-one/unbeliever-seven saying; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it idiomatic. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 31:27/q14/p2 Abḥur is a paucity plural suited to the numeral seven. — Burhān, Abū Ḥayyān, Biqāʿī and al-Ālūsī note grammatical matching even where the image indicates abundance. [3 notes: BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL]
- 31:27/q14/p3 Seven is a customary complete count with symbolic extensions. — Al-Rāzī derives it from seven climes, days and planets, three as minimal completeness and seven as the larger part of ten; he compares ritual threes, Hell's seven gates, Paradise's eight and al-Farrāʾ's 'wāw of eight.' [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(الفراء,النبي عليه السلام)]

## 31:27/q15 What action is left implicit?  [أَقْلَٰمٌۭ وَٱلْبَحْرُ كَلِمَٰتُ ٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
- 31:27/q15/p1 The pens and seas write God's words as ink and writing materials, then run out first. — Abū ʿUbaydah calls it compressed; al-Ṭabarī says pens break and ink dries. Al-Fārisī, al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī compare other omitted actions at 26:63, 2:196 and 27:28–29; al-Biqāʿī and Islahi note omitted ink. [16 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عبيدة); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(أبو عبيدة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(أبو الفتح); ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 31:27/q16 What is the response to law?  [مَّا نَفِدَتْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā naf idat is itself the answer.
- 31:27/q16/p1 Mā naf idat is the expressed answer. — Burhān says denial amid great supply proves denial with less; al-Ālūsī calls it the jawāb. [4 notes: BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL]
- 31:27/q16/p2 The answer about exhausted materials is omitted before the stated denial. — Burhān offers this as an alternative, with possible omitted conjunction. [2 notes: BURHAN]

## 31:27/q17 What does naf idat mean?  [مَّا نَفِدَتْ · meaning]
- 31:27/q17/p1 To reach an end through exhaustion over time. — Al-Māwardī contrasts it with perishing all at once; al-Zajjāj applies the denial to God's words. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL]

## 31:27/q18 What are God's kalimāt?  [كَلِمَٰتُ ٱللَّهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the term refers to knowledge, creative acts, eternal speech, revelation or promises.
- 31:27/q18/p1 God's decrees, possible creations, signs and wonders brought about by Be. — Al-Rāzī argues cause-for-effect naming and cites Jesus as a word; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 36:82 and 4:171; Islahi says signs manifest the creative command. Al-Ālūsī's Imam prefers this connection to 31:26. [21 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن); FARISI-HUJJA; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو علي); TABRISI(أبو علي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو علي,القفال); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن البصري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,قيل); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 31:27/q18/p2 God's limitless knowledge, known realities and wisdom. — Muqātil and al-Suddī include wonders; al-Naḥḥās details kinds, parts, qualities and change; al-Wāḥidī reasons limitless knowledge entails limitless expressing words. Abū ʿAlī distinguishes still-possible things from those already existing. [16 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); FARISI-HUJJA(قتادة); SULAMI(ابن عطاء); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي,السدي,مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جعفر النحاس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 31:27/q18/p3 God's eternal speech and meanings, including utterances, revelation and creative commands. — Al-Qushayrī contrasts finite writers with eternal word; Ibn ʿĀshūr includes speech to angels and prophets and the command Be. Al-Rāzī distinguishes created letters and arrangement from divine attributes. [2 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 31:27/q18/p4 God's favors for the obedient, or promised reward and threatened punishment. — Al-Māwardī reports Paradise favors; Abū Muslim extends this to unseen reward and punishment. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم)]
- 31:27/q18/p5 The Qur'an and further revelation to Muhammad. — Qatādah's reported occasion answers a prediction that recitation will soon end; Ibn ʿĀshūr says this reading implies much more revelation. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 31:27/q18/p6 Kalimāt has other Quranic senses outside this verse. — Al-Dāmghānī lists rites, Adam's received words, tawḥīd, creative wonders, Jesus, religion and the Quran. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]

## 31:27/q19 Why use the plural kalimāt?  [كَلِمَٰتُ ٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the small plural has force here.
- 31:27/q19/p1 Even a small number of God's words exceeds these supplies, all the more His total speech. — Al-Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Biqāʿī, Bursevī and al-Ālūsī make the a fortiori claim; al-Samīn invokes Ḥassān's jufanāt. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,حسان); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 31:27/q19/p2 A sound plural in a general construction need not mean a small number. — Al-Shaykh objects to the strict paucity reading; al-Samīn notes debate about whether the article marks generality. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)]

## 31:27/q20 What does the image establish?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the comparison asserts a precise quantity or simply proves inexhaustibility.
- 31:27/q20/p1 Finite materials, pages, writers and lives cannot exhaust divine words, knowledge, decrees or signs. — Muqātil says all creation's pens would fail; al-Ḥasan says the writer dies; Ibn ʿAṭiyyah says adding more cannot establish a limit. Burhān says non-exhaustion holds before and after ink is spent; Islahi says science has only found a drop. [32 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(عمرو); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو الجوزاء); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 31:27/q20/p2 The wording denies exhaustion without asserting a precise limit on the imagined materials. — Burhān reports careful scholars using al-Khiḍr's bird-and-sea analogy; Ibn ʿAṭiyyah also rejects a bound beyond the stated seas. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURHAN(بعض المحققين)]

## 31:27/q21 What follows about divine speech?  [كَلِمَٰتُ ٱللَّهِ · theology]
turns on: Whether kalimāt must denote eternal speech or can denote limitless referents.
- 31:27/q21/p1 God's speech is eternal and uncreated, so its meanings do not end. — Al-Thaʿlabī infers uncreatedness; al-Qurṭubī prefers eternal speech on contextual grounds; Bursevī infers Quranic pre-eternity; al-Rāzī distinguishes divine attribute from created letters. [6 notes: THALABI; QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري); BURSEVI]
- 31:27/q21/p2 Limitless knowledge and possibilities can be called words without treating them as eternal speech. — Abū ʿAlī confines some referents to future possibilities; Ibn ʿAṭiyyah observes this tends toward Muʿtazilī created-speech doctrine; al-Fārisī reports a wisdom-and-workmanship reading. [2 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(بعض أهل النظر); IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 31:27/q22 What do Mighty and Wise contribute?  [عَزِيزٌ حَكِيمٌۭ · interpretation]
- 31:27/q22/p1 God cannot be thwarted and nothing escapes His knowledge and wise governance, so His words do not end. — Muqātil says mighty in kingdom and wise in command; Ibn Kathīr extends wisdom through creation, law and speech. Ibn ʿĀshūr reads the closure against hopes of prophetic failure; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī links it to God's sole governance; Islahi links unity, prophethood and resurrection. [17 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 31:27/q23 What occasion is reported?  [* · reports]

## 31:27/q24 Where was the verse revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the Jewish exchange happened after migration or through Meccan intermediaries.
- 31:27/q24/p1 In Medina, as an exception to the Meccan sūra. — ʿAṭāʾ and Ibn ʿAbbās are cited; reports vary between two and three exceptional verses beginning here. Ibn ʿAṭiyyah prefers Medina. Ibn ʿĀshūr says prophetic direction then fixed its place. [9 notes: THALABI(عطاء بن يسار); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء بن يسار); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطاء); ITQAN(ابن عباس)]
- 31:27/q24/p2 In Mecca, whether as part of the sūra or after Jews instructed Quraysh. — Ibn Kathīr prefers the familiar Meccan classification despite Medinan reports; Ibn ʿĀshūr says a Meccan account fits its revealed sequence. [2 notes: THALABI(غيره); IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 31:27/q25 How does it connect to the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Which surrounding argument it advances.
- 31:27/q25/p1 It extends claims of divine ownership, knowledge and sole governance to limitless dominion and creative commands. — Al-Rāzī says 31:26 could seem finite; Ibn ʿĀshūr links hidden knowledge at 31:16 and 31:23; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī links exclusive governance; Biqāʿī connects resurrection wisdom. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(أصحاب التأويل من السلف من أصحاب ابن عباس); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 31:27/q25/p2 It supports the sūra's argument for revelation against Quranic opponents. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites the sūra's opening and 31:6–7; Ibn ʿĀshūr follows the theme to 31:34; Islahi says abundant signs answer demands for more. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 31:27/q25/p3 It offers signs of power and unity against polytheism. — Al-Qaffāl and al-Qurṭubī read the wonders as proofs; Islahi says their abundance leaves no excuse for idol worship. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 31:27/q26 How does it relate to 18:109?  [* · links]
- 31:27/q26/p1 The sea-as-ink image there likewise states inexhaustibility. — Many commentators cite 18:109; Ibn Kathīr reads its 'another like it' as unlimited repeated supply. Ibn ʿĀshūr links Luqmān with Cave and Dhū l-Qarnayn narratives, and distinguishes completion of words in 6:115 as fulfillment. [9 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 31:27/q27 How much is human knowledge beside God's?  [* · interpretation]
- 31:27/q27/p1 Much by a human measure, yet little beside God's knowledge. — ʿIkrimah says Torah wisdom suffices for salvation; al-Rabīʿ compares human knowledge to a drop beside seas. Ibn ʿĀshūr says God wisely discloses some knowledge and withholds other knowledge. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); DURR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 31:27/q28 What inward lesson is drawn?  [* · inward]
- 31:27/q28/p1 Wisdom can flow without end from God's inexhaustible treasury. — Abū Saʿīd al-Kharrāz describes sages' speech; Bursevī adds knowers receiving insight in a flash and finite pages unable to contain eternal meanings. [4 notes: SULAMI(أبو سعيد الخراز); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 31:27/q28/p2 God's pre-eternal words invite the servant to love and felicity. — Al-Junayd links continuous blessings with love, divine choosing and future felicity. [1 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد)]

## 31:27/q29 What broader examples illustrate inexhaustibility?  [* · interpretation]
- 31:27/q29/p1 Natural research, medicine, mercy and the afterlife far exceed human inquiry. — Al-Rāzī surveys bodies, minerals, plants, animals, illnesses and medicines; reports two ungraded Galen dream narratives; compares human time to God's domain and urges lasting deeds. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL(جالينوس)]
- 31:27/q29/p2 Worldly power passes and invites ascetic detachment. — Bursevī quotes ʿAṭṭār on death and Ibrāhīm ibn Adham. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الشيخ العطار)]

## 31:27/q30 How is the verse used in discussion of Quranic inimitability?  [* · rhetoric]
- 31:27/q30/p1 Its composition and meanings exceed human learned rhetorical craft. — Al-Bāqillānī contrasts teachable meter and badīʿ with consistent Quranic excellence; Burhān says varied retellings avoid tedium, with addition, omission and rearrangement; al-Rāghib says a short Quran holds uncountable meaning. [9 notes: BAQILLANI; BURHAN; ITQAN(الراغب)]
- 31:27/q30/p2 Its rhetoric calls for whole-Quran comparison of words, style and context. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ describes her lexical and comparative method, influence of her father and al-Khūlī, and the Arab eloquence miracle alongside Moses' and Jesus' signs. [12 notes: BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 31:27/q30/p3 The seven Ḥawāmīm emphasize revelation from the Mighty, Wise God. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes their consecutive revelation and canonical order, Ḥā Mīm openings, al-Shūrā's added letters and Arabic revelation formula. [2 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 31:27/q31 What ancillary comparisons do commentators make?  [* · links]
- 31:27/q31/p1 Lexical and poetic comparisons illuminate broad Arabic usage. — Ibn Jinnī cites dialectal ṣād/sīn/zāy variation; al-Jurjānī discusses Abū Tammām's metaphor of poetic ideas and word craft; Burhān reports a variant of Jarīr's line. [4 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(يونس والأصمعي); JURJANI-DALAIL(أبو تمام); BURHAN(الأصمعي)]
- 31:27/q31/p2 Adjacent discussions address resurrection and cross-reference other sūras. — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah reports a question about gradual growth and sudden resurrection; Ibn al-Jawzī cross-refers Hūd, al-ʿAnkabūt and al-Baqarah. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النقاش); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 31:27/q31/p3 Innamā is a non-governing restrictive construction unlike dependent annamā. — Al-Khalīl and Sībawayh cite independent-clause examples; al-Jurjānī reports mā ... illā analyses and warns the two constructions are not interchangeable. [7 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل); JURJANI-DALAIL(أبو إسحاق الزجاج,أبو علي,ناس من النحويين)]
