# 26:11: 15 questions

## 26:11/q01 Who are Pharaoh's people here?  [قَوْمَ فِرْعَوْنَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the possessive group designation includes its named ruler.
- 26:11/q01/p1 The Egyptian people of Pharaoh, identified with the wrongdoers to whom Moses is sent. — Muqātil locates them in Egypt and gives Pharaoh's name as Fītūs. Al-Ṭabarī calls them disbelievers; al-Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr identify the group through its injustice. Al-Bayḍāwī notes that naming the people leaves Pharaoh's evident role understood. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; YAHYA-SALLAM; BIQAI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 26:11/q01/p2 The expression may include Pharaoh himself among his people. — Al-Ālūsī compares the inclusion to Adam within the children of Adam. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 26:11/q02 How does 'Pharaoh's people' relate grammatically to 'the wrongdoers'?  [قَوْمَ فِرْعَوْنَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the repeated noun explains the first noun, substitutes for it, or is construed with the direct-address verb.
- 26:11/q02/p1 It is an apposition or explanatory apposition identifying the wrongdoers. — Al-Ṭabarī says the second 'people' specifies the first. Abū Ḥayyān prefers apposition because the familiar name removes ambiguity; al-Ālūsī says it marks them as exemplary wrongdoers. Al-Samīn and al-Bayḍāwī also allow a substitute analysis. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 26:11/q02/p2 It is a substitute (badal) for 'the wrongdoers'. — An unnamed view reported by Abū Ḥayyān and Abū al-Baqāʾ as reported by al-Ālūsī give this parse; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Samīn allow it alongside apposition. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(وقيل); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 26:11/q02/p3 On the direct-address reading, it may be the verb's object or a vocative. — Abū al-Baqāʾ gives both alternatives as reported by al-Samīn. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]

## 26:11/q03 Why repeat 'people' and name Pharaoh?  [قَوْمَ فِرْعَوْنَ · rhetoric]
- 26:11/q03/p1 The repetition specifies and emphasizes the intended people. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares Jarīr's repeated vocative 'yā Taym Taym ʿAdī'; Abū Ḥayyān says the familiar name resolves ambiguity. Al-Burūsawī says naming the people alone signals Pharaoh's prominence among those judged, while al-Bayḍāwī says Pharaoh's role is evident. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 26:11/q04 Why are these people called wrongdoers?  [قَوْمَ فِرْعَوْنَ · interpretation]
- 26:11/q04/p1 They combined disbelief and idolatry with oppression of the Israelites and others. — Al-Ṭabarī emphasizes disbelief; Abū Ḥayyān and Kur'an Yolu mention enslavement and the killing of sons, the latter also denial of God's existence and oneness. Al-Biqāʿī says they wronged themselves, Israelites, and other servants. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes associationism against oneself from violating others' rights, citing 2:114 and 6:82 for the Qur'anic range of ẓulm; he says the description makes their notorious oppression a reason for Moses's mission. Iṣlāḥī describes escalating rebellion without fear of wrath. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:11/q05 What does al-Suddī report about Pharaoh's pursuit of Moses?  [قَوْمَ فِرْعَوْنَ · reports]
- 26:11/q05/p1 Pharaoh pursued Moses with Haman leading an army of 1.7 million horses, none female. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits the report from al-Suddī. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي)]

## 26:11/q06 How is the verb in 'أَلَا يَتَّقُونَ' read?  [يَتَّقُونَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the commissioned speech describes absent people, turns to address them, or preserves a first-person object or address in the final vowel.
- 26:11/q06/p1 The common reading has third-person yāʾ: 'will they not fear?' — Al-Ṭabarī calls it narration before direct address; al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī say the people are absent from the immediate address. Al-Farrāʾ compares the alternation at 3:12. Al-Ṭabrisī glosses it as 'Is it not time for them to fear?' Al-Samīn notes an open final nūn and an omitted object, punishment; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ calls it rebuke from afar to be conveyed by Moses. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); JISHUMI(قراءة العامة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسلم بن يسار); TABATABAI]
- 26:11/q06/p2 A tāʾ reading directly addresses Pharaoh's people: 'will you not fear?' — Readers named across the notes include ʿUbayd ibn ʿUmayr, Abū Ḥāzim, ʿAbd Allāh ibn Muslim ibn Yasār, Ḥammād ibn Salama, Shaqīq ibn Salama, and Abū Qilāba. Al-Ṭabrisī calls it noncanonical. Ibn Jinnī supplies 'say to them,' comparing 13:23–24; al-Naḥḥās and al-Farrāʾ compare 3:12. Al-Zamakhsharī describes a sudden accusatory turn to the absent culprits, made present through Moses's delivery; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī say it heightens rebuke and reflection. [19 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(عبد الله بن مسلم بن يسار وحماد بن سلمة); THALABI(عبيد بن عمير); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); JISHUMI(عبيد بن عمير); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(عبد الله بن مسلم بن يسار وحماد بن سلمة); QURTUBI-FULL(عبيد بن عمير وأبو حازم); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,عبد الله بن مسلم بن يسار، وشقيق بن سلمة، وحماد بن سلمة، وأبو قلابة); SAMIN-DURR(عبد الله بن مسلم ابن يسار وحماد وشقيق بن سلمة); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري,عبد الله بن مسلم بن يسار)]
- 26:11/q06/p3 A reading has a kasra on the final nūn; one report also allows fatḥa. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Bayḍāwī report the kasra; Abū Ḥayyān reports fatḥa or kasra; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī report kasra. Their competing parses are treated separately below. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى); ALUSI-FULL]

## 26:11/q07 How is the reading with kasra on the nūn parsed?  [يَتَّقُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the vowel preserves an elided first-person object or results from a compressed vocative and imperative.
- 26:11/q07/p1 It means 'will they not fear Me?' with the first-person object yāʾ elided. — Al-Zamakhsharī describes loss of one of two adjacent nūns and then the yāʾ, leaving its kasra. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Samīn also allow the retained kasra to stand for the object. Abū Ḥayyān reports an underlying 'will they not fear Me?' and explains elision by adjacent sukūns; al-Ālūsī derives the form from tattaqūnanī. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى); ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:11/q07/p2 It is a vocative plus imperative: 'O people, fear Me.' — Al-Zamakhsharī compares the address at 2:34 and the form at 27:25; the proposed derivation loses the alif of yāʾ in speech and writing. Al-Bayḍāwī mentions the parallel 'alā yā usjudū'. Al-Samīn calls the parse remote; Abū Ḥayyān calls it far-fetched; al-Ālūsī says dropping both alifs is irregular. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 26:11/q08 What does fearing God mean here?  [يَتَّقُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether taqwā is glossed as worship, reverence of God, or protective caution expressed through obedience and faith.
- 26:11/q08/p1 To guard against God’s punishment and the consequences of wrongdoing through obedience, faith, and good deeds. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Baghawī say obedience averts punishment; al-Wāḥidī's Wajīz adds belief. Al-Ṭabrisī derives taqwā from making a protective barrier by doing good and avoiding blameworthy things; Ibn al-Jawzī describes taking protective precautions. Ibn ʿĀshūr gives fear and caution with an implicit object, the consequences of injustice, comparing 8:56. Study Quran's reported view includes mindfulness of the Hereafter's punishment. Al-Qāshānī evokes God's overwhelming force and annihilating wrath; al-Burūsawī combines faith, obedience, and averting punishment. [14 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; QURTUBI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators (Ṭ))]
- 26:11/q08/p2 To worship God. — Muqātil glosses the question as asking whether they will worship God. Al-Dāmaghānī says ittaqū can bear 'worship,' citing 16:2 and 16:51. [2 notes: MUQATIL; DAMGHANI]
- 26:11/q08/p3 To become reverent or mindful of God. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām describes the mission as calling them to fear God. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the phrase both denies present reverence and orders it; Study Quran reports both the question of reverence and, depending on vocalization, reverencing God. Al-Ālūsī says Moses speaks for God as at 2:186, so it can mean 'fear Me'. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q,R)]

## 26:11/q09 How is taqwā distinguished from faith and waraʿ?  [يَتَّقُونَ · meaning]
- 26:11/q09/p1 Taqwā is protective precaution; unlike faith it is unmixed, and unlike waraʿ it is not limited to uncertain matters. — Ibn al-Jawzī's teacher ʿAlī ibn ʿUbayd Allāh calls taqwā more commendable than faith because faith may be mixed with other things. Ibn al-Jawzī defines waraʿ as avoiding a doubtful matter whose cause is conjectural and restraining oneself from uncertain consequences. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(علي بن عبيد الله)]

## 26:11/q10 What is the force of 'أَلَا' in the question?  [أَلَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the form is one exhortative particle, interrogation plus negation, or an attention marker.
- 26:11/q10/p1 It is an exhortative particle urging the people to become God-fearing. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Burūsawī and al-Ālūsī take this as the apparent force; al-Ālūsī extends it to every reading. Al-Itqān distinguishes insistent exhortation from gentler invitation, both requesting action with verbs, citing 2:44, 26:11, 37:91 and 24:22. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it one particle of urging and makes the clause an explanation of 'go'. Al-Nasafī says it is time for them to fear; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says it commands reverence while denying its presence. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 26:11/q10/p2 It is interrogative hamza plus negative lā, denying that they fear God and expressing astonishment. — Al-Burhān says the combination asserts the negative proposition. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the interrogative as rejecting their absence of fear and registering Moses's astonishment. Al-Zamakhsharī's analysis, reported by al-Samīn, also treats it as a denial; Asad reports al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī reading it as the factual assertion that they refuse God-consciousness. [4 notes: BURHAN; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari and Razi); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 26:11/q10/p3 It is an attention particle. — Al-Samīn mentions this as another grammatical view; Abū Ḥayyān rejects it in favor of exhortation. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 26:11/q11 How does 'أَلَا يَتَّقُونَ' attach to Moses's commission?  [أَلَا يَتَّقُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the question is independent, implied speech to be delivered, or a circumstance modifying the wrongdoers; the last parse faces two stated syntactic objections.
- 26:11/q11/p1 It is a fresh, independent question after the command to go. — Abū Ḥātim recommends stopping after 'Pharaoh's people' because the meaning is complete. Al-Zamakhsharī sees a fresh divine remark registering extreme wrongdoing and amazement at confidence despite God's punishment; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Burūsawī and Ibn ʿĀshūr similarly make it a separate question. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it prepares Moses for entrenched resistance known from his upbringing. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو حاتم); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 26:11/q11/p2 It is speech for Moses to convey, with an implied 'tell them' or 'saying'. — Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Jinnī supply 'tell them'; Ibn Jinnī compares the implied 'they say' at 13:23–24. Al-Ṭabrisī gives this for the tāʾ reading; al-Ālūsī makes the phrase circumstantial with an implied speech verb. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats the exhortation as explaining 'go' and says the command can be reported by meaning, as in 5:117; he points to the later explicit command to go and speak at 26:16. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(عبد الله بن مسلم بن يسار وحماد بن سلمة); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 26:11/q11/p3 It is a circumstantial clause: they are wrongdoers while not fearing God. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī and al-Samīn attach the circumstance to the pronoun in 'the wrongdoers'. Abū Ḥayyān objects that 'Pharaoh's people' intervenes between governor and dependent and that material before interrogative hamza cannot govern what follows; al-Samīn records the Shaykh's same objections. Al-Ālūsī finds a reply that the intervening phrase is not foreign unconvincing. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,الزمخشري)-]

## 26:11/q12 What does the question convey about Pharaoh's people?  [أَلَا يَتَّقُونَ · interpretation]
- 26:11/q12/p1 It rebukes their established lack of piety and calls them toward it. — Al-Qurṭubī says the question implies they do not fear God and that Moses must call them to piety. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Bayḍāwī describe amazement at their injustice and audacity. Al-Biqāʿī hears anger over growing stubbornness during long respite; Iṣlāḥī hears anger, yearning, rebuke and nearness of punishment. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ calls the third-person wording a rebuke from afar. Asad reports al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī taking it as a factual assertion of refusal. [14 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari and Razi); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:11/q13 What is Moses to do with this warning?  [أَلَا يَتَّقُونَ · interpretation]
- 26:11/q13/p1 He is to go to Pharaoh's people, convey God's reproach, and call them away from injustice toward piety. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām and al-Burhān describe the charge as a call to fear God. Al-Wāḥidī adds belief; al-Qurṭubī says the question presupposes present lack of fear. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the compressed 'go' entails conveying the message, clarified at 26:16, and their description tells Moses where to begin. Al-Zamakhsharī says the remote audience is treated as present because Moses will deliver the speech; the address also urges Moses himself toward piety and instructs believers through unbelievers' example. [10 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Q)]

## 26:11/q14 How does this account serve the sūra's message and its audience?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connection emphasized is the story cycle's consolation and warning or this warning's repetition in later speeches.
- 26:11/q14/p1 Earlier prophetic stories console Muhammad, encourage perseverance, and warn his opponents through a repeated pattern of rejection and punishment. — Al-Ṭabrisī says they assure Muhammad of eventual victory. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ links repeated story endings to the opening rejection at 26:8 and says Muhammad's people follow the same path. Kur'an Yolu says Moses faced the harder task of summoning Pharaoh from an enslaved people, while Muhammad shared his audience's standing and met less powerful opponents; Moses's success encourages the Prophet and believers. [3 notes: TABRISI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 26:11/q14/p2 The warning style recurs in later prophets' addresses in this sūra. — Iṣlāḥī points to 26:125, 26:143, 26:162 and 26:178. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:11/q15 How does this telling relate to Moses's other accounts and the coming dialogue?  [* · links]
- 26:11/q15/p1 This passage compresses the commission to focus on Pharaoh's rejection, while other passages supply the opening call and the later command to speak. — Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts the omitted opening at 20:12–23, which expands Moses's honor and prophethood, with this focus on mission and rejection. He points to 26:16 for the explicit speech command and cross-references Moses at 2:51 and Pharaoh at 7:103. Al-Biqāʿī says foreknowledge of resistance anticipates Moses's request for divine help, comparing 25:30. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
