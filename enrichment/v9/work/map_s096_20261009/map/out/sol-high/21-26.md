# 21:26: 16 questions

## 21:26/q01 Who made the claim that the Merciful took a child?  [وَقَالُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the reported occasion identifies a particular Arab group or Jewish claimants, and whether the verse's scope extends beyond its occasion.
- 21:26/q01/p1 Khuzāʿa made the claim, sometimes specified as a group of that tribe. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Study Quran report Khuzāʿa as the occasion. Al-Qurṭubī and Study Quran add that they worshipped angels hoping for intercession; al-Rāzī and Ibn ʿĀshūr add a marriage to jinn, and al-Bursawī specifies leading jinn. Ibn ʿĀshūr locates them on Mecca’s outskirts; al-Ālūsī calls this the famous account. [15 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL(خزاعة); RAZI-FULL(خزاعة); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(خزاعة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(خزاعة); BURSEVI(حي من خزاعة); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some); ALUSI-FULL+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+]
- 21:26/q01/p2 Quraysh or other Arab polytheists made the claim, with al-Naḍr ibn al-Ḥārith named in one report. — Muqātil names Meccan unbelievers including al-Naḍr. Ibn al-Jawzī reports Ibn ʿAbbās naming Quraysh and Ibn Isḥāq naming al-Naḍr. Al-Wāḥidī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Asad, Kuran Yolu, and Study Quran mention Quraysh, Arabs, or pagans; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 16:57, Study Quran 37:149–153 and 43:16–17, and Kuran Yolu 16:57, 17:40, 43:15–20. [10 notes: MUQATIL(النضر بن الحارث); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وابن إسحاق); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q); IBNKATHIR-FULL(العرب)+; WAHIDI-WAJIZ+]
- 21:26/q01/p3 Jews made the claim, saying God intermarried with jinn and angels descended from that union. — Qatāda is reported by Yaḥyā ibn Sallām, ʿAbd al-Razzāq, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Durr, and al-Ālūsī; al-Qurṭubī notes Maʿmar’s recension also says 'some groups.' Al-Biqāʿī reports Jews as another antecedent. Al-Rāzī links the jinn claim to 37:158, though in his account Khuzāʿa says it. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL(اليهود); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); RAZI-FULL(خزاعة)]
- 21:26/q01/p4 The wording also encompasses Christians who call Jesus God's son and Jews who call Ezra His son. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the verse refutes all these claims; al-Jishumī mentions Christians and other unbelievers; al-Ālūsī says every attribution of offspring is condemned. Abu Ḥayyān and Kuran Yolu mention the Christian and Jewish claims; Study Quran gives Christian sonship as an alternative and cites 4:171. [7 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]

## 21:26/q02 What does اتَّخَذَ mean and how is it formed?  [ٱتَّخَذَ · meaning]
- 21:26/q02/p1 It means taking or making something one's own; here it can suggest treating someone as a child. — Al-Biqāʿī reads the verb as pretending or behaving as one with a child. Al-Rāghib, quoted by al-Bursawī, defines akhdh as placing or acquiring something, by taking or force, and notes figurative usage for fever or a captive. He derives ittakhadha from akhdh in the iftiʿāl form; it takes two objects and functions like jaʿala. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب)]

## 21:26/q03 Why is God called ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ here?  [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ · rhetoric]
- 21:26/q03/p1 The title recalls that all beings depend on His beneficence, making the offspring claim absurd. — Al-Biqāʿī says every existent receives its existence from the Merciful's beneficence. Al-Ālūsī says the title presents everything besides Him as His dependent creature and heightens the absurdity of a child. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 21:26/q04 Whom does the alleged وَلَدًا refer to?  [وَلَدًۭا · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase is read by its reported occasion about angels or by the wider range of claims it rebukes.
- 21:26/q04/p1 The alleged offspring are angels, called God's daughters. — Ibn ʿAbbās as reported by al-Wāḥidī, and al-Ṭabrisī, explicitly identify the child as angels. Muqātil, Qatāda's reports, Ibn Isḥāq, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and later accounts connect this with Arabs' or Jews' claim; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 16:57 and Study Quran 37:149–153, 43:16–17. [30 notes: MUQATIL(النضر بن الحارث); YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(خزاعة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(خزاعة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(خزاعة); BIQAI-FULL(اليهود,خزاعة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(حي من خزاعة); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q,some)]
- 21:26/q04/p2 The alleged offspring may include Jesus and Ezra as well as angels. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says Christians called Jesus God's son and Jews called Ezra His son; Abu Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī likewise extend the scope. Asad includes prophets such as Jesus and angels; Kuran Yolu mentions Jesus and angels. Study Quran gives Jesus' divine sonship as an alternative referent, citing 4:171. [8 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q)]

## 21:26/q05 What is the number and gender range of وَلَدًا?  [وَلَدًۭا · meaning]
- 21:26/q05/p1 Walad can denote one or many, sons or daughters; here the claim concerns daughters. — Al-Qurṭubī says it can be singular, plural, or collective like māl. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a collective with the same form for singular and plural, including sons and daughters, and cites 16:57 for the daughters in view. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 21:26/q06 Why is divine offspring impossible, and what kind of fatherhood is excluded?  [وَلَدًۭا · theology]
turns on: Whether 'father' means biological or adoptive filiation, or a restricted metaphor of special relation.
- 21:26/q06/p1 Literal offspring or adoption cannot apply to God. — Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī distinguish begetting, which entails bodily nature, from adoption, which substitutes a non-child for a child; when true filiation is impossible, its adoptive analogy fails. They distinguish this from possible special divine affection or friendship. Al-Jishumī adds that Christian eternal hypostases cannot form a son or make three one. Al-Rāzī argues resemblance and difference entail composition and contingency. Al-Biqāʿī argues a child shares a nature with its parent. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kuran Yolu say children imply need or deficiency; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 10:68. Asad cites 19:92 for God's remoteness from that imperfection. Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī argue that created, owned servants cannot be divine offspring. [20 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(some Muslim theologians); BIQAI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; JISHUMI+; RAZI-FULL+; TABRISI+]
- 21:26/q06/p2 A narrow metaphorical or symbolic title 'Father' can be applied to God, without implying begotten or exclusive divine offspring. — Study Quran reports some Muslim theologians permitting this narrow usage, citing 2:116; those theologians reject superior ontological status from begetting and exclusive sectarian filiation, citing 5:18: people are created mortals subject to God's forgiveness or punishment. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(some Muslim theologians)]

## 21:26/q07 What does سُبْحَٰنَهُۥ declare?  [سُبْحَٰنَهُۥ · interpretation]
- 21:26/q07/p1 It declares God exalted and remote from the claim that He has a child. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Kashshāf, al-Ālūsī, and Asad apply the declaration to the offspring claim. Al-Bursawī reports Baḥr al-ʿUlūm's exclamation at attributing child, spouse, or partner to the giver of blessings, and al-Kashf's point that glorification can express astonishment too. [13 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكشف,بحر العلوم); ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 21:26/q08 How is سُبْحَٰنَهُۥ parsed?  [سُبْحَٰنَهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the expression is taken as a noun, an elliptical verbal construction, or an imperative.
- 21:26/q08/p1 It is a verbal noun of glorification or a fixed noun for glorification. — Al-Bursawī gives both analyses, with the verbal noun conveying transcendence or remoteness. Al-Ālūsī also lists both. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:26/q08/p2 It is an elliptical 'I glorify Him' or an imperative to glorify Him. — Al-Bursawī supplies the implied verb and imperative analysis; al-Ālūsī also allows an imperative. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 21:26/q09 What does بَلْ do to the preceding claim?  [بَلْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether bal can retract a human claim quoted in divine speech, or must only begin a new topic.
- 21:26/q09/p1 It cancels the offspring claim and asserts the correction that they are servants. — Sībawayh illustrates correction with 'I passed a man—a donkey,' grammatical only when the latter replaces the earlier identification; he says the angels have already been mentioned. Al-Zarkashī rejects Ibn Mālik's transition-only rule and cites Qur'anic examples 21:5, 27:66, and 38:8. Al-Itqān says a clause following bal can reject a prior assertion; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 2:116 for this correction. [8 notes: SIBAWAYH; BURHAN(ابن مالك)+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; ITQAN+]
- 21:26/q09/p2 In the Qur'an, bal only moves to a new point and does not retract a prior assertion. — Al-Zarkashī reports Ibn Mālik's claim and rejects it. Al-Itqān attributes it to al-Basīṭ, Ibn Mālik, and Ibn al-Ḥājib, while Ibn Hishām calls it mistaken. Ibn al-Ḥājib argues cancellation of a true first assertion would imply error. Al-Itqān gives a case where bal shifts from success and prayer to preferring worldly life, and distinguishes bal before a single coordinated word, absent from the Qur'an. Al-Itqān adds that before a single word bal is a coordinating conjunction, with no Qur'anic instance. [3 notes: ITQAN(ابن الحاجب); BURHAN(ابن مالك)-; ITQAN(ابن مالك)-]

## 21:26/q10 How is بَلْ عِبَادٌ مُّكْرَمُونَ constructed?  [بَلْ عِبَادٌۭ مُّكْرَمُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether ʿibād is a resumed nominative predicate, a corrective replacement, or a hypothetical accusative object.
- 21:26/q10/p1 ʿIbādun is nominative as the predicate of an omitted 'they,' in a resumed statement. — Sībawayh allows a nominative after bal by a resumed clause with omitted huwa and notes even a new utterance may omit a understood phrase; he says the angels are known from context. Al-Jishumī treats it as a new statement detached from the quotation. Al-Samīn and al-Zarkashī supply hum; Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise supplies the angels as subject. [7 notes: SIBAWAYH; BURHAN+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; JISHUMI+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 21:26/q10/p2 The nominative can function as a corrective replacement after bal; a genitive badal is also grammatically conceivable. — Sībawayh compares raising after the accusative waladan with raising after a genitive, and allows a genitive badal governed by bāʾ in an analogous construction. His 'man—a donkey' example is felicitous as replacement, not predication. He says definiteness does not change constructions with lākin, bal, and lā bal; he contrasts aw's movement from certainty to doubt with am's direct choice and reports Yūnus rejecting a genitive in a parallel 'how [about] a woman' construction. [6 notes: SIBAWAYH(يونس)]
- 21:26/q10/p3 A hypothetical accusative ʿibādan would make it the object of 'took': God took honored servants instead of children. — Al-Zajjāj allows this outside the muṣḥaf but prefers the written nominative. Al-Naḥḥās reports Abū Isḥāq's equivalent wording and al-Farrāʾ's coordination with walad; al-Qurṭubī repeats both analyses. This is presented as a grammatical possibility, not the transmitted reading. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق,الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج,الفراء)]

## 21:26/q11 Whom does عِبَادٌۭ مُّكْرَمُونَ name?  [عِبَادٌۭ مُّكْرَمُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the corrective plural follows the angel claim specifically or covers the wider claims about Jesus and Ezra.
- 21:26/q11/p1 The phrase names the angels. — Muqātil and most commentators identify angels as the servants denied to be offspring. Al-Zajjāj transmits this as the tafsir view; Abu Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī's narrower reading. Al-Ṭabarṣī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī use the context of alleged angelic daughters; Sībawayh says they are already mentioned. Ibn Isḥāq and al-Thaʿlabī add their obedience in speech and action. [36 notes: SIBAWAYH; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; MUQATIL+; NASAFI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; STUDYQURAN+; TABATABAI+; TABRISI+; THALABI+; WAHIDI-BASIT+; WAHIDI-WAJIZ+; YAHYA-SALLAM+]
- 21:26/q11/p2 The phrase names the angels and Jesus son of Mary. — Al-Zajjāj states this view; al-Naḥḥās reports Abū Isḥāq with the same identification. [2 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); ZAJJAJ+]
- 21:26/q11/p3 The phrase includes angels, Jesus, and Ezra. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reads the verse as rejecting all three groups' filiation claims; Abu Ḥayyān says the phrase can include all three while reporting al-Zamakhsharī's angels-only interpretation. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]

## 21:26/q12 What makes these servants مُّكْرَمُونَ?  [مُّكْرَمُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether honor is described as God's favor and nearness, complete servitude, or protection from error; the notes do not make these mutually exclusive.
- 21:26/q12/p1 God honors, favors, selects, and brings them near. — Al-Jishumī glosses ikrām as honoring or showing esteem. Ibn ʿAbbās, reported by al-Wāḥidī, says God honored and selected the angels; Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Ṭabrisī echo selection. Al-Nasafī, Ibn Kathīr, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and al-Ālūsī specify exalted rank or nearness. Al-Kashshāf, Abu Ḥayyān, and al-Ālūsī say their nearness helped provoke the false child claim. Al-Bursawī says preferred over many, not all. [15 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 21:26/q12/p2 Their honor is worship and perfect, bestowed servitude. — Muqātil says God honored them through worship. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls complete servitude God's gift: angels know themselves only as servants, obey perfectly, and lack contrary traits. He contrasts angelic servitude with acquired human servitude and compares mukhliṣ with mukhlaṣ; honor is bestowed on both humans and angels. [3 notes: MUQATIL; TABATABAI]
- 21:26/q12/p3 Their honor includes protection from error or sin. — Al-Biqāʿī identifies the honor as protection from error; al-Jishumī calls angels accountable yet protected from sin, grounding it in their doing what they are commanded. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; JISHUMI]

## 21:26/q13 How is مُّكْرَمُونَ read?  [مُّكْرَمُونَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the rāʾ is light or geminated.
- 21:26/q13/p1 The common reading has mukramūn without doubling the rāʾ. — Abu Ḥayyān and al-Samīn contrast the majority reading with ʿIkrima's doubled reading; al-Kashshāf also mentions a reading as mukramūn. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عكرمة); SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]
- 21:26/q13/p2 ʿIkrima reads mukarramūn with a doubled rāʾ. — Abu Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī attribute it to ʿIkrima. Al-Samīn contrasts it with the general reading; al-Bayḍāwī also reports the doubled consonant. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عكرمة); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة)]

## 21:26/q14 What follows from the angels' being honored servants?  [عِبَادٌۭ مُّكْرَمُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the consequence stressed is obedience and dependence, or their accountability and preservation.
- 21:26/q14/p1 They obey God in speech and deed and cannot act as independent intercessors or objects of worship. — Ibn Isḥāq and al-Thaʿlabī say they do not speak ahead of God and act only by His command. Ibn Kathīr says obedience in word and deed is complete; Kuran Yolu cites 4:172 and includes Jesus' recognition of God's knowledge, power, and majesty. Al-Jishumī says worshipping angels is unwarranted because they worship God. Iṣlāḥī denies autonomous intercession, says they speak only with permission, and warns that claiming partnership ends in Hell. Al-Qurṭubī and Study Quran report Khuzāʿa worshipped them in hope of intercession. [8 notes: IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); THALABI; JISHUMI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 21:26/q14/p2 They are morally accountable yet preserved from sin. — Al-Jishumī infers accountability and protection from the statement that they do what God commands; al-Biqāʿī calls protection from error their honor. [2 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 21:26/q15 How does this verse fit the passage's argument?  [* · links]
turns on: Which part of the passage's wider argument the note emphasizes.
- 21:26/q15/p1 After refuting divine partners, the passage turns to the false claim of divine offspring. — Al-Rāzī says the preceding proof rules out partner, opposite, and rival; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the passage moves from worship of other gods to this belief. Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes the polytheists still acknowledged God as creator and provider. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 21:26/q15/p2 The passage reinforces the prophets' monotheism and God's wise justice over creation. — Iṣlāḥī says multiple independent divine wills would make the universe unsustainable and all prophets taught monotheism. He also says the universe is no amusement: its just, wise Creator will establish a day when truth defeats falsehood. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 21:26/q16 What report about Idrīs is transmitted alongside this verse?  [* · reports]
- 21:26/q16/p1 Idrīs preceded Noah, taught monotheism, allowed his people to choose their conduct, and they refused before God destroyed them. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām transmits this from ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(عبد الله بن عمرو)]
