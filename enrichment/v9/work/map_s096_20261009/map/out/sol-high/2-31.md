# 2:31: 54 questions

## 2:31/q01 How does ‘taught Adam the names’ work grammatically?  [وَعَلَّمَ ءَادَمَ ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the verb is active or passive and how its causative form governs objects.
- 2:31/q01/p1 A passive reading makes Adam the nominative subject standing for the suppressed agent. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān ascribe this to al-Yamānī and Yazīd; Ibn Jinnī reports Yazīd al-Barbarī and explains Adam's prominence as an effect of object foregrounding. [12 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(يزيد البربري); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو الفتح,اليماني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(اليماني ويزيد اليزيدي); SAMIN-DURR(قرئ); ALUSI-FULL(اليماني)]
- 2:31/q01/p2 In the active reading, God is subject, Adam the first object, and the names the second object of the causative verb. — Al-Naḥḥās calls this the grammarians' agreement. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn explain the doubled verb as a causative from one-object ʿalima; al-Ḥarīrī's proposed third object is rejected. [9 notes: NAHHAS; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو الحسين بن أبي الربيع,أبو علي الشلوبين,القاسم بن علي الحريري,بعض النحويين); SAMIN-DURR(أبو علي الشلوبين); ALUSI-FULL(الحريري)]

## 2:31/q02 How did God teach Adam?  [وَعَلَّمَ ءَادَمَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether knowledge was created directly, given through speech or an angel, or bestowed as a capacity for gradual naming.
- 2:31/q02/p1 God directly implanted or inspired necessary knowledge in Adam. — Al-Wāḥidī says God created it in Adam's heart; al-Qurṭubī prefers necessary inspiration. Al-Bayḍāwī allows direct casting into awareness; al-Ṭabrisī calls it an extraordinary opening of heart and tongue. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطاء); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(some exegetes); BURSEVI(كشف الكنوز)]
- 2:31/q02/p2 God showed Adam each thing and taught its name, purpose, benefit, and harm. — Al-Jishumī describes presentation of individual referents in every language; al-Nasafī mentions horse and camel. Ibn ʿAṭiyya records both physical showing and verbal description. [7 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); NASAFI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 2:31/q02/p3 God taught through an angel or the angels' language. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ṭabrisī report angelic speech or teaching of an angelic language. Al-Ālūsī doubts the intermediary explanation without a sound report. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL(some exegetes)]
- 2:31/q02/p4 God endowed Adam with a faculty to develop names and language gradually. — Elmalılı says an innate human naming power unfolded under nurture, perhaps before or with the spirit. Ibn ʿĀshūr says human linguistic capacity is divinely inspired regardless of how many words were taught. [12 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 2:31/q02/p5 God taught Adam by direct speech. — Abū Ḥayyān finds direct instruction most apparent; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports divine speech before earthly descent while preserving Moses's distinction. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:31/q03 What is the origin and grammatical status of Adam's name?  [ءَادَمَ · meaning]
turns on: Arabic derivations from earth or complexion compete with a foreign-name analysis; these yield different declension explanations.
- 2:31/q03/p1 Adam is named for the earth's surface from which he was made. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and others connect adīm with visible surface soil; reports of a handful from all earth explain the descendants' variety. Some grammarians treat the derivative as declinable when indefinite; al-Ṭabarī calls it a verbal proper name and diptote. [33 notes: TAB-FULL(أصحاب النبي,ابن عباس,رسول الله,سعيد بن جبير,علي); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو موسى الأشعري,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قوم); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس، ابن جبير، الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي,الشاعر,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أحمد والترمذي); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 2:31/q03/p2 Adam derives from adama, a complexion term, with reports varying between dark or reddish and white. — Al-Farrāʾ says adama differs between people and camels or gazelles; Dhū al-Rumma and al-Aʿshā are cited for color usage. Al-Naḍr says white, al-Ḍaḥḥāk dark brown. This account can make the name diptote even when indefinite. [17 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأعشى,الفراء,النضر بن شميل,ذو الرمة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI(صاحب العين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك، النضر بن شميل، قطرب); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك,النضر); SAMIN-DURR(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(الجواليقي وكثيرون); ELMALILI; BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 2:31/q03/p3 Adam is a foreign proper name; Arabic etymologies are secondary or morphologically unsound. — Al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and others point to properness plus foreignness for diptote status. Al-Ālūsī allows Arabic derivation only after Arabization; Elmalılı cites Hebrew, Syriac and other forms while preferring an original first-language name. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Arabic name may have been borrowed from Hebrew adāmā, earth, through the Torah account and Jewish storytelling. [25 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الطبري); SAMIN-DURR(الطبري,قيل); ALUSI-FULL(some scholars,الجوهري); ELMALILI(İbnü’l-Esîr,İmam Şa‘bî,Şehristânî); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; ELMALILI+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 2:31/q03/p4 Adam is a proper name on the Arabic afʿal pattern, with disagreement about declining it when indefinite. — Al-Naḥḥās records consensus on definite Adam; Khalīl and Sībawayh keep it diptote when indefinite, al-Akhfash declines it, and Abū Isḥāq favors Sībawayh. The plural varies with adjectival versus proper use. [9 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق,الخليل وسيبويه); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جعفر النحاس,الأخفش,الخليل وسيبويه); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 2:31/q03/p5 Other accounts derive Adam from affinity, model, Hebrew or Syriac soil, or identify him with Kayumers. — Al-Bayḍāwī calls affinity strained; al-Samīn lists a Hebrew soil derivation and al-Ṭabarī's quadriliteral verb. Elmalılı reports the Kuyumers identification but notes disagreement. [11 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الطبري,قيل); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(İbnü’l-Esîr,İmam Şa‘bî,Şehristânî)]
- 2:31/q03/p6 Arabic and Hebrew may each have inherited Adam independently from a common Semitic source. — Ibn ʿĀshūr offers shared Semitic inheritance as an alternative to borrowing from Hebrew. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:31/q04 Who is Adam in the account?  [ءَادَمَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the named individual also represents the human race.
- 2:31/q04/p1 Adam is the first human and representative of all humanity, including women and men. — Asad cites the angels' reference to future corruption and 7:11; Elmalılı treats Adam's original faculties as inherited by the species. Study Quran notes that the Qur'an does not explain the transition from primordial Adam to the first couple. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the Adam named after the dialogue as the first steward, the first human and father of humanity. [6 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:31/q04/p2 Adam bears the kunyas Abū Muḥammad and Abū al-Bashar. — Al-Baghawī and al-Suhaylī report these; al-Suhaylī connects Abū Muḥammad with the final prophet and distinguishes Paradise from earth. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(السهيلي)]

- 2:31/q05/p9 Names of broad kinds rather than each individual or later subtype. — ʿIkrima gives human, angel, jinn and bird; another report names mountains, horses and valleys, with later descendants assigning other labels. - 2:31/q05/p10 A word or power of naming that unlocks every name. — Al-Naqqāsh reports one word from Ibn ʿAbbās; Abū Ḥayyān reports the majority's act-of-naming reading; Kur'an Yolu describes a capacity to invent language. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النقاش عن ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:31/q05/p11 The hidden living realities preserved with God, of which worldly names are descended signs. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues from the living plural referents and 15:21's imperishable treasuries; their plurality is of ranks, not separate individuals. Verbal labels cannot account for Adam's distinction over angels. [16 notes: TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(الشهاب)-]
- 2:31/q05/p12 Adam was taught everything, even advanced grammar such as Sībawayh's. — Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī is reported with this extreme claim; Ibn ʿAṭiyya explicitly calls it wrong on several grounds. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي)-]

## 2:31/q06 What does ‘names’ mean here: labels, referents, or realities?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ism denotes uttered labels, the things named, or properties that reveal what a thing is.
- 2:31/q06/p1 The names are the meanings, attributes, properties and uses of things, with labels as signs. — Al-Zajjāj's theorists say knowing a horse's use is more informative than uttering 'horse'; al-Māwardī, Jishumī and Ṭabrisī say bare labels confer no benefit. Rāzī reports arguments from sima and sumu and Adam's superiority, though al-Shihāb objects that properties are not called names. [21 notes: ZAJJAJ(بعض أهل النظر); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أكثر العلماء); JISHUMI(ابن عباس ومجاهد وأكثر المفسرين,قتادة وغيره); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(من الناس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,الشهاب); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:31/q06/p2 The names are expressions or labels that denote things. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers this because teaching and the later request concern names. Al-Qurṭubī cites common usage and 87:1, 55:78, 53:23; al-Nasafī rejects teaching objects in place of labels. Al-Bayḍāwī distinguishes ordinary from grammatical usage. [13 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الأمة)+]
- 2:31/q06/p3 Adam learned both the labels and what they signify. — Al-Māwardī gives both as an interpretation because names without meanings are useless. Al-Ṭabrisī calls it stronger; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes Adam's direct essential knowledge from angels' later verbal information. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(some exegetes); ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 2:31/q06/p4 A name is a sign of a thing, ranging from proper labels to relational, derived and figurative names. — Al-Thaʿlabī's associates list inherent, transient, derived, genitive, simile, relational and generic names. Al-Zajjājī lists ten grammatical kinds. Al-Haralī and Elmalılı distinguish spoken sign from share of reality; Asad takes names as concepts. [27 notes: THALABI(أصحابنا); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاجي); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو حاتم أحمد بن حمدان الرازي,الحرالي); ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES(all philologists)]
- 2:31/q06/p5 The names stand for the named beings themselves. — Some exegetes allow metonymy or identity of name and named; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the pronoun could then refer to objects. Abū Ḥayyān reports the view but prefers literal names. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الأمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(some exegetes); IBNATIYYA-FULL-; NASAFI-FULL-]

## 2:31/q07 Which languages did Adam learn, and how did languages later differ?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ كُلَّهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether all historical languages were taught, only an original language was taught, or later people coined or differentiated languages.
- 2:31/q07/p1 Adam learned all languages; his descendants inherited them, dispersed, retained different tongues and forgot the others. — Al-Wāḥidī infers this from 'all'; al-Ṭabrisī allows differentiation after Noah. Al-Qurṭubī prefers Adam as first human speaker of all languages and restricts rival 'first Arabic' reports to later groups. Bursevī reports seven hundred thousand languages, lost and recovered. [18 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(جماعة من أهل التأويل); JISHUMI(أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل التأويل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); TABRISI(أبو علي الجبائي وعلي بن عيسى); QURTUBI-FULL(كعب الأحبار); RAZI-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قول غير مسمى); BURSEVI(خبر); STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī,al-Ṭabrisī); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 2:31/q07/p2 Adam learned names in his own or one original language; later peoples agreed on further names. — Al-Māwardī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya report a single language. Ibn ʿĀshūr places concrete first words in humanity's first language, with event nouns, verbs and adjectives developing later. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قول غير مسمى); IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 2:31/q07/p3 Arabic began with Gabriel, Noah, Ishmael, or Yaʿrub rather than Adam. — Kaʿb reports Gabriel to Noah and Shem; a Prophetic report calls Ishmael the first clear Arabic speaker at age ten; another gives Yaʿrub. Al-Qurṭubī reconciles these as first speakers in a particular lineage or group. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,كعب الأحبار); TABRISI]
- 2:31/q07/p4 Language development grew from Adam's divinely given capacity to name, think and speak. — Elmalılı considers naming, knowledge and speech intrinsic to human nature; later linguistic diversity arose from this capacity. Kur'an Yolu gives language invention as a possible reading. [20 notes: ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:31/q07/p5 Adam learned every language except the address used by God; that one arose by convention. — Al-Jishumī reports this alternative. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]

## 2:31/q08 Does the verse establish who first assigned words to meanings?  [وَعَلَّمَ ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ · theology]
turns on: Whether God's teaching entails divine assignment of all languages, human convention, a mixture, or leaves the issue undecided.
- 2:31/q08/p1 God instituted the original languages and taught their established meanings to Adam. — Al-Ashʿarī, al-Jubbāʾī and al-Kaʿbī infer prior assignments from 'taught the names' and angelic verification; Ibn Khuwayz Mindād sees detailed divine institution. Al-Ālūsī rejects an infinite regress from requiring prior human convention. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(الأشعري والجبائي والكعبي,بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن خويز منداد); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام الأشعري); BURSEVI(كشف الكنوز)]
- 2:31/q08/p2 People established word meanings by convention, with God's teaching able to convey that convention. — Rāzī says gestures could establish meanings and God could inspire or teach human-coined languages; al-Ālūsī attributes human convention to the Muʿtazila and Abū Hāshim requires a prior language. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو هاشم,المعتزلة); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:31/q08/p3 Some assignments are divine and others conventional. — Al-Ālūsī attributes this distribution to al-Ustādh and notes al-Ashʿarī allows some later human naming. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الأستاذ,الإمام الأشعري)]
- 2:31/q08/p4 The verse does not decide the origin of language because it leaves the teaching method open. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says all sides accept divine inspiration of linguistic capacity; al-Māzirī says the practical consequence concerns altering revealed words versus ordinary language, which Ibn ʿĀshūr endorses. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(المازري); IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 2:31/q08/p5 All peoples acquired different languages in one night. — Al-Māwardī reports this explanation of linguistic diversity but rejects it as impossible by common custom. [0 notes: MAWARDI-FULL-]

## 2:31/q09 What force does ‘all of them’ give to the names?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ كُلَّهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the article and kull require absolute exhaustiveness or a contextually known set.
- 2:31/q09/p1 The definite plural and kull emphatically include every name without restriction. — Al-Qushayrī says the generic plural and reinforcement establish certainty; al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī analyze kull as encompassing parts. - 2:31/q09/p2 Kull is a dependent emphasis of the names, with syntactic freedom unlike ajmaʿūn. — Al-Samīn says kullahā follows al-asmāʾ; al-Ṭabrisī cites 15:30 for ajmaʿūn following kull. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; TABRISI]
- 2:31/q09/p3 The article refers to the customary set in Adam's world, and kull exhausts that set. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says this set suffices to show Adam's merit; the plural is needed for each name and referent, while the claim that a definite singular is always more inclusive is imprecise. ## 2:31/q10 Is anything omitted from ‘the names’?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ · grammar]

- 2:31/q10/p1 The phrase means 'the names of the named things,' with the genitive omitted. — Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Zamakhsharī treat the article as replacing the omitted complement, citing 19:4; al-Samīn and Abū Ḥayyān say this substitution is not Basran. ## 2:31/q11 What does ‘then’ indicate between teaching and display?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]

- 2:31/q11/p1 It marks elapsed time, allowing Adam's knowledge to settle before the angels' sudden test. — Al-Qushayrī and Abū Ḥayyān contrast Adam's preparation with the angels' immediate demand; al-Samīn calls thumma temporal. - 2:31/q11/p2 It marks the display's higher rhetorical rank: the public proof of wisdom surpasses the lesson alone. — Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers this; al-Ālūsī says thumma and the later fa show special attention to Adam. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 2:31/q12 What does ‘displayed’ mean?  [عَرَضَهُمْ · meaning]

- 2:31/q12/p1 It means making something visible or manifest to another. — Al-Wāḥidī gives goods, troops and a book as examples; al-Layth's poem uses aʿraḍat for appearing and al-Farrāʾ compares 18:100. - 2:31/q12/p2 Its original sense is side or aspect, extended to presentation, width and reputation. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Ḥaralī explain showing an object's side; ʿirḍ also denotes a person's protected honor or qualities. ## 2:31/q18 What does ‘inform Me’ mean here?  [أَنۢبِـُٔونِى · meaning]

## 2:31/q20 What does ‘these’ point to, and how is it parsed?  [هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ · grammar]
turns on: Present referents versus names representing absent referents.
- 2:31/q20/p1 It points to named beings made present in the display. — Al-Mujāhid says these are the taught things; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān argue demonstratives normally point to visible present entities. - 2:31/q20/p3 It can point to absent referents through their names. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī allow names present by relation; Abū Ḥayyān finds the reading strained. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 2:31/q28 Was Adam's knowledge a miracle proving he was already a prophet?  [وَعَلَّمَ ءَادَمَ · theology]
turns on: Whether the knowledge was extraordinary proof of commission, and how angels could verify it.
- 2:31/q28/p1 Yes: the extraordinary command of all names was Adam's prophetic miracle. — Al-Jishumī says God perfected Adam's mind, commissioned him and made the naming a miracle; Muʿtazilites suggest Eve or angels as recipients. - 2:31/q28/p3 Adam was not yet a prophet because his later lapse preceded election or there was no suitable addressee. — Opponents cite 2:37, 20:122 and 6:124, the concern about major sin after prophethood, and the lack of a plausible audience; al-Rāzī finds the audience objection weak. ## 2:31/q32 What are words and how do they denote things?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ · meaning]

## 2:31/q39 What is knowledge, as invoked by Adam's lesson?  [وَعَلَّمَ · theology]
turns on: Theological, Muʿtazilite and philosophical definitions differ; al-Rāzī argues for its self-evidence.
- 2:31/q39/p1 Knowledge is what makes a knower know, or true apprehension or disclosure of the known. — Al-Ashʿarī, al-Qāḍī Abū Bakr, al-Isfarāyīnī, Ibn Fūrak and al-Qaffāl offer formulations. Al-Rāzī tests each for circularity, exclusion of divine or non-action knowledge, and obscurity. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو الحسن الأشعري,الأستاذ أبو إسحاق الإسفرايني,الأستاذ أبو بكر بن فورك,القاضي أبو بكر,القفال)]
- 2:31/q39/p2 Knowledge is certain belief producing tranquility of soul. — The Muʿtazila give this account; Rāzī's associates object that belief is distinct from knowledge and divine knowledge has no soul's tranquility. The Muʿtazila answer with a common element of belief. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(الأصحاب,المعتزلة)]
- 2:31/q39/p3 Knowledge is a form or image in the soul corresponding to the object. — Philosophers and al-Ghazālī compare cognition to a mirror image. Rāzī objects from absent objects, abstract relations, nonexistents, perception's location, heat and the philosophers' own account of mirrors. [12 notes: RAZI-FULL(الغزالي,الفلاسفة)]
- 2:31/q39/p4 Knowledge is too immediately evident to define by something clearer. — Al-Rāzī argues everyone directly knows their existence and place; knowing a relation requires knowing its terms. Failed definitions may reflect this extreme obviousness. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 2:31/q39/p5 Belief varies from certain true understanding to false certainty, doubt, expectation and illusion. — Al-Juwaynī divides immediate, inferred and imitative certainty and uncertain belief; al-Rāzī says flaws remain to be discussed. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(إمام الحرمين)]

## 2:31/q40 How do Arabic terms for knowing differ?  [وَعَلَّمَ ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ · meaning]
turns on: Different terms describe perception, memory, reasoning, conjecture, skill and wisdom.
- 2:31/q40/p1 Terms such as perception, awareness, conception, preservation, remembrance and understanding describe stages or modes of knowing. — Al-Rāzī distinguishes idrāk, shuʿūr, taṣawwur, ḥifẓ, tadhakkur and dhikr, raising a paradox about commanded remembrance and answering from introspective capacity. He further defines fahm, fiqh, ʿaql, dirāya and ḥikma, distinguishing divine from human usage. [21 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعض الصالحين)]
- 2:31/q40/p2 Knowledge may be certain, conjectural, intuitive, experienced or inferred by signs. — Al-Rāzī surveys yaqīn, thought, intuition, quick intelligence, first principles, deliberation, practical discernment, experience, opinion and physiognomy; he distinguishes praiseworthy evidence-based conjecture from blameworthy weak conjecture and sensory image from sleeping apparition. [23 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعض المحققين,بعض المفسرين,قالوا)]
- 2:31/q40/p3 Maʿrifa is particular knowledge, conceptual grasp, or recognition after earlier perception. — Al-Rāzī reports three analyses and says grasp of God's essence is beyond people; one may assent to the Necessary Being without such maʿrifa. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(آخرون)]

## 2:31/q41 What examples are given of knowledge's practical power?  [وَعَلَّمَ ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ · reports]
turns on: The sources offer legal, rhetorical and advisory cases rather than a single event.
- 2:31/q41/p1 Legal learning and verbal ingenuity resolve difficult disputes or avert danger. — Al-Rāzī recounts Abū Yūsuf on theft and oaths, Yaḥyā ibn Yaʿmar on maternal descent, Abū Ḥanīfa on representation, vows, theft and marriage, al-Shāfiʿī on consensus, and poets and courtiers escaping punishment through wording. His reports also mention ingenious answers about a ruler's fear and learning. [24 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو حنيفة,أبو يوسف,الربيع,الشافعي,الليث بن سعد,سفيان,محمد بن الحسن,يحيى بن يعمر)]
- 2:31/q41/p2 Knowledge and action improve souls, while ignorance and love of the world obstruct them. — Al-Rāzī compares learning with life, repentance and light, cites the ant's knowledgeable leadership and a trained dog's purified catch, and says the heart rules by knowledge. His verse and reports urge study, reflection, defense of truth and righteous practice. [17 notes: RAZI-FULL(الشاعر,حكيم)]

## 2:31/q42 What reports about Adam's creation and the surrounding events are cited?  [ءَادَمَ · reports]
turns on: Reports differ in details of earth gathering, body formation and sequence.
- 2:31/q42/p1 God formed Adam from mixed earth, producing human variety. — Reports from Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Suddī and the Prophet describe varied earth and the Angel of Death's handful; al-Qurṭubī says al-Tirmidhī grades the Prophetic report ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ. Bursevī adds Earth refusing Gabriel, Michael and Isrāfīl, weeping springs, forty cubits and return of bodies to their soil. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(أصحاب النبي,ابن عباس,رسول الله,سعيد بن جبير,علي); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو موسى الأشعري); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي); BURSEVI(روضة العلماء,وهب بن منبه); ALUSI-FULL(أحمد والترمذي)]
- 2:31/q42/p2 Adam's clay body was formed, lay for a period, then received the spirit; Iblis refused prostration. — Al-Suddī's account gives sticky clay, forty years, Iblis testing the hollow body, Adam sneezing and praising God, haste for fruit, and all angels but Iblis prostrating. Biqāʿī debates whether creation and prostration precede the naming demonstration. [14 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); BIQAI-FULL(البغوي,البيضاوي,الحرالي,يمان)]
- 2:31/q42/p3 A report places soil through Adam across Saturday to Friday, but its attribution and chronology are disputed. — Biqāʿī quotes Abū Hurayra's schedule; al-Mizzī notes a view that it came from Kaʿb. Al-Ṭabarī resolves a tension by saying days were named before their spans and Saturday's soil preceded Saturday's created reality. [10 notes: BURHAN(الطبري); BIQAI-FULL(أبو هريرة,المزي)]
- 2:31/q42/p4 Some angels who questioned Adam repented, and some reports add punishment or the Hārūt and Mārūt trial. — Al-Rāzī records Ibn ʿAbbās's troops-of-Iblis narrative, private angelic boasting, a burning-fire report, and the story in which Hārūt and Mārūt's censure of humans led to testing with desire and wine. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس,الجن وقتادة,من تمسكوا بقصة هاروت وماروت); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة والحسن)]

## 2:31/q43 What parallel account of Adam and naming is reported from the Torah?  [ءَادَمَ ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ · reports]
turns on: The Torah narrative differs in chronology and episodes.
- 2:31/q43/p1 The quoted Torah has Adam naming living creatures after their creation and before the woman is made from his rib. — Biqāʿī recounts heaven and earth, vegetation, lights, animals and humanity, then Eden, rivers and the forbidden tree; the serpent's deception, expulsion, cherub and Adam's 930 years follow. He explains cherub and compares textual versions. [9 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:31/q44 May earlier scriptures be consulted or transmitted while discussing Adam?  [* · law]
turns on: Permissibility varies with alteration, reader expertise and ritual state.
- 2:31/q44/p1 Qualified scholars may consult and transmit useful earlier-scripture material, without affirming unverified claims. — Biqāʿī cites 3:93, 5:48, the Prophet's Torah use in an adultery case, permission to narrate Israelite reports and counsel neither to affirm nor deny them. Ibn Ḥajar limits consultation to grounded scholars; al-Shāfiʿī allows useful books after inspection. [18 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو سعيد,أبو هريرة,ابن حجر,البغوي,الشافعي,المتولي,عائشة,عبد الله بن عمرو)]
- 2:31/q44/p2 Torah and Gospel use is restricted because of alteration, with some rulings depending on ritual impurity. — Al-Rāfiʿī and others forbid use of altered books; Biqāʿī limits this to known alteration. Al-Nawawī reports a preferred rule for touching during minor impurity, al-Baghawī allows some utterance during major impurity, and al-Qāḍī al-Ḥusayn gives a disputed extreme ruling. [12 notes: BIQAI-FULL(البغوي,الرافعي,الشافعي,القاضي الحسين,المتولي,المزني,النواوي,عائشة)]

## 2:31/q45 How is divine revelation said to be delivered?  [وَعَلَّمَ · theology]
turns on: Direct hearing versus transmission of meaning or exact wording.
- 2:31/q45/p1 Gabriel hears God's revelation and conveys it, while reported accounts distinguish relaying meaning from exact dictation. — Al-Suyūṭī cites heavenly trembling and chain-like sound reports and favors hearing; Abū Shāma connects descent language to Sunni eternal-Qur'an doctrine. Al-Juwaynī distinguishes equivalent expression from fixed dictated words. [5 notes: ITQAN(أبو شامة,الجويني,النبي ﷺ)]

## 2:31/q46 What other general observations about names are adduced?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ · sciences]
turns on: Miscellaneous naming and plural observations.
- 2:31/q46/p1 Names may be shared or multiplied, and spelling, plurality and derivation depend on usage. — Al-Suyūṭī notes shared or multiple sura names and prophetic names; al-Burhān discusses alif deletion in common foreign names and paucity plural patterns. These illustrate broader naming conventions. [5 notes: ITQAN(ابن عباس); BURHAN(أبو عمرو)]

## 2:31/q47 What does the transition 'so He said' indicate?  [فَقَالَ · rhetoric]
- 2:31/q47/p1 Fa marks an immediate consequence of the display, without a pause for reflection. — Abū Ḥayyān treats it as immediate succession; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the question follows as the result of the presentation. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:31/q48 How do names and relative clauses identify definite people?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether relative clauses are indefinite new information or recall an already known event.
- 2:31/q48/p1 A relative clause introduced by alladhī identifies a person through an event already known in general. — Al-Jurjānī says alladhī lets a clause modify a definite noun, analogous to dhū; he refines the claim that clauses are inherently indefinite because they convey new information. [4 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL(قالوا)]

## 2:31/q49 What does the challenge imply about God's authority to choose Adam?  [فَقَالَ أَنۢبِـُٔونِى · theology]
- 2:31/q49/p1 God's command and choice rest on His knowledge and sovereignty, not on creatures' judgment of what seems preferable. — Al-Qushayrī says God may command whom He wills and that good or bad follows divine judgment; Bursevī contrasts angelic worship with divine grace. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI]

## 2:31/q50 How are the angels' response and their limited knowledge described?  [ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ · interpretation]
- 2:31/q50/p1 They admitted inability and learned that knowledge is God's gift. — Al-Baghawī and al-Ṭabrisī report the admission; al-Ḥaralī treats it as submission and recognition that none has independent knowledge. [9 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(الملائكة); TABRISI(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]

## 2:31/q51 What does ‘upon the angels’ modify?  [عَلَى ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ · grammar]
- 2:31/q51/p1 The prepositional phrase attaches to ‘displayed them.’ — Al-Samīn explicitly gives this syntactic attachment. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:31/q52 What other name is reported for Adam?  [ءَادَمَ · reports]
- 2:31/q52/p1 Adam reportedly named himself Īsh, though Ibn ʿĀshūr suggests the reported form may be a corruption of Īth. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports that the Arabic Encyclopedia glosses Īsh as 'possessor of acquisition' and translates it as human or reader; he suspects the form was Īth and points to his discussion of 2:35. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(دائرة المعارف العربية)]

## 2:31/q53 How is the hamza in al-asmāʾ treated in recitation?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ · readings]
turns on: Reader-specific treatment of the hamza.
- 2:31/q53/p1 Ḥamza pauses before the hamza in al-asmāʾ and similar words. — Ibn Mujāhid reports Ḥamza's pause before the hamza in al-arḍ, al-asmāʾ and al-ākhira, while other readers do not pause. [1 notes: IBNMUJAHID(حمزة)]
- 2:31/q53/p2 Warsh transfers the hamza's vowel to the preceding consonant and omits the hamza. — Ibn Mujāhid reports this rule for Warsh from Nāfiʿ both within words and across word boundaries, citing 23:1 and 28:71. [1 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ورش عن نافع)]

## 2:31/q54 What gender is used in commands addressed to the angels?  [ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ أَنۢبِـُٔونِى · grammar]
- 2:31/q54/p1 Commands to the angels throughout the Qur'an use the masculine form. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī cites usjudū, anbiʾūnī and faqaʿū lahu sājidīn as examples. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH]
