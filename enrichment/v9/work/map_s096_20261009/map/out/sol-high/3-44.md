# 3:44: 36 questions

## 3:44/q01 What does “that” refer to?  [ذَٰلِكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the demonstrative points to the preceding narrative, a particular announcement, the discourse, or an inward reality.
- 3:44/q01/p1 The preceding accounts of Mary, Zechariah, John, Jesus, and related family events. — Al-Ṭabarī extends the reference to the narrative opened at 3:33; individual lists vary, including ʿImrān’s wife or Hannah, and some omit Jesus or John. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī singles out Mary's guardianship. [19 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 3:44/q01/p2 The glad tidings concerning the Messiah. — Al-Māwardī specifies the Messiah alone. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 3:44/q01/p3 The elevated, truthful, finely ordered discourse. — Al-Biqāʿī reads the demonstrative as pointing to the discourse itself. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 3:44/q01/p4 The unseen condition of the Prophet's own being. — Al-Qāshānī offers an inward interpretation addressed to the Prophet of the spirit. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 3:44/q02 How is “that is of the unseen reports” parsed?  [ذَٰلِكَ مِنْ أَنۢبَآءِ ٱلْغَيْبِ · grammar]
turns on: How the demonstrative, prepositional phrase, and revelation clause attach syntactically.
- 3:44/q02/p1 “That” is the subject and “of the unseen reports” its predicate. — Al-Naḥḥās allows this; Abū Ḥayyān prefers it; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī make the following revelation clause independent. [5 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 3:44/q02/p2 An omitted subject has “that” as predicate: “the matter is that.” — Al-Naḥḥās permits it. Al-Samīn gives circumstantial and pause analyses of the prepositional phrase. Abū al-Baqāʾ proposes it, but al-Ālūsī deems it unworthy of divine speech. [3 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)-]
- 3:44/q02/p3 “We reveal it” is the predicate of “that.” — The phrase “of the unseen reports” may modify the verb, describe its object, or be circumstantial from the demonstrative or its pronoun, as Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī record. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 3:44/q03 What does “reports” mean?  [أَنۢبَآءِ · meaning]
- 3:44/q03/p1 Accounts or news; its singular is nabaʾ. — Abū Mālik supplies the gloss; al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī identify the singular. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ibn al-Jawzī use the same sense. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو مالك); JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; MAJAZ; RAZI-FULL]

## 3:44/q04 What is “the unseen” here?  [ٱلْغَيْبِ · meaning]
- 3:44/q04/p1 What is hidden, absent from perception or knowledge, especially earlier events unknown to Muhammad. — Al-Ṭabarī derives it from ghāba, ‘be absent’; al-Jishumī opposes it to the perceptible. The Study Quran adds past, future, and other states of being. Al-Ṭabarī says few learned scriptural people knew these accounts. [12 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 3:44/q05 What does “We reveal it to you” mean, and what can waḥy denote?  [نُوحِيهِ إِلَيْكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the phrase is read as historical prophetic revelation or inward disclosure.
- 3:44/q05/p1 God communicates or sends this knowledge to Muhammad; waḥy broadly conveys meaning, often secretly, through revelation, inspiration, writing, gesture, speech, or a messenger. — Al-Ṭabarī glosses this instance ‘send down’ and catalogs bees (16:68), disciples (5:111), gesture (19:11), devilish suggestion (6:121), Gabriel's delivery (6:19), writing, and Kaʿb and Ruʾba's poetry. Al-Wāḥidī adds Moses' mother (28:7); al-Qurṭubī and Ibn Fāris add speed, sound, and cries for help; al-Bursevī adds 99:5. Al-Rāzī says Gabriel delivered it secretly. Al-ʿAjjāj's verse illustrates signaling or commanding. [35 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(كعب بن زهير); TABRISI(العجاج,رؤبة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن فارس,العجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 3:44/q05/p2 Inwardly, it is the hidden condition of the Prophet's own being disclosed to the Prophet of the spirit. — Al-Qāshānī interprets the address within the spiritual self. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 3:44/q06 To what does the pronoun in “We reveal it” return?  [نُوحِيهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun points to the demonstrative or to al-ghayb.
- 3:44/q06/p1 To “that,” the preceding account. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Jishumī, and al-Baghawī take the masculine pronoun from the demonstrative. Al-Ālūsī says this is the majority view. [4 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 3:44/q06/p2 To “the unseen,” including earlier and future accounts. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī prefer this because the present tense can cover continuing revelation, whereas reference to “that” confines it to completed events. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]

## 3:44/q07 Why is the revelation verb in the present tense?  [نُوحِيهِ · rhetoric]
- 3:44/q07/p1 It depicts revelation as continuing and renewed. — Abū Ḥayyān says an ongoing sense favors reference to al-ghayb; al-Ālūsī records this reading; al-Biqāʿī says revelation recurs in accounts like this. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قوم); BIQAI-FULL]

## 3:44/q08 What does disclosure of these unseen events establish?  [ذَٰلِكَ مِنْ أَنۢبَآءِ ٱلْغَيْبِ نُوحِيهِ إِلَيْكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the decisive evidence is Muhammad's lack of access to known reports or the absence of these details from available scripture.
- 3:44/q08/p1 It proves Muhammad's prophethood and the truth of revelation, since he neither witnessed the events nor learned them through books or teachers. — Al-Ṭabarī stresses obscurity even among scriptural communities, Muhammad's lack of reading, and the argument against their learned deniers. Al-Zajjāj cites consensus on his unletteredness. Al-Rāzī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, and others eliminate eyewitness, books, and instruction; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes altered scriptural accounts and compares 12:102 and 11:49. Elmalılı adds the duty to obey him. Abduh in Asad stresses the Qur'anic account's truth despite Christian-scripture differences. [45 notes: MUQATIL; IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); TAB-FULL(الحسن,محمد بن جعفر بن الزبير); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); TABRISI; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Muhammad 'Abduh); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Birçok müfessir,M. Reşîd Rızâ); DURR-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABATABAI+]
- 3:44/q08/p2 It particularly rebuts suspicion of borrowing because these details were absent among the People of the Book. — Abduh challenges the common account by citing verses where opponents alleged that Muhammad learned elsewhere. Rida replies that allegations do not alter historical circumstances; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says their books omit Qur'anic details about Zechariah. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Muhammed Abduh); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(M. Reşîd Rızâ)-]

## 3:44/q09 Why does the verse specifically deny Muhammad's presence?  [وَمَا كُنتَ لَدَيْهِمْ وَمَا كُنتَ لَدَيْهِمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the form is an ironic challenge or repeated independent proof.
- 3:44/q09/p1 It challenges deniers by leaving eyewitnessing as their absurd alternative once reading and instruction are excluded. — Al-Kashshāf compares 28:44, 28:46, and 12:102; al-Rāzī adds 11:49. Al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Bursevī, and Ibn al-Shaykh call the denial a taunt or mockery. Al-Ālūsī says the impossibility is evident. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL]
- 3:44/q09/p2 Each repeated denial of presence independently proves prophethood. — Shaykh al-Islām says the altered order in the second notice reinforces this. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الإسلام)]

## 3:44/q10 What does “with them” mean?  [لَدَيْهِمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the negation describes only physical presence or also direct awareness.
- 3:44/q10/p1 In their presence, with or among them. — Abū Mālik, al-Zajjāj, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, and others give this spatial sense. Al-Ḥarallī says ladā can imply presence with something elevated; al-Itqān compares ladā and ʿinda and distinguishes ladun by endpoint, governance, inflection, and annexation. Abū Ḥayyān compares contextual place reference in 100:4. [16 notes: MAJAZ; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(أبو مالك,محمد بن إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ITQAN(الراغب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 3:44/q10/p2 The Prophet was neither present nor aware of their affairs. — Ibn Isḥāq makes the negation cover knowledge as well as physical presence. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق)]

## 3:44/q11 Who is addressed and who are “they” in the presence clauses?  [وَمَا كُنتَ لَدَيْهِمْ · referent]
turns on: Literal disputants in the story versus powers in the inward reading.
- 3:44/q11/p1 The addressee is Muhammad; “they” are the unnamed disputants over Mary's guardianship. — Qatāda explicitly identifies Muhammad. Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī infer the plural antecedent from context; Asad specifies Temple priests including Zechariah. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); STUDYQURAN(IK, M)]
- 3:44/q11/p2 Inwardly, “they” are spiritual and psychic powers competing to govern the soul. — Al-Qāshānī places their conflict in the chest, before and during discipline, with spiritual powers prevailing by God's aid. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 3:44/q12 How do the two “when” clauses attach grammatically?  [إِذْ يُلْقُونَ إِذْ يَخْتَصِمُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether idh attaches to an existential predicate or kāna, and whether the second clause marks a distinct time.
- 3:44/q12/p1 The first temporal clause is governed by the existential sense of “with them”; the second parallels it. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn reject attaching the first to defective kāna, which supplies no event; al-Ālūsī rejects Abū al-Baqāʾ's kāna attachment. Al-Samīn parses the last absence in parallel. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الفارسي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); BIQAI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 3:44/q12/p2 The first “when” is governed by “you were not,” with kāna understood as eventive. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī offer this; al-Samīn suggests Fārisī may mean ‘you were present then.’ Al-Naḥḥās calls idh an accusative adverb of time. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; TABRISI(أبو علي); NAHHAS; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)-]
- 3:44/q12/p3 The second “when” is tied to “they dispute” at the time of the angels' speech. — Al-Zajjāj locates that dispute by 3:45. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]
- 3:44/q12/p4 The second “when” is governed by the second “you were not with them” or substitutes for the first if events coincide. — Al-Zajjāj reports the former; Abū ʿAlī offers the latter. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ; TABRISI(أبو علي)]

## 3:44/q13 Why is the imperfect used after “when”?  [يُلْقُونَ · grammar]
- 3:44/q13/p1 The imperfect expresses a past event in this temporal setting. — Abū Ḥayyān states this explicitly; al-Ṭabarī glosses it ‘when they were casting.’ [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TAB-FULL]

## 3:44/q14 What does “casting” mean here?  [يُلْقُونَ · meaning]
- 3:44/q14/p1 Throwing their objects as a way of drawing lots. — Al-Dāmghānī lists drawing lots among senses of alqā, alongside whispering, creating, placing, sending down, clothing, entering, throwing, speaking, and seating; Ibn Qutayba and others identify the action as a lot. [8 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; DAMGHANI; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]

## 3:44/q15 What were the “pens” cast?  [أَقْلَٰمَهُمْ · referent]
turns on: Ordinary writing-pen usage, lots or arrows, staffs, and inscribed name shafts; objections invoke the distinction from forbidden azlām.
- 3:44/q15/p1 Writing pens, especially pens used to write the Torah or revelation. — Al-Naḥḥās, al-Qāḍī in al-Rāzī, and al-Qurṭubī favor ordinary usage and reject identifying them with forbidden azlām; al-Qāḍī concedes derivation permits rods. Al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, and others say the scripture pens were selected for blessing. Al-Rāzī attributes this to most commentators; Ibn ʿAbbās calls the holders revelation scribes. [23 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(عطاء); NAHHAS; THALABI(قيل); JISHUMI(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الأكثرون,القاضي); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس، ابن جبير، السدي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; NAHHAS+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 3:44/q15/p2 Lottery arrows, lots, or marked shafts; some say divining arrows. — Ibn Qutayba equates these with azlām; al-Zajjāj calls them marked. Abū Muslim describes named slips or arrows like shares of a camel; al-Kāzarūnī says copper shafts. Asad translates ‘reeds’ and compares a Semitic practice. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī equates qalam with a share or lot. [29 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(عطاء); THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو مسلم,قيل); SAMIN-DURR; DURR-FULL(عطاء); ALUSI-FULL(الكازروني); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة، الزجاج)+; NAHHAS-; RAZI-FULL(القاضي)-; QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 3:44/q15/p3 Staffs or sticks cast into water. — Al-Rabīʿ is the chief transmitter; reports say Zechariah's staff faced the current and won. Ibn al-Jawzī lists this as a separate view. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الربيع); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الربيع بن أنس); RAZI-FULL(الربيع); DURR-FULL(الربيع); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 3:44/q15/p4 Slips or shafts inscribed with contestants' names. — Abū Muslim likens these to camel-share arrows; the source does not require they be writing pens. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو مسلم)]

## 3:44/q16 How is qalam derived and what other things can it mean?  [أَقْلَٰمَهُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Which root or cognate best explains the noun.
- 3:44/q16/p1 Aqlām is the plural of qalam, from cutting, trimming, or sharpening; the word can denote a pen, cut shaft, nail clipping, or related implement. — Al-Zajjāj compares repeated pen sharpening and nail trimming; al-Wāḥidī says both ends of lot arrows are cut. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn cite Zuhayr's unclipped lion claws and a crescent likened to a clipping; al-Ṭabrisī adds spear joints. Abū Muslim extends cutting to lots. Al-Samīn calls qalam a passive faʿl form. Al-Thaʿlabī notes the singular; Ibn Qutayba supplies azlām and zalam/zulam. [19 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة، الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زهير); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 3:44/q16/p2 Alternatively qalam is linked to qillāma, a thin weak plant. — Abū Ḥayyān also mentions qalamah and a nail clipping; al-Samīn records the weak-plant derivation. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 3:44/q17 Who took part in the lot and dispute?  [يُلْقُونَ أَقْلَٰمَهُمْ يَخْتَصِمُونَ · referent]
turns on: The reports identify the contestants at differing levels of specificity, and one calls them prophets.
- 3:44/q17/p1 Zechariah and a group of Israelite Temple attendants, priests, or sanctuary worshippers. — Mujāhid names Zechariah and companions; Ibn ʿAbbās calls them sanctuary worshippers and revelation scribes. Ibn Kathīr's ʿIkrima report has temple custodians; Asad identifies priests, and Kuranyolu says likely senior clergy. [13 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,مجاهد، الضحاك); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL(منهم من قال); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK, M)]
- 3:44/q17/p2 Scholars, religious leaders, and scribes of revelation. — Al-Rāzī calls them people of learning and devotion; Kuranyolu reasons that peers of Zechariah who accepted a lot were senior clergy. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(منهم من قال); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود وابن عباس وناس من الصحابة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 3:44/q17/p3 Prophets disputed Mary's custody. — Ibn ʿAbbās's report through ʿAṭāʾ in al-Wāḥidī calls the contestants prophets. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)]

## 3:44/q18 When was the lot held?  [إِذْ يُلْقُونَ أَقْلَٰمَهُمْ · reports]
turns on: Whether the account concerns initial custody or a later transfer, and whether narrative order is chronological.
- 3:44/q18/p1 At Mary's birth or presentation as a vowed child, to assign her first guardian. — Al-Ṭabrisī attributes this to most; al-Ālūsī calls it the relied-upon view. Al-Bāqir says the family disputed after birth, when her father was dead. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the objection from story order because Qur'anic accounts can repeat details, as at 12:102. [22 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,قتادة); THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI(الأكثر); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة); TABATABAI(الباقر عليه السلام); STUDYQURAN(IK, M); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 3:44/q18/p2 Later, when Zechariah could no longer support or raise Mary, especially in hardship or adulthood. — Ibn Isḥāq reports famine and a transfer of support; Saʿīd says Zechariah first took her without lots, then appealed for help. Some infer later timing from the Qur'an placing the lot after the initial guardianship. [7 notes: IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); MAWARDI-FULL(سعيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن إسحاق); TABRISI(بعضهم); JISHUMI(قتادة); TABATABAI(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 3:44/q18/p3 The lot happened twice, at the first placement and a later crisis. — Al-Ālūsī mentions this as still weaker than the later-only view. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 3:44/q19 Why did they draw lots or dispute over Mary?  [أَيُّهُمْ يَكْفُلُ مَرْيَمَ يَخْتَصِمُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the competition was for a coveted privilege or to allocate a burdensome duty.
- 3:44/q19/p1 They competed eagerly for the honor or reward of caring for her. — Qatāda and al-Ṭabrisī see divine amazement at their eagerness and Mary's distinction; Ibn Kathīr mentions reward, al-Biqāʿī honor, al-Rāzī her mother's prayers. Al-Ālūsī calls the Qatāda reading strained but better than its rival. [24 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI; JISHUMI(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور العلماء); TABRISI(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK, M); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 3:44/q19/p2 They resisted assuming her support amid severe hardship, so a lot assigned the burden. — Ibn Isḥāq and Saʿīd describe Zechariah seeking a replacement; al-Ṭabrisī says this view treats their resistance as the marvel. Al-Ālūsī calls it weak in reason and transmission. [6 notes: IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); MAWARDI-FULL(سعيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن إسحاق); JISHUMI(قتادة); TABRISI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]

## 3:44/q20 What prompted eagerness to care for Mary?  [مَرْيَمَ · reports]
turns on: Whether status, consecration, prophecy, or her intrinsic merit motivated the contestants.
- 3:44/q20/p1 Her father ʿImrān was their leader or imam. — Qatāda calls Mary the daughter of their chief; Ibn Kathīr's ʿIkrima report says custodians recognized her as their prayer leader's daughter. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); RAZI-FULL(قال بعضهم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 3:44/q20/p2 Her mother had consecrated her to divine worship and Temple service. — Al-Rāzī gives this explanation; al-Wāḥidī and Ibn Kathīr recount the vowed offering. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(قال بعضهم); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 3:44/q20/p3 Scripture foretold Mary and Jesus' significance. — Al-Rāzī reports this explanation; Kuranyolu repeats it. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(آخرون); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 3:44/q20/p4 Mary's own divine distinction and the reward of fostering her attracted them. — Qatāda, Ibn Kathīr, and al-Biqāʿī give merit, reward, and honor as grounds. [4 notes: JISHUMI(قتادة); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 3:44/q21 How was the lot conducted and what sign won it?  [إِذْ يُلْقُونَ أَقْلَٰمَهُمْ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on current, floating, stillness, and named slips; the verse leaves mechanics unstated.
- 3:44/q21/p1 Objects were cast in flowing water; Zechariah's alone resisted or moved upstream while the rest drifted, so he won. — ʿIkrima and al-Rabīʿ describe the countercurrent sign with pens or staffs. Ibn Kathīr places it in the Jordan. Al-Rāzī says the verse itself leaves the procedure unspecified; Abū Ḥayyān similarly leaves its true detail to God. [14 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الربيع,عكرمة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(الربيع); RAZI-FULL(الأكثرون,الربيع); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة، والسدي، وقتادة، والربيع بن أنس); DURR-FULL(الربيع,عكرمة); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 3:44/q21/p2 Zechariah's pen floated or rose above the other objects; some say the others sank. — Ibn ʿAbbās via ʿAṭāʾ describes his arrow floating while others sank; al-Suddī and Ibn Isḥāq report rising or standing above. A prophetic report in al-Qurṭubī calls rising a sign for a prophet. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(الربيع); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 3:44/q21/p3 The winning pen was the one that stayed still in the flowing water. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this variant without naming the winner in that sentence. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 3:44/q21/p4 They drew marked names or arrows, and the selected name assigned custody. — Abū Muslim compares camel-share arrows. Al-Shāfiʿī's later legal method uses hidden equal name slips in clay pellets; that is an analogy, not a claim about Mary's exact procedure. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو مسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي ومن قال بها)]
- 3:44/q21/p5 The verse does not specify exactly how the lot was performed. — Al-Rāzī says the report of countercurrent pens is extra detail; Abū Ḥayyān explicitly suspends judgment about receptacle, method, and outcome sign. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 3:44/q22 How many rounds or lots were used?  [أَقْلَٰمَهُمْ · reports]
turns on: The transmitted counts differ.
- 3:44/q22/p1 Three drawings were attempted. — Ibn ʿAbbās says a boy first drew Zechariah's pen; the others rejected that outcome and moved to a water test. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 3:44/q22/p2 Six lots were involved. — Al-Bāqir gives six, while al-Ālūsī notes disagreement over the number. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الباقر)]

## 3:44/q23 Who won or received Mary's guardianship?  [أَيُّهُمْ يَكْفُلُ مَرْيَمَ · reports]
turns on: Whether the lot assigned initial custody to Zechariah or later support to Jureij.
- 3:44/q23/p1 Zechariah won the lot and became Mary's guardian. — Qatāda, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Bāqir, and others report this; Qatāda says he was married to her sister, while other accounts call his wife her maternal aunt. - 3:44/q23/p2 Zechariah initially cared for Mary, but a later hardship lot selected Jureij, an Israelite carpenter and monk. — Ibn Isḥāq says Zechariah could no longer support her; this is a different chronology from a first-custody lot won by Zechariah. [1 notes: IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق)]

## 3:44/q24 What does “take care of” mean?  [يَكْفُلُ · meaning]
- 3:44/q24/p1 To take Mary in, raise her, guarantee her support, and undertake her affairs. — Al-Zajjāj explains kafāla as guaranteeing responsibility; al-Ṭabarī glosses it care, and al-Baghawī adds upbringing. ## 3:44/q25 How is “which of them would care for Mary” construed?  [أَيُّهُمْ يَكْفُلُ مَرْيَمَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the interrogative depends on an omitted cognitive verb, quoted speech, or apposition.
- 3:44/q25/p1 It is an interrogative subject and predicate in the slot of an omitted verb of looking, learning, or considering; the lot sought the answer. — Al-Ṭabarī says ayy remains subject despite an implied accusative sense; al-Naḥḥās cites Sībawayh and warns an accusative changes meaning. Al-Akhfash compares knowledge questions at 18:12 and 67:2, contrasting 19:69 and discussion of 6:154 and Arab relative clauses. Ibn al-Ḥājib and Ibn Mālik say ‘look’ can govern the interrogative. Some call the phrase purposive or circumstantial. [23 notes: AKHFASH(بعض العرب); TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن الحاجب وابن مالك,السكاكي)]
- 3:44/q25/p2 An omitted ‘they say’ makes the clause quoted speech: ‘Which of us will care for Mary?’ — Al-Zamakhsharī and others supply saying; al-Ālūsī notes critics call it purposeless, while defenders take ‘say’ as announcing the lot's judgment. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]
- 3:44/q25/p3 No verb is omitted; the interrogative is appositive to the plural pronoun. — Al-Ālūsī reports exacting scholars' proposal but calls it strained. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين)]

## 3:44/q26 What was their “dispute”?  [يَخْتَصِمُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the disputants are historical caretakers or faculties of the soul.
- 3:44/q26/p1 They openly argued or competed over who would be Mary's guardian. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya stresses spoken debate; al-Ṭabarī says they argued over entitlement. Al-Rāzī says it could precede or follow the lot, and Abū Ḥayyān says the dispute could be the lot itself or another later argument. - 3:44/q26/p2 Inwardly, psychic and spiritual powers contend in the chest for leadership of the soul. — Al-Qāshānī says discipline and divine help lead the spiritual powers to prevail. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 3:44/q27 Did the dispute precede or follow the lot?  [إِذْ يَخْتَصِمُونَ · reports]
turns on: The verse does not state whether the named dispute is before, during, or after drawing.
- 3:44/q27/p1 The dispute preceded the lot and prompted its use. — The first-guardianship accounts describe competing claims resolved by a draw; al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī acknowledge this sequence. - 3:44/q27/p2 The dispute followed the lot or was itself the lot contest. — Al-Rāzī leaves either order open; Abū Ḥayyān says the dispute may be identical with the drawing or later; al-Ālūsī records the after-lot view. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 3:44/q28 How does the passage connect to surrounding verses?  [* · links]
- 3:44/q28/p1 It bridges Mary's opening account to the continuing account of Jesus. — Al-Biqāʿī says the reminder stands near the middle to recall what preceded and prepare what follows, keeping Mary central in the answer to Najran's Christians; Elmalılı says Jesus' full story follows. ## 3:44/q30 What does the episode establish about drawing lots in law?  [يُلْقُونَ أَقْلَٰمَهُمْ · law]

- 3:44/q30/p1 Lots can fairly settle competing equal claims or allocate duties when no other criterion decides. — Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī infer disputed rights. ## 3:44/q36 How does the cited modern discussion relate empirical knowledge to religion and interpretation?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes distinguish empirical science's scope from the way religious texts are interpreted.
- 3:44/q36/p1 Science is empirical, testable, revisable, and limited to the shared physical world; religion and other human domains retain distinct roles and need mutual respect. — Y. Khuli contrasts logical mathematics with experimental science, details hypothesis testing and provisional progress, and says science and religion answer different civilizational needs. Baumer's Bacon example preserves scientific independence; Bachelard stresses correction; Feyerabend rejects science as the sole superior tradition. The author rejects both attacking science from scripture and freezing scripture to transient theories. [23 notes: YKHULI-TAJDID(بول فييرآبند,جاستون باشلار,فرانكلين باومر)]
- 3:44/q36/p2 Religious interpretation should remain open to reason, dialogue, changing circumstances, and renewed readings of sacred texts. — Y. Khuli invokes Schleiermacher through Tillich on ordinary religious language and Gadamer's hermeneutics, explaining text–reader encounter and methodical religious enlightenment. [7 notes: YKHULI-TAJDID(فردريش شلايرماخر)]
