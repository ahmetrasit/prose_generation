# 53:39: 16 questions

## 53:39/q01 How is وَأَن parsed and linked?  [وَأَن · grammar]
turns on: Which earlier construction governs the conjunction.
- 53:39/q01/p1 It continues the preceding clauses introduced by an, as part of the scriptures' reported contents. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Wāḥidī coordinate it with the previous clause. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the majority read fatḥa, while Abū al-Sammāl reads kasra from v. 42. Al-Samīn permits genitive, nominative, or accusative coordination and says lightened an is not separated from nonconjugating laysa. Bursevī gives li-l-insān as predicate and mā saʿā as subject; al-Ālūsī follows the preceding wa-anna. [7 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 53:39/q01/p2 It may coordinate with the earlier prepositional phrase or with the earlier allā clause. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says these produce reduced inna and explanatory an respectively; both reinforce 53:38. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:39/q02 What does لِلْإِنسَٰنِ mean grammatically?  [لِلْإِنسَٰنِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether lām signifies entitlement or is read as ʿalā.
- 53:39/q02/p1 The lām marks a person's due, possession, or particular entitlement. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya makes strict entitlement the pivot; al-Qurṭubī says ownership or obligation; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes lasting true ownership from possessions lost at death. Elmalılı says ikhtiṣāṣ and prefers verbal-noun mā because sought objects need not be obtained. Al-Rāzī contrasts lām for benefit with ʿalā for liability. [9 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); ELMALILI(İbn Atıyye); TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 53:39/q02/p2 The lām can mean ʿalā: what is upon or owed by a person. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this parse and Ibn ʿĀshūr lists it among harmonizations. Al-Ālūsī rejects it as contrary to apparent wording and the warning against the one who turned away and gave little. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 53:39/q03 Who does الْإِنسَٰنِ include?  [الْإِنسَٰنِ · referent]
turns on: Whether a generic human noun has universal or restricted scope.
- 53:39/q03/p1 It is generic and includes believers and unbelievers. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says a generic noun under negation is universal; Abū Ḥayyān calls this apparent. Al-Rāzī maintains it despite objections concerning kin's charity, fasting and prayer and tenfold reward; he calls the restricted reading weak. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 53:39/q03/p2 It means the unbeliever; believers may also receive others' efforts. — Al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas is repeatedly cited. Al-Rāzī objects that restricting the noun is weak. [9 notes: THALABI(الربيع بن أنس); BAGHAWI-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الربيع بن أنس); QURTUBI-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الربيع); BURSEVI(الربيع بن أنس); ALUSI-FULL(الربيع); IBNASHUR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); RAZI-FULL-]

## 53:39/q04 What does سَعَىٰ mean?  [سَعَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether saʿy is work, intention, causal contribution, or relinquished effort.
- 53:39/q04/p1 It denotes one's work, deeds, or earning. — Al-Thaʿlabī says deeds; al-Baghawī cites 92:4. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says earning. Bursevī and al-Rāghib define literal brisk walking slower than running, extended to earnest effort in good or evil; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the extension metaphor or metonymy. Al-Rāzī says seeking an object would normally take fīhi. [7 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب)]
- 53:39/q04/p2 It means what the person intends. — Al-Warrāq cites resurrection according to intention. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ distinguishes seeking God's pleasure from seeking reward. Asad cites a widely transmitted intention hadith and includes deliberate omissions and expressed belief in conscious action. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو بكر الوراق); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو بكر الوراق); SULAMI(ابن عطاء); BURSEVI(ابن عطاء); ASAD-NOTES(Asad,Prophet)]
- 53:39/q04/p3 One's striving includes one's causal contribution to another's deed. — Al-Ṭabrisī names a call to faith; Ibn al-Zāghūnī names kin, child, friend and service to religion; al-Biqāʿī and Islāḥī include ties, teaching, example and financial help. [5 notes: TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(علي بن عبيد الله الزاغوني); BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 53:39/q04/p4 It is the effort one repudiates as one's own. — Al-Wāsiṭī says a person has only what they disclaim of their striving. [1 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي)]

## 53:39/q05 Why is سَعَىٰ in the past tense?  [سَعَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 53:39/q05/p1 Past tense urges actual good work; future promises have not yet been earned. — Al-Rāzī contrasts already performed prayer and charity with unperformed promises, which are not recorded. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:39/q06 What is مَا in مَا سَعَىٰ?  [مَا سَعَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā denotes the act or its object.
- 53:39/q06/p1 It is a verbal noun denoting striving itself. — Bursevī and al-Ālūsī allow this. Elmalılı favors it because not every sought object becomes one's property; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers its broader sense in an echo of this verse. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI+]
- 53:39/q06/p2 It is a relative pronoun denoting what one strove for. — Bursevī and al-Ālūsī allow this. Al-Rāzī says the work itself is preserved with God, citing 99:7, or its reward is intended. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]

## 53:39/q07 What does the verse say a person receives?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the limit applies to all deeds, a kind of deed, strict entitlement, or material earnings.
- 53:39/q07/p1 One receives recompense for one's own deeds, good for good and evil for evil. — Muqātil locates it in the Hereafter; Ibn Qutayba and al-Wāḥidī stress preparation for it. Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq include good and evil; al-Rāzī reports this as well known. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts enduring deeds with wealth, children and status lost at death. [20 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(المشهور); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق)+]
- 53:39/q07/p2 The apparent point concerns benefits from good deeds. — Al-Rāzī and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite lām for benefit rather than ʿalā for liability. Al-Rāzī says v. 41's full recompense suits good while evil can be matched, reduced or forgiven. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 53:39/q07/p3 The restriction may concern evil deeds specifically. — Al-Qurṭubī cites a sound Muslim report: an unperformed good intention is credited, accomplished good multiplied up to seven hundred, and accomplished evil counted once. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة عن رسول الله)]
- 53:39/q07/p4 Only one's own earned deed is owed; more can come by divine grace. — Al-Ḥusayn ibn al-Faḍl reconciles the verse with multiplication, citing 2:261 or 2:245; al-Thaʿlabī tells Ibn Ṭāhir's questions about Cain and God's daily act and recounts al-Ḥusayn's reply and the other view of Cain's regret. Ibn ʿAṭiyya names intercession, relatives, multiplication and forgiveness. Al-Qurṭubī cites tenfold to seven-hundredfold and Abū Hurayra's two-millionfold report. Al-Rāzī says even tenfold reward is sought in hoping for God's promise, citing 6:160. [23 notes: THALABI(الحسين بن الفضل); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسين بن الفضل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسين بن الفضل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسين بن الفضل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الحسين بن الفضل); ALUSI-FULL(الحسين بن الفضل); ELMALILI(Hüseyin b. Fazl,İbn Atıyye); IBNASHUR-FULL(الحسين بن الفضل); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 53:39/q07/p5 Another's gift can benefit someone without becoming that person's earned entitlement. — Al-Qurṭubī compares children admitted to Paradise without deeds of their own. Elmalılı distinguishes true ownership from gifts and commanded cooperation. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says bodily and financial good can benefit both doer and recipient, citing Saʿd's charity for his mother. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ELMALILI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 53:39/q07/p6 The verse denies reliance on another's work. — Ibn ʿĀshūr lists this among harmonizations with reports of others' good deeds. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:39/q07/p7 Earnings should be proportional to one's labor. — Some readers infer material earnings proportionate to labor; Islāḥī rejects this as unrelated to the verse's context. [0 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Some people having a socialistic mentality)-]
- 53:39/q07/p8 Worldly provision tests gratitude and patience; the Hereafter rewards earned deeds. — Islāḥī distinguishes the distribution of worldly provision from final reward and punishment; Kur'an Yolu affirms honest labor's value before God and places voluntary choice within a test whose full secrets exceed human knowledge. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 53:39/q08 Can another person's deed benefit someone under this verse?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether entitlement, causal connection, proxy intention, or limited scope governs others' deeds.
- 53:39/q08/p1 Another's deed gives no earned reward apart from one's own effort. — The preceding ban on bearing another's burden is parallel, according to al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Study Quran. Islāḥī cites 2:134, Noah's son, Abraham's father, Lot's wife and Pharaoh's wife in 66:11 against automatic inherited reward. [13 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; QURTUBI-FULL(أكثر أهل التأويل)]
- 53:39/q08/p2 Another's deed can count as one's own through faith or causal effort. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Ālūsī and Ebussuud require faith, good conduct or a causal role. Al-Ālūsī recounts al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil's vow of a hundred camels, Hishām's fifty sacrifices and the Prophet's conditional statement about monotheism. Ibn Kathīr treats endowment, useful teaching and a child's prayer as traces of one's efforts, citing 36:12 and a hadith that a child is one's earning. Islāḥī adds ties of faith, teaching and aid. [13 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(علي بن عبيد الله الزاغوني); BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(عمرو بن شعيب عن أبيه عن جده); ELMALILI(Ebussuud); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]
- 53:39/q08/p3 Another's deed can count through a proxy intending it for the recipient. — Al-Zamakhsharī's second answer contrasts self-directed deeds with work for the deceased. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī treat the performer as agent; al-Ālūsī notes figurative usage. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 53:39/q08/p4 Believers can receive others' efforts because the restriction concerns unbelievers. — Al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas is transmitted by these sources. [9 notes: THALABI(الربيع بن أنس); BAGHAWI-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الربيع بن أنس); QURTUBI-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الربيع); BURSEVI(الربيع بن أنس); ALUSI-FULL(الربيع); IBNASHUR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس)]
- 53:39/q08/p5 Muslims can benefit from others because the rule concerned Abraham's and Moses's peoples. — ʿIkrima's account is cited repeatedly; Ibn ʿĀshūr treats acceptance of another's deed as a special concession. [11 notes: THALABI(عكرمة); WAHIDI-BASIT(عكرمة); BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عكرمة); TABRISI(عكرمة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عكرمة); BURSEVI(عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة); IBNASHUR-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 53:39/q08/p6 An unbeliever's good is paid in worldly life, leaving none for the Hereafter. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī and Ibn al-Jawzī recount ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ubayy's shirt for al-ʿAbbās and the Prophet's reciprocal shroud. [5 notes: THALABI(عبد الله بن أبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(غير مسمى); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الثعلبي)]
- 53:39/q08/p7 Reports show benefit through charity, pilgrimage, prayer, or continuing deeds. — They include Saʿd's charity for his mother, a dead father's or sister's Hajj, a child's Hajj, and posthumous prayer. Al-Qurṭubī calls charity undisputed, Bursevī cites many reports, and an unnamed report in al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says even a nāṣib's burden can be lightened by a gifted deed. [24 notes: THALABI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(رسول الله); BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي,سعد بن عبادة عن النبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سعد بن عبادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة عن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,سعد بن عبادة,عائشة); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,رواية غير مسماة); STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]

## 53:39/q09 Has this rule been abrogated or qualified?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether 52:21 alters a factual report, qualifies scope, or leaves the rule intact.
- 53:39/q09/p1 It was abrogated for this community by 52:21. — Ibn ʿAbbās is cited, including through al-Wālibī: children enter Paradise and rise by parental righteousness. Al-Qurṭubī adds family intercession and 4:11; Bursevī mentions it too. Al-Durr calls 52:21 a qualification; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports ʿIkrima's earlier-law view. [13 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الوالبي عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 53:39/q09/p2 It remains definitive; a report of fact cannot be abrogated. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Rāzī and Elmalılı challenge attribution or naskh's conditions. The majority holds the rule in force. Elmalılı says 52:21 is consistent and also pledges each person to earnings. Al-Ālūsī transmits Abū Ḥayyān's objection that a report, not a command, cannot be abrogated. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أكثر أهل التأويل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الجمهور); ELMALILI(İbn Abbas); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور)+]
- 53:39/q09/p3 It can be specified for Muslims without being abrogated. — Al-Ālūsī says an earlier-scripture report may be qualified. Ibn ʿĀshūr lists exception, specification or abrogation among harmonizations. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:39/q09/p4 Proxy worship depends on whether the rule remains operative. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī bar it absent abrogation, except for proven cases such as Hajj; the reported abrogation removes that bar. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أصحابنا); TABRISI]

## 53:39/q10 How does the verse connect to its surroundings and earlier scriptures?  [* · links]
turns on: Which nearby or intertextual frame explains the personal-accountability statement.
- 53:39/q10/p1 It belongs to the teachings in Abraham's and Moses's scrolls. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Nasafī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Study Quran connect it to 53:36–37; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī counts seventeen verses. Islāḥī contrasts their teachings and sacrifices with inherited claims. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 53:39/q10/p2 It completes the rule that no soul bears another's burden. — Al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say another's good cannot simply transfer any more than their sins; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it blocks an inference that only sins are personal. Al-Rāzī links Abraham's failed prayer for his father with both rules; he says the no-burden rule is the most apparent scroll content, though the scrolls hold more. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 53:39/q10/p3 It is compared with verses about varied effort, individual earnings and enduring traces. — Al-Thaʿlabī cites 92:4; Elmalılı cites 2:286 and 52:21. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects 36:12, 4:9 and 8:37 to traces of acts and descendants; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ uses an echo of this verse. [5 notes: THALABI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI]
- 53:39/q10/p4 It rebukes claims to ancestral rank without personal effort. — Islāḥī says people boasting of Abraham or Moses while withholding charity should heed these prophets' teaching. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:39/q11 What continuing benefit do cited reports describe?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which report and kind of continuing credit is cited.
- 53:39/q11/p1 After death, three sources continue: charity, beneficial teaching or practice, and a righteous child's prayer. — A Prophetic report is cited by al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr and Study Quran; Imam al-Ṣādiq names guided practice. Ibn Kathīr treats these as effects of one's own work, citing 36:12. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة عن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]
- 53:39/q11/p2 A sick believer still receives credit for habitual deeds performed while healthy. — Imam al-Ṣādiq reports the Prophet's account of God's instruction to the recording angel during illness. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 53:39/q11/p3 The Prophet recited these verses, said the return-to-God formula, and became subdued. — Ibn ʿAbbās reports the practice. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 53:39/q12 Can worship or gifts be performed for the deceased?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether the act discharges a duty, gifts reward, or is bodily or financial.
- 53:39/q12/p1 Proxy devotional acts are barred unless specific evidence permits them. — Al-Wāḥidī's school and al-Ṭabrisī name Hajj as an exception. Al-Qurṭubī reports agreement that no one prays for another. Ibn ʿĀshūr says an act cannot discharge another's personal duty, while assuming financial liability for accidental killing is prescribed support. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أصحابنا); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(أكثر أهل التأويل); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:39/q12/p2 Bodily acts differ from financial acts, and gifting reward is prayer for grace. — Abū Ḥayyān reports the bodily/financial distinction; Ibn al-Humām attributes one to Mālik and al-Shāfiʿī. Al-Khafājī locates dispute in discharge of another's bodily duty during or after life, with or without permission; feeding and asking God to convey reward are different. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن الهمام,الخفاجي)]
- 53:39/q12/p3 Both bodily and financial good can benefit the deceased. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya invokes Saʿd's charity for his mother; al-Qurṭubī reports ʿĀʾisha's retreat and manumission for her brother and the Prophet's permission of charity, water being best. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī separates gifting reward from proxy performance. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(سعد بن عبادة عن النبي,عائشة); TABATABAI(رواية غير مسماة)]
- 53:39/q12/p4 An heir's proxy fasting belonged to early Islam and was abrogated. — Al-Ṭaḥāwī preserves proxy Hajj but not fasting. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الطحاوي)]

## 53:39/q13 May pilgrimage be performed for another?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether Hajj is delegable and what conditions apply.
- 53:39/q13/p1 Proxy Hajj is allowed for a dead or unable person under varying conditions. — The Prophet authorizes it for a deceased father and likens a dead sister's vow to paying God's debt. Al-Shāfiʿī permits it; the Ḥanafīs require incapacity lasting to death, with recovery requiring personal Hajj; Aḥmad does not. Ibn Ḥabīb permits a son without bequest and an elderly person's proxy; Ibn al-Qaṣṣār allows it for dead but not incapacitated living persons; Ibn ʿArafa bought it for a ruler under a non-Mālikī school. [13 notes: THALABI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(رسول الله); TABRISI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(أحمد بن حنبل,ابن القصار,ابن حبيب,ابن عرفة,الحنفية,الشافعي,صاحب التوضيح); QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي وغيره)]
- 53:39/q13/p2 Mālik generally rejects it, except when a will stipulates it. — Al-Qurṭubī and Ibn ʿĀshūr report the limitation. Ibn al-Faras counts bequeathed obligatory Hajj as the deceased's striving; al-Bājī says Mālik enforced a bequest and required the hired proxy to have done Hajj first. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مالك); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن الفرس,الباجي,مالك)]
- 53:39/q13/p3 Some authorities dislike or forbid proxy Hajj. — Ibn al-Jallāb dislikes hiring for an incapable person but does not void the agreement. Some Mālikī books report a different Abū Ḥanīfa position; one Shāfiʿī opinion forbids it citing this verse. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو حنيفة,ابن الجلاب,الشافعي)]
- 53:39/q13/p4 The Prophet affirmed a child's Hajj and the mother's separate reward. — Al-Baghawī and Bursevī transmit the question and answer. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام)]

## 53:39/q14 Does gifted Qur'an recitation or voluntary worship reach the dead?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether recitation is transferable and how intention or payment affects it.
- 53:39/q14/p1 Recitation's reward does not reach the deceased. — Al-Shāfiʿī and followers cite the verse, absence of Prophetic recommendation or Companion precedent, and the rule that worship requires text rather than analogy. Al-Nawawī calls it the well-known Shāfiʿī view; the Itqān author's school agrees. Another Shāfiʿī view excludes voluntary prayer and other voluntary acts. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(الشافعي ومن اتبعه); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(النووي); IBNASHUR-FULL(الشافعي)]
- 53:39/q14/p2 Recitation and voluntary rewards can reach the deceased. — The other three imams, Aḥmad and some Shāfiʿīs are reported in favor; Ibn al-Faras reports Mālik's school permits it and Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects an unsupported blanket ban attributed to Mālik. Al-Biqāʿī includes recitation among beneficial ties. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares ʿĀʾisha's protective recitation over the Prophet and cites payment for al-Fātiḥa as healing. [11 notes: ITQAN(الأئمة الثلاثة); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(النووي); IBNASHUR-FULL(أحمد,أحمد بن حنبل,ابن الفرس,عائشة)]
- 53:39/q14/p3 Ask God after recitation to convey its reward; paid recitation apparently yields none. — Al-Ālūsī favors supplication after reciting, though some require initial intention. Payment for teaching is lawful. Ibn ʿĀshūr says hired Hajj is possible through its expense, but prayer and fasting apparently cannot be hired. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 53:39/q15 How is striving understood on the spiritual path?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the spiritual fruit is earned, divinely granted, or beyond ordinary striving.
- 53:39/q15/p1 Effort belongs to wayfaring; realization depends on divine carrying or attraction. — Al-Naṣrābādhī contrasts the paths of wayfaring and Reality, seeing the kingdom and seeing the King, with poems about the rare traveler and the beloved. Bursevī cites Jāmī: years of exertion cannot arrive without divine attraction. He says effort and attraction meet in God and many paths converge. [6 notes: SULAMI(النصرآباذي); BURSEVI(النصر ابادي)]
- 53:39/q15/p2 Striving, its visibility, recompense, annihilation, and renewed life mark successive stations. — Al-Warrāq maps vv. 39–43 to beginner, middle, end, loss of individual will, and second arising. Bursevī adds witnessing, subsistence, bodily nourishment and delayed lawful provision. [2 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر الوراق); BURSEVI(الوراق)]
- 53:39/q15/p3 Pure deeds earn Paradise, while vision and nearness are gifts to the spirit. — Al-Baqlī says deeds must lack ostentation; Paradise levels are earned, but unveiling, witness and intimacy come by God's grace. [2 notes: BURSEVI(البقلي)]
- 53:39/q15/p4 Reward and punishment follow settled dispositions; spiritual returns differ. — Al-Qāshānī says repeated acts shape dispositions, though divine decree governs immediate fortunes; he names bodily resurrection, return to primordial heart and return to gifted Real existence after annihilation, only the first universal. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 53:39/q15/p5 Self-knowledge and restraint of desire safeguard effort. — Muḥammad ibn ʿĪsā al-Hāshimī says most striving follows desires. [1 notes: SULAMI(محمد بن عيسى الهاشمي)]

## 53:39/q16 What rhetorical principles do the cited notes invoke?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether rhetorical force comes from contextual syntax or studied literary arrangement.
- 53:39/q16/p1 Meaning and arrangement, learned through poetry and grammar, ground rhetorical merit. — Al-Jurjānī says poetry, advanced grammar and taste allow one to understand Qur'anic inimitability; rhetorical causes should be investigated. Al-Jāḥiẓ warns against claiming nothing remains to learn. Tamīm, ʿAdī, Bashshār and al-Buḥturī liken verse making to intellectual craft and measured arrangement, not pleasant sounds or ease of letters. A group dismisses poetry and advanced grammar; al-Jurjānī condemns that stance. [13 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL(الجاحظ,هذه الطائفة)]
- 53:39/q16/p2 Context decides whether near-negation means near failure or no approach to occurrence. — Al-Jurjānī says 2:71's mā kādū yafʿalū implies eventual action, unlike the poem's lam yarahā wa-lam yakad; the conditional idhā gives past lam yakad future force. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
