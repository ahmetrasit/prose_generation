# 11:88: 63 questions

## 11:88/q01 Why does Shuayb address his people this way?  [قَالَ يَٰقَوْمِ · rhetoric]
- 11:88/q01/p1 He appeals gently to kinship and invites fair consideration. — Al-Biqaʿi says the hypothetical fosters impartial agreement; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it an eloquent, tactful appeal; al-Alusi reports commentators stressing gentle persuasion and fearlessness. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد)]

## 11:88/q02 What does 'Have you considered?' mean here?  [أَرَءَيْتُمْ · meaning]
- 11:88/q02/p1 It asks them to consider and tell him their judgment. — Al-Wahidi glosses it 'do you know?'; al-Biqaʿi says 'tell me'; Elmalılı asks them to examine their own condition. Al-Burhan says this usage can mean 'pay attention' without a real question. [4 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; BURHAN]

## 11:88/q03 How is the address element in 'Have you considered?' parsed?  [أَرَءَيْتُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the element is an address particle or a pronoun with syntactic case.
- 11:88/q03/p1 It is an address particle with no syntactic case. — Al-Burhan attributes the particle analysis to the majority and absence of case to Sibawayh. [2 notes: BURHAN(الجمهور,سيبويه)]
- 11:88/q03/p2 It is a pronoun or element assigned accusative case. — Al-Burhan says object pronouns with mental-seeing verbs make this defensible and reports al-Sakkaki's accusative analysis, citing 6:46. [2 notes: BURHAN(السكاكي,غيره)]
- 11:88/q03/p3 It is assigned nominative case. — Al-Burhan attributes this analysis to al-Farraʾ. [1 notes: BURHAN(الفراء)]

## 11:88/q04 What is the unspoken continuation of the conditional?  [أَرَءَيْتُمْ إِن كُنتُ عَلَىٰ بَيِّنَةٍۢ مِّن رَّبِّى وَرَزَقَنِى مِنْهُ رِزْقًا حَسَنًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the ellipsis asks about Shuayb's duty, his fidelity, the people's excuse, or the technical syntax of أرأيتم.
- 11:88/q04/p1 Would he abandon worship, revelation, or the mission and follow error? — Al-Zajjaj says the audience supplies the omitted answer. Al-Wahidi and al-Hawfi infer abandonment of worship; Ibn ʿAtiyya adds going astray or failing to convey; al-Qadi, al-Razi, and al-Baydawi stress betrayal after spiritual and material gifts. Ibn Ashur instead frames the warning as what could justify denial or avert its consequences. Islahi links the implied danger to 11:63; al-Biqaʿi leaves implications open. [27 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الحوفي); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية,الحوفي); ALUSI-FULL(القاضي,بعضهم,شيخ الإسلام); BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari, Razi and several other commentators)]
- 11:88/q04/p2 A true prophet must command abandoning idols and sin. — Al-Kashshaf argues this is why prophets are sent; al-Nasafi, al-Tibi, and quoted reports follow that logic. Al-Alusi says it rebuts the people's aim to silence him. Abu Hayyan and al-Samin report the reading but object to calling this a technical answer to أرأيتم. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 11:88/q04/p3 What excuse have they for rejecting him if he has proof? — Jishumi records the challenge to their accusation; Ibn Ashur asks what could justify denial or spare them its consequences. [2 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 11:88/q04/p4 Should he forbid their error, or should they require him to cheat? — Al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi preserve the first inference; al-Qurtubi also records the challenge to their right to demand fraud when God has enriched him. [3 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 11:88/q04/p5 The proposed answer is technically the second object of 'inform me.' — Abu Hayyan and the Shaykh reject labeling an interrogative supplied after أرأيتم the conditional answer; a declarative 'I go astray' does not follow, while an interrogative supplies the verb's second object. Al-Alusi reports this distinction. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]

## 11:88/q05 What is the clear proof from his Lord?  [بَيِّنَةٍۢ مِّن رَّبِّى · referent]
turns on: Whether the proof is an argument or inner certainty, prophetic knowledge, or a validating miracle.
- 11:88/q05/p1 Clear evidence, insight, or certainty of the truth he preaches. — Al-Tabari names monotheism and rejection of idols and fraud; Abu Imran calls it certainty. Al-Wahidi, Jishumi, al-Baghawi, Ibn Kathir, al-Biqaʿi, Elmalılı, and Kuran Yolu stress clear argument, insight, or rational and revealed evidence. Al-Razi specifies certainty that idolatry and fraud are wrong; Islahi sees an innate conviction. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(أبو عمران الجوني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 11:88/q05/p2 Prophetic knowledge, guidance, and religion. — Al-Razi and al-Baydawi treat the gift as knowledge and prophethood; Qashani reads certain proof of unity, and al-Qushayri describes light penetrating heedlessness. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; QUSHAYRI]
- 11:88/q05/p3 A miracle or sign authenticating prophethood. — Al-Razi treats it as a miracle in his third explanation; Tabataba'i and the Study Quran say it establishes a prophet's truth. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 11:88/q05/p4 The word can mean proof or clarification and has two morphological analyses. — Ibn ʿAtiyya says the feminine ending may intensify the sense or qualify an omitted feminine noun. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 11:88/q06 What is the good provision from God?  [وَرَزَقَنِى مِنْهُ رِزْقًا حَسَنًۭا · referent]
turns on: Whether rizq names lawful wealth, spiritual gifts, all blessings, or the recipient's manner of receiving it.
- 11:88/q06/p1 Lawful, wholesome or abundant wealth free of fraud. — Al-Dahhak and Ibn Abbas are quoted; Ibn Abbas and al-Kalbi call Shuayb wealthy. Ibn al-Anbari says the wealth was lawful and free of claims. Al-Wahidi calls it independence from cheating; Ibn ʿAtiyya notes their corrupt earnings. Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi prefer this reading. Al-Qadi and al-Baydawi stress God's grant without strenuous earning; Asad says wealth dispels a charge of self-interest. [29 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري,ابن عباس وأكثر المفسرين,الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك,القاضي); ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 11:88/q06/p2 Prophethood, revelation, or revealed law and wisdom. — Ibn Isa, al-Kashshaf, and others name prophethood; Tabataba'i specifies revelation containing knowledge and law; Islahi likens revelation sustaining the spirit to food sustaining the body, citing a saying attributed to Jesus. Ibn Ashur sees an analogy with Noah and Salih's mercy and accommodation to the people's wealth language at 11:87. [15 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الإسلام); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Jesus); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 11:88/q06/p3 Guidance, faith, divine enabling or obedience. — Muqatil identifies faith as guidance; al-Zajjaj reports enabled obedience. Al-Wahidi reports guidance and right conduct; al-Hasan combines religious guidance and enlarged wealth. [5 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT(جماعة من المفسرين); TABRISI(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 11:88/q06/p4 Knowledge and understanding. — Al-Thaʿlabi, Jishumi, al-Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Abu Hayyan, and al-Qurtubi preserve this gloss. [6 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 11:88/q06/p5 Every divine blessing, including material and spiritual gifts. — Al-Tabrisi reports this comprehensive reading; Elmalılı explicitly combines wealth, prophethood and wisdom, and Kuran Yolu allows wealth, prophethood or both. [3 notes: TABRISI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 11:88/q06/p6 Provision that sustains independence through divine care. — Al-Qushayri connects it with primordial care, welfare and protection; reported Sufi definitions are effortless receipt, awareness of the Provider, or provision inspiring generosity without forgetfulness. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI(قيل)]

## 11:88/q07 Why mention provision and its divine source?  [مِنْهُ رِزْقًا حَسَنًۭا · rhetoric]
- 11:88/q07/p1 It shows independence from fraud and worldly reward and grounds fidelity to God's call. — Al-Wahidi says lawful wealth made cheating needless. Al-Razi says the gift comes through God rather than earning; honor and disgrace rest with Him. Al-Alusi says proof supplies the reason to preach and provision removes a supposed motive to stop. Asad similarly dispels self-interest; Tabataba'i cites the refusal of a wage in 26:109. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI]

## 11:88/q08 What does Shuayb deny intending to do?  [وَمَآ أُرِيدُ أَنْ أُخَالِفَكُمْ إِلَىٰ مَآ أَنْهَىٰكُمْ عَنْهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether مخالفة points primarily to doing forbidden acts oneself, private gain, or mere opposition and dominance.
- 11:88/q08/p1 He will not forbid conduct and then practice it himself, openly or secretly. — Qatada, Ibn Abbas and many commentators give this sense. Al-Thawri and al-Ghunyān specify secret conduct; Ibn ʿAtiyya specifies fraudulent measures and weights. The cited poem and maxim condemn this hypocrisy. Al-Qushayri distinguishes imperfect performance of duties from the obligation to avoid every prohibition. [37 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو بكر,ابن عباس,قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الشاعر); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(صاحب الغنيان,قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الثوري,قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(جميع المفسرين من التابعين فمن بعدهم); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 11:88/q08/p2 He will not monopolize the gain or pleasure he tells them to abandon. — Al-Kashshaf and al-Nasafi describe rushing to forbidden desires. Jishumi, al-Tabrisi, Ibn ʿAtiyya, al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, al-Alusi, Elmalılı and Islahi specify keeping profits of fraud or market advantage for himself; Islahi says opponents may have alleged this because of a small business. [13 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 11:88/q08/p3 He does not oppose them merely to dominate, deprive, or contradict them. — Ibn Ashur prefers the broader sense, relating it to their charge in 11:87 and contrasting faultfinding with constructive correction; a report about Abu Bakr and Umar illustrates the idiom. Tabataba'i relates their fear of lost freedom to common law and shared interests. Asad reads the denial as not depriving people of their due at 11:85. [10 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو بكر الصديق وعمر); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 11:88/q09 How does 'oppose you toward' work in Arabic?  [أَنْ أُخَالِفَكُمْ إِلَىٰ مَآ أَنْهَىٰكُمْ عَنْهُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether خالف incorporates pursuit or opposition, and where إلى and the infinitival clause attach.
- 11:88/q09/p1 It means pursuing what another leaves, with 'toward' marking that direction. — Al-Kashshaf compares travelers moving toward and from water; al-Razi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin, al-Alusi, and Ibn Ashur repeat the distinction between خالف إلى and خالف عن. Al-Zajjaj explains the aim as doing what he forbids. [16 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج); IBNASHUR-FULL(الكشاف); TABATABAI]
- 11:88/q09/p2 The infinitival clause is the object of 'I want'; 'toward' joins the verb or an implied inclination. — Sibawayh treats أن plus verb as a verbal noun, citing 57:29; it refers to a later action, not a concurrent circumstance. Al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi call it an accusative complement. Abu Hayyan and al-Samin allow إلى attached to أخالف or implied مائلا; Tabataba'i makes the verb imply inclination. [10 notes: SIBAWAYH; NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(البحر); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 11:88/q09/p3 The clause can express purpose, or an omitted verb or preposition supplies the relation. — Al-Samin permits a purpose object with إلى attached to أريد. Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi also report retaining ordinary opposition and supplying 'inclined toward'; al-Alusi records omitted وأميل. Ibn Ashur's preferred construction makes both phrases depend on أريد, with a deleted preposition before the infinitive. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 11:88/q10 What ethical requirement does the pledge express?  [وَمَآ أُرِيدُ أَنْ أُخَالِفَكُمْ إِلَىٰ مَآ أَنْهَىٰكُمْ عَنْهُ · law]
- 11:88/q10/p1 The admonisher should act consistently with prohibitions and counsel. — Sahl says deeds should move the listener; Abu Uthman says speech, action and state must agree. Ibn Masud invokes this verse against hair extensions. Ibn Ashur links 2:44 and argues a communal legal address includes the messenger. Tabataba'i says common prohibitions bind the speaker too; al-Wahidi says Shuayb chooses the same obedience he urges. [13 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(أبو عثمان,بعضهم); IBNABIHATIM(مسروق); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 11:88/q10/p2 Failure to follow a prohibition brings blame on the speaker. — Two Prophetic reports transmitted by Ibn Kathir and al-Durr have Muhammad say that if he did otherwise, the blame would be his. Ibn Kathir relates a further report that true Prophetic reports accord with good conduct. [6 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(جد بهز بن حكيم,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,معاوية); DURR-FULL(النبي)]

## 11:88/q11 What reform does Shuayb seek?  [إِنْ أُرِيدُ إِلَّا ٱلْإِصْلَٰحَ · interpretation]
- 11:88/q11/p1 He seeks his people's religious, moral, and worldly good through worship, justice, and counsel. — Al-Tabari warns of punishment; Ibn Abbas specifies monotheism and piety. Al-Kashshaf and al-Nasafi list admonition and commanding right; al-Qurtubi says justice repairs worldly life and worship the next. Ibn Ashur calls this correction after criticism; Tabataba'i stresses benefit for all society. Qashani gives an inward purification reading. [29 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 11:88/q11/p2 Reform is doing good and making a corrupt condition sound. — Al-Damghani and Ibn al-Jawzi gloss it as beneficence and an upright change opposed to corruption; Ibn al-Jawzi cites a line about goodness after insult to parents, notes Thaʿlab's ṣaluḥa and Ibn al-Sikkit's ṣalaḥa/ṣaluḥa variants. [6 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,ابن السكيت,ثعلب)]
- 11:88/q11/p3 Reform begins with purifying self and others to receive wisdom. — Qashani gives the inward reading; al-Qushayri says good intention invites divine facilitation, while evil intention meets obstruction. [2 notes: QASHANI; QUSHAYRI]

## 11:88/q12 How is 'as much as I can' parsed?  [مَا ٱسْتَطَعْتُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether ما marks duration, the measure of reform, or its direct object.
- 11:88/q12/p1 It is temporal: for as long as he is able. — Al-Kashshaf, al-Razi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, al-Baydawi, al-Samin, and Ibn Ashur give this sense. Al-Alusi prefers it because it emphasizes continuous effort and avoids extra ellipsis. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 11:88/q12/p2 It specifies the amount of reform in apposition to 'reform.' — Al-Kashshaf and al-Razi give the relative pronoun analysis. Al-Alusi allows partitive, complete or inclusive apposition and supplies a resumptive pronoun if needed. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 11:88/q12/p3 It is an object: reform what he can of their corruption. — Al-Kashshaf cites a poetic analogy for the definite verbal noun governing its object; al-Razi and al-Samin report it. Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi object that Kufans disallow such government and Basrans regard it rare. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 11:88/q12/p4 An omitted object means the reform he can accomplish. — Al-Wahidi gives this ellipsis; al-Mawardi and al-Qurtubi note ability limits action, not the intention to reform. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 11:88/q13 What is the limit of his reforming ability?  [مَا ٱسْتَطَعْتُ · interpretation]
- 11:88/q13/p1 He can preach, warn, and strive, but cannot compel obedience. — Al-Zajjaj, Abu Ishaq, al-Wahidi, Ibn al-Jawzi, and al-Razi mark this boundary. Al-Mawardi and al-Qurtubi distinguish intention from achievable action; al-Biqaʿi and Kuran Yolu emphasize full effort. [11 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 11:88/q14 What does tawfiq mean?  [تَوْفِيقِىٓ · meaning]
- 11:88/q14/p1 Divine help that enables obedience, correct action, and attainment of truth. — Al-Zajjaj says no one obeys unaided. Al-Baghawi calls it facilitation of good; al-Tabrisi and al-Qurtubi say correctness or guidance. Al-Qushayri includes capacity, motives and means. Ibn Ashur explains the root as making things accord and theologians' definition as capacity and motivation; al-Biqaʿi calls it capacity fitting an obedient act. Al-Razi and Ibn Kathir apply it to successful reform. [30 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); STUDYQURAN]
- 11:88/q14/p2 Inwardly, unearned divine care or a guide from falsehood to truth. — Al-Nahrajuri denies a prior cause or merit; another saying sees preordained care. Jishumi defines it as what God knows leads to right action and as لطف realized in action, which can bless or test. [4 notes: SULAMI(النهرجوري,بعضهم); JISHUMI]

## 11:88/q15 Why is Shuayb's success only by God?  [وَمَا تَوْفِيقِىٓ إِلَّا بِٱللَّهِ · theology]
- 11:88/q15/p1 Human obedience and reform require God's aid, guidance, and will. — Al-Tabari says without God he would not reach right; al-Zajjaj, al-Wahidi, al-Tabrisi, and al-Razi apply this to everyone. Al-Alusi says the attribution guards against apparent self-praise after mentioning capacity. Tabataba'i reasons that God creates and sustains souls and deeds, citing 35:1, 34:21, 13:33 and 35:41, while humans retain free choice of acts. [37 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(أبو سليمان الضبي); SULAMI(النهرجوري,بعضهم); THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 11:88/q15/p2 God arranges or withholds the causes that bring a chosen act about. — Jaʿfar al-Sadiq's report treats prevented sin as success and permitted sin as abandonment, referring to 3:160; Tabataba'i explains these as God's active ordering of causes while the servant chooses. [5 notes: TABATABAI]

## 11:88/q16 How does 'by God' connect grammatically to tawfiq?  [إِلَّا بِٱللَّهِ · grammar]
- 11:88/q16/p1 The bāʾ marks God's aid, sometimes with an implied 'from the presence of.' — Al-Alusi reports grammarians supplying an omitted genitive to avoid treating the agent's act as an instrument; he adds that God's indication alone is insufficient without divine help. His generic reading extends the exclusion to every instance of success. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(كثير)]

## 11:88/q17 How is tawfiqi read?  [تَوْفِيقِىٓ · readings]
turns on: Which reader's transmitted pronunciation is followed.
