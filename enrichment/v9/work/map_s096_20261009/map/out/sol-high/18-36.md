# 18:36: 19 questions

## 18:36/q01 What does the Hour's 'standing' mean?  [ٱلسَّاعَةَ قَآئِمَةًۭ · meaning]
- 18:36/q01/p1 The Hour is the coming of resurrection; qāʾima figuratively describes its occurrence. — Bursevi calls it the time of resurrection; Alusi calls 'standing' a conventional metaphor for future realization. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:36/q02 What does the owner's claim about the Hour show of his belief?  [وَمَآ أَظُنُّ ٱلسَّاعَةَ قَآئِمَةًۭ · theology]
turns on: Whether 'I do not think' and conditional return express denial, real uncertainty, or a taunt.
- 18:36/q02/p1 He denies resurrection and its recompense. — Muqatil, Yahya, Ibn Abbas via Wahidi, Jishumi, Tabrisi and Ibn al-Jawzi call it denial; Zajjaj and Abu Ishaq also include denial of the world's end; Qatada adds denial of meeting God. Ibn Atiyya reports corrupt belief. The conditional boast remains a fallback. [15 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس); JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; THALABI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي رضي الله عنه)]
- 18:36/q02/p2 He doubts or considers resurrection unlikely, without categorically denying it. — Tabari and Ibn Zayd call it doubt; Tabatabai argues 'if I am returned to my Lord' allows possibility and invokes God, opposing al-Zamakhshari's alleged outright-denial and idol-worship reading. Tabrisi and Study Quran stress uncertainty. Abu Ali via Jishumi and Razi say even doubt is disbelief, citing the believer's 'Have you disbelieved?' Ibn Atiyya reports Qatada and Ibn Zayd. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد); JISHUMI(أبو علي,ابن زيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة وابن زيد); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI+]
- 18:36/q02/p3 He voices denial as a taunt or sarcastic reply to the believer. — Ibn Ashur says the claim does not logically follow from confidence in the garden; it ridicules the believer's expectation of divine reward. He likens it to al-As ibn Wa'il's taunt to Khabbab. Al-Kashshaf also presents the conditional as built on the companion's premise. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 18:36/q03 Why does he doubt the Hour?  [وَمَآ أَظُنُّ ٱلسَّاعَةَ قَآئِمَةًۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the assertion reflects deluded attachment or conversational mockery.
- 18:36/q03/p1 Prosperity produces prolonged hope, heedlessness, and complacency. — Al-Kashshaf names greed, respite and neglected consequences, and says even wealthy Muslims may live as though property cannot perish. Biqai names delight in present life. Study Quran invokes 28:88, 55:26 and 104:3 on mortality and the illusion wealth makes one immortal. Razi limits 'never' to his lifetime. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL]
- 18:36/q03/p2 He uses the denial to mock the believer's promised reward. — Ibn Ashur says confidence in the garden alone does not entail denial of the Hour, so the statement taunts the companion. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:36/q04 What does 'if I am returned to my Lord' mean or imply?  [وَلَئِن رُّدِدتُّ إِلَىٰ رَبِّى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the conditional invokes a real possibility, merely repeats the believer's premise, or signals belief.
- 18:36/q04/p1 It hypothetically grants the believer's premise of resurrection and return to God. — Zajjaj, semantic authorities via Wahidi, and Abu Hayyan infer that the believing companion had raised resurrection. Ibn Abbas via Wahidi interprets 'if resurrection is true'; Yahya says the man denies Paradise and return. Kashshaf, Jishumi, Baghawi, Baydawi and Nasafi all read the condition as adopting the companion's premise. Tabatabai says naming 'my Lord' admits possibility. Asad says return is being brought before God for judgment. [15 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 18:36/q04/p2 Mentioning 'my Lord' need not prove sound belief in God. — Bursevi notes that awareness of God can coexist with idolatry; Tabatabai instead uses the invocation to resist the claim that he categorically denied a return. [2 notes: BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 18:36/q05 Why is 'returned' expressed as ruddittu here?  [رُّدِدتُّ · rhetoric]
- 18:36/q05/p1 Passive 'sent back' conveys painful removal from the beloved garden. — Al-Burhan as cited by Bursevi and Alusi contrast 41:50's rajaʿtu, where no such aversion appears; Burhan notes the wording difference. [3 notes: BURHAN; BURSEVI(البرهان); ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:36/q06 What force do the oath lām and emphatic nūn give his claim?  [وَلَئِن لَأَجِدَنَّ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the intensified assertion is a sincere presumption or a taunt.
- 18:36/q06/p1 They intensify his confident but deluded promise of a better return. — Kashshaf calls it an oath; Baydawi and Nasafi say he swears because he expects continued favor. Tabatabai adds the first-person verbs 'I am returned' and 'I will surely find' to show self-credit rather than attributing return or gift to God. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 18:36/q06/p2 They heighten his sarcasm toward the believer. — Ibn Ashur explicitly reads the oath lām, emphatic nūn and munqalaban as part of sarcastic rhetoric. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:36/q07 What does the pronoun after 'better than' refer to, and how is it read?  [مِّنْهَا · readings]
turns on: The regional consonantal spellings and whether the antecedent is the nearby singular garden, the earlier pair, or all preceding property.
- 18:36/q07/p1 Minhā, singular: the garden he entered or just mentioned. — Ibn Mujahid and Abu Ali cite Abu Amr, Asim, Hamza and al-Kisai and Basran/Kufan codices. Abu Ali, Wahidi and Ibn al-Jawzi prefer the nearer singular garden of 18:35; Tabrisi, Samīn and others concur. Ibn Ashur adds Yaqub and Khalaf and sees later singular references. Jishumi also reports an alternative antecedent of all foregoing things under the singular reading. Abu Hayyan's note names Abu Amr and singular thamar. [19 notes: FARRA; IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي); NAHHAS(أهل المدينة); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي); TABRISI(أهل الحجاز وابن عامر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمرو وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عمرو); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); IBNASHUR-FULL; YAHYA-SALLAM; FARISI-HUJJA+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو علي)+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 18:36/q07/p2 Minhumā, dual: both gardens previously mentioned. — Ibn Mujahid and Abu Ali cite Ibn Kathir, Nafi and Ibn Amir; later reports add Abu Jaʿfar, Ibn al-Zubayr, Zayd b. Ali and other readers. Meccan, Medinan and Syrian codices contain the mīm. Nahhas and Qurtubi prefer it on the pair as nearer antecedent; Yahya explains one garden can be mentioned within two divided by a river. Qiraat-ER points to 18:32. Nashr uses this as evidence that a reading must agree with at least one regional Uthmanic codex, explicitly or approximately. [29 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير ونافع وابن عامر); NAHHAS(أهل المدينة); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وابن عامر); THALABI(أهل الحجاز والشام); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل الحجاز والشام); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير ونافع وابن عامر وابن الزبير); TABRISI(أهل الحجاز وابن عامر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو علي,ابن كثير ونافع وابن عامر); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(الحجازيان والشامي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الزبير وزيد بن علي وأبو بحرية وأبو جعفر وشيبة وابن محيصن وحميد وابن مناذر ونافع وابن كثير وابن عامر); SAMIN-DURR; NASHR; BIQAI-FULL(ابن كثير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن الزبير); IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER; WAHIDI-BASIT; NAHHAS+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 18:36/q07/p3 The singular pronoun may encompass all previously mentioned possessions. — Jishumi records this separate alternative to the specific garden antecedent. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 18:36/q08 What does the owner expect to find if returned?  [لَأَجِدَنَّ خَيْرًۭا مِّنْهَا مُنقَلَبًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'better' compares a future garden with the present one or his whole future lot with this world.
- 18:36/q08/p1 An afterlife return or abode better than his present garden or gardens, sometimes specified as Paradise. — Muqatil, Yahya, Tabari, Thalabi, Wahidi, Jishumi, Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Razi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, Biqai and others describe an imagined superior destination. Jishumi explicitly names Paradise. Kashshaf and Abu Hayyan say an afterlife outcome lasts whereas the earthly garden perishes. [37 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي رضي الله عنه); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 18:36/q08/p2 A better return in the Hereafter than his lot in this world generally. — Tabrisi records this as an alternative to a better garden specifically. [1 notes: TABRISI(قيل)]

## 18:36/q09 Why does he expect something better from God?  [لَأَجِدَنَّ خَيْرًۭا مِّنْهَا مُنقَلَبًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether his conditional promise is a sincere inference from wealth or deliberate mockery.
- 18:36/q09/p1 He takes worldly prosperity as proof of his own merit and future favor. — Tabari and Ibn Zayd say the given garden becomes his warrant. Zajjaj and Wahidi infer a reply to the believer. Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Razi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, Biqai, Bursevi, Alusi, Tabatabai and Study Quran describe projected status or entitlement. Razi and Bursevi counter with respite and gradual entrapment; Bursevi cites 82:6,14 against expecting generosity while disobeying. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan distinguish this presumption from al-As's deliberate mockery. [25 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,ابن زيد,ابن عباس); JISHUMI(ابن زيد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 18:36/q09/p2 He boasts sarcastically from the believer's premise rather than believing he will receive it. — Ibn Ashur likens him to al-As ibn Wa'il's sarcastic promise to Khabbab; al-Kashshaf calls the conditional wishful thinking. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan explicitly distinguish the two men and describe this owner's claim as presumption, which argues against equating their intent. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 18:36/q10 What does munqalaban mean?  [مُنقَلَبًۭا · meaning]
- 18:36/q10/p1 A return, final outcome, or destination, including an abode. — Muqatil and Jishumi give 'return'; Thalabi adds dwelling. Kashshaf, Razi, Abu Hayyan and Nasafi say return/outcome; Ibn Ashur specifies the place of return. [9 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:36/q11 What is the grammatical role of munqalaban?  [مُنقَلَبًۭا · grammar]
- 18:36/q11/p1 It is accusative specification (tamyīz), transposed from an underlying subject. — Kashshaf, Razi and Nasafi identify accusative specification; Abu Hayyan explicitly says it is converted from an underlying subject. Ibn Ashur also calls it specification. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:36/q12 What is the setting of the owner's exchange with the believer?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reported identities and circumstances of the interlocutors.
- 18:36/q12/p1 The wealthy owner shows his garden to his believing companion, who had raised resurrection. — Thalabi names the owner Fatrus; Qurtubi reports he led his believing brother around by the hand. Zajjaj, Wahidi's semantic authorities and Abu Hayyan infer that the believer had told him of the world's end and resurrection. [5 notes: THALABI; QURTUBI-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 18:36/q12/p2 Two brothers inherited wealth; one spent it for God, while the other mocked him. — Jishumi links their story with 37:50. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 18:36/q13 How does this claim connect with other passages?  [* · links]
turns on: Which parallel is in view and whether the owner's intention matches the other speaker.
- 18:36/q13/p1 It echoes 41:50's doubt of the Hour and expectation of the best with God. — Yahya, Kashshaf, Razi, Abu Hayyan, Tabatabai and others point to the parallel; Burhan, Bursevi and Alusi distinguish ruddittu here from rajaʿtu there because of attachment to the garden. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BURSEVI(البرهان); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 18:36/q13/p2 It resembles 19:77's promise of future wealth, though interpreters differ about the speakers' intent. — Ibn Kathir says 19:77 concerns al-As ibn Wa'il; Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan distinguish his deliberate mockery from this owner's presumed status. Ibn Ashur instead sees this claim as similar sarcasm. Kashshaf and Razi cite the passage. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 18:36/q13/p3 The two-person parable contrasts the faithful poor man and deluded wealthy opponent. — Jishumi says God honors the poor believer despite wealth disparities. Islahi says opponents make worldly success proof of their beliefs and deny or discount the afterlife; he contrasts the owner's arrogance with Dhū al-Qarnayn's gratitude and certainty. [3 notes: JISHUMI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 18:36/q14 What lesson does the verse's story teach?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The story's immediate warning and the broader purposes assigned to Quranic narratives.
- 18:36/q14/p1 Wealth and worldly permanence do not establish merit or safety before God. — Tabari calls his disbelief a wrong against himself, attracting anger and punishment. Qatada calls it ingratitude. Razi and Bursevi warn prosperity can be respite or entrapment. Tabatabai says the believer's correction and ruined garden refute permanent wealth and self-sufficiency. Study Quran connects wronging oneself with pride and 3:117, 10:44. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 18:36/q14/p2 Quranic stories recur to teach God's power, support of believers, and varied lessons. — Ibn Ashur says stories serve particular sermon occasions; repeat tellings strengthen memory, reach new believers and those who memorized different suras, vary wording to show eloquence, and combine to complete or clarify a purpose. Jonah's rescue illustrates deliverance (21:87–88); Joseph's and Moses' stories yield historical and legal lessons (12:76,79,10,13; 26:36,53). [15 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:36/q15 How is the verb wajada used in this verse and elsewhere?  [لَأَجِدَنَّ · meaning]
- 18:36/q15/p1 It means finding broadly, including hostile encounters, and is wider than thaqifa. — Bint al-Shati contrasts thaqifa, alert grasp or seizing an enemy, with wajada in 18:36 and 4:89, 9:5, 93:6–8, 38:44, 20:10, 12:94, 72:22, 73:20 and 2:110; thaqāf is a spear-straightening tool. [3 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 18:36/q16 What general Arabic rules bear on the doubled verb ruddittu?  [رُّدِدتُّ · grammar]
- 18:36/q16/p1 Geminate verbs retain or contract consonants according to inflection, dialect and pattern. — Sibawayh: #647 gives vowel harmony, fixed fatḥa, kasra before article/light nūn, weak hāʾ effects, Hijazi analogy, feminine plural sukūn, Bakr b. Waʾil forms ردن/ردت, and poetic dissimilation (Qanab). #801 contrasts regular أحسست/مسست/ظللت with rare أحست/مست/ظلت, cites 84:2–3, and explains movement, imāla and ishām in رد. #804 derives unassimilated and assimilated patterns from رددت, distinguishing root weight, intervening vowels and added radicals; #805 notes avoidance of heavy gemination and scarce weak patterns. #798 surveys weak-root patterns and their vowel and consonant substitutions, including analogy with رددت at r14. [91 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل,العرب,الكوفيون,قعنب بن أم صاحب)]

## 18:36/q17 How does the emphatic nūn in la-ajidanna behave grammatically?  [لَأَجِدَنَّ · grammar]
- 18:36/q17/p1 The heavy nūn is an added emphatic element, distinct from light nūn. — Sibawayh #765 calls emphatic nūns stem-external. #643: heavy nūn is doubled, stays after dual alif, takes kasra there but fatḥa elsewhere, and is separated from feminine plural nūn by alif; light nūn can be lost before the article, at pause or where forms would collide. He rejects Yunus's unheard اضربان/اضربنان. #642: Khalil treats light nūn like tanwin at pause but heavy nūn remains; feminine/plural endings and indicative nūn return, and the article or hamzat al-wasl deletes light nūn. Yunus proposes yāʾ/wāw at pause, which Sibawayh says standard usage does not support. [22 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل,العرب,يونس,يونس وناس من النحويين)]

## 18:36/q18 What other Arabic morphology and constructions illuminate the verse's wording?  [رُّدِدتُّ رَبِّى مُنقَلَبًۭا · grammar]
- 18:36/q18/p1 Sibawayh derives forms by recovering lost radicals, distinguishing noun patterns, and analyzing circumstantial accusatives and conventional ellipsis. — Sibawayh #618: diminutives normally restore substituted radicals, with named exceptions and evidence from plurals, poetry and Yunus; #619: medial alif restores wāw or yāʾ by plural evidence, and uncertain origins default to wāw; #634: collective nouns and proper names have distinct diminutive patterns, illustrated by قوم, رهط, سنين, أرضون and أفعال; #614: lost final radicals are recovered from diminutives/plurals, with competing roots for سنة/عضة and analyses of رب, قط, إن and أن. #765 r2–3 concerns augmental mīm and medial nūn. #182 gives accusative circumstance, measure and exchange examples, while Khalil notes limits on dropping prepositions and optional nominative forms. #113 sets out omitted verbs in fixed expressions, prices, vocatives, greeting and أما; Yunus and others differ over من أنت زيدا. #341 cites 5:1 to explain restored yāʾ/alif in shortened vocatives. [92 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل,بعض العرب,بعضهم,من أثق به,من العرب,من قال أنبياء,من قال النباء,منهم,يونس)]

## 18:36/q19 What rhetorical principles are cited alongside this verse?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether rhyme should be classed as sajʿ and which stylistic evidence demonstrates inimitability.
- 18:36/q19/p1 An extraordinary wished-for action is named, while an ordinary implied action may be omitted. — Al-Jurjani uses a blood-crying wish as evidence for explicit mention and a line of Tarafa to illustrate a recoverable omitted complement; going and standing can be understood from context. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL(طرفة)]
- 18:36/q19/p2 Quranic rhyme is a meaningful eloquent feature, described as sajʿ by some. — Itqan reports many non-Ashʿarites and lexicographers; Ibn Duraid explains the dove's repeated call; the ordering of Moses and Aaron in 20:70 and 7:122 is cited. [3 notes: ITQAN(أهل اللغة,ابن دريد,كثير من غير الأشاعرة)]
- 18:36/q19/p3 Quranic wording follows meaning and should not be called soothsayer-like sajʿ. — The Qadi in Itqan rejects the term on grounds of inimitability and the Prophet's condemnation of soothsayer style; rhyme arises incidentally from chosen meanings. [3 notes: ITQAN(القاضي)]
- 18:36/q19/p4 The Qur'an's recited style and effect on hearts are evidence of inimitability. — Bint al-Shati reports Muhammad Abduh's appeal to fair-minded European learners and al-Khattabi's sweetness and radiance; she says Abduh follows Jurjani and criticizes empty conventional praise. [4 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(أبو سليمان الخطابي,محمد عبده)]
