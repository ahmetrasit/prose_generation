# 55:1: 23 questions

## 55:1/q01 What does al-Raḥmān mean?  [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ · meaning]
turns on: The notes emphasize respectively the breadth of mercy, its first creative act, or what mercy means when attributed to God.
- 55:1/q01/p1 The One whose mercy is abundant, complete, boundless, and encompasses all beings. — Al-Zajjāj glosses it as abundant mercy and says earlier scriptures use the name. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it an intensive form derived from mercy. Ṭabrisī says its mercy encompasses everything; Bursawī distinguishes general worldly provision from forgiveness for believers in the next life, illustrating common bounty with a Persian poem about God's table shared by friend and enemy. Elmalılı says mercy and beneficence are boundless and continuous. Study Quran gives 'Infinitely Good' and 'Loving' as renderings. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 55:1/q01/p2 The One whose foundational mercy gives beings existence and their first perfections. — Al-Rāzī distinguishes prior mercy in creation from later mercy in provision, understanding, and aid. Qāshānī describes the initial gift of entities and their first perfections. Study Quran says existence becomes manifest through this Name as universal mercy before creation. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; QASHANI; STUDYQURAN]
- 55:1/q01/p3 Mercy in God means willing good or bestowing favor, rather than creaturely affection. — Bursawī derives the creaturely sense from affection or spiritual inclination, analogized to the womb bending over what it carries. He reports al-Ghazālī's account of God's care through creation, guidance to faith and happiness, joy in the afterlife, and the vision of His face. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الإمام الغزالي)]

## 55:1/q02 How does al-Raḥmān relate to al-Raḥīm?  [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ · theology]
turns on: The distinction is explained either by the sequence and scope of divine acts or by the relation of names to the Essence and acts.
- 55:1/q02/p1 Al-Raḥmān denotes the prior mercy of creation; al-Raḥīm denotes later mercy through sustaining and helping. — Al-Rāzī says only God creates, whereas people can imitate later mercy by feeding, clothing, and helping. Study Quran contrasts the universal, unqualified mercy of al-Raḥmān with the more particular mercy of al-Raḥīm, which can describe humans. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 55:1/q02/p2 Al-Raḥmān relates to the divine Essence; al-Raḥīm refers to mercy extended through God's acts. — Study Quran states the distinction twice and cites 1:3; Bursawī calls al-Raḥmān the name of the Essence encompassing the names and attributes. Ibn Burjān, reported by al-Biqāʿī, calls al-Raḥmān the manifest aspect of Allah and the hidden aspect of al-Rabb, with these names disclosing and veiling the Essence. [4 notes: STUDYQURAN; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL(ابن برجان)]

## 55:1/q03 Is al-Raḥmān a name reserved for God, and is it a proper name?  [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ · theology]
turns on: Whether the term is a proper name, a name-like epithet, or a meaningful attribute; al-Rāzī tests the proper-name claim against Arabic morphology and permissible naming.
- 55:1/q03/p1 It is a divine name or name-like epithet that cannot rightly be given to anyone else. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Ḥasan with Quṭrub state its exclusivity; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the attribute belongs uniquely to God. Ṭabrisī contrasts raḥim and raḥīm, which may describe humans. Al-Rāzī calls it an epithet made name-like by frequent use and grounds exclusivity in God's sole power to create; calling the Yamāmī man al-Raḥmān is linguistically possible but transgressive and based on false belief. Study Quran grounds exclusivity in universal creation through the Name and says 17:110 equates it with Allah. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن وقطرب); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 55:1/q03/p2 Al-Raḥmān may be analyzed as a proper name, as Allah is by some, allowing direct invocation. — Al-Rāzī reports appeal to 17:110 and to the vocative 'O All-Merciful.' On a proper-name analysis, he says God's reservation of a name makes its application to idols transgressive even if language permits it. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعض الناس); RAZI-FULL-]
- 55:1/q03/p3 Treating al-Raḥmān as a proper name is weak; its divine restriction follows the meaning of a name or attribute. — Al-Rāzī objects especially to the vocative. He argues that Allah with its article is unlikely to be a primary proper name, since its initial hamza drops as hamzat waṣl; Ilāh or Lāh can instead be the original proper name with an added article, or mean 'the worshipped,' a meaning belonging to God alone. He contrasts names such as al-Karīm and al-Wadūd, usable of people, with al-Khāliq, al-Qadīm, and 'the worshipped,' which are not. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 55:1/q04 Can al-Raḥmān be formed from letters at other sūrah openings?  [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ · interpretation]
- 55:1/q04/p1 Yes: the initials alif-lām-rāʾ, ḥā-mīm, and nūn combine to make this divine name. — Al-Māwardī attributes the account to Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and Ibn ʿAbbās; al-Qurṭubī attributes it to Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and ʿĀmir al-Shaʿbī. The named transmitters vary. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير وابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير)]

## 55:1/q05 How is the opening name construed grammatically?  [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the following verb is the predicate and whether al-Raḥmān is counted as a separate verse.
- 55:1/q05/p1 Al-Raḥmān is the subject and 'taught the Qur'an' is its predicate, possibly followed by further predicates. — Al-Naḥḥās says the predicate comes in the following construction. Al-Zamakhsharī makes subsequent verbal sentences successive predicates without conjunctions, each listing a favor and rebuking deniers; his analogy lists favors to Zayd before asking what kindness he could deny. Al-Rāzī calls the first analysis sounder. Abū Ḥayyān calls it the apparent parse. Al-Samīn ties this parse to not counting the name separately; Bursawī and Elmalılı say it begins the sentence. [9 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; RAZI-FULL+]
- 55:1/q05/p2 Al-Raḥmān is the predicate of an omitted subject: 'He/God is al-Raḥmān.' — Al-Rāzī calls this weaker because it depends on the standalone-verse reading. Al-Samīn and Ibn ʿĀshūr connect it to counting the word as a verse; Ṭabrisī says an elliptical 'God, the Merciful' preserves the verse-ending pattern. Elmalılı allows it because the word is a separate verse. [5 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]
- 55:1/q05/p3 Al-Raḥmān is a subject with an omitted predicate, such as 'Al-Raḥmān is our Lord.' — Al-Samīn offers this for the standalone-verse count; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the missing predicate is supplied by context. Abū Ḥayyān also reports an omitted-predicate parse. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 55:1/q06 Why does the sūrah open with this divine name?  [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ · rhetoric]
turns on: The opening is explained through the sūrah's theme of mercy or as an answer to denial of the divine name.
- 55:1/q06/p1 The name announces mercy as the source of the worldly, otherworldly, bodily, and spiritual gifts counted in the sūrah. — Al-Bayḍāwī points to worldly and otherworldly blessings. Bursawī adds bodily and spiritual gifts and attributes them to the name encompassing all names. Al-Biqāʿī says it signals immense Qur'anic beneficence beyond enumeration (14:34); Kur'an Yolu calls religion the greatest gift to rational beings and the Qur'an its highest human gift (1:1). Ibn ʿĀshūr connects these favors with the Prophet as mercy to all worlds (21:107). Qāshānī says the broad initial Name grounds the attributes that follow. [7 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 55:1/q06/p2 It names the deity whom the Meccans denied, making His subsequent works identify Him and rebuke them. — Muqātil and al-Wāḥidī say listing works leads people to recognize and worship Him alone after the denial in 25:60. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the bare name may expose their error like isolated opening letters, an analysis he calls strongest for such letters. He observes that no other sūrah opens directly with a divine name. Al-Sāmarrāʾī cites this opening as an example of compact wording that foregrounds the main purpose and a general class rather than individuating through repeated relatives and locatives (compare 2:255). [5 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR(بعضهم)]

## 55:1/q07 What occasion is reported for the opening revelation?  [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ · reports]
turns on: The reports connect it either to Meccan denial of the name, a claim of human instruction, or refusal of the basmala at Ḥudaybiyya.
- 55:1/q07/p1 Meccans rejected or questioned the name al-Raḥmān, prompting God to identify Himself through His works. — Muqātil, reported by al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and Abū Ḥayyān, connects refusal to prostrate in 25:60 with the revelation; al-Wāḥidī says 17:110 occasioned a claim that only Musaylima of Yamāma bore the name. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Study Quran report the question 'What/who is the Merciful?' or denial after hearing it invoked. Ṭabrisī also cites 17:110; Study Quran cites 13:30 for their disbelief and says some asked about the Prophet's frequent invocation. Ibn ʿĀshūr conditionally places the sūrah after al-Furqān if this is its occasion. [12 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Some commentaries)]
- 55:1/q07/p2 It answers Meccans who claimed that a human taught Muhammad. — Al-Baghawī reports this alternative occasion. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 55:1/q07/p3 It was revealed at the Ḥudaybiyya treaty when Suhayl ibn ʿAmr and others refused the basmala. — Abū Ḥayyān reports a Medinan occasion; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports it from Ibn ʿAbbās as the basis for a Medinan classification. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 55:1/q08 What does the opening's link to teaching the Qur'an convey?  [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ · interpretation]
- 55:1/q08/p1 Teaching and revealing the Qur'an are acts of mercy and the foremost divine favor. — Al-Zamakhsharī calls religion foremost and the Qur'an the summit, proof, and standard of heavenly books. Abū Ḥayyān says it heals hearts; Ibn Kathīr adds that God eases memorization and understanding for those shown mercy. Al-Biqāʿī says it clarifies divine pleasure and prohibitions and leads to the promised abode, citing 2:31; Bursawī calls it the basis of happiness, cites sayings about Qur'an-bearers and the best people learning and teaching it, and says it contains the realities of earlier scriptures. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Kashshāf's order of religious favor before creation and expression. Iṣlāḥī says mercy supplied revelation as education in place of demanded punishment; Kur'an Yolu calls the Qur'an religion's highest gift. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الكشاف); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 55:1/q09 Why is Qur'an teaching mentioned before human creation?  [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the first place marks the Qur'an's rank as a human favor or an ordering of celestial matters before earthly ones.
- 55:1/q09/p1 The Qur'an or religion is the greatest favor and the purpose for which humanity was created. — Al-Zamakhsharī says the purpose of knowing revelation and scripture is presented before human creation. Al-Rāzī says the following 'created humanity, taught him expression' explains how the greatest gift is received. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Kashshāf's ranking of revelation before creation and expression. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الكشاف)]
- 55:1/q09/p2 The sequence names heavenly gifts first and then pairs them with earthly counterparts. — Al-Rāzī says teaching the Qur'an to angels above precedes teaching humans expression below; he extends the pattern to sun and moon versus prostrating stars and trees (75:9; 55:6), and raised heaven versus placed earth (55:7; 55:10). [4 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 55:1/q10 What does 'taught the Qur'an' mean here?  [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the verb denotes instruction or making a revelatory sign.
- 55:1/q10/p1 God imparted knowledge of the Qur'an and made its learning possible. — Al-Rāzī defines teaching as imparting knowledge and says 3:7 poses no conflict: the learned may be joined to God or may learn difficult passages only within human capacity; on a stop at 'except God,' people know interpretation through His teaching, not independent intellect. Ibn Kathīr says God made memorization and understanding easy. Al-Rāzī also reports a metaphorical reading, 'made learnable,' comparing 54:17 and a person funding another's education. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 55:1/q10/p2 God made the Qur'an a sign and miracle proving prophethood. — Al-Rāzī compares its unique knowledge with the moon-splitting sign of 54:1; Kur'an Yolu adds that it proves Muhammad's prophethood and holds signs for reflective readers. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 55:1/q11 Who is understood as the recipient of Qur'an teaching?  [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ · referent]
turns on: The unstated second object of 'taught' may be general humanity, angels, or Muhammad.
- 55:1/q11/p1 Humanity generally receives the teaching. — Al-Rāzī prefers this because it completes the sūrah's account of universal favors; omission of the second object stresses general rather than one person's instruction, as when one says someone feeds people. Iṣlāḥī says human speech equips people to understand and teach each other through revelation. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; RAZI-FULL+]
- 55:1/q11/p2 Gabriel and other angels were taught before the Qur'an was sent to Muhammad. — Al-Rāzī cites 26:193–94 for its descent and 56:77–80 for the preserved Qur'an touched only by the purified before descent; this supports his celestial-before-earthly ordering. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 55:1/q11/p3 Muhammad is the recipient. — Al-Rāzī says this underscores that the Qur'an is God's speech rather than Muhammad's. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 55:1/q12 Why do the first clauses lack conjunctions?  [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ · rhetoric]
- 55:1/q12/p1 Each opening clause independently enumerates a favor and rebukes deniers; conjunctions return when clauses fit together. — Al-Zamakhsharī makes the verbs successive predicates of al-Raḥmān and illustrates their force by recalling gifts to Zayd after poverty, lowliness, and scarcity before asking which kindness could be denied. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 55:1/q13 Who can 'the human' in the following creation verse mean?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular 'human' denotes the species, its first member, or the Prophet.
- 55:1/q13/p1 Humanity as a kind. — Al-Nasafī lists this among three possible referents. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]
- 55:1/q13/p2 Adam. — Al-Nasafī lists this among three possible referents. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]
- 55:1/q13/p3 Muhammad. — Al-Nasafī lists this among three possible referents. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 55:1/q14 Is the sūrah Meccan or Medinan?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Conflicting transmitted classifications and occasions are weighed against reports of pre-proclamation recitation and the sūrah's consistent tone.
- 55:1/q14/p1 The whole sūrah is Meccan. — Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Asad, and Study Quran report a majority or most authorities; Ibn Kathīr holds this. Al-Suyūṭī calls it correct, invoking reports of the jinn's reply and a good-chain report from Asmāʾ hearing the Prophet recite near the Kaʿba before public proclamation; al-Ḥākim grades the jinn report sound on the two Shaykhs' condition. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers an entirely Meccan sūrah and cites Ibn Masʿūd's codex. Iṣlāḥī rejects Medinan attribution because no verse looks Medinan and the tone and rhythm are uniform; the opponents demanding a punishment sign are Meccan. [11 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ITQAN(أسماء بنت أبي بكر); IBNASHUR-FULL(جمهور الصحابة والتابعين); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Most scholars); IBNASHUR-FULL+; ITQAN+]
- 55:1/q14/p2 The whole sūrah is Medinan. — Abū Ḥayyān attributes it to Ibn Masʿūd and reports Ibn ʿAbbās both ways; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Ibn ʿAbbās linking it to Suhayl's refusal of the basmala at Ḥudaybiyya and Ibn Masʿūd's attribution. Asad says Zamakhsharī and Suyūṭī assign it to Medina, though the Suyūṭī note here prefers Mecca. Iṣlāḥī and Study Quran report this minority view. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Some people); STUDYQURAN(A minority); ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 55:1/q14/p3 The sūrah may have verses from both periods, including a reported Medinan exception at verse 29. — Abū Ḥayyān reports Ibn ʿAbbās's mixed accounts and an exception for 55:29. Asad says al-Bayḍāwī leaves open portions before and after migration. Bursawī records differing Meccan and Medinan reports without deciding. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Baydawi); BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]

## 55:1/q15 Where is the sūrah placed in revelation chronology?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Placement depends on whether the sūrah is classified as Meccan or Medinan and on the dating of al-Raʿd.
- 55:1/q15/p1 On a Meccan chronology it is forty-third, after al-Furqān and before Fāṭir, al-Ḥijr, and al-Naḥl. — Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers this order; denial of the name in 25:60 conditionally places it after al-Furqān, while the report of recitation before public preaching places it before al-Ḥijr. Al-Suyūṭī's Asmāʾ report also places it before al-Ḥijr. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ITQAN(أسماء بنت أبي بكر); IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 55:1/q15/p2 On a Medinan chronology it is ninety-eighth, after al-Raʿd and before al-Insān. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Jaʿbarī's ranking. Asad says placement just after sūrah 13 is unhelpful because al-Raʿd itself cannot be dated securely; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Meccan/Medinan disagreement prevents firm chronology. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الجعبري); ASAD-NOTES(Asad)-]

## 55:1/q16 Is al-Raḥmān counted as a verse by itself, and how many verses result?  [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ · sciences]
turns on: Regional verse-ending conventions, especially whether the opening name receives its own ayah mark.
- 55:1/q16/p1 Kufan readers count al-Raḥmān as a separate verse and total seventy-eight. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says Ḥafṣ copies mark an ayah after the name; he also gives Syria seventy-eight. Al-Samīn links separate counting to the omitted-subject or omitted-predicate parses. Ṭabrisī's ellipsis preserves the verse-ending pattern. Al-Rāzī regards the separate-verse parse as weaker. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]
- 55:1/q16/p2 The opening belongs with the following clause; other regional counts yield seventy-seven or seventy-six verses. — Al-Samīn ties the joined construction to making 'taught the Qur'an' its predicate. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Medina and Mecca at seventy-seven and Basra at seventy-six; he also says most verse counters treat the opening as one verse. Bursawī reports differing totals without numbers. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 55:1/q17 Where does this sūrah stand in the Mufaṣṣal division?  [* · sciences]
- 55:1/q17/p1 It begins the Mufaṣṣal, or its ninth division, in Ibn Masʿūd's arrangement. — Ibn Kathīr reports Ibn Masʿūd's statement that the Prophet paired the opening sūrahs of the Mufaṣṣal two by two. Al-Zarkashī reports Ibn al-Sayyid's attribution to Ibn Masʿūd's codex and adds that Aḥmad transmitted the arrangement; Ibn ʿĀshūr also cites that codex. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن مسعود); BURHAN(ابن السيد); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 55:1/q18 Why is the sūrah called 'The Bride of the Qur'an'?  [* · sciences]
- 55:1/q18/p1 The title evokes its many adornments and blessings. — Al-Biqāʿī lists garments, jewels, crowns, beauty, and perfection; Bursawī reports the title. Study Quran says Muslims use it after a well-known but unauthenticated hadith. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN]

## 55:1/q19 What is reported about the Prophet's recitation of the sūrah and the jinn's response?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in Companion transmitter and whether the Prophet himself read or heard the sūrah; their individual transmission routes receive different assessments.
- 55:1/q19/p1 Jābir reports the Prophet recited the entire sūrah to silent Companions and praised the jinn's better reply to its refrain. — Ibn Kathīr records al-Tirmidhī calling the report gharīb and known only through al-Walīd ibn Muslim from Zuhayr ibn Muḥammad; Aḥmad did not recognize it and objected to Syrian transmission from Zuhayr; al-Bazzār says it is known only through that route. Al-Suyūṭī also cites al-Tirmidhī and al-Ḥākim's jinn-response reports and says al-Ḥākim grades a report sound on the two Shaykhs' condition. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أحمد,البزار,الترمذي,جابر); ITQAN]
- 55:1/q19/p2 Ibn ʿUmar reports a recitation by or before the Prophet and the same comparison with the jinn. — The report varies over whether the Prophet read or heard it; Ibn Kathīr reports al-Bazzār saying it is known as prophetic only through this chain. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عمر,البزار)]

## 55:1/q20 How does this opening connect to the preceding sūrah?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources emphasize either the contrast of power and mercy or the progression from concise warning to fuller instruction.
- 55:1/q20/p1 Al-Qamar's majesty, power, punishment, and warning give way to al-Raḥmān's mercy, Qur'anic teaching, favors, and their refrain. — Al-Rāzī contrasts the split moon (54:1) with the Qur'an healing sinful hearts and the previous repeated punishment warning (54:16) with the favors refrain (55:13); he says 54:55's 'Sovereign, Powerful' leads to a Name combining might toward the wicked with mercy toward the righteous. Abū Ḥayyān says this sūrah expands the prior brief mention of the righteous abode and both groups' ends. Al-Biqāʿī says universal mercy completes kingship; Study Quran reports the complementary closing and opening names. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Some)]
- 55:1/q20/p2 Al-Qamar's concise warnings and stories lead into a fuller Qur'anic lesson for humans and jinn. — Iṣlāḥī says Quraysh should learn from messengers and history instead of demanding a punishment sign. Al-Biqāʿī says the new sūrah addresses both species, establishes proof, and repeats favors. Reporting Jaʿfar ibn al-Zubayr, he notes the whole Qur'an's miraculous order and al-Qamar's concise yet complete narratives (11:44; 15:94; 54:17–18); he gives the address to earth and sky and the command to proclaim as especially evident eloquence. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL(جعفر بن الزبير)]

## 55:1/q21 What is the sūrah's overall purpose and course?  [* · interpretation]
- 55:1/q21/p1 It displays universal mercy through creation, teaching, signs, and favors, while warning of punishment and describing both groups' outcomes. — Al-Biqāʿī says hope in divine gifts is joined to warning that further favor can cease through punishment. Study Quran says verses 1–25 present Creator, Teacher, and Revealer and created blessings, then heavenly blessings; it says God's endurance in verses 26–27 offsets passing creation. Iṣlāḥī describes Meccan opponents demanding a punishment sign and says human creation and speech equip people to learn by revelation. Al-Rāzī says the Name can imply might and vengeance toward the wicked as well as mercy and forgiveness toward the righteous. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL]

## 55:1/q22 How do the sūrah's beginning, basmala, and ending echo each other?  [* · links]
- 55:1/q22/p1 The basmala and opening announce divine perfection and mercy; the ending returns to the opening Name. — Al-Biqāʿī sees the basmala as naming perfection shown in creation, universal mercy in wondrous signs, and special mercy to the obedient in worship; he reports al-Rāzī that the closing Name recalls the opening and encompasses both teaching and punishment. Al-Qushayrī says the basmala joins majesty and bounty: majesty completes spirits' joy and makes God worshiped, while mercy gives bodily bliss and makes Him loved. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الرازي); QUSHAYRI]

## 55:1/q23 What inward significance is given to al-Raḥmān and divine teaching?  [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ · inward]
turns on: The inward notes relate the Name either to creation and manifestation or to divine teaching in the heavenly realm.
- 55:1/q23/p1 The Name is a source of creation and a disclosure of divine presence. — Study Quran reports Sufis saying all creation comes forth through the Breath of the Compassionate and notes 20:5 and 25:59 on the Compassionate assuming the Throne. Ibn Burjān, reported by al-Biqāʿī, relates al-Raḥmān to the manifest aspect of Allah and the hidden aspect of al-Rabb. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN(Sufis); BIQAI-FULL(ابن برجان)]
- 55:1/q23/p2 God's teaching of names and the Qur'an reflects a spiritual presentation before the heavenly assembly. — Al-Sulamī reports a comparison between teaching all names to angels and teaching Muhammad the Qur'an, which God presents to Himself; the disputing high assembly is identified as angels. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
