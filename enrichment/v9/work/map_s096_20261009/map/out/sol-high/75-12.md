# 75:12: 13 questions

## 75:12/q01 Who is addressed by 'your Lord'?  [رَبِّكَ · referent]
- 75:12/q01/p1 The Prophet is addressed first, and through him all people. — Elmalılı calls the Prophet the foremost addressee and extends the address to everyone, explicitly distinguishing it from address to the fugitive. Ibn ʿĀshūr places God's address to the Prophet in worldly time; al-Ālūsī calls it a new explanatory or confirming statement to him. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; ELMALILI+]

## 75:12/q02 What does calling God 'your Lord' convey here?  [رَبِّكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes give different contextual implications of rabb: the fugitive's master and the Prophet's supporting Lord.
- 75:12/q02/p1 The fugitive returns to the master who owns, governs, nurtures, guards, and benefits him. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī lists the senses of rabb as owner, master, governor, nurturer, guardian, and benefactor, and ties the choice to the fugitive's return to the one who manages his affairs. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:12/q02/p2 Calling Him the Prophet's Lord hints at His support for the Prophet through requital of his rejecters. — Ibn ʿĀshūr connects the possessive address to God's vindication of the Prophet against those who rejected his message. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 75:12/q03 Why is 'to your Lord' placed first?  [إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The order is explained by exclusivity and by the prominence of the answer to the fugitive.
- 75:12/q03/p1 Fronting confines the final destination or judgment to God alone. — Al-Ālūsī makes exclusivity depend on whether mustaqarr means destination or judgment. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the exclusive return in 3:28. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says no other refuge or barrier remains. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the order restricts destination and also serves the verse ending; his quotation of al-Kashshāf gives the same exclusive settlement and judgment, comparing 40:16. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الكشاف)]
- 75:12/q03/p2 It foregrounds the answer the fleeing person seeks. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the preposed phrase is the most important part of the answer to the fugitive's question. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:12/q04 What does 'that day' specify or emphasize?  [يَوْمَئِذٍ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes explain distinct effects of the temporal expression: restriction of settlement and emphasis on the Day and its signs.
- 75:12/q04/p1 It limits this inescapable settlement to the Day of Resurrection, unlike refuge available in worldly life. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says fronting 'that day' confines this settlement to that day, contrasting worldly refuge. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:12/q04/p2 Its repetition stresses the denied Day and the simultaneous occurrence of the three cosmic signs. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the repeated expression as emphasis on the day the deniers rejected and on the conjunction of the preceding signs; he says its tanwīn replaces an omitted clause. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 75:12/q05 How is 'that day' grammatically connected?  [يَوْمَئِذٍ · grammar]
- 75:12/q05/p1 An omitted verb governs 'that day,' while the tanwīn of idh stands for an omitted clause. — Al-Samīn says mustaqarr cannot govern a preceding time expression: a verbal noun cannot govern what precedes it, and a place noun cannot govern at all. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the omitted clause represented by tanwīn. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 75:12/q06 What can al-mustaqarr mean as an Arabic form?  [ٱلْمُسْتَقَرُّ · meaning]
turns on: The form can denote settling, its place, or its time; other glosses stress its endpoint sense. Al-Sāmarrāʾī explicitly intends the three grammatical senses together.
- 75:12/q06/p1 The act or state of settling. — Al-Rāzī interprets it as settling, with no settling or turning elsewhere. Al-Samīn and Elmalılı allow a verbal noun. Al-Sāmarrāʾī explains that forms can serve several functions, comparing faʿīl and mukhtār; he and the Ḥalaqāt version intend the act together with place and time because context excludes none. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 75:12/q06/p2 A place of settlement or final abode, whether temporary or everlasting. — Al-Jishumī compares maʾwā and mathwā and says such a place may be temporary or eternal. Al-Ṭabrisī says the destination or return can likewise be temporary or everlasting. Al-Samīn, Elmalılı, al-Nasafī, al-Rāzī, and al-Sāmarrāʾī allow a place noun; al-Sāmarrāʾī includes Paradise and Hell and argues that no context restricts this sense. [14 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI(ابن مسعود); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(البحر المحيط); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 75:12/q06/p3 The time of settlement. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the form can name an action, place, or time; the time includes judgment, how long people remain, and when they are dispatched to their final homes. The Ḥalaqāt version locates it at the gathering and intends all senses. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the noun a place-or-state form; al-Biqāʿī includes the time of rest under God's rule. [7 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BIQAI-FULL]
- 75:12/q06/p4 The endpoint or final destination, the terminus of a course. — Qatāda calls it the end or point of arrival in al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās, al-Māwardī, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Durr; al-Suddī compares the final end in 53:42. Al-Dāmghānī lists this sense and points to examples concerning the sun, a report, and every affair, citing 6:67 and 54:3. Al-Thaʿlabī reports al-Muqātil's 'terminus' and Yaman's 'return.' [9 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); NAHHAS(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 75:12/q07 What further sense or topics does al-Dāmghānī's entry list?  [ٱلْمُسْتَقَرُّ · meaning]
turns on: These are separate, unelaborated items in the lexical entry, not competing explanations of the verse.
- 75:12/q07/p1 It lists 'a little among a great many.' — Al-Dāmghānī gives this further sense without relating it to this verse in the excerpt. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]
- 75:12/q07/p2 It also names the number of the people of Badr as a topic. — Al-Dāmghānī's excerpt does not elaborate or give that number. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]
- 75:12/q07/p3 It also names the number of Saul's people as a topic. — Al-Dāmghānī's excerpt does not elaborate or give that number. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]

## 75:12/q08 What is the final settlement to the Lord on that Day?  [ٱلْمُسْتَقَرُّ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether mustaqarr names return or refuge with God, His exclusive judgment, the abodes He assigns, or also the time of settlement; several sources allow more than one sense.
- 75:12/q08/p1 All creatures finally return to God alone; He is their only refuge and end. — Al-Ṭabarī says God assigns every creature its rest; Qatāda and Ibn Masʿūd gloss the destination as end or return, and Ibn ʿAbbās says every affair ends with God. Al-Thaʿlabī includes creatures, deeds, and all things and cites 53:42, 96:8, 3:28, and 42:53; al-Baghawī cites 96:8, 3:28, 24:42, and 35:18. Al-Rāzī cites 96:8, 24:42, 42:53, and 53:42 for no turning elsewhere. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes everyone's movement toward meeting God through 84:6, 96:8, and 53:42. Al-Biqāʿī extends God's rule to speaking and silent creation, place and time, outwardly and inwardly. Elmalılı and al-Iṣlāḥī say the fugitive finds no shelter elsewhere; Kur'an Yolu adds that all gather before God and learn their good and evil deeds, including omissions. The Study Quran's Ṭ reports God Himself as each person's final end. [32 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); NAHHAS(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(ابن مسعود); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود,قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الكشاف); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 75:12/q08/p2 Their affair returns to God's judgment and command alone; no one else decides it. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports Qatāda's explanation that no one else judges or commands. Ibn ʿAṭiyya supplies the understood sense of judgment belonging to God. Al-Kashshāf and al-Sāmarrāʾī compare the sole judgment in 40:16. Al-Qurṭubī reports the afterlife where God settles and judges people. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī gives 5:40 and 28:88 for divine will and judgment; al-Biqāʿī includes every creature's inward and outward state. [11 notes: THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الكشاف)]
- 75:12/q08/p3 God assigns each person's final abode, Paradise or Hell, according to His will. — Ibn Zayd distinguishes the people of Paradise and Hell and cites 29:64 for the true life of the Hereafter. Al-Ṭabrisī describes the respective abodes of believers and unbelievers. Al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, and Elmalılı make admission depend on God's will. Al-Kashshāf, al-Ālūsī, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī offer it as an alternative; the latter cites 5:40 and 28:88. Al-Sāmarrāʾī quotes al-Kashshāf and al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ, the latter also assigning the abode by God's will. The Study Quran's Ṭ reports God alone chooses where each soul will reside. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(البحر المحيط,الكشاف); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 75:12/q08/p4 The settlement includes its time: God determines the duration and dispatch to final homes. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī adds the time of judgment, length of remaining, and departure for final homes to the action and place senses, saying the word's threefold scope is intended. His Ḥalaqāt version locates the time at the gathering. Al-Biqāʿī places the time of all creation's rest under God's judgment and will. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 75:12/q09 How is the clause 'to your Lord is the settlement' parsed?  [إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ ٱلْمُسْتَقَرُّ · grammar]
- 75:12/q09/p1 Al-mustaqarr is the subject and the preceding prepositional phrase is its predicate. — Al-Samīn explicitly identifies the subject and preposed predicate. Ibn ʿAṭiyya takes the implicit meaning to be that judgment or the like belongs to the Lord, and describes the subject's predicate as supplied through the preceding phrase. Al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr explain the effect of this predicate-first order as exclusivity. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 75:12/q10 Why choose the word al-mustaqarr?  [ٱلْمُسْتَقَرُّ · rhetoric]
- 75:12/q10/p1 It simultaneously evokes the act, place, and time of settlement, and its cadence suits the verse. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts this form with wording confined to only one sense; he adds God's determination of the time of judgment, duration of remaining, and dispatch to final homes, and says the three senses plus cadence make the word irreplaceable. The Ḥalaqāt account also intends all available senses because no contextual clue excludes any. [4 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 75:12/q11 Who speaks the statement about the final settlement?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the clause is God's fresh address to the Prophet or the fleeing person's continuation of speech; al-Ālūsī objects to al-Baḥr's latter reading.
- 75:12/q11/p1 God speaks it as a new explanatory or confirming address to the Prophet. — Al-Ālūsī explicitly prefers this and objects to al-Baḥr's reading. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it God's insertion addressed to the Prophet in worldly time, reminding him of God's sovereignty on that Day. Elmalılı makes the Prophet the first addressee and thereby includes everyone. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; ELMALILI+]
- 75:12/q11/p2 The fleeing human says it after realizing there is no escape. — Al-Qurṭubī reads 'No refuge; to your Lord is the destination' as the person's own words. Al-Ālūsī reports al-Baḥr's view that both clauses continue human speech and reports the possible self-correction after the human asks where to flee; al-Ālūsī finds al-Baḥr's reading problematic. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البحر); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:12/q12 How does the statement answer the surrounding scene of flight and judgment?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes explain either the question-and-answer sequence or its implied, unstated arrival of Resurrection.
- 75:12/q12/p1 The question 'Where to flee?' is answered: every path closes except return or refuge with God, followed by accountability for deeds. — Al-Iṣlāḥī reads 75:7–15 as answering demands to hasten judgment: upheaval closes escape and each answers for every deed despite concealment. Kur'an Yolu says all gather before God and hear the good and evil they did or failed to do. Al-Sāmarrāʾī makes 'to your Lord' the most important answer to what the fugitive seeks; Elmalılı says flight from God finds no other shelter. Al-Ālūsī reports both God's reply and the person's self-correction as possible sequences. [7 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 75:12/q12/p2 The arrival of Resurrection is left unstated because the flight question and final return already imply its terrors have arrived. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies this ellipsis in the sequence. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 75:12/q13 How can all move toward God while some are veiled from Him?  [* · theology]
- 75:12/q13/p1 Veiling means deprivation of divine honor, not ignorance of God or physical distance from Him. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī relates universal movement toward meeting God, citing 84:6, 96:8, and 53:42, to the veiling in 83:14–15 and explains the latter as exclusion from honor. [2 notes: TABATABAI]
