# 84:6: 15 questions

## 84:6/q01 Who is the human being addressed?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular address is universal, occasioned by a named person, or restricted to an individual or group.
- 84:6/q01/p1 Humanity generally, each accountable person. — Ibn Qutayba says singular address can include a collective and cites 39:8; in another work he invokes God's forming people in varied appearances (30:22). Al-Tabrisi specifies every accountable descendant of Adam; al-Bursevi includes believers, unbelievers, and sinners. Al-Biqa'i says the singular treats humanity as a kind before each individual is discussed. Al-Tabataba'i grounds universality in God's universal lordship. Al-Iṣlāḥī says the warning especially targets conceited people absorbed in worldly life; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it chiefly warns resurrection-denying polytheists while reminding and reassuring believers. Abu Hayyan says a named occasion still leaves the wording general. [12 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; JISHUMI(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 84:6/q01/p2 Al-Aswad ibn ʿAbd al-Asad. — Muqātil identifies him; Abu Hayyan adds that he disputed resurrection with his brother Abū Salama, while the wording applies generally. Ibn ʿĀshūr records spelling variation and allows a named man to be the occasion or the intended addressee. [6 notes: MUQATIL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل); IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل)]
- 84:6/q01/p3 Ubayy ibn Khalaf. — Abu Hayyan describes him as pursuing worldly gain, harming the Messenger, and persisting in disbelief; Ibn ʿĀshūr allows a named man to be occasion or intended addressee. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل)]
- 84:6/q01/p4 All unbelievers. — Al-Qurṭubī reports the paraphrase 'O unbeliever.' [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 84:6/q01/p5 Al-Ḥārith ibn ʿAmr al-Qurashī. — Al-Jishumī reports this as an occasion of revelation. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 84:6/q01/p6 The Prophet, in his toil to convey revelation and endure harm. — Abu Hayyan calls this view remote; on that reading, he would be rewarded at meeting God. [0 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 84:6/q02 Why is a singular human addressed?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · rhetoric]
- 84:6/q02/p1 The singular can address all people while focusing on each individual. — Ibn Qutayba cites comparable singular collective address in 39:8 and 82:6. Al-Biqa'i says it first treats humanity as a kind and then turns to each person and his record. Al-Bursevī says it addresses each believer, unbeliever, or sinner more emphatically than a plural would. [3 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 84:6/q03 What does kadhḥ or kādiḥ mean?  [كَادِحٌ كَدْحًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the root is understood through toil and scratching, return, walking, or its comparison with biting.
- 84:6/q03/p1 Strenuous, persistent work or exertion, in worldly or otherworldly life and in good or evil. — Ibn Qutayba and al-Qurṭubī include earning a livelihood. Al-Zajjāj, Abū ʿUbayda, al-Qurṭubī, al-Tabrisī, Abu Hayyan, and al-Samīn cite Ibn Muqbil's akdaḥ for livelihood; al-Qurṭubī, Abu Hayyan, and al-Samīn cite another poem. Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds intensity and speed and a poem about laboring through life. Al-Tabrisī compares the effort marks on a bull's hide; al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, Abu Hayyan, al-Bursevī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī connect it with scratching skin and effort that leaves a mark. Al-Layth defines it as one's good or evil work. Ibn ʿĀshūr says its original root sense is uncertain, while al-Rāghib defines exhausting work. Al-Qāshānī includes good and evil deeds. [28 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,الليث); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الشاعر,النبي); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مقبل,القتبي); BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن مقبل,الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL(الراغب); TABATABAI(الراغب,قيل); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 84:6/q03/p2 Return to the Lord. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk transmits Ibn ʿAbbās's gloss that one inevitably returns to the Lord and that kadhḥ means return. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك عن ابن عباس)]
- 84:6/q03/p3 Walking toward the Lord. — Ibn ʿĀshūr offers this as another possible sense of kādiḥ, with a different syntactic result for ilā. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 84:6/q03/p4 A sense related to biting with the teeth. — Al-Samīn reports al-Rāghib's comparison with kadm and al-Khalīl's distinction that kadhḥ is less than kadm. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الخليل,الراغب)]

## 84:6/q04 What does the person's striving toward the Lord consist of or lead to?  [كَادِحٌ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ كَدْحًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase describes deeds done for God, all human toil as a journey, or the final meeting as its immediate endpoint.
- 84:6/q04/p1 Every person works through deeds, good or evil, and thereby advances toward the Lord and an account. — Muqātil says one meets God bearing deeds. Tustarī, al-Qushayrī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn Kathīr, al-Bursevī and al-Kurʾān Yolu describe work, judgment, and corresponding outcomes. Al-Tabrisī specifies life's hardships and accountable descendants of Adam. Ibn ʿĀshūr says successive daily work reaches tomorrow and finally death. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the servant is owned and governed by God and accountable for choices. Al-Qāshānī mentions exertion in good or evil acts. Al-Iṣlāḥī describes an unbroken journey from birth through changing states to death, irrespective of status. Asad emphasizes sorrow, pain, drudgery and worry for everyone. [20 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(القتبي); QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 84:6/q04/p2 The person performs work for the Lord. — Al-Kalbī and al-Ḍaḥḥāk, with Qatāda in al-Baghawī, explain the work as deeds for God; al-Zajjāj reports this tafsīr, as does al-Suyūṭī's collection. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي والضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة والكلبي والضحاك); DURR-FULL(الضحاك,قتادة)]
- 84:6/q04/p3 Life's toil moves one toward meeting the Lord at death and thereafter. — Al-Zamakhsharī calls the destination death. Al-Bursevī speaks of death and subsequent states; Ibn ʿĀshūr explains how daily effort ends with death and return to God. Al-Qāshānī says every breath advances one swiftly through death. Asad says earthly toil ends at resurrection; al-Iṣlāḥī says this is the purpose of life's journey. [10 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; QASHANI; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 84:6/q04/p4 The person is carried inexorably toward meeting God and his appointed fate. — Al-Iṣlāḥī frames this as rebuke and notes that right-hand recipients receive an easy, happy reckoning; Kurʾān Yolu says no one lives forever and God alone calls all to account. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 84:6/q05 How does ilā work in 'toward your Lord'?  [إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ · grammar]
turns on: How to construe the preposition and its governing expression.
- 84:6/q05/p1 It retains its directional sense: striving leads to or ends at meeting God. — Al-Tabrisī prefers this construction and compares tabattal ilayhi (73:8), turning from creation or desiring God. Ibn ʿĀshūr says ilā marks the action's endpoint and makes kādiḥ the predicate of inna on the striving sense; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it gives the expression a journey sense. [5 notes: TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); TABRISI+]
- 84:6/q05/p2 Ilā has the sense of lām, 'for'. — Al-Tabrisī reports this alternative grammatical view. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 84:6/q05/p3 Ilā attaches to an omitted expression: one labors for oneself and arrives at the Lord. — Al-Tabrisī reports this possible construction. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 84:6/q05/p4 With kādiḥ meaning 'walking,' the ilā phrase is an adverbial adjunct. — Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts this with his striving parse, where the phrase is a predicate. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 84:6/q06 What is the grammatical force of kadhan?  [كَدْحًۭا · grammar]
- 84:6/q06/p1 It is an emphatic accusative verbal noun or cognate object. — Al-Naḥḥās calls it a verbal noun with emphatic force; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it an accusative cognate object emphasizing kādiḥ and says the striving is unavoidable. [2 notes: NAHHAS; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 84:6/q07 How is famulāqīhi connected grammatically to the preceding clause?  [فَمُلَٰقِيهِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether fa coordinates a predicate or clause, introduces an elided predicate, or relates to the conditional; al-Naḥḥās also records an inflectional explanation.
- 84:6/q07/p1 It is coordinated with kādiḥ or the preceding sentence, with a causal sequence. — Al-Samīn says the fa may coordinate with kādiḥ and that the causal relation is evident. He reports Ibn ʿAṭiyya's clause coordination as reconstructing 'then you meet it'; the Shaykh says this clause-only analysis is unnecessary because individual elements can be coordinated. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 84:6/q07/p2 It is a predicate with an omitted subject: 'then you meet it.' — Al-Samīn gives this as an alternative parse; Ibn ʿAṭiyya's reconstruction supplies 'you' and refers the pronoun to the effort. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية)]
- 84:6/q07/p3 It functions as, or indicates, the response to the preceding conditional. — Al-Samīn mentions this possibility; the detailed alternatives for the idhā clauses are mapped separately. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); TABRISI(الزجاج)]
- 84:6/q07/p4 It is nominative, with its original ḍamma omitted. — Al-Naḥḥās gives two explanations: heaviness, or yā resembling an unvowelled alif; he adds that some Arabs even omit fatḥa on yā for the latter reason. [2 notes: NAHHAS]
- 84:6/q07/p5 The clause is a necessary connective or adjective to ayy. — Al-Naḥḥās treats the phrase as an adjective to ayy, while reporting al-Akhfash's view that it is a necessary connective. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 84:6/q08 Whom or what does the pronoun in famulāqīhi mean one meets?  [فَمُلَٰقِيهِ · referent]
turns on: The pronoun's antecedent and whether meeting deeds is literal, metonymic for recompense, or a meeting with their record.
- 84:6/q08/p1 The Lord Himself, who judges and recompenses the person. — Al-Zajjāj reports this tafsīr; Abū Isḥāq, most exegetes cited by Ibn ʿAṭiyya, and Ibn ʿĀshūr take the pronoun this way. Al-Tabrisī says God alone judges. Al-Bayḍāwī makes the toil clause parenthetical and links meeting God with recompense. Ibn Kathīr says meeting God and one's deeds are compatible. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes a state with no screen from God's sole judgment; Ibn ʿĀshūr invokes inna to stress that none escapes. [18 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TUSTARI; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور المتأولين); TABRISI(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(القتبي); BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض الناس); BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 84:6/q08/p2 One's work, labor, or deed, including its consequence. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī report this; al-Wāḥidī al-Wajīz places the meeting at Resurrection. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says this can mean its good or bad reward. Al-Tabrisī says calling recompense 'the deed' magnifies its importance. Al-Samīn reports Ibn ʿAṭiyya's pronoun-to-effort parse. Ibn Kathīr cites the attributed saying to Jābir, 'act as you wish, for you will meet your deeds,' and Ibn ʿAbbās's report; al-Suyūṭī and Study Quran report similar Ibn ʿAbbās material, the latter citing 99:6–8. Al-Qāshānī notes possible harmful consequences. Ibn Kathīr says the Lord and deed readings are compatible. [18 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(قيل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض الناس); TABRISI(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,بعض الناس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAbbās)]
- 84:6/q08/p3 The recompense or punishment for one's deeds. — Al-Wāḥidī chooses reward as the intended encounter; al-Qushayrī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya specify good for good and evil for evil. Ibn Kathīr says the Lord reading entails recompense too. Al-Bursevī says no one escapes the outcome of worldly acts. [9 notes: QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض الناس); TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض الناس); BURSEVI; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 84:6/q08/p4 The record of one's deeds. — Al-Qurṭubī argues that the work itself is over and the following verse speaks of receiving the book. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 84:6/q09 What answers the preceding idhā conditions?  [فَمُلَٰقِيهِ · grammar]
turns on: Which following expression completes the two opening conditions, or whether their result is deliberately unspoken.
- 84:6/q09/p1 Verse 6, especially 'then you meet Him,' supplies or indicates the answer. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Tabrisī say famulāqīhi indicates it: at Resurrection one meets one's deed. Al-Mubarrad takes the whole address through the meeting clause as the answer and treats the striving clause as an insertion; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the conditional and answer govern each other in different respects and explains that idhā's past verb indicates certainty about the future. Al-Baghawī reports verse 6 as apodosis. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says verse 6 supplies the omitted result: people meet the Lord for reckoning. Elmalılı reports both the famulāqīhi and whole-verse versions. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); JISHUMI(المبرد); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو العباس المبرد); TABRISI(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(المبرد); TABATABAI]
- 84:6/q09/p2 The answer is omitted and inferred from the context of recompense or meeting. — Al-Farrāʾ says the familiar Qurʾānic pattern makes omission intelligible. Many grammarians cited by Ibn ʿAṭiyya say the listener knows it; others make famulāqīhi its governing word or supply an implicit verb. Al-Biqa'i infers judgment of everyone's striving. Al-Mubarrad permits an omitted 'you will meet'; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says verse 6 supplies the sense. Elmalılı lists omission for awe and a sense that everyone learns their deeds. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(آخرون منهم,كثير من النحاة); BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(المبرد); TABATABAI]
- 84:6/q09/p3 The answer lies in verse 7; verse 6 is parenthetical. — Elmalılı prefers an answer in one of the next two verses and specifically treats verse 7 as the answer. [2 notes: ELMALILI+]
- 84:6/q09/p4 An implied fa before 'O human' contains the answer. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this separate analysis. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل)]
- 84:6/q09/p5 The clauses can be adverbial, answer an implied question, or find their answer in the obedience verbs. — Elmalılı reports these further proposals, including an implied question about recompense. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 84:6/q10 How is kādiḥ ilā rabbika parsed in relation to inna?  [كَادِحٌ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether kādiḥ means striving or walking.
- 84:6/q10/p1 Kādiḥ, with ilā marking the destination, is the predicate of inna. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the preposition points to the action's endpoint, meeting God, when kādiḥ means striving. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 84:6/q10/p2 If kādiḥ means walking, the ilā phrase is an adverbial adjunct. — Ibn ʿĀshūr offers this as the alternative parsing. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 84:6/q11 Is there a reported variant in the wording or pronunciation?  [كَادِحٌ · readings]
- 84:6/q11/p1 Ṭalḥa assimilates the kāf of kādiḥ into the following min. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the assimilation. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(طلحة)]

## 84:6/q12 What conduct does the verse urge?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes give complementary exhortations about how to respond to inevitable striving.
- 84:6/q12/p1 Direct one's inevitable effort toward obedience and deeds that bring a good outcome. — Tustarī urges examining whether one's striving suits the Garden and nearness or the Fire and distance; he reports Kamhas's forty years of tears after taking a piece of a neighbor's wall for a guest who had eaten fish bought for one daniq. Qatāda says weak human striving should be made obedience if possible, with help only from God. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says life's speed makes this a warning to act righteously. Al-Bursevī urges saving actions and avoiding ruin, shame and disgrace. Maybudī urges mindfulness as each moment brings one nearer God. [8 notes: TUSTARI(كهمس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد عن قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(Maybudī)]
- 84:6/q12/p2 Act despite predestination: each person is eased toward what he was created to do. — Al-Bursevī reports the Prophet's reply to a question about how to act after pens had dried and destinies were decreed. [1 notes: BURSEVI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 84:6/q12/p3 Live and love mindful of death and separation, and act mindful of meeting deeds. — Ibn Kathīr reports Jābir's saying attributed to Gabriel addressing Muḥammad: live as you wish because you will die, love as you wish because you will part, and act as you wish because you will meet your deeds. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 84:6/q12/p4 A remorseful person awaits mercy, a self-admiring one condemnation, and each worker what was sent ahead. — Al-Bursevī cites this hadith to explain the outcome of deeds. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 84:6/q13 How does the verse ground accountability and resurrection?  [* · theology]
turns on: The argument from God's lordship and justice, and the scope of recompense for deeds.
- 84:6/q13/p1 Universal lordship makes each person's return, judgment, and recompense necessary. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues from lordship to servitude, responsibility, return, accounting, and recompense; God's ownership means a servant should will and do only what God commands. Al-Iṣlāḥī calls accountability the purpose for which the world was created and invokes innate justice and the world's purpose for the final separation. Al-Biqa'i says each person's striving is judged, with different outcomes for disbelievers and Muslims. Kurʾān Yolu says only God calls people to account. [7 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 84:6/q13/p2 Human effort is unavoidable and ends in a fitting good or evil outcome. — Al-Qushayrī and al-Baghawī say good meets good and evil meets evil. Al-Bursevī says there is no escape from recompense; Elmalılı says life's exertion ends in a painful or pleasant outcome from God. Al-Iṣlāḥī contrasts easy reckoning for the right-hand group with disgrace for the other. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 84:6/q14 How does this verse connect to the surrounding passage and other sūrahs?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connection is with the immediate judgment scene, a preceding or another sūrah, or related return language.
- 84:6/q14/p1 The cosmic opening leads to every person's deeds, record, judgment, and two outcomes. — Al-Ṭabarī links the sky splitting to seeing good or evil sent ahead. Al-Biqa'i reads the opening conditions as leading to repayment and the right-hand record as acceptance. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the Meccan sūrah concerns the Hour and reckoning with warning predominant. Al-Iṣlāḥī says right-hand recipients prosper and left-hand recipients are disgraced. Al-Qurṭubī points to the next verse's book-taking when identifying what is met. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 84:6/q14/p2 Al-Inshiqāq continues al-Muṭaffifīn's judgment of those who denied resurrection and recompense. — Al-Biqa'i says allies receive reward and enemies punishment after denying their Creator-King. Al-Iṣlāḥī says both sūrahs warn affluent deniers or people expecting worldly honor in the afterlife; the earlier sūrah reasons from human nature and this one from physical signs. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 84:6/q14/p3 The verse's toil contrasts with al-Tīn's honoring of humanity. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says this difference makes al-Tīn's fa fitting for continuing, unclouded reward; he cites 84:6 and 84:16–19. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 84:6/q14/p4 The expression recalls the other 'O human' verse, 82:6. — Al-Burhān notes that one opening asks what deceived a person about the generous Lord, whereas this one announces labor toward Him; Ibn Qutayba uses both for singular collective address. [2 notes: BURHAN; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]
- 84:6/q14/p5 Return language elsewhere in the passage helps explain the journey toward God. — Al-Tabrisī defines hawr as reversal or return, illustrated by returning from increase to deficiency and the expression about returning after being wound up; al-Ḍaḥḥāk's Ibn ʿAbbās gloss similarly takes kadhḥ as return. [2 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك عن ابن عباس)]

## 84:6/q15 What Resurrection scene is reported in connection with the verse?  [* · reports]
- 84:6/q15/p1 Creatures surge for 300 years before the Lord judges them. — Muqātil says the Lord then sits on the Throne, the angels stand in ranks, and creatures see Paradise and Hell. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
