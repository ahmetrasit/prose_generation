# 28:15: 49 questions

## 28:15/q01 Which city did Moses enter?  [ٱلْمَدِينَةَ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'city' names Egypt broadly, its royal capital, or a nearby settlement; the Qur'an itself does not name it.
- 28:15/q01/p1 Egypt, or its capital city where Pharaoh resided. — Ibn Shajara and Ibn Ishaq say Egypt; al-Jishumi prefers Egypt or its land. Al-Alusi calls it the best-known and most apparent identification. Islahi distinguishes the elite capital from the Israelites' settlement. [17 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن إسحاق); JISHUMI(السدي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن إسحاق); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن إسحاق); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(R); ALUSI-FULL+; JISHUMI+]
- 28:15/q01/p2 Memphis (Manf), in Egypt. — Al-Suddi's itinerary has Moses follow Pharaoh there; markets were closed and streets empty. Ibn Ashur calls it the northern capital; Bursevi places it west of the Nile and describes its royal history. [20 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); THALABI(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي,مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(ابن جرير); DURR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 28:15/q01/p3 A village called Khanin, Habin, Hain, or Habin, about two farsakhs from Egypt. — Muqatil locates Moses initially at Khanin and describes the city as a village; later tellings vary its spelling. Razi reports a town at that distance as the majority's account. [10 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); JISHUMI(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الجمهور); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 28:15/q01/p4 Heliopolis or Ayn al-Shams. — Al-Dahhak and later authorities give this identification. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الضحاك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); BIQAI-FULL(البغوي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 28:15/q01/p5 Alexandria. — Abu Hayyan and Alusi list this further possibility. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 28:15/q02 When were the city's people unaware?  [عَلَىٰ حِينِ غَفْلَةٍۢ مِّنْ أَهْلِهَا · reports]
turns on: Whether ghafla denotes a daily rest, a festival's distraction, or failure to remember Moses.
- 28:15/q02/p1 At midday, during the siesta, when streets and markets were empty. — This is attributed to Ibn Abbas, Qatada, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Ikrima, al-Suddi, and others. - 28:15/q02/p2 Between sunset and nightfall or the evening prayers. — Ibn Abbas, al-Qurazi and Wahb are cited; variants call the endpoints sunset, night prayer, isha and atama. [21 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(محمد بن كعب القرظي); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس والقرظي); JISHUMI(محمد بن كعب); BAGHAWI-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(وهب بن منبه); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,قيل)]
- 28:15/q02/p3 On a festival day, while people were playing. — Al-Hasan and Ali are named for this report; some combine it with the account of Moses' return after expulsion. - 28:15/q02/p5 An inattentive interval when entering the city was unusual; its exact hour is unspecified. — Baydawi and Bursevi stress the unusual opportunity. Tabari declines to decide the reason or hour; Razi says the Qur'an provides no evidence to rank the reports. Asad translates the general sense; Tabatabai includes midday or midnight as examples. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; RAZI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI; TAB-FULL+]

## 28:15/q03 Why and in what circumstances did Moses enter the city?  [وَدَخَلَ ٱلْمَدِينَةَ عَلَىٰ حِينِ غَفْلَةٍۢ · reports]
turns on: The reports put Moses either within Pharaoh's household, in fearful opposition, or returning from an earlier exile.
- 28:15/q03/p1 He followed Pharaoh there and arrived during Pharaoh's midday rest. — Al-Suddi says Moses had Pharaoh's son's status, clothes, and mounts; Pharaoh rode without him, so he followed to Memphis. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); THALABI(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); JISHUMI(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); RAZI-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل)]
- 28:15/q03/p2 He entered secretly because his opposition to Pharaoh had produced mutual fear. — Ibn Ishaq says Moses had Israelite adherents and repudiated Pharaoh's religion. Al-Dahhak says Moses deliberately chose an inattentive hour. Islahi says he visited to see his oppressed people. [18 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); THALABI(ابن إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن إسحاق); NAHHAS(الضحاك); JISHUMI(ابن إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن إسحاق); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن إسحاق); RAZI-FULL(القول الأول); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن إسحاق,الضحاك,قيل); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن إسحاق); ALUSI-FULL(ابن إسحاق); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 28:15/q03/p3 He returned after Pharaoh had expelled him years before. — Ibn Zayd's childhood account includes the wife's protection and the ember test causing his speech impediment. Wahidi argues the long-return chronology conflicts with this stage of the narrative. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد); JISHUMI(ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد,غيره); RAZI-FULL(ابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT-]
- 28:15/q03/p4 The verse does not establish why he entered or when this happened relative to prophethood. — Tabari retains the verse's wording without selecting a story; Razi says waw need not mark temporal sequence and the reports lack decisive Quranic evidence. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; TAB-FULL+]

## 28:15/q04 How does 'upon a time of heedlessness' work?  [عَلَىٰ حِينِ غَفْلَةٍۢ مِّنْ أَهْلِهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether ala is read temporally with 'entered', as circumstance, or with hin treated as extra.
- 28:15/q04/p1 It attaches to 'entered'; 'upon' marks time, effectively 'during' or 'in'. — Al-Nahhas says the preposition focuses heedlessness itself; Burhan and Itqan cite 2:102 for temporal use. Al-Alusi prefers this straightforward syntax. Ibn Ashur instead describes metaphorical elevation that conveys security. [9 notes: NAHHAS; BURHAN; ITQAN; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 28:15/q04/p2 The phrase is circumstantial of Moses, the city, or the entry. — On the subject reading Moses entered furtively; Abu al-Baqa also allows a qualifier of the city. Al-Alusi objects that this evades the ordinary construction. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 28:15/q04/p3 'Time' is superfluous and the meaning is 'upon heedlessness'. — Wahidi reports al-Farra's analysis. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)]

## 28:15/q05 How is 'time' read?  [حِينِ · readings]
turns on: The treatment of the adverb under annexation and the explanation for its open ending.
- 28:15/q05/p1 The common genitive form is ḥīni. — Al-Alusi treats it as the obvious construction governed by ʿalā. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 28:15/q05/p2 Abu Talib reads ḥīna with fatḥa. — Abu Hayyan and al-Samin explain building the adverb because of the following verbal sense; Al-Alusi reports an assimilation explanation too but finds the proposed grammar unconvincing. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو طالب القارئ); ALUSI-FULL(أبو طالب القارئ); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 28:15/q06 How is 'from its people' attached?  [غَفْلَةٍۢ مِّنْ أَهْلِهَا · grammar]
- 28:15/q06/p1 It qualifies 'heedlessness': the inattentiveness came from the city's people. — Al-Alusi says the indefinite phrase is more eloquent than annexation because nunation magnifies the heedlessness. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 28:15/q07 What does 'fighting' describe and what were the men doing?  [رَجُلَيْنِ يَقْتَتِلَانِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the post-indefinite verb is an adjective or an allowable circumstantial clause.
- 28:15/q07/p1 The verb clause qualifies the two men; they were fighting one another. — Al-Samin and Al-Alusi prefer adjectival attachment; Bursevi explains reciprocal form; Ibn Ashur says the fighting was severe mutual beating and Biqai describes grappling, strangling, and striking. [10 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN]
- 28:15/q07/p2 The clause is circumstantial: Moses found them while they fought. — Ibn Atiyya gives this parse; al-Samin notes that Sibawayh permits circumstantial clauses from indefinite nouns more broadly than most grammarians. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 28:15/q08 What caused the fight?  [يَقْتَتِلَانِ · reports]
turns on: Whether the quarrel arose from forced labor, religious opposition, or an unspecified worldly matter.
- 28:15/q08/p1 The Copt tried to force the Israelite to carry wood to Pharaoh's kitchen or bakery. — Reports call the Copt a cook or baker and say the Israelite refused; one says the Copt then threatened Moses with the work. Ibn Ashur calls this coercion the aggression. - 28:15/q08/p2 They disputed over religion. — Abu Ali is named for this explanation. [3 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 28:15/q08/p3 They disputed over some worldly affair, unspecified. — Jishumi and Alusi report this possibility. [2 notes: JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 28:15/q09 How is the fighting verb read?  [يَقْتَتِلَانِ · readings]
turns on: Whether the two tāʾs are articulated separately or assimilated.
- 28:15/q09/p1 The ordinary reading retains the two tāʾ sounds in yaqtatilān. — The two men are grammatically dual. [2 notes: BURHAN; BURSEVI]
- 28:15/q09/p2 Nuaym ibn Maysarah assimilates the two tāʾs, transferring the first vowel to qāf. — Abu Hayyan, al-Samin and Alusi transmit this reading. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(نعيم بن ميسرة); ALUSI-FULL(نعيم بن ميسرة)]

## 28:15/q11 What does 'party' mean here?  [شِيعَتِهِۦ · meaning]
turns on: Which aspect of affiliation shiʿa denotes.
- 28:15/q11/p1 A group affiliated with someone: followers, helpers, companions, or kin. — Ibn Faris gives helpers and factions; a cited poem uses shiʿa for what follows after. ## 28:15/q13 How do the repeated 'this one' demonstratives function?  [هَٰذَا مِن شِيعَتِهِۦ وَهَٰذَا مِنْ عَدُوِّهِۦ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the clauses are descriptive or circumstantial, and how present pointing operates in past narration.
- 28:15/q13/p1 They stage the past, absent men as if present before an observer. — Zajjaj offers both absent-description and eyewitness-style explanations; Abu Ali compares 12:110. Al-Mubarrad cites Jarir for pointing at an absent person; Ibn Ashur says paired demonstratives need not distinguish distance, comparing 4:143 and poetry. [18 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(المبرد); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:15/q13/p2 Each nominal demonstrative clause describes one of the two men. — Al-Nahhas parses subject and predicate. Al-Samin allows description or circumstance, but calls the latter remote because the state is not changing. Al-Alusi says demonstratives link the descriptions back to the men. [4 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 28:15/q14 What does the Israelite's appeal mean?  [فَٱسْتَغَٰثَهُ ٱلَّذِى مِن شِيعَتِهِۦ عَلَى ٱلَّذِى مِنْ عَدُوِّهِۦ · meaning]
- 28:15/q14/p1 He asked Moses to rescue or support him against his opponent. — The verb derives from ghawth, help. Baydawi and Alusi explain ala through the sense of support or victory; Alusi cites the next day's appeal. Ibn Ashur infers the Israelite was overpowered. - 28:15/q14/p2 The expression also exposes a duty to help the oppressed. — Qurtubi calls this a duty in every revealed law; Ibn Ashur says coerced work was unjust. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 28:15/q15 How is the appeal verb read?  [فَٱسْتَغَٰثَهُ · readings]

## 28:15/q22 Did Moses intend to kill the man?  [فَوَكَزَهُۥ فَقَضَىٰ عَلَيْهِ · interpretation]

## 28:15/q24 How should Moses' deed and remorse be judged?  [فَوَكَزَهُۥ فَقَضَىٰ عَلَيْهِ قَالَ هَٰذَا مِنْ عَمَلِ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنِ · theology]

- 28:15/q37/p1 Before dhāl, unvowelled ṣād approaches or becomes zāy, while moving ṣād is best kept clear. — Sībawayh says emphatic ṣād can approximate the nearest voiced zāy but full conversion is also spoken; moving ṣād cannot be replaced. Sīn in the same place shifts to zāy, and shīn or jīm may approximate it, with examples. [4 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 28:15/q38 How are certain defective nouns and vocatives formed?  [* · grammar]
turns on: The distinct morphological analogies for dual formation and vocative shortening.
- 28:15/q38/p1 Defective four-letter nouns form duals and plurals with yāʾ, whether their alif replaces a root or is augmentative. — Sībawayh explains both by analogy with corresponding verbs, including wāw-root forms and examples ḥublā and maʿzā. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 28:15/q38/p2 Quadriliteral-like wāw and yāʾ behave as added, radical-like letters and remain under vocative shortening. — Sībawayh cites qanūr and habīj, their capacity for further augmentations, and avoidance of deleting two letters; Manṣūr may shorten to Manṣ. [4 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 28:15/q39 How do commands, supplications and topicalization affect case and fāʾ?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Whether preposed nouns are command objects, topics, or resumptions of implied prior discourse.
- 28:15/q39/p1 An object before a command is normally accusative under an overt or implied verb. — Sībawayh covers direct and prepositional objects, amā clauses, and third-person commands; commands require a verb more strongly than interrogatives. A nominative topic is possible with an understood context, but 'Zayd fa-ḍribhu' cannot be an ordinary subject; its object pronoun must remain. [18 notes: SIBAWAYH; SIBAWAYH+]
- 28:15/q39/p2 A relative or generalized topic with conditional force can take fāʾ in its predicate. — Sībawayh gives all-man and relative-clause examples and cites 2:274. He distinguishes this from a simple Zayd topic. [4 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 28:15/q39/p3 Revelatory law verses begin with raised nouns under an implied context, though accusative readings exist. — Sībawayh analyzes 24:2, 5:38 and 4:16 through an antecedent topic about ordinances and notes some readers' accusative forms. [7 notes: SIBAWAYH(أناس)]

## 28:15/q40 What rhetorical principles about simile, metaphor and predication are cited?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: What part of rhetorical meaning comes from transfer, predication, qualification, or discourse knowledge.
- 28:15/q40/p1 Simile asserts likeness, while metaphor transfers a term and parable constructs a comparison. — Al-Jurjani distinguishes sensory likeness and intellectual tamthil, insists an explicit simile is literal, and says context settles a borrowed noun such as 'lion' or figurative verbs. [5 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 28:15/q40/p2 Metaphor's force exceeds merely stating a strong resemblance. — Al-Jurjani rejects explanations by resemblance alone or by wording-versus-meaning; the pearl, narcissus, rose and hail images work by asserting intensity delightfully. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 28:15/q40/p3 Unfavorable images in praise must be cushioned by favorable context. — Al-Jurjani analyzes night, wormwood and honey, the Mutanabbi's beauty and ugliness, and criticizes Abu Tammam's unguarded low-status names. [4 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 28:15/q40/p4 One may pair literal and figurative images only when the two referents truly differ in status. — Al-Jurjani rejects the two-rains example as both metaphorical, accepts the two-lions example, and explains the abstract benefit in al-Farazdaq's rain comparison. [3 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 28:15/q40/p5 A full proposition requires predication of one term of another, with qualifiers depending on that base. — Al-Jurjani identifies musnad and musnad ilayh and analyzes 'Zayd struck' and al-Farazdaq's negation; grammatical restrictions and intention build the meaning. [7 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR; JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 28:15/q40/p6 Complements and definiteness shape an act's and predicate's sense. — Al-Jurjani says a verbal noun's object changes the type of action; definite derived predicates express characteristic capacity, while alladhī identifies a referent by information already known. [4 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 28:15/q41 How does the Qur'an's arrangement and style establish its distinctiveness?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether distinctive form, mixed arrangement, or the unanswered challenge is the cited proof.
- 28:15/q41/p1 Its distinctive speech exceeds familiar poetry, rhymed prose, sermons, letters and ordinary speech. — Al-Rummani compares its departure from convention to a handwoven cloth exceeding the finest machine work and to miracles of the sea and staff; changing rhyme endings does not reproduce authorship. [4 notes: RUMMANI]
- 28:15/q41/p2 Mixing stories, warnings and rulings within a sura gives a listener access to proofs across subjects. — Al-Khattabi reports the contrary criticism that segregating topics would improve organization, then argues for the broad benefit of their combination. [2 notes: KHATTABI; KHATTABI+]
- 28:15/q41/p3 The prolonged challenge and opponents' failure to answer prove the Qur'an could not be fabricated. — Al-Baqillani notes charges of fabrication and borrowed tales; he argues that hostile opponents faced severe losses yet never produced an easy verbal counter in their own language. [4 notes: BAQILLANI]

## 28:15/q42 How should Qur'anic interpretation be approached?  [* · interpretation]
- 28:15/q42/p1 Reading requires understanding and action, with critical judgment about sources. — Tha'labi describes the Qur'an as overseer of earlier scriptures, warns that memorization alone is insufficient, and says trusted study distinguishes sound from unsound. He criticizes doctrinal innovators, jurists without tafsir expertise, and uncritical report collectors. [6 notes: THALABI]

## 28:15/q43 How is the Qur'an said to encompass knowledge and languages?  [* · sciences]
- 28:15/q43/p1 It encompasses earlier and later knowledge and alludes to many languages while selecting familiar forms. — Itqan cites Ibn al-Naqib on Arabic and foreign languages and al-Khuyyi on Persian istabraq as an irreplaceable concise name for fine thick brocade. [3 notes: ITQAN(ابن النقيب,الخويي)]
- 28:15/q43/p2 It indicates foundational knowledge and recounts all major earlier peoples, including Moses' killing of the Copt. — Al-Suyuti lists creation, histories, prophets and episodes of Moses from infancy through Midian and Pharaoh. [3 notes: ITQAN]

## 28:15/q44 What are the cited signs and virtues of the Qur'an?  [* · sciences]
- 28:15/q44/p1 Predictions and accurate ancient accounts are proofs; permanence, affective power and compact breadth are further virtues. — Itqan says future events occurred as foretold and ancient scriptures were known to an unlettered Prophet; it reports deaths upon hearing verses, unwearying recitation, divine preservation and breadth in few words, while separating these virtues from four central inimitability grounds. [6 notes: ITQAN]

## 28:15/q45 How are omitted agents used in Qur'anic style?  [* · rhetoric]
- 28:15/q45/p1 Passive, middle forms and figurative attribution can all omit an agent for a shared rhetorical purpose. — Bint al-Shati says grammar identifies forms but not the point of omitting the agent; she notes repeated passives in Resurrection scenes of trumpet, earth, mountains and Hell. [3 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 28:15/q46 What is the proper practice of istiʿādha and recitation pauses?  [* · readings]
turns on: Which recitation context and transmitted phonetic rule applies.
- 28:15/q46/p1 Audible istiʿādha cues listeners; whispering suits solitary and quiet recitation and is widely preferred in prayer. — Abu Shama conditions loudness on hearers. Al-Shafi'i has both choice and loudness recommendations; school reports prefer whispering, with practice from Abu Hurayra and Ibn Umar. The author gives conditions for recitation rounds. [8 notes: NASHR(أبو شامة,أبو علي الطبري,الشافعي,النووي,صاحب البيان); NASHR(الجمهور من أصحاب الشافعي)+]
- 28:15/q46/p2 Hamza may be eased, especially at pause, under transmitted Arabic practice. — Abu Shama calls the topic difficult; Ibn Mihran wrote on it and cites dialect evidence. The author says all readers ease some hamza, particularly Hijazi routes; a report claiming the Prophet and caliphs omitted it is rejected for weak transmission through Musa ibn Ubayda. [11 notes: NASHR(أبو شامة,أحمد,ابن عمر,ابن مهران,بعض العرب)]
- 28:15/q46/p3 Rūm and ishām at pause show a connected vowel to a listener, with restrictions on particular tanwīn forms. — The author favors al-Shatibi and al-Dani's balance and disputes Sibt al-Khayyat's claimed consensus. Isham is barred on yawma'idh but allowed on kull and ghawash because of vowel origins. [4 notes: NASHR(سبط الخياط); NASHR(الشاطبي والداني)+]

## 28:15/q47 What lexical and grammatical generalizations are attached to the verse's wording?  [* · grammar]
- 28:15/q47/p1 An enemy noun may denote one or many and may appear without feminine tāʾ. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Nahhas and Ibn Ashur discuss its number. Basrans allow feminine tāʾ by sense; al-Nahhas rejects the Kufan weak-wāw explanation because wāw is strong. [5 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; NAHHAS(البصريون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:15/q47/p2 A general wording may be added or omitted as meaning and context require. — Samarrai states this contextual principle. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 28:15/q48 What happened to the slain man's body?  [فَقَضَىٰ عَلَيْهِ · reports]
- 28:15/q48/p1 Moses buried the slain man in sand. — Abu Bakr ibn Abd Allah's associates give this report. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو بكر بن عبد الله)]

## 28:15/q49 How certain are the reported identifications of the city?  [ٱلْمَدِينَةَ · reports]
- 28:15/q49/p1 The origins of the proposed place identifications are unclear. — Study Quran compares Exodus 2:11–15 but says the source of the specific identifications is unclear. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
