# 7:92: 17 questions

## 7:92/q01 Who are those described as denying Shuʿayb?  [ٱلَّذِينَ كَذَّبُوا۟ شُعَيْبًۭا · referent]
- 7:92/q01/p1 The unbelieving people of Midian, including the oppressive chiefs who threatened Shuʿayb and his followers. — Elmalılı identifies them with those who threatened expulsion; Kur'an Yolu specifies Midian's unbelieving, oppressive elite and their deserved catastrophe. [2 notes: ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 7:92/q02 How is the first 'those who denied Shuʿayb' construed?  [ٱلَّذِينَ كَذَّبُوا۟ شُعَيْبًۭا كَأَن لَّمْ يَغْنَوْا۟ فِيهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the relative begins a fresh assertion or depends syntactically on the later clause or the chiefs' preceding speech; al-Samīn also disputes the precise antecedent of the attached reading.
- 7:92/q02/p1 It starts an independent nominal sentence as subject, with the comparison as predicate. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr give the subject–predicate analysis. Abū Ḥayyān rejects attached parses as strained. Al-Jurjānī, as reported by al-Qurṭubī, says the sentence begins anew; al-Wāḥidī also calls the repeated clause a fresh nominal sentence. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL(الجرجاني); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 7:92/q02/p2 The second relative clause and its predicate complete the first subject; the comparison is parenthetical or circumstantial to 'denied.' — Al-Samīn reports this parse; Abū al-Baqāʾ allows the second clause as predicate and the simile as a circumstance of the pronoun in كذبوا. Al-Ālūsī reports this among Abū al-Baqāʾ's alternatives. Abū Ḥayyān considers such attachment strained. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 7:92/q02/p3 The comparison and the second relative clause are two predicates of the first relative subject. — Al-Samīn gives the second relative as a second predicate, and allows it as first or second predicate in his survey. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 7:92/q02/p4 The first relative is attached to the earlier chiefs' speech as a modifier or apposition; the comparison is circumstantial. — Abū al-Baqāʾ allows modification or replacement of قومه. Al-Samīn reports apposition to الذين كفروا من قومه in 7:90, reports an adjective of that phrase from Abū al-Baqāʾ and the Shaykh, but refines its antecedent to الملأ and prefers apposition to, or an adjective of, الملأ. Abū Ḥayyān calls the attached analyses strained. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 7:92/q03 What does 'yaghnu fīhā' mean?  [يَغْنَوْا۟ فِيهَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether ghaniya is taken from residence, or from wealth and sufficiency; some reports paraphrase the resulting image more broadly as never having existed.
- 7:92/q03/p1 To dwell, remain, or settle there, often for a long time; the comparison says they seemed never to have inhabited it. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Ṭabarī, al-Aṣmaʿī through al-Zajjāj, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Rāzī, al-Samīn, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite ghaniya bi-makān and maghnā/maghānī ('dwelling[s]'). Al-Ṭabarī cites an anonymous yaghni line and Ruʾba's maghnā; al-Thaʿlabī and al-Qurṭubī cite Labīd and Ḥātim; al-Jishumī cites Muhalhil and Labīd; Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites the line between al-Ḥajūn and al-Ṣafā, neighbors' pact, 'وقد نغنى بها', 'المغانيا', and Muhalhil. Ibn ʿAbbās' reports range from inhabiting to inhabiting for a long time; al-Rāghib adds that long residence makes another place unnecessary. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites Muhalhil and Labīd; her Ibn ʿAbbās report also has an 'never existed' version. Al-Thaʿlabī includes staying and enjoying life in his broad gloss; al-Biqāʿī notes vanished leisure and self-sufficiency. [55 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(حاتم,لبيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن قتيبة,الأخفش); WAHIDI-BASIT(الشاعر,المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن عباس,لبيد,مهلهل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الشاعر,مهلهل); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس والأخفش,ابن قتيبة,الأصمعي,الزجاج,حاتم طيئ); RAZI-FULL(الشاعر); QURTUBI-FULL(حاتم طي,لبيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن قتيبة,الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس)]
- 7:92/q03/p2 To live there in ease, prosperity, enjoyment, or self-sufficiency. — Qatāda's gloss is widely transmitted, sometimes pairing dwelling with enjoyment. Al-Zajjāj, Ibn al-Anbārī, and al-Samīn connect the sense to wealth or freedom from need, rather than bare residence; al-Zajjāj and al-Ālūsī cite Ḥātim's verse contrasting poverty and wealth, and al-Samīn cites al-Aswad ibn Yaʿfur's 'بأنعم عيشة'. Ibn ʿAṭiyya's poetry survey finds residence accompanied by ease, perhaps from istighnāʾ; Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr report it. Al-Jishumī says a home makes its occupant independent of other places. Al-Rāghib likewise joins long residence with sufficiency. Al-Biqāʿī mentions their lost leisure and celebration. [30 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ(الشاعر حاتم طي); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري,الزجاج,قتادة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,قتادة); SAMIN-DURR(الأسود بن يعفر,الزجاج); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن الأنباري,غير واحد,قتادة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية); TABATABAI(الراغب)]
- 7:92/q03/p3 It says they seemed never to have been or existed there at all. — Ibn Zayd, Abū Mālik, Muqātil and later reports use the broad gloss 'never there'; al-Ṭabrisī and al-Itqān call it never existing, the latter citing Labīd's 'غنيت سبتا'. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ records Ibn ʿAbbās' variant specifying this world at the time of punishment, alongside 11:68 and 11:95. Al-Rāzī says both dwelling and prosperity senses yield the picture of never having occupied those homes. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(أبو مالك); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد ومقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة وابن زيد ومقاتل); RAZI-FULL; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس)]
- 7:92/q03/p4 The verb can also mean 'became beautiful.' — Al-Jishumī records this additional lexical use without applying it as the verse's main interpretation. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 7:92/q04 What does the comparison with never having lived there convey?  [كَأَن لَّمْ يَغْنَوْا۟ فِيهَا · interpretation]
turns on: The notes emphasize either the total removal of people and traces or the contrast between deep rootedness and a transient stay.
- 7:92/q04/p1 Their punishment so completely erased them and their traces that their former settled life seemed never to have happened. — Al-Ṭabarī says their town was left empty; Ibn ʿAṭiyya stresses complete destruction as a lesson. Al-Bayḍāwī says they were uprooted, and Abū Ḥayyān links the emptied place to 10:24 and a poem about Makkah without its inhabitants. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the point is erasure of traces rather than ordinary death, compares 10:24 and 69:8, and infers earthquake and lightning burned and buried bodies and overturned dwellings. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says the quake left them dead amid homes, kin, estates and possessions they had been deeply rooted in; Kur'an Yolu and The Study Quran also identify the catastrophic erasure. [21 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي,بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:92/q04/p2 It depicts even an established population as though it had stayed only briefly and could leave its land without difficulty. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ contrasts transient residents with people bound by kin, homes, estates, and possessions; the instant loss of that rooted life drives the simile. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 7:92/q05 What is the form of 'kaʾan' here?  [كَأَن · grammar]
- 7:92/q05/p1 It is a lightened form of kaʾanna before a verbal clause. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ attributes the lightening to its introduction of a verbal clause. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 7:92/q06 What does fronting the first relative clause convey?  [ٱلَّذِينَ كَذَّبُوا۟ شُعَيْبًۭا كَأَن لَّمْ يَغْنَوْا۟ فِيهَا · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes differ in the effect they draw from making the relative clause the subject: exclusive allocation, causal implication, or both.
- 7:92/q06/p1 It singles out the deniers for annihilation and implies the escape of Shuʿayb's followers. — Al-Kashshāf states exclusive destruction; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn report this from al-Zamakhsharī. Al-Samīn equates the claim of اختصاص with restriction but notes disagreement over its force in noun-first versus predicate-first formulations. Al-Ālūsī says the earlier contrast of the two groups and the relative subject can yield exclusivity. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:92/q06/p2 Making denial the subject hints that denial caused the lasting destruction. — Al-Ālūsī says the predicate's dependence on the relative subject signals causation and the believers' lasting escape; Ibn ʿĀshūr also reads the relative wording as punishment for denial. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:92/q07 How is the repeated 'those who denied Shuʿayb' construed in the second sentence?  [ٱلَّذِينَ كَذَّبُوا۟ شُعَيْبًۭا كَانُوا۟ هُمُ ٱلْخَٰسِرِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the repetition starts a self-contained sentence, completes the first subject, or is dependent by apposition or an implied verb.
- 7:92/q07/p1 It begins a fresh independent nominal sentence, with 'they were the losers' as its predicate. — Al-Kashshāf and Abū Ḥayyān parse it this way; al-Samīn calls this the clearest analysis. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Bursawī describe the second clause as a new or resumptive address. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء)+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 7:92/q07/p2 It is the predicate, or one of two predicates, of the first relative clause. — Abū al-Baqāʾ and al-Samīn allow this attached analysis, with the comparison circumstantial or as another predicate. Al-Ālūsī also reports it. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء)-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 7:92/q07/p3 It apposes the pronoun in 'yaghnaw.' — Abū al-Baqāʾ offers this parse, reported by Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī; Abū Ḥayyān prefers an independent subject. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]
- 7:92/q07/p4 It is an accusative specification after an implied 'I mean.' — Abū al-Baqāʾ offers the omitted أعني construction, also listed by al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī; Abū Ḥayyān prefers independence. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]

## 7:92/q08 Why repeat 'those who denied Shuʿayb' instead of using a pronoun?  [ٱلَّذِينَ كَذَّبُوا۟ شُعَيْبًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: Al-Ālūsī questions whether every rhetorical effect attributed to the single verse follows without taking both resumptions together.
- 7:92/q08/p1 The repetition intensifies blame, humiliation, the magnitude of their denial, loss, and deserved punishment. — Ibn al-Anbārī compares repeated Arab accusations; al-Rāzī says Arabs repeat for emphasis. Al-Kashshāf says the fresh opening rebuts and mocks the chiefs' advice and magnifies their fate; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī transmit this. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Bayḍāwī note the independent nominal sentences; al-Biqāʿī stresses the shame of losing bodies and souls. Al-Ṭībī says two resumptions answer two questions, first what happened after the quake and then whether ruin reached others; his second clause is emphatic and exclusive. Al-Ālūsī reports a customary Arab unconnected rebuke with the example of a brother who plundered property and violated protection. Al-Ālūsī objects that all of al-Kashshāf's effects are hard to infer from this verse alone, saying the two resumptions together suggest them more clearly. [16 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي,الكشاف,غير واحد); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 7:92/q08/p2 The repetition also warns the Arab polytheist audience through the earlier deniers' fate. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the repeated identification enumerates and awakens them, with the warning made explicit in 47:10. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:92/q09 Who are 'the losers'?  [هُمُ ٱلْخَٰسِرِينَ · referent]
- 7:92/q09/p1 Shuʿayb's deniers themselves, while his believing followers escaped and gained. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Biqāʿī, al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı, Ibn ʿĀshūr, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ, Iṣlāḥī, and The Study Quran set the answer against the chiefs' claim in 7:90 that following Shuʿayb would bring loss. Al-Kashshāf, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ṭībī say the second opening restricts great loss to the deniers. Al-Bayḍāwī says the believers gained in both abodes; al-Ālūsī notes their rescue is explicit in 11:94. [26 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:92/q10 What loss do the deniers suffer?  [ٱلْخَٰسِرِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Al-Māwardī preserves distinct glosses of loss by unbelief and by destruction; several later sources include both worldly and religious loss.
- 7:92/q10/p1 Loss through unbelief, or loss of religion and the next life. — Al-Māwardī reports unbelief as one explanation. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī include loss in religion alongside worldly loss. The Study Quran calls them deluded about true religion. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:92/q10/p2 Loss through destruction and loss of worldly life. — Al-Māwardī attributes the destruction gloss to Ibn ʿAbbās. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī say they lost both worldly and religious life; al-Biqāʿī specifies bodies and souls. Al-Ṭībī says the definite generic 'losers' magnifies the loss. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي)]
- 7:92/q10/p3 Khusr is the loss of one's capital. — Al-Jishumī gives the lexical sense and notes the related verb for incurring loss. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 7:92/q11 What does 'hum' add to 'they were the losers'?  [هُمُ · grammar]
- 7:92/q11/p1 It is a separating pronoun emphasizing and restricting loss to the deniers. — Al-Ṭabrisī says the emphatic separating pronoun leaves the definite following word a predicate, despite the usual issue of modifying a pronoun. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it adds restriction against the chiefs' claim in 7:90. [2 notes: TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:92/q12 How does the assertion 'they were the losers' gain emphasis?  [كَانُوا۟ هُمُ ٱلْخَٰسِرِينَ · rhetoric]
- 7:92/q12/p1 Fronting the deniers strengthens the assertion if kāna is a full verb; if a copula, the nominal sentence's stability supplies the emphasis. — Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes the two grammatical analyses of kāna; al-Ṭībī also says the definite generic 'losers' magnifies their loss. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي)]

## 7:92/q13 How does the verse answer the chiefs' earlier threats and warning?  [* · links]
- 7:92/q13/p1 The deniers, who threatened to expel Shuʿayb and the believers, were themselves driven out permanently by destruction. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī connect this to 7:88; Ibn Kathīr locates the punishment in the homes from which they had planned expulsion. Al-Bursawī and Iṣlāḥī stress the inversion, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says the quake instantly ended their confident grip on land and possessions. [9 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 7:92/q13/p2 The chiefs warned that Shuʿayb's followers would lose; the verse assigns the loss to the chiefs and deniers. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Ṭībī, al-Ālūsī, Iṣlāḥī and The Study Quran refer to 7:90; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says the earlier warning prepares the reversal whether following meant allegiance to Shuʿayb or his religious message. Elmalılı records the original warning. Al-Bursawī compares the implied rescue of believers to 11:58; al-Ālūsī compares its explicit account in 11:94. [19 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:92/q14 How does the verse clarify the preceding punishment account?  [* · links]
- 7:92/q14/p1 It names the deniers as the victims left implicit in 7:91 and states their destruction and loss. — Abū ʿAlī al-Jurjānī says the clause supplies the referent of the concealed preceding pronouns, comparing 11:67–68 and 5:71. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ notes the sequence of implicit victims followed by their explicit identification. Al-Ṭībī says the first resumption answers what happened after the earthquake and the second whether destruction reached anyone beyond them. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الجرجاني); TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي)]

## 7:92/q15 What does the selective punishment show?  [* · theology]
- 7:92/q15/p1 Its cause is a freely acting creator, rather than stars or nature, which would have struck followers too. — Al-Rāzī argues from the fact that only the deniers were punished in the same town. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 7:92/q15/p2 Its cause knows particulars and distinguishes obedient from disobedient people. — Al-Rāzī argues that the selective destruction requires knowledge of each person's state. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 7:92/q15/p3 It is a great miracle establishing Shuʿayb's truth. — For al-Rāzī, the survival of some alongside the punishment of others in the town proves the messenger's claim. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 7:92/q16 What wider lesson is drawn from the deniers' fate?  [* · interpretation]
- 7:92/q16/p1 Deniers may prevail briefly, but their traces vanish while truth's followers ultimately prevail. — Al-Bursawī states this lesson and reports Masnavi verses recalling the deniers publicly and urging alliance with the victorious side. [2 notes: BURSEVI]
- 7:92/q16/p2 Once Shuʿayb had exhausted efforts to rescue them from error, there was no reason to pity their fate. — Kur'an Yolu ties this judgment to his work to avert their catastrophe. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 7:92/q16/p3 The same law of response to a messenger applies to nations: accept reform or face destruction. — Iṣlāḥī says the nations were granted political authority, became ungrateful and unjust, rejected messengers and were destroyed after respite; the accounts warn Quraysh of the same judgment. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 7:92/q16/p4 These prophetic accounts teach the moral of calls, responses, and disasters; subsequent hardship aims at reform. — Kur'an Yolu describes the five preceding accounts as concise lessons rather than history for its own sake and says the ensuing calamities and hardships seek reform. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 7:92/q17 How does this verse fit the sūra's series of messenger accounts and warning?  [* · links]
- 7:92/q17/p1 It closes the messenger accounts of 7:59–92, which warn Quraysh with the fates of nations familiar across Arabia. — Iṣlāḥī says the promised messenger mission continues through these accounts; section VI uses Arabian histories and respite before destruction to warn Quraysh. Arabs knew vague traditions but lacked their moral, which the Qurʾān restores. The sequence spans nations known to the north, south and northwest and follows historical order beginning with Noah. The Study Quran says the Midian account matches the Thamūd account at 7:78. [6 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
