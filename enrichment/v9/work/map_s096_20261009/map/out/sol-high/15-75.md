# 15:75: 14 questions

## 15:75/q01 What does ‘that’ point to?  [ذَٰلِكَ · referent]
- 15:75/q01/p1 The account of Abraham’s angelic guests and Lot’s people, especially their destruction and the rescue of the faithful. — Alusi says the demonstrative points to the story just recounted. Ibn Ashur traces it from 15:51 through Abraham’s honored guests and promised son, their warning, Lot’s rescue, the wife’s fate, and punishment after continued defiance. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 15:75/q02 What are the signs in the account?  [ءَايَٰتٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether the signs encompass the narrated events or emphasize the still-visible physical traces.
- 15:75/q02/p1 The events of Lot’s story, including the destruction of the wrongdoers and rescue of believing household members. — Jishumi names both destruction and rescue. Bursevi includes the attempted assault on the guests, overturning of the city, and stones striking those present and absent. Ibn Ashur calls the narrative evidence about guidance and denial; Alusi says the story points to truth. [4 notes: JISHUMI; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 15:75/q02/p2 The visible, enduring remains of Lot’s punishment that observers can inspect. — Jishumi reports that the remains and the known, enduring signs are evident to those who have seen them or heard the reports; he says their survival allows learning and provides divine kindness. Ibn Kathir names visible traces. Biqai describes submerged land, foul unusable water, absence of animals, a dreadful appearance, and the contrast with neighboring fresh water and fertile terrain. Bursevi takes the remaining traces as the singular sign in 15:77. [7 notes: JISHUMI(الأصم، وأبو علي,الحسن، وقتادة، والضحاك، ومجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 15:75/q03 What does āyāt mean here?  [ءَايَٰتٍۢ · meaning]
- 15:75/q03/p1 Signs or markers. — Ibn Atiyya compares the ordinary expression ‘a sign between me and you.’ [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 15:75/q04 Why are the signs plural here but singular for believers in 15:77?  [ءَايَٰتٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 15:75/q04/p1 The plural indicates several proofs or aspects of the event, while the singular gathers the proof into one sign. — Burhan relates the plural to multiple proofs and the singular to the oneness of what they establish; it also relates the singular to believers’ quality of divine oneness. Bursevi says the plural covers the whole narrated story whereas 15:77 singles out its remaining visible traces. [3 notes: BURHAN; BURSEVI]

## 15:75/q05 How does ‘for the discerning’ attach grammatically?  [لِّلْمُتَوَسِّمِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the prepositional phrase modifies āyāt directly, an implied noun, or the clause’s predicate.
- 15:75/q05/p1 It attaches directly to ‘signs,’ identifying their recipients. — Samin prefers the direct attachment because āyāt already means signs. Alusi lists qualification of ‘signs’ as a possibility. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 15:75/q05/p2 It qualifies an omitted noun understood with ‘signs.’ — Samin allows an omitted noun as an adjectival construction, though he prefers direct attachment. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 15:75/q05/p3 It attaches to the predicate of the clause. — Alusi lists this as the other possible attachment. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 15:75/q06 Who are al-mutawassimīn, and what does the word mean?  [لِّلْمُتَوَسِّمِينَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word emphasizes close perception of marks, simple observation, reflection, the moral lesson drawn, or the observer’s righteousness; several sources regard these glosses as close.
- 15:75/q06/p1 Perceptive people who read outward marks closely and discern a person’s or thing’s underlying reality. — Mujahid, Ibn Abbas in Ata’s transmission, Jafar, Abu Ubayda, and others give insight or clear perception. Zajjaj, Abu Ishaq, Wahidi Wajiz, Kashshaf, Razi, Ibn Jawzi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Bursevi, Asad, and Study Quran describe sustained scrutiny until a true mark or inward quality is known. Tabari, Wahidi, Ibn Qutayba, Qurtubi, Samin, and Alusi use the idiom of discerning good in a person; Ibn Rawaha’s verse about the Prophet illustrates it. Jishumi, Qurtubi, Samin, and Alusi derive it from wasm, a distinguishing mark; Jishumi adds wasmi, the first rain marking the land with vegetation, and Samin notes ittisam and seeking spring pasture. Thaʿlab’s observer examines a person from head to foot. Zuhayr, the poems on recognizing lineage and visitors, the Ukaz observer, and Ibn Atiyya’s cited poetry support usage. Jishumi also cites a spear-marked person and explains dom as a tree. Ibn Kathir says sight and insight work together. Biqai says the trace provokes reflection. Qurtubi says related glosses are close; Alusi says the same. [62 notes: TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ(قيل); SULAMI; THALABI(شاعر آخر,شاعر مجهول,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو عبيدة,زهير بن أبي سلمى,عبدالله بن رواحة,مجاهد); QUSHAYRI(في التفسير); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو عبيدة,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الشاعر); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,الزجاج,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,غيره,مجاهد وابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة,الشعراء,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,ثعلب); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيدة,الشاعر); SAMIN-DURR(آخر,ابن رواحة,ثعلب,قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(النبي,جعفر بن محمد,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ثعلب,جعفر بن محمد,عبد الله بن رواحة); ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari and Razi); STUDYQURAN(Z)]
- 15:75/q06/p2 People who take warning or learn a lesson from what they see. — Qatada repeatedly gives this gloss; Tabari’s Ibn Zayd couples reflection with learning. Ibn Atiyya says the mark of destruction leads an observer to infer sin and avoid the same fate. Islahi defines close observation that makes the event an example. Alusi attributes this gloss to Mujahid, whereas most other sources attribute the insight gloss to him. [17 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 15:75/q06/p3 Observers or onlookers. — Ibn Abbas and al-Dahhak recur in this gloss; Abu Ubayda and Zuhayr’s poetic observer show how ordinary looking can imply discerning sight. Samin reports Thaʿlab’s fuller picture of inspection from head to foot. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); THALABI(ابن عباس والضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس، والضحاك); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 15:75/q06/p4 People who reflect or contemplate. — Muqatil, Ibn Zayd, al-Farra, and others give this gloss. Baydawi and Bursevi combine reflection with examining visible marks. Study Quran says deep reflection discovers reality behind outward forms. Razi includes contemplation among the exegetes’ explanations. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ(قيل); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل وابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد والفراء); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل، ابن زيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل وابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مالك عن بعض أهل المدينة); BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Z)]
- 15:75/q06/p5 People of righteousness and goodness. — Qurtubi specifies that this report from Ibn Abbas passes through Nahshal; Abu Hayyan likewise names Nahshal as the transmitter. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(نهشل عن ابن عباس)]

## 15:75/q07 How is exceptional discernment said to perceive hidden realities?  [لِّلْمُتَوَسِّمِينَ · inward]
turns on: Whether hidden knowledge is bestowed as illumination or inferred from observable signs, and how a genuine impression is recognized.
- 15:75/q07/p1 God grants light or unveiling to a purified heart, enabling true perception of otherwise hidden realities. — Tustari speaks of inward realities. Wasiti says sincerity in the expression of inward secrets becomes recognizable at its appointed time. Sulami’s transmitters describe unveiling above sense and intellect, a spirit purified by divine holiness, a heart refined by guidance, and truthful vision through the Lord; another says signs of lordship appear in gnostics’ secrets and speech. Muhammad ibn Khafif distinguishes states of messengers, disclosed inward rulings of saints, and inspired knowledge of distant events, illustrated by the Prophet, Abu Bakr, and Umar; he and Sulami’s other report contrast illumined people with those ruled by their souls. Qushayri says God reveals hidden matters to friends and describes firasah as inward perception that draws what is perceived from the heart without contrary proof; he likens mastery of the hidden to a lion’s prey. Qurtubi reports the need to clear the heart of worldly distractions, sins, corrupt character, and excess, and says Sufis call this a charism. Bursevi’s inspired-people report places correct intuition among the high ranks of saints. [15 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(أبو عثمان,الحسين,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,الواسطي,بعضهم,محمد بن خفيف); QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI-FULL(الصوفية); BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام)]
- 15:75/q07/p2 It is inferred from outward signs, including changes in character, grief, and subtle visible marks. — Sulami reports advice to watch when character changes and grief appears. Qurtubi records an explanation by deduction from signs, whether obvious or subtle; this distinguishes it from the Sufi charism account. The lexical explanations of tawassum repeatedly describe inferring an inner quality from its outward mark. [4 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL(الصوفية); NASAFI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari and Razi)]
- 15:75/q07/p3 True discernment is the first thought that faces no equal counter-thought. — Abu Hafs al-Haddad says an opposed first thought is ordinary thought or self-talk. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو حفص الحداد)]

## 15:75/q08 What degrees or kinds of discernment are described?  [لِّلْمُتَوَسِّمِينَ · inward]
turns on: The two transmitted threefold schemes classify discernment by different criteria: faculties and unveiling, or the recipient and kind of hidden matter.
- 15:75/q08/p1 It has three ascending degrees: sense perception, intellect, and granted unveiling of present or absent things. — A transmitter in Sulami places sight and hearing first, intellect second, and extraordinary unveiling above them. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 15:75/q08/p2 It comprises prophetic hidden states, saints’ disclosed inward rulings, and inspired awareness of distant unseen events. — Sulami’s report from Muhammad ibn Khafif illustrates these categories with the Prophet, Abu Bakr, and Umar and uses the believer’s-insight warning to direct people ruled by their souls toward the illumined. [1 notes: SULAMI(محمد بن خفيف)]

## 15:75/q09 What limits or cautions apply to spiritual discernment?  [لِّلْمُتَوَسِّمِينَ · inward]
turns on: The reports address different limits: the reliability and availability of insight, and proper conduct toward others.
- 15:75/q09/p1 Its insight is not available in every matter or at every moment, even to prophets. — Qushayri cites the Prophet’s conditional request that Aisha repent if she had committed the alleged act and Abraham’s and Lot’s failure to recognize their visitors as messengers. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 15:75/q09/p2 One should not claim discernment for oneself or exercise it upon believers, but should heed another’s discernment about oneself. — Abu Hafs gives this practical restraint alongside the warning to beware a believer’s insight. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو حفص)]

## 15:75/q10 What prophetic reports connect discernment with this verse?  [لِّلْمُتَوَسِّمِينَ · reports]
turns on: The transmitted sayings differ in wording and subject: the believer’s sight, speech, God’s discerning servants, or inspired predecessors.
- 15:75/q10/p1 Beware the believer’s insight: the believer sees by God’s light; versions connect this saying with recitation or explanation of the verse. — Abu Saʿid’s version is transmitted by Tustari, Tabari, Thalabi, Mawardi, Ibn Jawzi, Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, Durr, and Alusi; Tabari and Ibn Kathir also give Ibn Umar’s version. Sulami transmits the warning and Qurtubi cites the gloss ‘perceptive.’ Qurtubi calls al-Tirmidhi’s judgment unusual; Ibn Kathir reports that al-Tirmidhi knew the Abu Saʿid report only by that route; Sulami gives no grading. Alusi says al-Suyuti treats the verse as a foundation for physiognomy on this report. [15 notes: TUSTARI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); SULAMI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI(النبي ﷺ); MAWARDI-FULL(النبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي); DURR-FULL(النبي); ALUSI-FULL(السيوطي)]
- 15:75/q10/p2 The believer sees by God’s light and speaks by God’s enabling. — Tabari attributes this fuller wording to Thawban; Ibn Kathir repeats it, and Durr transmits the fuller version. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي); DURR-FULL(النبي)]
- 15:75/q10/p3 God has servants who recognize people by discernment or physiognomy. — Tabari, Ibn Kathir, and Durr transmit this from Anas. Ibn Kathir also gives al-Bazzar’s route and says Abu Bishr Ibn al-Muzallaq is reliable. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس عن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,النبي); DURR-FULL(أنس)]
- 15:75/q10/p4 Earlier nations had inspired people who spoke correctly by intuition, with Umar foremost in this community. — Bursevi explains the report’s conditional wording as emphasizing Umar’s distinction rather than doubt, and describes inspiration as an impression from the higher realm and a high rank among saints. [1 notes: BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام)]

## 15:75/q11 What concrete instances of discernment are reported?  [لِّلْمُتَوَسِّمِينَ · reports]
turns on: The accounts concern different people and predicted or perceived facts.
- 15:75/q11/p1 Umar called to Sariya, ‘The mountain, the mountain,’ as an instance of perceiving a distant need. — Tustari gives this example of discerning inward realities. [1 notes: TUSTARI(عمر)]
- 15:75/q11/p2 Jundub foresaw that the reputation-seeking reciter Mirdas would become a Kharijite leader, and he did. — Qurtubi gives the subsequent outcome as confirmation. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(جندب بن عبد الله البجلي)]
- 15:75/q11/p3 Hasan praised Amr ibn Ubayd conditionally, and Amr’s later Qadarite views estranged many companions. — Qurtubi preserves Hasan’s caveat ‘unless he changed’ and the later turn. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن البصري)]
- 15:75/q11/p4 Hasan praised Ayyub as Basra’s best young man without a qualification. — Qurtubi contrasts the unqualified praise with Hasan’s conditional words about Amr ibn Ubayd. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن البصري)]
- 15:75/q11/p5 Al-Shabi predicted that Dawud al-Azdi would die after head cauterization, and he did. — Qurtubi states that the prediction came true. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الشعبي)]
- 15:75/q11/p6 Umar foresaw a grave day for Muslims after observing Malik al-Ashtar, who later figured in civil strife. — Qurtubi names Malik’s later role as fulfillment. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب)]
- 15:75/q11/p7 Uthman saw in Anas’s eye that he had looked at a woman in the market. — Qurtubi reports Uthman calling this insight, proof, and truthfulness rather than revelation. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عثمان بن عفان)]
- 15:75/q11/p8 Ibn Abbas could discern whether a questioner was a jurist. — Qurtubi reports Ibn Abbas’s own claim. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 15:75/q12 May intuitive discernment establish legal judgments?  [لِّلْمُتَوَسِّمِينَ · law]
turns on: Whether insight counts as admissible legal proof, despite its possible ability to reveal meanings.
- 15:75/q12/p1 No; legal judgment against a person requires evidence recognized by sacred law, not intuitive perception. — Ibn al-Arabi says even real intuition cannot establish rulings; al-Qurtubi endorses the objection because it is not a categorical recognized means of judgment. Ibn al-Arabi says his teacher al-Shashi rebutted a Baghdad chief judge who followed Iyas ibn Muawiya in judging by intuition. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)+]
- 15:75/q12/p2 Some judges applied physiognomy or intuition in legal cases after Iyas ibn Muawiya’s example. — Ibn al-Arabi describes the Baghdad chief judge and his teacher’s rebuttal; Alusi reports the practice among some Malikis. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); ALUSI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)-]

## 15:75/q13 What lesson should observers draw from the signs?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes stress different lessons of the same event: a warning to current deniers, the contrast between the faithful and the guilty, or the duty and benefit of reflection.
- 15:75/q13/p1 God’s destruction of Lot’s people warns later wrongdoers, especially Quraysh, that denial can bring the same punishment. — Muqatil calls it a warning of like punishment. Tabari applies the lesson to Quraysh’s denial of their messenger and persistence in error. Hasan says the God who destroyed Lot’s people can destroy present unbelievers. Islahi says Quraysh, asking to see angels, should prepare for the fate shown by this historical evidence. KuranYolu says surviving ruins teach humanity, especially Quraysh. Bursevi says believers understand punishment followed wrongdoing, whereas others explain the ruins by chance or astrological configurations. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 15:75/q13/p2 The account demonstrates the destruction of the guilty and salvation of believing household members. — Jishumi identifies both fates. Ibn Ashur says the concluding verse shows guidance and its opposite, and the severe punishment of those who deny messengers. [2 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 15:75/q13/p3 The signs oblige reflective inquiry, which draws people toward truth and restrains falsehood. — Jishumi says the enduring remains provide divine kindness and make reflection obligatory. Biqai says the marks rebuke and incite observers to investigate. [3 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 15:75/q14 How do the remains connect Lot’s account to Quraysh and travelers?  [* · links]
- 15:75/q14/p1 Quraysh and other travelers passed the ruined settlement on the Hijaz–Syria route, so its traces made the warning accessible to them. — Islahi locates Sodom and Gomorrah on the route and says Quraysh passed the settlement. KuranYolu says Meccan traders going to Syria passed Sodom’s remains in Palestine; it reports Esed’s claim that aerial photographs confirmed an old northern-Hijaz route along the Dead Sea’s eastern shore, with Sodom and Gomorrah northeast of it. Biqai says the striking contrast with surrounding fertile land invites travelers to visit and ask what happened. [6 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Muhammed Esed); BIQAI-FULL]
