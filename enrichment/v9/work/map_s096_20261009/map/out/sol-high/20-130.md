# 20:130: 44 questions

## 20:130/q01 What speech must the Prophet endure, and why?  [فَٱصْبِرْ عَلَىٰ مَا يَقُولُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the command is read primarily against the opponents' speech and its delayed punishment or as inward formation under divine decree.
- 20:130/q01/p1 He must endure hostile denials, insults, and demands for punishment until God's appointed judgment, while continuing his mission. — Muqatil specifies denial of threatened punishment; Yahya, Tabari, Mawardi, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Razi, Bursevi and Ibn Ashur list accusations including sorcery, madness, poetry, lying and soothsaying. Tabari, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Bursevi, Alusi and Ibn Ashur relate patience to the fixed time of punishment; Razi adds continued preaching, and Islahi relates it to pressure from those demanding punishment. [18 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 20:130/q01/p2 He should patiently accept God's decree, recognizing that opponents act under divine power and that their objections train him spiritually. — Qashani presents patience through God and divine decree; Bursevi transmits the Najmi inward interpretation. Tabatabai joins acceptance of delayed judgment to rejecting shirk and praising God. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); TABATABAI]

## 20:130/q02 What does patience mean here as a virtue?  [فَٱصْبِرْ · interpretation]
- 20:130/q02/p1 It is self-restraint appropriate to reason and divine law, expressed differently in battle, calamity, and silence; enduring abuse can be good or obligatory. — Bursevi quotes Raghib's definition and his examples of courage, broadness of chest and keeping silent, with 2:177, 22:35 and 33:35; Jishumi says endurance of abuse is praiseworthy and sometimes obligatory. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الراغب); JISHUMI]

## 20:130/q03 Was the command to endure their speech abrogated by fighting?  [فَٱصْبِرْ عَلَىٰ مَا يَقُولُونَ · sciences]
turns on: Whether patient endurance of speech is incompatible with the later command to fight.
- 20:130/q03/p1 Yes; the later fighting command ended the earlier permission or duty simply to endure abuse. — Wahidi says God subsequently decreed their killing; Ibn Jawzi and the reported Kalbi-Muqatil, Baghawi and Bursevi accounts cite the fighting or sword verse. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم); JISHUMI-; BURSEVI(الكبير)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:130/q03/p2 No; combat and patience under verbal abuse can coexist, so the command remains applicable. — Jishumi argues that the passages do not conflict; Qurtubi reports that fighting did not eradicate all unbelievers; al-Kabir says fighting need not end endurance of speech; Alusi reads patience as composure, not cessation of fighting. [6 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكبير); ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI+]

## 20:130/q04 What does the command to glorify God require here?  [وَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether tasbih names ritual prayer, direct remembrance and transcendence, or an inward spiritual process; the time phrases and context are used to decide.
- 20:130/q04/p1 It means prayer, including praise and glorification, at the specified times. — Tabari explains prayer while praising God; Bursevi calls tasbih a part naming the whole; Ibn Ashur says prayer includes glorification. Most commentators in Razi and the early authorities map the phrases to prayers. Razi prefers the complete five-prayer construal among prayer interpretations; Tabatabai notes that this is the majority's identification, though the word alone does not compel it. Muqatil links the command to hoped-for afterlife reward. [24 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس,الأكثرون); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قول في التفسير); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(tefsirler); RAZI-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 20:130/q04/p2 It means declaring God's transcendence and majesty through remembrance, in speech, thought or action, rather than specifying ritual prayer. — Abu Muslim and Razi relate this reading to countering the opponents' denial and shirk; Razi considers it closer to context. Ibn Atiyya allows the formula subhan Allah wa bihamdihi after dawn and before sunset and cites seventy glorifications; Tabarsi allows continuous praise; Abu Hayyan distinguishes a spoken formula from semantic exaltation; al-Izz calls the ba instrumental and says negating deficiency affirms perfection. Alusi notes both an objection from specified times and a reply taking them as signs of continuity; Tabatabai says the night part complicates that reply. Kur'an Yolu cites the prayer chronology. Al-Qushayri takes the instruction as sustaining remembrance of God in every circumstance. [21 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو مسلم,ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,أبو مسلم,الإمام,العز بن عبد السلام); TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي)+; QUSHAYRI+; RAZI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:130/q04/p3 It signifies inward stripping of selfhood and manifestation of the Lord's qualities, with spiritual illumination across the named times. — Qashani reads the rising and setting as disclosure and concealment of spirit and night as the veiled ego; the Study Quran transmits Aj's path of patience and remembrance until selfhood vanishes and the Beloved is witnessed. [4 notes: QASHANI; STUDYQURAN(Aj)]

## 20:130/q05 How does prayer or remembrance help the Prophet endure?  [فَٱصْبِرْ وَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ · interpretation]
- 20:130/q05/p1 It brings solace, perseverance and divine aid amid abuse and hardship. — Razi compares 2:45 and says remembrance gives rest; Biqai says worship raises the soul; Bursevi cites 13:28 and the Prophet's recourse to prayer in distress; Alusi compares 2:45 and 2:153. Islahi extends the lesson to community hardship and says prayer teaches that events follow God's will for good, while the Prophet's role is to teach as well as recite. Ibn Ashur directs attention from worldly comforts to spiritual purification and the God-fearing outcome. Al-Qushayri explicitly traces distress to hearing hurtful speech and says remembrance and glorification relieve it. [16 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QUSHAYRI+]

## 20:130/q06 How is bi-ḥamdi rabbika construed?  [بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ · grammar]
turns on: The force of ba and whether praise is annexed to its agent or object.
- 20:130/q06/p1 It is circumstantial accompaniment: glorify or pray while praising God, especially for His help, guidance and blessings. — Tabari makes the praise God's object by comparison with an Arabic verbal noun construction; Kashshaf, Razi and Nasafi specify praise for enabling prayer; Baydawi adds guidance and blessings. Alusi takes the ba as accompaniment and praise as an object-directed verbal noun; Ibn Ashur calls it a circumstantial phrase with an agent relation; Samin likewise calls it a hal. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:130/q06/p2 The ba is instrumental, and divine praise may be understood as God's own praise of Himself. — Al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam permits agent, object and specifying annexation; Alusi prefers agent annexation because perfectly true praise is God's praise of Himself. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(العز بن عبد السلام); ALUSI-FULL+]

## 20:130/q07 Which worship is indicated before sunrise?  [قَبْلَ طُلُوعِ ٱلشَّمْسِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the temporal instruction specifies obligatory Fajr or includes direct and optional morning remembrance.
- 20:130/q07/p1 The dawn or Fajr prayer. — This is the repeated mapping from Muqatil onward. Ibn Ashur says the prayer times are clarified by mass-transmitted Sunna; the Jarir and two-asr reports identify the morning prayer; Biqai includes it at the first endpoint. Al-Tabataba'i transmits an ungraded prophetic report that identifies the pre-sunrise prayer as dawn prayer. [29 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(أكثر المتأولين); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قول في التفسير); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(النبي,قتادة); STUDYQURAN(JJ, Q); BIQAI-FULL+]
- 20:130/q07/p2 The time also accommodates direct glorification and remembrance after dawn, extending toward the forenoon prayer. — Ibn Atiyya cites the formula through the two Duha rakas; Abu Hayyan reports it. Bursevi transmits a report valuing remembrance until sunrise above freeing eighty descendants of Ishmael. Islahi includes ishraq. Al-Qushayri locates the pre-sunrise devotion at the day's beginning and says it blesses and brightens the morning. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); BURSEVI(الخبر); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QUSHAYRI+]

## 20:130/q08 Which worship is indicated before sunset?  [وَقَبْلَ غُرُوبِهَا · interpretation]
turns on: How broadly before sunset is taken, and whether tasbih is prayer or direct remembrance.
- 20:130/q08/p1 The afternoon or Asr prayer. — A widespread mapping supported by the Jarir report, the two-asr report transmitted by Durr and Alusi, and ordinary pre-sunset usage as Alusi observes. Alusi's note calling the sunset prayer here differs from his later alternative. Al-Tabataba'i transmits an ungraded prophetic report that identifies the pre-sunset prayer as afternoon prayer. [25 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(أكثر المتأولين); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(الجلالين,الطبري); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(النبي,قتادة); STUDYQURAN(JJ, Q); BIQAI-FULL+]
- 20:130/q08/p2 Both noon or Dhuhr and afternoon or Asr prayers. — They both fall after solar noon and before sunset, according to Bursevi and Alusi; Nasafi calls both part of the latter half of day. Razi reports Ibn Abbas's complete five-prayer mapping; Ibn Ashur allows this alongside Asr alone. [12 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 20:130/q08/p3 The sunset prayer. — Alusi calls the pre-sunset phrase the sunset prayer in one note, while elsewhere he reports the Asr and Dhuhr-Asr readings. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:130/q08/p4 Direct glorification before sunset, including a repeated formula. — Ibn Atiyya reports seventy glorifications before sunset bring forgiveness; Abu Hayyan and Alusi transmit the sunset report. Al-Qushayri places the devotion at the day's waning and says it makes the night pleasant and evening restful. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,رسول الله); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية); QUSHAYRI+]

## 20:130/q09 What do ānāʾ al-layl mean lexically?  [ءَانَآئِ ٱلَّيْلِ · meaning]
- 20:130/q09/p1 The hours, periods or watches of night; several singular forms are transmitted. — Majaz cites the Hudhali poet al-Mutanakhkhal or Abu Athila; Tabari cites a line attributed to al-Munkhil al-Sadi; Wahidi cites al-Asha; Jishumi and Ibn Atiyya cite a Hudhali line. Qurtubi, Alusi and Ibn Ashur list variant singulars including ini, inu, ina and ana; Tabatabai derives the plural from ina or inw; Alusi cites Raghib's parallel at 33:53 and al-Hutay'a on delaying supper. Al-Nahhas says the Basrans accept inni as the singular and al-Farra reports ini and ina; al-Tabrisi gives ina as singular and cites al-Sa'idi's poetic use of it for each hour. Al-Qushayri explicitly glosses the expression as night hours. [19 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأعشى); JISHUMI(الهذلي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الهذلي); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الحطيئة,الراغب); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; NAHHAS; QUSHAYRI; TABRISI(السعيدي)]
- 20:130/q09/p2 The Basran singular is inni; other paired forms attributed to al-Farra are rejected by leading linguists as unreliable usage. — Al-Nahhas says al-Asma'i, Ibn al-Sikkit and accomplished linguists reject those other paired forms, and cautions that most Kufan reports came from speakers judged ineloquent despite the transmitters' trustworthiness. [1 notes: NAHHAS+]

## 20:130/q10 Which night worship do the night hours designate?  [وَمِنْ ءَانَآئِ ٱلَّيْلِ فَسَبِّحْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase specifies one or two fixed evening prayers, spans all or part of night, or adds voluntary worship.
- 20:130/q10/p1 The sunset and nightfall prayers, Maghrib and Isha. — Qatada and Ibn Abbas transmissions place both in early night; Baydawi and Bursevi add that quiet and inclination to rest make this worship demanding. Alusi sees the renewed command and fronted hours as emphasis. [24 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس وقتادة); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL+]
- 20:130/q10/p2 The Isha prayer specifically. — Tabari first glosses the evening prayer after a portion of night and reports Ibn Zayd; Baghawi, Ibn Jawzi, Qurtubi, Nasafi and Study Quran give Isha alone; Kashshaf and Abu Hayyan use nightfall prayer. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد وابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(أكثر المتأولين); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 20:130/q10/p3 Prayer or remembrance throughout the whole night. — Ibn Abbas is reported by Tabari, Mawardi and Tabrisi; al-Hasan spells out beginning, middle and end in Tabari and Ibn Jawzi. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن)]
- 20:130/q10/p4 The middle of the night. — Ibn Abbas is reported with this more restricted time by Tabari and Ibn Jawzi. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(العوفي عن ابن عباس)]
- 20:130/q10/p5 The beginning of the night. — Baghawi transmits Ibn Abbas's early-night interpretation; Tabrisi relates it to Maghrib and the later evening prayer. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس)]
- 20:130/q10/p6 Additional voluntary night prayer. — Razi's second prayer reading treats the night watches as voluntary worship besides five obligatory prayers; Ibn Atiyya includes night voluntary prayer and pre-dawn rakas. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:130/q11 Why are the night hours singled out?  [وَمِنْ ءَانَآئِ ٱلَّيْلِ فَسَبِّحْ · interpretation]
- 20:130/q11/p1 Night worship brings greater focus, seclusion and merit because rest is tempting and effort is greater. — Kashshaf and Razi invoke 73:6 and 39:9; Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Biqai and Bursevi repeat the quiet, collected-heart and difficulty grounds. Ibn Ashur says the hours are foregrounded because neglect through rest is likely; Alusi says special importance warrants a renewed command. Al-Qushayri says solitary remembrance in the night hours perfects spiritual purity. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; QUSHAYRI+]

## 20:130/q12 How is min ānāʾ al-layl fasabbiḥ parsed?  [وَمِنْ ءَانَآئِ ٱلَّيْلِ فَسَبِّحْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the fronted phrase is governed directly by the following imperative or by an implied verb, and how fa functions.
- 20:130/q12/p1 The fronted partitive or starting-time phrase attaches to the second fasabbiḥ, with fa marking emphasis or necessary sequence. — Samin attaches the phrase to the second imperative; Ibn Ashur calls min a starting point and fa a response-like emphasis, comparing 17:79 and the hadith about parents; Tabatabai calls min partitive and compares the fronting in 2:40. Alusi reports direct government and explanatory or additional fa, and al-Khafaji says no added fa need be posited. [8 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الخفاجي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:130/q12/p2 The night-time phrase is governed by an omitted command to rise, after which fasabbiḥ is coordinated. — Alusi reports this parsing and calls it strained. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:130/q12/p3 The phrase can be parsed through coordination with an earlier fronted command, or fa can be explanatory. — Alusi lists coordination with wa-iyyaya farhabun and alternative explanations of fa. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:130/q13 Which worship occurs at the day's edges?  [وَأَطْرَافَ ٱلنَّهَارِ · interpretation]
turns on: Where the day's ends are placed, whether noon can count as an edge, and whether the command fixes prayer times or covers broad intervals.
- 20:130/q13/p1 The noon or Dhuhr prayer at the boundary of the day's two halves. — Qatada, Ibn Abbas, Mujahid and others locate noon at the end of one half and start of the next; Wahidi cites 66:4 for plural wording. Alusi and Tabatabai object that noon is not naturally an edge of the whole day. [16 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس رواية عطاء، مجاهد، قتادة,الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(الجلالين); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 20:130/q13/p2 Both noon and sunset prayers, noon at the meeting of the halves and sunset as a further edge. — Tabari calls sunset a third edge; Qurtubi and Study Quran repeat the shared-noon-boundary account; Alusi reports a three-edge version; Ibn Ashur allows solar-arc endpoints. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أكثر المتأولين); BURSEVI(الطبري); ALUSI-FULL(العجاج); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q); ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 20:130/q13/p3 Dawn and sunset prayers, repeating periods already named for emphasis. — Ibn Zayd, Kashshaf, Nasafi and Ibn Jawzi map the two edges this way; Kashshaf compares the specially repeated middle prayer of 2:238. Alusi says this assumes day runs from sunrise to sunset; Ibn Ashur also cites 2:238. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عكرمة); BURSEVI(العيون); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); TABATABAI-]
- 20:130/q13/p4 Dawn, noon and afternoon prayers or their four day endpoints. — Farra joins dawn to noon and afternoon, comparing 11:114; Biqai prefers first and last endpoints as dawn and afternoon and middle points as preparation and prayer. Abu Hayyan reports three or four literal-edge counts; Islahi adds chasht and Maghrib in his fuller margin schedule. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قول ثالث,قول ثان); BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL+; FARRA+]
- 20:130/q13/p5 Daytime voluntary prayers or remembrance across the day's portions. — Al-Hasan reports voluntary prayer; Razi and Ibn Atiyya add optional prayer; Baydawi and Bursevi extend it across daylight. Alusi rejects a restricted last-hours version as unnatural; Islahi specifically includes chasht. Al-Tabataba'i reports Abu Ja'far's interpretation of the day ends as voluntary daytime prayer, transmitted by al-Kafi from Zarara; he says it assumes tasbih denotes prayer generally, or glorification generally. [11 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(عمرو); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:130/q13/p6 Morning and evening or the day's recurring hours and sides, without fixing a particular prayer. — Wahidi reports morning-evening and Ibn al-Arabi's hours; Asad relates the extremities to 11:114; Tabatabai prefers broad intervals before sunrise and sunset, whose multiple subparts count as edges relative to noon and avoid repetition. Al-Tabataba'i reports al-Qummi's morning-and-evening gloss of the night times and day ends and says it supports his own broad-interval explanation. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأعرابي,بعض المفسرين); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI(القمي)+]

## 20:130/q14 Why does the verse use the plural aṭrāf for the day?  [أَطْرَافَ ٱلنَّهَارِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the plural is idiomatic for two, reflects daily recurrence, or counts more actual parts.
- 20:130/q14/p1 Arabic may use a plural for the two ends, including a shared boundary of the day's two halves. — Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, Ibn Jawzi and Tabatabai compare the dual hearts in 66:4; Kashshaf invokes the clearer dual in 11:114 and a poetic two-backs example; Razi says the minimum plural can denote two; Samin and Alusi cite poetic parallel. Alusi says two ends are unambiguous for a day and balance plural night hours. [13 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس رواية عطاء، مجاهد، قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(العجاج); TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 20:130/q14/p2 The two ends recur every day, so the generic day has a plural set of ends. — Abu al-Abbas, Ibn Atiyya, Razi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Tabatabai report recurrence or day as a genus; Razi cites 11:114. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قول خامس); TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين)]
- 20:130/q14/p3 The plural is literal because multiple daytime portions or three or four edges are counted. — Abu Hayyan reports a three-edge scheme at dawn, noon-afternoon and evening, and a four-edge scheme around sunrise, noon and sunset; Tabari and Alusi count noon's two sides and sunset as a third. Ibn Ashur considers endpoints of the solar arc. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قول ثالث,قول ثان,قول رابع); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 20:130/q14/p4 Aṭrāf means portions or hours, making the plural straightforward. — Ibn al-Arabi calls them hours; Samin gives true plural hours; Abu Hayyan reports this, and Alusi cites lexica for taraf as a portion or group. Tabatabai locates several subparts in the broad early and late intervals. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأعرابي); SAMIN-DURR(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قول خامس); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

In man li illa abuka sadiqan, min belongs with the father. A cited poetic accusative may describe an omitted amr. [8 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل,الكلحبة الثعلبي,بعضهم,كعب بن مالك,يونس)]

## 20:130/q32 What rhetorical examples accompany the cited discussion?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The cited examples illustrate different rhetorical mechanisms.
- 20:130/q32/p1 Poetry can assign an invented cause to a real event or imaginatively transform a comparison. — Jurjani uses the cloud's rain as sweat, fragrance bestowed by the buried, and the yellow sunset attributed to fear of parting; he notes attributions to al-Shibli and Abu al-Ba's. Al-Suli's wind actually blows a cloak but receives jealousy as a fictional motive, while Ibn Wahb invents time's hostility and its loving cause. [6 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR(أبو العباس الضبي,الشبلي)]
- 20:130/q32/p2 A layered comparison can approach metaphor when restoring an explicit simile would disrupt syntax or contradict its details. — Jurjani illustrates this with separation's sunset, estrangement's lunar eclipse and examples in which a hero is placed beyond a lion or death; cloud and sea images share the paradox. [2 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 20:130/q32/p3 A sentence's full image may emerge only at its final word, while independent coordinated comparisons remain separate. — Jurjani uses al-Farazdaq's final hajaiya, al-Qatami's dhi al-ghulla, and contrastive fragrance-face-fingertip clauses; he introduces Bashshar and Imru al-Qays for extended compositional similes. ## 20:130/q40 When does the noon prayer start and end?  [أَطْرَافَ ٱلنَّهَارِ · law]
turns on: Whether the relevant shadow limit is one or two object lengths, and whether afternoon prayer begins immediately when noon prayer ends.
- 20:130/q40/p1 It begins at the sun's zenith and ends when an object's shadow equals its length beyond its noon shadow. — Jishumi gives the zenith as the start and reports this end point from most jurists. [2 notes: JISHUMI]
- 20:130/q40/p2 It begins at the sun's zenith and ends when the shadow reaches twice the object's length. — Jishumi gives the zenith as the start and reports this later end point from some jurists. [2 notes: JISHUMI]
- 20:130/q40/p3 It begins at the zenith and ends when the shadow equals the object, but afternoon prayer is delayed until the shadow is twice its length. — Jishumi reports this third division between noon and afternoon prayer times. [2 notes: JISHUMI]

## 20:130/q42 When does the sunset prayer time begin and end?  [ءَانَآئِ ٱلَّيْلِ · law]
turns on: Whether twilight means the redness or the whiteness.

- 20:130/q42/p2 It begins at sunset and ends when the white twilight disappears. — Jishumi gives sunset and twilight's disappearance as the bounds and reports Abu Hanifa's identification of twilight as whiteness. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أبو حنيفة)]

## 20:130/q43 When does the night prayer become due?  [ءَانَآئِ ٱلَّيْلِ · law]
