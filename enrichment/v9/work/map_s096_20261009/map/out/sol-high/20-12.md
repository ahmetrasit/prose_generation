# 20:12: 40 questions

## 20:12/q01 How is إني read and construed after the call?  [إِنِّىٓ · readings]
turns on: Whether the particle begins direct speech or is syntactically governed by the call; the final yāʾ also varies.
- 20:12/q01/p1 Kasra: direct quoted speech after Moses was called. — Al-Tabari prefers it because يا موسى intervenes; al-Farisi cites the ensuing direct quotations at 20:13–14. Basrans posit an omitted قول; Kufans treat نادى as speech. Abu Ubayd chooses the common reading. Nafi opens the final yāʾ while other kasra readers generally quiet it; the two Medinese readers, Ibn Kathir and Abu Amr open it in the five annexed yāʾs enumerated by Ibn al-Jazari. Ibn Mujahid gives the surrounding first-person-yāʾ patterns: Nāfiʿ opens those listed except ولي فيها and أخي اشدد; Abū ʿAmr quiets ولي فيها and حشرتني أعمى; Ibn Kathīr quiets ولي فيها, ويسر لي أمري, لذكري إن, على عيني إذ and برأسي إني; Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, Ibn ʿĀmir and ʿĀṣim through Abū Bakr quiet all those listed. [23 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض عامة قراء المدينة والكوفة); IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو,ابن كثير,ابن كثير، أبو عمرو، عاصم، نافع، ابن عامر، حمزة، الكسائي,حمزة، الكسائي، ابن عامر، عاصم عن أبي بكر,نافع); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(عاصم وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي ونافع); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي,الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جعفر وابن كثير وأبو عمرو); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الباقون); NASHR(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وأبو جعفر,المدنيان وابن كثير وأبو عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور); QIRAAT-ER(Y-iḍ); FARISI-HUJJA+; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد)+; TAB-FULL+]
- 20:12/q01/p2 Fatḥa: a subordinate statement giving the content or cause of the call. — Ibn Kathir and Abu Amr, also Abu Jafar in several lists, read annī; the yāʾ is open. Al-Zajjaj gives the clause accusative position; others supply bi with نودي, supported by a poetic ناديت باسم example. Ibn Atiyya allows causal 'because'; al-Samin finds it weak, and al-Alusi finds the intervening vocative awkward. Al-Alusi instead supplies أعلم. Qiraat-ER offers 'Know that I am your Lord'. Ibn al-Jazari finds no explicit report for inferring Abu Jafar's yāʾ vowel. Al-Farisi adds that neither quoted clause can serve as deputy subject of passive نودي; the earlier Moses can. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the omitted preposition yields 'he was called that I am your Lord' and that the emphasis remains in either reading. [29 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض قراء المدينة والبصرة); IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير، أبو عمرو، عاصم، نافع، ابن عامر، حمزة، الكسائي); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي,الزجاج,القراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جعفر وابن كثير وأبو عمرو); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو وابن كثير وابن محيصن وحميد); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية,ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); NASHR(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وأبو جعفر); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو علي,ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); QIRAAT-ER(ḥf); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو عمرو وابن كثير); ALUSI-FULL+]

## 20:12/q02 What grammatical role does أنا play?  [أَنَا۠ · grammar]
- 20:12/q02/p1 It can be a subject, an emphasis, or a separator. — Al-Samin says it may be a subject with ربك as predicate, an emphasis of the first-person pronoun, or a separator. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 20:12/q03 Why repeat the first-person pronoun in إني أنا ربك?  [إِنِّىٓ أَنَا۠ رَبُّكَ · rhetoric]
- 20:12/q03/p1 The repetition confirms the speaker's identity and removes Moses' doubt. — Al-Zamakhshari calls it strengthened proof and secure recognition; Ibn al-Jawzi compares 15:89. Al-Tabarsi, al-Razi, al-Baydawi, al-Alusi and Bursevi likewise stress certainty. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(قالوا); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 20:12/q04 Why say 'your Lord' here?  [رَبُّكَ · rhetoric]
- 20:12/q04/p1 It marks a direct, personal audience with Moses and fits this sura's focus on his worship and mission. — Tabatabai contrasts the later 'I am Allah,' which states the revealed message. Al-Samarrai contrasts al-Naml and al-Qasas, which say 'Lord of the worlds'; Ta Ha directly instructs Moses in worship and prayer, promises signs and recounts his life. [6 notes: TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 20:12/q04/p2 'Your Lord' reassures Moses before an unseen speaker, since a lord cares gently for the one under his care. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says this personal address calmed Moses' fear of the unseen voice. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:12/q05 How did Moses know who was speaking?  [إِنِّىٓ أَنَا۠ رَبُّكَ · theology]
turns on: Whether revelation itself supplies certainty or Moses needs an inferential sign.
- 20:12/q05/p1 God gave Moses immediate, necessary certainty in revelation. — Al-Alusi favors knowledge created in Moses without inference. Tabatabai says a prophet's initial revelation is directly certain; 42:51 frames divine communication, and rational proof would make prophecy an acquired inference. [3 notes: TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:12/q05/p2 He inferred divine identity from a miracle or distinctive signs. — The Mu'tazila require a miracle because necessary knowledge would remove moral responsibility; al-Alusi records prior or later miracle views and questions whether unique-sounding attributes alone withstand satanic suggestion. A report from Wahb says the omnidirectional voice and God's nearness assured Moses; other reports describe hearing from all six directions and through all his limbs, or an unchanged sound regardless of distance. Ibn ʿĀshūr says extraordinary created sounds with no visible human or angelic intermediary let Moses recognize God's intended address. [16 notes: SULAMI(جعفر); WAHIDI-BASIT(نوف); BAGHAWI-FULL(وهب); KASHSHAF-FULL(رُوي); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(المعتزلة,بعضهم,وهب); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 20:12/q06 How was God's speech heard by Moses?  [إِنِّىٓ أَنَا۠ رَبُّكَ · theology]
turns on: The nature of divine speech and whether an audible form is direct, created, or a representation of inner speech.
- 20:12/q06/p1 Moses heard God's own eternal verbal speech directly, without an intermediary. — A Sunni group in al-Alusi says successive letters need temporal sequence only with an organ such as a tongue; without one the speech can be heard at once. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(جماعة من أهل السنة)]
- 20:12/q06/p2 He heard created sound in the tree, while God's speech remains eternal. — Al-Alusi attributes this to Sunni scholars of Transoxiana. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أهل ما وراء النهر)]
- 20:12/q06/p3 The divine verbal speech was originated. — Groups al-Alusi calls innovators differ over whether events can subsist in God; those denying this locate the created sound in a body. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أهل البدعة)]
- 20:12/q06/p4 He spiritually received inner speech that was later represented as words. — Al-Ashari describes hearing beyond letters, sound and rational comprehension; another account compares familiar word forms produced in intellect and common sense to a dream. Al-Baydawi likewise says spiritual reception is then represented bodily without fixed organ or direction. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الأشعري,بعضهم); BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 20:12/q06/p5 Moses heard extraordinary created sounds that indicated God's intent and inner speech, which itself transcends letters, sounds and hearing. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says no human or angel was visible; the sounds signified God's inner speech subsisting in Him, and he cites 4:164. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:12/q07 Can this command coexist with eternal divine speech?  [فَٱخْلَعْ · theology]
turns on: Whether an eternal command can have an addressee who comes to exist later.
- 20:12/q07/p1 An eternal imperative to Moses before his existence is absurd. — The Mu'tazila compare it to addressing absent people in an empty house. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة)]
- 20:12/q07/p2 God's speech is eternal but was not an imperative or prohibition in eternity. — Al-Razi reports this first reply from his school. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا)]
- 20:12/q07/p3 The eternal speech is already an order, and Moses later becomes its addressee without a change in it. — Al-Razi's school compares eternal divine power yielding capacity for action later. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا)]

## 20:12/q08 How does ف connect the sandal command to the declaration?  [فَٱخْلَعْ · grammar]
- 20:12/q08/p1 The command follows as a consequence of the declaration of God's lordship. — Al-Alusi says the preceding self-identification motivates removing the sandals. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the sequence signals that the divine speech itself made the place sacred. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:12/q09 What do اخلع and نعل mean literally?  [فَٱخْلَعْ نَعْلَيْكَ · meaning]
- 20:12/q09/p1 The command is to take off the two coverings of Moses' feet. — Al-Wahidi gives khalaʿ as remove and naʿl as a foot covering; Abu Hayyan says it can be leather, iron, or wood. Al-Alusi notes naʿl is feminine, gives its diminutive, and cites al-Farra's poetry for an alternate vowel on its second radical. Ibn ʿĀshūr defines khalaʿ as separating an attached object and describes the sandals as thick leather coverings fastened by straps to shield feet from ground and stones. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:12/q10 What were Moses' sandals made of?  [نَعْلَيْكَ · reports]
turns on: Whether the footwear was impure donkey hide, pure cowhide, or divinely created.
- 20:12/q10/p1 They were made of hide from an unritually dead donkey, often said to be untanned. — Qatada, Ka'b, Ali, Ikrima and others transmit it; Ibn Masud attributes a report to the Prophet. Al-Alusi calls the transmitted report unusual. Some say donkey hide without specifying carrion. - 20:12/q10/p2 They were ritually pure or slaughtered cowhide. — Al-Hasan, Mujahid, Sa'id ibn Jubayr and others give this alternative; Kashifi explicitly prefers processed cowhide. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون,الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن وسعيد بن جبير وقتادة ومجاهد وابن جريج); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(الحسن ومجاهد وسعيد بن جبير وابن جريج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وسعيد بن جبير ومجاهد وقتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن ومجاهد وسعيد ابن جبير وابن جريج); BURSEVI(الكاشفي)+]
- 20:12/q10/p3 God created the sandals. — Abu Hayyan records this report along with Moses casting them beyond the valley. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 20:12/q11 Why was Moses told to remove the sandals?  [فَٱخْلَعْ نَعْلَيْكَ · interpretation]

- 20:12/q11/p1 Their dead-animal hide was impure and should not enter the sacred valley. — Al-Muqatil, Ka'b, Qatada and others identify carrion donkey hide. Al-Tabari objects that neither Quran nor authoritative report establishes this; the verse itself grounds the order in the valley's sanctity. Al-Razi notes tanning would purify hide under a Prophetic ruling, raising an abrogation issue if carrion was the reason. Abu Sufyan says Moses' feet also blessed the valley. Bursevi says the exchange of blessing is mutual. - 20:12/q11/p3 Bare feet express humility and reverence before God or the sacred place. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan prefer royal-court etiquette and say the sandals' substance does not matter; al-Zamakhshari and al-Nasafi see the Quran as honoring the place. Al-Razi cites the following valley clause; early Muslims' barefoot circumambulation, Sa'id's Kaaba analogy, Malik's reverence for Medina, Bishr al-Hafi's bare feet and Abu Hanifa's contrasting royal-carpet anecdote illustrate decorum. - 20:12/q11/p4 The sandals' protection against dirt and insects was no longer needed in the pure, safe valley. — Abu Muslim says God reassured Moses that the place was pure and he was safe. [2 notes: TABRISI(أبو مسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم)]

## 20:12/q13 What inward act do the two sandals represent?  [فَٱخْلَعْ نَعْلَيْكَ · inward]
turns on: The symbolic referents proposed for the paired sandals and how far a figurative reading may go.
- 20:12/q13/p1 Cast off attachment to both this world and the next to devote oneself wholly to God. — Al-Qushayri, al-Razi, Bursevi, al-Ghazzali and Sufi transmitters give this; Ibn Ajibah calls the two sandals realms of existence and the removal a way to witness the Existentiator. Al-Alusi calls the allegory remote. Study Quran notes the sandals' later Sufi poetic and literary use, including Ibn Qasi's Taking Off the Sandals. Al-Ālūsī says turning from both worlds leaves the heart wholly absorbed in knowing God, attending to nothing else. [11 notes: QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAjībah,My, Qu,al-Ghazzālī)]
- 20:12/q13/p2 Set aside family, possessions, livelihood, self and bodily nature. — Ibn Ata turns the heart away from the created world or lower self; dream interpretations associate sandals with wife and child. Bursevi reports wife and sheep and Sheikh Aftade's nature, self and livelihood; Biqai adds that Moses settles without turning back toward family. Al-Alusi includes family and wealth but calls this remote. [12 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,جعفر); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الأسرار المحمدية,الشيخ افتاده); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 20:12/q13/p3 Shed spiritual states, actions, and one's own attributes before God. — Al-Shibli speaks of shedding everything of oneself; Ibn Ata names separation and connection. Al-Qushayri adds nearness and distance, union and separation, annihilation and subsistence; Qashani describes effacing attributes and acts in the sacred realm. [8 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الشبلي,قيل); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI]
- 20:12/q13/p4 Leave behind the two proofs or premises once direct knowledge of God is reached. — Al-Razi and Bursevi give this analogy; Rumi's roof and polished-mirror verses and the king's letter illustrate why further proof is needless. Al-Shibli washing his books makes the same point. Al-Ālūsī reports the two premises of a proof as the sandals: direct knowledge in the valley makes inference dispensable. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(الشبلي,المثنوي,بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 20:12/q13/p5 The command is a broad metonymy for total devotion. — Al-Burhan describes a metonymic mode whose whole sentence conveys a gist beyond the surface. Al-Razi objects that this opens the door to esoteric readings that erase the actual sandal command. Al-Ālūsī reads removal as clearing the conscience of distractions before the subsequent inward acquisition. [2 notes: BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL; BURHAN(فخر الدين الرازي)-]
- 20:12/q13/p6 Taking off the sandals abandons flight; casting the staff abandons pursuit, leaving Moses wholly directed to God. — Al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī pair feet and sandals with fleeing, hands and staff with pursuing; surrendering both ends self-directed seeking and escape, even for Moses. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:12/q14 What does Ṭuwā signify in an inward reading of the valley?  [بِٱلْوَادِ ٱلْمُقَدَّسِ طُوًۭى · inward]
- 20:12/q14/p1 It is the purified spiritual realm or Divine Presence where worldly existence is folded away. — Al-Qushayri says the valley and divine precinct are free of defects; Qashani calls it the spirit-world with material stages folded beneath it. Ibn Ajibah calls it the inner sanctuary and Ocean of Divine Presence where Moses' soul is enwrapped. Sulami's wordplay says disobedience is folded from his heart. Al-Ālūsī calls the sacred valley the realm of God's holy majesty and the transcendence of His might. [5 notes: SULAMI(قيل); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAjībah); ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:12/q15 Does the sandal command govern prayer or mosque footwear?  [فَٱخْلَعْ نَعْلَيْكَ · law]
turns on: Whether Moses' occasion-specific command supplies a general rule for worship footwear.
- 20:12/q15/p1 It gives no general objection to praying or circumambulating in clean sandals. — Al-Razi says the valley's honor made the order specific; the Prophet prayed in sandals, removed them only after Gabriel reported soil, and questioned those who imitated him. Ibn Masud rebuked Abu Musa's imitation and recalled prayer in socks and sandals. Al-Qurtubi cites 7:31 on adornment to support clean sandals and al-Nakhai's wish that removed shoes go to the needy. Al-Alusi notes reports favoring footwear. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Moses' particular circumstance does not establish a general requirement to remove sandals for prayer. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود); ALUSI-FULL(الأصم); IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 20:12/q15/p2 Shoes should be removed in mosques for cleanliness, humility and decorum. — Islahi treats this as mandatory and rejects the claim that clean shoes are permitted; Study Quran compares removing shoes before entering the Kaaba or a mosque. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN(IK); ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Some people)-]
- 20:12/q15/p3 Clean shoes may be worn in a mosque. — Islahi reports this view and rejects it because even clean shoes breach mosque decorum. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Some people); ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]

## 20:12/q16 Where should removed sandals be placed during prayer?  [نَعْلَيْكَ · law]
turns on: Whether between the feet is acceptable and how to avoid bothering neighboring worshippers.
- 20:12/q16/p1 Between one's feet, without inconveniencing others. — Abu Hurayra reports a Prophetic instruction and repeats it to al-Maqburi. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 20:12/q16/p2 Ahead of a follower, while left placement is for an imam or one praying alone. — Al-Qurtubi says a follower should avoid harming the person on his left and places them ahead rather than between the feet. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 20:12/q16/p3 Between the feet is an innovation. — Jubayr ibn Mut'im says this. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(جبير بن مطعم)]

## 20:12/q17 How are impurities removed from sandals?  [نَعْلَيْكَ · law]
turns on: Which impurities rubbing can remove and when washing remains necessary.
- 20:12/q17/p1 Rubbing or scraping can purify leather sandals, including disputed animal impurities. — Al-Qurtubi prefers rubbing for leather socks and sandals, citing Abu Sa'id's report. Al-Awza'i and Abu Thawr allow earth-rubbing generally for the disputed animal filth; Malikis differ. Abu Hanifa allows scraping dried filth but requires washing wet filth, including urine. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة,الأوزاعي وأبو ثور); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 20:12/q17/p2 Water is required to remove the listed impurities. — Al-Shafi'i requires water for all; Malik, al-Shafi'i and most jurists agree that blood, human excrement and urine need washing. Abu Hanifa also requires washing when filth is wet. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة,الشافعي,مالك والشافعي وأكثر العلماء)]

## 20:12/q18 What does المقدس mean?  [ٱلْمُقَدَّسِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the adjective emphasizes purification or blessing; some sources combine them.
- 20:12/q18/p1 Purified and set apart from impurity or evil. — Qutrub and Ibn Atiyya give 'purified'; al-Qurtubi derives it from quds, purity, and relates holy land to God removing unbelievers and settling believers. Bursevi adds separation from evil; Biqai says the precinct is purified of what is unfit for a king's court. Ibn ʿĀshūr glosses muqaddas as purified and sanctified; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ calls it the purified site of God's manifestation, speech and commissioning of Moses. - 20:12/q18/p2 Blessed, with divine favor and abundance. — Yahya ibn Sallam, Ibn Abbas and Mujahid give this gloss; Tabarsi specifies ample provision and fertility. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس، مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد)]

## 20:12/q19 Why is this valley sacred?  [بِٱلْوَادِ ٱلْمُقَدَّسِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the ground's holiness follows a specific divine association or the land's purification alone.
- 20:12/q19/p1 God specially favored it through His call, revelation or prescribed worship there. — Al-Qurtubi says God can favor places, times and creatures; merely removing unbelievers cannot explain this valley's distinct holiness. Tabatabai says places and times have no intrinsic rank, gaining sanctity by noble event or worship. Islahi likewise says all earth is equal in itself and special association with God confers sacredness. Asad ties double holiness to the voice and Moses' prophetic appointment. Ibn ʿĀshūr says an honored event confers a place's holiness, here divine address; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ also associates its purification with God's manifestation, speech and Moses' commission. [10 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari); IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI]
- 20:12/q19/p2 It was purified by removal of unbelievers and settlement of believers. — Al-Qurtubi gives this etymological account of the holy land, then says it alone cannot explain this particular valley's sanctity. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 20:12/q20 Where is Ṭuwā and what is the valley like?  [بِٱلْوَادِ طُوًۭى · referent]
turns on: The differing geographical identifications and physical descriptions.
- 20:12/q20/p1 It is a deep, rounded valley. — Al-Dahhak likens it to a well; al-Tha'labi records its circular depth. Ibn ʿĀshūr describes a narrow valley like folded cloth or a deep one like a lined well; he notes a different valley outside Mecca named Dhū Ṭuwā, where arriving pilgrims are advised to bathe. - 20:12/q20/p3 It is at Aylah. — Mubashshir ibn Ubayd calls it a valley there, blessed twice and read without nunation. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(مبشر بن عبيد); ITQAN(مبشر بن عبيد)]
- 20:12/q20/p4 It is at the base of Sinai. — Islahi describes it as a broad plain at Sinai's base. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:12/q20/p5 It is in Syria. — Al-Jawhari identifies Ṭuwā as a Syrian place name. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الجوهري)]
- 20:12/q20/p6 It is by the mountain where Moses stood. — Ibn Zayd says Moses received what God gave him at this valley near the mountain. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد)]

- 20:12/q23/p3 It is a verbal noun or adjective of repeated sanctification, or attached to the call. — Al-Hasan uses a verbal noun from folding twice; al-Farisi allows an adjective like thanā and sawā; al-Alusi attaches it to المقدس or نودي. - 20:12/q23/p6 A verbal noun conveys an implicit imperative to traverse the valley and ascend. — Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers this command sense within the verbal-noun analysis and cites a report that Moses climbed to the valley's upper end. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 20:12/q24 Is the final yāʾ of الوادي pronounced?  [بِٱلْوَادِ · readings]
- 20:12/q24/p1 It may be restored or omitted without changing the meaning. — Qiraat-ER notes a ten-reader restored yāʾ and a shortened form following the rasm. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the noun originally ends in yāʾ, often drops it, and retains it in dual and annexed forms. [5 notes: NASHR(الكسائي); QIRAAT-ER(Yz); IBNMUJAHID(خلف عن الكسائي); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:12/q27 How is the fiery encounter understood inwardly?  [* · inward]
- 20:12/q27/p1 Divine light and love consumed Moses' selfhood and revealed God through a fiery veil. — Qashani calls the fire a curtain of majesty and the declaration a veiled manifestation. Bursevi reads light as love and fire as ardent love, burning away selfhood. Ja'far says Moses was annihilated before an address borne and answered by God. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI; SULAMI(جعفر)]

## 20:12/q28 What does the verse show about God and place?  [* · theology]
- 20:12/q28/p1 The divine speaker is not contained by the valley or by time. — Biqai says the purified courtly setting also signals transcendence of spatial and temporal containment. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:12/q28/p2 The divine manifestation may be veiled in created fiery form. — Qashani treats 'I am your Lord' as manifestation behind fiery curtains of majesty. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:12/q29 What is the scope of figurative interpretation here?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:12/q29/p1 A metonymic reading can convey the whole sentence's gist beyond its apparent words. — Al-Burhan describes this rhetorical mode; al-Razi objects that it could turn the real sandal order into mere total devotion, opening boundless esoteric reinterpretation. [1 notes: BURHAN; BURHAN(فخر الدين الرازي)-]

## 20:12/q30 What is said here about the preceding divine istiwāʾ?  [* · theology]

- 20:12/q30/p1 The sounder interpretation is divine power. — Al-Jishumi prefers Abu Ali's view and calls the Kullabi account of an act or attribute named istiwāʾ unintelligible. [1 notes: JISHUMI+]

## 20:12/q31 Who identifies himself to Moses in this address?  [إِنِّىٓ أَنَا۠ رَبُّكَ · interpretation]
- 20:12/q31/p1 God himself is the speaker addressing Moses. — Ibn Kathir takes the words as God's explicit self-identification. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 20:12/q32 What legal status does untanned carrion leather have?  [نَعْلَيْكَ · law]
- 20:12/q32/p1 It is impure, with two noted dissenters. — Al-Qurtubi says jurists generally agree that untanned dead-animal leather is impure, except al-Zuhri and al-Layth. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 20:12/q33 What court custom is used to explain taking off the sandals?  [فَٱخْلَعْ نَعْلَيْكَ · interpretation]
- 20:12/q33/p1 Shoes are removed when entering a king's court. — Al-Qushayri reports this as an analogy for Moses' presence before God. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 20:12/q34 How does the ruling on tanning affect the carrion-hide explanation?  [نَعْلَيْكَ · law]
- 20:12/q34/p1 The later Prophetic ruling purifies tanned hide and would abrogate an earlier ban if that ban explained Moses' removal. — Al-Razi cites the Prophet's saying that any tanned hide becomes pure. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 20:12/q35 How has the sandal image appeared in Sufi literature?  [نَعْلَيْكَ · inward]
- 20:12/q35/p1 It became a recurring image in poetry and named works. — Study Quran notes Ibn Qasi's book Taking Off the Sandals among its literary afterlives. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 20:12/q36 What root is Ṭuwā derived from?  [طُوًۭى · meaning]
- 20:12/q36/p1 A root meaning to fold or become enwrapped. — Study Quran gives the etymology; it informs interpretations of the valley as folded or doubled. Ibn ʿĀshūr derives the doubled-sanctity explanation from folding and likens a narrow valley to folded cloth. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل)]

## 20:12/q37 What does inna contribute to the declaration?  [إِنِّىٓ · rhetoric]
- 20:12/q37/p1 It confirms the surprising message and keeps Moses from doubting who is speaking. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says inna strengthens the assertion for Moses when the unseen speaker identifies himself. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:12/q38 What does wād mean in the valley phrase?  [بِٱلْوَادِ · meaning]
- 20:12/q38/p1 A pass between mountains or hills. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives this lexical sense and notes the underlying final yāʾ, often omitted but retained in dual and annexed forms. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:12/q39 What lesson is drawn from Moses relinquishing his sandals and staff?  [فَٱخْلَعْ نَعْلَيْكَ · inward]
- 20:12/q39/p1 Even Moses put aside his only two possessions to approach God; a sinner carrying many sins should consider his own approach. — Al-Rāzī contrasts Moses' two belongings, sandals and staff, with the sinner's thousand burdens of sin. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 20:12/q40 How is the sequence after removing the sandals read inwardly?  [فَٱخْلَعْ نَعْلَيْكَ · inward]
- 20:12/q40/p1 First clear the conscience of distractions, then adorn it and acquire what it lacked. — Al-Ālūsī takes the removal as emptying and what follows as adornment and acquisition. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
