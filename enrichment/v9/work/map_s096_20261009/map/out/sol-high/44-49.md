# 44:49: 11 questions

## 44:49/q01 What does the command to taste convey?  [ذُقْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the command is explained by its object, its humiliating force, or Bursevī's account of newly perceived prior pain.
- 44:49/q01/p1 Experience the punishment of Hell. — Muqātil calls it torment; al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Bursevī and the Study Quran say punishment. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls tasting a metaphor for experiencing; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it conveys full perception of pain and pleasure. The Study Quran links other punishment-as-tasting verses (3:106, 3:181, 4:56, 6:30, 7:39, 8:35, 8:50, 10:52, 22:22, 32:14, 32:20, 34:42, 46:34). Ṭabrisī reports Muqātil's scene: the keeper lifts the sinner over the fire, his head parts from his brain, and extremely hot water is poured in; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the address occurs amid successive punishments. Iṣlāḥī says they taste the torment they had questioned. [11 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(JJ)]
- 44:49/q01/p2 The imperative insults, threatens, and humiliates the condemned. — Muqātil calls it mockery and humiliation; Bursevī says the punishment is degrading; Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies a humiliating imperative. Al-Burhān calls it denial and rebuke, and al-Biqāʿī says it recalls the messengers' worldly warning of the sinner's lowliness. Al-Itqān lists threat, insult, humiliation through transformation, and challenge exposing inability among imperative functions; it also uses this verse as sarcastic address. [7 notes: MUQATIL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 44:49/q01/p3 He begins to feel the pain of injustice that he failed to sense during life. — Bursevī says heedlessness and veiling numbed him to punishment already present in this world; death awakens perception of it. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 44:49/q02 Who speaks the taunt?  [ذُقْ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْكَرِيمُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the utterance has an identified divine or angelic speaker, is implicit quoted speech, or is the situation expressed as speech.
- 44:49/q02/p1 An angel or the guardians of Hell speak it. — Muqātil names the keeper; al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, Iṣlāḥī and the Study Quran identify an angel or wardens. The Study Quran says they execute God's command. [10 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل التفسير); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(IK, Q)]
- 44:49/q02/p2 God directly addresses the disbelievers. — The Study Quran reports this interpretation. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 44:49/q02/p3 The situation and punishment themselves express the taunt, with no identified speaker. — Iṣlāḥī reports this reading as an alternative to speech by the wardens. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 44:49/q02/p4 It is a quoted utterance introduced by an omitted verb of saying, with the speaker left implicit. — Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies an ellipsis such as 'say to him' or 'it is said to him'. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 44:49/q03 How is إِنَّكَ read and construed?  [إِنَّكَ · readings]
turns on: The vowel on the hamza determines an independent assertion and pause or a subordinate causal construction; commentators note their meanings can converge.
- 44:49/q03/p1 Most readers use kasra, innaka, as a new independent clause: 'indeed you are'. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Fārisī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Samīn, Ibn al-Jazarī's Nashr and Ibn ʿĀshūr contrast it with al-Kisāʾī's fatḥa. Al-Naḥḥās says the clause begins a sentence and prefers the supported majority reading; al-Qurṭubī says one pauses after 'taste'. Al-Fārisī and Abū ʿAlī (via Ibn al-Jawzī) say the independent assertion echoes Abū Jahl's own claim; al-Samīn says it still conveys a cause. The Study Quran renders it 'surely you'. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ; FARISI-HUJJA(الكسائي); THALABI(الكسائي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكسائي والآخرون); TABRISI(الكسائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو علي,الكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(العوام، الحسن عن علي، الكسائي); SAMIN-DURR(الكسائي); NASHR(الكسائي); IBNASHUR-FULL(الكسائي); STUDYQURAN(some); NAHHAS+]
- 44:49/q03/p2 Al-Kisāʾī reads fatḥa, annaka, making the clause causal: 'because you are'; other witnesses also transmit it. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Fārisī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Samīn, al-Nashr, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Qiraat-ER note the variant. Al-Qurṭubī reports al-Ḥasan from ʿAlī; al-Ālūsī names al-Ḥasan ibn ʿAlī. Al-Fārisī expands it as 'taste because you are'; al-Bayḍāwī also allows attachment to an omitted 'punishment'; Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies an implied lām of purpose, and Qiraat-ER notes a subordinating or object-like link. Al-Qurṭubī says no pause after 'taste'. Al-Biqāʿī says speech and enacted punishment meet the sinner's former self-praise; the Study Quran links doubt to haughtiness. Al-Naḥḥās objects that this boast is less than the disbelief the construction would imply. [22 notes: ZAJJAJ(الكسائي); FARISI-HUJJA(الكسائي); THALABI(الكسائي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكسائي والآخرون); TABRISI(الكسائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو علي,الكسائي); RAZI-FULL(قارئ أنك); QURTUBI-FULL(العوام، الحسن عن علي، الكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL(الكسائي); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الكسائي); NASHR(الكسائي); BIQAI-FULL(الكسائي); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن بن علي بن أبي طالب والكسائي); IBNASHUR-FULL(الكسائي); QIRAAT-ER; STUDYQURAN(some); NAHHAS-]

## 44:49/q04 What does the detached pronoun أَنتَ do?  [أَنتَ · grammar]
- 44:49/q04/p1 It singles out and emphatically reinforces the addressee. — Al-Biqāʿī says it isolates the supposedly mighty sinner against those who exposed his worthlessness. Ibn ʿĀshūr says a detached nominative emphasizes the attached pronoun in innaka and that such emphasis requires this nominative form; he says the emphasis heightens sarcasm. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 44:49/q05 What does ٱلْعَزِيزُ denote?  [ٱلْعَزِيزُ · meaning]
- 44:49/q05/p1 A mighty, powerful, protected person who prevails. — Muqātil and al-Dāmghānī give 'impregnable' or mighty; al-Dāmghānī cites parallel descriptions of strength in 4:158, 48:7, 63:8, 4:139 and 35:10. Al-Biqāʿī specifies one who overcomes and is never overcome. The Abu Jahl reports frame it as power and protection in Mecca's valley. [5 notes: MUQATIL; DAMGHANI; BIQAI-FULL; ZAJJAJ(الكسائي); FARISI-HUJJA]

## 44:49/q06 What does ٱلْكَرِيمُ denote here?  [ٱلْكَرِيمُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether karīm describes the usual honorable trait or the condemned man's inflated self-description.
- 44:49/q06/p1 Noble or generous, with dignity and proud self-regard. — Al-Biqāʿī combines generosity, dignity of soul and proud refusal. The reports of Abū Jahl's boast use nobility, honor or generosity as near wordings; al-Wāḥidī al-Wajīz has 'generous'. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
- 44:49/q06/p2 Self-honoring, arrogant or noble only in one's own estimation. — Muqātil gives conceited or self-honoring; al-Dāmghānī says it is the condemned man's own claim. Ibn al-Jawzī's Nuzha reports an exegetical sense 'arrogant' or 'self-important', used mockingly; Qatāda and Ibn ʿAbbās give 'in his own eyes'. [6 notes: MUQATIL; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة)]

## 44:49/q07 Why is the condemned called 'the mighty, the noble'?  [ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْكَرِيمُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the titles repeat the sinner's self-praise, invert their surface meaning, or acknowledge real but useless social status.
- 44:49/q07/p1 The titles throw his former boast or self-image back at him in scorn. — Muqātil, al-Majāz, Qatāda and many later commentators say Abū Jahl styled himself the mightiest and noblest in Mecca; the keepers repeat his claim. Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda and Abū ʿAlī say the titles are true only in his own eyes or claim. Al-Fārisī and al-Wāḥidī cite Zahrat al-Yaman's self-praise and Jarīr's retort; al-Fārisī and al-Wāḥidī compare the alleged partners in 28:62. Ṣāḥib al-Naẓm compares the unbelievers' mocking address in 15:6. Al-Samīn also cites Jarīr. Al-Biqāʿī says repeating what the sinner and followers believed compounds the rebuke; al-Burhān calls it taḥakkum and speech framed by the addressee's belief. Ṭabāṭabāʾī recalls his expected good standing with God (41:50); the Study Quran says the speech ridicules worldly self-image. [41 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(ابن عباس); FARISI-HUJJA(بعض الرواة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو علي الفارسي,أهل التفسير,صاحب النظم); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو علي,قتادة); RAZI-FULL(أبو جهل); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL(الكسائي); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Aj, IJ, Q, Ṭ,IJ, Ṭ, Z)]
- 44:49/q07/p2 The apparent praise is sarcastic reversal: he is now lowly, weak and humiliated. — Ibn Qutayba records the opposite-sense gloss; Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits Ibn ʿAbbās's 'neither mighty nor noble'; Qatāda and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr similarly negate the titles and stress insult. Al-Rāzī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Burhān, Ibn ʿĀshūr and others call it ironic antithesis or opposite-sense metaphor. Al-Qurṭubī compares the ironic 'forbearing, right-minded' in 11:87; al-Itqān compares opposite-sense glad tidings and similar taunts, while al-Sāmarrāʾī cites 'glad tidings' of punishment in 4:138. Al-Burhān and al-Sāmarrāʾī say the surrounding punishment disambiguates praise as condemnation. Al-Biqāʿī points to the sinner's present ruin; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the mockery adds to punishment. [29 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير,قتادة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير ومقاتل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURHAN(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); ITQAN; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 44:49/q07/p3 He really had worldly standing among his people or family, but it cannot help him now. — Ibn ʿĪsā, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Māwardī as cited by Ibn al-Jawzī, and al-Ālūsī give this reading. Al-Rāzī adds that he relied on power apart from God and is made to see its failure. Bursevī includes the person's standing among his people alongside self-estimation. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 44:49/q08 Whom does the address target?  [ذُقْ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْكَرِيمُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the Abu Jahl occasion limits the address or exemplifies a wider class.
- 44:49/q08/p1 Abū Jahl is the specific addressee or occasion. — Muqātil, Qatāda, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Dāmghānī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Burhān, al-Durr, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī and the Study Quran identify him. Al-Burhān cites this as specific address meant for its specific addressee. His Quraysh leadership and boasts identify him in the reports. [28 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(ابن عباس); FARISI-HUJJA; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل التفسير,عكرمة والكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(أبو جهل); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي ﷺ,قتادة); BURHAN; DURR-FULL([],قتادة); BURSEVI(أبو جهل); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة); STUDYQURAN(Aj, IJ, Q, Ṭ,some)]
- 44:49/q08/p2 The address applies to the punished disbeliever or every sinner who makes the same claim. — Al-Ālūsī expressly extends it beyond Abū Jahl to every such sinner at the Resurrection. Bursevī says the verse threatens Abu Jahl and his likes; Ṭabāṭabāʾī, Iṣlāḥī and the Study Quran describe sinners, disbelievers or Hell's inhabitants more generally. [9 notes: BURSEVI(أبو جهل); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(IJ, Ṭ, Z,IK, Q)]

## 44:49/q09 What was reported to have happened with Abū Jahl and this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in the occasion and timing of the boast: a worldly boast or prophetic warning before Badr, a naming episode before the Zaqqum verses, or a claim after the order to drag him into Hell.
- 44:49/q09/p1 He boasted that none in Mecca's valley or between its two mountains was mightier or nobler; the verse answered his claim. — Muqātil says he called himself Quraysh's mightiest and noblest; Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās and many exegetes vary the boast as the strongest, most honored, generous, or most protected in the valley. Al-Thaʿlabī says the phrase sharply affirms his own claim in derision. Al-Durr reports Qatāda's question of how Muḥammad could threaten such a man. [25 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ(الكسائي); FARISI-HUJJA; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أهل التفسير); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(أبو جهل); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BURHAN; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(أبو جهل); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة); STUDYQURAN(Aj, IJ, Q, Ṭ)]
- 44:49/q09/p2 After the Prophet warned him, Abū Jahl claimed neither the Prophet nor God could harm him; he was killed and humbled at Badr, and the verse recalled his words. — ʿIkrima and al-Kalbī via al-Wāḥidī connect the warning with 75:34; al-Qurṭubī reports the Prophet commanded to say 'woe to you'; Ibn Kathīr cites 75:34–35. Al-Rāzī and Bursevī transmit the additional denial that the Prophet or his Lord could do anything to him. Bursevī remarks on the confused simultaneous oath by God and denial of divine power. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عكرمة والكلبي); RAZI-FULL(أبو جهل); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي ﷺ); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة); DURR-FULL; BURSEVI(أبو جهل); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 44:49/q09/p3 He called himself 'the mighty, the noble' as his name; the Zaqqum verses then descended. — Al-Durr gives an unnamed short report. Al-Ālūsī adds a scene where Abū Jahl asked Quraysh to guess his name, rejected ʿUmar, al-Jallās and Abū al-Ḥakam, then supplied the two titles. [2 notes: DURR-FULL([]); ALUSI-FULL]
- 44:49/q09/p4 After the command to drag him into Hell, he claimed unmatched might and nobility, and God replied with this taunt. — Al-Durr transmits this timing from Qatāda. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 44:49/q10 What does the taunt teach about power, disbelief and punishment?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The explanations emphasize failed worldly strength, resurrection denial, just reversal, or an inward account of pain formerly hidden by heedlessness.
- 44:49/q10/p1 Worldly power, self-exaltation and supposed invulnerability collapse under God's punishment. — Al-Rāzī says the sinner trusted power apart from God and must see what became of him. Al-Biqāʿī contrasts the boast with the warnings he denied; Bursevī says Abū Jahl's denial of divine power amid an oath by God shows unbelief's confusion and ego, like demanding stones from heaven. Kur'an Yolu says worldly power left such people helpless. The Study Quran links punishment to disbelief and actions; Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes their mistaken expectation of honor and a good outcome with God (41:50). [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(JJ); TABATABAI]
- 44:49/q10/p2 Arrogant denial or doubt of the Hereafter and accountability brings the torment they questioned. — Asad connects the taunt with disbelief in life after death and accountability, citing 96:6. Iṣlāḥī says disbelief in resurrection caused Quraysh's rebellion and heedlessness, and that the sinners taste the torment they doubted. The Study Quran notes parallel chastisements for denial in 32:20, 34:42, 52:13–16 and 83:17; on the fatḥa reading it links doubt causally to haughtiness. [5 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 44:49/q10/p3 The scene displays just recompense and the reversal of the sinner's former condition. — Iṣlāḥī says the passage uses intellectual and historical evidence for reward and punishment and depicts different fates for believers and disbelievers with complete justice. Al-Qāshānī reads a corrupted primordial nature as reversing bodily pleasure, might and psychic dignity into spiritual pain, humiliation and abasement. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QASHANI]
- 44:49/q10/p4 Worldly heedlessness prevented him from sensing pain already caused by his injustice. — Bursevī says death removes the veil and reveals punishment that was present but unfelt in life. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 44:49/q11 How does this scene connect with the surrounding account of judgment?  [* · links]
- 44:49/q11/p1 It follows arguments for judgment and depicts the disbelievers' fate alongside the believers' different fate. — Iṣlāḥī says the section first gives intellectual and natural arguments for reward and punishment, identifies Quraysh's denial of the afterlife as the root of rebellion, then portrays each group's fate under complete justice. The Study Quran compares other depictions of the damned being told to taste punishment (32:20, 34:42, 52:13–16, 83:17). [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
