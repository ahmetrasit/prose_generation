# 2:21: 31 questions

## 2:21/q01 Why does the passage turn to a direct call?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ · rhetoric]
turns on: The sources give different explanations or scopes for the wording.
- 2:21/q01/p1 It rouses all the groups just described and makes the command vivid, serious, and easier to receive. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī call this iltifāt after believers, unbelievers, and hypocrites, comparing 1:5. Rāzī adds honor, nearness, spiritual ascent, and relief from hard duties. Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī stress renewed attention, encouragement and mercy; Ibn ʿĀshūr likens it to a mentor who rebukes then consoles. [20 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:21/q01/p2 The shift is from specific address to general address. — An unnamed exegete gives this description; Abū Ḥayyān rejects it because no specific direct address preceded the verse, unless 'address' loosely means discourse. [0 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعض المفسرين)-]

## 2:21/q02 What work does the call particle yā do here?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether distance is literal, figurative, or merely a grammatical convention.
- 2:21/q02/p1 It calls and alerts the listener; distance can signal inattention, rank, humility, or urgency. — Al-Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samin, Zarkashī, Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Suyūṭī note yā's broad or original distant use. Supplicants say yā rabb despite God's nearness out of humility and eagerness. Rāzī argues the vocative performs a call, unlike the proposition 'I call Zayd'; his analogy makes human invocation of the transcendent Lord possible. [28 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ITQAN; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:21/q02/p2 It is usable for near and far addressees alike. — Abū Ḥayyān calls yā the broadest and the Qurʾān's only vocative particle; Ālūsī reports grammarians who treat it as common to all vocatives. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:21/q03 How is ayyuhā al-nās constructed?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether al-nās is the required nominative descriptor of a vocative ayy, an accusative by position, or part of a relative construction.
- 2:21/q03/p1 Ayy is a built vocative bridge to the definite al-nās; al-nās clarifies it in the nominative and hā adds attention. — Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, al-Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Ṭabrisī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samin, and Ālūsī explain the bridge: direct yā plus al- would double definiteness; ambiguous ayy requires its descriptor, while hā reinforces the call or compensates for omitted annexation. Al-Khalīl's nominative rule and dialectal hā with ḍamma are reported. The Qurʾān repeats the layered call because weighty commands, warnings and promises demand attention. [56 notes: ZAJJAJ(سيبويه عن الخليل); NAHHAS(أبو العباس,بعض بني مالك من بني أسد); WAHIDI-BASIT(النحويون); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو علي); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بنو مالك من بني أسد); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(ابن الشجري,الجمهور,الشيخ منصور موهوب بن أحمد,المازني); ALUSI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+; ZAJJAJ(المازني)-]
- 2:21/q03/p2 The dependent noun may take the accusative by the vocative's underlying syntactic position. — Al-Māzinī and Abū ʿUthmān allow this by analogy with yā Zayd al-ẓarīf; Zajjāj, Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samin and Ālūsī object that actual Arab usage keeps this indispensable descriptor nominative. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ(المازني); NAHHAS(أبو عثمان المازني); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,المازني); JISHUMI(المازني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عثمان); ALUSI-FULL(المازني); TABRISI-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL(المازني)-]
- 2:21/q03/p3 Ayy is relative, with an omitted subject or clause, and al-nās completes that construction. — Al-Akhfash gives the relative parse; Abū Ḥayyān records 'O those who are the people.' Samin favors direct address, while Ālūsī prefers the majority parse because it needs no deletion and avoids relative-clause restrictions. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ(أبو الحسن الأخفش); NAHHAS(الأخفش); JISHUMI(الأخفش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو الحسن); SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش); ALUSI-FULL(الأخفش); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:21/q03/p4 The final vowel of al-nās is built rather than case inflection. — Abū Nizār says no governing ʿāmil accounts for its nominative; Manṣūr and Ibn al-Shajarī argue for inflection or analogy with the ordinary vocative ḍamma; Ālūsī finds these arguments weak. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو نزار); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:21/q03/p5 The article on al-nās compensates for repeated vocative particles. — Abū Nizār proposes hā and al- in place of repeated yā; Ibn al-Shajarī rejects the imagined original yā ayy yā yā rajul and says the article can mark presence. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو نزار); ALUSI-FULL(ابن الشجري)-]

## 2:21/q04 What does al-nās mean as a word?  [ٱلنَّاسُ · meaning]
turns on: The proposed root behind the name.
- 2:21/q04/p1 It denotes human beings as a species. — Ibn al-Jawzī lists insān as singular and nās and anāsī as plurals; other senses of related forms include sight, sound, knowledge and company. He records a doubled-s form for thirst, witnessed by a rajaz line, and the name al-nāsiya for water-scarce Mecca. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن فارس,الراجز); IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 2:21/q04/p2 Its root points to human visibility or manifest presence. — Ibn Fāris connects ins with being visible. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن فارس)]
- 2:21/q04/p3 Its name derives from movement toward aims. — Ibn al-Jawzī relates nās to naws, movement. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 2:21/q04/p4 Its name derives from forgetfulness. — Ibn al-Jawzī records the proposed derivation of anas from human forgetfulness. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 2:21/q05 Who are the people addressed?  [ٱلنَّاسُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the definite plural and context include everyone or designate a particular group.
- 2:21/q05/p1 All morally responsible people, believers and unbelievers, are addressed. — Ṭabarī includes the disbelieving and hypocritical groups and other accountable people. Ibn ʿAbbās is reported to include all; Baghawī and Ṭabrisī except children and the insane. Rāzī, Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī argue a definite plural without a prior referent is general, supported by exception and emphatic generalizers. Abū Ḥayyān prefers this without evidence to restrict it. Ibn ʿĀshūr details duties for polytheists, materialists, scripture communities and believers. Kuran Yolu stresses no distinction of religion, race, language, place or class. [33 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور,الحنابلة,العلامة الثاني,بعض أجلة المحققين,كثير من الشافعية); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس)+; ALUSI-FULL(بعض أجلة المحققين)+]
- 2:21/q05/p2 It addresses the people or polytheists of Mecca. — Wāḥidī's asbāb and concise gloss, Nasafī and some lexical scope examples identify Meccans; Ibn ʿAbbās's Meccan/Medinan address rule is also cited. [5 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; NASAFI-FULL(علقمة); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين)]
- 2:21/q05/p3 It addresses unbelievers and hypocrites. — Ibn Hishām and the report from Ibn ʿAbbās give these two groups. Ṭabarī includes them within the wider address. [3 notes: IBNHISHAM; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس)]
- 2:21/q05/p4 It addresses hypocrites and Jews. — Muqātil states this; Ibn al-Jawzī and Abū Ḥayyān also report his view. [3 notes: MUQATIL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل)]
- 2:21/q05/p5 It addresses Arab polytheists and other unbelievers. — Al-Suddī's report names these; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports all polytheists, while noting that they know God as creator and provider despite general ignorance. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جماعة من المفسرين)]
- 2:21/q05/p6 It addresses Jews specifically. — Al-Ḥasan and Mujāhid are named by Ibn al-Jawzī and Abū Ḥayyān. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن ومجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن ومجاهد)]

## 2:21/q06 Does 'O people' identify Meccan revelation?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ · sciences]
turns on: Whether the address formula is an absolute criterion or a usual tendency.
- 2:21/q06/p1 A people-address is Meccan and a believers-address Medinan. — Reports ascribed to ʿAlqama, Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, al-Ḥasan, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Maymūn and ʿUrwa give this rule; some add 'children of Adam.' ʿUrwa associates stories and punishments with Mecca and legal duties with Medina. Al-Bazzār notes most chains stop at ʿAlqama, with Qays alone linking it further. [27 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-ASBAB(علقمة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن وعلقمة); KASHSHAF-FULL(علقمة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(ابن عباس والحسن); RAZI-FULL(علقمة والحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL(علقمة والحسن); NASAFI-FULL(علقمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وعلقمة); BURHAN(البزار,الجعبرى,عروة بن الزبير,علقمة); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,الضحاك,عبد الله بن مسعود,عروة,عكرمة,علقمة,ميمون بن مهران); ITQAN(عبد الله,علقمة,ميمون بن مهران,هشام بن عروة عن أبيه); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود وعلقمة)]
- 2:21/q06/p2 It is a tendency, not a universal rule: Medinan passages also say 'O people.' — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān cite this wholly Medinan sūra; Zarkashī and Suyūṭī add al-Nisāʾ and the believers-address in Meccan al-Ḥajj. Makkī limits the rule to most cases; the Qāḍī rejects deriving it from Medina's believer count. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وعلقمة); ITQAN(ابن الحصار,ابن عطية وابن الغرس,القاضي,غيره,مكي); BURHAN+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]

## 2:21/q07 Does this general address include later people and the Prophet?  [ٱلنَّاسُ · law]
turns on: Whether direct address reaches absent generations literally, by predominance, or by separate evidence.
- 2:21/q07/p1 Later generations are bound through separate proof, although direct speech initially addresses those present. — Rāzī says absent people cannot hear a face-to-face command, but mass-transmitted evidence extends Muhammad's law. Most non-Hanbalī jurists and Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguish present hearers from later recipients who learn the rule as information. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:21/q07/p2 The address itself generally includes later generations. — Bayḍāwī and Hanbalīs appeal to the enduring mission and continuing legal use; many Shāfiʿīs take literal generality, while Ālūsī leans toward inclusion by eloquent predominance of present and absent. [7 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الحنابلة,العلامة الثاني,بعض أجلة المحققين,كثير من الشافعية); ALUSI-FULL(بعض أجلة المحققين)+]
- 2:21/q07/p3 The Prophet too is included in a general address to people. — Suyūṭī reports the majority and al-Zuhrī's analogous believers-address. [1 notes: ITQAN]
- 2:21/q07/p4 The Prophet is excluded as conveyor, or included except after 'say.' — Suyūṭī reports both qualifications. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 2:21/q08 What does the command to worship require?  [ٱعْبُدُوا۟ رَبَّكُمُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether worship is specified as monotheism, obedience, or comprehensive servitude.
- 2:21/q08/p1 It calls for exclusive worship and affirmation of God's oneness. — Ibn ʿAbbās repeatedly glosses worship as tawḥīd, and Baghawī reports every Qurʾānic instance thus. Ṭabarī adds exclusive obedience; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Nasafī and Bursevī exclude partners. Ibn ʿĀshūr makes belief the first step because shirk defeats true submission. Qāshānī reads it inwardly as unity of divine acts. [18 notes: MUQATIL; IBNHISHAM; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); SULAMI(بعضهم); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI; QASHANI]
- 2:21/q08/p2 It requires submitting to and obeying God. — Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī's second Ibn ʿAbbās report, Ṭabarī, Thalabī and Mujāhid emphasize obedience; Qurʾānic worship is abasement and submission in Ibn ʿĀshūr. Qushayrī and Bursevī add perseverance, humility and avoidance of prohibitions. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); TABRISI; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:21/q08/p3 The one command applies differently by audience: begin, increase, or persevere in worship. — Al-Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī and Ālūsī answer the objection that believers already worship: adding worship is a new instance. Bursevī specifies tawḥīd for polytheists, obedience for sinners, sincerity for hypocrites and perseverance for the obedient. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds materialists and the People of the Book. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(قائل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 2:21/q09 Does this command obligate unbelievers to perform religious duties?  [ٱعْبُدُوا۟ · law]
turns on: Whether the command covers subsidiary duties or only belief until conversion.
- 2:21/q09/p1 Unbelievers are also accountable for ritual duties, with belief as their prerequisite. — Rāzī says an imperative includes acquiring prerequisites, as water and purification; Bayḍāwī concurs. Ālūsī reports Iraqi and Shāfiʿī evidence from zakāt and the sinners' admissions about prayer and feeding the poor (9:34; 74:42–44), while both sides exempt make-up duties after conversion. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(من لم يقل بذلك); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حنيفة,العراقيون والشافعية)]
- 2:21/q09/p2 Unbelievers owe belief, but this verse does not establish subsidiary ritual duties. — Bukhara scholars restrict their obligation to faith; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the unbeliever's worship here means affirming God, unity and the Messenger. Ālūsī limits the practical dispute to afterlife punishment; both sides agree on penal and transaction rules. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو حنيفة,البخاريون); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:21/q10 Can God command those who will not worship or cannot perform the act?  [ٱعْبُدُوا۟ · theology]
turns on: Whether foreknowledge, motives, ability, and prerequisites make obligation impossible.
- 2:21/q10/p1 The command can bind even those known not to comply; it can include a prerequisite act of belief. — Ṭabarī uses 2:6 against a requirement for prior divine assistance. Rāzī answers that worship presupposes faith; his theological reply allows God as owner to impose even what others call impossible, while the general wording is qualified for children, insane, forgetful, unaware and incapable people (2:233). Ālūsī reports the same proof. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL(من الناس,من لم يقل بذلك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الطبري); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:21/q10/p2 Such commands are impossible or purposeless. — Rāzī reports objections: unbelievers cannot recognize an unknown commander; equal motives paralyze choice, unequal motives force it; foreknowledge fixes acts; God cannot benefit and could confer benefits without burdens; divine creation of acts makes orders redundant; outward duties might obstruct inner knowledge. He answers the first three by noting the self-contradiction of a duty to deny duty, and defends God's unrestricted ownership. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL(قائل,منكرو التكليف)]
- 2:21/q10/p3 A generic command could be fulfilled by one act, but every act of worship remains commanded. — Rāzī distinguishes semantic genus from its fitting cause: glorification and submission make each worship act apt for command. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 2:21/q11 Why call God 'your Lord'?  [رَبَّكُمُ · rhetoric]
- 2:21/q11/p1 Lordship, nurture, mastery and prior favor make worship and gratitude due to Him. — Bayḍāwī grounds duty in lordship; Abū Ḥayyān adds ownership and correction of servants' affairs. Harālī says rabb is more accessible through visible nurture than the divine name Allāh. Ālūsī distinguishes this immediate motive from perfected worship for God's essence. Samarrai contrasts rabb's guidance and blessing with Allāh in warning contexts. Qāshānī sees blessings drawing love. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; QASHANI; ELMALILI]

## 2:21/q12 What does 'who created you' do in the description of Lord?  [ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَكُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the clause praises the recognized Lord or distinguishes Him from alleged lords.
- 2:21/q12/p1 It praises God and gives the reason He deserves worship. — Bayḍāwī, Wāḥidī, Bursevī, Biqāʿī and Ālūsī make the relative clause laudatory and causal; the creation and provision that follow establish divine oneness. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 2:21/q12/p2 For polytheists, it identifies the true Lord against their supposed lords. — Al-Zamakhsharī and Abū Ḥayyān say the name rabb is shared in polytheist usage, while 'who created you' singles out God. They invoke the Arabs' acknowledgment at 43:87. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL+]

## 2:21/q13 How is alladhī khalaqakum parsed?  [ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: The sources give different explanations or scopes for the wording.
- 2:21/q13/p1 Alladhī modifies rabbakum, and khalaqakum is its relative clause. — Naḥḥās takes kum as khalaqa's object; Samin also lists an accusative adjective parse. [2 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:21/q13/p2 Alladhī may instead be accusative or nominative by severance (qatʿ). — Samin lists both; Ibn al-Jazarī says an implied huwa or a commendatory accusative permits a good stop despite Sajawandī's objection. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; NASHR]

## 2:21/q14 What does khalq mean?  [خَلَقَكُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether it denotes divine origination, measured shaping, or both.
- 2:21/q14/p1 It is bringing something into existence without a prior model. — Wāḥidī calls human measuring metaphorical; Baghawī, Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī, Samin and Ālūsī retain origination as God's unique act. Rāzī reports Sunni consensus that 'no creator but God' favors this sense; Abū Ḥayyān and Samin cite 59:24 against denying God the name Creator. [14 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(جمهور أهل السنة والجماعة); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:21/q14/p2 Its root sense is measuring, proportioning, shaping, or designing; Arabic applies that to humans too. — Wāḥidī, Rāzī, Qurtubī, Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samin and Ālūsī cite making shoes or waterskins, Zuhayr's line and al-Ḥajjāj's comparison of forming and completing. ʿAbd al-Jabbār uses 23:14 and 5:110 for human creators but distinguishes God's knowledge of outcomes. Jishumī distinguishes creation, origination and action. Ṭabrisī adds khuluq as temperament and khalāq as share. [23 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(ابن الأنباري,القاضي عبد الجبار); QURTUBI-FULL(الحجاج,شاعر); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(المعتزلة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الحجاج,زهير); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:21/q14/p3 God cannot be called Creator if khalq requires estimation and calculation. — Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Baṣrī argues measurement implies deliberation; Abū Ḥayyān, Samin and Ālūsī reject this because khalq also means origination and revelation names God Creator. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو عبد الله البصري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله البصري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو عبدالله البصري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 2:21/q15 What does human creation prove about God and worship?  [خَلَقَكُمْ · theology]
turns on: The sources give different explanations or scopes for the wording.
- 2:21/q15/p1 The acknowledged, ordered creation of humans and their world proves a capable, knowing Creator who alone deserves worship. — Zajjāj, Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Nasafī invoke the Arabs' admission in 43:87. Ṭabarī contrasts God with powerless idols. Rāzī develops arguments from origination and contingent properties and records the egg, mulberry leaf, ship, voices, parentage, shipwreck and confession under distress; Ibn Kathīr adds tracks and dung, stars, seas, mountains, plants and poetry. Abū Ḥayyān orders five signs: people, forebears, earth, sky, rain and fruit, which show power, wisdom, knowledge and governance. [61 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(أبو حنيفة رحمه الله,أبو حنيفة رضي الله عنه,أحمد بن حنبل رضي الله عنه,الخصم,الشافعي رضي الله عنه,القاضي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,جعفر الصادق رضي الله عنه,مالك); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعض المفسرين,قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو حنيفة,أبو نواس,أحمد بن حنبل,أعرابي,ابن المعتز,الشافعي,مالك); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:21/q15/p2 Creation and beneficence explain why worship is owed, but do not make God indebted for reward. — The Qāḍī takes creation as the ground of duty. Rāzī's school says fulfilling a duty earns no wage; Bayḍāwī compares a worker already paid. Ālūsī says innumerable prior benefits cannot be repaid, and Elmalılı denies worship constrains God's will. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا,القاضي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 2:21/q16 Is rational inquiry into God warranted by this verse?  [خَلَقَكُمْ · theology]
turns on: Whether inferential proofs yield faith and are sanctioned by revelation and prophetic practice.
- 2:21/q16/p1 Yes: creation invites sound inference, and prophetic religion uses such proofs. — Rāzī says knowing the Creator precedes worship and is inferential. He ranks theology by its subject, need, proof, permanence and Qurʾānic prominence; gives examples from Abraham, Moses and Muhammad about unity, prophecy and resurrection; cites reflection commands, debate with wisdom (16:125), anti-imitation verses, and prophetic analogies. He answers scriptural and early-authority objections by distinguishing sound reasoning from obstinate or innovative disputation. [70 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو هريرة,عبادة بن الصامت)]
- 2:21/q16/p2 No: inference is unreliable, involuntary, religiously risky or an innovation. — Rāzī reports objections based on regress and unknowable essences, failure to distinguish knowledge from error, inability to seek unknown concepts, conflicting schools, conversions without detailed proof, Qurʾānic warnings (43:58; 6:68), reports about reflecting on creation or leaving predestination, and sayings of Mālik, Sufyān and Shāfiʿī against kalām. Rāzī counters that these objections themselves reason theoretically and their voluntary formulation contradicts them. [30 notes: RAZI-FULL(الخصم,الشافعي,رسول الله,سفيان بن عيينة,مالك بن أنس)]

## 2:21/q17 Who are 'those before you'?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase is limited to human forebears or includes beings earlier in time or rank.
- 2:21/q17/p1 They are earlier generations and ancestors, also created by God. — Muqātil, Ṭabarī's report, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, Thalabī, Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ṭabrisī and Durr make the parallel creation explicit. Rāzī says ancestors are the audience's roots and their creation benefits descendants; Ṭabrisī adds that descendants' blessings are incomplete without theirs. [12 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(السدي، أبو مالك، أبو صالح، ابن عباس، مرة، ابن مسعود، وناس من أصحاب النبي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي ومجاهد); THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI]
- 2:21/q17/p2 It can include any beings preceding people in time or essential rank. — Bayḍāwī broadens precedence; Ālūsī allows those still existing but prior in rank and notes qabla's temporal, spatial and figurative uses. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:21/q18 Why mention and order the earlier generations?  [خَلَقَكُمْ وَٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 2:21/q18/p1 Their creation reinforces proof, gratitude and warning; the audience comes first because it is nearest to itself. — Ibn al-Jawzī says it blocks denial and recalls reward and punishment; Qurtubī and Bursevī say ancestors' deaths warn descendants. Abū Ḥayyān, Zarkashī and Ālūsī explain placing present humans before earlier ones, earth before sky, and beneficiaries before gifts by mental nearness, importance, and dependence on life and reason. [13 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعض المفسرين,قيل); BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:21/q19 How is 'and those before you' constructed?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the temporal phrase is sufficient as a relative clause or requires an understood predicate.
- 2:21/q19/p1 Alladhīna coordinates with the object of khalaqakum; min qablikum locates them before the audience. — Naḥḥās, Ṭabrisī, Bayḍāwī, Samin, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī assign accusative case. Abū Ḥayyān explains that a temporal predicate is understood, 'from a time before yours'; Wāḥidī and Ālūsī also note min's force. [12 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:21/q19/p2 An omitted creation clause supplies the relative connection. — Abū al-Baqā reconstructs 'those whom He created before your creation,' reported by Samin and Ālūsī. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]

## 2:21/q20 What variant readings are reported here?  [خَلَقَكُمْ وَٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ · readings]

## 2:21/q21 What does laʿalla convey here?  [لَعَلَّكُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the particle is purpose, human hope, a divine assurance, or an exhortation.
- 2:21/q21/p1 It expresses hope or expectation from the human addressees' standpoint. — Sībawayh and others cite Moses and Aaron being told to speak gently in hope Pharaoh might remember (20:44), though God knew the outcome. Ibn ʿAṭiyya attaches hope to worshippers' reflection; Qushayrī, Ṭabrisī, Bayḍāwī, Bursevī, Ālūsī and Elmalılı say it keeps worshippers between fear and hope, diligent rather than self-satisfied. Al-Ālūsī prefers a broad expectation sense between occurrence and nonoccurrence. [54 notes: THALABI(سيبويه); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(سيبويه); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه ورؤساء اللسان); TABRISI(سيبويه,قيل); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أبو بكر بن الأنباري,بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سيبويه); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(سيبويه); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(سيبويه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سيبويه); SAMIN-DURR(سيبويه); BURSEVI(السعدي,الكواشي); BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL(التفتازاني,الجمهور,الرضى); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين)+; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو المعالي)+; ZAJJAJ(سيبويه)+]
- 2:21/q21/p2 It means 'so that,' expressing purpose. — Muqātil, Ṭabarī, Qutrub, Ibn al-Anbārī, Ibn Kaysān and others support causal use. - 2:21/q21/p3 From God, hopeful language functions as an assured promise or demand. — Baghawī, al-Zamakhsharī, Nasafī, Zarkashī, Ālūsī, Durr and Study Quran compare a great or generous speaker's modest promise with certainty of fulfillment. [16 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(كثير من المفسرين); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); NASAFI-FULL(سيبويه); BURHAN(أهل السنة,الزمخشري,المعتزلة,مالك وأبو حنيفة); DURR-FULL(عون بن عبد الله بن غنية); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Z)]
- 2:21/q21/p4 The particle also has conjectural, interrogative, or 'as though' uses elsewhere. — Ibn al-Anbārī gives examples of conjecture, possibility and interrogation; the cited 26:129 is explained as 'as though you would live forever.' - 2:21/q21/p5 It invites people to act with the possibility of reaching taqwa. — Qurtubī records this as a third gloss; Samin attributes an open-outcome reading to Mahdawī and Abū al-Baqā. ## 2:21/q22 To what does laʿallakum tattaqūn attach?  [لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether it qualifies the worship command or the act of creation, and whether a circumstantial parse is possible.
- 2:21/q22/p1 It attaches to 'created you': God created people with the means and prospect of piety. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya appeals to every child's fitra. Al-Zamakhsharī says humans received reason, desire, capacity, guidance and choice; he extends the clause to predecessors by rhetorical dominance. Ṭabrisī compares 51:56. Bayḍāwī calls the purposive creation reading linguistically weak. Ālūsī allows metaphor or an outside observer's expectation, while rejecting failed divine will as a premise. [22 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري,بعض المفسرين); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BAYDAWI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(المهدوي)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:21/q22/p2 It attaches to 'worship': worshippers act in hope of becoming God-fearing. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya's Sībawayh report, Qurtubī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samin, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Ālūsī point to the imperative as the main assertion. Qurtubī and Mahdawī say those destined for Hell were not created to become pious. Abū Ḥayyān rejects the creation reading's Muʿtazilī premises and the circumstantial parse because literal hope cannot be a state of God or newly created humans. ## 2:21/q23 What does tattaqūn mean here, and what is guarded against?  [تَتَّقُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the target is shirk, punishment, the Fire, forbidden acts, or a broader station of obedience.
- 2:21/q23/p1 It is guarding oneself through obedience and avoidance of forbidden acts and punishment. — Ṭabarī explains avoidance of anger and earning divine pleasure; Mujāhid glosses obedience. Zajjāj stresses prohibitions; Qushayrī, Ṭabrisī, Biqāʿī, Kuran Yolu and Elmalılı describe self-restraint. Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān distinguish worship as means from protective taqwa as result, avoiding a tautology; Qurtubī cites paying what one owes to ward off a claim, ʿAlī's shielding behind the Prophet, and ʿAntara's cavalry sheltering behind him. [22 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); QUSHAYRI; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب,عنترة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:21/q23/p2 The implied thing guarded against is Hell or divine punishment. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk names the Fire, with five prayers specifically in Ibn Abī Ḥātim; Thalabī includes illicit gain. Wāḥidī and Baghawī say worship protects from punishment. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 2:21/q23/p3 The implied thing guarded against is associating partners with God. — Muqātil and reports from Ibn ʿAbbās give shirk as the omitted object; Bursevī extends this to anything worshipped besides God. [5 notes: MUQATIL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI]
- 2:21/q23/p4 Its highest inward form is abandoning all but God. — Bayḍāwī, Bursevī and Ālūsī call this the wayfarer's highest degree; their citations of 32:16 and 17:57 keep fear and hope together. Saʿdī warns worship alone does not guarantee sincere faith. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(السعدي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:21/q23/p5 In the cross-cited contexts, it means abstaining during fasting or avoiding retaliatory killing. — Ṭabarī's comments on 2:179 and fasting explain respectively fear of qisas and abstention from food, drink and intercourse; al-Suddī compares Christians' abstention. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,السدي)]

## 2:21/q24 How are the final clause and its verb formed?  [لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ · grammar]
- 2:21/q24/p1 Laʿalla governs kum as its noun and tattaqūn as its indicative predicate; tattaqūn derives from wiqāya. — Naḥḥās and Ṭabrisī give the particle's noun and predicate; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Samin derive tattaqūn by wāw-to-tāʾ assimilation and vowel or hiatus changes. Samin records dialectal laʿalla forms and genitive government, and Abū Ḥayyān names Banū ʿUqayl for genitive use. [7 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش وأبو زيد); SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:21/q25 How does this verse connect to its context and the sūra?  [* · links]
- 2:21/q25/p1 It turns classification into a universal worship call and develops proofs of unity, prophecy, resurrection and the coming prohibition of partners. — Rāzī, Jishumī and Biqāʿī trace the Book, the three groups, Israel, qibla, law and proofs; the next verse presents creation and provision and forbids rivals. Elmalılı links Fātiḥa's Lord, 'You alone we worship,' and the opening muttaqīn. Ibn ʿĀshūr links creation in 2:21–22 to life, death, return and human origins in 2:28–29. Ibn Kathīr reads the ordered worlds as proof of exclusive worship. ## 2:21/q26 What inward understanding of servitude is drawn from the command?  [ٱعْبُدُوا۟ رَبَّكُمُ · inward]
- 2:21/q26/p1 Worship realizes exclusive dependence, gratitude, humility and recognition of divine acts. — Qāshānī distinguishes unity of acts from denying creaturely mediation; he says divine acts appear through creatures as spirit acts through body, while the messenger conveys divine words to veiled people. Bursevī connects the call with the forgotten primordial covenant. Biqāʿī sets out heart's knowledge, soul's humility, limbs' observance, sincerity beyond hypocritical imitation, acceptance of decree, and the decisive teaching common across laws; he links 96:1 and 51:56. ## 2:21/q27 What grammatical details govern the imperative 'worship your Lord'?  [ٱعْبُدُوا۟ رَبَّكُمُ · grammar]
- 2:21/q27/p1 The imperative uses a connecting hamza, drops its nūn, and takes rabbakum as direct object. — Naḥḥās explains the hamza's ḍamma instead of root kasra, quotes Sībawayh against a fiʿil pattern, and reports Kufan jussive versus Basran formal explanations for the lost nūn. ## 2:21/q28 What stops or recitation divisions are discussed around the verse?  [* · sciences]
- 2:21/q28/p1 A complete stop can occur before a verse end, and a stop before the relative clause can be good under an independent parse. — Ibn al-Jazarī cites the transition to this address as a complete-stop example and replies to Sajawandī: alladhī can be an implied huwa predicate or commendatory phrase; he considers Makkī's accusative parses remote. [2 notes: NASHR]

## 2:21/q29 What broader lexical or grammatical illustrations do the notes attach to this address?  [* · grammar]
- 2:21/q29/p1 The notes also compare related vocative, relative, plural, and connective constructions. — Naḥḥās records Banū Mālik's hā vowel; Abū Ḥayyān reports disputed uses of ayy and compares a widening address in poetry and a report to the Prophet's kin. ## 2:21/q30 What wider admonitions and reports are tied to the call?  [* · reports]
- 2:21/q30/p1 The command recalls repentance, conversion, accountability and exemplary humility. — Al-Taysīr takes the call as rebuke of ingratitude and mercy for inadvertent opposition.
