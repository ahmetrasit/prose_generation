# 84:13: 18 questions

## 84:13/q01 What does the opening inna do here?  [إِنَّهُۥ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the particle chiefly marks a causal explanation or amazement at a reversal.
- 84:13/q01/p1 It introduces an explanation of the preceding statement's cause. — Bursevi calls the clause a fresh explanation of the preceding statement. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 84:13/q01/p2 It emphasizes amazement at the reversal being described. — Ibn Ashur says emphatic inna is characteristic of expressions of amazement and cites Umar's remark to Hudhayfa about his boldness toward the Prophet. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 84:13/q02 Who is the person described by innahu?  [إِنَّهُۥ · referent]
- 84:13/q02/p1 One of those given their records behind their backs, identified as polytheists or disbelievers. — Ibn Ashur identifies the two groups of 84:7–15 as believers receiving records in the right hand and polytheists receiving them behind their backs; Study Quran calls the latter disbelievers who rejected return and reckoning. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 84:13/q03 How is kana's phrase parsed?  [كَانَ فِىٓ أَهْلِهِۦ مَسْرُورًا · grammar]
turns on: The syntactic role of the phrase after kana; Nahhas favors the predicate analysis.
- 84:13/q03/p1 The phrase is the predicate of kana. — Nahhas gives this as the normal parsing and considers the circumstantial parsing unlikely. [1 notes: NAHHAS+]
- 84:13/q03/p2 The phrase may be parsed as a circumstantial accusative. — Nahhas allows this parsing as possible but unlikely. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 84:13/q04 Can kana describe a quality continuing beyond the past?  [كَانَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the predicate established in the past remains or has ended; Itqan states both grammatical possibilities.
- 84:13/q04/p1 Yes: a past-established predicate may continue. — Itqan reports the grammarians' rule that kana may describe a past-established quality that remains. Ibn Abbas answers a question about God having 'been' mighty and wise by saying that He is mighty and wise in Himself. [2 notes: ITQAN(ابن عباس)]
- 84:13/q04/p2 It may instead describe a quality that ended. — Itqan gives this as the other grammatical possibility, without applying the divine-attribute example to it. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 84:13/q05 When was he happy among his family?  [كَانَ فِىٓ أَهْلِهِۦ مَسْرُورًا · interpretation]
- 84:13/q05/p1 During worldly life. — Qatada, Zajjaj, and al-Dahhak explicitly place the joy in this world; the other cited authors also describe it as worldly pleasure, before the afterlife outcome. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 84:13/q06 Who are his ahl here?  [أَهْلِهِۦ · referent]
turns on: Whether ahl names literal family and associates or a group defined by shared sinful inclinations.
- 84:13/q06/p1 His household, family, relatives, or people around him. — Bursevi explicitly includes family and relatives; Muqatil says his people, and Elmalili says family and people. Baydawi speaks of his worldly household. [4 notes: MUQATIL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]
- 84:13/q06/p2 The people sharing his sinful inclinations. — Asad glosses 'his people' this way and compares his note on 75:33. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 84:13/q07 What relation does fi express in fi ahlihi?  [فِىٓ أَهْلِهِۦ · meaning]
turns on: Whether fi marks being among the family or being together with them in shared happiness.
- 84:13/q07/p1 He was among his family or relatives. — Bursevi gives the ordinary 'among' sense as one possibility. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 84:13/q07/p2 He was with them, all happy together. — Bursevi gives this collective sense and cites the usage 'came to me with a group' to show that fi can mean 'with.' [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 84:13/q08 What was the character of his happiness?  [مَسْرُورًا · interpretation]
turns on: The notes variously specify ordinary pleasure, social pride, indulgence, enjoyment of sin, or heedlessness as the content of the joy; these can overlap.
- 84:13/q08/p1 Comfortable, pleased, and happy in worldly family life. — Zajjaj and al-Dahhak state the worldly family joy without a further motive; Elmalili calls it comfort and pleasure among family and people. Qatada likewise glosses it as worldly life. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; DURR-FULL(الضحاك); ELMALILI]
- 84:13/q08/p2 Boastful joy in honor, wealth, blessings, and status. — Muqatil glosses the joy as being honored among his people; Baydawi and Biqai specify arrogance over wealth and status. Qashani adds that blessings veiled their giver; Bursevi mentions luxury, wealth, and status. [5 notes: MUQATIL; BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 84:13/q08/p3 Pleasure from pursuing desires and indulgence. — Wahidi and Baghawi say he followed his own desires and indulged what his soul craved; Bursevi adds pleasures to wealth and status. Qurtubi's report from Ibn Zayd describes Hell's people as laughing and indulging themselves. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد)]
- 84:13/q08/p4 Joy in defying God's command and committing sins without remorse. — Tabari attributes the worldly joy to defiance and sins; al-Jubba'i says he enjoyed sin and felt no regret. Tabrisi reports the argument that delight in sin presumes disbelief in resurrection, since certainty of judgment would make it unlikely. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي)]
- 84:13/q08/p5 Carefree joy absorbed in this world and heedless of the afterlife. — Tabrisi says he was free of concern for the afterlife and religious hardship; Abu Hayyan calls him arrogantly indulgent and heedless of God and his outcome, comparing 28:76. Ibn Kathir says he ignored consequences; Elmalili adds disregard for suffering people and worldly change. Tabatabai says adornment made him forget the afterlife and cites 40:75; Study Quran associates the joy with denial of resurrection and compares 83:31. Baydawi also connects joy to disbelief in return to God. [7 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 84:13/q09 Why is his worldly happiness blameworthy?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Which neglected duty or belief the sources emphasize: obedience and restraint, resurrection, or concern for others and family.
- 84:13/q09/p1 It was bound up with sin, self-indulgence, and limiting concern to this life. — Tabari names defiance of God's command and sins; Jishumi condemns worldly pleasure, short worldly aspirations, and turning from the afterlife as ending in regret. Wahidi and Baghawi cite desire; Biqai says the warning should bring repentance. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 84:13/q09/p2 It rested on heedlessness or denial of resurrection and judgment. — Al-Jubba'i describes unregretted sin; Tabrisi reports that delight in sin implies disbelief in resurrection because certainty of judgment would restrain it. Baydawi says he supposed he would not return to God; Qashani describes belief that only time rules life and death; Study Quran says the disbelievers rejected return and reckoning. [5 notes: TABRISI(الجبائي); BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; STUDYQURAN]
- 84:13/q09/p3 His lack of concern extended to others' suffering and his relatives' Hereafter. — Elmalili says he ignored suffering people as well as worldly change and the afterlife. Islahi says the group neglected both their own and their relatives' Hereafter; future favors require sacrifice. [2 notes: ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 84:13/q10 What becomes of his worldly happiness?  [* · interpretation]
- 84:13/q10/p1 It is reversed by humiliation, grief, and punishment in the Hereafter. — Muqatil says God will humble the once-honored man at resurrection. Tabrisi contrasts brief joy with unending grief; Ibn Kathir says brief heedless joy leads to prolonged sorrow. Baydawi says God knows his acts and will repay him. Ibn Ashur describes amazement at the reversal into Hell's pain and a call for destruction, with parallels at 73:11 and 83:31. Islahi says the doomed long for death on entering Hell but cannot escape its torment and lose a joyful family reunion through neglect of their relatives' Hereafter. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 84:13/q11 How does his joy contrast with the believer's experience?  [* · links]
- 84:13/q11/p1 The believer bears concern in the world and gains joy and reunion in the Hereafter; the heedless person's course is reversed. — Tabrisi contrasts the believer's religious concern and lasting joy with brief joy and unending grief. Ibn Zayd, reported by Qurtubi, says Paradise's people were fearful, sorrowful, tearful, and compassionate before reward, while Hell's people laughed and indulged; he cites 52:26–27. Abu Hayyan says the believer is sorrowful and reflective. Mujahid, reported by Durr, says the believer returns happily to family in Paradise. Study Quran cites the hadith that the world is the believer's prison and disbeliever's garden. Biqai describes omitted grief and self-accounting before the believer's family joy; Islahi says the doomed forfeit family reunion. [7 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 84:13/q12 How does this statement fit its surrounding passage and sura?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes point to different scales of context: the immediate division of outcomes, the paired fates within the passage, and continuity with the preceding sura.
- 84:13/q12/p1 It explains the fate of those given their records behind their backs within the passage's division of humanity by outcome. — Ibn Ashur says 84:7–15 details the return to God in 84:6: believers receive books in the right hand, polytheists behind their backs, and the passage divides them by outcome; he also mentions 84:14. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 84:13/q12/p2 It contrasts with the believer's easy reckoning and happy family return through an interlocking omission. — Biqai says easy reckoning, an earlier result, implies its opposite later, while the later cause of the doomed man's family joy implies the believer's earlier grief and self-accounting. Islahi says the doomed group's death wish, an effect, is placed before its cause to sharpen the contrast with the believers' reunion. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 84:13/q12/p3 It continues the preceding sura's account of reward and punishment for belief or denial of resurrection. — Biqai connects al-Inshiqaq to the close of al-Mutaffifin: allies are rewarded and enemies punished for denying resurrection and judgment before their Creator-King; he mentions 83:1. Study Quran compares the disbelievers' joy with 83:31. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 84:13/q13 Who are his family in the inward reading?  [أَهْلِهِۦ · inward]
turns on: Whether the inner 'family' denotes luminous spiritual or dark bodily faculties.
- 84:13/q13/p1 The luminous spiritual faculties corresponding to the upper spirit. — Qashani's inward reading presents this as one possible referent of the family. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 84:13/q13/p2 The dark bodily faculties corresponding to the lower self. — Bursevi also gives this as the other inward referent; the two alternatives correspond to upper spirit and lower self. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 84:13/q14 What does the report about Abu Talib's household say happened?  [* · reports]
- 84:13/q14/p1 During a famine Muhammad raised Ali and al-Abbas raised Jafar from Abu Talib's large household; Ali later followed Muhammad. — Ibn Hisham reports Mujahid's account. He also records a variant naming Aqil and Talib as the sons Abu Talib wanted to keep with him. The note states no connection to 84:13. [1 notes: IBNHISHAM(مجاهد)]

## 84:13/q15 What did the reports say about distributing or taking the Kaaba's treasure?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports describe different proposals and incidents, with disagreement over whether dedicated treasure could be redirected.
- 84:13/q15/p1 Umar considered distribution, but Ubayy denied his right to redistribute what God and the Messenger had assigned. — Azraqi reports Umar's proposal and Ubayy's objection twice, once under Umar and once under Ubayy. These notes state no connection to 84:13. [2 notes: AZRAQI(أبي بن كعب,عمر بن الخطاب)]
- 84:13/q15/p2 Ali challenged treating the treasure as booty because its owner would arrive at the end of time. — Azraqi reports Ali's challenge when Umar again proposed taking the treasure for good causes; Ali describes its future owner as tall and brown-complexioned. The note states no connection to 84:13. [1 notes: AZRAQI(علي بن أبي طالب)]
- 84:13/q15/p3 The Prophet and Abu Bakr left the dedicated gold untouched despite a proposal to spend it on war. — Azraqi reports 70,000 uqiyyas of gold dedicated to the Kaaba and Ali's wartime proposal. The note states no connection to 84:13. [1 notes: AZRAQI(وذكروا)]
- 84:13/q15/p4 Al-Husayn ibn al-Hasan seized much of the treasury in 200 AH for his side's war. — He argued that the Kaaba could not use idle money while his side needed it. Azraqi's note states no connection to 84:13. [1 notes: AZRAQI(الحسين ابن الحسن العلوي)]
- 84:13/q15/p5 Muhammad ibn Jafar borrowed 5,000 dinars in 200 AH, and al-Mamun later repaid the loan. — Azraqi reports the custodians lent the money for Muhammad's cause against a promise of repayment; after he abdicated, al-Mamun repaid them and they restored it to the treasury. The note states no connection to 84:13. [1 notes: AZRAQI(مسافع بن عبد الرحمن الحجبي)]

## 84:13/q16 What do the reports say about guarding and giving to the Kaaba treasury?  [* · reports]
turns on: These are separate reports about the treasury's persistence, risks of taking from it, and where gifts should go.
- 84:13/q16/p1 The treasury still contained the old treasure in 188 AH. — Azraqi reports the custodians' testimony. The note states no connection to 84:13. [1 notes: AZRAQI(بعض الحجبة)]
- 84:13/q16/p2 Taking its money was feared to bring loss, illustrated by a custodian's difficult death after taking 400 dinars. — Azraqi reports the dying custodian's confession, his father's pledge to replace 400 dinars from his best property, and the easing of the son's distress before death. Another report calls the treasure al-abraq and says custodians warned sons that mixing its money with their own brought loss, at least a difficult death. Neither note states a connection to 84:13. [2 notes: AZRAQI(عبد الله بن زرارة)]
- 84:13/q16/p3 A donor should give jewelry to needy people because the Kaaba already has wealth. — Azraqi reports Ibn Umar's rebuke to an Iraqi donor and its repetition to the narrator carrying jewelry. The note states no connection to 84:13. [1 notes: AZRAQI(ابن عمر)]

## 84:13/q17 What did Abu Bakr do while accompanying the Prophet to Thawr?  [* · reports]
- 84:13/q17/p1 He guarded the Prophet on the journey and at the cave entrance. — Azraqi reports Ibn Abi Mulayka's account that Abu Bakr alternated in front of and behind the Prophet for fear of attacks and entered the cave first to check it. Azraqi also relays that he put his foot over a hole plugged by a stone lest a creature emerge and harm the Prophet. Neither note states a connection to 84:13. [2 notes: AZRAQI(ابن أبي مليكة)]

## 84:13/q18 What did the Prophet say about acting despite one's written destination?  [* · reports]
- 84:13/q18/p1 Each person's place in Paradise or Hell is written, yet one must act because each is made easy for a course. — Bukhari gives two reports from Ali of the funeral conversation: the Prophet rejects relying passively on the written destination and cites the giving verse, 92:5; one note also mentions 92:6. Neither note states a connection to 84:13. [2 notes: BUKHARI(علي)]
