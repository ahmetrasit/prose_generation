# 12:26: 21 questions

## 12:26/q01 What does Joseph say she did?  [هى رَٰوَدَتْنِى عَن نَّفْسِى · interpretation]
- 12:26/q01/p1 She sought to seduce Joseph, while he refused and fled. — Al-Zajjaj states the reversal; al-Baghawi and Study Quran specify illicit sex and refusal; Ibn Kathir and al-Suddi describe her pursuit and pulling his shirt. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Bg); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); TAB-FULL(السدي); TABATABAI]

## 12:26/q02 Why did Joseph disclose her pursuit?  [قَالَ هى رَٰوَدَتْنِى · interpretation]
- 12:26/q02/p1 He answered her false accusation to clear himself; without it he would have kept silent. — Mawardi, Kashshaf, Tabarsi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Biqai, Tabatabai and others stress restraint until she accused him. Nawf says the slander angered him; Razi mentions protection of life and reputation; Baydawi mentions threatened prison or punishment; Alusi adds prophethood's trust and that he sought safety rather than exposure. Qurtubi says her claim that he loved her could not stand. [16 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(نوف); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(نوف الشامي); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(نوف الشامي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 12:26/q03 Why does Joseph say 'she' rather than address her directly?  [هِىَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether a present or mentally present person licenses a third-person pronoun without a previously stated antecedent.
- 12:26/q03/p1 The third person shows modesty, courtesy, and a wish to avoid direct reproach. — Samin calls it good etiquette instead of hādhihi or tilka; Biqai, Abu Hayyan, and Alusi connect it to restraint or turning away. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:26/q03/p2 The third-person pronoun can refer to a person physically or mentally present without a prior verbal antecedent. — Ibn Malik states the rule; Siraj al-Bulqini adds sensory or mental presence. Alusi prefers it, citing a litigant's statement, Moses before he is named in 28:26, and the legal force of hiyya taliq. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن مالك,السراج البلقيني); ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-]
- 12:26/q03/p3 The pronoun has an available contextual antecedent rather than being licensed by presence alone. — Abu Hayyan takes hiyya back to the woman's euphemistic ahlik and the pronoun in istajirhu back to Moses; Burhan says the antecedent is clear from the story. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 12:26/q04 What does the order of 'she pursued me' convey?  [هِىَ رَٰوَدَتْنِى · rhetoric]
- 12:26/q04/p1 Fronting 'she' assigns the pursuit to her and reverses her accusation. — Ibn Ashur identifies subject-before-verb restriction in the separated dialogue; Tabatabai calls the answer a reversal contrast. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 12:26/q04/p2 Joseph's plain, unemphatic reply shows calm assurance. — Tabatabai notes the absence of oath or emphatic language: he neither panicked nor flattered her. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 12:26/q06 Why was the witness described as from her family?  [مِّنْ أَهْلِهَآ · rhetoric]
- 12:26/q06/p1 A relative's statement was especially persuasive against the woman's claim. — Baydawi and Nasafi say God placed testimony on her family's tongue; Kashshaf says God stirred a relative to vindicate Joseph; Alusi says kinship removed suspicion, except that an infant's miracle would be conclusive regardless of kinship. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:26/q06/p2 His kinship did not prevent a fair judgment. — Kuranyolu says the family witness did not take sides despite the relationship; Ibn Ashur suggests he initially believed her, but the evidence reversed his attempt to substantiate her claim. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 12:26/q07 What does it mean that the witness 'testified'?  [وَشَهِدَ شَاهِدٌ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the word describes formal prior knowledge, inferential judgment, evidentiary function, or a miraculous spontaneous declaration.
- 12:26/q07/p1 He clarified or judged by an inference, with the shirt as actual evidence. — Wahidi-Basit calls witnessing figurative, citing God's testimony to oneness in 3:18 and the public display of disbelief; Mujahid calls it judgment. Ibn Atiyya and Tabatabai distinguish an inferred indication from ordinary perception-based testimony; Asad says he merely proposed the test. The conditional explains the testimony according to Ibn Ashur. [16 notes: FARRA; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 12:26/q07/p2 His conditional words count as testimony because they confirm Joseph and disprove her. — Kashshaf and Nasafi stress its evidentiary function; Alusi reports the same; Ibn Ashur extends this to the shirt itself; Tabatabai says testimony is a statement and allows the quotation. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 12:26/q07/p3 He may have heard speech or observed the scene and so had prior knowledge. — Ibn al-Anbari proposes words heard behind the door or from Joseph; Jubba'i argues the normal sense of witness requires such prior knowledge, favoring an adult. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL(الجبائي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:26/q07/p4 It may point to a spontaneous utterance by a speaking infant. — Tabatabai suggests that calling the declaration testimony can signal an unconsidered utterance, supporting the cradle report; he compares scripture's witnessing by hearing, sight, skin, and believers in 41:20 and 63:1. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 12:26/q08 How is the conditional sentence attached to 'testified'?  [وَشَهِدَ إِن كَانَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether shahida introduces quoted speech or itself denotes the clarifying judgment.
- 12:26/q08/p1 The conditional is direct speech, with an implied 'he said' or an omitted introductory anna. — Farra permits anna before in kāna and compares 4:11 and poetry; Kashshaf takes it as quoted or implied speech; Tabarsi compares the inheritance address; Samin gives Basran implied fa-qāla versus Kufan shahida meaning 'said'; Tabatabai says testimony itself functions as a statement. [6 notes: FARRA; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI]
- 12:26/q08/p2 The conditional is the inferential content of the witness's clarification. — Wahidi-Basit prefers this established commentators' view; Ibn Ashur says the shirt test explains what he testified. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNASHUR-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT+]

## 12:26/q09 How does 'a witness from her family' work grammatically?  [شَاهِدٌ مِّنْ أَهْلِهَآ · grammar]
- 12:26/q09/p1 'From her family' modifies 'witness' and makes the same-root subject informative. — Samin says shahida shāhid without the modifier would be tautological; Farra construes it as 'a speaker from her family'. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; FARRA]

## 12:26/q10 What does the conditional 'if his shirt was torn' mean in time?  [إِن كَانَ قَمِيصُهُۥ قُدَّ · grammar]
turns on: Whether in shifts past-form kāna into a future condition, whether an omitted expression supplies that force, or whether the condition can concern an already past state.
- 12:26/q10/p1 The past form expresses a future condition or future discovery of an already existing tear. — Nahhas says the conditional is jussive despite past form; Abu Ishaq glosses 'if it is known', citing Zuhayr; Kashshaf and Alusi make the discovery future. Burhan says in ordinarily projects to future and prefers past form replacing imperfect without changed meaning; Ibn al-Da'i cites 4:43. Tabarsi's al-Mubarrad says 'if it should be', and Abu Bakr al-Sarraj says 'if it becomes torn'. [15 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق,زهير); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(أبو بكر السراج,المبرد); QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); BURHAN(ابن الضائع)+; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT-]
- 12:26/q10/p2 An omitted saying or future auxiliary accounts for the past verb. — Al-Mazini posits omitted qawl; Ibn Asfur, Shalubin, and Ibn al-Sarraj posit a future conditional auxiliary, illustrated by 5:116. Ibn al-Da'i rejects the additional ellipsis; Ibn Khuruf instead supplies an omitted subject and clause for the conditional answer. [4 notes: NAHHAS(المازني); BURHAN(ابن السراج,ابن عصفور والشلوبين); ALUSI-FULL(ابن خروف); BURHAN(ابن الضائع)-]
- 12:26/q10/p3 Kāna is a broad basic verb that can stand for various actions, including 'be known'. — Muhammad ibn Yazid says it can express all actions; Abu Ishaq and Qurtubi construe this as knowledge yet to occur. Abu Bakr al-Razi lists eternity, ended past, present, future and becoming; Ibn Berri says an eternal antecedent quality cannot cease, while Mansur ibn Fallah makes continuity or cessation context-dependent. Burhan questions a blanket cessation reading. [7 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق,محمد بن يزيد); QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN(أبو بكر الرازي,ابن بري,منصور بن فلاح اليمني)]
- 12:26/q10/p4 Conditional kāna can retain genuine past reference. — Al-Mubarrad says the particle does not change kāna's tense; Burhan says the majority reject that exceptional rule and regards the verb as event and time. Samarrai says the shirt condition concerns a past or unrealized past, citing 10:41 and a prior promise; a poet's lament for sons supplies another challenge to an absolute future rule. [5 notes: BURHAN(المبرد); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(الشاعر,النحاة); BURHAN-]
- 12:26/q10/p5 The condition is an inferential challenge, not a sign of uncertainty. — Ibn al-Anbari compares 'if destiny is real, striving is futile'; Alusi likens it to a conditional reply to a boast of kindness. The issue is whether the known state of the shirt will be established to the hearers. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)]

## 12:26/q11 How are 'front' and 'back' read and analyzed?  [مِن قُبُلٍۢ · readings]
turns on: The vowel and tanwīn on directional nouns, and whether they are indefinite nouns, terminal adverbs with omitted possessors, or fixed names.
- 12:26/q11/p1 The common reading gives front and back ḍamma with tanwīn. — Ibn Atiyya reports the majority form; he also records other shortened genitive or unmarked endings. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 12:26/q11/p2 Some read min qablu and min duburu with ḍamma and no tanwīn, as terminal adverbs. — Nahhas attributes three ḍammas to Yahya ibn Ya'mar and Ibn Abi Ishaq; Tabrisi adds Nuh al-Qari and Ibn Jinni's suppressed possessor, comparing 30:4; Qurtubi similarly mentions omitted possessors and alternate endings. Kashshaf allows a silent middle radical. [6 notes: NAHHAS(يحيى بن يعمر وابن أبي إسحاق); TABRISI(ابن جني,ابن يعمر وابن أبي إسحاق ونوح القارئ); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 12:26/q11/p3 Some read the directional nouns with fatḥa or as fixed names. — Abu Ishaq allows fatḥa for both endpoints; Kashshaf attributes to Ibn Abi Ishaq a treatment as proper feminine names; Qurtubi reports diptote-like fatḥa. Ibn Atiyya notes an adverbial analysis that Abu Hatim calls poor Arabic. [4 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 12:26/q12 What do 'from the front' and the two uses of min mean?  [مِن قُبُلٍۢ مِنَ ٱلْكَٰذِبِينَ · meaning]
- 12:26/q12/p1 Qubl means the front or forepart; min marks where the tear begins, while in 'among the liars' it is partitive. — Damghani says Joseph's shirt was torn from the front, a stated variant to the later back-tear outcome; Tabarsi distinguishes the starting-point min from the partitive min. Alusi allows the front of Joseph or of the garment. [3 notes: DAMGHANI; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 12:26/q13 What would a front or rear tear prove?  [قَمِيصُهُۥ قُدَّ مِن قُبُلٍۢ فَصَدَقَتْ وَهُوَ مِنَ ٱلْكَٰذِبِينَ · interpretation]
- 12:26/q13/p1 A front tear favors her story and makes Joseph's denial false; a rear tear favors his flight and makes her accusation false. — Muqatil, Tabari, Mawardi and others explain that an aggressor approaching face-to-face would be torn in front, while a fleeing Joseph could be grabbed behind. Kashshaf and Baydawi add either defensive tearing by her or his front catching as he rushed after her. Ibn Ashur says a front tear fits her claim of holding him for punishment. The claim and denial have explicit and implied truth values according to Alusi. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي، مقاتل,الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن وعكرمة وقتادة والضحاك ومجاهد والسدي); BAYDAWI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عكرمة,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN(Kl)]

## 12:26/q14 What evidence and outcome settled the dispute?  [قَمِيصُهُۥ فَصَدَقَتْ وَهُوَ مِنَ ٱلْكَٰذِبِينَ · interpretation]
- 12:26/q14/p1 The rear tear established Joseph's account and exposed the woman's accusation. — Suddi says the cousin proposed the test at the door; Kashshaf says Qitfir recognized innocence; Durr says the husband called it women's plotting, told Joseph to turn away and his wife to seek forgiveness. Ibn Ashur sees divine favor when the test reversed the relative's apparent initial support of her. Tabatabai adds the pair racing to the door and perhaps the women’s astonishment and her later admission. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); KASHSHAF-FULL; DURR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 12:26/q14/p2 Multiple prior signs also favored Joseph before the shirt was inspected. — Razi lists his apparent slave status, flight, lack of adornment, long honorable conduct, explicit denial versus her vague charge, and reported impotence of her husband. The husband paused in light of these signs, and the tear reinforced the judgment. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 12:26/q15 Why does the test mention her truth first?  [فَصَدَقَتْ وَهُوَ مِنَ ٱلْكَٰذِبِينَ · rhetoric]
- 12:26/q15/p1 The sequence gently grants her possible truth and only indirectly calls Joseph a liar before reversing the case. — Burhan treats the order as considerate reasoning that leads the opponent toward the eventual conclusion. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 12:26/q16 What does 'he is among the liars' specify?  [وَهُوَ مِنَ ٱلْكَٰذِبِينَ · interpretation]
- 12:26/q16/p1 It would make Joseph false in his claim that she solicited him. — Alusi says lying concerns Joseph's specific counterclaim; he adds that truth and falsehood can apply to explicit statements and their implications. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 12:26/q17 Does the shirt test support legal judgment from circumstantial signs?  [قَمِيصُهُۥ قُدَّ · law]
turns on: Whether the witness was an adult drawing a fallible inference or an infant whose miraculous speech was conclusive.
- 12:26/q17/p1 Yes, an adult witness's reasoned use of the tear supports judging from indicators. — Ibn Atiyya cites jurists who use the verse; Qurtubi makes the legal inference conditional on an adult witness. He cites Malik on disputed goods held by thieves and Muhammad, Shurayh, and Iyas on allocating household property by its apparent use. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مالك,محمد)]
- 12:26/q17/p2 If the witness was a speaking infant, his miracle proves the case without establishing that legal inference. — Qurtubi distinguishes miraculous infant testimony from an adult's deduction; Nahhas and Jubba'i likewise say an infant's speech itself is stronger proof than the shirt. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL(الجبائي)]

## 12:26/q18 How does this shirt connect to Joseph's other shirts?  [قَمِيصُهُۥ · links]
- 12:26/q18/p1 Joseph's shirt serves as evidence at three narrative stages. — Mawardi names the false blood exposing the brothers in 12:18, the rear tear vindicating Joseph, and the shirt restoring Jacob's sight in 12:96. Sha'bi adds that a wolf would have torn the bloodied shirt and cites 12:93. Samarrai connects the shirt to slavery, prison and reunion. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الشعبي); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 12:26/q19 How many cradle-speakers include Joseph's witness, and how are the reports assessed?  [شَاهِدٌ · reports]
turns on: The competing transmitted lists and whether the Sahihayn's three-speaker report is exhaustive.
- 12:26/q19/p1 A four-speaker report includes Joseph's witness, Jesus, Jurayj's companion, and Pharaoh's hairdresser's child. — Reports attributed to Ibn Abbas, Abu Hurayra, and the Prophet give four, with variant wording and ordering. Alusi reports Suyuti's authentication through Ahmad, Ibn Hibban and al-Hakim. Suyuti says al-Tibi sought reconciliation; a narrow-versus-broad infancy reconciliation is called strained by Alusi. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس، أبو هريرة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير والضحاك); KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(رسول الله); ALUSI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,جلال السيوطي); ALUSI-FULL(جلال السيوطي)+; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي)-]
- 12:26/q19/p2 Some lists name three speakers and create tension with the four-speaker report. — Abu Hurayra names Jesus, Joseph's companion and Jurayj's companion; Hilal says the witness was one of three. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan note that Bukhari and Muslim list only three, including a nursing infant instead of Joseph's witness; al-Tibi objects on that basis. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو هريرة); WAHIDI-BASIT(هلال بن يساف); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وأبو هريرة وابن جبير وهلال بن يساف والضحاك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة); ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي)]
- 12:26/q19/p3 An expanded account names five or as many as eleven cradle-speakers. — Ibn Atiyya mentions a report adding Pharaoh's hairdresser's son as fifth; Suyuti adds a nursing infant and a People of the Ditch child and gathers eleven in a poem, while noting Zamakhshari's fifth. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(جلال السيوطي)]

## 12:26/q20 How does the verse relate to the wider story and other scriptures?  [* · links]
- 12:26/q20/p1 The Qur'anic episode differs from Genesis and illustrates divine direction through events. — Asad says Genesis lacks 12:26–34 and has the husband believe the accusation immediately and imprison Joseph; the Qur'anic sura illustrates God's hidden direction, compared with 2:216. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 12:26/q20/p2 The wife's later admission and Joseph's later authority illuminate the outcome. — Ibn Ata says she was not yet wholly absorbed in love, later preferred Joseph and confessed in 12:51. Burhan contrasts her desire and failed worldly power with Joseph's obedience and eventual authority in 12:56. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطاء); BURHAN]
- 12:26/q20/p3 The passage's arrangement calls for contemplation as part of Qur'anic inimitability. — Burhan urges attention to its order and compares 40:28. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 12:26/q21 What other nearby wording and linguistic examples do the sources note?  [* · links]
- 12:26/q21/p1 Related remarks concern adjacent accusations, cuts, and other cited readings. — Abu Hayyan says bisūʾin need mean only unpleasant conduct, even harsh address or striking. He and Samin report Zayd ibn Ali's accusative aw ʿadhāban alīman, with al-Kisai supplying 'be punished'; Samin analyzes mā, man, illā an yusjan and aw. Samin also reports qut instead of qudd, Yaqub's distinction for intact skin or cloth, and a poetry example. Bint Shati compares unique qidada in 72:11 with the shirt's tearing. Nashr records assimilation examples from al-Kahf and 72:3 and a class of vowel patterns at other verses. [10 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زيد بن علي); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية,زيد بن علي,يعقوب); BINTSHATI-IJAZ; NASHR]
