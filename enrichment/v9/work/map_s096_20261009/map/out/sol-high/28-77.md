# 28:77: 31 questions

## 28:77/q01 What should Qārūn seek with God's gifts?  [وَٱبْتَغِ فِيمَآ ءَاتَىٰكَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلدَّارَ ٱلْءَاخِرَةَ · interpretation]
- 28:77/q01/p1 Use God's gifts, especially wealth, in obedience, charity and other good works to gain Paradise and its reward. — Muqātil calls the goal the Garden; al-Ṭabarī says obedience now; al-Suddī names charity, nearness to God and kinship; al-Wāḥidī adds gratitude. ʿAlī names health, strength, youth and wealth. Al-Kashshāf and Ibn ʿĀshūr include obligatory and recommended works; al-Biqāʿī says possessions perish. Islahi says wealth tests whether one serves God or wealth. Study Quran applies the counsel generally. [39 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(السدي); THALABI(علي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,علي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(علي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(علي); DURR-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI(علي,كشف الأسرار); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 28:77/q02 How forceful is the command to seek?  [وَٱبْتَغِ · rhetoric]
- 28:77/q02/p1 It calls for strenuous pursuit. — Al-Biqāʿī emphasizes the effort in ibtaghi. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 28:77/q03 What alternate reading of seek is reported?  [وَٱبْتَغِ · readings]
- 28:77/q03/p1 Wa-ttabiʿ, follow, is reported instead of wa-btaghi. — Ibn al-Jawzī names Abū al-Mutawakkil and Ibn al-Sumayfiʿ and describes the doubled tāʾ, kasra on bāʾ and still undotted ʿayn; al-Kashshāf and al-Ālūsī also report it. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو المتوكل وابن السميفع); ALUSI-FULL]

## 28:77/q04 How does fīmā ātāka Allāh work?  [فِيمَآ ءَاتَىٰكَ ٱللَّهُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether fī locates the pursuit within the gift or expresses its use as a means.
- 28:77/q04/p1 Fī locates the pursuit, literally or figuratively, within God's gifts. — Al-Samīn attaches the phrase to ibtaghi or an omitted circumstantial phrase. Al-Bursawī contrasts fīmā with bimā: act while owning wealth and having bodily capacity. Al-Ālūsī allows acting within wealth. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 4:5 and poetry for figurative location in the greater part of the treasure. [6 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:77/q04/p2 Fī marks the means or cause: spending God's gift secures the afterlife. — Al-Ālūsī gives this as an alternative. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 28:77/q05 What is mā in fīmā ātāka?  [فِيمَآ ءَاتَىٰكَ ٱللَّهُ · grammar]
- 28:77/q05/p1 It is either a verbal noun particle or a relative meaning that which. — Al-Samīn gives both parses. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 28:77/q06 What is the worldly share one must not forget?  [وَلَا تَنسَ نَصِيبَكَ مِنَ ٱلدُّنْيَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the share is enduring deeds, permitted enjoyment, limited subsistence, or what physically remains at death; some authors explicitly prefer or reject a reading.
- 28:77/q06/p1 It is good action during one's lifetime that endures as benefit in the afterlife. — Mujāhid identifies obedience rewarded later; Ibn ʿAbbās says work here for the hereafter; Ibn Zayd says send deeds ahead. Some specify lifetime, health, youth and strength as opportunities. Al-Sulamī reports sincerity and guarding sanctities. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says only what remains in the next life is one's true share. Elmalılı and Islahi reject a transient-enjoyment interpretation; Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes this makes the admonition stern. [46 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,عون بن عبد الله,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم,مجاهد); SULAMI(أبو عثمان,الجنيد,سفيان الثوري); THALABI(مجاهد وابن زيد؛ ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الوالبي,مجاهد,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد وابن زيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس والجمهور); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس ومجاهد والجمهور); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس والجمهور); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد,منصور); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد,منصور); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 28:77/q06/p2 It is lawful provision, enjoyment and prudent worldly welfare. — Qatāda and al-Ḥasan stress lawful pursuit; Mālik permits living, eating and drinking without extravagance. Ibn ʿAtiyya prefers gentler counsel that does not alienate Qārūn; Ibn al-ʿArabī calls Qatāda's lawful-share reading best. Ibn Kathīr names food, clothing, housing and marriage, alongside rights of God, self, family and guests. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a personal right after paying God's due. Study Quran says it counters severe asceticism. Elmalılı and Islahi reject this reading. [49 notes: MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(آخرون,ابن جريج,الحسن,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(الحسن,قتادة,مالك); THALABI(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن وقتادة,مالك); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,ابن عطية,الحسن وقتادة,مالك); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن,الزمخشري,مالك); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة,مالك); IBNASHUR-FULL(قتادة,مالك); TABATABAI(قيل); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(others); IBNATIYYA-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)+; ELMALILI(bazıları)-; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Our exegetes)-]
- 28:77/q06/p3 Keep enough for yourself and your family, and give the surplus away. — Al-Ḥasan repeatedly gives this counsel, sometimes specifying a year's subsistence; Manṣūr names family provision; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the small food, drink and clothing view good, dismissing alternatives as contextually unsuited. [25 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن); TAB-FULL(الحسن); IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); SULAMI(بعضهم); THALABI(منصور بن زاذان); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زاذان,الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن,منصور بن زاذان); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); DURR-FULL(الحسن); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 28:77/q06/p4 It is ultimately a shroud, coffin or grave: accumulated wealth is left behind. — Al-Thaʿlabī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya give the shroud, the latter citing poetry; al-Qurṭubī cites poems about two shrouds and embalming materials or cotton and a shroud. Al-Bursawī cites Kāshifī and Saʿdī; Elmalılı quotes Persian verse. [12 notes: THALABI(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الثعلبي); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(الشيخ سعدي,الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL(الشاعر); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 28:77/q06/p5 It is charitable good given away rather than property left to heirs. — Al-Jishumī distinguishes spending from property retained until death; al-Suddī names charity and kinship. [2 notes: JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي)]
- 28:77/q06/p6 It is refusal to be deceived by a world that never endures. — An unnamed inward view in al-Sulamī warns against settling into worldly life. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 28:77/q06/p7 For a gnostic it is fragrance, women and the delight of prayer. — Al-Bursawī reports these as signs of good scent, beauty and joy of heart. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعض العارفين)]

## 28:77/q07 How can worldly provisions be used inwardly?  [نَصِيبَكَ مِنَ ٱلدُّنْيَا · inward]
- 28:77/q07/p1 Use them for bodily obedience, heartfelt knowledge of God, verbal remembrance and inward witnessing. — Al-Qushayrī reports this inward reading of the worldly share. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 28:77/q08 What does do not forget contribute?  [وَلَا تَنسَ · rhetoric]
- 28:77/q08/p1 It means not to abandon or neglect one's portion. — Abū ʿUbayda and Ibn Qutayba gloss it as not leaving; al-Bursawī describes nisyān as loss through weak memory, distraction or deliberate neglect; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it figurative neglect. [4 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:77/q09 How does the negative command function?  [وَلَا تَنسَ · grammar]
- 28:77/q09/p1 It is prohibitive in form but permits taking a worldly share. — Ibn al-Jawzī calls this a prohibitive use of lā. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the wāw parenthetical and says the clause softens the exhortation so Qārūn does not think all pleasure forbidden. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:77/q10 What does naṣīb mean?  [نَصِيبَكَ · meaning]
- 28:77/q10/p1 An allotted share or portion, with the possessive indicating a personal right. — Ibn ʿĀshūr derives it from naṣb, something set out and distinguished, and applies the right to fitting lawful enjoyment. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:77/q11 How should one do good as God has done good?  [وَأَحْسِن كَمَآ أَحْسَنَ ٱللَّهُ إِلَيْكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether goodness is directed to God's creatures, expressed as obedient gratitude to God, or given an unusual reported gloss.
- 28:77/q11/p1 Show generosity and kindness to people and other creatures with God's gifts. — Al-Ṭabarī says spend wealth properly; Muqātil names charity; Ibn ʿAṭiyya singles out the needy. Al-Rāzī extends kindness to status, cheerful face, welcome and good mention. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the omitted object extends to self, people, animals and all creatures one can help; a hadith prescribes excellence in everything. Islahi says divine graciousness should be reflected in people. [28 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد); THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الشيخ سعدي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:77/q11/p2 Answer God's blessings with worship, gratitude, obedience and fulfilled duties. — Ibn ʿAbbās says worship; Yaḥyā ibn Sallām says perform obligations; al-Qurṭubī cites the hadith defining iḥsān as worshipping as if one sees God. Ibn al-ʿArabī says the glosses converge in using blessings in obedience. Al-Rāzī cites 14:7's increase for gratitude. Al-Qushayrī says belief is prerequisite for Qārūn's good deeds. [17 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد,يحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(يحيى بن سلام); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 28:77/q11/p3 Seek lawful provision as God made lawful goods available. — Al-Māwardī offers the gloss, repeated by Ibn al-Jawzī. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي)]
- 28:77/q11/p4 Do good to the Fire as God did good to you. — Al-Baghawī reports this unexplained alternative. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل)]
- 28:77/q11/p5 An unexplained gloss is do not cut off. — Al-Māwardī provides no object or explanation. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 28:77/q12 What inward good is called for?  [وَأَحْسِن كَمَآ أَحْسَنَ ٱللَّهُ إِلَيْكَ · inward]
turns on: The inward focus may be humble recognition of grace or full devotion.
- 28:77/q12/p1 Recognize divine favor without thinking oneself deserving. — Al-Qushayrī reports this inward sense. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 28:77/q12/p2 Turn wholly to God in sincere worship. — Al-Sulamī transmits al-Qayyim's call to honor knowledge of God and answer gifts of faith and service with dutiful worship. [1 notes: SULAMI(القيم)]

## 28:77/q13 What does the kāf in as God did good express?  [كَمَآ أَحْسَنَ ٱللَّهُ إِلَيْكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether causation is a distinct sense of kāf or follows from comparison.
- 28:77/q13/p1 It compares the two acts of beneficence, with causation arising from comparison. — Ibn ʿĀshūr takes mā as a verbal noun and says each blessing calls for corresponding gratitude; he cites 2:198 and acknowledges grammarians' causal terminology but denies an independent causal sense. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:77/q13/p2 It can directly mean because: do good because God favored you. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī allow this alternative; Ibn ʿĀshūr argues against treating it as independent. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 28:77/q14 What corruption is Qārūn warned against?  [وَلَا تَبْغِ ٱلْفَسَادَ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether fasād is broad disobedience, Qārūn's oppression, misuse of wealth, idolatrous invitation or coin clipping.
- 28:77/q14/p1 Disobedience and sins in the land. — Muqātil says sin in response to God's favors; al-Baghawī generalizes that every disobedient person seeks corruption. [7 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 28:77/q14/p2 Qārūn's continuing oppression, injustice and transgression against others. — Al-Ṭabarī says his people warned him against oppressing them because of wealth. Al-Bayḍāwī includes any act leading to injustice and calls it rebuke of prior wrongs. Al-Ālūsī says stop persisting; Ibn ʿĀshūr warns that helping some does not excuse harming others. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 28:77/q14/p3 Wealth abuse such as miserliness, hoarding, extravagance and arrogance. — Al-Biqāʿī names miserliness, extravagance and belittling others; Elmalılı says trapping wealth itself is corruption. Islahi says theft and robbery are outward signs, while arrogance and violating God's rights are the core. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes misuse of status and influence. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]
- 28:77/q14/p4 Inviting others to worship someone besides God. — Al-Wāḥidī reports al-Kalbī's gloss. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي)]
- 28:77/q14/p5 Clipping or cutting gold and silver. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib's identification. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن المسيب)]

## 28:77/q15 What inward corruption is warned against?  [ٱلْفَسَادَ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ · inward]
- 28:77/q15/p1 Misusing spiritual potential against religious law corrupts the land of the spirit. — Al-Bursawī transmits the Najmī interpretation: following nature damages human and spiritual preparedness. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 28:77/q16 What can fī al-arḍ attach to?  [فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ · grammar]
- 28:77/q16/p1 It may depend on tabghi, al-fasād, or an omitted circumstance. — Al-Samīn calls the omitted circumstantial analysis remote. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 28:77/q17 Which earth is meant by in the earth?  [ٱلْأَرْضِ · referent]
- 28:77/q17/p1 The land inhabited by Qārūn's people, a part of the globe. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says corruption in that area counts as corruption within the earth as a whole. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:77/q18 Why is earth singular here and elsewhere?  [ٱلْأَرْضِ · rhetoric]
- 28:77/q18/p1 It denotes the lower realm or a collective abode, rather than counted earths. — Al-Zarkashī contrasts earth with the plural heavens in 65:12: their distinct entities suit a plural, while earth is the lower realm, like a pebble beside a desert. He calls earth this world's abode; one view excludes the heavens from the world. Heaven too is singular for encompassing height, as in 67:16-17 and 10:61, but plural in 34:3 for full dominion. A hadith of wrongful land taking treats seven earths as countable tracts. [7 notes: BURHAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 28:77/q19 What does God's not loving corrupters mean?  [إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يُحِبُّ ٱلْمُفْسِدِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether lack of love is expressed as rejection of deeds and favor or as lack of nearness.
- 28:77/q19/p1 God rejects their acts and does not approve, favor or honor them. — Ibn ʿAbbās mentions deeds and practice; al-Bayḍāwī attributes divine dislike to evil acts. Al-Biqāʿī denies worldly prosperity proves divine favor. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the clause explains the ban; Islahi likens corrupters to weeds destined for removal. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:77/q19/p2 God does not draw near to corrupters. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits Sufyān ibn ʿUyayna's lā yaqrub; al-Māwardī attributes it to Ibn Qutayba. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سفيان بن عيينة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن قتيبة)]

## 28:77/q20 What does divine dislike imply about willing corruption?  [إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يُحِبُّ ٱلْمُفْسِدِينَ · theology]
- 28:77/q20/p1 God does not will corruption; if He created it, He would will it. — Al-Jishumī uses the clause to refute the Mujabbira account of creation and will. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 28:77/q21 Who delivered the admonition to Qārūn?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural counsel is attributed to believing kinsmen or Moses.
- 28:77/q21/p1 Believers among Qārūn's people or kinsmen addressed him. — Al-Ṭabrisī calls this usual; al-Ālūsī says it is the verse's apparent sense. Kur'an Yolu describes believers enlightened by faith; Islahi calls them learned men. [5 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:77/q21/p2 Moses addressed him, in the plural form. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī report this alternative. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 28:77/q22 Why did Qārūn fail to heed the counsel?  [* · interpretation]
- 28:77/q22/p1 Admonition could not benefit one denied acceptance, and Qārūn answered with ingratitude. — Al-Qushayrī compares advice to seed on saline ground; al-Rāzī calls the counsel unimprovable but says Qārūn claimed wealth came from his own knowledge. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL]

## 28:77/q23 What happened during Qārūn's sinking?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports recount different episodes and grounds for his sinking or its halt.
- 28:77/q23/p1 The earth swallowed him at Moses' prayer; Qārūn appealed to Moses through kinship, not directly to God. — Al-Qushayrī says Moses continued commanding the earth to take him. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 28:77/q23/p2 When Jonah's fish reached Qārūn, God stopped his sinking because he asked about Moses. — Al-Qushayrī says the fish circled the seas and God credited Qārūn's kinship concern. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 28:77/q24 What did Qārūn believe?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the religious counsel and divine gift presuppose belief.
- 28:77/q24/p1 He acknowledged the afterlife and was an Israelite monotheist, yet doubted Moses' promises and prescriptions. — Al-Rāzī infers an acknowledged afterlife from the counsel; Ibn ʿĀshūr describes his religious affiliation and doubt. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:77/q24/p2 He was an unbeliever who nevertheless received worldly blessings. — Al-Qushayrī says an unbeliever has no good deed in the relevant sense but may receive worldly gifts. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 28:77/q25 How do the counsel's clauses work together?  [* · rhetoric]
- 28:77/q25/p1 Arrangement makes disparate admonitions unusually cohesive. — Al-Bāqillānī says five semantically distant clauses become harmonious, compares 28:68 and 28:58, and praises Quranic narrative compression of admonition, proverb, wisdom, oneness and transcendence. Ibn ʿĀshūr says beneficence is singled out to prepare the appeal to God's favor; the corruption ban bars kindness to some from covering harm to others. Al-Biqāʿī says permitted enjoyment needs a restraint on greed. [6 notes: BAQILLANI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:77/q26 How does the episode fit neighboring verses and the sūra?  [* · links]
- 28:77/q26/p1 It displays God's rule and the danger of relying on wealth after scenes of helplessness in the hereafter. — Al-Biqāʿī connects prior powerless partners and 29:41's spider house and recalls God's rights taught by messengers. Al-Ālūsī compares 28:76's arrogant rejoicers; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 2:205's corruption warning. Islahi says Qārūn's fate shows gifts are from God, not skill or idols. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 28:77/q27 What broader counsel on worldly life is transmitted with the verse?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The sayings emphasize either life's brevity or balance with lawful worldly needs.
- 28:77/q27/p1 Use temporary life for the afterlife and avoid destructive attachment to abundance. — Al-Bāqillānī reports that afterlife focus brings inner sufficiency while world focus brings poverty and disorder; life is a test and little remains. ʿAlī calls life preparation for a contest, warns of desires and long hopes, and values good deeds above gains. Ibn Qutayba explains spring growth as warning of excess. Al-Sulamī makes the world serve the afterlife and God; al-Biqāʿī adds ascetic nearness; al-Bursawī's couplet says treasure remains only when given away. [13 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; BAQILLANI(النبي,علي); SULAMI(أبو عثمان); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 28:77/q27/p2 Cultivate lawful worldly welfare while pursuing the afterlife. — Ibn ʿUmar says work for this world as if living forever and for the next as if dying tomorrow; Study Quran records alternative attribution to Prophet or ʿAlī. ## 28:77/q28 Which report urges seizing opportunities before they pass?  [* · reports]
- 28:77/q28/p1 The Prophet urged taking youth, health, wealth, free time and life before their opposites. — Al-Baghawī reports it through ʿAmr ibn Maymūn and calls it mursal; al-Bursawī repeats it. Al-Rāzī reports a related exhortation to prepare before old age and death, after which Paradise or Hell remains. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(رسول الله); RAZI-FULL(النبي)]

- 28:77/q29/p1 Transmit religious knowledge, since its receiver may understand better. — Al-Bāqillānī reports the prophetic exhortation. [1 notes: BAQILLANI(النبي)]

[1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 28:77/q30 What ancillary lexical and proverbial explanations appear with these notes?  [* · meaning]
turns on: These are separate ancillary lexical observations.
- 28:77/q30/p1 The hundred camels saying distinguishes a herd from the rare conspicuous riding camel. — Ibn Qutayba calls the pasture herd hunayda, identifies the riding camel by maturity and appearance, and takes the comparison to teach equal law and retaliation regardless of rank, like the comb-teeth and donkey-teeth sayings. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]
- 28:77/q30/p2 Ḥabaṭ is a camel's belly swelling from overgrazing; yulam means nearly killing it. — Ibn Qutayba explains these terms in the warning about destructive spring growth. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]
- 28:77/q30/p3 Settling like a gazelle means staying quietly inactive in a house. — Ibn Qutayba compares this with a gazelle in its lair. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]
- 28:77/q30/p4 Clothed yet naked describes thin garments that do not cover. — Ibn Qutayba applies it to women. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]
- 28:77/q30/p5 A closed bag in a peace document signifies a loyal heart free of rancor. — Ibn Qutayba calls chests ʿiyāb and cites an unnamed poet. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]
- 28:77/q30/p6 Few is a relative quantity without a fixed measure. — Ibn al-Jawzī contrasts it with many; he also says usury's growth tends to loss, records a proverb for unknown lineage, describes qilla as an unspecified lifted amount with a drinking poem and as a mountain peak, and says istaqalla means departing. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 28:77/q30/p7 Lā can negate and can convey lam. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites a poem and separately notes prohibition in this verse. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 28:77/q30/p8 The root ḥ-s-n also conveys beauty and virtue. — Study Quran notes the root's semantic range. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 28:77/q31 What ancillary classification and reciter notices are supplied?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The notices concern separate facts.
- 28:77/q31/p1 The entire Sūrat al-Qaṣaṣ is Meccan. — Ibn al-Jawzī attributes this to al-Ḥasan, ʿAṭāʾ and ʿIkrima. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وعطاء وعكرمة)]
- 28:77/q31/p2 Ibn al-Naffākh was a trustworthy Egyptian hadith scholar of simple life who died in 314 AH. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports Ibn Yūnus's notice. [1 notes: NASHR(ابن يونس)]
- 28:77/q31/p3 Ibn Nahshal was a precise and virtuous recitation imam at Isfahan's mosque who died in 294 AH. — Ibn al-Jazarī records his office and reputation. [1 notes: NASHR]
