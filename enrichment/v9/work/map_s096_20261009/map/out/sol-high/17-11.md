# 17:11: 20 questions

## 17:11/q01 What does يَدْعُ mean here?  [وَيَدْعُ · meaning]
- 17:11/q01/p1 It means to ask or seek something, whether by spoken prayer or by action. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Labīd's poetic use for seeking. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says all pursuit is a request of God, who alone gives and withholds, citing 55:29 and 14:34. Elmalılı and Ālūsī allow deeds that invite punishment as a figurative request. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 17:11/q02 Why is the final wāw absent from وَيَدْعُ?  [وَيَدْعُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the graphic omission merely follows liaison, eases adjacent letters, or also carries rhetorical force.
- 17:11/q02/p1 The underlying wāw remains grammatically, but connected pronunciation drops it before the following still lām, and the spelling follows pronunciation. — Al-Farrāʾ cites 38:16, 4:146, 40:32, 77:6 and poetry, including كفاك كف and تخف for تخفي; Thaʿlabī and Qurṭubī add 96:18, 42:24, 50:41, 54:5. Naḥḥās limits loss of a singular root wāw to jussive or liaison and restores it at pause outside Qurʾānic orthography. Al-Farrāʾ, as reported by Rāzī and Ibn ʿĀshūr, says writing the wāw would also be sound. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Nasafī, Ṭabrisī and Abū Ḥayyān stress spelling according to recitation; Rāzī and Ālūsī infer faithful transmission of the heard text. [20 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 17:11/q02/p2 The spelling omits one of two adjacent wāws for ease. — Burhān distinguishes the essential root wāw from the adjacent wāw and treats the written deletion as an easing of their sequence. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 17:11/q02/p3 The omission iconically signals the speed and ease of human action, especially rushing to ask for evil. — Burhān and al-Marrākushī in Itqān extend this account to four verbs and their agents and objects. Biqāʿī says the truncated spelling evokes foolish desire tending to ruin, while noting that pronunciation need not omit the wāw. [5 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN(المراكشي); BIQAI-FULL]

## 17:11/q03 How may a reader pause on وَيَدْعُ and comparable written omissions?  [وَيَدْعُ · readings]
turns on: Whether pause follows the written form, an attested restoration of the underlying wāw, or should be avoided.
- 17:11/q03/p1 Pause according to the codex spelling, without restoring the omitted letter. — Ibn al-Jazarī says all readers pause this way at the four wāw omissions; Itqān states the general rule of matching the ʿUthmānic spelling, while acknowledging transmitted exceptions. Burhān records this practice for يمح. [4 notes: NASHR(جميع القراء); ITQAN(أبو عمرو); BURHAN(يعقوب)]
- 17:11/q03/p2 Some transmitted routes restore the underlying wāw at pause. — Al-Dānī transmits Yaʿqūb through two teachers, though Ibn al-Jazarī calls it an individual route; Itqān lists a restored wāw in yadʿu al-insān, yawm yadʿu al-dāʿ and sanadʿu al-zabāniya among exceptions. Burhān records Yaʿqūb restoring wāw in يمح and says omission there is not jussive. [3 notes: NASHR(يعقوب عن أبي الفتح وأبي الحسن); ITQAN; BURHAN(يعقوب)]
- 17:11/q03/p3 One should avoid pausing on these forms because the pause would violate either spelling or the underlying form. — Makkī and others give this caution; Ibn al-Jazarī objects that emergency stops are allowed and sound transmission can justify a form even where it departs from spelling or underlying morphology. [1 notes: NASHR(مكي وغيره); NASHR-]

## 17:11/q04 Which wider weak-root and deletion patterns are brought into the discussion of يَدْعُ?  [وَيَدْعُ · grammar]
turns on: The notes supply different morphological analogies, most of which discuss other forms rather than this word directly.
- 17:11/q04/p1 The imperfect يَدْعُ can follow a regular pattern even when its corresponding perfect is unattested. — Sībawayh says يدع and يذر are treated as if ودعت and وذرت existed, supported by frequent usage. Khalīl compares unattested bases for استحيت with يدع and يذر; Sībawayh discusses further analogies from آي, غاية, آية, حاي, استحوذت, استطيبت, أول, آءة, يوم, حيوة and لويت. Analyses of استحيت differ on whether one yāʾ or both are deleted and vowels transferred. [12 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل,غيره)]
- 17:11/q04/p2 Weak radicals and frequent usage allow various substitutions, assimilations and irregular omissions; the cited paradigms supply morphological analogies. — Sībawayh analyzes doubled waw roots by turning a waw to yāʾ, avoiding first and final waw together, and compares يديت, الوزوزة, الوحوحة, غزوت, رميت, أحييت, حييت, اقتتل, احواوت, احواويت, احوييت, احوويت, شي, صيد, بيض, لي, ريا, رية, معاي and معايا. He explains لم أبل from باليت by consecutive quiescents and frequency, reports Khalīl's لم أبله, and distinguishes لا أبالي. These examples are wider morphology, not direct variant readings of this verse. [29 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل,العرب,بعض العرب,من قال ريا,يونس)]
- 17:11/q04/p3 Guttural radicals influence vowel patterns, with dialectal and exceptional forms illustrating how an imperfect may differ from a presumed regular paradigm. — Sībawayh sets four regular vowel patterns and faʿīl alternatives; Tamīm uses initial kasra, while the Hijaz follows analogy. He discusses رؤف and رؤوف, بئيس, مغيرة, معين, إحب, نحب, يحب, and أجيء, including effects of vowel assimilation and unattested perfects. [9 notes: SIBAWAYH(أهل الحجاز,بعض العرب,تميم)]

## 17:11/q05 Who is ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ who calls for evil?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular is generic, identifies a disbelieving group or individual, or uses Adam as the ancestral example.
- 17:11/q05/p1 Human beings generally, especially when angry or distressed. — Zajjāj explicitly calls it a human-wide trait; Ibn al-Jawzī transmits him. Wāḥidī, Mujāhid and Qatāda call this the common explanation. Ālūsī allows the general term to describe some moments or a subset; Bursevī similarly says a species-wide attribution can concern some people or occasions. Ṭabāṭabāʾī insists the referent is all humanity, and Rāzī prefers the generic lesson even if Adam is intended. [15 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد وقتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج وغيره); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 17:11/q05/p2 The disbeliever or a disbelieving group that demands punishment. — Zamakhsharī and Nasafī depict mockery; Abū Ḥayyān includes deniers of the afterlife and Quraysh. Ālūsī prefers this contextual reading of the first occurrence, while Ibn ʿĀshūr calls 'the human' a common Qurʾānic expression for the unbeliever, citing 19:66–67. Ṭabāṭabāʾī explicitly disagrees with limiting the referent to unbelievers. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(tefsirler); ALUSI-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 17:11/q05/p3 Al-Naḍr ibn al-Ḥārith in particular. — Muqātil and reports from Ibn ʿAbbās link him to the demand for stones or punishment in 8:32. Zamakhsharī says he was executed; Bayḍāwī specifies captivity at Badr. Qurṭubī frames the identification as an occasion of revelation; al-Dāmaghānī also names him. [10 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); DAMGHANI; KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 17:11/q05/p4 Adam, whose haste stands for that of his descendants. — Some commentators take the noun specifically as Adam; Ibn al-Anbārī says his mention represents his progeny. The incomplete animation and attempts to rise are used as evidence. Abū Ḥayyān objects that the wording does not fit; Ālūsī says it can fit but prefers general humanity. [8 notes: THALABI(قوم من المفسرين); MAWARDI-FULL(إبراهيم والضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); MUQATIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سلمان الفارسي، ابن عباس)-]

## 17:11/q06 What evil is being called for بِٱلشَّرِّ, and how does it compare with good?  [بِٱلشَّرِّ بِٱلْخَيْرِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether evil is an angry spoken curse, unsuspected harm, demanded punishment, unlawful or short-sighted pursuit, or the consequence of action; the hardship/ease reading also depends on the bāʾ.
- 17:11/q06/p1 People angrily curse themselves, family, children, servants, animals or property, despite not wanting the harm fulfilled. — Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid and many authors name different targets. Ibn ʿAbbās gives 'God curse him' and 'be angry with him'; Ḥumayd ibn Hilāl rebukes cursing mounts or sheep. Al-Ḥasan says people later ask for good. 10:11 is repeatedly cited for the peril of an immediate answer. [43 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(حميد بن هلال,قتادة); FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد وقتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن عباس، وقتادة، ومجاهد، والأصم، وأبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس، مجاهد، قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس والحسن وقتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); SAMIN-DURR+]
- 17:11/q06/p2 People ask for something they take to be good, though its real consequence is harmful. — Rāzī blames haste and surface appearances; Bayḍāwī and Bursevī allow this alongside curses. Kur'an Yolu, Study Quran and Asad invoke 2:216: human judgment can reverse good and bad, while God knows; divine guidance supplies the objective standard. Ṭabāṭabāʾī defines good and evil as true felicity and misery. [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI]
- 17:11/q06/p3 Disbelievers ask mockingly for threatened punishment to arrive, as eagerly as they would seek a benefit. — Abū Muslim cites 8:32, 22:47 and 13:6; Quraysh asked for stones, though guidance and mercy would have served them. Ibn ʿĀshūr reads the verbal noun as comparing impatient expectation of threatened evil and promised good; the threat has an appointed time. Islahi sees the demand as rejecting respite for reform. [21 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(tefsirler); STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 17:11/q06/p4 People seek forbidden things as eagerly as lawful things. — Ṭabrisī reports the contrast of forbidden and permitted pursuits. Qurṭubī quotes Ibn Jāmiʿ's poem about a worshipper wanting a woman borne in a camel litter and supplies al-Jawharī's meanings of maḥmil: support, a type of litter, or sword strap. [4 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الجوهري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 17:11/q06/p5 People choose a small immediate advantage over larger lasting reward, even at future cost. — Māwardī says present gain can cause afterlife harm; Qurṭubī contrasts a small immediate benefit with a great lasting reward. Elmalılı relates the preference to the next passage on immediate life in 17:18. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 17:11/q06/p6 People urgently pray for relief in hardship but neglect prayer and praise in ease. — Ibn ʿAbbās links this with 10:12 and calls remembrance and obedience in good times better. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the contrast; Abū Ḥayyān reports it with bāʾ meaning 'in' but rejects it because دعاءه presupposes an actual prayer for good. Samīn calls the construction remote. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 17:11/q06/p7 Sinful acts can amount to calling for the punishment they bring. — Ālūsī explicitly calls this a figurative reading and says persistent acts make punishment deserved. Elmalılı says people invite evil through deeds; Bursevī says hastiness appears in action as well as speech. Ṭabāṭabāʾī's wider definition of request includes pursuit. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 17:11/q07 How are the two bāʾ prepositions construed?  [بِٱلشَّرِّ بِٱلْخَيْرِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether bāʾ marks the requested content, the circumstance of prayer, or its cause.
- 17:11/q07/p1 Both attach to the prayer as the evil or good requested. — Samīn prefers this ordinary reading; Ālūsī says it is the apparent sense and rejects alternatives. Ibn ʿĀshūr says bāʾ may strengthen the verb's link to its object, or دعاء may imply hastening, comparing 5:6 and 42:18. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 17:11/q07/p2 Both mean 'in': people pray in adversity as they pray in prosperity. — Abū Ḥayyān reports the reading but says دعاءه presupposes an actual good-time prayer; Samīn says the interpretation leaves the thing asked for neither evil nor good and calls it remote. Ālūsī rejects it as against the apparent sense. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 17:11/q07/p3 Both bāʾ prepositions are causal. — Abū al-Baqāʾ is reported by Samīn, who says the sense does not support it; Ālūsī also regards causal bāʾ as contrary to the apparent sense. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 17:11/q08 What is the syntax and force of دُعَآءَهُۥ بِٱلْخَيْرِ?  [دُعَآءَهُۥ بِٱلْخَيْرِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the accusative is explained directly as a verbal noun or through an understood comparison and ellipsis.
- 17:11/q08/p1 It is an accusative verbal noun of the verb, comparing the person's evil prayer with his good prayer. — Akhfash compares انك منطلق انطلاقا; Naḥḥās reports this and says the noun is annexed to its agent. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it compares the haste of both prayers. Naḥḥās permits an implied 'like' in sense but rejects a deleted kāf as the cause of case, comparing عمرو كالأسد and عمرو الأسد. [5 notes: AKHFASH; NAHHAS(الأخفش); SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 17:11/q08/p2 An omitted comparison, 'like his invocation for good,' supplies its sense. — Naḥḥās reports al-Farrāʾ's explanation and says adjective and genitive complement stand for their nouns. Ālūsī explains the accusative by omitted mithla and comparison particle. Naḥḥās disputes only a claim that ellipsis of kāf determines the case. [3 notes: NAHHAS(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL; NAHHAS-]

## 17:11/q09 Who is the beneficiary of the good prayer?  [بِٱلْخَيْرِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the comparison is within one person's prayers or between al-Naḍr and believers.
- 17:11/q09/p1 The same person or those close to him for whom he also rashly asks harm. — The common account contrasts people's prayers for health, safety and blessings with angry curses against themselves and their dependants. [3 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI]
- 17:11/q09/p2 The believers asking for mercy, contrasted with al-Naḍr's request for punishment. — Wāḥidī transmits this through ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās alongside the identification of al-Naḍr and 8:32. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)]

## 17:11/q10 What does عَجُولًۭا mean?  [عَجُولًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the epithet stresses premature timing, low endurance, unconsidered desire, rash curses, or inherited disposition; these meanings can overlap.
- 17:11/q10/p1 Inclined to seek things before their proper time. — Jishumī defines ʿajala thus. Biqāʿī contrasts it with surʿa, doing something at its earliest proper time. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls ʿajūl an intensive of ʿājil; Elmalılı stresses demanding the future prematurely. [4 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 17:11/q10/p2 Impatient and unable to bear either ease or hardship. — Ibn ʿAbbās supplies this gloss in several sources; al-Kāshifī describes rapid shifts of state, and al-Durr gives the nuances of irritability and impatience. [6 notes: THALABI(مجاهد); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 17:11/q10/p3 Impulsively asking or doing whatever comes to mind without considering consequences. — Zamakhsharī, Nasafī, Bayḍāwī, Bursevī and Ālūsī stress want of reflection; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says haste pursues desire before judging benefit and harm, not merely repeated demands for punishment. Kur'an Yolu commends reason, experience, trustworthy knowledge and Qurʾānic standards. [16 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(مجاهد، وقتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 17:11/q10/p4 Hastily asking for evil to occur, even though it is disliked. — Mujāhid and Qatāda connect the final description directly to the rash prayer in the first clause; Ṭabrisī gives the same explanation. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة ومجاهد); JISHUMI(مجاهد، وقتادة); TABRISI(مجاهد)]
- 17:11/q10/p5 An inherited human disposition exemplified by Adam's premature attempt to rise or seek food. — Muqātil and Ibn ʿAṭiyya trace humanity's haste to Adam; Salman, Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid and Ibn Masʿūd provide differing creation-scene details. Rāzī says the wider moral applies whether or not Adam is intended. [5 notes: MUQATIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; THALABI(سلمان الفارسي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 17:11/q11 What happened in the reports about Adam's haste?  [وَكَانَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ عَجُولًۭا · reports]
turns on: The stage of Adam's animation and what he hurried to do; Jishumī disputes the physical possibility of one account.
- 17:11/q11/p1 He tried to rise or walk before the spirit had completed its entry, commonly when it had reached his navel or middle. — Ibn ʿAbbās says head-first animation made flesh and blood, then Adam admired himself and tried to rise at the navel; Bayḍāwī says he fell. Muqātil says middle, and some accounts say before the legs were animated. Jishumī reports a feet version but rejects the account because an incomplete body could not intend or act; Abū Ḥayyān objects that the verse's wording does not fit. [13 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(إبراهيم والضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(سلمان الفارسي); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سلمان الفارسي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سلمان الفارسي وابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(a report); JISHUMI(ابن عباس)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سلمان الفارسي، ابن عباس)-]
- 17:11/q11/p2 Seeing his body formed from the head down, he asked God to finish him before night while his legs were unfinished. — Salman gives the before-night detail; Mujāhid says eyes came first and the request was before sunset. Ibn Kathīr adds that Adam sneezed and praised God when the soul reached his head. Other reports vary between spirit, formation and completion of the legs. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(سلمان الفارسي); THALABI(سلمان الفارسي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(سلمان الفارسي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سلمان الفارسي); QURTUBI-FULL(سلمان); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سلمان الفارسي وابن عباس); DURR-FULL(سلمان الفارسي,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(سلمان الفارسي,مجاهد)]
- 17:11/q11/p3 He rushed toward Paradise's fruit, craving food before his legs received the spirit. — Ibn Masʿūd links this with 21:37. Ālūsī reports that al-Qurṭubī attributed a version where Adam saw the fruit as spirit reached his eyes and moved toward it at the stomach, but Ālūsī treats the attribution cautiously. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود); ALUSI-FULL(القرطبي)]

## 17:11/q12 What force does كَانَ have in the description of human haste?  [وَكَانَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ عَجُولًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the past form indicates a completed state or an enduring disposition in context.
- 17:11/q12/p1 It marks a settled or original disposition, not a condition that necessarily ended. — Burhān says with human qualities kāna expresses an ingrained potential that becomes actual, comparing 70:19 and 70:21. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it an established trait and cites 18:54. Sāmarrāʾī distinguishes enduring disposition from a terminated past and compares Iblīs in 18:50; he notes kāna has more temporal uses than a simple complete/incomplete classification. Burhān argues that attachment to time can span all times and discusses eternal divine capacities separately. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 17:11/q12/p2 In other contexts kāna can describe a past state that has ended. — Sāmarrāʾī gives being asleep or at home as examples and says this is one temporal use, not the meaning in every occurrence. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 17:11/q13 How does the conjunction وَ connect the final clause?  [وَكَانَ · grammar]
turns on: The cited notes address different conjunction effects: contextual explanation and vivid narration.
- 17:11/q13/p1 It can introduce an explanatory state: 'as it is' or 'given that' humanity is hasty. — Asad explicitly glosses the conjunction this way in context. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 17:11/q13/p2 A present verb coordinated with a past report can vividly portray one past scene; coordination need not make the events simultaneous. — Jurjānī uses the Ibn ʿAtīk report to illustrate the vivid present, and contrasts constructions where a negative particle precedes the present, with and without wāw. This is a wider rhetorical observation attached to the passage. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 17:11/q14 What happened in the reports about a curse involving the Prophet and a freed captive?  [* · reports]
turns on: The story varies in the woman's name and identity of the captive; related sound reports establish a broader supplication principle.
- 17:11/q14/p1 Sawda loosened a captive's bonds; after his escape the Prophet angrily prayed against her, then said an undeserved curse from him would become mercy. — Zajjāj, Thaʿlabī, Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Qurṭubī, Bayḍāwī and others give the story, often as occasion of revelation. Zamakhsharī says she raised her hands fearing they would be cut. Ibn ʿAṭiyya's version says the Prophet was bound in Sawda's house, an important variation in who was captive. Ālūsī reports al-Wāqidī's Aisha version instead and separately treats words to Aisha as correction; he cautions that reports have problems. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري أبو نصر); BAYDAWI-FULL(روي); ALUSI-FULL(عائشة)]
- 17:11/q14/p2 The Prophet stipulated that harm or curses he directed at an undeserving believer become expiation, purification, mercy and nearness to God. — Qurṭubī cites a Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim report from Abū Hurayra and parallels from Aisha and Jābir; Ālūsī calls the stipulation a sound report. Al-Māzirī, quoted via al-Nawawī, says an angry correction can be lawful while hidden unworthiness is known by God. Another explanation makes curse-like phrases habitual Arab speech; Ālūsī notes problems in some reports. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); ALUSI-FULL(المازري,النبي ﷺ)]

## 17:11/q15 Are rash prayers for harm answered?  [وَيَدْعُ ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ بِٱلشَّرِّ · theology]
turns on: Whether divine mercy is taken as a usual delay or an absolute bar; the Prophetic warning supports possible acceptance.
- 17:11/q15/p1 God mercifully withholds an immediate answer that would destroy the targets. — Al-Farrāʾ, Ṭabarī, Thaʿlabī and others stress divine restraint; 10:11 says that hastening evil as people hasten good would end their term. The examples include curses against children, wealth and selves. [9 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس والحسن وقتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 17:11/q15/p2 A harmful prayer can be answered if it coincides with an hour of divine response. — Prophetic warnings in Ibn Kathīr and al-Durr forbid cursing oneself, children or wealth for this reason. Ālūsī cites Jābir's report and explicitly rebuts the absolute claim that divine generosity prevents every such answer. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(النبي ﷺ)]

## 17:11/q16 What discipline of asking and acting follows from the verse?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The first counsel emphasizes deliberation in requests; the inward counsel emphasizes remembrance and surrender of choice.
- 17:11/q16/p1 Avoid rash harmful prayers and assess what truly benefits before asking or acting. — Elmalılı advises patient prayer for good and righteous deeds; Ālūsī says seekers should wait to understand their state; Kur'an Yolu recommends reason, knowledge, experience, trusted sources and Qurʾānic standards. Ibn ʿĀshūr says God times both good and evil wisely. Bursevī cites a believer/hypocrite contrast, Adam's advice to pause and an Arab saying that haste breeds regret; he exempts prompt prayer, burial, marriage, debt payment, feeding a guest and repentance. [8 notes: ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI(آدم,أعرابي)]
- 17:11/q16/p2 Remember God and surrender choice in requests; remembrance brings better gifts without the hazard of misguided asking. — Sahl gives this as the safest supplication. Tustarī and Sulamī say constant remembrance supplies desired goods without destructive requests; a divine saying promises the occupied remembrancer more than petitioners receive. Ālūsī repeats Sahl. [5 notes: TUSTARI(سهل); SULAMI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,سهل); ALUSI-FULL(سهل)]

## 17:11/q17 How does the verse fit its surrounding passage and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes connect the verse either to guidance and judgment, the following cycle of time, or cross-references about haste.
- 17:11/q17/p1 After the Qurʾān's guidance, it rebukes people who turn from that gift and rashly invite the warned punishment. — Rāzī says people ignore the Qurʾān's laws and explanations; al-Kashf says they refuse its best path. Ālūsī contrasts the guide and guided or disobedient hearer. Ṭabāṭabāʾī links haste with neglect of guidance and the record of deeds. Islahi says Jews and Quraysh are invited to the straight path while blind rivals demand a sign of punishment. Kur'an Yolu points to 17:9. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الإسلام,صاحب الكشف); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 17:11/q17/p2 The warning leads into night and day because fixed terms unfold with time despite impatient demands. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says haste cannot change an appointed term, while night and day show time passing. Ṭabrisī says God grants night and day for human benefit after the warning despite ingratitude. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABRISI]
- 17:11/q17/p3 Other verses illustrate the same problem of haste, misjudgment or short-sighted desire. — 10:11 concerns divine delay of requested evil; 10:12 contrasts supplication in hardship and ease; 8:32 records the demand for stones; 2:216 reverses apparent good and bad; 21:37 describes humanity as created of haste, supported by Zajjāj as quoted in Burhān. Elmalılı relates immediate gain to 17:18, and Study Quran cites 5:41, 5:62, 27:46, 6:57–58 and 29:29. [6 notes: BURHAN(الزجاج); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); ASAD-NOTES; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]

## 17:11/q18 How reliable are the ancillary reports used around this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether ancillary narrations have sufficient transmission and corroboration.
- 17:11/q18/p1 Reports from the Prophet, ʿAlī, Salmān and others are set aside when their chains and independent support are weak. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the reports on these verses in both Sunni and Shiʿi routes lack support from the Qurʾān, the Prophet or decisive reason, and their chains are weak. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 17:11/q18/p2 A Muslim report describes Satan recognizing Adam's lack of restraint from his hollow formation. — Qurṭubī cites Anas ibn Mālik in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim as an ancillary report on human weakness. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أنس بن مالك)]

## 17:11/q19 What grammatical analogies are cited for omitted endings and adjacent letters?  [وَيَدْعُ · grammar]
turns on: The analogies concern omission of weak endings and spelling that distinguishes grammatical categories.
- 17:11/q19/p1 Arabic verse and prose can omit a final wāw or yāʾ when the preceding vowel indicates it. — Al-Farrāʾ cites سندع الزبانية, ويدع الإنسان and three poetic examples for wāw, and ʿAntara's تكحلي وتخضب for feminine yāʾ with surviving kasra. [2 notes: FARRA]
- 17:11/q19/p2 An alif after a plural wāw disambiguates it from other endings, whereas the singular root wāw has no such alif. — Naḥḥās reports al-Akhfash distinguishing plural marker from conjunction wāw; Aḥmad ibn Yaḥyā says detached object from emphatic pronoun. Naḥḥās gives the specific plural versus root-wāw distinction. [3 notes: NAHHAS(أحمد بن يحيى,الأخفش)]
