# 69:5: 11 questions

## 69:5/q01 Who were Thamūd?  [ثَمُودُ · referent]
- 69:5/q01/p1 They were the people of the prophet Ṣāliḥ. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Biqāʿī identify their prophet as Ṣāliḥ. Bursevī says they were Arabs living at al-Ḥijr between Syria and the Ḥijāz, on the Syrian pilgrims' route. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 69:5/q02 How is the verb 'they were destroyed' read?  [فَأُهْلِكُوا۟ · readings]
turns on: Which verbal form or, in the separate report, which consonantal reading is transmitted.
- 69:5/q02/p1 The common reading is passive, fa-uhlikū, 'they were destroyed.' — Abū Ḥayyān calls this the majority reading; al-Samīn calls it the general reading. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(زيد ابن علي)]
- 69:5/q02/p2 Zayd ibn ʿAlī reads active fa-halakū, 'they perished.' — Al-Samīn derives it from the triliteral halaka; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī also report the reading. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(زيد ابن علي); ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن علي)]
- 69:5/q02/p3 A separate later passage has lā yakhfā with yāʾ or lā takhfā with tāʾ. — Al-Fārisī reports Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī for yāʾ, other readers for tāʾ, and judges both sound; this variant concerns a later verse, not the wording of 69:5. [1 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(حمزة والكسائي)]

## 69:5/q03 Why is destruction expressed in the passive?  [فَأُهْلِكُوا۟ · rhetoric]
- 69:5/q03/p1 The passive foregrounds the terrifying destruction, while God as its agent is already understood. — Al-Biqāʿī says the tangible horror receives attention; Bursevī says God is the known actor, so the event matters; al-Ālūsī explicitly says God destroyed Thamūd. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 69:5/q04 What does the root and descriptive force of al-ṭāghiya convey?  [بِٱلطَّاغِيَةِ · meaning]
- 69:5/q04/p1 It conveys exceeding a proper limit, including excessive force, severity, or rebellion. — Al-Zajjāj illustrates excess by the overflowing water of 69:11 and compares ʿĀd's fierce wind in 69:6. Al-Wāḥidī says anything ṭāghī exceeds its measure. Al-Rāzī adduces 69:11 and 53:17 for exceeding bounds. Al-Qurṭubī and Iṣlāḥī cite the floodwater in 69:11; Iṣlāḥī also cites Thamūd's rebellion in 91:11. Al-Nasafī and Study Quran stress extreme severity, and Elmalılı allows either a transgressing event or tyranny. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 69:5/q05 What is al-ṭāghiya by which Thamūd were destroyed?  [بِٱلطَّاغِيَةِ · referent]
turns on: Whether bi-l-ṭāghiya gives the physical instrument or event of destruction, the people's moral cause, an agent whose act implicated them, or its location; the parallel with ʿĀd's wind and the bāʾ's force are central arguments.
- 69:5/q05/p1 An overwhelming cry or blast destroyed them. — Qatāda repeatedly calls it a cry beyond ordinary cries, sometimes one cry that silenced and killed them; al-Qurṭubī connects the single cry of 54:31. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās and Ibn ʿAṭiyya favor it because 69:6 similarly names ʿĀd's destructive wind. Al-Biqāʿī and Bursevī say earth and hearts trembled. Ibn Kathīr joins the cry and quake, and Elmalılı calls the same event a cry, thunderous blast and accompanying quake. Al-Ālūsī relates the shriek to 11:67 and the thunderbolt to 41:17; al-Bursevī and al-Ālūsī reconcile quake accounts as accompanying or caused by the shriek. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī favors a mode of destruction in the following sequence; Study Quran cites 11:67 and 54:31. Al-Durr also reports Ibn ʿAbbās for the cry, despite other transmissions of his view. [34 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); NAHHAS(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة)+; NAHHAS+; TAB-FULL+]
- 69:5/q05/p2 Their own transgression, disbelief, or sins brought their destruction. — Muqātil links rebellion to rejecting Ṣāliḥ. Abū ʿUbayda and Ibn Qutayba give transgression or unbelief; Mujāhid says sins, while Ibn Zayd specifies rebellion against God's signs and Book and cites 91:11. Al-Wāḥidī attributes this view to most exegetes and grammarians, including al-Kalbī, Muqātil, Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid. Al-Ḥasan and others take the form as a verbal noun; al-Baghawī also allows an adjective for their outrageous act. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites Sūrat al-Shams; Study Quran says this moral cause can coexist with a destructive calamity. Reports differ on Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Zayd and al-Kalbī, who also appear with other explanations. [30 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ(أهل اللغة); NAHHAS(قيل); THALABI(مجاهد وابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,أكثر أهل التفسير والعربية والكلبي ومقاتل وابن عباس ومجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد وأبو عبيدة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس، مجاهد، مقاتل، أبو عبيدة، ابن قتيبة,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن,مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس وابن زيد,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وأبو عبيدة وابن زيد); TABATABAI(قيل); STUDYQURAN(R); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KASHSHAF-FULL-; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج,القاضي)-; NASAFI-FULL(قيل)-; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)-]
- 69:5/q05/p3 An overwhelming earthquake or upheaval destroyed them. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq prefer a quake. Al-Bayḍāwī cites their denial of the Calamity in 69:4; Kuranyolu links their denial and disobedience to 7:73–79 and 11:61–68. Asad points to 7:78 and translates the event as upheaval. Al-Bursevī and al-Ālūsī associate or causally connect the quake with the blast, the latter citing 7:78; Elmalılı and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ list it among Qur'anic descriptions. [15 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق)+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 69:5/q05/p4 A thunderbolt, perhaps a lightning strike, destroyed them. — Al-Kalbī and a report from Ibn ʿAbbās name a thunderbolt. Al-Ālūsī cites 41:17 alongside the shriek; Elmalılı includes a thunderous blast among descriptions; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Study Quran list Qur'anic thunderbolt accounts, the latter citing 41:13 and 51:44. Iṣlāḥī infers from al-Dhāriyāt a thunderbolt from striped winter clouds and says God can turn ordinary lightning into a weapon. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 69:5/q05/p5 It names the calamity or severe destructive event without specifying its form. — Al-Ṭabarī first calls it an overwhelming calamity. Al-Naḥḥās reports an overwhelming event or group. Al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Bayḍāwī and Study Quran speak of an event exceeding ordinary severity; al-Rāzī cites 69:11 and 53:17. Al-Qurṭubī supplies an elided 'deed or event.' Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers a destructive mode because the succeeding verses list such modes. Study Quran holds that this can stand beside the moral-cause reading. [16 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(قيل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI; WAHIDI-BASIT; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وأبو عبيدة وابن زيد,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 69:5/q05/p6 It names their particular transgressive deed, especially hamstringing the she-camel. — Mujāhid and Ibn Zayd describe the tyrannical act; al-Thaʿlabī cites 91:11, and al-Baghawī allows an adjective for the deed. Al-Ālūsī specifies hamstringing as the punished deed while favoring a destructive event in the verse. The wording may thus point to the act as cause. [7 notes: THALABI(مجاهد وابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وابن زيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد وابن زيد); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد وابن زيد)]
- 69:5/q05/p7 It names the individual who hamstrung or killed the she-camel. — Ibn Zayd and al-Suddī are reported for this view; al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Ālūsī say the whole people were destroyed because they approved his act. Al-Rāzī, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī call the feminine form an intensive designation; al-Qurṭubī gives analogous personal titles. Study Quran says an individual may have persuaded the people to hamstring the camel, citing 91:13 and 54:23–30. [9 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN]
- 69:5/q05/p8 It names a transgressive faction among Thamūd. — Al-Rāzī says a faction plotted the camel's hamstringing and brought destruction through its ill omen. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān report a tyrannical group; al-Ālūsī ranks it below the event explanation on parallelism grounds. Study Quran allows a powerful minority that persuaded the people to kill the camel. [6 notes: NAHHAS(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN]
- 69:5/q05/p9 It names a woman who hamstrung the she-camel. — Al-Māwardī transmits this specifically from Ibn Zayd, whereas other reports identify an unnamed or male individual. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد)]
- 69:5/q05/p10 It names the place where Thamūd were destroyed. — Al-Karmānī reports this view through al-Itqān. [1 notes: ITQAN(الكرماني)]

## 69:5/q06 How does the form al-ṭāghiya work grammatically in these explanations?  [بِٱلطَّاغِيَةِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the feminine form is a verbal noun, modifies a suppressed noun, or intensifies a designation for a person.
- 69:5/q06/p1 It can be a feminine verbal noun meaning transgression. — Language authorities, Abū Isḥāq and al-Zajjāj compare ʿāfiya and ʿāqiba. Al-Ḥasan gives the same analysis; al-Samīn adduces ṭughwā in 91:11. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī say this parsing fits the ensuing ʿĀd clause poorly. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ(أهل اللغة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد وأبو عبيدة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وابن زيد وأبو عبيدة); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وأبو عبيدة وابن زيد); TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 69:5/q06/p2 It can be an adjective with an omitted noun, such as cry, event, or deed. — Al-Wāḥidī reports Qatāda's omitted 'cry'; al-Rāzī supplies an omitted event; al-Qurṭubī supplies deed or event; al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī allow a quality of the rebellious act. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 69:5/q06/p3 For a person or faction, the feminine form can be an intensive designation. — Al-Rāzī, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī apply this to the camel's killer; al-Qurṭubī compares other personal designations, such as narrator, prodigy, scholar and genealogist. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 69:5/q07 What does the bāʾ in bi-l-ṭāghiya express?  [بِٱلطَّاغِيَةِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the named thing is an instrument of destruction or a reason for it; al-Qāḍī disputes the proposed causal wording.
- 69:5/q07/p1 With the cry it marks the instrument by which they were destroyed. — Al-Samīn compares 'I worked with the adze'; al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās and Ibn ʿAṭiyya infer instrument from ʿĀd's wind in 69:6. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 69:5/q07/p2 With transgression, deed, or person it marks the cause of their destruction. — Al-Samīn gives this for all non-cry interpretations. Al-Qāḍī objects that a causal sense should have used wording meaning 'for it' rather than bi-ṭāghiya. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; NAHHAS(قيل); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(القاضي)-]

## 69:5/q08 How does this clause relate to the account of ʿĀd in the next verse?  [فَأَمَّا ثَمُودُ فَأُهْلِكُوا۟ بِٱلطَّاغِيَةِ · links]
turns on: Whether the paired clauses need to name the same kind of cause, as the following wind appears to name the means of punishment.
- 69:5/q08/p1 Both clauses name the punishment or physical means of destruction. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī use ʿĀd's wind in 69:6 to favor a Thamūd calamity. Al-Zajjāj and the reported Zamakhsharī argue the clauses otherwise lose balance; al-Ālūsī specifies that the balance concerns parallel causes, since both peoples could be called transgressors. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī also find the verbal-noun reading discordant. Al-Jishumī identifies blast and wind and notes the account begins and ends with 'day'. Al-Biqāʿī says a shared denial is followed by distinct punishments. [14 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNATIYYA-FULL+; NAHHAS+; TAB-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 69:5/q08/p2 The first clause can give Thamūd's rebellion while the second gives ʿĀd's wind. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī transmit or allow the verbal-noun analysis while noting its poor fit with the next clause. Study Quran explicitly lets moral cause and destructive calamity both apply. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN; KASHSHAF-FULL-; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج)-; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)-]

## 69:5/q09 How are the cry, earthquake, and thunderbolt accounts related?  [بِٱلطَّاغِيَةِ · links]
- 69:5/q09/p1 They can describe one event: a shriek accompanied by or causing an earthquake, also described as a thunderous blast. — Bursevī says cry and quake are the same event. Al-Ālūsī says the shriek caused the quake, so verses can cite a near or remote cause; he cites 11:67, 7:78 and 41:17. Elmalılı brings together cry, thunderous blast and quake, citing 11:94, 51:44, 7:78 and 29:37. Ibn Kathīr joins cry and quake in one report; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Study Quran list the different Qur'anic descriptions. [7 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 69:5/q10 What brought punishment on Thamūd, and what does their fate teach?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Which part of the account supplies the stated lesson: their own denial, collective assent to the camel-killer, or God's power displayed in punishment.
- 69:5/q10/p1 Their rebellion, unbelief, and rejection of their prophet or God's signs brought their destruction. — Muqātil says transgression led to rejecting Ṣāliḥ; Ibn Zayd cites disobedience to God's signs and Book and 91:11. Al-Jishumī says denial is their own culpable act. Kuranyolu cites 7:73–79 and 11:61–68 for denial of the afterlife and disobedience. Al-Biqāʿī treats both peoples' denial as the common cause. Al-Ālūsī specifies the she-camel's hamstringing as the culpable deed. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد وابن زيد); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 69:5/q10/p2 The community bore the consequence of the camel-killer's act because they approved and supported it. — Al-Rāzī says the whole people approved the single man's act; al-Qurṭubī calls him their leader; al-Ālūsī repeats their approval. Study Quran reports that a minority or individual persuaded others to hamstring the she-camel, citing 91:13 and 54:23–30. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN]
- 69:5/q10/p3 The disaster warns later people and shows God's power to bring the Resurrection. — Kuranyolu expressly makes their fate a lesson for Muslims and proof that the power causing such disasters can raise the dead. Iṣlāḥī says God can turn life-serving forces into destroyers when people rebel, and modern scientific advances do not remove helplessness before Him. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the nine verses recall peoples destroyed for denying Resurrection and answer what the Great Event is, beside the later trumpet account. [4 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]

## 69:5/q11 What inward meaning is drawn from Thamūd and their calamity?  [* · inward]
- 69:5/q11/p1 Thamūd represent people of outward knowledge veiled from inward truth. — Al-Qāshānī calls them people of little water and sees deniers as negligent or excessive; the overwhelming calamity unveils the inward, disembodied realm, effaces their knowledge, and destroys the body. He says both minor and major resurrections strike and annihilate: the minor is denied through worldly heedlessness, the major through lack of witnessing and veiling. Bursevī says rejection of the inward path and failure to follow a genuine saint brings the fire of distance and veiling, leaving spiritual corruption beneath outward rectitude. [4 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]
