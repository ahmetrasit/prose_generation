## 7:20/q02 What does waswasa mean here?  [فَوَسْوَسَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the emphasis lies on a faint covert sound or confused overabundant speech.
- 7:20/q02/p1 A faint, concealed, often repeated prompting or speech, audible or inward. — Al-Layth compares inner speech, wind in reeds, and jewelry; Ruʾba's hunter and prayer supply poetic usage (al-Wāḥidī, Ṭabarī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn ʿĀshūr). Ibn Fāris calls it a hidden sound. Al-Samarrāʾī distinguishes broad waswasa from discord-specific nuzgh (12:100, 41:36) and notes 114:4–5; al-Qurṭubī gives waswasa/wiswās/waswas forms and applies waswās to Satan. - 7:20/q02/p2 Excessive, disordered speech. — Abū Muslim says this explains calling a confused person mawsūs. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم)]

## 7:20/q03 What is the grammatical behavior of waswasa and its derived description?  [فَوَسْوَسَ · grammar]
- 7:20/q03/p1 It is intransitive, and the derived description normally has kasra, with a preposition identifying the recipient. — Al-Zamakhsharī compares other sound verbs; al-Rāzī gives ululation and howling. Al-Samīn reports Ibn al-Aʿrābī's stricter distinction and other usage; al-Wāḥidī transmits the man's self-whispering form. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأعرابي)]
- 7:20/q03/p2 The inward whisperer is described by a form referring to his own self. — Al-Azharī links it to 50:16 and Ruʾba's hunter; al-Samīn likewise cites 50:16. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأزهري); SAMIN-DURR]

## 7:20/q04 Who is the devil who whispered?  [ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ · referent]
turns on: The notes distinguish Iblis's identity from competing etymologies of his title.
- 7:20/q04/p1 Iblis himself. — Muqātil and Ibn ʿAṭiyya identify him explicitly. [2 notes: MUQATIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 7:20/q04/p2 Satan is named for satanic rebellion or distance from guidance. — ʿIkrima gives the first explanation; al-Rāzī derives it from shatana and extends it to rebellious humans, jinn, and animals, citing 6:112. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عكرمة); RAZI-FULL]
- 7:20/q04/p3 The name may derive from shāṭa, becoming void. — Al-Rāzī reports this second etymology, explaining the rebel nullifies his own interest. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 7:20/q05 How did Satan's whisper reach the pair?  [فَوَسْوَسَ لَهُمَا ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ · reports]

Al-Zajjāj likewise favors direct address. Elmalılı says expulsion did not remove access from four directions. Elmalılı suggests the snake might symbolize a venomous human life force. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(نقلة القصص); ELMALILI(حسن البصري); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن)-]

## 7:20/q06 What does the preposition in waswasa lahumā convey?  [لَهُمَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether lām simply identifies the recipients or evokes Satan's pretended concern.
- 7:20/q06/p1 It means 'to the two of them,' interchangeable with ilayhimā. — Al-Akhfash and al-Ṭabarī compare ghariḍtu lahu with 'longed for him'; al-Ṭabarī cites Ruʾba. Al-Ṣiḥāḥ says Arabic allows varied prepositions with the verb. Al-Naḥḥās identifies the pair as Adam and Eve. [8 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(الأخفش); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(الصحاح); JISHUMI]
- 7:20/q06/p2 Lahumā makes Satan sound like a sincere adviser acting for their benefit; ilayhimā merely marks direction. — Al-Māwardī and al-Ṭabrisī distinguish counsel from casting a thought. Al-Jishumī connects the choice to his pretended sincerity; al-Rāzī and al-Samīn say 'for their sake.' [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:20/q07 Was exposing their nakedness Satan's purpose or an unintended result?  [لِيُبْدِىَ لَهُمَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether Iblis knew exposure would follow the eating; the Pharaoh and Moses analogy supports consequential lām.
- 7:20/q07/p1 It was his purpose. — Al-Jishumī and al-Samīn prefer purpose. Al-Zamakhsharī says exposure would distress them; al-Ṭabrisī says Iblis knew exposure would cause expulsion; Abū ʿAlī names loss of Garden blessings. Al-Rāzī allows prior knowledge from angels or the Preserved Tablet and humiliation as metaphor. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows inference or inspiration; 35:6 shows enmity. Al-Samarrāʾī relates the outcome to 7:22. [22 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI(أبو علي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; JISHUMI+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 7:20/q07/p2 The lām marks the result; Satan aimed at sin, while exposure followed. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, and al-Rāzī compare Pharaoh's household taking Moses in 28:8; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says many commentators hold it. Al-Bursawī prefers this reading. Ibn ʿĀshūr ties it to Satan's possible ignorance of the precise effect. Al-Samīn counters that Satan could have learned it (7:17). [15 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI+; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 7:20/q09 What does wūriya mean and how is it formed?  [وُۥرِىَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether its derivation is wārā or warāʾ.
- 7:20/q09/p1 It was hidden or covered, from wārā/muwārāt. — Ibn Qutayba gives tawārī and muwārāh; al-Jishumī compares covering a dead person with earth (5:31). ## 7:20/q10 How is wūriya read and analyzed?  [وُۥرِىَ · readings]
turns on: The passive's two wāws and permitted or attested phonological substitutions.
- 7:20/q10/p1 The usual passive has two wāws. — Al-Samīn says the majority reads the passive of wārā with alif changed to wāw; al-Ālūsī identifies root and added wāw, and al-Nasafī explains why the long second wāw need not become hamza. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:20/q10/p2 An alternate form begins with hamza, ūri. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī allow it outside Qurʾānic usage, comparing uqqitat; ʿAbd Allāh reads it according to al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī. First-wāw hamzation is optional here under their stated phonological rule. [6 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(عبد الله); ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله); NASAFI-FULL(عبد الله)]
- 7:20/q10/p3 Ibn Waththāb reads wari with one wāw. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya records it; al-Samīn says it implies a transitive triliteral verb whose existence needs evidence. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن وثاب); SAMIN-DURR(يحيى بن وثاب)]

## 7:20/q11 What had concealed their nakedness?  [مَا وُۥرِىَ عَنْهُمَا · reports]
turns on: Reports of light, clothing, and bodily concealment compete with figurative readings.
- 7:20/q11/p1 A light veiled each one's private parts. — Wahb ibn Munabbih says neither saw the other's nakedness; al-Ṭabarī links 20:121. Al-Qurṭubī says only the pair saw each other after the light vanished; Elmalılı repeats this report. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه); THALABI(وهب); DURR-FULL(وهب بن منبه); ALUSI-FULL(وهب بن منبه); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(Wahb ibn Munabbih); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 7:20/q11/p2 A garment covered them, described as nails or like a fingernail. — Al-Suddī says Iblis knew about their nakedness from angelic books while Adam did not; al-Wāḥidī says their clothes fell, and al-Qurṭubī gives a torn-garment account. Elmalılı reports a nail-like covering. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(السدي); ELMALILI; DURR-FULL(السدي)]
- 7:20/q11/p3 They did not perceive their own or each other's private parts before eating. — Al-Bayḍāwī and Abū Ḥayyān state this; Elmalılı says God initially concealed them; Study Quran says disobedience removed a divinely ordered cover or light. Al-Qushayrī takes the wording as divine care: no outsider saw the exposure. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; QUSHAYRI]
- 7:20/q11/p4 The concealment may concern inner realities or lost honor, not bodily cover. — A group cited by Ibn ʿAṭiyya takes the exposure figuratively; he says leaf-covering in 7:22 weighs against this unless their Garden garments tore. Al-Ālūsī says the literal sense is apparent, while al-Rāzī uses loss of honor as a purposive reading. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(طائفة); RAZI-FULL; QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]

## 7:20/q12 What are their sawʾāt?  [سَوْءَٰتِهِمَا · meaning]
turns on: Literal anatomy, bodily synecdoche, or inward shame.
- 7:20/q12/p1 Their shameful private parts, front and rear. — Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī derive the name from shame at exposure; Ibn ʿAṭiyya specifies genitals and anus. Study Quran notes the root 'bad/evil' and the common exegetical gloss. [20 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(JJ, Ṭ, Z)]
- 7:20/q12/p2 Their entire bodies, named by the most shameful part. — Qatāda supplies this synecdoche; Abū Ḥayyān reports it. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة)]
- 7:20/q12/p3 Their hidden moral and animal traits. — Al-Qāshānī reads inclination to nature as exposing pleasures, vices, and animal or predatory actions; Asad treats unveiling as loss of innocence and onset of conscious choice. [2 notes: QASHANI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 7:20/q13 How is sawʾātihimā read?  [سَوْءَٰتِهِمَا · readings]
turns on: Hamza retention, easing, assimilation, and recitational length.
- 7:20/q13/p1 The majority keeps plural sawʾātihimā with hamza. — Al-Samīn specifies no vowel transfer or assimilation; Abū Ḥayyān surveys the plural and singular variants. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 7:20/q13/p2 Some read a singular with eased hamza and doubled wāw. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya names al-Ḥasan and Mujāhid; al-Samīn gives the same. Al-Ālūsī also reports a singular retaining original hamza. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن ومجاهد); SAMIN-DURR(مجاهد والحسن); ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:20/q13/p3 Some read plural sawātihimā with doubled wāw. — Abū Jaʿfar, Shayba, al-Ḥasan, and al-Zuhrī are named by Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ṭabrisī; Sībawayh records it as dialect. The hamza becomes wāw and assimilates. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو جعفر بن القعقاع وشيبة بن نصاح والحسن والزهري); TABRISI(أبو جعفر وشيبة والحسن والزهري); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الحسن وأبو جعفر وشيبة بن نصاح); ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:20/q13/p4 Another plural transfers the hamza vowel to wāw without assimilation. — Al-Samīn records this; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī describe the phonological alternatives. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:20/q13/p5 Readers differ over the length of the vowel in sawʾah and sawʾāt. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports poetic medium/full length, most authorities excluding sawʾāt's wāw from extra length, while al-Dānī and al-Ahwāzī do not; he construes medium versus short and lists four Warsh combinations. Related cases of shayʾ, mawʾil, mawʾūdah and Ḥamza's pause delimit the rule. [6 notes: NASHR(أبو الطيب بن غلبون وصاحب العنوان وابن بليمة,ابن غلبون وابن العنوان والطرسوسي وابن بليمة والخزاعي,الجمهور,المهدوي وابن سفيان وابن شريح وأبو محمد والجمهور)]

## 7:20/q14 Why is sawʾāt plural with a dual suffix?  [سَوْءَٰتِهِمَا · grammar]
turns on: Idiomatic plural for dual versus four distinct body parts.
- 7:20/q14/p1 Arabic may use a plural for the dual to avoid repeated dual morphology. — Al-Samīn prefers this and cites 66:4; the singular reading supports it. Al-Ālūsī compares 'your two hearts'; al-Bursawī offers the same. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 7:20/q14/p2 The plural literally counts four parts, front and rear of each person. — Al-Samīn and al-Bursawī report this; al-Ālūsī finds it remote. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 7:20/q15 What moral point is drawn from the exposure?  [سَوْءَٰتِهِمَا · interpretation]
- 7:20/q15/p1 Unnecessary exposure of private parts is naturally shameful, even alone or before a spouse. — Al-Zamakhsharī calls it repugnant to instinct and reason, which Abū Ḥayyān identifies with Muʿtazilī rational moral judgment. Al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, and al-Bursawī echo the point; the latter cites ʿAlī and ʿĀʾisha on avoiding sight. Study Quran reports a legal qualification for marriage. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI(عائشة رضي الله عنها,علي رضي الله عنه); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R,many commentators)]
- 7:20/q15/p2 Exposure and sin show human vulnerability and the need to observe divine limits. — Elmalılı says humans do not fully know their own nature; will allows ascent or fall, while command and religion protect against Satan's aim. [2 notes: ELMALILI]

## 7:20/q16 How does 'and he said' relate to the whisper?  [وَقَالَ · grammar]
- 7:20/q16/p1 It coordinates with and explains the whisper, presenting its content. — Al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī treat the speech as explanatory; al-Zajjāj says 7:21's oath supports direct address. Al-Samarrāʾī says Satan spoke aloud as well as whispering. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ZAJJAJ; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 7:20/q17 What does the ban concerning this tree mean?  [مَا نَهَىٰكُمَا رَبُّكُمَا عَنْ هَٰذِهِ ٱلشَّجَرَةِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the tree is explained as an eating prohibition, sexual symbol, or deliberately unspecified.
- 7:20/q17/p1 God had forbidden eating from the tree; Satan falsely recast the motive as withholding angelhood or immortality. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī specify eating. Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr call the claim a lie; Muqātil says the alternatives would ensure one benefit. Ibn Kathīr cites 20:120; Study Quran points to false hope (4:119–120). [11 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:20/q17/p2 The forbidden fruit or tree is associated with sex. — Süleyman Ateş associates the fruit with sexual organs; Kuran Yolu reports Alexandrian Jewish, Philonic, and Church Father readings of the tree as sexual relations. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Süleyman Ateş,İskenderiye Yahudiliği, Philon ve bazı kilise babaları)]
- 7:20/q17/p3 Its species need not be specified beyond the Qurʾānic account. — Kuran Yolu follows al-Shawkānī in preferring restraint about the tree's nature. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]

- 7:20/q19/p1 It precisely identifies and emphasizes the particular tree. — Al-Biqāʿī notes the combined demonstrative and noun. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 7:20/q21 How does the exception after illā work?  [إِلَّآ أَن تَكُونَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the omitted sense is a noun of aversion, a negative particle, or a different scope for the prohibition.
- 7:20/q21/p1 An omitted noun supplies 'from aversion/fear that you become.' — Al-Akhfash compares iyyāka an tafʿala; Basrans including Sībawayh prefer an elided noun. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Samīn favor noun ellipsis over particle ellipsis; al-Naḥḥās parses malakayni as predicate and an as avoidance. Al-Zamakhsharī reads fear. [16 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية من أهل البصرة); NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه والبصريون); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأخفش والزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+; TABRISI+]
- 7:20/q21/p2 An omitted lā yields 'lest you become angels or immortals.' — Al-Ṭabarī cites 4:176, Ibn Kathīr also 16:15; Kufans and Ibn al-Anbārī give the grammatical account. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ṭabrisī oppose this parsing. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الكوفيون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; TABRISI-]
- 7:20/q21/p3 Only angels or immortals, not the human pair, were forbidden the tree. — Al-Sayyid al-Murtaḍā proposes this; al-Ālūsī finds it unconvincing and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says 20:120 points otherwise. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(السيد المرتضى); TABATABAI(السيد المرتضى)]
- 7:20/q21/p4 The exception particle illā itself is a preserved quoted form in grammatical naming. — Sībawayh compares illā with duflā and contrasts quoted immā, interrogative ammā/allā, and unquoted topical ammā; his wider chapter illustrates preserved names through poets, compounds, relative clauses, tanwīn and vocatives. Al-Khalīl distinguishes fused compounds and quoted phrases. [36 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]

## 7:20/q22 How is malakayn read?  [مَلَكَيْنِ · readings]
turns on: Vocalization of the lām and the force of 20:120 as evidence.
- 7:20/q22/p1 With fatḥa on lām: two angels, the majority reading. — Al-Ṭabarī insists on the transmitted regional reading and disallows others; Mujāhid and several readers transmit it. Al-Naḥḥās calls the kasra version anomalous and rejects Abū ʿUbayd's 20:120 argument. Al-Rāzī calls the canonical reading mass-transmitted; al-Zajjāj thinks the angel form may have been recited. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); NAHHAS(أبو جعفر,أبو عبيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,النحاس); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس ويحيى بن أبي كثير); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); JISHUMI; TAB-FULL+]
- 7:20/q22/p2 With kasra on lām: two kings or rulers. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Yaḥyā, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Zuhrī, and others are named by successive sources; Abū ʿUbayd invokes 20:120's unfading kingdom. Abū ʿAmr objects that no kings preceded Adam; al-Naḥḥās answers that 20:120 means enduring Paradise. Al-Qāshānī reads rule over faculties and animals. [20 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس ويحيى بن أبي كثير); NAHHAS(يحيى بن أبي كثير والضحاك); THALABI(ابن عباس والضحاك ويحيى بن أبي معين); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس ويحيى بن أبي كثير والضحاك); TABRISI(يحيى بن أبي كثير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزهري); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL(قرئ); QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن بن علي والضحاك ويحيى بن كثير والزهري وابن حكيم); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس والحسن والضحاك ويحيى بن أبي كثير والزهري وابن حكيم عن ابن كثير); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس ويحيى بن أبي كثير); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد)+; TAB-FULL-; NAHHAS(أبو جعفر,أبو عبيد)-; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس)-; RAZI-FULL-]
- 7:20/q22/p3 Even fatḥa 'angel' can convey the promise of kingship. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads it through 20:120's imperishable dominion. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 7:20/q23 What sort of angelhood did the promise offer?  [أَن تَكُونَا مَلَكَيْنِ · interpretation]
turns on: Literal change of form, rank or innate qualities, and specific promised capacities.
- 7:20/q23/p1 A bodily change into angelic form. — Al-Māwardī reports a claim that God's law permitted transformation; al-Ṭabrisī says Satan promised angelic forms. Al-Bursawī denies transformation in essence, while Saʿdī says Ashʿarīs allow it because bodies are homogeneous. Al-Bursawī differentiates human constitution from angels' and devils' transformations. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; BURSEVI(سعدي المفتي)]
- 7:20/q23/p2 Angelic rank, qualities, or freedom from appetite rather than changed essence. — Al-Māwardī reports rank instead of form; al-Qushayrī says the pair sought freedom from desire and hope. Al-Rāzī mentions strength, light, and residence by Throne and Kursī; Ibn al-Anbārī mentions closeness to God. Al-Biqāʿī notes flight and changing form; Elmalılı notes freedom from eating and drinking. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:20/q23/p3 They would become angels like God or like those circling a tree branch. — Ibn ʿAbbās gives the 'like God' wording; Wahb describes angels circling one branch and says Satan promised that status. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,ابن منبه); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,وهب بن منبه)]
- 7:20/q23/p4 Angels know good and evil, have wings, form, and honor. — Al-Ḥasan emphasizes form, wings, and honor; al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, al-Nasafī and al-Qurṭubī report knowledge of good and evil as the appeal. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); NAHHAS(الحسن); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 7:20/q23/p5 The pair would live long alongside angels. — Abū Sulaymān al-Dimashqī gives this as one alternative to immortality without death. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي)]

## 7:20/q24 Does the promised angelhood prove angels superior to humans or prophets?  [مَلَكَيْنِ · theology]
turns on: Whether desiring an angelic quality entails conceding an overall hierarchy.
- 7:20/q24/p1 It suggests angels occupy a higher rank. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Naḥḥās infer superiority; al-Naḥḥās invokes 11:31 and 4:172, al-Ḥasan points to form, wings, and honor, and another account cites obedience and freedom from sin. Islāḥī says Adam regarded angels as higher. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NAHHAS(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BAYDAWI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن فورك)-]
- 7:20/q24/p2 It does not establish absolute superiority over humans or prophets. — Ibn Fūrak says angelhood might mean no appetite for food; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī mention innate traits. Al-Rāzī says Adam may not yet have been a prophet, or may have sought power, light, or proximity, not rank; he notes angels had prostrated to Adam. Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Zajjāj and others favor believers; al-Kalbī excepts four named angels. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن فورك); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن فورك,الكلبي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن فورك); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:20/q25 Who are al-khālidīn?  [ٱلْخَٰلِدِينَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether khulūd promises endless earthly life, a long stay, or life after resurrection.
- 7:20/q25/p1 Those who never die and remain forever in the Garden. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Suddī, Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb, Wahb, and later commentators give this gloss; Ibn ʿAbbās says if they missed angelhood they would still never die there. Asad translates 'those who endure.' [17 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(السدي); THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي); BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 7:20/q25/p2 Khulūd can mean prolonged life rather than endless duration. — Al-Rāzī uses this to answer the claim that Adam's acceptance would deny resurrection; al-Ālūsī echoes it. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:20/q25/p3 It may mean enduring life after death in Paradise. — Al-Ālūsī reports this resolution but says it departs from the apparent sense. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 7:20/q26 What is conveyed by min al-khālidīn instead of just khālidīn?  [مِنَ ٱلْخَٰلِدِينَ · rhetoric]
- 7:20/q26/p1 Membership in the enduring group strengthens the promise. — Al-Samīn compares 66:12. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 7:20/q27 Does aw offer a choice or both promised benefits?  [أَوْ · grammar]
turns on: The scope of aw: alternative outcomes or a cumulative inducement.
- 7:20/q27/p1 It presents two possible alternatives without implying doubt. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says both may be possible or one excluded; al-Rāzī reports a literal choice. Muqātil says if not angels, then immortal. [6 notes: MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL(آخرون); IBNASHUR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 7:20/q27/p2 It offers angelhood and immortality together. — Al-Rāzī reports that combined benefits heighten temptation. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 7:20/q28 Did the pair believe Satan's claim?  [وَقَالَ مَا نَهَىٰكُمَا · theology]
turns on: Whether eating implies assent and what Adam could yet know about death.
- 7:20/q28/p1 They ate under desire without believing or even probably assenting to him. — Al-Ḥasan says belief would have been unbelief; al-Rāzī prefers desire as sufficient motive and reports careful scholars denying assent. Al-Samarrāʾī reports Adam saying he had not expected a false oath by God. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(الحسن,المحققون,عمرو بن عبيد); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(آدم); RAZI-FULL+]
- 7:20/q28/p2 Their taking immortality literally need not imply disbelief. — Al-Rāzī says khulūd may be long life; even eternal life need not deny known revelation if mortality had not yet been disclosed. Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise says death and resurrection are learned by revelation; al-Ālūsī repeats the answer. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:20/q28/p3 They may have been persuaded by repeated deceptive approaches. — Al-Rāzī says knowing Iblis as enemy did not make Adam immune to persistence; Muqātil reports Iblis claiming prior creation and superior knowledge. Al-Bursawī says he first mourned their coming death and separation. [3 notes: MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 7:20/q29 Why did the promised benefits tempt them?  [مَلَكَيْنِ أَوْ تَكُونَا مِنَ ٱلْخَٰلِدِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether their longing is read as personal gain, the wish for divine company, or a warning against greed.
- 7:20/q29/p1 Angelic qualities and lasting life appeared desirable. — Al-Māwardī names elevated status and eternal delight; al-Qushayrī names freedom from desire; Elmalılı describes envy and a material route to life. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Satan offered one or both benefits; Study Quran calls false hope a satanic tactic. Al-Māwardī says they knew God fully but not all His rulings, allowing temptation; Asad says the wish to live forever promised likeness to God (20:120). [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL(آخرون,بعضهم); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES]
- 7:20/q29/p2 They sought lasting life with God, not private gain. — Al-Qushayrī prefers this to an interpretation of greed because of prophetic dignity. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI+]
- 7:20/q29/p3 Their desire for permanence exposed the danger of greed. — Al-Qushayrī says it produced abasement, ordeal, and fear; the danger is greater in transient life. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 7:20/q29/p4 Only permanence, rather than angelic rank, drew them. — Al-Ālūsī cites the parallel in Ṭā Hā, where lasting life is promised before the eating. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:20/q30 What inward lesson is drawn from the whisper and exposure?  [* · inward]
turns on: Different inward applications of the same narrative.
- 7:20/q30/p1 Whispering moves from natural inclination through the self into inward thought and planning. — Sahl is transmitted by al-Tustarī and al-Sulamī; al-Tustarī also describes enemy prompting as invitation, confidence after acceptance, then waiting for an opening. [3 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل)]
- 7:20/q30/p2 An evil whisper can become the occasion of repentance and divine favor. — Abū Sulaymān al-Dārānī says Satan meant evil, while Adam's lapse disciplined him and led to confession in 7:23; al-Qushayrī sees divine care in attributing the lapse to Satan. [2 notes: SULAMI(أبو سليمان الداراني); QUSHAYRI]
- 7:20/q30/p3 Worldly sensory benefit masquerades as angelic pleasure and lasting rule. — Al-Qāshānī reads bodily nature as counterfeit angelic perception and its king reading as rule over faculties and animals; 20:120 echoes the promise. [4 notes: QASHANI]
- 7:20/q30/p4 Loss of union after hoped-for permanence makes the episode a lesson in separation. — Al-Qushayrī reports entry in morning and exit at midday, a poetic evil-eye comparison, and another poem on hidden separation destroying gathered joy. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI(قائلهم,قيل)]

## 7:20/q31 How does this account connect to the surrounding clothing passages and other retellings?  [* · links]
turns on: How 7:20 functions amid the exposure, clothing, and parallel Garden narratives.
- 7:20/q31/p1 The exposure leads into the leaf-covering and clothing warning. — Al-Biqāʿī invokes 7:27 and al-Samarrāʾī links 7:22 to 7:26: clothing veils outer nakedness and piety inner faults. Study Quran compares 20:118–121 and 7:22, 26–32, and the Biblical sequence; the warning addresses Adam's descendants. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:20/q31/p2 Al-Aʿrāf's fuller whisper scene makes Satan's scheme vivid for its audience. — Al-Burhān contrasts al-Baqara's 'caused them to slip' with al-Aʿrāf's whisper. Ibn ʿĀshūr says al-Aʿrāf addressed polytheists needing this detail; al-Jishumī attributes repeated stories to changing audiences over twenty-odd years and to varied inimitable expression. [5 notes: BURHAN; JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:20/q31/p3 The broader Garden account is treated as a test of human nature. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī places Adam in a heavenly intermediate garden before earthly law, neither eternal Paradise nor ordinary earthly life; humanity matures under commands after descent. He points readers to al-Baqara and interprets Adam as humanity's representative. [5 notes: TABATABAI]

## 7:20/q32 What does the episode teach about Satan's agency and human responsibility?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: What larger lesson sources attach to the temptation.
- 7:20/q32/p1 Satan seeks disobedience through persuasive falsehood while the pair remain responsible. — Ibn Kathīr says envy drove deception; al-Islāḥī connects the granted respite to access. Al-Qushayrī reports Adam's rebuke and Satan's reply asking about Adam's own devil. Study Quran says Satan whispers and commands indecency. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QUSHAYRI(آدم وإبليس); STUDYQURAN]
- 7:20/q32/p2 The story also figures the order of creation and the original sin of pride. — Al-Ṣādiq calls Iblis's first sin egotism or arrogance; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reconciles the reports. ʿAlī's sermon says Iblis and hosts resisted the prostration command through pride; Jaʿfar and al-Ṣādiq warn against religious analogy after Iblis's fire/clay analogy. ʿAlī calls him the first disbeliever. [8 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,أمير المؤمنين عليه السلام,الصادق عليه السلام,جعفر,علي عليه السلام)]
- 7:20/q32/p3 Satan is an unseen influence requiring thoughtful attention. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī criticizes casual cursing or attribution of thoughts without reflecting on the Qurʾānic account; al-Ṣādiq compares devils' pressure on believers to wasps on meat; al-Riḍā derives Iblis from despair. [3 notes: TABATABAI(الرضا عليه السلام,الصادق عليه السلام)]

## 7:20/q33 What wider reports illustrate satanic prompting and seeking protection?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reported examples and lexical parallels cited around satanic whispering.
- 7:20/q33/p1 The reports describe jinn, devils, and refuge prayers in varied settings. — Al-Rāzī cites warnings about household jinn, the Night Journey afrit and Gabriel's prayer, Kaʿb's refuge formula, Khālid's nightmare prayer, the Prophet's recitation to jinn, Jesus' vision of Satan at the heart, Satan flowing like blood, and a report that everyone has a satan. He explains aʿūdhu as seeking protection or clinging to grace, rajīm as cursed or pelted, and the Hearing/Knowing formula by God's knowledge of hidden whispers or Qurʾānic wording. [15 notes: RAZI-FULL(النبي,جبريل,خالد بن الوليد,عيسى ابن مريم,كعب الأحبار,يحيى بن سعيد)]
- 7:20/q33/p2 One source glosses an oath to the pair as swearing to both. — Abū ʿUbayda also notes the root q-s-m can mean distributing shares. [1 notes: MAJAZ]

## 7:20/q34 What broader language and rhetoric principles are invoked in discussing Qurʾānic wording?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Different general principles and reports supplied alongside this verse's language.
- 7:20/q34/p1 Explicit naming and repetition can convey force that pronouns or allusion do not. — ʿAbd al-Qāhir cites Abū Yaʿqūb, Dhū al-Rumma's line, and 17:105, 112:1–2; al-Jāḥiẓ's account of Qays ibn Khārija supplies a speech example. [4 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL(أبو عثمان الجاحظ,أبو يعقوب)]
- 7:20/q34/p2 Poetry witnesses rare Arabic usage, with debate over how many synonyms Arabic has. — Al-Burhān says Ibn ʿAbbās used poetry as the Arabs' register and demanded more witnesses for doctrinal words; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports synonym and distinction schools, al-Aṣmaʿī's stone terms, Ibn Khālawayh's animal and sword terms, and Abū ʿAlī's objection that many are adjectives. [7 notes: BURHAN(ابن عباس); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(أبو علي الفارسي,ابن خالويه,الأصمعي)]
- 7:20/q34/p3 Qurʾānic eloquence and interpretation depend on discernment and transmitted explanation. — Al-Bāqillānī discusses grief in preceding speech, creative commands (36:82, 7:166, 26:63), al-Buḥturī and Musaylima, the Prophet's comparison of divine speech, and literary training. Al-Burhān says Qurʾān and hadith mutually clarify; Ibn Barjān finds a Qurʾānic basis even for the stoning judgment. [8 notes: BAQILLANI(رسول الله); BURHAN(ابن برجان)]
- 7:20/q34/p4 The refuge surahs' Qurʾānic status rests on sound transmission despite a reported codex omission. — Al-Nawawī states consensus, Ibn Ḥazm calls the contrary story fabricated, al-Bāqillānī limits the objection to codex inscription, and Zirr reports Ubayy's account of Gabriel teaching the Prophet. [4 notes: BURHAN(ابن حزم,القاضي أبو بكر بن الطيب,النووي,زر بن حبيش)]
