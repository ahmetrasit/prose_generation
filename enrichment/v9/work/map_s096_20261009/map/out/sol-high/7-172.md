# 7:172: 48 questions

## 7:172/q01 What does 'when' ask the audience to recall?  [وَإِذْ · grammar]
- 7:172/q01/p1 It is the object of an omitted command to remember the covenant. — Al-Zajjaj makes idh accusative; Ibn Ashur calls it past time, not an adverb. Islahi treats it as recalling a definite, though forgotten, event. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); JISHUMI(أبو علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 7:172/q02 What does God's taking mean here?  [أَخَذَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether taking denotes extraction, God's encompassing possession, or the binding covenant; the surrounding wording and reported scene guide the reading.
- 7:172/q02/p1 God extracted or brought offspring into being from their forebears. — Ibn Ashur compares 6:46. Tabatabai says taking separates offspring without diminishing the source, yielding distinct accountable persons. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 7:172/q02/p2 Taking is encompassing and ownership. — Tabatabai connects it with God's proof over all creatures. [2 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 7:172/q02/p3 It is the binding imposition of a covenant, figured as a weight on the back. — Abu Hayyan cites covenant formulas at 4:154 and 2:83 and specifies monotheism and exclusive worship. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)]
- 7:172/q02/p4 Divine taking brings the nonexistent into existence by God's power. — The Najmiyya distinguishes God's creation from a creature's taking an existing thing; it cites 19:9. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 7:172/q03 Who are the children of Adam addressed in the covenant?  [بَنِىٓ ءَادَمَ · referent]
turns on: The plural backs and the following excuse about polytheist ancestors are read either as specifying a subgroup or as part of an account embracing every human.
- 7:172/q03/p1 All humanity through the final generation is included. — Ubayy's report includes everyone born before the Hour; Alusi rejects a restriction to Jewish ancestors. Tabatabai argues otherwise untaken persons could plead ignorance. [21 notes: MUQATIL; MUSNAD-AHMAD(أبي بن كعب); TAB-FULL(أبي بن كعب,ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL(أبي بن كعب,قيل); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Many commentators); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 7:172/q03/p2 Only certain descendants, particularly those with idolatrous ancestors or reached by prophets, are meant. — The plural backs excludes Adam's immediate children, and the next verse's ancestral-shirk excuse narrows the group; Bursevi excludes people who had no offspring. [10 notes: NAHHAS; IBNABIHATIM(عبد الملك بن أبي يزيد ووهب بن منبه); TABRISI(الجبائي والقاضي); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(نافع وأبو عمر وابن عامر ويعقوب); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 7:172/q03/p3 The messengers were specifically addressed. — Abd al-Malik ibn Abi Yazid and Wahb ibn Munabbih are named for this view. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عبد الملك بن أبي يزيد ووهب بن منبه)]

## 7:172/q04 How is 'from their backs' related to 'children of Adam'?  [مِنۢ بَنِىٓ ءَادَمَ مِن ظُهُورِهِمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the plural backs is simply an appositive account of generational origin or excludes the Adam-back account.
- 7:172/q04/p1 The latter is a partitive apposition specifying the former: descendants are drawn from human fathers' backs. — The repeated min reinforces apposition; Samīn cites analogous part-whole idiom, while Ibn Ashur cites 6:99. Alusi prefers the partitive parsing. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)+]
- 7:172/q04/p2 The apposition is of inclusion, relating bodies or members to the people. — Abu al-Baqa gives this parsing; Alusi questions its rationale. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)-]
- 7:172/q04/p3 The wording describes successive generations, so Adam's own back need not be repeated. — Al-Kinani and others say all are Adam's offspring and descend through one another; Ibn Ashur argues Adam's immediate children are included a fortiori. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ(قوم); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكناني,صاحب النظم); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 7:172/q04/p4 The plural backs specifically excludes a direct taking from Adam's single back as the verse's meaning. — The Mu'tazilites and Tabarsi stress the plural nouns; Ibn Atiyya considers a root-and-branch reconciliation weak. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); TABRISI(المحققون); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL-; IBNASHUR-FULL-; TABATABAI-]

## 7:172/q06 Where was the primordial extraction said to occur?  [أَخَذَ رَبُّكَ · reports]

- 7:172/q06/p1 At Nu'man near Arafah. — Prophetic and Ibn Abbas reports identify the valley; variants call it Batn or Wadi Nu'man and connect it with the Day of Arafah. [16 notes: MUQATIL; MUSNAD-AHMAD(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,سعيد بن حبير); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); THALABI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عباس عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ITQAN(ابن عباس عن النبي)]

[10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 7:172/q06/p3 Between Mecca and Taif. — Al-Kalbi and Muqatil are named for this site. [5 notes: THALABI(الكلبي); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي); JISHUMI(قيل)]

## 7:172/q07 When did the covenant occur relative to Adam's descent and human births?  [وَإِذْ أَخَذَ · reports]
turns on: Reports place Adam before or after descent, while interpretive readings locate the covenant in birth, prior non-temporal existence, or more than one event.
- 7:172/q07/p1 Before Adam's descent from Paradise. — Al-Suddi places it in the lowest heaven; some place it in Paradise. Reports also give a time when the Throne was over water. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود وناس من الصحابة (من طريق السدي)); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,رسول الله); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 7:172/q07/p2 After Adam's descent to earth. — Reports locate the event at Arafah or in India after descent; some say Adam was in water when descendants emerged. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:172/q07/p3 As each person develops in the father's loins or mother's womb, and later reaches understanding. — This is the worldly, generational reading; Kuranyolu distinguishes possible stages of the innate endowment. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ(قوم); MAWARDI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI(عدة من المفسرين); TABATABAI-]
- 7:172/q07/p4 Before spirits were breathed into the descendants' bodies. — Islahi calls it an actual event, with innate cognizance lasting after the occasion is forgotten. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 7:172/q07/p5 There were multiple covenant-taking events or kinds of covenant. — Sufi masters propose this to reconcile reports, while Alusi says their language suggests different kinds; Al-Shirazi distinguishes present rational from primordial verbal covenant. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL(السادة الصوفية,قطب الحق والدين العلامة الشيرازي); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 7:172/q08 What became of the extracted descendants before their earthly births?  [ذُرِّيَّتَهُمْ · reports]
- 7:172/q08/p1 God returned them to Adam's or their fathers' loins, and each decreed person would be born. — Ibn Abbas says their number neither increases nor decreases and the Hour waits for all who testified. ## 7:172/q09 What does the word 'offspring' mean and how is it derived?  [ذُرِّيَّتَهُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Proposed roots connect the word either to small scattered particles or to God's act of creating.
- 7:172/q09/p1 It means progeny and can denote one person, a group, or a collective. — Abu Ali compares the individual in Zechariah's prayer (3:38–39; 19:5–7) and collective bashar (12:31; 64:6; 14:10). [17 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو علي); TABRISI(أبو علي); RAZI-FULL(الواحدي ويونس); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:172/q09/p2 It derives from dharr, tiny scattered particles or ants. — Proposals include faʿūlūla with rāʾ changed to yāʾ, faʿīla resolving doubling, and a nisba with fatḥa changed to ḍamma; the narrative pictures particles or seeds. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; FARISI-HUJJA; JISHUMI; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:172/q09/p3 It derives from dhara'a, to bring creatures into being and make them manifest. — Abu Ali compares the contracted form al-bariyya; one further proposal invokes the scattering winds of 18:45. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; FARISI-HUJJA; JISHUMI]

## 7:172/q11 What is the grammatical object of 'took'?  [أَخَذَ ذُرِّيَّتَهُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the verb directly takes the descendants or has an unspoken covenant as its object; the singular and plural readings affect case marking.
- 7:172/q11/p1 Their offspring is the direct object. — Al-Hawfi and Samīn use this straightforward parsing; Jishumi notes a form-dependent case analysis. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; JISHUMI]
- 7:172/q11/p2 An omitted 'covenant' is the object, with offspring appositional or an omitted genitive. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan supply the covenant; Samīn says the singular needs an omitted annexed noun, while the feminine plural permits genitive apposition. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(باقي السبعة); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)]

## 7:172/q12 Why use these words and this order for the taking?  [أَخَذَ مِن ظُهُورِهِمْ ذُرِّيَّتَهُمْ · rhetoric]
- 7:172/q12/p1 The wording dignifies and strengthens the covenant and gives prominence to the descendants. — Alusi says 'took' signals selection and covenantal gravity; Biqai sees fathers' strong backs and human capacity; Alusi says delaying the object creates anticipation and keeps its pronoun after its antecedent. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

- 7:172/q13/p3 God established innate knowledge and rational signs, so their selves testify by their condition. — Zamakhshari, Nasafi and others treat the encounter as representation; Ibn Kathir compares circumstantial witness in 6:130, 9:17, 100:7 and 14:34. - 7:172/q13/p4 God made direct witnessing of His lordship possible in a prior realm. — Tabatabai reads the knowledge as immediate experience, with the self, tongue and heart aligned; some Imami reports say knowledge was fixed even when the scene was forgotten. ## 7:172/q14 What does 'Am I not your Lord?' assert?  [أَلَسْتُ بِرَبِّكُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the confirmation is a figurative depiction of evidence or an actual communication in an unseen mode.
- 7:172/q14/p1 It is a confirmatory question demanding acknowledgment of God's exclusive lordship. — Al-Zajjaj and Thalabi call it confirmation; Bursevi and Alusi explain Lord as unrestricted master and nurturer. Ibn Ashur compares 46:34; Bint al-Shati notes interrogative laysa with prefixed bā emphatically affirms. ## 7:172/q15 What did the descendants' answer affirm, and how was it given?  [قَالُوا۟ بَلَىٰ · interpretation]

## 7:172/q16 Why did they answer with 'balā', and what would 'naʿam' mean?  [بَلَىٰ · grammar]

- 7:172/q16/p3 Naʿam is ambiguous after a confirmatory negative question; balā is the safe explicit answer. — Al-Saffar, Ibn Hisham and Alusi distinguish affirmation of spoken negation from intended affirmation; Alusi prefers that Ibn Abbas meant insufficiency, not unbelief. [8 notes: BURHAN(الصفار); ITQAN(ابن هشام); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عصفور,ابن هشام,البدر الدماميني,الشلوبين); ALUSI-FULL+]

## 7:172/q19 How does 'lest you say' attach grammatically?  [أَن تَقُولُوا۟ · grammar]

## 7:172/q22 How does the covenant establish proof despite forgotten testimony?  [أَن تَقُولُوا۟ يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ إِنَّا كُنَّا عَنْ هَٰذَا غَٰفِلِينَ · theology]

## 7:172/q46 What additional implications are drawn for innate recognition and the covenant's scope?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: How much the covenant itself establishes and whether its unseen scene is literal, inward, or represented through innate recognition.
- 7:172/q46/p1 The testimony depicts human dependence and a shared capacity to recognize God. — Elmalili renders the exchange as self-witness; Wāḥidī calls God's question the testimony. Biqai says a rational covenant can be read alongside an actual realm-of-atoms address if sound. [3 notes: ELMALILI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BIQAI-FULL]
- 7:172/q46/p2 A first covenant alone does not benefit people who reject later prophetic messages. — Study Quran reports the distinction between primordial acknowledgment and subsequent refusal. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Some commentators)]
- 7:172/q46/p3 Some objection to rational insight also applies to literal testimony if divine guidance or continued certainty is withheld. — Alusi challenges al-Turbushti's argument by asking what insight would benefit one without guidance. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:172/q46/p4 Sahl says divine knowledge distinguishes future deniers and affirmers and the Hour awaits all covenant souls. — He also says God displayed power to elicit the answer, whose assent concerned creation's purpose and trial. [2 notes: TUSTARI]
- 7:172/q46/p5 The Day of Alast names the timeless encounter after the opening of God's question. — Study Quran attributes the phrase to Rumi and other Muslim authors. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Rūmī and some Muslim authors)]

## 7:172/q47 By what means was the acknowledgment elicited?  [وَأَشْهَدَهُمْ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether testimony came through prophets, implanted reason, or direct speech in the earlier realm.
- 7:172/q47/p1 God addressed people through prophets after their reason matured. — Mawardi gives this as an alternative to an immediate primordial address. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 7:172/q47/p2 God gave them reason, enabling them to recognize and testify themselves. — Mawardi lists this alternative; Razi says deniers of the first covenant treat the dialogue as a representation of rational evidence. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 7:172/q47/p3 The direct covenant dialogue occurred as a real exchange. — Razi says those affirming the first covenant read the dialogue literally; Tabatabai notes that a narrator's added wording should not be mistaken for the Imam's own text. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 7:172/q48 What is distinctive about the composition of the covenant verse?  [* · rhetoric]
- 7:172/q48/p1 Its account of divine lordship is especially subtle in meaning and remarkable in composition. — Tabatabai introduces his detailed analysis of taking, distinct descendants and witnessing with this observation. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
