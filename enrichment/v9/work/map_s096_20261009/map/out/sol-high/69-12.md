# 69:12: 23 questions

## 69:12/q01 What does “it” in “make it a reminder” refer to?  [لِنَجْعَلَهَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun points to the physical vessel, the preceding rescue and drowning, the carrying, the broader account, or the sūra’s future Judgment; al-Rāzī tests whether the following pronoun can share its antecedent.
- 69:12/q01/p1 The flood event: the drowning of Noah’s deniers and rescue of the believers. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī name both sides of the event. Al-Rāzī prefers it because both feminine pronouns should have the same antecedent and an ear cannot retain the Ark itself. Al-Ālūsī also prefers it. Al-Biqāʿī includes the ark’s preservation among the acts. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق)+]
- 69:12/q01/p2 Noah’s Ark, preserved as a visible reminder. — Al-Farrāʾ identifies the vessel. Qatāda says early Muslims saw it; Ibn Jurayj locates its planks on al-Jūdī. Al-Kāshifī says the ship reminds people through rescue and destruction, and Kashf al-Asrār says its memorial endures. [20 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); NAHHAS(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,السدي,قتادة); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جريج,قيل); TABATABAI(ابن جريج); STUDYQURAN; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)-; RAZI-FULL(الفراء)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 69:12/q01/p3 Ships of the same kind that God preserved for travel across the seas. — Ibn Kathīr derives the generic category from al-jāriya and cites 43:12–13 and 36:41–42; he prefers this to Qatāda’s report of the original Ark’s survival. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة)+]
- 69:12/q01/p4 The act of carrying Noah’s ancestors in the vessel. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this clause explains why they were carried and treats the act of carrying as a feminine event. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 69:12/q01/p5 The whole account of divine reward and punishment. — Iṣlāḥī says the antecedent is the entire account, beyond the vessel alone. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 69:12/q01/p6 The Resurrection and Judgment described in this sūra. — The Study Quran presents this as an alternative to the ship. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 69:12/q02 What became of Noah’s Ark, according to the reports?  [لِنَجْعَلَهَا · reports]
turns on: Whether the reports concern surviving timbers of the original Ark, later alleged fragments, or the continued class of ships.
- 69:12/q02/p1 Its remains survived for early members of Muḥammad’s community to see, with planks reported on Mount al-Jūdī. — Qatāda contrasts its survival with other ships reduced to ashes or ruins. Ibn Jurayj names al-Jūdī; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī relates this to 11:44 and says his Sūrat Hūd discussion supports the report. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); NAHHAS(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن جريج); TABATABAI(ابن جريج)]
- 69:12/q02/p2 Pieces were found much later in the Islamic period. — Al-Ālūsī reports the claim but says their authenticity is unknown. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 69:12/q02/p3 Ships of its kind, rather than the original Ark, continue to exist. — Ibn Kathīr prefers this account of what God preserved for seafaring, citing 43:12–13 and 36:41–42. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة)+]

## 69:12/q03 What does “reminder” mean here?  [تَذْكِرَةًۭ · meaning]
turns on: Whether tadhkirah is explained as the remembered warning and memorial or as the guidance that completes proof.
- 69:12/q03/p1 A lesson, admonition, and lasting memorial that people can recall and pass on. — Al-Baghawī glosses the noun as lesson and admonition. Elmalılı says witnesses transmit what reason alone cannot know; Iṣlāḥī says it was meant for survivors and descendants, though people forgot and rebelled. Al-Farrāʾ says later ears should preserve the account. [11 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); NASAFI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]
- 69:12/q03/p2 Guidance that shows the path and establishes God's proof, even if a person does not respond. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes the reminder that presents guidance from receptive awareness that actually leads a person to its goal. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 69:12/q04 What lesson is the reminder meant to teach?  [تَذْكِرَةًۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Which consequence of the rescue and destruction a source draws out: divine attributes, conduct, ancestral gratitude, or resurrection.
- 69:12/q04/p1 God saves believers and punishes deniers, showing His power, wisdom, mercy, and effective will. — Al-Wāḥidī’s people of meanings infer a powerful directing agent. Al-Jishumī adds favor to those saved. Al-Rāzī and al-Bayḍāwī include dominance and mercy; al-Biqāʿī includes knowledge, might, and turning to God; Asad emphasizes punishment and saving grace. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]
- 69:12/q04/p2 Later people should heed the warning, obey, believe, and avoid the earlier peoples’ disobedience and punishment. — Ibn Zayd says hearts retain the good and evil heard by ears. Al-Qurṭubī warns this community against disobeying the Messenger. Bursevī says preserving and relaying the account promotes saving faith and communal survival; Iṣlāḥī warns that descendants forgot and rebelled. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 69:12/q04/p3 Descendants of the rescued should remember God's favor and give thanks. — Al-Qurṭubī says this community descended from those carried in their ancestors’ loins. Ibn ʿĀshūr extends the gratitude to all descendants of the saved; al-Ṭabrisī invites gratitude for divine blessings. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 69:12/q04/p4 The earlier catastrophes establish resurrection's possibility and the certainty of Judgment. — Al-Rāzī argues that divine power makes resurrection possible and wisdom makes it certain. Iṣlāḥī says punishment of messenger-rejecters holds in this world and the Hereafter. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 69:12/q05 What does “taʿīhā” mean, and how is it related to waʿy?  [وَتَعِيَهَآ · meaning]
- 69:12/q05/p1 It means to take in, retain, and remember within oneself; awʿā or īʿāʾ can mean storing objects in an external container. — Al-Akhfash cites ordinary use for hearing and storing provisions and an al-Basīṭ line; al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī cite a line about packed provisions. Al-Ṭabrisī cites a line contrasting collecting books with retaining knowledge. Al-Zajjāj, al-Bayḍāwī, and others mark the internal versus external distinction; al-Māwardī also phrases it as retention for oneself versus another. Bursevī cites a poet on stored evil and a Prophetic warning to Asmāʾ not to hoard. [23 notes: MAJAZ; AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الشاعر); JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(النبي); ALUSI-FULL]

## 69:12/q06 What does the “it” in “taʿīhā” refer to?  [وَتَعِيَهَآ · referent]
- 69:12/q06/p1 The reminder. — Al-Naḥḥās explicitly makes the feminine suffix refer to the preceding tadhkirah. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 69:12/q07 How is “wa-taʿīhā” construed grammatically?  [وَتَعِيَهَآ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the lām governing the verb belongs to the preceding purpose construction or is understood as an imperative lām.
- 69:12/q07/p1 It is a subjunctive imperfect coordinated with “li-najʿalahā.” — Al-Samīn says the ordinary kasra and light yāʾ belong to the imperfect of waʿā in this coordination. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 69:12/q07/p2 It is an imperative, “wa-li-taʿiyahā”: let an ear hear and retain it. — Al-Jishumī reports a parse with an imperative lām. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 69:12/q08 How is “wa-taʿīhā” read?  [وَتَعِيَهَآ · readings]
turns on: The vowels on ʿayn and yāʾ, whether a reduced vowel is full sukūn, and whether a reported doubled yāʾ is genuine or misheard.
- 69:12/q08/p1 With a full kasra on ʿayn and a light yāʾ. — Al-Fārisī gives his own Qunbul recitation, like Ḥamza and others, against reports of Qunbul’s sukūn. The majority or common reading is recorded by Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, and al-Samīn. [10 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(القواس عن ابن كثير وسليم عن حمزة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); RAZI-FULL(ابن كثير); JISHUMI(حمزة); TABRISI(ابن كثير); QURTUBI-FULL(طلحة، حميد، الأعرج); SAMIN-DURR]
- 69:12/q08/p2 With sukūn on ʿayn. — Reports name Ibn Kathīr or Qunbul, Ibn Muṣarrif, Ṭalḥa, Ḥumayd, al-Aʿraj, Abū ʿAmr, and sometimes ʿĀṣim. Al-Fārisī, al-Wāḥidī, and Ibn ʿAṭiyya treat the prefix as inseparable from the verb and compare fakhidh, katif, kabid, or 24:52; al-Naḥḥās compares arinī. Ibn ʿAṭiyya doubts a poetic analogy because verse rhythm and necessity differ. Al-Jishumī finds it unsatisfactory and al-Naḥḥās calls reported ʿĀṣim and Ṭalḥa forms erroneous. [15 notes: NAHHAS(طلحة); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن كثير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير والحلواني وقنبل وابن مصرف); RAZI-FULL(ابن كثير); QURTUBI-FULL(طلحة، حميد، الأعرج); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن كثير); TABRISI(ابن كثير); SAMIN-DURR(ابن مصرف وأبو عمرو وقنبل); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مصرف وأبو عمرو); NAHHAS(طلحة,عاصم)-; JISHUMI(ابن كثير وعاصم في بعض الروايات)-]
- 69:12/q08/p3 With a reduced or concealed kasra on ʿayn, between full kasra and sukūn. — Reports differ: al-Qawwās from Ibn Kathīr, Sulaym from Ḥamza, or Ḥamza alone are named; al-Ālūsī also reports an unnamed concealed kasra. [5 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(القواس عن ابن كثير وسليم عن حمزة); JISHUMI(حمزة); TABRISI(ابن كثير); SAMIN-DURR(حمزة); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 69:12/q08/p4 With a doubled yāʾ, or an emphatically articulated yāʾ mistaken for doubling. — The reported attribution is to ʿĀṣim and sometimes Ḥamza. Abū Ḥayyān calls literal doubling erroneous but allows emphatic articulation or a pause pronunciation carried into connected speech; al-Samīn rejects doubling and pause-like doubling. Al-Ālūsī allows emphatic articulation or assimilation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(عاصم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(عاصم وحمزة)-]
- 69:12/q08/p5 With sukūn on the yāʾ. — Al-Samīn names Ḥamza and Mūsā b. ʿAbd Allāh al-ʿAbsī; it can begin a new clause or coordinate with the accusative, its vowel suppressed because a weak letter makes it heavy. Al-Ālūsī also compares 5:89. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(حمزة وموسى بن عبد الله العبسي); ALUSI-FULL(حمزة وموسى بن عبد الله العبسي)]

## 69:12/q09 What is an “attentive ear”?  [أُذُنٌۭ وَٰعِيَةٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the description stresses mere hearing, retention and practice, belief, an illumined inner hearer, or the response to divine guidance.
- 69:12/q09/p1 A hearer who receives, understands, remembers, and benefits from God's message, with reflection, action, and transmission. — The sources vary in emphasis: Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid stress preservation; Qatāda adds understanding and benefit from the Book; al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, and Ibn al-Jawzī add action. Al-Ṭabrisī adds recounting, al-Bayḍāwī sharing, and al-Biqāʿī service to others. Ibn Kathīr says sound hearing and judgment suffice to heed the blessing; Bursevī cites a Prophetic saying on a comprehending listener. [50 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس,الفراء,قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الفراء,قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمران الجوني); TABRISI(ابن عباس,الفراء,قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة,الضحاك); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عمران,السدي,قتادة,مجاهد); ITQAN; BURSEVI(النبي); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI]
- 69:12/q09/p2 An ear that hears or listens to the proclamation. — Ibn ʿAbbās supplies a listening gloss; al-Māwardī reports his shorter hearing gloss; Mujāhid has a transmitted alternative of 'hearing.' [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 69:12/q09/p3 A believing ear. — Ibn Jurayj supplies this explicit gloss. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج)]
- 69:12/q09/p4 A person of understanding and an illuminated heart who comprehends and reflects. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya makes the hearer’s enlightened heart explicit; Abū ʿImrān al-Jawnī says it understands from God. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمران الجوني); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عمران الجوني); DURR-FULL(ابن عمران)]
- 69:12/q09/p5 A recipient of guidance whose awareness carries the person toward the human goal. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī treats awareness as human response to divine guidance, rewardable as the person's act although also attributable to God. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 69:12/q10 Why is awareness ascribed to an ear?  [أُذُنٌۭ وَٰعِيَةٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 69:12/q10/p1 The ear conveys what the heart or its owner understands and remembers, so the ear receives their quality figuratively. — Bursevī says hearts possess awareness and ears deliver reports to them. Al-Ālūsī says hearing alone is literally the ear’s act; preservation and remembrance belong to its owner. Al-Wāḥidī also says the meaning applies to the ear's owner. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 69:12/q11 Why is “an ear” singular and indefinite?  [أُذُنٌۭ وَٰعِيَةٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 69:12/q11/p1 Truly attentive hearers are few, and even one is immensely valuable. — Al-Rāzī says one such ear counts as the great majority before God; al-Zamakhsharī and al-Iṣfahānī are cited similarly, although Abū Ḥayyān calls the claim excessive. Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursevī add that one hearer can help save and perpetuate multitudes; al-Biqāʿī says the form also magnifies the ear. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الأصبهاني والزمخشري); BURSEVI(الكشاف); ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-]

## 69:12/q12 How is “udhun” read?  [أُذُنٌۭ · readings]
turns on: Whether dhāl has ḍamma or is lightened to sukūn; the notes say the sense is unchanged.
- 69:12/q12/p1 With ḍamma on dhāl. — Al-Nasafī says all readers except Nāfiʿ use this form. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]
- 69:12/q12/p2 With sukūn on dhāl, the lightened form. — Nāfiʿ is named by al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī; the Qirāʾāt source says both forms are canonical and have the same meaning. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(نافع); ALUSI-FULL(نافع); QIRAAT-ER]

## 69:12/q13 What is the grammatical gender of “udhun”?  [أُذُنٌۭ · grammar]
- 69:12/q13/p1 It is feminine. — Al-Naḥḥās adduces its diminutive udhayna. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 69:12/q14 What reports connect ʿAlī with the attentive ear?  [أُذُنٌۭ وَٰعِيَةٌۭ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ between a prayer at revelation, a command to teach and retain before revelation, and a physical identification; one source grades the Burayda account unsound.
- 69:12/q14/p1 At revelation, the Prophet prayed that ʿAlī’s ear be the attentive ear; ʿAlī thereafter forgot nothing he heard. — Makhūl’s form is widespread; al-Ḥasan and ʿAbd Allāh b. al-Ḥasan give similar versions. Ibn Kathīr identifies Makhūl’s report as mursal. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī lists further chains and reports that al-Ghāyat al-Marām counts sixteen and al-Burhān cites thirty from Sunnī and Shīʿī sources. [22 notes: TAB-FULL(علي); IBNABIHATIM(مكحول); MAWARDI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); JISHUMI(عبد الله بن الحسن بن الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم وعلي بن أبي طالب); TABRISI(علي,مكحول); RAZI-FULL(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QASHANI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مكحول); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(النبي); ALUSI-FULL(النبي); TABATABAI(النبي,مكحول); STUDYQURAN(Prophet,ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib)]
- 69:12/q14/p2 God commanded the Prophet to draw ʿAlī near and teach him, guaranteeing that he would retain the teaching; the verse was then revealed. — Burayda, or Abu Burda/Abu Barza in variant attributions, transmits this account. ʿAlī’s version has the Prophet call him the ear retaining his knowledge. Al-Suyūṭī calls the Burayda report unsound; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī records further chains and report counts. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(بريدة الأسلمي); IBNABIHATIM(بريدة); WAHIDI-ASBAB(بريدة); JISHUMI(أبو بردة الأسلمي); TABRISI(عكرمة); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن بريدة الأسلمي); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); SUYUTI-LUBAB(بريدة); TABATABAI(النبي); STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]
- 69:12/q14/p3 The Prophet held ʿAlī by the ear and identified it as the intended ear. — Al-Naqqāsh supplies this physical gesture in Bursevī’s citation. [1 notes: BURSEVI(النقاش)]

## 69:12/q15 What other report illustrates retention of the Prophet’s words?  [وَٰعِيَةٌۭ · reports]
- 69:12/q15/p1 Abū Hurayra spread his cloak at the Prophet’s instruction and never forgot the sermon. — Bursevī says the Emigrants and Helpers were occupied with livelihoods while Abū Hurayra remained close to the Prophet; Bursevī uses the account to discuss eloquent speech and contrasts ʿAlī’s retention by prayer alone. [2 notes: BURSEVI(أبو هريرة)]

## 69:12/q16 What inward meaning is given to the attentive ear and the Ark?  [* · inward]
- 69:12/q16/p1 Receptive ears are emptied of self and other things, understand divine secrets, and preserve the primordial covenant; the Ark recalls the holy realm. — Al-Wāsiṭī says natural agitation is ignorance. Al-Qāshānī identifies humanity’s true original home with divine presence and says ʿAlī safeguarded the secrets, citing his primordial nature and precedence in faith and migration. Bursevī reports an eternal naming of aware ears that hear every divine address. [5 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); QASHANI; BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 69:12/q17 How does the attentive ear connect with 50:37?  [أُذُنٌۭ وَٰعِيَةٌۭ · links]
- 69:12/q17/p1 Both passages describe the person with a heart or attentive hearing who receives the reminder. — Al-Qurṭubī and the Study Quran explicitly cite 50:37. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 69:12/q18 How does this verse connect the earlier stories with what follows in the sūra?  [* · links]
- 69:12/q18/p1 The destroyed peoples’ stories lead into the Great Resurrection and Judgment. — Al-Rāzī says the stories prove divine power and wisdom before the stages of resurrection. Elmalılı calls them lesser resurrections and compares the trumpet scenes in 27:87 and 39:68. Iṣlāḥī sees verses 1–12 as evidence of punishment in both worlds. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 69:12/q19 Why does the narrative end with Noah’s people?  [* · links]
- 69:12/q19/p1 It moves from nearer destroyed people to Noah’s people, whose flood was universal and known to later people. — Al-Biqāʿī says Thamūd was nearest Mecca, making the first warning forceful, while Noah’s people covered the earth and were known to descendants. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 69:12/q20 What names of the sūra are reported in connection with this verse?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the title is the established al-Ḥāqqa or al-Jaʿbarī’s otherwise unattested al-Wāʿiya.
- 69:12/q20/p1 Al-Ḥāqqa is its established name. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says it was used in the Prophet’s time and in Qurʾāns, hadith collections, and tafsīrs. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 69:12/q20/p2 Al-Wāʿiya is a reported title, perhaps drawn from verse 12. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites al-Jaʿbarī for it and says he found no earlier precedent. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:12/q21 What does the verse teach about guidance and human response?  [* · theology]
- 69:12/q21/p1 Created beings receive fitting guidance; people also develop through chosen deeds, so revelation shows the way and completes proof, while response determines their lasting happiness or misery. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 20:50 and 87:2–3 for creative guidance, 4:165 for messengers completing proof, 76:3 for the choice of gratitude or rejection, and 8:42 for the established proof. He says acquired dispositions constitute human life’s goal, and awareness remains a rewardable human act although attributable to God. [9 notes: TABATABAI]

## 69:12/q22 How do events and human actions affect one another here?  [* · interpretation]
- 69:12/q22/p1 External events can shape human actions, and human faith and piety can in turn affect external blessings. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī draws the first direction from this verse and cites 7:96 for the reverse direction. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 69:12/q23 How is “tadhkiratan” read?  [تَذْكِرَةًۭ · readings]
- 69:12/q23/p1 With tanwīn in the standard reading. — The Qirāʾāt source identifies this form. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
