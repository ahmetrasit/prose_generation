# 82:8: 21 questions

## 82:8/q01 What does أَىِّ convey here?  [أَىِّ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ayy only selects among possibilities or also expresses contextual wonder.
- 82:8/q01/p1 It specifies whichever one of the possible forms God chooses. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says ayy originally asks what distinguishes one item from its peers, citing 80:18 and 7:185; here it describes an indefinite noun or is annexed to one. Elmalılı's first analysis makes it adjectival, meaning any form; his second makes it conditional. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 82:8/q01/p2 It conveys wonder at the extraordinary, perfected form. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says wonder is a conventional use and perfection comes from context, not the word itself; he reads the resulting form as complete and wondrous. Al-Zamakhsharī senses wonder in ayy, though al-Samīn objects to the syntax he builds on it. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 82:8/q02 What does صُورَةٍ denote in the verse?  [صُورَةٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether form means bodily variation within humanity, kin resemblance, possible animal forms, or inward qualities; some sources allow more than one scope.
- 82:8/q02/p1 A particular human bodily form, with varying sex, build, height, color, beauty, health, and features. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Zajjāj give tall, short, handsome, or otherwise; al-Qushayrī adds wise arrangement of limbs. Ibn Qutayba gives colors and cites 30:22. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī include male and female. Elmalılı extends physical variation to health and intelligence. Al-Iṣlāḥī says distinct faces and forms exhibit effortless creative skill. [16 notes: ZAJJAJ; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(مكحول); SAMIN-DURR; ELMALILI; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Some)]
- 82:8/q02/p2 A form resembling a parent or another ancestor or relative. — Mujāhid names father, mother, paternal and maternal uncles; al-Qurṭubī adds other relatives. A Prophetic report says God brings forth lineages back to Adam after semen settles in the womb. Ibn Kathīr cites the sounder camel analogy for a remote ancestral trait. Al-Durr also transmits a report about ninety-nine veins from each parent. Elmalılı says inherited traits can come from remote ancestors. [25 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,مجاهد); NAHHAS(مجاهد); THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(النبي,مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,مجاهد والكلبي ومقاتل); TABRISI(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي عن موسى بن علي عن أبيه عن جده,مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(عبد الله بن بريدة,علي بن رباح,مالك بن الحويرث,مجاهد); ITQAN(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ELMALILI(Hazret-i Peygamber)]
- 82:8/q02/p3 God could have given a person an animal or another nonhuman form. — Muqātil allows a form other than human. Reported examples vary: dog, donkey, pig, ape or monkey, horse, beast, and bird; al-Biqāʿī includes unknown forms. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reads it as a threat; Ibn Kathīr stresses divine power and gracious choice of an upright human form; the Study Quran suggests punishment and cites 2:65. Al-Ṣādiq contrasts the actual best human form with animal forms. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(أبو صالح,إسماعيل عن سفيان,عكرمة); THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المتأولين); TABRISI(الصادق,عكرمة وأبو صالح); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة وأبو صالح); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو صالح,عكرمة,قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو صالح,عكرمة); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(Others); TABATABAI(الصادق عليه السلام,القمي)]
- 82:8/q02/p4 An inner or moral form: faith or disobedience, felicity or misery, salvation or damnation. — Al-Qushayrī reads image as attribute and fī as ʿalā. The Study Quran reports faith and disobedience as inner qualities, and felicity and wretchedness as states of salvation and damnation, citing 11:105. Elmalılı includes righteousness and its opposite among possible moral traits. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(Qu and R)]

## 82:8/q03 What lexical distinction is made for صُورَةٍ?  [صُورَةٍ · meaning]
- 82:8/q03/p1 With ṣād, ṣūra denotes what contains an object within, unlike sūra with sīn, which encloses from outside. — Al-Zarkashī in al-Burhān makes this consonantal distinction. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 82:8/q04 How does فِىٓ picture the relation between person and form?  [فِىٓ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the spatial preposition depicts an enveloping state or shifts to ʿalā in an inward reading.
- 82:8/q04/p1 It figuratively presents the person's form as a containing state that completely characterizes him. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says fī means association; the circumstantial image is represented as a container. Al-Qushayrī's inward reading instead glosses fī as ʿalā. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 82:8/q04/p2 It has the sense of ʿalā when image means an inward attribute. — Al-Qushayrī uses this prepositional sense for felicity, misery, faith, and disobedience. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 82:8/q05 What is the function of مَّا in مَّا شَآءَ?  [مَّا شَآءَ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical status of mā and the resulting relation of shāʾa to ṣūra and rakkabaka.
- 82:8/q05/p1 Mā is an extra emphatic particle; God assembled the person in whichever form He wished. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Zajjāj connect this with bodily variations. Al-Samīn says shāʾa describes ṣūra; Abū al-Baqāʾ supplies the omitted return pronoun in the modifying clause. Al-Ṭabrisī and Elmalılı give the same emphatic parse. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ELMALILI]
- 82:8/q05/p2 Mā is conditional: God forms the person in whatever image He wills. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq give the conditional paraphrase. Al-Ṭabrisī says the interrogative phrase then needs an omitted governing verb under Sībawayhi's rule, rather than the overt verbs. Al-Bayḍāwī makes rakkabaka the answer; al-Samīn also records an omitted answer. Elmalılı's conditional reading points to possible future reshaping and warning; he says the ancestral report supports a conditional sense in ayy or mā. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ELMALILI]
- 82:8/q05/p3 Mā is accusative as a verbal noun with shāʾa, describing the manner God wished. — Al-Samīn attributes this to al-Zamakhsharī's analysis with fī attached to ʿadalaka, yielding a fine arrangement; al-Samīn includes it among three possible analyses of mā. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)]
- 82:8/q05/p4 Mā is a relative pronoun, accusative as an absolute object: God formed the structure He chose. — Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies the omitted object of shāʾa, ‘He willed it,’ and cites 3:6. Elmalılı mentions a possible separate relative clause in the analysis attaching fī to ʿadalaka. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 82:8/q06 Which verb or implicit element governs فِىٓ أَىِّ صُورَةٍ?  [فِىٓ أَىِّ صُورَةٍ · grammar]
turns on: The reach of the prepositional phrase, permissible government of interrogative ayy, and whether the preceding light ʿadalaka can take fī.
- 82:8/q06/p1 It attaches to رَكَّبَكَ: God assembled the person in the selected form. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya ascribes this to the majority. Al-Qurṭubī argues the light ʿadalaka construction takes ilā, not fī, and says al-Farrāʾ rejected the contrary attachment. Al-Samīn and Abū al-Baqāʾ give this analysis; Ibn ʿĀshūr places the pause after the preceding ʿadalaka. Elmalılı gives it as one sound reading. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; BAYDAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 82:8/q06/p2 It attaches to the preceding عَدَلَكَ, describing the form in which God proportioned the person. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this alternative. Al-Bayḍāwī holds it; al-Zamakhsharī understands a wondrous form, but al-Samīn objects that interrogative ayy cannot follow its governor. Elmalılı considers the analysis sound, with mā shāʾa rakkabaka a separate relative, indefinite, or conditional clause. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المتأولين); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ELMALILI; QURTUBI-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 82:8/q06/p3 It is circumstantial, governed by an omitted element: God assembled the person while present in a form. — Al-Samīn reports this as a second analysis. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 82:8/q06/p4 It attaches to the preceding sequence of creating, proportioning, and making upright. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says this permits a pause after ‘in whatever image’; his other analysis attaches the phrase to rakkabaka and pauses after ʿadalaka. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 82:8/q06/p5 With conditional mā, an omitted verb must govern the interrogative phrase. — Al-Ṭabrisī invokes Sībawayhi's rule: the interrogative phrase cannot be governed by the overt rakkabaka or ʿadalaka after it. Al-Samīn likewise says even a confirming interrogative cannot have its governor before it, comparing kayfa, annā, and informative kam. Another analysis attaches fī to one of shāʾa and rakkabaka so the interrogative properly precedes mā. [3 notes: TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; SAMIN-DURR+]

## 82:8/q07 What does رَكَّبَكَ mean?  [رَكَّبَكَ · meaning]
- 82:8/q07/p1 God composed the person by joining and fitting parts into the form He chose. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya defines tarkīb as composition and joining one thing to another; al-Qushayrī relates bodily limbs to wise designs. Al-Iṣlāḥī reads the sequence of creation verbs as sketching or making, then perfecting, pointing to initial and final stages. The report in al-Itqān glosses it as ‘He shaped you.’ [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QUSHAYRI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ITQAN(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 82:8/q08 How does رَكَّبَكَ connect syntactically with the preceding clause?  [رَكَّبَكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the unexpressed link is explanatory or an omitted conjunction.
- 82:8/q08/p1 It explains the preceding عَدَلَكَ without an expressed conjunction. — Al-Bayḍāwī says explanation accounts for lack of coordination. Al-Samīn likewise calls it explanatory rather than joined with fāʾ. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 82:8/q08/p2 It is coordinated with عَدَلَكَ while its conjunction is omitted. — Al-Ṭabrisī states this under the emphatic mā analysis. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 82:8/q09 How is رَكَّبَكَ pronounced before the following كَلَّا?  [رَكَّبَكَ · readings]
turns on: The transmission and phonetic treatment of adjacent kāfs at the verse boundary.
- 82:8/q09/p1 The adjacent kāfs assimilate across the verse boundary. — Al-Akhfash gives conditions for assimilation according to which letter is vowelled. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Khārija from Nāfiʿ. Ibn al-Jazarī reports al-Ahwāzī from Ruways and says al-Mabhaj includes it; the locus is among Ruways's optional assimilations. [5 notes: AKHFASH; IBNATIYYA-FULL(خارجة عن نافع); NASHR(الأهوازي عن رويس,الباقون عن رويس)]
- 82:8/q09/p2 The two kāfs are pronounced clearly without assimilation. — Al-Akhfash also describes conditions for leaving them distinct. Ibn al-Jazarī reports clear articulation through Abū al-Ṭayyib and Ibn Muqsim from al-Tammār from Ruways, and through other transmitters; both pronunciations are sound from Ruways. His list spans fourteen optional assimilation places, including 82:8–9. [4 notes: AKHFASH; NASHR(أبو الطيب وابن مقسم عن التمار عن رويس,الباقون عن رويس)]

## 82:8/q10 What do the reported accounts say happens when a child's resemblance is determined?  [صُورَةٍ رَكَّبَكَ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in the mechanism and detail used to explain resemblance.
- 82:8/q10/p1 After semen settles in the womb, God brings forth ancestral lineages reaching Adam, allowing resemblance beyond the parents. — The Prophet's reported answer to a questioner invokes this verse. Al-Biqāʿī and al-Durr's Mālik b. al-Ḥuwayrith version adds that the man's fluid spreads through the woman's veins and nerves and that God gathers the ancestral veins on day seven; other transmissions omit the timing. Al-Wāḥidī uses the report to support Mujāhid's kin-likeness reading. The al-Ṭabrisī route is through al-Riḍā and his forefathers. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(النبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي عن موسى بن علي عن أبيه عن جده); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(علي بن رباح,مالك بن الحويرث); ITQAN(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ELMALILI(Hazret-i Peygamber)]
- 82:8/q10/p2 Each parent has ninety-nine veins that ask God for their likeness when a child is formed. — Al-Durr transmits this in a report about a black Anṣārī newborn. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(عبد الله بن بريدة)]
- 82:8/q10/p3 A newborn's unexpected color can reflect an ancestral trait, like the color of a camel among its herd. — Ibn Kathīr cites the report from Abū Hurayra in both Ṣaḥīḥs as a bloodline analogy. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 82:8/q11 How are the ancestral-lineage reports evaluated?  [صُورَةٍ رَكَّبَكَ · reports]
turns on: The specific transmission and chain being evaluated.
- 82:8/q11/p1 The particular lineage report would settle the verse if sound, but its chain is unestablished or weak. — Ibn Kathīr calls Muṭahhar b. al-Haytham abandoned according to Abū Saʿīd b. Yūnus and notes Ibn Ḥibbān's criticism. Al-Itqān labels its transmitted version weak. Al-Wāḥidī cites a version without grading it. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ITQAN(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 82:8/q11/p2 A transmitted ancestral-veins version has trustworthy transmitters or a good chain. — Al-Biqāʿī says the transmitters of Mālik b. al-Ḥuwayrith's day-seven account are trustworthy; al-Durr calls that account good-chain. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(مالك بن الحويرث)]

## 82:8/q12 Whom does the second-person address concern?  [رَكَّبَكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether second-person address is individual or generic.
- 82:8/q12/p1 It addresses humankind generally, not a single individual. — Ibn Qutayba compares ‘O human, striving toward your Lord’ at 84:6 and rejects treating the address as one particular person. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]
- 82:8/q12/p2 It is taken as an address to a particular person in a transmigration reading. — Ibn Qutayba reports the reading only to reject it, explicitly objecting that the address is generic. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(قوم); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL-]

## 82:8/q13 Does the verse teach transmigration of souls?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the singular address denotes one soul in successive forms or humanity in varied created forms.
- 82:8/q13/p1 Some take the verse to imply transmigration of souls. — Ibn Qutayba reports this interpretation from some people and rejects it because the address is not to one individual. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(قوم); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL-]
- 82:8/q13/p2 The verse describes God's varied formation of people and does not imply transmigration. — Ibn Qutayba understands a generic address and varied human beauty, complexion, and other forms; he links variation of colors to 30:22. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL+]

## 82:8/q14 What is the effective cause of inherited forms and traits?  [شَآءَ رَكَّبَكَ · theology]
- 82:8/q14/p1 God's will governs form and inherited traits, including traits from remote ancestors. — Elmalılı says heredity exists and matters, but remote ancestors' traits show judgment belongs to God rather than nature. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the generous Lord governs humanity's finest form and arrangements, citing 95:4, while people have no hand in them. Al-Ṣādiq says God could have made a different form but made humanity best. [4 notes: ELMALILI; TABATABAI; TABRISI(الصادق)]

## 82:8/q15 What does human formation in this verse teach about accountability?  [* · interpretation]
- 82:8/q15/p1 God's purposeful, effortless formation of people shows His power to resurrect and judge them and calls for gratitude and obedience. — Al-Iṣlāḥī argues first creation makes recreation no harder; divine creativity, wisdom, mercy, and justice imply reward and punishment. Human distinctness and vicegerency demand fulfilling responsibility; denial merits punishment. Al-Biqāʿī says the conception report establishes permanent servitude to God and warns against self-reliance. Elmalılı urges faith, gratitude, obedience, and good works. Kuranyolu sees ingratitude in rebellion despite bodily and mental gifts. [13 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 82:8/q16 Do the willed sides of human nature inherently conflict morally?  [* · theology]
- 82:8/q16/p1 No: because God willed both, flesh and spirit have no inherent moral conflict. — Asad makes this inference from the divinely willed sides of human nature. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 82:8/q17 How does this verse serve al-Infiṭār's argument and its neighboring verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether rebuke is present in the wording of the fashioning verse itself or arises from the larger sequence.
- 82:8/q17/p1 Human creation supplies evidence of divine power, purpose, and mercy leading to resurrection, judgment, and the rebuke of heedlessness. — Al-Iṣlāḥī contrasts al-Takwīr's argument from revelation with al-Infiṭār's argument from creation; he says criminals' respite permits reform and recording angels preserve their deeds. Kuranyolu connects formation with ingratitude. Ibn Qutayba says following kallā denies the addressee's delusion. Ibn Hishām, however, objects that a proposed rebuke is not apparent in the fashioning verse and that strained rebuke readings occur elsewhere too. [7 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; ITQAN(ابن هشام)-]
- 82:8/q17/p2 The fashioning verse itself does not plainly express rebuke. — Ibn Hishām calls attempted rebuke readings strained here, in the standing of people, and in ‘then its explanation is upon Us,’ especially after long separation in the third example. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن هشام)]

## 82:8/q18 How is al-Infiṭār related to al-Takwīr?  [* · links]
- 82:8/q18/p1 They are counterpart sūras sharing form, style, meaning, and an opening cataclysm, while developing different proofs. — Al-Iṣlāḥī says both address wealthy, arrogant Quraysh who trusted their buildings; al-Takwīr argues from the Qur'an's source, revelation, and bearer, while al-Infiṭār argues from divine creativity, power, wisdom, justice, and mercy. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 82:8/q19 When in Meccan revelation is al-Infiṭār dated?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The chronology assigned to the sūra by the unnamed authorities.
- 82:8/q19/p1 Some authorities place it in early Mecca. — Asad reports this view without naming its proponents in the note. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]
- 82:8/q19/p2 Other authorities consider a late Meccan date more likely. — Asad reports this as the more likely dating according to others. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]

## 82:8/q20 Is divine speech here understood literally or metaphorically by some interpreters?  [* · theology]
- 82:8/q20/p1 Some say God's utterance is a production of meanings rather than real speech and treat much Quranic speech metaphorically. — Ibn Qutayba reports their analogy of saying a leaning wall ‘spoke.’ The note does not supply his own argument on this specific claim. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(قوم)]

## 82:8/q21 What principles do the cited discussions of images, comparison, and figurative attribution give?  [صُورَةٍ · rhetoric]
turns on: These are distinct rhetorical accounts in cited discussions, not opposing readings of the verse.
- 82:8/q21/p1 Representation presents one image through another, while explicit simile presents two; abstract likeness needs a sensory model. — Al-Jurjānī uses a mirror versus direct sight, the moon's physical distance and near light for status and generosity, a directly seen flower, and a branch whose borrowed quality vanishes when comparison ends. [4 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 82:8/q21/p2 Poetic wonder can assert an impossible sight as fact; a broad shared device leaves each specific image distinct. — Al-Jurjānī discusses two suns, suns rising from beloveds' homes without an east, a walking moon, and embracing lions. He says specific wonders prevent a charge of plagiarism; a reverse technique transfers a subtle feature of the comparison's source to its target so simile or metaphor seems to disappear. [6 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 82:8/q21/p3 A complete metaphor would lend a name its full literal use; naming Zayd a lion does not transfer ‘lion’ without restriction. — Al-Jurjānī compares metaphor to lending clothing for full use; ‘Zayd’ restricts ‘lion,’ while ‘I meant a gazelle’ retains the animal noun's ordinary sense. [3 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 82:8/q21/p4 Figurative attribution presupposes a real bearer or agent behind the attributed property or action. — Al-Jurjānī says a man's courage as lion-like first presupposes courage in the lion; a knife cutting or sword killing presupposes the wielder; a prince's building or minting presupposes workers. He excludes the disbelievers' literal claim that time destroys, citing 45:24. [5 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR; JURJANI-ASRAR-]
