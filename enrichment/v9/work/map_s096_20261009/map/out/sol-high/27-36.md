# 27:36: 25 questions

## 27:36/q01 Who or what came to Solomon?  [جَآءَ سُلَيْمَٰنَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the omitted subject is supplied from the messenger, the sent object, or the queen's action; the following return command and the earlier sending supply contextual evidence.
- 27:36/q01/p1 The queen's messenger or delegation came bearing her gift. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Iṣlāḥī explain that a grammatically singular messenger can represent a group. Al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī and al-Ālūsī cite the following singular command to return; al-Nasafī and al-Itqān name the envoy Mundhir. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes that a royal gift would travel in a caravan. [20 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(قيل); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI; ITQAN; BIQAI-FULL(أبو حيان); BURHAN(الكسائي); ALUSI-FULL+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 27:36/q01/p2 The queen's gift or wealth itself came to Solomon. — Yazīd ibn Rūmān says the gifts arrived. Al-Zajjāj, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī allow the gift as the implicit subject; al-Ālūsī prefers understanding that she sent it and it arrived. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(يزيد بن رومان); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 27:36/q01/p3 The queen's act of sending is the implied subject. — Al-Ālūsī mentions this alternative but prefers that the gift reached Solomon. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 27:36/q02 Why is 'came' singular, and what plural form is reported?  [جَآءَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether agreement follows a singular collective messenger or expresses the multiple envoys explicitly.
- 27:36/q02/p1 The singular agrees with a messenger, even if he represents a delegation. — Al-Farrāʾ says a single envoy, reportedly a woman, can be described by the queen's plural; al-Ṭabarī says Arabic can use plural for an unspecified individual; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān explain that rasūl spans singular and plural and gender. Al-Shanqīṭī is not cited; Iṣlāḥī takes the delegation as a whole. Al-Kisāʾī treats the agent as recoverable from context; al-Zarkashī says it is implicit, not grammatically deleted. [10 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL(أبو حيان); BURHAN(الكسائي)]
- 27:36/q02/p2 A reported reading has 'they came,' agreeing explicitly with the messengers. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Ṭabarī attribute jāʾū to ʿAbd Allāh; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Samīn also report plural fa-irjiʿū. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī mention the variant. [6 notes: FARRA(عبد الله); TAB-FULL(عبد الله); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(عبد الله); ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله)]

## 27:36/q03 When did Solomon speak these words?  [فَلَمَّا جَآءَ قَالَ · reports]
turns on: Whether the narrative sequence is immediate or compresses the intervening embassy tests.
- 27:36/q03/p1 He spoke when the envoys arrived. — Al-Ālūsī prefers the apparent sequence and suggests eagerness to guide them. Al-Elmalılı summarizes immediate refusal. [2 notes: ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 27:36/q03/p2 He spoke after the presentation, puzzles, and tests. — Bursevī says the surface sequence compresses events; al-Ālūsī reports an alternative based on Wahb and other story accounts. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 27:36/q04 Whom does Solomon's plural address include?  [أَتُمِدُّونَنِ أَنتُم · referent]
turns on: Whether the sender is included in speech to her representatives; al-Ālūsī cites the later singular command, while al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites who sent the gift.
- 27:36/q04/p1 It addresses the envoys present and, through them, the queen and her people. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Biqāʿī allow the sender to stand within the address; Bursevī uses masculine plural for present envoy over absent queen. Al-Ālūsī prefers extending the rebuke to the sender in light of the singular return command. Ibn ʿĀshūr says an envoy transmits speech to the sender. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues the envoys did not themselves send the gift. [7 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNABIHATIM(يزيد بن رومان); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 27:36/q04/p2 It addresses the envoy and companions or delegation present. — Al-Nasafī says the address is to the envoys; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī allow an envoy and party. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that the envoys did not send the gift. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-]

## 27:36/q05 What does 'are you supplying me with wealth?' mean?  [أَتُمِدُّونَنِ بِمَالٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: The surface question states financial support; the bribery reading uses the preceding demand for submission and the following threat to explain its purpose.
- 27:36/q05/p1 Solomon reproaches their attempt to add wealth to his resources, since he needs none of it. — Al-Farrāʾ's imdād is additional supply; Abū Zayd distinguishes supplying people from other senses of extension; Bursevī says the verb takes its second object with bi. Al-Māwardī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī point to Solomon's visible riches. Al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn stress the interrogative's rejection and disdain. [17 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو زيد); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; FARRA; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 27:36/q05/p2 The gift is an attempted bribe to make Solomon tolerate the queen's rule and unbelief. — Ibn Kathīr says Solomon wanted Islam or force, not treasures; Ibn ʿĀshūr ties the bribe to the queen's unanswered demand for submission in 27:31. Iṣlāḥī says accepting it would concede rule to disbelief. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects the subsequent threat to their refusal. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 27:36/q06 How is أَتُمِدُّونَنِ read and formed?  [أَتُمِدُّونَنِ · readings]

Al-Fārisī says the protective nūn need not assimilate. Readings accounts vary in naming transmitters. ## 27:36/q07 What was the queen's gift?  [بِمَالٍۢ بِهَدِيَّتِكُمْ · reports]
turns on: The reports list different contents; al-Zajjāj's inference from māl favors monetary value.
- 27:36/q07/p1 It included money, gold, or silver. — Al-Zajjāj infers money from Solomon's word māl; Ibn ʿĀshūr says gold and wealth seem likely. The paving accounts describe the envoys' precious bricks. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNASHUR-FULL; THALABI(بعض الروايات); NASAFI-FULL]
- 27:36/q07/p2 It included a box and puzzle objects: pearl, bead, staff, and a vessel. — Gabriel revealed a sealed box containing an unpierced pearl and crooked-holed carnelian bead, according to al-Thaʿlabī. Al-Māwardī adds the Himyarite royal staff and the request for water from neither sky nor earth. Al-Ālūsī recalls these reports. [4 notes: THALABI(جبريل عليه السلام); MAWARDI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; MUQATIL]
- 27:36/q07/p3 It included male and female attendants, horses, and other worldly goods. — Wahb ibn Munabbih, Yazīd ibn Rūmān and Ibn Lahiʿa specify attendants, Arabian or fine-bred horses; Ibn Lahiʿa gives one hundred horses. Ibn ʿAbbās says the attendants' sex was disguised. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,ابن لهيعة,يزيد بن رومان)]

## 27:36/q08 Why is the offered wealth called 'māl' without specification?  [بِمَالٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 27:36/q08/p1 Its indefinite form belittles the offer against Solomon's God-given resources. — Al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī explicitly draw this from the indefinite; Bursevī adds that māl is passing acquisition, also called ʿaraḍ, citing al-Mufradāt's saying that wealth moves from house to house. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; BURSEVI(المفردات)]

## 27:36/q09 What Arabic sound rules bear on the alif of māl?  [بِمَالٍۢ · readings]
turns on: Sībawayh's examples weigh kasra, yāʾ, emphatic articulation, distance, case ending and pause; these are general phonetic analogies, not a reported variant of this verse.
- 27:36/q09/p1 The alif can incline toward imāla where an underlying yāʾ or kasra favors it. — Sībawayh lists māl with nāb and bāʿ as alifs standing where yāʾ could stand; some imale māl in genitive or all cases by analogy with Mālik. Stable forms like ʿimād and ʿābid differ from case-varying māl. Rāʾ with kasra after an alif can favor imāla even against an earlier emphatic; imāla of kāfir is more common than qādir, which Hudbah's verse attests. He notes variation in kāfir, manābir, fār, ḥimār, dinānīr, ʿimrān, ʿafran, and related forms. [19 notes: SIBAWAYH(هدبة بن خشرم)]
- 27:36/q09/p2 Emphatic consonants, proximity, word form, and pause can block or weaken imāla. — Sībawayh says seven emphatics raise the tongue and block imāla adjacent to or after alif, even across intervening letters; ṣaqt for sabaqt illustrates articulatory accommodation. Earlier kasra can counter a more distant emphatic, with qifāf versus miṣbāḥ and rare ʿirqan/ḍīqan analogies. Particles usually resist imāla unless named; ba, ta and vocative yā are special. Geminate fāʿil, mafāʿil and hollow forms resist it. Rāʾ acts like a doubled sound and blocks or permits inclination according to case, distance and neighboring qāf; at pause the lost kasra no longer inclines dāʿ. Fatḥ is favored in fāriq and firāsh. [37 notes: SIBAWAYH(بعض من يقول قفاف,بعض من يقول مررت بالحمار,بعضهم)]

## 27:36/q10 What is the force of the initial fa in 'what God gave me'?  [فَمَآ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether fa versus a hypothetical wāw encodes the addressees' ignorance or only links the explanation; al-Samīn challenges the inference.
- 27:36/q10/p1 Fa makes God's superior gift the consequent explanation for Solomon's rebuke. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says it gives the reason and consequence; al-Nasafī and al-Zamakhsharī contrast a hypothetical wāw, which would presuppose the envoys already knew Solomon's wealth, whereas fa informs them. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that the queen apparently did not know. [7 notes: NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]
- 27:36/q10/p2 The proposed contrast between fa and wāw is insufficiently demonstrated. — Al-Samīn questions whether the particle alone conveys the addressees' knowledge and asks why Solomon attributes the gifts to God rather than merely saying he is richer. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR; NASAFI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)-]

## 27:36/q11 What is the grammatical role of mā in the comparison?  [فَمَآ · grammar]
- 27:36/q11/p1 Mā is a relative pronoun: what God gave Solomon is better than their wealth. — Bursevī parses it as a relative pronoun, making the whole clause the subject of the comparison. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 27:36/q12 What is 'what God gave me'?  [فَمَآ ءَاتَىٰنِۦَ ٱللَّهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether God's comparative gift denotes spiritual office plus kingdom, material wealth alone, or inward knowledge. Al-Ālūsī weighs the first as more fitting.
- 27:36/q12/p1 It is God's gifts of prophethood, religion, wisdom, kingship, and material power. — Muqātil includes Islam, prophethood and troops; al-Thaʿlabī adds wealth and wisdom; al-Nasafī includes blessing. Ibn Kathīr adds vast wealth and forces, and Asad includes insight into hidden realities. Al-Ālūsī prefers prophethood and unique sovereignty as primary; al-Nasafī argues religion brings lasting benefit and he already has ample goods. [22 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; THALABI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 27:36/q12/p2 It can be understood chiefly as Solomon's material wealth. — Al-Ālūsī allows the reading but finds it less eloquent and contextually weaker than prophethood plus sovereignty. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 27:36/q12/p3 In an inward reading, it is certain knowledge, sacred realities, intellectual delight, and luminous vision. — Al-Qāshānī contrasts these with sensory and imaginary adornments. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 27:36/q13 How is ءَاتَىٰنِۦَ read?  [ءَاتَىٰنِۦَ · readings]
turns on: Pronouncing, opening or omitting the first-person yāʾ in connection and pause, and al-Kisāʾī's imāla; codex spelling and avoidance of adjacent vowelless sounds are invoked.
- 27:36/q13/p1 The yāʾ is omitted, leaving ā­tān with kasra on the nūn. — Ibn Kathīr, Ibn ʿĀmir, Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim, Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī are named. The codices omit yāʾ; al-Farrāʾ favors following written form where usage supports it. Qirāʾāt notes call it a dialectal pausal short form. Ibn Khālawayh distinguishes short ātā from long ātānī by derivation from coming versus giving; al-Zarkashī reports Abū al-ʿAbbās's symbolic account of the omitted yāʾ as an enduring spiritual gift. [14 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وابن عامر وأبو بكر عن عاصم وحمزة والكسائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي وأبو بكر عن عاصم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو ونافع وحفص); QIRAAT-ER; NASHR(المدنيان وأبو عمرو وحفص ويعقوب,نافع وأبو عمرو وأبو جعفر وحفص ورويس); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; BURHAN(أبو العباس); FARRA+]
- 27:36/q13/p2 The yāʾ is retained with fatḥa in connected recitation and usually dropped at pause. — Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, Ḥafṣ, Abū Jaʿfar and Ruways are named across accounts. Al-Qurṭubī notes the yāʾ is absent from every codex; Ibn al-Jazarī records differing reports on pause for several transmitters. Ibn Khālawayh says the imams' lengthened form derives from giving. [14 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وابن عامر وأبو بكر عن عاصم وحمزة والكسائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمرو ونافع وحفص عن عاصم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو ونافع وحفص); BAYDAWI-FULL(نافع وأبو عمرو وحفص); NASAFI-FULL(مدني وأبو عمرو وحفص); NASHR(المدنيان وأبو عمرو وحفص ويعقوب,نافع وأبو عمرو وأبو جعفر وحفص ورويس); QIRAAT-ER; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA(الأئمة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو ونافع)]
- 27:36/q13/p3 Some retain the yāʾ at pause or pronounce it with sukūn. — Al-Qurṭubī says Yaʿqūb keeps it at pause but drops it in connection to avoid two vowelless sounds; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Bayḍāwī report a still yāʾ. The reports on exact transmitters vary. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(يعقوب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); BAYDAWI-FULL(نافع وأبو عمرو وحفص); NASHR(المدنيان وأبو عمرو وحفص ويعقوب); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA]
- 27:36/q13/p4 Al-Kisāʾī inclines the tāʾ/alif by imāla while other readers open it. — Al-Fārisī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Bayḍāwī report this distinction; Ibn al-Jazarī refers to his imāla discussion. [5 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وابن عامر وأبو بكر عن عاصم وحمزة والكسائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL(نافع وأبو عمرو وحفص); NASHR; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA]

## 27:36/q14 Why does Solomon ascribe his gift to God?  [ءَاتَىٰنِۦَ ٱللَّهُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the attribution chiefly expresses humility or serves the clause's information structure; both can coexist.
- 27:36/q14/p1 He acknowledges divine favor and avoids boasting of personal wealth. — Al-Samīn directly gives this reason; al-Ālūsī says the clause explains his refusal indirectly, without boasting. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī's indefinite māl makes the contrast stronger. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 27:36/q14/p2 The wording makes the comparison a new explanation rather than a presupposed circumstance. — Al-Ālūsī says 'what God gave me is better than what He gave you' would require a known, confirmatory state; Ibn ʿĀshūr similarly says wāw would suggest prior knowledge by the queen. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 27:36/q15 What were the queen's people given?  [مِّمَّآ ءَاتَىٰكُم · referent]
- 27:36/q15/p1 They were given worldly wealth, ornaments, and luxury. — Al-Nasafī identifies their possessions as worldly adornment; Muqātil and others contrast them with Solomon's religion and kingship. [9 notes: NASAFI-FULL; MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 27:36/q16 What does the turn with بَلْ do?  [بَلْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether bal is described as a move to their motive, to the practical refusal, or to exclusive contrast.
- 27:36/q16/p1 It moves from rejecting the money to explaining the offer by their worldly motive. — Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī say Solomon exposes the only sort of gain they recognize; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it also indirectly refuses and returns the gift. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 27:36/q16/p2 It signals a contrast: only they rejoice in the gift, while Solomon does not. — Al-Suyūṭī says the definite subject and affirmative predicate restrict rejoicing to them after his rejection. Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise says fronting antum makes the gift impress them alone. [2 notes: ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 27:36/q17 Whose gift is 'your gift'?  [بِهَدِيَّتِكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the possessive names the recipient or giver. Al-Samīn says no gift from Solomon is reported; Bursevī cites the envoys' shame at their own offering.
- 27:36/q17/p1 It is the gift received by the addressees; they rejoice when given gifts. — Al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī allow a noun of gift to attach to its recipient; the Shaykh in al-Samīn prefers this. Bursevī prefers it because the embassy had found its own gift meagre before Solomon's display. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a gift brought to them. [12 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); BURSEVI+; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)+; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 27:36/q17/p2 It is the gift given by the queen's party; they rejoice in offering it. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Rāzī report pride in what they give; al-Samīn argues no report says Solomon sent a gift to them, so the possessive should name the giver. Al-Ālūsī notes the indefinite 'gift' may echo pride in the elaborate tests. [14 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMIN-DURR+; BURSEVI-]

## 27:36/q18 Why do they rejoice in the gift?  [أَنتُم بِهَدِيَّتِكُمْ تَفْرَحُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'your gift' attaches to recipient or giver, whether joy is literal pride or ironic return, and whether the sentence states motive or spiritual contrast.
- 27:36/q18/p1 They love receiving gifts and accumulating worldly goods; Solomon does not. — Muqātil says people rejoice in exchanged gifts, whereas Solomon wants Islam. Al-Rāzī connects their joy to material desire; al-Bayḍāwī says they know only outward life; Bursevī prefers this reading in context, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports it. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī allow recipient attribution. [25 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(يزيد بن رومان); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN; BURSEVI+]
- 27:36/q18/p2 They boast of the costly gift they themselves sent or can afford to send. — Al-Rāzī reports this as a second view; al-Bayḍāwī and Abū Ḥayyān report social pride; al-Samīn favors the giver reading; the al-Irshād account adds self-importance and a sense of obligating Solomon. Bursevī objects that they were ashamed after seeing his wealth. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد); STUDYQURAN; SAMIN-DURR+; BURSEVI-]
- 27:36/q18/p3 Their delight in gifts shows they lack awareness of lasting spiritual value. — Al-Qāshānī says Solomon's party rejoices in what comes from God; Jaʿfar says worldly goods are too small to rejoice or grieve over. Asad and the Study Quran say the queen's party prizes material power. [5 notes: QASHANI; SULAMI(جعفر); ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; BURSEVI(جعفر الصادق)]
- 27:36/q18/p4 Solomon ironically tells them to take the gift back and enjoy it themselves. — Al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī report this euphemistic refusal; al-Ālūsī calls it obscure. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the predicate indirectly announces the return. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 27:36/q18/p5 They expect a larger material or political return from sending a ruler a gift. — Al-Ālūsī reports that the hoped-for return could include sparing their country from destruction. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 27:36/q19 Why is أَنتُم placed before 'rejoice'?  [أَنتُم تَفْرَحُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether word order or explicit subject alone yields restriction; some notes make context decisive.
- 27:36/q19/p1 Fronting the subject restricts rejoicing to them and excludes Solomon. — Al-Suyūṭī says a definite subject with affirmative predicate can restrict; al-Sakkākī supports explicit subjects and fronting, while Ibn ʿĀshūr applies the restriction directly. Al-Suyūṭī rebuts al-Falak al-Dāʾir, citing al-Sakkākī; he also traces fuller conditions to ʿAbd al-Qāhir and notes context distinguishes mere emphasis from restriction. [6 notes: ITQAN(السكاكي,الشيخ بهاء الدين); IBNASHUR-FULL; ITQAN+; ITQAN(صاحب الإيضاح,صاحب الفلك الدائر)-]
- 27:36/q19/p2 Some rhetoricians question whether mentioning or fronting a subject itself always conveys exclusivity. — Al-Falak al-Dāʾir reportedly denies the rule, and the author of al-Īḍāḥ objects to al-Sakkākī; Ibn al-Athīr and Ibn al-Nafīs instead emphasize a preposed predicate. Al-Suyūṭī rejects the denial. [3 notes: ITQAN(ابن الأثير وابن النفيس,صاحب الإيضاح,صاحب الفلك الدائر); ITQAN-]
- 27:36/q19/p3 In related constructions, an indefinite subject can restrict by kind or number. — Al-Suyūṭī supplies 'a man, not a woman' or 'one man, not two' as analogies for subject-based restriction. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 27:36/q20 What did Solomon display and solve before returning the gift?  [فَلَمَّا جَآءَ بِهَدِيَّتِكُمْ · reports]
turns on: The narrative reports differ in objects, numbers, methods, and the envoys' identity.
- 27:36/q20/p1 He solved the box, bead, staff, water, and disguised-attendant tests, then sent the gift back. — Muqātil says Gabriel disclosed the box and horse sweat supplied the water. Al-Thaʿlabī describes a termite and white worm threading the pearl and bead, the attendants' washing styles, and return. Al-Māwardī gives the staff's ground-first end and horse sweat; Ibn Lahiʿa adds three questions, including a forgotten question about God's color. Bursevī's al-Kāshifī gives a washing variant. [13 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,ابن لهيعة); THALABI(الأرضة,جبريل عليه السلام,دودة بيضاء); MAWARDI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); TABRISI]
- 27:36/q20/p2 He staged a vast court of gold and silver, troops, and animals to show his wealth and power. — Al-Thaʿlabī gives a nine-farsakh pavement, walls, exotic sea creatures, eighty thousand chairs, jinn, humans and beasts; the envoys were ashamed, and some placed their bricks in a gap. Al-Suddī, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Nasafī and al-Biqāʿī give versions with gold-covered streets or a thousand palaces. [10 notes: THALABI(بعض الروايات); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL]
- 27:36/q20/p3 The accounts differ whether the envoys were men or women. — Al-Māwardī reports the disagreement without selecting one. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 27:36/q21 What does the verse teach about wealth, faith, and gifts?  [* · interpretation]
- 27:36/q21/p1 Religious guidance and divine gifts outrank worldly wealth; Solomon wanted submission, not payment. — Muqātil, al-Wāḥidī and al-Biqāʿī say he sought Islam and obedience. Iṣlāḥī and Kuranyolu stress that a prophet's mission is religion, not booty or purchased friendship; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the threat follows refusal. Jaʿfar and Bursevī counsel detachment, with a Persian couplet calling the world carrion and the Mathnawī contrasting spiritual dominion with clay-bound rule. [14 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SULAMI(جعفر); BURSEVI(المثنوي,جعفر الصادق); ASAD-NOTES; NASAFI-FULL]
- 27:36/q21/p2 An inward lesson is to choose lasting faith and nearness over sensory and imagined adornment. — Al-Qāshānī names certain knowledge and luminous visions; al-Taʾwīlāt, reported by Bursevī, stresses religious commands, afterlife ranks and lasting felicity. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات)]

## 27:36/q22 What follows from Solomon's handling of the gift?  [* · law]
- 27:36/q22/p1 A ruler may display grandeur to visiting envoys. — Ibn Kathīr draws this from the report of Solomon adorning a thousand palaces; al-Biqāʿī relates a similar display. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL]
- 27:36/q22/p2 Returning a polytheist's gift is recommended when it serves a religious interest. — Al-Ālūsī limits the inference to cases with a religious purpose, rather than an unconditional rule. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 27:36/q23 How does the refusal connect to the demand for submission and the next verse?  [* · links]
- 27:36/q23/p1 The gift bypassed Solomon's demand for submission, so he returned it and threatened force if they refused. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 27:31; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the threat is conditional even though the wording attaches it to the messenger's return. Abū Ṣāliḥ reports the gift and army warning; Iṣlāḥī and Kuranyolu say the goal was faith. The Study Quran notes the narrative ends with the queen's faith. [9 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNABIHATIM(أبو صالح); TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 27:36/q23/p2 The story also illustrates the queen's prudence relative to her male advisers. — Kuranyolu draws this lesson from the larger encounter. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 27:36/q24 What general syntactic patterns do the accompanying grammar excerpts illustrate?  [* · grammar]
turns on: These notes give general grammar examples without applying them to the syntax of 27:36.
- 27:36/q24/p1 After aw, a subjunctive may rely on unspoken an; a raised verb may coordinate or begin a new clause. — Sībawayh gives aw in the sense 'unless' with Imruʾ al-Qays, Ziyād and al-Ḥuṣayn. Dhū al-Rumma and Ṭarafa illustrate raised alternatives. Al-Khalīl parses al-Aʿshā's alternatives; Yūnus favors a fresh clause and Sībawayh calls it easier for 42:51. Zuhayr illustrates strained semantic coordination, and ʿAmr ibn Maʿdīkarib supplies a comparison idiom. [13 notes: SIBAWAYH(الحصين بن حمام المري,الخليل,ذوا الرمة,زهير,زياد الأعجم,طرفة بن العبد,عمرو بن معدي كرب); SIBAWAYH(يونس)+]
- 27:36/q24/p2 A verb between conditional jussives may substitute for or describe the first act; conjunctions normally keep it jussive. — Sībawayh uses Zuhayr, al-Ḥuṭayʾa, al-Aṣmaʿī's report, and al-Khalīl on 25:68-69 to distinguish explanation, circumstantial description, and independent raising. He rejects semantically unrelated substitutions except as speech slips. Thumma/fa/waw/aw govern a linked jussive; al-Khalīl allows weak accusative after fa/waw via implicit an but prefers jussive. Completed conditions allow independent raising; 3:111, 47:38, 2:284, 2:271 and 7:186 illustrate case distinctions. Tense and negation affect acceptable responses. [29 notes: SIBAWAYH(بعض القراء,بعضهم); SIBAWAYH+]
- 27:36/q24/p3 Commands, questions, wishes, offers, and prohibitions can govern jussive results when a conditional relation is intended. — Sībawayh and al-Khalīl explain the implicit in-condition and causal test. 61:10-12, 15:3, 6:91, 20:77, 14:31, and 39:64, plus poetry of Jābir, al-Akhṭal and Ṭarafa, illustrate jussive, circumstantial and independent raised alternatives. Actual past facts are not conditional; a noncausal following clause may be raised; rare noun-like complements and an-infinitives are noted. [21 notes: SIBAWAYH(العرب)]

## 27:36/q25 What general textual, rhetorical, and transmission observations occur in the accompanying excerpts?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: These are source excerpts with no expressed application to a particular word of 27:36.
- 27:36/q25/p1 Abū Jaʿfar's recitation has early Medinan transmission and standing. — Ibn Mujāhid traces it through Ibn ʿAyyāsh to Ubayy and the Prophet; Ibn Abī al-Zinād and Yaʿqūb ibn Jaʿfar praise his proficiency and Ibn ʿAbbās connection. Abū Jaʿfar recalls teaching before 63 AH, following Ibn ʿAyyāsh's muṣḥaf, and Umm Salama's blessing. Ibn Mujāhid says he records additional ʿĀṣim variants at their locations. [7 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو جعفر,أم سلمة,ابن أبي الزناد,يعقوب بن جعفر بن أبي كثير الأنصاري)]
- 27:36/q25/p2 Sound pairing and rhyme are effective when wording remains apt to meaning. — Al-Jurjānī lists tajnīs pairs and good sajʿ in prayer, prose and sayings; Prophetic sayings supply examples. A Bedouin's rhymed complaint used precise rukāb, as al-Jāḥiẓ argues against substituting other camel terms. [5 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR(الجاحظ,النبي)]
- 27:36/q25/p3 The verb sharā can name either side of a property exchange. — Ibn al-Jawzī says a buyer gives the price and seller the good; sharā is an antonymic term and in the cited other verse means selling. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 27:36/q25/p4 Some Quranic words can have foreign origins yet be Arabic by naturalized usage. — Abū ʿUbayd reconciles both descriptions; al-Jawālīqī and Ibn al-Jawzī favor his account. [2 notes: ITQAN(أبو عبيد القاسم بن سلام,الجواليقي وابن الجوزي)]
- 27:36/q25/p5 The Quran's eloquence uses apt words and innovative prose styles beyond poetry and soothsayer rhyme. — Ibn ʿĀshūr discusses context-sensitive literal and figurative choice, semantic and syntactic subtlety, and varied expression. He compares poetry's inherited openings and meter, old sermons and soothsayer rhyme, and reports al-Walīd, Unays, ʿUtba and al-Naḍr denying that the Quran is poetry or chant. ʿUmar called poetry the Arabs' knowledge; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Quran is smooth, memorable prose despite its length. [14 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(أنيس بن جنادة الغفاري,الوليد بن المغيرة,عمر)]
