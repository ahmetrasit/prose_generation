# 86:5: 18 questions

## 86:5/q01 How does the opening fāʾ connect this command to what precedes?  [فَلْيَنظُرِ · links]
turns on: Whether the preceding guardian directly licenses the command or an explanatory fāʾ needs an understood premise or omitted sentence; al-Ālūsī denies the latter need.
- 86:5/q01/p1 It follows from the preceding guardian: reflection on creation shows return and judgment are certain and calls for deeds fit for the record. — Kashshāf, Qurṭubī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and Biqāʿī connect the command to the watcher or recording guardian of 86:4; al-Ālūsī says the fāʾ follows from the prior account without an omitted sentence. If the guardian is God or an angel, al-Ālūsī urges awareness of the watcher and action pleasing to God; if it is intellect, he urges use of that faculty. Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites the Angel of Death (32:11), God's taking souls at death and sleep (39:42), and recorded deeds (45:29) as ways preservation works; preserved souls and deeds ground return and recompense. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the link to 86:4 indirectly proves second creation no harder than first. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 86:5/q01/p2 The fāʾ conveys an implicit premise: if resurrection appears impossible, inspect the first creation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the omitted premise is understood from the context. His account gives the question argumentative force. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 86:5/q01/p3 The fāʾ is explanatory and requires an omitted sentence. — Al-Ālūsī attributes this to al-Ṭībī and objects that the sentence is already complete without deletion. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 86:5/q02 What does the command to look mean?  [فَلْيَنظُرِ · meaning]
- 86:5/q02/p1 It commands thoughtful, inward consideration and inference, rather than merely seeing with the eyes. — Naḥḥās explicitly says looking is with the heart. Ibn al-Jawzī reports a Qurʾānic sense of reflection, citing 6:99, 10:101, 80:24 and 88:17, and his teacher ʿAlī ibn ʿUbayd Allāh describes deriving judgment and knowledge through reflection. Biqāʿī calls it insight; Ibn ʿĀshūr says naẓar is intellectual reflection that leads to inferred knowledge. Jishumī adds that contemplating bodily form and changes identifies a maker and governor, and that the path to knowing God is the first duty because knowledge and worship depend on it. [7 notes: NAHHAS; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,شيخنا علي بن عبيد الله); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; JISHUMI]

## 86:5/q03 How is the imperative verb formed?  [فَلْيَنظُرِ · grammar]
- 86:5/q03/p1 Its underlying form is فلينظر; the imperative lām makes the verb jussive. — Naḥḥās gives the underlying form and identifies the imperative lām as the jussive governor. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 86:5/q04 What is the person asked to examine?  [فَلْيَنظُرِ ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ مِمَّ خُلِقَ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes vary in how far reflection ranges beyond the first substance: bodily development and present self, or the sky and guardian of the surrounding passage.
- 86:5/q04/p1 The person should examine the substance and first origin from which God created him. — Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, Baghawī and Ibn al-Jawzī explain the command as considering what the Lord made the human from. Kashshāf says the next verse answers it with spurting fluid. Quṭb also invites comparison of the original state with what the person has become; Elmalılı translates it as forcefully spurting water emerging between backbone and breast. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the question awakens needed knowledge, comparing 80:18; Biqāʿī says this origin deserves full attention because its recognition brings eternal happiness, while human weakness and distraction call for an explicit answer. [10 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; KASHSHAF-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 86:5/q04/p2 The person should study the body's structure, transformations, and present self as well as its starting substance. — Jishumī specifies body, structure, form and successive states. Rāzī and Elmalılı infer a wise, deliberate Creator from intricate human form arising from a simple or lowly drop; Elmalılı describes mucus becoming a rational, remembering, aspiring person and points to breath of the soul. Quṭb asks the person to consider what he has become. Elmalılı says modern embryology, though commonly pursued for medicine, can clarify divine power and moral, religious and human duties. [6 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 86:5/q04/p3 Reflection includes the sky, the night star, and the protection and surveillance surrounding the human. — Elmalılı asks the person to look at heaven and the piercing or lofty night star, consider both inward and outward protection and surveillance, avoid evil and work toward a happy end. This reads the command in the wider sūra setting. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 86:5/q05 Who is the human addressed here?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · referent]
turns on: Whether al-insān is generic, the resurrection denier as a class, a named individual in a reported occasion, or a person marked by self-admiration.
- 86:5/q05/p1 Human beings generally, the sons of Adam, are addressed. — Qurṭubī explicitly glosses al-insān as the son of Adam. General language in Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Quṭb and Elmalılı treats the command as applicable to a person or humanity. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; ELMALILI]
- 86:5/q05/p2 The immediate addressee is a denier of resurrection and judgment. — Muqātil's reading is reported by al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī: the denier should infer restoration from initial creation out of a drop. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Kuran Yolu and Study Quran likewise identify resurrection deniers; Bursevī describes the denier as marked by ignorance and forgetfulness, rejecting resurrection, gathering and judgment. Ṭabarī frames the verse as a challenge to such deniers. [10 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); TABRISI(مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); BURSEVI; TAB-FULL]
- 86:5/q05/p3 It is Abū Ṭālib ibn ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib. — Damghānī names Abū Ṭālib; Ibn al-Jawzī reports the identification from some exegetes. [2 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين)]
- 86:5/q05/p4 It is Abū al-Ashaddayn, whose boast is linked to the verse's revelation. — ʿIkrima is quoted by al-Durr as saying he boasted no one could dislodge him from a hide and he could face ten of Hell's nineteen guards. In al-Suyūṭī's report the name is Abū al-Ashadd, Quraysh would face the remaining nine, and 74:30 supplies the nineteen; these are name and wording variants of the report. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(عكرمة); SUYUTI-LUBAB(عكرمة)]
- 86:5/q05/p5 It is the person absorbed in and admiring himself. — Biqāʿī characterizes the addressed human this way, making contemplation of humble origin a corrective to self-regard. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 86:5/q06 How does مِمَّ خُلِقَ function as a question?  [مِمَّ خُلِقَ · grammar]
- 86:5/q06/p1 It asks from what substance the human was created; the interrogative clause governs the construction after ينظر, and the answer follows. — Naḥḥās says the question is complete; Qurṭubī, Nasafī and Bursevī read 'from what?' as an interrogative, with creation from fluid answered at 86:6. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī say من is governed by خلق and the interrogative clause is accusative after فلينظر, whose action is suspended by the question. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls من a preposition of origin fronted with interrogative ما, and says the interrogation stops نظر from taking a direct object. [8 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 86:5/q07 Why is the alif of interrogative mā absent in مِمَّ?  [مِمَّ · grammar]
- 86:5/q07/p1 The alif drops when interrogative mā follows a preposition. — Naḥḥās derives مِمَّ from مما and says the deletion marks interrogative mā. Burhān contrasts it with relative or declarative mā and cites fīma, ʿamma and mimma, with 2:91, 79:43, 78:1 and 86:5. Bursevī cites ease and an earlier discussion of 78:1. Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise makes government by a preposition the condition. [4 notes: NAHHAS; BURHAN; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 86:5/q08 Why pose and then answer the question of origin?  [مِمَّ خُلِقَ · rhetoric]
- 86:5/q08/p1 The question draws attention to indispensable knowledge, then the text supplies the answer to make the proof immediate. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the answer follows swiftly to prove resurrection possible. Biqāʿī says weak, distracted humans require the answer to be explained. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the question is not a real request for information, compares 80:18 for awakening knowledge, and notes another question may answer it as at 78:1–2. Elmalılı says the passage condenses the stages of 23:12–16 to the most immediate, evident starting point, spurting fluid; the longer account has nine stages: clay extract, drop, clinging form, lump, bones, flesh, new creation, death and resurrection. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 86:5/q09 What origin does the answer identify for human creation?  [مِمَّ خُلِقَ · interpretation]
- 86:5/q09/p1 The human is created from a drop or forcefully emitted fluid, with parental contributions in its formation. — Ṭabarī calls it emitted, poured fluid and records two unnamed poetic lines using tarāʾib with gold, saffron, neck and chest imagery as linguistic evidence. Qushayrī says the gushing fluid comes from the father's loins and the mother's nurturing. Al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Ṭabrisī speak of a sperm drop; Kashshāf says the next verse names spurting fluid. Elmalılı translates it as water spurting between backbone and breast; Iṣlāḥī emphasizes that bodily fluid is an ordinary substance within God's reach. Bursevī says it is matter untouched by life. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURSEVI]

## 86:5/q10 Why is the creator left unnamed in the passive خُلِقَ?  [خُلِقَ · rhetoric]
- 86:5/q10/p1 Creation itself makes God as maker self-evident, so the passive omits Him. — Biqāʿī says the craft reveals the agent, whose identity needs no statement and whose work no other can produce. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the same implication appears in the next verse's passive construction at 86:6. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 86:5/q11 Why pause between the repeated خُلِقَ at this verse end and the next verse?  [خُلِقَ · rhetoric]
- 86:5/q11/p1 A pause separates the identical consecutive words and should be chosen according to syntax and the reader's breath. — Burhān illustrates his recommendation with the خلق at the end of 86:5 and start of 86:6, and with 9:108. He divides three levels of syntactic attachment into six pause kinds, five defined by speech and one by breath; a pause need not stop breath, while a breath break entails a pause. Most pauses are optional except when syntax requires one. The reader should choose the best permissible place to replenish air. Tartīl helps proper pausing, supported by 73:4. [5 notes: BURHAN; BURHAN+]

## 86:5/q12 What does reflection on human formation show about the Creator?  [* · theology]
- 86:5/q12/p1 Complex life emerging from a simple origin reveals a knowing, willing, wise and powerful maker and governor. — Qushayrī infers God's complete power, knowledge and will. Jishumī points to bodily form and changing states, and makes knowing God the prerequisite of sound knowledge and worship. Rāzī says intricate arrangements from a simple drop especially show a freely choosing, wise Creator; the person knows his own state best and repeatedly sees the process in children and animals. Elmalılı repeats those two advantages, describes lowly mucus becoming a rational, remembering, aspiring human, and says medical study of embryology can serve theology and moral duties. Quṭb takes first creation as evidence of divine determination and governance, so humans are not neglected. Elmalılı's cited Muḥammad b. Aḥmad al-Iskandarānī says God creates functions that preserve individuals and species. [13 notes: QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI(Muhammed b. Ahmed el-İskenderânî); QUTB-ZILAL]

## 86:5/q13 Does first creation show that the same human can be raised again?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the recreated body alone must be numerically identical; Ṭabāṭabāʾī answers the objection by locating personal identity in the preserved soul.
- 86:5/q13/p1 Yes: the maker of a person from a drop or scattered components can restore that person for judgment. — Muqātil, Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Ṭabrisī, Kashshāf, Qurṭubī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Biqāʿī, Kuran Yolu, Study Quran and Iṣlāḥī draw the inference from beginning to return; Ibn Kathīr cites 30:27 and says restoration is more readily acknowledged than origination. Rāzī says the original body assembled matter dispersed among parents and the world, so dispersed remains can be gathered again; Elmalılı adds formation and infusion of a soul. Bursevī stresses creation from lifeless matter and calls return easier by rational analogy. Ibn ʿĀshūr says second creation is no harder; Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds that the soul and deeds are preserved, and identity belongs to the soul rejoined to a recreated body, despite bodily similarity. Elmalılı's 23:12–16 stages end in death and resurrection. Qushayrī sees God's complete power; the proof prepares for recompense. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI(مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); QUSHAYRI; TABATABAI+]
- 86:5/q13/p2 No: power to form another body would yield a similar body, not the identical person, so power alone does not prove resurrection. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this objection only to reject it: the preserved soul supplies the person's identity when joined to a recreated body. [0 notes: TABATABAI-]

## 86:5/q14 Is the question chiefly a rebuke that reduces the human to a lowly body?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the bodily origin exhausts the verse's picture of the human; Elmalılı invokes the commander's capacity for thought and self-awareness.
- 86:5/q14/p1 Yes: it rebukes the human by focusing on perceptible bodily form and low origin. — Elmalılı reports this reading from some interpreters. The note treats the human soul through the perceptible body and emphasizes lowliness. [1 notes: ELMALILI(bazıları); ELMALILI-]
- 86:5/q14/p2 The command also treats the human as a thinking observer capable of self-awareness and moral ascent. — Elmalılı rejects a bodily-only reading as incomplete, citing self-awareness at 75:14. He says the passage shows human weakness under surveillance alongside God's ability to create, preserve, elevate and resurrect; reflection begins purifying hidden matters before their exposure at 86:9 and resisting pride. Biqāʿī's self-admiring addressee likewise needs correction. [3 notes: ELMALILI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI+]

## 86:5/q15 What response in conduct should this reflection produce?  [* · interpretation]
- 86:5/q15/p1 Prepare for return and recompense by avoiding evil and keeping a record of pleasing deeds. — Kashshāf, Qurṭubī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and Biqāʿī connect origin, resurrection and the guardian's record: act so the recorder writes what will benefit or please one at judgment. Al-Ālūsī says awareness of God or the appointed angel should move one to pleasing action; even if the guardian is intellect, one should use it and prepare for return. Elmalılı adds avoidance of evil, purification of secrets before exposure at 86:9, restraint from pride and base impulses, and a sound heart, faith and good works. Iṣlāḥī says the delay of judgment is respite removing excuses, and no arrogant one can escape when God seizes him. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 86:5/q16 What purpose of human creation may the command disclose?  [* · interpretation]
- 86:5/q16/p1 Humans are created for worship, knowledge, service, heed and love of God. — Study Quran relates the question to the call to worship at 51:56. It reports Maybudī's account of prostration, heed, service, knowledge and love, with tongues adorned by testimony, hearts by knowledge and joy, and bodies by worship, mentioning 2:231. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(Maybudī)]

## 86:5/q17 What role do will and intercourse have in human reproduction?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether intercourse itself constitutes the reproductive act or prepares the encounter of male and female materials; both accounts also distinguish reproduction from control by will alone.
- 86:5/q17/p1 Reproduction is not governed by will alone; God establishes functions that preserve individuals and the species. — Elmalılı cites Muḥammad b. Aḥmad al-Iskandarānī: dependence on will alone would disrupt the species' increase. [1 notes: ELMALILI(Muhammed b. Ahmed el-İskenderânî)]
- 86:5/q17/p2 Intercourse is the sole reproductive act from which reproduction arises. — This is one formulation attributed by Elmalılı to Muḥammad b. Aḥmad al-Iskandarānī. [1 notes: ELMALILI(Muhammed b. Ahmed el-İskenderânî)]
- 86:5/q17/p3 Intercourse is preparatory: it brings together and deposits male and female materials, whether or not will governs it. — This distinct formulation is also attributed by Elmalılı to Muḥammad b. Aḥmad al-Iskandarānī. [1 notes: ELMALILI(Muhammed b. Ahmed el-İskenderânî)]

## 86:5/q18 How does this verse serve the sūra's wider argument?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes describe different scales of coherence: the immediate oath and guardian within al-Ṭāriq, and a parallel with al-Burūj.
- 86:5/q18/p1 It joins the sūra's oath, guardian and final judgment: God governs creation and will expose recorded deeds for reward or punishment. — Biqāʿī says the sūra exalts the Qurʾān's truth about believers' reward, disbelievers' punishment and exposed secrets; its name al-Ṭāriq signals the true promise through the oath and sworn matter, while the guardian's record leads to reflection on origin. Quṭb says the sūra moves from a cosmic touch linking soul and universe to God's decree and governance, sworn by heaven and the nightcomer at 86:1; first creation shows humans are not aimlessly abandoned. Elmalılı links heaven and star, surveillance, human weakness, divine preservation and resurrection. Iṣlāḥī says signs in the world, the human and the Creator's attributes make judgment certain, and its delay is respite. [8 notes: BIQAI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 86:5/q18/p2 Al-Ṭāriq parallels al-Burūj in theme, introduction and ending, while using different style and arguments. — Iṣlāḥī identifies al-Burūj as this sūra's counterpart and specifies their shared theme, differing discourse style and arguments, and close resemblance in openings and endings. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
