# 13:14: 39 questions

## 13:14/q01 Who does “to Him” refer to?  [لَهُۥ · referent]
- 13:14/q01/p1 God. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān identify the pronoun as God. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 13:14/q02 What does daʿwah mean here?  [دَعْوَةُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the noun is explained by ordinary usage or collective reference.
- 13:14/q02/p1 Calling or requesting the addressee to turn toward the caller, often for help or favor. — Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes the core call from its religious use for deliverance or blessing, since literal turning toward God is impossible. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says request and fulfillment complete, but do not define, call and response. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 13:14/q02/p2 A singular noun that can stand for the supplications of many callers. — Al-Wāḥidī prefers Abū Bakr's reading; callers properly ask the One able to answer, grant and not disappoint. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-BASIT+]

## 13:14/q03 What is “the call of truth”?  [دَعْوَةُ ٱلْحَقِّ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether “truth” names the confession, the quality or recipient of supplication, God Himself, or the summons to true religion.
- 13:14/q03/p1 The confession of divine oneness, especially “There is no god but God.” — Muqātil calls it sincerity and unity; Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿAlī, Qatāda, Ibn Zayd and many later authorities identify the testimony or tawḥīd. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān extend its sense to related law. Ibn Zayd denies the title deity to others. Al-Farrāʾ gives the testimony verbatim; Sībawayh connects the annexed noun to an earlier “God is greatest.” [34 notes: MUQATIL; SIBAWAYH; FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس,قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,علي,قتادة); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); THALABI(ابن عباس,علي); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(عكرمة عن ابن عباس، الفراء، الزجاج، قتادة، وابن زيد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن عباس، وقتادة، وابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,علي); TABRISI(ابن عباس وقتادة وابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(علي وابن عباس والجمهور); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,علي بن أبي طالب); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس، قتادة، مالك عن محمد بن المنكدر,علي بن أبي طالب); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,علي بن أبي طالب); STUDYQURAN(AF, Kl, My)]
- 13:14/q03/p2 The true, rightly directed and answered supplication or worship that belongs to God alone. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Jubbāʾī connect it to sincere monotheist prayer; al-Bayḍāwī cites the following answer clause. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī invokes 2:186 and 40:60; al-Ālūsī says it is free of error and waste. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes deserved invocation from one originating in monotheism. Study Quran cites 40:60; Biqāʿī allows hearing and answering as God wills. [39 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(بعض المتأخرين); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي); RAZI-FULL(بعض المفسرين,صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم,صاحب الكشف); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Kl); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 13:14/q03/p3 Calling upon God because He Himself is al-Ḥaqq, the Real. — Al-Ḥasan's explanation is repeatedly transmitted. Al-Rāzī argues contingent beings can perish but the Necessary Existent cannot; al-Kashf says the explicit divine name reinforces exclusivity. Abū Ḥayyān objects that this yields the awkward “God has God's call”; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī contest that objection. [21 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); JISHUMI(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(الحسن,صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن,بعض المتأخرين); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن,صاحب الكشف); ASAD-NOTES; TABRISI(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 13:14/q03/p4 God's true summons to people to affirm and worship Him, or His guidance to truth and Islam. — ʿAṭāʾ and al-Ḍaḥḥāk make God the caller; al-Ālūsī supplies an omitted object “to worship.” Elmālılı cites 10:35 for God alone guiding directly to truth; Jishumī calls the truth Islam. Biqāʿī allows God's command clarifying truth or decree making the summoned submit. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء والضحاك); JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ELMALILI]
- 13:14/q03/p5 Calling God alone in fear or danger, when no other object of prayer remains. — Al-Māwardī favors the context of idols and cites 17:67; al-Qurṭubī records his preference, and Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī repeat it. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الماوردي); MAWARDI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي)+]
- 13:14/q03/p6 The call of the true religion. — Al-Wāḥidī reports this as a separate possible expansion of the genitive. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 13:14/q04 How does the genitive construction “call of truth” work?  [دَعْوَةُ ٱلْحَقِّ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the two nouns denote one qualified thing or a relation between the call and its object or origin.
- 13:14/q04/p1 It joins two expressions for the same true invocation, or a noun with its qualifying truth. — Al-Ṭabarī and Jishumī compare دار الآخرة in 12:109; al-Thaʿlabī and al-Wāḥidī say the nouns differ in form; Ibn al-Jawzī says they name one reality. Al-Samīn describes reversed adjective annexation; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī permits attributive annexation. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 13:14/q04/p2 It is a real annexation: a call directed to the Real, God, or a call belonging to truth. — Al-Zamakhsharī gives both relationships; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says truth and falsehood divide effective from futile calls; Ibn ʿĀshūr allows the call to originate in monotheism. Abū Ḥayyān objects to the divine-name version, while al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī defend it. [13 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(صاحب الكشف); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]

## 13:14/q05 How does the opening reserve true invocation for God?  [لَهُۥ دَعْوَةُ ٱلْحَقِّ · rhetoric]
- 13:14/q05/p1 The fronted “to Him” conveys entitlement and exclusivity, reinforced by the denial of any response from other gods. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the lām figurative possession by right; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes preposing and the next clause. Al-Wāḥidī says creatures owe God this invocation. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 13:14/q06 What inward call of truth and false call are described?  [دَعْوَةُ ٱلْحَقِّ · inward]
turns on: Whether inward response is described as illumination through truth or annihilation of self and orientation to the Essence.
- 13:14/q06/p1 Truth summons by or toward God and illuminates the responsive heart; ego and Satan draw it toward error. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says answering truth brings one to God, whereas ego brings ruin; Junayd excludes ego and Satan and describes lights without doubt. Al-Qushayrī contrasts proof heard with understanding and heedless obedience to satanic and self-interested promptings. [9 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الجنيد,بعضهم,جعفر); QUSHAYRI]
- 13:14/q06/p2 The answered call belongs to one annihilated from self and abiding in God, while selfhood or attachment to divine names veils response. — Al-Qāshānī connects pure religion to 39:3 and distinguishes God's Essence from names and attributes; self-standing petitioners address imagined objects. [4 notes: QASHANI]

## 13:14/q07 Who are “those who call besides Him” or “those called”?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ مِن دُونِهِۦ · referent]
turns on: Whether the relative refers to the invoked objects or their callers, and whether those objects are only idols or include rational beings.
- 13:14/q07/p1 The relative phrase denotes idols or false gods invoked by polytheists; the callers and an object or resumptive pronoun are understood. — Most commentators identify idols, including al-Wāḥidī's “all interpreters.” Ibn ʿĀshūr makes idolaters the subject of يدعون and omits its object pronoun; al-Bayḍāwī gives both grammatical routes. The Study Quran cites passages on false gods. al-Wāḥidī al-Wajīz names the polytheists as callers. [28 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(جميع المفسرين); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Z)]
- 13:14/q07/p2 The phrase also includes rational beings worshipped or petitioned besides God, such as angels, jinn, spirits, and humans. — Elmālılı says الذين ordinarily denotes rational beings, so restricting it to inanimate idols misses them; he includes sought intercessors and helpers. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says informed devotees recognize such beings behind idols, yet none controls life, death, benefit or harm. [5 notes: ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 13:14/q08 What does “apart from Him” mean?  [مِن دُونِهِۦ · meaning]
- 13:14/q08/p1 Other than, apart from, or away from God. — Abū ʿUbayda compares dūnahu with ʿanhu and cites a poet's dūnī; al-Ṭabarī cites usage for falling short of someone. Asad allows “side by side with God.” [4 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ASAD-NOTES]

## 13:14/q09 How is “they call” read and whom does it address?  [يَدْعُونَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the verb is the usual third-person report or a transmitted direct address.
- 13:14/q09/p1 A reported second-person تدعون directly addresses the callers. — Al-Yazīdī transmits it from Abū ʿAmr; al-Samīn says it supports the alternative parsing. Al-Ālūsī names al-Bazdawī's route through Abū ʿAmr. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(اليزيدي عن أبي عمرو بن العلاء); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(اليزيدي عن أبي عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(البزدوي)]
- 13:14/q09/p2 The usual third-person يدعون describes polytheists calling the idols. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the callers include Quraysh and other Arabs; Abū Ḥayyān supplies an omitted idol object under this parse. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 13:14/q10 Why are invoked idols described with rational plural forms?  [ٱلَّذِينَ لَا يَسْتَجِيبُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether grammatical rationality reflects worshippers' treatment of idols or the mixed referents themselves.
- 13:14/q10/p1 The worshippers treat idols as rational agents, so the text follows their conception. — Al-Wāḥidī points to 7:195; al-Samīn also allows that the set of worshipped beings includes both rational and inanimate objects. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this conventional Arab usage. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 13:14/q10/p2 The forms naturally cover rational beings worshipped alongside inanimate idols. — Elmālılı argues the apparent relative wording includes rational beings and so cannot be limited to idols. [2 notes: ELMALILI]

## 13:14/q11 What do istijābah and “they answer” mean?  [لَا يَسْتَجِيبُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the question asks the verb's lexical sense or the incapacity it asserts here.
- 13:14/q11/p1 The idols cannot hear, answer, grant benefit, avert harm, or give even a minimal response. — Al-Ṭabarī calls the idols deficient; several explain that inert stone or images cannot perceive a plea. Ibn ʿĀshūr says bāʾ plus indefinite شيء denies even a promise or excuse; the Study Quran cites 5:76, 10:18, 25:55, 35:13, 7:194, 31:29, 34:22 and Last Day passages 18:52 and 28:64. [50 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(الحسن,الضحاك عن ابن عباس,علي، وعطاء، وأبو مسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 13:14/q11/p2 Responding to a caller or following the requested action. — Abū ʿUbayda, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī cite poetry for “answer”; Biqāʿī emphasizes compliance with the caller's will. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the augmented form intensifies answering; Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī note a shade of seeking in istijābah. [9 notes: MAJAZ; JISHUMI(الشاعر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(اليزيدي عن أبي عمرو بن العلاء); TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 13:14/q12 Can creatures answer a plea independently of God?  [لَا يَسْتَجِيبُونَ لَهُم بِشَىْءٍ · theology]
- 13:14/q12/p1 No creature has independent power to hear, affect, create, or answer; all interaction depends on God's will. — Elmālılı notes that ordinary public cries can be heard, rejects a forced restriction to inward prayer, and argues that separate beings cannot connect by themselves. He cites clouds, lightning, Zayd and ʿAmr, breath and physicians, 2:186, and God's power to bring water to the thirsty. People and angels respond only by His leave. Ṭabāṭabāʾī grounds the denial in God's sole dominion; Kuran Yolu contrasts Creator and created. [20 notes: ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 13:14/q13 What is the force of “with anything”?  [بِشَىْءٍ · rhetoric]
- 13:14/q13/p1 It denies the smallest answer, even a verbal promise or excuse, much less fulfillment. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says bāʾ marks what one answers with, unlike outcome fulfillment without it at 12:34; the indefinite شيء belittles any imaginable reply. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 13:14/q14 How does the exception “except like” attach syntactically?  [إِلَّا كَبَٰسِطِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the analogy is construed through an omitted response noun and whether kāf is particle or noun.
- 13:14/q14/p1 It excepts an implied kind of answering: the idols answer only as water answers the thirsty caller, namely not at all. — Abū Isḥāq expands the omitted governing noun; al-Nasafī says a verb implies verbal noun, time, place and manner. Al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān analyze annexation of the verbal noun with implicit water as subject; Abū al-Baqāʾ compares 41:49. Al-Ālūsī calls the exception absolute and allows active or passive verbal-noun analysis; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it strengthens the denial. Sībawayh's broader examples show exceptions substituting for underlying syntactic slots despite redundant prepositions or لا; he compares Tamīm and Ḥijāz case, accusative after لست, negative أحد, weak reorderings of particles, and attachment to a negative verb. [19 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; SIBAWAYH]
- 13:14/q14/p2 The kāf may be a particle modifying an omitted verbal noun, or, on another analysis, a noun of likeness. — Al-Ṭabrisī gives both estimates; Abū al-Baqāʾ says particle kāf contains a return pronoun while nominal kāf does not. Al-Samīn says most grammarians reject nominal kāf except al-Akhfash, and permits adjective or circumstantial accusative for the omitted noun. [5 notes: TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]

## 13:14/q15 What situation does the water image depict?  [كَبَٰسِطِ كَفَّيْهِ إِلَى ٱلْمَآءِ لِيَبْلُغَ فَاهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the hands gesture from afar, reach down a well, remain open in water, pursue a reflection, or belong to the Cain report.
- 13:14/q15/p1 A thirsty person calls or gestures to distant, insensible water, hoping it will move to his mouth; it never does. — Mujāhid repeatedly specifies tongue and hand. Al-Zajjāj says this and the well image have the same outcome; Jishumī reports the judge favors it. Al-Ālūsī sees urgent need and mockery in the composite comparison; water cannot perceive gestures, just as idols cannot hear. Biqāʿī says إلى shows the hands do not reach water. [40 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ(بعضهم); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد وأبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(مجاهد، والأصم); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Z); JISHUMI(القاضي)+]
- 13:14/q15/p2 Someone stretches open fingers into or toward water to scoop it, but cannot retain or carry it to the mouth. — Qatāda and al-Ḍaḥḥāk stress hands remaining spread; Ibn Qutayba says an implicit scooping action is understood. Al-Ālūsī distinguishes open باسط from closing a hand; Ibn ʿĀshūr reads the open-handed failed scoop. Al-ʿAwfī's Ibn ʿAbbās says hands already in water cannot lift it. - 13:14/q15/p3 A thirsty person at the edge of a well reaches down without a rope or bucket; its water cannot rise to him. — ʿAlī, ʿAṭāʾ and al-Farrāʾ supply the well or lack of drawing equipment. Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī say “water” stands for its source, supported by a poem about a dug well. Al-Wāḥidī distinguishes reaching without a means from calling distant water; al-Ālūsī says it returns to the first explanation. - 13:14/q15/p4 A thirsty person sees his reflection in distant water and mistakes it for reachable help. — Ibn ʿAbbās is transmitted through ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥa and Ibn Abī Ṭalḥa, as well as other reports; al-Māwardī says the impression deceives him. - 13:14/q15/p6 Cain is suspended over the sea, feeling the water yet unable to reach it. — Bukayr ibn Maʿrūf's report adds summer heat and winter snow. Al-Ālūsī rejects the report as unreliable. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(بكير بن معروف); ALUSI-FULL(بكير بن معروف)-]

## 13:14/q16 What kind of comparison is the water image?  [كَبَٰسِطِ كَفَّيْهِ إِلَى ٱلْمَآءِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the likeness is analyzed as a whole scene, a proverbial futile act, or its moral outcome.
- 13:14/q16/p1 An individual proverb for futile pursuit, comparable to writing on water. — Abū ʿUbayda and Ibn Qutayba cite Arab usage and poems on grasped water; al-Ālūsī reports and challenges the claim that a meager result is obtained, saying the result is none. [8 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيد وابن قتيبة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو عبيدة,بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 13:14/q16/p2 A compound scene of urgent need, failed action, and insensible object, depicting utter failure of idols to respond. — Al-Rummānī combines need, grief and the warning to pray only to God; Būrsawī names a multiple-part parable. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the exception spans caller, object, call and response and works ironically; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it only appears to be a response. [8 notes: RUMMANI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 13:14/q16/p3 The image warns that devotion to a powerless god gives neither benefit nor protection, through life or after death. — Qatāda says the worshipper dies in the practice; Ibn Kathīr explicitly extends the loss to this life and the next; Islahi calls the false gods self-appointed deities and intercessors, a mirage. [7 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 13:14/q17 How is bāsiṭ read?  [بَٰسِطِ كَفَّيْهِ · readings]
turns on: Whether the participle is annexed or carries nunation.
- 13:14/q17/p1 A reported reading gives bāsiṭ tanwīn: “like a spreader of his two palms.” — Al-Ālūsī says it makes the person generic; al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī and Abū Ḥayyān also report it. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قراء)]
- 13:14/q17/p2 The usual reading annexes bāsiṭ to “his two palms.” — Al-Ālūsī says its ordinary sense is spreading the fingers, not clasping them; an anonymous line supports the open hand. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الشاعر)]

## 13:14/q18 What do kaff and the two palms denote?  [كَفَّيْهِ · meaning]
- 13:14/q18/p1 The two open palms or gripping parts of the hands. — Abū Ḥayyān gives akuff and kufūf as its plurals and treats its root as a verbal noun; Biqāʿī links the name to gathering fingertips. Asad notes the literal dual “his two palms.” [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 13:14/q19 What can a right-hand image imply in Arabic poetry?  [كَفَّيْهِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the poem's context is generosity and agency or physical combat.
- 13:14/q19/p1 It can signify deliberate care, entrusted action, and generous purpose. — Al-Jurjānī reads al-Shammākh's banner as ʿArāba's generosity in supplying the poet; al-Buḥturī's hand in a patron's right hand indicates agency and names Yūnus ibn Bughā. He cites Sulaymān ibn Qutta as a contextual test. [4 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 13:14/q19/p2 It can denote physical strength in an appropriate battle context, but that does not fit this poem of generosity. — Al-Jurjānī rejects this interpretation of al-Shammākh's line because the context is provision and praise rather than combat. [0 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR-]

## 13:14/q20 What does the preposition “toward water” imply?  [إِلَى ٱلْمَآءِ · grammar]
- 13:14/q20/p1 It gives the endpoint of stretched hands, which need not actually touch the water. — Biqāʿī sees distance in إلى; Ibn ʿĀshūr says باسط already carries the motion toward its endpoint. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 13:14/q21 Can water rise to a caller outside its ordinary nature?  [ٱلْمَآءِ · theology]
- 13:14/q21/p1 God can make water rise contrary to its natural tendency as a miracle; saints reaching the ultimate cause may dispense with ordinary means. — Būrsawī distinguishes ordinary inability from divine power over nature. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 13:14/q22 What does the purpose lām attach to?  [لِيَبْلُغَ · grammar]
- 13:14/q22/p1 It attaches to the action of stretching the hands, with water as the implicit subject of “reach.” — Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Samīn and Būrsawī make باسط the governor; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the lām purposive. [5 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 13:14/q23 Who or what is the subject of “reach” and the pronouns in “he does not reach it”?  [لِيَبْلُغَ فَاهُ وَمَا هُوَ بِبَٰلِغِهِۦ · grammar]
turns on: Which noun the pronouns resume, and whether a participle predicated of another subject must name its own subject.
- 13:14/q23/p1 Water is the subject: it does not reach the person's mouth. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows water as هو and mouth as the suffix. Al-Samīn, al-Nasafī, Biqāʿī, Būrsawī and Ibn ʿĀshūr give the same natural reading; the water subject of يبلغ is explicit in several grammatical notes. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 13:14/q23/p2 The mouth is the subject and water the object: the mouth cannot reach water in that posture. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn allow this reversal; al-Ālūsī repeats Abū Ḥayyān's point that neither reaches the other. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]
- 13:14/q23/p3 The person stretching hands is هو and water is the suffix: the person cannot attain it. — Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Samīn report this; al-Ālūsī repeats it. Al-Samīn and Abū al-Baqāʾ reject a related participial parse lacking an explicit water subject, which would require “the water” after the participle. [3 notes: TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)-]

## 13:14/q24 Whose invocation is the disbelievers' prayer?  [دُعَآءُ ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether “their prayer” is made to idols or to God.
- 13:14/q24/p1 Their appeal or worship directed to idols. — Muqātil equates calling idols with worship; al-Naḥḥās says it cannot bring a response; al-Qurṭubī identifies shirk. Al-Ālūsī says the ending repeats the preceding futility for emphasis; al-Baghawī links 6:24. [12 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 13:14/q24/p2 Their supplication addressed to God, which is lost or unanswered. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk via Ibn ʿAbbās says their voices are screened from God; al-Zamakhsharī says God does not answer them while idols cannot answer. Al-Ālūsī limits the Ibn ʿAbbās report to afterlife requests, since some such worldly calls receive an answer. [11 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,قيل)]

## 13:14/q25 Can God answer a disbeliever's prayer?  [دُعَآءُ ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ · theology]
turns on: Whether “in error” rules out any answered prayer, and how to account for reported answers to Iblīs and Pharaoh.
- 13:14/q25/p1 Their prayer to God goes unanswered because their voices are veiled from Him. — Ibn ʿAbbās is transmitted through al-Ḍaḥḥāk and others; al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī describe the resulting waste. Al-Ālūsī records and qualifies the view. [10 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI-]
- 13:14/q25/p2 Some prayers of disbelievers are answered, though appeals concerning the afterlife may fail. — Būrsawī cites kalām and law, Iblīs and Pharaoh's private prayer at low Nile water; he infers still greater hope for believers. Al-Ālūsī cites Iblīs to restrict the veiled-voice explanation to the afterlife, while noting another view does not require even that restriction. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,قيل)]

## 13:14/q26 What does “in error” mean here?  [فِى ضَلَٰلٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ضلال emphasizes ruin, moral misdirection, or an appeal that never reaches an answer.
- 13:14/q26/p1 Loss, ruin, falsehood, and an effort yielding no benefit. — Ibn ʿĀshūr glosses ضلال as loss and ruin and فِي as figurative entrenchment; al-Ṭabarī and others link it to idols' total inability. Būrsawī lists waste, loss and falsehood. [20 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 13:14/q26/p2 Straying from truth and right guidance because the appeal is shirk. — Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī describe departure from the right course; al-Qurṭubī names shirk. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 13:14/q26/p3 The invocation misses the path to response or its object and so cannot bring benefit. — Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī distinguish the path of response; al-Qurṭubī cites 7:37 for an appeal that has gone astray from its caller. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 13:14/q27 Why does the ending name disbelievers generally and restate their failure?  [وَمَا دُعَآءُ ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ إِلَّا فِى ضَلَٰلٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 13:14/q27/p1 It generalizes the failed idol appeal to the class of disbelievers and explicitly states the caller's loss after showing the invoked object's incapacity. — Biqāʿī says every such call is futile; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the clause is conjoined to state the caller's disappointment and summarize the argument. Al-Ālūsī says the idol-prayer reading repeats the previous line for emphasis. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 13:14/q28 What other Qur'anic wording is connected to this ending?  [وَمَا دُعَآءُ ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ إِلَّا فِى ضَلَٰلٍۢ · links]
turns on: Which other verse the source connects to this ending.
- 13:14/q28/p1 It repeats part of 40:50. — The Study Quran points to the shared closing statement. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 13:14/q28/p2 The disappearance of disbelievers' fabricated gods in 6:24 parallels their failed appeal. — Al-Baghawī cites the verse for idols failing them in need. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL]

## 13:14/q29 How does this verse connect with the preceding thunderbolt and disputes?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the link is the particular Arbad story, a general warning, or the sequence of arguments for God's uniqueness.
- 13:14/q29/p1 It follows the Arbad and ʿĀmir episode: God's unseen destruction answered the Prophet's prayer. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī connect 13:13's severe stratagem to Arbad's lightning death; Ibn Zayd's report has plague killing ʿĀmir. The Prophet prayed against them; al-Bayḍāwī says the result may also prove his truthfulness. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,بعضهم)]
- 13:14/q29/p2 It generally threatens disputing unbelievers with punishment or an answered prophetic prayer, and exposes their false worship. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī give this general alternative. Al-Ālūsī says a Jew or tyrant occasion could lead to the same link. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 13:14/q29/p3 It concludes proofs of God's uniqueness, power and knowledge by restricting worship and response to Him. — Biqāʿī recalls 12:105–106 and the sūra's revelation theme; Ibn ʿĀshūr refers to first and second creation. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says knowledge and power lead to the exclusivity of true invocation; Islahi frames those demanding punishment while trusting false deities. [8 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 13:14/q30 What occasion or narrated episode is attached to this passage?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which report, if any, gives a particular event behind the verse.
- 13:14/q30/p1 Arbad was killed by lightning and ʿĀmir by plague after the Prophet prayed against them. — Ibn Zayd links this to hidden and spoken speech, protecting angels, and the disbelievers' futile prayer; al-Zamakhsharī and al-Ālūsī report the Prophet's prayer, with no grading stated by al-Ālūsī. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله); ALUSI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 13:14/q30/p2 An unnamed man with a camel-like swelling died after returning to al-Sululiyya's house, and the passage was revealed about him. — Al-Wāḥidī reports his repeated lament, death on horseback, and revelation extending from secret and public speech through this verse. [1 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB]
- 13:14/q30/p3 The Cain-over-water punishment is reported as the image's referent. — Bukayr ibn Maʿrūf supplies sea, summer heat and winter snow; al-Ālūsī rejects the report as unreliable. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(بكير بن معروف); ALUSI-FULL(بكير بن معروف)-]

## 13:14/q31 Is this passage Makkan or Madinan?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether any verses in this section or sūra are excepted from its Makkan classification.
- 13:14/q31/p1 A Meccan report excepts the lightning-to-true-call passage from a larger classification. — Abū Ḥayyān records the report covering 13:12 through 13:14. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 13:14/q31/p2 The entire sūra is Makkan, with no Madinan verse. — Islahi rejects the codices' Madinan classification. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 13:14/q32 What does the verse teach about devotion to God and false gods?  [* · theology]
- 13:14/q32/p1 Only God deserves worship and can answer; created or imaginary rivals have no independent power. — Muqātil says idol worship is loss; Ibn Zayd denies any other deity's title; Kuran Yolu contrasts Creator and created; Islahi says self-appointed deities and intercessors cannot benefit. The Study Quran cites other verses on their inability; Elmālılı grounds all efficacy in divine unity. [24 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 13:14/q33 How do literary experts recognize distinctive speech?  [* · rhetoric]
- 13:14/q33/p1 They detect borrowing, resemblance and departures from familiar genres through detailed knowledge of diction, meaning and style. — Al-Bāqillānī uses al-Buḥturī, Abū Tammām, Abū Nuwās, Muslim and al-Mutanabbī as tests; a claimant's boast does not establish expertise. He says confusion about unprecedented speech reflects inadequate skill. [6 notes: BAQILLANI]

## 13:14/q34 What sermon is transmitted in material accompanying this verse?  [* · reports]
- 13:14/q34/p1 The Prophet's farewell sermon declares lives, property and honor inviolable, cancels pre-Islamic blood claims and usury, states family and trust duties, and commands transmission. — Al-Bāqillānī reports the sacred day/month/place analogy; consent for property; Rabīʿa's blood claim; al-ʿAbbās's usury with 2:279; four sacred months with 9:36; warning against mutual killing and Satan's discord; wives' rights and nushūz with 4:34; provision and clothing; return of trusts; and conveying to absentees. [10 notes: BAQILLANI(النبي)]

## 13:14/q35 What makes verbal arrangement eloquent?  [* · rhetoric]
- 13:14/q35/p1 Grammatical meanings and relations create the construction; bare word order and sound do not by themselves add merit. — Al-Jurjānī illustrates with ضحك and خرج; added words matter when they realize grammatical relations and qualities. [2 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 13:14/q36 Where may recitation restart?  [* · sciences]
- 13:14/q36/p1 A voluntary restart must begin at a self-contained meaningful point, graded by completeness and harm as stopping is. — Ibn al-Jazarī states the rule while discussing start and stop categories. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 13:14/q37 How does Qur'anic expression use parables and concision?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the discussion concerns constructed parables or the broader economy and variety of Qur'anic expression.
- 13:14/q37/p1 It develops symbolic scenes into explicit, inventive parables, including the futile water-seeker. — Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts forgotten settings of Arab proverbs with Qur'anic scenes and lists storm-driven ash at 14:18, a falling associator at 22:31 and a mirage at 24:39–40. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 13:14/q37/p2 Its varied sura styles and concise wording allow layered meanings and contextually signaled ellipsis. — Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts ceremonial openings (1:1, 2:1–2) and direct openings (47:1, 9:1); cites compressed teachings in 24:52, 28:7, 2:179; omissions in 2:177 and 26:63; general-for-particular statements; and al-Zamakhsharī on abbreviation at 74:1. He says subtle meanings may exceed one scholar's grasp. [14 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(أحد بطارقة الروم,الزمخشري)]

## 13:14/q38 How do derivative counts vary between al-Baqara and Āl ʿImrān?  [* · rhetoric]
- 13:14/q38/p1 Al-Baqara has 34 instances of itāʾ derivatives and Āl ʿImrān has 19, fitting their different expressive atmospheres. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī supplies the counts. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 13:14/q39 What Sufi pilgrimage report accompanies the discussion?  [* · reports]
