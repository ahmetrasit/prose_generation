# 50:27: 18 questions

## 50:27/q01 Who is the speaking قرينه?  [قَرِينُهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether the companion is the tempter, the recorder, a human influence, or an inner appetite; the alleged dispute and the relation to 50:23 inform the identifications.
- 50:27/q01/p1 The person's assigned Satan or jinn devil, his companion in this life and at judgment. — Muqatil says he embellished falsehood and evil. Qatada, Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ibn Jurayj and others identify Satan; Ibn Qutayba chooses this reading as reported by Wahidi, and Nasafi says the wording supports Mujahid. Tabarsi says he is paired in punishment; Damghani and Elmalılı extend the companionship from this world to the next. Tabatabai cites 43:36–38 for the accompanying whisperer. Qurtubi reports al-Mahdawi's claim of no disagreement, while Qurtubi himself also transmits a contrary angel report. Ibn Qutayba's Gharib refers readers to his Mushkil explanation; Study Quran directs them to 50:23. Kuranyolu connects the companion to Satan in 14:22. [26 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; THALABI; QUSHAYRI(الشيطان); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة والجمهور); RAZI-FULL(الزمخشري); QURTUBI-FULL(المهدوي); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; NASAFI-FULL(مجاهد)+; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل وابن قتيبة)+]
- 50:27/q01/p2 The angel assigned to record the person's words and deeds. — Ibn Abbas and Muqatil are named by Thalabi and Qurtubi; al-Kalbi and Sa'id ibn Jubayr by Wahidi; Baghawi also names Sa'id. Al-Farra chooses this interpretation in Wahidi. Qushayri describes the angel defending the timing of his record. Razi reports it as an alternative to Satan, in discussion of the companion of 50:23. [12 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس ومقاتل,سعيد بن جبير); QUSHAYRI(الملك الموكل); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي وسعيد بن جبير); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس وسعيد بن جبير ومقاتل,سعيد بن جبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء,سعيد بن جبير); RAZI-FULL(غيره); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس ومقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)+]
- 50:27/q01/p3 A human companion, such as a corrupt scholar or leader whom the person followed. — Tabarsi reports this as another identification. [1 notes: TABRISI(غير مسمى)]
- 50:27/q01/p4 The sinner's instinctive, unrestrained appetites, symbolized by Satan. — Asad contrasts this figure with moral consciousness or reason in 50:23 and compares 41:25, 43:36, and Razi's explanation of 14:22. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 50:27/q02 Is this companion the same as the one in 50:23?  [قَرِينُهُۥ · links]
turns on: Whether the repeated word qarin denotes one continuing figure or different forms of companionship.
- 50:27/q02/p1 The companion can be the same one mentioned in 50:23. — Ibn Ashur identifies him as the companion who had said the record was ready; Razi's discussion considers that identification and the resulting difficulty if the speaker is Satan. Study Quran sends readers to 50:23 for the companion's meaning. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 50:27/q02/p2 Companionship takes several forms, so the companions need not be identical. — Ibn Atiyya explicitly cautions against identifying them; Asad contrasts the moral consciousness or reason of 50:23 with the appetites speaking here. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 50:27/q03 How can Satan's denial fit a claim that he prepared the person in 50:23?  [قَرِينُهُۥ مَآ أَطْغَيْتُهُۥ · links]
turns on: How to understand Satan's earlier preparation: causation, embellishment without compulsion, or a boast later withdrawn.
- 50:27/q03/p1 The two claims appear to conflict if Satan's preparation means that he misguided the person. — Razi raises the difficulty when the earlier statement that the person is ready for punishment is assigned to Satan. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الزمخشري)-]
- 50:27/q03/p2 Satan prepared the path by adorning it, without compelling the person to follow. — Razi reports al-Zamakhshari's reconciliation. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 50:27/q03/p3 Satan spoke at different times: he boasted first, then retracted on seeing shared punishment. — Razi reports al-Zamakhshari's alternative, relating the boast to 38:82–85 and the retraction to the sight of his own penalty. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الزمخشري)]

## 50:27/q04 Why does قال قرينه begin without a conjunction?  [قَالَ قَرِينُهُۥ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the omission mainly marks a new quoted response or continuity in a sequence of unjoined addresses.
- 50:27/q04/p1 It starts a fresh quoted reply in dialogue; the earlier coordinated statement joined concurrent events. — Razi says 50:23 joins the soul's arrival with its driver and the witness's speech, whereas 50:27 has no paired simultaneous events and the preceding fa requires no waw. Baydawi, Nasafi, Samin, and Alusi likewise contrast the coordinated arrival and companion's speech with this independent response. Nasafi compares the Moses–Pharaoh dialogue. Ibn Ashur calls the uncoordinated speech Qur'anic dialogue style and evidence of omitted prior speech. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 50:27/q04/p2 It continues the companion's address in the sequence of replies without conjunctions. — Bursevi compares the unjoined beginnings of ربنا, لا تختصموا in 50:28, and ما يبدل القول لدي in 50:29. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 50:27/q05 What accusation or exchange does the companion's speech answer?  [قَالَ قَرِينُهُۥ مَآ أَطْغَيْتُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the alleged cause of the man's fate is Satan's seduction or an angel's disputed record or timing.
- 50:27/q05/p1 The condemned person blames Satan for leading him into rebellion; Satan answers the charge. — Ibn al-Jawzi calls the exchange abbreviated; Razi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Samin, Bursevi, Alusi, Elmalılı, and Ibn Ashur reconstruct the omitted accusation. Razi and Bursevi cite the command not to quarrel in 50:28; Razi and Ibn Ashur compare 38:59–64. Ibn Ashur says the unbeliever tries to disown his disbelief, blaming the one who adorned it; Bursevi reports al-Kashifi's account of the devil brought forward before Hell. Wahidi reports Ibn Qutayba's comparison to disputation and mutual blame in 37:27–33; Tabatabai recalls the gathered wrongdoers and companions in 37:22. Wahidi says God then tells both disputants not to quarrel before Him. [16 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة والجمهور); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة); TABATABAI]
- 50:27/q05/p2 The unbeliever accuses the recording angel of adding to the record; the angel denies adding anything. — Sa'id ibn Jubayr says the angel wrote only what the person said and did; al-Kalbi is also named for the angel account. [5 notes: THALABI(سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,الكلبي وسعيد بن جبير); BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 50:27/q05/p3 The unbeliever says the angel hurried him away from repentance; the angel denies hastening him. — Al-Farra prefers this account in Wahidi; the man remained far from repentance. Ibn al-Jawzi also reports al-Farra's version. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)+]
- 50:27/q05/p4 Al-Walid says the angel hurried him into a slip or rebellion; the angel says he wrote only after the act. — Thalabi and Qurtubi attribute the identification of al-Walid and the recording angel to Ibn Abbas and Muqatil. Qushayri specifies that the record was not premature. [3 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس ومقاتل); QUSHAYRI(الملك الموكل); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس ومقاتل)]

## 50:27/q06 Why does the speaker say ربنا?  [رَبَّنَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the plural is explained by the scene's awe, the two disputants, or the sense of Lordship.
- 50:27/q06/p1 The plural address suits the awe of this scene, unlike a singular request for a personal favor. — Razi compares singular رب in 7:143, 71:28, 12:33, 66:11, and 38:79. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 50:27/q06/p2 The devil says our Lord because he and the human are disputing together before God. — Tabatabai says the pronoun includes both disputants. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 50:27/q06/p3 The address invokes God as their Lord and caretaker. — Bursevi glosses the address. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 50:27/q07 What does أطغيته mean?  [أَطْغَيْتُهُۥ · meaning]
- 50:27/q07/p1 It means causing someone to exceed bounds or become rebellious or tyrannical. — Jishumi defines ṭughyān as exceeding the limit in corruption and aṭghā as causing transgression. Ibn Ashur specifies arrogance, oppression, or unbelief, notes yāʾ and wāw verb forms, and explains causation by commanding or enticing. Bursevi glosses making the person tyrannical or causing tyranny. Ibn al-Jawzi's lexical note includes deviation or error as a Quranic sense here. [4 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]

## 50:27/q08 What does the speaker deny in ما أطغيته?  [مَآ أَطْغَيْتُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the denial concerns Satan's coercion or influence, or an angel's allegedly unfair record and timing.
- 50:27/q08/p1 Satan denies forcing or independently causing the person's error; he only invited, whispered, embellished, or encouraged what the person chose. — Muqatil says Satan lacked power to mislead without God's authority; Wahidi reports his claim that the man was already forsaken by God. Al-Qurazi specifically denies compulsion. Qushayri, Tabarsi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Ibn Kathir, Bursevi, Alusi, Elmalılı, and Tabatabai emphasize the man's choice. Tabarsi, Baydawi, Ibn Kathir, Bursevi, Alusi, Wahidi Wajiz, Kuranyolu, Study Quran, and Tabatabai compare 14:22: Satan had no authority beyond a call that people answered and should blame themselves. Kuranyolu includes that neither side can rescue the other and Satan denied their association of him with God. Bursevi cites a hadith contrasting the messenger's inability to guide everyone with Iblis's mere adornment. Ibn Ashur says the denial answers the suspicion created by close association. [23 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(القرظي); QUSHAYRI(الشيطان); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(الحديث); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 50:27/q08/p2 The companion denies leading the person astray or making him disbelieve. — Tabari and Nahhas specify transgression into disbelief. Thalabi, Baghawi, Ibn Kathir, and Elmalılı word it as denial of misguidance or seduction. Ibn Atiyya says a general denial of causal influence is false, since Satan whispered and beautified evil. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 50:27/q08/p3 The recording angel denies adding to or prematurely making the record, or hastening the person. — Sa'id ibn Jubayr says the angel recorded only words and deeds actually committed; Qushayri says only after the slip. Al-Farra's favored angel account denies both hastening and adding. The charges vary: adding entries, barring repentance, or rushing the man into error. [8 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس ومقاتل,سعيد بن جبير); QUSHAYRI(الملك الموكل); BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء,سعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس ومقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)+]

## 50:27/q09 Who is the person referred to by the object pronoun in أطغيته?  [أَطْغَيْتُهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether the exchange is between external companions and a human, or between appetites and conscious reason.
- 50:27/q09/p1 The accused human, identified by some as al-Walid ibn al-Mughira al-Makhzumi. — Muqatil names al-Walid and says he lived in prolonged loss. Thalabi and Qurtubi report Ibn Abbas and Muqatil naming al-Walid in the angel version; the other accounts speak of the condemned disbeliever or human companion. [3 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(ابن عباس ومقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس ومقاتل)]
- 50:27/q09/p2 The person's faculty of conscious, controlling reason. — Asad reads the object pronoun as him or it, referring to reason in his account of appetites speaking. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 50:27/q10 Why does the companion invoke the person's prior distant error?  [وَلَٰكِن كَانَ فِى ضَلَٰلٍۭ بَعِيدٍۢ · interpretation]
- 50:27/q10/p1 The person was already deeply astray and freely receptive to evil, so the companion's prompting did not compel or originate his transgression. — Tabari and Nahhas describe a path away from guidance and truth. Wahidi reports Muqatil's detail that he was forsaken by God. Baghawi says he would not return; Baydawi and Bursevi say temptation works on unstable judgment inclined to wickedness. Ibn Kathir says he welcomed falsehood and resisted truth. Biqai says his pervasive disposition made him rush toward what angers God without the slightest prompting. Ibn Ashur reads كان as original and enduring, compares the disavowal in 2:166, and argues a mere follower is not so firmly established as an independent investigator. Tabatabai and Study Quran assign responsibility to the human who accepted suggestion. Asad instead applies the point to the conscious mind's departure from moral truth, explaining 50:24–25. Bursevi quotes Attar's Persian couplet of unsatisfied hunger and the unrewarded sieve as an illustration. [21 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]

## 50:27/q11 What does كان convey about the person's misguidance?  [كَانَ · rhetoric]
- 50:27/q11/p1 The misguidance was original and enduring in the person's makeup. — Ibn Ashur says the perfect verb establishes this duration; his reading of بعيد reinforces it. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 50:27/q12 What does ضلال بعيد convey?  [ضَلَٰلٍۭ بَعِيدٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether distance is from guidance and truth or, in the inward reading, from unity and innate light.
- 50:27/q12/p1 Error far from the path of guidance or truth. — Tabari describes departure from guidance; Nahhas and Baghawi from truth; Ibn al-Jawzi and others gloss the phrase as far from guidance. Muqatil associates it with prolonged worldly loss. Ibn Ashur says distance is a metaphor like a distant place or time and echoes 4:116. [6 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 50:27/q12/p2 Distance from unity and original nature, with the soul turned to the lower realm and veiled in darkness. — Qashani's inward account says this condition makes satanic whispering preferable to angelic inspiration; he compares 14:22. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 50:27/q13 What does بعيد add to the description of misguidance?  [بَعِيدٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 50:27/q13/p1 It intensifies the error as extreme, entrenched, and difficult to reverse. — Ibn Atiyya says return to guidance was difficult. Ibn Ashur treats distance as metaphorical, compares 4:116, and says it reinforces the enduring force of كان. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 50:27/q14 What does the companion's denial teach about responsibility and causation?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether denial of compulsion also excludes Satan's causal role through suggestion, and how that role relates to divine creation.
- 50:27/q14/p1 Each person answers for accepting evil; Satan could invite, but could not force the choice. — Tabarsi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Baydawi, Ibn Kathir, Bursevi, and Tabatabai make the distinction between invitation and acceptance; 14:22 repeatedly supplies the parallel. Study Quran says people would not heed Satan unless already astray. Jishumi says God justly holds each accountable and wrongs no one by disputing on the basis of what He knows. Bursevi's cited hadith says even the messenger cannot guide everyone by his own power, and Iblis only adorns. [12 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(الحديث); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 50:27/q14/p2 Satan did cause transgression by whispering and beautifying evil, while God created it under His prior decree. — Ibn Atiyya says Satan's denial is false if made general; God's creation by decree is just and admits no other lord. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 50:27/q15 What does the quarrel show about companions at judgment?  [* · interpretation]
- 50:27/q15/p1 Companions disown one another and try to shift blame when judgment comes. — Tabari states the lesson and reports Ibn Zayd and Abu Imran explaining the companion's words as disavowal. Baghawi and Ibn Kathir say Satan disowns the unbeliever. Study Quran reports Tabari's innocence interpretation and compares Satan's disavowal and fear of God in 59:16. Ibn Ashur compares 2:166. Ibn Qutayba links pairing wrongdoers and companions to Arab practice of pairing camels and to 37:22 and 44:54; Wahidi compares the mutual blame in 37:27–33. Tabatabai recalls the gathering in 37:22. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو عمران,ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة); TABATABAI]

## 50:27/q16 What does the exchange signify in an inward reading?  [* · inward]
- 50:27/q16/p1 It depicts conflict among inner faculties, especially estimative and intellectual powers, and opposing impulses such as anger and desire. — Qashani says the dialogue is an imaginative form that fixes its meaning in the heart. The powers cooperate when satisfied, but blame one another in loss because each seeks its own benefit and is veiled from unity. Distance from innate light and attraction to the lower realm make satanic whispering effective instead of angelic inspiration; he cites 14:22 and regards the sinner's acquired kinship with the devil as his fault. [5 notes: QASHANI]

## 50:27/q17 What reports are cited to illuminate companions and the quarrel?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports address different aspects: the companion's identity and mutual attack at judgment.
- 50:27/q17/p1 Everyone has a jinn companion; God helped the Prophet against his, and it submitted and urged only good. — Abd al-Razzaq and al-Durr transmit the report from the Prophet. [2 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(رسول الله)]
- 50:27/q17/p2 Haritha saw the people of Hell attacking one another, and the Prophet approved his observation. — Qashani transmits Haritha's report and says the Prophet also approved the devil's assertion that the man was already far astray. [2 notes: QASHANI(حارثة)]

## 50:27/q18 How does this scene fit the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes emphasize different features of the same surrounding judgment passage.
- 50:27/q18/p1 It belongs to the account of resurrection and recompense from the trumpet blast to God's promise of more. — Ibn Ashur locates the account from 50:20 through 50:35. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 50:27/q18/p2 It helps vividly contrast the dreadful fate of disbelievers with the good fate of believers. — Islahi describes the passage's judgment scene and its contrast. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
