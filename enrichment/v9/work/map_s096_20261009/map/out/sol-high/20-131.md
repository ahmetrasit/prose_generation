# 20:131: 28 questions

## 20:131/q01 What does extending one's eyes forbid?  [وَلَا تَمُدَّنَّ عَيْنَيْكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'extending' describes a lingering gaze itself or grief over the wealth missed.
- 20:131/q01/p1 A prolonged gaze of admiration, desire, or attachment to others' worldly goods; a passing glance is excused. — Kashshaf says extending the gaze means lingering without turning away; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī find it stronger than mere looking. Rāzī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī, and Ālūsī excuse a brief glance. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls fixed sight a metonym for love. Zamakhsharī and Rāzī invoke Qārūn's spectators (28:79–80). Ibn ʿĀshūr says amazement, rather than admiration, is intended; Islāḥī attributes the Prophet's concern to compassion for elites, not greed. [32 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; MAWARDI-FULL-; JISHUMI(قيل)-; RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم)-]
- 20:131/q01/p2 It forbids regret or grief over others' fortune rather than a literal look. — Māwardī reports grief as a second sense; Jishumī reports a regretful gaze. Abū Muslim explicitly opposes a literal looking interpretation in Rāzī. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم)]

## 20:131/q02 Why say 'extend your eyes'?  [تَمُدَّنَّ عَيْنَيْكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the wording emphasizes duration of looking or also evokes inward sight.
- 20:131/q02/p1 The expression intensifies looking into sustained attention and longing. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī contrast it with ordinary looking. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares extending a hand toward a desired object; Bursevī traces madd to stretching and notes imdād often concerns desirable things, madd harm. Ālūsī proposes ellipsis or figurative attribution. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:131/q02/p2 The two eyes signify both outer sight and inner vision. — Bursevī, following al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya, connects divine vision to freeing both forms of sight from attachment; he cites 18:24. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 20:131/q03 To whom does the warning apply?  [وَلَا تَمُدَّنَّ عَيْنَيْكَ · referent]
- 20:131/q03/p1 It addresses the Prophet and instructs believers as well. — Abū Ḥayyān says he was least attached to adornment; Ālūsī reports the communal intent. Jishumī says it disciplines believers in renunciation; Muqātil specifies Meccan unbelievers as those whose riches are viewed. [4 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:131/q03/p2 It speaks to the Prophet's concern for the Meccan and Ṭāʾif elites. — Islāḥī says they offered wealth if he stopped preaching, and his concern was compassionate. Study Quran identifies Makkans rejecting him. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 20:131/q04 What are the things God gave them to enjoy?  [مَا مَتَّعْنَا بِهِۦٓ · meaning]
- 20:131/q04/p1 Worldly goods and pleasures, especially the wealth of affluent people. — Mujāhid names the rich. Rāzī and al-Kabīr list sights, sounds, scents, clothes, and marriage. Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī include children, homes, transport, and food; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds gardens, livestock, and women (28:60). Jishumī explains imtāʿ as perceived pleasure and intensive mattaʿa as giving enjoyment; Rāzī says the intensive form suggests repeated abundance. Bursevī links matāʿ to extended benefit or household goods. [10 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI(الكبير)]
- 20:131/q04/p2 They are God-given resources, entrusted to their recipients. — Islāḥī rejects treating wealth simply as inheritance or intelligence; Bursevī cites a hadith that people are successors watched by God and agents over entrusted wealth. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURSEVI]

## 20:131/q05 What does 'أزواجا منهم' denote?  [أَزْوَٰجًۭا مِّنْهُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether azwāj names paired or grouped recipients, their households, or the varieties of goods; Asad broadens the recipients.
- 20:131/q05/p1 Groups, kinds, or counterparts among the unbelievers. — Māwardī, Jishumī, Rāzī, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī derive it from paired resemblance; Ubayy says they resemble one another in turning from right. Ibn ʿAbbās calls them classes. Kashshāf, Nasafī, Ālūsī, and Abū Ḥayyān identify disbelieving groups; Bursevī includes polytheists, Jews, and Christians. Asad reports this majority view; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the indefinite plural minimizes them. [17 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبي بن كعب); JISHUMI(بعضهم); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); ASAD-NOTES(most of the commentators); MAWARDI-FULL]
- 20:131/q05/p2 Men or individual people among them. — Al-Farrāʾ and Zajjāj say men; Rāzī transmits al-Kalbī and Zajjāj. Abū Muslim suggests individuals successively; Jishumī reports a reading that points to people. [5 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم,قيل); RAZI-FULL(الكلبي والزجاج)]
- 20:131/q05/p3 Their male and female pairs, spouses, families, or households. — Jishumī reports males and females; Ibn ʿĀshūr says shared children, homes, and furnishings are enjoyed by each spouse, including each other's beauty; Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the household view. [3 notes: JISHUMI(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم)]
- 20:131/q05/p4 Varieties of the worldly provisions themselves. — Jishumī and Ālūsī report azwāj describing different delights or an omitted object of such kinds. [2 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:131/q05/p5 Many others generally, beyond the truth deniers. — Asad prefers a broad injunction against envy, rendering the phrase 'so many others.' [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES+]

## 20:131/q06 How does 'أزواجا' function grammatically?  [أَزْوَٰجًۭا مِّنْهُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether azwāj is the direct object or a ḥāl after the relative clause.
- 20:131/q06/p1 It is the object of mattaʿnā, with minhum modifying or explaining it. — Qurṭubī calls it the object; Abū Ḥayyān says minhum modifies it; Ālūsī says min is explanatory and the phrase is fronted for emphasis. Samīn also allows this parse. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:131/q06/p2 It is a circumstantial accusative linked to the pronoun, while minhum supplies the people affected. — Kashshāf, Nasafī, Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Samīn relay this; Samīn says mā is followed once according to its form and once by its plural meaning. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)]

## 20:131/q07 What is the 'flower of worldly life'?  [زَهْرَةَ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether zahrah is the bloom of worldly goods or a plural adjective for those who enjoy them.
- 20:131/q07/p1 The world's adornment, brightness, freshness, or beautiful bloom. — Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Suddī, and many commentators gloss adornment. Ibn Qutayba and Wāḥidī's linguists compare tree blossoms; Ṭabrisī gives luminous complexion, radiant suras, and a radiant day. Qurṭubī contrasts the plant flower with ẓuhrah, a star; Ibn ʿĀshūr includes clothing, livestock, gardens, women, children (28:60). Damghānī links 18:46 and 28:60. Bursevī notes a dinar is called a flower for brief freshness; the Prophet in Ibn Kathīr and Durr names earth's blessings worldly bloom. [35 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل اللغة,ابن عباس، السدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(ابن عباس وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عزيز); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة والسدي); DURR-FULL(أبو سعيد,قتادة); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:131/q07/p2 It can describe radiant worldly people, as the plural of zāhir. — Zamakhsharī compares the bright faces and finery of the indulgent with the pallor and rough clothes of the devout; Samīn cites the plural patterns fājir/fajarah and bārr/bararah. Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Ālūsī transmit it; Ālūsī objects that the adjective denotes a stable quality and the circumstantial parse is poor. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BAYDAWI-FULL(يعقوب); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 20:131/q08 Why call worldly life a flower?  [زَهْرَةَ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا · rhetoric]
- 20:131/q08/p1 It dazzles briefly and fades like a plant's blossom. — Jishumī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya stress quick disappearance; the Prophet calls the world sweet and green in Jishumī and Bursevī. Ṭabarī calls its attractiveness a passing deception. Ibn Kathīr, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Islāḥī stress transience; Bursevī compares it with honey's bitter aftermath, carrion once seen through, and Ḥāfiẓ's deceitful woman or costly bride. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 20:131/q08/p2 The word itself can rebuke a base, misguided attraction. — Ālūsī answers an objection to a censure reading by saying the image names a low desire despite natural attraction. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:131/q09 How is زهرة read?  [زَهْرَةَ · readings]
turns on: Whether hāʾ is quiescent or opened; some explain the latter as dialectal or a plural.
- 20:131/q09/p1 With a still hāʾ, the general or majority reading. — Zajjāj, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn, N​​ashr, and Ibn ʿĀshūr record it; both vocalizations carry the same root sense per Qirāʾāt ER. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI(يعقوب); BAGHAWI-FULL(يعقوب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(يعقوب وسهل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); NASHR(يعقوب); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن وأبو حيوة وطلحة وحميد وسلام ويعقوب وسهل وعيسى والزهري); IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER(skn)]
- 20:131/q09/p2 With fatḥa on hāʾ, attributed especially to Yaʿqūb and other readers. — Jishumī and Bayḍāwī call it a dialect form; Ibn al-Jawzī names Ibn Masʿūd, Ḥasan, Zuhrī and Yaʿqūb; Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī add Abū Ḥaywah, Ṭalḥah, Ḥumayd, Salām, Sahl and ʿĪsā. Qirāʾāt ER says syllable weight changes, not root sense. Ibn Jinnī contrasts Basran restriction to attested glottal dialects with Kūfan extension to pharyngeals; Ālūsī records that Kūfan rule. [21 notes: ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI(يعقوب); BAGHAWI-FULL(يعقوب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(يعقوب وسهل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود والحسن والزهري ويعقوب); QURTUBI-FULL(عيسى بن عمر); BAYDAWI-FULL(يعقوب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن وأبو البر هيثم وأبو حيوة وطلحة وحميد وسلام ويعقوب وسهل وعيسى والزهري); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); NASHR(يعقوب); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جني,الحسن وأبو حيوة وطلحة وحميد وسلام ويعقوب وسهل وعيسى والزهري,الكوفيين); IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER(skn); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL]

## 20:131/q10 How is 'زَهْرَةَ' parsed?  [زَهْرَةَ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا · grammar]
turns on: The accusative's governor and its relation to mā, bihi, or azwājan; particular objections turn on definiteness and intervening syntax.
- 20:131/q10/p1 An accusative object or result of mattaʿnā, or an implicit 'made/gave.' — Farrāʾ adduces a Banū Fuqʿas poem, though Ṭabarī finds its prepositional governance weak. Zajjāj says God made life a flower for them; Wāḥidī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī and Qurṭubī transmit this. Kashshāf, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Samīn, Bursevī and Ālūsī include a second object or elided verb. [15 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:131/q10/p2 A circumstantial accusative describing mā, bihi, or the granted goods. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya invokes incomplete definiteness; Ṭabrisī transmits Zajjāj's two possible anchors. Qurṭubī prefers ḥāl with dropped tanwīn and al-ḥayāh appositive to mā (36:40); Samīn supports tanwīn elision at two sukūns with poetry. Makkī prefers the ḥāl in Abū Ḥayyān; Ibn ʿĀshūr and Islāḥī use it. [12 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مكي)+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 20:131/q10/p3 An appositive to bihi or its syntactic place. — Kashshāf, Rāzī, Qurṭubī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Samīn and Ālūsī cite it. Ibn al-Ḥājib finds an accusative substitute from a prepositional phrase weak and substitution from a pronoun disputed. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن الحاجب)-]
- 20:131/q10/p4 An appositive to azwājan, with omitted 'possessors of' or by identification. — Kashshāf, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Samīn and Ālūsī report it; Ālūsī rejects directly identifying people as enjoyment. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:131/q10/p5 An appositive to the syntactic position of mā. — Samīn reports the proposal; Ālūsī repeats it. Qurṭubī objects that li-naftinahum belongs to mattaʿnā; Samīn and Abū Ḥayyān report the same intervening-clause objection. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(آخرون)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مكي)-]
- 20:131/q10/p6 An accusative of censure or specification. — Kashshāf calls it censure or specification; Farrāʾ permits specification of mā or bihi on Kūfan grammar despite the definiteness objection. Ālūsī answers an objection that the world is naturally attractive. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,الفراء); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:131/q10/p7 An adjective of azwājan. — Samīn reports Abū al-Baqāʾ's objection to a definite adjective for an indefinite noun, answering by tanwīn elision. Ālūsī rejects Farrāʾ's reported adjective parse for the definiteness mismatch. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء)-]
- 20:131/q10/p8 A cognate accusative, considered weak. — Qurṭubī compares ṣunʿ Allāh and waʿd Allāh (27:88; 30:6) but finds this possible parse weak. [0 notes: QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 20:131/q11 What does 'لِنَفْتِنَهُمْ فِيهِ' mean?  [لِنَفْتِنَهُمْ فِيهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether fitnah is an open test of gratitude and duty, a particular obstacle to faith, or punishment and misguidance.
- 20:131/q11/p1 God tests recipients through favors, exposing gratitude or ingratitude. — Qatāda and al-Suddī gloss testing; Wāḥidī specifies gratitude. Jishumī says it distinguishes truthful and grateful from false and ungrateful; Islāḥī contrasts favor testing gratitude with hardship testing patience (20:40). Ibn Kathīr says few thank God. Ibn ʿĀshūr says every provision carries a fitting trial and God thereby establishes proof (23:55–56). [30 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,السدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 20:131/q11/p2 The trial is the harder religious duty wealth imposes. — Jishumī lists alms and duties for property, livestock, enslaved men and women, and also spending, pilgrimage and jihad; Kalbī and Muqātil in Rāzī note easier access to pleasure and sin. Ṭabrisī mentions obedience to truth and wealth's rights. [4 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); RAZI-FULL(الكبي ومقاتل); TABRISI]
- 20:131/q11/p3 The trial is the difficulty wealthy rulers face in following an impoverished Prophet. — Jishumī reports the hardship of leaving status; Ṭabrisī stresses the contrast in wealth. [2 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI]
- 20:131/q11/p4 Their abundance is a punishment, misguidance, or a route to deserved punishment. — Ibn Baḥr and Abū Muslim name punishment here and hereafter; Ibn ʿAbbās says favors increase disbelief and transgression, preferred by Wāḥidī as fitting unbelievers. Rāzī cites 9:55; Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ālūsī, and Study Quran include punishment in the afterlife. Ṭabrisī cites the saying that the world has not a gnat wing's weight with God. [17 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ع); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Z); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)+]
- 20:131/q11/p5 Worldly gifts turn recipients away from their interests. — Māwardī offers this as a possible explanation; Qushayrī details distraction, arrogance, excess, and disobedience. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI]

## 20:131/q12 How do the syntax and reading of 'لِنَفْتِنَهُمْ فِيهِ' work?  [لِنَفْتِنَهُمْ فِيهِ · grammar]
turns on: The lām's force and fī's figurative location, alongside a reported verbal variant.
- 20:131/q12/p1 The phrase attaches to mattaʿnā; fī figuratively places the trial within each provision, and lām marks consequence. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares Pharaoh's household taking Moses, whose eventual enmity was a consequence rather than their aim (28:8); he illustrates figurative fī by 4:5 and Sabrah ibn ʿAmr's verse. He says social causes produce abundance whose outcome is trial. Ālūsī also attaches the phrase to mattaʿnā. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:131/q12/p2 Nāfiʿ or ʿĀṣim is reported with ḍamma on the nūn, from أفتنه. — Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn transmit al-Aṣmaʿī from Nāfiʿ, making a person recipient of trial; Ālūsī transmits from ʿĀṣim and glosses causing trial within it. The transmitter's named reader varies. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأصمعي عن نافع); SAMIN-DURR(الأصمعي); ALUSI-FULL(الأصمعي عن عاصم)]

## 20:131/q13 What is 'the provision of your Lord'?  [وَرِزْقُ رَبِّكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether rizq names a future reward, a present material provision, contentment, revelation or guidance, or spiritual sustenance.
- 20:131/q13/p1 Paradise or reward stored in the afterlife. — Yaḥyā, Ṭabarī, Wāḥidī, Muqātil in Ibn al-Jawzī, Baghawī, Jishumī, Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr, and many others identify it as Paradise or reward. Ibn ʿAbbās says resurrection. Ibn Kathīr cites the promise of 93:5; Study Quran compares 28:60, 42:36 and 20:71–73. Asad also allows spiritual sustenance in the hereafter. [29 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,السدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(Ṭū); JISHUMI+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 20:131/q13/p2 Lawful or modest sufficient worldly provision joined with obedience. — Māwardī contrasts lawful lasting provision with abundant transgressive wealth; Qushayrī contrasts lawful little with unlawful much and remembrance with forgetfulness. Kashshāf says illicit wealth is not called provision; Rāzī adds contentment and patience. Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī, and Biqāʿī stress lawful sufficiency. [10 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(رسول الله); MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 20:131/q13/p3 Contentment with one's lot and trust in God. — Sula­mī transmits Abū Bakr ibn Ṭāhir's renunciation, an anonymous combination of trust and contentment, and Abū ʿUthmān's reliance over striving; Ibn al-Jawzī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Ālūsī report contentment. Study Quran preserves trust alone and trust with contentment. [9 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر,أبو عثمان,بعضهم); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Su)]
- 20:131/q13/p4 Prophethood, Islam, and divine guidance. — Kashshāf, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Ālūsī include these; Ṣāḥib al-Kashf prefers this worldly sense as fitting the context. Islāḥī says revealed nourishment cultivates piety. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL(صاحب الكشف)+]
- 20:131/q13/p5 The Qur'an granted to the Prophet. — Abū Shāmah and al-Sakhāwī say it surpasses others' worldly gifts; Itqān reports the same. [3 notes: BURHAN(أبو شامة,السخاوى); ITQAN(أبو شامة وغيره)]
- 20:131/q13/p6 Future lands and spoils for believers. — Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Ālūsī report this worldly promise. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:131/q13/p7 A gift or allotted nourishment, including spiritual knowledge and illumination. — Jishumī gives the broad gift sense; Bursevī lists worldly or otherworldly shares and bodily food, then inward nourishment by knowledge, wisdom, primordial effusion, and manifestation. Qāshānī names afterlife realities and lights. [5 notes: JISHUMI; QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 20:131/q14 Why is the Lord's provision better and more lasting?  [خَيْرٌۭ وَأَبْقَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Which provision is in view, and whether endurance belongs to the gift itself or its final effect.
- 20:131/q14/p1 Its reward endures while worldly splendor perishes and may harm its owners. — Yaḥyā says it never ends; Ṭabarī says no depletion. Rāzī contrasts the temporary goods; Qurṭubī links reward to patience; Biqāʿī says God's sufficient lawful provision avoids excess. Ibn ʿĀshūr says comparative khayr weighs benefits against harms and trials, and gratitude preserves benefit; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ surveys baqā opposing perishing across 28:60, 42:36, 55:27, 87:17, 16:96, 18:46 and other passages. [29 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; STUDYQURAN(Ṭū)]
- 20:131/q14/p2 Present provision with obedience or knowledge has a better outcome than illicit abundance. — Qushayrī values God's pleasure or continuing witness; Jishumī says the Prophet's life gift ends well while theirs ends in Fire and is not coveted; Rāzī and Bursevī speak of contented sufficiency. Islāḥī says believers need no patronage from ungrateful elites. [7 notes: QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI(قيل); RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:131/q14/p3 The possessive 'your Lord's' honors pure provision. — Biqāʿī says it signals lawfulness; Ibn ʿĀshūr says disbelievers' sustenance is rhetorically detached because it is mixed with divine anger and ingratitude's effects. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:131/q15 What occasion of revelation is reported for this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the narrated pledge marks actual revelation despite the sūra's Meccan setting and the verse's context.
- 20:131/q15/p1 Abū Rāfiʿ says a Jewish seller or lender demanded collateral for flour or food, and the Prophet pledged his armor before the verse consoled him. — Accounts differ in whether it was sale, loan or food for a guest, whether Rajab was specified, and whether 15:87 and 20:132 also descended. Wāḥidī and Kashshāf give no grading. Suyūṭī uses the report to propose a Medinan exception. [20 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو رافع); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو رافع); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو رافع); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو رافع); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض الناس); TABRISI(أبو رافع); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو رافع مولى رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL(أبو رافع); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو رافع); DURR-FULL(أبو رافع); SUYUTI-LUBAB(أبو رافع); BURSEVI(أبو رافع); ALUSI-FULL(السيوطي); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو رافع); TABATABAI(أبو رافع); ITQAN(أبو رافع)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(القاضي أبو محمد)-; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية)-; IBNASHUR-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 20:131/q15/p2 The verse belongs to its Meccan context; the collateral incident did not cause its descent. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya argues the sūra is Meccan whereas the pawn occurred late in Medina and the Prophet died with his armor pledged; Qurṭubī reports him. Ṭabāṭabāʾī finds the verse's conclusion incompatible with the story. Ibn ʿĀshūr suggests Abū Rāfiʿ confused recitation of a familiar verse with fresh descent, or that 'descent' meant recitation. ## 20:131/q16 What reports describe the verse's consolation and its use?  [* · reports]
turns on: The separate reported speakers, actions and circumstances.
- 20:131/q16/p1 The Prophet or Ubayy warns that failing to accept God's consolation and watching others' goods prolongs grief. — Sulamī reports an ungraded Prophetic saying; Māwardī reports a caller's announcement, Ṭabrisī reports the Prophet sitting upright and repeating the consolation, and Ibn al-Jawzī quotes Ubayy. Ubayy adds that counting only food, drink or clothes as blessings diminishes knowledge and invites punishment; Baghawī adds trust in God prevents regret. [7 notes: SULAMI(النبي); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبي بن كعب); TABRISI(أبو عبد الله,أبي بن كعب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبي بن كعب); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبي بن كعب)]
- 20:131/q16/p2 ʿUrwa recited the verse after seeing rulers' wealth, went home and called his household to prayer. — Ṭabarī, Jishumī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Rāzī, Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr and Durr preserve variants: seeing rulers, visiting rich people, or hearing reports; some say he recited through wa-abqā and prayed. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(عروة); JISHUMI(هشام بن عروة بن الزبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عروة بن الزبير); RAZI-FULL(عروة بن الزبير); QURTUBI-FULL(عروة بن الزبير); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبوه); DURR-FULL(عروة,عروة بن الزبير)]
- 20:131/q16/p3 The Prophet drew away from impressive camels in response to the verse. — Qurṭubī relates that he covered himself with his garment as he passed the fat, dung-covered camels of Banū al-Muṣṭaliq. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 20:131/q17 How does this verse connect to nearby passages?  [* · links]
- 20:131/q17/p1 It follows the command to bear opponents' words and glorify God, turning the Prophet from their worldly status to prayer and lasting provision. — Ṭabarī and Rāzī describe the sequence from 20:130; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī connect it to earlier nations and delayed punishment. Jishumī says the prior account of unbelievers leads to their present state; Sulamī's Wāsiṭī links consolation to 20:132; Rāzī connects later objections to the earlier patience command. Kur'an Yolu summarizes the closing sūra as endurance, worship, and a blessed future. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; SULAMI(الواسطي); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 20:131/q17/p2 It parallels the warning in al-Ḥijr and other contrasts between passing goods and divine reward. — Wāḥidī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Qurṭubī say the phrase is explained at 15:88; Ibn Kathīr links 15:87–88's Qur'an gift. Ṭabāṭabāʾī parallels 9:85; Kashshāf and Rāzī cite Qārūn at 28:79–80; Study Quran cites 28:60 and 42:36. [10 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭū)]

## 20:131/q18 What ethical counsel is drawn from others' worldly display?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:131/q18/p1 Believers should avert admiring attention from ostentatious wealth and practice contentment. — Pious scholars in Kashshāf and others say spectators reward tyrants' displays; al-Ḥasan urges seeing sin's humiliation beneath garments and mounts. Jishumī urges little and detachment; Wāsiṭī consoles the poor. A reported hadith counsels comparison with more devout and less wealthy people; Jesus warns that enthroning the world enslaves. Qushayrī's poem disciplines the eye for admiring other than the beloved. [18 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(رسول الله); SULAMI(الواسطي); QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL(العلماء من أهل التقوى); RAZI-FULL(أبو الدرداء,الحسن,عيسى ابن مريم); NASAFI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(سفيان); BURSEVI(الحسن,الواسطي,عيسى بن مريم); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:131/q18/p2 Worldly ease is no proof of divine favor; lawful goods may be enjoyed and shared responsibly. — Kur'an Yolu warns against envy while affirming lawful enjoyment, aid to others, endurance of deprivation, and seeking God's favor. Rāzī reports that God looks at hearts and deeds rather than forms and wealth; Abū Ḥayyān cites the world as cursed except what is sought for God. Ibn Kathīr portrays the Prophet sharing all he received and not storing it. The report of ʿUmar contrasts his sparse home with Caesar and Khosrow, whose goods came early. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(النبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(رسول الله); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 20:131/q18/p3 The world's sweetness can enslave and deceive the attached. — Bursevī cites Khujandī's honey with greater bitter trouble, Mathnawī's carrion, and Ḥāfiẓ's seductive bride; Qushayrī cautions that distraction breeds arrogance and excess. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI]

## 20:131/q19 How is the verse read inwardly?  [* · inward]
- 20:131/q19/p1 The outer and inner gaze must turn from worldly and even afterlife enjoyments toward God alone. — Tustarī and Sahl name satanic whispers, disobedience, egoistic hopes, and habitual ease as barriers to remembrance. Bursevī follows Najmī in distinguishing bodily and inward vision, and says enjoyment of either abode may block beholding divine beauty. Qushayrī depicts extreme ascetic exertion; Shiblī questions being occupied even by Paradise's delights. [7 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل); QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(الشبلي)]
- 20:131/q19/p2 Extending the gaze represents the soul's shifting inclinations, while true provision is spiritual light and knowledge. — Qāshānī sees worldly adornments as tests for worldly people and names afterlife realities and knowledge as the lasting provision; Bursevī speaks of nourishment for the holy spirit. [4 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 20:131/q20 Is 20:131 Meccan or Medinan?  [* · sciences]

- 20:131/q20/p1 It is Meccan with the sūra. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya uses the Meccan setting to object to Abū Rāfiʿ's Medinan incident; Kur'an Yolu doubts Medinan reports because verses 130–131 have Meccan style; Study Quran says context favors Mecca. [4 notes: ITQAN(أبو رافع); ALUSI-FULL(السيوطي); IBNASHUR-FULL(السخاوي); STUDYQURAN(some)]

## 20:131/q21 What recitational and counting details attach to the verse ending?  [زَهْرَةَ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا · sciences]
- 20:131/q21/p1 Some regional verse counts include 'زهرة الحياة الدنيا' as an ending. — Nashr says the Medinan, Meccan and Basran counters include it among five disputed endings in Ṭā Hā; its imāla treatment across eleven sūras depends on six counting systems, with Medinan counting guiding Nāfiʿ and Basran Abū ʿAmr. The reliably transmitted choice for al-Azraq from Warsh and the other cited reader in the preceding imāla discussion is fatḥ. [3 notes: NASHR]

## 20:131/q22 What morphological rule is illustrated by زهرة?  [زَهْرَةَ · grammar]
- 20:131/q22/p1 Words bearing a noun's sense need not take an open tāʾ. — Zarkashī lists zahrah alongside ṣibghah, zalzalah, taḥillah, riḥlah and ḥammālah, citing 30:30, 101:4, 66:2, 106:2 and 111:4. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 20:131/q23 What reports connect knowledge-seeking with provision?  [وَرِزْقُ رَبِّكَ · reports]
- 20:131/q23/p1 God guarantees or preserves provision for those who seek knowledge. — Durr reports Ziyād al-Ṣudāʿī's Prophetic saying that God guarantees provision, and Abū Saʿīd's report that angels shade and bless a seeker without diminishing livelihood. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري,زياد الصدعي)]

## 20:131/q24 What other Qur'anic topics appear in the excerpted source material?  [* · links]
- 20:131/q24/p1 Several Qur'anic passages establish the jinn and devils' existence and action. — Rāzī cites jinn hearing the Qur'an (46:29), devils in Solomon's era (2:102), their building and making vessels (34:13), their diving and chains (38:37–38), wind and jinn serving Solomon (34:12), the challenge to jinn and humans (55:33), and stars guarding heaven from devils (37:6). An ungraded Abū Saʿīd report about a snake and a dying newlywed cuts off before the Prophet's response. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري)]
- 20:131/q24/p2 The Qur'an has concise, varied, ordered eloquence. — Zarkashī praises its inimitable concision, wisdom, literal and figurative speech, and changes in rhetorical form and tone. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 20:131/q25 What does the cited discussion say about the Fātiḥa?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The reported place and recurrence of its revelation, plus the meaning of its name.
- 20:131/q25/p1 It was revealed in Mecca. — Ālūsī reports ʿAlī, Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda and most Companions. He prefers eyewitness Companion testimony; 15:87's seven oft-repeated verses may mean the Fātiḥa or seven long sūras. Future-oriented past tense (48:1) is possible, though 20:131's link suggests earlier delivery. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(علي وابن عباس وقتادة وأكثر الصحابة); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:131/q25/p2 It was revealed in Medina. — Ālūsī reports Mujāhid's isolated claim, counted a mistake. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 20:131/q25/p3 It descended both in Mecca and Medina. — Ālūsī reports a view tied to prayer's obligation and the qibla change, then rejects repetition because descent cannot recur and reports of benefits, readings and basmala do not prove a second descent. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:131/q25/p4 Some verses are Meccan and some Medinan. — Ālūsī reports and calls this weak. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 20:131/q25/p5 The source also explains surah and Fātiḥa as names and grammatical forms. — Ālūsī derives surah from remnant, enclosure or rank, defines it as an independent unit with transmitted name, rejects a purported ban on saying 'Sura X' because Anas's report is weak or fabricated and Ibn ʿUmar's only his statement, and analyzes Fātiḥa as the opening of the compiled Book in divine knowledge, the Preserved Tablet or Bayt al-ʿIzza. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:131/q26 What inward disturbance can worldly testing produce?  [لِنَفْتِنَهُمْ فِيهِ · meaning]
- 20:131/q26/p1 Fitnah is fear, anticipation, and confused mental turmoil. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says shirk gives disbelievers gloom and apprehension, with a further trial in the afterlife. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:131/q27 What determines a person's worldly allotment?  [مَا مَتَّعْنَا بِهِۦٓ · theology]
- 20:131/q27/p1 God's wisdom makes each allotment appropriate to its recipient's decreed destiny. — Asad gives this as the significance of what God grants a person. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

- 20:131/q28/p1 Patience and indifference to worldly abundance receive God's reward. — Abū Ḥayyān transmits this as one explanation of the Lord's provision. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]
