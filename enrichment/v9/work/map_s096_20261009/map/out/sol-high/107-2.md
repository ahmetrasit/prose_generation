# 107:2: 19 questions

## 107:2/q01 What does the fāʾ in فذلك do?  [فَذَٰلِكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the opening question implies a condition, supplies a causal link, or permits a superfluous fāʾ.
- 107:2/q01/p1 It answers an understood condition, such as ‘if you want to know or rebuke the denier.’ — Sibawayh supplies ‘if you wanted to rebuke him’; al-Samīn gives ‘if you reflect or seek to know’; al-Ālūsī supplies ‘if you do not know the denier’; Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Elmalılı describe a conditional inference. [6 notes: BURHAN(سيبويه); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 107:2/q01/p2 It marks the causal result of the preceding question's invitation to identify the denier. — Al-Ālūsī links it to the suspense of the opening question; Elmalılı allows causation; al-Rāzī says denial produces the orphan abuse named after the fāʾ. [4 notes: ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ELMALILI; RAZI-FULL]
- 107:2/q01/p3 It is an extra particle. — Al-Akhfash permits omission of fāʾ where Sibawayh requires it and cites 6:121 as parallel. [2 notes: BURHAN(الأخفش)]

## 107:2/q02 How is فذلك الذي construed with the opening question?  [فَذَٰلِكَ ٱلَّذِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether the demonstrative opens an explanatory nominative sentence or is coordinated as an accusative object; critics dispute its referent and the alleged ellipsis.
- 107:2/q02/p1 فذلك begins a nominative explanatory clause identifying the denier. — Abū Ḥayyān calls this the immediate, more eloquent parse; al-Samīn gives subject with the relative as predicate, or predicate of an omitted subject with the relative as modifier. The demonstrative points to the same denier, and what follows occupies the second-object place of araʾayta. Al-Samīn answers that the preceding question permits a forward-pointing demonstrative, and calls the construction a response to an interrogative; his analogy fa-hal ʿasaytum (47:22) defends interrogative before a performative. [14 notes: ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(the Shaykh); ALUSI(أبو حيان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN+; SAMIN-DURR(the Shaykh)+]
- 107:2/q02/p2 فذلك is accusative, coordinated with the denier of verse 1; araʾayta's answer is omitted and indicated by the later woe. — Al-Zamakhsharī takes the later plural worshippers as a generic singular's description; al-Samīn says the question then asks whether denial, orphan harm, and neglect of the poor are commendable. Abū Ḥayyān objects that the demonstrative needlessly repeats the same referent, a more idiomatic coordination would use a relative clause or verb, and the alleged omitted answer is actually the second object. Al-Kashf objects that a pronoun for two entities cannot naturally become ‘worshippers’; al-Samīn replies that the demonstrative can point forward and the question context allows it. [9 notes: KASHSHAF; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(the Shaykh)-; ALUSI(أبو حيان)-]

## 107:2/q03 Does the interrogative construction allow a performative answer?  [فَذَٰلِكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the answer is judged as an ordinary declarative clause or a response licensed by an interrogative context.
- 107:2/q03/p1 An interrogative hamza cannot introduce naʿma or biʾsa, since they are performative rather than declarative. — Abū Ḥayyān and the Shaykh state the objection. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN; SAMIN-DURR(the Shaykh); SAMIN-DURR-]
- 107:2/q03/p2 The preceding interrogative permits such a response to its inquiry. — Al-Samīn cites fa-hal ʿasaytum in 47:22, where what follows ʿasā answers the question, and says grammarians call this a response to the preceding interrogative, not an ordinary statement. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-; SAMIN-DURR(the Shaykh)-]

## 107:2/q04 To whom does فذلك الذي point?  [فَذَٰلِكَ ٱلَّذِى · referent]
turns on: Whether the description is generic or tied to a historical individual; the identification reports name different people.
- 107:2/q04/p1 It describes the class of people who deny judgment, even if one person occasioned the wording. — Bayḍāwī permits a generic relative; Bursevī contrasts the whole class with a particular man; al-Biqāʿī says hidden denial appears through conduct; Study Quran says a first individual does not confine the accusation; Elmalılı says the definite predicate restricts it to deniers. [8 notes: BAYDAWI; BURSEVI; BIQAI; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; KASHSHAF-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 107:2/q04/p2 It points to a particular known denier, identified as al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil. — Mujāhid and al-Kalbī are named in al-Wāḥidī's occasion account; Study Quran attributes it to Muqātil and al-Kalbī. Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī, and Kur'an Yolu also list him; their reports do not settle the competing names. [7 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB(مقاتل والكلبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Muqātil and al-Kalbī); BAYDAWI; BURSEVI]
- 107:2/q04/p3 It points to Abū Sufyān. — Ibn Jurayj connects him with a stick-struck orphan asking for food after camel slaughter; the camel count varies across reports. [7 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن جريج); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(ابن جريج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI(ابن جريج); STUDYQURAN(Ibn Jurayj)]
- 107:2/q04/p4 It points to Abū Jahl. — Bayḍāwī, Bursevī, and al-Ālūsī report the guardian who repelled an orphan seeking his own property; Bursevī recounts the subsequent approach to the Prophet. [6 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI(قيل)]
- 107:2/q04/p5 It points to al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra. — Listed among alternative identifications without a separate incident in these notes. [4 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI(قيل)]
- 107:2/q04/p6 It points to a miserly hypocrite or hypocrites. — Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī name a miserly hypocrite; Abū Ḥayyān reports two hypocrites. [5 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI(قيل)]
- 107:2/q04/p7 It points to ʿUmar or ʿAmr ibn ʿĀʾidh. — Abū Ḥayyān writes ʿUmar; al-Ālūsī writes ʿAmr, a name variant in the notes. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI(قيل)]
- 107:2/q04/p8 The unnamed leader is Abū Lahab. — Iṣlāḥī identifies him by his control over the House's financial resources at the sūra's revelation. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 107:2/q05 Why use the demonstrative and relative description here?  [فَذَٰلِكَ ٱلَّذِى · rhetoric]
- 107:2/q05/p1 The demonstrative disparages a person far removed from goodness and makes his vice visible; the relative clause fixes the trait as certain. — Al-Ālūsī says the demonstrative replaces a pronoun for contempt or to show the reason for judgment, and the relative clause establishes the description. Al-Biqāʿī sees distance from good and a heart deprived of mercy; Iṣlāḥī says the description exposes the inner cause of hardheartedness; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says it prompts the hearer to inspect the traits. [8 notes: ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNATIYYA; BIQAI]

## 107:2/q06 What does يدع convey in describing the orphan's treatment?  [يَدُعُّ · meaning]

- 107:2/q06/p1 It is a forceful, harsh shove or expulsion, often with rebuke or injury. — Al-Farrāʾ, Ibn Qutayba, al-Zajjāj, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Bint al-Shāṭiʾ and Iṣlāḥī compare 52:13, where people are driven into Hell. Al-Jishumī adduces shaking a measure, urging an unsteady person and goats. Ibn ʿAbbās cites Abū Ṭālib's verse for Arab usage; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ favors coercive shove over mere withholding, since Hell's condemned have no denied right, and says payment alone does not excuse rude repulsion. Ibn Jinnī says the lighter reading's shunning remains close to this common sense. Al-Rāzī adds scolding and beating. Al-Qāshānī relates it to a predatory soul; Quṭb stresses injury and humiliation. ## 107:2/q07 How is يدع read?  [يَدُعُّ · readings]
turns on: Gemination and vowel of the verb, with a separate reported long-wāw form.
- 107:2/q07/p1 The majority read yaduʿʿu with ḍamma on dāl and doubled ʿayn. — Al-Jishumī and al-Samīn derive forceful pushing from the doubled form; Abū Ḥayyān attributes it to the majority. Corpus Coranicum prefers it for its harsher force and fit with the following geminated ḥaḍḍa. [5 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; CORPUSCORANICUM+]
- 107:2/q07/p2 A lighter yadaʿu, with fatḥa on dāl and undoubled ʿayn, means leaving or neglecting the orphan. — Attributed especially to Abū Rajāʾ, and also ʿAlī, al-Ḥasan and al-Yamānī. Ibn ʿAṭiyya's notes vary on which consonant is called light; al-Jishumī connects the form to wadaʿa and adduces Prophetic and poetic usage against denying its past tense or verbal noun. Corpus Coranicum finds the majority reading contextually stronger. [24 notes: MAJAZ; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو رجاء); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(أبو رجاء); THALABI(أبو رجاء); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو رجاء العطاردي); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(أبو رجاء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو رجاء); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI(قرئ); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(علي والحسن وأبو رجاء واليماني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(علي والحسن وأبو رجاء واليماني); ALUSI-FULL(علي); CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 107:2/q07/p3 A reading with a final long wāw describes an ostentatious invitation without feeding. — Al-Rāzī reports the reading and connects it with exploiting or coercing the orphan. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 107:2/q08 Does the verb describe a repeated habit?  [يَدُعُّ · grammar]
- 107:2/q08/p1 Yes: the imperfect or doubled consonant marks persistent conduct, not a lone repentant lapse. — Al-Rāzī invokes 53:32 and distinguishes believers' fleeting lapses from deniers' persistence; Elmalılı reads the imperfect verbs as habitual and links them with ongoing neglect of duties after God's rescue from hunger and fear in 106:4. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 107:2/q09 What is an اليتيم here?  [ٱلْيَتِيمَ · meaning]
- 107:2/q09/p1 A child who has lost his father. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ supplies the definition. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 107:2/q10 What historical incident is reported behind this description?  [* · reports]
turns on: The named person and the reported circumstances; the Abū Sufyān reports also vary on how many camels were slaughtered.
- 107:2/q10/p1 An orphan sought food from Abū Sufyān after he slaughtered camels, and he struck the child with a stick. — Ibn Jurayj supplies the occasion; al-Wāḥidī says two camels each week, Abū Ḥayyān says a camel weekly, and other accounts mention a camel or weekly camels without the same number. These numerical variants remain explicit here. [7 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن جريج); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(ابن جريج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI(ابن جريج); STUDYQURAN(Ibn Jurayj)]
- 107:2/q10/p2 An orphan under Abū Jahl's guardianship came unclothed seeking his own money, and he pushed the child away. — Bursevī adds that Quraysh sent the boy to Muḥammad in mockery; the Prophet accompanied him and Abū Jahl paid. Abū Jahl said he feared a spear by Muḥammad, answering an accusation that he had converted. [5 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI(قيل)]
- 107:2/q10/p3 The opening about the judgment-denier and orphan-repeller was revealed about al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil. — Al-Wāḥidī attributes this to Mujāhid and al-Kalbī, while Study Quran names Muqātil and al-Kalbī; Kur'an Yolu reports the occasion without incident details. [3 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB(مقاتل والكلبي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Muqātil and al-Kalbī)]

## 107:2/q11 What ethical significance does repelling the orphan have?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: These are complementary emphases: diagnosis of denial and the positive duty owed to vulnerable people.
- 107:2/q11/p1 Cruelty to an orphan exposes denial of judgment or religion: faith and fear of reckoning should produce mercy and care. — Al-Rāzī calls abuse an active vice and failure to urge food an omission, examples rather than a complete list; al-Biqāʿī says denial hardens the heart and makes outward signs reveal hidden disbelief, but Abū Jaʿfar ibn al-Zubayr cautions that a Muslim may commit such acts without thereby necessarily being a denier. Quṭb says true assent prompts action, not merely verbal claim or ritual, and distinguishes God's measure from juristic status. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says fear of consequence would bring mercy. Corpus Coranicum links eschatological awareness and solidarity with 92:5–9 and its absence with 104:1–3. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says social duty exceeds private worship; Kur'an Yolu calls these examples especially destructive Meccan conduct and Ibn ʿĀshūr's broad condemnation of cruelty. [41 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; KASHSHAF; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); ABUHAYYAN; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; CORPUSCORANICUM; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI]
- 107:2/q11/p2 It demands respect for the weak, protection of their rights, and practical solidarity. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ surveys orphan pairings with poor, captive, traveler and enslaved people (76:8; 90:16; 93:6–8; 2:177; 4:36) and the orphan's social right (8:41; 59:7; 2:177,215,83; 4:36). Al-Qurṭubī reports a Prophetic promise of Paradise for one caring for a Muslim orphan until self-sufficiency, without grading. Iṣlāḥī cites 89:17, Abū Bakr's maxim that the weak are strongest until their rights arrive, and calls for upholding their cause. Kur'an Yolu urges organized charitable institutions; al-Biqāʿī cites a Prophetic supplication for love of the poor and says withholding kind words foretells withheld wealth. Al-Qushayrī quotes mercy being removed only from a wretched heart. [17 notes: QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Abū Bakr (rta)); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 107:2/q12 How do the orphan and poor clauses work together?  [* · rhetoric]
- 107:2/q12/p1 The first names active injury and the second omission of encouragement; each implies the counterpart left unstated in the other. — Al-Rāzī contrasts the active offense and omitted urging; al-Biqāʿī says the orphan's shove implies contempt in the second clause and the poor clause's urging implies encouragement owed in the first; both signal injury to the weak and neglected kindness. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 107:2/q13 How does the passage relate orphan abuse to later prayer failings?  [* · links]
- 107:2/q13/p1 Social and devotional failings together reveal a failure of sincere faith, with the later warning intensifying the rebuke. — Al-Kashshāf says the first warning shows sin's link to weak certainty before woe to worshippers; al-Thaʿlabī transmits Qatāda on heedless prayer and Ibn ʿAbbās on hypocrites who pray publicly but not privately (4:142), plus a Prophetic warning about lacking hope of reward or fear when abandoning prayer. Al-Biqāʿī likens trait descriptions to reports about hypocrisy and fornication, and links 8:4's believers who pray and spend. Elmalılı calls religious pretenders more surprising than open deniers. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says prayer neglect, display and withheld aid resemble hypocrisy. Corpus Coranicum sees cross correspondence: verses 1,4–6 are religious omissions, verses 2–3,7 social ones. Study Quran says hypocrisy is not limited to Muslims (63:1–8). [17 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة,مجاهد); KASHSHAF; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN]

## 107:2/q14 How does this verse connect with other passages and nearby sūras?  [* · links]
- 107:2/q14/p1 It belongs to a recurring Qur'anic pattern of honoring orphans and feeding the needy, grounded in God's favors and final accountability. — Corpus Coranicum compares vice lists 89:17–18, virtue list 90:13–16, and 93:9; 69:34; 74:44, plus 83:1–4 and Biblical orphan/widow/poor pairings and Matthew 25's eschatological charity. It says 106's earlier favors complement this sūra's future accountability. Al-Biqāʿī compares arrogance with the elephant people (105:1) and the miser's illusion of immortal wealth (104:3). Elmalılı connects Quraysh (106), Humaza (104), and Kawthar's summing of earlier sūras, especially from 93. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ contrasts God's sheltering the orphan Prophet with the repeller. Study Quran links orphan inheritance passages. [17 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN]

## 107:2/q15 What inward meaning is drawn from the orphan's repulsion?  [* · inward]
- 107:2/q15/p1 The predatory self overpowers the orphan-like heart and drives it from spiritual nourishment. — Bursevī explicitly reports this inward reading; al-Qāshānī describes the excessive predatory soul harming the weak orphan in the outward description. [2 notes: BURSEVI; QASHANI]

## 107:2/q16 Where and when was the sūra containing this verse revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether later references to hypocritical prayer and display fit Mecca or imply a Medinan setting, and whether the sūra was revealed in parts.
- 107:2/q16/p1 The whole seven-verse sūra is Meccan, likely early. — Al-Jishumī attributes Meccan classification to Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan, ʿIkrima and Qatāda. Asad puts it early, probably after sūra 102; Study Quran says most call it early Meccan after 102. Quṭb reports this dating among the alternatives. [5 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عباس، الحسن، عكرمة، قتادة); QUTB-ZILAL; ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 107:2/q16/p2 The first three verses are Meccan and the rest Medinan. — Al-Jishumī attributes a split to Muqātil through the mention of the needy. ## 107:2/q17 What virtue is reported for reciting this sūra?  [* · sciences]
- 107:2/q17/p1 A report from Ubayy promises God's forgiveness to its reciter who pays zakat. — Al-Jishumī transmits it from the Prophet through Ubayy without a grading. [1 notes: JISHUMI(النبي)]

## 107:2/q18 How does this Qur'anic phrase relate to poetry's meter?  [* · rhetoric]
- 107:2/q18/p1 The opening can coincide with khafīf meter without making the Qur'an poetry. — Al-Burhān treats complete lines, multiple lines and hemistichs that happen to scan, and reports the al-Māʿūn opening as khafīf; Abū Nuwās recast the lines in khafīf and adjusted them to فذاك الذي in his poem. [3 notes: BAQILLANI(أبو نواس); BURHAN(قالوا)]

## 107:2/q19 What alternative reading is reported for the next verse's exhortation?  [* · readings]
- 107:2/q19/p1 Zayd ibn ʿAlī reads wa-lā yuḥāḍḍ, meaning people do not urge one another. — Al-Samīn derives it from muḥāḍḍa and points to discussion at 89:18; Corpus Coranicum notes an alternate reading in the exhortation to feed the poor. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(زيد بن علي); CORPUSCORANICUM]
