# 80:19: 14 questions

## 80:19/q01 What does nuṭfa denote?  [نُّطْفَةٍ · meaning]
- 80:19/q01/p1 A small quantity of reproductive fluid from which a human is created. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the basic sense as a little water, with a passive noun pattern like a handful or scoop, and says usage came to mean reproductive fluid, the material of animal creation; Ibn ʿAṭiyya explicitly includes the man's and woman's fluids. Al-Qurṭubī, al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī, and al-Biqāʿī describe the drop as base, filthy, or slight; Ibn al-Jawzī calls it a sperm drop. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 80:19/q02 Why describe the human origin as a drop?  [مِن نُّطْفَةٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the mention chiefly belittles the origin to rebuke pride or displays the wonder of creation as proof of resurrection; Ibn ʿĀshūr expressly denies the former emphasis.
- 80:19/q02/p1 Its lowliness rebukes human arrogance, disobedience, and ingratitude. — Al-Qurṭubī calls the fluid small, base, and lifeless and asks why a person is self-deceived; he reports al-Ḥasan's reminder that a person passed twice through the urinary passage. Al-Naḥḥās says obedience makes one noble despite lowly origin. Al-Biqāʿī contrasts filthy beginnings, bodily waste, and a foul corpse with knowledge and honor bestowed by the Creator, calling for gratitude. Al-Bursawī says the gifted beautiful form leaves no grounds for arrogance; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī links the indefinite drop to rejection of arrogance and disobedience. Al-Zamakhsharī, as reported by al-Burhān, treats the question as belittling the origin. [10 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); BURHAN(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 80:19/q02/p2 The slight material highlights the wonder and wisdom of God's creation and the possibility of recreating the human. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the focus is the greatness of creation, not rebuking pride; he links first creation to resurrection, citing 50:15 and 86:5–8. Al-Study Quran adduces 18:37, 16:4, 40:67, and 86:6–8 as parallels for creation from a drop and divine omnipotence. Al-Biqāʿī uses the contrast between lowly origin and bestowed honor in the resurrection argument. [7 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL]

## 80:19/q03 What does the indefinite wording contribute?  [نُّطْفَةٍ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the indefinite expression signals a lowly origin or the context instead emphasizes the greatness of creation.
- 80:19/q03/p1 The indefinite wording belittles the drop and the human's origin. — Al-Suyūṭī's Itqān says the indefinite 'thing' in the preceding question points to low origin before the answer specifies a drop (80:18–19). Al-Ālūsī says belittlement may come from that indefinite 'thing' or from 'drop.' Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī attributes the belittlement specifically to the indefinite nuṭfa. [3 notes: ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 80:19/q04 Why is 'from a drop' put first?  [مِن نُّطْفَةٍ · rhetoric]
- 80:19/q04/p1 It echoes the preceding question and stresses the material from which the wondrous human is made. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the fronted phrase matches the fronted question and alerts the listener to divine wisdom in making a remarkable creature from a simple or slight source. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 80:19/q05 How is 'from a drop' connected to 'He created him'?  [مِن نُّطْفَةٍ خَلَقَهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: The pause after 'from a drop' and whether an implied verb is supplied before the expressed 'He created him.'
- 80:19/q05/p1 'From a drop' directly modifies 'He created him,' with no pause after the drop. — Al-Bursawī calls this the more apparent construction and says treating 'He created him, then proportioned him' as a new sentence would duplicate the idea of measured creation. [1 notes: BURSEVI+]
- 80:19/q05/p2 Pause after 'from a drop,' supply an understood 'He created him,' and take 'He created him, then proportioned him' as a fresh explanation. — Al-Bursawī reports al-Sajāwandī's pause and its supplied verb; al-Bursawī objects that this makes the ensuing new sentence redundant because creating already involves measured quantities and qualities. [1 notes: BURSEVI(السجاوندي); BURSEVI-]

## 80:19/q06 How does the answer relate to the preceding question?  [مِن نُّطْفَةٍ خَلَقَهُۥ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the response is treated as a formal answer to a question about substance or as apposition or emphasis rather than a literal answer.
- 80:19/q06/p1 It is formally a question and answer about the substance of creation, building anticipation and directing thought to the possibility of recreation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the anticipation to the question about the great news in 78:1–2; he says the question asks from what substance the person was made, not whether God made him, and adduces 50:15 and 86:5–8 for the analogy to resurrection. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 80:19/q06/p2 The answer is not a literal answer but an apposition to the question, or an emphatic restatement. — Al-Ālūsī offers apposition and, alternatively, emphasis; he says the indefinite 'thing' or 'drop' supplies the belittling force. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 80:19/q07 Why repeat 'He created him' in the answer?  [خَلَقَهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 80:19/q07/p1 The repetition emphasizes the precision of the remarkable creation and gives the answer balanced expression. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the verb could be understood from the question but its expression calls attention to precision; he calls the result balanced, neither ellipsis nor excess. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 80:19/q08 What does 'then proportioned him' mean?  [فَقَدَّرَهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the measure concerns fitting bodily and mental design, prenatal developmental stages, allotted life and destiny, or stages across the lifespan; several sources combine these senses.
- 80:19/q08/p1 God fashioned and fitted the human's body, organs, qualities, and capacities in precise proportions. — Al-Zajjāj and those reporting him describe a proper or upright form and cite 18:37; al-Qurṭubī also cites 82:7. Al-Kalbī, Ibn al-Sāʾib, and the report from Ibn ʿAbbās name the head, eyes, hands, feet, appearance, beauty or ugliness, and height. Al-Rāzī says each organ's quantity and qualities suit its benefit, citing 25:2; al-Bursawī extends fitness to use and inward capacities. Al-Zamakhsharī speaks of what suits and distinguishes the person and invokes 25:2. Ibn ʿĀshūr defines taqdīr as precise ordered making, citing 25:2, and includes growth, faculties, intellect, agency, reflection, and chosen acts. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī extends measure to essence, attributes, and acts, with limits the human must respect; Asad includes organic functions of body and mind and adaptation to natural conditions. Al-Ālūsī says fitting limbs and forms explains the apt sequence with fa; al-Bayḍāwī gives this as one of two explanations. [27 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عكرمة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; TABRISI]
- 80:19/q08/p2 God determined the successive stages of development in the womb until the human was complete. — Muqātil details drop, clot, lump, bones, and finally spirit, with form determined before birth. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Qushayrī, Ibn Jurayj, and others name successive fetal stages; al-Wāḥidī reports Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil on determination in the womb. Al-Farrāʾ and those reporting him combine stages with sex and felicity. Al-Wāḥidī's Wajīz continues to birth and mentions 80:20; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī present this as an alternative to fitting form and organs. [18 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، مقاتل,الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABRISI]
- 80:19/q08/p3 God apportioned each person's sex, lifespan, provision, deeds, and fortune or misery. — Al-Farrāʾ combines male or female and wretched or blessed with fetal stages; Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds organs, lifespan, and other matters according to divine will. The report from Ibn ʿAbbās includes bodily appearance and felicity or misery. Ibn Kathīr specifies lifespan, provision, deeds, and destiny and uses this as evidence of recreation. Al-Bursawī links sex and fortune to development, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī extends divine measure to human acts and allotted limits. [10 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; TABRISI]
- 80:19/q08/p4 God measured the gradual stages of human life from childhood through youth to old age. — Iṣlāḥī compares these life stages to the moon's phases in 36:39 and adduces 23:12–16; their order points to divine power, purpose, resurrection, and judgment. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 80:19/q09 How is the dāl in qaddarahu read?  [فَقَدَّرَهُۥ · readings]
turns on: Whether the dāl is doubled in recitation.
- 80:19/q09/p1 The dāl is doubled: qaddarahu. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes this reading to the majority of readers. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس)]
- 80:19/q09/p2 The dāl is pronounced without doubling. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes this variant to some readers. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض القراء)]

## 80:19/q10 What does the fāʾ between creation and measuring convey?  [خَلَقَهُۥ فَقَدَّرَهُۥ · grammar]
- 80:19/q10/p1 Measured formation follows as a consequence of creation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the fāʾ marks measurement as creation's effect, equipping a person to grow, reason, act, and exercise judgment; he cites 25:2. Al-Ālūsī says interpreting measurement as fitting the human with suitable limbs and forms makes the sequence with fa apt. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 80:19/q11 What are the three darknesses around the developing fetus?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the three enclosing layers are identified as belly, womb, and placenta or as three functional membranes.
- 80:19/q11/p1 They may be the belly, womb, and placenta. — Al-Biqāʿī presents this identification as a possibility. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 80:19/q11/p2 They are three membranes with different functions. — Al-Biqāʿī reports anatomists: the placenta feeds the fetus through the navel, while the other membranes receive urine and bodily vapors. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أهل التشريح)]

## 80:19/q12 Do the past tense creation verbs describe a recurring event?  [خَلَقَهُۥ فَقَدَّرَهُۥ · grammar]
- 80:19/q12/p1 Yes; they describe a recurring phenomenon despite their past tense form. — Asad applies this to verses 18–22 as a sequence. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 80:19/q13 What does this creation and measuring teach?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes stress different implications of the same sequence: proof of resurrection, divine care and moral accountability, or rebuke of human pride; Ibn ʿĀshūr disputes the pride rebuke as the focus.
- 80:19/q13/p1 It demonstrates God's power to recreate the human and thus supports resurrection and judgment. — Ibn Kathīr calls decreed creation proof of God's power to recreate. Al-Biqāʿī says creation and the food cycle support the resurrection warning, with 79:45 as the call to warn. Ibn ʿĀshūr reasons from first creation to recreation, citing 50:15 and 86:5–8. Iṣlāḥī says the reminders about creation, life's sources, death, and resurrection expose fleeting pride; the measured sequence entails judgment and recompense, and 36:39 and 23:12–16 illuminate its stages. Kur'an Yolu urges deniers and doubters to reflect on existence after nonexistence; the Study Quran cites 18:37, 16:4, 40:67, and 86:6–8 for divine omnipotence. [12 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 80:19/q13/p2 It shows wise, beneficent divine governance and calls for gratitude and observance of human limits. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the ordered sequence also recalls divine beneficence. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God creates, measures, guides, causes death, burial, and resurrection while the human owns none of that governance; even food reveals subtle care and immeasurable mercy, for which he cites 14:34. He says allotted measure in essence, attributes, and acts sets bounds the human should not transgress. Al-Biqāʿī says knowledge and honor were given by the Creator and call for gratitude. Al-Naḥḥās says obedience raises the human from lowly origin; Iṣlāḥī sees purposeful providence and recompense in measured life stages. [7 notes: NAHHAS; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 80:19/q13/p3 It exposes the folly of arrogance and ingratitude in one dependent on the Creator. — Al-Qurṭubī and al-Ḥasan point to humble origin; al-Biqāʿī contrasts granted honor with filthy origin, waste, and death; al-Bursawī says the beautiful form is a benefaction; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls neglect of God's care injustice and ingratitude; Iṣlāḥī says these reminders expose transitory pride. Ibn ʿĀshūr expressly says the mention of fluid emphasizes creation's greatness rather than a rebuke of pride. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 80:19/q14 How does this passage connect with its surrounding context?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the verses form one context with the preceding passage or belong to a separately reported occasion.
- 80:19/q14/p1 It continues the surrounding warning: creation, food, death, and resurrection disclose divine power and human dependence. — Al-Biqāʿī relates the sura's warning to those who fear, citing 79:45; creation and food purge receptive hearts of resurrection denial. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī allows the verses to continue the previous passage as one context and describes the sequence of measuring, guidance, death, burial, resurrection, and food. Iṣlāḥī also links creation, life's sources, death, resurrection, and judgment. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 80:19/q14/p2 Some commentators treat these verses as revealed for a separate occasion. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this view while allowing a continuous context; he says the separate occasion is discussed later. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
