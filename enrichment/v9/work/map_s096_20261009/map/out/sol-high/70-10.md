# 70:10: 8 questions

## 70:10/q01 How is yasʾalu and the clause read?  [وَلَا يَسْـَٔلُ · readings]
turns on: Whether the verb has fatḥa or ḍamma on yāʾ, and whether the second ḥamīm is included; al-Farrāʾ disputes the passive's compatibility with the received explanation and readers' agreement.
- 70:10/q01/p1 The verb is active: a close person does not ask another. — Al-Farrāʾ says the readers agree on the active form and rejects the passive as contrary to the explanation; Ibn ʿAṭiyya names the seven readers, al-Ḥasan, Medinans, Ṭalḥa and others; Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Jishumī and al-Biqāʿī call it the majority or general reading. Al-Qurṭubī specifies fatḥa on the yāʾ. The active wording is explained as failure to ask after another or seek help, owing to personal preoccupation. [9 notes: JISHUMI(القراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(الآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(السبعة والحسن والمدنيون وطلحة والناس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأكثرون); QURTUBI-FULL(شيبة والبزي عن عاصم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); BIQAI-FULL; FARRA+]
- 70:10/q01/p2 The verb is passive: a close person is not asked about another, summoned, or made answerable for another. — Ibn Kathīr through al-Bazzī is named by al-Wāḥidī and al-Baghawī; al-Jishumī adds al-Barjamī from ʿĀṣim and al-Ḥasan. Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds Abū Jaʿfar, Shayba in a differing transmission, and Abū Ḥaywa. Ibn al-Jawzī supplies Muʿāwiya, Abū Razīn, al-Ḥasan, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Mujāhid, ʿIkrima, Ibn Muḥayṣin and Ibn Abī ʿAbla. Al-Qurṭubī reports Shayba and al-Bazzī from ʿĀṣim, differing from other attributions. Al-Ālūsī notes disagreement for some transmitters. QIRAAT-ER attributes it to some of the Ten and contrasts passive impossibility with active refusal. The reading has ḍamma on yāʾ. Al-Farrāʾ objects that it conflicts with the exegesis and readers' agreement. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن كثير); JISHUMI(البزي عن ابن كثير، والبرجمي عن عاصم، والحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(البزي عن ابن كثير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير والبزي وأبو جعفر وشيبة وأبو حيوة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(معاوية، أبو رزين، الحسن، سعيد بن جبير، مجاهد، عكرمة، ابن محيصن، ابن أبي عبلة، أبو جعفر); QURTUBI-FULL(شيبة والبزي عن عاصم); NASAFI-FULL(البزي والبرجمي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حيوة وشيبة وأبو جعفر والبزي); BIQAI-FULL(ابن كثير); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيوة وشيبة وأبو جعفر والبزي); QIRAAT-ER(Pss); FARRA-; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)-]
- 70:10/q01/p3 A reported reading omits the second ḥamīm. — Al-Zajjāj says the shorter wording yields the sense that intimates recognize one another, consonant with 'they see them' in 70:11; he contrasts a reading retaining the second ḥamīm, whose syntax yields the sense that a relative does not ask about a relative. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ]
- 70:10/q01/p4 A reported reading retains the second ḥamīm. — Al-Zajjāj contrasts it with the reading that omits the word and says its construction means no relative asks about a relative; he suggests angels are those being seen in 70:11. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ]

## 70:10/q02 How does saʾala govern ḥamīman and any unspoken complement?  [يَسْـَٔلُ حَمِيمًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether ḥamīman is the person directly asked or an 'about' complement after deletion of a preposition, and how that deletion works with the passive.
- 70:10/q02/p1 Ḥamīman is the direct object: one asks a close person, with the thing asked left understood. — Al-Wāḥidī illustrates asking Zayd about his state or affairs. Al-Jishumī calls ḥamīman the object. Al-Ālūsī says the second object is omitted. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the unspoken question about the friend's condition is clear from their seeing each other in 70:11. Al-Burhān says saʾala can take two objects like aʿṭā, either of which may be unexpressed, including asking someone for a thing or to do something. Al-Biqāʿī takes the active wording as asking another about anything. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 70:10/q02/p2 An omitted preposition yields 'ask about a close person.' — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Jishumī and al-Ālūsī give this alternative to direct object syntax: the verb becomes transitive after the preposition is dropped. Al-Ṭabrisī says the object shifts with deletion of the preposition; the plural wording reflects many intimates despite singular ḥamīm. Al-Burhān describes a second two-object pattern, likened to selecting someone from a group, and applies it here to asking about an intimate while too distracted to think of him. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL]
- 70:10/q02/p3 On the passive reading, an omitted preposition allows ḥamīman to stand in the accusative. — Al-Wāḥidī says Ibn Kathīr's passive also omits the preposition: a relative is not asked about another in order to learn his condition. Al-Ṭabrisī says the former object of the preposition appears as accusative ḥamīman. Al-Qurṭubī reports the construction 'no close friend is asked about another.' [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن كثير); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 70:10/q03 What does ḥamīm mean here?  [حَمِيمٌ حَمِيمًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether closeness here is restricted to kinship or also includes intimate friendship and alliance.
- 70:10/q03/p1 A close blood relative or kinsperson. — Ibn ʿAbbās, cited by al-Wāḥidī, adds mutual protective anger. Al-Ṭabrisī derives the sense from nearness and cites poetry. Ibn al-Jawzī notes the related senses hot water and ḥamiyya, hot anger or jealousy, while also listing close relative. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ adds one whose affairs a person cares about. [14 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]
- 70:10/q03/p2 An intimate, close friend or ally, whether related by blood or not. — Al-Akhfash, reported by al-Ṭabrisī, defines one specially loved regardless of blood tie and explains al-ḥāmah as one's close circle. Abū ʿUbayda and al-Dāmghānī allow both relative and intimate; Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes an ally. Ibn al-Jawzī's other lexical senses, hot water and hot anger or jealousy, are not proposed as this verse's referent. [6 notes: MAJAZ; DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الأخفش); BURSEVI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 70:10/q04 What does the verse say close people fail to ask on that Day?  [وَلَا يَسْـَٔلُ حَمِيمٌ حَمِيمًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'ask' concerns another's condition, a request for assistance, sins or intercession, accountability, or summons; the active and passive readings support different construals, and seeing in 70:11 can explain recognition or why inquiry is unnecessary.
- 70:10/q04/p1 They do not ask after one another's condition, or even speak, because terror leaves each absorbed in his own plight. — Muqātil adds that the man does not speak to kin. Al-Farrāʾ says they recognize one another briefly, then recognition ends. Al-Wāḥidī compares the mother forgetting her nursling (22:2) and the flight from a brother (80:34–37). Qatāda, Mujāhid and later sources emphasize personal preoccupation; Ibn Kathīr says this holds despite seeing kin in the worst state and cites Ibn ʿAbbās via al-ʿAwfī: they recognize then flee. Al-Ālūsī prefers Qatāda's first report as the most terrifying explanation. Elmalılı says they cannot even ask about a nearby person, still less search for someone farther away. Bursevī argues that if kin do not speak, strangers certainly will not. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ takes this as evidence of the Day's severity. [31 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(القراء,قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(الآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأكثرون,مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس عن العوفي); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)+]
- 70:10/q04/p2 No inquiry is needed because each person's condition is visible or recognizable. — Al-Jishumī says it is known from features. Qatāda says everyone can see the other's condition; al-Ṭabrisī's anonymous explanation specifies black faces and blue eyes of disbelievers versus radiant color and white faces of believers. Al-Ṭabrisī also invokes 'they see them' in 70:11. Al-Ālūsī gives Qatāda's second report that the state is plain. Al-Biqāʿī says sight rules out concealment as the reason for no question, while he favors distraction. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL]
- 70:10/q04/p3 One close person does not ask another for help or benefit, knowing none can be given. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān state this for the active reading. Al-Ālūsī quotes al-Baḥr for it but prefers Qatāda's first, more alarming explanation. Al-Biqāʿī adds that no soul can help another and kinship ties dissolve; only piety matters. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور,قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البحر)]
- 70:10/q04/p4 One close person does not ask another to bear his sins, having despaired of such help. — Al-Ḥasan is named by al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī. Abū Ḥayyān also records this explanation; al-Ālūsī gives the despair motive. On the passive reading, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī phrase it as no one being asked to take a relative's sins upon himself. [6 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن); TABRISI(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حيوة وشيبة وأبو جعفر والبزي,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 70:10/q04/p5 No one asks a close person to intercede for him. — Abū Ḥayyān includes intercession among the explanations; al-Ālūsī reports it as a separate view. Al-Ṭabrisī juxtaposes the plea in 26:101 that there is no intercessor or close friend. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI]
- 70:10/q04/p6 On the passive reading, no one asks, summons, or calls for a close person to appear or provide aid. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says everyone, criminal and believer, bears an identifying mark, so no one has to be summoned. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī list being summoned or asked to come; al-Ālūsī also mentions help. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حيوة وشيبة وأبو جعفر والبزي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيوة وشيبة وأبو جعفر والبزي,قيل)]
- 70:10/q04/p7 On the passive reading, no relative is questioned or held accountable for another's deeds or sins. — Al-Qurṭubī cites 74:38, each soul being responsible for its own deeds. Al-Nasafī adds that no one is accountable for another's sin. Al-Biqāʿī compares worldly rulers holding a favored person's kin responsible; God's power applies equally to all. Ibn ʿAṭiyya also transmits an explanation that no one is questioned about another's sin and deeds for accountability. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL(شيبة والبزي عن عاصم); NASAFI-FULL(البزي والبرجمي); BIQAI-FULL(ابن كثير)]
- 70:10/q04/p8 On the passive reading, no one asks a relative about where the other relative is or about the other's condition. — Al-Farrāʾ paraphrases the passive as asking a friend where his friend is but rejects it as contrary to the agreed reading and exegesis. Al-Wāḥidī repeats the objection yet says Ibn Kathīr's passive can mean learning the other's condition. Al-Baghawī and Ibn al-Jawzī give the 'where' wording. Al-Ṭabrisī likens it to ordinary friends and relatives asking after one another. Al-Ālūsī gives condition inquiry as another passive explanation. QIRAAT-ER describes the passive as inability to ask. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن كثير); BAGHAWI-FULL(البزي عن ابن كثير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(معاوية، أبو رزين، الحسن، سعيد بن جبير، مجاهد، عكرمة، ابن محيصن، ابن أبي عبلة، أبو جعفر); TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); QIRAAT-ER(Pss); FARRA-; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)-]
- 70:10/q04/p9 With the second ḥamīm omitted, close associates recognize one another. — Al-Zajjāj connects this shorter reading to 'they see them' in 70:11; he contrasts the included-word reading, where one relative does not ask about another. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]

## 70:10/q05 What does the indefinite repetition of ḥamīm convey?  [حَمِيمٌ حَمِيمًۭا · rhetoric]
- 70:10/q05/p1 It extends the scene to every pair of close people. — Bursevī explicitly says the indefinite wording generalizes the statement to every close-friend pair; al-Ṭabrisī observes that plural language can refer to many intimates despite singular ḥamīm. [2 notes: BURSEVI; TABRISI]

## 70:10/q06 How does the sight of others in the next verse bear on this verse?  [* · links]
turns on: Who is seen in 70:11, and whether sight supplies recognition, makes inquiry unnecessary, or sharpens the terror of failing to ask.
- 70:10/q06/p1 People see or recognize one another, so their failure to ask occurs despite visibility. — Ibn Qutayba says they see and recognize each other. Al-Zajjāj links the shorter reading to recognition. Al-Ṭabrisī says seeing removes the need to ask; al-Baghawī, cited by al-Biqāʿī, says every human or jinn is in view of a companion. Ibn Kathīr reports Ibn ʿAbbās through al-ʿAwfī: recognition is followed by flight. Al-Biqāʿī says absence of questions comes from self-concern, not concealment. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the suffering friend is seen and the omitted object of asking is therefore clear. Asad likewise says friends can see one another. [11 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس عن العوفي); BIQAI-FULL(البغوي); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 70:10/q06/p2 In the reading that retains the second ḥamīm, those seen may be angels. — Al-Zajjāj proposes this for the retained-word construction while taking the verse itself to say no relative asks about another. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]

## 70:10/q07 How does this verse fit the surrounding Day of Judgment scene and related verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes trace different parts of the same scene: the earlier cosmic and fire imagery, visible but neglected intimates, later ransom, and parallels elsewhere in the Qurʾān.
- 70:10/q07/p1 It shows terror dissolving ordinary kinship concern, between cosmic upheaval and the criminal's escalating wish to ransom himself with relatives and all humanity. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the opening 'day when the sky...' can be a new sentence, with this statement replacing an embedded subject; terror explains the failure to ask and frames the ransom wish. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ traces the escalation from intimates not asking to ransom with ever dearer relatives and everyone on earth. Iṣlāḥī combines cosmic upheaval, collapse of social bonds, the family and tribal ransom, and Hell seizing those who avoided the call and hoarded wealth. Kur'an Yolu attributes the willingness to sacrifice spouse, children, close kin and all earth to the sinner's psychological terror. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes that the citation proceeds from the sky like muhl in 70:8 to this verse; her accompanying note quotes al-Rāghib on the preceding fire's flame becoming still and al-Qurṭubī that this does not reduce torment. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 70:10/q07/p2 Other Qurʾānic passages depict each person occupied with himself, fleeing family, and unable to carry another's burden. — Al-Wāḥidī cites 22:2 and 80:34–37. Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī and StudyQuran cite 80:37. Ibn Kathīr also cites 31:33, 35:18, 23:101 and 80:34–37; StudyQuran mentions 23:101, 31:33 and 35:18. Al-Ṭabrisī also sets this beside 26:101, the absence of intercessors and close friends. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ)]

## 70:10/q08 Who is the sinner described in the surrounding scene?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the surrounding designation is restricted to unbelievers or extends to sinners generally.
- 70:10/q08/p1 The sinner may refer to unbelievers. — Kur'an Yolu reports this as one explanation of the sinner in the surrounding passage. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler)]
- 70:10/q08/p2 The sinner may refer more generally to sinners. — Kur'an Yolu reports this as the broader explanation of the sinner in the surrounding passage. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler)]
