# 11:41: 34 questions

## 11:41/q01 Who says “board”?   [وَقَالَ · referent]
turns on: The pronoun in “said” and the first person “my Lord.”
- 11:41/q01/p1 Noah speaks to the believers he was commanded to carry. — Al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn ʿĀshūr connect the address to the preceding order to carry them (11:40); Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes “my Lord” and al-Ālūsī and Abū Ḥayyān argue God would not naturally say it. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 11:41/q01/p2 God speaks to Noah and his companions. — Reported as an alternative by al-Qurṭubī and al-Samīn; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī object from “my Lord” rather than “your Lord.” [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 11:41/q02 Whom does “board” address?  [ٱرْكَبُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether a plural imperative extends to the animals carried aboard.
- 11:41/q02/p1 The human believers and family whom Noah was ordered to carry. — Abū Ḥayyān says the imperative suits rational humans, not animals; Ibn ʿĀshūr ties it to 11:40. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says household and other believers, or all aboard. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 11:41/q02/p2 The address can include all aboard, with human beings governing the plural. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this view but prefers the address to humans alone; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ allows everyone aboard. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 11:41/q03 What does “board” mean here?  [ٱرْكَبُوا۟ · meaning]
- 11:41/q03/p1 Enter or mount the ark. — Al-Wāḥidī says rakiba broadly means mounting animals, ships, land or sea, even debt burdening a person; Ibn Aḥmar poetically calls ship passengers rukbān. Al-Bayḍāwī and Ibn ʿĀshūr liken the moving vessel to a land mount; al-Ālūsī reports literal and metaphorical accounts. Damghānī compares 36:42 and 29:65; Ibn al-Jawzī cites 16:8 and narrower camel terminology. [15 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن أحمر); DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن فارس); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب,القاضي البيضاوي,بعض المحققين); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 11:41/q04 How does the command connect to loading the ark?  [وَقَالَ ٱرْكَبُوا۟ · links]
- 11:41/q04/p1 The command follows God's order to carry them, with loading or intervening events left understood. — Al-Ṭabarī infers the omitted carrying from the earlier order and present command. Al-Ṭabrisī supplies the flood's onset and Noah's recognition of God's sign; al-Ālūsī places the saying after the pairs were loaded. Al-Biqāʿī says Noah gathered them promptly; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 11:40. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 11:41/q05 What does “in it” refer to?  [فِيهَا · referent]
- 11:41/q05/p1 The ark or ship. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes the feminine pronoun despite the grammatically masculine fulk. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the vessel of planks and nails at 54:13. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 11:41/q06 Why is “in” used with “board”?  [ٱرْكَبُوا۟ فِيهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether fī is required by an included sense of entering, fills an omitted-object construction, or adds emphasis.
- 11:41/q06/p1 “Board” includes entering or traveling inside the vessel, so fī retains its locative force. — Al-Samīn and Abū Ḥayyān explain the included sense of entering; al-Biqāʿī calls containment usual. Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts ʿalā for animals with fī for a ship. Al-Ālūsī prefers the entry analysis; he also reports that moving vessels take fī without requiring passengers to be below deck. [9 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(القاضي البيضاوي,بعض المحققين,بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 11:41/q06/p2 The object “water” is omitted: ride the water while in the ark. — Al-Wāḥidī says Arabic normally takes the ship as direct object, making fīhā circumstantial; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Qurṭubī, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī report the supplied object. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى); SAMIN-DURR(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 11:41/q06/p3 Fī is an emphatic addition meaning “board it,” while also pointing to the ark's interior. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Qurṭubī compare 12:43 and say it avoids suggesting the deck. Al-Suyūṭī lists ten senses of fī with comparative verses, choosing emphasis here; al-Burhān allows emphatic fī. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(قيل); BURHAN; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 11:41/q06/p4 Fī expresses spatial containment, which can be physical or conceptual. — Al-Burhān distinguishes four combinations of physical and conceptual place and contents, with physical containment literal. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 11:41/q07 What is the force of “in God's name” here?  [بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase voices blessing at particular moments, states divine power, or is Noah's own or the passengers' utterance.
- 11:41/q07/p1 Invoke God's name for blessing as the ark sails and moors. — Mujāhid includes boarding, sailing, and docking; al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ emphasize blessing. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says attachment to God's name protects an act from ruin, error, and loss; Ibn ʿĀshūr takes bāʾ as accompaniment. [16 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 11:41/q07/p2 The ark sails and moors by God Himself, His name, power, command, or permission. — Ibn ʿAbbās adds God's power; al-Zajjāj says “by God's name” means by God. Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ deny independent efficacy to the vessel; Ibn Kathīr cites 69:11–12 and 54:13–15. Al-Burṣawī links origin and return to 53:42. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس في رواية عطاء); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 11:41/q07/p3 The basmala is Noah's invocation, not a merely impersonal statement. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ prefers Noah as speaker and verbal-noun senses; al-Biqāʿī reports both Noah saying it and his directing passengers to say it. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 11:41/q07/p4 The passengers say the invocation at Noah's direction. — Al-Muqātil says Noah tells them to pronounce it at starting and stopping; al-Biqāʿī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ report this alternative. [3 notes: MUQATIL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 11:41/q08 What grammatical role does “in God's name” play?  [بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ مَجْر۪ىٰهَا وَمُرْسَىٰهَآ · grammar]
turns on: The syntactic governor, whether the wording is one sentence or two, and whether a circumstantial clause has a pronoun linking it to its subject.
- 11:41/q08/p1 It is circumstantial to the riders: they board naming God. — Al-Fārisī compares 5:61; al-Ālūsī calls bāʾ accompaniment or supplies “saying.” The sailing and mooring words are times of the invocation, not times to embark, as Makkī stresses. [22 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو علي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(مكي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الكشاف)]
- 11:41/q08/p2 It forms a complete nominal sentence: the ark's sailing and mooring are by God's name. — Al-Farrāʾ and Ibn al-Anbārī make bi the predicate and the nouns subjects; al-Naḥḥās also reports an omitted mawḍiʿ. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān say a circumstantial link to “board” lacks a returning pronoun, though it can link to “in it” or stand independently. Al-Ālūsī says the sentence reassures against drowning. [27 notes: TAB-FULL(قراء أهل المدينة والبصرة وبعض الكوفيين,قراء الكوفيين); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أحمد بن يحيى,ابن الأنباري,الفراء); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو علي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(مكي); BURSEVI(الكواشي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 11:41/q08/p3 The phrase is a prospective circumstantial description of the ship sailing and mooring by God. — Al-Kashshāf compares 39:73. Al-Taqrīb objects that a clause implies present action, while al-Kashf permits prospective clauses; al-Ālūsī finds the defense awkward and also rejects attaching such a clause to riders without a link. Al-Raḍī allows omission of links in obvious accompaniment, but al-Ālūsī finds it inapplicable. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الرضي,الكشف); ALUSI-FULL(التقريب)-]
- 11:41/q08/p4 The bāʾ is governed by an omitted verb of beginning or of the particular action begun. — Aḥmad ibn Yaḥyā supplies a verb; al-Rāzī allows “begin in God's name.” Al-Suyūṭī says rhetoricians supply the particular act, such as “I read” or “I eat,” whereas grammarians supply a generic “I begin” or “my beginning” (1:1). [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أحمد بن يحيى); RAZI-FULL; ITQAN]
- 11:41/q08/p5 The words form a circumstantial phrase attached to either the riders or the ark, according to its internal syntax. — Abū ʿAlī in al-Ṭabrisī restricts a fronted-predicate sentence to the pronoun in fīhā because it lacks a link to the riders. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Makkī make the same linking objection. [5 notes: TABRISI(أبو علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(مكي)]

## 11:41/q09 What happens when Noah pronounces the formula?  [بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ مَجْر۪ىٰهَا وَمُرْسَىٰهَآ · reports]
- 11:41/q09/p1 The ark moves when he says it intending to sail and stops when he says it intending to moor. — This is al-Ḍaḥḥāk's recurring report, also related without attribution by al-Kashshāf and others; al-Ṭabarī explicitly says Noah's intent determined which motion followed. [18 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); NAHHAS(الضحاك); THALABI(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك); TABRISI(الضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); SAMIN-DURR; DURR-FULL(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك)]

## 11:41/q10 What practice does the ark invocation support?  [بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ · law]
- 11:41/q10/p1 Name God on boarding a ship or mount and at the start of an undertaking. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr urges gratitude and Noah's words on boarding and God's name on an animal; he says Satan tells a rider who omits it to sing or walk. Al-Qurṭubī generalizes to every action; al-Rāzī says initial remembrance brings blessed completion. Ibn Kathīr links 23:28–29 and 43:12–14. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 11:41/q11 What reports promise protection when boarding?  [بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ · reports]
- 11:41/q11/p1 Reciting the basmala with Noah's words and a verse on God's measure protects from drowning. — Al-Qurṭubī cites a report through al-Ḥusayn ibn ʿAlī naming the full basmala; Ibn Kathīr reports a version attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās with 39:67, while al-Durr reports versions citing 6:91 and a promise lasting until disembarkation. Al-Itqān transmits the al-Ḥusayn report with 39:67. [7 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ITQAN(الحسين بن علي)]

## 11:41/q12 What is meant by God's “name” in the expression?  [بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ism contributes independently to the phrase or is syntactically inserted.
- 11:41/q12/p1 “Name” is an inserted word, so the expression means “by God.” — Al-Bayḍāwī cites a poetic line, “Then the name of peace be upon you both”; al-Ālūsī cites Labīd and understands divine power, command or permission. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:41/q12/p2 The divine name Allah is God's greatest name. — Jābir ibn Zayd argues it precedes every other name throughout the Qur'an; al-Ḍaḥḥāk assigns the basmala's letters to splendor, glory and sovereignty, and says Allah is creation's deity. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك,جابر بن زيد)]
- 11:41/q12/p3 The alif of ism is omitted in the written basmala through frequent use. — Al-Burhān reports the outward scholars' majority restriction: omission occurs when ism follows bāʾ and is annexed to Allah. [1 notes: BURHAN(علماء الظاهر)]

## 11:41/q13 What alternative wording of the invocation is reported?  [بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ · readings]
turns on: Different transmitted wording.
- 11:41/q13/p1 Ubayy's codex has “ʿalā ismi Allāh” instead of “bi-smi Allāh.” — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the codex wording. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبي)]
- 11:41/q13/p2 Ibn Masʿūd recited wording referring to its sailing and anchoring. — Al-Durr preserves the variant without further linguistic argument. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود)]

## 11:41/q14 How are the vowels and forms of “its sailing and mooring” read?  [مَجْر۪ىٰهَا وَمُرْسَىٰهَآ · readings]
turns on: The mīm vowels distinguish causative and basic verb patterns; the participial forms name God as agent, and reciters also vary imāla and following vowels.
- 11:41/q14/p1 Both initial mīms have ḍamma, yielding causative verbal nouns. — Attributed to the Medinan, Meccan, Basran and many named readers; Abū ʿUbayd and Abū Ḥātim prefer it. Al-Thaʿlabī compares 12:67 and 17:80; al-Baghawī adds 23:29. Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites Labīd. Some reports differ on the following rāʾ and sīn. Al-Burṣawī says the a/u forms of majrāhā are synonymous verbal nouns and the u-form of mursāhā denotes coming to rest. [22 notes: TAB-FULL(قراء أهل المدينة والبصرة وبعض الكوفيين,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وعاصم في رواية أبي بكر وابن عامر); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وعاصم وابن عامر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمران الجوني، ابن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(قراءة أهل الحرمين وأهل البصرة); NASAFI-FULL(حمزة وعلى وحفص); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); BURSEVI(الكواشي); THALABI(الباقون، أبو عبيد، أبو حاتم)+]
- 11:41/q14/p2 Majrāhā has fatḥa on mīm, with kasra or imāla at rāʾ; mursāhā has ḍamma on mīm. — Attributed to Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, Ḥafṣ and others. Al-Ṭabarī prefers the first fatḥa because 11:42 says “it sails,” retaining second ḍamma by reciters' consensus; Ibn ʿĀshūr says ḍamma avoids confusion with a mooring place. Abū Razīn and Abū al-Mutawakkil have fatḥa on rāʾ. Ibn Masʿūd has imāla. Qirāʾāt-Er identifies imāla as a dialectal tilt. [23 notes: TAB-FULL(قراء الكوفيين); NAHHAS; FARISI-HUJJA(حمزة والكسائي وحفص عن عاصم); JISHUMI(حمزة والكسائي وحفص); BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وحفص); IBNATIYYA-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وحفص عن عاصم); TABRISI(أهل الكوفة غير أبي بكر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو رزين، أبو المتوكل,ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(الأعمش وحمزة والكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(حمزة وعلى وحفص); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وحفص عن عاصم وخلف); TABATABAI(قرئ); QIRAAT-ER; BURSEVI(الكواشي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري)+; TAB-FULL+]
- 11:41/q14/p3 Both initial mīms have fatḥa, deriving from the simple verbs for sailing and settling. — Attributed variously to Ibn Muḥayṣin, Yaḥyā ibn Waththāb, al-Aʿmash, Ibn Masʿūd and others; reports differ on imāla and the following consonant vowels. Al-Thaʿlabī cites an Imruʾ al-Qays line for the noun pattern; al-Ṭabarī cites ʿAntarah's tarsū. Several sources say the nouns can denote action, time, or place. [20 notes: AKHFASH(بعضهم); TAB-FULL(بعض الكوفيين); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(يحيى بن وثاب); THALABI(محمد بن محيصن); JISHUMI(محمد بن محيصن); BAGHAWI-FULL(محمد بن محيصن); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعمش وابن مسعود); TABRISI(ابن محيصن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو الجوزاء، ابن يعمر,أبو منصور اللغوي,يحيى بن وثاب); QURTUBI-FULL(يحيى بن وثاب); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:41/q14/p4 Both words are active participles, mujrihā and mursīhā, describing God as the one who causes sailing and mooring. — Reported from Abū Rajāʾ, Mujāhid and other readers; some lists include al-Nakhaʿī, al-Jaḥdarī, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Ḥasan and Qatāda. - 11:41/q14/p5 Different canonical reciters vary the imāla or openness of the following consonants while retaining the reported mīm readings. — Ibn al-Jawzī says Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr, Ibn ʿĀmir and Ḥafṣ open sīn; Nāfiʿ and Abū Bakr use an intermediate vowel, Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī and Khalaf incline it. Abū ʿImrān and Ibn Jubayr raise both mīms with open rāʾ and sīn; al-Nasafī reports Abū ʿAmr with kasra on rāʾ. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمران الجوني، ابن جبير); NASAFI-FULL(حمزة وعلى وحفص); QIRAAT-ER]

## 11:41/q15 How are the active-participle forms parsed?  [مَجْر۪ىٰهَا وَمُرْسَىٰهَآ · grammar]
turns on: Whether annexed participles can be definite enough to modify God, or instead require apposition, an implied subject, or accusative syntax.
- 11:41/q15/p1 They are genitive attributes of God. — Al-Ṭabarī considers genitive likelier than circumstantial accusative; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū al-Baqāʾ use adjective analysis. Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī object that the future participle's annexation remains indefinite, unless it becomes definite as al-Khalīl permits in some cases. [10 notes: AKHFASH(بعضهم); TAB-FULL(أبو رجاء العطاردي); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن وثاب وأبو رجاء العطاري والنخعي والجحدري والكلبي والضحاك بن مزاحم ومسلم بن جندب وأهل الشام); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد وسليمان بن جندب وعاصم الجحدري وأبو رجاء العطاردي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء,الخليل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 11:41/q15/p2 They are appositions or predicate substitutes for God's name. — Abū Ḥayyān favors predicate substitutes over indefinite adjectives; al-Samīn calls them appositions and al-Ālūsī finds badal sounder. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:41/q15/p3 They are nominative after an omitted “He.” — Al-Zajjāj, al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī allow an implicit huwa. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد وسليمان بن جندب وعاصم الجحدري وأبو رجاء العطاردي)]
- 11:41/q15/p4 They are accusative circumstantial modifiers, or an accusative of praise. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī allow the circumstantial case; al-Zajjāj gives the praise parse, and Makkī calls his estimated circumstantial analysis difficult. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو رجاء العطاردي); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد وسليمان بن جندب وعاصم الجحدري وأبو رجاء العطاردي); SAMIN-DURR(مكي)]

## 11:41/q16 What does “its sailing” denote?  [مَجْر۪ىٰهَا · meaning]

## 11:41/q26 What forgiveness and mercy does the closing clause describe?  [إِنَّ رَبِّى لَغَفُورٌۭ رَّحِيمٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause emphasizes pardon for sins, unearned grace, or care sustaining the ship.
- 11:41/q26/p1 God forgives the passengers' former sins and mercifully saves them after repentance. — Al-Muqātil identifies rescue from punishment; Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports Saʿīd ibn Jubayr's former idolatry. - 11:41/q26/p2 Their rescue came through unearned divine grace rather than the merit of their faith or deeds. — Al-Rāzī warns survivors against crediting their own knowledge; al-Burṣawī and al-Ālūsī identify a Sunni account of sheer grace. - 11:41/q26/p3 God's mercy protects the ark's course and mooring from failure and the passengers from drowning. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says the visible means work only through God's sustaining care and that the invocation rests on forgiveness and mercy. [3 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 11:41/q27 Why does Noah mention forgiveness and mercy at this point?  [إِنَّ رَبِّى لَغَفُورٌۭ رَّحِيمٌۭ · rhetoric]

- 11:41/q27/p1 The words explain rescue and reassure the passengers amid danger. — Al-Kashshāf says they could not be saved without these attributes; al-Rāzī warns against self-credit. - 11:41/q27/p2 It follows destruction of unbelievers to balance divine vengeance with mercy. — Ibn Kathīr compares 7:167 and 13:6. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 11:41/q27/p3 It signals that obedience brings mercy, as boarding brought rescue. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī make the analogy; al-Ālūsī reports an implied rationale, “obey so God may save you.” [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 11:41/q27/p4 It directly gives the reason for the boarding command. — Some infer the passengers' eventual salvation from the clause; al-Ālūsī objects that a direct causal connection is absent unless an unstated rationale is supplied. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL-]

- 11:41/q28/p1 The ark figures reliance on God or the Sharia as a means of rescue from spiritual danger. — Al-Qushayrī stresses God's name and favor over schemes; al-Rāzī likens a knowledge seeker to a vessel of reflection amid waves of error. - 11:41/q28/p2 God's greatest Name is the fully realized human knower through whom law enters and stands in the material world. — Al-Qāshānī includes prophets, imams and scholars; he reads forgiveness as removal of dark bodily traits and mercy as knowledge and unveiling that save from drowning. - 11:41/q28/p3 God forgives those saved by boarding and has mercy on those who board in obedience to the command. — Al-Burṣawī contrasts the latter with instinctive boarding; he cites hypocrites and Iblis as examples of ineffective imitation. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

- 11:41/q29/p1 It pairs naming God at departure with thanksgiving and prayer on reaching safety. — Ibn Kathīr compares 23:28–29 and 43:12–14 and recommends naming God on a ship or animal; al-Qurṭubī and Ibn Kathīr connect the protection formula with 39:67. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
