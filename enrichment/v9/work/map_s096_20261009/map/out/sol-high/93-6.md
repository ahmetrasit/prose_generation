# 93:6: 24 questions

## 93:6/q01 What does the opening question do?  [أَلَمْ · rhetoric]
- 93:6/q01/p1 It expects assent, emphatically affirming the established favor and reminding the Prophet of it. — Al-Burhān says a report interrogative presses an already known admission, cannot be formed with hal according to Abū al-Fatḥ, and can coordinate with an affirmative clause (94:1; 105:2). Al-Ālūsī describes the hamza as negating the negation. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it confirmatory and pictures a distressed person met with aid. [9 notes: BURHAN(أبو الفتح,بعضهم); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 93:6/q02 How does 'find' work here?  [يَجِدْكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether wajada denotes knowledge with two objects, encounter with a circumstantial accusative, or a figurative act of selection; literal discovery of new information is rejected for God.
- 93:6/q02/p1 It means know, taking 'you' and 'orphan' as two objects. — Al-Naḥḥās allows seeing or knowing with two objects; al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī explicitly parse two accusatives. Al-Rāghib and al-Ālūsī say divine finding must be pure knowledge, since God has no organs and cannot discover what was outside His prior knowledge and decree. Elmalılı notes that creaturely finding follows absence or ignorance. [17 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI(الراغب,الرضي,بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 93:6/q02/p2 It means find or encounter; 'you' is its object and 'orphan' describes the circumstance. — Al-Bayḍāwī permits this alongside the two-object analysis. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers the one-object construction for all three favor verses. Al-Biqāʿī takes finding as encounter followed by care. Al-Ālūsī allows it only figuratively of God, whose knowledge precedes all events. [8 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 93:6/q02/p3 Finding figuratively expresses God's choosing the Prophet from creation. — Elmalılı prefers istifāʾ: finding a rare valuable object images selection, repeated across the three verses. [1 notes: ELMALILI+]
- 93:6/q02/p4 It could mean bringing the Prophet into existence. — Elmalılı explicitly calls this possible but nonstandard. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 93:6/q02/p5 The word foregrounds inward reassurance and belongs to a family of words for existence, knowledge, ecstasy and possession. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ calls it a heart verb that attends to the Prophet's inner state. Elmalılı traces wājid to existence, finding, ecstasy and wealth; God possesses existence, knowledge, power and wealth. [2 notes: BINTSHATI; ELMALILI]

## 93:6/q03 Can God be described as mawjūd in connection with wajada?  [يَجِدْكَ · theology]
- 93:6/q03/p1 Later scholars allow it, while earlier scholars avoided the passive form for created existence. — Elmalılı notes that revealed divine names include Wājid and al-Ḥaqq. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 93:6/q04 What does 'orphan' mean here?  [يَتِيمًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether yatīm carries its ordinary paternal-loss sense, the pearl metaphor, or a spiritual/allegorical sense.
- 93:6/q04/p1 A child whose father died before puberty, as Muhammad's father did. — Al-Māwardī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr define it by paternal death; al-Rāghib, cited by Bint al-Shāṭiʾ, adds the limit of puberty. Several notes stress that both parents eventually died. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says Quranic contexts associate orphans with poverty, exposed property and oppression (2:83; 4:10; 89:17; 107:2, among others), and eleven passages pair orphanhood with poverty. [23 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(الراغب); DURR(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); DURR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); BINTSHATI(الزمخشري)+]
- 93:6/q04/p2 It means unmatched and peerless, like a unique pearl. — The pearl expression is cited by al-Māwardī, Mujāhid and others; the referent is unmatched honor and virtue among Quraysh, or potentially all creation in al-Ālūsī's suggested improvement. Elmalılı treats the sense as an additional rhetorical echo. Al-Zamakhsharī calls the interpretation inventive or eccentric; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ dismiss it. [15 notes: THALABI(عبد الوهاب بن مجاهد عن أبيه); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); BURSEVI(الكشاف); ALUSI(الزمخشري); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI(الرازي); KASHSHAF-; KASHSHAF-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)-; BINTSHATI(الزمخشري)-]
- 93:6/q04/p3 It means solitary, and God placed him among companions. — Al-Tustarī explains yatīm as solitary rather than identifying it with paternal loss. [1 notes: TUSTARI]
- 93:6/q04/p4 It signifies the solitary soul veiled by its own qualities from the true father, the Holy Spirit. — Al-Qāshānī says divine care and education restore the soul, with a fatherly guardian teaching and purifying it. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 93:6/q04/p5 It refers allegorically to lacking a teacher. — ʿIṣām al-Dīn pairs this with lacking knowledge and a community needing guidance; al-Ālūsī calls the threefold allegory strained. [1 notes: ALUSI(عصام الدين); ALUSI-]

## 93:6/q05 What sequence of losses and guardianship do the reports give?  [يَتِيمًۭا · reports]
turns on: The mother's age is given as two, six or eight; the father's death is placed before birth or shortly afterward.
- 93:6/q05/p1 His father died before his birth; his mother when he was six; his grandfather when he was eight; then Abu Talib cared for him. — Ibn Shihāb says ʿAbd Allāh died while seeking dates in Yathrib for ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib; al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı say Muhammad was a six-month fetus. Ibn Kathīr reports the six- and eight-year ages; Rāzī's historians and the full account agree. Al-Kāshshāf instead gives his mother's age as eight; other reports give two. [13 notes: RAZI(أهل الأخبار); RAZI-FULL(أهل الأخبار); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR(ابن شهاب); DURR-FULL(ابن شهاب); ALUSI(روي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES]
- 93:6/q05/p2 His father died before birth; his mother died when he was eight; Abu Talib then raised him. — Al-Kashshāf says the father died when Muhammad was a six-month fetus. Abū Ḥayyān repeats this chronology. Al-Biqāʿī and al-Ṭabrisī say the grandfather died when Muhammad was eight, with al-Ṭabrisī giving two rather than eight for his mother's death. [7 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI(جعفر الصادق); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI(صاحب الكشاف)]
- 93:6/q05/p3 His father died before birth or soon after, his mother at two, and his grandfather at eight. — Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī give two for the mother's death; al-Ṭabrisī allows the father's death shortly after birth. [2 notes: TABRISI; TABATABAI]
- 93:6/q05/p4 His father may have died shortly after his birth. — Ibn Kathīr reports this as an alternative; Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise allows death very early in infancy. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 93:6/q05/p5 The notes establish parental loss and succession of grandfather and uncle without fixing all ages. — Al-Māwardī describes care first by ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, then Abū Ṭālib. ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr stresses the loss of both parents. [9 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; DURR(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); DURR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; NASAFI]

## 93:6/q06 How did God shelter the Prophet?  [فَـَٔاوَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether shelter names a home and guardians, later companions, direct divine nurture, a nurse, or shelter extended to others through him.
- 93:6/q06/p1 He provided human guardians, especially Abu Talib, who raised, supported and protected him. — Muqātil and many others name Abū Ṭālib; al-Māwardī and al-Ṭabrisī include ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib first. Al-Farrāʾ says divine contentment relieved need for Abū Ṭālib without wealth. Ibn Kathīr says the uncle defended him despite remaining an idolater, and the Anṣār sheltered him after the uncle died. Qutb says God inclined hearts to care for him. Study Quran adds Khadīja as refuge; Islahi compares Moses' providential upbringing and cites 89:17 and 107:2. [45 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI(مقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI(أهل الأخبار); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; ALUSI(روي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; BINTSHATI+]
- 93:6/q06/p2 He gave him a home and place to dwell. — Al-Ṭabarī leaves the human guardian unspecified. Al-Māwardī's literal reading adds a compassionate guardian after his father's and grandfather's deaths. [5 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI(مقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL]
- 93:6/q06/p3 He gave him a home of his own and independence from Abu Talib's care. — Al-Kalbī, also named Ibn al-Sāʾib, offers this as an alternative to continued guardianship. [3 notes: MAWARDI(الكلبي); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب)]
- 93:6/q06/p4 He sheltered him among companions or the Ansar. — Al-Tustarī joins the solitary Prophet to his companions; Mujāhid's pearl reading gives protective companions. Ibn Kathīr details the later Anṣār's defense and fighting for him after Abū Ṭālib's death. [5 notes: TUSTARI; QURTUBI(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 93:6/q06/p5 He sheltered him in intimate divine care and selected him for the message. — Al-Māwardī and al-Ṭabrisī attach this to the peerless-person reading; al-Qushayrī says God nurtured him to an intimate station none shared. Elmalılı speaks of sheltering his heart; al-Qāshānī adds spiritual education. [7 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; TABRISI; QASHANI; ELMALILI]
- 93:6/q06/p6 He sheltered the Prophet through a wet nurse who came to love him. — Al-Ālūsī reports that wet nurses initially refused him, but blessing through his companionship changed the nurse's heart; he prefers the ordinary orphaned-child account. [2 notes: ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 93:6/q06/p7 He made the Prophet himself a refuge for orphans and protector of people. — Al-Māwardī reverses the image: one who had been cared for became caretaker. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ similarly says God shelters orphans, guides the lost and enriches the needy through him; al-Sāmarāʾī says shelter reaches others through or for him. [8 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(الماوردي); SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 93:6/q07 What does āwā mean in this verse?  [فَـَٔاوَىٰ · meaning]
- 93:6/q07/p1 It means to gather someone into a dwelling, protection or care. — Al-Ālūsī defines sheltering as joining one thing to another; Elmalılı as gathering and lodging. Ibn ʿĀshūr gives the literal root sense of returning to a dwelling, figuratively sponsorship and meeting needs; he says God developed the Prophet's understanding and character despite orphanhood. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says fourteen Quranic uses convey haven or refuge and that the open verb implies safety from social and psychological harms rather than restoring his father or riches. [8 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI]

## 93:6/q08 How is fa-āwā read, and what can the alternate form mean?  [فَـَٔاوَىٰ · readings]
turns on: Whether the verb is quadriliteral or triliteral, and whether the shorter verb is taken as sheltering/refuge or mercy.
- 93:6/q08/p1 The common reading has the transitive quadriliteral fa-āwā, 'He sheltered.' — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn contrast the majority's quadriliteral with Abū al-Ashhab's triliteral. Al-Samīn notes that the shepherd's present tense āwī demonstrates the triliteral, since quadriliteral would be uʾwī, with two hamzas kept distinct as in 70:13. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور وأبو الأشهب العقيلي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR]
- 93:6/q08/p2 Abu al-Ashhab's shorter triliteral can mean 'He sheltered him' or 'He took refuge.' — Al-Zamakhsharī cites a shepherd's usage for the shelter sense; al-Samīn explains its morphology. Al-Ālūsī adds the refuge possibility and verbal nouns. [9 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI(أبو الأشعث); ALUSI-FULL(أبو الأشعث)]
- 93:6/q08/p3 Abu al-Ashhab's shorter triliteral means 'He showed mercy.' — Al-Ashhab al-ʿUqaylī is the reported reader. Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites aw aytu li-fulān; Abū Ḥayyān cites a poet's line; al-Zamakhsharī cites shepherd language for an alternative shelter sense. Some sources name him Abū al-Ashʿath, a naming variant. [19 notes: THALABI(أشهب العقيلي); JISHUMI(أشهب العقيلي); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(الأشهب العقيلي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأشهب العقيلي); TABRISI(أشهب العقيلي); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور وأبو الأشهب العقيلي,الشاعر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI(أبو الأشعث); ALUSI-FULL(أبو الأشعث); BINTSHATI(الرازي)]

## 93:6/q09 Why is the object of 'sheltered' unspoken?  [فَـَٔاوَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the omitted object simply supplies 'you' from context or deliberately broadens the beneficiaries.
- 93:6/q09/p1 The understood object is 'you'; omission is concise and preserves the verse endings. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr apply the same account to guided and enriched. Ibn ʿĀshūr also cites the omission after qalaa because waddaka supplies its object, comparing 33:35. [6 notes: FARRA; THALABI; ALUSI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 93:6/q09/p2 Its openness includes sheltering Muhammad and others through or for him. — Al-Sāmarāʾī says āwā is normally transitive and compares explicit objects at 8:26, 70:13, 12:69 and 49:17; leaving one unexpressed honors the Prophet and widens shelter, guidance and provision. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ likewise includes people served through his mission. A reported version in al-Ālūsī proposes expansive generosity, though he objects that the following fa-clauses resist it. [5 notes: BINTSHATI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ALUSI(قيل)-]

## 93:6/q10 Why was the Prophet made an orphan?  [يَتِيمًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: The notes offer distinct proposed wisdoms: freedom from human claims, empathy, reliance, public proof of character, divine education, miraculous reversal, honor for orphans and humility.
- 93:6/q10/p1 So no creature could claim a parental right over him. — Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq's answer is repeatedly transmitted; al-Biqāʿī ties it to full orphanhood before puberty. [10 notes: THALABI(جعفر بن محمد الصادق); IBNATIYYA(جعفر بن محمد الصادق); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جعفر بن محمد الصادق); TABRISI(الصادق); QURTUBI(جعفر بن محمد الصادق); QURTUBI-FULL(جعفر بن محمد الصادق); ABUHAYYAN(جعفر الصادق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(جعفر الصادق); BIQAI(جعفر الصادق); BIQAI-FULL(جعفر الصادق)]
- 93:6/q10/p2 To teach him orphans' needs, so he would defend them. — Al-Rāzī compares Joseph's refusal to eat his fill lest he forget the hungry; Joseph's own saying is also reported. [3 notes: RAZI(يوسف عليه السلام); RAZI-FULL]
- 93:6/q10/p3 To direct his reliance toward God alone. — Al-Rāzī compares Abraham's trust and Mary's attribution of provision to God at 3:37. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 93:6/q10/p4 So public scrutiny of a vulnerable orphan would establish his faultless character. — Al-Rāzī says people notice and amplify an orphan's faults; finding none left no objection to his prophethood. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 93:6/q10/p5 To show his excellence came from God rather than parental education. — Al-Rāzī notes that fathers ordinarily teach and discipline children; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 'My Lord disciplined me and made my discipline excellent.' [3 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 93:6/q10/p6 To make his supreme rise despite orphanhood and poverty a miracle. — Al-Rāzī says the two are normally worldly disadvantages, so the outcome reverses expectations. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 93:6/q10/p7 To make shared orphanhood a reason to honor orphans. — Al-Rāzī adduces a report urging honor and room for a child named Muhammad; the comparison extends the Prophet's designation to other orphans. [3 notes: RAZI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL]
- 93:6/q10/p8 To keep the Prophet mindful of God's gifts and prevent self-admiration. — Al-Rāzī states this as a wisdom of recalling the circumstances. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 93:6/q11 What does Abu Talib report about the child in his care?  [فَـَٔاوَىٰ · reports]
- 93:6/q11/p1 He guarded Muhammad closely and observed exceptional modesty, fragrance, devotion and character. — Abū Ṭālib tells al-ʿAbbās the child stayed by him or in his bed, sometimes seemed miraculously absent, smelled of musk, asked privacy when undressing, invoked the One God before meals and praised Him afterward, and avoided lies, idle laughter, pagan customs and childish play. Al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı also cite a couplet about noble signs in the cradle; al-Rāzī mentions the monk Baḥīrā among well-known marvel reports. [11 notes: RAZI(أبو طالب); RAZI-FULL(أبو طالب); ALUSI(أبو طالب,شاعر); ALUSI-FULL(أبو طالب); ELMALILI(أبو طالب)]

## 93:6/q12 What larger assurance do the recalled favors give?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the emphasis is reassurance through past care or the duty that follows those favors.
- 93:6/q12/p1 Past sustained care assures the Prophet of continuing favor, fulfillment of the promise and no abandonment. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī say the earliest favors invite patient expectation of more. Al-Rāzī compares continuing nurture to bringing a sound pregnancy to term, contrasts Pharaoh's self-serving reminder to Moses at 26:18, and cites completing bounty at 2:150. Ibn ʿĀshūr argues repeated care cannot be coincidence and says it reassures believers and warns obstinate opponents. Al-Ṭabarī and Qatāda frame orphanhood, perplexity and poverty as states before the mission. Elmalılı says each later state surpassed the former; Qutb calls the recollection intimate and tender. [33 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN; IBNKATHIR(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 93:6/q12/p2 The sequence of favors prepares the Prophet for future duty and care of the vulnerable. — Islahi moves from day/night and developmental trials to reassurance, promised better times, earlier help and duties. Study Quran likewise says consolation leads to care for the downtrodden and preaching. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 93:6/q13 What duties follow from sheltering the orphan?  [* · links]
- 93:6/q13/p1 Shelter orphans, guide seekers, aid the needy and acknowledge God as the source of even those good works. — Al-Rāzī says divine benefaction is a lifelong debt heavier than a financial one: pardon does not erase it and no servant can fully repay God's gifts. Verses 93:9–11 answer the three past favors. Al-Itqān calls the pairing laf wa-nashr; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ contrasts God's shelter with the denier who repels an orphan. Al-Sāmarāʾī cites the report that an orphan's caretaker will be near the Prophet in Paradise. [8 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ITQAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 93:6/q14 How do Sūrat al-Ḍuḥā and Sūrat al-Sharḥ relate?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether shared favor language outweighs the distinction between distress and expanded comfort.
- 93:6/q14/p1 Both recall divine favors, with Sūrat 93 addressing external needs and Sūrat 94 spiritual relief. — Study Quran says 94:1 repeats the interrogative pattern of 93:6, also implied in 93:7–8; both reassure, while 94 adds exertion and turning to God. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 93:6/q14/p2 They may be counted as one sūrah because both enumerate favors. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports this view, comparing shelter for the orphan with expansion of the breast. [1 notes: BINTSHATI(بعضهم)]
- 93:6/q14/p3 The two openings describe different states and should not be equated. — Bursevī rejects an unidentified comparison: the first sūrah depicts distress from unbelievers, the second an opened breast and comfort. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 93:6/q15 What does the report of the Prophet's request for other prophets' gifts say?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the miracle-comparison narrative is plausible for a prophet and whether its response is favor or reproach.
- 93:6/q15/p1 God answered his comparison by enumerating shelter, guidance, provision and further blessings, and the Prophet affirmed them. — Versions mention Abraham, Moses, David, Solomon, wind, fire, friendship, raised remembrance, the opened breast, removed burden, and 2:285–286. Ibn ʿAbbās' version says the Prophet regretted the request; another places it on the Night Journey. The reports vary in their lists and occasion. [17 notes: IBNABIHATIM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI(النبي عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(ابن عباس عن النبي); BAGHAWI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]
- 93:6/q15/p2 The stories that he asked for gifts during or after the Night Journey are unsound. — Al-Jishumī says a prophet asks only with permission, and a permitted request must be answered. Al-Qāḍī argues prophets do not ask without permission and God's answer would otherwise sound like reproach. [3 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI(القاضي); RAZI-FULL(القاضي)]
- 93:6/q15/p3 A pause in revelation, if established, served a benefit and taught use of the exception formula. — Al-Jishumī says forgetting 'if God wills' could instruct believers but could not delay their interests; he rejects a blameworthy account of neglect. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 93:6/q16 What occasion or pause is associated with the sūrah's revelation?  [* · reports]
turns on: The notes give different triggers, durations and reconstructions of the revelation pause.
- 93:6/q16/p1 Jewish questions followed by a promised answer without 'if God wills' preceded a pause. — Al-Thaʿlabī lists questions about Dhū al-Qarnayn, the Cave Companions and the Spirit. [1 notes: THALABI(المفسرون)]
- 93:6/q16/p2 A puppy in the house prevented Gabriel's entry. — Al-Jishumī reports Gabriel's statement that angels do not enter a house with a dog or image. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 93:6/q16/p3 A cut finger, a recited couplet and Umm Jamīl's taunt preceded a short pause. — Jundub ibn Sufyān says revelation stopped two or three nights; Umm Jamīl claimed the Prophet's devil had left him, and Sūrat al-Ḍuḥā followed. [1 notes: JISHUMI(جندب بن سفيان)]
- 93:6/q16/p4 The pause lasted twelve days. — Ibn Jurayj gives twelve days; Ibn ʿĀshūr proposes about twelve days for a second pause, as respite to build capacity for revelation. [3 notes: JISHUMI(ابن جريج); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 93:6/q16/p5 The pause lasted fifteen days. — Attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās by al-Jishumī. [1 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عباس)]
- 93:6/q16/p6 The pause lasted twenty-five days. — Al-Jishumī gives the number without a named transmitter. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 93:6/q16/p7 The pause lasted forty days. — Al-Jishumī attributes this to Muqātil; Ibn ʿĀshūr uses forty days for a first pause and roughly twelve for a second, placing this sūrah at the third revelation to reconcile al-Muddaththir reports. [2 notes: JISHUMI(مقاتل); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 93:6/q16/p8 Gabriel said that he too missed the Prophet but descended only on divine command. — Al-Jishumī transmits this exchange as another pause report. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 93:6/q16/p9 The conflicting reports are unclear, and the pause gave respite for revelation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr finds narrator uncertainty and reconstructs two pauses, the second about twelve days. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 93:6/q17 Why was takbīr connected to these blessings?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the reported response is framed as joy or gratitude.
- 93:6/q17/p1 The Prophet proclaimed takbīr for joy at God's gifts. — Ibn al-Jazarī connects joy to gifts for the Prophet and his community and satisfaction in both lives; an earlier explanation begins with shelter of the orphan. [2 notes: NASHR(قيل)]
- 93:6/q17/p2 He proclaimed takbīr in gratitude for the gifts. — Al-Nashr reports this alternative. [1 notes: NASHR(قيل)]

## 93:6/q18 What merit is reported for reciting al-Ḍuḥā?  [* · sciences]
- 93:6/q18/p1 A report promises accepted intercession and deeds numbered by orphans and petitioners. — Al-Bayḍāwī transmits this recitation virtue. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 93:6/q19 What do dār and related words mean in the lexical notes?  [فَـَٔاوَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: The lexical evidence applies these related forms to places, time, occupations, habits and tribal groups.
- 93:6/q19/p1 Dār is an enclosed inhabited plot, extended to any inhabited place. — Ibn al-Jawzī gives the common usage in the context of dwelling. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 93:6/q19/p2 Dawārī can signify time because it turns people through states. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites a line by al-ʿAjjāj. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 93:6/q19/p3 Dārī can mean a perfumer. — A Prophetic comparison of a righteous companion with a perfumer supports the sense. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 93:6/q19/p4 Al-dārī can mean a person who stays in his home. — Ibn al-Jawzī says he rarely leaves. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 93:6/q19/p5 Dūr can mean tribes. — Ibn al-Jawzī explains the Prophet's question about the best dūr of the Anṣār this way. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 93:6/q20 What does the note say about sound interpretation of this verse?  [* · interpretation]
- 93:6/q20/p1 Tafsīr explains the intended meaning of words and passages and requires deep linguistic and transmitted learning. — Al-Sāmarāʾī includes hadith, Sunna and occasions of revelation; slight familiarity with the language is insufficient. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 93:6/q21 Can 'find' mean see in this verse?  [يَجِدْكَ · meaning]
- 93:6/q21/p1 Yes: God saw Muhammad as an orphan and sheltered him. — Al-Dāmghānī gives seeing as a sense of wajada and also points to 4:91. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]

## 93:6/q22 How do inward readings understand God's finding and disclosure of the Prophet?  [يَجِدْكَ · inward]
turns on: Whether finding evokes primordial seeking or the unveiling of a previously unrecognized rank.
- 93:6/q22/p1 Finding follows seeking; the Prophet was the sought beloved from pre-eternity. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says the Prophet was sought outwardly and sent as an envoy to creation. [1 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
- 93:6/q22/p2 He was hidden and unrecognized among Meccans until God revealed his prophetic rank. — One anonymous authority says the disclosure guided the fortunate and destroyed the wretched; another says God singled him out with favors and made his worth manifest despite his people's ignorance. [2 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]

## 93:6/q23 What did the exchange about Abu Talib's poetry say?  [* · reports]
- 93:6/q23/p1 Muhammad was already noble; Abu Talib could have recognized God's blessing in him. — Muslima cites the three reminders, and ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr replies that Muhammad had already brought great good. [2 notes: DURR(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); DURR-FULL(مسلمة بن مخلد)]

## 93:6/q24 What comparison is made between communities accompanied by a fourth?  [* · links]
- 93:6/q24/p1 A community whose fourth was a dog contrasts with Muhammad's community, whose fourth is God. — Al-Rāzī links 18:22 with 58:7 to magnify God's companionship. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
