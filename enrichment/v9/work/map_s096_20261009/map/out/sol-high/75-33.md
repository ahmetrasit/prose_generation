# 75:33: 19 questions

## 75:33/q01 What does ثُمَّ convey here?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
- 75:33/q01/p1 It marks remoteness or abhorrence at his proud departure. — Al-Alusi says he ought instead to fear God's anger and walk humbly. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 75:33/q02 What does his going away express?  [ذَهَبَ · interpretation]
- 75:33/q02/p1 He leaves the admonition and obedience behind, returning heedlessly after denial. — Al-Nahhas says he treats the warning lightly; al-Wahidi says he denied the Qur'an and turned from faith; Kuranyolu describes blind bigotry, closed ears and heart; Islahi says he responds to reminders with conceit. [4 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 75:33/q03 Whom or what does أَهْلِهِ refer to?  [أَهْلِهِۦ · referent]
turns on: Whether the destination is understood literally as relations or place, or figuratively as his prior outlook.
- 75:33/q03/p1 His household, people, clan, home, or town. — Abu Hayyan glosses it as his people or clan; Bursevi allows household; Elmalili says family, home, or town. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]
- 75:33/q03/p2 His companions. — Bursevi gives this as another possibility beside his household. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 75:33/q03/p3 What he had come from. — Asad renders the return this way without specifying its object in this note. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 75:33/q03/p4 An arrogant belief rooted in social materialism. — Asad's note breaks off mid-phrase, so its fuller explanation is unavailable. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 75:33/q04 Why does his family figure in this proud return?  [أَهْلِهِۦ · interpretation]
- 75:33/q04/p1 Family and prosperity feed his sense of being secure and right. — Islahi says wealth and children make him dismiss poorer admonishers; the conceited treat family as pride and good fortune, like the orchard owner who says his orchard will never perish (18:35). The Qur'an also depicts such people returning engrossed to family (83:31). Believers instead fear failing their family and incurring God's anger (52:26). [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 75:33/q05 What does يَتَمَطَّىٰ mean?  [يَتَمَطَّىٰٓ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the bodily extension is an affected walking gait or a lazy stretch; both can express arrogance.
- 75:33/q05/p1 He struts or swaggers proudly, with an affected gait. — Muqatil associates such walking with Banu al-Mughira; Zayd ibn Aslam and later transmitters with Banu Makhzum. Ibn Abbas calls it conceit or proud strutting. Al-Tabari, al-Farra, al-Zajjaj and others describe twisting or swaying the back; al-Kashshaf, al-Nasafi and Ibn Ashur note lengthened steps. Ibn Kathir adds gloating, insolence, laziness and lack of purpose. Bursevi says he boasts of denial; Biqai says he should have shown shame and humility. Al-Qurtubi explains the gait as stretching and twisting the back; al-muṭayṭāʾ is also described as swinging or extending the arms. [58 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,زيد بن أسلم,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(زيد بن أسلم); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,زيد بن أسلم); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، المفسرون,مقاتل، زيد بن أسلم); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل,مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة وزيد بن أسلم); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; STUDYQURAN]
- 75:33/q05/p2 He stretches himself lazily or heavily, a gesture of disregard and arrogance. — Al-Tha'labi says the word can describe stretching on rising from sleep. Al-Jishumi specifies stretching the belly from laziness. Al-Qurtubi stresses disregard; al-Majma' as quoted by Tabataba'i calls it lazy bodily stretching. Al-Samin says he may have begun stretching himself. [5 notes: THALABI(قيل); JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI(المجمع)]

## 75:33/q06 From what is يَتَمَطَّىٰ derived?  [يَتَمَطَّىٰٓ · meaning]
turns on: The proposed root: back, stretching with consonant substitution, an ultimate dāl in madd, or maṭw.
- 75:33/q06/p1 From al-maṭā, the back, which the strutter twists or arches. — Al-Farra, al-Zajjaj, al-Tabari, Zayd ibn Aslam and many others connect the word to the back and its proud twisting or swaying. Bursevi specifies an alif substituted for wāw. Al-Majma', quoted by Tabataba'i, traces even lazy stretching back to arching the back. Al-Qurtubi mentions thick basin water called al-muṭīṭa because it spreads or stretches. [20 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(زيد بن أسلم); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء، الزجاج); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI(المجمع); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:33/q06/p2 From yatamaṭṭaṭ or maṭṭ, stretching; a strutter extends his body or steps. — Ibn Qutayba compares the consonant change with yataẓannā from yataẓannan. Al-Tha'labi says an emphatic consonant becomes yāʾ; al-Nasafi and al-Samin say this avoids three like consonants, al-Samin adducing taẓannaytu, qaṣṣaytu aẓfārī and a verse with taqaḍḍī. Al-Razi compares taqaṣṣā from taqaṣṣaṣa. Al-Kashshaf, Ibn Ashur, Bursevi and Biqai explain the stretched stride; al-Samin says this root yields a sense close to the back derivation. Al-Qurtubi and al-Samin compare thick basin water whose name reflects spreading or extension. [21 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة، ابن قتيبة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(قيل)]
- 75:33/q06/p3 Ultimately from madd, extending, with the root consonant originally dāl. — Ibn Qutayba says maṭṭ and madd share a sense and pictures extending a hand while swaggering; Ibn al-Jawzi transmits this, and al-Jishumi also traces belly stretching to madd. Ibn al-Jawzi notes al-muṭayṭāʾ as the related gait. [4 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة)]
- 75:33/q06/p4 From maṭw, stretching oneself. — Al-Wahidi cites a report of Abu Bakr passing Bilal while Bilal stretched in the sun as usage for the sense. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 75:33/q07 How does يَتَمَطَّىٰ function grammatically?  [يَتَمَطَّىٰٓ · grammar]
- 75:33/q07/p1 It is a circumstantial expression describing the subject of ذَهَبَ. — Al-Nahhas calls it accusative as a circumstance; al-Samin and Bursevi identify its subject with the subject of ذَهَبَ. [3 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]

## 75:33/q08 Whose characteristic walk was this?  [يَتَمَطَّىٰٓ · referent]
turns on: The tribal identification reported with the gait; al-Wahidi's attribution to Muqatil differs from the surviving Muqatil note.
- 75:33/q08/p1 It was a gait associated with Banū Makhzūm. — Zayd ibn Aslam names Banū Makhzūm; al-Wahidi also attributes this identification to Muqatil. Ibn Atiyya says Abu Jahl often walked this way, and al-Alusi reports he belonged to that clan. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); THALABI(زيد بن أسلم); MAWARDI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل، زيد بن أسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 75:33/q08/p2 It was a gait associated with Banū al-Mughīra. — Muqatil says they strutted as they walked. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 75:33/q09 What physical conduct and judgment are attached to this gait?  [يَتَمَطَّىٰٓ · interpretation]
- 75:33/q09/p1 The strutter sways, extends or swings his arms, and the gait is censured. — Al-Tabari transmits a Prophetic criticism of al-muṭayṭāʾ as arm swinging and swaying. Al-Mawardi says the gait called al-maṭīṭā is prohibited. Al-Samin defines al-muṭayṭāʾ as strutting with extended arms; Bursevi says the hadith uses it for an extended gait and arms and explains al-ba's there as the severity of battle. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); MAWARDI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]

## 75:33/q10 What warning is reported about a community adopting this gait?  [يَتَمَطَّىٰٓ · reports]
turns on: The reported wording of the consequence, especially whether it says God sets people over one another or their strength turns inward; al-Alusi adds rule by the worst.
- 75:33/q10/p1 When they strut and Romans and Persians serve them, God sets some of them over others. — Al-Tha'labi reports this prediction, and Ibn Atiyya attributes it to the Prophet. [2 notes: THALABI(النبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي)]
- 75:33/q10/p2 Their strength turns against itself, or their conflict is among themselves. — Al-Kashshaf reports this form; al-Qurtubi gives it without attribution and explains al-muṭayṭāʾ as swaggering with extended arms. Al-Alusi adds that their worst will rule their best. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 75:33/q11 Who is the person described by the verse?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the passage singles out Abu Jahl or speaks generally about the human denier; al-Alusi observes the two can overlap.
- 75:33/q11/p1 Abū Jahl ibn Hishām. — Qatada, Mujahid and Ibn Zayd identify him; Ibn Atiyya says most interpreters take the whole passage this way and that Abu Jahl's habitual Banū Makhzūm gait nearly identifies him outright. Al-Alusi reports revelation about Abu Jahl and notes that the earlier 'Does man think' (75:3) was also said to concern him; he says the verbally distant coordination need not exclude one instance of a general human statement. Al-Tabrisi presents the identification as something said. [14 notes: FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور المتأولين); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 75:33/q11/p2 The human being mentioned earlier in the passage. — Tabataba'i relates the pronouns to the human of 75:3; belief would affirm the Qur'an's true call, and prayer is worship through salat, the central pillar of religion. Al-Alusi says a general human statement can still include Abu Jahl as an instance. [2 notes: TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 75:33/q12 What encounter illustrates the strutting gait?  [* · reports]
- 75:33/q12/p1 The Prophet compared a Qurashī man's walk with the strut described here. — Mujahid reports Muhammad seeing the man walk and saying he walked as the one in the verse did; the report does not name that passerby. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 75:33/q13 What does the swaggering return reveal about the denier?  [* · interpretation]
- 75:33/q13/p1 He is arrogant, self-satisfied and indifferent to the consequences of rejection. — Biqai says indulgence spoiled both practical and intellectual faculties until indifference became a boast; Ibn Kathir calls him gloating, insolent, conceited and idle. Bursevi says he boasts of denial and turning away; al-Alusi says he should fear divine anger and walk humbly. Islahi links the conceit to wealth and children, while Study Quran likens the strut to mockers of the Prophet who winked and called believers astray. Tabataba'i calls the homeward strut a metaphor for pride. Asad renders the going as arrogant. [16 notes: THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 75:33/q14 How does this return fit the denier's other faults and their consequences?  [* · interpretation]
- 75:33/q14/p1 The passage joins rejection of belief with failure in worship; the swagger is a prior habit or the outward climax of those faults. — Al-Qurtubi counts four faults: failure to believe the Messenger, failure to pray, denial and turning away from prayer; Qatada says the swagger predated the denial and is no fifth fault. Al-Razi says both unbelief and leaving prayer merit blame and punishment. Tabataba'i calls salat religion's central pillar. Islahi says prayer and spending in God's way were the two provisions missing for the exacting journey. Al-Samarra'i says this Qiyama figure openly denies, turns away and struts. [7 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 75:33/q15 How does his trip home contrast with the journey to his Lord in 75:30?  [* · links]
- 75:33/q15/p1 He freely went home before, but will be driven to his single Lord at an appointed time. — Al-Samarra'i says the worldly trip could be made at will and had many possible destinations, so the destination was not fronted; the trip to his Lord is forced, slave-like, and its fronted destination signals no choice. The daily journey needs no date, whereas the final journey occurs on the appointed day of departure. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:33/q16 How does the swaggering back relate to the threatened calamity?  [* · links]
- 75:33/q16/p1 The fāqira that breaks the spine fits the back he used to twist proudly. — Al-Samarra'i says this makes the punishment more fitting than a generic calamity. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:33/q17 How does this scene answer the earlier question about resurrection?  [* · links]
- 75:33/q17/p1 The man asks when resurrection will come yet prepares only for ruin. — Al-Tibi calls it a wondrous contrast with 75:6–7: the intervening passage answers him and the revelation instruction is a digression. Al-Qaffal allows the ensuing divine words to be a command for Muhammad to warn the enemy of an unbearable near misfortune. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); RAZI-FULL(القفال)]

## 75:33/q18 What other Quranic scenes are compared with this return?  [* · links]
- 75:33/q18/p1 Other passages show the arrogant or mocking returning happily to their people. — Ibn Kathir compares al-Mutaffifin 83:34 and al-Inshiqaq 84:13–15. Study Quran compares mockers who wink, return blithely to their people and call believers astray (83:30–32; 2:14; 84:13–14). Islahi points to 83:31 and contrasts believers' fear for their family in 52:26; he also cites the boastful orchard owner in 18:35. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 75:33/q19 How does this figure compare with those in Sura Muhammad?  [* · links]
- 75:33/q19/p1 The Qiyama figure deserves a stronger threat because his denial and strut are overt. — Al-Samarra'i says the people in Sura Muhammad (47:20) might outwardly believe and pray, whereas this figure denies, turns away and then struts. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
