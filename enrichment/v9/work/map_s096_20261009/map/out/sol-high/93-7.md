# 93:7: 9 questions

## 93:7/q01 What does wajadaka mean and how does it work here?  [وَوَجَدَكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether finding is encounter, perception, inward recognition, or divine selection.
- 93:7/q01/p1 God encountered or perceived the Prophet in a given condition. — Ibn al-Jawzī gives the root as encountering or sensing something previously unperceived, and notes usage for lost property, grief, anger, and financial means; Ibn Fāris cites poetry for anger. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the verb takes one object with ḍāllan as a circumstantial state; another lexical face is seeing. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن فارس,بعض المفسرين); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 93:7/q01/p2 The Prophet was the beloved sought by God in pre-eternity. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says finding follows seeking and makes the Prophet the sought beloved, an envoy through whom orphans, the lost, and the needy benefit. [2 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
- 93:7/q01/p3 Finding represents God's selection of a rare, valuable person. — Elmalılı prefers istifā, divine selection, as the rhetorical force repeated over the three verses. [1 notes: ELMALILI+]
- 93:7/q01/p4 Finding emphasizes the Prophet's inward state and reassurance. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ calls wajada a heart verb that foregrounds emotional reassurance. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 93:7/q02 What does ḍāllan mean in Arabic usage?  [ضَآلًّۭا · meaning]
turns on: The word's range includes losing a path, uncertainty, forgetfulness, love, obscurity, and religious deviation.
- 93:7/q02/p1 A lone desert tree can be called ḍālla because travelers find their way by it. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Jishumī, al-Rāzī, and al-Qurṭubī use this Arab idiom in their interpretation; al-Rāzī parallels the saying that wisdom is a believer's lost property. [7 notes: THALABI(بعض أهل الكلام); JISHUMI; RAZI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL(بعض المتكلمين)]
- 93:7/q02/p2 It can mean intense love or attachment. — Al-Sulamī cites 12:95; al-Thaʿlabī cites 12:8 and 12:95; al-Māwardī adds two poetry lines. Al-Qurṭubī and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ add Qurʾānic and poetic usage, including 12:30. [7 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); THALABI; MAWARDI; QURTUBI(الشاعر); QURTUBI-FULL; BINTSHATI]
- 93:7/q02/p3 It can mean forgetting or being unaware. — Thaʿlab cites the debt verse about one witness reminding another; al-Māwardī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ also invoke 2:282. Al-Qurṭubī compares 20:52 and 12:3. Ibn al-Jawzī's Zād quotes Thaʿlab that God guided the forgetful Prophet to remembrance. [8 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ثعلب); MAWARDI; TABATABAI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; QURTUBI; BINTSHATI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ثعلب)]
- 93:7/q02/p4 It can denote something hidden, submerged, or indistinguishable. — Al-Rāzī cites an idiom of submerged water; al-Qurṭubī uses 32:10 for being lost in earth at burial. Bursawī also records the sense absent. [4 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 93:7/q02/p5 It means losing a path or being uncertain how to reach a desired destination. — Ibn Qutayba includes confusion, departure from truth, or leaving a road. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes a wrong route from uncertainty and intends the latter. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ traces spatial usage to metaphorical and religious usage; Qurʾānic association with paths and 32:10 preserve the physical sense. [5 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن قتيبة); IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI]
- 93:7/q02/p6 It can denote any departure from a straight way, slight or grave, intentional or accidental. — Al-Rāghib's definition, cited by Bursawī and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ, permits different senses for prophets and unbelievers. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الراغب); BINTSHATI(الراغب)]
- 93:7/q02/p7 Related Qurʾānic metaphor calls a person without guidance dead and their guidance life. — Al-Dāmghānī cites 6:122, 35:22, 27:80, and 21:45 for mīt as one astray; this supplies an analogy for guidance language. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]

## 93:7/q03 What state does 'He found you ḍāllan' describe?  [وَوَجَدَكَ ضَآلًّۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether it describes missing revealed knowledge, searching, prior religious error, his people, obscurity, literal loss, or a specific unmet need.
- 93:7/q03/p1 He did not yet know revelation, prophetic signs, or religious law, and God taught him. — Al-Zajjāj and many later authors cite 42:52; others cite 12:3, 2:282, and 26:20. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Biqāʿī add inspiration and sound reasoning; Bursawī says reason alone cannot discover these rulings. Al-Biqāʿī calls lack of guidance the effect of lacking a teacher. Al-Ṭabrisī links teaching seekers in 93:10 to this favor. ## 93:7/q04 What is affirmed or denied about the Prophet's pre-prophetic belief and protection?  [ضَآلًّۭا · theology]
turns on: Whether unbelief was possible in reason, and what scripture and prophetic protection establish about this case.
- 93:7/q04/p1 The Prophet never practiced shirk or unbelief; ḍāllan cannot impute it to him. — Al-Kashshāf invokes prophetic protection and 12:38; al-Rāzī cites 53:2. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says he never worshipped an idol despite some shared customs. Al-Biqāʿī says sound reason and primordial nature led him to monotheism; al-ʿĀshūr cites his public life in 10:16 and 23:69. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports the majority rejection; Elmalılı stresses his reputation for trustworthiness. [34 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أصحابنا); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا,الجمهور); QURTUBI(الكلبي والسدي); QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي، السدي); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI(جمهور المفسرين); STUDYQURAN(others); RAZI(الجمهور من العلماء)+; RAZI(الكلبي,بعض الناس)-]
- 93:7/q04/p2 Prior unbelief is rationally possible in general, but transmitted evidence excludes it here. — Al-Wāḥidī's school requires transmission to decide and even allows rational possibility of later faith and prophethood or removal of prophethood; al-Rāzī's school cites 53:2 to settle this case. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أصحابنا); RAZI(أصحابنا); RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا)]
- 93:7/q04/p3 A reported Sunni interpretation links the verse to the Prophet's pre-Islamic arrangement of his daughter's marriage. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya transmits Thaʿlab's attribution without developing its argument; the full version repeats it. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA(أهل السنة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ثعلب عن أهل السنة)]
- 93:7/q04/p4 God protected the young Prophet from intentions to join Meccan entertainments. — ʿAlī's report says he twice intended to join nighttime wedding festivities, slept until sunrise each time, and never again intended evil before revelation. [3 notes: RAZI(علي عليه السلام); RAZI-FULL(علي)]
- 93:7/q04/p5 Prior unbelief is rationally impossible because it would repel people from the Prophet. — Al-Rāzī attributes this rationale to the Muʿtazila. [2 notes: RAZI(المعتزلة); RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة)]
- 93:7/q04/p6 The extent of pre-prophetic protection from grave indecent sins is disputed. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says strict scholars of his school exempt prophets from them, while the Muʿtazila deny such immunity because reason itself identifies grave wrongs. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]

## 93:7/q05 How does 'fa-hadā' describe God's guidance?  [فَهَدَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: The object and mode of guidance vary across the preceding readings.
- 93:7/q05/p1 God guided the Prophet by revelation to scripture, law, monotheism, and the straight way. — Gabriel's gloss is guidance to God's religion; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī, and others add inspiration and sound reflection. Ibn Kathīr quotes 42:52. Al-Ṭabrisī speaks of reason, proofs, and divine favor amid idolatry; Elmalılı emphasizes divine selection and teaching. [14 notes: MUQATIL(جبريل عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 93:7/q05/p2 The verb 'guided' omits its understood object for concision and verse ending rhyme. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Ālūsī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr derive the object from the second-person pronoun; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ argues the open wording extends to those guided through his mission. Al-Ālūsī objects that the following fa-clauses resist a proposed expansive-generosity account. [6 notes: THALABI; ALUSI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; ALUSI(قيل)-]
- 93:7/q05/p3 God guided others through the Prophet and his revealed law. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ makes him a means of guidance to the lost. Al-Sāmarrāʾī cites 42:52, saying the Prophet too guides to a straight path. [5 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 93:7/q05/p4 This is likely the earliest Qurʾānic use of hadā and resonates with scriptural guidance motifs. — Corpus Coranicum notes biblical and psalmic parallels for personal divine guidance and Syriac dbar as a technical parallel, while listing later Qurʾānic occurrences. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 93:7/q06 How is this clause joined grammatically to the preceding question?  [وَوَجَدَكَ فَهَدَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether coordination follows the affirmative implication or the earlier negated verb.
- 93:7/q06/p1 It coordinates with the affirmative sense implied by the rhetorical question. — Al-Burhān says a rhetorical question of report is affirmative, allowing an affirmative clause to join it, with parallels 94:1 and 105:2. Al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı allow this reading. [4 notes: BURHAN; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(الواحدي); ELMALILI]
- 93:7/q06/p2 It coordinates directly with the prior verb under lam's negation. — Al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı give this as the other syntactic possibility; Ibn ʿĀshūr describes the rhetorical question as expecting assent. [3 notes: ALUSI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 93:7/q07 What inward meaning is drawn from the Prophet's being lost and guided?  [ضَآلًّۭا فَهَدَىٰ · inward]
turns on: Which spiritual lack, bewilderment, or transition the inward reading describes.
- 93:7/q07/p1 He sought pure divine love and was guided to gnosis through nearness and affection. — Tustarī and al-Sulamī describe a cup of nearness, knowledge of God's love, and pre-eternal love; Jaʿfar says God made Himself known. Other voices describe longing and ecstatic bewilderment. Al-Najmiyya describes passage from annihilation to sobriety and complete knowledge. [18 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الجريري,بعضهم,جعفر); QUSHAYRI; THALABI(أبو بكر الوراق وغيره); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI(الحريري,جعفر الصادق,قيل); ALUSI-FULL(الحريري)]
- 93:7/q07/p2 He was unaware of his own worth, and God disclosed his noble rank. — Sahl and other voices in al-Sulamī, al-Thaʿlabī, and al-Ālūsī describe unveiling of status and the signs of prophethood. [7 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(بعضهم); THALABI(بسام بن عبد الله); QUSHAYRI; ALUSI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL]
- 93:7/q07/p3 He was bewildered in witnessing divine transcendence, then guided by God to God. — Al-Qushayrī and al-Najmiyya describe divine bewilderment followed by knowledge or sobriety; al-Wāsiṭī warns ordinary categories of misguidance and guidance do not apply to his supreme rank. [5 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي,بعضهم); QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); QASHANI]
- 93:7/q07/p4 The natural self lacked the path to gnosis and was redirected toward knowledge and law. — Tustarī and Sahl describe the soul's poverty; al-Thaʿlabī adds desire as its motive. [3 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل); THALABI(سهل)]
- 93:7/q07/p5 He moved from inference and conventional forms to direct recognition. — Bandār says divine recognition made proofs and witnesses unnecessary; Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says he was lost from convention, not gnosis. [3 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,بندار بن الحسين); THALABI(بندار بن الحسين)]
- 93:7/q07/p6 A person's search for their virtue ends when God reveals it. — Al-Sulamī transmits this more general inward application. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]

## 93:7/q08 How do these remembered favors connect to the sūra's reassurance and duties?  [* · links]
turns on: How past care supports promised future care and the later commands.
- 93:7/q08/p1 Past shelter, guidance, and enrichment reassure the Prophet that God has not abandoned him and will continue His care. — Al-Ṭabarī frames the passage as blessings; Qatāda calls these the Prophet's prior circumstances. Ibn ʿĀshūr says steady care from childhood proves the promise and warns opponents. Elmalılı says each later state surpasses the former; Qutb stresses guidance's comfort during the pause; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ, Islahi, and Study Quran describe the same movement through the sūra. [13 notes: TAB(قتادة); IBNKATHIR(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 93:7/q08/p2 The matching response is to shelter orphans, guide seekers, provide help, and remember God's enabling favor. — Al-Rāzī calls benefaction an unrepayable lifelong debt; service of orphans and petitioners depends on God's help. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Itqān connect teaching a seeker with the Prophet's being taught; the sūra closes with duties corresponding to favors. [9 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن); ITQAN(مجاهد وغيره); STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 93:7/q08/p3 The sequence of conditions and responses is a matched listing of divine favors. — Al-Itqān defines laf wa-nashr as listing items and matching details; al-Itqān also notes the metaphor of life as guidance. Ibn ʿĀshūr reads the three states as aid to someone in distress. [3 notes: ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 93:7/q08/p4 Sūrah 94 echoes the grammatical form and extends the blessings of Sūrah 93. — Study Quran says 94:1 follows 93:6's construction, implied in 93:7–8; Sūrah 93 addresses external needs and Sūrah 94 spiritual relief. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 93:7/q08/p5 The sūra and Psalm 13 share lament and reassurance motifs. — Corpus Coranicum identifies structural and thematic links with psalmic lament prayers. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 93:7/q09 What report has God remind the Prophet of these favors?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether a report recounts comparison with earlier prophets, the request's propriety, or another setting.
- 93:7/q09/p1 After the Prophet asked about favors given to earlier prophets, God recalled shelter, guidance, enrichment, and other gifts. — The reports vary: comparison with Abraham, Moses, David, or Solomon; opened breast, removed burden, raised renown, al-Baqara's closing verses, and divine friendship. He answers each reminder with praise or affirmation. Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Baghawī, al-Rāzī, al-Durr, Ibn Kathīr, al-Nashr, and Jishumī preserve variants. [13 notes: MUQATIL(جبريل عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNABIHATIM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); JISHUMI(ابن عباس عن النبي); BAGHAWI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASHR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 93:7/q09/p2 The request-for-gifts version is implausible because prophets ask only by permission. — Al-Qāḍī objects that a permitted request must be answered and a reproachful reply would be inappropriate; Jishumī calls Night Journey gift stories unsound. [3 notes: RAZI(القاضي); RAZI-FULL(القاضي); JISHUMI]
- 93:7/q09/p3 The pause served a benefit or a period of preparation. — Jishumī argues a proven pause must benefit believers; Ibn ʿĀshūr proposes respite and capacity for arduous revelation. A report has Gabriel say he descends only by divine command. [4 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 93:7/q09/p4 The revelation pause lasted twelve, fifteen, twenty-five, or forty days in differing reports. — Jishumī names Ibn Jurayj for twelve, Ibn ʿAbbās for fifteen, an unnamed report for twenty-five, and Muqātil for forty. Ibn ʿĀshūr suggests two pauses, forty then about twelve days, with this sūra the third revelation, to reconcile reports. [6 notes: JISHUMI(ابن جريج,ابن عباس,مقاتل); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 93:7/q09/p5 The Prophet was already noble despite orphanhood and modest Arab wealth. — Ibn ʿAmr explains the parents' loss and limited wealth while discussing Abū Ṭālib's poetry. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص)]
- 93:7/q09/p6 Takbīr at the end suits the sūra's account of divine favors. — Al-Nashr gives this rationale. [1 notes: NASHR]
- 93:7/q09/p7 Reciting al-Ḍuḥā brings intercession and rewards measured by orphans and petitioners. — Al-Bayḍāwī transmits the reported virtue. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 93:7/q09/p8 A pause of revelation was attributed to a puppy in the Prophet's home. — Jishumī reports Gabriel saying angels do not enter a house with a dog or image. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
