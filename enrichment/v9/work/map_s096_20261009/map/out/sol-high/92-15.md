# 92:15: 12 questions

## 92:15/q01 What does lā yaṣlāhā mean?  [لَا يَصْلَىٰهَآ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ṣalā names any exposure, permanent residence, or a particularly intense exposure.
- 92:15/q01/p1 To enter the Fire and feel its heat. — Al-Qurṭubī glosses ṣalā as experiencing the Fire's heat; his fuller wording states that the person does not experience it. Al-Wāḥidī similarly speaks of enduring its heat. [5 notes: QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI(الواحدي); RAZI-FULL(الواحدي)]
- 92:15/q01/p2 To abide with the Fire, enduring its heat permanently. — Al-Wāḥidī distinguishes the unbeliever's remaining from a sinful believer who may avoid the Fire or escape it. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says ṣaly means adhering to and remaining with it, a permanence decreed for unbelievers. [4 notes: RAZI(الواحدي); RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); TABATABAI]
- 92:15/q01/p3 To undergo intense or enveloping exposure to fire. — Ibn Kathīr says the fire surrounds the person on every side. Ibn al-Munīr reports language scholars' analogy of a sheep thrust between layers of embers: lesser exposure need not count as this ṣalā. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR; ALUSI(ابن المنير)]
- 92:15/q01/p4 To warm oneself by the Fire. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports this gloss from some exegetes. [1 notes: BINTSHATI(المفسرون)]

## 92:15/q02 How does the clause attach grammatically to the Fire just mentioned?  [لَا يَصْلَىٰهَآ إِلَّا ٱلْأَشْقَى · grammar]
- 92:15/q02/p1 It is either a second adjective of the Fire or a circumstantial clause about it. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives both parses; his fuller account links the clause to the particular Fire prepared for disbelievers. Al-Burhān observes that adjectival agreement ordinarily follows the preceding word's form, while agreement by sense is rarer. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN]

## 92:15/q03 Who can undergo the Fire under the restriction 'only'?  [لَا يَصْلَىٰهَآ إِلَّا ٱلْأَشْقَى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the exception is literal or rhetorical, what Fire and degree of exposure it names, and whether other texts qualify it.
- 92:15/q03/p1 Only unbelievers undergo lasting residence or the full heat; sinful believers may enter and later leave. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya limits the negation to eternal burning and rejects the Murjiʾite extension to every duration. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Elmalılı allow temporary entry; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 2:39. Al-Alūsī distinguishes more wretched unbelievers from sinners. Al-Alūsī says this also avoids making everyone less pious enter or suffer that extreme torment. [17 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA; RAZI(الواحدي); RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI+]
- 92:15/q03/p2 The restriction is rhetorical: exemplary opponents are singled out as if Fire and Paradise belonged to them alone. — Al-Zamakhsharī answers the objection that others also burn or are saved by reading the pair as emphatic contrast. Al-Kashf praises the reading; al-Alūsī notes its Muʿtazilite premise concerning major sinners but ultimately favors it if descriptive implication is accepted. Al-Samīn reports al-Zamakhsharī's preference. [12 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI(الزمخشري); QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري); NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI(الزمخشري); ALUSI+; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)+]
- 92:15/q03/p3 This is one particular Fire or level for deniers; other levels and punishments remain possible. — Abū Isḥāq al-Zajjāj distinguishes the Fire mentioned here from other ranks of Hell, including the hypocrites' lowest rank; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 2:24 as identifying a Fire prepared for unbelievers. Al-Qāḍī proposes an indefinite 'blaze' as one level; al-Rāzī counters that 70:15–16 can call all Hell a blaze. Al-Alūsī notes that the following restriction about the most pious complicates the one-level reading. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); QURTUBI(أبو إسحاق الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو إسحاق الزجاج); ALUSI(القاضي); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو إسحاق الزجاج); IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNASHUR(أبو إسحاق الزجاج)+; RAZI-FULL-]
- 92:15/q03/p4 Taken literally, the restriction exempts all but unbelievers from any burning or threatened punishment. — The Murjiʾites infer this from the wording, and Ibn ʿAṭiyya records their denial of burning of any duration. Al-Qāḍī objects that such a reading would even exempt an unbeliever who had not denied and turned away, and would embolden sinful believers. Al-Zajjāj and al-Nasafī reject the inference; Jishumī cites consensus, license to sin and the threat against misers and sinners. Al-Alūsī observes the apparent tension with 92:17 and says sound reports must be read alongside threats to major sinners. Al-Qāḍī's objection that it invites sin is recorded in both al-Rāzī note sets. [6 notes: RAZI(المرجئة); QURTUBI(أبو إسحاق الزجاج); IBNASHUR(أبو إسحاق الزجاج); ALUSI(أبو هريرة); JISHUMI-; IBNATIYYA(المرجئة)-; NASAFI-; QURTUBI(أبو إسحاق الزجاج)-; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو إسحاق الزجاج)-; IBNASHUR(أبو إسحاق الزجاج)-; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو إسحاق الزجاج)-; RAZI(القاضي)-]
- 92:15/q03/p5 Other punishment verses qualify the apparent general exclusion. — Al-Rāzī gives this as another answer. His rebuttal to the charge of encouraging sin also points to present disgrace, divine anger or withholding honor and reward, and punishment in forms besides Hell. Al-Qurṭubī and al-Zajjāj cite 4:48: forgiveness of lesser sins for whomever God wills implies that some non-polytheists may be punished. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI; QURTUBI(أبو إسحاق الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI+]
- 92:15/q03/p6 Only those fitting the stated traits approach it; all unbelievers fit denial and turning away. — Abū Muslim gives this as his first explanation; Jishumī prefers the first. Al-Rāzī replies to al-Qāḍī that every unbeliever denied the Prophet and turned away from examining his signs. Al-Qāḍī's objection supposes an unbeliever lacking both traits. [3 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); RAZI-FULL(القاضي); JISHUMI+; RAZI(القاضي)-]
- 92:15/q03/p7 The most wretched is the person most entitled to Hell, without excluding others from it. — Abū Muslim offers this as a third explanation, and al-Qāḍī similarly treats the whole blaze as Hell while reading the exception as superior entitlement. Al-Rāzī rejects it as an unsupported departure from the apparent sense. [3 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); RAZI(القاضي); RAZI-; RAZI-FULL-]
- 92:15/q03/p8 The Fire is named for its majority or its intended occupants, without excluding others. — Abū Muslim offers this as a second way to understand 'only,' alongside his other explanations; he invokes 66:6. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم)]
- 92:15/q03/p9 An ellipsis adds sinners like the most wretched to those who enter. — Jishumī reports the supplied wording: 'the most wretched, and whoever is like him among the sinners.' [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 92:15/q03/p10 The address is contextual: none of the warned audience enters this Fire except those among them being addressed as deniers. — Ibn ʿĀshūr paraphrases 'none of you enters this fire except you'; he says early Islam presented unbelievers and wholehearted pious converts as the two relevant groups, before major sins among converts complicated that contrast. The next verse gives the converse. [2 notes: IBNASHUR]
- 92:15/q03/p11 Some unbelievers enter despite not having committed each specified act. — Jishumī reports this further view concerning denial, miserliness and turning away, but says the first explanation is best. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 92:15/q03/p12 The exception singles out the uniquely most miserable servant. — Qutb calls the one who undergoes it the most wretched of all servants and says no greater misery is imaginable; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the idea of one uniquely most wretched human because eternal afterlife misery exceeds temporary worldly suffering. Al-Baghawī states the bare restriction that only the most wretched enters. [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; BAGHAWI; TABATABAI-]

## 92:15/q04 What does al-ashqā mean as a form?  [ٱلْأَشْقَى · meaning]
turns on: Whether afʿal has comparative force or serves as an emphatic name for the ordinary class.
- 92:15/q04/p1 The wretched person or class, with afʿal functioning without a comparison. — Abū ʿUbayda and several exegetes equate al-ashqā with al-shaqī. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says Arabic can use afʿal for an emphatic active participle and cites Ṭarafa's awḥad; al-Qurṭubī's semanticists compare akbar/kabīr and ahwan/hayyin and cite 30:27. Al-Samīn invokes nonexclusive burning; al-Alūsī says this still leaves problems concerning unbelievers, poor pious people, children and the mentally ill, and cites Ṭarafa's wāḥid. [14 notes: NAHHAS(أبو عبيدة); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; QURTUBI(بعض أهل المعاني); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI(أبو عبيدة); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(طرفة)]
- 92:15/q04/p2 It is an elative or intensive form: the most wretched, paired with the most pious. — Jishumī calls ashqā and atqā elatives from shaqī and taqī. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls them intensifying degrees; al-Samīn reports al-Zamakhsharī's preference for comparative force. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the form need not identify one uniquely worst human, since permanent afterlife misery surpasses worldly suffering. [5 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)+; TABATABAI+]

## 92:15/q05 Who is al-ashqā?  [ٱلْأَشْقَى · referent]
turns on: Whether the description names a class, a divinely known person, or a reported historical individual.
- 92:15/q05/p1 The unbeliever who denies and turns away. — Al-Ṭabarī identifies denial of God's signs; al-Baghawī and al-Nasafī say denial of the Messenger and departure from faith. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Wāḥidī invoke 92:16. Al-Alūsī contrasts the unbeliever with a sinful believer; Elmalılı specifies denial of guidance and truth. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the deniers knew they rejected the Messenger and Qurʾān; Iṣlāḥī says conclusive warning left rejecters no excuse. [18 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-QT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; RAZI; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI+]
- 92:15/q05/p2 Umayya ibn Khalaf, or Umayya and similar deniers. — Ibn ʿAbbās is reported to connect revelation with Umayya and peers who denied Muḥammad and earlier prophets; al-Ḍaḥḥāk's transmission names those like him. Some paired identifications allow Umayya instead of Abū Jahl; al-Itqān names Umayya alone. These reports vary over whether peers are included. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); RAZI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI(ابن عباس عن الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك، ابن عباس); ITQAN; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)]
- 92:15/q05/p3 Abū Jahl. — Several reports give Abū Jahl as an alternative to Umayya, paired with Abū Bakr as the most pious; al-Nasafī names Abū Jahl directly. [8 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)]
- 92:15/q05/p4 The polytheist. — Al-Ḥasan names the polytheist; one transmission adds 'who denied and turned away' through Muʿādh rather than al-Ḥasan. Al-Wāḥidī points to 92:16, and al-Jawzī gives the same identification. Al-Qāshānī adds lack of readiness, shirk, denial of God and stubborn turning away. [5 notes: TAB(الحسن); TAB-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QASHANI]
- 92:15/q05/p5 Whoever God already knows to be wretched. — Al-Farrāʾ gives this identification; al-Naḥḥās, al-Wāḥidī and al-Qurṭubī transmit it. [5 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); QURTUBI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء)]
- 92:15/q05/p6 One who does neither an act of obedience nor avoidance of sin for God's sake. — Abū Umāma and a report from Abū Hurayra give this definition in sayings attributed to the Prophet; the latter is cited by Murjiʾites, while Sunnis read it with other punishment texts. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR(أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(أبو أمامة); DURR-FULL(أبو أمامة); ALUSI(أبو هريرة)]
- 92:15/q05/p7 The owner of the palm who refused to give it up. — Al-Thaʿlabī associates the miser who denied reward and was eased toward Hell with the palm owner; a Medinan version has him take fallen dates from a poor family's children. The accounts differ about the buyer's payment and destination of the tree. [3 notes: THALABI(عطاء); STUDYQURAN(several commentators)]
- 92:15/q05/p8 A Khazraji hypocrite who died persisting in hypocrisy. — Al-Qurṭubī's fuller contextual account makes this identification, alongside the report of an Anṣārī palm owner and Abū al-Daḥdāḥ. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 92:15/q05/p9 The worst among the people of Hell, while other sinners may enter. — Al-Farrāʾ offers this as an alternative in al-Naḥḥās; since those worst inhabitants are unbelievers, sinful believers can still enter. [1 notes: NAHHAS(الفراء)]

## 92:15/q06 What marks the denier who is called most wretched?  [ٱلْأَشْقَى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether denial denotes disbelief in revelation, rejection of the Messenger, or a failure of obedience.
- 92:15/q06/p1 Denial of the Messenger and his message, and turning away from him or the Qurʾān. — Al-Māwardī reports this as a second reading; Abū Umāma specifies Muḥammad's message, and Ibn ʿĀshūr says the hearers knew they turned from the Messenger and Qurʾān. Ibn ʿAbbās' report adds earlier prophets. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(أبو أمامة); DURR(أبو أمامة); DURR-FULL(أبو أمامة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); RAZI(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:15/q06/p2 Denial of God's signs or Book and turning from obedience. — Al-Ṭabarī says he does not affirm God's signs. Qatāda says he lies about God's Book and turns from God's obedience; the transmission from al-Ḥasan has Muʿādh add the next verse's phrase. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the relative clause makes actions the cause of the outcome. [7 notes: TAB(الحسن); TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:15/q06/p3 Takdhīb can mean failing in obedience without an explicit verbal denial. — Some Arab grammarians liken it to saying fighters 'lied' by retreating; they cite an Arab expression for fighters who did not retreat and compare the Event with no falsehood in 56:2. [2 notes: TAB(بعض أهل العربية)]

## 92:15/q07 Why single out al-ashqā against al-atqā?  [ٱلْأَشْقَى · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the labels denote generic classes, intensive degrees, or hyperbolically singled-out representatives.
- 92:15/q07/p1 The two eminent opponents are portrayed as if Fire and Paradise were reserved for them. — Al-Zamakhsharī uses hyperbole to heighten the contrast, with Abū Jahl or Umayya opposite Abū Bakr in some identifications; al-Samīn says he retains the elative force. Al-Alūsī favors this if descriptive implication is admitted, though he notes the premise about punishment of major sinners. Al-Biqāʿī connects the emphatic distinction with unequal abodes. [13 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI(الزمخشري); QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري); NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI(الزمخشري); BIQAI; ALUSI+; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)+]
- 92:15/q07/p2 They name the opposed moral classes, the wretched and the pious. — Abū ʿUbayda's generic reading identifies unbelievers and believers rather than only extreme individuals; al-Samīn reports this because Fire and deliverance are not limited to extreme cases. Iṣlāḥī says the contrast is rejection versus belief, rather than degrees of wickedness or piety. Corpus Coranicum says the second moral opposition is named directly. [4 notes: NASAFI(أبو عبيدة); SAMIN-DURR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 92:15/q07/p3 Their intensive forms and relative clauses make deeds the causes of opposed outcomes. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says ashqā and atqā express degree, their relative clauses point to actions causing the verdicts, and their forms make near-paronomasia or near-rhyme; Jishumī calls them elatives and says tajannub pictures the pious far from Hell on Paradise's side. [6 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; JISHUMI]

## 92:15/q08 What reports explain refusing Paradise or entering the Fire?  [لَا يَصْلَىٰهَآ إِلَّا ٱلْأَشْقَى · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in whether they describe refusal of Paradise, flight from God, or the acts defining the Fire's entrant.
- 92:15/q08/p1 Everyone enters Paradise except the one who refuses by denial and turning away. — Abū Hurayra's report connects the refusal with 92:16; some versions say the Prophet recited that verse when asked who refuses. Another version defines refusal as disobedience to the Prophet and acceptance as obedience. [8 notes: TAB(أبو هريرة); TAB-FULL(أبو هريرة); THALABI(أبو هريرة); QURTUBI-FULL(مكحول، أبو هريرة); DURR(أبو هريرة); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة,أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 92:15/q08/p2 Only a wretched person enters the Fire: one who neither obeys God nor avoids disobedience for His sake. — Abū Umāma and Abū Hurayra transmit versions of this saying; al-Alūsī notes Murjiʾite use and the Sunni reading together with punishment texts concerning major sinners. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR(أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(أبو أمامة); DURR-FULL(أبو أمامة); ALUSI(أبو هريرة)]
- 92:15/q08/p3 Everyone in the community enters Paradise except one who bolts or flees from God like a bad camel. — Abū Umāma explains that such a person denied Muḥammad's message and turned away; the reported wording varies between fleeing, rebelling and bolting, and one version explicitly attributes the simile to the Prophet. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو أمامة); DURR(أبو أمامة,أبو أمامة الباهلي); DURR-FULL(أبو أمامة,أبو أمامة الباهلي عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 92:15/q09 What palm-tree account is connected with the wretched and pious figures?  [ٱلْأَشْقَى · reports]
turns on: Whether the Medinan palm account is an occasion of revelation for a possibly early Makkan sūrah.
- 92:15/q09/p1 A palm owner refused the Prophet's offered palm in Paradise; another man bought and donated his tree. — Al-Thaʿlabī's report names Abū al-Daḥdāḥ as buyer and says the Prophet gave the tree to the neighbor; he treats the owner as miser and denier. Study Quran's version says the owner snatched dates from poor children and the buyer paid forty palms. Al-Qurṭubī reports an Anṣārī owner and maps ease to Paradise, hardship to Hell and death to loss of wealth. The accounts vary in these details. [4 notes: THALABI(عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(several commentators)]
- 92:15/q09/p2 The palm account is unlikely to be the occasion if the sūrah is early Makkan. — Study Quran instead reads the verses as a general condemnation of those who rejected Muḥammad. Kur'an Yolu also says a reported Abū Bakr occasion for verses 5–19 leaves the message and rulings general. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]

## 92:15/q10 How does this warning fit the sūrah's surrounding contrasts?  [* · links]
turns on: How the last stanza mirrors and explains the sūrah's earlier contrast.
- 92:15/q10/p1 It completes a paired account of opposed behavior and recompense. — Corpus Coranicum describes two antitheses across 5–13 and 14–21: the final stanza reverses saved/damned order, links the blessed sections by ittaqā and atqā, and places reward or warning before conduct where the earlier account places conduct first. The blaze in 14 makes the enigmatic threat in 10 concrete, comparable to disclosure in 104:4–7 and 102:3–8. Iṣlāḥī says 15–21 explains traits and reward of those cast in or shielded; al-Biqāʿī says the restriction keeps the abodes distinct. [7 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI]
- 92:15/q10/p2 The warning addresses persistent Quraysh deniers through prophetic revelation. — Iṣlāḥī says Quraysh's persistent deniers face the blaze while those spending to attain purity are protected. Kur'an Yolu says God shows the right path and warns through revelation and prophets about Hell as the wrong path's consequence. Ibn ʿĀshūr reads the contextual address as directed to the deniers themselves. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR]
- 92:15/q10/p3 Later lines elaborate piety, selfless giving and promised satisfaction. — Al-Māwardī reports Qatāda's reading that no favor is repaid, but one seeks God's face and reward; Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Masʿūd apply it to Abū Bakr's freeing of Bilāl. Satisfaction may mean abundant gifts or contentment that supports obedience. Kur'an Yolu reports an Abū Bakr occasion while affirming general application. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود,قتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 92:15/q10/p4 The term al-ashqā echoes other early Makkan sūras. — Corpus Coranicum compares its use with 91:12 and 87:11, described as Group II early Makkan sūras. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 92:15/q11 What reaction to the Fire warning is reported?  [* · reports]
- 92:15/q11/p1 ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz wept and could not continue reciting it at Maghrib. — Mālik's account, transmitted through al-Qurṭubī by Ibn ʿĀshūr, says he switched to another sūrah. [2 notes: IBNASHUR(مالك); IBNASHUR-FULL(مالك)]

## 92:15/q12 What reading observations accompany the neighboring fire warning?  [* · readings]
turns on: Different transmitted vocalizations and route practice in the nearby passage.
- 92:15/q12/p1 Abū al-Zubayr reads the preceding verb with an extra tāʾ: tatalaẓẓā. — Al-Kashshāf transmits the variant. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو الزبير)]
- 92:15/q12/p2 Some transmitted routes open non-final forms, while others read them between vowels. — Al-Nashr says Abū ʿAmr opens listed non-verse-final words on relevant routes, but follows his usual between-vowels reading for Mūsā where transmitted. Al-Azraq's Warsh routes through Ibn Ghalbūn and others open them; al-Taysīr and related routes read between vowels because such words are written with yāʾ, including fa-ammā man ṭaghā. [2 notes: NASHR(أبو عمرو,الأزرق عن ورش)]
