## 96:1/q01 What does iqraʾ mean?  [ٱقْرَأْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether it denotes voicing text, attentive reading, public proclamation, or gathering words.
- 96:1/q01/p1 Read or recite specified words, from memory or writing; ordinary Arabic recitation. — Rāzī cites Qurʾānic use of unqualified recitation for the Qurʾān (75:18; 17:106); his full account also invokes 73:20. ELMALILI: Recitation is audibly voicing connected words in sequence, whether from memory or a page and aloud or quietly; silent study and recollection are figurative senses. ELMALILI: A book need not already be written to be a book, and reading does not require writing; the fullest recitation is from memory. ELMALILI: A prophetic report says reading from a page is rewarded because it supports accurate retention and memorization. ELMALILI: The Prophet's own recitation was the fullest kind, from memory by divine instruction, whether for himself, prayer, teaching others, or dictation. IBNASHUR: Qara'a means to pronounce specified speech, whether it is written or memorized by heart. IBNASHUR-FULL: Iqra literally commands reading aloud of specified written or memorized words; the grammatical discussion parallels the command in al-Nahl.; cites 16:98 TABATABAI: Qaraʾa can mean mentally combining written words, uttering them aloud, or reading them to another person; in the last sense it is also tilāwa.; cites 98:2 BINTSHATI (الطبري): Al-Tabari did not allegorize the command because its ordinary Arabic meaning, reciting from a written or unwritten text, was clear; Arabs knew rabb as owner and object of worship. CORPUSCORANICUM (Nöldeke and Schwally): Nöldeke and Schwally object that qaraʾa elsewhere in the Quran means recite from a text or memory, supporting the usual translation recite in the name of your Lord.; cites 16:98, 17:93, 69:19, 73:20, 87:6 ISLAHI-TADABBUR: Iqraʾ can be a teacher's instruction to read, but it can also direct someone to read or recite aloud to others. ISLAHI-TADABBUR: Qur'an 7:204 and 17:45 show that iqraʾ can mean reciting the Qur'an aloud to an audience; the usage is indicated by these verses.; cites 7:204, 17:45 [13 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI(الطبري); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; CORPUSCORANICUM(Nöldeke and Schwally)+]

BIQAI-FULL: “In the name of your Lord” means beginning with the basmala, seeking the benefactor's aid, or mentioning His name; in the last reading, the name stands for His exalted attributes.
