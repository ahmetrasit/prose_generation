# 26:21: 19 questions

## 26:21/q01 What does 'I fled' mean?  [فَفَرَرْتُ · meaning]
- 26:21/q01/p1 It means escaping or fleeing away. — Al-Suddi glosses it as escape (Yahya). Jishumi equates flight and escape. Ibn al-Jawzi gives the related mafar (destination), al-farr (fugitives), and farartu ʿan al-amr (avoiding something); exegetes include escape from death or killing, citing 33:16. He also records the related iftarr for exposed teeth in laughter and farāra for a horse's revealing eye. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]

## 26:21/q02 Why did Moses flee from them?  [فَفَرَرْتُ مِنكُمْ خِفْتُكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether fear concerns retaliation or harm to Moses, the people's religious conduct, or impending divine punishment.
- 26:21/q02/p1 He feared Pharaoh and his people would kill or harm him after he killed the Egyptian. — Maqatil, reported by Wahidi, says they would kill him for the man he had killed. Jishumi specifies the Egyptian he struck; Biqai adds Pharaoh's force and inciters and calls the blow accidental and the victim's blood legally permissible. Bursevi says he also feared harm and punishment beyond his offense. Razi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi, Tabataba'i and Kuran Yolu cite the council's plot and warning in 28:20; Tabataba'i also cites his fearful departure in 28:21. Qurtubi cites 28:21. Qushayri says fear for his life is a literal, blameless reason when resistance is beyond one's power. [20 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 26:21/q02/p2 He feared their reaction to his religious dissent and aid to the Israelites. — Jishumi reports this as an alternative explanation of the danger. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 26:21/q02/p3 He feared divine punishment would descend on them for their idolatry or Pharaoh's claim to godhood. — Sulami reports fear of punishment descending. Qushayri relates this to their idolatry or Pharaoh's claim to be their only god at 28:38. [2 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); QUSHAYRI]
- 26:21/q02/p4 He feared their boldness toward God, dishonor of messengers, and lack of signs of divine favor. — Ibn Ata gives this inward explanation in Sulami. [1 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]

## 26:21/q03 Where did Moses flee?  [فَفَرَرْتُ · reports]
- 26:21/q03/p1 He fled from Egypt to Midian. — The sources connect the journey to fear of Pharaoh's people. Kuran Yolu locates Midian north of the Gulf of Aqaba; Study Quran cites 28:21–22. [12 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 26:21/q04 Who is included in the plural 'you' from whom Moses fled and whom he feared?  [مِنكُمْ خِفْتُكُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural includes Pharaoh's circle or is a plural form addressed to one person.
- 26:21/q04/p1 Pharaoh together with his officials and people who plotted against Moses. — Al-Kashshaf as reported by Razi contrasts these plurals with singular references to Pharaoh's favor and enslavement. Abu Hayyan contrasts them with singular 'you claim' and 'you enslaved'; Alusi and Ibn Ashur connect them to the council's plot in 28:20. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 26:21/q04/p2 The plural can address Pharaoh as one person. — Burhan lists this among cases of plural wording for a singular addressee. [2 notes: BURHAN]

## 26:21/q05 Why was Moses not liable to retaliation for the Egyptian's death?  [خِفْتُكُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether the defense concerns intent, the victim's legal status, or the absence of an established retaliation law.
- 26:21/q05/p1 The killing was mistaken rather than intentional. — Jishumi reports this ground; Biqai likewise says the deed was accidental. [2 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); BIQAI-FULL]
- 26:21/q05/p2 The Egyptian's blood was legally permissible. — Jishumi reports this ground; Biqai also describes the victim's blood as permissible. [2 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); BIQAI-FULL]
- 26:21/q05/p3 Retaliation had not yet been instituted, so Moses feared oppression rather than lawful retribution. — Jishumi reports this legal explanation. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]

## 26:21/q06 How is لَمَّا read and construed here?  [لَمَّا · readings]
turns on: Whether lammā is the doubled form marking an occasion or the light form made from prepositional lām and verbal-noun mā.
- 26:21/q06/p1 The common reading has a doubled mīm and links the flight to the occasion of fear. — Abu Hayyan and Samin report Sibawayh's account of it as a particle requiring a consequence and al-Farisi's account of it as an adverb meaning 'when.' [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR]
- 26:21/q06/p2 A kasra on the lām and light mīm mean 'because I feared you.' — Ibn al-Jawzi attributes the reading to Asim al-Jahdari, al-Dahhak and Ibn Yamar. Abu Hayyan reports it from Hamza and glosses it as 'they frighten you'; Samin and Alusi report Hamza's reading and analyze lām as a preposition and mā as a verbal noun marker. Samin compares Hamza's reading of 3:81. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عاصم الجحدري والضحاك وابن يعمر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(حمزة); SAMIN-DURR(حمزة); ALUSI-FULL(حمزة)]

## 26:21/q07 What does 'bestowed' mean here?  [فَوَهَبَ · meaning]
- 26:21/q07/p1 It means giving or donating a gift. — Jishumi equates gift, connection, and donation. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 26:21/q08 What makes a gift legally valid?  [فَوَهَبَ · law]
- 26:21/q08/p1 Offer and acceptance form a legally valid gift contract. — Jishumi states this is agreed by consensus. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 26:21/q09 Must a recipient take possession for a gift to become valid?  [فَوَهَبَ · law]
turns on: Whether delivery and possession complete the contract's validity.
- 26:21/q09/p1 Yes; taking possession and delivery are required. — Jishumi attributes this to most jurists. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أكثر الفقهاء)]
- 26:21/q09/p2 No; the gift is valid without possession. — Jishumi attributes this to al-Hadi. [1 notes: JISHUMI(الهادي)]

## 26:21/q10 Can an undivided share of divisible property be gifted?  [فَوَهَبَ · law]
turns on: Whether an undivided share can be the object of a valid gift when division is possible.
- 26:21/q10/p1 No. — Jishumi attributes this view to Iraqi jurists. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أهل العراق)]
- 26:21/q10/p2 Yes. — Jishumi attributes this view to al-Shafiʿi. [1 notes: JISHUMI(الشافعي)]

## 26:21/q11 What does God's bestowal imply about the source of Moses' knowledge or mission?  [فَوَهَبَ رَبِّى حُكْمًۭا · theology]
- 26:21/q11/p1 Knowledge, judgment and the mission are God's gifts rather than Moses' independent acquisition. — Jishumi says God creates necessary knowledge; acquired knowledge follows His proofs, reason and prompting; revealed knowledge follows revelation. Razi says the verb 'bestowed' expressly shows God created the judgment in Moses. Tabataba'i says both judgment and messengerhood were gifts without acquisition. [3 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 26:21/q12 How is حُكْمًا read?  [حُكْمًۭا · readings]
turns on: The vowel on the kāf, including Samin's account of vowel assimilation.
- 26:21/q12/p1 The majority read ḥukman with sukūn on the kāf. — Abu Hayyan records the majority form. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عيسى)]
- 26:21/q12/p2 ʿĪsā reads ḥukuman with ḍamma on the kāf. — Ibn Atiyya describes ḍamma on both ḥāʾ and kāf; Samin explains the second vowel as assimilation. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عيسى); SAMIN-DURR(عيسى)]

## 26:21/q13 What is the ḥukm God gave Moses?  [حُكْمًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ḥukm names prophethood itself, a distinct cognitive or practical gift, the Torah's law, divine grace, or transcendent wisdom; the following mention of messengership and the Torah's later date are explicit tests.
- 26:21/q13/p1 Prophethood, sometimes understood together with its wisdom and knowledge. — Al-Suddi is cited by Tabari, Ibn Abi Hatim, Wahidi, Qurtubi, Durr and Alusi; Ibn Abbas by Wahidi. Ibn Atiyya includes prophetic wisdom; Nasafi and Kuran Yolu include knowledge too. Abu Hayyan and Bursevi's Fath al-Rahman say messengerhood then adds a higher rank. Ibn Ashur places the beginning when God spoke to Moses. [18 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، والسدي); JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي وغيره); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI(فتح الرحمن); ALUSI-FULL(السدي); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL-; TABATABAI-; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)-]
- 26:21/q13/p2 Knowledge, understanding, sound judgment, intellect or divinely given guidance. — Maqatil is cited for knowledge and understanding by Wahidi, Baghawi and Ibn al-Jawzi; Jishumi prefers this because messengerhood is paired separately. Razi similarly prefers knowledge over prophethood because the next clause names the latter; he includes reason, judgment and knowledge of divine unity as needed for a messenger. Tabrisi includes knowing wisdom's demands, Torah, lawful and unlawful matters, and rulings. Biqai specifies practical knowledge fit for wise judges. Tabataba'i specifies insight into reality applied skillfully to action; he compares stages of soundness in childhood and mature reasoning and cites 3:79, 6:89 and 45:16 for distinguishing judgment from prophethood. Islahi connects it to divine guidance needed beyond uprightness and intellect, citing 11:17 and 26:83. Study Quran also mentions decision-making power, intelligence and understanding; Alusi says some restrict the knowledge to what distinguishes prophethood. [26 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(وقيل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(السدي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); JISHUMI(قيل)+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 26:21/q13/p3 The Torah or its divine legal judgments. — Zajjaj identifies the Torah because it contains God's legal judgments; Qurtubi cites him. Tabrisi includes the Torah among the contents of knowledge, while Tabataba'i sees it as a later culmination of growing judgment after Pharaoh drowned, citing 5:43. Wahidi objects that the Torah was given only after Pharaoh drowned. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ; TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); TABATABAI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)-]
- 26:21/q13/p4 Divine graces (luṭf). — Razi reports the Muʿtazilite explanation but calls it very weak because grace is given to all without withholding, so its specification here requires a reason. [0 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة)-]
- 26:21/q13/p5 Wisdom beyond demonstration, acquisition and reason. — Qashani gives this inward account and says God made Moses a messenger with that wisdom. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 26:21/q14 When was Moses given ḥukm and made a messenger?  [فَوَهَبَ حُكْمًۭا وَجَعَلَنِى · reports]
turns on: Whether ḥukm is a single grant after flight, specifically on return, or one of several growing grants.
- 26:21/q14/p1 After the flight and the earlier killing, with the commission following God's address and signs. — Jishumi says the striking and flight preceded prophethood. Alusi likewise says the killing preceded Moses' mission. Ibn Ashur says prophethood began when God spoke and the commission followed the sign, divine choice and command to go to Pharaoh, citing 7:144 and 20:24. Razi, Ibn Ashur and Islahi stress the change from his prior state. [7 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:21/q14/p2 God gave knowledge and wisdom when Moses returned from Midian, then sent him to Pharaoh. — Bursevi specifies the return from Midian. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 26:21/q14/p3 Ḥukm came in increasing degrees before the killing, after flight, and after Pharaoh drowned. — Tabataba'i reconciles the earlier grant in 28:14 with this verse; the Torah later culminates the sequence, and he compares childhood soundness with mature reason and management. He cites 5:43 for the Torah's judgments. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 26:21/q15 What does being made one of the messengers add to the gift of ḥukm?  [وَجَعَلَنِى مِنَ ٱلْمُرْسَلِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase merely identifies Moses' prophetic company, marks a rank beyond prophethood, or stresses the source and standing of his commission.
- 26:21/q15/p1 It says Moses was included among the prophets and sent as God's messenger. — Jishumi and Tabrisi gloss the group as the prophets. Baghawi and Yahya state the appointment; Kuran Yolu adds that Moses and Aaron were sent to Pharaoh and his people. Ibn Ashur links the mission to God's command in 20:24. [11 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 26:21/q15/p2 It marks the further, higher rank of messengerhood beyond prophethood. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan say not every prophet is a messenger; Bursevi's Fath al-Rahman and Alusi give the same reason for mentioning messengerhood after a ḥukm understood as prophethood. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(فتح الرحمن); ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:21/q15/p3 It presents messengerhood as a divine gift and an established divine practice. — Tabataba'i says it was bestowed without acquisition, undercutting Pharaoh's inference from prior familiarity. Alusi says placing Moses among the messengers magnifies the office and shows the mission is no novelty. [3 notes: TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 26:21/q16 How does Moses' account of flight and divine appointment answer Pharaoh?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the emphasis falls on Moses' newly granted authority or on Pharaoh's culpability and claimed favor.
- 26:21/q16/p1 His present God-given wisdom and mission supersede the earlier killing and establish his authority now. — Baydawi says wisdom and messengership answer Pharaoh's attempt to discredit prophecy through the killing. Razi contrasts God's benefit with Pharaoh's wrongdoing. Ibn Kathir says obedience now brings safety and defiance ruin. Ibn Ashur calls the sequence purposeful because present state and final outcome matter more than the past. Islahi says Pharaoh must hear Moses without dwelling on the past. Tabataba'i says prior familiarity predicts only acquired status, whereas this mission is divine. Samarrai links the reply to 26:18–19. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 26:21/q16/p2 The flight exposes Pharaoh's treatment of Moses as harmful rather than a favor. — Razi says Pharaoh's threat caused Moses' fear and flight, leaving Pharaoh no favor regarding the killing. Baydawi says Moses does not deny the true upbringing but treats it as a calamity stemming from Israelite enslavement; Samarrai says he overturns Pharaoh's boast by naming that enslavement. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 26:21/q17 How do the notes assess Moses' earlier killing?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the note concerns intent, moral recognition, or frank acknowledgment of the past act.
- 26:21/q17/p1 It was unintended: his blow was meant as discipline. — Alusi gives the intended purpose of the blow. Biqai calls the deed accidental; Jishumi reports mistake as one reason retaliation was unwarranted. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 26:21/q17/p2 Moses realized the killing was a grievous sin. — Asad links this realization to 28:15–16 and his note on 28:15. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 26:21/q17/p3 Moses openly acknowledges the killing as part of his past. — Islahi says guidance and appointment changed his status after this acknowledged act. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:21/q18 What guidance is drawn from Moses' fear and flight?  [فَفَرَرْتُ خِفْتُكُمْ · inward]
turns on: Whether the flight illustrates prudent protection, the spiritual consequences of fearing God, or a divinely induced retreat.
- 26:21/q18/p1 One may flee danger when unable to resist, and a believer should seek a place where religion is safe. — Sulami reports that flight from what cannot be endured is the conduct of messengers; Abu Bakr al-Warraq specifies protection from desire, innovation and misguidance. Qushayri says literal fear for one's life and flight without power to resist is not blameworthy. [3 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر الوراق,بعضهم); QUSHAYRI]
- 26:21/q18/p2 Fear of God makes all things fear a person; lacking it makes a person fear everything. — Sulami transmits this maxim from an unnamed authority. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 26:21/q18/p3 God can cast fear on someone to draw him away from people and unveil special secrets. — Bursevi reports this from some Sufi masters as a path to a high station. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الكبار)]

## 26:21/q19 What inward condition does the surrounding exchange depict?  [* · inward]
- 26:21/q19/p1 The veiled self cannot know the Real, prophetic wisdom or religious law and resists revelation. — Qashani describes it as asserting itself, resisting obedience and seeking to overpower revelation. [1 notes: QASHANI]
