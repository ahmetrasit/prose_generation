# 33:43: 28 questions

## 33:43/q01 How does this verse answer the preceding commands to remember and glorify God?  [هُوَ ٱلَّذِى يُصَلِّى عَلَيْكُمْ · links]
turns on: The notes give different links to the surrounding passage.
- 33:43/q01/p1 God and the angels bless those who remember Him; His response rewards and encourages their remembrance. — Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Durr say remembrance has no fixed limit and applies in every condition, unlike other duties. Rāzī says divine mercy despite human forgetfulness encourages remembrance. Ibn Kathīr and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite 2:152 and the divine report that God remembers the servant in a better assembly. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the prayer a like but greater reward for morning and evening remembrance. Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts 9:67 with 2:152 and identifies remembrance with mercy and light. Kashshāf and Nasafī connect mercy with God's commands to remembrance, prayer, obedience and good. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 33:43/q01/p2 The passage turns from the believers' promised victory to address and honor the Prophet as their leader. — Al-Biqāʿī relates the turn to the preceding admonition and promised victory. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 33:43/q02 Who receives the prayer announced here?  [عَلَيْكُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the occasion restricts the scope of the address.
- 33:43/q02/p1 It was revealed about the Helpers, the Anṣār. — Muqātil says God forgives them and orders angels to ask forgiveness for them. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 33:43/q02/p2 Its mercy extends to all believers, beyond the first hearers. — Rāzī says the closing term believers is glad tidings beyond the original audience. Al-Ālūsī says it affirms mercy to all believers. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 33:43/q03 What occasion is reported for the revelation of 33:43?  [هُوَ ٱلَّذِى يُصَلِّى عَلَيْكُمْ وَمَلَٰٓئِكَتُهُۥ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in setting and in who voiced the question.
- 33:43/q03/p1 After 33:56 announced prayer upon the Prophet, Abū Bakr or the Companions asked whether believers shared that honor, and this verse answered. — The reports vary in speaker: Anas or Mujāhid relate Abū Bakr's remark; Ibn ʿAbbās relates a question by Emigrants and Helpers, and al-Wāḥidī mentions their congratulations. Kashshāf says the consequent wording was 'their greeting'; Muqātil calls this the revealed answer. All link the answer to the honor announced in 33:56. [12 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(أنس بن مالك); WAHIDI-ASBAB(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(أنس); BAGHAWI-FULL(أنس); KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو بكر); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); SUYUTI-LUBAB(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(أبو بكر)]
- 33:43/q03/p2 One Miʿrāj account says the verse through 'merciful' was revealed directly to the Prophet at the two-bows distance. — Bursevī's account says God told Muḥammad to wait because his Lord was praying; Muḥammad objected that God needs no prayer, and God answered that His mercy preceded anger. It says Gabriel was absent there. [2 notes: BURSEVI]
- 33:43/q03/p3 A second Miʿrāj version has Gabriel stop the Prophet in the seventh heaven and say the Lord is praying. — The answer in this version calls God the Holy Lord of angels and Spirit and says His mercy preceded anger. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 33:43/q04 What does God's ṣalāt upon believers mean?  [يُصَلِّى عَلَيْكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: The sources gloss divine ṣalāt by mercy, forgiveness, praise, honor, blessing, public good mention, general care, proximity or inward gifts.
- 33:43/q04/p1 God shows believers mercy, compassion and blessing. — Yaḥyā reports Ibn ʿAbbās; al-Ḥasan's Israelite report says mercy precedes wrath. Ibn Qutayba cites verses pairing prayer and mercy; Wāḥidī cites 2:157, as does Ibn Kathīr. Kashshāf and Nasafī appeal to the closing 'merciful.' Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds blessing and beautiful praise; Ibn ʿĀshūr says praise and directing mercy in both worlds. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it special mercy yielding lasting felicity. Biqāʿī describes compassionate favor. Jishumī reports divine mercy as the first gift in a fivefold account for one who establishes prayer, and as God's part in a fourfold account of ṣalāt. [38 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن عباس,الحسن); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; NAHHAS; THALABI(الأخفش); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون وأهل المعاني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(سعيد بن جبير والحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(الشافعي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(غيره); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,بعضهم); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 33:43/q04/p2 God forgives believers. — Al-Suddī says forgiveness follows obedience. Al-Kalbī says that when God is pleased with a servant He announces forgiveness to the angels and asks them to seek it too. Ibn Qutayba and the paired glosses include mercy with forgiveness. [12 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,المفسرون وأهل المعاني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI(سعيد بن جبير والحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); BURSEVI]
- 33:43/q04/p3 God praises believers, including before the angels. — Abū al-ʿĀliya's explanation, transmitted by al-Bukhārī via al-Rabīʿ according to Ibn Kathīr, specifies praise before the angels. Ibn Kathīr says praise and mercy do not conflict; Ibn ʿĀshūr pairs good mention with mercy. A gloss reported by Jishumī says God praises the believers while the angels pray for them. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبو العالية); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(أبو العالية); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو العالية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو العالية); IBNASHUR-FULL; JISHUMI(الأخفش)]
- 33:43/q04/p4 God honors or esteems believers. — The first three transmit Sufyān's interpretation; Biqāʿī says divine favor and angelic prayer together display the recipients' honor. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(سفيان); TABRISI(سفيان); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سفيان); BIQAI-FULL]
- 33:43/q04/p5 God blesses believers. — Abū ʿUbayda cites al-Aʿshā's use of the verb. Ibn Qutayba, al-Akhfash, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī combine blessing with mercy or forgiveness. [6 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI(الأخفش); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 33:43/q04/p6 God spreads a good reputation or beautiful mention of the servant among people. — Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī mark this as another explanation; Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes beautiful praise among divine ṣalāt. The same gloss reported by Jishumī says God spreads the believers' good reputation. [4 notes: THALABI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; JISHUMI(الأخفش)]
- 33:43/q04/p7 God cares for the believers' good and welfare. — Al-Bayḍāwī includes making honor visible; Abū Ḥayyān says God wills their good through mercy, and al-Ālūsī prefers the general sense on evidence of the closing mercy clause. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أصحابنا); ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 33:43/q04/p8 God draws servants near. — Al-Qushayrī starts from prayer as supplication and gives proximity as God's act. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 33:43/q04/p9 God's prayer marks or creates inward faith, truth, purification and contentment. — Abū Bakr ibn Ṭāhir lists faith's light, success, truth, removal of misleading desires and false aims, and contentment in decree. Al-Baqlī describes pre-eternal election in knowledge and love, forgiveness, and appointment of angels while the servant attends to God. Qāshānī describes disclosures of acts and attributes in answer to glorification, since the Essence overwhelms creatures; he cites Gabriel's inability to advance in BURSEVI's parallel account only indirectly. Al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya says eternal prayer precedes and enables remembrance and love. [5 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر); QASHANI; BURSEVI(أبو بكر بن طاهر,عرائس البقلي)]

## 33:43/q05 What do the angels' ṣalāt and their role mean?  [وَمَلَٰٓئِكَتُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether angelic prayer is described by its petition, its welfare-seeking aim, or its effect.
- 33:43/q05/p1 They supplicate for believers, especially asking God for forgiveness. — The notes vary between general supplication, prayer for mercy, and specific forgiveness; al-Wāḥidī says God commands the request, and al-Kalbī recounts God's announcement of forgiveness. Qushayrī distinguishes sinners' forgiveness and the obedient's beneficence. Biqāʿī adds protection from sins and afflictions and carrying remembrance between God and prophets. Ibn Kathīr cites 40:7–9; Islahi cites 40:7 and 42:5. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 42:5 and 10:3: their intercession needs God's permission. Al-Nasafī quotes their prayer for blessing. Jishumī reports that angelic forgiveness is a gift to one who establishes prayer and is the angels' part in a fourfold account of ṣalāt; another gloss contrasts their prayer with God's praise of believers. [45 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن عباس,السدي); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; NAHHAS; THALABI(الأخفش); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو العالية,مقاتل بن حيان); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الكلبي,المقاتلان); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس وأبو العالية,قيل); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو العالية,مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,مقاتل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,بعضهم); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; JISHUMI(الأخفش,قيل)]
- 33:43/q05/p2 Their prayer is intercession for believers. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explains that supplication is intercession, authorized by God according to 42:5 and 10:3. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 33:43/q05/p3 They attend to believers' welfare. — Al-Bayḍāwī pairs forgiveness with concern for benefit; Abū Ḥayyān says angelic petitions and divine mercy share the intended good. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 33:43/q05/p4 They are treated as bringing mercy because their prayers are answered. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī infer agency from answered prayer; al-Bayḍāwī calls supplication mercy for that reason. Abū Ḥayyān reports the view. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 33:43/q05/p5 Angels watch over believers as well as praying for them. — Study Quran connects this care to believers' remembrance and prayers in 33:41–42. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 33:43/q06 What does the single ṣalāt verb mean when predicated of both God and angels?  [يُصَلِّى عَلَيْكُمْ وَمَلَٰٓئِكَتُهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: The dispute concerns a single common sense, multiple senses in one word, and possible lexical derivations.
- 33:43/q06/p1 It conveys a common concern, inclination or care for the recipients' good, realized differently by God and angels. — Rāzī says mercy and seeking forgiveness imply one shared concern. Al-Bayḍāwī derives shared welfare and honor figuratively from ṣalw. Al-Ālūsī's school uses a general figurative sense because it disallows two literal senses; he allows metaphor from supplication or metonymy from care, and prefers it because the close mentions mercy. Abū Ḥayyān favors shared intended good over al-Zamakhsharī's agency account. Ibn ʿĀshūr says each agent receives the suited implication of one common sense, making homonymy or a general metaphor unnecessary; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it turning or inclination. [11 notes: NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أصحابنا); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 33:43/q06/p2 The one word bears both senses at once: divine mercy and angelic asking forgiveness. — Rāzī finds the reported Shāfiʿī view plausible, including literal and figurative senses together. Al-Ālūsī reports those permitting two senses; al-Samīn says coordination is available on such a theory. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم,من يجوز استعمال اللفظ في معنيين); SAMIN-DURR; RAZI-FULL(الشافعي)+; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 33:43/q06/p3 Prayer fundamentally means supplication; God's act is mercy, while angels ask mercy or forgiveness. — Language scholars cited by al-Naḥḥās use al-Aʿshā's line and its preceding plea that God spare his father pain and illness. Al-Jishumī says Islamic usage transferred supplication to specified ritual acts. Al-Ālūsī describes a metaphor from supplication through willing good. Ibn ʿĀshūr combines supplication and good mention. [5 notes: NAHHAS(أهل اللغة); QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 33:43/q06/p4 Its base is mercy, and its uses belong to one category. — Al-Naḥḥās reports Muḥammad ibn Yazīd's alternative derivation. [1 notes: NAHHAS(محمد بن يزيد)]
- 33:43/q06/p5 Its mercy sense extends metaphorically from bending in bowing and prostration to tender inclination toward someone. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī describe bodily bending, then kindly turning, then compassion; al-Bayḍāwī offers mercy and spiritual inclination as an alternative. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 33:43/q06/p6 A split-subject analysis assigns mercy to God and forgiveness to angels without giving one word two literal senses. — Al-Ālūsī reports this agent-based solution and likens it to the split-subject parse; al-Samīn allows coordination under shared meaning or metaphor. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); SAMIN-DURR]
- 33:43/q06/p7 A mercy gloss can preserve the verb's distinctive construction and tender sense despite lexical objections. — Al-Ālūsī answers objectors who say raḥima is transitive while ṣallā takes ʿalā, that their gloss would license 'he had mercy upon him,' and that 2:157 coordinates blessings and mercy. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(المعترضون)-]
- 33:43/q06/p8 The verb can mean to call out or proclaim. — Jishumī reports al-Akhfash's gloss of yuṣallī as 'calls out.' [1 notes: JISHUMI(الأخفش)]

## 33:43/q07 Why are the angels' prayers attributed to God?  [هُوَ ٱلَّذِى يُصَلِّى عَلَيْكُمْ وَمَلَٰٓئِكَتُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: The explanations identify divine causation and appointment.
- 33:43/q07/p1 God creates and commands the angels' action. — Al-Biqāʿī explains the attribution by divine creation and command. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 33:43/q07/p2 God appoints angels to care and seek forgiveness while believers attend to Him. — Al-Baqlī links their appointment to God's pre-eternal election and love. [1 notes: BURSEVI(عرائس البقلي)]

## 33:43/q08 How is 'and His angels' joined grammatically?  [وَمَلَٰٓئِكَتُهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the noun shares the verb's subject position or begins a clause with an omitted predicate.
- 33:43/q08/p1 It is coordinated with the implicit subject of yuṣallī. — Bursevī and al-Ālūsī say the intervening phrase makes a separate emphatic pronoun unnecessary. Al-Samīn says this requires shared meaning or metaphor because the agents' prayers differ. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 33:43/q08/p2 It is a new subject with an omitted predicate, 'they pray.' — Al-Samīn reports the possibility under the same shared-sense or metaphor theories. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 33:43/q09 What does the initial 'He' do?  [هُوَ · rhetoric]
- 33:43/q09/p1 It reinforces and establishes the claim, especially the prayer's purpose of bringing believers into light. — Ibn ʿĀshūr emphasizes the pronoun before the verbal predicate. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 33:43/q10 What does the imperfect form yuṣallī suggest?  [يُصَلِّى · rhetoric]
- 33:43/q10/p1 Prayer recurs as remembrance and glorification recur, or is renewed for other acts and signs of faith. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reads recurrence from the imperfect. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 33:43/q11 Why is God's prayer presented through a relative clause?  [ٱلَّذِى يُصَلِّى عَلَيْكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 33:43/q11/p1 It presents God's prayer as known to believers already. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says they may infer it from God as the source of all good, or learn it from revelation or the Prophet. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 33:43/q12 Where does the purpose clause attach and what work does it do?  [لِيُخْرِجَكُم · grammar]
- 33:43/q12/p1 It attaches to 'prays': divine and angelic care aims to bring believers from darkness into light. — Al-Ālūsī says prayer and mercy supply the purpose. Ibn ʿĀshūr makes it an immediate reward while believers remember and glorify God. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the purpose is rescue from disbelief into faith; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says special mercy yields lasting afterlife success. Study Quran cites parallels at 2:257, 5:16, 14:1, 14:5, 57:9 and 65:11. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 33:43/q13 Who actually brings believers out of darkness?  [لِيُخْرِجَكُم · theology]
turns on: The notes distinguish the direct guiding agent from the means of guidance.
- 33:43/q13/p1 God alone does; angels' prayer assists but angels cannot guide. — Bursevī argues from the singular verb and God's status as the true guide. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 33:43/q13/p2 God guides by mercy, revelation, prophets and the angels' prayers. — Rāzī says guidance operates through mercy; al-Baghawī and Ibn Kathīr add angels' prayers. Kur'an Yolu says prophets and revelation guide people toward the life God approves. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 33:43/q14 What do the darknesses and light signify?  [ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ ٱلنُّورِ · interpretation]
turns on: The imagery is read as conversion, moral reform, sustained guidance, inward transformation or passage into the next world.
- 33:43/q14/p1 Disbelief, shirk or idolatry gives way to faith, Islam or monotheism. — Al-Muqātil and al-Daḥḥāk identify shirk or disbelief and faith. Wāḥidī specifies monotheism and affirmation of mercy; al-Wajīz adds certainty and Islam. Al-Bayḍāwī includes sin and obedience. Study Quran reports Aj and JJ. Kur'an Yolu pairs unbelief with distress and faith with light. [20 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); NAHHAS(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(مقاتل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Aj, JJ); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 33:43/q14/p2 Error or misguidance gives way to guidance. — Al-Ḥasan says God also protects from misguidance that had not occurred, like gratitude for an averted danger. Qatāda and Ibn Zayd give the plain gloss. Al-Ṭabrisī attributes guidance to God's favors; Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Qurṭubī say the verse can confirm existing guidance. Jishumī transmits Ibn Zayd's gloss of the transition as movement from misguidance to guidance. [14 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد); TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); JISHUMI(ابن زيد)]
- 33:43/q14/p3 Ignorance of God becomes knowledge of Him, guidance and certainty. — Al-Ṭabrisī says ignorance leads to Hell and knowledge to Paradise. Al-Biqāʿī says knowledge produces guidance. Ibn Kathīr adds certainty, and Bursevī includes spiritual knowledge. An explanation reported by Jishumī compares ignorance to darkness and knowledge, which guides, to light. [7 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطبرسي); BURSEVI; JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 33:43/q14/p4 Sin and disobedience become obedience and righteous practice. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī connect this to God's commands to obedience. Al-Biqāʿī says it is practiced faith. Kur'an Yolu includes failure to worship in darkness and righteous deeds in light. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(Aj); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 33:43/q14/p5 The Fire gives way to Paradise. — Al-Māwardī reports it as a third interpretation, followed by later transmitters. An explanation reported by Jishumī attributes rescue from the darkness of Hell to the light of Paradise to God's kindness, guidance, and command. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL(الماوردي); JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 33:43/q14/p6 The graves give way to resurrection. — Abū Ḥayyān transmits this among other reports. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-]
- 33:43/q14/p7 Heedlessness or forgetfulness becomes remembrance and awareness. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects this to 33:41: abundant remembrance brings mercy and light. Study Quran reports Aj's similar interpretation. [2 notes: TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
- 33:43/q14/p8 Inward darkness of self, false aims and separation becomes insight, witnessing, unity and spiritual realization. — Qushayrī lists rescue through connection, from self-planning to witnessing decree, from self to insight, and from separation to realized unity; another inward reading says God prevents shirk and confirms signs of faith. Qāshānī describes angelic aid and disclosure of Names, moving from lower-self acts and qualities toward divine acts, qualities and Essence. Bursevī adds law's illumination and realization of spiritual reality. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; BURSEVI]
- 33:43/q14/p9 The passage can mean preserving and increasing faith already received. — Al-Ḥasan likens protection from unrealized error to thanking God for danger averted. Al-Qurṭubī says it confirms existing guidance; al-Kāshifī and Ibn ʿĀshūr reason that the believers were already guided when prayer reached them. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 33:43/q15 How is the word 'darknesses' read and derived?  [ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ · readings]
- 33:43/q15/p1 Its vowel can shift from ḍamma to fatḥa for ease, with two morphological explanations. — Al-Kisāʾī derives the form through ẓulum and ẓulma; another explanation deletes the heavy ḍamma. [1 notes: NAHHAS(الكسائي)]

## 33:43/q16 What is the lexical sense of nūr here?  [ٱلنُّورِ · meaning]
- 33:43/q16/p1 It is radiance, described as a body with a particular color. — Al-Jishumī contrasts munīr, which illuminates from itself or causes illumination. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 33:43/q17 What does God's mercy toward the believers entail?  [وَكَانَ بِٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ رَحِيمًا · interpretation]
turns on: The notes emphasize distinct effects and illustrations of mercy.
- 33:43/q17/p1 He guides, forgives, protects, rewards and raises believers by His special mercy. — Al-Ṭabarī says obedient believers are not punished. Al-Ṭabrisī says faith causes the great gift of reward. Ibn al-Jawzī says raḥīm is mercy specifically for believers. Al-Bayḍāwī includes welfare, higher rank and angels' care. Qāshānī says perfections accord with readiness. Ibn Kathīr describes guidance in this world away from callers to unbelief and innovation, and protection at the greatest terror with angels' news of Paradise and rescue from Hell. Al-Biqāʿī adds sincerity and higher ranks in Gardens. Bursevī says mercy precedes creation of angels and believers, does not vary with circumstances, and cites a Persian couplet comparing it with a sea unmuddied by floodwater. Ibn ʿĀshūr says mercy exceeds ṣalāt, including all good through words, deeds and kindness. Jishumī reports a five-gift account promising one who establishes prayer God's mercy and great reward in the afterlife, alongside angelic forgiveness and a greeting of peace. [15 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 33:43/q17/p2 He has mercy more profound than a mother's care for her child. — Ibn Kathīr cites Anas's report of a mother rescuing her child and the Prophet's assurance God would not cast His beloved into Fire; he judges its chain to meet the two Ṣaḥīḥs' criteria though none of the six books included it. He also cites ʿUmar's report in al-Bukhārī that God is more merciful than a mother. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 33:43/q17/p3 God approves faith and righteous deeds while allowing freedom to disbelieve and sin. — Kur'an Yolu ties God's mercy to the life He approves. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 33:43/q18 Why does the closing clause call God merciful?  [وَكَانَ بِٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ رَحِيمًا · rhetoric]
turns on: The clause is described as reassurance or as confirmation of the prayer's meaning.
- 33:43/q18/p1 It reassures believers and gives them glad tidings. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī call it comfort; Rāzī stresses all believers are included. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 33:43/q18/p2 It confirms that God's prayer means mercy and affirms the preceding explanation. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī use the close as evidence for ṣalāt as mercy; al-Ālūsī says it affirms the prior statement; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it concluding amplification. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 33:43/q19 Why say 'the believers' instead of 'you'?  [بِٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ · rhetoric]
- 33:43/q19/p1 Naming their faith identifies it as the cause of special mercy and praises them. — Al-Ṭabrisī says faith is the cause of reward; al-Ālūsī says the wording either praises them by naming faith or signals the cause; Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts the explicit noun with a pronoun. [3 notes: TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 33:43/q20 What is the grammar and force of kāna raḥīman?  [وَكَانَ بِٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ رَحِيمًا · grammar]
turns on: The notes address grammatical function and rhetorical force.
- 33:43/q20/p1 Raḥīman is kāna's predicate and its unspoken subject is God. — Al-Jishumī gives the iʿrāb. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 33:43/q20/p2 Kāna establishes God's mercy as a verified, familiar attribute. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says other verses attest the established quality. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 33:43/q21 Why does raḥīm govern the believers with bāʾ?  [بِٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ رَحِيمًا · grammar]
- 33:43/q21/p1 It follows the government of its near-synonym raʾūf. — Al-Zarkashī says Arabic ordinarily uses raḥimtu bihi but raʾuftu bihi; the meanings' closeness permits parallel bāʾ, as in 9:117. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 33:43/q22 What do reports say when people ask whether or how God prays?  [يُصَلِّى · reports]
turns on: The reported settings differ between Moses, the Prophet's answer, and two Miʿrāj versions.
- 33:43/q22/p1 Israelites asked Moses; God answered that His prayer is mercy, which precedes or extinguishes wrath and encompasses all. — The accounts vary in whether mercy precedes anger, extinguishes anger, or encompasses everything; Yaḥyā adds that without it they would perish. Al-Naḥḥās's account says they asked the Prophet about his Lord's prayer, though he cites the Israelite question. [7 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن); NAHHAS; THALABI(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(السدي)]
- 33:43/q22/p2 Asked how God prays on servants, the Prophet replied with glorification and 'My mercy preceded My wrath.' — Both transmit a report from an unnamed group. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); QURTUBI-FULL(فرقة)]
- 33:43/q22/p3 A Miʿrāj account has Muḥammad ask why the self-sufficient Lord prays and God answer with mercy preceding anger. — Bursevī places the exchange at two-bows distance, without Gabriel, and connects it to revelation of this verse. [2 notes: BURSEVI]
- 33:43/q22/p4 A second Miʿrāj version has Gabriel announce God's prayer in the seventh heaven and an answer naming His holiness and prior mercy. — This version names the Holy Lord of angels and Spirit. [2 notes: BURSEVI]
- 33:43/q22/p5 The Prophet first proclaimed divine transcendence because the questioner imagined an improper bodily sense of God's prayer. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī explain the opening glorification as a guard against an unsuitable conception. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 33:43/q23 Whose speech is the Prophet's reported answer about divine prayer?  [يُصَلِّى · reports]
turns on: Whether the glorifying words are divine speech or the Prophet's preface.
- 33:43/q23/p1 The entire phrase, including 'Most Holy, Most Pure,' is God's speech and His prayer on servants. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī report this parsing of the answer. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 33:43/q23/p2 The glorification is Muḥammad's preface, and 'My mercy preceded My wrath' is God's explanation. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya explains the Prophet's preface as declaring divine transcendence against the questioner's improper understanding. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 33:43/q24 Does this verse support reading the prior morning and evening commands as prayer times?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the preceding time phrases concern prayer times and whether a report from an early period can be supported by a Medinan verse.
- 33:43/q24/p1 The prayer wording supports reading morning and evening as prayer times. — Al-Biqāʿī prefers that reading on the basis of God's prayer upon believers. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL+]
- 33:43/q24/p2 The verse is Medinan, so it cannot establish an early two-prayer prescription at the day's edges. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya also calls the report of that early prescription weak. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 33:43/q24/p3 In the preceding command, al-aṣīl runs from ʿAṣr to night. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives the time's extent. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 33:43/q25 What broader distinction or intertext does this verse illustrate?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes supply several distinct cross-text and surah links.
- 33:43/q25/p1 The believers' receipt of God's and angels' prayer is a great blessing and evidence of this community's excellence. — Al-Qurṭubī cites 3:110. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 33:43/q25/p2 The angels' prayer here and in 33:56 is seeking forgiveness. — Ibn al-Jawzī compares both verses; Islahi uses 33:56 for the distinction between divine mercy and angelic petition. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 33:43/q25/p3 The darkness-to-light motif has Quranic and scriptural parallels. — Study Quran cites 2:257, 5:16, 14:1, 14:5, 57:9, 65:11; compares Isaiah 42:16 and 1 Peter 2:9; and points to 6:1 and 24:35. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 33:43/q25/p4 The connection between this prayer and the preceding passage is raised without an answer. — Al-Jishumī poses the question. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 33:43/q26 What does Gabriel's reported limit show in the inward reading?  [* · inward]
- 33:43/q26/p1 Direct disclosure of the Essence overwhelms creatures; even Gabriel could not advance a fingertip without burning. — Qāshānī uses Gabriel's reported words to distinguish disclosure of acts and attributes from the overwhelming Essence. [2 notes: QASHANI(جبريل عليه السلام)]

## 33:43/q27 What five gifts does one who establishes prayer receive according to the reported account?  [* · interpretation]
- 33:43/q27/p1 Divine mercy, angelic forgiveness, God's mercy toward the worshipper, a greeting of peace, and great reward in the afterlife. — Jishumī reports this fivefold account for one who establishes prayer. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]

## 33:43/q28 How does the reported fourfold account distinguish prayer by God, angels, the Prophet, and believers?  [يُصَلِّى · meaning]
- 33:43/q28/p1 God's prayer is mercy; angels seek forgiveness; the Prophet intercedes; believers offer greetings. — Jishumī reports this fourfold attribution of ṣalāt. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]
