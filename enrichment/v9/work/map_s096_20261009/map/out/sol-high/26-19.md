# 26:19: 20 questions

## 26:19/q01 What deed does Pharaoh recall?  [وَفَعَلْتَ فَعْلَتَكَ · referent]
- 26:19/q01/p1 Moses killed an Egyptian or Copt connected to Pharaoh. — Mujāhid and Qatāda say he killed a person; Ibn ʿAbbās and other exegetes specify a Copt. Ibn Isḥāq, Ibn Zayd, and al-Biqāʿī say Pharaoh counted the victim among his own people or followers. Al-Nasafī and al-Būrsawī call him Pharaoh's baker; al-Būrsawī names him Fatun, while Ibn ʿĀshūr reports the baker identification anonymously. Al-Qurṭubī says Pharaoh raised the killing against Moses' claim to be sent by God. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the attached action noun can designate the result of the act. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares 20:78. KuranYolu says the death followed Moses' unintended blow while defending an Israelite, citing 28:16. [28 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة,مجاهد,محمد بن إسحاق); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس والمفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 26:19/q02 What does the doubled verb pattern imply in Arabic usage?  [فَعَلْتَ · grammar]
- 26:19/q02/p1 Doubling the middle radical can mark repeated or abundant action, while the light form remains valid. — Sībawayh distinguishes repeated wounding, eating, killing, straightening, roaming, and circling; al-Farazdaq's doors opened and closed repeatedly illustrate it. He cites مفتحة at 38:50 and وفجرنا الأرض عيونا, with the note mentioning 78:14, for abundance. He also distinguishes generic action nouns قعدة and جلسة from more specific derivatives and compares صوف/صوفة and ريح/رائحة. [7 notes: SIBAWAYH(الفرزدق)]

## 26:19/q03 Which derived verb forms can take direct objects?  [فَعَلْتَ · grammar]
- 26:19/q03/p1 Many derived patterns are intransitive, although some extended forms are attested with objects. — Sībawayh excludes انفعلته, explains انطلقت as potentially intransitive rather than passive, and gives احرنجمته, افعنلل, افعلل, اطمأننت, and اشمأززت as further restrictions. He reports object-taking افعوعل and افعول in احلولي دماثا and اعلوطته; Ḥumayd al-Hilālī's verse attests the first. He adds صعررته, a fawʿala form such as مكوكبة, and اعروريت مني أمرا قبيحا. He says transitive derivatives are fewer, comparing basic verbs and their relation to agents and objects. [9 notes: SIBAWAYH(حميد الهلالي)]

## 26:19/q04 Why can an imperfect verb remain indicative in a noun's syntactic slot?  [فَعَلْتَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether nominal case government can extend to verbs.
- 26:19/q04/p1 The verb occupies a nominal position, but nominal governors do not impose noun case on verbs. — Sībawayh illustrates subject and predicate slots with يقول زيد ذاك and زيد يقول ذاك, adjectival and complement slots with رجل يقول and حسبته ينطلق, and an initial slot after هلا. In ائتني بعدما تفرغ, ما plus the verb amounts to الفراغ; أن زيدا جاء and لو أن زيدا جاء are noun-like, though لو مجيء زيد is unused. He treats كدت أفعل, عسى يفعل, قد جعل يقول, صار يقول, أخذ يقول, and the exclamation ما أحسن زيدا similarly; kāda and ʿasā avoid nominal complements because of their affinity with أن, and كدت أن أفعل is poetic only. [11 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 26:19/q04/p2 The verb is indicative solely because of initial position, ابتداء. — Sībawayh rejects this because it would incorrectly predict accusative or genitive verbs in nominal slots where nouns receive those cases. [0 notes: SIBAWAYH-]

## 26:19/q05 How is فَعْلَتَكَ read, and what does each vowel convey?  [فَعْلَتَكَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the action noun points to one occurrence or the manner and kind of action; al-Ṭabarī also invokes the regional readers' consensus.
- 26:19/q05/p1 The common fatḥa reading names one occurrence: Moses' particular killing. — Al-Ṭabarī says the intended single act rules out kasra; al-Zajjāj and al-Qurṭubī prefer this more common reading. Al-Jishumī reports the common reading, al-Farrāʾ is cited by Ibn al-Jawzī, and Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn ʿĀshūr, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, and al-Ālūsī give the same one-act analysis. Sībawayh's general rule forms a single occurrence with feminine tāʾ, as in إعطاءة, إخراجة, احترازة, انطلاقة, استخراجة, اقعنساسة, اغديدانة, تعذيبة, ترويحة, تقلبة, تغافلة, مقاتلة, مراماة, تجاورة, and تركة واحدة. [15 notes: JISHUMI(قراءة العامة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(الشعبي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SIBAWAYH; QURTUBI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 26:19/q05/p2 Al-Shaʿbī's kasra reading names the deed's manner, state, kind, or characteristic blow. — Al-Ṭabarī reports the reading but objects that it conflicts with the regional readers. Ibn Jinnī compares posture, sitting, walking, eating, and source alternation نشدته نشدا. Al-Zajjāj compares sitting once with a way of sitting; al-Jishumī says a manner or instance. Al-Ṭabrisī compares ركبة and جلسة and says the pattern may also be a verbal noun. Ibn al-Jawzī cites al-Farrāʾ's possibility of a repeated type; al-Qurṭubī adds known habit and a poet's description of a woman's gait. Al-Bayḍāwī interprets the kasra as one killing by a blow, while al-Samīn names the blow. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(الشعبي); THALABI(الشعبي); JISHUMI(الشعبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الشعبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الشاعر); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشعبي); ALUSI-FULL(الشعبي); TAB-FULL(الشعبي)-]

## 26:19/q06 What forms do quadriliteral action nouns take?  [فَعْلَتَكَ · grammar]
- 26:19/q06/p1 The regular quadriliteral source is فعللة, with other patterned variants and some lookalike nouns that are not sources. — Sībawayh gives دحرجة and زلزلة, explains the tāʾ as replacing long alif in زلزال, and notes فعلالا variants. He compares الزلزال and القلقل with tafʿīl, relates فعلال to فيعال in فاعلت, and gives احرنجاما and اطمئنانا for extended استفعل patterns. He excludes الطمأنينة and القشعريرة as source forms of اطمأننت and اقشعررت, comparing النبات against أنبت. [6 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 26:19/q07 Why is the deed described obliquely and repeatedly?  [فَعَلْتَ فَعْلَتَكَ ٱلَّتِى فَعَلْتَ · rhetoric]
- 26:19/q07/p1 The vague repetition makes the killing notorious and grave, and presses responsibility onto Moses. — Ibn al-Shaykh says leaving the deed unnamed magnifies it. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Moses knows the referent, while the repeated relative expression heightens notoriety and fixes responsibility. Al-Ālūsī says its ambiguity impugns Moses' prophethood. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the repetition an intensifier of gravity and compares 20:78. Asad renders its condemning force with 'heinous' and refers to 28:4–22. Iṣlāḥī says the ambiguity reflects the act's gravity. [7 notes: BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:19/q08 What is the basic sense of kufr?  [ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ · meaning]
- 26:19/q08/p1 Kufr can mean covering or concealing, hence concealing a received blessing. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports Ibn Fāris's designation of remote, rarely visited land and its inhabitants or villages as kufr-related forms; a cited poet uses kafara for clouds covering stars. Study Quran relates ingratitude to covering a blessing and cites 2:276, 31:32, and 43:15. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(الشاعر); STUDYQURAN]

## 26:19/q09 What charge does Pharaoh make by calling Moses one of the kāfirīn?  [وَأَنتَ مِنَ ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ · interpretation]

Al-Farrāʾ selects this sense. Al-Suddī names care during childhood. Ibn Zayd and Ibn Isḥāq join favor, killing, and denial of kindness. Al-Bayḍāwī mentions Pharaoh's close associates, al-Nasafī his baker, and Ibn ʿĀshūr says Moses defended an enslaved Israelite instead of identifying with Pharaoh's household, reversing lineage and adoption (33:4). Study Quran notes Moses' life among Egyptians; KuranYolu says the fatal blow was unintended while defending an Israelite (28:16). Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī broadens the alleged ingratitude to spared life, upbringing, damaged land, and Pharaoh's claimed authority over Israel; he rejects reducing it to Pharaoh's personal favor alone. ## 26:19/q10 How is وَأَنتَ مِنَ ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ attached to the preceding clause?  [وَأَنتَ مِنَ ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the wāw introduces a circumstance simultaneous with the killing or a fresh accusation about Moses now.
- 26:19/q10/p1 It is circumstantial: Moses acted while in the state Pharaoh alleges. — Al-Naḥḥās gives this option. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Būrsawī apply it to ingratitude while killing; Study Quran calls it adverbial. Ibn ʿĀshūr attaches it to the subject of فعلت. Al-Ālūsī prefers it because it matches Moses' answering clause and permits an ordered reply to Pharaoh's two charges. [7 notes: NAHHAS; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 26:19/q10/p2 It is an independent or coordinated new reproach about Moses' present state. — Al-Samīn lists an independent sentence. Al-Bayḍāwī makes it a fresh judgment of present opposition; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports al-Ḥasan's fresh statement. Ibn ʿĀshūr connects the active participle with present disbelief; al-Ālūsī reports al-Ḥasan's alternatives. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:19/q11 Could Pharaoh's accusation mean Moses truly disbelieved before prophethood?  [ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ · theology]
turns on: Whether apparent past association establishes actual unbelief, given prophetic protection and dissimulation.
- 26:19/q11/p1 No; Moses' outward association or concealment did not make him an unbeliever. — Al-Rāzī rejects true prior unbelief as fabricated or ignorant, saying Moses used dissimulation and prophets cannot disbelieve before mission. Al-Nasafī says Moses was protected and lived among them under dissimulation. Al-Ālūsī extends protection before and after prophethood and doubts Pharaoh would have tolerated known open dissent. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Moses had not openly opposed their faith though inwardly he did. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Moses' later الضالين specifically denies unbelief; al-Ṭabarī reports Ibn Masʿūd's 'ignorant' wording. [7 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(السدي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TAB-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 26:19/q11/p2 Pharaoh's words can be taken as alleging real past religious unbelief. — Al-Ṭabarī allows Pharaoh to frame Moses under Moses' present creed; al-Rāzī reports but rejects this reading. [1 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL-; NASAFI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 26:19/q12 What does Pharaoh's combined reproach try to achieve?  [* · interpretation]
- 26:19/q12/p1 He uses Moses' upbringing and the killing to discredit or silence his prophetic demand. — Al-Ṭabarī and Iṣlāḥī note the narrative's omitted delivery of the message before Pharaoh speaks. Al-Wāḥidī's people of meanings say Pharaoh arrogantly belittles the caller and changes the subject. Al-Qurṭubī says he questions how a known killer can claim divine mission. Al-Bayḍāwī says he magnifies the killing after reciting his alleged favor. Ibn ʿĀshūr says he hopes to frighten Moses into silence under color of punishment, treats Aaron as aide to the principal messenger, and notes Moses delays answering the upbringing charge. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Iṣlāḥī set out the full objection: an Israelite raised in the palace killed one of Pharaoh's people, yet now claims prophethood and demands Israel's release. Iṣlāḥī adds Pharaoh's intolerance of an Israelite making the demand. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:19/q13 How does Moses' subsequent reply answer this charge?  [* · links]
- 26:19/q13/p1 He acknowledges the killing first, explains his ignorance or misguidance, and denies that it defeats his mission. — Al-Biqāʿī notes his reversed order and cites al-Ṭabarī that Arabs use misguidance for ignorance and vice versa. Al-Burhān says Moses did not know the blow would kill and did not deny God's favor. Ibn ʿĀshūr says he answers the killing first because Pharaoh meant to silence him; he has no fear of a blood claim because he trusts God's protection. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that either reading concedes the apparent act but defeats Pharaoh's inference, and الضالين denies prior unbelief. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says Moses admits the deed and explains fleeing in fear, citing 26:20–21. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 26:19/q14 What background details do reports give about Moses' upbringing and return?  [* · reports]
- 26:19/q14/p1 Pharaoh's household raised Moses before the killing, and Moses later returned as a prophet. — Al-Ṭabarī locates the years in Pharaoh's household before the killing. KuranYolu says Moses' mother set him afloat because Pharaoh ordered Israelite baby boys killed; servants found him and he grew up in the palace. Al-Qurṭubī says eleven years and some months separated his flight from his prophetic return. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 26:19/q15 How can nouns be complete and related to verbs syntactically?  [* · grammar]
- 26:19/q15/p1 A noun's completeness blocks annexation, while verbs govern subjects and several kinds of accusative or prepositional dependents. — Al-Jurjānī lists dual and plural nūn, explicit or estimated tanwīn, and prior annexation as marks of completeness, citing ملء الأرض ذهبا at 3:91. He lists direct objects, adverbs, accompaniment, causal objects (4:114), kāna predicates, circumstantials, specification, and exception among verb-governed nouns. Prepositions mediate where direct government is impossible; accompaniment wāw and إلا mediate although the verb supplies the accusative. [4 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 26:19/q16 How does an unstated question shape an ensuing assertion?  [* · rhetoric]
- 26:19/q16/p1 The speaker can answer a question implied by context; its answer keeps the verb when the question was not spoken. — Al-Jurjānī illustrates with a poet answering an imagined charge of lying, another poet's 'I am ill' followed by sleeplessness and grief, al-Mutanabbī naming who erased a dwelling after denying the winds did, and al-Walīd ibn Yazīd naming what erased a faded house. He distinguishes answers to explicit questions, which may omit the verb, from answers to implied questions, which must state it for recovery. [5 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 26:19/q17 How is idhan analyzed as a reply or temporal expression?  [* · grammar]
- 26:19/q17/p1 The governing particle idhan selects a subjunctive, whereas temporal idh with compensating tanwīn permits an indicative; idhan itself is responsive. — Al-Burhān distinguishes the two forms and says a restriction to governing particles selecting the present is necessary; otherwise one form could limit what follows to a time or situation in a conversational promise and reply. He reports Sībawayh that idhan means a reply and cannot independently open speech. [3 notes: BURHAN(سيبويه)]

## 26:19/q18 What practice of takbīr at Quran completion is reported?  [* · sciences]
- 26:19/q18/p1 Takbīr was practiced or recommended during Ramadan recitation and completion, with variation in its placement. — Ibn al-Jazarī says detailed Shāfiʿī jurisprudence rarely mentions it despite attribution to al-Shāfiʿī, though al-Sakhāwī, al-Jaʿbarī, and Abū Shāma mention it incidentally. Ibrāhīm al-Fazārī reports his father's surprise at Abū Shāma's following al-Shāfiʿī, and Abū al-Thanāʾ Maḥmūd ibn Jumla's fatwas and occasional tarāwīḥ practice. The author saw teachers practice and recommend it and did so in Damascus and Egypt. Some said takbīr after each sūra through a one-rakʿa completion, then after the final sūra before bowing; the next rakʿa opened with al-Fātiḥa and al-Baqara. Others placed it after al-Fātiḥa and basmala before the next sūra; a Shāfiʿī objected to a child's customary completion takbīr. [5 notes: NASHR(والد إبراهيم الفزاري)]

## 26:19/q19 Can the understood act behind فَعْلَتَكَ be expressed with either grammatical gender?  [فَعْلَتَكَ · grammar]
- 26:19/q19/p1 Arabic permits feminine فعلة or masculine فعل for the understood act. — Al-Farrāʾ says both references to an understood action are permissible. [1 notes: FARRA]

## 26:19/q20 Is a theological sense of kufr fitting for Pharaoh's accusation?  [ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ · interpretation]
- 26:19/q20/p1 Some commentators reject a theological sense because Pharaoh was not motivated by theological concerns. — Study Quran reports this argument from commentators cited as Th. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(some commentators (Th))]
