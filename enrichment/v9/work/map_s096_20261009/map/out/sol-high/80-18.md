# 80:18: 8 questions

## 80:18/q01 From what origin was the human created?  [مِنْ أَىِّ شَىْءٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the answer names the reproductive material, specifies both contributors, or describes the transition from nonexistence.
- 80:18/q01/p1 From a lowly drop of fluid; creation then proceeds through further stages. — Farrāʾ names sperm, then a clot and later stages; Qushayrī likewise describes a drop, clot, and successive formation. Zamakhsharī as reported by Burhān, Itqān, Ibn Kathīr, Study Quran, and Islāḥī specify a lowly or impure drop; Study Quran cites 18:37, 16:4, 40:67, and 86:6–8. Kashshāf, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Bayḍāwī stress the lowliness of the material. Zajjāj, Wāḥidī-Wajīz, Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and Burhān say the next verse supplies the answer; Burhān uses its explicit 'from a drop' to fix the sense. Ibn Kathīr adds the decreed lifespan, provision, deeds, and blessed or wretched end; Farrāʾ also mentions male or female and blessed or wretched fate. [16 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN(الزمخشري); ITQAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 80:18/q01/p2 From both the man's fluid and the woman's fluid. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya explicitly identifies both fluids as the indicated substance. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 80:18/q01/p3 God brought the human into existence after nonexistence. — Kur'an Yolu uses this origin to invite deniers and doubters of resurrection to reflect. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 80:18/q02 What is the force of the question about the human's origin?  [مِنْ أَىِّ شَىْءٍ خَلَقَهُۥ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes assign several compatible rhetorical effects to the same interrogative: abasement, affirmation, challenge, reminder of favor, and anticipation; context and the answer in 80:19 are the stated cues.
- 80:18/q02/p1 It humbles the human by recalling the contemptibly low origin that makes pride and ingratitude unfitting. — Ṭabarī applies the challenge to a disbeliever too proud to obey or affirm oneness; Qurṭubī likewise asks how such an unbeliever can be arrogant and calls for wonder. Rāzī says the question intensifies belittlement. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Burhān say context determines whether such a question exalts or belittles, and Burhān uses verse 19's drop to decide for belittlement here; Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares 77:13. Itqān cites the indefinite 'thing' as a belittling device. Kashshāf, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bayḍāwī, Zamakhsharī as reported by Burhān, Biqāʿī, Bursawī, and the reported view in Ālūsī stress the paltry origin. Bursawī connects it to ingratitude toward the benefactor; Islāḥī says an impure drop defeats boasts of purity, superiority, or entitlement to Paradise. [16 notes: TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 80:18/q02/p2 Its interrogative form serves as an assertion or reminder that God created the human, preparing the stated answer. — Zajjāj calls it formally interrogative but assertive; Wāḥidī-Basīṭ says it affirms God's creation and oneness; Wāḥidī-Wajīz and Baghawī call it rhetorical confirmation. Biqāʿī says it prompts acknowledgment while belittling origin. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it asks which substance formed the human, not whether God created him. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 80:18/q02/p3 It asks whether creation supplies any ground for disbelief or resistance to faith; the answer will show none. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this second question heightens the astonishment of 80:17 because wonder concerns events with no apparent cause, and prepares the answer in 80:19. Ṭabarī and Qurṭubī similarly ask what in the disbeliever's origin could license arrogance. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 80:18/q02/p4 It draws attention to God's favor in bringing the human into existence. — Bayḍāwī calls creation a particular favor; Ālūsī says the question starts the account of favors from creation through life. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 80:18/q02/p5 Its question-and-answer form builds anticipation for the proof that follows. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the form to the question about the great news in 78:1–2. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 80:18/q03 Who does the pronoun in 'created him' denote?  [خَلَقَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun is taken as generic humanity or specifically the condemned unbeliever in context.
- 80:18/q03/p1 The human being generally. — Rāzī speaks of human beings, and Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī describe human creation generally; their discussion includes those who disbelieve. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 80:18/q03/p2 The unbeliever whose arrogance or denial is being challenged. — Ṭabarī identifies the addressee as the disbeliever who resists obedience and oneness; Qurṭubī calls him this unbeliever. Ibn ʿĀshūr applies the argument to deniers of resurrection. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 80:18/q04 Who is the unexpressed subject of 'created him'?  [خَلَقَهُۥ · grammar]
- 80:18/q04/p1 God is the understood subject. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the act of creating makes the subject plain, and polytheists acknowledged God created humanity. Ṭabāṭabāʾī extends the observation to the following verbs: innate knowledge, even among polytheists, recognizes God alone as creator. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 80:18/q05 What lesson does recalling creation establish?  [مِنْ أَىِّ شَىْءٍ خَلَقَهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Which inference the reminder foregrounds: power to restore life, the human's lack of grounds for pride, or God's ongoing decree and care.
- 80:18/q05/p1 The first creation proves God's power to recreate and resurrect the human. — Ibn Kathīr treats the lowly drop and apportioned destiny as proof of recreation. Ibn ʿĀshūr argues by analogy with first creation, citing 50:15 and 86:5–8 in one account and 50:15, 86:5, 86:8 in another. Islāḥī points to creation's phases in 23:12–16 and their culmination in restoration after death; Study Quran cites 18:37, 16:4, 40:67, and 86:6–8 as parallels showing omnipotence. Kur'an Yolu urges deniers and doubters to reflect on existence after nonexistence. Biqāʿī presents human creation and the food cycle as evidence of power. [12 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 80:18/q05/p2 Humble origins expose the injustice of human pride, disobedience, and ingratitude. — Muqātil says God explains creation to make the human reflect; Naḥḥās contrasts creation from something lowly with nobility gained through obedience. Bursawī says arrogance and ingratitude are unfitting toward the benefactor. Islāḥī says the reminders expose transitory pride and rejects Quraysh's presumed future rank, comparing 70:36–39; an impure drop gives no claim to purity, superiority, or Paradise. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the human owns none of God's governance and, citing 14:34, that overlooking even the care shown in food is astonishing injustice. Ṭabarī relates arrogance to refusal of obedience and oneness. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 80:18/q05/p3 Creation displays God's comprehensive decree, formation, and subsequent governance of human life. — Farrāʾ says God determines blessed or wretched outcome and sex; Ibn Kathīr adds lifespan, provision, deeds, and final fate. Qushayrī describes God's staged formation and proportioning. Ṭabāṭabāʾī lists creating, measuring, guiding, causing death, burial, and resurrection, and says the human owns none of it; Ālūsī presents the course from creation through life as divine favors. [5 notes: FARRA; QUSHAYRI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 80:18/q06 How does this question connect to its surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the verses continue the prior context or have a distinct occasion; otherwise the notes emphasize different links in the same creation and resurrection sequence.
- 80:18/q06/p1 It introduces the origin and stages of creation, which the next verse answers and the passage continues. — Farrāʾ describes sperm, clot, and later stages. Zajjāj, Wāḥidī-Wajīz, Baghawī, Kashshāf, Ibn al-Jawzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Burhān, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī point to the answer in 80:19. Rāzī calls origin the first of three stages treated in the passage; Ālūsī says it opens the account of favors from creation onward. [14 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURHAN(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 80:18/q06/p2 It explains the preceding denunciation by answering rejection of resurrection through the fact of first creation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly connects it to 80:17 and calls resurrection denial a major ground of unbelief. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the second question heightens the first question's astonishment, while Biqāʿī says resurrection denial drives the turn to origin. Islāḥī describes creation, life's sources, death, and return to Judgment as the sequence. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 80:18/q06/p3 The passage develops the sura's warning by using creation and food as signs of divine power and care. — Biqāʿī relates the warning to those who fear, the purification of receptive hearts, resurrection, and the food cycle, citing 79:45. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says even food exhibits subtle governance and mercy, citing 14:34. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 80:18/q06/p4 These verses may continue the preceding passage as one context. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī allows continuous context while noting another view of the occasion. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 80:18/q06/p5 Some commentators give these verses a separate occasion of revelation. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this alternative without details here. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 80:18/q07 How is the past tense of 'created him' understood?  [خَلَقَهُۥ · grammar]
- 80:18/q07/p1 It describes a recurring phenomenon despite its past tense. — Asad applies this observation to verses 18–22 as a whole. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 80:18/q08 What do the supplied side notes discuss outside this verse's wording?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: These are distinct discussions of other expressions and supply no stated interpretation of 80:18.
- 80:18/q08/p1 An early authority derives al-raḥim from al-Raḥmān. — Ibn Qutayba thinks the unnamed early authority may be ʿAlī; the term is not in 80:18. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(علي)]
- 80:18/q08/p2 Some interpretations assign lexical or cosmological senses to isolated letters elsewhere. — Ibn Qutayba reports Ṭā Hā as 'man,' Yā Sīn as 'human,' and Nūn as an inkwell from some commentators; another interpreter says Nūn is a whale, Ḥā Mīm a settled decree, and Qāf a mountain encircling earth. Ibn Qutayba declines these interpretations because he does not know their sources or explanations. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(آخر,قوم من المفسرين); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL-]
- 80:18/q08/p3 Ṣād pronounced with kasra on its dāl is linked to muṣādāh, opposition or matching. — Ibn Qutayba exempts this interpretation from his skepticism about the other letter explanations. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL+]
- 80:18/q08/p4 Nominative lā khawfun ʿalayhim permits two syntactic analyses. — Sāmarrāʾī says the phrase may attach to khawf with an omitted predicate or itself function as the predicate; this phrase is not in 80:18. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 80:18/q08/p5 Accusative lā khawfa ʿalayhim permits one syntactic analysis. — Sāmarrāʾī says ʿalayhim must be the predicate; attachment to the construct would require accusative with tanwīn, not a word built on fatḥa. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
