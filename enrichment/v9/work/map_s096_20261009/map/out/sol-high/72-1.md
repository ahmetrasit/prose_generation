# 72:1: 44 questions

## 72:1/q01 Whom is the Prophet told to address with this report?  [قُلْ · interpretation]
- 72:1/q01/p1 Muhammad is to tell his people, especially Quraysh, what God disclosed about the jinn. — Tabrisi says the news was previously unknown to them; Razi and Biqai say it discloses the jinn episode to the Prophet's companions and people; Islahi says Quraysh is the immediate audience. [9 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 72:1/q02 What does revelation mean here and how did the Prophet learn of the listening?  [أُوحِىَ إِلَىَّ · interpretation]
- 72:1/q02/p1 God informed Muhammad by revelation, through Gabriel; he had not learned the jinn's impressions directly. — Wahidi and Qurtubi identify God as revealer; Tabrisi and Biqai mention the angel Gabriel and the passive's magnification; Ibn Ashur argues the wording implies prior ignorance; Islahi says the jinn did not tell him directly. Razi defines wahy as rapid, discreet casting of meaning, either by inspiration or angelic descent; Bursevi calls it hidden communication. [11 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES; BUKHARI(ابن عباس)]

## 72:1/q03 How is أُوحِىَ read and derived?  [أُوحِىَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the verb uses the common quadriliteral or a triliteral dialect, and how far initial wāw-to-hamza analogy extends.
- 72:1/q03/p1 The common passive reading is ūḥiya, from the four-letter verb awḥā. — Farra says readers unanimously use the passive; Ibn Atiyya calls it the majority reading; Razi reports an alif in awha; Abu Hayyan and Samin call it the quadriliteral form. [6 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); RAZI-FULL(يونس وهرون عن أبي عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(العتكي عن أبي عمرو وابن أبي عبلة وأبو إياس)]
- 72:1/q03/p2 A rare reading uses the synonymous triliteral waḥiya or waḥā, retaining initial wāw. — Nahhas, Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi, Abu Hayyan, Samin and Alusi cite al-Ajjaj's line waḥā lahā al-qarār as evidence. Transmissions name Ibn Abi Abla, al-Ataki from Abu Amr, Abu Iyas and others; forms and attributions vary. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(العجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(العجاج,جوية بن عائذ); TABRISI(العجاج); RAZI-FULL(يونس وهرون عن أبي عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(العتكي عن أبي عمرو وابن أبي عبلة وأبو إياس,العجاج); ALUSI-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة والعتكي عن أبي عمرو وجؤبة بن عائذ الأسدي)]
- 72:1/q03/p3 A rare reading has aḥiya or aḥyi, changing the triliteral's initial wāw to hamza. — Farra names Juwayya and compares 81:11; Zajjaj and Nahhas compare 77:11 and uqit; several transmitters name Zayd ibn Ali and Ibn Abi Abla. Zamakhshari allows change under damma, Mazini extends it to kasra; Abu Hayyan, Samin's sheikh and Alusi dispute an unrestricted rule because waw's position matters. Tabrisi also reports qil aḥyi ilayya. [24 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(جوية بن عائذ الأسدي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة,جوية بن عائذ، ابن أبي عبلة); TABRISI(جوية بن عابد); RAZI-FULL(قُرِىء); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المازني,زيد بن علي وجوية وابن أبي عبلة); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,الشيخ,زيد بن علي والكسائي وابن أبي عبلة); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري,المازني,بعض الأجلة,زيد بن علي وجؤبة وابن أبي عبلة); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 72:1/q04 Why is the revealer left unnamed in أُوحِىَ?  [أُوحِىَ · rhetoric]
- 72:1/q04/p1 The passive magnifies and gives prominence to God's revelation. — Jishumi calls the unnamed agent a mark of glorification; Tabrisi says God sent it through an angel; Biqai says the passive foregrounds the revelation from the one revealer. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 72:1/q05 How does أَنَّهُ ٱسْتَمَعَ function in the passive sentence?  [أَنَّهُ ٱسْتَمَعَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether a prepositional phrase may serve as deputy subject while the revealed content remains accusative.
- 72:1/q05/p1 The opened anna clause, meaning the jinn's listening, is the passive subject of ūḥiya. — Akhfash says it is the revelation's content; Nahhas calls it nominative; Zamakhshari, Baydawi, Nasafi, Razi, Samin and Ibn Ashur make the nominalized clause deputy subject. Razi compares 6:19. Ibn Ashur says anna stresses an extraordinary report, and its pronoun is a pronoun of the affair; Bursevi and Alusi also identify that pronoun. [15 notes: AKHFASH; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; NASHR]
- 72:1/q05/p2 The phrase ilayy can be the passive subject, leaving the anna clause accusative. — Samin attributes this permission to the Kufans and al-Akhfash; Alusi explicitly attaches ilayy to the passive verb as deputy subject. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 72:1/q06 How are the sūra's following inna and anna clauses read and connected?  [أَنَّهُ إِنَّا · readings]
turns on: Whether each clause is God's disclosure or the jinn's reported speech, and whether opened clauses attach to ūḥiya or to their affirmation.
- 72:1/q06/p1 The opening annahu is fatḥa and the quoted innā samiʿnā is kasra; subsequent revelation clauses open and jinn speech clauses break. — Farra and Zajjaj report agreement on innā here; Nahhas, Kashshaf, Razi, Nasafi, Bursevi, Elmalili and Biqai explain kasra as independent speech after qālū. Zajjaj's grammarians prefer separating revelation from jinn speech; Zajjaj, Ibn Khalawayh, Jishumi, Ibn Atiyya and Nashr give the coordination rule. Conditional response fa takes kasra. Kashshaf notes exceptions at 72:18-19; Nasafi discusses 72:16,18,28. [20 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حاتم); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(القاشاني); NASHR; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); ZAJJAJ+]
- 72:1/q06/p2 Most later anna clauses may be opened as further objects of the jinn's affirmation or coordinated with the first revelation clause. — Farra supplies understood belief; Zajjaj makes āmannā bihi mean affirming the Lord's greatness; Kashshaf and Razi describe coordination with belief. Razi answers awkward clauses by taking faith as affirmation and testimony, while Zajjaj rejects direct coordination to a genitive pronoun without repeating its preposition. Ibn Atiyya and Burhan favor coordination under ūḥiya for later opened clauses. [9 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حاتم); BURHAN; BURHAN+; ZAJJAJ-; RAZI-FULL-]
- 72:1/q06/p3 Named readers differ in which later clauses they open: Ibn Kathir and Abu Amr open four; Nafi and Abu Bakr differ at 72:19; Ibn Amir, Hamza, Kisai, Hafs and others open a wider sequence. — Farra reports Alqama and companions opening later clauses, Nafi against most Medinans, and Asim's speech/revelation split. Zajjaj notes conflicting Asim transmissions. Ibn Mujahid, Farisi, Jishumi, Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi and Nasafi detail reader lists and exceptions. Ibn Atiyya rejects a purported Abu Amr kasra at 72:1 as unestablished; Tabrisi warns Abu Jafar transmission and its rule disagree. [18 notes: FARRA(إبراهيم عن علقمة,عاصم,نافع والحسن ومجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNMUJAHID(ابن عامر وحمزة والكسائى وحفص عن عاصم,ابن كثير وأبو عمرو,عاصم فى رواية أبى بكر ونافع، والمفضل عن عاصم); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر,ابن عامر وحفص عن عاصم وحمزة والكسائي وخلف,ابن كثير وأبو عمرو,نافع وأبو بكر عن عاصم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عامر، حمزة، الكسائي، حفص,ابن كثير، أبو عمرو,عاصم,علقمة); TABRISI(أبو جعفر); NASAFI-FULL(شامي وكوفي غير أبي بكر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو)-; TABRISI(الرواة عن أبي جعفر)-]
- 72:1/q06/p4 Later innā clauses are generally kasra, with only the opening annahu excepted. — Ibn Qutayba calls the alternation and speaker attribution a difficulty of the sūra and states this overall pattern; Farra reports Asim's extensive kasra. His description of case as genitive is preserved as reported. [3 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عاصم)]

## 72:1/q07 Where does recitation stop in the later linked clauses?  [أَنَّهُ إِنَّا · readings]
- 72:1/q07/p1 With kasra at 72:19 stop at 72:18; with fatḥa carry the syntactic connection through 72:19. — Burhan and the Mustawfa author in Itqan connect the stop to the variant reading; Burhan says dependent quoted speech cannot be severed from its governing phrase and rejects an oath analysis at 72:16. Ibn Atiyya coordinates opened 72:19 to 72:1. [6 notes: BURHAN; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ITQAN(صاحب المستوفى); BURHAN+]

## 72:1/q08 May the transferred vowel in قُلْ أُوحِىَ receive rawm or ishmām at pause?  [قُلْ أُوحِىَ · readings]
turns on: Whether a temporary transferred vowel behaves like an intrinsic inflectional vowel at pause.
- 72:1/q08/p1 No: its ḍamma is transferred and disappears at pause, so it cannot license rawm or ishmām. — Nashr distinguishes intrinsic from transferred vowels, excludes quiescent forms and non-nunated fatḥa, permits sukun or rawm with intrinsic or connected kasra but not ishmām, and rejects Makki's plural-mim analogy because mim lacks a prior vowel. [5 notes: NASHR]
- 72:1/q08/p2 Makki permits rawm and ishmām for connected plural mīm by analogy with pronoun hāʾ. — Nashr reports and rejects this analogy: plural mim has no prior vowel. [1 notes: NASHR(مكي); NASHR-]

## 72:1/q09 What does ٱسْتَمَعَ imply and what is its unstated object?  [ٱسْتَمَعَ · grammar]
- 72:1/q09/p1 The jinn deliberately listened to the Qur'an, whose object is omitted because their following words supply it. — Ibn Ashur and Alusi supply Qur'an from the sequel and 46:29; Bursevi distinguishes intentional listening from mere hearing; Biqai stresses exceptional attention; Asad includes acceptance of revelation. Qurtubi instead supplies the Prophet as the object from context; Samin says min al-jinn modifies nafar. [10 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 72:1/q10 Why did the jinn seek out this recitation?  [ٱسْتَمَعَ ٱلْجِنِّ · reports]
- 72:1/q10/p1 Heavenly news was blocked by guards and meteors after Muhammad's mission, so jinn or Iblis's envoys searched for the cause and found his recitation. — Muqatil says heaven had been unguarded between Jesus and Muhammad; Suddi says guarding accompanied a prophet or manifest sign. Farra, Ibn Abbas reports, Mawrdi, Qurtubi and Durr describe search across regions. Suddi has Iblis smell soil to locate Mecca; Qurtubi reports that jinn formerly added nine false words to each true heavenly word. The Taif people first feared the heavens had perished until Abd Yalayl advised checking the fixed stars. Razi and Qurtubi explain that those pelted may be jinn called devils because of rebellion. [24 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; MUSNAD-AHMAD(ابن عباس); IBNHIBBAN(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي,عبد يا ليل بن عمرو); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عبد الملك); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); BUKHARI(ابن عباس); MUSLIM(ابن عباس)]

## 72:1/q11 Where and under what circumstances did the jinn hear the Prophet?  [ٱسْتَمَعَ نَفَرٌۭ · reports]
turns on: The Ibn Abbas report of an unnoticed hearing and the Ibn Masud reports of an instructed visit, including conflicting accounts of Ibn Masud's presence.
- 72:1/q11/p1 They encountered him at Batn Nakhlah near Ukaz or Tihama, usually while he recited in the dawn prayer. — Ibn Abbas accounts describe travel toward Ukaz with companions, an unnoticed dawn recitation and return to their people; Muqatil places the group at night before finding dawn prayer. Ibn Ishaq puts it after Taif. Zajjaj relates 46:29, where some ask the others to be silent. Wahidi Basit also reports a night prayer and their affectionate crowding. Tirmidhi grades Ibn Abbas's report hasan sahih; Tabrisi cites both Sahihs. [34 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); MUSNAD-AHMAD(ابن عباس); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TUSTARI; ZAJJAJ; IBNHIBBAN(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عبد الملك); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; MUSLIM(ابن عباس); BUKHARI(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(JJ); TIRMIDHI(ابن عباس)]
- 72:1/q11/p2 In a summoned encounter, he went to the jinn at Mecca or al-Hajun and recited to them; Ibn Masud traditions vary over whether he accompanied him. — Abu Ubayda names al-Hajun; Ibn Masud reports a divine order, jinn caller, boundary line, jinn like partridges or Zutt, traces and fires, and a tree testifying to prophethood. One route says Ibn Masud was absent that night and saw traces later; another places him at the line. Bayhaqi notes sound reports support absence but other routes place him there. Jinn requested provisions of bones and dung. Tabaṭaba'i records a separate pledge of seventy-one thousand at a date-palm valley. [23 notes: MUJAHID(أبو عبيدة بن عبد الله بن مسعود); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود); JISHUMI(ابن مسعود); RAZI-FULL(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(البيهقي,النبي,عامر الشعبي عن علقمة عن ابن مسعود); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); ALUSI-FULL(ابن تيمية,ابن مسعود); TABATABAI(عبد الله بن مسعود,علي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)+]

## 72:1/q12 Did Muhammad see or intentionally recite to the listeners in this verse?  [أُوحِىَ ٱسْتَمَعَ · reports]
turns on: Whether the verse describes the unobserved first hearing or an instructed encounter, and how Ibn Abbas and Ibn Masud's reports are weighed.
- 72:1/q12/p1 No; the jinn heard an ordinary recitation without his seeing or addressing them, and revelation informed him afterward. — Ibn Abbas's report says neither sight nor direct recitation; Razi argues that otherwise the Prophet would not attribute knowledge to revelation. Qurtubi cites the wording istamaʿa, and Biqai, Alusi, Ibn Ashur and Islahi infer the same. Tirmidhi grades the report hasan sahih. [21 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(ابن عباس); IBNHIBBAN(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); TIRMIDHI(ابن عباس)]
- 72:1/q12/p2 Yes, he saw or met jinn and recited directly to them when God directed or summoned them. — Mawrdi attributes this to those citing 46:29 and gives Ibn Masud's ordered recitation. Jishumi reports a seen group of seven from Nasibin, who were sent to others; Qurtubi says the Prophet could see jinn in their own forms. Ibn al-Arabi prefers Ibn Masud's eyewitness evidence over Ibn Abbas's report. [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود,ابن مسعود والضحاك وطائفة); JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود,البيهقي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)+]

## 72:1/q13 How many jinn encounters occurred and how is this account related to 46:29?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the jinn listening in 72:1 and 46:29 is one event, or the reports concern repeated visits.
- 72:1/q13/p1 This is the same listening event narrated in 46:29-32. — Ibn Ishaq, Zajjaj, Wahidi, Ibn Jawzi, Razi, Abu Hayyan, Biqai's report from Abu Hayyan, Islahi and Study Quran identify the shared account; Ibn Atiyya says its story was treated there. The jinn's return as warners echoes that passage. [17 notes: IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(عاصم عن ذر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو حيان); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 72:1/q13/p2 There were at least two encounters: an unnoticed first hearing and a later requested recitation; reports about Mecca, Nakhlah and 46:29 may refer to different visits. — Razi and Bayhaqi reconcile Ibn Abbas and Ibn Masud by sequence; Qurtubi reports both orders of Mecca and Nakhlah and Bayhaqi's sequence. Abu Hayyan reports Hira without Ibn Masud followed by Hajun with him; Biqai favors multiple events on hadith evidence and distinguishes Nasibin from Nineveh. Kuranyolu also places a later seen meeting. [12 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(البيهقي,قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البيهقي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL+]
- 72:1/q13/p3 One encounter can reconcile both reports if Muhammad did not know the jinn's private words, or learned their report only afterward. — Razi proposes that he recited without knowing their words and deeds, or saw believing jinn and later received revelation of what they told their people. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 72:1/q13/p4 The differing accounts may reflect six visits. — Alusi gives this as a way to explain the varying reports and notes dating in the eleventh year of prophethood. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الواقدي)]

## 72:1/q14 What did the Prophet recite when the jinn listened?  [قُرْءَانًا · reports]
turns on: The reported identity of the passage heard; the verse itself names only Quran.
- 72:1/q14/p1 He recited the opening of Sūrat al-ʿAlaq. — Ikrima names the sura; Qurtubi specifies Read in the name of your Lord, 96:1. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عكرمة); BIQAI-FULL(عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 72:1/q14/p2 He recited Sūrat al-Raḥmān. — Abu Hayyan, Biqai and Alusi preserve an unnamed alternative report. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عكرمة); BIQAI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 72:1/q14/p3 The omitted object may have been Sūrat Ṭā Hā. — Bursevi lists Taha alongside the Quran and iqra as possible supplied objects. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 72:1/q15 How many listeners were there?  [نَفَرٌۭ · reports]
turns on: Different transmitted counts and whether reports describe the same gathering or later adherents.
- 72:1/q15/p1 Seven jinn listened. — Ibn Ishaq says seven from Nisibin; Mujahid divides them into three from Harran and four from Nasibin and gives names; Qushayri says seven later drew seventy converts. Some accounts locate Nusaybin in Yemen. Abu Hayyan says this count conflicts with Ikrima's twelve thousand. [11 notes: IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(عاصم عن زر,قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زر وعكرمة); BIQAI-FULL(القشيري,قيل); DURR-FULL(عبد الملك); ALUSI-FULL(زر)]
- 72:1/q15/p2 Nine jinn listened. — Muqatil speaks of nine from Yemen alongside nobles from Nasibin; Farra says Iblis sent nine; Zirr includes Zawbaa; Dahhak identifies a Yemeni Nasibin distinct from Iraq's; some versions list all nine names, with spelling variation. [11 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TUSTARI; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك,زر بن حبيش); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(جويبر عن الضحاك,قيل); BIQAI-FULL(البغوي); ITQAN]
- 72:1/q15/p3 Twelve thousand jinn from the island of Mosul listened. — Ikrima's report is preserved by Mawrdi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi; Abu Hayyan asks how it can be reconciled with seven and Alusi observes that nafar then simply means group. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زر وعكرمة,عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 72:1/q15/p4 Seventy thousand followed the Prophet before dawn on the Night of the Jinn. — Makḥūl distinguishes those followers among jinn and says the passage concerned jinn alone. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مكحول)]
- 72:1/q15/p5 Seventy-one thousand jinn pledged to the Prophet in a separate date-palm valley account. — The report through Ali lists prayer, fasting, alms, pilgrimage, jihad and counsel; Tabatabai says the manner of their alms and jihad is unknown. [2 notes: TABATABAI(علي)]

## 72:1/q16 From where and from which group did the listeners come?  [نَفَرٌۭ ٱلْجِنِّ · referent]
turns on: The different transmitted origins, group affiliations and whether more than one delegation is being described.
- 72:1/q16/p1 They came from Nasibin, sometimes located in Yemen and sometimes in the upper Euphrates or Iraq. — Thalabi, Baghawi and Nasafi state Nasibin; Dahhak specifies a Yemeni village distinct from Iraqi Nasibin; Asad cites Tabari for upper Euphrates and tentatively suggests Jews from Syria or Mesopotamia. Muqatil also speaks of nobles from Nasibin. [12 notes: MUQATIL; IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); TUSTARI; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(جويبر عن الضحاك); BIQAI-FULL(البغوي); ASAD-NOTES(Tabari)]
- 72:1/q16/p2 They came from Yemen or Tihama. — Muqatil identifies nine Yemeni jinn; Zajjaj reports Yemen as alternative to Nasibin; Iblis's search in some reports targets Tihama. [3 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; DURR-FULL(عبد الملك)]
- 72:1/q16/p3 They were a mixed group from Harran and Nasibin. — Mujahid, Zirr and Asim's reports split seven into three from Harran and four from Nasibin; Alusi calls the latter Yemeni. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(عاصم عن زر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زر وعكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(زر)]
- 72:1/q16/p4 They came from the island of Mosul. — Ikrima's report pairs that origin with twelve thousand. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زر وعكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 72:1/q16/p5 The Meccan visitors were from Nasibin and the Nakhlah visitors from Nineveh. — Qurtubi preserves this distinction; Biqai favors multiple encounters on hadith evidence. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL+]

## 72:1/q17 What religion did the listening jinn previously follow?  [ٱلْجِنِّ · referent]

## 72:1/q18 What can نَفَرٌۭ mean?  [نَفَرٌۭ · meaning]

## 72:1/q19 What does ٱلْجِنِّ denote in this verse?  [ٱلْجِنِّ · referent]
turns on: Whether jinn names a hidden nonhuman kind or, in this context, previously unknown human strangers.
- 72:1/q19/p1 Hidden, rational, accountable beings, usually understood as subtle fiery or airy bodies distinct from humans and angels. — Tabrisi contrasts fire, human clay and angelic light; Baydawi, Biqai, Bursevi, Alusi and Kuranyolu describe intelligence, hiddenness, fire or air. Kuranyolu derives jinn from janna, to conceal, gives singular jinnī, and says Islamic sources class them as a third rational species; Alusi compares singular rūmī. Some can take shapes or be seen by prophets. Kuranyolu cites 55:15 and 51:56. [13 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 72:1/q19/p2 Jinn here may be strangers or beings previously unseen by the Qur'an's first audience. — Asad offers this tentative reading from 46:29-32 and considers them possibly Jews from Syria or Mesopotamia. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 72:1/q20 Do jinn exist externally, and what sort of beings could they be?  [ٱلْجِنِّ · theology]
turns on: Whether scriptural jinn are external beings, bodies, immaterial spirits, or a name without an external referent.
- 72:1/q20/p1 Jinn exist as bodily beings, subtle or even dense, whose different natures can sustain life and extraordinary acts. — Razi argues division into subtle and dense proves no common essence, as accident categories show; bodies may differ essentially despite shared space and dimensions. He permits life in an atom without bodily structure and unseen dense bodies. Alusi argues subtle fiery bodies could have distinct essences and modern philosophers posit remarkable subtle bodies. Qurtubi says all created things are composite and rejects physicians' denial; Kuranyolu describes fire-created conscious agents. Razi also reports and rejects the objection that dividing bodies into subtle and dense presupposes a shared essential bodily reality. [23 notes: RAZI-FULL(الفلاسفة,القائلون بالقول الثاني,بعض القائلين بالقول الثاني); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL+]
- 72:1/q20/p2 Jinn are independent noncorporeal lower spirits of many good and evil kinds, able to know, perceive and influence bodies. — Razi reports ancient spiritualists: lower spirits respond faster but are weaker than celestial spirits; independent substances attach first to air then dense bodies, analogized to the soul's fine vapor from the heart. Qashani portrays earthly souls in subtle elemental forms, some philosophers calling them suspended images; prophets and saints attest them. Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi report this theory. [16 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحاب الروحانيات,بعض المثبتين); BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI(بعض الحكماء); BURSEVI(القاشاني); ALUSI-FULL(الفلاسفة وأصحاب الروحانيات)]
- 72:1/q20/p3 Jinn are departed human souls that can attach to and help similar living souls. — Razi reports good helpers called angels and inspiration, evil helpers devils and whispering; Baydawi mentions separated souls. Alusi says this conflicts with the Salaf and the apparent Quran and hadith. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(من الناس); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 72:1/q20/p4 Many philosophers deny external jinn, treating Ibn Sina's airy shape-changing animal as a definition of a name. — Razi and Alusi distinguish Ibn Sina's nominal definition from affirmation of an entity; Bursevi reports broad philosophical denial. Alusi calls denial of external existence clear unbelief; Qurtubi rejects physicians who infer non-eating simple substances. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن سينا); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن سينا); QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 72:1/q21 Must bodily structure exist for jinn to live and act?  [ٱلْجِنِّ · theology]
turns on: Whether observed dependence of life on structure establishes rational necessity.
- 72:1/q21/p1 No; life and power can inhere in an individual atom without an organized body. — Ashari and most followers deny structure as a condition. Razi argues one life cannot inhere in many substrates; assigning lives to all dependent parts creates circular dependence, and miracles can break observed regularity. Thus God can grant an atom knowledge and power. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(الأشعري وجمهور أتباعه); RAZI-FULL+]
- 72:1/q21/p2 Yes; life disappears when bodily structure is broken and persists while it remains. — The Muʿtazilites infer necessity from observed destruction of living structure; Razi objects that observation cannot settle unobserved possibilities. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); RAZI-FULL-]

## 72:1/q22 How may present jinn remain unseen?  [ٱلْجِنِّ · theology]
turns on: Whether perception follows necessarily from sound senses and ordinary conditions.
- 72:1/q22/p1 Even a dense visible body need not be perceived when ordinary conditions for seeing are met. — Ashari allows nonperception; Razi cites distant objects appearing small despite equal conditions for all parts and circularity if seeing one part depends on another. He treats habit and rational necessity consistently and notes unseen powerful angels among people. Qurtubi says the Prophet could see jinn and angels in their own forms. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(الأشعري); QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 72:1/q22/p2 Adequate conditions necessarily bring perception of a present body. — Muʿtazilites argue that otherwise nearby drums and horns could pass unnoticed; Razi responds that their distinction between habit and necessity is arbitrary, and their account cannot explain unseen powerful beings. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(الأشعري,المعتزلة); RAZI-FULL-]

## 72:1/q23 How are jinn related to humans and devils, and what is their fate?  [ٱلْجِنِّ · theology]
turns on: Their ancestry from Iblis or al-Jann, and whether faith grants Paradise in either case.
- 72:1/q23/p1 Jinn descend from Iblis, as humans descend from Adam; both include believers and unbelievers with reward and punishment. — Hasan says believers of either race are God's allies and unbelievers are devils. Mawrdi and Qurtubi preserve his account; Kuranyolu stresses jinn's free will and accountability. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن البصري); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن البصري); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 72:1/q23/p2 Jinn descend from al-Jann and are distinct from Iblis's devils, which live until his death. — Ibn Abbas through Dahhak says jinn die and have believers and unbelievers, whereas devils descend from Iblis; on this view believers enter Paradise through faith. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 72:1/q23/p3 Believing jinn enter Paradise, even if they descend from Iblis. — Hasan states this for Iblis-descended jinn; Qurtubi cites 55:56 as establishing jinn in Paradise; Tustari reports jinn living, eating and drinking in its wild tracts and cites 55:74. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); TUSTARI]
- 72:1/q23/p4 Believing jinn descended from Iblis are spared Hell but do not enter Paradise. — Mujahid's view is preserved by Mawrdi and Qurtubi. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 72:1/q23/p5 Jinn eat and drink and are accountable to revelation. — Qurtubi infers eating from their request for bones and cites Quran and Sunna against denial; Tabatabai cites 51:56 for worship and their belief in 72:1-2; Tabrisi infers rationality, Arabic understanding, miracle recognition, moral accountability and missionary activity. ## 72:1/q24 What other powers, limits and appearances are attributed to jinn?  [ٱلْجِنِّ · theology]
- 72:1/q24/p1 They are shape-changing hidden agents with limited perception and power, and can interact with humans. — Kuranyolu says they reproduce, travel quickly, see humans, know some otherwise unknown things but not the unseen (34:14), cannot obtain heavenly angelic reports (15:18), and served Solomon (34:12-13). ## 72:1/q25 How have peoples understood jinn or spirit beings?  [ٱلْجِنِّ · theology]

## 72:1/q40 What other reports illustrate the Qur'an's wondrous guidance and merits?  [قُرْءَانًا عَجَبًۭا · reports]

## 72:1/q41 What jinn encounters or legal consequences are reported alongside this story?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the reason for warning snakes is local to Medina or applies wherever Muslim jinn may live.
- 72:1/q41/p1 Reports describe long-lived jinn believers and later conflict among believing and unbelieving jinn. — Tustari's Sahl saw a giant worshipper in an ancient Aad palace, whose robe lasted nine hundred years and who said unlawful food and sin wear bodies and clothes; Durr and Suyuti give a seven-hundred-year robe. Bursevi's traveler buried a wounded snake named Amr, said by two jinn women to be the last original listener, killed in jinn fighting; they offered compensation. [5 notes: TUSTARI(الجني); DURR-FULL(سهل بن عبد الله); SUYUTI-LUBAB(سهل بن عبد الله); BURSEVI(عيزار بن حريث)]
- 72:1/q41/p2 Bones and dung are jinn provisions and must not be used for cleansing. — Ibn Masud's recitation reports include a request for food and fodder; Qurtubi says law of water and cleansing materials was discussed under al-Hijr and al-Tawbah. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي,عامر الشعبي عن علقمة عن ابن مسعود)]
- 72:1/q41/p3 A house snake should be warned three times before being killed, because some are resident jinn; the rule applies beyond Medina. — Qurtubi cites a Muwatta report of a newly married man and a large snake and a sahih warning report; the Prophet calls a snake persisting after warning an unbeliever. Qurtubi rejects Medina-only restriction because the rationale is a snake's Islam and reports place jinn elsewhere. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الموطأ,النبي)]
- 72:1/q41/p4 The house-snake warning applies only in Medina. — An unnamed view cites a sahih report that Medina's jinn accepted Islam; Qurtubi rejects the geographic restriction. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL-]
