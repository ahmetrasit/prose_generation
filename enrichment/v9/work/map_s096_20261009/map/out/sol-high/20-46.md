# 20:46: 12 questions

## 20:46/q01 How does “He said” connect this answer to the preceding exchange?  [قَالَ · links]
- 20:46/q01/p1 It resumes the narrative after the messengers voiced their fear and introduces God's answer, before the renewed command to go to Pharaoh. — Bursevī calls it an answer to the implied question of God's reply; Ālūsī notes the shift from Moses' quoted speech (20:45); Ibn ʿĀshūr situates it after the signs and daunting mission and notes the subsequent renewed instruction. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:46/q02 What does “Do not fear” promise Moses and Aaron?  [لَا تَخَافَآ · interpretation]
- 20:46/q02/p1 Pharaoh will not kill or otherwise harm them before they deliver the message; God will protect and enable them. — Muqātil cites 28:35 for divine defence. Yaḥyā specifies safety until delivery. Ṭabarī adds help with answers. Qurṭubī reports that Pharaoh and his people cannot reach them. Rāzī and Ālūsī relate the reassurance to the feared haste and transgression of 20:45; Rāzī says it does not guarantee freedom from rejection or opposition to the signs. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it an indirect assertion that neither feared outcome will occur. Bursevī calls it consolation and a promise of aid; the Mathnawī couplet says reassurance nourishes the fearful. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God will not abandon them. [10 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(العلماء); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI(المثنوي); TABATABAI]

## 20:46/q03 How do Arabic warning formulas leave their commands unspoken?  [لَا تَخَافَآ · grammar]
- 20:46/q03/p1 Frequent warning formulas can suppress an understood command, with pronouns, paired nouns, or verbal nouns supplying the warning. — Sībawayh gives إياك for اتق or باعد, while in نفسك the suppressed verb cannot be expressed. In إياك والأسد both are objects, with واو joining the warned objects. إياك إياه and the reply إياي likewise imply guarding oneself. رأسه والحائط can take واو as مع or as coordination of two omitted commands, both good Arabic; paired warnings omit verbs through context and usage, while رأسك may retain an imperative. الحذر الحذر and النجاء النجاء stand for الزم الحذر and عليك النجاء. Poems by ʿAmr ibn Maʿdīkarib, al-Kumayt and Dhū al-Iṣbaʿ attest such idioms. [8 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 20:46/q04 Is natural fear compatible with prophethood and trust in God?  [لَا تَخَافَآ · theology]
turns on: Whether the reassurance describes ordinary human response or God's creative removal of fear.
- 20:46/q04/p1 Yes; prophets and the faithful can naturally fear harm while trusting God, so the prohibition consoles rather than requiring a voluntary suppression of human fear. — Qurṭubī's scholars reject the claim that prophets do not fear. Qurṭubī cites Moses' fear in Midian and before the magicians (28:20–21, 28:18, 20:67–68), the Prophet's trench and the Companions' migration; Asmāʾ bint ʿUmays' report in Muslim recalls the Abyssinian emigrants' persecution and fear. His scholars say denial of natural fear is false and picture an unarmed person facing a lion. Al-Ḥasan reports ʿĀmir ibn ʿAbd Allāh facing a lion while professing fear of God alone, an example answered by Moses' fear. Bursevī says natural fear is outside choice and cites the Mathnawī's fearful person who receives reassurance. [10 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أسماء بنت عميس,الحسن البصري,العلماء,مسلم); BURSEVI(المثنوي)]
- 20:46/q04/p2 God can remove fear from human nature through a creative command. — Bursevī compares God's making the fire cool and safe for Abraham in 21:69. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 20:46/q05 What does God's being “with you both” mean?  [مَعَكُمَآ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether مع chiefly denotes special protective aid or God's knowing and witnessing presence; notes also rule out a bodily location.
- 20:46/q05/p1 God protects, supports, and gives Moses and Aaron victory over Pharaoh. — Tustarī and Sulamī's Sahl add constant watching and witnessing, power, help and confirmation. Ṭabarī adds supplying answers. Wāḥidī says the promise responds to fear. Jishumī says they may deliver confidently despite Pharaoh's tyranny and excludes bodily spatial presence; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī compare a prince's protective 'being with' someone. Ṭabrisī links protection to 28:35. Rāzī notes the idiom can be a prayer. Zamakhsharī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say vigilant protection makes fear needless. Biqāʿī contrasts kings absent from envoys and speaks of protecting allies and destroying enemies. Bursevī's Ibn al-Shaykh also excludes physical location. Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses care beyond universal presence and knowledge. Burhān gives aid as one sense of مع and cites 9:40, 3:76, 57:4, 9:123, 26:62; its inward usage can mark shared quality, belief or state (66:8). [33 notes: TUSTARI; TAB-FULL; SULAMI(سهل); THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ)]
- 20:46/q05/p2 God is with them by knowing, seeing, and possessing power over all that they and Pharaoh say and do. — Jishumī reports this as an alternative, including both their and Pharaoh's speech and acts. Burhān says divine مع can express knowledge and witnessing and cites 57:4 among its comparisons. Wāḥidī says God is not unaware of them, removing anxiety. [3 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); BURHAN; WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 20:46/q06 Why did the messengers' fear draw this assurance?  [إِنَّنِى مَعَكُمَآ · interpretation]
- 20:46/q06/p1 They sought to hear God's promise; His assurance calmed and empowered them for the mission. — Qushayrī says prophecy should remove fear, so their voiced fear aimed to elicit 'I am with you'; effective obligation requires empowering the two to go to Pharaoh. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 20:46/q07 Whose words and acts does God hear and see here?  [أَسْمَعُ وَأَرَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Which implicit objects, if any, the unqualified verbs evoke, and whether the point is specific surveillance of Pharaoh or unrestricted vigilant care.
- 20:46/q07/p1 He hears and sees all speech, action, and circumstances between the two messengers and Pharaoh, leaving nothing hidden and enabling protection. — Ṭabarī and Ḥajjāj include the exchange and revelation of answers; Thaʿlabī includes both sides' speech and everyone's acts. Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr tie comprehensive perception to repelling harm and granting good. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī stress nothing is hidden. Ibn Kathīr adds that Pharaoh's forelock is controlled by God and that he cannot speak, breathe or strike without permission. Biqāʿī includes both messengers and their enemy; Bursevī's Ibn al-Shaykh says this makes one with God secure against a tyrant. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(حجاج); THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:46/q07/p2 He hears the two messengers' words or prayer and sees their actions. — Ibn al-Jawzī, Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān pair their words with their actions; Samīn reports these as possible omitted objects. Ibn ʿAbbās in Wāḥidī, Baghawī and Nasafī specifies hearing and answering their prayer, while seeing and preventing hostile intentions toward them. [7 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 20:46/q07/p3 He hears Pharaoh's reply or questions and sees Pharaoh's deeds or designs against the messengers. — Muqātil treats seeing as knowing what Pharaoh says and compares God's showing in 4:105 with making known. Wāḥidī al-Wajīz and al-Kalbī pair Pharaoh's speech and deeds; Ibn ʿAbbās as reported by Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn does likewise, the former calling both knowledge. Ṭabrisī says God inspires an answer and averts Pharaoh's intentions. Ibn Jurayj, reported by Jishumī and al-Durr, says God reveals their reply or guidance. Al-Qaffāl, reported by Rāzī and Ālūsī, connects hearing to Pharaoh's not listening and seeing to his murderous transgression; Ālūsī finds his object estimate unpersuasive. [11 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن جريج); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الكلبي); RAZI-FULL(القفال); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); ALUSI-FULL(القفال); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:46/q07/p4 The verbs characterize God generally as a watchful, capable hearer and seer, without limiting their objects. — Zamakhsharī offers this alongside explicit objects; Bayḍāwī calls Him a capable protector. Rāzī says a guardian who might miss harm differs from God. Ibn ʿĀshūr says no feared word or act escapes divine knowledge. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشرى)]

## 20:46/q08 How does the lack of stated objects after “I hear and see” work?  [أَسْمَعُ وَأَرَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether context supplies particular suppressed objects or the verbs are used absolutely to affirm vigilance.
- 20:46/q08/p1 The objects are omitted but understood from the scene: speech and acts between the messengers and Pharaoh. — Zamakhsharī offers their words and actions as explicit semantic objects; Samīn reports 'your words' and 'your actions' or Pharaoh's answer and deeds. Ālūsī estimates whatever is said or done between them and Pharaoh. Al-Qaffāl's narrower estimate connects the objects to Pharaoh's refusal to listen and transgression; Ālūsī rejects that estimate. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(القفال); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:46/q08/p2 The verbs function without specified objects, expressing God's unrestricted hearing, seeing, and protective capacity. — Zamakhsharī gives the objectless reading; Samīn compares economy of ellipsis to يحيي ويميت in 3:156. Ālūsī reports Zamakhsharī and says the transitive verbs function intransitively; he rejects al-Tībī's comparison with al-Mutanabbī because this usage completes the meaning of protection and aid. Ibn ʿĀshūr says objects need not be stated because nothing escapes God. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشرى); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 20:46/q09 Why is hearing mentioned before sight?  [أَسْمَعُ وَأَرَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the order stresses revelation's dependence on hearing or the felt nearness conveyed by hearing.
- 20:46/q09/p1 Hearing is prior because receiving revelation depends especially on it. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī favors this: a blind person can understand a message, whereas informing a deaf person is harder; prophets were not deaf although one could be blind. He cites 76:2 and 25:73. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH+]
- 20:46/q09/p2 Hearing ordinarily has a shorter range than sight and thus conveys nearness in God's reassurance. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī offers this as another possible rationale in three notes; sight can reach farther. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 20:46/q10 Are God's hearing and sight distinct from His knowledge?  [أَسْمَعُ وَأَرَىٰ · theology]
turns on: Whether 'with you' already asserts knowledge and makes the two later verbs redundant unless they name distinct attributes; Ṭabāṭabāʾī disputes both the premise and its doctrinal force.
- 20:46/q10/p1 Yes; hearing and sight are additional divine attributes, not reducible to knowledge. — Jishumī's teachers hold this against the Baghdadi school; Jishumī adds that being alive without defect grounds hearing and sight, while actual sensory perception occurs when objects exist. Rāzī and Ālūsī argue that 'I am with you' already denotes knowledge, so hearing and sight must add something. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this redundancy argument as the view of some. [5 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); JISHUMI(مشايخنا)+; TABATABAI-]
- 20:46/q10/p2 Hearing and sight reduce to God's knowledge, or His essential attributes are identical with one another and His essence. — Jishumī reports the Baghdadi school's reduction to knowledge. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says 'with you' denotes presence and witnessing rather than knowledge, so the redundancy proof is weak; certain proofs establish identity of essential attributes and essence, and speculative wording cannot override certainty in doctrine. Abū Ḥayyān reports Ibn ʿAbbās taking hearing and sight here as knowledge; Muqātil explains seeing as knowing Pharaoh's words and compares 4:105. [6 notes: JISHUMI(البغدادية); TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); MUQATIL]

## 20:46/q11 What additional reports or examples accompany this reassurance?  [* · reports]
turns on: These are separate reports and examples with different actors and episodes.
- 20:46/q11/p1 God taught Moses a phrase to say to Pharaoh, glossed as the Living before and after everything. — Ibn Kathīr reports ʿAbd Allāh's 'هيا شراهيا' and calls the chain good but the report unusual; al-Durr reports Ibn Masʿūd's variant 'Ahyā shirā hyā.' Both attribute the gloss to al-Aʿmash; the phrase's rendering varies. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 20:46/q11/p2 Pharaoh's clothing and power need not frighten Moses and Aaron, since Pharaoh cannot act except by God's permission. — Ibn ʿAbbās' report says Pharaoh cannot speak or blink without permission. God could have adorned the two messengers more richly; their scant worldly share does not lessen their allotment. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 20:46/q11/p3 God guards His friends from worldly danger and avenges those who intimidate them. — Ibn ʿAbbās' report compares God to a shepherd steering animals from harmful places so their hearts become illuminated and pure; one who frightens God's friend challenges Him and will face vengeance on Judgment Day. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 20:46/q11/p4 A young imprisoned preacher escaped a sealed room and credited the same divine agent who placed him there with bringing him out. — Bursevī says al-Rashīd found him in a garden afterward, wept, and honored him. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 20:46/q12 How does this reassurance fit the joint mission of Moses and Aaron?  [* · links]
- 20:46/q12/p1 The reassurance addresses both as partners in conveying God's message to Pharaoh. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī traces their joint sending, joint expression of fear, shared reassurance, delivery and Pharaoh's address to both, citing 20:29, 20:42, 20:63, 20:90 and 20:92. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
