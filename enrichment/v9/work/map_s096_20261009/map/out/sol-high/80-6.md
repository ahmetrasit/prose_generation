# 80:6: 11 questions

## 80:6/q01 Who is the “you” who attends to him?  [فَأَنتَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the verses identify the actor as the Prophet, and whether the reported conduct accords with his praised character and earlier commands.
- 80:6/q01/p1 The Prophet is directly addressed as the one attending to the self-sufficient man. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Biqāʿī, and others describe the Prophet turning toward the man. Ṭabāṭabāʾī's discussion of 80:5–12 also describes the rebuked pattern as the Prophet attending to wealthy people. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI(المرتضى)-]
- 80:6/q01/p2 The censured actor is unnamed in the verses; a report identifies an Umayyad man in the Prophet's company. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the passage never identifies the Prophet as the one who frowned and turned away. He cites al-Ṣādiq's report of an Umayyad man recoiling when Ibn Umm Maktūm arrived and al-Murtaḍā's view that such treatment of the poor does not resemble the Prophet. He invokes 68:4, its early revelation, commands to warn kin and be gentle to believers (26:214–15), and 15:88, 15:94; reason itself judges the conduct ugly even before a prohibition. [8 notes: TABATABAI(الصادق,المرتضى); TABATABAI+]

## 80:6/q02 What does the explicit and fronted “you” emphasize?  [فَأَنتَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Ibn ʿĀshūr offers either intensification of the action or restriction of its blame to the Prophet's station.
- 80:6/q02/p1 It sharpens the direct rebuke and emphasizes the intensity of the attention. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the explicit subject pronoun intensifies the Prophet's forceful attention and the reproach. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says fronted anta and the pronouns lahu and ʿanhu fix and emphasize the rebuke; he rejects an honorific account of the change from third to second person because the subsequent direct address is stronger and lacks conciliatory intimacy. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 80:6/q02/p2 It restricts the action to the Prophet, for whom it is unbecoming because of his high rank. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives this as an alternative: the same degree of attention would be less blameworthy from another person. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 80:6/q03 Who is “him”?  [لَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: The notes variously leave the wealthy addressee generic, name al-ʿAbbās, or situate him among named Quraysh chiefs.
- 80:6/q03/p1 The self-sufficient wealthy man, understood as a pagan leader whom the Prophet hoped to guide. — Al-Ṭabarī links the man's self-sufficiency to wealth and the Prophet's hope for his Islam. Al-Biqāʿī says the Prophet hoped the man and his followers would convert; al-Kur'an Yolu calls him one of the wealthy pagan leaders. Al-Thaʿlabī places the exchange before Quraysh leaders. [12 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 80:6/q03/p2 Al-ʿAbbās is the man concerned. — Al-Ṭabarī transmits Sufyān's report that the passage was revealed concerning al-ʿAbbās. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(سفيان)]
- 80:6/q03/p3 The chiefs included ʿUtba, Abū Jahl, Ubayy, and Umayya ibn Khalaf. — Al-Biqāʿī lists these chiefs when explaining the Prophet's hope that the wealthy man and his followers would convert; the account concerns a group rather than a single identified ‘him’. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 80:6/q04 What does taṣaddā mean here?  [تَصَدَّىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the echo derivation yields the narrower action of addressing him, instead of the common sense of turning toward and attending to him.
- 80:6/q04/p1 To turn toward, present oneself to, approach, or attend closely to someone, including listening to him. — Abū ʿUbayda glosses it as taʿarruḍ; al-Fārisī cites Dhū al-Rumma's mare turning toward her mate, while Abū ʿUbayda explains al-qalwah as the mare following the colt. Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Qushayrī specify turning one's face; Ibn Qutayba says making oneself available. Al-Rāʿī's and the anonymous poems illustrate leaning or turning toward a radiant-faced person. Al-Zajjāj, as cited by al-Rāzī, connects the sense to what faces one and mentions 8:35. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the attention metaphorically intense; al-Burṣawī adds preoccupation and its opposite, turning away. Jishumī's reported gloss includes listening to the wealthy person while turning away from the poor. [29 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عبيدة,ذو الرمة); QUSHAYRI; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الراعي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الراعي); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 80:6/q04/p2 To address or speak to him. — Jishumī reports this as an alternative derived from ṣadā, the echo. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]

## 80:6/q05 What root or image explains taṣaddā?  [تَصَدَّىٰ · meaning]
turns on: The sources propose the image of facing, thirst, or echo; al-Samīn prefers facing because he finds the other derivations strained.
- 80:6/q05/p1 It comes from ṣadad, something facing or standing opposite another. — Al-Qurṭubī illustrates this with one house directly opposite another. Al-Samīn says the usual derivation starts from taṣaddad, with one identical consonant changed to a weak letter to avoid gemination, comparing taẓannayt, qaṣṣayt aẓfārī, and poetic taqaḍḍī. Al-Burṣawī reports a similar account; al-Iṣlāḥī says the morphological change is regular. Al-Zajjāj, cited by al-Rāzī, says the root evokes what stands before one and mentions 8:35. Al-Ālūsī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Elmalılı also report this root. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 80:6/q05/p2 It evokes ṣadā, thirst: one exposes oneself eagerly as a thirsty person seeks water. — Jishumī likens it to a thirsty person's approach to water; al-Qurṭubī also notes muṣādāh as opposition. Al-Mufradāt, cited by al-Burṣawī, uses the thirsty person's eagerness. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī list it as a possibility. Al-Samīn says deriving the exposure sense from thirst requires forcing it. [5 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(المفردات); ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 80:6/q05/p3 It evokes ṣadā, an echo: speech or a response is presented back or awaited. — Jishumī says the echo's name may reflect dryness like a thirsty throat or its seeming to present speech by repeating it; he reports the gloss ‘address him’. Elmalılı relays Bahr's image of waiting eagerly for a response like an echo from distant mountains. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī list echo as a possible root. Al-Samīn describes the sound heard in empty places and hard objects but calls the derivation of exposure strained. [5 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 80:6/q06 How is taṣaddā read and formed?  [تَصَدَّىٰ · readings]
turns on: Whether the original two tāʾs are reduced by deletion or assimilation, retained, or the verb is read with a prefixed ḍamma; transmitters' attributions differ.
- 80:6/q06/p1 A light ṣād in taṣaddā results from dropping one of the two tāʾs of tataṣaddā. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Naḥḥās, al-Fārisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, and others explain deletion as easing the meeting of two tāʾs. Attributions include Kūfans and Abū ʿAmr, the remaining readers besides Ibn Kathīr and Nāfiʿ, and specifically ʿĀṣim, Abū ʿAmr, Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamza, and al-Kisāʾī. Al-Biqāʿī says the light form suggests the action was slight; Elmalılı identifies this and talahhā as shortened second-person present verbs. Al-Farrāʾ accepts a recitation of تصدى without specifying its vocalization. [20 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الكوفيون وأبو عمرو); FARISI-HUJJA(الباقون); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ونافع وابن كثير); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل الحجاز، والآخرون); TABRISI(أهل الحجاز); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج,عاصم وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحرميان وأبو عمرو); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الباقون); FARRA+]
- 80:6/q06/p2 A doubled ṣād assimilates a tāʾ of tataṣaddā into ṣād. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Fārisī, and Ibn al-Jawzī explain assimilation, the latter citing closeness of articulation; Qirāʾāt-ER says it intensifies the onset and compresses the underlying sequence. Attributions vary: Ibn Kathīr and Nāfiʿ; the Hijaz readers; Abū Jaʿfar, Nāfiʿ, and Ibn Kathīr; Nāfiʿ and Ibn Muḥayṣin; or the two sanctuary readers. Abū Ḥayyān says the two Medinan readers and Abū ʿAmr, with qualification, double zāy and ṣād in their respective words. [21 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(المدنيون); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ونافع وابن كثير); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل الحجاز، والآخرون); TABRISI(أهل الحجاز); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير ونافع); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(نافع وابن محيصن); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن كثير ونافع); NASAFI-FULL(حجازى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحرميان وأبو عمرو); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الحرميان); IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وابن كثير وأبو جعفر); QIRAAT-ER(Assim)]
- 80:6/q06/p3 Tataṣaddā is read with both original tāʾs and a light ṣād. — Ibn al-Jawzī attributes this reading to Ubayy ibn Kaʿb, Abū al-Jawzāʾ, and ʿAmr ibn Dīnār. The two-tāʾ form is also the underlying form proposed by al-Zajjāj and others for the shortened and assimilated readings. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبي بن كعب وأبو الجوزاء وعمرو بن دينار)]
- 80:6/q06/p4 A reading begins with ḍamma on a single tāʾ and a light ṣād, allowing the sense ‘you are prompted or directed to attend to him.’ — Al-Ṭabrisī attributes the ḍamma in this word and talahhā to Abū Jaʿfar; Ibn al-Jawzī attributes the form to Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn al-Sumayfiʿ, and al-Jaḥdarī. Al-Rāzī, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī attribute it to Abū Jaʿfar and explain the inner prompting as zeal for the man's conversion. Al-Samīn says the verb works both transitively and intransitively; al-Bayḍāwī allows either active turning or being summoned, while al-Ālūsī calls it passive. Al-Ṭabrisī also reports an interpretation involving worldly adornment and tidings drawing attention to the wealthy, contrasted with talahhā as turning from the blind man. [8 notes: TABRISI(أبو جعفر الباقر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود وابن السميفع والجحدري); RAZI-FULL(أبو جعفر); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو جعفر,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو جعفر)]

## 80:6/q07 How does this sentence sit between the two “as for” clauses?  [* · grammar]
- 80:6/q07/p1 It is parenthetical, and its wāw is parenthetical, between the self-sufficient man's clause and the eager visitor's clause. — Ibn ʿĀshūr locates the clauses at 80:5 and 80:8. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 80:6/q08 What does the conditional “as for” framing imply about this attention?  [* · rhetoric]
- 80:6/q08/p1 Whenever a self-sufficient person appeared, the Prophet would turn toward him; the conditional form exaggerates the persistence of that attention. — Ibn ʿĀshūr glosses ammā as ‘whatever may occur’ and makes its attachment to this clause a means of emphasis. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 80:6/q09 What happened in the encounter behind this attention?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ over the named person and, especially, who performed the censured conduct.
- 80:6/q09/p1 The Prophet attended to Quraysh leaders in hope of their conversion while Ibn Umm Maktūm repeatedly sought instruction, and turned away from him. — Al-Thaʿlabī says the Prophet thought the elite might reject a following made up of blind, poor, and enslaved people. Al-Biqāʿī says he hoped the wealthy man and his followers would convert and names ʿUtba, Abū Jahl, Ubayy, and Umayya ibn Khalaf among the chiefs. Ibn Kathīr says the Prophet hoped for guidance but was not accountable if the rich man failed to purify himself. [3 notes: THALABI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 80:6/q09/p2 Sufyān reported that the passage was revealed concerning al-ʿAbbās. — Al-Ṭabarī transmits Sufyān's report without further details in this note. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(سفيان)]
- 80:6/q09/p3 An Umayyad man with the Prophet recoiled, frowned, and turned away when Ibn Umm Maktūm came. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī quotes a report from al-Ṣādiq in al-Majmaʿ, according to which God recounted and condemned the Umayyad man's act. [1 notes: TABATABAI(الصادق)]

## 80:6/q10 What guidance or rebuke does this attention convey?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes differ on whom the rebuke ultimately targets and whether the alleged conduct can be attributed to the Prophet; they also stress different practical lessons.
- 80:6/q10/p1 The Prophet should honor and teach receptive poor seekers and warn everyone equally instead of favoring wealthy or eminent people. — Abū ʿUthmān, cited by al-Sulamī, says God commanded companionship with and honor for the poor and forbade love of the wealthy. Al-Qushayrī calls the passage a reproach on behalf of the poor. Ibn Kathīr includes eminent and weak, rich and poor, masters and slaves, men and women, adults and children; God guides whom He wills. Al-Iṣlāḥī says the Prophet must teach eager seekers and is not accountable for those who refuse to come. Ṭabāṭabāʾī's 80:5–12 reading contrasts scornful wealthy people unconcerned with purification and an earnest God-fearing seeker. [7 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان); QUSHAYRI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]
- 80:6/q10/p2 Pursuing a wealthy person who turns away is improper, especially given the Prophet's high station. — Al-Burṣawī says a noble person does not eagerly approach one who turns away. Al-Ālūsī likewise cites a verse about the impropriety of seeking the company of one who rejects yours. Al-Biqāʿī says luxury and self-sufficiency predispose rejection and wrongdoing, whereas affliction can humble and soften hearts. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 80:6/q10/p3 The direct rebuke ultimately targets haughty leaders who ignore the message and seek exclusion of the Prophet's poor companions. — Al-Iṣlāḥī says those leaders wanted the poor companions removed as a condition of their own attendance, even though the Prophet is the person addressed. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 80:6/q10/p4 Attributing disdain for the believing poor to the Prophet is untenable, whether or not a new prohibition had been revealed. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the excuse that this became sinful only afterward: reason already condemns preferring wealth over piety, and 68:4 praises the Prophet's character. He and al-Murtaḍā invoke that character; Ṭabāṭabāʾī further cites earlier commands at 26:214–15 and 15:88, 15:94. [7 notes: TABATABAI(المرتضى); TABATABAI+]

## 80:6/q11 How does this contrast fit the sūra and its neighboring sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The two notes identify different aspects of the sūra's connection with al-Nāziʿāt.
- 80:6/q11/p1 The sūra's call to warn those who fear is developed through resurrection, human creation, and food as signs of God's power; receptivity explains the contrast. — Al-Biqāʿī relates the warning of those who fear in 79:45 to purification of receptive hearts and finds rejection astonishing in light of the signs. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 80:6/q11/p2 ʿAbasa pairs with al-Nāziʿāt in theme, discourse style, and reasoning, while changing the order of topics. — Al-Iṣlāḥī cites 79:45 and says the altered sequence gives this sūra its distinct majesty. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
