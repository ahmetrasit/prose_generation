# 3:37: 47 questions

## 3:37/q01 What did God’s good acceptance of Mary mean?  [فَتَقَبَّلَهَا بِقَبُولٍ حَسَنٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether acceptance concerns the female sanctuary vow, God’s lifelong care, immediate reception at birth, or protection and guidance.
- 3:37/q01/p1 God accepted the vowed girl for sanctuary service in place of the expected boy. — Ṭabarī, Zajjāj, Thaʿlabī, and later exegetes say girls had not previously been accepted for this role; Ibn ʿĀshūr connects the exception to later legal change. [30 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(شرحبيل بن سعد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); MAJAZ]
- 3:37/q01/p2 God guided, purified, protected, and favored Mary. — Ibn ʿAbbās says she was set on the path of the blessed; al-Ḥasan says she was never punished or afflicted; later readers link acceptance to 3:36 and 3:42. [16 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس,الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن); QUSHAYRI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); STUDYQURAN; SULAMI(الواسطي,جعفر)]
- 3:37/q01/p3 God undertook Mary’s upbringing and care. — Al-Ḥasan and others gloss acceptance as divine guardianship; Tustarī reads it as emancipation from ego and worldliness. [10 notes: TUSTARI; THALABI(بعضهم); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قوم); BURSEVI; QUSHAYRI]
- 3:37/q01/p4 God received Mary from the outset of her life, before she could serve. — Zamakhsharī cites al-Qaṭāmī and the proverb about beginnings; Bayḍāwī and Abū Ḥayyān explain taqabbala through istaqbala. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 3:37/q01/p5 God accepted the mother’s prayer for protection or arranged Mary’s guardianship. — The Turkish commentary gives both interpretations; the first invokes protection from Satan, the second the contest narrated in 3:44. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); RAZI-FULL(امرأة عمران)]

## 3:37/q02 Who does the pronoun in 'her Lord' refer to?  [رَبُّهَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun points to Mary or to her mother Hanna.
- 3:37/q02/p1 Mary: her Lord brought her to her proper perfection. — Al-Ālūsī prefers this antecedent. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]
- 3:37/q02/p2 Hanna: the Lord addressed by the mother’s prayer. — Al-Ālūsī reports the alternative because Hanna had just spoken. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 3:37/q03 How is 'with good acceptance' constructed?  [بِقَبُولٍ حَسَنٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether qabūl is an unmatched verbal noun, an implied verb’s noun, a means or quality of acceptance, or whether the bāʾ is extra.
- 3:37/q03/p1 Qabūl is a verbal noun used for taqabbul because the two verbs share the meaning of acceptance. — Ṭabarī compares takallama kalāman; Zajjāj gives the expected taqabbulan; Naḥḥās, Wāḥidī, Rāzī, and others cite parallel poetry and meaning-based cognate objects. [22 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الشاعر,القطامي,امرؤ القيس,رؤبة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN]
- 3:37/q03/p2 Qabūl names the means or quality by which she was accepted. — Zamakhsharī and Nasafī identify special selection; Abū Ḥayyān compares instrument nouns; Bayḍāwī and Nasafī also supply an omitted 'possessor of'. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 3:37/q03/p3 The bāʾ is extra and qabūl is an emphatic cognate object. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the added bāʾ makes acceptance seem an instrument and emphasizes care; al-Ālūsī says the unusual noun conveys complete approval. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 3:37/q03/p4 An implied matching verb governs the verbal noun. — Zajjāj proposes an estimated acceptance verb, but al-Samīn calls the analysis strained. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج)]

## 3:37/q04 How is qabūl pronounced?  [بِقَبُولٍ حَسَنٍۢ · readings]
turns on: Whether its first consonant takes fatḥa as attested or ḍamma by analogy.
- 3:37/q04/p1 With fatḥa on qāf. — Abū ʿAmr says he had not heard the ḍamma and finds this open faʿūl verbal noun exceptional; Sībawayh lists parallels, though authorities vary in their five examples. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو عمرو بن العلاء); THALABI(أبو عمرو الكسائي والأئمة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عمرو بن العلاء,سيبويه); JISHUMI(أبو عمرو بن العلاء,سيبويه); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(أبو عمرو,سيبويه); RAZI-FULL(سيبويه); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو والكسائي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن الأعرابي)]
- 3:37/q04/p2 With ḍamma on qāf also. — Farrāʾ, Zajjāj, Kisāʾī, Ibn al-Aʿrābī and others allow the regular analogy with dukhūl and khurūj; al-Samīn cites a poetic example. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج,ثعلب عن ابن الأعرابي); JISHUMI(الكسائي); TABRISI(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الفراء والزجاج وثعلب عن ابن الأعرابي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو والكسائي,الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن الأعرابي,الفراء والزجاج)]

## 3:37/q05 What does the wording of divine acceptance suggest?  [فَتَقَبَّلَهَا بِقَبُولٍ حَسَنٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 3:37/q05/p1 It depicts special, sustained and effortless divine care. — Rāzī distinguishes the effort suggested by taqabbul from the unforced qabūl, applying both metaphorically to God; Biqāʿī cites al-Ḥarallī on successive stages of blessing. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 3:37/q06 What is Mary's 'good growth'?  [وَأَنۢبَتَهَا نَبَاتًا حَسَنًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase emphasizes bodily growth, religious and moral formation, both, or Jesus as its fruit.
- 3:37/q06/p1 Righteous worship, obedience, knowledge, and purity. — Tustarī specifies remembrance, bodily service, and knowledge of the heart; Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda and others emphasize chastity and freedom from sin; some report her precocious fasting and prayer at nine. [19 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; THALABI(ابن عباس); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,قيل); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI]
- 3:37/q06/p2 Sound and unusually rapid bodily development. — Ibn ʿAbbās says she grew in a day as others grow in a year, without excess or deficiency; later sources contrast literal biology with a figurative educational reading. [18 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); THALABI(ابن جريج,ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن جريج,ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bazı müfessirler)]
- 3:37/q06/p3 Excellent upbringing in every physical and moral circumstance. — Zamakhsharī calls growth a metaphor; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān include body and character; Kuran Yolu prefers attentive education and care over exceptional biological growth. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Reşîd Rızâ,Zemahşerî); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 3:37/q06/p4 Her growth culminated in Jesus as its remarkable fruit. — Qushayrī and Ibn ʿAṭāʾ use a fruit image; Kuran Yolu records the explanation that her growth means becoming a prophet’s mother. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عطاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bazı müfessirler); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]

## 3:37/q07 How does nabātan relate grammatically to anbatahā?  [وَأَنۢبَتَهَا نَبَاتًا حَسَنًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether it is a meaning-based cognate object or depends on an omitted verb.
- 3:37/q07/p1 It is a cognate object by meaning although the matching form would be inbātan. — Zajjāj, Naḥḥās, Wāḥidī and others say causative 'made grow' contains 'grew'; poetry by Imruʾ al-Qays and other witnesses illustrates the shift. [21 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري,الزجاج); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج، ابن الأنباري,امرؤ القيس); QURTUBI-FULL(الشاعر,القطامي,المفضل,امرؤ القيس,رؤبة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 3:37/q07/p2 An omitted verb 'she grew' governs nabātan. — Ibn al-Anbārī and later grammarians estimate fa-nabatat nabātan ḥasanan. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن الأنباري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]

## 3:37/q08 Why say nabātan rather than inbātan?  [نَبَاتًا حَسَنًۭا · rhetoric]
- 3:37/q08/p1 The noun also credits Mary’s receptive, willing growth. — Sāmarrāʾī says inbātan would attribute the process solely to God; nabātan presents Mary responding and flourishing. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 3:37/q08/p2 The paired forms distinguish hidden nurturing from visible bodily completion. — Biqāʿī transmits al-Ḥarallī’s reading of the form-IV verb and the growth noun. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]

## 3:37/q09 How is the sequence of the three verbs read?  [فَتَقَبَّلَهَا وَأَنۢبَتَهَا وَكَفَّلَهَا · readings]
turns on: Past narration versus a supplication voiced by Mary's mother.
- 3:37/q09/p1 Mujāhid reads imperatives as Hanna’s prayer: accept, nurture, and appoint Zakariya. — Naḥḥās parses rabbahā as a vocative; al-Ālūsī says God answered all three requests. [11 notes: NAHHAS(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير; نافع; أبو عمرو; ابن عامر; عاصم; حمزة; الكسائي; أبي بن كعب; مجاهد; المزني); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); SAMIN-DURR(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 3:37/q09/p2 Past verbs narrate God’s acceptance, growth, and appointment. — The majority read taqabbalahā with Rabb as subject; the common guardianship readings differ in agency at the third verb. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 3:37/q10 What does kafala mean here?  [وَكَفَّلَهَا · meaning]
- 3:37/q10/p1 Taking responsibility for Mary’s care, maintenance, and protection. — Abū ʿUbayda, Zajjāj, Rāzī, and others define a kāfil as the one who supports and guards; Ṭabarī cites animal-care verses and examples; legal guarantee is its root sense. ## 3:37/q12 How is Zakariya's name read and inflected?  [زَكَرِيَّا · readings]

## 3:37/q13 Why is the name Zakariya indeclinable in its long and short forms?  [زَكَرِيَّا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the operative cause is the fused feminine alif or foreign proper-name status.
- 3:37/q13/p1 Its fused final feminine alif prevents declension even when indefinite. — Zajjāj, Naḥḥās and Fārisī distinguish alif from removable feminine tāʾ; indefinite indeclinability is their evidence against Abū Ḥātim. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو حاتم); FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو علي; الزجاج; أبو حاتم; مكي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حاتم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو علي); SAMIN-DURR(أبو علي الفارسي)]
- 3:37/q13/p2 It is indeclinable because it is a foreign proper name. — Ibn Khalawayh gives this rule; Zajjāj and Naḥḥās report and dispute the explanation, especially for the indefinite form. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ(بعض النحويين); NAHHAS; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; ALUSI-FULL]
