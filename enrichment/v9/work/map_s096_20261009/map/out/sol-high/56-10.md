# 56:10: 14 questions

## 56:10/q01 What does sabq literally and figuratively mean?  [ٱلسَّٰبِقُونَ · meaning]
- 56:10/q01/p1 Sabq literally means going or reaching ahead in travel or a race, then extends to precedence in rank, good, and reward. — Al-Bursevī says the travel sense is primary; al-Rāghib calls attaining excellence a borrowing. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites al-Nābigha's image of a swift horse reaching the hunt before the others to explain rank precedence. The Study Quran gives go first, outstrip, and win a race. Al-Iṣlāḥī rejects reading the class names as spatial positions before God. [8 notes: BURSEVI(الراغب); IBNASHUR-FULL(النابغة); STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 56:10/q02 Who are the forerunners, and in what do they precede others?  [ٱلسَّٰبِقُونَ · referent]

- 56:10/q02/p1 They are the first to believe in God and respond to the prophets, among every community or the earliest Muslims. — Qatāda and Ibn ʿAbbās say every nation contributes forerunners; al-Wāḥidī cites 9:100 for Ibn ʿAbbās's explanation. Muqātil and ʿIkrima stress immediate response when truth appears; al-Māwardī specifies Islam before Muhammad's emigration or arrival, before hope or fear could move converts. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the class continues into Muhammad's community. Al-Iṣlāḥī stresses early supporters' costly service with person and wealth and cites 57:10: pre-conquest spending and fighting ranked higher, though both groups receive good reward. Al-Sulamī differentiates prophets first in faith in God from truthful persons and martyrs first in faith in prophets. ## 56:10/q03 How does the repetition of al-sābiqūn work?  [وَٱلسَّٰبِقُونَ ٱلسَّٰبِقُونَ · grammar]
turns on: The disputed syntax is subject and predicate, emphatic repetition with a following predicate, adjective plus predicate, or an ellipsis; the subject-predicate parse may praise renowned rank or assign different objects to each occurrence.
- 56:10/q03/p1 The first al-sābiqūn is the subject and the second its predicate, acclaiming a renowned rank beyond fuller description. — Sībawayh's analysis is preferred by Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī prefer this over emphasis. Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Samīn, and others compare Abū al-Najm's 'my poetry is my poetry' to repeated renown. Al-Rāzī says their state surpasses knowledge; al-Qāshānī says no praise can encompass it. Al-Iṣlāḥī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say no predicate better conveys their rank. Al-Ālūsī says no different objects need be supplied. Ibn ʿĀshūr's general comments, citing al-Sakkākī, connect rhetorical analysis with Qur'anic inimitability. [30 notes: FARRA; KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو النجم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو النجم,سيبويه); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو يعقوب السكاكي); TABATABAI(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Aj, Z); QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 56:10/q03/p2 The second noun emphatically repeats the first; 'those are the brought-near' supplies the predicate. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Zajjāj, al-Ṭabarī and others give this parse. Al-Kashshāf places a sound pause after the second noun and says it parallels the other groups. Al-Samīn describes the next words as a new clause in predicate position and compares 7:26. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the repetition expresses amazement. Al-Ālūsī rejects it for weakening the division and later contrast; al-Nasafī favors the predicate parse. [17 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 56:10/q03/p3 The second al-sābiqūn predicates a different precedence: first in faith or obedience here, then first in mercy or Paradise. — Al-Zajjāj and his transmitters distinguish obedience and mercy; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn allow faith, obedience, or good works paired with Paradise or mercy. Ṣāḥib al-Murshid gives obedience/mercy and good/Paradise. Al-Ālūsī prefers the complete predicate without supplying different objects. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(صاحب المرشد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 56:10/q03/p4 The second noun is an adjective modifying the first, with the predicate following. — Some grammarians give this parse. Abū Isḥāq allows it; Abū Ḥayyān allows it when the two words have different objects. Al-Ālūsī defends adjectival analysis by saying the definite article need not be relative here. Al-Samīn rejects a bare adjective as pointless and suggests its proponents meant emphasis. [5 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض النحويين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 56:10/q03/p5 The first noun is coordinated with the right-hand group, or stands as a separate clause after a pause. — Al-Rāzī reports coordination with the preceding right-hand people before the clause 'those are the brought-near.' Al-Samīn rejects the pause-and-new-clause version because it strains coordination and requires an insertion. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 56:10/q03/p6 Each group label serves as predicate of an omitted topic; the repeated label introduces its description. — Al-Irshād says the division naturally names categories before describing them; earlier descriptions preview the other groups' eventual states, while the forerunners receive description immediately. Al-Ālūsī favors this sequence. A criticism says the parse only identifies groups and leaves the two earlier questions unexplained; another analysis allows the questions to modify prior labels after an implied saying. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 56:10/q03/p7 The phrase tacitly follows an omitted question, 'What are the forerunners?' — Al-Bursevī infers that question from the two preceding 'what are the companions of the right/left?' questions. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 56:10/q04 What is their rank among the three groups?  [ٱلسَّٰبِقُونَ · interpretation]
- 56:10/q04/p1 They are the third, most excellent class, brought near above the companions of the right. — Al-Ṭabarī calls them the third group in 56:7; Ibn Kathīr makes them a smaller elite including messengers, prophets, truthful people and martyrs. Al-Biqāʿī links them to the first two gardens in al-Raḥmān and calls them people of the heart. Kuran Yolu makes them the leading part of those given the right-hand record, citing 56:11 and 56:88. Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts their highest Paradise rank with the lesser rank of the right-hand group. The Study Quran reports the highest parts of Heaven. ## 56:10/q06 What brings the forerunners near to God?  [ٱلسَّٰبِقُونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether the notes ground nearness in prior divine gift or in recompense corresponding to the servants' deeds.
- 56:10/q06/p1 Precedence in obedience and good deeds is answered by precedence in mercy, honor, and Paradise. — Al-Kalbī and al-Rabīʿ align worldly and afterlife precedence. Ibn Kathīr says recompense matches deeds, citing 3:133 and 57:22; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī pairs works with corresponding forgiveness and mercy, citing 57:21. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); THALABI(الربيع عن أنس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 56:10/q06/p2 God's choice and gift bring them near; their attributed racing does not itself produce nearness. — Al-Sulamī says divine friendship was given before their existence. Al-Qāsim reasons that, though God attributes racing to servants, their act itself cannot make them near or it would truly belong to them. Al-Qushayrī reports a promised highest reward and haste toward allotted good. [4 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(القاسم); QUSHAYRI]

## 56:10/q10 Why are the forerunners placed after the other groups and described next?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes explain either the delayed placement and immediate description by merit and exhortation, or the absence of an interrogative by their inexpressible rank.
- 56:10/q10/p1 The best group is mentioned third so its excellent state can be described next and its merit draw attention. — Al-Rāzī says their merit warrants priority before detailing the left; al-Biqāʿī says the highest rank among the right encourages excellence while leaving the left undivided as warning. Al-Bursevī and al-Ālūsī say third placement leads directly into their excellent states. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds curiosity, emulation, and a deliberate unfolding of the three groups through proximity and relative priority. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 56:10/q10/p2 Their known greatness makes an interrogative challenge unnecessary. — Al-Rāzī contrasts the question about the right-hand companions in 56:8: people already admit they cannot grasp the forerunners' state. Ibn ʿĀshūr says repeating their name conveys honor more strongly than the exclamatory wording for the right. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 56:10/q11 What kind of standing do the three groups represent?  [* · interpretation]
- 56:10/q11/p1 The classes express moral standing by faith and deeds at Judgment, rather than physical placement before God. — Al-Iṣlāḥī says Judgment overturns worldly standards, divides humanity in verses 1–10, and raises or abases by faith and deeds; spatial right, left, front, and back make no sense before God. Ibn Zayd distinguishes knowledge ruling desire from desire ruling knowledge; the latter leads to Hell. Al-Itqān says the examples of neglecting obligations, fulfilling them, and exceeding in good deeds illustrate rather than exhaust the classes. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); ITQAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 56:10/q12 How does this threefold grouping connect with other Qurʾānic passages and the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes draw different correspondences: 35:32's moral classes, al-Raḥmān's garden levels, al-Ṣāffāt's ranking, and the sūra's judgment theme.
- 56:10/q12/p1 The three groups correspond to the wrongdoers, moderate believers, and those foremost in good in another Qurʾānic division. — Ibn Kathīr associates the sūra's beginning and end with 35:32, on one reading of 'wronging oneself.' Al-Burhān and al-Itqān make the three classifications exhaustive; al-Itqān says concrete examples do not exhaust the general classes. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN; TABATABAI]
- 56:10/q12/p2 The three ranks continue al-Raḥmān's gradation of punishment and two levels of gardens. — Abū Ḥayyān calls the sūra Meccan and following al-Raḥmān; he maps unbelievers, lesser believers, and superior believers to its garden pairs and to this sūra's opening and closing groups, citing 55:62. Al-Biqāʿī assigns the forerunners the first two gardens. The Study Quran says al-Wāqiʿa completes al-Raḥmān's Paradise/Hell contrast. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 56:10/q12/p3 The sūra's movement toward judgment organizes the fate of all three classes. — Al-Iṣlāḥī says verses 1–10 establish Judgment and its threefold evaluation; verse 3's raising and lowering becomes the three groups. The Study Quran identifies their afterlife fates as the sūra's central theme. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 56:10/q12/p4 The few forerunners here occupy a higher degree than other servants mentioned in al-Ṣāffāt. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares the lower degree of servants in 37:40 with the forerunners' highest ranks in Paradise. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 56:10/q13 How are people paired within the three classes?  [* · reports]
- 56:10/q13/p1 Each person is joined with peers who did like deeds. — Al-Nuʿmān ibn Bashīr reports the Prophet's explanation of the three classes; Ibn Kathīr connects it to souls paired in 81:7. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 56:10/q14 What report compares righteous humans with angels?  [* · reports]
- 56:10/q14/p1 God refuses angels' request for the Hereafter in place of Adam's children's worldly goods and does not equate them with righteous offspring of one created by His hand. — ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr recounts three appeals and God's contrast with one created by 'Be,' then recites these verses. Al-Dārimī's version specifies righteous offspring and appears in Refutation of the Jahmiyya. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو,عثمان بن سعيد الدارمي)]
