# 35:14: 22 questions

## 35:14/q01 Who are those invoked besides God?  [تَدْعُوهُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun is confined to the nearby idol context or extends to every alleged deity; Iṣlāḥī distinguishes imaginary deities from beings that exist.
- 35:14/q01/p1 The immediate referents are idols, lifeless objects worshipped as gods. — Al-Suddī identifies idols in Ibn Abī Ḥātim and al-Durr; al-Nasafī and Bursawī explicitly identify them. Kur'an Yolu reports that exegetes generally take this view. Al-Burhān says the conditional pronoun points to idols. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي); NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); BURHAN]
- 35:14/q01/p2 The wording includes all beings or things treated as gods, including living and insentient objects. — Kur'an Yolu prefers the general scope and names people, animals, sun and moon. Al-Qurṭubī includes angels, jinn, prophets, devils and idols; Bursawī includes jinn and humans. Al-Balkhī allows angels or Jesus alongside idols. Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes angels and holy humans as supposed lords; Asad adds saints, relics, fetishes and deified forces. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABRISI(البلخي); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 35:14/q01/p3 Some supposed deities are imagined and never existed; actual righteous or evil beings are distinct cases. — Iṣlāḥī says invented deities cannot hear or answer because they do not exist; actual angels and prophets will disown worshippers, while jinn and devils will say that the worshippers chose their own misfortune. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 35:14/q02 What does calling them and your duʿāʾ mean?  [تَدْعُوهُمْ دُعَآءَكُمْ · meaning]
turns on: The notes distinguish a spoken call, a petition for help, worship, and other uses of the d-ʿ-w word family.
- 35:14/q02/p1 It means calling out or summoning them. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām and al-Suddī explain it as a call; al-Dāmghānī cites 54:6, 21:45, 27:80 and 30:52 for calling. Ibn al-Jawzī lists this verse among instances of calling aloud, alongside 17:52, 21:45, 54:10 and 54:6. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 35:14/q02/p2 It is a plea or request for aid, relief or benefit. — Al-Qurṭubī specifies calling in hardship; Bursawī says aid and relief from harm. Al-Biqāʿī covers requests for help as well as worship. Ibn al-Jawzī defines duʿāʾ as a lower person's request of a higher and lists seeking aid at 2:23, 10:38 and 40:26; he gives daʿā, yadʿū, duʿāʾ and notes duʿwa as one instance. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 35:14/q02/p3 Calling can denote worship or invocation of a supposed god. — Al-Biqāʿī explicitly includes invocation in worship. Ibn al-Jawzī lists worship as a duʿāʾ sense at 6:71, 10:106, 28:88, 25:68, 25:77 and 29:42. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 35:14/q02/p4 The broader word family includes speech, invitation, lineage and other idiomatic usages. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites speech at 7:5, 10:10 and 21:15; food invitation normally takes fatḥa and lineage kasra, while Abū ʿUbayda says ʿAdī al-Ribāb reverse them. The hadith's milk caller is a first trickle that calls forth more milk; walls can be said to collapse, and dawāʿī al-dahr denotes time's vicissitudes. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أبو عبيدة,أهل التفسير)]

## 35:14/q03 How is the opening conditional constructed?  [إِن تَدْعُوهُمْ وَلَوْ سَمِعُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: The notes describe complementary syntactic features: the relation of in and law, negation, and the recoverable object.
- 35:14/q03/p1 The first conditional has its result, and the following law clause adds a hypothetical concession. — Al-Naḥḥās says law carries the first conditional's sense although law normally does not serve as its result. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the first sentence as a second predicate of those invoked in 35:13; he says waw coordinates the law clause rather than introducing a parenthesis. Al-Burhān explains simple in versus conditional nouns and adverbial forms, and says in can introduce certain or impossible premises for proof, tact or mere supposition. [5 notes: NAHHAS; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN]
- 35:14/q03/p2 The conditional also illustrates negation and omitted objects in Arabic syntax. — Al-Burhān says lā with a conditional can negate future action, as here and at 59:12; Ibn Mālik says lā may negate a whole class or narrower object according to context. Its non-operating use can leave a nominative topic and repeat lā. The implied object of a question about whether idols hear is the invocation, supplied explicitly by this verse. A law answer may be past in form and commonly takes lām. [5 notes: BURHAN(ابن مالك)]

## 35:14/q04 How is the verb for calling read?  [تَدْعُوهُمْ · readings]
turns on: Second-person tāʾ versus third-person yāʾ.
- 35:14/q04/p1 The majority read tadʿūhum with second-person tāʾ. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls this the majority reading; al-Zamakhsharī also records the tāʾ alternative. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 35:14/q04/p2 Al-Ḥasan and Yaʿqūb read yadʿūna with third-person yāʾ. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya names both readers; al-Zamakhsharī records the yāʾ alternative as yadʿūn. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن ويعقوب); KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 35:14/q05 Why do the invoked fail to hear the call?  [لَا يَسْمَعُوا۟ دُعَآءَكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the referent is inert matter, a living servant with limited attention, an imagined deity, or any being's lack of divine hearing.
- 35:14/q05/p1 Idols are inanimate or insentient and have no capacity to hear or understand. — Al-Ṭabarī distinguishes meaningful understanding from sound; Ibn Kathīr says idols have no souls. Al-Qurṭubī adds they neither see nor hear; Bursawī says hearing is not a property of inanimate matter. Al-Rāzī contrasts idols with the good word God receives at 35:10. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the statement alerts polytheists to incapacity, though they believed idols heard them. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNABIHATIM(السدي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 35:14/q05/p2 Living holy beings may be occupied, distant, or lack independent power to hear appeals. — Al-Balkhī suggests angels or Jesus do not attend because they are occupied; al-Ālūsī adds distance. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says angels and holy humans hear only when God enables it. [3 notes: TABRISI(البلخي); ALUSI-FULL(البلخي); TABATABAI]
- 35:14/q05/p3 Imaginary gods cannot hear because they do not exist. — Iṣlāḥī contrasts invented gods with actual righteous and evil beings. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 35:14/q05/p4 No supposed god has divine ability to receive every open or secret plea, near or far. — Kur'an Yolu clarifies that the claim concerns godlike access to worshippers everywhere, rather than ordinary human hearing. Iṣlāḥī says the deities were unaware of pleas in this life and would not hear distress calls. [4 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 35:14/q06 Can angels or sanctified souls hear appeals made to them?  [لَا يَسْمَعُوا۟ · theology]
turns on: The evidence for access to petitions by holy beings and the extent of their dependence on God.
- 35:14/q06/p1 There is no transmitted proof that they hear such appeals; reason allows the possibility but cannot establish it. — Al-Ālūsī specifically discusses appeals from non-idolatrous callers. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 35:14/q06/p2 They hear only when God enables them, and may be occupied with other matters. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī denies independent hearing to angels and holy humans; al-Balkhī and al-Ālūsī suggest occupation or distance. [3 notes: TABATABAI; TABRISI(البلخي); ALUSI-FULL(البلخي)]

## 35:14/q07 What does their failure to respond mean, even if they heard?  [وَلَوْ سَمِعُوا۟ مَا ٱسْتَجَابُوا۟ لَكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether istijāba requires speech, successful aid, or acceptance of the religious claim contained in the plea; Ibn ʿĀshūr uses both verbal and practical senses.
- 35:14/q07/p1 They cannot speak or give a verbal answer; hearing alone does not confer speech. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Biqāʿī, Bursawī and al-Ālūsī distinguish hearing from a reply; Ibn al-Jawzī says God could grant hearing without the capacity to answer. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Iṣlāḥī note that istijāba can mean responding to a person's call. [15 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABRISI]
- 35:14/q07/p2 They cannot grant requests, benefit worshippers, or avert harm. — Al-Suddī in Ibn Abī Ḥātim and al-Durr says they bring no good; Qatāda in al-Ṭabarī and al-Qurṭubī denies benefit. Al-Kāshifī glosses bringing benefit and warding off harm; Bursawī argues that one unable to benefit itself cannot benefit others. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the verb also means giving the requested thing; Ibn Kathīr says they cannot fulfill requests. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 35:14/q07/p3 They would refuse the invocation because they reject the divinity, association or disbelief implied by it. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī say idols never claim divinity; al-Bayḍāwī offers their disavowal as the alternative. Al-Qurṭubī says even living, understanding idols would obey God rather than answer a call to disbelief. Al-Biqāʿī says compliance would displease God; al-Ālūsī says close servants cannot verbally endorse a false divine status and practical aid is not their duty. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); NAHHAS(قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL]

## 35:14/q08 Why introduce hypothetical hearing after denying it?  [وَلَوْ سَمِعُوا۟ · rhetoric]
- 35:14/q08/p1 It grants the worshippers' premise for argument, then points to their visible lack of response. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says it anticipates the objection that idols might hear. Al-Rāzī says hearing is disputable in reason but nonresponse can be observed. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the supposition concessive and argues that repeated unanswered requests before visible idols show their incapacity or incomprehension; the worshippers cannot all be blamed as unworthy. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:14/q09 What is meant by your shirk, and whose act is it?  [بِشِرْكِكُمْ · meaning]
- 35:14/q09/p1 It is the worshippers' act of associating the invoked with God in divinity and worship. — Al-Suddī glosses it as worshipping idols; al-Rāzī compares 31:13. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Samīn say the verbal noun is annexed to its agent, the worshippers; Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise assigns the possessive to human perpetrators who made idols partners. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي); RAZI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:14/q10 What do the alleged deities disown on Resurrection Day?  [وَيَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ يَكْفُرُونَ بِشِرْكِكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the disavowal is explained as their repudiation of worship and association or as a symbol of humanity's final perception.
- 35:14/q10/p1 They repudiate the worship and divine partnership attributed to them, often denying that they ordered or deserved it. — Muqātil links their denial of having commanded worship to 10:29; al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq phrase it as saying the people did not worship them. Qatāda says they neither accept nor acknowledge association. Al-Bayḍāwī cites 10:28; al-Nasafī 28:63; Ibn Kathīr 46:5–6 and 19:81–82; Study Quran 10:28, 59:16, 14:22 and 46:6. Ibn ʿĀshūr includes denial of invitation or approval and defines kufr as repudiation with aversion. Iṣlāḥī links the angels' denial at 34:40–41. Al-Qushayrī adds that belief then is too late after moral responsibility ends. [35 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق,قتادة); THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 35:14/q10/p2 Their disavowal depicts humanity's final recognition that these objects of worship have no ultimate reality. — Asad extends the objects to saints, angels, relics, fetishes and deified forces and reads their repudiation as a symbol of final perception. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 35:14/q11 How can the worshipped beings disavow their worshippers?  [يَكْفُرُونَ بِشِرْكِكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether lifeless idols are made to speak, their state is personified, or living worshipped beings deliver the denial themselves.
- 35:14/q11/p1 God enables the idols or objects to speak and testify against their worshippers. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says God grants cognition; al-Ṭabrisī says God makes idols speak to rebuke and deny requesting worship. Al-Qurṭubī says they are resurrected for testimony; al-Rāzī says wood and stone speaking is known only by revelation. Al-Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say God makes them speak, as does Kur'an Yolu. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 35:14/q11/p2 Their inert condition itself testifies against worship, so the disavowal is figurative. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares Dhū al-Rumma's ruined-dwelling poem, where lifeless remains seem to speak; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī use the same image. Al-Balkhī says inert idols reveal the mistake as they glorify God by displaying signs of Him. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(البلخي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 35:14/q11/p3 Living beings disown the worship openly according to their own standing. — Al-Qurṭubī includes angels, jinn, prophets and devils and cites Jesus in 5:116. Al-Ālūsī cites angels' denial in 34:40–41. Iṣlāḥī distinguishes righteous angels and prophets, who disown worship, from evil jinn and devils, who say the worshippers chose misfortune and receive no help. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 35:14/q12 Why use person-like pronouns for the invoked?  [تَدْعُوهُمْ يَسْمَعُوا۟ يَكْفُرُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether person-like agreement follows the idolaters' imagined personhood or a mixed group with rational beings.
- 35:14/q12/p1 The wording follows and mocks the worshippers' treatment of idols as discerning persons. — Bursawī says worshippers foolishly attributed discernment to idols and the verse predicates hearing and answering of them. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the rational relative pronoun and pronouns follow the polytheists' framing mockingly, with 35:13 in view. [2 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 35:14/q12/p2 The group includes rational beings, whose pronouns predominate over the idols. — Bursawī explicitly includes jinn and humans alongside idols as an alternative explanation. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 35:14/q13 To whom is the closing address directed?  [يُنَبِّئُكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular second person identifies the Prophet specifically or serves as a general address.
- 35:14/q13/p1 The singular address is to the Prophet Muhammad. — Al-Rāzī says God reveals an otherwise unknowable future of speaking wood and stone to him; the source is all-knowing. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this as a turn away from opponents who do not understand; al-Biqāʿī describes the turn to a person capable of understanding and obedience. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 35:14/q13/p2 It addresses any hearer through a singular proverbial form. — Al-Rāzī gives this alternative; al-Ālūsī says any listener is included. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats it as a general proverb, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports that a particular address can intend anyone, comparing 18:17–18. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 35:14/q14 Who is the expert informer?  [خَبِيرٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether khabīr directly names God, could refer to the idols' testimony, or states a general principle whose context points to God.
- 35:14/q14/p1 God is the uniquely expert informer. — Muqātil, al-Zajjāj, Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda and many authors identify Him. Al-Ṭabarī stresses knowledge of past and future; al-Wāḥidī says no one equals His knowledge of creatures. Al-Rāzī says reason cannot know that idols will speak at Judgment; al-Biqāʿī says God's report cannot be challenged. Al-Ṭabrisī mentions knowledge of benefit, harm, good and corruption. Elmalılı connects this to God's exclusive grant of prophethood; Kur'an Yolu and Study Quran stress unseen future events. [27 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس ومقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة وغيره); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Aj, Q, Z,Aj, Z, R,IK, Ṭ)]
- 35:14/q14/p2 The idol or worshipped beings may be the informants, testifying from direct knowledge of their own disavowal. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya proposes that none can report better about itself; Abū Ḥayyān relays this. Al-Ālūsī reports the proposal but calls it far-fetched. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL(إبن عطية)-]
- 35:14/q14/p3 The maxim speaks generically of a knowledgeable informant, while its context identifies God's reliable report. — Al-Zamakhsharī says only a knower tells a matter's truth; Abū Ḥayyān reports him. Ibn Kathīr says expert knowledge encompasses outcomes and final destinations; Iṣlāḥī says real knowledge can disclose hidden matters. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the intended speaker is identified through the general maxim. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:14/q15 What does khabīr convey?  [خَبِيرٍۢ · meaning]
- 35:14/q15/p1 It denotes certain, penetrating knowledge of subtle and hidden realities. — Al-Zarrūqī says the All-Aware knows subtle things others reach only through testing and artifice. Al-Ghazālī says no movement, stillness, agitation or tranquility in either dominion is hidden from God. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls khabīr an intensive adjective for certain knowledge, naming a class of genuine experts. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الزروقي,الغزالي); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:14/q16 What does the closing maxim do?  [وَلَا يُنَبِّئُكَ مِثْلُ خَبِيرٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes identify its truth-confirming function, its role as a tadhyīl, and its proverbial form; these can coexist.
- 35:14/q16/p1 It certifies the preceding report because its speaker knows the idols and their future. — Al-Zamakhsharī says only an expert gives an accurate account; al-Bayḍāwī says this confirms God's account and refutes idol claims. Al-Nasafī says God knows the causes of delusion and hidden matters; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the closing authenticates preceding reports. Iṣlāḥī urges heed of hidden truth instead of fancies; Study Quran distinguishes truths of the idols and Resurrection from what reason alone can infer. [13 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Aj, Z, R,IK, Ṭ); RAZI-FULL]
- 35:14/q16/p2 It is a tadhyīl, a closing sentence that repeats and reinforces the argument. — Al-Zarkashī calls the close of the passage on powerless worshipped things a tadhyīl. Al-Suyūṭī defines tadhyīl as a following sentence containing the prior meaning, clarifying it for some and consolidating it for others; he compares 34:17, 17:81, 21:34 and 3:185. [2 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN]
- 35:14/q16/p3 It is phrased as a proverb or maxim that can circulate independently. — Jaʿfar ibn Shams al-Khilāfa lists it among Qurʾānic expressions used proverbially; Ibn ʿĀshūr describes the singular address as a general proverb. [2 notes: ITQAN(جعفر بن شمس الخلافة); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:14/q17 Why are inform, like and expert worded this way?  [يُنَبِّئُكَ مِثْلُ خَبِيرٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes address complementary features of lexical choice, ellipsis, indefiniteness and idiomatic comparison.
- 35:14/q17/p1 The compact, indefinite expression generalizes the expert's unequalled authority. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the objects of inform and expert and the dimension of likeness are omitted as recoverable; indefinite khabīr under negation rules out any equal and thereby identifies the speaker. Iṣlāḥī says indefiniteness magnifies the true knower. The Lubāb author says the likeness idiom does not attribute an actual likeness to God; it means no report is as reliable. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURSEVI(صاحب لباب)]
- 35:14/q17/p2 Nabaʾ is chosen for significant news, and mathl denotes an equal or similar counterpart. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says nabaʾ concerns a momentous event. He derives mathl as equality in amount, hence a double, or in quality, hence similarity, noting a rare faʿl pattern for an active participle like shibh, nidd and khidn. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:14/q18 How is the hamza of yunabbiʾuka represented or read?  [يُنَبِّئُكَ · readings]
turns on: The written carrier and phonetic treatment of hamza, with related cases governed by different surrounding vowels and rasm.
- 35:14/q18/p1 In this kasra-then-ḍamma environment, the rasm uses yāʾ for the hamza; readers differ over its realization. — Ibn al-Jazarī says al-Akhfash's rule writes yāʾ in yunabbiʾuka and sanaqriʾuka instead of customary wāw. The majority facilitate between hamza and wāw, while al-Dānī and others following the rasm favor yāʾ substitution. He notes that a hamza without its own written shape can reflect gemination or dropping in a dialect and that comparable forms such as suʾila follow the customary rule. [4 notes: NASHR; NASHR+]
- 35:14/q18/p2 For related hamza sequences, deletion with ḍamma before a following wāw is preferred by al-Dānī and rasm-following readers. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports al-Dānī's preference, while rejecting facilitation between hamza and yāʾ and replacement by wāw in that context; kasra before the wāw is weak and only three of six proposed faces are sound, including between hamza and wāw and deletion options. [2 notes: NASHR; NASHR(أبو عمرو الداني)+; NASHR-]
- 35:14/q18/p3 Other analogues have their own rasm and sound reading rules; several proposed transfers are unsound. — Ibn al-Jazarī records variant omission of an alif for open hamza after fatḥa, and prefers broad allowance for arayta variation. Naʾā's nūn-and-alif rasm permits both readings at 17:83 and 41:51; raʾā's added yāʾ in two Najm instances marks imāla. At pause, yastahziʾūn-like forms allow madd, tawassuṭ or qaṣr. ## 35:14/q20 What theological argument does the verse make against worshipping others?  [* · theology]
- 35:14/q20/p1 Alleged gods cannot benefit or harm worshippers now and will provide no aid in the afterlife; worship belongs to God alone. — Al-Ṭabarī asks why worship powerless beings rather than the Creator who controls benefit and harm and gave blessings. Al-Rāzī rebuts the claim that visible, approachable idols confer honor and says their lack of worldly benefit is followed by loss of afterlife benefit. Al-Qushayrī stresses that belief after Resurrection is useless; Iṣlāḥī says helplessness grows clearer then. Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 4:172: Christ and near angels are God's servants and depend on Him. Elmalılı adds that God alone grants prophethood. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI; ELMALILI]

## 35:14/q21 How does this verse figure in discussion of Qurʾānic eloquence?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes weigh semantic arrangement and constructed imagery against mere sound or unintegrated parallel phrases.
- 35:14/q21/p1 Eloquence depends on meaningful composition and rhetorical devices, not bare phonetic ease. — Al-Jurjānī cites this verse among examples of metaphor, allusion, representation, figurative speech and concision, alongside 19:4, 2:93, 36:37, 15:94, 12:80, 47:4, 2:16, 8:58 and 8:57. He rejects ease of letters as the basis of inimitability. In Asrār he argues that rearranging Imruʾ al-Qays's opening ruins its identity; grammar and intended meanings govern order, and the perceived elegance of wording arises intellectually rather than from sound or bare lexemes. Familiar diction can independently help, while vulgarity may lie in meaning. [6 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR; JURJANI-DALAIL; JURJANI-DALAIL+]
- 35:14/q21/p2 The strongest compositional effect joins parts into one interwoven structure rather than merely listing parallels. — Al-Jurjānī illustrates integrated paired similes with Imruʾ al-Qays, al-Farazdaq and Bashshār; he prefers Ziyād al-Aʿjam's sea comparison for subtler weaving. He contrasts easy passages that string parallel phrases like beads without a new overall image. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL; JURJANI-DALAIL+]

## 35:14/q22 What broader contrast with service to devils frames the warning?  [* · interpretation]
- 35:14/q22/p1 Grateful obedience lets God subordinate devils, while ingratitude lets devils dominate people. — Iṣlāḥī cites David and Solomon as recipients of worldly favors who stayed grateful and made devils follow them rather than serving devils. He says God subordinates devils to His followers, while ungrateful people become their servants. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
