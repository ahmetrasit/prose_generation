# 25:43: 22 questions

## 25:43/q01 How is the hamza in أَرَءَيْتَ read?  [أَرَءَيْتَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the second hamza is softened, omitted, or preceded by compensatory lengthening.
- 25:43/q01/p1 The second hamza is softened between vowels. — Qirāʾāt-ER identifies this as a common Ḥijāzī pronunciation. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
- 25:43/q01/p2 The second hamza is omitted. — Qirāʾāt-ER calls this a canonical simplification of the consonant cluster. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
- 25:43/q01/p3 A long alif precedes the hamza. — Qirāʾāt-ER calls the lengthening compensatory. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 25:43/q02 How does أَرَءَيْتَ govern the following clauses?  [أَرَءَيْتَ مَنِ أَفَأَنتَ تَكُونُ عَلَيْهِ وَكِيلًا · grammar]
turns on: Whether raʾayta is visual or cognitive and whether the two questions make one construction.
- 25:43/q02/p1 It is cognitive: man is its first object, and the following interrogative supplies or replaces its second object. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn take man as first object. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the interrogative suspends government and substitutes for an omitted object, with no pause at hawāhu; he compares 39:19. Al-Ālūsī records but rejects the cognitive analysis. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 25:43/q02/p2 It is visual seeing: man is its object and the relative clause describes that person. — Al-Ālūsī explicitly prefers visual seeing and rejects the cognitive two-object analysis. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]

## 25:43/q03 What do the two questions rhetorically convey?  [أَرَءَيْتَ أَفَأَنتَ تَكُونُ عَلَيْهِ وَكِيلًا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the clauses are independent astonishment followed by denial or a connected double interrogative.
- 25:43/q03/p1 The first calls for astonishment at their ignorance or conduct; the second denies the Prophet's power or duty to guard them. — Rāzī and Bursawī allow information or question generally but choose astonishment here. Al-Qurṭubī notes their acknowledgment of the Creator alongside stone worship without proof. Al-Bayḍāwī and Elmalılı call the first confirmatory. Ibn ʿĀshūr's independent-clause analysis makes the fāʾ introduce the consequence; Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿAṭiyya see consolation and an end to grief. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; ELMALILI+; QURTUBI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 25:43/q03/p2 In a connected construction, the first question is rhetorical denial and the second elaborates and reinforces it. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the reinforcing pair to 17:49; the second clause substitutes for the object of cognitive raʾayta. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 25:43/q04 Who makes desire his god, and how?  [مَنِ ٱتَّخَذَ إِلَٰهَهُۥ هَوَىٰهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether desire itself governs obedience, prompts a chosen idol, or identifies a specific person or class.
- 25:43/q04/p1 Anyone who follows desire as an authority, obeying it in religion or conduct instead of truth and proof. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Zajjāj, al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī stress abandoning evidence; al-Ḥasan and Qatāda say every want is acted on without piety. Ibn ʿAṭiyya contrasts the evil-commanding soul with reason. Ibn Kathīr compares 35:8; Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 36:60–61 on obedience as worship. Ibn ʿĀshūr includes idolatry and other wrongs; al-Ālūsī says safety lies in following God's law in all matters. The anonymous poem cited by al-Qurṭubī pictures an ascetic praying to a woman first. [41 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ(قيل); IBNABIHATIM(الحسن,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة,الحسن,الزجاج); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(القتيبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,قتادة); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL(الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس,صاحب الكشاف وابن عطية); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Aj, Z); ALUSI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+]
- 25:43/q04/p2 It describes idolaters whose choice of stone or tree idol changes whenever a more attractive object appears. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Saʿīd b. Jubayr report the white-stone replacement; al-Kalbī includes trees. Abū Rajāʾ also recalls eating blood with al-ʿilḥiz and announcing lost idols. Al-Farrāʾ prefers worship prompted by desire. Al-Ālūsī says these reports need not make hawā the desired object. [25 notes: ZAJJAJ(قيل); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(سعيد بن جبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(أبو رجاء العطاردي,ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(some commentators (Aj, Q, R)); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)+]
- 25:43/q04/p3 The verse was revealed about al-Ḥārith ibn Qays al-Sahmī, who worshipped what he fancied. — Muqātil names him; al-Naqqāsh, al-Kashshāf, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursawī and al-Ālūsī repeat the identification. The reported acts vary between worshipping a fancied object and replacing stones. [7 notes: MUQATIL(الحارث بن قيس السهمى); MAWARDI-FULL(النقاش); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 25:43/q04/p4 It especially describes a hypocrite who acts on every desire. — Al-Ḥasan reports this in Yaḥyā and Ibn Abī Ḥātim; al-Durr distinguishes idolaters bowing to sun or moon from a hypocrite submitting to desire. Al-Ālūsī says al-Ḥasan includes sinners among qibla people. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); DURR-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 25:43/q04/p5 It describes a disbeliever or polytheist led by desire without divine proof. — Ibn ʿAbbās calls him a disbeliever led astray according to prior knowledge. Yaḥyā calls him a polytheist; Ibn al-Jawzī reports Qatāda's unbeliever. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes the generic singular ilāh can cover many gods. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 25:43/q04/p6 It pictures one who acknowledges the Creator but chooses to worship a stone. — ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās says the person knows God as creator, yet worships a stone; al-Baghawī and al-Ṭabrisī recount abandonment of worship of the Creator. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(عطاء)]

## 25:43/q05 What verb pattern and object structure does ٱتَّخَذَ have?  [ٱتَّخَذَ · grammar]
- 25:43/q05/p1 It is form افتعل and, as a causative verb, takes two objects. — Sībawayh says افتعل starts with a connecting hamza and tāʾ and follows انفعل in inflection. He contrasts انفعل, استفعل, افعاللت, افعللت, افعوعلت and افعولت, explains active/passive vowels and connecting-alif movement, and cites compensatory hāʾ or sīn in هرقت, أهرقت and أسطاع. Ibn ʿĀshūr likens two-object اتخذ to كسا and أعطى: its first object becomes the state named by its second. [13 notes: SIBAWAYH; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 25:43/q06 Which noun is the first object of ٱتَّخَذَ, and what does the order convey?  [ٱتَّخَذَ إِلَٰهَهُۥ هَوَىٰهُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the written order is ordinary or advances the second object, and whether the advance signals emphasis or restriction.
- 25:43/q06/p1 Desire is the first object; 'his god' is the advanced second object, emphasizing or restricting deity to desire. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Burhān, al-Itqān and Bursawī call the advance emphatic; Ibn al-Munīr, al-Biqāʿī and Elmalılı read exclusivity. Al-Ṭībī compares 2:275: deity is the original comparison, desire the likeness. Al-Farāʾid instead says the order marks the deity role and rejects the exclusivity analogy; al-Ālūsī says context determines the predicate. Al-Samīn calls it preposing, not poetic inversion. Ibn ʿĀshūr accepts this sense but also accepts ordinary order. [23 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL(أبو حيان); ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير,الطيبي,المفتاح,شيخ الإسلام,صاحب الفرائد); IBNASHUR-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; BURHAN+; BURSEVI+; ELMALILI+; ITQAN+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; RAZI-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; TABATABAI(عدة من المفسرين)-]
- 25:43/q06/p2 'His god' is the first object and desire the second; the wording makes the former become the latter. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn say both nouns are definite and ordinary order stands; inversion would rely on poetic license. Rāzī finds inversion weak. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says they recognize an obeyed deity but wrongly make desire their authority, and rejects the advanced-object account as contextually unsuitable. Ibn ʿArafa chose only ordinary order; Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects that exclusivity and accepts both readings. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عرفة,سعيد بن جبير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 25:43/q06/p3 An omitted preposition gives the sense 'a deity according to his desire.' — Al-Wāḥidī offers an omitted preposition or a nominalized desired object; al-Qurṭubī reports al-Kalbī's omitted preposition with a changing idol. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي)]

## 25:43/q07 What variant is read for إِلَٰهَهُۥ?  [إِلَٰهَهُۥ · readings]
turns on: Whether the variant noun is singular feminine, a name for the sun, plural, or a possessive form.
- 25:43/q07/p1 A feminine ilāha or ilāhata is read, meaning the worshipped one. — Ibn Hurmuz is credited by Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī; the feminine hāʾ intensifies the sense, and the noun is declinable. Al-Samīn calls it a preposed indefinite second object. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن هرمز); SAMIN-DURR(ابن هرمز); ALUSI-FULL(ابن هرمز)]
- 25:43/q07/p2 The feminine ilāha or alāha means the sun. — Al-Aʿraj is credited by Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ṭabrisī. Al-Ṭabrisī cites poetry and says alāha with ḍamma is indeclinable. Al-Lawāmiḥ says the form can be declined because some dialects add the article; al-Samīn records both a diptote objection and the article-based reply. Al-Ālūsī dismisses the sun analysis. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعرج); TABRISI(الأعرج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(صاحب اللوامح); SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى); ALUSI-FULL(صاحب اللوامح)-]
- 25:43/q07/p3 Some Medinans read plural ālihatan, treating types of desire as multiple gods. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī report the plural with tanwīn and a preposed second object. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعض أهل المدينة); SAMIN-DURR(بعض المدنيين); ALUSI-FULL(بعض المدنيين)]
- 25:43/q07/p4 A reading 'your deity' can mean your worship or the sun you worship. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports both interpretations, comparing the possessive to 'your sun.' [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 25:43/q08 What does هَوَىٰهُ mean as a word?  [هَوَىٰهُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether hawā denotes inclination itself or the desired object.
- 25:43/q08/p1 Hawā is the soul's inclination, appetite, or bare preference. — Al-Wāḥidī cites a poetic use for inclination. Abū Ḥayyān calls it the heart's inclination. Bursawī traces it from what one loves to the desired object, with later blameworthy usage; he excludes reason and transmitted proof. Elmalılı calls it spontaneous preference, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī says unmoderated inclination. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 25:43/q08/p2 Here hawā names what unbelievers worship besides God, or an idol. — Ibn ʿAbbās is cited by al-Wāḥidī and al-Jishumī; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports him as saying desire itself is the god worshipped apart from God. Al-Ālūsī objects that stone reports do not demand the sense 'desired thing.' [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 25:43/q09 What does وَكِيلًا mean here?  [وَكِيلًا · meaning]
turns on: Whether agency here is guardianship, control, guarantee, or support.
- 25:43/q09/p1 A guardian or custodian who takes charge and protects. — Yaḥyā describes watching deeds until requital; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says one who undertakes the matter. Al-Qurṭubī includes guarding return to faith and rescuing from corruption. Al-Zajjāj, al-Māwardī via Yaḥyā, al-Baghawī and others give guardian. [11 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 25:43/q09/p2 One who controls, dominates, or has authority over another. — Al-Suddī gives control in Yaḥyā and al-Māwardī; al-Dāmghānī compares 6:107. Ibn al-Jawzī reports authority, while Ibn ʿAbbās denies that the Prophet possesses it. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)]
- 25:43/q09/p3 A guarantor. — Ibn Qutayba gives this alongside guardian; al-Māwardī attributes it to al-Kalbī. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي)]
- 25:43/q09/p4 A supporter or helper. — Qatāda supplies this gloss in Ibn Abī Ḥātim and al-Māwardī. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة)]

## 25:43/q10 What does the question about the Prophet as wakīl deny?  [أَفَأَنتَ تَكُونُ عَلَيْهِ وَكِيلًا · interpretation]
- 25:43/q10/p1 The Prophet cannot force, guide, or guard this person into faith; he is charged with warning and conveying the message. — Muqātil denies control over the person's choice; al-Naḥḥās glosses compulsion. Al-Kashshāf compares 50:45 and 88:22; Rāzī adds 2:256. Al-Ṭabrisī says a guardian must be able to fulfill his task. Ibn ʿĀshūr links 25:42 and 10:99. Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 28:56. Bursawī says the denial does not prohibit calling them, since warning removes excuses. The Study Quran cites 88:22 on the Prophet not being a warder. [35 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Aj, Q); BIQAI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; NASAFI-FULL+]

## 25:43/q11 Why is أَنتَ placed before تَكُونُ?  [أَفَأَنتَ تَكُونُ · rhetoric]
- 25:43/q11/p1 The fronted pronoun emphasizes that the Prophet cannot be their keeper by persistent calls to belief. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 10:99. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 25:43/q12 Who does عَلَيْهِ refer to?  [عَلَيْهِ · referent]
- 25:43/q12/p1 The person who has made desire his god. — Bursawī specifies the antecedent. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 25:43/q13 What does the verse teach about human choice and divine guidance?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the explanation stresses people's originating choices or God's control of guidance.
- 25:43/q13/p1 The cited actions and refusals require a choosing human agent. — Al-Jishumī argues that human acts are originated by people. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 25:43/q13/p2 Ultimate guidance is God's alone; the Prophet cannot rescue one God has let stray. — Ibn Kathīr says the following verse concerns one decreed wretched and astray. Ibn Abī Ḥātim's Ibn ʿAbbās says God led the person astray by prior knowledge. Al-Qurṭubī calls the passage a rebuttal to the Qadariyya; Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 28:56. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 25:43/q14 Does the verse's denial of guardianship remain operative?  [أَفَأَنتَ تَكُونُ عَلَيْهِ وَكِيلًا · sciences]
turns on: Whether the fighting verse supersedes this wording or its consoling force remains.
- 25:43/q14/p1 It was abrogated by the fighting or sword verse. — Al-Kalbī is cited by al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī and Ibn al-Jawzī; al-Wāḥidī also reports the view anonymously. Al-Qurṭubī reports it as one view. Rāzī's al-Kalbī note instead concerns abrogation of 2:256, which bears on the linked no-compulsion argument. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الكلبي)]
- 25:43/q14/p2 It remains a consolation for the Prophet. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this alternative to abrogation. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 25:43/q15 What report ranks followed desire among false deities?  [إِلَٰهَهُۥ هَوَىٰهُ · reports]
- 25:43/q15/p1 A report attributed to the Prophet says followed desire is the greatest or most hateful false deity beneath heaven. — Abū Umāma transmits the 'greatest' version in al-Durr, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı and Ṭabāṭabāʾī; al-Ālūsī gives no grading. Bursawī's variant says 'most hateful to God.' [5 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو أمامة); BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام); ALUSI-FULL(أبو أمامة); ELMALILI(Ebû Ümâme); TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]

## 25:43/q16 What inward reading is drawn from worship of desire?  [إِلَٰهَهُۥ هَوَىٰهُ · inward]
turns on: Whether desire is a veil of love, a cause of spiritual death, or an origin of corrupt customs.
- 25:43/q16/p1 Loving and becoming absorbed in anything for its own sake amounts to worshipping one's passion and the loved object. — Al-Qāshānī says a veil makes one resemble what one loves and Satan's prompting divides attention among gods. Najmiyya, cited by Bursawī, says occupation with anything besides God worships desire. Bursawī calls false gods branches of the first worshipped desire; resisting it leads to faith. [4 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 25:43/q16/p2 Following desire kills the self, while remembrance and opposing desire give it life. — Abū Sulaymān is quoted by al-Sulamī and Bursawī; heedlessness and immersion in sin lead to spiritual death. [3 notes: SULAMI(أبو سليمان); BURSEVI(أبو سليمان رحمه الله)]
- 25:43/q16/p3 Human desire stems from the union of Iblīs and the world, producing deviant customs and religions. — Bursawī attributes this to Sayyid Ḥasanī's Tarab al-majālis. [1 notes: BURSEVI(سيد حسني رحمه الله)]

## 25:43/q17 What dream interpretations does Bursawī connect to desire?  [* · inward]
turns on: What the dream's death signifies: weakened practice or desire leaving the heart.
- 25:43/q17/p1 Dreaming of the Prophet's death signals deficient religious practice. — Bursawī reports elders who liken this to losing divine presence. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أكابر)]
- 25:43/q17/p2 The dreamed death of a 'god' can signify the exit of a once-worshipped desire and greater presence with God. — Bursawī attributes this alternative to Jāmī. [1 notes: BURSEVI(جامي رحمه الله)]

## 25:43/q18 What practical lesson is drawn from submitting to desire?  [* · interpretation]
- 25:43/q18/p1 Desire blinds judgment and enslaves people, leaving them unable to grasp guidance and sometimes worse than animals. — Islāḥī contrasts guiding intellect with blind appetite, describes Satan's web and loss of natural bounds. Kur'an Yolu contrasts reasoned assessment with bodily pleasure, passing wants and status, and extends the criticism of Meccan polytheists across history. Abū Ḥayyān likens ignorance of consequences to animals. Bursawī's Mathnawī contrasts genuine believer-gold with counterfeit unbelievers exposed to the same lust. [9 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(المثنوي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 25:43/q19 How does this verse connect with its context and other verses?  [* · links]
- 25:43/q19/p1 It consoles the Prophet amid rejection and follows the opponents' mockery and conduct. — Al-Biqāʿī says the Prophet grieves out of concern for his people. Ibn ʿĀshūr links the denial to 25:42 and identifies man with those who see punishment there; singular pronouns agree with the form man. Ṭabāṭabāʾī lists mockery, caprice, powerless idols and refusal to prostrate. Islāḥī compares the Moses and Noah narratives and says the next sūra elaborates former nations' fate. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 25:43/q19/p2 Other verses parallel the inability to guide or compel and the sealing of a desire-led person. — Rāzī compares 88:22, 50:45 and 2:256. Ibn Kathīr compares 35:8. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says 35:22 summarizes the inability to make the dead hear and 45:23 elaborates the desire-led person's sealed guidance; Study Quran compares 88:22. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 25:43/q20 What general rhetorical principles are illustrated alongside this wording?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Which rhetorical effect is being considered: generic predication or metaphorical transfer.
- 25:43/q20/p1 A generic predicate distributes a quality across a class; an addressed definite predicate presents one addressee as its complete embodiment. — Al-Jurjānī contrasts 'the brave is protected' with 'you are the brave one.' [2 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 25:43/q20/p2 A metaphor's force lies in a subtle, novel change of meaning rather than sound. — Al-Jurjānī says the word's letters remain unchanged. [1 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 25:43/q21 What sura classification is reported?  [* · sciences]
- 25:43/q21/p1 A minority calls most of the sura Madinan, except verses 68–70 as Makkan. — The Study Quran reports this minority view. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(A minority)]

## 25:43/q22 What is said about the address form here?  [أَرَءَيْتَ · rhetoric]
- 25:43/q22/p1 Its explanation is deferred elsewhere in the exegesis. — Islāḥī points to another discussion without giving the explanation here. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
