# 82:6: 20 questions

## 82:6/q01 Who is the human addressed?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular address is generic, restricted by the subsequent denial of judgment, or tied to a particular reported occasion; reports differ in the named person.
- 82:6/q01/p1 Humanity generally, especially anyone deluded into disobedience. — Burhan calls this a general address intended generally; Ibn Qutayba and Itqan note singular address can encompass a collective. Bursevi includes all disobedient people because the context moves from every soul to the righteous (82:5), and says the later plural denial can follow a generic singular. Elmalılı says a particular occasion does not narrow general wording. Ibn Atiyya describes humanity created from nothing. [7 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ELMALILI+]
- 82:6/q01/p2 Disbelievers, particularly deniers of resurrection and judgment. — Tabari and Wahidi specify disbelievers. Qurtubi and Kuranyolu specify deniers of resurrection, the afterlife, and judgment. Tabatabai cites the ensuing denial in 82:9 and says denial of judgment entails denial of religious law and lordship. Elmalılı reports appeal to the following denial. Kuranyolu says this is the special target rather than a single person or all indiscriminately. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(السهيلي); ELMALILI(birçokları); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 82:6/q01/p3 Al-Walid ibn al-Mughira. — Ata transmits this as the occasion; Ibn Abbas is also cited. Bursevi reports it among competing names. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(السهيلي); ELMALILI(Atâ)]
- 82:6/q01/p4 Abu al-Ashadd or al-Ashaddayn, with the name Asid or Kilda ibn Asid or ibn Kilda. — The name and patronymic vary in the reports. Muqatil calls him one-eyed and very strong, records his boast about holding Paradise's gate, and says he died at Mecca's conquest. Wahidi says he struck the Prophet without immediate punishment. Ibn Jawzi records both reversed name orders; Qurtubi calls him al-Jumahi. Elmalılı gives Asad ibn Kelede. [5 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL; ELMALILI(Kelbî ve Mukātil)]
- 82:6/q01/p5 Ubayy ibn Khalaf. — Ikrima gives this identification or occasion in Qurtubi, Durr, Suyuti, and Elmalılı. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة); DURR-FULL(عكرمة); SUYUTI-LUBAB(عكرمة); ELMALILI(İkrime)]
- 82:6/q01/p6 Al-Aswad ibn Shariq. — Al-Kalbi and Muqatil say he struck the Prophet and initially escaped punishment. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل)]
- 82:6/q01/p7 Usayd ibn Khalaf. — Damghani gives this name. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]
- 82:6/q01/p8 Umayya ibn Khalaf. — Al-Suhayli names him while retaining application of the general wording to others. [1 notes: BURSEVI(السهيلي)]
- 82:6/q01/p9 Al-Aswad ibn Kalada. — Bursevi lists this among alternative reports. [1 notes: BURSEVI(السهيلي)]
- 82:6/q01/p10 The rich, arrogant Quraysh who relied on their buildings and ignored warnings. — Islahi identifies the audience of both al-Infitar and al-Takwir this way. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 82:6/q02 How can a singular human address apply beyond one person?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · grammar]
- 82:6/q02/p1 A singular form can address a collective or a generic human. — Ibn Qutayba compares 84:6 and 39:8. Itqan likewise notes collective reference. Bursevi explains that a later plural accusation can follow a generic singular, just as one clan member's act can be attributed to the clan. [3 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; ITQAN; BURSEVI]

## 82:6/q03 Why address the sinner as a human?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · rhetoric]
- 82:6/q03/p1 Human nature and creation themselves testify against ingratitude. — Tabatabai says the person's human qualities collectively become proof against him. Ibn Atiyya asks how one created from nothing could disobey, associate others with God, or disbelieve. [2 notes: TABATABAI; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 82:6/q04 How is mā parsed in the familiar wording?  [مَا · grammar]
- 82:6/q04/p1 It is an interrogative noun in the nominative as the subject; the second-person suffix is the object of gharra. — Nahhas calls mā a complete noun; Samin identifies the common triliteral reading and its subject function. [2 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR]

## 82:6/q05 How is mā gharraka read and construed?  [مَا غَرَّكَ · readings]
turns on: Whether mā is an ordinary question or an exclamation, and whether the hamza form expresses causation, a question, or astonishment.
- 82:6/q05/p1 The familiar reading is interrogative mā with triliteral gharraka, though it can also convey astonishment. — Samin records the common triliteral form and nominative interrogative mā. Biqai says the famous reading carries astonishment as well as the question. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير والأعمش)]
- 82:6/q05/p2 Sa'id ibn Jubayr and al-A'mash read mā agharraka with hamza; it may ask what caused deception, or exclaim at how deceived or heedless the human is. — Ibn Jinni reports a lengthened exclamatory mā, relates the reading to 2:175 and an analogy of two omitted genitives as in 20:96, and also allows an interrogative causative sense. He glosses gharra al-rajul as becoming heedless. Tab risi derives an exclamation from ghirr or gharara; a ghurur derivation is possible but irregular. Burhan and Itqan list this as a rare third Qur'anic exclamatory form beside 2:175 and 80:17. Biqai describes its hamza as interrogative, 'Did He delude you?'. [14 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(سعيد بن جبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جبير); TABRISI(سعيد بن جبير); SAMIN-DURR(ابن جبير والأعمش); BURHAN(سعيد بن جبير); BIQAI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير والأعمش); ITQAN(سعيد بن جبير)]

## 82:6/q06 What does it mean to be deceived about God?  [غَرَّكَ · meaning]
turns on: The notes distinguish deceptive enticement and false assurance from a possible lexical derivation expressing heedlessness.
- 82:6/q06/p1 To be enticed into falsehood, neglecting God's due and feeling falsely safe from real punishment. — Zajjaj and Wahidi describe enticement into neglect; Wahidi compares the false assurance of 35:5. Baghawi adds abandonment of obligations. Jishumi defines ghurur as an ignorantly imagined safety from what should be feared; Bursevi says the danger remains real. Qushayri and Elmalılı apply it to disobedience. Biqai adds seeing a bad deed as good or presuming certain pardon. Al-Harith ibn Hilliza's line illustrates gharur in Tabrisi. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; TABRISI(الحرث بن حلزة); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]
- 82:6/q06/p2 It can mean becoming heedless or ignorant. — Ibn Jinni glosses gharra al-rajul, fahuwa ghārr, as the man's becoming heedless; Tabrisi offers roots ghirr and gharara for the exclamatory reading. [2 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; TABRISI]

## 82:6/q07 What deluded the human being?  [مَا غَرَّكَ · interpretation]
turns on: The reports offer different immediate causes: Satan, ignorance, divine respite or concealment or gifts, worldly life, and inward distraction; Elmalılı subsumes several under ignorance.
- 82:6/q07/p1 Satan, the enemy empowered over the human, deluded him. — Tabari and Qatada call Satan the appointed enemy; al-Hasan calls him a wicked devil. Baydawi and Bursevi describe his reassurance that divine generosity permits sin. Elmalılı calls it Satanic embellishment and compares 57:14, then says susceptibility to it reduces to ignorance. [13 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(الحسن البصري); ELMALILI(Katâde)]
- 82:6/q07/p2 Ignorance or foolishness deluded him. — Prophetic reports give this reply; Umar cites the human's ignorance in 33:72, and Ibn Umar, Ibn Abbas, al-Rabi, al-Hasan, and Umar are likewise reported. Tustari's Umar ibn Wasil calls it ignorance in departing from God's protection. Elmalılı favors this explanation because fear grows with knowledge (35:28); postponed punishment, concealment, and Satan's embellishment all reduce to ignorance. [21 notes: TUSTARI(عمر بن واصل); WAHIDI-BASIT(الربيع بن خثيم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي); TABRISI(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن عن عمر,النبي,عمر); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس والربيع بن خثيم والحسن,ابن عمر,عمر); DURR-FULL(ربيع بن خثيم,صالح بن مسمار,عمر بن الخطاب); BURSEVI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ELMALILI(Hasan,Hazret-i Peygamber,Hazret-i Ömer); TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); ELMALILI+]
- 82:6/q07/p3 God's forbearance and delay of punishment made the sinner presume safety. — Muqatil, Baghawi, Qurtubi, and Elmalılı tie it to punishment not arriving at once. Qushayri includes divine respite, while Ibn al-Sammak's poem and Abu Bakr ibn Tahir's poem warn that prolonged reprieve emboldens wrongdoing. Ali's servant trusted his master's forbearance and was freed after saying so. Islahi says respite was for reform, but criminals misconstrue it. [16 notes: THALABI(أبو بكر بن طاهر الأبهري,بعضهم,مقاتل); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو بكر بن طاهر الأبهري,ابن السماك,علي); BIQAI-FULL(أبو بكر الوراق); ELMALILI(Mukātil); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 82:6/q07/p4 God's concealment of sins, or His lowered veils, deceived him. — The poet in Thalabi pairs concealment with reprieve. Fudayl's imagined answer names lowered veils, and Qurtubi says karim can mean one who conceals. Dhu al-Nun warns that those living under the veil often fail to realize they are deluded; Ali warns that concealment and favors draw people in gradually. Elmalılı treats reliance on concealment as a form of ignorance. [12 notes: THALABI(الشاعر,ذو النون المصري); BAGHAWI-FULL(الفضيل بن عياض); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض العلماء); TABRISI(أمير المؤمنين,الفضيل بن عياض); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن السماك,الفضيل بن عياض,ذو النون المصري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الفضيل بن عياض); ELMALILI(Fuzayl b. İyaz)]
- 82:6/q07/p5 God's generosity, pardon, and grace emboldened the sinner. — Al-Warraq directly answers 'the Generous One's generosity'; ishari readers infer that answer from al-Karim. Mansur cites God's grace and pardon; Qushayri describes a God who sees yet conceals and can punish yet delays. Ibn Atiyya sees divine kindness toward sinful believers in the epithet. Biqai reports al-Warraq adding forbearance. Ibn Kathir and Elmalılı reject making the epithet a warrant for this answer. [13 notes: THALABI(أبو بكر الوراق,أهل الإشارة,منصور بن عمار); QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(غيره); TABRISI; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو بكر الوراق); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو بكر الوراق,البغوي); BIQAI-FULL(أبو بكر الوراق); IBNKATHIR-FULL-; ELMALILI-]
- 82:6/q07/p6 God's gentleness and earlier or continuing kindness deluded him. — Nasr ibn Maghlis and al-Suddi name gentleness; Yahya ibn Muadh points to past and present favors. Ali warns that favors can draw a person in gradually. [6 notes: THALABI(نصر بن مغلس,يحيى بن معاذ); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي,يحيى بن معاذ); TABRISI(أمير المؤمنين,يحيى بن معاذ)]
- 82:6/q07/p7 Worldly life and Satan together delude him. — Study Quran names both and cites 31:33 and 35:5. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 82:6/q07/p8 Wealth, pride, and long reprieve delude the sinner. — Abu Bakr ibn Tahir's verse says these made him confront God without fear of consequences. [2 notes: THALABI(أبو بكر بن طاهر الأبهري); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو بكر بن طاهر الأبهري)]
- 82:6/q07/p9 Anything other than God entering the heart and cutting it off deludes the person. — Tustari connects the delusion to a veil over the heart despite divine gentleness and generosity. [2 notes: TUSTARI]

## 82:6/q08 What does the question itself do?  [مَا غَرَّكَ بِرَبِّكَ ٱلْكَرِيمِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the question primarily censures and warns, prohibits being deceived, or cues the answer of divine generosity; some readings also allow exclamation.
- 82:6/q08/p1 It rebukes and threatens the person for disobedience, disbelief, and ingratitude. — Ibn Atiyya invokes creation from nothing. Baydawi says generous naming must deter presumption. Ibn Kathir explicitly calls it a threat, rejecting a prompted excuse; Tabatabai says failing to thank a beneficent Lord is inexcusable. Elmalılı's sūra context is warning, and Asad says none is wholly immune to fitnah, comparing 8:25 and the answer in 82:9. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ELMALILI+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 82:6/q08/p2 It functions as a prohibition: let nothing beguile you. — Burhan gives the general principle that interrogation can perform commands, as in 'will you not reflect?' meaning 'reflect.' Itqan compares prohibitory questions that urge fearing God rather than people. [3 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN]
- 82:6/q08/p3 Its wording prompts the reply that divine generosity deluded the person. — The people of mystical allusion and Tab risi infer the answer from the epithet al-Karim; Qushayri dramatizes the sinner emboldened by repeated generosity. Ibn Kathir rejects that inference as weak, and Elmalılı rejects it because it conflicts with the sūra's warning tone. [5 notes: THALABI(أهل الإشارة); QUSHAYRI; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(البغوي); IBNKATHIR-FULL-; ELMALILI-]
- 82:6/q08/p4 It also expresses astonishment at how deluded the person is. — Biqai hears astonishment even in the familiar reading. The hamza reading of Sa'id ibn Jubayr is more explicitly exclamatory in Burhan and Itqan. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير والأعمش); BURHAN(سعيد بن جبير); ITQAN(سعيد بن جبير)]

## 82:6/q09 What does calling God 'your Lord' add?  [بِرَبِّكَ · rhetoric]
- 82:6/q09/p1 It recalls God's care and governing lordship, making rebellion against His beneficence especially ugly. — Biqai says divine care should recall the purpose of following God's law. Tabatabai says a managing Lord showers blessings and deserves gratitude; rebellion deserves punishment. Tabrisi calls Him the person's Creator and says the deluded person follows falsehood and disobeys Him. Kuranyolu groups rabb with karim as gracious divine attributes of beauty. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; TABRISI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 82:6/q10 What does al-Karim mean here?  [ٱلْكَرِيمِ · meaning]
turns on: The explanations emphasize different acts of generosity: pardon and concealment, pure giving, disproportionate reward, or the broad pattern of benefaction.
- 82:6/q10/p1 Forgiving, pardoning, and concealing faults. — Ibn Qutayba associates forgiveness with nobility; Ibn Jawzi reports forgiving or generous. Qurtubi connects Fudayl's 'lowered veils' to karim as one who conceals. [3 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); QURTUBI-FULL(الفضيل بن عياض)]
- 82:6/q10/p2 A pure benefactor, giving without self-interest, supplying what is due and more while not demanding what is owed. — Tabrisi gives these two definitions: no gain sought or harm averted, and giving beyond obligation without exacting one's own claim. Tabatabai likewise says God seeks no benefit or repayment, tolerates wrong, and forgives ignorant sins. [3 notes: TABRISI; TABATABAI]
- 82:6/q10/p3 One who accepts a little and gives much, even transforming sins into good deeds. — Tabrisi gives the concise definition and then the stronger sign of changing sins into good deeds. [2 notes: TABRISI]
- 82:6/q10/p4 One who pardons when able, fulfills promises, gives lavishly, protects petitioners, and deals gently and directly with them. — Al-Ghazali, quoted by Biqai, also says such a one refuses others' rejection of a petitioner, admonishes without exacting all dues, and needs no intermediaries. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الإمام الغزالي)]
- 82:6/q10/p5 The gracious divine attribute of beauty. — Kuranyolu pairs karim with rabb as names of God's graciousness to servants. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 82:6/q11 Why name the Lord al-Karim, and what response should His generosity produce?  [بِرَبِّكَ ٱلْكَرِيمِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the epithet supplies an excuse of reliance on generosity or sharpens the moral reproach; Ibn Kathir and Elmalılı explicitly reject the former inference.
- 82:6/q11/p1 His generosity heightens the rebuke and should lead to gratitude, reverent fear, faith, and obedience. — Baydawi, Biqai, Bursevi, and Elmalılı deny that generosity requires neglecting wrongdoers or equating obedient and disobedient people; Bursevi adds that neglect would wrong victims and cites 15:49-50. Biqai says a noble benefactor is forbearing but formidable. Ibn Kathir says ugly acts must not repay generosity. Elmalılı says generosity joined to wisdom makes 'sin freely' a false analogy, compares 35:5, and notes shame and remorse in believers. Tabatabai says ingratitude is worse where the giver seeks no return. Ali's saying about servants' poor manners reflecting a master's discipline reinforces the moral analogy. [18 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(علي رضي الله عنه); BURSEVI; ELMALILI(Bazı ârifînin,Hazret-i Peygamber); TABATABAI; IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 82:6/q11/p2 The epithet cues the sinner's answer that the Generous One's generosity deluded him. — People of ishara and Tabrisi say the chosen epithet suggests this response; Ibn Atiyya connects it to kindness toward sinful believers. Elmalılı says the warning context rules it out, and Ibn Kathir calls the inference weak. [4 notes: THALABI(أهل الإشارة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(البغوي); IBNKATHIR-FULL-; ELMALILI-]

## 82:6/q12 What happened to an offender who struck the Prophet in the occasion reports?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on whether they merely mention delayed punishment or narrate the Prophet's immediate response and release.
- 82:6/q12/p1 He struck the Prophet and was not punished immediately, prompting the verse. — Wahidi attaches this to Abu al-Ashaddayn; Baghawi and Ibn Kathir attach it to al-Aswad ibn Shariq. Elmalılı repeats the incident without settling the name. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل); ELMALILI]
- 82:6/q12/p2 After striking the Prophet, the man was thrown down, requested safety, promised not to harm him, and was released. — Bursevi cites a report in Zahrat al-Riyad specifying the blow to the crown and the subsequent encounter. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 82:6/q13 What meeting and questions at resurrection does this verse evoke?  [* · reports]
- 82:6/q13/p1 Each person meets God alone and is asked what deluded him, how he used his knowledge, and how he answered the messengers. — Ibn Masud's report appears in Thalabi, Baghawi, Tabrisi, and Qurtubi; Ibn Kathir transmits a related hadith with the question about the messengers. [5 notes: THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن مسعود); TABRISI(عبد الله بن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 82:6/q14 What inwardly veils a person from God?  [مَا غَرَّكَ · inward]
turns on: The inward explanations identify either an intruding object of attention or abandonment of divine protection.
- 82:6/q14/p1 Anything other than God entering the heart cuts it off from Him. — Tustari says the believer's heart is nearest to the servant and an alien presence becomes a veil despite divine gentleness and generosity. Beholding God with the heart distances one from all else. [3 notes: TUSTARI]
- 82:6/q14/p2 Ignorantly leaving God's protection deludes the servant. — Umar ibn Wasil gives this answer on reciting the verse. [1 notes: TUSTARI(عمر بن واصل)]

## 82:6/q15 How should the servant live with God in response to this address?  [* · inward]
turns on: The notes describe complementary practices of watchful conduct and dependent reliance.
- 82:6/q15/p1 Guard the heart in awareness of God and persevere in works under His watch. — Tustari says surrendering the heart to God makes Him govern and straighten the limbs; the heart's testimony reflects how carefully the limbs were guarded. He urges awareness that the Creator is with creatures wherever they are. [3 notes: TUSTARI]
- 82:6/q15/p2 Seek God's pleasure, bring needs to Him, and rely on Him while recognizing one's weakness. — Tustari says God grants contentment through forbearance, urges staying at His door, and contrasts human ignorance, weakness, and incapacity with God's knowledge, strength, and ability; such reliance makes air, open space, earth, and sky alike. [4 notes: TUSTARI]

## 82:6/q16 Can an exclamation attributed to God express astonishment?  [مَا غَرَّكَ · theology]
turns on: Whether astonishment requires ignorance of a hidden cause and how divine language can refer to an audience's experience.
- 82:6/q16/p1 True astonishment at God is disallowed if it implies a hidden cause or that something made Him great. — Burhan reports this objection from eternal divine attributes. [2 notes: BURHAN(قيل)]
- 82:6/q16/p2 Language of marveling is permitted as magnification, without implying a cause of God's greatness. — Burhan reports that worshippers' belief in God's power licenses it and cites a poem about His bringing the distant near. Other scholars say astonishment magnifies the matter itself, without requiring another agent; a cause may simply be hidden to us. [2 notes: BURHAN(بعض المحققين,قيل)]
- 82:6/q16/p3 Divine exclamation describes what merits wonder from the audience's perspective, which God knows. — Burhan prefers this view; learned scholars similarly interpret divine exclamation, prayer, and hope through the hearer. Sibawayh explains 20:44 as Moses and Aaron acting on their own hope, an explanation praised by Ibn al-Da'i. [2 notes: BURHAN(المحققون); BURHAN+]

## 82:6/q17 How does this warning fit the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources emphasize different links: sequence from resurrection to denial, proof from creation, or a parallel warning elsewhere.
- 82:6/q17/p1 The resurrection announcement leads to a warning and rational appeal, with the person's denial of judgment exposed next. — Elmalılı says the passage moves from resurrection to reflection. Tabatabai reads 82:9 as naming the denier; Asad says it supplies the answer and compares the broad danger of fitnah in 8:25. Study Quran says 82:6–12 reproach and warn about the Last Day. Islahi says the present respite gives reform time, while recording angels ensure nothing is hidden. [6 notes: ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 82:6/q17/p2 Creation and formation of humans show God's ability, wisdom, mercy, and justice, making resurrection and judgment certain and purposeful. — Islahi argues that re-creation cannot be harder than first creation, and that signs in human formation entail a day of reward and punishment. Tabatabai makes lordship and generosity the proof against ingratitude. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]
- 82:6/q17/p3 The warning repeats the Qur'anic caution not to be deceived about God. — Elmalılı identifies the teaching with 35:5; Islahi elsewhere explicitly connects a warning to 82:6. [2 notes: ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 82:6/q18 How is al-Infitar related to al-Takwir?  [* · links]
- 82:6/q18/p1 They are counterparts in form, style, meaning, and opening catastrophe, but al-Infitar argues chiefly from creation and divine justice. — Islahi says both address arrogant rich Quraysh. Al-Takwir argues from the Qur'an's authenticity, source, revelation, and bearer, with rejecters unable to distinguish dawn from night; al-Infitar argues from God's creative power, wisdom, justice, and mercy toward a coming reckoning. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 82:6/q19 Where else does the Qur'an begin a verse with 'O human'?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · links]
- 82:6/q19/p1 Two verses do: this question about the Generous Lord and 84:6 about striving toward the Lord. — Burhan counts the two openings; Ibn Qutayba also cites 84:6 when discussing singular collective address. [2 notes: BURHAN; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]

## 82:6/q20 When was the surah revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The reported authorities differ on whether its Meccan revelation was early or late.
- 82:6/q20/p1 Some authorities place it in early Mecca. — Asad reports this dating. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]
- 82:6/q20/p2 Others consider it more likely among the last Meccan revelations. — Asad reports this later dating as more likely. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]
