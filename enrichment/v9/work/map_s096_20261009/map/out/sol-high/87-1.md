# 87:1: 34 questions

## 87:1/q01 What does sabbih command?  [سَبِّحِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether tasbih names declaration, a particular utterance, prayer, vocal praise, or a comprehensive devotional act.
- 87:1/q01/p1 Declare God free of all defects, false claims, rivals, and unworthy attributes and acts. — Muqatil makes this denial of shirk and affirmation of God's oneness. Zajjaj, Ibn Atiyya, Jishumi, Biqai, Ibn Ashur and others include evil, change, deficiency, idolatry, anthropomorphism, coercion, and claims of polytheists; Jishumi adds wisdom and goodness of divine acts. Elmalili includes spoken, inward, and practical worship; Qutb insists on inward awareness, not rote repetition. Ibn Ashur says the religious sense may be borrowed from Hebrew; Corpus Coranicum instead adduces Syriac shabbeh and the Hebrew psalmic imperative. [40 notes: MUQATIL(مقاتل); TUSTARI; TAB(بعض أهل التأويل); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(بعضهم); THALABI(قوم); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,الزجاج); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 87:1/q01/p2 Say 'Glory be to my Lord, the Most High' as the command's verbal fulfillment. — Ibn Abbas, Qatada and groups of Companions and Successors explain the command by the formula; transmitted recitation practice supports it. Alusi says an utterance of subhan requires treating ism as inserted. Tabari and Nahhas cite the Prophet's and Companions' practice in support of their interpretations. [17 notes: THALABI(جماعة من الصحابة والتابعين); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT(قيل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن عباس وقتادة); BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجمهور); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(ابن عباس,القمي); ALUSI+; NAHHAS+; TAB+]
- 87:1/q01/p3 Magnify and exalt the Lord. — Tabari reports that none is greater or higher. Ibn Abbas and al-Suddi say ism is added to magnify the referent; Labid's verse supports that idiom. Jarir's verse is cited for vocal devotional praise. [8 notes: TAB(بعض أهل التأويل); MAWARDI(ابن عباس والسدي); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس والسدي,لبيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI(ابن عباس والسدي,جرير); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس والسدي)]
- 87:1/q01/p4 Pray to the Lord or pray using His name or command. — Ibn Abbas variously says by the Lord's command or in His name, likening it to beginning in God's name with an omitted preposition. Mawardi adds mentioning Him at prayer's opening or holding Him in mind at intention; Razi cites prostration reports and 30:17. Elmalili notes prayer as a conventional tasbih sense but objects that the omitted preposition is against the surface construction; Alusi doubts the Ibn Abbas report. [29 notes: TAB(آخرون); TAB-FULL(آخرون); NAHHAS(بعضهم); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI(unattributed); MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI; QURTUBI(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس,الحسن,قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس,الحسن); ABUHAYYAN(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(إبن عباس); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:1/q01/p5 Raise the voice in remembrance and praise of God. — Mawardi and Qurtubi cite Jarir's line about pilgrims chanting and magnifying God; Bursevi says this makes ism meaningful. [6 notes: MAWARDI(unattributed); MAWARDI-FULL(جرير); QURTUBI(جرير,قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); BURSEVI(جرير)]
- 87:1/q01/p6 Practice sustained inward remembrance, obedience, and moral discipline. — Tustari joins outward denial of rivals to inward witnessing in prayer. Sulami forbids idle talk and lies afterward. Qushayri describes knowledge of the names and inward swimming in exaltedness; Islahi links remembrance and tahajjud to comfort, perseverance, protection from Satan, and a sound grasp of God. Qutb calls praise an experiential gift. [12 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI; QUSHAYRI; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 87:1/q02 What does ism mean here?  [ٱسْمَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the direct object is the divine word, an honorific stand-in for God, the act of naming, an attribute, or a broader sign.
- 87:1/q02/p1 God's name itself is the object of sanctification. — Tabari, Abu Hayyan, Alusi and Elmalili emphasize the direct object; the name must not be given to idols, misinterpreted, or used irreverently. Ibn Ashur says spoken reports and sacred attributes are applied to names designating God; purely inward contemplation concerns God directly. Elmalili says the clearest sense is revealed linguistic names and attributes, though signs can indicate Him more broadly. [25 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; TAB-FULL+; TAB+]
- 87:1/q02/p2 Ism is an honorific added expression, or stands for God, the one named. — Ibn Abbas and al-Suddi use Labid's 'name of Peace' to illustrate the idiom; Razi says Arabic mentions an exalted person's name instead of the person. Abu Ali adduces 'the name of peace be upon you.' Baghowi and Qurtubi argue that worshippers say 'Glory to my Lord,' not to His name. Alusi conditionally favors insertion if sabbih means utter subhan; Elmalili says sanctification of the referent still proceeds through its name. [41 notes: THALABI(قوم); MAWARDI(ابن عباس والسدي); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس والسدي,لبيد); JISHUMI(أبو علي); BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI(لبيد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(ابن عباس والسدي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس والسدي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BURSEVI(بعضهم,سعدي المفتي); ALUSI(كثير); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(لبيد); ALUSI+; QURTUBI+]
- 87:1/q02/p3 Ism means naming, mention, or the act of speaking of God. — Tabari and others require fearful humility. Ibn Atiyya cites Arabic usage and the hadith on God's ninety-nine names for naming as an act. Qurtubi reports this but favors the named referent. [19 notes: TAB(آخرون); TAB-FULL(آخرون); NAHHAS(بعضهم); THALABI(آخرون); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA(قوم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الثعلبي); QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI(جمع); ALUSI-FULL; QURTUBI-]
- 87:1/q02/p4 Ism means God's attribute or attributes. — Abu Muslim cites 7:180; Jishumi says to cleanse divine attributes of what is unfitting. Elmalili describes a Sufi account in which names are attributes and effects follow them. [5 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); RAZI(أبو مسلم); RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 87:1/q02/p5 Ism means God's oneness. — Damghani compares 73:8, taking glorification and remembrance of the name as affirmation of divine unity. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]
- 87:1/q02/p6 Ism means God's effects or created signs. — Isam al-Din says effects point to God as a name does and cites 67:3. Alusi finds it remote. Elmalili says one must not blame creatures insofar as they are God's work, but finds the sense strained for lack of a contextual cue; effects remain distinct from names in the Sufi account. [5 notes: ALUSI(عصام الدين); ALUSI-FULL(عصام الدين); ELMALILI(İsâmuddin); ELMALILI-]
- 87:1/q02/p7 Ism comprises whatever makes God known, including names, attributes, reason, and natural signs. — Abduh relates this to God's face in 55:27-78 and Adam's taught names in 2:31; this rules out likeness, incarnation, partners and offspring, while verses 2-3 identify Creator and Guide. Elmalili allows it as metaphor but finds leaving the ordinary linguistic sense remote. [4 notes: ELMALILI(Şeyh Abduh)]

## 87:1/q03 Do sabbih isma and sabbih bi-ismi mean the same thing?  [سَبِّحِ ٱسْمَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether bi- is freely alternating, semantically distinct, or understood as omitted in an interpretation of prayer.
- 87:1/q03/p1 Both Arabic constructions convey the same glorification. — Al-Farra affirms both forms; Thalabi restricts the equivalence to contexts of remembrance and glorification. Razi reports the comparison with 56:74. Samarrai lists Quranic transitive and prepositional patterns. [7 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(الفراء); THALABI(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); RAZI(الفراء); RAZI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; NAHHAS-; WAHIDI-BASIT-]
- 87:1/q03/p2 The direct object sanctifies the name; bi- means glorifying God by means of His name. — Nahhas rejects a simple deletion of the preposition and says transitivity changes sense. Wahidi distinguishes the object from instrument; Elmalili says the chosen direct object deliberately sanctifies the name itself. Ibn Ashur says bi pairs speech with speech in other praise constructions. [8 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI(الواحدي); RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; NAHHAS+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 87:1/q03/p3 An omitted bi- permits reading the command as praying in the Lord's name. — Ibn Abbas compares 'begin in God's name'; Alusi doubts the transmission and Elmalili says the analysis opposes the apparent wording. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(إبن عباس); ELMALILI]

## 87:1/q04 Is God's name identical with the one named?  [ٱسْمَ · theology]
turns on: Whether ism denotes a spoken designation or the divine referent, and whether the customary formula proves identity.
- 87:1/q04/p1 The name is identical with the named God in this context. — The author of al-Nazm says only God, not a distinct name, can be glorified. Baghowi and Qurtubi cite 'Glory to God/my Lord,' never 'Glory to God's name'; Sa'di al-Mufti uses the hadith formula likewise. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم,سعدي المفتي); QURTUBI+; RAZI-; RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 87:1/q04/p2 The wording and the entity differ; calling them identical equivocates or is tautological. — Razi says a word differs from its referent, while identity follows trivially if both terms denote the entity; perhaps confusion arose because 'name' is itself a noun for a timeless meaning. He rejects the formula argument since the verse admits several analyses. Alusi rejects identity. [9 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 87:1/q05 How must God's names be guarded?  [ٱسْمَ · interpretation]
turns on: The scope of sanctifying a divine designation: assignment, interpretation, occasion and manner of speech, authorization, and figurative poetic address.
- 87:1/q05/p1 Do not transfer exclusive divine names to idols, false claimants, or creatures as God's equals. — Tabari cites al-Lat and al-Uzza; Nahhas derives the idol names from al-Aziz and Allah. Razi adds Musaylima's 'Merciful of Yamama.' Alusi distinguishes exclusive names from shared names used as equal providers. Tabatabai expands the rule to attributing divine acts to others and associating intercessors. [29 notes: TAB(آخرون); TAB-FULL(بعضهم); NAHHAS(بعضهم); MAWARDI(الطبري); MAWARDI-FULL(الطبري); IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(الطبري); QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري); ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; TAB-FULL+; TAB+]
- 87:1/q05/p2 Interpret divine names without unworthy implications such as embodiment, spatial settlement, compulsion, or false likeness. — Kashshaf specifically opposes coercion and anthropomorphism. Baydawi and Alusi reject unjustified figurative interpretation as well as impossible literal senses; Ibn Ashur makes divine perfection the governing rule for ambiguous descriptions. Elmalili cites 42:11. [18 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; BURSEVI; ALUSI(الكشاف); ALUSI-FULL(الكشاف); ELMALILI(Sâhib-Keşşâf); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:1/q05/p3 Utter God's names only with humility, understanding, and in fitting contexts. — Tabari and Thalabi stress reverent remembrance; Sulami warns against lies and idle talk afterward. Bursevi names yawning, relieving oneself, and excessive oaths. Alusi cites Malik declining to invoke God before a beggar who disliked hearing it. Ibn Ashur joins speech to understanding. [28 notes: TAB(آخرون); TAB-FULL(آخرون); NAHHAS(بعضهم); SULAMI(بعضهم); THALABI(آخرون); BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA(قوم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الثعلبي); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; KASHSHAF; BURSEVI; ALUSI(الإمام,مالك); ALUSI-FULL(مالك); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI]
- 87:1/q05/p4 Avoid provoking unbelievers to insult God. — Tabari and Razi connect this to the prohibition on reviling pagan deities in 6:108. [4 notes: TAB(غيرهم); TAB-FULL(آخرون); RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 87:1/q05/p5 Use only names established by revelation when naming God. — Razi cites 17:110 and the distinction from pagan whistling and clapping; Alusi's critics cite 7:180 against addressing God as Layla or Na'im. Bursevi reports the fixed-name position. Ibn Ashur says al-A'la is not separately counted among beautiful names because al-Ali already names the quality and names require name-like established usage. [9 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI(بعض الأجبلة); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR]
- 87:1/q05/p6 Any designation free of deficiency may name God even if revelation did not establish it. — Razi attributes this to the Mu'tazilites, contrasting it with his revealed-name position. [2 notes: RAZI(المعتزلة); RAZI-FULL]
- 87:1/q05/p7 Poetic Layla and Na'im are defensible as extended metaphors, without literally naming God by them. — Alusi reports a defense by allusion and analogy to allegorical readings of 2:67, says critics remain unsatisfied, and prefers a whole-expression metaphor; his full account compares metaphorical basmala interpretation. [6 notes: ALUSI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI+; ALUSI(بعض الأجبلة)-]

## 87:1/q06 What does rabbika evoke?  [رَبِّكَ · rhetoric]
- 87:1/q06/p1 The Lord is the creator, nurturer, benefactor, and governor; 'your' gives the Prophet a special personal address. — Biqai says the Lord formed and nurtured the Prophet in noble qualities; Qutb emphasizes tender care; Ibn Ashur says Rabb rather than Allah calls up creation and governance and the possessive honors the Prophet. [5 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR]

## 87:1/q07 Who is commanded to glorify?  [سَبِّحِ رَبِّكَ · referent]
- 87:1/q07/p1 The Prophet is directly addressed, with a charge that guides believers too. — Ibn Ashur identifies the addressee; Biqai says the perfected Messenger understands how to glorify fully; Islahi reads the address as strengthening the Prophet amid his preaching fears. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 87:1/q08 What does al-aʿla qualify?  [ٱلْأَعْلَى · grammar]
turns on: Case and the attachment of the following relative clause 'who created' in 87:2.
- 87:1/q08/p1 It qualifies rabb, 'Lord,' in the genitive. — Nahhas prefers this. Kashshaf, Alusi and Study Quran call it the more natural or majority parse. Abu Hayyan and Samin note that if 'who created' in 87:2 qualifies rabb, an intervening modifier of ism would separate them. Corpus Coranicum compares the Hebrew epithet following a divine title. [16 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN(قالوا); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; CORPUSCORANICUM; NAHHAS+]
- 87:1/q08/p2 It qualifies ism, 'name,' in the accusative. — Jishumi and Tabrisi connect this to God's exalted names. Abu Hayyan says 'who created' must then be detached as a predicate of an omitted subject or an accusative of praise; Samin also allows it to qualify ism. Alusi says such a name shares its essential meaning with no other; Elmalili says all divine names are beautiful though of ranks. [19 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN(قالوا); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]

## 87:1/q09 What kind of highness does al-aʿla express?  [ٱلْأَعْلَى · theology]
turns on: Whether highness denotes spatial direction, absolute excellence and power, ungraspable greatness, or a non-comparative adjective.
- 87:1/q09/p1 Supremacy in power, sovereignty, perfection, and rank, without spatial location. — Jishumi cites al-Farazdaq's poetry for honor and strength; Tabrisi compares al-akbar. Razi argues a finite or infinite corporeal deity is impossible and creation in 87:2 suits power, not distance. Ibn Ashur reads the superlative as absolute superiority and figurative enduring perfection, citing 79:24 and al-Ghazali's sensory versus intellectual ranks. Qutb evokes limitless horizons. [30 notes: JISHUMI(الفرزدق); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR(الغزالي); TABATABAI]
- 87:1/q09/p2 God is elevated in a physical direction. — Razi attributes this proof from al-a'la to corporealists, then rejects it by arguments about finite and infinite dimensions and by the context of praise and creation. [1 notes: RAZI(المجسمة); RAZI-; RAZI-FULL-]
- 87:1/q09/p3 God's grandeur, favors, and rightful claims exceed all human knowledge, praise, thanks, and obedience. — Razi gives this as one sense; Bursevi says knowers cannot grasp His height and should be humbled. [3 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 87:1/q09/p4 Al-aʿla can simply mean al-ʿali, 'high,' as al-akbar can mean 'great.' — Razi offers this comparison as another lexical option. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 87:1/q09/p5 The 'Great' and 'Highest' epithets imply two different lords. — Razi reports a skeptic's objection tied to 56:74 and answers that reason establishes one Creator and neither verse compares Him to another lord. [1 notes: RAZI(الملحدون); RAZI-; RAZI-FULL-]

## 87:1/q10 Why is the Lord called al-aʿla here?  [ٱلْأَعْلَى · rhetoric]
turns on: The explanatory role of the epithet and the extent to which verse-end cadence explains its form.
- 87:1/q10/p1 His superlative rank makes transcendence fitting, and the following acts establish His power and care. — Biqai says rank is absolute. Elmalili says the title explains why sanctification is owed and verses 2-5 establish it; Ibn Ashur selects creation, guidance and provision as fitting attributes, and links divine highness to the Quran's dignity and the Prophet's reception. [7 notes: BIQAI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 87:1/q10/p2 The ending contributes to the sura's cadence, but its choice carries meaning beyond rhyme. — Burhan compares the extra endings in al-A'la and al-'Alaq; Bint al-Shati rejects making cadence the sole reason for al-a'la rather than al-'ula, comparing 92:20. Qutb hears the opening's extended rhythm. [3 notes: BURHAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; QUTB-ZILAL]

## 87:1/q11 What variant wording or pronunciation is reported for this opening?  [سَبِّحِ ٱسْمَ رَبِّكَ ٱلْأَعْلَى · readings]
turns on: Whether transmitted subhana phrases are variant text or recitation responses, and how the final vowel is pronounced.
- 87:1/q11/p1 The opening is recited as 'Subhana rabbiya al-aʿla,' sometimes followed by 'who created and proportioned.' — Ibn Umar, Ubayy, Ali, Abu Musa, Ibn al-Zubayr, Malik b. Dinar and others are named across sources. Reports vary between an actual alternate reading and a response after the canonical verse. Qurtubi denies the phrase is Quranic text; Corpus Coranicum retains the common imperative because similar imperatives open early Meccan passages. [20 notes: TAB(أبي بن كعب,ابن عمر); TAB-FULL(ابن عمر); MAWARDI(أبي وابن عمر); MAWARDI-FULL(علي وابن عباس وابن عمر); KASHSHAF(علي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(ابن عمر وعلي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو موسى الأشعري,ابن عمر); RAZI(علي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(أبي بن كعب); QURTUBI-FULL(علي وابن عباس وابن عمر وابن الزبير وأبو موسى وابن مسعود); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI(أبي بن كعب); ALUSI-FULL(أبي بن كعب); CORPUSCORANICUM(Muʿǧam); QURTUBI(علي وابن عباس وابن عمرو وابن الزبير وأبو موسى وابن مسعود)-]
- 87:1/q11/p2 The opening is also reported as 'Subhana ismi rabbika al-aʿla.' — Ibn Umar is heard using this form, attributed to Ubayy's reading; the reports vary in whether the possessive of subhana is ismi or rabbi. [3 notes: DURR(ابن عمر); DURR-FULL(أبي بن كعب,ابن عمر)]
- 87:1/q11/p3 A shorter wording, 'Sabbih rabbaka al-aʿla,' is reported. — Durr attributes this to Ibn al-Zubayr and al-Dahhak, who also directed the reciter to add the glorification. [2 notes: DURR(الضحاك,عبد الله بن الزبير)]
- 87:1/q11/p4 The common imperative sabbih isma rabbika al-aʿla is retained. — Corpus Coranicum cites analogous early Meccan imperative openings, including 69:52 and 56:74; Qurtubi treats the added subhana phrase as devotional response rather than Quran. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(علي وابن عباس وابن عمر وابن الزبير وأبو موسى وابن مسعود); CORPUSCORANICUM(Muʿǧam)+; QURTUBI(علي وابن عباس وابن عمرو وابن الزبير وأبو موسى وابن مسعود)+]
- 87:1/q11/p5 The rhyme ending al-aʿla admits imala. — Ibn al-Jazari says the imala of endings from al-a'la through Musa was treated in his earlier chapter. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 87:1/q12 What should a reciter say after this verse?  [سَبِّحِ ٱلْأَعْلَى · reports]
- 87:1/q12/p1 Say 'Subhana rabbiya al-aʿla' as a response to the command, including in prayer. — Reports name the Prophet, Ibn Abbas, Ali, Ibn Umar, Umar, Abu Musa, Ibn al-Zubayr, al-Dahhak, Qatada and al-Baqir. Ibn Abbas also advised responding at 75:40; Ali said he obeyed the command rather than added Quran. Qurtubi recommends the response and denies it is Quranic text. - 87:1/q32/p1 Ambiguous descriptions must be interpreted in a way befitting the Most High's complete perfection. — Ibn Ashur makes divine highness the standard for reading other attributes and requires figurative interpretation when literal description conflicts with it. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 87:1/q33 What does God’s uniqueness mean for His attributes?  [ٱلْأَعْلَى · theology]
- 87:1/q33/p1 God alone deserves His attributes and transcends the attributes of created things. — Jishumi presents this as a further explanation of the epithet. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 87:1/q34 What did Ibn Umar say about speaking of God's name?  [ٱسْمَ ٱلْأَعْلَى · reports]
- 87:1/q34/p1 He instructed not to say 'upon God's name,' since God's name is Most High. — Nafi transmits the remark; Qurtubi's full account repeats it. [2 notes: QURTUBI(نافع عن ابن عمر); QURTUBI-FULL(نافع عن ابن عمر)]
