# 80:2: 24 questions

## 80:2/q01 How is أن جاءه construed?  [أَن جَآءَهُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether أن is causal or temporal; for the causal construal, its case and which preceding verb governs it; an interrogative reading requires an implied clause.
- 80:2/q01/p1 It gives the cause: he frowned or turned away because the blind man came. — Al-Farra, al-Tabari, al-Zajjaj, Ibn Atiyya, Ibn al-Jawzi and al-Tabataba'i give the causal sense. Jishumi glosses the frown and turning of the face on his arrival. Al-Zajjaj and al-Nahhas call it accusative; al-Nahhas supplies an omitted li-anna. Al-Nasafi calls it a purpose expression. Al-Samin says both actions followed the arrival, and notes the familiar case dispute after deletion of the preposition. [12 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(من النحويين); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI; JISHUMI]
- 80:2/q01/p2 The causal expression is governed by تولى, the nearer verb. — Al-Tabrisi names the nearest verb; al-Samin prefers the Basran analysis because it avoids ellipsis for the second competing verb; Bursevi also attributes it to Basran grammarians. Al-Baydawi allows this attachment. [5 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(المبصريون); SAMIN-DURR+]
- 80:2/q01/p3 The causal expression is governed by عبس. — Al-Samin attributes this to Kufan grammarians; al-Nasafi and al-Baydawi record the alternative attachment. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 80:2/q01/p4 An omitted lām governs أن in the genitive, relating the arrival to both verbs. — Al-Nahhas reports genitive through omitted lām. Ibn Ashur calls lām deletion regular and links both verbs by syntactic competition. [2 notes: NAHHAS(من النحويين); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 80:2/q01/p5 أن means إذ, ‘when.’ — Al-Nahhas records this as a third analysis; Makki transmits it according to al-Samin. [2 notes: NAHHAS(من النحويين); SAMIN-DURR(مكي)]
- 80:2/q01/p6 On the interrogative reading an omitted verb is supplied from ‘he frowned and turned away.’ — Al-Tabrisi supplies the omitted clause and gives the sense ‘Has the blind man just come to him?’ Al-Qurtubi reports al-Hasan's analysis and pause after تولى. [2 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن)]

## 80:2/q02 How is the opening أن read and where is the pause?  [أَن جَآءَهُ · readings]
turns on: The opening hamza's number and length, interrogative force, and whether recitation pauses after تولى.
- 80:2/q02/p1 The usual declarative reading has one short hamza and continues without a pause after تولى. — Al-Samin calls this the usual reading; al-Qurtubi says its recitation does not pause after تولى. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 80:2/q02/p2 It is read interrogatively with a lengthened opening hamza and a pause after تولى. — Al-Farra and al-Tabari compare an interrogative reading of 68:14. Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi attribute this to al-Hasan, Ibn Atiyya also to Isa. Ibn al-Jawzi lists Ubayy, al-Hasan, Abu al-Mutawakkil and Abu Imran. Al-Tabrisi calls al-Hasan's reading noncanonical. Reports vary in describing its hamza and length. [6 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(بعض القراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن، عيسى); TABRISI(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبي بن كعب والحسن وأبو المتوكل وأبو عمران); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 80:2/q02/p3 It is read interrogatively with two open hamzas, sometimes with an intervening alif. — Ibn al-Jawzi names Ibn Masud and Ibn al-Sumayfi for two short hamzas. Al-Samin names Zayd ibn Ali, Isa and Abu Imran al-Jawni for two hamzas and cites al-Zamakhshari's آأن with intervening alif; al-Baydawi reports that form. Al-Farra's report also describes two open hamzas. The interrogative asks whether the arrival caused the frown. [5 notes: FARRA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود وابن السميفع); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)]

## 80:2/q03 Who is the blind man?  [ٱلْأَعْمَىٰ · referent]
- 80:2/q03/p1 He is Ibn Umm Maktum, the blind Muslim who came seeking instruction. — Mujahid and Qatada place him in Banu Fihr; reports variously supply personal names and lineages, treated separately below. Ibn Ashur says the definite description refers to a particular known visitor. Islahi reports exegetical agreement. The occasion reports connect his visit to instruction, guidance or recitation. [54 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الضحاك,عائشة,عروة,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-ASBAB(عائشة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس,ابن عباس,عائشة,غير واحد من المفسرين); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو مالك,أنس,ابن عباس,الضحاك,مجاهد,مسروق); ITQAN; SUYUTI-LUBAB(أنس,عائشة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(بعض روايات الشيعة,طرق أهل السنة,طرق الشيعة,مجمع البيان); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(All exegetes); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 80:2/q04 What personal name is reported for Ibn Umm Maktum?  [ٱلْأَعْمَىٰ · reports]
turns on: The differing personal names and lineages transmitted for the man called Ibn Umm Maktum.
- 80:2/q04/p1 He is Abd Allah ibn Shurayh ibn Malik ibn Rabiah, with lineage variants. — Al-Baghawi supplies the Fihri lineage; al-Tabataba'i quotes al-Majma with it, and Asad calls him Abd Allah ibn Shurayh. Bursevi and Alusi list Abd Allah ibn Shurayh ibn Malik among alternative names. Names and ancestor spellings vary in reports. [5 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); ASAD-NOTES]
- 80:2/q04/p2 He is Amr ibn Qays ibn Zaida. — Ibn al-Jawzi gives Amr ibn Qays; Bursevi identifies this person as Khadija's cousin. Alusi says this is the most famous identification according to Jami al-usul. Muqatil's account also calls him Umm Maktum's son Amr ibn Qays. [4 notes: MUQATIL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 80:2/q04/p3 He is Abd Allah ibn Zayd or Zaida. — Qatada's report in al-Tabari has Abd Allah ibn Zayd; al-Mawardi records Abdallah ibn Zaida of Banu Fihr. The forms vary. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 80:2/q04/p4 He is Abd Allah ibn Amr. — Ibn al-Jawzi reports this as another name; Alusi lists it among the alternatives. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 80:2/q04/p5 He is Abd Allah ibn Abi Musrah al-Amar. — Muqatil gives this name alongside Amr ibn Qays and lineages. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 80:2/q05 Who was Umm Maktum?  [ٱلْأَعْمَىٰ · reports]
turns on: The genealogy behind the man's kunya; reports also differ on the mother's given name.
- 80:2/q05/p1 She was his mother. — Al-Nasafi names her as mother; al-Sadi corrects al-Kashshaf and cites Ibn Abd al-Barr, calling her Atika bint Amir. Alusi calls Umm Maktum the mother's kunya but names Atika bint Abd Allah al-Makhzumiyya. The reported personal name varies. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; BURSEVI(السعدي)+]
- 80:2/q05/p2 She was his paternal grandmother. — Al-Farra identifies her this way. Bursevi cites al-Sadi's objection to the Kashshaf's version, and Alusi says al-Zamakhshari was wrong on this point. [1 notes: FARRA; BURSEVI(السعدي)-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 80:2/q06 What does al-aʿmā mean here?  [ٱلْأَعْمَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word is discussed in its literal use for this man or its wider figurative range.
- 80:2/q06/p1 It denotes physical loss of sight. — Al-Damghani cites this visitor and 24:61; Ibn al-Jawzi states blindness of sight. Bursevi recognizes this literal sense alongside a figurative sense. The report of his needing others to identify dawn confirms his physical blindness. [4 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; BURSEVI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 80:2/q06/p2 Blindness can figuratively mean loss of insight. — Bursevi gives this additional Arabic sense, while saying the definite article identifies the familiar Ibn Umm Maktum. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 80:2/q07 Was Ibn Umm Maktum born blind?  [ٱلْأَعْمَىٰ · reports]
turns on: Conflicting biographical reports about when his blindness began.
- 80:2/q07/p1 He was born blind. — Alusi reports this and says it explains his mother's kunya. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 80:2/q07/p2 He became blind after once seeing. — Alusi records this as the alternative report. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 80:2/q08 Why call the visitor ‘the blind man’?  [ٱلْأَعْمَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the description primarily explains his interruption, heightens his claim to compassion, or sharpens the reproach.
- 80:2/q08/p1 His blindness excuses his interruption and makes his need for care especially clear. — Al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, al-Biqai and Ibn Ashur say it prompts compassion or softens the rebuke; al-Biqai says it explains his insistence and repairs his hurt. Elmali extends the lesson to all guidance seekers with excuses or needs. Al-Tabataba'i stresses his fear of God and need for guidance. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 80:2/q08/p2 The description sharpens reproach for turning away from someone blind. — Al-Baydawi says it may suggest that blindness itself prompted the aversion; al-Tabataba'i says the fearful seeker deserved special attention. Ibn Atiyya contrasts disability with the unbeliever's wealth and human inclination to slight disability. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 80:2/q08/p3 Naming an impairment is permissible when useful or as a clear identifying nickname. — Ibn Atiyya cites transmitters known as al-Amash, al-Araj and al-Aftas. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 80:2/q09 Who frowned and turned away?  [جَآءَهُ · referent]
turns on: Whether third-person wording and the Prophet's established character allow or rule out identifying him as the actor; an al-Sadiq report names an Umayyad man.
- 80:2/q09/p1 The Prophet did so when Ibn Umm Maktum came. — The common occasion reports make the Prophet the subject. Bursevi explicitly identifies the pronoun. Islahi says the later ‘you know’ addresses the Prophet. The third-person opening is explained as a rhetorical choice by several authors. [33 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الضحاك,عائشة,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-ASBAB; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(غير واحد من المفسرين); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو مالك,أنس,ابن عباس); SUYUTI-LUBAB(عائشة); BURSEVI(المبصريون); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(طرق أهل السنة,طرق الشيعة,مجمع البيان); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; JISHUMI(المرتضى)-; TABATABAI(المرتضى)-]
- 80:2/q09/p2 Someone else, specifically an Umayyad man in al-Sadiq's report, frowned and turned away. — Al-Sadiq's account places the Umayyad man with the Prophet. Jishumi and al-Tabrisi point to the third-person wording and the Prophet's praised character (68:4; 3:159); al-Murtada says neglecting poor Muslims does not resemble his conduct. Al-Tabataba'i adds early Meccan commands in 26:214–15 and 15:88,94 and argues that wealth preference is rationally ugly even without a prohibition. [12 notes: JISHUMI(المرتضى); TABRISI(الصادق); TABATABAI(الصادق,المرتضى,بعض روايات الشيعة); JISHUMI+; TABATABAI+]

## 80:2/q10 What happened when the blind man came?  [جَآءَهُ ٱلْأَعْمَىٰ · reports]
turns on: Competing accounts of the occasion and the actor; transmission assessments and variant details of the interruption.
- 80:2/q10/p1 He sought instruction while the Prophet invited Quraysh leaders; the Prophet turned from him and revelation followed. — Reports say he repeatedly asked for guidance, Qur'an teaching, a recitation or explanation, unaware the Prophet was occupied. The Prophet hoped the leaders' conversion would draw followers. Al-Tirmidhi notes that a version via Hisham ibn Urwa omits Aisha, as does the Muwatta; Ibn Kathir calls the Ibn Abbas report via al-Awfi strange and objectionable because its chain was criticized. Asad calls the traditions well authenticated. Al-Tabataba'i notes Sunni and some Shi'i versions of this account; versions differ on which leaders were present. [47 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,عائشة,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-ASBAB(عائشة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس,ابن عباس,عائشة,غير واحد من المفسرين,هشام بن عروة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو مالك,أنس,ابن عباس,الضحاك,مسروق); SUYUTI-LUBAB(أنس,عائشة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الطبري); TABATABAI(السيوطي,طرق أهل السنة,طرق الشيعة,مجمع البيان); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some narratives); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 80:2/q10/p2 He came after hearing two angels in the Sacred Mosque and sought repentance; the Prophet returned to al-Abbas and Umayya, and the man left despairing. — Muqatil gives the distinctive angelic scene: one angel mentioned Abu Talib's calling people to Islam without seeing them. The man repeated his plea before departing, then the verses came. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 80:2/q10/p3 He came with a seeing guide, pressed past the guide despite a restraining gesture, and the Prophet frowned. — Ibn Zayd's report says the Prophet gestured to the guide to hold him back; al-Qurtubi calls the man's persistence somewhat rough. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد)]
- 80:2/q10/p4 The Umayyad man with the Prophet recoiled from Ibn Umm Maktum and frowned; God condemned that conduct. — Al-Sadiq's account is quoted by al-Tabrisi and al-Tabataba'i; it differs over who performed the frown, while keeping Ibn Umm Maktum's entry. [3 notes: TABRISI(الصادق); TABATABAI(الصادق,بعض روايات الشيعة)]

## 80:2/q11 Which leaders were present in the encounter?  [جَآءَهُ · reports]
turns on: The different names and numbers supplied by occasion reports. Ibn al-Arabi objects to identifications involving al-Walid, Umayya or al-Abbas on a Medina chronology, whereas Alusi says Ibn Umm Maktum was Meccan and could meet them.
- 80:2/q11/p1 Quraysh leaders or a wealthy notable, without names. — Many accounts leave the leaders unnamed. Al-Dahhak speaks of one wealthy polytheist; Aisha's report through Ibn Kathir adds that the notables saw no fault in what the Prophet said. Bursevi says leaders' conversion might draw the public. [24 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(الضحاك,عائشة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-ASBAB(عائشة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عائشة,غير واحد من المفسرين); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك); SUYUTI-LUBAB(عائشة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some narratives); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 80:2/q11/p2 Utba, Abu Jahl and al-Abbas. — Ibn Abbas's reports name this trio; Ibn Kathir questions the al-Awfi transmission of his report. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 80:2/q11/p3 Al-Abbas and Umayya ibn Khalaf. — Muqatil and al-Qushayri name these two. [2 notes: MUQATIL; QUSHAYRI]
- 80:2/q11/p4 Umayya ibn Khalaf. — Qatada and Abu Malik name Umayya alone; Mujahid pairs him with Utba as the self-sufficient men. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(أبو مالك,مجاهد)]
- 80:2/q11/p5 Ubayy ibn Khalaf. — Qatada's separate version and Anas's report name Ubayy. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس); DURR-FULL(أنس)]
- 80:2/q11/p6 Utba and Shayba. — Aisha's report through Masruq names them; al-Hakim authenticated it. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(مسروق)]
- 80:2/q11/p7 Utba, Abu Jahl, al-Abbas, Ubayy and Umayya. — Al-Baghawi and al-Majma as cited by al-Tabataba'i give this larger list. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; TABATABAI(مجمع البيان)]
- 80:2/q11/p8 Utba and Umayya. — Mujahid identifies these as the self-sufficient men. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 80:2/q12 Could al-Walid, Umayya or al-Abbas have met Ibn Umm Maktum at this event?  [جَآءَهُ · reports]
turns on: Whether the encounter is located in Mecca or Medina.
- 80:2/q12/p1 No: a Medina location makes those Meccan figures impossible participants. — Ibn al-Arabi argues Ibn Umm Maktum was in Medina while al-Walid and Umayya were in Mecca, died unbelievers, and Umayya never came to Medina. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)+; ALUSI-FULL(القرطبي)-]
- 80:2/q12/p2 Yes: Ibn Umm Maktum was Meccan and met the named Quraysh leaders. — Alusi directly corrects al-Qurtubi's chronology. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(القرطبي)+; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)-]

## 80:2/q13 Why did the Prophet turn away in the usual account?  [جَآءَهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the immediate concern was a lost opening for conversion, leaders' view of poor followers, or harm they might inflict on the visitor.
- 80:2/q13/p1 He was intent on converting Quraysh leaders, hoping their people would follow, and disliked losing the moment to an interruption. — Al-Farra, al-Zajjaj, al-Qurtubi, al-Biqai, Bursevi and Ibn Ashur stress the hoped-for conversion of leaders and followers. Al-Qurtubi notes Ibn Umm Maktum already believed; Ibn Ashur mentions fear that the gathering would disperse. Bursevi allows that the Prophet may simply not have heard the low voice. [14 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(غير واحد من المفسرين); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 80:2/q13/p2 He feared leaders would disdain followers who were blind, poor or enslaved. — Al-Tha'labi, al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi, Mujahid and al-Majma describe this concern. Islahi says the leaders already disparaged poor companions as evidence against prophethood; he denies that poverty or blindness itself caused the Prophet's displeasure. [7 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 80:2/q13/p3 He may have wished to spare his companion insult from arrogant leaders. — Islahi offers this additional possible motive. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 80:2/q14 Was the blind visitor discourteous?  [جَآءَهُ ٱلْأَعْمَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: What the visitor knew of the meeting and how to assess his persistence or passage past his guide.
- 80:2/q14/p1 No: he did not know the Prophet was occupied. — Jishumi says he would not have persisted had he known. Al-Qurtubi says interruption would be poor manners only with knowledge of the meeting and hope for conversion. Bursevi and the occasion reports describe his inability to see the situation. [4 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BURSEVI]
- 80:2/q14/p2 He showed some roughness or deserved to be ignored for discourtesy. — Ibn Zayd's guide report says he pressed ahead; al-Qurtubi calls that rough. Al-Tabrisi reports a view that God nevertheless rebuked the response lest he think poverty caused rejection and status won the leaders' welcome. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); TABRISI]

## 80:2/q15 How did the Prophet treat Ibn Umm Maktum afterward?  [جَآءَهُ ٱلْأَعْمَىٰ · reports]
turns on: Different reports describe immediate pursuit, later personal welcome, and a lasting change in public conduct.
- 80:2/q15/p1 He honored him, welcomed him as the man whose encounter brought divine reproof, and asked what he needed. — Numerous accounts report later solicitude. Al-Thawri adds that he spread his cloak. Ibn Abbas reports repeated offers of help. Al-Sadiq's alternative account says the Prophet welcomed him saying God would never reprove him on his account; the kindness made Ibn Umm Maktum withdraw out of bashfulness, as al-Tabataba'i explains. [22 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة); THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-ASBAB; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(الصادق); QURTUBI-FULL(الثوري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس,ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); DURR-FULL(أنس,ابن عباس,الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(الصادق); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 80:2/q15/p2 He followed the man and sent someone to find him. — Al-Qushayri reports this from a tafsir account. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 80:2/q15/p3 He never again frowned at or turned away from a poor person. — Al-Qushayri reports this; al-Hakam says he was thereafter never seen courting a rich person or turning from a poor one. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; DURR-FULL(الحكم)]

## 80:2/q16 How often did Ibn Umm Maktum govern or lead in Medina for the Prophet?  [ٱلْأَعْمَىٰ · reports]
turns on: The different reported counts, and whether the count concerns governing as deputy or leading prayers.
- 80:2/q16/p1 He deputized for the Prophet twice. — Qatada, al-Dahhak, al-Tha'labi, al-Qushayri, al-Baghawi, al-Thawri, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, al-Biqai and others report twice during campaigns; Kuran Yolu says he led prayers twice. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك,قتادة); THALABI; QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الثوري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك); BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 80:2/q16/p2 He deputized three times. — Bursevi records this alternative number. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 80:2/q16/p3 He led prayers in Medina thirteen times. — Alusi attributes this count to Ibn Abd al-Barr, alongside a general report of deputizing. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 80:2/q17 What do later reports say of Ibn Umm Maktum's role at al-Qadisiyya and death?  [ٱلْأَعْمَىٰ · reports]
turns on: Conflicting reports about whether he died in the campaign or returned to Medina.
- 80:2/q17/p1 Anas saw him at al-Qadisiyya in armor with a black banner. — Multiple transmitters repeat Anas's eyewitness report; al-Qurtubi adds that he was mounted. Bursevi says the battle saw Umar's forces defeat the Persians and take spoils. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(أنس بن مالك); THALABI(أنس بن مالك); BAGHAWI-FULL(أنس بن مالك); QURTUBI-FULL(أنس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس بن مالك); BIQAI-FULL(أنس بن مالك); BURSEVI(أنس); ALUSI-FULL]
- 80:2/q17/p2 He died as a martyr at al-Qadisiyya during the conquest of al-Madain. — Alusi reports this version in the period of Umar. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 80:2/q17/p3 He returned to Medina and died there. — Alusi records this alternative. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 80:2/q18 What do reports say about his calls to prayer and attending prayer?  [ٱلْأَعْمَىٰ · reports]
- 80:2/q18/p1 He was the Prophet's second caller to prayer and gave the dawn call after Bilal's night call. — Bursevi calls him the second muezzin and quotes the Prophet's instruction to eat and drink until his call. Ibn Umar's report adds that, being blind, he waited for others to tell him dawn had appeared. [3 notes: BURSEVI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 80:2/q18/p2 Despite blindness and difficulty reaching the mosque, he was told to attend on hearing the call. — Abu Umama reports his age, frailty, blindness and unsuitable guide, yet no exemption; Kab ibn Ujra's report says he lacked a guide and should answer God's caller. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو أمامة,كعب بن عجرة)]

## 80:2/q19 How should the frown be judged?  [* · theology]
turns on: Prophetic conduct and culpability: unseen discourtesy, a new prohibition, prophetic standards, rational moral judgment, or excess zeal in preaching.
- 80:2/q19/p1 It was not sinful but occasioned instruction toward the highest manners. — Jishumi and al-Tabrisi argue a blind man could not see the frown, so it did not hurt him; God still trains the Prophet to prioritize the believer and avoid alienation or a repeated habit. Al-Qurtubi says attending the poor believer was better, though outreach to the rich had a purpose. [4 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 80:2/q19/p2 It was permissible before this newly revealed prohibition, though it could become sinful afterward. — Al-Jubba'i gives the principle that later prohibition does not retroactively make an earlier act sinful. [1 notes: TABRISI(الجبائي); TABATABAI-]
- 80:2/q19/p3 Even a minor discourtesy became a major sin when committed by a prophet. — Asad says prophetic status explains the severity of the divine rebuke. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 80:2/q19/p4 Attributing contempt for the poor to the Prophet is incompatible with reason and his praised character. — Al-Tabataba'i rejects al-Jubba'i's timing defense: reason already judges wealth preference ugly. He cites the earlier praise in 68:4 and commands in 26:214–15 and 15:88,94. Al-Murtada and Jishumi likewise appeal to the Prophet's conduct toward Muslims. [7 notes: JISHUMI(المرتضى); TABATABAI(المرتضى); TABATABAI+]
- 80:2/q19/p5 The Prophet overextended himself from devotion to the mission, rather than disdain for disability or poverty. — Islahi says he sought the leaders' conversion so intensely that he neglected eager companions; Moses' eagerness at Tur is a comparison. The rebuke is aimed in effect at leaders wanting poor followers excluded. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 80:2/q20 What priority does the encounter teach?  [* · interpretation]
- 80:2/q20/p1 Attend to the receptive believer seeking guidance before courting influential but indifferent people. — Al-Zajjaj says not to neglect a Muslim for someone else. Jishumi says to underscore the guided believer's importance. Al-Qurtubi says a poor believer is better than a rich person and compares 6:52, 18:28 and 8:67. Al-Tabrisi says he may purify himself or heed a reminder. Ibn Kathir extends the rule across status, wealth, sex and age: warn all equally, for God guides whom He wills. Islahi says the Prophet is responsible for eager learners, not refusers. Kuran Yolu describes the visitor's fear of God, desire for Qur'anic light and purification. [17 notes: ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 80:2/q20/p2 The man gained lasting honor from the event, unlike Pharaoh, who failed to heed a reminder. — Al-Biqai draws this comparison to stress the value of receptivity. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 80:2/q21 Why does the opening describe the actor in the third person?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether indirection primarily honors and softens, sharpens accountability, or builds suspense.
- 80:2/q21/p1 The indirect form honors or gently prepares the Prophet before direct address. — Jishumi says direct ‘you frowned’ would sound harsher; al-Qurtubi sees reassurance in the turn to direct address. Al-Biqai compares Sad ibn Muadh's indirectness when judging Banu Qurayza. Alusi says the deed seems unlike the Prophet. Ibn Ashur says reproach arrives gradually, comparing 9:43 and reports that pardon was announced before the lapse. Al-Tabataba'i reports a view that later direct address restores intimacy. [9 notes: QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(عياض ناقلا عن عون بن عبد الله والسمرقندي); TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 80:2/q21/p2 The third-person form and later direct address intensify the rebuke. — Al-Tabataba'i says the shift increases accountability; Asad says third-person forms sharpen the reproof. Elmali sees a move from outward appearance to inward meaning. [3 notes: ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 80:2/q21/p3 Unnamed pronouns create suspense until later references identify the people and the incident. — Ibn Ashur points to the opening two verbs and later references to the blind and rich men, including 80:6. Islahi says the unidentified opening makes readers wonder, while later ‘you know’ identifies the Prophet; avoiding his name avoids suggesting simple rudeness was the cause. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 80:2/q22 What does this self-reproving revelation show?  [* · interpretation]
- 80:2/q22/p1 The Prophet faithfully conveyed even revelation that reproved him. — Ibn Zayd's saying asserts he would have concealed these verses if he concealed any revelation; Ibn Atiyya adds verses concerning Zayd and Zaynab. Asad treats the public conveyance as evidence of revelation's objectivity and connects it with 53:3. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI(ابن زيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(كثير من العلماء، ابن زيد، عائشة); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); ASAD-NOTES]

## 80:2/q23 How does this event fit the sūra and its neighboring themes?  [* · links]
- 80:2/q23/p1 The sūra corrects neglect of receptive people, then argues from creation, food and resurrection to warning and recompense. — Al-Biqai links the warning of those who fear in 79:45 with the sūra's proofs of divine power from human creation and food. Al-Tabataba'i traces the rebuke, human dependence despite ingratitude, resurrection and recompense. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 80:2/q23/p2 ʿAbasa pairs with al-Naziʿat in theme, style and reasoning, while rearranging the topics. — Islahi compares the pair and cites 79:45. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 80:2/q24 Where and under what names is this sūra known?  [* · sciences]
- 80:2/q24/p1 It is Meccan, described also as early Meccan. — Al-Tabataba'i calls its Meccan provenance unquestionable; Study Quran calls it early Meccan. [2 notes: TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 80:2/q24/p2 It is also called al-Sakhkhah and al-Safarah. — Study Quran derives those titles from verses 33 and 15 respectively. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
