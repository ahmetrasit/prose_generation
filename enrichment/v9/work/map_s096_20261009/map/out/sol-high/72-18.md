# 72:18: 18 questions

## 72:18/q01 How is the opening hamza of wa-anna read?  [وَأَنَّ · readings]
turns on: Whether the clause is read as a dependent fatḥa clause or an independent kasra clause.
- 72:18/q01/p1 The canonical readers open the hamza: wa-anna. — Ibn Mujāhid and al-Fārisī detail the differing fatḥ and kasr patterns in other clauses of the sūra; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports consensus here, as do al-Qurṭubī, Ibn al-Jazarī, Elmalılı, and Ibn ʿĀshūr. Fatḥ makes this clause part of revelation; al-Farrāʾ notes one pattern that keeps this phrase accusative even while kasr is used elsewhere. Al-Naḥḥās gives the deleted causal lām or coordination as explanations. After quoted speech or a conditional fāʾ, kasr is used, according to Ibn Khālawayh, Abū Ḥātim, al-Qurṭubī, and Sībawayh as reported by al-Ṭabrisī. [23 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن عامر وحمزة والكسائى وحفص عن عاصم,ابن كثير وأبو عمرو,عاصم فى رواية أبى بكر ونافع، والمفضل عن عاصم); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARRA; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حاتم,ابن عامر، حمزة، الكسائي، حفص,ابن كثير، أبو عمرو,عاصم,نافع، عاصم); QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(السبعة); NASHR; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(القراء العشرة); TABRISI(سيبويه); NAHHAS]
- 72:18/q01/p2 Ibn Hurmuz and Ṭalḥa read wa-inna with kasra. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn identify these two readers; al-Ālūsī treats it as a fresh sentence, while al-Samīn also allows a causal construal. Sībawayh says it is good Arabic, as reported by al-Fārisī and al-Ṭabrisī; his analogies include Q 23:52 and a line of al-Farazdaq. [7 notes: SIBAWAYH; FARISI-HUJJA(سيبويه); TABRISI(سيبويه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(ابن هرمز وطلحة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن هرمز وطلحة)]

## 72:18/q02 How does wa-anna al-masājida connect syntactically to its context?  [وَأَنَّ ٱلْمَسَٰجِدَ فَلَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether an omitted causal preposition governs the clause, revelation governs it, or kasra starts a new sentence.
- 72:18/q02/p1 It is part of what was revealed to the Prophet, coordinated with the earlier revelation clause. — Exegetes in Sībawayh's report compare 72:19; al-Fārisī, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥātim, and Ibn ʿĀshūr connect it to 72:1. Al-Naḥḥās also permits a nominative coordinated analysis. Al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī treat it as another revealed item. Al-Nasafī also sees the lām as linking to the following command; Elmalılı connects the passage to 72:16. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the ownership statement explains the command. [19 notes: SIBAWAYH(المفسرون); FARISI-HUJJA(سيبويه); WAHIDI-BASIT(سيبويه); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حاتم,سيبويه); TABRISI(سيبويه); RAZI-FULL; NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(السبعة)]
- 72:18/q02/p2 An unspoken causal lām gives 'because the mosques belong to God, invoke no one with Him.' — Al-Khalīl's analysis is preferred by Sībawayh, who cites fronting and postponement with lām, analogous preposition deletion, Q 23:52 and 106:1, and labbayka inna l-ḥamda. Al-Fārisī adds 21:92 and 106:1; al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, and al-Ālūsī draw the same parallels. Al-Zajjāj allows accusative after omitted lām or genitive by Arab usage, citing wa-baladin laysa bihi anīs; al-Naḥḥās gives the accusative. Al-Bayḍāwī objects that the implied lām removes the explanatory work of the fāʾ. [25 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; FARISI-HUJJA(الخليل); WAHIDI-BASIT(الخليل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الخليل بن أحمد); TABRISI(الخليل); RAZI-FULL(الخليل); QURTUBI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الخليل); SAMIN-DURR(السبعة); ALUSI-FULL(الخليل بن أحمد); SIBAWAYH(الخليل)+; BAYDAWI-FULL-]
- 72:18/q02/p3 With kasra, it can begin a fresh independent sentence. — Sībawayh regards this as sound Arabic, illustrating independent inna with Q 23:52 and al-Farazdaq's verse; al-Fārisī and al-Ṭabrisī report his judgment. Ibn Hurmuz and Ṭalḥa read accordingly. [6 notes: SIBAWAYH; FARISI-HUJJA(سيبويه); TABRISI(سيبويه); SAMIN-DURR(ابن هرمز وطلحة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن هرمز وطلحة)]

## 72:18/q03 What does al-masājid denote?  [ٱلْمَسَٰجِدَ · referent]
turns on: The scope of masājid and whether its plural denotes buildings, all earth, bodily organs, acts of prostration, or a particular sanctuary.
- 72:18/q03/p1 Purpose-built houses of prayer and remembrance, including houses of worship across religious communities. — Muqātil explicitly includes churches and synagogues alongside Muslim mosques. Ibn ʿAbbās, most commentators cited by al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bursawī, and Elmalılı support worship houses; al-Qurṭubī and Elmalılı prefer the general explanation. Al-Ṭabrisī and Study Quran also give all communities. Elmalılı distinguishes lexical coverage of all faiths from conventional Muslim usage and notes that Muslim-specific mosques had not yet been built. The people of the Book's associationism is cited by Muqātil, Qatāda, Mujāhid, and others. Al-Māwardī calls any prayer place a masjid; al-Farrāʾ, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, and others give kasra on the singular's jīm for places, while al-Farrāʾ also allows fatḥa; al-Wāḥidī lists exceptional kasra place nouns. Study Quran notes the English 'mosque' derives from masjid. [42 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(عكرمة); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(أكثر المفسرين,ابن عباس عن عكرمة,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; FARISI-HUJJA; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(الأكثرون,الواحدي); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الخليل); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Bg); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة); ELMALILI+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 72:18/q03/p2 Every place of prostration on earth, whether designated as a mosque or not. — Al-Ḥasan cites the Prophetic saying that the whole earth was made a mosque for this community; Ibn ʿAṭiyya stresses undesignated places and al-Wāḥidī that earth is its Creator's. Al-Qurṭubī calls the earth-as-mosque report sound. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that permission to worship anywhere does not make every place ordinarily a mosque. [23 notes: THALABI(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن,النبي); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); BIQAI-FULL(سعي بن جبير وطلق بن حبيب); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن,النبي صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم); ELMALILI(حسن البصري); TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Prophet); TABRISI(الحسن); TABATABAI-]
- 72:18/q03/p3 The seven bodily parts used in prostration. — Reports vary in listing face or forehead and nose, hands or palms, knees, feet or toes; their shared seven-part account is retained. They are God's creatures and should bow to no other, say Ibn ʿAṭāʾ, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Ibn al-Anbārī, and others. Ibn al-Anbārī prefers it. Some sources give the singular masjad with fatḥa on jīm, as a bodily prostration point or instrument. The Imam al-Jawād gives this answer when questioned by al-Muʿtaṣim. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says their belonging to God is by religious designation; duʿāʾ there is prostration or prayer. [45 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(آخر); ZAJJAJ; SULAMI(ابن عطاء); THALABI(ابن عطاء,سعيد بن جبير وطلق بن حبيب); MAWARDI-FULL(الربيع); WAHIDI-BASIT(سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(قيل); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عطاء); TABRISI(أبو جعفر بن علي بن موسى الرضا,سعيد بن جبير والزجاج والفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير، ابن الأنباري، الفراء); RAZI-FULL(ابن الأنباري,سعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب وطلق بن حبيب,عطاء); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BIQAI-FULL(البغوي,سعي بن جبير وطلق بن حبيب); BURSEVI(ابن عطاء); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطاء وابن جبير والزجاج والفراء,المعتصم); ELMALILI(سعيد بن جبير); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر,الإمام الجواد عليه السلام,سعيد بن جبير والفراء والزجاج); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Bg); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري)+]
- 72:18/q03/p4 Prostration or the prayers themselves, as a verbal noun. — Ibn Qutayba compares masjid to maḍrab beside ḍarb, and others give the plural as kinds of prostration or prayer. Al-Ḥasan, Ibn Shajara, and later reports say all prayer belongs to God. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī dismisses this interpretation as unsupported. [18 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; THALABI(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن,قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî); TABRISI(الحسن); TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 72:18/q03/p5 The Sacred Mosque, Kaʿba, or Mecca, the qibla of other mosques. — Some explain the plural by its sides, its many mosques, or the many mosques that face it. Ibn ʿAbbās through ʿAṭāʾ names Mecca; Islahi sees a warning to Quraysh and cites 9:17; Kur'an Yolu reports Ibn ʿĀshūr's warning against idols in the Kaʿba. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that a plural does not fit a single place and rejects a sanctuary reading as unsupported. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL(عطاء عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI(ابن عباس); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr); TABATABAI(قيل); TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 72:18/q03/p6 The Sacred Mosque together with Jerusalem. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this two-place identification among alternatives, but argues that a plural does not fit two places. [1 notes: TABATABAI(قيل)]

## 72:18/q04 What does saying the mosques are God's signify?  [لِلَّهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the genitive emphasizes exclusive devotion, honor, or both.
- 72:18/q04/p1 They belong to God and are dedicated to His worship, so no partner may be invoked there. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī and others infer exclusive invocation. Al-Biqāʿī extends the point to earth, sky, bodily parts, and senses God created. Al-Ālūsī says association is especially abhorrent in mosques, though unity is due everywhere. The Bursawī ranks the sanctity of the Sacred Mosque, Prophet's Mosque, Jerusalem, congregational, neighborhood, street, and home mosques. [13 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 72:18/q04/p2 Attribution to God honors and distinguishes mosques; human and place names can still identify them. — Al-Qurṭubī says names may indicate location or endowment, citing a sound horse-racing report involving named sites; al-Bursawī adds builder or location. Al-Qurṭubī says the Sacred House is singled out. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي); BURSEVI]

## 72:18/q05 What does the ban on invoking anyone with God require?  [فَلَا تَدْعُوا۟ مَعَ ٱللَّهِ أَحَدًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether duʿāʾ names worship generally, bodily prostration specifically, or access to invocation.
- 72:18/q05/p1 Worship, pray to, and invoke God alone, without partners. — Al-Zajjāj specifies prayer; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 40:60 for duʿāʾ as worship. Qatāda, Mujāhid, Ibn Jurayj and others invoke Jewish and Christian worship in their houses as a warning; al-Māwardī also reports the Meccan Hums talbiya assigning God a partner, and al-Qurṭubī rebukes polytheists in the Sacred Mosque. Al-Bursawī says worshipping another exclusively is worse still. Islahi says alleged deities cannot rescue Quraysh. Al-Naḥḥās says the plural jussive prohibition drops its nūn. [32 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); FARRA; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(أبو جعفر محمد بن علي); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(الأكثرون); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; NAHHAS; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 72:18/q05/p2 The prostrating limbs must not be lowered in prostration to anyone besides their Creator. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ and others connect the ban to the bodily reading of masājid; Ibn al-Anbārī reasons that using God-created limbs for another denies His favor. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads duʿāʾ as prostration, or prayer containing it. [11 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); THALABI(ابن عطاء); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير، ابن الأنباري، الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب وطلق بن حبيب,عطاء); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BURSEVI(ابن عطاء); ELMALILI(سعيد بن جبير); TABATABAI]
- 72:18/q05/p3 No one has a right to bar God's servants from remembering or invoking Him in His house. — Sahl's reading is preserved by al-Tustarī and al-Sulamī: what belongs to God cannot be restrained by another. [2 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل)]

## 72:18/q06 What work does the fāʾ before the prohibition do?  [فَلَا · grammar]
- 72:18/q06/p1 It marks the consequence of God's ownership: therefore invoke no one else. — Al-Bursawī calls it causal. Al-Ālūsī reports that with an implied causal lām some call the fāʾ formally extra, while others hear an implied condition or say the fāʾ reinforces or signals the lām. Al-Bayḍāwī argues that a supplied lām would erase the fāʾ's explanatory value. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 72:18/q07 What event or audience prompted the verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports name differing immediate settings: distant jinn, separation from people, Quraysh's control of the Kaʿba, or a pagan talbiya.
- 72:18/q07/p1 Distant jinn asked how they could reach the Prophet's mosque and pray with him; the verse assured them worship is accepted where they are. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr's report recurs through al-Ṭabarī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Suyūṭī, and al-Ālūsī. Al-Qurṭubī says the mosque is for remembrance and obedience; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī state the reassurance about distance. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); JISHUMI(سعيد بن جبير); BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); TABRISI(سعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جبير); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); SUYUTI-LUBAB(سعيد بن جبير); ALUSI-FULL]
- 72:18/q07/p2 The jinn sought to join prayer in the Prophet's mosque; they were told to pray without mixing with people. — Al-Aʿmash's version is reported by Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Māwardī, Ibn Kathīr and al-Suyūṭī. Ibn ʿAbbās is cited by al-Suyūṭī for a request for permission to attend. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الأعمش); MAWARDI-FULL(الأعمش); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الأعمش); DURR-FULL(الأعمش); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس)]
- 72:18/q07/p3 After Quraysh took control of the Kaʿba, the verse assured the Prophet he could worship God anywhere. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān report this occasion and the answer that every place belongs to God. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 72:18/q07/p4 The Meccan Hums' talbiya assigned God a partner, so the verse forbade association. — Al-Māwardī transmits the report from Abū Jaʿfar Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبو جعفر محمد بن علي)]
- 72:18/q07/p5 It was revealed to Muhammad after the account of the jinn. — Al-Farrāʾ reports this from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: FARRA(ابن عباس)]

## 72:18/q08 Which mosques existed when the verse was revealed?  [ٱلْمَسَٰجِدَ · reports]
- 72:18/q08/p1 The Sacred Mosque and the Jerusalem mosque were then the only two. — Ibn ʿAbbās's report names Jerusalem as Bayt al-Maqdis or Aelia/Ilyāʾ across Ibn Abī Ḥātim, Ibn Kathīr, al-Suyūṭī and al-Ālūsī. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 72:18/q09 What should a person say or do when entering and leaving a mosque?  [ٱلْمَسَٰجِدَ فَلَا تَدْعُوا۟ · law]
turns on: The reports give distinct entry remembrance and foot-first supplications.
- 72:18/q09/p1 On entering, declare God's oneness and greet the Messenger. — Al-Thaʿlabī says Muslims adopted this after revelation; al-Ḥasan calls a declaration of sole divinity and invocation Sunnah, since the prohibition implies God's remembrance. [4 notes: THALABI(الحسن); TABRISI(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 72:18/q09/p2 Enter right-foot first asking deliverance from the Fire; leave left-foot first asking abundant good and ease. — Al-Māwardī reports this through al-Ḍaḥḥāk from Ibn ʿAbbās; al-Qurṭubī repeats it and states no grading. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي)]

## 72:18/q10 Which activities belong in mosques?  [ٱلْمَسَٰجِدَ · law]
turns on: Whether a use serves worship or violates a mosque's dignity, especially judicial disputes.
- 72:18/q10/p1 Mosques are reserved for worship, supplication, learning, and other acts done for God; ordinary worldly use is improper. — Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Qurṭubī mention trade, meetings, passageways and mockery; Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds quarrels, oaths and litigation abuse, explaining why he stopped hearing cases in Almeria's congregational mosque. Al-Jishumī says worldly work is disliked. A Prophetic report rejects announcing lost property, because mosques were not built for that. [7 notes: THALABI(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL(النبي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 72:18/q10/p2 Several practical uses are permitted. — Al-Qurṭubī permits distributing funds and charity, detention, restraining captives, sleeping, housing the sick, access for neighbors, and truthful poetry. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 72:18/q10/p3 Abū Ḥanīfa permits judicial work in mosques as religious business. — Al-Jishumī records the juristic disagreement. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 72:18/q10/p4 Al-Shāfiʿī dislikes judicial work in mosques. — Al-Jishumī records this position; Ibn ʿAṭiyya's Almeria experience illustrates the disturbance litigation can cause. [2 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 72:18/q11 May mosques and comparable public works be endowed?  [ٱلْمَسَٰجِدَ · law]
- 72:18/q11/p1 The community agrees that mosques, bridges and cemeteries can be endowed; other property is disputed. — Al-Qurṭubī states the consensus and identifies the remaining juristic dispute. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 72:18/q12 What do the reports specify about the limbs used in prostration?  [ٱلْمَسَٰجِدَ · reports]
turns on: Whether the nose is counted with the forehead among the seven obligatory points or named as an additional recommended point.
- 72:18/q12/p1 The Prophet was commanded to prostrate on seven bodily parts. — Ibn ʿAbbās lists forehead, hands, knees and toes, sometimes including the nose by gesture; al-ʿAbbās says seven joints prostrate with the servant. Elmalılı and al-Kashshāf repeat the forehead or face, nose, hands, knees and feet variants. The report also forbids gathering hair or clothing during prostration. [9 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس,العباس بن عبد المطلب); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,العباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ELMALILI]
- 72:18/q12/p2 Eight bones may touch the ground, but seven are obligatory; placing the nose is Sunnah. — Al-Ṣādiq specifies forehead, palms, knees and big toes as the obligatory seven under the verse. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله)]

## 72:18/q13 What does this verse imply for the thief's hand?  [ٱلْمَسَٰجِدَ لِلَّهِ · law]
- 72:18/q13/p1 Cut at the finger joints and leave the palm, because the palm is one of God's prostration points. — Abū Jaʿfar gave this answer when al-Muʿtaṣim asked where the hand should be cut. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر)]

## 72:18/q14 What travel and prayer merits are reported for particular mosques?  [ٱلْمَسَٰجِدَ · reports]
turns on: The reports address separate travel and prayer-reward measures.
- 72:18/q14/p1 Travel for worship is specified for three mosques. — Al-Qurṭubī cites the Prophetic report and says hadith authorities transmitted it. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي)]
- 72:18/q14/p2 Prayer in the Prophet's mosque exceeds a thousand prayers elsewhere, except in the Sacred Mosque. — Al-Qurṭubī transmits the Prophetic report. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي)]
- 72:18/q14/p3 Prayer in the Sacred Mosque exceeds a hundred prayers in the Prophet's mosque. — Ibn al-ʿArabī reports a sound route; al-Qurṭubī affirms reliable transmitters and says he discussed it in his treatment of Ibrāhīm. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)]

## 72:18/q15 Is the earth's use as mosque and purifier distinctive to this community?  [ٱلْمَسَٰجِدَ · interpretation]
turns on: How to understand the earlier prophets' ability to pray while traveling, and whether the privilege is place, purification, or community scope.
- 72:18/q15/p1 Prayer across the whole earth is a distinctive privilege of Muhammad's community. — Al-Ālūsī reports the common claim that prior communities were restricted to synagogues and churches, citing a sound hadith about the earth as mosque and purifier. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(النبي صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم)]
- 72:18/q15/p2 The distinct privilege is the combined gift of the earth as mosque and purifier, especially dry ablution. — Al-Ālūsī reports this answer to the objection that traveling prophets must have prayed outside worship buildings. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 72:18/q15/p3 The comparison concerns earlier communities, not their prophets. — Al-Ālūsī offers this answer; he says al-Khiḍr, if alive, belongs to Muhammad's community, citing the statement that Moses would have to follow Muhammad if alive. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 72:18/q16 How does the verse connect to other verses and the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Which nearby narrative or other Qur'anic passage supplies the explanatory link.
- 72:18/q16/p1 It follows the jinn's listening and states revealed guidance about God's sole right to worship. — Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kur'an Yolu link it to 72:1; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the jinn story leads into the Prophet's mission, unity, resurrection and protection from error. Elmalılı says the clauses explain rushd, the right path, and links this verse with 72:16. [6 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 72:18/q16/p2 Other verses reinforce the consecration and proper use of God's houses. — Al-Kashshāf cites 2:114 against blocking God's name; al-Qurṭubī cites 22:26 on purifying His House; Elmalılı compares 2:114 and Islahi cites 9:17 for the general rule alongside the Sacred House. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 72:18/q17 What inward meaning is given to mosques and invocation?  [ٱلْمَسَٰجِدَ فَلَا تَدْعُوا۟ · inward]
turns on: The inward readers apply the mosque and the command to faculties, desires, the heart, or bodily purification.
- 72:18/q17/p1 Mosques signify the perfection of each faculty in submission to the heart, and of heart and spirit, where God's right or attribute appears. — Al-Qāshānī extends 'mosques' to the perfection of anything. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 72:18/q17/p2 Invoking another means following the ego's aims, caprice, pleasures and desires. — Al-Qāshānī calls these associations with God according to one's nature. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 72:18/q17/p3 The heart's mosques should be purified for God's Greatest Name alone. — Al-Bursawī excludes partial names from the heart's sanctuary. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 72:18/q17/p4 Bodily prostration purifies limbs used to pursue sins and desires. — Al-Bursawī says prostration atones and erases sin through the very limbs that carry out action. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 72:18/q18 What pausal vowel rules are discussed alongside this passage?  [وَأَنَّ · readings]
turns on: Different accounts of rawm and different consonant shapes at pause.
- 72:18/q18/p1 Reciters bar rawm on a non-nunated fatḥa, while grammarians permit it across all three vowels. — Ibn al-Jazarī says reciters count rawm as part of the vowel whereas grammarians regard it as hidden movement, exemplified by an yaḍriba; Sībawayh allows rawm with accusative or genitive but not ishmām, supporting the grammarians. [2 notes: NASHR(سيبويه)]
- 72:18/q18/p2 Rawm differs from ikhtilās and ikhfaʾ in the reciters' usage. — Ibn al-Jazarī equates the latter two, notes loose use of ikhfaʾ for rawm, and questions al-Dānī's equation. [1 notes: NASHR]
- 72:18/q18/p3 A feminine tāʾ changed to hāʾ at pause allows neither rawm nor ishmām; retained written tāʾ allows both. — Ibn al-Jazarī explains that the retained consonant normally bears the vowel. [1 notes: NASHR]
- 72:18/q18/p4 At pause, an open doubled final consonant keeps its shadda and takes sukūn. — Ibn al-Jazarī allows two consecutive quiescent letters at pause and rejects stopping on fatḥa. [1 notes: NASHR]
