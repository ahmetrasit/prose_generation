# 89:30: 21 questions

## 89:30/q01 How is the command to enter read?  [وَٱدْخُلِى · readings]
turns on: The transmitted plural, singular, explicit body, and expanded codex wordings, and whether singular servant denotes one body or the servants collectively.
- 89:30/q01/p1 The common reading has the plural 'My servants' followed by 'enter My Garden'. — Al-Farrāʾ contrasts the common plural with Ibn ʿAbbās's singular; al-Zajjāj prefers the majority reading and interpretation; Sālim ibn ʿAbd Allāh transmits the ordinary wording. [4 notes: FARRA(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA(سالم بن عبد الله); ZAJJAJ+]
- 89:30/q01/p2 A variant has singular 'My servant'. — Al-Farrāʾ attributes it to Ibn ʿAbbās alone, while Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ālūsī list Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿIkrima, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Yamānī, Mujāhid, Abū Jaʿfar and others. Al-Kalbī and Abū Shaykh understand the spirit entering a body; Ibn ʿAṭiyya also allows singular ʿabd to be generic, as believers are one hand. Corpus Coranicum refers ʿabd to 53:10. [8 notes: FARRA(ابن عباس); TAB(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA(ابن عباس وعكرمة وأبو شيخ والضحاك واليماني ومجاهد وأبو جعفر); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 89:30/q01/p3 Ibn Masʿūd's reading explicitly says to enter the body of God's servant. — Al-Nasafī invokes this reading to support taking the addressed soul as a spirit entering bodies. [2 notes: KASHSHAF(ابن مسعود); NASAFI-FULL]
- 89:30/q01/p4 Ubayy's codex expands the address and concludes with entry into God's servant. — Al-Kashshāf reports wording that first has the soul return to its Lord content and pleasing; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the expanded address to the assured soul. [2 notes: KASHSHAF(أبي); IBNATIYYA(أبي بن كعب)]

## 89:30/q02 Why does entry among servants take fī while entry into the Garden takes a direct object?  [وَٱدْخُلِى جَنَّتِى · grammar]
- 89:30/q02/p1 Fī marks entry into a nonliteral sphere, while a real container is entered directly. — Abū Ḥayyān contrasts figurative containment in the servants with literal Garden containment. Al-Ālūsī repeats his explanation. Al-Burhān distinguishes physical and conceptual container and contents, taking physical containment as literal and one mixed metaphor as closest to it. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN; BURHAN; ALUSI(أبو حيان); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]

## 89:30/q03 When is entry into the Garden commanded or promised?  [وَٱدْخُلِى جَنَّتِى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the address is at death, in the grave interval, at resurrection, after judgment, or at several stages; and whether Garden entry is immediate or final.
- 89:30/q03/p1 The entry commands are spoken at resurrection. — Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ḍaḥḥāk place the soul's return to its body then; al-Ṭabarī prefers this because the next words command entry among servants and Garden. Abū Ṣāliḥ distinguishes departure at death from entry commands on Resurrection. ʿIkrima and al-Ḍaḥḥāk put the address there; al-Qaffāl treats the imperative as a future report in meaning. [22 notes: TAB(ابن عباس,الضحاك); TAB-FULL(الضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(أبو صالح); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو صالح); DURR(أبو صالح); DURR-FULL(أبو صالح); ALUSI(أبو صالح,عكرمة والضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة والضحاك); ELMALILI(Ebû Sâlih); RAZI(القفال); TAB-FULL+; TAB+]
- 89:30/q03/p2 The address comes after resurrection and completed reckoning. — Elmalılı cites the context, including 89:24, and finds this the most apparent timing, while allowing other reports to fit. Al-Ālūsī reports an after-reckoning reading of return to the promised Garden and righteous company; the claimed prior residence is explained by potential presence in Adam's loins. [6 notes: ELMALILI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI+]
- 89:30/q03/p3 The soul is addressed at death and enters the company of the near servants then, while the lasting Garden follows at resurrection. — Ibn Zayd and a group place the address at death; al-Ālūsī distinguishes immediate spiritual company from deferred lasting bodily Paradise and says certainty permits future fulfillment to be expressed as immediate. Al-Ḥasan describes God taking a believer's soul, mutual satisfaction, and admitting it among the righteous into Paradise. [12 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); DURR(الحسن); DURR-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI(ابن زيد وجماعة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد)]
- 89:30/q03/p4 Both entries can occur immediately after death, with provisional enjoyment of Paradise before the Hour. — Al-Ālūsī reports joining righteous souls or their bodies and temporary Garden enjoyment; he cites a hadith placing believers' souls in birds' crops in Paradise and an athar granting a believer half the promised Garden at death. [5 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 89:30/q03/p5 The believer is given the good news of Paradise at death, resurrection, and gathering. — Usāma ibn Zayd and Zayd ibn Aslam transmit three stages; al-Māwardī's version names death, resurrection and Paradise itself. Ibn Kathīr says angels announce it at death and rising from the grave. Elmalılı allows each death to count as a person's resurrection or the commands to be treated together. [10 notes: TAB(أبو أسامة بن زيد عن أبيه); IBNABIHATIM(زيد بن أسلم); MAWARDI(أسامة بن زيد); DURR-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); ALUSI(زيد بن أسلم); ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); ELMALILI(Zeyd b. Eslem); IBNKATHIR]
- 89:30/q03/p6 The address comes after death but before resurrection, when the soul is shown its future Garden home and returned for grave questioning. — Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb al-Quraẓī says the believer sees the Garden home, returns to the body, and awaits Munkar and Nakīr; al-Ālūsī gives no grading of the report. [4 notes: ALUSI(محمد بن كعب القرظي); ALUSI-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي)]
- 89:30/q03/p7 The commands can address the soul continually in earthly life. — An inward application calls for turning to God in every matter, joining sincere servants by good works, and potential Garden entry. Al-Ālūsī explicitly says this does not count as a sound interpretation. [0 notes: ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 89:30/q04 What does God's Garden here designate?  [جَنَّتِى · referent]
turns on: Whether the possessive names the ordinary promised abode, mercy, a highest particular garden, or a further divine life.
- 89:30/q04/p1 Paradise, the lasting abode and prepared reward of the righteous. — The majority in al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī identify the eternal dwelling. Al-Ṭabrisī says it is promised and prepared with delights; al-Bayḍāwī calls it the abode of reward; al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī stress bodily happiness. Al-Kāshshāf, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and Elmalılı say entry is together with God's servants. The Study Quran says most take the Garden as Paradise generally. [22 notes: MAWARDI(الجمهور); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور); KASHSHAF; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN; ALUSI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 89:30/q04/p2 God's Garden means His mercy or protective care. — Muqātil says Garden entry is through the mercy entered among God's servants; Muḥammad ibn Muzāḥim and al-Ḍaḥḥāk gloss it as mercy. Sayyid Quṭb adds protection and secure closeness rooted in trust in God's decree. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB(محمد بن مزاحم); TAB-FULL(محمد بن مزاحم); MAWARDI(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); QUTB-ZILAL]
- 89:30/q04/p3 It is the Garden of Eden, highest of the gardens. — Al-Biqāʿī identifies this particular Garden with the highest garden. [2 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 89:30/q04/p4 It is the Garden of God's Essence or Presence. — Al-Qāshānī calls it the garden particular to God, of the Essence; the Study Quran reports a highest Garden of the Divine Essence in God's Presence among progressive hereafter levels. [2 notes: QASHANI; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 89:30/q04/p5 Two paradises are involved: the Garden itself and life and permanence through divine life and permanence. — Al-Tustarī distinguishes the Garden from this further divine mode of life and permanence. [1 notes: TUSTARI]

## 89:30/q05 What reward is expressed by the two entries, and why is the Garden mentioned second?  [وَٱدْخُلِى جَنَّتِى · interpretation]
turns on: How the company of servants relates to Paradise: a distinct superior spiritual reward followed by bodily reward, or the linked fulfillment of the faithful soul's life.
- 89:30/q05/p1 Joining the servants is the superior spiritual happiness; Garden entry completes it with bodily happiness. — Al-Rāzī contrasts immediate spiritual felicity with bodily Paradise after the great Resurrection, accounting for fa versus wa; al-Ālūsī says superior spiritual companionship is ordered first. Al-Thaʿlabī reports an inverted sequence explaining entry into Paradise with God's servants. [8 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; THALABI(مقاتل والقرظي وأبو عبيدة)]
- 89:30/q05/p2 Entry among the servants and into Paradise marks the contented soul's successful final outcome. — Elmalılı describes the soul's life among the righteous as ten nights before Eid al-Adha and Paradise as the dawn of the greatest feast; Islahi says the soul passed the test of fortune and hardship, fulfilling others' rights, gratitude and patience. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says abandoning independence and submitting being, acts and choices establish complete servitude. Kuranyolu cites 9:72 for divine pleasure as a greater final gift; Elmalılı prays for this company, Paradise and vision of God. [8 notes: ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 89:30/q06 Why is the Garden called 'My Garden'?  [جَنَّتِى · rhetoric]
- 89:30/q06/p1 The first-person possessive honors the Garden and its recipient. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the honor to the seat of truth with the Mighty King in 54:55; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this special possessive occurs nowhere else in the Qurʾān. Kuranyolu similarly treats 'My servants' as supreme honor. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 89:30/q07 What explains the shift to God's first-person speech?  [جَنَّتِى · rhetoric]
- 89:30/q07/p1 'My servants' and 'My Garden' shift from the preceding third-person mention of 'your Lord', and the Garden's possessive makes the shift fitting. — Ibn ʿĀshūr describes this change of person in both possessives and finds the attribution of Paradise to God especially apt after the third-person reference. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 89:30/q08 Why is 'enter' repeated before the Garden?  [وَٱدْخُلِى · rhetoric]
- 89:30/q08/p1 Repeating the command emphasizes Garden entry and heightens the recipients' joy. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says a single verb could have governed both objects; spelling out the two entries details the previous general good news and renews joy. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 89:30/q09 How should the divine commands be understood as speech?  [وَٱدْخُلِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether an omitted future speech formula governs the imperatives or the sūra closes with a direct present address.
- 89:30/q09/p1 An implied 'God will say' makes the address a future declaration and creative command. — Elmalılı attributes this to the majority and connects it to the preceding context; al-Qaffāl likewise says the imperative has report meaning. [2 notes: ELMALILI; RAZI(القفال)]
- 89:30/q09/p2 They can be a direct legislative address heard by tranquil souls now. — Elmalılı finds the separated closing address plausible, with direct hearing of the promise; Kuranyolu says the unintroduced address is a warm honor bringing afterlife happiness into present life. [2 notes: ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 89:30/q10 How does entry among God's servants relate to this Garden entry?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the servants are righteous companions, obedience, bodies receiving spirits, or proximate spirits; the mirror explanation is expressly disputed.
- 89:30/q10/p1 The soul joins the righteous or chosen servants and enters Paradise with them. — Qatāda repeatedly glosses the servants as the righteous; Abū ʿUbayda says 'with' or 'among' the chosen and cites 27:19. Ibn ʿAṭiyya places them at the gathering when Hell-bound people separate. Al-Kāshshāf, al-Qurṭubī and others take the two commands as entry with God's servants. [17 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA(آخرون); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); ABUHAYYAN; DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI; ELMALILI; THALABI(مقاتل والقرظي وأبو عبيدة)]
- 89:30/q10/p2 Entering the servants means entering God's obedience, and Garden entry means entering His mercy. — Muqātil and Muḥammad ibn Muzāḥim interpret the two commands through obedience and mercy, making the second depend on the first. [5 notes: MUQATIL; TAB(محمد بن مزاحم); TAB-FULL(محمد بن مزاحم)]
- 89:30/q10/p3 The spirit enters bodies, then the prepared reward. — Al-Kalbī reads the singular servant as the spirit's return to a body; Abū Shaykh says likewise. Ibn Masʿūd's reading explicitly supplies body. Al-Ālūsī cites Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Jubayr construing the plural generically as bodies; another analysis makes 'Lord' the soul's bodily owner. [13 notes: TAB(ابن عباس,الكلبي); KASHSHAF(ابن مسعود,قيل); IBNATIYYA(ابن عباس وعكرمة وأبو شيخ والضحاك واليماني ومجاهد وأبو جعفر); BAYDAWI; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن جبير); ELMALILI]
- 89:30/q10/p4 The soul joins the proximate spirits, whose lights reflect upon one another. — Al-Bayḍāwī and Elmalılı report mutual radiance like facing mirrors. Al-Ālūsī rejects the polished-mirror image, its increasing knowledge, as rhetorical speculation; this does not reject proximity itself. [2 notes: BAYDAWI; ELMALILI; ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 89:30/q11 What does the preceding 'return to your Lord' mean in relation to these entries?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The time of the address and whether 'Lord' denotes God, His promise or command, or the body's owner; 'return' also raises the issue of prior residence.
- 89:30/q11/p1 It is departure from worldly life at death, before the resurrection entry commands. — Abū Ṣāliḥ distinguishes the death-time return from the commands at resurrection; al-Ḥasan describes the believer's soul resting in God at death. [10 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو صالح); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو صالح); DURR(أبو صالح); DURR-FULL(أبو صالح); ALUSI(أبو صالح); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Ebû Sâlih)]
- 89:30/q11/p2 It is return to the promised destination of Paradise and righteous company after judgment. — Al-Ālūsī reports the reply to the objection that 'return' presupposes previous residence: souls were potentially in Adam's loins in the Garden. He compares reports from ʿAlī and Ibn ʿAbbās reading maʿād in 28:85 as Paradise, while noting dependence on the disputed claim that Adam's Garden is the final Garden. [6 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 89:30/q11/p3 It is return to God's gracious command, treatment or honored station. — Al-Ālūsī explains the return as being treated without distress and placed in a station of honor. [3 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 89:30/q11/p4 It is return to God's general favor from the intermediate realm or from the period around resurrection and judgment. — Al-Ālūsī gives alternative starting points in barzakh, after resurrection or after judgment. [3 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 89:30/q11/p5 The person returns to God's station at resurrection. — ʿIkrima and al-Ḍaḥḥāk take nafs as the person, rabb as God and supply an omitted station. [3 notes: ALUSI(عكرمة والضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة والضحاك)]
- 89:30/q11/p6 The spirit returns to its body's owner before joining the servants and reward. — This construes rabb as the body formerly owned by the soul and makes the Garden the later abode of reward. [3 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 89:30/q11/p7 At death the soul returns to God's sacred realm and joins His near servants. — Ibn Zayd and a group place the address at death; al-Ālūsī makes nafs the spirit and the return to the holy realm. [4 notes: ALUSI(ابن زيد وجماعة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد)]
- 89:30/q11/p8 The soul returns to its body for questioning after seeing its Garden home at death. — Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb specifies the interval before resurrection and Munkar and Nakīr. [4 notes: ALUSI(محمد بن كعب القرظي); ALUSI-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي)]

## 89:30/q12 To whom does this promise apply?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether named occasions restrict the addressee; al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī argue the wording reaches all believers.
- 89:30/q12/p1 It applies generally to faithful, tranquil souls; named persons are illustrative occasions. — ʿIkrima and al-Farrāʾ give the general reading; al-Kashshāf calls it apparent. Al-Nasafī says the lesson follows general wording, and al-Ālūsī says named Companions are examples within that wording. Qatāda identifies the assured believer; Elmalılı and Islahi describe the contented soul that succeeds in the test. [14 notes: MAWARDI(عكرمة والفراء); KASHSHAF; ALUSI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+]
- 89:30/q12/p2 Abū Bakr is promised this address. — Ibn ʿAbbās reports the verse was recited while Abū Bakr sat with the Prophet and admired it; the Prophet said an angel would say it to him at death. Al-Ālūsī cites a similar al-Ḥākim al-Tirmidhī version. [2 notes: MAWARDI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(النبي)]
- 89:30/q12/p3 It was revealed about ʿUthmān after he purchased the Rūma well for public drinking. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and a report through Ibn ʿAbbās associate revelation with the endowment; al-Ālūsī names the route through Juwaybir from al-Ḍaḥḥāk and regards this as an example, not a limit. [7 notes: MAWARDI(الضحاك); ABUHAYYAN(قيل); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 89:30/q12/p4 It was revealed about Ḥamza. — Burayda transmits this identification; al-Ālūsī treats it as an illustrative case. [8 notes: MAWARDI(بريدة الأسلمي); KASHSHAF; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 89:30/q12/p5 It concerns Khubayb ibn ʿAdī. — Reports say God turned his face toward the qibla after his captors turned it toward Medina, and they could not turn it back; al-Ālūsī treats him as an example. [7 notes: KASHSHAF; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 89:30/q12/p6 Ibn ʿAbbās identifies the soul as the Prophet. — Al-Ālūsī cites a report through Ibn Mardawayh and treats this too as illustrative. [3 notes: ALUSI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 89:30/q13 What report connects this verse with Ibn ʿAbbās's burial?  [* · reports]
- 89:30/q13/p1 A strange bird entered Ibn ʿAbbās's bier at Ṭāʾif and was not seen leaving; an unseen voice recited the tranquil-soul verses at his grave. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr transmits the event in several sources; versions say coffin or bier and place the recitation at the grave or its edge. [12 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); THALABI(سعيد بن جبير); BAGHAWI(سعيد بن جبير); BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR(سعيد بن جبير); BIQAI(سعيد بن جبير); BIQAI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); DURR(سعيد بن جبير); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير)]

## 89:30/q14 What other death reports are connected with the verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: Different reports describe different persons and events rather than competing details of one event.
- 89:30/q14/p1 A captive martyr's severed head recited the verse in Roman lands, leading three apostates back to Islam. — Qubāth ibn Razīn Abū Hāshim recounts the event in Ibn Kathīr. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR(قباث بن رزين أبو هاشم)]
- 89:30/q14/p2 At death the believer rests in a spacious, illuminated grave like a sleeping bride, while the unbeliever is sent to torment. — ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr reports light from the Qurʾān or like the sun and a foul coarse covering for the unbeliever. [1 notes: THALABI(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص)]
- 89:30/q14/p3 Angels direct the solitary to Paradise homes, but they seek a good life solely with God. — Al-Tustarī reports the solitary say they withdrew for God alone and sought no other object. [1 notes: TUSTARI]

## 89:30/q15 What reward is reported for reciting Sūrat al-Fajr?  [* · sciences]
- 89:30/q15/p1 Recitation during the ten nights brings forgiveness; recitation on other days brings light on Resurrection Day. — The report is ascribed to the Prophet in al-Kashshāf and the two al-Bayḍāwī notes. [3 notes: KASHSHAF(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI(رسول الله); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 89:30/q16 What does the passage imply about the soul's existence before embodiment?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether 'return' entails a soul's existence before its body.
- 89:30/q16/p1 Some readings suggest souls existed before bodies in a realm of dominion or the unseen. — Al-Ālūsī presents this as a possible implication of the return language. [3 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 89:30/q16/p2 Most theologians and Plato's followers hold that souls are created when bodies become ready. — Al-Ālūsī records this counterview without deciding it here. [3 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 89:30/q17 What inward account of the soul's path is given?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the inward meaning is a journey from worldly attachment, a scheme of soul stations, or an attempted literal timing in earthly life that al-Ālūsī rejects.
- 89:30/q17/p1 The soul attached to worldly life must leave it and follow the path of the afterlife toward God. — Ahl al-ishāra in al-Baghawī interpret return to God as abandoning worldly attachment. [2 notes: BAGHAWI(بعض أهل الإشارة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل الإشارة)]
- 89:30/q17/p2 The tranquil, content and approved soul are three spiritual stations. — Al-Ālūsī attributes this three-rank account to Sufi teachers, referring the details to their books. [3 notes: ALUSI(السادة الصوفية); ALUSI-FULL(السادة الصوفية)]
- 89:30/q17/p3 The earthly-life commands describe continual obedience, sincere company and potential Garden entry. — Al-Ālūsī reports the reading but says it is not sound exegesis. [0 notes: ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 89:30/q18 How does this ending relate to the rest of Sūrat al-Fajr?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the close is read as the sūra's integrated promise and oath fulfillment or as a later positive expansion of its earlier threat.
- 89:30/q18/p1 It completes warning with good news to believing souls, encouraging good and deterring evil. — Ibn ʿĀshūr sees the usual joining of promise and warning; Sayyid Quṭb hears secure closeness after the earlier scenes, and Islahi ties the peaceful soul to success in the trial of wealth, deprivation and duties to others. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 89:30/q18/p2 The ending fulfills the dawn oath through resurrection and the soul's festival-like arrival in Paradise. — Al-Biqāʿī says resurrection is the apparent subject of the opening oath at 89:1, without which creation would be vain; he praises the composition's inimitability. Elmalılı likens righteous company to the ten nights and Paradise entry to the festival dawn. [3 notes: BIQAI; ELMALILI]
- 89:30/q18/p3 Verses 27–30 are later additions that create a positive afterlife ending opposite the Hell scene. — Corpus Coranicum says later additions in 23–24 and 27–30 decode the opening oath and expand a four-verse eschatological unit. Garden entry answers Hell in 89:23; three additions use direct speech, with God's positive address superseding human responses to allotment and threatened Hell. [4 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 89:30/q19 How does this ending lead into the next sūra?  [* · links]
- 89:30/q19/p1 The next sūra answers the rebuke of hoarding and neglecting the needy with freeing slaves and feeding in famine; the tranquil soul follows the path to contentment. — Al-Biqāʿī's thematic connection is noted by al-Ālūsī. Al-Ālūsī says the next sūra is mostly held Meccan, despite Medinan or mixed proposals; 'this city' weighs against those, although some reports date its opening after the conquest. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 89:30/q20 Should the two entry commands be separated by a verse boundary?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether close syntactic relation overrides the approximate end rhyme used for verse division.
- 89:30/q20/p1 The related imperatives should remain in one verse. — Neuwirth rejects the Kūfan division on syntactic and thematic unity. [0 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM(Neuwirth)-]
- 89:30/q20/p2 The two commands may be divided as in the Kūfan count because ʿibādī and jannatī form an approximate rhyme. — Corpus Coranicum counters Neuwirth by allowing nonclassical vowel and consonant variation, comparing other Qurʾānic pairs at 89:15–16, 23–24 and 27–28. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 89:30/q21 What prayer associated with the tranquil soul is cited?  [* · reports]
- 89:30/q21/p1 A prophetic supplication asks for a tranquil soul that believes in meeting God, accepts His decree and is content with His gift. — Al-Ālūsī quotes it while commenting on the soul addressed by the passage. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
