# 52:14: 19 questions

## 52:14/q01 What does the near demonstrative “this” indicate?  [هَٰذِهِ · rhetoric]
- 52:14/q01/p1 The Fire is immediately before the deniers, who have reached its edge. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the near feminine demonstrative places them at Hell's edge and prepares for the following challenge about what they see. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 52:14/q02 What is the Fire identified here?  [ٱلنَّارُ · referent]
- 52:14/q02/p1 It is the actual Hellfire and punishment they denied, now present before them. — Wāḥidī al-Wajīz calls it the very punishment denied. Biqāʿī describes the fire as a burning body that destroys what it touches and occupies its victims' attention, unlike their former play. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says its present reality fulfills what prophets foretold. The other cited sources identify it with the worldly deniers' rejected Fire. [14 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 52:14/q03 Who speaks the words “This is the Fire”?  [هَٰذِهِ ٱلنَّارُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the speakers are specifically Hell's keepers or the angels escorting the condemned; the Study Quran calls them angels without specifying their role.
- 52:14/q03/p1 Hell's keepers speak to the condemned. — Baghawī, Ṭabrisī, Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, and Bursawī identify the speakers as the keepers or guards of Hell; Ibn Kathīr calls their speech rebuke and reproach. [5 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 52:14/q03/p2 Angels address the punished; Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies them as the angels escorting the condemned. — The Study Quran attributes verses 14–16 to angels addressing the inhabitants of the Fire. Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies the escorting angels as the speakers. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 52:14/q04 How is the quoted speech introduced?  [هَٰذِهِ ٱلنَّارُ · grammar]
- 52:14/q04/p1 A verb of saying is omitted: they are told, “This is the Fire.” — Majāz reconstructs fa-yuqālu hādhihi al-nār. Zajjāj and Naḥḥās supply 'it is said to them'; Rāzī supplies an omitted qāla. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise read an unspoken introduction to quoted speech; Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes the resulting reproach and Ibn ʿĀshūr draws the speaker from context. [6 notes: MAJAZ; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 52:14/q05 Who is addressed by the announcement?  [هَٰذِهِ ٱلنَّارُ · referent]
- 52:14/q05/p1 The deniers now being punished in the Fire are addressed. — Bayḍāwī identifies the addressees as the people being punished. Muqātil places the address after the disbelievers are driven into Hell; the Study Quran calls them the inhabitants of the Fire. [3 notes: MUQATIL; BAYDAWI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 52:14/q06 What had they been denying?  [ٱلَّتِى كُنتُم بِهَا تُكَذِّبُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes variously specify the denied object as the Fire and its punishment, the revelation announcing it, or resurrection and punishment disclosed by the clause.
- 52:14/q06/p1 They denied the Fire's existence and its punishment during worldly life. — Ibn ʿAbbās, as reported by Wāḥidī, explicitly locates the denial in worldly life. Wāḥidī al-Wajīz calls the Fire the punishment they denied; Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes its existence and punishment of sinners. Biqāʿī says the denial was extreme, continual, and the root of their other denials. [12 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 52:14/q06/p2 They rejected the revelation that announced this Fire. — Bursawī says bihā is attached to tukadhdhibūn and that they denied the revelation speaking of the Fire. Ālūsī likewise explains the denial as rejection of revelation about it. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says prophets conveyed its existence and punishment through revelation, which they denied. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 52:14/q06/p3 The clause exposes their denial of resurrection and punishment. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the relative clause uncovers the error of denying resurrection and punishment, now directly seen by the deniers. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 52:14/q07 What does bihā attach to?  [بِهَا تُكَذِّبُونَ · grammar]
- 52:14/q07/p1 The prepositional phrase bihā attaches to tukadhdhibūn. — Bursawī gives this syntactic attachment while explaining the object of denial as the revelation about the Fire. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 52:14/q08 Why is bihā placed before tukadhdhibūn?  [بِهَا تُكَذِّبُونَ · rhetoric]
- 52:14/q08/p1 Its fronting highlights this particular denial of the Fire. — Biqāʿī says their denial of the Fire was extreme, repeated continually in worldly life, and the root of their other denials. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 52:14/q09 What is the force of confronting them with their former denial?  [ٱلَّتِى كُنتُم بِهَا تُكَذِّبُونَ · rhetoric]
- 52:14/q09/p1 It rebukes the deniers after the truth has become visible and exposes their earlier error. — Wāḥidī al-Basīṭ says the exposed truth reproaches their prior claims. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the announcement a rebuke and reproach, as does Ibn Kathīr. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the relative clause exposes the error of denying resurrection and punishment. Abū Ḥayyān says the subsequent command to enter the Fire reproaches them; Iṣlāḥī recalls the Fire they mocked and denied. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 52:14/q10 When and how is this announcement made?  [* · reports]
- 52:14/q10/p1 It is said as the condemned are driven into Hell after judgment. — Muqātil details the sequence: Hell's keepers shackle disbelievers' hands to their necks, bind their forelocks to their feet behind their backs, and push them face-first into Hell before the announcement. Elmalılı says they are shoved into the Fire; Iṣlāḥī says forcefully driven there after their worldly pranks. Wāḥidī places the reproach after the truth is exposed. Ibn ʿĀshūr's near demonstrative puts them at Hell's edge. [5 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 52:14/q11 How does the announcement lead into the following challenge?  [* · links]
- 52:14/q11/p1 Seeing the Fire sets up the challenge to their perception and the order to enter it. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says 'this' prepares for lā tubṣirūn. Abū Ḥayyān says that after they are told this is Hell, the next lines confront possible claims that magic changed what they see or that their own sight is defective, then order them into the Fire as reproach. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 52:14/q12 Which other verses resemble this address?  [* · links]
- 52:14/q12/p1 It is comparable to 83:17 and resembles 77:29. — The Study Quran cites both parallels without further argument. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 52:14/q13 Which reading should guide interpretation of this verse?  [* · readings]
- 52:14/q13/p1 The established mutawātir reading in the current muṣḥaf should govern interpretation. — Iṣlāḥī bases his verse and word interpretations on that reading. He argues that interpreting through other readings sacrifices the Qur'an's eloquence, purpose, and wisdom. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 52:14/q14 What method and aim does Iṣlāḥī give for interpreting this verse?  [* · interpretation]
- 52:14/q14/p1 He interprets through Arab usage, context, and Qur'anic parallels to protect attentive readers from misleading exegesis. — Iṣlāḥī says this method leaves no doubt. He describes his exegesis as guarding against Orientalist, Jewish, and extremist Shiite mischief. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 52:14/q15 How is this passage held together within the sūrah?  [* · links]
- 52:14/q15/p1 Its verses form a coherent sequence under the sūrah's central theme. — Iṣlāḥī says removing even one verse risks damaging the sequence. He describes verses 1–16 as a severe warning to obstinate Quraysh: historical and universal facts show punishment cannot be hastened or averted, before Judgment and the deniers' fate are depicted. The Study Quran says the sūrah moves from oaths, punishment, and Judgment through the deniers' fate and the reverent's reward, then answers accusations, challenges deniers, and counsels patience and praise. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 52:14/q16 How does this sūrah connect with its predecessors?  [* · links]
- 52:14/q16/p1 Its received place after Qāf and al-Dhāriyāt continues their case for Judgment and punishment. — Iṣlāḥī says the Qur'an's present arrangement was set by God and His Prophet according to religious philosophy and divine-law wisdom rather than historical order, unlike the Torah and Gospel. He says every group and successive sūrah is linked, so changing placement would leave a gap in meaning. He identifies al-Ṭūr as the group's third sūrah after Qāf and al-Dhāriyāt; those sūrahs establish resurrection and Judgment with intellectual, historical, inward, and outward proofs and present reward and punishment together (51:5–6). The Study Quran says the opening half follows Sūrah 51's promised Day and punishment (51:59–60), affirms the promise (51:5, 51:22), and warns further. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 52:14/q17 Where and roughly when was Sūrah al-Ṭūr revealed?  [* · sciences]
- 52:14/q17/p1 It is Makkan, most likely from the latter half of the Makkan period. — The Study Quran reports commentator agreement on its Makkan origin and gives the latter-half estimate. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(commentators)]

## 52:14/q18 After which sūrah was al-Ṭūr reportedly revealed?  [* · sciences]
- 52:14/q18/p1 Some commentators place its revelation immediately after Sūrah 32, al-Sajdah. — The Study Quran reports this as the view of some commentators. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]

## 52:14/q19 Why is the sūrah called al-Ṭūr?  [* · sciences]
- 52:14/q19/p1 It is named for the Mount mentioned in its opening verse. — The Study Quran says most take al-Ṭūr wherever it occurs in the Qur'an to mean Mount Sinai (52:1; 2:63, 2:93, 19:52, 20:80, 28:29, 28:46), while the full name occurs in 23:20 and 95:2. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
