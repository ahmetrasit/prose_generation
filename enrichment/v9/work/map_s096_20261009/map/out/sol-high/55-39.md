# 55:39: 13 questions

## 55:39/q01 What does “on that day” refer to and how does it connect to the preceding condition?  [فَيَوْمَئِذٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the fāʾ clause is the apodosis or the apodosis is omitted.
- 55:39/q01/p1 It answers the preceding “when” and refers to the day the sky splits. — Al-Naḥḥās calls it the answer to idhā. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn say the tanwīn replaces “when the sky split”; al-Samīn makes the fāʾ the conditional answer. Al-Nasafī identifies the day likewise. [4 notes: NAHHAS; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 55:39/q01/p2 The condition's answer is omitted, with a terrifying event understood. — Al-Samīn offers this alternative. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 55:39/q01/p3 The negative verb governs the time adverb. — Al-Samīn says lā does not block government, citing his discussion at al-Fātiḥa. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 55:39/q02 What sequence does the initial fāʾ mark?  [فَيَوْمَئِذٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Temporal succession, immediate consequence, or rhetorical order.
- 55:39/q02/p1 It marks temporal succession after the sky splits. — Al-Rāzī lists this possibility. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 55:39/q02/p2 It marks immediate consequence: punishment begins without time for questioning. — Al-Rāzī contrasts undelayed afterlife punishment with worldly delay, citing 55:31. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 55:39/q02/p3 It orders the warning: after escape through heaven is denied, offenders have no time even to answer. — Al-Rāzī gives this rhetorical sequence. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 55:39/q03 To whom does the singular pronoun in “his sin” refer?  [ذَنۢبِهِۦٓ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical antecedent and whether the intercession reading changes the pronoun's reference.
- 55:39/q03/p1 It refers to a human, following the singular form and prior grammatical rank of ins. — Al-Bayḍāwī cites grammatical gender; Bursawī and al-Ālūsī cite prior rank. Al-Kashshāf and al-Ālūsī explain an individual human. Al-Biqāʿī says the singular focuses each person's particular sin. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 55:39/q03/p2 It refers to either human or jinn, with the other parallel clause understood. — Al-Samīn supplies “nor is a jinn asked about his sin.” [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 55:39/q03/p3 It refers to an implicit sinner placed before the verb, whether human or jinn. — Al-Rāzī prefers this as the subtler grammatical solution. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+]
- 55:39/q03/p4 It can be understood as the explicit phrase “a sinner's sin.” — Al-Rāzī supplies this answer to the grammatical objection his second reading raises. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 55:39/q03/p5 Under the intercession reading it points to someone other than its grammatical subject. — Al-Rāzī compares “they killed themselves,” which need not mean each killed himself. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 55:39/q04 What questioning is denied, and how does that fit passages affirming questioning?  [لَّا يُسْـَٔلُ عَن ذَنۢبِهِۦٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Disagreement turns on the question's purpose, stage, asker and addressee, available evidence, or whether any sin remains.
- 55:39/q04/p1 No inquiry to learn deeds is needed because God knows and deeds are recorded; affirmative questions can rebuke or ask why. — Muqātil and al-Ḥasan cite preserved deeds; al-ʿAwfī's Ibn ʿAbbās, Ḥasan and Qatāda cite angelic records. Ibn ʿAbbās distinguishes whether an act happened from why it happened in light of 15:92. Al-Zajjāj, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Rāzī and others distinguish information from reproach. Al-Rāzī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefer this. Al-Ālūsī objects to a strict sin-versus-cause division because other verses affirm questions about sins themselves. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites swift reckoning (14:51); Kur’an Yolu says a full hearing shows justice and mercy. [39 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن وقتادة والعوفي عن ابن عباس,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن وقتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(قول آخر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن وقتادة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Aj); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس)+; RAZI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,الفراء)+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 55:39/q04/p2 No identification is needed because visible marks disclose the criminals. — Mujāhid and Ibn ʿAbbās invoke 55:41. Al-Farrāʾ, Qatāda and al-Zajjāj describe black faces and blue eyes; believers have ablution radiance. Al-Zajjāj cites 3:106 and 20:102. Al-Bayḍāwī places the marks at emergence from graves. Al-Rāzī says no sinner hides in a crowd. Islahi adds foreheads and limb testimony; Kur’an Yolu adds records. Al-Wāḥidī approves al-Farrāʾ's explanation. [27 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); FARRA; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن ومجاهد); TABRISI(قول آخر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن ومجاهد عن ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)+]
- 55:39/q04/p3 Questioning and its absence occur at different stations of the long Resurrection Day. — Ibn Qutayba cites 70:4; Ibn ʿAbbās identifies one station. ʿIkrima and Qatāda distinguish occasions. Al-Kashshāf cites 15:92 and 37:24; Ibn Kathīr compares 77:35–36 with 15:92–93. Al-Biqāʿī cites 75:30 and says the day is not bounded by ordinary nights. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes sealed mouths, speaking limbs and marks. Al-Rāzī reports this solution but prefers the difference in questioning's purpose. [20 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(عكرمة); BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة وعكرمة); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة); NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عكرمة وقتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN(غيره); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(غيره); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 55:39/q04/p4 The denial is at emergence and gathering; accounting and questioning follow. — Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursawī say marks show ranks as people leave graves and assemble. Al-Ālūsī reports the same sequence. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 55:39/q04/p5 Questioning occurred earlier; later mouths were sealed and limbs testified. — Ibn Qutayba adds settled cases, distributed books and marked faces. Al-Kalbī places this after accounting. Qatāda invokes 36:65. Abū Hurayra reports the hypocrite's sealed mouth. Asad cites the record (18:49) and limb testimony (24:24); Study Quran adds ears, eyes and skins (41:20), also citing 28:78, 77:35–36, 17:71, 50:17–18, 69:19–28 and 82:11–12. ʿĀʾisha reports judgment, forgiveness and seeing deeds in the grave. [16 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة عن النبي,قتادة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عائشة); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); STUDYQURAN(Aj, IK); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة)+]
- 55:39/q04/p6 No questioning occurs once sinners are sent to or settled in Hell. — Al-Qurṭubī reports a post-settlement explanation. Ibn Kathīr points to criminals driven into the Fire (55:41); Kur’an Yolu describes passage between fire and boiling water. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 55:39/q04/p7 People do not ask one another about their condition because each is preoccupied. — Ibn ʿAbbās is reported by al-Māwardī and Ibn al-Jawzī; another report compares 28:78 and 2:119. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 55:39/q04/p8 An innocent person is not asked about a sinner's wrongdoing. — Abū al-ʿĀliya is reported by al-Qurṭubī; Abū Ḥayyān also attributes it to Qatāda. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو العالية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو العالية وقتادة)]
- 55:39/q04/p9 Angels are not asked about people because they had already raised their deeds in worldly life. — Al-Māwardī reports this distinct explanation from Mujāhid. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 55:39/q04/p10 No one is asked to pardon or intercede for another; everyone petitions God alone. — Al-Rāzī argues that “ask” may take two objects and the prepositional phrase names the topic. He addresses the objection that “ask about” usually means inquiry or reproach and that recognition by marks in 55:41 appears ill suited to this reading. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]
- 55:39/q04/p11 An unrepentant believer punished in the barzakh reaches Resurrection with no sin left to ask about. — Al-Ṭabrisī attributes this to al-Riḍā. Al-Ālūsī reports it but rejects the attribution and forcing the verse into this sense. [2 notes: TABRISI(الرضا); ALUSI-FULL(الطبرسي); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 55:39/q04/p12 Bewilderment overwhelms criminals in this scene; questioning happens elsewhere. — Al-Ṭabrisī points to 37:24 for the other occasion. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 55:39/q04/p13 Questions about monotheism and messengers occur; questions about religious branches are denied. — Al-Ḥalīmī's solution appears in al-Burhān and al-Itqān with 37:24 and 7:6. [2 notes: BURHAN(الحليمي); ITQAN(الحليمي)]
- 55:39/q04/p14 Questions of reproach occur, while questions seeking an excuse or establishing proof do not. — Al-Itqān reports this distinction. [1 notes: ITQAN(قيل)]
- 55:39/q04/p15 The cited affirmations and denial appear contradictory. — Objectors in Ibn Qutayba cite 15:92–93; Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes the apparent tension that demands reconciliation. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(قالوا); IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 55:39/q05 Why is the verb of questioning passive?  [يُسْـَٔلُ · rhetoric]
- 55:39/q05/p1 It magnifies the event and makes the sinner's state publicly knowable. — Al-Biqāʿī says the passive construction suggests knowledge of the sinner's state across eras. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 55:39/q06 Who are the “humans” here?  [إِنسٌۭ · referent]
turns on: Ordinary human referents or an inward human aspect.
- 55:39/q06/p1 They are human individuals, or some of humankind. — Al-Kashshāf says ins means some humans; al-Biqāʿī emphasizes each particular sinner. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 55:39/q06/p2 Inwardly, they are the outward-oriented side of a person. — Al-Qāshānī pairs this with the inward-oriented jinn aspect. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 55:39/q07 What does “jānn” mean here?  [جَآنٌّۭ · meaning]
turns on: Literal jinn or an inward aspect.
- 55:39/q07/p1 It denotes a jinni or the jinn collectively, using an ancestor's name for descendants. — Muqātil glosses a jinni; Thaʿlab gives plural jinān. Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī invoke the ancestor; al-Nasafī compares Hāshim. Bursawī takes the term collectively. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ثعلب عن عمرو عن أبيه,مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 55:39/q07/p2 Inwardly, it is a person's inward-oriented side. — Al-Qāshānī pairs it with the outward-oriented human side. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 55:39/q08 How was “jānn” read with hamza, and why?  [جَآنٌّۭ · readings]
- 55:39/q08/p1 Al-Ḥasan and ʿAmr ibn ʿUbayd added a hamza to avoid two adjacent vowelless sounds. — Al-Kashshāf and Abū Ḥayyān report the reading; al-Samīn refers to it; al-Ālūsī says the unhamzated sequence is also permitted. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن وعمرو بن عبيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن وعمرو بن عبيد); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن، عمرو بن عبيد)]

## 55:39/q09 Why are humans named before jinn?  [إِنسٌۭ جَآنٌّۭ · rhetoric]
turns on: The human audience or the strangeness of jinn.
- 55:39/q09/p1 Humans are the main addressees; their messenger was human and familiar examples teach clearly. — Al-Biqāʿī adds that mentioning jinn shows God's power over both groups. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 55:39/q09/p2 The order may highlight how astonishing the jinn's creation is. — Al-Burhān notes repeated human-before-jinn order and cites 55:39, 55:56 and 72:5. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 55:39/q10 What does the verse teach about sinners' fate and accountability?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Which reading supplies the lesson about punishment, ransom, intercession, or accountability.
- 55:39/q10/p1 Sinners are exposed and punished without delay or escape. — Al-Rāzī links undelayed punishment to 55:31 and failed escape and defense to 55:33, 55:35. Islahi describes exposed foreheads and seizure; Kur’an Yolu describes fire and boiling water. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites swift reckoning (14:51). [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]
- 55:39/q10/p2 Lack of questioning humiliates the guilty. — Al-Rāzī compares dusty, gloomy and blackened faces (80:40–41; 3:106). [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 55:39/q10/p3 No ransom is accepted. — Al-Rāzī's second reading completes the sequence denying escape (55:33) and defense (55:35). [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 55:39/q10/p4 No intercessor or merciful protector is available. — Al-Rāzī derives this from the intercession reading. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 55:39/q10/p5 The verse affirms jinn's creation and responsibility and proceeds to resurrection and requital. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives this point; Elmalılı says denied investigation does not cancel reckoning or responsibility. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 55:39/q11 How does the verse connect with surrounding passages and the refrain?  [* · links]
turns on: How the denial works in the judgment sequence and in the refrain.
- 55:39/q11/p1 The warning moves from the split sky and failed escape to recognition and punishment. — Al-Zajjāj links 37:24 with marks in 55:41, 3:106 and 20:102; al-Qurṭubī compares 28:78. Al-Rāzī invokes 55:33 and 55:35; Ibn Kathīr and Kur’an Yolu continue to Hell. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees a shift from first creation to second creation, reckoning and recompense. His preceding challenge concerns escape from God's dominion: aqṭār are directions, sulṭān may mean power, proof or kingship, and jinn come first there for speed. He rejects a scientific-penetration reading as contextually unfitting. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]
- 55:39/q11/p2 The refrain includes favors to believers and divine vengeance on defiance. — Al-Bayḍāwī names believers' favors; Bursawī says punishment of enemies benefits the beloved and destroys commanding-self traits, citing 6:45. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 55:39/q11/p3 God's preceding “attention” names the change from present creation to resurrection and requital. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it does not conflict with God's undistracted power or daily new affair. He glosses shawāẓ as smokeless flame or green fragment, nuḥās as smoke or smokeless flame, mutual help as ineffective, and the split heaven as rose-red leather. [7 notes: TABATABAI(الراغب)]

## 55:39/q12 Is there an inward reading of the absence of questioning?  [* · inward]
turns on: Settled disposition, spiritual marks, or effacement of the lower self.
- 55:39/q12/p1 Settled souls reach their stations without questioning; intermediate souls are stopped and questioned. — Al-Qāshānī says original light or fixed darkness determines the endpoint. Intermediate souls retain recoverable capacity; punishment varies with entrenchment until renewed readiness. The eternally wretched and those entering Paradise without reckoning are never stopped; 37:24 concerns some ultimately released. [6 notes: QASHANI]
- 55:39/q12/p2 Obedient spirits shine while the rebellious lower self carries darkened marks. — Bursawī locates lights of obedience on the spirit-human and sins on the dark self's face and neck; vengeance destroys commanding-self traits. [2 notes: BURSEVI]
- 55:39/q12/p3 After effacement of the lower self and subsistence in God, no sin remains. — Study Quran reports the Sufi view that action then follows God's will. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Sufi perspective)]

## 55:39/q13 What other interpretive principles occur in these source discussions?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The cited methodological issue.
- 55:39/q13/p1 A contradiction cannot rest on one identical cause, and impossible questions require explaining their terms. — Al-Burhān says an added distinction reveals an incomplete cause and uses standing and sitting at once as an impossible question. Al-Itqān resolves Adam's creation descriptions as successive states and the staff's serpents as shape versus light movement. [4 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN(الزركشي)]
- 55:39/q13/p2 A pronoun may precede its antecedent in wording but follow it in syntactic rank. — Al-Itqān gives this grammatical principle. [1 notes: ITQAN]
- 55:39/q13/p3 Legal force can be inferred from prohibitions, permissions, praise, threats and rewards. — Al-Burhān lists signs of prohibition, permission and praise implying recommendation or obligation. Al-Itqān reports counts of 500 or 150 legal verses and Ibn ʿAbd al-Salām's broader inference from manners, ethics, worldly and otherworldly benefits, and signs such as forgiveness, safety and healing. [7 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN(الشيخ عز الدين بن عبد السلام,الغزالي)]
- 55:39/q13/p4 Other apparent tensions may be resolved by differing subjects or abrogation. — Al-Itqān reports al-Shādhilī's monotheism-versus-deeds distinction for 3:102 and 64:16, an abrogation proposal, and the distinction of fair rights from emotional inclination in 4:3 and 4:129. [3 notes: ITQAN(الشيخ أبو الحسن الشاذلي,قيل)]
