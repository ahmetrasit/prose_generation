# 79:22: 7 questions

## 79:22/q01 What does ثم convey in the sequence of Pharaoh's acts?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether ثم points to elapsed time or a higher stage of defiance.
- 79:22/q01/p1 It marks a lapse of time before his further action. — Bursevi says trying to undo Moses' mission required time; Alusi likewise says the attempt took a long time. Biqai places the turn after a delay and many major events. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 79:22/q01/p2 It marks an escalation in Pharaoh's purpose. — Ibn Ashur says Pharaoh advanced from denial and disobedience to turning away, hurrying to suppress the call, and finally claiming divinity after sensing that the call might spread. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 79:22/q02 What does Pharaoh's turning away mean, and from what did he turn?  [أَدْبَرَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether أدبر is a figurative rejection, a literal departure or frightened flight, or an inward or ironic movement; the serpent account's timing and Pharaoh's response to Moses' sign are cited against flight.
- 79:22/q02/p1 He rejected truth, faith, or obedience to God. — Muqatil says he turned from truth; Tabari specifies Moses' call to obedience, fear, and God's oneness. Wahidi al-Basit, Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, and Qurtubi say faith or truth. Baghawi adds denial that the two signs came from God. Ibn Atiyya reports Mujahid's rejection of faith; Abu Hayyan attributes this figurative reading to the majority. Baydawi, Bursevi, and Alusi say obedience. Elmalili adds rebellion against God's command and Pharaoh's taunt, 'Who is the Lord of the worlds?' (26:23); he says the turn aimed toward destruction. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 79:22/q02/p2 He physically turned and fled, especially in fear of the serpent. — Ibn Atiyya reports exegetes who say he left the place and fled Moses. Al-Jubba'i, as reported by Tabrisi, says he fled the huge serpent; Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, and Study Quran also connect flight to the serpent or greatest sign. Nasafi adds that his haste showed rashness and impulsiveness. Abu Hayyan reports the literal flight as one view. Alusi transmits an account of frightened flight, defecation, a stampede, and 25,000 deaths. Alusi objects that this timing fits neither a flight after the magicians' customary gathering nor clearly the delay marked by ثم if before it; he also allows that the wording could stress the startling flight. Mujahid rejects physical running, and Bint al-Shati calls the frightened-flight reading forced and unsupported: a culture used to magic and staged serpents would not so terrify Pharaoh, and in 20:57 and 20:71 he remains defiant even after the magicians submit. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المفسرين); TABRISI(الجبائي); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(بعض الآثار); STUDYQURAN(R); MUJAHID(مجاهد)-; ALUSI-FULL-; BINTSHATI(مفسرون)-]
- 79:22/q02/p3 He turned away from Moses, his speech, or the sign he showed. — Nasafi says he turned from Moses; Bint al-Shati says from Moses and his great sign. Bursevi says from speaking with Moses. Tabrisi describes his turn as seeking something to defeat Moses' great proof, which only increased his error; Ibn Kathir says he turned to confront the manifest miracle with falsehood. Tabatabai says idbār is turning away and relates it to opposition to Moses' mission. [6 notes: TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI]
- 79:22/q02/p4 He literally left the gathering to work against Moses. — Bursevi offers leaving the gathering as an alternative sense. Alusi similarly offers a literal departure from the gathering to work against the sign or mission, without making the departure a terrified flight. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 79:22/q02/p5 His turn led into greater tyranny and ruin. — Biqai says that after a delay and many major events he persisted in still greater tyranny. Elmalili says he could not prevail in denial and rebellion, but exerted himself in the wrong direction and headed toward destruction. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 79:22/q02/p6 Idbār literally means turning one's back and can mark affairs going wrong. — Jishumi contrasts it with iqbāl, movement toward good and affairs becoming sound. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 79:22/q02/p7 He turned inwardly from the unity of attributes toward the station of the soul. — Qashani attributes the movement to hostility and the soul's dominance. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 79:22/q02/p8 Adbara can ironically mean that he 'began' his course toward ruin. — Alusi presents this as a possible reading while discussing difficulties with the serpent-flight account. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 79:22/q03 What was Pharaoh doing as he strove?  [يَسْعَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether يسعى describes corrupt action, a plan against Moses and his mission, the specific gathering of magicians, intended harm, or literal panicked running.
- 79:22/q03/p1 He worked to defeat Moses and suppress his mission. — Ibn Atiyya reports Mujahid's view that he opposed Moses' cause and law and tried to resolve his challenge. Tabrisi says he sought to defeat the great proof but only erred further. Baydawi says he tried to undo the mission; Nasafi says he plotted against Moses; Abu Hayyan says he struggled against him. Ibn Kathir says he opposed a manifest miracle with falsehood; Alusi says he thwarted the mission and sign. Ibn Ashur says he hurried to suppress the call after recognizing its danger of spreading. Tabatabai explains saʿy as energetic striving in opposition to Moses. Bint al-Shati says he confronted the danger and tried to prevent belief; she cites his defiance and threats after the magicians submitted (20:57, 20:71), and says familiar magic and staged serpents make terrified flight implausible. Islahi says Pharaoh plotted a contest with Moses and Aaron. Study Quran reports a quickly devised plan after he recognized the message's power. [13 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(R); BINTSHATI+]
- 79:22/q03/p2 He worked corruption, disobedience, and evil in the land. — Mujahid construes the striving as seeking corruption, citing 5:33, and rejects bodily running. Tabari reports the same gloss from Mujahid and says Pharaoh disobeyed God and did what angers Him, citing 28:20. Wahidi al-Basit, Wahidi al-Wajiz, Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Tabrisi, and Qurtubi describe spreading corruption in the land. Qurtubi also calls his conduct disbelief in Moses and disobedience to his Lord. Biqai says he persisted in greater tyranny; Elmalili calls it hard work in the wrong direction, toward destruction. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; MUJAHID(مجاهد)+]
- 79:22/q03/p3 He hurried away or fled in terror. — Ibn Atiyya reports the literal running and flight from Moses. Al-Jubba'i, reported by Tabrisi, attributes it to fear of the huge serpent; Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Alusi's transmitted account, and Study Quran also connect it to the serpent or greatest sign. Nasafi calls the haste rash and impulsive. Abu Hayyan reports flight for his life. Mujahid explicitly rejects bodily running; Bint al-Shati rejects panicked flight as unsupported, citing Pharaoh's later defiance (20:57, 20:71) and the culture's familiarity with staged serpents. Alusi questions its fit with the gathering of magicians and the delay signaled by ثم. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المفسرين); TABRISI(الجبائي); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(بعض الآثار); STUDYQURAN(R); MUJAHID(مجاهد)-; ALUSI-FULL-; BINTSHATI(مفسرون)-]
- 79:22/q03/p4 He hurried to gather magicians for a contest against Moses. — Muqatil cites 20:60 for the gathering. Ibn Kathir connects it to opposing Moses' manifest miracle. Islahi says Pharaoh arranged a contest with Moses, Aaron, and expert magicians; his advisers warned that ordinary magicians could lose. [3 notes: MUQATIL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 79:22/q03/p5 He strove to harm Moses. — Qurtubi transmits this as another explanation, without specifying a method. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]

## 79:22/q04 How does يسعى function grammatically?  [يَسْعَىٰ · grammar]
- 79:22/q04/p1 It is a circumstantial expression describing Pharaoh as he turns away. — Nahhas treats the verb as the state accompanying أدبر. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 79:22/q05 What do the serpent accounts say happened?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports give different events and details around the staff's transformation.
- 79:22/q05/p1 Pharaoh fled as the staff became a huge serpent, and the crowd stampeded. — Alusi transmits an account in which the serpent opened its mouth, Pharaoh defecated, and 25,000 people died in the stampede. Al-Jubba'i, as reported by Tabrisi, says Pharaoh fled in fear; Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, and Study Quran also report flight upon seeing the serpent or greatest sign. Alusi objects to the account's place in the sequence if the magicians had already gathered, or to ثم's delay if they had not. [6 notes: TABRISI(الجبائي); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض الآثار); STUDYQURAN(R); ALUSI-FULL-; BINTSHATI(مفسرون)-]
- 79:22/q05/p2 The serpent rose a mile, threatened Pharaoh, and became a staff again. — Alusi transmits a separate report: it threatened Pharaoh at Moses' command and returned to a staff when Moses took it. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض الآثار)]

## 79:22/q06 What does Pharaoh's conduct show about the origin of human acts?  [* · theology]
- 79:22/q06/p1 Human acts originate from the servants themselves. — Jishumi cites Pharaoh's transgression, denial, turning away, and calling out as evidence. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 79:22/q07 How does this episode serve the surah's purpose?  [* · links]
- 79:22/q07/p1 It helps establish resurrection, gathering, and humanity's final fate. — Biqai says the surah displays humanity's end after the preceding surahs' fuller exposition; its oaths show the matter has reached a decisive climax. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
