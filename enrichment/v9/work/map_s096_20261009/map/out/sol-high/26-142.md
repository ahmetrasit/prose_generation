# 26:142: 14 questions

## 26:142/q01 What time does 'when' mark?  [إِذْ · grammar]
- 26:142/q01/p1 It marks the extended period during which Thamūd denied the message. — Bursevī explains the temporal clause as giving the duration of their denial. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 26:142/q02 What does calling Ṣāliḥ 'their brother' mean and achieve?  [أَخُوهُمْ · referent]
- 26:142/q02/p1 He was their brother by lineage, so his familiar character and language made his appeal intelligible and credible. — Muqātil, Yaḥyā ibn Sallām, Jishumī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Bursevī identify lineage; Yaḥyā and Bursevī explicitly exclude brotherhood in religion. Bursevī says this connection made his trustworthiness and language known to them and helped them understand and believe. Biqāʿī says they knew Ṣāliḥ for truthfulness, trustworthiness, compassion and integrity. Samarrāʾī compares Shuʿayb: he belongs to Midian and is called its brother when addressing it (7:85; 11:84), but not when addressing the Aykah; the other Shuʿarāʾ prophets address their own peoples (23:36). [8 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 26:142/q03 Does calling Ṣāliḥ their brother affect his prophetic protection?  [أَخُوهُمْ صَٰلِحٌ · theology]
- 26:142/q03/p1 No; prophets are preserved before their mission and infallible afterward. — Bursevī gives this protection as the reason the lineage term poses no problem. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 26:142/q04 What lineage is given for Ṣāliḥ?  [صَٰلِحٌ · reports]
- 26:142/q04/p1 He is the son of ʿUbayd ibn Āṣif ibn Kāshiḥ ibn Ḥāḏir ibn Thamūd. — Bursevī supplies this chain of descent. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 26:142/q05 What is Ṣāliḥ's place among Arab prophets?  [صَٰلِحٌ · reports]
- 26:142/q05/p1 He is one of the Arab prophets, sent to the Arab people of Thamūd. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya lists Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Shuʿayb and Muḥammad as the Arab prophets; he says Ismāʿīl spoke Arabic but had Syriac ancestry and fathered the Arabs living today. Ibn Kathīr calls Thamūd an Arab people. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 26:142/q06 Where was Ṣāliḥ sent and where did Thamūd live?  [صَٰلِحٌ · reports]
- 26:142/q06/p1 Ṣāliḥ was sent from al-Ḥijr, where Thamūd lived between Wādī al-Qurā and Syria. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports Qatāda's statement that Ṣāliḥ was sent from al-Ḥijr. Ibn Kathīr locates the Arab people of Thamūd at al-Ḥijr between Wādī al-Qurā and Syria. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 26:142/q07 What mission did Ṣāliḥ announce to his people?  [قَالَ صَٰلِحٌ · interpretation]
- 26:142/q07/p1 As God's trustworthy messenger to Thamūd, he conveyed God's religion and called them to worship God alone and obey the message. — Ibn Kathīr calls him God's servant and messenger and says he summoned Thamūd to exclusive worship and obedience, which they rejected and opposed. Kur'an Yolu says he was sent as a prophet to convey God's religion. Iṣlāḥī explains his ensuing declaration as denying that he is a false prophet or bluffer and affirming that he faithfully conveys God's message. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 26:142/q08 When did Thamūd live relative to other peoples?  [صَٰلِحٌ · reports]
- 26:142/q08/p1 Thamūd came after ʿĀd and before Abraham. — Ibn Kathīr gives this sequence. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 26:142/q09 What happened to Ṣāliḥ and his people according to Ibn ʿAbbās's report?  [صَٰلِحٌ · reports]
- 26:142/q09/p1 They first believed, then abandoned Islam after Ṣāliḥ's death; God revived and recommissioned him, but they rejected the she-camel sign, hamstrung it and were destroyed. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports this sequence from Ibn ʿAbbās: the people demanded a sign after Ṣāliḥ returned; the she-camel came, then they disbelieved and hamstrung it. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس)]

## 26:142/q10 What later journey passed through Thamūd?  [صَٰلِحٌ · reports]
- 26:142/q10/p1 The Prophet passed by Thamūd on a campaign journey toward Syria, reached Tabūk, then returned to Medina to prepare for it. — Ibn Kathīr supplies this account of the Prophet's route and return. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 26:142/q11 What does Ṣāliḥ's opening appeal ask his people to do?  [أَلَا تَتَّقُونَ · interpretation]
- 26:142/q11/p1 He gently calls them to heed the warning and fear God and His punishment. — Muqātil and Elmalılı gloss the appeal as fearing God; Yaḥyā ibn Sallām gives the same sense and refers to the earlier account. Jishumī specifies God's punishment, Bursevī specifies punishment for associating partners with Him, and al-Ṭabarī specifies punishment for disobedience and following corrupt people. Biqāʿī describes the question as a gentle invitation to take heed. [7 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]

## 26:142/q12 How does Ṣāliḥ's later speech explain his opening rebuke?  [أَلَا تَتَّقُونَ · interpretation]
- 26:142/q12/p1 Their secure and prosperous life will end; they will leave this world and return to God for accountability. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the later speech explains the rebuke by showing that their way of life cannot last and that they return to God. Kur'an Yolu says this telling warns against arrogance founded on power and wealth, stresses worldly life's transience and points to accountability in the hereafter. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 26:142/q13 How does Ṣāliḥ's opening compare with the other prophetic openings in this sūra?  [* · links]
- 26:142/q13/p1 It repeats the appeal and narrative pattern used for the other prophets. — Iṣlāḥī says the formulation repeats the end of the preceding prophetic account, where he had already explained it (26:124, 26:126). Yaḥyā ibn Sallām also refers the appeal to the earlier account. The Study Quran says Ṣāliḥ's opening follows the pattern discussed for Noah and points to the openings at 26:105–109, 26:123–127, 26:160–164 and 26:176–180. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 26:142/q14 How does this account relate to other Qur'anic tellings of Ṣāliḥ?  [* · links]
- 26:142/q14/p1 The Qur'an tells Ṣāliḥ's story in several places, each highlighting different aspects. — Kur'an Yolu makes this comparison. Asad directs readers to the account at 7:73 and to a version he cites at 2:61–68. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
