# 92:4: 21 questions

## 92:4/q01 What does سَعْيَكُمْ mean here?  [سَعْيَكُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word names human action itself or the consequences it brings.
- 92:4/q01/p1 People's work, deeds, or purposeful pursuits. — Ibn Qutayba and Ibn al-Jawzi give its basic sense as quick walking, below running; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls its use for work metaphorical. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ adds persistence and directed purpose, citing 20:20, 20:66, 66:8, 57:12 for literal or figurative movement, 21:94, 17:19, 18:104 for sustained work, and 2:114, 5:33, 5:64, 76:22, 53:40, 88:9 for directed aims. Jishumī includes earning; Corpus Coranicum compares 88:8, 76:22, 53:39. Ṭabāṭabāʾī combines swift walking with purposeful deeds. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ikrima, and Muḥammad b. Kaʿb simply gloss it as action or deeds. [27 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNABIHATIM(عكرمة); NAHHAS(محمد بن كعب); THALABI(عكرمة وسائر المفسرين); JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(المفسرون); IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI(عكرمة وسائر المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة وسائر المفسرين); ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR(عكرمة); DURR-FULL(عكرمة); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 92:4/q01/p2 The consequences of people's effort. — Iṣlāḥī cites 53:39–40 and explicitly takes saʿy as the consequence of effort rather than effort itself. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 92:4/q02 Who is addressed by كُمْ?  [سَعْيَكُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the address is treated as general or situated among Meccans.
- 92:4/q02/p1 All people, including believers and unbelievers, especially people accountable for their acts. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly includes both groups; Biqāʿī identifies morally accountable people. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says direct general address supports a universal lesson, and Rāzī cites 92:4 and 92:14 for general scope. [5 notes: BIQAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BINTSHATI+]
- 92:4/q02/p2 The people of Mecca. — Muqātil locates the contrast in Meccan deeds, good and evil; al-Wāḥidī transmits this formulation. [2 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)]

## 92:4/q03 Why call human activity سَعْي?  [سَعْيَكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 92:4/q03/p1 The word evokes exertion and persistent work directed toward an aim. — Biqāʿī says each person puts utmost effort into work. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ explains its literal walking sense and the sustained, purposeful work it implies, illustrating directed aims with 2:114, 5:33, 5:64, 76:22, 53:40, 88:9. [4 notes: BIQAI; BINTSHATI]

## 92:4/q04 What does شَتَّىٰ mean?  [شَتَّىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether separation here means variation between pursuits or the disarray of efforts.
- 92:4/q04/p1 Different, separate, or far apart. — Al-Farrāʾ, Qatāda, and many others gloss it as diverse. Jishumī links its family of words to distance and division; Ṭabrisī compares shattān for distant things; Qurṭubī says unlike deeds are far apart. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ distinguishes shattā, variation opposed to concord, from ashtāt, scattering opposed to gathering, citing 99:6, 59:14, 24:61 and the Qurʾanic uses for plants, efforts, and divided hearts. Wāḥidī points to varied plants at 20:53; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites a line by Taʾabbaṭa Sharran on diverse destinations and paths. [27 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BIQAI; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:4/q04/p2 Scattered, disordered, or fragmented. — Elmalılı reads this as uncoordinated human effort; he connects dispersion to the call to hold together in 3:103. Other glosses also use 'scattered' without his social inference. [5 notes: ELMALILI; BAYDAWI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI+]

## 92:4/q05 What is the form and predication of شَتَّىٰ?  [شَتَّىٰ · grammar]
turns on: How a plural or abstract description is predicated of the singular verbal noun.
- 92:4/q05/p1 It is the plural of شَتِيت, predicated of a collectively plural سَعْي. — Kashshāf, Rāzī, Qurṭubī, Bayḍāwī, Biqāʿī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Iṣlāḥī give the singular shatīt; Qurṭubī compares marīḍ/marḍā and Biqāʿī qatīl/qatlā. Ālūsī says the annexed generic verbal noun includes all endeavors, explaining its plural predicate. [12 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BIQAI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:4/q05/p2 It may function as an abstract or feminine verbal noun meaning separation. — Ālūsī reports the abstract-noun analysis and says treating the striving as separation itself heightens the assertion. [2 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 92:4/q05/p3 It may imply a possessive term: effort possessing separation. — Ālūsī reports a predicate with an implied dhū or dhāt. [2 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 92:4/q05/p4 It may be an adjective meaning scattered. — Ālūsī reports the adjectival analysis. [2 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 92:4/q06 Where is the answer to the opening oath?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Whether the emphatic clause serves as the stated oath response or the response is supplied by ellipsis.
- 92:4/q06/p1 This verse is the oath's answer. — Ṭabarī says learned authorities agree and transmits Qatāda; Naḥḥās, Baghāwī, Qurṭubī, Rāzī, and others identify the clause as the answer. The oath begins with the veiling night. [18 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); NAHHAS; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:4/q06/p2 The oath's answer may be left unstated. — Al-Samīn allows omission as in comparable passages; Ālūsī also allows an omitted answer beside Qatāda's explicit-answer reading. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)]

## 92:4/q07 Why is the clause emphatic?  [إِنَّ لَشَتَّىٰ · rhetoric]
- 92:4/q07/p1 The emphasis marks a surprising or disputed divergence. — Biqāʿī says the audience shares a common aim, making divergent efforts surprising. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says inna and the lām stress the contested, unseen afterlife outcomes rather than ordinary worldly work. [2 notes: BIQAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 92:4/q08 In what respects do people's efforts differ?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the diversity concerns moral deeds, their consequences, goals and sincerity, character, social coordination, or gendered expectations.
- 92:4/q08/p1 People pursue morally opposed deeds and aims: good and evil, faith and unbelief, obedience and sin, guidance and error. — Muqātil speaks of Meccans' good and evil deeds. Ṭabarī contrasts disbelief/disobedience with faith/obedience; Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds what pleases or angers God. Qurṭubī and Rāzī stress the distance between opposing actions, citing 59:20, 32:18, 45:21, 35:21; Biqāʿī adds belief, speech, salvation, and sin, citing 16:35. Ibn Kathīr connects moral opposites to the oath's opposites; Ṭabāṭabāʾī names giving, piety, faith versus miserliness, self-sufficiency, denial. Elmalılı cites 32:18 and 45:21; Quṭb points to 92:5–9. Study Quran says redemption or perdition with no middle path. Broad glosses by Qatāda, Nasafī, Bayḍāwī and others leave the later verses to specify the difference. [53 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-QT; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(المفسرون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(النبي,عكرمة وسائر المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة وسائر المفسرين); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI(مفسّرین); QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; BINTSHATI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN]
- 92:4/q08/p2 People's efforts lead to different results and recompense, Paradise or Fire. — Ibn ʿAbbās is reported by Wāḥidī, Ṭabrisī, and Ibn al-Jawzī. Māwardī gives this as a distinct possibility; Rāzī connects guidance to Gardens and error to Fire and cites 59:20, 32:18, 45:21. Qurṭubī reports the alternative. Ālūsī makes varied reward the point; Quṭb distinguishes nature, motive, direction, result, and recompense. Asad notes good and bad ends produce divergent consequences, comparing 91:8. Al-Sāmarrāʾī includes times, agents, states, and destinations. [20 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,الزجاج); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(مفسّرین); QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 92:4/q08/p3 People differ in worldly, afterlife, and spiritual goals and in inward quality. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ lists speech and action, intention alone, heart with action, worldly or afterlife aims, and striving solely for God; he ranks the transient world lowest and God highest, citing 43:32. Qushayrī lists desire, status, piety, remembrance, God's approval and God Himself, as well as combined bodily obedience, sincerity, counsel, and speech. Ṭabrisī reports worldly versus afterlife pursuits and self-liberation versus self-destruction. [5 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء); QUSHAYRI; TABRISI]
- 92:4/q08/p4 People differ in temperament and disposition. — Māwardī and Qurṭubī contrast compassion and harshness, patience or gentleness and rashness, generosity and miserliness; Māwardī says the oath can be directed to this point. Quṭb adds tastes, ideas, and concerns, picturing each person as inhabiting a different world. [6 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 92:4/q08/p5 People's efforts are fragmented and mutually obstructive. — Elmalılı says inconsistent, disordered action contrasts with the unity and cooperation needed for success; he cites 3:103 and 11:119. Conflicting desires, disbelief, idolatry, and sin scatter hearts and damage society; futile efforts cancel one another. He compares the fallen idolater at 22:31 and deeds like ashes at 14:18. [6 notes: ELMALILI; ELMALILI+]
- 92:4/q08/p6 A person's aspiration or conduct may cross the expected male–female pattern. — Biqāʿī says a man may have feminine aspiration and a woman masculine action, so bodily sex does not fix the kind of striving. [1 notes: BIQAI]

## 92:4/q09 What inward meaning is drawn from differing striving?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the inner difference is a hierarchy of aims, a prior allotment of stations, or the lack of inward acceptance despite outward goods.
- 92:4/q09/p1 Striving spans ranks of sincerity, intention, nearness, and orientation toward God. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ ranges from speech or deed to intention, heart and action, worldly aims, afterlife aims, and God alone; 43:32 illustrates allotted ranks and the highest Godward striving is not lost. Qushayrī ranges from desire and status to remembrance, approval, and God Himself, including body, sincerity, counsel, and speech. [3 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء); QUSHAYRI]
- 92:4/q09/p2 One should see one's striving as allotted by God before creation, with distinct spiritual stations. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ likens seekers, knowers, lovers, those who arrive, and those taking divine qualities to ranks of courtiers and intimates; he cites 43:32. [1 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
- 92:4/q09/p3 Attraction to righteous people, good acts, or wise speech can exist without acceptance, sincerity, or truthfulness. — Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Nahrawānī says this is how God treats a servant He dislikes. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو عبد الله النهرواني)]

## 92:4/q10 What does differing striving imply about God's decree and human acts?  [* · theology]
turns on: How divine decree and moral responsibility are related in the opposing human acts.
- 92:4/q10/p1 Divergent good and evil acts occur under God's inspiration, creation, and prior decree. — Ibn al-Zubayr ties divergence to God's eternal decree and testing and to purified and corrupted souls at 91:10; he cites inspiration at 91:8, human deeds created by God at 37:96, and God's being unquestioned at 21:23, opposing Qadariyya and Muʿtazila. Biqāʿī says divine inspiration covers giving and piety as well as stinginess and denial, with guidance and both worlds belonging to God; he cites 92:5, 92:7, 92:12, and 12:105. [6 notes: BIQAI(أبو جعفر ابن الزبير); BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير)]
- 92:4/q10/p2 Opposing actions cannot both receive God's approval, so divine will does not excuse idolatry. — Biqāʿī argues that opposite deeds cannot both please or anger God and rejects the polytheists' appeal to God's will for their idolatry at 16:35. [1 notes: BIQAI]

## 92:4/q11 What event is reported behind the contrast of efforts?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which named people and transaction the reported occasion identifies; one Bilāl account also varies the payment amount.
- 92:4/q11/p1 Abū Bakr's freeing of Bilāl is contrasted with Umayya and Ubayy, sons of Khalaf, who tortured him. — Ibn Masʿūd's reports say Abū Bakr bought Bilāl and freed him for God; Māwardī specifies a cloak and ten coins, while Elmalılı says a cloak and ten uqiyyas. The reports also gloss affirming the good as monotheism and the hard path as Hell. Al-Wāḥidī's ʿAbd Allāh report contrasts Abū Bakr and Umayya. Qurṭubī says most commentators favor an Abū Bakr occasion, citing Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn al-Zubayr, and others. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن مسعود); MAWARDI(ابن مسعود); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود); WAHIDI-ASBAB(عبد الله); DURR(عبد الله بن مسعود); ELMALILI(İbn Mes‘ûd); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 92:4/q11/p2 The verse was revealed concerning Abū Bakr and Abū Sufyān. — Al-Farrāʾ, Wāḥidī, and Rāzī report this pairing; Wāḥidī's Basīṭ excerpt ends before further context. [5 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-QT; RAZI(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL(المفسرون)]
- 92:4/q11/p3 The occasion was Abū Bakr's spending on Muslims contrasted with Umayya b. Khalaf's miserliness and unbelief. — Al-Qaffāl reports this sura-wide account and expressly says its message applies generally. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال)]
- 92:4/q11/p4 The contrast arose from Abū al-Daḥdāḥ obtaining a palm for a neighbor from an Anṣārī owner. — ʿAṭāʾ says fallen dates entered the neighbor's home. The owner refused the Prophet's offer of a tree in Paradise; Abū al-Daḥdāḥ traded his garden or orchard for the palm, which the Prophet gave to the neighbor. The giver and miser are thus Abū al-Daḥdāḥ and the Anṣārī. Qurṭubī maps ease to Paradise, hardship to Hell, and death to loss of wealth and notes an Abū al-Daḥdāḥ report at 2:245. [3 notes: BAGHAWI(عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 92:4/q12 Does the verse's lesson extend beyond its reported occasion?  [* · interpretation]
- 92:4/q12/p1 Yes; the address and warning apply generally to human efforts. — Al-Qaffāl says the Abū Bakr and Umayya context has a general message; Rāzī points to 92:4 and the warning at 92:14. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers a universal reading whatever the occasion because the passage addresses people directly, and says the Fire warning is general before specifying the denier at 92:15–16, comparing 70:15–17. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI+]

## 92:4/q13 How do the opening oaths relate to this statement?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the oath pairs chiefly frame moral contrast or demonstrate the paired structure of this life and the Hereafter.
- 92:4/q13/p1 Visible opposed pairs foreshadow opposed human deeds and their outcomes. — Ibn Kathīr says the oaths' opposites match human good and evil. Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts the created human body and sexes with the preceding sura's oath by the shaped soul. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says night covering and day brightening, then male and female, prepare the moral contrasts of 92:5–18. Neuwirth sees the elemental pairs as a structural matrix for aims and recompense; Corpus Coranicum connects cosmic and moral duality to 91:1–8 and sura 55. Al-Sāmarrāʾī adds opposed times, agents, states, and destinations. [10 notes: IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; ASAD-NOTES; CORPUSCORANICUM(Neuwirth); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 92:4/q13/p2 The creation's paired order points to an afterlife counterpart and a necessary Judgment. — Iṣlāḥī says the outer and inner worlds establish an afterlife; otherwise creation lacks purpose and the righteous and wrongdoers would be equal, contrary to divine wisdom. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 92:4/q14 Why do the oaths say male and female rather than 'the pair'?  [* · rhetoric]
- 92:4/q14/p1 The separate terms stress difference, while 'the pair' suggests likeness and union. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts al-Layl with al-Qiyāma and al-Najm, which discuss embryonic development and divine power; he cites 75:37–39, 53:43–45, 38:58, and 30:21. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 92:4/q15 What wording of the preceding oath is reported from Ibn Masʿūd?  [* · readings]
- 92:4/q15/p1 His reading says 'the day when it shines' and 'the male and the female,' omitting 'what He created.' — Al-Durr transmits this through Abū Isḥāq from ʿAbd Allāh; the variant concerns the oath to which 92:4 responds. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو إسحاق)]

## 92:4/q16 How does this statement lead into the following verses?  [* · links]
- 92:4/q16/p1 It summarizes divergent efforts before the following verses specify the contrasting paths and fates. — Kashshāf, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ say the later verses explain shattā. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the summary builds suspense for the giver and God-fearing person. Qurṭubī's prophetic image makes one person buy and free himself and another sell and ruin himself; al-Thaʿlabī and Baghawī give the same contrast, with Abū Mālik al-Ashʿarī transmitting that everyone begins the day selling himself. Al-Sāmarrāʾī links God's guidance in 92:12 to the righteous example in 92:5–7; without seeking guidance, people diverge. [19 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI(أبو مالك الأشعري); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو مالك الأشعري); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI(النبي,عكرمة وسائر المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة وسائر المفسرين); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 92:4/q17 How does the verse relate to the preceding sura?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the inter-sura link is the prior soul's moral state or the shared pattern of cosmic duality.
- 92:4/q17/p1 Al-Layl develops al-Shams's purified and corrupted soul into the divergent paths and fates of human effort. — Abū Ḥayyān says the next sura identifies traits behind the success and failure of 91:9–10, then warns of Fire. Ibn al-Zubayr and Biqāʿī link divergence to divine inspiration and decree and to Thamūd's fate. Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts al-Shams's shaped soul, intellect, and knowledge with al-Layl's created bodies and sexes. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ sees corresponding light/dark and male/female polarity. Iṣlāḥī calls the two suras a pair and says al-Layl explains what corrupts or purifies the soul. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI(أبو جعفر ابن الزبير); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:4/q17/p2 The same cosmic and moral duality found in al-Shams structures this oath. — Corpus Coranicum compares 91:1–8 and notes its wider recurrence in sura 55. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 92:4/q18 Why must divergent good and evil have different final outcomes?  [* · theology]
- 92:4/q18/p1 Judgment is required by divine wisdom and human moral reason. — Iṣlāḥī says the paired creation implies an afterlife counterpart: without judgment life is pointless, the righteous and wrongdoers would be equal, and every deed could go unanswered; he cites 23:115 and 68:35–36. The contrast between deeds and outcomes also appears at 59:20, 32:18, and 45:21 in Rāzī's argument. [7 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 92:4/q19 What practical lesson is drawn from the diverse efforts?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the stated diversity yields a call to collective unity or to judging people by conduct.
- 92:4/q19/p1 Fragmentation should yield to shared thought and ordered, cooperative action. — Elmalılı prefers this as the clearest warning against pre-Islamic fragmentation; he cites 3:103. He says conflicting aims make toil futile and harm individuals and society, comparing 22:31 and 14:18. [6 notes: ELMALILI; ELMALILI+]
- 92:4/q19/p2 Human worth depends on religious, moral, and humane conduct rather than sex. — Kur'an Yolu says efforts differ in kind, quality, and purpose, leading into poverty, generosity, and miserliness; biological sex is not the measure. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 92:4/q20 How does the sura's open wording serve this contrast?  [* · rhetoric]
- 92:4/q20/p1 Leaving the oath, answer, and later virtues unrestricted permits a broad contrast. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says a skilled speaker chooses ambiguity or precision according to meaning and that omitting a preposition can allow several readings. He contrasts al-Layl's unrestricted oath and answer, giving, piety, and al-ḥusnā with al-Shams's more specified wording across 91:1–15. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 92:4/q21 How does spiritual light or darkness shape the divergence?  [* · inward]
- 92:4/q21/p1 Light draws some toward spiritual good, while darkness draws others into evil. — Qāshānī attributes the opposed inclinations to the dominance of light or darkness. [1 notes: QASHANI]
