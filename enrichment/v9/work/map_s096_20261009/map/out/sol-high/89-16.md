# 89:16: 37 questions

## 89:16/q01 Who is the person tested and speaking?  [ٱبْتَلَىٰهُ فَيَقُولُ · referent]
turns on: Whether reports name a particular occasion or the definite 'human' describes a class or genus.
- 89:16/q01/p1 Umayya ibn Khalaf. — Muqātil relates his argument with ʿAbd Allāh ibn Nufayl and Ibn Khaṭal; al-Kalbī and Muqātil are cited together by al-Baghawī. Some describe Umayya as a disbeliever who called riches honor. [8 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI(الكلبي ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل); RAZI(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(مقاتل); ITQAN]
- 89:16/q01/p2 The class of disbelievers who deny recompense and judge by wealth. — Al-Baghawī reports this as an alternative to a named occasion. Al-Qurṭubī extends the description to every unbeliever and observes that some Muslims share the error. [11 notes: BAGHAWI(قيل); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 89:16/q01/p3 The human kind in its ordinary tendency, without saying every individual does this. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī takes the definite article in al-insān as marking the genus; its members can turn gifts into punishment. [4 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 89:16/q01/p4 ʿUtba ibn Rabīʿa and Abū Ḥudhayfa ibn al-Mughīra. — Ibn ʿAbbās is cited for a pair of persons; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ's version calls the first ʿUtba b. Abī Rabīʿa. [4 notes: RAZI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; BINTSHATI(ابن عباس)]
- 89:16/q01/p5 Ubayy ibn Khalaf. — Al-Kalbī is cited for this name; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ also names Muqātil alongside him, differing from reports about Umayya. [4 notes: RAZI(الكلبي); RAZI-FULL(الكلبي); BINTSHATI(الكلبي ومقاتل)]
- 89:16/q01/p6 ʿUtba ibn Rabīʿa alone, with the description applying generally. — Al-Suhaylī reports him as the occasion, as transmitted by Bursawī. [1 notes: BURSEVI(السهيلي)]

## 89:16/q02 What happened in the reported occasion?  [ٱبْتَلَىٰهُ رِزْقَهُۥ فَيَقُولُ · reports]
- 89:16/q02/p1 Umayya claimed wealth proved God's honor after Nufayl urged him to do good. — Muqātil says Umayya cited 47:11 and compared his riches with Nufayl's poverty; Ibn Khaṭal replied that God might have done so. Later sources report the Umayya occasion more briefly. [5 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI(الكلبي ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل); ITQAN]

## 89:16/q03 What does God's testing through restricted provision test?  [ٱبْتَلَىٰهُ رِزْقَهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the trial is described by the response it exposes or by a possible beneficial outcome.
- 89:16/q03/p1 It tests patience, contentment, and continued obedience; expansion tests gratitude. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, and others cite 21:35. Al-Biqāʿī emphasizes distress; Islahi adds fulfillment of others' rights and the peaceful soul. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ also compares further tests in 47:4,31, 7:141, 14:6 and 2:49. [37 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; QURTUBI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI; TABATABAI; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BAGHAWI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 89:16/q03/p2 It may be protective care that redirects a person toward God and later honor. — Muqātil says poverty lightens reckoning; Fath al-Mawṣilī rejoiced during household deprivation. Bursawī says it teaches patience and shields from enemies' greed; Elmalılı says it may earn reward. Al-Bayḍāwī notes abundance can bring enemies and worldly absorption. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI(فتح الموصلي); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم); ELMALILI]

## 89:16/q04 How do the two conditional clauses fit grammatically?  [وَأَمَّآ إِذَا مَا فَيَقُولُ · grammar]
- 89:16/q04/p1 Each 'he says' is a predicate; the second clause supplies an implied human subject. — Al-Zamakhsharī treats the intervening time phrase as delayed in sense. Al-Nasafī says the fāʾ follows ammā's conditional force. Bursawī calls the fāʾs results of the trials. [11 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 89:16/q05 How can 'idhā mā' be analysed?  [إِذَا مَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā removes the time-adverb sense.
- 89:16/q05/p1 It may retain adverbial force, which al-Suyūṭī considers likelier for Qur'anic idhā mā. — Al-Suyūṭī contrasts Sībawayh's particle account of unattested Qur'anic idh mā with al-Mubarrad's adverbial account; he cites 42:37 and 9:92. Sībawayh treats idh as like 'time' and idhā as an adverb resembling ʿinda. [3 notes: ITQAN; SIBAWAYH]
- 89:16/q05/p2 It may be treated as a particle. — Al-Suyūṭī permits this analysis of Qur'anic idhā mā, mentioning Sībawayh's treatment of idh mā. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 89:16/q06 Why use idhā for the condition?  [إِذَا مَا · rhetoric]
- 89:16/q06/p1 Idhā ordinarily presents a confirmed occurrence. — Al-Zarkashī contrasts in for uncertain or hypothetical cases, often with a present verb, and notes definite versus indefinite forms can mark particular and general kinds. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 89:16/q07 What does ibtalāhu mean here?  [ٱبْتَلَىٰهُ · meaning]
- 89:16/q07/p1 God tried the person, here through poverty and constricted provision. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya glosses ibtalāhu as 'tested him' and contrasts naʿʿamahu, 'made him enjoy blessing.' Al-Qurṭubī specifies poverty; al-Ṭabarī adds the person's overlooked health. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA; TAB; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI; BAYDAWI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 89:16/q08 How is qadara or qaddara read?  [فَقَدَرَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the dāl is pronounced once or geminated; some transmissions assign readers differently.
- 89:16/q08/p1 The dāl is light: qadara. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers this as the authoritative majority reading; al-Qurṭubī prefers it with 65:7. Reports generally call it the majority reading, though Abū Ḥayyān also lists variant transmissions for Abū Jaʿfar, ʿĪsā, Khālid, and al-Ḥasan. [26 notes: FARRA(عاصم والأعمش وعامة القراء); TAB(عامة قراء الأمصار); TAB-FULL(عامة قراء الأمصار); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; JISHUMI(ابن عامر وأبو جعفر); BAGHAWI(أبو جعفر وابن عامر); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(جمهور الناس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); TABRISI(أبو جعفر، ابن عامر); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور وأبو جعفر وعيسى وخالد والحسن وابن عامر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; NASHR; IBNASHUR(الجمهور); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عامر وأبو جعفر); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عامر)+; QURTUBI+; TAB-FULL+; TAB+]
- 89:16/q08/p2 The dāl is doubled: qaddara. — Reports attribute it principally to Abū Jaʿfar and Ibn ʿĀmir; also Nāfiʿ, al-Ḥasan, ʿĪsā, Khālid, Syrian readers and Yazīd in particular transmissions. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān take doubling as intensity, not a transitive causative. [33 notes: FARRA(نافع وأبو جعفر); TAB(عامة قراء الأمصار); TAB-FULL(أبو جعفر القارئ); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; JISHUMI(أبو عمرو,ابن عامر وأبو جعفر); BAGHAWI(أبو جعفر وابن عامر); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(الحسن وأبو جعفر وعيسى,خالد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن وأبو جعفر وعيسى); TABRISI(أبو جعفر، ابن عامر); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عامر); NASAFI(الشامي ويزيد); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور,الجمهور وأبو جعفر وعيسى وخالد والحسن وابن عامر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; NASHR; ALUSI(أبو جعفر وعيسى وخالد والحسن وابن عامر); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR(ابن عامر وأبو جعفر); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عامر وأبو جعفر); QIRAAT-ER]

## 89:16/q09 What does restricting rizq mean?  [فَقَدَرَ رِزْقَهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb stresses scarcity, sufficient allotment, or the act of exact measure.
- 89:16/q09/p1 God narrowed provision, often to basic subsistence without surplus. — Ibn Qutayba and Abū ʿAmr compare qadara/qaddara with qattara; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls allotting a measured amount a euphemism for scarcity, opposed to expansion (42:27). Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites 34:36,39; 13:26; 17:30; 30:37; 39:52; 42:12; 65:7. Biqāʿī calls constriction metaphorical. [37 notes: AKHFASH(بعضهم); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(أبو عمرو بن العلاء); TUSTARI; TAB(ابن زيد); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(زيد); BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA(الحسن وأبو جعفر وعيسى,جمهور الناس,خالد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور,الحسن وأبو جعفر وعيسى); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(ابن زيد); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; TABATABAI(الإمام الصادق); BINTSHATI+; IBNATIYYA+]
- 89:16/q09/p2 The doubled qaddara can mean God gave the person enough, a measured sufficiency. — Abū ʿAmr distinguishes it from the light form's constriction and argues that one given enough would not complain of humiliation. Al-Akhfash says God sets provision by measure; al-Jishumī derives both forms from measurement. [9 notes: AKHFASH; TAB(أبو عمرو بن العلاء); TAB-FULL(أبو عمرو بن العلاء); JISHUMI(أبو عمرو); QURTUBI(أبو عمرو); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو); BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-]
- 89:16/q09/p3 The doubled form emphasizes precise apportionment or calculation. — Qirāʾāt-Er takes Form II as intensified measuring. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ traces qadr and taqdīr through measure, judgment, ability and divine regulation, also noting high rank in Laylat al-Qadr; al-Jishumī connects ability to a measured extent. [4 notes: QIRAAT-ER; BINTSHATI; JISHUMI]

## 89:16/q10 Whose provision is meant by rizqahu?  [رِزْقَهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the pronoun is read as the agent or the recipient of provision.
- 89:16/q10/p1 The suffix refers to the tested human, as object of the allotment. — Ibn ʿĀshūr allows this analysis of the verbal noun. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 89:16/q10/p2 The suffix refers to God, as subject of the allotment. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives this as the other possible analysis. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 89:16/q11 What is the origin of rizq as a word?  [رِزْقَهُۥ · meaning]
- 89:16/q11/p1 It is a Persian loanword mediated through Aramaic. — Corpus Coranicum lists early Meccan occurrences at 51:22, 51:57–58, and 56:82. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 89:16/q12 Why are provision and restriction ascribed to God?  [ٱبْتَلَىٰهُ فَقَدَرَ رِزْقَهُۥ · theology]
- 89:16/q12/p1 God established the worldly causes and occasions by which people receive them. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says events coincide with people at particular opportunities and times under God's laws. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 89:16/q13 How is rabbī read?  [رَبِّىٓ · readings]
turns on: Whether the possessive yāʾ is opened or left still.
- 89:16/q13/p1 Its final yāʾ has fatḥa. — Reports assign this to Nāfiʿ, Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr, Abū Jaʿfar, Yaʿqūb, or the readers of the two Hijaz sanctuaries, with differing lists; Qirāʾāt-Er explicitly describes full vocalization. [7 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI; NASAFI(الحجازي وأبو عمرو); NASHR; IBNASHUR(نافع وابن كثير وأبو عمرو); IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وابن كثير وأبو عمرو); QIRAAT-ER]
- 89:16/q13/p2 Its final yāʾ is still. — The same reports attribute sukūn to the remaining readers, with assignment depending on the transmission. [4 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI; IBNASHUR(الباقون); IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وابن كثير وأبو عمرو)]

## 89:16/q14 Why put rabbī before the verb?  [رَبِّىٓ أَهَٰنَنِ · rhetoric]
- 89:16/q14/p1 It strengthens the claim and makes it sound certain and unhesitating. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the person asserts his conviction firmly. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 89:16/q15 How is the final first-person yāʾ of ahānani read?  [أَهَٰنَنِ · readings]
turns on: How the verse-ending yāʾ is transmitted and how pause, analogy and the consonantal codex interact.
- 89:16/q15/p1 It is retained in connection and at pause. — Al-Bazzī from Ibn Kathīr, Yaʿqūb, and in some reports Qunbul retain it; al-Fārisī argues a verb can keep final yāʾ even where nouns like qāḍin lose it. Al-Qurṭubī calls it an original ending. [17 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو علي,ابن كثير,البزي عن ابن كثير); JISHUMI(ابن كثير ويعقوب); BAGHAWI(أهل الحجاز والبصرة,ابن كثير ويعقوب); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل الحجاز والبصرة وابن كثير ويعقوب); QURTUBI(البزي وابن محيصن ويعقوب); NASHR; ABUHAYYAN(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وباقي السبعة); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(ابن كثير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير); IBNASHUR(ابن كثير); IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وابن كثير وابن عامر وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي ويعقوب); QIRAAT-ER]
- 89:16/q15/p2 It is retained in connection and omitted at pause. — Nāfiʿ and Abū Jaʿfar are commonly assigned this; connected speech preserves the underlying form, while pause and the codex support omission. Al-Aʿshā's line illustrates deletion. [36 notes: SIBAWAYH(أبو عمرو,النابغة); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو علي,أبو عمرو,سيبويه,علي بن نصر عن أبي عمرو,نافع); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ونافع,أبو عمرو); IBNATIYYA(نافع وأبو عمرو); BAGHAWI(أهل الحجاز والبصرة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل الحجاز والبصرة وابن كثير ويعقوب); QURTUBI(أبو عمرو,المدنيون); NASHR; ABUHAYYAN(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وباقي السبعة); SAMIN-DURR(الشاعر); ALUSI(نافع); ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير); IBNASHUR(نافع); IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وابن كثير وابن عامر وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي ويعقوب); QIRAAT-ER; FARISI-HUJJA+; NASHR+]
- 89:16/q15/p3 It is omitted in both connection and pause. — ʿĀṣim, Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamza, and al-Kisāʾī are usually listed; reports vary on Qunbul and Yaʿqūb. Readers invoke ease, verse-final cadence, and absence from the codex; the nūn has sukūn at pause and its kasra may carry the omitted yāʾ's force. [31 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو زيد,أبو علي,الكسائي,عاصم وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي,قنبل وعاصم وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي); JISHUMI(أبو عمرو,ابن عامر وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي); BAGHAWI(الآخرون); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل الحجاز والبصرة وابن كثير ويعقوب); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(الباقون); NASHR; ABUHAYYAN(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وباقي السبعة); SAMIN-DURR(الشاعر); ALUSI(باقي السبعة); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR(ابن عامر وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي ويعقوب); IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وابن كثير وابن عامر وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي ويعقوب)]

## 89:16/q16 What does ahānan mean?  [أَهَٰنَنِ · meaning]
- 89:16/q16/p1 He treated me with lowliness and degradation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr defines ihāna this way; al-Qurṭubī gives the person's claim in those terms. Qirāʾāt-Er says the full yāʾ expresses the object 'me'. [4 notes: QURTUBI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]

## 89:16/q17 Does constrained provision show that God humiliates someone?  [فَقَدَرَ رِزْقَهُۥ أَهَٰنَنِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether temporary material conditions disclose God's final judgment, or are tests whose outcome and divine purpose remain to be learned.
- 89:16/q17/p1 The speaker assumes that scarcity is divine humiliation or punishment. — Al-Ṭabarī describes the mistaken inference and the person's overlooked health; Qatāda calls the reaction swift disbelief. Qutb and Asad note the person reads deprivation as punishment or injustice; Ibn ʿĀshūr traces it to denial of the afterlife and assumed permanence of present conditions. [46 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN(أبو العباس); ALUSI; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ZAJJAJ-; RAZI-; DURR(الحسن,مجاهد)-; DURR-FULL(الحسن,مجاهد)-]
- 89:16/q17/p2 No; scarcity is a trial, and withholding extra favor is not humiliation. — Al-Zamakhsharī distinguishes a freely granted favor from an insult and compares a host giving no gift. Abū Ḥayyān cites poor righteous people including Dāwūd al-Iṣfahānī. Al-Rāzī says wealth can expose one to robbery or death, bind one to pleasures, or worsen separation at death; deprivation can increase worship and safety. Honor rests in obedience, faith and final outcome. [107 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURHAN(أبو العباس); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(الحسن,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(الحسن,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI(ابن القيم,بعض المفسرين,بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(God in a ḥadīth qudsī,al-Rāzī); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+; BINTSHATI(الإمام الطبري)+]

## 89:16/q18 What does the following 'No!' reject?  [أَهَٰنَنِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the first statement's 'honor' names a real gift only, or also asserts an unwarranted judgment of the recipient's merit.
- 89:16/q18/p1 Only the claim that reduced provision is humiliation; God did call abundance an honor. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī note that giving a favor is honor while withholding it need not be insult; the text says God honored the rich man but never says He humiliated the poor one. [11 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 89:16/q18/p2 Both claims, because wealth does not prove deserved honor and scarcity does not prove disgrace. — Al-Zamakhsharī gives deserved honor as an explanation, citing the claim in 28:78; al-Ālūsī prefers this account over rejecting humiliation alone. Al-Ḥasan ties real disgrace to failure to honor orphans (89:17). Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses that true divine intent is known by revelation and law, not fortune. [28 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; JISHUMI; NASAFI; DURR(الحسن,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(الحسن,مجاهد); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(ابن القيم); ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+]

## 89:16/q19 What is the proper measure of honor and humiliation?  [رَبِّىٓ أَهَٰنَنِ · theology]
turns on: Whether the standard is present obedience and nearness or final recompense; both displace material wealth.
- 89:16/q19/p1 Honor is obedience or nearness to God; humiliation is disobedience or being forsaken. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Mujāhid and others contrast obedience with sin. Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds faith and good works. The Study Quran cites 49:13 for nobility as reverence. [15 notes: WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI; NASAFI; DURR(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(God in a ḥadīth qudsī); BINTSHATI(الإمام الطبري)+]
- 89:16/q19/p2 True honor and humiliation are finally known in the afterlife. — Some exegetes confine the terms to afterlife status; Muqātil says poverty lightens reckoning. Al-Rāzī argues worldly pleasure ending in damnation is no happiness, and hardship ending in salvation no disgrace. [10 notes: BINTSHATI(بعض المفسرين); MUQATIL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; JISHUMI]

## 89:16/q20 Why does the person voice this claim?  [فَيَقُولُ رَبِّىٓ أَهَٰنَنِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the immediate reaction is explained by distress and forgotten gifts or by a settled worldly creed.
- 89:16/q20/p1 Constricted means cause distress and preoccupation, and he overlooks other gifts. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Kalbī cite healthy limbs; al-Rāzī also names reason, religion and protection. Bursawī contrasts complaint with patient recognition of the test; Elmalılı notes even little provision warrants gratitude. Al-Ṭabarī's Qatāda calls the reaction swift disbelief. [15 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI]
- 89:16/q20/p2 Materialist values and denial of resurrection make fortune seem the measure of worth. — Al-Rāzī cites 30:7 and 22:11, and Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the boastful inference in 41:50. Qutb locates it in Meccan rank and greed; al-Qurṭubī says even some Muslims share it. [17 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 89:16/q21 Is 'he says' literal and why use the imperfect?  [فَيَقُولُ · rhetoric]
- 89:16/q21/p1 It is actual speech voicing belief, repeated whenever the condition recurs. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says people boast when blessed and complain when constrained; 3:75 likewise uses saying for a defended belief. The imperfect verbs convey recurrent, renewed claims. [6 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 89:16/q22 Why does the wording say He restricted provision rather than He humiliated him?  [فَقَدَرَ رِزْقَهُۥ أَهَٰنَنِ · rhetoric]
- 89:16/q22/p1 Restriction itself is not humiliation; many righteous people have little wealth. — Abū Ḥayyān cites Dāwūd al-Iṣfahānī; al-Biqāʿī says the wording preserves proper address of God and His saints. Al-Rāzī notes the first clause says God honored him but this clause does not call deprivation humiliation. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 89:16/q23 Why is the second condition introduced by wāw rather than the first clause's fāʾ?  [وَأَمَّآ · rhetoric]
- 89:16/q23/p1 Mercy and blessings precede wrath and pain, and blessings are more frequent. — Al-Rāzī cites 14:34 and says fāʾ suits the first, more frequent condition. [3 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 89:16/q24 How does this passage connect to its neighbors and the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the verses are read as the sūra's immediate moral continuation or as later inserted speech units.
- 89:16/q24/p1 It contrasts God's watch and final reckoning with human fixation on worldly pleasure. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and al-Bayḍāwī link it to 89:14. Al-Ālūsī compares the anxious human of 70:19–21; Elmalılı and Islahi connect the trial to orphan and poor people's rights and the tranquil soul. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ sees the natural contrasts preparing for the opposed fates. [22 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 89:16/q24/p2 The passage belongs to a later inserted pair echoed by other inserted speech passages. — Corpus Coranicum proposes 15–16 were added, with the akrama/ahāna opposition echoing the failure to honor orphans in 17. It compares the human response to Hell in 23b–24 and says God's address to the tranquil soul supersedes those negative speeches. [3 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 89:16/q25 What are the sūra's title, revelation setting, and verse count?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether long verse length and proposed cross-rhyme justify subdivision.
- 89:16/q25/p1 It is Sūrat al-Fajr, generally Meccan, tenth after al-Layl and before al-Ḍuḥā; verse counts differ. — Ibn ʿĀshūr notes al-Dānī's report of a Medinan opinion and counts of 32 in Medina and Mecca, 30 in Kufa and Syria, and 29 in Basra; 15 and 16 are separate endpoints in the first count. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 89:16/q25/p2 Dividing these verses further is doubtful. — Corpus Coranicum reports Neuwirth's length argument and proposed cross-rhyme like 101:6–9; it calls the length inconclusive given likely insertion, and finds the 69:19/25 comparison strained. The akramanī/ahānanī rhyme is approximate, like 23–24 and 29–30. [3 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 89:16/q26 What other guidance follows from this trial?  [* · interpretation]
- 89:16/q26/p1 Give thanks and share with the vulnerable in prosperity; meet hardship with patience, self-examination and trust. — Elmalılı connects neglect of the afterlife to failure to honor orphans and feed the poor. Islahi calls for care of others; Kur'an Yolu adds organized solidarity and possible self-correction. Qutb says faith supports active trust. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes deprivation may otherwise breed despair and false abandonment. [13 notes: ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI(بعضهم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 89:16/q27 What broader Arabic grammar bears on these forms?  [إِذَا مَا أَهَٰنَنِ · grammar]
turns on: These are distinct background grammatical observations used alongside discussion of particles, readings and ellipsis.
- 89:16/q27/p1 Adverbs and naming follow several gender and inflection patterns. — Sībawayh treats most adverbs as masculine by diminutives and default; he discusses qabla/baʿda, ayna/kayfa/matā, ḥaythu, ammāma, ladun, ʿan, mundhu, and names made from words. He contrasts ḥasb with qaṭ and explains alif-lām's definiteness. [11 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 89:16/q27/p2 A final yāʾ can be kept or dropped at pause according to its grammatical environment. — Sībawayh contrasts final yāʾ with forms whose preceding consonant is still, the connected vocative, and alif which is light and normally remains. Al-Nābigha and al-Aʿshā attest deletion; Labīd's alif loss needs poetic meter. [10 notes: SIBAWAYH(النابغة); FARISI-HUJJA(إسماعيل عن نافع وورش عن نافع)]
- 89:16/q27/p3 Weak radicals follow distinct noun and adjective patterns. — Sībawayh says faʿlā nouns such as ṭūbā and kusā change medial yāʾ to wāw, unlike adjectives, and compares final yāʾ in shirwā/taqwā with adjectival ṣadyā/khazyā; source weak radicals may remain. [3 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 89:16/q27/p4 Alternative questions use aw for whether any alternative obtains and am for which one. — Sībawayh illustrates with persons, actions, fronting, paired predicates, Jarīr and Ḥassān; Ṣafiyya's food/Qurashī question asks which kind of thing, not which food. He also describes parenthetic ʿAmr and occasional equivalence of am and aw. [15 notes: SIBAWAYH(صفية بنت عبد المطلب)]
- 89:16/q27/p5 A conditional sentence joins two full clauses with a condition and response particle. — Al-Rāzī gives a sun-shining/day-existing example and contrasts nominal and verbal sentence primacy: simple noun versus composite verb, countered by nouns' unsuitability as predicates. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 89:16/q27/p6 Optional deletion needs no separate signal if sense and wording remain intact. — Al-Zarkashī gives this general ellipsis rule. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 89:16/q28 What illustrative Arabic rhetoric appears alongside this verse's discussion?  [فَيَقُولُ أَهَٰنَنِ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes offer separate illustrations of Arabic figures and criticism.
- 89:16/q28/p1 An object may name its impression or action through established usage. — Al-Jurjānī discusses rings and their imprints, a whip and its stroke, and hand as power; Abū ʿAlī restores an older elided genitive, while feminine agreement in a poem shows current usage has moved beyond it. The Prophet's 'longest hand' for generosity illustrates the link. [6 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR(الشيخ أبو علي,النبي)]
- 89:16/q28/p2 A poet's praise can fail through weak linkage, stock comparisons, and inverted order. — Al-Bāqillānī criticizes praise above stars, a cloud simile whose withholding does not distinguish cloud from patron, and beginner-like inversion; Ibn ʿAbbād reports Ibn al-ʿAmīd esteemed the praise line. [5 notes: BAQILLANI(إسماعيل بن عباد)]

## 89:16/q29 What rules are given for accepting a Qur'anic reading?  [فَقَدَرَ أَهَٰنَنِ · readings]
- 89:16/q29/p1 An accepted reading needs sound transmission, codex consonants, and eloquent Arabic. — Abū Shāma gives the three criteria. A Shāfiʿī jurist adds mutawātir transmission and communal acceptance, bars readings outside the ten in and out of prayer, and says anomalous readings serve Arabic study. Reading by untransmitted meaning is graver; persistent anomalous recitation should be stopped and disciplined. Al-Qurṭubī invokes Companion agreement on the codex. [6 notes: BURHAN(الشيخ شهاب الدين أبو شامة,شيخ الشافعية); QURTUBI(الباقون)]

## 89:16/q30 What theological issue about divine control is discussed in the surrounding source?  [رَبِّىٓ · theology]
- 89:16/q30/p1 Seeking refuge is real only when one acknowledges that all things are from and through God. — Al-Rāzī raises objections about Satan's creation, power, freedom, regress, unequal burdens and foreknowledge; he cites the Prophet's refuge in God's pleasure from wrath and in God from God. He takes divine words as the effective command kun, connects lofty spirits with 79:5, and describes higher refuge as absorption in divine unity. [13 notes: RAZI-FULL(الرسول صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 89:16/q31 What categories of names are discussed in the surrounding grammar?  [رَبِّىٓ · grammar]
- 89:16/q31/p1 Proper, generic, and derived names differ in reference and information conveyed. — Al-Rāzī disputes theologians who say proper names convey nothing: they identify their bearer without an attribute. He distinguishes genus proper names from generic nouns and reports grammarians' diptote argument for Usāma. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(المتكلمون)]

## 89:16/q32 What examples illustrate how people respond to changing fortune?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether immediate fortune is treated as decisive or changeable.
- 89:16/q32/p1 Bedouins praised the religion in good fortune and disparaged it when misfortune came. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this reaction among Bedouins who came to Medina. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA(الأعراب)]
- 89:16/q32/p2 The Ghassānids hoped for consequences and did not suppose good or evil would last unchanged. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites al-Nābigha's praise of their refusal to infer permanent fortune from present conditions. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(النابغة)]

## 89:16/q33 Why does the passage single out restricted provision?  [فَقَدَرَ رِزْقَهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 89:16/q33/p1 The addressed pagans were generally healthy; scarcity was their salient hardship. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says they chiefly died by killing or old age among themselves or their kin. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 89:16/q34 What does a mistaken judgment of fortune reveal about deeds?  [* · interpretation]
- 89:16/q34/p1 People may think they act well while their deeds and beliefs are misdirected. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 18:103–105. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]

## 89:16/q35 How does the person describe the trial of scarce provision?  [ٱبْتَلَىٰهُ رِزْقَهُۥ أَهَٰنَنِ · interpretation]
- 89:16/q35/p1 He wrongly takes the restriction as proof of divine humiliation. — Ibn Kathīr explicitly calls this inference mistaken. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR]

## 89:16/q36 What other temporal pattern is attached to hardship and ease?  [* · links]
- 89:16/q36/p1 Hardship is followed by relief, and ease is later followed by difficulty. — Muqātil explains the repeated promise of 94:5–6 in these terms. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
