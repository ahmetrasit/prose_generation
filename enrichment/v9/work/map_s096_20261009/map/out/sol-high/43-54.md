# 43:54: 15 questions

## 43:54/q01 What did Pharaoh do when he 'made light of' his people?  [فَٱسْتَخَفَّ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb describes provoking quick action, producing foolish judgment, finding prior weakness, making people pliant, or compelling them; al-Wahidi disputes the omitted step needed for the 'found foolish' reading.
- 43:54/q01/p1 He stirred, provoked, or urged them into a quick response. — Al-Farra and Ibn Ziyad explain verbal agitation; al-Farra in al-Mawardi adds inducements. Ibn Isa says he called them to falsehood and they hastened. Muqatil and al-Wahidi connect the rousing to Pharaoh's deceptive claim in 40:29. Al-Wahidi cites 30:60 for being unsettled or driven into ignorance. Al-Qurtubi cites the ordinary usage of joy unsettling someone and 30:60. Al-Alusi says lightness metaphorically means speed in answering. Ibn Ashur compares an object made light after being heavy: reflection and hesitation gave way to swift compliance. Al-Raghib allows making them hasten. The Study Quran reports submission as a possible sense; Kuranyolu lists hastening among the verb's senses. [22 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زياد,ابن عيسى,الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل والفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 43:54/q01/p2 He deceived them into foolish, frivolous judgment. — Al-Nahhas says he made his claim seem light, leading to disbelief in Moses. Al-Kalbi in al-Mawardi says their obedience exposed their ignorance. Al-Wahidi says deception and the claim in 40:29 unsettled them and induced ignorance; 30:60 uses the verb for being driven into ignorance. Al-Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, and al-Qamus mention departure from sound judgment. Al-Tabrisi says they accepted a false kingship argument, although property proves no truth and miracles suffice for messengers without angels. Al-Biqa'i calls Pharaoh's speech beguiling and self-degrading. Bursevi says their judgment, not bodily speed, was light. Ibn Ashur says they had inwardly credited Moses after his signs, then accepted Pharaoh's claims. Islahi calls this luring simpletons; Kuranyolu lists deception and exploitation of ignorance; the Study Quran gives becoming foolish as an alternative. [22 notes: NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل والفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(غيره); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الأعرابي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(القاموس); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 43:54/q01/p3 He found them already foolish or mentally weak. — Al-Baghawi says Pharaoh found the Egyptians ignorant. Ibn al-A'rabi, reported by Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi, says their understanding was light or deficient; al-Alusi treats the verbal form as finding and the attribution to the people as figurative. Al-Qurtubi says this sense alone requires an omitted call to error before obedience follows; al-Wahidi rejects the required omission as impermissible. Al-Kashifi says Pharaoh found their minds light through this successful trick. Al-Raghib also allows finding them light in body and resolve; Bursevi allows finding them credulous. [7 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الأعرابي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن الأعرابي); BURSEVI(الراغب,الكاشفي); WAHIDI-BASIT-]
- 43:54/q01/p4 He made them pliant and easy to lead. — Ibn Atiyya says Pharaoh bent them to his aim. Abu Hayyan explicitly gives pliancy as another view. Al-Nasafi also reports that Pharaoh made his words effective among them or requested ready obedience; Kuranyolu lists directing them as he wished. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 43:54/q01/p5 He overpowered or humiliated them into following. — Al-Qurtubi cites the usage of استخف به for insulting someone. Kuranyolu includes belittling people for weakness among possible senses. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 43:54/q02 How does the form of استخف work here?  [فَٱسْتَخَفَّ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the added letters intensify an action upon the people or mark finding their prior quality.
- 43:54/q02/p1 The added sīn and tāʾ intensify أخف. — Ibn Ashur compares the verse on Satan causing a slip (3:155) and an Arabic expression for provoking a sensible person's anger. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 43:54/q02/p2 The form can mean finding a quality already present. — Al-Alusi, reporting Ibn al-A'rabi, compares ahmadtu: Pharaoh found their intellect deficient; attributing the lightness to the people is figurative. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن الأعرابي)]

## 43:54/q03 Who are Pharaoh's 'people' here?  [قَوْمَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether qawm denotes the Egyptian people broadly or only the elite summoned in Pharaoh's company.
- 43:54/q03/p1 They are Pharaoh's Egyptian or Coptic people. — Muqatil identifies them as Copts; al-Wahidi al-Wajiz likewise says Coptic people, and al-Baghawi calls them Egyptians. Al-Biqa'i describes the Egyptians' pride and forceful resolve when worldly authority did not confront them. [4 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 43:54/q03/p2 They are the courtiers present at Pharaoh's summons. — Ibn Ashur restricts the phrase to the elite in his company; he says his address quickly swayed them despite their readiness to follow Moses on seeing the signs. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:54/q04 What did their obedience to Pharaoh consist of?  [فَأَطَاعُوهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether obedience is described generally, as denial of Moses and affirmation of Pharaoh, or as a reported act in the pursuit.
- 43:54/q04/p1 They accepted Pharaoh's call to reject Moses and his message. — Muqatil calls it obedience in denial; Ibn Ziyad in al-Mawardi says Pharaoh's speech moved them to deny Moses. Ibn Abbas and Muqatil in al-Wahidi say they accepted Pharaoh's claim and rejected Moses. Al-Qurtubi describes acceptance of disbelief. Ibn Atiyya calls it disbelief. Ibn Ashur says the courtiers had been ready to follow Moses after his signs before Pharaoh swayed them. [9 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زياد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 43:54/q04/p2 They obeyed Pharaoh's orders and acknowledged his authority. — Al-Baydawi and al-Alusi say they did what he commanded. Al-Biqa'i specifies recognition of his kingship and grandeur and affirmation of his lordship, alongside rejection of Moses' command. Al-Tabrisi says they obeyed an invalid claim based on kingship. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABRISI]
- 43:54/q04/p3 They complied in pursuing Moses under Pharaoh's age restriction. — Ikrimah, reported in al-Durr, says Pharaoh let out nobody older than forty or younger than twenty; the report exemplifies his control of those pursuing Moses. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(عكرمة)]

## 43:54/q05 Why did they obey Pharaoh so readily?  [فَأَطَاعُوهُ فَٰسِقِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes offer moral corruption, ignorance, fear, worldly authority, and the audience's recent wavering as explanations.
- 43:54/q05/p1 Their own wickedness and departure from God's obedience made them receptive. — Ibn Atiyya cites sinfulness and corruption; al-Baydawi and Abu Hayyan cite wickedness; al-Nasafi says they had left God's religion. Al-Biqa'i says Moses' signs had surpassed bracelets and angels, leaving only affinity in evil character; he calls their defiance entrenched. Al-Alusi says their transgression explains following a transgressing leader. Bursevi says they were outside God's obedience and sound reason. Ibn Ashur says إنهم كانوا قوما فاسقين grammatically supplies the cause. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 43:54/q05/p2 Their ignorance and weak judgment left them easy to deceive. — Al-Kalbi in al-Mawardi says their obedience displayed ignorance; Ibn al-A'rabi in al-Qurtubi says their minds were light and reason limited. Bursevi calls them profoundly ignorant and astray. Al-Tabatabai connects foolish judgment to obedience; Islahi says Pharaoh lured simpletons. Kuranyolu says he exploited ignorance and spectacle to preserve power. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الأعرابي); BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 43:54/q05/p3 Fear of Pharaoh moved them to comply. — Al-Qushayri says fear produced their obedience but made it insincere; Bursevi's inward reading says obedience to the commanding self begins in fear before becoming willing. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 43:54/q05/p4 Worldly rank, wealth, and accustomed leadership swayed them. — Al-Biqa'i says worldly leadership commands weakly attached people, while they resist an unfamiliar leader despite great deeds; he says Egyptians yielded when faced with the worldly means they were used to obeying. Bursevi says they trusted transient wealth and rank and looked down on Moses. Al-Tabrisi rejects the premise that property proves truth or that a messenger needs angels when miracles exist. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABRISI]
- 43:54/q05/p5 Their recent unbelief and wavering after Moses' signs made a slight pretext enough. — Ibn Ashur says they had deified Pharaoh, then wavered after Moses came, before returning to him at the slightest pretext. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:54/q06 What does فاسقين mean for these people?  [فَٰسِقِينَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether fāsiqīn is stated as general disobedience, specifically unbelief, or a spiritual condition.
- 43:54/q06/p1 They were disobedient sinners who had departed from God's obedience. — Muqatil calls them disobedient; al-Wahidi applies disobedience to the people Moses had led. Al-Tabrisi, al-Qurtubi, and Bursevi say they were outside obedience to God. Al-Biqa'i says this departure had become second nature. Al-Tabatabai calls them transgressors and sinners. Al-Wahidi al-Wajiz calls them sinful. Bursevi adds that they had completely withdrawn from Moses' way. [10 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]
- 43:54/q06/p2 It means unbelievers here. — Ibn Ashur adduces 7:145, where the Qur'an describes these people in connection with the abode of the wicked; he says their unbelief had become manifest after Moses called them away from idolatry. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 43:54/q06/p3 Their disobedience made them spiritually weightless. — Islahi says faith and a relation to God give a person weight; their lack of faith left them insignificant and easy for Satan to lead. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 43:54/q07 What is the function of إنهم كانوا قوما فاسقين?  [إِنَّهُمْ كَانُوا۟ قَوْمًۭا فَٰسِقِينَ · grammar]
- 43:54/q07/p1 It gives the cause of their obedience; إن is not emphatic here. — Ibn Ashur says their unbelief was already evident once Moses had called them away from idolatry, so emphasis is unnecessary. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:54/q08 What does the verse suggest about obedience under fear or force?  [فَأَطَاعُوهُ · interpretation]
- 43:54/q08/p1 Fearful or compelled compliance is not sincere obedience and cannot be trusted. — Al-Qushayri says genuine obedience comes from willingness, whereas Pharaoh's people acted from fear. Bursevi warns that a compelled enemy would cut a hand rather than kiss it when given an opening. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI]

## 43:54/q09 How is Pharaoh's domination read inwardly?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the verse is applied to the commanding self's rule within a person or to purification from Pharaoh-like traits.
- 43:54/q09/p1 The commanding self dominates heart and spirit through desire and violations of religious law. — Al-Ta'wilat al-Najmiyya, reported by Bursevi, says the self dominates the heart, spirit, and their attributes; obedience starts in fear and becomes willing as they absorb its traits. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 43:54/q09/p2 One should purify the base traits seen in Pharaoh and Satan and seek divine guidance. — Bursevi reflects on what made Satan and Pharaoh what they became, urging purification before the return to God and praying for God's care and guidance. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 43:54/q10 What did Ikrimah report about Pharaoh's pursuit of Moses?  [* · reports]
- 43:54/q10/p1 He let out no pursuer above forty or below twenty. — Al-Durr reports Ikrimah's statement as an example of Pharaoh making his people comply in the pursuit. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(عكرمة)]

## 43:54/q11 What did Abu al-Darda say about Pharaoh's worldly provision?  [* · reports]
- 43:54/q11/p1 Had the world weighed even a gnat's wing to God, Pharaoh would not have received a drink. — Al-Tha'labi reports Abu al-Darda's saying. [1 notes: THALABI(أبو الدرداء)]

## 43:54/q12 What political lesson is drawn from Pharaoh's sway over his people?  [* · interpretation]
- 43:54/q12/p1 A ruler can use ignorance, spectacle, and worldly prestige to secure obedience and preserve power. — Kuranyolu says totalitarian rulers fear an informed, truth-seeking public able to claim rights, so Pharaoh exploited ignorance and spectacle. Al-Biqa'i says recognized worldly leadership wins allegiance even when its speech degrades its own case, whereas an unfamiliar leader meets resistance despite great deeds. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL]

## 43:54/q13 How does the Pharaoh account serve this surah's message?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the passage is tied to the Prophet's opponents and their fate, the shared call to monotheism, or the surah's overall unity.
- 43:54/q13/p1 It parallels the idolaters' opposition to the Prophet and warns of their fate. — The Study Quran compares the idolaters and Pharaoh's notables. Islahi says Pharaoh and his chiefs rejected Moses' successive signs for the same arrogance as Quraysh and met the fate Quraysh will face; he says Moses and Jesus called to monotheism. Kuranyolu says Moses' confrontation supplies lessons within the Prophet's struggle for tawhid. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 43:54/q13/p2 It illustrates the shared prophetic call to worship God alone. — Kuranyolu says scripture and its remnants show God never permitted worship of another being; Moses' struggle illustrates the same tawhid taught by every prophet. Islahi likewise invokes Moses and Jesus' monotheistic mission. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 43:54/q13/p3 It fits the surah's single, coherent central theme. — Islahi says every part of Sūrah al-Zukhruf relates deeply to its central theme and the surah is harmonious from beginning to end. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 43:54/q14 What does the account show about unbelievers' response to hardship?  [* · interpretation]
- 43:54/q14/p1 Hardship revives a buried belief in God, but they return to denial when it passes. — Kuranyolu says worldly incentives and suggestions bury unbelievers' deep belief; disasters sent in mercy revive it temporarily. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 43:54/q15 What further image is invoked for Pharaoh's vanished sovereignty?  [* · interpretation]
- 43:54/q15/p1 A poet's line stresses that Pharaoh's sovereignty has vanished. — Kuranyolu invokes the poet but the note does not quote the line. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(şair)]
