# 55:8: 13 questions

## 55:8/q01 How is أَلَّا تَطْغَوْا۟ construed?  [أَلَّا تَطْغَوْا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether an gives purpose to God's setting the balance or explains a command implicit in that act; the following commands favor a prohibition for some, while others require a preceding speech sense for explanatory an.
- 55:8/q01/p1 It is a purpose clause: 'so that/lest you transgress,' with negative lā and a subjunctive verb governed by an, often after an understood purpose lām. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Naḥḥās, al-Farrāʾ, al-Zamakhsharī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī and al-Burhān transmit this possibility; al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī describe an accusative clause after an omitted preposition. Al-Samīn prefers it and links the omitted lām to 'He set the balance'; al-Būrsawī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr give the same connection. Al-Burhān compares subjunctives after fāʾ and lām. The Study Quran's translation uses a subjunctive. Al-Ālūsī rejects the claim that establishing a balance to prevent transgression is incoherent. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ objects that this reading strains the coordination of commands with a statement. [17 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; SAMIN-DURR+; TABATABAI-]
- 55:8/q01/p2 An is explanatory and lā is prohibitive, giving an independent command with a jussive verb: 'Do not transgress.' — Al-Farrāʾ prefers the prohibition because an imperative follows; al-Zajjāj, al-Naḥḥās and al-Wāḥidī invoke the following prohibition or command in 55:9. Al-Zajjāj relates the explanation to God's setting the balance in 55:7. Al-Rāzī says prescribing justice can itself mean 'do not transgress.' Ibn ʿĀshūr says instituting the balance implies a command to justice. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ coordinates 'establish' with this command and takes the ensuing 'do not diminish' as reinforcement. Abū Ḥayyān and the Shaykh object that explanatory an needs a preceding expression with the meaning of speech; al-Samīn adds that an unspoken qawl cannot be explained by an. Makkī and Abū al-Baqāʾ adopt the analysis; Abū al-Baqāʾ supplies implied 'that is' and a saying. A response in al-Ālūsī finds speech in revelation and the messengers' instruction. The Study Quran notes an imperative translation, and its cited Aj distinguishes its warning about withholding another's due from the subjunctive warning about tyranny and oppression, citing 11:84 and 83:1–3. [22 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري وابن عطية,مكي); BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية، الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Aj); FARRA+; NAHHAS+; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري وابن عطية)-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-]

## 55:8/q02 What variant reading changes the wording of this prohibition?  [أَلَّا تَطْغَوْا۟ · readings]
- 55:8/q02/p1 ʿAbd Allāh/Ibn Masʿūd reads لا تطغوا without أن, as a direct prohibition. — Al-Farrāʾ and Ibn ʿAṭiyya attribute this to ʿAbd Allāh or his codex. Al-Zamakhsharī takes a saying as understood; al-Ālūsī likewise finds an implied saying and identifies the verb as jussive. Al-Bayḍāwī calls it quoted speech. [5 notes: FARRA; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مصحف ابن مسعود); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله)]

## 55:8/q03 How does Ibrāhīm's reading of the preceding 'setting the balance' affect this clause?  [أَلَّا تَطْغَوْا۟ · readings]
- 55:8/q03/p1 Ibrāhīm reads the preceding وضع as a verbal noun annexed to الميزان, whose relation to أن لا تطغوا depends on its case. — Al-Ālūsī reports a vowelless ḍād in وضع and genitive الميزان. If وضع is nominative, it is apparently a subject with this clause as predicate after an omitted preposition; if accusative, its governor is omitted or supplied. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(إبراهيم)]

## 55:8/q04 What does ṭughyān or transgressing mean here?  [تَطْغَوْا۟ · meaning]
turns on: The sense of ṭughyān follows whether the balance is justice, a weighing device, or judgment.
- 55:8/q04/p1 It is going beyond the proper limit of justice or right, thus committing injustice or oppression. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Bayḍāwī, Ibn al-Shaykh as cited by al-Būrsawī, and others gloss it as excess, inclination, oppression or exceeding fairness. Al-Dāmghānī lists injustice, disbelief and lying among meanings of ṭughyān. Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies deliberate rejection of right and contempt for its holders when justice is at issue. Al-Qāshānī speaks of exceeding moderation. Al-Ṭabrisī's al-Zajjāj says it passes from truth and fairness into fraud or falsehood. [17 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
- 55:8/q04/p2 With a physical balance or measure, it is cheating, short measure, or unfair excess in weighing or trade. — Muqātil, Abū ʿUbayda, Ibn Qutayba and al-Ṭabarī connect the prohibition to cheating or shortchanging. Al-Māwardī transmits Muqātil's conditional explanation; al-Qurṭubī adds Ibn ʿAbbās's customer being weighed for. Al-Būrsawī urges truthful trade in a Persian gloss, while Ibn al-Shaykh defines ṭughyān as exceeding a limit. Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts short measure with deliberate rejection of justice. The Study Quran's Aj cites 11:84 and 83:1–3 when explaining withholding dues. [14 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
- 55:8/q04/p3 With the balance as judgment, it is distorting a judgment. — Al-Māwardī gives this conditional explanation; al-Qurṭubī repeats it alongside the justice and physical-scale senses. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 55:8/q05 What does الميزان denote in this command?  [ٱلْمِيزَانِ · referent]
turns on: Whether balance names a material device or the action of weighing, justice or judgment, or the Qur'an and religious order; several authors intentionally combine senses.
- 55:8/q05/p1 It is a physical scale or measuring instrument used for quantities and fair transactions. — Muqātil connects the balance with measures. Al-Māwardī reports Muqātil's weighing-goods interpretation; al-Zamakhsharī extends it to every instrument for weighing or measuring quantities, used in transactions and giving and taking. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says the ordinary scale is most likely here because the following injunctions require fair weight. Al-Rāzī says the repeated noun supports weighing and includes exchanges and like-for-like sales. [10 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 55:8/q05/p2 It is justice or a standard of right, encompassing due order and equity. — Al-Māwardī reports Mujāhid's justice reading; al-Ṭabrisī reports al-Zajjāj's. Al-Rāzī says 'setting the balance' can mean prescribing justice. Al-Biqāʿī extends justice to both cosmic realms; the Qur'an measures knowledge and a scale measures sensible things. Asad calls it a standard of right. Kuran Yolu describes divinely given religious and ethical order, with justice putting things in place, rejecting false divinity and giving rights-holders their due. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes both justice and devices together, and sees convergent word senses. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ permits the earlier balance to be any truth or justice standard. [13 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 55:8/q05/p3 It is weighing itself, with the instrument's noun standing for the act. — Al-Rāzī prefers weighing or justice for this second occurrence, compares using 'trust' for trusting and 'appointed time' for promising, and says repetition and the surrounding phrase support weighing. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 55:8/q05/p4 It is judgment, whose transgression is distortion. — Al-Māwardī supplies this third conditional sense; al-Qurṭubī repeats it. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 55:8/q05/p5 It can be the Qur'an as the foundation and measure of religion. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports this alternative and says the Qur'an joins rational with transmitted evidence; al-Biqāʿī likewise calls the Qur'an a balance for knowledge beside the material scale. [2 notes: TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 55:8/q06 How broad is the conduct governed by the command?  [تَطْغَوْا۟ ٱلْمِيزَانِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the balance is applied to commercial measures alone or also to moral, religious and inward order; these scopes are compatible in sources that combine senses.
- 55:8/q06/p1 It governs fair dealings and giving other people their due in measures, weights, and transactions. — Qatāda urges doing for others what one wants from them, since justice sustains society; al-Būrsawī repeats this in measures. Ibn ʿAbbās warns of the ruin caused by measures and weights and corrects a man overweighing, citing 55:9. Al-Zamakhsharī applies instruments to judgments and transactions. Al-Rāzī says the wording includes both weighing for a customer and a seller's taking excess for himself in exchange or like-for-like sales. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says shortchanging can harm buyer or seller. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(قتادة); TABATABAI]
- 55:8/q06/p2 It also governs justice in worship, knowledge, action, spiritual states, rights, and the match between inner and outer life. — Al-Qushayrī includes rights owed to God and people, sincerity in deeds, truthfulness in states, and no deceit or hidden shirk inwardly. Al-Qāshānī applies the virtuous mean to all matters and faculties. Al-Biqāʿī includes commodities, knowledge, action, outward and inward life, and word and deed. Elmalılı extends it to Sharia, material and spiritual weighing, God's rulings and rights. Kuran Yolu connects it to humane religious law and giving each rights-holder their due. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 55:8/q07 Does the prohibition cover unavoidable measurement error?  [تَطْغَوْا۟ ٱلْمِيزَانِ · law]
- 55:8/q07/p1 No; it targets deliberate excess, while humanly unavoidable imprecision is excused. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān make the same distinction between deliberate overstepping and inescapable error. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 55:8/q08 Why repeat الميزان in this passage?  [ٱلْمِيزَانِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the repetition chiefly specifies the prohibition, emphasizes justice, or carries distinct and layered senses.
- 55:8/q08/p1 Repetition states explicitly the object whose transgression is prohibited. — Al-Ṭabrisī gives this explanation while reporting the Qur'an sense of balance. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 55:8/q08/p2 Repetition underlines justice as fundamental to creation and human transactions. — The Study Quran explicitly connects three repetitions with this emphasis. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 55:8/q08/p3 The repeated noun permits distinct or overlapping senses in its occurrences. — A view reported by al-Rāzī makes the first balance justice and the second the instrument; he prefers the reverse, the first instrument and the second weighing or justice. He also says repetition supports the weighing sense here. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the noun and surrounding verbs, prepositions and qisṭ converge on both justice and devices; he calls this double meaning a feature of Qur'anic inimitability. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]

## 55:8/q09 Why name the balance and say 'in the balance' here?  [فِى ٱلْمِيزَانِ · rhetoric]
turns on: The first explanation concerns the chosen noun and the second the scope conveyed by the preposition; the notes do not set them against each other.
- 55:8/q09/p1 Naming the instrument extends the ban to unfair weighing on either side of a transaction. — Al-Rāzī says 'in weighing' might be heard as only weighing for a customer, whereas the instrument noun also stops a seller taking excess for himself, especially in exchange and like-for-like sales. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 55:8/q09/p2 The metaphorical 'in' bars even a slight transgression involving the balance. — Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts such partial involvement with destroying the entire balance and cites 4:5 and a verse by Ṣabra for partial use of something. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 55:8/q10 How does this balance relate to the balance set in the preceding verse?  [ٱلْمِيزَانِ · links]
turns on: Whether the repeated balance shifts from justice to instrument, instrument to justice or weighing, general standard to specific practice, or deliberately carries both.
- 55:8/q10/p1 The preceding balance is justice prescribed by God, and this balance is the material instrument people must use fairly. — Al-Rāzī reports this assignment as a view he does not prefer; it distinguishes a divine prescription from abuse of the instrument. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL-]
- 55:8/q10/p2 The preceding balance is the instrument God set up, while this balance is weighing or justice that gives each person their due. — Al-Rāzī prefers this reversal; his examples of a noun standing for a verbal noun explain how the second can mean weighing. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 55:8/q10/p3 The preceding balance can be a general standard of truth or justice, whose particular consequence here is fair weighing. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ expressly describes this general-to-specific relation, while preferring the ordinary scale in verse 8. [2 notes: TABATABAI]
- 55:8/q10/p4 Both occurrences can carry justice and the physical balance together. — Ibn ʿĀshūr bases the double sense on the noun and its surrounding verbs, prepositions and qisṭ; al-Biqāʿī similarly places divine justice across cosmic realms and distinguishes the Qur'an's intellectual balance from the scale's sensible one. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 55:8/q11 How does the surrounding passage develop this command?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes trace different connections: creation and cosmic order, the next weighing command, or the audience established before the refrain.
- 55:8/q11/p1 The raised sky and created order lead into the balance and a human duty to maintain justice. — Muqātil places it after prostration of stemless plants and trees through moving shadows and the sky raised 500 years above earth. Al-Burhān describes the balance between the paired sky and earth in the sūra's paired structure, citing 55:3–7, 10, 14–15, 17 and 19. Abū Ḥayyān moves from the Qur'an as highest knowledge to a balance ordering affairs, citing the book and balance in 57:25. Iṣlāḥī says the first signs show God's love of justice and rejection of violations; the cosmic balance reveals His character, and transgression brings disorder and punishment here and hereafter. The Study Quran reports, without grading, the Prophet's saying that heavens and earth are founded on justice. [6 notes: MUQATIL; BURHAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]
- 55:8/q11/p2 The following command to establish fair weight and prohibition of loss clarify or reinforce this prohibition. — Al-Zajjāj appeals to 55:9 in favor of a prohibitive construction; Ibn ʿAbbās cites 55:9 while correcting a man at the scale. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ coordinates the command to establish weight and treats the final prohibition as reinforcement. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI]
- 55:8/q11/p3 The address to accountable humans and jinn fits the sūra's context before its refrain. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī points to the prior teaching of the Qur'an and command about justice in weighing to explain why both audiences are addressed. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 55:8/q12 What reported incidents or sayings illustrate this injunction?  [* · reports]
turns on: These are different reported warnings, actions, exhortations and sayings rather than competing versions of one event.
- 55:8/q12/p1 Ibn ʿAbbās warned the mawālī that failures in measures and weights ruined earlier peoples. — Al-Ṭabarī transmits the warning; al-Māwardī repeats it while explaining short measure. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل)]
- 55:8/q12/p2 Ibn ʿAbbās saw a man overweigh, told him to straighten the balance, and cited the next verse. — Al-Ṭabarī reports the encounter and the citation of 55:9. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 55:8/q12/p3 Qatāda urged reciprocal fairness in dealings and fulfilment of dues because justice sustains society. — Al-Ṭabarī reports his counsel; al-Būrsawī repeats it with specific reference to fulfilling measures as one wishes others to do. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(قتادة)]
- 55:8/q12/p4 The Prophet is reported to have said that the heavens and earth are founded on justice. — The Study Quran gives this report without a grading. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]

## 55:8/q13 How should Qur'anic eloquence and alleged imitations be assessed?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether transmitted-era Arabic eloquence tests Qur'anic expression or the Qur'an supplies the standard, and how much attention alleged imitations merit.
- 55:8/q13/p1 The Qur'an itself should be the sound standard for Arabic usage, and feeble imitations by later pretenders deserve little standing. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ disputes al-Khaṭṭābī's test against eloquence transmitted in the compilation era. She still values his early rhetorical and critical discussion but says his attention to later prophetic pretenders' imitations gives them undeserved standing. [2 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 55:8/q13/p2 Qur'anic expressions can be defended against claims of departure from accepted eloquent Arabic by examining poetic conventions and alleged imitations. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports al-Khaṭṭābī's method and his treatment of Musaylima's poor efforts; she objects to its standard and to giving such imitations attention, while valuing his critical discussion. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الخطابي); BINTSHATI-IJAZ-]
