[QURTUBI:1:1/r77] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya rejects the inference that Gabriel never brought al-Fātiḥa; the report about another angel may mean Gabriel announced him.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r78] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | prefers | Qurṭubī says another angel brought the gifts while Gabriel brought Qurʾānic recitation in Mecca. | mentions 26:193
[QURTUBI:1:1/r79] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الثعلبي | reports | Some say al-Fātiḥa is both Meccan and Medinan because Gabriel brought it twice; Qurṭubī prefers his reconciliation.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r8] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Scholars agree seeking refuge is not Qurʾān; the majority use the Qurʾānic formula.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r80] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Since the basmala is not an al-Fātiḥa verse on the preferred view, prayer moves directly from takbīr to al-Fātiḥa without pause or opening supplication.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r81] QURTUBI, d. 671 · عمر وابن مسعود | reports | ʿUmar and Ibn Masʿūd opened prayer with “Glory and praise to You, O God”; Sufyān, Aḥmad, Isḥāq, and Ḥanafīs followed.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r82] QURTUBI, d. 671 · علي | reports | Al-Shāfiʿī preferred ʿAlī’s report that the Prophet said “I have turned my face” after the opening takbīr; Muslim records it.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r83] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن المنذر | reports | Ibn al-Mundhir confirms a brief pause and the Prophet’s supplication asking distance from sins and purification; Abū Hurayra also used it.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r84] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبو سلمة بن عبد الرحمن | reports | Abū Salama says an imam has two pauses that worshippers should use for recitation; al-Awzāʿī and Aḥmad favored the report.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r85] QURTUBI, d. 671 · مالك | reports | Mālik requires the imam and individual worshipper to recite al-Fātiḥa in every prayer unit.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r86] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن خويز منداد | reports | Ibn Khuwayz Mundād says Mālik invalidates a two-unit prayer if al-Fātiḥa is omitted in one; longer prayers have reports of repeating or prostrating for forgetfulness.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r87] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عبد البر وابن القاسم | prefers | Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr and Ibn al-Qāsim prefer cancelling the missed unit and replacing it.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r88] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الحسن البصري والمغيرة بن عبد الرحمن | reports | Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, most Basrans, and al-Mughīra hold one recitation of al-Fātiḥa anywhere in prayer suffices.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r89] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبو حنيفة والثوري والأوزاعي | reports | Abū Ḥanīfa, al-Thawrī, and al-Awzāʿī allow another passage to suffice if al-Fātiḥa is deliberately omitted.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r9] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masʿūd reports adding “the All-Hearing, All-Knowing”; the Prophet says Gabriel taught the shorter formula.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r90] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبو يوسف ومحمد بن الحسن | reports | Abū Yūsuf requires three verses or one long verse; Muḥammad ibn al-Ḥasan also permits one verse or a meaningful phrase.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r91] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الطبري | reports | Al-Ṭabarī requires al-Fātiḥa each unit but allows an equivalent passage; Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr rejects substitution.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r92] QURTUBI, d. 671 · العلماء | holds | By consensus, an imam’s recitation covers a latecomer who catches him bowing; one who catches him standing recites.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r93] QURTUBI, d. 671 · مالك وأصحابه | reports | In quiet prayer, Mālik’s school says one should not omit recitation behind the imam; omission is improper but does not invalidate prayer.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r94] QURTUBI, d. 671 · مالك | reports | Mālik’s well-known view bars the follower from reciting al-Fātiḥa or another passage when the imam recites aloud.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r95] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الشافعي وأحمد بن حنبل | reports | Al-Shāfiʿī’s Egyptian view and Aḥmad require every follower to recite al-Fātiḥa each unit, whether the imam recites aloud or quietly.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r96] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الشافعي | reports | Al-Shāfiʿī first held that a follower reads when the imam is quiet but not loud; in Egypt he gave two views for loud recitation.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r97] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن وهب وأشهب وابن عبد الحكم وابن حبيب | reports | Ibn Wahb, Ashhab, Ibn ʿAbd al-Ḥakam, Ibn Ḥabīb, and Kūfans say the follower recites nothing, relying on the imam’s recitation counting for him.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r98] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | prefers | Qurṭubī prefers al-Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad, and Mālik’s alternate view: al-Fātiḥa is required from everyone in every unit.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r99] QURTUBI, d. 671 · عمر وابن عباس وأبو هريرة وأبي بن كعب | reports | Qurṭubī cites Companions who require al-Fātiḥa in every unit.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r1] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | Al-Fatiha is Umm al-Qur'an because it opens the Qur'an and is like its root and origin, hence al-Asās.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r10] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · قراء مكة والكوفة وفقهاؤهما وابن المبارك والشافعي | reports | Meccan and Kufan reciters and jurists, Ibn al-Mubārak, and al-Shāfiʿī hold that the basmala is part of al-Fatiha and every sūra.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r11] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · قراء المدينة والبصرة والشام وفقهاؤها ومالك والأوزاعي | reports | The reciters and jurists of Medina, Basra, and Syria, Mālik, and al-Awzāʿī reject its being part of every sūra.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r12] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · أبو حنيفة | reports | Abū Ḥanīfa gave no explicit ruling here; Bayḍāwī infers that he considered the basmala outside the sūra.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r13] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · محمد بن الحسن | reports | Muḥammad ibn al-Ḥasan said that everything between the two covers is God's speech.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r14] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · النبي | reports | Abū Hurayra reports the Prophet saying al-Fatiha has seven verses and its first is the basmala.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r15] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · النبي | reports | Umm Salama reports the Prophet counted the basmala and “Praise belongs to God, Lord of the worlds” as one verse.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r16] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | The reports explain the disagreement whether the basmala is a separate verse or part of the following verse.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r17] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | There is consensus that the muṣḥaf text is God's speech and agreement to include the basmala in muṣḥafs despite rigorous exclusion of non-Qur'anic matter.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r18] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | prefers | The bāʾ attaches to omitted “I read,” since what follows is recited; this is better than “I begin,” unsupported by context, or “my beginning,” requiring extra ellipsis.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r19] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | Fronting the phrase emphasizes exclusivity and reverence, accords with God's name preceding recitation, and makes it the instrument of an act not ritually complete without it. | mentions 11:41, 1:5
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r2] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | It is also Umm al-Qur'an because it gathers praise, worship of commands and prohibitions, promise and threat, theoretical wisdom, practical guidance, and knowledge of the blessed and wretched.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r20] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · النبي | reports | The Prophet is quoted saying every important matter not begun with the basmala is deficient.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r21] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | reports | Alternatively, bāʾ denotes accompaniment: “I read while blessed by God's name.”
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r22] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | The basmala and rest of the sūra are put on servants' tongues to teach invocation for blessing, praise of God's favors, and asking His bounty.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r23] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | The bāʾ is kasra as a bound genitive preposition; command and possessive lām are likewise kasra before nouns to distinguish them from lām of inception.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r24] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · أصحابنا البصريون | reports | Basran grammarians derive ism from a form whose ending was dropped for frequent use; hamzat al-waṣl allows its consonant-initial form to begin a word.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r25] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | Forms asmāʾ, asāmī, samā, and samīt support this derivation; poetry attests the form samy.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r26] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | prefers | Bayḍāwī rejects metathesis as irregular and derives ism from sumūw, elevation, since a name raises and marks its referent.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r27] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · الكوفيون | reports | The Kufans derive ism from sima, “mark”: its original was wasm, with initial wāw deleted and hamzat al-waṣl added to reduce irregularity.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r28] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | rejects | Bayḍāwī objects that Arabic has no precedent for hamza entering a word after its first letter has been deleted.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r29] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | Sam and sim are also forms; a poem attests sam in the phrase “in the name of the one in every sūra.”
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r3] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | Its other names include al-Kanz, al-Wāfiya, al-Kāfiya, al-Ḥamd, al-Shukr, al-Duʿāʾ, Taʿlīm al-Masʾala, and al-Ṣalāt; the latter names reflect its supplication and prayer recitation.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r30] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | If ism means the spoken word, it differs from its referent: transient sounds vary across peoples and eras and may be multiple or singular, unlike the named entity.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r31] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | If ism means the entity itself, it is the named thing, though this sense is not established in common usage.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r32] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | In Qur'anic commands to glorify God's name, “name” means the word: as God's essence and attributes must be kept from defects, so must their names from obscenity and discourtesy. | mentions 55:78, 87:1
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r33] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | reports | Alternatively, “name” is an insertion without independent meaning, as in the poet's line “then the name of peace be upon you both.”
