[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماشذمنالمعتلعلىالأصل)#805/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Arabic has sound and weak patterns, and the regular weak forms follow the same principles as corresponding sound-root forms described earlier.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماشذمنالمعتلعلىالأصل)#805/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Some patterns are scarce or omitted because Arabic speakers avoid accumulating heavy gemination; forms of ردد may be used or dropped for that reason.

[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماشذمنالمعتلعلىالأصل)#805/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Other sparse forms include ددن and يديت.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماشذمنالمعتلعلىالأصل)#805/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A pattern may be left unbuilt even where a similar form exists elsewhere: رشاء lacks a plural on فعل, and the corresponding weak pattern is omitted too.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماشذمنالمعتلعلىالأصل)#805/r8] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Nouns may occur on patterns omitted from verbs; Sībawayh says this and the other departures from the root pattern have been discussed above.
