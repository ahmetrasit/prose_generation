LUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī allows that they may wish and ask to return to worldly life; naming the wish by its object is like calling an intention to stand standing, as in the prayer verse. | mentions 23:99, 5:6
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p135/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · مجاهد وأبو عبيدة | reports | A reading attributed to Mujāhid and Abū ʿUbayda attaches the clause to the tasting of punishment and takes the return as a return to this world, rather than repentance.
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p135/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | A passive reading of يرجعون supports the interpretation that they hope to be returned to worldly life, since it cannot naturally mean their own repentance.
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p135/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | prefers | Al-Ālūsī prefers the transmitted explanation of repentance; the passive reading can still fit if the punishment turns them from disbelief to faith.
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p135/r8] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · سيبويه | reports | Sībawayh explains لعل here as expressing hope from the addressees' perspective.
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p135/r9] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · إبن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās glosses لعل here as كي, with the sense that the punishment may expose them to repentance.
[ELMALILI:32:21-22/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The nearer, lesser punishment is worldly punishment; the greater punishment is the punishment of the afterlife.
[TABATABAI:32:15-30#3/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم | reports | Abu Idris al-Khawlani reports that the Prophet identified the nearer punishment as worldly disasters, illnesses, and hardships for transgressors, but purification and expiation for believers.
[TABATABAI:32:15-30#4/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · أبو جعفر وأبو عبد الله عليهما السلام | reports | Al-Majma' reports from Abu Ja'far and Abu Abdillah that the nearer punishment is the Beast and the Dajjal.
[TABATABAI:32:19-23/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Because the hoped-for return is repentance, the nearer punishment is worldly warning, such as famine, illness, or killing; the greater is punishment on Resurrection Day.
[TABATABAI:32:19-23/r9] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | reports | One explanation says al-adnā suits warning better than al-aṣghar, which makes punishment sound lesser, or al-ab'ad, which does not fit the threatening context.
[ASAD-NOTES:32:21#n16/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | Al-adna literally means 'nearer' and here refers to suffering in this world; Asad points to his note on 52:47. | mentions 52:47
[ASAD-NOTES:32:21#n17/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | The phrase literally means that they might return to righteousness.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-6-2:p132/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Believers value truth, bow to Qur'anic verses, pray at night, and spend for God; their concealed reward contrasts with the unequal treatment of obedient and disobedient people.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-6-2:p144/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The preceding section explains why people evaded faith in the Qur'an; the succeeding verses describe believers and their reward, as well as opponents' worldly and next-world fate.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:32:20/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Tefsirlerde genellikle | reports | Commentaries generally identify the greater punishment as punishment in the afterlife.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:32:20/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Tefsirlerde genellikle | reports | Commentaries generally explain the nearer punishment as worldly calamities and hardships.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:32:20/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Bazı müfessirler | reports | Some exegetes interpret the nearer punishment as punishment in the grave.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:32:20/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · İbn Mes‘ûd | reports | A report from Ibn Masʿūd reads the verse as pointing to the polytheists’ defeat at the Battle of Badr.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:32:20/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | prefers | Because it is imposed to allow return from disbelief, the nearer punishment most likely means worldly material and spiritual distress that gives people a chance for self-examination.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:32:20/r7] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The next verse supports this reading: a person given such opportunities who remains stubborn has reached the height of injustice and definitively deserves punishment. | mentions 32:22
[MUSLIM:7070/r1] MUSLIM · أُبَىِّ بْنِ كَعْبٍ | reports | Ubayy ibn Kaʿb identifies the lesser punishment as worldly calamities, the Romans, and either the assault or the smoke; Shuʿba is uncertain which of those last two was meant.
[STUDYQURAN:32:21/r1] STUDYQURAN · IK, JJ | reports | The lesser punishment is punishment in this worldly life.

[STUDYQURAN:32:21/r5] STUDYQURAN · JJ | reports | The lesser affliction can be a mercy if it prompts reflection and return to belief, the human being's natural state. | mentions 7:172, 30:30
