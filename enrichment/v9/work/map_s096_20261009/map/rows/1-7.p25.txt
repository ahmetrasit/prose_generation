[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r15] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · القشيري | reports | Al-Qushayrī interprets the straight path as the way of Sunna and the community.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r16] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الترمذي | reports | Al-Tirmidhī interprets the straight path as the way of fear and hope.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r17] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · عمرو بن عبيد | reports | ʿAmr b. ʿUbayd interprets the straight path as the bridge over Hell.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r18] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · بعضهم | reports | One Sufi reading asks for guidance through becoming absent from the path, so that one is not bound to the path; Abū Ḥayyān says such readings do not follow the wording, while acknowledging their experiential insight.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r19] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الجنيد | reports | Al-Junayd says asking for guidance amid bewilderment is among the clearest pre-eternal attributes; the request seeks the qualities of servanthood to avoid absorption in those attributes.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r2] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Grammarians disagree about how الذين is recognized; unlike الذي, it is restricted to rational beings.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r20] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الفخر الرازي | reports | Al-Rāzī rejects identifying the path as the Qurʾān or Islam and its laws because those favored people supposedly lived before the Qurʾān and this Islamic dispensation.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r21] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | rejects | Abū Ḥayyān says al-Rāzī's objection works only if the favored people are proved to be pre-Islamic; the source will report differing identifications of them, so that premise is unsettled.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r22] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The request for guidance follows naturally from the preceding declaration of worship and reliance: worship is sound only through guidance to the clear route that reaches its goal.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r23] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الحسن والضحاك | reports | Al-Hasan and al-Dahhāk read صراطاً مستقيماً without the definite article; صراط الذين then apposes a definite expression to an indefinite one, as in 42:52. | mentions 42:52
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r24] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · جعفر الصادق | reports | Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq reads صراط مستقيم with an annexation, meaning the upright religion; with this and the majority reading, صراط الذين is a clarifying apposition of one definite expression to another.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r25] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The apposition identifies the somewhat ambiguous previously mentioned straight path, repeats the request for emphasis, and directs it to the route of those favored.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r26] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | A rare alternative says the second path is different from the first, as if a conjunction had been read between them; the commentators disagree about what this second path denotes.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r27] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · جعفر بن محمد | reports | Jaʿfar b. Muḥammad says the path is knowledge of God and understanding Him.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r28] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | Other explanations identify the path as observing the obligations and following the Sunan, or as inward agreement with outward practice in the completion of blessing.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r29] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The explanation of inward and outward blessing is linked to 31:20, which says God has poured out both manifest and hidden blessings upon you. | mentions 31:20
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r3] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Niʿma denotes ease and softness of living; the south wind and ostrich are also named from softness, and anʿama ʿalayhi means to bestow abundantly.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r30] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · ابن مسعود وعمر وابن الزبير وزيد بن علي | reports | Ibn Masʿūd, ʿUmar, Ibn al-Zubayr, and Zayd b. ʿAlī read صراط من أنعمت عليهم.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r31] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | Among the proposed referents of those favored are prophets, angels, and the unaltered communities of Moses and Jesus; Abū Ḥayyān lists these as alternatives rather than restricting the wording to one group.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r32] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās is cited for identifying those favored as believers.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r33] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · ابن عطية عن ابن عباس والجمهور | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya says Ibn ʿAbbās and the majority mean the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs, and the righteous, deriving this from 4:69. | mentions 4:69
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r34] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan identifies those favored as the Companions of the Prophet.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r35] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · فرقة | reports | One group identifies those favored as the believers among the Children of Israel.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r36] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās also identifies them as Moses' followers before they changed their religion.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r37] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · قتادة | reports | Qatāda limits those favored to the prophets.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r38] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · أبو العالية | reports | Abū al-ʿĀliya identifies those favored as Muḥammad, Abū Bakr, and ʿUmar.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r39] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · وكيع | reports | Wakīʿ identifies those favored as Muslims.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r4] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Anʿama is one of twenty-four functions of the form أفعل: it makes someone possess the base noun, while implying benefaction and taking على instead of its original direct object.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r40] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The wording leaves the blessing unrestricted, so it can encompass every kind of blessing through a general use of the category.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r41] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | Proposed explanations of God's favor include creating people for felicity, rescuing them from destruction, and guiding them to follow the Messenger; the wording itself does not specify a restriction.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r42] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · المعتزلة | reports | The Muʿtazila affirm that God bestows blessing on unbelievers, while other theologians deny it.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r43] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The prepositional phrase عليهم is in the accusative position because it is governed by an active verb; the same applies to a preposition governed by a non-passive verb or equivalent.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r44] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The active attribution of favor appeals for acceptance of the request: since God has already favored them, answering their plea for guidance is consistent with His beneficence.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r45] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The prayer asks that guidance to the path of the favored continue: those who praise God and declare worship and reliance already possess guidance, but ask for its permanence.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r46] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Ghayr is always masculine singular; it expresses difference in some respect, originates as an adjective, can mark exception, and remains annexed. It cannot take al and does not ordinarily become definite by annexation.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r47] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | Ibn al-Sarrāj says ghayr becomes definite when its complement is one specified entity; Sībawayh allows a non-pure annexation to become definite by intent. The commentators also discuss its fronting in predication.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r48] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | In predication, ghayr or mithl tends to precede its complement; the cited line means the speaker is unlike people who are deceived, and reversed order would distort the point.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r49] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Anger is a change in disposition caused by something disliked; it can also mean turning away, since turning away is one consequence of anger.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r5] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The attached tāʾ is the second-person masculine singular pronoun; أنت consists of two pronouns and is a compound.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r50] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Lā can be a particle of negation, prohibition, or augmentation; Abū Ḥayyān rejects the Kufan view that it can be a noun.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r51] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Ḍalāl can mean ruin, concealment, loss, ignorance of an object's location, waste, neglect, forgetting, departure from the proper route, bewilderment, or hesitation; Qurʾanic usage also includes lack of detailed knowledge and love.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r52] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | The majority read ghayr in the genitive; al-Khalīl reports Ibn Kathīr's accusative reading, also attributed to ʿUmar, Ibn Masʿūd, ʿAlī, and ʿAbd Allāh b. al-Zubayr.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r53] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · أبو علي | reports | Abū ʿAlī parses genitive ghayr as an apposition either to الذين or to the pronoun in عليهم; Abū Ḥayyān calls both analyses weak because apposition by an adjective is weak.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r54] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · سيبويه | reports | Sībawayh parses genitive ghayr as an adjective, treating it as definite through annexation to عليهم; Ibn al-Sarrāj offers a different account of its definiteness.
