87:3/q05 Is God said to compel or command misguidance? [فَهَدَىٰ · theology] (turns on: Whether directing to, mentioning, or decreeing error entails compelling or commanding it.)
  87:3/q05/p1 No; God commands and approves obedience while forbidding sin and misguidance. — reasons: Qatāda says God neither compels nor commands sin and does not approve it, answering any reading of guidance that would make Him do so.
  87:3/q05/p2 The verse's guidance can include a path to error or an implied act of leading astray. — reasons: Mujāhid's reports say God guides toward good and evil or right and error; al-Farrāʾ proposes the implicit opposite. These sources do not all make the same claim about compulsion.
87:3/q06 What does the fa in فَهَدَىٰ express? [فَهَدَىٰ · grammar]
  87:3/q06/p1 Guidance follows as the effect or completion of determination. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the fa linking creation and proportioning: each measured creature is directed to its allotted use. Al-Biqāʿī says creation needs proportion for balance, while ordainment needs guidance for completeness.
87:3/q07 Who is the unspoken object of فَهَدَىٰ? [فَهَدَىٰ · grammar] (turns on: Whether general wording encompasses all created things or only beings capable of apprehending direction.)
  87:3/q07/p1 It is left general, then contextually limited to creatures able to apprehend direction. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr includes humans and animals with perception and will but excludes fruiting trees and seed production, which he calls determined rather than guided.
  87:3/q07/p2 The created object may be humans, humans and animals, or all creation. — reasons: Study Quran reports these possibilities for 'created' in the preceding verse; Ibn ʿĀshūr gives the corresponding human-specific reading of determination and guidance.
87:3/q08 How are divine attributes linked by the conjunctions and relative pronoun? [وَٱلَّذِى قَدَّرَ فَهَدَىٰ · rhetoric]
  87:3/q08/p1 The repeated relative pronoun and conjunction emphasize distinct divine predicates. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the repetition amplification. Al-Zamakhsharī and Abū al-Baqāʾ say conjunction makes each adjective independent; al-Burhān compares 68:10 and 57:3 and says conjunction suits attributes that differ widely. Al-Biqāʿī says wāw joins ordainment to proportion to establish both attributes together.
87:3/q09 Do the perfect verbs describe only completed past acts? [قَدَّرَ فَهَدَىٰ · grammar]
  87:3/q09/p1 No; their perfect form is aspectual, and divine creation is ongoing and sustaining. — reasons: Corpus Coranicum points to the repeated growth and decay of pasture in verses 4–5 and compares 53:43–49, 96:1–5, and 95:4–5.
87:3/q10 What inward guidance does the verse describe? [فَهَدَىٰ · inward] (turns on: Which spiritual stage or disposition the guidance addresses.)
  87:3/q10/p1 Heedless people build the world, while worshippers prefer the afterlife. — reasons: Al-Qushayrī contrasts the objects of their efforts.
  87:3/q10/p2 Ascetics recognize the world's passing nature and reject it. — reasons: Al-Qushayrī gives this as another degree of guidance.
  87:3/q10/p3 Scholars contemplate created signs and remain with their inferences. — reasons: Al-Qushayrī says they recognize God through examining His signs and creation.
  87:3/q10/p4 Seekers turn toward the glory of God's attribute and devote themselves to Him. — reasons: Al-Qushayrī says they prefer Him and expend their effort seeking Him.
  87:3/q10/p5 Gnostics enter the sanctity of His description, watching and then witnessing Him. — reasons: Al-Qushayrī portrays successive stages of awareness.
  87:3/q10/p6 Monotheists abandon familiar things and seek God alone in His sovereign sublimity. — reasons: Al-Qushayrī names this as a distinct orientation.
  87:3/q10/p7 Knowers finally rest in incapacity before God beyond proof, explanation, vision, separation, and union. — reasons: Al-Qushayrī locates the divine beyond these modes.
  87:3/q10/p8 God plants human perfection within a person and guides its actualization through purification. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī says refinement brings the potential perfection forth.
87:3/q11 What reports illustrate divine determination and guidance? [* · reports] (turns on: Which reported incident is used to illustrate the point.)
  87:3/q11/p1 God decreed all creatures' destinies fifty thousand years before creating heaven and earth. — reasons: Ibn ʿAmr reports the Prophet's statement; the report also says the Throne was over water. Bursawī cites its timing.
  87:3/q11/p2 A very old blind snake found a healing plant and regained sight. — reasons: Al-Kashshāf calls it a thousand-year-old snake; one version says fresh fennel, another fresh rue in a distant garden. Al-Qurṭubī repeats the fennel version. The plant names vary in the notes.
  87:3/q11/p3 Animals show directed instinct in hunting, nest-building, and storing food. — reasons: Bursawī names a weasel catching a mouse, spiders and bees making nests, and ants splitting damp seeds and drying them so they do not sprout.
  87:3/q11/p4 A diving bird escaped a crow that kept stealing its fish by dragging the crow underwater. — reasons: Bursawī attributes the observation to an unnamed observer.
  87:3/q11/p5 A monkey exposed a seller who diluted milk by throwing the price of the added water into the sea. — reasons: Bursawī reports this as a warning against dishonest sale.
  87:3/q11/p6 A dog repeatedly exposed a well containing its murdered owner, leading to the killer's confession and execution. — reasons: Bursawī transmits the story as an instance of animal guidance.
  87:3/q11/p7 A palm tree weakened from attachment to another tree and recovered when bound to one of its branches. — reasons: Bursawī presents the account as another instance of directed natural behavior.
87:3/q12 How does this verse support the call to glorify God and relate to the surrounding sūras? [* · links] (turns on: Which surrounding passage or theological purpose supplies the connection.)
  87:3/q12/p1 Creation, proportion, determination, and guidance display God's power, wisdom, and worthiness of glorification. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says they answer how the Lord is known and why He merits praise; Abraham's words in 26:78, Moses' reply in 20:49–50, and the first revelation in 96:1–4 use the same proof. The wonders are abundant and observable. Al-Qurṭubī's teacher says the clauses show divine exaltedness. Elmalılı says finite creatures imply the Lord's transcendence and grounds for trust amid disbelievers' schemes.
  87:3/q12/p2 The verse's acts counter the disbelievers' schemes and prepare the Prophet for his mission. — reasons: Jishumī connects it with the preceding warning; al-Biqāʿī and Ibn al-Zubayr tie the plot in 86:15 to God's power and wisdom in 87:1–3. Iṣlāḥī says al-Ṭāriq promises only temporary respite and this sūra turns to reassure the Prophet. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the One who shaped him will secure his retention of revelation and ease his law (87:6, 87:8).
  87:3/q12/p3 The verse parallels other statements that creation is measured and creatures directed. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr cites Moses' 20:50 and the predestination report; Asad links 25:2 and 20:50. Corpus Coranicum links qaddara to 80:19 and hadā to 93:7; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 15:21, 80:20, and 2:148.
  87:3/q12/p4 The ordered creation described in the verse bears on the sūra's themes of oneness, revelation, and recompense. — reasons: Qutb describes the cosmos as a place of praise, the Prophet's preservation and ease, and the message's roots in earlier scriptures; Study Quran says creation is properly formed without flaw and cites 67:3–4.
87:3/q13 Does 'Great' alongside 'Highest' imply more than one deity? [* · theology]
  87:3/q13/p1 No; God's oneness is established, and the wording does not say He is higher than another lord. — reasons: Al-Rāzī rejects the objection to the opening description of God.
87:3/q14 What reports concern the sūra's recitation and revelation? [* · reports] (turns on: Which report or practice is in view.)
  87:3/q14/p1 The sūra was revealed after Companions asked what to say in prostration. — reasons: Abū Hurayra reports that the Prophet then instructed them to say 'Glory to my Lord, the Most High.'
  87:3/q14/p2 The Prophet stopped Ḥaḍramī from appending his own line about a pregnant woman to the verses. — reasons: The report says he called the recitation sufficient.
  87:3/q14/p3 The Prophet loved the sūra and recited it with al-Ghāshiya at Eid and Friday prayer. — reasons: Qutb cites ʿAlī for the Prophet's love and Muslim for the paired recitation, sometimes on the same day.
87:3/q15 What are the sūra's place and verse count? [* · sciences]
  87:3/q15/p1 It is Meccan and has nineteen verses. — reasons: Qutb states this while describing the Prophet's fondness for the sūra.
87:3/q16 How is the sūra's rhyme ending pronounced? [* · readings]
  87:3/q16/p1 Its rhyme endings from al-Aʿlā through Mūsā are subject to imāla. — reasons: Ibn al-Jazarī says their treatment appeared earlier in his imāla chapter.
87:3/q17 How does varying related Qur'anic expressions convey meaning? [* · rhetoric] (turns on: Which purpose of expressive variation is being discussed.)
  87:3/q17/p1 Taṣrīf expresses one meaning through varied, connected meanings and forms, clarifying it from several sides. — reasons: Al-Rummānī illustrates derivatives of ملك, from owner to king and dominion, and عرض, from appearance and turning away to poetic meter; the linked senses illuminate a shared core.
