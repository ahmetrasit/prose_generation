 911 · ابن أبي شهاب | reports | Ibn Abi Shihab explains the Prophet's description of his speech as comprehensive: many matters from earlier books are gathered into one or two expressions.
[BURSEVI:v9p187#2/r1] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | holds | Zaygh means deviation from uprightness; the Prophet's sight did not turn even slightly away from what he saw.
[BURSEVI:v9p187#2/r2] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | holds | He neither exceeded bounds amid overwhelming sights, but retained them accurately, nor turned from the marvels he was enabled and commanded to see.
[BURSEVI:v9p187#2/r3] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | prefers | The author's preferred inference is that the Prophet saw God with physical eyesight while awake: describing the eye as steady indicates waking vision, whereas a heart-vision would mention the heart.
[BURSEVI:v9p187#2/r4] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | rejects | The alternative that baṣar means inner sight is possible in isolation, but the author rejects it because the passage supplies no contextual evidence for that reading.
[BURSEVI:v9p187#2/r5] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · الكاشفي | reports | Al-Kashifi says Muhammad's eye neither turned aside nor exceeded its permitted bounds; this praises his good etiquette and high aspiration, as he looked only toward the everlasting divine beauty.
[BURSEVI:v9p187#2/r6] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · التأويلات النجمية | reports | Al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya reads this as the Prophet's total poverty of self: his bodily sight ignored worldly attractions, his spiritual sight ignored afterlife rewards, and both attended wholly to God, His names, attributes, and manifestations.
[BURSEVI:v9p187#2/r7] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · البقلي | reports | Al-Baqli applies the verse to the second vision: the first had nothing besides God, while this one shows the beloved's complete steadiness and desire to behold his Lord without turning even toward a noble thing.
