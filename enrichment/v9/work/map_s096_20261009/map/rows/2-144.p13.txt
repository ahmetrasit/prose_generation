[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#2/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | في may attach to verbal noun تقلب with ordinary locative sense; it may mean إلى, but al-Samīn sees no need because this noun is attested with في, as in 3:196. | mentions 3:196
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A second view attaches في to نرى and takes في to mean من; heaven is mentioned honorifically, not to claim God occupies a direction.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqāʾ makes في السماء an accusative حال from وجهك, attached to an omitted element.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#2/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | تقلب is annexed to its subject; it cannot be annexed to its object, for that verbal noun would be التقليب.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | There is no need to supply an omitted مضاف meaning sight of your face, a rare usage; face is the noblest feature and one turned by a petitioner, though another view takes it as metonym for sight.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The فاء marks consequence, and the clause answers an omitted oath, فوالله لنولينك.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | نولي governs two objects, the suffix ك and قبلة; ترضاها is a clause in the accusative qualifying قبلة.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#3/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | reports | The shaykh says the promise implies an omitted حال: God sees the Prophet turning his face toward heaven while seeking a different qibla from the one he faces.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#3/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | ولي governs وجهك and شطر as two objects; it can also take its second object with إلى.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#3/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · النحاس | reports | Al-Naḥḥās allows شطر as locative adverb, leaving ولي with one object; al-Zamakhsharī mentions only that analysis, but al-Samīn finds the two-object analysis clearer.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#3/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | شطر can mean half or part, or direction and toward; lines 762–767 attest the directional sense, including Ibn Aḥmar's شطر نجد.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#3/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن أحمر | reports | Ibn Aḥmar's شطر نجد means toward Najd and witnesses the directional sense of شطر.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#3/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | شطر also means far away: الشاطر is a youth distant from neighbors and home, شطير is distant, and شطر إليه can mean turning toward; al-Rāghib also uses الشاطر for distancing from truth, plural شطار.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#4/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The clearest analysis takes ḥaythumā as conditional; the added mā is required for that force, contrary to al-Farrāʾ.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#4/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | On the conditional analysis, kuntum is jussive, wallū answers the condition, and ḥaythumā is an accusative adverb governed by kuntum; Q 17:110 illustrates the construction. | mentions 17:110
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#4/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | reports | Although ḥayth is a locative noun normally annexed to a clause in the genitive, its connection with mā removes annexation and gives it conditional force and verbal governance.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#4/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | rejects | Abū al-Baqāʾ treats ḥayth as a nonconditional adverb governed by wallū; the author rejects this because added mā necessarily makes it conditional.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#4/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Wallū derives from waliyū: the heavy ḍamma on yā is dropped, then yā is deleted at the meeting of two sukūns and the preceding vowel is rounded to match the pronoun.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#4/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Shatrahu has two analyses: it is either the direct object or an adverb of direction, as discussed earlier.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#5/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الجمهور | reports | For the majority, the clause anna-hu al-ḥaqq supplies both objects of yaʿlamūn.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#5/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash allows the clause to supply one object of a verb taking two, with the second object omitted.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#5/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A further analysis has the clause supply one object because yaʿlamūn means to recognize or know.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#5/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | One proposed antecedent of the pronoun in annahu is the turning toward the qibla implied by wallū.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#5/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A second proposed antecedent of the pronoun in annahu is the direction, shatrahu.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#5/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A third view refers the pronoun to the Prophet; this shifts from direct address in fa-lanuwalliyannaka to speaking of him in the third person.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#5/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Min rabbihim modifies an omitted circumstantial expression describing al-ḥaqq: the truth is from their Lord.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#5/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The third-person reading may address the People of the Book or refer back to the believers, with a shift from the preceding address.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144#5/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The direct-address reading most naturally addresses the believers; it may instead turn to the People of the Book to spur them on.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Some grammarians say قد shifts a present verb into past meaning, applying it here and citing poetry with قد نرى أمس.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:144/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī glosses قد نرى as perhaps we see and takes it to suggest abundant seeing, citing قد أترك القرن.
