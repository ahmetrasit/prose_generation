[TABATABAI:82:19/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم | reports | A report reaching Salih ibn Mismar says the Prophet recited the question about what deluded a person regarding his generous Lord, then answered that it was ignorance.
[TABATABAI:82:6/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The addressee is the human who denies Judgment Day, as the surrounding passage indicates; denial of judgment entails denial of religious law and of God's lordship. | mentions 82:9
[TABATABAI:82:6/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Addressing him as a human makes his human qualities collectively serve as proof against his ingratitude.
[TABATABAI:82:6/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Linking the rebuke to God's lordship and generosity makes the question an argument: rebellion against a managing Lord who pours out blessings is plainly ugly and deserves punishment.
[TABATABAI:82:6/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Ingratitude is especially blameworthy because the generous Lord seeks no benefit or repayment, tolerates wrongdoing, and forgives sins committed in ignorance.
[TABATABAI:82:6/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The question is a rebuke for a specific inexcusable ingratitude: failing to thank a generous Lord for His blessings.
