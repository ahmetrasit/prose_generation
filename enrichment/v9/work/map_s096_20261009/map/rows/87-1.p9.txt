[ALUSI:87:2/r17] ALUSI, d. 1270 · عصام الدين | reports | ʿIṣām al-Dīn suggests that “name” means God’s effects: sanctify His works and do not fault creatures as created, in keeping with 67:3; al-Ālūsī finds this remote. | mentions 67:3
[ALUSI:87:2/r18] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | prefers | Al-Ālūsī allows an explicit or inserted “name” if sabbiḥ means “declare pure,” but if it means “say subḥān,” insertion is necessary; practice and Ubayy’s reported wording support that reading.
[ALUSI:87:2/r19] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبي بن كعب | reports | A report attributed to Ubayy ibn Kaʿb reads the command as “Glory be to my Lord, the Most High,” supporting the inserted-name analysis.
[ALUSI:87:2/r2] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Names exclusive to God must not be applied to others; shared names must not be used in a way that treats God and a creature as equal providers.
[ALUSI:87:2/r20] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | rejects | Al-Ālūsī rejects the claim that the name is identical with the named, which some infer from this and similar verses.
[ALUSI:87:2/r21] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Al-Aʿlā most naturally describes the Lord: His elevation means supremacy and power, not physical location; the early authorities likewise deny spatial elevation, even without figurative reinterpretation.
[ALUSI:87:2/r22] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | reports | A possible alternative makes al-Aʿlā describe the name: it is exalted above sharing its essential meaning with another name.
[ALUSI:87:2/r3] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | God’s names should be protected from casual or irreverent utterance, including in unsuitable places and before someone who dislikes hearing them without necessity.
[ALUSI:87:2/r4] ALUSI, d. 1270 · مالك | reports | Mālik, out of scrupulous reverence, avoided telling a beggar who disliked it that God would provide; he praised God’s name too highly to invoke it in a phrase the listener resented.
