[TABRISI:96:2/r16] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The opening command tells the Prophet to recite in God's name and invoke Him by His beautiful names; honoring the name honors the named one, which only someone who knows God and believes in worship can truly do. | mentions 17:110, 87:1

[RAZI:96:1/r1] RAZI, d. 606 · المفسرون | reports | The commentators say Sūrat al-ʿAlaq was the first revelation.
[RAZI:96:1/r10] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | A third explanation is to perform the act for God and dedicate it to Him; what is devoted to God is beyond Satan’s interference.
[RAZI:96:1/r11] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | Mentioning God before a permissible act can figuratively attribute it to Him and protect it from Satan, who shares food when God’s name is omitted.
[RAZI:96:1/r12] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | A permissible act may be dedicated to God when it helps one obey Him, turning it into worship.
[RAZI:96:1/r13] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Although Allāh is nobler as a name of the divine essence than rabb, worship is urged effectively through God’s active relation as Lord, which makes it incumbent.
[RAZI:96:1/r14] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Because this was among the first revelations and the Prophet was frightened, rabb reassures him: the One who raised him will not abandon him; beginning care entails completion.
[RAZI:96:1/r15] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | Rāzī contrasts God’s adding Himself to the Prophet through lordship with describing the Prophet as His servant in the night journey, and cites a saying that ʿAlī and the Prophet belong to one another. | mentions 17:1
