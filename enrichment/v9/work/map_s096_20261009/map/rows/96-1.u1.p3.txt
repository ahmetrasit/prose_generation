96:1/q09 What practice does the opening command imply for the basmala? [بِٱسْمِ رَبِّكَ · law] (turns on: Whether it requires or supports saying the basmala at each sūra opening.)
  96:1/q09/p1 Begin every sūra or Qurʾān recitation with the basmala. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī explicitly prefers this inference against omitting the basmala; Rāzī argues the opening requires it at every sūra, and al-Qurṭubī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya report the same practice. BIQAI: The commentators say this indicates that recitation is not complete without invoking God's name; honoring the name honors its bearer and all that is attributed to Him. BIQAI-FULL (قالوا): They say recitation is not complete without naming God; naming Him honors both the name and its bearer and all His attributes. ALUSI: On this reading one must begin by saying in God's name; opening with another name would not fulfill the command. ALUSI: Some use the command to argue that the basmala is part of every sūra, though al-Ālūsī says the inference needs discussion. ALUSI (قتادة): Qatāda explains the command as beginning in the Lord’s name: say “In God’s name,” then recite; without the basmala, or with an idol’s name, the command would not be fulfilled. ALUSI-FULL (قتادة): According to a reading attributed to Qatada, the command means begin or open with your Lord's name, saying the basmala and then reading; this supports requiring basmala when reading and has been used to argue it belongs to every surah, though al-Alusi notes debate. BINTSHATI (الفخر الرازي): Al-Razi takes the command to recite in God’s name as implying that every Quran recitation should begin with God’s name.
  96:1/q09/p2 The phrase directs the first recitation to begin with God's name without proving every sūra's basmala obligatory. — reasons: Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī construe the opening as a circumstance of this recitation, without stating the wider rule.
  96:1/q09/p3 The inference that it is part of or required for every sūra remains disputed. — reasons: ALUSI: Some use the command to argue that the basmala is part of every sūra, though al-Ālūsī says the inference needs discussion. 96:1/q10 Is the basmala itself part of the earliest revelation or of each sūra? [بِٱسْمِ رَبِّكَ · sciences] (turns on: Whether reports omit it, place it first, or allow a later descent.)
  96:1/q10/p1 The basmala was the first revelation, followed by Iqraʾ. — reasons: ALUSI (عكرمة والحسن): A report from ʿIkrima and al-Ḥasan says the first Qurʾānic revelation was bismi Allāh al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm, followed by the opening of Iqraʾ.; cites 1:1, 96:1 ALUSI (ابن عباس): Al-Ḍaḥḥāk's report from Ibn ʿAbbās says Gabriel first instructed the Prophet to seek refuge, then to say the basmala. ALUSI-FULL (عكرمة، الحسن): Ikrima and Hasan say the basmala was the first Quranic revelation; al-Dahhak's report from Ibn Abbas says Gabriel first instructed Muhammad to seek refuge and say it.
