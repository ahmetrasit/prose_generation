39:23/q34 What does God's leading someone astray mean here? [وَمَن يُضْلِلِ ٱللَّهُ · theology] (turns on: Whether misguidance is created in the person, grace withheld, loss of Paradise, or a result of rejecting the Quran.)
  39:23/q34/p1 God creates or decrees misguidance in one who turns away, leaving no rescuer. — reasons: Nasafī, Bursevī and Ālūsī explicitly speak of created misguidance; Biqāʿī says none can overturn divine judgment; Ṭabarī relates it to failure to affirm the Quran. Qurṭubī and Thaʿlabī say the clause refutes the Qadariyya; Rāzī refers to the debate at 6:125.
  39:23/q34/p2 God withholds luṭf or leaves a transgressor to his own hard heart. — reasons: Zamakhsharī gives withholding grace from wrongdoers and alternatively grace's ineffectiveness on hardened hearts; al-Najmiyya says leaving a person to his intellect and depriving him of faith in prophets makes philosophical proof unable to save him.
  39:23/q34/p3 It means turning someone away from Paradise and divine mercy. — reasons: Jubbāʾī explains loss of the path to Paradise; Abū Muslim says straying from God and mercy; another view makes it deprivation of increase in grace.
  39:23/q34/p4 It describes the different human responses to a revelation that guides and heals. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Quran supplies guidance, yet some turn away; Biqāʿī says hardened hearts remain unmoved by the same revelation, which then misleads. Bursevī reports the image of believer faces lit and hypocrite faces burned by the Quran.
39:23/q35 Why can no one guide the person God misleads? [فَمَا لَهُۥ مِنْ هَادٍ · interpretation]
  39:23/q35/p1 No guide can reverse God's judgment or overcome the hardened person's refusal. — reasons: Ṭabarī says no one can enable acceptance of the Quran; Biqāʿī appeals to sovereign decree; Islahi to unworthiness; Study Quran cites parallels at 4:88, 7:186, 13:33, 39:36 and elsewhere, interpreting the unsoftened heart as unguided. Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes God and His authorized guides; Bursevī's al-Najmiyya says unaided philosophical proof is insufficient. Al-Jāmī counsels seekers of realization not to travel without a guide.
39:23/q36 How is hād pronounced in pause or connection? [هَادٍ · readings] (turns on: Retention or omission of the final yāʾ in pausal performance.)
  39:23/q36/p1 Some readers preserve the final yāʾ in pause, and Yaʿqūb-style performance preserves a hādī-like glide. — reasons: Qurṭubī names Ibn Kathīr and Ibn Muḥayṣin at this and 39:36; QIRAAT-ER describes the Yaʿqūb-style continuation and pause texture.
  39:23/q36/p2 Other readers omit the final yāʾ at pause. — reasons: Qurṭubī contrasts the remaining readers with Ibn Kathīr and Ibn Muḥayṣin.
39:23/q37 How does 39:23 connect to its surrounding passage? [* · links] (turns on: The links made are to the earlier 'best' command, the rain parable, parallel response verses, and the passage's close.)
  39:23/q37/p1 It identifies the 'best' saying to be followed in 39:17–18 as the Quran. — reasons: Abū Muslim, Qurṭubī and Ṭabrisī connect the earlier command to this naming of the Quran. Jishumī makes following truth over falsehood and calling others to truth consequences of hearing the best.
  39:23/q37/p2 It resumes the sura's Quranic guidance theme and draws the lesson of the rain parable. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr connects 39:21–23 to the guidance theme begun at 39:2, says growth parallels the receptive listener, and sees hardened hearts as their own rust, comparable with 2:6–7. Biqāʿī says this verse answers the preceding puzzle that remembrance can harden some hearts. Bursevī warns that complacency, even in permissible pleasure, hardens hearts while hardship recalls God.
  39:23/q37/p3 It reconciles trembling with hearts resting in remembrance elsewhere. — reasons: Burhān and Itqān distinguish settled certainty from fear of deviation, citing 13:28 and 8:2; Baghawī cites 13:28 for the ensuing tranquility.
  39:23/q37/p4 The final clause concludes the explanation of Quranic guidance. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a conclusion; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the statement recurs elsewhere; Biqāʿī notes ellipsis by correspondence between guidance and misguidance.
39:23/q38 What aspects of the Quran's status are illustrated by this verse? [* · interpretation]
  39:23/q38/p1 Its rank makes recitation, preservation, and devotion especially valuable. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya collects reports: the Quran as supreme gift, greatest intercessor, best worship, nobility of bearers, rewards for hundred/two hundred/three hundred verses, double reward for difficult recitation, and peril of neglect. ʿUmar comments on 35:32. Bāqillānī notes preservation and recitation in prayer, opened by seeking refuge at 16:98; a sermon urges ongoing attention to God's speech and remembrance.
  39:23/q38/p2 The Quran is an integrated discourse whose whole and details reinforce each other. — reasons: Samarrāʾī quotes Rāzī comparing it to one sura or even one verse; his study covers imagery, rhythm, diction and openings/endings. Burhān lists 'best discourse' among Quranic titles.
39:23/q39 What related Quranic sciences claims arise from calling it a Book? [كِتَٰبًۭا · sciences] (turns on: The passage's side discussions concern fixed order, the Anfāl/Barāʾa exception, and names of suras.)
  39:23/q39/p1 The canonical order of suras is prescribed, matching the Preserved Tablet. — reasons: Ālūsī prefers this on prophetic indication and Companion consensus; al-Anbārī says Gabriel placed every verse and sura, and al-Kirmānī and Ṭībī cite annual reviews. Ālūsī treats isolated contrary reports as inadequate and explains ʿUthmān's action as compatible with a textual sign.
  39:23/q39/p2 Al-Anfāl and Barāʾa may be an exception settled by ʿUthmān's judgment. — reasons: Ālūsī records Ibn ʿAbbās's question to ʿUthmān and al-Bayhaqī's qualified position, which al-Suyūṭī welcomed; reports about the last Barāʾa verses figure in the compilation discussion.
  39:23/q39/p3 Sura names are likely received, not freely inferred from themes. — reasons: Burhān says a clever reader could otherwise multiply names endlessly; it surveys names of Barāʾa, Fātiḥa and others.
39:23/q40 Is 39:23 reported as a Medinan exception? [* · sciences]
  39:23/q40/p1 Some add this verse to reported Medinan exceptions. — reasons: Itqān cites Ibn al-Jazarī; Study Quran says other commentators regard verses 10 and 23 as Medinan.
