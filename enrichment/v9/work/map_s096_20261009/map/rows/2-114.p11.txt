114#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | أن يذكر may be the second object of منع, as in منعته كذا.

[SAMIN-DURR:2:114#2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A third analysis makes أن يذكر an appositive of encompassment to مساجد: preventing mention of His name there.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:114#2/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A fourth analysis deletes a preposition, with الأصل من أن يذكر; the usual positions allow accusative or genitive.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:114#2/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | في خرابها attaches to سعى.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:114#2/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqāʾ treats خراب as an اسم مصدر meaning التخريب, annexed to its object because it operates like a verb; the cited عطائك illustrates verbal-noun operation.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:114#2/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Another analysis takes خراب as verbal noun of خرب المكان يخرب, meaning seeking the place itself be ruined through lack of maintenance; it is annexed to its subject, as in منزل خراب and Ghaylān's الخرب.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:114#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | لهم is the fronted predicate of كان, and أن يدخلوها its subject as an infinitive phrase; the clause predicates أولئك.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:114#3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | خائفين is a حال of the subject of يدخلوها; the exception excludes every state of entry except fear.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:114#3/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبي | reports | Ubayy reads خيفا, plural of خائف like ضرب for ضارب; its original is خوف like صوم, with both wāws replaced by yāʾs as in صوم/صيم.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:114#3/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The construction follows the singular form of من in منع and سعى, then its plural sense in أولئك and what follows.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:114#3/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | لهم في الدنيا خزي and what follows are independent; they cannot be حال because disgrace is constant, not limited to mosque entry.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:114/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | من is an interrogative subject and أظلم its elative predicate; the question means no one is more unjust.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:114/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The same formula concerns fabricating lies about God, rejecting His signs, and lying about Him, raising how each can be the most unjust. | mentions 6:21, 32:22, 39:32
[SAMIN-DURR:2:114/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · السائل | reports | One answer restricts each superlative to its class: among preventers, forgers, and liars, no one exceeds the named offender in that class.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:114/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A second answer says they are worst because they were first to commit such acts; later followers do not surpass those who initiated the practice.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:114/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | A third answer says denying anyone greater injustice does not deny injustice itself: the groups may tie for maximal injustice despite different means.

[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p387#2/r3] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · قتادة | reports | In another report, Qatada calls the Christians God's enemies and says hatred of the Jews drove them to help the Babylonian Magian Nebuchadnezzar destroy Jerusalem's Temple.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p387#2/r4] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · السدي | reports | Al-Suddi says Christians backed Nebuchadnezzar's destruction and disposal of corpses in the Temple; the Romans helped because Israel had killed John son of Zechariah, a report also attributed to al-Hasan al-Basri.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p387#2/r5] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd identifies the preventers as the Meccan polytheists who barred the Prophet at Hudaybiya, forcing him to sacrifice at Dhu Tuwa and make a truce; they objected that he had killed their fathers at Badr.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p387#2/r6] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd explains striving to ruin the mosques as cutting off those who maintain them through remembrance and come for pilgrimage or ʿumra.

[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p387#2/r8] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · ابن جرير | prefers | Ibn Jarir prefers the Christian account, arguing that Quraysh did not seek to ruin the Kaaba whereas the Romans did seek to ruin Jerusalem's Temple.

[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p388/r2] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | The sequence moves from rebuking Jews and Christians to the polytheists who expelled the Prophet and his companions and barred them from prayer at the Sacred Mosque; Ibn Kathir cites 8:34, 9:17–18, and 48:25. | mentions 8:34, 9:17, 9:18, 48:25

[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p388/r4] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | Maintaining mosques means remembering God, establishing His law, and cleansing them of impurity and idolatry, not merely decorating or constructing them.
