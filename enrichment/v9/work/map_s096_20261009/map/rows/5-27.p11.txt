[IBNASHUR-FULL:v6p169/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Idh is a temporal adverb attached to nabāʾ, locating the account at the time the two offered their sacrifices.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v6p169/r10] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Jealousy over the distinction of acceptance drove the murder; envy was the first crime to appear on earth.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v6p169/r11] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Habil’s reply is admonition and a disclaimer of any cause for murder: acceptance belongs to God and comes from the God-fearing, implicitly showing his brother was not pious and that the accepted offering gives no grounds for killing.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v6p169/r12] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | If muttaqūn has its ordinary religious sense, the claim restricts acceptance to the pious; that may have been their law, later superseded by Islam’s acceptance of a believer’s good deeds despite other lapses.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v6p169/r13] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | Another reading takes “pious” to mean sincere in the act, making nonacceptance evidence of insincerity; Ibn ʿAshur says this stretches the customary meaning of taqwa.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v6p169/r14] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | A third reading understands acceptance as complete acceptance, indicated by a sacrifice being consumed by fire, like complete guidance for the pious and the full afterlife reserved for them. | mentions 2:2, 43:35
