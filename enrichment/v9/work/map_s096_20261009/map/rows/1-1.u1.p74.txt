1:1/q36 Why is al-Fātiḥa called the seven oft-repeated? [* · sciences]
  1:1/q36/p1 Abu Hurayra reports the Prophet naming the sura Umm al-Qur’an, Fatiha al-Kitab, and the seven oft-repeated verses; no grading is given. — reasons: MAWARDI (أبو هريرة): Abu Hurayra reports the Prophet naming the sura Umm al-Qur’an, Fatiha al-Kitab, and the seven oft-repeated verses; no grading is given. IBNJAWZI-ZAD (رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم): Abū Hurayra reports that the Prophet said no comparable sūra was revealed in the Torah, Gospel, Psalms, or Qur'an; al-Fātiḥa is the seven oft-repeated verses and the great Qur'an given to him. QURTUBI (أبو هريرة): Abū Hurayra transmits the names Umm al-Qurʾān, Umm al-Kitāb, and the seven oft-repeated verses; al-Tirmidhī grades the report ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ.
  1:1/q36/p2 Seven are oft-repeated because Fātiḥa is recited in every prayer, obligatory or voluntary; 15:87. — reasons: TAB (author): Seven are oft-repeated because Fātiḥa is recited in every prayer, obligatory or voluntary; 15:87. TAB (الحسن البصري): Al-Ḥasan identifies seven oft-repeated verses as Fātiḥa, repeated in every recitation or perhaps prayer; 15:87. MAWARDI (author): It is oft-repeated because it is recited in every obligatory and voluntary prayer.
  1:1/q36/p3 Prophet names Fātiḥa Mother of Qurʾān, Opening of Book and seven oft-repeated verses; Ṭabarī calls report sound. — reasons: TAB (رسول الله): Prophet names Fātiḥa Mother of Qurʾān, Opening of Book and seven oft-repeated verses; Ṭabarī calls report sound. DURR (النبي): The Prophet identifies al-Hamd as the Mother of the Qur'an and Book, seven oft-repeated verses, al-Fatiha, and the Great Qur'an.
  1:1/q36/p4 ʿAlī recited the basmala when opening a sura in prayer, said omitting it diminishes the prayer, and called it the completion of the seven oft-repeated verses and the Great Qurʾān. — reasons: THALABI (علي بن أبي طالب): ʿAlī recited the basmala when opening a sura in prayer, said omitting it diminishes the prayer, and called it the completion of the seven oft-repeated verses and the Great Qurʾān. DURR-FULL (علي): Ali recited the basmala when beginning a surah in prayer and said omitting it diminishes the prayer; it completes the seven oft-repeated verses.
  1:1/q36/p5 Ubayy reports that the Prophet called the Fātiḥa the unmatched Mother of the Qur’an and seven oft-repeated verses, divided between God and servant, with the servant receiving what he asks. — reasons: JISHUMI (أبى بن كعب): Ubayy reports that the Prophet called the Fātiḥa the unmatched Mother of the Qur’an and seven oft-repeated verses, divided between God and servant, with the servant receiving what he asks. QURTUBI (رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم): The Prophet tells Ubayy ibn Kaʿb that no Torah or Gospel passage matches the Mother of the Qurʾān, the seven oft-repeated verses divided between God and servant.
  1:1/q36/p6 It is the seven oft-repeated because scholars agree that it consists of seven verses. — reasons: BAGHAWI (author): It is the seven oft-repeated because scholars agree that it consists of seven verses. BAYDAWI (author): It is called the Seven Oft-Repeated because it comprises seven verses by agreement.
  1:1/q36/p7 It is called oft-repeated because it is recited in every prayer cycle. — reasons: BAGHAWI (author): It is called oft-repeated because it is recited in every prayer cycle. RAZI-FULL (author): It is called the oft-repeated because it is recited in prayer and then followed by another sura. | mentions 15:87
  1:1/q36/p8 One explanation of al-Mathani is that the surah is repeated in every prayer cycle. — reasons: IBNATIYYA (author): One explanation of al-Mathani is that the surah is repeated in every prayer cycle. RAZI-FULL (author): Another explanation is that it is repeated in every prayer cycle. | mentions 15:87
  1:1/q36/p9 Abu Saʿid ibn al-Muʿalla reports that the Prophet promised to teach him the Qur'an's greatest surah and identified it as al-Hamd, the seven oft-repeated and the great Qur'an. — reasons: IBNKATHIR (رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم): Abu Saʿid ibn al-Muʿalla reports that the Prophet promised to teach him the Qur'an's greatest surah and identified it as al-Hamd, the seven oft-repeated and the great Qur'an. DURR (النبي): The Prophet told Abu Sa'id ibn al-Mualla that al-Hamd is the greatest sura, the seven oft-repeated verses, and Great Qur'an given to him.
  1:1/q36/p10 Fatiha is the seven oft-repeated and Mother of the Book, its greatest and earliest part; Mecca is Mother of Villages because oldest; 3:96. — reasons: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL (author): Fatiha is the seven oft-repeated and Mother of the Book, its greatest and earliest part; Mecca is Mother of Villages because oldest; 3:96.
  1:1/q36/p11 Abū al-Najm’s verse pairs Fātiḥa’s praise with Qurʾān and oft-repeated, supporting the name’s association with Fātiḥa. — reasons: TAB (author): Abū al-Najm’s verse pairs Fātiḥa’s praise with Qurʾān and oft-repeated, supporting the name’s association with Fātiḥa.

  1:1/q36/p13 Calling Fātiḥa seven oft-repeated does not prevent whole Qurʾān or later sūras also being mathānī for another understood reason. — reasons: TAB (author): Calling Fātiḥa seven oft-repeated does not prevent whole Qurʾān or later sūras also being mathānī for another understood reason.

  1:1/q36/p15 Before reciting, standing, sitting, or another action, the phrase means beginning that act by naming and remembering God. — reasons: TAB (author): Before reciting, standing, sitting, or another action, the phrase means beginning that act by naming and remembering God.
