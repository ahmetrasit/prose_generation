27:36/q11 What is the grammatical role of mā in the comparison? [فَمَآ · grammar]
  27:36/q11/p1 Mā is a relative pronoun: what God gave Solomon is better than their wealth. — reasons: Bursevī parses it as a relative pronoun, making the whole clause the subject of the comparison.
27:36/q12 What is 'what God gave me'? [فَمَآ ءَاتَىٰنِۦَ ٱللَّهُ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether God's comparative gift denotes spiritual office plus kingdom, material wealth alone, or inward knowledge. Al-Ālūsī weighs the first as more fitting.)
  27:36/q12/p1 It is God's gifts of prophethood, religion, wisdom, kingship, and material power. — reasons: Muqātil includes Islam, prophethood and troops; al-Thaʿlabī adds wealth and wisdom; al-Nasafī includes blessing. Ibn Kathīr adds vast wealth and forces, and Asad includes insight into hidden realities. Al-Ālūsī prefers prophethood and unique sovereignty as primary; al-Nasafī argues religion brings lasting benefit and he already has ample goods.
  27:36/q12/p2 It can be understood chiefly as Solomon's material wealth. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī allows the reading but finds it less eloquent and contextually weaker than prophethood plus sovereignty.
  27:36/q12/p3 In an inward reading, it is certain knowledge, sacred realities, intellectual delight, and luminous vision. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī contrasts these with sensory and imaginary adornments.
27:36/q13 How is ءَاتَىٰنِۦَ read? [ءَاتَىٰنِۦَ · readings] (turns on: Pronouncing, opening or omitting the first-person yāʾ in connection and pause, and al-Kisāʾī's imāla; codex spelling and avoidance of adjacent vowelless sounds are invoked.)
  27:36/q13/p1 The yāʾ is omitted, leaving ā­tān with kasra on the nūn. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr, Ibn ʿĀmir, Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim, Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī are named. The codices omit yāʾ; al-Farrāʾ favors following written form where usage supports it. Qirāʾāt notes call it a dialectal pausal short form. Ibn Khālawayh distinguishes short ātā from long ātānī by derivation from coming versus giving; al-Zarkashī reports Abū al-ʿAbbās's symbolic account of the omitted yāʾ as an enduring spiritual gift.
  27:36/q13/p2 The yāʾ is retained with fatḥa in connected recitation and usually dropped at pause. — reasons: Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, Ḥafṣ, Abū Jaʿfar and Ruways are named across accounts. Al-Qurṭubī notes the yāʾ is absent from every codex; Ibn al-Jazarī records differing reports on pause for several transmitters. Ibn Khālawayh says the imams' lengthened form derives from giving.
  27:36/q13/p3 Some retain the yāʾ at pause or pronounce it with sukūn. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī says Yaʿqūb keeps it at pause but drops it in connection to avoid two vowelless sounds; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Bayḍāwī report a still yāʾ. The reports on exact transmitters vary.
  27:36/q13/p4 Al-Kisāʾī inclines the tāʾ/alif by imāla while other readers open it. — reasons: Al-Fārisī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Bayḍāwī report this distinction; Ibn al-Jazarī refers to his imāla discussion.
27:36/q14 Why does Solomon ascribe his gift to God? [ءَاتَىٰنِۦَ ٱللَّهُ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the attribution chiefly expresses humility or serves the clause's information structure; both can coexist.)
  27:36/q14/p1 He acknowledges divine favor and avoids boasting of personal wealth. — reasons: Al-Samīn directly gives this reason; al-Ālūsī says the clause explains his refusal indirectly, without boasting. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī's indefinite māl makes the contrast stronger.
  27:36/q14/p2 The wording makes the comparison a new explanation rather than a presupposed circumstance. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī says 'what God gave me is better than what He gave you' would require a known, confirmatory state; Ibn ʿĀshūr similarly says wāw would suggest prior knowledge by the queen.
27:36/q15 What were the queen's people given? [مِّمَّآ ءَاتَىٰكُم · referent]
  27:36/q15/p1 They were given worldly wealth, ornaments, and luxury. — reasons: Al-Nasafī identifies their possessions as worldly adornment; Muqātil and others contrast them with Solomon's religion and kingship.
27:36/q16 What does the turn with بَلْ do? [بَلْ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether bal is described as a move to their motive, to the practical refusal, or to exclusive contrast.)
  27:36/q16/p1 It moves from rejecting the money to explaining the offer by their worldly motive. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī say Solomon exposes the only sort of gain they recognize; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it also indirectly refuses and returns the gift.
  27:36/q16/p2 It signals a contrast: only they rejoice in the gift, while Solomon does not. — reasons: Al-Suyūṭī says the definite subject and affirmative predicate restrict rejoicing to them after his rejection. Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise says fronting antum makes the gift impress them alone.
27:36/q17 Whose gift is 'your gift'? [بِهَدِيَّتِكُمْ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the possessive names the recipient or giver. Al-Samīn says no gift from Solomon is reported; Bursevī cites the envoys' shame at their own offering.)
  27:36/q17/p1 It is the gift received by the addressees; they rejoice when given gifts. — reasons: Al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī allow a noun of gift to attach to its recipient; the Shaykh in al-Samīn prefers this. Bursevī prefers it because the embassy had found its own gift meagre before Solomon's display. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a gift brought to them.
  27:36/q17/p2 It is the gift given by the queen's party; they rejoice in offering it. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Rāzī report pride in what they give; al-Samīn argues no report says Solomon sent a gift to them, so the possessive should name the giver. Al-Ālūsī notes the indefinite 'gift' may echo pride in the elaborate tests.
27:36/q18 Why do they rejoice in the gift? [أَنتُم بِهَدِيَّتِكُمْ تَفْرَحُونَ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether 'your gift' attaches to recipient or giver, whether joy is literal pride or ironic return, and whether the sentence states motive or spiritual contrast.)
  27:36/q18/p1 They love receiving gifts and accumulating worldly goods; Solomon does not. — reasons: Muqātil says people rejoice in exchanged gifts, whereas Solomon wants Islam. Al-Rāzī connects their joy to material desire; al-Bayḍāwī says they know only outward life; Bursevī prefers this reading in context, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports it. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī allow recipient attribution.
  27:36/q18/p2 They boast of the costly gift they themselves sent or can afford to send. — reasons: Al-Rāzī reports this as a second view; al-Bayḍāwī and Abū Ḥayyān report social pride; al-Samīn favors the giver reading; the al-Irshād account adds self-importance and a sense of obligating Solomon. Bursevī objects that they were ashamed after seeing his wealth.
  27:36/q18/p3 Their delight in gifts shows they lack awareness of lasting spiritual value. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī says Solomon's party rejoices in what comes from God; Jaʿfar says worldly goods are too small to rejoice or grieve over. Asad and the Study Quran say the queen's party prizes material power.
  27:36/q18/p4 Solomon ironically tells them to take the gift back and enjoy it themselves. — reasons: Al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī report this euphemistic refusal; al-Ālūsī calls it obscure. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the predicate indirectly announces the return.
  27:36/q18/p5 They expect a larger material or political return from sending a ruler a gift. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports that the hoped-for return could include sparing their country from destruction.
27:36/q19 Why is أَنتُم placed before 'rejoice'? [أَنتُم تَفْرَحُونَ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether word order or explicit subject alone yields restriction; some notes make context decisive.)
  27:36/q19/p1 Fronting the subject restricts rejoicing to them and excludes Solomon. — reasons: Al-Suyūṭī says a definite subject with affirmative predicate can restrict; al-Sakkākī supports explicit subjects and fronting, while Ibn ʿĀshūr applies the restriction directly. Al-Suyūṭī rebuts al-Falak al-Dāʾir, citing al-Sakkākī; he also traces fuller conditions to ʿAbd al-Qāhir and notes context distinguishes mere emphasis from restriction.
  27:36/q19/p2 Some rhetoricians question whether mentioning or fronting a subject itself always conveys exclusivity. — reasons: Al-Falak al-Dāʾir reportedly denies the rule, and the author of al-Īḍāḥ objects to al-Sakkākī; Ibn al-Athīr and Ibn al-Nafīs instead emphasize a preposed predicate. Al-Suyūṭī rejects the denial.
  27:36/q19/p3 In related constructions, an indefinite subject can restrict by kind or number. — reasons: Al-Suyūṭī supplies 'a man, not a woman' or 'one man, not two' as analogies for subject-based restriction.
27:36/q20 What did Solomon display and solve before returning the gift? [فَلَمَّا جَآءَ بِهَدِيَّتِكُمْ · reports] (turns on: The narrative reports differ in objects, numbers, methods, and the envoys' identity.)
  27:36/q20/p1 He solved the box, bead, staff, water, and disguised-attendant tests, then sent the gift back. — reasons: Muqātil says Gabriel disclosed the box and horse sweat supplied the water. Al-Thaʿlabī describes a termite and white worm threading the pearl and bead, the attendants' washing styles, and return. Al-Māwardī gives the staff's ground-first end and horse sweat; Ibn Lahiʿa adds three questions, including a forgotten question about God's color. Bursevī's al-Kāshifī gives a washing variant.
  27:36/q20/p2 He staged a vast court of go
