9:103/q20 What does purifying them through charity mean? [تُطَهِّرُهُمْ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the object of cleansing is sin, conduct, property, or inward attachment.)
  9:103/q20/p1 The charity removes sins, including the stain of staying behind and attachment to wealth. — reasons: Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and many successors identify cleansing of sin; al-Bayḍāwī adds attachment to property as the source of recurrence. Abū ʿAlī compares removing people's moral refuse to cleansing and distinguishes legal impurity from bodily dirt, citing 9:28. Al-Qurṭubī's lexicon defines ṭahūr as pure and purifying. The Study Quran cites prophetic purification with teaching in 2:129, 2:151, 3:164 and 62:2.
  9:103/q20/p2 Purification is obedience and sincerity. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās glosses it thus in al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Abī Ḥātim; the action exhibits repentance.
  9:103/q20/p3 In an inward reading it removes attachment to created things and egoic darkness. — reasons: Al-Sulamī reports purification from the stain of created things; Ruwaym describes cleansing inner secrets and refining souls. Al-Qāshānī says wealth feeds desires and charity detaches the soul from them.
  9:103/q20/p4 The alms reform the people. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī reports this interpretation.
  9:103/q20/p5 The purification pertains to their wealth itself. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī reports this alternative referent.
9:103/q21 How are the two purification verbs parsed? [تُطَهِّرُهُمْ وَتُزَكِّيهِم بِهَا · grammar] (turns on: Whether the feminine tāʾ refers to ṣadaqa or addresses the Prophet, and whether the verbs are raised or jussive.)
  9:103/q21/p1 Both verbs address the Prophet, who purifies and develops them by taking charity. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj, Abū Isḥāq, Abū ʿAlī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ālūsī, and later commentators prefer connected second-person address; both can be circumstantial, adjectival with omitted 'by it', or resumed. The presence of bihā strongly favors address in al-Zamakhsharī's argument. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Nasafī say the second verb must be address. Al-Samīn lists syntactic combinations.
  9:103/q21/p2 The first verb describes charity; the second addresses the Prophet or starts a new clause. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī prefers the adjective for the first because the canonical readers raise it, and a new clause for the raised second. Basran grammarians and al-Akhfash allow repetition for emphasis; others make the second circumstantial. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Samīn object to Makkī's mixed parse under coordination, and Abū Ḥayyān says it needs a rare implied subject.
  9:103/q21/p3 The verbs can be jussive as the result of the imperative. — reasons: Kufan grammarians favor jussive for a Prophet-addressed verb lacking a pronoun returning to charity; al-Zajjāj gives 'if you take, you purify' and al-Naḥḥās quotes a poetic parallel. Others note the actual raised reading is circumstantial or adjectival.
  9:103/q21/p4 Both verbs can describe charity itself, with bihā emphasizing the charitable cause. — reasons: Al-Akhfash, Basran grammarians, al-Naḥḥās, and al-Qurṭubī allow feminine charity as subject; al-Ṭabrisī and al-Nasafī constrain the second verb to direct address.
9:103/q22 What variant forms of the purification verbs are reported? [تُطَهِّرُهُمْ وَتُزَكِّيهِم بِهَا · readings] (turns on: The transmitted case and verbal form, including causative and jussive variants.)
  9:103/q22/p1 Al-Ḥasan reads a lighter causative form IV of the first verb. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī derive it from aṭhara; some describe a silent ṭāʾ or light initial form.
  9:103/q22/p2 Both verbs, or the first, are read in the jussive as the command's result. — reasons: Muslima ibn Muḥārib and Ibn Muḥārib are named for both; al-Kashshāf and later sources report the jussive. Al-Ālūsī reports Muslima's tazakkahum without yāʾ.
  9:103/q22/p3 The common reading raises the first verb and retains the yāʾ in the second. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Jishumī say the nominative is circumstantial, not resultative; al-Zajjāj and Ibn al-Jawzī insist the yāʾ follows the muṣḥaf. Al-Rāzī reports the transmitted yāʾ in both verbs.
9:103/q23 What does 'you sanctify or grow them by it' mean? [وَتُزَكِّيهِم بِهَا · interpretation] (turns on: Whether growth refers to status, virtue, wealth, or inward development.)
  9:103/q23/p1 It elevates them from the rank of hypocrites to sincere believers and increases good deeds. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī and others give the rank change; al-Ālūsī warns that it implies actual hypocrisy, contrary to his sounder view. Al-Biqāʿī and al-Bayḍāwī speak of increased deeds.
  9:103/q23/p2 It intensifies cleansing, makes them upright, or adorns them with virtues after sins are removed. — reasons: Al-Ḥasan, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī and Kur'an Yolu gloss intensification. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes removal of sins from acquiring virtues and reward; Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares pruning a tree to promote fruit.
  9:103/q23/p3 It increases or blesses their wealth despite payment reducing it. — reasons: Al-Rāzī explains the paradox of increase through giving; al-Baghawī and al-Nasafī record wealth growth.
  9:103/q23/p4 In an inward reading it refines souls and cleanses inner secrets. — reasons: Ruwaym and al-Qāshānī understand it as spiritual growth after detachment.
9:103/q24 What does 'pray for them' require in meaning? [وَصَلِّ عَلَيْهِمْ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the command is ordinary supplication, funeral prayer, divine acceptance, or an inward act.)
  9:103/q24/p1 It means supplicate for them, especially seeking forgiveness, blessing, mercy, and acceptance. — reasons: Muqātil, al-Farrāʾ, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ṭabarī, al-Shāfiʿī, and most commentators gloss it as prayer for forgiveness or blessing. Arabic ṣalāt means supplication; al-Aʿshā's wine-jar poem and another protection poem illustrate it. Al-ʿĀshūr distinguishes the Prophet's prayer from God's mercy. Some explain ʿalayhim as conveying kindness. Abū ʿUbayda and Ibn Fāris offer several derivations of the ritual prayer's name, from supplication, bowing, the horse-tail place, or softened wood.
  9:103/q24/p2 It can mean perform funeral prayer for them. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān lists this possibility; al-Ālūsī considers ordinary supplication more apparent despite a report from Ibn ʿAbbās. Yazīd ibn Thābit's report calls the Prophet's prayer over the dead mercy.
  9:103/q24/p3 In an inward reading it directs Prophetic spiritual aspiration and light toward them. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī treats it as an outpouring of companionship; al-Sulamī reads it as turning hearts to the afterlife and away from the world.
  9:103/q24/p4 It means accepting them and turning toward them in repentance. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī reports this gloss from Ibn ʿAbbās.
9:103/q25 What did the Prophet actually say or do when praying for donors? [وَصَلِّ عَلَيْهِمْ · reports] (turns on: Reports distinguish habitual donor prayer, a suggested collection formula, and the specific penitents.)
  9:103/q25/p1 He prayed for contributors, including the family of Abū Awfā, and sometimes said 'O God, bless them'. — reasons: The Abū Awfā reports occur across Ibn Qutayba, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Durr, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī; al-Ṭabrisī notes Bukhārī and Muslim, while al-Qurṭubī cites Muslim. Jābir and his wife also received a prayer on request. A report says prayer reaches a man, child and grandchild.
  9:103/q25/p2 The collector can ask God to reward what was given and bless what remains. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, and al-Shāfiʿī give this or a close formula; al-Ālūsī adds 'make it purification for you'.
  9:103/q25/p3 After revelation, the Prophet prayed and sought forgiveness for the repentants. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this.
9:103/q26 Is prayer for a charity donor required of the collector or recipient? [وَصَلِّ عَلَيْهِمْ · law] (turns on: The imperative's force and whether donor request, type of gift, or recipient's office changes it.)
  9:103/q26/p1 The prayer is recommended, not required. — reasons: Al-Māwardī argues God rewards charity, not the collector; al-Ālūsī prefers recommendation in all cases. The Prophet's practice establishes it as the collector's sunnah in al-Nasafī and others.
  9:103/q26/p2 The collector or imam must pray for the donor. — reasons: Al-Māwardī cites the imperative's apparent force; al-Baghawī records the duty. Bashīr ibn al-Khaṣāṣiyya even advises those overcharged by collectors to gather assets and obtain their prayer.
  9:103/q26/p3 It is required for obligatory zakat but recommended for voluntary charity. — reasons: Al-Baghawī and al-Ālūsī report this reverse distinction.
  9:103/q26/p4 It is required of the ruler but recommended of a poor recipient. — reasons: Al-Māwardī says presenting to the ruler displays obedience deserving thanks; al-Baghawī records the same division.
  9:103/q26/p5 It is required for voluntary charity but recommended for obligatory zakat. — reasons: Al-Māwardī records this distinction.
  9:103/q26/p6 It is recommended for a donor to the ruler but required of a poor recipient. — reasons: Al-Māwardī says the poor recipient has not been individually designated.
  9:103/q26/p7 It is required when the donor asks, recommended otherwise. — reasons: Al-Māwardī gives this sixth view; Abū Awfā requested prayer in one report.
9:103/q27 Was the command to pray for them abrogated? [وَصَلِّ عَلَيْهِمْ · sciences] (turns on: Whether 9:84 addresses the same persons and practice as 9:103.)
