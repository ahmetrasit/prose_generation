9:103/q38 How is the word 'sakan' read or construed? [سَكَنٌۭ · grammar] (turns on: The noun's active or passive force and a consonantal reading variant.)
  9:103/q38/p1 It is an active sense: something that calms them. — reasons: Al-Samīn favors this because the verse says 'for them'; a passive noun would normally require 'to it'. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the wording metonymy for the cause of tranquility.
  9:103/q38/p2 It is a passive-form noun, like a thing spent or broken. — reasons: Abū al-Baqāʾ explains its masculine form this way; al-Bursevī repeats a passive-form analogy.
  9:103/q38/p3 A reading gives the kāf of sakan sukūn. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī reports the variant.
9:103/q39 What does the final statement say God hears and knows? [وَٱللَّهُ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the closing attributes refer chiefly to the penitents' speech or the Prophet's supplication.)
  9:103/q39/p1 He hears the penitents' request, confession, repentance and speech, and knows their remorse, intentions, grief and sincerity. — reasons: Muqātil emphasizes their request to have wealth accepted; al-Ṭabarī includes all creatures' speech, and al-Wāḥidī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Qāshānī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursevī and others specify remorse. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this reassures all hearers charged with duties.
  9:103/q39/p2 He hears the Prophet's prayer and knows its object, worthiness, reforming intent and wise response. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī includes what the Prophet seeks for them, Ibn Kathīr says God knows who deserves it, and Ibn ʿĀshūr says hearing hints at answering prayer and knowing at its good effect. Al-Biqāʿī stresses the Prophet's sincerity; al-Ālūsī adds the divine wisdom required.
  9:103/q39/p3 He hears their confession and knows how they handle charity. — reasons: Al-Ṭabrisī gives this paired explanation.
9:103/q40 How does the command connect to the surrounding verses? [* · links] (turns on: Whether the connection is the penitents' practical repair, visible obedience, or a wider closing summary.)
  9:103/q40/p1 It follows the confession and repentance in 9:102 and repairs the missed Tabūk contribution. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says repentance repairs what can be repaired: they missed fighting and spending, and Tabūk depleted communal funds. Al-Biqāʿī sees the partial taking as compassionate confirmation of acceptance. Al-Rāzī's expiation advocates argue a new general zakat order would interrupt this link; zakat advocates answer that willing payment tests repentance.
  9:103/q40/p2 It relates to the visible duties by which belief can be known, unlike concealed hypocrisy. — reasons: Al-Karmānī and Durra al-Tanzīl contrast inward hypocrisy known to God and outward obedience seen by God, the Messenger and believers.
  9:103/q40/p3 The final names confirm the prayer's rationale and may conclude the preceding two verses. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī offers both scopes for the closure.
9:103/q41 What does the verse suggest about repentance and inward reform? [* · inward]
  9:103/q41/p1 The Prophet's presence and aspiration convey tranquility and purify egoic attachment. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī says wealth fuels desires and spiritual training first weakens them; his attention and companionship bring a settled light to the heart. Al-Sulamī reports turning hearts from the world toward the afterlife.
  9:103/q41/p2 True repentance requires admission, humility, tears, forgiveness-seeking, and leaving deliberate sin. — reasons: Al-Bursevī treats confession as Adam's legacy, reports his two hundred years of weeping, cites Ḥāfiẓ's verse on Adam's sin, urges blessings upon the Prophet and prayer for believers, defines divine forgiveness as concealment, and says mistakes and forgetfulness are pardoned.
