  1:1/q30/p2 Al-Raḥmān concerns this world; al-Raḥīm concerns the next. — reasons: THALABI (يحيى بن معاذ الرازي): Yaḥyā ibn Muʿādh says al-Raḥmān provides for worldly livelihoods and al-Raḥīm for the afterlife. THALABI (مجاهد): Mujāhid says al-Raḥmān is for the people of this world and al-Raḥīm for the people of the afterlife. BAGHAWI (author): The invocation “Rahman of this world, Rahim of the hereafter” reflects universal Rahman but particular Rahim; people may call another merciful but not Rahman. TABRISI (عيسى ابن مريم): A prophetic report quotes Jesus as saying Rahman is merciful in this world and Rahim is merciful in the afterlife. BAYDAWI (author): By quantity, al-Raḥmān is worldly mercy shared by believers and unbelievers, while al-Raḥīm is afterlife mercy for believers. NASAFI (author): A supplication calls God Rahman of this world because mercy there reaches believer and unbeliever, and Rahim of the afterlife because there it is specific to believers. ALUSI (author): One distinction makes raḥmān’s worldly mercy encompass believers and unbelievers and raḥīm’s afterlife mercy favor believers; al-Ālūsī questions the allocation and its assumptions. ALUSI-FULL (بعضهم): Some say Rahman is more intensive by the number of recipients, covering believers and unbelievers in worldly life, while Rahim is reserved for believers in the afterlife. ELMALILI (author): A transmitted explanation calls God the Rahmān of this world and Rahīm of the afterlife, or the Rahmān of believers and unbelievers but Rahīm only of believers; the author clarifies that God is both names in both realms. | mentions 33:43
  1:1/q30/p3 Al-Raḥmān is more intensive or comprehensive than al-Raḥīm. — reasons: IBNATIYYA-FULL (author): Rahman marks mercy at its utmost limit, is used only of God, and is more emphatic than rahim, which is more emphatic than rāḥim. BAYDAWI (author): Al-Raḥmān is more emphatic than al-Raḥīm because the expanded form indicates greater meaning by quantity or quality. NASAFI-FULL (author): Rahman and Rahim are both derived from mercy, but Rahman is more emphatic because it has two augmentative patterns versus one in Rahim; added form signals added meaning. ABUHAYYAN (قيل): One view says the two names are synonymous; another distinguishes them, making raḥmān more emphatic and encompassing major blessings while raḥīm completes it with subtle blessings. ABUHAYYAN (ثعلب): Thaʿlab says raḥmān is more emphatic in praise and raḥīm is gentler. SAMIN-DURR (الزمخشري): Al-Zamakhshari and the author hold Rahman to be more intensive and exclusive to God; despite normal progression from weaker to stronger, Rahim follows to cover subtler blessings after Rahman covers great ones. ELMALILI (author): Rahman is more emphatic in encompassing every creature, while Rahim is more emphatic in actual beneficence, which also operates through servants.
