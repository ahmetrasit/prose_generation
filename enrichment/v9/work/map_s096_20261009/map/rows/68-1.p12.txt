[ELMALILI:68:1-6#3/r16] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | One should not insist that a single possible sense is the intended one; reports about familiar meanings should not be rejected when they are not very weak and have intelligible, useful interpretations.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#3/r17] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Elmalılı reads Ibn ʿAbbās’s multiple reports as separate possible aspects, each offered with a benefit, rather than as mutually exclusive claims to the sole intended meaning.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#3/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İbn Atıyye ve Râzî | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Rāzī cite an anonymous poet’s use of nūn as evidence for the sense “inkpot.”
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#3/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | On the inkpot reading, the oath is by inkpot and pen because writing is done with them; books and writing are highly important and useful, though the pen alone conveys this too.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#3/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Muâviye b. Kurre | reports | Muʿāwiya b. Qurra is said to transmit as a Prophetic report that nūn is a tablet of light.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#3/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Elmalılı suggests the light-tablet image may derive from the sun or earth appearing as a n-shaped point in the sky’s arc.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#3/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İbn Abbas | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās is also reported to identify nūn as the final letter of the name al-Raḥmān.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#3/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Ca‘fer-i Sâdık | reports | Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq is reported to say that nūn is a river in Paradise.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#3/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The lexicon also gives nūn as inkpot, fish, sword-edge, a fish-shaped sword, a correct word, and the dimple in an infant’s chin (nūna).
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#3/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Since the feminine suffix marks singularity, nūn can be a generic noun for sword and the oath could be by sword and pen, although no report supports this reading here.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#4/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Elmalılı says to recall reports about the first-created pen when considering nūn, yet its intended sense remains among the ambiguous matters whose interpretation only God knows (3:7). | mentions 3:7
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#4/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Sâhib-Keşşâf | reports | The Kashshāf says the oath honors the Pen because its creation and design display immense wisdom and its benefits exceed description.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#4/r11] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İbn Atıyye | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya says those who identify nūn as Behmūt take the Pen to be the cosmic pen God created and commanded to write the universe, with yasturun’s pronoun referring to angels.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#4/r12] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İbn Abbas | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās explicitly identifies the subject of yasturun as people when the Pen means the familiar human writing tool.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#4/r13] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İbn Atıyye | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya treats the oath as covering writing as a whole, the foundation of knowledge and worldly and afterlife affairs; the Pen is the tongue’s sibling and a universal divine gift.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#4/r14] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İmâm Râzî | reports | Al-Rāzī’s generic reading includes pens used in heaven and earth: God’s gifts of speech and writing (55:3–4; 96:3–5) let people communicate beyond those nearby. | mentions 55:3, 55:4, 96:3, 96:4, 96:5
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#4/r15] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İmâm Râzî | reports | Al-Rāzī’s second view is the particular first-created Pen, a luminous pen stretching between heaven and earth that writes events, lifespans, and deeds until the Hour.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#4/r16] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Kādî (Ebû Bekr Bâkıllânî olmalı) | reports | The Qāḍī, probably Abū Bakr al-Bāqillānī, reads the command to the Pen metaphorically: a writing tool is not a living, accountable intellect, so “write” means God brings events about by effective power, as in 2:117. | mentions 2:117
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#4/r17] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İmâm Râzî | reports | Al-Rāzī reports the claim that the Pen is intellect, the source of creatures: reports naming the first creation as Pen, intellect, or a primordial substance are reconciled as describing one thing.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#4/r18] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Kādî Beyzâvî | reports | Al-Bayḍāwī summarizes al-qalam as either the writer of the Tablet or the instrument used for writing; it is sworn by because its benefits are numerous.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#4/r19] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Suyûtî | reports | Al-Jalālayn says the Pen is that by which the universe was written on the Preserved Tablet; Elmalılı says al-qalam could sound generic, but al-Suyūṭī intends a particular known Pen.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#4/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Although the recitation makes nūn’s literal identity as a disjoint letter certain, this does not remove ambiguity: its relation to pen and writing must be inferred, making it a symbol with further possible senses.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#4/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | It cannot be determined whether nūn itself contains an oath; if it does, the wāw before al-qalam is oath wāw, while the wāw before mā is connective.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#4/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Commentators give two analyses of al-qalam: the definite article may mark a particular known pen (ʿahd) or the genus of pens.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#4/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İbn Cerîr | reports | Ibn Jarīr identifies the known pen as the one God created and commanded to write what would occur through the Resurrection.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#4/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Resûlullah | reports | The Prophet reports that God first created the Pen and ordered it to write the decree, which it recorded for all that had happened and would happen forever; ʿUbāda’s deathbed counsel to his son stresses faith in good and bad destiny.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#4/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Mücâhid | reports | Mujāhid glosses “nūn and the Pen” as that by which remembrance is written.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#4/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Elmalılı concludes that the referential Pen is the one that writes the Preserved Tablet or the Qurʾān.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#4/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Other commentators take al-qalam generically, as in “taught by the pen” (96:4). | mentions 96:4
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#5/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The particular Pen also belongs to the genus; the familiar human pen taught in 96:4 is the plain example, and human writing has recorded thought and memory from creation onward and will continue. | mentions 96:4
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#5/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Yasturun is a sound masculine plural, normally used for rational beings; attributing writing to an unmentioned rational plural behind the singular Pen points to its unseen intelligent users as the real writers.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#5/r11] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The visible tool signifies its hidden rational user; the Pen stands for the writing it produces, and the connective wāw joins an oath encompassing the Pen, writings, and their intelligent authors.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#5/r12] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | From pen and writing one should infer the world of intellects and meanings, then the First Pen that writes them into human intellect, and finally its Lord, the Lord of the worlds.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#5/r13] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | For the human Pen, its writers are rational people—consciousness and intelligence rather than visible bodies; for the Highest Pen, angels write forms of things and meanings, while God writes them.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#5/r14] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The pronoun in yasturun can refer to human writers with the human Pen or to angels with the Highest Pen, explaining why commentaries give either “people” or “angels.”
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#5/r15] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Turkish lacks the sound masculine plural form, so “the Pen and what they write” does not express the shift from the instrument to its intelligent users.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#5/r16] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Yasturun is imperfective and indicates continuity into the present and future; Turkish translations cannot capture all of this tense and aspect nuance.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#5/r17] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The Highest Pen was active when these verses descended and continues to write; the report that the Pen has dried means what has occurred is written, not that writing of future events has ceased.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#5/r18] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Complete finality in decree pertains to God’s knowledge, the Mother of the Book; with respect to the Preserved Tablet and created existence, God erases and establishes (13:39). | mentions 13:39
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#5/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Even when the oath refers to one particular Pen, it honors the genus of pens and therefore also gives dignity to the human pen.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#5/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | With a general audience, the human pen would be the apparent sense, as in 96:4–5; under a particular reference, one or several pens may be intended, and metaphor may point to the primordial pen of nature. | mentions 96:4, 96:5
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#5/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | prefers | Because the address is specifically to Muḥammad, recipient of divine favor and noble character, who neither read nor wrote before revelation nor knew the Book (29:48; 42:52), Elmalılı prefers the divine primordial Pen, also called the First or Highest Pen; he finds this view in Ibn Jarīr and al-Jalālayn. | mentions 29:48, 42:52
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#5/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | In uṣūl terms, al-qalam explicitly denotes the divine Pen and indicates the human pen by implication; those saying “genus” encompass both literal and metaphorical senses but do not distinguish explicit text from apparent indication.
