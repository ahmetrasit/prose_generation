20:132/q35 What is the rhetorical point of the comparison with night in the ancillary notes? [* · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the comparison takes only universal reach or also a connotation of pursuit.)
  20:132/q35/p1 Night illustrates universal reach without importing its darkness into the comparison. — reasons: The Prophet's report says Islam will reach wherever night reaches; Jurjānī explains that an incidental unfavorable feature need not be counted. He compares the sun as apt for a welcome blessing and says even seemingly harsh praise can be hyperbolic rather than insulting.
  20:132/q35/p2 The choice of night may also suggest someone evading a resentful pursuer. — reasons: Jurjānī says day and night both reach everywhere, but offers this possible defense of night as the chosen image.
20:132/q36 What general treatments of hamza are described in the ancillary notes? [* · readings] (turns on: The notes distinguish phonetic practice and exceptions from disputed reports about early pronunciation.)
  20:132/q36/p1 Hamza may be softened by transfer of its vowel or substitution as a long vowel, with stated exceptions. — reasons: Itqān says hamza is hard to articulate and Quraysh and the Hijaz commonly softened it, often in Ibn Kathīr, Warsh from Nāfiʿ, and Abū ʿAmr. Warsh's naql transfers the vowel to a preceding still final consonant, as in qad aflaḥa; Yaʿqūb's transmitters from Warsh retain hamza in kitābiyah innī ẓanantu. Abū ʿAmr's ibdāl changes still hamza to a long vowel. The chapter selects four broad kinds but the excerpt details two.
  20:132/q36/p2 Hamza is retained where stillness, difficulty, or ambiguity requires it. — reasons: Itqān says jussive and construct-state stillness, or a harder and ambiguous result from dropping hamza, preserve it; moving hamza is always verified.
  20:132/q36/p3 Reports claim the Prophet and early caliphs omitted hamza, or distinguish nabī from nabīʾ. — reasons: The Ibn ʿUmar report via Nāfiʿ claims nonpronunciation until later; Abū Shāma rejects it because Mūsā ibn ʿUbayda is weak. An Abū Dharr report says the Prophet corrected nabīʾ; al-Dhahabī calls it anomalous and Ḥumrān untrustworthy.
