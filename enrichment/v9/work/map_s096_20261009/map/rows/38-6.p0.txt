[MUQATIL:38:5-6/r2] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | reports | The chiefs were twenty-seven nobles, including al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra, Abū Jahl, Umayya, and Ubayy ibn Khalaf.
[MUQATIL:38:5-6/r3] MUQATIL, d. 150 · الوليد بن المغيرة | reports | Al-Walīd told them to go to Abū Ṭālib and remain firm in worship of their gods; the passage compares this firmness with 25:42 and God's answer in 41:24. | mentions 25:42, 41:24
[MUQATIL:38:5-6/r4] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | reports | The chiefs approached Abū Ṭālib, calling him their elder and leader, and asked him to arbitrate between them and his nephew.
[MUQATIL:38:5-6/r5] MUQATIL, d. 150 · أبو طالب | reports | Abū Ṭālib summoned the Prophet and asked him to grant his people a fair settlement instead of leaning wholly against them.
[MUQATIL:38:5-6/r6] MUQATIL, d. 150 · قريش | reports | Quraysh asked Muhammad to stop mentioning their gods, promising to leave him and his God alone.
[MUQATIL:38:5-6/r7] MUQATIL, d. 150 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Muhammad offered them a single word that would make Arabs obey them and non-Arabs submit; Abū Jahl agreed to give that and ten more.
[MUQATIL:38:5-6/r8] MUQATIL, d. 150 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Muhammad asked them to say there is no god but God; they recoiled, left, and complained that he would make their many gods one.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابآخرمنأبوابإن)#506/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | After حتى, إنّ can begin an independent clause and is not governed by حتى, just as a nominal sentence follows حتى زيد ذاهب.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابآخرمنأبوابإن)#506/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Treating حتى أن as a subordinate verbal noun would be unsuitable here: حتى الانطلاق or حتى الخبر would turn the clause into a report, so it is parsed as an initial clause.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابآخرمنأبوابإن)#506/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | فإذا إنه likewise begins a new clause; the cited verse-like line means فإذا هو عبد القفا واللهازم, while فإذا أنه عبد can instead mean that his state is servitude and baseness.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابآخرمنأبوابإن)#506/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | Al-Khalīl parses حتى أنك أحمق as though one said حتى حمقك, then placed أن in that nominal position.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابآخرمنأبوابإن)#506/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | Al-Khalīl rejects كما أنك on the pattern كما أنت because إن cannot begin every clause; يوم الجمعة إنك ذاهب and كيف إنك صانع are not allowed.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتكسرفيهأوائلالأفعالالمضارعةللأسماءكماكسرتثاني#712/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Sībawayh uses انطلق as an inchoative-pattern example: imperfect forms in related derived patterns take kasra in their initial prefixes by analogy with forms beginning with hamzat al-waṣl, although that alif was omitted for ease.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتكونفيهأنبمنزلةأي)#511/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | Al-Khalīl analyzes أن امشوا in the verse as explanatory أي: they went out saying ‘Go and persevere’, not as saying they went out by walking.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتكونفيهأنبمنزلةأي)#511/r10] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | When lightened, أن retains its governing force: poetic كأن وريديه رشاء خلب and أن هالك كل من يحفى وينتعل are understood with the omitted heavy particle and its pronoun.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتكونفيهأنبمنزلةأي)#511/r11] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The poetic nominative كأن وريداه is possible with an understood pronoun, by analogy with إنّه من يأتها تعطه or a clause such as كأن ظبية تعطو.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتكونفيهأنبمنزلةأي)#511/r12] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Sībawayh says treating the lightened construction like إنما or لكن after deleting the pronoun would also be a strong analysis.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتكونفيهأنبمنزلةأي)#511/r13] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | أن بسم الله is understood as أنه بسم الله because no overt subject has been introduced; the implied pronoun accounts for the construction.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتكونفيهأنبمنزلةأي)#511/r14] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The hidden pronoun is confirmed by the awkwardness of أن يقول ذاك without أن لا or a future marker; if أن were an initial particle, the following verb would be indicative.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتكونفيهأنبمنزلةأي)#511/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The same explanatory construction appears in ما قلت لهم إلا ما أمرتني به أن اعبدوا الله, where أن introduces the content of the command. | mentions 5:117
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتكونفيهأنبمنزلةأي)#511/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In كتبت إليه أن افعل and أمرته أن قم, one analysis makes أن the subjunctive particle linked to an imperative, as الذي links to a verb addressed to the listener.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتكونفيهأنبمنزلةأي)#511/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The ability to add بَـ in أوعزت إليه بأن افعل supports the subjunctive-particle analysis; if أن were أي, the preposition could not attach as it does to nouns.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتكونفيهأنبمنزلةأي)#511/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A second analysis of كتبت إليه أن افعل makes أن explanatory أي, like its use after انطلق in the verse.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتكونفيهأنبمنزلةأي)#511/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In وآخر دعواهم أن الحمد لله and أن لا إله إلا الله, أن is heavy with an understood pronoun, as if أنه الحمد لله; it is neither subjunctive أن nor explanatory أي. | mentions 10:10
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتكونفيهأنبمنزلةأي)#511/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In وناديناه أن يا إبراهيم قد صدقت الرؤيا, the clause means ناديناه أنك قد صدقت الرؤيا يا إبراهيم. | mentions 37:104-105
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتكونفيهأنبمنزلةأي)#511/r8] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | Al-Khalīl allows the call construction also to be understood as أي; أرسل إليه أن ما أنت وذا is likewise explanatory, while بأنك ما أنت وذا shows the heavy form in use.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتكونفيهأنبمنزلةأي)#511/r9] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | For the reading أن غضب الله عليها in the fifth oath, Sībawayh understands heavy أن with its pronoun omitted; it is not light أن before a noun unless that pronoun is intended. | mentions 24:9
