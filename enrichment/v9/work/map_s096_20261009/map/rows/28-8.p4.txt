[ALUSI-FULL:v20p47/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | If it means error, the closing clause is parenthetical and reinforces the error already implied by the ironic claim that they raised an enemy and grief; if it means sin, it reinforces their guilt.

[ELMALILI:28:7-13/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Their being sinners explains why Moses would become their enemy and grief: God afflicted them in this way, and otherwise they would not have taken him in.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v20p74/r10] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The lām is the preposition lām kay, governing a verbal noun understood from أن plus the subjunctive verb and ordinarily marking the agent's motivating cause.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v20p74/r11] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The family adopted Moses from compassion, not to make him their enemy; because enmity and grief were the outcome, the result is metaphorically phrased as the cause, a dependent metaphor according to most rhetoricians, unlike al-Sakkaki.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v20p74/r8] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Al-iltiqāt is the act of picking up something cast on the ground or elsewhere, whether deliberately or inadvertently.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v20p74/r9] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The act is attributed to Pharaoh's household because one of the women around Pharaoh's daughter retrieved Moses's chest from the Nile, as Exodus chapter 2 relates.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v20p76/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The pronoun refers to Pharaoh's ruling household as the dynasty, state, or nation; Moses became the enemy of a later Pharaoh and his regime.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v20p76/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Ḥuznan is a verbal noun with an omitted relation meaning grief for them; it is not a source noun used as an adjective for emphasis, and attributing grief to Moses is a causal metaphor.

[IBNASHUR-FULL:v20p76/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Their criminality explains God's making Moses their enemy and grief, as punishment for oppressing the Israelites and worshipping idols.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v20p76/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Khāṭiʾ is the active participle of khaṭiʾa, to commit a sin; its verbal noun is khaṭʾ, distinct from khaṭaʾ, which means unintentional error and comes from أخطأ. | mentions 96:16, 17:31, 33:5

[IBNASHUR-FULL:v20p76/r7] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · أبو عبيد وابن قتيبة | reports | Abu Ubayd and Ibn Qutayba instead treat the two forms as synonyms or dialect variants; al-Zamakhshari's explanation here appears to follow them.
