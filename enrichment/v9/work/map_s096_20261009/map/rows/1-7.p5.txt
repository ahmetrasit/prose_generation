[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p57/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · ابن كثير | reports | Ibn Kathīr joins the plural mim to a wāw after either a kasra or damma on hāʾ.

[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p57/r4] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · ورش | reports | Warsh keeps hāʾ kasra and mim still, but inserts a wāw when an original alif follows, as in sawāʾ ʿalayhumu.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p57/r5] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | reports | Abū ʿAmr, ʿĀṣim, Ibn ʿĀmir, and al-Kisāʾī generally use kasra hāʾ and still mim; before a following sukūn, readers differ over mim and sometimes hāʾ.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p57/r6] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أحمد بن موسى | holds | Aḥmad b. Mūsā says the variation of hāʾ depends on whether it follows kasra or silent yāʾ; elsewhere it is damma, while mim is damma or still.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p59/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · سيبويه | reports | Sibawayh reports that some say ʿalayhumu by assimilating to the preceding yāʾ; Abū Ḥātim identifies it as al-Aʿraj’s reading.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p59/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | reports | One defense says hāʾ shares yāʾ’s articulation, so the hāʾ should follow the yāʾ; another treats hāʾ as related to yāʾ by its articulation.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p59/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · عاصم وأبو عمرو والكسائي وابن عامر | reports | Their kasra-hāʾ, still-mim reading deletes wāw because the plural mim itself prevents confusion with the dual, and deletion lightens speech.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p59/r4] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · عاصم وأبو عمرو والكسائي وابن عامر | reports | They preserve kasra on hāʾ because it is the same pronoun hāʾ as in ʿalayhi and its cause for kasra remains unchanged after mim is added.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p59/r5] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · حمزة | reports | Ḥamza treats damma hāʾ as original, like hum when independent; the waw reading is described as old and Qurayshī, Ḥijāzī, and Yemeni eloquent usage.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p59/r6] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · حمزة | reports | Ḥamza limits damma to these three forms because before a visible noun, ʿalā, ladā, and ilā end in alif and hāʾ is not kasra after alif.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p59/r7] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو حاتم | reports | Damma on a mim before a following sukūn restores its original vowel after movement becomes necessary; this is common in the two Holy Cities.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p59/r8] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | reports | Kasra mim before a following sukūn assimilates kasra to kasra, avoiding the heaviness of damma after kasra.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p61/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · الكسائي | reports | Al-Kisāʾī keeps hāʾ kasra unless the mim meets a following sukūn, then restores both hāʾ and mim to damma to avoid switching from kasra to damma.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p61/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | prefers | The author prefers kasra on hāʾ because it is easier to pronounce and is the majority reading.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p61/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · سيبويه | reports | Sibawayh explains kasra hāʾ after yāʾ or kasra by its hidden quality and closeness to yāʾ and alif; adjacent kasra also changes wāw to yāʾ.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p61/r4] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | reports | When the next word begins with sukūn, kasra mim is preferred as lighter; it restores an original vowel, and Ḥasan’s alayhimi preserves the connecting yāʾ.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p63/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Abū ʿAlī argues that hāʾ resembles alif in articulation and hiddenness, so a preceding kasra or yāʾ draws its damma toward kasra as imāla draws alif toward yāʾ.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p63/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | The pronunciation ḍarabtu ḍarbih shows hāʾ can trigger imāla even without a preceding kasra or yāʾ, like imāla in some proper names.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p63/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Hāʾ and alif both clarify a preceding vowel in pause; al-Khalīl permits either hāʾ or alif after a consonant, supporting their parallel treatment in ʿalayhim.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p63/r4] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | The optional hāʾ or alif suffix is allowed because each resembles and can stand for the other; alif also occurs in the same pause pattern after bāʾ, tāʾ, and thāʾ.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p63/r5] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Although hāʾ lacks the sustained sound of alif, their shared hiddenness, weakness, and articulation allow one to be brought near the other.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p64/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Hāʾ can replace other letters in pause just as alif can, another sign of their similarity.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p64/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Pronoun hāʾ’s hiddenness is shown by movements inserted before it in pause, and by their disappearance in liaison when hāʾ itself makes the vowel clear.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p64/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | rejects | The reported phrase lam aḍribihimā is treated as liaison pronounced like pause, not as a general rule for inflectional sukūn; inflectional endings do not assimilate freely.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p64/r4] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | A jussive consonant may move to avoid a following sukūn, as in the cited yattaqihi form, unlike a freely altered inflectional ending.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p67/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | The phrase اشتر لنا سويقا can be explained by treating the liaison as pause, among the analyses of movement before pronoun hāʾ.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p67/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Hāʾ behaves like weak letters: it can be deleted word-finally, as in shāh, shafah, and sanah, and shared rules strengthen the analogy.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p67/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Hāʾ substitutes for yāʾ in ذه/هذه; Ḥijāzī and Tamīmī dialects differ in whether it drops or becomes yāʾ in pause and liaison.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p67/r4] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Hāʾ also alternates with yāʾ under doubling, as in dahdayt for dahdaht and related forms.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p67/r5] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو زيد | reports | Abū Zayd records the forms māhat and tamūhu/tamīhu, and a poetic line using māh as a word for a heart softened by tears.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p67/r6] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Some speakers kasra hāʾ despite an intervening consonant, as in minhum; this weaker analogy still supports kasra when yāʾ is adjacent.

[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p69/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Other Bakrī speakers say bikum and faḍlu aḥlāmikum with kasra on kāf, since kāf shares hāʾ’s whispering and pronoun function.
