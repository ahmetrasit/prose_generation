[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:1#3/r5] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | God's lām is emphatic when preceded by fatḥa or ḍamma; omitting the alif in pronunciation is an error that invalidates prayer and does not form an explicit oath, though poetry may force it.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:1#3/r6] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | الرحمن and الرحيم are intensive forms of mercy; human mercy is tenderness, but divine names refer to the resulting beneficence rather than an emotional state.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:1#3/r7] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | الرحمن is stronger than الرحيم because the added form indicates added meaning; this difference can concern quantity or quality.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:1#3/r8] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | reports | On a quantitative reading, God is “Merciful of this world” to believers and unbelievers alike, and “Compassionate in the next” specifically to believers.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:1#3/r9] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | reports | On a qualitative reading, الرحمن covers both worlds while الرحيم covers this world, since all afterlife blessings are immense but worldly blessings range from great to small.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:1#4/r1] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | prefers | Al-Bayḍāwī's stronger grammatical view is that الرحمن is indeclinable, despite its exclusive association with God and the usual feminine patterns of its class.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:1#4/r2] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | These particular names teach that only the true God deserves to be invoked for every need: He owns all blessings, immediate and future, great and small, and alone enables their delivery and use.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:1/r1] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · قراء مكة والكوفة وفقهاؤهما وابن المبارك والشافعي | reports | They count the basmala as part of al-Fātiḥa and every sura.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:1/r10] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report says that any significant matter begun without the basmala is cut off or incomplete.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:1/r11] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | reports | An alternative takes ب as accompaniment: read while seeking blessing through God's name.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:1/r12] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | The basmala and following words through the sura are placed on the servants' tongues to teach invocation, praise, and asking for grace.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:1/r13] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | The bāʾ is kasra because these particles are obligatorily prepositional, like the kasra of the imperative lām and the genitive lām before nouns.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:1/r14] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · أصحابنا البصريين | reports | The Basrans derive اسم from a shortened form whose ending was dropped through frequent use; its beginning was made still and hamzat al-waṣl added to start it with a vowel.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:1/r2] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · قراء المدينة والبصرة والشام ومالك والأوزاعي | reports | They disagree that the basmala is part of every sura.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:1/r3] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | reports | Abū Ḥanīfa stated no position here, so al-Bayḍāwī infers he did not regard it as part of the sura; Muḥammad al-Ḥasan says all text between the covers is God's speech.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:1/r4] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · أبو هريرة | reports | The Prophet reportedly called the basmala the first of al-Fātiḥa's seven verses.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:1/r5] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · أم سلمة | reports | Umm Salama reports that the Prophet counted the basmala as a verse when reciting al-Fātiḥa.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:1/r6] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | The disagreement concerns whether the basmala is an independent verse or counted with what follows; Muslims agree it is Qur'an and is written in the codices, which were carefully kept free of additions such as آمين.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:1/r7] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | prefers | The prefixed ب is attached to an omitted verb meaning “I read,” since what follows is read; each actor supplies the verb suited to what the naming begins.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:1/r8] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | rejects | “I begin” is less suitable because nothing in the wording matches or indicates it, while “my beginning” requires an extra ellipsis.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:1/r9] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | Putting the governed phrase first gives emphasis, exclusivity, and reverence; the name precedes recitation and makes an act religiously complete, as in the cited verses. | mentions 11:41, 1:5
[NASAFI:1:1/r1] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | prefers | The Fatiha is Makkan, Madinan, or both; Nasafi prefers both: it descended at Mecca when prayer became obligatory and again at Medina when the qibla turned to the Kaaba.
[NASAFI:1:1/r10] NASAFI, d. 710 · قراء مكة والكوفة والشافعي وأصحابه | reports | Meccan and Kufan reciters and al-Shafi‘i’s school count the basmala as a verse of the Fatiha and every sura, and recite it aloud; they argue the early Muslims wrote it in the Qur’an despite orders to exclude non-Qur’anic material.
[NASAFI:1:1/r11] NASAFI, d. 710 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says whoever omits the basmala has omitted 114 verses from God’s Book.
[NASAFI:1:1/r12] NASAFI, d. 710 · أبو هريرة عن النبي | reports | Abu Hurayra reports God’s dialogue dividing the Fatiha between Himself and the servant: praise, glorification, and exaltation belong to God; the worshipper’s request for guidance and the response to it belong to the servant, while worship and aid are shared.
[NASAFI:1:1/r13] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | Since the division report begins with al-hamdu lillah, Nasafi takes it as evidence that the basmala is not part of the Fatiha; he says it is not part of another sura either, by consensus. | mentions 1:2
[NASAFI:1:1/r14] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | Nasafi answers that the basmala is still Qur’an, revealed to separate the suras; its Qur’anic status does not require it to belong to a sura’s verse count.
[NASAFI:1:1/r15] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | The basmala’s bā’ attaches to an omitted verb supplied from context, such as ‘I read’ or ‘I recite’; a traveler, slaughterer, or other actor similarly supplies the action being begun.
[NASAFI:1:1/r16] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | The omitted action comes after the basmala so the invoked name is foregrounded and exclusive: monotheists distinguish their beginning from those who invoked al-Lat and al-‘Uzza.
[NASAFI:1:1/r17] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | In ‘Read in your Lord’s name’ the verb comes first because it opens the first-revealed sura and the command to read is more important; alternatively, read is intransitive, or the following ‘in your Lord’s name’ is the object of a later ‘read.’ | mentions 96:1
[NASAFI:1:1/r18] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | The name relates to recitation as oil relates to growth in ‘it grows with oil’: reciting with God’s name seeks blessing and teaches worshippers to honor it. | mentions 23:20
[NASAFI:1:1/r19] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | The bā’ takes kasra because it is always a particle governing genitive, matching its vowel to its grammatical function.
[NASAFI:1:1/r2] NASAFI, d. 710 · النبي عليه السلام | reports | The Fatiha is called Umm al-Qur’an on the basis of the Prophet’s statement that prayer is invalid without reciting Umm al-Qur’an.
[NASAFI:1:1/r20] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | Ism begins with a silent consonant, so an initial hamza is added when starting speech but omitted in connected speech; some instead move the first consonant and say sim. Its ending is deleted, its original form is sumuw, and its derivation denotes elevation because naming publicizes and honors the named.
[NASAFI:1:1/r21] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | The alif of ism is omitted in this written basmala because it is frequent and unpronounced; the bā’ is lengthened to compensate, and ‘Umar ibn ‘Abd al-‘Aziz instructed his scribe to lengthen the bā’, distinguish the sīns, and round the mīm. | mentions 96:1
[NASAFI:1:1/r22] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | Allah derives from al-ilah, like al-nas from al-unas: the hamza drops and the definite article substitutes for it. Ilah once named any true or false object of worship, then chiefly the true one; Allah without hamza is exclusive to the true God.
[NASAFI:1:1/r23] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | Allah is a name rather than an attribute: one predicates unity and self-sufficiency of God but does not call another thing ‘a god’; divine attributes require a named subject to qualify.
[NASAFI:1:1/r24] NASAFI, d. 710 · الخليل والزجاج ومحمد بن الحسن والحسين بن الفضل | reports | Al-Khalil, al-Zajjaj, Muhammad ibn al-Hasan, and al-Husayn ibn al-Fadl deny that the name Allah has a derivation.
[NASAFI:1:1/r25] NASAFI, d. 710 · قيل | reports | One derivation connects Allah with alih, ‘to be bewildered’: minds are dazed by trying to know God, which explains the spread of error and rarity of sound reflection.
[NASAFI:1:1/r26] NASAFI, d. 710 · قيل | reports | Another derivation makes Allah a verbal noun from alaha, ‘to worship,’ meaning ma’luha, ‘the worshipped’; Nasafi compares this noun-for-participle pattern to ‘God’s creation’ meaning what He created. | mentions 31:11
[NASAFI:1:1/r27] NASAFI, d. 710 · الجمهور | prefers | Most reciters emphasize the lām of Allah after fatḥa or ḍamma and lighten it after kasra; some lighten it in every context and others emphasize it throughout.
[NASAFI:1:1/r28] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | Rahman is fa‘lan from mercy and denotes mercy encompassing everything, like ghadban denotes being filled with anger; rahim is fa‘il. Rahman is more intensive because its form has two augmentations rather than one, and greater length signals greater meaning.
[NASAFI:1:1/r29] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | A supplication calls God Rahman of this world because mercy there reaches believer and unbeliever, and Rahim of the afterlife because there it is specific to believers.
