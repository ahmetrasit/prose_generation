20:49/q10 How does Moses answer this question, and why? [* · interpretation]
  20:49/q10/p1 He answers with a concise proof from God's acts of creating, forming, and guiding creatures, rather than defining a divine genus. — reasons: Al-Ṭabrisī says God has no genus and is known through acts. Kuranyolu says every creature owes its nature, form, guidance and existence to God. Al-Rāzī says this question calls for reasoning, whereas the later question about past generations calls for unseen transmitted knowledge that Moses lacks. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Moses answers because Pharaoh named him.
20:49/q11 How does this question relate to Pharaoh's question in 26:23? [* · links] (turns on: Whether the two formulations mark successive stages of inquiry or both serve Pharaoh's confrontation.)
  20:49/q11/p1 The 'who' question comes first; after Moses establishes God's existence, Pharaoh turns to the difficult 'what' question about essence. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says the who-question naturally precedes the what-question; his preferred reconstruction begins with Pharaoh's lordship claim, then an undeniable proof, then the essence question that people cannot answer. Study Quran attributes this deeper theological sequence to him.
  20:49/q11/p2 Pharaoh's questions in both passages are confrontational challenges. — reasons: Study Quran reports other commentators rejecting the serious theological reconstruction and referring to 26:23-24.
20:49/q12 What does the exchange teach about establishing knowledge of God? [* · theology] (turns on: Whether a messenger or scripture can be the first ground for knowing God before their own authority is established.)
  20:49/q12/p1 Reasoned proof of the Creator precedes proof of a messenger; blind imitation cannot establish knowledge of God. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says Moses establishes the Creator before his own prophethood. He rejects the Taʿlīmiyya appeal to a messenger's statement and the Ḥashwiyya appeal to scripture and Sunnah for the first knowledge of God.
  20:49/q12/p2 Knowledge of God first comes from a messenger's statement. — reasons: Al-Rāzī reports this Taʿlīmiyya claim only to reject it from the order of Moses' argument.
  20:49/q12/p3 Knowledge of God and religion first comes from scripture and Sunnah. — reasons: Al-Rāzī reports this Ḥashwiyya claim only to reject it from the order of Moses' argument.
20:49/q13 How should a believer handle a hostile question such as Pharaoh's? [* · interpretation]
  20:49/q13/p1 Hear the opponent, answer with proof and good admonition, and avoid force or alienation. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says Moses debated despite Pharaoh's power and Pharaoh first avoided violence lest he appear ignorant; even Pharaoh demanded proof. He says a question must be answered so doubt does not remain, cites 16:125 for wise invitation and 9:6 for protection while a polytheist hears God's word. Al-Biqāʿī likewise says Pharaoh chose debate to avoid being called foolish.
20:49/q14 May the words of a false opponent be quoted? [* · interpretation]
  20:49/q14/p1 Yes; the Qur'an quotes Pharaoh and deniers of prophethood and resurrection. — reasons: Al-Rāzī draws the permission from this verse and comparable Qur'anic quotations.
20:49/q15 What do accounts say happened during the brothers' audience with Pharaoh? [* · reports] (turns on: The reports differ in added events, especially Wahb's forty-day delay, which al-Ālūsī compares with 20:59.)
  20:49/q15/p1 They waited for admission, declared their mission, showed the staff and white hand, and Pharaoh's chiefs urged a contest with magicians. — reasons: Al-Durr's account says Pharaoh fled a huge open-mouthed serpent, begged Moses to stop it, and the white hand's leprous appearance vanished in the sleeve. It says the chiefs called the brothers magicians seeking to displace Egyptians and take their kingdom and ease, rejected Moses' request, and sought numerous magicians (26:54).
  20:49/q15/p2 They waited at Pharaoh's gate, then entered after a severe screen was raised. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī attributes this admission account to Ibn ʿAbbās; it adds the screen to the more general waiting in al-Durr.
  20:49/q15/p3 Moses entered fearlessly through trained lions; his serpent killed many, and Pharaoh requested forty days before lions escorted Moses out. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports this from Wahb. He objects that a forty-day reprieve conflicts with Moses' later festival-day appointment (20:59), and says there was apparently only one encounter; he also notes Wahb's focus on Moses could omit Aaron.
  20:49/q15/p4 Asiya urged Pharaoh to accept the brothers' call, while Haman opposed losing mastery and lordship. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī transmits this as a reported account.
  20:49/q15/p5 Accounts beyond revelation should be used only when their soundness is established. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī gives this caution about the encounter stories.
20:49/q16 How does this exchange fit the sūra's account of Moses? [* · links]
  20:49/q16/p1 It continues the account of the brothers' joint mission, signs, the magicians, Israel's escape, and Pharaoh's drowning. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says this stretch had not appeared in earlier sūras and the speech is God's instruction, as Pharaoh's reply confirms. Ṭabāṭabāʾī outlines the mission, magicians' defeat and conversion, escape and drowning. Al-Sāmarrāʾī stresses Aaron's partnership across 20:29, 20:42, 20:63, 20:90 and 20:92.
20:49/q17 What lesson does the passage offer those calling to divine unity? [* · interpretation]
  20:49/q17/p1 They should keep trusting God in every circumstance, as the episodes of Moses' life teach, especially to the Prophet. — reasons: Kuranyolu makes this the passage's lesson.
20:49/q18 What inward state does Pharaoh's question reveal? [* · inward]
  20:49/q18/p1 It reveals the self veiled from its Lord. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī gives this inward reading.
20:49/q19 May the sūras be read in an order different from the familiar codex order? [* · sciences] (turns on: Whether the sūra sequence binds recitation as strictly as the order of verses and letters.)
  20:49/q19/p1 Yes; reading or beginning with any sūra is permissible. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr takes the unordered reading report as predating ʿUthmān's provincial codices and prescribed order. ʿĀʾisha says beginning with any sūra does no harm. A sound report from Ḥudhayfa and Ibn Masʿūd says the Prophet recited al-Baqara, al-Nisāʾ, then Āl ʿImrān at night.
  20:49/q19/p2 No; changing sūra order corrupts an established arrangement like changing verses or letters. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr reports Ibn al-Anbārī's argument.
20:49/q20 Who fixed the order of sūras and verses in the codex? [* · sciences] (turns on: Whether the sūra sequence follows revelation, a fixed inherited order, or ʿUthmān's judgment; verse order is treated separately.)
  20:49/q20/p1 Verse order within each sūra is fixed by the Prophet, while sūra order could vary and the Uthmanic order partly reflects ʿUthmān's judgment. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr says ʿĀʾisha dictated verse order instead of allowing rearrangement. He cites Ibn ʿAbbās' question about the missing basmala before Barāʾa and al-Anfāl's classification among long sūras as evidence of ʿUthmān's judgment, calling its chain good and strong.
  20:49/q20/p2 ʿAlī intended to arrange the Qur'an by revelation order. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr reports ʿAlī's resolve; this is a proposed arrangement, not an account of the familiar codex's final form.
20:49/q21 Which first revelation mentioning Paradise and Hell did ʿĀʾisha mean? [* · sciences] (turns on: Whether ʿĀʾisha named one first sūra or a broader early group.)
  20:49/q21/p1 She meant Sūrat Iqraʾ, possibly. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr treats this as a possible referent of ʿĀʾisha's remark.
  20:49/q21/p2 She may instead have meant the category of Mufaṣṣal sūras containing promise and warning. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr offers this if her remark did not mean Sūrat Iqraʾ.
20:49/q22 Why did commands and prohibitions come after the first calls to faith? [* · sciences]
  20:49/q22/p1 People first embraced faith and then received law gradually through divine wisdom and mercy. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr gives this explanation of the revelation sequence.
