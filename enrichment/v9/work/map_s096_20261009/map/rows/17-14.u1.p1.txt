17:14/q14 What is the syntactic role of حَسِيبًا? [حَسِيبًا · grammar] (turns on: Whether the accusative specifies the sufficiency or describes the person's state.)
  17:14/q14/p1 It is an accusative specification, clarifying how the self suffices. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj states tamyīz. Al-Naḥḥās, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī also mention a circumstantial alternative; al-Sarrāj specifically posits an omitted subject for that parse.
  17:14/q14/p2 It is a circumstantial accusative. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās allows ḥāl as well as specification; al-Ṭabrisī attributes this with an omitted subject to al-Sarrāj. Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī report it too.
17:14/q15 Why is حَسِيبًا masculine with feminine نَفْس? [بِنَفْسِكَ حَسِيبًا · grammar] (turns on: Whether agreement follows the referent's sense or the noun's formal shape.)
  17:14/q15/p1 Nafs means a person, so the description follows the person's sense. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī and others also point to male-associated roles such as witness, judge and trustee; al-Samīn cites a poem. Al-Bursawī takes nafs as person; al-Ālūsī also mentions the adjective pattern. Abū Isḥāq permits feminine ḥasība outside Qur'anic wording.
  17:14/q15/p2 Nafs lacks a visible feminine marker, allowing masculine description. — reasons: Ibn al-Anbārī compares sky and earth, citing 73:18; Ibn al-Jawzī and Abū Ḥayyān cite poetry with masculine description of earth, including أبقل.
17:14/q16 How does عَلَيْكَ connect syntactically and semantically? [عَلَيْكَ حَسِيبًا · grammar] (turns on: Whether ḥasīb is construed as an accountant or a sufficient witness.)
  17:14/q16/p1 It depends on the sense of ḥasīb as one who reckons against a person. — reasons: Al-Nasafī cites the idiom 'reckoned this against him'; al-Samīn cites al-Zamakhsharī and examples from Sībawayh; al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī agree. Ibn ʿĀshūr says ʿalayka implies testimony against the person.
  17:14/q16/p2 It marks the matter about which a sufficient witness answers the claimant. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī and al-Samīn give the witness construction as an alternative.
17:14/q17 What does it mean that the person suffices as their own reckoner? [كَفَىٰ بِنَفْسِكَ عَلَيْكَ حَسِيبًا · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the sufficient self-testimony is direct self-accounting or bodily testimony.)
  17:14/q17/p1 One's own knowledge, record and confession suffice to account for and judge one's deeds, proving the judgment fair. — reasons: Muqātil and al-Ṭabarī say no outside witness or recorder is needed; al-Ḥasan repeatedly calls this justice. Al-Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Bursawī say it removes excuses and establishes proof. Ibn Kathīr says the person remembers the deeds and no alien deed appears; Ibn al-Jawzī contrasts Paradise by grace and Hell by sin. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 66:7 for unavoidable evidence.
  17:14/q17/p2 The person's own limbs testify when the tongue denies the deeds. — reasons: Muqātil illustrates this through 6:23 and 75:14; al-Biqāʿī says the record omits nothing and limbs answer denial.
17:14/q18 Who is addressed by the command? [ٱقْرَأْ بِنَفْسِكَ · referent] (turns on: Whether a specific report about an unbeliever limits the general address.)
  17:14/q18/p1 Every person, believer and unbeliever, faces the command and account. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī explicitly rejects restricting it to unbelievers merely because al-Suddī tells an unbeliever's story. Ibn ʿĀshūr points back to the person in 17:13; the believer and unbeliever reports cover both.
  17:14/q18/p2 In al-Suddī's reported episode, an unbeliever asks to audit himself and is told to read. — reasons: The unbeliever invokes God's declaration that He is not unjust to servants. Al-Ālūsī says this episode does not limit the verse's general address.
17:14/q19 What afterlife events does this verse establish? [* · interpretation]
  17:14/q19/p1 There will be a record, resurrection, precise reckoning and just recompense for one's own deeds. — reasons: Al-Jishumī explicitly names afterlife reckoning and records. Al-Ṭabarī says angels keep the register; Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses exhaustive knowledge. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says deeds cling to their doer and their consequences inevitably appear. Iṣlāḥī says nobody bears another's burden; Kuranyolu says each sees the result; Study Quran cites 3:117, 10:44 and 29:40 for people wronging themselves.
17:14/q20 What does the command imply about who creates the act of reading? [ٱقْرَأْ · theology]
  17:14/q20/p1 The command would lose its function if God created the person's act of reading. — reasons: Al-Jishumī uses the imperative itself against the doctrine that God creates the commanded act.
17:14/q21 What does self-accounting imply for responsibility and divine justice? [كَفَىٰ بِنَفْسِكَ حَسِيبًا · theology]
  17:14/q21/p1 People are accountable for their deeds; reckoning proves God does not wrong them. — reasons: Al-Jishumī argues that determinism would make God accountable for the deed, and that creating disbelief while preventing faith would make accounting pointless and unjust. Al-Bursawī says the person's own confession establishes proof. Study Quran says actions lead to salvation or condemnation, while mercy remains beyond calculation.
17:14/q22 How should people prepare now for this self-accounting? [بِنَفْسِكَ حَسِيبًا · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the practical counsel concerns reviewing deeds or prioritizing current acts.)
  17:14/q22/p1 Audit and weigh one's deeds before the final presentation while amendment is possible. — reasons: ʿUmar's repeated counsel adds preparing for the great presentation; al-Bursawī stresses that tomorrow offers no repair. Al-Ḥasan urges a sound answer to questioning or a shroud for the Fire. Al-Sulamī contrasts righteous people reckoned now with wicked people reckoned later. Elmalılı urges daily examination. A Kashf al-Asrār report's king makes his self recount each evening's words, deeds, movements and stillness, which it finds hard to bear even for one day.
  17:14/q22/p2 In choosing among worthy acts, perform the one nearer to and weightier with God first. — reasons: Sahl's context concerns duties, sunnas and voluntary acts; Tustarī presents this as practical self-accounting.
17:14/q23 Where does self-accounting take place? [بِنَفْسِكَ حَسِيبًا · interpretation]
  17:14/q23/p1 It may be private between servant and God or public between the person and others. — reasons: Sahl gives both spheres as forms of self-accounting.
17:14/q24 Why can a person be said to have authored their book? [كِتَٰبَكَ · interpretation]
  17:14/q24/p1 The person's own acts dictated its contents. — reasons: Yaḥyā ibn Muʿādh says people should read the book because they themselves dictated it; Study Quran's reported image makes memory dictate the record while tongue, saliva and limbs form its writing implements.
17:14/q25 How does this account connect with the preceding passage? [* · links] (turns on: Which preceding theme supplies the transition to each person's record and judgment.)
  17:14/q25/p1 Revelation and warning have supplied proof, leaving no excuse when records are judged. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says the completed account of signs and warning leads to the record; al-Ālūsī relays his proof of oneness, prophethood and resurrection. Kuranyolu likewise connects revelation, faith, reward and punishment to responsibility.
  17:14/q25/p2 God's worldly and religious benefits call for obedience, which is then examined. — reasons: Al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī give this as a second link.
  17:14/q25/p3 Creation is for worship, and the sun, moon, night and day aid obedience that judgment assesses. — reasons: Al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī cite 51:56; the latter says judgment asks whether people obeyed or rebelled.
  17:14/q25/p4 The passage moves from God's guidance-book to the person's exhaustive deed-book. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī cites 18:49: the personal book shows whether the first book was followed.
  17:14/q25/p5 The precise personal record makes the preceding general declaration of God's knowledge concrete. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr links encouragement and warning to 17:9–12, saying specific human deeds follow the general 'everything' for emphasis.
  17:14/q25/p6 Human haste and desire in the surrounding rebuke explain why the enduring deed-record must be reviewed. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī says people confuse benefit and harm and pursue desires though every act remains with its doer.
  17:14/q25/p7 The larger section calls Jews and Quraysh to the straight path and contrasts acceptance with rejection. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī says it rebukes demands for punishment by people blind to signs and alludes to God's treatment of nations.
17:14/q26 How does this verse illustrate the seven revealed modes in a reported classification? [* · sciences]
  17:14/q26/p1 It exemplifies the mode concerning resurrection and accounting. — reasons: Al-Burhān reports a ninth explanation of the seven modes as knowledge types: creation, unity, transcendence, attributes, commands, mercy and punishment, resurrection and accounting, and prophethood; it gives this verse as the accounting example. Al-Qāḍī says all seven noncontradictory transmitted modes entered the codex except nonmutawātir material; al-Ṭabarī and most later scholars favored one, al-Shāṭibī distinguished Abū Bakr's and ʿUthmān's compilations, and al-Ṭaḥāwī viewed the modes as temporary recitation ease.
