## THE CURRENT MAP
1:3/q01 Where does al-Raḥmān come from? [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ · meaning] (turns on: Arabic mercy derivation versus a Hebrew loan.)
  1:3/q01/p1 It is Arabic and derived from mercy. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī prefers Arabic patterns laḥfān, nadmān and ghaḍbān; al-Layth derives both names from mercy. Ibn ʿĀshūr says sister languages and Yemenite rakhim/rakhma cannot prove borrowing; Jarīr's line is inconclusive. Jishumī affirms the root.
  1:3/q01/p2 It is a Hebrew loan adapted into Arabic. — reasons: Abū al-ʿAbbās says its wide circulation contrasts with distinctively Arabic al-Raḥīm; al-Mubarrad proposes original khāʾ and cites Jarīr. Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects the claim for lack of proof.
1:3/q02 Was al-Raḥmān known as a divine name before Islam? [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ · sciences] (turns on: The evidential force of pre-Islamic verse and Meccan objections.)
  1:3/q02/p1 It was unfamiliar or contested among most pre-Islamic Arabs. — reasons: Abū al-ʿAbbās says Quraysh knew only the Yamāma claimant and a few poets learned it from scriptural people. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 25:60, 13:30, Meccan al-Mulk's repetition and the later Musaylima poem, yet allows disuse or denial rather than complete ignorance.
  1:3/q02/p2 Pre-Islamic Arabic poetry shows the name was known. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī records the objection from poetry; he answers that 25:60 may question the attribute or mock it. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the question need not prove foreign origin.
1:3/q03 What does al-Raḥmān signify? [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ · meaning] (turns on: Whether it marks intrinsic abundant mercy, creation, or a special scope.)
  1:3/q03/p1 It names abundant intrinsic mercy reaching creation. — reasons: Muqātil calls it merciful; Abū ʿUbayda and Majāz call it possession of mercy. Jishumī and Ibn ʿĀshūr link its form to intensity; Ibn ʿĀshūr says its effects are universal and steady. Elmalılı associates it with essential willing of good and creation from nonexistence.
  1:3/q03/p2 It designates God's creation or foundational gifts that creatures cannot give. — reasons: Jishumī reports both creation against provision and foundational blessings against general mercy; Rāzī points to formation from semen (40:64). Study Quran invokes the Breath and immutable archetypes; Elmalılı describes creating rational free agents.
  1:3/q03/p3 It denotes a special name and action. — reasons: Sulamī transmits a view making both name and action specific, while Ibn ʿĀshūr reports the name's exclusive or proper-name status.
1:3/q04 How is al-Raḥmān formed grammatically? [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ · grammar] (turns on: Whether faʿlān here is an enduring adjective despite a transitive source verb.)
  1:3/q04/p1 It is an enduring quality adjective whose pattern can convey fullness. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī and many investigators support this. Ibn ʿĀshūr explains that a transitive verb may shift to habitual intransitive sense even if raḥuma is unattested. Al-Zajjāj's ghaḍbān illustrates fullness; Ibn ʿĀshūr qualifies the larger-form rule with diminutive yāʾ and ḥadhir/ḥādhir exceptions.
  1:3/q04/p2 It cannot be an enduring quality adjective because its verb is transitive. — reasons: Ibn Mālik compares it to rabb and malik; Ibn ʿĀshūr answers by semantic conversion.
1:3/q05 Does indefinite raḥmān take full case inflection? [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ · grammar] (turns on: The diptote criteria for faʿlān and whether its feminine is faʿlānah.)
  1:3/q05/p1 It is diptote. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Raḍī and Ibn Mālik choose this treatment; Ibn ʿĀshūr locates the dispute in the Kāfiya's criteria.
  1:3/q05/p2 It fully declines. — reasons: Banū Asad give every faʿlān adjective a faʿlānah feminine and therefore decline it.
1:3/q06 Who may bear the name al-Raḥmān? [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ · referent]
  1:3/q06/p1 Only God properly bears al-Raḥmān. — reasons: Al-Ḥasan, Jishumī, Rāzī, Biqāʿī, Quṭb and Ibn ʿĀshūr contrast it with raḥīm, applied to the Messenger in 9:128. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls Musaylima's post-Islamic title an act of disbelief, not normal earlier usage.
1:3/q07 Is imāla transmitted in al-Raḥmān? [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ · readings]
  1:3/q07/p1 Qutayba from al-Kisāʾī transmits imāla in al-Raḥmān. — reasons: Ibn al-Jazarī adds this unlisted transmission alongside imāla in al-ʿālamīn; the same note also records lengthening elsewhere by Kurram from Nāfiʿ and al-Ahwāzī from Warsh.
1:3/q08 What does al-Raḥīm signify? [ٱلرَّحِيمِ · meaning] (turns on: Whether it stresses enduring quality, enacted mercy, or recipients.)
  1:3/q08/p1 It denotes compassionate mercy manifest in benefaction, often reaching particular recipients. — reasons: Muqātil calls it compassionate through mercy. Majāz and Abū ʿUbayda call it merciful or possessing mercy. Elmalılı makes it the active manifestation of mercy; Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses numerous objects and notes 9:128 applies it to the Prophet. Ṭabāṭabāʾī gives it lasting believer-directed mercy.
  1:3/q08/p2 It marks a general name and action. — reasons: Sulamī reports this paired with a specific raḥmān.
1:3/q09 What grammatical form is al-Raḥīm? [ٱلرَّحِيمِ · grammar] (turns on: Whether faʿīl is intensive and transitive, or an enduring adjective.)
  1:3/q09/p1 It is an intensive form retaining the verb's transitive force. — reasons: Sībawayh says so; Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly prefers it, while agreeing both analyses express intensity.
  1:3/q09/p2 It is an enduring quality adjective. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī and the majority reject faʿīl as an intensive pattern and compare marīḍ and saqīm; Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers Sībawayh.
1:3/q10 Where should recitation pause around al-Raḥīm? [ٱلرَّحِيمِ · readings] (turns on: Whether to stop at this verse end or join onward, and how to begin attached phrases.)
  1:3/q10/p1 Joining onward is the majority practice. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya says most grammatically join raḥīm to al-ḥamdu and join its initial alif; the vowel may resolve adjacent consonants. Ibn al-Jazarī dislikes beginning with syntactically dependent rabb al-ʿālamīn and comparable phrases except at verse ends.
  1:3/q10/p2 A pause after al-Raḥīm is transmitted. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites Umm Salama's report and some Kūfans cutting the initial alif of al-ḥamdu. Ibn al-Jazarī calls stopping at such meaningful verse endings good by Sunna.
1:3/q11 How do the two mercy names differ in scope? [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether their scopes coincide or divide by recipients, time, gifts, or essence and effect.)
  1:3/q11/p1 They are equivalent or near-synonymous expressions of mercy. — reasons: Majāz cites nadmān and nadīm, with poetry by Burj, al-Nuʿmān, Barīq and Ḥassān. Al-Wāḥidī and Jishumī report synonymy; Quṭrub and al-Zajjāj make the pair verbal emphasis. Ibn ʿĀshūr objects that context calls for added meaning.
  1:3/q11/p2 Al-Raḥmān reaches all creation, while al-Raḥīm especially reaches believers or the saved. — reasons: Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and al-Ṣādiq give the all-creation/believer contrast; Jishumī makes the latter forgiveness. Al-Ḥarālī's nearer reading distinguishes general benefits from felicity-leading mercy. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says creative effusion is universal, while guidance and enduring salvation concern believers. Study Quran pictures sun and individual ray and cites 33:43.
  1:3/q11/p3 Al-Raḥmān denotes essential mercy; al-Raḥīm denotes its enacted, recipient-directed mercy. — reasons: Elmalılı makes the former a stable essential will and the latter active beneficence, noting some reverse them. Ibn ʿĀshūr puts intrinsic character before its many objects. Study Quran calls these universal essential and particular attributive mercy.
  1:3/q11/p4 Their difference is uncertain; together they embrace every mode of mercy. — reasons: Quṭb declines to rank their scopes, while Muqātil and Khālid b. Ṣafwān say one is more tender than the other without fixing which. Jishumī says both intensify divine blessing.
  1:3/q11/p5 Al-Raḥmān concerns worldly mercy and al-Raḥīm the afterlife. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī reports this with the greater intensity of the first. Elmalılı reports earlier exegetes' afterlife interpretation, while Rāzī sees worldly mercies followed by unbounded reward and possible pardon after death.
  1:3/q11/p6 Al-Raḥmān creates or gives unproducible foundational gifts; al-Raḥīm provides or rewards acquired deeds. — reasons: Jishumī contrasts creation with provision and uncreatable first blessings with merciful possession. Rāzī contrasts divine creation with gifts servants can produce, then formation from semen with perfected Garden for imperfect obedience. Study Quran contrasts bringing existence and sustaining nourishment.
  1:3/q11/p7 Al-Raḥmān gives blessings; al-Raḥīm protects from sin or harm. — reasons: Sulamī reports blessing versus protection from sin. Al-Ḥarālī reports created benefits versus harm prevention as one of two readings.
  1:3/q11/p8 Al-Raḥmān denotes enacted mercy and al-Raḥīm essential mercy. — reasons: Elmalılı records interpreters who reverse his allocation without naming them.
  1:3/q11/p9 Al-Raḥmān grants general existence regardless of request; al-Raḥīm responds to free striving. — reasons: Elmalılı distinguishes existence and life provision for every rational creature from wanted gifts under one divine will, related to choice and final recompense.
1:3/q12 Why are both mercy names joined? [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether pairing reinforces one meaning or adds a distinct aspect and reassurance.)
