[STUDYQURAN:7:172/r33] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | In Quranic and prophetic context, the pretemporal recognition creates an innate disposition to worship God and accept prophetic reminders of inwardly known truths.
[STUDYQURAN:7:172/r34] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Those who reject prophets are willfully ignorant, denying truths that should resonate with the deep but forgotten knowledge in an uncorrupted soul.
[STUDYQURAN:7:172/r35] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Kuffār commonly means “disbelievers,” but etymologically means covering over innate awareness of God’s Lordship and Oneness.
[STUDYQURAN:7:172/r36] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Together with messengers sent to all peoples, the covenant serves as proof against those who knowingly and capriciously deny prophetic messages.
[STUDYQURAN:7:172/r37] STUDYQURAN · Some reports attributed to the Prophet | reports | Some narrations use the verse to connect the pretemporal covenant with predestinarian ideas.
[STUDYQURAN:7:172/r38] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The verse’s literal reading suggests all people made the same covenant and therefore share the same possibility of recognizing God in earthly life.
[STUDYQURAN:7:172/r39] STUDYQURAN · Some reports | reports | Some reports say progeny from Adam’s right side or God’s Right Hand were destined for the Garden, while those from the left or other Hand were destined for Hell.
[STUDYQURAN:7:172/r4] STUDYQURAN · Some commentators | reports | Some consider the humanity-wide covenant subsequent to the prophets’ covenant. | mentions 3:81, 33:7
[STUDYQURAN:7:172/r40] STUDYQURAN · Some reports | reports | Other reports say some covenant witnesses answered reluctantly or deceptively.
[STUDYQURAN:7:172/r41] STUDYQURAN · Other authorities | rejects | Other authorities reject the claim that some covenant witnesses answered reluctantly or deceptively.
[STUDYQURAN:7:172/r42] STUDYQURAN · Some early Twelver Shiite traditions | reports | These traditions say covenant witnesses were also asked to attest Muhammad’s prophethood and the Shiite Imams’ authority, but only some accepted the latter.
[STUDYQURAN:7:172/r43] STUDYQURAN · Some early Sufi authors, including al-Ḥakīm al-Tirmidhī | reports | They describe the covenant as distinguishing the spiritual elect from the common people.
[STUDYQURAN:7:172/r44] STUDYQURAN · author | prefers | Although the covenant may shape moral destiny by giving humans innate capacity to recognize religious truth, the plain meaning more plausibly affirms universal potential for spiritual attainment and accepting revelation.
[STUDYQURAN:7:172/r5] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The Quran also names covenants with believers generally. | mentions 5:1, 5:7, 6:152, 13:20, 13:25, 16:91, 16:95, 57:8
[STUDYQURAN:7:172/r6] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The Quran names covenants with Abraham and the Israelites. | mentions 2:124, 2:125, 2:40, 2:63, 2:83, 2:84, 2:93, 4:154, 5:70
[STUDYQURAN:7:172/r7] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The Quran names covenants with Christians and the People of the Book collectively. | mentions 5:14, 3:187
[STUDYQURAN:7:172/r8] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | This direct, universal covenant is unmediated and can ground later, specific covenants mediated by prophets.
[STUDYQURAN:7:172/r9] STUDYQURAN · Commentators | reports | Commentators connect the verse to fiṭrah, the primordial nature originally given to all humans, in which innate recognition of God’s Oneness is humanity’s essence. | mentions 30:30
[STUDYQURAN:7:intro/r28] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Verse 172 presents a universal, pretemporal covenant in which all human beings are bound to recognize God's Lordship; it grounds subsequent covenants between God and humanity mediated through prophets.
[TIRMIDHI:3075/r1] TIRMIDHI · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | In the report Umar transmits, the Prophet explains the verse as God bringing descendants from Adam’s back and assigning one group to Paradise and one to Fire, each doing the deeds of its destination.
[TIRMIDHI:3075/r2] TIRMIDHI · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Asked what use deeds have if destinies are set, the Prophet says God enables the person destined for Paradise to do its deeds until death, and likewise the person destined for Fire.
[TIRMIDHI:3075/r3] TIRMIDHI · أبو عيسى | holds | Tirmidhi grades the report hasan but notes Muslim b. Yasar did not hear directly from Umar; some versions insert an unnamed intermediary.
