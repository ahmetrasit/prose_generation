[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#2/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ba is also discussed as extra in the construction beginning a-wa-lam yaraw anna Allah and similar expressions. | mentions 46:33
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#2/r12] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | A noun, technically, signifies a meaning in itself without time or meaningful parts; linguistically it identifies its referent, and naming makes a word signify that meaning.

[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ba is regularly added to the subject of kafa, as in God sufficing his servant; a poem shows the preposition omitted with the same construction. | mentions 4:6

[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#2/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ba may be irregularly added to the predicate of la, the negative counterpart of laysa; the verse means no intercessor can avail anything.

[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Scholars dispute whether a name is identical with its named referent; this creates an apparent problem of a thing being annexed to itself.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#3/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Basrans derive ism from sumu, elevation, because the name raises and reveals its referent; Kufans derive it from wasm, a mark, semantically plausible but morphologically unsound.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | prefers | Abu al-Baqa's best answer is that name here means naming, which differs from the name itself and its referent.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#3/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The author reports Abu al-Baqa's second answer: supply an omitted annex, yielding in the name of the named God.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#3/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The author reports Abu al-Baqa's third solution, treating ism as superfluous, like the poetic phrase meaning peace be upon you.

[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#3/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · قطرب | reports | Qutrub says the added word serves honor and exaltation.

[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#3/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Apparent self-annexation also occurs in name-plus-title and noun-plus-description phrases; grammarians explain these by meaning-name shifts or omitted annexes.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#4/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Basrans cite the plural asma, diminutive sumayy, and related Arabic forms as evidence for derivation from sumu; a wasm derivation would predict awsam and wasim, which are not used.
