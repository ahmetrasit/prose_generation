[ALUSI:89:27/r10] ALUSI, d. 1270 · صاحب البديع | reports | The author of al-Badīʿ says ayyā may be masculine in address to a feminine noun, by analogy with its unchanged form before dual and plural addressees; grammatical gender shifts are beside the tranquil soul's concern.
[ALUSI:89:27/r2] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | The wording implies God saying this, either directly as He spoke to Moses or through an angel; al-Ālūsī considers the call most likely after judgment is complete.
[ALUSI:89:27/r3] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Omitting an explicit says heightens the contrast between the person's wish for the past and God's address to this soul; the sentence is coordinated with the preceding one. | mentions 89:24
[ALUSI:89:27/r4] ALUSI, d. 1270 · قيل | reports | One view takes nafs as the self or essence: the rational faculty is restless while ascending through causes to the Necessary Cause, then rests upon knowing God and turns from all else.
[ALUSI:89:27/r5] ALUSI, d. 1270 · قيل | prefers | Another good interpretation is the believing soul settled in truth and certainty, untouched by doubt or inward disturbance.
[ALUSI:89:27/r6] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | On the certainty reading, this soul is the remembering and admonished one, unlike the earlier human; remembrance tracks certainty, as 13:19 says the people of understanding take heed. | mentions 13:19
[ALUSI:89:27/r7] ALUSI, d. 1270 · قيل | reports | A third view identifies the soul as the believer who dies in faith and is safe from fear and grief on Resurrection Day; it contrasts with the preceding person's sorrow. | mentions 89:24
