87:2/q09 How does this creation clause bear on God's exaltedness? [ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ · theology] (turns on: How the surrounding acts establish divine worthiness of praise and what the superlative language does or does not imply.)
  87:2/q09/p1 It shows exalted power and lordship through creation and proportioning, in a way befitting God's majesty. — reasons: Al-Rāzī rejects a spatial reading because distance does not establish worthiness of praise, while these acts display power.   87:2/q09/p2 The descriptions 'Great' and 'Highest' do not imply multiple gods or a comparison with another lord. — reasons: Al-Rāzī rejects that objection because God's oneness is already established and no other lord is mentioned.
87:2/q10 What inward significance is given to creation and fashioning? [خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ · inward] (turns on: Whether fashioning's spiritual consequence is distinctive guidance, readiness for the perfect spirit, or receptivity to an allotted effusion.)
  87:2/q10/p1 Humans receive a special gift of guidance; pride belongs in guidance and piety rather than created form. — reasons: Al-Sulamī cites 49:13; Bursawī reports a related view that creatures share basic creation but differ through special guidance.
  87:2/q10/p2 The outward human constitution is proportioned to receive the most complete spirit and every perfection. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī speaks of a particular temperament; Bursawī transmits the same reading as the body receiving the perfect spirit.
  87:2/q10/p3 Each being is formed to receive its allotted divine effusion according to innate capacity. — reasons: Bursawī attributes this interpretation to the Najmiyya.
87:2/q11 How do creation and fashioning support the command to glorify God? [ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ · links] (turns on: Whether the clause's immediate lesson is the observable proof warranting glorification or the gradual divine ordering that calls for patience.)
  87:2/q11/p1 They answer why the Lord is known and worthy of glorification by displaying observable divine power, wisdom, and guidance. — reasons: Al-Rāzī calls creation and guidance a central prophetic proof: Abraham says his Creator guides him (26:78), Moses describes the giver of each thing's form and guidance (20:49–50), and the opening revelation joins creation and teaching (96:1–4). He says their abundant marvels are directly observable. Jishumī says the sūra answers the preceding warning with God's attributes and power. Al-Biqāʿī and Ibn al-Zubayr connect the preceding servants' or unbelievers' plots with the command to glorify and the demonstration of power and wisdom. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the first four verses prove absolute lordship; Ibn ʿĀshūr says they indicate oneness through human creation and sustenance.
  87:2/q11/p2 The ordered acts explain the gradual maturing of created physical and intellectual capacities and counsel patient obedience. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī says verses 1–5 address the Prophet's preaching fears; divine plans unfold by wisdom, so delays are wise and hardship can benefit people in both lives.
