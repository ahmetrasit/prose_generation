53:36/q13 How many scrolls does Abu Dharr's report assign to Abraham? [صُحُفِ · reports]
  53:36/q13/p1 Ten written scrolls or pages were given to Abraham. — reasons: Ibn Ashur reports Abu Dharr asking the Prophet about revealed books and receiving the number ten for Abraham. Bursevi's Abu Dharr report gives 104 books overall: ten pages for Adam, fifty for Seth, thirty for Idris, ten for Abraham, then the Torah, Gospel, Psalms, and Quran.
53:36/q14 What did Abraham fulfill in the following description? [* · interpretation] (turns on: The reports variously specify comprehensive obedience, particular acts of worship, or the rule of individual liability as the fulfillment.)
  53:36/q14/p1 He fully discharged God's commands, trials, vows, and duty of servitude. — reasons: Zajjaj lists his resolve to sacrifice his son, endurance of his people's hostility and the fire, and circumcision. Mawrdi reports Ibn Abbas on obedience, Hasan on every command and vow, and trials of sacrifice, fire, and being called a liar. Ibn Kathir reports Sa'id ibn Jubayr and al-Thawri that he fulfilled every command. Tabrisi glosses waffa as completing his commands; Tabatabai says he perfectly discharged servitude and cites the completed trials of 2:124. Thalabi includes conveying God's message and the rule against another's burden.
  53:36/q14/p2 He fulfilled each day's worship with four midmorning rakas. — reasons: Mawrdi reports this from the Prophet through Abu Umama.
  53:36/q14/p3 He fulfilled morning and evening glorification. — reasons: Mawrdi reports through Anas and his father that Abraham recited the glorification verse at both times, citing 30:17.
  53:36/q14/p4 He established the principle that no one bears a relative's offense. — reasons: Mawrdi reports al-Hudhayl that Abraham opposed the earlier practice of charging a person with a child's or parent's offense, connecting this to 53:38; Thalabi includes this principle in his mission.
53:36/q15 What changed in liability for relatives' offenses around Abraham? [* · reports]
  53:36/q15/p1 Abraham rejected an earlier practice of charging people for relatives' offenses, and individual liability prevailed afterward. — reasons: Mawrdi reports al-Hudhayl on liability for a child's or parent's offense; Qurtubi reports al-Hudhayl ibn Shurahbil that this occurred between Noah and Abraham; Elmalili reports Abu Hayyan that before Abraham people were charged for relatives' or masters' crimes and that from Abraham's law through Moses' they were not.
53:36/q16 How does the scrolls passage connect to its surrounding verses? [* · links] (turns on: The notes emphasize either continuity with the rebuke and recompense in 53:30–33 or the section's broader movement into scriptural truths and commentary on Quraysh.)
  53:36/q16/p1 It continues the theme of recompense and redirects the person turning away to prior teaching. — reasons: Razi connects 53:30–31 with the scrolls and 53:38 because both explain recompense; he proposes a shift from idolaters to a scriptured person who stopped giving God's rights when Muhammad came (53:33, 53:38). Ibn Ashur calls the second question another rebuke, and Kuran Yolu says it follows the earlier criticism.
  53:36/q16/p2 It marks a turn from reflection on creation to enduring scriptural truths and their application to Quraysh. — reasons: Study Quran describes the third section moving from creation to truths in Moses' and Abraham's scriptures. Islahi says the passage extends the original scriptural statement and becomes commentary on Quraysh from verse 40 onward.
53:36/q17 What occasion is reported for the passage's revelation? [* · reports]
  53:36/q17/p1 It followed an offer of transport to a man who could not obtain a mount from the Prophet and wanted to catch the army. — reasons: Durr reports this account from Darraj Abu al-Samh.
53:36/q18 What distinction is drawn between 'the right' and 'any right' in nearby wording? [* · rhetoric]
  53:36/q18/p1 The definite phrase identifies a particular justification for killing; the indefinite denies any justification and stresses aggression. — reasons: Samarrai gives this contrast for the nearby wording.
53:36/q19 What distinction is reported between al-nabiyyin and al-anbiya'? [* · meaning]
  53:36/q19/p1 Al-nabiyyin describes those being informed, whereas al-anbiya' names prophets after becoming prophets. — reasons: Samarrai reports al-Kubaisi's distinction and adds 'God knows best.'
