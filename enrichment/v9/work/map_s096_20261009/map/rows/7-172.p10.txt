[NASAFI-FULL:v2p45#3/r1] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | The opening إذ means “remember when”; من ظهورهم is an apposition to بني آدم, meaning from the backs of Adam's descendants, and the offspring are extracted from their fathers' loins.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p45#3/r2] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | The testimony is illustrative: God set evidence of His lordship and unity before them, and their implanted rational faculties witnessed it by distinguishing guidance from error, as though He had asked and they acknowledged His unity.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p45#3/r3] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | The purpose is to prevent them from claiming on Judgment Day that they were unaware, since the evidence and their reason had alerted them.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p45#3/r4] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | إنا كنا عن هذا غافلين means they might claim they had not been alerted to it.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p218/r13] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | A hadith transmitted through multiple routes describes God drawing Adam's descendants from his back and taking their pledge that He alone is Lord and God.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p218/r14] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Reports differ about how, in what form, where, and when the descendants were extracted; Abū Ḥayyān says their apparent wording conflicts with the verse and attempts to reconcile them are strained.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p218/r15] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | prefers | He prefers al-Zamakhsharī's figurative account: God installed rational signs of His lordship and unity, so minds and consciences effectively testified to them.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p219/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī treats the exchange as representation, like God's command to a thing to be and the earth and heaven's response; the cited verses and Arabic poetry portray meaning without literal speech. | mentions 36:82, 41:11
