55:1/q21 What is the sūrah's overall purpose and course? [* · interpretation]
  55:1/q21/p1 It displays universal mercy through creation, teaching, signs, and favors, while warning of punishment and describing both groups' outcomes. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says hope in divine gifts is joined to warning that further favor can cease through punishment. Study Quran says verses 1–25 present Creator, Teacher, and Revealer and created blessings, then heavenly blessings; it says God's endurance in verses 26–27 offsets passing creation. Iṣlāḥī describes Meccan opponents demanding a punishment sign and says human creation and speech equip people to learn by revelation. Al-Rāzī says the Name can imply might and vengeance toward the wicked as well as mercy and forgiveness toward the righteous.
55:1/q22 How do the sūrah's beginning, basmala, and ending echo each other? [* · links]
  55:1/q22/p1 The basmala and opening announce divine perfection and mercy; the ending returns to the opening Name. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī sees the basmala as naming perfection shown in creation, universal mercy in wondrous signs, and special mercy to the obedient in worship; he reports al-Rāzī that the closing Name recalls the opening and encompasses both teaching and punishment. Al-Qushayrī says the basmala joins majesty and bounty: majesty completes spirits' joy and makes God worshiped, while mercy gives bodily bliss and makes Him loved.
55:1/q23 What inward significance is given to al-Raḥmān and divine teaching? [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ · inward] (turns on: The inward notes relate the Name either to creation and manifestation or to divine teaching in the heavenly realm.)
  55:1/q23/p1 The Name is a source of creation and a disclosure of divine presence. — reasons: Study Quran reports Sufis saying all creation comes forth through the Breath of the Compassionate and notes 20:5 and 25:59 on the Compassionate assuming the Throne. Ibn Burjān, reported by al-Biqāʿī, relates al-Raḥmān to the manifest aspect of Allah and the hidden aspect of al-Rabb.
  55:1/q23/p2 God's teaching of names and the Qur'an reflects a spiritual presentation before the heavenly assembly. — reasons: Al-Sulamī reports a comparison between teaching all names to angels and teaching Muhammad the Qur'an, which God presents to Himself; the disputing high assembly is identified as angels.
