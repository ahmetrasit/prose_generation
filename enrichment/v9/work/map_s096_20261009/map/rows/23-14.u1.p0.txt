## THE CURRENT MAP
23:14/q01 What does the opening thumma signal? [ثُمَّ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether thumma marks elapsed time, elevated rank, or both.)
  23:14/q01/p1 A real interval or remote transition before the drop becomes a clot. — reasons: Al-Burhān notes intervening stages; al-Biqāʿī says the white drop may spoil or begin development; Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses the startling change in density and color. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports complete separation between stages.
  23:14/q01/p2 A rise in rank or wonder, rather than necessarily a long interval. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Biqāʿī describe the transformation as extraordinary; al-Ālūsī permits rank for thumma.
23:14/q02 What is the nutfa and how does it become the next stage? [ٱلنُّطْفَةَ · interpretation]
  23:14/q02/p1 The reproductive fluid develops in the womb into the red clot. — reasons: Muqātil describes fluid becoming blood; al-Ṭabarī says the secure womb holds it to its appointed development (23:13); al-Rāzī treats the change as new qualities and accidents; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, Bursevī describe white semen turning red. Ibn Kathīr says both parents' fluids contribute and cites Ibn Masʿūd's report that the drop spreads to hairs and nails before becoming a clot. Elmalılı speaks of implantation or fertilization.
  23:14/q02/p2 The semen can be called dead before God makes it living. — reasons: Al-Dāmghānī compares 2:28, 40:11, 3:27 and 30:19; al-Burhān relays Ibn Masʿūd's living-from-dead reading and an account of all animals before life.
23:14/q03 What does ʿalaqa mean and why is it called that? [عَلَقَةًۭ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the term describes blood, attachment, leech-like appearance, or their combination.)
  23:14/q03/p1 A piece of blood, congealed or clotted, that becomes the embryo. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba calls it one clot, singular of ʿalaq; al-Māwardī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya call it fresh blood; Jishumī distinguishes congealed from flowing blood. Al-Rāghib as reported by Bursevī and other exegetes use clotted blood; al-Biqāʿī emphasizes its thick intense redness. Al-Ālūsī calls it qualitative motion of the blood.
  23:14/q03/p2 The name points to attachment or clinging in the womb. — reasons: Al-Māwardī says it is the first attachment; Jishumī gives the root's senses of connection, a tether, and marriage obligations, citing a hadith with ʿalāʾiq. Elmalılı and Kur'an Yolu relate it to implantation and uterine attachment; Ibn ʿĀshūr says maternal blood nourishes the attached embryo through uterine veins.
  23:14/q03/p3 The embryo at this stage resembles a leech. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr describes its elongation and redness; al-Sāmarrāʾī in both works invokes modern observations for the resemblance.
23:14/q04 What does muḍgha mean and what is its form? [مُضْغَةًۭ · meaning]
  23:14/q04/p1 A small, soft morsel of flesh of a chewable size, initially without distinct form. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba compares ghurfa for a scoopful; Jishumī gives mudāgh for chewed food and mudāgha for what remains in the mouth. Al-Sāmarrāʾī stresses teeth marks and modern resemblance to chewed flesh; al-Ālūsī treats the clot-to-flesh change as substantial rather than merely qualitative.
23:14/q05 How are the embryonic stages physically transformed? [فَخَلَقْنَا ٱلْعَلَقَةَ مُضْغَةًۭ فَخَلَقْنَا ٱلْمُضْغَةَ عِظَٰمًۭا · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the account is a simple sequence or explains transformations through qualities, substance, and underlying matter.)
  23:14/q05/p1 The clot becomes a morsel, which is formed or hardened into bones. — reasons: Muqātil and al-Thaʿlabī give the sequence. Jishumī calls bones strengthened; Ibn Kathīr describes forming head, hands, feet, sinews and veins; al-Biqāʿī describes heat and hardening. Bursevī says the greater part hardens after forty-three days; Ibn ʿĀshūr says bones form inside the morsel. Al-Ālūsī considers small and large bones and changing dispositions of matter; Elmalılı likens soft fetal bones hardening later to an egg shell, and calls them the body's framework.
  23:14/q05/p2 The stages arise from one underlying matter through changing forms and qualities. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī accepts continuous underlying matter unless evidence shows otherwise; he describes the clot to morsel as substantial change and reports al-Bayḍāwī's different account of which stages share an underlying reality.
23:14/q06 How long do the stages last and when does the angel act? [ٱلنُّطْفَةَ عَلَقَةًۭ مُضْغَةًۭ ثُمَّ أَنشَأْنَٰهُ · reports] (turns on: The forty-day and four-month reports distinguish stages, recording, and ensoulment; one report also apportions bodily tissues by parental fluid.)
  23:14/q06/p1 The drop, clot, and morsel each last forty days or nights, then the spirit is breathed. — reasons: Yaḥyā gives forty nights each; al-Baghawī reports forty days between stages. Ibn Kathīr cites Ibn Masʿūd's Prophetic report: afterward an angel breathes spirit and records provision, lifespan, deeds and destiny, with final destiny possibly differing from apparent deeds; he notes both Ṣaḥīḥ collections transmit it. Ibn ʿĀshūr grades the hadith sound.
  23:14/q06/p2 An angel acts after forty nights or four months, recording or breathing as the reports describe. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr cites Ḥudhayfa b. Usayd's forty-night recording of sex, destiny, deeds, effects, trials, and provision, then a sealed record; Anas describes the angel reporting each stage and recording if God wills creation. ʿAlī says breathing occurs after four months in three darknesses. The reports vary in emphasis and timing.
  23:14/q06/p3 The man’s fluid yields bones and sinews, and the woman’s fluid flesh and blood. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr transmits a Prophetic answer to a Jewish question, with the questioner confirming agreement with earlier prophets; his general account also names both parents' fluids.
23:14/q07 How does khalaqnā work in the transformation clauses? [خَلَقْنَا · grammar]
  23:14/q07/p1 It means made or transformed and takes two objects here. — reasons: Al-Nasafī contrasts ordinary single-object khalaqa; al-Samīn says it carries the meaning of jaʿala, while jaʿala can conversely mean create with one object, citing 6:1.
  23:14/q07/p2 Creation of a changed form entails God replacing qualities and adding parts. — reasons: Al-Rāzī describes removal and creation of accidents, plus additional components.
23:14/q08 Why do thumma and fa alternate through the stages? [ثُمَّ فَخَلَقْنَا فَكَسَوْنَا ثُمَّ أَنشَأْنَٰهُ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the conjunctions distinguish time, closeness of change, or rank.)
  23:14/q08/p1 Fa marks close succession, while thumma marks a larger interval or more remote transition. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says stages after the clot ascend without delay; Ibn ʿĀshūr says proximity is relative even when each lasts long. Al-Suyūṭī says intervals depend on the events. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports that thumma marks complete break, fa nearer succession. Al-Ālūsī says more surprising transitions receive thumma. Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursevī connect the conjunctions to distinct kinds of change.
  23:14/q08/p2 The final thumma marks the far higher rank of the living new human. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursevī say it marks a great difference between two creations; Ibn ʿĀshūr says life begins with ensoulment and thumma marks rank; al-Ālūsī allows rank in addition to time.
23:14/q09 How are the two bone nouns read? [عِظَٰمًۭا ٱلْعِظَٰمَ · readings] (turns on: The transmitted forms, whether singular is generic, and the value of the plural in a body with many bones.)
  23:14/q09/p1 Both are plural: ʿiẓāman and al-ʿiẓām. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī prefers the agreement of the authoritative readers; Abū ʿAlī prefers the plural because other Qurʾānic references to bones are plural (17:49, 79:11, 36:78). A human has many bones; al-Ālūsī says shapes and hardness vary, with one reported count of 248. The rasm can hold forms with or without alif.
  23:14/q09/p2 Both are singular: ʿaẓman and al-ʿaẓm, a generic or collective noun. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀmir and Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim are regularly named; Ḥafṣ and other transmitters differ. Al-Fārisī cites nouns such as insān and dirham and Abū Zayd's collective qarād; al-Zajjāj cites singular bone for many throats; al-Samīn cites 19:4, 2:7 and poetry about hides. It also matches singular flesh. Al-Ālūsī disputes restricting this to poetic necessity.
  23:14/q09/p3 The first noun is singular and the second plural. — reasons: Ibn Jinnī prefers the progression from preceding singular embryonic nouns to general plurality; he cites poetry including Ṭufayl's singular ʿaẓm. Reports vary over the readers assigned. The singular can mean the generic substance.
  23:14/q09/p4 The first noun is plural and the second singular. — reasons: Ibn Jinnī finds reverting to singular less apt than progressing from singular to plural; the singular still denotes bone collectively. Reports differ over Mujāhid's reading.
  23:14/q09/p5 Ibn Masʿūd's wording adds sinew to bone before the flesh covering. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya report a reading in which the drop is made bone and sinew, then clothed with flesh.
23:14/q10 What bones or bodily structure are meant? [عِظَٰمًۭا · referent] (turns on: Whether the singular bone denotes the skeletal substance generally or a particular axial bone.)
  23:14/q10/p1 The developing skeleton and bodily support. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr says the morsel is shaped into head, limbs, bone, sinews and veins; al-Biqāʿī describes bones from head to feet; Elmalılı calls bones the framework and pillars; al-Ālūsī stresses their variety.
  23:14/q10/p2 The bone refers specifically to the backbone. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr reports Ibn ʿAbbās's identification and a Ṣaḥīḥ hadith that all of Adam's body decays except the tailbone, from which he was created and will be reassembled.
