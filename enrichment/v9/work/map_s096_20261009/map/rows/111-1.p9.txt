[IBNKATHIR:111:1/r2] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · ابن عباس | reports | In the Bukhari report from Ibn Abbas, the Prophet calls Quraysh to gather at al-Batha, asks whether they would believe a warning of an imminent enemy, and warns of severe punishment; Abu Lahab objects that this was why they were gathered, curses the Prophet, and the sūra is revealed.
[IBNKATHIR:111:1/r3] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · ابن عباس | reports | An alternate wording has Abu Lahab shake dust from his hands and say, 'Perish you all day; was this why you gathered us?' before the revelation.
[IBNKATHIR:111:1/r4] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Ibn Kathir distinguishes the first phrase as a prayer against Abu Lahab and the second as a statement that his ruin has occurred.
[IBNKATHIR:111:1/r5] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Abu Lahab was the Prophet's paternal uncle, Abd al-Uzza ibn Abd al-Muttalib, also known by the kunya Abu Utba; he was nicknamed Abu Lahab for his shining face.
[IBNKATHIR:111:1/r6] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Ibn Kathir says Abu Lahab repeatedly harmed the Messenger, hated and scorned him, and belittled him and his religion.
[IBNKATHIR:111:1/r7] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · ربيعة بن عباد الديلي | reports | Rabi'a ibn Abbad recalls seeing the Prophet preach that affirming God's oneness brings success in the market of Dhu al-Majaz, while Abu Lahab followed him calling him a lying Sabi. Abu Lahab was handsome, squint-eyed, and wore two braids; people identified him as the Prophet's uncle.
[IBNKATHIR:111:1/r8] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Ibn Kathir notes that Ahmad alone transmits this account; Rabi'a says he was not a child then, since he could already carry a waterskin.
[IBNKATHIR:111:1/r9] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · ربيعة بن عباد الديلي | reports | In Ibn Ishaq's account reported by Rabi'a, the Prophet asks tribes to worship God alone, believe and protect him as he delivers God's message. Abu Lahab warns that the Prophet would make them abandon al-Lat, al-Uzza, and their allied jinn of Banu Malik ibn Uqays for innovation and misguidance, and urges them not to follow him. Ahmad and al-Tabarani also transmit this wording.

[BURHAN:v1p438/r1] BURHAN, d. 794 · الشيخ أبو الحسن الأشعري | reports | Al-Ashari, Qadi Abu Bakr, Ibn Hibban, and others deny that one passage is better than another: all is God’s speech, and calling one best implies deficiency in another.

[BURHAN:v1p438/r8] BURHAN, d. 794 · عز الدين | reports | Izz al-Din takes a middle position: speech about God is better than speech about others, so al-Ikhlas is better than the surah about Abu Lahab; al-Ghazali and Ibn al-Arabi adopted this.
