## THE CURRENT MAP
53:35/q01 What does ʿindahu convey here? [عِندَهُۥ · meaning]
  53:35/q01/p1 It asks whether he knows or possesses knowledge. — reasons: Al-Damghani glosses ʿindahu as 'does he know' and compares 52:41.
53:35/q02 Why is ʿindahu placed first? [عِندَهُۥ · rhetoric]
  53:35/q02/p1 Fronting it stresses how strange his claimed possession of unseen knowledge would be. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says the order underscores his distance from that status.
53:35/q03 How does the question aʿindahu ʿilm al-ghayb function in the earlier construction? [أَعِندَهُۥ عِلْمُ ٱلْغَيْبِ · grammar]
  53:35/q03/p1 It is the second object of the earlier arāʾayta, whose first object is omitted. — reasons: Al-Samīn says the first object is omitted for concision; Bursevī reports Ibn al-Shaykh's gloss of arāʾayta as 'inform me.'
53:35/q04 What unseen matter does the question challenge him to know? [عِلْمُ ٱلْغَيْبِ · interpretation] (turns on: Which hidden claim his conduct presumes: transferable punishment, future poverty, his reward, the truth of revelation, resurrection, or hidden knowledge more generally. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers the worldly-future reading because 53:38 itself addresses transfer of sins.)
  53:35/q04/p1 Whether another person can bear his sins or punishment for him. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı, and Ibn ʿĀshūr apply the question to his companion's undertaking. Al-Zajjāj as reported by al-Wāḥidī also connects knowing to that undertaking. Al-Zamakhsharī as stated in al-Kashshāf and reported by Abū Ḥayyān asks whether his brother's promise is true. Ibn ʿAṭiyyah and Abū Ḥayyān frame it as whether assuming another's burden would benefit him. Al-Rāzī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī connect the supposed transfer to 53:38; Ibn ʿĀshūr says he paid someone to carry the consequences of leaving Islam.
  53:35/q04/p2 Whether continued giving would exhaust his wealth and leave him poor. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr says the miser plainly has no unseen knowledge that his money will run out. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this future course of worldly life. The Study Quran likewise includes whether his wealth remains safe if he withholds charity.
  53:35/q04/p3 Whether he can know his afterlife condition, punishment, or recompense generally. — reasons: Ibn Qutaybah includes the affairs of the Hereafter; Ibn al-Jawzī reports al-Farrāʾ on seeing his afterlife condition and Ibn Qutaybah on hidden afterlife affairs. Abū Ḥayyān reports the afterlife-condition view and al-Zajjāj's view that he sees his sin lifted there. Al-Rāzī defines unseen knowledge as what is hidden from creatures and says direct sight occurs only when faith no longer benefits; al-Qurṭubī includes unseen punishment and afterlife events.
  53:35/q04/p4 Whether he can foresee that his small gift or earlier deeds suffice for reward. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī asks whether he knows every benefit and harm and what God accepts, so can judge his small donation sufficient. Islahi says misers give little yet presume high rank in the Hereafter; the Study Quran mentions confidence that earlier deeds suffice. Islahi also depicts people acting as though Paradise's luxuries are already visible.
  53:35/q04/p5 Whether he knows that what he heard from the Quran is false. — reasons: Al-Māwardī reports this possibility. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī also report it; Mujāhid, as reported in al-Durr, identifies the unseen as the Quran and asks whether al-Walīd saw falsehood while visiting the Prophet and Abū Bakr.
  53:35/q04/p6 Whether revelation came to him and authorized his judgment that his conduct was right. — reasons: Al-Māwardī and al-Ālūsī report al-Kalbī's view; Abū Ḥayyān reports it with the nuance that he saw what had been withheld from him as true.
  53:35/q04/p7 Whether he knows there will be no resurrection or recompense. — reasons: Muqātil says he would need unseen knowledge that he will not be resurrected to justify persistence in disbelief, comparing 52:41 and 68:47. Al-Māwardī reports the possibility that he knew there is no resurrection and thus expected no recompense.
  53:35/q04/p8 Whether he possesses knowledge of hidden matters as though he could see them. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī states the general question. Al-Rāzī calls the unseen what creatures cannot know; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it hidden realms inferred from evidence but expressed as witnessed. Al-Biqāʿī extends the supposed knowledge to every benefit, harm, and divine acceptance.
  53:35/q04/p9 Whether he was informed of the content of Moses' scrolls. — reasons: Bursevī gives this alternative as ignorance of what is in Moses' scrolls, meaning the books of the Torah.
53:35/q05 What does yarā mean here? [يَرَىٰٓ · meaning] (turns on: Whether seeing the unseen is a figure for knowing it or is treated as direct visual perception; several commentators explicitly allow both.)
  53:35/q05/p1 It denotes knowledge or inward perception. — reasons: Sībawayh as reported by al-Naḥḥās glosses it as knowing. Al-Zajjāj, reported also by al-Wāḥidī, illustrates this with a blind person saying he sees Zayd as intelligent. Bursevī and Ibn al-Shaykh use 'knows'; al-Ālūsī calls this the more immediate sense. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Zajjāj allow this meaning alongside sight.
  53:35/q05/p2 It denotes actual sight of what is hidden from others. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Ālūsī allow physical sight. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the verb visually with the unseen as its omitted object. Al-Rāzī says such direct sight would only be available when faith no longer benefits.
53:35/q06 What objects or content are supplied for yarā? [يَرَىٰٓ · grammar] (turns on: Whether context supplies the unseen itself, the man's conduct and judgment, a different implied content, or no determinate objects; al-Burhān rejects leaving both objects entirely unspecified.)
  53:35/q06/p1 Its omitted object is the unseen, perceived as plainly as something visible. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī says raʾā governs two omitted objects and the unseen is seen as plainly as the visible. Al-Fārisī, reported by al-Burhān, also supplies two objects and compares 6:73, 'Knower of the unseen.' Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies the unseen as the omitted visual object.
  53:35/q06/p2 The omitted objects are what he does and his belief that it is true and right. — reasons: Ibn Khurūf, as reported by al-Burhān, says context identifies the two omitted objects.
  53:35/q06/p3 He sees his own judgment as sufficient, with no expressed object. — reasons: Al-Rāzī offers this alternative: the man thinks he needs neither guide nor warner.
  53:35/q06/p4 The verb's objects are wholly unspecified. — reasons: Al-Burhān says some scholars adopt this analysis but rejects it for giving the verse no determinate meaning.
  53:35/q06/p5 It refers to 'the parts.' — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān records this brief alternative gloss without further explanation.
53:35/q07 How is fa-huwa yarā joined to the question? [فَهُوَ يَرَىٰٓ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the visible nominal clause is read directly or replaced by an implied subjunctive verb; al-Samīn finds no need for the replacement.)
  53:35/q07/p1 The fāʾ marks a consequence and the following words form a nominal clause. — reasons: Bursevī and al-Ālūsī call the fāʾ consequential. Al-Samīn prefers the straightforward nominal-clause construction.
  53:35/q07/p2 It stands for a verb, fa-yarā, subjunctive as an answer to the question. — reasons: Al-Samīn reports Abū al-Baqāʾ's analysis but finds the ellipsis unnecessary.
53:35/q08 What does placing huwa before yarā achieve? [فَهُوَ يَرَىٰٓ · rhetoric]
  53:35/q08/p1 It strengthens the assertion of seeing and heightens wonder at his confidence. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr connects this emphasis to the bargain he made.
53:35/q09 Whom does the question address? [* · referent] (turns on: Whether the preceding relative expression names the man in a particular report or presents a representative figure; Islahi disputes the report's chain and fit.)
  53:35/q09/p1 It addresses the person who turned away in the preceding passage, portrayed as a miser. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the pronouns refer to that person and the question repudiates him. Ibn Kathīr and Islahi read the conduct as withholding gifts; the Study Quran calls him the man who stopped giving charity.
  53:35/q09/p2 It concerns al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīrah in a reported incident. — reasons: Islahi reports exegetes' identification of this Quraysh chief and his abandoned plan to embrace Islam. Mujāhid's report in al-Durr places al-Walīd with the Prophet and Abū Bakr. Islahi objects that the report lacks a sound chain and that the generic relative expression has been treated as a proper name; Kuran Yolu likewise calls the episode reports unreliable and discordant with Qur'anic principles.
  53:35/q09/p3 It is a representative miserly idolater, depicting a wider group. — reasons: Islahi says al-ladhī and al can introduce a parable of a group's collective character, comparing the woman unravelling her thread in 16:92. He describes Arabs who refuse to spend yet expect Paradise through alleged deities and racial superiority; al-Walīd, Abū Jahl, and other wealthy Quraysh can recognize themselves in it.
53:35/q10 What incident is reported behind the rebuke? [* · reports] (turns on: The reports differ over the actor and transaction, and Islahi and Kuran Yolu question the reliability or relevance of the reported episode.)
  53:35/q10/p1 Al-Walīd abandoned conversion after someone promised to shield him from the Hereafter for payment, of which he paid only part. — reasons: Islahi recounts the exegetes' report. He objects that it has no sound chain, its link to the verses is unclear, and the Quran is not rebuking al-Walīd for breaking a promise. Kuran Yolu says such episode reports are unreliable and their details sit uneasily with Qur'anic principles.
