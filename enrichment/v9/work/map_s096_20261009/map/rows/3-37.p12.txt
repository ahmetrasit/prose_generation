[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#2/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Nabātan may be a verbal noun not matching the verb’s form, since the expected noun is inbāt.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#2/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid reads the three verbs as a supplication by Mary’s mother: rabbahā is a vocative, and Zakariyyā remains a second object as in the Kufan reading.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#2/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Ḥafṣ and al-Akhawayn shorten Zakariyyā wherever it occurs in the Qurʾān; the other seven readers lengthen it, and both forms are common Hijazi dialects.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#2/r12] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Though the foreign proper name would ordinarily be indeclinable for foreignness and properness, grammarians explain its form by analogy with feminine alif; indefinite indeclinability motivated this account.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#2/r13] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The argument for a feminine-alif analogy is that the name is indeclinable when indefinite; Abū Ḥātim allowed tanwīn in the indefinite, apparently treating it as foreign and proper, but was faulted.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Alternatively, an implicit matching verb governs nabātan, estimated as fa-nabatat nabātan ḥasanan.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The Kufans read the causative doubled kaf and put Zakariyyā in the accusative; Abū Bakr lengthens the name but also reads it accusative, while the others use a light kaf and nominative.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#2/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | With the Kufan doubled verb, Zakariyyā is the second of two objects; the short form has an understood accusative ending, while Abū Bakr’s long form shows fatḥa.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The other seven readers have a light verb, transitive to Mary alone, with Zakariyyā as subject; the readings do not differ in meaning.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#2/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبي | reports | Ubayy reads wa-akfalahā, using the hamza causative as in akramahā.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#2/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The hamza causative has the same two-object syntax and meaning as the Kufan doubled reading: God is subject, Mary the first object, and Zakariyyā the second.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#2/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · عبد الله المزني | reports | ʿAbd Allāh al-Muzanī reads kafalahā with a light kaf and kasra on fāʾ.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#2/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The kasra form is a dialect, kafila yakfulu, alongside the common kafala yakfulu; its syntax follows the other readers, with Zakariyyā as subject.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو علي الفارسي | reports | Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī argues the final hamza cannot be an original or augment letter, so it must mark feminine alif; he applies the argument to the short form too.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Al-Samīn accepts Abū ʿAlī’s analysis for an Arabic word with known derivation and inflection, but foreign names are treated analogically as Arabic forms.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#3/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Another form is zakariyy, with final doubled y and no alif; it is declinable. Abū ʿAlī explains its endings as substituted nisba yāʾs, since an original foreign ending would prevent declension.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#3/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The declinable zakariyy form is a Najdi usage and is used by neighboring tribes.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#3/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash records another form, zakar, on the pattern of ʿAmr.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#3/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Kafāla originally means guarantee, then extends to taking someone in and embracing responsibility; its forms include kāfil and kafīl.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#4/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · سيبويه | reports | Sībawayh treats al-miḥrāb as a locative adverb after dakhala; this is exceptional because a specified place normally requires fī.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#4/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash treats the noun after dakhala as a direct object; al-Samīn rejects this because non-place use requires fī, as in dakhaltu fī al-amr.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#4/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو عبيدة | reports | Abū ʿUbayda defines miḥrāb as the most honored and foremost seat, including such a place in a mosque.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#4/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو عمرو بن العلاء | reports | Abū ʿAmr ibn al-ʿAlāʾ takes miḥrāb to mean a palace because of its height and nobility.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#4/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الأصمعي | reports | Al-Aṣmaʿī defines miḥrāb as a chamber; Imruʾ al-Qays uses maḥārīb for rulers’ chambers.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#4/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A verse by ʿUmar ibn Abī Rabīʿa likewise uses miḥrāb for a chamber approached by climbing a ladder.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#4/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | For this verse, miḥrāb means the familiar mosque sanctuary; earlier definitions describe the word generally, not a disagreement about this verse.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#4/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Miḥrāb is also said to derive from ḥarb because people compete for it.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#4/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن ذكوان | reports | Ibn Dhakwān applies imāla to miḥrāb at two places in this sūra, where a kasra precedes and follows its alif; non-genitive uses allow imāla or tafkhīm. | mentions 38:21
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#5/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Wajada means found, encountered, or came upon and takes one object, rizqan; ʿindahā is normally an adverbial of finding.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#5/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abū al-Baqāʾ allows ʿindahā to be a circumstantial phrase modifying rizqan, since it could originally qualify it as an adjective and attach to an omitted predicate.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#5/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Wajada governs kullamā as a temporal adverb; Abū al-Baqāʾ calls it the conditional answer because kullamā resembles a conditional.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#5/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abū al-Baqāʾ says qāla yā Maryam is a new sentence, not a substitute for wajada because their meanings differ.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#5/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abū al-Baqāʾ also permits coordination with a deleted fāʾ, by analogy with conditional answers and a cited poem.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#5/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | Al-Samīn rejects the proposed parallel with 6:121: the conditional answer there is omitted, while inna-kum la-mushrikūn answers an implicit oath before the condition. | mentions 6:121
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#5/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The poetic example permits omission of the fāʾ only by poetic necessity and does not establish the ordinary construction.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#5/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The wajada clause is circumstantial to the subject of dakhala, and qāla itself answers kullamā: whenever Zakariyyā entered having found provision, he spoke.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#5/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The indefinite rizqan either magnifies the provision or indicates a particular kind of it.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#6/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Annā is a fronted predicate and hādhā a delayed subject; Abū ʿUbayda explains annā as min ayna, “from where.”
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#6/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | An alternative takes the question to ask how the provision came about; al-Kumayt’s line pairs annā with min ayna.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#6/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abū al-Baqāʾ parses annā as an adverb governed by the existential sense in laka, with laka raising hādhā; al-Samīn says this is unnecessary.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37#6/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The statement that God provides whom He wills may be independent divine speech or Mary’s speech, in which case it is accusative.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The majority read taqabbalahā as a past form with Rabb as its subject; God accepted Mary in place of the vowed boy, since a girl was not accepted for such a vow.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | An anonymously cited line uses al-qabūl with ḍamma on qāf, supporting that pronunciation.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjāj instead makes qabūlan governed by an implicit matching verb, estimating yutaqabbilu ḥasanan and qabilahā qabūlan ḥasanan; al-Samīn considers this strained.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37/r12] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | If the bāʾ is not extra, qabūl names a means by which something is accepted, like ladūd or saʿūṭ; it then marks Mary’s special status in place of the male vow.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Tafaʿʿala may mean the simple verb: God accepted Mary, approving her in place of the male vow; this is the interpretation reported in tafsīr.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The simple-verb use of tafaʿʿala is illustrated by taʿajjaba/ʿajiba and tabarraʾa/bariʾa.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Tafaʿʿala may also mean istifʿala: God took Mary into His care from the outset; al-Qatami’s istqbalat witnesses this sense.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The idiom khudh al-amra bi-qawābilihi and pairs such as taʿaẓẓama/istaʿẓama illustrate the frequent istifʿala sense of tafaʿʿala.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | On one analysis the bāʾ is extra and qabūlan is a verbal noun with its augment omitted; the expected form with taqabbala would be taqabbulan.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:37/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Qabūl is a verbal noun on the faʿūl pattern with fatḥa, as in qabiltu a
