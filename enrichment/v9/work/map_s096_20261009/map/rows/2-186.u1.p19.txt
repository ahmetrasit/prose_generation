2:186/q24 What other formal features of the verse are noted? [* · rhetoric] (turns on: The sources give different analyses, reports, or applications of this wording.)
  2:186/q24/p1 The order puts divine reassurance before the servant's duty. — reasons: QUSHAYRI, d. 465 (author): “Let them respond to Me” is a duty, while “I answer the caller” is reassurance and ease; God puts the reassurance before the obligation. SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT (author): The answer to the conditional precedes its condition, suggesting that God answers the servant even before the servant begins praying. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): God answers despite being wholly self-sufficient, while servants respond despite total need; this asymmetry displays divine generosity. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): God does not condition His answer on the servant answering first, so divine response is prior grace rather than payment for obedience; this refutes the Muʿtazila’s claim.
  2:186/q24/p2 The verse's first-person pronouns and present verb stress continued divine closeness. — reasons: TABATABAI, d. 1402 (author): God answers without an intermediary, asserts nearness with emphasis, and uses an adjective rather than a verb to express settled, continuing closeness. TABATABAI, d. 1402 (author): The compact verse repeats the first-person pronoun seven times, a unique pattern in the Qur'an. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): The wording assigns the servant to God and God to the servant: 'My servants ask about Me' and 'I am near.'
  2:186/q24/p3 The closing expression is a distinctive Qur'anic ending. — reasons: ITQAN, d. 911 (author): The endings after the command to lower the gaze and after the command to supplicate and respond are distinctive Quranic fawasil without a parallel.
