29:17/q25 How should reports of an occasion of revelation for a verse be assessed? [* · reports] (turns on: Reliability of transmitted occasions and whether a specific triggering event limits general Qurʾānic wording.)
  29:17/q25/p1 Claims require transmitted eyewitness evidence, since invented occasions and ignorant assertions mislead. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī condemns invented reports and says his book preserves heard and studied transmitted occasions, starting with revelation's beginnings and then treating verses with a transmitted cause. Ibn ʿĀshūr quotes his warning and agrees that weak unmarked reports create false impressions.
  29:17/q25/p2 Occasions can clarify a verse, but should be scrutinized; general wording normally governs beyond its occasion. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says verses may indicate their own occasion, transmission can establish others, and ignoring the topic risks error; he excuses early compilers' breadth but criticizes later acceptance of weak reports. He says revelation guides society without awaiting events. Most scholars privilege general wording; he rejects a minority restriction to the occasion and warns against reports that distort general or unrestricted language.
  29:17/q25/p3 An occasion confines a verse's general wording to that specific event. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this as a deviant minority claim and rejects its tendency to restrict general wording.
29:17/q26 How is apparent conflict between human 'creating' and God's sole creation resolved? [وَتَخْلُقُونَ · theology]
  29:17/q26/p1 God alone originates; human خلق here is fabrication of a lie, while Jesus's خلق is shaping clay. — reasons: Al-Qāḍī Abū Bakr, reported in Burhān and Itqān, cites God's sole creative power (6:102; 39:62) and the clay bird of 5:110. Ibn Qutayba and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ likewise distinguish divine origination from human shaping and false speech; al-Rāghib's reported distinction reflects objections to calling the Qurʾān created.
29:17/q27 What principles address alleged contradictions or abrogation when this verse is cited? [* · sciences] (turns on: Whether the apparent opposition can be reconciled, and if not how chronology or consensus establishes abrogation.)
  29:17/q27/p1 Revelation and sound reason do not genuinely conflict; apparent differences should be reconciled by meaning and context. — reasons: Al-Qāḍī Abū Bakr gives خلق here versus God's creation and Jesus's shaping as an example. Al-Ṣayrafī says genuine contradiction would require opposite meanings in every respect, which Qurʾān and Sunna do not have; abrogation concerns different times.
  29:17/q27/p2 If two verses cannot be reconciled, chronology or consensus identifies the later abrogating rule. — reasons: Al-Isfarāyīnī says known chronology settles it, otherwise consensus on one practice identifies the abrogating verse, and says no unresolved case falls outside these conditions.
29:17/q28 What neighboring textual or recitational details are cited with this verse? [* · links] (turns on: The notes cite distinct neighboring reading details rather than competing analyses of one word.)
  29:17/q28/p1 A neighboring verb يبدا is read with softened rather than fully realized hamza. — reasons: Ibn Jinnī reports al-Zuhrī and argues true replacement would lead to yāʾ then alif, comparing Abū Zayd's poetic ملا for ملأ.
  29:17/q28/p2 A connected recitation reads أوثانا مودةً with accusative tanwīn and بينكم accusative. — reasons: Al-Durr transmits this from ʿĀṣim together with his light reading of afkan.
29:17/q29 What practical ruling is associated with avoiding an idol? [أَوْثَٰنًۭا · law]
  29:17/q29/p1 Do not treat a prayer staff as an idol; pray standing if able, otherwise sitting, otherwise lying. — reasons: Ibn ʿUmar gives this response when asked about a sick person's praying on a staff.
29:17/q30 What material are the idols referred to here made from? [أَوْثَٰنًۭا · referent]
  29:17/q30/p1 They are idols made of stone. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī reports this identification from Ibn ʿAbbās.
29:17/q31 What does the reported saying of Jesus imply about seeking provision and the two worlds? [فَٱبْتَغُوا۟ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرِّزْقَ · inward]
  29:17/q31/p1 People truly desire neither this world nor the next if they do not obey the Lord of either. — reasons: Tustarī reports Jesus saying obedience to the Lord of either world would bring it, yet people obey neither.
