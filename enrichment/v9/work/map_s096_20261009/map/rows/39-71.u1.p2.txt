39:71/q34 Why does the conclusion describe Hell's people first? [* · links]
  39:71/q34/p1 Warning dominates here, so the account starts with those facing wrath. — reasons: Biqa'i notes the warning-heavy setting. Ibn Ashur says renewed warning addresses people unmoved by repeated admonition, whereas the saved have already responded and their mention repeats good news and praise. Islahi says the closing fates awaken those complacent about their hopes.
39:71/q35 How should the afterlife scene be understood? [* · theology]
  39:71/q35/p1 Its realities belong to faith; the descriptions make them vivid and call for belief and action. — reasons: Kuranyolu says resurrection, judgment, scales, path, Paradise and Hell are unique, unrealized events beyond human knowledge. Figurative descriptions evoke helpful associations; exegeses are personal, not binding certainties. One should believe Qur'an and authentic hadith and follow their purpose.
39:71/q36 What does the passage imply about the Qur'an as proof? [* · links]
  39:71/q36/p1 The Qur'an's clear Arabic warning establishes compelling proof; rejecting its signs merits punishment. — reasons: Baqillani cites 26:192–195 for revelation through the Trustworthy Spirit to Muhammad. He says sūras with disconnected letters establish inimitability; 40:4–6 call disputing God's signs disbelief and recount prior punishment; 40:7 praises believers through the angels' prayer.
39:71/q37 How are the related singular and plural forms written and paused on? [كَلِمَةُ · readings]
  39:71/q37/p1 Singular readers pause by their usual hāʾ or tāʾ practice, whereas plural readers stop with tāʾ. — reasons: Nashr says codices generally write these forms with tāʾ; al-Dani notes an Iraqi-copy exception in Yunus. The Ghafir spelling may indicate either number, though tāʾ copies there are read plural as far as known.
39:71/q38 What does a report say Hell does to its people? [إِلَىٰ جَهَنَّمَ · reports]
  39:71/q38/p1 It lashes them with a neck that strips flesh from their bones down to the ankles. — reasons: Durr reports this from Abu Hurayra.
39:71/q39 What wider warning does their confession recall? [قَالُوا۟ بَلَىٰ · links]
  39:71/q39/p1 Other passages describe the deniers' admission that they rejected revelation despite a warner. — reasons: Islahi cites 67:8–10, where they regret not listening or reasoning. Study Quran cites 67:9–10 for denial of revelation and the missed chance to avoid the Blaze.
39:71/q40 Why is the sūra called al-Zumar? [زُمَرًا · sciences]
  39:71/q40/p1 Its name comes from both disbelievers and believers being driven in throngs to their destinations. — reasons: Study Quran gives the title's source.
39:71/q41 What further comparison does the gate opening invite? [فُتِحَتْ أَبْوَٰبُهَا · links]
  39:71/q41/p1 The opening of Hell's gates is compared with 16:29. — reasons: Study Quran gives this cross reference.
39:71/q42 How does greeting at Paradise's gates compare with this scene? [خَزَنَتُهَآ · reports]
  39:71/q42/p1 Paradise's people receive greetings at three ranks: from keepers, from angels at every gate, or from God. — reasons: Al-Sulami reports Ibn Ata's ranks: the lowest are greeted by keepers, the middle by angels entering through every gate, and the highest by God; he cites 39:73, 13:23 and 36:58.
