[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:29:45/r9] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Prayer observed with care for outward pillars and inward qualities is incompatible with indecency and evil and restrains the worshipper from them.
[STUDYQURAN:29:45/r1] STUDYQURAN · IJ, Q, R | reports | “Prayer” here refers to the obligatory prayers.
[STUDYQURAN:29:45/r10] STUDYQURAN · Prophet | reports | The Prophet compares the five daily prayers to washing in a river five times daily: prayer removes one’s sins just as washing removes filth.
[STUDYQURAN:29:45/r11] STUDYQURAN · al-Ghazzālī | reports | Al-Ghazzālī says prayer was prescribed to polish the heart and renew remembrance of God, not merely to perform its words and motions.
[STUDYQURAN:29:45/r12] STUDYQURAN · al-Tustarī | reports | Al-Tustarī says prayer that does not restrain shameful and unjust acts, and lacks the adornment of shunning them, is tainted and must be purified.
[STUDYQURAN:29:45/r13] STUDYQURAN · IJ, Sh, Ṭ | reports | One meaning is that remembering God is greater than everything else, including other acts of worship.
[STUDYQURAN:29:45/r14] STUDYQURAN · Bḍ, IJ, Sh, Su, Ṭ, Z | reports | Another meaning is that God’s remembrance of human beings exceeds their remembrance of Him.
[STUDYQURAN:29:45/r15] STUDYQURAN · Su | reports | This reading is explained by God’s remembrance being eternal while human remembrance is temporal.
[STUDYQURAN:29:45/r16] STUDYQURAN · Sh, Ṭ | reports | Some hold that both the believer’s remembrance of God and God’s remembrance of believers are meant at once.
[STUDYQURAN:29:45/r17] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Sufi interpretation joins both senses: full remembrance requires annihilation of selfhood, so God remembers Himself through people beyond time and place.
[STUDYQURAN:29:45/r18] STUDYQURAN · IJ, Ṭ | reports | Some read the phrase to mean that remembering God during prayer is greater than the indecency and abomination prayer prevents.
[STUDYQURAN:29:45/r19] STUDYQURAN · R | reports | Another reading is that believers’ remembrance of God is greater than idolaters’ remembrance of their gods.
[STUDYQURAN:29:45/r2] STUDYQURAN · IJ, Ṭ | reports | Some interpret “prayer” as Quran recitation, a form of supererogatory worship; al-Rāzī rejects this because the verse already commands Quran recitation.
[STUDYQURAN:29:45/r20] STUDYQURAN · Bḍ, Z | reports | A further reading takes “remembrance of God” as prayer, which is greater than every other act of obedience.
[STUDYQURAN:29:45/r21] STUDYQURAN · Prophet | reports | The Prophet is reported to say that the world and everything in it are accursed except remembrance of God and what supports it.
[STUDYQURAN:29:45/r3] STUDYQURAN · al-Rāzī | rejects | Al-Rāzī argues against interpreting prayer as recitation because the verse has already mentioned reciting the Quran.
[STUDYQURAN:29:45/r4] STUDYQURAN · IJ | reports | The paired commands may mean that prayer prevents sin when one reflects on the Quran recited during it.
[STUDYQURAN:29:45/r5] STUDYQURAN · IJ | reports | Others understand prayer in and of itself to prevent indecency and abomination.
[STUDYQURAN:29:45/r6] STUDYQURAN · Prophet / Ibn Masʿūd | reports | A saying, variously attributed to the Prophet or his Companion Ibn Masʿūd, says prayer that fails to prevent indecency and abomination only increases distance from God.
[STUDYQURAN:29:45/r7] STUDYQURAN · Z | reports | Prayer may not prevent sin without sincerity and repentance; God accepts only from the reverent, as 5:27 says. | mentions 5:27
[STUDYQURAN:29:45/r8] STUDYQURAN · Prophet | reports | The Prophet says a young man who prayed with him but inclined toward indecency would soon be restrained by his prayer.
[STUDYQURAN:29:45/r9] STUDYQURAN · Ṭ | reports | Indecency may mean fornication, while abomination may mean disobedience.
