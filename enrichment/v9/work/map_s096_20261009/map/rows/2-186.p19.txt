[TABATABAI:2:186#6/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The preceding reports call for sincerity in prayer; they do not deny created causes, which God appoints as intermediaries rather than independent sources of effect.
[TABATABAI:2:186#6/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Innate awareness recognizes a true, unfailing giver behind needs and also knows apparent causes may fail; therefore one should not rely on a visible cause as though separate from the real source.
[TABATABAI:2:186#6/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | When a requested need is fulfilled, the request has in truth reached God through causes; asking a visible cause as if it alone sufficed is an imaginative construction, not innate awareness.
[TABATABAI:2:186#6/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Innate desire can oppose a person's words or imagined wishes, like the sick child who rejects bitter medicine while naturally seeking health; the natural order is sound, whereas imagination often errs, so the reports concern the heart’s true request.
[TABATABAI:2:186#7/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم | reports | The Prophet teaches believers to pray while certain of the response.
