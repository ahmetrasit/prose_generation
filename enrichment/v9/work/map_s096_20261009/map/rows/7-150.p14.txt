[SAMIN-DURR:7:150#3/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · البصريون | reports | On the Basran account of the fatḥa reading, ابن أم is a compound like خمسة عشر, built on fatḥa rather than an iḍāfa construction.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:150#3/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الكوفيون | reports | The Kufans treat ابن as annexed to أم, itself annexed to the first-person yāʾ; yāʾ becomes alif and is deleted, with fatḥa substituting for it, so ابن is inflected and أم is genitive.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:150#3/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · البصريون | reports | For the kasra reading, Basrans treat the whole compound as annexed to the speaker's yāʾ, built with kasra as its substitute; they reject an ordinary iḍāfa because this genitive complement is not a vocative from which yāʾ may be dropped.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:150#3/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الكوفيون | reports | For the kasra reading, Kufans take the kasra as inflection, with the deleted yāʾ represented by kasra, analogously to fatḥa representing a deleted alif.

[SAMIN-DURR:7:150#4/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The standard reading has damma on ت and kasra on م in تشمت, from the quadriliteral أشمت, with الأعداء as its object.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:150#4/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن محيصن ومجاهد | reports | Ibn Muḥayṣin reads تشمت with fatḥa on ت and kasra on م; Mujāhid has fatḥa on both, and both take الأعداء as object.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:150#4/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The clearest analysis makes the triliteral تشمت itself transitive like أشمت; this needs no implied subject or object.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:150#4/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن جني | reports | Ibn Jinnī suggests that تشمت is addressed to God, with an implied object for الأعداء; the author rejects this as needless, since the triliteral verb is already transitive.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:150#4/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | prefers | Abū al-Baqāʾ reads the implied subject in the alternative parse as Moses himself, which the author says is preferable to making God the subject.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:150#4/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The analogy with God mocking or scheming is valid only as a rhetorical counter to the opponents' mockery or scheming; such countering is absent here. | mentions 2:14, 3:54
[SAMIN-DURR:7:150#5/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Al-ishmāt and شماتة mean delight in a misfortune that befalls one's enemy; a poem says death is preferable to enemies' gloating.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:150#5/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | An etymology relates شماتة to an animal's legs, because a gloating heart shifts between joy and grief as an animal's legs shift.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:150#5/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو عبيد | reports | Abū ʿUbayd says the form with shīn is the more elevated of the two ways of saying a prayer for a sneezer.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:150#5/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ثعلب | reports | Thaʿlab says the original is the form with sīn, from samt, meaning direction and guidance.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:150#5/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | One explanation of the shīn form of the sneezer's prayer is a wish that God steady the sneezer as an animal's legs are steady.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:150#5/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Another explanation treats the intensive form as privative: may God remove gloating from the sneezer; with sīn it means restoring him to his former condition after his disturbance.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:150#5/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو بكر | reports | Abū Bakr records the usages سمته and سمت عليه, including the hadith wording وسمت عليهما.
