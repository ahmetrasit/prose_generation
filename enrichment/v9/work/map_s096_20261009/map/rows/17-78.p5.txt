[RAZI-FULL:v21p22#2/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · عطاء | reports | In Ibn Jurayj’s report, ʿAṭāʾ says ghassaq is the beginning of night as it enters.

[RAZI-FULL:v21p22#2/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · الأزهري | reports | Al-Azharī places ghassaq at the disappearance of twilight, when darkness gathers and intensifies; he compares it to an eye full of tears or a wound full of blood.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p22#2/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | prefers | Al-Rāzī prefers accumulated darkness at twilight’s disappearance: this interpretation includes all four prayers in the interval, whereas onset darkness would include only ẓuhr and maghrib.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p22#2/r7] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | If ghassaq is the first darkness, the verse names noon, first maghrib, and dawn, making noon shared by ẓuhr and ʿaṣr and first night shared by maghrib and ʿishāʾ; combining is then allowed for travel, rain, and similar excuses, not without excuse at home.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p22#2/r8] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | If ghassaq is accumulated darkness at the disappearance of white twilight, the endpoint leaves prayer lawful before then; this fits the view that the prayers become due at red twilight.

[RAZI-FULL:v21p22/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | rejects | The sunset evidence does not exclude noon: dulūk means inclination or change, which occurs at sunset, while sunset is only one kind of such movement, as human and horse are both animals.

[RAZI-FULL:v21p22/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · الواحدي | reports | Al-Wāḥidī takes the lām in li-dulūk as causal: prayer becomes due at the sun’s decline, so it is performed on account of that decline.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p23/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The commentators agree that Qurʾān al-fajr means the dawn prayer; it is coordinated with al-ṣalāh, with the sense, establish prayer and establish the dawn recitation.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p23/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The wording shows that prayer is not complete without recitation.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p23/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | prefers | Because the command attaches recitation to dawn’s first appearance, obligation would seem to begin then; consensus removes obligation, leaving preference, so al-Rāzī says the verse supports al-Shāfiʿī’s view that praying at first light (taghlīs) is better than waiting for brightening.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p23/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Jurists hold that dawn prayer should have a longer recitation than other prayers; singling out Qurʾān at dawn marks the feature as especially complete and desirable.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p23/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · الجمهور | reports | The majority say dawn prayer is witnessed because night and day angels gather behind the imam; day angels arrive during prayer, and after it night angels ascend reporting the worshippers and God’s forgiveness.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p23/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | This angelic gathering supports taghlīs: night angels remain in the darkness at the prayer’s start, day angels arrive as its extended recitation brings daylight, so both witness it.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p24/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Another reading of witnessed dawn prayer is that the change from darkness to light displays God’s power; contemplating the world’s transition from death and stillness to life and motion during prayer illuminates knowledge and certainty.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p24/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | This spiritual interpretation depends on beginning at taghlīs, when the transformation can be observed during the prayer.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p24/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | A third interpretation is witnessing through a large congregation: dawn prayer purifies hearts especially strongly, and worshippers reflect one another’s knowledge of God like facing mirrors reflecting sunlight, bringing spaciousness and comfort.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p24/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Sleep clears the mind’s tablet of worldly impressions; rising straight into worship and recitation inscribes sacred thoughts that prevent corrupt worldly desires from taking hold, so the benefit requires prayer at first light.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p24/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Because love of the world, greed, envy, and rivalry are diseases of hearts, prophets act like skilled physicians; beginning obedience on waking helps reduce this hard-to-cure illness and is therefore prescribed.
