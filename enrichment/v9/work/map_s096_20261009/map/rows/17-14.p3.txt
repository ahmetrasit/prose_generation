[ALUSI-FULL:v15p33/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | A mystical-philosophical interpretation identifies the book with the soul bearing hidden imprints of deeds, revealed after bodily separation; the author says this is remote from the plain sense and treats resurrection as a lesser resurrection.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p33/r8] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الإمام | reports | The Imam says the narrated outward events are true and the verse can also bear spiritual meanings, so one should affirm both; Khafaji says this need not contradict transmitted reports.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p33/r9] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | rejects | The author doubts that the spiritual reading is an apparent meaning or that Qatada’s statement supports it; reading the illiterate person’s record literally is itself an alternative interpretation.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p34/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الإمام | reports | The Imam’s first coherence link is that the Qur’an has supplied needed proofs of divine oneness, prophethood, and resurrection, leaving no excuse when people face judgment.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p34/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الإمام | reports | Second, God’s many religious and worldly benefits require service and obedience, so each person is questioned about words and deeds.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p34/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الإمام | reports | Third, since God created humans to worship and made material things useful for them, judgment asks whether each person obeyed or rebelled. | mentions 51:56
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p34/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | The author offers another link: the preceding passage describes God’s book of guidance, while this one describes the human record that omits no small or great deed; a person’s record reflects whether he followed that first book. | mentions 18:49
