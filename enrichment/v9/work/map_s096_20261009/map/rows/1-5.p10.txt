[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:5#2/r7] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | Repeating إياك explicitly reserves seeking help for God, while placing worship first preserves rhyme and teaches that presenting a means before one's request aids its acceptance.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:5#2/r8] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | Saying “we worship” might sound boastful, so “we seek Your help” clarifies that worship itself depends on God's aid and enabling.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:5#2/r9] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | reports | The conjunction may instead express circumstance: “we worship You while seeking Your help.”
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:5/r1] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | After identifying the one worthy of praise, the discourse turns from absence to direct address and from reasoning to witnessing: the servants reserve worship and seeking aid for Him.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:5/r2] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | The shift of person is an Arabic device that refreshes speech and engages listeners, as in 10:22 and 35:9 and the cited poetry of Imruʾ al-Qays. | mentions 10:22, 35:9
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:5/r3] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | إيا is the accusative pronoun stem and the attached letters distinguish speaker, addressee, and absent person; the attached forms have no grammatical case of their own.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:5/r4] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · الخليل | reports | Al-Khalīl considered the following suffix to be annexed to إيا, citing a colloquial example; al-Bayḍāwī says this evidence is anomalous and unreliable.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:5/r5] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | reports | Other grammatical analyses treat إيا as the pronoun with an added support element, or treat the entire form as the pronoun.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:5/r6] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | reports | Readings include opening إيا's hamza and replacing it with hāʾ.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:5/r7] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | ʿIbāda is the utmost submission and abasement, and is used only for submission to God.
[NASAFI:1:1/r12] NASAFI, d. 710 · أبو هريرة عن النبي | reports | Abu Hurayra reports God’s dialogue dividing the Fatiha between Himself and the servant: praise, glorification, and exaltation belong to God; the worshipper’s request for guidance and the response to it belong to the servant, while worship and aid are shared.
[NASAFI:1:5/r1] NASAFI, d. 710 · الخليل وسيبويه | reports | Al-Khalil and Sibawayh parse iyya as an independent pronoun; for Sibawayh the kaf is an uninflected address particle, while for al-Khalil it is an attached pronoun in annexation to iyya.
[NASAFI:1:5/r2] NASAFI, d. 710 · الكوفيون | reports | The Kufans regard iyaka as a complete pronoun rather than dividing it into iyya and the kaf.
[NASAFI:1:5/r3] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | Putting the object before the verb expresses exclusivity: worshippers devote worship, the utmost humility and submission, and their request for aid to God alone.
[NASAFI:1:5/r4] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | The verse shifts from speaking about God in the third person to addressing Him directly after praise establishes His attributes and greatness; the worshipper turns to the uniquely worthy addressee to worship and seek help from no other.
[NASAFI:1:5/r5] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | Iltifat can shift from third person to address, address to third person, or third person to first person; Nasafi illustrates the pattern with shifts in Qur’an 10:22 and 35:9 and Imru’ al-Qays’s poem, where the speaker changes person across its three lines. | mentions 10:22, 35:9
[NASAFI:1:5/r6] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | Arabs favor shifts of style because they engage listeners, renew attention, and make listening more pleasurable; particular placements also carry subtleties recognized by expert scholars.
[NASAFI:1:5/r7] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | reports | Worship precedes asking for aid either because presenting a means before asking for a need makes a response likelier, or to preserve the verse order, as with al-Rahman’s precedence despite the greater rhetorical force of the other name.
[NASAFI:1:5/r8] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | The unqualified request for aid embraces every kind of help; alternatively, it asks God’s help and enabling for worship, with ‘guide us’ specifying the requested assistance. | mentions 1:6
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p7#3/r1] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | reports | Sibawayh treats iya as a detached pronoun and the kaf as an address particle without syntactic position; al-Khalil treats iya as the pronoun with the kaf attached to it.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p7#3/r2] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · الكوفيون | reports | The Kufans treat the whole form iyaka as one pronoun.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p7#3/r3] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | Fronting the object marks exclusivity: worship and the seeking of aid are directed to God alone; worship is the utmost submission and abasement.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p7#3/r4] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | The shift from third-person reference to direct address is iltifat; Arabic rhetoric uses such shifts to refresh attention and heighten reception, and the cited Qur'anic and poetic examples illustrate its forms. | mentions 10:22, 30:48
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p7#3/r5] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | Direct address follows the great descriptions of God: the one worthy of praise, humility, and aid is addressed as the uniquely distinguished Lord described above.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p7#3/r6] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | reports | Worship precedes asking for aid because presenting the means before a request brings a response closer; alternatively, the order follows the preceding arrangement of the verses.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p7#3/r7] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | The request for aid is unrestricted and covers every need; alternatively it asks for God's help and enabling grace in performing acts of worship, which the following request for guidance specifies.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:5/r1] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Iyyā takes attached first, second, or third person pronouns and forms an independent accusative pronoun, not an overt noun with an annexed suffix, contrary to an unnamed claimant.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:5/r10] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Annexing iyyā to an overt noun is rare, or a poetic necessity; the text gives examples of both.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:5/r11] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | In the familiar warning use, iyyā can contain a nominative pronoun that is followed by a nominative emphatic pronoun.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:5/r12] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الجمهور | reports | The majority explain worship as humility and submission, which Abū Ḥayyān later identifies as the basic lexical sense.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:5/r13] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · ابن السكيت | reports | Ibn al-Sikkīt gives tajrīd, stripping or devotion, as an alternative meaning of worship.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:5/r14] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The transitive sense changes with form: ʿabadt al-rajul means I humbled the man, while ʿabadt Allāh means I humbled myself to God.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:5/r15] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الحسن، وأبو مجلز، وأبو المتوكل | reports | Al-Ḥasan, Abū Majliz, and Abū al-Mutawakkil read iyyāka yaʿbudu with yāʾ and a passive verb.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:5/r16] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · بعض أهل مكة | reports | Some Meccans read naʿbud with the dāl unvowelled.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:5/r17] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · زيد بن علي، ويحيى بن وثاب، وعبيد بن عمير الليثي | reports | Zayd ibn ʿAlī, Yaḥyā ibn Waththāb, and ʿUbayd ibn ʿUmayr al-Laythī read naʿbud with kasra on its initial nūn.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:5/r18] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Istiʿāna means seeking aid; the form istifʿala has twelve uses, including request, transformation, treating something as something, and several forms of equivalence or substitution.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:5/r19] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · صاحب اللوامح | reports | The author of al-Lawāmiʿ reports a form wayyāka, with hamza changed to wāw, but is unsure whether it came from al-Farrāʾ or Arab usage.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:5/r2] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | Grammarians differ over whether the pronoun is the whole form or only part of it, whether the suffixes are letters or nouns, and whether iyyā is added solely to host pronouns.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:5/r20] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The change to wāw reflects aversion to hamza; this reverses the alternative tendency to replace a kasra-bearing wāw with hamza.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:5/r21] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · أبو الحسن بن سيده | reports | Ibn Sīda says tamāsaktu, masaktu, amsaktu, and istamsaktu bi-shayʾ have the same sense, holding fast to something; this adds two equivalence uses to istifʿala.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:5/r22] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Istiʿāna means asking for help, as in other verbs of request.
