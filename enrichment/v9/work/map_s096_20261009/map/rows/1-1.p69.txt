[IBNASHUR:1:1/r102] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The noun ism denotes a word assigned to a particular concrete or abstract entity, whether an individual or a kind.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r103] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أئمة البصرة | reports | Basran grammarians derive ism from sumūw, elevation, since names often denote things esteemed; its original form had a final weak wāw deleted for ease or frequent use.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r104] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The forms asmāʾ, asāmī, sumayy, and sammaytu support a weak final wāw; Ibn ʿĀshūr says these inflections make the Basran morphology stronger.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r105] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أبو خالد القناني | reports | A rajaz line by Abū Khālid al-Qanānī uses suman for name; Ibn Yaʿīsh doubts it proves the form, while ʿAbd al-Ḥakīm appeals to its written ya.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r106] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | prefers | Ibn ʿĀshūr favors the Basran derivation on morphological evidence; spelling does not establish pronunciation, while the Kufan account has stronger semantic motivation.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r107] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · الكوفيون | reports | Kufan grammarians derive ism from wasm, mark, because a name is a sign; they say initial wāw was dropped and hamzat al-waṣl preserves the form.
