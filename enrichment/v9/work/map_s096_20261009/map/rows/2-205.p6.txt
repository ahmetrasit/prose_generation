[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p331/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | Reported interpretations specify corruption in religion, supply أهل before الفساد, gloss it as sins God does not approve (Ibn Abbas), or use love figuratively for command: God does not command corruption.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p331/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib defines corruption as moving something from a praiseworthy state without a valid purpose, which does not occur in God's acts.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p331/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | These interpretive maneuvers generally follow theologians who take divine love to mean will.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p331/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn Atiyya says love adds a quality of preference beyond will; because God loves only what is wholly good, corruption can be willed without being loved.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p331/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · جمهور العلماء | prefers | The majority reject the Muʿtazili equation of love and will: a person may want a painful wound without loving it, and 39:7 likewise says God does not approve disbelief. | mentions 39:7
