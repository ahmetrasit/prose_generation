## THE CURRENT MAP
92:18/q01 Who is the Godfearing giver? [ٱلَّذِى · referent] (turns on: Whether the named occasion limits the wording, and whether the Medinan palm story can be the occasion of an early Makkan sūra.)
  92:18/q01/p1 Abu Bakr. — reasons: Tustari calls him the truthful one, who abandoned the perishable for the everlasting; Thaʿlabi, Baghawi, and others connect the verse to his freeing persecuted Muslims. Itqan claims consensus and denies general wording. Kuranyolu and Study Quran report this as an occasion while allowing a general message.
  92:18/q01/p2 Abu al-Dahdah. — reasons: Thaʿlabi and Qurtubi connect him to the donated date palm; Qurtubi contrasts him with its miserly owner. Study Quran recounts this as a Medinan report, but doubts it as the occasion if the sūra is early Makkan.
  92:18/q01/p3 Ali. — reasons: Al-Razi reports the Shiʿi identification, linked to alms given while bowing in 5:55; they reject the Abu Bakr report.
  92:18/q01/p4 Anyone who gives seeking purification; Abu Bakr can be one instance. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says an unusual named instance does not restrict general wording. Kuranyolu says the message and rulings remain general regardless of occasion. Study Quran's early Makkan dating favors a general condemnation and praise.
92:18/q02 What does يُؤْتِى convey as a word? [يُؤْتِى · meaning]
  92:18/q02/p1 Giving readily and generously. — reasons: Bint al-Shati compares a flood and a fruiting tree that yield readily; she cites 2:265, 13:35, 14:25, and 18:33. She says Quranic forms of zakat recur with giving, citing 23:4 and 24:37, 21:73, 4:162, 2:177, 9:11, 9:18, 24:56, 2:43, 2:83, 2:110, 2:277, 4:77, 9:5, 22:41, 22:78, 5:12, and 30:39.
  92:18/q02/p2 Giving or spending wealth. — reasons: Alusi glosses the verb as giving and spending.
92:18/q03 Where or for what is the wealth given? [يُؤْتِى مَالَهُۥ · interpretation]
  92:18/q03/p1 It is spent in charity, obedience, and good causes, including help to the poor. — reasons: Baydawi, Biqai, and Alusi specify good causes; Nasafi specifies the poor. Ibn Kathir says obedience to God, purifying what God granted in religious and worldly life. Jishumi includes charity and other obedient acts. Qutb adds that giving is voluntary, without seeking thanks.
92:18/q04 Does the giving denote an obligation or voluntary good works? [يُؤْتِى مَالَهُۥ · law] (turns on: Whether the verse's giving is read as imposed alms or as freely chosen good works.)
  92:18/q04/p1 It includes fulfilling the wealth obligations God imposed, including obligatory alms. — reasons: Tabari says the obligations purify the giver of sins. Jishumi names charity and obligatory alms. Bint al-Shati describes religious zakat as obligatory wealth and says God's blessing and reward cause growth.
  92:18/q04/p2 The apparent setting is recommended or voluntary giving. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya explicitly calls the apparent sense recommended works; Qutb describes the giver as acting voluntarily.
92:18/q05 What does مَالَهُۥ denote? [مَالَهُۥ · meaning]
  92:18/q05/p1 Useful owned property and its yields; in some Arab usage, specifically date palms. — reasons: Ibn Ashur names livestock, land, private wells, and trees as generic māl. He says some Arabs, including Yathrib's people, used the word for date palms.
92:18/q06 Must the giver give all his wealth? [مَالَهُۥ · interpretation] (turns on: The distinction between what the generic wording requires and what a report says one giver did.)
  92:18/q06/p1 No; the generic construct does not imply totality. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says māl in this construction is generic, so the verse does not require surrendering everything owned.
  92:18/q06/p2 Abu Bakr spent all forty thousand he had. — reasons: Urwa's account in Thaʿlabi says he freed Bilal and others and gave away all forty thousand; this is a reported individual act, not a rule about the verse's grammar.
92:18/q07 What does يَتَزَكَّىٰ mean lexically? [يَتَزَكَّىٰ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the verb describes the giver's sought purity or the act of paying zakat.)
  92:18/q07/p1 To seek purification and growth in goodness. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya gives both purity and increase. Ibn Ashur defines tazakki as seeking zakāʾ, growth in goodness; Bint al-Shati traces growth to ripening fruit and explains nonphysical goodness and blessing, self-praise in 53:32, and refinement in 3:164 and 62:2. Corpus Coranicum compares 91:9 and 91:15.
  92:18/q07/p2 To pay zakat or alms. — reasons: Kashshaf offers the verb form for giving zakat; Nasafi and Abu Hayyan also derive it from zakāh or tazkiya. Bint al-Shati says all 32 Quranic occurrences of the religious term zakat mean obligatory wealth, and compares 9:103 where wealth is purified; Study Quran notes the shared root.
92:18/q08 What purification does the giver seek? [يَتَزَكَّىٰ · interpretation] (turns on: Which object or aspect of purification the commentators emphasize: standing before God, removal of sins, or the giver and wealth together.)
  92:18/q08/p1 Purity and growth before God, with His acceptance and pleasure rather than public praise. — reasons: Abu Ishaq, Wahidi, Baghawi, Kashshaf, Ibn al-Jawzi, Qurtubi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi, and Qutb reject ostentation and reputation. Qutb adds self-exaltation, repayment, and thanks; Ibn Ashur adds benefit and increased reward and contrasts idolaters' pride, display, mischief, and debauchery. Elmalili includes spiritual growth and prosperity. Corpus Coranicum says the longer portrait makes generosity ethical purification for the Lord's face, not repayment.
  92:18/q08/p2 Cleansing himself from sins. — reasons: Tabari and Nahhas state this directly; Alusi offers it as an alternative to seeking pure, growing wealth before God.
  92:18/q08/p3 Purifying both the person and the wealth God granted. — reasons: Ibn Kathir includes the self, wealth, and gifts in religion and worldly life. Islahi says believers spend to cleanse their wealth; Study Quran says charity purifies giver and wealth.
92:18/q09 What inward attachments are removed by this giving? [يَتَزَكَّىٰ · inward]
  92:18/q09/p1 Love of rivals, attachment to otherness, attention to anything besides God, and hidden shirk. — reasons: Qashani names each attachment as what the gift purges.
92:18/q10 How is يَتَزَكَّىٰ related grammatically to يُؤْتِى? [يُؤْتِى يَتَزَكَّىٰ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the imperfect states the giver's circumstance, substitutes for a preceding clause or verb, or depends on omitted purpose particles; objections concern case matching and the source of nominative inflection.)
  92:18/q10/p1 An accusative circumstantial clause describing the subject as he gives. — reasons: Jurjani says an affirmative imperfect circumstantial clause normally lacks wāw, citing this verse, 74:6, 7:186, and poetry. Abu Hayyan calls it usual and al-Zamakhshari's substitute strained; Bint al-Shati prefers it. Ibn Ashur says this parse conveys the giver's motive and increased reward.
  92:18/q10/p2 A substitute for the relative clause, with no independent syntactic position. — reasons: Kashshaf, Baydawi, Nasafi, and others report or allow this. Alusi records the objection that the relative clause has no case to follow, and the response that correspondence can mean matching the presence or absence of case; al-Sayyid Isa defines a follower as a second item that would be inflected if inflectable. Abu Hayyan and Bint al-Shati call it strained.
  92:18/q10/p3 The verb alone substitutes for the preceding verb. — reasons: Alusi reports this second substitution. Critics say its nominative is already explained by absence of a governor; the response allows a case to be intended despite an independent license, and al-Sayyid Isa states the follower test.
  92:18/q10/p4 An implied purposive li-an precedes the verb; both lām and an are omitted. — reasons: Tabrisi invokes deletion of an with a line by Tarafa. Alusi reports that deleting lām then an permits a raised or subjunctive verb, illustrated by two transmitted readings of Tarafa's aḥḍur.
92:18/q11 How is يَتَزَكَّىٰ read? [يَتَزَكَّىٰ · readings] (turns on: Whether the tāʾ of tazakkā remains pronounced or assimilates into zāy.)
  92:18/q11/p1 The common reading is yatazakkā, imperfect of tazakkā. — reasons: Abu Hayyan and al-Samin call it the majority or common reading.
  92:18/q11/p2 Al-Hasan ibn Ali reads yazakkā, assimilating tāʾ into zāy. — reasons: Abu Hayyan, al-Samin, and Alusi attribute the assimilated form to al-Hasan ibn Ali ibn al-Hasan ibn Ali ibn Abi Talib; Alusi Full writes it يزكي.
92:18/q12 What does the giving teach about favors and repayment? [* · interpretation]
  92:18/q12/p1 The giver seeks God's reward without settling a favor owed to anyone. — reasons: Razi says repaying a benefactor merely settles a debt; reward follows acting on God's command. Ibn Kathir says Abu Bakr owed no one repayment although Arab leaders benefited from him. Qutb rejects thanks and repayment, and Kuranyolu says all blessings come from God. Corpus Coranicum links the ethical motive to seeking the Lord's face.
92:18/q13 What happened when Abu Bakr freed persecuted Muslims? [* · reports]
  92:18/q13/p1 He freed Bilal and six others before the migration. — reasons: Ibn Ishaq names Amir ibn Fuhayra, Umm Umays, Zahra, al-Nahdiyya and her daughter, and a Banu al-Muammal girl. Baghawi Full's list varies by omitting Zahra but says six more. Urwa says seven including Bilal and Amir. Reports emphasize their vulnerability and torture.
