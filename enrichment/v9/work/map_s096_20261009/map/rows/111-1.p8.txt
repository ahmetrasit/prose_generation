[NASAFI:111:1/r12] NASAFI, d. 710 · مكي | reports | Nasafi attributes the form أبي لهب to the Meccan reader (Makkī).
[NASAFI:111:1/r2] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | Tabba means ruin; an Arabic expression he cites describes someone ruined by old age, and he glosses the hands as perished.
[NASAFI:111:1/r3] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | reports | An unattributed report says Abu Lahab picked up a stone to throw at the Prophet, explaining the ruin of his hands.
[NASAFI:111:1/r4] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | Nasafi also glosses وَتَبَّ as meaning that Abu Lahab himself perished completely.
[NASAFI:111:1/r5] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | Alternatively, the hands are made ruined while the intended meaning is the ruin of his whole person, as in the expression cited from 22:10. | mentions 22:10
[NASAFI:111:1/r6] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | Nasafi glosses وَتَبَّ as “that happened and came to pass”; the quoted poetic line uses وقد فعل to illustrate this sense.
[NASAFI:111:1/r7] NASAFI, d. 710 · ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masʿūd’s reading وَقَد تَبَّ supports the sense that the ruin had indeed come to pass.
[NASAFI:111:1/r8] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | reports | After 26:214 was revealed, Muhammad summoned Quraysh at Ṣafā, elicited their trust in his warning, warned them of the Hour, and Abu Lahab rebuked him; this sūra was then revealed. | mentions 26:214
[NASAFI:111:1/r9] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | The kunya is an honorific, used because Abu Lahab was better known by it than by his personal name.
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p361#6/r1] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | Tabbat means destruction; the hands perished because, according to a report, Abū Lahab took a stone to throw at the Prophet, or the wording means his whole person perished as in the idiom about what one's hands earned.
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p361#6/r2] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | The second tabba means he was destroyed and that this had happened; Ibn Masʿūd's reading wa-qad tab supports the completed event.
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p361#6/r3] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | reports | After the command to warn near relatives, the Prophet called people at Ṣafā and warned Banū ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib and Banū Fihr that he was a warner before the Hour; Abū Lahab cursed him, prompting revelation. | mentions 26:214
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p361#6/r4] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | reports | Abū Lahab's kunya may be an honorific by which he was widely known, dislike of his name ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā, or a fitting reference to his future fiery fate.
[QASHANI:111:1-4/r1] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | Abu Lahab’s hands, the cause of his evil deeds, perish, and his evil self perishes according to its own readiness; he deserves fire by essence and quality, and his kunya signals permanent attachment to it.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r1] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Sūrat al-Masad is Meccan.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r10] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | Another explanation says Abū Lahab picked up a stone with his hands to throw at the Messenger, so destruction was attributed to them.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r11] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The first tabbat is apparently a curse or imprecation, while wa-tabb reports that the destruction occurred; ʿAbd Allāh’s reading qad tabb supports the report.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r12] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · عبد الله | reports | ʿAbd Allāh reads wa-qad tabb, making the second clause explicitly report the destruction as accomplished.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r13] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | A report links the sūra’s descent to the Prophet’s public warning at Ṣafā and his warning of punishment.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r14] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | The report says the Prophet told Ṣafiyya and Fāṭima that he could not avail them against God and invited them to ask what they wished from his property.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r15] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | The Prophet called Quraysh’s clans at Ṣafā, gave the hypothetical warning of an approaching army and warned them of punishment; Abū Lahab cursed him, the gathering dispersed, and the sūra descended.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r16] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · أبو لهب | reports | Abū Lahab replied, “May you perish all day; was this why you gathered us?” and the sūra then descended.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r17] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Abū Lahab’s given name was ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā; he was the Prophet’s paternal uncle.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r18] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · ابن محيصن وابن كثير | reports | Ibn Muḥayṣin and Ibn Kathīr read Abī Lahab with a sukūn on hāʾ; the other seven readers use fatḥa.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r19] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | All readers retain fatḥa in dhāta lahab because it ends the verse, while sukūn would spoil its cadence.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r2] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | After the previous sūra describes people entering God’s religion, this one turns to a man who did not enter it and lost while the Meccans embraced faith.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r20] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī treats the sukūn in Lahab as an alteration of a proper name, like the ḍamma in Shams Mālik.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r21] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | rejects | The author says the established form of Abū Lahab has fatḥa; Shams Mālik need not be an altered name, since Shams could be a name transferred from the plural of shams.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r22] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | One explanation says he was called Abū Lahab for his handsome, radiant face.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r23] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | Another says God avoided his given name, ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r24] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | Another explanation is that the kunya was more commonly used than his personal name.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r25] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | Another view sees irony: his fate in the Fire matched his name, as people call an evil person Abū al-Sharr and a good person Abū al-Khayr.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r26] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | Another view says God chose the lesser honor of a kunya over the personal name, just as the prophets are named rather than addressed by kunya.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r3] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās glosses tabbat as having failed or been disappointed.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r4] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · قتادة | reports | Qatāda glosses tabbat as having lost.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r5] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · ابن جبير | reports | Ibn Jubayr glosses tabbat as having perished.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r6] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · عطاء | reports | ʿAṭāʾ glosses tabbat as having gone astray.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r7] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · يمان بن رياب | reports | Yamān ibn Riyāb says the hands became empty of every good; the author regards these glosses as close in meaning.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r8] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | An Arabic expression, umm tāba, means a woman ruined by old age and incapacity.
[ABUHAYYAN:111:1/r9] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Destruction is attributed to the hands because most work is done with them, though the person himself is meant, as in “for what your hands have sent ahead.” | mentions 22:10
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p565/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Following the prior sura's mass conversion, this one describes Abu Lahab as a loser who stayed outside the faith embraced by Mecca.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p565/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن عباس وقتادة وابن جبير وعطاء ويمان بن رياب | reports | They gloss tabbat as failed, lost, perished, strayed, or emptied of all good; the author says these meanings are close.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p565/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Ruin is attributed to the hands because actions are mostly done with them, though the true object is the self, as in 22:10. | mentions 22:10
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p565/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | Another view says Abu Lahab took a stone in his hands to throw at the Messenger, explaining the specific attribution.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p565/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | prefers | The apparent reading is an imprecation in tabbat followed by a report that it came to pass; Abd Allah's wa-qad tabb supports this.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p565/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · النبي | reports | After 26:214 was revealed, the Prophet called relatives and Quraysh to warn them of punishment; Abu Lahab cursed the gathering, after which this sura was revealed. | mentions 26:214
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p566/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Abu Lahab's personal name was Abd al-Uzza; the author identifies his family relation to the Prophet.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p566/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن محيصن وابن كثير | reports | They read Abu Lahab with a silent h; the other seven readers open it, while the fixed rhyme in dhata lahab keeps the opening vowel.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p566/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Zamakhshari calls the silent h a change in proper names, like the altered pronunciation of Shams Malik.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p566/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | The author says that explanation is not necessary: Abu Lahab is normally pronounced with an open h, and Shams Malik could simply have been named Shams from a plural form.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p566/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | Several explanations are offered for the kunya: his bright looks, its greater currency than his name, his destiny in fire, or its lower rank than a personal name; the last is linked to naming prophets rather than giving them kunyas.
