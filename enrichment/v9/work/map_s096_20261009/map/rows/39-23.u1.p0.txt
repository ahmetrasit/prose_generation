## THE CURRENT MAP
39:23/q01 What does 'God sent down' establish about the Quran? [ٱللَّهُ نَزَّلَ · interpretation] (turns on: The form of the verb and the theological implication of naming God.)
  39:23/q01/p1 It is revelation and divine speech delivered through Gabriel and the Prophet. — reasons: KURANYOLU says Gabriel and the Prophet transmit God's speech; Ibn Kathir calls it the great Book revealed to His Messenger. Bāqillānī, Bursevī, and Biqāʿī ground its rank in its divine source.
  39:23/q01/p2 Nazzala stresses progressive, continuing revelation. — reasons: Asad bases his present tense on the verb form; Biqāʿī says stages train listeners and answer objections. Burhān catalogs both nazzala and anzala across many suras, including this one.
39:23/q02 Why is God's name made the opening subject? [ٱللَّهُ نَزَّلَ · rhetoric]
  39:23/q02/p1 The fronted divine name magnifies the revelation and its unmatched divine source. — reasons: Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Ālūsī make exaltation the point; Abū Ḥayyān compares topicalizing a king and cites 22:75; Ālūsī derives perfection of speech from the perfect Speaker and reports Zamakhsharī's restrictive reading.
39:23/q03 What is the 'best discourse'? [أَحْسَنَ ٱلْحَدِيثِ · referent] (turns on: Whether ḥadīth here denotes the revealed Book broadly or its narratives specifically.)
  39:23/q03/p1 The Quran. — reasons: Mujāhid, Muqātil, Ṭabarī and successors identify it; Qurṭubī links it to the preceding command to follow the best saying; Burhān lists it among Quranic names.
  39:23/q03/p2 The best narratives or stories. — reasons: Ḍamghānī and Ibn al-Jawzī give the narrative sense; the companions' request for stories is relevant.
39:23/q04 Why is this discourse called the best? [أَحْسَنَ ٱلْحَدِيثِ · interpretation] (turns on: The grounds given for 'best': form, content, divine status, and effect.)
  39:23/q04/p1 Its expression is uniquely eloquent and inimitable. — reasons: Māwardī, Ṭabrisī, Rāzī, Bursevī, and others cite eloquence, composition, genre, pleasing style and strong diction. Bāqillānī contrasts poetry, sajʿ and ordinary prose, cites 4:82, and notes human critics' disagreement; Jurjānī's eight poets illustrate human literary craft and claims. Samarrāʾī stresses the artistic unity of varied diction.
  39:23/q04/p2 Its truth, wisdom, comprehensiveness, and beneficial guidance make it best. — reasons: Māwardī calls it the most complete book; Ṭabrisī lists proof, law, exhortation, stories, promise and warning; Rāzī surveys divine essence and attributes, cosmology, nature, human origins, prophecy, law, ethics, angels, scriptures and resurrection with numerous cited verses, concluding no book equals its range. KURANYOLU names truth, justice and salvation; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says falsehood cannot touch it.
  39:23/q04/p3 It is God's uncreated or eternal speech. — reasons: Qushayrī explicitly grounds the superlative in uncreatedness; Bursevī contrasts God's eternal speech with originated human speech.
  39:23/q04/p4 Its palpable power over hearts proves its excellence and inimitability. — reasons: Khaṭṭābī says sweetness and majesty converted would-be killers; Bāqillānī describes awe, joy and conviction; ʿIyāḍ and Burhān compare 59:21 and cite moved believers and even opponents. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Jubayr at 52:35–37 and ʿUtba at 41:13. Study Quran calls bodily reaction an attribute of beauty.
39:23/q05 How should ellipses be chosen when the Quran is the best discourse? [أَحْسَنَ ٱلْحَدِيثِ · rhetoric]
  39:23/q05/p1 Choose the shortest, most fitting and specific implied wording. — reasons: Suyūṭī minimizes ellipsis at 65:4; ʿIzz al-Dīn chooses the Kaaba's sanctity over construction at 2:125, which Suyūṭī prefers, and specific field compensation over general affair at 21:78. The ground is the Quran's excellence.
39:23/q06 Why is the Quran called ḥadīth? [ٱلْحَدِيثِ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the title reflects communication, relative lateness, or rhetorical matching.)
  39:23/q06/p1 It is speech or a communicated report. — reasons: Māwardī compares prophetic speech; Qurṭubī says the Prophet related it and cites 7:185, 53:59, 18:6, 4:87, 68:44; Raghib includes what one hears or receives in revelation or dream. Ibn al-Jawzī links speech and new information and gives other senses of the root.
  39:23/q06/p2 It is later than earlier revealed books. — reasons: Māwardī and Ṭabrisī give relative lateness of revelation.
  39:23/q06/p3 It is a matching expression (mushākala). — reasons: Ālūsī allows this alternative usage.
39:23/q07 Does calling the Quran ḥadīth imply that God's speech is created? [ٱلْحَدِيثِ · theology] (turns on: Whether 'new' describes the divine speech itself or its temporal recitation and verbal expression.)
  39:23/q07/p1 The verbal Quran of successive letters and sounds is originated. — reasons: Rāzī reports arguments from ḥadīth in 52:34 and 56:81, descent into a locus, the comparison 'best,' and kitāb as a gathered object; he accepts their force for letters, sounds and utterances. Ālūsī reports the Ashʿarite distinction for verbal speech.
  39:23/q07/p2 The term does not establish createdness of the eternal speech. — reasons: Qushayrī distinguishes newly occurring recitation from what is recited, comparing remembrance and divine names at 21:2; Bursevī and Ālūsī say ḥadīth means discourse spoken or told, without temporal origination of the divine speech.
39:23/q08 What occasion is reported for this verse? [ٱللَّهُ نَزَّلَ أَحْسَنَ ٱلْحَدِيثِ · reports]
  39:23/q08/p1 Companions asked the Prophet for stories or a fresh narration, and this verse was revealed. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd, Saʿd and others vary the request: a story, good accounts of past times, or relief from weariness. Related reports place 12:3 and 57:16 at subsequent requests; Ibn al-Jawzī says these requests directed listeners back to the Quran. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the report an application rather than a strict occasion.
39:23/q09 How is kitāban related syntactically to 'best discourse'? [كِتَٰبًۭا · grammar] (turns on: Whether kitāban renames the discourse, describes its state, or specifies it.)
  39:23/q09/p1 It is an appositive or substitute for 'best discourse'. — reasons: Zajjāj, Naḥḥās, Zamakhsharī, Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān, Biqāʿī and others adopt it; al-Samīn calls it clearest.
  39:23/q09/p2 It is a circumstantial accusative describing 'best discourse'. — reasons: Zamakhsharī allows it; Abū Ḥayyān questions definiteness by annexation, while al-Samīn and Ālūsī defend it, adducing qualification by mutashābih or the force of 'written'.
  39:23/q09/p3 It is an accusative of specification distinguishing the Quran from other speech. — reasons: Jishumī states this analysis.
39:23/q10 Why is the revelation described as a written Book? [كِتَٰبًۭا · interpretation]
  39:23/q10/p1 Its written form preserves a lasting guide for reading until Resurrection. — reasons: KURANYOLU explains the documentary form and enduring public benefit; Biqāʿī says the Book gathers all good. Rāzī points to his discussion of the Book at 2:2.
39:23/q11 What does mutashābihan say the Quran resembles? [مُّتَشَٰبِهًۭا · meaning] (turns on: Whether comparison is among Quranic passages or with earlier scriptures; internal resemblance includes confirmation and literary equality.)
  39:23/q11/p1 Its own parts resemble, confirm, and explain one another without contradiction. — reasons: Ṭabarī cites Qatāda on verses and letters, Saʿīd on mutual confirmation, and 2:25; most later commentators cite uniformly true, wise, clear, beautiful and inimitable wording and meaning. Rāzī notes long human works vary while repeated Moses accounts remain equally eloquent. Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 4:82; Islahi notes unity of teaching despite different styles and themes. Samarrāʾī cites studies of imagery, sound, diction, coherence, and Rāzī's image of the Quran as a single sura or verse.
  39:23/q11/p2 It resembles earlier revealed scriptures while surpassing them. — reasons: Māwardī, Ṭabrisī and Qurṭubī name shared commands, prohibitions, promises and warnings, with the Quran more comprehensive and inimitable; Burhān gives a second exposition of earlier accounts.
  39:23/q11/p3 It is internally alike in eloquence and literary quality. — reasons: Qushayrī, Ṭabrisī, Rāzī and Study Quran emphasize uniformly excellent composition and diction.
39:23/q12 How does mutashābih here relate to other uses of the term? [مُّتَشَٰبِهًۭا · links]
  39:23/q12/p1 Here it describes the whole Quran as mutually consistent, distinct from ambiguous verses in 3:7. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Study Quran distinguish the uses. Burhān contrasts obscurity with mutual confirmation and discusses knowability and scholarly inquiry. Itqān reports whole-muḥkam, whole-mutashābih and partitioned views, preferring the partition in 3:7 while defining this whole-book quality as truth and inimitability. Biqāʿī says this wording avoids a form that might suggest obscurity.
