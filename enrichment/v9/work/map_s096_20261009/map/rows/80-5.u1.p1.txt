80:5/q08 How does this self-sufficiency contrast with fear and receptivity in the surrounding verses? [* · links]
  80:5/q08/p1 Self-sufficiency tends toward rejection, while fear, trial, and desire for reform make a person receptive. — reasons: Corpus Coranicum calls istaghnā the antonym of ittaqā, relates 80:5 to the piety of 80:9, and notes 92:5 and 96:7. Al-Biqāʿī says luxury invites disbelief and wrongdoing, while affliction can humble and purify; the deeply tried can be more receptive to fear. Iṣlāḥī contrasts those absorbed in worldly pleasures and heedless of the afterlife with those who fear meeting God. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ links the grandeur of wealth to arrogance toward truth, citing 96:7–8.
80:5/q09 How is this passage related to al-Nāziʿāt and the sūra's larger argument? [* · links] (turns on: Al-Biqāʿī stresses the warning and resurrection argument; Iṣlāḥī stresses shared discourse with a different order of topics.)
  80:5/q09/p1 It develops the call to warn those who fear through signs of resurrection and purification. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī relates the sūra to 79:45. Human creation and the cycle of food demonstrate God's power, making rejection astonishing; warning reaches hearts receptive to fear.
  80:5/q09/p2 ʿAbasa pairs with al-Nāziʿāt in theme, style, and reasoning, but rearranges their topics. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī cites 79:45 and says the changed topic sequence gives this sūra its distinct majesty.
