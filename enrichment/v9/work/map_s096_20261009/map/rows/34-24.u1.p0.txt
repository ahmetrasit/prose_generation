## THE CURRENT MAP
34:24/q01 Whom is the Prophet told to question about provision? [مَن يَرْزُقُكُم · referent]
  34:24/q01/p1 The Meccan polytheists or unbelievers, especially those worshipping angels or idols. — reasons: Muqatil specifies Meccans who worship angels; Yahya, al-Wahidi, and Ibn Abbas identify polytheists or people addressed by the Prophet.
34:24/q02 How is man yarzuqukum parsed? [مَن يَرْزُقُكُم · grammar]
  34:24/q02/p1 Man is a complete interrogative noun, nominative subject, with yarzuqukum as its predicate. — reasons: Al-Nahhas gives the case and predication.
34:24/q03 What provision comes from the heavens and earth? [مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the two realms name physical sources, God's decree and lawful acquisition, or also spiritual sustenance.)
  34:24/q03/p1 Rain comes from above and vegetation, crops, fruit, or food from the earth. — reasons: Muqatil, al-Kalbi in al-Mawardi, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Bursevi, and Study Quran name rain and plants; al-Tabari adds sun, moon, stars, livelihoods, and food for humans and livestock; al-Qurtubi adds their benefits and earthly water.
  34:24/q03/p2 Heavenly provision is God's decree for His servants; earthly provision is what He permits them to take lawfully. — reasons: Al-Mawardi offers this as an alternative to rain and vegetation.
  34:24/q03/p3 Provision includes bodily food and inward knowledge and unveilings for spirits. — reasons: Bursevi says inward provision is nobler and gives enduring life, while bodily food gives passing strength; God creates and distributes both more or less widely as He wills.
34:24/q04 Why are the heavens plural here but earth singular, unlike the singular heaven in Yunus? [ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ · rhetoric]
  34:24/q04/p1 The plural points beyond the visible rain-cloud sky to higher or varied heavenly provision, while people know only one earth. — reasons: Al-Suhayli says they admit rain from clouds but not what lies above; al-Burhan contrasts Yunus 10:31, whose singular rests on that admission, with Saba, which supplies the answer and stresses varied provision. Al-Biqa'i says all sustenance depends on both realms and they know no other earth.
34:24/q05 What does asking who provides establish? [مَن يَرْزُقُكُم مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ · theology] (turns on: Whether the question is read solely as proof of God's unique provision or also as a challenge about provision withheld.)
  34:24/q05/p1 God alone provides, so powerless idols have no claim to worship and the sole provider deserves thanks and exclusive worship. — reasons: Al-Wahidi cites 10:31; al-Kashshaf cites 10:31-32 and 13:16; al-Qurtubi lists rain and celestial and earthly benefits idols cannot make; Ibn Kathir links sole creation and provision to sole divinity; Ibn Ashur says divinity cannot be divided; Tabataba'i notes even idolaters recognize God alone creates provision. Al-Razi sets divine benefit here beside God's sole removal of harm in 34:22, 6:17, and 10:107; he contrasts ordinary worship for benefit with the elect's worship for God's greatness. Al-Baydawi links powerless false gods to 19:87; al-Biqa'i stresses that acknowledged provision had not produced exclusive worship.
  34:24/q05/p2 The question also asks who could provide if God withheld His provision. — reasons: Study Quran cites 67:21 for this possible implication.
34:24/q06 Who supplies the answer Allah, and why is it said? [قُلِ ٱللَّهُ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether Allah is their actual reply, the Prophet's supplied reply to silence, or an answer demanded of them.)
  34:24/q06/p1 The Prophet answers on their behalf because God is the only possible answer, though opponents may stay silent, hesitate, or refuse its implication. — reasons: Al-Tabari says the obvious answer was first omitted and the Prophet supplies it if they profess ignorance; al-Wahidi says they can name no other provider. Al-Kashshaf, al-Nasafi, al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi, al-Alusi, and Tabataba'i say they inwardly assent but may not speak from stubbornness or fear of having to worship God alone. Ibn Atiyya calls them stunned; al-Razi compares acknowledgment in distress and heedlessness in ease, citing 30:33. Ibn Ashur treats Allah as their anticipated answer as at 10:31; Ibn al-Jawzi says they would give no other answer.
  34:24/q06/p2 The Meccans themselves answer Allah, acknowledging that God provides. — reasons: Muqatil supplies the implied words 'we are provided' and cites 10:31. Al-Mawardi says they did not deny divine provision; al-Biqa'i and Ibn Ashur say the confession is already known.
  34:24/q06/p3 They are ordered to answer Allah themselves so their admission becomes proof against them. — reasons: Al-Mawardi states this as an alternative account.
34:24/q07 Why is the command qul repeated before Allah? [قُلْ قُلِ · rhetoric]
  34:24/q07/p1 The repetition heightens attention to the announced answer and the importance of proclaiming it. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says the first command already marked public proclamation as important.
34:24/q08 Where does the discourse divide after qul Allah? [قُلِ ٱللَّهُ وَإِنَّآ · grammar]
  34:24/q08/p1 Allah closes the question and answer; an understood further 'then say' introduces the challenge over guidance. — reasons: Muqatil says the speech breaks there; Sahib al-Nazm in al-Wahidi makes the omitted command explicit; Ibn al-Jawzi treats Allah as the end of a sentence and the next statement as another command.
34:24/q09 Can the qaf in yarzuqukum assimilate into the kaf? [يَرْزُقُكُم · grammar]
  34:24/q09/p1 Yes; assimilation turns the qaf into a kaf. — reasons: Al-Nahhas records the phonetic possibility.
34:24/q10 What is the form of inna? [وَإِنَّآ · grammar]
  34:24/q10/p1 Inna is shortened from innana by removal of one nun. — reasons: Al-Nahhas gives this derivation.
34:24/q11 Who are we and you in the challenge? [وَإِنَّآ أَوْ إِيَّاكُمْ · referent]
  34:24/q11/p1 We are Muhammad, his companions, or monotheists; you are the Meccan idolaters or polytheists. — reasons: Muqatil, al-Tha'labi, al-Mawardi, al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi, and al-Alusi identify these opposed camps; al-Naqqash adds that God provides for both. Al-Baydawi says the former worship the provider with intrinsic power, while the latter worship lowly inanimate things.
34:24/q12 How is iyyakum joined to inna? [إِيَّاكُمْ · grammar] (turns on: Whether coordination with inna's expressed subject or its syntactic position controls the scope of the guidance predicate.)
  34:24/q12/p1 Iyyakum is coordinated with the expressed subject of inna; coordination by syntactic position would use antum. — reasons: Al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi distinguish iyyakum from 'aw antum'; al-Nahhas says the latter would restrict guidance to the first group. Al-Qurtubi says the former favors the first group for guidance, while al-Mubarrad allows that reading either way. Al-Samin and Bursevi also coordinate it with ism inna; al-Alusi mentions the detached pronoun after elision.
  34:24/q12/p2 The guidance phrase may apply to the first group in the coordinated construction. — reasons: Abu al-Abbas permits this and likens the challenge to saying that one of two people is mistaken while letting the listener identify him.
34:24/q13 What force does aw have in the challenge? [أَوْ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the particle keeps genuine alternative syntax, functions like wa, has an added alif, or coordinates two conditional outcomes.)
  34:24/q13/p1 Aw retains its ordinary alternative force; the alternatives are apparently open, although the speaker knows the truth. — reasons: Al-Farra's dirham example permits one or two, never three; al-Wahidi and Ibn al-Jawzi report him against literal equivalence to wa. Basran grammarians say known facts can be stated disjunctively without doubt; Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi preserve normal disjunction. Al-Biqa'i calls the alternatives unspecified for unbiased judgment; Ibn Ashur calls the dilemma exhaustive. Al-Burhan and al-Itqan say the listener is left to identify the truth.
  34:24/q13/p2 Aw has the force of coordinating wa, pairing our guidance with your error. — reasons: Abu Ubayda and others give the direct paraphrase; Jarir's line joins Thalaba and Riyah, and al-Qurtubi cites another poet joining Riyah and Rizam. Al-Nazzam cites 28:73 and Imru al-Qays; al-Alusi reports Abu Ubayda's bread-and-bone line. Al-Farra is reported in some sources as supporting the meaning but rejects literal grammatical identity in others; al-Wahidi says exegetes privileged intended meaning over surface syntax. Al-Alusi calls this far-fetched; Ibn Atiyya says Abu Ubayda's parse lacks support.
  34:24/q13/p3 The alif of aw is added, leaving a coordinating sense. — reasons: Muqatil compares the added alif to 25:44; Ibn Abbas via Ata and some cited by al-Baghawi give the same explanation.
  34:24/q13/p4 Aw means that both parties are guided if they believe, or both in error if they do not. — reasons: Bursevi reports this conditional reading from unnamed interpreters.
34:24/q14 Does the challenge require an omitted predicate? [وَإِنَّآ أَوْ إِيَّاكُمْ لَعَلَىٰ هُدًى أَوْ فِى ضَلَٰلٍ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the surface alternatives themselves form a complete predicate, an omitted counterpart must be supplied, or ordered pairing distributes the two predicates.)
  34:24/q14/p1 No: the combined alternative is the predicate, and one group occupies one of the two states. — reasons: Abu Hayyan uses 'Zayd or Amr is in the palace or mosque' and rejects an unnecessary deletion. Al-Alusi prefers the same reading with market or home; al-Bahr agrees no ellipsis is needed here, while noting a simpler 'Zayd or Amr is standing' would need it. Al-Samin reports al-Shaykh's account but says it interprets rather than parses.
