[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:172/r14] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Because the matter belongs to the unseen and exceeds human knowledge, one should affirm that a covenant was taken without insisting on its precise nature; the duty is to believe humans can know God’s lordship and to keep their pledge, as 7:174 indicates. | mentions 7:174
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:172/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | The first interpretation says that before creating the world God gathered all future human souls in the spirit realm, made them witness His existence and affirm His lordship, and took a covenant from them.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:172/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | On this interpretation, the people themselves witnessed the covenant, some witnessed for others, or God and the angels were declared its witnesses.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:172/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | The covenant blocks people from pleading ignorance of their responsibility or excusing inherited unbelief and idolatry as their ancestors’ invention.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:172/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | This precreation reading was held by early Salaf and Sufi scholars and by most Sunni and Shi‘i theologians.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:172/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | The second interpretation takes the covenant figuratively: it occurs as each human body is created, rather than before the world.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:172/r7] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | Accounts place this endowment either during creation of offspring in the father’s loins or during formation in the womb; God implants in human nature the capacity to recognize and understand His existence and oneness.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:172/r8] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | God gives each person sufficient mental and psychological capacity to believe and creates many inner and outer signs of His existence and oneness; the covenant scene figuratively portrays this capacity.
