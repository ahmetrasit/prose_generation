 548 · النبي | reports | The Prophet prayed for mercy on Moses: eyewitnessing the people's trial was not like being told of it; although Moses knew God's report was true and held to the tablets, seeing the people made him angry and he cast them.
[TABRISI:7:150-151#2/r14] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو علي الجبائي | reports | One explanation is that Moses, thinking deeply in anger, treated Aaron like himself and grabbed him as a person may grab his own beard or lip; customs can make the same act honor in one setting and insult in another.
[TABRISI:7:150-151#2/r15] TABRISI, d. 548 · الشيخ المفيد أبو عبد الله بن النعمان | reports | Another explanation is that Moses displayed anger at the people's apostasy and pain at their misguidance to show its gravity and deter similar conduct later.
[TABRISI:7:150-151#2/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Moses returned from his meeting with God, saw his people devoted to the calf, and was angry and distressed.
[TABRISI:7:150-151#2/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās explains asaf as grief.
[TABRISI:7:150-151#2/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو الدرداء | reports | Abū al-Dardāʾ explains asaf as intense anger.
[TABRISI:7:150-151#2/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو مسلم | reports | Abū Muslim takes anger and asaf to mean the same thing, repeated for emphasis with different wording, as illustrated by poetry.
[TABRISI:7:150-151#2/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Another explanation is that Moses was angry because his people worshipped the calf and grieved over missing his meeting with God.
[TABRISI:7:150-151#2/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | “How bad you acted after me” condemns their conduct while Moses was away at the appointed meeting.
[TABRISI:7:150-151#2/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن عباس | reports | He explains “Did you hasten your Lord's command?” as failing to wait for the appointed promise.
[TABRISI:7:150-151#2/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan says it refers to the promise of forty nights: the people thought Moses had died because he had not returned after thirty nights.
[TABRISI:7:150-151#3/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | A third explanation is that Moses pulled Aaron close to speak privately and investigate the people through him; Aaron then established his innocence, after which Moses prayed for them both.
[TABRISI:7:150-151#3/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | A fourth explanation is that Moses compassionately pulled Aaron close to calm his similar distress; Aaron feared ignorant people would mistake the gesture for disrespect, so he clarified his innocence and Moses prayed to remove suspicion.
[TABRISI:7:150-151#3/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو مسلم | reports | A fifth explanation is that Moses rebuked Aaron for asking, as in Ṭā Hā, why he had not followed him when he saw the people stray. | mentions 20:92
[TABRISI:7:150-151#3/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan swears Aaron was Moses' full brother; Moses invoked their mother because mentioning the mother better appeals for compassion.
[TABRISI:7:150-151#3/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Aaron says the people treated him as weak and nearly killed him because of his forceful objection.
[TABRISI:7:150-151#3/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Aaron asks Moses not to make the enemies rejoice through an act whose outward appearance suggests disrespect, and not to group him with calf worshippers as one of the wrongdoers.
[TABRISI:7:150-151/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن عامر وأهل الكوفة عن عاصم | reports | They read ibn um with kasra here and in Ṭā Hā; the other readers put ibn um in the accusative in both places.
[TABRISI:7:150-151/r10] TABRISI, d. 548 · الزجاج | reports | Readers who use kasra treat ibn um as a single name and then annex it to the speaker's self-reference.
[TABRISI:7:150-151/r11] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Asaf can mean anger tinged with regret over what passed, grief, or yearning; khalafa means acting after someone either well or badly; haste is doing something before its proper time, whereas speed is doing it at the first possible time.
[TABRISI:7:150-151/r12] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | ʿAjaltuhu means I preceded him, while aʿjaltuhu means I urged him on; shamātah is an enemy's joy at another's bad outcome.
[TABRISI:7:150-151/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · مجاهد | reports | In a noncanonical reading, Mujāhid has tašmit with fatḥa on tāʾ and mīm and al-aʿdāʾ in the accusative; he also reports yashmit with yāʾ.
[TABRISI:7:150-151/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The accusative vocalization in ibn um reflects the frequent use of the phrase as a single compound name, as in “son of my mother” or “son of my uncle”; some Arabs retain the yāʾ in ibn ummī.
[TABRISI:7:150-151/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · سيبويه | reports | Sībawayh cites the compound vocatives yā ibn umm and yā ibn ʿamm, more common than yā ibn abī or yā ghulāma ghulāmī.
[TABRISI:7:150-151/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Some Arabs say yā ibn ummī, retaining the possessive yāʾ; a poet likewise addresses “son of my mother” and “sibling of my soul.”
[TABRISI:7:150-151/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو علي | reports | In the compound vocative, ibn's fatḥa is a built ending resembling its case vowel, not the accusative of an annexed vocative; he compares lā rajula and the command-form makānaka.
[TABRISI:7:150-151/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو علي | rejects | He rejects analyzing the form as yā ibn ammā with a deleted alif, unlike a deleted possessive yāʾ in yā ghulām, because that yāʾ is retained in the fuller phrase.
[TABRISI:7:150-151/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | The poem yā bint ʿammā shows that alif is retained even where possessive yāʾ may be dropped; the cited Qur'anic reading likewise preserves yāʾ in 92:2. | mentions 92:2
[TABRISI:7:150-151/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Alif deletion occurs in some poetic forms, such as ibn al-maʿallā for al-maʿallā and bilahtafī for bilahtafā, but not in ordinary speech; on that rare analysis ibn would be accusative while umm's ending resembles the mīm in bint ʿammā.
