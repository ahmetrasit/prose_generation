[NASAFI:1:2/r6] NASAFI, d. 710 · النبي | reports | A Prophetic report calls praise the head of gratitude and says a servant has not thanked God without praising Him; praise leads because the tongue makes gratitude more public than the hidden heart or ambiguous bodily acts.
[NASAFI:1:2/r7] NASAFI, d. 710 · قيل | reports | Another distinction makes madh praise inherent perfections, shukr praise for beneficence, and hamd broad enough to cover both.
[NASAFI:1:2/r8] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | For Nasafi, the definite article in al-hamd is comprehensive, against the Mu‘tazila; its attachment to Allah, the name of the divine essence, gathers all perfections and reflects the doctrine of created acts.
