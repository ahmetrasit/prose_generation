7:150/q35 Who are the wrongdoers? [ٱلْقَوْمِ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · referent]
  7:150/q35/p1 The wrongdoers are the calf worshippers. — reasons: MUJAHID (author): Aaron asks not to be grouped with the wrongdoers, the calf worshippers. TAB-FULL (author): Mujahid: wrongdoers are calf worshippers. IBNABIHATIM (author): Mujahid: wrongdoers are calf worshippers. WAHIDI-WAJIZ (author): Aaron asks Moses not to let the calf worshippers rejoice over him by striking or humiliating him, or count him among the wrongdoers. BAGHAWI-FULL (author): ‘The people weakened me’ means the calf worshippers overpowered Aaron and nearly killed him; he asks Moses not to let the enemies rejoice or count him among the wrongdoers, meaning calf worshippers. TABRISI (author): Aaron asks Moses not to make the enemies rejoice through an act whose outward appearance suggests disrespect, and not to group him with calf worshippers as one of the wrongdoers. IBNJAWZI-ZAD (author): Aaron asks Moses not to count him among the wrongdoers in his anger and punishment of him; the wrongdoers are the calf worshippers. NASAFI-FULL (author): The enemies are the calf worshippers; Moses should not do what they wish by humiliating or harming Aaron, or treat him as one of the wrongdoers by sharing his anger with them. DURR-FULL (مجاهد): Mujahid explains Moses's plea not to be counted with the wrongdoers as a plea not to be counted with the calf worshippers. TABATABAI (هارون): Aaron says the people overpowered him and nearly killed him because he opposed their calf worship; he asks Moses not to let enemies gloat or count him among wrongdoers.

7:150/q37 What practical or legal lessons are drawn from the episode? [* · law] (turns on: Different rulings on fear while forbidding wrong, anger, gloating, and ecstatic conduct.)
  7:150/q37/p1 Anger calls for restraint and purification, while its legal effects are disputed. — reasons: WAHIDI-BASIT (author): Moses was angry at his people for worshipping the calf and grieved because God had tested them. | mentions 20:85 WAHIDI-BASIT (ابن قتيبة): Ibn Qutayba also prefers the anger sense, glossing the verb as making someone angry and becoming angry. | mentions 43:55 QURTUBI-FULL (عطية): The Prophet said anger comes from Satan, who was created from fire, and fire is extinguished with water; an angry person should perform ablution. No grading is stated. QURTUBI-FULL (ابن العربي): Ibn al-ʿArabī says anger does not alter legal rulings as some claim: Moses' actions continued in their ordinary course despite his anger. QURTUBI-FULL (أبو ذر): A report in Abū Dāwūd's Muṣannaf from Abū Dharr says the Prophet advised an angry person who is standing to sit; if anger remains, to lie down. No grading is stated. QURTUBI-FULL (أبو وائل القاص): Abū Wāʾil recounts that ʿUrwa ibn Muḥammad became angry with a visitor, left, returned after ablution, and then reported a Prophetic teaching transmitted through his father and grandfather ʿAṭiyya. QURTUBI-FULL (author): Anger is like an ember burning in the heart; the Prophet instructed an angry person to lie down, and, if anger remained, to bathe, extinguishing it with rest and water. SAMIN-DURR (author): Alternatively أسفا is apposition to غضبان: partitive if it means intensely angry, or apposition of inclusion if it means grieved; their closeness makes this possible.
  7:150/q37/p2 Fear of being killed can excuse silence while forbidding wrong. — reasons: WAHIDI-WAJIZ (author): Moses learned Aaron was innocent of blame because Aaron had protested so strongly that he feared being killed. QURTUBI-FULL (author): Aaron's fear that the people would kill him shows that a person who fears being killed while forbidding wrong may remain silent. IBNASHUR-FULL (author): Aaron’s “they nearly killed me” shows that he resisted them forcefully and then yielded for fear of being killed.
  7:150/q37/p3 Ecstasy does not excuse deliberate damage or forbidden conduct. — reasons: QURTUBI-FULL (ابن عقيل): Those who knowingly enter places where music overwhelms their reason are responsible for the resulting damage; legal duties remain because they were warned before entering. QURTUBI-FULL (ابن عقيل): If the claimed ecstasy is genuine, it is natural intoxication; if false, people act corruptly while sober, so neither account makes it safe and avoiding doubtful settings is obligatory.
