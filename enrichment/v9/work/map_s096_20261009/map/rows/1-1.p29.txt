[RAZI-FULL:v1p170/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · عمرو بن دينار | reports | Amr ibn Dinar rebukes al-Fadl al-Raqashi for claiming the basmala is not Qur’an, since it marks the end of one sura and start of another.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p170/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · عبد الله بن المبارك | reports | Ibn al-Mubarak says omitting the basmala omits 113 verses; similar statements are attributed to Ibn Umar and Abu Hurayra.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p170/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · أبو حنيفة | reports | For those who do not count the basmala as a verse of al-Fatiha, Abu Hanifa and several other jurists require it quietly; Malik forbids it in obligatory prayer but permits a choice in voluntary prayer.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p170/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · الشافعي | reports | Al-Shafi‘i’s position entails reading the basmala in every rak‘a; Abu Hanifa is reported both to require it before al-Fatiha in every rak‘a and to require it only at the start of the prayer.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p170/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · الشافعي | reports | Al-Shafi‘i prefers repeating the basmala before every sura, citing the report that an important matter begun without it is cut off.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p170/r7] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Razi’s school holds that a menstruating woman or a person in major ritual impurity may not recite the basmala.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p170/r8] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | Scholars agree that invoking God in ablution is recommended, and most do not require it because the ablution verse omits it. | mentions 5:6
[RAZI-FULL:v1p171/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · أهل الظاهر | reports | Literalists require the basmala in ablution, while Ishaq invalidates ablution for deliberate omission but excuses forgetfulness.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p171/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | Whether meat from an animal slaughtered without the basmala is lawful is a famous dispute, with Qur’anic commands both to invoke God over offerings and not eat what was not named over. | mentions 22:36, 6:121
[RAZI-FULL:v1p171/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | Scholars recommend beginning every activity with the basmala, from sleep and waking to worship, entering and leaving home, eating, drinking, giving, and receiving; they also recommend it at birth, death, burial, resurrection, and the gathering.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p171/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | The basmala at the gathering is said to distance the fire from the speaker by its blessing.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p171/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Razi rejects translated Qur’an as sufficient for prayer for both able and unable reciters; Abu Hanifa allows it for either, while Abu Yusuf and Muhammad allow it only for the unable.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p171/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Razi calls Abu Hanifa’s view far from sound; al-Samarqandi and al-Dabusi explicitly abandoned it.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p171/r7] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The Prophet prayed using the revealed Arabic Qur’an throughout his life, so believers must follow that practice; Razi notes the irony that Abu Hanifa treats habitual head-wiping as a condition but not habitual Arabic recitation. | mentions 6:153
[RAZI-FULL:v1p171/r8] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The rightly guided caliphs prayed with Arabic Qur’an, so their practice binds believers under commands to follow Abu Bakr and Umar and the caliphs’ Sunna.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p172#2/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · أبو هريرة | reports | Ibn al-Mundhir transmits Abu Hurayra’s report that prayer is invalid without reciting the Opening; a Persian rendering is either called Qur’an, which Razi deems ignorant and contrary to consensus, or not Qur’an.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p172#2/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · عبد الله بن أبي أوفى | reports | When a man could not recite Qur’an, the Prophet taught him glorification, praise, and takbir instead of a translation, showing the alternative for inability.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p172#2/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | If Qur’an could be translated as Qur’an, Christians could claim its opening was copied from the Gospel’s matching formula; no one makes that claim.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p172#2/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | A Persian rendering of the cave passage or insults and requests becomes ordinary human speech, barred in prayer; the Arabic original remains distinct through its miraculous arrangement. | mentions 18:19, 68:11, 68:13, 2:61
[RAZI-FULL:v1p172/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The Prophet and Companions recited only Arabic in prayer; the saved group follows their practice. Muslims throughout Islamic lands agree on Arabic recitation, and departing from believers’ way is condemned. | mentions 4:115
[RAZI-FULL:v1p172/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Prayer requires Qur’an recitation, and Persian words are not Qur’an: revelation is in clear Arabic, messengers speak their people’s language, and the challenge to produce its like would be contradicted if a Persian rendering counted. | mentions 26:192, 26:195, 14:4, 41:44, 17:88
[RAZI-FULL:v1p173/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | If every language’s translation were Qur’an, revelation would have more than the seven canonical forms, contradicting the report that it came in seven forms.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p173/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The Hanafi principle would also validate prayer with matching verses from Torah, Gospel, or ordinary writings, an outcome Razi says is alien to Islam.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p173/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | A translation of al-Fatiha reduces it to generic praise found in any sermon; allowing this would make prayer valid with all sermons, an unacceptable result.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p173/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | If translation were permitted, the Prophet would have authorized Persians, Romans, and Ethiopians to pray in their languages; such a popular, easier permission would have spread and erased the Arabic Qur’an.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p173/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Although a non-Arab may understand meaning more readily in translation, prayer seeks remembrance and reflection; treating translation as valid would perversely make Arabic recitation prohibited. | mentions 20:14, 47:24
[RAZI-FULL:v1p173/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Prayer preserves both meaning and Arabic wording: the wording carries the inimitable eloquence, and its required recitation safeguards memorization and fulfills God’s promise to preserve the Reminder. | mentions 15:9
[RAZI-FULL:v1p174/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · عبد الله بن مسعود | reports | Opponents cite Ibn Mas‘ud teaching an Ajami to replace al-athim with al-yatim, then saying Quranic error is replacing a mercy verse with a punishment verse rather than using a synonym. | mentions 44:43
[RAZI-FULL:v1p174/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · المخالف | reports | Opponents argue that the Qur’an was in earlier scriptures, whose wording was Hebrew or Syriac rather than the Arabic wording, so meaning can be Qur’an in another language. | mentions 26:196, 87:19
[RAZI-FULL:v1p174/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · المخالف | reports | Opponents cite God’s statement that this Qur’an was revealed to warn people who do not understand Arabic, arguing that communicated meaning in their language is still Qur’an. | mentions 6:19
[RAZI-FULL:v1p174/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | rejects | Razi objects that Hanafis selectively invoke Ibn Mas‘ud: they reject his reported uncertainty about faith, though they rely on him here; reports that he omitted al-Fatiha and the protective suras likely reflect views he later abandoned.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p174/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | rejects | Being in earlier scriptures means the Qur’an’s stories are found there, while warning people means warning them of its meaning; this limited figurative reading is warranted by decisive proofs. | mentions 26:196, 6:19
[RAZI-FULL:v1p174/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · الشافعي | reports | Al-Shafi‘i’s newer view requires the follower to recite; his earlier view requires it only when the imam recites quietly, as also held by Malik and Ibn al-Mubarak, while Abu Hanifa discourages recitation behind the imam.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p174/r7] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Qur’an 73:20 commands recitation for both a solitary worshipper and a follower behind an imam. | mentions 73:20
[RAZI-FULL:v1p175/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The Prophet recited in prayer, so believers follow him; saying that congregational following blocks recitation merely introduces a counterargument. | mentions 6:153
[RAZI-FULL:v1p175/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The command to establish prayer includes all the Prophet’s prayer actions, including reciting al-Fatiha. | mentions 2:43
[RAZI-FULL:v1p175/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The hadith no prayer without the Opening is general; Razi says the opposing report exempting those behind an imam has been criticized.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p175/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The Prophet’s instruction to a Bedouin to read whatever Qur’an is easy applies equally to the follower and the solitary worshipper.
