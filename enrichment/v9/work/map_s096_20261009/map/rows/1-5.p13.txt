[IBNKATHIR:1:5/r15] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | reports | One explanation of the plural nūn is that the speaker represents the whole class of worshippers, including fellow believers, especially in congregational prayer or when leading them.
[IBNKATHIR:1:5/r16] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · بعضهم | reports | Another explanation allows a plural of honor: the servant is noble while worshipping, but should not claim collective standing outside worship because everyone depends on God.
[IBNKATHIR:1:5/r17] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · بعضهم | reports | A third view says we is more humble than I worship because the singular could imply that one person alone can fully worship or properly praise God.
[IBNKATHIR:1:5/r18] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Servanthood is an exalted station; God calls His Messenger His servant at the revelation, public worship, and night journey, and directs him to worship amid rejection. | mentions 18:1, 72:19, 17:1, 15:97, 15:98, 15:99
[IBNKATHIR:1:5/r19] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · بعضهم | reports | Some argue that servanthood is nobler than messengerhood because worship proceeds from creation to God, while the messenger's mission proceeds from God to creation; Ibn Kathir rejects the claim and its reasoning.
[IBNKATHIR:1:5/r2] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · عمرو بن فايد | reports | Amr ibn Fayd's light yāʾ with kasra is a rejected anomalous reading because iyā means sunlight.
[IBNKATHIR:1:5/r20] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · بعض الصوفية | reports | Some Sufis criticize worship motivated by reward or avoidance of punishment, and even worship as honor through religious duties, arguing that the highest worship is for God's own perfection.
[IBNKATHIR:1:5/r21] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · آخرون | rejects | Others rebut the Sufi criticism: worship directed to God can still seek reward and protection from punishment, as the Prophet affirms to a Bedouin asking for Paradise and refuge from Hell.
[IBNKATHIR:1:5/r3] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · بعض القراء | reports | Some read ayyaka with an open hamza and geminated yāʾ, while others substitute hāʾ for hamza, as illustrated by a poetic line.
[IBNKATHIR:1:5/r4] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · يحيى بن وثاب والأعمش | reports | All readers open the initial nūn of nastaʿīn except Yahya ibn Waththab and al-A'mash, who use kasra, a dialect of several Arab tribes.
[IBNKATHIR:1:5/r5] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | In ordinary language ʿibādah is rooted in humility, as in a subdued road or camel; in religious usage it combines complete love, submission, and fear.
[IBNKATHIR:1:5/r6] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Fronting and repeating iyyāka marks special concern and restriction: worship is for God alone and reliance is on Him alone, the perfection of obedience.
[IBNKATHIR:1:5/r7] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · بعض السلف | reports | Some early authorities call this the secret of the Fatiha, itself the secret of the Qur'an: the first clause renounces shirk and the second renounces self-power through surrender to God.
[IBNKATHIR:1:5/r8] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Worship and reliance recur together elsewhere in the Qur'an, including commands to worship God and trust Him. | mentions 11:123, 67:29, 73:9
[IBNKATHIR:1:5/r9] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | The speech shifts from speaking of God in the third person to addressing Him directly because praise brings the worshipper near and present before Him.

[BURHAN:v1p16/r2] BURHAN, d. 794 · بعض السلف | reports | Some early authorities say each Qurʾānic word has an outward sense, an inward sense, a limit, and a point of departure; relations among words exceed human enumeration.
[BURHAN:v1p16/r3] BURHAN, d. 794 · القاضي أبو بكر بن العربي | reports | Ibn al-ʿArabī groups the Qurʾān’s core subjects into monotheism, reminder—including promise, warning, Paradise, Hell, and purification—and rulings, including duties and commands.
[BURHAN:v1p16/r4] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | holds | Al-Fātiḥa is the Mother of the Book because it contains all three fields: monotheism through 1:4, rulings in 1:5, and reminder from 1:6 onward.
[BURHAN:v1p16/r5] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | reports | The claim that Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ equals a third of the Qurʾān is explained either as a reward granted by God or as a meaning-based division, since it contains monotheism. | mentions 112:1
[BURHAN:v1p16/r6] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | reports | One explanation for the title “Mother of the Book” is that al-Fātiḥa precedes the rest of the Qurʾān as a mother precedes a daughter.
[BURHAN:v1p16/r7] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | reports | Another explanation of “Opening” is that al-Fātiḥa opens the gates of Paradise, as discussed elsewhere.
[BURHAN:v1p16/r8] BURHAN, d. 794 · أبو الحكم بن برجان | reports | Ibn Barrajān divides Qurʾānic knowledge into God’s names and attributes, prophethood and its proofs, and legal obligation and trial; the last is hardest because it is unfamiliar and neglected.
