[ALUSI-FULL:v1p69#2/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī’s Kashshāf and Fāʾiq suggest hamd and madḥ are synonymous, defining hamd as praise and description with beauty; al-Ālūsī judges this likely but rejects it.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p69#2/r8] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | prefers | Al-Ālūsī holds that madḥ can praise involuntary qualities, unlike hamd; that distinction explains why God says al-hamdu lillāh, since God is a freely acting agent.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p69/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الدواني | reports | Al-Dawānī explains that praise without belief matching its content would become mockery, taking belief to mean the act of inward exaltation rather than propositional assent.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p69/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Al-Ālūsī answers that hamd can be performative, so correspondence to belief is inapplicable; ordinary speakers also use iʿtiqād to mean regard or esteem for someone.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p69/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | rejects | He rejects two replies—that the speaker believes the attributed quality, or that the praise is figurative—because the first contradicts plain experience and the second often contradicts the facts.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p69/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | A statement’s not being believed does not prevent its literal or figurative use; deliberate falsehoods can serve emphasis, wit, testing, or imaginative purposes.

[ALUSI-FULL:v1p69/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الأكثر | reports | Most authorities interpret God praising Himself as reporting His entitlement to praise, commanding praise, speaking through servants, or figuratively displaying His perfect attributes; al-Sayyid favors the last explanation.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p70/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | Definitions distinguish gratitude as bodily deed versus hamd as speech, outward versus inward blessings, or tongue-only thanks for blessings; al-Rāghib’s account limits it to blessing.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p70/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الطيبي | reports | Al-Ṭībī calls gratitude by speech, deeds, and heart the technical usage of legal theorists, who mean the obligatory worship of the benefactor; al-Ālūsī rejects the claim that linguistic gratitude is only verbal.

[ALUSI-FULL:v1p70/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · صاحب التجريد | reports | Some add a fourth form, God thanking God, since only God can thank Him fully; a cited poem contrasts gratitude to people by heart, word, and deed with gratitude to God through His own thanking.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p70/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Hamd and gratitude overlap in part, but hamd is stronger because it openly discloses blessing while ingratitude conceals it; verbal praise is clearer than hidden belief or deeds that may be habitual.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p70/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Calling the state more eloquent than speech is a rhetorical claim: worshipful actions may be indistinguishable from ordinary habits, whereas verbal praise explicitly names God’s glory.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p71#2/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī calls comprehensive scope an imagined error and takes the article as genus; the ensuing debate addresses whether that reading protects the Muʿtazilī account of human acts.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p71#2/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | One argument says Muʿtazilīs assign praise for human deeds to humans because they create their acts; the reply is that even good deeds depend on God’s power and enabling, so praise ultimately returns to Him.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p71#2/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | Another defense treats genus in rhetorical context as the complete instance; a critic replies that comprehensive scope can likewise treat everything else as negligible, so neither reading avoids the theological difficulty unaided.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p71#2/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | A third argument derives genus from verbal nouns’ basic, non-comprehensive meaning; the reply is that surrounding indications can still make a verbal noun comprehensive.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p71#2/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | rejects | Al-Ālūsī rejects the claim that genus is the obvious common reading: in rhetorical settings a definite generic article commonly conveys comprehensiveness, especially when exclusive praise is at issue.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p71#2/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | prefers | He prefers genus because its exclusivity follows directly from the wording and logically entails exclusivity of every instance, making the proof stronger than separately asserting total scope.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p71#2/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | He challenges the claim that genus exclusivity is immediately apparent: its inference is too subtle to be the obvious reading, and the manuscript ends while he disputes the argument’s rhetorical strength.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p71/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · ابن عمر | reports | Ibn ʿUmar transmits the saying that praise is gratitude’s head and that no servant thanks God without praising Him; al-Ālūsī notes its interrupted chain but says a supporting report strengthens it.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p71/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Praise is like the head because speech makes meaning clearest, and the head is the most evident, elevated, and sustaining member; this may explain why God names praise first.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p71/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | Other rationales say praise is a fitting beginning for the Lord and that praise exceeds thanks because it need not arise from a benefit received by the speaker.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p71/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | Praise can concern withholding as well as giving and is thus unbounded, while gratitude answers a finite gift; averting harm is more urgent than gaining benefit, favoring praise first.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p71/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Praise has a narrower occasion but a broader object than gratitude; its inclusion may signal a sacred allusion, and tasbīḥ is omitted because praise already includes both negative and positive perfections.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p71/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Every tasbīḥ counts as praise, not vice versa: glorification negates deficiencies, whereas praise also affirms positive qualities, so its comprehensiveness suits the Qur’an; priority changes with context.

[ALUSI-FULL:v1p72/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Al-Ālūsī says treating genus as perfection and treating comprehensive praise as exhaustive both require interpretation; making everything else negligible adds an unnecessary step.
