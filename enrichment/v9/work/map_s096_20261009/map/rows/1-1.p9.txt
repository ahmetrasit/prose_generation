[JISHUMI:v1p200/r10] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | holds | Al-Raḥmān and al-Raḥīm derive from mercy, whose basis is benefaction to the needy; both are intensive forms, with faʿlān more emphatic than faʿīl because it departs further from the base pattern.
[JISHUMI:v1p200/r2] JISHUMI, d. 494 · أبى بن كعب | reports | Ubayy reports that the Prophet called the Fātiḥa the unmatched Mother of the Qur’an and seven oft-repeated verses, divided between God and servant, with the servant receiving what he asks.
[JISHUMI:v1p200/r3] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | prefers | Ism derives from sumu, elevation, rather than simah, mark: its plural is asmāʾ, its diminutive sumayy, and words losing an initial radical do not take hamzat al-waṣl in this way.
[JISHUMI:v1p200/r4] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | reports | The proposed root of ism is elevation (also linked with samāʾ), a mark, or the verb meaning to rise and become manifest as a sign of the underlying meaning.
[JISHUMI:v1p200/r5] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | holds | Ism can weigh fiʿl with either ḍamma or kasra, but not faʿl with fatḥa, since its regular plural would be afʿul rather than afʿāl.
[JISHUMI:v1p200/r6] JISHUMI, d. 494 · سيبويه | reports | One derivation takes Allah from ilāh, dropping the hamza and making the alif-lām an obligatory substitute so the name becomes a proper name.
[JISHUMI:v1p200/r7] JISHUMI, d. 494 · الخليل وأبي علي | reports | Al-Khalīl and Abū ʿAlī regard Allah as an un-derived name; not every word must be derived, or derivation would regress without end.
[JISHUMI:v1p200/r8] JISHUMI, d. 494 · النضر بن شميل | reports | A group including al-Naḍr b. Shumayl derives Allah from taʾalluh, worship; Ibn ʿAbbās read ilāhatak as your worship, and a poetic line uses taʾalluh for devotion.
[JISHUMI:v1p200/r9] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | reports | Other derivations connect Allah with seeking refuge in someone, bewilderment (Abū ʿAmr), finding repose with someone (al-Mubarrad), or concealment from lāha.
[JISHUMI:v1p203/r1] JISHUMI, d. 494 · أبي عمرو بن العلاء | reports | Abū ʿAmr explains Allah as the one whose essential greatness leaves minds bewildered.
[JISHUMI:v1p203/r10] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | reports | A further distinction assigns al-Raḥmān to creation and al-Raḥīm to provision.
[JISHUMI:v1p203/r11] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | reports | The pairing intensifies the attribution of mercy and teaches that all blessings come from God.
[JISHUMI:v1p203/r12] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | reports | Because Arabs commonly knew Allah but not al-Raḥmān, the names are paired to show that Allah, al-Raḥmān, and al-Raḥīm all name and describe Him.
[JISHUMI:v1p203/r13] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | reports | Another explanation says al-Raḥmān is more frequent in the Torah, al-Raḥīm in the Gospel, and Allah in the Qur’an, so the pairing shows all refer to God.
[JISHUMI:v1p203/r14] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | holds | Al-Raḥmān precedes al-Raḥīm because its stronger intensity and exclusive use make it like a proper name, and the more definite name comes first.
[JISHUMI:v1p203/r15] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | holds | The three names in the basmala serve seeking aid: God can bestow every blessing and has ample mercy and beneficence.
[JISHUMI:v1p203/r16] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | holds | Beginning an important undertaking by naming God is recommended because it seeks His aid, acknowledges divinity, and admits His blessing; a report says an important matter begun without it is cut off.
[JISHUMI:v1p203/r2] JISHUMI, d. 494 · المبرد | reports | Al-Mubarrad explains Allah as the one whose remembrance creatures find repose in.
[JISHUMI:v1p203/r3] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | reports | Another reported explanation is that God is seen but does not see.
[JISHUMI:v1p203/r4] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | rejects | The gloss “the worshipped one” is wrong because others besides God are worshipped and are not gods.
[JISHUMI:v1p203/r5] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | rejects | Defining Allah as “the one entitled to worship” wrongly implies that He was not God in eternity.
[JISHUMI:v1p203/r6] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | reports | One view treats al-Raḥmān and al-Raḥīm as synonymous names for the merciful, like nadmān and nadīm.
[JISHUMI:v1p203/r7] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | reports | Another view distinguishes the names because other beings may be called raḥīm but not raḥmān.
[JISHUMI:v1p203/r8] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | reports | One distinction makes al-Raḥmān the provider for all creatures and al-Raḥīm the forgiver of all believers.
[JISHUMI:v1p203/r9] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | reports | Another makes al-Raḥmān the giver of foundational blessings only God can create, while al-Raḥīm denotes one possessing mercy.
