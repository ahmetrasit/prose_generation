[SAMIN-DURR:7:172#3/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الواحدي | reports | Al-Wāḥidī argues that if the descendants said شهدنا, أن تقولوا must attach to وأشهدهم, since شهدنا أن تقولوا would make the witnesses themselves say that they might say it; the author replies that some descendants can testify that others will say it.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:172#3/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الجرجاني | reports | Al-Jurjānī transmits another view: the covenant story ends at قالوا بلى, and شهدنا begins a new report meaning we witness, as in al-Ḥuṭayʾa's line using شهد for يشهد; then أن تقولوا depends on that verb.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:172#3/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو عمرو | reports | Abū ʿAmr reads يقولوا in both places in the third person, while the others read تقولوا in the second person; the third person suits شهدنا as God's speech, while another analysis makes it attach to وأشهدهم and treats قالوا شهدنا as an interruption, with the second person as a shift in address.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:172#3/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | One view, held by many, takes the covenant literally: God brought Adam's descendants out like particles, made them testify, and all recognized Him; believers spoke truth in pre-eternity while polytheists spoke dissemblingly.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:172#3/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Another view, including al-Zamakhsharī's, treats the testimony as representation rather than a literal primordial scene, comparing it to the speaking heavens and earth in 41:11. | mentions 41:11
[SAMIN-DURR:7:172#4/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The continuation illustrates the reported representational reading with a scriptural example and poems in which saddle straps address a camel's belly and the east wind speaks. | mentions 41:11
[SAMIN-DURR:7:172/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | من ظهورهم is an appositive with a repeated preposition for من بني آدم; the apposition may be of inclusion or part to whole, and the latter is the apparent reading, as in striking someone's back or cutting his hand. | mentions 43:33, 7:75
[SAMIN-DURR:7:172/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abū al-Baqāʾ considers the apposition one of inclusion.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:172/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī considers it part-to-whole apposition, which the author calls apparent.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:172/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | ذريتهم is the direct object; the Kufans and Ibn Kathīr read it singular, while the remaining readers use the plural ذرياتهم.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:172/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | reports | For the plural reading, the Shaykh allows an omitted object understood as the covenant, with ذرياتهم as apposition to the pronoun in ظهورهم; taking the covenant from the descendants' backs is metaphorical language for a weighty binding pledge of monotheism. | mentions 4:154
[SAMIN-DURR:7:172/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | holds | The Shaykh says the singular reading must be the object of أخذ with an omitted annexed noun, meaning the covenant of their descendants; unlike the feminine plural, its accusative ending cannot also mark genitive apposition.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:172/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Across Yā Sīn and al-Ṭūr, the Kufans and Ibn Kathīr use the singular, Ibn ʿĀmir uses the plural, and Abū ʿAmr and Nāfiʿ mix them; Abū ʿAmr agrees in Yā Sīn, while Nāfiʿ agrees only with al-Ṭūr's first occurrence. | mentions 36:41, 52:21
