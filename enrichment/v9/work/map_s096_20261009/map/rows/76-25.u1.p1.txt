  76:25/q10/p1 Remembrance and worship support the Prophet's patience, resolve and persistence amid opponents' words and attacks. — reasons: Rāzī places remembrance after the prohibition on obeying wrongdoers and beside night prostration and glorification in 76:26. Elmalılı connects patient endurance and invocation with 2:153. Ibn ʿĀshūr says continued preaching and varied remembrance help him endure opponents; his parallels are 15:97–98 and 73:8,10. Iṣlāḥī urges trust in God and patience through prayer. Kur'an Yolu says devotion strengthens resistance to polytheists. Sāmarrāʾī links remembrance to crisis and cites 15:97–99, 8:45, Jonah's rescue through glorification in 37:143–144 and 21:87, and glorification's high worth. Study Quran reads it as counsel amid worldly tribulations, citing 15:97–98.
  76:25/q10/p2 Remembrance remedies spiritual harm and prepares for worship through knowledge of God. — reasons: Biqāʿī calls unhindered remembrance the remedy, says knowing God is worship's aim, and calls thought joined to worship its highest form.
  76:25/q10/p3 The preceding ban concerns sinful obedience, while requests involving neither sin nor disbelief may be accepted. — reasons: Biqāʿī distinguishes outward from inward sin and cites 6:120; this qualifies the preceding command not to obey unbelievers.
  76:25/q10/p4 The revelation and guidance call the Prophet to obey God alone. — reasons: Rāzī places the instruction after the gift of revelation in 76:23.
  76:25/q10/p5 Opponents resist because they prefer immediate gains to the certain Hereafter. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī says they fabricate objections despite the Hereafter's certainty and the ease with which God can recreate them.
