[QURTUBI-FULL:1:5/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ثعلب | reports | Thaʿlab says dāna has opposite meanings: to obey, disobey, become powerful, become low, and subdue; din can also mean habit, affair, a ruler’s way, or illness.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:5/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | Poetry uses din for obedience, habit or custom, a ruler’s way of life, and illness, with examples from ʿAmr ibn Kulthum, al-Muthaqib, Zuhayr, and al-Lihyani.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:5/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Iyyaka naʿbudu shifts from third-person praise to direct address; worship means obedient submission, and speaking the verse confesses God’s lordship amid others worshipping idols. | mentions 76:21, 10:22
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:5/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Iyyaka nastaʿin asks for help, support, and success; one report says confessing both worship and dependence escapes both fatalism and the Qadariyya.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:5/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The object is fronted for emphasis and because Arabs put the important item first; the repeated pronoun prevents reading the clause as seeking aid from someone else.
[BAYDAWI:1:5/r1] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | After naming the one deserving praise and describing His distinguishing attributes, the discourse directly addresses Him; this moves from proof and absence to presence and witnessing, the knower's culmination after reflection on God's names, gifts, and works.
[BAYDAWI:1:5/r10] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | Istiʿāna is seeking help. Necessary assistance includes capacity, conceiving the act, tools, and material; when these obtain, one can be morally commanded.
[BAYDAWI:1:5/r11] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | Nonessential help makes an act easier or motivates it, like a mount for one able to walk; it is not required for valid moral responsibility.
[BAYDAWI:1:5/r12] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | reports | The request may concern every important matter or specifically performance of worship.
[BAYDAWI:1:5/r13] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | reports | The implicit “we” may include reciter, guardian angels, and those at congregational prayer, or all monotheists; sharing their worship and need seeks acceptance through their blessing and explains communal prayer.
[BAYDAWI:1:5/r14] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | Fronting the object signals reverence, importance, and exclusivity; Ibn ʿAbbās glosses it “We worship You and no other.”
[BAYDAWI:1:5/r15] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | The worshipper attends first to God and views worship as a noble relation, not personal achievement; the gnostic reaches communion through total attention to God and loss of self-awareness.
