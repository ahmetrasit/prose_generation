80:2/q20 What priority does the encounter teach? [* · interpretation]
  80:2/q20/p1 Attend to the receptive believer seeking guidance before courting influential but indifferent people. — reasons: Al-Zajjaj says not to neglect a Muslim for someone else. Jishumi says to underscore the guided believer's importance. Al-Qurtubi says a poor believer is better than a rich person and compares 6:52, 18:28 and 8:67. Al-Tabrisi says he may purify himself or heed a reminder. Ibn Kathir extends the rule across status, wealth, sex and age: warn all equally, for God guides whom He wills. Islahi says the Prophet is responsible for eager learners, not refusers. Kuran Yolu describes the visitor's fear of God, desire for Qur'anic light and purification.
  80:2/q20/p2 The man gained lasting honor from the event, unlike Pharaoh, who failed to heed a reminder. — reasons: Al-Biqai draws this comparison to stress the value of receptivity.
80:2/q21 Why does the opening describe the actor in the third person? [* · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether indirection primarily honors and softens, sharpens accountability, or builds suspense.)
  80:2/q21/p1 The indirect form honors or gently prepares the Prophet before direct address. — reasons: Jishumi says direct ‘you frowned’ would sound harsher; al-Qurtubi sees reassurance in the turn to direct address. Al-Biqai compares Sad ibn Muadh's indirectness when judging Banu Qurayza. Alusi says the deed seems unlike the Prophet. Ibn Ashur says reproach arrives gradually, comparing 9:43 and reports that pardon was announced before the lapse. Al-Tabataba'i reports a view that later direct address restores intimacy.
  80:2/q21/p2 The third-person form and later direct address intensify the rebuke. — reasons: Al-Tabataba'i says the shift increases accountability; Asad says third-person forms sharpen the reproof. Elmali sees a move from outward appearance to inward meaning.
  80:2/q21/p3 Unnamed pronouns create suspense until later references identify the people and the incident. — reasons: Ibn Ashur points to the opening two verbs and later references to the blind and rich men, including 80:6. Islahi says the unidentified opening makes readers wonder, while later ‘you know’ identifies the Prophet; avoiding his name avoids suggesting simple rudeness was the cause.
80:2/q22 What does this self-reproving revelation show? [* · interpretation]
  80:2/q22/p1 The Prophet faithfully conveyed even revelation that reproved him. — reasons: Ibn Zayd's saying asserts he would have concealed these verses if he concealed any revelation; Ibn Atiyya adds verses concerning Zayd and Zaynab. Asad treats the public conveyance as evidence of revelation's objectivity and connects it with 53:3.
80:2/q23 How does this event fit the sūra and its neighboring themes? [* · links]
  80:2/q23/p1 The sūra corrects neglect of receptive people, then argues from creation, food and resurrection to warning and recompense. — reasons: Al-Biqai links the warning of those who fear in 79:45 with the sūra's proofs of divine power from human creation and food. Al-Tabataba'i traces the rebuke, human dependence despite ingratitude, resurrection and recompense.
  80:2/q23/p2 ʿAbasa pairs with al-Naziʿat in theme, style and reasoning, while rearranging the topics. — reasons: Islahi compares the pair and cites 79:45.
80:2/q24 Where and under what names is this sūra known? [* · sciences]
  80:2/q24/p1 It is Meccan, described also as early Meccan. — reasons: Al-Tabataba'i calls its Meccan provenance unquestionable; Study Quran calls it early Meccan.
  80:2/q24/p2 It is also called al-Sakhkhah and al-Safarah. — reasons: Study Quran derives those titles from verses 33 and 15 respectively.
