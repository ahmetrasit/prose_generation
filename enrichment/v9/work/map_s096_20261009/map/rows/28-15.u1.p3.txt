28:15/q41 How does the Qur'an's arrangement and style establish its distinctiveness? [* · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether distinctive form, mixed arrangement, or the unanswered challenge is the cited proof.)
  28:15/q41/p1 Its distinctive speech exceeds familiar poetry, rhymed prose, sermons, letters and ordinary speech. — reasons: Al-Rummani compares its departure from convention to a handwoven cloth exceeding the finest machine work and to miracles of the sea and staff; changing rhyme endings does not reproduce authorship.
  28:15/q41/p2 Mixing stories, warnings and rulings within a sura gives a listener access to proofs across subjects. — reasons: Al-Khattabi reports the contrary criticism that segregating topics would improve organization, then argues for the broad benefit of their combination.
  28:15/q41/p3 The prolonged challenge and opponents' failure to answer prove the Qur'an could not be fabricated. — reasons: Al-Baqillani notes charges of fabrication and borrowed tales; he argues that hostile opponents faced severe losses yet never produced an easy verbal counter in their own language.
28:15/q42 How should Qur'anic interpretation be approached? [* · interpretation]
  28:15/q42/p1 Reading requires understanding and action, with critical judgment about sources. — reasons: Tha'labi describes the Qur'an as overseer of earlier scriptures, warns that memorization alone is insufficient, and says trusted study distinguishes sound from unsound. He criticizes doctrinal innovators, jurists without tafsir expertise, and uncritical report collectors.
28:15/q43 How is the Qur'an said to encompass knowledge and languages? [* · sciences]
  28:15/q43/p1 It encompasses earlier and later knowledge and alludes to many languages while selecting familiar forms. — reasons: Itqan cites Ibn al-Naqib on Arabic and foreign languages and al-Khuyyi on Persian istabraq as an irreplaceable concise name for fine thick brocade.
  28:15/q43/p2 It indicates foundational knowledge and recounts all major earlier peoples, including Moses' killing of the Copt. — reasons: Al-Suyuti lists creation, histories, prophets and episodes of Moses from infancy through Midian and Pharaoh.
28:15/q44 What are the cited signs and virtues of the Qur'an? [* · sciences]
  28:15/q44/p1 Predictions and accurate ancient accounts are proofs; permanence, affective power and compact breadth are further virtues. — reasons: Itqan says future events occurred as foretold and ancient scriptures were known to an unlettered Prophet; it reports deaths upon hearing verses, unwearying recitation, divine preservation and breadth in few words, while separating these virtues from four central inimitability grounds.
28:15/q45 How are omitted agents used in Qur'anic style? [* · rhetoric]
  28:15/q45/p1 Passive, middle forms and figurative attribution can all omit an agent for a shared rhetorical purpose. — reasons: Bint al-Shati says grammar identifies forms but not the point of omitting the agent; she notes repeated passives in Resurrection scenes of trumpet, earth, mountains and Hell.
28:15/q46 What is the proper practice of istiʿādha and recitation pauses? [* · readings] (turns on: Which recitation context and transmitted phonetic rule applies.)
  28:15/q46/p1 Audible istiʿādha cues listeners; whispering suits solitary and quiet recitation and is widely preferred in prayer. — reasons: Abu Shama conditions loudness on hearers. Al-Shafi'i has both choice and loudness recommendations; school reports prefer whispering, with practice from Abu Hurayra and Ibn Umar. The author gives conditions for recitation rounds.
  28:15/q46/p2 Hamza may be eased, especially at pause, under transmitted Arabic practice. — reasons: Abu Shama calls the topic difficult; Ibn Mihran wrote on it and cites dialect evidence.   28:15/q46/p3 Rūm and ishām at pause show a connected vowel to a listener, with restrictions on particular tanwīn forms. — reasons: The author favors al-Shatibi and al-Dani's balance and disputes Sibt al-Khayyat's claimed consensus. Isham is barred on yawma'idh but allowed on kull and ghawash because of vowel origins.
