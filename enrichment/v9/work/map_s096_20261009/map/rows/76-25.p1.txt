[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:76:25/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Morning and evening remembrance and worship strengthen the Prophet's devotion, resolve, and resistance to the polytheists' attacks.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:76:25/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Râzî | reports | One reported view takes remembrance as the five daily prayers: morning means dawn prayer, evening means noon and afternoon prayers, night prostration means sunset and night prayers, and extended night prayer is obligatory for the Prophet but recommended for his community.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:76:25/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Râzî | reports | A second reported view understands morning and evening remembrance as verbal glorification and belief in God's transcendence throughout day and night, rather than prayer alone.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:76:25/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · İbn Âşûr | reports | A third view includes all these forms of remembrance: obligatory prayer, voluntary prayer, supplication, and seeking forgiveness.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:76:25/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | prefers | The author takes the verses broadly as stressing worship and devotion, especially prayer at parts of the night alongside glorification, supplication, repentance, and seeking forgiveness.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p350/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | After commanding patience and forbidding obedience to sinners and ingrates, God commands abundant remembrance; the author links remembrance to moments requiring patience and crisis.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p350/r2] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | The Qur'an links glorification with distress and struggle: it follows the Prophet's constricted heart and accompanies steadfastness in battle. | mentions 15:97, 15:98, 15:99, 8:45
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p350/r3] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | Jonah's continued glorification saved him from remaining in the whale; remembrance and glorification are among the purest, highest deeds, so they follow distress logically. | mentions 37:143, 37:144, 21:87
[STUDYQURAN:76:25/r1] STUDYQURAN · Aj, JJ, Ṭs | reports | The verses may enjoin naming God in the daily prayers; Aj also reads them as remembrance of God at every moment.
[STUDYQURAN:76:25/r2] STUDYQURAN · Aj | reports | In context, the command counsels remembering God amid worldly tribulations; many Sufis take verse 25 as invoking God’s Supreme Name, Allāh. | mentions 15:97, 15:98
