## THE CURRENT MAP
33:43/q01 How does this verse answer the preceding commands to remember and glorify God? [هُوَ ٱلَّذِى يُصَلِّى عَلَيْكُمْ · links] (turns on: The notes give different links to the surrounding passage.)
  33:43/q01/p1 God and the angels bless those who remember Him; His response rewards and encourages their remembrance. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Durr say remembrance has no fixed limit and applies in every condition, unlike other duties. Rāzī says divine mercy despite human forgetfulness encourages remembrance. Ibn Kathīr and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite 2:152 and the divine report that God remembers the servant in a better assembly. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the prayer a like but greater reward for morning and evening remembrance. Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts 9:67 with 2:152 and identifies remembrance with mercy and light. Kashshāf and Nasafī connect mercy with God's commands to remembrance, prayer, obedience and good.
  33:43/q01/p2 The passage turns from the believers' promised victory to address and honor the Prophet as their leader. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī relates the turn to the preceding admonition and promised victory.
33:43/q02 Who receives the prayer announced here? [عَلَيْكُمْ · referent] (turns on: Whether the occasion restricts the scope of the address.)
  33:43/q02/p1 It was revealed about the Helpers, the Anṣār. — reasons: Muqātil says God forgives them and orders angels to ask forgiveness for them.
  33:43/q02/p2 Its mercy extends to all believers, beyond the first hearers. — reasons: Rāzī says the closing term believers is glad tidings beyond the original audience. Al-Ālūsī says it affirms mercy to all believers.
33:43/q03 What occasion is reported for the revelation of 33:43? [هُوَ ٱلَّذِى يُصَلِّى عَلَيْكُمْ وَمَلَٰٓئِكَتُهُۥ · reports] (turns on: The reports differ in setting and in who voiced the question.)
  33:43/q03/p1 After 33:56 announced prayer upon the Prophet, Abū Bakr or the Companions asked whether believers shared that honor, and this verse answered. — reasons: The reports vary in speaker: Anas or Mujāhid relate Abū Bakr's remark; Ibn ʿAbbās relates a question by Emigrants and Helpers, and al-Wāḥidī mentions their congratulations. Kashshāf says the consequent wording was 'their greeting'; Muqātil calls this the revealed answer. All link the answer to the honor announced in 33:56.
  33:43/q03/p2 One Miʿrāj account says the verse through 'merciful' was revealed directly to the Prophet at the two-bows distance. — reasons: Bursevī's account says God told Muḥammad to wait because his Lord was praying; Muḥammad objected that God needs no prayer, and God answered that His mercy preceded anger. It says Gabriel was absent there.
  33:43/q03/p3 A second Miʿrāj version has Gabriel stop the Prophet in the seventh heaven and say the Lord is praying. — reasons: The answer in this version calls God the Holy Lord of angels and Spirit and says His mercy preceded anger.
33:43/q04 What does God's ṣalāt upon believers mean? [يُصَلِّى عَلَيْكُمْ · interpretation] (turns on: The sources gloss divine ṣalāt by mercy, forgiveness, praise, honor, blessing, public good mention, general care, proximity or inward gifts.)
  33:43/q04/p1 God shows believers mercy, compassion and blessing. — reasons: Yaḥyā reports Ibn ʿAbbās; al-Ḥasan's Israelite report says mercy precedes wrath. Ibn Qutayba cites verses pairing prayer and mercy; Wāḥidī cites 2:157, as does Ibn Kathīr. Kashshāf and Nasafī appeal to the closing 'merciful.' Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds blessing and beautiful praise; Ibn ʿĀshūr says praise and directing mercy in both worlds. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it special mercy yielding lasting felicity. Biqāʿī describes compassionate favor.
  33:43/q04/p2 God forgives believers. — reasons: Al-Suddī says forgiveness follows obedience. Al-Kalbī says that when God is pleased with a servant He announces forgiveness to the angels and asks them to seek it too. Ibn Qutayba and the paired glosses include mercy with forgiveness.
  33:43/q04/p3 God praises believers, including before the angels. — reasons: Abū al-ʿĀliya's explanation, transmitted by al-Bukhārī via al-Rabīʿ according to Ibn Kathīr, specifies praise before the angels. Ibn Kathīr says praise and mercy do not conflict; Ibn ʿĀshūr pairs good mention with mercy.
  33:43/q04/p4 God honors or esteems believers. — reasons: The first three transmit Sufyān's interpretation; Biqāʿī says divine favor and angelic prayer together display the recipients' honor.
  33:43/q04/p5 God blesses believers. — reasons: Abū ʿUbayda cites al-Aʿshā's use of the verb. Ibn Qutayba, al-Akhfash, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī combine blessing with mercy or forgiveness.
  33:43/q04/p6 God spreads a good reputation or beautiful mention of the servant among people. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī mark this as another explanation; Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes beautiful praise among divine ṣalāt.
  33:43/q04/p7 God cares for the believers' good and welfare. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī includes making honor visible; Abū Ḥayyān says God wills their good through mercy, and al-Ālūsī prefers the general sense on evidence of the closing mercy clause.
  33:43/q04/p8 God draws servants near. — reasons: Al-Qushayrī starts from prayer as supplication and gives proximity as God's act.
  33:43/q04/p9 God's prayer marks or creates inward faith, truth, purification and contentment. — reasons: Abū Bakr ibn Ṭāhir lists faith's light, success, truth, removal of misleading desires and false aims, and contentment in decree. Al-Baqlī describes pre-eternal election in knowledge and love, forgiveness, and appointment of angels while the servant attends to God. Qāshānī describes disclosures of acts and attributes in answer to glorification, since the Essence overwhelms creatures; he cites Gabriel's inability to advance in BURSEVI's parallel account only indirectly. Al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya says eternal prayer precedes and enables remembrance and love.
33:43/q05 What do the angels' ṣalāt and their role mean? [وَمَلَٰٓئِكَتُهُۥ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether angelic prayer is described by its petition, its welfare-seeking aim, or its effect.)
  33:43/q05/p1 They supplicate for believers, especially asking God for forgiveness. — reasons: The notes vary between general supplication, prayer for mercy, and specific forgiveness; al-Wāḥidī says God commands the request, and al-Kalbī recounts God's announcement of forgiveness. Qushayrī distinguishes sinners' forgiveness and the obedient's beneficence. Biqāʿī adds protection from sins and afflictions and carrying remembrance between God and prophets. Ibn Kathīr cites 40:7–9; Islahi cites 40:7 and 42:5. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 42:5 and 10:3: their intercession needs God's permission. Al-Nasafī quotes their prayer for blessing.
  33:43/q05/p2 Their prayer is intercession for believers. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr explains that supplication is intercession, authorized by God according to 42:5 and 10:3.
  33:43/q05/p3 They attend to believers' welfare. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī pairs forgiveness with concern for benefit; Abū Ḥayyān says angelic petitions and divine mercy share the intended good.
  33:43/q05/p4 They are treated as bringing mercy because their prayers are answered. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī infer agency from answered prayer; al-Bayḍāwī calls supplication mercy for that reason. Abū Ḥayyān reports the view.
  33:43/q05/p5 Angels watch over believers as well as praying for them. — reasons: Study Quran connects this care to believers' remembrance and prayers in 33:41–42.
33:43/q06 What does the single ṣalāt verb mean when predicated of both God and angels? [يُصَلِّى عَلَيْكُمْ وَمَلَٰٓئِكَتُهُۥ · meaning] (turns on: The dispute concerns a single common sense, multiple senses in one word, and possible lexical derivations.)
  33:43/q06/p1 It conveys a common concern, inclination or care for the recipients' good, realized differently by God and angels. — reasons: Rāzī says mercy and seeking forgiveness imply one shared concern. Al-Bayḍāwī derives shared welfare and honor figuratively from ṣalw. Al-Ālūsī's school uses a general figurative sense because it disallows two literal senses; he allows metaphor from supplication or metonymy from care, and prefers it because the close mentions mercy. Abū Ḥayyān favors shared intended good over al-Zamakhsharī's agency account. Ibn ʿĀshūr says each agent receives the suited implication of one common sense, making homonymy or a general metaphor unnecessary; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it turning or inclination.
  33:43/q06/p2 The one word bears both senses at once: divine mercy and angelic asking forgiveness. — reasons: Rāzī finds the reported Shāfiʿī view plausible, including literal and figurative senses together. Al-Ālūsī reports those permitting two senses; al-Samīn says coordination is available on such a theory.
  33:43/q06/p3 Prayer fundamentally means supplication; God's act is mercy, while angels ask mercy or forgiveness. — reasons: Language scholars cited by al-Naḥḥās use al-Aʿshā's line and its preceding plea that God spare his father pain and illness. Al-Jishumī says Islamic usage transferred supplication to specified ritual acts. Al-Ālūsī describes a metaphor from supplication through willing good. Ibn ʿĀshūr combines supplication and good mention.
  33:43/q06/p4 Its base is mercy, and its uses belong to one category. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās reports Muḥammad ibn Yazīd's alternative derivation.
  33:43/q06/p5 Its mercy sense extends metaphorically from bending in bowing and prostration to tender inclination toward someone. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī describe bodily bending, then kindly turning, then compassion; al-Bayḍāwī offers mercy and spiritual inclination as an alternative.
  33:43/q06/p6 A split-subject analysis assigns mercy to God and forgiveness to angels without giving one word two literal senses. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports this agent-based solution and likens it to the split-subject parse; al-Samīn allows coordination under shared meaning or meta
