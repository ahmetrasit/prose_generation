[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p62#2/r3] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · author | holds | The sīn form preserves the word’s original consonant; ṣād replaces sīn to harmonize its hissing quality with ṭāʾ’s emphatic articulation.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p49/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | reports | Ibn Kathīr is reported with both sīn and ṣād; Abū ʿAmr with sīn, ṣād, and an intermediate sound, while al-Aṣmaʿī reports zāy; others use ṣād, with Ḥamza between ṣād and zāy.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p49/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | reports | The sīn reading is defended as preserving the word’s original consonant, since a dialect using ṣād before ṭā obscures that origin.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p49/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | reports | The ṣād reading is defended as easier and more pleasing because its emphatic articulation resembles ṭā; sīn is voiceless and farther from it.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p49/r4] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | reports | The zāy reading is explained as voicing the consonant to match ṭā, with ease supported by the variants ṣaqr, saqr, and zaqr.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p49/r5] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | reports | The intermediate sound aims for ease without becoming pure zāy or ṣād, thus avoiding confusion with either.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p49/r6] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو بكر | prefers | Ibn al-Sarrī prefers ṣād for ease, sound, and prevalence; it is not confusing because the relevant dialect avoids sīn before ṭā, and most readers use it.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p49/r7] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو بكر | rejects | He rejects the pure zāy report as likely a mishearing of Abū ʿAmr’s intermediate pronunciation and says it should not govern recitation.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p49/r8] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو بكر | rejects | He rejects the intermediate reading as difficult and nonstandard, though not impossible Arabic; ṣād is more eloquent, common, and close to ṭā, while Abū Ḥātim says pure zāy is unknown.
