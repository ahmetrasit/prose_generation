[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r11] STUDYQURAN · Twelver Shiite school | reports | The Twelver Shiite school requires fifteen sucklings, with variants requiring ten.
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r12] STUDYQURAN · most legal scholars | reports | Most legal scholars require nursing to occur in the child's first two to three years.
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r13] STUDYQURAN · community consensus | reports | The community's consensus is that the exception for unconsummated marriage clearly applies only to stepdaughters, not their mothers.
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r14] STUDYQURAN · some early authorities | reports | Some early authorities reportedly held that a man's wife's mother is prohibited only after he consummates marriage with the wife.
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r15] STUDYQURAN · some | reports | Some reportedly read an addition limiting the prohibition on wives' mothers to those whose marriage was consummated.
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r16] STUDYQURAN · early scholars | reports | As in the discussion of 4:21, early scholars disputed which intimate contact legally constituted consummation. | mentions 4:21
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r17] STUDYQURAN · some | reports | Some held that any intimate contact with a woman prohibited marriage to her daughter.
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r18] STUDYQURAN · Shāfiʿī school | reports | The Shāfiʿī school held that this prohibition applies only in the case of marriage.
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r19] STUDYQURAN · some commentators | reports | Some derive rabībah and rabīb from a stepparent undertaking stepchildren's upbringing, an idea reinforced by “in your care.”
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r2] STUDYQURAN · al-Shāfiʿī | reports | Al-Shāfiʿī suggests a biological daughter born out of wedlock might not be covered by the marriage prohibition.
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r20] STUDYQURAN · a minority tradition | reports | A minority tradition treats being in the stepfather's care as a condition and says stepdaughters outside his home remain marriageable.
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r21] STUDYQURAN · most commentators | reports | Most regard “in your care” as descriptive, not a legal condition; ordinary close family contact is what makes stepchildren forbidden spouses.
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r22] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The ban on marrying sons' former wives applies whether or not the marriage was consummated.
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r23] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The sons-from-loins limitation includes wives of milk-sons but excludes adoptive sons.
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r24] STUDYQURAN · commentators | reports | Commentators connect the rule to the Prophet's marriage to Zayd ibn Ḥārithah's former wife; because Zayd was adopted, not from his loins, the marriage was permissible. | mentions 33:4, 33:37, 33:40
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r25] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Marrying two sisters together is prohibited, while marriages arranged before revelation could stand.
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r26] STUDYQURAN · the Prophet | reports | The Prophet reportedly told converts who had two wives who were sisters to divorce one.
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r3] STUDYQURAN · all other schools of law | reports | All other legal schools prohibit marrying any biological daughter, including one born out of wedlock.
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r4] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | A milk-mother is a nonbiological woman who nurses a child; parents may pay a wet nurse under 2:233 and 65:6. | mentions 2:233, 65:6
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r5] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Milk-siblings are otherwise unrelated children nursed by the same woman, and suckling creates a legal status like blood relations.
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r6] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | A woman who nursed a man is his maḥram, requiring no covering before her and prohibiting marriage; these rules also apply to her husband's and her biological children and others she nursed.
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r7] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | A man may marry the sister of his milk-son or the mother of his milk-brother as exceptions to the suckling rules.
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r8] STUDYQURAN · ʿĀʾishah | reports | ʿĀʾishah maintained that a revealed verse set ten sucklings for establishing milk relations, later changed to five.
[STUDYQURAN:4:23/r9] STUDYQURAN · a more widely transmitted report | reports | A report says one or two sucklings do not establish legal milk relations, leading some to require at least three.
