## THE CURRENT MAP
7:206/q01 Who are those with your Lord? [ٱلَّذِينَ عِندَ رَبِّكَ · referent] (turns on: Whether the phrase specifies angels, includes all spiritually near servants, or receives an inward reading.)
  7:206/q01/p1 The angels, sometimes specified as the angels of the highest assembly. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās and other exegetes are cited by al-Wāḥidī; al-Ṭabrisī cites al-Ḥasan. Al-Qurṭubī calls the identification unanimous; Ibn ʿĀshūr relates their model role to the Messenger's rank. Elmalılı cites 39:75.
  7:206/q01/p2 All who are brought near to God, not angels alone. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī says remembrance lifts the veil of absence although all things are otherwise equally related to God; limiting the phrase to angels would defeat the exhortation to human remembrance.
  7:206/q01/p3 Prophets, messengers, and Imams. — reasons: Reported by Ṭabāṭabāʾī from Tafsīr al-Qummī.
  7:206/q01/p4 Those annihilated in divine unity and abiding through God. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī describes steadfast spiritual subsistence; Najmiyya as transmitted by Bursevī describes effacement of actions, traits, and selves.
  7:206/q01/p5 A metaphor for utter God-consciousness. — reasons: Asad's formulation.
7:206/q02 What does ʿind convey here? [عِندَ رَبِّكَ · meaning] (turns on: Whether nearness describes rank or favor, an honored place or mission, proximity to the Throne, or spatial proximity to God.)
  7:206/q02/p1 Figurative nearness in rank, honor, mercy, and favor, without spatial proximity. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj cites mercy and favor while God is in heaven and earth (6:3); al-Rāzī compares minister and doorkeeper and cites the divine report about broken hearts. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, and others deny bodily location; al-Burhān cites 3:169. Al-Ṭabrisī says God transcends place.
  7:206/q02/p2 An honorific association with their distinguished dwelling and the place where divine commands issue. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī cites people of meaning; al-Rāzī describes the realm of lights, spirits, obedience, and blessings.
  7:206/q02/p3 Their location is wholly governed by God's decree. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī report a place where nothing occurs save His decree; al-Ṭabrisī likens their relation to soldiers under a prince's authority.
  7:206/q02/p4 They are God's messengers to creation, as troops can be said to be with a distant ruler. — reasons: The analogy is reported by al-Naḥḥās, al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, and al-Qurṭubī.
  7:206/q02/p5 Near the Lord's Throne. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports this as another interpretation.
  7:206/q02/p6 Physical proximity implying God occupies a place and direction. — reasons: Al-Rāzī attributes this to anthropomorphists and rejects it by rational and transmitted proofs discussed under 7:54 and 10:3.
7:206/q03 What range of meaning does ʿind have in Arabic? [عِندَ · meaning]
  7:206/q03/p1 It primarily conveys nearness, literal or figurative, and context can make it mean before or behind. — reasons: Al-Burhān compares ladā and waraʾ and cites 18:79, 14:16, 2:91, and 59:14 for contextual direction.
7:206/q04 Why is the group described by a relative clause instead of named as angels? [ٱلَّذِينَ عِندَ رَبِّكَ · rhetoric]
  7:206/q04/p1 The description signals their exalted station and urges people to emulate them. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says the wording invites competition in their conduct.
7:206/q05 What does their lack of istikbār mean? [لَا يَسْتَكْبِرُونَ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the absence of pride is explained as obedient humility or inward effacement of ego.)
  7:206/q05/p1 They do not act proudly or deem themselves too exalted for worship. — reasons: Al-Jishumī derives istikbār from the form istifʿāl, seeking greatness by rejecting truth. Abū Ḥayyān says pride imagines superiority and obstructs obedience; al-Biqāʿī says angels neither show nor seek pride despite purity and rank.
  7:206/q05/p2 They have no ego veil or independent self-claim. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī speaks of witnessing differentiation in unity; Bursevī's Najmiyya account says effacing one's acts removes pride.
7:206/q06 What is their ʿibāda? [عِبَادَتِهِۦ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether ʿibāda is general obedience and reverence or specifically prayer.)
  7:206/q06/p1 Obedient submission and humble reverence to God. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī identifies humility as worship; Ibn al-Jawzī reports obedience; al-Ālūsī and Bursevī stress doing what was commanded. Kuran Yolu defines worship as acknowledging Lordship and showing respect by specified acts.
  7:206/q06/p2 Prayer with humility within it. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī reports this alternative.
7:206/q07 What does their tasbīḥ mean? [وَيُسَبِّحُونَهُۥ · meaning] (turns on: Whether tasbīḥ is transcendence in thought and speech, a formula, prayer, or inward self-negation.)
  7:206/q07/p1 They declare God transcendent above evil, imperfection, and all unfitting attributes. — reasons: Al-Jishumī stresses inadmissible attributes; Ibn ʿĀshūr includes speech and belief; al-Biqāʿī links angelic purity to freedom from desire; Kuran Yolu adds exaltation.
  7:206/q07/p2 They say Subḥān Allāh and remember God. — reasons: Al-Baghawī supplies the utterance; Ibn al-Jawzī reports it as a distinct gloss; Muqātil says remembrance.
  7:206/q07/p3 Their glorification is prayer. — reasons: Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports this from al-Suddī.
  7:206/q07/p4 It negates association by effacing selfhood. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī and al-Ālūsī's inward interpretation.
7:206/q08 What does the imperfect verb suggest about glorification? [وَيُسَبِّحُونَهُۥ · grammar]
  7:206/q08/p1 Its action recurs and continues. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr notes renewed continuous praise; al-Sāmarrāʾī says tasbīḥ and istighfār commonly take a verb for renewed action, citing 62:1.
7:206/q09 What does their sujūd mean? [يَسْجُدُونَ · meaning] (turns on: Whether sujūd denotes the bodily act, prayer, humble submission, or inward self-effacement.)
  7:206/q09/p1 Actual prostration to God, including prostration in prayer. — reasons: Al-Ṭabrisī attributes the prayer detail to al-Ḥasan; Kuran Yolu describes lowering the head to the ground before God alone.
  7:206/q09/p2 Prayer. — reasons: Muqātil, Ibn al-Jawzī, and al-Qurṭubī use prayer as the gloss.
  7:206/q09/p3 Humble submission, especially exclusive servitude to God. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās contrasts sinners; al-Ṭabrisī gives humility among alternatives; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī, and al-Ālūsī stress worship without association.
  7:206/q09/p4 Complete inward annihilation of selfhood. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī says God alone remains after creation perishes; al-Ālūsī repeats the inward reading.
7:206/q10 Why is lahu placed before yasjudūn? [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether fronting principally creates exclusivity or serves the surrounding verse endings.)
  7:206/q10/p1 To restrict prostration to God alone and censure those who worship others. — reasons: Al-Rāzī notes the restriction; Abū Ḥayyān reports this view; Bursevī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say the fronting censures polytheists, and Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that the imperfect conveys continuity.
  7:206/q10/p2 To make the clause fit the verse ending. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān prefers this over restriction; al-Ṭībī also mentions the verse ending alongside the moral rationale.
7:206/q11 Why are glorification and prostration mentioned in that order? [وَيُسَبِّحُونَهُۥ وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · rhetoric]
  7:206/q11/p1 Inward knowledge and purification of God precede bodily worship, which grows from them. — reasons: Al-Rāzī identifies heart then body; Abū Ḥayyān says freedom from pride produces both, with prostration the servant's closest state. Al-Biqāʿī says speech and action both return to the heart and cites the saying on nearness in prostration.
7:206/q12 How can exclusive prostration to God fit the angels' prostration to Adam? [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · interpretation] (turns on: Which angels were commanded to prostrate, or whether a particular command qualifies the verse's general wording.)
  7:206/q12/p1 Only earthly angels prostrated to Adam, not the great heavenly angels. — reasons: Al-Rāzī cites al-Ghazālī and 15:30, 38:73.
  7:206/q12/p2 The command concerning Adam is a particular exception to a general statement. — reasons: Al-Rāzī reports that the particular prevails over the general, citing 15:30 and 38:73.
7:206/q13 Why are the angels' worship and humility cited here? [* · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the emphasis falls on emulation and rebuke, misplaced hopes for intercession, or spiritual joining through remembrance.)
  7:206/q13/p1 Their greater rank and constant devotion urge humans to worship and rebuke arrogant unbelievers. — reasons: Muqātil contrasts Meccans; al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, and Study Quran reason from more honored angels to humans. Al-Rāzī cites 19:31 and 15:99; al-Kashshāf stresses exclusive worship; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls rebuke of polytheists the primary hint and cites Ibn Dāra's poem for emulation. Kuran Yolu says worship lifts humans beyond animal limits.
  7:206/q13/p2 Their own worship challenges idolaters' expectation of angelic intercession. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī says idolaters slight God and the Prophet while expecting angels to intercede; the angels themselves glorify and prostrate.
  7:206/q13/p3 It calls the servant to join the company of those near God through remembrance. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects the verse to the previous command; Iṣlāḥī says earthly rememberers join the angelic assembly; Elmalılı says remembrance and prostration open the way from the Heights to Paradise.
7:206/q14 Does citing angels as models establish that they outrank prophets or humans? [ٱلَّذِينَ عِندَ رَبِّكَ · theology] (turns on: Whether exhortation by an angelic model entails an absolute ranking.)
  7:206/q14/p1 Yes, since the Messenger is urged to worship by appeal to their worship. — reasons: Al-Rāzī reports Abū Bakr al-Aṣamm's argument.
  7:206/q14/p2 No; acquired human imitation of angelic traits can be more meritorious.
