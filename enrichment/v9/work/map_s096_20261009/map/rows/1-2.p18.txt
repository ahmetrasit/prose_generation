[ABUHAYYAN:1:2/r2] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · ابن الأنباري | rejects | Ibn al-Anbārī treats ḥamd as an inversion of madḥ; Abū Ḥayyān rejects this because a jewel may be praised but not thanked.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:2/r20] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The article in al-ḥamd may be anaphoric, defining the familiar praise; essential, defining the nature; or generic, encompassing all praises. A verbal noun is ordinarily not pluralized.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:2/r21] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · ابن الأعرابي | reports | Ibn al-Aʿrābī records the plural aḥmad for ḥamd, apparently treating its different kinds as distinct, and cites a poem using aḥmadī.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:2/r22] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · القراء السبعة | prefers | The nominative reading is stronger and agreed upon by the seven canonical readers: it states that praise is established and enduringly belongs to God, including praise by God and others.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:2/r23] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | With nominative al-ḥamdu the lām marks entitlement; with accusative al-ḥamda a verb such as “I praise God” must be supplied, conveying renewed action.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:2/r24] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | prefers | For the accusative reading, Abū Ḥayyān prefers an omitted verb directly indicated by the wording, such as “I praise God,” over “I say” or “adhere to praise.”
[ABUHAYYAN:1:2/r25] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | In the accusative reading the lām is explanatory, not a strengthening of transitivity, since the verbal noun cannot govern this phrase as its object.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:2/r26] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · زيد بن علي وطائفة | reports | Zayd ibn ʿAlī and a group read rabb al-ʿālamīn in the accusative as praise; the reading weakens if the following adjectives remain genitive.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:2/r27] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الأهوازي | reports | Al-Ahwāzī reports Zayd ibn ʿAlī reading rabb, raḥmān, and raḥīm all in the accusative, avoiding the mismatch in case. | mentions 1:3
[ABUHAYYAN:1:2/r28] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | rejects | Abū Ḥayyān rejects parsing accusative rabb as an implied verb of praise or as apposition: one rests on imagined syntax, the other is interrupted by raḥmān raḥīm. | mentions 1:3
[ABUHAYYAN:1:2/r29] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Rabb may be a verbal noun used adjectivally or an active participle with deleted alif; as a title it is exclusive to God, while others require a possessive complement.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:2/r3] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | prefers | Views say ḥamd and shukr are synonymous, ḥamd is broader, or shukr praises God's acts while ḥamd praises His attributes; Abū Ḥayyān prefers ḥamd as broader.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:2/r30] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The article in al-ʿālamīn is exhaustive; ʿālam is an irregular collective and its sound masculine plural is even more irregular because it violates some conditions of that pattern.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:2/r31] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | prefers | Abū Ḥayyān prefers that al-ʿālamīn denote morally responsible beings, supported by the verse about signs for the knowledgeable; Ḥafṣ's kasra on the lām clarifies the sense. | mentions 30:22
[ABUHAYYAN:1:2/r4] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The lām can express possession, transfer of ownership, entitlement, relation, cause, communication, exclamation, specification, result, location, endpoint, and superiority; Qur'anic and Arabic examples illustrate these functions. | mentions 16:72, 4:105, 28:8, 21:47, 17:78, 7:57, 17:107
