[TABATABAI:2:186#11/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The statement that the pen has recorded what will occur explains why one should ask God: events are determined by Him, while His sovereignty and will remain active and effective at every moment.
[TABATABAI:2:186#11/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · اليهود وغيرهم | reports | An objection says the requested need is either already decreed, and therefore necessary, or not decreed, and therefore impossible; either way prayer cannot affect it.
[TABATABAI:2:186#11/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | Predetermination does not make a thing independent of its causes; prayer itself is one cause, so when it occurs its effect can occur. This is what the reports mean by saying prayer is part of decree.
[TABATABAI:2:186#11/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم | reports | A Prophetic report says that nothing averts decree except prayer.
[TABATABAI:2:186#12/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · الصادق عليه السلام | reports | Al-Ṣādiq says prayer can avert a decree even after it has been firmly established.
[TABATABAI:2:186#12/r10] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · الصادق عليه السلام | reports | In the same exchange, al-Ṣādiq explains that God replaces money only when it is lawfully earned and rightly spent; each dirham spent that way is replaced. | mentions 34:39
[TABATABAI:2:186#12/r11] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The reported etiquettes of prayer help bring a servant closer to the true reality of prayer and petition.
[TABATABAI:2:186#12/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · أبو الحسن موسى عليه السلام | reports | Abū al-Ḥasan Mūsā urges prayer because it averts a decreed calamity even when its implementation is all that remains.
[TABATABAI:2:186#12/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · الصادق عليه السلام | reports | Al-Ṣādiq again says prayer averts a firmly decreed outcome and calls it the key to mercy and success in every need; what is with God is attained only by prayer.
[TABATABAI:2:186#12/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | These reports point to persistence: repeatedly pursuing a sincere intention purifies it.
[TABATABAI:2:186#12/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · اسماعيل بن همام عن أبي الحسن عليه السلام | reports | A single private prayer by a servant equals seventy public prayers.
[TABATABAI:2:186#12/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Keeping prayer private protects the sincerity of the request.
[TABATABAI:2:186#12/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · الصادق عليه السلام | reports | Al-Ṣādiq says prayer remains veiled until the petitioner invokes blessings upon Muḥammad and his family.
[TABATABAI:2:186#12/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · الصادق عليه السلام | reports | Al-Ṣādiq says one who presents forty believers before praying will be answered.
[TABATABAI:2:186#12/r9] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · الصادق عليه السلام | reports | Asked why “Call Me and I will answer” seemed unfulfilled, al-Ṣādiq says to obey God and follow the proper form: praise and glorify Him, recall and thank His blessings, bless Muḥammad and his family, confess sins, and seek forgiveness. | mentions 40:60
[TABATABAI:2:186#13/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | The passage cites a saying that when God wills to answer a servant, He opens supplication for him.
[TABATABAI:2:186#13/r10] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · الصادق عليه السلام | reports | Al-Ayyashi transmits Ja'far al-Sadiq's gloss that responding and believing mean knowing God can grant what they ask.
[TABATABAI:2:186#13/r11] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · أبو عبد الله عليه السلام | reports | Al-Majma' reports Abu Abd Allah's gloss that belief means being certain God can grant what people ask of Him.
[TABATABAI:2:186#13/r12] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · أبو عبد الله عليه السلام | reports | Al-Majma' reports Abu Abd Allah's gloss that guidance means reaching and being guided to the truth.
[TABATABAI:2:186#13/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم | reports | Ibn Umar reports the Prophet saying that whoever is granted an opening for supplication is granted the gates of mercy.
[TABATABAI:2:186#13/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم | reports | A variant of the Ibn Umar report says that an opening for supplication opens the gates of Paradise.
[TABATABAI:2:186#13/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · أئمة أهل البيت | reports | Reports through the Imams of the Prophet’s Household say that whoever is given supplication is given its answer; al-Tabataba'i says this follows from the prior discussion.
[TABATABAI:2:186#13/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم | reports | Mu'adh ibn Jabal transmits the Prophet's saying that true knowledge of God would make mountains move through one's supplication.
[TABATABAI:2:186#13/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Ignorance of God's station and reliance on causes lead people to confine effects to customary causes, even when they treat causes only as necessary intermediaries.
[TABATABAI:2:186#13/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Even people who deny that ordinary causes act by themselves may assume their mediation is indispensable; al-Tabataba'i says this assumption conflicts with God's complete sovereignty.
[TABATABAI:2:186#13/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Causation and the mediation of causes are real, but neither reason nor revelation confines events to their ordinary causes; only intrinsic impossibilities remain unattainable.
[TABATABAI:2:186#13/r9] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Knowing God means accepting that supplication can bring about customary impossibilities that are not intrinsically impossible; the main prophetic miracles are answered prayers.
[TABATABAI:2:186#2/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads divine nearness as absolute: true divine ownership makes humans His servants and brings Him nearer than anything; the cited verses also describe this nearness. | mentions 56:85, 50:16, 8:24
[TABATABAI:2:186#2/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Because God’s dominion and encompassing rule cover every possibility, He can answer any creature’s prayer and remove its need through giving and action.
[TABATABAI:2:186#2/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · اليهود | rejects | The Jews hold that after God created things and fixed their decrees, control passed from Him; thus there is no abrogation, badāʾ, or response to prayer.
[TABATABAI:2:186#2/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · القدرية | rejects | The Qadariyya say God has no agency in human actions; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects this denial of divine sovereignty over acts.
[TABATABAI:2:186#2/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم | reports | A Prophetic report transmitted by both schools calls the Qadariyya the Magians of this community.
[TABATABAI:2:186#2/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | God alone owns absolutely; created things possess only what He grants and permits, and what He neither wills nor permits cannot occur despite human effort. | mentions 35:15
[TABATABAI:2:186#2/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The verse gives both the rule of answered prayer and its cause: people are God's servants, so He is near them, and His nearness grounds His unrestricted response.
[TABATABAI:2:186#2/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The condition “when he calls Me” requires the caller to possess the stated quality genuinely, as in listening to an adviser when he truly advises or honoring a person who truly knows.
[TABATABAI:2:186#2/r9] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The promise applies to a genuine caller whose innate knowledge and desire unite heart and tongue; prayer is what the heart bears and the tongue of natural disposition voices, not words used in jest or falsehood.
[TABATABAI:2:186#3/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The uncounted blessings people “asked” for in 14:34, and all beings’ daily asking in 55:29, are requests made by innate need rather than spoken words; 55:29 makes the point clearer. | mentions 14:34, 55:29
[TABATABAI:2:186#3/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | A request made by natural disposition to God does not miss its answer; an apparently unanswered prayer must lack either genuine asking or exclusive reliance on God.
[TABATABAI:2:186#3/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | One failure is that the person has not truly asked: ignorance may lead someone to request what cannot happen or what they would not want if they knew its reality, as with asking for cure rather than a dead person's return to life.
[TABATABAI:2:186#3/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Another failure is a heart attached to ordinary causes or imagined powers as sufficient or effective; despite verbal invocation, the person has not truly and exclusively asked God.
[TABATABAI:2:186#4/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The passages in 6:40–41 and 6:63–64 show that hardship strips away imagined partners and causes, returning people to innate monotheism; in ease they again attribute efficacy to causes and forget God. | mentions 6:40, 6:41, 6:63, 6:64
[TABATABAI:2:186#4/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | In 40:60, “My worship” means calling upon God: the verse invites prayer and promises a response while explicitly naming prayer as worship. | mentions 40:60
[TABATABAI:2:186#4/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The threat of Hell for abandoning prayer concerns abandoning worship altogether, not merely one branch of worship; this shows that prayer is the root of worship. | mentions 40:60
[TABATABAI:2:186#4/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Other passages depict prayer through fear and hope, earnest calling, hidden invocation, and the response to believers who do good; they illuminate this verse’s subject. | mentions 40:14, 7:56, 21:90, 7:55, 19:3, 19:4, 42:26
[TABATABAI:2:186#5/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The prayer passages just cited contain prayer’s pillars and etiquette; their central requirement is sincerity, with heart and tongue aligned, detachment from every cause besides God, and reliance on Him. | mentions 25
