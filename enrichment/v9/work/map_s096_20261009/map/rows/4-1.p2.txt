[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p118#2/r1] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · author | reports | The Basran objection says an overt noun cannot coordinate with a genitive pronoun unless the preposition is repeated, since the pronoun and its preposition form one unit.
[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p118#2/r2] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · author | reports | The Basrans also appeal to the rule against coordinating an unemphasized noun with a raised pronoun and to the Prophetic prohibition on swearing by anyone besides God.
[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p118#2/r3] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · البصريون | rejects | The Basrans reject treating the genitive as poetic license because the Qur'an contains no place of metrical necessity.
[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p118#2/r4] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · الكوفيون | reports | The Kufans permit the genitive by supplying an omitted preposition, and support the reader on that analysis.
[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p118#2/r5] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · العجاج | reports | Al-ʿAjjāj's answer خير means بخير, illustrating an omitted preposition in ordinary speech.
[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p118#2/r6] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · author | reports | A cited poetic line supplies another example of an omitted preposition: its opening رسم is understood as ورب رسم.
[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p118#2/r7] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · بعضهم | reports | Another interpretation is to fear God regarding kinship ties, by not severing them.
[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p118#2/r8] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · الكوفيون | prefers | Although the Kufans allow and defend the genitive, they still choose the accusative reading.
[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p118/r4] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · author | holds | The light reading of تساءلون drops one of the two tāʾs from تتساءلون; the geminated reading assimilates the second tāʾ into sīn.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v3p118#3/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · ابن كثير ونافع وابن عامر | reports | Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, and Ibn ʿĀmir read تساءلون with the first sīn doubled.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v3p118#3/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · عاصم وحمزة والكسائي | reports | ʿĀṣim, Ḥamza, and al-Kisāʾī read تساءلون with a light sīn.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v3p118#3/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · علي بن نصر وهارون بن موسى وعبيد بن عقيل وعبد الوهاب بن عطاء | reports | These transmitters report Abū ʿAmr reading the form lightly; al-Wāqidī via ʿAdī ibn al-Faḍl and Khārija ibn Muṣʿab also transmit the light form.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v3p118#3/r4] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · اليزيدي وعبد الوارث | reports | Al-Yazīdī and ʿAbd al-Wārith transmit the doubled form from Abū ʿAmr; Abū Zayd reports both, while ʿAbbās permits either but says Abū ʿAmr reads lightly.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v3p118#3/r5] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | holds | The doubled form comes from تتساءلون, with tāʾ assimilated to sīn because they share articulation near the tongue-tip and upper incisors and are both voiceless.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v3p118#3/r6] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | holds | The light form drops one tāʾ from تتفاعلون because adjacent similar consonants are eased by deletion; such clusters can also be eased through assimilation or substitution.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v3p118#3/r7] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | reports | The word طست illustrates substitution of a second sīn with tāʾ because of their closeness and shared voicelessness; al-ʿAjjāj's line is cited for طست.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v3p118#3/r8] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو عثمان | reports | Abū ʿUthmān cites a verse whose final word الطس illustrates the same consonant pattern.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v3p121/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · حمزة | reports | Ḥamza reads والأرحام in the genitive; the other readers read it in the accusative.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v3p121/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | holds | The accusative can coordinate with the syntactic position of the prepositional phrase, or with واتقوا, meaning to honor kinship ties and not sever them.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v3p121/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | rejects | The genitive reading coordinates الأرحام with the pronoun governed by bāʾ; Abū ʿAlī considers this weak in analogy and rare in usage, so prefers avoiding it.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v3p121/r4] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | holds | A bound pronoun replaces tanwīn and is too closely attached to its noun for a ظاهر noun to coordinate with it, unlike a visible genitive complement that can be separated by an adverb.
[IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB:v1p179/r1] IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB, d. 392 · أبو عبد الرحمن عبد الله بن يزيد | reports | Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ʿAbd Allāh ibn Yazīd reads والأرحام in the nominative.
[IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB:v1p179/r2] IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB, d. 392 · author | holds | He parses nominative الأرحام as a topic with an omitted predicate: the kinship ties are also something people must revere and protect; this raises their emphasis above an accusative or genitive adjunct.
[IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB:v1p179/r3] IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB, d. 392 · author | holds | An omitted predicate is recoverable from context, whereas deleting an accusative or genitive الأرحام would leave no indication that kinship ties were intended; raising the phrase emphasizes it, as in al-Farazdaq’s complaint.
[IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB:v1p179/r4] IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB, d. 392 · author | reports | A separate semantic explanation of the raised poetic phrase treats both coordinated descriptions as remaining after the speaker called only for one of them.
