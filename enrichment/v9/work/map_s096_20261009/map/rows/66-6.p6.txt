[BURSEVI:v10p44/r11] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | holds | The believers are told to avoid the Fire prepared for unbelievers, as in al-Baqara, to intensify the warning; sinful believers share that abode below unbelievers, so the command concerns avoiding sin, not apostasy, which al-Tafsir al-Kabir calls unlikely. | mentions 2:24
[BURSEVI:v10p44/r12] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | holds | The angels are the keepers and punishers of the Fire, the Zabaniyah, numbered among its nineteen guardians.
[BURSEVI:v10p44/r13] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | holds | على does not mean physically atop the Fire; it indicates authority, responsibility, mastery, and control over its affairs and inhabitants.
[BURSEVI:v10p44/r14] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · القاشاني | reports | Al-Qashani interprets the angels as celestial and spiritual powers governing terrestrial affairs, including planetary and zodiacal spirits and other powers entrusted with bodily nature.
[BURSEVI:v10p44/r15] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · القاشاني | reports | Al-Qashani says human souls could ascend to the realm of dominion and influence those powers if freed from bodily ranks; immersed in bodily concerns and material forms, they instead become subject to and imprisoned by those powers.
[BURSEVI:v10p44/r2] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | reports | A variant reads وقودها with ḍamma, understood by supplying ‘cause of its fuel’ or as an intensive usage.
[BURSEVI:v10p44/r3] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | holds | الناس means unbelieving humans and jinn; jinn are not separately named because the warning addresses humans and jinn follow human unbelievers, whose denial came first.
[BURSEVI:v10p44/r4] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | holds | The stones burn as fuel, showing the Fire’s extreme heat and force because burning stones instead of wood indicates greater heat.
[BURSEVI:v10p44/r5] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · رسول الله | reports | The Prophet is cited as saying that your fire is one part of seventy of the Fire of Hell.
[BURSEVI:v10p44/r6] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas identifies the stones as sulfur, describing it as intensely hot, quick to ignite, foul-smelling, smoky, and adhesive to bodies, making torment with it more severe.
[BURSEVI:v10p44/r7] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | holds | People become fuel once they arrive in Hell, while the stones serve as fuel before they arrive.
[BURSEVI:v10p44/r8] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | reports | Bursevi cites al-Kashifi’s Persian verses about stones from which unbelievers fashioned idols and gold or silver, along with the Quranic warning about idols as fuel, to illustrate the worshippers’ misplaced trust. | mentions 21:98
[BURSEVI:v10p44/r9] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | reports | Another interpretation takes the stones as people so hardened against accepting truth that they are like stones or harder, as in the cited verse. | mentions 2:74
[BURSEVI:v10p45#2/r1] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | holds | They carry out their commands without sluggishness, delay, omission, or addition.
[BURSEVI:v10p45#2/r2] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · القاضي | reports | Al-Qadi reads the first clause as their past non-disobedience and the second as their continuing future performance of commands.
[BURSEVI:v10p45#2/r3] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · بعضهم | reports | Some explain the shift from past to future by noting that disobedience or its absence follows a command, while punishment of the wretched recurs in the future.
[BURSEVI:v10p45#2/r4] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · بعض الكبار | reports | Some great masters infer the infallibility of all heavenly angels: they are intellects without conflicting desire or appetite, unlike humans; the source distinguishes angels that never ascend from earth and angels that never descend from heaven.
[BURSEVI:v10p45#2/r5] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · بعض الكبار | reports | The same discussion says these angels have no prohibitions to avoid and so miss the reward for refraining, unlike humans and earthly angels, who earn reward both by obeying commands and avoiding prohibitions.
[BURSEVI:v10p45#2/r6] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · الكرماني | reports | Al-Kirmani asks whether abstention counts as an action; the answer is that refraining needs intention to obey the Law and earn reward, but not merely to avert punishment.
[BURSEVI:v10p45#2/r7] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · الكرماني | reports | The common statement that omissions need no intention applies to merely escaping liability; for reward, one must intend abstention in obedience, as with leaving fornication.
[BURSEVI:v10p45/r1] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | holds | غلاظ means rough or harsh, with hearts empty of compassion and mercy.
[BURSEVI:v10p45/r2] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | holds | شداد is the plural of شديد and means strong or powerful.
[BURSEVI:v10p45/r3] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | reports | Another gloss says they are harsh in speech and forceful in action, able to exact vengeance on God’s enemies and unmoved by pleas for mercy; their nature is wrath and severity.
[BURSEVI:v10p45/r4] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | reports | A report describes one angel’s mouth as a year’s journey across and says a single blow with his mace sends seventy thousand people falling into Hell.
[BURSEVI:v10p45/r5] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | reports | ما may be a verbal noun, making the phrase a supplement explaining God’s command, or a relative pronoun after an omitted preposition, meaning the angels do not refuse the order and are resolved to carry it out.
[BURSEVI:v10p45/r6] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · الكاشفي | reports | Al-Kashifi says they cannot be bribed into disobeying an order, unlike the bribable agents of worldly kings.
