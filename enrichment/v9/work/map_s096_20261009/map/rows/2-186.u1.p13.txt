2:186/q13 What virtue or practical value does supplication have? [دَعْوَةَ ٱلدَّاعِ · interpretation] (turns on: The sources give different analyses, reports, or applications of this wording.)
  2:186/q13/p1 Prayer opens divine mercy and may bring aid, forgiveness, and protection. — reasons: QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 (النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم): Ibn Umar reports the Prophet saying that whoever is granted openness in supplication has the doors of response opened for him. DURR-FULL, d. 911 (الحسن): Al-Hasan gives an aphorism: ships are the keys to the seas, roads to the earth, and supplication to heaven. DURR-FULL, d. 911 (ابن عمر عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم): Ibn ʿUmar reports that whoever is granted an opening in supplication is granted the doors of response; al-Tirmidhi’s wording adds that the doors of mercy open and asking for well-being is especially beloved. TABATABAI, d. 1402 (رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم): A report attributed to the Prophet and transmitted by both schools says that prayer is the believer’s weapon. TABATABAI, d. 1402 (علي عليه السلام): In his testament to al-Ḥusayn, ʿAlī says God placed the keys to His treasures in the servant’s hands by permitting requests, so supplication opens the doors of blessings and mercy. TABRISI, d. 548 (أنس بن مالك): Anas ibn Mālik's version gives the response as God being more generous and better, repeated three times.
