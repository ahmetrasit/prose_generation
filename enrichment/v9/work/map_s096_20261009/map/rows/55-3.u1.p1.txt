55:3/q07 What purpose for human creation and capacity does the passage convey? [* · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the emphasis is humanity's receptivity to revelation, the demand for exclusive worship of its creator, or the responsibility to reason from its created intellectual capacity.)
  55:3/q07/p1 Humans were made capable of receiving Qur'anic guidance, learning it, and teaching one another. — reasons: Al-Zamakhshari ties creation to religion and knowing revelation. Abu Hayyan calls humans the intended learners. Al-Samarra'i cites 51:56 on worship as their purpose. Islahi says merciful Qur'anic teaching, human creation, and speech make people fit to learn through revelation instead of punishment, and speech lets them understand and teach one another. The Study Quran reports that humans were made to know revelation before worldly creation. Al-Alusi speaks of perfection in knowledge.
  55:3/q07/p2 Acknowledging God as humanity's creator implies worshiping Him alone. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says the pagans admit God created humanity but ignore the implication of exclusive worship; the clause rebukes that error.
  55:3/q07/p3 Human reason should support belief beyond what can be directly seen. — reasons: Islahi says speech entails inference from particulars to principles and the ability to form opinions. He cites Aristotle on intellect distinguishing humans from animals and says insistence on seeing a reality before believing, despite this capacity, makes people no better than animals.
55:3/q08 What inward account of the human and divine teaching is drawn from this passage? [* · inward] (turns on: Whether the inward emphasis is learning the path after initial ignorance, disclosure of prior teaching, the use of remembrance and reflection, or an intellect planted in original nature.)
  55:3/q08/p1 God creates a person ignorant of Him and then teaches that person the way to Him. — reasons: Al-Sulami reports al-Junayd's account of ignorance at creation followed by guidance toward God.
  55:3/q08/p2 The spirit and teaching precede worldly disclosure, with expression unveiling what was taught before. — reasons: Al-Sulami reports al-Wasiti that the spirit was disclosed in pre-eternity and bayan discloses prior teaching; Bursevi reports a pre-eternal readiness to receive teaching and distinguishes Quran's unified origin from Furqan's differentiated end.
  55:3/q08/p3 Remembrance and reflection can cut a human off from God or bring nearness and knowledge, according to their aim. — reasons: Al-Sulami reports al-Wasiti: self-interested remembrance and reflection estrange; when for and through God, they connect, purify the senses, and let the clear heart behold His dominion. More of both brings more nearness and knowledge.
  55:3/q08/p4 Qur'anic intellect is placed in original human nature and appears in embodied form. — reasons: Al-Qashani describes its emergence in this world through the wondrous human form.
55:3/q09 Does the distinction between teaching the Qur'an and creating humanity imply the Qur'an is uncreated? [* · theology]
  55:3/q09/p1 Yes; the Qur'an is not called created where humanity repeatedly is, and it is God's prior speech and knowledge. — reasons: The Itqan reports an argument that humanity is called created in eighteen places, while the Qur'an is not so called in fifty-four, and this passage distinguishes teaching it from creating humanity. Bursevi attributes the same counts and argument to Abu al-Su'ud. Al-Samarra'i says the Qur'an is God's speech and part of His knowledge and existed before the human being.
55:3/q10 What can praise alongside a report imply about its ruling? [* · law]
  55:3/q10/p1 Such praise can indicate obligation or recommendation. — reasons: The Itqan quotes Ibn Abd al-Salam's list of signs of permissibility—permission, removal of blame, benefit, allowance, and silence about prohibition—and says accompanying praise indicates obligation or recommendation.
