[ELMALILI:2:286#2/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | A second construal says properly directed effort earns benefit, while blind striving after harm costs the person; capacity-calibrated divine duties guide people toward benefit and away from loss.
[ELMALILI:2:286#2/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Without duties people would be idle, while demands beyond capacity would waste effort; either result would mean God did not will His servants’ good, contrary to the mercy of the Compassionate.
[ELMALILI:2:286#2/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | On this reading, both occurrences of “what” may denote good: benefit actually attained and good that was not attained; even blind, fruitless striving wastes effort and may lead to worse harm.
[ELMALILI:2:286#2/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | A third construal treats gains made for oneself and others as truly in one’s own favor, while selfish gain driven by appetite harms the self, since even earning depends on other people; divine duty reconciles self-interest and altruism.
[ELMALILI:2:286#2/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | A fourth construal says one’s own earnings secure rights and benefit, whereas living on another’s earnings is against oneself; duties protect people from dependence and secure rights through charity and fair exchange, while forbidding begging, usury, and aggression.
[ELMALILI:2:286#2/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | prefers | Most exegetes favor the first construal and attach the others to it; Elmalılı calls that first reading the main explanation because it is clear and contains the secrets of divine obligation.
[ELMALILI:2:286#2/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | God’s answer to the believers’ plea calms their anxiety through ease and relief, making Qur’anic recitation a direct dialogue and intimate prayer for the Prophet and his community; Elmalılı compares this style with al-Fatiha. | mentions 1:1
[ELMALILI:2:286#2/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Although the response is separated by a pause, it is phrased as if the believers themselves were voicing it: they would immediately accept and repeat the promised ease because it matches their own hopes.
[ELMALILI:2:286#3/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Forgetfulness and error fall into two kinds: some may excuse the person, while others do not, because a person can be responsible for failing to take reasonable precautions.
[ELMALILI:2:286#3/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Kaffâl | reports | Kaffal says the fifth book claimed as Torah contains further examples of the Jews’ harsh laws; believers prayed for protection from such severity, which God later removed, as 7:157 states. | mentions 7:157
[ELMALILI:2:286#3/r11] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The petition remains necessary because wusʿ, though broader than capacity, is also commonly used for capacity; a burden proportionate to capacity may still be severe, so the prayer asks God to make the apparent sense one of ease.
[ELMALILI:2:286#3/r12] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Actions can be broader than divine legislation: oppressive conditions may come as discipline through tyrants such as Pharaoh acting against God’s law. The verb “load” allows for this, and the surah’s Israelite stories have signaled both sources of hardship.
[ELMALILI:2:286#3/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Someone who notices impurity on clothing but delays removing it and later forgets before praying is not excused, because the initial delay was negligent; someone who never noticed it is excused.
[ELMALILI:2:286#3/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | A hunter who shoots without checking whether a person may be present, then hits someone, is not excused because he failed to take the necessary care.
[ELMALILI:2:286#3/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | A person who fails to learn religious and legal duties, or fails to review them after learning them, is not excused for later forgetting; some causes of forgetfulness are within human capacity to prevent.
[ELMALILI:2:286#3/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Involuntary error does not change the intrinsic harm or unlawfulness of the resulting act, just as swallowed poison remains harmful or an accidental fall into a well remains dangerous; people must avoid such outcomes as far as possible.
[ELMALILI:2:286#3/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Some legal duties remain in error cases such as accidental killing, and forgetfulness or error does not remove compensation for harm to other people; the plea seeks freedom from punishment, not freedom from obligation, including for causes and consequences.
[ELMALILI:2:286#3/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · رسول الله | reports | Elmalılı relates the plea to the Prophetic hadith, “My community’s error and forgetfulness are lifted,” while preserving liability for harm to others.
[ELMALILI:2:286#3/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Isr is a heavy burden or bond that holds the bearer in place, associated with captivity and confinement; it can also refer to a weighty covenant, subjection, the womb, and kinship.
[ELMALILI:2:286#3/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · müfessirîn | reports | Exegetes describe severe requirements imposed on earlier peoples, especially Jews: fifty daily prayers, a quarter of property as tax, cutting away impurity on clothing, exile, swift executions, self-killing for repentance, immediate punishment for rebellion, and prohibition of some lawful foods after an error.
[ELMALILI:2:286#4/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The plea covers unbearable burdens whether legal, which may drive people to revolt, or existential, which become torment and destruction; God could impose such things as a test or punishment, so believers ask for protection in every form.
[ELMALILI:2:286#4/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Elmalılı says the forthcoming Sura of Al-i Imran begins as a response to this prayer for victory. | mentions 3:1

[ELMALILI:2:286#4/r15] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Elmalılı closes by praying that God grant worshippers a great share of the promised response when they understand these supplications and follow what they require.
[ELMALILI:2:286#4/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The assurance of ease is glad tidings for Muhammad’s community, whose faith, obedience, sincerity, sound will, and adherence to truth suit this gift; unbelievers do not escape duty and instead impose desire-driven, unjust demands on one another.
[ELMALILI:2:286#4/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Every community’s law is a divinely imposed burden: believers receive a straight law as mercy, while unbelievers receive a crooked one as the effect of justice and wrath; what counts as justice differs with their spiritual condition, and God turns hearts, so the plea asks for steadfastness in Islam.
[ELMALILI:2:286#4/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | “Pardon us” asks God to erase the effects of sins. [ELMALILI:2:286#4/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | He explains forgiveness as covering and concealing people’s faults, suppressing their blemishes, and not exposing them publicly.
[ELMALILI:2:286#4/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The request for mercy asks God to bestow blessings on the petitioners.
[ELMALILI:2:286#4/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Mawlana means our patron, owner and master, guardian of our affairs, and helper; Elmalılı links it to the declaration that God is the protector of those who believe. | mentions 2:257
[ELMALILI:2:286#4/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The believers ask for material and spiritual victory over unbelievers in defending the truth and raising God’s word.
