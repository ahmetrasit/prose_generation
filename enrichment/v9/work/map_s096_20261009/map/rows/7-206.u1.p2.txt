7:206/q29 May one perform it during an obligatory prayer? [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law] (turns on: Whether an extra sajdah is intrinsically excluded or only avoided to prevent congregational confusion.)
  7:206/q29/p1 No, in any obligatory prayer, whether alone or together and whether recited quietly or aloud. — reasons: Mālik's well-known ruling regards it as an added prostration.
  7:206/q29/p2 Yes where the congregation will not be confused, especially when alone. — reasons: Another rationale in al-Qurṭubī is avoiding confusion. The Abū Rāfiʿ report describes Abū Hurayra prostrating during ʿIshāʾ at al-Inshiqāq, citing the Prophet's practice.
7:206/q30 May it be performed in voluntary congregational prayer when it could confuse worshippers? [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law] (turns on: How much weight to give possible disruption in voluntary group prayer.)
  7:206/q30/p1 Yes, even if confusion is feared; certainly when it is not feared. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī states this as the ruling, including solitary voluntary prayer.
  7:206/q30/p2 No when it may confuse the congregation. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī reports this alternative.
7:206/q31 What should be said during recitation prostration? [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law] (turns on: Which transmitted supplication or glorification is used.)
  7:206/q31/p1 Recite glorification, especially subḥāna rabbī al-aʿlā three times. — reasons: Bursevī prefers this and also gives alternatives asking the Merciful for forgiveness or the Turner of hearts for steadfastness; al-Asrār al-Muḥammadiyya favors the latter. His cited Persian scholar gives site-specific requests at al-Isrāʾ, al-Aʿrāf or al-Sajdah, and al-Najm.
  7:206/q31/p2 Use the Prophet's supplication about one's face prostrating to its Creator, with requests for reward and pardon. — reasons: Bursevī reports creation, hearing, sight, provision, and acceptance; ʿĀʾisha reports repeated use at night, graded authentic by al-Tirmidhī.
  7:206/q31/p3 Ask God to lift burden, record reward, and store the sajdah as treasure. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās transmits this Prophetic supplication in al-Qurṭubī.
  7:206/q31/p4 Ask for useful knowledge and deeds that raise one's rank. — reasons: Ibn ʿUmar's Prophetic report in al-Ālūsī.
7:206/q32 What merit or warning is reported for prostrating? [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · reports] (turns on: Which Prophetic report is being cited.)
  7:206/q32/p1 Satan withdraws weeping when a human prostrates: the human obeys and gains Paradise, while he refused and faces Fire. — reasons: Reported from Abū Hurayra and in multiple commentaries; al-Qurṭubī says its commanded prostration concerns an obligation, while the Prophet's regular practice supports recommendation here.
  7:206/q32/p2 Each prostration raises a degree and removes a sin, or grants both. — reasons: Al-Baghawī reports Thawbān from the Prophet; al-Ālūsī reports ʿĀʾisha.
7:206/q33 What virtue is reported for reciting Sūrat al-Aʿrāf? [* · reports]
  7:206/q33/p1 It brings a screen from Iblīs on Judgment Day and Adam's intercession. — reasons: Al-Kashshāf and al-Bayḍāwī report this from the Prophet.
7:206/q34 What spiritual counsel is drawn from prostration here? [* · inward] (turns on: Whether counsel focuses on chosen human prostration or universal submission.)
  7:206/q34/p1 Prostration is the proper response to divine command and a source of human honor. — reasons: Bursevī's Persian verses shame refusal, the Shaykh al-Islām's maxim censures a head absent from prostration, and another couplet couples generosity with nobility and prostration with honor.
  7:206/q34/p2 All beings already submit to God's power before and during existence. — reasons: Bursevī says pre-eternal nonexistence bowed to God's power to create; existing beings surrender to His decrees of annihilation, creation, and preservation.
7:206/q35 What theological limits are set on inward readings? [* · theology]
  7:206/q35/p1 God remains transcendent; talk of annihilation cannot imply indwelling, union, fusion, or identity of God and servant. — reasons: Bursevī explicitly rejects these claims and says God remains as in eternity.
7:206/q36 Is quiet remembrance preferable? [* · interpretation]
  7:206/q36/p1 Yes, quiet remembrance is best. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī draws the inference and cites Aḥmad's report, 'The best remembrance is silent.' He also condemns contemporary ignorant Sufis' religiously and rationally objectionable practices.
7:206/q37 How does this sūra's ending suit its theme? [* · links]
  7:206/q37/p1 It closes by urging worship through the angels' example. — reasons: Al-Itqān compares distinctive endings of al-Māʾida, al-Anʿām, al-Anfāl, al-Tawba, Yūnus, Hūd, Yūsuf, and al-Raʿd; Kuran Yolu says the close calls for praise by heart and tongue, humble prostration, and preferring God's pleasure to desire.
7:206/q38 How are al-Anfāl and Barāʾa arranged after al-Aʿrāf? [* · sciences] (turns on: Length, thematic and opening links, codex order, and whether arrangement was fixed by revelation.)
  7:206/q38/p1 They form or function as a pair among the seven long sūras. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports early authorities treating the two as one and al-Naḥḥās's report from ʿUthmān that they were called twin sūras in the Prophet's time. He lists divergent seventh-sūra identifications: Yūnus, al-Kahf, Barāʾa, or the pair.
  7:206/q38/p2 Barāʾa's great length warrants placement here. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports its affinity to the preceding long sūras and says shifting it after six shorter ones weakens the connection.
  7:206/q38/p3 Their placement reflects judgment because their order was not fixed by revelation. — reasons: A reported view says placing the pair among the seven long sūras shows the Prophet died before clarifying the order. Al-Ālūsī calls this far-fetched: a Jewish scholar's question and ʿUthmān's reply are not explicit evidence; he also rejects an earlier rationale that conflicts with the question's apparent implication for Barāʾa's missing basmala.
  7:206/q38/p4 Yūnus belongs with the next five sūras rather than immediately after al-Aʿrāf. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī favors their shared stories, alif-lām-rā openings, Book references, Meccan origin, comparable length except al-Ḥijr, and prophet names; he notes al-Raʿd's angelic name and qualified opening-pattern parallels. An alternative would move Yūnus and Hūd as in Ubayy's codex.
  7:206/q38/p5 Other codex ordering can follow descending sūra length and local thematic links. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī says Ibn Masʿūd placed al-Anfāl after al-Nūr, whose promise of inheriting land is fulfilled by al-Anfāl's recall of earlier weakness (24:55, 8:26). He says he has an additional explanation for the link to the previous sūra.
7:206/q39 What background reports about the sūra are appended here? [* · sciences] (turns on: These are separate appended reports on historical setting, commentary completion, and future communal trouble.)
  7:206/q39/p1 Al-Aʿrāf is classed as late Meccan and reportedly followed Ṣād and preceded al-Anʿām. — reasons: Study Quran's introduction also summarizes its warning about rejecting signs and messengers through earlier prophetic stories and their consequences.
  7:206/q39/p2 Bursevī completed the commentary in early Ṣafar 1101 AH before proceeding to al-Anfāl. — reasons: His colophon records the first ten days of the month.
  7:206/q39/p3 Gabriel warned the Prophet of later discord, unjust rulers, and misguided reciters, with restraint and patience as protection. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmits ʿUmar's report about the Prophet.
7:206/q40 How does Ibn Kathīr urge believers to emulate the angels? [* · interpretation]
  7:206/q40/p1 Imitate their abundant, constant worship and orderly prayer rows. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr says their unceasing glorification and prostration invite devotion; he cites a hadith urging Muslims to fill first rows and stand close together as angels do.
7:206/q41 Why do counts of Qurʾanic prostration sites differ? [* · sciences]
  7:206/q41/p1 Reports and practice vary, as does interpretation of prostration commands. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī says some read commands as recitation prostration and others as required prayer prostration.
7:206/q42 Why do rulings on permitted times differ? [* · law]
  7:206/q42/p1 Scholars weigh the recitation sajdah against general bans on prayer after dawn and ʿAṣr and differ over those bans' rationale. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī's explanation.
