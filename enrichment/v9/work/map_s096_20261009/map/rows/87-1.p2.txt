[IBNATIYYA:87:1/r1] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | holds | The sūra is Meccan according to most scholars; al-Naqqash's report that al-Dahhak called it Medinan is weak, apparently prompted by its mention of Eid prayer.
[IBNATIYYA:87:1/r10] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · قوم | reports | Some interpret the command as requiring one to mention the Lord's name only while reverent.
[IBNATIYYA:87:1/r11] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas takes the phrase to mean pray in the name of your Most High Lord, as in “begin in God's name,” with a preposition omitted.
[IBNATIYYA:87:1/r2] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | holds | Here sabbiḥ means to sanctify and declare God above imperfections, changing conditions, and the polytheists' claims.
[IBNATIYYA:87:1/r3] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | holds | In eloquent Arabic ism can mean the named referent or the naming itself; the hadith about God's ninety-nine names illustrates the latter usage.
[IBNATIYYA:87:1/r4] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | holds | If ism means the named referent, it is an added connective, so the command simply means: glorify your Lord.
[IBNATIYYA:87:1/r5] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | holds | Alternatively, the command sanctifies God's name itself by forbidding its use as a title for an idol or false deity.
[IBNATIYYA:87:1/r6] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | holds | Al-aʿlā can grammatically modify either ism (name) or rabb (Lord).
[IBNATIYYA:87:1/r7] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · ابن عمر وعلي | reports | Ibn Umar and Ali read the opening as “Glory be to my Lord, the Most High”; it is also reported in Ubayy's codex and from Abu Musa, Ibn al-Zubayr, and Malik ibn Dinar.
[IBNATIYYA:87:1/r8] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas reports that the Prophet said “Glory be to my Lord, the Most High” when reciting this verse; Ibn Masud, Ibn Amir, and Ibn al-Zubayr did likewise.
[IBNATIYYA:87:1/r9] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · عقبة بن عامر الجهني | reports | The Prophet instructed the Companions to place this verse in prostration; the report is transmitted from Uqbah ibn Amir.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p468#2/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | Sabbiḥ means glorify and sanctify God, declaring Him free of defects, other gods, and the claims of polytheists.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p468#2/r2] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | The word ism can refer to its bearer, making it virtually redundant here, or to the act and form of naming itself.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p468#2/r3] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | The verse can mean glorify the Lord Himself, or protect His name from being applied to an idol; al-Aʿla may modify either the name or the Lord.

[TABRISI:87:7-9/r11] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Al-aʿlā may grammatically modify Lord in the genitive or name in the accusative.
[TABRISI:87:7-9/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Al-aʿlā is like al-akbar: supreme in power and authority, without implying that God occupies a place.
[TABRISI:87:7/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · الباقر | reports | Al-Bāqir advises the reciter to say “Glory be to my Lord, the Most High,” even silently during prayer.
[TABRISI:87:7/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Al-aʿlā means the all-capable one, with no one more capable, who overpowers everyone.
[TABRISI:87:7/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Another interpretation makes al-aʿlā an adjective of the name: glorify God by His highest name, since all His beautiful names are highest.
[TABRISI:87:7/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās also glosses the command as praying in the name of the Most High Lord.

[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p87/r6] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | reports | Two explanations are offered for “the name of your Lord”: the word “name” may be an added link with no independent force, or it may be essential.
[RAZI:87:1/r1] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | Rāzī gives two readings of the command: sanctify God’s name, or treat “name” as an added expression and sanctify God Himself.
[RAZI:87:1/r10] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | A second major reading, favored by a group of careful scholars, treats “name” as an added expression: the intended command is to glorify the Lord.
[RAZI:87:1/r11] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Arabic convention mentions the name or remembrance of an exalted person instead of naming that person directly.
[RAZI:87:1/r12] RAZI, d. 606 · لبيد | reports | Labīd’s phrase “the name of Peace be upon you” uses “name of Peace” to mean Peace itself.
[RAZI:87:1/r13] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | One meaning of glorifying God is avoiding conduct toward unbelievers that provokes them to insult God. | mentions 6:108
[RAZI:87:1/r14] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Glorifying God in His essence means believing He is neither a substance nor an accident.
[RAZI:87:1/r15] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Glorifying God in His attributes means believing they are neither originated, finite, nor deficient.
[RAZI:87:1/r16] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | In His acts, glorification means affirming God’s absolute ownership, which leaves no one entitled to object to His decisions.
[RAZI:87:1/r17] RAZI, d. 606 · المعتزلة | reports | The Muʿtazilites define glorifying God’s acts as affirming that everything He does is good and right, and that He neither does nor approves evil.
[RAZI:87:1/r18] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Rāzī’s position is that God should be named only by names established through revelation.
[RAZI:87:1/r19] RAZI, d. 606 · المعتزلة | reports | The Muʿtazilites permit any name that suggests no deficiency, whether or not revelation authorized it.
[RAZI:87:1/r2] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | On the first reading, the command may forbid applying God’s name to others, as polytheists named an idol al-Lāt and Musaylima called himself the Merciful of Yamāma.
[RAZI:87:1/r20] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Rāzī says God imposes duties for no benefit to Himself, purely by ownership; the Muʿtazilite view is that duties serve people’s interests.
[RAZI:87:1/r21] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | Some have used this verse to argue that a name is identical with the named entity.
[RAZI:87:1/r22] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Rāzī says the dispute is poorly framed: if “name” means the word and “named” the entity they differ; if both mean the entity, their identity is tautological.
[RAZI:87:1/r23] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | He suggests the confusion may come from “name” itself being a noun denoting a timeless meaning, leading later scholars to generalize an earlier formulation.
[RAZI:87:1/r24] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | An argument for identity says people do not say “Glory be to the name of God”; therefore this command means glorify the Lord, who is Himself called by a name.
