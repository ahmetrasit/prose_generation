[IBNABIHATIM:v1p27/r3] IBNABIHATIM, d. 327 · قتادة | reports | Qatada glosses the worlds as whatever God described among His creation.

[NAHHAS:v1p169/r1] NAHHAS, d. 338 · البصريون | reports | The Basrans parse al-ḥamdu as a nominative subject by inception.
[NAHHAS:v1p169/r10] NAHHAS, d. 338 · author | holds | The two forms are known dialects and transmitted readings, each with a grammatical rationale.
[NAHHAS:v1p169/r11] NAHHAS, d. 338 · الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan is reported to read al-ḥamdu with the form preserved by Ismāʿīl ibn ʿAyyāsh's transmission.

[NAHHAS:v1p169/r13] NAHHAS, d. 338 · author | holds | Tamīm uses the kasra; the common word favors it because ḍamma is heavy before a following kasra, so vowels are harmonized for ease.
[NAHHAS:v1p169/r14] NAHHAS, d. 338 · سيبويه | reports | Sībawayh treats the lām in lillāh as an added preposition whose underlying vowel is fatḥ; it is kasra with an overt noun to distinguish it from emphatic lām.
[NAHHAS:v1p169/r15] NAHHAS, d. 338 · author | holds | Rabb is genitive as an adjective of Allah; al-ʿālamīn is genitive by annexation, with its yāʾ marking the case.
[NAHHAS:v1p169/r16] NAHHAS, d. 338 · سيبويه | reports | Sībawayh says the nūn of al-ʿālamīn compensates for the loss of case vowel and tanwīn.
[NAHHAS:v1p169/r17] NAHHAS, d. 338 · أبو العباس | reports | Abū al-ʿAbbās says the nūn substitutes for tanwīn alone.
[NAHHAS:v1p169/r18] NAHHAS, d. 338 · أبو إسحاق | reports | Abū Isḥāq says the nūn substitutes for the case vowel; its fatḥ distinguishes it from the dual nūn.
[NAHHAS:v1p169/r19] NAHHAS, d. 338 · الكسائي | reports | Al-Kisāʾī allows rabb al-ʿālamīn in the accusative as a circumstantial expression, like rabb and god as praise.
[NAHHAS:v1p169/r2] NAHHAS, d. 338 · الكسائي | reports | Al-Kisa'i makes al-ḥamdu nominative through the pronoun implied in its predicate, treating the lām like a verb.
[NAHHAS:v1p169/r20] NAHHAS, d. 338 · أبو حاتم | reports | Abū Ḥātim explains the accusative as an implied I praise Allah, Lord of the worlds.
[NAHHAS:v1p169/r21] NAHHAS, d. 338 · أبو إسحاق | reports | Abū Isḥāq permits the accusative as an annexed vocative.
[NAHHAS:v1p169/r22] NAHHAS, d. 338 · ابن كيسان | reports | Ibn Kaysān rejects an annexed-vocative parse because it would split the utterance into two clauses, and instead parses the accusative as praise.
[NAHHAS:v1p169/r23] NAHHAS, d. 338 · author | holds | Rabb al-ʿālamīn may also be nominative, with an implied huwa: he is Lord of the worlds.
[NAHHAS:v1p169/r24] NAHHAS, d. 338 · author | holds | The word rabb has extended forms rabbāhu, rabbahu, and rabbathu, developed through b-to-y and then y-to-t changes.
[NAHHAS:v1p169/r3] NAHHAS, d. 338 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ makes al-ḥamdu nominative by the grammatical position of the lām, treating the preposition like a noun that stands independently.
[NAHHAS:v1p169/r4] NAHHAS, d. 338 · author | holds | The grammarians use different labels for prepositions: al-Kisa'i says attributes, al-Farrāʾ loci, and Basrans adverbial containers.
[NAHHAS:v1p169/r5] NAHHAS, d. 338 · ابن عيينة ورؤبة ابن العجاج | reports | Ibn ʿUyayna and Ruʾba ibn al-ʿAjjāj read al-ḥamdu in the accusative as a verbal noun, a usage of Qays and al-Ḥārith ibn Samāma.
[NAHHAS:v1p169/r6] NAHHAS, d. 338 · author | prefers | The nominative is preferable in wording because a definite noun is being reported about, and in meaning because it includes the speaker's praise and everyone else's.
[NAHHAS:v1p169/r7] NAHHAS, d. 338 · author | holds | The accusative reading does not include the speaker's own praise in the same way.
[NAHHAS:v1p169/r8] NAHHAS, d. 338 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ transmits two further vocalized forms of al-ḥamdu lillāh.
[NAHHAS:v1p169/r9] NAHHAS, d. 338 · علي بن سليمان | reports | ʿAlī ibn Sulaymān says neither of al-Farrāʾ's two forms is acceptable to Basran grammarians.
