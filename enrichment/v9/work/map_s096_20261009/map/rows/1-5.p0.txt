[TAB:1:5/r10] TAB, d. 310 · بعض أهل الغفلة | reports | Some inattentive readers took the verse as an instance of fronting and delaying the intended order, like Imruʾ al-Qays's line about enough money and seeking more.
[TAB:1:5/r11] TAB, d. 310 · author | rejects | Al-Tabari rejects the delayed-order analogy: having enough money does not prevent seeking more, whereas worship and divine aid entail one another, so their stated order is sound.
[TAB:1:5/r12] TAB, d. 310 · author | holds | The fronted object pronoun is repeated with each verb because each verb governs its own object; Arabic speech is more eloquent when the object is repeated, though omission is permissible when the pronoun follows the verb.
[TAB:1:5/r13] TAB, d. 310 · بعض من لم يمعن النظر | reports | Some who did not examine closely compared the repeated “You alone” to the repeated “between” in verses by ʿAdī ibn Zayd and al-Aʿshā of Hamdān.
[TAB:1:5/r14] TAB, d. 310 · author | rejects | Al-Tabari rejects the analogy: “between” requires two complements and is incomplete when one is omitted, but “You alone we worship” is already a complete clause, and each verb properly takes the repeated object.
[TAB:1:5/r2] TAB, d. 310 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbas reports Gabriel's gloss that worship means to affirm God's oneness, fear Him, and hope in Him alone.
