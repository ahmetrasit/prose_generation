20:13/q12 How did Moses physically listen at this moment? [فَٱسْتَمِعْ · reports]
  20:13/q12/p1 He stood at or leaned on a stone, hands folded, chin lowered, dressed in wool. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya transmits Abū al-Faḍl al-Jawharī; Qurṭubī cites Ibn ʿAṭiyya's father citing al-Jawharī; Abū Ḥayyān repeats the account. The reports vary slightly between standing against a stone, standing by one and leaning on another, and leaning on a stone.
20:13/q13 What is Moses to listen to? [لِمَا يُوحَىٰٓ · referent] (turns on: Whether the phrase points specifically to the declaration immediately following or to the revelation and instructions more broadly.)
  20:13/q13/p1 God's declaration of exclusive divinity and the command to worship Him in 20:14. — reasons: Muqātil calls it God's declaration of oneness; Ibn al-Jawzī quotes 'I am God; there is no god but Me, so worship Me'; Abū Ḥayyān likewise identifies God's declaration of exclusive divinity.
  20:13/q13/p2 The revelation now being spoken to Moses, including its commands and prohibitions. — reasons: Yaḥyā, al-Baghawī and Ibn Kathīr identify the revelation being given now; al-Bursawī specifies commands and prohibitions, while al-Wāḥidī says listening includes acting on both.
20:13/q14 Does mā mean revealed content or the act of revelation? [لِمَا يُوحَىٰٓ · grammar] (turns on: Whether mā is a relative pronoun or a verbal-noun particle.)
  20:13/q14/p1 A relative pronoun: that which is revealed. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī offer this as one sense; Abū Ḥayyān favors it as the natural meaning; al-Samīn identifies the relative-pronoun parsing.
  20:13/q14/p2 A verbal noun: revelation itself. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī offer revelation itself as the alternative; al-Samīn calls this the verbal-noun parsing.
20:13/q15 Which verb governs the lām phrase 'to what is revealed'? [ٱخْتَرْتُكَ فَٱسْتَمِعْ لِمَا يُوحَىٰٓ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the phrase is governed by the command to listen or by the verb of selection; the objection to technical dual government is the missing repeated pronoun.)
  20:13/q15/p1 It attaches to 'listen'; the lām can function as an added preposition before its object. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Samīn offer this attachment; al-Samīn prefers it as apparent, as does Abū Ḥayyān. Al-Bursawī calls the lām added before the object, comparing radifa lakum in 27:72; al-Samīn also cites 27:72 for an extra lām. Abū Ḥayyān argues that assigning technical government to 'chose' violates the two-governor rule without a repeated pronoun; al-Samīn reports his expected form as fastamiʿ lahu limā yūḥā. Al-Ālūsī cites this objection but also reports a reply allowing alternative coordination.
  20:13/q15/p2 It can attach to 'I chose you': Moses was chosen for revelation and must listen to it. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī allow the attachment; al-Samīn reports al-Zamakhsharī and others as describing tanāzuʿ, but clarifies they mean semantic suitability rather than shared technical government. Al-Ṭabrisī reports Abū ʿAlī's special parsing of the plural as wa-li-annā ikhtarnāka, making that prepositional phrase the object of 'listen.' Al-Ālūsī records a reply permitting either attachment by alternative coordination.
20:13/q16 Why is 'is revealed' passive? [يُوحَىٰٓ · rhetoric]
  20:13/q16/p1 Its agent is already known, and the passive suits the verse ending. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān says the known agent is suppressed and the passive fits the verse's final sound.
20:13/q17 How does the call unfold across the following verses? [* · links]
  20:13/q17/p1 The passage first states Moses' prophethood, then develops his mission through the staff question and command to go to Pharaoh. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the next eleven verses as containing both prophecy and mission; the mission begins with the staff question and ends with the order to approach Pharaoh, and 19:51 names Moses both prophet and messenger.
20:13/q18 Can Moses' call be God's revelation if a veil or intermediary is involved? [* · theology]
  20:13/q18/p1 Yes; attention passes beyond the unseen intermediary, so Moses experiences the message as God's. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares passages describing a veil or intermediary in Moses' call, citing 19:52 and 28:30 along with 20:13.
20:13/q19 What adjacent reports concern God's speech to Moses? [* · reports]
  20:13/q19/p1 God spoke to Moses twice, while Muhammad saw his Lord twice and Adam disobeyed twice. — reasons: Muqātil reports this from Kaʿb without grading it.
20:13/q20 What is reported about a partridge's cry? [* · reports]
  20:13/q20/p1 Its cry says, 'The Merciful rose over the Throne.' — reasons: Muqātil reports this from ʿAbd al-Ḥamīd b. Yūsuf without grading it.
20:13/q21 What does the adjacent divine istiwāʾ mean? [* · theology] (turns on: Whether istiwāʾ denotes power or a distinct act or attribute.)
  20:13/q21/p1 It means power. — reasons: Al-Jishumī prefers Abū ʿAlī's explanation as sounder and calls the Kullābī account unintelligible.
  20:13/q21/p2 It is an act or attribute called istiwāʾ. — reasons: Al-Jishumī attributes this account to the Kullābīs and argues against its intelligibility.
20:13/q22 How is the nearby command to establish prayer for remembrance understood? [* · interpretation] (turns on: Whether remembrance is within prayer, its entry, or recall of a forgotten prayer.)
  20:13/q22/p1 Remember God during prayer. — reasons: Al-Māwardī reports Mujāhid's explanation.
  20:13/q22/p2 Begin prayer by remembering God, since prayer is entered only through His remembrance. — reasons: Al-Māwardī reports this as another interpretation.
  20:13/q22/p3 Pray when one remembers a missed prayer. — reasons: Al-Māwardī reports Ibrāhīm's interpretation and Saʿīd b. al-Musayyib's Prophetic report that whoever forgets a prayer should perform it on remembering.
