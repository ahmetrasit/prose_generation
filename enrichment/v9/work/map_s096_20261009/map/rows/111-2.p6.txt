IR:111:1/r10] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | Exegetes say what Abu Lahab earned means his children, wealth, status, and reputation; none could save him from a bad worldly end.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:111:1/r11] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | Shawkani also gives the verse as a question: what benefit did his wealth bring him, and what did he earn?
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:111:1/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | In year two of the Hijra Abu Lahab contracted smallpox and could not attend Badr, but sent a replacement and financially supported the polytheists; he reportedly died of grief seven days after their defeat.
[STUDYQURAN:111:2/r1] STUDYQURAN · many | reports | Many interpret what Abū Lahab earned or committed as referring to his children.
[STUDYQURAN:111:2/r2] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | The verse answers Abū Lahab's boast that, if his nephew were right, he would ransom himself with wealth and children.
[STUDYQURAN:111:intro/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Al-Masad is considered one of the earliest Makkan surahs; its name comes from the final word, said to denote a Hell punishment, and it is also called al-Lahab, “The Flame.”
[STUDYQURAN:111:intro/r2] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Abū Lahab's nickname means “Father of Flame” and refers to his bright red complexion.
[STUDYQURAN:111:intro/r3] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | After the Prophet warned Quraysh from a hill, Abū Lahab cursed him for summoning them; God then revealed the five verses.
[STUDYQURAN:111:intro/r4] STUDYQURAN · many | reports | Many say the surah followed immediately after 26:214; its five short verses describe the Hell punishment of Abū Lahab and his wife. | mentions 26:214
