[ALUSI-FULL:v29p23#2/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · معاوية بن قرة | reports | A report raised to the Prophet says ن is a tablet of light and the Pen a pen of light, writing what will occur until Judgment Day.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p23#2/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · جعفر الصادق | reports | Ja‘far al-Sadiq identifies ن as a river among the rivers of Paradise.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p23#2/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · البحر | reports | Al-Bahr says none of the proposed meanings of ن is sound except that it is a letter name; al-Alusi questions whether this accounts for al-Hakim’s authentication and the weight of al-Diya’s collection, while noting the many conflicting reports from Ibn Abbas.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p23#2/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | rejects | Al-Alusi finds the fish or river readings syntactically awkward and the inkpot reading poorly attested; the Qamus alone does not establish it, Ibn Atiyya’s verse is unverified, and the proposed fish-to-ink metaphor is strained.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p23#2/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | rejects | Al-Alusi dismisses the storytellers’ reports about this passage as unreliable and says they should not be heeded.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p23#2/r8] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The Pen deserves the oath if it is the first-created Tablet Pen or the angels’ pens; ordinary human pens also merit honor for their many benefits, especially recording God’s books.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p23/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | At pause ن is read with sukūn; most readers keep it still and assimilate it into wāw, with some nasalizing and others not, while other readers use kasra or fatḥa.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p23/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | Ibn Abbas, Ibn Abi Ishaq, and Isa (in one transmission) read fatḥa; it may mark two meeting sukūns, or an elided oath particle in a genitive construction, or an elided imperative such as اذكر with نصب rather than fatḥa.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p23/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | If ن is the surah’s name, its case may be explained as accusative after an implied verb or nominative as the predicate of an omitted subject; it is indeclinable as a feminine proper name.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p23/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · السلف | reports | The early authorities regard ن as one of the ambiguous verses; many later scholars instead take it to be a letter name, supported by its written form and lack of ordinary inflection.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p23/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · ابن عباس | reports | A report authenticated by al-Diya and al-Hakim says God created the fish called اليهموت, spread the earth on it, and steadied the trembling earth with mountains; Ibn Abbas then recited the verse. Mujahid also reports the fish interpretation.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p23/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · ابن عباس والحسن وقتادة والضحاك | reports | Ibn Abbas, al-Hasan, Qatada, and al-Dahhak also say ن means an inkpot.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p23/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari denies that ن meaning an inkpot is established in recognized Arabic; Ibn Atiyya allows a dialectal or Arabized foreign usage and cites a disputed poem.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p24/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | The pronoun may refer to the singular Tablet Pen, pluralized honorifically, or to writing instruments generally, plural because they are many; in the latter case writing is attributed to the tool metonymically and it stands for its rational users.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p24/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | Alternatively, the pronoun refers to the scribes or recorders implied by the Pen, or to the Pen’s owners by metonymy or an omitted possessive construction.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p24/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | rejects | Taking ما to mean من is an awkward forced reading; it is more naturally relative, meaning what they write, or a verbal noun, meaning their writing.

[ELMALILI:68:1-6#2/r15] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | He derives Yehmūt from words for madness, raging attackers such as a flood or camel, and the boundless desert or severe famine; it can then denote volcano or space and the earth’s imprisoned, compressed, eruptive core.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#2/r16] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The names describe different aspects: Yehmūt’s severity and terror, Hūt’s convulsion, and Nūn’s groan or circular rotation around the center.
[ELMALILI:68:1-6#2/r17] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · مفسرون | reports | Some exegetes, relying on the reports, take Nūn as an oath by the fish, meaning Yehmūt.
