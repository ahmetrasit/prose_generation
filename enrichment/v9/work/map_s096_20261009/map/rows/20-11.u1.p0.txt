## THE CURRENT MAP
20:11/q01 Does faʾ require the call to follow Moses' arrival without an interval? [فَلَمَّآ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the sequential faʾ permits the short interval related in reports.)
  20:11/q01/p1 A brief interval may have passed before the call. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says the sequential faʾ does not preclude a short interval. Al-Ālūsī says the interval in the transmitted accounts does not defeat them.
  20:11/q01/p2 The verse's apparent immediate sequence excludes reports of an interval. — reasons: Some Muʿtazilites appeal to the apparent sequence of arrival and call.
20:11/q02 Why is Moses' arrival expressed with atā here? [أَتَىٰهَا · rhetoric]
  20:11/q02/p1 It fits the sūra's preference for atyan over majiʾ. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī counts more than fifteen uses of atyan and four of majiʾ in Ṭā Hā, against eight of majiʾ and thirteen of atyan in al-Naml; compare 27:8.
20:11/q03 What does the pronoun in atāhā refer to? [أَتَىٰهَا · referent] (turns on: Whether the feminine pronoun points back to the seen fire or to the tree at the call.)
  20:11/q03/p1 The fire Moses had seen or thought he saw. — reasons: Yaḥyā says it was what Moses thought was fire; al-Māwardī calls it fire that was light. Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī explicitly identify the feminine pronoun with fire. Study Quran cites 20:10 and reports a version in which the fire consumed the tree.
  20:11/q03/p2 The tree from which God called Moses. — reasons: Study Quran reports this interpretation from JJ.
20:11/q04 Where did Moses arrive? [أَتَىٰهَا · reports]
  20:11/q04/p1 At the valley named Tuwa, the purified valley. — reasons: Muqātil gives Tuwa as the valley's name and explains it as 'purified.'
20:11/q05 What kind of tree held the apparent fire? [أَتَىٰهَا · reports] (turns on: The reports identify the tree by different species; al-Būrsawī says the listed trees do not contain fire like other trees.)
  20:11/q05/p1 A jujube tree. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās reports a jujube in al-Wāḥidī and al-Ālūsī; al-Ṭabrisī reports him likewise. Al-Nasafī offers jujube or osage; al-Būrsawī lists jujube among several identifications.
  20:11/q05/p2 An ʿawsaj or osage tree. — reasons: Al-Nasafī allows osage; al-Būrsawī lists ʿawsaja; al-Ālūsī reports awsj.
  20:11/q05/p3 A samura tree. — reasons: Al-Būrsawī lists green samura; al-Ālūsī transmits it from ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd.
  20:11/q05/p4 An al-ʿalīq or blackberry-like thorn tree. — reasons: Al-Būrsawī's list describes a blackberry-like thorn tree; al-Ālūsī's Wahb report calls the great green tree al-ʿalīq. These descriptions vary.
20:11/q06 What did the fire and tree look like together? [أَتَىٰهَا · reports] (turns on: Whether the fire preserved the green tree or consumed it.)
  20:11/q06/p1 Brilliant white fire or light appeared in a green tree without consuming it or being dimmed by its moisture. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī, and al-Būrsawī describes the bright flame and living green tree; al-Rāzī says the glow ran bottom to top. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī describe white flame in green growth. Wahb in al-Ālūsī says the great flame grew without burning the tree.
  20:11/q06/p2 The fire consumed the tree. — reasons: Study Quran reports this interpretation from IK, R, Ṭ, Bg, and R, with reference to 20:10.
20:11/q07 How did the apparent fire behave as Moses approached? [أَتَىٰهَا · reports]
  20:11/q07/p1 It withdrew as he approached and came toward him when he drew back or stopped seeking it. — reasons: Wahb in al-Ṭabarī says Moses feared this motion. Al-Rāzī's Wahb report adds that Moses carried dry sticks, the fire repeatedly lured him, then vanished as a branch and dazzling light shone skyward. Al-Nasafī reports the reciprocal movement. Al-Ālūsī's Wahb report has repeated advance and retreat, a pillar of light reaching the sky, Moses' fear, angelic praise, and calm; it states no grading.
20:11/q08 What else did Moses perceive at the fire? [أَتَىٰهَا · reports]
  20:11/q08/p1 He saw intense light, heard angelic praise, and was awed before being calmed. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Rāzī reports great light and angels glorifying God. Al-Būrsawī adds light too intense for the eyes, Moses covering his eyes, fear and astonishment, and subsequent calm. Wahb in al-Ālūsī also culminates his report in angelic praise and calm.
  20:11/q08/p2 He found no person beside the fire. — reasons: Al-Nasafī and Ibn ʿAbbās as reported by al-Būrsawī say no one was there.
20:11/q09 Was the phenomenon real fire or light? [أَتَىٰهَا · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the visible phenomenon was actual veiling fire or divine light perceived and named as fire.)
  20:11/q09/p1 It was light, called fire because Moses perceived it so. — reasons: Al-Māwardī describes the fire as light. Al-Ālūsī reports Ibn ʿAbbās calling it light from God's light and Abū Ḥayyān preferring light to actual fire; he says al-Māwardī also adopts that view.
  20:11/q09/p2 It was fire itself and one of God's veils. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports Saʿīd ibn Jubayr. He also cites Abū Mūsā's Prophetic report that God's veil is fire and its unveiling would burn creatures reached by His face's radiance; al-Baghawī cites it and al-Khāzin says Muslim recorded it.
20:11/q10 How is Moses' fire classified by eating and drinking? [أَتَىٰهَا · interpretation]
  20:11/q10/p1 It neither eats nor drinks. — reasons: Al-Būrsawī and al-Ālūsī report a fourfold scheme: ordinary fire eats but does not drink, green-tree fire drinks but does not eat, Hellfire does both, and Moses' fire does neither.
20:11/q11 How is Moses' fire classified by light and burning? [أَتَىٰهَا · interpretation]
  20:11/q11/p1 It gives light without burning. — reasons: Al-Būrsawī and al-Ālūsī report a fourfold contrast: ordinary fire has both light and burning, Hellfire burns without light, and Moses' fire shines without burning. Al-Būrsawī says trees have neither; al-Ālūsī specifies tree fire as neither.
20:11/q12 What happened with Moses' sandals when he arrived? [أَتَىٰهَا · reports]
  20:11/q12/p1 He removed sandals made from the hide of an unritually dead donkey. — reasons: Muqātil says God ordered their removal at Tuwa; Qatāda as reported by Yaḥyā says Moses removed them and then approached.
20:11/q13 What does nūdiya mean here? [نُودِىَ · meaning]
  20:11/q13/p1 He was told or addressed. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās uses 'he was told' to explain the variant readings of yā Mūsā annī. Al-Nasafī and Asad give the literal passive sense 'he was called.'
20:11/q14 What is the passive subject of nūdiya? [نُودِىَ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the passive promotes Moses, an understood verbal noun, or the quoted clause; the last is challenged because a clause cannot be the subject.)
  20:11/q14/p1 Its implicit subject is Moses. — reasons: Abū ʿAlī in al-Ṭabrisī allows Moses because he was already mentioned. Al-Samīn prefers this, as does al-Ālūsī as the apparent reading.
  20:11/q14/p2 An implicit verbal noun is the subject: the calling occurred. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports this proposal; al-Samīn calls the equivalent 'the call was called' weak. Abū ʿAlī in al-Ṭabrisī also allows an implicit pronoun rather than the quoted clause.
  20:11/q14/p3 The quoted words yā Mūsā function as the subject. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports that the call is taken to include speech and the quoted wording to be its intended message. Abū ʿAlī in al-Ṭabrisī and al-Samīn object that a clause cannot be a passive subject.
20:11/q15 How is the quoted address attached to nūdiya? [نُودِىَ يَٰمُوسَىٰٓ · grammar] (turns on: Whether 'called' itself introduces speech or an omitted verb of saying is supplied.)
  20:11/q15/p1 Calling itself has the force of saying, so the words are the call's speech. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī attributes this construction to the Kūfans.
  20:11/q15/p2 An unspoken verb 'he was told' introduces the quoted address. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī attributes the omitted saying verb to the Basrans: Moses was called and told 'O Moses.' Al-Naḥḥās glosses nūdiya as 'he was told.'
20:11/q16 What effect does the passive wording of nūdiya have? [نُودِىَ · rhetoric]
  20:11/q16/p1 Concealing the caller makes the summons greater when Moses is named. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī points to the sequence from an unnamed caller to Moses' explicit name.
  20:11/q16/p2 Omitting the agent subtly suggests the sign appeared suddenly to Moses. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī links the passive's omission to the sudden appearance of the sign.
20:11/q17 Who produced the call to Moses? [نُودِىَ يَٰمُوسَىٰٓ · theology] (turns on: Whether God's address was unmediated or made audible through light.)
  20:11/q17/p1 God addressed Moses directly and alone. — reasons: Al-Māwardī reports this view.
  20:11/q17/p2 God made His inseparable light voice the call. — reasons: Al-Māwardī reports that the light spoke to calm Moses enough to bear God's command.
20:11/q18 From where was the call heard? [نُودِىَ يَٰمُوسَىٰٓ · reports]
  20:11/q18/p1 From the tree or its vicinity. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī says Moses heard the tree call; Wahb in al-Ṭabrisī says Moses heard a voice without seeing its place. Al-Qurṭubī specifies the tree's direction and vicinity and compares Sūrat al-Qaṣaṣ. Study Quran reports God called from a tree in the valley.
20:11/q19 How did Moses know the caller was his Lord? [نُودِىَ يَٰمُوسَىٰٓ · theology] (turns on: Whether recognition at the first revelation requires evidentiary inference or is immediate in the experience of revelation.)
  20:11/q19/p1 He recognized God by a miracle or divinely created knowledge. — reasons: Wahb in al-Ṭabrisī says Moses heard but could not locate the voice and knew the caller by the response and a miracle.
