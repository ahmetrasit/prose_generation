[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p18/r2] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | rejects | Ibn Kathir says this permissive position is not clear from Shafi'i's al-Umm and al-Mukhtasar, where Shafi'i presented two possible readings without committing, although his discussion slightly favors permissibility.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p18/r3] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · أبو حنيفة | reports | Ibn al-Sabbagh transmits permissibility from Abu Hanifa through the narration of al-Hasan ibn Ziyad.

[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p18/r5] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · الشافعي، المزني، ابن الصباغ، أبو يوسف، محمد، أحمد بن حنبل | reports | The second view forbids prey killed by impact without a wound: it is one Shafi'i position, chosen by Muzani, favored by Ibn al-Sabbagh's wording, reported from Abu Hanifa by Abu Yusuf and Muhammad, and famous from Ahmad.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p18/r6] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | prefers | Ibn Kathir prefers the prohibition as more consistent with legal principles and the foundations of the law.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p18/r7] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم (حديث رافع بن خديج) | reports | The Prophet says to eat what causes blood to flow when God's name is pronounced, in response to Rafi ibn Khadij's question about slaughtering with reeds; the report is in both Sahih collections.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p18/r8] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | Though prompted by a specific case, the majority of legal theorists apply the hadith's general wording; the Prophet's ruling that every intoxicating drink is forbidden illustrates the rule beyond honey wine alone.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p19/r1] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | Against the objection that Rafi's report concerned slaughtering tools, Ibn Kathir argues that its wording commands eating whatever makes blood flow and names God, so it governs both the tool and the animal slaughtered.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p19/r2] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · المزني | reports | Muzani's method restricts the unrestricted dog report by the arrow report: an arrow that kills by its flat side is not edible, while one that penetrates is; both are hunting means and the prey dies from impact.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p19/r3] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · المزني | reports | Muzani's analogy parallels restricting unqualified emancipation in expiation for ẓihār by the requirement of faith in homicide expiation; the analogy takes precedence over general wording for those who accept the principle.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p19/r4] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | On the analogy, a dog that kills by its weight is unlawful like an arrow that kills by its flat side, because both are hunting tools and the prey dies from blunt force; analogy takes precedence over generality according to the four imams and majority.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p20/r1] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | The lawgiver applies the category of beaten prey during hunting: when a dog strikes with its flat side, the Prophet calls it wakidh and forbids eating; no scholar distinguishes this while exempting prey killed by impact, so that permissive claim would violate consensus. | mentions 5:3
