al-Mughīra, al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil, ʿUmar ibn ʿĀʾidh, two hypocrites, or Abū Sufyān, who struck an orphan asking for food after his weekly sacrifice; Ibn Jurayj gives the last report.

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p553/r10] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The author says a more idiomatic construction would coordinate a following relative clause or use a verb directly, rather than insert the demonstrative in al-Zamakhsharī's proposed parse.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p553/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī offers an alternative syntax joining fa-dhālika to the one who denies judgment, omitting the answer to araʾayta, and making the woe to praying hypocrites a description standing for their pronoun; generic singular carries a plural sense.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p553/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | The author calls al-Zamakhsharī's accusative parsing of fa-dhālika strange and unnatural: a nominative subject is immediate, while an accusative would require repeating the verb and pointing needlessly to the person.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p554/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | He rejects the proposed parsing that makes the demonstrative refer to a separate entity: fa-dhālika points back to the one who denies, so they are not two entities.

[SAMIN-DURR:107:2#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · زيد بن علي | reports | Zayd ibn Ali reads wa-la yuḥāḍḍ, from muḥāḍḍa, meaning they do not urge one another; the root was discussed in al-Fajr. | mentions 89:18
[SAMIN-DURR:107:2/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The fa of fadhālika can answer an implied condition such as if you reflect or seek to know the person.
[SAMIN-DURR:107:2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Alternatively, it coordinates fadhālika with alladhi yukadhdhib; the answer to arayta is omitted, and the question asks whether denial, harming orphans, and neglecting the poor is commendable.
[SAMIN-DURR:107:2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Under the first parse the demonstrative is nominative, either as subject with the relative as predicate or as the predicate of an omitted subject with the relative as modifier; under the second it is accusative by coordination.
[SAMIN-DURR:107:2/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · the Shaykh | rejects | The Shaykh rejects accusative coordination as syntactically strange: the natural reading is a new nominative subject, while accusative requires repeating the verb and demonstrative awkwardly points to the prior object.
[SAMIN-DURR:107:2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · the Shaykh | rejects | He says fadhālika refers to the single denier, so it cannot coordinate one entity with another; the omitted answer to arayta is its second object, not an answer.
[SAMIN-DURR:107:2/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · the Shaykh | rejects | He objects that hamza does not enter upon naʿma or biʾsa because they are performative rather than declarative.
[SAMIN-DURR:107:2/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The author answers that an interrogative context makes explaining the object of inquiry natural, unlike the Shaykh's example without a question.
[SAMIN-DURR:107:2/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The demonstrative can point forward to the person described after it rather than back to the denier, as in an imperative followed by a description.
[SAMIN-DURR:107:2/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The author replies that this is a response to the preceding interrogative, as grammarians call it, rather than an ordinary declarative answer.
