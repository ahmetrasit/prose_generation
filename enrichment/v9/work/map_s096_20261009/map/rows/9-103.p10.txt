[ALUSI-FULL:v11p14/r10] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | A reading has the form tutahhiruhum, from the causative verb athhara, meaning the same as tahhara, 'to purify.'
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p14/r11] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | With the yā retained, 'and you purify them' may predicate an omitted subject, serve as a circumstantial clause attached to the command or its response, or begin a new sentence.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p14/r12] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | As a new sentence, it means the Prophet increases their good deeds or wealth through the charity, or intensifies their purification; interpreting it as raising them from hypocrites' ranks to sincere believers' ranks would imply they were hypocrites, contrary to the sounder view, and is tied here to the jussive reading.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p14/r13] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الزمخشري | reports | The apparent argument in al-Kashshaf makes the ta address the Prophet only, because the text says 'by it'; saying the charity itself purifies them is distant from Quranic eloquence.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p14/r14] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · مسلمة بن محارب | reports | Muslama ibn Muharib reads the verb without its yā: tazakkahum.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p14/r15] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The command means pray and seek forgiveness for them; the preposition 'upon' is used because the verb carries a sense of kindness, and the ordinary supplication sense is more apparent than funeral prayer despite a report from Ibn Abbas.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p14/r16] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الشافعي | reports | Al-Shafi'i recommends the imam say to the giver: May God reward you for what you gave, make it purification for you, and bless what remains with you.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p14/r17] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | prefers | Some require the imam's prayer for a donor; others require it for obligatory alms but recommend it for voluntary charity, or require it of the imam and recommend it of the poor recipient. Al-Alusi's preferred ruling is recommendation in every case.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p14/r18] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | prefers | Sakan is tranquility or what the soul rests in; one reading treats the Prophet's prayer itself as tranquility by hyperbole, another compares resorting to his prayer with resorting to a home, and al-Alusi prefers the first. His supplication soothes their hearts and assures them God has accepted them.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p14/r19] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · غير واحد من السبعة | reports | Several of the seven canonical readers use the plural 'your prayers' to match the multiple people being prayed for.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p14/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | Some narrations say the Prophet then took one third of their wealth.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p14/r20] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | God hears their confession, repentance, and supplication, and knows their regret, sorrow, and sincerity; alternatively He hears the Prophet's prayer and knows what wisdom requires.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p14/r21] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The closing names either reinforce the rationale for the prayer or form a concluding confirmation of the preceding two verses and all they contain.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p14/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | prefers | Because obligatory zakat had already been prescribed, al-Alusi takes this charity as expiation for their sins, in keeping with its stated purifying effect.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p14/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الجبائي | reports | Al-Jubba'i says the charity is zakat; taking it here prevents people from thinking these men were like certain hypocrites whose charity was rejected.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p14/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | On the expiation reading, 'their wealth' refers to these men who confessed; on the zakat reading, it may refer to owners of wealth generally.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p14/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The plural 'wealth' indicates that the collection is taken from all categories of property.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p14/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The prepositional phrase may attach to 'take' or be an omitted circumstantial modifier of 'charity.'
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p14/r8] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The ta in 'you purify them' addresses the Prophet; the jussive reading makes the clause a result of the command, while the nominative reading allows a circumstantial clause, an adjective for charity with an implied 'by it,' or an independent sentence, as Abu al-Baqa says.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p14/r9] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | On the adjective construction, the ta may instead be feminine third person referring to the charity, with no need to supply 'by it' because the following phrase indicates it.
[ELMALILI:9:101-106#2/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The quoted legal discussion says collecting alms belongs to the imam; a payer's direct payment to the poor does not suffice because the imam's right to collect remains.
[ELMALILI:9:101-106#2/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · عمر بن عبد العزيز | reports | Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz instructed collectors to take half a dinar per twenty from Muslim trade goods and one dinar per twenty from dhimmi goods, only after a year, citing a report traced to someone who heard the rule from the Prophet.
[ELMALILI:9:101-106#2/r11] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · عمر بن الخطاب | reports | Umar ibn al-Khattab had earlier instructed collectors to take a quarter-tithe from Muslims, a half-tithe from dhimmis, and a full tithe from those at war with the Muslims.
[ELMALILI:9:101-106#2/r12] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | For Muslims, collection is obligatory zakat and requires the conditions of a completed year, nisab, and valid ownership; if zakat is not due, nothing is taken.
[ELMALILI:9:101-106#2/r13] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Umar's practice followed the Prophet's collection of livestock, fruits, and crops; because trade goods circulated openly in Muslim lands and no Companion objected, the rule became consensus, which Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz also followed with the cited hadith.
[ELMALILI:9:101-106#2/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | The Prophet sent collectors for livestock and ordered them to take alms at the animals' locations and watering places.
[ELMALILI:9:101-106#2/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The Prophet's condition to the Thaqif delegation that they would not be assembled or tithed meant they need not bring livestock to collectors; collectors would visit watering and likely grazing places to collect.
[ELMALILI:9:101-106#2/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The same collection procedure applies to zakat on crops and fruit.
[ELMALILI:9:101-106#2/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Zakat on hidden assets was brought to the Prophet and the first caliphs, Abu Bakr, Umar, and Uthman.
[ELMALILI:9:101-106#2/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · عثمان | reports | Uthman announced in Ramadan that debt should be paid first and the remaining wealth purified with zakat; he then left payment to the poor to each person's choice, ending the imam's collection right through a binding act of a just imam.
[ELMALILI:9:101-106#2/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Uthman's policy was a valid agreement made by a just imam, binding on the community and therefore ending the imam's collection right in that case.
[ELMALILI:9:101-106#2/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Collectors were not sent for hidden wealth because it was concealed in protected homes and shops, agents could not enter them, and owners could not be compelled to produce all their assets; owners brought this zakat and their declarations were accepted.
[ELMALILI:9:101-106#2/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | When hidden assets circulated between towns as trade and became visible, collectors were appointed to take their zakat, as with livestock.
[ELMALILI:9:101-106/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Taking مِن as partitive, Elmalılı reports that the Prophet took one third of their property, not all of it.
[ELMALILI:9:101-106/r11] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Hasen-i Basrî | reports | Hasan al-Basri says this is a special, voluntary expiation for sins, not obligatory zakat or a financial penalty; it depends on willingness and intention.
[ELMALILI:9:101-106/r12] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · ekser-i fukahâ | reports | Most jurists take the command to concern obligatory zakat: the passage addresses the wealthy, love of wealth caused the sin, and willingly paying their due zakat would demonstrate sincere repentance and remove hypocrisy.
[ELMALILI:9:101-106/r13] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | prefers | Elmalılı favors the obligatory-zakat reading while allowing extra voluntary gifts; on the alternative reading, the command requires the Prophet to take and accept expiatory alms, and thus more clearly applies to obligatory zakat.
[ELMALILI:9:101-106/r14] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | The Prophet's statement that he was not ordered to take their property may concern the refusal to accept alms from some, such as Hatib ibn Saʿlaba.
[ELMALILI:9:101-106/r15] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The Prophet could not take charity for himself: obligatory alms were forbidden to the Prophet's family, and both obligatory and voluntary charity were forbidden to him.
[ELMALILI:9:101-106/r16] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The Prophet's collection under the cited hadith was in his capacity as imam, who must collect and distribute alms; the verse establishes this duty and connects to the collectors of 9:60. | mentions 9:60
[ELMALILI:9:101-106/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Once released, they offered their property for alms and purification; the Prophet said he had not been ordered to take anything from them, after which this verse was revealed.
