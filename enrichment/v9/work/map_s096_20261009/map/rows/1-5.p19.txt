[ALUSI:1:5/r73] ALUSI, d. 1270 · قيل | reports | One view explains the repeated object as explicitly identifying God as the one from whom help is requested; without it, the helper could remain unspecified.
[ALUSI:1:5/r74] ALUSI, d. 1270 · قيل | reports | Another view says repetition prevents the mistaken implication that one draws near to God only by combining worship with asking for aid.
[ALUSI:1:5/r75] ALUSI, d. 1270 · قيل | reports | A third view treats the repetition as emphasis, by analogy with saying a house is between Zayd and ʿAmr.
[ALUSI:1:5/r76] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | rejects | Al-Ālūsī rejects the emphasis account: the second object is governed by a separate verb, so it is not merely emphatic repetition.
[ALUSI:1:5/r77] ALUSI, d. 1270 · قيل | reports | Another reported purpose is to teach worshippers to renew mention of God whenever they have a need.
[ALUSI:1:5/r78] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | prefers | Al-Ālūsī prefers the view that repetition marks two different relations: worship relates to God directly, while seeking aid relates through an attribute or means.
[ALUSI:1:5/r79] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | The unexpressed object of seeking aid may be unrestricted, like an omitted object that implies generality; choosing only some needs would require an arbitrary preference.
[ALUSI:1:5/r8] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | reports | The form wayyāka, with hamza changed to wāw, occurs, but al-Ālūsī cannot tell whether it comes from readers or Arab usage.
