79:18/q08 What rule for calling people to religion is drawn from the wording? [هَل لَّكَ · interpretation]
  79:18/q08/p1 A caller should begin with kindness, gentle speech, and restraint. — reasons: Al-Jishumī says this is an obligation. Al-Rāzī links Moses' invitation to 20:44 and the instruction to Muhammad in 3:159, requiring avoidance of harshness. Study Quran says Moses was told to invite Pharaoh peacefully to follow revelation first, as the Prophet invited Quraysh. Al-Bursevī says tact can draw a resistant hearer from obstinacy.
79:18/q09 How does purification lead into guidance and fear in the next verse? [تَزَكَّىٰ · links]
  79:18/q09/p1 Cleansing precedes adornment; knowledge of God through guidance then produces fear. — reasons: Al-Bursevī invokes 35:28 and makes fear the aim of knowing God. Al-Ālūsī similarly says purification comes first as cleansing before adornment. Al-Qāshānī's inward account makes knowledge of the unity of the Essence dissolve selfhood. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports fear from faith motivating obedience and restraining sin.
79:18/q10 How does this address relate to Moses' instruction in Ṭā Hā? [* · links]
  79:18/q10/p1 It enacts and explains the command in 20:44 to speak gently to Pharaoh. — reasons: Al-Rāzī cites 20:44 alongside 3:159. Al-Bursevī says the unthreatening offer spells out what purification and guidance entail. Al-Ālūsī says it draws Pharaoh down from arrogance. Al-Elmalılı says these verses unpack the gentle address and condense Pharaoh's tyranny into his boast. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Moses offers guidance. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ also cites 20:42–44 and its hope Pharaoh may remember or fear. Study Quran compares Muhammad's peaceful invitation to Quraysh.
79:18/q11 What report explains Moses' concern that Pharaoh would refuse? [* · reports]
  79:18/q11/p1 Moses asked why he should go if Pharaoh would not accept; God ordered him on, saying twelve thousand angels seek knowledge of destiny without attaining it. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī attributes the report to Sakhr ibn Juwayriyya; al-Bursevī reports the same episode without that attribution in the note.
79:18/q12 How does the Pharaoh episode serve the sūra's wider argument? [* · links] (turns on: The notes draw either the sūra's resurrection warning through Pharaoh's failure or the proof of divine lordship and fair warning through a messenger.)
  79:18/q12/p1 Pharaoh's failed worldly power supports the sūra's warning about final fate and resurrection. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says the sūra establishes resurrection and gathering after the preceding sūras' fuller exposition; its oaths bring the matter to a climax. He says Pharaoh's reputation and projects failed to save him, citing 28:38 and 40:37, and that God reassured Moses against Pharaoh's power.
  79:18/q12/p2 It warns resurrection-denying polytheists, consoles the Prophet, and establishes God's lordship through Moses' authorized mission. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says Pharaoh's destruction proves Moses' divine authorization, which entails God's lordship over people against the claim of lesser lords. Study Quran notes the wider rule that no community is punished before a messenger brings guidance, citing 28:59, 26:208, 6:131, 10:13, and 15:4.
79:18/q13 What grammatical analogies involving “هل” and neighboring forms appear in the cited discussions? [هَل · grammar] (turns on: The cited grammar chapter offers distinct analogies rather than one analysis of the verse; its explicit disagreement concerns whether من قدام is indefinite or definite and feminine.)
  79:18/q13/p1 Particle-like or ambiguous adverbial forms are restricted in declension and can have fixed vocalization. — reasons: Sībawayh lists أين، متى، كيف، حيث، إذ، إذا، قبل and بعد as ambiguous, non-fully-declinable adverbs, akin to interjections or non-nouns. قبل and بعد cannot freely stand like nouns and resemble particles across times; قبل، بعد and حيث take final ḍamma, though some analogize حيث to أين. He notes لدن's limited jussive behavior, its likeness to قط, and قط/حسب for ‘only’; لد abbreviates لدن, restored before pronouns, and resembles عن. Where two unvowelled consonants meet, the latter gets a vowel, but stays still after a vowel, as with هل، بل، أجل، نعم and جير. He also notes ḍamma following ḍamma in a form like رد يا فتى.
  79:18/q13/p2 Several comparison and place expressions shift between adverbial, nominal, adjectival, and elliptical uses. — reasons: Al-Khalīl says مع takes accusative since معا is independent and pronoun constructions are adverbial. He treats من عل as more declinable; he and Sībawayh contrast أول and حكم with invariant الذي and من. Al-Khalīl reads أول in مذ عام أول as adjective with من عامك omitted, and also as a noun; أول من أمس and بعد غد omit comparisons. Sībawayh says ابدأ به أول omits من كذا, obligatory in عام أول. Al-Khalīl records rare adverbial مذ عام أول. Sībawayh makes أسفل an adverb in زيد أسفل منك, comparing 8:42, and comparative أفعل in جاء من أسفل, comparing 33:10. Al-Khalīl says دون، فوق، تحت، قبل، بعد accept complements and nominal uses; poetry attests من تحت، من عل، من أمامه and من دون. A poetic أولا can be adjective or adverb; al-Jaʿdī exemplifies accusative أماما and خلفا by analogy with يمنة and شأمة.
  79:18/q13/p3 من أمام and related bare place terms are indefinite unless attached to a definite noun. — reasons: Al-Khalīl prefers this on Arab usage; Sībawayh cites ʿAlawiyyīn and Tamīmī forms من قديديمة and من وريئة, comparing صباحا and other time adverbs. Yūnus instead treats من قدام as definite and feminine diptote; Sībawayh says none of his Arab informants used that analysis.
  79:18/q13/p4 Yūnus treats من قدام as definite and feminine, hence diptote. — reasons: Sībawayh reports this to reject it against the Arab usage he consulted.
  79:18/q13/p5 When used as a personal name, these forms change shape like لو، هل، بل، ليت and ذا. — reasons: Sībawayh uses هل among the examples of forms adapted on naming.
  79:18/q13/p6 Poetic necessity can make معكم rhyme like هل. — reasons: Sībawayh cites al-Rāʿī's verse.
  79:18/q13/p7 هل is also analyzed in the summons هلم, whose derivation and dialectal inflection are disputed. — reasons: Al-Itqān says هلم is an invitation. One derivation combines hā with lammamtu ‘I gathered or set right’ after dropping alif; another parses hal umm, ‘will you approach?’, linked to هل لك إلى. Hijaz leaves هلم unchanged in dual/plural, while Tamīm inflects it.
  79:18/q13/p8 General rhetorical and syntactic examples show expressions used beyond their literal function. — reasons: Al-Itqān lists prepositions, commands, prohibitions, questions, wishes, hopes and calls as rhetorical forms. Al-Burhān records two other disputed analyses: for accompaniment waw, some make waw govern its object, while the preferred account lets the preceding verb govern through waw; for a verb joined to a verbal noun, Kūfans allow it, while the preferred account uses conjunction and implicit أن.
