[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v2p292/r7] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن عباس، عطاء | reports | One legal view makes not eating a condition for training every hunting animal, so a catch it eats cannot be eaten by people.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v2p292/r8] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · سعد بن أبي وقاص، ابن عمر، أبو هريرة، سلمان الفارسي | reports | A second legal view says abstaining from the catch is not a condition for any kind of trained animal; its catch is lawful even if it eats.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v2p292/r9] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · الشعبي، النخعي، السدي | prefers | The preferred view requires abstention from land predators, but not birds: birds are trained to eat and their catches are permitted, whereas land predators are trained not to eat.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v2p294/r1] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash treats min in the command to eat from what the animals catch as an extra particle, as in the cited expression in 24:43. | mentions 24:43

[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v2p294/r3] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | reports | The other interpretation refers the pronoun to eating, making the invocation recommended rather than a condition.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v2p294/r4] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · سعيد بن جبير | reports | The command to fear God means not deeming lawful game over which God's name was not mentioned.

[RAZI-FULL:v11p113#2/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The verse requires hunting animals to be trained; the Prophet told ʿAdī ibn Ḥātim to eat from prey caught by a trained dog after pronouncing God's name.
[RAZI-FULL:v11p113#2/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · الشافعي | reports | Al-Shāfiʿī says a dog is trained when it follows on release, holds prey without eating, answers its handler, and does not flee; repeated performance suffices, with custom determining the threshold and prevailing Abū Ḥanīfa opinion agreeing.
[RAZI-FULL:v11p113#2/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · الحسن البصري | reports | Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī says a hunting animal becomes trained after one successful instance.
[RAZI-FULL:v11p113#2/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · أبو حنيفة | reports | In another report from Abū Ḥanīfa, an animal is trained after two repetitions; Aḥmad also holds this view.
[RAZI-FULL:v11p113#2/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · أبو يوسف ومحمد | reports | Abū Yūsuf and Muḥammad require three repetitions for training.
[RAZI-FULL:v11p113#2/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · صاحب الكشاف | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī reports a lightened reading of mukallibīn and notes that the forms afʿala and faʿʿala often share a meaning.

[RAZI-FULL:v11p113/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | One syntactic analysis supplies an omitted word: lawful foods and the catch of trained hunting animals; the following command to eat what they catch signals the omission.

[RAZI-FULL:v11p113/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | An alternative analysis takes the hunting-animal clause as a new sentence whose predicate is the command to eat what the animals catch, requiring no ellipsis.
