  36:14/q26/p1 On the apostles reading, the disciples were not prophets during or after Jesus's life. — reasons: Bursevi infers this from their status as Jesus's envoys and cites the Prophet's saying, 'There is no prophet between me and him,' taking 'him' as Jesus.
  36:14/q26/p2 The saying may exclude only a prophet bringing an independent law, leaving a prophet who confirms prior law possible. — reasons: Bursevi explicitly allows this narrower sense of the cited saying.
36:14/q27 How does the verse connect to its surrounding account and to Muhammad's audience? [* · links] (turns on: Whether the passage is read chiefly as a historical warning, a typological confirmation of Muhammad, or a discourse sequence.)
  36:14/q27/p1 The town's rejection is a warning and consolation for Muhammad and his Meccan opponents. — reasons: Al-Alusi says the sending completes the parable and consoles the Prophet. Islahi says Quraysh should see that rejecting messengers, blaming signs, treating warning as bad luck and threatening them leads to destruction. Kuranyolu says it warns Muhammad's deniers and strengthens followers; Study Quran says Muhammad is told to give Quraysh an example of people who treated earlier bearers as they treat him. Al-Razi adds that rejection of envoys sent with God's leave is rejection of the one who sent them.
  36:14/q27/p2 The third also suggests Muhammad's apostleship confirming and strengthening the missions of Moses and Jesus. — reasons: Elmalili prefers this representational aim and cites 2:97, 3:3 and 5:48.
  36:14/q27/p3 The narrative unfolds through implied questions and answers throughout the passage. — reasons: Al-Jurjani reads the arrival, denial, threats and responses through this discourse pattern, comparing 15:57–58 and tracing it across 36:13, 15–17 and 19–20.
