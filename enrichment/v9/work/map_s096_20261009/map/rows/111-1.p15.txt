[IBNASHUR:111:1/r30] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Ab can denote someone devoted to or associated with what follows; the author uses familiar kunyas and Taftazani's account of names as omens to explain the suggestive Abu Lahab epithet.
[IBNASHUR:111:1/r31] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · التفتزاني | reports | Al-Taftazani's discussion of proper names and auspicious naming is cited to explain the kunya's implied association with Hell.
[IBNASHUR:111:1/r32] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · الشاعر | reports | An anonymous poet jokes that Abu al-Mahasin has no son among his children, illustrating how a person's name can suggest an outcome that is not borne out literally.
[IBNASHUR:111:1/r33] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أبو هريرة | reports | Abu Hurayra reports that Abu Lahab's daughter told the Prophet that people called her the daughter of the firewood of Hell, suggesting another possible sense of her father's kunya.
[IBNASHUR:111:1/r34] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Most readers pronounce lahab with a fatha; Ibn Kathir reads the h with sukun, a dialect form.
[IBNASHUR:111:1/r35] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The sukun may reflect the common Arabic tendency to suppress a vowel on a medial consonant after an initial consonant with a vowel.
[IBNASHUR:111:1/r36] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Alternatively, the pronunciation may result from changing a vowel when a common noun becomes a proper name.
[IBNASHUR:111:1/r37] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أبو الفتح بن جني | reports | Ibn Jinni explains the altered vowel in Shums as a proper-name change, like Maʿdi Yakrib, Tahluk, and Mawhab.
[IBNASHUR:111:1/r38] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Abu Salma, Zuhayr's father's kunya, similarly has a changed vowel because Salma, originally a feminine name, was transferred to a man's name.
[IBNASHUR:111:1/r4] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أبو حيان | reports | Abu Hayyan titles it Surat al-Lahab; Ibn ʿAshur says he has not seen this title elsewhere.
[IBNASHUR:111:1/r40] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The second clause may coordinate a curse on Abu Lahab himself with the prior curse on his hands, intensifying the rebuke; the whole and part meanings justify coordination.
[IBNASHUR:111:1/r41] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Because the clauses differ as whole and part, their semantic difference licenses the conjunction; verbal emphasis would not be conjoined with waw, as discussed in Surat al-Kafirun. | mentions 109:1
[IBNASHUR:111:1/r42] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Alternatively, the second clause is circumstantial with waw al-hal, not a prayer, but confirmation that the destruction invoked has occurred; the preceding line is then ridicule or a wish for more destruction.
[IBNASHUR:111:1/r43] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · النابغة | reports | Al-Nabigha's line says that God did indeed repay ʿAdi with the stated punishment, illustrating a circumstantial clause that confirms a fulfilled outcome.
[IBNASHUR:111:1/r44] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · عبد الله بن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masʿud's reading adds qad before tabb, supporting the circumstantial analysis and making the preceding words a taunt rather than a request for destruction.
[IBNASHUR:111:1/r45] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · عبد الله بن رواحة | reports | Ibn Rawaha's poem about his martyrdom at Mu'ta uses a prayer for guidance followed by confirmation that he was indeed guided, illustrating the circumstantial reading.
[IBNASHUR:111:1/r5] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · ابن العربي | reports | Ibn al-ʿArabi titled it Sura of what came from Abu Lahab; Ibn ʿAshur treats that as a descriptive heading, not a name.
[IBNASHUR:111:1/r6] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The sura is Meccan by consensus and is counted as the sixth sura revealed, after al-Fatiha and before al-Takwir.
[IBNASHUR:111:1/r7] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | reports | An unattributed report dates the sura's revelation to the fourth year of the Prophet's mission.
[IBNASHUR:111:1/r8] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · ابن عباس | reports | In the two Sahihs, Ibn ʿAbbas reports that the Prophet called Quraysh together on Safa with a warning call, said they would believe his warning about an imminent enemy, and Abu Lahab cursed him and asked if that was why he gathered them; the sura then came down.
[IBNASHUR:111:1/r9] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Ya sabahah is a call used to warn people of an enemy approaching in the morning.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p599/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | The sura is called Tabbat in most codices and by al-Tirmidhi and many exegetes, after its opening word.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p599/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | Some codices and tafsirs call it al-Masad; many exegetes call it Sura of Abu Lahab, while Abu Hayyan uses al-Lahab.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p599/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | Ibn al-Arabi's title What Happened to Abu Lahab is only a descriptive heading, not the sura's name.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p599/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The sura is Meccan by consensus, fifth in verse count, and sixth in revelation order after al-Fatiha and before al-Takwir.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p599/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · ابن عباس | reports | A report dates its revelation to the fourth year of the mission; the two Sahih collections relate the occasion at Safa, where Abu Lahab cursed the Prophet after his warning.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p599/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · سعيد بن جبير | reports | His report from Ibn Abbas gives the same Safa incident and ties it to the command to warn the nearest kin. | mentions 26:214

[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p600#2/r10] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | The positive converse appears in a blessing that the speaker's mouth not be chipped and an Asadi man's prayer for Musawwar's hands after he aided him.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p600#2/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · عبد الرحمن بن الحكم | reports | His cited invective begins by calling down ruin, illustrating the hostile opening convention.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p600#2/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · أبو تمام | reports | His invective likewise opens with a string of threats and disasters as a hostile prelude.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p600#2/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · أبو بكر بن الخازن | reports | He adapts Abu Tammam's opening in a congratulatory poem for a birth, showing the opening technique can suit praise too.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p600#2/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Tabb means ruin and destruction; the wording is both a curse and rebuke, answering Abu Lahab's insult with fitting requital.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p600#2/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The hands are singled out because Abu Lahab reportedly insulted the Prophet and seized a stone to throw at him.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p600#2/r7] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · طارق المحاربي | reports | He recounts seeing Muhammad preach at Dhu al-Majaz while Abu Lahab followed, injured his legs, and called him a liar.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p600#2/r8] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The curse falls on the hands because they caused the Prophet's injury, as Arabic idioms also curse or praise a person's mouth and hands.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p600#2/r9] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · النابغة | reports | His verse calls down God's punishment on hands, illustrating the curse's conventional reference to the instrument of harm.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p602/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · أبو الفتح بن جني | reports | He allows a proper name to change its usual vowel after becoming a proper name, as in compounds such as Maadd Yakrib.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p602/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Abu Salma, with a damma, likewise transfers the feminine name Salma to the name of Zuhayr's father, since men are rarely given women's kunyas.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p602/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | وَتَبَ may coordinate a second curse with تبت يدا; if the hands are the instrument of harm, the second curse covers Abu Lahab wholly and intensifies the first.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p602/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The whole-person curse is coordinated with the part because they differ in scope; verbal emphasis of identical wording would not take a coordinating waw.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p602/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | Alternatively, wa-tab is a circumstantial assertion rather than a curse, meaning the called-down ruin has in fact occurred, as in al-Nabigha's verse.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p602/r7] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · عبد الله بن مسعود | reports | His reading adds وقد تب, supporting the interpretation that the preceding statement expresses contempt rather than a prayer for ruin.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p602/r8] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · عبد الله بن رواحة | reports | His poem about his own death uses a similar circumstantial construction to convey a confirmed outcome, not a curse.
