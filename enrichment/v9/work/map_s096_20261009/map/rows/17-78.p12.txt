[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:17:78/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The discussion shifts from divinity, afterlife, and prophethood to prayer, the most important worship; commentators say this verse, revealed after the Isra prayer obligation, indicates the five daily prayers.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:17:78/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · İbn Âşûr | reports | Duluk can mean the sun passing its zenith toward sunset, or completing three quarters of its apparent daily circle (the afternoon), and is also used for sunset; Ibn Ashur therefore includes noon, afternoon, and evening.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:17:78/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · İbn Âşûr | reports | Ibn Ashur takes the particle ila as indicating that duluk includes more than one prayer time.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:17:78/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Şevkânî | reports | Shawkani lists three readings of duluk: the sun’s decline from zenith, sunset, or the period from decline until sunset; Razi calls the first the majority view.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:17:78/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Ghasaq means darkness and refers to night prayer after all twilight disappears; Quran al-fajr refers to morning prayer.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:17:78/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Quran al-fajr also implies recitation within prayer generally, though dawn prayer is singled out as an example and the Prophet recited more at dawn.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:17:78/r7] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Fahreddin er-Râzî | reports | On one reading, duluk covers noon and afternoon, ghasaq covers evening and night, and Quran al-fajr covers dawn, yielding three named periods for five prayers.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:17:78/r8] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Fahreddin er-Râzî | reports | Razi infers that pairing noon with afternoon and evening with night permits combining those prayers; other evidence limits combining to reasons such as travel.

[STUDYQURAN:17:78/r1] STUDYQURAN · most commentators | reports | The verse is commonly read as covering the five canonical prayers; most take the sun's decline as the beginning of decline and the time of ẓuhr, while some take it as maghrib.
[STUDYQURAN:17:78/r2] STUDYQURAN · most commentators | reports | The period from sun's decline to nightfall can cover ẓuhr, ʿaṣr, maghrib, and ʿishāʾ; Shiites often combine the two afternoon and two evening prayers.
[STUDYQURAN:17:78/r3] STUDYQURAN · some Sunni schools of law | reports | Some Sunni schools take the darkening of night simply as the sun's disappearance, while Jaʿfarī law waits until its rays disappear or the evening's middle begins.
[STUDYQURAN:17:78/r4] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Specific prayer-time interpretations may project the five prayers' regularity from middle Madinah back onto this Makkan revelation.
[STUDYQURAN:17:78/r5] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The dawn recitation is the fajr prayer between dawn and sunrise, or, in one view, specifically its Quran recitation.
[STUDYQURAN:17:78/r6] STUDYQURAN · several aḥādīth | reports | The dawn recitation is witnessed by angels of both day and night.
[STUDYQURAN:17:78/r7] STUDYQURAN · some Sufi commentaries | reports | The prayer times also point to continuous inward prayer, the heart's remembrance and invocation of God, which is the Sufi goal.
