[QASHANI:96:1-2/r2] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | The Lord who created means that He manifested Himself under the form of creation: the Prophet appears in his own form, stands through God in created form, and returns from divine unity to creaturely differentiation.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:1/r1] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Sura al-ʿAlaq is Meccan, and its opening was the first revelation, sent down in the cave of Hira according to reports established in Sahih al-Bukhari and other sources.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:1/r10] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · قيل | reports | Another parsing makes باسم ربك the object of اقرأ, the material to be read, like reading الحمد لله.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:1/r11] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · قيل | reports | Another explanation is to begin each sura's recitation with بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:1/r12] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash treats the bāʾ as meaning على, citing the command to board the ship in God's name as meaning on God's name. | mentions 11:41
[ABUHAYYAN:96:1/r13] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · قيل | reports | One interpretation is to recite the Qur'an beginning in the name of one's Lord.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:1/r14] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الزمخشري وقتادة | reports | Al-Zamakhshari parses باسم ربك as an accusative circumstantial phrase: say the basmala and then recite; Qatada is also credited with this explanation.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:1/r15] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · أبو عبيدة | reports | Abu ʿUbayda calls the bāʾ redundant and takes the command to mean remember your Lord.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:1/r16] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · أبو عبيدة | reports | Abu ʿUbayda also calls ism redundant and understands the phrase as reciting with the Lord's aid and success.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:1/r17] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The verse says your Lord because rabb conveys the one who nurtured you and looked after your welfare.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:1/r18] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The direct address signals special attachment and intimacy: the addressee has no lord besides Him.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:1/r19] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Because Arabs called idols lords, God is described as Creator, an attribute the idols cannot share.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:1/r2] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · جابر | reports | Jabir gives a contrary report that the first revelation was al-Muddaththir.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:1/r20] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The object of created is left unstated to stress God's exclusive creation of everything.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:1/r3] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · أبو ميسرة عمرو بن شرحبيل | rejects | Abu Maysara ʿAmr ibn Shurahbil said al-Fatiha was first revealed, but Abū Ḥayyān judges this report unsound.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:1/r4] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الزمخشري عن ابن عباس ومجاهد | reports | Al-Zamakhshari, reporting Ibn ʿAbbas and Mujahid, says this was the first sura revealed.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:1/r5] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · أكثر المفسرين | reports | Al-Zamakhshari says most exegetes hold that al-Fatiha was first revealed, followed by al-Qalam.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:1/r6] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Following the preceding mention of human creation in the best formation and what later happened to humanity, this sura recalls human stages and God's favor, then its transgression and its afterlife outcome. | mentions 95:4
[ABUHAYYAN:96:1/r7] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الجمهور | reports | The majority pronounce اقرأ with a quiescent hamza.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:1/r8] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الأعشى عن أبي بكر عن عاصم | reports | Al-Aʿsha, through Abu Bakr from ʿAsim, drops the hamza; Abū Ḥayyān explains it as a form like قرأ يقرأ, yielding an imperative like اسع.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:1/r9] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The preposition is apparently attached to اقرأ as an instrumental phrase, while its object is omitted: read what is revealed to you.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p506/r10] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Calling God Creator excludes the idols that Arabs called lords; the unexpressed object is universal, meaning that He created everything.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p506/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الجمهور | reports | The majority read iqraʾ with a hamza; al-Aʿshāʾ, transmitting from Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim, drops it by analogy with the imperfect verb.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p506/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | The bāʾ may mark an instrument or accompaniment, with an omitted object meaning “read what is revealed”; alternatively God’s name is the object, or one reads the basmala at each sūra’s opening.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p506/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash takes bāʾ as “upon,” as in the command to board the ark in God’s name. | mentions 11:41
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p506/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī makes “in the name of your Lord” a circumstantial accusative: begin by saying the basmala, then read; Abū Ḥayyān notes the analysis is also attributed to Qatāda.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p506/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · أبو عبيدة | reports | Abū ʿUbayda regards the bāʾ as extra, meaning “mention your Lord”; he also regards “name” as extra, yielding “read with your Lord’s aid.”
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p506/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The choice of “your Lord” evokes the one who nurtured and cared for the Prophet; direct address indicates exclusive intimacy, with no lord for him but God.
[SAMIN-DURR:96:1#2/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The second khalaqa may explain the first, initially leaving the creation unspecified and then singling out human creation to magnify it.
[SAMIN-DURR:96:1#2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Alternatively, the first verb omits its object, understood universally as all things, since it is unqualified.
[SAMIN-DURR:96:1/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The common reading has a quiescent hamza as an imperative from reading; ʿĀṣim in al-Aʿshā's transmission opens rāʾ as if hamza became alif and was deleted in the imperative, with a poetic parallel.
[SAMIN-DURR:96:1/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī takes bi-smi as circumstantial: begin by invoking your Lord's name, then recite.
[SAMIN-DURR:96:1/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو عبيدة | reports | Abū ʿUbayda offers an extra bāʾ, yielding read the name of your Lord, or treats the name as an added expression meaning remember your Lord.
[SAMIN-DURR:96:1/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The bāʾ may mark seeking aid, with an omitted object: recite what is revealed to you while seeking help through your Lord's name.
[SAMIN-DURR:96:1/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash makes bāʾ mean ʿalā, as in ride upon it in God's name, citing 11:41. | mentions 11:41
