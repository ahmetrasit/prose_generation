## THE CURRENT MAP
92:16/q01 What can kadhdhaba mean here? [كَذَّبَ · meaning] (turns on: Whether takdhib is confined to explicit rejection or also names failure in conduct.)
  92:16/q01/p1 It is religious denial or rejection of the truth. — reasons: Bint al-Shati defines ordinary kadhb as a statement at odds with reality or one's inward state, or a disappointed expectation; she says Qur'anic takdhib denotes denial of God, His signs, blessings, revelation, messengers, meeting, or the Last Day. Corpus Coranicum traces the term through early Meccan punishment legends, warnings, vice lists and judgment formulas, with possible earlier psalmic or Syriac kaddeb usage for lying, deception, treachery and religious denial; a Qur'anic coinage is therefore unnecessary.
  92:16/q01/p2 It can mean failing to obey, without spoken denial. — reasons: Al-Farra and grammarians reported by al-Tabari liken it to a fighter who retreats after facing the enemy. Abu Tharwan's praise of Banu Numayr means that they fight truly and do not retreat. Al-Farra, al-Tabari and al-Qurtubi compare 56:2: the Event has no 'falsehood' because it certainly occurs. Al-Nahhas cites another Arab idiom meaning someone persisted without falling short.
92:16/q02 What did the person deny? [كَذَّبَ · referent] (turns on: The notes variously supply the Messenger and revelation, God's signs or Book, and truth or the divine call as the implicit object.)
  92:16/q02/p1 He denied the Messenger, his message, or the Qur'an he brought. — reasons: Abu Umama names what Muhammad brought; Muqatil names the Qur'an. Al-Mawardi gives denial of the Messenger as an alternative to Qatada's Book. Al-Wahidi combines the Messenger and Qur'an. Ibn Abbas's Umayya report names Muhammad and earlier prophets; al-Nasafi says the messengers. Ibn Ashur says the early audience recognized denial of the Messenger and turning from the Qur'an.
  92:16/q02/p2 He denied God's Book or signs. — reasons: Qatada says God's Book. Al-Tabari says God's signs, which the person failed to affirm; al-Razi also names God's signs.
  92:16/q02/p3 He denied truth, guidance, God, or the true call. — reasons: Al-Baydawi and al-Alusi say truth; Qashani says God, with stubbornness and shirk; Elmalili says God's guidance; Qutb says the call and promised guidance; Tabatabai says the true call. Bint al-Shati describes the denier without a narrower object.
92:16/q03 What does tawallā mean here, and from what does he turn? [وَتَوَلَّىٰ · interpretation] (turns on: The implicit object is variously faith or revelation, obedience, and truth or its proofs.)
  92:16/q03/p1 He turns away from faith, the Messenger, or the Qur'an. — reasons: Abu Umama says he turns away from Muhammad; Muqatil, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi and al-Nasafi say faith. Ibn Ashur says the Qur'an. Bint al-Shati gives the literal sense of turning away or retreating and cites parallel pairings with disbelief or denial at 88:22-23, 75:31-32, 20:48 and 70:15-16.
  92:16/q03/p2 He turns away from obedience or practical action. — reasons: Qatada specifies obedience to God, al-Mawardi alternatively obedience to the Messenger. Al-Baydawi and al-Alusi likewise say obedience. Al-Razi says obedience and Ibn Kathir explains refusal to act with one's limbs. Al-Biqai adds refusal to give wealth for self-purification.
  92:16/q03/p3 He turns away from truth, guidance, or examining the proofs. — reasons: Al-Biqai calls this arrogant and stubborn rejection of truth; Elmalili and Qutb emphasize guidance; Tabatabai calls it turning from the true call. Al-Razi argues that every unbeliever turns away from examining the prophet's proofs, so all fit the wording.
92:16/q04 Who is the person described by 'the one who denied and turned away'? [ٱلَّذِى كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ · referent] (turns on: Whether the relative description marks a general class, disobedient refusers, or particular Meccan opponents.)
  92:16/q04/p1 The description identifies an unbeliever or polytheist as the wretched person. — reasons: Al-Hasan identifies the most wretched as a polytheist, while Mu'adh adds the phrase 'who denied and turned away.' Ibn Atiyya says the denial marks the preceding ashqa as an unbeliever. Al-Razi, al-Nasafi, al-Baydawi, al-Biqai, Elmalili, Ibn Ashur and Tabatabai read the category as unbelievers. Tabatabai says it is any unbeliever answering the true call this way, rather than one individual; Elmalili distinguishes a sinner who turns away without denial.
  92:16/q04/p2 It includes whoever refuses God or the Messenger through disobedience. — reasons: Abu Hurayra's and Abu Umama's reports explain the person as a refuser of Paradise, one who flees God. Prophetic reports define refusal as disobedience and the wretch as one who neither obeys God nor abstains from sin for Him. Al-Farra's figurative sense allows practical failure to count as denial.
  92:16/q04/p3 The wretched one was Umayya ibn Khalaf and others like him. — reasons: Ibn Abbas says the passage was revealed about Umayya and others who denied Muhammad and earlier prophets; the report through al-Dahhak likewise names Umayya and those like him. Al-Zamakhshari offers Abu Jahl or Umayya as the eminent unbeliever balanced against Abu Bakr.
  92:16/q04/p4 It identifies whoever was wretched in God's prior knowledge. — reasons: Al-Farra is reported by al-Qurtubi as locating the person's wretchedness in God's knowledge.
92:16/q05 How do denial and turning away work together? [كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ · interpretation]
  92:16/q05/p1 Denial is inward rejection, and turning away is failure in outward action. — reasons: Ibn Kathir assigns denial to the heart and refusal of action to the limbs. Al-Biqai assigns denial to the knowing faculty and arrogant turning away to the acting faculty, including refusal to give purifying wealth. Corpus Coranicum notes the established pairing in 96:13, 79:21-22 and 75:32; 88:23 pairs turning away with kafara instead.
92:16/q06 How does the relative clause explain the warning? [ٱلَّذِى كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ · grammar] (turns on: Ibn Ashur gives alternative syntactic attachments for the preceding exclusion clause.)
  92:16/q06/p1 It specifies the wretched people and shows that their acts cause their outcome. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says the clause identifies people who knew they had denied the Messenger and turned from the Qur'an, and that the relative clauses for the wretched and pious point to the actions causing their respective fates.
  92:16/q06/p2 The preceding exclusion clause can be another descriptor or a circumstantial clause. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says this clause concerns the particular Fire prepared for disbelievers, comparing 2:24, and that 92:17 confirms it.
92:16/q07 What force does 'the most wretched' have in identifying this person? [ٱلَّذِى · grammar] (turns on: Whether af'al is noncomparative, emphatically superlative, or a class description grounded in eternal misery.)
  92:16/q07/p1 Ashqa means 'wretched' without comparative force. — reasons: Abu Ubayda and Ibn Atiyya cite Tarafa's awhad for Arabic af'al used as fa'il. Ibn al-Jawzi gives the same proof; al-Razi compares 'not the one-and-only.' Al-Samin says this reading allows the Fire and its avoidance to include less extreme cases. Al-Burhan notes that adjectives normally agree in form with their antecedents, while agreement by meaning is rarer.
  92:16/q07/p2 It retains a superlative or comparative force for an eminent unbeliever. — reasons: Al-Zamakhshari balances an eminent unbeliever, Abu Jahl or Umayya, against Abu Bakr and calls the exclusive wording hyperbole as though Fire and Paradise were created for the two. Bint al-Shati calls the definite unqualified ashqa absolutely most wretched. She distinguishes the Thamud construct ashqaha, limited to their own most wretched member, and compares definite ashqa in al-A'la and al-Layl; she traces shaqa' as hardship, then Qur'anic misguidance and disobedience through 20:123, 23:106, 11:105-106, 20:117 and Maryam. Al-Raghib says misery entails toil but not all toil is misery. Islahi says rejected messengers after conclusive warning have no excuse, so are the most wretched.
  92:16/q07/p3 It names any unbeliever, since eternal misery outweighs temporary worldly suffering. — reasons: Tabatabai says ashqa does not single out the most wretched human being; the relative description marks any unbeliever who denies the true call and turns away.
92:16/q08 Whom does the Fire's restriction include or exclude? [ٱلَّذِى كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ · theology] (turns on: Whether the exclusion is absolute, limited to a particular Fire or duration, figurative, or supplied with omitted entrants; whether the traits cover all unbelievers.)
  92:16/q08/p1 Only unbelievers enter Hell or are threatened with its punishment. — reasons: The Murji'ites infer exclusivity from the warning's restriction to the denier. Al-Qadi points out that a literal restriction would even exclude an unbeliever who had not denied and turned away, and says it appears to assure sinful believers that their sin cannot harm them. Jishumi and al-Zajjaj reject the exclusionary inference.
  92:16/q08/p2 The verse concerns one particular Fire or level, so other sinners may face punishment. — reasons: Abu Ishaq al-Zajjaj says Hell has ranks; al-Qurtubi mentions the hypocrites' lowest level and other threatened punishments. Al-Wahidi likewise distinguishes levels. Ibn Ashur compares the specific Fire prepared for unbelievers at 2:24 and points to 92:17. Al-Zajjaj and al-Qurtubi cite 4:48: discretionary forgiveness of sins short of shirk would be idle if all such sinners were exempt.
  92:16/q08/p3 The exclusion is about permanent burning; sinful believers may enter but do not remain. — reasons: Al-Wahidi says sinful non-polytheists are omitted in abbreviated wording and yusliha denotes enduring heat, not necessarily eternity; abiding applies to the denying polytheist. Al-Nasafi and al-Biqai explicitly limit eternal exposure to the unbeliever.
