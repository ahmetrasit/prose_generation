[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p442/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | Exegetes disagree whether these verses followed the first five immediately; prayer's mention seems problematic if obligatory prayer began at the Night Journey years later. [TABATABAI:96:7-14/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Raʾā here means mental judgment, not visual sight; the human is both subject and object, and the clause explains why he transgresses: he supposes himself independent.
[TABATABAI:96:7-14/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The self-sufficient person attends to himself and outward means, forgets the Lord who blessed him, and then transgresses.
[BINTSHATI:v2p24/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | The warning turns to the human tendency to become arrogant about knowledge and status among created beings.
[BINTSHATI:v2p24/r5] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | طغيان means exceeding bounds, especially the tyranny of despots; استغناء is the opposite of need, and كلا rebukes and restrains. | mentions 79:17, 93:8
[BINTSHATI:v2p25/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | rejects | The cause is not absorption in money or status; the text says the human imagines himself independent of his Creator and is lured by acquired knowledge into overstepping his place.
[BINTSHATI:v2p27/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الفخر الرازي | reports | Al-Razi’s second solution reads الإنسان generically: despite creation and blessings, people transgress, forget their return, and forbid worship when they should follow guidance and enjoin piety.
[CORPUSCORANICUM:92:8:anmerkung/r1] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | holds | Istaġnā is an antonym of ittaqā and appears near taqwā in 96:7; in 80:5 the positive counterpart is piety in 80:9. | mentions 92:5, 96:7, 80:5, 80:9
[CORPUSCORANICUM:96:7:anmerkung/r1] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | holds | The particle an may be explanatory and causal, meaning because, as in 43:5. | mentions 43:5
[CORPUSCORANICUM:96:7:anmerkung/r2] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | holds | After verbs of perception, rāʾā takes an ordinary accusative suffix with reflexive meaning; al-Ṭabarī illustrates the usage with a parallel phrase.
[CORPUSCORANICUM:96:7:anmerkung/r3] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | holds | The early uses of istaġnā contrast implicitly with taqwā or fear of God, a central theme in late antique Christian monastic piety. | mentions 92:8, 92:9, 80:5, 64:6, 96:12, 92:5, 80:9
[CORPUSCORANICUM:96:7:anmerkung/r4] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | prefers | The author recommends translating istaġnā as considering oneself self-sufficient rather than behaving arrogantly: the self-sufficient person permits himself arrogance.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p388/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The Quraysh reject guidance arrogantly because wealth makes them feel self-sufficient and indifferent to God; their stated objections disguise this real motive and their love of the world.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:96:6/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Müfessirlerin çoğunluğu | reports | Most exegetes identify the criticized human as Abu Jahl; a report says he vowed to trample Muhammad while praying, but retreated when confronted by a fiery barrier and winged beings, and the Prophet said angels would have seized him; the event occasioned 96:6-19.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:96:6/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Beyond the occasion, the passage criticizes people in every age who forget God in life's struggle, rely only on themselves, and claim to need no divine help.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:96:6/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Kallā is a negative particle indicating that what follows should not happen; here it rebukes arrogant ingratitude, self-sufficiency, and turning from truth.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:96:6/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Humans are weak and depend on God and society; God owns and can remove all resources, so boasting, disobedience, superiority, and claiming control of one's fate reflect ignorance, weak faith, and lack of insight.
[QIRAAT-ER:96:7:2#1/r1] QIRAAT-ER · author | holds | The shortened-vowel form raʾahu contrasts with raʾāhu prosodically and does not change the root meaning.
[STUDYQURAN:96:6/r3] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Self-sufficiency conveys a negative claim to independence from God and is the root of rebelliousness.
