96:1/q25 What happened after the opening revelation? [* · reports] (turns on: Whether and how revelation paused, resumed, and reassured the Prophet.)
  96:1/q25/p1 After a pause, Gabriel appeared between heaven and earth and al-Muddaththir began. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī says the renewed appearance of the same angel and the command to cover show al-Muddaththir followed Iqraʾ; al-Qurṭubī's al-Zuhrī report places it after 96:5. DURR (جابر بن عبد الله الأنصاري): Jābir recounts the resumed revelation: Gabriel appeared seated between heaven and earth, and God sent the opening of Sūrat al-Muddaththir; revelation then intensified.; cites 74:1, 74:2, 74:3, 74:4, 74:5 DURR-FULL (النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم): After Waraqa's death and a pause in revelation, the Prophet sees the Hira angel seated between heaven and earth and retreats in fear. ITQAN: A second authentic report places al-Muddaththir after the pause in revelation: the Prophet saw the same angel previously encountered at Ḥirāʾ, showing the Ḥirāʾ revelation came earlier. ALUSI (عائشة): The account says Waraqa soon died and revelation then paused. ALUSI (جابر بن عبد الله): Relating the pause in revelation, Jābir says the angel appeared seated between heaven and earth; the Prophet returned frightened, and God then revealed al-Muddaththir. ALUSI: The wording about the interruption and renewed revelation shows why Jābir's report does not establish al-Muddaththir as first overall: it was first after the pause. ALUSI (صاحب الكشف): Al-Kashf reconciles Jābir's report by taking his claim to mean the first complete sūra, while al-Muddaththir was first after the interval. ALUSI (صاحب شرح صحيح مسلم): The commentary on Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim says Iqraʾ was first absolutely and al-Muddaththir first after the pause; it calls the Fātiḥa-first view clearly invalid. ALUSI-FULL (عائشة): Aisha’s account says revelation paused soon after the opening verses; a report from Jabir then recounts how revelation resumed with al-Muddaththir. ALUSI-FULL (جابر بن عبد الله): Jabir's account of Muddaththir explicitly concerns the period after revelation paused and then resumed, undermining its use as the first revelation overall. ALUSI-FULL (الكشف): Al-Kashf takes Jabir's claim to concern the first complete surah, while Sahih Muslim's commentary distinguishes Iqra as first overall from Muddaththir as first after the hiatus; the claim that al-Fatiha came first is plainly false. IBNASHUR: Ibn Ashur reconciles Jabir's report by taking al-Muddaththir as the first surah after the interruption in revelation, just as al-Duha followed a second interruption, as al-Itqan explains. IBNASHUR-FULL (جابر): Jabir says al-Muddaththir was first; Ibn Ashur reconciles this as the first revelation after the pause, like al-Duha after the second pause.
