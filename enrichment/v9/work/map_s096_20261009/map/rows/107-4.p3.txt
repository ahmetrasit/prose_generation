[IBNASHUR:107:4/r2] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | If the whole sura is Meccan, the worshipers are the same class as those who deny judgment, repel the orphan, and fail to urge feeding the poor.
[IBNASHUR:107:4/r3] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Calling them worshipers replaces a pronoun with another listed trait, avoiding a monotonous run of six parallel traits; placing woe here shows all their traits occasion it, as al-Kashshaf hints.

[IBNASHUR:107:4/r6] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The plural suits the apparent reading that the denier is a class; if an earlier view meant one specific person, the plural generalizes to include him among others with these traits.
[IBNASHUR:107:7/r10] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The author proposes a general principle: when revelation is attached to an earlier passage, its wording is shaped to fit that context, which explains the consequential fa.

[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p563/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The surah has six reported titles: al-Maʿun, Araʾayta, Araʾayta alladhi, al-Maʿun wa-l-Din wa-Araʾayta, al-Din, al-Takdhib, and al-Yatim.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p563/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | Most say it is Meccan; reports from Ibn Abbas and Qatada call it Medinan, while a view attributed to Ibn Abbas makes the first three verses Meccan and the rest Medinan.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p563/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · هبة الله الضرير | prefers | The author calls the split-revelation account most plausible: the latter verses address hypocrites.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p563/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | If Meccan, it ranks seventeenth in revelation order, after al-Takathur and before al-Kafirun.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p563/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | Most verse counters count six verses; al-Alusi says the Iraqi schools count six, while Ali al-Nuri al-Safaqsi reports seven in the Himsi count and six elsewhere.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p563/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The surah exposes deniers of resurrection through abuse of the weak, refusal to feed the poor, and neglect of prayer and alms without fear of God's punishment.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p566/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The fā explicitly links the warning to the preceding description; on a wholly Meccan reading, the same denier neglects prayer, performs it for show, and withholds aid.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p566/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Calling them “prayers” is mockery: they do not truly pray and are not Muslims, as in 74:43–44; naming them explicitly avoids an awkward list of six traits.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p566/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The placement of wayl before the explicit noun signals punishment for all the preceding vices, as al-Kashshaf hints.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p566/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · مالك | reports | Ibn Wahb and Ashhab transmit from Malik the Medinan interpretation that these heedless prayers are hypocrites.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p566/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The plural fits the generic deniers; if a particular person is intended, it generalizes the warning to others sharing neglect of prayer, ostentation, and refusal of aid.
[TABATABAI:107:1-7/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The surah warns people professing religion whose traits resemble hypocrites: neglecting prayer, acting ostentatiously, and withholding aid, all incompatible with belief in requital.
[TABATABAI:107:1-7/r10] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Those heedless of prayer do not care if they miss it entirely, miss some prayers, or delay it beyond its preferred time.
[TABATABAI:107:1-7/r11] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The consequence-marker applies the denier of requital to these praying people; they display belief while practically denying requital, so they are not free of hypocrisy.
