## THE CURRENT MAP
53:40/q01 What does سعيه mean? [سَعْيَهُۥ · meaning] (turns on: Whether striving names outward work or its inward intention.)
  53:40/q01/p1 A person's work, deeds, or effort. — reasons: Abū ʿUbayda and Ibn Qutayba gloss saʿy as work or deed. Bursevī links the sense to 92:4, where efforts differ, and says these teachings are in the scrolls.
  53:40/q01/p2 A person's intention. — reasons: Al-Warrāq cites the report that people are raised according to their intentions.
53:40/q02 How does سعيه clarify the preceding ما in 53:39? [سَعْيَهُۥ · links]
  53:40/q02/p1 It supports reading ما as a verbal noun denoting the work accomplished. — reasons: Rāzī prefers this reading; a verbal noun can denote its object, just as خلق الله can mean God's created thing.
53:40/q03 How is وأن سعيه connected with the preceding scriptural statements? [وَأَنَّ سَعْيَهُۥ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the coordination keeps verse 40 inside the earlier scriptures or introduces a further statement.)
  53:40/q03/p1 It coordinates with the earlier أن and remains among the teachings in the scriptures. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya traces the coordination to the understood أن in verse 38; he says the majority read fatḥa while Abū al-Sammāl reads kasra from verse 42 onward. Al-Samīn allows genitive, nominative, or accusative case for both وأن clauses. Ibn ʿĀshūr says emphatic أن fits this consequence of a person's possessing only his own effort.
  53:40/q03/p2 It begins Qur'anic commentary on the earlier scriptural statement rather than remaining within it. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr alternatively coordinates it with relative ما under the preposition bi in verses 36–37: the people were not told this truth. Islāhī says extensions grounded in the original statement begin at verse 40 and turn the discourse toward Quraysh.
53:40/q04 Does أن require an omitted pronoun of شأن here? [وَأَنَّ سَعْيَهُۥ · grammar]
  53:40/q04/p1 No; سعيه itself serves as the noun of أن. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says this also removes any need to reduce أن.
53:40/q05 What time does سوف mark? [سَوْفَ · grammar]
  53:40/q05/p1 It marks a future event, usually understood as distant. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr gives the usual force of سوف. Elmalılı says the effort's fruit need not appear immediately, yet the effort is not lost.
53:40/q06 How is يُرى read and grammatically construed? [يُرَىٰ · readings] (turns on: Whether an active verb can take an implied object pronoun without leaving أن improperly construed.)
  53:40/q06/p1 The passive reading means that effort is shown or seen. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī and al-Baghawī derive it from 'I showed him the thing'; al-Ālūsī also gives that derivation. Al-Samīn prefers visual perception on the passive because interpreting it as knowing needs an omitted second object. Al-Zajjāj allows an active construction but prefers this reading.
  53:40/q06/p2 An active reading is permissible with an implied object pronoun referring to effort. — reasons: Abū Isḥāq, al-Khalīl, Sībawayh and associates, Muḥammad ibn Yazīd, and al-Zajjāj permit it as سوف يراه. Al-Mubarrid says أن necessitates the omitted pronoun because the clause would otherwise have two governors; the Basran analogy is إن زيدا ضربت. Al-Samīn says only أن governs سعيه, while يرى governs the pronoun, so this is not double government by تنازع or اشتغال; he rejects calling the pronoun far-fetched.
  53:40/q06/p3 The active reading is forbidden or weak. — reasons: The Kufans say أن and يرى would both govern سعيه. Abū al-Baqāʾ calls the active fatḥ reading anomalous and weak because it leaves the noun of أن without a predicate. Al-Zajjāj calls analogous إن زيدا سوف أكرم weak unless a pronoun is supplied or the construction changed to إن زيدا سأكرم. Al-Samīn disputes the alleged double government and the objection to an implied pronoun.
53:40/q07 What does it mean that effort will be seen? [سَعْيَهُۥ يُرَىٰ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether seeing denotes direct presentation of deeds, knowledge of them, their recompense, or a visible embodiment.)
  53:40/q07/p1 The deeds or effort themselves will be displayed and witnessed at the Resurrection. — reasons: Muqātil says worldly deeds appear in the Hereafter. Al-Zajjāj says God already knows them, but the servant sees them on his balance. Al-Naḥḥās and al-Wāḥidī include good and bad deeds; al-Baghawī and al-Nasafī specify the scale. Al-Burṣawī, al-Ālūsī, and Elmalılı add the record. Rāzī says disclosure brings joy to believers and grief to unbelievers. Ibn Kathīr compares 9:105; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ cites 3:30 and 99:6–8 for deeds made present.
  53:40/q07/p2 The person's effort will be made known or understood. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba glosses 'will be seen' as 'will be known'; al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī transmit him. Asad says God makes people understand their life deeds. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes seeing as a metaphor for certain, widespread public knowledge and compares 7:49.
  53:40/q07/p3 What is seen is the effort's recompense. — reasons: Al-Jishumī transmits this as a second view of Judgment Day; al-Qurṭubī says God shows a person the recompense. Al-Ṭabrisī explains seeing the deeds as being repaid for them. Rāzī calls a figurative reading in which one sees the reward of service before a king remote: verse 41 separately promises full recompense.
  53:40/q07/p4 A past deed may itself return in a visible, even beautiful, form. — reasons: Rāzī reports a righteous deed shown in beautiful form and argues that every existent can be seen and God can restore what ceased to exist. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows embodied visible deeds or identification by signs, citing 18:49, the believers' light in 66:8, and a banner assigned to each traitor.
  53:40/q07/p5 God may be the one who sees the effort. — reasons: Rāzī cites 9:105 for this alternative.
53:40/q08 By whom will the striving be seen? [يُرَىٰ · referent] (turns on: Whether the passive points chiefly to the doer, other witnesses, or intentionally leaves the observer open.)
  53:40/q08/p1 The person who performed it will see it. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj places it in the servant's balance; al-Wāḥidī, al-Nasafī, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı, Asad, and Study Quran likewise emphasize the person's sight or understanding. Al-Ṭabrisī says the servant sees and receives full repayment.
  53:40/q08/p2 God and other witnesses, as well as or besides the performer, will see it. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya names God and those present; Rāzī allows God as viewer and compares 9:105. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says the passive suggests someone besides the performer. Study Quran links 9:105, where God, the Messenger, and believers see deeds.
  53:40/q08/p3 The passive leaves the viewer unspecified. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says being seen itself brings pleasure or shame regardless of who sees it.
53:40/q09 How are efforts displayed or identified at reckoning? [سَعْيَهُۥ يُرَىٰ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the display is through scales, written records, visible forms or signs, or public disclosure.)
  53:40/q09/p1 They are visible on the balance. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj, al-Naḥḥās, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī reporting al-Zajjāj, al-Nasafī, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, and Elmalılı locate the deeds on the scales.
  53:40/q09/p2 They are seen written in the person's record. — reasons: Al-Jishumī reports this view; Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, and Elmalılı mention record and scale together.
  53:40/q09/p3 They may appear as embodied deeds or as identifying signs. — reasons: Rāzī reports righteous deeds in beautiful form and says God can restore what has ceased. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 18:49 and 66:8, the light going ahead of believers, and the Prophetic report of a banner for every traitor.
  53:40/q09/p4 Their truth may be established through public knowledge. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the exchange in 7:49 and calls seeing a metaphor for certain, widespread knowledge.
53:40/q10 Why is the viewer left unnamed by the passive verb? [يُرَىٰ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the passive chiefly universalizes the effect of exposure or points to additional observers.)
  53:40/q10/p1 The fact of being seen itself produces joy or shame whoever sees. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī explicitly links the unspecified viewer to pleasure for good effort and shame for evil effort.
  53:40/q10/p2 The passive implies an observer besides the person who performed the deed. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ draws this inference from the passive form.
53:40/q11 What is the effect of showing people's deeds? [سَعْيَهُۥ يُرَىٰ · interpretation]
  53:40/q11/p1 It honors and gladdens the good, and rebukes, grieves, or shames wrongdoers. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya says public presentation honors good people and rebukes wrongdoers; Rāzī says believers rejoice, perhaps before everyone, while unbelievers grieve. Al-Biqāʿī says being seen itself gives pleasure or shame. Al-Ālūsī calls it glad tidings and rebuke. Ibn ʿĀshūr speaks of good reputation or public disgrace.
53:40/q12 How does seeing effort relate to its full recompense in the next verse? [سَعْيَهُۥ يُرَىٰ · links] (turns on: Whether 'seen' already means reward or precedes the distinct recompense of verse 41.)
  53:40/q12/p1 Display comes first; full repayment follows. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Bayḍāwī, and al-Ṭabrisī make the sequence explicit. Al-Naḥḥās, Ibn Kathīr, Islāhī, Kur'an Yolu, and Study Quran couple presentation with just recompense. Al-Ṭabrisī takes the following pronoun as referring to effort: the effort itself is repaid, with lasting reward for obedience. Rāzī says the next verse's separate mention of repayment argues against taking seeing as reward.
  53:40/q12/p2 Seeing can itself mean receiving the recompense. — reasons: Al-Jishumī reports that what appears on Judgment Day is reward; al-Qurṭubī says God shows the person's recompense. Rāzī rejects this as remote because verse 41 separately addresses recompense.
