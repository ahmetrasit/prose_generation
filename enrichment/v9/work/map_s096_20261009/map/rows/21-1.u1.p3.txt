21:1/q30 What divine attributes or spiritual states does the opening evoke? [* · inward] (turns on: How the announced account is read as a present spiritual warning and encounter.)
  21:1/q30/p1 The divine names express justice, mercy, sovereignty, respite and decisive judgment. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī describes God as wise and just, Allah as unrivaled King, the All-Merciful as granting existence, and the Merciful as saving whom He wills afterward.
  21:1/q30/p2 God's favor shelters the obedient and gives neglecters respite, while repentance is met with mercy and arrogance crushed. — reasons: Al-Qushayrī connects confession to remembrance, concealment of sin to divine favor, and return to mercy.
  21:1/q30/p3 God enables outward striving and grants inner realization and witnessing. — reasons: Al-Qushayrī says striving brings future recompense while abiding witnessing grants immediate nearness; obedient and disobedient people receive reward and punishment respectively.
  21:1/q30/p4 The minor resurrection can make accounting spiritually present now. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī says people who understood resurrection would already witness their own accounting.
21:1/q31 How do theologians classify closeness and forgetfulness? [ٱقْتَرَبَ غَفْلَةٍ · theology] (turns on: Whether forgetfulness is knowledge loss, a distinct meaning, or a condition of the heart.)
  21:1/q31/p1 Closeness is a created state, and forgetfulness is loss of needed knowledge. — reasons: Jishumī reports theologians classing proximity with combination, separation, motion and rest. Al-Qāḍī defines forgetfulness as loss of necessary ordinary knowledge.
  21:1/q31/p2 Being or occurrence can be treated as a separate genus of state. — reasons: Jishumī reports this alternative classification among theologians.
  21:1/q31/p3 Forgetfulness can be an independent entity or meaning. — reasons: Jishumī reports this view without further argument.
  21:1/q31/p4 Forgetfulness can be corruption in the heart. — reasons: Jishumī records this as another account.
21:1/q32 What did the opening inspire in reported poetry or preaching? [* · reports] (turns on: Which reported response gives poetic or homiletic expression to the warning.)
  21:1/q32/p1 It inspired a couplet on people heedless while death grinds on. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr relays Ibn ʿAsākir's report that Abū Nuwās praised Abū al-ʿAtāhiya's verse as drawn from this opening.
  21:1/q32/p2 It accompanies preaching that the world is nearly spent. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī quotes a predecessor likening the remaining world to a little liquid in a vessel.
21:1/q33 What is the status of Qur'anic speech discussed with this passage? [* · theology] (turns on: Whether 'speech' means recited conventional signs or a wider disclosure, and what can bear temporal predicates.)
  21:1/q33/p1 Recited speech is a conventional sign of meaning; its sound tokens arise in time, while the convention itself has no temporal status. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī says each reciter's sounds are distinct, and speech counts as one by shared intended meaning. Ordered eternal letters are impossible; conventional speech is neither a real act nor an essential attribute, although external sounds are acts. His analysis treats meaningful verses as neither eternal nor originated in themselves.
  21:1/q33/p2 Speech can be understood more broadly as whatever reveals hidden reality. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī says effects disclose latent perfection and essential attributes disclose essence; philosophers call God's knowledge, power, life and world His speech. Divine knowledge is eternal, created signs originated, and revelation originated as imparted but eternal as known. He rejects multiplying divine attributes from such reductions.
  21:1/q33/p3 The dispute over an eternal or created recited Qur'an is unproductive. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī says eternal sounds are impossible and divine knowledge needs no additional Qur'an attribute; multiplying every reduced attribute would produce limitless attributes. Scripture's plain use concerns recited speech.
21:1/q34 What variant writing or recitation issue is reported nearby? [* · readings]
  21:1/q34/p1 A wāw may be dropped in connected recitation and then omitted in writing. — reasons: Al-Lawāmiḥ says avoiding two sukūns accounts for the spelling, comparing 42:24; al-Ālūsī cites a poetic line where ḍamma betrays the missing wāw, although some grammarians consider the omission poetic necessity.
