[ALUSI:96:1/r32] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | The command does not impose an impossible task: the Prophet knows that whatever is revealed as Qurʾān is what he is commanded to recite, and the command can include its own wording.
[ALUSI:96:1/r33] ALUSI, d. 1270 · الآمدي | reports | Al-Āmidī uses the repeated command and question in the beginning-of-revelation report as evidence that clarification may be delayed: Gabriel says read three times before specifying what to read.

[ALUSI:96:1/r35] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | rejects | Al-Ālūsī questions using the brocade account to explain the command as a recitation of visible writing, and notes that al-Āmidī's handling of the report also has a problem.
[ALUSI:96:1/r36] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Calling God the Prophet's Lord evokes nurturing and gradual development toward fitting perfection; the possessive pronoun signals that revelation brings him to the highest human completion.
[ALUSI:96:1/r37] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | The description recalls God's first favor to the Prophet and signals God's power to teach him to read in the gentlest way.
[ALUSI:96:1/r38] ALUSI, d. 1270 · قيل | reports | Another explanation says the creator description confirms that the intended Lord is God, since Arabs called idols lords but did not attribute creation to them.
[ALUSI:96:1/r39] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Khalaqa can be intransitive, meaning the one who creates, or have a general omitted object, meaning the one who created everything; either way its force is universal.
[ALUSI:96:1/r4] ALUSI, d. 1270 · عائشة | reports | Al-Ḥākim and al-Bayhaqī authenticated a report from ʿĀʾisha giving the same account that Iqraʾ was first.
[ALUSI:96:1/r40] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | The author raises, without yet answering in this passage, why humanity is singled out in the next verse if the creation description already has universal force. | mentions 96:2
[ALUSI:96:1/r5] ALUSI, d. 1270 · مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid says the first revelations were Iqraʾ bi-smi rabbik followed by Nūn wa-l-qalam.
[ALUSI:96:1/r6] ALUSI, d. 1270 · جابر بن عبد الله | reports | In a report in both Ṣaḥīḥ collections, Jābir ibn ʿAbd Allāh answers that Yā ayyuhā al-muddaththir was first; he responds to those who cite Iqraʾ by narrating the Prophet's account.
[ALUSI:96:1/r7] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبو ميسرة عمرو بن شرحبيل | reports | A mursal report with trustworthy narrators, transmitted by al-Bayhaqī and al-Wāḥidī, supports the view that al-Fātiḥa came first.
[ALUSI:96:1/r8] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | rejects | The Fātiḥa-first report could describe what was revealed after Iqraʾ and al-Muddaththir, and the reports supporting the other order are stronger.
[ALUSI:96:1/r9] ALUSI, d. 1270 · جابر بن زيد | reports | Jābir ibn Zayd gives this sequence: Iqraʾ, Nūn, al-Muzzammil, al-Muddaththir, then al-Fātiḥa.
[ALUSI:96:5/r11] ALUSI, d. 1270 · بعضهم | rejects | A very remote analysis attaches “in the name” in the basmala to the first recite and “in your Lord’s name” to the second, making the fronting specify God’s name as the required opening.
[ALUSI:96:5/r12] ALUSI, d. 1270 · بعضهم | reports | Another analysis leaves “in God’s name” with its customary attachment, treats recite as initiating the reading, and attaches “your Lord” to the second command.
[ALUSI:96:5/r13] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | prefers | Al-Ālūsī prefers attaching “in your Lord’s name” to the adjacent verb; placing the verb first suits this opening surah, where recitation is especially important.
[ALUSI:96:5/r14] ALUSI, d. 1270 · قتادة | reports | Qatāda explains the command as beginning in the Lord’s name: say “In God’s name,” then recite; without the basmala, or with an idol’s name, the command would not be fulfilled.
[ALUSI:96:5/r15] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Fronting the prepositional phrase would instead make opening in God’s name the requested act, while recitation itself would be presupposed rather than commanded.
[ALUSI:96:5/r16] ALUSI, d. 1270 · من علق الجار بالثاني | reports | Those attaching the phrase to the second command answer that the first command already establishes recitation itself as required.

[ALUSI-FULL:v30p177#3/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · عائشة | reports | Al-Hakim and al-Bayhaqi authenticate a report from Aisha likewise identifying Iqra as first; Mujahid says it was followed by Nun and the pen.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p177#3/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · جابر بن عبد الله | reports | A report from Jabir calls Ya ayyuhal-Muddaththir the first revelation, but the earlier group offers several responses to it.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p177#3/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · أبو ميسرة عمرو بن شرحبيل | reports | A trustworthy-transmitters but mursal report argues that al-Fatiha came first; the response is that it could refer to what followed Iqra and Muddaththir, and other reports are stronger.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p177#3/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · جابر بن زيد | reports | Jabir ibn Zayd gives the order Iqra, Nun, al-Muzzammil, al-Muddaththir, then al-Fatiha.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p177#3/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | Another view holds that only the opening of Iqra through what he did not know was revealed first and the remainder came later, as suggested by Aisha's account of the first revelation.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p178#2/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Read has an implied object, what is revealed of the Quran; the verb is not intransitive and باسم ربك is not its object with an extra ba, contrary to Abu Ubayda.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p178#2/r10] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Who created recalls God's original favor and ability to teach reading; it may mean the one who creates or the one who created everything, and it rules out idols as true lords because Arabs did not ascribe creation to them.

[ALUSI-FULL:v30p178#2/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · النووي | reports | Some argue that the basmala is not Quran at the beginnings of surahs because it is absent from sound accounts of the first revelation; al-Nawawi reports this argument, while proponents answer that it descended later.

[ALUSI-FULL:v30p178#2/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The command is not impossible even if immediate: the Prophet knows that whatever is revealed as Quran is what he must read, so the command itself can be part of the material to read.
