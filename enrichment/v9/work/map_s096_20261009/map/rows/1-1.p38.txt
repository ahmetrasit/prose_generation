[BAYDAWI:1:1/r37] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | reports | Allāh is said to derive from ilāh, with hamza deleted and replaced by the article; ilāh originally meant any deity but came to designate the true deity.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r38] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | reports | Proposed roots of ilāh include worship, bewilderment, and finding rest with someone, since hearts find tranquility in God's remembrance.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r39] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | reports | Other proposals compare worshippers' clinging in hardship to a calf clinging to its mother, or derive the word from fleeing in fear and receiving refuge.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r4] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · النبي | reports | The Prophet is quoted saying al-Fatiha is a cure for every illness, explaining its names al-Shāfiya and al-Shifāʾ.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r40] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | reports | A further proposal derives it from walaha, bewilderment, with wāw changed to hamza under kasra; Bayḍāwī objects that its plural is āliha, not awliha.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r41] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | reports | Another derivation takes it from lāh, hidden and elevated: God is beyond sight and above all things; a cited poem uses lāh.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r42] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | reports | One view makes Allāh the unique proper name: it is described but not itself descriptive and uniquely names the one whose attributes are predicated.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r43] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | reports | That view argues that a merely descriptive reading would not make “no deity but God” a statement of unity or exclude partnership.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r44] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | prefers | Bayḍāwī prefers that Allāh was originally descriptive but became name-like through exclusive use, allowing predicates while excluding descriptive use and partners.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r45] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | The unqualified essence is unknowable to humans and cannot be named; a merely proper-name reading also fails to explain “He is God in the heavens.” | mentions 6:3
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r46] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | reports | Another proposal derives the word from Syriac lāhā, arabized by dropping the final alif and adding lām.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r47] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | reports | The lām is traditionally velarized after fatḥa or ḍamma; some say it is always velarized.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r48] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | Deleting Allāh's alif is an error that invalidates prayer and does not establish an explicit oath, though poetic necessity may require it.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r49] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | Al-Raḥmān and al-Raḥīm are intensive forms from raḥima, like ghaḍbān from ghaḍiba and ʿalīm from ʿalima.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r5] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | It is called the Seven Oft-Repeated because it comprises seven verses by agreement.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r50] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | Mercy linguistically is tenderness of heart and an inclination entailing benefaction; a womb is named raḥim because it inclines over what it contains.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r51] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | Divine names are understood by resultant acts and ends, not by the passive beginnings of human mercy.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r52] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | Al-Raḥmān is more emphatic than al-Raḥīm because the expanded form indicates greater meaning by quantity or quality.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r53] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | reports | By quantity, al-Raḥmān is worldly mercy shared by believers and unbelievers, while al-Raḥīm is afterlife mercy for believers.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r54] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | reports | By quality, al-Raḥmān denotes mercy in both worlds and al-Raḥīm mercy in this world, since afterlife blessings are immense and worldly ones vary in size.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r55] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | Though rhetoric might progress from lesser to greater, al-Raḥmān comes first because worldly mercy precedes the afterlife.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r56] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | Al-Raḥmān precedes as God's exclusive name, the true ultimate benefactor; others give from mixed motives and only mediate blessings God creates and enables.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r57] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | reports | Alternatively, al-Raḥmān denotes great blessings and al-Raḥīm completes it by including blessings outside that category.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r58] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | reports | The order may also preserve the sūra's verse-end rhyme.
[BAYDAWI:1:1/r59] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | prefers | Bayḍāwī's preferred analysis leaves al-Raḥmān diptote despite its exclusivity to God, preventing usual feminine forms in this pattern.
