[MUQATIL:4:23/r1] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | The listed mothers, daughters, sisters, aunts, and nieces are forbidden through blood relationship.
[MUQATIL:4:23/r2] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | Foster mothers and sisters, mothers-in-law, and stepdaughters of wives with whom the man has consummated marriage are forbidden.
[MUQATIL:4:23/r3] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | If he has not consummated marriage with their mothers, there is no sin in marrying the daughters.
[MUQATIL:4:23/r4] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | A son's wife is forbidden when the son is the man's biological child, born from his loins rather than adopted; this is affinity.
[MUQATIL:4:23/r5] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | Combining two sisters is forbidden, except when one is held in ownership and he marries her to someone else.
[MUQATIL:4:23/r6] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | The exception for past conduct means God forgives intercourse with two sisters before the prohibition.

[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماينصبفىالألف)#57/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | After the interrogative hamza, a fronted object is accusative through an understood verb reconstructed from the clause; accusative is the preferred, standard construction.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماينصبفىالألف)#57/r10] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A clause beginning with a noun related to the fronted noun can be parsed like a new nominal clause; making that related noun an object licenses accusative on the initial noun.

[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماينصبفىالألف)#57/r12] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | If the fronted noun is analyzed as governed by the hidden verb, a verb requiring a preposition should make it genitive; overt and understood words otherwise share their governance.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماينصبفىالألف)#57/r13] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In conditional senses, ḥaythu and idhā make a following noun accusative when a verb follows, but allow a nominative noun when followed by a nominal predicate.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماينصبفىالألف)#57/r14] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Idh permits an initial nominative noun, especially with a present-tense predicate; a past-tense predicate is awkward because idh is used in obligatory narrative contexts.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماينصبفىالألف)#57/r15] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A fronted noun is accusative when the later coordinated phrase is semantically connected to it, as through a shared description; without that connection, the construction is not valid.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماينصبفىالألف)#57/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The omitted verb has the same interpretation as the overt explanatory verb used in comparable noninterrogative fronting.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماينصبفىالألف)#57/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | When the clause subject is related to the fronted noun, that subject's nominative case makes the fronted noun nominative; an unrelated object instead takes accusative.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماينصبفىالألف)#57/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | If the related noun is made the object and another noun the subject, the initial noun becomes accusative, as in the switch from Zayd as subject to his brother as object.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماينصبفىالألف)#57/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A fronted noun with a following prepositional phrase is case-marked as though the verb governed it directly; removing the phrase reveals the accusative pattern.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماينصبفىالألف)#57/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A fronted noun followed by a passive or intransitive verb plus a preposition must be nominative when the clause without that phrase is nominative.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماينصبفىالألف)#57/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | An omitted word retains the case governance it would have if expressed, whether genitive, accusative, or nominative.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماينصبفىالألف)#57/r8] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | When the interrogative hamza falls on a pronoun starting a new clause, nominative is ordinary, though accusative fronting remains good Arabic.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماينصبفىالألف)#57/r9] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A time adverb does not separate the interrogative from the object: the object remains accusative when fronted before a verb followed by an adverbial phrase.
