[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#7/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ḍalāl may mean concealment or disappearance, destruction, deviation from the straight path, or forgetfulness; the forgetfulness sense in 2:282 is supported by its following remembrance clause. | mentions 32:10, 2:282
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#7/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Amin is not part of the Quran by consensus; it means respond and is an indeclinable imperative noun.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#7/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | rejects | Abu al-Baqa rejects treating Amin as a divine name because a singular definite vocative should take damma and divine names require transmitted authorization.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#7/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الفارسي | reports | Al-Farisi explains the divine-name reading by an implicit pronoun referring to God within the imperative noun; the author calls this a good analysis.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#7/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Amin has long and short forms, each attested in poetry.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#8/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Some treat the long form as foreign because its pattern resembles Qābīl and Hābīl; geminating mīm is commonly called erroneous, though transmitted from al-Ḥasan and Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#8/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الجوهري | reports | Al-Jawhari transmits the common judgment that geminating mim is erroneous, though the reading is also reported from al-Hasan and Jafar al-Sadiq.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#8/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الحسين بن الفضل | reports | Al-Ḥusayn b. al-Faḍl derives the geminated form from amma, to intend or aim toward God, as in the Qur'anic phrase about those intending the Sacred House. | mentions 5:2
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ṣirāṭ alladhīna is a complete apposition, a definite noun replacing another definite noun.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Grammarians enumerate seven kinds of apposition; the first three are undisputed, while some assert apposition of clarification and cite a prayer report, which does not apply to Qur'anic wording.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ذي الرمة | reports | Dhū al-Rumma's line is cited for apposition by mistake or forgetfulness because ḥawa is pure black and laʿas is black tinged with red; the author says these do not occur in eloquent speech.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | Arguments for whole-from-part apposition in the Ṭalḥa and Imruʾ al-Qays lines fail: the first reflects an omitted annexed noun, and the second treats a day as a time-span.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · جعفر بن محمد | reports | Jaʿfar b. Muḥammad says the second ṣirāṭ differs from the first and denotes knowledge of God; the author finds the required omitted conjunction problematic.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Apposition may be definite or indefinite, overt or pronominal in several combinations; its function is clarification after ambiguity and semantic emphasis through an implied repetition of the governing word.
[IBNKATHIR:1:5/r11] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · الله تعالى | reports | In a divine report, God divides the prayer between Himself and His servant, responds to each recited section, calls 1:5 shared between them, and grants the servant's request.
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r1] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | “Path of those You favored” explains the straight path; grammatically it may be apposition or explanatory apposition.
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r10] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari reports an accusative circumstantial reading of ghayr, attributed to the Prophet and ʿUmar and also transmitted from Ibn Kathir; its circumstantial referent is the pronoun in ʿalayhim.
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r11] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | The favored path belongs to people of guidance, uprightness, obedience to God and His messengers, and compliance with commands and prohibitions.
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r12] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Those subject to anger know the truth but turn away; the astray lack knowledge and wander without reaching the truth.
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r13] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | The added negation marks two distinct corrupt routes, those of the Jews and Christians.
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r14] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · بعض النحاة | reports | Some grammarians take ghayr as an exception, making it a disconnected exception because the angered are outside the favored group; Ibn Kathir says his own account is better.
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r15] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | prefers | Ibn Kathir prefers construing ghayr as “the path other than that of the angered,” with the omitted noun supplied by context.
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r16] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | The Nabigha al-Dhubyani line illustrates omitting the described noun and retaining its adjective; here “path” is omitted before “other than the angered.”
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r17] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · بعض النحاة | reports | Some grammarians call la before the astray redundant and cite an al-ʿAjjaj line; Ibn Kathir rejects this and treats it as reinforcing the negation.
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r18] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · عمر بن الخطاب | reports | ʿUmar read “and other than the astray”; Ibn Kathir says this report has a sound chain and treats it as explanatory, supporting emphatic negation and separation of the two paths.
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r19] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · أبي بن كعب | reports | A similar reading is attributed to Ubayy ibn Kaʿb; Ibn Kathir understands it as explanatory rather than as the verse's wording.
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r2] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | The favored people are the four groups named in 4:69: prophets, the truthful, martyrs, and the righteous. | mentions 4:69, 4:70
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r20] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Jews are characterized by abandoning known truth and thus anger, while Christians seek a goal without knowledge of its way because they do not follow the true Messenger; each group is also both astray and under anger.
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r21] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | The Qurʾan calls the Jews recipients of anger and specifically describes Christians as astray, supporting the verse's characteristic labels. | mentions 5:60, 5:77
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r22] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Qurʾanic references to Jewish anger and wrongdoing support the identification: Israel's children incur anger for rejecting revelation and are condemned for sin and failure to stop wrongdoing. | mentions 2:90, 5:60, 5:78, 5:79
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r23] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | In the report from ʿAdi ibn Hatim, the Prophet identifies the angered as Jews and the astray as Christians; al-Tirmidhi grades this route hasan gharīb, and Ibn Kathir notes other routes and wordings.
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r24] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | At Wadi al-Qura the Prophet points to Jews as the angered and Christians as the astray; other transmitters send the report without naming the eyewitness, while ʿUrwa's route names Ibn ʿUmar.
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r25] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · أبو ذر | reports | A report from Abu Dharr gives the same Prophetic explanation; Ibn Hajar calls Ibn Mardawayh's chain hasan.
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r26] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · السدي | reports | Al-Suddi, transmitting from Ibn ʿAbbas, Ibn Masʿud, and Companions of the Prophet, identifies the Jews as angered and Christians as astray.
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r27] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbas, through al-Dahhak and Ibn Jurayj, gives the same identification; al-Rabiʿ ibn Anas, ʿAbd al-Rahman ibn Zayd, and others agree, and Ibn Abi Hatim reports no disagreement among exegetes.
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r28] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | A report about Zayd ibn ʿAmr says he refused Jewish or Christian conversion because each demanded a share in divine anger, remained on his natural disposition and avoided idols, while his companions became Christians; Waraqa later accepted the Prophet's revelation.
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r29] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Scholars may excuse confusing dād and ẓāʾ because their articulation points are close and both are voiced, fricative, and emphatic; the saying “I am the most eloquent to pronounce dād” has no basis.
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r3] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · ابن عباس | reports | In al-Dahhak's report, the favored path is obedience and worship, including angels, prophets, the truthful, martyrs, and righteous; Ibn Kathir calls this the broadest account. | mentions 4:69
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r30] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | The surah praises and magnifies God through His names and attributes, recalls the afterlife, teaches supplication, disavowal of human power, sincere worship and divine oneness, and asks for guidance and steadfastness on the upright religion.
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r31] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | The requested straight path leads to crossing the eschatological bridge and reaching Paradise with prophets, the truthful, martyrs, and righteous; the surah urges good deeds and warns against false paths.
[IBNKATHIR:1:7/r32] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | The verse attributes favor directly to God, leaves the agent of anger unexpressed although God is its true agent, and assigns misguidance to those who embody it although He misleads by His decree; cited verses affirm His exclusive control of guidance and misguidance. | mentions 58:14, 18:17, 7:186
