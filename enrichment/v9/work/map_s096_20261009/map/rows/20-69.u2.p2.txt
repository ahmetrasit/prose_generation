20:69/q29 What general principle does the defeat of magic illustrate? [* · interpretation]
  20:69/q29/p1 Truth eventually erases falsehood; magic cannot overcome divine truth. — reasons: Tabatabai cites 13:17, 17:81, 42:24, 28:50 and 61:5. Islahi says magic deceives sight and imagination. Bursevi's Najmiyya citation contrasts God's making with human craft; Study Quran links Moses' words to 10:77.
  20:69/q29/p2 Illusory magic cannot yield genuine lasting happiness. — reasons: Tabatabai calls its object unreal; Bursevi distinguishes temporary worldly advancement from lasting success, using the example of a materially successful student who remains a loser.
20:69/q30 What was the wider setting and purpose of the contest? [* · interpretation]
  20:69/q30/p1 Pharaoh used influential magic against Moses' leadership; the contest established his prophethood. — reasons: Kuran Yolu describes Israelite presence and return aspirations as political anxiety, elites' pressure and rewards for magicians, magic's social-religious force, and Pharaoh's expectation of victory. God's miracles supplied another test; Moses' superiority was more than a contest win.
20:69/q31 How is this account connected to other passages? [* · links]
  20:69/q31/p1 It is compared with parallel Quranic and Biblical accounts. — reasons: Study Quran cites 7:117–119, 10:77 and 20:89; Kuran Yolu directs comparison with 7:103–126 and the Biblical account. Ibn al-Jawzi refers talqamu to 7:117; Alusi says 7:117 gives the command by meaning.
20:69/q32 What inward sense is given to the staff and magic? [* · inward]
  20:69/q32/p1 The staff is truth-lit intellect consuming decorated false doubts. — reasons: Qashani says the intellect is controlled and joined to holy light; sorcery is deceptive embellishment.
  20:69/q32/p2 God's making and firm design overcome human craft. — reasons: Bursevi reports al-Ta'wilat al-Najmiyya's contrast of the staff as God's making with the magicians' handiwork.
20:69/q33 Is this verse reported as a remedy for sorcery? [* · reports]
  20:69/q33/p1 It is recited with other verses over water as a cure. — reasons: Layth says he heard that 10:81, 7:117–122 and 20:69 are recited over vessel water poured on a bewitched person's head.
20:69/q34 What recitation and assimilation details are reported? [* · readings]
  20:69/q34/p1 كَيْدُ سَٰحِرٍ belongs to a sīn assimilation inventory. — reasons: Ibn al-Jazari says al-Dani omitted it by oversight. Authorities discuss showing movement in idgham through rawm or ishmam; Abu al-Karam cites this phrase. Abu Ahmad's mīm gesture is read as rawm. With a preceding weak quiescent letter madd lengths differ; with a sound one, both full idgham and ikhfa or rawm are valid, without obligatory izhar.
  20:69/q34/p2 Al-Bazzi doubles the initial tāʾ in connected recitation. — reasons: NASHR lists this among 31 future verbs and records Ibn Dhakwan's raised fāʾ and Hafs's light qāf here. Ibn Khalawayh also reports Ibn Kathir's doubled initial tāʾ here among more than thirty similar forms.
  20:69/q34/p3 The al-Naba occurrence follows its verse-ending pattern. — reasons: NASHR mentions this nearby agreement.
20:69/q35 How does the public miracle lead to knowledge of the Creator? [* · theology]
  20:69/q35/p1 Its instant creation and shrinking of a huge animal supply public causal premises pointing to a governing Creator; conviction also requires God to create the premises, their ordering, and the conclusion in people's hearts. — reasons: Razi says observers could infer a world-governing Creator because events require causes and humans cannot produce this transformation. He says deniers knew the premises but chose ignorance; reasoning succeeds only when God creates the premises, awareness of their order, and conclusions by divine decree.
