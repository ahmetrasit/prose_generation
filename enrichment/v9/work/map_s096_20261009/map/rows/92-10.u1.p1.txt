92:10/q10 What do the reports teach about written destiny and human action? [* · theology] (turns on: Whether creation for a destined course entails particular deeds, and how divine knowledge relates to the command to act.)
  92:10/q10/p1 Each destination and its deeds are decreed, yet people must act; each is enabled for the deeds appointed to them. — reasons: ʿAlī's funeral report locates the exchange at Baqīʿ; variants name a Bedouin, two youths, Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, Jābir, Surāqa, and Abū al-Dardāʾ. The youths answer that they will strive. Al-Tirmidhī grades a version ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ; Ibn Kathīr says Aḥmad alone has the Abū al-Dardāʾ route. Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān's question follows 54:49. Al-Rāzī says the answer fits God's unchangeable knowledge; al-Biqāʿī says the blessed and wretched were designated eternally.
  92:10/q10/p2 The hadith's creation purpose is worship, so it does not establish fixed evil deeds. — reasons: Al-Qaffāl cites 51:56, that jinn and humans were created only to worship. Al-Rāzī rejects this answer as weak against the Prophet's instruction to act according to God's unchangeable knowledge.
  92:10/q10/p3 Written destiny denotes God's exact prior knowledge; deeds through death reveal it and are the means to one's end. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes revealed commands from hidden knowledge. He says righteous action signals a good end; the opposite completes the comparison. Al-Alūsī says servants should perform their duties and leave lordship's hidden matters to God. Ibn ʿĀshūr offers two connections for the recitation: it explains facilitation or supports the command to act.
  92:10/q10/p4 The verses are used by the Prophet when describing Paradise and urging work. — reasons: Al-Burhān notes the Prophet paired al-Layl with the call to work, 32:17 with unseen delight, and 56:30 with a tree's long shade.
92:10/q11 What report connects giving and withholding with this passage? [* · reports]
  92:10/q11/p1 Angels pray for replacement for each giver and loss for each withholder. — reasons: Abū al-Dardāʾ transmits the Prophet's saying: two angels call at sunrise, or morning and evening in al-Qurṭubī, and the giving and withholding verses are recited. Al-Māwardī gives a Qatāda–Khulayd–Abū al-Dardāʾ route without grading.
92:10/q12 What ethical or legal point is drawn from this contrast? [* · law]
  92:10/q12/p1 Generosity is praiseworthy and miserliness blameworthy when giving is due. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī cites 2:3 and 2:274. He defines generosity by giving where appropriate and miserliness by withholding what is due; praise, blame, reward, and punishment determine the judgment. Ibn ʿĀshūr says giving is singled out to urge Muslims to give, distinguishing them from polytheists.
  92:10/q12/p2 Blameworthy, unrewarded spending is waste and may justify legal restraint. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī calls such spenders brothers of devils and subject to interdiction.
  92:10/q12/p3 Prudent withholding can merit praise and qualify a person to manage others' property. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī distinguishes restraint that brings no blame or punishment from miserliness.
  92:10/q12/p4 Since worldly ease and next-world hardship both belong to God, people should follow His commands. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī notes that ease and hardship can occur in either life.
92:10/q13 How does the verse relate to its surrounding verses and the sūra? [* · links] (turns on: Whether the connection concerns the mirrored clauses, the later Fire image, the preceding sūra, or other verses.)
  92:10/q13/p1 It completes the second half of a structured contrast between conduct and recompense. — reasons: Al-Irshād says giving and withholding lead their clauses because they ground subsequent piety, belief, denial, or self-sufficiency. Ibn ʿĀshūr names combination with division and four antitheses. Corpus Coranicum notes mirrored conduct-to-result sequences, cross-rhyme, near-homophonous ease/hardship, and the first-person plural identifying God's promised recompense; its final stanza reverses the order. Neuwirth places verse 11 with verses 12–13.
  92:10/q13/p2 The later Fire warning makes the earlier difficult outcome explicit. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ compares 92:14's blazing Fire; Corpus Coranicum says verse 14 resolves the enigmatic threat of verse 10.
  92:10/q13/p3 The sūra unfolds the preceding sūra's success and failure through the traits leading to Fire or safety. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān cites 91:9–10 and the later warning.
  92:10/q13/p4 The ease/hardship pairing echoes other Qur'anic promises and contrasts. — reasons: Corpus Coranicum compares 94:5–6 and the messenger-directed promise in 87:8. Ibn ʿĀshūr also notes that the recited verse can explain the hadith's facilitation or support its command to act.
92:10/q14 What does the contrasting easy course denote? [* · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the easier counterpart means a beneficial condition generally or Paradise specifically.)
  92:10/q14/p1 It is a good and pleasing condition, broadly what benefits or suits a person without hardship. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya places the condition in this life and the next; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the feminine form may mark an afterlife condition or place and broadens the sense to what suits its possessor.
  92:10/q14/p2 It denotes Paradise. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this from Zayd ibn Aslam and Mujāhid.
92:10/q15 Why does the contrast use divine plural speech for both paths? [* · rhetoric]
  92:10/q15/p1 The majestic plural marks the remoteness of either course. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says good is surrounded by hardship, while reason and original nature check evil.
92:10/q16 What conduct characterizes the miser in this contrast? [* · interpretation]
  92:10/q16/p1 He withholds God's due, considers himself self-sufficient, and denies the promise. — reasons: Al-Durr reports this explanation from Qatāda, paired with the giver who pays God's due, avoids prohibitions, and believes the promise.
