[TABATABAI:19:64-65#3/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | prefers | Samiyy means one sharing the name Lord, because the argument establishes God’s absolute lordship over everything.
[TABATABAI:19:64-65#3/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | rejects | The author rejects glosses of samiyy as a figurative equal, a child, or as a shared proper name meaning God, Allah, deity, al-Rahman, or the creator and giver of life and death.
[TABATABAI:19:64-65#3/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Repeatedly addressing the angels’ speaker as your Lord prepares the declaration of oneness: the angels’ Lord is the Prophet’s Lord, so the call to worship is His; the surah’s opening has the same coherent aim.
[TABATABAI:19:64-65#3/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Worship and perseverance express unity of worship; remaining constant in worship is implied by, rather than a separate sense beyond, the command to worship.
[TABATABAI:19:64-65#4/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The proposed continuation makes the two verses confirm the preceding revelation: Gabriel was commanded to deliver the surah and then clarify that he descended only by God’s command, whose call is to worship the sole Lord.
[TABATABAI:19:64-65#4/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | prefers | The king-and-messenger analogy shows how Gabriel’s explanatory words remain a message from God; this reading fits the verses better and more clearly than varying, weak occasion reports.
[TABATABAI:19:64-65/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | These two verses interrupt the surrounding passage, yet their Qur’anic style indicates that they are Gabriel’s speech revealed by God; this supports reports that the Prophet asked why Gabriel’s coming was delayed and received this reply by revelation.
[TABATABAI:19:64-65/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · ابن عباس | reports | Reports, including one attributed to Ibn ʿAbbas in al-Majmaʿ, say the Prophet found Gabriel’s revelation delayed and asked him about it.
[TABATABAI:19:64-65/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · بعضهم | rejects | The author rejects proposed links that insert an implied statement by Gabriel, connect the verses to Gabriel’s earlier words to Mary, or put them in the mouths of the pious in Paradise; context and eloquence disfavor them.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p560/r1] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas glosses samiyyan as a son, supported by a poetic line.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p560/r2] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · author | rejects | Bint al-Shati notes that although the Qur'an uses walad and awlād forty-six times, she finds no reading of samiyy as son in either Maryam verse. | mentions 19:7
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p560/r3] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · author | holds | In Arabic, one's samiyy is a namesake or counterpart; the related verbs can mean matching or competing, while simah is a mark and ism a distinguishing name. | mentions 19:7
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p560/r4] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · author | holds | The word family appears widely: ism twenty-seven times, asmāʾ twelve, tasmiyah once, its verb forms eight times, and the passive participle musammā twenty-one times. | mentions 53:23
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p560/r5] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · الطبري | reports | Al-Tabari, citing Ibn Abbas and others, interprets the question as asking whether God has an equal or likeness; none exists, and all creation acknowledges him as sole creator.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p560/r6] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · الطبري | reports | The cited interpretation denies any partner or peer for God and says every creature recognizes him as its creator.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p561/r4] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib reads samiyyan as a counterpart who truly deserves God’s name and attributes, not merely someone sharing a name.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p561/r5] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · الراغب | holds | Al-Raghib says divine names may be used of people, but their meanings differ when applied to God.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p561/r6] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · author | holds | The author suggests al-Raghib alludes to names such as ʿAli, ʿAziz, Raʾuf, and Karim, often used as shortened forms of names beginning with ʿAbd.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p561/r7] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · author | holds | The author notes that early Muslims also used honorifics such as al-ʿAli bi-Allah and al-Zahir bi-amr Allah.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p497/r6] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Gabriel’s speech urges the Prophet to wait steadfastly for God’s decree and not hurry.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p532/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Gabriel’s words urge patience and clarify that he and the angels have no independent agency: they only follow God’s commands.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p532/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Because revelation is the Prophet’s support in conflict, he naturally awaits Gabriel anxiously; like a soldier awaiting guidance, he must wait for help at its appointed time, as Qur’anic instruction elsewhere also teaches. | mentions 20:114
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p533/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | prefers | The final question may be Gabriel’s speech or a divine insertion, but the meaning is unchanged; Islahi prefers that Gabriel is speaking because the Prophet is watched over by the Lord of all creation.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p533/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Worship here has its wider sense of obedience, as it does in 19:44. | mentions 19:44
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p533/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The emphatic form of the word for perseverance magnifies the meaning; with the preposition lām, it also conveys waiting, nuances difficult to preserve in translation.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p533/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Because God has no peer, no one can thwart his intentions; the Prophet should continue his work and God will ease his hardships and accomplish his purposes.
