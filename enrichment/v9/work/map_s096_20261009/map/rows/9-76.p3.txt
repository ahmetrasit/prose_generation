[TABATABAI:9:75-77/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | One group of poor hypocrites promised to give charity and be righteous if enriched, then became miserly and turned away when God gave them wealth; other hypocrites criticized both affluent and poor believers who donated, and God judged them all hypocrites and beyond forgiveness.
[TABATABAI:9:75-77/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | The context presents a real event, and the reports say these verses were revealed about Tha'laba; the author defers the story to the following report discussion and says the verses' meaning is clear.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:9:75/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | rejects | The commonly reported occasions of revelation for the first two verses conflict with historical evidence.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:9:75/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The passage exemplifies contradictions among people whose faith cannot discipline their conduct and points to hypocrisy in its context and timing.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:9:75/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Hz. Peygamber | reports | Prophetic hadiths say deeds depend on intentions and that someone earns reward for intending good but being unable to do it.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:9:75/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Hypocrites who turn to God for self-interest, promise charity if favored, then become miserly and turn away lose responsibility and cannot be trusted.
[STUDYQURAN:9:75/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Like other descriptions of hypocrites in this sūrah, these verses are understood generally.
[STUDYQURAN:9:75/r2] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | Some commentators connect the passage with Thaʿlabah ibn Ḥāṭib, who promised charity if wealthy, prospered after receiving spoils, moved away, gradually stopped congregational and Friday prayers, then refused zakāh as jizyah; the Prophet rebuked him.
