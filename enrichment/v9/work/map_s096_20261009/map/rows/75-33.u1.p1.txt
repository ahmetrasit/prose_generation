75:33/q16 How does the swaggering back relate to the threatened calamity? [* · links]
  75:33/q16/p1 The fāqira that breaks the spine fits the back he used to twist proudly. — reasons: Al-Samarra'i says this makes the punishment more fitting than a generic calamity.
75:33/q17 How does this scene answer the earlier question about resurrection? [* · links]
  75:33/q17/p1 The man asks when resurrection will come yet prepares only for ruin. — reasons: Al-Tibi calls it a wondrous contrast with 75:6–7: the intervening passage answers him and the revelation instruction is a digression. Al-Qaffal allows the ensuing divine words to be a command for Muhammad to warn the enemy of an unbearable near misfortune.
75:33/q18 What other Quranic scenes are compared with this return? [* · links]
  75:33/q18/p1 Other passages show the arrogant or mocking returning happily to their people. — reasons: Ibn Kathir compares al-Mutaffifin 83:34 and al-Inshiqaq 84:13–15. Study Quran compares mockers who wink, return blithely to their people and call believers astray (83:30–32; 2:14; 84:13–14). Islahi points to 83:31 and contrasts believers' fear for their family in 52:26; he also cites the boastful orchard owner in 18:35.
75:33/q19 How does this figure compare with those in Sura Muhammad? [* · links]
  75:33/q19/p1 The Qiyama figure deserves a stronger threat because his denial and strut are overt. — reasons: Al-Samarra'i says the people in Sura Muhammad (47:20) might outwardly believe and pray, whereas this figure denies, turns away and then struts.
