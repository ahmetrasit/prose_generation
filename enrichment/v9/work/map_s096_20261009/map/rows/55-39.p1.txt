[RAZI-FULL:v29p104#2/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | To resolve the pronoun objection, one can treat “his sin” as an explicit noun phrase meaning a sinner’s sin, without a referential pronoun.

[RAZI-FULL:v29p104#2/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | The initial “then” may mark temporal succession after the sky splits, immediate logical succession as punishment starts without time for questioning, or rhetorical order after the failed attempt to escape through the heavens.

[RAZI-FULL:v29p104#3/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | “Not asked” may mean no inquiry to identify the guilty, no rebuke asking why a sinner acted, or no request for pardon and intercession on another’s behalf.
[RAZI-FULL:v29p104#3/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | rejects | The intercession reading is challenged because “ask about” normally marks inquiry or reproach, while asking someone to grant something takes a direct object.
[RAZI-FULL:v29p104#3/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | In reply, a verb of asking can take two objects; with inquiry the second is omitted and the following prepositional phrase states what information was requested.
[RAZI-FULL:v29p104#3/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The intercession reading can construe each human as not asking about another human’s sin; the pronoun is attached to the grammatical subject as in “they killed themselves,” though it does not mean each killed himself.
[RAZI-FULL:v29p104#3/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | On that construal, no human or jinn is asked to pardon another; all petition God, and God alone is the one asked at that time.
[RAZI-FULL:v29p104#3/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | An objection to the intercession reading is that the next verse’s statement that criminals are recognized by their marks does not fit it; al-Rāzī has supplied the grammatical and interpretive reply above.
