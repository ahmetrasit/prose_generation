[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:74:11/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The passage’s general lesson is that gratitude to the giver of blessings is a basic moral duty grounded in reason and justice.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:74:11/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Failing to thank God for life and material and spiritual gifts is grave ingratitude, especially when it advances denial, polytheism, and injustice.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p821/r2] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | The singular form is used for divine oneness or another context, such as an individual punishment.
[STUDYQURAN:74:11/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The passage is commonly believed to target al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīrah, a determined opponent of the Prophet in Mecca.
[STUDYQURAN:74:11/r2] STUDYQURAN · Ibn ʿAbbās | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās relates that al-Walīd’s heart softened after hearing the Prophet recite; Abū Jahl pressured him to denounce Muhammad, and al-Walīd praised the Quran’s unlike-poetry, sweet, abundant speech before branding it inherited sorcery, prompting this passage.
[STUDYQURAN:74:11/r3] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | One reading takes “alone” as God, emphasizing His uniqueness as Creator.
[STUDYQURAN:74:11/r4] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | Alternatively, the human being begins and ends life alone before God. | mentions 6:94, 19:80, 19:95
[STUDYQURAN:74:11/r5] STUDYQURAN · Bg | reports | Al-Waḥīd is also reported as an epithet of al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīrah.
