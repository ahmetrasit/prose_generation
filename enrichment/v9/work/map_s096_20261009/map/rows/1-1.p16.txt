[TABRISI:1:1#3/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · الأخفش وأبو علي الفارسي | reports | They analyze the basmala as an implicit nominal clause, with the prepositional phrase filling the place of its omitted predicate.
[TABRISI:1:1#3/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو علي الفارسي | rejects | Abu Ali rejects attaching bā to the hidden verbal noun ibtidaʾ because this would leave the implied subject without a predicate.
[TABRISI:1:1#3/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو عمرو الجرمي | reports | The bā is kasra because it must be pronounceable at the start of speech; kasra was chosen as the appropriate connecting vowel.
[TABRISI:1:1#3/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو علي الحسن بن عبد الغفار الفارسي | reports | Abu Ali al-Farsi says any vowel would have served for starting the word; some Arabs use fatḥa, though the author calls that dialect weak.
[TABRISI:1:1#3/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The hamzat al-waṣl of ism drops in connected pronunciation and is omitted in writing here because the basmala is frequent and unambiguous, unlike the less frequent Qur'anic form in 96:1. | mentions 96:1
[TABRISI:1:1#4/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | God's lām is pronounced emphatically after fatḥa or ḍamma to glorify Him and distinguish the name from al-Lāt.
[TABRISI:1:1#4/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Allah is genitive by annexation, and Rahman and Rahim are genitive as adjectives of Allah.
[TABRISI:1:1#4/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | One interpretation makes the basmala an appeal for aid through God's beautiful names and exalted attributes; another simply means seek help from God.
[TABRISI:1:1#4/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو عبيدة | reports | Abu Ubayda treats the word ism as an extra connective and takes the basmala to mean Allah Himself, as in Labid's verse.
[TABRISI:1:1#4/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | A name can stand for its referent because speech or address attached to the name is in reality attached to the named thing.
[TABRISI:1:1#4/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | prefers | The preferred reading is begin by naming God: the command teaches opening actions with God's name, as in eating, drinking, and slaughter, rather than merely reporting His majesty.
[TABRISI:1:1#4/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | God alone merits worship because He can create bodies, give life, and bestow blessings; defining deity only by earned benefaction wrongly excludes God before creation.
[TABRISI:1:1#4/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Rahman precedes Rahim because Rahman functions like a proper name exclusive to God, while Rahim can describe others too.
[TABRISI:1:1#5/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · عيسى ابن مريم | reports | A prophetic report quotes Jesus as saying Rahman is merciful in this world and Rahim is merciful in the afterlife.
[TABRISI:1:1#5/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · بعض التابعين | reports | Some Successors say Rahman embraces all creation while Rahim is especially for believers; the author explains this by creation and provision for all and guidance, Paradise, honor, and forgiveness for believers.
[TABRISI:1:1#5/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · الصادق | reports | Al-Sadiq formulates the distinction as a special name with a general attribute for Rahman, and a general name with a special attribute for Rahim.
[TABRISI:1:1#5/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · عكرمة | reports | Ikrima says Rahman signifies one mercy and Rahim a hundred mercies.
[TABRISI:1:1#5/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · النبي | reports | The Prophet says God has one hundred mercies, sent one to earth for mutual compassion and reserved ninety-nine to show mercy on Judgment Day.
[TABRISI:1:1#5/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Another report says God will take the earthly mercy back and complete it to one hundred to show mercy to His servants on Judgment Day.
[TABRISI:1:1/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · أصحابنا | holds | The author's companions hold that the basmala is a verse of al-Fatiha and every sura; omitting it invalidates obligatory and supererogatory prayer, and they favor audible recitation.
[TABRISI:1:1/r10] TABRISI, d. 548 · سيبويه | reports | On this analysis the alif in lah comes from yā; Sibawayh explains a transposition of the letters and cites lahi as evidence.
[TABRISI:1:1/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · حمزة وخلف ويعقوب واليزيدي | reports | These readers do not use the basmala to separate suras; the other readers do, except between al-Anfal and al-Tawba.
[TABRISI:1:1/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · علي بن موسى الرضا | reports | Al-Rida says the basmala is nearer to God's Greatest Name than the black of the eye is to its white.
[TABRISI:1:1/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · النبي | reports | The Prophet promises absolution for a child, the child's parents, and the teacher when the child learns and recites the basmala.
[TABRISI:1:1/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masud says reciting the basmala protects a person from the nineteen keepers of Hell, one letter serving as protection from each.
[TABRISI:1:1/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · الصادق | reports | Al-Sadiq condemns those who call the basmala an innovation when they recite it aloud, calling it the greatest verse in God's Book.
[TABRISI:1:1/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | prefers | The author prefers deriving ism from sumu, elevation: its original waw is supported by its plural asma and diminutive sumayy.
[TABRISI:1:1/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Allah is an exclusive divine name and is not applied to anyone besides God.
[TABRISI:1:1/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · سيبويه | reports | One analysis derives Allah from lah; the article marks glorification and exaltation, not definiteness, since God's names are already definite.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p7/r1] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن عمر | reports | Ibn ʿUmar says the basmala was revealed in every sūra.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p7/r10] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ records forms with smah among some Qays speakers and some Quḍāʿa speakers, citing a line that uses the form.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p7/r11] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | Qarḍāb means a cutter, and a sword can be called qarḍāb.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p7/r12] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · العلماء | reports | Scholars disagree whether the divine name Allāh is derived or an underived proper name.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p7/r13] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · الخليل | reports | One view attributed to al-Khalīl says Allāh is not derived and its alif-lām cannot be dropped as it can from al-Raḥmān.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p7/r14] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · الخليل عن سيبويه | reports | A second report from al-Khalīl, transmitted by Sībawayh, holds that Allāh is derived.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p7/r15] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · أبو سليمان الخطابي | reports | Al-Khaṭṭābī transmits an etymology from a-l-h, meaning to seek refuge; God is so named as the one who protects and grants safety.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p7/r16] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · غيره | reports | Another etymology derives Allāh from wālah, with initial wāw changed into hamza, as in wisāda and wishāḥ becoming ishāḥ.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p7/r2] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · العلماء | reports | Scholars disagree whether the basmala is a complete verse; Aḥmad has two transmitted positions.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p7/r3] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · العلماء | reports | Scholars also disagree whether it is part of al-Fātiḥa; two positions are transmitted from Aḥmad.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p7/r4] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · القائلون إنها من الفاتحة | reports | Those who count it as part of al-Fātiḥa require its recitation in prayer because al-Fātiḥa itself is obligatory.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p7/r5] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · القائلون إنها ليست من الفاتحة | reports | Those who do not count it as part of al-Fātiḥa regard reciting it in prayer as sunnah, while Mālik does not recommend its recitation.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p7/r6] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · أحمد ومن وافقه | reports | Aḥmad and the listed Companions and early authorities do not regard reciting the basmala aloud in audible prayers as recommended.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p7/r7] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · الشافعي | reports | Al-Shāfiʿī recommends reciting the basmala aloud; this view is also reported from Muʿāwiya, ʿAṭāʾ, Ṭāwūs, and Mujāhid.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p7/r8] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | Bismillāh is elliptical for I begin or I began in God's name.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p7/r9] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | The word ism has five reported forms, including ism, asm, sim, sum, and samā; the cited poetry witnesses these forms.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p9/r1] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · بعضهم | reports | Some derive the divine name from bewilderment: hearts are bewildered when contemplating God's greatness.

[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p9/r12] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | Al-Fātiḥa is so named because the book begins with it in recitation and writing; Umm al-Qur'an and Umm al-Kitāb because it leads the book; al-sabʿ al-mathānī is explained later in 15:87. | mentions 15:87
