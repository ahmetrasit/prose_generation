[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:228/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | A pregnant divorcee’s iddat ends at childbirth, a woman who does not menstruate counts months, and a menstruating woman waits three qurūʾ. | mentions 65:4
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:228/r40] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | These sound reports show the major changes in relations between women and men that occurred quickly through Islam’s revolution.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:228/r41] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · eski müfessir ve müctehidler | reports | Earlier exegetes and jurists explained men’s one-degree surplus by men’s physical strength, superior intellect, and stronger will.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:228/r42] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Because men are physically stronger, differences in rights and duties that rest on physical strength are natural.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:228/r43] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Hz. Peygamber | reports | The claim of men’s greater intellect was based on a hadith saying that women described as deficient in reason and religion can prevail over men.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:228/r44] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | rejects | The hadith’s purpose is not to explain an intellectual difference between women and men.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:228/r45] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Hz. Peygamber | reports | When women asked the Prophet what the hadith meant, he identified deficient reason with requiring two female witnesses for one male witness, and deficient religion with not praying or fasting during menstruation. | mentions 2:282
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:228/r46] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Women do not pray during menstruation and later make up fasting; this exemption cannot be a negative deficiency in religion.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:228/r47] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Here “religion” means obligation, so deficiency in religion means being exempt from an obligation.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:228/r48] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The hadith describes a fact and warns women not to misuse the ability to influence and beguile men despite the stated conditions.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:228/r49] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The author does not deny psychological differences, including differences in will, or the physiological and biological differences between the sexes.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:228/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Qurʾ in Arabic denotes the transition boundary from menstruation to purity or from purity to menstruation, explaining juristic disagreement over its sense.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:228/r50] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | With exceptions, men generally bear the heavy burden and primary role of providing the household’s livelihood.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:228/r51] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Men’s one-degree surplus in rights rests on these fundamental and enduring differences.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:228/r52] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The additional right is qiwāmah, the right of protection and management explained in Surat al-Nisa. | mentions 4:34
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:228/r53] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Eski fıkıh âlimleri | reports | Earlier jurists unanimously accepted men’s precedence in family and state leadership, while differing in other legal and governance areas.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:228/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · hayız mânası veren müctehidler | reports | Some jurists understand qurʾ as menstruation, hayḍ.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:228/r7] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · temizlik mânası veren müctehidler | reports | Other jurists understand qurʾ as purity, tuhr.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:228/r8] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | A divorce conforming to the Sunnah takes place only while the woman is in a state of purity.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:228/r9] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · temizlik mânası veren müctehidler | reports | On the purity interpretation, the purity days in which divorce occurs count as one qurʾ, shortening iddat.
[QIRAAT-ER:2:228:43#1/r1] QIRAAT-ER · vhr | reports | The pronoun suffix is read with either kasra or ḍamma, a dialectal phonological variant without semantic difference.
[QIRAAT-ER:2:228:49#1/r1] QIRAAT-ER · vhr | reports | The suffix again has a kasra versus ḍamma reading, representing dialectal pronunciation without a semantic change.
[STUDYQURAN:2:228/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | In ṭalāq, the husband declares divorce and the wife waits three courses before the divorce becomes final; they abstain from sex during that period.
[STUDYQURAN:2:228/r10] STUDYQURAN · others | reports | Another interpretation gives women a right to sexual relations comparable to men's.
[STUDYQURAN:2:228/r11] STUDYQURAN · Ibn ʿAbbās | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās said he beautified himself for his wife as she beautified herself for him.
[STUDYQURAN:2:228/r12] STUDYQURAN · others | reports | Another reading frames mutual rights as protection from harm: husbands should not trap wives in repeated divorce and reconciliation, and wives should not hide a pregnancy.
[STUDYQURAN:2:228/r13] STUDYQURAN · others | reports | Others see marriage as shared closeness, love, and peace; al-Rāzī adds that a woman's share may be greater.
[STUDYQURAN:2:228/r14] STUDYQURAN · al-Rāzī | reports | Al-Rāzī says one could argue that the woman's share in marriage is more abundant.
[STUDYQURAN:2:228/r15] STUDYQURAN · many commentators | reports | Many commentators understand men's degree as noblesse oblige, a responsibility to exercise their advantage for women's benefit.
[STUDYQURAN:2:228/r16] STUDYQURAN · Ibn ʿAbbās | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās interprets the degree as men's promoting good relations and generosity in wealth and virtuous conduct; greater power requires self-restraint.
[STUDYQURAN:2:228/r17] STUDYQURAN · others | reports | Others connect men's degree with economic advantage, obligations, and the ability to fight in God's way, while noting some female Companions fought too.
[STUDYQURAN:2:228/r18] STUDYQURAN · others | reports | Others attribute men's degree to traits such as strength and intelligence.
[STUDYQURAN:2:228/r19] STUDYQURAN · Aj | reports | Spiritually, the soul has rights owed to it and rights it owes; the spirit governs the soul, yet spiritual progress occurs through it, so it should be treated gently and allowed rest.
[STUDYQURAN:2:228/r2] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | One reported interpretation counts each qurʾ as the menstrual flow itself.
[STUDYQURAN:2:228/r3] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | Another reported interpretation counts each qurʾ from the cessation of menstrual flow.
[STUDYQURAN:2:228/r4] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | A third interpretation counts the ritual washing that permits the wife to pray.
[STUDYQURAN:2:228/r5] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | A divorced woman must not conceal a possible pregnancy from the husband who divorced her.
[STUDYQURAN:2:228/r6] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Because husbands initiated this form of divorce, they may restore the marriage before the waiting period ends.
[STUDYQURAN:2:228/r7] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | A wife's divorce options fall under khulʿ rather than ṭalāq, as discussed at 2:229 and 4:20. | mentions 2:229, 4:20
[STUDYQURAN:2:228/r8] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The bracketed words The women supply the feminine plural pronoun in the translation.
[STUDYQURAN:2:228/r9] STUDYQURAN · some commentators | reports | One interpretation is reciprocal duties: wives obey husbands, while husbands treat wives well and respectfully.
