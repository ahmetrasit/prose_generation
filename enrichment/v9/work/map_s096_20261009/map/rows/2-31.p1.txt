[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايبينونحركتهوماقبلهمتحرك)#731/r11] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In majīʾ ma jiʾta and mithl ma anta, pause hāʾ is obligatory because majīʾ and mithl are independent nouns; unlike the clipped particles, the first element is a noun.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايبينونحركتهوماقبلهمتحرك)#731/r12] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · العرب | reports | A pause hāʾ may follow the weak alif in hāʾulāʾ and hahunā for clarity; it is avoided with inflected nouns such as afʿā and aʿmā, where it could be confused with possessive hāʾ.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايبينونحركتهوماقبلهمتحرك)#731/r13] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · العرب | reports | Hāʾ is not attached after a preceding sukūn; many Arabs also omit it in pause when the ending is clear from context.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايبينونحركتهوماقبلهمتحرك)#731/r14] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Vocatives and lament forms add hāʾ after long alif, wāw, or yāʾ in pause to sustain and clarify the call, then drop it in connected speech.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايبينونحركتهوماقبلهمتحرك)#731/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Speakers who make the yāʾ quiescent do not add hāʾ in pause, since nothing has been deleted from the connected form.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايبينونحركتهوماقبلهمتحرك)#731/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · العرب | reports | Some forms add hāʾ after Hī and Hū in pause because their final yāʾ and wāw are not inflectional endings and are not left fully quiescent.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايبينونحركتهوماقبلهمتحرك)#731/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The same pause hāʾ appears in forms such as khudh-hu bi-ḥukmika; speakers who omit it with the earlier pronouns omit it here too.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايبينونحركتهوماقبلهمتحرك)#731/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · العرب | reports | Hāʾ and alif can both clarify a weak ending in pause: ḥayhalā becomes ḥayhal bi-ʿAmr in connection, though ḥayhal may also remain.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايبينونحركتهوماقبلهمتحرك)#731/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Anā retains final alif in pause and has no hāʾ variant; its short, hidden nūn and status as a minimal independent word distinguish it from Hū.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايبينونحركتهوماقبلهمتحرك)#731/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | No pause hāʾ is added to a fully inflected ending such as aḥmar, whose final changes for case and accepts the article.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايبينونحركتهوماقبلهمتحرك)#731/r8] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Verbs such as ẓanna and ḍaraba likewise take no pause hāʾ because their final consonants vary with inflection.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايبينونحركتهوماقبلهمتحرك)#731/r9] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Interrogative particles clipped from mā, such as ʿalāma, fīma, lima, bima, and ḥattāma, preferably take hāʾ in pause because deletion leaves a weak ending.
[SIBAWAYH:الذيلميتعدهفعلهإلىمفعولوالمفعولالذيلميتعدإليهفعلفاعلو#16/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Sībawayh frames the discussion around whether a verb's action extends to an object and whether that object receives an action from an agent.
[SIBAWAYH:الذيلميتعدهفعلهإلىمفعولوالمفعولالذيلميتعدإليهفعلفاعلو#16/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Active and passive participles, and verbal nouns, can work like a verb that governs one object.
[SIBAWAYH:الذيلميتعدهفعلهإلىمفعولوالمفعولالذيلميتعدإليهفعلفاعلو#16/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Adjectives and other word forms modeled on verbs may govern similarly, but they do not have the full strength of a verb or its participles.
[SIBAWAYH:وذلكقولكضربعبداللهزيدافعبداللهارتفعههناكماارتفعفيذهب#20/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In ضرب عبد الله زيدا, ʿAbd Allāh is the raised subject as in ذهب, while Zayd is the object governed by the transitive verb.
[SIBAWAYH:وذلكقولكضربعبداللهزيدافعبداللهارتفعههناكماارتفعفيذهب#20/r10] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Spatial distances can be governed like spans of time: ذهبت فرسخين and سرت الميلين parallel ذهبت شهرين and سرت اليومين.
[SIBAWAYH:وذلكقولكضربعبداللهزيدافعبداللهارتفعههناكماارتفعفيذهب#20/r11] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Temporal complements are stronger because verb forms encode past and future, whereas places have no corresponding verb forms derived from them.
[SIBAWAYH:وذلكقولكضربعبداللهزيدافعبداللهارتفعههناكماارتفعفيذهب#20/r12] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Places resemble named entities and physical bodies, while time is the passage of night and day and thus closer to the verbal event.
[SIBAWAYH:وذلكقولكضربعبداللهزيدافعبداللهارتفعههناكماارتفعفيذهب#20/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The object may precede the subject without changing their roles; this is good, common Arabic, used to foreground what matters more to the speaker.
[SIBAWAYH:وذلكقولكضربعبداللهزيدافعبداللهارتفعههناكماارتفعفيذهب#20/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Even an intransitive verb governs its verbal noun because the verb identifies an event but does not specify its kind or manner.
[SIBAWAYH:وذلكقولكضربعبداللهزيدافعبداللهارتفعههناكماارتفعفيذهب#20/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Verbal nouns can specify intensity, manner, number, or a subtype of an event, as in قعدتين and قعد القرفصاء.
[SIBAWAYH:وذلكقولكضربعبداللهزيدافعبداللهارتفعههناكماارتفعفيذهب#20/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Verbs govern temporal expressions because their forms indicate whether an event occurred in past time or will occur in future time.
[SIBAWAYH:وذلكقولكضربعبداللهزيدافعبداللهارتفعههناكماارتفعفيذهب#20/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Temporal nouns may be treated as adverbs or left without that label; verbs can govern them broadly, as they govern event nouns.
[SIBAWAYH:وذلكقولكضربعبداللهزيدافعبداللهارتفعههناكماارتفعفيذهب#20/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Verbs also govern derived place nouns and locations, since an event entails a place even when the speaker does not name it.
[SIBAWAYH:وذلكقولكضربعبداللهزيدافعبداللهارتفعههناكماارتفعفيذهب#20/r8] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | rejects | Sībawayh reports that some speakers say ذهبت الشام by analogy with an indefinite place, but calls this anomalous because ذهب does not select Syria specifically.
[SIBAWAYH:وذلكقولكضربعبداللهزيدافعبداللهارتفعههناكماارتفعفيذهب#20/r9] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | He cites Saʿida ibn Juʾayya's verse about the fox following a path as poetic evidence in the discussion of verbs and spatial complements.
[SIBAWAYH:وهيتجريعلىثمانيمجارعلىالنصبوالجروالرفعوالجزموالفتحوالضم#4/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Sībawayh distinguishes eight inflectional and fixed-ending categories, which collapse into four pairs in pronunciation: nasb/fatḥ, jarr/kasr, rafʿ/ḍamm, and jazm/pause.
[SIBAWAYH:وهيتجريعلىثمانيمجارعلىالنصبوالجروالرفعوالجزموالفتحوالضم#4/r10] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | He illustrates fixed endings on non-declinable nouns with fatḥ, kasr, ḍamm, and pause examples such as حيث, أولاء, قبل, and من.
[SIBAWAYH:وهيتجريعلىثمانيمجارعلىالنصبوالجروالرفعوالجزموالفتحوالضم#4/r11] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Past verbs have fatḥ; their verbal pattern resembles the imperfect, appears as an adjective, and can occupy a conditional clause.
[SIBAWAYH:وهيتجريعلىثمانيمجارعلىالنصبوالجروالرفعوالجزموالفتحوالضم#4/r12] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Imperatives end in pause because they are neither used as adjectives nor occupy the imperfect's syntactic role.

[SIBAWAYH:وهيتجريعلىثمانيمجارعلىالنصبوالجروالرفعوالجزموالفتحوالضم#4/r15] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The dual noun uses an uninflected long-vowel case ending: alif in rafʿ and yāʾ in jarr and nasb, distinguishing it from the sound plural.
