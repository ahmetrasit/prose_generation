1:1/q32 Is the basmala a verse of al-Fātiḥa? [* · sciences] (turns on: Whether a Qurʾānic opening verse belongs to al-Fātiḥa itself or stands outside its verse count.)
  1:1/q32/p1 The basmala is a verse of al-Fātiḥa. — reasons: TAB (عظم أهل الكوفة): Most Kufan authorities count basmala as one of Fātiḥa’s seven verses; also transmitted from Companions and Successors. THALABI (ابن عباس): Ibn ʿAbbās identifies the seven oft-repeated verses as al-Fātiḥa and counts the basmala as its seventh verse. | mentions 15:87 IBNATIYYA (الشافعي): Al-Shafi'i counts the basmala as a verse of al-Fatiha. IBNATIYYA-FULL (author): The Jabir and Abu Hurayra reports would imply that the basmala is a verse of al-Fatiha if sound. RAZI-FULL (author): Al-Tha‘labi transmits a version in which the Prophet counted the basmala as a complete verse of al-Fatiha; when reports conflict, affirmation outweighs omission. QURTUBI (الشافعي): Al-Shāfiʿī counts the basmala in al-Fātiḥa but has two positions about other sūras. BAYDAWI-FULL (أم سلمة): Umm Salama reports that the Prophet counted the basmala as a verse when reciting al-Fātiḥa. IBNKATHIR (أم سلمة): A report from Umm Salama says the Prophet recited and counted the basmala as a verse at the start of the Fatiha; Ibn Kathir notes the chain is weak because of ʿUmar ibn Harun. BURHAN (author): Al-Shafiʿi counts the basmala and makes the rest of 1:7 one verse; Abu Hanifa omits the basmala and divides its two phrases. Both count seven verses, but al-Shafiʿi's division better preserves the endings because anʿamta ʿalayhim does not match prior endings. DURR (النبي): The Prophet says to recite the basmala with al-Hamd, calls al-Fatiha the Mother of the Qur'an and seven oft-repeated verses, and counts the basmala as an ayah. DURR (علي): Ali identifies al-Hamd as the seven oft-repeated verses and counts the basmala as the seventh ayah. DURR (أم سلمة): Umm Salama says the Prophet recited al-Fatiha verse by verse and counted the basmala as an ayah. DURR-FULL (محمد بن كعب القرظي): Muhammad ibn Kab al-Qurazi counts the basmala as part of al-Fatiha's seven verses. ITQAN (أم سلمة): Umm Salama reports that the Prophet recited al-Fatiha verse by verse, counted the basmala as a verse, and did not count “ʿalayhim”; al-Suyuti says this supports the majority's seven-verse count. ITQAN (النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم): Umm Salama reports that the Prophet counted the basmala as a verse in his recitation of the opening sūra, but did not count عليهم as one. ALUSI (بعض الشافعية وحمزة): Some Shāfiʿīs and Ḥamza, with a view attributed to Aḥmad, count the basmala as a verse of al-Fātiḥa alone. ALUSI (أم سلمة): A report attributed to Umm Salama has the Prophet count the basmala as al-Fātiḥa’s first verse; al-Ālūsī questions the chain because Abū Mulayka’s hearing from her is unestablished. ALUSI (ابن خزيمة والحاكم): Their version has the Prophet count the basmala as a verse while reciting in prayer; al-Ālūsī reads it as the first Qurʾānic verse, not necessarily al-Fātiḥa’s. ALUSI-FULL (أم سلمة): A report attributed to Umm Salama has the Prophet count the basmala as the first Fatiha verse and the following phrases as the next verses. ALUSI-FULL (author): Alusi presents Razi's eighth proof: Shafii counts the basmala, while Abu Hanifa counts sirat alladhina anamta alayhim; Razi calls the Hanafi count weak and says seven verses require the basmala.
  1:1/q32/p2 The basmala is not a verse of al-Fātiḥa. — reasons: TAB (عظم قرأة أهل المدينة ومتقنيهم): Most leading Medinan reciters exclude basmala from the seven and count those You favored as seventh verse. TAB-FULL (author): Ṭabarī argues the basmala is not a verse of the Opening: otherwise al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm would repeat immediately, word for word and sense for sense, without an intervening divider. KASHSHAF
