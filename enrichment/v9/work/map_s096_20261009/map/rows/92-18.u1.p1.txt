92:18/q15 How did Abu Bakr obtain Bilal's freedom? [* · reports] (turns on: The reports differ on whether the payment was gold, an unnamed stronger enslaved man, or Nustas.)
  92:18/q15/p1 He bought Bilal with gold. — reasons: Aʿta from Ibn Abbas says Bilal endured torture, the Prophet reassured him, then Abu Bakr bought him with gold. Study Quran says the Prophet told Abu Bakr Bilal was tortured for God; idolaters later alleged an earlier favor prompted the purchase.
  92:18/q15/p2 He exchanged a stronger enslaved man for Bilal and freed him. — reasons: Hisham ibn Urwa says Abu Bakr challenged Umayya's torture before the exchange; Baghawi Full also reports the exchange.
  92:18/q15/p3 He traded Nustas, a wealthy enslaved man who refused Islam, for Bilal. — reasons: Saʿid ibn al-Musayyib says the emancipation was solely for God and God promised repayment in the hereafter.
  92:18/q15/p4 He bought Bilal from Umayya; the consideration is unspecified. — reasons: Biqai locates the purchase amid midday torture and Bilal's repeated 'One, One'; idolaters claimed repayment of a past favor, which revelation refuted.
92:18/q16 What did Bilal endure before he was freed? [* · reports]
  92:18/q16/p1 Umayya tortured him for his monotheistic faith while he affirmed God's oneness. — reasons: Ibn Ishaq puts the torture under a boulder in Mecca's noonday heat and quotes 'Ahad, Ahad.' Aʿta adds the Prophet's reassurance; Study Quran's account says Bilal had desecrated Kaʿbah idols and the Prophet promised the One would save him.
92:18/q17 What happened in the palm tree account associated with this passage? [* · reports]
  92:18/q17/p1 Abu al-Dahdah bought a disputed palm and gave it for a poor neighbor after its owner refused the Prophet's Paradise offer. — reasons: Thaʿlabi describes fruit falling into a neighbor's yard; Study Quran says the owner snatched dates from poor children, refused the offer, and Abu al-Dahdah paid forty palms before the Prophet transferred the tree. Qurtubi maps miser and giver to the sūra's contrasting outcomes. Study Quran doubts this is the occasion if the sūra is early Makkan.
92:18/q18 What is reported about Zahra's sight? [* · reports]
  92:18/q18/p1 Her sight returned after she denied that idols had power over her blindness. — reasons: Baghawi, Baghawi Full, and Biqai say Quraysh attributed her impaired sight to idols; she rejected their power and recovered. The accounts vary in naming her Zahra or Umm Umays.
92:18/q19 What other reports illustrate Abu Bakr's devotion or standing? [* · reports]
  92:18/q19/p1 He told the Prophet he had kept God and His Messenger for himself. — reasons: Tustari reports the Prophet's question about what Abu Bakr retained after giving.
  92:18/q19/p2 The Prophet hoped Abu Bakr would be called from every gate of Paradise. — reasons: Ibn Kathir Full reports a saying that gates call those who spend two kinds of wealth in God's path; asked whether anyone receives every call, the Prophet named Abu Bakr as hoped for.
  92:18/q19/p3 The Prophet said God and believers would accept only Abu Bakr. — reasons: Biqai treats the report as supporting Abu Bakr's unique closeness to the Prophet and his contrast with the worst unbeliever.
  92:18/q19/p4 Bilal later became the first caller to prayer and one of the Prophet's treasurers. — reasons: Study Quran supplies this aftermath of his freeing.
92:18/q20 How does this verse connect with the sūra's contrasting figures and outcomes? [* · links]
  92:18/q20/p1 The giver's conduct contrasts with denial, hoarding, and the fire; purification leads toward rescue and satisfaction. — reasons: Biqai contrasts cleansing bad traits with denial in 92:16. Bint al-Shati contrasts the miser's useless wealth in 92:11 with the gift in 92:18 and frames both by the opening oath. Islahi describes Quraysh's warning, believers' response, fire and protection. Kuranyolu links 92:14 with 92:17–20. Corpus Coranicum sees the two antitheses of conduct and recompense in 5–13 and 14–21.
  92:18/q20/p2 The final stanza reverses and mirrors the earlier portrayal of saved and damned. — reasons: Corpus Coranicum says the blessed outer sections share ittaqā in 92:5 and atqā in 92:17; earlier behavior precedes outcome, whereas final warning and promise precede conduct. Its longer portrait of the pious clarifies ethical purification and the Lord's face.
  92:18/q20/p3 The promise of Abu Bakr's satisfaction parallels God's promise to satisfy the Prophet. — reasons: Biqai says Abu Bakr is the closest in the community to the Prophet's station; the report of God's and believers' acceptance supports this.
  92:18/q20/p4 The sūra's wealth motif suits emphatic wording of God's ownership. — reasons: Samarrai says a lām of emphasis on divine ownership fits the repeated concern with wealth and possession; he contrasts 53:25, where that wealth context is absent.
92:18/q21 What rhetorical work does the contrast around this verse perform? [* · rhetoric]
  92:18/q21/p1 Reciprocal omission lets the denier's opposite and giver's opposite be understood. — reasons: Biqai calls this ḥatbāk: first naming denial implies its opposite for the pious; later naming giving implies its opposite for the denier.
  92:18/q21/p2 The relative descriptions make conduct the cause of each outcome. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says clauses describing most wretched and most pious indicate why each receives the stated fate.
92:18/q22 What grammatical principle applies to agreement in the verse's modifying construction? [* · grammar]
  92:18/q22/p1 Agreement normally follows the preceding word's form; agreement by meaning is less common. — reasons: Burhan states this rule for adjectives and similar constructions.
