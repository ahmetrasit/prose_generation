21:1/q19 What does the verse teach about rational and revealed proof of requital? [* · theology] (turns on: Whether reason independently establishes the moral premise of reward and punishment.)
  21:1/q19/p1 Requital for good and evil is required by reason and divine wisdom. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī say reason requires it; al-Būrsawī argues treating obedience and sin alike would violate wisdom and justice. Al-Biqāʿī says reason already requires the requital their later confession confirms.
  21:1/q19/p2 That rational claim depends on whether moral good and evil are knowable by reason. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī says Ashʿarites strongly deny the premise needed to make that argument.
21:1/q20 Is the reckoning itself an affirmed article of belief? [حِسَابُهُمْ · theology] (turns on: Whether the reported sectarian denials concern reckoning itself or other eschatological features.)
  21:1/q20/p1 The verse establishes real reckoning and recompense; only polytheists deny it in this sense. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī cites 2:284 and distinguishes denial of the balance or bridge from denial of the account. His gloss of believers includes bodily resurrection and recompense among necessarily known teachings.
  21:1/q20/p2 Muʿtazilites and Khārijites have been said to deny reckoning or interpret it as justice. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī reports the charge; al-Nasafī includes the balance, bridge, pool and intercession in it. Al-Ālūsī counters that standard kalām accounts report other denials without denial of reckoning.
21:1/q21 What spiritual response should the near account prompt? [* · interpretation] (turns on: Which conduct follows from taking the warning seriously.)
  21:1/q21/p1 People should repent, prepare, reckon themselves, and shorten worldly hopes. — reasons: Sulamī's authorities urge waking, vigilance and self-accounting; al-Rāzī says nearness and concealed timing promote repentance. Al-Qurṭubī calls each death a person's Hour; al-Nasafī urges remembrance and avoidance of the heedless; Kur'an Yolu calls for gratitude, obedience, repentance, worship and good works.
  21:1/q21/p2 The warning can make hearers abandon worldly projects and interests. — reasons: A Companion stopped building a wall on hearing the newly revealed verse; ʿĀmir ibn Rabīʿa refused a valley allotment after the revelation displaced worldly concern. Abū al-ʿAtāhiya's death couplet was said to be inspired by the verse.
  21:1/q21/p3 The heedless delay awakening until regret or death. — reasons: Sulamī reports that persistent sin blinds hearts after most of life is spent; al-Qushayrī says worldly engrossment ends at death, whereas the spiritually absorbed have a different waking.
21:1/q22 Why leave the exact time of the account unstated? [ٱقْتَرَبَ حِسَابُهُمْ · rhetoric]
  21:1/q22/p1 Concealing it encourages preparation, like concealing the time of death. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says the unspecified time benefits those obligated to repent; al-Būrsawī likewise calls concealment more fitting.
21:1/q23 How was this warning reported to have come to its audience? [* · reports] (turns on: Whether the reported setting emphasizes original addressees, their mockery, or listeners' immediate reactions.)
  21:1/q23/p1 It was revealed concerning Meccan unbelievers who denied resurrection. — reasons: Muqātil and Ibn al-Jawzī name Mecca's unbelievers; al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī specify resurrection denial.
  21:1/q23/p2 It answered mockery that earlier warnings of the Hour had not yet happened. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān says Quraysh called resurrection and judgment false or distant after the preceding warning. Al-Ālūsī reports their taunt at the delay, temporary cessation of activity on hearing the warning, renewed fear, then renewed mockery; he cites 17:51.
  21:1/q23/p3 A Companion stopped building when he heard the newly descended sūra. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī report he vowed never to build again after hearing that judgment was near.
  21:1/q23/p4 ʿĀmir ibn Rabīʿa declined land because the newly revealed sūra displaced worldly interests. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr and al-Durr relate his refusal of part of a valley granted by the Prophet.
21:1/q24 What is reported about the sūra's revelation and count? [* · sciences] (turns on: For the count, whether the Kūfan 112 or other 111 is followed.)
  21:1/q24/p1 Sūrat al-Anbiyāʾ is Meccan. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda, al-Aṣamm and Ibn al-Zubayr report Meccan revelation; Abū Ḥayyān and Ṭabāṭabāʾī call it undisputed, the latter citing the flow. Asad, citing al-Itqān, places it in the last Meccan group.
  21:1/q24/p2 It has 112 verses in the Kūfan count. — reasons: Jishumī prefers that count and attributes it to the Commander of the Faithful; al-Durr and al-Būrsawī also state 112.
  21:1/q24/p3 Other verse counts give 111. — reasons: Jishumī records them while saying the Kūfan 112 is soundest.
  21:1/q24/p4 Ibn Masʿūd counted it among his earliest acquired sūras. — reasons: He groups it with al-Kahf, Maryam and Ṭā Hā; al-Durr also includes al-Isrāʾīl and calls them inherited old recitation.
21:1/q25 What merit is reported for reciting this sūra? [* · sciences]
  21:1/q25/p1 A report promises easy reckoning and greetings from every prophet named in it. — reasons: Jishumī transmits the report from Ubayy, attributed to the Prophet, without an evaluation in the note.
21:1/q26 How does this opening connect with the end of Ṭā Hā? [* · links] (turns on: Whether the preceding close chiefly sets up final judgment, a demanded worldly sign, accountability for blessings, or consolation.)
  21:1/q26/p1 It makes the preceding warning of final judgment imminent. — reasons: Jishumī and al-Biqāʿī connect 20:135's promise that people will know who follows the right way with the unveiled, witnessed Day of Reckoning. Iṣlāḥī says the two passages dovetail and the opening needs no introduction.
  21:1/q26/p2 It announces the approaching worldly punishment sign demanded by Quraysh. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī says Ṭā Hā told Quraysh to wait for the punishment sign they demanded; the Messenger's advent makes their accountability imminent.
  21:1/q26/p3 It follows the warning against coveting worldly blessings because people will answer for them. — reasons: Ibn al-Zubayr, reported by al-Biqāʿī, cites 20:131 and 102:8: abundance is a trial and every blessing will be questioned.
  21:1/q26/p4 It continues consolation through prophetic struggles and the prospect of reward. — reasons: Ibn al-Zubayr says earlier warnings about contentious people and ruined nations called for fuller explanation. Ṭā Hā consoles through Moses and Adam; al-Anbiyāʾ follows with judgment, reward, admonitions, signs and destruction of rejecters.
21:1/q27 How does the sūra's prophetic material advance its purpose? [* · links] (turns on: Whether the prophetic narratives chiefly prove the Hour, defend the current messenger, or establish the unity of revelation.)
  21:1/q27/p1 Prophetic stories collectively prove the Hour's certainty, divine power, and judgment. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says no one story proves all aspects; together they show the sole Creator cannot be impeded, never breaks His promise, and judges great and lowly.
  21:1/q27/p2 Earlier prophets answer charges against Muḥammad and show revelation's shared message. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī traces accusations of magic, dreams, fabrication and poetry into accounts of earlier prophets, monotheism, resurrection, judgment and the pious inheriting earth. Iṣlāḥī says the revelation matches earlier messages and rejecters repeat ruined nations' fate. Iṣlāḥī also says Quraysh imagined the world as amusement for its Creator; the prophetic histories correct that view.
  21:1/q27/p3 Their stories carry a continuous call to divine oneness and one believing community. — reasons: Asad stresses unity of revelation and religious experience, linking one God to one community and brotherhood across outward labels, citing 21:92 and 21:108.
21:1/q28 How are al-Anbiyāʾ's contents arranged and related to earlier sūras? [* · links] (turns on: Whether the organizing relation is its prophetic selection, warning sequence, or opening form.)
  21:1/q28/p1 It details prophets omitted or only briefly mentioned in Maryam and Ṭā Hā. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī says Ṭā Hā expands Moses from Maryam, while al-Anbiyāʾ details other prophets and briefly recalls several. Iṣlāḥī says Ṭā Hā recounts Moses and this sūra cites other prophets.
  21:1/q28/p2 It moves from warning Quraysh through prophetic histories back to their coming fate. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī divides it into three sections: warning, historical proofs of Qur'anic truth, then return to warning, including predicted conquest of Mecca and reassurance to Muslims.
  21:1/q28/p3 Its opening is a declarative announcement. — reasons: Al-Zarkashī classifies al-Anbiyāʾ among twenty-three sūras with declarative openings, alongside al-Qamar and al-Raḥmān.
21:1/q29 What overarching theme does this opening establish for the sūra? [* · links] (turns on: Which of the sūra's connected themes is placed at its center.)
  21:1/q29/p1 The Hour, resurrection and universal judgment are certain and near. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī includes death as a route to the Hour and judgment of eminent and humble; the Creator has no partner to impede it.
