[QURTUBI:1:6/r1] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | The servant asks God to direct and show him the straight way of guidance leading to intimacy with and nearness to God.
[QURTUBI:1:6/r10] QURTUBI, d. 671 · عامر بن الطفيل | reports | Sirat means road in Arabic; Amir ibn al-Tufayl's verse uses it in this sense.
[QURTUBI:1:6/r11] QURTUBI, d. 671 · جرير | reports | Jarir also uses sirat to mean a road, supporting its ordinary Arabic sense.
[QURTUBI:1:6/r12] QURTUBI, d. 671 · النقاش | reports | Al-Naqqash says sirat is the Roman-language word for road.
[QURTUBI:1:6/r13] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عطية | rejects | Ibn Atiyya judges al-Naqqash's claim that sirat is Roman to be very weak.
[QURTUBI:1:6/r14] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | reports | A reading with sin, sirat, relates to istirat, swallowing: the road is imagined to swallow its traveler.
[QURTUBI:1:6/r15] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | reports | Other pronunciations put the consonant between zay and sad or use pure zay; sin is original.
[QURTUBI:1:6/r16] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farra reports pure zay as a dialect of Udra, Kalb, and Banu al-Qayn, who also pronounce asdaq as azdaq; al-Azd/al-Asd and lasiq/lasiq vary similarly.
[QURTUBI:1:6/r17] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Sirat is the second object of guidance: the verb can take it with a preposition, as in 37:23, or without one here. | mentions 37:23
[QURTUBI:1:6/r18] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Mustaqim describes a path without crookedness or deviation, as also stated in 6:153. | mentions 6:153
[QURTUBI:1:6/r19] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | The underlying form is mustaqwim; its vowel moves to qaf and waw becomes ya because the preceding consonant has kasra.
[QURTUBI:1:6/r2] QURTUBI, d. 671 · بعض العلماء | reports | The sura's prayer joins praise and requests; this prayer is excellent because the supplicant uses God's own words.
[QURTUBI:1:6/r3] QURTUBI, d. 671 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A hadith says nothing is more precious to God than supplication; no grading is supplied.
[QURTUBI:1:6/r4] QURTUBI, d. 671 · قيل | reports | Another explanation is: guide us by enabling us to use the prophetic practices in fulfilling Your obligations.
[QURTUBI:1:6/r5] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | The root idea of guidance is inclination: 7:156 says “we have turned toward You”; related usages describe swaying, a gift passing between owners, and sacrificial livestock driven to the sanctuary, hence a prayer that hearts incline to truth. | mentions 7:156
[QURTUBI:1:6/r6] QURTUBI, d. 671 · فضيل بن عياض | reports | Al-Fudayl identifies the straight path as pilgrimage; al-Qurtubi calls this particular and prefers the general meaning.
[QURTUBI:1:6/r7] QURTUBI, d. 671 · محمد بن الحنفية | reports | Ibn al-Hanafiyya identifies the straight path as God's religion, the only religion He accepts from worshippers.
[QURTUBI:1:6/r8] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبو العالية | reports | Abu al-Aliya says the path is the Prophet and his two Companions; Asim al-Ahwal transmits this view.
[QURTUBI:1:6/r9] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan affirms that Abu al-Aliya spoke truly and sincerely in identifying the path as the Prophet and his two Companions.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:5-6/r11] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Ihdina is a prayer from servant to Lord: guide and show us the straight path leading to intimacy and nearness.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:5-6/r12] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · بعض العلماء | reports | Some say the sura’s first half gathers praise and its second half petitions; this prayer is especially excellent because the servant uses words spoken by the Lord of the worlds.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:6/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A hadith says nothing is more honored by God than supplication.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:6/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · بعض العلماء | reports | Another explanation asks for guidance through observing the sunnas in fulfilling obligations; the root may mean to incline, so the prayer asks God to turn hearts toward truth. | mentions 7:156
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:6/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الفضيل بن عياض | reports | Al-Fudayl ibn ʿIyad identifies the straight path as the pilgrimage route, but the author prefers the general meaning.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:6/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · محمد بن الحنفية | reports | Muhammad ibn al-Hanafiyya says the straight path is God’s religion, the only religion He accepts from servants.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:6/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · عاصم الأحول عن أبي العالية | reports | Abu al-ʿAliya identifies the path as Muhammad and his two successors, Abu Bakr and ʿUmar; al-Hasan affirms that this is truthful counsel.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:6/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Ṣirāṭ is the Arabic word for road; a claim that it is Roman is very weak according to Ibn ʿAtiyya.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:7/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | The word has readings with sīn, between zāy and ṣād, or pure zāy; sīn is original, while the zāy dialect is associated with ʿUdhra, Kalb, and Banu al-Qayn.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:7/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Ṣirāṭ is the second object of guidance, which may take a preposition or not; mustaqīm means without bending, from mustaqwim with a sound change. | mentions 37:23, 6:153
[BAYDAWI:1:6/r1] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | “Guide us” explains the requested assistance—as if God asked how to help—or singles out its greatest aim.
[BAYDAWI:1:6/r10] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | reports | A gnostic who has reached God seeks guidance along the journey to Him, removal of spiritual darkness and bodily veils, and vision by God's holy light.
[BAYDAWI:1:6/r11] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | reports | Command and supplication share wording and meaning but differ by superiority or inferiority; another view says the difference is rank.
[BAYDAWI:1:6/r12] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | Ṣirāṭ is linked to sarata, swallowing: the road seems to swallow travelers, hence the name luqam, “gulper.”
[BAYDAWI:1:6/r13] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | Ṣād replaces original sīn to match emphatic ṭāʾ; the ṣād may be colored with zāy to approach the replaced letter.
[BAYDAWI:1:6/r14] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · ابن كثير برواية قنبل ورويس عن يعقوب | reports | Ibn Kathīr through Qunbul and Ruways from Yaʿqūb read original sīn; Ḥamza uses the mixed pronunciation and others read ṣād, the Quraysh dialect.
[BAYDAWI:1:6/r15] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | Ṣirāṭ's plural is suruṭ, like kutub; like ṭarīq, it may be masculine or feminine.
[BAYDAWI:1:6/r16] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | Al-mustaqīm means level or straight; its intended referent is the path of truth.
[BAYDAWI:1:6/r17] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | reports | An alternative identifies the straight path as the religion of Islam.
[BAYDAWI:1:6/r2] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | Hidāya is gentle direction, normally used for good; “lead them to Hell's path” is sarcastic. Related hawādī are the foreparts that lead wild animals. | mentions 37:23
[BAYDAWI:1:6/r3] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | Hadā normally governs li- or ilā but here is treated like ikhtāra in “Moses chose his people.” | mentions 7:155
[BAYDAWI:1:6/r4] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | God's kinds of guidance are innumerable like His blessings, yet can be classified in an ordered scheme. | mentions 14:34
[BAYDAWI:1:6/r5] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | The first kind grants capacities for finding one's interests, including reason, inner senses, and external perception.
[BAYDAWI:1:6/r6] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | The second kind presents signs distinguishing truth from falsehood and good from corruption; verses say people were shown the two paths and Thamud chose blindness. | mentions 90:10, 41:17
[BAYDAWI:1:6/r7] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | The third kind guides through messengers and revealed books, exemplified by leaders guiding by God's command and the Qur'an guiding to the upright path. | mentions 21:73, 17:9
[BAYDAWI:1:6/r8] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | The fourth kind unveils realities to hearts through revelation, inspiration, or true dreams; only prophets and saints attain it. | mentions 6:90, 29:69
[BAYDAWI:1:6/r9] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | The request may seek increased guidance, perseverance in it, or higher ranks built upon it.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:6/r1] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | “Guide us” specifies the requested aid or names its chief purpose; guidance is gentle direction and is normally for good, while its use for the path to Hell in 37:23 is ironic. | mentions 37:23
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:6/r2] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | Divine guidance has four ordered kinds: faculties for recognizing interests, distinguishing signs, messengers and revealed books, and disclosure of realities by revelation, inspiration, or true dreams.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:6/r3] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | Scripture illustrates guidance by signs at 2:10, 41:17, 21:73, 17:9, 6:90, and 29:69; the last kind is reserved for prophets and saints. | mentions 2:10, 41:17, 21:73, 17:9, 6:90, 29:69
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:6/r4] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | The request seeks an increase in guidance already granted, steadfastness in it, or the further ranks that follow it; the spiritually realized ask to travel toward God and shed bodily veils.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:1:6/r5] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | reports | Command and supplication share wording and meaning but differ through the speaker's elevated or lower rank, or simply through rank.
