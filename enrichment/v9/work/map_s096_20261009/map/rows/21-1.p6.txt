5-english:p94/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · Ibn ‘Abbās | reports | “People” refers to the idolaters of Makkah; using a general term while they evade God's reminder conveys aversion and regret.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-5-english:p94/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The near accountability is factual: after a messenger conclusively communicates truth, a nation has one option, faith; refusal brings destruction, and heedlessness with evasion compounds guilt.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:21:1/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | God created the world, life, and death as a test of who does the best deeds, gave people countless blessings, and asks them to thank Him, obey His commands, and avoid His prohibitions.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:21:1/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The verse warns that people will soon be called to account for those blessings and urges them to prepare for that day.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:21:1/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | A believer is heedless when, despite believing in the afterlife, they fail to prepare, avoid sin, repent, fulfill worship carefully, or do good works.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:21:1/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | A disbeliever is heedless when they ignore their creator, purpose, and accountability for good and evil, and turn away from warnings by prophets and revelation.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:21:1/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The verse uses the past tense because the Day of Judgment is certain to come.
[STUDYQURAN:21:1/r1] STUDYQURAN · R | reports | The opening verse of al-Anbiyāʾ announces that the time of reckoning is approaching. | mentions 54:1
[STUDYQURAN:21:1/r2] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Ḥisāb denotes the accounting of the Day of Judgment, also called the Day of Reckoning. | mentions 38:16, 40:28
[STUDYQURAN:21:1/r3] STUDYQURAN · some theologians (Q, Z) | reports | Some theologians say more time has passed since creation than remains until the world’s end.
[STUDYQURAN:21:1/r4] STUDYQURAN · R | reports | The chronological argument rests on iqtaraba ordinarily indicating that one is closer to one’s destination than one’s origin.
[STUDYQURAN:21:1/r5] STUDYQURAN · M | reports | From God’s perspective reckoning is near in relation to His knowledge, not temporally, since He knows all moments simultaneously; humans think it distant. | mentions 70:1, 70:6, 70:7
[STUDYQURAN:21:1/r6] STUDYQURAN · Q | reports | Commentators say each person’s certain death is reason enough to regard reckoning as near, regardless of when the world ends.
[STUDYQURAN:21:1/r7] STUDYQURAN · Ṭ, Q | reports | Being heedless means caring only for this world and paying no attention to the Hereafter.
[STUDYQURAN:21:1/r8] STUDYQURAN · Aj | reports | Sufis distinguish those heedless through worldly engrossment from righteous believers mindful of the Hereafter.
[STUDYQURAN:21:1/r9] STUDYQURAN · Aj | reports | Another kind of heedlessness arises from being so absorbed in remembrance of God that one gives no thought to the Garden or Fire.
