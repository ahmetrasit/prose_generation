[IBNASHUR:1:1/r22] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The path of those favored points to the exemplary past of communities and individuals; the final phrase evokes stories and details of misguided nations recounted in the Qur’an. | mentions 1:7
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r23] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Fātiha gives the reciter a general grasp of the Qur’an and invites fuller study according to capacity; this is why it is recited in every prayer rakʿa.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r24] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أبو سعيد ابن المعلى | reports | In al-Bukhari, the Prophet identifies al-Ḥamdu lillāh as the seven oft-repeated verses and the mighty Qur’an given to him. | mentions 1:2
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r25] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · الحسن البصري | reports | Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī dissents from the seven-verse count and says Fātiha has eight verses.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r26] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · الحسين الجعفي | reports | Al-Ḥusayn al-Juʿfī is reported to count six verses in Fātiha.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r27] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · بعض الناس | reports | An unspecified group is reported to count nine verses; that count requires excluding the basmala and combining two other verse units.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r28] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Mathānī has several possible morphological analyses; all derive from pairing a second with a first, and the verse is called repeated because it is recited in every rakʿa.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r29] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · عمر بن الخطاب | reports | A view attributed to ʿUmar says the verses are repeated because another sura is joined to them in each rakʿa; Ibn ʿĀshūr considers this sound.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r3] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Fātiha derives from fatḥ, opening by removing an obstruction to an intended place; figuratively it means the first thing, by analogy with the first person entering through a door.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r30] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · عائشة | reports | A sound hadith from ʿĀʾisha says prayers were first prescribed as two rakʿas, then travel prayer remained so while resident prayer was lengthened; Ibn ʿĀshūr uses this to explain the title.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r31] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | reports | Another view takes mathānī as repetition in prayer generally, supported by Arabic use of dual forms for repeated action and by the Qur’an’s recurrent stories and themes. | mentions 67:4, 39:23
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r32] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | rejects | The claim that Fātiha is called mathānī because it descended twice, in Mecca and Medina, is very remote: the sura is agreed to be Meccan, making a second descent pointless.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r33] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Fātiha opens the suras as a preface to a speech or book, while its summary of Qur’anic aims makes it a rhetorically apt introduction.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r34] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The majority agree Fātiha is Meccan; the author’s preferred chronology is al-ʿAlaq, then al-Muddaththir, then Fātiha. | mentions 96:1
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r35] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | reports | Other accounts place Sūrat Nūn wa-l-Qalam and al-Muzzammil before Fātiha.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r36] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · بعضهم | reports | Some say Fātiha was the first complete sura revealed, in contrast to al-Qalam, which they regard as having come in fragments.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r37] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · بعض العلماء | reports | Some scholars conclude Fātiha descended when prayer became obligatory, so Muslims recited it in prayer from the outset.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r38] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · جابر بن زيد | reports | A report attributed to Jābir ibn Zayd places Fātiha fifth in the order of sura revelation.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r39] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Its later revelation does not conflict with its first position: once enough Qur’an existed to form a Book, Fātiha was revealed as its preface.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r4] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Fātiha is not used here in its literal sense of opening; it names the beginning, either as a verbal noun or as an opener that initiates the whole.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r40] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أبو هريرة | reports | The Ṣaḥīḥayn report has God divide the prayer between Himself and the servant, answering each recited passage from praise through the request for guidance. | mentions 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r41] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | This report confirms seven verses arranged as three, one, and three. | mentions 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r42] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أهل المدينة | reports | Medinan verse-counting does not count the basmala and treats anʿamta ʿalayhim as a separate verse.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r43] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أهل مكة وأهل الكوفة | reports | Meccan and Kufan counting treats the basmala as a verse and anʿamta ʿalayhim as part of the following verse.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r44] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · الحسن البصري | reports | Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī counts both the basmala and anʿamta ʿalayhim as separate verses.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r45] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Basmala is a coined noun formed by blending letters from bismi and Allāh; naḥt creates a compact expression from a frequently spoken phrase.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r46] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Arabic used blending for ambiguous affiliations, such as ʿabshamī from ʿAbd Shams; later speakers extended the method to shorten common phrases.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r47] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Poets al-Rāʿī and ʿUmar ibn Abī Rabīʿa attest basmala as a verb for saying bismillah; common usage extended it to the full phrase despite omitted letters.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r48] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Arabic forms the regular verbal noun basmala and the active participle from basmala; no passive participle is attested.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r49] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · المطرز | reports | Al-Muṭarriz lists coined verbs for formulae including basmala, sabḥala, ḥayʿala, ḥawqala, ḥamdala, halala, jaʿala, ṭayqala, and damʿaza.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r5] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · ابن عباس | reports | Forms such as bāqiya, ṭāghiya, khāṭiʾa, and ḥāqqa can function as verbal nouns; Ibn ʿAbbās glosses al-ṭāghiya as the people’s transgression.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r50] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Muslims agree the basmala is Qur’anic because it occurs in Solomon’s letter; beginning important acts with it is also established, though the cited hadith is described as ḥasan and is not in the Sunan or Mustadrak. | mentions 27:30
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r51] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | reports | The report that any important matter begun without the basmala is cut off is described as ḥasan, though major Sunan and Mustadrak compilers did not include it.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r52] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Most scholars say early muṣḥaf scribes wrote the basmala at every sura except Barāʾa; its presence at Fātiha’s beginning is undisputed but is not itself a sura divider.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r53] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The dispute concerns whether Qur’anic status recurs at sura openings, not whether the basmala is Qur’an at all; Ibn Rushd al-Ḥafīd makes this distinction.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r54] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · مالك والأوزاعي وفقهاء المدينة والشام والبصرة | reports | Mālik, al-Awzāʿī, and jurists of Medina, Syria, and Basra hold that the basmala is not a verse at sura openings, though it is part of the verse in Sūrat al-Naml. | mentions 27:30
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r55] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · الشافعي وأحمد وإسحاق وأبو ثور | reports | One view of al-Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad, Isḥāq, Abū Thawr, and most Meccan and Kufan jurists counts it as a verse at the start of Fātiha only.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r56] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · عبد الله بن المبارك والشافعي | reports | Ibn al-Mubārak and al-Shāfiʿī’s view judged more authentic by Ibn ʿĀshūr count it as a verse at the beginning of every sura.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r57] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أبو حنيفة | reports | No direct statement is transmitted from Abū Ḥanīfa; al-Zamakhsharī inferred that he did not regard it as part of the suras, an inference Ibn ʿĀshūr accepts.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r58] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · الباقلاني | reports | Al-Bāqillānī argues proof of Qur’anic status would require mass transmission, which would preclude disagreement, or solitary reports, which would make the Qur’an probabilistic and invite claims of textual alteration.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r59] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · ابن العربي | reports | Ibn al-ʿArabī adds that disagreement itself shows it is not Qur’an, since the Qur’an is not disputed.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r6] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The first thing may be called Fātiha as the object of opening, where the action first appears, or as an active participle converted into a name for the beginning.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r60] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · عبد الوهاب | reports | ʿAbd al-Wahhāb argues the Prophet conveyed Qur’an uniformly and publicly; had the basmala belonged to Fātiha he would have made that clear, excluding secret or missing Qur’an.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r61] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · الغزالي | reports | Al-Ghazālī replies that denying the basmala’s Qur’anic status faces the same transmission dilemma; its written Qur’anic script weakens the presumption of non-Qur’anic status, so proof is needed.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r62] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | rejects | Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects al-Ghazālī and al-Rāzī’s reply as question-begging: it ultimately relies on the basmala’s written placement, which requires separate examination.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r63] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أبو هريرة | reports | In the Muwattaʾ report of God dividing prayer, the response begins after the servant says al-ḥamdu lillāh; Mālik uses the omission of the basmala as evidence. | mentions 1:2
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r64] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أبي بن كعب | reports | In a Muwattaʾ and Ṣaḥīḥayn report, the Prophet asks Ubayy how he opens prayer; Ubayy recites from al-ḥamdu lillāh through the end without the basmala. | mentions 1:2
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r65] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أنس بن مالك | reports | Anas reports that the Prophet, Abū Bakr, and ʿUmar began recitation with al-ḥamdu lillāh and did not mention the basmala at the beginning or end. | me
