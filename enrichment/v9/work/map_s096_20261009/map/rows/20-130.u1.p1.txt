20:130/q11 Why are the night hours singled out? [وَمِنْ ءَانَآئِ ٱلَّيْلِ فَسَبِّحْ · interpretation]
  20:130/q11/p1 Night worship brings greater focus, seclusion and merit because rest is tempting and effort is greater. — reasons: Kashshaf and Razi invoke 73:6 and 39:9; Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Biqai and Bursevi repeat the quiet, collected-heart and difficulty grounds. Ibn Ashur says the hours are foregrounded because neglect through rest is likely; Alusi says special importance warrants a renewed command.
20:130/q12 How is min ānāʾ al-layl fasabbiḥ parsed? [وَمِنْ ءَانَآئِ ٱلَّيْلِ فَسَبِّحْ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the fronted phrase is governed directly by the following imperative or by an implied verb, and how fa functions.)
  20:130/q12/p1 The fronted partitive or starting-time phrase attaches to the second fasabbiḥ, with fa marking emphasis or necessary sequence. — reasons: Samin attaches the phrase to the second imperative; Ibn Ashur calls min a starting point and fa a response-like emphasis, comparing 17:79 and the hadith about parents; Tabatabai calls min partitive and compares the fronting in 2:40. Alusi reports direct government and explanatory or additional fa, and al-Khafaji says no added fa need be posited.
  20:130/q12/p2 The night-time phrase is governed by an omitted command to rise, after which fasabbiḥ is coordinated. — reasons: Alusi reports this parsing and calls it strained.
  20:130/q12/p3 The phrase can be parsed through coordination with an earlier fronted command, or fa can be explanatory. — reasons: Alusi lists coordination with wa-iyyaya farhabun and alternative explanations of fa.
20:130/q13 Which worship occurs at the day's edges? [وَأَطْرَافَ ٱلنَّهَارِ · interpretation] (turns on: Where the day's ends are placed, whether noon can count as an edge, and whether the command fixes prayer times or covers broad intervals.)
  20:130/q13/p1 The noon or Dhuhr prayer at the boundary of the day's two halves. — reasons: Qatada, Ibn Abbas, Mujahid and others locate noon at the end of one half and start of the next; Wahidi cites 66:4 for plural wording. Alusi and Tabatabai object that noon is not naturally an edge of the whole day.
  20:130/q13/p2 Both noon and sunset prayers, noon at the meeting of the halves and sunset as a further edge. — reasons: Tabari calls sunset a third edge; Qurtubi and Study Quran repeat the shared-noon-boundary account; Alusi reports a three-edge version; Ibn Ashur allows solar-arc endpoints.
  20:130/q13/p3 Dawn and sunset prayers, repeating periods already named for emphasis. — reasons: Ibn Zayd, Kashshaf, Nasafi and Ibn Jawzi map the two edges this way; Kashshaf compares the specially repeated middle prayer of 2:238. Alusi says this assumes day runs from sunrise to sunset; Ibn Ashur also cites 2:238.
  20:130/q13/p4 Dawn, noon and afternoon prayers or their four day endpoints. — reasons: Farra joins dawn to noon and afternoon, comparing 11:114; Biqai prefers first and last endpoints as dawn and afternoon and middle points as preparation and prayer. Abu Hayyan reports three or four literal-edge counts; Islahi adds chasht and Maghrib in his fuller margin schedule.
  20:130/q13/p5 Daytime voluntary prayers or remembrance across the day's portions. — reasons: Al-Hasan reports voluntary prayer; Razi and Ibn Atiyya add optional prayer; Baydawi and Bursevi extend it across daylight. Alusi rejects a restricted last-hours version as unnatural; Islahi specifically includes chasht.
  20:130/q13/p6 Morning and evening or the day's recurring hours and sides, without fixing a particular prayer. — reasons: Wahidi reports morning-evening and Ibn al-Arabi's hours; Asad relates the extremities to 11:114; Tabatabai prefers broad intervals before sunrise and sunset, whose multiple subparts count as edges relative to noon and avoid repetition.
20:130/q14 Why does the verse use the plural aṭrāf for the day? [أَطْرَافَ ٱلنَّهَارِ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the plural is idiomatic for two, reflects daily recurrence, or counts more actual parts.)
  20:130/q14/p1 Arabic may use a plural for the two ends, including a shared boundary of the day's two halves. — reasons: Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, Ibn Jawzi and Tabatabai compare the dual hearts in 66:4; Kashshaf invokes the clearer dual in 11:114 and a poetic two-backs example; Razi says the minimum plural can denote two; Samin and Alusi cite poetic parallel. Alusi says two ends are unambiguous for a day and balance plural night hours.
  20:130/q14/p2 The two ends recur every day, so the generic day has a plural set of ends. — reasons: Abu al-Abbas, Ibn Atiyya, Razi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Tabatabai report recurrence or day as a genus; Razi cites 11:114.
  20:130/q14/p3 The plural is literal because multiple daytime portions or three or four edges are counted. — reasons: Abu Hayyan reports a three-edge scheme at dawn, noon-afternoon and evening, and a four-edge scheme around sunrise, noon and sunset; Tabari and Alusi count noon's two sides and sunset as a third. Ibn Ashur considers endpoints of the solar arc.
  20:130/q14/p4 Aṭrāf means portions or hours, making the plural straightforward. — reasons: Ibn al-Arabi calls them hours; Samin gives true plural hours; Abu Hayyan reports this, and Alusi cites lexica for taraf as a portion or group. Tabatabai locates several subparts in the broad early and late intervals.
20:130/q15 How is wa-aṭrāfa al-nahār read and syntactically connected? [وَأَطْرَافَ ٱلنَّهَارِ · readings] (turns on: The transmitted case ending and whether the coordination follows an earlier accusative time phrase or the genitive night phrase.)
  20:130/q15/p1 The common accusative coordinates with before sunrise or with the syntactic position of the night-hours phrase. — reasons: Farra, Tabari and Ibn Ashur attach it to the earlier temporal phrase; Kashshaf reports coordination with night hours; Samin and Alusi allow either; Tabatabai supplies an omitted time preposition and calls it comitative.
  20:130/q15/p2 Al-Hasan and Isa ibn Umar read it genitive, coordinated with ānāʾ al-layl. — reasons: Farra considers genitive possible but says it is unreported in his recitation account; Abu Hayyan and Samin name the two readers.
20:130/q16 What is promised by laʿallaka tarḍā? [لَعَلَّكَ تَرْضَىٰ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether satisfaction is reward, intercession, broad success, inward acceptance of decree, or divine approval; also whether laʿalla marks hope or assured favor.)
  20:130/q16/p1 The Prophet will be satisfied by divine reward in the Hereafter. — reasons: Yahya transmits al-Hasan's assurance; Ibn Abbas in Wahidi calls it a guaranteed reward and return; Ibn Zayd says additional reward; Abu Hayyan says hope describes reward not yet seen, though another view treats divine hope as certain. Muqatil, Baghawi, Razi, Qurtubi and Study Quran connect satisfaction with recompense for worship; Jishumi says deeds deserve reward.
  20:130/q16/p2 He will be content through intercession for his community or exalted rank. — reasons: Mawardi, Baghawi, Razi, Bursevi and Tabatabai connect intercession to 93:5; Razi also cites 17:79 for elevated favor. Farisi allows God's gift of a high rank.
  20:130/q16/p3 Satisfaction covers God's worldly victory and honor as well as afterlife intercession and Paradise. — reasons: Tabrisi and Tabatabai report the full range of promises; Biqai and Islahi include triumph, fulfillment of hope and success in both lives, comparing 93:5.
  20:130/q16/p4 Persistent glorification leads to inner contentment with God's decree or with whatever He gives. — reasons: Tabatabai prefers this contextual sense and says familiarity with God's acts quiets distraction; Study Quran reports general contentment with worldly gifts; Kur'an Yolu calls its result peace. Qashani describes purification and refinement.
  20:130/q16/p5 God will be pleased with the Prophet or make him pleased through obedient worship or a gift. — reasons: Tabari and Farisi relate the passive to obedience, God-given contentment and 19:55; Baghawi relates divine pleasure to Mary's account; Abu Ubayd prefers the passive because it bears both divine approval and a grant of contentment. Qiraat-ER emphasizes satisfaction as granted.
  20:130/q16/p6 Laʿalla keeps the worshipper hopeful of God's favor without feeling secure, and makes the promised outcome a gracious possibility. — reasons: Biqai stresses hope rather than security; Alusi says the promise does not oblige God to reward, while Abu Hayyan reports the competing view that divine hope necessarily comes true.
20:130/q17 Which command does laʿallaka tarḍā qualify? [لَعَلَّكَ تَرْضَىٰ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the final purpose attaches only to worship or to the combined patience-and-worship command.)
  20:130/q17/p1 It qualifies glorification or worship at the named times. — reasons: Bursevi explicitly attaches it to tasbih; Alusi reports this construction as a pleasing reward; Kashshaf takes remembrance at those times as the route to contentment.
  20:130/q17/p2 It qualifies both patience and prayer, with satisfaction through victory and the spread of the mission. — reasons: Alusi reports this wider attachment.
