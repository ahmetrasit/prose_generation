30:11/q15 Why is إِلَيْهِ placed before the return verb? [إِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ · rhetoric]
  30:11/q15/p1 It restricts the destination to God alone. — reasons: Ālūsī says fronting the destination conveys restriction. 30:11/q16 How is تُرْجَعُونَ read? [تُرْجَعُونَ · readings] (turns on: The verb's person and voice: direct address or third-person narration, and passive return or active voluntary return.)
  30:11/q16/p1 Second-person plural passive, turjaʿūn: you are returned. — reasons: Ibn Mujāhid names Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamzah, al-Kisāʾī, Ḥafṣ from ʿĀṣim, and ʿAyyāsh from Abū ʿAmr; Fārisī gives the first six and also cites Ibn ʿAbbās's report from Abū ʿAmr. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, Biqāʿī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr call this the majority or general reading. Jishumī says most retain the ḍamma, unlike Yaʿqūb's open vowel. Qirāʾāt ER contrasts its passive compulsion with active agency. Wāḥidī, Zamakhsharī, Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and others also report the tāʾ form.
  30:11/q16/p2 Third-person plural passive, yurjaʿūn: they are returned. — reasons: Ibn Mujāhid, Fārisī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Baghawī, and Ibn al-Jawzī name Abū ʿAmr and Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim. Bayḍāwī, Biqāʿī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr additionally name Rawḥ; Nasafī names Sahl. Jishumī says it refers to creation. Wāḥidī, Bursevī, and Fārisī say it follows the plural sense of al-khalq and surrounding third-person narration. Abū Ḥayyān calls its readers the two fathers. Qirāʾāt ER says third person makes the return a universal cosmic principle.
  30:11/q16/p3 Third-person plural active, yarjiʿūn: they return voluntarily. — reasons: Qirāʾāt ER says the active form gives subjects agency instead of passive compulsion.
  30:11/q16/p4 Second-person plural active, tarjiʿūn: you return voluntarily. — reasons: Qirāʾāt ER says this keeps direct address while assigning human agency. Jishumī says Yaʿqūb opens the vowel throughout the Qurʾān, unlike the readers' usual ḍamma.
30:11/q17 Why change from narration to direct address at the return? [تُرْجَعُونَ · rhetoric]
  30:11/q17/p1 Direct address makes the return explicit and presses its warning on the hearers. — reasons: Fārisī and Wāḥidī compare the move to direct address in the Fātiḥah, citing 1:1 and 1:4 or 1:2 and 1:5. Ibn al-Jawzī identifies the shift from report to address. Bayḍāwī says it emphasizes the intended point, Biqāʿī says it is more explicit, and Bursevī says it intensifies the warning. Ālūsī says it directly confronts polytheists with a stronger personal threat; Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the polytheists as its addressees.
30:11/q18 How can the final verb be plural after singular يُعِيدُهُۥ? [ٱلْخَلْقَ يُعِيدُهُۥ تُرْجَعُونَ · grammar]
  30:11/q18/p1 The object pronoun follows the singular form al-khalq, while a third-person plural return follows its collective meaning. — reasons: Wāḥidī states this contrast explicitly in two notes. Fārisī says the yāʾ reading follows the plural sense of creation. Ibn al-Jawzī identifies al-khalq as created beings despite the singular object pronoun. Baghawī notes the singular reference. Bursevī says the variant third person follows al-khalq's collective sense; Ṭabāṭabāʾī takes the plural return pronoun as referring to created beings.
30:11/q19 How does this verse connect with its neighboring verses? [* · links]
  30:11/q19/p1 It supplies proof of resurrection after the deniers' fate and leads to the account of what follows their return. — reasons: Rāzī says the earlier mention of Hell implies resurrection, which God then proves, and 30:12 describes what happens upon return. Ibn ʿĀshūr says 30:10 recalls deniers' worldly end and the succession of nations to prepare the case for resurrection and judgment. Biqāʿī says the explicit conclusion recaps the preceding proof and affects the soul. The Study Quran describes the movement from disbelievers' failure to reflect toward the differing ends of believers and disbelievers.
30:11/q20 How does this verse express the sura's central theme? [* · links]
  30:11/q20/p1 It states God's creation, life from death, and the power and promise of resurrection. — reasons: Asad identifies this theme across 30:11, 30:16, 30:19, and 30:27.
30:11/q21 What distinction does final judgment make among claimants to faith? [* · interpretation]
  30:11/q21/p1 God separates sincere believers from hypocrites who claim faith but avoid hardship. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī says justice requires this sifting of sincere followers from mere claimants.
30:11/q22 What warnings about religious error accompany this verse? [* · theology] (turns on: The two reports condemn different forms of religious error: kalām and denial of revelation.)
  30:11/q22/p1 Engaging in kalām leads to heresy, and its originator bears the burden of the innovation and its imitators until resurrection. — reasons: Bursevī reports al-Shāfiʿī's warning and says imitators' own burdens are not diminished.
