[BINTSHATI:v2p192/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | The Quran extends responsibility beyond private worship: rejecting the orphan and failing to urge food for the poor counts as denying the religion and neglecting public solidarity and mercy.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p504/r1] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says yaduʿʿu al-yatim means pushing the orphan away from his due, and cites a line by Abu Talib.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p504/r2] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farra likewise explains the phrase as pushing the orphan away from his right and wronging him; the root indicates movement, pushing, and disturbance.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p504/r3] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · الطبري وابن الأثير | reports | Al-Tabari also reports 'subdues' and 'pushes away'; Ibn al-Athir explains pushing as expelling.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p504/r4] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · author | prefers | The author prefers coercive, harsh pushing with rebuke over merely withholding an orphan's right: in 52:13 the deniers are thrust into Hell despite having no right being denied. | mentions 52:13
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p504/r5] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · author | holds | An orphan can be treated harshly even if his property is paid; fulfilling the formal entitlement alone does not excuse rejecting him rudely, as al-Asas defines the phrase as pushing him harshly.
