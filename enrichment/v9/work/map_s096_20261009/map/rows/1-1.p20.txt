[RAZI-FULL:v1p113/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | God must be knowing, powerful, and living; the necessary conceptual distinction between God's essence and His being knowing or powerful shows these are not identical.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p113/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | We can conceive God as existent while not attending to His power or knowledge, and conceive power without knowledge, showing distinct concepts.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p113/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Knowledge concerns necessary, impossible, and possible things, while power concerns only possible things, proving their difference.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p113/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Power brings the possible into existence but knowledge does not, so the two attributes differ.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p113/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Not existent contradicts existent, whereas not knowing does not; what is negated by existence and knowledge claims is therefore distinct, likewise for power.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p113/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | These arguments establish that God has attributes, but they might be relational: power is capacity to produce, and knowledge is awareness, each grounded in the divine essence.
