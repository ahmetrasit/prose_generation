[ALUSI-FULL:v30p242#3/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Those heedless of prayer are unconcerned enough to miss it altogether or let its time expire, or they perform it unlike the Prophet and early Muslims, hurriedly and without reverence or attention.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p242#3/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · أبو العالية | reports | Abu al-Aliya explains heedlessness as turning one's gaze right and left.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p242#3/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · قتادة | reports | Qatada says it is not caring whether one has prayed.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p242#3/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas and a group identify it as delaying prayer beyond its appointed time.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p242#3/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · سعد بن أبي وقاص | reports | A report from Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas raises this interpretation to the Prophet, but al-Hakim and al-Bayhaqi judge its stopped Companion form sounder.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p242#3/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · أبو العالية | reports | A second explanation from Abu al-Aliya is that a person does not know how many units he completed, even or odd.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p242#3/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · بعضهم | reports | Some interpret heedlessness as abandoning prayer; on a total-abandonment reading the people named are those marked as worshippers, while occasional abandonment means the congregational worshippers.
[ELMALILI:107:2-7#3/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Religious pretenders who neglect prayer, show off, and withhold even small help are more astonishingly blameworthy than disbelievers who deny religion and mistreat orphans and the poor.
[ELMALILI:107:2-7#3/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | After the address to the Prophet to see and understand, Sūrat al-Kawthar condenses the preceding suras, especially those from al-Ḍuḥā, as a final account of Muhammad's rank. | mentions 93:1
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:1/r2] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | Although Qutb leans toward a wholly Medinan setting because hypocrisy and ostentation were unfamiliar in Mecca, he allows later verses to have been appended for thematic continuity.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:1/r3] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | The seven short verses overturn conventional ideas of faith and disbelief and reveal the creed’s mercy and immense good for humanity.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:1/r5] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | Religion is an integrated way of life whose worship, rites, and personal and social duties purify hearts and lead people to cooperation, care, and flourishing.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:4/r1] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | The warning of ruin applies to people who are heedless of their prayer.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:4/r2] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | Those heedless worshippers are the ones who show off and withhold small acts of help.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:4/r3] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | They perform prayer but fail to establish it.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:5/r1] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | They carry out prayer’s movements and recite its supplications, but their hearts and spirits do not experience its recitations, prayers, or glorifications.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:6/r1] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | They pray for people’s approval instead of sincerely for God, so they remain heedless of the prayer they are performing.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:6/r2] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | The required act is establishing prayer, which requires conscious presence and standing before God alone.
[IBNASHUR:107:5/r1] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The relative clause restricts the woe to worshipers inattentive to their prayer, not worshipers generally, and further reinforces the mockery.
[IBNASHUR:107:5/r2] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Using ʿan with inattentive means they have gone beyond establishing prayer and abandoned it; the verse is unrelated to the legal rules for mistakes within prayer.
[IBNASHUR:107:5/r3] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | One possible meaning is people who pray only for show and abandon prayer when alone.
[IBNASHUR:107:5/r4] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Another possible meaning is people who pray without intention or sincerity, seeming heedless while praying; calling them heedless is mockery. | mentions 4:142
[IBNASHUR:107:7/r3] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · هبة الله بن سلامة | reports | Hibat Allah ibn Salama reports in al-Nasikh wa-l-Mansukh that these three verses were revealed about ʿAbd Allah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul.
[IBNASHUR:107:7/r5] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Sahw literally means losing awareness of something previously known; here it is a mocking metaphor for deliberate turning away and abandonment.
[IBNASHUR:107:7/r6] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The metaphor resembles saying that people forget their false gods, where forget means turn away from them. | mentions 6:41
[IBNASHUR:107:7/r7] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The author also compares metaphorical heedlessness with turning away from God's signs, in two other verses. | mentions 7:136, 10:7
[IBNASHUR:107:7/r8] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | rejects | The threat is not for genuine forgetfulness in prayer, since forgetfulness is lifted from this community; the description instead mocks people called worshipers who do not pray.
[IBNASHUR:107:7/r9] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The ruling on real forgetfulness confirms the mockery: their label as worshipers describes people who in fact do not pray.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p566/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The fā explicitly links the warning to the preceding description; on a wholly Meccan reading, the same denier neglects prayer, performs it for show, and withholds aid.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p566/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Calling them “prayers” is mockery: they do not truly pray and are not Muslims, as in 74:43–44; naming them explicitly avoids an awkward list of six traits.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p566/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The placement of wayl before the explicit noun signals punishment for all the preceding vices, as al-Kashshaf hints.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p566/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · مالك | reports | Ibn Wahb and Ashhab transmit from Malik the Medinan interpretation that these heedless prayers are hypocrites.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p566/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The plural fits the generic deniers; if a particular person is intended, it generalizes the warning to others sharing neglect of prayer, ostentation, and refusal of aid.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p567/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The relative clause restricts the warning to prayer-neglecters and reinforces the taunting use of “prayers.”
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p567/r10] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · عائشة | reports | Aisha interprets maʿun as water, fire, and salt.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p567/r11] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | This reading condemns miserliness at its worst: refusing things that cost nothing to lend.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p567/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Sahun is followed by ʿan to convey abandoning prayer; the verse does not concern legal rulings about forgetfulness during prayer.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p567/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | They may pray only to be seen, abandoning prayer when alone, or pray without intention and sincerity; in either case calling them heedless is mockery.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p567/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Riyāʾ means presenting a false good appearance so people praise one; samʿa is often paired with it.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p567/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The verb is attested only in form III, whose reciprocal pattern suits repeated acts of showing.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p567/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Al-Maʿun can mean financial assistance: they withhold surplus wealth or charity, which was obligatory early on before fixed zakat.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p567/r7] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · سعيد بن المسيب | reports | Said ibn al-Musayyab and Ibn Shihab say maʿun means money in Quraysh dialect.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p567/r8] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · مالك | reports | Ashhab transmits Malik's view that maʿun means zakat; al-Raʿi's poem pairs it with prayer, meaning zakat and prayer.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p567/r9] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | Maʿun can also mean household utensils and tools whose lending costs the owner nothing.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p568/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Sahw literally means inadvertent forgetfulness, but here it sarcastically means deliberate neglect; genuine forgetfulness in prayer is not the target because it is excused for this community.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p568/r7] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The deliberate-neglect sense parallels “forgetting” one's associates as turning away, and being heedless of signs in al-Aʿraf and Yunus.
