33:43/q22 What do reports say when people ask whether or how God prays? [يُصَلِّى · reports] (turns on: The reported settings differ between Moses, the Prophet's answer, and two Miʿrāj versions.)
  33:43/q22/p1 Israelites asked Moses; God answered that His prayer is mercy, which precedes or extinguishes wrath and encompasses all. — reasons: The accounts vary in whether mercy precedes anger, extinguishes anger, or encompasses everything; Yaḥyā adds that without it they would perish. Al-Naḥḥās's account says they asked the Prophet about his Lord's prayer, though he cites the Israelite question.
  33:43/q22/p2 Asked how God prays on servants, the Prophet replied with glorification and 'My mercy preceded My wrath.' — reasons: Both transmit a report from an unnamed group.
  33:43/q22/p3 A Miʿrāj account has Muḥammad ask why the self-sufficient Lord prays and God answer with mercy preceding anger. — reasons: Bursevī places the exchange at two-bows distance, without Gabriel, and connects it to revelation of this verse.
  33:43/q22/p4 A second Miʿrāj version has Gabriel announce God's prayer in the seventh heaven and an answer naming His holiness and prior mercy. — reasons: This version names the Holy Lord of angels and Spirit.
  33:43/q22/p5 The Prophet first proclaimed divine transcendence because the questioner imagined an improper bodily sense of God's prayer. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī explain the opening glorification as a guard against an unsuitable conception.
33:43/q23 Whose speech is the Prophet's reported answer about divine prayer? [يُصَلِّى · reports] (turns on: Whether the glorifying words are divine speech or the Prophet's preface.)
  33:43/q23/p1 The entire phrase, including 'Most Holy, Most Pure,' is God's speech and His prayer on servants. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī report this parsing of the answer.
  33:43/q23/p2 The glorification is Muḥammad's preface, and 'My mercy preceded My wrath' is God's explanation. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya explains the Prophet's preface as declaring divine transcendence against the questioner's improper understanding.
33:43/q24 Does this verse support reading the prior morning and evening commands as prayer times? [* · links] (turns on: Whether the preceding time phrases concern prayer times and whether a report from an early period can be supported by a Medinan verse.)
  33:43/q24/p1 The prayer wording supports reading morning and evening as prayer times. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī prefers that reading on the basis of God's prayer upon believers.
  33:43/q24/p2 The verse is Medinan, so it cannot establish an early two-prayer prescription at the day's edges. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya also calls the report of that early prescription weak.
  33:43/q24/p3 In the preceding command, al-aṣīl runs from ʿAṣr to night. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives the time's extent.
33:43/q25 What broader distinction or intertext does this verse illustrate? [* · links] (turns on: The notes supply several distinct cross-text and surah links.)
  33:43/q25/p1 The believers' receipt of God's and angels' prayer is a great blessing and evidence of this community's excellence. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī cites 3:110.
  33:43/q25/p2 The angels' prayer here and in 33:56 is seeking forgiveness. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī compares both verses; Islahi uses 33:56 for the distinction between divine mercy and angelic petition.
  33:43/q25/p3 The darkness-to-light motif has Quranic and scriptural parallels. — reasons: Study Quran cites 2:257, 5:16, 14:1, 14:5, 57:9, 65:11; compares Isaiah 42:16 and 1 Peter 2:9; and points to 6:1 and 24:35.
  33:43/q25/p4 The connection between this prayer and the preceding passage is raised without an answer. — reasons: Al-Jishumī poses the question.
