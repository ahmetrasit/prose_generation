2:31/q45 How is divine revelation said to be delivered? [وَعَلَّمَ · theology] (turns on: Direct hearing versus transmission of meaning or exact wording.)
  2:31/q45/p1 Gabriel hears God's revelation and conveys it, while reported accounts distinguish relaying meaning from exact dictation. — reasons: Al-Suyūṭī cites heavenly trembling and chain-like sound reports and favors hearing; Abū Shāma connects descent language to Sunni eternal-Qur'an doctrine. Al-Juwaynī distinguishes equivalent expression from fixed dictated words.
2:31/q46 What other general observations about names are adduced? [ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ · sciences] (turns on: Miscellaneous naming and plural observations.)
  2:31/q46/p1 Names may be shared or multiplied, and spelling, plurality and derivation depend on usage. — reasons: Al-Suyūṭī notes shared or multiple sura names and prophetic names; al-Burhān discusses alif deletion in common foreign names and paucity plural patterns. These illustrate broader naming conventions.
2:31/q47 What does the transition 'so He said' indicate? [فَقَالَ · rhetoric]
  2:31/q47/p1 Fa marks an immediate consequence of the display, without a pause for reflection. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān treats it as immediate succession; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the question follows as the result of the presentation.
2:31/q48 How do names and relative clauses identify definite people? [ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ · grammar] (turns on: Whether relative clauses are indefinite new information or recall an already known event.)
  2:31/q48/p1 A relative clause introduced by alladhī identifies a person through an event already known in general. — reasons: Al-Jurjānī says alladhī lets a clause modify a definite noun, analogous to dhū; he refines the claim that clauses are inherently indefinite because they convey new information.
2:31/q49 What does the challenge imply about God's authority to choose Adam? [فَقَالَ أَنۢبِـُٔونِى · theology]
  2:31/q49/p1 God's command and choice rest on His knowledge and sovereignty, not on creatures' judgment of what seems preferable. — reasons: Al-Qushayrī says God may command whom He wills and that good or bad follows divine judgment; Bursevī contrasts angelic worship with divine grace.
2:31/q50 How are the angels' response and their limited knowledge described? [ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ · interpretation]
  2:31/q50/p1 They admitted inability and learned that knowledge is God's gift. — reasons: Al-Baghawī and al-Ṭabrisī report the admission; al-Ḥaralī treats it as submission and recognition that none has independent knowledge.
2:31/q51 What does ‘upon the angels’ modify? [عَلَى ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ · grammar]
  2:31/q51/p1 The prepositional phrase attaches to ‘displayed them.’ — reasons: Al-Samīn explicitly gives this syntactic attachment.
