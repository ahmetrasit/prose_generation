  50:16/q22/p1 It answers resurrection deniers by moving from creation and God's knowledge to recording, death and requital. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya says these verses prove resurrection and requital. 50:16/q23 What responses should awareness of divine nearness evoke? [* · inward]
  50:16/q23/p1 It produces fear and awe in some, tranquility and intimacy in others, and calls for obedience and remembrance. — reasons: Qushayrī gives the contrasting effects; Biqāʿī repeats him. Al-Sulamī reports that al-Kharrāz describes travelers reached by God without seeking or flight, and al-Wāsiṭī grounds nearness in creation, gathering and spirit and says self-knowledge comes through God in a measure humans can bear. Another report says awareness leads to obedience and remembrance in public and private. Study Quran says people can remain distant from God despite His constant nearness.
50:16/q24 How is the vocabulary of nearness used beyond spatial distance? [* · meaning]
  50:16/q24/p1 Qurb can indicate time, relation, honor, benefit, care or power as well as place. — reasons: Elmalılı cites Rāghib's range across many verses, including 2:186, 34:37 and 7:56. He says the verse allows power and knowledge, while spatial interval would contradict divine encompassing.
50:16/q25 Why can a speaker call the near God with the distant vocative يَا? [* · rhetoric]
  50:16/q25/p1 The caller feels humility and remoteness from divine favor despite God's nearness. — reasons: Al-Burhān notes that يَا normally calls someone distant.
50:16/q26 What does the cited love poem's figurative language mean? [* · meaning] (turns on: The poetic alternatives turn on whether aʿshār evokes shattered pieces or the ten shares of gaming arrows.)
  50:16/q26/p1 Its command to the heart means a command to the speaker, not a command to a literally acting heart. — reasons: Bāqillānī rejects literal heart-command as impossible; he calls the first line weak because self-deprecation would justify abandonment despite the lover's claim to deserve attachment, while the second hemistich is unusually intimate.
  50:16/q26/p2 The garment image can stand for the body, or for separation and abandonment. — reasons: Bāqillānī reports the body reading by comparison with 74:4. Abū ʿUbayda says tansal means to part.
  50:16/q26/p3 Aʿshār means the speaker's heart is shattered into pieces. — reasons: Al-Aṣmaʿī compares a broken vessel called aʿshār; Bāqillānī finds this more likely.
  50:16/q26/p4 The two eyes are the muʿallā and raqīb arrows, holding seven and three camel shares, so she takes the whole heart. — reasons: Bāqillānī objects that if she already owns the whole heart, her tears cannot newly make it wholly hers.

  50:16/q27/p1 The human is a wondrous, comprehensive creature combining opposing traits and watched in every state. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī lists intimacy and arrogance, memory and forgetfulness, ignorance and knowledge, obedience and disobedience, and says God appoints guardians over every state.

  50:16/q29/p1 It returns to the human being whom God created. — reasons: Būrsawī says God is nearer to that human than his jugular vein.
