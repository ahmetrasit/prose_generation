[SAMIN-DURR:1:2/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib says hamd can concern chosen and innate qualities, while shukr answers a favor; he also explains mahmud as praised, Muhammad as much-praised, and Ahmad as surpassing others in praise.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r44] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | In the divine hadith, “prayer” means recitation, as in 17:110; because the Fatiha details the division, its recitation is a major pillar of prayer. | mentions 17:110
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r45] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | “Qur'an of dawn” in 17:78 means the dawn prayer, as the sound reports say angels of night and day witness it; scholars agree prayer requires recitation. | mentions 17:78
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r1] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · القراء السبعة | reports | The seven canonical readers vocalize the dāl of al-ḥamdu with ḍamma, making al-ḥamdu a subject and li-llāhi its predicate.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r10] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Ibn Kathir questions Ibn Jarir's equation: praise is verbal commendation for necessary or bestowed qualities, while thanks applies only to bestowed qualities and may be inward, spoken, or enacted.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r11] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Ibn Kathir resolves the scope dispute by saying praise covers more kinds of objects but is speech-only, whereas thanks uses more kinds of means but concerns bestowed qualities alone.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r12] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · الجوهري | reports | Al-Jawhari defines praise as the opposite of blame, says taḥmīd is more emphatic than ḥamd, and regards ḥamd as broader than thanks.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r13] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · الجوهري | reports | Al-Jawhari defines thanks as praising a benefactor for what he bestowed and says using the preposition li- is more eloquent.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r14] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Ibn Kathir distinguishes praise from eulogy: eulogy can concern living or dead people and even objects, precede or follow a favor, and address necessary or bestowed qualities.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r15] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · علي بن أبي طالب | reports | When Umar asks what al-ḥamdu lillāh means after learning other formulas of glorification, Ali answers that it is a phrase God approves for Himself.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r16] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says al-ḥamdu lillāh is a word of thanks; when a servant says it, God says that His servant has thanked Him.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r17] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas explains praise as thanks, humble submission, and acknowledgment of God's favors, guidance, and origination.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r18] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · كعب الأحبار | reports | Ka'b al-Ahbar says al-ḥamdu lillāh is God's praise.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r19] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak calls al-ḥamdu lillāh the cloak of the Most Merciful and notes that a hadith has come with a similar meaning.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r2] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · سفيان بن عيينة ورؤبة بن العجاج | reports | Sufyan ibn Uyaynah and Ru'bah ibn al-Ajjaj read al-ḥamda with fatḥa as an implied verbal object.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r20] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report attributed to the Prophet says that whoever says al-ḥamdu lillāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn has thanked God, who then increases him.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r21] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Companion al-Aswad ibn Sari asks to recite praises of his Lord, and the Prophet replies that his Lord loves praise.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r22] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet says the best remembrance is lā ilāha illā Allāh and the best supplication is al-ḥamdu lillāh; al-Tirmidhi grades this report ḥasan gharīb.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r23] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Anas reports that when a servant praises God for a blessing, the reward for that praise is better than the blessing received.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r24] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · القرطبي | reports | Al-Qurtubi transmits a report that praising God would be better than possessing the whole world; he explains that the enduring reward for praise exceeds transient worldly enjoyment. | mentions 18:46
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r25] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Ibn Umar reports that angels cannot calculate the full reward for a servant's praise fitting God's majesty, so God tells them to record it as said until He rewards him.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r26] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · طائفة | reports | A group holds that al-ḥamdu lillāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn is better than lā ilāha illā Allāh because it combines praise with monotheism.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r27] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · آخرون | reports | Others hold that lā ilāha illā Allāh is superior because it distinguishes faith from unbelief and is the formula people must profess; reports also call it the best remembrance.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r28] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | The definite article in al-ḥamdu encompasses every kind and form of praise as belonging to God, as expressed in a transmitted prayer.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r29] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Rabb means owner and disposer, and in Arabic also master or one who manages something toward its good; each meaning applies to God.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r3] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · ابن أبي عبلة | reports | Ibn Abi Abla reads both dāl and lām with ḍamma by assimilating the second vowel to the first; Ibn Kathir calls the reading anomalous.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r30] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | The unqualified title al-Rabb is used only for God; for other possessors it must be annexed, and some say al-Rabb is the greatest name.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r31] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Al-ʿālamīn is the plural of ʿālam, encompassing every created being besides God; created kinds in heavens, earth, land, and sea are worlds, as is each generation.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r32] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas describes the worlds as all creation belonging to God: heavens, earths, their inhabitants and everything between them, known and unknown.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r33] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · ابن عباس وسعيد بن جبير وعكرمة | reports | Ibn Abbas, through Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Ikrimah, and other early authorities interpret the worlds as jinn and humans; Ibn Abi Hatim's chain is said to be unreliable.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r34] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · القرطبي | reports | Al-Qurtubi supports the interpretation of the worlds as jinn and humans by citing the description of the Qur'an as a warning to the worlds. | mentions 25:1
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r35] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · الفراء وأبو عبيدة | reports | Al-Farra and Abu Ubayda restrict the term worlds to rational beings—humans, jinn, angels, and devils—and exclude animals.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r36] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · زيد بن أسلم وأبو عمرو بن العلاء | reports | Zayd ibn Aslam and Abu Amr ibn al-Ala define worlds as everything with a spirit that receives provision.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r37] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · مروان بن محمد بن مروان بن الحكم | reports | Ibn Asakir reports Marwan's claim that God created seventeen thousand worlds, with the inhabitants of the heavens and earth forming one and God alone knowing the rest.
[IBNKATHIR:1:2/r38] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · قتادة | reports | Qatadah says each kind or species is a world.
