verse describing a people who strayed earlier, misled many, and left the right way. | mentions 5:77
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r44] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · مكي | reports | Makkī says lā prevents readers from mistaking the astray for a coordinate of الذين.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r45] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | reports | Another reported explanation treats lā as emphatic, with the force of ghayr.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r46] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · الطبري | reports | Al-Ṭabarī calls lā extra, citing a rajaz line where ألا تسخرا is understood as أن تسخر.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r47] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | rejects | The author rejects al-Ṭabarī’s reading of al-Aḥwaṣ’s line as proof that lā is extra: it means they reproach him for wanting not to love amusement, so lā is operative.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r48] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · الطبري | reports | Al-Ṭabarī also cites ما منعك أن لا تسجد and a poem after an earlier negation, where lā may be omitted because the opening negation governs the later clause. | mentions 7:12
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r49] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · عمر بن الخطاب وأبي بن كعب | reports | ʿUmar and Ubayy read a second ghayr before “the astray”; both accusative and genitive forms are reported for each ghayr.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r5] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | reports | Some Arabs inflect the plural relative, using اللذون in the nominative.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r50] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | holds | In answer to al-Ṭabarī’s question about assigning one label to each group, the author says Jewish transgression, obstinacy, rejection despite signs, and killing prophets characteristically warrant wrath; Christians’ basic unbelief is astray shared by all unbelievers.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r51] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | rejects | Calling Christians astray does not support the Qadari argument that they created their own misguidance: as with a wall collapsing or a tree moving, God created their misguidance while they acquired the act of straying.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r52] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · أيوب السختياني | reports | Ayyūb al-Sakhtiyānī reads الضألين with a short hamza to avoid two adjacent sukūns, treating it as a dialect form.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r53] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | holds | Consensus counts al-Fātiḥa as seven verses, ending them respectively at العالمين, الرحيم, الدين, نستعين, المستقيم, أنعمت عليهم, and ولا الضالين; he says the weak dispute over the basmala was discussed earlier.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r54] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · أبو هريرة عن النبي | reports | The Prophet tells worshippers to say Āmīn when the imam says “nor those who are astray”; the angels in heaven say it, and agreement with them brings forgiveness of past sins.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r55] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · جبريل | reports | A report says Gabriel taught the Prophet al-Fātiḥa at its revelation; after the Prophet recited it, Gabriel told him to say Āmīn.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r56] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · علي بن أبي طالب | reports | ʿAlī calls Āmīn the seal of the Lord of the worlds, with which the believing servant seals his supplication.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r57] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · النبي | reports | A report says the Prophet heard a man supplicate and said his request was assured if he concluded; when asked how, he answered: with Āmīn.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r58] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · الحسن بن أبي الحسن وأحمد بن يحيى ثعلب | reports | Most scholars understand Āmīn as “O God, answer” or “Answer, Lord,” and al-Ḥasan and Thaʿlab are named for this explanation.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r59] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · جعفر بن محمد ومجاهد وهلال بن يساف | reports | Another view makes Āmīn one of God’s names; it is reported from Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad, Mujāhid, and Hilāl ibn Yasāf.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r6] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | holds | The form الذي is conventionally written with one lām in singular and plural because it is so frequent.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r60] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | reports | A further report calls Āmīn the name of a seal used to stamp the books of the people of Paradise, which they receive by faith.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r61] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | holds | The author infers from these reports that supplicants should say Āmīn at the end of every supplication and after al-Fātiḥa outside prayer, without loud recitation.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r62] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · بعض العلماء | reports | One legal view has every worshipper—imam, praying alone, or follower—say Āmīn if they recite or hear al-Fātiḥa.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r63] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · مالك | reports | In the Mudawwana, Mālik says the imam does not say Āmīn, while followers say it quietly and a person praying alone says it.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r64] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · مالك | reports | Other reports from Mālik say that the imam says Āmīn either quietly or aloud, or does not say it during audible prayer.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r65] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · ابن حبيب | reports | Ibn Ḥabīb holds that the imam should say Āmīn.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r66] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · ابن بكير | reports | Ibn Bukayr says the imam may choose whether to say it.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r67] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | holds | The disagreement concerns the imam; no disagreement applies to the person praying alone or the follower except for Ibn Nāfiʿ’s position.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r68] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · ابن نافع | reports | Ibn Nāfiʿ says a follower says Āmīn only if he hears the imam reach “nor those who are astray”; if too far away to hear, he omits it.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r69] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · ابن عبدوس | reports | Ibn ʿAbdūs tells a distant follower to estimate the length of the recitation and then say Āmīn.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r7] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · ابن عباس وجمهور من المفسرين | reports | They identify the favored people as prophets, the truthful, martyrs, and righteous, citing the description of those whom God guides to a straight path. | mentions 4:66, 4:67, 4:68, 4:69
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r70] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | holds | Āmīn is built on fatḥa because two sukūns meet; fatḥa is lighter with yāʾ than the other vowel options.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r71] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | reports | Some Arabs lengthen Āmīn; a poetic line repeats the long form and imagines needing two thousand Āmīns.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r72] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · جبير بن الأضبط | reports | Some Arabs shorten it to أمين, as in a line attributed to Jubayr ibn al-Aḍbaṭ.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r73] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | reports | One unnamed explanation takes agreeing with the angels to mean agreement in the response to prayer.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r74] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | reports | A second unnamed explanation takes it to mean sincerity of intention.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r75] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | reports | A third unnamed explanation takes it to mean agreement in timing.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r76] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | prefers | The author prefers agreement in timing together with sincere intention and wholehearted turning to God; the response follows from this state.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r77] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | holds | He concludes that a person in this state is upon the straight path.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r8] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās also glosses the favored people simply as the believers.
[IBNATIYYA:1:7/r9] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · الحسن بن أبي الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan identifies the favored people as the Companions of Muḥammad.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p75#2/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · نافع | reports | Nafiʿ is reported with a choice of ḍamma on the hāʾ and a silent mīm; the author infers that his customary reading was with mīm silent.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p75#2/r10] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · ابن جني | reports | Ibn Jinni reports readings with and without a lengthened wāw and with mīm silent.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p75#2/r11] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · الحسن وعمرو بن فائد | reports | Al-Hasan and ʿAmr ibn Faʾid read ʿalayhimi with a final kasra on the mīm.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p75#2/r12] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · الأعرج | reports | Al-Aʿraj reads the hāʾ and yāʾ with kasra and mīm with ḍamma, without lengthening the yāʾ.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p75#2/r2] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · ابن كثير | reports | Ibn Kathir joins a ḍamma-bearing mīm to a wāw after the hāʾ, whether that hāʾ is pronounced with ḍamma or kasra.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p75#2/r3] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · ورش | reports | Warsh reads hāʾ with kasra and leaves the mīm unvocalized, except when it precedes an original alif, when he inserts a wāw in pronunciation.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p75#2/r4] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · أبو عمرو وعاصم وابن عامر والكسائي | reports | Abu ʿAmr, ʿAsim, Ibn ʿAmir, and al-Kisaʾi read the hāʾ with kasra and the mīm silent; before a following consonant, their treatment of hāʾ and mīm vowels differs.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p75#2/r5] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · عاصم وابن كثير ونافع | reports | Before a consonant, ʿAsim, Ibn Kathir, and Nafiʿ keep hāʾ kasra and give mīm ḍamma, as in “upon them is humiliation.” | mentions 2:61, 3:112
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p75#2/r6] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · أبو عمرو | reports | Abu ʿAmr keeps both hāʾ and mīm with kasra in the same environment. | mentions 36:14
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p75#2/r7] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · الكسائي | reports | Al-Kisaʾi gives both hāʾ and mīm ḍamma in the same environment. | mentions 2:61, 28:23
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p75#2/r8] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · أبو بكر أحمد بن موسى | reports | Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn Musa says variation in hāʾ depends on whether it follows kasra or a quiescent yāʾ; elsewhere only ḍamma occurs, while mīm may be silent or have ḍamma in the stated contexts.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p75#2/r9] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · صاحب الدلائل | reports | The Dalāʾil source lists five transmitted combinations of hāʾ and mīm vowels, with or without a lengthened wāw or yāʾ.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p75/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · عمر بن الخطاب وابن الزبير | reports | ʿUmar and Ibn al-Zubayr read “the path of those whom You favored” as “the path of whom You favored.”
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p75/r10] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | prefers | If the favored are Muhammad, Abu Bakr, and ʿUmar, their persons metaphorically constitute the path; the author says this interpretation is stronger in meaning.
