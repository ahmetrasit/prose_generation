[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p471/r5] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · ابن فارس | reports | Ibn Fāris says the root r-j-ʿ denotes return and repetition, and explains al-rajʿ as rain because the sky gives rain and returns to give it again.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p471/r6] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · أبو هلال العسكري | reports | Abū Hilāl al-ʿAskarī distinguishes rujūʿ from iyāb: iyāb is return to the intended endpoint, as in 88:25. | mentions 88:25
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p471/r7] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · author | holds | The author suggests this semantic distinction may have informed Nāfiʿ’s question about iyāb, implying that it is not simply synonymous with rujūʿ.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p261/r7] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Verses 11–14 invoke a general physical phenomenon to show that the threatened Judgement is certain, not jest, and that people should prepare for it.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:86:11/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · müfessirler | reports | One interpretation of the sky’s “return” is rain or rain-bearing clouds: the sky takes up earthly water as vapor, turns it into rain, and returns it, in a recurring cycle.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:86:11/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · İbn Kesîr | reports | Another interpretation refers to stars, the sun, and the moon repeatedly setting and rising.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:86:11/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | prefers | The Turkish translation adopts the interpretation about the recurring setting and rising of stars, the sun, and the moon.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:86:11/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | By swearing by the sky and earth where these majestic events occur, the passage teaches that just as they are real manifestations of God’s power, the Qur’an is true and manifests His attribute of speech.
[STUDYQURAN:86:11/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The seasonal cycles and vegetation growth are signs of God's power to resurrect, paralleling the emergence of humans from base fluid in verses 6–8. | mentions 86:6, 86:7, 86:8
[STUDYQURAN:86:11/r2] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | In reference to heaven, rajʿ, or resurgence, refers to rains that return periodically.
[STUDYQURAN:86:11/r3] STUDYQURAN · IK, Ṭ, Ṭs | reports | These commentators interpret the resurgent sky as rain or the clouds that bring rain.
[STUDYQURAN:86:11/r4] STUDYQURAN · Ṭ | reports | Another reported interpretation takes the resurgent sky as the continual return of the sun, moon, and other celestial bodies.
