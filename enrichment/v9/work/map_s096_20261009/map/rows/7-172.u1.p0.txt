## THE CURRENT MAP
7:172/q01 What does 'when' ask the audience to recall? [وَإِذْ · grammar]
  7:172/q01/p1 It is the object of an omitted command to remember the covenant. — reasons: Al-Zajjaj makes idh accusative; Ibn Ashur calls it past time, not an adverb. Islahi treats it as recalling a definite, though forgotten, event.
7:172/q02 What does God's taking mean here? [أَخَذَ · meaning] (turns on: Whether taking denotes extraction, God's encompassing possession, or the binding covenant; the surrounding wording and reported scene guide the reading.)
  7:172/q02/p1 God extracted or brought offspring into being from their forebears. — reasons: Ibn Ashur compares 6:46. Tabatabai says taking separates offspring without diminishing the source, yielding distinct accountable persons.
  7:172/q02/p2 Taking is encompassing and ownership. — reasons: Tabatabai connects it with God's proof over all creatures.
  7:172/q02/p3 It is the binding imposition of a covenant, figured as a weight on the back. — reasons: Abu Hayyan cites covenant formulas at 4:154 and 2:83 and specifies monotheism and exclusive worship.
  7:172/q02/p4 Divine taking brings the nonexistent into existence by God's power. — reasons: The Najmiyya distinguishes God's creation from a creature's taking an existing thing; it cites 19:9.
7:172/q03 Who are the children of Adam addressed in the covenant? [بَنِىٓ ءَادَمَ · referent] (turns on: The plural backs and the following excuse about polytheist ancestors are read either as specifying a subgroup or as part of an account embracing every human.)
  7:172/q03/p1 All humanity through the final generation is included. — reasons: Ubayy's report includes everyone born before the Hour; Alusi rejects a restriction to Jewish ancestors. Tabatabai argues otherwise untaken persons could plead ignorance.
  7:172/q03/p2 Only certain descendants, particularly those with idolatrous ancestors or reached by prophets, are meant. — reasons: The plural backs excludes Adam's immediate children, and the next verse's ancestral-shirk excuse narrows the group; Bursevi excludes people who had no offspring.
  7:172/q03/p3 The messengers were specifically addressed. — reasons: Abd al-Malik ibn Abi Yazid and Wahb ibn Munabbih are named for this view.
7:172/q04 How is 'from their backs' related to 'children of Adam'? [مِنۢ بَنِىٓ ءَادَمَ مِن ظُهُورِهِمْ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the plural backs is simply an appositive account of generational origin or excludes the Adam-back account.)
  7:172/q04/p1 The latter is a partitive apposition specifying the former: descendants are drawn from human fathers' backs. — reasons: The repeated min reinforces apposition; Samīn cites analogous part-whole idiom, while Ibn Ashur cites 6:99. Alusi prefers the partitive parsing.
  7:172/q04/p2 The apposition is of inclusion, relating bodies or members to the people. — reasons: Abu al-Baqa gives this parsing; Alusi questions its rationale.
  7:172/q04/p3 The wording describes successive generations, so Adam's own back need not be repeated. — reasons: Al-Kinani and others say all are Adam's offspring and descend through one another; Ibn Ashur argues Adam's immediate children are included a fortiori.
  7:172/q04/p4 The plural backs specifically excludes a direct taking from Adam's single back as the verse's meaning. — reasons: The Mu'tazilites and Tabarsi stress the plural nouns; Ibn Atiyya considers a root-and-branch reconciliation weak.
7:172/q05 What happened when the descendants were taken and addressed? [أَخَذَ ذُرِّيَّتَهُمْ وَأَشْهَدَهُمْ · interpretation] (turns on: The grammatical contrast between Adam's back and descendants' backs, the status of extraction reports, whether pre-birth testimony could be remembered and serve as proof, and whether speech can be real outside ordinary bodily life.)
  7:172/q05/p1 God actually extracted Adam's progeny before earthly life and elicited a spoken covenant. — reasons: Reports describe a touch, stroke, shoulder strike, combing or scattering; particles or formed spirits were given understanding. Al-Zajjaj invokes the speaking ant and praising mountains; Tabatabai argues speech can be real beyond ordinary organs. Razi and Ibn Atiyya defend combining the verse with the reports.
  7:172/q05/p2 The dialogue represents natural generation and the rational recognition of God's oneness from signs. — reasons: Zamakhshari compares 41:11 and 16:40; poems personify limbs and the wind. Tabarsi also cites 9:17. Ibn Kathir cites circumstantial testimony at 6:130 and 9:17 and stresses the plural backs. Abu Hayyan finds the hadith reconcilations strained. Bursevi treats the exchange as signs in souls and horizons.
  7:172/q05/p3 Humanity truly witnessed in a prior divine realm whose precedence is not ordinary chronological pre-birth life. — reasons: Tabatabai distinguishes measured worldly unfolding from the complete existence with God in 15:21, 36:82–83 and 54:50; the same humanity witnesses directly in malakut. He rejects identifying later persons with tiny bodily particles.
  7:172/q05/p4 The covenant was taken from souls before their bodies were created. — reasons: Muhammad ibn Ka'b and others report pre-body acknowledgment; one view locates it in Paradise and another after descent. The Study Quran identifies this as a pretemporal pact.
  7:172/q05/p5 Affirm the covenant without fixing its unseen mechanism. — reasons: Kuranyolu says the duty is to accept that humans can know God's lordship and keep the pledge, since precise unseen details exceed human knowledge.
7:172/q06 Where was the primordial extraction said to occur? [أَخَذَ رَبُّكَ · reports] (turns on: The geographical and descent-stage variants in transmitted reports; Alusi questions the basis of India and Kaaba locations.)
  7:172/q06/p1 At Nu'man near Arafah. — reasons: Prophetic and Ibn Abbas reports identify the valley; variants call it Batn or Wadi Nu'man and connect it with the Day of Arafah.
  7:172/q06/p2 In India, where Adam descended. — reasons: Reports vary among Dajni, Dahna, Barhaba and Rahba; Ibn Atiyya also mentions Sind.
  7:172/q06/p3 Between Mecca and Taif. — reasons: Al-Kalbi and Muqatil are named for this site.
  7:172/q06/p4 In heaven or Paradise before descent. — reasons: Some reports specify the lowest heaven; others Paradise or heaven generally.
  7:172/q06/p5 At the Kaaba, with particle-like descendants surrounding Adam. — reasons: Alusi reports this location but says it and India lack a dependable basis.
7:172/q07 When did the covenant occur relative to Adam's descent and human births? [وَإِذْ أَخَذَ · reports] (turns on: Reports place Adam before or after descent, while interpretive readings locate the covenant in birth, prior non-temporal existence, or more than one event.)
  7:172/q07/p1 Before Adam's descent from Paradise. — reasons: Al-Suddi places it in the lowest heaven; some place it in Paradise. Reports also give a time when the Throne was over water.
  7:172/q07/p2 After Adam's descent to earth. — reasons: Reports locate the event at Arafah or in India after descent; some say Adam was in water when descendants emerged.
  7:172/q07/p3 As each person develops in the father's loins or mother's womb, and later reaches understanding. — reasons: This is the worldly, generational reading; Kuranyolu distinguishes possible stages of the innate endowment.
  7:172/q07/p4 Before spirits were breathed into the descendants' bodies. — reasons: Islahi calls it an actual event, with innate cognizance lasting after the occasion is forgotten.
  7:172/q07/p5 There were multiple covenant-taking events or kinds of covenant. — reasons: Sufi masters propose this to reconcile reports, while Alusi says their language suggests different kinds; Al-Shirazi distinguishes present rational from primordial verbal covenant.
7:172/q08 What became of the extracted descendants before their earthly births? [ذُرِّيَّتَهُمْ · reports]
  7:172/q08/p1 God returned them to Adam's or their fathers' loins, and each decreed person would be born. — reasons: Ibn Abbas says their number neither increases nor decreases and the Hour waits for all who testified. Reports differ on whether the return is to Adam alone or successive fathers.
7:172/q09 What does the word 'offspring' mean and how is it derived? [ذُرِّيَّتَهُمْ · meaning] (turns on: Proposed roots connect the word either to small scattered particles or to God's act of creating.)
  7:172/q09/p1 It means progeny and can denote one person, a group, or a collective. — reasons: Abu Ali compares the individual in Zechariah's prayer (3:38–39; 19:5–7) and collective bashar (12:31; 64:6; 14:10).
  7:172/q09/p2 It derives from dharr, tiny scattered particles or ants. — reasons: Proposals include faʿūlūla with rāʾ changed to yāʾ, faʿīla resolving doubling, and a nisba with fatḥa changed to ḍamma; the narrative pictures particles or seeds.
  7:172/q09/p3 It derives from dhara'a, to bring creatures into being and make them manifest. — reasons: Abu Ali compares the contracted form al-bariyya; one further proposal invokes the scattering winds of 18:45.
7:172/q10 How is 'their offspring' read? [ذُرِّيَّتَهُمْ · readings] (turns on: Whether the collective singular sufficiently conveys all offspring or a plural better marks the many generations.)
  7:172/q10/p1 It is read as singular dhurriyyatahum, collective in sense. — reasons: Ibn Kathir, Asim, Hamza and Kisai, or Mecca and Kufa, use it; its collective force is compared with bashar and 24:31. Nashr notes the pattern across Yasin and al-Tur.
  7:172/q10/p2 It is read as plural dhurriyyatihim, explicitly covering multiple generations. — reasons: Nafi, Abu Amr, Ibn Amir and in some enumerations Abu Jafar or Yaqub use it. Broken plurals can be pluralized; the plural fits zuhurihim.
  7:172/q10/p3 A reported reading has singular dhurriyyatahu, referring to Adam. — reasons: Al-Kashshaf records it without a named reader.
