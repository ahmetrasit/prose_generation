[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p25/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Though exegetes did not usually treat jurisprudential theory as tafsir material, rulings on commands, prohibitions, and general wording are used; usul al-fiqh contributes in two ways.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p25/r10] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | A Qur’anic passage explaining another is also internal exegesis, including specification of general wording, qualification of the unrestricted, clarification of the ambiguous, and implications of wording.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p25/r11] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · أبو علي الفارسي | reports | Ibn Hisham quotes Abu Ali al-Farisi that the Qur’an is like one sura: an accusation that the recipient of the Reminder is mad is answered elsewhere by the assurance that the Prophet is not mad by his Lord’s grace. | mentions 15:6
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p25/r12] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | rejects | Ibn Ashur says it is too broad to call the entire Qur’an one sura: some verses interpret others, but some stand independently, and a meaning in one passage need not apply to analogous passages.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p25/r13] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Tafsir remains a principal Islamic science in general even when it borrows other sciences for fuller detail; its status as a root and its detailed dependence on them operate in different respects, as Abd al-Hakim explains.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p25/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | First, usul al-fiqh explains Arabic interpretive methods often omitted by grammarians, such as implication and the contrary implication; al-Ghazali also counts it among Qur’anic and legal sciences.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p25/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Second, usul al-fiqh systematizes inference rules and equips the exegete to derive legal meanings from Qur’anic verses.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p25/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · عبد الحكيم | reports | Abd al-Hakim counts kalam as a prerequisite because tafsir presupposes establishing that God speaks.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p25/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · الآلوسي | reports | Al-Alusi says kalam is needed to know what may or may not be ascribed to God, especially in ambiguous attribute verses such as “The Merciful sat upon the Throne”; Ibn Ashur says this is closer than Abd al-Hakim’s rationale and derives from al-Jurjani. | mentions 20:5
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p25/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | rejects | Ibn Ashur rejects both arguments: God’s speech was established before kalam and is not a tafsir prerequisite; knowledge of divine possibility likewise needs kalam only for extended argument, and an advanced need does not make a discipline tafsir material.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p25/r7] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | rejects | Unlike al-Suyuti, Ibn Ashur does not count jurisprudence itself as a tafsir source: understanding the Qur’an does not depend on fiqh, which is later than and derived from tafsir.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p25/r8] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Fiqh helps expand exegesis through detailed inference, legal meanings, etiquette, and other fields; al-Baydawi says only someone learned in all religious sciences and Arabic and literary disciplines should undertake it.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p25/r9] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Prophetic and Companion reports explaining verses are themselves tafsir, not external disciplines from which tafsir derives.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p61/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Denying madness establishes the very thing the polytheists meant to deny: the Prophet’s messengership, since they substituted madness for the claim of being a messenger.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p61/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The response outmatches the polytheists’ assertion in 68:51 with three reinforcements: an oath, the emphatic ب after negation, and a negative nominal sentence whose form signals an established fact. | mentions 68:51
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p61/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · صاحب الكشاف | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī construes بنعمة ربك as a circumstantial phrase attached to the pronoun in مجنون: the madness is absent through God’s favor, with ب indicating accompaniment or cause.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p61/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · ابن الحاجب | reports | Ibn al-Ḥājib attaches the phrase to the verbal meaning implicit in ما, estimating that madness is negated by God’s favor.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p61/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · ابن هشام | reports | Ibn Hishām favors Ibn al-Ḥājib’s analysis in مغني اللبيب, but it conflicts with grammarians’ consensus against attaching an adverbial to a particle, apart from narrow allowances by Abū ʿAlī and Abū al-Fatḥ.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p61/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | prefers | Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers a parenthetical clause with ب attached to an omitted predicate, تقديره أن ذلك بنعمة ربك, matching Arabic usage and the construction in 11:41. | mentions 11:41
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p61/r7] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · الفضل بن عباس اللهبي | reports | Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the poet al-Faḍl ibn ʿAbbās al-Lāhibī’s usage of بنعمة الله as evidence for attaching the phrase to an omitted clause.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p61/r8] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The discussion has a parallel in the earlier phrase فما أنت بنعمة ربك بكاهن ولا مجنون. | mentions 52:29
