[JURJANI-ASRAR:v1p208/r1] JURJANI-ASRAR, d. 471 · author | holds | When likeness rests on sensory qualities and familiar dispositions it is simile; intellectual resemblance may be called tamthīl, as when light represents the Qurʾān or life represents knowledge.
[JURJANI-ASRAR:v1p208/r2] JURJANI-ASRAR, d. 471 · author | holds | Metaphor transfers a word for comparison, hyperbole, or concision; a parable-maker instead establishes a likeness, and any transferred word within it is incidental to that purpose.
[JURJANI-ASRAR:v1p208/r3] JURJANI-ASRAR, d. 471 · author | holds | An explicit simile with its proper comparison word is literal speech, since otherwise every simile would be metaphorical, which is untenable.
[JURJANI-ASRAR:v1p208/r4] JURJANI-ASRAR, d. 471 · author | holds | A borrowed generic noun such as ‘lion’ can denote either the animal or a brave person; contextual evidence and surrounding words decide which sense is intended.
[JURJANI-ASRAR:v1p208/r5] JURJANI-ASRAR, d. 471 · author | holds | Verbs and adjectives can share the same literal-or-figurative ambiguity when predicated of an indefinite noun capable of either sense.
[JURJANI-ASRAR:v1p219/r1] JURJANI-ASRAR, d. 471 · author | rejects | The line ‘you are like the night that overtakes me’ cannot become hyperbolic identity with night unless a quality is supplied; darkness is an ill-suited trait to address directly to a praised person.

[JURJANI-ASRAR:v1p219/r3] JURJANI-ASRAR, d. 471 · author | holds | The Mutanabbī surrounds the word ‘ugly’ with favorable descriptions, like an ill-omened star pressed between benefic stars, so its negative effect is canceled.

[JURJANI-ASRAR:v1p316/r1] JURJANI-ASRAR, d. 471 · author | holds | A sentence conveys information through affirmation or negation; a single noun or verb cannot supply that predicative relation by itself.
[JURJANI-ASRAR:v1p316/r2] JURJANI-ASRAR, d. 471 · author | holds | Affirmation and negation each require two terms: what is predicated and what it is predicated of, corresponding to subject and predicate or verb and agent; these are musnad and musnad ilayh.
