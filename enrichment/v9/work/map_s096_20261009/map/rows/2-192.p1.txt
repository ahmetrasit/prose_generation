 d. 1393 · author | holds | If the opponents stop fighting, Muslims should stop killing them; believers should make forgiveness their practice.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p206#2/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | God's forgiveness and mercy do not logically follow from the enemy's cessation, so the clause elliptically implies “then forgive them”; this omission urges and supports the believers' forgiveness.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p206#2/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Intahaw originally means to respond to a prohibition and then broadened to desist from an act or intention, since forbidding asks someone to leave an act whether begun or not.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p206#2/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · النابغة | reports | Al-Nabigha's line illustrates nahy used for forbidding the Banu Dhibyan from undertaking certain acts.
[TABATABAI:2:190-195#8/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | Al-Durr al-Manthūr transmits the preceding interpretation through several routes from Ibn ʿAbbās and others.
[TABATABAI:2:190-195#8/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The apparent unity of the passage indicates that these verses were revealed together.
[TABATABAI:2:191-193/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Here desisting means stopping fighting at the Sacred Mosque, not ending all fighting by accepting Islam; the latter is the sense of the second desistance clause in 2:193. | mentions 2:193
[TABATABAI:2:191-193/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Each “if they desist” clause limits its nearest preceding statement, so the two clauses have different referents rather than repeating one another.
[TABATABAI:2:191-193/r9] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The statement that God is forgiving and merciful puts the cause in place of the effect, giving the reason for the ruling.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:191/r40] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | If the attackers desist, Muslims should cease fighting and not use violence against those who have stopped; God’s forgiveness and mercy are declared.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:191/r41] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | One report says that after the Hijra the Prophet first set out with many Muslims to visit Mecca, was blocked by the Quraysh at Hudaybiya, sought a peaceful settlement, and received permission to fight in these verses after a polytheist unit attempted an attack. | mentions 2:194
[STUDYQURAN:2:190/r16] STUDYQURAN · some commentators | reports | Some read “if they desist” to mean the idolaters stop idolatry and become Muslims.
[STUDYQURAN:2:190/r17] STUDYQURAN · others | reports | Others say they need only desist from fighting and hostility.
[STUDYQURAN:2:190/r18] STUDYQURAN · author | rejects | The conversion-as-final-goal interpretation is problematic in the verse's plain sense and in light of 9:6, which requires safe conduct for an idolater seeking asylum. | mentions 9:6
[STUDYQURAN:2:190/r19] STUDYQURAN · author | rejects | The author notes that this reading leaves little explained: hostilities begin for political reasons such as fighting and expulsion, yet supposedly end only through spiritual repentance and conversion.
[STUDYQURAN:2:190/r5] STUDYQURAN · some commentators | reports | Some say the passage came after Ḥudaybiyah: Muslims had turned back from the ʿumrah in 628 under treaty terms, and as the next year's pilgrimage approached they feared attack and fighting in the sacred precinct during sacred months, so the verses set engagement guidelines.
