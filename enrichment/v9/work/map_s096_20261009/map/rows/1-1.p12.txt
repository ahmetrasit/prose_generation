KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · قراء مكة والكوفة وفقهاؤهما | reports | They hold that the basmala is a verse of al-Fatiha and every surah; al-Shafi'i and his companions agree and recite it aloud.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p45#2/r4] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | reports | The opposing jurists argue that the early community included the basmala in the codex while excluding Amin, so it must be Qur'an.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p45#2/r5] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says that whoever omits the basmala has omitted 114 verses from God's Book.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p45#2/r6] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | The preposition bi is attached to an omitted verb such as I read or recite in God's name; the reciter supplies the action begun by the basmala.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p46#2/r1] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | In the first-revealed surah, placing the command to read before the basmala is more fitting because the reading command is paramount. | mentions 96:1
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p46#2/r2] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | reports | One analysis makes the name relate to reciting as a pen relates to writing: the believer makes the act God's by beginning with His name, as a prophetic report says.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p46#2/r3] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report from the Prophet says that an important matter begun without God's name is cut off.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p46#2/r4] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | prefers | The other analysis treats reciting as occurring in the blessing of God's name; al-Zamakhshari calls this account more idiomatic and better. | mentions 23:20
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p46/r1] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | The omitted governing verb is placed after the basmala because God's name, the more important element, should come first and signal exclusive invocation of God.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p46/r2] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | The preposed divine name conveys exclusivity, as in the explicit object-first wording of You alone we worship. | mentions 1:5
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p46/r3] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | The basmala's omitted verb is illustrated by Arabic prayers for a newly married couple and by a poet's account of being called to a meal.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p46/r4] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | The construction is supported by Noah's words about embarking and landing in God's name. | mentions 11:41
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p48/r1] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | The divine basmala and praise on worshippers' tongues teach them how to invoke, praise, glorify, and magnify God.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p48/r2] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | One-letter particles usually take fatḥa; lām takes kasra to distinguish it from the introductory lām, while bi takes kasra because it is inherently a preposition.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p48/r3] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | The noun ism begins with a consonant cluster, so speakers add a connecting hamza at utterance onset; some instead pronounce sim or sum by moving the initial consonant.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p48/r4] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | Ism is a noun with a deleted ending, originally sumw, derived from elevation because naming raises and publicizes the named one.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p48/r5] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | The word nabz for a nickname is related to nabr, raising the voice; nabz also names the outer peel of a palm tree.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p48/r6] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | The alif in bism is omitted in writing because connected use is frequent; the lengthened bā compensates for it, and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz told his scribe to lengthen the bā. | mentions 96:1
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p48/r7] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | Allah derives from al-ilah; the generic word for a deity became specific to the true God, just as al-najm came to denote the Pleiades.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p49/r1] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | Allah, with its hamza deleted, is uniquely the name of the true God and is not used for another being; verbs meaning to deify derive from it.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p49/r2] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | Allah is a noun, not an adjective: it can be qualified but does not itself qualify an unspecified thing, and divine attributes require a named subject.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p49/r3] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | He also links Allah to aliha, to be bewildered: minds are dazed by trying to comprehend God, and lack of sound reflection lets error spread.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p49/r4] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj says pronouncing Allah's lām emphatically is a sunnah; universal Arab practice shows it was inherited generation after generation.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p49/r5] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | Rahman is the emphatic fa'lan form of mercy, while Rahim is fa'il; the former is more intensive, and added form signals added meaning.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p49/r6] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | Rahman functions as an exclusive, established epithet for God, like Allah, and is not ordinarily used of anyone else.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p50/r1] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | rejects | Calling Musaylima Rahman of Yamama, including in his poet's praise, is presented as a facet of the Banu Hanifa's unbelieving perversity.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p50/r2] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | Rahman should be indeclinable by analogy with fa'lan adjectives such as عطشان and سكران, even though its divine specialization prevents a feminine form.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p50/r3] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | God's mercy is figurative language for His beneficence: a kindly ruler does good to subjects, whereas harshness withholds favor.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p50/r4] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | He raises the question why the more emphatic attribute precedes the lesser when normal usage would progress from the lesser to the greater.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p51/r1] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | The ordering is complementary: Rahman covers great and foundational blessings, and Rahim completes it by covering blessings that are delicate and subtle.
