## THE CURRENT MAP
20:69/q01 What does the command to cast mean? [وَأَلْقِ · meaning]
  20:69/q01/p1 Throw or cast the object away. — reasons: Jishumi equates ilqāʾ with ṭarḥ, nabdh, and ramy.
20:69/q02 Who is addressed by the command? [وَأَلْقِ · referent] (turns on: The implicit addressee may be Moses or the staff.)
  20:69/q02/p1 Moses is addressed. — reasons: Qurtubi gives Moses as one possible addressee; Ibn Kathir says God inspired him to cast the staff.
  20:69/q02/p2 The staff may be addressed. — reasons: Qurtubi gives this as another grammatical possibility.
20:69/q03 What is in Moses' right hand? [مَا فِى يَمِينِكَ · referent]
  20:69/q03/p1 His staff. — reasons: Muqatil says it became a serpent when cast; Biqai and Alusi relate it to God's earlier question at 20:17.
20:69/q04 Why call the staff 'what is in your right hand'? [مَا فِى يَمِينِكَ · rhetoric] (turns on: The explanations turn on small appearance, extraordinary power, right-hand connotations, and prior mention.)
  20:69/q04/p1 The indirect wording magnifies its extraordinary power. — reasons: Razi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi and Alusi contrast it with the many implements. Alusi says its earlier miraculous state supports this; Tabatabai stresses God's power to make it staff or serpent.
  20:69/q04/p2 It belittles the solitary small staff, reassuring Moses. — reasons: Razi, Qurtubi, Baydawi and Abu Hayyan contrast a small rod with many ropes and staffs. Nasafi says it may belittle their implements. Alusi objects that this ignores the staff's prior extraordinary state.
  20:69/q04/p3 It recalls God's earlier question and encourages Moses. — reasons: Biqai and Alusi cite 20:17; Alusi says this wording suits removal of fear, while 7:117 gives the command by meaning.
  20:69/q04/p4 The right hand evokes blessing and good fortune. — reasons: Abu Hayyan proposes this; Alusi objects that the address to Moses was not in Arabic.
  20:69/q04/p5 It shows created things lack fixed intrinsic reality. — reasons: Tabatabai says God can make the same object a staff or living serpent.
20:69/q05 Where did Moses' staff come from? [مَا فِى يَمِينِكَ · reports] (turns on: Mawardi transmits differing origin reports.)
  20:69/q05/p1 An ʿawsaj thorn tree. — reasons: Mawardi reports Wahb.
  20:69/q05/p2 Paradise. — reasons: Mawardi reports Ibn Abbas, who says Moses killed ʿUj ibn ʿAnaq with it.
20:69/q06 What does تَلْقَفْ mean? [تَلْقَفْ · meaning]
  20:69/q06/p1 To seize swiftly or swallow, here by mouth. — reasons: Hasan specifies its mouth. Jishumi compares laqifa, talaqqafa and iltaqafa; Kisa'i says taking quickly. Qurtubi cites laqif thaqif for a deft person and also wall or basin collapse; Bursevi cites taking by hand or mouth. Razi stresses speed.
  20:69/q06/p2 The intensive form conveys eager or forceful devouring. — reasons: Qiraat-ER says Form V makes the staff eagerly snatch by itself; Biqai says omitted tāʾ signals speed; the doubled tāʾ evokes the effort of swallowing.
20:69/q07 How is تَلْقَفْ read and construed? [تَلْقَفْ · readings] (turns on: The ending marks command consequence versus circumstance or assertion, while the consonants reflect different forms and routes.)
  20:69/q07/p1 Indicative with raised fāʾ and doubled qāf, as circumstance or new clause. — reasons: Ibn Amir, especially Ibn Dhakwan, is named. Zajjaj allows the syntax but says it was not established for his recitation. Al-Farisi allows Moses or staff as subject, using 8:17 and 5:95; Tabrisi cites 99:4. Qiraat-ER emphasizes independent assertion.
  20:69/q07/p2 Jussive with doubled qāf, answering the command. — reasons: Zajjaj and others supply an implied condition: if cast, it swallows. Al-Farisi says it can describe the staff or address Moses. Alusi derives the form from tatalaqqaf after one tāʾ is dropped.
  20:69/q07/p3 Jussive with light qāf and still lām, from laqifa. — reasons: Hafs is widely named; Qurtubi also names Sulami, Abu Hayyan adds Abu Jafar and Isma, and Ibn Mujahid and Farisi report Qunbul or al-Nabbal. Nasafi also names Ibn Dhakwan.
  20:69/q07/p4 Doubled initial tāʾ from tatalaqqaf. — reasons: Al-Bazzi and Ibn Fulayh transmit it from Ibn Kathir, in connected recitation. Ibn Atiyya notes no initial alif is then needed. Al-Farisi objects that hamzat al-wasl cannot enter imperfect verbs; Ahmad ibn Musa starts lightly. Tabrisi gives the same difficulty; NASHR lists 31 such verbs.
  20:69/q07/p5 Talqamu, with mīm, is transmitted. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzi names Ibn Masud, Ubayy, Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Abu Raja, referring to 7:117; Qurtubi lists synonymous talaqqama.
20:69/q08 Who performs the swallowing in تَلْقَفْ? [تَلْقَفْ مَا فِى يَمِينِكَ · grammar] (turns on: The feminine prefix may follow the staff by sense or indicate Moses as addressee.)
  20:69/q08/p1 The staff, with feminine agreement by sense. — reasons: Al-Farisi cites 33:31 and 6:160; Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, Samin, Bursevi and Alusi say mā means feminine ʿaṣā. Abu Hayyan notes formal agreement would use yāʾ; Samin says that form was not read.
  20:69/q08/p2 Moses, the thrower, may be the subject. — reasons: Al-Farisi compares attribution through cause with 8:17; Abu al-Baqa permits Moses as subject of the indicative, but Samin finds this far-fetched. Tabrisi allows the human addressee.
20:69/q09 What did the staff swallow? [تَلْقَفْ مَا صَنَعُوٓا۟ · referent]
  20:69/q09/p1 The ropes and staffs that appeared to move like snakes. — reasons: Muqatil, Yahya, Qatada and later sources identify the props. Tabrisi distinguishes the physical objects from the illusion the magicians made. Qushayri says they only appeared serpentine; Bursevi and Alusi say the motion frightened Moses.
  20:69/q09/p2 Their deceptive display or magical fabrication. — reasons: Baydawi, Nasafi, Tabatabai and Islahi describe the trick swallowed, stressing illusion's nullification.
20:69/q10 What happened when Moses cast the staff? [وَأَلْقِ تَلْقَفْ · reports]
  20:69/q10/p1 It became a serpent, swallowed the props, and returned to a staff. — reasons: Sa'id says it was huge and Pharaoh threatened crosswise amputation and crucifixion; Suddi says magicians professed faith; Wahb says Moses feared the apparent snakes. Tabrisi says it circled the ranks. Ibn Kathir describes eyes, limbs, neck, head and fangs in daylight; Razi says its growth and return exceeded craft.
  20:69/q10/p2 The swallowed ropes and staffs equaled three hundred camel loads. — reasons: Nahhas says only God knows where they vanished; Mawardi and Ibn Atiyya give the same load.
  20:69/q10/p3 The growing serpent crossed a river or sea with its tail. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya's unnamed account says Pharaoh watched from an eighty-cubit pavilion, was frightened, and the magicians believed.
  20:69/q10/p4 The magicians saw Paradise, Hell, and their rewards before prostrating. — reasons: Tha'labi also recounts Pharaoh's believing wife's vision of Paradise and death before a stone struck her body.
20:69/q11 How many magicians or implements are reported? [مَا صَنَعُوٓا۟ · reports] (turns on: The accounts vary in whether they give the number of magicians.)
  20:69/q11/p1 Seventy thousand magicians and three hundred camel loads of props. — reasons: Tha'labi's Qasim report gives the magician count; Ibn Atiyya adds the load, apparent motion and eighty-cubit pavilion.
  20:69/q11/p2 Three hundred camel loads of props, with no magician count. — reasons: Nahhas and Mawardi give this quantity; Nahhas says only God knows the props' destination.
20:69/q12 What does صَنَعُوٓا۟ mean? [صَنَعُوٓا۟ · meaning]
  20:69/q12/p1 They fabricated or contrived the display. — reasons: Razi notes Arabic can call a lie manufactured speech; Baydawi likewise says fabricated. Biqai stresses long practice; Bursevi defines ṣunʿ as skillful action, distinct from mere action and normally not attributed to animals or inert things.
20:69/q13 Why call the display 'what they made'? [مَا صَنَعُوٓا۟ · rhetoric]
  20:69/q13/p1 It exposes the work as human fabrication and belittles its illusion. — reasons: Bursevi and Alusi say the moving props deceived; Tabatabai contrasts unrestricted divine power with fabrication; Tabrisi says the actual ropes and staffs were not made by the magicians.
  20:69/q13/p2 It stresses their extensive training. — reasons: Biqai calls the display a product of long practice.
20:69/q14 How does مَا function in إِنَّمَا صَنَعُوا۟? [إِنَّمَا صَنَعُوا۟ · grammar] (turns on: The status of mā determines whether kayd is predicate or object.)
  20:69/q14/p1 Relative pronoun: 'what they made' is a magician's plot. — reasons: Zajjaj, Nahhas, Ibn Atiyya, Samin, Ibn Ashur and others make mā the subject of inna and kayd its predicate; Tabrisi and Samin posit an omitted resumptive. Ibn Ashur says this simply reports their act, without restriction, despite written joining.
  20:69/q14/p2 Extra or restrictive particle: kayd is the object of ṣanaʿū. — reasons: Zajjaj compares innamā ḍarabtu Zaydan; Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, Samin, Baydawi and Alusi cite the associated accusative. Tabari rejects its uninflected siḥr form because the authoritative readers agree against it.
  20:69/q14/p3 Infinitival particle: 'their making' is a plot. — reasons: Nahhas and Nasafi allow the nominalized action; Samin says this needs no omitted resumptive; Alusi lists it among three construals.
  20:69/q14/p4 Indefinite descriptive mā: 'something they made'. — reasons: Bursevi and Alusi permit this construal.
20:69/q15 How is the hamza of إِنَّمَا read? [إِنَّمَا · readings] (turns on: The opened hamza makes the clause causal.)
  20:69/q15/p1 Opened hamza yields a causal clause: because their work is a trick. — reasons: Abu Ishaq allows it and Qurtubi reports it; Zajjaj says he does not know it as a reading here.
  20:69/q15/p2 Kasra introduces the explanation of their work. — reasons: Zajjaj says the known reading is stronger in sense.
