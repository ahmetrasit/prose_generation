[RAZI:1:1/r12] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Understanding the implied beginning requires surveying all obedience: true creeds and sincere deeds, their proofs, and answers to objections, a body of inquiry that may exceed ten thousand questions.
[RAZI:1:1/r13] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | The refuge formula negates improper beliefs and acts, while the basmala affirms what ought to be believed and done; sound reason and clear truth support this order.

[RAZI:1:1/r3] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Seeking refuge in God means seeking protection from every prohibited act, including sins and wrong beliefs.

[RAZI:1:1/r5] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Each deviant Muslim sect errs across many questions concerning God’s essence, attributes, rulings, acts and names, as well as compulsion, decree, justice, reward, resurrection, promises, threats, legal status and imamate.
[RAZI:1:1/r6] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Razi says the roughly seven hundred deviant sects outside the Muslim community add further errors in rational theology and questions of essences and attributes, all covered by seeking refuge.
[RAZI:1:1/r7] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Prohibited actions include whatever the Qur’an, mass-transmitted or solitary reports, communal consensus, or sound analogy forbids; their number exceeds a thousand and the refuge formula encompasses them all.
[RAZI:1:1/r8] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | One can seek refuge from something only after knowing it and recognizing its falsehood and ugliness; thus this formula contains thousands of substantial, certain questions.
