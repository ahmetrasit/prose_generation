[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p532/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Gabriel effectively apologizes that angels descend and bring revelation only at God’s command, so delay is not their fault and they cannot act independently.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p532/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The angels’ total subordination rebuts Arab polytheists who worshipped them as God’s daughters and expected their intercession; if even Gabriel is powerless, other angels have still less power.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p532/r5] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The assurance that the Lord forgets nothing comforts the Prophet that no angel can exploit a lapse in awareness to carry out a plan independently.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:19:64/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Abdullah b. Abbas | reports | Ibn Abbas reports that the Prophet asked Gabriel to visit him more often, and these verses were revealed in response.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:19:64/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | Another report says Quraysh questioned the Prophet, he promised a near answer, and when Gabriel was delayed they claimed his Lord had forgotten him; the Prophet grieved and these verses followed.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:19:64/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Müfessirlerin genel kabulü | reports | The prevailing exegetical view is that these are God's words, commanding Gabriel to answer the Prophet accordingly.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:19:64/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The free rendering says angels descend only at their Lord's command; all places and times belong to God, and delayed revelation reflects wisdom rather than forgetfulness.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p37/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | Maryam's wording suits the context of God's ownership, provision, and inheritance; Ayat al-Kursi instead concerns knowledge, so “He knows what is before and behind them” prepares for the limit on their knowledge. | mentions 2:255
[STUDYQURAN:19:64/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The verse reportedly followed a revelation hiatus of twelve, fifteen, or forty nights, when the Prophet was asked about the Cave's people, Dhu’l-Qarnayn, and the Spirit; the delay troubled him and idolaters said God had abandoned him. | mentions 18:9, 18:83, 17:85, 93:1, 93:2, 93:3
[STUDYQURAN:19:64/r2] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | After Gabriel returned, the Prophet reportedly asked why he did not visit more often; the verse gives Gabriel's response, and its opening We refers to the archangel.
[STUDYQURAN:19:64/r3] STUDYQURAN · minority interpretation | reports | A minority reads the speaker as the people of Paradise, who settle there only by God's command.
[STUDYQURAN:19:64/r4] STUDYQURAN · some commentators | reports | Before and behind may refer to temporal periods—world and Hereafter, before creation and after annihilation, or past and future—or spatially to earth and Heaven; what lies between may be the two trumpet blasts or earthly life, showing God's command of all time and space. | mentions 39:68
[STUDYQURAN:19:64/r5] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Not forgetful reassures the Prophet that God has not forgotten him; revelation's intervals are providential and ultimately beneficial. | mentions 93:3
[STUDYQURAN:19:intro/r13] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The surah moves from revelation by God’s command to criticism of the Prophet and Quran, rejects claims God has a child, and ends with the silence of destroyed generations. | mentions 19:64, 19:65, 19:88, 19:95, 19:98
