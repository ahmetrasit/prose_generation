## THE CURRENT MAP
2:40/q01 Who are the Children of Israel? [يَٰبَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · referent]
  2:40/q01/p1 Jacob's descendants, named for Jacob son of Isaac son of Abraham. — reasons: Tabari compares Children of Adam; the Prophet elicited Jewish confirmation that Israel is Jacob; Abu Hayyan and Biqai stress ancestral honor.
2:40/q02 What does the name Israel mean? [إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · meaning] (turns on: The reported Hebrew and narrative derivations differ.)
  2:40/q02/p1 Servant of God, equivalent to Abd Allah. — reasons: Ibn Abbas parses Isra as servant and El as God; Abdullah ibn al-Harith confirms El; angelic names are compared.
  2:40/q02/p2 God's chosen one. — reasons: Tha'labi, Kashshaf and others report the Hebrew derivation; Abu Hayyan attributes it to Ibn Abbas and others.
  2:40/q02/p3 One whom God strengthened or firmly formed. — reasons: Al-Mahdawi derives Isra from strength; Kur'an Yolu finds this probable; Abu Hayyan calls popular etymologies weak.
  2:40/q02/p4 Man of God. — reasons: Al-Qaffal says Hebrew Isra means human.
  2:40/q02/p5 Night migrant toward God. — reasons: Al-Suhayli and other accounts connect the name to Jacob's night migration; Abu Hayyan questions popular etymologies.
  2:40/q02/p6 Other proposed meanings include God's sword and one striving in God's path. — reasons: Kur'an Yolu reports these.
2:40/q03 Who is addressed by this call? [يَٰبَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · referent] (turns on: Whether ancestral favors count for unbelieving descendants.)
  2:40/q03/p1 Jews of Muhammad's time, especially scholars in and around Medina. — reasons: Ibn Abbas and the majority identify Jewish scholars; Bursevi names Qurayza and Nadir; Ibn Atiyya includes believers and unbelievers.
  2:40/q03/p2 Jews and Christians, the People of Scripture. — reasons: Jishumi reports Abu Ali; Tabrisi and Abu Hayyan link the address to the general call and Adam story.
  2:40/q03/p3 Only Israelites who believed in Muhammad. — reasons: Makki argues unbelievers cannot receive favor; Ibn Atiyya notes the direct address then calls for continued commitment.
  2:40/q03/p4 All descendants or accountable people generally. — reasons: Jishumi reports a general reading; Abu Hayyan extends the duties to everyone.
2:40/q04 Why call them by Israel's name? [يَٰبَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · rhetoric]
  2:40/q04/p1 It honors a righteous ancestor and urges obedience to his faith. — reasons: Abu Hayyan cites attachment to ancestors (43:22; 2:170); Burhan compares sons of Abdullah; Alusi says conduct matters more in a prophetic family.
  2:40/q04/p2 The vocative draws attention to the ensuing commands. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya calls ya attention getting; Abu Hayyan compares 2:21 and 2:35.
2:40/q05 How is Banī Isrāʾīl formed grammatically? [بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · grammar] (turns on: The disputed lost radical and range of collective descent.)
  2:40/q05/p1 Banī is an accusative construct vocative; Israel is genitive in position but diptote. — reasons: Zajjaj supplies implicit 'I call'; Nahhas notes deleted nūn and accusative yā; Ibn Atiyya cites al-Khalil and Abu Ali; the plural can take feminine agreement.
  2:40/q05/p2 Ibn derives from yā connected to building. — reasons: Zajjaj, Abu Ishaq and Jawhari prefer this; the father is the foundation and the son a branch. Fatūwa answers the banūwa objection.
  2:40/q05/p3 Ibn derives from wāw. — reasons: Al-Akhfash cites banūwa and more frequent deletion of wāw; Nahhas, Samin and Alusi dispute those grounds.
  2:40/q05/p4 The plural title includes women as well as men. — reasons: Alusi says ibn literally denotes male sons but the construct commonly covers all descendants; al-Salikuti calls this figurative. Alusi notes prohibited metaphorical divine sonship.
2:40/q06 How is Israel pronounced or read? [إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · readings] (turns on: Transmitted pronunciations of a foreign proper name differ.)
  2:40/q06/p1 The common form has long alif, hamza and yāʾ. — reasons: Jishumi, Tabrisi and Samin call it eloquent; Abu Hayyan and Alusi attribute it to the majority; foreign words admit Arabic alteration.
  2:40/q06/p2 Forms omit or ease hamza with varying length. — reasons: Reports name Hasan, Zuhri, Abu Ja'far, Nafi', A'mash, Isa, Warsh and Ibn Abi Ishaq. Saffar questions deletion of initial hamza.
  2:40/q06/p3 Forms omit yā or replace lām with nūn. — reasons: Tamimi Isra'in has poetic and phonological parallels; Umayya attests Isral; imāla is also transmitted.
2:40/q07 What accounts explain Jacob's name and history? [إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · reports] (turns on: The narratives and counts differ.)
  2:40/q07/p1 Jacob wrestled an angel and was named Israel. — reasons: Abu Majliz says Jacob seized the angel and demanded a name; Durr adds a blow to his thighs and calls it an angelic name.
  2:40/q07/p2 Jacob captured a jinn called Il that extinguished Temple lamps. — reasons: Ka'b describes Jacob's Temple service; Samin mentions a mixed-language name.
  2:40/q07/p3 Jacob and Esau's rivalry, Isaac's blessings and Jacob's flight are recounted. — reasons: Tha'labi reports the twins' birth, Rebecca's disguise, blessings and Esau's threat; Abu Hayyan mentions night migration to their uncle.
  2:40/q07/p4 Reports differ over prophets with two names. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzi singles out Jacob besides Muhammad; al-Khalil lists five pairs; an Ibn Abbas report singles out Israel and Jesus; Jawhari calls Messiah underived.
2:40/q08 What does 'remember' ask them to do? [ٱذْكُرُوا۟ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether recollection alone or gratitude is intended.)
  2:40/q08/p1 Preserve the favor in heart and memory, without forgetting. — reasons: Mawardi and Ibn Atiyya oppose recollection to forgetting; Qurtubi regards inward retention as a good explanation.
  2:40/q08/p2 Give thanks, praise and obey the Giver. — reasons: Baghawi transmits Hasan; Kashshaf requires recognition, magnification and obedience; Ibn Jawzi says memory without gratitude is incomplete; Wahidi identifies belief in Muhammad as required gratitude.
  2:40/q08/p3 Bring something to attention by heart or tongue. — reasons: Jishumi and Tabrisi list honor, scripture, prayer and speech among dhikr's uses; Harali defines recalling what was forgotten.
2:40/q09 How do the forms and senses of dhikr differ? [ٱذْكُرُوا۟ · meaning]
  2:40/q09/p1 Heart remembrance and spoken mention differ by vowel or are synonymous. — reasons: Al-Kisai assigns damma to heart and kasra to speech; others treat both alike; Alusi weighs homonymy against a shared general sense.
  2:40/q09/p2 The imperative derives from the remembrance verb. — reasons: Nahhas explains deletion of nūn and connecting alif and initial damma; Farra equates a form with tadhakkaru; Baydawi reports udhkuru and idhtakiru.
2:40/q10 What does niʿmah mean? [نِعْمَتِىَ · meaning] (turns on: Whether a benefit's ultimate outcome affects its name.)
  2:40/q10/p1 A bestowed benefit intended kindly. — reasons: Damghani and Ibn Jawzi gloss favor; Jishumi requires good intent; Razi excludes pure harm, self-serving help and poisoned gifts; Harali includes bodily and spiritual suitability.
  2:40/q10/p2 All benefits ultimately come from God. — reasons: Razi cites 16:53 and 31:14: God creates intermediaries, capacity, motive and guidance; people may still be thanked, as a hadith says.
  2:40/q10/p3 Worldly benefits bless even disbelievers. — reasons: Razi prefers Baqillani, citing creation, provision, rescue, security and prosperity; the dispute is partly terminological.
  2:40/q10/p4 Worldly benefits that end in eternal harm are not blessings to disbelievers. — reasons: Some Sunnis compare them to poisoned sweets and cite 3:178.
  2:40/q10/p5 All accountable people receive equal religious aid; some also require the best worldly aid. — reasons: Razi attributes the latter to Baghdadi Mu'tazilites, not Basrans; Sunnis say God creates both benefits and harms.
2:40/q11 Which favor or favors are recalled? [نِعْمَتِىَ ٱلَّتِىٓ أَنْعَمْتُ عَلَيْكُمْ · referent] (turns on: Whether examples restrict the unspecified favor.)
  2:40/q11/p1 Israel's many historic favors, including favors to ancestors. — reasons: Sources enumerate rescue from Pharaoh, the parted sea, land, prophets, books, manna, quails, shade, springs, pardon and Torah. Ibn Abbas adds nighttime light, unworn clothes and untangled hair; 5:20 is a parallel.
  2:40/q11/p2 The Torah's knowledge of Muhammad and life in his era. — reasons: Ibn al-Anbari says failing to follow him is like forgetting entrusted knowledge; Kashshaf and Nasafi count reaching his time as favor; Elmali emphasizes guidance.
  2:40/q11/p3 All favors, beginning with creation, faculties and Islam. — reasons: Ibn Zayd calls Islam greatest (49:17); Jishumi and Tabrisi include reason, senses, provision and protection; Ibn Atiyya prefers the generic word over restricted examples.
2:40/q12 Why can singular niʿmah mean many favors? [نِعْمَتِىَ · grammar]
  2:40/q12/p1 It is a generic or collective singular. — reasons: Tha'labi, Jishumi, Baghawi, Tabrisi and others cite 14:34 or 16:18; Ibn Jawzi compares Arabic singulars used collectively.
2:40/q13 How do favors to ancestors count for descendants? [أَنْعَمْتُ عَلَيْكُمْ · rhetoric] (turns on: Ancestral benefit and the motive for direct address.)
  2:40/q13/p1 Descendants inherit ancestral benefit and honor. — reasons: Zajjaj cites Arabic attribution of forebears' deeds to descendants; Razi adds existence itself; Alusi invokes shared religious inheritance.
  2:40/q13/p2 Naming their own favor encourages gratitude over envy. — reasons: Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi note that people readily value their own gifts; attribution to God directs gratitude to Him.
  2:40/q13/p3 Recalling past gifts raises hope for future favor. — reasons: Razi says a repeated benefactor is expected to continue kindness, restraining opposition.
