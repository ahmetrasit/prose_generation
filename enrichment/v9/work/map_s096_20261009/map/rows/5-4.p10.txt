[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#4/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas, Abu Hurayra, al-Nakhaʿi, Qatada, Ibn Jubayr, Ata, Ikrima, al-Shafii, Ahmad, Ishaq, Abu Thawr, al-Nuʿman, and his companions say prey is lawful only if the predator did not eat any of it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#4/r9] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · النعمان | reports | This first view says an animal that eats has held the prey for itself rather than its owner; Abu Hanifa applies the no-eating condition to dogs and cheetahs, but not birds.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#5/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | He cites Muslim’s report forbidding the prey if the dog has eaten from it, setting up the contrary report below.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#5/r10] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الجمهور من العلماء | reports | Most scholars hold that prey remains edible when the hunting animal drank its blood.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#5/r11] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · عطاء | reports | ʿAta says that drinking blood does not count as eating from the prey.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#5/r12] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الشعبي وسفيان الثوري | reports | Al-Shaʿbi and Sufyan al-Thawri disliked eating prey from which the animal drank blood.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#5/r13] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | There is no disagreement that the injury causing permissibility must be established with certainty; doubt bars eating.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#5/r14] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | If another dog is found with the hunter’s dog, it is presumed to have joined on its own rather than being released by another hunter; scholars do not differ on this presumption.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#5/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو ثعلبة الخشني | reports | The Prophet permits game caught by a dog released in God’s name, even if the dog ate some, and what the hunter’s hand brings back; the report is in Abu Dawud.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#5/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | A version attributed to ʿAdi is unsound; the sound report from him is the Muslim narration.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#5/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | prefers | He prefers reconciling the reports: treat the prohibition as scrupulous caution and the permission as legal allowance; the Prophet advised ʿAdi, who had means, to abstain and needy Abu Thaʿlaba that he could eat.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#5/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو عمر | reports | Abu ʿUmar holds that Abu Thaʿlaba’s report abrogates ʿAdi’s, since the Prophet answered that the hunter could eat even when the dog ate.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#5/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | rejects | He questions abrogation because the chronology is unknown and prefers reconciling both reports unless their dates are known.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#5/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أصحاب الشافعي | reports | They allow eating when the dog ate from extreme hunger; otherwise they disallow it as evidence of deficient training.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#5/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · النخعي والثوري وأصحاب الرأي وحماد بن أبي سليمان وابن عباس | reports | Some early authorities distinguish dogs and cheetahs, whose eating from prey bars it, from falcons, whose eating does not bar it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#5/r9] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · النخعي والثوري وأصحاب الرأي وحماد بن أبي سليمان وابن عباس | reports | Their rationale is that dogs and cheetahs can be struck and rebuked, whereas birds cannot; training a bird is limited to answering a call and coming when urged, since beating harms it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#6/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Do not eat prey mixed with dogs other than the one named over, because the hunter named only his own dog.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#6/r10] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | A report permits eating an animal found after being absent for a day with only the arrow mark.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#6/r11] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو ثعلبة | reports | Abu Thaʿlaba’s Abu Dawud version adds that the prey may be eaten after three days unless it has begun to stink.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#6/r12] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | That report conflicts with the instruction to eat what was killed in view and leave what died unseen.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#6/r13] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Ismaʿ is killing quickly while the hunter sees it; inmaʿ is when the shot animal disappears and dies unseen, with the verb describing a shot that vanishes and then dies.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#6/r14] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Imruʾ al-Qays’s line illustrates the verb for a shot that disappears from view.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#6/r15] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · علماؤنا | reports | One reported position allows unseen prey whether it was killed by arrow or dog.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#6/r16] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | A second position bars any prey that disappeared from view.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#6/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | If another hunter released a dog that jointly caught the prey, the two hunters share it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#6/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | If one dog inflicted the mortal wound before the other arrived, the prey belongs to that dog’s hunter.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#6/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Do not eat a shot animal found drowned, since it is uncertain whether the water or the arrow killed it; the cited report allows eating after a day only when the arrow’s mark alone is found.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#6/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | If prey dies in the dogs’ mouths without being wounded, it is not edible because it was strangled, like an animal killed by a blunt knife before its throat was cut.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#6/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | If the hunter could seize and slaughter the prey but lets it die, it is not edible; once accessible, he was negligent in performing the required slaughter.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#6/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | If he catches it and it dies while he reaches for a knife already with him, it is edible; if he must leave to fetch a knife, it is not.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#6/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الشافعي وابن القاسم | reports | One Shafiʿi position requires a wound before prey caught by a hunting animal is edible, citing the verse’s phrase “trained hunters.”
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#6/r9] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أشهب | reports | Ashhab’s other Shafiʿi-cited position allows prey that died from the dog’s impact, even without a wound.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#7/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | The second position bars unseen prey for fear another creature may have helped kill it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#7/r10] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الحسن البصري | reports | Al-Hasan al-Basri disliked hunting with a trained dog owned by a Jew or Christian.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#7/r11] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · جابر بن عبد الله والحسن وعطاء ومجاهد والنخعي والثوري وإسحاق | reports | These authorities disliked hunting with a Magian’s dog, hawk, or falcon.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#7/r12] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · مالك والشافعي وأبو حنيفة | reports | They allow a Muslim to hunt with a Magian’s dog, treating it like the Magian’s knife.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#7/r13] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · جمهور الأمة | reports | Most scholars allow hunting by a Jew or Christian, while Malik distinguishes their hunting from their slaughter and rejects it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#7/r14] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · مالك | reports | Malik cites the verse about game within reach of hands and spears because it does not name Jews or Christians among permitted hunters. | mentions 5:94
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#7/r15] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن وهب وأشهب | reports | Ibn Wahb and Ashhab hold that a Jew’s or Christian’s hunted game is lawful just like their slaughtered meat.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#7/r16] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · كتاب محمد | reports | Muhammad’s legal text disallows hunting or slaughter by a Sabian, described as people between Jews and Christians with no religion.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#7/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · علماؤنا | reports | A third reported position allows unseen prey killed by arrow but not by dog, since an arrow has one clear cause while a hunting animal may kill in several ways.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#7/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · مالك | reports | In a report outside the Muwatta, Malik says not to eat prey found dead after the night if the arrow, hawk, or dog had not inflicted the mortal wound.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#7/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو عمر | reports | Abu ʿUmar infers that Malik allows prey whose mortal wound was inflicted even if it spent the night, though Malik dislikes it because of Ibn ʿAbbas’s report.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#7/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن عباس والثوري | reports | Ibn ʿAbbas reports, and al-Thawri similarly says, that prey absent overnight or for a day should not be eaten or is disliked.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#7/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الشافعي | reports | Al-Shafiʿi’s analogical reasoning is that prey should not be eaten if the hunter lost sight of where it fell.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#7/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الأوزاعي وأشهب وعبد الملك وأصبغ | reports | They allow prey found dead the next day with the hunter’s arrow or dog mark when the wound was mortal, even if it spent the night.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#7/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | “Unless it stinks” explains the dislike: stench makes food repulsive by ordinary nature, so eating is disliked but remains lawful, as the Prophet ate rancid fat.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#7/r9] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | Another explanation is fear of harm to the eater; on this rationale eating is forbidden when the danger is certain.
[QURTUBI-FULL:5:4#8/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · مالك والشافعي وأبو حنيفة وأصحابهم وجمهور الناس | reports | The majority, including Malik, al-Shafiʿi, and Abu Hanifa, prohibit eating game hunted by a Magian.
