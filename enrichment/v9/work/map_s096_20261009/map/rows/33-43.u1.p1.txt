33:43/q07 Why are the angels' prayers attributed to God? [هُوَ ٱلَّذِى يُصَلِّى عَلَيْكُمْ وَمَلَٰٓئِكَتُهُۥ · interpretation] (turns on: The explanations identify divine causation and appointment.)
  33:43/q07/p1 God creates and commands the angels' action. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī explains the attribution by divine creation and command.
  33:43/q07/p2 God appoints angels to care and seek forgiveness while believers attend to Him. — reasons: Al-Baqlī links their appointment to God's pre-eternal election and love.
33:43/q08 How is 'and His angels' joined grammatically? [وَمَلَٰٓئِكَتُهُۥ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the noun shares the verb's subject position or begins a clause with an omitted predicate.)
  33:43/q08/p1 It is coordinated with the implicit subject of yuṣallī. — reasons: Bursevī and al-Ālūsī say the intervening phrase makes a separate emphatic pronoun unnecessary. Al-Samīn says this requires shared meaning or metaphor because the agents' prayers differ.
  33:43/q08/p2 It is a new subject with an omitted predicate, 'they pray.' — reasons: Al-Samīn reports the possibility under the same shared-sense or metaphor theories.
33:43/q09 What does the initial 'He' do? [هُوَ · rhetoric]
  33:43/q09/p1 It reinforces and establishes the claim, especially the prayer's purpose of bringing believers into light. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr emphasizes the pronoun before the verbal predicate.
33:43/q10 What does the imperfect form yuṣallī suggest? [يُصَلِّى · rhetoric]
  33:43/q10/p1 Prayer recurs as remembrance and glorification recur, or is renewed for other acts and signs of faith. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr reads recurrence from the imperfect.
33:43/q11 Why is God's prayer presented through a relative clause? [ٱلَّذِى يُصَلِّى عَلَيْكُمْ · rhetoric]
  33:43/q11/p1 It presents God's prayer as known to believers already. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says they may infer it from God as the source of all good, or learn it from revelation or the Prophet.
33:43/q12 Where does the purpose clause attach and what work does it do? [لِيُخْرِجَكُم · grammar]
  33:43/q12/p1 It attaches to 'prays': divine and angelic care aims to bring believers from darkness into light. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī says prayer and mercy supply the purpose. Ibn ʿĀshūr makes it an immediate reward while believers remember and glorify God. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the purpose is rescue from disbelief into faith; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says special mercy yields lasting afterlife success. Study Quran cites parallels at 2:257, 5:16, 14:1, 14:5, 57:9 and 65:11.
33:43/q13 Who actually brings believers out of darkness? [لِيُخْرِجَكُم · theology] (turns on: The notes distinguish the direct guiding agent from the means of guidance.)
  33:43/q13/p1 God alone does; angels' prayer assists but angels cannot guide. — reasons: Bursevī argues from the singular verb and God's status as the true guide.
  33:43/q13/p2 God guides by mercy, revelation, prophets and the angels' prayers. — reasons: Rāzī says guidance operates through mercy; al-Baghawī and Ibn Kathīr add angels' prayers. Kur'an Yolu says prophets and revelation guide people toward the life God approves.
33:43/q14 What do the darknesses and light signify? [ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ ٱلنُّورِ · interpretation] (turns on: The imagery is read as conversion, moral reform, sustained guidance, inward transformation or passage into the next world.)
  33:43/q14/p1 Disbelief, shirk or idolatry gives way to faith, Islam or monotheism. — reasons: Al-Muqātil and al-Daḥḥāk identify shirk or disbelief and faith. Wāḥidī specifies monotheism and affirmation of mercy; al-Wajīz adds certainty and Islam. Al-Bayḍāwī includes sin and obedience. Study Quran reports Aj and JJ. Kur'an Yolu pairs unbelief with distress and faith with light.
  33:43/q14/p2 Error or misguidance gives way to guidance. — reasons: Al-Ḥasan says God also protects from misguidance that had not occurred, like gratitude for an averted danger. Qatāda and Ibn Zayd give the plain gloss. Al-Ṭabrisī attributes guidance to God's favors; Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Qurṭubī say the verse can confirm existing guidance.
  33:43/q14/p3 Ignorance of God becomes knowledge of Him, guidance and certainty. — reasons: Al-Ṭabrisī says ignorance leads to Hell and knowledge to Paradise. Al-Biqāʿī says knowledge produces guidance. Ibn Kathīr adds certainty, and Bursevī includes spiritual knowledge.
  33:43/q14/p4 Sin and disobedience become obedience and righteous practice. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī connect this to God's commands to obedience. Al-Biqāʿī says it is practiced faith. Kur'an Yolu includes failure to worship in darkness and righteous deeds in light.
  33:43/q14/p5 The Fire gives way to Paradise. — reasons: Al-Māwardī reports it as a third interpretation, followed by later transmitters.
  33:43/q14/p6 The graves give way to resurrection. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān transmits this among other reports.
  33:43/q14/p7 Heedlessness or forgetfulness becomes remembrance and awareness. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects this to 33:41: abundant remembrance brings mercy and light. Study Quran reports Aj's similar interpretation.
  33:43/q14/p8 Inward darkness of self, false aims and separation becomes insight, witnessing, unity and spiritual realization. — reasons: Qushayrī lists rescue through connection, from self-planning to witnessing decree, from self to insight, and from separation to realized unity; another inward reading says God prevents shirk and confirms signs of faith. Qāshānī describes angelic aid and disclosure of Names, moving from lower-self acts and qualities toward divine acts, qualities and Essence. Bursevī adds law's illumination and realization of spiritual reality.
  33:43/q14/p9 The passage can mean preserving and increasing faith already received. — reasons: Al-Ḥasan likens protection from unrealized error to thanking God for danger averted. Al-Qurṭubī says it confirms existing guidance; al-Kāshifī and Ibn ʿĀshūr reason that the believers were already guided when prayer reached them.
33:43/q15 How is the word 'darknesses' read and derived? [ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ · readings]
  33:43/q15/p1 Its vowel can shift from ḍamma to fatḥa for ease, with two morphological explanations. — reasons: Al-Kisāʾī derives the form through ẓulum and ẓulma; another explanation deletes the heavy ḍamma.
33:43/q16 What is the lexical sense of nūr here? [ٱلنُّورِ · meaning]
  33:43/q16/p1 It is radiance, described as a body with a particular color. — reasons: Al-Jishumī contrasts munīr, which illuminates from itself or causes illumination.
33:43/q17 What does God's mercy toward the believers entail? [وَكَانَ بِٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ رَحِيمًا · interpretation] (turns on: The notes emphasize distinct effects and illustrations of mercy.)
  33:43/q17/p1 He guides, forgives, protects, rewards and raises believers by His special mercy. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī says obedient believers are not punished. Al-Ṭabrisī says faith causes the great gift of reward. Ibn al-Jawzī says raḥīm is mercy specifically for believers. Al-Bayḍāwī includes welfare, higher rank and angels' care. Qāshānī says perfections accord with readiness. Ibn Kathīr describes guidance in this world away from callers to unbelief and innovation, and protection at the greatest terror with angels' news of Paradise and rescue from Hell. Al-Biqāʿī adds sincerity and higher ranks in Gardens. Bursevī says mercy precedes creation of angels and believers, does not vary with circumstances, and cites a Persian couplet comparing it with a sea unmuddied by floodwater. Ibn ʿĀshūr says mercy exceeds ṣalāt, including all good through words, deeds and kindness.
  33:43/q17/p2 He has mercy more profound than a mother's care for her child. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr cites Anas's report of a mother rescuing her child and the Prophet's assurance God would not cast His beloved into Fire; he judges its chain to meet the two Ṣaḥīḥs' criteria though none of the six books included it. He also cites ʿUmar's report in al-Bukhārī that God is more merciful than a mother.
  33:43/q17/p3 God approves faith and righteous deeds while allowing freedom to disbelieve and sin. — reasons: Kur'an Yolu ties God's mercy to the life He approves.
33:43/q18 Why does the closing clause call God merciful? [وَكَانَ بِٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ رَحِيمًا · rhetoric] (turns on: The clause is described as reassurance or as confirmation of the prayer's meaning.)
  33:43/q18/p1 It reassures believers and gives them glad tidings. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī call it comfort; Rāzī stresses all believers are included.
  33:43/q18/p2 It confirms that God's prayer means mercy and affirms the preceding explanation. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī use the close as evidence for ṣalāt as mercy; al-Ālūsī says it affirms the prior statement; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it concluding amplification.
33:43/q19 Why say 'the believers' instead of 'you'? [بِٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ · rhetoric]
  33:43/q19/p1 Naming their faith identifies it as the cause of special mercy and praises them. — reasons: Al-Ṭabrisī says faith is the cause of reward; al-Ālūsī says the wording either praises them by naming faith or signals the cause; Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts the explicit noun with a pronoun.
33:43/q20 What is the grammar and force of kāna raḥīman? [وَكَانَ بِٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ رَحِيمًا · grammar] (turns on: The notes address grammatical function and rhetorical force.)
  33:43/q20/p1 Raḥīman is kāna's predicate and its unspoken subject is God. — reasons: Al-Jishumī gives the iʿrāb.
  33:43/q20/p2 Kāna establishes God's mercy as a verified, familiar attribute. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says other verses attest the established quality.
