 al-khiṣām may be a verbal noun (‘in disputation’) or a plural of khaṣm, ‘opponent.’
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p99#2/r6] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | reports | If khiṣām is a verbal noun, the construction means ‘most contentious in disputation’; if it is the plural of khaṣm, it means ‘the fiercest of opponents in contention.’

[QASHANI:2:204-205/r2] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | The person whose speech pleases claims love while being a fierce opponent, since he remains in the lower self and heresy; he speaks only in worldly life, not from the heart in the afterlife.

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p316/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الراجز | reports | A rajaz line uses ladad for a fierce opponent who overcomes his peers.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p316/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Khiṣām is the verbal noun of arguing and can be a plural of khaṣm, with forms khaṣm, khuṣūm, and khiṣām.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p316/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The underlying sense of disputation is probing deeply into a matter; container corners are also called khuṣūm, singular khaṣm.
