75:18/q08 How is the hamza in 'qaraʾnāhu' read? [قَرَأْنَٰهُ · readings] (turns on: Whether the noncanonical form changes the verb to second person and removes hamza and alif.)
  75:18/q08/p1 The usual form retains the hamza in qaraʾnāhu. — reasons: Al-Samīn contrasts the noncanonical form with the expected qaraʾtahu, describing loss of hamza and alif in that form.
  75:18/q08/p2 Abū al-ʿĀliya reads a noncanonical qarat(t)ahu without hamza or alif, as second-person past 'when you intend to recite it'. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān reports open qāf, rāʾ and tāʾ in the three occurrences and explains the altered morphology. Al-Samīn derives it from qaraʾtahu by changing hamza to alif and deleting that alif for ease, comparing wa-law tar mā al-ṣibyān where the expected form is tarā and mā is extra.
75:18/q09 How is the hamza in 'qurʾānahu' pronounced or lightened? [قُرْءَانَهُۥ · readings] (turns on: Which hamza easing operations Arabic grammar permits and which Qurʾānic readers actually transmit; the comparison extends beyond this word.)
  75:18/q09/p1 Ibn Kathīr transfers the hamza's vowel in this Qurʾān form; a lightened quranahu loses the medial glottal catch. — reasons: Ibn al-Jazarī includes fa-ttabiʿ qurʾānahu with qurʾān al-fajr and qurʾānan farraqnāhu. QIRAAT-ER notes that lightening preserves the recitational noun and possessive suffix.
  75:18/q09/p2 Some grammarians allow hamza substitution, assimilation or intermediate realization after a consonant, even across words. — reasons: Abū ʿAmr reports Arab usage; Ibn Sawwār and Ibn Shīṭā permit substitution and assimilation across words. Kufan grammarians allow intermediate hamza after any vowelless letter. Abū Ḥayyān objects that the unrestricted Kufan rule conflicts with Arabic usage. Abū al-ʿAlāʾ extends intermediate realization after long-vowel wāw and yā as after alif; Ibn al-Jazarī calls this very weak because transfer and assimilation remain possible after wāw and yā. Al-Dānī attributes examples in mawʾilan and al-mawʾūda to Ibn Abī Hāshim, finding the written form especially helpful in mawʾilan.
  75:18/q09/p3 Readers restrict transfer after a vowelless consonant and retain full hamza after plural mīm, especially at a pause. — reasons: Ibn al-Jazarī endorses the readers' restriction: shifting hamza would change plural mīm's original ḍamma; some connect the mīm before hamza to restore it. Al-Sakhāwī says readers in his tradition agree on full hamza at a pause.
  75:18/q09/p4 Some grammarians allow deletion of hamza after long-vowel wāw or yā, but Qurʾān readers reject it. — reasons: Examples given are tazdarī aʿyunukum and adʿū ilā. Ibn al-Jazarī reports that no Qurʾān reader accepts this deletion.
75:18/q10 What occasion or conduct led to this instruction, and what happened afterward? [* · reports] (turns on: Whether the reported haste is explained solely by fear of forgetting or also by the urgency of the Prophet's mission; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī disputes the fit of the fear account.)
  75:18/q10/p1 The Prophet hurried or moved his lips under the strain of revelation, fearing loss; afterward he listened to Gabriel and recited after Gabriel left. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās's versions mention bowed head, moving lips or tongue, fear of forgetting, God placing the Qurʾān in his breast, and subsequent recitation. Al-Qurṭubī cites Muslim and Bukhārī and notes Ibn ʿAbbās and Saʿīd demonstrating the lip motion; Ibn Kathīr says the report comes by multiple routes and gives Bukhārī's lowered-head wording. Al-Biqāʿī gives no grading. Al-Suyūṭī calls the report sound and uses it against al-Qaffāl's record reading. Farāhī says fear of losing even a letter was tied to fear of responsibility.
  75:18/q10/p2 The Prophet hurried to memorize each new revelation; a sūra's end became known when Bismillāh descended. — reasons: Al-Suyūṭī transmits this separate Ibn ʿAbbās report, adding the sign that marked the end of a sūra.
  75:18/q10/p3 The Prophet's haste also arose from pressure to guide his people and answer opponents while revelation came gradually. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī says opponents pressed him and objected that a divine Qurʾān was not revealed at once; guidance was needed for his mission, strategy, spiritual, moral and intellectual needs and replies to continuing challenges. Revelation strengthened his heart and soul. During divinely wise pauses he looked heavenward; when Gabriel came he eagerly preserved every word. His love of knowledge and duty were noble, but patience was required. Farāhī adds fear of accountability and the wish for more guidance alongside fear of loss.
75:18/q11 How do these words fit the surrounding sūra? [* · links] (turns on: Whether 75:16–19 is an inserted revelation instruction or a continuation of the resurrection account; reported occasion and the parallel at 20:114 are used to decide.)
  75:18/q11/p1 They interrupt the resurrection passage to teach the Prophet etiquette in receiving revelation. — reasons: Al-Khaṭṭābī records the objection that 75:16–19 separates 75:14–15 from 75:20–21. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the instruction resembles 20:114 and an insertion needs no flanking sign; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares insertions at 19:64 and the prayer instruction at 2:238 amid marriage rulings. Al-Suyūṭī says attentive understanding has priority over rushed memorization and rejects the claim by some Shiʿites that material had dropped from the sūra; the sound revelation report weighs against a record reading.
  75:18/q11/p2 The revelation instruction is integrated with the sūra's account of conscience and resurrection. — reasons: Elmalılı says it joins the beginning and end through conscience. Al-Sāmarrāʾī sees a parallel between gathering bones and sun with moon at Judgment and gathering and reciting the Qurʾān in earthly life. Al-Bayḍāwī says a rebuke of haste in the foundational matter reinforces the sūra's wider rebuke of haste. Iṣlāḥī relates the passage to adversaries' pressure and gradual revelation; 20:114–115 gives a similar call for patience.
  75:18/q11/p3 They continue the resurrection scene as instructions to a person reading a record of deeds. — reasons: Al-Qaffāl cites the surrounding account of deeds at 75:13–15 and 17:14; the frightened person stammers while God gathers the record, requires confession and explains requital. Al-Rāzī says it is a strong warning but unsupported by reports. Al-Suyūṭī rejects it because of the sound revelation report; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the 20:114 parallel supports the revelation reading.
  75:18/q11/p4 They refer to the gathering and disclosure of Judgment itself. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this reading of the question about Judgment, the gathering of its people, its description and the trumpet blast, then dismisses it.
75:18/q12 What does the verse teach about receiving and using revelation? [* · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the direct instruction to Muhammad also guides every later reader and listener.)
  75:18/q12/p1 Wait patiently for revelation, attend to it, trust God's preservation, then recite and practice it. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj forbids premature recitation; Ibn Kathīr describes collection in the heart, enabled recitation and divine explanation as three stages. Al-Bayḍāwī says haste is blameworthy even in religion's foundation. Al-Zamakhsharī, as reported by Abū Ḥayyān, says the Prophet hurried to memorize and ask about meaning, so preservation is assured and questions wait until the angel finishes. Al-Biqāʿī extends following to fluent repetition and character-forming practice. Iṣlāḥī adds that God determines timing, collection, arrangement and elucidation; the Prophet can accept each portion without anxiety or heeding demands for revelation at once. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī disputes that fear of forgetting explains the wording.
  75:18/q12/p2 Believers too should read the whole message patiently, attend to its words and understand before concluding. — reasons: Asad applies it to all who read, hear or study the Qurʾān, comparing 20:114 and rejecting hasty conclusions from isolated verses, mechanical fluency and pleasure in beautiful sound without grasping the message. Study Quran likewise urges reflection on all teachings before hasty conclusions.
75:18/q13 Does following this recital bear on the Qurʾān's arrangement? [* · sciences]
  75:18/q13/p1 The Qurʾān was collected and ordered during the Prophet's lifetime, and he conveyed its final sequence. — reasons: Farāhī infers this from the command to follow the newly recited text and says the sequence matched the Guarded Tablet. He cites Qurʾān and Hadith evidence that the Prophet recited whole sūras in order, Companions memorized them that way, and he assigned each new verse its place in a sūra. Authentic agreed reports say Gabriel recited the completed Qurʾān to him in its true sequence.
