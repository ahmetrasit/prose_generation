2:186/q17 What grammatical force do the two imperative lāms have? [فَلْيَسْتَجِيبُوا۟ لِى وَلْيُؤْمِنُوا۟ بِى · grammar]
  2:186/q17/p1 The lām commands and governs the jussive; it can be silent after fāʾ or wāw. — reasons: JISHUMI, d. 494 (author): The lām is an imperative and required for third-person commands; with a present addressee it may be omitted or retained, is originally kasra, and may be vowelless after a single letter. QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 (author): The sīn is extra and the lām is an imperative; it jussives the verb because it makes it future-only, like a conditional particle, or because it only precedes verbs. NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 (author): The lām in ‘let them believe in Me’ is an imperative particle. ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 (قيل): Another view takes response as talbiya, the pilgrim’s formula “labbayk Allāhumma labbayk”; the lām is a command lām and is not reported read with kasra. SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 (author): The lām in fa-l-yastajībū is the imperative lām. BURHAN, d. 794 (author): The command lām is kasra when it begins a clause and is silent after wāw or fāʾ, as in this verse’s two commands. BURHAN, d. 794 (author): The jussive lām is a lām of command, marking that the speaker requests an action; it normally governs a verb whose subject is not the addressee.
