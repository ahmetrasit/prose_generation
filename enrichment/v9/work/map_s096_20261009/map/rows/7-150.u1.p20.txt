7:150/q38 How is this scene read inwardly? [* · inward] (turns on: Whether the images represent spiritual faculties or Moses' loss of intimate divine communion.)
  7:150/q38/p1 Moses figures the spirit, Aaron the heart, and the calf worldly attachment. — reasons: BURSEVI (التأويلات النجمية): The inward reading addresses the spirit’s qualities: they hastened back to worldly adornment and attachment before God’s command, interrupting the spiritual journey. BURSEVI (التأويلات النجمية): Seekers and attendants of kings should turn from worldly things during the spiritual path; only after crossing the ego’s and desire’s wastes may they return to guide people to God. BURSEVI (author): In the inward reading, Aaron is the heart, Moses the spirit, and their enemies the ego, Satan, and desire; the wrongdoers are heart’s qualities that worship the calf of the world. BURSEVI (author): The heart itself remains naturally oriented to loving and seeking God, though its qualities can take on the ego’s vices; the ego remains oriented to worldly desire while its qualities pass through spiritual states. ALUSI-FULL (صبغة الله أفندي الحيدري): Haydari's alternative is that Moses lost self-control and the tablets fell involuntarily; describing this as throwing treats failure to restrain himself as intentional, since the righteous are judged by the standard of the spiritually near. BURSEVI (author): If left to itself even briefly, the ego returns to its original nature; God’s established law does not change.
  7:150/q38/p2 Moses' sorrow reflects leaving divine communion for created people. — reasons: MAWARDI-FULL (بعض المتصوفة): Some Sufis say he was angry at leaving communion with the Real to address created beings. QUSHAYRI (author): Even a harmonious reunion with his people would have been troubled for Moses by losing intimate speech with God and returning to witness created beings. QUSHAYRI (ويقال): An unnamed interpretation assigns the sin to the Israelites and the apologetic to Moses and Harun, as an expression of pure servitude. QURTUBI-FULL (author argues against): He rejects the Sufi analogy between Moses' act and ecstatic musicians throwing or tearing clothes while claiming loss of awareness excuses them.
