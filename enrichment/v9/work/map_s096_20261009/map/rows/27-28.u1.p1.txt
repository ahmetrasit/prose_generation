ʾ of 'fa-alqih' read? [فَأَلْقِهْ · readings] (turns on: The suffix's vowel, whether its vowel is lengthened, and whether a pause form can be used in connected speech.)
  27:28/q13/p1 With kasra extended by a following yāʾ. — reasons: Al-Fārisī names Ibn Kathīr, Ibn ʿĀmir, al-Kisāʾī and some Nāfiʿ and Abū ʿAmr routes; Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds Warsh, and Ibn al-Jawzī repeats the three readers. Al-Fārisī prefers this regular form and calls suppression of yāʾ mainly poetic; Qirāʾāt-ER says the long ī extends the suffix vowel for recitational rhythm. Al-Fārisī cites Abū al-Ḥasan's dialect claim but says Sībawayh omitted it and treats the poetic case as necessity.
  27:28/q13/p2 With kasra on the hāʾ but no connected yāʾ, including reduced or light kasra. — reasons: Nāfiʿ and several Qālūn routes are reported; al-Baghawī names Abū Jaʿfar, Yaʿqūb and Qālūn for light kasra and others for full kasra. Ibn al-Jawzī names Qālūn; al-Nasafī names Yazīd, Qālūn and Yaʿqūb. Hishām has both this and the connected form according to al-Samīn. Al-Naḥḥās lists deletion retaining kasra; Qirāʾāt-ER notes kasra instead of stillness.
  27:28/q13/p3 With the hāʾ still, without a following vowel letter. — reasons: Reports name Abū ʿAmr, ʿĀṣim, Ḥamza, Abū Bakr and particular routes, with source variation over Abū Jaʿfar. Al-Nashr says Ḥafṣ makes it still, hence all of ʿĀṣim, and notes route-specific Abū Jaʿfar forms. Al-Naḥḥās reports grammarians treating stillness as pause but ʿAlī ibn Sulaymān rejects this allowance in connected speech; Bursevī instead allows ease or a pause form. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī claims vowelless hāʾ in every recitation, whether connected or paused, as hāʾ of pause.
  27:28/q13/p4 With ḍamma on the hāʾ and a following wāw. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās lists the wāw form, Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports some readers, and Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī name Muslim ibn Jundub. Al-Samīn calls ḍamma the original vowel.
  27:28/q13/p5 With ḍamma retained but the wāw omitted. — reasons: Abū Isḥāq's fivefold list, reported by al-Naḥḥās, includes this form.
27:28/q14 What grammatical forms do 'kitābī hādhā' and 'alqih' have? [بِّكِتَٰبِى هَٰذَا فَأَلْقِهْ · grammar] (turns on: The analyses address separate grammatical features of the phrase.)
  27:28/q14/p1 Hādhā can qualify kitābī, replace it appositionally, or explain it in apposition. — reasons: Al-Samīn lists the three analyses.
  27:28/q14/p2 Alqih is a jussive command with an object suffix referring to the letter. — reasons: Bursevī identifies the suffix and explains silent hāʾ as ease or pause pronunciation.
27:28/q15 What does 'then turn away from them' mean? [ثُمَّ تَوَلَّ عَنْهُمْ · meaning] (turns on: How far the bird moves, and whether tawallā describes temporary concealment or final departure.)
  27:28/q15/p1 Move aside or hide nearby, out of sight but close enough to watch and hear their reply. — reasons: Wahb and Ibn ʿAbbās give the nearby reading; al-Ṭabarī prefers it because the queen's consultation happened after receipt and had to be witnessed. Ibn Abī Ḥātim's Zuhayr specifies some distance; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Rāzī, Bursevī and others describe concealment and listening. Muqātil says withdraw and watch, al-Biqāʿī adds a safe position beyond reach, and Ibn Kathīr says the bird left her private chamber courteously.
  27:28/q15/p2 Leave the recipients and return to Solomon after seeing their answer. — reasons: Ibn Zayd gives this sense in al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān; Yaḥyā ibn Sallām says leave after delivery. This requires taking observation as preceding final departure or as occurring meanwhile.
  27:28/q15/p3 Tawallā denotes turning aside, departing or showing disregard. — reasons: Muqātil's gloss in al-Wāḥidī is withdrawal; another al-Wāḥidī gloss adds outward disregard. Al-Dāmghānī illustrates departing with 9:92; Ibn al-Jawzī contrasts the sense with other Qurʾānic uses and cites Moses' movement toward shade; one lexical sense of the same verb is assuming authority, such as a governor, though it is not applied to this command.
27:28/q16 In what order should delivery, withdrawal and observation be understood? [فَأَلْقِهْ ثُمَّ تَوَلَّ عَنْهُمْ فَٱنظُرْ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether withdrawal is a nearby concealed position compatible with watching, or a final departure that must follow observation.)
  27:28/q16/p1 As written: deliver, withdraw nearby, then observe from concealment. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī says the bird must witness the later consultation; al-Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn prefer the order without rearrangement. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says this leaves the recipients to the letter's contents without escort or pressure. Al-Biqāʿī says thumma allows time to verify the delivery. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls inversion untenable; al-Ālūsī finds it unsuitable because full departure prevents observation.
  27:28/q16/p2 The intended order is deliver, observe the reply, then withdraw or return. — reasons: Ibn Zayd supplies the delayed order; al-Zajjāj approves because fronting and delaying are common. Al-Farrāʾ calls it a semantic ordering that urges haste. Abū ʿAlī proposes the same inversion; al-Burhān argues one cannot observe after actually withdrawing, and al-Itqān affirms the inversion.
27:28/q17 Why should the hoopoe withdraw after delivery? [ثُمَّ تَوَلَّ عَنْهُمْ · rhetoric] (turns on: The explanations emphasize royal etiquette, freedom of deliberation, or safe surveillance; Iṣlāḥī disputes the deliberation claim.)
  27:28/q17/p1 Standing back is proper etiquette before a monarch while the messenger remains close enough to hear. — reasons: Wahb is cited by al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī; Ibn Kathīr calls the bird's withdrawal courteous.
  27:28/q17/p2 The recipients should face the letter's substance without the envoy escorting or pressing them. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives this explanation. Iṣlāḥī rejects a related claim that the courtiers needed privacy to talk freely before a foreign envoy, because the narrative never says they believed birds could understand speech.
  27:28/q17/p3 The bird should get to a safe listening place after its bearer's identity has become clear. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says it first fluttered above them until recognized, then withdrew beyond their reach but still able to hear.
27:28/q18 What does 'look' mean here? [فَٱنظُرْ · meaning] (turns on: Whether naẓara is a verb of cognition and reflection or means waiting for the reply.)
  27:28/q18/p1 Observe or reflect on their response and find out what they say. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān allows mental consideration; Bursevī says reflection and discovery; al-Ālūsī prefers reflection and learning their speech. Al-Qurṭubī reports a gloss of knowing, with 78:40 cited. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the following question suspends the verb from its object.
  27:28/q18/p2 Wait for the response. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn explain that this is not a cognition verb and does not suspend the following clause; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī cite 57:13.
27:28/q19 What are they to 'return'? [مَاذَا يَرْجِعُونَ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the response is viewed as a formal answer, their internal consultation, or the acceptance or rejection disclosed by it.)
  27:28/q19/p1 An answer or reply to Solomon's letter, which the hoopoe is to learn. — reasons: Al-Baghawī and Ibn al-Jawzī gloss it as their answer; al-Wāḥidī al-Wajīz says the queen's answer; Ibn Zayd says the bird must see it before returning to Solomon; Elmalılı notes that obtaining a response distinguishes this mission from simple carriage.
  27:28/q19/p2 What they say back and forth among themselves as they consult about the letter. — reasons: Al-Rāzī compares reciprocal answering in 34:30; al-Qurṭubī reports the internal-speech gloss; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and Bursevī describe hearing their talk. Al-Ṭabarī's reasoning rests on the queen's consultation following delivery; al-Ālūsī prefers this evident sense and says God enabled the hoopoe to understand what it heard.
  27:28/q19/p3 Their reaction will show whether they accept obedience and submission or refuse. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī gives this inward account of the response the bird is to inspect.
27:28/q20 How is 'mādhā yarjiʿūn' parsed? [فَٱنظُرْ مَاذَا يَرْجِعُونَ · grammar] (turns on: Whether unẓur means cognition or waiting, and whether mādhā is one interrogative, a split interrogative plus relative, or a relative whole.)
  27:28/q20/p1 Mādhā is one interrogative object of yarjiʿūn, and the question suspends unẓur if it means consider. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān states the single-interrogative option but calls yarjiʿūn its predicate; al-Samīn rejects that combination because the verb cannot govern the interrogative and also predicate it, citing 26:227. Al-Samīn retains the object analysis with the whole interrogative clause under suspended unẓur. Bursevī reports Ibn al-Shaykh's object parse; al-Ālūsī calls yarjiʿūn transitive; Ibn ʿĀshūr agrees the interrogation suspends unẓur and points to 16:24.
  27:28/q20/p2 Mā is interrogative and dhā is relative, with yarjiʿūn its relative clause and an understood resumptive object. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān and the Shaykh give the relative alternative; al-Samīn accepts it under suspended unẓur. Ibn al-Shaykh, cited by Bursevī, calls mā the subject and dhā meaning 'the one which' its predicate; al-Ālūsī explicitly notes the omitted resumptive.
  27:28/q20/p3 If unẓur means wait, mādhā is a wholly relative object directly governed by it, without suspension. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī make this conditional parse; the latter two cite 57:13 for waiting.
