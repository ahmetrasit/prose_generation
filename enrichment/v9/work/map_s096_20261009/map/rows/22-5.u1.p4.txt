22:5/q59 What exhortations and analogies are drawn from the creation and aging sequence? [* · interpretation] (turns on: Humility, struggle with denial, and the passage of youth.)
  22:5/q59/p1 Creation from dust should humble people and discourage pride. — reasons: Bursevī quotes a hadith that God made earth tractable and created people from dust to humble them, while pride bars Paradise.
  22:5/q59/p2 The reader should struggle against inner denial and seek divine protection. — reasons: Bursevī urges day and night purification and prays for refuge from enemies and deviation.
  22:5/q59/p3 Youth passes and desire consumes the years; old age may bring contentment after knowledge. — reasons: Bursevī quotes Saʿdī's lament for youth and Nasafī's paradox of ignorance before knowledge as greed versus ignorance after knowledge as acceptance.
  22:5/q59/p4 The ordering of the angelic fate terms reflects the prevalence of misery. — reasons: Bursevī says the miserable are destined for Fire, the happy for Paradise, and misery comes first because most people belong to it.
22:5/q60 What dispute about secondary source attribution is recorded? [مُّخَلَّقَةٍۢ غَيْرِ مُخَلَّقَةٍۢ · reports] (turns on: Whether Asad's cited authorities support his rendering.)
  22:5/q60/p1 Kur'an Yolu disputes Asad's attribution of 'complete yet incomplete' to Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatāda. — reasons: The authors say the cited reports only say complete versus incomplete; Bayḍāwī does not transmit Ibn ʿAbbās here, and the adjacent Khāzin text may explain the mistake.
22:5/q61 What social setting does Islāḥī give for the resurrection argument? [* · links]
  22:5/q61/p1 Idolaters harassed the Prophet and demanded punishment as visible proof when their stance on the Hereafter was challenged. — reasons: Islāḥī says the verse redirects them to signs in their own development and everyday world.
22:5/q62 How does the plant's movement further illustrate human destiny? [ٱهْتَزَّتْ وَأَنۢبَتَتْ · inward]
  22:5/q62/p1 Plant growth from deficiency toward perfection parallels humans' rise to perfected resurrection life. — reasons: Biqāʿī reports Rāzī's comparison with lasting life, sufficiency, knowledge, purity, and freedom from worldly defects for the blessed.
22:5/q63 What other minor readings or lexical parallels occur in notes on this verse? [* · readings] (turns on: Related hamza and accent examples.)
  22:5/q63/p1 Farrāʾ rejects another intrusive-hamza reading as an analogy for his objection to وربأت. — reasons: He cites al-Ḥasan's ولأدرأتكم in 10:16 as a mistaken hamza not accepted in recitation.
22:5/q64 How is the revived earth's motion explained as growth? [ٱهْتَزَّتْ · meaning]
  22:5/q64/p1 Its stirring can simply mean that it grows. — reasons: Māwardī reports al-Kalbī's brief gloss.
22:5/q65 Why is this resurrection proof introduced here? [* · links]
  22:5/q65/p1 Resurrection is the principal issue under dispute in the preceding context. — reasons: Ṭabrisī says this is why the next proof is introduced; Islāḥī describes idolaters' challenge and demand for punishment.
