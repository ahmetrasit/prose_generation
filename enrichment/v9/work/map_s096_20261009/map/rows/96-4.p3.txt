ch God taught.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:4/r1] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari calls the teaching of scientific benefits a supreme act of generosity: God teaches people what they did not know and moves them from ignorance to knowledge. | mentions 96:5
[ABUHAYYAN:96:4/r10] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak identifies the person taught as Idris.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:4/r11] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · قيل | reports | Another view identifies the person taught as Adam, the first to write.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:4/r2] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | He highlights writing as the finest knowledge because of its immense benefits.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:4/r3] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari says writing preserves sciences, wisdom, ancient reports and writings, and revealed books; without it religious and worldly affairs could not be sustained.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:4/r4] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | He says the very existence of writing and the pen suffices as evidence of God's precise wisdom and subtle governance.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:4/r5] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · بعضهم | reports | An unnamed poet likens pens to speckled snakes that move swiftly to the farthest reach, their black shafts moving only when white nibs play over them.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:4/r7] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The two objects of علم are omitted because the point is to attribute teaching to God.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:4/r8] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · ابن الزبير | rejects | A reading attributed to Ibn al-Zubayr supplies الخط after علّم; Abū Ḥayyān treats it as an explanatory gloss, not Qur'anic text, because it differs from the codex spelling.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:4/r9] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | On the apparent reading, the one taught is every person who writes with a pen.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p507/r10] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The two objects of ʿallama are omitted because the passage's purpose is to attribute teaching to God.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p507/r11] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن الزبير | reports | A reading attributed to Ibn al-Zubayr inserts “who taught writing by the pen”; the author treats it as explanatory gloss, not Qur'an, because it differs from the codex consonantal text.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p507/r12] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | prefers | The apparent teacher is everyone who writes with a pen; other views identify Idris or Adam, the first to write.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p507/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Writing preserves sciences, wisdom, earlier reports, statements, and revealed books; without it religious and worldly affairs could not be ordered.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p507/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | An unnamed poet compares writing pens to swift, dark serpents and describes their progress as if white blades played through them.
[SAMIN-DURR:96:4-5/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The phrase about teaching by the pen and teaching humanity resembles create/create humanity, so the same grammatical analyses apply.
[IBNKATHIR:96:1/r11] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Knowledge occurs mentally, orally, and in writing; written knowledge entails both mental and oral forms, while neither mental nor oral knowledge necessarily entails writing.
[IBNKATHIR:96:1/r12] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | reports | An unnamed athar urges people to preserve knowledge by writing it down.
[IBNKATHIR:96:1/r2] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · عائشة | reports | ʿĀʾisha reports that true dreams began revelation, then the Prophet loved solitude, worshiped at Ḥirāʾ for several nights, and Khadīja supplied his provisions.
[IBNKATHIR:96:1/r3] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · عائشة | reports | ʿĀʾisha recounts Gabriel's three forceful embraces in the cave, Muhammad's repeated reply that he could not read, and Gabriel's recitation of the opening revelation; Muhammad returned trembling.
[IBNKATHIR:96:1/r4] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · خديجة | reports | In ʿĀʾisha's report, Khadīja reassures Muhammad that God will never disgrace him, citing his kinship ties, truthfulness, bearing burdens, hospitality, and help in adversity.
[IBNKATHIR:96:1/r5] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · ورقة بن نوفل | reports | Waraqa ibn Nawfal, Khadīja's relative, was a pre-Islamic Christian who wrote Arabic and Gospel material in it; he identified the angel as the messenger sent to Moses, predicted opposition and expulsion, and promised support if alive.
[IBNKATHIR:96:1/r6] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | reports | Ibn Kathīr adds, with the qualifier “as has reached us,” that after Waraqa's death revelation paused and grief brought Muhammad repeatedly to mountain peaks, where Gabriel reassured him he was truly God's messenger.
