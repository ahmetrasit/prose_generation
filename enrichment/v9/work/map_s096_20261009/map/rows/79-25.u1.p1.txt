79:25/q13 What inward lesson follows from Pharaoh's claim? [* · inward] (turns on: Whether the lesson is the soul's humility or a distinction between two apparently similar utterances.)
  79:25/q13/p1 The soul must humble itself and renounce claims to lordship. — reasons: Qashani says the fearful soul should soften and break. Sulami reports Bishr that God made Pharaoh's tongue utter unreal lordship, and Sari that a servant in a master's guise becomes a punished example, refuted even by himself.
  79:25/q13/p2 Pharaoh's self-exalting claim differs from al-Hallaj's self-effacing utterance. — reasons: Al-Sumnani's spiritual encounter says Pharaoh saw only himself and diminished God, while al-Hallaj saw only God. Rumi's verses compare stone to precious stone, enemy to lover of light, curse to mercy; he disclaims union and incarnation. Study Quran repeats the contrast.

  79:25/q14/p1 Those endorsing Pharaoh's claim share his condemnation and Fire. — reasons: Al-Biqa'i names al-Hallaj, Ibn Arabi, Ibn al-Farid and followers, charging them with applying the Greatest Name to themselves and everything, contrary to the Qur'an.
  79:25/q14/p2 Al-Hallaj's utterance differs in meaning and fate. — reasons: Al-Sumnani and Rumi distinguish self-effacement from Pharaoh's self-glorification; Rumi says one brings mercy and the other curse.
79:25/q15 Why was Iblis singled out for a curse compared with Pharaoh? [* · theology] (turns on: Intention, precedence, direct defiance, or responsibility for followers.)
  79:25/q15/p1 His intention was worse. — reasons: Bursawi transmits this answer from As'ilat al-Hikam.
  79:25/q15/p2 He first initiated disobedience and discord. — reasons: The source says later lordship claims and oppression followed his whispering in word, deed and intention.
  79:25/q15/p3 He defied God directly and never confessed or humbled himself. — reasons: Pharaoh and others confronted prophets and intermediaries, sometimes confessing or supplicating to creatures.
  79:25/q15/p4 He originated disbelief and bears followers' misguidance. — reasons: The reported burden continues until Judgment Day.
79:25/q16 What human capacity made Pharaoh's claim possible? [* · inward]
  79:25/q16/p1 Humans combine majesty and beauty and can claim the greatest perfection. — reasons: Bursawi says humans were created with two hands, unlike Iblis, who has only majesty and never claimed a station he lacked, and angels, who have pure beauty.
79:25/q17 Is an alternate wording of the paired punishment reported? [ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ وَٱلْأُولَىٰٓ · readings]
  79:25/q17/p1 A reported reading says 'of the last and the first'. — reasons: Study Quran attributes it to Qurtubi, Tabari and Tabrisi; explanations link it to either the two boasts or denial of Moses followed by the highest-lord boast.
79:25/q18 What disobedience does al-Kalbī connect to the paired punishment? [ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ وَٱلْأُولَىٰٓ · interpretation]
  79:25/q18/p1 The later punishment is for Pharaoh’s disobedience, alongside an earlier act. — reasons: Al-Kalbī, as reported by Abd al-Razzaq, calls the Hereafter punishment retribution for disobedience; Tabari’s report speaks of the later act’s punishment for disobedience and the earlier act.
