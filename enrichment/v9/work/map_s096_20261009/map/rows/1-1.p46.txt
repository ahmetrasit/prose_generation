[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r11] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · سفيان بن عيينة | reports | Sufyan ibn ʿUyayna calls the surah al-Waqiya, the protecting one.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r110] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · داود وأحمد | reports | Dawud and one narration from Ahmad treat the basmala as an independent opening verse of each surah, not part of the surahs.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r111] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · الشافعي | reports | Al-Shafiʿi recites the basmala aloud with both Fatiha and the following surah when opening prayer; many early and later authorities also did so.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r112] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · أبو هريرة | reports | Abu Hurayra recited the basmala aloud and said his prayer most resembled the Prophet's; Ibn Kathir cites several scholars who authenticated the report.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r113] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbas reports that the Prophet opened prayer with the basmala; al-Tirmidhi says its chain is not strong.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r114] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · أنس بن مالك | reports | Anas describes the Prophet's recitation as prolonged, with lengthening on each word of the basmala.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r115] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · أم سلمة | reports | Umm Salama says the Prophet paused phrase by phrase in recitation, and al-Daraqutni calls the report's chain sound.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r116] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · أنس بن مالك | reports | When Muʿawiya omitted the basmala in Medina, the Muhajirun present objected; on his next prayer he recited it.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r117] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · أبو حنيفة والثوري وأحمد | reports | Another group does not recite the basmala aloud; Ibn Kathir says this is established from the four caliphs, Ibn Mughaffal, and other early authorities.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r118] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · مالك | reports | Malik does not recite the basmala in prayer either aloud or quietly, relying on reports that the Prophet and first caliphs began with al-Hamd and did not mention it.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r119] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | The legal disagreement is narrow: scholars agree a prayer is valid whether the basmala is recited aloud or quietly.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r12] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · يحيى بن أبي كثير | reports | Yahya ibn Abi Kathir calls it al-Kafiya: it suffices in place of other surahs, while none can replace it; this is also stated in a mursal report.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r120] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · ابن عباس | reports | A report says the basmala is one of God's names, extremely close to the Greatest Name, like the closeness between the black and white of the eye.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r121] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · عيسى ابن مريم | reports | A report explains the letters as bāʾ for God's splendor, sīn for His glory, and mīm for His dominion; Rahman is merciful in both worlds and Rahim in the next. Ibn Kathir calls the report very strange and possibly Israelite rather than Prophetic.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r122] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak is said to have transmitted a similar explanation of the basmala.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r123] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report from Burayda says the basmala was revealed to Muhammad and Solomon alone.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r124] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · جابر بن عبد الله | reports | Jabir's report describes clouds fleeing, winds and sea becoming still, animals listening, devils pelted, and God's promise of blessing when His name is said over something.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r125] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masʿud reportedly says reciting the basmala shields a person from each of the nineteen keepers of Hell; Ibn ʿAtiyya supports this with a report of more than thirty angels hastening to record praise.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r126] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report says not to curse Satan after a fall because that makes him swell with pride; saying “in God's name” makes him shrink like a fly (another version says like a house).
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r127] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Because the basmala humbles Satan, it is recommended at the beginning of actions and speech.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r128] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | reports | A report recommends beginning a sermon with the basmala and calls an affair begun without it defective.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r129] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | reports | The basmala is recommended on entering the toilet, based on reports transmitted on that practice.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r13] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari also records the names Surat al-Salah and al-Kanz.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r130] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A hadith judged hasan says there is no ablution for one who does not mention God's name; scholars differ over whether it is obligatory when remembered or absolutely.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r131] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | reports | The basmala is recommended before slaughter according to al-Shafiʿi and a group; others require it if remembered, and some require it absolutely.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r132] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet instructed his stepson ʿUmar ibn Abi Salama to say God's name, eat with his right hand, and eat from what is nearest; some scholars make the basmala obligatory at meals.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r133] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet taught spouses to say God's name and ask protection from Satan before intercourse; if a child is conceived, Satan will not harm it.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r134] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | The preposition bi in the basmala may attach to an implied noun, “my beginning,” or verb, “I begin”; Qur'anic usage supports both. | mentions 11:41, 96:1
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r135] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | The implied action varies with the activity begun—standing, sitting, eating, drinking, reciting, ablution, or prayer—and the basmala seeks blessing, aid to completion, and acceptance.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r136] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · ابن عباس | reports | In a report on the first revelation, Gabriel tells Muhammad to say the basmala and explains it as beginning, standing, and sitting in remembrance of God; Ibn Kathir elsewhere calls the report weak and disconnected. | mentions 96:1
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r137] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · أبو عبيدة وسيبويه | reports | One position identifies a name with the named object; Abu ʿUbayda and Sibawayh hold this view, as do al-Baqillani and Ibn Furak.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r138] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · فخر الدين الرازي | reports | Al-Razi reports that the Hashwiyya, Karramiyya, and Ashʿariyya identify name and named but distinguish both from naming, while the Muʿtazila distinguish name from named and equate it with naming; he chooses a third view.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r139] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · فخر الدين الرازي | prefers | Al-Razi's preferred view is that the name differs from both the named thing and the act of naming.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r14] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · ابن عباس وقتادة وأبو العالية | reports | Ibn ʿAbbas, Qatada, and Abu al-ʿAliya hold that the surah was revealed in Mecca.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r140] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · فخر الدين الرازي | reports | Al-Razi argues that names can exist without their referents, one thing can have many names, one name can refer to many things, and a word is an accident while its referent may be an entity.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r141] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · فخر الدين الرازي | reports | Al-Razi says God's many names refer to one God and that calling God by those names indicates their distinction from Him. | mentions 7:180, 56:74, 56:96
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r142] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · فخر الدين الرازي | reports | Those who equate name and named cite “blessed is the name of your Lord”; al-Razi answers that honoring the name honors the sacred essence, and a divorce utterance refers to the person bearing that name. | mentions 55:78
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r143] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Allah is the proper name of the Lord and is called the Greatest Name because all the other attributes are predicated of Him. | mentions 59:22, 59:23, 59:24, 7:180, 17:110
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r144] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A sound hadith says God has ninety-nine names; whoever comprehends them enters Paradise, though the enumerated lists in al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah differ.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r145] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Allah is a name not used for anyone else, and Arabic does not clearly derive it from an ordinary verb.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r146] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · الشافعي والخطابي وإمام الحرمين والغزالي والخليل وسيبويه | reports | These scholars regard Allah as an underived, fixed proper name; al-Khattabi argues that the vocative “ya Allah” works, whereas “ya al-Rahman” does not.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r147] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · رؤبة بن العجاج | reports | Ruʾba's line uses taʾalluh, worship, as evidence that Allah is derived from the root for worship.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r148] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · ابن عباس ومجاهد | reports | Ibn ʿAbbas's reading “wa-yadharuka wa-ilahatak” is explained as “your worship”; Mujahid gives the same gloss.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r149] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | reports | Those who derive Allah from divinity cite that God is worshipped in heaven and earth and is a god in both realms. | mentions 6:3, 43:84
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r15] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · أبو هريرة ومجاهد وعطاء بن يسار والزهري | reports | Abu Hurayra, Mujahid, ʿAta ibn Yasar, and al-Zuhri hold that the surah was revealed in Medina.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r150] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · الخليل وسيبويه | reports | Al-Khalil and Sibawayh derive the word from ilah, with the article replacing an initial hamza, by analogy with al-nas from unas; another proposal takes it from lah for exalted.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r151] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · الكسائي والفراء | reports | Al-Kisaʾi and al-Farraʾ derive it from al-ilah, with the hamza dropped and the two lams assimilated, like lakinna from lakin ana. | mentions 18:38
