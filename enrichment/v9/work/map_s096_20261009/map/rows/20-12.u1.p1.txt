20:12/q14 What does Ṭuwā signify in an inward reading of the valley? [بِٱلْوَادِ ٱلْمُقَدَّسِ طُوًۭى · inward]
  20:12/q14/p1 It is the purified spiritual realm or Divine Presence where worldly existence is folded away. — reasons: Al-Qushayri says the valley and divine precinct are free of defects; Qashani calls it the spirit-world with material stages folded beneath it. Ibn Ajibah calls it the inner sanctuary and Ocean of Divine Presence where Moses' soul is enwrapped. Sulami's wordplay says disobedience is folded from his heart.
20:12/q15 Does the sandal command govern prayer or mosque footwear? [فَٱخْلَعْ نَعْلَيْكَ · law] (turns on: Whether Moses' occasion-specific command supplies a general rule for worship footwear.)
  20:12/q15/p1 It gives no general objection to praying or circumambulating in clean sandals. — reasons: Al-Razi says the valley's honor made the order specific; the Prophet prayed in sandals, removed them only after Gabriel reported soil, and questioned those who imitated him. Ibn Masud rebuked Abu Musa's imitation and recalled prayer in socks and sandals. Al-Qurtubi cites 7:31 on adornment to support clean sandals and al-Nakhai's wish that removed shoes go to the needy. Al-Alusi notes reports favoring footwear.
  20:12/q15/p2 Shoes should be removed in mosques for cleanliness, humility and decorum. — reasons: Islahi treats this as mandatory and rejects the claim that clean shoes are permitted; Study Quran compares removing shoes before entering the Kaaba or a mosque.
  20:12/q15/p3 Clean shoes may be worn in a mosque. — reasons: Islahi reports this view and rejects it because even clean shoes breach mosque decorum.
20:12/q16 Where should removed sandals be placed during prayer? [نَعْلَيْكَ · law] (turns on: Whether between the feet is acceptable and how to avoid bothering neighboring worshippers.)
  20:12/q16/p1 Between one's feet, without inconveniencing others. — reasons: Abu Hurayra reports a Prophetic instruction and repeats it to al-Maqburi.
  20:12/q16/p2 Ahead of a follower, while left placement is for an imam or one praying alone. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi says a follower should avoid harming the person on his left and places them ahead rather than between the feet.
  20:12/q16/p3 Between the feet is an innovation. — reasons: Jubayr ibn Mut'im says this.
20:12/q17 How are impurities removed from sandals? [نَعْلَيْكَ · law] (turns on: Which impurities rubbing can remove and when washing remains necessary.)
  20:12/q17/p1 Rubbing or scraping can purify leather sandals, including disputed animal impurities. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi prefers rubbing for leather socks and sandals, citing Abu Sa'id's report. Al-Awza'i and Abu Thawr allow earth-rubbing generally for the disputed animal filth; Malikis differ. Abu Hanifa allows scraping dried filth but requires washing wet filth, including urine.
  20:12/q17/p2 Water is required to remove the listed impurities. — reasons: Al-Shafi'i requires water for all; Malik, al-Shafi'i and most jurists agree that blood, human excrement and urine need washing. Abu Hanifa also requires washing when filth is wet.
20:12/q18 What does المقدس mean? [ٱلْمُقَدَّسِ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the adjective emphasizes purification or blessing; some sources combine them.)
  20:12/q18/p1 Purified and set apart from impurity or evil. — reasons: Qutrub and Ibn Atiyya give 'purified'; al-Qurtubi derives it from quds, purity, and relates holy land to God removing unbelievers and settling believers. Bursevi adds separation from evil; Biqai says the precinct is purified of what is unfit for a king's court.
  20:12/q18/p2 Blessed, with divine favor and abundance. — reasons: Yahya ibn Sallam, Ibn Abbas and Mujahid give this gloss; Tabarsi specifies ample provision and fertility. Al-Tabari combines purified and blessed, as does al-Nasafi.
20:12/q19 Why is this valley sacred? [بِٱلْوَادِ ٱلْمُقَدَّسِ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the ground's holiness follows a specific divine association or the land's purification alone.)
  20:12/q19/p1 God specially favored it through His call, revelation or prescribed worship there. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi says God can favor places, times and creatures; merely removing unbelievers cannot explain this valley's distinct holiness. Tabatabai says places and times have no intrinsic rank, gaining sanctity by noble event or worship. Islahi likewise says all earth is equal in itself and special association with God confers sacredness. Asad ties double holiness to the voice and Moses' prophetic appointment.
  20:12/q19/p2 It was purified by removal of unbelievers and settlement of believers. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi gives this etymological account of the holy land, then says it alone cannot explain this particular valley's sanctity.
20:12/q20 Where is Ṭuwā and what is the valley like? [بِٱلْوَادِ طُوًۭى · referent] (turns on: The differing geographical identifications and physical descriptions.)
  20:12/q20/p1 It is a deep, rounded valley. — reasons: Al-Dahhak likens it to a well; al-Tha'labi records its circular depth.
  20:12/q20/p2 It is in Palestine. — reasons: Al-Hasan locates the twice-sanctified valley there.
  20:12/q20/p3 It is at Aylah. — reasons: Mubashshir ibn Ubayd calls it a valley there, blessed twice and read without nunation.
  20:12/q20/p4 It is at the base of Sinai. — reasons: Islahi describes it as a broad plain at Sinai's base.
  20:12/q20/p5 It is in Syria. — reasons: Al-Jawhari identifies Ṭuwā as a Syrian place name.
  20:12/q20/p6 It is by the mountain where Moses stood. — reasons: Ibn Zayd says Moses received what God gave him at this valley near the mountain.
20:12/q21 What does Ṭuwā mean here? [طُوًۭى · meaning] (turns on: Whether Ṭuwā is a place name, a doubled sanctification or call, an act of treading or crossing, a night expression, or a personal name.)
  20:12/q21/p1 It is the proper name of the valley. — reasons: Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ibn Zayd and others identify it as a name; Ibn Zayd calls it ancient. Al-Wahidi, Ibn Kathir and al-Alusi prefer this. Al-Tabari and al-Baydawi take it as appositional clarification; it appears also at 79:16. The name can still be explained by doubled sanctity, and spellings vary.
  20:12/q21/p2 It means twice or repeatedly: the valley was sanctified or blessed twice. — reasons: Qatada, al-Hasan and others report double blessing. Al-Farisi notes thanā denotes a repeated occurrence; Ibn al-Sayyid derives Ṭuwā from folding twice. Adi ibn Zayd's poetry supports ṭawā for repetition, though transmitters also read thanā. Asad favors this sense because God spoke there and Moses became a prophet; some make the repeated event two divine calls instead of doubled sanctity.
  20:12/q21/p3 It tells Moses to tread or traverse the valley, especially barefoot. — reasons: Ibn Abbas, Ikrima, Mujahid and Sa'id transmit this; Sa'id likens bare feet in Ṭuwā to entering the Kaaba. Some describe Moses crossing the valley by night and ascending its upper part, deriving the sense from folding or crossing a valley.
  20:12/q21/p4 It denotes the night hour when Moses was called. — reasons: Qutrub makes it an adverb of المقدس; al-Kirmani reportedly calls it an Arabized word for night. Al-Tha'labi includes a derivation from night.
  20:12/q21/p5 It is a man's Hebrew name, used as a vocative. — reasons: Al-Alusi records this as an isolated interpretation.
20:12/q22 How is Ṭuwā pronounced? [طُوًۭى · readings] (turns on: The initial vowel, tanwīn, and in one reported form the word's consonants.)
  20:12/q22/p1 With ḍamma and tanwīn, fully declined. — reasons: The Kufans and Ibn Amir are repeatedly named; Asim, Hamza and al-Kisai are included, and al-Suyuti records Asim's form. It is commonly parsed as a masculine valley or place name. Al-Tabari prefers tanwīn because it also fits a verbal noun. Al-Farra notes this may be indeclinable in some analyses.
  20:12/q22/p2 With ḍamma and no tanwīn. — reasons: Ibn Kathir, Nafi, Abu Amr and Abu Jafar are named. Al-Nahhas prefers nondeclension, as does Ibn Khalawayh for the verse endings. Analyses include a feminine region name, a shifted proper name like Umar, foreignness, or land-name analogy with Hunayn and Hira. This reading appears at 79:16 too.
  20:12/q22/p3 With kasra and tanwīn. — reasons: Al-Hasan, al-A'mash, Abu Haywa, Ibn Abi Ishaq, Abu al-Sammal and Ibn Muhaysin are named. Al-Zajjaj likens its declension to ma'an or dil'; some explain the form as repeated sanctification.
  20:12/q22/p4 With kasra and no tanwīn. — reasons: Abu Zayd reports this from Abu Amr; Ikrima is associated with kasra. It may be an uninflected place-name, as al-Farra's Hunayn and Hira analogies illustrate.
