75:17/q11 What happened as the Prophet received revelation, and why did he hurry? [* · reports] (turns on: Whether haste chiefly reflected fear of forgetting during recitation, or also concern for understanding and receiving guidance for the mission.)
  75:17/q11/p1 He moved his tongue or lips during Gabriel's recitation, fearing loss or forgetting, then listened and recited after Gabriel left. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās describes the strain of revelation, and Ibn ʿAbbās and Saʿīd demonstrate the movement. Bukhārī and Muslim transmit it by multiple routes; Bukhārī also has him lower his head as Gabriel arrived. Al-Tirmidhī is cited by Elmalılı. Qatāda's version also ascribes the movement to fear of forgetting. Al-Ṭabarī distinguishes hurried reception before gathering from later memory review, which he finds chronologically less fitting. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the fear account as obscuring the command to follow the completed recitation; he also calls the report's fit with the verses obscure.
  75:17/q11/p2 He eagerly sought further revelation for his mission and guidance amid pressure from opponents, as well as fearing loss. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī says his opponents demanded answers and challenged piecemeal revelation; pauses had divine wisdom, yet the Prophet looked heavenward for help. Revelation renewed strength, guided his strategy and spiritual, moral, and intellectual needs, and enabled responses. Farāhī adds fear of responsibility for even one lost letter. Al-Zamakhsharī says he also hurried to ask about meaning; al-Rāzī contrasts reliance on repetition with turning to God, comparing 20:114.
75:17/q12 What should the assurance teach about receiving and studying revelation? [* · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the instruction is applied specifically to the Prophet's reception or also to every later reader and listener.)
  75:17/q12/p1 The Prophet should listen patiently, trust God's preservation and timing, and attend to the revealed portion and its meaning. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says fear of forgetting is answered by divine aid; al-Jishumī stresses reflection and knowledge of meanings and rulings over mere memorization. Al-Bayḍāwī calls haste blameworthy even here. Iṣlāḥī links patience to gradual revelation and 20:114–15, the Prophet's noble motives, and his duty to convey and explain; al-Zamakhsharī says questions about meaning wait until recitation ends.
  75:17/q12/p2 Every Qur'an student should read attentively and grasp the whole message before hasty conclusions. — reasons: Asad extends the passage to anyone reading, listening, or studying, compares 20:114, warns against isolated verses, mechanical fluency, and attraction to sound without meaning, and sees one integrated message rather than detached maxims, narratives, or laws. Study Quran likewise calls for reflection on all teachings.
75:17/q13 What does the promise imply about preservation of the received Qur'an? [* · theology]
  75:17/q13/p1 The Qur'an has been guarded against loss, addition, and textual alteration. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās rejects a claim that some remained unrevealed; Sufyān contrasts God's direct care of the Qur'an (15:9) with Torah and Gospel entrusted to their people (5:44). Al-Zarkashī invokes 15:9 and communal agreement that the codex is certain and protected for practice. Farāhī cites 41:41–42 and 15:9 and reports communal agreement. Named Imāmī authorities reject disappearance; al-Qummī calls the codex precisely the revelation without an extra letter, and Murtaḍā says weak dissenting reports cannot defeat certainty.
75:17/q14 When and how were the Qur'an's parts collected and ordered? [* · sciences] (turns on: The distinction between memorized and ordered revelation in the Prophet's lifetime and Abū Bakr's later gathering from existing materials.)
  75:17/q14/p1 The entire revelation was memorized and arranged during the Prophet's life, with divinely guided placement and final recitation. — reasons: Al-Zarkashī says the four famous Companion preservers were examples, not the only memorizers, while noting an exception concerning Barāʾa. Iṣlāḥī says the Prophet instructed collectors where verses belonged and reviewed extant revelation with Gabriel each Ramadan, twice in the final Ramadan. Farāhī infers an ordered sequence from the instruction to follow the recital, says it matched the Guarded Tablet, cites whole sūrahs recited and memorized in order, and says agreed authentic reports make Gabriel's final recitation settle doubts.
  75:17/q14/p2 After Yamāma's heavy losses, Abū Bakr commissioned Zayd to gather written and memorized Qur'an. — reasons: Al-Bukhārī's Zayd report says ʿUmar feared further deaths among reciters, persuaded a hesitant Abū Bakr despite the Prophet not having performed this collection, and Zayd, a trusted young revelation scribe, assembled material from writing media and memories after his own hesitation.
75:17/q15 How does this verse fit its surrounding passage? [* · links] (turns on: Whether 75:16–19 form an instructional interruption or continue and echo the surrounding resurrection theme.)
  75:17/q15/p1 Verses 75:16–19 interrupt the resurrection account to instruct the Prophet about revelation. — reasons: Al-Khaṭṭābī reports the objection that they disrupt movement from self-witness (75:14–15) to love of worldly life (75:20–21). Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī accepts an interruption and compares 20:114, adding that an interruption needs no overt signs before and after it; al-Bayḍāwī sees a rebuke of haste even in revelation.
  75:17/q15/p2 The revelation passage is integrated with the sūra's account of conscience, impatience, and gathering. — reasons: Elmalılı joins the preceding conscience discussion to the end of the passage; al-Sāmarrāʾī compares God's gathering of bones and of sun with moon at Judgment to earthly gathering and reciting of Qur'an. Al-Rāzī compares the unbeliever fleeing God with the Prophet's need to turn from repetition to God.
  75:17/q15/p3 The passage continues the resurrection scene through the reading of a person's record. — reasons: Al-Qaffāl points to 75:13–15 and 17:14 and calls it a strong warning without reported support; al-Balkhī and al-Bayḍāwī portray fear, confession, judgment, and requital. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects this because the prophetic interruption is plausible and 20:114 supports it.
  75:17/q15/p4 The passage concerns haste to ask about Judgment and God's gathering of its people. — reasons: A further interpretation reported by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī takes recitation as revealing Judgment's description and explanation as the trumpet blast; he rejects it.
75:17/q16 What inward sense is given to gathering and recitation? [* · inward] (turns on: Whether the inward/outward division describes a secret deposit, effacement at unity, or reliance on God across all affairs.)
  75:17/q16/p1 The Qur'an is gathered inwardly and recited outwardly. — reasons: Al-Wāsiṭī describes the deposit in the innermost secrets and explanation in inward hearts, with recitation aloud in public; al-Bursawī summarizes his inward/outward distinction.
  75:17/q16/p2 God gathers it in the addressee at unity and recites through one effaced from self. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī describes absence of independent existence or trace at this station.
  75:17/q16/p3 The Prophet should rely on God for every affair, including inner retention and easy utterance. — reasons: An unnamed authority in al-Sulamī extends the promise from Qur'anic gathering in the breast and ease on the tongue to divine care for all affairs.
75:17/q17 How is heard recitation related to divine speech? [* · theology]
  75:17/q17/p1 The listener hears created recitation and through it understands God's eternal speech. — reasons: Al-Zarkashī reports this from some of his colleagues.
75:17/q18 Why is an alif written in Qur'an? [قُرْءَانَهُۥ · rhetoric]
  75:17/q18/p1 Its written alif reflects clear, accessible revelation as compared with the comprehensive Book's higher aspect. — reasons: Al-Zarkashī says written alif marks a concrete, lower, knowable reality and omission a metaphysical or attributive higher one. The Qur'an details verses established in the Book and is nearer understanding; he contrasts its written alif with its omission in كتاب, citing 11:1, 41:3, and 75:17.
75:17/q19 How do the divine plural forms here relate to singular forms elsewhere in the sūra? [* · rhetoric]
  75:17/q19/p1 The sūra uses both plural divine actions of gathering and ability and singular actions of creating and proportioning. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī juxtaposes 'We are able,' 'We gather,' and 'We shape' with 'He created' and 'He proportioned.'
