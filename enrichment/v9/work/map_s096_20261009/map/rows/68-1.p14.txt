68:1-33#4/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The Pen's writing matches all events in reality, with nothing occurring contrary to what was written.
[TABATABAI:68:1-33#4/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · أبو هريرة | reports | A parallel report says the Pen was sealed and would not speak again until the Day of Resurrection.
[TABATABAI:68:1-33#5/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak reports that Quraysh insulted ʿAli after the Prophet honored him; the passage identifies the slanderers as those astray and ʿAli as among the guided.
[TABATABAI:68:17/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The disconnected letter Nun was discussed earlier in the interpretation of al-Shura.
[TABATABAI:68:17/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Qalam is the familiar pen; satar can mean a row of writing, planted trees, or people standing in a row, while the verb means writing line by line.
[TABATABAI:68:17/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The oath concerns pen and writing in general, a great divine gift that preserves hidden events and meanings from the barriers of time and distance.
[TABATABAI:68:17/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | God taught speech and writing, as stated in 55:3–4 and 96:4–5; the oath honors a gift like other created blessings sworn by in the Quran. | mentions 55:3, 55:4, 96:4, 96:5
[TABATABAI:68:17/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | reports | Other readings take ma as a verbal noun meaning writing, the pen as the highest pen and its writing as the angels' record, or the writing surface as the Preserved Tablet; the author regards these options as weak.
[TABATABAI:68:17/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | reports | Further options take the plural as honorific, or interpret pen and writing as writers and their written works; the author also calls these possibilities weak.
[TABATABAI:68:29-33/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · الصادق عليه السلام | reports | A report explains Nun as a river in Paradise that God froze into ink; a luminous pen wrote in the luminous Preserved Tablet everything that was and will be until Resurrection.
[ASAD-NOTES:68:1#n1/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Asad | holds | This is chronologically the first occurrence of the disjointed letters that precede some Qur'anic surahs; Asad points to Appendix II for theories about them.
[ASAD-NOTES:68:1#n1/r2] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Asad | rejects | Some early commentators cited by Tabari take nun as an abbreviation for a homonymous noun meaning great fish or inkwell; Asad rejects this on the grammatical grounds advanced by Zamakhshari and Razi.

[BINTSHATI:v2p39/r1] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | The surah is early Meccan except verses 17–33 and 48–50, and is commonly placed after al-ʿAlaq as the second revelation; despite ordering disputes it reflects the first revelation’s atmosphere. | mentions 96:1
[BINTSHATI:v2p39/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · بعض المفسرين | reports | Some say the surah, or most of it, was revealed about al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīrah and Abū Jahl ibn Hishām.
[BINTSHATI:v2p39/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · بعض المفسرين | reports | A proposed connection to the preceding surah says the opening clears the Prophet of accusations of madness, praises his patience and character, and answers the disbelievers’ claims of poetry and magic.
[BINTSHATI:v2p39/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | rejects | The author says this coherence argument assumes muṣḥaf order: al-Mulk was revealed far later, around the seventy-seventh surah, over seventy revelations after al-Qalam.
[BINTSHATI:v2p40/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | rejects | Because the muṣḥaf writes N as one letter, Bint al-Shati sets aside al-Ṭabarī's views that it means a fish, a tablet of light, or an inkwell: those nouns would be written with three letters.
[BINTSHATI:v2p40/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · ابن عباس ومجاهد | rejects | A report identifies N with the fish beneath the seventh earth that bears the earths; it was created before heaven and earth, and the earth's movement was steadied by mountains.
[BINTSHATI:v2p40/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī admits uncertainty about whether “inkwell” is a linguistic or revealed term, a common noun or a proper name, and whether it is diptote.
[BINTSHATI:v2p40/r5] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · قول آخر | rejects | Another view makes N the name of the sura; Bint al-Shati finds this unhelpful because the sura would simply be named after its opening letter, as Ṣād and Qāf are.
[BINTSHATI:v2p40/r6] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الراغب الأصفهاني | prefers | Al-Rāghib reads N as the familiar letter of the Arabic alphabet, the interpretation Bint al-Shati finds reassuring.
[BINTSHATI:v2p41/r1] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | Al-Qalam is the first sura to open with a disjoined letter; Bint al-Shati says 28 more followed, 26 Meccan and three from the early Medinan period. | mentions 2:1, 3:1, 13:1
[BINTSHATI:v2p41/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | The disjoined letters comprise fourteen distinct letters, half the letters of the Arabic alphabet.
[BINTSHATI:v2p41/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · أصحاب هذا القول | reports | One view treats the letters as signs for divine names or attributes, but its adherents do not agree on expansions: N may stand for Helper or Light, for example.
[BINTSHATI:v2p41/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · قول | rejects | A view calls the letters marks made by the revelation scribes; Bint al-Shati objects that human marks would then occur inside Qur'anic verses after the basmala.
[BINTSHATI:v2p41/r5] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · حيى بن أخطب | reports | The abjad theory is identified as an Isrāʾīliyyāt report: Huyayy calculates the letters in alif-lām-mīm as 71 years, then alif-lām-mīm-ṣād as 161 and alif-lām-rāʾ as 271, and questions whether to follow a prophet with that term; the Prophet replies with further letters, leaving the Jews unsure of the term. | mentions 2:1, 7:1, 13:1
[BINTSHATI:v2p41/r6] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · قول | reports | Another view makes the letters alerts to what follows, often a mention of the Qur'an.
[BINTSHATI:v2p42/r1] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | reports | Al-Rāzī and Ibn al-Qayyim elaborate the view that the opening letters alert readers to what follows, and Ibn Kathīr surveys its occurrences.
[BINTSHATI:v2p42/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الشعبي | reports | The letters are among the ambiguous matters whose knowledge God reserves; al-Shaʿbī says every book has a secret and the Qur'an's secret is its opening letters.
[BINTSHATI:v2p42/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · ابن القيم | reports | Ibn al-Qayyim says the letters honor the alphabet as the building blocks of God's speech and revealed books, and as a gift that enables people to speak.
[BINTSHATI:v2p42/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | prefers | Bint al-Shati finds a still closer explanation in the letters showing that the Qur'an is made from the same letters familiar to its Arabic audience, exposing their inability to produce its like.
[BINTSHATI:v2p42/r5] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | In this early Meccan sura, N draws attention to the mystery of the letter in the Qur'an's inimitable eloquence.
[BINTSHATI:v2p42/r6] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | Because the sura answers pagans who deny the Prophet and call the Qur'an old legends, its opening prepares the challenge to produce its like and brings them to confront the proof in their own language and letters.
[BINTSHATI:v2p43/r1] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | The first revealed word, “Read,” points to God's great sign in creating humans from a clinging form and teaching by the pen; Al-Qalam then opens with N to point to letters as the basis of reading, knowledge, and eloquence. | mentions 96:1, 96:2, 96:3, 96:4, 96:5
[BINTSHATI:v2p43/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | An isolated letter supplies no meaning and is nearly only a sound; its greater mystery appears when it takes its place in a word.
