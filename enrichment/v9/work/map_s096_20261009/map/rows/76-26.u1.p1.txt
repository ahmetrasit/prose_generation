76:26/q14 How is the night command read inwardly? [وَمِنَ ٱلَّيْلِ فَٱسْجُدْ لَهُۥ لَيْلًا طَوِيلًا · inward]
  76:26/q14/p1 Night is the station of self or heart; prostration is annihilation of self, and its length is enduring in that station. — reasons: Al-Qashani describes return to creation after annihilation for religious guidance, requiring the heart's veil and the self's presence; prostration sees self subsist through God and humanity perish, without duality or ego; the long night is eternal abiding. Bursevi reports the Najmiyya mapping of prostration to annihilation of self, bowing to attributes, and standing to actions, through the night of nature and dusk of human embodiment.
76:26/q15 How do these commands relate to the preceding remembrance instruction? [* · links] (turns on: Whether the preceding remembrance is specified as the daily prayers, verbal invocation, or a general request embracing both.)
  76:26/q15/p1 They spell out the prescribed prayers and add the night vigil. — reasons: Al-Razi reports the five-prayer reading: morning is dawn, evening covers noon and afternoon, night prostration covers Maghrib and Isha, then long glorification is vigil. Ibn Ashur reports Ibn Abbas and Ibn Zayd assigning morning and evening to the five prayer times, with evening including noon. Kuran Yolu reports Razi's version and the different legal force of the extended vigil. Study Quran reports Aj, JJ, and Ṭs reading remembrance as naming God in daily prayer; Aj also allows remembrance at every moment. Ibn Ashur cites 11:114-115 calling prayer remembrance.
  76:26/q15/p2 Remembrance encompasses verbal glorification and belief in God's transcendence throughout day and night. — reasons: Kuran Yolu reports this as Razi's second reading, rather than prayer alone. Study Quran reports Aj's reading of remembrance at every moment and, in worldly tribulations, invocation of God; it adds that many Sufis read verse 25 as invoking the Supreme Name Allah, comparing 15:97-98.
  76:26/q15/p3 Remembrance is a broad instruction embracing required and optional prayer, supplication, and other devotion. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says the general request includes prophetic supererogatory devotion as well as required acts. Kuran Yolu reports Ibn Ashur's inclusion of obligatory and voluntary prayer, supplication, and seeking forgiveness, and prefers a broad stress on worship with night prayer, glorification, repentance, and supplication.
76:26/q16 What purpose do the night commands serve in their context? [* · links] (turns on: How the night commands function in the surrounding address and contrast with opponents.)
  76:26/q16/p1 They sustain the Prophet's patience and resolve while opponents trouble him. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says varied remembrance supports continued preaching and endurance of opponents' speech; he cites parallel commands to glorify, prostrate, remember, and persevere (15:97-98, 73:8, 10). Islahi says the Prophet should trust God and seek patience through prayer and remembrance. Kuran Yolu says morning and evening worship strengthens devotion and resistance to polytheists. Al-Tibi, reported by Alusi, connects endurance of sinners' hostility with devotion through day and night, citing 15:97-98. Asad casts night worship as consolation in unhappiness and dark surroundings. Elmalili connects the long night with waiting for God's decree, urging the Prophet and indirectly the community to stay awake in worship and preparation.
  76:26/q16/p2 The following contrast concerns disbelievers' preference for immediate gains over the Hereafter. — reasons: Islahi says they manufacture objections despite the Hereafter's certainty and God's ease in recreating them; this explains the surrounding contrast with the Prophet's patient devotion.
