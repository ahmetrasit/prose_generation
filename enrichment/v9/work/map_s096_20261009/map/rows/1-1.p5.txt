[THALABI:v1p104/r2] THALABI, d. 427 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report from Ṭalḥa ibn ʿUbayd Allāh says that whoever omits the basmala has omitted a verse from God’s Book.
[THALABI:v1p104/r3] THALABI, d. 427 · علي بن أبي طالب | reports | The author notes that ʿAlī included the basmala among the verses he counted as the Mother of the Book.
[THALABI:v1p104/r4] THALABI, d. 427 · المهاجرون والأنصار | reports | When Muʿāwiya omitted the basmala while leading prayer in Medina, the Emigrants and Helpers called out that he had forgotten it, so he repeated the prayer with it.
[THALABI:v1p105#2/r1] THALABI, d. 427 · author | prefers | The author says reciting the basmala aloud in prayer is sunnah, citing commands to read in the Lord’s name and to remember the Lord’s name when praying. | mentions 96:1, 87:15
[THALABI:v1p105#2/r2] THALABI, d. 427 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report relayed by al-Mahdī from his father and grandfather, Ibn ʿAbbās, says the Prophet recited the basmala aloud.
[THALABI:v1p105#2/r3] THALABI, d. 427 · عطاء وطاوس ومجاهد | reports | Al-Layth reports that ʿAṭāʾ, Ṭāwūs, and Mujāhid recited the basmala aloud.
[THALABI:v1p105#2/r4] THALABI, d. 427 · عبد الله بن عباس، عبد الله بن عمر، عبد الله بن الزبير، عبد الله بن صفوان | reports | A report says the four ʿAbādila opened recitation with the basmala and voiced it.
[THALABI:v1p105#2/r5] THALABI, d. 427 · جعفر بن محمد | reports | Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq says the Prophet’s family agreed on voicing the basmala, making up missed night prayer in daytime, and speaking well of Abū Bakr and ʿUmar and their companion.
[THALABI:v1p105#2/r6] THALABI, d. 427 · جعفر بن محمد الصادق | reports | Al-Ṣādiq calls audible recitation the truth and links it to the verse about people turning away when God alone is mentioned in the Qurʾān. | mentions 17:46
[THALABI:v1p105/r1] THALABI, d. 427 · المهاجرون | reports | A separate report says Muʿāwiya recited the basmala for al-Fātiḥa but not the next sura; the Emigrants challenged him, and he repeated the prayer while reading that sura.
[THALABI:v1p105/r2] THALABI, d. 427 · author | holds | The author argues from the arrangement of verse endings and the basmala’s placement that it was written where revealed, rather than inserted merely to divide suras.
[THALABI:v1p105/r3] THALABI, d. 427 · author | holds | The author adds that scribes used black ink for the basmala, unlike non-Qurʾānic marginal material, which supports its status as Qurʾān.
[THALABI:v1p106/r1] THALABI, d. 427 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Abū Hurayra reports that Gabriel taught the Prophet prayer, after which the Prophet stood, said takbīr, and voiced the basmala.
[THALABI:v1p106/r2] THALABI, d. 427 · الزهري | reports | Al-Zuhrī calls reciting the basmala in the Fātiḥa part of prayer’s sunnah and says one who omits it has not recited the sura; he names ʿAmr ibn Saʿīd as the first to abandon it in Medina.
[THALABI:v1p106/r3] THALABI, d. 427 · أنس بن مالك | reports | Anas reports that he did not hear the Prophet, Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, or ʿUthmān recite the basmala aloud.
[THALABI:v1p106/r4] THALABI, d. 427 · author | rejects | The author says Anas’s report only denies raising the voice; it does not deny that the basmala is part of the sura, and “not aloud” need not mean whispering or omitting it.
[THALABI:v1p107/r1] THALABI, d. 427 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says the Prophet voiced the basmala in Mecca, prompting pagans to mock the mention of the “god of al-Yamāma,” meaning Musaylima; the author connects this to the command for moderate volume. | mentions 17:110
[THALABI:v1p107/r2] THALABI, d. 427 · أنس بن مالك | reports | Anas says the Prophet, Abū Bakr, and ʿUmar opened recitation with al-ḥamdu lillāh; the author interprets this as opening prayer with the sura called al-Ḥamd, not beginning at verse two.
[THALABI:v1p92/r1] THALABI, d. 427 · author | holds | The prefixed bā is an extra particle of attachment or inclusion, as in writing with a pen, where the writing adheres to the pen.
[THALABI:v1p92/r10] THALABI, d. 427 · author | holds | The basmala means that beings came into existence through God and creatures subsist through Him; ism distinguishes the one invoking blessing from its object.
[THALABI:v1p92/r11] THALABI, d. 427 · author | holds | The author identifies name with the named, the existent thing, its essence, and itself, citing the announcement that a boy’s name is Yaḥyā and then addressing him as Yaḥyā. | mentions 19:7
[THALABI:v1p92/r2] THALABI, d. 427 · author | holds | The bā is always kasra because it is an incomplete, inclined letter; imāla signals kasra.
[THALABI:v1p92/r3] THALABI, d. 427 · سيبويه | reports | Sibawayh explains the kasra by saying that kasra is the bā’s only function.
[THALABI:v1p92/r4] THALABI, d. 427 · المبرد | reports | Al-Mubarrad says its kasra restores it to its original form, comparing yāʾ with kasra as wāw with ḍamma and alif with fatḥa.
[THALABI:v1p92/r5] THALABI, d. 427 · القيسي | reports | Al-Qaysi says the opening bā was lengthened and likened to alif-lām so God’s Book would begin with an exalted, emphatic letter.
[THALABI:v1p92/r6] THALABI, d. 427 · عمر بن عبد العزيز | reports | ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAziz instructed scribes to lengthen the bā, show the sīn, separate them, and round the mīm in reverence for God’s speech.
[THALABI:v1p92/r7] THALABI, d. 427 · أبو خالد بن يزيد المرادي | reports | Abu Khalid ibn Yazid al-Muradi says the alif of ism was dropped for frequent use and its length was transferred to the bā; where ism retains alif, the bā keeps its ordinary form.
[THALABI:v1p92/r8] THALABI, d. 427 · author | holds | The phrase has an omitted command, understood as “say” or “begin” in God’s name.
[THALABI:v1p92/r9] THALABI, d. 427 · آدم | reports | Adam treats ism as an added word, glossing the phrase as “By God, the Merciful, the Compassionate, He is”; Labid’s verse uses ism for “peace.”

[THALABI:v1p93/r2] THALABI, d. 427 · author | holds | The commands to glorify or bless God’s name use “name” to mean God Himself; the report about Mudar likewise says no one would worship Him. | mentions 87:1, 55:78
