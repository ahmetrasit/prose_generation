## THE CURRENT MAP
50:16/q01 What does God's creation of the human mean? [خَلَقْنَا · interpretation] (turns on: Whether خلقنا refers only to initial formation or to continuing dependence and renewal.)
  50:16/q01/p1 God brought the human into ordered, wise existence. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya defines creation as ordered, measured, wise bringing into being.
  50:16/q01/p2 Human creation and existence are continuously renewed and dependent on God. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes ongoing changing existence; the present knowledge clause and enduring nearness accompany it. Būrsawī says Sufis and theologians posit renewed substances and accidents; the Mathnawī images recurring waves and al-Shaʿrānī distinguishes changing attributes from entities. Būrsawī applies this to reports of the Kaaba turning toward Rābiʿa, whose truth requires unveiling.
50:16/q02 Why is human creation stated so emphatically here? [وَلَقَدْ خَلَقْنَا · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the emphasis chiefly grounds knowledge of inward states or the resurrection argument.)
  50:16/q02/p1 The emphatic assertion supports the following claim to know hidden human states. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says lām and qad anticipate the coordinated knowledge clause: people acknowledge creation but fail to grasp God's knowledge. Elmalılı hears an oath by divine majesty and says creation grounds the inference to knowledge and guards the Creator–creature distinction.
  50:16/q02/p2 The reminder of first creation establishes the power to recreate people. — reasons: Al-Kāshifī says Meccan polytheists admitted the first creation without matter or aid, so resurrection from matter is possible despite Satanic doubt. Islāhī links creative power and knowledge to resurrection.
50:16/q03 Who is ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ here? [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ · referent] (turns on: Whether the singular is generic or singles out Adam before generalization.)
  50:16/q03/p1 Humanity generally, especially the addressed polytheists and Adam's descendants. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Qurṭubī and Study Quran take a generic human; Ibn ʿĀshūr says polytheists are the immediate audience (50:19–22). Al-Dāmghānī and Ibn al-Jawzī compare other generic uses, including 76:2. Al-Jawzī says the word has many Qurʾanic referents.
  50:16/q03/p2 Adam specifically, with the lesson then extending to his descendants. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī report this alternative; Qurṭubī and Study Quran identify his inner prompting as eating from the forbidden tree. Būrsawī says Adam is included and Satan prompted the thought. Ibn al-Jawzī cites other Adam verses, 23:12, 55:14 and 76:1.
50:16/q04 How is the word ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ explained linguistically? [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ · meaning] (turns on: The competing etymologies appeal to familiarity, visibility, or forgetfulness and differ over morphology.)
  50:16/q04/p1 It is a singular noun for a human, with nās and anāsī as plurals. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī calls it indeclinable in his account.
  50:16/q04/p2 It derives from intimacy or familiarity with one's kind. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī reports this proposed etymology.
  50:16/q04/p3 It derives from visibility or perception. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba connects it with anastu, 'I saw' (20:10); Basran grammarians analyze it as faʿlān and explain the diminutive's added yāʾ by analogy with layla.
  50:16/q04/p4 It derives from forgetting a covenant. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās is reported to connect the name to a forgotten covenant; linguist exegetes point to the diminutive unaysiyān as restoring a dropped yāʾ.
50:16/q05 What does نَفْسُهُۥ designate? [نَفْسُهُۥ · referent]
  50:16/q05/p1 The person's heart or inward self. — reasons: Muqātil explicitly calls the whispering self the heart; al-Dāmghānī gives 'heart' as a sense of nafs, comparing 17:25. Other glosses describe the heart speaking inwardly.
50:16/q06 What does تُوَسْوِسُ mean? [تُوَسْوِسُ · meaning]
  50:16/q06/p1 It is faint, hidden, inward speech or sound, extended to thoughts. — reasons: Māwardī calls it abundant indistinct secret inner talk and cites Ruʾba. Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares faint jewelry noise and says it often concerns what is not good; Qurṭubī cites al-Aʿshā's jewelry line. Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Būrsawī, Ālūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī give the quiet-sound or jewelry sense. Ibn ʿĀshūr says metaphorical waswās and waswasa cover varied thoughts, estimates and intentions better than narrower terms.
50:16/q07 What is included in the soul's whisper here? [مَا تُوَسْوِسُ بِهِۦ نَفْسُهُۥ · interpretation] (turns on: The notes distinguish the broad range of inner activity from specific moral, historical, and inward applications.)
  50:16/q07/p1 All private inward speech, thoughts, feelings, intentions and secrets. — reasons: Ṭabarī and al-Thaʿlabī say nothing in the heart is hidden. Zajjāj stresses concealment; al-Wāḥidī compares 7:20; Bayḍāwī and Nasafī include whatever comes to mind. Elmalılı lists feelings, imaginings, memories, fancies and decisions; Study Quran includes intentions behind acts and words even unknown to oneself, citing many knowledge verses. Ibn Kathīr includes good and evil promptings; Ibn ʿĀshūr's wording covers thoughts, estimates and intentions.
  50:16/q07/p2 It particularly includes appetites, corrupt traits and beliefs that trouble the heart. — reasons: Qushayrī lists display before people, bad character and rancor; Būrsawī adds appetite and corrupt belief. Qurṭubī treats the encompassing warning as against secret sin.
  50:16/q07/p3 In the setting of denied resurrection, it includes doubt that scattered remains can be raised. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī states the skeptic's doubt about dust; al-Biqāʿī says deniers inwardly know God's power, the Qurʾan's truth and the Messenger's veracity but speak denial from envy, rivalry, pride and ambition.
  50:16/q07/p4 On the Adam reading, it includes his inclination to eat from the tree. — reasons: Qurṭubī and Study Quran report this; Būrsawī attributes Adam's thought to Satanic prompting.
  50:16/q07/p5 One inward account assigns each person exactly 70,000 thoughts daily. — reasons: Būrsawī reports gnostics comparing them to the 70,000 angels entering the Frequented House: each thought yields a fitting angel that ascends and intercedes; hearts stay inhabited.
50:16/q08 Why is whispering assigned to the self? [تُوَسْوِسُ بِهِۦ نَفْسُهُۥ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the named self is read chiefly as an epistemic focus or in light of Satanic access to inner thoughts.)
  50:16/q08/p1 The subject is the human's innermost thought, which illustrates God's encompassing knowledge. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī says Satan also whispers, but the self is named because God's knowledge of the human is at issue.
  50:16/q08/p2 Prophetic inward selves are shielded from Satan even when he approaches outward senses. — reasons: Būrsawī reports gnostics that prophets do not act on such suggestions; he distinguishes saints, who may be protected from suggestions but whose hearts remain reachable, because prophets bear revealed law.
50:16/q09 How do مَا and بِهِۦ work grammatically? [مَا بِهِۦ · grammar] (turns on: The antecedent of بِهِۦ follows whether مَا denotes the whispered thing or the act of whispering.)
  50:16/q09/p1 Mā is relative; the pronoun in bihi returns to what the self whispers. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ and al-Naḥḥās allow the return to mā; Bayḍāwī likens bāʾ to 'a sound in something.' Ālūsī attaches it to tawaswasa and calls accompaniment or redundancy weak. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes bāʾ as extra for emphasis, comparing 5:6; Ṭabāṭabāʾī takes it as instrument or cause.
  50:16/q09/p2 Mā is a verbal noun; the pronoun returns to the human. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ and al-Naḥḥās give the human alternative. Bayḍāwī says bāʾ transitivizes the verb; Būrsawī says it makes the person experience whispering and reports al-Kashshāf's idiom 'a person told himself' alongside 'his self told him.' Ālūsī cites Labīd's speaking-self line.
50:16/q10 How can a pronoun be repeated or followed by another pronoun? [بِهِۦ · grammar]
  50:16/q10/p1 A separate pronoun may confirm or stand in apposition to an attached pronoun, subject to its case and construction. — reasons: Sībawayh gives بك أنت and رأيتك أنت, distinguishes this from ordinary adjectival description, and uses إياك/إياه for accusative and أنت/هو for nominative apposition. Pronouns do not adjectivally describe overt nouns; an overt noun may take pronominal apposition as an independent restatement. He finds مررت به وبزيد هما awkward and repeats the preposition: مررت به وبزيد بهما.
50:16/q11 What does divine knowledge of the whisper establish? [وَنَعْلَمُ مَا تُوَسْوِسُ بِهِۦ نَفْسُهُۥ · interpretation] (turns on: The notes differ over whether the emphasis merely establishes unlimited divine knowledge or also contrasts it with the angels' limited record.)
  50:16/q11/p1 No inward or outward state is hidden from the Creator, even before its thought emerges. — reasons: Ṭabarī, Zajjāj, Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Baghawī and Ṭabrisī stress concealed thoughts. Al-Biqāʿī adds future thoughts before emergence; Būrsawī says God knows before creation essentially and after it actually, and creates thoughts; Ibn ʿĀshūr says knowledge of inner speech entails knowledge of what earth removes from corpses. Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts God's direct knowledge with angelic knowledge of acts; Islāhī connects it to resurrection.
  50:16/q11/p2 God knows passing thoughts which recording angels cannot perceive or write. — reasons: Elmalılı says angels do not know these hidden thoughts, although God created them; the following context confirms this distinction. He distinguishes unsettled thoughts from intention, resolve and decision.
