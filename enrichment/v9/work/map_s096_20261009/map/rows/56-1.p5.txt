[TABATABAI:56:1-4/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The conditional clause has no stated apodosis to magnify the event; its outcome is understood from the sūra’s account of people’s states, approximately that believers prosper and disbelievers lose.
[TABATABAI:56:1/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The clause about the earth's shaking is appositional to, or explanatory of, “when the Event occurs.”

[TABATABAI:56:10/r15] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · القمي | reports | Al-Qummi says the Event is the certain Resurrection and glosses “lowering” as God's enemies and “raising” as His friends.

[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p135/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | This is the seventh and final Makkan sūrah in its group, closing the Makkan portion.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p135/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The sūrah sums up the reward-and-punishment discussion from Qāf through al-Raḥmān, replacing prior detailed arguments with the certain outcome.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p135/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Earlier sūrahs grounded the case in reason, human nature, and evidence from the inner and outer worlds.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p135/r5] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Verses 1–10 assert the certain arrival of Judgment, which evaluates faith and deeds and divides humanity into three groups.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p137/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Calling the Judgment the al-wāqiʿah conveys that its occurrence is certain.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p138/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Arguments for Judgment have already been given and disbelievers’ objections answered; they cannot escape the certain event, so must prepare for a favorable outcome.

[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p138/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The threat is no false warning: the event will come, and people seeking a favorable fate should prepare for it.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:56:1/r7] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Waqi'a means an important event that occurs and whose occurrence is certain; the term names the Resurrection because its coming is beyond doubt.

[STUDYQURAN:56:1/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | With a full stop after verse 1, idhā introduces an event too terrifying to state directly.
[STUDYQURAN:56:1/r2] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Read continuously with verse 2, idhā forms an adverbial clause: when the Event occurs, none can deny its occurrence.
[STUDYQURAN:56:1/r3] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | The verse may refer back to the previous sūrah: its promised blessings for the companions of the right and the foremost arrive when the Event befalls.
[STUDYQURAN:56:1/r4] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | In either reading, the Event is the eschatological end of the world leading to the Day of Judgment.
[STUDYQURAN:56:1/r5] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | Al-wāqiʿah derives from waqaʿa, to fall, happen, or occur, expressing inevitability: what must happen will happen.
[STUDYQURAN:56:1/r7] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The Event can also allude to an individual's transformation through sudden or profound spiritual realization.

[STUDYQURAN:56:intro/r5] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Five overlapping sections cover Judgment's signs, the three groups' destinies, proofs of resurrection, the revelation, and death and the Afterlife.
