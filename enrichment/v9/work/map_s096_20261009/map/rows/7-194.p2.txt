[SAMIN-DURR:7:194#2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Al-Samīn inventories three parses for the reading: negative إن governing like ما, light operative إن governing both accusatives, or accusatives governed by an implied verb that supplies the predicate's meaning.

[SAMIN-DURR:7:194#2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Al-Samīn says making the relative pronoun accusative in that last parse is weak because governing with light إن was already described as rare.

[SAMIN-DURR:7:194/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The standard reading has إن geminated, with the relative pronoun as its subject and عباد as its predicate.

[SAMIN-DURR:7:194/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Al-Samīn says the negative particle's accusative government is established in prose and poetry, citing an unattested-by-name poetic line with مستوليا.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:194/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Critics say the negative reading contradicts the canonical affirmation that the idols are servants like people; the answer is that humans can harm and benefit whereas inert idols cannot, so the reading reinforces the canonical meaning a fortiori.
