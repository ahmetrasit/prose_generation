## THE CURRENT MAP
55:4/q01 Who is the human taught in “taught him”? [عَلَّمَهُ · referent] (turns on: Whether the pronoun resumes the human species, Adam, or Muhammad in the preceding verse.)
  55:4/q01/p1 Humankind collectively, the human species. — reasons: Zajjaj, Qushayri, Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ṭabrisī, Bursevī, and Ālūsī read the preceding الإنسان generically. Qushayri and Ṭabrisī say the capacity distinguishes the species from animals; Ṭabrisī calls this the broadest and most apparent interpretation, and Ālūsī the immediately apparent one.
  55:4/q01/p2 Adam. — reasons: Muqātil and Tustarī speak of Adam. Wāḥidī attributes this to al-Kalbī, Qatāda, and Muqātil; Baghawī and Ibn al-Jawzī to Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatāda. The ensuing instruction is variously names, languages, comprehensive explanation, worldly and afterlife knowledge, or the Greatest Name. Ṭabāṭabāʾī considers the Adam-and-names reading remote.
  55:4/q01/p3 Muhammad, the Prophet. — reasons: Zajjaj reports a Qurʾān explanation; ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās calls the gift eloquent Qurʾānic speech; Ibn Kaysān describes knowledge and disclosure of past and future events, Judgment, or explanation of revelation. Ālūsī cites 16:44. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the specialized reading remote.
55:4/q02 What is the bayān God taught? [ٱلْبَيَانَ · meaning] (turns on: Whether bayān denotes the unrestricted capacity to disclose and grasp meaning, verbal expression, a particular taught knowledge or medium, or a special lesson to Adam or Muhammad.)
  55:4/q02/p1 Articulate or eloquent speech, pronunciation, and verbal communication. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba, Ibn Zayd, Abū al-ʿĀliya, Murra, al-Ḥasan, and others give speech. Zamakhsharī, Nasafī, Qāshānī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Ālūsī stress expression of the inward and human distinction from animals; Abū Ḥayyān notes a mute person cannot receive verbally conveyed learning. Rāzī contrasts the formed body with knowledge expressed in speech. Ibn Kathīr prefers pronunciation because Qurʾānic recitation requires articulated letters; Ālūsī prefers this ordinary means of Qurʾān learning. Qāshānī speaks of expressing the Qurʾānic intellect within.
  55:4/q02/p2 The broad human faculty of making and grasping meaning, including speech, thought, and other expression. — reasons: Ṭabarī prefers unrestricted scope: religion and worldly life, law, livelihoods, and speech. Māwardī says reason apprehends what expression discloses; Qushayrī says bayān clarifies meanings. Bāqillānī lists speech, condition, gesture, and sign, with degrees of clarity; the story of Bāqil losing a gazelle while indicating eleven illustrates failed communication. Bīqāʿī includes particulars, universals, inference, action, writing, and gesture. Rāghib, via Bursevī, defines it as disclosure broader than speech. Bursevī, Ālūsī, Elmalılı, Asad, and Kuranyolu include understanding others. Asad cites Rāghib and Tāj al-ʿArūs and recalls the names in 2:31; Study Quran includes articulation, comprehension, and clear evidence. Kuranyolu extends expression to tears, laughter, feelings, art, and action.
  55:4/q02/p3 Recognition of good and evil, benefit and harm, or truth and falsehood. — reasons: Qatāda says people learn what to do or leave; al-Ḍaḥḥāk and al-Rabīʿ give the moral distinction, while Qurtubī reports benefit and harm. Ibn Kathīr reports al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Qatāda, and others; Study Quran adds truth and falsehood through revelation. Qatāda's version makes such discrimination evidence against people.
  55:4/q02/p4 The names of all things taught to Adam. — reasons: Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Qurtubī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī's Bahr al-ʿUlūm, Ālūsī, and Study Quran report this. Study Quran and Ṭabāṭabāʾī relate it to 2:31; Ṭabāṭabāʾī considers it remote.
  55:4/q02/p5 Knowledge of the lawful and unlawful and of religious obligations. — reasons: Qatāda says this includes worldly and afterlife matters and gives grounds for accountability; al-Ṭabarī includes law within the unrestricted general sense. Thaʿlabī adds good and evil and what to do or avoid. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān expressly treat the legal gloss as one part of broad bayān. Study Quran links moral and legal discrimination to revelation.
  55:4/q02/p6 All languages taught to Adam. — reasons: Thaʿlabī, Baghawī, and Bahr al-ʿUlūm give 700,000 languages with Arabic finest; Ibn al-Jawzī, Qurtubī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Ālūsī report languages without that number. Ibn al-Jawzī sets this beside Adam's names and explanation of all things.
  55:4/q02/p7 Writing or script with the pen. — reasons: Yamān is cited by Thaʿlabī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Qurtubī, and Ālūsī; Mawardī reports the view. Thaʿlabī cites 96:4, and Qurtubī 96:4–5. Ālūsī dismisses it as an alternative gloss.
  55:4/q02/p8 The Qurʾān as clarification or the Prophet's Qurʾānic explanation. — reasons: Zajjaj says it clarifies everything; Wāḥidī says the Qurʾān is bayān. ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās calls Muhammad's eloquent speech the Qurʾān. Rāzī reads it as expansion of the earlier Qurʾān teaching and cites 3:138, where it separates truth and falsehood. Study Quran also cites 3:138. Ālūsī reports Ibn Kaysān's explanation of revelation, including general or ambiguous passages and 16:44, but calls identifying bayān with the Qurʾān itself very remote. Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise calls the Muhammad-and-Qurʾān reading remote.
  55:4/q02/p9 Guidance or the paths of guidance and error. — reasons: Māwardī transmits Ibn Jurayj's simple guidance gloss; Ibn al-Jawzī calls it paths of guidance; Ālūsī specifies the path of guidance and the path of error; Abū Ḥayyān reports the guidance view among alternatives.
  55:4/q02/p10 The history of past and future events disclosed to Muhammad. — reasons: Ibn Kaysān, in Wāḥidī, Baghawī, and Ibn al-Jawzī, includes earlier and later peoples and Judgment Day; Qurtubī likewise says the Prophet told of what was and will be.
  55:4/q02/p11 The language given to each people. — reasons: Al-Suddī is reported by Thaʿlabī, Baghawī, and Qurtubī; Qushayrī gives the same explanation while treating the human as a species.
  55:4/q02/p12 What one says and what is said to one. — reasons: Muhammad ibn Kaʿb's two-way formulation is reported by Thaʿlabī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and Abū Ḥayyān; it includes receiving speech as well as speaking.
  55:4/q02/p13 The Greatest Name through which all things were taught. — reasons: Ṭabrisī attributes this to al-Ṣādiq; Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī report its attribution to Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq.
  55:4/q02/p14 Knowledge of this world and the next. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān lists this among Adam interpretations; Ālūsī attributes it to Qatāda. Qatāda's formulation in Ṭabarī likewise speaks of distinguishing worldly and afterlife matters, including law.
  55:4/q02/p15 Comprehensive explanation of all things taught to Adam. — reasons: Muqātil says Adam learned speech as explanation of everything. Ibn al-Jawzī reports explaining everything as distinct from learning all names or languages.
  55:4/q02/p16 The way to God taught to a human created without knowledge of Him. — reasons: Junayd, as transmitted by Sulamī, and the anonymous companions of thoughts, as transmitted by Māwardī, give this inward meaning.
55:4/q03 How are language and expression acquired through God's teaching? [عَلَّمَهُ ٱلْبَيَانَ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether teaching means direct bestowal of languages or creation of the nature and social conditions in which people form them.)
  55:4/q03/p1 God directly gives or institutes particular languages. — reasons: Rāzī says the verse may support divine institution of languages. Al-Suddī, as reported by Thaʿlabī, Baghawī, and Qurtubī, says God taught each people its language; the Adam traditions instead give him every language.
  55:4/q03/p2 God creates human nature for socially developed, conventional language. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī denies revelation or inspiration of ready-made languages: social need leads people through signs, sounds, and speech; human nature supports conventional links of words to meanings, and writing subsequently represents words and completes speech. He cites 30:22.
55:4/q04 How is teaching bayān related to teaching the Qurʾān? [عَلَّمَهُ ٱلْبَيَانَ · links] (turns on: Whether bayān is the medium that makes Qurʾān instruction possible, the Qurʾān itself, or the mode specified by the subsequent clauses.)
  55:4/q04/p1 Expression and understanding equip humans to learn, recite, convey, and interpret the Qurʾān. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr connects pronunciation to reciting its letters; Bīqāʿī calls the faculty the means of learning. Bursevī, Ālūsī, Elmalılı, and Ebussuud emphasize understanding others as well as speaking. Elmalılı invokes Adam's names, prophetic communication, and communities' access to scripture. Iṣlāḥī says the capacity permits mutual teaching and learning by revelation instead of punishment. Study Quran links 2–4 to learning and reciting God's Word, citing 44:58 and 54:17, 22, 32, 40.
  55:4/q04/p2 Bayān is the Qurʾān itself, so the later clause unpacks the first teaching. — reasons: Rāzī makes it an elaboration of the concise initial reference and cites 3:138; Wāḥidī calls the Qurʾān a clarification of everything. Ālūsī formulates this as a possible repetition but rejects it as very remote; Study Quran reports the Qurʾān as a name of bayān with 3:138.
  55:4/q04/p3 Human creation identifies the learner and bayān explains how teaching occurs. — reasons: Ālūsī transmits al-Irshād's account of how the clauses relate; Rāzī compares 96:4–5, where teaching with the pen leaves the recipient unnamed before teaching humanity what it did not know.
55:4/q05 How do the clauses about creating the human and teaching bayān work grammatically and rhetorically? [عَلَّمَهُ ٱلْبَيَانَ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the asyndetic clauses function as independent predicates, a confirming assertion, or an almost substitutive pairing.)
  55:4/q05/p1 They are successive predicates of al-Raḥmān without conjunction, enumerating distinct favors. — reasons: Zama
