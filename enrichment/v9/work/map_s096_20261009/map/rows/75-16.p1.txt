[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماكانتالياءوالواوفيلامات)#789/r10] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In some plurals yāʾ is preferred, as in thady and ʿaṣī; some speakers analogize these forms to ʿatū, while the yāʾ plural remains common.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماكانتالياءوالواوفيلامات)#789/r11] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A wāw preceded by kasra always turns into yāʾ, as in ghāzin and related forms; Sibawayh cites ʿAbd Yaghūth's verse as evidence for the alternation.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماكانتالياءوالواوفيلامات)#789/r12] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | After an added alif, a final weak radical is hamzated, as in qaḍāʾ and namāʾ, because it is more defective after fatḥa than after ḍamma or kasra.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماكانتالياءوالواوفيلامات)#789/r13] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In dialectal reduced forms such as ghazi and shaqi, yāʾ remains because the form was already yāʾ after an original vowelled radical; this differs from a form like raḍiyū with a still radical.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماكانتالياءوالواوفيلامات)#789/r14] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | With raḍiyū, the radical stays still like ghazi; if it were kasra-vowelled, two sukūns would meet. Sarūw likewise varies by whether its vowel is retained.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماكانتالياءوالواوفيلامات)#789/r15] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Forms from hamzated roots show yāʾ conversion for movement: jīʾ becomes jī when lightened, and quadriliteral jūyi likewise becomes jī, like muwayqin and luwayya; this is unlike ghazā.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماكانتالياءوالواوفيلامات)#789/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The imperfect vowel of a verb with final wāw or yāʾ is controlled by the following weak radical: wāw verbs use yafʿulu and yāʾ verbs use yafʿilu.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماكانتالياءوالواوفيلامات)#789/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The faʿula pattern occurs with wāw, as sarā/yasrū, but not yāʾ: Arabs avoid transferring the lighter yāʾ to the heavier wāw throughout a verb's inflection.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماكانتالياءوالواوفيلامات)#789/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A final wāw after ḍamma is weak and resists ḍamma, while fatḥa is tolerated as lighter; Sibawayh illustrates with yaghzūka and anṣaba forms.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماكانتالياءوالواوفيلامات)#789/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A final yāʾ after kasra resists genitive inflection, as final wāw after ḍamma does; accusative fatḥa remains because it is lighter.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماكانتالياءوالواوفيلامات)#789/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Final wāw and yāʾ after fatḥa become alif in forms such as ramā/yarmā and ghazā/yaghzā; the weak radical's vowel is carried by what follows.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماكانتالياءوالواوفيلامات)#789/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Before consonantal suffixes, final weak radicals retain their original wāw or yāʾ because the lām is not mobile; alif conversion occurs only when it is underlyingly vowelled.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماكانتالياءوالواوفيلامات)#789/r8] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A declensional final wāw after ḍamma changes to yāʾ and the preceding vowel becomes kasra, as in dalw/adlin, to accommodate case and suffixes; non-final wāw can remain.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماكانتالياءوالواوفيلامات)#789/r9] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | When a consonant precedes weak wāw or yāʾ, the pair behaves like sound radicals; examples include ẓaby and dalw, though speakers also analogize some forms to inflected nouns.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمالايردمنهذهالأحرفالثلاثةلتحركمابعدهاوسأخبركلم#728/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In jussive singular weak verbs such as lam yakhaf, lam yabiʿ, and lam yaqul, the weak letter stays deleted; its deletion is not reversed when the following context no longer requires it.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمالايردمنهذهالأحرفالثلاثةلتحركمابعدهاوسأخبركلم#728/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In dual jussives such as lam yakhāfā, lam yaqūlā, and lam yabīʿā, the long vowels are fixed dual markers; only the nūn is deleted for jussive mood.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابنظيرماذكرنامنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءاتوالواوات#656/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | For wāw-medial faʿl nouns, the small-count plural is usually afʿāl, avoiding the heavy ḍamma in afʿul.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابنظيرماذكرنامنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءاتوالواوات#656/r10] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The plurals arwāḥ and riyāḥ, like abār and biʾār, retain the patterns used for wāw-medial nouns rather than following the yāʾ class.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابنظيرماذكرنامنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءاتوالواوات#656/r11] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Other wāw-medial faʿl nouns take afʿāl in small counts and fiʿlān in larger counts; this distinguishes them from the yāʾ class.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابنظيرماذكرنامنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءاتوالواوات#656/r12] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | For feminine weak-medial nouns such as ʿaybah, ḍayʿah, and rawḍah, larger plurals follow sound-noun patterns; small counts can add -āt without moving the medial weak letter.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابنظيرماذكرنامنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءاتوالواوات#656/r13] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Some wāw-medial feminine nouns instead use fuʿul broken plurals, as nūbah/nuwab, jawbah/juwab, dawlah/duwal, and qaryah/qurā.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابنظيرماذكرنامنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءاتوالواوات#656/r14] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The yāʾ-medial forms ḍayʿah/ḍayʿ and khaymah/khiyam use fuʿul as a non-regular pattern, by analogy with sound nouns.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابنظيرماذكرنامنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءاتوالواوات#656/r15] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A feminine pattern with a weak second radical can form small counts with -āt without changing that radical; without -āt it may take a broken plural such as duwal or suq.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابنظيرماذكرنامنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءاتوالواوات#656/r16] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Another feminine weak pattern permits both sound plurals in -āt and broken plurals: qīmah/qiyam, rībah/ribāt and rib, and dīmah/dīm.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابنظيرماذكرنامنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءاتوالواوات#656/r17] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Feminine faʿlah nouns such as nāqah commonly form plurals on fiʿāl, though fuʿul also occurs; their small-count plurals take -āt.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابنظيرماذكرنامنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءاتوالواوات#656/r18] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Some feminine weak forms have exceptional plurals such as ʾaynūq and qiyam; the poet's tārāt/tīr witnesses a yāʾ-related broken plural.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابنظيرماذكرنامنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءاتوالواوات#656/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Larger counts of these wāw nouns commonly take fiʿāl; fiʿlān also occurs, while some forms remain limited to afʿāl.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابنظيرماذكرنامنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءاتوالواوات#656/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A few forms preserve the original afʿul pattern despite its rarity; qaws/aqwus is given, alongside the rajaz witness athwāb.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابنظيرماذكرنامنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءاتوالواوات#656/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | For yāʾ-medial faʿl nouns, afʿāl is the small-count pattern, with occasional afʿul; larger counts favor fuʿūl, lighter than the wāw pattern.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابنظيرماذكرنامنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءاتوالواوات#656/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Hollow nouns such as qāʿ, tāj, and jār take afʿāl for small counts and fiʿlān for larger counts; fiʿāl is infrequent and fuʿūl is avoided.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابنظيرماذكرنامنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءاتوالواوات#656/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · يونس | reports | Yūnus says feminine hollow faʿl nouns such as dār, sāq, and nār form small-count plurals on afʿul.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابنظيرماذكرنامنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءاتوالواوات#656/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Sībawayh questions a feminine-gender explanation for afʿul, since feminine nouns such as raḥā, qafā, qadam, and ghanam also show other patterns.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابنظيرماذكرنامنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءاتوالواوات#656/r8] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Larger-count plurals of dār and sāq include fuʿul, with variants such as dīrān and diyār; nīb for the she-camel avoids the disliked yāʾ-and-ḍamma sequence.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابنظيرماذكرنامنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءاتوالواوات#656/r9] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Nouns on fīl take afʿāl for small counts and fuʿūl for larger counts; some also use plural forms in -ah, while afʿāl may be the only attested pattern.
[SIBAWAYH:اعلمأنعلامةإضمارالمنصوبالمتكلمنىوعلامةإضمارالمجرورالمتكلم#424/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The first-person suffix is -ni with an accusative host, as in ḍarabani, and -ī with a genitive host, as in ghulāmī and maʿī.
[SIBAWAYH:اعلمأنعلامةإضمارالمنصوبالمتكلمنىوعلامةإضمارالمجرورالمتكلم#424/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In frequent particles such as inna, kaʾanna, laʿalla, and lākinna, the nūn before the first-person yāʾ can be dropped to avoid doubled consonants.
[SIBAWAYH:اعلمأنعلامةإضمارالمنصوبالمتكلمنىوعلامةإضمارالمجرورالمتكلم#424/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Laʿallī lacks the nūn because lām is close to nūn; nūn can assimilate to lām, so the frequently used form drops it.
[SIBAWAYH:اعلمأنعلامةإضمارالمنصوبالمتكلمنىوعلامةإضمارالمجرورالمتكلم#424/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The noun الضاربي accepts genitive inflection, whereas verbs use -ni to avoid a noun-like kasra on the verb's final bāʾ; kasra in اضرب الرجل is only from meeting two sukūns.
[SIBAWAYH:اعلمأنعلامةإضمارالمنصوبالمتكلمنىوعلامةإضمارالمجرورالمتكلم#424/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Under poetic necessity, Zayd al-Khayl uses ليتي, treating the suffix like a noun's -ī, since what follows is genitive in both constructions.
[SIBAWAYH:اعلمأنعلامةإضمارالمنصوبالمتكلمنىوعلامةإضمارالمجرورالمتكلم#424/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | With forms such as ʿannī, qadnī, qaṭnī, minnī, and ladunnī, the final consonant stays still; nūn preserves the familiar first-person nūn-yāʾ marker.
