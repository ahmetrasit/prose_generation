[QURTUBI:1:2/r54] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | With the definite article, Rabb is specific to God; without it, the term can apply to God or a servant, but God alone owns owners and owned things, creates and provides, and cannot lose His dominion as human owners can.
[QURTUBI:1:2/r55] QURTUBI, d. 671 · قتادة | reports | Qatāda says al-ʿālamīn is the plural of ʿālam and means every existing thing other than God, with no singular on this pattern, like rahṭ or qawm.
[QURTUBI:1:2/r56] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الحسين بن الفضل | reports | Al-Ḥusayn ibn al-Faḍl says the worlds are the people of each era, citing the address about men among the worlds; al-ʿAjjāj and Jarīr also use ʿālam for human communities. | mentions 26:165
[QURTUBI:1:2/r57] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says the worlds are jinn and humans, citing the Qur’an’s description of the Prophet as a warner to all worlds, since he was not sent as a warner to animals. | mentions 25:1
[QURTUBI:1:2/r58] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الفراء وأبو عبيدة | reports | Al-Farrāʾ and Abū ʿUbayda restrict the term to rational beings: humans, jinn, angels, and devils, excluding animals because the plural form is for rational beings; al-Aʿshā’s verse is cited.
[QURTUBI:1:2/r59] QURTUBI, d. 671 · زيد بن أسلم | reports | Zayd ibn Aslam says the worlds are the beings who receive sustenance.
[QURTUBI:1:2/r6] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Abu ʿAbd Allah explains that the person first receives the world and then the enduring word of praise, which is better because the world perishes while the word remains among the righteous deeds. | mentions 18:46
[QURTUBI:1:2/r60] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبو عمرو بن العلاء | reports | Abū ʿAmr ibn al-ʿAlāʾ says the worlds are spiritual beings; a similar Ibn ʿAbbās gloss includes every living creature moving on earth.
[QURTUBI:1:2/r61] QURTUBI, d. 671 · وهب بن منبه | reports | Wahb ibn Munabbih says God has eighteen thousand worlds, of which this world is one.
[QURTUBI:1:2/r62] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبو سعيد الخدري | reports | Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī says God has forty thousand worlds, with the inhabited world from east to west counting as one.
[QURTUBI:1:2/r63] QURTUBI, d. 671 · مقاتل | reports | Muqātil says there are eighty thousand worlds, forty thousand on land and forty thousand at sea.
[QURTUBI:1:2/r64] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الربيع بن أنس عن أبي العالية | reports | Al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas transmits from Abū al-ʿĀliya that jinn and humans are worlds and that each of earth’s four corners contains 1,500 worlds created to worship God.
[QURTUBI:1:2/r65] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | prefers | Qurtubi prefers the first view, that the worlds include every creature and existent thing, supported by Pharaoh asking about the Lord of the worlds and Moses answering that He is Lord of the heavens, earth, and all between them. | mentions 26:23, 26:24
[QURTUBI:1:2/r66] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الزجاج والخليل | reports | Al-Zajjāj derives ʿālam from knowledge or a sign: it is everything God created in this life and the next and it points to its maker and planner; al-Khalīl likewise says a sign or marker indicates a thing.
[QURTUBI:1:2/r67] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الجنيد | reports | A man praised God before al-Junayd but stopped before saying Lord of the worlds; al-Junayd urged him to complete the verse, explaining that created things vanish when paired with the Eternal.
[QURTUBI:1:2/r68] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Rabb may be read in the nominative by syntactic disconnection, meaning He is Lord of the worlds, or in the accusative as a word of praise.
[QURTUBI:1:2/r7] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | reports | Another wording says that what the servant gave was more than what he received; this frames praise as the servant’s gift and the world as God’s gift, though both ultimately come from God.
[QURTUBI:1:2/r8] QURTUBI, d. 671 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Ibn ʿUmar reports that angels could not record a servant’s praise fitting God’s majesty, so God told them to write it as the servant had said until He rewards him.
[QURTUBI:1:2/r9] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | The language note explains that aʿḍala means a matter became difficult and intractable; muʿaḍḍalāt are hardships, and the verb also describes obstructed childbirth.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:1-7/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The terms madhma and ahmada describe blame versus praise, finding something agreeable, and becoming worthy of praise; hamda can mean one who overpraises, while hamadat al-nar is its crackling.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:1-7/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو جعفر الطبري | reports | Al-Tabari and al-Mubarrad treat hamd and shukr as synonymous; al-Sulami also attributes this position to Jaʿfar al-Sadiq and Ibn ʿAtaʾ, who says praise is thanks for God teaching it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:1-7/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن عطية | rejects | Ibn ʿAtiyya says “praise to God, in thanks” restricts praise to a blessing and therefore argues against equating praise and gratitude.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:1-7/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · بعض العلماء | reports | Some say gratitude is broader because it uses tongue, limbs, and heart, whereas praise is only verbal.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:1-7/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · بعض العلماء | reports | Others say praise is broader: it includes gratitude and commendation, can stand in place of gratitude, but gratitude cannot replace praise.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:1-7/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbas calls praise the expression of every grateful person, citing Adam’s praise at a sneeze and praise by Noah, Abraham, David and Solomon, the Prophet, and Paradise’s people. | mentions 23:28, 14:39, 27:15, 17:111, 35:34, 10:10
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:2#2/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The expressions for a matter becoming difficult or blocked explain the report’s wording about the two angels; either verb form may be used without the extra yāʾ.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:2#2/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Abu Malik al-Ashʿari reports that purification is half of faith, praise fills the balance, and glorification with praise fills the space between heaven and earth.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:2#2/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · طائفة من العلماء | reports | One group prefers alhamdulillah because it combines praise with the monotheism expressed by “There is no god but God.”
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:2#2/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · طائفة من العلماء | reports | Another group prefers “There is no god but God” because it repels unbelief and shirk and is the profession for which people are fought.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:2#2/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن عطية | prefers | Ibn ʿAtiyya chooses the monotheistic formula, citing the report that it is the best utterance of the Prophet and earlier prophets.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:2#2/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Muslims agree God is praised for all blessings; since faith is a blessing and belongs among all created things, faith is God’s act and creation, against the Qadariyya.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:2#2/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Hamd means complete praise; the definite article encompasses every kind of praise, all of which God deserves for His beautiful names and exalted attributes.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:2#2/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | A poet uses hamd in a small-number plural form; hamd is the opposite of blame, tahmid is more emphatic, and Muhammad names one rich in praiseworthy qualities.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:2#3/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet admitted that he could not enumerate praise of God, illustrating the creature’s inability to fulfill God’s due praise.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:2#3/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | Other explanations say God praised Himself because servants cannot fully praise Him, or because abundant blessings exceed their capacity; divine praise removes the burden of gratitude.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:2#3/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | prefers | Hamd praises the praised one’s attributes without prior favor, while shukr praises a benefactor for a favor; thus hamd is broader and fits the verse’s scope.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:2#3/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | Hamd can mean satisfaction, as in saying one was pleased with someone or in the “praised station”; a Prophetic expression likewise means “I approve for you.” | mentions 17:79
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:2#3/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · جعفر الصادق | reports | Jaʿfar al-Sadiq offers an esoteric reading of the letters of hamd: unity, dominion, and permanence; knowing these attributes is the reality of praising God.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:2#3/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · شقيق بن إبراهيم | reports | Shafiq says gratitude requires recognizing the giver, accepting what is given, and not disobeying while one has strength.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:2#3/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | God praises Himself and opens His Book with praise, while scripture and the Prophet forbid human self-praise; human praise is compromised by motives, unlike God’s eternal self-praise. | mentions 53:32
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:2#3/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · علماؤنا | reports | The scholars say an imperfect creature should not praise himself to attract benefits or ward off harms.
[QURTUBI-FULL:1:2#4/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · القراء السبعة | reports | The seven readers and most people read alhamdu with the dāl in the nominative; Sufyan ibn ʿUyayna and Ruʾba report an accusative reading with an implied verb.
