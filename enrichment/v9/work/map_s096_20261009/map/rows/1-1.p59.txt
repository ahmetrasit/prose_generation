[ALUSI:1:1/r81] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | reports | Al-Ālūsī lists many authorities for silent basmala recitation, including Sufyān, Ibn al-Mubārak, Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn al-Zubayr, Mujāhid, and Aḥmad.
[ALUSI:1:1/r82] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Al-Ālūsī says the explicit sound evidence for audible recitation is limited; reports of occasional loud recitation can demonstrate that it is read without making it habitual.
[ALUSI:1:1/r83] ALUSI, d. 1270 · الدارقطني | reports | Al-Dāraquṭnī reportedly concluded, when pressed to identify sound evidence for audible basmala, that no report on the matter is sound.
[ALUSI:1:1/r84] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | rejects | Conflicting Anas reports should be weighed by their chains; al-Ālūsī rejects accusing Anas of lying to protect an allegation about Umayyad pressure.
[ALUSI:1:1/r85] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | rejects | Al-Rāzī’s six arguments for loud recitation fail: membership would not require identical volume, praise may be quiet, and reports and inferred commands are inconclusive.
[ALUSI:1:1/r86] ALUSI, d. 1270 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī favors the bāʾ of accompaniment: blessing and reverence are readily understood, and accompaniment suggests God’s name pervades the whole act.
[ALUSI:1:1/r87] ALUSI, d. 1270 · البيضاوي | reports | Al-Bayḍāwī’s reading makes the bāʾ instrumental or one of seeking help; al-Ālūsī ultimately prefers this interpretation.
[ALUSI:1:1/r88] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | rejects | Attachment, superiority, redundancy, and oath are also proposed for the bāʾ, but al-Ālūsī rejects these readings.
[ALUSI:1:1/r89] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | prefers | He prefers seeking divine aid because it expresses humility, denies independent human power, and anticipates “You alone we worship and You alone we ask for help.” | mentions 1:5
[ALUSI:1:1/r9] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Sūra may derive, with hamza, from the remnant of something, or without hamza from a building tier, an enclosing city wall, or elevation.
[ALUSI:1:1/r90] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | The instrumental sense also fits 96:1 as a response to the Prophet’s “I am not a reader,” and echoes the command to seek God’s help. | mentions 96:1, 7:128
[ALUSI:1:1/r91] ALUSI, d. 1270 · ابن جرير | reports | Ibn Jarīr supplies an implied “I recite,” the specific act of the person uttering the basmala; al-Ālūsī finds this broad and contextually apt.
[ALUSI:1:1/r92] ALUSI, d. 1270 · الفراء وابن عباس | reports | Al-Farrāʾ, with a view attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās, supplies an imperative addressed to people, urging them to begin in God’s name.
[ALUSI:1:1/r93] ALUSI, d. 1270 · النحويون | reports | Grammarians supply a general “I begin”; they argue it suits every basmala, but al-Ālūsī says it fails to convey that the entire recitation is aided by God’s name.
[ALUSI:1:1/r94] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | prefers | Al-Ālūsī prefers Ibn Jarīr’s specific verb because it makes the whole recitation occur with divine aid; the implied generic beginning adds little.
[ALUSI:1:1/r95] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | rejects | An implied verbal noun such as “my beginning” adds an extra omitted predicate and loses the ongoing force of a finite verb.
[ALUSI:1:1/r96] ALUSI, d. 1270 · ابن عربي | reports | Ibn ʿArabī reads the phrase’s implied predicate as the beginning and emergence of the cosmos through the divine names, an allusive reading beyond ordinary syntax.
[ALUSI:1:1/r97] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | prefers | Al-Ālūsī favors placing the implied act before the basmala: God’s name precedes it in rank, and the order suggests beginning through divine aid.
[ALUSI:1:1/r98] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | rejects | He rejects a delayed implied verb because its placement fragments the construction or makes mercy seem to cause a reading that already occurred.
[ALUSI:1:1/r99] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | The prepositional phrase is “stable” under one terminology because its governing word is omitted; definitions of stable and non-stable adverbials vary by convention.
