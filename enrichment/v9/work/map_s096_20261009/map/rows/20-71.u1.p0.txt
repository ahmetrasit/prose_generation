## THE CURRENT MAP
20:71/q01 Who speaks in this verse, and to whom? [قَالَ · referent]
  20:71/q01/p1 Pharaoh speaks to the believing magicians. — reasons: Yahyā identifies Pharaoh as speaker; Muqātil identifies the magicians as those addressed; Ṭabarī's report from Wahb places the outburst after their belief and Pharaoh's defeat.
20:71/q02 How is ءَامَنتُمْ read? [ءَامَنتُمْ · readings] (turns on: Whether an interrogative hamza is supplied, and how the second hamza is transmitted or eased; both sentence forms can express Pharaoh's rebuke.)
  20:71/q02/p1 As a declarative, without an interrogative hamza. — reasons: Ibn Mujāhid calls the unlengthened form established for Ḥafṣ and also records Ibn Kathīr and Warsh. Fārisī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya give the same three; Bayḍāwī names Qunbul and Ḥafṣ. Ibn al-Jazarī distinguishes Ruways and al-Aṣbahānī from Warsh, a solitary report through al-Khuzāʿī against other Azraq routes, and divergent reports for Qunbul. Ibn ʿĀshūr gives the later route distribution. Fārisī, Bursawī, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Ālūsī say the declarative still rebukes.
  20:71/q02/p2 As an interrogative with one hamza followed by a lengthened vowel. — reasons: Ibn Mujāhid, Fārisī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ibn al-Jawzī name Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr and Ibn ʿĀmir, while Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies Qālūn, Warsh via al-Azraq, Abū Jaʿfar and Rawḥ too. Fārisī says Abū ʿAmr avoids his usual inserted alif between hamzas because of the crowded interrogative hamza, pattern hamza and root alif. The interrogative censures the magicians as effectively as the statement.
  20:71/q02/p3 As an interrogative with two hamzas, the second lengthened or realized. — reasons: Ibn Mujāhid, Fārisī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ibn al-Jawzī name Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī and Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds Khalaf. Fārisī notes that Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī keep their regular form. Ibn al-Jazarī gives further routes for Warsh and Hishām and distinguishes Hishām's fully realized from intermediate hamza; Qirāʾāt ER notes easing the second hamza by tashīl without losing the question.
20:71/q03 Which letter is extra in the written forms of ءَامَنتُمْ? [ءَامَنتُمْ · readings] (turns on: Whether the waw itself is superfluous or represents a hamza whose eased pronunciation the extra alif signals.)
  20:71/q03/p1 The alif is extra, and the waw represents the hamza to signal easing in connected recitation. — reasons: Ibn al-Jazarī argues against al-Dānī's analysis of several spellings.
  20:71/q03/p2 The waw is extra. — reasons: This is al-Dānī's view as reported and rejected by Ibn al-Jazarī.
20:71/q04 Who does لَهُۥ refer to? [لَهُۥ · referent] (turns on: Whether the pronoun follows the immediately mentioned Moses or is understood of his Lord by the parallel wording and the substance of the magicians' faith.)
  20:71/q04/p1 Moses. — reasons: Muqātil, Yaḥyā, Naḥḥās, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Asad identify Moses; Ālūsī calls him the apparent referent. Ibn ʿĀshūr says both pronouns in the statement refer to Moses. The al-Aʿrāf parallel has bihi, whose referent Abū Ḥayyān says could be Moses or God.
  20:71/q04/p2 God, believed in through Moses or because of his signs. — reasons: Al-Baḥr al-ʿUlūm takes the pronoun as their Lord and compares 7:123. Biqāʿī describes belief in God through following or affirming Moses. Ālūsī reports both a direct divine referent and a causal lām analysis, but calls the direct referent discontinuous.
20:71/q05 What does belief with لَـ mean here? [ءَامَنتُمْ لَهُۥ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the lām conveys simple assent, allegiance or following, a typical human object of belief, or cause through Moses.)
  20:71/q05/p1 It means believing or affirming Moses; Arabic uses both āmana lahu and āmana bihi. — reasons: Wāḥidī and Abū Ḥayyān compare 10:83, 17:90 or 2:55, 12:17, 29:26 and the parallel 7:123; Qurtubī cites Lot at 29:26. Ṭabrisī distinguishes belief as the opposite of disbelief with bāʾ from assent to a person with lām. Jishumī says īmān lexically means assent, while religious usage names specified acts.
  20:71/q05/p2 The lām adds following, submission or allegiance to the person believed. — reasons: Jishumī contrasts following with mere belief using bāʾ. Bayḍāwī and Bursawī say the verb incorporates following; Ālūsī derives the force from submission, inqāda lahu, but rejects a derivation from ittabaʿa lahu because ittabaʿa takes a direct object. Ibn ʿĀshūr says lām implies faith arising for his sake, while bāʾ gives cause.
  20:71/q05/p3 The lām commonly marks belief in a human, while bāʾ commonly marks belief in God. — reasons: Wāḥidī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī's report from al-Baḥr cite Quranic examples; Kashshāf cites 9:61. Abū Ḥayyān notes that the parallel 7:123 uses bihi and its pronoun may denote Moses or God; Wāḥidī says the parallel has the same meaning.
  20:71/q05/p4 The lām is causal: they believed in God because of Moses and the signs. — reasons: Ālūsī reports this analysis; Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes faith arising for someone's sake from faith arising because of someone.
20:71/q06 What is Pharaoh's charge in قَبْلَ أَنْ ءَاذَنَ لَكُمْ? [قَبْلَ أَنْ ءَاذَنَ لَكُمْ · interpretation]
  20:71/q06/p1 They believed and acted before Pharaoh authorized them. — reasons: Muqātil and Ibn Kathīr say they affirmed Moses before any order or leave; Yaḥyā stresses that they had already done it. Qurtubī calls it a rebuke for transgression. Ṭabrisī says Pharaoh foolishly imagined belief required his authorization. Ibn ʿĀshūr says he made unauthorized belief into a punishable insolence after losing the debate; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says his claim of sovereignty made him demand that recognition of truth await his leave.
20:71/q07 Does 'before I permit you' imply he might permit belief later? [قَبْلَ أَنْ ءَاذَنَ لَكُمْ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether 'before' carries a tactical promise of later leave or only marks that no leave had been granted.)
  20:71/q07/p1 Pharaoh insinuates that later permission was possible, using hope as well as fear to restrain the crowd. — reasons: Biqāʿī says stressing 'before' keeps onlookers from following; Ibn ʿĀshūr says Pharaoh claimed he would have allowed belief to make the audience doubt the apparent miracle.
  20:71/q07/p2 No later permission is implied or expected; the point is simply that they acted without it. — reasons: Bursawī and Ālūsī deny this inference, with 18:109 cited in the discussion.
20:71/q08 What is an idhn, and how does it differ from an order? [ءَاذَنَ · meaning]
  20:71/q08/p1 Permission is notification or release to act, without necessarily willing or obliging the action; an order entails the commander's will. — reasons: Jishumī gives the original sense of notification; Ṭabrisī contrasts the permission 'hunt' at 5:2 with the order to establish prayer at 6:72; Ālūsī reports Ṭabrisī's distinction. Bursawī calls permission informing another that an act is approved; Ibn ʿĀshūr glosses adhina lahu as authorizing it.
20:71/q09 What does كَبِيرُكُمُ mean in Pharaoh's accusation? [لَكَبِيرُكُمُ · meaning] (turns on: Whether kabīr describes the instructional relation or preeminence and leadership in the craft; al-Suddī and Wāḥidī exclude mere seniority in age.)
  20:71/q09/p1 Their teacher or master in magic. — reasons: Abū ʿUbaydah reports a Meccan boy calling his employer kabīrī; Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Kisāʾī report Meccan or Hijazi usage for a teacher. Kashshāf, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī and Nasafī cite the same usage. Ṭabrisī says pupils might not match their teacher. Qurtubī says Pharaoh knew the charge false because the magicians learned before Moses arrived or was born.
  20:71/q09/p2 Their chief or leader, the greatest or most knowledgeable sorcerer, rather than their eldest. — reasons: Al-Suddī specifies knowledge rather than age; Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq says greatest magician. Wāḥidī says Arabic kabīr denotes leadership and cites the most knowledgeable leader in 12:80. Kashshāf describes rank and skill; Ṭabrisī says followers could oppose their chief but refrained out of respect. Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Bursawī and Ālūsī combine mastery with magical expertise.
20:71/q10 Why did Pharaoh say Moses taught the magicians magic? [إِنَّهُۥ لَكَبِيرُكُمُ ٱلَّذِى عَلَّمَكُمُ ٱلسِّحْرَ · interpretation]
  20:71/q10/p1 He fabricated prior training and collusion to explain away their defeat and conversion and keep the public from following them. — reasons: Wāḥidī's People of Meanings and Ṭabrisī say the chief story turns the crowd away; Rāzī calls the pupils' feigned defeat a second insinuation after his claim that instantaneous belief lacked reflection. Ibn Kathīr and Bursawī say Pharaoh knew it false; Qurtubī notes they learned magic before Moses was born or arrived. Biqāʿī, Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī stress a staged public plot; Ṭabāṭabāʾī relates the city-expulsion charge at 7:123. Iṣlāḥī calls it an excuse from a defeated ruler whose own assembled forces had converted; Ibn ʿĀshūr likens it to a losing party alleging corruption or treason.
20:71/q11 How are the threats to cut and crucify read? [فَلَأُقَطِّعَنَّ وَلَأُصَلِّبَنَّكُمْ · readings] (turns on: Whether the transmitted verb forms have simple or intensive consonants, and whether intensity stresses repetition, manner, or force.)
  20:71/q11/p1 With lighter forms of both verbs, from simple cutting and crucifying. — reasons: Kashshāf and Rāzī report an unnamed reading; Qurtubī assigns it to Ibn Muḥayṣin here and in al-Aʿrāf, with open hamza and lighter doubling; Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī also record it. Abū Ḥayyān refers readers to the earlier al-Aʿrāf discussion.
  20:71/q11/p2 With doubled, intensive forms of both verbs. — reasons: Bursawī says the first implies an oath and repeated cutting, with the same intensification in crucifying; Ālūsī says doubling intensifies action; Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the forms as manner and forceful nailing, while Asad emphasizes multiple mutilations and crucifixions; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls crucifixion intensified or multiplied.
