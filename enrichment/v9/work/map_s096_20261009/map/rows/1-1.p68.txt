[ELMALILI:1:1#8/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | On the lamiyya reading the adjectives can be construed before or after the genitive; on the bayaniyya reading the whole epitheted name is cited as one name and then explains ism.
[ELMALILI:1:1#8/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The bayaniyya analysis treats the genitive inflection as distributed over three words to stress the divine proper name, a necessary inflection rather than ordinary narration grammar.
[ELMALILI:1:1#8/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Müfessirînin çoğu | prefers | Most commentators prefer the lamiyya construction because its parsing is explicit and the meanings of the names and attributes are directly intended.
[ELMALILI:1:1#8/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Bazıları | prefers | Some prefer the bayaniyya construction, citing Labid's line using ism al-salam; the Mevlid author also reflects it with Allah adı.
[ELMALILI:1:1#8/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The omitted verb is the action the speaker is about to undertake, such as reading, writing, eating, or beginning, and the opening ba attaches the name to it.
[ELMALILI:1:1#8/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The verb and subject are omitted to let anyone connect the basmala to any intended act; ba and the speaker's situation identify the ellipsis, as in Turkish formulas like devletle selâmetle.
[ELMALILI:1:1#8/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Although Arabic normally delays a complement, placing the ba phrase first gives emphasis and exclusivity; compared with 96:1, basmala foregrounds Allah's name rather than the act of reading. | mentions 96:1
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Combining Allah with Rahman and Rahim as one name foregrounds Allah but still fails to connect the act directly with mercy.
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | rejects | Elmalılı says Abduh's deputyship reading amounts to acting as God's representative and self-effacement, appropriate only to special stations such as prophethood, sainthood, rule, and authority.
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r11] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | rejects | That meaning cannot apply generally: saying one eats in God's name is improper, though saying one eats food God bestowed would be sound; worship is for God but not done as His deputy.
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r12] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Reports that the basmala is the key to every book and every important act imply a general meaning for lawful acts; one of the commentators' attachment or seeking-aid senses supplies it.
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r13] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Abduh's explanation is sound for the Quran's revelation, recitation, and writing because of the basmala's position at their beginnings, and it matters in special settings too.
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Removing the participle still leaves Allah's proper name insufficiently foregrounded and separates it from the mercy attributes; listing three names may imply three divine essences, though singular ismiyle helps avert that implication.
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The basmala's words, composition, rhetoric, sound, and even its sweet pattern of letters cannot be fully carried into Turkish, so it should be recited in Arabic and understood through explanation.
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | rejects | He rejects corrupting the tawhid key with a rendering like esirgeciyi bağışlayıcı tanrı adıyla.
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The basmala's interpretive pivot is ba, which connects Allah's name, the first source and ultimate object of existence and progress, to the intended act.
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Human acts are not their own complete cause: will is a moment in the stream of existence, and success depends on the outpouring between the First Cause and human will, first Rahmani and finally Rahimi.
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The basmala's ba makes this universal relation conscious; its secret is said to lie in the ba's dot, and Hâkânî's poem links the basmala's continuity to the existence of things.
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · E‘âzım-ı müfessirîn | reports | The great commentators say ba here means either attachment and accompaniment or seeking aid; the former makes the basmala circumstantial, the latter an indirect object.
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Şeyh Abduh | reports | Abduh says customary in the name of means acting for an authority, by its command and on its behalf; the Muslim's basmala can express beginning an act for Allah and by His command.
[ELMALILI:1:1/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The basmala in 27:30 is plainly part of that verse and certainly Qurʾān by explicit, unanimously established transmission; surah-opening basmalas raise a separate question. | mentions 27:30
[ELMALILI:1:1/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | The Shāfiʿī argument concludes that even if the basmala is not a whole verse of al-Fātiḥa, it is part of a verse, making its recitation obligatory and audible in prayer.
[ELMALILI:1:1/r11] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Ahmed b. Hanbel | reports | Like al-Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad b. Ḥanbal has two transmitted positions, reflecting uncertainty between the two hadith reports.
[ELMALILI:1:1/r12] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İmam Mâlik | reports | Mālik holds that opening basmalas are neither part of al-Fātiḥa or other surahs nor a separate Qurʾānic portion; they are written for separation and blessing and are not recited in prayer.
[ELMALILI:1:1/r13] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İmam Mâlik | reports | Mālik bases his view on the need for Qurʾānic status to be explicit and mutawātir at every locus and on the practice of Medina’s people.
[ELMALILI:1:1/r14] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | prefers | The sound Ḥanafī position reconciles the other arguments: each opening basmala is an independent Qurʾānic verse, revealed to separate surahs and for blessing, and belongs to none of them.
[ELMALILI:1:1/r15] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Because the basmala is independent rather than part of al-Fātiḥa, reciting it in every prayer is not obligatory like al-Fātiḥa, though beginning each recitation and important undertaking with it is sunnah.
[ELMALILI:1:1/r16] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Ḥanafīs say it at the start of each prayer unit’s recitation, not in the middle, and recite it silently even in audible prayers so it will not appear part of the surah.
[ELMALILI:1:1/r17] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Outwardly the basmala has four words, but in reality and legal reckoning it has seven because bāʾ and the two definite articles count as words, while bāʾ also implies a verb and its agent.
[ELMALILI:1:1/r18] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | In the basmala’s special orthography the alif of ism is omitted and the opening bāʾ is lengthened; frequent use explains this abbreviation, while the writing also hints at the force of connection.
[ELMALILI:1:1/r19] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Certain hadith allusions read the letters bāʾ, sīn, and mīm as signs of God’s splendor, radiance, and sovereignty.
[ELMALILI:1:1/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Whether the basmala written before surahs is a verse within them, a verse-part, or an independent Qurʾānic unit is a scholarly issue tied to faith, prayer, and recitation.
[ELMALILI:1:1/r20] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The Qurʾānic wisdom concerns the relation of God to the world, especially people and their acts; the basmala’s bāʾ expresses this relation of divinity and servitude.
[ELMALILI:1:1/r21] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The basic meaning of bāʾ is attachment, with senses such as accompaniment, assistance, connection, and oath; commentators read it here as accompaniment or assistance, with an omitted verb meaning begin, read, or the like.
[ELMALILI:1:1/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Saîd b. Cübeyr, Zührî, Atâ, İbn Mübârek | reports | These early authorities hold that every surah-opening basmala is one verse of that surah, yielding 113 such verses in the Qurʾān.
[ELMALILI:1:1/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İmam Şâfiî ve ashâbı | reports | In his later position al-Shāfiʿī and his followers adopt the view that each surah-opening basmala is a verse.
[ELMALILI:1:1/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | On this view the basmala is al-Fātiḥa’s first verse, while anʿamta ʿalayhim is not a verse ending, so they recite the basmala aloud in prayer.
[ELMALILI:1:1/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Their argument is that the early community wrote the basmalas in the muṣḥafs while preserving the Qurʾān’s purity and excluding even Āmīn.
[ELMALILI:1:1/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İbn Abbas | reports | A report from Ibn ʿAbbās says that omitting the basmala omits 114 verses from God’s Book.
[ELMALILI:1:1/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Ebû Hüreyre | reports | A report from Abū Hurayra says the Prophet counted al-Fātiḥa as seven verses and named the basmala as its first.
[ELMALILI:1:1/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Ümmü Seleme | reports | A report from Umm Salama says the Prophet counted the basmala together with al-ḥamdu lillāh as one verse.
[QUTB-ZILAL:1:1/r1] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | Al-Fatiha is Meccan and has seven verses; Muslims recite it at least seventeen times daily, more with voluntary prayers, and may repeat it without limit.
