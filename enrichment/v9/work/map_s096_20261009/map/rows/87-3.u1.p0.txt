## THE CURRENT MAP
87:3/q01 How is قَدَّرَ read? [قَدَّرَ · readings] (turns on: Whether the light form merely shares the doubled form's sense or also conveys ability or ownership.)
  87:3/q01/p1 The majority double the dāl, reading qaddara. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ and al-Ṭabarī prefer this by the readers' agreement or consensus. Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows decree or measured proportion; al-Qurṭubī relates it to estimation (25:2). Al-Samīn and Rāzī compare the forms in 77:23.
  87:3/q01/p2 Al-Kisāʾī reads the dāl lightly, qadara; reports also attribute it to ʿAlī and al-Sulamī. — reasons: Al-Kisāʾī is called the sole reader among the canonical readers; al-Farrāʾ attributes al-Sulamī's form to ʿAlī. Al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Wāḥidī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr say the two forms mean the same. Jishumī allows a distinction between power and apportionment; al-Qaffāl reads the light form as ownership and governance; Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī mention power or measure; Qirāʾāt-ER lists measuring, determining, estimating, or ability; Study Quran says it may affirm God's direct power over creation.
87:3/q02 What does قَدَّرَ mean? [قَدَّرَ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the determination names the universal assignment of measure or a particular allotted feature; the light reading also permits power or ownership.)
  87:3/q02/p1 God assigned each created thing its particular measure, properties, capacities, acts, circumstances, and destiny. — reasons: Al-Rāzī cites 15:21 and lists essence, size, duration, place, beauty, guidance and misguidance. Elmalılı cites 65:3 and 15:21, stressing divine knowledge and will and the unknown secret of destiny; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes particular existence arising from an undetermined plenitude and cites 15:21, 54:49, 25:2, 57:22, and 64:11. Ibn ʿĀshūr says measure starts with length, capacity, weight, or count and here means ordered organs and powers. Al-Biqāʿī gives hand, leg, ear, and eye examples. Al-Bayḍāwī, Bursawī, and Ālūsī include kinds, species, individuals, qualities, acts, and lifespans.
  87:3/q02/p2 It means forming or shaping a creature, especially in the womb. — reasons: Al-Dāmghānī calls it shaping well in the womb; al-Māwardī describes formation there and emergence fully formed. Study Quran reports the human-specific account of forming the first human after breathing in the Spirit and providing nourishment.
  87:3/q02/p3 It means assigning a particular provision, sustenance, or useful benefit. — reasons: ʿAṭāʾ says every animal receives what suits it; al-Wāḥidī's additional report has God determine the offspring He wills. The accounts of livelihood distinguish assigned food from God's placement of useful properties in things.
  87:3/q02/p4 It means determining males and females and their reproductive course. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī says God determined male and female animal forms; Muqātil and al-Kalbī pair that with the guidance of mating. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats reproductive powers as preserving the species.
  87:3/q02/p5 It means fixing gestation, including a term of about nine months with possible variation. — reasons: Al-Suddī's report in al-Thaʿlabī and al-Qurṭubī specifies nine months, less, or more; other transmitters state only the allotted period until birth.
  87:3/q02/p6 It means decreeing felicity and misery, or sins followed by repentance. — reasons: Mujāhid and al-Wāsiṭī describe assigned destinies; Tustarī distinguishes God's care for the felicitous from leaving the wretched to themselves. Al-Sulamī separately reports allotted sins followed by repentance.
  87:3/q02/p7 It means creating people for recompense. — reasons: Al-Māwardī's fifth possibility pairs this creation with guidance to action.
  87:3/q02/p8 Other lexical senses include making or appointing, knowing, and the light form's power or ownership. — reasons: Al-Dāmghānī lists making or appointing and knowledge without examples in the excerpt. Al-Qaffāl derives the light reading as owning and governing what God creates; al-Qurṭubī also allows possession, while Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī mention power. Qirāʾāt-ER gives measuring, determining, estimating, and ability.
87:3/q03 What is God said to guide creatures toward? [فَهَدَىٰ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the phrase is unrestricted or names a particular kind of guidance, and whether religious guidance is read as its principal sense.)
  87:3/q03/p1 Every creature is guided toward its allotted function, course, or benefit; particular examples do not limit the verse. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, Ālūsī, and Elmalılı expressly keep the scope general. Al-Ṭabarī cites lack of evidence for restriction; Elmalılı cites 20:50. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī, and Bursawī name inclination, inspiration, signs, and revelation. Ibn ʿĀshūr gives speech, craft, grazing, nursing, and bee architecture; al-Biqāʿī says ordainment needs guidance for completeness. Elmalılı includes planetary orbits, attraction, gases, stones, crystals, salt, coal, and cells. Qutb says provision accompanies creatures throughout life; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 15:21, 80:20, and 2:148.
  87:3/q03/p2 People are shown good and evil, right and error, felicity and misery; livestock are guided to pasture. — reasons: Mujāhid's versions vary between assigned misery or happiness and showing moral alternatives. Al-Rāzī connects the latter to perception and the ability to seek benefit and avoid harm, citing 76:3 and 91:7–8. Al-Zajjāj says the person may prove grateful or ungrateful.
  87:3/q03/p3 God directs each person along the felicity or misery already allotted to that person. — reasons: Tustarī says God takes charge of the felicitous and leaves the wretched to themselves; al-Wāsiṭī says each group follows its assigned path. These reports describe an outcome, while other Mujāhid reports describe moral alternatives.
  87:3/q03/p4 God directs males and females, especially animals, in how to mate. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī's preferred explanation says each male finds a female of its own species and learns the species-specific manner of mating; ʿAṭāʾ and al-Qurṭubī compare 20:50. Ibn Qutayba calls the direction inspiration. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats reproductive powers as preserving species.
  87:3/q03/p5 The fetus is guided out of the womb at the completion of its allotted term. — reasons: Al-Suddī specifies approximately nine months, sometimes shorter or longer. Al-Māwardī also says the child emerges fully formed.
  87:3/q03/p6 Creatures are guided to seek their apportioned livelihood, and animals to their food or pasture. — reasons: ʿAṭāʾ and Abū Muslim say every animal receives a fitting provision; al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī add that God teaches its use. Al-Rāzī says God cares for them. Al-Qummī says an initial measure precedes God's guiding whom He wills toward things.
  87:3/q03/p7 People are taught to discover and use the beneficial properties God placed in things. — reasons: Al-Ṭabrisī gives food, medicine, and poison as examples; al-Qurṭubī includes extraction of useful properties.
  87:3/q03/p8 A newborn is guided to its mother's breast and to suckling. — reasons: Tustarī attributes infant sustenance to divine care and distinguishes explanatory guidance from God's taking charge, citing 76:3. Al-Biqāʿī notes the newborn's instinctive opening of its mouth; Iṣlāḥī pairs implanted need with means and capacity.
  87:3/q03/p9 Humans are led to religion, faith, monotheism, and right conduct through reason, signs, revelation, or prophetic invitation. — reasons: Abū ʿAlī connects forms and livelihood with knowledge of unity; Jishumī includes lifespans, signs, and completed reason. Al-Rāzī explicitly prefers religious guidance after physical formation and faculties, arguing that prior clauses already cover worldly constitution; he cites 42:52 for invitation. Qatāda distinguishes God's command and approval of obedience from sin. Iṣlāḥī joins moral awareness and prophetic guidance to trials and consequences.
  87:3/q03/p10 God guides sinners to repentance after allotting their sins. — reasons: Al-Sulamī reports this formulation; al-Thaʿlabī and Ibn al-Jawzī transmit it among other views.
  87:3/q03/p11 Some are guided to trust God's guarantee quietly instead of striving for their apportioned provisions. — reasons: Al-Sulamī contrasts this with the reported view that people are guided to seek their provisions.
  87:3/q03/p12 People are created for recompense and guided to action. — reasons: This is al-Māwardī's fifth possibility.
  87:3/q03/p13 People are guided to perceive and use their endowed faculties and capacities. — reasons: Al-Rāzī describes bodily components fitted to receive faculties and guidance creating the faculties in organs for their beneficial acts. Ibn ʿĀshūr gives a human-specific possibility: completed faculties followed by perception and reason. Study Quran reports a reading of the preceding 'created' as humans specifically, humans and animals, or all creation.
  87:3/q03/p14 God's acts themselves direct rational observers to recognize His oneness, knowledge, power, and majesty. — reasons: Al-Rāzī lists oneness, majesty, self-sufficiency, and uniqueness; al-Qurṭubī lists oneness, knowledge, and power.
  87:3/q03/p15 Guidance includes explaining the way and God's protective care. — reasons: Tustarī differentiates explanation from God's taking charge and uses infant sustenance as the case of care; al-Naḥḥās reports the gloss that God makes matters clear.
