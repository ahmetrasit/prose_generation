## THE CURRENT MAP
75:19/q01 What does “then” express here? [ثُمَّ · grammar] (turns on: Whether ثم denotes temporal succession, rhetorical rank, simple coordination, or the timing of the divine statement; Ibn ʿĀshūr disputes an inference from sequence to necessary delay.)
  75:19/q01/p1 It marks sequence with an interval: explanation follows reception, collection, and recitation. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī illustrates ordinary delayed succession with ʿAmr arriving after Zayd. Al-Biqāʿī ties the interval to the gravity of explanation and the time of need. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says explanation follows gathering and recitation; al-Bursawī says action may not be demanded before its needed clarification.
  75:19/q01/p2 It marks a higher rank of explanation over the guarantee of collecting and reciting, rather than an obligatory time lapse. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says ثم coordinates the whole clause, so it cannot prove that clarification always comes later. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also calls the ordering one of rank, even while saying explanation follows gathering and recitation.
  75:19/q01/p3 It can be equivalent to wāw, simply joining the clauses. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī reports this application; al-Zarkashī reports it for comparable examples but maintains that ثم retains its original sense.
  75:19/q01/p4 It introduces God's subsequent statement to Muhammad that explanation is His responsibility. — reasons: Al-Qaffāl compares a construction in al-Balad 90:13 and 90:17; al-Rāzī says this abandons the apparent meaning without evidence.
75:19/q02 What does “upon Us” convey? [عَلَيْنَا · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the fronted expression merely promises divine responsibility or also restricts the source of clarification.)
  75:19/q02/p1 God undertakes and guarantees the explanation. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says علينا has this force in both clauses; al-Biqāʿī extends the guarantee to wording, arrangement, and meaning, regardless of Gabriel's bell-like or ordinary delivery. Ibn Kathīr describes preservation, recitation, and explanation as three stages; Islahi includes collection and arrangement.
  75:19/q02/p2 Fronting “upon Us” restricts clarification of difficult passages to God. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares the preceding verse's same fronting.
75:19/q03 Why is the divine undertaking plural? [عَلَيْنَا · rhetoric]
  75:19/q03/p1 The plural honors God and includes Gabriel's reciting and clarifying at God's command. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī says God directs and the angel transmits.
75:19/q04 What is the lexical sense of bayān? [بَيَانَهُۥ · meaning]
  75:19/q04/p1 It is making a meaning apparent and distinct to the mind, the opposite of concealment or obscurity. — reasons: Al-Jishumī emphasizes distinction from other meanings; al-Ṭabrisī contrasts bayān with hiding and obscuring.
75:19/q05 What does the pronoun in “explain it” refer to? [بَيَانَهُۥ · referent] (turns on: Who is addressed in 75:16–19 and whether the intervening passage concerns Gabriel's revelation, human conscience, a person's record, or the Judgment.)
  75:19/q05/p1 It refers to the Qur'an or its recitation being revealed to Muhammad. — reasons: Elmalılı says the prophetic addressee and later mention of Qur'an settle the unexpressed antecedent; 97:1 also has an implied antecedent. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls قرآن a verbal noun for recitation, like غفران and فرقان, citing Ḥassān's elegy for ʿUthmān; Elmalılı allows either the verbal noun or the recited text. One report narrows the referent to what Gabriel had already recited.
  75:19/q05/p2 It can refer to a human being's inward knowledge or conscience. — reasons: Elmalılı notes that the antecedent is not explicit and makes this possible if the address is universal rather than specifically prophetic.
  75:19/q05/p3 It refers to the person's record and deeds at resurrection. — reasons: Al-Qaffāl links the address to 75:13–15 and 17:14; al-Bayḍāwī says the recipient hurries and stumbles from fear. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the evidence is gathered and demanded for acknowledgment. Al-Rāzī objects that this leaves the apparent prophetic sense without evidence; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites the resemblance to 20:114 against it.
  75:19/q05/p4 It refers to the Judgment Day whose occurrence and description are in question. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the view that the command forbids haste in asking when it comes; he calls it untenable.
75:19/q06 What does God promise by “explain it”? [بَيَانَهُۥ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether bayān means legal exposition, semantic clarification, clear wording and recitation, the Qur'an's own revelation, or the fulfillment of judgment and reward.)
  75:19/q06/p1 He will clarify the Qur'an's legal limits, rulings, lawful and forbidden matters. — reasons: Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, Qatāda, and others give the legal sense. Qatāda includes obedience, disobedience, and avoidance of the forbidden; Abū Ḥayyān adds general and specific wording. Al-Durr's Ibn ʿAbbās connects this with the command to follow the recitation.
  75:19/q06/p2 He will make the Qur'an clear on the Prophet's tongue or have him recite it to people. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās is reported with both formulations; al-Māwardī adds Gabriel's teaching and the Prophet's subsequent recital. Ibn al-Jawzī says he listened, then recited after Gabriel left. The Ṣaḥīḥ reports cited by al-Ālūsī support this; al-Ālūsī favors recitation of the whole Qur'an because only some of it is ambiguous. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the wording becomes clear to hearers without a written copy. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describe preservation and unchanged recital to people.
  75:19/q06/p3 He will clarify unclear or ambiguous Qur'anic meanings and their intended rulings after preservation or recitation. — reasons: Al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī say the Prophet hurried to ask about difficult meanings. Al-Jishumī includes clear and ambiguous passages; al-Bursawī specifies the intended sense of general and ambiguous wording. Ibn Kathīr and Study Quran say God inspires the Prophet with intended, legislated meaning. Al-Ṭabrisī's Qatāda places this after memorization; al-Ālūsī reports it but objects that bayān need not mean resolving ambiguity.
  75:19/q06/p4 He will send it down as a clear Arabic Qur'an that itself contains clarification for people. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj adds that it has no crookedness; al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, and Ibn al-Jawzī report the revelation and its public clarity.
  75:19/q06/p5 He will fulfill the Qur'an's promises and threats through recompense in the afterlife. — reasons: Al-Ḥasan's version specifies repayment on Resurrection; al-Qurṭubī adds clarification of promises and threats as well as bringing them about.
  75:19/q06/p6 He will remind the Prophet of the Qur'an. — reasons: Al-Jishumī reports this from Ibn ʿAbbās.
  75:19/q06/p7 He will manifest separate Qur'anic meanings in the heart and self at the station of detail. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī gives this inward reading.
  75:19/q06/p8 He will explain a resurrected person's record and the punishment his deeds warrant. — reasons: Al-Qaffāl has God read and gather deeds, require confession, and then explain punishment, calling it a strong warning while acknowledging no supporting report. Al-Bayḍāwī describes requital after the person's fearful reading; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says explanation follows if the person denies the evidence.
  75:19/q06/p9 He will clarify the Judgment through the trumpet blast. — reasons: In the reported reading, God first gathers those destined for Judgment and reveals its description; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the reading.
  75:19/q06/p10 He will explain the Qur'an's words, arrangement, and meanings through the Prophet and later scholars. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says Gabriel may deliver it as a bell-like sound or ordinary speech. Al-Zarkashī cites 16:44 for the Prophet and community scholars, while distinguishing sound explanation of intended meaning from apparent ambiguity to limited readers.
75:19/q07 Who conveys or supplies the promised explanation? [عَلَيْنَا بَيَانَهُۥ · referent] (turns on: Whether the verse foregrounds direct divine clarification to Muhammad, explanation entrusted to the Prophet and scholars, or clarification supplied within the Qur'an.)
  75:19/q07/p1 God supplies it to the Prophet, who communicates it through his tongue. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās reports Gabriel's recitation followed by the Prophet's; al-Biqāʿī says it then reaches others by Muhammad. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it is preserved in his heart and audible without a written copy. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says Gabriel transmits under God's direction.
  75:19/q07/p2 The Prophet is charged with explaining it, with scholars of his community also bearing its clarification. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān reports the Prophet's responsibility; al-Zarkashī cites 16:44 and says the most reliable explanation discloses true intended meaning. Al-Biqāʿī includes the community's scholars.
  75:19/q07/p3 Explanatory Qur'anic verses themselves supply its clarification. — reasons: Elmalılı says Qur'anic verses explain one another; Islahi identifies later explanatory verses; Muhammad ʿAbduh says proper reading makes the Qur'an its own best commentary.
75:19/q08 When may clarification follow the original address? [ثُمَّ بَيَانَهُۥ · law] (turns on: Whether ثم establishes a time gap, whether bayān specifically means clarification of an ambiguous address, and whether obligation or explanation itself is postponed.)
