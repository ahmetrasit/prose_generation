## THE CURRENT MAP
92:12/q01 Why is the assertion emphatic? [إِنَّ لَلْهُدَىٰ · rhetoric] (turns on: Which objection the emphatic wording is understood to answer.)
  92:12/q01/p1 The emphatic particles answer the objection that God would have compelled everyone to believe if He wished. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says the imagined objection excuses idolatry and cites 43:20.
  92:12/q01/p2 The emphasis counters people's rejection of God's guidance and their claim to define guidance themselves. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī cites the rarity of belief in 12:103 and the misleading majority in 6:116; the emphasis strengthens both the exclusive source and the path's destination.
92:12/q02 How does the lām in la-l-hudā work and why is its alif absent? [لَلْهُدَىٰ · grammar]
  92:12/q02/p1 It is an emphatic lām attached to al-hudā; its alif is omitted to distinguish it from the negative lā. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās gives both the syntactic attachment and the orthographic reason.
92:12/q03 What does 'upon Us' imply about God's obligation to guide? [عَلَيْنَا · theology] (turns on: Whether ʿalā describes God's gracious self-commitment, a requirement of wisdom, or a duty owed to servants.)
  92:12/q03/p1 God undertakes guidance by His own grace and wisdom before holding people accountable; no creature has an entitlement over Him. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī distinguishes God's gracious undertaking from sovereign power and denies a right over Him. Ibn ʿĀshūr says ʿalā with a verbal noun signifies obligation, fulfilled by innate discernment and messengers before reckoning. Al-Ālūsī invokes wise decree and rejects an imposed duty; Quṭb calls the undertaking gracious.
  92:12/q03/p2 Divine wisdom or justice requires God to establish the proofs and make religious duties clear. — reasons: Al-Jishumī says otherwise God would be misleading people in religion. Al-Ṭabrisī says the possibility of divine misguidance would undermine the duty. Al-Rāzī relates this to creation for worship in 51:56 and to God's purpose of benefit, mercy, and lasting bliss. Al-Bayḍāwī offers decree or wisdom as the ground.
  92:12/q03/p3 The wording establishes a duty owed by God to servants, including making feasible commands and meaningful proofs. — reasons: Al-Rāzī reports the Muʿtazila's inference from ʿalā: God excuses servants, commands only within capacity, does not impose the impossible, and proofs would be pointless unless servants originated their acts independently. Al-Rāzī says his school's replies are known but does not spell them out.
92:12/q04 Why is the predicate placed before 'guidance'? [عَلَيْنَا لَلْهُدَىٰ · rhetoric]
  92:12/q04/p1 Fronting 'upon Us' restricts true guidance to God. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī says guidance from another source is excluded and fails to reach Him. Reversing the order to 'inna al-hudā la-ʿalaynā' would retain the general sense or stress the destination, but would lose this restriction; the emphatic particles reinforce it.
92:12/q05 What guidance or promise does God undertake here? [عَلَيْنَا لَلْهُدَىٰ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether hudā here names clarification of a way, the way's destination, God's acts concerning guidance and error, reward, or exclusive divine agency.)
  92:12/q05/p1 God makes the right way clear through proofs, law, messengers, revelation, and discernment. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī and al-Thaʿlabī specify truth versus falsehood and obedience versus sin. Qatāda specifies lawful versus forbidden; al-Zajjāj and Yaḥyā b. Sallām distinguish guidance from error. Muqātil glosses hudā as clarification. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī cite proofs and revealed laws; al-Rāzī grounds clarification in creation for worship (51:56). Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān add perception, citing 16:9. Al-Qāshānī lists intellect, senses, reasoned and transmitted evidence. Al-Biqāʿī stresses manifest proofs. Elmalılı adds books, messengers, worldly signs, and inspiration; Quṭb says these leave no excuse. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds inborn moral discernment before prophetic reminders. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 16:9, 33:4, 76:3 for a shown path one may follow or leave, and 42:52 and 12:108 for prophetic direction. The Study Quran calls 16:9 a parallel, and Corpus Coranicum directs readers to its note on 93:7 for hadā and hudā.
  92:12/q05/p2 Whoever follows the guided path travels toward God, His reward, and His Garden. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ and many transmitters compare 16:9. Al-Wāḥidī explicitly names God's reward and Garden; Ibn Kathīr and the Study Quran say the Hereafter. Al-Sāmarrāʾī also cites 11:56 and 73:19. Al-Ālūsī reports this gloss but finds its syntactic attachment unlikely. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls worship a straight path to God, citing 51:56, 3:51, 42:52–53.
  92:12/q05/p3 The wording encompasses both God's guidance and His misguidance, although only guidance is named. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ compares 16:81, where clothing protects from heat and by implication cold. Other transmitters also cite 3:26 for good in God's hand, 5:64 for God's bounty, and 36:83 for universal dominion. Ibn ʿAbbās through ʿAṭāʾ says God guides friends to obedience and prevents enemies from obeying. The Muʿtazila object that 16:9 assigns the straight road to God but not the crooked road.
  92:12/q05/p4 God undertakes to reward the guidance He has given. — reasons: Al-Māwardī proposes this further possibility; al-Qurṭubī specifies reward of the person God guided.
  92:12/q05/p5 The guidance is God's act of bringing whom He wills to the end or enabling obedience. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās through ʿAṭāʾ says God guides friends and bars enemies. The Imam al-Riḍā says God guides and misguides whom He wills while rejecting a claim that knowledge is acquired by reasoning, appealing to Banū Hāshim's closeness and right. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ distinguishes real creative guidance from legislative guidance, citing 20:50 and 76:3; he reserves guidance that brings one to the end for God as Lord. Ibn ʿAṭiyya argues that if this verse promised guidance into faith to everyone, there would be no unbelievers.
  92:12/q05/p6 Guidance belongs to God alone as its source or charge. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī compares 28:56, which denies the Prophet power to guide whomever he loves, but objects that an implied 'entrusted' verb strains the syntax. Elmalılı says people cannot create guidance themselves, though they choose whether to accept it. Al-Sāmarrāʾī derives exclusivity from predicate fronting; the Study Quran parallels 16:9. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ also allows prophetic guidance toward the path in 42:52 and 12:108.
  92:12/q05/p7 Hudā is guidance to the straight path, conventionally contrasted with error and disbelief. — reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ gives this lexical usage, alongside the religious sense of al-ākhira and al-ūlā as afterlife and this world.
92:12/q06 How do divine guidance and human choice relate? [لَلْهُدَىٰ · theology] (turns on: Whether guidance only discloses a choice, produces obedience, or brings a person to the end; and how human inclination is understood.)
  92:12/q06/p1 God shows the path and enables discernment; people choose whether to accept and follow it. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya says everyone receives perception, then acquires what is allotted; guidance into faith for all would leave no unbelievers. Al-Ṭabrisī leaves response to people. Elmalılı entrusts acceptance to human will; Quṭb says the signs leave no excuse. Ibn ʿĀshūr says each inclination leads to facilitation of its corresponding path, and al-Iṣlāḥī says acceptance benefits people themselves. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says the Qur'an guides all yet heals believers and increases wrongdoers' loss (17:82); his path may be followed or left (76:3).
  92:12/q06/p2 God guides His friends into obedience and prevents His enemies from obeying. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās through ʿAṭāʾ supplies this as an explanation of how the omitted opposite, misguidance, is present.
  92:12/q06/p3 God brings people to the end, while His misguidance is only retributive withholding of mercy. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ distinguishes this from initial misguidance and calls it God's prerogative as Lord. His creative and legislative categories draw on 20:50 and 76:3; the Imam's report says God guides and misguides whom He wills.
  92:12/q06/p4 God cannot lead people astray from religion because He is bound to establish clarifying proof. — reasons: Al-Jishumī explicitly draws this implication; al-Ṭabrisī says divine misguidance would undermine the duty of clarification. The Muʿtazila cite 16:9 to reject assigning crooked paths to God.
  92:12/q06/p5 Human inclination is acquired action in al-Ashʿarī's account, servant capacity in the Muʿtazilite account, and compulsion for determinists. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr lists these three names for the inclination underlying choice without deciding among them in that note.
92:12/q07 How does this verse fit the surrounding argument and the sūra? [* · links] (turns on: Which surrounding unit and function the interpreter foregrounds: moral choice, stanza structure, wealth, divine dominion, or the ensuing warning.)
  92:12/q07/p1 It resumes the contrast in human striving by showing that God has clarified the way before people face the consequences of their choices. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says God adds guidance, encouragement and warning after the different outcomes. Al-Ālūsī says this resumes and confirms the preceding argument. Ibn ʿĀshūr relates it to 92:5–10 and responsibility for wrongdoing. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says diverse striving would converge if people sought God's guidance, and the next righteous description exemplifies it. Al-Iṣlāḥī connects 92:11–14 with revelation to Quraysh, their free acceptance, and God's power over both worlds.
  92:12/q07/p2 The divine self-predication is the hinge between the sūra's contrasting sections and supports its promises and threats. — reasons: Corpus Coranicum describes two antitheses of behavior and recompense, first in 92:5–13 and then in 92:14–21. It says God's statement separates the second and third stanzas and affirms power
