20:11/q20 How is the marvel before Moses' commission classified? [نُودِىَ · theology] (turns on: Whether a marvel may precede Moses' prophetic commission as his own precursor miracle.)
  20:11/q20/p1 It is an irhāṣ, a precursor miracle. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī classifies the pre-prophetic marvel this way and says Muʿtazilites reject it.
  20:11/q20/p2 A miracle cannot occur before Moses' commission unless it belongs to another prophet. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports this Muʿtazilite objection.
20:11/q21 How is annī or innī in the following divine speech read and construed? [نُودِىَ يَٰمُوسَىٰٓ · readings] (turns on: Whether the hamza has fatḥa and attaches the following clause to the call, or kasra and starts quoted speech; reports differ on the named readers and nuances of attachment.)
  20:11/q21/p1 With fatḥa, annī: he was called with or because of the words 'I am your Lord.' — reasons: Al-Zajjāj and al-Naḥḥās take an to be accusative; Abū ʿAlī in al-Ṭabrisī says calling can take a preposition. Al-Jishumī glosses 'because I am your Lord.' Named readers vary: al-Naḥḥās includes al-Ḥasan, Abū Jaʿfar, Abū ʿAmr; al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī list Abū Jaʿfar, Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr; al-Kashshāf lists Abū ʿAmr and Ibn Kathīr. Abū Ḥayyān gives Ibn Kathīr and Abū ʿAmr's wa-annī.
  20:11/q21/p2 With kasra, innī begins direct quoted speech: 'O Moses, I am your Lord.' — reasons: Al-Zajjāj, al-Jishumī, al-Kashshāf, Abū ʿAlī in al-Ṭabrisī, and Abū Ḥayyān describe a fresh quotation or supplied saying verb; Abū Ḥayyān calls it the majority reading. Al-Farrāʾ allows the call to include annī and Mūsā. Al-Naḥḥās reports an interpretation in which Moses says 'I am.'
20:11/q22 Is the final yāʾ of innī opened or left still? [نُودِىَ يَٰمُوسَىٰٓ · readings] (turns on: The reported recitation of the final yāʾ in the following innī.)
  20:11/q22/p1 Abū Jaʿfar, Nāfiʿ, Ibn Kathīr, and Abū ʿAmr open the final yāʾ. — reasons: Al-Jishumī gives this reader list.
  20:11/q22/p2 The remaining readers leave the final yāʾ quiescent. — reasons: Al-Jishumī contrasts them with the four who open it.
20:11/q23 How does this call connect with other accounts of Moses' call? [* · links]
  20:11/q23/p1 It is the call from the blessed spot at the valley's right side and from the tree. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr compares 28:30. Al-Qurṭubī also refers to Sūrat al-Qaṣaṣ for the direction of the call. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 19:52, 28:30, and 20:13 to say an intermediary or veil can accompany divine revelation.
20:11/q24 What inward significance is given to Moses' encounter and call? [* · inward]
  20:11/q24/p1 The divine call enveloped Moses and overwhelmed his selfhood; God alone bears and answers His own address. — reasons: Jaʿfar in al-Sulamī says every hair perceived the call from every direction, awe and majesty confirmed it, and God's self-identification left Moses stunned and annihilated as an intermediary locus.
  20:11/q24/p2 The fire is a veil of majesty through which God's manifestation addresses Moses. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī says Moses connects with the fire and is called beyond fiery curtains; 'I am your Lord' is the divine manifestation in fiery form.
  20:11/q24/p3 Fire figures ardent love and its light dissolves Moses' selfhood into single-hearted orientation. — reasons: Al-Būrsawī distinguishes light as love from fire as longing: their fullness lets essential light appear as attribute-fire and burn away selfhood.
20:11/q25 What do qabas and miqbās mean in the preceding request for fire? [* · meaning]
  20:11/q25/p1 Qabas is a flame; miqbās is a firebrand or tool for taking fire. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī supplies these lexical meanings in the discussion attached to the call.
20:11/q26 Can verbs from qabas describe taking both fire and knowledge? [* · meaning] (turns on: Whether the two verbal forms keep fire and knowledge distinct or both extend to either.)
  20:11/q26/p1 The uses are distinguished: qabastu is for fire, while aqbastu someone knowledge is for seeking knowledge for that person. — reasons: Al-Yazīdī as reported by al-Qurṭubī draws this distinction. Al-Qurṭubī also says qabistu and iqtibās can mean taking fire, while iqtibās can mean acquiring knowledge.
  20:11/q26/p2 Both aqbastuhu and qabastuhu can apply to fire or knowledge. — reasons: Al-Kisāʾī as reported by al-Qurṭubī permits both verbs for either object. Al-Qurṭubī also notes iqtibās can refer to fire-taking or knowledge acquisition.
20:11/q27 What does hudā mean in the preceding search? [* · meaning]
  20:11/q27/p1 It means hādiyan, a guide. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī supplies the gloss.
20:11/q28 What view of divine istiwāʾ is noted in the preceding discussion? [* · theology] (turns on: Whether istiwāʾ is understood as power or as a distinct act or attribute; al-Jishumī says the latter is unintelligible.)
  20:11/q28/p1 It means power. — reasons: Al-Jishumī prefers Abū ʿAlī's view as sounder and finds the Kullābī account unintelligible.
  20:11/q28/p2 It is an act or attribute called istiwāʾ. — reasons: Al-Jishumī attributes this to the Kullābīs and calls it unintelligible.
