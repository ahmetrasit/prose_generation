[THALABI:v1p96/r1] THALABI, d. 427 · قوم | reports | Some say the name came from Syriac lāhā, whose final long vowel was removed when the word entered Arabic.
[THALABI:v1p96/r2] THALABI, d. 427 · النضر بن إسماعيل | reports | Al-Naḍr derives Allah from taʾalluh, worship and devotion; Ibn ʿAbbas’s reading of “your worship” is offered as evidence. | mentions 7:127
[THALABI:v1p96/r3] THALABI, d. 427 · ابن عباس والضحاك | reports | Ibn ʿAbbas and al-Daḥḥāk derive ilāh from seeking refuge and relying on someone: creatures turn to God in crises, and He protects them.
[THALABI:v1p96/r4] THALABI, d. 427 · أبو عمرو بن العلاء | reports | Abu ʿAmr derives the name from bewilderment: minds are perplexed by God’s essence, greatness, and unknowable nature.
[THALABI:v1p96/r5] THALABI, d. 427 · المبرد | reports | Al-Mubarrad derives it from finding repose with someone; creatures become calm through remembrance of God. | mentions 13:28
[THALABI:v1p96/r6] THALABI, d. 427 · علي بن عبد الرحيم القناد | reports | Al-Qannād derives it from walāh, loss of reason at losing a beloved; the author says hearts long for God in love and tremble at His remembrance.
[THALABI:v1p98/r1] THALABI, d. 427 · علي بن عبد الرحيم القناد | reports | The Kummayt verse illustrates hearts becoming lovesick for God and yearning at His remembrance.
[THALABI:v1p98/r10] THALABI, d. 427 · author | holds | The source notes a heavy pronunciation of the lām in Allah, with the tongue pressed to the palate in reverence for the name.
[THALABI:v1p98/r11] THALABI, d. 427 · قوم | reports | Some say al-Raḥmān and al-Raḥīm are synonymous names for the merciful; mercy is either God’s will of good, an essential attribute, or withholding deserved punishment and doing good, an act-attribute.
[THALABI:v1p98/r12] THALABI, d. 427 · قوم | reports | Those who distinguish the names say al-Raḥmān’s intensive pattern denotes one filled with mercy.
[THALABI:v1p98/r2] THALABI, d. 427 · قوم | reports | Some derive ilāh from concealment: God is manifest through signs of lordship yet hidden from minds in His modality.
[THALABI:v1p98/r3] THALABI, d. 427 · قوم | reports | Another derivation is from elevation, since lāh means to rise.
[THALABI:v1p98/r4] THALABI, d. 427 · قوم | reports | One proposed derivation links the name to alāhat, illustrated by a line about delaying the goddess’s return.
[THALABI:v1p98/r5] THALABI, d. 427 · قوم | reports | A derivation from dwelling in a place gives the sense of the eternally abiding and enduring.
[THALABI:v1p98/r6] THALABI, d. 427 · قوم | reports | Another gloss describes God’s essence as His power to subdue.
[THALABI:v1p98/r7] THALABI, d. 427 · الحارث بن أسد المجلسي | reports | Al-Ḥārith says Allah comes from “made them needy”: servants need their Creator for benefits and harms like distressed, overpowered people.
[THALABI:v1p98/r8] THALABI, d. 427 · شهر بن حوشب | reports | Shahr ibn Ḥawshab glosses Allah as the Creator of everything.
[THALABI:v1p98/r9] THALABI, d. 427 · أبو بكر الوراق | reports | Abu Bakr al-Warrāq gives the terse gloss “He is.”
[THALABI:v1p99/r1] THALABI, d. 427 · قوم | reports | One view makes al-Raḥmān merciful to all creation through creation and provision, while al-Raḥīm specially guides believers in this life and grants them Paradise and vision in the next. | mentions 7:156, 33:43
[THALABI:v1p99/r2] THALABI, d. 427 · جعفر بن محمد الصادق | reports | Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq summarizes the distinction as al-Raḥmān being a specific name with general scope and al-Raḥīm a general name with specific scope.
[THALABI:v1p99/r3] THALABI, d. 427 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās calls the two names subtle, with one more delicate than the other.
[THALABI:v1p99/r4] THALABI, d. 427 · مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid says al-Raḥmān is for the people of this world and al-Raḥīm for the people of the afterlife.
[THALABI:v1p99/r5] THALABI, d. 427 · الضحاك | reports | Al-Ḍaḥḥāk assigns al-Raḥmān’s mercy to heaven’s inhabitants through their dwelling, obedience, and protection, and al-Raḥīm’s to earthly people through messengers, scriptures, counsel, and relief from calamities.
[THALABI:v1p99/r6] THALABI, d. 427 · عكرمة | reports | ʿIkrima says al-Raḥmān is associated with one mercy and al-Raḥīm with one hundred; he derives this from the Prophetic report that one mercy was sent to earth and ninety-nine are reserved for Judgment Day.
[THALABI:v1p99/r7] THALABI, d. 427 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet reports that God has one hundred mercies: one is shared among creation, while ninety-nine are reserved to show mercy to servants on the Resurrection Day.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r1] MAWARDI, d. 450 · author | holds | The Qur’an and Sunna establish that a reciter seeks refuge before reciting, saying I seek refuge in God from Satan the accursed.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r10] MAWARDI, d. 450 · unattributed | reports | Alternatively, al-rajim means one who is pelted, specifically with stars.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r11] MAWARDI, d. 450 · unattributed | reports | Another explanation of one who is pelted is that he is ill-omened.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r12] MAWARDI, d. 450 · unattributed | reports | A third explanation is that al-rajim means accursed, that is, expelled.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r13] MAWARDI, d. 450 · author | holds | In the Prophetic formula, Satan’s puff means pride, his spittle means magic, and his provocation means madness.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r14] MAWARDI, d. 450 · قتادة | reports | Qatada holds that the Fatiha is Meccan.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r15] MAWARDI, d. 450 · مجاهد | reports | Mujahid holds that the Fatiha is Medinan, contrary to Qatada’s view.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r16] MAWARDI, d. 450 · أبو هريرة | reports | Abu Hurayra reports the Prophet naming the sura Umm al-Qur’an, Fatiha al-Kitab, and the seven oft-repeated verses; no grading is given.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r17] MAWARDI, d. 450 · author | holds | It is called Fatiha al-Kitab because the written Qur’an begins with it and it is first in recitation.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r18] MAWARDI, d. 450 · author | holds | It is called Umm al-Qur’an because it precedes the rest, which follow it; an army’s banner is also called umm because the army follows it, as Labid’s verse illustrates.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r19] MAWARDI, d. 450 · author | holds | The years already lived are also called umm because they precede a person’s present, supported by the cited verse about fifty years.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r2] MAWARDI, d. 450 · أبو سعيد الخدري | reports | Abu Sa‘id reports that the Prophet also sought refuge in God the All-Hearing, All-Knowing from Satan’s puff, spittle, and provocation; no grading is stated.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r20] MAWARDI, d. 450 · الأكثرون | reports | Most scholars allow calling the Fatiha Umm al-Kitab because al-Kitab means the Qur’an.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r21] MAWARDI, d. 450 · الحسن وابن سيرين | reports | Hasan and Ibn Sirin reject calling it Umm al-Kitab, reserving that title for the Preserved Tablet in 43:4. | mentions 43:4
[MAWARDI:1:1/r22] MAWARDI, d. 450 · unattributed | reports | Makka is called Umm al-Qura because it precedes the other towns.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r23] MAWARDI, d. 450 · unattributed | reports | Alternatively, Makkah is Umm al-Qura because the earth was spread out from it and originated there, like children originating from a mother.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r24] MAWARDI, d. 450 · author | holds | The Fatiha is called the seven oft-repeated because everyone agrees it has seven verses.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r25] MAWARDI, d. 450 · author | holds | It is oft-repeated because it is recited in every obligatory and voluntary prayer.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r26] MAWARDI, d. 450 · author | holds | Calling it al-mathani does not preclude applying that name to other passages; al-A‘sha of Hamdan’s verse calls other passages al-mathani and the long suras.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r27] MAWARDI, d. 450 · author | holds | All agree the basmala is Qur’an in sura 27, but disagree whether it belongs in the Fatiha and at the opening of every sura.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r28] MAWARDI, d. 450 · الشافعي | reports | Al-Shafi‘i and a group affirm the basmala as part of the Fatiha and sura openings.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r29] MAWARDI, d. 450 · أبو حنيفة | reports | Abu Hanifa and others deny that the basmala is part of the Fatiha and sura openings.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r3] MAWARDI, d. 450 · unattributed | reports | One explanation takes seeking refuge as seeking protection from one possessing power.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r30] MAWARDI, d. 450 · أبو عبيدة وطائفة | reports | Abu Ubayda and others take ism in the basmala as redundant, so the phrase means Allah, the Merciful, the Compassionate; Labid’s use of ism al-salam is their poetic parallel.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r31] MAWARDI, d. 450 · قطرب | reports | Qutrub says the added ism exalts and magnifies God’s mention, distinguishing it from mention of created beings.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r32] MAWARDI, d. 450 · الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash says the added ism shifts the phrase from an oath to an intention of blessing.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r33] MAWARDI, d. 450 · الجمهور | reports | The majority hold that ism in the basmala is purposeful, not redundant.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r34] MAWARDI, d. 450 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farra construes the preposition as an imperative: begin in the name of God.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r35] MAWARDI, d. 450 · الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj construes it as a statement: I began in the name of God.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r36] MAWARDI, d. 450 · author | holds | The connective alif is omitted in spelling and pronunciation because of frequent use and joining; by contrast it remains written in 96:1 because that wording is less frequent. | mentions 96:1
[MAWARDI:1:1/r37] MAWARDI, d. 450 · author | holds | A name points to its referent, whereas an attribute conveys information about what is described; when an attribute is made a name it does both.
[MAWARDI:1:1/r38] MAWARDI, d. 450 · قوم | rejects | Some claim names of entities are the entities themselves and the spoken form is merely naming; al-Mawardi rejects this, since it would also make names of actions identical to the actions, which is untenable.
