[ABUHAYYAN:96:10/r10] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farraʾ calls the situation astonishing and a rebuke: Abu Jahl forbids a worshipper who follows guidance and piety, while the forbidding man denies and turns away; God sees his deed.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:10/r11] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farraʾ explains that piety is paired with prayer because Abu Jahl objected both to the Prophet's prayer and call to God; the Prophet reformed himself through prayer and others through enjoining piety.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:10/r12] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAtiyya says the closing question ألم يعلم completes the reproach and threat for each of the three preceding conditional formulations, compressing what could otherwise be stated after each.

[ABUHAYYAN:96:10/r14] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | rejects | Abū Ḥayyān rejects that parsing: the second object of أرأيت must be an interrogative clause, as in Qur'anic examples, rather than the relative plus conditional proposed by al-Zamakhshari. | mentions 53:33, 53:34, 53:35, 19:77, 19:78, 56:58, 56:59
[ABUHAYYAN:96:10/r15] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Abū Ḥayyān instead parses the first أرأيت with the relative as its first object; the later two instances have omitted objects recoverable from the servant or forbidding man and from the final interrogative clause.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:10/r16] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The successive omissions are not a case of syntactic competition, since clauses cannot be pronominally omitted; they are ordinary ellipses.
