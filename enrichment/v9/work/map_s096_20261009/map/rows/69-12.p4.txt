[TABATABAI:69:12#2/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | Al-Burhān cites related reports through Saʿd b. ʿAbd Allāh from Abū ʿAbd Allāh, al-Kulaynī from him, and Ibn Bābawayh from Jābir from Abū Jaʿfar; it also cites reports ending with ʿAlī through ʿUmar b. ʿAlī, Burayda, and Zur b. Ḥubaysh.
[TABATABAI:69:12#2/r9] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | Al-Ghāyat al-Marām counts sixteen reports on this point from both schools, while al-Burhān says Muḥammad b. al-ʿAbbās recorded thirty from Sunnī and Shīʿī sources.
[TABATABAI:69:12/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The Lord’s general creative guidance equips every kind of creature to move toward its fitting perfection, as expressed in the cited verses. | mentions 20:50, 87:2, 87:3
[TABATABAI:69:12/r10] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The verse also indicates that external events can affect human actions.
[TABATABAI:69:12/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Humans share this existential development with material creatures but also develop legislatively through chosen actions that form qualities and states in this life.
[TABATABAI:69:12/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Those acquired dispositions constitute the human being’s lasting, happy life and the goal of human existence.
[TABATABAI:69:12/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | This need for chosen moral development motivates religious law through messengers, revealed books, and guidance, completing the proof against people. | mentions 4:165
[TABATABAI:69:12/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Revealed guidance first shows the straight path and leaves humans to choose gratitude or rejection. | mentions 76:3
[TABATABAI:69:12/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Following the path yields a good, happy life; turning away brings lasting misery, while the proof is established either way. | mentions 8:42
[TABATABAI:69:12/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | “A reminder” points to guidance that shows the way: a reminder completes God’s proof but may or may not produce a response.
[TABATABAI:69:12/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The aware ear points to guidance that carries a person to the goal; awareness is an instance of being led by divine guidance.
[TABATABAI:69:12/r9] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Awareness is not attributed only to God because the passage invites people and promises reward for responding; reward follows awareness as a human act, though it is also attributable to God.
[TABATABAI:69:6-12/r18] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The clause explains why they were carried; the feminine pronoun refers to the act of carrying, considered as an event.
[TABATABAI:69:6-12/r19] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The flood and rescue were made a reminder, instructive example, and admonition.
