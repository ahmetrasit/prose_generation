16:16/q08 Who are 'they' in 'they are guided'? [هُمْ · referent] (turns on: Whether the third person singles out Quraysh for their expertise or generalizes the guidance to all people; the shift of grammatical person is explained accordingly.)
  16:16/q08/p1 Quraysh, especially their trading travelers skilled in star navigation. — reasons: Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samin, Bursevī and Study Quran cite their journeys and skill; the shift from direct address makes their obligation to reflect, thank God or affirm tawḥīd especially strong. Ālūsī reports and develops this reading. Biqāʿī includes Quraysh as the first and most knowledgeable addressees while extending the statement outward.
  16:16/q08/p2 All travelers or earth's inhabitants, with a shift from direct address to third person. — reasons: Biqāʿī extends the address from Quraysh to Arabs and all inhabitants. Ālūsī reports the preceding addressees broadly. Ṭabāṭabāʾī suggests the shift avoids repeating the second-person 'you find guidance' of the prior verses.
16:16/q09 Why are 'by the star' and 'they' placed emphatically? [وَبِٱلنَّجْمِ هُمْ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the marked word order mainly restricts the group or means of guidance, ranks star guidance, or serves cadence with emphasis.)
  16:16/q09/p1 Fronting and the detached pronoun restrict or emphasize star guidance for Quraysh in particular. — reasons: Bayḍāwī, Nasafī and Bursevī connect the order with Quraysh's exceptional knowledge and debt of gratitude. Samin calls both frontings restrictive; Abū Ḥayyān cites Zamakhsharī's paraphrase 'by the star in particular.'
  16:16/q09/p2 The fronting highlights the importance and superiority of stellar guidance. — reasons: Biqāʿī says other forms rank below star guidance; Ibn ʿĀshūr says sailors' reliance on stars makes both fronted elements important.
  16:16/q09/p3 Fronting serves the verse ending's cadence, while the detached pronoun adds emphasis. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī mention cadence; Ālūsī combines it with emphasis of 'they.'
16:16/q10 What purposes were the stars created to serve? [بِٱلنَّجْمِ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the list of divinely assigned uses is limited to three or includes calculation of years.)
  16:16/q10/p1 Adorning heaven, stoning devils, and guiding people are their three stated purposes. — reasons: Yaḥyā links the lamps and missiles to 67:5 and guidance to 6:97. Qatāda states the three purposes in Ṭabarī, Thaʿlabī and Baghawī, adding that other purposes claimed without knowledge are baseless; Ḥassān b. Bilāl calls such attribution false, sinful and innovative.
  16:16/q10/p2 Those functions also include marking years and reckoning. — reasons: Zajjāj lists adornment, stoning devils, guiding travelers, and marking years and calculation.
16:16/q11 What may people learn or claim from the stars? [بِٱلنَّجْمِ · law] (turns on: The distinction drawn between practical observation of routes, direction and times and forecasts claiming knowledge of future events.)
  16:16/q11/p1 Learning stars for routes, qibla and times is permissible. — reasons: ʿUmar permits enough astronomy for roads and qibla, then advises learning genealogy for kinship (Bursevī). Ālūsī reports permission to learn what enables times, qibla and routes. Durr reports Mujāhid saw no harm in learning lunar stations; early authorities in Bursevī classify astronomy as the science of times.
  16:16/q11/p2 Astrological prediction of future events is forbidden or unreliable. — reasons: Bursevī reports a prophetic saying that learning astrology takes a branch of sorcery; al-Ḥāfiẓ specifies foretelling from planetary motions and conjunctions, knowledge reserved to God. Bursevī says a prediction of destructive winds from a 581 AH conjunction failed, and reports a student's advice to avoid astrologers (and doctors). Qatāda and Ḥassān b. Bilāl reject claimed stellar purposes beyond the known uses.
16:16/q12 Who was first said to study stars and calculation? [بِٱلنَّجْمِ · reports]
  16:16/q12/p1 The prophet Idrīs. — reasons: Bursevī transmits this as an unnamed report.
16:16/q13 How should the qibla be found when its direction is uncertain? [بِٱلنَّجْمِ هُمْ يَهْتَدُونَ · law] (turns on: Whether practical star bearings yield exact orientation or only an adequate general direction; the geographical objections concern changing meridians and uncertain coordinates.)
  16:16/q13/p1 Use available indications, including stars, other heavenly bodies, winds and terrestrial signs, and reason toward the likeliest direction. — reasons: Rāzī accepts the jurists' inference from route guidance and requires reasoning when signs appear. Qurṭubī distinguishes visible Kaaba, which must be precisely faced with the whole body, from the unseen Kaaba, whose direction is inferred from sun, moon, stars and winds. Ibn al-ʿArabī says ordinary people can use conspicuous fixed al-Jady and Farqads, while experts use risings, settings and north/south positions; he instructs placing the pole behind the left shoulder.
  16:16/q13/p2 Stars and other means establish the general qibla direction, but exact determination is difficult or impossible. — reasons: Ibn al-Bannāʾ says a star above Mecca does not keep the same bearing in another city, al-Jady's shoulder or side rule depends on an uncertain meridian, the midsummer zenith differs between cities, and even Indian-circle methods depend on uncertain latitude and longitude. Worshippers need only seek the direction.
16:16/q14 What follows if someone prays toward a wrong direction? [بِٱلنَّجْمِ هُمْ يَهْتَدُونَ · law] (turns on: Whether the error reflects a failure to use available signs or is discovered after carrying out the required effort.)
  16:16/q14/p1 If signs were apparent and the person failed to reason, the prayer must be repeated or is invalid. — reasons: Rāzī calls such error negligence and requires repetition when it becomes clear. Qurṭubī says a person able to reason out the qibla who prays away from it has not prayed validly.
  16:16/q14/p2 If the error is discovered afterward, repeating within the prayer time is advised but not obligatory. — reasons: Qurṭubī says the original duty was fulfilled as commanded.
16:16/q15 What should one do if no qibla signs appear? [بِٱلنَّجْمِ هُمْ يَهْتَدُونَ · law] (turns on: Whether equal uncertainty permits selecting one direction or requires covering every possibility for certainty.)
  16:16/q15/p1 Choose any direction because none has evidence favoring it. — reasons: Rāzī reports this proposed rule when all directions are equal.
  16:16/q15/p2 Pray toward every direction to discharge the duty with certainty. — reasons: Rāzī prefers this rule. He analogizes an unknown missed prayer to making up all five; against the claim that only one is due, he argues that if all must be performed, all are obligatory.
16:16/q16 What inward guidance is read in the star and signs? [وَعَلَٰمَٰتٍۢ بِٱلنَّجْمِ · inward] (turns on: Which spiritual referents the physical signs symbolize and whether guidance is attributed directly to God or mediated through named figures.)
  16:16/q16/p1 The signs are landmarks of the path and stars of gnosis, yet God alone guides the seeker to Himself. — reasons: Al-Mālikī in Sulamī says no created sign or route reaches God apart from God; arrival and severance follow prior decree.
  16:16/q16/p2 The star symbolizes the Messenger, and the signs symbolize the Imams or Ahl al-Bayt. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmits Abū ʿAbd Allāh's interpretation and treats it as inner meaning, supported by a report naming the Prophet the star and the Ahl al-Bayt earth's signs and protection.

  16:16/q17/p1 Created navigational aids are among God's blessings and call people to reflection and gratitude. — reasons: Jishumī groups them with animals, trees and fruits and says reflection makes reasoning obligatory rather than uncritical imitation.
