## THE CURRENT MAP
31:27/q01 What does law imply in this supposition? [وَلَوْ · grammar] (turns on: Whether an unreal condition entails that the consequent fails without it.)
  31:27/q01/p1 It links an immense hypothetical to non-exhaustion without implying exhaustion otherwise. — reasons: Sībawayh, Ibn Mālik, Burhān and Ibn ʿĀshūr separate the conditional link from independent truth; Burhān says fewer supplies strengthen the denial. Al-Samīn rejects the simple counterfactual slogan.
  31:27/q01/p2 It only joins past condition and result without asserting either's failure. — reasons: Al-Itqān reports this first view and Ibn Hishām's objection; al-Zamakhsharī links verbal propositions.
  31:27/q01/p3 It conventionally negates the result when its condition fails. — reasons: Al-Itqān reports the widespread account but notes this verse as a difficulty; Burhān tests it against Suhayb's obedience and 8:23.
31:27/q02 How does annamā function after law? [أَنَّمَا · grammar] (turns on: Whether law licenses a nominal predicate and whether an existential predicate is supplied.)
  31:27/q02/p1 The anna clause has mā as noun and aqlām as nominal predicate. — reasons: Sībawayh says annamā can stand where anna can, unlike independent innamā; al-Mubarrad makes the clause a subject of implied existence, while others move an omitted predicate before or after. Al-Ṣaffār and poems support the construction.
  31:27/q02/p2 A nominal predicate after law anna is valid and refutes a strict verbal requirement. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn cite common Arabic and poems; Ibn al-Ḥājib concurs.
  31:27/q02/p3 A verbal predicate is required only with a derived predicate; aqlām is outside that rule. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī narrows al-Zamakhsharī's rule and excludes a wish construction at 33:20.
  31:27/q02/p4 The noun after law is subject to an omitted existential verb. — reasons: Burhān reports the Kufan estimate 'if it were established that.'
31:27/q03 What does min shajaratin specify? [مِن شَجَرَةٍ · grammar] (turns on: Whether specification attaches to mā or its locative pronoun.)
  31:27/q03/p1 It specifies mā or its locative pronoun and encompasses the whole class of earthly trees. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī give these grammatical analyses; al-Ālūsī compares an indefinite in a conditional and Ibn ʿAbbās's date-and-locust analogy.
31:27/q04 Why is shajarah singular? [مِن شَجَرَةٍ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the singular distributes each tree or names the whole class.)
  31:27/q04/p1 It distributes the picture tree by tree until none is omitted. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Rāzī, Biqāʿī and Bursevī stress exhaustive individual treatment.
  31:27/q04/p2 It denotes the class; each tree can yield many pens from its branches. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān cites indefinite singular parallels at 2:106 and 35:2; al-Ālūsī objects that a lone singular does not formally mean 'tree by tree' and one pen per tree would weaken the image; Ibn ʿĀshūr describes suitable branches.
31:27/q05 What is aqlām? [أَقْلَٰمٌۭ · meaning]
  31:27/q05/p1 Pens are cut and sharpened writing tools, potentially many from each tree. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī describes trimmed branches; Bursevī derives qalam from cutting and distinguishes kinds of cutting; Ibn ʿĀshūr describes a split reed. Al-Ālūsī calls aqlām formally a paucity plural because qilām is rare.
  31:27/q05/p2 No parallel supply of pens is needed, because one pen writes many books. — reasons: Al-Rāzī contrasts quickly consumed ink with a pen's long use; al-Biqāʿī infers omitted ink and more trees.
31:27/q06 Which sea is intended? [وَٱلْبَحْرُ · referent] (turns on: Whether the article points to the known ocean, all seas, or fresh water.)
  31:27/q06/p1 The sea generically, including Earth's seas. — reasons: Al-Rāzī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī make the first sea generic; al-Ṭībī takes its article as replacing Earth's possessive pronoun.
  31:27/q06/p2 The great encircling ocean. — reasons: Bursevī describes its inlets, depth, islands, jinn cities and Iblīs's throne; al-Ālūsī prefers the known greatest sea.
  31:27/q06/p3 Fresh water that grows trees for pens. — reasons: Abū ʿUbaydah argues salt water cannot grow the pens; Majāz notes inland country may also be called a sea.
  31:27/q06/p4 Bahr lexically denotes broad or abundant water and can have figurative uses. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī cites a broad-striding horse, a prophetic use for abundance, briny water poetry, bāḥir for a fool and baḥrah for a locality.
31:27/q07 How is al-baḥr read? [وَٱلْبَحْرُ · readings] (turns on: Which transmitted case or wording is followed.)
  31:27/q07/p1 Accusative al-baḥra, attributed chiefly to Abū ʿAmr and Yaʿqūb. — reasons: Ibn Mujāhid says Abū ʿAmr alone in his list; al-Nashr names two Basrans, while other accounts add Ibn Abī Isḥāq and ʿĪsā. Al-Ṭabarī accepts both cases.
  31:27/q07/p2 Nominative al-baḥru, the majority reading. — reasons: Ibn Mujāhid and al-Fārisī name the majority; Ibn ʿUmar reports prophetic nominative recitation. Al-Ṭabarī accepts both cases.
  31:27/q07/p3 Indefinite nominative wa-baḥrun is read by Ibn Masʿūd and Ṭalḥah. — reasons: Ibn Jinnī supplies 'there is a sea'; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn allow a circumstantial wāw; al-Ālūsī prefers the stated verb as predicate.
  31:27/q07/p4 Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad reads 'the sea is its ink' (madāduhu). — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyyah calls madād a verbal noun; al-Ṭabrisī rejects taking seawater merely as addition to other ink.
31:27/q08 How is the sea clause parsed? [وَٱلْبَحْرُ يَمُدُّهُۥ · grammar] (turns on: Whether it is circumstantial or coordinated within the law clause.)
  31:27/q08/p1 It is a new circumstantial nominal clause with al-baḥr as subject and yamudduhu as predicate. — reasons: Sībawayh estimates 'the sea is in this state'; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī prefer wāw al-ḥāl, with no linking pronoun required. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it supplies the simultaneous ink state.
  31:27/q08/p2 Accusative al-baḥr coordinates with mā, the noun of anna. — reasons: Sībawayh gives the verse as an example. Ibn al-Ḥājib says yamudduhu must predicate the sea if no other predicate is given; al-Ṭabrisī prefers elided 'ink' as predicate and makes the verb circumstantial.
  31:27/q08/p3 Nominative al-baḥr coordinates with the syntactic position or sense of anna. — reasons: Ibn Jinnī cites 9:3; al-Zamakhsharī permits it. Abū Ḥayyān says it depends on a disputed subject parse after law, while al-Ālūsī questions crossing an omitted predicate.
  31:27/q08/p4 Accusative al-baḥr depends on an omitted verb explained by yamudduhu. — reasons: Bayḍāwī, al-Samīn and Qiraat-ER report this; al-Ālūsī also mentions an omitted thābit.
  31:27/q08/p5 The sea clause implicitly means 'earth's sea' to provide a possessive link. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī proposes this; Abū Ḥayyān objects that the nominal circumstantial clause needs no such link and the article-for-pronoun parse is Kufan.
31:27/q09 How is yamudduhu read? [يَمُدُّهُۥ · readings] (turns on: Whether the verb is from madda or amadda and whether the subject is treated as feminine.)
  31:27/q09/p1 Some read the causative form from amadda with ḍamma on the yāʾ. — reasons: Attributed variously to al-Aʿraj, al-Ḥasan, Ibn Hurmuz, Ibn Muṣarrif and Ibn Masʿūd; Ibn Jinnī compares army reinforcements and rivers, citing 8:9. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it loses the inkwell nuance.
  31:27/q09/p2 Some read feminine tamudduhu. — reasons: Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿAbbās or Ubayy are named; the seven seas are construed as feminine subject.
  31:27/q09/p3 The majority reads masculine yamudduhu from madda. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyyah calls it the common reading; Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies its vowels.
31:27/q10 What does yamudduhu mean here? [يَمُدُّهُۥ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the verb evokes ink supply or a general extension.)
  31:27/q10/p1 Seven seas continually replenish the first as ink replenishes an inkpot. — reasons: Al-Yazīdī compares adding water to a pot; al-Zamakhsharī, Nasafī and al-Ālūsī use an inkwell image; al-Ālūsī notes the imperfect form, and Ibn ʿĀshūr says ink is implicit.
  31:27/q10/p2 Madda extends or adds gradually, while amadda denotes external assistance or supply. — reasons: Al-Māwardī, Abū Zayd, Ibn ʿAṭiyyah and al-Qurṭubī report distinctions, comparing rivers, wells, money and troops; Ibn Qutaybah connects this use to midād ink.
  31:27/q10/p3 Madda and amadda can be synonymous dialect variants. — reasons: Groups reported by Ibn ʿAṭiyyah and al-Qurṭubī, and Ibn al-Shaykh, see no material difference.
31:27/q11 What does the pronoun in yamudduhu refer to? [يَمُدُّهُۥ · referent]
  31:27/q11/p1 The first sea. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī and al-Ṭabrisī make the antecedent explicit.
31:27/q12 What does min baʿdihi mean? [مِنۢ بَعْدِهِۦ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the relation is spatial/additive or temporal.)
  31:27/q12/p1 From behind or in addition to the first sea. — reasons: Majāz pictures seven seas flowing into the first; al-Baghawī says behind and additional; al-Ālūsī allows this.
  31:27/q12/p2 After the first sea's ink has been used up. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī presents this as an alternative.
31:27/q13 What are the seven added seas? [سَبْعَةُ أَبْحُرٍۢ · referent] (turns on: Whether they are imagined equivalents or particular physical waters.)
  31:27/q13/p1 Actual seas or rivers identified in reports. — reasons: Ibn ʿAmr reports alternating seven fire and water seas below the sea; Bursevī cites China, Tibet, India, Sind, Persia, East and West, and separately suggests seven named rivers; al-Irshād speaks of mountain-fed waterways. No grading is stated.
  31:27/q13/p2 Imagined seas comparable to the first. — reasons: Al-Rāzī places them beyond existing seas; al-Ālūsī makes each match the ocean in extent and water; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī agrees.
  31:27/q13/p3 A literal seven seas surrounding the world is unconfirmed Israelite material. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr rejects deriving a fixed cosmography from the number.
