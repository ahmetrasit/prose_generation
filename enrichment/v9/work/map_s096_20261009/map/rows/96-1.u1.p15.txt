96:1/q42 What is distinctive about this sūra's opening form? [* · rhetoric]
  96:1/q42/p1 It is one of the Qurʾānic sūras opening with an imperative. — reasons: Al-Burhān lists six sūras opened by commands, including Iqraʾ. ITQAN (أبو شامة): Abu Shama counts six sūras that open with a command, including those beginning qul, iqraʾ, and sabbiḥ. CORPUSCORANICUM: The opening imperative follows hymn openings in Q 87, 73, and 74, and likely addresses the preacher; such commands also apply by example to the wider community of hearers.; cites 87:1, 73:1, 73:2, 73:3, 73:4, 74:1, 74:2, 74:3, 74:4, 74:5, 74:6, 81:1, 93:1, 94:1, 108:1
  96:1/q42/p2 Its opening previews the Qurʾān's recitation, divine name, teaching, and principles. — reasons: ITQAN: The opening of “Read” similarly previews the Qur’an: it commands reading, begins in God’s name, signals legal knowledge and the principles of religion, and reports God’s teaching of what humans did not know; it can therefore be called the Qur’an’s title. IBNASHUR: Opening with “Read” foretells that the Messenger will recite a book despite having recited no book before the Qur'an, as 29:48 states; he told Gabriel, “I am not a reader.”; cites 29:48 IBNASHUR: This opening is a fine instance of barāʿat al-istihlāl, an apt opening that anticipates the Qur'an's significance. IBNASHUR-FULL: Opening with 'Recite' announces that the Messenger will become a reciter of scripture although he had not recited scripture before revelation; it is a fitting opening for the Qur'an.; cites 29:48
96:1/q43 What is reported of Waraqa after his death? [* · reports]
  96:1/q43/p1 The Prophet saw Waraqa in Paradise wearing silk because he believed. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Ṭabrisī report the Prophet seeing Waraqa in Paradise clothed in silk because he believed.
  96:1/q43/p2 Waraqa's poem affirms Muhammad's mission and angelic revelation. — reasons: Waraqa's poem in al-Thaʿlabī and al-Ṭabrisī names Aḥmad, Gabriel, and Michael and contrasts the saved and doomed.
96:1/q44 What report connects this passage to ʿUmar's conversion? [* · reports]
  96:1/q44/p1 ʿUmar heard his sister recite al-ʿAlaq, then accepted Islam. — reasons: DURR (ثوبان): Thawbān reports the Prophet’s prayer for God to strengthen Islam through ʿUmar; after hearing his sister recite al-ʿAlaq, ʿUmar sought the message and embraced Islam. DURR-FULL (عمر بن الخطاب): Thawban's report says Umar heard his sister reciting the opening passage, came to Muhammad, and accepted Islam after the Prophet invited him to testify to God's oneness and Muhammad's messengership.
