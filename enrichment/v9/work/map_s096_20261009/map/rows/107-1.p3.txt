[ABUHAYYAN:107:1/r6] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Arayta most likely means akhbirnī, takes two objects, and leaves the second understood; al-Ḥawfī estimates it as “is he not deserving of God’s punishment?”

[ABUHAYYAN:107:2/r10] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | rejects | What al-Zamakhsharī calls an omitted answer to arayta is not an answer; it occupies the place of arayta’s second object.

[ABUHAYYAN:107:2/r8] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | rejects | Al-Zamakhsharī’s accusative coordination is an unusual construction; the immediate, more eloquent parse makes fa-dhālika a nominative subject.
[ABUHAYYAN:107:2/r9] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | rejects | The proposed coordination of one entity with another is invalid: fa-dhālika points to the same individual as alladhī yukadhdhib.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p551/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الجمهور | reports | Most commentators call the surah Meccan; Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatada say Madinan, while Hibat Allāh says its halves came down in Mecca and Medina.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p551/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Since the previous surah recalled God's favors to Quraysh, this one threatens those who deny resurrection and recompense.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p551/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | The passage is attributed to Abū Jahl, al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra, al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil, ʿUmar ibn ʿĀʾidh, two hypocrites, or Abū Sufyān, who struck an orphan asking for food after his weekly sacrifice; Ibn Jurayj gives the last report.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p552/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الحوفي | reports | Al-Ḥawfī takes araʾayta as inform me, with two objects: al-ladhī supplies one and the other is omitted as whether he deserves God's punishment.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p552/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī supplies the omitted second object as asking who the person is; the added address kāf in Ibn ʿAbbās's reading supports inform me, since visual raʾā does not take it.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p552/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الحوفي | reports | Al-Ḥawfī also allows literal seeing, with no ellipsis; the question prompts recognition of a person with the described traits.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p552/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Al-dīn means recompense through reward and punishment; al-Zamakhsharī says denial of recompense appears as harshly pushing an orphan away and refusing to urge one's household to feed a poor person.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p552/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن عباس ومجاهد | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās glosses denial as rejecting God's rule; Mujāhid says accounting, while other views say recompense or the Qur'an.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p553/r10] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The author says a more idiomatic construction would coordinate a following relative clause or use a verb directly, rather than insert the demonstrative in al-Zamakhsharī's proposed parse.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p553/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī offers an alternative syntax joining fa-dhālika to the one who denies judgment, omitting the answer to araʾayta, and making the woe to praying hypocrites a description standing for their pronoun; generic singular carries a plural sense.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p553/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | The author calls al-Zamakhsharī's accusative parsing of fa-dhālika strange and unnatural: a nominative subject is immediate, while an accusative would require repeating the verb and pointing needlessly to the person.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p554/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | He rejects the proposed parsing that makes the demonstrative refer to a separate entity: fa-dhālika points back to the one who denies, so they are not two entities.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p554/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | The alleged omitted answer to arāʾayta is not an answer; it functions as the second object of arāʾayta.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p554/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | He rejects an interrogative hamza before niʿma, since niʿma and biʾsa are performative expressions and interrogation applies only to declarative statements.
