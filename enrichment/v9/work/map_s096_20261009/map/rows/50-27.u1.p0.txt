## THE CURRENT MAP
50:27/q01 Who is the speaking قرينه? [قَرِينُهُۥ · referent] (turns on: Whether the companion is the tempter, the recorder, a human influence, or an inner appetite; the alleged dispute and the relation to 50:23 inform the identifications.)
  50:27/q01/p1 The person's assigned Satan or jinn devil, his companion in this life and at judgment. — reasons: Muqatil says he embellished falsehood and evil. Qatada, Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ibn Jurayj and others identify Satan; Ibn Qutayba chooses this reading as reported by Wahidi, and Nasafi says the wording supports Mujahid. Tabarsi says he is paired in punishment; Damghani and Elmalılı extend the companionship from this world to the next. Tabatabai cites 43:36–38 for the accompanying whisperer. Qurtubi reports al-Mahdawi's claim of no disagreement, while Qurtubi himself also transmits a contrary angel report. Ibn Qutayba's Gharib refers readers to his Mushkil explanation; Study Quran directs them to 50:23. Kuranyolu connects the companion to Satan in 14:22.
  50:27/q01/p2 The angel assigned to record the person's words and deeds. — reasons: Ibn Abbas and Muqatil are named by Thalabi and Qurtubi; al-Kalbi and Sa'id ibn Jubayr by Wahidi; Baghawi also names Sa'id. Al-Farra chooses this interpretation in Wahidi. Qushayri describes the angel defending the timing of his record. Razi reports it as an alternative to Satan, in discussion of the companion of 50:23.
  50:27/q01/p3 A human companion, such as a corrupt scholar or leader whom the person followed. — reasons: Tabarsi reports this as another identification.
  50:27/q01/p4 The sinner's instinctive, unrestrained appetites, symbolized by Satan. — reasons: Asad contrasts this figure with moral consciousness or reason in 50:23 and compares 41:25, 43:36, and Razi's explanation of 14:22.
50:27/q02 Is this companion the same as the one in 50:23? [قَرِينُهُۥ · links] (turns on: Whether the repeated word qarin denotes one continuing figure or different forms of companionship.)
  50:27/q02/p1 The companion can be the same one mentioned in 50:23. — reasons: Ibn Ashur identifies him as the companion who had said the record was ready; Razi's discussion considers that identification and the resulting difficulty if the speaker is Satan. Study Quran sends readers to 50:23 for the companion's meaning.
  50:27/q02/p2 Companionship takes several forms, so the companions need not be identical. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya explicitly cautions against identifying them; Asad contrasts the moral consciousness or reason of 50:23 with the appetites speaking here.
50:27/q03 How can Satan's denial fit a claim that he prepared the person in 50:23? [قَرِينُهُۥ مَآ أَطْغَيْتُهُۥ · links] (turns on: How to understand Satan's earlier preparation: causation, embellishment without compulsion, or a boast later withdrawn.)
  50:27/q03/p1 The two claims appear to conflict if Satan's preparation means that he misguided the person. — reasons: Razi raises the difficulty when the earlier statement that the person is ready for punishment is assigned to Satan.
  50:27/q03/p2 Satan prepared the path by adorning it, without compelling the person to follow. — reasons: Razi reports al-Zamakhshari's reconciliation.
  50:27/q03/p3 Satan spoke at different times: he boasted first, then retracted on seeing shared punishment. — reasons: Razi reports al-Zamakhshari's alternative, relating the boast to 38:82–85 and the retraction to the sight of his own penalty.
50:27/q04 Why does قال قرينه begin without a conjunction? [قَالَ قَرِينُهُۥ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the omission mainly marks a new quoted response or continuity in a sequence of unjoined addresses.)
  50:27/q04/p1 It starts a fresh quoted reply in dialogue; the earlier coordinated statement joined concurrent events. — reasons: Razi says 50:23 joins the soul's arrival with its driver and the witness's speech, whereas 50:27 has no paired simultaneous events and the preceding fa requires no waw. Baydawi, Nasafi, Samin, and Alusi likewise contrast the coordinated arrival and companion's speech with this independent response. Nasafi compares the Moses–Pharaoh dialogue. Ibn Ashur calls the uncoordinated speech Qur'anic dialogue style and evidence of omitted prior speech.
  50:27/q04/p2 It continues the companion's address in the sequence of replies without conjunctions. — reasons: Bursevi compares the unjoined beginnings of ربنا, لا تختصموا in 50:28, and ما يبدل القول لدي in 50:29.
50:27/q05 What accusation or exchange does the companion's speech answer? [قَالَ قَرِينُهُۥ مَآ أَطْغَيْتُهُۥ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the alleged cause of the man's fate is Satan's seduction or an angel's disputed record or timing.)
  50:27/q05/p1 The condemned person blames Satan for leading him into rebellion; Satan answers the charge. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzi calls the exchange abbreviated; Razi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Samin, Bursevi, Alusi, Elmalılı, and Ibn Ashur reconstruct the omitted accusation. Razi and Bursevi cite the command not to quarrel in 50:28; Razi and Ibn Ashur compare 38:59–64. Ibn Ashur says the unbeliever tries to disown his disbelief, blaming the one who adorned it; Bursevi reports al-Kashifi's account of the devil brought forward before Hell. Wahidi reports Ibn Qutayba's comparison to disputation and mutual blame in 37:27–33; Tabatabai recalls the gathered wrongdoers and companions in 37:22. Wahidi says God then tells both disputants not to quarrel before Him.
  50:27/q05/p2 The unbeliever accuses the recording angel of adding to the record; the angel denies adding anything. — reasons: Sa'id ibn Jubayr says the angel wrote only what the person said and did; al-Kalbi is also named for the angel account.
  50:27/q05/p3 The unbeliever says the angel hurried him away from repentance; the angel denies hastening him. — reasons: Al-Farra prefers this account in Wahidi; the man remained far from repentance. Ibn al-Jawzi also reports al-Farra's version.
  50:27/q05/p4 Al-Walid says the angel hurried him into a slip or rebellion; the angel says he wrote only after the act. — reasons: Thalabi and Qurtubi attribute the identification of al-Walid and the recording angel to Ibn Abbas and Muqatil. Qushayri specifies that the record was not premature.
50:27/q06 Why does the speaker say ربنا? [رَبَّنَا · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the plural is explained by the scene's awe, the two disputants, or the sense of Lordship.)
  50:27/q06/p1 The plural address suits the awe of this scene, unlike a singular request for a personal favor. — reasons: Razi compares singular رب in 7:143, 71:28, 12:33, 66:11, and 38:79.
  50:27/q06/p2 The devil says our Lord because he and the human are disputing together before God. — reasons: Tabatabai says the pronoun includes both disputants.
  50:27/q06/p3 The address invokes God as their Lord and caretaker. — reasons: Bursevi glosses the address.
50:27/q07 What does أطغيته mean? [أَطْغَيْتُهُۥ · meaning]
  50:27/q07/p1 It means causing someone to exceed bounds or become rebellious or tyrannical. — reasons: Jishumi defines ṭughyān as exceeding the limit in corruption and aṭghā as causing transgression. Ibn Ashur specifies arrogance, oppression, or unbelief, notes yāʾ and wāw verb forms, and explains causation by commanding or enticing. Bursevi glosses making the person tyrannical or causing tyranny. Ibn al-Jawzi's lexical note includes deviation or error as a Quranic sense here.
50:27/q08 What does the speaker deny in ما أطغيته? [مَآ أَطْغَيْتُهُۥ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the denial concerns Satan's coercion or influence, or an angel's allegedly unfair record and timing.)
  50:27/q08/p1 Satan denies forcing or independently causing the person's error; he only invited, whispered, embellished, or encouraged what the person chose. — reasons: Muqatil says Satan lacked power to mislead without God's authority; Wahidi reports his claim that the man was already forsaken by God. Al-Qurazi specifically denies compulsion. Qushayri, Tabarsi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Ibn Kathir, Bursevi, Alusi, Elmalılı, and Tabatabai emphasize the man's choice. Tabarsi, Baydawi, Ibn Kathir, Bursevi, Alusi, Wahidi Wajiz, Kuranyolu, Study Quran, and Tabatabai compare 14:22: Satan had no authority beyond a call that people answered and should blame themselves. Kuranyolu includes that neither side can rescue the other and Satan denied their association of him with God. Bursevi cites a hadith contrasting the messenger's inability to guide everyone with Iblis's mere adornment. Ibn Ashur says the denial answers the suspicion created by close association.
  50:27/q08/p2 The companion denies leading the person astray or making him disbelieve. — reasons: Tabari and Nahhas specify transgression into disbelief. Thalabi, Baghawi, Ibn Kathir, and Elmalılı word it as denial of misguidance or seduction. Ibn Atiyya says a general denial of causal influence is false, since Satan whispered and beautified evil.
  50:27/q08/p3 The recording angel denies adding to or prematurely making the record, or hastening the person. — reasons: Sa'id ibn Jubayr says the angel recorded only words and deeds actually committed; Qushayri says only after the slip. Al-Farra's favored angel account denies both hastening and adding. The charges vary: adding entries, barring repentance, or rushing the man into error.
50:27/q09 Who is the person referred to by the object pronoun in أطغيته? [أَطْغَيْتُهُۥ · referent] (turns on: Whether the exchange is between external companions and a human, or between appetites and conscious reason.)
  50:27/q09/p1 The accused human, identified by some as al-Walid ibn al-Mughira al-Makhzumi. — reasons: Muqatil names al-Walid and says he lived in prolonged loss. Thalabi and Qurtubi report Ibn Abbas and Muqatil naming al-Walid in the angel version; the other accounts speak of the condemned disbeliever or human companion.
  50:27/q09/p2 The person's faculty of conscious, controlling reason. — reasons: Asad
