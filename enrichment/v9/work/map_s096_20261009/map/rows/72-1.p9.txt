[ALUSI-FULL:v29p81#3/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · زيد بن علي وجؤبة وابن أبي عبلة | reports | Zayd ibn ʿAlī, Juʾba in a report from al-Kisāʾī, and Ibn Abī ʿAblah in another report read aḥiya, replacing initial wāw with hamza, as in waʿd/aʿd.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p81#3/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī calls replacing an initial wāw with hamza freely permitted when the wāw is ḍamma-vowelled.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p81#3/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · المازني | reports | Al-Māzinī extends the change to a kasra-vowelled wāw, citing ishāḥ, iʿāʾ, and isāda; one of his positions restricts it to attested forms.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p81#3/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The generalization for ḍamma-vowelled wāw is criticized because initial, medial, and final positions have different rules and disputed details.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p81#3/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · بعض الأجلة | reports | Some eminent grammarians further hold that wāw preceded by ḍamma changes regularly, extending the claim even to a fatḥa context.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p81#3/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The phrase “to me” is attached to the passive verb and supplies its deputy subject.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p82#2/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Jinn is singular jinnī, as rūm is singular rūmī.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p82#2/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Jinn are rational bodies dominated by fiery nature, as 55:15 says; another view describes them as airy. All or some can assume different shapes, are usually hidden, and may be seen by prophets and chosen servants. | mentions 55:15
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p82#2/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | There is no rational bar to subtle fiery bodies differing in essence from other subtle bodies and receptive to life, power, and extraordinary acts; nature exceeds human understanding.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p82#2/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Modern philosophers posit subtle bodies with astonishing properties; Al-Ālūsī allows jinn to be bodies of that kind.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p82#2/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · ابن سينا | reports | Most philosophers deny jinn; Ibn Sīnā defines a jinn as an airy animal taking different shapes. Al-Ālūsī treats this as a nominal definition, but says denying external existence is clear unbelief.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p82#2/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الفلاسفة وأصحاب الروحانيات | reports | Many ancient philosophers and spiritualists affirm jinn as lower spirits: independent, noncorporeal substances of differing kinds, some good and some evil, whose number only God knows.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p82#2/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · قيل | reports | One theory says departed human souls gain power and may assist similar later bodies: good souls are angels whose aid is inspiration; evil souls are devils whose aid is temptation. Al-Ālūsī says this conflicts with the Salaf and the apparent Qurʾān and hadith.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p82#2/r8] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Muslims and most religious communities affirm jinn exist while differing about their nature; Al-Ālūsī directs readers to Ākām al-Marjān and al-Tafsīr al-Kabīr for detail.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p82/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The clause beginning anna is interpreted as a verbal noun, and its pronoun refers to the matter being discussed.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p82/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The object of “listened” is the Qurʾān, omitted because the following words make it clear; al-Aḥqāf supplies this context. | mentions 46:29
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p82/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Nafar is commonly three to ten, but al-Ḥarīrī wrongly restricts it to three-to-ten men: eloquent usage can exceed ten, as al-Shaʿbī’s “a dozen-odd men” shows.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p82/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الشعبي | reports | Al-Shaʿbī uses nafar for a dozen-odd people.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p82/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Nafar is not restricted to men or humans, since it is used here of jinn.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p82/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · المجمل | reports | Al-Mujmal says raht and nafar may denote up to forty; raht share one father, unlike nafar.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p82/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Nafar can also mean a people or group, as in 18:34 and Imruʾ al-Qays’s verse. | mentions 18:34
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p82/r8] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الكرماني | reports | Al-Kirmānī records another language usage: nafar can mean one man, as used to explain a sound hadith.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p83#2/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · عكرمة | reports | ʿIkrima identifies what the jinn heard as “Read in the name of your Lord.”

[ALUSI-FULL:v29p83#2/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The verbal noun “wonder” is used adjectivally for emphasis.

[ALUSI-FULL:v29p83/r10] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · ابن مسعود | reports | Al-Bayhaqī cites Ibn Masʿūd: after ʿishāʾ the Prophet led him to a place, drew a line he must not cross, then met jinn resembling Zutt; returning near dawn, he said the voices were jinn bidding him farewell and greeting.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p83/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · عكرمة | reports | ʿIkrima says twelve thousand listeners came from the Island of Mosul; Al-Ālūsī notes that nafar must then mean a group.

[ALUSI-FULL:v29p83/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The wording suggests the Prophet learned of the jinn’s listening by revelation, not direct observation; reports that he saw them are reconciled as separate incidents.

[ALUSI-FULL:v29p83/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · البيهقي | reports | Al-Bayhaqī reconciles Ibn ʿAbbās’s limited denial with 46:29, which says the Prophet spoke with the jinn, called them, and made them messengers to others. | mentions 46:29
