[QURTUBI:1:1/r25] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عمر | reports | On a journey at night, the Prophet sought protection from the land’s evils and creatures, including lions, snakes, scorpions, and inhabitants.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r26] QURTUBI, d. 671 · خولة بنت حكيم | reports | The Prophet promises protection to anyone who says God’s complete words when stopping somewhere; al-Tirmidhī grades it ḥasan gharīb ṣaḥīḥ.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r27] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Istiʿādha means seeking refuge, shelter, or protection from something disliked.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r28] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | In aʿūdhu, the ḍamma shifts to ʿayn because the vowel is heavy on wāw.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r29] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Shayṭān derives from shaṭana, “to be far,” because Satan is distant from good; the root describes a deep, distant well.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r3] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الجمهور | reports | Most scholars recommend seeking refuge for recitation outside prayer; prayer is disputed.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r30] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | rejects | Some derive shayṭān from burning or perishing, with nūn extra; Sibawayh’s tashayṭana supports derivation from shaṭana instead.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r31] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Any rebellious being among jinn, humans, or animals may be called a shayṭān, marking distance from truth and insolence.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r32] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Rajīm means expelled and disgraced; rajm originally means stoning and can also mean killing, cursing, expulsion, or insult. | mentions 26:116, 19:46
[QURTUBI:1:1/r33] QURTUBI, d. 671 · علي بن أبي طالب | reports | ʿAlī reports seeing the Prophet curse an elephant-shaped figure at al-Ṣafā; it identifies itself as Satan and says it joins the conception of anyone who hates ʿAlī.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r34] QURTUBI, d. 671 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet tells Ubayy ibn Kaʿb that no Torah or Gospel passage matches the Mother of the Qurʾān, the seven oft-repeated verses divided between God and servant.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r35] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبو سعيد بن المعلى | reports | In al-Bukhārī’s report, the Prophet calls al-Fātiḥa the greatest sūra, the seven oft-repeated verses, and the magnificent Qurʾān.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r36] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عبد البر | reports | Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr notes uncertainty over the narrator’s name; Qurṭubī lists variants but identifies him as a leading Anṣārī.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r37] QURTUBI, d. 671 · مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid reports that Satan rang four times, including when al-Fātiḥa was revealed, and says it was revealed in Medina.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r38] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الأشعري والقاضي أبو بكر وابن حبان | reports | A group including al-Ashʿarī, al-Qāḍī Abū Bakr, and Ibn Ḥibbān denies superiority among sūras, verses, or divine names.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r39] QURTUBI, d. 671 · يحيى بن يحيى | reports | Yaḥyā ibn Yaḥyā calls ranking Qurʾānic parts erroneous and says Mālik disliked repeatedly reciting one sūra to the exclusion of others.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r4] QURTUBI, d. 671 · عطاء | reports | Al-Naqqāsh reports that ʿAṭāʾ regards seeking refuge in prayer as obligatory.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r40] QURTUBI, d. 671 · البستي | reports | Al-Bustī explains al-Fātiḥa’s superiority over Torah and Gospel as greater reward for this community, not intrinsic superiority in God’s speech.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r41] QURTUBI, d. 671 · البستي | reports | Al-Bustī interprets “greatest sūra” as greatest in reward.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r42] QURTUBI, d. 671 · إسحاق بن راهويه وابن العربي وابن الحصار | prefers | The preferred view allows gradation by remarkable meanings and density of teaching, not by the Qurʾān’s shared status as God’s speech.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r43] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبي بن كعب | reports | The Prophet asks Ubayy which verse is greatest; Ubayy answers the Throne Verse, and the Prophet praises his knowledge.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r44] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن العربي | reports | Ibn al-ʿArabī argues that Torah, Gospel, and Qurʾān are the best scriptures, so superiority over the best entails superiority over all.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r45] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Al-Fātiḥa’s twenty-five words contain Qurʾānic sciences: praise, attributes, worship, dependence, guidance, and lessons about rejecters.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r46] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Its honor includes being divided between God and servant; worship is not valid without it, like al-Ikhlāṣ equaling a third through comprehensive tawḥīd.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r47] QURTUBI, d. 671 · علي بن أبي طالب | reports | A report attributed to ʿAlī says al-Fātiḥa, the Throne Verse, 3:18, and 3:26 hang at the Throne without a veil. | mentions 2:255, 3:18, 3:26
[QURTUBI:1:1/r48] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Qurṭubī counts twelve names for al-Fātiḥa.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r49] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Al-Fātiḥa is called al-Ṣalāh because the divine report calls it prayer and prayer is not valid without it.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r5] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن سيرين والنخعي | reports | Ibn Sīrīn and al-Nakhaʿī seek refuge in every prayer unit, applying the command generally.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r50] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | It is called Sūrat al-Ḥamd because it contains praise.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r51] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | It is called the Opening of the Book because it begins Qurʾānic recitation, muṣḥaf writing, and prayer.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r52] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الحسن وأنس وابن سيرين | reports | The majority permit Umm al-Kitāb, while al-Ḥasan, Anas, and Ibn Sīrīn reserve it for the whole Qurʾān or Preserved Tablet. | mentions 3:7, 43:4
[QURTUBI:1:1/r53] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبو هريرة | reports | Abū Hurayra transmits the names Umm al-Qurʾān, Umm al-Kitāb, and the seven oft-repeated verses; al-Tirmidhī grades the report ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r54] QURTUBI, d. 671 · يحيى بن يعمر | reports | Yaḥyā ibn Yaʿmar explains Umm al-Qurʾān by analogy with Mecca and Merv as regional “mothers.”
[QURTUBI:1:1/r55] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Umm can mean origin or leading source; al-Fātiḥa’s name may reflect its position and inclusion of Qurʾānic sciences.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r56] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الجمهور | reports | Al-Fātiḥa is called al-Mathānī because it repeats in every prayer unit; another explanation says it was reserved for this community.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r57] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | It is called the magnificent Qurʾān because it contains praise, worship, dependence, supplication for guidance, and lessons about rejecters.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r58] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبو سعيد الخدري | reports | A report from Abū Saʿīd calls al-Fātiḥa a cure for every poison; al-Dārimī transmits it.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r59] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبو سعيد الخدري | reports | The ruqya name is established by Abū Saʿīd’s report of the man who recited al-Fātiḥa over a sick chief.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r6] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبو حنيفة والشافعي | reports | Abū Ḥanīfa and al-Shāfiʿī seek refuge only in the first unit, treating the prayer’s recitation as one reading.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r60] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الشعبي وابن عباس | reports | Al-Shaʿbī recommends al-Fātiḥa for flank pain and transmits Ibn ʿAbbās’s account naming it the basis of the Qurʾān.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r61] QURTUBI, d. 671 · سفيان بن عيينة | reports | Sufyān ibn ʿUyayna calls it al-Wāfiya because it cannot be halved across two prayer units.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r62] QURTUBI, d. 671 · يحيى بن أبي كثير | reports | Yaḥyā ibn Abī Kathīr calls it al-Kāfiya because it suffices in place of other sūras, while none replaces it.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r63] QURTUBI, d. 671 · محمد بن خلاد الإسكندراني | reports | A report attributed to the Prophet says Umm al-Qurʾān replaces other passages, but nothing else replaces it.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r64] QURTUBI, d. 671 · المهلب | reports | Al-Muhallab locates al-Fātiḥa’s ruqya specifically in “You alone we worship and ask for help.”
[QURTUBI:1:1/r65] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | prefers | Qurṭubī says the whole sūra is ruqya: the Prophet asked how it was a ruqya, not how it contained one.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r66] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Calling al-Fātiḥa Umm al-Kitāb or al-Mathānī does not exclude other uses; the Qurʾān itself is called a repeated book. | mentions 39:23
[QURTUBI:1:1/r67] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās identifies the seven mathānī as the seven long sūras; scholars differ whether the seventh is Yūnus or al-Anfāl with al-Tawba. | mentions 2:1, 7:1, 10:1
[QURTUBI:1:1/r68] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Mathānī may denote sūras after the first and before short sūras; al-Anfāl follows the long ones in size, while miʾūn exceed one hundred verses.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r69] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الأمة | holds | The community agrees al-Fātiḥa has seven verses; reports of six or eight are anomalous.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r7] QURTUBI, d. 671 · مالك | reports | Mālik does not prescribe refuge in obligatory prayer but does prescribe it in Ramadan night prayer.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r70] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Al-Fātiḥa is Qurʾān by consensus; Ibn Masʿūd explained its omission from his muṣḥaf as shorthand.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r71] QURTUBI, d. 671 · عبد الله بن مسعود | reports | Asked why he omitted al-Fātiḥa from his muṣḥaf, Ibn Masʿūd said he would have written it before every sūra.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r72] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عباس وقتادة وأبو العالية | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda, and Abū al-ʿĀliya call al-Fātiḥa Meccan; other authorities call it Medinan; some split its revelation. | mentions 15:87
[QURTUBI:1:1/r73] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | prefers | Qurṭubī prefers the Meccan view: al-Ḥijr is Meccan and names seven oft-repeated verses; prayer was prescribed in Mecca. | mentions 15:87
