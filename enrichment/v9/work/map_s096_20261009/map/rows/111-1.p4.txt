[IBNATIYYA:111:1/r7] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · عبد الله بن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masud's reading has “and he has surely perished” (وقد تب) instead of “and he perished” (وتب).
[IBNATIYYA:111:1/r8] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | holds | Abu Lahab is Abd al-Uzza ibn Abd al-Muttalib, the Prophet's paternal uncle.
[IBNATIYYA:111:1/r9] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · author | holds | Despite being the Prophet's uncle, Abu Lahab had already been destined for wretchedness.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p534#2/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | reports | After the command to warn his nearest relatives (26:214), the Prophet called Safiyya and Fatima, saying he could not save them from God and inviting them to ask for his wealth; then he summoned Quraysh at Safa, warned them of punishment, and Abu Lahab cursed him, after which the surah came down. | mentions 26:214
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p534#2/r2] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | Tabbat means she lost; tabab means loss and destruction. The hands stand for earning, gaining, and holding possessions, and the second phrase makes his ruin certain.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p534#2/r3] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · عبد الله بن مسعود | reports | Ibn Mas‘ud’s reading adds qad, ‘already,’ to the statement that Abu Lahab perished.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p534#2/r4] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | Abu Lahab was the Prophet’s paternal uncle, ‘Abd al-‘Uzza ibn ‘Abd al-Muttalib, but had been destined for misery.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p534#2/r5] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | reports | Ibn Kathir and Ibn Muhaysin read Abu Lahab with a silent h; the other readers vocalize it, and all retain fatḥa in ‘a blazing fire.’
[TABRISI:111:3-4/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Abu Lahab curses Muhammad after the Safa warning; the surah is then revealed, and al-Bukhari includes the report in his Sahih.
[TABRISI:111:3-4/r10] TABRISI, d. 548 · مقاتل | reports | Muqatil says Abu Lahab was so named for his beauty and bright face, with cheeks that seemed to flame.
[TABRISI:111:3-4/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · مقاتل | reports | Muqatil explains “perished are Abu Lahab's hands” as his hands and he himself being ruined; hands stand for work because most work is done by them, and the ultimate loss is his soul in the Fire.
[TABRISI:111:3-4/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · قيل | reports | An alternative takes “hand” as an auxiliary expression, like “the hand of time” or “the hand of the year,” with a cited poem as evidence.
[TABRISI:111:3-4/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · قيل | reports | Another interpretation says Abu Lahab's hands were emptied of every good; al-Farraʾ reads the first phrase as a curse and the second as a report that he has perished.
[TABRISI:111:3-4/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · قيل | reports | A further view treats both phrases as reports: his hands never earned good, and he himself was ruined as well; the verse says he perished in every respect.
[TABRISI:111:3-4/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Abu Lahab is Muhammad's paternal uncle, known for intense hostility and opposition; Tariq al-Muharibi recounts seeing him follow Muhammad at Dhu al-Majaz and call him a liar.
[TABRISI:111:3-4/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · طارق المحاربي | reports | Tariq al-Muharibi reports that people identified Muhammad as a prophet and Abu Lahab as his uncle who called him a liar while following him and injuring his legs.
[TABRISI:111:3-4/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | One explanation says God uses Abu Lahab's familiar epithet because it was more commonly used than his personal name.
[TABRISI:111:3-4/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · قيل | reports | Another explanation avoids calling him Abd al-Uzza, since he is God's servant rather than a servant of al-Uzza.
[TABRISI:111:3-5/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن كثير | reports | Ibn Kathir reads the hāʾ in Abu Lahab with sukūn; the other readers pronounce it with fatḥ.
[TABRISI:111:3-5/r12] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The root tabb and the noun tabāb mean ruin that leads to destruction; masad is rope made of palm fiber, with amsād as its plural, as a cited poem shows.
[TABRISI:111:3-5/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو علي | holds | Abu Ali treats Lahab and Lahab as dialect forms like al-shamʿ and al-nahr; the unanimous fatḥ in “dhāt lahab” supports that vocalization.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p256#2/r6] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن عباس | reports | In a report graded by al-Bukhari and Muslim as being in their Ṣaḥīḥayn, Ibn Abbas says the Prophet summoned Quraysh at al-Safa after 26:214, warned them of punishment, and Abu Lahab cursed him and objected; this occasioned the surah. | mentions 26:214
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p259/r1] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | Tabbat means Abu Lahab's hands were ruined, while wa-tabb says that he himself was ruined.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p259/r10] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن قتيبة | reports | Ibn Qutayba transmits from al-Asmaʿi that Abu Amr and Abu Sufyan ibn al-Ala disliked their kunyas; if Abu Lahab's personal name was his kunya, it was the name by which he was known.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p259/r2] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farraʾ reads the first clause as a curse and the second as a report that it came true, like a curse followed by its fulfillment.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p259/r3] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | Mentioning Abu Lahab's two hands stands for his whole self, an Arab usage of naming a part for the whole.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p259/r4] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | He illustrates the part-for-whole expression with the verse about what one's hands have sent ahead. | mentions 22:10
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p259/r5] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · مجاهد | reports | Mujahid interprets the ruined hands and ruin as referring to Abu Lahab's children.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p259/r6] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | Abu Lahab was the Prophet's paternal uncle; it is said his personal name was Abd al-Uzza.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p259/r7] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن كثير | reports | Ibn Kathir alone reads the hāʾ of Abī Lahab with sukūn; Abu Ali likens this to dialect variants in other words.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p259/r8] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | Asked why God used Abu Lahab's honorific, the first answer is that if his name was Abd al-Uzza, naming him that way would invoke a name carrying shirk.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p259/r9] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | A second answer is that many people are known by their kunyas and their personal names are unknown.
[RAZI:111:1/r1] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | After creation for worship and Sūrat al-Kāfirūn’s contrast between Muḥammad’s obedience and the unbelievers’ idolatry, al-Naṣr promises the obedient victory and reward, while al-Masad depicts the disobedient’s worldly loss and punishment hereafter. | mentions 51:56, 109:1, 110:1, 111:1
[RAZI:111:1/r10] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | If an act belongs to faith or is its weakest part, abandoning it brings ruin; Abū Lahab’s abandonment of belief, speech, and action, together with false belief, speech, and action, certainly merits that meaning.
[RAZI:111:1/r11] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | A second explanation is “she lost”: tabāb is loss that ends in destruction, as in the verse saying they increased people only in loss. | mentions 11:101
[RAZI:111:1/r12] RAZI, d. 606 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās explains tabbat as frustration: Abū Lahab’s trusted status once let him turn visitors away from Muḥammad, but his later hostility made his accusations suspect and defeated his aim.
[RAZI:111:1/r13] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | The mention of the hand may reflect Abū Lahab’s habit of putting a hand on visitors’ shoulders and pushing them away while calling Muḥammad mad.
[RAZI:111:1/r14] RAZI, d. 606 · عطاء | reports | ʿAṭāʾ explains tabbat as “was defeated”: Abū Lahab thought his hand was uppermost and expected to expel, humiliate, and overpower Muḥammad.
[RAZI:111:1/r15] RAZI, d. 606 · ابن وثاب | reports | Ibn Waththāb explains the hands as left empty of every good.
[RAZI:111:1/r16] RAZI, d. 606 · طارق المحاربي | reports | Ṭāriq al-Muḥāribī reports seeing Muḥammad preach in the market while a man behind him stoned him until his heels bled and called him a liar; people identified the man as Abū Lahab.
[RAZI:111:1/r17] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | “Hands” can mean the whole person, as in Qurʾānic expressions about what one’s hands have sent ahead or what God’s hands made, and in the proverb “your hands tied it.” | mentions 22:10, 36:71
[RAZI:111:1/r18] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | Other explanations take “his hands” as his religion and worldly and future fortunes, or as the means of bringing benefit and warding off harm.
[RAZI:111:1/r19] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | Another explanation is that one hand is a weapon and the other a shield.
[RAZI:111:1/r2] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | The disparity between people’s worldly fortunes is a test of what God gives; the sinner faces swift punishment, while the obedient receives forgiveness and mercy. | mentions 6:165
[RAZI:111:1/r20] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | A report says Muḥammad visited Abū Lahab at night to invite him again; after a kid goat miraculously praised the Prophet, Abū Lahab tore its hands and cursed it, which occasioned the verse.
[RAZI:111:1/r21] RAZI, d. 606 · محمد بن إسحق | reports | Ibn Isḥāq reports that Abū Lahab mocked Muḥammad’s promises about the afterlife, blew on his empty hands, and said he saw nothing in them.
[RAZI:111:1/r22] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | One analysis makes the first clause an imprecation and the second a factual report that it came true, supported by Ibn Masʿūd’s reading “and he was indeed ruined.” | mentions 80:17
[RAZI:111:1/r23] RAZI, d. 606 · ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masʿūd reads the second clause as “wa-qad tabb,” “and he was indeed ruined,” supporting the factual reading.
