[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p162/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | “We shall inherit” means God will take his actual property and children, not inherit his words; the phrase is an appositional clarification of the object pronoun.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p162/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Inheritance figuratively means dispossession or implies his death; it reminds him that death is near and threatens his destruction.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p162/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | His sons ʿAmr, a Companion, and Hishām, martyred at Ajnādayn, became Muslims; this promises the Prophet good news and gives al-ʿĀṣ grief.
[TABATABAI:19:80#2/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · خباب بن الأرت | reports | The report continues that ʿĀṣ said he would return after death with wealth and children to repay Khabbāb; the author notes similar accounts from al-Ṭabarānī, Saʿīd ibn Manṣūr via al-Ḥasan and an unnamed Companion, and Ibn ʿAbbās via Companions, without grading them.
[TABATABAI:19:80#2/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | The reports do not fit the context: they make the saying a mockery by someone who denied resurrection, whereas the passage answers it with a serious argument.
[TABATABAI:19:80/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | On the author’s preferred reading, the man dies and leaves behind his claim that disbelief would give him wealth and children; it remains attached to him as a sin preserved by God.
[TABATABAI:19:80/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | He comes to God alone, without anything he relied on or used for support, carrying only the statement preserved against him and facing its consequence.
[TABATABAI:19:80/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · الأكثر | reports | On the majority reading, the speaker is ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil or al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra, who swears he will receive wealth and children in Paradise after resurrection; God asks whether he knows the unseen or has a covenant, then strips him of possessions and brings him alone.
[ASAD-NOTES:19:78#n68/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | God inheriting what the man claimed is a metaphor for taking over what once belonged to or was vested in him.
[ASAD-NOTES:19:78#n69/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | Appearing alone means having no external support and relying only on God's grace and mercy. | mentions 6:94, 19:95
[BUKHARI:4735/r1] BUKHARI · خباب | reports | Khabbab reports that al-As ibn Wa'il promised to pay his debt from wealth and children after resurrection; the passage was revealed to reject that boast and foretell punishment and his solitary return to God.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p539/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The response rejects the boast furiously: disbelief brings punishment that increases for arrogance, and on Judgment Day the claimant loses all God-given possessions and appears alone and empty-handed.
[STUDYQURAN:19:79/r3] STUDYQURAN · Ṭ, Ṭs | reports | God inherits the wealth and children the disbeliever claims he will possess, since God is the Inheritor and all things return to Him. | mentions 19:40, 15:23, 21:89, 28:58
[STUDYQURAN:19:79/r4] STUDYQURAN · Ṭ, Ṭs | reports | Coming to God alone means arriving without the wealth and children that brought honor and security in worldly life. | mentions 19:95, 6:94
