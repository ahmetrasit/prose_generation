[TABATABAI:7:172#3/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The two verses mean that God distinguished every descendant, made each testify to their own dependence, and took the covenant of His lordship so humanity cannot plead ignorance or inherited shirk.
[TABATABAI:7:172#3/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · عدة من المفسرين | reports | Several commentators take the time-setting as worldly life: God brings descendants through birth, displays His signs and their total dependence, and they acknowledge Him by the testimony of their state.
[TABATABAI:7:172-173#2/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · القوم | reports | A number of reports are cited for a primordial world of particles in which God extracted Adam's descendants, made them recognize Him, and took their covenant of lordship as proof for Judgment Day.
[TABATABAI:7:172-173#2/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | The first objection says that if the extracted descendants were not rational they could not understand God's address, but if rational they should remember the covenant for it to establish proof; people recall no existence before earthly life.
[TABATABAI:7:172-173#2/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | A second objection deems it implausible that a vast multitude forgot a known event when the Qur'an depicts people in Paradise and Hell recalling worldly events; it says this could otherwise permit the transmigrationists' account of repeated bodies. | mentions 37:51, 38:62
[TABATABAI:7:172-173#2/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | A third objection argues that the wording speaks of descendants from the backs of Adam's children, and the polytheist-ancestor excuse excludes Adam's immediate offspring from the primordial scene. | mentions 7:173
[TABATABAI:7:172-173/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The passage says the covenant and testimony were meant to complete God's proof by closing two excuses people might otherwise raise on Judgment Day for their polytheism.
[TABATABAI:7:172-173/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | If God had not made anyone testify, all people could plead that they were unaware of His lordship, and an unaware person is not liable to punishment.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#10/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم | reports | The Prophet describes right and left groups affirming God's lordship, being mixed, returned to Adam's loins, and assigned their eventual homes; an interlocutor asks why they were mixed.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#10/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم | reports | Asked what people should do, the Prophet replies that each group performs deeds suited to its dwelling.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#10/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The Prophet's phrase about the Throne over water denotes that the covenant-taking preceded what follows, not that souls existed temporally before bodies as many proponents of the atom-world suppose.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#10/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Each eventual home calls for fitting worldly deeds: the people of Paradise do good and those of Hell do evil; inviting people to good remains meaningful even with a settled destination, as 2:148 shows. | mentions 2:148
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#10/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | There is no conflict between bliss or misery being determined relative to complete causes and remaining undetermined relative to human choice, which is itself only a partial cause; the author refers back to 7:29–30. | mentions 7:29, 7:30
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#11/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says God took Adam's covenant, recorded his lifespan, provision, and afflictions, then brought his descendants out like tiny particles and took their covenant too.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#11/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Ibn Abbas's many versions vary in wording but share the extraction of Adam's descendants and their covenant as their core meaning.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#11/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · ابن عباس، ابن مسعود وناس من الصحابة | reports | A report says God drew white descendants from Adam's right and dark descendants from his left, declared their destinations, took their covenant, and made them witnesses so they could not plead ignorance or inherited shirk; it links this to 3:83 and 6:149. | mentions 3:83, 6:149
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#11/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The atom-covenant report is transmitted in both suspended and connected forms from many Companions and Imams through Sunni and Shi'a routes, making a claim of semantic mass-transmission plausible.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#11/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم | reports | Abd al-Rahman ibn Qatada, a Companion, reports the Prophet saying God created Adam and drew creation from his back, some destined for Paradise and some for Hell; the continuation asks what deeds people should perform.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#12/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم | reports | When asked what to do after hearing that some were destined for Paradise and others for Hell, the Prophet answers: act according to the sites of destiny.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#12/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The Prophet's answer does not cancel choice: God decreed outcomes through our choices, so deeds remain ours while occurring in accordance with decree.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#12/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · أبو جعفر عليه السلام | reports | Abu Ja'far explains that the human fitra is innate knowledge of God; he says the descendants were brought from Adam like particles, shown God, and thereby came to know their Lord.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#12/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم | reports | The Prophet says every newborn is born with fitra, meaning knowledge that God is the child's Creator, and the author connects this to the Qur'anic statement that people identify God as creator. | mentions 29:61
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#13/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | Al-Ayyashi transmits the middle of Zurarah's report in the same wording, supporting the author's account of the verse's literal witnessing and address against those who deny it.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#13/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · أبو جعفر عليه السلام | reports | In a version transmitted in al-Ma'ani, Abu Ja'far's wording is that God showed them His creation rather than Himself.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#13/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | Al-Tabatabai suspects that the alternate wording was deliberately paraphrased to avoid an imagined implication of corporeality; he calls the change corrupting and prefers the wording in al-Kafi and al-Ayyashi, which has no connection to corporeality.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#13/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · أبو عبد الله عليه السلام | reports | Zurarah reports that knowledge was fixed in people's hearts, they forgot the covenant scene but will recall it, and otherwise no one would know their Creator and Provider.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#13/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · علي بن الحسين عليه السلام | reports | Ali ibn al-Husayn saw no objection to coitus interruptus, citing the covenant: everyone from whom God took a covenant will emerge, even if conceived on a hard stone.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#13/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | The author notes that the report is also transmitted from Ali ibn al-Husayn by Ibn Abi Shayba and Ibn Jarir, and that a similar report is attributed to the Prophet through Abu Sa'id al-Khudri.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#13/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Because the reports about the primordial realm are so numerous, the author omits most after citing enough to convey their meaning and reserves reports about the prophets' covenant for another section.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#2/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Some reports in the debate are sound or reliable in transmission; the author says he will assess them in the following report discussion.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#2/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · المثبتون | reports | Proponents picture Adam’s seminal substance divided across descendants into atoms, each made the same fully conscious future person, addressed about lordship, then returned to the loins until birth while retaining knowledge but forgetting the event.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#2/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | This atom-person account is impossible and contradicted by Quran and hadith: it makes a tiny bodily particle identical to a later person with that person’s mind and senses, bearing obligations and recompense.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#2/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Human identity is the soul, an entity beyond matter that comes into being with the present body; therefore a particle in Adam’s substance cannot already be the later accountable person.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#3/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Both immediate and inferential knowledge, including assent that God is one’s sustaining Lord, arise through bodily development and ultimately depend on external and internal sensations.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#3/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | If proof requires reason along with recognition, the primordial atom loses reason on returning to the loins; reason is also absent in childhood, disrupting accountability.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#3/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | If recognition alone completes proof, witnessing and covenant-taking seem unnecessary, although the verse presents them as serving that purpose; this collapses into the deniers’ interpretation.
[TABATABAI:7:172-174#3/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | If proof requires testimony, instruction, and covenant together, forgetting them cancels proof; if they merely produce lasting recognition, proof would also bind fetuses, children, the mentally impaired, and the ignorant, contrary to reason and revelation.
