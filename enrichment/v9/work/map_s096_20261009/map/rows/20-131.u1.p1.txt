20:131/q11 What does 'لِنَفْتِنَهُمْ فِيهِ' mean? [لِنَفْتِنَهُمْ فِيهِ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether fitnah is an open test of gratitude and duty, a particular obstacle to faith, or punishment and misguidance.)
  20:131/q11/p1 God tests recipients through favors, exposing gratitude or ingratitude. — reasons: Qatāda and al-Suddī gloss testing; Wāḥidī specifies gratitude. Jishumī says it distinguishes truthful and grateful from false and ungrateful; Islāḥī contrasts favor testing gratitude with hardship testing patience (20:40). Ibn Kathīr says few thank God. Ibn ʿĀshūr says every provision carries a fitting trial and God thereby establishes proof (23:55–56).
  20:131/q11/p2 The trial is the harder religious duty wealth imposes. — reasons: Jishumī lists alms and duties for property, livestock, enslaved men and women, and also spending, pilgrimage and jihad; Kalbī and Muqātil in Rāzī note easier access to pleasure and sin. Ṭabrisī mentions obedience to truth and wealth's rights.
  20:131/q11/p3 The trial is the difficulty wealthy rulers face in following an impoverished Prophet. — reasons: Jishumī reports the hardship of leaving status; Ṭabrisī stresses the contrast in wealth.
  20:131/q11/p4 Their abundance is a punishment, misguidance, or a route to deserved punishment. — reasons: Ibn Baḥr and Abū Muslim name punishment here and hereafter; Ibn ʿAbbās says favors increase disbelief and transgression, preferred by Wāḥidī as fitting unbelievers. Rāzī cites 9:55; Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ālūsī, and Study Quran include punishment in the afterlife. Ṭabrisī cites the saying that the world has not a gnat wing's weight with God.
  20:131/q11/p5 Worldly gifts turn recipients away from their interests. — reasons: Māwardī offers this as a possible explanation; Qushayrī details distraction, arrogance, excess, and disobedience.
20:131/q12 How do the syntax and reading of 'لِنَفْتِنَهُمْ فِيهِ' work? [لِنَفْتِنَهُمْ فِيهِ · grammar] (turns on: The lām's force and fī's figurative location, alongside a reported verbal variant.)
  20:131/q12/p1 The phrase attaches to mattaʿnā; fī figuratively places the trial within each provision, and lām marks consequence. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr compares Pharaoh's household taking Moses, whose eventual enmity was a consequence rather than their aim (28:8); he illustrates figurative fī by 4:5 and Sabrah ibn ʿAmr's verse. He says social causes produce abundance whose outcome is trial. Ālūsī also attaches the phrase to mattaʿnā.
  20:131/q12/p2 Nāfiʿ or ʿĀṣim is reported with ḍamma on the nūn, from أفتنه. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn transmit al-Aṣmaʿī from Nāfiʿ, making a person recipient of trial; Ālūsī transmits from ʿĀṣim and glosses causing trial within it. The transmitter's named reader varies.
20:131/q13 What is 'the provision of your Lord'? [وَرِزْقُ رَبِّكَ · referent] (turns on: Whether rizq names a future reward, a present material provision, contentment, revelation or guidance, or spiritual sustenance.)
  20:131/q13/p1 Paradise or reward stored in the afterlife. — reasons: Yaḥyā, Ṭabarī, Wāḥidī, Muqātil in Ibn al-Jawzī, Baghawī, Jishumī, Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr, and many others identify it as Paradise or reward. Ibn ʿAbbās says resurrection. Ibn Kathīr cites the promise of 93:5; Study Quran compares 28:60, 42:36 and 20:71–73. Asad also allows spiritual sustenance in the hereafter.
  20:131/q13/p2 Lawful or modest sufficient worldly provision joined with obedience. — reasons: Māwardī contrasts lawful lasting provision with abundant transgressive wealth; Qushayrī contrasts lawful little with unlawful much and remembrance with forgetfulness. Kashshāf says illicit wealth is not called provision; Rāzī adds contentment and patience. Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī, and Biqāʿī stress lawful sufficiency.
  20:131/q13/p3 Contentment with one's lot and trust in God. — reasons: Sula­mī transmits Abū Bakr ibn Ṭāhir's renunciation, an anonymous combination of trust and contentment, and Abū ʿUthmān's reliance over striving; Ibn al-Jawzī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Ālūsī report contentment. Study Quran preserves trust alone and trust with contentment.
  20:131/q13/p4 Prophethood, Islam, and divine guidance. — reasons: Kashshāf, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Ālūsī include these; Ṣāḥib al-Kashf prefers this worldly sense as fitting the context. Islāḥī says revealed nourishment cultivates piety.
  20:131/q13/p5 The Qur'an granted to the Prophet. — reasons: Abū Shāmah and al-Sakhāwī say it surpasses others' worldly gifts; Itqān reports the same.
  20:131/q13/p6 Future lands and spoils for believers. — reasons: Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Ālūsī report this worldly promise.
  20:131/q13/p7 A gift or allotted nourishment, including spiritual knowledge and illumination. — reasons: Jishumī gives the broad gift sense; Bursevī lists worldly or otherworldly shares and bodily food, then inward nourishment by knowledge, wisdom, primordial effusion, and manifestation. Qāshānī names afterlife realities and lights.
20:131/q14 Why is the Lord's provision better and more lasting? [خَيْرٌۭ وَأَبْقَىٰ · interpretation] (turns on: Which provision is in view, and whether endurance belongs to the gift itself or its final effect.)
  20:131/q14/p1 Its reward endures while worldly splendor perishes and may harm its owners. — reasons: Yaḥyā says it never ends; Ṭabarī says no depletion. Rāzī contrasts the temporary goods; Qurṭubī links reward to patience; Biqāʿī says God's sufficient lawful provision avoids excess. Ibn ʿĀshūr says comparative khayr weighs benefits against harms and trials, and gratitude preserves benefit; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ surveys baqā opposing perishing across 28:60, 42:36, 55:27, 87:17, 16:96, 18:46 and other passages.
  20:131/q14/p2 Present provision with obedience or knowledge has a better outcome than illicit abundance. — reasons: Qushayrī values God's pleasure or continuing witness; Jishumī says the Prophet's life gift ends well while theirs ends in Fire and is not coveted; Rāzī and Bursevī speak of contented sufficiency. Islāḥī says believers need no patronage from ungrateful elites.
  20:131/q14/p3 The possessive 'your Lord's' honors pure provision. — reasons: Biqāʿī says it signals lawfulness; Ibn ʿĀshūr says disbelievers' sustenance is rhetorically detached because it is mixed with divine anger and ingratitude's effects.
20:131/q15 What occasion of revelation is reported for this verse? [* · reports] (turns on: Whether the narrated pledge marks actual revelation despite the sūra's Meccan setting and the verse's context.)
  20:131/q15/p1 Abū Rāfiʿ says a Jewish seller or lender demanded collateral for flour or food, and the Prophet pledged his armor before the verse consoled him. — reasons: Accounts differ in whether it was sale, loan or food for a guest, whether Rajab was specified, and whether 15:87 and 20:132 also descended. Wāḥidī and Kashshāf give no grading. Suyūṭī uses the report to propose a Medinan exception.
  20:131/q15/p2 The verse belongs to its Meccan context; the collateral incident did not cause its descent. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya argues the sūra is Meccan whereas the pawn occurred late in Medina and the Prophet died with his armor pledged; Qurṭubī reports him. Ṭabāṭabāʾī finds the verse's conclusion incompatible with the story. Ibn ʿĀshūr suggests Abū Rāfiʿ confused recitation of a familiar verse with fresh descent, or that 'descent' meant recitation.
20:131/q16 What reports describe the verse's consolation and its use? [* · reports] (turns on: The separate reported speakers, actions and circumstances.)
  20:131/q16/p1 The Prophet or Ubayy warns that failing to accept God's consolation and watching others' goods prolongs grief. — reasons: Sulamī reports an ungraded Prophetic saying; Māwardī reports a caller's announcement, Ṭabrisī reports the Prophet sitting upright and repeating the consolation, and Ibn al-Jawzī quotes Ubayy. Ubayy adds that counting only food, drink or clothes as blessings diminishes knowledge and invites punishment; Baghawī adds trust in God prevents regret.
  20:131/q16/p2 ʿUrwa recited the verse after seeing rulers' wealth, went home and called his household to prayer. — reasons: Ṭabarī, Jishumī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Rāzī, Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr and Durr preserve variants: seeing rulers, visiting rich people, or hearing reports; some say he recited through wa-abqā and prayed.
  20:131/q16/p3 The Prophet drew away from impressive camels in response to the verse. — reasons: Qurṭubī relates that he covered himself with his garment as he passed the fat, dung-covered camels of Banū al-Muṣṭaliq.
20:131/q17 How does this verse connect to nearby passages? [* · links]
  20:131/q17/p1 It follows the command to bear opponents' words and glorify God, turning the Prophet from their worldly status to prayer and lasting provision. — reasons: Ṭabarī and Rāzī describe the sequence from 20:130; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī connect it to earlier nations and delayed punishment. Jishumī says the prior account of unbelievers leads to their present state; Sulamī's Wāsiṭī links consolation to 20:132; Rāzī connects later objections to the earlier patience command. Kur'an Yolu summarizes the closing sūra as endurance, worship, and a blessed future.
  20:131/q17/p2 It parallels the warning in al-Ḥijr and other contrasts between passing goods and divine reward. — reasons: Wāḥi
