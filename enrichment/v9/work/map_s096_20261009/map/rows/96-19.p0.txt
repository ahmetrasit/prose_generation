[MUQATIL:96:17-19/r2] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | The Prophet is told not to obey Abū Jahl by abandoning prayer; prostration means prayer to God and drawing near means obeying Him.
[MUQATIL:96:9-19/r7] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | The Prophet must not obey the prohibition on prayer, but must prostrate and draw near. Polytheists began with prostration, then bowed, stood, and asked idols; God later changed the Prophet's order to standing, bowing, then prostrating.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمالميقعإلااسماللقبيلة)#543/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Majūs and Yahūd are nouns used only for a tribe, with feminine gender prevailing; Imruʾ al-Qays’s simile ‘like the fire of Majūs’ illustrates the usage.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمالميقعإلااسماللقبيلة)#543/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | When Majūs names a man, it is diptote, just like ʿAmmān.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمالميقعإلااسماللقبيلة)#543/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The definite forms al-Yahūd and al-Majūs use the article as in nisba adjectives, with the nisba yāʾs deleted; without the article, majūs is indefinite, like majūsiyyīn without its article.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمالميقعإلااسماللقبيلة)#543/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Naṣārā is an indefinite plural of naṣrān and naṣrāna; prose normally uses the nisba forms, but the plural omits the nisba yāʾs, like nadāmā from nadmān, and means al-naṣrāniyyīn.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمالميقعإلااسماللقبيلة)#543/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Poetry uses naṣārā as an indefinite; a cited line describes the cupbearer of naṣārā, and another poetic example attests the feminine singular naṣrāna.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمنالجمعبالواووالنونوتكسيرالاسم)#592/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | Al-Khalīl explains tribal plurals in wāw-nūn, such as al-Ashʿarūn and al-Aʿjamūn, as analogical additions alongside broken plurals; the pattern is not productive for every such name.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمنالجمعبالواووالنونوتكسيرالاسم)#592/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | Al-Khalīl treats maqtawī/maqtawīn like al-Ashʿarī/al-Ashʿarīn.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمنالجمعبالواووالنونوتكسيرالاسم)#592/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | One possible account of maqtawīn is retention of the original form, parallel to maqātawa; Abū al-Khaṭṭāb reports this word from Arabs, though not all Arabs know it.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمنالجمعبالواووالنونوتكسيرالاسم)#592/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Alternatively, maqtawīn may follow madharwīn because it has no singular used independently.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمنالجمعبالواووالنونوتكسيرالاسم)#592/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | Al-Khalīl analyzes naṣārā as a plural of naṣrī and naṣrān, like nadmān/nadāmā and mihri/mahārā, with a yāʾ dropped and replaced by alif.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمنالجمعبالواووالنونوتكسيرالاسم)#592/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | prefers | Sībawayh prefers deriving naṣārā from the attested feminine singular naṣrāna, like ashʿath/ashāʿith; he calls this more analogical because naṣrī is unattested.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمنالجمعبالواووالنونوتكسيرالاسم)#592/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Abū al-Akhzar al-Ḥammānī’s verse, which explicitly uses naṣrāna, supports Sībawayh’s analysis of naṣārā as a plural of naṣrāna.
