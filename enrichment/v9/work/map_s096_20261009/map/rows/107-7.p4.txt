[QURTUBI:107:7/r1] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عباس، علي، مالك | reports | The first reported sense is obligatory alms: al-Dahhak transmits it from Ibn Abbas, it is also reported from Ali, and Malik holds it; the referent is hypocrites withholding zakat.
[QURTUBI:107:7/r10] QURTUBI, d. 671 · قيل | reports | A seventh reported interpretation is water and pasture.
[QURTUBI:107:7/r11] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farra says it can mean water alone, quoting an Arab speaker and a line about a cloud pouring forth water.
[QURTUBI:107:7/r12] QURTUBI, d. 671 · عبد الله بن عمر | reports | Abd Allah ibn Umar interprets maʿun as withholding a due right.
[QURTUBI:107:7/r13] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | Al-Tabari transmits from Ibn Abbas that maʿun is the yield from benefits of property, deriving from maʿn, ‘a little.’
[QURTUBI:107:7/r14] QURTUBI, d. 671 · قطرب | reports | Qutrub derives maʿun from scarcity: maʿn means a little, as in the Arab expression ‘he has neither saʿna nor maʿna.’
[QURTUBI:107:7/r15] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Qurtubi explains that zakat, charity, and similar good deeds are called maʿun because they are a little portion of much.
[QURTUBI:107:7/r16] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الجوهري | reports | Al-Jawhari suggests maʿun derives from maʿuna, ‘help,’ with the alif replacing the final hāʾ.
[QURTUBI:107:7/r17] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن العربي | reports | Ibn al-Arabi analyzes maʿun as a passive participle from ‘to help’; help supplies the force, tools, and means that make an undertaking possible.
[QURTUBI:107:7/r18] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash cites a fluent Bedouin using maʿun for obedience and compliance, and supports this sense with a rajaz verse about camels submitting or giving.
[QURTUBI:107:7/r19] QURTUBI, d. 671 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Aisha asks what may not be withheld; the Prophet names water, fire, and salt and describes great reward for sharing each, including water where it is scarce. Al-Thaʿlabi cites the report and Ibn Majah records it; Qurtubi says its chain is weak. | mentions 5:32
[QURTUBI:107:7/r2] QURTUBI, d. 671 · مالك | reports | Malik says the hypocrite performs prayer for show, does not regret missing it, and withholds the zakat God made obligatory; Abu Bakr ibn Abd al-Aziz transmits the report.
[QURTUBI:107:7/r20] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الماوردي | reports | Al-Mawardi offers another possibility: maʿun is assistance that is easy to provide, but God has made its provision burdensome.
[QURTUBI:107:7/r21] QURTUBI, d. 671 · عكرمة | reports | Ikrima says refusing any single item does not warrant woe; woe belongs to one who combines neglecting prayer, showing off, and stinginess with maʿun.
[QURTUBI:107:7/r22] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | prefers | Qurtubi prefers that the passage concerns hypocrites, who combine neglecting prayer, riya, and withholding wealth, as described in 4:142 and 9:54. | mentions 4:142, 9:54
[QURTUBI:107:7/r23] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Qurtubi says a Muslim who displays only one of these traits incurs a corresponding share of blame, especially when withholding aid is obligatory as prayer is obligatory.
[QURTUBI:107:7/r24] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Outside necessity, refusing ordinary assistance is blameworthy as a breach of good character.
[QURTUBI:107:7/r3] QURTUBI, d. 671 · زيد بن أسلم | reports | Zayd ibn Aslam says that if prayer were hidden from hypocrites as zakat is hidden, they would not pray.

[QURTUBI:107:7/r5] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن مسعود، ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Masud and Ibn Abbas interpret maʿun as the household's useful items, such as an axe, pot, or fire.
