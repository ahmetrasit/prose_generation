[ELMALILI:1:6#2/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The second mention of “path” explains the first by apposition, which can be whole or partial; the intended focus remains the apposition, while the first phrase prepares and reinforces the meaning rather than being discarded.
[ELMALILI:1:6#2/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The following appositional clauses clarify the path's essence and attributes: those whom God favored, those not under anger, and those not astray.
[ELMALILI:1:6#2/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The full appositional construction must be considered before the intended meaning and referent of the straight path can be determined.
[ELMALILI:1:6#3/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Because God is Lord of all worlds, His laws operate in every realm; laws may be named for their maker or their subject, but “divine laws” and “God's way” are more accurate for all of them.
[ELMALILI:1:6#3/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Knowing divine laws is science; laws that lead to good are religion, community, or sharia. Seeking religion outside divine ordinance is false, yet not every divine law is religion: the law that a suicide dies is real, but suicide is evil; good-conforming acts are religious, evil-conforming ones contrary to religion.
[ELMALILI:1:6#3/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Human-made laws cannot constitute true science or religion; people should discover and reveal God's laws rather than legislate them. Archimedes, Newton, Aristotle, and Abu Hanifa discovered laws rather than inventing them.
[ELMALILI:1:6#3/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Since divinely established laws are straight, “straight path” also conveys a clear, busy main road for revealing hidden laws and avoiding crooked human inventions unrelated to divine aid.
[ELMALILI:1:6#3/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Guidance's orientation to good does not erase the laws that lead to evil; it frames good laws positively as commands and evil laws negatively as prohibitions, and the following verse's favored/not-angered/not-astray clauses show both aspects. | mentions 1:7
[ELMALILI:1:6#3/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | With the definite article marking the covenant path, verse 6 is a complete definition of the true religion and the following material explains it. | mentions 1:7
[ELMALILI:1:6#4/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | In'am means giving and conveying a blessing; connecting it with “ala” conveys the Benefactor's exaltedness and the blessing's loftiness.
[ELMALILI:1:6#4/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Liberty cannot coherently belong to someone who rejects God or serves another; its guarantee lies in worshipping God alone. The straight path begins in servitude to God, aims first at the great worldly blessing of liberty, rests on life and faith, and depends on divine aid and guidance.
[ELMALILI:1:6#4/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Ni'ma first means a pleasurable state or bliss and then anything that causes it; it relates to softness. A proverb says some have wealth but no enjoyment, like someone with bread who cannot taste it; true blessing includes the ability to enjoy.
[ELMALILI:1:6#4/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | God's blessings cannot be counted; they may broadly be considered as worldly or afterlife blessings. | mentions 14:34, 16:18
[ELMALILI:1:6#4/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Worldly blessings are bestowed or acquired: bestowed gifts include spiritual and bodily capacities, while acquired gifts include moral purification, knowledge, virtue, social standing, and wealth.
[ELMALILI:1:6#4/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Afterlife blessings include forgiveness, attaining God's pleasure, and eternal repose in the highest station with the nearest angels; they too divide into bestowed/acquired and spiritual/physical forms.
[ELMALILI:1:6#4/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | An apparent present blessing may later become a calamity, and an apparent hardship may become happiness; real happiness is what ends safely, so the request is for blessings that bring a safe outcome rather than immediate pleasure.
[ELMALILI:1:6#4/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The qualification that excludes anger and misguidance restricts the potentially all-inclusive favor to lasting good: afterlife blessings and their worldly precursors, especially life, liberty, faith, sound conscience, good character, social rectitude, beneficial knowledge, and righteous action.
[ELMALILI:1:6#4/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | In Islamic usage, liberty means owning one's rights, as explained in Kashf al-Pazdawi; its opposite is enslavement through another's ownership of those rights.
[ELMALILI:1:6#4/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Rights originate in divine ordinance; if human law can alter or control them without consent, a person is partly enslaved and duties depend on another's arbitrary will rather than right.

[ELMALILI:1:6/r12] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Guidance and being guided are related; those already guided ask for steadfastness or a higher degree, while the previously unled ask to attain the basic guidance.
[ELMALILI:1:6/r13] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The definite article on “the path” may mark a known covenant path belonging to God, or the generic class of road that metaphorically applies to God's path.
[ELMALILI:1:6/r14] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Most readings use ṣād as the Quraysh form, smoother and more eloquent due to emphatic sounds; Qunbul from Ibn Kathir and Ruways from Yaqub transmit sīn, while Hamza's ishmām hints at zāy in the Qays dialect (as Abu Hayyan reports).
[ELMALILI:1:6/r15] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Although Arabic “sirat” means a great, busy highway, Turkish speakers know it as the required afterlife bridge over Hell; the text hints at that image and Amr ibn Ubayd transmitted that interpretation, but exegetes take its primary intent as a representative metaphor for truth and Islam.
[ELMALILI:1:6/r16] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Arabic terms distinguish general road (tarīq), traveled road (sabīl), and busy, straight, great, or clear ways (including sirat and sharia); “sirat” therefore evokes sharia.
[ELMALILI:1:6/r17] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Mustaqim means completely straight with no bend; because a road may still slope, the adjective adds flatness, so “straight” alone does not fully convey it, though Turkish also uses it for true or truthful.
[ELMALILI:1:6/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Guidance gently and delicately indicates how to reach a desired destination: it may show the way, take someone along it, or lead them all the way; showing is non-arriving direction (irshad), while taking there is tawfiq.
[ELMALILI:1:6/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Guidance concerns a good goal and gentle method; applying it to an evil destination, as in the direction to Hell, is figurative sarcasm or insinuation. | mentions 37:23
