[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:204/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | One human type speaks attractively but has evil intent and acts as a corrupter and destroyer.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:204/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | Reports connect the revelation to hypocrites who appeared friendly before the Prophet and acted destructively behind his back.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:204/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Râzî | reports | Razi says the verses censure bad qualities rather than particular people, so anyone bearing those qualities deserves censure.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:204/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Even if the verses originally concerned one or several hypocrites in the Prophet’s time, their warning applies to everyone who adopts hypocrisy, ostentation, corruption, or destruction.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:204/r7] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The phrase is rendered here as taking power, though it can also mean leaving the addressee’s presence.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:204/r8] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Hypocritical and ostentatious people flatter others and invoke God as witness, then after leaving or gaining office act from enmity and damage livelihoods and descendants.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:204/r9] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Muhammed Abduh | reports | Abduh reads “destroying crops and offspring” as a metaphor for imposing severe hardships on people to satisfy selfish desires and passions.
[STUDYQURAN:2:204/r1] STUDYQURAN · IK, R, Ṭ | reports | Most cited commentators prefer to read 2:204–7 as universal teaching on hypocrisy, despite reports of specific incidents.
[STUDYQURAN:2:205/r1] STUDYQURAN · Ṭ | reports | Turning away may mean becoming angry or simply leaving the presence of those one is deceiving.
[STUDYQURAN:2:205/r2] STUDYQURAN · some commentators | reports | Some say the verse describes a particular hypocrite who burned fields and hamstrung a donkey.
[STUDYQURAN:2:205/r3] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Tillage denotes crops and agriculture.
[STUDYQURAN:2:205/r4] STUDYQURAN · IK, R, Ṭ | reports | Offspring may mean livestock, or more broadly any offspring including human beings.
[STUDYQURAN:2:205/r5] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Fasād literally means rot and here broadly covers corruption, decadence, and immorality.
[STUDYQURAN:2:205/r6] STUDYQURAN · some Muslims | reports | Some Muslims also apply earthly corruption to social injustice and environmental destruction. | mentions 2:11, 2:30
