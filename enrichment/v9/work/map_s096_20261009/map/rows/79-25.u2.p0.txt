## THE CURRENT MAP
79:25/q01 Who is the one God seized? [فَأَخَذَهُ · referent]
  79:25/q01/p1 Pharaoh. — reasons: Bint al-Shati identifies Moses' Pharaoh.
79:25/q02 What does God’s seizing him convey? [فَأَخَذَهُ · interpretation]
  79:25/q02/p1 God overpowered and punished Pharaoh. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says أخذ literally means taking by hand but figuratively conveys power and triumph; 54:42 and 69:10 show this usage. Tabataba'i likewise calls the taking punishment. Al-Biqa'i emphasizes God's sole sovereignty and irresistible force; Ibn Kathir cites 11:99 and 28:41 for vengeance and deterrence.
79:25/q03 What does نَكَالَ mean? [نَكَالَ · meaning] (turns on: The sense emphasized: exemplary deterrence, fettered humiliation, or incapacity.)
  79:25/q03/p1 A severe exemplary punishment that deters others. — reasons: Jishumi derives it from prevention, comparing shackles and refraining. Al-Layth in Razi cites refusing an oath or a restraining fetter; Razi describes a disgraceful spectacle. Qurtubi, Bursawi, Alusi, Elmalili and Ibn Ashur stress warning witnesses or hearers. Ibn Ashur traces the root to retreat and cites 2:66. Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Samin and Bursawi equate نكال with تنكيل as سلام with تسليم. Bint al-Shati lists root forms at 73:12, 4:84, 2:66 and 5:38; Qurtubi recalls al-Muzzammil.
  79:25/q03/p2 An animal restraint or bridle, extended to humiliation of the fettered. — reasons: Bint al-Shati gives the primary concrete meaning and the extension through degradation.
  79:25/q03/p3 It also connotes weakness and inability. — reasons: Study Quran says God rendered Pharaoh powerless to oppose Moses.
79:25/q04 How is نَكَالَ parsed? [فَأَخَذَهُ نَكَالَ · grammar] (turns on: Which governor or omitted element licenses the accusative; Samin objects to the circumstantial reading.)
  79:25/q04/p1 A cognate accusative governed by أخذ in its sense of punishing. — reasons: Zajjaj, Abu Ishaq and others say 'taking' here means punishing; Wahidi's al-Mubarrad cites 11:102. Ibn Atiyya attributes government by أخذ to Sibawayh; Razi's Zajjaj compares 'a severe leaving'. Ibn Ashur says the accusative specifies the kind of taking. Samin allows figurative usage in verb or noun; Alusi and Elmalili list the parse.
  79:25/q04/p2 An implied verb نكّل governs it, or it confirms the clause. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan attribute the implied verb to al-Mubarrad. Baydawi also supplies one. Zamakhshari calls it a confirming verbal noun, comparing 4:122 and 2:138 in Samin. Abu Hayyan objects that this needs a verb inferred from the clause.
  79:25/q04/p3 An understood noun 'seizure' governs it: a deterrent seizure. — reasons: Farra gives this paraphrase in Razi and Qurtubi; Nahhas and Wahidi report punitive seizure. Alusi lists object of the verb.
  79:25/q04/p4 An object of purpose: God seized him to punish as a warning. — reasons: Samin, Alusi and Elmalili offer this parse.
  79:25/q04/p5 Accusative after an omitted preposition: 'with exemplary punishment'. — reasons: Qurtubi reports it; Alusi includes it.
  79:25/q04/p6 A circumstantial accusative in a participial sense. — reasons: Alusi lists it. Samin calls it weak: نكال is definite and requires an unmeasured interpretation such as جهدك or طاقتك.
79:25/q05 What do ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ and ٱلْأُولَىٰٓ designate? [ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ وَٱلْأُولَىٰٓ · referent] (turns on: Whether the pair names realms, specific boasts or other offenses, periods of life or punishment, or generations; some reports reverse the boasts.)
  79:25/q05/p1 The Hereafter and this world, with Fire there and drowning here. — reasons: Qatada, Hasan, Ibn Zayd and many authors specify drowning and Fire. Al-Dahhak adds casting ashore and later chains above Fire. Study Quran adds the plagues and cites 20:78–79 and 7:135–137; Asad cites 7:137. Al-Biqa'i cites 28:40–42. Ibn Kathir calls this correct; Tabataba'i and Kur'an Yolu prefer it; Bint al-Shati says context gives no signal for two boasts. Ibn Ashur distinguishes worldly literal nakal from figurative Hereafter chastisement, since no one is deterred on Judgment Day; Bursawi says أخذ spans literal worldly and figurative future taking. Tabataba'i says both punishments deter. Bint al-Shati presents punishment in both the afterlife and worldly life as the fate of tyrants.
  79:25/q05/p2 His earlier claim to know no god but himself and later claim to be the highest lord. — reasons: Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrima, Sha'bi and others link 28:38 and 79:24. Qurtubi says punishment was delayed after the first claim but fell for both. Samin says descriptions such as 'statements' are omitted. Tabataba'i calls the view obscure; Bint al-Shati objects that context gives no sign of it.
  79:25/q05/p3 The later offense was the highest-lord claim, the earlier one denial of Moses, disbelief or disobedience. — reasons: Abu Razin and Abu Zayd say prior unbelief and rebellion. Qatada and Study Quran specify denial of Moses. Al-Qaffal identifies rejection after the sign and the boast, preferred by Razi because 79:20–24 names both in order. Tabataba'i finds the story-order reading obscure.
  79:25/q05/p4 The first and last of Pharaoh's deeds or sins, embracing all his offenses. — reasons: Mujahid speaks of first and last deeds, or disbelief and disobedience; Baydawi speaks of deeds in general. Tabataba'i finds this obscure.
  79:25/q05/p5 Affliction at the beginning and end of Pharaoh's life. — reasons: Mawardi and Qurtubi report Mujahid; Abu Hayyan repeats it.
  79:25/q05/p6 The reverse assignment of the two boasts: highest-lord first, sole-divinity claim last. — reasons: Abu Hayyan attributes this to Ibn Abbas; Alusi reports a reversal and an interval of forty years. Ibn Atiyya notes disagreement about their order.
  79:25/q05/p7 Respite first and Hell last. — reasons: Mawardi reports al-Rabi and cites 40:46.
  79:25/q05/p8 Drowning at the beginning of a day and Fire at its end. — reasons: Ibn Jawzi reports al-Rabi ibn Anas.
  79:25/q05/p9 His deed for the afterlife and his deed in the earlier life. — reasons: Tabari reports this gloss from Mujahid.
  79:25/q05/p10 A lesson to former and latter generations. — reasons: Razi describes the turn from Pharaoh's deeds and words to his fate as a lesson to generations.
79:25/q06 What does the genitive relation to the paired terms mean? [نَكَالَ ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ وَٱلْأُولَىٰٓ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the construction locates punishment or identifies its causes.)
  79:25/q06/p1 Punishment 'in' the two abodes. — reasons: Ibn Ashur explicitly gives this sense; Alusi allows it, and Bursawi says both abodes are named because punishment occurs in each.
  79:25/q06/p2 Punishment 'for' the two offenses. — reasons: Alusi calls this annexation of effect to cause and also notes a 'for' sense.
  79:25/q06/p3 An understood noun such as 'statements' accompanies the adjectives. — reasons: Samin gives this on the two-boasts reading.
79:25/q07 How much time separated Pharaoh's claims? [ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ وَٱلْأُولَىٰٓ · reports] (turns on: The number and whether it measures between claims or after the final claim.)
  79:25/q07/p1 Forty years. — reasons: Muqatil, Ibn Abbas, al-Sha'bi, Abdullah ibn Amr, Khaythama and others give forty between claims. Razi says he was spared forty years after the first. Ibn Atiyya records another report measuring forty years after the highest-lord claim.
  79:25/q07/p2 Thirty years. — reasons: Mawardi attributes the interval to Mujahid and al-Suddi; Ibn Jawzi reports from al-Suddi thirty years after the later claim. Nasafi lists thirty.
  79:25/q07/p3 Twenty years. — reasons: Nasafi lists twenty among reported numbers.
79:25/q08 Why does the Hereafter come before the first life? [ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ وَٱلْأُولَىٰٓ · rhetoric] (turns on: Semantic emphasis versus matching the verse endings.)
  79:25/q08/p1 Its punishment is greater, more severe and lasting. — reasons: Al-Biqa'i says the order emphasizes severity and explicitly rejects rhyme as explanation; Ibn Ashur and Bint al-Shati agree. Bint al-Shati Ijaz calls the order semantically necessary to the threat, comparing 93:4–5, and argues that verse endings join sound to meaning, never forcing word order for ornament.
  79:25/q08/p2 The order matches the verse endings. — reasons: Burhan compares 53:25. Al-Biqa'i and Bint al-Shati contest a rhyme-only cause.
79:25/q09 How can a past-tense seizure include future punishment? [فَأَخَذَهُ ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ · rhetoric]
  79:25/q09/p1 The future is treated as already begun after death. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says Pharaoh begins tasting punishment and sees his final destination after death, as a hadith relates.
79:25/q10 What does punishment in both abodes teach? [* · theology]
  79:25/q10/p1 Punishment in one abode does not exclude punishment in the other. — reasons: Jishumi states the principle. Alusi notes that those accepting Pharaoh's faith instead confine the punishment to worldly drowning.
  79:25/q10/p2 Tyrants meet punishment in both the afterlife and worldly life. — reasons: Bint al-Shati presents Pharaoh's punishment in both as the fate of tyrants.
79:25/q11 What reports explain Pharaoh's claim and delayed punishment? [* · reports] (turns on: The reports address different aspects of the claim and delay.)
  79:25/q11/p1 Pharaoh asked protection from a snake despite claiming divinity. — reasons: Tabrisi's anonymous account says his fear and plea for help disprove divinity.
  79:25/q11/p2 He was spared four hundred years because God repaid his courtesy and hospitality. — reasons: Tabrisi reports Ibn Abbas's account of Moses asking why a blasphemer was spared so long.
  79:25/q11/p3 God said only one afraid of missing out makes Pharaoh's claim. — reasons: Tabrisi transmits through Abu Ja'far a Prophetic report about Gabriel's question.
79:25/q12 How does Pharaoh's fate fit the sura and other passages? [* · links]
  79:25/q12/p1 It consoles Muhammad, urges patience, warns deniers and supports resurrection. — reasons: Tabrisi connects the Moses account to denier
