20:13/q11 What does 'listen' ask Moses to do? [فَٱسْتَمِعْ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the command is explained chiefly as concentrated hearing or as obedient action flowing from it.)
  20:13/q11/p1 Attend silently and intently to the revelation, ready to understand it. — reasons: Ibn al-Anbārī glosses listening as silence and attention. Al-Rāzī says Moses must ready his whole mind for a grave matter; choice gives hope and mercy while the command gives awe and fear. Al-Ṭabrisī adds careful, steady attention. Qurṭubī calls attentive listening a discipline that brings understanding, citing 39:18, 17:47 and 7:204; Sufyān b. ʿUyayna orders listening before understanding, memorizing, acting and teaching, and says sincere listening brings light. Wahb specifies still limbs, lowered gaze, attentive hearing, present mind and intent to understand and act. Al-Iṣlāḥī says attention focuses Moses for revelation. Al-Ālūsī says the command signals the greatness of the revelation and the need to prepare for it; he reports Wahb's account of still limbs, lowered gaze, focused hearing and present understanding. Ibn ʿĀshūr says istamiʿ can convey aṣghi, attentive listening.
  20:13/q11/p2 Listen so as to carry out the revealed commands and avoid prohibitions. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī explicitly interprets listening as acting on God's commands and prohibitions; Wahb includes a resolve to act in proper listening, and al-Bursawī says Moses listens to revealed commands and prohibitions. Al-Ālūsī reports Wahb's inclusion of a resolve to act on what God loves in attentive listening.
20:13/q12 How did Moses physically listen at this moment? [فَٱسْتَمِعْ · reports]
  20:13/q12/p1 He stood at or leaned on a stone, hands folded, chin lowered, dressed in wool. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya transmits Abū al-Faḍl al-Jawharī; Qurṭubī cites Ibn ʿAṭiyya's father citing al-Jawharī; Abū Ḥayyān repeats the account. The reports vary slightly between standing against a stone, standing by one and leaning on another, and leaning on a stone. Al-Ālūsī reports Abū al-Faḍl al-Jawharī's details that Moses stood on one stone, leaned on another, placed his right hand over his left, lowered his chin and listened intently.
20:13/q13 What is Moses to listen to? [لِمَا يُوحَىٰٓ · referent] (turns on: Whether the phrase points specifically to the declaration immediately following or to the revelation and instructions more broadly.)
  20:13/q13/p1 God's declaration of exclusive divinity and the command to worship Him in 20:14. — reasons: Muqātil calls it God's declaration of oneness; Ibn al-Jawzī quotes 'I am God; there is no god but Me, so worship Me'; Abū Ḥayyān likewise identifies God's declaration of exclusive divinity.
  20:13/q13/p2 The revelation now being spoken to Moses, including its commands and prohibitions. — reasons: Yaḥyā, al-Baghawī and Ibn Kathīr identify the revelation being given now; al-Bursawī specifies commands and prohibitions, while al-Wāḥidī says listening includes acting on both. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the immediate object as the revelation then being delivered and says listening to later revelations follows a fortiori.
20:13/q14 Does mā mean revealed content or the act of revelation? [لِمَا يُوحَىٰٓ · grammar] (turns on: Whether mā is a relative pronoun or a verbal-noun particle.)
  20:13/q14/p1 A relative pronoun: that which is revealed. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī offer this as one sense; Abū Ḥayyān favors it as the natural meaning; al-Samīn identifies the relative-pronoun parsing. Al-Ālūsī reports the relative reading of mā as 'what is revealed' within the independent-address analysis.
  20:13/q14/p2 A verbal noun: revelation itself. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī offer revelation itself as the alternative; al-Samīn calls this the verbal-noun parsing. Al-Ālūsī also reports the verbal-noun reading, 'revelation,' within that analysis.
20:13/q15 Which verb governs the lām phrase 'to what is revealed'? [ٱخْتَرْتُكَ فَٱسْتَمِعْ لِمَا يُوحَىٰٓ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the phrase is governed by the command to listen or by the verb of selection; the objection to technical dual government is the missing repeated pronoun.)
  20:13/q15/p1 It attaches to 'listen'; the lām can function as an added preposition before its object. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Samīn offer this attachment; al-Samīn prefers it as apparent, as does Abū Ḥayyān. Al-Bursawī calls the lām added before the object, comparing radifa lakum in 27:72; al-Samīn also cites 27:72 for an extra lām. Abū Ḥayyān argues that assigning technical government to 'chose' violates the two-governor rule without a repeated pronoun; al-Samīn reports his expected form as fastamiʿ lahu limā yūḥā. Al-Ālūsī cites this objection but also reports a reply allowing alternative coordination. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the lām reinforces istamiʿ's link to its object; alternatively, taking istamiʿ in the sense of aṣghi explains the lām with attentive listening.
  20:13/q15/p2 It can attach to 'I chose you': Moses was chosen for revelation and must listen to it. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī allow the attachment; al-Samīn reports al-Zamakhsharī and others as describing tanāzuʿ, but clarifies they mean semantic suitability rather than shared technical government. Al-Ṭabrisī reports Abū ʿAlī's special parsing of the plural as wa-li-annā ikhtarnāka, making that prepositional phrase the object of 'listen.' Al-Ālūsī records a reply permitting either attachment by alternative coordination. Ibn ʿĀshūr offers the attachment to 'I chose you for revelation' and treats the intervening command as parenthetical.
20:13/q16 Why is 'is revealed' passive? [يُوحَىٰٓ · rhetoric]
  20:13/q16/p1 Its agent is already known, and the passive suits the verse ending. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān says the known agent is suppressed and the passive fits the verse's final sound. Al-Ālūsī adds that an active verb would not supply the verse ending furnished by the passive.
20:13/q17 How does the call unfold across the following verses? [* · links]
  20:13/q17/p1 The passage first states Moses' prophethood, then develops his mission through the staff question and command to go to Pharaoh. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the next eleven verses as containing both prophecy and mission; the mission begins with the staff question and ends with the order to approach Pharaoh, and 19:51 names Moses both prophet and messenger.
  20:13/q17/p2 The call begins with kindly choice and ends with a severe warning, teaching that mercy precedes wrath and the servant lives between hope and fear. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī contrasts the opening choice in 20:13 with the closing warning in 20:16 and draws the lessons of mercy before wrath and hope alongside fear.
  20:13/q17/p3 The address reasons from God's identity and oneness to worship and prayer, then the Hour and requital, warning against deniers, and the ruin that follows disobedience. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr traces how each command and prohibition in the address follows from the preceding point.
20:13/q18 Can Moses' call be God's revelation if a veil or intermediary is involved? [* · theology]
  20:13/q18/p1 Yes; attention passes beyond the unseen intermediary, so Moses experiences the message as God's. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares passages describing a veil or intermediary in Moses' call, citing 19:52 and 28:30 along with 20:13.
20:13/q19 What adjacent reports concern God's speech to Moses? [* · reports]
  20:13/q19/p1 God spoke to Moses twice, while Muhammad saw his Lord twice and Adam disobeyed twice. — reasons: Muqātil reports this from Kaʿb without grading it.
20:13/q20 What is reported about a partridge's cry? [* · reports]
  20:13/q20/p1 Its cry says, 'The Merciful rose over the Throne.' — reasons: Muqātil reports this from ʿAbd al-Ḥamīd b. Yūsuf without grading it.
20:13/q21 What does the adjacent divine istiwāʾ mean? [* · theology] (turns on: Whether istiwāʾ denotes power or a distinct act or attribute.)
  20:13/q21/p1 It means power. — reasons: Al-Jishumī prefers Abū ʿAlī's explanation as sounder and calls the Kullābī account unintelligible.
  20:13/q21/p2 It is an act or attribute called istiwāʾ. — reasons: Al-Jishumī attributes this account to the Kullābīs and argues against its intelligibility.
20:13/q22 How is the nearby command to establish prayer for remembrance understood? [* · interpretation] (turns on: Whether remembrance is within prayer, its entry, or recall of a forgotten prayer.)
  20:13/q22/p1 Remember God during prayer. — reasons: Al-Māwardī reports Mujāhid's explanation.
  20:13/q22/p2 Begin prayer by remembering God, since prayer is entered only through His remembrance. — reasons: Al-Māwardī reports this as another interpretation.
  20:13/q22/p3 Pray when one remembers a missed prayer. — reasons: Al-Māwardī reports Ibrāhīm's interpretation and Saʿīd b. al-Musayyib's Prophetic report that whoever forgets a prayer should perform it on remembering.
20:13/q23 Does God's direct speech to Moses establish his superiority over Muhammad? [ٱخْتَرْتُكَ · theology]
  20:13/q23/p1 No; God addressed Muhammad directly on the Night Journey, unveiled and with a vision that Moses was not granted. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī argues from the direct address to Muhammad on the Night Journey while discussing God's speech to Moses; he mentions 4:164 and 17:1.
20:13/q24 What does the predication in 'I chose you' accomplish? [وَأَنَا ٱخْتَرْتُكَ · rhetoric]
  20:13/q24/p1 It reinforces the surprising announcement of choice so Moses will not doubt it; it does not restrict the assertion. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr explains that verbal predication strengthens unexpected news without making it exclusive.
