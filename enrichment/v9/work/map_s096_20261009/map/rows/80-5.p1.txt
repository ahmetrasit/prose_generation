[TABATABAI:80:5-12/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | The proposed honorific reading of the switch from third to second person does not fit the direct address that follows, whose rebuke is stronger and has no conciliatory intimacy.

[TABATABAI:80:5-12/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The self-sufficient person is the wealthy one whose status and grandeur in others' eyes foster arrogance toward the truth. | mentions 96:7, 96:8
[TABATABAI:80:5-12/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The rebuked pattern is that the Prophet attends to wealthy people who scorn the truth, unconcerned whether they purify themselves, while neglecting the person earnestly seeking purification and fearing God.
[ASAD-NOTES:80:3#n2/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | The self-sufficient person is, in Asad's reading, someone who feels no need for divine guidance, especially the arrogant pagan chiefs addressed by the Prophet.
[CORPUSCORANICUM:92:8:anmerkung/r1] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | holds | Istaġnā is an antonym of ittaqā and appears near taqwā in 96:7; in 80:5 the positive counterpart is piety in 80:9. | mentions 92:5, 96:7, 80:5, 80:9
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p169/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | ʿAbasa pairs with al-Nāziʿāt and shares its central theme, discourse style, and reasoning; the changed sequence of topics gives this sūrah its distinct majesty. | mentions 79:45
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p169/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Though the Prophet is addressed in the rebuke, its intended target is the haughty leaders who ignore his message and want poor companions excluded to secure their own attendance.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p169/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The Prophet's responsibility is to teach those who come eagerly, including devoted companions, rather than pamper absent leaders; he is not accountable for those who refuse to come.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p173/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | God used the accidental incident to teach the Prophet to focus on companions eager for reform, rather than pampering indifferent people.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p173/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The Prophet initially concentrated on Quraysh because he was directed to warn his near family, and their conversion could open Arabia to Islam. | mentions 26:214
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p173/r5] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | After Quraysh’s arrogance made reform unlikely, pursuing them cost time better spent educating poor believers, so the incident enabled a change in strategy.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p175/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The Prophet’s devotion to persuading Quraysh leaders led him to neglect his own comfort and risk leaving little time to teach poor believers; he had assumed more than God required.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p176/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Only God-fearing people who seek reform merit the Prophet’s attention and constitute his responsibility.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p177/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The indifferent are absorbed in worldly pleasures and do not consider the afterlife, whereas those who fear meeting God are receptive; the Prophet pursued the former while neglecting the latter.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:80:1/r7] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The translation “one who considers himself wholly self-sufficient” describes the typical wealthy Meccan notables and tribal chiefs.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:80:1/r8] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Proud of their wealth and followers, these leaders persisted in disbelief and claimed or acted as if they did not need guidance from God and His Messenger.
[STUDYQURAN:80:1/r4] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Istaghna literally means self-sufficient and connotes seeing oneself as self-sufficient in relation to God.
