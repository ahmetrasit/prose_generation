  1:2/q16/p3 It is the Mother of the Qurʾān because it gathers its aims. — reasons: RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): It is called the Mother of the Qur’an because its verses encompass the Qur’an’s four central aims: theology, resurrection, prophethood, and divine decree. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): A second reason for calling it the Mother of the Book is that all revelation comprises praise, worshipful service, and requests for unveiling; the sura includes each in that order. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): As the Mother of the Qur’an, the Fātiḥa is the source from which other suras branch; every being besides God is evidence of His divinity. QURTUBI, d. 671 (author): One derivation traces Rabb to tarbiya, since God manages and nurtures creation; the stepdaughter is called rabība because her mother’s husband raises her. In this sense it is an attribute of action, while owner or master denotes an attribute of essence. | mentions 4:23 BIQAI-FULL, d. 885 (author): A surah's name summarizes its purpose, as a title signals the details within; Adam demonstrated this naming principle when he named things before the angels. BIQAI-FULL, d. 885 (author): The Fatiha's many titles converge on مراقبة: it is the Mother of the Book, foundation, oft-repeated, treasure, healing, sufficing, complete, protecting, incantation, praise, thanks, supplication and prayer. BIQAI-FULL, d. 885 (الحرالي): Al-Harali says the Qur'an's sevenfold gathering in Muhammad's heart relates to the Mother of Qur'an's universals as the Qur'an's gathering in his heart relates to the Mother. ITQAN, d. 911 (الإمام فخر الدين): Fakhr al-Din maps the Qur’an’s four aims—God, resurrection, prophethood, and divine decree—onto the Fatiha: praise, Judgment Day, worship and reliance, and guidance through the surah’s end; this supports its name Umm al-Qur’an. | mentions 1:2, 1:4, 1:6, 1:7 ITQAN, d. 911 (علي): Ali said, as transmitted by Ibn Abi Jamra, that he could load seventy camels with commentary on the Umm al-Qur’an; the source explains the remark by showing how much each phrase requires. BURSEVI, d. 1127 (author): Fatiha is the Mother of the Qur'an because it gathers the Qur'an's purposes: affirming divinity and prophethood, and establishing decree and predestination. IBNASHUR, d. 1393 (author): The surah's preparation rejects atheism and materialism, polytheism, stubbornness, and errors corrupting sound knowledge or true law; this is why it is called the Mother of the Quran. | mentions 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7 IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 (author): The names Umm al-Qur'an and Umm al-Kitab are established in the Sunna; “mother” denotes a thing's origin and source. IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 (أبو سعيد الخدري): A report in Sahih al-Bukhari says Abu Sa'id al-Khudri recited Umm al-Qur'an over a person suffering from a sting as a healing prayer. IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 (النبيء صلى الله عليه وسلم): A sound hadith calls a prayer without Umm al-Qur'an deficient. IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 (author): One explanation of Umm al-Qur'an is that it begins and opens the Qur'an, making it like its origin. IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 (author): A third explanation of Umm al-Qur'an is that it contains the Qur'an's theoretical wisdom and practical rulings, including theology, prophecy, admonition, worship, transactions, morals, and law. IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 (author): These aims help explain why al-Fatiha is called Umm al-Qur'an. SAMARRAI-LAMASAT (author): The praise covers time from eternity to eternity, echoed by praise in the first and last life and at the end of judgment; this fullness makes al-Fatiha worthy to be the Mother of the Book. | mentions 28:70, 39:75, 10:10 SAMARRAI-LAMASAT (الرازي): The surah is truly the Mother of the Book because it contains both doctrinal foundations and the two pillars of action. SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT (author): Al-Fatiha contains faith and good works, affirming God, the Last Day, angels, messengers, and scriptures, and joins lordship's unity to the unity of worship; it is therefore the Mother of the Book. STUDYQURAN (author): The sūrah is seen as a synthesis and the Quran’s most important chapter, hence Umm al-kitāb; the title also refers to 3:7 and the celestial archetype of scripture. | mentions 3:7, 13:39, 43:4 STUDYQURAN (author): It is called the Mother of the Quran because it contains the meaning of the entire Quran.   1:2/q16/p4 It is named for its role in prayer. — reasons: RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): Many names indicate the honor of the named; Fātiḥat al-Kitāb is so called because it opens codices, teaching, recitation, and prayer, or because praise opens speech, or because it was the first sura sent down. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): The prophetic statement “no prayer without the Opening of the Book” directly supports the requirement. QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 (رسول الله [ ص ]): The Prophet says prayer is invalid without reciting the Opening of the Book; the report is in both Sahih collections. STUDYQURAN (author): Al-Fātiḥah means The Opening: it opens the Book, each prayer cycle, many everyday acts, and may open the breast to faith.
  1:2/q16/p5 It is the Seven Oft-Repeated through its verses and repeated recitation. — reasons: BURSEVI, d. 1127 (author): It is called the seven oft-repeated because it has seven verses, each can stand for seven Qur'anic portions, and reciting them is said to earn the whole Qur'an's reward or close seven gates of Hell. BURSEVI, d. 1127 (author): The name al-mathani is explained by repetition in prayer or each rak'a, intercession in each rak'a, or revelation twice, in Mecca and Medina. IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 (author): On this reading, mathani in al-Fatiha resembles the repeated narratives and themes in the Qur'an called mathani in al-Zumar. | mentions 39:23 IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 (author): Mathani is a plural of muthanna, with alternative vocalized or lightened forms; al-Jurjani notes that each can also be feminine. IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 (author): A further view takes mathani to mean repeated in prayer, citing Arabic use of the dual for repeated action. 1:2/q17 Must the Fātiḥa be recited in prayer? [* · law] (turns on: The jurists differ over whether this particular sūra is necessary for valid prayer.)
  1:2/q17/p1 The Fātiḥa is required in prayer. — reasons: TABRISI, d. 548 (زيد بن علي): Zayd ibn Ali's fatḥa on the bā of rabb is understood as demonstrating that the form is possible, not as a recitation. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): Many names indicate the honor of the named; Fātiḥat al-Kitāb is so called because it opens codices, teaching, recitation, and prayer, or because praise opens speech, or because it was the first sura sent down. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): The command to perform the dawn recitation in Quran 17:78 is a command to read and therefore makes recitation obligatory. | mentions 17:78 RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (أبو الدرداء عن النبي): A man asked the Prophet whether prayer includes recitation; when the man concluded that it was obligatory, the Prophet approved him. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (ابن مسعود عن النبي): In another report, the Prophet answers a question about reciting in prayer by asking whether there can be prayer without recitation. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (الأصم): Al-Asamm argues that the Prophet described prayer as things seen, whereas recitation is not visible and must therefore fall outside prayer; Razi answers that seeing with two objects means knowing. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): The prayer's two halves arise through the verses of al-Fatiha; since prayer entails this division and it entails this surah, al-Fatiha is necessary for valid prayer. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): If al-Fatiha is a constituent of prayer, omitting it means the prayer's very essence does not exist, so the hadith's negation can remain literal. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): Because the established form of the Prophet's prayer included al-Fatiha, the command to establish prayer requires it; the command recurs over a hundred times in the Quran. | mentions 2:43 RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): The rightly guided caliphs consistently recited al-Fatiha; reports in both Sahihs say the Prophet, Abu Bakr, and Umar began recitation with al-Hamd, so their practice should be followed. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): Muslims everywhere recite al-Fatiha in prayer; even if some regard it as recommended rather than obligatory, abandoning the unanimous practice leaves the believers' path and incurs Quran 4:115's warning. | mentions 4:115 RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): The Prophet identifies al-Fatiha as a surah unmatched in the Torah, Gospel, or Psalms; the Companions' answer about what they recite in prayer indicates a known universal practice.
