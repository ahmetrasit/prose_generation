[BINTSHATI:v2p16/r5] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الفخر الرازي | reports | Al-Razi says Rabbika comforts the Prophet: God has nurtured him from an embryo into a knowing believer, so he should neither fear nor expect abandonment; the command also obliges him to act.
[BINTSHATI:v2p16/r6] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | prefers | She centers Rabbika on the Prophet’s long search for guidance: the Creator revealed Himself as the Lord worthy of worship over created Arabian idols.
[BINTSHATI:v2p16/r7] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | rejects | She rejects supplying an unstated object for خلق and keeps the first verse’s creation unqualified; the following verse then specifies the human being.
[BINTSHATI:v2p17/r1] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | The first verse states creation generally, while the next specifies the human, recipient of revelation and the one uniquely able to read, learn, and bear religious responsibility.
[BINTSHATI:v2p17/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · بعض المفسرين | reports | Al-Razi transmits an argument that naming God as Creator leads idolaters gradually to acknowledge Him: they know they did not create themselves and that their handmade idols did not create them.
[BINTSHATI:v2p17/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | The context plainly asserts the Creator’s lordship; humans are singled out because they read, learn, bear obligations, and receive the revealed words.
[BINTSHATI:v2p18/r1] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · أبو حيان | reports | Abu Hayyan says Rabbuka comforts Muhammad and identifies his exclusive Lord; the Creator attribute follows because Arabs called idols lords, though idols cannot create.
[BINTSHATI:v2p20/r1] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · بعض المفسرين | reports | Some exegetes, as al-Razi transmits, say the first اقرأ is for the Prophet himself and the second for proclamation to others.
[BINTSHATI:v2p20/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · بعض المفسرين | reports | A second reported interpretation makes the first command to read for learning and the second to teach.
[BINTSHATI:v2p20/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · بعض المفسرين | reports | A third reported interpretation says the first recitation is in prayer and the second outside prayer.
[BINTSHATI:v2p20/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | prefers | She prefers the straightforward context: the command to recite is reiterated, and revelation specifies what the Prophet should read in his Lord’s name.
