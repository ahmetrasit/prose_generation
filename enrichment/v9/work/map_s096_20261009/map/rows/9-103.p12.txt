<<9:103 notes, part 12 of 0..12>>
Tabuk, some people brought their property for purification and distribution and asked the Prophet to pray for forgiveness; he initially declined, and the verse then came down.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:9:103/r10] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Zakat literally includes increase, growth, purification, praise, and blessing; technically it is the prescribed share taken from qualifying wealth for Qur'anic recipients, and also the act of removing that share.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:9:103/r11] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The Qur'an's frequent pairing of zakat with prayer signals their strong connection; zakat is worship with moral and economic aims that benefits both the giver and society.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:9:103/r12] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Qur'an 9:60 lists zakat recipients, while Prophetic words and practice clarify eligible payers and assets, thresholds, amounts, timing, and payment method; jurists differ on some details. | mentions 9:60
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:9:103/r13] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The prevailing view makes the Prophet the subject of both purification verbs; if the first verb instead describes charity, the verse means taking charity that purifies them.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:9:103/r14] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Tathir removes the harmful effects sins leave on people, while tazkiya means cleansing thoroughly and increasing blessing.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:9:103/r15] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The Prophet's prayer for them means praying for them and asking forgiveness for their sins.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:9:103/r16] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | His prayer is explained as mercy that assures them their repentance is accepted, brings peace to their hearts, and honors them.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:9:103/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · âlimlerin bir kısmı | reports | Some scholars, following the occasion report, understand the charity here as voluntary donations taken to expiate sins.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:9:103/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · bazı âlimler | reports | Other scholars reading the same report say the repentant people already owed zakat and asked the Prophet to accept the zakat they had previously withheld.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:9:103/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Fakihlerin çoğunluğu | reports | Most jurists treat the verse as the start of a new ruling that addresses collecting obligatory zakat.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:9:103/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | From early Mecca, the Qur'an linked belief in God's oneness with establishing socioeconomic balance and first built social awareness before setting detailed financial rules.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:9:103/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Eight Meccan verses use zakat, but not for the later fixed-threshold, fixed-rate Medinan levy; zakat was still a general duty whose amount Muslims set to their circumstances.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:9:103/r7] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | After the Hijra, financial duties received fuller treatment: zakat appears as a definite noun in 22 more Medinan verses, while sadaqa, generally treated as synonymous, occurs in 12 Medinan verses.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:9:103/r8] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The fixed-threshold zakat was probably made obligatory in year two after the Ramadan fast, though this dating is uncertain.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:9:103/r9] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The view that zakat became obligatory in year nine can be understood as dating the start of its regular state collection and distribution.
[QIRAAT-ER:9:103:13#1/r1] QIRAAT-ER · Lvw | reports | The plural ṣalawāt reading presents the Prophet's prayers as multiple invocations or an ongoing supplicatory practice.
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r5#10/r1] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · الكرماني | reports | Al-Karmani contrasts hypocrites, whose inward states only God knows (with the Prophet informed by Him), with believers, whose outward obedience is seen by God, the Messenger, and fellow believers.
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r5#10/r4] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · درة التنزيل | reports | The cited reading says the Prophet was commanded to have believers perform visible duties; unlike concealed hypocrisy, these deeds can be seen by God, the Messenger, and believers, which explains naming the latter witnesses.
[STUDYQURAN:9:103/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | A report connects the verse with the repentant men of 9:102: after they offered their wealth, the Prophet initially said he had not been commanded to accept it; revelation then directed them to give a third as expiation. | mentions 9:102
[STUDYQURAN:9:103/r10] STUDYQURAN · commentators | reports | The Prophet's blessings comfort people because hearing his prayers makes them glad and God's response brings peace to their hearts.
[STUDYQURAN:9:103/r11] STUDYQURAN · commentators | reports | Another explanation is that the Prophet's blessings are a mercy for the people.
[STUDYQURAN:9:103/r12] STUDYQURAN · commentators | reports | Many hold that this form of blessing is restricted to the Prophet: Muslims invoke blessings only on him, and only he may bless others.
[STUDYQURAN:9:103/r13] STUDYQURAN · al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī | rejects | Al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī reject restricting this form of blessing to the Prophet because he is an exemplar for all actions.
[STUDYQURAN:9:103/r14] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Muslims invoke blessings and peace upon Muhammad in prayer and when his name is mentioned, but invoke only peace for other prophets.
[STUDYQURAN:9:103/r2] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | Some Arabian tribes after the Prophet's death used this interpretation to argue that alms were owed only to him personally and refused to give them to Abū Bakr.
[STUDYQURAN:9:103/r3] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | On the first interpretation, the charitable offering is expiation charity.
[STUDYQURAN:9:103/r4] STUDYQURAN · commentators | reports | The other main opinion takes the offering as institutional obligatory alms, usually called zakāh.
[STUDYQURAN:9:103/r5] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | This verse cannot restrict obligatory alms to payments made directly to the Prophet, since other verses specify recipients such as the poor, indigent, beggar, and deprived. | mentions 9:60, 51:19
[STUDYQURAN:9:103/r6] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The Quran also describes messengers as purifying people, often together with teaching the Book and imparting knowledge. | mentions 2:129, 2:151, 3:164, 62:2
[STUDYQURAN:9:103/r7] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Giving charity purifies a person from the defilement of sin and disobedience to God.
[STUDYQURAN:9:103/r8] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The commentary refers the meaning of blessings to its discussions at 2:3, 9:99, and 33:56. | mentions 2:3, 9:99, 33:56
