2:2/q11 What is hudā and what guidance does it describe? [هُدًۭى · meaning] (turns on: Whether guidance requires a response or simply points the way.)
  2:2/q11/p1 It is direction, clarification, proof, light, and a way out of error. — reasons: Al-Shaʿbī gives leading away from error, al-Suddī light, Saʿīd clarification; al-Ṭabarī explains showing the way. Al-Fārisī discusses the verb's second object with ilā or lām. Ibn ʿAṭiyya glosses it as right guidance and clarification.
  2:2/q11/p2 Guidance entails reaching the goal. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī argues from opposition to error, praise of the guided and ihtadā as responsive form; al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī counter each inference.
  2:2/q11/p3 Guidance can mean indication without attainment. — reasons: Al-Rāzī cites Thamūd in 41:17 and 'I guided him, but he was not guided'; al-Ālūsī distinguishes being shown from following. Elmalılı says the Qur’an as speech instructs while tawfīq is God's act.
  2:2/q11/p4 The word accommodates direction and successful arrival. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports competing literal and figurative accounts, then includes both and averting destruction; Elmalılı distinguishes non-conveying and conveying guidance.
2:2/q12 How is hudan parsed and formed? [هُدًۭى · grammar] (turns on: Its case, governor, source form, and gender.)
  2:2/q12/p1 It is accusative as a detached adjective or circumstance. — reasons: The Book, demonstrative or pronoun in fīhi can supply its referent; Ibn ʿAṭiyya weakens the last, while Abū Ḥayyān notes difficulty in qualifying an absence. The same note permits an accusative circumstance. He also permits accusative circumstance.
  2:2/q12/p2 It is nominative as a predicate, new sentence, or praise. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj and later grammarians offer predicate of dhālika, omitted huwa, or after fīhi; al-Ṭabarī critiques one Kufan combination. Al-Qurṭubī takes hudā as a nominative subject with preceding fīhi as predicate. Al-Bayḍāwī also permits hudā after a fronted fīhi predicate with a pause at rayb. Ibn Kathīr gives nominative description as an Arabic possibility.
  2:2/q12/p3 It is a rare verbal noun or source substitute, with disputed gender. — reasons: Sībawayh calls its pattern rare; Abū ʿAlī compares weak roots. Al-Liḥyānī treats it as masculine, while Banū Asad and Ibn ʿAṭiyya allow feminine; related derivatives include daylight, offerings and gifts.
2:2/q13 Who are the muttaqīn? [لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ · referent] (turns on: Whether the term demands basic faith, obedient practice, scrupulosity, inner perfection, or future readiness.)
  2:2/q13/p1 Those fulfilling obligations and avoiding prohibitions. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī prefers this broad definition; al-Ḥasan and ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz agree. Ibn ʿAbbās adds fear of punishment and hope in mercy.
  2:2/q13/p2 The scrupulous who avoid grave, minor, doubtful, or harmless matters. — reasons: Al-Kalbī names major sins; a hadith counsels leaving harmless matters for fear of harm. Ibn al-Muʿtazz's thorn-path poem includes minor sins; al-Zamakhsharī prefers excluding minor sins from the minimum.
  2:2/q13/p3 Believers who renounce shirk and affirm revelation. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Suddī and Muʿādh give the basic faith threshold; al-Wāḥidī includes believers with major sins. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Māwardī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya oppose making this the whole definition.
  2:2/q13/p4 People of inward sincerity, humility, restraint, and dependence on God. — reasons: Sahl, Junayd, al-Dārānī, al-Sulamī and al-Qushayrī offer inward tests; al-Thaʿlabī records self-scrutiny, patience, altruism, gentleness and good character among many descriptions. Ibn ʿUmar says a pious person does not see himself above anyone.
  2:2/q13/p5 All ranks, from faith to undistracted devotion, including prospective piety. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī gives three ranks; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī pairs taqwā with each faith level. Al-Zamakhsharī names those approaching it by their future state; Elmalılı traces capacity and growth.
  2:2/q13/p6 People described next by faith, prayer, spending, and certainty. — reasons: Qatāda points to 2:3; Kur’an Yolu adds faith in earlier revelation and the Hereafter.
2:2/q14 What is the root sense of muttaqīn? [لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ · meaning]
  2:2/q14/p1 It means guarding oneself, from waqā and wiqāya. — reasons: Sources explain wāw-to-tāʾ change and plural morphology; armor, hand shielding and a thorny path illustrate protection. Asad emphasizes awareness of God's presence, while Elmalılı joins positive action with restraint.
2:2/q15 Why can the God-fearing adjective stand without an expressed noun? [لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the adjective's implicit noun and plural scope are supplied by context.)
  2:2/q15/p1 The understood noun is 'people'; context permits omission in praise. — reasons: Al-Burhān says an adjective can stand alone when it uniquely identifies its noun or when praise centers it; thus the phrase means guidance for God-fearing people.
  2:2/q15/p2 Plural forms have context-sensitive scope. — reasons: Al-Burhān says four broken paucity forms and two sound plurals inherently indicate few, while other plurals may denote few or many according to context.
2:2/q16 Why is guidance specified for the God-fearing? [هُدًۭى لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the phrase restricts potential guidance, actual benefit, continuing guidance, or later attainment.)
  2:2/q16/p1 They benefit from and follow guidance available to everyone. — reasons: 2:185 names all people; 79:45 analogously singles out receptive hearers; 17:82 says wrongdoers increase in loss. Al-Ālūsī rejects adding an omitted 'and disbelievers'.
  2:2/q16/p2 Those already guided gain further or continuing guidance. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī cites 1:6; Elmalılı says guidance continues at every rank, while al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes earlier innate and subsequent scriptural guidance.
  2:2/q16/p3 The term anticipates people about to become pious. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī cite names based on imminent state; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes that hudā as a verbal noun has no fixed tense.
  2:2/q16/p4 An omitted 'and disbelievers' extends the audience. — reasons: Ibn al-Anbārī compares an omitted cold after garments protecting from heat; al-Ālūsī rejects this ellipsis.
2:2/q17 How does the verse link to its sūra and other passages? [* · links] (turns on: Whether the connection is between clauses, with al-Fātiḥa, or with parallel sūras.)
  2:2/q17/p1 Four unjoined clauses advance from challenge to Book, certainty, and guidance. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Bayḍāwī explain each clause's rhetorical force; Abū Ḥayyān prefers three after the letters. The denial anticipates the 2:23 challenge.
  2:2/q17/p2 The Book answers al-Fātiḥa's prayer for a straight path. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī connects the opening believer and rejecter groups to paths to follow and avoid; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī emphasizes the sūra's affirmation of revelation and law.
  2:2/q17/p3 Opening titles and guidance terms vary with each sūra's purpose. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī counts Book and Qur’an uses across sūras and contrasts the God-fearing with beneficent people for whom mercy is added; al-Itqān compares Luqmān 31:3.
2:2/q18 What reports or historical claims accompany the verse? [* · reports]
  2:2/q18/p1 Reports describe the opening groups, rehearsal, and recitation. — reasons: Mujāhid differs on the hypocrite count; al-Rabīʿ names confederate leaders. Ibn ʿAbbās reports Jibrīl's final rehearsal and a dawn-prayer recitation.
  2:2/q18/p2 Jewish denial is reported as an occasion. — reasons: Muqātil names Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf and Kaʿb ibn Asad; al-Thaʿlabī separately names Mālik ibn al-Ṣayf.
  2:2/q18/p3 The promised Book and mercy were written or announced before this revelation. — reasons: ʿIyāḍ reports the unwashable Book, Abū Hurayra God's mercy preceding wrath, and Qāshānī cites Jesus on revelation and Mahdī's interpretation.
  2:2/q18/p4 Al-Baqara starts Medinan revelation and belongs among the long sūras. — reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ dates the opening after the Hijra; al-Itqān cites Ibn ʿAbbās on the seven long sūras.
2:2/q19 What inward readings are offered? [* · inward] (turns on: Whether guidance is inward disclosure or a journey shaped by prior readiness.)
  2:2/q19/p1 Purified hearts realize the Book as certain guidance and divine intimacy. — reasons: Al-Sulamī describes love and knowledge inscribed inwardly; al-Qushayrī likens the Book to consoling letters from the Beloved; Tustarī centers surrender of independent power.
  2:2/q19/p2 The Book guides distinct classes of souls according to readiness. — reasons: Qāshānī's seven final classes include two veiled groups that cannot benefit and five guided classes from virtue to belovedness; lovers and beloveds need the Book at different stages.
2:2/q20 What wider claims are drawn from its status as Book and guidance? [* · interpretation] (turns on: Which claim about proof, scope, knowledge, or scripture's nature is emphasized.)
  2:2/q20/p1 Inimitability and its many Qur’anic titles establish divine status and varied effects. — reasons: Al-Bāqillānī argues eloquent rivals would answer an achievable challenge; al-Rāzī catalogs proof, light, healing, mercy, criterion, reminder, truth and other titles with verses and reports.
  2:2/q20/p2 Its certainty and concise wording support knowledge, moral life, and multiple readings. — reasons: Elmalılı invokes observa
