31:10/q22 What grows through rain in فَأَنۢبَتْنَا? [فَأَنۢبَتْنَا فِيهَا مِن كُلِّ زَوْجٍۢ كَرِيمٍ · referent] (turns on: Vegetation, all natural kinds, or humans from earth.)
  31:10/q22/p1 Every kind of plant, vegetation, tree, or crop. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī ties rain to plants; al-Māwardī names trees and crops; Yaḥyā adds every color; Ibn ʿĀshūr includes grain, fruit, fodder, truffles.
  31:10/q22/p2 Growth encompasses animals, plants, and minerals. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya names all three.
  31:10/q22/p3 It can refer to people grown from earth, noble if destined for Paradise, base if for Hell. — reasons: Al-Shaʿbī's view occurs in al-Māwardī, al-Qurṭubī and Ibn Kathīr. Al-Qurṭubī adds semen from soil, supported by apparent Qurʾānic wording.
31:10/q23 What does فِيهَا refer to? [فِيهَا · referent]
  31:10/q23/p1 Earth. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr gives the antecedent; Asad renders it 'thereon'.
31:10/q24 What does زَوْجٍ mean here? [زَوْجٍۢ · meaning] (turns on: Species classification or pairing.)
  31:10/q24/p1 A kind, class, or species. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya rules out opposite of an individual; al-Rāzī says species divide in pairs, trees/non-trees and fruit-bearing/non-fruit-bearing; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 20:53 and 22:5; Asad cites 26:7.
  31:10/q24/p2 It alludes to pairing in plants and in all things. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ refers to plant pairing; Bursevī says everything pairs by opposite, likeness or composition, such as substance/accident or matter/form.
31:10/q25 What makes a زَوْجٍ كَرِيمٍ noble? [زَوْجٍۢ كَرِيمٍ · meaning] (turns on: Beauty, benefit, ripeness, or worth.)
  31:10/q25/p1 It is beautiful, fine, or excellent in appearance and ordered formation. — reasons: Yaḥyā mentions colors; Qatāda and Ibn ʿAbbās say good or beautiful. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān distinguish every kind's ordered craft from select beautiful things. Bursevī quotes al-Mufradāt on excellence in one's field; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 27:29's precious book.
  31:10/q25/p2 It is useful, beneficial, or generous in yield. — reasons: Ibn ʿĪsā says good fruit, Ṭabrisī pleasant fruit, al-Bayḍāwī plentiful benefit; Bursevī, al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ say useful; al-Rāzī and Study Quran allow production without reckoning or return; al-Biqāʿī adds freshness and animal sustenance.
  31:10/q25/p3 It is ripe. — reasons: Al-Māwardī reports Ibn Kāmil.
  31:10/q25/p4 It is valuable or esteemed. — reasons: Al-Māwardī suggests high price; al-Rāzī says honored, opposite despised; Ibn ʿĀshūr says precious within its kind and cites 27:29.
31:10/q26 What do rain and plant growth provide? [مَآءًۭ فَأَنۢبَتْنَا · interpretation]
  31:10/q26/p1 Nourishment and care for inhabited earth. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says rain feeds and plants sustain animals; al-Rāzī says stable seeds germinate; Ibn ʿĀshūr lists grain, fruit, fodder and truffles.
31:10/q27 What inward meaning is given to rain? [وَأَنزَلْنَا مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ مَآءًۭ · inward]
  31:10/q27/p1 Rain from inward heaven nourishes gardens of people near and present with God. — reasons: Al-Qushayrī contrasts outward rain feeding verdant gardens.
31:10/q28 Where is the heavens-without-visible-pillars wording paralleled? [بِغَيْرِ عَمَدٍۢ تَرَوْنَهَا · links]
  31:10/q28/p1 At 13:2 in Sūrat al-Raʿd. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Samīn, Elmalılı, Asad and Ibn ʿĀshūr refer there. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ uses it for both construals; Study Quran for pronoun dispute; al-Riḍā joins it with 51:7.
31:10/q29 Where is the mountain-stabilizing wording paralleled? [رَوَٰسِىَ أَن تَمِيدَ بِكُمْ · links]
  31:10/q29/p1 At 16:15 in Sūrat al-Naḥl. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Asad and Study Quran point there.
31:10/q30 Where is the dispersal of creatures paralleled? [وَبَثَّ فِيهَا مِن كُلِّ دَآبَّةٍۢ · links]
  31:10/q30/p1 At 2:164 in al-Baqara. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr notes the paired mention of creatures and wind patterns; Study Quran cites it on provision.
31:10/q31 Where are the rain and plant descriptions paralleled? [وَأَنزَلْنَا مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ مَآءًۭ مِن كُلِّ زَوْجٍۢ كَرِيمٍ · links]
  31:10/q31/p1 Rain resembles 2:164 and 13:17; plants recall 20:53, 22:5, and 26:7. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr names the first four; Qatāda and Asad cite 26:7.
31:10/q32 What do the signs in this verse establish about God? [* · theology]
  31:10/q32/p1 Creation shows power, wisdom, knowledge, mercy, and God's sole right to divinity. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī says might and wisdom show perfect power and knowledge; al-Ālūsī rebukes idolatry; Ibn ʿĀshūr says idols create nothing and lack wisdom, and useful creatures show mercy. Ibn Kathīr stresses God's knowledge of animals; al-Biqāʿī cites precise acts.
31:10/q33 How does the verse fit its sūra's surrounding argument? [* · links] (turns on: Connections differ among attributes, Qurʾān rejection, Luqmān, and 31:27.)
  31:10/q33/p1 Creation demonstrates the preceding divine might and wisdom and prepares oneness. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī cites precision; Elmalılı cites direct divine speech; al-Ālūsī rebukes idolatry; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it 'proof of proof': thing, evidence, and evidence for evidence.
  31:10/q33/p2 Creation supports God's promise against the buyer who derides the Qurʾān. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ links arrogant recitation and promises of Paradise or punishment; Iṣlāḥī challenges alleged deities to show their creation.
  31:10/q33/p3 If Quraysh asked about Luqmān and his son, these verses through 31:12 introduce his wisdom. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr presents this conditionally; Iṣlāḥī warns fathers who pressure sons from Islam, echoing 29:8.
  31:10/q33/p4 A preceding Jewish claim about the Torah's small share of divine knowledge led to the trees-as-pens verse. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān reports the exchange with the Prophet and connects it to 31:27.
