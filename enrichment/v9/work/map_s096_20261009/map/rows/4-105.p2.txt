SI, d. 548 · عكرمة وابن عباس | reports | They say Banū Ubairiq planted the stolen property on a Jew, Zayd ibn al-Suhaym, and asked the Prophet to argue for them; he intended to do so and punish the Jew before revelation came.

[TABRISI:4:105-106#2/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The Prophet is to judge among people by what God has shown him, that is, what God taught him in His Book.
[TABRISI:4:105-106#2/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The Prophet must not defend someone who betrayed a Muslim or a protected non-Muslim in person or property against the party seeking the right that was violated.
[TABRISI:4:105-106#2/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Although addressed to the Prophet, the admonition is for his community: he defended someone who appeared just and faithful but was inwardly otherwise; God is exalted above all sins and indecencies.
[TABRISI:4:105-106/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | The revelation is placed in the case of three Banū Ubairiq brothers: Bishr, Bashīr, and Mubashshir; Bashīr, called Abū Ṭuʿma, wrote satires against the Prophet’s companions and attributed them to others.
[TABRISI:4:105-106/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Abū Ṭuʿma broke into the upper room of Rifāʿa ibn Zayd and stole food, a sword, and armor; Rifāʿa complained to his nephew Qatāda ibn al-Nuʿmān.
[TABRISI:4:105-106/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | When Qatāda and Rifāʿa investigated, the brothers blamed the theft on Labīd ibn Sahl; Labīd confronted them, accusing them of theft and hypocrisy and warning them to stop slandering the Prophet.
[TABRISI:4:105-106/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · قتادة بن النعمان | reports | Qatāda told the Prophet that a bad household had attacked his uncle and broken into his upper room, taking food and weapons; the Prophet first told him to investigate.
[TABRISI:4:105-106/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · أسير بن عروة | reports | Usayr ibn ʿUrwa gathered men from the brothers’ clan and accused Qatāda and Rifāʿa of slandering a reputable, righteous household; after hearing him, the Prophet rebuked Qatāda when he returned.
[TABRISI:4:105-106/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Qatāda left wishing he had died rather than speak to the Prophet; Rifāʿa replied that God was the one to seek help from. The passage from this verse through “God does not forgive associating partners” was revealed.
[TABRISI:4:105-106/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | When Bashīr learned that the Qurʾān had exposed him, he fled to Mecca and apostatized; he stayed with Sulāfa bt. Saʿd, whom Ḥassān satirized in a poem.
