[ALUSI:1:1/r42] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبو بكر التونسي | reports | Abū Bakr al-Tūnisī transmits a consensus that God opened every revealed book with the basmala; another report calls it the opening of every book.
[ALUSI:1:1/r43] ALUSI, d. 1270 · السيوطي عن السرميني | reports | Al-Suyūṭī, citing al-Sarmīnī, reports “the basmala is the opening of every book”; al-Ālūsī cautions that the transmission is the reporter’s responsibility.
[ALUSI:1:1/r44] ALUSI, d. 1270 · السرميني | reports | Al-Sarmīnī argues the Qurʾānic basmala is unique, citing the staged change from “in Your name” to “in God’s name,” then “the Merciful,” then the full formula. | mentions 11:41, 17:110, 27:30
[ALUSI:1:1/r45] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | rejects | Al-Ālūsī replies that lack of an earlier command does not prove earlier absence, and only the Qurʾān’s Arabic wording and arrangement may be distinctive.
[ALUSI:1:1/r46] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | reports | Outside the sūra context of 27:30, scholars held ten positions: from nowhere a Qurʾānic verse to a verse or verse-part in all or selected sūras. | mentions 27:30
[ALUSI:1:1/r47] ALUSI, d. 1270 · ابن عباس وابن المبارك وأهل مكة وأهل الكوفة وغالب أصحاب الشافعي والإمامية | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn al-Mubārak, Meccan and most Kufan readers, most Shāfiʿīs, and Imāmīs hold it is a verse in every sūra except al-Tawba.
[ALUSI:1:1/r48] ALUSI, d. 1270 · بعض الشافعية وحمزة | reports | Some Shāfiʿīs and Ḥamza, with a view attributed to Aḥmad, count the basmala as a verse of al-Fātiḥa alone.
[ALUSI:1:1/r49] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أهل المدينة ومالك والأوزاعي وأبو عمرو ويعقوب | reports | The Medinese, Mālik, al-Awzāʿī, Abū ʿAmr, and Yaʿqūb hold it is a separate verse revealed to mark sūra openings; al-Ālūsī calls this the common Hanafi doctrine.
[ALUSI:1:1/r5] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | prefers | The Meccan setting of 15:87 is often used to date al-Fātiḥa, but the argument depends on identifying the seven mathānī with it and on denying prior divine endowment. | mentions 15:87
[ALUSI:1:1/r50] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Al-Ālūsī says the Hanafi position treats the basmala as an independent Qurʾānic verse, not part of al-Fātiḥa, while many Hanafis require its recitation in prayer.
[ALUSI:1:1/r51] ALUSI, d. 1270 · الزيلعي والزهيدي وابن وهبان | reports | Al-Zaylaʿī and al-Zāhidī report the sound Hanafi view as obligation, including each rakʿa with obligatory recitation; Ibn Wahbān prescribes prostration for forgetful omission.
[ALUSI:1:1/r52] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | prefers | Al-Ālūsī endorses the cautious view that omitting the basmala warrants the forgetfulness prostration, against Qāḍīkhān and the author of al-Khulāṣa.
[ALUSI:1:1/r53] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أم سلمة | reports | A report attributed to Umm Salama has the Prophet count the basmala as al-Fātiḥa’s first verse; al-Ālūsī questions the chain because Abū Mulayka’s hearing from her is unestablished.
[ALUSI:1:1/r54] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبو عبيد وأحمد وأبو داود | reports | They transmit Umm Salama’s report that the Prophet paused verse by verse, beginning with the basmala; al-Ālūsī says this proves its Qurʾānic status, not its membership in al-Fātiḥa.
[ALUSI:1:1/r55] ALUSI, d. 1270 · ابن خزيمة والحاكم | reports | Their version has the Prophet count the basmala as a verse while reciting in prayer; al-Ālūsī reads it as the first Qurʾānic verse, not necessarily al-Fātiḥa’s.
[ALUSI:1:1/r56] ALUSI, d. 1270 · الدارقطني | reports | Al-Dāraquṭnī’s version says the Prophet counted the basmala but did not pause after “those You favored”; al-Ālūsī says it is not explicit that it belongs to al-Fātiḥa. | mentions 1:7
[ALUSI:1:1/r57] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبو هريرة | reports | A report from Abū Hurayra says the seven verses of al-Fātiḥa begin with the basmala; al-Ālūsī reads the wording as counting seven al-Ḥamd verses and separately affirming the basmala as Qurʾān.
[ALUSI:1:1/r58] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبو هريرة | reports | Al-Dāraquṭnī’s report urges reciting the basmala before al-Ḥamd and calls it one of its verses; al-Ālūsī offers a figurative reading that preserves seven verses without it.
[ALUSI:1:1/r59] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبو هريرة | reports | In the sound Muslim report, God divides prayer between Himself and the servant; the three verses before the central pair and three after total seven without the basmala. | mentions 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7

[ALUSI:1:1/r61] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبو ميسرة | reports | An author-cited report from Abū Maysara says the Prophet first heard the call in Mecca, then recited al-Fātiḥa; al-Ālūsī says it would strongly prove Meccan origin if sound.
[ALUSI:1:1/r62] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبي بن كعب | reports | Abū Hurayra reports that the Prophet asked Ubayy the Qurʾān’s greatest verse; Ubayy answered the basmala and the Prophet affirmed it, proving a verse but not its place in al-Fātiḥa.
[ALUSI:1:1/r63] ALUSI, d. 1270 · جابر بن عبد الله | reports | Al-Thaʿlabī transmits Jābir’s report that the Prophet instructed him to say the basmala when beginning prayer; al-Ālūsī says it does not establish sūra-part status.
[ALUSI:1:1/r64] ALUSI, d. 1270 · علي | reports | Al-Thaʿlabī attributes to ʿAlī the practice of beginning each sūra in prayer with the basmala and saying omission diminishes the prayer; al-Ālūsī does not accept this as proof of inclusion.
[ALUSI:1:1/r65] ALUSI, d. 1270 · طلحة بن عبيد الله | reports | A report attributed to Ṭalḥa says whoever leaves the basmala has left a verse of God’s Book; al-Ālūsī says such reports establish its Qurʾānic status only.
[ALUSI:1:1/r66] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبو هريرة | reports | A report says the Prophet rebuked a worshipper for beginning al-Ḥamd without the basmala and called omission a broken prayer; al-Ālūsī questions its wording and evidentiary force.
