[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p220/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · المبرد | reports | On al-Mubarrad’s parse, when the sky splits and the earth is spread, the human meets his Lord after striving toward Him.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p220/r7] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · المبرد | reports | Al-Mubarrad also allows the answer to be omitted and inferred from “you will meet Him”: the implied answer is “you will meet.”
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p221/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The primary purpose is to warn polytheists because they denied resurrection; for believers the address is a reminder and glad tidings.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p221/r10] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Kadhan is an accusative cognate object emphasizing kadiḥ, construed with the sense “striving toward God”; the striving is unavoidable.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p221/r11] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The object pronoun in “meeting Him” refers to the Lord; no one escapes meeting God, hence the emphatic inna.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p221/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · قيل | reports | One reported view identifies the addressed person as al-Aswad ibn ʿAbd al-Asad; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes variant spellings in biographical and exegetical works.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p221/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · قيل | reports | Another reported identification is Ubayy ibn Khalaf.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p221/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Either named individual may have occasioned the revelation, or one may have been intended from the outset.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p221/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The root k-d-ḥ has several possible senses, and Ibn ʿĀshūr says its exact original meaning cannot be determined confidently.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p221/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · الراغب | reports | Al-Rāghib’s apparent definition is exhausting oneself through work and toil.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p221/r7] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The preposition ilā suggests an action that ends at meeting God; kadiḥ may be understood as “striving,” making the phrase a predicate of inna.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p221/r8] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | On the “striving” sense, daily work reaches toward tomorrow until life ends in death, which returns the person to God; thus striving ends in meeting Him.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p221/r9] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Alternatively, kadiḥ may mean “walking”; then the phrase with ilā is an adverbial adjunct rather than a predicate.
[TABATABAI:84:1-6/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The Meccan surah describes the Hour and humanity's journey to its Lord for an account based on the record, with warning predominating over glad tidings.
[TABATABAI:84:1-6/r10] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib defines kadh as striving and exertion.
[TABATABAI:84:1-6/r11] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | reports | Another definition is exerting the self in work until the work leaves an effect on it; the preposition ila gives the expression a sense of journey.
[TABATABAI:84:1-6/r12] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The servant, as owned and governed by God, journeys toward his Lord; he should will and do only what his Lord commands and is accountable for his choices and acts.
[TABATABAI:84:1-6/r13] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The verse argues for resurrection: lordship entails servitude, servitude entails responsibility, responsibility requires return and accounting, and accounting entails recompense.
[TABATABAI:84:1-6/r14] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Meeting God means reaching a state where only His judgment rules and nothing can screen the human from his Lord.
[TABATABAI:84:1-6/r15] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The addressee is humanity as a whole, since God's lordship applies universally to every human.
[TABATABAI:84:1-6/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The conditional in verse 1 has an omitted result, supplied by verse 6: humanity meets its Lord, who reckons and recompenses it for its deeds. | mentions 84:6
[ASAD-NOTES:84:1#n4/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | Asad says earthly life is painful toil for everyone, whether consciously recognized or not, because sorrow, pain, drudgery, and worry outweigh rare happiness; this toil leads to meeting God at resurrection.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p237/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Islahi links al-Inshiqāq with al-Muṭaffifīn: both warn deniers of recompense and describe two final groups, with right-hand recipients succeeding and left-hand recipients disgraced.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p237/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Both sūrahs address affluent people who deny judgment or expect worldly honor in the afterlife; innate awareness of justice and the world's purpose require a final separation, dooming those who ignore it.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p237/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The preceding sūrah reasons from human nature, whereas al-Inshiqāq reasons from signs in the physical world.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p237/r5] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Man is rebuked for being inexorably drawn toward meeting his Lord and fate; those given their accounts in their right hands will have an easy, happy reckoning.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p241/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Though humanity is addressed generally, the warning particularly targets conceited people absorbed in worldly life, who are inevitably being drawn to meet their Lord.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p242/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Kadh means to strive with great labor; human life is an unbroken journey from birth through changing stages and conditions to death, regardless of status.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p242/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Meeting the Lord is the purpose of life's journey: people must be held accountable, the objective for which this world was created.
