92:17/q09 What qualities make this person God-fearing? [ٱلْأَتْقَى · interpretation] (turns on: Which traits the adjacent verses and reported exemplary acts attach to the title.)
  92:17/q09/p1 He avoids idolatry and sins, and purifies himself through sincere giving for God. — reasons: Al-Biqa'i speaks of a firm knowing faculty and peak piety; al-Wahidi reports Muqatil's sincere affirmation of God's oneness. Ibn Kathir calls him pure. Islahi connects heedfulness, judgment and cleansing wealth; Kuran Yolu traces taqwā to protection from danger and explains that giving seeks God's approval without repayment. The Study Quran notes charity purifies giver and wealth, with tazakkā related to zakāh. Corpus Coranicum calls the Lord's face a biblical metaphor.
  92:17/q09/p2 He gives without settling a debt or repaying a favor, seeking God and His Messenger alone. — reasons: Tustari recounts Abu Bakr's answer about what he retained; Ibn Ata says the highest pious abandon the transient world. Al-Razi says a repayment earns no reward, whereas giving in response to God's command does. The Bilal reports say revelation answered a claim of prior favor.
  92:17/q09/p3 He is the fortunate counterpart to the most wretched. — reasons: Qutb places the two figures against one another, drawing on verses 15–16.
92:17/q10 What inward state does the most God-fearing reach? [ٱلْأَتْقَى · inward] (turns on: Whether the piety is expressed as renunciation, union, or posthumous light.)
  92:17/q10/p1 The most pious abandons the transient world for God and His Messenger. — reasons: Tustari and Ibn Ata cite Abu Bakr's giving and his answer that he kept God and the Messenger; Ibn Ata calls ordinary pious people ascetics and the highest those who abandon the world entirely.
  92:17/q10/p2 The absolutely God-fearing rejects all otherness and is absorbed in unity, escaping every level of fire. — reasons: Qashani contrasts this with stopping at attributes: such a person may be forgiven but remains veiled from the Essence's joy.
  92:17/q10/p3 Good deeds, including night prayer, bring light after death. — reasons: Al-Biqa'i reports this as an interpretation of spiritual light becoming sensory light.
