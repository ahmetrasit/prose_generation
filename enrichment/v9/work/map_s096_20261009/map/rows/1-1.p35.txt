[QURTUBI:1:1/r182] QURTUBI, d. 671 · نحاة البصرة | reports | Basran grammarians parse bismillah as the predicate of an omitted beginning or as governed by “established” or “fixed.”
[QURTUBI:1:1/r183] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | The alif in ism is omitted in the basmala through frequent use; it remains in the less common “Read in the name of your Lord.” | mentions 96:1
[QURTUBI:1:1/r184] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الكسائي وسعيد الأخفش | reports | Al-Kisāʾī and Saʿīd al-Akhfash also omit the alif before al-Raḥmān and al-Qāhir; Yaḥyā ibn Waththāb omits it only before Allah.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r185] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | reports | Three explanations for the bāʾ’s kasra are matching its function, the genitive of nouns, or distinguishing it from kāf used as a noun.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r186] QURTUBI, d. 671 · امرؤ القيس | reports | Imruʾ al-Qays’s phrase “like the water bird” illustrates kāf functioning as a noun rather than a particle.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r187] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الجوهري | reports | Ism has a lost wāw and pattern ifʿ; its plural and diminutive are preserved by usage and poetry.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r188] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أحمد بن يحيى | reports | Aḥmad ibn Yaḥyā derives the form with ḍamma from samawtu and with kasra from samaytu; Arabic also uses sum and sim.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r189] QURTUBI, d. 671 · البصريون | reports | The Basrans derive ism from sumūw, elevation, explaining names as raising or distinguishing referents.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r19] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Seeking refuge fulfills the command and protects against Satan’s whispering during recitation. | mentions 22:52
[QURTUBI:1:1/r190] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الكوفيون | reports | The Kūfans derive ism from sima, a mark, because a name marks its referent.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r191] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | prefers | Qurṭubī prefers the Basran derivation: diminutive sumayy and plural asmāʾ point to s-m-w rather than wasm.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r192] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أهل السنة | reports | The Basran derivation entails God always possessed names and attributes independent of creation; Qurṭubī identifies this as Sunni doctrine.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r193] QURTUBI, d. 671 · المعتزلة | rejects | The Kūfan derivation is linked to the Muʿtazilite claim that creatures assigned God names and attributes which disappear with creation.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r194] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أهل الحق | reports | The people of truth, including al-Qāḍī Abū Bakr and Ibn Fūrak, say name and named are identical.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r195] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن الحصار | reports | Ibn al-Ḥaṣṣār contrasts those who say names designate only essence with those who say they denote attributes of the essence.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r196] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Allah is the greatest, most comprehensive divine name, unique to God and not pluralized; 19:65 may mean no one bears that name. | mentions 19:65
[QURTUBI:1:1/r197] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Allah means the true existent who gathers divine and lordly attributes, the one alone worthy of worship.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r198] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الخليل وسيبويه | reports | Al-Khalīl and Sibawayh derive Allah from ilāh, with the article replacing hamza, as nās derives from unās.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r199] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الكسائي والفراء | reports | Al-Kisāʾī and al-Farrāʾ explain bismillah as bi-ism al-ilāh, with hamza deletion and lām assimilation.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r20] QURTUBI, d. 671 · جبير بن مطعم | reports | Jubayr ibn Muṭʿim reports the Prophet’s opening prayer asking protection from Satan’s hamz, nafkh, and nafth; ʿAmr glosses them as madness, pride, and poetry.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r200] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الخليل | reports | Another proposal derives ilāh from walah, bewilderment, because minds are overwhelmed by God’s attributes.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r201] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الضحاك | reports | Al-Ḍaḥḥāk says God is called ilāh because creatures turn to Him with needs and plead in hardship.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r202] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الخليل بن أحمد | reports | Al-Khalīl says creatures worship God; the verb has two dialectal vowels.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r203] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | On the reading “your gods” in 7:127, Ibn ʿAbbās and others gloss it as “your worship.” | mentions 7:127
[QURTUBI:1:1/r204] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | reports | Other proposals derive Allah from elevation, worship, or the pronoun hāʾ extended by a possessive lām and article.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r205] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الشافعي وأبو المعالي والخطابي والغزالي | reports | Al-Shāfiʿī, al-Juwaynī, al-Khaṭṭābī, and al-Ghazālī treat Allah as a non-derived proper name with inseparable alif-lām.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r206] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الخطابي | reports | Al-Khaṭṭābī argues the article belongs to Allah’s name because it accepts the vocative yā Allah, unlike al-Raḥmān or al-Raḥīm.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r207] QURTUBI, d. 671 · بعضهم | reports | Some say al-Raḥmān is not derived: if from mercy it would take an object, and Arabs would not ask “What is al-Raḥmān?” | mentions 25:60
[QURTUBI:1:1/r208] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن العربي | reports | Ibn al-ʿArabī says pagans knew the referent but not the attribute, asking what al-Raḥmān was rather than who.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r209] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن الحصار | reports | Ibn al-Ḥaṣṣār counters that 13:30 explicitly says the disbelievers rejected al-Raḥmān. | mentions 13:30
[QURTUBI:1:1/r21] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبو سعيد الخدري | reports | Abū Saʿīd reports that the Prophet began night prayer with praise and sought refuge before reciting.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r210] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الجمهور | reports | Most scholars derive al-Raḥmān from mercy as an intensive form, uniquely divine and not dualized or pluralized.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r211] QURTUBI, d. 671 · عبد الرحمن بن عوف | reports | A report graded ṣaḥīḥ by al-Tirmidhī quotes God saying “I am al-Raḥmān,” who created kinship and derived its name from His own.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r212] QURTUBI, d. 671 · المبرد | rejects | Al-Mubarrad claims al-Raḥmān is Hebrew paired with Arabic al-Raḥīm; Qurṭubī rejects the claim.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r213] QURTUBI, d. 671 · قتادة | reports | Qatāda says the basmala is God praising Himself; al-Zajjāj approves it as adjectival praise.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r214] QURTUBI, d. 671 · قطرب | reports | Quṭrub says al-Raḥmān and al-Raḥīm are paired for emphasis; al-Zajjāj approves this rhetoric.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r215] QURTUBI, d. 671 · محمد بن يزيد | reports | Muḥammad ibn Yazīd explains the paired names as favor after favor, renewed blessing, and a promise sustaining hope.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r216] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبو عبيدة | reports | Abū ʿUbayda treats al-Raḥmān and al-Raḥīm as synonyms, like nadmān and nadīm.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r217] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الجمهور | prefers | The majority distinguish them: faʿlān marks overwhelming action, while faʿīl can be active or passive; al-Raḥmān is specific in name but broad in action.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r218] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبو علي الفارسي | reports | Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī says al-Raḥmān covers all mercy but is exclusive to God, while al-Raḥīm especially concerns believers.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r219] QURTUBI, d. 671 · العرزمي | reports | Al-ʿArzamī says al-Raḥmān’s mercy reaches all creation through rain and senses, while al-Raḥīm guides believers.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r22] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبو القاسم | reports | Sulaymān ibn Sālim gives an expanded formula including refuge in God the Mighty and the basmala; Ibn ʿAṭiyya dislikes embellishments without declaring them forbidden.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r220] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن المبارك | reports | Ibn al-Mubārak says al-Raḥmān gives when asked while al-Raḥīm is angered when not asked; hadith supports this.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r221] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās calls the names soft, one softer than the other; al-Khaṭṭābī objects and al-Ḥusayn ibn al-Faḍl corrects it to “gentle.”
[QURTUBI:1:1/r222] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الحسين بن الفضل | reports | Al-Ḥusayn ibn al-Faḍl says the narrator confused “gentler” with “softer”; gentleness is divine.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r223] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أكثر العلماء | reports | Most scholars reserve al-Raḥmān for God, citing 17:110 and 43:45; Musaylima’s self-title became attached to him as “the liar.” | mentions 17:110, 43:45
[QURTUBI:1:1/r224] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن العربي | reports | Ibn al-ʿArabī reports al-Raḥmān may be the greatest name of God.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r225] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Al-Raḥīm can describe creatures; al-Raḥmān precedes it because it is more comprehensive, matching Qurʾānic order.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r226] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | reports | Another interpretation makes al-Raḥīm a description of Muḥammad, through whom people reach God’s favor by following his message.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r227] QURTUBI, d. 671 · علي بن أبي طالب | reports | A saying attributed to ʿAlī calls the basmala a cure and aid; al-Raḥmān aids believers and al-Raḥīm belongs to the repentant righteous.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r228] QURTUBI, d. 671 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | An esoteric report interprets basmala letters and says al-Raḥmān shows compassion to all creation, while al-Raḥīm is gentle toward believers.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r229] QURTUBI, d. 671 · كعب الأحبار | reports | Kaʿb al-Aḥbār assigns basmala letters to divine glory, majesty, and sovereignty; another reading maps each letter to a divine name.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r23] QURTUBI, d. 671 · سليمان بن صرد | reports | When two men quarreled and one became enraged, the Prophet said seeking refuge from Satan would dispel his anger; Muslim and al-Bukhārī transmit it.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r230] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أم سلمة | reports | A report from Umm Salama pauses after al-Raḥīm with a cut hamza at al-Ḥamd; some Kūfans read this way.
[QURTUBI:1:1/r231] QURTUBI, d. 671 · جمهور الناس | reports | Most readers connect al-Raḥīm to al-Ḥamd, using the genitive and connecting hamza.
