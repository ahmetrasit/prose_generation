[TABATABAI:9:97-106#5/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Islam assigns society a share in wealth's benefits through obligations such as zakat and khums; this answers society's need for resources and resembles provisions in earlier law codes and customary systems.
[TABATABAI:9:97-106#5/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The first distinctive feature is that zakat and similar shares attach when new wealth arises, such as harvest or trading profit, and the remaining shares belong to the owner or worker.
[TABATABAI:9:97-106#5/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | He suggests 2:29 and 4:5 may imply that newly arising wealth first belongs wholly to society, after which a share is assigned to the individual; zakat or khums remains society's share, while the individual owns the rest. | mentions 2:29, 4:5
[TABATABAI:9:97-106#5/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Once ownership has settled, Islam imposes no further claim on the owner's property merely because of conditions or circumstances; charges on persons, land or commerce at special times are treated as usurpation, except emergencies threatening society's survival.
[TABATABAI:9:97-106#6/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The second feature favors individuals in distributing public shares: zakat has eight categories, only one for God's cause, while the others serve people; khums has six shares, with five assigned to the Prophet, kin, orphans, needy and travelers.
[TABATABAI:9:97-106#6/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Because individuals are society's constituent elements, reducing class differences and balancing social forces requires improving individuals and bringing their circumstances closer; spending communal wealth on prestige projects while rich and poor diverge does not solve social harm.
[TABATABAI:9:97-106#6/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Third, a Muslim may in some circumstances pay obligatory zakat directly to eligible poor or needy recipients rather than route it through the ruler or collector.
[TABATABAI:9:97-106#6/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | This direct-distribution right reflects the independence Islam grants individuals, like the authority to give safe conduct to hostile non-Muslims; the ruler may prohibit it when public Muslim interests require, and must be obeyed.
