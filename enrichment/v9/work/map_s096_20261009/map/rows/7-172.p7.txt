[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p474/r8] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The accounts vary on God’s action and the progeny’s form: He touched Adam’s back with His right hand or struck his shoulder and brought forth descendants like particles or mustard seeds; Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb says they were souls given forms.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p474/r9] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · عبد الله بن عمر | reports | Ibn ʿUmar transmits from the Prophet that the progeny were drawn from Adam like a comb through hair, given minds like Solomon’s ant, and covenanted to God as Lord with no other deity; they pledged and were told prophets would remind them.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p475/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · الطرطوشي | reports | Al-Ṭurṭūshī says the covenant binds people even when they do not remember it in this life, just as a divorce witnessed against someone still binds after he forgets it.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p475/r10] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · السدي وعبد الله بن عمر عن النبي | reports | Alternatively, the angels say We have testified, which allows a pause at balā; al-Suddī explains the speakers as God and His angels, and Ibn ʿUmar transmits this from the Prophet.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p475/r11] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · القراء | reports | All seven readers except Abū ʿAmr read an taqūlū, directly addressing those present; Abū ʿAmr reads an yaqūlū, narrating about absent people, also read by Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Jubayr, and Ibn Muḥayṣin; the readings suit the two interpretations.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p475/r12] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | The particle an is accusative as the object of an implied fear, meaning for fear that they would say this.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p475/r2] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · نافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر | reports | Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, and Ibn ʿĀmir read dhurriyyātihim in the plural intensive form.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p475/r3] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · ابن كثير وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي | reports | Ibn Kathīr, ʿĀṣim, Ḥamza, and al-Kisāʾī read dhurriyyatahum; the singular here functions collectively.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p475/r4] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the discussion of the word dhurriyya has already appeared in Sūrat Āl ʿImrān.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p475/r5] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | reports | A story says the prophets appeared among the extracted souls like lamps; Adam admired David and gave him forty years from his own thousand-year lifespan, then disputed with the Angel of Death when he died at 960.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p475/r6] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · الضحاك بن مزاحم | reports | Al-Ḍaḥḥāk says those who die young remain on the first covenant, while those who reach maturity receive the second covenant of this present life.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p475/r7] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · الزجاج عن قوم | reports | Al-Zajjāj transmits a view that each person’s birth and maturity bring a rational recognition of God through the signs, which constitutes the covenant.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p475/r8] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | rejects | Ibn ʿAṭiyya rejects that interpretation as weak and contrary to the transmitted reports.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p475/r9] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · الطبري | reports | Al-Ṭabarī says shāhidnā may be words spoken by some souls to others: they testified so people could not claim at the Resurrection that they lacked knowledge and faith; on this reading one should not pause at balā.
[TABRISI:7:172-174/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The covenant was made so people could not plead ignorance or excuse their own shirk by saying their forefathers were polytheists and they merely followed them.
[TABRISI:7:172-174/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The particle interpretation is problematic: unintelligent particles could not know monotheism, while intelligent ones should remember the covenant; that memory is needed for it to count as evidence.
[TABRISI:7:172-174/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | It is implausible for a vast company of intelligent people to forget shared knowledge; people in Paradise remember worldly events, and allowing such forgetting invites transmigration.
[TABRISI:7:172-174/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · علي بن عيسى عن أبي بكر بن الإخشيد | reports | Ibn al-Ikhshid allows the particle report to be true but not the verse's interpretation; its benefit is to inspire noble habits of gratitude and recognition of God's lordship, like the report that people are born on fitra.
[TABRISI:7:172-174/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو الهذيل | reports | Abu al-Hudhayl says al-Hasan al-Basri and his companions held that children's bliss in Paradise rewards their faith in the particle covenant.
[TABRISI:7:172-174/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | A second interpretation takes the covenant as human development from parental loins through the womb, successive stages, and eventual life as morally responsible people.
[TABRISI:7:172-174/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · الرماني وأبو مسلم وابن الإخشيد | reports | In the second interpretation, God's signs in human creation make people recognize His unity as though He had addressed them and they had answered; this is figurative witnessing, not a literal scene.
[TABRISI:7:173-174/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · الرماني وأبو مسلم وابن الإخشيد | reports | The figurative interpretation is illustrated by God's command to heaven and earth, which they answered though no literal speech or reply occurred (41:11). | mentions 41:11

[TABRISI:7:173-174/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · شاعر | reports | An unnamed poem gives eyes a speaking role: they say “we hear and obey” and shed tears, illustrating figurative testimony.
[TABRISI:7:173-174/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · خطيب | reports | A preacher's example says to ask the earth who split its rivers and planted its trees: if it does not answer in speech, it answers through reflection.
[TABRISI:7:173-174/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · الجبائي والقاضي | reports | A third view says God gave a particular group of Adam's descendants mature intellect and had messengers address them about knowing God and obeying Him; they affirmed the covenant.
[TABRISI:7:173-174/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · الجبائي والقاضي | reports | This view concerns only some people, not all: they had polytheist ancestors, and children directly from Adam were not taken from the backs of human ancestors.
[TABRISI:7:173-174/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · الأزهري عن بعضهم | reports | One grammatical view makes “we witnessed” the angels' words, so people would not claim ignorance; al-Azhari attributes this to some authorities.
[TABRISI:7:173-174/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | rejects | The angel interpretation conflicts with the apparent reading: “they said yes” is complete, the people themselves then say “we witnessed,” and angels have not been mentioned.
[TABRISI:7:173-174/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | “Lest you say at Resurrection that we were unaware” means you failed to notice the proof, had no established argument, or lacked mature reasoning.
[TABRISI:7:173/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Ibn Kathir and the Kufans read dhurriyyatahum in the singular form; the remaining readers use the plural.
[TABRISI:7:173/r10] TABRISI, d. 548 · جماعة من المفسرين | reports | One interpretation says God extracted Adam's descendants from his loins as tiny particles, presented them to Adam, and covenanted that they worship Him alone; He also assigned their provisions.
[TABRISI:7:173/r11] TABRISI, d. 548 · جماعة من المفسرين | reports | In this account the descendants affirm God's lordship, the angels witness, and God makes the descendants intelligent enough to hear and understand His address.
[TABRISI:7:173/r12] TABRISI, d. 548 · جماعة من المفسرين | reports | The account says the particles returned to Adam's loins; those who remained Muslim preserved the original fitra, while unbelievers changed from it, supported by both raised and stopped reports.
