Ibn Abbas and Ka'b say the Sidra is beneath the Throne, where knowledge of every angel, prophet, and other creature ends; what lies beyond is known only to God.
[ELMALILI:53:11-14/r22] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Dahhâk | reports | Dahhak's reported view is that every matter from the divine command reaches the Sidra and goes no farther.
[ELMALILI:53:11-14/r23] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Fahreddin Râzî | reports | Razi records an alternative that sidra is a noun of one occurrence from rider/rikba, so the phrase means supreme astonishment; the Prophet was not bewildered or disoriented.
[ELMALILI:53:11-14/r24] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Fahreddin Râzî | prefers | Razi explicitly says the first interpretation, the physical sidra, is the sound one.
[ELMALILI:53:11-14/r25] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Ebussuud | reports | Abu Suud suggests muntaha may mean God, by 53:42, so Sidrat al-Muntaha is God's sidra, as property is attributed to its owner. | mentions 53:42
