[RAZI-FULL:v1p97/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Nouns precede verbs in rank because a verb needs a subject to be uttered, while the word naming its subject can be uttered without a verb; noun-noun constructions make sentences, whereas verb-verb combinations do not.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p97/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Names for simple essences precede derived names: essences are simple while derived forms are compounds.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p97/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Names of attributes may precede names of independent entities because we know entities through their attributes, and what makes something known is known first.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p98#2/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Human thought knows God only as existent, everlasting, possessed of negative attributes, or possessed of relational attributes; the unique essence differs from all four and so is unknown.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p98#2/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Human concepts arise from the five senses, bodily states, intellectual concepts such as existence and possibility, or combinations; since God's essence differs from these, it is inconceivable to creatures.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p98#2/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | God's essence causes all its real, relational, and negative properties; if the essence were known, all those properties would necessarily be known, which they are not.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p98#2/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · بعضهم | reports | Some argue that finite creaturely intellects cannot reach the infinite God, so only God knows God.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p98#2/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | He distinguishes indirect from essential knowledge: inferring a builder from a building reveals no nature, just as inferring the Creator from contingent things gives only indirect knowledge of God.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p98#2/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | In ordinary experience, essential apprehension comes through knowledge or sight; whether God could be essentially known through one or both, or through further modes, exceeds reason's ability to decide.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p98/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | God differs from creation by His own essence, not through an added property: if the difference required another property, regress or circularity would result.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p98/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | God is neither body nor substance: these are negative concepts, whereas His unique essence is positive and real.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p98/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | God's essence is not identical with power or knowledge, since these are relational notions while His essence is independently subsistent.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p99/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · قدماء الفلاسفة | reports | Ancient philosophers deny that God has a name for His unique essence because naming directs a hearer to a referent, but no creature knows that essence; they allow names for knowable consequences such as eternal and necessary.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p99/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Others say God could grant a close servant knowledge of His unique essence, in which case naming that essence would be possible; the issue rests on the preceding premises.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p99/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | If an essence-name can be assigned, it must be the greatest name and remembrance because their honor follows the honor of what is known and remembered.
[RAZI-FULL:v1p99/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | A prophet or angel who encountered that name while its meaning was manifest might be obeyed by all physical and spiritual realms.
