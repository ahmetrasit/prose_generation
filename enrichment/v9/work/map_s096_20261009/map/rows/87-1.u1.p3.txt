87:1/q28 How is this opening classified among sura opening types? [* · rhetoric]
  87:1/q28/p1 It combines a command with praise, within the broader kinds of Quranic openings. — reasons: Abu Shama calls 87:1 both command and praise and surveys openings by command, condition, vocative, oath, prayer, question and report; his causal class is exemplified by 106:1.
87:1/q29 What syntactic construction is elsewhere compared with 87:1? [سَبِّحِ ٱسْمَ · grammar]
  87:1/q29/p1 A verb of glorification may substitute for a verb of remembrance while a prepositional phrase precedes the subject. — reasons: Burhan parses yusabbih as a substitute for yudhkar, with lahu fiha a fronted predicate and rijal the subject, citing 87:1 by analogy.
87:1/q30 What morphological pattern does al-aʿla illustrate? [ٱلْأَعْلَى · grammar]
  87:1/q30/p1 It is an afʿal form with the definite article. — reasons: Burhan lists three af'al patterns: construct, definite with al, and unmarked followed by min introducing the compared item, citing 63:8.
87:1/q31 How does sanctifying the name relate to knowing God's essence? [ٱسْمَ · theology]
  87:1/q31/p1 Pure names express the essence's transcendence and are the human route to knowing it in this life. — reasons: Elmalili says minds know God through names, attributes or effects, while direct manifestation belongs to the afterlife; he relates this to 87:14-17.
87:1/q32 How should divine highness govern other attributes? [ٱلْأَعْلَى · theology]
  87:1/q32/p1 Ambiguous descriptions must be interpreted in a way befitting the Most High's complete perfection. — reasons: Ibn Ashur makes divine highness the standard for reading other attributes and requires figurative interpretation when literal description conflicts with it.
87:1/q33 What does God’s uniqueness mean for His attributes? [ٱلْأَعْلَى · theology]
  87:1/q33/p1 God alone deserves His attributes and transcends the attributes of created things. — reasons: Jishumi presents this as a further explanation of the epithet.
87:1/q34 What did Ibn Umar say about speaking of God's name? [ٱسْمَ ٱلْأَعْلَى · reports]
  87:1/q34/p1 He instructed not to say 'upon God's name,' since God's name is Most High. — reasons: Nafi transmits the remark; Qurtubi's full account repeats it.
