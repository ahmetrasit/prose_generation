[ELMALILI:1:5/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | In legal usage, worship is a special act of obedience that earns reward, depends on intention, and expresses nearness to God.
[ELMALILI:1:5/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Taat is an act rewarded whether or not it depends on intention or the doer knows for whom it is done.
[ELMALILI:1:5/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Qurbat is a rewarded act performed with awareness of the person one seeks to approach, even when intention is not required.
[ELMALILI:1:5/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Every worship act is both qurbat and taat, and every qurbat is taat, but the reverse implications do not hold.
[ELMALILI:1:5/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Reflection to know God is taat but not qurbat while its intended object remains unknown, and without intention it is not worship.
[ELMALILI:1:5/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Qur'an recitation, charity, aid, endowment, and freeing slaves without required intention are qurbat and taat but not ibadat; prayer, fasting, alms, pilgrimage, and jihad with intention are all three.
[ELMALILI:1:5/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Legal worship is conscious obedience and nearness directed only to God through the person's whole spiritual and bodily, outward and inward existence.
