## THE CURRENT MAP
80:19/q01 What does nuṭfa denote? [نُّطْفَةٍ · meaning]
  80:19/q01/p1 A small quantity of reproductive fluid from which a human is created. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the basic sense as a little water, with a passive noun pattern like a handful or scoop, and says usage came to mean reproductive fluid, the material of animal creation; Ibn ʿAṭiyya explicitly includes the man's and woman's fluids. Al-Qurṭubī, al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī, and al-Biqāʿī describe the drop as base, filthy, or slight; Ibn al-Jawzī calls it a sperm drop.
80:19/q02 Why describe the human origin as a drop? [مِن نُّطْفَةٍ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the mention chiefly belittles the origin to rebuke pride or displays the wonder of creation as proof of resurrection; Ibn ʿĀshūr expressly denies the former emphasis.)
  80:19/q02/p1 Its lowliness rebukes human arrogance, disobedience, and ingratitude. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī calls the fluid small, base, and lifeless and asks why a person is self-deceived; he reports al-Ḥasan's reminder that a person passed twice through the urinary passage. Al-Naḥḥās says obedience makes one noble despite lowly origin. Al-Biqāʿī contrasts filthy beginnings, bodily waste, and a foul corpse with knowledge and honor bestowed by the Creator, calling for gratitude. Al-Bursawī says the gifted beautiful form leaves no grounds for arrogance; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī links the indefinite drop to rejection of arrogance and disobedience. Al-Zamakhsharī, as reported by al-Burhān, treats the question as belittling the origin.
  80:19/q02/p2 The slight material highlights the wonder and wisdom of God's creation and the possibility of recreating the human. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says the focus is the greatness of creation, not rebuking pride; he links first creation to resurrection, citing 50:15 and 86:5–8. Al-Study Quran adduces 18:37, 16:4, 40:67, and 86:6–8 as parallels for creation from a drop and divine omnipotence. Al-Biqāʿī uses the contrast between lowly origin and bestowed honor in the resurrection argument.
80:19/q03 What does the indefinite wording contribute? [نُّطْفَةٍ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the indefinite expression signals a lowly origin or the context instead emphasizes the greatness of creation.)
  80:19/q03/p1 The indefinite wording belittles the drop and the human's origin. — reasons: Al-Suyūṭī's Itqān says the indefinite 'thing' in the preceding question points to low origin before the answer specifies a drop (80:18–19). Al-Ālūsī says belittlement may come from that indefinite 'thing' or from 'drop.' Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī attributes the belittlement specifically to the indefinite nuṭfa.
80:19/q04 Why is 'from a drop' put first? [مِن نُّطْفَةٍ · rhetoric]
  80:19/q04/p1 It echoes the preceding question and stresses the material from which the wondrous human is made. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says the fronted phrase matches the fronted question and alerts the listener to divine wisdom in making a remarkable creature from a simple or slight source.
80:19/q05 How is 'from a drop' connected to 'He created him'? [مِن نُّطْفَةٍ خَلَقَهُۥ · grammar] (turns on: The pause after 'from a drop' and whether an implied verb is supplied before the expressed 'He created him.')
  80:19/q05/p1 'From a drop' directly modifies 'He created him,' with no pause after the drop. — reasons: Al-Bursawī calls this the more apparent construction and says treating 'He created him, then proportioned him' as a new sentence would duplicate the idea of measured creation.
  80:19/q05/p2 Pause after 'from a drop,' supply an understood 'He created him,' and take 'He created him, then proportioned him' as a fresh explanation. — reasons: Al-Bursawī reports al-Sajāwandī's pause and its supplied verb; al-Bursawī objects that this makes the ensuing new sentence redundant because creating already involves measured quantities and qualities.
80:19/q06 How does the answer relate to the preceding question? [مِن نُّطْفَةٍ خَلَقَهُۥ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the response is treated as a formal answer to a question about substance or as apposition or emphasis rather than a literal answer.)
  80:19/q06/p1 It is formally a question and answer about the substance of creation, building anticipation and directing thought to the possibility of recreation. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the anticipation to the question about the great news in 78:1–2; he says the question asks from what substance the person was made, not whether God made him, and adduces 50:15 and 86:5–8 for the analogy to resurrection.
  80:19/q06/p2 The answer is not a literal answer but an apposition to the question, or an emphatic restatement. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī offers apposition and, alternatively, emphasis; he says the indefinite 'thing' or 'drop' supplies the belittling force.
80:19/q07 Why repeat 'He created him' in the answer? [خَلَقَهُۥ · rhetoric]
  80:19/q07/p1 The repetition emphasizes the precision of the remarkable creation and gives the answer balanced expression. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says the verb could be understood from the question but its expression calls attention to precision; he calls the result balanced, neither ellipsis nor excess.
80:19/q08 What does 'then proportioned him' mean? [فَقَدَّرَهُۥ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the measure concerns fitting bodily and mental design, prenatal developmental stages, allotted life and destiny, or stages across the lifespan; several sources combine these senses.)
  80:19/q08/p1 God fashioned and fitted the human's body, organs, qualities, and capacities in precise proportions. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj and those reporting him describe a proper or upright form and cite 18:37; al-Qurṭubī also cites 82:7. Al-Kalbī, Ibn al-Sāʾib, and the report from Ibn ʿAbbās name the head, eyes, hands, feet, appearance, beauty or ugliness, and height. Al-Rāzī says each organ's quantity and qualities suit its benefit, citing 25:2; al-Bursawī extends fitness to use and inward capacities. Al-Zamakhsharī speaks of what suits and distinguishes the person and invokes 25:2. Ibn ʿĀshūr defines taqdīr as precise ordered making, citing 25:2, and includes growth, faculties, intellect, agency, reflection, and chosen acts. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī extends measure to essence, attributes, and acts, with limits the human must respect; Asad includes organic functions of body and mind and adaptation to natural conditions. Al-Ālūsī says fitting limbs and forms explains the apt sequence with fa; al-Bayḍāwī gives this as one of two explanations.
  80:19/q08/p2 God determined the successive stages of development in the womb until the human was complete. — reasons: Muqātil details drop, clot, lump, bones, and finally spirit, with form determined before birth. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Qushayrī, Ibn Jurayj, and others name successive fetal stages; al-Wāḥidī reports Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil on determination in the womb. Al-Farrāʾ and those reporting him combine stages with sex and felicity. Al-Wāḥidī's Wajīz continues to birth and mentions 80:20; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī present this as an alternative to fitting form and organs.
  80:19/q08/p3 God apportioned each person's sex, lifespan, provision, deeds, and fortune or misery. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ combines male or female and wretched or blessed with fetal stages; Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds organs, lifespan, and other matters according to divine will. The report from Ibn ʿAbbās includes bodily appearance and felicity or misery. Ibn Kathīr specifies lifespan, provision, deeds, and destiny and uses this as evidence of recreation. Al-Bursawī links sex and fortune to development, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī extends divine measure to human acts and allotted limits.
  80:19/q08/p4 God measured the gradual stages of human life from childhood through youth to old age. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī compares these life stages to the moon's phases in 36:39 and adduces 23:12–16; their order points to divine power, purpose, resurrection, and judgment.
80:19/q09 How is the dāl in qaddarahu read? [فَقَدَّرَهُۥ · readings] (turns on: Whether the dāl is doubled in recitation.)
  80:19/q09/p1 The dāl is doubled: qaddarahu. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes this reading to the majority of readers.
  80:19/q09/p2 The dāl is pronounced without doubling. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes this variant to some readers.
80:19/q10 What does the fāʾ between creation and measuring convey? [خَلَقَهُۥ فَقَدَّرَهُۥ · grammar]
  80:19/q10/p1 Measured formation follows as a consequence of creation. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says the fāʾ marks measurement as creation's effect, equipping a person to grow, reason, act, and exercise judgment; he cites 25:2. Al-Ālūsī says interpreting measurement as fitting the human with suitable limbs and forms makes the sequence with fa apt.
80:19/q11 What are the three darknesses around the developing fetus? [* · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the three enclosing layers are identified as belly, womb, and placenta or as three functional membranes.)
  80:19/q11/p1 They may be the belly, womb, and placenta. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī presents this identification as a possibility.
  80:19/q11/p2 They are three membranes with different functions. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī reports anatomists: the placenta feeds the fetus through the navel, while the other membranes receive urine and bodily vapors.
80:19/q12 Do the past tense creation verbs describe a recurring event? [خَلَقَهُۥ فَقَدَّرَهُۥ · grammar]
  80:19/q12/p1 Yes; they describe a recurring phenomenon despite their past tense form. — reasons: Asad applies this to verses 18–22 as a sequence.
