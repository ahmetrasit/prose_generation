20:130/q29 Does confusing ḍād and ẓāʾ invalidate prayer? [وَسَبِّحْ · law]
  20:130/q29/p1 No; the letters are sufficiently similar and the distinction is difficult, so it is not an obligation. — reasons: Razi prefers this ruling: both share voicing, frication and emphatic quality; softened dad approaches za. He argues that no reported question to the Prophet or Companions imposed a distinction even as non-Arabs entered Islam. A quoted saying about the Prophet's eloquent dad underscores its distinctive Arabic articulation.
20:130/q30 What other recitation limits are reported for prayer? [وَسَبِّحْ · law]
  20:130/q30/p1 Untransmitted anomalous vowel readings are forbidden in prayer; emphatic lām cannot follow kasra. — reasons: Razi says a valid Qur'anic reading would be mass-transmitted, citing kasra in al-hamdu and damma in lillah; he does not extend that proof outside prayer. He reports disagreement over emphatic lam as dialect, but consensus against it after kasra because the transition is too hard.
20:130/q31 What general grammatical observations appear in the cited exception examples? [* · grammar]
