hetorical sciences of maʿānī and bayān are needed to grasp God's intended meanings. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr lists lexicon, morphology, syntax, maʿānī and bayān, plus established speech, poetry and native intuition; he says maʿānī and bayān expose detailed meaning and inimitability and were once called dalāʾil al-iʿjāz. Al-Zamakhsharī says law, kalām, reports, preaching, grammar or lexicon alone do not suffice and warns against harming the Qurʾān's fine structure and challenge. Al-Sakkākī requires both rhetorical sciences and calls God al-Ḥakīm for wise speech's elevated aims; his 'warns' and warning of woe target untrained interpretation, whose rare successes do not justify it. Al-Jurjānī says unusually rich Qurʾānic style requires the sciences absent natural eloquence. Ibn Rushd cites 26:195 for Arabic's necessity, calls denial ignorant, and says corruptly motivated denial warrants repentance and discretionary discipline. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes the aspiration to all divine meaning from meanings signaled by rhetoric and context; meanings are effectively inexhaustible and interpreters differ in acuity and knowledge.
75:18/q15 How do gathering and recitation relate inwardly and outwardly? [قُرْءَانَهُۥ · inward]
  75:18/q15/p1 Gathering is inward, whereas recitation is outward. — reasons: Al-Bursawī reports this distinction from al-Wāsiṭī.
75:18/q16 How well does the Ibn ʿAbbās revelation report fit the verses? [* · reports]
  75:18/q16/p1 The report says revelation weighed on the Prophet, who moved his lips; he was then told to listen and recite afterward. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes similar reports from Ibn Jubayr and others but finds the fit between this explanation and the verses obscure.
