[ITQAN:v1p74/r4] ITQAN, d. 911 · عبيد بن عمير | reports | Ubayd ibn Umayr reports that Gabriel instructed the Prophet to read and then recited اقرأ باسم ربك, which the Prophet called the first revelation.

[ITQAN:v1p75/r2] ITQAN, d. 911 · الزهري | reports | Al-Zuhrī recounts that while the Prophet was at Ḥirāʾ, an angel brought a silk sheet inscribed with the opening verses of al-ʿAlaq.
[ITQAN:v1p75/r4] ITQAN, d. 911 · author | prefers | One reconciliation distinguishes the first complete sura, al-Muddaththir, from the first verses revealed, the beginning of al-ʿAlaq.
[ITQAN:v1p75/r5] ITQAN, d. 911 · author | holds | A second authentic report places al-Muddaththir after the pause in revelation: the Prophet saw the same angel previously encountered at Ḥirāʾ, showing the Ḥirāʾ revelation came earlier.
[ITQAN:v1p75/r7] ITQAN, d. 911 · author | prefers | The final reconciliation says al-ʿAlaq was first for prophethood, while al-Muddaththir was first for the mission and command to warn.
[ITQAN:v1p76/r1] ITQAN, d. 911 · ابن حجر | reports | Ibn Hajar distinguishes the first revelation prompted by an earlier event—the Prophet's fright and covering himself—from “Read,” which was revealed initially without a prior cause. | mentions 74:1
[ITQAN:v1p76/r2] ITQAN, d. 911 · الكرماني | reports | Al-Kirmani argues that Jabir inferred his view rather than narrating it, so Aisha's transmitted report should take precedence.

[ITQAN:v2p494#3/r5] ITQAN, d. 911 · ابن هشام | rejects | Ibn Hisham notes that the first five verses of al-ʿAlaq were the earliest revelation, then kallā opened a new statement, which challenges the rebuke-only reading.
[ITQAN:v3p283/r2] ITQAN, d. 911 · أبو شامة | reports | Abu Shama counts six sūras that open with a command, including those beginning qul, iqraʾ, and sabbiḥ.

[ITQAN:v3p298/r7] ITQAN, d. 911 · الخطابي | reports | Al-Khaṭṭābī says the Companions placed al-Qadr after al-ʿAlaq, inferring that the pronoun in 'We sent it down' refers to the command 'Read'; Ibn al-ʿArabī calls the inference remarkable. | mentions 96:1, 97:1
[BURSEVI:v10p363#2/r1] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | holds | Calling God the Creator recalls the first blessing and proves that the One who gave humanity life and its perfections can teach reading; creation belongs solely to Him.
