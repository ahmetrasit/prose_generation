[IBNASHUR:92:7/r10] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | reports | One solution keeps facilitate literal and treats the construction as rhetorical inversion: the original sense is making the easy or hard path easy for the person, with inversion intensifying the facilitation.

[IBNASHUR:92:7/r12] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | reports | A second solution uses facilitate figuratively for preparation; li- is causal, and al-yusra and al-usra denote Paradise and Hell, with entry understood after the preposition.
[IBNASHUR:92:7/r13] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | On the preparation interpretation, making entry quick is likened to making a difficult thing easy, since difficulty is associated with delay and ease with speed; the cited verse uses easy in the sense of quick. | mentions 50:44
[IBNASHUR:92:7/r14] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The same reading makes facilitation follow the acts already produced by giving, restraint, affirmation, or denial, and therefore concerns continuing those acts or increasing them.
[IBNASHUR:92:7/r15] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | reports | A further possibility keeps facilitation literal without inversion: al-yusra and al-usra describe the corresponding conditions, and facilitation means persisting in the deed.
