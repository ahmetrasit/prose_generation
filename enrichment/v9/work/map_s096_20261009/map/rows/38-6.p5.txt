[IBNASHUR-FULL:v23p210/r8] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Ṣabr is persistence and attachment, originally said of tying an animal; in hardship it means staying composed without panic.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v23p210/r9] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The call to remain steadfast in worship of the gods parallels the people’s claim that the Prophet nearly led them astray had they not persisted in their worship. | mentions 25:42
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v23p212/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Their claim that this is a contrived matter meant for some ulterior purpose is intended to strengthen suspicion that the Prophet’s call is false.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v23p212/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The demonstrative refers to what they heard at the gathering: the Prophet’s call to say there is no god but God.
[TABATABAI:38:4-6/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The elites' departure after speaking suggests Quraysh's chiefs had gathered with the Prophet to negotiate over his call to monotheism and rejection of idols; their failure accords with reports on the occasion of revelation.
[TABATABAI:38:4-6/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The wording elides an introductory verb of saying: they left saying to one another, or possibly to their followers, to carry on and remain devoted to their gods.
[TABATABAI:38:4-6/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Their exhortation means not to abandon worship of their gods even though the Prophet criticized and disparaged them.
[TABATABAI:38:4-6/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | They take the Prophet's call as a means to a natural desire for sovereignty and leadership, like the accusation against Noah's messenger in 23:24. | mentions 23:24
[TABATABAI:38:7/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | rejects | One anonymous reading takes the phrase to mean that the Prophet's persistence and firmness show he seeks some great thing; the author finds it weak and inconsistent with the context.
[TABATABAI:38:7/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | rejects | Another anonymous reading treats the event as a calamity destined for them, leaving no option but to persist and endure; the author rejects it as contextually weak.
[TABATABAI:38:7/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | rejects | A third reading says patience is the virtue required of them in such circumstances; the author says this and similar readings do not fit the context.
[ASAD-NOTES:38:6#n9/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | The phrase means a thing desired or to be desired, hence a sensible course of action.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-6-2:p475/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Like the previous sūrahs in this group, Ṣād is founded on tawḥīd; Quraysh oppose its reminder from arrogance and polytheism and will believe only when it is too late.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-6-2:p475/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Verses 1–11 present the Qur’an as a reminder rejected from arrogance, paralleling earlier nations; Quraysh too will believe only after seeing their fate, when faith is useless.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-6-2:p475/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Quraysh cannot accept a messenger from among themselves, so they call him sorcerer and liar; their leaders rally people to defend idols and ancestral religion.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-6-2:p481/r5] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | When people were influenced by the Prophet’s monotheist message, Quraysh leaders would leave and urge others to leave too, avoid listening, and remain devoted to their deities.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-6-2:p481/r6] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The leaders present this as the proper course: resist the religion’s effort to make them abandon their ancestral faith and keep worshipping their gods until death.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-6-2:p481/r7] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | They tell the masses that Muhammad seeks to make them forsake the religion of their ancestors, so they must cling to their gods despite his effort.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:38:5/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Muhammad’s uncompromising monotheism and rejection of inherited polytheism astonished the pagans; Meccan leaders came to see their traditions, status, and interests threatened.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:38:5/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | Hadith, tafsir, and historical accounts say that after ridicule and insults failed, a delegation including Abu Jahl asked Abu Talib to withdraw his protection; Muhammad defended monotheism and invited them to a faith promising a bright future, and they angrily left.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:38:5/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | One reported reading takes the leaders’ words to mean that monotheism is Muhammad’s political plan to rule them and make them his dependents, which they will reject.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:38:5/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | Another reported interpretation treats the situation as a calamity destined by time, from which escape is impossible.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:38:5/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | prefers | The authors prefer the reading that the pagans urge one another to hold fast to their religion and defeat Muhammad; this is reflected in their rendering, “That is what is asked of you.”
