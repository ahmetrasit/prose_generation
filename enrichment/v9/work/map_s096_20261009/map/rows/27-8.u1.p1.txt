  27:8/q12/p1 Ubayy and others read 'the fire was blessed' (būrikat al-nār). — reasons: Reports attribute it to Ubayy, Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid and ʿIkrima. Wāḥidī says this meaning does not fit the standard reading.
  27:8/q12/p2 Ubayy read 'the earth and those around it were blessed'. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī, Rāzī and Ālūsī cite it for the blessed-place interpretation.
  27:8/q12/p3 An explanatory reading specifies the angels around the fire. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī report transmission from Ubayy, Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid and ʿIkrima; both note it differs from the written codex and is generally classed as commentary.
27:8/q13 Who or what is blessed as 'the one in the fire'? [مَن فِى ٱلنَّارِ · referent] (turns on: Whether man is personal or nonpersonal; whether 'in' is literal location, nearness, ellipsis for place or divine power, or a spiritual reading.)
  27:8/q13/p1 God, disclosed by His light and speech, without spatial enclosure. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Jubayr, al-Ḥasan and others are reported to identify God; reports say His light was in the tree or that He called from light. Wāḥidī analogizes 'God came from Sinai' to being known there. Ibn al-Jawzī takes it as the holy caller, not indwelling; Qurṭubī and Ālūsī insist manifestation entails no location. Rāzī calls the Ibn ʿAbbās attribution fabricated; Abū Ḥayyān reinterprets sound reports as God's power. Baghawī cites a Torah description of God's coming from Sinai, Seir and Paran, associating them with Moses, Jesus and Muhammad and Paran with Mecca.
  27:8/q13/p2 Moses, present at or seeking the place of the fire. — reasons: Wāḥidī prefers an implied 'seeker of the fire'; nearness can count as being in it, says Abū ʿAlī. Thaʿlabī and Rāzī likewise read the phrase as God's greeting to Moses, with angels around him. Qurṭubī reports an omitted fī or nearby location. Ṭabāṭabāʾī finds Moses among those around the fire in the Ṭā Hā parallel, but reports his placement inside as another view.
  27:8/q13/p3 Angels stationed in the fire or light. — reasons: Al-Suddī says angels were inside; Naḥḥās reports Qatāda naming both inner and outer groups angels. Ṭabrisī says they praised God aloud. Nasafī links their blessing to Moses' commissioning and miracles; some sources put Moses around them.
  27:8/q13/p4 God's light or the luminous sign within the fire. — reasons: Qatāda, al-Ḥasan, Ibn ʿĪsā and others name the light. Wāḥidī says man can mean mā and God created light in what Moses saw; Qurṭubī also reports blessing a sign placed in it. Study Quran distinguishes the light within the fire from fire itself. Samīn reports nonrational man for the light, coupled with surrounding places.
  27:8/q13/p5 The fire itself. — reasons: Mujāhid and Ibn ʿAbbās report this repeatedly. Some treat min as mā and mā or min as extra; Ubayy's explanatory reading directly says the fire was blessed. Wāḥidī objects that the standard wording does not support it, while Thaʿlabī, Baghawī and others invoke the blessed spot of 28:30.
  27:8/q13/p6 The location of the fire, the blessed plot or valley; in its widest sense the Syrian region. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī take an implied place and sometimes all its inhabitants. They invoke 28:30's blessed plot and 21:71's Syrian land, home and burial place of prophets and revelation. God spoke, commissioned Moses and showed miracles there; Ubayy's earth reading supports it. Bursawī adds the fertility poem.
  27:8/q13/p7 God's power, authority, dominion, or proof manifested there. — reasons: Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Ṭabrisī and others supply an omitted noun; Abū Ḥayyān says sound God-referent reports must be read this way. The reading denies bodily presence.
  27:8/q13/p8 The unburned tree within the fire. — reasons: Māwardī and Abū Ḥayyān report the tree itself as the blessed referent; Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports it among interpretations. Ṭabāṭabāʾī judges most of the reported identifications forced, without specifying this one individually.
  27:8/q13/p9 Inwardly, Moses' heart reaching the fire of divine attributes. — reasons: Qāshānī says the heart gains true perfections and the prophetic station of intimate speech.
  27:8/q13/p10 Metaphorically, prophets' illumination, with Moses in it and believers around him. — reasons: Asad takes the light as divine illumination granted to prophets; the Turkish commentary reports his Moses-and-believers application.
27:8/q14 Who or what is 'around it' and included in the blessing? [وَمَنْ حَوْلَهَا · referent] (turns on: Whether 'who' designates attending persons, nearby land, divine light, or inward faculties.)
  27:8/q14/p1 The angels. — reasons: Muqātil, Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan, ʿIkrima and others repeatedly identify them; Wāḥidī says they stood around the light praising and sanctifying God. Some explanatory readings append 'of the angels'.
  27:8/q14/p2 Moses together with the angels. — reasons: Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb connects the next verse's address to Moses by name; Wāḥidī calls this universal, and Thaʿlabī and Baghawī frame it as a greeting. The site and people may both receive blessing.
  27:8/q14/p3 Moses alone, near the fire. — reasons: Abū Ṣakhr reports Moses around God's light; Baghawī and Ṭabrisī place him near but outside it, while angels are inside. Abū ʿAlī distinguishes nearby from entering using 9:101; Ibn al-Jawzī reports Moses alone.
  27:8/q14/p4 The surrounding places and blessed Syrian land, possibly with all angels. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān allows nonrational man for places; Biqāʿī includes all angels and sacred lands across time; Ālūsī describes the valley and neighboring prophetic land.
  27:8/q14/p5 God's light. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj reports this as one of the surrounding referents; Asad describes divine light encompassing spiritual illumination. Asad cites al-Zamakhsharī for the lexical sense of ḥawlahā, 'around it'.
  27:8/q14/p6 Inwardly, Moses' spiritual faculties and celestial angels. — reasons: Qāshānī says they receive unveiling, secret wisdom, sacred confirmations and states of spiritual tasting.
  27:8/q14/p7 Moses' believers in prophetic spiritual illumination. — reasons: Asad's metaphorical account is reported by Kur'an Yolu.
27:8/q15 Why is blessing announced at the start of the call? [بُورِكَ مَن فِى ٱلنَّارِ وَمَنْ حَوْلَهَا · interpretation] (turns on: Whether blessing functions as a greeting, announces a sanctified event and place, or carries optative force.)
  27:8/q15/p1 It honors and greets Moses and the angels, reassuring him before his commission. — reasons: Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī and Qurṭubī compare the greeting of Abraham's household in 11:73. Abū Ḥayyān says it prepares Moses for conversation; Iṣlāḥī says it dispels fear of a voice in the dark valley. Ṭabāṭabāʾī links it to his purification and election in 20:11–14.
  27:8/q15/p2 It marks the place and people as blessed by God's speech, Moses' prophethood and the miracles. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī, Rāzī and Bursevī name the event's religious consequences and blessing spread through Syria. Bayḍāwī says the opening gives glad tidings of the great affair; Ālūsī adds the valley's prophetic history. Elmalılı calls a prophetic station sacred.
  27:8/q15/p3 The blessing is a prayer form used as a report. — reasons: Ṭabrisī says the prayer form intends a declarative statement; Biqāʿī describes a supplication for the fire seeker.
27:8/q16 Who says 'Glory be to God' here? [وَسُبْحَٰنَ ٱللَّهِ · referent] (turns on: Whether the doxology is in the divine call, Moses' response, the attendants' response, or a parenthetical address.)
  27:8/q16/p1 It is part of God's call to Moses. — reasons: Ibn Shajara says God speaks it and blesses the one who glorifies Him, but Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī find that expansion poorly supported. Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and Bursevī place the doxology within the call; Biqāʿī may coordinate it as a predicate.
  27:8/q16/p2 Moses exclaimed it on hearing the call. — reasons: Al-Suddī says he sought God's aid and declared Him transcendent; Bayḍāwī says he expressed wonder. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers a doctrinal declaration over the mere wonder reading.
  27:8/q16/p3 Those around the fire said it. — reasons: Qurṭubī reports an omitted speech verb: 'those around it said, Glory be to God.'
  27:8/q16/p4 It is a parenthetical address to Muhammad. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn and Ālūsī report a second-person address interrupting the two speeches.
27:8/q17 What does the glorification assert or do? [سُبْحَٰنَ ٱللَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ · theology] (turns on: Whether the primary force is divine transcendence, wonder at the event, or inward purification.)
  27:8/q17/p1 It declares God free of defect, evil, created likeness, embodiment and spatial limits. — reasons: Zajjāj and language scholars define subḥān as exalting God above evil; Wāḥidī, Baghawī and Abū ʿUbayd say imperfection. Ṭabrisī specifies no body, substrate or speech instrument; Rāzī says it protects divine essence and wisdom. Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī, Ālūsī, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Iṣlāḥī use it to block a localizing or humanizing inference from the fire and speech.
  27:8/q17/p2 It expresses wonder and announces that the Lord of all worlds wills this momentous event. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Bursevī, Ālūsī and Elmalılı connect the phrase with the encounter's greatness; Biqāʿī says lordship reflects God's complete essence and acts, including His care for Moses as child and adult. Iṣlāḥī urges trust in the universal Lord's providence.
