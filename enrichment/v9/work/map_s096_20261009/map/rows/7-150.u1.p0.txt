7:150/q01 When did Moses learn of the calf worship? [وَلَمَّا رَجَعَ مُوسَىٰٓ إِلَىٰ قَوْمِهِۦ · reports] (turns on: Whether 7:150 describes anger before arrival and whether 20:85 supplies prior notice.)
  7:150/q01/p1 God had informed Moses at the mountain before his return; Ṭā Hā 20:85 supports this. — reasons: MUQATIL (author): Moses returned angry and sorrowful over calf worship, already informed by God on the Mount. TAB-FULL (author): Moses returned angry and grieved after God told him the Samaritan had led his tested people astray. IBNABIHATIM (author): Prophet: God informed Moses but he cast tablets only after seeing idolatry. IBNATIYYA-FULL (الطبري): Al-Tabari says God informed Moses before his return that the Israelites had been led astray by the calf, so Moses returned angry. RAZI-FULL (أبو مسلم): Abu Muslim says Moses already knew beforehand; al-Razi prefers this because the verse says he was angry while returning, before reaching them, and Ta Ha says God told him at the appointed meeting. | mentions 20:85 RAZI-FULL (author): Al-Razi’s first argument for prior knowledge is that Moses was already angry and grieving while still on his way, before reaching the people. RAZI-FULL (author): His second argument is that Ta Ha says God informed Moses of the event at the appointed meeting. | mentions 20:85 QURTUBI-FULL (الطبري): Al-Ṭabarī says God had told Moses before he returned that the people had been tested by the calf, so Moses came back angry. ABUHAYYAN-FULL (الطبري): Al-Tabari says God informed Moses before he returned that his people had been seduced by the calf, explaining his anger; the author links this to the verse in Ta-Ha. | mentions 20:85 IBNKATHIR-FULL (ابن عباس عن النبي): The Prophet says Moses did not throw the tablets when God informed him that his people had fallen into trial; seeing them himself moved him to throw them, for seeing is unlike hearing. BIQAI-FULL (author): Moses returned from intimate communion angry and sorrowful because God had informed him that his people worshipped the calf; his anger was for God. DURR-FULL (النبي): The Prophet says seeing is unlike hearing: God told Moses his people had been led astray, but when Moses saw them he cast the tablets and some broke. BURSEVI (author): Moses returned from Mount Sinai intensely angry; the word asaf means anger, as in “when they angered Us.” God had informed him during the conversation that his people worshipped the calf. | mentions 43:55 ALUSI-FULL (ابن عباس): Ibn Abbas reports from the Prophet that God had told Moses his people were tested, but Moses did not cast the tablets until he saw them; some then broke. TABATABAI (author): Moses returned angry after God told him that his people had gone astray; he reproached them for badly succeeding him and hastening their Lord's affair. TABATABAI (author): The opening says Moses already knew of the people's apostasy before seeing them, because God told him at the appointment, as Ta-Ha recounts. | mentions 20:85 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR (İbn Cerîr et-Taberî): Tabari says God informed Moses at Sinai that his people had fallen into this trial; 20:85 supports this view. | mentions 20:85 STUDYQURAN (author): Moses was already angry and aggrieved before seeing the people because God told him on the mountain that they had gone astray. | mentions 20:86, 20:83, 20:84, 20:85 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR (author): The Torah's account also agrees with Tabari's view. KASHSHAF-FULL (author): One report says the Samaritan told them Moses had died and would not return when he displayed the calf. ABUHAYYAN-FULL (الزمخشري): Al-Zamakhshari reports that the Samaritan told them the calf was their and Moses' god and Moses would not return because he had died.
  7:150/q01/p2 Moses learned of the calf worship only on encountering the people. — reasons: RAZI-FULL (author): The verse neither rules out Moses knowing of the calf worship beforehand nor requires it; he could have learned on reaching the people. RAZI-FULL (قوم): Some say Moses discovered the calf worship only when he confronted the people on arriving. KURANYOLU-TEFSIR (İbn İshak): Ibn Ishaq's report says Moses did not know his people had worshipped the calf until he returned from Sinai.
  7:150/q01/p3 The verse itself leaves the timing of Moses' knowledge open. — reasons: RAZI-FULL (author): The verse neither rules out Moses knowing of the calf worship beforehand nor requires it; he could have learned on reaching the people.
7:150/q02 What does أَسِفًا mean here? [أَسِفًۭا · meaning] (turns on: Arabic usage, 43:55, poetic and hadith examples, and whether the word denotes anger, sorrow, or both.)
  7:150/q02/p1 Intense anger, beyond ordinary anger — reasons: TAB-FULL (author): Asaf is intense anger and vexation. TAB-FULL (author): Abu al-Dardāʾ makes asaf a degree beyond anger. ZAJJAJ (author): Asaf means intense anger, as 43:55. MAWARDI-FULL (الأخفش): Al-Akhfash glosses asaf as intense anger. MAWARDI-FULL (السدي): Al-Suddi glosses asaf as being incensed. WAHIDI-BASIT (أبو الدرداء، عطاء عن ابن عباس، الزجاج): They explain asaf as intense anger; the passage cites 43:55 as evidence, and al-Zajjaj is said to prefer this view. | mentions 43:55 BAGHAWI-FULL (أبو الدرداء): Abū al-Dardāʾ says أسفا means intense anger; Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Suddī say it means grief, the most intense grief. KASHSHAF-FULL (author): Asaf means intense anger, as in the linked verse about God's anger and punishment; another view takes it to mean grief. | mentions 43:55 TABRISI (أبو الدرداء): Abū al-Dardāʾ explains asaf as intense anger. TABRISI (أبو مسلم): Abū Muslim takes anger and asaf to mean the same thing, repeated for emphasis with different wording, as illustrated by poetry. IBNJAWZI-ZAD (ابن قتيبة): Ibn Qutayba and al-Zajjaj explain asaf as intense anger. IBNJAWZI-ZAD (أبو الدرداء): Abu al-Darda says asaf is a state beyond anger and more severe than it. RAZI-FULL (أبو الدرداء وعطاء عن ابن عباس): Al-Dahhak? No: Abu al-Darda and Ata, reporting from Ibn Abbas, define asaf as intense anger; al-Zajjaj chooses this sense, supported by 43:55. | mentions 43:55 QURTUBI-FULL (author): Asafan means intense anger; Abū al-Dardāʾ says it is a degree beyond anger and more intense. SAMIN-DURR (author): Alternatively أسفا is apposition to غضبان: partitive if it means intensely angry, or apposition of inclusion if it means grieved; their closeness makes this possible. SAMIN-DURR (author): أسف can mean that anger intensified, as in the Qurʾanic usage in 43:55. | mentions 43:55 IBNKATHIR-FULL (أبو الدرداء): Abu al-Darda explains أسف as the most intense anger. DURR-FULL (أبو الدرداء): Abu al-Darda says asaf is a state beyond anger and more intense than it. DURR-FULL (محمد بن كعب): Muhammad ibn Ka'b says asaf means intense anger. BURSEVI (author): Moses returned from Mount Sinai intensely angry; the word asaf means anger, as in “when they angered Us.” God had informed him during the conversation that his people worshipped the calf. | mentions 43:55 ALUSI-FULL (أبو الدرداء ومحمد القرظي وعطاء والزجاج): They take أسفا to mean intense anger, while other authorities take it to mean sorrow. STUDYQURAN (author): Asifan can mean sadness as well as intense anger. WAHIDI-BASIT (الأعشى): Al-A'sha uses asif for anger, picturing a man as if his severed hand were stained with blood. SAMIN-DURR (author): أسف describes an established, settled quality, while أسف on the فاعل pattern is used for a temporary state. BURHAN (ابن عباس): Ibn Abbas explains غضبان أسفا as “enraged.” ELMALILI (Ebu’d-Derdâ): Abu al-Darda and many exegetes prefer the sense of intense anger, since the passage centers on Moses’ anger. ELMALILI (author): Elmalılı favors the intense-anger reading in context, rendering the phrase as Moses returning angry and extremely angry. SAMIN-DURR (author): Al-Aʿshā's description of a man as أسيف uses the word in the sense of angry.
  7:150/q02/p2 Grief or sadness — reasons: TAB-FULL (author): Al-Suddī glosses asaf as sadness. TAB-FULL (author): Ibn Abbas: asaf means sadness here but anger in 43:55. MAWARDI-FULL (ابن عباس): Ibn Abbas glosses asaf as grief. WAHIDI-BASIT (الكلبي): Al-Kalbi specifically glosses asaf as grief over the calf worship. WAHIDI-BASIT (ابن عباس، السدي، الحسن): Ibn Abbas, al-Suddi, and al-Hasan explain asaf as grief; al-Kalbi gives the same sense. TABRISI (ابن عباس): Ibn ʿAbbās explains asaf as grief. IBNJAWZI-NUZHA (author): Asaf means grief here, as in Jacob's lament for Joseph. | mentions 12:84 IBNJAWZI-ZAD (ابن عباس): One explanation of asaf is grief; al-Hasan and al-Suddi share this view. RAZI-FULL (ابن عباس والحسن والسدي): Ibn Abbas, al-Hasan, and al-Suddi also interpret asaf as grief; Aisha described Abu Bakr as asif, meaning sorrowful. BAYDAWI-FULL (author): Asifan means intensely angry, or it may mean sorrowful. ABUHAYYAN-FULL (author): Asaf means grief; the verb is used as “he grieved.” ABUHAYYAN-FULL (ابن عباس والحسن والسدي): Ibn Abbas, al-Hasan, and al-Suddi interpret asaf as sorrowful. ABUHAYYAN-FULL (الزمخشري وابن عطية): Al-Zamakhshari and Ibn Atiyya say asaf can mean grieving or intensely angry, and it most often means sorrowful. SAMIN-DURR (author): أسف can also mean grief; a poem describing a time passing in sorrow illustrates the meaning. DURR-FULL (ابن عباس): Ibn Abbas explains asaf as grief or sadness. ALUSI-FULL (ابن عباس والحسن وقتادة): Ibn Abbas, al-Hasan, and Qatada are reported to understand أسفا as sorrow. IBNABIHATIM (author): Hasan and Malik ibn Dinar: sorrow. WAHIDI-BASIT (عائشة): Aisha called Abu Bakr an asif, an example of the word's grief sense. WAHIDI-BASIT (أبو عبيد): Abu Ubayd defines asif as one who is quick to grief and melancholy. DURR-FULL (قتادة): Qatada says Moses was sad because of what his people did after he left them. QURTUBI-FULL (ابن عباس والسدي): They explain that Moses returned saddened by what his people had done. SAMIN-DURR (author): A hadith calls Abū Bakr a man أسيف, meaning grieved.
