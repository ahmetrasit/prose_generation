[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p47/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · فضيل بن عياض | reports | Fuḍayl ibn ʿIyāḍ interprets the path as the route of pilgrimage.

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p47/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · عمرو بن عبيد | reports | ʿAmr ibn ʿUbayd identifies the path as the bridge over Hell.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p47/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الجنيد | reports | Al-Junayd interprets asking for guidance amid bewilderment as asking for the qualities of servanthood rather than absorption in God's eternal attributes; Abū Ḥayyān says such mystical readings exceed the wording.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p47/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الفخر الرازي | reports | Al-Rāzī objects that prior recipients of divine favor could not have followed the Qur'an or Islamic law; Abū Ḥayyān replies that this assumes, without proof, that the blessed are only predecessors.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p47/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The plural suffix links ihdinā to We worship and We seek help: those who confess worship ask together for clear guidance, since worship requires reaching its proper path.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p48/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · جعفر الصادق | reports | Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq reads “straight path” in annexation, meaning the religion that is upright.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p48/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | On al-Ḥasan’s and al-Ḍaḥḥāk’s reading, “the path of those” is a definite apposition to an indefinite, like “a straight path, God’s path.” | mentions 43:52
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p48/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | On al-Ṣādiq’s and the majority’s readings, “the path of those” is an apposition of one definite to another, clarifying the previously somewhat vague “straight path.”
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p48/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | This apposition restates the request for guidance, making it firmer and more emphatic by directing it to the path of those God favored.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p54/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The shift to direct address in “You alone we worship,” “You alone we seek help,” and “guide us” is iltifāt.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p54/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | “Guide us” asks for the continuance of guidance, not its initial acquisition alone.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:6#2/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ihd has pattern ifʿ, with its final weak yāʾ deleted by analogy to the jussive, where a weak final letter drops.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:6#2/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Some color ṣād toward zāy: Khalaf does so wherever the word occurs, Khallād only in its first occurrence, and pure zāy is also reported; the muṣḥaf writes ṣād.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:6#2/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ṣirāṭ is masculine in Tamīm and feminine in the Hijaz; its plural patterns vary with gender.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:6#2/r12] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Mustaqīm is an active participle of istaqāma with the simple verb's sense, meaning straight; its underlying form is mustaqwim, altered like nastaʿīn.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:6#2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Hidāya can mean guidance, direction, or going ahead, as in the forelocks of horses; Imruʾ al-Qays uses the latter sense.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:6#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · امرؤ القيس | reports | The cited line uses al-hādiyāt for those preceding the rest of the horses.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:6#2/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Hidāya also means making clear, as in the statement that Thamūd were shown the way, and inspiration toward what benefits each creature. | mentions 41:17, 20:50
[SAMIN-DURR:1:6#2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Another reported sense is supplication or calling, while deriving it here from inclination is mistaken because that belongs to a different root. | mentions 13:7, 7:56
[SAMIN-DURR:1:6#2/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الراغب | reports | Al-Rāghib defines guidance as gentle indication; hadaytu is for directing, while ahdaytu is for giving a gift.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:6#2/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Al-ṣirāṭ is a readily traversed road, though some omit the qualification of ease; cited lines simply attest the road sense.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:6#2/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ṣirāṭ derives from swallowing: either the traveler swallows the road or the road swallows the traveler; this also explains calling it a mouthful or something that swallows.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:6#2/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The original consonant is sīn; Qunbul reads it, while the common ṣād is an assimilation near the emphatic sound, as in cited lexical parallels.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:6/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ihdinā is an imperative form used here as a supplication; a command from superior to inferior is an order, the reverse a prayer, and one between equals a request.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:6/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The subject is obligatorily implicit anta; nā is the first object and a single attached pronoun form for first-person plural or royal first person across nominative, accusative, and genitive positions.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:6/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The claim that yāʾ has the same one form across cases fails because nominative yāʾ addresses a woman, unlike the first-person yāʾ in accusative and genitive use.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:6/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | Hum can look alike across cases, but nominative hum is detached while accusative and genitive hum are attached, unlike nā.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:6/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Al-ṣirāṭ is the second object, and al-mustaqīm is its adjective, agreeing in the four relevant features.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:6/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Hadā normally governs its first object directly and its second with ilā or lām; the preposition is omitted here, yielding direct government of both objects. | mentions 42:52, 17:9
[SAMIN-DURR:1:6/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · البصريون | reports | Basrans hold that the imperative is indeclinable, whereas Kufans treat it as inflected.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:6/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The Kufan derivation of iḍrib from an imperative jussive with deleted lām and imperfect prefix is unnecessary; the author reserves a fuller refutation for a better place.
