7:172/q22 How does the covenant establish proof despite forgotten testimony? [أَن تَقُولُوا۟ يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ إِنَّا كُنَّا عَنْ هَٰذَا غَٰفِلِينَ · theology] (turns on: Whether an unremembered primordial pledge, present innate knowledge, or a messenger's renewed disclosure supplies binding evidence.)
  7:172/q22/p1 The original pledge binds, and prophetic reminders or innate knowledge restore its force despite forgetting the scene. — reasons: Al-Turtushi compares a forgotten witnessed divorce; Wāḥidī and Baghawī stress a truthful miracle-bearing messenger. Defenders say essential knowledge persists although the occasion fades; Tabatabai says resurrection removes worldly veils.
  7:172/q22/p2 The proof is innate reason, self-awareness and evident signs present in earthly life. — reasons: Creation shows a governor; an adult can infer God from self and world. Ibn Kathir argues this is independent of accepting an otherwise deniable report, and Tabatabai says everyone senses dependence regardless of status.
  7:172/q22/p3 A forgotten pre-birth event cannot justly ground responsibility. — reasons: Mu'tazilites and Tabarsi argue an intelligent multitude should remember such testimony, else transmigration becomes plausible; unintelligent particles cannot pledge. Tabatabai asks whether accountability rests on vanished reason, bare recognition, or forgotten words, and says ordinary worldly causes suffice.
  7:172/q22/p4 Responsibility for basic monotheism holds even without a prophet; the prophetic message adds a second proof. — reasons: Islahi says innate guidance leaves no excuse; later revelation doubles responsibility. Biqai instead says mercy delayed accountability until messengers came.
7:172/q23 What does the testimony prevent people from saying on Judgment Day? [أَن تَقُولُوا۟ يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ · interpretation]
  7:172/q23/p1 They cannot plead ignorance of God's lordship or blame inherited association of partners. — reasons: The next verse gives the ancestral excuse; the witnesses and later reminders block both. Some sources specifically apply this to Arab polytheists.
7:172/q24 What does the covenant report say about predestination and deeds? [أَخَذَ رَبُّكَ ذُرِّيَّتَهُمْ · theology] (turns on: Whether differentiation at the extraction defines individual fate and how divine decree relates to human choice and justice.)
  7:172/q24/p1 God divided descendants into people of Paradise and Fire, and enables each to perform the deeds of the assigned destination. — reasons: Umar and other reports answer the challenge 'what are deeds for?' with facilitation until death; versions mention right and left, white and black, two handfuls, fixed lifespans and provision. Tirmidhi grades Umar's route hasan but notes disconnection.
  7:172/q24/p2 Decreed outcomes and human choice coexist because choice is part of the complete causes of destiny. — reasons: Tabatabai cites 2:148 and 7:29–30: destinations call for fitting acts, while human choice is a partial cause. Umar responds that people should strive.
  7:172/q24/p3 The reported allotment is not injustice because God knows what each person will do and is answerable to none. — reasons: Nahhas appeals to foreknowledge; Ibn al-Arabi says creator and creatures cannot be judged by identical limits and all acts belong to God.
  7:172/q24/p4 The verse itself gives a common covenant, while destiny reports add a distinct feature. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says Malik's report explains an implication or adds material elicited by a question; Study Quran prefers universal potential for spiritual attainment.
7:172/q25 What distinct covenants or reminders accompanied the acknowledgment? [أَلَسْتُ بِرَبِّكُمْ · reports] (turns on: Whether the verse's lordship covenant alone is in view, or reports and related verses add prophetic, communal or Imamic obligations.)
  7:172/q25/p1 God promised messengers and books to remind humanity of the first pledge and warn against shirk. — reasons: Ubayy's report and variants say prophets were sent as reminders; some connect 30:30, 53:56 and 7:102 with the covenant.
  7:172/q25/p2 Prophets received an additional covenant to convey revelation and messengership. — reasons: Ubayy describes them shining like lamps and relates this separate charge to 33:7; Tustari likewise describes taking prophets from Adam's loins.
  7:172/q25/p3 The universal direct covenant grounds later particular covenants with prophets and religious communities. — reasons: Study Quran lists believers, Abraham, Israelites, Christians and People of the Book, and distinguishes later mediated obligations.
  7:172/q25/p4 Some early Twelver reports add affirmation of Muhammad and the Imams, with only some accepting the Imams. — reasons: Tabatabai also reports an Imami shadow-world test and an acknowledgment conditioned by divine love or hatred.
7:172/q26 What do the reports say about Adam, David, and the displayed descendants? [ذُرِّيَّتَهُمْ · reports] (turns on: The reports differ in numerical details of lifespan and what inequalities Adam observed.)
  7:172/q26/p1 Adam gave David forty years from his thousand-year life after seeing David's light, then forgot or denied it at death. — reasons: Versions give David sixty years before the gift, record it while the pens were wet, or say Adam died at 960; descendants inherit forgetfulness and denial. Al-Tirmidhi grades the Abu Hurayra report hasan sahih.
  7:172/q26/p2 Adam gave David forty years from a lifespan reported in another form as sixty years. — reasons: One Ibn Kathir route phrases Adam's own allotted years as sixty, diverging from the widely reported thousand; variants are kept separate by their numerical detail.
  7:172/q26/p3 God varied descendants' wealth, appearance or afflictions so that people would give thanks. — reasons: Adam asked why they were unequal; God said He loved to be thanked. Some versions specify illness, poverty or outward appearance.
7:172/q27 How sound are the reports used to explain the verse? [* · reports] (turns on: Whether reports are raised or stopped, whether chains include missing or unknown transmitters, and whether broad agreement outweighs variants.)
  7:172/q27/p1 The Umar report has a broken or uncertain chain but is graded hasan or supported by other routes. — reasons: Abu Umar and Tirmidhi say Muslim ibn Yasar did not hear from Umar; Yahya ibn Ma'in questions Muslim and Nu'aym. Qurtubi accepts its meaning from other routes; Ibn Kathir explains Malik's omitted intermediary and Darqutni prefers corroborating versions.
  7:172/q27/p2 The extraction-and-address wording is more securely a Companion statement than a Prophetic report. — reasons: Tabari notes reliable transmitters stop at Abdullah ibn Amr; Ibn Kathir prefers Ibn Abbas and Ibn Amr stopped versions. Al-Hakim calls the raised Ibn Abbas chain sound, but Ibn Kathir says stronger and more numerous versions stop there.
  7:172/q27/p3 The numerous extraction reports support a real covenant in broad meaning, despite individual transmission issues. — reasons: Qurtubi cites several Companions; Tabatabai says Sunni and Shi'i routes make semantic mass transmission plausible. Ibn Kathir accepts extraction and division but separates them from the explicit testimony wording.
  7:172/q27/p4 The wording 'He showed them Himself' is preferable to 'He showed them His creation.' — reasons: Tabatabai suspects al-Ma'ani paraphrased the former to avoid corporeality; he prefers al-Kafi and al-Ayyashi, and denies corporeality follows. One narration supports literal witnessing.
7:172/q28 What happens to children who die before later responsibility? [ذُرِّيَّتَهُمْ · theology] (turns on: Whether the first acknowledgment benefited every child equally or the sincerity of the primordial response affects their outcome.)
  7:172/q28/p1 They remain on the first covenant and fitra, with Paradise or purity attributed to them. — reasons: Ibn Abbas distinguishes the first covenant from the later accountable pledge; Muqatil and Qurtubi extend this to children of polytheists. Al-Hasan's circle says their Paradise bliss rewards primordial faith.
  7:172/q28/p2 Unwilling testimony does not benefit children of the wretched. — reasons: Wāḥidī records opponents of Muqatil's view who argue the wretched confessed under compulsion.
  7:172/q28/p3 Children of polytheists serve Paradise's people because they never reached their parents' shirk. — reasons: A report from Anas gives this particular outcome while retaining their first-covenant status.
7:172/q29 How is the Black Stone connected with the covenant? [وَأَشْهَدَهُمْ · reports] (turns on: Whether its benefit is intrinsic, only obedient imitation, or covenantal testimony by God's leave.)
  7:172/q29/p1 The covenant record was placed in the Stone, which witnesses or will testify for those touching it rightly. — reasons: Ali says it can benefit and harm by God's Book; reports give it eyes and a tongue at Judgment. Fatima bint Husayn and Ja'far call touching it fulfillment of the pledge.
  7:172/q29/p2 The Stone itself cannot harm or benefit; Umar kisses it because the Prophet did. — reasons: Ali answers Umar with the covenant-witness account.
  7:172/q29/p3 It is God's right hand on earth and a stone from Paradise. — reasons: Ibn Abbas likens touching it to greeting God; Ata says impurities altered its original state.
7:172/q30 What bearing does the fixed covenant have on coitus interruptus? [ذُرِّيَّتَهُمْ · law]
  7:172/q30/p1 It is permissible or futile as a means of preventing a decreed life: every covenanted person will emerge. — reasons: Ali ibn Husayn applied the verse to the practice; reports say a decreed child would be born even if seed fell on a stone.
