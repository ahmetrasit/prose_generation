sal: every name of any referent, without restriction or prior specification.
[TABATABAI:2:31/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The wording 'displayed them' indicates that each name's referent has life and knowledge while remaining concealed in the unseen.
[TABATABAI:2:31/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Although the genitive could sometimes be partitive, here the context of manifesting God's full power and the angels' deficiency requires a possessive relation, not 'some of.'
[TABATABAI:2:31/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Thus the names are realities hidden from the heavenly and earthly realms, beyond the universe's encompassing domain.
[TABATABAI:2:31/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī links this account to 15:21: everything has imperishable treasuries with God, unmeasured and unlimited; limits arise only at the stage of descent and creation. | mentions 15:21
[TABATABAI:2:31/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Multiplicity in God's treasuries is multiplicity of ranks and degrees, not numerical plurality; the measure and limit appear only when things descend and are created. | mentions 15:21
[TABATABAI:2:31/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The displayed beings are exalted realities preserved with God behind the veils of the unseen; worldly names descend through their goodness and blessing, and all earthly and heavenly things derive their light and beauty from them.
[TABATABAI:2:31/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Although many, these beings do not multiply as separate individuals or differ as distinct persons; their plurality consists in ranks and degrees, and the names descend from them in that mode.
