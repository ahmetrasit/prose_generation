[WAHIDI-BASIT:v15p257#2/r4] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | Here the sperm-drop means the child of Adam, formed from the father’s semen; Arabic calls a small amount of water nuṭfa.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v15p257#2/r5] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | Nuṭfa derives from dripping: it can mean a clear remnant of water, a cloud’s dripping, or rain lasting through the night; semen is so named because it drips.

[WAHIDI-BASIT:v15p258/r1] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | ʿAlaqa is thick, congealed blood before it dries, and a piece of that blood; the cited al-Qaṭāmī line witnesses the word ʿalaq.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v15p258/r2] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | The drop that forms a child thickens into blood; when the clot becomes flesh it is a muḍgha, a piece of flesh, as the human heart is a bodily morsel.

[WAHIDI-BASIT:v15p258/r4] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | reports | A hadith cited here says each stage lasts forty days—drop, clot, then morsel—after which an angel is sent to breathe in the spirit.

[WAHIDI-BASIT:v15p259/r1] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · ابن الأعرابي | reports | Ibn al-Aʿrābī says mukhallaqa is a stage whose form has begun to appear, while ghayr mukhallaqa has not yet been shaped.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v15p259/r10] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | prefers | He takes mukhallaqa here as fully created through the breathing of the spirit: a fetus completed alive goes to term, while miscarriage is incomplete in this sense.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v15p259/r2] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | The author identifies Ibn al-Aʿrābī’s explanation of mukhallaqa as the word’s lexical sense.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v15p259/r3] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid, in Ibn Nujaḥ’s transmission, says the words describe miscarriage: a miscarried fetus is both formed and unformed.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v15p259/r4] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · السدي | reports | Al-Suddī details the miscarriage stages: a woman may miscarry a drop, a clot, unformed flesh, partly formed flesh, or a fully formed fetus.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v15p259/r5] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | He supports the miscarriage interpretation by reading ‘We establish in wombs what We will’ as what reaches its full term rather than being miscarried.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v15p259/r6] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says mukhallaqa is what lives and ghayr mukhallaqa is miscarriage.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v15p259/r7] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid, in Khusayf’s transmission, identifies mukhallaqa as the child and ghayr mukhallaqa as miscarriage.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v15p259/r8] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās, in ʿAṭāʾ’s transmission, says the formed fetus is one for whom the covenant was taken, whereas the unformed one had no covenant and is not created.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v15p259/r9] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · عبد الله بن مسعود | reports | Alqama reports from Ibn Masʿūd that an angel asks whether the drop is formed; if not, the womb expels it, but if so, the angel records sex, provision, lifespan, and destiny from the Preserved Book.
