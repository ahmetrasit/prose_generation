[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:28:76/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | One reading says the afterlife is the main aim while one may also lawfully enjoy the good things of this world.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:28:76/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | prefers | The contextually stronger reading is that worldly life is brief, so one must not live as if it were eternal but use it in pursuit of the afterlife.
[STUDYQURAN:28:77/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Korah is told to use wealth for good works rather than waste it or hoard it; this advice applies generally.
[STUDYQURAN:28:77/r2] STUDYQURAN · some commentators | reports | One reading says not to waste life by neglecting good works, charity, or preparation for the Hereafter.
[STUDYQURAN:28:77/r3] STUDYQURAN · others | reports | Others understand the phrase as warning against extreme asceticism and detachment, and as permission to enjoy what God has made lawful.
[STUDYQURAN:28:77/r4] STUDYQURAN · ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿUmar | reports | Al-Qurṭubī quotes Ibn ʿUmar: work for this world as if living forever and for the Hereafter as if dying tomorrow; the saying is sometimes attributed to the Prophet or ʿAlī.
[STUDYQURAN:28:77/r5] STUDYQURAN · some | reports | Some say one’s portion in this world means one’s coffin or grave, evoking remembrance of death.
[STUDYQURAN:28:77/r6] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The root ḥ-s-n in be good also carries the senses of beauty and virtue.
