[TABRISI:2:228#4/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | While the waiting period remains, husbands may restore their wives to the prior marital state; that right expires when the period ends.
[TABRISI:2:228#4/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | A husband alone may revoke a revocable divorce without his wife's consent, a new contract, or witnesses; the right applies only to revocable divorces although the opening is general.
[TABRISI:2:228#4/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Men used to divorce once, wait until the period nearly ended, revoke, and repeat the cycle before divorcing a third time, thereby harming the wife.
[TABRISI:2:228#4/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Revocation is permitted for reconciliation rather than harm, but this condition concerns permissibility, not legal effect; the community agrees the legal effects still apply when harm is intended.
[TABRISI:2:228#4/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | This concise principle gives women rights corresponding to their obligations, governed by accepted standards of decent treatment.
[TABRISI:2:228#4/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Women's rights include good companionship, freedom from harm, equitable division, maintenance, and clothing; the husband's include obedience, marital exclusivity, and preserving his paternity.
[TABRISI:2:228#4/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · النبي | reports | A report prompted by a woman identified as Muʿādh's wife says a husband must not strike his wife's face or insult her, and must feed and clothe her as he does himself and not abandon her.
[TABRISI:2:228#4/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · النبي | reports | The Prophet reminds men that they received women in God's trust; wives must preserve marital exclusivity, and husbands owe them customary maintenance and clothing, with any corrective strike non-injurious.
[TABRISI:2:228#5/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · مجاهد وقتادة | reports | Mujāhid and Qatāda say men's degree includes women's obedience, men's authority to dissolve the marriage, a larger inheritance share, and jihad.
[TABRISI:2:228#5/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās reads the degree as a man's obligation to show extra generosity and not demand every right in full.
[TABRISI:2:228#5/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjāj says women receive pleasure from men as men receive it from women, while men have a degree through spending and care.
[TABRISI:2:228#5/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · علي بن إبراهيم | reports | ʿAlī ibn Ibrāhīm's tafsir says men's rights over women are greater than women's rights over men.
[TABRISI:2:228#5/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · الباقر | reports | A report from al-Bāqir says a wife should obey her husband, seek permission for household charity, voluntary fasting, and leaving home, and not deny him sex; the husband's right is paramount, and the Prophet says he would have ordered prostration to him if it were permitted.
[TABRISI:2:228#6/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | God is powerful over whatever He wills, cannot be prevented or overcome, and acts according to wisdom.
[TABRISI:2:228#6/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | One view says the rule is abrogated for a woman divorced before consummation and for a pregnant divorcee by 33:49 and 65:4. | mentions 33:49, 65:4
[TABRISI:2:228#6/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Another view treats those two cases as specified exceptions to this verse rather than abrogated portions.
[TABRISI:2:228/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Qurūʾ has the few plural aqruʾ and the more commonly used aqraʾ or qurūʾ; the common plural form signals the many women governed by the rule although each waits three periods.
[TABRISI:2:228/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | One root sense of qurʾ is gathering, as with the gathering of letters in recitation or a womb gathering around a pregnancy.
[TABRISI:2:228/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · عمرو بن كلثوم | reports | ʿAmr ibn Kulthūm's line uses qaraʾ for a womb that has not gathered a fetus, illustrating the root sense of gathering.
[TABRISI:2:228/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | On the gathering sense, a woman is said to have aqraʾ when she menstruates as blood gathers in the womb; qurʾ can also mean purity as blood gathers in the body.
[TABRISI:2:228/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | A second root sense is a regularly recurring time, which can designate either menstruation or purity.
[TABRISI:2:228/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | A poet's line about the time when winds blow illustrates the sense of a regularly recurring time.
[TABRISI:2:228/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · الأعشى | reports | Al-Aʿshā's lines about his wives' qurūʾ use the term for periods of purity, supporting that interpretation.
[TABRISI:2:228/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Baʿūla is the plural of baʿl, husband; the husband is called this because he is above the woman through marital authority.
