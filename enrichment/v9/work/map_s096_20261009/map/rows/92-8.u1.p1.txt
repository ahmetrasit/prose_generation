9/p1 A palm owner's refusal and Abu al-Dahdah's gift occasioned the contrast. — reasons: Ata says the owner's dates fell into a neighbor's home; he rejected the Prophet's offer of a palm in Paradise. Abu al-Dahdah bought the tree with his garden and passed it to the neighbor. Ibn Abbas's version says the buyer exchanged forty palms and the poor household was deprived of fallen dates; the giver received a promised palm in Paradise.
  92:8/q09/p2 Abu Bakr's buying and freeing Bilal occasioned the contrast with Umayya and Ubayy. — reasons: Baghawi's anonymous report specifies a garment and ten coins; Ibn Masud's report says Abu Bakr did it for God and contrasts the brothers' striving.
  92:8/q09/p3 Two angels' daily prayer over spenders and hoarders occasioned the verses. — reasons: Reports vary between sunset and sunrise. Abu al-Darda's version says the two angels address all but humans and jinn and ask God to replace the spender's gift and destroy the hoarder's wealth. Al-Mawardi gives a chain via Qatada and Khulayd without grading.
92:8/q10 What does the Prophet's decree report say about acting? [* · theology]
  92:8/q10/p1 Destinations and deeds are decreed, yet people must act, for each is facilitated toward the work or path appointed. — reasons: Ali's funeral report says each place in Paradise or Hell is written. Other wordings ask whether work is new or settled, and the Prophet insists on action. Al-Tirmidhi's version is graded hasan sahih by Qurtubi. The report of two young men says they resolved to strive; reports via al-Sulami invoke 54:49. Ibn Hibban's Shu'ba notes Mansur corroborating Sulayman al-A'mash. Jabir's version names Suraqa; al-Nazzal's cites an Arab questioner. Burhan says the Prophet cited the verses among Paradise descriptions alongside 32:17 and 56:30.
92:8/q11 How does the verse connect to other passages and the sūra's theme? [* · links] (turns on: Which textual relation is being traced: the preceding sūra, parallel language, wealth and fate, the oath, or coordination.)
  92:8/q11/p1 It expands the prior sūra's success and failure into traits, warning, and fate. — reasons: Abu Hayyan connects 91:9–10 to this sūra's traits and its warning of Fire.
  92:8/q11/p2 Its claim of self-sufficiency echoes other Qur'anic uses and contrasts with piety. — reasons: Asad and Study Quran cite 96:6–7; Corpus Coranicum calls istaghna the antonym of ittaqa and compares 96:7 and 80:5, 80:9. Alusi says al-husna follows interpretations of 92:6.
  92:8/q11/p3 The fate of the miser and spender is clarified by other verses about wealth, ease, and divine ownership. — reasons: Qurtubi refers to 3:180 for wealth's consequence; Corpus Coranicum compares 94:5–6 and 87:8 with ease and hardship. Samarrai says the emphatic lam of God's ownership fits this wealth-centered sūra, unlike 53:25's context without wealth. Ibn Kathir recalls the maxim that a good deed breeds good and a sin breeds sin.
  92:8/q11/p4 The sūra's oath frames divergent lives and afterlife fates. — reasons: Bint al-Shati says night, daylight, and creation set up the denier's fire and the pious giver's satisfaction. Corpus Coranicum notes the divine plural authenticates the paired promises with the oath.
  92:8/q11/p5 The surrounding clauses coordinate as one despite an omitted conjunction. — reasons: Burhan cites the lack of waw in coordinated clauses here and compares 3:118 and 9:92.
92:8/q12 How does this verse turn from the giver to the miser? [* · links]
  92:8/q12/p1 The passage turns from the one who purifies himself and receives reward to the one who corrupts himself and faces a wretched fate. — reasons: Biqai links the transition to the preceding promise in 92:7.
92:8/q13 What does the majestic plural signal in the two paths? [* · rhetoric]
  92:8/q13/p1 It stresses how unlikely either facilitated course is. — reasons: Biqai says good is difficult because surrounded by aversions, whereas reason and primordial nature warn against evil.
