  1:1/q04/p1 Al-Farra derives ism from wasm, a mark, because a name distinguishes its referent. — reasons: MAWARDI (الفراء): Al-Farra derives ism from wasm, a mark, because a name distinguishes its referent.   1:1/q04/p2 Al-Khalil and al-Zajjaj derive ism from sumuww, elevation, because a name raises its referent above others. — reasons: MAWARDI (الخليل والزجاج): Al-Khalil and al-Zajjaj derive ism from sumuww, elevation, because a name raises its referent above others. MAWARDI-FULL (الخليل والزجاج): They derive ism from elevation, because a name raises the person named above others.
  1:1/q04/p3 Baghawi prefers sumuww: ism diminishes as sumayy and its verb is samiitu; a wasm derivation would yield wasīm and wasamtu. — reasons: BAGHAWI (author): Baghawi prefers sumuww: ism diminishes as sumayy and its verb is samiitu; a wasm derivation would yield wasīm and wasamtu. BAGHAWI-FULL (author): The derivation from sumuw is stronger: ism diminishes as sumayy, and its verb is sammaytu; a wasm derivation would yield wasīm and wasamtu.
  1:1/q04/p4 The Kufans derive ism from wasama, 'to mark,' making a name a sign identifying its referent. — reasons: RAZI-FULL (الكوفيون): The Kufans derive ism from wasama, 'to mark,' making a name a sign identifying its referent. QURTUBI (الكوفيون): The Kūfans derive ism from sima, a mark, because a name marks its referent.
