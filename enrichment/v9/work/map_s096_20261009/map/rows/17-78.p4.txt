[TABRISI:17:78-81#2/r10] TABRISI, d. 548 · الشيخ أبو جعفر | rejects | Shaykh Abū Jaʿfar rejects that inference: sunset concerns maghrib until twilight, while decline may summarize all five prayers rather than one prayer’s time.
[TABRISI:17:78-81#2/r11] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The author answers that decline to nightfall can be shared time for four prayers: ẓuhr and ʿaṣr share decline-to-sunset, and maghrib and ʿishāʾ share sunset-to-nightfall.
[TABRISI:17:78-81#2/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Qurʾān al-fajr is accusative by an understood command, aqim; nāfilatan laka is accusative as a circumstantial qualifier.

[TABRISI:17:78-81#2/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · النخعي | reports | Al-Nakhaʿī, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, and al-Suddī take dulūk as sunset, making the prayer maghrib; this is also reported from Ibn Masʿūd and Ibn ʿAbbās.
[TABRISI:17:78-81#2/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | prefers | The author prefers decline because it lets the passage encompass all five obligatory prayers rather than only one prayer time.
[TABRISI:17:78-81#2/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | On the preferred reading, decline-time prayers are ẓuhr and ʿaṣr, nightfall prayers are maghrib and later ʿishāʾ, and Qurʾān al-fajr is dawn prayer.
[TABRISI:17:78-81#2/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatāda call ghasaq the first onset of night; Mujāhid calls it sunset, al-Jubbāʾī its darkness, and al-Bāqir and al-Ṣādiq midnight.
[TABRISI:17:78-81#2/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · بعض أصحابنا | reports | Some companions infer that ẓuhr’s time extends to the end of the day because the verse makes the interval from decline to nightfall a prayer time.

[TABRISI:17:78-81/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | With fatḥa, barāḥ is a proper name for the sun on the pattern of Qaṭām and Ḥadhām; with kasra, it means the observer’s palm, as al-Farrāʾ explains.
[TABRISI:17:78-81/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Ghasaq denotes the onset or appearance of night’s darkness; the same root describes a bursting sore revealing what is inside.
