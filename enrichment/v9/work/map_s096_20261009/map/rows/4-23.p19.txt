[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | من الرضاعة is an accusative circumstantial phrase modifying the sisters, understood as sisters who are from nursing.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#2/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو حيوة | reports | Abu Haywa reads الرضاعة with kasra on the راء.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | من نسائكم may be a circumstantial phrase modifying ربائبكم, meaning stepdaughters from your women.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#2/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Alternatively, من نسائكم may describe the implicit pronoun in في حجوركم, because a relative clause contains a pronoun.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#2/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | ربائب is the plural of ربيبة, a spouse's daughter, and ربيب is the masculine; they are named for each spouse's rearing the other's child.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#2/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The phrase اللاتي في حجوركم does not imply a legal restriction; it reflects the usual circumstance of stepdaughters being in the household.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#2/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | حجور is the plural of حجر, with either vowel on حاء; it first means the front of a garment and then protection or shelter.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | اللاتي دخلتم بهن modifies the second نسائكم governed by من; consummation with the mother is required for the stepdaughter to be prohibited.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | It cannot modify both occurrences of نسائكم because they have different governing prepositions and a shared adjective cannot modify nouns with different governors.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#3/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | It also cannot modify both on legal grounds: mothers-in-law are prohibited by contract alone according to the majority, whereas stepdaughters require consummation with their mother.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#3/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari's explanation makes the stepdaughter prohibited only when her mother has been consummated with; without that, she remains lawful.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#3/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | If the phrase is linked to both mothers-in-law and stepdaughters, من would have to mean specification for one and source or origin for the other, an impermissible double sense in one expression.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#3/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | If linked only to mothers-in-law, the nearer stepdaughter phrase should receive it absent a compelling reason, and everyone agrees mothers-in-law are prohibited without qualification.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#3/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari allows من to mean connection and link the phrase to both women and stepdaughters, comparing the mutual relationship in 9:67 and the relationship between daughters and mothers. | mentions 9:67
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#4/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | An anonymous poem uses منك and مني to express connection between two people, offered as evidence for that sense of من.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#4/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A second anonymous poetic line likewise uses من to express reciprocal connection.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#4/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · علي وابن عباس وزيد وابن عمرو وابن الزبير | reports | A reported reading adds اللاتي دخلتم بهن after mothers-in-law, making their prohibition conditional on consummation; Ibn Abbas reportedly swore this alone was revealed.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#4/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas reportedly swore that the conditional wording was the only revelation.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#4/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | reports | The Shaykh says he knows no one who gives من the meaning of connection; the cited verse, poem, and report are all interpreted differently.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#5/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | حلائل is the plural of حليلة, meaning wife, named because she dwells with her husband wherever he is; it is an active feminine form.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#5/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Al-Farra also cites the spoken complaint إذا أقوم اشتكى صلبي for that dialectal form.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#5/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | An anonymous poem uses حليلها for the husband, illustrating the masculine counterpart.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#5/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj and others derive حليلة from حلال, with each spouse being permitted to the other, so فعيل is passive; the author objects to the feminine ending unless it is treated as a frozen noun like نطيحة or ذبيحة.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#5/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Another derivation uses حل as the opposite of tying: each spouse undoes the other's waist wrapper.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#5/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The phrase الذين من أصلابكم clarifies that the prohibited sons' wives are those of biological sons, since son can also refer to an adopted child.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#5/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | A son through nursing has the same rule as a biological son here, established by the Sunnah and thus not a counterexample to the verse.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#5/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | أصلاب is plural of صلب, the back, named for its firmness; the Hijazi pronunciation with ḍamma then sukūn is the most eloquent.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#5/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Al-Farra reports that Tamim and Asad pronounce the word صلب with fatḥa on both consonants.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#5/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | An anonymous poem witnesses the dialectal pronunciation in the phrase في صلب.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#6/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | وأن تجمعوا is in the nominative, coordinated with the nominative element of حرمت: combining two sisters in marriage is prohibited.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#6/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Combining two sisters in ownership is unanimously allowed, while intercourse with both by right of ownership is disputed.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#6/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The exception after ما قد سلف is disconnected and accusative, meaning God forgives what occurred in pre-Islamic times.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#6/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | An alternative takes the exception as connected: a pre-Islamic marriage of two sisters remains valid after Islam, but the man must choose one and leave the other.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23#6/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | Although a similar sense was possible for the earlier ما قد سلف in 4:22, the connected exception cannot work here because it yields a false meaning. | mentions 4:22
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The common plural of أم is أمهات, with an extra ه distinguishing rational mothers from non-rational ones; an anonymous line illustrates أمات for young animals.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqa asks why أخت restores the omitted consonant in its plural but بنت does not, explaining this by analogy of each feminine plural to its masculine.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Usage can reverse the common pattern, and one poet uses both forms for rational mothers in a single couplet.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The poet's line witnesses the forms الأمهات and أماتكا being used for rational mothers.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The form أمهة is also attested, with an added ه followed by feminine تاء; a poem uses it.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Given the singular أمهة, أمهات may be its plural; the added ه also appears in هبلع and هجرع.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | بنات is the feminine plural of ابن; the source previously discussed its derivation and pattern at 2:40. | mentions 2:40
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farra says بنات is the plural of بنة with an open باء, with kasra signaling an omitted radical; the author says this works only if that radical is ياء, while the correct root is واو.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Another grammarian gives the original as بنوة; the author reconciles this with the بنة account, since both require the missing root letter to be واو.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:23/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The تاء in بنت and أخت compensates for an omitted root consonant rather than marking femininity: the preceding consonant is still, and the تاء does not become هاء in pause.
