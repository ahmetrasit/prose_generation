## THE CURRENT MAP
75:18/q01 What does 'when We recite it' mean? [قَرَأْنَٰهُ · meaning] (turns on: Whether qaraʾa here denotes audible revelation, gathering, clarification, or an inward event; al-Ṭabarī tests gathering against the binding force of individual verses before compilation.)
  75:18/q01/p1 When the revelation is recited to Muhammad, through Gabriel. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ specifies Gabriel's recitation. Al-Ṭabarī reports Ibn ʿAbbās glossing it as revelation to Muhammad. Al-Zajjāj and al-Bursawī stress that the recitation must finish before Muhammad starts; al-Ālūsī calls God's attribution figurative. Al-Wāḥidī's analysis also allows 'our reciting it.'
  75:18/q01/p2 When God gathers and composes its parts. — reasons: Abū ʿUbayda cites ʿAmr ibn Kulthūm's use of qaraʾa for containing a fetus and the idiom about a woman never conceiving; Ibn Qutayba takes the verb as collection. Al-Ṭabarī grants that gathering is Arabic, but argues that making obligation depend on a compiled text would leave 96:1 and 74:1 unbinding before compilation.
  75:18/q01/p3 When God makes it clear or explains it. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās explains qaraʾnāhu as clarification and couples it with acting upon what is clarified; al-Suyūṭī gives the same gloss.
  75:18/q01/p4 When God brings it into being in the servant's annihilation. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī gives an inward reading in which recitation occurs during annihilation and is followed by subsistence.
75:18/q02 Who is the reciter denoted by 'We'? [قَرَأْنَٰهُ · referent] (turns on: The distinction between God as source and Gabriel as immediate reciter, and how divine attribution works.)
  75:18/q02/p1 Gabriel recites to Muhammad by God's command, so God attributes the recitation to Himself. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil identify Gabriel as the immediate reciter. Al-Zamakhsharī says the Prophet must follow him rather than prompt him; al-Rāzī compares attribution to God with 4:80. Al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Sāmarrāʾī likewise distinguish the angel's delivery from God's source or command. KURANYOLU cites 2:97, 16:102 and 26:193 for Gabriel's mediation. Al-Biqāʿī says the pronoun can refer to the Qurʾān or, alternatively, to Gabriel's reading; Abū Ḥayyān reports the angel as its referent. Al-Burhān compares other divinely commanded agents, including winds and angels moving clouds and rain reviving earth.
  75:18/q02/p2 God is the source of the recitation and enables its human understanding. — reasons: Asad grounds the divine attribution in God's revelation of the Qurʾān and His enabling people to understand it; al-Sāmarrāʾī says God directs while Gabriel transmits.
75:18/q03 Why does God say 'We recite'? [قَرَأْنَٰهُ · rhetoric]
  75:18/q03/p1 The majestic plural honors and heightens God's command to wait for His agent's recitation. — reasons: Al-Bursawī says the plural magnifies the command to wait; al-Ālūsī says its emphasis reinforces waiting rather than competing. Al-Rāzī sees Gabriel's honor in attributing his act to God; al-Burhān and al-Sāmarrāʾī explain plural attribution through agents under God's command, comparing the Messenger's obedience with God's at 4:80 and other mediated acts.
75:18/q04 What do the pronouns referring to 'it' designate? [قَرَأْنَٰهُ قُرْءَانَهُۥ · referent] (turns on: Who is addressed in 75:16–19 and whether those lines are an inserted instruction about revelation or continue the resurrection scene.)
  75:18/q04/p1 The Qurʾān being revealed to the Prophet. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the object with the Qurʾān in 'do not move with it'; Elmalılı says the implied antecedent is sufficient as at 97:1, since the addressee is the Prophet and Qurʾān is subsequently named. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports a narrower view that the pronoun means the portion already recited to him.
  75:18/q04/p2 The person's record of deeds at resurrection. — reasons: Al-Qaffāl and another account understand the passage as a frightened person's hurried reading of his record; God gathers and reads the deeds and later explains their requital. Al-Suyūṭī objects that a sound revelation report instead addresses Muhammad; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says an insertion needs no contextual signal and compares 20:114.
  75:18/q04/p3 What is within a person, namely conscience or inward knowledge. — reasons: Elmalılı notes that the antecedent is unstated and that a universal human addressee permits this reference; he ultimately prefers the Qurʾān because the address is specifically to Muhammad.
  75:18/q04/p4 The description of the coming Judgment. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the interpretation that one should not hurry to ask when Judgment will come: God gathers its people, reveals its description, and clarifies it by the trumpet blast; he rejects this reading.
75:18/q05 What does 'Qurʾān' or 'its qurʾān' mean here? [قُرْءَانَهُۥ · meaning] (turns on: The proposed root-sense of qaraʾa, and whether the command takes effect at each recitation or only after collection.)
  75:18/q05/p1 It is the verbal noun 'recitation' or 'reading,' also used for the recited text. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ compares qirāʾa/qurʾān with other paired verbal nouns: rujḥān/rujūḥ, maʿrifa/ʿirfān, ṭawāf/ṭawafān. Ibn Qutayba and al-Qurṭubī agree; al-Ṭabarī prefers Ibn ʿAbbās's derivation and cites ʿAmr ibn Kulthūm. He answers that an act-name can name its object, as kitāb names what is written. Elmalılı allows both noun and recited text; al-Sāmarrāʾī compares ghufrān and ʿudwān, saying the noun later became the revelation's proper name.
  75:18/q05/p2 It denotes collection or joining of parts, and by extension the assembled book. — reasons: Abū ʿUbayda derives the name from joining its sūras, adducing ʿAmr ibn Kulthūm's line about containing a fetus and the idiom mā qaraʾat salyan. Qatāda explains qurʾān as the verbal noun of gathering; Ibn Qutayba glosses following its collection. Al-Ṭabarī concedes this is Arabic but rejects making the obligation wait for compilation, citing 96:1 and 74:1.
  75:18/q05/p3 It denotes God's clarification through recitation. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās derives the word from qaraʾa in the sense of making clear and explains that one must follow the clarification once it is given; al-Ṭabarī prefers this reading over a collection sense.
75:18/q06 How is the Qurʾān's name related to Furqān and Kitāb? [قُرْءَانَهُۥ · meaning] (turns on: The distinct senses and usage patterns of the revelation's names.)
  75:18/q06/p1 Qurʾān names the gathered book; Furqān names its separation of truth from falsehood and believer from unbeliever. — reasons: Abū ʿUbayda repeats this distinction; he also compares Furqān's form with qanaʿān, a judgment satisfying both disputants.
  75:18/q06/p2 Qurʾān first names recitation and then revelation; Kitāb derives from writing and names what is already inscribed in the Preserved Tablet. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī says Qurʾān is a verbal noun before it becomes a proper name. He says a sūra introduced as Book tends to repeat kitāb more, one introduced as Qurʾān repeats qurʾān more, and one introduced by both uses them at roughly equal rates.
75:18/q07 What must the addressee do when the recitation occurs? [فَٱتَّبِعْ قُرْءَانَهُۥ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether following is immediate attentive reception, later repetition, lasting obedience and conveyance, collection, or a reading of a different addressee; accounts often combine listening, subsequent recitation and action.)
  75:18/q07/p1 Listen silently and attentively to the recitation, allowing it to finish. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās repeatedly glosses following as listening, sometimes lowering the head; al-Rāzī prefers listening over a legal command and al-Wāḥidī says this is why the Prophet was told to cease reading. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Sāmarrāʾī specify following with mind and thought. Al-Biqāʿī adds ear and mind; al-Suyūṭī says comprehension outranks hurried memorization. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī accepts the attention gloss though he reads the command as subsequent recitation.
  75:18/q07/p2 Recite it oneself after Gabriel has finished, following his reading. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās's reports, al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī specify reciting after Gabriel leaves; al-Bursawī says begin immediately without delay. Al-Bayḍāwī adds repetition until the wording is firmly established. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī takes the completed divine recitation as the point after which the Prophet reads. Ibn Kathīr describes reception, recitation and explanation as three stages.
  75:18/q07/p3 Act on what the Qurʾān says, obeying its commands and avoiding its prohibitions. — reasons: Qatāda repeatedly specifies the lawful and forbidden; al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Ibn ʿAbbās also give acting upon its contents. Al-Ṭabarī prefers this practical force because every revealed verse binds before compilation, giving 96:1 and 74:1 as examples; al-Naḥḥās praises Ibn Abī Ṭalḥa's report from Ibn ʿAbbās. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the command-and-prohibition reading remote.
  75:18/q07/p4 Follow the collected or composed Qurʾān as it is assembled. — reasons: Abū ʿUbayda understands the command as taking, following and acting on the joined portions; Ibn Qutayba glosses it as following its collection. Al-Ṭabarī argues that this cannot postpone obligation until compilation.
  75:18/q07/p5 Review or repeat the recitation until it becomes firmly fixed in memory. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī says repeated imitation establishes it in the mind; al-Biqāʿī adds fluency and practice until the passage becomes good character. Al-Ālūsī reports this as a further view, whereas al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it remote.
  75:18/q07/p6 Follow the message, act on it, and convey or call people to it. — reasons: Al-Jishumī reports conveying it to the Prophet's community as part of acting on it. Iṣlāḥī says the Prophet should read, act and call others to the portion received, while God sets its pace and arrangement; Asad stresses following its expressed message, word by word.
  75:18/q07/p7 Conform to a prior recita
