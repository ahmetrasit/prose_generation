[TABATABAI:107:1-7/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The surah may be Meccan or Medinan; another view says its first half is Meccan and second half Medinan.
[TABATABAI:107:5-7/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · عبد صالح عليه السلام | reports | Asked about those heedless of prayer, the righteous servant replies that it means neglect.
[TABATABAI:107:5-7/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Other narrations convey the same meanings about prayer neglect.
[ASAD-NOTES:107:1#n3/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | Asad specifies that the people are knowingly heedless of their prayers.
[ASAD-NOTES:107:intro/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | The surah was revealed in the mission's early years, probably after surah 102; its title comes from al-ma'un in the last verse.
[ASAD-NOTES:107:intro/r2] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · some commentators | rejects | Some commentators date verses 4–7 to Medina, but Asad rejects this view for lack of historical or textual evidence.
[BINTSHATI:v2p188/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | Sahw means forgetfulness and heedlessness; the Qur'an uses it only in al-Dhāriyāt and this verse. | mentions 51:11-14
[BINTSHATI:v2p188/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | The verse warns of heedlessness about prayer, not forgetfulness during prayer.
[BINTSHATI:v2p188/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | rejects | The author first rejects the interpretation as mistakes during prayer: such slips are neither sin nor wrongdoing, can happen to any believer, and are repaired by prostration of forgetfulness and extra prayers according to law.
[BINTSHATI:v2p188/r5] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الطبري | reports | Al-Tabari reports one interpretation as delaying prayer until its allotted time has passed.
[BINTSHATI:v2p188/r6] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الطبري | reports | Another interpretation is abandoning prayer without intending to make it up.
[BINTSHATI:v2p188/r7] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās identifies them as hypocrites who pray ostentatiously when seen and leave prayer when unobserved.
[BINTSHATI:v2p188/r8] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الطبري | prefers | Al-Tabari prefers that they are heedless and distracted about prayer, careless whether they pray at all.
[BINTSHATI:v2p189/r1] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · بعض المفسرين | reports | Some commentators say inattentiveness means missing prayer times; others mean abandoning prayer itself.
[BINTSHATI:v2p189/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari adds careless, distracted performance: rushed movements without humility, fidgeting or looking around, and not knowing what was recited.
[BINTSHATI:v2p189/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الرازي | reports | Al-Razi objects that calling them prayerful conflicts with interpreting heedlessness as abandonment, and that abandonment alone is not hypocrisy or unbelief.
[BINTSHATI:v2p189/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الرازي | reports | Al-Razi answers that they count as prayerful outwardly while forgetting prayer inwardly, as in the description of people who rise lazily and show off. | mentions 4:142
[BINTSHATI:v2p189/r5] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | The author treats the next clauses as attached to the inattentive worshippers: riya is showing people an outward state unlike one's inward one, near hypocrisy but also used more broadly.
[BINTSHATI:v2p191/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | The warned worshippers do perform prayer, but are heedless of standing before the Creator and of prayer's power to curb arrogance and cultivate humility and conscience.
[BINTSHATI:v2p191/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | A prayer that leaves its worshipper indifferent to an orphan's needs or a poor person's right to food cannot come from a humble, believing heart; it becomes a show for gain or protection.
[BINTSHATI:v2p192/r1] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | The offering teaches that God receives piety, not animal meat or blood; the verse makes inward God-consciousness decisive. | mentions 29:45
[BINTSHATI:v2p192/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | The verse on righteousness joins prayer and zakat with keeping promises and patience, showing that virtue includes lived faith and care. | mentions 2:177
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p202/r2] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · author | rejects | Bint al-Shati rejects interpreting sahūn as ordinary forgetfulness during prayer; the preposition ʿan points away from prayer rather than to a lapse within it.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p202/r3] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · author | holds | Incidental forgetfulness in prayer is not a sin or wrong; it can be repaired by prostration for forgetfulness or by sunnah and voluntary prayers, which is why it cannot be the condemned lapse.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p202/r4] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · الطبري | rejects | Al-Tabari says the condemned are heedless of prayer, distracted from it, and thereby sometimes neglect prayer itself or its time; Bint al-Shati says she is not persuaded by this reading.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p202/r5] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī adds that they may pray unlike the Prophet and early Muslims, pecking through the prayer without humility or avoiding disliked acts.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p203/r1] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · author | holds | The preposition عن makes the warning concern heedlessness of prayer's purpose: standing before God, restraining wrongdoing, cultivating humility, and prompting care for orphans and the poor. | mentions 107:6, 90:11, 90:12, 90:13, 90:14, 90:15, 90:16, 90:17
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p203/r2] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · author | rejects | This is not forgetfulness during prayer, abandoning prayer or its time, or fidgeting and yawning; some pray on time and feign humility for an audience or benefit, turning prayer into ostentation and formal hypocrisy. | mentions 107:6
[CORPUSCORANICUM:107:4-5:anmerkung/r1] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | holds | The root s-h-w here likely means the same as the later frequent term ghafla, religious heedlessness and negligence condemned also by Ephrem, according to Andrae; compare 51:11. | mentions 51:11
[CORPUSCORANICUM:107:4-5:anmerkung/r2] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | reports | For ṣallā and ṣalāh, the notes refer to the discussion under 108:2. | mentions 108:2
[CORPUSCORANICUM:107:4-7:kommentar/r1] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | holds | The second section opens with the emphatic wail that introduces a catalogue of vices.
[CORPUSCORANICUM:107:4-7:kommentar/r2] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | holds | Verses 4–6 target the criticized people's neglect of ritual prayer.
[CORPUSCORANICUM:107:4-7:kommentar/r4] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | holds | The two groups cross-correspond: verse 1 and verses 4–6 concern religious omissions, while verses 2–3 and verse 7 concern social omissions; the first group stresses social failings and the second religious ones.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p469/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The custodians appear to pray, but their prayers lack spirit and devotion and are pretentious; insincerity toward God also makes a person a poor neighbour to others.
