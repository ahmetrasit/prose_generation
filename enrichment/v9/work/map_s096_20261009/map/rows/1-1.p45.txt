[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#6/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The overt iqra bism rabbik is explained either by the priority of reading in the first-revealed sura or by attaching the phrase to a later iqra; the latter creates a long interruption and makes the second iqra redundant. | mentions 68:1
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#6/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farra' takes the omitted governing verb as an imperative: you read in God's name.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#6/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj instead takes it as a declarative, I read or begin in God's name.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#6/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The genitive Allah may be governed by the annexed noun, the implicit preposition, or the annexation relation; the author prefers the preposition.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#6/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Allah is a proper name exclusively for the true deity; ilah before contraction also has this exclusive sense, while bare ilah can name false gods too. | mentions 21:22, 23:117, 25:43
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#6/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari says Allah became a proper name through common usage prevailing over other uses.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#6/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The author prefers that Allah is primitive rather than derived and says it is the most definite of nouns; a dream story has Sibawayh praise God for making its name the most definite.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#6/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | One derivation links the name to lāha, to rise, and the sun-name ilāha; an elaborate explanation of lahanaka from lillahi innaka is condemned as contrived.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#7/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The author calls the derivation of lahanaka in the cited verse by deleting the preposition, article, alif, and shifting a vowel an ugly analysis.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#7/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Another derivation is from lāha yalūhu, to be hidden; on both analyses the original form is lāh and later article assimilation yields Allah.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#7/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A third derivation is alaha, with senses worship, find repose, be bewildered, and fear; the name describes creatures worshipping, seeking refuge, wondering, and fearing God. | mentions 7:127
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#7/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · رؤبة | reports | Ru'ba's word ta'alluh witnesses the worship sense; the verse phrase wa-yadharuka wa-ālihataka is also read as your worship. | mentions 7:127
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#7/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A report urging thought about God's blessings, not God Himself, and Ali's saying that reflection on divine attributes ends in bewilderment support the bewilderment sense.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#7/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | On the al-ilah derivation, hamza is dropped through frequent use, like anās becoming nās; an alternative account transfers its vowel before deletion.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#8/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الخليل | reports | Al-Khalil derives Allah from walah, because all creatures long for God; philosophers say all things love God, supported by the verse that everything glorifies Him. | mentions 17:44
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#8/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | This derivation changes initial waw to hamza, like ishaah and i'aah from wishah and wi'aah; the name then has the pattern fa'al meaning worshipped or wondered at.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#8/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The author rebuts al-Khalil: if hamza replaced waw, the original forms should remain pronounceable, and the word should pluralize as awliha; neither occurs.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#8/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الخليل | reports | Al-Khalil can answer that the replacement became obligatory because this name has unique grammatical properties and its plural preserves that replacement.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#8/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Whether the article in Allah is original depends on whether the name is derived; if derived it is original, otherwise added.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#8/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The alif was omitted in writing to distinguish Allah from the idol al-Lat; another explanation invokes the word al-lāh, while a third calls the spelling a rare form fixed by frequent use.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#8/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The lām of Allah is emphatic except after kasra; al-Zamakhshari's unqualified statement should be understood this way, while Abu al-Baqa's report of always-light pronunciation is rejected.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#8/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Readers disagree about the lām after an imāla-like open vowel; al-Susi has one reading with a light lām in hatta nara Allah jahratan.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#9/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · السهيلي وابن العربي | reports | Al-Suhayli and Ibn al-Arabi unusually say the article is original; the author objects that this would make the pattern fa'al regularly take tanwin, which Allah does not.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#9/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو زيد البلخي | reports | Abu Zayd al-Balkhi reports that Allah is a Syriac loan from laha; a poem preserves the supposed original form lahi.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#9/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | A strange proposal derives the name from the pronoun hu plus a possessive lām and article; the author says it resembles mysticism, not linguistic scholarship.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1#9/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | rejects | Al-Zamakhshari rejects treating Allah as an adjective: it is used as a noun, while adjectives require a noun they describe and cannot all remain without one.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The verb basmala means to say bismillah, like hawqala, haylala, and hamdala for saying their respective formulas.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ba can mean exchange or substitution, as in the prophetic saying about red camels and in a poem wishing for replacement companions.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ba also marks an oath and location, as in swearing by God and saying Zayd is in Mecca.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1/r12] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ba can make a verb transitive, as in God taking away their light. | mentions 2:17
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1/r13] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | A poet's ba in drinking from seawater means from it, showing the partitive use.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1/r14] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ba can mark price or equivalence in buying something for a thousand.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1/r15] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ba can mean away from, as in the sky splitting away from clouds; some restrict this sense to contexts of asking, as in ask an expert about it. | mentions 25:25, 25:59
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1/r16] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Alqama's line uses ba with asking women to mean asking about them, illustrating the restricted sense of departure.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | These clipped verbs resemble nisba coinages that combine names, as in hadrami, abqasi, and abshami from tribal names; a poem uses abshamiyya.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Because basmala and haylala are non-analogical, some classify them as later colloquial coinages.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الماوردي | reports | Al-Mawardi calls the person who says bismillah mubasmil and labels that usage a later coinage.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · عمر بن أبي ربيعة | reports | Ibn Abi Rabi'a's verse attests basmala as established poetic usage, while Tha'lab and al-Mutarriz transmit it without calling it a late coinage.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The preposition ba here expresses seeking aid: the implied sense is that I recite while relying on God.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ba can mark real or figurative attachment, illustrated by wiping the head and passing by Zayd.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ba can mean cause, as in God forbidding things because of the wrongdoing of the Jews. | mentions 4:160
[SAMIN-DURR:1:1/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ba can mark accompaniment, as in Zayd going out with his clothes.

[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r10] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbas calls the surah the foundation of the Qur'an and says its foundation is the basmala.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r104] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | The Companions opened God's Book with the basmala; scholars agree it is part of the verse in Surat al-Naml, while disputing its status at surah openings. | mentions 27:30
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r105] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A sound Abu Dawud report from Ibn ʿAbbas says the Prophet recognized a surah's division when the basmala was revealed; it is also reported mursal from Saʿid ibn Jubayr.
