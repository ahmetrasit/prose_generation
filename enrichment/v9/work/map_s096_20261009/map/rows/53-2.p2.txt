[STUDYQURAN:53:2/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | “Your companion” refers to the Prophet Muhammad.
[STUDYQURAN:53:2/r2] STUDYQURAN · some commentators | reports | Some take ḍalla, being astray, as the opposite of guidance (hudā).
[STUDYQURAN:53:2/r3] STUDYQURAN · R | reports | R identifies ghawā, to err, as the opposite of sound judgment (rushd); R is also cited among those explaining erring as ignorance from adherence to corrupt creed.
[STUDYQURAN:53:2/r4] STUDYQURAN · Iṣ, R, Ṭb | reports | Other commentators understand erring as ignorance caused by adherence to a corrupt creed.
[STUDYQURAN:53:2/r5] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | On this distinction, straying is lack of guidance, while erring is following religion incorrectly.
[STUDYQURAN:53:2/r7] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The Prophet's lack of caprice means he did not author revelation and his own speech is guided; together, the claims affirm true religion, correct practice, and freedom from whim.
[STUDYQURAN:53:intro/r10] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The opening responds to the disbelievers’ claim in 52:33 that the Prophet invented the revelation. | mentions 52:33
[STUDYQURAN:53:intro/r11] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The sūrah is famous for its rhetorical power and the dispute over the meaning of its opening section.
[STUDYQURAN:53:intro/r12] STUDYQURAN · many commentators | reports | Many commentators identify the passage as the Prophet’s two visions of Gabriel in his true form, unlike Gabriel’s usual human appearance.
[STUDYQURAN:53:intro/r13] STUDYQURAN · many others | reports | Many others read the passage as the Prophet seeing God, by heart alone or by both eye and heart, during the Night Journey and Ascension.
