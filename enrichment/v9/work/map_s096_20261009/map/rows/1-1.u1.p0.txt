1:1/q01 Why is بِسْمِ written with or without its alif? [بِسْمِ · grammar] (turns on: Whether usage and script convention, pronunciation, or inability to pause explains the omitted alif.)
  1:1/q01/p1 The alif of bism is omitted because the familiar phrase is frequent and shortened. — reasons: AKHFASH (author): The alif is omitted in writing bism because the phrase is frequent and the prefixed bāʾ already supplies the linkage; it remains in bismi al-Raḥmān and similar phrases. THALABI (أبو خالد بن يزيد المرادي): Abu Khalid ibn Yazid al-Muradi says the alif of ism was dropped for frequent use and its length was transferred to the bā; where ism retains alif, the bā keeps its ordinary form. MAWARDI (author): The connective alif is omitted in spelling and pronunciation because of frequent use and joining; by contrast it remains written in 96:1 because that wording is less frequent. | mentions 96:1 WAHIDI-BASIT (author): The written alif is dropped from bism Allāh because the familiar phrase is unmistakable and Arabic favors brevity when meaning is clear; unlike the less frequent formula in al-Wāqiʿa, al-Ḥāqqa. BAGHAWI (author): The alif in ism is omitted for lightness because the expression is used very frequently. BAGHAWI-FULL (author): The alif is omitted from ism for ease and frequent use; al-Qutaybi explains the lengthened ba as an honorific opening, and Umar b. Abd al-Aziz ordered careful lettering. KASHSHAF-FULL (author): The alif in bism is omitted in writing because connected use is frequent; the lengthened bā compensates for it, and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz told his scribe to lengthen the bā. | mentions 96:1 IBNATIYYA (author): The alif is omitted from the written basmala for brevity and ease because the phrase is so frequently used. IBNATIYYA (الكسائي وسعيد الأخفش، ويحيى بن زياد): Al-Kisa'i and Sa'id al-Akhfash omit the alif in bism al-Rahman and bism al-Qahir; Yahya b. Ziyad says omission applies only with Allah because that phrase is especially frequent. Ibn Atiyya says the alif remains with other names. TABRISI (author): The hamzat al-waṣl of ism drops in connected pronunciation and is omitted in writing here because the basmala is frequent and unambiguous, unlike the less frequent Qur'anic form in 96:1. | mentions 96:1 QURTUBI (author): The alif in ism is omitted in the basmala through frequent use; it remains in the less common “Read in the name of your Lord.” | mentions 96:1 BAYDAWI (أصحابنا البصريون): Basran grammarians derive ism from a form whose ending was dropped for frequent use; hamzat al-waṣl allows its consonant-initial form to begin a word. BAYDAWI (author): The written alif is omitted by orthographic convention due to frequency, and the bāʾ is lengthened in compensation. BAYDAWI-FULL (author): The written alif is omitted from بسم through frequent use, with the bāʾ lengthened in its place. BAYDAWI-FULL (أصحابنا البصريين): The Basrans derive اسم from a shortened form whose ending was dropped through frequent use; its beginning was made still and hamzat al-waṣl added to start it with a vowel. NASAFI (author): The alif of ism is omitted in this written basmala because it is frequent and unpronounced; the bā’ is lengthened to compensate, and ‘Umar ibn ‘Abd al-‘Aziz instructed his scribe to lengthen the bā’, distinguish the sīns, and round the mīm. | mentions 96:1 NASAFI-FULL (عمر بن عبد العزيز): Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz instructed his scribe to lengthen the ba, make the sin strokes clear, and round the mim; the written alif is omitted here through frequent use. ABUHAYYAN-FULL (author): The basmala's deleted verb is one rhetorical omission, and the alifs of bismillāh and raḥmān are also omitted in writing because of frequent use. BURHAN (علماء الظاهر): The outward scholars explain the omission as abbreviation from frequent use: the alif of ism is dropped only when appended to Allah and preceded by ba according to the majority. ALUSI (author): The written basmala omits the alif of ism; explanations include frequent usage, the bāʾ replacing the initial hamza, and whether the governing word is stated. | mentions 96:1 ELMALILI (author): In the basmala’s special orthography the alif of ism is omitted and the opening bāʾ is lengthened; frequent use explains this abbreviation, while the writing also hints at the force of connection.
