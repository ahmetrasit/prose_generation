[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p33/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | prefers | Ibn Ashur prefers that the verse refers to those who remained unbelievers; the emphatic statement then answers their apparent rejection and threatens them for opposing what they knew.

[TABATABAI:2:142-151#7/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Numerous reports show that the qibla command was revealed for obligatory prayer and applies specifically to it.
[TABATABAI:2:142/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | The verses form a coherent sequence announcing the Kaaba as the Muslims' qibla; reports alleging disorder or abrogation conflict with the apparent text and should not be accepted.

[TABATABAI:2:144-146/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | People of the Book know the ruling is true either because their books attest the Prophet's truth or because they identify his qibla; either way scripture contains it explicitly or implicitly, yet they conceal it.
[TABATABAI:2:144/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The Prophet looked toward heaven in expectation of revelation and hoped for a qibla that would honor him; this did not mean he disliked Jerusalem.
[TABATABAI:2:144/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Being pleased with a new qibla does not entail resentment toward the former one; the Prophet remains above such dissatisfaction.
[TABATABAI:2:144/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · روايات واردة في شأن نزول الآية | reports | Reports about the occasion say Jews taunted and boasted that Muslims followed their qibla; grieving, the Prophet went out at night and waited for revelation, after which the verse came.
[TABATABAI:2:144/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Keeping the former qibla would have answered the Jews' taunt because the Prophet's obedience was no disgrace; the new qibla instead silenced them and fixed the duty.
[TABATABAI:2:144/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Shatr means a part or side, and the side of the Sacred Mosque in this command is the Kaaba.
[TABATABAI:2:144/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The wording parallels the former command toward a side of the Farthest Mosque, replacing its known rock with the Kaaba; naming the Sacred Mosque adds nuances lost by saying Kaaba or Sacred House.

[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:144/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Abraham, forefather of the prophets representing the three great religions and pioneer of monotheism, built the Kaaba as a sanctuary, making it the most worthy qibla.

[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:144/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The next step after making the Kaaba qibla was to remove its idols and restore it, as at its founding, as the center and symbol of monotheism.
