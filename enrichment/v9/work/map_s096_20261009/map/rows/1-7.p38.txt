[ALUSI:1:7/r65] ALUSI, d. 1270 · جبريل | reports | Abū Maysara reports that Gabriel taught the Opening to the Prophet and instructed him to say Āmīn after wa-lā al-ḍāllīn; the report is cited from Ibn Abī Shayba and al-Bayhaqī without a grading.
[ALUSI:1:7/r66] ALUSI, d. 1270 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī reports the Prophet’s instruction that the congregation say Āmīn when the imam recites the final words, so that God will love them; Muslim, Abū Dāwūd, al-Nasāʾī, Ibn Mājah, and Ibn Abī Shayba transmit it.
[ALUSI:1:7/r67] ALUSI, d. 1270 · الحنفية | reports | The Ḥanafīs conceal Āmīn, following ʿAlī and Ibn Masʿūd; the Shāfiʿīs say it aloud. The report of the majority’s loud recitation is interpreted as instruction or discussion.
[ALUSI:1:7/r68] ALUSI, d. 1270 · الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan says the imam should not say Āmīn because he is the supplicant; this is also found in a less famous report from Abū Ḥanīfa, whereas the famous view is that he says it silently.
[ALUSI:1:7/r69] ALUSI, d. 1270 · عبد الله بن مغفل | reports | Reports from ʿAbd Allāh ibn Mughaffal and Anas, cited from al-Kashshāf, say the Prophet concealed Āmīn.
[ALUSI:1:7/r7] ALUSI, d. 1270 · قيل | rejects | A further interpretation makes the path adherence to obligations and Prophetic practices; al-Ālūsī dismisses it as a departure from the straight path.
[ALUSI:1:7/r70] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Āmīn is not part of the Qurʾān by consensus; a slight pause separates it from the sūra. Al-Ālūsī rejects Mujāhid’s contrary attribution as utterly false because it appears in no muṣḥaf.
[ALUSI:1:7/r71] ALUSI, d. 1270 · غير واحد | reports | Some authorities say that anyone who claims Āmīn is Qurʾān has committed unbelief.
[ALUSI:1:7/r72] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Āmīn is an Arabic particle functioning as a verb, built on fatḥa to avoid meeting two quiescent letters; its correct meaning is respond, while another view gives it a broader sense including a synonym.
[ALUSI:1:7/r73] ALUSI, d. 1270 · قيل | reports | Another view says Āmīn is used for a broader meaning that includes respond and its synonym.
[ALUSI:1:7/r74] ALUSI, d. 1270 · قيل | reports | A strange view derives the Arabicized word from the foreign hamīn because fāʿīl is not an Arabic pattern; a response says it may be a unique pattern with analogues, or originally short and lengthened.
[ALUSI:1:7/r75] ALUSI, d. 1270 · قيل | reports | Another unusual view treats Āmīn as a name of God because it contains a hidden pronoun referring to Him; al-Ālūsī finds this rationale even stranger.
[ALUSI:1:7/r76] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | reports | Readers disagree whether the alif in Āmīn is inherently long or short; groups support each origin.
[ALUSI:1:7/r77] ALUSI, d. 1270 · الواحدي | reports | Al-Wāḥidī calls a doubled mīm a dialect form; another view calls it an error. Since Āmīn is a non-Qurʾānic supplication with a sound meaning, some say that pronunciation does not invalidate prayer.
