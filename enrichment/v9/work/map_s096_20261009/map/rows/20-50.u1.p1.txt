20:50/q09 What does 'then' mark between giving and guiding? [ثُمَّ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the sequence is temporal, ranked, or both.)
  20:50/q09/p1 It marks real succession: formation precedes later guidance or powers. — reasons: Al-Rāzī argues bodies precede cognitive and motive faculties, citing Adam's form before spirit and human stages in 15:29 and 23:12–14; Bursevī similarly says creation precedes endowment with powers. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows both temporal and ranked sequence and compares the paths shown in 90:8–10.
  20:50/q09/p2 It marks rank or ontological order, without requiring elapsed time. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī makes the imparting of spirit majestic beside other differences. Al-Ālūsī says guidance follows creation in order of being even without time separating them. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says bodily beings' progression can also unfold in time; Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise allows both.
20:50/q10 Why say God 'gave' creation? [أَعْطَىٰ · rhetoric]
  20:50/q10/p1 The verb presents creation as a bestowed blessing. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says 'gave' reminds hearers that creation is a blessing and proof of lordship. Al-Burhān says the verb suits beings whose existence lasts only a moment.
20:50/q11 What does Moses' formulation establish about the Lord? [قَالَ رَبُّنَا ٱلَّذِىٓ أَعْطَىٰ هَدَىٰ · theology] (turns on: How acts of creation and guidance establish divine lordship and human response.)
  20:50/q11/p1 Creation and guidance prove one self-sufficient, powerful, knowing Lord to whom beings depend. — reasons: Al-Qushayrī, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Rāzī infer a creator and guide from the acts; al-Rāzī cites 87:1–3 and 26:77–78, biological design, bees' hexagonal cells, and the body's reversed elemental order. Death proves powers contingent; neither humans nor parents designed them. A bodily cause regresses, so an incorporeal necessary manager acts by choice, knows, and has unlimited power. Al-Biqāʿī rejects nature and stars as explanations for differentiated creatures. Al-Bayḍāwī stresses God's self-sufficiency; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says creating entails ownership and governance, including only dependent delegated possession (3:26). Ibn ʿĀshūr includes Pharaoh under 'everything'; he knows he did not create it. Al-Iṣlāḥī cites birdsong and plants, and 87:1–3; human capacities and sciences remain God's gifts.
  20:50/q11/p2 The created world directs the self to God through proof and obliges following guidance. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī calls this guidance of the self by proof; al-Jishumī rejects imitation, necessity, inspiration and anthropomorphist accounts of knowing God, and says innovators lack salvation. Al-Ālūsī says Moses' method rebuts blind imitation and learning God solely from a messenger's assertion; one must hear objections to answer them, and even Pharaoh argued rather than abusing the claimant. Al-Iṣlāḥī cites 75:14–15 for inner testimony and warns that intellect and scientific achievement should not breed defiance.
20:50/q12 Why answer Pharaoh with these acts and words? [قَالَ رَبُّنَا ٱلَّذِىٓ أَعْطَىٰ هَدَىٰ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the distinctive wording serves proof, the immediate call to guidance, or a timeless view of creation.)
  20:50/q12/p1 The answer is concise and comprehensive and supplies proof that Pharaoh cannot claim. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī and those citing him call it clear to an unbiased seeker; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says Pharaoh cannot share the attributes. Al-Bayḍāwī says Pharaoh was confounded and changed subject; al-Iṣlāḥī calls the answer gentle yet cornering. Al-Ālūsī contrasts the mere title 'Lord of the worlds' with a description of origination, dependence and differing ranks; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise says it supplies proof. Bursevī says the act points to its agent.
  20:50/q12/p2 Guidance is foregrounded because Pharaoh's exchange concerns a call to guidance. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says universal guidance particularly suits the context.
  20:50/q12/p3 The past tense expresses ongoing, timeless creation. — reasons: Asad reads the grammatical past as an unceasing present.
20:50/q13 Did Pharaoh already know God? [ٱلَّذِىٓ · theology] (turns on: Whether the relative description presupposes Pharaoh's recognition of a creator.)
  20:50/q13/p1 He knew of God but rebelled or questioned His essence. — reasons: Al-Rāzī and some in al-Ālūsī say a relative description assumes recognition. They cite Pharaoh's acknowledgment in 17:102, opponents' inner certainty in 27:14, 28:39 as denial of resurrection, his 'who' question and 'madman' charge in 26:23,27, and his rational awareness of his origin and limited realm. Al-Ālūsī says the question may concern unknowable essence after a proof of existence, but calls the arguments disputable; the Study Quran says God's essence is inaccessible, citing 26:23–24 and 3:13,15.
  20:50/q13/p2 He was ignorant of God in some manner. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports possibilities of denial of a maker, necessary-cause philosophy, star or idol worship, indwelling, or anthropomorphism. He says Pharaoh's claim to lordship over subjects demands their obedience, not necessarily that he created everything.
20:50/q14 How does this verse connect with other guidance passages and what follows? [* · links] (turns on: Which other passages illuminate this general proof and its place in the exchange.)
  20:50/q14/p1 It parallels other creation-and-guidance verses and leads from general proof to particular signs. — reasons: Al-Rāzī cites 87:1–3 and 26:77–78, then says the next verse begins with the earth as a specific proof after the general one. Al-Damghānī and Ibn al-Jawzī connect inspired livelihood and mating to 87:2–3; Asad traces the idea to this early passage. Ibn Kathīr's reported deterministic gloss also cites 87:3. Al-Iṣlāḥī invokes 87:1–3 for Moses' answer.
  20:50/q14/p2 The exchange points to divine signs and the question of God's inaccessible essence. — reasons: The Study Quran links Pharaoh's later question to 26:23–24 and cites 3:13,15 for the limits of human description.
20:50/q15 What inward reading follows from creation and guidance? [* · inward] (turns on: Whether the inward point concerns universal living glorification or differing preparedness for destined ends.)
  20:50/q15/p1 Every being has life and its own capacity to know and glorify God. — reasons: Bursevī reports spiritual masters who distinguish formal life in humans, jinn and angels from meaningful life in inanimate things and plants; he cites 17:44 and ties knowledge to each thing's intellect, spirit and life.
  20:50/q15/p2 Each being is prepared and eased toward its allotted purpose. — reasons: The Najmiyya reading says believers are receptive to faith and follow prophets, while disbelievers are disposed to rejection. Bursevī cites the Prophetic command to act because each is eased toward what they were created for; al-Maghribī's poem assigns obedience and disobedience, and Mālik and Riḍwān, to different people.
