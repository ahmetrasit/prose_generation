[FARRA:v1p4/r1] FARRA, d. 207 · author | holds | The two pronunciations of the pronoun in ʿalayhim are both Arabic dialects.
[FARRA:v1p4/r2] FARRA, d. 207 · author | holds | With ḍamma on the hāʾ, its original vowel is retained across nominative, accusative, and genitive forms.
[FARRA:v1p4/r3] FARRA, d. 207 · author | holds | With kasra on the hāʾ, speakers ease the ḍamma after a silent yāʾ; the same two options occur after a preceding kasra, as in bihim.
[FARRA:v1p4/r4] FARRA, d. 207 · author | holds | After a yāʾ that has become a pronounced alif because its preceding vowel is fatḥa, the pronoun takes only ḍamma.
[FARRA:v1p7/r1] FARRA, d. 207 · author | holds | Genitive ghayri is an adjective of alladhīna, not of the pronoun in ʿalayhim; its definite annexation permits this description because neither expression denotes a specified person.
[FARRA:v1p7/r2] FARRA, d. 207 · author | holds | Ghayri can be accusative as a detached description of ʿalayhim, or genitive after repeating ṣirāṭ as “the path of those other than …”.
[FARRA:v1p7/r3] FARRA, d. 207 · author | holds | In wa-lā al-ḍāllīn, ghayr means lā, so lā is repeated; if ghayr meant “other than,” repeating lā would be ungrammatical.
[FARRA:v1p7/r4] FARRA, d. 207 · author | rejects | Farra rejects the claim that ghayr in al-ḥamd means “other than” and lā is extra; extra lā requires a preceding negation.
[FARRA:v1p7/r5] FARRA, d. 207 · author | rejects | The verse “in a well without ḥawr” does not prove an extra lā: here lā is a true negation, while poetry allows an extra lā after an earlier negation.
[FARRA:v1p7/r6] FARRA, d. 207 · author | holds | In the cited phrase “a well that returns nothing,” ḥāra means that the action leaves no perceptible trace, like grinding that produces no result.
