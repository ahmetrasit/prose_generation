7:206/q15 What incident is reported behind the verse? [* · reports]
  7:206/q15/p1 Meccan unbelievers refused or questioned prostrating at the Prophet's command; the verse answered them with the angels' example. — reasons: Muqātil, al-Māwardī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and al-Kāshifī cite the objection in 25:60; al-Kāshifī says aversion increased.
7:206/q16 How does the verse connect to the preceding commands and the sūra's story? [* · links] (turns on: Whether the link is chiefly to nearby remembrance commands or to the sūra's opening narrative.)
  7:206/q16/p1 It explains and motivates the preceding commands to listen, remember, and supplicate by the angels' example. — reasons: Al-Jishumī cites 7:204; Ibn ʿĀshūr says emphatic in marks a rationale rather than doubt and links 7:203–204 to believers' prostration; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says remembrance brings one among the near; Iṣlāḥī reads the ending as help to persevere against objections.
  7:206/q16/p2 The ending recalls the opening Adam story: angels prostrate humbly whereas Iblīs refused proudly. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī also relates it to the opening command to follow the Qurʾan; al-Sāmarrāʾī traces pride and prostration across the sūra.
7:206/q17 What other Qurʾanic descriptions illuminate the angels here? [* · links] (turns on: Which parallel passage or report supplies the connection.)
  7:206/q17/p1 Other verses describe their continuous glorification, standing, and prostration. — reasons: Al-Rāzī cites 37:165–166 and 39:75; Study Quran cites 13:13, 39:75, 42:5 and prostration at 16:49; the sky-groaning report has angels standing, bowing, and prostrating in every handspan.
  7:206/q17/p2 It echoes 4:172, where Jesus and angels do not disdain God's service. — reasons: Study Quran identifies the parallel.
7:206/q18 What level of remembrance does this verse call for? [* · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the emphasis is degree of continuity or inward as well as outward form.)
  7:206/q18/p1 Regular but intermittent remembrance below the angels' uninterrupted glorification. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares 21:19–20 and 41:38, where the near glorify without weariness.
  7:206/q18/p2 Remembrance includes inward awareness as well as speech and bodily action. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites creation's glorification and prostration at 17:44, 55:6, 13:15.
7:206/q19 Why is this a place of recitation prostration? [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law]
  7:206/q19/p1 Reciters prostrate here to imitate the angels and oppose unbelievers' refusal; it is the first such place in muṣḥaf order. — reasons: Al-Māwardī, Ibn Kathīr, and Ibn ʿĀshūr call it first. Al-Ṭabrisī reports consensus; Ibn Kathīr extends consensus to listeners. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Ibn ʿAbbās that sajdah sites frustrate polytheists or emulate prophets, including David at 38:24 under 6:90. Bursevī adds remediation of deficiency as another general purpose.
7:206/q20 May one bow instead of prostrating at this marker? [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law]
  7:206/q20/p1 Yes, either bowing or prostration is allowed. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports al-Nakhaʿī via al-Naqqāsh.
7:206/q21 How many recitation prostrations are counted in the Qurʾān? [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · sciences] (turns on: Which sites are included, especially the second al-Ḥajj prostration, Ṣād, al-Ḥijr, and the short sūras; al-Qurṭubī cites varying reports and disagreement over commands.)
  7:206/q21/p1 Fifteen, including two in al-Ḥajj and the short sūras. — reasons: Ibn Ḥabīb, a report from Ibn Wahb, and Isḥāq are cited; Study Quran says fifteen. The report to ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ gives fifteen but ʿAbd al-Ḥaqq doubts Ibn Munayn; ʿUqbah's report for two in al-Ḥajj contains very weak Ibn Lahīʿah.
  7:206/q21/p2 Fourteen, counting Ṣād but omitting the second al-Ḥajj prostration. — reasons: Ibn Wahb and the Ḥanafīs; al-Qurṭubī prefers omitting second al-Ḥajj because its report is unsound. Abū Ḥayyān compares Abū Ḥanīfa's locations with al-Shāfiʿī's.
  7:206/q21/p3 Fourteen, counting the second al-Ḥajj prostration but omitting Ṣād. — reasons: Al-Shāfiʿī's count; Abū Ḥayyān says the two fourteen-count positions differ in those locations.
  7:206/q21/p4 Sixteen, adding al-Ḥijr 15:98. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān report the addition.
  7:206/q21/p5 Eleven, omitting second al-Ḥajj and three in al-Mufaṣṣal. — reasons: The famous Mālikī count, also attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn ʿUmar; Abū al-Dardāʾ reports eleven with the Prophet and lists the sites.
  7:206/q21/p6 Ten, omitting second al-Ḥajj, Ṣād, and three in al-Mufaṣṣal. — reasons: Attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās.
  7:206/q21/p7 Four: al-Sajdah, Fussilat, al-Najm, and al-ʿAlaq. — reasons: Reported by al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān.
  7:206/q21/p8 Twelve. — reasons: Al-Kāshifī as reported by Bursevī; he notes al-Shāfiʿī and Aḥmad accept the last al-Ḥajj sajdah while Abū Ḥanīfa excludes it, and schools differ on Ṣād because its wording mentions bowing though the Prophet prostrated.
7:206/q22 At which words in Fussilat is its recitation prostration? [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law] (turns on: Whether precaution favors the later phrase or the report locating it at the earlier phrase.)
  7:206/q22/p1 At 41:38, lā yasʾamūn. — reasons: Bursevī follows ʿUmar and Ibn Masʿūd as precaution: delaying is allowed while doing it too early is not.
  7:206/q22/p2 At 41:37, in kuntum iyyāhu taʿbudūn. — reasons: Bursevī reports this from ʿAlī and says al-Shāfiʿī follows it.
7:206/q23 Is recitation prostration obligatory? [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law] (turns on: Whether the command, Satan report, and prophetic practice imply obligation or recommendation; al-Qurṭubī relies on ʿUmar's public statement.)
  7:206/q23/p1 No, it is a confirmed sunnah or recommended. — reasons: Al-Ṭabrisī names al-Shāfiʿī and his companions; al-Qurṭubī cites Mālik, al-Shāfiʿī, and ʿUmar's public statement before Companions without objection, which he treats as consensus. He says the Satan report concerns an obligatory prostration elsewhere and prophetic practice indicates recommendation. The majority and Study Quran agree.
  7:206/q23/p2 Yes, it is immediately obligatory on reciter and listener and made up if missed. — reasons: Abū Ḥanīfa cites the unqualified command, Satan's grief at Adam's child's obedience and reward, and the Prophet's practice; Bursevī states its immediate and make-up consequences.
7:206/q24 Who is required or invited to prostrate when the verse is heard? [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law]
  7:206/q24/p1 The reciter and attentive listener prostrate; an incidental hearer is not obligated. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr says there is consensus for reciter and listener; Abū Ḥanīfa requires it for one who intended to listen; ʿUthmān limits it to attentive listening. ʿImrān's answer implies no duty for an unprepared hearer; Salmān and al-Sāʾib declined in relevant settings.
7:206/q25 What conditions apply to recitation prostration? [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law] (turns on: Whether recitation prostration takes the purity conditions of prayer.)
  7:206/q25/p1 Prayer-like conditions apply: purity, intention, qibla, and permissible time. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī lists the conditions; Abū Ḥayyān adds freedom from impurity and ritual defilement. Al-Zuhrī requires purity and qibla at home but allows a mounted traveler any direction.
  7:206/q25/p2 Purity is not required. — reasons: Al-Bukhārī reports Ibn ʿUmar prostrating without purity; Ibn al-Mundhir and Ibn al-Munkadir report al-Shaʿbī likewise.
7:206/q26 What takbīr and hand raising accompany the prostration? [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law] (turns on: Whether rules inside prayer carry over outside it, especially hand raising and takbīr.)
  7:206/q26/p1 Say takbīr and raise the hands for it. — reasons: Al-Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad, and Isḥāq are cited by al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān; Ibn ʿUmar reports the Prophet saying takbīr on descent and ascent.
  7:206/q26/p2 Say takbīr on descent and ascent within prayer; hand raising applies within prayer, with differing reports outside. — reasons: Mālik's well-known position; al-Qurṭubī adds most jurists require takbīr outside prayer, and Abū Ḥayyān notes the varying reports.
  7:206/q26/p3 For a seated worshipper, stand with takbīr, prostrate with takbīr, then sit. — reasons: Bursevī says this is fuller and unnecessary delay is disliked.
7:206/q27 Does recitation prostration end with salām? [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law] (turns on: Whether analogy to prayer requires an exit salām; the two compilers give opposite majority attributions.)
  7:206/q27/p1 No salām is required. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī reports this as majority practice; Abū Ḥayyān instead attributes no salām to a group of early authorities and Isḥāq.
  7:206/q27/p2 Salām is required. — reasons: Ibn al-ʿArabī favors it because an act entered with takbīr needs an exit as prayer does, more so than funeral prayer. Al-Qurṭubī attributes this to early authorities and Isḥāq, while Abū Ḥayyān calls it the majority view.
7:206/q28 At what times may recitation prostration be performed? [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law] (turns on: How a cause-specific sajdah interacts with bans on prayer after dawn and ʿAṣr, and why those bans apply.)
  7:206/q28/p1 At any time, as prayer with a specific cause. — reasons: Al-Shāfiʿī and others are reported by al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān.
  7:206/q28/p2 Until dawn brightens or until the sun yellows after ʿAṣr. — reasons: One Mālikī view reported by al-Qurṭubī.
  7:206/q28/p3 Not after dawn and not after ʿAṣr. — reasons: One Mālikī view reported by al-Qurṭubī; Abū Ḥayyān also reports bans after dawn and afternoon prayers.
