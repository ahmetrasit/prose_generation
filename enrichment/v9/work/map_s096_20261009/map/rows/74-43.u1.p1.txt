  74:43/q11/p1 Begin good deeds promptly; sustained practice supports belief, while persistent abandonment can lead to error. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī urges racing toward good deeds even before intention is fully sincere. He says prompt practice gives conviction form and that persistently abandoning deeds can make one shed sound belief and fall into error.
74:43/q12 How do the admissions connect with the sūra's themes and surrounding verses? [* · links]
  74:43/q12/p1 The confession contrasts the damned with praying, generous believers and gathers the sūra's concerns with deeds, belief, and Judgment. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says the damned lack two virtues and retain two vices, failures of action and belief. Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts them with believers marked by prayer, almsgiving, and belief in the Day of Recompense. Ibn Kathīr reads prayer and feeding the poor as worship of God and goodness toward humans. Iṣlāḥī says nature's signs argue for the Hereafter, the Qurʾān warns people to prepare, and evaders remain stubborn.
74:43/q13 How does their confession bear on individual accountability and intercession? [* · interpretation]
  74:43/q13/p1 Each soul is pledged to its own deeds; ancestry, creed, deities, and intercession cannot save someone whose deeds lead to Hell. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī says only a soul's own deeds free it or lead it to Hell, and neither others' deeds nor intercession benefit it. He says the sinners' confession confirms this: ancestry, creed, deities, and their intercession avail nothing before God.
