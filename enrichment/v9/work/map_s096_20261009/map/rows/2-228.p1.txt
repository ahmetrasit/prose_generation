[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيالحروفالمتقاربةالتيهيمنمخرجواحد#808/r22] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Nūn assimilates into yāʾ with or without nasalization: yāʾ resembles wāw and is close to rāʾ, which itself is close to nūn.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيالحروفالمتقاربةالتيهيمنمخرجواحد#808/r23] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Before other oral consonants, nūn may be a hidden sound articulated through the nose; this is lighter and remains identifiable because no other sound comes from that place.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيالحروفالمتقاربةالتيهيمنمخرجواحد#808/r24] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | When nūn is assimilated with nasalization into rāʾ, lām, yāʾ, or wāw, its articulation stays oral and only takes on nasal resonance.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيالحروفالمتقاربةالتيهيمنمخرجواحد#808/r25] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Nūn remains clearly articulated before hamza and the five throat consonants because they are distant from it and do not belong to its articulation class.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيالحروفالمتقاربةالتيهيمنمخرجواحد#808/r26] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A hidden nūn does not assimilate into following throat consonants, nor do they become nūn, because they are distant and unlike it.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيالحروفالمتقاربةالتيهيمنمخرجواحد#808/r27] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | When nūn and mīm occur within one word, they are clearly distinguished, as are nūn before wāw and yāʾ, to avoid confusion with a geminate form.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيالحروفالمتقاربةالتيهيمنمخرجواحد#808/r28] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | Al-Khalīl analyzes the derived form from wajila as awjal, by analogy with amḥā, because the added nūn occurs in a pattern without a doubled wāw.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيالحروفالمتقاربةالتيهيمنمخرجواحد#808/r29] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Nūn before rāʾ or lām is not normally left clear because the nearby articulation makes clarity heavy; assimilation risks confusion with genuine gemination.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيالحروفالمتقاربةالتيهيمنمخرجواحد#808/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Alif does not assimilate even with a neighboring alif: doing so would change both long vowels into other sounds, so it also resists nearby consonants.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيالحروفالمتقاربةالتيهيمنمخرجواحد#808/r30] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Definite-article lām must assimilate before thirteen consonants, including ṭāʾ; this rule accounts for the assimilated lām in al-muṭallaqāt.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيالحروفالمتقاربةالتيهيمنمخرجواحد#808/r31] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The article lām's obligatory assimilation set comprises eleven tongue-tip consonants plus ḍād and shīn, whose articulation extends toward neighboring places.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيالحروفالمتقاربةالتيهيمنمخرجواحد#808/r32] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | With lām outside the definite article, assimilation into rāʾ is preferable because rāʾ is closest and most similar; clear articulation remains valid in Hijazi speech.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيالحروفالمتقاربةالتيهيمنمخرجواحد#808/r33] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Assimilation of non-article lām into ṭāʾ, dāl, tāʾ, ṣād, zāy, or sīn is allowed but less natural than before rāʾ because these sounds are farther away.

[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيالحروفالمتقاربةالتيهيمنمخرجواحد#808/r35] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | reports | Ṭarīf's verse attests to non-article lām assimilating into shīn, producing hīshiʾ for hal shayʾ.

[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيالحروفالمتقاربةالتيهيمنمخرجواحد#808/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Long yāʾ or wāw after fatḥa resist assimilation into adjacent consonants, because this would turn a sound with length and softness into one without them.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيالحروفالمتقاربةالتيهيمنمخرجواحد#808/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A following consonant cannot assimilate into a long yāʾ, since that would introduce softness into a sound that lacks it.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيالحروفالمتقاربةالتيهيمنمخرجواحد#808/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Mīm does not assimilate into bāʾ: when nūn changes before bāʾ, speakers choose mīm, so mīm is already the sound they turn to.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيالحروفالمتقاربةالتيهيمنمخرجواحد#808/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Fāʾ does not assimilate into bāʾ because of their articulation; bāʾ can assimilate into fāʾ through proximity and oral-consonant assimilation.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيالحروفالمتقاربةالتيهيمنمخرجواحد#808/r8] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Rāʾ resists assimilation into lām or nūn because its trill and spread would be impaired; lām and nūn may assimilate into rāʾ without that loss.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيالحروفالمتقاربةالتيهيمنمخرجواحد#808/r9] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Shīn does not assimilate into jīm because its extended, diffuse articulation makes the change objectionable; jīm may assimilate into shīn.
