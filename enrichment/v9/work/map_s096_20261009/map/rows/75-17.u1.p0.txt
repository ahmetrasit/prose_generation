## THE CURRENT MAP
75:17/q01 Why does the promise begin with inna? [إِنَّ · rhetoric]
  75:17/q01/p1 It links the command against haste to God's guarantee and emphasizes that extraordinary guarantee. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the particle supplies a causal connection that would be lost without it, comparing 9:103; perfect retention and reproduction after hearing alone also call for emphasis.
75:17/q02 What does 'upon Us' say about God's undertaking? [عَلَيْنَا · theology] (turns on: Whether divine obligation rests on the promise or on the wisdom required for prophethood.)
  75:17/q02/p1 God undertakes and guarantees the gathering and recitation of revelation. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr calls ʿalaynā a guarantee in both clauses; al-Rāzī and Jishumī observe that ʿalā conveys obligation. Jishumī links the Prophet's commission to his duty to explain enough to remove a responsible person's excuse.
  75:17/q02/p2 The obligation to preserve revelation follows from God's promise. — reasons: Al-Rāzī attributes this basis of obligation to his school.
  75:17/q02/p3 Divine wisdom requires preservation for prophethood to fulfill its purpose. — reasons: Al-Rāzī reports the Muʿtazilites' argument that revelation cannot serve its prophetic purpose if forgotten.
75:17/q03 Why is 'upon Us' placed before 'its gathering'? [عَلَيْنَا جَمْعَهُۥ · rhetoric]
  75:17/q03/p1 Fronting 'upon Us' restricts the guarantee to God alone. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī says God alone can gather, preserve, and enable accurate complete recitation; later placement would remove exclusivity and change the sense. He illustrates ordinary fronting of prepositional phrases with 'Khalid explains this to you'; the present order also suits the verse ending.
75:17/q04 What does the pronoun 'it' refer to? [جَمْعَهُۥ قُرْءَانَهُۥ · referent] (turns on: Whether 75:16–19 interrupts the resurrection account to address the Prophet receiving revelation, or continues that account; Elmalılı also considers a general human address.)
  75:17/q04/p1 The Qur'an or revelation being sent to the Prophet. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī and Elmalılı allow an implicit antecedent from context, comparing 97:1; Elmalılı notes the passage later names the Qur'an and the address is specifically to the Prophet. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God gathers its parts and recites without omission; Abū Ḥayyān identifies the Qur'an as the referent of 'in your chest.'
  75:17/q04/p2 The person's record of deeds at resurrection. — reasons: Al-Qaffāl relates the pronoun to the record shown after 75:13–15 and compares 17:14; al-Balkhī and al-Bayḍāwī describe the distressed reader of that record.
  75:17/q04/p3 The person's inward knowledge or conscience. — reasons: Elmalılı says an address to humanity generally could supply this otherwise unexpressed antecedent.
  75:17/q04/p4 The coming Judgment and those destined for it. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the view that God gathers those destined for Judgment and reveals its description.
75:17/q05 What is meant by God's gathering it? [جَمْعَهُۥ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether gathering concerns revelation retained in the Prophet, its assembled portions, or eschatological evidence and people.)
  75:17/q05/p1 God fixes the revelation in the Prophet's breast or heart for secure memory. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ḍaḥḥāk describe placing it in his chest or establishing it in his heart. Al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī add that no meaning escapes him; Abū Ḥayyān reports Gabriel's repetition until it settles there. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the heart-and-tongue explanation remote.
  75:17/q05/p2 God preserves and assembles the revelation, arranging its parts and recitation. — reasons: Qatāda repeatedly glosses the phrase as preservation and composition. Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī specify ordering as revealed; Iṣlāḥī includes both the Prophet's memory and assembly of separate portions. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes collection of its parts without omission.
  75:17/q05/p3 God gathers the person's deeds and the evidence of them for judgment. — reasons: Al-Qaffāl, al-Balkhī, and al-Bayḍāwī connect it with the distressed reader of the record, who must submit or confess; al-Qaffāl calls the warning strong but acknowledges no supporting reports.
  75:17/q05/p4 God gathers those destined for Judgment. — reasons: The reported Judgment-day reading then makes recitation its revealed description and explanation the trumpet blast.
75:17/q06 Why pair 'its gathering' with 'its qurʾān'? [جَمْعَهُۥ قُرْءَانَهُۥ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether qurʾān adds the act of reciting, specifies a kind of gathering, or itself means gathering.)
  75:17/q06/p1 The second term specifies or adds to the general act of gathering. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī says general gathering can be paired with the specific Qur'an or recitation, unlike 'reading people'; the second term can add a sense as 'warn' adds to 'inform.' Ibn Kathīr describes successive preservation, recitation, and explanation; al-Zarkashī distinguishes heart, tongue, and hearing.
  75:17/q06/p2 Both can denote collection, with external existence and inward memorization distinguished. — reasons: Al-Rāzī offers this distinction to avoid redundancy if qurʾān also means collection.
  75:17/q06/p3 Both terms jointly refer to composition and collection. — reasons: Abū ʿUbayda treats the pairing figuratively as arrangement of parts; Ibn Qutayba and Abū ʿUbayda relate the root to joining sūras and the fetus idiom.
75:17/q07 What does 'its qurʾān' mean, and whose recitation is promised? [قُرْءَانَهُۥ · meaning] (turns on: Whether qurʾān denotes recitation or collection, and on the recitation sense whether the immediate reciter is Muhammad, Gabriel, or God.)
  75:17/q07/p1 God enables the Prophet himself to recite the revelation reliably. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ḍaḥḥāk call qurʾān a verbal noun of reading; the Prophet recites after it is fixed in his heart. Al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī compare 87:6, distinguishing his recitation from Gabriel's on another reading. Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites Ḥassān's verse; al-Ālūsī says he may recite whenever he wishes. Jishumī includes repeating and explaining it; al-Bursawī specifies ordered joining of letters and words.
  75:17/q07/p2 Qurʾān means collection, composition, or arrangement of revelation. — reasons: Abū ʿUbayda and Ibn Qutayba derive the name from joining sūras. The she-camel or woman carrying no fetus, ʿAmr ibn Kulthūm's لم تقرأ جنينا, water collected in a basin and its maqrāh, and a gathered qarya attest qaraʾa as collecting; al-Zajjāj relates the name to collected speech. Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī compare the verbal-noun forms rujḥān and nuqṣān. Al-Ṭabarī says both composition and clarification are valid Arabic, while each revealed verse bound the Prophet before full compilation. Al-Ālūsī questions the reported composition account, though he also records its etymology.
  75:17/q07/p3 God recites the revelation to the Prophet so that he understands or retains it. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Wāḥidī explicitly add understanding; Muqātil and al-Rāzī connect recitation with memorization and freedom from forgetting, the latter comparing 87:6. Ibn ʿAbbās's alternate report treats it as divine clarification through recitation, followed by the Prophet's following its contents; al-Ṭabarī says this is valid Arabic.
  75:17/q07/p4 Gabriel repeats or recites it to the Prophet. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ says Gabriel's repetition assures the Prophet's recitation; ʿAṭāʾ and al-Rāzī describe repetition until memorized. Al-Wāḥidī contrasts Gabriel as reciter with the Prophet on al-Zajjāj's interpretation.
  75:17/q07/p5 Qurʾān is the verbal noun 'recitation,' without identifying a single reciter here. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ equates qirāʾa and qurʾān as verbal nouns; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī cite Ḥassān's nightlong recitation verse, as does Ibn ʿĀshūr. Al-Bursawī compares ghufrān; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds furqān; Elmalılı also allows the recited text as the noun's sense, and al-Nasafī compares 20:114.
  75:17/q07/p6 It means collecting the recitation in the Prophet's chest. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī reports this alternate sense and calls the noun a verbal form like rajḥān and nuqṣān; Ibn ʿAṭiyya also reports collection and arrangement in the breast.
  75:17/q07/p7 It is God's reading of the person's deeds or record at resurrection. — reasons: The record interpretation makes the reader acknowledge its contents and face explanation through requital; al-Qaffāl calls it a strong warning but notes lack of reports.
  75:17/q07/p8 It is God's revelation of Judgment's description. — reasons: A reported interpretation says the gathering is of Judgment's people and its explanation comes with the trumpet blast.
75:17/q08 How does the verbal noun 'qurʾānahu' work grammatically? [قُرْءَانَهُۥ · grammar]
  75:17/q08/p1 It is a verbal noun annexed to its object, with its agent unexpressed. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī says the pronoun is the object of 'our gathering and reciting it,' avoiding the absurdity of adding a named Qur'an to itself; al-Samīn paraphrases 'your reciting it.' Al-Ālūsī also understands an omitted annexed word on the recitation sense.
75:17/q09 Is there a variant reading of 'qurʾānahu'? [قُرْءَانَهُۥ · readings]
  75:17/q09/p1 A reading eases the hamza, as in wa-qurānahu / wa-qarānahu. — reasons: The qirāʾāt note assigns hamza easing to Ḥamza while retaining the q-r-ʾ field; al-Samīn reports Abū al-ʿĀliya's جمعه وقرانه and فاتبع قرانه through Ibn ʿAṭiyya and relates it to Ibn Kathīr's earlier variant discussion.
