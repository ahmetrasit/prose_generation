[RAZI-FULL:v22p15#2/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The command may express reverence for the holy place: Moses should approach barefoot, humbly and in awe while hearing God’s speech; the next clause explains why.
[RAZI-FULL:v22p15#2/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | An inward reading takes sandals in dreams to signify wife and child: Moses is told not to let concern for them occupy his heart.
[RAZI-FULL:v22p15#2/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | Another inward reading makes the sandals attachment to both this world and the next; Moses should be wholly absorbed in knowing God, the holy valley signifying divine purity.
[RAZI-FULL:v22p15#2/r7] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | A third reading likens the two sandals to premises used to infer the Creator: after reaching divine knowledge, one should cease attending to the premises and become absorbed in God.
[RAZI-FULL:v22p16/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · المعتزلة | reports | The Muʿtazila argue that if God’s speech were eternal, He would have commanded Moses before his existence, like absurdly addressing absent people in an empty house.
[RAZI-FULL:v22p16/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · أصحابنا | holds | The first reply is that God’s speech is eternal, but it was not an imperative or prohibition in eternity.
[RAZI-FULL:v22p16/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · أصحابنا | holds | The second reply treats the eternal speech as an order whose persistence makes the person commanded in the future without any change in that speech, analogously to eternal power yielding capacity for action later.
[RAZI-FULL:v22p16/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The verse does not establish that praying or circumambulating in sandals is disliked: if removal honored the valley, that command was specific to the occasion.
