<<90:6 notes, part 4 of 0..4>>
-LAMASAT · الكشاف | reports | Al-Kashshaf reports lubadan with damma or kasra as a plural of lubda or libda, meaning a mass and hence great quantity.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p260/r5] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | The piled wealth echoes the disbelievers massing against the Messenger and Muslims to stop the call, paralleling their joint attempt to destroy his mission. | mentions 72:19
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p261/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | Labad contributes to the surah's collective pattern: parents and offspring, paired faculties and paths, several ascent duties, believers advising one another, and disbelievers gathered in Hell; the plain word many would lose this effect. | mentions 80:20, 76:3
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p262/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · التبيان | reports | Al-Tibyan notes that lam marks the past, matching the past-tense boast: does the person think nobody saw what he spent and destroyed?
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p263/r3] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | The tongue and lips enable his boast that he spent a heap, so he uses God’s gifts while opposing God’s allies, messengers, and message.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p265/r2] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | He failed to repay God’s favors with righteous deeds such as freeing people and feeding orphans and the poor; approved giving benefits at God’s sight, unlike wasteful boasting, which is like ruined crops. | mentions 3:117
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p272/r4] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | The man who boasts of wasting much money spent it in the wrong place, since he neither fed a hungry person nor freed anyone.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p274/r5] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | The boastful spender needs mercy so he directs his wealth to the needy instead of wasting it uselessly; the enslaved person also needs compassion.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p281/r2] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | The hoarder who gathers and locks away wealth is repaid with sealed Hell doors and columns; al-Balad instead describes someone who squandered wealth. | mentions 104:2, 104:3
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p283/r4] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | Other expressions preserve breadth: the questioned person may be anyone or a particular person, and his claim to have spent much may describe display, opposition to the Prophet, another purpose, or a lie.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p283/r5] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | Labad can be a plural meaning heaps or a singular adjective meaning piled up or obstinate.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p284/r6] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | The surah is woven around five threads: human toil, breadth of meaning, collective action, patience, and mercy.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p392/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | This is the Qur’an’s only use of ‘destroyed’ for money; the word fits the sūra’s hardship, death, obstacle, famine, and wasteful spending, where money spent wrongly is ruined. | mentions 90:4, 90:11, 90:14, 90:19
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p392/r2] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | Lubad means much gathered together and may be an intensive singular or a plural of lubda; the gathered mass suits the hostile crowds assembled against the Prophet. | mentions 72:19
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p392/r3] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | The image of gathering recurs across the sūra, and ‘much’ alone would miss this shared sense; the wording serves the sūra’s rhetoric. | mentions 90:3, 90:4, 90:11, 90:17, 90:20
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p403/r2] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | Someone who spent heaps of wealth should have directed some of it to orphans, the poor, and debtors instead of wasting it unjustly.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p408/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | Al-Humazah's hoarder thinks wealth makes him immortal; shutting him into Hell's sealed columns fittingly mirrors his hoarding, unlike al-Balad's description of someone who squandered wealth. | mentions 104:2-9
[STUDYQURAN:90:5/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The question concerns whether man expects accountability for all his acts; wealth-deluded people forget God controls provision and can remove it at any time, and answer for how they obtain and spend it regardless of philanthropy. | mentions 13:26, 17:30, 28:82, 29:62, 30:37, 34:36, 34:39
[STUDYQURAN:90:5/r2] STUDYQURAN · Q, Ṭs | reports | Some identify the speaker as Ḥarith ibn ʿĀmir, who complained he had spent his wealth on expiations and payments since entering Muhammad's religion.
