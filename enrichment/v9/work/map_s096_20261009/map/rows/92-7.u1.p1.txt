  92:7/q12/p2 God grants the believer special enabling so obedience outweighs disobedience. — reasons: Rāzī reports this inference of his Ashʿarī associates. Abū Ḥayyān objects to a Muʿtazilite implication in Zamakhsharī's account, adding that taysīr prepares for either outcome by contrast; Ibn Kathīr connects intended good or evil to divine success or abandonment.
92:7/q13 Do deeds lead to further deeds of the same kind? [* · interpretation]
  92:7/q13/p1 Good deeds bring further good deeds, while sins bring further sins. — reasons: Early authorities give this maxim; Ibn Kathīr relates it to the following opposite clause. Ibn ʿĀshūr says prior giving, piety and affirmation cause and are rewarded by continued deeds; Elmalılı says good actions multiply means to the goal, and Kur'an Yolu says the habit of generosity stabilizes.
92:7/q14 What occasions or incidents are reported for this promise? [* · reports] (turns on: The narratives identify different acts and people as the setting of the promise; the palm-sale versions also vary in the exchange price.)
  92:7/q14/p1 Abū Bakr freed vulnerable converts despite his father's advice to free strong protectors. — reasons: Ibn Isḥāq and ʿĀmir ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn al-Zubayr say he sought God's face or reward; Ibn Kathīr locates the freeing in Mecca. Some versions emphasize tortured converts; a Bilāl version names Umayya ibn Khalaf and a garment and ten coins. Another Ibn ʿAbbās route names Abū Sufyān as the opposed miser.
  92:7/q14/p2 A stingy palm owner denied fallen dates to a poor family; Abū al-Daḥdāḥ bought the tree and donated it. — reasons: ʿAṭāʾ says the owner refused the Prophet's offer of a Paradise palm. Versions say Abū al-Daḥdāḥ traded an orchard; Ibn ʿAbbās says forty palms. The buyer receives a promised Paradise palm. The contrasting figures' efforts are mapped to verses 5–10.
  92:7/q14/p3 At sunrise two angels pray for replacement of the spender's gift and ruin of the hoarder's wealth. — reasons: Māwardī transmits Qatāda through Khulayd from Abū al-Dardāʾ; the Prophet then recites these verses, with no grading stated.
92:7/q15 How does this promise fit the sūra's paired paths? [* · links]
  92:7/q15/p1 It is the good half of the paired sequence: conduct leads to felicity, while opposed conduct leads to hardship. — reasons: Ṭabarī contrasts the giver with the self-sufficient miser; Rāzī explains divergent striving and reward or punishment. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Corpus Coranicum name paired giving/stinginess, piety/self-sufficiency, affirmation/denial and ease/hardship; Ibn ʿĀshūr says naming giving encourages generosity. Corpus Coranicum notes symmetric behavior-to-consequence sequences, cross-rhyme, and the last stanza's reversed order, with outer blessed sections joined by ittaqā/atqā (92:5, 17). Abū Ḥayyān links the sūra to success and failure in 91:9–10.
92:7/q16 What other scriptural passages illuminate this promise? [* · links]
  92:7/q16/p1 It echoes the promise of an easy way in 87:8 and relates to other passages on ease, guidance and reward. — reasons: Quṭb calls 87:8 the promise to the Messenger; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the pairing appears only there and here. Corpus Coranicum compares ease/hardship in 94:5–6, Study Quran adds God's desire for ease in 2:185 and appointment of ease for reverence in 65:4, and Asad points to his 87:8 note. Burhān says the Prophet used al-Layl when describing Paradise, alongside 32:17 and 56:30.
92:7/q17 What practical response does the contrast of ease and hardship call for? [* · interpretation]
  92:7/q17/p1 Follow God's commands and strive for the good end despite difficulty in resisting desire. — reasons: Sāmarrāʾī says ease and hardship in either life belong to God; Islahi says the virtuous struggle against base desires, grow stronger, and finally reach the company and Paradise of 89:29–30. The young men in Qurṭubī's report resolve to work after hearing the Prophet's teaching.
92:7/q18 How does God's facilitation unfold over a person's life? [* · theology]
  92:7/q18/p1 God progressively leads the giver through good deeds toward an end already present in divine knowledge. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya describes the visible, gradual unfolding of facilitation and distinguishes it from God's prior knowledge of the outcome.
92:7/q19 What is the counterpart of this easy outcome? [* · interpretation]
  92:7/q19/p1 Al-ʿusrā is hardship or punishment, including Hell. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr allows a condition of hardship or its place, citing 74:9–10 for the hard day of disbelievers.
92:7/q20 Which deeds mark the giver and the contrasting miser? [* · interpretation]
  92:7/q20/p1 The giver pays God's due, avoids prohibitions and believes His promise; the miser withholds that due and denies the promise. — reasons: Qatāda adds self-sufficiency to the miser's profile.
92:7/q21 Can divine preparation concern both outcomes? [* · theology]
  92:7/q21/p1 God may withhold favors so obedience is hard, and taysīr may prepare a person for either outcome. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān reports Zamakhsharī's formulation, including paths to Paradise and Hell and 6:125; he rejects what he calls its Muʿtazilite implication and offers preparation for both outcomes by contrast.
