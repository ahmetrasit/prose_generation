[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p156/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | Abū Ḥayyān rejects the reply about matching the syntax: it would say worship so that you may worship, a poor meaning and expression.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p156/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The command clause is the main requested action, and its hope clause addresses those same addressees; this supports connecting لعل to worship.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p160/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The passage gives five signs: creating the addressees and their forebears, then earth as a bed, heaven as a building, and rain and fruit; humans come first as familiar, earth before sky, and effects after causes.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p160/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · قيل | reports | Another explanation puts accountable humans first because life, ability, desire, and reason are prerequisites for benefiting from the heavens, earth, water, and fruit.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p161/r10] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | He also objects that this parsing makes human creation serve the purpose of fearing punishment, which follows al-Zamakhshari's previously discussed Muʿtazilite doctrine.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p161/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari attaches the prohibition to لعل, parses تجعلوا as subjunctive after a hope, and understands creation as aimed at fear of God's punishment.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p161/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | Abu Hayyan rejects al-Zamakhshari's parsing because Basran grammarians do not allow a subjunctive as the answer to hope; only Kufans analogize it to an interrogative answer.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p163/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · بعض المفسرين | reports | Some exegetes say these creations are singled out because they invite reflection, display benefits in both lives, and prove the Creator's existence, power, wisdom, life, will and exclusive creation and governance.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p163/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · بعض المفسرين | reports | They explain the human creation's precedence by its intricate workmanship, clearer in delicate bodies, and by self-reflection being mentally nearer; the passage cites the call to observe oneself. | mentions 51:21
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p163/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · بعض المفسرين | reports | Another reason for mentioning humans first is Arab custom of placing what matters most first; God gives special care to humanity as his noblest creation. | mentions 17:70
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p163/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · بعض المفسرين | reports | The sequence can also put the beneficiary before the gifts, since God made these things as benefits for Adam's children and prepared them as blessings.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p164/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The two verses' craft, wisdom and clear proofs show that God alone creates and provides; rivals neither create nor provide, and can neither benefit nor harm. | mentions 6:62

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p164/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · بعض أصحاب الإشارات | reports | The allusion concludes that recognition of God as the real creator should lead people to single him out in divinity and worship, bringing them to guidance.

[SAMIN-DURR:2:21#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Khalq can mean origination and creation, unique to God, or measuring and shaping, which is not exclusive to God.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:21#3/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn Atiyya sees laʿalla as attached to khalqakum: every child is born on the natural disposition and may therefore be expected to become God-fearing.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:21#3/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · المهدوي | rejects | Al-Mahdawi objects that this cannot attach to creation, since those created for Hell were not created in order to become God-fearing.

[SAMIN-DURR:2:21#3/r14] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The author attaches laʿalla to uʿbudu: worship is the commanded purpose, while the relative clause describes or praises the one worshipped rather than supplying a competing attachment.
