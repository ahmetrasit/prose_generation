[SULAMI:v1p30/r3] SULAMI, d. 412 · بعضهم | reports | Dropping letters from Allah leaves successive divine indications—lillah, lahu, then hāʾ—which is the utmost signification.
[SULAMI:v1p30/r4] SULAMI, d. 412 · بعضهم | reports | Created beings are enraptured variously: their secret by divine majesty, heart by forms of gnosis, or tongue by constant remembrance.

[SULAMI:v1p30/r6] SULAMI, d. 412 · بعضهم | reports | Uttering Allah through letters cannot truly name Him, since He exceeds letters, concepts, and imaginings; the formula is accepted because no other mode of affirming oneness is possible.

[SULAMI:v1p31/r2] SULAMI, d. 412 · أبو سعيد الخراز | reports | God first called servants to the single name Allah; people of realities need that name, while further descriptions in the chapter serve successively lower ranks.

[SULAMI:v1p31/r4] SULAMI, d. 412 · بعضهم | reports | In Allah, the two lām letters and hāʾ are connected while alif is separate, indicating that the reference to God is not made through the servant's own self.
[SULAMI:v1p31/r5] SULAMI, d. 412 · جعفر | reports | The four letters form a complete, unique name: alif is the pillar of oneness, the two lām letters signify the Tablet of the Pen and of Prophecy, and hāʾ is the utmost indication; God exceeds apprehension.
[SULAMI:v1p32/r1] SULAMI, d. 412 · author | holds | All enduring graces for believers issue through al-Rahman, and no creature can bear that name because creatures cannot give what lasts.
[SULAMI:v1p32/r2] SULAMI, d. 412 · author | holds | Al-Rahman's mercy lets seekers detach from everything below Him; its worldly bounty reaches friend and enemy alike.
[SULAMI:v1p32/r3] SULAMI, d. 412 · بعضهم | reports | Al-Rahman signifies the sweetness of benefaction, witnessed nearness, and careful service.
[SULAMI:v1p32/r4] SULAMI, d. 412 · author | holds | Lovers, the fearful, repentant, and sinners each receive a distinct spiritual fruit from the meanings of al-Rahman: intimacy, security, purification, or remorse and seeking forgiveness.
[SULAMI:v1p32/r5] SULAMI, d. 412 · ابن عطاء | reports | Al-Rahman means God's aid and victory.
[SULAMI:v1p32/r6] SULAMI, d. 412 · الواسطي | reports | One approaches al-Rahman only through His all-encompassing mercy, while al-Rahim is approached through obedience; the Prophet also bears the latter name (9:128). | mentions 9:128

[SULAMI:v1p33/r2] SULAMI, d. 412 · بعضهم | reports | The Prophet, called compassionate and merciful toward believers, is a means by which people reach God and the two divine names; the Merciful accepts the returning person despite faults and preserves even one who turns away. | mentions 9:128
[SULAMI:v1p33/r3] SULAMI, d. 412 · ابن عطاء | reports | Al-Rahim signifies God's affection and mercy.
[SULAMI:v1p33/r4] SULAMI, d. 412 · جعفر | reports | Al-Rahman suits those sought by God, immersed in lights and realities; al-Rahim suits seekers remaining with themselves and tending outward practice.
[SULAMI:v1p33/r5] SULAMI, d. 412 · جعفر | reports | Al-Rahman's mercy reaches back into pre-eternity and has no endpoint, whereas al-Rahim connects mercy with worldly life, guidance, and provisions.
