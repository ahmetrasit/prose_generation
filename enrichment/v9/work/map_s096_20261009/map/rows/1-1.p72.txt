[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p146/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The genitive phrase is an adverbial complement attached to an omitted verb, not a stable predicate; the action being begun identifies the verb.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p146/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The estimated verb names the particular action being started, rather than a generic “I begin,” because that action supplies the contextual clue.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p146/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | This differs from the grammarians' debate about estimating a noun or verb after stable adverbials used as predicates or circumstances; a loose adverbial normally attaches to an action verb.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p146/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | A specific action verb extends the blessing of God's name across the action's parts; the omission also makes the formula usable before any action without altering Qur'anic wording.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p146/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | This is concise ellipsis, unlike stable adverbial predicates such as “good is with you,” whose complement is not normally made explicit.
