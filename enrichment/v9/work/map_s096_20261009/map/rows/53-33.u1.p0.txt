## THE CURRENT MAP
53:33/q01 How is the hamza in 'Have you seen' read? [أَفَرَءَيْتَ · readings] (turns on: Different pronunciations of the hamza: elision, lengthening, or an intermediate realization.)
  53:33/q01/p1 The hamza of raʾā is omitted to ease pronunciation. — reasons: QIRAAT-ER says the grammar remains unchanged.
  53:33/q01/p2 The hamza is lengthened to ā by madd badal. — reasons: QIRAAT-ER says the resulting stretch gives the question rhetorical emphasis.
  53:33/q01/p3 The hamza is realized between a full glottal stop and omission. — reasons: QIRAAT-ER describes this as a dialectal variant.
53:33/q02 What does 'Have you seen' mean and how is it construed? [أَفَرَءَيْتَ · grammar]
  53:33/q02/p1 It means 'tell me'; the relative expression is its first object and the following question is its second. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī give both objects. Al-Samīn also glosses the neighboring arāʾayta as 'tell me.'
53:33/q03 How does the opening fāʾ connect this question with what precedes? [أَفَرَءَيْتَ · links]
  53:33/q03/p1 It draws the question from the preceding teaching and makes the man's conduct startling in that light. — reasons: Rāzī relates it to God's knowledge, power, recompense, and people's need for the Prophet's guidance after 53:29–32. Ibn ʿĀshūr relates the astonishment to 53:31's promised recompense and the rule that each has only what he strives for; he also notes the intervening warning against pride in good deeds and others' praise. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the fāʾ branches from the preceding discussion.
53:33/q04 Who is the one who turned away? [ٱلَّذِى تَوَلَّىٰ · referent] (turns on: Whether al-ladhī singles out a historical individual or depicts a type, and whether the conflicting occasion reports are reliable; Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses its individual force, while Rāzī and Islāḥī allow a general or representative sense.)
  53:33/q04/p1 Al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra. — reasons: Muqātil, Mujāhid, Ibn Zayd, and others identify him. Accounts say he heard or followed the Prophet, sometimes with Abū Bakr; a pagan shamed him for leaving his fathers' religion and offered to carry his punishment for payment. He returned to shirk, paid only part, then withheld the rest. One report says he had pledged faith; others say only that he neared Islam. Study Quran reverses the payment direction, saying al-Walīd offered a follower money to return to Quraysh religion. Rāzī reports the identification but prefers a general referent. Islāḥī says the account lacks a sound chain and fits the verse poorly; Kuranyolu also disputes the reports.
  53:33/q04/p2 A representative person or class who turns from reminder, belief, or good works, rather than one necessarily named individual. — reasons: Rāzī connects the referent to the self-sufficient people of 53:29; Arabic can resume a general man in singular form, as in 28:84. His broader reading has one seek worldly life, contribute a little toward the hereafter, then stop; Asad adopts this reading, though his excerpt does not identify the earlier verses. Islāḥī says a generic relative can make one figure portray a group, like the woman unraveling yarn in 16:92; his figure is a miserly idolater among wealthy Quraysh who expects Paradise through alleged deities and ancestry. Biqāʿī extends the wording to apostates, hypocrites, and believers who abandon good deeds. Kuranyolu describes someone briefly compassionate but persistently stingy. Study Quran allows anyone ignoring others' needs.
  53:33/q04/p3 ʿUthmān, whose foster brother ʿAbd Allāh ibn Saʿd ibn Abī Sarḥ persuaded him to curtail charity. — reasons: Accounts attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Suddī, al-Kalbī, and al-Musayyib ibn Sharīk say the brother offered to bear his sins for a camel and gear or money; ʿUthmān temporarily stopped or reduced charity and later resumed it. Some connect his turning to leaving his post at Uḥud. Rāzī calls the account unestablished and unlike ʿUthmān; Ibn ʿAṭiyya declares him innocent. Ibn ʿĀshūr says he would not have listened to Ibn Abī Sarḥ.
  53:33/q04/p4 Al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil al-Sahmī. — reasons: Al-Suddī says he sometimes agreed with the Prophet on certain matters.
  53:33/q04/p5 Al-Naḍr ibn al-Ḥārith. — reasons: Al-Ḍaḥḥāk says he gave five camels, described also as five falāʾis, to a poor emigrant or poor Muslims to induce apostasy, promising to carry their sin; the recipients vary in the reports.
  53:33/q04/p6 Abū Jahl. — reasons: Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb al-Quraẓī says he acknowledged that Muhammad commanded noble conduct but did not believe.
  53:33/q04/p7 An unnamed convert who paid another man to assume his punishment. — reasons: Ibn Zayd's accounts describe a new Muslim shamed or approached by a former co-religionist, who offered to bear his sins for money; the convert paid a little, then bargained or documented the agreement. One version does not name the man.
  53:33/q04/p8 An unnamed man headed to the Prophet for good but accepted a pagan's offer to take his provisions and bear his sin. — reasons: ʿAṭāʾ ibn Yasār gives this account in al-Thaʿlabī and al-Jishumī.
  53:33/q04/p9 An unnamed man in an expedition who accepted a friend's camel for the friend to take on his sins. — reasons: ʿIkrima says the man lacked a mount and his friend offered one in this exchange.
  53:33/q04/p10 An unnamed man whom another transported to catch up with an army after the Prophet could not mount him. — reasons: Darrāj's reports describe the offered transport; the Lubāb version says the traveler could catch the army with his good deeds and places revelation through 53:41 after the event.
  53:33/q04/p11 An unnamed Muslim who had spent generously in God's cause but stopped after others promised to bear his sins. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this occasion without naming the spender; the warning of poverty and discontinued spending define the incident.
  53:33/q04/p12 ʿAbd Allāh ibn Saʿd ibn Abī Sarḥ, the man who dissuaded ʿUthmān from giving before his own conversion. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this identification from al-Wāḥidī's Asbāb and al-Kashshāf, then relays Ibn ʿAṭiyya's rejection of the account because ʿUthmān would not have listened to him.
53:33/q05 What does 'turned away' mean here? [تَوَلَّىٰ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the omitted object of turning is faith or truth, remembrance, the Uḥud position, or charitable spending; the following verse supports the spending reading for Ṭabāṭabāʾī.)
  53:33/q05/p1 He turned away from truth, Islam, or following and remaining steadfast in it. — reasons: Muqātil, Ibn ʿAbbās, and others gloss it as leaving truth or faith. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says he turned from God's command. Abū Ḥayyān notes unqualified tawallā often metaphorically means not entering faith; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the man stopped considering Islam after nearly embracing it. Al-Thaʿlabī's account says he returned from belief; Biqāʿī broadens the wording to apostates, hypocrites, and backsliders.
  53:33/q05/p2 He turned from revelation or remembrance to worldly pursuits. — reasons: Rāzī links the person to those in 53:29 who neglect reminder out of self-sufficiency and seek only worldly life; Biqāʿī says the person had formerly been devoted to remembrance and confident of his family's faith.
  53:33/q05/p3 He left his post at Uḥud. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī takes the verse as concerning ʿUthmān, who later returned to better conduct; the ʿUthmān report in al-Thaʿlabī and al-Ṭabrisī also connects his turning with Uḥud. Abū Ḥayyān presents this as al-Zamakhsharī's alternative to turning from Islam.
  53:33/q05/p4 He turned away from spending in God's cause and stopped giving. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the next verse as specifying the turning; the man first gave a little and then ceased altogether. Kuranyolu describes a brief feeling of compassion overcome by attachment to wealth and lasting stinginess.
53:33/q06 What happened in the reported occasion for these verses? [* · reports] (turns on: The reports give different people, exchanges, and expedition settings; some commentators challenge their chains, fit with the wording, or fit with ʿUthmān's character.)
  53:33/q06/p1 Al-Walīd approached or accepted Islam, was shamed by a pagan, and paid part of a promised sum for that man to bear his punishment before withholding the rest. — reasons: Versions vary between approaching Islam, accepting it, or pledging to the Prophet; some place him with the Prophet and Abū Bakr. The pagan invoked ancestral religion. Al-Thaʿlabī explains the small gift as payment to the guarantor and akdā as withholding the remainder. Study Quran instead says al-Walīd offered a Prophet follower money to return to Quraysh religion.
  53:33/q06/p2 ʿUthmān curtailed charity after his foster brother offered to bear his sins for payment, then resumed giving; some relate the turning to Uḥud. — reasons: The price is described as a camel and gear or money; some say he stopped a portion of charity, others that he ceased giving. Al-Kashshāf says he later returned to better conduct. Ṭabāṭabāʾī gives an unnamed generous Muslim with a similar poverty warning.
  53:33/q06/p3 An unnamed new Muslim paid an opponent to take responsibility for his sins and then limited or documented the payment. — reasons: Ibn Zayd's versions mention a friend's shaming, an offer of relief from all punishment, partial payment, bargaining, and a witnessed written agreement.
  53:33/q06/p4 Al-Naḍr gave five camels to a poor emigrant or poor Muslims to apostatize and promised to bear the sin. — reasons: Al-Ḍaḥḥāk's accounts differ on whether the recipient was singular or plural; Abū Ḥayyān calls the payment five falāʾis.
  53:33/q06/p5 Al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil sometimes agreed with the Prophet on certain matters. — reasons: Al-Suddī gives this as the occasion without further incident details.
  53:33/q06/p6 Abū Jahl acknowledged Muhammad's call to noble manners but did not believe. — reasons: Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb al-Quraẓī supplies this incident.
