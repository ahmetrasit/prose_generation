80:6/q10 What guidance or rebuke does this attention convey? [* · interpretation] (turns on: The notes differ on whom the rebuke ultimately targets and whether the alleged conduct can be attributed to the Prophet; they also stress different practical lessons.)
  80:6/q10/p1 The Prophet should honor and teach receptive poor seekers and warn everyone equally instead of favoring wealthy or eminent people. — reasons: Abū ʿUthmān, cited by al-Sulamī, says God commanded companionship with and honor for the poor and forbade love of the wealthy. Al-Qushayrī calls the passage a reproach on behalf of the poor. Ibn Kathīr includes eminent and weak, rich and poor, masters and slaves, men and women, adults and children; God guides whom He wills. Al-Iṣlāḥī says the Prophet must teach eager seekers and is not accountable for those who refuse to come. Ṭabāṭabāʾī's 80:5–12 reading contrasts scornful wealthy people unconcerned with purification and an earnest God-fearing seeker.
  80:6/q10/p2 Pursuing a wealthy person who turns away is improper, especially given the Prophet's high station. — reasons: Al-Burṣawī says a noble person does not eagerly approach one who turns away. Al-Ālūsī likewise cites a verse about the impropriety of seeking the company of one who rejects yours. Al-Biqāʿī says luxury and self-sufficiency predispose rejection and wrongdoing, whereas affliction can humble and soften hearts.
  80:6/q10/p3 The direct rebuke ultimately targets haughty leaders who ignore the message and seek exclusion of the Prophet's poor companions. — reasons: Al-Iṣlāḥī says those leaders wanted the poor companions removed as a condition of their own attendance, even though the Prophet is the person addressed.
  80:6/q10/p4 Attributing disdain for the believing poor to the Prophet is untenable, whether or not a new prohibition had been revealed. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the excuse that this became sinful only afterward: reason already condemns preferring wealth over piety, and 68:4 praises the Prophet's character. He and al-Murtaḍā invoke that character; Ṭabāṭabāʾī further cites earlier commands at 26:214–15 and 15:88, 15:94.
80:6/q11 How does this contrast fit the sūra and its neighboring sūra? [* · links] (turns on: The two notes identify different aspects of the sūra's connection with al-Nāziʿāt.)
  80:6/q11/p1 The sūra's call to warn those who fear is developed through resurrection, human creation, and food as signs of God's power; receptivity explains the contrast. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī relates the warning of those who fear in 79:45 to purification of receptive hearts and finds rejection astonishing in light of the signs.
  80:6/q11/p2 ʿAbasa pairs with al-Nāziʿāt in theme, discourse style, and reasoning, while changing the order of topics. — reasons: Al-Iṣlāḥī cites 79:45 and says the altered sequence gives this sūra its distinct majesty.
