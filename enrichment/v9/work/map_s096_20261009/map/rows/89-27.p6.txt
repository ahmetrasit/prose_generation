[ALUSI:89:30/r27] ALUSI, d. 1270 · السادة الصوفية | reports | Sufi authors see three stations in the passage—tranquil, content, and approved—and explain each in their works.
[ALUSI:89:30/r28] ALUSI, d. 1270 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Citing a teaching of the Prophet to some Companions, transmitted from Abū Umāma by al-Ṭabarānī and Ibn ʿAsākir, al-Ālūsī offers a prayer for a soul that believes in meeting God, accepts His decree, and is content with His provision.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p130/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | This passage depicts the state of one at rest through God's remembrance and obedience, contrasted with the person settled in worldly life; God is understood to be speaking, directly or through an angel, likely after judgment.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p130/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | One interpretation makes the nafs the self: reason ascends through causes to the First Cause, and the soul becomes calm upon knowing and relying wholly on God.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p130/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | Another, which al-Alusi calls a good interpretation, is the believer certain of truth, whose heart has no doubt or disturbance; this links remembrance to the strength of certainty and 39:9. | mentions 39:9
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p130/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | A further view is the believer who feels no fear or grief at resurrection; Abu's reading “secure, at rest” supports it and contrasts with the earlier grieving person.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p130/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · أبو | reports | Abu reads “O secure soul, at rest,” connecting the two descriptions.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p130/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · زيد | reports | Zayd reads the vocative particle without its tāʾ; the grammarian in al-Badiʿ explains why the form stays masculine with a feminine addressee, an analysis al-Alusi dismisses as irrelevant.
[ELMALILI:89:16-26#3/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The next passage contrasts the fate of the person attached to worldly pleasures with that of the soul content with God, His remembrance, obedience, and approval.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The third account of tranquility is that hearts rest only through remembrance of God, as both Qur’an and rational proof establish. | mentions 13:28
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Mücâhid | reports | Mujahid says it is certain that God is its Lord and submits to His command in every deed.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r11] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Another formulation says the soul is certain God is its Lord, gives its heart only to Him, and has a heart that beats by His command; it is repentant and humble. | mentions 8:2, 22:34, 22:35, 39:54
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r12] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Reports describing certainty of meeting God and a heart beating with longing for Him also point to the complementary Qur’anic descriptions of believers and the humble. | mentions 8:2, 22:34, 22:35, 39:54
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r13] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Sufi writers discuss the soul’s ranks, stations, and states; Sufism as a whole revolves around the maxim that knowing oneself leads to knowing one’s Lord.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r14] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Sufis enumerate seven soul-states: commanding, self-reproaching, tranquil, content, accepted, inspired, and purified.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r15] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The preceding account contrasts the ignorant, worldly commanding soul and belatedly self-reproaching soul with the tranquil soul that understood truth in time. | mentions 89:24
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r16] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Exegetes differ on whether this address comes at death, resurrection, or after the reckoning; the author considers the last timing most apparent from the context. | mentions 89:24
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Razi’s first rational proof says the intellect keeps seeking a cause beyond every contingent cause until it reaches the Necessary Existent, where it can rest.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Razi’s second proof says humans have limitless needs but finite things cannot meet them; tranquility therefore requires God’s limitless perfection.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The tranquil soul chooses knowledge of God for God alone and is attached to Him in love, desire, abiding, and speech.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | A tranquil soul is defined by rising through unstable causes to know the First Source, relying only on God, and obeying Him with exclusive sincerity. | mentions 2:112, 2:207
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | This definition entails submission to God with excellence, certainty of release from fear and sorrow, and dedicating the self to seek only God’s pleasure. | mentions 2:112, 2:207
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | True freedom means escaping the animal ego, self-reproach, and bondage to anything besides God, then doing every good one can with life and wealth in God’s path. | mentions 90:8
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İbn Abbas | reports | Ibn Abbas defines the tranquil soul as one that confirms the truth.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Katâde | reports | Qatadah describes it as assured by God’s promise and confirming His word.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#3/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İkrime | reports | Ikrima and Dahhak report that the address and command to return occur at resurrection; on this reading the soul returns to its Lord for eternal life.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#3/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | prefers | Though Abu Salih’s reading seems apparent, the context favors the address after resurrection and completed reckoning; the reports can fit by treating each death as a person’s resurrection or the commands together as a unit. | mentions 89:24
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#3/r11] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Zeyd b. Eslem | reports | Zayd ibn Aslam reports that the tranquil soul is given this address at death, resurrection, and the gathering, and is promised Paradise.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#3/r12] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | The majority understand an omitted “God will say” from the preceding context, making the address and commands declarative and creative rather than legislative.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#3/r13] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The author also considers a direct, legislative divine address at the surah’s conclusion plausible; separation from what precedes highlights that the tranquil souls themselves should hear it.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#3/r14] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | prefers | Some exegetes take the address as applying to the tranquil soul in worldly life at all times; the author finds this clearer and more useful.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#3/r19] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The life of a tranquil soul among the righteous is like the ten nights before Eid al-Adha, and its entry into Paradise is the dawn of the greatest festival of bliss.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#3/r20] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The commentator prays that he and his readers return to God content and accepted, join His sincere servants, and reach Paradise and the vision of God.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#3/r21] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | The closing supplication asks God for a tranquil soul that believes in meeting Him, accepts His decree, and is content with His gift.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#3/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İbn Cübeyr | reports | Ibn Jubayr reports that when this verse was recited before Abu Bakr, the Prophet told him an angel would say it to him at death.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#3/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Hakîm-i Tirmizî | reports | Hakim al-Tirmidhi transmits a similar report from Abu Bakr through Sulaym ibn Amir.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#3/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | If the address occurs at death, nafs means spirit and returning is the spirit’s departure from the body toward its Lord.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The address is narrated as a future divine saying, preparing the listener for that day; the believer had already found tranquility through faith, sincerity, and offering good to God. | mentions 89:24
[ELMALILI:89:27-30/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Some commentators take nafs here to mean the self or essence, while others take it to mean the spirit; mutmainna can accordingly describe either.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30/r11] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Râzî | reports | Razi’s first account of tranquility is attaining certainty in the truth that no doubt can disturb, as in Abraham’s request for his heart to be reassured. | mentions 2:260
[ELMALILI:89:27-30/r12] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Râzî | reports | Razi’s second account is security from fear and grief, promised at death, resurrection, and entry into Paradise. | mentions 10:62, 41:30
[ELMALILI:89:27-30/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Nafs basically means a thing’s own essence; for a person it is the self that says “I” and has unified awareness through different states.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The self has both perceiving and perceived aspects; in sleep, heedlessness, fainting, or death those aspects may be considered apart, and nafs can name either sentient desire or rational discerning spirit.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The self’s unity is grounded in the higher reality of God, who comes between a person and their heart and gathers people to Him. | mentions 8:24
[ELMALILI:89:27-30/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The spirit is from the Lord’s command, and God takes souls at death and during sleep; separation from ordinary awareness is return to God, not annihilation. |
