27:36/q21 What does the verse teach about wealth, faith, and gifts? [* · interpretation]
  27:36/q21/p1 Religious guidance and divine gifts outrank worldly wealth; Solomon wanted submission, not payment. — reasons: Muqātil, al-Wāḥidī and al-Biqāʿī say he sought Islam and obedience. Iṣlāḥī and Kuranyolu stress that a prophet's mission is religion, not booty or purchased friendship; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the threat follows refusal. Jaʿfar and Bursevī counsel detachment, with a Persian couplet calling the world carrion and the Mathnawī contrasting spiritual dominion with clay-bound rule.
  27:36/q21/p2 An inward lesson is to choose lasting faith and nearness over sensory and imagined adornment. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī names certain knowledge and luminous visions; al-Taʾwīlāt, reported by Bursevī, stresses religious commands, afterlife ranks and lasting felicity.
27:36/q22 What follows from Solomon's handling of the gift? [* · law]
  27:36/q22/p1 A ruler may display grandeur to visiting envoys. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr draws this from the report of Solomon adorning a thousand palaces; al-Biqāʿī relates a similar display.
  27:36/q22/p2 Returning a polytheist's gift is recommended when it serves a religious interest. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī limits the inference to cases with a religious purpose, rather than an unconditional rule.
27:36/q23 How does the refusal connect to the demand for submission and the next verse? [* · links]
  27:36/q23/p1 The gift bypassed Solomon's demand for submission, so he returned it and threatened force if they refused. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 27:31; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the threat is conditional even though the wording attaches it to the messenger's return. Abū Ṣāliḥ reports the gift and army warning; Iṣlāḥī and Kuranyolu say the goal was faith. The Study Quran notes the narrative ends with the queen's faith.
  27:36/q23/p2 The story also illustrates the queen's prudence relative to her male advisers. — reasons: Kuranyolu draws this lesson from the larger encounter.
