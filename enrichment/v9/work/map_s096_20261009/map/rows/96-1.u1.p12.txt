  96:1/q31/p1 It develops al-Tīn's human creation and judgment themes. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān connects al-Tīn's best human form and later fate with this sūra's stages of humanity and its outcome. 96:1/q32 What does the opening teach about knowledge and divine guidance? [* · interpretation] (turns on: The connected themes of creation, teaching, recitation, faith, and responsibility.)
  96:1/q32/p1 God gives reading, writing, and knowledge; knowing Him through creation leads to received guidance. — reasons: BIQAI: Creation is named before command because it is perceptible, easier to conceive, and clearer evidence of God's existence, power, and wisdom; knowing God through His acts is the first obligation. BIQAI: The opening introduces foundational knowledge by recalling man's creation and some of his good and bad states, prompting wonder at God's power and reflection on His signs.; cites 95:4 BIQAI-FULL: Creation is placed before command because it is visible, easier to understand, and a clear proof of existence, power, and wisdom; knowing God through His acts is the first obligation. BIQAI-FULL: The Qur'an is the healing, clarifying guide to humanity's beginning and end, so the command is to read it reflectively; its description as guidance and mercy reinforces this role.; cites 25:1
