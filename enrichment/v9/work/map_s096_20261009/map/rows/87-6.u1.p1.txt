  87:6/q10/p2 He does not abandon acting on revelation’s commands and prohibitions, except superseded practice or concessions. — reasons: Junayd says acting on commands and prohibitions is meant; Ibn Kaysān praises his answer in Sulamī and Qurṭubī. Māwardī uniquely connects the concession version to forbidding shirk. Al-Ālūsī calls this reading far-fetched.
  87:6/q10/p3 The promise includes every object of forgetting, not only the recited revelation. — reasons: Biqāʿī explicitly includes anything else; Bursevī says the exception ranges over the broadest class of objects. Ālūsī reports perfect memory as a distinct view, then rejects it because of early transmitted interpretation and the consequential fāʾ.
  87:6/q10/p4 It guarantees retention of revealed law and sūras while also calling for continued attention and review. — reasons: These sources distinguish the verse from 75:16 and explain that neglecting review remains avoidable.
  87:6/q10/p5 It concerns the truth heard by the Prophet. — reasons: Bursevī attributes this wording to al-Rāghib.
87:6/q11 What does the exception for what God wills allow? [فَلَا تَنسَىٰٓ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the exception identifies an actual removal, ordinary lapse, deferred revelation, limited practice, or a rhetorical assertion of divine will.)
  87:6/q11/p1 God can remove abrogated recitation, often with its ruling, so it is forgotten or no longer practiced. — reasons: Ṭabarī explicitly prefers abrogation and withdrawal. Baghawī, Kashshāf, Rāzī and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite 2:106. Rāzī says it ceases to be recited in prayer then leaves memory. Qurṭubī prefers lifting recitation for a benefit, appealing to the normal force of an exception and the written yāʾ. Iṣlāḥī calls the removed directives temporary and says God signals their end.
  87:6/q11/p2 God may cause actual forgetting as He wills, without specifying abrogation. — reasons: Qatāda repeatedly states this open exception. Qurṭubī transmits Ibn ʿAbbās through Abū Ṣāliḥ and Qatāda through Saʿīd. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the object of God’s choosing is omitted by normal Arabic usage.
  87:6/q11/p3 The exception is formal or rhetorical; no forgetting is intended or actually occurs. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ compares 11:107 and ordinary oaths that qualify an intended complete gift. Al-Zamakhsharī adopts the empty-exception reading; Rāzī reports al-Kalbī that no forgetting occurred. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the exception preserves God’s unlimited power though He will not exercise it, citing 11:108.
  87:6/q11/p4 It allows a temporary lapse followed by recollection, without permanent loss. — reasons: Several cite the prayer omission and Ubayy’s query; Abū Ḥayyān instead cites ʿAbbād’s recitation reminding the Prophet of verses. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it an ordinary human lapse for which God supplies a reminder.
  87:6/q11/p5 Forgetting may establish a practice for the community. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān attributes this to Ibn ʿAbbās and includes it among actual exceptions.
  87:6/q11/p6 God may defer a revelation that Muhammad has not yet recited. — reasons: Māwardī attributes this to Ibn ʿĪsā; Qurṭubī reports it among alternatives.
  87:6/q11/p7 Any forgetting is confined to minor etiquette or sunna, not a required ruling. — reasons: Rāzī’s reported argument is that loss of an obligatory ruling would damage the law.
  87:6/q11/p8 The exception concerns material outside the duty to convey, which he may forget before conveying. — reasons: Qushayrī adds that God knows the secret and the public.
  87:6/q11/p9 The exception reserves an awareness for his praiseworthy station at resurrection. — reasons: Qāshānī allows loss of awareness or forgetting now for that later station.
  87:6/q11/p10 It asserts God’s unrestricted will even within a firm promise or enduring law. — reasons: Qutb says the Qur’an repeatedly emphasizes this freedom. Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses God could cause forgetting though He does not. Rāzī reports al-Farrāʾ that preservation is divine favor rather than Muhammad’s own power, citing 17:86 and 39:65.
  87:6/q11/p11 The qualification teaches saying “if God wills” about the future or keeps the Prophet attentive. — reasons: Rāzī cites 18:23–24 for the formula and separately reports vigilance over what might be excepted.
87:6/q12 What prompted the reassurance about forgetting? [فَلَا تَنسَىٰٓ · reports] (turns on: Whether the earlier anxiety appeared as inward rehearsal, speaking ahead of Gabriel, or Gabriel’s direct reassurance.)
  87:6/q12/p1 The Prophet rehearsed revelation inwardly or repeatedly out of fear of forgetting. — reasons: Mujāhid emphasizes private inward rehearsal; Ibn ʿAbbās gives the fear and divine assurance. Durr cites a similar report from Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ through al-Ḥākim. Qutb attributes the repetition to love and responsibility for every letter.
  87:6/q12/p2 He began repeating a verse or moving his tongue before Gabriel had finished, fearing he would lose it; the verse relieved his haste. — reasons: Thaʿlabī attributes it to Mujāhid and al-Kalbī; Wāḥidī adds Muqātil. Ibn ʿĀshūr rates the Ibn ʿAbbās account authentic and says the Prophet then recited exactly as Gabriel did. Iṣlāḥī adds that he wished for the whole revelation at once and was told revelation follows a wise timetable. Suyūṭī’s Ibn ʿAbbās chain contains Juwaybir, whom he rates very weak.
  87:6/q12/p3 Gabriel himself assured the Prophet amid fear of forgetting, with an account mentioning forgotten or lifted nonlegal verses and Moses’ tablets. — reasons: Durr transmits this wider narrative from Ibn ʿAbbās, mentioning 87:7.
87:6/q13 What reports illustrate a lapse or omission after the promise? [فَلَا تَنسَىٰٓ · reports] (turns on: Whether the reports imply temporary recall failure, withdrawn revelation, or no actual lapse.)
  87:6/q13/p1 He omitted a verse in prayer; Ubayy wondered whether it was abrogated, and the Prophet said he had forgotten it. — reasons: Bursevī notes his answer followed Ubayy’s question; ʿĀʾisha reports this incident in Ibn ʿĀshūr.
  87:6/q13/p2 A man’s night recitation reminded him of omitted verses. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr transmits ʿĀʾisha’s account.
  87:6/q13/p3 ʿAbbād’s recitation reminded him of verses in a sūra. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān cites this for a temporary lapse.
  87:6/q13/p4 A stoning verse and a warning against disowning one’s fathers had once been revealed. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr cites ʿUmar for material no longer recited.
  87:6/q13/p5 After this verse the Prophet forgot nothing. — reasons: Al-Kalbī is cited by Rāzī; Ibn ʿAbbās is transmitted through Abū Ṣāliḥ by Qurṭubī, who says until death, apart from what God willed.
87:6/q14 How does this promise relate to the passages against hurrying revelation? [* · links] (turns on: Whether the passages address the same moment of haste or different duties of preserving and reviewing revelation.)
  87:6/q14/p1 It belongs with 75:16–19 and 20:114: the Prophet need not hurry his tongue because God gathers and preserves revelation. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says al-Qiyāmah came later, so 87:6 is the earlier pledge; Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers the order 87:6, 75:16–19, then 20:114. Iṣlāḥī says revelation supplied support against opposition and answers to objections, explaining the eagerness.
  87:6/q14/p2 It is distinct from 75:16: here the law and sūras are preserved while review remains the Prophet’s duty. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān report this alternative to the direct identification with al-Qiyāmah.
87:6/q15 What sign or miracle does this promise provide? [* · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the sign lies in the extraordinary memory, the fulfilled advance promise, or both.)
  87:6/q15/p1 An unlettered Prophet retains an extensive revelation accurately without normal study, writing, or repetition. — reasons: Rāzī calls this beyond ordinary capacity. Ālūsī places it beside Qur’anic inimitability and reports of unseen events. Bursevī says he received knowledge without writing and became a source of knowledge about writing, rules and crafts. Kuran Yolu says the miracle continues in communal reading and memorization.
  87:6/q15/p2 A prediction made early in the Meccan period and later fulfilled is itself foreknowledge and a sign. — reasons: Rāzī stresses the early timing; Biqāʿī counts the unlettered reading and fulfilled prediction as two signs.
87:6/q16 How does this verse advance the sūra’s argument and sequence? [* · links] (turns on: How divine guidance, the opening command, and the preceding sūra prepare the pledge.)
  87:6/q16/p1 After general creation and guidance, God now gives the Messenger special guidance through revelation and his mission. — reasons: Biqāʿī says divine attributes and acts prepare for prophethood and an exemplary Messenger, while revelation revives spirits by knowledge as God revives bodies. Ibn ʿĀshūr links God’s proportioning and guidance with equipping the Prophet to retain revelation and receive an easy law. Iṣlāḥī sees the same gradual development in vegetation and prophecy.
  87:6/q16/p2 The promise follows the call to glorify because complete glorification involves reciting revelation and eases the Prophet’s fear. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the opening command implicit glad tidings now made explicit for full delivery.
  87:6/q16/p3 Worldly attachments obstruct the special glorification this promise serves. — reasons: Bursevī compares worldly pleasure, food and drink to quickly withered debris and says attachment veils a person’s possible perfection.
  87:6/q16/p4 God’s knowledge of hidden and manifest supports the pledge and grounds the exception. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God knows the Prophet’s concern and obedience; the shift to 'He knows' points to divine power.
  87:6/q16/p5 The sūra steadies the Prophet amid rejection with preservation, ease, a gracious law, and reminders. — reasons: Qutb names oneness, revelation and after
