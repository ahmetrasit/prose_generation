[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p244#2/r1] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | The passage concerns hypocrites who expect reward for praying in the Prophet's presence but do not fear punishment when they skip prayer, so they pray for show when he is there and leave it otherwise. | mentions 107:6
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p244#2/r2] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masud says they did not abandon prayer entirely—doing so would be unbelief—but failed to keep its appointed times.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p244#2/r3] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says they delay their prayers beyond their proper times.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p244#2/r4] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · أبو العالية | reports | Abu al-Aliya says the person is so heedless that he does not know how many units he has prayed, even or odd.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p244#2/r5] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · بعض العلماء | rejects | Some scholars reject this interpretation because the Prophet himself once forgot in prayer, the verse says 'about their prayer' rather than 'within it,' and such forgetfulness is beyond ordinary human ability.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p244#2/r6] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | The author thinks Abu al-Aliya meant habitual, not rare, forgetfulness: persistent inattention shows a heart that does not respect prayer, which is what the verse condemns.
[RAZI:107:4/r10] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | A possible reply says they are misleaders outwardly but have forgotten prayer inwardly, like the hypocrites described in 4:142 as lazy, ostentatious, and remembering God only a little. | mentions 4:142
[RAZI:107:4/r11] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Another reply distinguishes a hypocrite who forgets God throughout prayer from a believer who may become heedless in only some parts; this makes heedlessness in prayer a believer’s lapse and heedlessness about prayer a disbeliever’s trait.
[RAZI:107:4/r12] RAZI, d. 606 · سعد بن أبي وقاص ومسروق والحسن ومقاتل | reports | Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ, Masrūq, al-Ḥasan, and Muqātil say the heedless are those who fail to observe prayer times and conditions and do not care whether they pray.
[RAZI:107:4/r13] RAZI, d. 606 · كثير من العلماء | reports | Many scholars hold that the Prophet never lapsed in prayer; rather, God allowed him to perform the act of a forgetful person so that he could demonstrate the law, since practical demonstration is stronger.
[RAZI:107:4/r14] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | The Prophet’s and Companions’ prayer lapses are repaired either by prostration of forgetfulness or by sunnah and voluntary prayers.
[RAZI:107:4/r15] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | A second kind is inattentiveness during prayer, including failure to keep religious knowledge and intentions present.
[RAZI:107:4/r16] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | A third kind is leaving prayer without making it up and taking it outside its time; the hypocrite’s performative prayer is worse than simply abandoning prayer because it mocks religion.
[RAZI:107:4/r6] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | The threatened traits are heedlessness about prayer, ostentation, and withholding small assistance; all are sins, but do not ordinarily make someone a hypocrite, prompting exegetes to explain the severe threat.
[RAZI:107:4/r7] RAZI, d. 606 · author | prefers | The relied-upon answer restricts the warning to hypocrites who perform these acts; it implies disbelievers incur extra punishment for forbidden acts and neglected duties, supporting al-Shāfiʿī’s view that they are addressed by the branches of religious law.
[RAZI:107:4/r8] RAZI, d. 606 · عطاء عن ابن عباس | reports | ʿAṭāʾ reports Ibn ʿAbbās’s distinction: “in their prayer” could threaten believers, but “about their prayer” describes those who forget prayer and are empty of it.
[RAZI:107:4/r9] RAZI, d. 606 · author | rejects | Al-Rāzī calls that explanation weak: treating heedlessness about prayer as abandoning it conflicts with their being called worshippers, and abandonment alone is neither hypocrisy nor unbelief.
[RAZI:107:5/r1] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | The discussion follows orphan abuse and failure to urge feeding: mistreatment wrongs creatures, while prayer without reverence is worse because it neglects service to the Creator.
[RAZI:107:5/r10] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Rāzī answers that they count as worshipers outwardly but have forgotten prayer altogether inwardly, as hypocrites rise lazily, show off, and remember God only a little. | mentions 4:142
[RAZI:107:5/r11] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Complete failure to remember God in every part of prayer reflects a hypocrite’s belief that prayer is useless; a believer may instead become inattentive during some parts, so heedlessness “in” prayer differs from heedlessness “from” it.
