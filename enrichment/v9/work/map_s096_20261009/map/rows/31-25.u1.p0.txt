## THE CURRENT MAP
31:25/q01 Who is asked, and what do they concede about creation? [وَلَئِن سَأَلْتَهُم مَّنْ خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ لَيَقُولُنَّ ٱللَّهُ · interpretation]
  31:25/q01/p1 The unbelievers or polytheists, including the Arab idolaters and Quraysh, concede that God created the heavens and the earth. — reasons: Muqatil, Yahya, al-Tabari, al-Wahidi, al-Tabrisi, and Islahi state the answer directly. Al-Baydawi, Bursevi, al-Alusi, and the Study Quran stress that the creator is too evident to deny; al-Tabatabai sees a monotheistic disposition, while Asad calls the answer a vague habit. Ibn Kathir says their worshipped partners are God's own creation and property; Ibn Ashur includes the stone of the idols among what heaven and earth contain. Al-Razi and Abu Hayyan note that the confession coexists with worship or invocation of another deity.
31:25/q02 Why will they answer that God created them? [لَيَقُولُنَّ ٱللَّهُ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the admission comes from evident proof, a monotheistic disposition, or unexamined habit.)
  31:25/q02/p1 The evidence for God's creation is so obvious that even obstinate idolaters must admit it. — reasons: Al-Baydawi says the proof that no other can create is clear; Bursevi says the fact is too evident to deny; al-Alusi calls their admission compelled; the Study Quran says Quraysh still recognized a supreme creator and cites 10:31, 23:84–85, 29:61, 29:63, 39:38, 43:9, and 43:87.
  31:25/q02/p2 Their answer expresses an underlying disposition toward monotheism. — reasons: Al-Tabatabai says they acknowledge God without recognizing the implications.
  31:25/q02/p3 Their answer is a vague habitual acknowledgment of an ultimate cause. — reasons: Asad says they give it without seeing that it logically requires surrender to God alone.
31:25/q03 What do the heavens and earth encompass here? [ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ · interpretation]
  31:25/q03/p1 They encompass the whole created world, including the stones from which idols are made. — reasons: Ibn Ashur points to the comprehensive reach of the phrase and mentions the parallel in 29:61.
31:25/q04 What is understood in the brief answer ٱللَّهُ, and why is it brief? [لَيَقُولُنَّ ٱللَّهُ · grammar] (turns on: Which unspoken predicate the one-word reply supplies; the surah's purpose also explains its brevity.)
  31:25/q04/p1 The answer means 'God created them'; the verb is supplied by the preceding question. — reasons: Burhan explicitly says خلقهن is omitted because the question provides it. Al-Alusi prefers this reconstruction. Al-Biqaʿi says naming the subject alone suits this surah's purpose of wisdom, while al-Zukhruf 43:9 gives a fuller formulation.
  31:25/q04/p2 The answer may mean 'God is their creator.' — reasons: Al-Alusi mentions this as a possible reconstruction but favors 'God created them.'
31:25/q05 Who is told to say 'Praise be to God'? [قُلِ · referent]
  31:25/q05/p1 Muhammad, or any listener to the exchange, is addressed. — reasons: Al-Tabrisi gives both scopes for the command.
31:25/q06 Why is 'Praise be to God' commanded here? [ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ · interpretation] (turns on: What the command responds to: creation and benefaction, manifest proof, guidance, or the Prophet's vindication.)
  31:25/q06/p1 God alone merits praise and gratitude as creator and benefactor; the confession excludes praise or worship of powerless others. — reasons: Al-Tabari contrasts the creator with powerless idols; al-Wahidi praises Him for creation; al-Kashshaf, al-Nasafi, and al-Razi derive exclusive thanks and worship from creation; al-Alusi adds benefaction; Islahi rejects crediting beings with no role in creation or governance; the Study Quran transmits al-Tabari's explanation. Kuran Yolu says no other being may receive divinity, divine function, sanctity, or worship.
  31:25/q06/p2 Praise God for making the proof clear and extracting an admission that exposes the opponents' error. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya says the proof is clear; al-Tabrisi reports al-Jubbaʾi's thanks for a religion whose truth even an opponent must acknowledge; al-Baydawi says their forced admission refutes their belief; Abu Hayyan praises the manifest proof; Bursevi and al-Alusi stress unmistakable signs of oneness; al-Tabatabai stresses the unwitting admission.
  31:25/q06/p3 Praise God for guidance to His religion and the enabling grace of knowing Him. — reasons: Al-Tabrisi addresses Muhammad or any hearer and names guidance and grace; al-Qurtubi says God alone deserves praise for guiding people to His religion.
  31:25/q06/p4 Praise God for the public vindication of the Prophet's truth and his deniers' falsehood. — reasons: Al-Razi relates the command to the consolation and vindication indicated by the confession.
31:25/q07 What does بَلْ turn away from? [بَلْ · rhetoric]
  31:25/q07/p1 It turns from an omitted judgment that their claim is false. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya proposes an ellipsis like 'their claim is false'; Abu Hayyan says the elided clause invalidates their claim, since an informed person would affirm no deity beside God.
31:25/q08 Why does the verse say 'most' of them? [أَكْثَرُهُمْ · interpretation] (turns on: Who is excluded from 'most': existing monotheists, future converts, or people who know yet suppress the truth.)
  31:25/q08/p1 Some were genuine exceptions, such as early monotheists. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya names Zayd ibn ʿAmr, Quss, and Waraqa ibn Nawfal.
  31:25/q08/p2 Some of those addressed were destined to convert. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya offers this as another way to take 'most' literally.
  31:25/q08/p3 A few understand the implication but knowingly refuse it. — reasons: Al-Tabatabai compares their inward denial to Pharaoh's people in 27:14.
31:25/q09 What do most of them fail to know? [لَا يَعْلَمُونَ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether ignorance concerns monotheism's implications, failure to practice known truth, useful knowledge itself, exclusive creation and provision, or resurrection.)
  31:25/q09/p1 They fail to grasp the proof and the consequence of their confession: God alone deserves praise and worship, without partners. — reasons: Muqatil names God's oneness; al-Tabari says they misplace praise and gratitude; al-Wahidi says association after confession exposes ignorance; al-Baghawi, al-Kashshaf, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, al-Alusi, al-Tabatabai, Asad, and Islahi emphasize the unheeded entailment. Al-Tabrisi says they miss the proof; al-Qurtubi attributes this to failure to consider or reflect. Abu Hayyan connects it to their invalid claim; Ibn Kathir says the confession is proof they do not understand. Al-Alusi adds ignorance of why God is praised here.
  31:25/q09/p2 They know God controls matters, but fail to act on that knowledge by abandoning shirk. — reasons: Al-Biqaʿi says knowledge without practice counts as no knowledge and can be worse than ignorance; Bursevi says their failure to worship God alone belies their admission.
  31:25/q09/p3 They have no useful knowledge of God at all. — reasons: Al-Biqaʿi reasons that genuine knowledge of God would benefit them; al-Razi regards an unrestricted denial of knowledge as more emphatic.
  31:25/q09/p4 They do not affirm God's exclusive creation and provision. — reasons: Al-Biqaʿi calls this contradiction one that obliges acceptance of the Prophet's claim of absolute divine unity.
  31:25/q09/p5 They do not know that they will be resurrected. — reasons: Yahya ibn Sallam gives resurrection as the unrecognized matter.
31:25/q10 Does لَا يَعْلَمُونَ leave its object unstated? [لَا يَعْلَمُونَ · grammar] (turns on: Whether an object is supplied from 'Praise be to God' or the verb remains absolute.)
  31:25/q10/p1 Yes; it absolutely denies knowledge, without any specific object. — reasons: Al-Razi likens the construction to saying someone gives and withholds without naming an object; he prefers its emphatic unrestricted force.
  31:25/q10/p2 No; its understood object is that all praise belongs to God. — reasons: Al-Razi reports this second grammatical analysis but prefers the absolute construction.
31:25/q11 How does لَا يَعْلَمُونَ relate to the parallel لَا يَعْقِلُونَ? [لَا يَعْلَمُونَ · rhetoric]
  31:25/q11/p1 The two endings convey the same meaning with a stylistic variation. — reasons: Ibn Ashur compares this surah's wording with al-ʿAnkabut 29:63.
31:25/q12 What follows from acknowledging God as creator? [* · theology]
  31:25/q12/p1 Creation entails God's exclusive lordship, governance, praise, and worship; created idols cannot share His divinity. — reasons: Al-Kashshaf and al-Nasafi say the confession binds them to thank and worship God alone. Al-Razi argues that all creation needs Him; al-Qurtubi asks why they worship another; Ibn Kathir says the partners are God's property. Al-Biqaʿi says the idols are artifacts, not co-creators. Al-Tabatabai says governance cannot be separated from creation: God gives and withholds and alone is hoped for and feared. Asad says ultimate causation requires surrender. Islahi says others have no role in creating or governing. Kuran Yolu excludes divinity, divine function, sanctity, and worship for any other being.
31:25/q13 Can acknowledgment of God coexist with shirk, and how? [* · theology]
  31:25/q13/p1 Yes; people may profess God as creator while worshipping, invoking, or relying on idols and intermediaries. — reasons: Al-Damghani uses such profession as a sense of faith and compares the People of the Book's partial acceptance of scripture alongside rejection of other parts and messengers. Al-Qushayri says confessors return to error; al-Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, al-Razi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, and Kuran Yolu describe partners worshipped despite the confession. Ibn Kathir says those partners are God's own creation and property; Kuran Yolu says Arab idolaters sought refuge in intermediary gods.
