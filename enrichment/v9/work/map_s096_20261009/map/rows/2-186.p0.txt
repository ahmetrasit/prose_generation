[MUQATIL:2:186/r1] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | reports | Under the first fasting rule, food, drink, and sex became forbidden after the night prayer or after sleeping before praying it, just as during the day.
[MUQATIL:2:186/r2] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | reports | ʿUmar had sex with his wife after praying ʿishāʾ, regretted it, and confessed to Muhammad, asking whether he could have a dispensation.
[MUQATIL:2:186/r3] MUQATIL, d. 150 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet told ʿUmar he had not been justified in what he did; ʿUmar returned saddened.
[MUQATIL:2:186/r4] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | reports | Sarma ibn Anas worked in his garden, came home exhausted, fell asleep before eating, and woke to find food forbidden; he said fasting had worn him down.
[MUQATIL:2:186/r5] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | reports | After Muslim men confessed to similar conduct and asked how to repent, God revealed the passage, assuring them of His nearness and response to prayer.
[MUQATIL:2:186/r6] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | God is near in answering; “let them respond to Me” means obey, and “believe in Me” means affirm Him, so they may be guided.

[SIBAWAYH:إلاأنهاتعطفالاسمهناعلىمالايكونمابعدهإلارفعاعلىكلحال|#117/r10] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The omitted كنت or تكون is recoverable because those verbs commonly occur in these ‘with’ constructions and preserve their intended meaning.
[SIBAWAYH:إلاأنهاتعطفالاسمهناعلىمالايكونمابعدهإلارفعاعلىكلحال|#117/r11] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In present-state expressions such as أنت وشأنك, speakers do not use كان or يكون; they build the utterance directly on the nominal beginning.
[SIBAWAYH:إلاأنهاتعطفالاسمهناعلىمالايكونمابعدهإلارفعاعلىكلحال|#117/r12] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · أبو الخطاب | reports | Abū al-Khaṭṭāb reports that trusted Arabs recited أتوعدني بقومك with accusative, reflecting an understood verbal construction.
[SIBAWAYH:إلاأنهاتعطفالاسمهناعلىمالايكونمابعدهإلارفعاعلىكلحال|#117/r13] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الراعي | reports | Al-Rāʿī reportedly recited أزمان قومي والجماعة with accusative, construed with an elided كان because the event is past.
[SIBAWAYH:إلاأنهاتعطفالاسمهناعلىمالايكونمابعدهإلارفعاعلىكلحال|#117/r14] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Ṣirma al-Anṣārī’s verse illustrates carrying a phrase on a frequent underlying verbal construction, as with لست بمدرك.
[SIBAWAYH:إلاأنهاتعطفالاسمهناعلىمالايكونمابعدهإلارفعاعلىكلحال|#117/r15] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Al-Akhwaṣ’s line is carried on ليسوا بمصلحين, showing how an understood verbal phrase supplies the construction.
[SIBAWAYH:إلاأنهاتعطفالاسمهناعلىمالايكونمابعدهإلارفعاعلىكلحال|#117/r16] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | ʿĀmir ibn Juwayn al-Ṭāʾī’s line reflects an understood أن, which poets often use here under metrical pressure.
[SIBAWAYH:إلاأنهاتعطفالاسمهناعلىمالايكونمابعدهإلارفعاعلىكلحال|#117/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | ‘You and your business’ reports a present state and stays nominative; waw can also join two subjects under a shared predicate.
[SIBAWAYH:إلاأنهاتعطفالاسمهناعلىمالايكونمابعدهإلارفعاعلىكلحال|#117/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | أنت أعلم وعبد الله can mean ‘you are more knowledgeable with Abdullah’; alternatively, you and Abdullah are both subjects of ‘more knowledgeable.’
[SIBAWAYH:إلاأنهاتعطفالاسمهناعلىمالايكونمابعدهإلارفعاعلىكلحال|#117/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | ما أنت and كيف أنت act as nominal beginnings, so nouns joined after them with the sense ‘with’ remain nominative; this differs from a verb such as صنعت.
[SIBAWAYH:إلاأنهاتعطفالاسمهناعلىمالايكونمابعدهإلارفعاعلىكلحال|#117/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A line attributed to Ziyād al-Aʿjam (or another poet) shows waw meaning ‘with’ while the following noun is nominative.
[SIBAWAYH:إلاأنهاتعطفالاسمهناعلىمالايكونمابعدهإلارفعاعلىكلحال|#117/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The Arabs’ expression إنك ما وخيرا means إنك مع خير, confirming maʿa as the conjunction’s sense.
[SIBAWAYH:إلاأنهاتعطفالاسمهناعلىمالايكونمابعدهإلارفعاعلىكلحال|#117/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Abū ʿAntara’s line coordinates Jarwa with the speaker in the nominative, since إني functions as a nominal beginning rather than a verb.
[SIBAWAYH:إلاأنهاتعطفالاسمهناعلىمالايكونمابعدهإلارفعاعلىكلحال|#117/r8] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Accusative after ما أنت or كيف أنت is not a regular construction; it would require treating a nonverbal expression like a verb.
[SIBAWAYH:إلاأنهاتعطفالاسمهناعلىمالايكونمابعدهإلارفعاعلىكلحال|#117/r9] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | reports | Some Arabs rarely say كيف أنت وزيدا or ما أنت وزيدا; Sībawayh explains the accusative through an understood تكون or كنت.
