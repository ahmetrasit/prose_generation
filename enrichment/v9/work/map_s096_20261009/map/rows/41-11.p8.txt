[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:41:11/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The verb i’tiyā, “come,” is figurative for God’s creative command bringing the heavens and earth into being, so the authors render it “come to the scene of existence”; duhān marks this as a cosmic creation command.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:41:11/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Duhān literally means smoke; exegetes have interpreted it as water vapor (for example, Shawkani).
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:41:11/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | prefers | In light of scientific developments, Esed’s reading of duhān as hydrogen gas appears accurate according to current knowledge.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:41:11/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The previous two verses discuss earth’s creation and blessings, but verse 11 does not say earth was created before heaven; here thumma signals heaven’s greater importance rather than chronological sequence, so the authors translate it “moreover,” agreeing with Shawkani and Ibn Ashur.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:41:11/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Because earth and heavens were not created at the same time, the command to come is understood as God saying “be, come into existence” to each when He willed to create it.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:41:11/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | The statement that heaven and earth came obediently is also interpreted figuratively: God’s will and power apply absolutely to creation, and His command cannot fail to be fulfilled (Shawkani).
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p596/r5] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | طوعاً means willingly and spontaneously, whereas طائعاً means obedient to God's will.
