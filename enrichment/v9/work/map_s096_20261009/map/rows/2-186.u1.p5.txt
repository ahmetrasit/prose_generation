2:186/q07 How is 'I answer' parsed and related to the conditional? [أُجِيبُ إِذَا دَعَانِ · grammar] (turns on: The sources give different analyses, reports, or applications of this wording.)
  2:186/q07/p1 The conditional fāʾ implies an omitted 'say to them'. — reasons: JISHUMI, d. 494 (author): The question most likely concerns God's attribute, not His action; an implied 'say' makes the answer 'I am near,' and this rules out spatial location because God would not be near every petitioner if located in a place. TABRISI, d. 548 (author): The question most likely concerns God's attribute, not an action, since the answer says He is near; an implied say introduces the reply. ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 (author): The fāʾ in “I am near” answers the conditional; an omitted “say to them” is needed because the consequence is the report of nearness, not nearness itself. SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 (author): An implied command to speak follows the conditional fāʾ: say to them that God is near, since nearness is the condition's reported consequence.
  2:186/q07/p2 It may be a second predicate of 'inna'. — reasons: TABRISI, d. 548 (author): Idhā is a temporal adverb for the implied command Tell them, as indicated by fa-innī qarīb. It cannot be governed by qarīb or ujību because an inna predicate cannot govern outside its clause; ujību is a second predicate of inna. SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 (author): Alternatively, ajību is a second predicate of innī, following qarīb as the first predicate.
  2:186/q07/p3 The second 'when' is a temporal adverb governed by 'I answer'. — reasons: ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 (author): The governing word for idhā is ajību. SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 (الشيخ): The second idhā may be a temporal adverb governed by ajību, meaning that He answers at the time of calling.
  2:186/q07/p4 The verbal noun 'call' is generic, not limited to one act. — reasons: ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 (author): The wording apparently promises an answer to every caller: both daʿwah and al-dāʿī are generic, and the singular verbal noun does not mean one instance. SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 (author): The tāʾ in daʿwa marks the verbal noun's form, not a single occurrence; like raḥma and najda, it does not make the noun singular in event count.
  2:186/q07/p5 It may begin a new clause or be circumstantial. — reasons: WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 (author): Ajibu may be parsed as accusative circumstantial, meaning I am near while answering; its future form is marked nominative by its initial prefix.
  2:186/q07/p6 It may describe 'near' as an adjectival clause. — reasons: WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 (author): Alternatively, ajibu may start a new clause or attach to qarib as its adjective, meaning near and answering.
  2:186/q07/p7 The first 'when' depends on an implied command to say. — reasons: SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 (author): The first idhā is governed by the implied speech verb, fa-qul, in the reconstructed sentence.
  2:186/q07/p8 The second 'when' may be conditional, with an implicit or fronted answer. — reasons: SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 (الشيخ): The second idhā may instead be conditional, with its answer omitted as indicated by ajību, or ajību itself may be the answer under analyses allowing it before the condition.
