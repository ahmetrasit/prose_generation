[QASHANI:1:1/r7] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | reports | The first intellect is addressed in a report that God created no creature dearer or more honored, and acts of giving, taking, reward, and punishment occur through it.
[QASHANI:1:1/r8] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | The basmala has eighteen pronounced letters and nineteen written letters; when words are separated, the count becomes twenty-two.
[QASHANI:1:1/r9] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | Eighteen signifies eighteen thousand worlds, whose principal realms are the dominion, kingdom, Throne, Footstool, heavens, elements, and three genera of offspring.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r1] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · علي وابن عباس وعلي بن الحسين وقتادة وأبو العالية وعطاء وابن جبير ومحمد بن يحيى بن حبان وجعفر الصادق | reports | These authorities hold that al-Fātiḥa is Meccan; Abū Ḥayyān says the claim is supported by the seven oft-repeated verses being mentioned in a Meccan sūra. | mentions 15:87
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r10] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الكوفي | reports | The Kufan derivation makes the root of ism w-s-m, whereas Abū Ḥayyān prefers the Basran s-m-w analysis.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r11] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | Grammarians assign al- uses including particular or generic reference, presence, evocation of a quality, predominance, relative meaning, and fixed or nonfixed augmentation.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r12] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الأكثرون | reports | Most authorities consider Allāh a proper name for the true deity only, originally un-derived.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r13] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · ابن إسحاق وأبو عمر والنضر والمبرد | reports | Competing derivations connect Allāh to rising, concealment, fear, bewilderment, worship, or rest; Abū Ḥayyān reports these without choosing among them.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r14] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الخليل والقناد | reports | Al-Khalīl and al-Qannād derive Allāh from w-l-h, “to be transported with delight,” with hamza substituted for wāw; Abū Ḥayyān calls the proposal weak.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r15] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The plural āliha can be parsed as faʿāl with a passive meaning, like kitāb meaning “written.”
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r16] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | If Allāh derives from al-ilāh, al- marks predominance: ilāh can name true or false deities, while Allāh names only the true deity; the comparison to al-Najm is challenged.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r17] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | On the other proposed derivations, al- is an inseparable extra article; omitting it in lāh abūka is anomalous.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r18] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · أبو بكر بن العربي والسهيلي | rejects | They say al- is part of Allāh and the hamza became connective through frequent use; Abū Ḥayyān rejects this because the resulting form would be fʿāl and would not explain the lack of tanwīn.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r19] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · أبو يزيد البلخي | reports | Abū Yazīd al-Balkhī calls Allāh a foreign word, citing Jewish and Christian lāhā and saying Arabs adopted and changed it.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r2] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · أبو هريرة وعطاء بن يسار ومجاهد وسواد بن زياد والزهري وعبد الله بن عبيد بن عمير | reports | These authorities hold that al-Fātiḥa is Medinan, contrary to the Meccan attribution.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r20] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · قيل | reports | A reported view treats Allāh as an attribute rather than a personal name because God is not sensed or immediately apprehended and is known through His attributes; Abū Ḥayyān calls this unusual.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r21] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | The final alif in Allāh is variously said to be omitted to distinguish its spelling from lāhū, to lighten it, or because of a spelling convention.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r22] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Raḥmān is an intensive faʿlān form from mercy, with al- marking predominance; it is used only of God, like His proper name.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r23] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · ثعلب | reports | Thaʿlab reports the unusual claim that raḥmān is foreign, with its khāʾ adapted to Arabic ḥāʾ.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r24] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Raḥīm is an intensive faʿīl form; faʿīl is one of the five standard intensive patterns, with some grammarians adding another faʿīl pattern.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r25] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · العملس بن عقيل | reports | The poet al-ʿAmalas ibn ʿUqayl uses raḥīm to mean marḥūm, “one shown mercy.”
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r26] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الكوفيون | reports | The Kufans parse the bāʾ as instrumental and the phrase as accusative with an implied “I begin”; they posit that implied verb for acts begun with the formula.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r27] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī supplies a delayed verb such as “I read” or “I recite,” and claims fronting yields exclusivity; Abū Ḥayyān disputes the latter claim.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r28] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · سيبويه | reports | Sībawayh says fronting an object is good Arabic just as ordinary order is; Abū Ḥayyān cites this against the claim that fronting necessarily entails exclusivity.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r29] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · البصريون | reports | The Basrans parse ism as nominative in an implied nominal sentence, approximately “my beginning is fixed/stable in the name of God”; Abū Ḥayyān weighs this against the verbal parse.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r3] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · قيل | reports | A further view calls al-Fātiḥa both Meccan and Medinan.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r30] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Ism is a conventional word denoting an existent, perceptible or intelligible, without marking time; its referent is the named thing, while naming makes the word a sign for that meaning.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r31] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Abū Ḥayyān distinguishes the word, its referent, and the act of naming; a judgment attributed literally to a name can instead use “name” metaphorically for its referent.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r32] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · السهيلي | reports | Al-Suhaylī reads “glorify the name of your Lord” as an insertion directing people to glorify the Lord and ensuring remembrance includes spoken utterance as well as inward recollection. | mentions 87:1
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r33] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · السهيلي | reports | Al-Suhaylī explains “you worship only names” as a metaphor for idols with false, unreal names, so their worship is effectively worship of invented names. | mentions 12:40
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r34] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الكسائي والأخفش | reports | Al-Kisāʾī and al-Akhfash allow dropping the alif in bi-sm with other divine names; al-Farrāʾ restricts deletion to the full basmala.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r35] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الأعلم | reports | Al-Aʿlam treats raḥmān as a proper name and therefore appositional rather than adjectival, citing its independent use in other verses. | mentions 20:5, 55:1
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r36] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · السهيلي | rejects | Al-Suhaylī rejects apposition and explanatory apposition because Allāh needs no clarification; raḥmān is an epithet of praise, though it can function like a proper name.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r37] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · قيل | reports | One view says the two names are synonymous; another distinguishes them, making raḥmān more emphatic and encompassing major blessings while raḥīm completes it with subtle blessings.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r38] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī favors raḥmān for foundational blessings and raḥīm as a complement for finer, subtler blessings.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r39] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | prefers | Abū Ḥayyān holds that the names intensify mercy in different ways, so their combination is not mere repetition: faʿlān suggests fullness or dominance, while faʿīl suggests repeated occurrence at objects of mercy.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r4] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · أبي | reports | A report from Ubay identifies al-Fātiḥa as the seven oft-repeated verses and one of the seven long sūras, revealed some time after al-Ḥijr.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r40] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The intensive forms differ in transitivity: faʿlān does not take an object, whereas faʿīl does, like an active participle.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r41] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid distinguishes the names as mercy for this world and mercy for the next.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r42] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report through Ibn Masʿūd and Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī attributes to the Prophet the same distinction; Abū Ḥayyān says it must be accepted if sound.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r43] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · القرطبي | reports | Al-Qurṭubī gives the reverse allocation: raḥmān for the next life and raḥīm for this life.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r44] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الضحاك | reports | Al-Ḍaḥḥāk says the two names concern the inhabitants of heaven and earth.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r45] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · عكرمة | reports | ʿIkrima distinguishes one mercy from one hundred mercies.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r46] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · المزني | reports | Al-Muzanī distinguishes blessings of worldly life from blessings of religion.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r47] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · العزيزي | reports | Al-ʿAzīzī assigns raḥmān to universal gifts such as rain and the senses, and raḥīm to believers through guidance and divine care.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r48] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · المحاسبي | reports | Al-Muḥāsibī distinguishes mercy toward souls from mercy toward hearts.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r49] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · يحيى بن معاذ | reports | Yaḥyā ibn Muʿādh relates the names to needs of the afterlife and worldly life.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r5] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Prayer was prescribed in Mecca, and no prayer in Islam is known to have been performed without al-Fātiḥa.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:1/r50] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الصادق | reports | Al-Ṣādiq says the generally framed name concerns provision broadly, while the specially framed name concerns forgiveness for believers.
