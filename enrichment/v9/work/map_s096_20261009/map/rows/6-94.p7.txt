[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#5/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | reports | The Shaykh objects that a subject cannot be omitted; al-Samīn replies that Abū al-Baqāʾ means it is unexpressed while its substitute stands in its place.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#5/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya likewise supplies an omitted subject such as “connection” or “bond”; al-Samīn calls the analysis clear.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#5/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | reports | The Shaykh rejects Ibn ʿAṭiyya’s omitted-subject account on the grounds stated above.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#5/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A fourth analysis makes baynakum the subject, indeclinable because it is annexed to a nondeclinable word.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#5/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A poetic example keeps mithla fatḥa although it is attached to the nominative damm, illustrating this analysis.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#6/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A second anonymous line has ghayra in the accusative form even though it is the subject of yamnaʿ.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#6/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | reports | The Shaykh adds that “the severing severed” would also imply the opposite of the intended meaning, as if connection had resulted.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#6/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | Al-Samīn says the Shaykh’s objection does not refute al-Zamakhsharī because the latter explicitly bases the parse on the stated interpretive paraphrase.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#6/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · النابغة | reports | Al-Nābigha’s line has maqālata with fatḥa though it substitutes for a nominative clause, because it is annexed to indeclinable أن and its clause.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#6/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Both taqaṭṭaʿa and ḍalla seek “what you used to claim” as subject. Basrans prefer applying the later verb; Kufans prefer the earlier one.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#6/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · البصريون | reports | On the Basran preference to apply the later verb, ḍalla raises mā; taqaṭṭaʿa receives an implicit subject and governs baynakum as an adverb.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#6/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الكوفيون | reports | On the Kufan preference to apply the earlier verb, taqaṭṭaʿa raises mā and ḍalla has an implicit subject; baynakum remains an adverb governed by taqaṭṭaʿa.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#6/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الكوفيون | reports | A further analysis supplies a deleted relative noun, “what is between you”; Kufan grammarians allow this omission and cite Arabic poetry.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#6/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · عبد الله ومجاهد والأعمش | reports | The reading “لقد تقطع ما بينكم” reported from ʿAbd Allāh, Mujāhid, and al-Aʿmash supports supplying the deleted relative noun.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#6/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī paraphrases the clause as “the severing occurred between you,” construing the verb as predicated of its verbal noun by interpretation.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#6/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | reports | The Shaykh objects that this requires deleting the subject or using a verb and subject with the same sense, lacking distinct predicate and subject.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#7/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | On the second reading, بين is broadened from an adverb into a declinable noun and assigned the verb's action.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#7/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عطية | rejects | Ibn Atiyya objects that Arabs do not attest بين meaning connection; he suggests either inference from this verse or figurative distance and separation.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#7/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The author rejects Ibn Atiyya's objection: the named authorities are reliable, and Abu Amr's report alone would suffice to establish the usage.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#7/r12] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الفارسي | prefers | Al-Farisi explains the connection sense metaphorically: بين came to signify bonds such as partnership and kinship, making metaphor preferable to homonymy.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#7/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Qur'anic usage, Sibawayh, and poetry show بين used as an inflected noun, including in genitive constructions. | mentions 41:5, 18:78, 18:61, 5:106
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#7/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · عنترة | reports | Antarah's line uses بين in بين المنسمين as an inflected noun between camel foot joints.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#7/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · مهلهل | reports | Muhalhil's line offers another inflected use of بين in بعيدة بين جاليها.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#7/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Another poetic example parses بينها as the subject of مخالف; it is built because it is annexed to an indeclinable word.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#7/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Poetic examples use بينها and raised أمام and دون, illustrating inflected adverbial nouns.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#7/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو عمرو وابن جني والمهدوي والزهراوي | reports | They take بين as a noun meaning connection; on this account it can denote either connection or separation, like الجون.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#7/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو عمرو | reports | Abu Amr explains تقطع بينكم as your bond being severed; Abu Ubayd transmits his explanation.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#7/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزجاج | prefers | Al-Zajjaj says the raised reading is better and explains the phrase as your bond having been cut.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#8/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو علي | reports | Abu Ali argues that raised بين is the adverb broadened into a noun, not a verbal noun meaning separation, which would yield the unsuitable sense your separation was severed.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#8/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو عبيد | reports | Abu Ubayd reads بين in the nominative, citing Arabic uses of بين as a noun without ما, including Qur'anic examples and poetry. | mentions 18:61, 18:78
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#8/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الكسائي | reports | Al-Kisai reads بين accusatively, following the wording of Abdullah's reading لقد تقطع ما بينكم.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#8/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزجاج | prefers | Al-Zajjaj prefers the nominative but allows the accusative; both mean that the association between them was severed.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#8/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | A third explanation paraphrases the meaning as the group being scattered, but the author says this does not provide a grammatical parse.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#9/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | ما in ما كنتم may be a relative noun, an indefinite noun described by a clause, or a masdariyya particle.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#9/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | If ما is relative or indefinite, its antecedent and both objects of تزعمون are elided for concision, understood as you deem them partners or intercessors.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#9/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | If ما is masdariyya, there is no antecedent to recover, but the second object of تزعمون must still be supplied.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94#9/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The cited poem illustrates omission of the second object after تحسب: its sense is you deem their love a disgrace to me.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Furādā is accusative as a circumstantial phrase describing the subject of “you came to Us.”
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Related usage includes farada yaf rudu furūdan, fārid, afrad, and feminine fardāʾ; its plural is fard, like ḥumr from aḥmar/aḥmarāʾ.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · عيسى بن عمر وأبو حيوة | reports | ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar and Abū Ḥaywa read furādan, the declinable form used in Tamīm.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94/r12] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abū al-Baqāʾ records a rare anomalous reading with tanwīn, treating فراد as a sound noun, a rare plural like nuwwām and rijāl.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94/r13] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Another anomalous reading is فراد without tanwīn, indeclinable like aḥād and rubāʿ as a shifted adjective.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94/r14] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · خارجة عن نافع وأبي عمرو | reports | Khārija reports that Nāfiʿ and Abū ʿAmr read fardā, analogizing the group to a feminine collective as in “you see people drunk.” | mentions 22:2
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The past verb may express a future event assured to happen, as with “God’s command has come” and “the people of Paradise called.”
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Alternatively, the verb is past and depicts the scene from the perspective of the day they are told this, when their coming is already past.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ allows furādā to be a plural of several forms: fard, farīd, fard, and fardān.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن قتيبة | reports | Ibn Qutayba derives it as the plural of fardān, on the pattern of sukkārā from sukkārān and ʿujālā from ʿajlān.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:94/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الراغب | reports | Al-Rāghib reports the derivation as plural of farīd, like radīf/radāfā and asīr/asārā.

[SAMIN-DURR:6:94/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The expression means “one by one”; an anonymous poetic line uses furādā alongside “in pairs” as evidence for this sense.
