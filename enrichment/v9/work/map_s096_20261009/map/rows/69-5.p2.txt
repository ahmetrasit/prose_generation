<<69:5 notes, part 2 of 0..2>>
:69:1-5/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | The taghiya that destroyed Thamud is variously described in Qur'anic accounts as a shriek, earthquake, or thunderbolt.
[TABATABAI:69:6-12/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | reports | An alternative takes al-ṭāghiya as a verbal noun like ṭughyān or ṭughwā, meaning the Thamūd were destroyed because of their rebellion; the Sun passage supports this sense. | mentions 91:11
[TABATABAI:69:6-12/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | prefers | He prefers the first interpretation because the following verses enumerate modes of destruction; Thamūd’s al-ṭāghiya should therefore denote how they were destroyed. | mentions 11:67, 7:78, 41:17
[ASAD-NOTES:69:1#n4/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Asad | holds | Asad directs readers to compare the destruction of Thamud with 7:78. | mentions 7:78
[ASAD-NOTES:69:1/r2] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Asad | holds | The translation says Thamud and Ad were overtaken by calamity: Thamud by upheaval, Ad by a storm lasting seven nights and eight days, leaving bodies like hollow palm trunks.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p449/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Al-ṭāghiyah means something exceeding its bounds; God turns life-serving forces into destroyers when people exceed limits, as Thamūd did in rebellion. | mentions 91:11, 69:11
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p449/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The Qur’an calls Noah’s floodwater ṭaghā al-māʾ and says Thamūd denied because of rebellion. | mentions 69:11, 91:11
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p449/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Although the sūrah leaves Thamūd’s calamity unnamed, Qur’anic indications discussed in al-Dhāriyāt suggest a thunderbolt from striped winter clouds.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p449/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | An ordinary lightning bolt can become a weapon of destruction by God’s will, and modern scientific advances do not remove humanity’s helplessness before Him.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:69:4/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Thamud denied the afterlife and disobeyed its prophet, so a very severe earthquake destroyed them; “ṭāghiya” describes its overwhelming severity. | mentions 7:73, 7:79, 11:61, 11:68
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:69:4/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Their fates serve the Muslim community as lessons and as evidence that the power which brought these disasters can also bring about the Resurrection. | mentions 7:65, 7:72, 11:50, 11:60
[STUDYQURAN:69:5/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | Ṭāghiyah denotes utmost severity and may indicate Thamūd’s calamity: a cry, earthquake, or thunderbolt. | mentions 11:67, 54:31, 7:78, 41:13, 51:44
[STUDYQURAN:69:5/r2] STUDYQURAN · R | reports | Ṭāghiyah can also mean rebellion or upheaval, from ṭughyān.
[STUDYQURAN:69:5/r3] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | A calamity describes how Thamūd were destroyed, while rebellion describes their sinfulness; the two explanations can both apply.
[STUDYQURAN:69:5/r4] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | Ṭāghiyah may mean those who overwhelmed or oppressed Thamūd: a powerful minority or individual persuaded them to hamstring God’s she-camel and leave her for dead. | mentions 91:13, 54:23, 54:24, 54:25, 54:26, 54:27, 54:28, 54:29, 54:30
