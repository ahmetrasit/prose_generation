20:43/q10 What does sending prophets to enemies teach? [* · theology] (turns on: The cited explanations emphasize different beneficiaries and lessons of a mission among enemies; Ibn ʿAṭāʾ distinguishes its outward destination from its inward purpose.)
  20:43/q10/p1 It exposes the enemy's inability to guide anyone and the emptiness of claims without divine proof. — reasons: Al-ʿArāʾis, quoted by Bursevī, compares the enemy to a doctor unable to treat others or himself; it also says the confrontation cuts off Pharaoh's argument and warns every claimant lacking proof from God.
  20:43/q10/p2 It teaches prophets that election is not produced by worldly causes and calls for gratitude. — reasons: Al-ʿArāʾis gives this lesson in Bursevī's report and adds that the mission may draw prepared believers from among unbelievers.
  20:43/q10/p3 God sends prophets to rescue His believing friends amid enemies. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭāʾ, cited by al-Sulamī and Bursevī, points especially to the magicians; al-Sulamī's version says enemies themselves lack standing with God to warrant a prophetic mission.
20:43/q11 How should Pharaoh's transgression affect the brothers' manner of preaching? [إِنَّهُۥ طَغَىٰ · interpretation]
  20:43/q11/p1 Even a great transgressor should be addressed gently, without harshness or violence. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls gentle speech an essential etiquette of inviting people to faith. Kur'an Yolu says the contrast between Pharaoh's great transgression and the command to speak gently shows the importance of method and tone for successful preaching.
20:43/q12 What inward figures do Moses, Aaron, and Pharaoh represent? [* · inward]
  20:43/q12/p1 Moses is the heart, Aaron the intellect, and Pharaoh the tyrannical commanding soul. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī says heart and intellect confront the soul with God's proofs and remembrance, while God encourages and protects them in that struggle.
20:43/q13 What report describes Moses' arrival at Pharaoh's door? [إِلَىٰ فِرْعَوْنَ · reports]
  20:43/q13/p1 Moses waited outside Pharaoh's door for a year without finding a messenger. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī labels this an Israelite report and says it consoles later believers who face oppressors.
20:43/q14 What illustrations of departure accompany the command to go? [ٱذْهَبَآ · reports] (turns on: The two illustrations are an account of prompt obedience to a dream command and a poetic longing to depart.)
  20:43/q14/p1 A spiritual master obeyed a dream command to migrate from Filibe to Constantinople. — reasons: Bursevī says his master dreamed at midmorning, then woke, prayed, washed, departed on foot, and left his family behind.
  20:43/q14/p2 A poetic speaker longs to leave his present station and hear news from the beloved's street. — reasons: Bursevī quotes a couplet of Ḥāfiẓ in which companions will ask for such news on the day of departure.
20:43/q15 Which other passages does the command to Moses and Aaron recall? [* · links]
  20:43/q15/p1 It echoes Moses' commission in 20:24 and other accounts of his mission. — reasons: The Study Quran links the dual command to 20:24, 79:17, 26:10–11, 27:12, and 28:32.
