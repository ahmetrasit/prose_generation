06 · سعد بن أبي وقاص ومسروق والحسن ومقاتل | reports | Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ, Masrūq, al-Ḥasan, and Muqātil explain the phrase as failing to guard prayer times and conditions and not caring whether one prays.
[RAZI:107:5/r13] RAZI, d. 606 · كثير من العلماء | reports | Many scholars say the Prophet did not truly lapse in prayer; God enabled him to perform an act resembling a lapse so that he could demonstrate the law, whose practical demonstration is stronger.
[RAZI:107:5/r14] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | One category is lapses by the Prophet and Companions, repaired either by the prostration of forgetfulness or by sunnah and voluntary prayers.
[RAZI:107:5/r15] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | A second category is distraction within prayer, including failure to summon one’s knowledge and intentions.
[RAZI:107:5/r16] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | A third category is leaving prayer without making it up and letting its time pass; a hypocrite’s token prayer is worse than leaving prayer because it mocks religion.
[RAZI:107:5/r2] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | The verse answers the objection that prayer deters indecency: these worshipers’ prayer itself is made up of ostentation and heedlessness.
[RAZI:107:5/r3] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Neglecting compassion toward creation and reverence for God completes their wretchedness; “woe” marks a grave crime, as in three other warnings. | mentions 83:1, 2:79, 104:1
[RAZI:107:5/r4] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | A report says each person in Hell wails according to his sin, including love of status, pre-Islamic zeal, or prayer; one should say, “Woe to me if God does not forgive me.”
[RAZI:107:5/r5] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | The severe threat concerns three acts—heedlessness about prayer, showing off, and withholding al-maʿūn—raising the question why sins that do not by themselves make one an unbeliever draw such punishment.
[RAZI:107:5/r6] RAZI, d. 606 · author | prefers | The relied-upon answer reads “those who pray” as hypocrites who commit these acts, so the threat is directed at them.
[RAZI:107:5/r7] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | On this reading, unbelievers receive additional punishment for violating prohibitions and neglecting obligations, supporting al-Shāfiʿī’s view that they are addressed by the law’s subsidiary commands.
[RAZI:107:5/r8] RAZI, d. 606 · ابن عباس | reports | ʿAṭāʾ transmits Ibn ʿAbbās’s distinction: “heedless in their prayer” would threaten believers, whereas “heedless from their prayer” describes people who do not remember it and are detached from it.
[RAZI:107:5/r9] RAZI, d. 606 · author | rejects | Rāzī calls Ibn ʿAbbās’s explanation weak: heedlessness from prayer cannot mean simply abandoning it, since the verse calls them worshipers, and such abandonment alone is neither hypocrisy nor unbelief.
[RAZI:107:6/r8] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | The warning about heedlessness covers missing prayer time and inattentiveness within prayer; this verse adds ostentation, so prayer should be free of all three faults.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p106#2/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | One link is that mistreating the orphan and withholding help show hypocrisy, while prayer without humility is an even clearer sign because it concerns service to the Creator.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p106#2/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | Another link answers the objection that prayer restrains indecency: this prayer cannot restrain evil when it consists of ostentation and inattention.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p106#2/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | A third link says orphan abuse neglects compassion for God’s creatures, and heedlessness in prayer neglects reverence for God; failing in both completes wretchedness.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p107#2/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | Many scholars say the Prophet did not forget in prayer; God instead allowed him to perform a forgetful person’s action so the law would be demonstrated more clearly by practice.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p107#2/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | If prophetic or Companion forgetfulness occurred, it was repaired by prostration of forgetfulness, sunnah, or voluntary prayer; inner distraction is another category, while leaving prayer past its time without making it up is hypocritical mockery and worse than simply leaving it.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p107#3/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | A hypocrite displays faith while concealing unbelief; a show-off displays extra humility absent from the heart to seem devout, or differs from a hypocrite because his public prayer is better than his private prayer.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p107#3/r7] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Heedlessness from prayer indicates both praying outside its time and being distracted within it; with ostentation added, prayer must be free from all three conditions.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p107/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The threat covers three sins: heedlessness about prayer, ostentation, and withholding small aid; since these do not alone make a Muslim hypocrite, commentators explain why the warning is so severe.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p107/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | prefers | The preferred answer limits the description to hypocritical worshippers: their extra punishment for forbidden acts and omitted duties supports al-Shafi‘i’s view that unbelievers are addressed by the subsidiary laws.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p107/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · عطاء عن ابن عباس | reports | If the verse said inattentive in their prayer, believers would be threatened; inattentent from their prayer means someone who forgets prayer and is wholly disengaged from it.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p107/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | rejects | Razi rejects interpreting heedlessness from prayer simply as abandoning it: the text still calls them worshippers, and abandonment alone is neither hypocrisy nor unbelief.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p107/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | A reply is that they count as Muslims outwardly but forget prayer in reality, as hypocrites do; total forgetfulness of God’s remembrance throughout prayer marks a hypocrite, while a believer may be inattentive during some parts. | mentions 4:142
[RAZI-FULL:v32p107/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · سعد بن أبي وقاص ومسروق والحسن ومقاتل | reports | They interpret heedlessness as failure to maintain prayer times and conditions, not caring whether one prays.
[QURTUBI:107:5/r1] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas identifies the prayer-neglecter as one who expects no reward for praying and fears no punishment for abandoning prayer.
[QURTUBI:107:5/r10] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says these are hypocrites who leave prayer in private but perform it in public, as in 4:142. | mentions 4:142
[QURTUBI:107:5/r11] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Qurtubi takes the following description of people who show off as evidence that the prayer-neglecters are hypocrites.
[QURTUBI:107:5/r12] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says that had the verse said ‘heedless in their prayer,’ it would have described believers.
[QURTUBI:107:5/r13] QURTUBI, d. 671 · عطاء | reports | Ata praises the verse for saying ‘about their prayer’ rather than ‘in their prayer.’
[QURTUBI:107:5/r14] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari distinguishes neglecting prayer through abandonment and indifference, characteristic of hypocrites or brazen sinners, from incidental distraction during prayer.
[QURTUBI:107:5/r15] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari notes that distraction during prayer is nearly unavoidable even for Muslims, including the Prophet, so jurists discuss the prostration of forgetfulness.
[QURTUBI:107:5/r16] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن العربي | reports | Ibn al-Arabi says avoiding forgetfulness is impossible; the Prophet and Companions forgot, while those who never forget fail to reflect on prayer and attend only to its count; prophetic lapses came from greater thoughts or satanic distraction.
[QURTUBI:107:5/r2] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas also says they delay their prayers beyond their appointed times.
[QURTUBI:107:5/r3] QURTUBI, d. 671 · إبراهيم | reports | Ibrahim, in a report from al-Mughira, explains the lapse as wasting prayer time.
[QURTUBI:107:5/r4] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبو العالية | reports | Abu al-Aliya says they fail to pray on time and do not complete bowing or prostration.
[QURTUBI:107:5/r5] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Qurtubi says the warning that later people abandoned prayer in Maryam supports the interpretation of neglecting its times. | mentions 19:59
[QURTUBI:107:5/r6] QURTUBI, d. 671 · إبراهيم | reports | Another report from Ibrahim describes someone who raises his head from prostration while turning his gaze aside.
[QURTUBI:107:5/r7] QURTUBI, d. 671 · قطرب | reports | Qutrub interprets the lapse as failing to recite or remember God.
[QURTUBI:107:5/r8] QURTUBI, d. 671 · عبد الله | reports | Abdullah's reading has lāhūn, ‘heedless,’ in place of sāhūn.
[QURTUBI:107:5/r9] QURTUBI, d. 671 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet explains the verse as people who negligently postpone prayer until after its proper time; Saʿd ibn Abi Waqqas transmits this report.
