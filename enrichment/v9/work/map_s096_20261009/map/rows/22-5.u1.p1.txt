22:5/q15 How is لنبين read? [لِّنُبَيِّنَ لَكُمْ · readings] (turns on: First-person nūn or third-person yāʾ.)
  22:5/q15/p1 The common reading uses nūn: 'so that We make clear.' — reasons: Rāzī and later grammarians contrast this with third-person variants.
  22:5/q15/p2 Ibn Abī ʿAblah and others read with yāʾ: 'so that He make clear.' — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī also names Abū ʿImrān al-Jawnī; Ālūsī calls it a shift in grammatical person.
22:5/q16 How is ونقر read and connected syntactically? [وَنُقِرُّ · readings] (turns on: Indicative independent clause or accusative purpose; variant yāʾ and pouring-root forms.)
  22:5/q16/p1 The majority read indicative nuqirru, a new statement about retaining embryos. — reasons: Sībawayh, Farrāʾ, Zajjāj and others separate it from the purpose clause; Zajjāj argues humans were created for guidance, not simply retention; Kashf prefers coordination with 'We created.'
  22:5/q16/p2 An accusative/subjunctive reading coordinates retention, sometimes delivery, with 'to make clear.' — reasons: Transmitted from ʿĀṣim or Yaʿqūb; Zamakhsharī takes retention through birth and accountability as a second purpose; Abū al-Baqāʾ proposes an outcome lām, challenged by al-Samīn.
  22:5/q16/p3 Third-person variants use يقر or يُقر, with indicative or accusative endings. — reasons: Ibn Masʿūd, Abū Rajāʾ, Abū al-Jawzāʾ and Abū Isḥāq al-Sabīʿī are named; some yāʾ variants also govern يخرجكم.
  22:5/q16/p4 Yaʿqūb's نَقَرُّ derives from pouring water. — reasons: Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, al-Samīn, and Ālūsī distinguish the open nūn and ḍamma on qāf from the usual retention verb.
22:5/q17 What is kept in the womb, and until when? [وَنُقِرُّ فِى ٱلْأَرْحَامِ مَا نَشَآءُ إِلَىٰٓ أَجَلٍۢ مُّسَمًّۭى · interpretation] (turns on: Whether retention means avoiding miscarriage, completing formation, or fulfilling an assigned lifespan.)
  22:5/q17/p1 God retains selected pregnancies to completed birth rather than allowing miscarriage. — reasons: Mujāhid glosses completion; Ibn Zayd says stay until emergence; several sources give six months as a minimum and differing maximums. Islāḥī stresses God's choice of sex and form.
  22:5/q17/p2 Retention lasts until the embryo completes formation. — reasons: Ṭabrisī reports Mujāhid's gloss; some accounts tie the term to individual fetal development.
  22:5/q17/p3 The phrase includes whoever is assigned a specified lifespan. — reasons: Ṭabrisī offers this alternative; Ṭabarī mentions God's decree of life to a fixed term.
22:5/q18 What is the appointed term, and what gestation lengths are reported? [أَجَلٍۢ مُّسَمًّۭى · interpretation] (turns on: The term is birth, but reports differ on maximum gestation.)
  22:5/q18/p1 The term is delivery; gestation varies, with six months often given as the minimum. — reasons: Wāḥidī and Bayḍāwī allow up to four years; Bursevī cites Abū Ḥanīfa's two, Shāfiʿī's four, and Mālik's five years, with anecdotes of prolonged pregnancies. Ibn ʿĀshūr says usual gestation is nine months nine days.
22:5/q19 How are ما نشاء and its pronoun read or chosen? [مَا نَشَآءُ · readings]
  22:5/q19/p1 The ordinary form uses nūn with fatḥa. — reasons: The wording mā covers pregnancy including the inanimate morsel, as Qurṭubī notes; Biqāʿī and Islāḥī stress God's choice of which fetus continues.
  22:5/q19/p2 Ibn Waththāb reads the nūn with kasra. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and Ālūsī transmit this vocalization.
22:5/q20 Why is طفل singular, and what does it mean? [طِفْلًۭا · grammar] (turns on: Generic or distributive singular, verbal noun, or singular licensed by the plural suffix.)
  22:5/q20/p1 A generic or distributive singular stands for children or each child. — reasons: Abū ʿUbayda cites poetry and 66:4; al-Samīn and Rāzī allow each of you; Samarrāʾī says the verse treats the human species; the noun generally covers birth to puberty.
  22:5/q20/p2 The singular functions as a verbal noun, applicable to one or many. — reasons: Ibn Jurayj compares ʿadl and zawr; al-Mubarrad cites 24:31; Wāḥidī discusses verbal-noun and specification parses; Ṭabarī is reported with specification.
  22:5/q20/p3 The plural object suffix already indicates a group, making a plural predicate unnecessary. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī reports this distinct grammatical account; al-Suyūṭī transmits an elided distributive repetition.
22:5/q21 What are the lexical limits and related uses of طفل? [طِفْلًۭا · meaning]
  22:5/q21/p1 A tender young child, usually from birth to puberty; related roots have several senses. — reasons: Abū al-Haytham and Qurṭubī discuss gender and number; Qurṭubī and al-Samīn add delicate, afternoon, rain, and animal-young uses, with poetry.
22:5/q22 How does ثم لتبلغوا connect to the preceding clause? [ثُمَّ لِتَبْلُغُوٓا۟ أَشُدَّكُمْ · grammar] (turns on: A purpose of delivery, an omitted verb, an added thumma, or a purpose tied to womb retention.)
  22:5/q22/p1 Reaching maturity is the purpose of bringing children out and raising them. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr defends the causal lām and rejects alternatives; Kur'an Yolu prefers causality; Ālūsī says the repeated lām emphasizes responsibility.
  22:5/q22/p2 An omitted verb supplies 'then We let you live or grow' before the purpose clause. — reasons: The author of al-Naẓm, Ālūsī and Islāḥī supply the intervening development.
  22:5/q22/p3 Thumma is additional or functions like a simple conjunction. — reasons: Al-Naẓm and Qurṭubī record this, comparing 39:73; Ibn ʿĀshūr instead treats thumma as marking rank or reinforcing the earlier sequence.
  22:5/q22/p4 Maturity may be the aim of womb retention and delivery. — reasons: Al-Ṭībī allows this; Ālūsī rejects coordination directly with li-nubayyina as spoiling the structure.
22:5/q23 What is reaching full strength, and when does it occur? [أَشُدَّكُمْ · meaning] (turns on: Puberty, broader complete strength, and reported numerical ages.)
  22:5/q23/p1 It is complete physical strength, intellect, and discernment. — reasons: Zajjāj sets it between thirty and forty; other sources mention eighteen, thirty-two, thirty-six, forty, forty-five. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it youth with gathered capacities.
  22:5/q23/p2 It is the onset of puberty or sexual maturity. — reasons: Yaḥyā gives this gloss, and Ṭabrisī offers it as an alternative; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the verse may refer to orphan puberty.
22:5/q24 What is the grammatical form of أشد? [أَشُدَّكُمْ · grammar] (turns on: A plural with no used singular or a plural of shiddah.)
  22:5/q24/p1 It is a plural-like form with no ordinary singular. — reasons: Zamakhsharī suggests multiple aspects of strength; Nasafī repeats the form; Ālūsī surveys proposals.
  22:5/q24/p2 It is analyzed as a plural of shiddah. — reasons: Bayḍāwī compares anʿām from niʿmah; Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī report the analysis.
22:5/q25 When do some people die relative to maturity and old age? [وَمِنكُم مَّن يُتَوَفَّىٰ · interpretation] (turns on: Before maturity, at or after maturity, or simply before senility.)
  22:5/q25/p1 Some die before reaching maturity. — reasons: Yaḥyā compares 40:67; Ibn ʿAbbās and many later commentators supply this implied timing; Islāḥī includes childhood and adolescence.
  22:5/q25/p2 Some die before reaching decrepit old age, including at or after maturity. — reasons: Māwardī, Baghawī and Ṭabāṭabāʾ infer contrast with the return to old age; Rāzī specifies death at full strength; Ālūsī objects to a circumstantial parse that forces death exactly at maturity.
22:5/q26 How is يتوفى read or construed? [يُتَوَفَّىٰ · readings] (turns on: Passive taking of life versus active completion or divine taking.)
  22:5/q26/p1 The usual passive form means a person is taken at death. — reasons: Bursevī identifies death and taking of the soul; Ālūsī says the passive leaves the understood divine agent unspoken.
  22:5/q26/p2 An active variant takes God as subject and the person as object. — reasons: Abū Ḥātim transmits it; al-Samīn and Ālūsī give this parse.
  22:5/q26/p3 An active variant takes the person as subject, meaning fulfillment of a lifespan. — reasons: Naḥḥās reports Abū Isḥāq; al-Samīn compares 2:234; Ālūsī records the same.
22:5/q27 What is the most decrepit age? [أَرْذَلِ ٱلْعُمُرِ · meaning] (turns on: Whether it names senility generally or a fixed age.)
  22:5/q27/p1 Senile old age with bodily weakness and reduced intellect; no fixed onset. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya rejects a rigid 75-year threshold because many remain sound at eighty; Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī likewise note variation. Ṭabrisī says decline is worse than childhood weakness because childhood may improve.
  22:5/q27/p2 It begins at a reported numerical age, seventy-five or one hundred. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya questions the report of seventy-five from ʿAlī; a report attributed to al-Ṣādiq says one hundred.
