22:40/q37 What protection do non-Muslim worship places receive? [صَوَٰمِعُ وَبِيَعٌۭ وَصَلَوَٰتٌۭ · law] (turns on: Whether the verse itself protects existing treaty sanctuaries including fire temples, and what conditions apply.)
  22:40/q37/p1 Existing treaty communities' churches and synagogues may not be destroyed; their covenant protects them. — reasons: Ibn Khuyz Mindad compares worship sites to protected homes and property; Study Quran extends treaty protection within and beyond Muslim lands.
  22:40/q37/p2 The verse itself does not protect fire temples, though the dhimma covenant does. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says God is not remembered there in the relevant sense.
  22:40/q37/p3 Protected communities may not add new sanctuaries or expand old ones; unauthorized additions may be removed. — reasons: Ibn Khuyz Mindad distinguishes protected existing buildings, new additions, and temples in enemy territory; he also discourages Muslims entering or praying there.
  22:40/q37/p4 A mosque may be demolished in order to rebuild it. — reasons: Ibn Khuyz Mindad cites Uthman's rebuilding of the Prophet's mosque.
22:40/q38 Does 'in which God's name is often mentioned' describe all the sites or mosques alone? [يُذْكَرُ فِيهَا ٱسْمُ ٱللَّهِ كَثِيرًۭا · referent] (turns on: The nearest-noun rule favors mosques; coordinated-description syntax and historical remembrance support all four.)
  22:40/q38/p1 It describes all four worship-place kinds. — reasons: Ibn Ashur argues a post-coordination adjective normally returns to all; Dahhak, Muqatil and Kalbi say all remember God. Samin finds this more apparent; Kuran Yolu rejects inferring other faiths had abandoned remembrance. Ibn Ashur links the sites' remembrance to why polytheists attack them.
  22:40/q38/p2 It describes the mosques alone. — reasons: Nahhas cites the nearest antecedent; Razi prefers this as mosque honor, and Abu Sulayman says the other sites were mostly associated with shirk. Elmalili reads it as highlighting Islam's worship aim.
  22:40/q38/p3 The phrase applies to all before abrogation, and remembrance may continue afterward. — reasons: Alusi rejects the claim that supersession stops God's name being remembered there.
22:40/q39 What does abundant remembrance mean? [كَثِيرًۭا · meaning] (turns on: Frequency of repetition or continuity through time.)
  22:40/q39/p1 It means repeated or continuously sustained mention of God. — reasons: Ibn Ashur favors remembrance across all times; Kuran Yolu calls it the central aim of worship in all revealed religions.
  22:40/q39/p2 It marks Islam's primary worship aim and may contrast other institutions' later practice. — reasons: Elmalili draws the contrast; Kuran Yolu reports and disputes the implied abandonment.
22:40/q40 Why say God's name explicitly in the remembrance clause? [ٱسْمُ ٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
  22:40/q40/p1 The explicit name may mark different ways and degrees of sincerity with which communities invoke Him. — reasons: Biqa'i contrasts their differing invocations.
22:40/q41 What does it mean to help God? [مَن يَنصُرُهُۥٓ · interpretation] (turns on: Helping God's religion, Prophet, allies, or monotheistic cause, with jihad or all obedience in view.)
  22:40/q41/p1 Support His religion, law, Prophet, allies, and truth. — reasons: Razi says God cannot literally need aid; the promise embraces battle, proof, knowledge and obedience. Ibn Atiyya extends it to all who aid truth until Resurrection.
  22:40/q41/p2 Accept and conduct jihad in defense of His religion. — reasons: Razi reports this narrower view; Islahi says actual effort to protect divine symbols conditions promised help.
  22:40/q41/p3 Establish one of His revealed laws. — reasons: Zajjaj says each law permits only what its prophet brought and forbids what that prophet forbade.
  22:40/q41/p4 Help God's Prophet specifically, Muhammad. — reasons: Ibn Abbas names Muhammad; Muqatil says the promise was fulfilled in him and his community.
  22:40/q41/p5 Affirm God's oneness. — reasons: Muqatil links 57:25 and identifies help with tawhid.
22:40/q42 What does naṣr mean? [يَنصُرُهُۥٓ · meaning] (turns on: Help versus revenge, rain, or coming to a place in broader Arabic usage.)
  22:40/q42/p1 Here it means giving help to God's cause. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzi cites 59:12 and 47:7.
  22:40/q42/p2 In other uses naṣr may mean rain or coming to a place, while intiṣār can mean revenge. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzi quotes a poem using unṣurī for visiting a land.
22:40/q43 How does God fulfill the promised help? [وَلَيَنصُرَنَّ ٱللَّهُ مَن يَنصُرُهُۥٓ · theology] (turns on: Worldly victory, argument and knowledge, later historical fulfillment, or help in the Hereafter.)
  22:40/q43/p1 He grants victory over enemies, including the early Muslim conquests. — reasons: Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi cite Arab chiefs, Persian kings and Byzantine emperors; Biqa'i includes ordinary causes and miracles; Ibn Kathir cites 37:171–173 and 58:21. The later praise of emigrants is said to anticipate their conduct if empowered.
  22:40/q43/p2 He supports believers in argument, knowledge, obedience, and steadiness. — reasons: Razi names proof and learning; Ibn Kathir links 47:7–8.
  22:40/q43/p3 God needs no aid; human striving lets believers receive benefits and His promised support. — reasons: Bahr al-Ulum says God could destroy enemies unaided; Elmalili links the servant's will to religious acts; Islahi says effort opens the way to help.
  22:40/q43/p4 His aid is victory here and a proof in the Hereafter. — reasons: Yahya ibn Sallam makes the twofold distinction.
  22:40/q43/p5 He alternates hardship and victory as tests without negating the promise. — reasons: Bursevi says forced faith would cancel moral responsibility; trials expiate believers' sins, and a dream report depicts reward for an oppressed victim.
22:40/q44 Why is the promise so emphatic? [وَلَيَنصُرَنَّ ٱللَّهُ · rhetoric]
  22:40/q44/p1 The oath lām and emphatic nūn guarantee divine support and urge endurance in battle. — reasons: Kashshaf compares rulers' authoritative promises and 22:38; Ibn Ashur notes both markers. Burhan notes the added lam occurs twice in al-Hajj.
22:40/q45 What does the explicit name of God in the aid promise accomplish? [وَلَيَنصُرَنَّ ٱللَّهُ مَن يَنصُرُهُۥٓ · rhetoric]
  22:40/q45/p1 It universalizes the rule to anyone who supports God and ties aid to that quality. — reasons: Biqa'i contrasts the name with an available pronoun.
22:40/q46 What do 'strong' and 'mighty' affirm of God? [لَقَوِىٌّ عَزِيزٌ · theology] (turns on: Distinct glosses of might emphasize invincibility, exaltation, or retribution.)
  22:40/q46/p1 He has unrestricted power to aid believers and cannot be overcome or resisted. — reasons: Tabari contrasts idols' inability to create or protect a fly (22:73); Ibn Kathir invokes creation and decree; Ibn Ashur distinguishes strength as power and might as invulnerability.
  22:40/q46/p2 Mighty can stress vengeance against enemies. — reasons: Muqatil's gloss and Yahya ibn Sallam's account emphasize retribution.
  22:40/q46/p3 Mighty can mean exalted and noble. — reasons: Qurtubi transmits Zajjaj's gloss.
22:40/q47 Why does strength come before might? [لَقَوِىٌّ عَزِيزٌ · rhetoric]
  22:40/q47/p1 Strength causes victory and thus leads to might. — reasons: Samarrai's discussions phrase this as power enabling conquest and domination.
22:40/q48 How can a following noun be raised after inna? [إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ · grammar] (turns on: An independent subject versus coordination with a hidden predicate pronoun.)
  22:40/q48/p1 It is preferably an independent nominative subject after the completed inna clause. — reasons: Sibawayh cites 9:3, contrasts lā and lā bal coordination, and notes that layta favors accusative because wish and assertion should not mix.
  22:40/q48/p2 It may be raised by coordination with an implicit predicate pronoun, but explicit hūwa is clearer. — reasons: Sibawayh calls this weaker; the Jarir line illustrates a prepositional predicate's implicit pronoun.
22:40/q49 When and why was permission to fight first given? [* · reports] (turns on: The occasion reports and placement arguments relate expulsion to a new permission for defensive fighting.)
  22:40/q49/p1 After the Prophet's departure and Quraysh's persecution, 22:39–40 first permitted fighting. — reasons: Urwa and others say Muslims had been ordered to call, endure, and pardon. Ibn Abbas reports Abu Bakr's fear and recognition that fighting would follow. Ibn Zayd says the reprieve lasted ten years.
  22:40/q49/p2 It permitted fighting those who blocked the emigrants' migration. — reasons: Mujahid relates this to specific Meccan emigrants.
  22:40/q49/p3 It permitted defense if Quraysh barred pilgrimage to the House, including fighting in the Haram or sacred months. — reasons: Islahi says Quraysh used sanctuary rules as a shield while occupying Abraham's House and accusing Muslims of impiety.
22:40/q50 Was this verse revealed in Mecca or Medina? [* · sciences] (turns on: Destination of exile and the disputed dating of the sūra.)
  22:40/q50/p1 This passage was revealed in Medina, after migration. — reasons: Ibn Ashur is certain; Islahi says 22:38–41 are Medinan explanatory verses within a Makkan sura.
