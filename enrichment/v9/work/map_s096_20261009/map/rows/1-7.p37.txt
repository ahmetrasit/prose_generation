[ALUSI:1:7/r17] ALUSI, d. 1270 · ابن عباس | prefers | Al-Ālūsī prefers Ibn ʿAbbās’s report in al-Ṭabarī: the blessed include prophets, angels, martyrs, the truthful, and all who obey and worship God; other views restrict the class to some members. | mentions 4:69
[ALUSI:1:7/r18] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | The unrestricted blessing embraces every blessing, in line with the verse saying that people cannot enumerate God’s blessings. | mentions 14:34
[ALUSI:1:7/r19] ALUSI, d. 1270 · قيل | reports | One view says God blessed them by creating them for felicity.
[ALUSI:1:7/r2] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Apposition reinforces the predication, since it repeats the governing relation, and explains the straight path as the Muslims’ path, emphatically attesting to its rightness.
[ALUSI:1:7/r20] ALUSI, d. 1270 · قيل | reports | Another view says God blessed them by rescuing them from destruction; a further view says the blessing was guidance.
[ALUSI:1:7/r21] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Using the active second-person verb is an appeal: since God has already bestowed favor, the worshipper asks Him to grant guidance too; the cited verse of poetry illustrates that God taught people to ask in order to give.
[ALUSI:1:7/r22] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | The accompanying poem says that God would not have taught people to ask unless He intended to grant their hopes; al-Ālūsī gives no poet’s name.
[ALUSI:1:7/r23] ALUSI, d. 1270 · اللغويون | reports | The linguists list ten forms: hāʾ ḍamma/kasra with variable mīm and optional wāw or yāʾ. Attributions name Ḥamza, the majority, al-Ḥasan, ʿUmar b. Khālid/Fāʾid, Ibn Kathīr/Qālūn, al-Aʿraj, Muslim b. Jundub, Ibn Hurmuz, and al-Khaffāf from Abū ʿAmr.
[ALUSI:1:7/r24] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Ghayr is a matching apposition to alladhīna. Making it an apposition to the pronoun in ʿalayhim is semantically awkward; the relative clause still contains its pronoun because the antecedent is not truly discarded.
[ALUSI:1:7/r25] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | rejects | Treating ghayr as an adjective meaning different is weak: ghayr has become a noun and usually does not modify an expressed noun.
[ALUSI:1:7/r26] ALUSI, d. 1270 · سيبويه | reports | Sībawayh analyzes ghayr as an explanatory or restrictive adjective of alladhīna; al-Ālūsī explains that the relative is definite generically but can function like an indefinite when only an unspecified subset is intended.
[ALUSI:1:7/r27] ALUSI, d. 1270 · المحققون من علماء العربية | reports | Some authoritative Arab grammarians allow ghayr to become definite by annexation between two opposite definite terms, as in movement versus stillness; this analysis is adopted by those who rely on it.
[ALUSI:1:7/r28] ALUSI, d. 1270 · ابن السري | reports | Ibn al-Sarrī says ghayr becomes definite when annexed to a definite having only one opposite, since the contrast is then exclusive.
[ALUSI:1:7/r29] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | The objection from 35:37 does not disprove this definiteness: ṣāliḥan may be a fronted circumstantial accusative, or ghayr alladhī may be an apposition to it. | mentions 35:37
[ALUSI:1:7/r3] ALUSI, d. 1270 · قيل | reports | An alternative analysis calls the second phrase an adjective of the first ṣirāṭ.
[ALUSI:1:7/r30] ALUSI, d. 1270 · عمر بن الخطاب | reports | ʿUmar read ghayra in the accusative; a noncanonical report attributes the same reading to Ibn Kathīr. It is best parsed as a circumstantial accusative governed by anʿamta, though other analyses are discussed.
[ALUSI:1:7/r31] ALUSI, d. 1270 · الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash permits the accusative as either a disconnected or connected exception if bestowal is understood generally.
[ALUSI:1:7/r32] ALUSI, d. 1270 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ rejects the exception analysis because ghayr then means siwā and the negative lā cannot be coordinated with it unless lā is treated as extra; al-Ālūsī notes the usage after wāw is unattested. | mentions 7:12
[ALUSI:1:7/r33] ALUSI, d. 1270 · الأخفش | reports | A report ascribed to al-Akhfash treats the exception as semantically negative, allowing lā to coordinate by meaning and removing al-Farrāʾ’s objection.
[ALUSI:1:7/r34] ALUSI, d. 1270 · الخليل | prefers | Al-Khalīl parses the accusative as governed by an omitted verb, aʿnī, and al-Ālūsī endorses him: exception is problematic and the circumstantial analysis requires an unpersuasive indefinite ghayr.
[ALUSI:1:7/r35] ALUSI, d. 1270 · بعضهم | reports | Some posit an ellipsis, supplying ghayra ṣirāṭ al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim, and offer several possible syntactic attachments; al-Ālūsī prefers the unelliptical straightforward syntax.
[ALUSI:1:7/r36] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | The root of anger denotes intensity; the word is also used for a hard rock, a vicious snake, and a scowling she-camel.
[ALUSI:1:7/r37] ALUSI, d. 1270 · السعد | reports | In his commentary on al-Miftāḥ, al-Saʿd defines anger as a movement of the soul whose origin is the will to take revenge.
[ALUSI:1:7/r38] ALUSI, d. 1270 · السعد | reports | In his commentary on al-Kashshāf, al-Saʿd defines divine anger as the will to take revenge.
[ALUSI:1:7/r39] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | reports | A definition in Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid describes anger as a state of the soul followed by the spirit’s outward motion seeking revenge; another account calls it a change when the heart’s blood boils.
[ALUSI:1:7/r4] ALUSI, d. 1270 · قيل | reports | One unusual transmitted interpretation treats the second path as different from the first, as if a conjunction were understood.
[ALUSI:1:7/r40] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | reports | A hadith warns people to beware of anger, likening it to a glowing ember in the heart and pointing to swollen neck veins and reddened eyes; no grading or source is given here.
[ALUSI:1:7/r41] ALUSI, d. 1270 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī explains God’s anger as His will to punish sinners, bringing punishment upon them as an angry king does to subjects under his authority.
[ALUSI:1:7/r42] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Following the early generations, al-Ālūsī affirms anger as an eternal divine attribute fitting God’s majesty without specifying how or claiming to know its reality, like mercy.
[ALUSI:1:7/r43] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | The hadith God’s mercy preceded His anger means either that mercy’s effects are greater or that mercy appeared earlier.
[ALUSI:1:7/r44] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Ḍalāl originally means destruction, as in the verses about being lost in the earth and God destroying deeds; in religion it means departing from truth. | mentions 32:10, 47:8

[ALUSI:1:7/r46] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبو زيد | reports | Abū Zayd records the dialectal forms daʾaba and shaʾaba; this pronunciation is also found in ʿAmr ibn ʿUbayd’s reading of 55:39. | mentions 55:39
[ALUSI:1:7/r47] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | reports | The cited poem’s word fādaʾhamat exemplifies the same hamzated form; grammarians disagree whether the usage can be extended by analogy.
[ALUSI:1:7/r48] ALUSI, d. 1270 · عمر بن الخطاب | reports | A reading with wa-ghayr in place of wa-lā is reported from ʿUmar and ʿAbd Allāh ibn al-Zubayr; the muṣḥaf’s mutawātir reading is wa-lā, which reinforces the negation implicit in ghayr.

[ALUSI:1:7/r50] ALUSI, d. 1270 · ابن عباس | reports | Al-Ṭabarī transmits the same identification from Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Masʿūd; Ibn Abī Ḥātim says he knows no disagreement among Qurʾān commentators about it.
