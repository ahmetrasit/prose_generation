92:11/q09 What does the funeral report teach about written fate and action? [* · reports]
  92:11/q09/p1 Each person's place is written, yet the Prophet commands action because everyone is facilitated toward their outcome. — reasons: Thaʿlabī reports ʿAlī's account: at a funeral a man asked whether they should rely on the written places in Paradise and Hell. The Prophet answered 'Act' and recited the passage on giving and withholding.
92:11/q10 What is reported about the surah's place of revelation? [* · sciences]
  92:11/q10/p1 Some called the surah Medinan on the basis of the Abū al-Daḥdāḥ story. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says Ibn ʿAbbās's report attributes revelation through al-ʿusrā to the episode and is gharīb; he cautions that 'revealed concerning' may mean a verse applies to a story rather than that the story caused revelation.
92:11/q11 What lexical distinction is drawn around 'yughni'? [يُغْنِى · meaning]
  92:11/q11/p1 Ghinā may mean self-sufficiency without wealth, whereas tharā normally denotes possessing wealth. — reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ compares the terms with reference to 93:8 and allows figurative use of tharā.
92:11/q12 What does the nearby verb 'fasanuyassiruhu' mean? [* · meaning]
  92:11/q12/p1 It means 'We will prepare him,' as Arabic yassarat can describe sheep ready to lamb. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ supplies the sheep idiom and a poet's line about two masters whose sheep are ready for lambing.
92:11/q13 What does the separate root rādiʾa signify? [تَرَدَّىٰٓ · meaning]
  92:11/q13/p1 It is a distinct root meaning base or defective. — reasons: Naḥḥās gives rādiʾ and murdīʾ as examples, separating this root from the explanation of taraddā.
92:11/q14 What literary weaknesses does Bāqillānī find in the cited poems? [* · rhetoric] (turns on: The notes assess different lines and images rather than record a disagreement over one line.)
  92:11/q14/p1 The first line has little information and several weak or incoherent images. — reasons: Bāqillānī says polished wording lacks meaning, scent appearing only while the women stand is an unnecessary limitation, the clove breeze weakens the initial musk comparison, and the east-wind phrase hangs syntactically.
  92:11/q14/p2 The tears passage uses filler, repetition, and an implausible, weak image. — reasons: 'From me' and 'on the neck' add nothing; the saddle's wetness is repeated to pad meter. Bāqillānī says tears would more likely fall on ground or clothing and could have been described as soaking the beloveds' dwellings.
  92:11/q14/p3 The second line lacks beauty and striking meaning despite its unusual place name. — reasons: Bāqillānī warns against mistaking an unfamiliar location for strong diction.
  92:11/q14/p4 Al-Khabzarzī has a stronger and more remarkable poem than the criticized line. — reasons: Bāqillānī offers this as a comparative literary judgment.
92:11/q15 Can applying too much grammar spoil an utterance? [* · grammar]
  92:11/q15/p1 For each clause a grammatical rule either applies correctly or fails; correctness cannot become excessive. — reasons: Jurjānī rejects the comparison with too much salt and Abū Bakr al-Khwārizmī's dislike of 'much inflection.' Each sentence must be correct separately; one correct sentence cannot repair another. Grammar is likened to a balance pointer indicating equality.
92:11/q16 What meaning is given for saʿy in the surah's oath response? [* · meaning]
  92:11/q16/p1 It literally means brisk, forceful walking and figuratively work or striving. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the metaphor while analyzing the oath's response.
92:11/q17 What other examples illustrate broad wording that bears multiple readings? [* · rhetoric]
  92:11/q17/p1 Indefinite expressions can denote either the object or degree of an act; more specific wording can settle the scope. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares 'do not associate anything' in 4:36 with a verse using 'anyone' in 18:110, and says kathīran also permits several readings, with 4:160 among his examples.
92:11/q18 Why is ownership emphasized elsewhere in the surah? [* · rhetoric]
  92:11/q18/p1 The emphatic lām suits the surah's repeated concern with wealth and possession. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts 53:25, whose context lacks wealth and omits this lām.
