[ABUHAYYAN:96:9/r4] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · قيل | reports | An account calls this the first congregational prayer in Islam: Muhammad prayed with Abu Bakr, Ali, and other early believers when Abu Talib and Jaʿfar passed by.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:9/r5] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · أبو طالب | reports | Abu Talib urged Ali and Jaʿfar to support and defend their cousin the Prophet, then departed happily; he composed verses pledging not to abandon the Prophet and urging his sons to defend him.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:9/r6] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The apparent addressee of the first and second أرأيت is the Messenger; the pronouns remain consistent on this reading.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:9/r7] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · قيل | reports | Another reading shifts the address to the disbeliever: if the worshipper's prayer is guidance and a call to God and piety, would he still forbid him? In this reading the conditional pronouns refer to the forbidding person.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:9/r8] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · ابن عطية والفراء | reports | Ibn ʿAtiyya and al-Farraʾ take the pronoun in if he is on guidance to refer to the worshipper, not the forbidding person.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:9/r9] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari frames an ironic conditional: if the forbidding man were on a sound path and enjoining what he regards as piety, or instead denying truth and turning from true religion, does he not know God sees and requites him? This is a threat.
[SAMIN-DURR:96:9/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī takes alladhī yanhā and its conditional clause as the two objects of arāʾayta; the first condition's answer is omitted but inferred from the second, and the middle arāʾayta is emphatic filler.
[SAMIN-DURR:96:9/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The author analyzes three occurrences of arāʾayta: the third has an explicit interrogative second object and an omitted first; the first and second objects are recovered from parallel occurrences, with both objects omitted from the middle one.
[SAMIN-DURR:96:9/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | This is deletion licensed by contextual indication, not grammatical competition for objects, because whole clauses cannot be suppressed as individual words are.
[SAMIN-DURR:96:9/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | Al-Zamakhsharī's proposed conditional answer without fāʾ is not valid; grammarians require fāʾ before an interrogative answer, absent poetic necessity.

[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p439/r1] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · أبو هريرة | reports | Abu Hurayra reports Abu Jahl swearing to trample Muhammad while he prayed; as he approached he recoiled, describing a trench of fire, terror, and wings. The Prophet says angels would have snatched him limb by limb; the narrator is unsure whether the revelation context was part of Abu Hurayra's report. Ibn Kathir says Ahmad, Muslim, al-Nasa'i, and Ibn Abi Hatim transmit it through Mu'tamir.

[BIQAI:96:9/r1] BIQAI, d. 885 · أبو هريرة رضي الله عنه | reports | Abū Hurayra's report in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim recounts Abū Jahl approaching Muhammad to smash his head with a rock, then recoiling in terror; versions describe wings, a fiery trench, or a huge camel blocking him.
[BIQAI:96:9/r2] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | When araʾayta is not visual, it means inform me: know inwardly with a clarity comparable to eyesight.
[BIQAI:96:9/r3] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | The verb yanhā depicts the forbidding as renewed and continuous.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p483/r5] BIQAI-FULL, d. 885 · author | holds | When raʾayta is not literal sight, it means “tell me” or “inform me”; here the question presses the listener to know the forbiddance clearly.
