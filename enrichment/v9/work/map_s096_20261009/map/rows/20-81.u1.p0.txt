## THE CURRENT MAP
20:81/q01 Who receives the command to eat? [كُلُوا۟ · referent] (turns on: Whether the command is direct recalled speech to Israel or a later address with an omitted speech formula.)
  20:81/q01/p1 The Israelites are addressed in the recalled event. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī says God told Israel to eat.
  20:81/q01/p2 Muhammad's contemporaries are addressed through an elliptical recollection of their ancestors. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the implied speech is 'it was said to them'; the rebuke recalls ancestral ingratitude, though he prefers the direct reading.
20:81/q02 What force does the command to eat have? [كُلُوا۟ · law]
  20:81/q02/p1 It grants permission to eat. — reasons: Al-Ṭabrisī states permission; al-Rāzī compares permission to hunt after consecration (5:2); al-Biqāʿī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also read permissive force.
20:81/q03 Why is the command to eat and its provision phrased this way? [كُلُوا۟ مِن طَيِّبَٰتِ مَا رَزَقْنَٰكُمْ · rhetoric]
  20:81/q03/p1 It recalls and completes a generous divine blessing. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī calls it a reminder of blessings; al-Biqāʿī says good provision signals abundance and 'what We provided' magnifies the gift; al-Ālūsī says it opens a fresh permission and completes the blessing.
20:81/q04 What are the good things provided? [طَيِّبَٰتِ مَا رَزَقْنَٰكُمْ · meaning] (turns on: Whether ṭayyib refers to legal permissibility, agreeable taste, or both.)
  20:81/q04/p1 Lawful food, including the manna and quails. — reasons: Muqātil and al-Suddī specify manna and quails; al-Rāzī reports al-Kalbī and Muqātil: it came down untouched by human hands; al-Qurṭubī says it carried no doubtful human claim. Al-Rāghib, cited by al-Bursawī, requires permissible acquisition, amount, and place.
  20:81/q04/p2 Pleasant or delicious food, especially manna and quails. — reasons: Al-Shāfiʿī makes ṭayyib what people find agreeable; Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes a juristic dispute about repulsive or impure animals. Al-Rāzī says manna and quails are pleasant; al-Bursawī describes natural taste.
  20:81/q04/p3 Food that is both lawful and pleasant. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya expressly combines the senses; al-Rāzī says they share a common meaning; al-Ālūsī also allows both qualities.
20:81/q05 How does the wording relate good things to all divine provision? [طَيِّبَٰتِ مَا رَزَقْنَٰكُمْ · grammar]
  20:81/q05/p1 The adjective and its noun describe provision as good, without dividing God's provision into good and bad. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī invokes 45:16 and takes the phrase as an adjective joined to its noun.
20:81/q06 How do manna and quails explain this provision and connect to other passages? [مَا رَزَقْنَٰكُمْ · links]
  20:81/q06/p1 They are the freely given sustenance of Moses' followers, recalled also elsewhere. — reasons: Asad cites 2:57 and 7:160; philologists say mann is sweet desert resin or an unearned gift, and Qāmūs says salwā is quail or comfort after deprivation. Asad treats the pair metonymically; Study Quran connects 2:57, 2:60, and 7:160; al-Rāzī points to an earlier treatment in al-Baqara.
20:81/q07 How is divine person expressed in the provision and preceding verbs? [رَزَقْنَٰكُمْ · readings] (turns on: Which divine first-person form the readers transmit and whether the gift's personal attribution is stressed.)
  20:81/q07/p1 First-person plural forms express the divine 'We'. — reasons: Ibn Mujāhid lists Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, Ibn ʿĀmir and ʿĀṣim; Abū ʿAmr omits an alif in waʿadnākum. Al-Rāzī calls this the other readers' usage; al-Nasafī says Kufans except ʿĀṣim use singular forms.
  20:81/q07/p2 First-person singular forms express the divine 'I'. — reasons: Ibn Mujāhid names Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī; al-Rāzī notes an exception in the manna clause; al-Bayḍāwī records variant promise forms. Qiraat-ER says the singular emphasizes God's personal gift.
20:81/q08 What inward provision is meant by eating good things? [كُلُوا۟ مِن طَيِّبَٰتِ مَا رَزَقْنَٰكُمْ · inward] (turns on: The inward referent assigned to food and nourishment.)
  20:81/q08/p1 Wholesome knowledge feeds and revives the heart. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī makes receiving good knowledge the heart's eating.
  20:81/q08/p2 Provision received in conscious witness, gratitude, and spiritual realization is good. — reasons: Al-Qushayrī reports provision obtained without disobedience, received while witnessing the Sustainer, and producing gratitude; he distinguishes enabling for self, affirmation for heart, realization for spirit. God's elect privately taste now what others openly receive in the afterlife (51:16).
  20:81/q08/p3 Manna and quails signify divine attributes and noble character to embody. — reasons: Al-Najmiyya, reported by al-Bursawī, maps manna to attributes and quail to gracious character; divine care rescues spirit and heart from the Pharaoh-like soul and supports this embodiment.
20:81/q09 What does transgressing concerning provision mean? [وَلَا تَطْغَوْا۟ فِيهِ · interpretation] (turns on: Which misuse of provision is emphasized: social injustice, ingratitude, unlawful use, hoarding, excess, or spiritual conceit.)
  20:81/q09/p1 Do not wrong other people by taking their share or oppressing them. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās identifies interpersonal wrongdoing, specified by al-Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān as taking another's provision; al-Wāḥidī adds insolence over the blessing. Al-Suyūṭī glosses transgression as injustice.
  20:81/q09/p2 Do not exceed God's permitted bounds or disobey His commands. — reasons: Muqātil compares Pharaoh's transgression; al-Naḥḥās says abundance must not induce disobedience; al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Muqātil include wronging oneself by exceeding permission; Ibn Kathīr includes exceeding need. Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes Israel's reckless excess.
  20:81/q09/p3 Do not deny the blessing or neglect gratitude. — reasons: Al-Kalbī says denial makes them transgressors; al-Kashshāf includes pleasure distracting from gratitude; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites their complaint of one food (2:61); Study Quran connects that request with ingratitude.
  20:81/q09/p4 Do not store more manna or quails than allowed. — reasons: Muqātil, al-Suddī and later commentators name hoarding; Qatāda allows a double gathering before the Sabbath when Israel did not work. Ibn ʿAbbās specifies the daily limit as a day and night.
  20:81/q09/p5 Do not cross from lawful food to unlawful food or consume it unlawfully. — reasons: Al-Qushayrī and al-Ṭabrisī state this crossing; al-Kalbī includes trading lawful things for forbidden ones.
  20:81/q09/p6 Do not use or spend God's provision to support disobedience. — reasons: Al-Māwardī and al-Ṭabrisī forbid making provision an aid to sin; al-Kashshāf lists sinful spending and withholding the poor's due; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī add waste, pride, and failure to pay obligations.
  20:81/q09/p7 Do not eat more than the minimum needed or eat heedlessly. — reasons: Sahl says excess intoxicates and distracts remembrance; al-Qushayrī separately reports taking beyond subsistence and eating without mindfulness.
  20:81/q09/p8 Do not exchange manna and quails for lesser food. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī connects this answer to Israel's request at 2:61.
  20:81/q09/p9 Do not credit God's favors to one's own noble descent. — reasons: Asad identifies arrogance about descent from Abraham.
  20:81/q09/p10 The ego must not claim perfection, beauty, or lordship through divine gifts. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī describes self-display in spiritual light; al-Bursawī warns that ecstatic 'I am the Real' or 'Glory be to me' can reflect human nature, and temporary states do not establish stations.
20:81/q10 How is the verb 'do not transgress' read? [تَطْغَوْا۟ · readings]
  20:81/q10/p1 Zayd ibn ʿAlī reads the ghayn with ḍamma. — reasons: Al-Samīn derives it from ṭaghā yaṭghū and compares ghadā yaghdū; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī transmit the reading.
20:81/q11 What happened when Israel stored the provision? [وَلَا تَطْغَوْا۟ فِيهِ · reports]
  20:81/q11/p1 Stored manna or food became wormy or spoiled, and without the storage food would not have spoiled. — reasons: Muqātil says it became wormy and foul; Ibn ʿAbbās says food, meat, or manna would not have spoiled absent the deed; Qatāda reports an exception before Sabbath, when enough was gathered for Friday and Saturday. One prophetic report couples this with Eve and marital betrayal; Muqātil also gives that comparison.
20:81/q12 What do the connected reports say of the calf? [* · reports]
  20:81/q12/p1 Al-Sāmirī blamed Moses' delay on borrowed ornaments and made a lowing calf from them. — reasons: ʿAbd al-Razzāq transmits Qatāda: al-Sāmirī used a horse hoof-trace and claimed the calf was their god and Moses' god. Al-Kalbī says Gabriel's horse bore life, and its trace made the calf low. Study Quran links Israel's idol request and worship to 7:138–141 and 20:87–88.
20:81/q13 How are the nearby words bi-mulkinā read and explained? [* · readings]
  20:81/q13/p1 Kasra means ownership, ḍamma authority, and fatḥa the verbal noun of malaka yamliku. — reasons: Ibn Khālawayh supports authority with 40:16, 'whose is the dominion today?' The word is in the following calf passage.
20:81/q14 What grammatical form expresses the threatened consequence? [فَيَحِلَّ عَلَيْكُمْ غَضَبِى · grammar] (turns on: Whether the fa marks a resulting subjunctive or coordinates a jussive prohibition.)
  20:81/q14/p1 The verb is subjunctive after consequential fa, with an omitted an. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī and al-Bursawī call it the answer to the prohibition; al-Samīn prefers the accusative analysis because the consequence is clear.
  20:81/q14/p2 The verb may be jussive, coordinated with 'do not transgress'. — reasons: Abū al-Baqāʾ allows it, but al-Samīn objects that it would wrongly prohibit wrath from descending.
