[IBNMUJAHID:v1p104/r5] IBNMUJAHID, d. 324 · ابن كثير وأبو عمرو | reports | The sīn reading of الصراط is transmitted from Ibn Kathīr and Abū ʿAmr through named reciters, while Ibn Kathīr also has transmissions with ṣād; Abū ʿAmr is reported with all three sibilant realizations.
[IBNMUJAHID:v1p106/r1] IBNMUJAHID, d. 324 · عريان بن أبي سفيان عن أبي عمرو | reports | One transmission from Abū ʿAmr places the sound between ṣād and zāy; the ordinary reading preserves clear ṣād, while Ḥamza’s imāla approaches zāy and cannot be represented precisely in writing.
[IBNMUJAHID:v1p106/r2] IBNMUJAHID, d. 324 · الكسائي عن حمزة | reports | Reports differ on whether Ḥamza shades only quiescent ṣād toward zāy or also moving ṣād; some say he gives the zāy outright only when ṣād is quiescent.
[IBNMUJAHID:v1p106/r3] IBNMUJAHID, d. 324 · الكسائي | reports | Al-Kisāʾī follows Ḥamza in ṣād before dāl in قصد and related forms; he says sīn is more common in Arabic but reads ṣād to follow the written Qurʾānic text. | mentions 16:9, 28:23, 6:46, 52:37, 88:22
[IBNMUJAHID:v1p106/r4] IBNMUJAHID, d. 324 · author | holds | Sīn is etymologically original, but scribes and readers use ṣād to ease articulation beside emphatic ṭāʾ: both sounds can be made with the tongue raised, unlike sīn and ṭāʾ together.
[IBNMUJAHID:v1p108#2/r1] IBNMUJAHID, d. 324 · ابن كثير | reports | Ibn Kathīr connects plural mīm to wāw whether the preceding hāʾ is kasra or ḍamma.
[IBNMUJAHID:v1p108#2/r2] IBNMUJAHID, d. 324 · نافع | reports | Nafiʿ has reports allowing raised or quiescent mīm; the author’s own recitation uses sukūn. Warsh keeps hāʾ kasra and joins mīm to wāw before an initial vowel. | mentions 2:6
[IBNMUJAHID:v1p108#2/r3] IBNMUJAHID, d. 324 · عاصم ونافع وابن كثير وابن عامر | reports | With a following consonant, these readers keep hāʾ kasra and raise mīm; Abū ʿAmr raises neither, while Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī raise both. | mentions 2:61, 28:23
[IBNMUJAHID:v1p108#2/r4] IBNMUJAHID, d. 324 · author | holds | Kasra on hāʾ follows a preceding kasra or yāʾ because hāʾ is close to yāʾ; plural wāw is retained as the original plural marker, parallel to dual alif in عليهما.
[IBNMUJAHID:v1p108/r1] IBNMUJAHID, d. 324 · author | holds | Shading ṣād toward voiced zāy matches the voicing of ṭāʾ while retaining a sibilant; the same voicing rationale applies to the cited ṣād+dāl words. | mentions 16:9, 28:23, 6:46
[IBNMUJAHID:v1p108/r2] IBNMUJAHID, d. 324 · حمزة | reports | Ḥamza pronounces hāʾ with ḍamma and mīm quiescent in عليهم, إليهم, and لديهم; other readers use kasra on hāʾ.
[IBNMUJAHID:v1p110/r1] IBNMUJAHID, d. 324 · عاصم وأبو عمرو وابن عامر والكسائي | reports | These readers keep hāʾ kasra and mīm sukūn; the dual alif already distinguishes two, while plural mīm distinguishes many, so wāw can be omitted for ease.
[IBNMUJAHID:v1p110/r2] IBNMUJAHID, d. 324 · حمزة | holds | Ḥamza’s ḍamma on hāʾ reflects its underlying form, since the pronoun begins with ḍamma in هم.
[IBNMUJAHID:v1p110/r3] IBNMUJAHID, d. 324 · author | holds | When mīm meets a following consonant it must move; some restore its original ḍamma while leaving hāʾ kasra, others match kasras to avoid a heavy ḍamma after kasra.
[IBNMUJAHID:v1p110/r4] IBNMUJAHID, d. 324 · الكسائي | reports | Al-Kisāʾī restores both mīm and hāʾ to ḍamma before a following consonant, avoiding the transition from kasra to ḍamma.
[IBNMUJAHID:v1p110/r5] IBNMUJAHID, d. 324 · حمزة | holds | Ḥamza limits hāʾ-ḍamma to عليهم, إليهم, and لديهم: when followed by an overt noun, their yāʾ becomes alif, which cannot follow a kasra, so the pronoun follows the overt form. | mentions 8:16, 6:1, 2:142
