[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r59] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | rejects | Abū Ḥayyān says the alleged proof that lā is extra in the verse of the generous man is not decisive: lā itself is the object of أبى, and البخل is its apposition or a causal object.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r6] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | Most grammarians treat على as a preposition; Sībawayh and a group of later Basran grammarians treat it as a ظرف noun except in certain constructions. It can express literal or figurative elevation and several other relations.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r60] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الخليل | reports | An alternative analysis, attributed to al-Khalīl, treats ghayr as an accusative with an implicit أعني, 'I mean'.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r61] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The phrase عليهم is in the nominative position as the passive verb's deputy subject; grammarians dispute whether a prepositional phrase can occupy that role.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r62] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Lā reinforces the negation already contained in ghayr and is attached to المغضوب عليهم to avoid suggesting that الضالين is coordinated with الذين.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r63] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · عمر وأبي | reports | ʿUmar and Ubayy read غير الضالين, with reports of both accusative and genitive on the rāʾ in the two occurrences; Abū Ḥayyān says the reading marks the angered and astray as distinct, while lā is the likelier emphasis.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r64] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī uses the alleged parallel between ghayr and lā to allow a governed object before ghayr; Abū Ḥayyān calls the argument very weak, since similarity of meaning does not transfer syntax without Arabic attestation.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r65] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | rejects | Some supply an omitted صراط before ghayr; Abū Ḥayyān finds the estimate weak because it creates awkward apposition or adjective order and other difficult attachment analyses.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r66] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · أيوب السختياني | reports | Ayyūb al-Sakhtiyānī reads ولا الضألين, turning the alif into hamza to avoid two adjacent sukūns; Abū Ḥayyān says the sound change is not productive, though Abū Zayd and Abū al-Fatḥ attest it as a dialect and cite Arab usage and poetry.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r67] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The relative clause of الذين uses a past verb to make God's favor definite and established; the clause with أل uses a participle encompassing all times, and makes the anger passive rather than directly ascribing it to God.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r68] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The passive wording for those angered is tactful in a humble plea: the worshipper attributes favor to the One asked for guidance but avoids confronting Him with the description of vengeance.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r69] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The blessing intended is religious; those angered and those astray are general categories covering everyone who incurs anger or goes astray.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r7] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The suffix هم is a masculine plural pronoun referring to rational absent people, and it can occupy nominative, accusative, or genitive position.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r70] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · ابن مسعود وابن عباس ومجاهد والسدي وابن زيد | reports | Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, al-Suddī, and Ibn Zayd identify those angered as Jews and those astray as Christians.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r71] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · عدي بن حاتم عن رسول الله | reports | The Jews-and-Christians identification is also related from ʿAdī b. Ḥātim from the Prophet; Abū Ḥayyān says it must be adopted if the report is sound.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r72] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | Another reported identification makes those angered the Jews and those astray the polytheists; Abū Ḥayyān notes other views without specifying them here.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r73] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · بعضهم | reports | A Sufi restriction interprets those angered as people who neglect proper courtesy while serving God, and the astray as those who perceive that conduct; Abū Ḥayyān says the wording does not support such restrictions.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r74] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | God's anger may mean His intent to requite a sinner, making it an attribute of essence because His knowledge precedes creation and sin; alternatively it is imposing punishment, an attribute of action.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r75] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Anger precedes misguidance in the wording although it results from it because it contrasts with favor: favor brings good and requital brings harm; the order also suits the sūra's cadence.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r76] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The conjunctive wāw does not itself establish temporal priority; if Jews and Christians are intended, the order may reflect chronology or the Jews' greater hostility, as they came earlier.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r77] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The sūra's closing phrases are an instance of parallel cadence (sajʿ mutawāzī), with the final words matching in meter and rhyme; the author also lists specification, apposition, and word order among its rhetorical features.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r78] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | Abū Ḥayyān concludes that the sūra is seven verses by consensus apart from disregarded dissent; Meccan and Kufan counters differ over whether the basmala or صراط الذين is an āya, while ʿAmr b. ʿUbayd counts eight and Ḥusayn al-Juʿfī six.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r79] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | He says the basmala at each sūra's opening is not an āya, while Ibn al-Mubārak anomalously counted it as an āya in every sūra; Abū Ḥayyān says he does not know the principle behind determining āya length.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r8] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | reports | The ten reported pronunciations vary the hāʾ and mīm between ḍamma and kasra, mīm sukūn or vowel, and a following yāʾ or wāw: Hamza, the majority, al-Hasan, Ibn Kathīr, Qālūn, al-Aʿraj, and al-Khaffāf are among those named; Ibn Mujāhid adds ʿUmar b. Fāʾid, and ʿAmr b. Fāʾid is also cited.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r80] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Reports about the sūra's letter count, supposed occasions of revelation, basmala's merit, and many titles are not necessarily causes of revelation or core tafsīr; only sound Prophetic clarification, occasions, and abrogation are indispensable to tafsīr.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r81] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The word Āmīn is not Qurʾān; Abū Ḥayyān omits discussion of its forms, meaning, and prayer ruling, along with introductory istiʿādha, as material outside his intended tafsīr scope.
[ABUHAYYAN:1:7/r9] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · علي | reports | Ali identifies the straight path as the Qurʾān; Ibn ʿAbbās is also cited for this interpretation.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p45/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Alladhīna is a relative pronoun, usually yāʾ in every case but sometimes wāw in the nominative; it refers to rational beings, unlike alladhī.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p45/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | Deleting the nūn of the relative pronoun is allowed, though some grammarians restrict it to poetic necessity unless it is not for specification.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p45/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Niʿma is ease and comfort; anʿamta makes someone possess a blessing, with ʿalā arising through an added sense of gracious favor.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p45/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Anʿama illustrates one of the twenty-four meanings of form IV, making its object a possessor of the root's meaning, here a blessing.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p46/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الأكثرون | reports | Most grammarians treat ʿalā as a preposition, but Sībawayh treats it as a locative noun except in set uses; its main sense is literal or figurative elevation.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p46/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | ʿAlā can also mean about, with, in, alongside, because of, from, or serve as an extra particle.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p46/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | The pronoun hum is masculine plural and rational, and is inflected for nominative, accusative, and genitive positions.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p46/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · حمزة | reports | Among ten reported forms of ʿalayhim, Ḥamza reads the hāʾ with ḍamma and the mīm quiescent.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p46/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الجمهور | reports | The majority read the hāʾ of ʿalayhim with kasra and leave the mīm quiescent.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p46/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan lengthens the kasra of hāʾ and mīm with a following yāʾ.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p46/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن كثير وقالون | reports | Ibn Kathīr and, in one report, Qālūn use kasra on hāʾ, ḍamma on mīm, and a following wāw.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p46/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الأعرج والخفاف عن أبي عمرو | reports | Abū Ḥayyān lists forms that lengthen the mīm with wāw or omit it, and other combinations of hāʾ and mīm vowels, among the ten reported ʿalayhim readings.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p48/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · جعفر الصادق | reports | Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq reads “straight path” in annexation, meaning the religion that is upright.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p48/r10] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās is reported to identify those favored as believers.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p48/r11] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · وكيع | reports | Wakīʿ identifies those favored as prophets and believers, or as Muslims.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p48/r12] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes the majority’s identification to the four groups named in 4:69, and also records Ibn ʿAbbās’s view that they are believers. | mentions 4:69
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p48/r13] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan identifies the favored people as the Companions of Muḥammad.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p48/r14] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · فرقة | reports | One group identifies them as the believing Israelites.
