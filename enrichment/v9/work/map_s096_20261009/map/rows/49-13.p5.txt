JAWZI-NUZHA, d. 597 · أهل التفسير | reports | The first Quranic sense is excellence or superiority, including God’s honoring Adam’s children and the noble book, messenger, and person.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v7p473/r1] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن عباس | reports | One occasion says the verse concerned Thabit ibn Qays, who insulted a man for not making room by calling him the son of a certain woman; Ibn Abbas's report is cited under 49:11. | mentions 49:11
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v7p473/r2] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · مقاتل | reports | Muqatil says the verse followed the conquest of Mecca: Bilal called the prayer from the Kaaba, provoking remarks from ʿAttab, al-Harith, Suhayl, and Abu Sufyan about his race and status.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v7p473/r3] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · يزيد بن شجرة | reports | Yazid ibn Shajara says the verse followed the Prophet's visit to a sick black servant and his personally washing, shrouding, and burying him after death.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v7p473/r4] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | The man and woman are Adam and Eve; people are equal in lineage, so the verse rebukes boasting about ancestry.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v7p473/r5] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · الجمهور من المفسرين وأهل اللغة | reports | Most exegetes and linguists say shuʿub are large groups such as Mudar and Rabiʿa, while qabaʾil are smaller clans such as Bakr and Tamim.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v7p473/r6] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · عطاء عن ابن عباس | reports | A report from Ibn Abbas through ʿAtaʾ identifies shuʿub as clients and qabaʾil as Arabs.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v7p473/r7] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · أبو رزين | reports | Abu Razin says shuʿub are mountain people who claim no affiliation, while qabaʾil are Arab tribes.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v7p473/r8] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · أبو سليمان الدمشقي | reports | Abu Sulayman al-Dimashqi also reports the inverse arrangement: qabaʾil are roots and shuʿub are branches growing from them.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v7p474/r1] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | The purpose is for people to recognize one another by closeness and distance of kinship, not to boast; the most honored before God is the most pious.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v7p474/r2] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · أبي بن كعب وابن عباس والضحاك وابن يعمر وأبان عن عاصم | reports | These readers read li-taʿrafu with a silent ʿayn, kasra on the rāʾ, and no alif.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v7p474/r3] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · مجاهد وأبو المتوكل وابن محيصن | reports | Mujahid, Abu al-Mutawakkil, and Ibn Muhaysin read the form with one doubled tāʾ, an alif, and an ungeminated rāʾ.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v7p474/r4] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · أبو نهيك والأعمش | reports | Abu Nuhayk and al-Aʿmash read litaʿarra fu with two tāʾs, a doubled rāʾ, and no alif.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v7p474/r5] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي ومجاهد وأبو الجوزاء | reports | Abu ʿAbd al-Rahman al-Sulami, Mujahid, and Abu al-Jawzaʾ read the hamza in an akramakum with fatḥa.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v7p474/r6] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farraʾ says opening the hamza means “so you may know that the most honored is the most pious”; though li-taʿrifu might be expected, li-taʿarafu is also permissible in that sense.

[RAZI-FULL:v28p117#2/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Apart from faith and disbelief, people share the grounds used for worldly boasting: wealth and lineage can belong to either rich or poor, believer or unbeliever, so these do not establish superiority without piety.

[RAZI-FULL:v28p117#2/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | The alternative is that each person addressed was created from a father and mother, emphasizing that all belong to the same human kind.
[RAZI-FULL:v28p117#2/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Rāzī says faith and disbelief produce a profound moral distinction, comparing the unbeliever to livestock or worse and describing the believer as truly human, while all remain one bodily kind.
[RAZI-FULL:v28p117#3/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · قيل | reports | An objection says lineage has customary and legal weight, including a restriction on a noblewoman marrying a Nabati.
[RAZI-FULL:v28p117#3/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Rāzī answers that when religious and divine honor appears, worldly rank by lineage or wealth loses standing, as a great light or sound overwhelms lesser ones.
[RAZI-FULL:v28p117#3/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Religious offices such as judgeship and testimony suit any learned, righteous person of high or low status; a profligate is unfit even with Qurashi lineage or great wealth.
[RAZI-FULL:v28p117#3/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | If two people share strong religion, lineage can favor one in people's eyes, but not before God, since inherited nobility is not earned through effort. | mentions 53:39
[RAZI-FULL:v28p117#3/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Rāzī says lineage is singled out among worldly boasts because it is the highest and most stable: wealth can be gained by the poor, and beauty or age do not last.
[RAZI-FULL:v28p117#3/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Creation from the same parents and by the same Creator rules out superiority based on origin or maker; only qualities acquired after birth, especially piety and nearness to God, can distinguish people.
[RAZI-FULL:v28p118/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | One reading distinguishes dispersed peoples whose common ancestor is unknown, such as non-Arabs, from tribes joined by a known ancestor, such as Arabs and Israelites.
[RAZI-FULL:v28p118/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | Another reading treats the groups as nested ranks: tribes contain peoples, then clans, subdivisions, branches, and relatives.
[RAZI-FULL:v28p118/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | One proposed purpose of peoplehood and tribes is mutual support rather than boasting.
[RAZI-FULL:v28p118/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | prefers | Rāzī also reads the purpose as mutual recognition rather than estrangement, since mockery, faulting, and backbiting lead to estrangement. | mentions 49:11, 49:12
