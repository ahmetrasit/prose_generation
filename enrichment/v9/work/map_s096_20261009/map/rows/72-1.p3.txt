[RAZI-FULL:v30p131#2/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · أصحاب الروحانيات | reports | Many ancient philosophers and spiritualists affirm jinn and call them lower spirits; they say these respond faster but are weaker than celestial spirits, which respond more slowly but are stronger.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p131#2/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · بعض المثبتين | reports | One group of those affirming jinn says they are independent substances, neither bodies nor states inhering in bodies; sharing a negation does not make them equal to God.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p131#2/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · بعض المثبتين | reports | These proponents say the immaterial beings differ in essence and moral kind: some are good or noble, others evil or base, and only God knows their kinds and number.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p131#2/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · بعض المثبتين | reports | They maintain that immaterial jinn can know reported events, hear, see, and perform particular deeds; some kinds can perform feats beyond human power.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p131#2/r7] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · بعض المثبتين | reports | The proponents analogize jinn attachment to bodies with the human soul’s attachment through fine vaporous spirits formed from the blood’s finest parts in the left side of the heart.

[RAZI-FULL:v30p132#2/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Dividing bodies into subtle and dense does not prove that all bodies share a common essence, just as dividing accidents into quality and quantity does not establish shared essential properties.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p132#2/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | A subtle airy body could differ essentially from other air, with its nature entailing distinctive knowledge and extraordinary power; this makes jinn and their ability to assume forms plausible.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p132#2/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · الأشعري وجمهور أتباعه | prefers | Al-Ashʿarī and most followers deny that bodily structure is a condition for life.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p132#2/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | prefers | Their argument is that one life cannot inhere simultaneously in many substrates; if each equal part had its own life, dependence between the parts would create a circle, so an individual part can live independently.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p132#2/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · المعتزلة | reports | The Muʿtazilites argue that structure is necessary because life disappears when the structure breaks down and remains while it stays intact.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p132#2/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | rejects | Rāzī rejects that induction as proof of necessity: observed cases do not establish the state of unobserved cases, and the inference assumes that miracles cannot break ordinary patterns.
