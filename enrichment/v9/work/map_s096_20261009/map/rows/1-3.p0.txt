[MUQATIL:1:1-4/r3] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | Al-Raḥmān and al-Raḥīm are two closely related names, one more delicate than the other; al-Raḥmān means merciful and al-Raḥīm means compassionate through mercy.
[MUQATIL:1:5-7/r8] MUQATIL, d. 150 · أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report from Abū Hurayra says the Prophet related that God divided this sūra between Himself and the servant: praise is thanks, the two mercy names are praise, the King verse is glorification, and the rest belongs to the servant; seeking help is God's special acknowledgment of the servant's plea.
[MAJAZ:v1p21/r1] MAJAZ, d. 209 · author | holds | Al-Raḥmān means possessor of mercy, while al-Raḥīm means merciful; Arabic can use two forms from one word with one meaning.
[MAJAZ:v1p21/r2] MAJAZ, d. 209 · author | holds | This pairing reflects Arabic breadth of expression, like nadmān and nadīm; Burj ibn Mushir’s verse uses nadmān for a drinking companion.
[MAJAZ:v1p21/r3] MAJAZ, d. 209 · author | holds | Al-Nuʿmān ibn Naḍla’s verse likewise uses nadmān for a companion, illustrating the paired forms.
[MAJAZ:v1p21/r4] MAJAZ, d. 209 · author | holds | Barīq al-Hudhalī’s verse calls Abū Zayd both brother and nadīm, witnessing the companion sense of nadīm.
[MAJAZ:v1p21/r5] MAJAZ, d. 209 · author | holds | Ḥassān ibn Thābit also uses nadīm for a drinking companion in support of the Arabic synonym pair.
[TAB:1:3/r1] TAB, d. 310 · author | holds | Al-Ṭabarī does not repeat the explanation of the two names because he has already explained them in the basmala commentary.
[TAB:1:3/r2] TAB, d. 310 · author | holds | He argues that the basmala is not a verse of the Fātiḥa: otherwise the same complete verse would occur twice in immediate succession without a separating passage.
[TAB:1:3/r3] TAB, d. 310 · جماعة من أهل التأويل | reports | Some exegetes say al-ḥamdu lillāh separates the repeated phrase only on the surface, since the intended order places al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm before Rabb al-ʿālamīn. | mentions 1:2
[TAB:1:3/r4] TAB, d. 310 · جماعة من أهل التأويل | reports | They place Malik yawm al-dīn beside Rabb al-ʿālamīn because both describe sovereignty, and place the mercy names beside other praise of God. | mentions 1:4
[TAB:1:3/r5] TAB, d. 310 · author | holds | He says Arabic often puts a phrase before the words it semantically modifies, citing Jarīr and Qur'an 18:1–2 to support the opposing word-order analysis while retaining his conclusion about the basmala. | mentions 18:1, 18:2
[TAB:1:3/r6] TAB, d. 310 · author | holds | Jarīr's line and the wording of al-Kahf 18:1–2 illustrate this delayed-before order; he treats the examples as further evidence that the basmala is not a Fātiḥa verse. | mentions 18:1, 18:2
[TAB:1:7/r54] TAB, d. 310 · أهل الإلحاد الطاعنون في القرآن | reports | Critics ask why al-Fātiḥa has seven verses when, they argue, the meaning of its remaining verses is already contained in “Master of Judgment Day” and “You alone we worship and ask for help.”
[TAB:1:7/r55] TAB, d. 310 · author | holds | Earlier scriptures each contain some meanings found together in the Qur'an: the Torah gives admonitions and detail, the Psalms praise and glorify, and the Gospel admonishes and reminds; the Qur'an gathers these and adds more.
[TAB:1:7/r56] TAB, d. 310 · author | holds | Al-Ṭabarī says the prior books lack a miracle certifying their recipients, whereas the Qur'an's unmatched composition defeats all attempts to produce even a short surah and proves Muhammad's prophethood.
[TAB:1:7/r57] TAB, d. 310 · author | holds | The Qur'an's varied contents—encouragement, warning, commands, prohibitions, stories, argument, and parables—also make its extended expression purposeful.
[TAB:1:7/r58] TAB, d. 310 · author | holds | Al-Fātiḥa's inimitable arrangement proves prophethood, while its praise and glorification direct people to recognize God's greatness, sovereignty, power, and dominion and to thank Him for His favors.
[TAB:1:7/r59] TAB, d. 310 · author | holds | The account of those favored through knowledge of God and His enabling teaches that every religious and worldly blessing comes from Him, directing requests to God rather than idols or rivals.
[TAB:1:7/r60] TAB, d. 310 · author | holds | Recounting punishments for disobedience warns people away from sin and from incurring God's anger, whose consequences lead to destruction.
[TAB:1:7/r61] TAB, d. 310 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Abū Hurayra reports that the Prophet said God answers the worshipper's recitation: He calls al-ḥamdu praise, al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm commendation, Mālik Yawm al-Dīn glorification, and the rest of the surah the servant's share.
[TAB:1:7/r62] TAB, d. 310 · أبو هريرة | reports | A second Abū Hurayra version gives the same recitation exchange but is not attributed to the Prophet.
[TAB:1:7/r63] TAB, d. 310 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Jābir ibn ʿAbd Allāh reports the divine saying that the prayer is divided between God and His servant, with the servant receiving what he asks; the first three praises are God's, and what follows is for the servant.
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p28/r1] IBNABIHATIM, d. 327 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | In a sacred report, God says that when the worshipper says praise belongs to God, the servant has praised Him, and when he says the two mercy names, he has extolled Him.
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p28/r2] IBNABIHATIM, d. 327 · الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak says al-Rahman encompasses all creation, while al-Rahim is specifically for believers.
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p28/r3] IBNABIHATIM, d. 327 · خالد بن صفوان | reports | Khalid ibn Safwan says both names convey tenderness, with one more tender than the other.
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p28/r4] IBNABIHATIM, d. 327 · الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan repeats that al-Rahman is a name people cannot appropriate for themselves.
[NAHHAS:v1p171/r1] NAHHAS, d. 338 · author | holds | The paired names may be accusative as praise, both nominative with an implied subject, one nominative and one accusative, or the first genitive with the second nominative or accusative.
[RUMMANI:v1p97/r9] RUMMANI, d. 384 · author | holds | Nearby letters include mīm with nūn in al-Fātiḥa and dāl with bāʾ in the quoted Qur'anic phrase endings.
[SULAMI:v1p35#2/r1] SULAMI, d. 412 · author | holds | Al-Rahman oversees the secrets of saints and unveils Himself to prophets' spirits; al-Rahim tenderly sustains all creatures, good and wicked, in worldly life.
[SULAMI:v1p35#2/r2] SULAMI, d. 412 · بعضهم | reports | One view makes al-Rahman specific in both name and action, and al-Rahim general in both.
[SULAMI:v1p35#2/r3] SULAMI, d. 412 · بعضهم | reports | Al-Rahman gives blessings, while al-Rahim grants protection from sin.
[SULAMI:v1p35#2/r4] SULAMI, d. 412 · بعضهم | reports | Al-Rahman signifies divine disclosure, al-Rahim divine governance.
[SULAMI:v1p35#2/r5] SULAMI, d. 412 · بعضهم | reports | Al-Rahman reveals lights and al-Rahim preserves entrusted secrets.
[SULAMI:v1p35#2/r6] SULAMI, d. 412 · author | holds | God's names cannot be comprehended in their true reality; they reveal only creatures' share, and whoever claims to explain God's essence through them goes astray, since God is beyond knowledge (20:110). | mentions 20:110
[SULAMI:v1p35#2/r7] SULAMI, d. 412 · جعفر | reports | Al-Rahman supplies outward food, drink, and health as well as inward intellect, knowledge, understanding, senses, and aspiration.
[THALABI:v1p103/r4] THALABI, d. 427 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | In the divine report dividing prayer between God and the servant, the basmala glorifies God, al-ḥamdu praises Him, al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm extols Him, Mālik delegates the matter, the worship-and-help verse is shared, and guidance is granted to the servant.
[WAHIDI-ASBAB:v1p19/r1] WAHIDI-ASBAB, d. 468 · الأكثرون | reports | Most authorities say the Opening was revealed in Mecca and was among the Qur'an's earliest revelations.
[WAHIDI-ASBAB:v1p19/r2] WAHIDI-ASBAB, d. 468 · أبو ميسرة | reports | Abu Maysara reports that before revelation the Prophet fled when a caller addressed him; Waraqa advised him to stay and listen, after which the call instructed him to attest to God's oneness and Muhammad's messengerhood and recite the Opening.
[WAHIDI-ASBAB:v1p19/r3] WAHIDI-ASBAB, d. 468 · علي بن أبي طالب | reports | Ali reports that the Opening descended in Mecca from a treasure beneath the Throne.
[WAHIDI-ASBAB:v1p19/r4] WAHIDI-ASBAB, d. 468 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas reports that when the Prophet recited the basmala and the Opening in Mecca, Quraysh responded with a curse or similar insult.
[WAHIDI-ASBAB:v1p19/r5] WAHIDI-ASBAB, d. 468 · مجاهد | reports | Mujahid alone says the Opening is Medinan; al-Husayn ibn al-Fadl calls this an isolated slip against the scholarly majority.
[WAHIDI-ASBAB:v1p19/r6] WAHIDI-ASBAB, d. 468 · الواحدي | holds | Al-Wahidi argues it must be Meccan because the Meccan Hijr 15:87 calls the seven oft-repeated a gift to the Prophet; it is unreasonable to say he prayed for over a decade without the Opening. | mentions 15:87
[WAHIDI-ASBAB:v1p19/r7] WAHIDI-ASBAB, d. 468 · أبو هريرة | reports | Abu Hurayra reports that after Ubayy recited the Opening, the Prophet said no comparable surah was revealed in the Torah, Gospel, Psalms, or Qur'an, calling it the seven oft-repeated and mighty Qur'an given to him.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p455/r1] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | Divine mercy means God's willing good and blessing for its recipients and is an essential attribute; human mercy is tenderness and compassion.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p455/r10] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | reports | Some scholars say al-Raḥmān is more emphatic than al-Raḥīm, like ʿallām compared with ʿalīm; they call the former mercy for this world and the latter mercy for the afterlife.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p455/r2] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · أبو العباس | reports | Abū al-ʿAbbās explains pairing al-Raḥmān with al-Raḥīm by treating the first as Hebrew and the second as Arabic.
