[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p56/r2] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · الرازي | reports | Invoking the congregation’s mercy, the worshipper praises, worships, seeks help, asks guidance, follows the righteous, and flees the rejected as a group.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p58/r2] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | The blessed are those who have followed the straight path, while those astray are lost from it; the Quran often pairs guidance and misguidance as opposites. | mentions 16:93, 2:26
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p60/r4] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | The next phrase identifies the straight path as the way of the blessed, excluding the angry and the astray and encompassing every accountable person.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p61/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | People are either blessed, or wretched in two ways: knowing truth but violating it, or lacking knowledge of truth and being astray. | mentions 18:103-104
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p61/r2] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · البيضاوي | reports | One proposed reading identifies the angry as sinners and the astray as those ignorant of God.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p61/r3] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | No accountable person falls outside these categories: the blessed know and obey, while the wretched are sinners or ignorant.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p61/r4] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · الرازي | reports | Al-Razi classifies accountable people as obedient, sinful, or ignorant of religion and unbelieving, corresponding to the three Quranic groups.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p62/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · الرازي | reports | Complete blessing combines knowing truth and acting on it; lacking action makes one sinful and angry upon, while lacking knowledge makes one astray. | mentions 4:93, 10:32

[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p63/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · التفسير القيم | reports | Living out worship and seeking aid in knowledge, action, and inward state cures corruption of the heart and intention.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p63/r3] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · التفسير القيم | reports | Asking for the straight path cures ignorance; healing these spiritual diseases makes a person among the blessed, unlike those corrupt in intention or knowledge.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p63/r4] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | The past tense in You blessed identifies established recipients, including prophets, truthful ones, martyrs, and righteous people. | mentions 4:69
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p63/r5] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | Using a past verb for the blessed, then nominal forms for the angry and astray, distinguishes established past beneficiaries from the enduring qualities of the latter groups.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p63/r6] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | A present-tense blessing would omit the earlier prophets and righteous, imply no established past recipients, and obscure continuity in one path from Adam onward.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p64/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | The past tense allows the community of the blessed to grow as each present recipient joins those already blessed; the present tense could imply only a small current group.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p64/r2] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | The noun forms angry upon and astray cover all times and make those conditions enduring.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p64/r3] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | Calling them the blessed ones would not name the benefactor; the greatness of a gift and the honor it confers depend on who bestowed it.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p65/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | Attributing blessing to the greatest giver magnifies both the gift and the recipient’s honor, as a ruler’s favor exceeds a lesser official’s.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p65/r2] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | God attributes good and favor to Himself but leaves evil’s agent unmentioned, as in the saying that all good is in God’s hands and evil is not from Him. | mentions 72:10, 17:83
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p65/r3] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | All blessing truly comes from God, so the Quran attributes the act of bestowing blessing to Him. | mentions 16:53, 4:72, 28:17, 43:59
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p66/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | The sole Quranic attribution of blessing to someone besides God occurs when the Prophet’s special favor to Zayd is mentioned after God’s favor to him. | mentions 33:37
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p66/r2] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | The passive angry upon leaves the agent broad, including God and those angry for Him; even former allies disown and curse the condemned in the afterlife. | mentions 6:94, 29:25
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p66/r3] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · قيل | reports | The passive avoids attributing anger directly to the God being addressed in a humble request for favor and also prepares the ending’s parallelism and rhyme.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p67/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · التفسير القيم | reports | Blessing is good and favor, while anger is retribution; God names the more complete and prior quality and leaves the contrasting agent unstated, as elsewhere in the Quran. | mentions 72:10
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p67/r2] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · التفسير القيم | reports | God alone is the source of blessing, while angels, prophets, messengers, and allies also share anger at His enemies; the passive wording signals this. | mentions 16:53
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p67/r3] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · التفسير القيم | reports | Omitting the agent of anger belittles its targets, while naming the giver of blessing honors the recipients.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p67/r4] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | The nominal forms make anger and misguidance fixed conditions that persist in this world and the next, unlike temporary verb forms. | mentions 17:72
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p68/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | Adding lā before the astray makes the exclusion apply to either group separately, rather than only excluding people who combine anger and misguidance.
