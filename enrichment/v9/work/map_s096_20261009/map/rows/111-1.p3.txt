[KASHSHAF:111:1/r12] KASHSHAF, d. 538 · غير مسمى | reports | An unnamed reader has يدا أبو لهب, preserving the familiar form Abū Lahab and supporting the first explanation for using his kunya.
[KASHSHAF:111:1/r13] KASHSHAF, d. 538 · author | holds | He cites forms such as ʿAlī ibn Abū Ṭālib and Muʿāwiya ibn Abū Sufyān, where Abū is left unchanged so altering the familiar form will not confuse the hearer.
[KASHSHAF:111:1/r14] KASHSHAF, d. 538 · author | holds | He illustrates the importance of familiar name forms with Meccan governor Fulayta ibn Qasim's two sons, both called ʿAbd Allāh, one marked genitive and the other accusative; a Meccan man was known only by the genitive form.
[KASHSHAF:111:1/r15] KASHSHAF, d. 538 · author | holds | A second explanation is that Abu Lahab's personal name was ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā, so the kunya was used in its place.
[KASHSHAF:111:1/r16] KASHSHAF, d. 538 · author | holds | A third explanation is that his fate in a fire with flames matches his kunya, making it fitting to name him by it.
[KASHSHAF:111:1/r17] KASHSHAF, d. 538 · author | holds | Abū Lahab can function as a trait-based label, like Abū al-Sharr for an evil person and Abū al-Khayr for a good one.
[KASHSHAF:111:1/r18] KASHSHAF, d. 538 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | As an example of a trait-based kunya, the Messenger called Abū al-Muhallab Abū Ṣafra because of the yellow color in his face.
[KASHSHAF:111:1/r19] KASHSHAF, d. 538 · غير مسمى | reports | An alternative account says he was called Abū Lahab for his flushed, radiant cheeks.
[KASHSHAF:111:1/r2] KASHSHAF, d. 538 · author | holds | Tabāb means destruction; the expression أشابة أم تابة describes someone ruined by old age and debility.
[KASHSHAF:111:1/r20] KASHSHAF, d. 538 · author | holds | Zamakhsharī suggests that using the kunya this way could mock Abu Lahab and his pride in his bright cheeks.
[KASHSHAF:111:1/r21] KASHSHAF, d. 538 · غير مسمى | reports | A reading gives أبي لهب with sukūn; Zamakhsharī treats it as an alteration of proper names, like saying Shams ibn Mālik with ḍamma.
[KASHSHAF:111:1/r3] KASHSHAF, d. 538 · author | holds | He initially interprets the phrase as a curse that Abu Lahab's hands perished.
[KASHSHAF:111:1/r4] KASHSHAF, d. 538 · author | holds | He takes wa-tabba as saying that Abu Lahab himself perished entirely.
[KASHSHAF:111:1/r5] KASHSHAF, d. 538 · author | reports | A report says Abu Lahab picked up a stone to throw at the Messenger, explaining the curse that his hands perish.
[KASHSHAF:111:1/r6] KASHSHAF, d. 538 · author | holds | Alternatively, his hands are made to perish, with the hands standing for his whole person; Zamakhsharī compares 22:10. | mentions 22:10
[KASHSHAF:111:1/r7] KASHSHAF, d. 538 · author | holds | Alternatively, wa-tabba means that the matter happened and came to pass; he supports this completed-event sense with a poetic line ending وقد فعل.
[KASHSHAF:111:1/r8] KASHSHAF, d. 538 · ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masʿūd reads وقد تب, which Zamakhsharī cites to support the sense that the matter had indeed come to pass.
[KASHSHAF:111:1/r9] KASHSHAF, d. 538 · author | reports | A report says that after 26:214 was revealed, the Prophet called his clan at Ṣafā, tested whether they would believe a warning about cavalry at the mountain, then warned them of the Hour; Abu Lahab protested, and this sūra was revealed. | mentions 26:214
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p819#2/r1] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | reports | When ordered to warn his nearest kin, the Prophet called the clans at Safa; Abu Lahab cursed him for summoning them and the sūra was revealed.

[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p819#2/r11] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | reports | Abu Lahab once called his sons evil earnings after one pushed him while he tried to break up their fight; a Prophetic report says a son is a man’s earning.

[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p819#2/r5] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | reports | His kunya also suits his destiny in a blazing fire; it may mock his glowing cheeks and pride in them.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p819#2/r6] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | reports | Other kunyas name people for traits, and the Prophet called Abu al-Muhallab Abu Safra for the yellowness of his face.

[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p819#2/r8] KASHSHAF-FULL, d. 538 · author | holds | ما أغنى is an incredulous question or a negation; وما كسب may mean his earnings or what he acquired.
