## THE CURRENT MAP
74:31/q01 Who are the companions of the Fire? [أَصْحَٰبَ ٱلنَّارِ · referent] (turns on: Whether the phrase denotes literal angelic guards, an elliptical construction, or allegorical human faculties.)
  74:31/q01/p1 The angels appointed to guard and administer Hell, rather than its inmates or human jailers. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and others connect them to the nineteen over Saqar in 74:30; Bursevī contrasts the normal sense in 59:20; Elmalılı names Mālik as chief. Al-Wāḥidī reports ḥaddād as jailer; Islahi distinguishes prison guards from inmates.
  74:31/q01/p2 The phrase elliptically means the keepers of the people of the Fire. — reasons: Kashf al-asrār supplies an omitted genitive; Bursevī rejects it because angels guard the Fire itself.
  74:31/q01/p3 The angelic powers of human perception and conception, whose misuse brings afterlife suffering. — reasons: Asad links this allegorical reading to the preceding 'over it' and to moral discrimination and ascent.
74:31/q02 Why are the Fire's keepers angels? [إِلَّا مَلَٰٓئِكَةًۭ · interpretation]
  74:31/q02/p1 Angels cannot be overpowered by the condemned, and their different nature prevents pity. — reasons: Al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, and others stress their strength, obedience, and lack of kinship. Al-Rāzī contrasts compassionate human messengers and answers that God can preserve angels from fire pain; al-Biqāʿī cites the angel's cry destroying Thamūd; 66:6 describes them as stern.
  74:31/q02/p2 Their appointment shows pure servants manage Hell as they manage the universe. — reasons: Islahi contrasts angels with jinn or devils, emphasizing exact obedience.
74:31/q03 What does their number count? [عِدَّتَهُمْ · referent] (turns on: Whether nineteen names individual chiefs, thousands, ranks, or an unspecified kind of count.)
  74:31/q03/p1 Nineteen individual angels, commonly understood as chiefs with many other angelic attendants. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī cites the majority and a report of seventy thousand reins each carried by seventy thousand angels; al-Nasafī notes Mālik as chief. The report of Abū al-ʿAwwām contrasts nineteen with nineteen thousand.
  74:31/q03/p2 Nineteen thousand keepers. — reasons: A Banū Tamīm man argues from the statement that their number is a trial; Abū al-ʿAwwām affirms the answer in Mujāhid's report.
  74:31/q03/p3 Nineteen groups or ranks of angels. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports an omitted classifier but considers it weak against the transmitted report and the mockery of a small number.
  74:31/q03/p4 The number may leave open whether it counts individuals or kinds. — reasons: Elmalılı says the uncertainty itself tests unbelievers.
74:31/q04 Why exactly nineteen keepers? [عِدَّتَهُمْ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the number's wisdom is inaccessible or can be derived from arithmetic, Hell's organization, or human faculties.)
  74:31/q04/p1 Its precise wisdom belongs to God's free choice and remains unknown to creatures. — reasons: Al-Rāzī compares every finite body's determinate number and every event's determinate time; al-Ālūsī prefers consigning this ambiguous matter to God and criticizes speculative rationales. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the count was chosen for an undisclosed wisdom.
  74:31/q04/p2 Nineteen combines nine, the largest single digit, and ten, the smallest greater number. — reasons: Al-Māwardī offers the possible wisdom; al-Ālūsī reports this symbolic explanation without endorsing it.
  74:31/q04/p3 The nineteen divide into six leading, six driving, six striking, and Mālik as chief. — reasons: Al-Nasafī reports this functional scheme.
  74:31/q04/p4 Nineteen corresponds to nineteen levels of Saqar or nineteen kinds of torment. — reasons: Al-Nasafī transmits both alternatives.
  74:31/q04/p5 The keepers correspond to stabilizing mountains; Hell's original number was one hundred ninety. — reasons: Al-Nasafī reports this further explanation.
  74:31/q04/p6 Nineteen is derived from schemes of levels and categories of sinners. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī records six unbeliever classes with three overseers each plus one for sinful Muslims, or five levels leaving two for major and minor sinners or men and women; the Fire was first prepared for disbelievers. He also reports twelve animal faculties and seven natural powers, and twenty-four hours less five prayer hours, judging the philosophical scheme weak.
  74:31/q04/p7 The number symbolizes causes of human corruption in an inward reading. — reasons: Bursevī's Najmī source maps nine causes of soul corruption and a hell of distance, expulsion, curse and veiling; seven prostrating limbs plus animal nature, or anger and desire, complete nineteen. Nine signifies domination and loss of mercy.
74:31/q05 What makes the number a fitna for unbelievers? [فِتْنَةًۭ لِّلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether fitna is epistemic testing through a surprising count, punitive consequence, empowered few, or a misread allegory.)
  74:31/q05/p1 The unexpectedly small number exposes unbelievers' mockery, denial, and assumption they can overpower the keepers. — reasons: Sources mention sneering at nineteen rather than twenty, its seeming inability to punish all jinn and humans, denial of resurrection, and reluctance to accept divine power. Al-Biqāʿī says the obscurity tests dispositions; Islahi compares mockery of the Night Journey and zaqqūm in 17:60; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and others gloss fitna as test.
  74:31/q05/p2 Fitna means punishment in the Hereafter. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī compares 51:13–14; Study Quran reports the same alternative.
  74:31/q05/p3 The number is a demanding obligation through which God demonstrates that few empowered angels suffice. — reasons: Al-Jubbāʾī contrasts nineteen empowered angels with one hundred thousand strong angels; al-Aṣamm says their strength can equal a greater host; al-Naḥḥās urges recognition of God's power.
  74:31/q05/p4 The number is an allegory whose literalization misleads deniers. — reasons: Asad says the literal count distracts from the allegory; Islahi says eschatological similes describe realities known fully only to God, invoking 3:7.
  74:31/q05/p5 The trial is the disclosure of the count, not the creation of the guardians. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī, Bursevī, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguish announcing the number from making angels; Ibn ʿĀshūr places the trial in its mention in 74:30.
74:31/q06 How is 'we made their number only a trial' constructed? [وَمَا جَعَلْنَا عِدَّتَهُمْ إِلَّا فِتْنَةًۭ · grammar] (turns on: Whether fitna names a cause through metonymy or the condition imposed on unbelievers.)
  74:31/q06/p1 Fitna is jaʿalnā's second object and denotes the cause or occasion of a trial, with effect named for cause. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn supply an omitted 'cause'; Bursevī says the number cannot literally equal the unbelievers' temptation; al-Zamakhsharī and al-Ālūsī take the number's announcement as the effective making. Abū Ḥayyān rejects al-Zamakhsharī's substitution as unwarranted.
  74:31/q06/p2 Fitna is the condition of trial occurring to unbelievers; the restricted object names that outcome. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr analyzes the emptied exception in jaʿalnā's second-object slot and says the implicit point is the disclosure's trial.
74:31/q07 What incident prompted the answer that the guards are angels? [وَمَا جَعَلْنَآ أَصْحَٰبَ ٱلنَّارِ إِلَّا مَلَٰٓئِكَةًۭ · reports] (turns on: Which reported taunt and speaker elicited the verse.)
  74:31/q07/p1 Abū Jahl mocked the nineteen and challenged Quraysh to overpower the guards; a strongman offered to take seventeen while others took two. — reasons: Accounts vary the strongman's name among Abū al-Ashadd, Abū al-Ashaddayn, Abū al-Asad and al-Ḥārith ibn Kalda; some report a ten-to-one challenge, a cowhide feat, or a boast of pushing ten right and nine left. Ibn Kathīr discusses divergent wrestler identifications; Ibn Isḥāq reports later avoidance of public recitation.
  74:31/q07/p2 The verse answered an unnamed boast that even the least strong could enter Hell and then reach Paradise. — reasons: Al-Baghawī transmits this alternative preceding boast.
74:31/q08 What happened when Jews asked about Hell's keepers? [لِيَسْتَيْقِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ أُوتُوا۟ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · reports]
  74:31/q08/p1 They asked the Prophet or Companions the count; the Prophet indicated nineteen with his fingers and they affirmed it. — reasons: Jābir's versions vary between ten then nine and ten, ten, then nine; al-Barāʾ's version says revelation followed a Companion's deferral. Ibn al-ʿArabī calls Jābir's report authentic but notes the apparent Medinan setting; Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ālūsī say Jews could meet Meccans or travel, and Ibn al-ʿArabī offers that Ansar had not heard the verse or did not know nineteen named keepers.
74:31/q09 Why do scripture recipients become certain? [لِيَسْتَيْقِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ أُوتُوا۟ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether certainty arises from a matching scriptural number, divine power, or an allegorical continuity.)
  74:31/q09/p1 The Qur'anic nineteen matches their Torah or Gospel, confirming the report, Muhammad's prophethood, and revelation's divine origin. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Qatāda, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and many commentators transmit the scriptural count. Al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ṭabrisī stress that Muhammad had not read their books; al-Rāzī adds that hearing the Messenger removes doubts about textual alteration, while proclaiming a mockable detail shows sincerity. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Islahi note certainty need not lead every informed person to faith.
  74:31/q09/p2 The count demonstrates God's power to empower one being as many. — reasons: Jishumī reports this as another account of what becomes certain.
  74:31/q09/p3 They recognize an allegory and the Qur'an's rational continuity with earlier revelation. — reasons: Asad says understanding the symbolism convinces earlier revelation recipients.
