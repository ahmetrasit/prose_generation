[BIQAI:1:1/r10] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | The Fātiḥa establishes God's right to all praise and perfection, His exclusive sovereignty over this world and the next, and His sole right to worship and assistance, while asking guidance to the successful and rescue from the doomed.
[BIQAI:1:1/r11] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | Its central purpose is servants' vigilant awareness of their Lord so they worship Him alone; all other themes serve that end.
[BIQAI:1:1/r12] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | This purpose requires proving God's encompassing knowledge and His unique status as Creator, King, and Owner; prophets and revealed laws gather people to truth and teach them their Sovereign and what pleases Him, a purpose the Fātiḥa gathers in knowledge and action.
[BIQAI:1:1/r13] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | Since people are gathered to know God for worship, invoking His name at every movement and stillness leads to watchfulness, reverent fear, and belief that affairs originate from and return to Him; hence the Fātiḥa begins with the basmala.

[BIQAI:1:1/r15] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | The two refuge sūras are therefore appropriately connected to the Fātiḥa.
[BIQAI:1:1/r16] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | Biqāʿī reads the refuge formula's initial hamza and final mīm as signs of creation's beginning and return; the basmala's labial opening and repeated final mīm point to return in worldly governance, death in the intermediate realm, and resurrection.
