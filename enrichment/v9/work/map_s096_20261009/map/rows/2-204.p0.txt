[MUQATIL:2:204-206/r1] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | reports | The passage concerns al-Akhnas ibn Shariq, who professed love for Muhammad and swore he would follow Islam; the Prophet welcomed him though his inward intent differed.
[MUQATIL:2:204-206/r2] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | His appeal to God refers to his oath; he claimed that his words were true in his heart.
[MUQATIL:2:204-206/r3] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | Aldd al-khiṣām means arguing falsely; 19:97 likewise describes people as fiercely disputatious. | mentions 19:97
[MUQATIL:2:204-206/r9] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | reports | Al-Akhnas was also called Ubayy ibn Shariq; he earned the name by turning back 300 Banu Zuhra men from Badr, arguing Muhammad was their nephew and should be spared whether truthful or false.
[FARRA:v1p123/r1] FARRA, d. 207 · author | holds | The verse’s al-add term is illustrated by Arab use for a fierce disputant; the cited, unattributed line and forms ladda/ladadtuhu describe prevailing in argument.
[MAJAZ:v1p70/r8] MAJAZ, d. 209 · author | holds | Aladd al-khiṣām means intensely contentious; al-ibil and al-add can also describe a wicked person, and ludd is the plural of ladīd.
[MAJAZ:v1p70/r9] MAJAZ, d. 209 · author | reports | Al-Musayyib ibn ʿAlas’s verse calls the obstinate person al-ibil al-muṣammam, supporting the usage of ludd.
[ABDURRAZZAQ:v1p81#2/r1] ABDURRAZZAQ, d. 211 · عائشة | reports | ʿĀʾisha reports that the most detested kind of man to the Messenger of God was the stubborn, contentious opponent.

[IBNHISHAM:v2p174#2/r1] IBNHISHAM, d. 213 · ابن هشام | holds | Al-add is a person who stirs up conflict, intensifying the quarrel; its plural is lud. Ibn Hisham cites Qur'an 19:97 and Mahllal's poem, which has a transmitted variant for the final word. | mentions 19:97

[IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB:v1p80#3/r1] IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB, d. 276 · author | holds | Aladd al-khiṣām means the fiercest in disputation; a man can be called aladd and a people lud.
