27:10/q19 How does 'wa-lam yuʿaqqib' attach grammatically? [وَلَمْ يُعَقِّبْ · grammar]
  27:10/q19/p1 It coordinates with 'wallā' or is another circumstantial clause. — reasons: Al-Samīn gives both alternatives.
27:10/q20 Why does the weak ending disappear in 'lam yakhaf'? [يَخَافُ · grammar]
  27:10/q20/p1 It is weak-letter loss distinct from deletion before a following consonant. — reasons: Sībawayh contrasts it with dropping final alif, yā after kasra and wāw after ḍamma in ḥublā, maʿzā, yarmī, yaqḍī, yaghzū and yadʿū. Duals and akhshaw/ramaū retain endings to avoid number or gender ambiguity; opening yā could mimic accusative.
27:10/q21 What does 'ladayya' mean? [لَدَىَّ · meaning] (turns on: Presence generally or the particular revelatory setting.)
  27:10/q21/p1 With Me or in My presence. — reasons: Qatāda and al-Zajjāj give ʿindī. Al-Khafājī says 'at My meeting' and Ibn al-Shaykh 'under My judgment'; Sāmarrāʾī stresses nearness.
  27:10/q21/p2 At the extraordinary place and time of revelation. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī specifies this setting; al-Māwardī and others restrict the assurance because messengers fear God at other times.
27:10/q22 What is Moses told not to fear? [يَٰمُوسَىٰ لَا تَخَفْ · interpretation] (turns on: The immediate snake, all creatures, all harm, or the earlier killing.)
  27:10/q22/p1 The snake and its harm. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī identify the immediate object; al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī say prophetic security from punishment removes reason to fear the snake. Ibn Kathīr ties it to the coming commission.
  27:10/q22/p2 Any creature besides God. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Bursawī add trust in God; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Nasafī say chosen messengers fear none but Him.
  27:10/q22/p3 Anything harmful in God's presence. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 28:31 and divine proximity as safety. Al-Ālūsī says this may be reassurance rather than a literal ban, or may forbid the suspicion causing fear.
  27:10/q22/p4 Also his lingering fear over killing the Egyptian. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba says the serpent fear comes first, then repentance removes fear over the killing; al-Bursawī sees the allusion too.
27:10/q23 When and why do messengers not fear in God's presence? [إِنِّى لَا يَخَافُ لَدَىَّ ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · theology] (turns on: Which circumstance ladayya restricts and which fear is excluded.)
  27:10/q23/p1 While receiving revelation, though they fear God at other times. — reasons: Al-Māwardī, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı restrict the time; absorption in divine speech excludes other fear. Al-Ālūsī cites 35:28. Sāmarrāʾī cites the Prophet's declaration of being most God-fearing.
  27:10/q23/p2 When commanded to display a miracle. — reasons: Al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī transmit this narrower occasion, allowing fear otherwise.
  27:10/q23/p3 Because God grants His chosen messengers safety from punishment, while reverence remains. — reasons: Al-Ḥasan says secure in both lives as God's friends; al-Baghawī cites the Prophet's greater fear of God; al-Ṭabrisī connects safety to avoiding grave wrong and neglected duty; al-Biqāʿī says only a wrongdoer fears a just ruler. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees divinely taught security rather than innate immunity.
  27:10/q23/p4 They do not fear a bad end with God. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī gives the alternative; al-Ālūsī says it means the hereafter, since some messengers were killed. A marginal Ashʿarī view says fear would imply distrust of assurance.
27:10/q24 Can prophets fear a bad final outcome despite assurance? [لَا يَخَافُ لَدَىَّ ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · theology] (turns on: Whether promise forecloses hidden conditions and rational possibility of sin.)
  27:10/q24/p1 Yes, divine devising or an undisclosed condition can still be feared. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī cites the Prophet's prayer about hearts, 7:99, fearful promised Companions, and protected angels; Gabriel and Michael reportedly feared Iblīs's fate. He concludes sin is legally barred but rationally possible, and God owes no act. Al-Ālūsī allows God to create certain knowledge of safety but sees no evidence that He grants it in this life.
  27:10/q24/p2 No, God has secured them from a bad end. — reasons: A marginal Ashʿarī reading says such fear would imply distrust of God; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī report the interpretation.
27:10/q25 How is prophetic infallibility understood here? [ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · theology] (turns on: Whether immunity is intrinsic, contingent, or choice preserving.)
  27:10/q25/p1 God does not create sin in prophets, though He could. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī quotes Ashʿarī doctrine in al-Mawāqif: God is not obligated to withhold sin.
  27:10/q25/p2 Knowledge creates a restraining disposition that can cease. — reasons: The philosophers say knowledge of sin's harms and obedience's merits, strengthened by revelation, forms the disposition.
  27:10/q25/p3 An innate property makes sin impossible. — reasons: Some infer security from this; al-Ālūsī says the argument fails. Al-Jurjānī says compelled abstention merits no praise and invokes prophetic humanity.
  27:10/q25/p4 A created restraint preserves choice; impossibility is legal, not rational. — reasons: Ibn al-Humām describes lack of power with a restraint that does not compel; al-Ālūsī relates Māturīdī teaching and Ibn Ḥajar's legal impossibility. Al-Jurjānī cites 18:110 and reward for choice.
27:10/q26 How is the exception after 'messengers' construed? [ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the wrongdoer is a messenger, a separate person, or part of implied clauses.)
  27:10/q26/p1 A disconnected exception about someone other than the messengers. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān prefers this because prophets are protected; al-Qurṭubī reports a sentence break and al-Burhān says it is the majority reading. Ibn Qutayba relates the next verse to Moses' former killing.
  27:10/q26/p2 A connected exception through implied clauses: others fear, except a repentant wrongdoer. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ supplies omitted others. Ibn Qutayba rejects it because nothing expressed cues the ellipsis; al-Naḥḥās calls it impossible and gives a reductio about 'I strike people except Zayd'.
  27:10/q26/p3 A connected exception allowing a minor prophetic lapse. — reasons: Reports from al-Ḥasan, Muqātil, Ibn Jurayj and al-Ḍaḥḥāk imply this. Al-Zamakhsharī lists Adam, Jonah, David, Solomon, Joseph's brothers and Moses' Copt killing, citing Moses' confession in 28:16. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports protection from grave and vile minor sins, with disagreement on others.
  27:10/q26/p4 The particle means 'and' or 'and neither'. — reasons: A group gives conjunctive illā; Abū Ḥayyān objects that conjunction includes while exception excludes. Asad renders 'and neither' and refers to 4:29.
27:10/q27 What happens to a wrongdoer who reforms? [ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · theology] (turns on: Whether the clause addresses ordinary wrongdoers or prophets.)
  27:10/q27/p1 Repentance and good action bring forgiveness and safety. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī says God forgives the repentant; Qatāda and Maymūn say there is no safety before repentance; Mujāhid describes replacing evil with good. Al-Ḥasan relates Moses' repentance after the Egyptian killing.
  27:10/q27/p2 Prophets may commit minor lapses outside delivery but need not persist. — reasons: Al-Qushayrī holds this; those denying prophetic sin place Moses' fear before prophethood. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says major and vile minor sins are excluded, other cases disputed.
27:10/q28 What inward lesson is drawn from the staff and serpent? [وَأَلْقِ عَصَاكَ تَهْتَزُّ كَأَنَّهَا جَآنٌّ · inward] (turns on: The symbol is the disciplined self, worldly support, or divine majesty.)
  27:10/q28/p1 The staff is the sanctified self released under God's command. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī sees shaking as the self's renewed life after discipline; independent action becomes a veil and trial, while divine command and love do not. Messengers subsist after annihilation.
  27:10/q28/p2 The staff is any support besides God, which appears as a serpent. — reasons: Al-Bursawī cites Najmī taʾwīl and a Mathnawī couplet rejecting reliance on anything but grace; another couplet says fantasies become visible at resurrection.
  27:10/q28/p3 The serpent is divine majesty, witnessed safely by messengers. — reasons: ʿArāʾis al-bayān says they know lordship's secrets; Najmī taʾwīl says those fleeing to God from all else are secure.
27:10/q29 How did the staff sign establish Moses' commission? [وَأَلْقِ عَصَاكَ ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · interpretation]
  27:10/q29/p1 It proved the divine speaker and prepared him to face Pharaoh. — reasons: Al-Qushayrī says God's informing could give necessary or inferential knowledge, verified by staff and white hand; al-Qurṭubī says each prophet needs a personal sign. Iṣlāḥī says appointment is unexpected, accepted anxiously after conviction; the staff becomes like a sword against enemies.
