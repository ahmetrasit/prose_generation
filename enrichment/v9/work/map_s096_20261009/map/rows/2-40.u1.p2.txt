2:40/q27 What governs iyyāya? [وَإِيَّٰىَ فَٱرْهَبُونِ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the governor is elided, spoken after rearrangement, or nominal.)
  2:40/q27/p1 An elided imperative governs the detached accusative. — reasons: Zajjaj, Wahidi, Jishumi and others say the overt verb already has its attached object; Samin prefers a postposed estimate; Burhan places this in the general elided-governor category and compares other constructions.
  2:40/q27/p2 The construction may be nominal or governed by the overt verb after rearrangement. — reasons: Qurtubi allows an understood 'I am your Lord'; Abu Hayyan and Samin derive an attention-command structure with object repetition.
  2:40/q27/p3 A raised-topic analysis supplies omitted predication. — reasons: Tabrisi compares Sibawayh's verse and 5:38 and 24:2.
2:40/q28 What does fa do in fa-rhabūn? [فَٱرْهَبُونِ · grammar] (turns on: Whether fa is conditional, coordinate or pleonastic.)
  2:40/q28/p1 It marks an implied condition: if anything is feared, fear Me. — reasons: Baydawi and Bursevi make it explicit; Ibn Ashur calls it universal and cites 74:3; Abu Hayyan discusses an elided attention command.
  2:40/q28/p2 It coordinates an elided command with the overt command. — reasons: Al-Miftah proposes continued or heightened fear; Alusi objects to simple coordination of two particles but admits a refined elliptical account; later scholars prefer it.
  2:40/q28/p3 It is extra. — reasons: Samin reports this analysis.
2:40/q29 How is the final yāʾ read? [فَٱرْهَبُونِ · readings] (turns on: Codex spelling, pause practice, dialect and transmission.)
  2:40/q29/p1 Most omit it at verse end, even in connection. — reasons: Zajjaj and Wahidi cite cadence; Jishumi cites codex and ease; Ibn Ashur invokes Hudhayl dialect, pause treatment and Sibawayh; Tabrisi notes nūn kasra.
  2:40/q29/p2 Yaʿqūb and some others retain it. — reasons: Nahhas and Ibn Atiyya name Ibn Abi Ishaq; Tabrisi names Ibn Kathir in connection; Nashr and Ibn Ashur report Ya'qub in pause and connection, matching Hijazi usage.
2:40/q30 What inward meanings are given to fear? [وَإِيَّٰىَ فَٱرْهَبُونِ · inward] (turns on: Punishment, thoughts or divine majesty as the object.)
  2:40/q30/p1 Fear is inner light and certainty reached by striving. — reasons: Tustari joins piety, tawhid, heart sight and trust; nearness excludes indwelling or union; certainty affects the Resurrection scale.
  2:40/q30/p2 Awe guards the heart's thoughts and belongs to knowledge. — reasons: Sulami defines inner apprehension; Sahl contrasts awe in certainty with piety under divine stratagem.
  2:40/q30/p3 Punishment fear is temporary; awe of majesty endures. — reasons: Razi's gnostics, Abu Hayyan and Alusi distinguish outward practitioners and people of the heart.
  2:40/q30/p4 Fear now brings security on Judgment Day. — reasons: Razi reports the saying that God does not combine two fears or securities; greater present fear yields greater future safety.
2:40/q31 What obligations and theological principles does the verse establish? [* · theology] (turns on: The basis of reciprocal promise and human duty.)
  2:40/q31/p1 Gratitude, covenant fidelity and fear are duties. — reasons: Tabrisi calls concealing favor a grave sin; Razi says favor and prior pledge aggravate breach; Baydawi and Bursevi summarize promise and threat.
  2:40/q31/p2 Human acts are real. — reasons: Tabrisi argues denial would void covenant, command, promise, threat, messenger and scripture.
  2:40/q31/p3 God's promised reward must occur because His report is truthful. — reasons: Razi rejects Mu'tazili rational indebtedness and his Ash'ari school's bare denial as complete accounts, preferring truthful promise.
  2:40/q31/p4 Only God deserves praise and fear as creator. — reasons: Razi says idols cannot benefit or harm (26:72-73 etc); Qushayri says a powerless being is not feared.
  2:40/q31/p5 Muhammad's mission addresses Israelites too. — reasons: Razi draws this from the passage; Ibn Kathir calls them to Islam.
2:40/q32 How does the address fit the surrounding passage? [* · links] (turns on: How the universal call gives way to ancestral history.)
  2:40/q32/p1 It turns from general proofs and Adam to Israel's favors and duties. — reasons: Tabari says Jewish knowledge of ancestral reports confirms revelation; Razi charts invitation, inducement, warning and detailed favors; Tabrisi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi and Kur'an Yolu explain the turn to the People of Scripture. Tabatabai counts twelve episodes of rescue, Sinai, calf, thunderbolt and revival, showing care despite ingratitude.
  2:40/q32/p2 Israel is led from favor to Benefactor; Muhammad's community from Benefactor to favor. — reasons: Sulami, Razi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Bursevi compare 2:152.
  2:40/q32/p3 It pairs encouragement with warning and closes in submission. — reasons: Ibn Kathir and Abu Hayyan describe the sequence of promise, faith, bodily/financial duties and final fear.
2:40/q33 What earlier-scripture evidence points to Muhammad? [وَأَوْفُوا۟ بِعَهْدِىٓ · links] (turns on: How explicit and identifiable the predictions were.)
  2:40/q33/p1 Prior scriptures are read as promising Muhammad. — reasons: Razi adduces Deuteronomy's prophet from Israel's brothers, Paran, Habakkuk, Isaiah's city, Ishmael's great nation, Abraham's prayer, Jesus' Ahmad and Paraclete announcements, and Daniel's stone kingdom. He replies to objections about 'among you,' Paran as fire, Jerusalem and the Messiah; Ibn Razin and Christian transmissions give variants.
  2:40/q33/p2 The predictions may have been obscure or known only to a few. — reasons: Razi says explicit public time and place would be hard to hide, prompting some to favor a general covenant; defenders say the details were not universally plain.
2:40/q34 How does covenant language illuminate 'I seek refuge in God'? [* · links]
  2:40/q34/p1 Seeking refuge is a prayer for protection under reciprocal servitude and lordship. — reasons: Razi says the declarative form asks protection, and discusses the present/future imperfect, first-person self-narration and bāʾ attachment.
2:40/q35 What wider conduct is drawn from the command passage? [* · interpretation]
  2:40/q35/p1 Keeping commands requires abstaining from hidden and public wrongs and excess. — reasons: Biqai transmits Harali on greed, usury, spying, mockery, sexual misconduct and pride; worship without abstinence is medicine without diet. The Qur'an can guide or testify against; lawful breadth permits gratitude and restrictions cultivate patience.
  2:40/q35/p2 An unattributed dialogue depicts temptations opposed to obedience. — reasons: Durr lists corrupt book, recitation, food, drink, house, snares, caller and market.
2:40/q36 What does divine speech allow commentators to say? [* · theology] (turns on: Whether the common idiom is permissible.)
  2:40/q36/p1 Arabic idiom permits 'God narrated' when a verse or expression is the subject. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya cites primordial covenant wording, Abu Amir's verse, Umm Salama, 'God knows' and al-Ajjaj; he avoids the idiom when possible but defends it. Abu Ali questions one poetic proof as possibly erroneous.
  2:40/q36/p2 Some legal theorists prohibit such divine attributions. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya reports and answers the objection.
