[JISHUMI:v1p215/r3] JISHUMI, d. 494 · حمزة | reports | Ḥamza reads the hāʾ in ʿalayhim, ilayhim, and ladayhim with ḍamma throughout the Qur’an.
[JISHUMI:v1p215/r4] JISHUMI, d. 494 · يعقوب | reports | Yaʿqūb gives ḍamma to each hāʾ preceded by a vowelless yāʾ, including forms such as ʿalayhim and fīhim, and reportedly also where that yāʾ drops.
[JISHUMI:v1p215/r5] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | reports | Readers vary between kasra and ḍamma on the hāʾ, and between a vowelless, kasra, or ḍamma mīm of the plural, especially before a following hamzat al-waṣl; the author lists each school’s distributions.
[JISHUMI:v1p215/r6] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | holds | Ḍamma on hāʾ restores its underlying form; kasra is influenced by the preceding yāʾ, avoiding a heavy shift from ḍamma beside yāʾ, while ḍamma on mīm restores its base and appended wāw lengthens it.
[JISHUMI:v1p215/r7] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | holds | Inʿām, iḥsān, and ifḍāl are near-synonyms, though one may act beneficently toward oneself but cannot bless oneself; niʿma derives from softness and denotes good intended benefit.
[JISHUMI:v1p215/r8] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | holds | God blesses both believers and unbelievers; His first favor to a servant is creating that person alive for benefit.

[BAGHAWI:1:7/r16] BAGHAWI, d. 516 · author | reports | The remaining readers use a ḍamma on mīm and kasra on hāʾ throughout, owing to the yāʾ or preceding kasra and the mīm's original ḍamma.
