[ELMALILI:4:105-106/r19] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | In the occasion of revelation, the traitors are Tuʿma and his helpers; generally the wording applies to unjust and immoral people like them.
[ELMALILI:4:105-106/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The Prophet is to settle every doctrinal or practical dispute by God's truth and absolute revelation, giving everyone equally the right they deserve.
[ELMALILI:4:105-106/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The book's central purpose is to establish a basis for judging; the Prophet's distinctive task is to use revelation and its knowledge as the sole standard where revelation has spoken.
[ELMALILI:4:105-106/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Other proofs are only outward appearances, whereas revelation is necessary knowledge corresponding to the truth's essence.
[ELMALILI:4:105-106/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | A judge must pursue truth rather than personal preference and must never decide against a right he knows with certainty; the verse validates knowledge only when it is witnessed certainty, not opinion or ijtihād.
[ELMALILI:4:105-106/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Hazret-i Ömer | reports | Umar warns people not to call their own opinion something God showed them: that wording denotes revelation particular to the Prophet, while their opinions remain conjecture.
[ELMALILI:4:105-106/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Judges cannot rule from mere opinion or conviction; they must investigate evidentiary means and follow what those proofs establish.
[ELMALILI:4:105-106/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Because the goal is to reach the truth, a judge may never rule against a fact known through witnessed certainty.
[ELMALILI:4:105-106/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Do not defend traitors or feud against innocent people on their behalf, even if the innocent belong to another community; do not act as their lawyer.

[TABATABAI:4:105-106/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Verse 4:105 applies in a particular case what 2:213 states generally: God sent the Book to settle human disputes. | mentions 2:213
[TABATABAI:4:105-106/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Unlike the general statement in 2:213, this verse grants the Messenger the right to judge and makes his own judgment authoritative. | mentions 2:213
[TABATABAI:4:105-106/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Judging a concrete dispute requires the judge's own determination of which general rule applies; knowing the laws and rights alone does not settle the case.
[TABATABAI:4:105-106/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The phrase means God formed the Prophet's judgment and disclosed the ruling to him, not merely that He taught him general laws, as some have suggested.
[TABATABAI:4:105-106/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | God taught the Prophet the Book's rules and wisdom, then added the judgment He gave him so the Prophet could adjudicate and end disputes.
[TABATABAI:4:105-106/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The prohibition is coordinated with the preceding declarative sentence, which functions as a command: judge among them and do not advocate for traitors.
[TABATABAI:4:105-106/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | A khasim is someone who defends a claim or its equivalent.
[TABATABAI:4:105-106/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The Prophet is forbidden to defend traitors against people claiming their rights, thereby voiding the rightful party's claim.
[TABATABAI:4:105-106/r9] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | prefers | The context suggests betrayal means any wrongful violation of another's rights, not only breach of entrusted deposits, though a specific case could sometimes be attached to a general rule.
[TABATABAI:4:105-109/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The passage forms one unit urging judicial justice and forbidding judges to favor wrongdoers over the rightful, while teaching that religion is a lived truth rather than a name.
[TABATABAI:4:105-109/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Although betrayal often means breach of a trust, here the context points to theft and similar acts; because believers are like one self, violating another believer's property betrays the community.
[TABATABAI:4:105-109/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | His reconstruction is that some people stole, accused an innocent person, and pressed the Prophet to judge for the thief and against the accused.
[TABATABAI:4:105-109/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The passage fits the reported occasion about Abu Tuʿmah ibn al-Abyraq's theft, though al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cautions that most occasion reports apply remembered stories to verses they resemble.
[TABATABAI:4:105-109/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The verses establish the binding authority of the Prophet's judicial decisions.
