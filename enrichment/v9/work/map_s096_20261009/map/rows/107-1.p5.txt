[ELMALILI:107:1/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Mukātil | reports | Muqātil reports that the verse was revealed about ʿĀṣ b. Wāʾil al-Sahmī, who denied the Resurrection and acted immorally.

[ELMALILI:107:1/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | In the orphan account, Quraysh mocked Abū Jahl for giving in; he said two spears appeared beside the Prophet and he feared being struck if he refused.
[ELMALILI:107:1/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İbni Abbas | reports | One report from Ibn ʿAbbās says the verse concerns a hypocrite who is both miserly and ostentatious.
[ELMALILI:107:1/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The sūra may have been revealed for one or all of these people, but its ruling applies to everyone like them.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:1/r1] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | prefers | Reports differ on dating: some call the whole surah Meccan, while others call its first three verses Meccan and the rest Medinan; Qutb calls the mixed account likelier.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:1/r2] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | Although Qutb leans toward a wholly Medinan setting because hypocrisy and ostentation were unfamiliar in Mecca, he allows later verses to have been appended for thematic continuity.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:1/r3] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | The seven short verses overturn conventional ideas of faith and disbelief and reveal the creed’s mercy and immense good for humanity.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:1/r4] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | Religion is not outward forms or ritual; worship matters only when sincere devotion produces good deeds and sound social conduct.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:1/r5] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | Religion is an integrated way of life whose worship, rites, and personal and social duties purify hearts and lead people to cooperation, care, and flourishing.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:1/r6] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | A verbal claim to Islam and performance of prayer or other rites do not prove faith; its signs must appear in the person’s conduct.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:1/r7] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | Faith, once settled in the heart, immediately moves a person to righteous action; failure to move shows that faith is absent.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:1/r8] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | The opening question summons anyone able to see to identify the person who denies the Judgment.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:2/r1] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | The Qur’an answers who denies the Judgment by pointing to the person who shoves the orphan and refuses care for the poor, surprising under conventional definitions of faith.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:3/r3] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | True assent transforms the heart and prompts kindness and care for fellow humans who need protection.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:3/r4] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | God asks for deeds that confirm words; words without confirming action are worthless before Him.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:3/r5] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | These opening verses state with unusual clarity the spirit of the creed and the nature of the religion.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:3/r6] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | Qutb declines to debate juristic boundaries of faith and Islam: those govern legal transactions, while this surah states God’s measure of the underlying reality.
[IBNASHUR:107:1/r1] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | reports | The surah is commonly called al-Māʿūn because that word occurs uniquely here; other recorded names include Araʾayta, used in some commentaries, a fifth-century Qayrawān muṣḥaf, and al-Bukhārī.
[IBNASHUR:107:1/r10] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Adding the demonstrative and relative clause after fāʾ heightens suspense, then lets the description strike and firmly occupy the listener’s attention.
[IBNASHUR:107:1/r11] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The expected plain wording would directly coordinate the denial with pushing the orphan and failing to urge feeding the poor.
[IBNASHUR:107:1/r12] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The demonstrative singles out the denier for full recognition and inspection, or treats him as so obvious that he can be pointed at.
[IBNASHUR:107:1/r13] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The fāʾ links the second attribute to the first and makes the combined attributes support the judgment; denial, orphan abuse, and neglect of feeding are joined as one person’s qualities.
[IBNASHUR:107:1/r14] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | This makes resurrection denial ugly through its vices and opposition to moral responsibility, warning Muslims away from either trait as characteristic of those who reject recompense.
[IBNASHUR:107:1/r15] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The imperfect verbs “denies,” “pushes,” and “urges” convey that the conduct is repeated and continuous.
[IBNASHUR:107:1/r16] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Belief in resurrection and recompense is the true restraint that roots good conduct until it becomes a natural habit, without needing commands or fear of enforcement.
[IBNASHUR:107:1/r17] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The vision is physical/visual and takes one object; famous deniers and their notorious deeds are portrayed as if visibly seen.
