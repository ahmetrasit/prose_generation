## THE CURRENT MAP
31:10/q01 What does the creation of the heavens entail? [خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ · interpretation]
  31:10/q01/p1 God originated the seven heavens. — reasons: Muqātil specifies seven; Ṭabrisī says God brought them into being; Bursevī adds the Throne and Footstool.
  31:10/q01/p2 The heavens exist by God's power and choice, not by necessity or nature. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says this holds whether they are spherical or flat; their place in limitless space depends on divine choice.
31:10/q02 Are the heavens flat or spherical? [ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether reports require flatness or observation and 21:33 favor circularity.)
  31:10/q02/p1 The heavens are spherical. — reasons: Al-Rāzī reports astronomers' agreement; al-Ghazālī prefers this on sensory evidence, interpreting contrary reports and citing 21:33 for circularity.
  31:10/q02/p2 The heavens are spread flat like a plate. — reasons: Al-Rāzī attributes this to most commentators.
31:10/q03 Do the heavens have pillars, and what does تَرَوْنَهَا describe? [بِغَيْرِ عَمَدٍۢ تَرَوْنَهَا · grammar] (turns on: Whether tarawnahā describes the heavens or restricts ʿamad, and what unseen support denotes.)
  31:10/q03/p1 The heavens have no pillars; people see them standing without supports. — reasons: Muqātil and al-Ḥasan read fronting. Ibn ʿAṭiyya makes tarawnahā circumstantial accusative of the heavens or an independent nominative clause, preferring this majority view. Al-Kashshāf calls visibility evidence. Ṭabrisī says physical supports would be visible, need support themselves, and cause regress. Al-Qurṭubī reports ʿAlī ibn Sulaymān and Makkī preferring a new sentence. Al-Ālūsī says vision is visual. Al-Qushayrī invokes God's decree, will and governance; al-Biqāʿī sees sheer power.
  31:10/q03/p2 The heavens have unseen pillars or supports. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid and ʿIkrima report them. Tarawnahā can qualify ʿamad in the genitive; hidden support may be God's power and will. Al-Biqāʿī sees subtle wisdom. Al-Riḍā likens joined heaven and earth to interwoven fingers, citing 51:7 and 13:2.
  31:10/q03/p3 God's special people are metaphysical supports of the heavens and world order. — reasons: Bursevī says their departure without successors near Resurrection ends visible world order; al-Ālūsī says the perfect-human claim has no Qurʾānic or Sunna basis.
31:10/q04 What does عَمَدٍ mean? [عَمَدٍۢ · meaning]
  31:10/q04/p1 Pillars or supports, the plural of ʿimād. — reasons: Al-Nashr describes a tall firm structure preventing an elevated object from leaning. Bursevī and al-Ālūsī say the plural suits multiple heavens; al-Ālūsī compares أهب/إهاب.
31:10/q05 How is عَمَدٍ read here? [عَمَدٍۢ · readings]
  31:10/q05/p1 All readers give both letters fatḥa. — reasons: Al-Nashr identifies the form as plural of ʿimād.
31:10/q06 Do the heavens occupy a place? [ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ · theology] (turns on: What counts as place.)
  31:10/q06/p1 They occupy a chosen position within limitless space without physical support. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says divine power keeps them there.
  31:10/q06/p2 The heavens as a whole have no place if place means a surface on which they rest. — reasons: Al-Rāzī reports this distinction between position in air and resting on earth.
31:10/q07 Does the verse establish that the heavens are immobile? [ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ · interpretation]
  31:10/q07/p1 It does not establish heavenly immobility. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī rejects the inference attributed to Pythagoras's followers and their further doctrines.
31:10/q08 Who is addressed by تَرَوْنَهَا and بِكُمْ? [تَرَوْنَهَا بِكُمْ · referent]
  31:10/q08/p1 The polytheists. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the direct address in both expressions.
31:10/q09 What are رَوَٰسِىَ? [رَوَٰسِىَ · meaning]
  31:10/q09/p1 Firmly fixed mountains. — reasons: Bursevī derives rawāsī from rāsiya and rasa, become fixed; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī add loftiness. Al-Qushayrī recognizes this outward meaning.
31:10/q10 What does أَلْقَىٰ mean? [وَأَلْقَىٰ · meaning]
  31:10/q10/p1 Cast or place where something can be found and seen; later, simply place. — reasons: Bursevī explains the lexical development.
31:10/q11 Why describe mountains as cast into the earth? [وَأَلْقَىٰ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ رَوَٰسِىَ · rhetoric]
  31:10/q11/p1 Their casting makes even vast mountains easy before God's greatness. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī remarks that familiar mountains are below; Bursevī compares tossing pebbles and cites God's command 'Be'.
31:10/q12 What does أَن تَمِيدَ بِكُمْ mean, and how is it construed? [أَن تَمِيدَ بِكُمْ · grammar]
  31:10/q12/p1 The mountains were placed lest earth sway, shake, or move with inhabitants. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya defines mayd as side-to-side movement; Bursevī says it describes a great body's motion and bāʾ makes it transitive. Al-Ṭabarī cites anonymous rajaz 'أبى أن يزال ملهبا', glossed lā yazāl. Ṭabrisī and al-Rāzī allow aversion or purpose; al-Qurṭubī calls it accusative aversion and reports the Kūfan 'lest'. Al-Naqqāsh says move away; Yaḥyā says move with. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 16:15.
31:10/q13 How do the mountains stabilize the earth, and why does that matter? [رَوَٰسِىَ أَن تَمِيدَ بِكُمْ · interpretation]
  31:10/q13/p1 They anchor earth so creatures can live and grow on stable ground. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī says they stop sideward shifts; Qatāda says creatures otherwise lack a settled surface. Ibn Kathīr says earth lies over water. Al-Rāzī says heaviness resists water and winds, while shifting land defeats crops, travel and grazing; seeds need stable ground. Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursevī say earth's similar parts would change positions. Al-Ālūsī adds innumerable other purposes, waters and winds, and reports theories of minute center-of-gravity shifts from moving heavy bodies or large seas formed by water and windblown sand. Bursevī quotes Saʿdī's water-and-pegs poem; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ sees a direct earthquake link.
31:10/q14 What happened when earth first moved and God fixed it? [وَأَلْقَىٰ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ رَوَٰسِىَ · reports] (turns on: The number of mountains and sequence of stronger things differ.)
  31:10/q14/p1 Mountains anchored the moving earth; angels learned stronger things ending with a human. — reasons: Yaḥyā's al-Ḥasan report gives mountain, iron, fire, water, wind, human. Al-Māwardī's al-Ḥasan report has angels fear no creature could survive and names wind and Adam's child after mountains, omitting middle links. Bursevī notes great prior upheaval.
  31:10/q14/p2 Nineteen mountains branching through earth stabilized it like pegs. — reasons: Al-Māwardī likens the rocking to a ship; Bursevī cites al-Ḍaḥḥāk through al-Muwaḍḍiḥ, naming Qāf, Abū Qubays, Jūdī, Lebanon and Sinai.
  31:10/q14/p3 Some reports count more than nineteen mountains. — reasons: Bursevī refers to his discussion under al-Ḥijr.
31:10/q15 What inward meaning do the fixed mountains have? [رَوَٰسِىَ · inward] (turns on: Different inward analogies.)
  31:10/q15/p1 They are spiritual substitutes and pillars aiding creation and warding off calamity. — reasons: Al-Qushayrī sets this beside the outward sense.
  31:10/q15/p2 They are the earth's bones and veins in a cosmic body. — reasons: Bursevī reports some saying bones and veins; Sufis make sun and moon its eyes and stars lights in subtle veins, known by unveiling rather than philosophy.
31:10/q16 Is the earth spherical? [ٱلْأَرْضِ · interpretation]
  31:10/q16/p1 Earth is spherical. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī accepts al-Ghazālī's view, mentions Ibn ʿAbbās and philosophers, and cites sense and inference.
31:10/q17 What does بَثَّ mean? [وَبَثَّ · meaning]
  31:10/q17/p1 Create, bring into view, scatter, and spread. — reasons: Yaḥyā and al-Suddī gloss create; al-Kalbī extends; al-Māwardī, al-Nasafī and Bursevī emphasize dispersal. Bursevī compares wind scattering dust and a soul disclosing grief; al-Ālūsī cites 56:6 and 101:4.
31:10/q18 What does دَآبَّةٍ encompass? [دَآبَّةٍۢ · meaning]
  31:10/q18/p1 Every kind of moving animal or creature on earth. — reasons: Al-Māwardī derives the name from motion; Bursevī says dabb/dabīb is light walking, especially insects but used generally. Ibn Kathīr notes forms and colors known only to God; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ specifies crawlers; Study Quran includes all animals.
31:10/q19 What does spreading animals show or accomplish? [وَبَثَّ فِيهَا مِن كُلِّ دَآبَّةٍۢ · interpretation] (turns on: Provision, knowledge, and manifestation are emphasized.)
  31:10/q19/p1 It manifests mercy and provision. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī places dispersal after stable settlement; al-Rāzī says stability enables travel and grazing; Study Quran reports al-Ṭabarī on nourishment with 2:164; Ibn ʿĀshūr lists food, milk, wool, hides, transport and sea creatures.
  31:10/q19/p2 It displays God's knowledge and power. — reasons: Study Quran reports Ibn Kathīr; Ibn Kathīr says only the Creator knows animal variety.
  31:10/q19/p3 It reveals God bringing absent things into existence. — reasons: Bursevī draws this implication from bathth.
31:10/q20 What are the مَآءً from ٱلسَّمَآءِ? [وَأَنزَلْنَا مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ مَآءًۭ · meaning]
  31:10/q20/p1 Rain descending from above or from clouds. — reasons: Bursevī says heaven is what is above and shades; al-Ālūsī says upper direction, allowing a figurative canopy only if observation demands it. Al-Biqāʿī says no creature can produce rain.
31:10/q21 Why shift to first-person plural in أَنزَلْنَا and أَنۢبَتْنَا? [وَأَنزَلْنَا فَأَنۢبَتْنَا · rhetoric] (turns on: Emphasis, divine agency, and pronoun limits are given.)
  31:10/q21/p1 It emphasizes divine care in recurring rain and growth. — reasons: Al-Rāzī calls this refreshing iltifāt, illustrating with speakers and Bakr. Bursevī calls the plural majestic; al-Ālūsī stresses recurrence sustaining animals; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls blessing widely felt; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ sees concern; Elmalılı relates it to majesty, wisdom and oneness.
