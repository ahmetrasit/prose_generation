[ALUSI-FULL:v22p86/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الحليمي | reports | The prayer-like salutation is what al-Halimi says is meant when he describes blessing as having the meaning of salutation; it requests that God confer peace and therefore carries a form of exaltation.

[ALUSI-FULL:v22p86/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · القرطبي | reports | A view taken by al-Suyuti and others, reported from the majority by Qadi Iyad and called correct by al-Qurtubi, permits asking mercy only when joined to blessing or salutation; al-Ghazali likewise forbids an unaccompanied request.
[ALUSI-FULL:v22p86/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الغزالي | reports | Al-Ghazali cites 24:63 and argues that, although blessing can mean mercy, prophets are distinguished by the honorific blessing, which for them is more specific than ordinary mercy. | mentions 24:63
[ALUSI-FULL:v22p87/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · ابن حجر الهيثمي | prefers | Ibn Hajar al-Haytami says the Bedouin's prayer for mercy, approved by the Prophet, shows that mercy may be requested without accompanying blessing or salutation; this specific approval takes precedence over the verse's general implication.
[ALUSI-FULL:v22p87/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · ابن حجر الهيثمي | reports | Ibn Hajar interprets those who say such a request is impermissible as denying neutral permissibility, allowing that it is disliked or خلاف الأولى; Zayn al-Din says they agree one should not initiate the formula may God have mercy on him, a recommendation al-Alusi endorses.
[ALUSI-FULL:v22p87/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الطحطاوي | reports | Al-Tahtawi advises against saying may God forgive or pardon the Prophet because it may imply a deficiency; al-Alusi leans the same way, while noting that seeking forgiveness need not imply sin and can request higher rank, as the Prophet sought forgiveness repeatedly.

[IBNASHUR-FULL:v22p101/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Salām is a greeting meaning safety and peace; it began as mutual assurance against attack or vengeance, then became a mark of honor and kindness, as al-Nabigha’s verse illustrates.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v22p101/r7] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · النابغة | reports | Al-Nabigha’s verse uses ‘greeting and peace’ as the courtesy people exchange.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v22p101/r8] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Because salutation was already familiar, the companions did not ask its form; the command is to address peace to the Prophet, not to ask God to grant him peace as with prayer.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v22p103/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Sunni convention permits including the Prophet’s family, companions, and righteous believers after him in the blessing, but not blessing them independently; this distinguishes religious ranks rather than prohibiting it.
