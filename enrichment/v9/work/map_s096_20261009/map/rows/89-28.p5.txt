[ALUSI:89:30/r9] ALUSI, d. 1270 · عكرمة والضحاك | reports | ʿIkrima and al-Ḍaḥḥāk place the address at resurrection; on one analysis nafs means the person, Lord means God, and an implied noun supplies the station to which she returns.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p130#2/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | “Return to your Lord” can mean returning after judgment to the station of divine care and honor, where the blessed wait before each is called to account.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p130#2/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | Alternatively, it tells the soul to turn its heart away from anxious concern about its deeds and attend wholly to God; after its accepted reckoning, the words reassure it that the matter is finished.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p130#2/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Calling her “the tranquil soul” is a rhetorical prompt, like telling a famous brave person to act bravely when he hesitates; “to your Lord” adds tenderness.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p130#2/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | “Pleased” may mean pleased with God's endless gifts, or specifically relieved by an easy accounting and accepted deeds; al-Alusi says the latter is weak.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p130#2/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | rejects | A view takes the two states as pleased with God and accepted by Him, but al-Alusi questions its fit with the context.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p130#2/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Both adjectives are circumstantial accusatives; the first is apparently prospective, though some take it as simultaneous, and the second advances to the greater divine approval of 9:72. | mentions 9:72
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Mücâhid | reports | Mujahid says it is certain that God is its Lord and submits to His command in every deed.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r11] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Another formulation says the soul is certain God is its Lord, gives its heart only to Him, and has a heart that beats by His command; it is repentant and humble. | mentions 8:2, 22:34, 22:35, 39:54
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r15] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The preceding account contrasts the ignorant, worldly commanding soul and belatedly self-reproaching soul with the tranquil soul that understood truth in time. | mentions 89:24
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r16] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Exegetes differ on whether this address comes at death, resurrection, or after the reckoning; the author considers the last timing most apparent from the context. | mentions 89:24
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r17] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | On the preferred timing, returning means leaving the place of reckoning for the place God graciously commands, or turning the heart from concern over acceptance to God Himself.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r18] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The pleased soul is content with the Lord who brought it to eternal bliss; the accepted soul is approved by God for its sound heart and good striving and reaches divine pleasure.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The tranquil soul chooses knowledge of God for God alone and is attached to Him in love, desire, abiding, and speech.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | A tranquil soul is defined by rising through unstable causes to know the First Source, relying only on God, and obeying Him with exclusive sincerity. | mentions 2:112, 2:207
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | This definition entails submission to God with excellence, certainty of release from fear and sorrow, and dedicating the self to seek only God’s pleasure. | mentions 2:112, 2:207
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#2/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | True freedom means escaping the animal ego, self-reproach, and bondage to anything besides God, then doing every good one can with life and wealth in God’s path. | mentions 90:8
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#3/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İkrime | reports | Ikrima and Dahhak report that the address and command to return occur at resurrection; on this reading the soul returns to its Lord for eternal life.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#3/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | prefers | Though Abu Salih’s reading seems apparent, the context favors the address after resurrection and completed reckoning; the reports can fit by treating each death as a person’s resurrection or the commands together as a unit. | mentions 89:24
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#3/r11] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Zeyd b. Eslem | reports | Zayd ibn Aslam reports that the tranquil soul is given this address at death, resurrection, and the gathering, and is promised Paradise.

[ELMALILI:89:27-30#3/r13] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The author also considers a direct, legislative divine address at the surah’s conclusion plausible; separation from what precedes highlights that the tranquil souls themselves should hear it.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#3/r14] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | prefers | Some exegetes take the address as applying to the tranquil soul in worldly life at all times; the author finds this clearer and more useful.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#3/r15] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | On the worldly reading, returning is a voluntary return to God and His decree in every matter, accompanied by contentment in prosperity and adversity.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#3/r16] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Explicit contentment is difficult even for the tranquil soul and is the perfection of the content soul; divine acceptance depends on that contentment.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#3/r19] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The life of a tranquil soul among the righteous is like the ten nights before Eid al-Adha, and its entry into Paradise is the dawn of the greatest festival of bliss.
[ELMALILI:89:27-30#3/r20] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The commentator prays that he and his readers return to God content and accepted, join His sincere servants, and reach Paradise and the vision of God.
