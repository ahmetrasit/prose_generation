 Qur'an teaching mentioned before human creation? [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the first place marks the Qur'an's rank as a human favor or an ordering of celestial matters before earthly ones.)
  55:1/q09/p1 The Qur'an or religion is the greatest favor and the purpose for which humanity was created. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī says the purpose of knowing revelation and scripture is presented before human creation. Al-Rāzī says the following 'created humanity, taught him expression' explains how the greatest gift is received. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Kashshāf's ranking of revelation before creation and expression.
  55:1/q09/p2 The sequence names heavenly gifts first and then pairs them with earthly counterparts. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says teaching the Qur'an to angels above precedes teaching humans expression below; he extends the pattern to sun and moon versus prostrating stars and trees (75:9; 55:6), and raised heaven versus placed earth (55:7; 55:10).
55:1/q10 What does 'taught the Qur'an' mean here? [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the verb denotes instruction or making a revelatory sign.)
  55:1/q10/p1 God imparted knowledge of the Qur'an and made its learning possible. — reasons: Al-Rāzī defines teaching as imparting knowledge and says 3:7 poses no conflict: the learned may be joined to God or may learn difficult passages only within human capacity; on a stop at 'except God,' people know interpretation through His teaching, not independent intellect. Ibn Kathīr says God made memorization and understanding easy. Al-Rāzī also reports a metaphorical reading, 'made learnable,' comparing 54:17 and a person funding another's education.
  55:1/q10/p2 God made the Qur'an a sign and miracle proving prophethood. — reasons: Al-Rāzī compares its unique knowledge with the moon-splitting sign of 54:1; Kur'an Yolu adds that it proves Muhammad's prophethood and holds signs for reflective readers.
55:1/q11 Who is understood as the recipient of Qur'an teaching? [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ · referent] (turns on: The unstated second object of 'taught' may be general humanity, angels, or Muhammad.)
  55:1/q11/p1 Humanity generally receives the teaching. — reasons: Al-Rāzī prefers this because it completes the sūrah's account of universal favors; omission of the second object stresses general rather than one person's instruction, as when one says someone feeds people. Iṣlāḥī says human speech equips people to understand and teach each other through revelation.
  55:1/q11/p2 Gabriel and other angels were taught before the Qur'an was sent to Muhammad. — reasons: Al-Rāzī cites 26:193–94 for its descent and 56:77–80 for the preserved Qur'an touched only by the purified before descent; this supports his celestial-before-earthly ordering.
  55:1/q11/p3 Muhammad is the recipient. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says this underscores that the Qur'an is God's speech rather than Muhammad's.
55:1/q12 Why do the first clauses lack conjunctions? [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ · rhetoric]
  55:1/q12/p1 Each opening clause independently enumerates a favor and rebukes deniers; conjunctions return when clauses fit together. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī makes the verbs successive predicates of al-Raḥmān and illustrates their force by recalling gifts to Zayd after poverty, lowliness, and scarcity before asking which kindness could be denied.
55:1/q13 Who can 'the human' in the following creation verse mean? [* · referent] (turns on: Whether the singular 'human' denotes the species, its first member, or the Prophet.)
  55:1/q13/p1 Humanity as a kind. — reasons: Al-Nasafī lists this among three possible referents.
  55:1/q13/p2 Adam. — reasons: Al-Nasafī lists this among three possible referents.
  55:1/q13/p3 Muhammad. — reasons: Al-Nasafī lists this among three possible referents.
55:1/q14 Is the sūrah Meccan or Medinan? [* · sciences] (turns on: Conflicting transmitted classifications and occasions are weighed against reports of pre-proclamation recitation and the sūrah's consistent tone.)
  55:1/q14/p1 The whole sūrah is Meccan. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Asad, and Study Quran report a majority or most authorities; Ibn Kathīr holds this. Al-Suyūṭī calls it correct, invoking reports of the jinn's reply and a good-chain report from Asmāʾ hearing the Prophet recite near the Kaʿba before public proclamation; al-Ḥākim grades the jinn report sound on the two Shaykhs' condition. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers an entirely Meccan sūrah and cites Ibn Masʿūd's codex. Iṣlāḥī rejects Medinan attribution because no verse looks Medinan and the tone and rhythm are uniform; the opponents demanding a punishment sign are Meccan.
  55:1/q14/p2 The whole sūrah is Medinan. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān attributes it to Ibn Masʿūd and reports Ibn ʿAbbās both ways; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Ibn ʿAbbās linking it to Suhayl's refusal of the basmala at Ḥudaybiyya and Ibn Masʿūd's attribution. Asad says Zamakhsharī and Suyūṭī assign it to Medina, though the Suyūṭī note here prefers Mecca. Iṣlāḥī and Study Quran report this minority view.
  55:1/q14/p3 The sūrah may have verses from both periods, including a reported Medinan exception at verse 29. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān reports Ibn ʿAbbās's mixed accounts and an exception for 55:29. Asad says al-Bayḍāwī leaves open portions before and after migration. Bursawī records differing Meccan and Medinan reports without deciding.
55:1/q15 Where is the sūrah placed in revelation chronology? [* · sciences] (turns on: Placement depends on whether the sūrah is classified as Meccan or Medinan and on the dating of al-Raʿd.)
  55:1/q15/p1 On a Meccan chronology it is forty-third, after al-Furqān and before Fāṭir, al-Ḥijr, and al-Naḥl. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers this order; denial of the name in 25:60 conditionally places it after al-Furqān, while the report of recitation before public preaching places it before al-Ḥijr. Al-Suyūṭī's Asmāʾ report also places it before al-Ḥijr.
  55:1/q15/p2 On a Medinan chronology it is ninety-eighth, after al-Raʿd and before al-Insān. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Jaʿbarī's ranking. Asad says placement just after sūrah 13 is unhelpful because al-Raʿd itself cannot be dated securely; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Meccan/Medinan disagreement prevents firm chronology.
55:1/q16 Is al-Raḥmān counted as a verse by itself, and how many verses result? [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ · sciences] (turns on: Regional verse-ending conventions, especially whether the opening name receives its own ayah mark.)
  55:1/q16/p1 Kufan readers count al-Raḥmān as a separate verse and total seventy-eight. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says Ḥafṣ copies mark an ayah after the name; he also gives Syria seventy-eight. Al-Samīn links separate counting to the omitted-subject or omitted-predicate parses. Ṭabrisī's ellipsis preserves the verse-ending pattern. Al-Rāzī regards the separate-verse parse as weaker.
  55:1/q16/p2 The opening belongs with the following clause; other regional counts yield seventy-seven or seventy-six verses. — reasons: Al-Samīn ties the joined construction to making 'taught the Qur'an' its predicate. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Medina and Mecca at seventy-seven and Basra at seventy-six; he also says most verse counters treat the opening as one verse. Bursawī reports differing totals without numbers.
55:1/q17 Where does this sūrah stand in the Mufaṣṣal division? [* · sciences]
  55:1/q17/p1 It begins the Mufaṣṣal, or its ninth division, in Ibn Masʿūd's arrangement. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr reports Ibn Masʿūd's statement that the Prophet paired the opening sūrahs of the Mufaṣṣal two by two. Al-Zarkashī reports Ibn al-Sayyid's attribution to Ibn Masʿūd's codex and adds that Aḥmad transmitted the arrangement; Ibn ʿĀshūr also cites that codex.
55:1/q18 Why is the sūrah called 'The Bride of the Qur'an'? [* · sciences]
  55:1/q18/p1 The title evokes its many adornments and blessings. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī lists garments, jewels, crowns, beauty, and perfection; Bursawī reports the title. Study Quran says Muslims use it after a well-known but unauthenticated hadith.
55:1/q19 What is reported about the Prophet's recitation of the sūrah and the jinn's response? [* · reports] (turns on: The reports differ in Companion transmitter and whether the Prophet himself read or heard the sūrah; their individual transmission routes receive different assessments.)
  55:1/q19/p1 Jābir reports the Prophet recited the entire sūrah to silent Companions and praised the jinn's better reply to its refrain. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr records al-Tirmidhī calling the report gharīb and known only through al-Walīd ibn Muslim from Zuhayr ibn Muḥammad; Aḥmad did not recognize it and objected to Syrian transmission from Zuhayr; al-Bazzār says it is known only through that route. Al-Suyūṭī also cites al-Tirmidhī and al-Ḥākim's jinn-response reports and says al-Ḥākim grades a report sound on the two Shaykhs' condition.
  55:1/q19/p2 Ibn ʿUmar reports a recitation by or before the Prophet and the same comparison with the jinn. — reasons: The report varies over whether the Prophet read or heard it; Ibn Kathīr reports al-Bazzār saying it is known as prophetic only through this chain.
55:1/q20 How does this opening connect to the preceding sūrah? [* · links] (turns on: The sources emphasize either the contrast of power and mercy or the progression from concise warning to fuller instruction.)
  55:1/q20/p1 Al-Qamar's majesty, power, punishment, and warning give way to al-Raḥmān's mercy, Qur'anic teaching, favors, and their refrain. — reasons: Al-Rāzī contrasts the split moon (54:1) with the Qur'an healing sinful hearts and the previous repeated punishment warning (54:16) with the favors refrain (55:13); he says 54:55's 'Sovereign, Powerful' leads to a Name combining might toward the wicked with mercy toward the righteous. Abū Ḥayyān says this sūrah expands the prior brief mention of the righteous abode and both groups' ends. Al-Biqāʿī says universal mercy completes kingship; Study Quran reports the complementary closing and opening names.
