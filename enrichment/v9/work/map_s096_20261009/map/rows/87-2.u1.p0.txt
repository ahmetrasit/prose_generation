## THE CURRENT MAP
87:2/q01 How does 'who created' attach grammatically to the preceding words? [ٱلَّذِى · grammar] (turns on: Whether the clause modifies Lord or name despite the intervening adjective and the problem of assigning creation to a name, or instead begins an implicit predication or praise.)
  87:2/q01/p1 It qualifies 'Lord' or 'Most High' as another adjective. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās says both adjectives can share one referent and compares the idiom yā Yazīd al-karīm dhū al-jumma. Abū Ḥayyān reports the possible attachment to rabb, while al-Ālūsī objects that an adjective of another noun intervenes.
  87:2/q01/p2 It qualifies 'name'; creation is predicated of the name through its named referent or creative attribute. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān reports the grammatical possibility if al-aʿlā qualifies ism. Al-Ālūsī finds a name's creating implausible and questions the proposed metonymy. Elmalılı says creation may aptly be assigned to the Creator-name as the effect of its attribute of bringing into being, citing Sunni and Sufi formulations.
  87:2/q01/p3 It stands independently as the predicate of an omitted subject or as an accusative of praise. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān says this allows al-aʿlā to qualify ism. Al-Ālūsī prefers this solution to the intervening-adjective and name-as-creator difficulties; Elmalılı also prefers treating the clause independently.
87:2/q02 What does 'created' mean here? [خَلَقَ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the verb describes origination, an initial form, or the assembly of parts before their proportioning.)
  87:2/q02/p1 God brought things into existence from nonexistence. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says God can bring about whatever He wills without difficulty. Elmalılı says the Creator, as such, transcends creatures' origination, possibility, and need for a cause.
  87:2/q02/p2 Creation can mean making an initial sketch or form that is subsequently perfected. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī cites the bird example at 3:49 for the sense of making an effigy; for a human, the initial sketch passes through stages before completion.
  87:2/q02/p3 Creating gathers the parts of a composed creature. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes gathering parts in creation from placing them proportionately through fashioning.
87:2/q03 Whom or what does God create and fashion, and in what way? [خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ · interpretation] (turns on: The unstated object of 'created' may be every creature, living beings, humanity, Adam, or composed beings; the accounts also differ over whether fashioning concerns form, useful capacities, equal rulings, understanding, worship, or stages of gestation. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the usual rule favoring a general omitted object; al-Ālūsī rejects unsupported restrictions.)
  87:2/q03/p1 God creates all things and fashions them in sound, balanced, precise order without flaw. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī glosses taswiyah as adjustment; al-Naḥḥās adds that the results are good. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, and Abū Ḥayyān see harmony as evidence of a knowing, wise maker; Ibn ʿAṭiyyah says it displays power and oneness. Al-Rāzī attributes the order to God's knowledge and power. Al-Ālūsī says each essence and attribute is formed as wisdom requires, rejects narrower objects as unsupported, and notes that the omitted object gives general scope. Al-Qurṭubī recalls his explanation at 82:7; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites precise fingertips and 91:7 and 75:38. Q 67:3–4 is cited by Study Quran for the absence of flaw. Al-Ṭabrisī says creatures are alike in sound construction, and another account says God forms things according to His will and wisdom as testimony to His oneness; al-Biqāʿī says even opposed elements are harmonized. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the omitted object is general by the usual rule when context does not specify it, adducing the majority, ʿAṭāʾ, and 20:50.
  87:2/q03/p2 God fashions every kind or creature with what it needs for its own completion, activity, benefit, and livelihood. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursawī stress perfection and sustenance; al-Nasafī adds benefit. Al-Biqāʿī says the balance of opposed elements shows knowledge, power, wisdom and free choice, and pairs striking with the hand, walking with the leg, hearing with the ear and sight with the eye. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the scorpion's bent defensive tail; Asad emphasizes advance adaptation to conditions of existence. Kuranyolu cites 15:29 for fitting form.
  87:2/q03/p3 God creates each creature in an excellent or beautiful form. — reasons: ʿAṭāʾ says creation is in its best form, though the preserved comment ends mid-sentence; Ibn ʿAbbās says God beautified what He created. Ibn Kathīr calls each creature's form the finest.
  87:2/q03/p4 God creates living beings and proportions their limbs and senses. — reasons: Al-Kalbī names hands, feet or legs, and eyes; al-Rāzī adds each animal's needed organs, tools and senses. Al-Ālūsī reports this reading but says nothing in the ellipsis requires restriction to living beings.
  87:2/q03/p5 God creates the human being with upright, balanced, beautiful stature. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj contrasts upright humanity with inverted cattle and beasts; al-Rāzī links the beautiful form to 95:4. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Zajjāj's human object, while al-Ālūsī says this restriction lacks textual support.
  87:2/q03/p6 God brings humans into being and then perfects their form. — reasons: Al-Māwardī transmits this as one possibility. Iṣlāḥī says the human sketch matures gradually; Muqātil names the womb stages of drop, clot and morsel of flesh. Study Quran describes the first human's shaping after the divine Spirit, without irregularity, with measured form and nourishment.
  87:2/q03/p7 God creates humans complete and makes each limb correspond proportionately to its counterpart. — reasons: Al-Māwardī reports the corresponding-limbs explanation separately from simple perfection.
  87:2/q03/p8 God creates Adam and perfects or fashions his form. — reasons: Al-Ḍaḥḥāk specifies Adam; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the pairing with fashioning suggests this and cites 15:29.
  87:2/q03/p9 God creates people in their fathers' loins and fashions them in their mothers' wombs. — reasons: Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī transmit this division of the two acts.
  87:2/q03/p10 God creates bodies and fashions understanding. — reasons: Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī report this distinction between bodily and intellectual formation.
  87:2/q03/p11 God creates people through beneficence and makes them equal in His rulings. — reasons: Al-Māwardī gives this as a third possibility; al-Ālūsī reports the broader assertion that creatures are alike in their rulings and perfection, with none perfected at another's expense.
  87:2/q03/p12 God creates humanity and equips it for religious obligation and worship. — reasons: Al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī give this as a human-specific explanation of proportioning.
  87:2/q03/p13 God gives humans faculties and tools enabling them to perform the actions of other animals. — reasons: Al-Rāzī records this as a second human-specific explanation.
  87:2/q03/p14 God creates the human in balanced form, with innate disposition and testimony to His lordship. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj connects human formation with its natural disposition and testimony that God is its Lord.
  87:2/q03/p15 The clause concerns composed creatures: creation collects their parts and fashioning proportionately places them. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī relates this account to the first four verses' depiction of divine governance and absolute lordship.
87:2/q04 What is the sense of 'fashioned' (sawwā) as a word? [فَسَوَّىٰ · meaning] (turns on: The word's range includes equality, adjustment, straightening, improvement, and perfection; Iṣlāḥī says the context selects perfection.)
  87:2/q04/p1 It means making proportionate, adjusted, straight, or precise. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī calls it adjustment; Jishumī says equality in form, rank, status, or any respect. Al-Ālūsī calls proportion its original sense; Ibn ʿĀshūr glosses it as straightening and precise making, citing 91:7 and 75:38. Iṣlāḥī also notes senses of improving and flattening, while choosing perfection here.
  87:2/q04/p2 In this context it means completing or perfecting what is made. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī contrasts possible senses of improving or flattening and says context selects perfecting; he also pairs an initial sketch with its later completion. Ibn ʿAṭiyyah and Qutb speak of perfection suited to the creature.
87:2/q05 What does the 'fa' between creating and fashioning express? [فَسَوَّىٰ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the ordered verbs mark a conceptual and causal sequence accompanying one act, or emphasize observable stages of human development.)
  87:2/q05/p1 It makes fashioning the consequence and chief point of creation, ordered in thought though simultaneous in occurrence. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr compares Ibn Ziyāba's poem, where a raid, plunder, and escape provide the ordered groundwork for grief; creation likewise precedes fashioning in the observer's understanding. Al-Biqāʿī says creation requires proportion for balance.
  87:2/q05/p2 The sequence reflects a gradual development from initial making to completed form. — reasons: Muqātil describes drop, clot, and morsel of flesh in the womb before proportionate formation; Iṣlāḥī says God's works unfold gradually in wisdom, and a human sketch passes through stages before completion.
87:2/q06 Do the perfect verbs describe only past creation or continuing activity? [خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ · grammar]
  87:2/q06/p1 Their perfect form is aspectual; creation continues as ongoing world-sustaining activity. — reasons: Corpus Coranicum points to pasture's recurring growth and decay in 87:4–5 and compares 53:43–49, 96:1–5, and 95:4–5.
