الراغب | reports | Al-Rāghib defines walāʾ and tawālī as close succession, extended from spatial proximity to relations such as kinship, friendship, support, and belief; wilāya can mean support or taking charge.
[TABATABAI:5:55-56#7/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · الراغب | reports | Al-Rāghib says tawallā with a direct object denotes turning toward it; with ʿan, expressed or understood, it denotes turning away. | mentions 2:144
[TABATABAI:5:55-56#7/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The concept of wilāya began with physical and temporal closeness, then extended to abstract relations because people first engage with sensible things.
[TABATABAI:5:55-56#7/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Abstract wilāya is a special closeness that grants a right to manage another’s affairs: examples include heirs, guardians, supporters, God, prophets, rulers, and other forms of authority or bond.
[TABATABAI:5:55-56#7/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The phrases about turning backs and turning away mean facing the battle’s opposite direction or directing oneself away from the intended course.
[TABATABAI:5:55-56#7/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The coordinated list under one singular wali indicates one sense of wilāya for God, the Messenger, and believers; the next verse likewise treats them together as God’s party. | mentions 5:56
[TABATABAI:5:55-56#8/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | God’s ontological wilāya permits Him to act in anything and manage creation as He wills; Qur’anic parallels describe Him as the sole guardian and one nearer than the jugular vein. | mentions 42:9, 32:4, 12:101, 42:44, 50:16, 8:24
[TABATABAI:5:55-56#8/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | God’s supporting wilāya may belong to this domain; Qur’an says He is the believers’ patron and helper, while unbelievers have no patron. | mentions 47:11, 66:4, 30:47
[TABATABAI:5:55-56#8/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | God also has wilāya over believers’ religious affairs through legislation, guidance, direction, and enabling. | mentions 2:257, 3:68, 45:19, 33:36
[TABATABAI:5:55-56#8/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | God’s wilāya in the Qur’an reduces to ontological and legislative authority, also describable as real and conventional wilāya.
[TABATABAI:5:55-56#8/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The Prophet’s distinctive wilāya is legislative: he conveys law, calls and educates the community, governs it, and judges its affairs. | mentions 33:6, 4:105
[TABATABAI:5:55-56#9/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The Prophet’s legislative authority includes guiding people to God, teaching scripture, clarifying revelation, commanding obedience, and judging by what God revealed; God does not assign him support-wilāya over the community. | mentions 42:52, 62:2, 16:44, 4:59, 33:36, 5:49
[TABATABAI:5:55-56#9/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The Prophet’s authority entails absolute obedience and is God’s legislative wilāya delegated to him, since obedience to the Messenger is obedience to God. | mentions 4:59, 33:36
[TABATABAI:5:55-56#9/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The wilāya shared by God, the Messenger, and believers is one kind: original in God and granted derivatively to the others by His permission.
[TABATABAI:5:55-56#9/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | If God’s and believers’ wilāya meant different things, the verse would distinguish them as Qur’an does by repeating īmān when it has different senses. | mentions 9:61
[TABATABAI:5:55-56/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · جماعة من مفسري القوم | reports | Some exegetes treat these verses as continuous with the bans on alliance and read wilaya as support, restricted to God, the Messenger, and true believers; they connect them to 3:68, 33:6, 8:72, and 9:71. | mentions 3:68, 33:6, 8:72, 9:71
