1:2/q06 What does the lām in lillāh express? [لِلَّهِ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the lām marks God's right, possession, explanation, or cause.)
  1:2/q06/p1 The lām marks God's entitlement to praise. — reasons: TABRISI, d. 548 (الأخفش وأبو علي الفارسي): They parse al-ḥamdu as a nominative subject with an omitted predication such as is due or is established for God; the phrase lillah supplies its meaning. TABRISI, d. 548 (ابن جني): Ibn Jinni says kasra on ḥamd and ḍamma on lillah signal a close subject-predicate bond, making the two parts sound like one word. TABRISI, d. 548 (سيبويه): The kasra of lām in the jussive verb li-yafʿal distinguishes it from emphatic lām. IBNJAWZI-NUZHA, d. 597 (بعض المفسرين): Some derive ʿālam from ʿalāma, a sign pointing to its Creator; that derivation supports taking it for all created existence. IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 (الجمهور): Most readers pronounce the lām of lillāh with kasra; Ibn ʿAbla uses ḍamma, a dialect of some Rabīʿa according to al-Farrāʾ. IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 (ابن السميفع): Ibn al-Sumayfa reads al-ḥamda with fatḥa on dāl and kasra on lām. IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 (أبو نهيك): Abū Nuhayk reads both dāl and lām in al-ḥamd lillāh with kasra. IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 (أهل النظر): The derivation of ʿālam from ʿalāma, “sign,” supports the speculative theologians' view: the world is named as a sign pointing to its Creator.
