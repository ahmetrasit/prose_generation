## THE CURRENT MAP
38:7/q01 What is the 'this' which they say they have not heard? [بِهَٰذَا · referent] (turns on: Whether the demonstrative points specifically to the oneness demanded in the preceding verses, to Muhammad's message broadly, or to the account of Arab religious origins.)
  38:7/q01/p1 Muhammad's call to worship one God, making the many gods one. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and Bursevī specify tawḥīd. Ibn ʿĀshūr ties the demonstrative to making many gods one in 38:5–6. Al-Ṭabarī's Ibn ʿAbbās report includes both worship of God alone and Muhammad's scripture; the Abū Ṭālib reports give the declaration 'There is no god but God.'
  38:7/q01/p2 What Muhammad teaches or reports from God more generally. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī calls it the Prophet's teaching; al-Ālūsī, what Muhammad is saying. Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes everything he reports from God, and al-Samin specifies the teaching of divine oneness in the proposed end-time faith.
  38:7/q01/p3 The account of the Arabs' earlier monotheist history and origins. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī says prophetic history from Noah to Abraham, Abraham and Ishmael's settlement in Arabia, and the Kaaba's original purpose challenged Quraysh's account of its ancestry; Quraysh called that account fabricated because their later history and inherited reports lacked it.
38:7/q02 How does their claim of never hearing this express their objection? [مَا سَمِعْنَا بِهَٰذَا · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether inherited silence is taken as evidence against the claim or the word 'later' itself concedes an earlier tradition.)
  38:7/q02/p1 They infer that an unheard doctrine is invented, appealing to inherited custom; the inference is unsound. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says the denial of hearing signals rejection and accusation, and can explain their previous assertion that the matter is contrived; bi-hādhā implies 'anything like this,' while repeating the demonstrative marks the claim distinctly. Al-Rāzī says treating ancestral silence as proof exposes invalid taqlīd. Bursevī calls following ancestral error ignorant and quotes a Persian warning that an apparent route to the Kaaba may lead to Turkistan. Al-Biqāʿī says reflection can disclose monotheism even without inherited testimony. Study Quran's R says their premise was that a message unheard among predecessors must be false; Iṣlāḥī says they demanded ancestral transmission of the earlier history.
  38:7/q02/p2 Their restriction to the later religion implies awareness of monotheism in the earlier Abrahamic tradition. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says Abraham and his descendants knew oneness before ʿAmr ibn Luḥayy; they also knew resurrection. He cites ʿUbayd ibn al-Abraṣ's line about the displaced Banū Asad serving until resurrection, with Ibn al-Furāt's account of Ḥujr ibn ʿAmr's campaign as its background. Reports from Ibn Masʿūd and Ibn ʿAbbās identify ʿAmr ibn ʿĀmir or ʿAmr ibn Luḥayy as the first idolater or changer of Abraham's religion; at Mecca the Prophet rejected images of Abraham and Ishmael holding divining arrows.
38:7/q03 What does milla mean here, and how is it used of pagan custom? [ٱلْمِلَّةِ · meaning]
  38:7/q03/p1 It means religion; applying it to inherited polytheist custom is figurative usage. — reasons: Ibn Zayd glosses milla as religion. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 2:120 and 12:37–38 on Jewish, Christian and Joseph's ancestral religions. Bursevī says its proper use, like dīn, is the divine law prescribed through prophets for reward and nearness; the pagan application is analogical.
38:7/q04 What can ākhira mean in this phrase and elsewhere? [ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ · meaning] (turns on: The contextual referent of a relative word meaning what comes after; the other senses are illustrated by other Qur'anic contexts.)
  38:7/q04/p1 Later or last: a thing following another of its kind. — reasons: Ibn Zayd calls it the other or later religion; al-Dāmghānī says latest prior community or the later of two punishments. Ibn al-Jawzī explains that al-ākhir has something before it, and notes uses for 'finally,' a group's rear, and a saddle's back. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls ākhira the feminine of ākhir and cites the later creation in 29:20.
  38:7/q04/p2 The Resurrection or Last Day. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī cites 2:4 and 27:3.
  38:7/q04/p3 Paradise or the lasting reward of the God-conscious. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī cites 2:102, 28:83, 43:35 and 42:20.
  38:7/q04/p4 Hell. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī cites the worshipper fearing the Hereafter while hoping for mercy in 39:9.
  38:7/q04/p5 The grave or life after death. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī cites God's making believers firm in this life and the next, 14:27.
  38:7/q04/p6 The final punishment or destruction of the Israelites. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī cites 17:7.
38:7/q05 Which religion or period is 'the later religion'? [ٱلْمِلَّةِ ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ · referent] (turns on: Whether 'later' points to the last scriptural community, the speakers' own or contemporary tradition, a future community, or a different historical period; the notes test these against context, ancestral practice and the opponents' knowledge of Christianity.)
  38:7/q05/p1 Christianity, the last prior religious community or religion of Jesus. — reasons: Mujāhid is transmitted both for and against this identification. Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Quraẓī/Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb, al-Kalbī, Muqātil, al-Suddī and others are also named. Muqātil, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı and Ibn ʿĀshūr say Christians' Trinity or association of another god made the opponents claim not to have heard monotheism there; al-Ālūsī rejects attributing this belief to Jesus himself. Al-Ṭabarī's Ibn ʿAbbās report and al-Durr's Mujāhid report say Quraysh argued Christians would have told them if the Qur'an were true. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that Christianity had little standing among Quraysh; Iṣlāḥī says the context would support it only if they believed in Christianity or Muhammad based his call on it.
  38:7/q05/p2 Quraysh's own inherited religion or Arab custom in their time. — reasons: Mujāhid and Qatāda are commonly cited; Qatāda adds their own era, and Ibn ʿAṭiyya describes Arab tradition. Al-Wāḥidī calls it their forefathers' religion. Bursevī says their tradition followed earlier religions. Ibn ʿĀshūr adduces the Abū Ṭālib deathbed question about ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib's religion. Iṣlāḥī connects it to the absence of monotheism from later Arab history. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī extends it to accepted later or near-contemporary peoples' religions, contrasted with ancient tales.
  38:7/q05/p3 Judaism and Christianity, and possibly the inherited ancestral tradition, taken together. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj says leaders found no such teaching in Christianity, Judaism or their fathers' tradition. Ibn al-Jawzī transmits al-Farrāʾ and al-Zajjāj: Jews associated ʿUzayr with God and Christians professed three in one.
  38:7/q05/p4 The interval between Jesus and Muhammad, or their own historical time. — reasons: Al-Ḥakam identifies the period between Jesus and Muhammad; al-Ḥasan and Qatāda instead phrase the objection as not having heard this arise in their time. These reports vary on the period's limits.
  38:7/q05/p5 A coming end-time religion or prophet whose monotheism they claim no one foretold. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites anticipations by monks, Shīq, Saṭīḥ and Israelites. Al-Ḥasan says they claimed not to have heard such an end-time event would occur. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Samin name scriptural scholars and soothsayers as alleged silent witnesses; al-Samin calls the assertion an extreme lie. Al-Ālūsī reads the phrase circumstantially and says the monotheist, idol-breaking prophet was in fact widely expected.
  38:7/q05/p6 The People of the Book's testimony to Muhammad's prophethood, allegedly absent. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī and Kur'an Yolu give this as an alternative paraphrase: Quraysh had heard nothing from Jews and Christians affirming Muhammad as a true messenger.
  38:7/q05/p7 The first pre-Islamic religion, through a claimed Coptic reversal of first and last. — reasons: Al-Zarkashī reports the claim that Copts call the first 'last' and the last 'first'; al-Suyūṭī transmits Shīdhala's identification of the first pre-Islamic religion by the same explanation.
  38:7/q05/p8 Religions current in the speakers' later age, including Christianity and other plural or incarnate divinities. — reasons: Asad's 'faiths of latter days' includes Christianity's Trinity and Hinduism's triad. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī similarly contrasts contemporary or near-contemporary accepted religion with ancient peoples' tales, though he rejects singling out Christianity.
38:7/q06 How does 'in the later religion' attach grammatically? [فِى ٱلْمِلَّةِ ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the prepositional phrase governs the act of hearing or supplies the setting of the demonstrative.)
  38:7/q06/p1 It modifies 'we heard': they have not heard such a doctrine within that religion. — reasons: Al-Samin allows a direct attachment to samiʿnā. Bursevī makes it an adverbial of hearing in the inherited Quraysh religion. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows attachment to hearing and paraphrases the denial as never hearing this before now.
  38:7/q06/p2 It is a circumstantial modifier of 'this,' with an understood omitted phrase. — reasons: Al-Samin describes a suppressed circumstantial phrase qualifying hādhā. Al-Ālūsī's alternative parsing makes the phrase describe 'this' and thus an alleged end-time monotheist religion. Ibn ʿĀshūr also allows the circumstantial reading.
38:7/q07 How are the two clauses linked to the surrounding exhortation? [مَا سَمِعْنَا بِهَٰذَا إِنْ هَٰذَآ إِلَّا ٱخْتِلَٰقٌ · links]
  38:7/q07/p1 The unheard-of claim supports and explains the chiefs' charge of invention, summing up their call to persist with the gods. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says the first sentence may stand independently or explain their earlier charge, and the second explains 'we
