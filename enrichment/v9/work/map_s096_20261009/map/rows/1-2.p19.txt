[SAMIN-DURR:1:2#2/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The article in al-hamd is analyzed as universal, generic, or referring to known praise; al-Zamakhshari chooses the generic reading.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:2#2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | rejects | Al-Zamakhshari rejects the universal reading; the author infers that praise here is performed by the servant, who cannot produce every praise by himself and others.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:2#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Hamd is fundamentally a verbal noun and therefore normally has no dual or plural; Ibn al-A'rabi nevertheless reports an af'al plural with poetic evidence.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:2#2/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The common reading raises al-hamd as subject with a deleted nominal predicate, the preferred estimate, though a verb can also be supplied.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:2#2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The author prefers a nominal estimate because it is mandatory in fa-idha and amma clauses; the relative clause al-ladhi fi al-dar instead requires a verb but is not structurally comparable.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:2#2/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | A prepositional phrase or adverb functioning as clause predicate, relative clause, adjective, or circumstantial phrase has an omitted governor; a generic kā'in cannot be overt except in a poetic anomaly. | mentions 27:40
[SAMIN-DURR:1:2#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The omitted governor can generally be a noun or verb, but relative clauses require a verb and the fa-idha and amma constructions require a noun; grammarians differ over the default elsewhere.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:2#3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A rare accusative al-hamda is parsed either as a verbal noun replacing I praise, or as an imperative-like source meaning say praise; al-Tabari favors the command interpretation, but the author prefers declarative force.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:2#3/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The accusative may alternatively be a direct object of recite or read, but the verbal-noun analysis is better because the wording itself supports it.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:2#3/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | Raising al-hamd is stronger and more expressive of stable praise, while accusative signals renewal; Abraham's salām is better than the angels' salāman by the verse's instruction to answer more beautifully. | mentions 11:69, 4:86
