[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p231/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd defines rabbānī as an authority who cultivates and reforms people.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p231/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن جبير | reports | Ibn Jubayr defines rabbānī both as wise and pious and as a scholar who acts on his knowledge.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p231/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjāj defines rabbānī as a teacher.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p233/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · مجاهد والحسن | reports | Mujāhid and al-Ḥasan read taʿlamūn with fatḥa and gemination, treating it as a shortened form of tataʿallamūn; grammarians differ over which initial tāʾ was dropped.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p233/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · أبو حيوة | reports | Abū Ḥaywa reads tadrusūn with a kasra on rāʾ and is also reported to read it with a geminated rāʾ, meaning that they teach others.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p233/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | For the geminated tadrusūn reading, the doubling may mark causation or simply intensification.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p233/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | Another reading derives tadrusūn from adrasa with the same meaning as darasa, like the paired forms akrama and karuma or anzala and nazala.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p233/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī says authority and leadership should arise from knowledge and study; he likens study without practice to a beautiful tree that bears no useful fruit.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p233/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī further says that someone who learns without acting has no connection with God, since only obedience establishes that relationship.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p233/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | Abū Ḥayyān detects a Muʿtazilī implication in al-Zamakhsharī's claim: that a person is not a believer or scholar without action and that action is a condition of sound faith.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#2/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn Atiyya lists عبيد and عبدى as forms plural in meaning; grammarians disagree whether they are synonymous, whether عباد is reserved for God and عبيد/عبدى for humans, or whether عبدى intensifies servitude.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#2/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | لي modifies عباد, while من دون attaches to عباد through its verbal sense and may be a second adjective or a circumstantial phrase after the noun is specified.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عطية | holds | Ibn Atiyya’s usage survey says عباد connotes elevation and obedience without contempt, citing 2:207, 21:26, 39:53, and 5:118; that positive sense explains its use in 39:53. | mentions 2:207, 21:26, 39:53, 5:118
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عطية | holds | Ibn Atiyya says عبيد can be contemptuous, citing Imru al-Qays’s phrase عبيد العصا and Hamza’s وهل أنتم إلا عبيد لأبي; he reads 41:46 likewise as pitying the servants’ limited power. | mentions 41:46
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#2/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عطية | holds | Ibn Atiyya says عباد is unsuitable here because it implies obedience, unlike its fitting use in قل يعبادي الذين أسرفوا. | mentions 39:53
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | reports | Al-Shaykh says عبيد is certainly a plural, while عبدى is a collective noun with a feminine ending; Ibn Atiyya may mean both are semantic plurals.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#2/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | rejects | Al-Shaykh rejects Ibn Atiyya’s semantic distribution: عباد is common because فِعال is the regular plural of this pattern, while فَعيل is rare, as Sibawayh notes.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#2/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | reports | Al-Shaykh explains عبيد at 41:46 as chosen for rhyme with بعيد and شهيد, and its occurrence at 50:29 for rhyme with وعيد and مزيد. | mentions 41:46, 50:29
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#2/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | reports | Al-Shaykh says عبيد and عباد have the same meaning and argues that the contempt in Imru al-Qays’s line and Hamza’s remark comes from context, not the word.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#2/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The author says al-Shaykh’s dismissal of Ibn Atiyya’s usage survey is unfounded without a counterexample, and rejects assigning contempt only to context when the word itself is the better source.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | reports | Al-Shaykh also cites 50:29, where عبيد suits the neighboring rhyme words وعيد and مزيد. | mentions 50:29
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The author rejects the claim that contempt in عبيد is supplied only by context, arguing that lexical wording takes precedence when either word or context could explain it.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#4/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | كونوا requires an implied يقول; ربانيون is its plural subject complement.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#4/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | One derivation relates رباني to الرب, with extra alif and nūn intensifying the relational adjective; ordinary, non-intensive forms are رقبي، شعري، and لحوي.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#4/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · سيبويه | reports | Sibawayh explains the extra letters in رباني as specifying mastery of knowledge of the Lord, analogous to شعراني، لحياني, and رقباني.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#4/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A second derivation relates رباني to ربّان, a teacher of good who governs people and teaches religion; its ending intensifies the quality as in عطشان and ريان.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#4/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | On the second derivation the form can be an adjective-based nisba, like أحمري, illustrated by the anonymous verse أطربا وأنت قنسري.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#4/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Exegetes gloss كونوا ربانيين as become jurists and learned scholars; after Ibn Abbas died, Muhammad ibn al-Hanafiyya called him the rabbi of this community.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#5/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The باء in بما كنتم is causal: become learned because you used to do the described acts.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#5/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | The causal باء may attach to كونوا, to ربانيين because it carries verbal meaning, or to an omitted phrase as an adjective of ربانيين; the last analysis is unclear to the author.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#5/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | ما is a verbal noun particle; reading it as a relative pronoun is strained because it needs an omitted antecedent and fails the shared-governor condition.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#5/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · نافع | reports | Nafi, Ibn Kathir, and Abu Amr read تعلمون from علم يعلم, meaning know or learn; it takes one object.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#5/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · باقي السبعة | reports | The other canonical readers read تُعَلِّمون from the causative form, which takes two objects; the first is omitted as people or students, though no object may be intended if teaching the Book itself is the point.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#5/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The causative reading intensifies the praise: every teacher is learned, not every learned person teaches, and rabbani suggests teaching others rather than private knowledge alone.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#5/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Others prefer the simple reading because it avoids an unexpressed object and matches تدرسون; both readings are mass-transmitted, so the author refuses to rank them.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#5/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan and Mujahid read تَعَلَّمون from تعلم, originally تتعلمون with one of the two initial tāʾs deleted; the final clause بما كنتم تدرسون follows the same pattern.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#6/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الجمهور | reports | The standard tadrusūna has fatḥa on tāʾ and ḍamma on rāʾ, from triliteral darasa, matching taʿallamūna from triliteral ʿalima.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#6/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | A shadda on taʿallamūna does not require one on tadrusūna: the people first taught others, then studied, reciting the studied text to them. | mentions 17:106
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#6/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو حيوة | reports | In one transmission, Abū Ḥaywa reads the rāʾ with kasra, a weak dialect; darasa has two present-tense dialects.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#6/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو حيوة | reports | In another transmission, he reads the form with shadda: either intensively, like light taʿallamūna, or causatively with two understood objects, making others study.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#6/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | A reading from adrasa is also reported, as in tukramūna from akrama; here afʿala means faʿʿala, as in adrasa/darrasa, akrama/karrama, and anzala/nazzala.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#6/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Dars means repetition and persistence; studying a book or Quran is repeating it, and studying a book can mean preserving its trace by memorization.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:79#6/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Because Quran recitation is continuous, the verse calls its continuance dars; the same root describes a house whose trace has disappeared, with ṭalal ʿārif and dāris synonymous.
