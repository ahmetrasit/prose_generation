[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابأو)#478/r18] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Sībawayh says the coordination analysis is remote because if it were ordinary coordination it would also work after fa and wāw; the semantic relation can instead prompt a new clause, as in لا تأته فيشتمك.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابأو)#478/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The subjunctive after أو can mean إلا أن; Imruʾ al-Qays's أو نموت فنعذرا means unless we die, parallel to لألزمنك أو تقضيني.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابأو)#478/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Rafʿ after أو is grammatical either by coordinating the following clause with the first or by starting a new clause.

[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابأو)#478/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In commands such as الزمه أو يتقيك, the subjunctive means unless he protects you; raising is a fresh clause because the clauses cannot be coordinated.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابأو)#478/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · زياد الأعجم | reports | Ziyād al-Aʿjam's verse وكنت إذا غمزت قناة قوم كسرت كعوبها أو تستقيما illustrates subjunctive with the sense إلا أن; rafʿ would instead start a clause.

[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالراء)#720/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Rāʾ behaves as if doubled, so it prevents imāla in راشد and فراش and strengthens fatḥ of nearby alifs.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالراء)#720/r10] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | They often open حمار قاسم for qāf, though imāla is more common than in مال قاسم because the intervening pattern has two kasras; جارم قاسم does not behave identically.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالراء)#720/r11] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | reports | Speakers who say حمار قاسم also say سفار قبل because rāʾ can change in construct phrases or as the ending of a masculine name.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالراء)#720/r12] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In فار قبل and كافر قبل, a single silent consonant intervenes and the following rāʾ is kasra, making imāla required for these speakers; a speaker who says جاد does not say فار.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالراء)#720/r14] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Imāla in دنانير is more expected because rāʾ is farther from the alif; genitive behavior matches كافر, and some speakers also say مناشيط.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالراء)#720/r15] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | At pause, forms such as داع do not imale because the kasra is not pronounced; in حمار, the doubled rāʾ similarly governs the preceding alif.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالراء)#720/r16] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Some imale the hāʾ and preceding vowel in مهارى; Arab forms ضربة and أخذه liken hāʾ to alif, so the preceding sound is imaled.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالراء)#720/r17] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The rāʾ is weaker than qāf in these contexts: speakers who say أراد أن يضربها قاسم also say راشد, and those who say بمال قاسم also say بمال راشد.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالراء)#720/r18] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Forms عفرا, عيرا, and عمران can be compared with علقا, ضيقا, and حمقان; some imale عفرا because initial kasra precedes a final alif, treating it like حبلى.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالراء)#720/r19] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Imāla in عفرا is especially plausible by analogy with عرقا and forms of يعقرها; if kasra can incline the alif, yā is an even stronger trigger.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالراء)#720/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | When rāʾ follows an alif, the alif is not imaled in nominative or accusative forms like حمار; in genitive it is imaled because rāʾ acts like two kasra consonants.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالراء)#720/r20] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | They imale النغران because initial kasra precedes an alif after a root consonant, which they liken to an alif built into a word such as حبلى.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالراء)#720/r21] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | They say عمران with imāla but not برقان or حمقان because those forms contain emphatic consonants.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالراء)#720/r22] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | reports | Some who imale عمران also imale عقران, since the emphatic is distant; in فراش and جراب fatḥ is preferred because their alif is added, not like feminine حبلى.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالراء)#720/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A kasra on rāʾ after alif can overcome an earlier emphatic, allowing imāla in قارب, غارم, and طارد.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالراء)#720/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Forms such as فارق and مفاريق take fatḥ like ناعق, منافق, and مناشيط because an emphatic follows the alif.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالراء)#720/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | reports | Some Arabs imale الكافرون, الكافرين, الكافر, and المنابر when a consonant separates rāʾ from alif; rāʾ is distant and close in articulation to yā.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالراء)#720/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | reports | Another group opens these alifs in nominative and accusative but imales them in genitive, treating case as the condition for imāla.
