[RAZI-FULL:v2p21#2/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī sees four independent, unjoined statements—alif-lām-mīm, “that Book,” “no doubt in it,” and “guidance for the God-fearing”—as a tightly interlinked sequence.
[RAZI-FULL:v2p21#2/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Al-Rāzī favors the sequence’s rhetorical force: the opening signals the challenge, the Book is declared supremely perfect, doubt is denied as testimony to that perfection, and guidance confirms its certainty.
[RAZI-FULL:v2p21#2/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The four clauses gain force respectively through ellipsis and delicate allusion, the grandeur of definiteness, placing “doubt” before “in it,” and ellipsis plus the indefinite verbal noun “guidance” instead of the adjective “guiding.”
[RAZI-FULL:v2p21/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Although the Qur’an is evidence for believers and unbelievers alike about the Creator, religion, and the Messenger’s truth, al-Rāzī names the God-fearing because they are the ones who benefited from it; if guidance means conveying to the goal, it applies only to them. | mentions 79:45, 36:11
[RAZI-FULL:v2p21/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | rejects | An objection says the Qur’an cannot be guidance when it contains ambiguous passages, depends on reason to distinguish clear from ambiguous, and appears to contain opposing claims about compulsion and free choice.
[RAZI-FULL:v2p21/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · علي بن أبي طالب | reports | Al-Rāzī cites ʿAlī’s warning to Ibn ʿAbbās, sent to debate the Khārijites, not to argue from the Qur’an because it admits two interpretations.
[RAZI-FULL:v2p21/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | rejects | Al-Rāzī replies that ambiguous and concise passages remain guidance because their intended meaning can be identified through reason or transmitted evidence.
[RAZI-FULL:v2p21/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | rejects | A further objection says the Qur’an cannot guide to knowledge of God, His attributes, or prophecy if its own authority must already be established for it to prove them.
