[TABRISI:2:114/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Alternatively, an is an accusative object of purpose: they prevented worship because they disliked God's name being mentioned there.

[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p133#4/r1] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن عباس وآخرون | reports | One occasion reported by Ibn Abbas and others is the Romans' support for Nebuchadnezzar's destruction of Jerusalem's Temple after the Israelites killed John son of Zechariah; corpses were thrown there.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p133#4/r2] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd says the verse concerns the polytheists who kept the Messenger from entering Mecca at Hudaybiyya.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p133#4/r3] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | reports | The reported meanings of ruining mosques are physically demolishing them or preventing God's name from being mentioned there.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p133#4/r4] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · السدي | reports | Al-Suddi reads this as a prediction: thereafter a Roman would enter Jerusalem only fearing execution or being made to pay the poll tax.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p133#4/r5] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | reports | Another reading treats the statement as an injunction to strive against them so that no one enters the mosques except in fear.
[RAZI-FULL:v4p10#2/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Although the wording appears to call mosque obstruction the greatest injustice, shirk is called great injustice in 31:13, and adultery or murder may seem worse; this is only a general statement subject to specification. | mentions 31:13
[RAZI-FULL:v4p10#2/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The apparent reading makes the very perpetrators who prevented and ruined the mosque the people forbidden to enter except fearfully.
[RAZI-FULL:v4p10#2/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | If the clause is general, one reading is that they ought to enter God's mosques in awe, trembling before believers who might overpower them; oppression made this rightful state unrealized.
[RAZI-FULL:v4p10#2/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Another reading promises Muslim dominion over the Sacred Mosque and other mosques: polytheists would fear arrest, punishment, or death unless they became Muslim; the promise was fulfilled by the ban on polytheist pilgrimage after Abu Bakr's Hajj and the Jews' expulsion from Arabia.
[RAZI-FULL:v4p10#2/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | A third interpretation takes fear as the humiliation and abasement imposed by jizya.
[RAZI-FULL:v4p10#2/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | A fourth view bars polytheists from the Sacred Mosque except for a proceeding that entails fear, such as a dispute, trial, or argument; 9:17 is cited as evidence. | mentions 9:17

[RAZI-FULL:v4p10#2/r8] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Though phrased as a statement, the clause can mean that people must not enable them to enter or leave them free access, as in 33:53. | mentions 33:53
