## THE CURRENT MAP
2:206/q01 Who is described by this warning? [لَهُ · referent] (turns on: Whether the verse is tied to a specific occasion or applies to a class by its traits.)
  2:206/q01/p1 The hypocrite or unbeliever of the surrounding description. — reasons: Tabari and Tabrisi link him to corruption of crops and offspring; Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi describe an arrogant unbeliever or hypocrite. Bursavi calls him a hypocritical corrupter, and Ibn Kathir a wicked man.
  2:206/q01/p2 Al-Akhnas ibn Shurayq in particular. — reasons: Al-Suddi names him; Tabrisi says he feigned affection for the Prophet and Islam; Elmalılı identifies him as a Thaqafi allied to Banu Zuhra who professed love, then burned a Muslim field and killed livestock. Nasafi says he was admonished for destruction.
  2:206/q01/p3 The warning applies generally to anyone with the described traits, including sinners who are not hypocrites. — reasons: Tabari transmits 'any sinner and hypocrite'; Ibn Atiyya extends it to sinners sharing traits; Razi argues the five traits do not prove hypocrisy and pride on admonition alone does not prove it. Elmalılı, Kur'an Yolu and Study Quran retain a general application despite specific reports.
  2:206/q01/p4 An unnamed particular hypocritical pretender. — reasons: Ibn Abbas says the three verses concern someone whose exterior opposes his interior; a report from al-Sadiq identifies a particular person without naming him.
  2:206/q01/p5 The man who defiantly killed Khubayb. — reasons: Tha'labi identifies the executioner who killed Khubayb after being told to fear God; Baghawi names the spear wielder Salaman, whose defiance increased.
2:206/q02 What occasion of revelation is reported for these verses? [* · reports] (turns on: Reports locate the passage in different events or treat its class description as general.)
  2:206/q02/p1 Al-Akhnas's profession of Islam and subsequent destruction of crops and livestock. — reasons: Elmalılı gives his tribe, alliance, profession before the Prophet, and burning of a Muslim field and killing of animals.
  2:206/q02/p2 The al-Raji expedition and the hypocrites' mockery of its martyrs. — reasons: Ibn Abbas and al-Dahhak recount Quraysh's false conversion request, six Muslims lured, three killed, and Khubayb and Zayd captured; Ibn Abbas reports hypocrites calling the deaths pointless. Ibn Jawzi's report names al-Zubayr, al-Miqdad and Khubayb with the hypocrites. Tha'labi adds Khubayb's refusal of treachery and miraculous fruit in Mecca. Baghawi reports Umar marveling that God protected Asim's body from polytheists after death, in keeping with Asim's vow not to touch or be touched by one.
  2:206/q02/p3 Suhayb's escape after giving up his wealth to reach Medina. — reasons: Nasafi reports polytheists attempting to make him leave Islam, killing companions with him, then allowing him to travel after taking his wealth.
  2:206/q02/p4 One who enjoins right and forbids wrong until killed. — reasons: Nasafi also reports this possible occasion or application.
  2:206/q02/p5 Hypocrites generally, with no exclusive individual occasion. — reasons: Ibn Abbas and al-Hasan reports identify hypocrites; Kur'an Yolu reports they appeared friendly before the Prophet while acting destructively in secret. Study Quran says most cited commentators prefer a universal teaching despite incident reports.
2:206/q03 Who tells him to fear God? [وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَهُ · referent] (turns on: Whether an unnamed passive clause permits identifying a historical speaker.)
  2:206/q03/p1 The Prophet addressed the man. — reasons: Wahidi says the Messenger summoned al-Akhnas to respond inwardly and outwardly, and Razi reports this from him.
  2:206/q03/p2 Any person may admonish him; the speaker is unstated. — reasons: Biqa'i explicitly says anyone may warn him. Razi objects to Wahidi's identification: the verse neither names a speaker nor establishes that an actual exchange happened, though the Prophet summoned everyone to piety.
2:206/q04 What does the call to fear God ask him to do? [ٱتَّقِ ٱللَّهَ · interpretation] (turns on: How broadly the unstated object of 'fear' and the surrounding faults are supplied.)
  2:206/q04/p1 Revere God and guard against His punishment and disobedience. — reasons: Tabrisi derives ittiqa' from taking protection against danger; al-Asamm includes fearing punishment and avoiding disobedience. Baghawi gives the simple command, while Kur'an Yolu emphasizes reverence and respect for right rules.
  2:206/q04/p2 Stop the corruption, hypocrisy and harmful acts he is practicing. — reasons: Tabari and Tabrisi specify destruction of crops and offspring; Wahidi-Wajiz calls it gentle warning to stop; Bursavi includes wrongdoing and hypocrisy. Razi extends it to worldly ambition, false oaths, obstinate defense of falsehood, corruption and destroying crops and progeny.
  2:206/q04/p3 Bring his inner state into accord with his outward profession. — reasons: Al-Asamm includes this formulation; Wahidi's reported summons demanded an inward and outward response.
2:206/q05 How does he respond to the admonition? [أَخَذَتْهُ ٱلْعِزَّةُ بِٱلْإِثْمِ · interpretation] (turns on: The attachment and force of bi-l-ithm: compelled action, prior cause, accompaniment, or qualification of ʿizza.)
  2:206/q05/p1 Pride and anger make him reject correction and persist or advance in wrongdoing. — reasons: Qatada says he presses further into sin when told to hold back; Kashshaf lists harming others, stubbornness, and rejecting the preacher. Qushayri says he deems advice beneath him; Ibn Kathir says anger makes him refuse truth. Biqa'i contrasts those who stop when corrected; Ibn Ashur describes anger at counsel.
  2:206/q05/p2 The wording allows pride to compel him into the forbidden sin. — reasons: Muqatil and Tustari link sinful zeal to 38:2; Wahidi and others use the idiom of being compelled or bound, including jāhiliyya partisanship. Baghawi specifies injustice; Baydawi says the very sin forbidden. Razi identifies ignoring counsel itself as the sin.
  2:206/q05/p3 His pride arises from prior sin or unbelief in his heart and blocks acceptance. — reasons: Mawardi, al-Hasan and others connect the cause to sin already inside; Razi names disbelief, ignorance and neglect of proof and compares 38:2. This is an alternative to reading the sin as what pride drives him toward.
  2:206/q05/p4 Pride accompanies him while he is sinning, or pride itself is tainted with sin. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Samin and Alusi give the accompaniment reading; Biqa'i describes arrogance during base, unlawful conduct.
  2:206/q05/p5 His 'dignity' is acquired through sin and is therefore counterfeit. — reasons: Tabataba'i attaches bi-l-ithm to al-ʿizza and sets outward status against a hypocritical heart. He rejects attachment to the verb as causal or causative because those construals would treat this as genuine dignity.
2:206/q06 What does أَخَذَتْهُ convey? [أَخَذَتْهُ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the image stresses seizure and enclosure or the resulting compulsion.)
  2:206/q06/p1 Taking or grasping, extended to overwhelming and enclosing a person. — reasons: Jishumi and Tabrisi give the opposite of 'give'; Abu Hayyan says pride surrounds him like a grasping hand. Ibn Ashur traces literal hand-taking to control, overpowering, receiving and enveloping, citing 9:5, 6:42 and 3:81.
  2:206/q06/p2 Taking by compulsion, binding or captivity. — reasons: Wahidi's linguistic authorities treat taking by a thing as compelling it; Kashshaf and Baydawi explain force into sin, while Alusi imagines pride making him a captive shackled by sin.
2:206/q07 What does ٱلْعِزَّةُ mean here? [ٱلْعِزَّةُ · meaning] (turns on: The word's base sense of strength versus its contextual sense as zeal or claimed status.)
  2:206/q07/p1 Strength, might, protection or invulnerability, turned into haughtiness in context. — reasons: Jishumi and Tabrisi define strength warding off humiliation; Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi and Ibn Ashur add force, tribal backing or confidence that no one may challenge him. Ibn Jawzi distinguishes scriptural uses: might at 26:44 and 38:82, power at 4:139, zeal at 38:2; Qurtubi cites 38:23 and Ibn Ashur cites a proverb, al-Samaw'al and 2:209. Biqa'i recalls 10:65 to say all true might belongs to God.
  2:206/q07/p2 Partisan, jāhiliyya zeal and proud obstinacy. — reasons: Muqatil and Tustari compare 38:2; Ibn Abbas is reported glossing it as zealotry with a cited poem; Ibn Jawzi's third Quranic use cites 38:2. Qurtubi quotes a poet on ignorant pride and gives unbelief born of zeal; Qashani interprets egoistic zeal.
  2:206/q07/p3 A felt dignity acquired from sinful worldly status, unlike God-given dignity. — reasons: Tabataba'i cites 3:26, 63:8 and 4:139 for genuine dignity, and interprets this man's apparent dignity as counterfeit.
2:206/q08 Why add بِٱلْإِثْمِ to the pride? [ٱلْعِزَّةُ بِٱلْإِثْمِ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the addition primarily disambiguates a potentially good quality or defines a counterfeit source of dignity.)
  2:206/q08/p1 It marks this pride as blameworthy, since dignity or strength can be praiseworthy. — reasons: Abu Hayyan names the device tatmim and cites 5:54, 63:8 and 4:139; Samin gives 63:8 and 5:54. Ibn Ashur contrasts 63:8 and says the pride obstructs admonition; Tabataba'i contrasts true dignity from God.
  2:206/q08/p2 It exposes the claimant's sinful status as counterfeit dignity. — reasons: Tabataba'i says linking the preposition to ʿizza makes the contrast between a hypocritical heart and outwardly impressive words or status explicit.
2:206/q09 How is the bāʾ in بِٱلْإِثْمِ construed? [بِٱلْإِثْمِ · grammar] (turns on: Its syntactic attachment and whether it signals action, cause, accompaniment, or sinful quality.)
  2:206/q09/p1 It is causative or transitive: pride drives him into sin. — reasons: Wahidi relates the idiom 'made him do'; Abu Hayyan reports Zamakhshari but observes this bāʾ normal
