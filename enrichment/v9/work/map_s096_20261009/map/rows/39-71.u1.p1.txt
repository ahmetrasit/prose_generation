39:71/q13 What is the point of the keepers' question? [وَقَالَ لَهُمْ خَزَنَتُهَآ أَلَمْ يَأْتِكُمْ رُسُلٌۭ · interpretation]
  39:71/q13/p1 It rebukes and humiliates the condemned for rejecting a warning they received. — reasons: Thalabi, Baghawi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Ibn Kathir, Abu Hayyan, Biqa'i, Alusi and Islahi call it reproach; Biqa'i says it compounds punishment. Jishumi says it shows their acts came from themselves. Study Quran cites parallel rebukes at 40:50 and 67:8–9. Kuranyolu says it also warns living readers while change remains possible.
39:71/q14 How is 'did messengers come to you' read? [أَلَمْ يَأْتِكُمْ · readings] (turns on: The gender agreement of the verb with messengers.)
  39:71/q14/p1 The verb has masculine yāʾ: yaʾtikum. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya calls this the majority; Abu Hayyan likewise contrasts it with a feminine reading.
  39:71/q14/p2 The verb has feminine tāʾ: taʾtikum. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya names al-Aʿraj; Abu Hayyan, Samin and Alusi name Ibn Hurmuz.
39:71/q15 Who are the messengers 'from among you'? [رُسُلٌۭ مِّنكُمْ · referent] (turns on: Whether 'from among you' stresses common humanity or the same community.)
  39:71/q15/p1 They are human messengers from the condemned people's own kind or peoples. — reasons: Muqatil calls them from among the people. Ibn Atiyya, Ibn Kathir, Abu Hayyan, Biqa'i, Bursevi and Alusi say shared speech, access and understanding strengthened the proof. Tabataba'i explicitly says human messengers; Nasafi calls them children of Adam.
  39:71/q15/p2 The messengers are specifically from the unbelievers' own communities. — reasons: Tabari phrases the question as messengers from among the disbelievers and sent to their peoples; Muqatil says from among the people addressed.
39:71/q16 How does minkum attach grammatically? [مِّنكُمْ · grammar]
  39:71/q16/p1 It may qualify 'messengers' or attach to the action of coming. — reasons: Samin gives both analyses.
39:71/q17 Is there a reading other than rusul? [رُسُلٌۭ · readings]
  39:71/q17/p1 A reported variant has nudhur, 'warners', instead of rusul. — reasons: Alusi reports the variant without naming its reciter.
39:71/q18 What are the Lord's signs recited by the messengers? [ءَايَٰتِ رَبِّكُمْ · referent]
  39:71/q18/p1 They are revealed scriptures, including God's Quranic signs and proofs. — reasons: Muqatil identifies Quranic signs. Tabari adds God's revealed Book and proofs. Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Bursevi identify scriptures revealed to prophets; Abu Hayyan says they brought glad tidings and warnings. Alusi notes they were revealed for the hearers' benefit; Ibn Kathir says their recital established proof.
39:71/q19 Why call the signs those of 'your Lord'? [ءَايَٰتِ رَبِّكُمْ · rhetoric]
  39:71/q19/p1 The wording softens admonition and recalls the good news they could have received. — reasons: Biqa'i says it makes acceptance likelier, and links 'Lord' to the omitted majesty in the second half of an iḥtibāk.
39:71/q20 What does 'reciting to you' add? [يَتْلُونَ عَلَيْكُمْ · interpretation]
  39:71/q20/p1 It denotes repeated reminder and delivery of signs. — reasons: Biqa'i relates continual reminder to the sura's concern with worship and deficient souls' need for reminders; the other exegetes describe recitation of revelation as completing proof.
39:71/q21 How is yatlūna related grammatically to rusul? [يَتْلُونَ عَلَيْكُمْ · grammar]
  39:71/q21/p1 It is a second adjective qualifying messengers. — reasons: Samin gives this syntactic analysis.
39:71/q22 What meeting is meant by 'your day'? [لِقَآءَ يَوْمِكُمْ هَٰذَا · referent] (turns on: Whether 'day' names Resurrection as a whole or specifically the time of entering Hell.)
  39:71/q22/p1 It is the time of their entry into Hell. — reasons: Kashshaf and Razi say Arabic calls hardship a 'day'; Bursevi adds Resurrection is not exclusive to unbelievers. Alusi prefers this as the time of actual punishment but allows Resurrection to include it or their particular punishment. Baydawi and Nasafi agree.
  39:71/q22/p2 It is the Day of Resurrection or Judgment. — reasons: Muqatil and Qurtubi name Resurrection or Judgment; Tabari associates the warning with what they meet that day and cites 6:130. Abu Hayyan says the warning included that day and its punishment; Thalabi calls it meeting God then. Alusi permits Resurrection as including entry or particular punishment.
39:71/q23 Why emphasize warning of this day? [وَيُنذِرُونَكُمْ لِقَآءَ يَوْمِكُمْ هَٰذَا · rhetoric]
  39:71/q23/p1 Warning frightens and restrains; 'this' makes its threatened outcome present. — reasons: Bursevi explains frightening them with consequences. Biqa'i says warning restrains more forcefully and the demonstrative makes the meeting concrete; his iḥtibāk reads omitted glad tidings beside it.
39:71/q24 What is the reported reading of the warning verb? [وَيُنذِرُونَكُمْ · readings]
  39:71/q24/p1 A reported form is nadhar, 'we warn', with ḍamma on nūn. — reasons: Kashshaf reports this reading.
39:71/q25 What do the condemned acknowledge with balā? [قَالُوا۟ بَلَىٰ · interpretation]
  39:71/q25/p1 They affirm that messengers came, recited, and warned, establishing proof against them. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya explains balā answers a negative question affirmatively; naʿam would affirm that none came. Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan say it acknowledges completed proof. Bursevi and Study Quran say confession comes too late to help or avert punishment; Study Quran cites 10:33, 22:18, 36:7, 40:6.
39:71/q26 Should recitation stop at balā? [بَلَىٰ · readings]
  39:71/q26/p1 Continuing without a stop is preferable. — reasons: Burhan says five instances are disputed and the continuation links backward and forward. Itqan likewise lists the five in al-Baqara, al-Zumar, al-Zukhruf, al-Hadid and al-Mulk. Burhan notes the broader discipline of stopping and resuming guides recitation, interpretation and inference, naming al-Zajjaj, Ibn al-Anbari, Ibn Abbad, al-Dani and al-Umani.
39:71/q27 What does ḥaqqat mean? [حَقَّتْ · meaning]
  39:71/q27/p1 It became binding, due, or came to pass. — reasons: Baghawi says it became binding; Itqan gives 'became due' or 'already happened'.
39:71/q28 What is the 'word of punishment'? [كَلِمَةُ ٱلْعَذَابِ · referent] (turns on: Whether the phrase denotes a specific divine utterance, prior decree, or punishment consequent on deeds.)
  39:71/q28/p1 It is God's declaration that He will fill Hell with Satan, his followers, jinn and humankind. — reasons: Muqatil and Bursevi cite God's statement to Satan at 38:85; Thalabi cites 32:13; Baghawi, Qurtubi, Baydawi and Abu Hayyan cite 11:119; Ibn al-Jawzi cites 7:18. Tabataba'i instead cites 2:39, the declaration that sign-deniers abide in Fire. Islahi calls the decree comprehensive: whoever follows Satan enters with him. The scriptural references and wording vary.
  39:71/q28/p2 It is God's prior judgment that these people are wretched and will remain in Hell. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya invokes 38:85 for eternal Fire; Baydawi specifies wretchedness and Hell; Razi says one under this decree cannot escape, so the blessed do not become wretched or vice versa. Biqa'i says it was established in eternity. Alusi allows this decree or the declaration to Iblis.
  39:71/q28/p3 It is the inevitable consequence of unrepented sinful choice and rejection. — reasons: Tabari reports Qatada's emphasis on deeds; Kashshaf, Nasafi, Ibn Kathir, Bursevi, Alusi, Asad and Islahi identify denial, error, opposition or bad choice as what makes the sentence due. Ibn Kathir cites 67:8–11; Nasafi cites 23:106. Bursevi says they claimed God sent nothing.
39:71/q29 Why name 'the disbelievers' again instead of using a pronoun? [عَلَى ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ · rhetoric]
  39:71/q29/p1 The explicit noun identifies disbelief as the quality causing their punishment. — reasons: Baydawi says it specifies who is condemned; Abu Hayyan, Biqa'i and Alusi say it names the trait deserving punishment. Alusi says the reply admits guilt rather than excusing it.
39:71/q30 Does the exchange make revelation a condition of obligation? [أَلَمْ يَأْتِكُمْ رُسُلٌۭ مِّنكُمْ · theology] (turns on: Whether the rebuke establishes that no obligation binds before revealed law.)
  39:71/q30/p1 Yes; liability follows the arrival of messengers and their proof. — reasons: Razi argues the rebuke would be pointless if revelation were not a condition. Baydawi and Bursevi likewise ground accountability in delivery of scripture; Alusi reports this use of the verse.
  39:71/q30/p2 The inference is disputed; rational duties may still bind before revelation. — reasons: Alusi says the argument assumes a disputed generality; rational-duty proponents can say post-message disbelief deserves sharper rebuke. The reciting and warning verbs preclude making the messengers mere intellect.
39:71/q31 Is fixed decree or rejection of prophets the basis of the condemnation? [حَقَّتْ كَلِمَةُ ٱلْعَذَابِ · theology] (turns on: Whether entry is explained by an inescapable decree or their rejection of delivered proofs.)
  39:71/q31/p1 The divine sentence takes effect and cannot be escaped. — reasons: Razi says the wretched cannot become blessed; Biqa'i calls the decree eternal. Alusi allows a decree of wretchedness, while also citing their bad choice.
  39:71/q31/p2 Their arrogant rejection of prophets and proofs makes them liable. — reasons: Razi reports the Muʿtazila's objection: if fixed decree alone determined entry, the angels' rebuke would be pointless. Jishumi says the rebuke proves their acts arose from themselves; Asad calls punishment the consequence of unrepented sin.
39:71/q32 What happens after the keepers' exchange? [وَقَالَ لَهُمْ خَزَنَتُهَآ · interpretation]
  39:71/q32/p1 The condemned are ordered into Hell. — reasons: Abu Hayyan notes the order follows the exchange.
