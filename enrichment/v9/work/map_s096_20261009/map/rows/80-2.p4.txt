[ASAD-NOTES:80:1#n1/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | reports | Asad cites well-authenticated traditions: while the Prophet sought to persuade leading pagan Meccan chiefs and the wider community, blind follower Abd Allah ibn Shurayh (Ibn Umm Maktum) asked him to repeat or explain earlier Qur'anic passages.
[ASAD-NOTES:80:1#n1/r2] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | reports | Asad says Muhammad was annoyed by the interruption, turned away from the blind man, and was immediately reproved in the sūra's first ten verses.
[ASAD-NOTES:80:1#n1/r3] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | reports | Asad reports that in later years the Prophet welcomed Ibn Umm Maktum with a humble acknowledgment that God had rebuked him on his account.
[ASAD-NOTES:80:1#n1/r4] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | Asad argues that conduct amounting to minor discourtesy in an ordinary person became a major sin deserving divine rebuke when committed by a prophet.
[ASAD-NOTES:80:1#n1/r5] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | Asad says the use of third-person forms in verses 1–2 particularly sharpens the Qur'anic rebuke.
[ASAD-NOTES:80:1#n1/r6] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | Asad takes the Prophet's public conveyance of God's reproof as evidence that revelation is objective, connecting it to 53:3. | mentions 53:3

[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p169/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Though the Prophet is addressed in the rebuke, its intended target is the haughty leaders who ignore his message and want poor companions excluded to secure their own attendance.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p169/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The Prophet's responsibility is to teach those who come eagerly, including devoted companions, rather than pamper absent leaders; he is not accountable for those who refuse to come.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p172/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · All exegetes | reports | All exegetes agree that the blind man was the poor companion ʿAbdullāh ibn Umm Maktūm.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p172/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · some narratives | reports | Some narratives describe the Prophet speaking with Quraysh leaders when Ibn Umm Maktūm entered without recognizing the meeting’s delicate nature; the Prophet disliked the interruption.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p172/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The Prophet’s displeasure was not due to the man’s poverty or blindness; he may have feared losing a chance to persuade Quraysh leaders, who might reject a gathering of poor companions.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p173/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Quraysh leaders objected that the Prophet’s companions were poor and destitute, treating this as evidence against his prophethood.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p173/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The Prophet may also have feared that arrogant leaders would disrespect his dear companion, causing still greater distress.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p173/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | God used the accidental incident to teach the Prophet to focus on companions eager for reform, rather than pampering indifferent people.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p174/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The unnamed addressee leaves readers wondering who the incident concerns, while “you know” indicates that the Prophet is being addressed and neglects his own flock for lost sheep.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p174/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The initial lack of a specified addressee reassures readers; naming the Prophet would suggest the rebuke was for rudeness toward a blind man, which was not its cause.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p174/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The later direct address identifies the rebuke’s occasion: the Prophet exceeded his duty through zeal, as prophets can overreach from devotion; Moses likewise arrived early at Ṭūr from eagerness to please God.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p175/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The Prophet’s devotion to persuading Quraysh leaders led him to neglect his own comfort and risk leaving little time to teach poor believers; he had assumed more than God required.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:80:1/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The Prophet was speaking with pagan leaders, hoping their conversion would make the public follow; when Ibn Umm Maktum arrived with a question, the Prophet frowned and did not answer, prompting God’s rebuke.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:80:1/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | A report says the Prophet later greeted Abdullah with affection, saying, “Welcome to the man on whose account my Lord reproached me.”
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:80:1/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | Zamakhshari reports that the Prophet twice appointed Abdullah to lead prayers in Medina when he left on campaign.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:80:1/r9] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The blind man came in fear of God, seeking illumination by the Qur’an to escape ignorance and be purified of sins.
