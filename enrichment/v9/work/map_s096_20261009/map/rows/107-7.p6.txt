[SAMIN-DURR:107:7/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · قطرب | reports | Qutrub derives ‘ma‘un’ as fa‘ul from ma‘n, ‘a small thing’, citing ‘ma-lahu ma‘na’ for someone with little property.
[SAMIN-DURR:107:7/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A second derivation makes it a passive participle of ‘a‘ana’; its original form would be ma‘wun, with metathesis and vowel change producing the attested form and pattern maf‘ul.
[SAMIN-DURR:107:7/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | That derivation has three irregularities: a passive from form IV normally has the maf‘al pattern, it uses metathesis, and it changes an unvocalized weak letter to alif; the author calls comparison with ‘taba’ and ‘ṣama’ weak.
[SAMIN-DURR:107:7/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A possible answer to the final irregularity is that the wāw was vocalized before metathesis, as the reconstructed ‘ma‘wun’ suggests.
[SAMIN-DURR:107:7/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The preferred derivation is ma‘una, with alif replacing the feminine tā’; on this account ma‘un means anything people use and benefit from, such as an axe, bucket, or fire striker.
[SAMIN-DURR:107:7/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الأعشى | reports | Al-A‘sha’s poem uses ‘ma‘unihi’ for useful provisions or resources; it is offered as poetic evidence for the preferred sense.
[SAMIN-DURR:107:7/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The object of ‘yamna‘un’ is omitted because people or petitioners are understood, or because the point is to condemn withholding trifling things that everyone recognizes should not be denied.
[IBNKATHIR:107:7/r1] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | They fail to worship their Lord properly and show no kindness to people, not even by lending useful things whose owners retain and recover them.
[IBNKATHIR:107:7/r10] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · عبد الله بن مسعود | reports | Abu al-Ubaydayn asked Ibn Masud, who defined al-ma'un as common loaned items such as an axe, pot, and bucket; two transmission routes give this answer.
[IBNKATHIR:107:7/r11] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · عبد الله بن مسعود | reports | A report through al-Masudi and Salama ibn Kuhayl repeats Ibn Masud's definition as items people lend one another, including an axe, pot, and bucket.
[IBNKATHIR:107:7/r12] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · أصحاب رسول الله | reports | Ibn Masud reports that the Prophet's Companions used to say al-ma'un meant the bucket, axe, and pot, things one cannot do without.
[IBNKATHIR:107:7/r13] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · أصحاب النبي | reports | Sa'd ibn Iyad reports from the Prophet's Companions the same definition of al-ma'un as the loaned tools just mentioned.
[IBNKATHIR:107:7/r14] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · عبد الله بن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masud again defines al-ma'un as things people lend one another, such as an axe or bucket.

[IBNKATHIR:107:7/r17] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · عبد الله بن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masud defines al-ma'un as borrowed household implements, including a pot, scales, and bucket.
