[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p205/r12] BAGHAWI-FULL, d. 516 · author | reports | Supplication has manners and conditions that cause acceptance; those who fulfill them are eligible for an answer, while those who violate them transgress and do not deserve one.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p205/r2] BAGHAWI-FULL, d. 516 · author | reports | One reading of istijāba is “answer me,” meaning obey me; answering can mean obedience or granting a request, while God's answering is giving and the servant's answering is obedience.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p205/r3] BAGHAWI-FULL, d. 516 · author | reports | Another reading is “ask Me to answer”; in substance, the instruction is to obey God.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p205/r4] BAGHAWI-FULL, d. 516 · author | holds | The purpose is that they be guided.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p205/r5] BAGHAWI-FULL, d. 516 · author | reports | To explain why many prayers seem unanswered despite this promise, one view takes supplication here as obedience and response as reward.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p205/r6] BAGHAWI-FULL, d. 516 · author | reports | Other explanations limit the general wording: God answers if He wills, if the request accords with the decree, if the answer is good for the petitioner, or if the request is not impossible; the first is compared to 6:41. | mentions 6:41
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p205/r7] BAGHAWI-FULL, d. 516 · أبو هريرة | reports | The Prophet says God answers so long as a person does not pray for sin or severing kinship and does not become impatient; impatience is repeating the plea, seeing no answer, despairing, and abandoning prayer.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p205/r8] BAGHAWI-FULL, d. 516 · author | reports | One general reading takes “I answer” to mean “I hear”; the verse promises an answer to the call, not necessarily the requested object, just as a master can answer a servant without granting the request.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p205/r9] BAGHAWI-FULL, d. 516 · author | reports | Another explanation says God never frustrates a prayer: He grants the requested matter if decreed, otherwise stores its reward for the Hereafter or averts harm; a report from ʿUbāda supports this.
