:2:228#4/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | rejects | The Prophet’s permission to divorce during any part of a nonconsummated purity, without specifying its beginning or end, is evidence against al-Zuhri’s timing restriction.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#4/r10] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو حنيفة | reports | Abu Hanifa agrees that a woman divorced while menstruating does not count that menstruation or the following purity, but begins with the menstruation after that purity; al-Qurtubi instead counts the purity.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#4/r11] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Arabic may use a plural time expression for a partial total: 2:197 calls the pilgrimage months, though the period is two months and part of a third, so three quruʾ can likewise include partial units. | mentions 2:197
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#4/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أشهب | reports | Ashhab says the marital bond and inheritance continue until the blood is confirmed as menstruation, so a nonmenstrual discharge is not mistaken for it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#4/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · رسول الله | reports | The Kufans cite the Prophet’s instruction to Fatima bint Abi Hubaysh: treat the bleeding as a vein, stop praying at menstruation, then purify and pray between periods.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#4/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الكوفيون | reports | The Kufans infer from 65:4, which names menstruation as what women may despair of and assigns months in its absence, that menstruation is the waiting-period measure. | mentions 65:4
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#4/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · عمر | reports | Umar said before the Companions that a slave woman’s waiting period is two menstruations, half the free woman’s; he would have made it one and a half if possible.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#4/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Because no Companion objected to Umar, al-Qurtubi treats his ruling as consensus, supported by ten Companions including the four caliphs.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#4/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | prefers | Al-Qurtubi reads the three quruʾ as three complete menstrual periods: a purity-based count could include the remainder of the purity in which divorce occurred, then only part of another.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#4/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | On his view, a woman divorced during an unconsummated purity starts at the next menstruation, counts three periods, and leaves the waiting period after bathing from the third.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#4/r9] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | He invokes the grammatical gender of quruʾ alongside masculine ayyām in 69:7 as a response in the debate over the intended sense of quruʾ. | mentions 69:7
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#5/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The phrase in 2:197, “pilgrimage months,” refers to two months and part of a third, illustrating plural wording for a partial amount. | mentions 2:197
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#5/r10] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · رسول الله | reports | The majority cite a report from Aisha that the divorce allotment for a slave woman is two and her quruʾ are two menstruations; al-Qurtubi notes that Mazahir ibn Aslam alone transmitted it and is weak.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#5/r11] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن عمر | reports | Ibn Umar is reported to have said that whichever spouse is enslaved reduces the divorce allotment; a group of scholars followed him.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#5/r12] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · عكرمة | reports | Ikrima, al-Zuhri, and al-Nakhaʿi interpret what is in the wombs as menstruation.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#5/r13] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · عمر | reports | Umar and Ibn Abbas interpret what is in the wombs as pregnancy.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#5/r14] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · مجاهد | reports | Mujahid says the phrase includes both menstruation and pregnancy, on the premise that a pregnant woman can menstruate.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#5/r15] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Because only women know their menstruation or purity and the waiting period depends on it, their statement about its ending or continuation is accepted and they are entrusted with disclosure.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#5/r16] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · سليمان بن يسار | reports | Sulayman ibn Yasar says people were not ordered to inspect women’s private parts; the matter was entrusted to women as persons trusted to report it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#5/r17] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The ban on concealment protects the husband: falsely claiming menstruation removes his revocation right; falsely denying it can impose extra maintenance or prevent revocation until the term expires; concealing pregnancy can likewise block his right.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#5/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · سعيد بن جبير | reports | Among scholars who take quruʾ as menstruations, Saʿid ibn Jubayr, Tawus, Ibn Shubruma, and al-Awzaʿi say the waiting period ends after bathing from the third period, when revocation also ends.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#5/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · شريك | reports | Sharik says that even if a woman delays bathing for twenty years, her husband retains the right of revocation until she bathes.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#5/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · إسحاق بن راهويه | reports | Ishaq ibn Rahawayh says that entering the third menstruation ends the marriage bond and revocation right, though she may not marry until bathing from it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#5/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | A similar position is attributed to Ibn Abbas, but al-Qurtubi calls it weak and counters it with 2:234, where reaching the term precedes freedom to act about oneself. | mentions 2:234
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#5/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الشافعي | reports | Al-Shafiʿi counts the transition from purity into menstruation as a quruʾ, shortening the waiting period: a divorce at the very end of purity is counted at once, and transition into the third ends the bond.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#5/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الجمهور | reports | The majority of scholars hold that a menstruating slave woman waits two menstrual periods after divorce.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#5/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن سيرين | reports | Ibn Sirin says he would regard a slave woman’s waiting period as the same as a free woman’s unless a Prophetic practice established otherwise, since the practice deserves following.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#5/r9] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الأصم عبد الرحمن بن كيسان وداود بن علي | reports | Al-Asamm Abd al-Rahman ibn Kaysan, Dawud ibn Ali, and other literalists say the waiting-period verses apply equally to slave and free women.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#6/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · قتادة | reports | Qatada says the verse was revealed because women in pre-Islamic times concealed pregnancy so they could attribute the child to a new husband.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#6/r10] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The conditional is a severe warning that reinforces the ban on concealment and the duty to state truthfully what is in the womb.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#6/r11] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The conditional does not imply that unbelievers may conceal; like “if you are my brother, do not wrong me,” it means faith should keep a person from such conduct.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#6/r12] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Baʿūla is the plural of baʿl, “husband”; he is so called for his elevation over the wife through the marital rights he possesses.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#6/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · رجل من أشجع | reports | A man from Ashjaʿ told the Prophet he feared his divorced pregnant wife might remarry and the child be attributed to another man; the verse was revealed and the Ashjaʿi woman returned to him.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#6/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن المنذر | reports | Ibn al-Mundhir says the scholars known to him reject a claim of three periods and completed waiting period within ten days, unless the woman says she miscarried a fetus with discernible form.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#6/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · مالك | reports | Malik accepts a claim of completed waiting period when the stated duration is one in which it could normally end; if the duration is exceptionally rare, he reports two opinions.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#6/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · مالك | reports | In the Mudawwana, Malik accepts a claim of three periods in a month if women confirm it; Shurayh agrees, and Ali praised Shurayh’s judgment.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#6/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · علي بن أبي طالب | reports | Ali told Shurayh “qālūn,” meaning he had judged correctly and well, on accepting a woman’s claim when other women corroborated it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#6/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · محمد | reports | In Muhammad’s book, the woman is not believed to have completed the three periods in less than a month and a half.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#6/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو ثور | reports | Abu Thawr sets the minimum at forty-seven days, reasoning from a minimum purity of fifteen days and a minimum menstruation of one day.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#6/r9] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · النعمان | reports | Al-Nuʿman and al-Shafiʿi do not believe a claim of completed waiting period in less than sixty days.
