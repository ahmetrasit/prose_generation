[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p433#2/r1] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | Grammarians give the bāʾ names such as attachment, instrumental aid, and addition; each describes a real use.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p433#2/r10] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | The prepositional phrase remains in an accusative syntactic position, as shown when a following conjunct is accusative and by Sibawayh's cited verse.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p433#2/r2] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | Attachment means joining one's sphere of capacity to the person or thing expressed after bāʾ.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p433#2/r3] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | Instrumental bāʾ marks a tool used to perform an action, as in striking with a sword or writing with a pen.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p433#2/r4] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | Additive bāʾ associates the act of passing with the person passed, as in passing by Zayd.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p433#2/r5] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | reports | Some grammarians call bāʾ, kāf, and lām surplus because their one-letter forms are short and can blend with the following word.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p433#2/r6] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | prefers | Expert grammarians avoid calling them surplus: bāʾ and lām are additive particles, while kāf is a preposition.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p433#2/r7] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | These are functioning particles that govern the genitive case of the nouns they precede, like min, ʿan, and fī.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p433#2/r8] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | Prepositions supply a link because verbs such as wonder, pass, and go are too weak in ordinary usage to reach these nouns directly.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p433#2/r9] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | The added preposition makes those verbs reach their nouns; genitive distinguishes indirect connection from direct verbal accusative, while nominative is already occupied by the subject.
