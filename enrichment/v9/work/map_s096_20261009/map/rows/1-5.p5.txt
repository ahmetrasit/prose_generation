[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p72/r9] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · بعضهم | reports | A further view treats ayyā as an obscure form for the accusative, with suffixes distinguishing addressee, absent person, and speaker but carrying no case role.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p73/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | Nastaʿīn derives from nastaʿūn by moving the wāw's vowel to the ʿayn and changing wāw to yāʾ after kasra.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p73/r2] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | The verbal noun istiʿāna derives from istiʿwānan; the wāw shifts its vowel, then a long vowel is dropped and hāʾ compensates for it.
[TABRISI:1:5#2/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Fronting iyyaka makes the worship and request exclusive: we worship You alone and ask help from You alone.
[TABRISI:1:5#2/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Worship is the highest gratitude: utmost submission joined with utmost reverence, owed only to God as giver of life's foundational blessings.
[TABRISI:1:5#2/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | rejects | The author rejects defining worship simply as obedience: obeying a parent or master does not itself make a person their worshiper, and idolaters worship without receiving commands from idols.
[TABRISI:1:5#2/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Nastaʿīn means asking God for success and assistance in worship and all affairs; tawfiq combines all causes needed for an act to occur.
[TABRISI:1:5#2/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | One explanation for repeating iyyaka is to prevent the mistaken impression that worship and seeking help cannot be separated.
[TABRISI:1:5#2/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Another explanation treats the repetition as emphasis, though the author questions that account because the second iyyaka is governed by a second verb.
[TABRISI:1:5#3/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | A further explanation is that the verse teaches renewing mention of God whenever a new need arises.
[TABRISI:1:5#3/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Although divine aid is necessary, the verse puts worship before asking for help: it requests the appeal for aid, which can follow an act of worship, not the aid itself.
[TABRISI:1:5#3/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | The prayer seeks help for future worship, and asking remains fitting under obligation because the servant shows dependence; prayer may also be needed before God grants a further grace. | mentions 21:112
[TABRISI:1:5#3/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | rejects | The author rejects using this prayer to prove that ability coincides with the act: one may ask for unprovided divine favors that strengthen motivation and ease obedience.
[TABRISI:1:5#3/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | A second reason to ask is to retain capacity for future obedience over time or to avoid acts that would cancel capacity, depending on one's theory of its persistence.
[TABRISI:1:5#3/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The move from third-person reference to direct address is a familiar Arabic rhetorical shift, illustrated by Labid and Abu Kathir al-Hudhali.
[TABRISI:1:5#4/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · الكسائي | reports | Al-Kisa'i supplies an implicit command to say these words, as in Qur'anic scenes where people say 'Our Lord, we have seen' and 'Peace'. | mentions 32:12, 13:23
[TABRISI:1:5/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Worship linguistically means abasement, as in a road worn smooth by traffic or a camel coated with tar; seeking assistance means asking for help.
[TABRISI:1:5/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو إسحاق الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj parses iyyaka as an accusative pronoun stem with the following kaf in genitive annexation.
[TABRISI:1:5/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو علي الفارسي | rejects | Abu Ali rejects treating iyyā as a noun: its form changes and it cannot stand in nominative or genitive contexts like an ordinary noun.
[TABRISI:1:5/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · المبرد عن الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash, transmitted by al-Mubarrad, treats iyyā as a singular pronoun whose ending changes with person and number; kaf marks address and has no case role.
[TABRISI:1:5/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The author treats yā, hā, and the other suffixes in iyyā as person markers attached to a pronoun, and allows emphatic pronouns after it.
[TABRISI:1:5/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Nastaʿīn originally had waw from maʿūna; the waw changed to yā under the preceding kasra, with its vowel shifted to the preceding consonant.
[TABRISI:1:5/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Naʿbudu and nastaʿīn are indicative because they occupy positions that can be filled by nouns; the present tense takes inflection because it resembles a noun.

[RAZI:1:5/r1] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Worship is performing an act that has been commanded, out of reverence for the commander.
[RAZI:1:5/r2] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Worship requires proving that the world has one God, able to do all possible things, knowing all knowable things, and free of need; He commands and forbids, so creatures must obey and submit.
[RAZI:1:5/r3] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | To fulfill “You alone we worship,” one must then detail all commands and prohibitions; the religious law books cover the obligations of the present dispensation.
[RAZI:1:5/r4] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | The obligations encompassed by worship also include those God revealed through earlier prophets and those He assigned to the angels in heaven from their creation.
[RAZI:1:5/r5] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Legal works explain bodily duties, while duties of the heart are greater, weightier and more elevated; books of ethics and statecraft address them across different religions and peoples.
[RAZI:1:5/r6] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | All these fields of law, worship, ethics and governance fall under “You alone we worship”; the verse contains an ocean of questions that human reason reaches only slightly.
