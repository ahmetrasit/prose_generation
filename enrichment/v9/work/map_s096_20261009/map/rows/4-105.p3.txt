[RAZI-FULL:v11p27/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · أبو علي الفارسي | reports | Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī considers three senses of arāka: visual perception is impossible for a judgment; making someone see two things would require a third object; the remaining sense is opinion or knowledge.
[RAZI-FULL:v11p27/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Here as God showed you means as God made you know: certain knowledge, free of doubt, resembles sight in clarity and force.
[RAZI-FULL:v11p27/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · عمر | reports | ʿUmar says no one should claim to have judged as God showed him, because that designation belongs only to the Prophet; ordinary judgment is conjecture, not certain knowledge.
[RAZI-FULL:v11p27/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | The learned authorities infer that the Prophet judged only by revelation and explicit textual proof.
[RAZI-FULL:v11p27/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | The implication drawn is that the community too must judge only by text, citing the order to follow the Prophet; this would make analogical reasoning forbidden. | mentions 7:158

[BAYDAWI-FULL:4:105/r1] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | reports | The occasion was Tuʿma ibn Ubayriq of Banu Ẓafar stealing a coat of mail from neighbor Qatada ibn al-Nuʿman in a leaky flour sack, hiding it with the Jew Zayd ibn al-Samin, and denying the theft; flour led investigators to Zayd, who said Tuʿma gave it to him and was backed by Jewish witnesses.
