[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p73/r4] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | The shared opening of the doubled dāl in raddahā and raddā likewise shows that hāʾ does not block the alif-like pattern.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p73/r5] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | These analogies lead to the reading that deletes the weak glide after the plural mim rather than counting moving hāʾ as a separator.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p76/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | When moving hāʾ is not counted, retaining its following yāʾ or wāw would leave two adjacent sukūns; deletion is therefore favored in fīhi hudan and khudhūhu.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p76/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Weak letters are deleted in many settings where other letters remain, including suffixes, possessive forms, pauses, and rhyme, so deleting the glide after mim is ordinary.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p76/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Poetic examples demonstrate deletion of weak endings in verse and rhyme; the text also reports a variant wording for the line with sawāfa.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p76/r4] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Deleting waw or yāʾ after ʿalayhim is light and unambiguous because these suffixes are often dropped, even when nothing remains to mark them.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p76/r5] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Repeated deletion of weak endings makes it legitimate to apply the same deletion to nouns as well as other forms.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p78/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Final alif is retained in pauses and rhyme while yāʾ may be deleted; exceptional deletion of alif is poetic necessity, so it does not refute the rule.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p78/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Since plural mim can take alif, wāw, or yāʾ and alif is not deleted, loss of wāw or yāʾ creates no ambiguity.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p78/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Both lexical and grammatical weak endings are deleted elsewhere, even without a surviving marker; that makes deletion from ʿalayhim especially fitting.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p78/r4] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Retaining the mim vowel could restore the missing glide through vowel lengthening, so a still mim best realizes the intended lightening.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p80/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | A kasra or damma may be lengthened into the very yāʾ or wāw that was deleted; still mim prevents that reversal and preserves the lighter form.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p80/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Plural mim always follows a kasra or damma, both heavy, unlike singular pronoun hāʾ, which can follow fatha or sukūn; this licenses still mim.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p80/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | The shared deletion in pause follows naturally because plural glide deletion already occurs in liaison, and pause permits still more changes.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p82/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | A vowelled mim would create four or five consecutive moving syllables, a sequence avoided in Arabic word patterns and poetic meters; deleting its vowel prevents that sequence.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p82/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Arabic meter alternates deletions in mustafʿilun to avoid five consecutive movements, an analogy for removing mim’s vowel.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p82/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | reports | Ḥamza’s damma reading treats the derived yāʾ as nonessential, like alif, so hāʾ takes damma as it does after alif; forms ʿalāk and ilāk support this.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p84/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Arabic alternations such as ḥāḥayt, ṭāʾī, and ḥārī support the close relation of yāʾ and alif; alif from wāw is comparatively rare.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p84/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Against the claim that dāwiya has an alif derived from wāw, Abū ʿAlī suggests it may derive from daw with a nisba ending, supported by a cited poetic use.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p84/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | The common analogy should prevail over a rare alternative unless evidence forces the exception; poetic shortening of nisba yāʾ does not do so.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p84/r4] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو زيد | reports | Abū Zayd reports an eloquent Arab saying ḍarabat yadāh and waḍaʿtuhu ʿalāh; al-Khalīl used such speech to explain forms with alif-like treatment.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p87/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Ṭayyiʾ uses yāʾ where other dialects use final alif in both pause and liaison, as in afʿay for afʿā; this supports treating yāʾ in ʿalayhim like alif.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p87/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Ḥijāz and Qays use final hāʾ in both pause and liaison, paralleling Ṭayyiʾ’s yāʾ substitution and strengthening the analogy.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p87/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | The attested dialect and the analogous Ḥijāzī/Tamīmī alternations establish the language and make its use for Ḥamza’s reading legitimate.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p87/r4] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | rejects | Replacing alif with wāw is weaker than replacing it with yāʾ because it is less attested and less supported by analogy.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p87/r5] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Poetic meter balances duration, so the longer alif cannot share the rhyme position of yāʾ and wāw.

[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p89/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Alif’s greater duration appears in how hamza is softened after alif but not after yāʾ or wāw, strengthening its distinct treatment.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p89/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Yāʾ and wāw do not assimilate into alif because alif is long and always still; sibilants likewise resist stops because their extra hiss is absent from stops.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p89/r4] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | A derived letter can retain the behavior of its source: yāʾ originating from alif in ʿalayhim is treated as alif, so hāʾ is rounded as after alif.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p91/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Examples such as ruwiya, ruwiyya, and nuwwi show that a replacement wāw can inherit the behavior of its source; bīs similarly keeps its original kasra after hamza is dropped.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p91/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Therefore the yāʾ in ʿalayhim may behave as the alif from which it derives, supporting Ḥamza’s damma on hāʾ.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p91/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | Yāʾ can also be fixed across case positions like alif, preserving one surface form; he cites ayādī sabā, bādī badā, qālī qalā, and maʿdīkarib.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p91/r4] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو علي | holds | These fixed-yāʾ forms occur in accusative position, whether analyzed as one of the cited nominal patterns or as an attached ending.
