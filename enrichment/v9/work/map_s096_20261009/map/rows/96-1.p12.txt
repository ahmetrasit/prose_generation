[IBNASHUR:96:1/r12] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The opening hints that Muhammad's community will come to know reading, writing, and learning.
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r13] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The surah directs him to reflect on God's creation, especially the astonishing human being formed from a clot, as a starting point for inquiry.
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r14] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The surah threatens those who deny the Prophet and try to stop him from prayer, calling others to guidance, and practicing piety.
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r15] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | It assures Muhammad that God knows his opponents, will subdue them, and will support His messenger.
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r16] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | It steadies the Messenger in the truth he received, in prayer, and in drawing near to God, and tells him not to be daunted by enemies whose power God's power overcomes.
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r17] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · عائشة | reports | Aisha's report is Ibn Ashur's basis for identifying these five verses as the first revelation to Muhammad in Hira.
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r18] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Opening with “Read” foretells that the Messenger will recite a book despite having recited no book before the Qur'an, as 29:48 states; he told Gabriel, “I am not a reader.” | mentions 29:48
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r19] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | This opening is a fine instance of barāʿat al-istihlāl, an apt opening that anticipates the Qur'an's significance.
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r2] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Mushafs and most commentaries call it Surat al-Alaq because that word occurs near its beginning; some tafsirs use the name too.
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r20] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Qara'a means to pronounce specified speech, whether it is written or memorized by heart.
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r21] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Ibn Ashur points to the earlier discussion of reciting the Qur'an and seeking refuge from Satan in 16:98. | mentions 16:98
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r22] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The imperative is literal, asking for reading now or in the near future; the Prophet is to say what will shortly be dictated, since no text had yet been dictated or given to him.
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r23] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The command resembles a teacher telling a student to write before dictating: it prepares the student for the text that is about to be supplied.
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r24] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · عائشة | reports | Aisha's report in the two Sahih collections describes Gabriel pressing the Prophet three times; each time he answered that he could not read, and at the third command Gabriel recited the opening verses through “what he did not know.”
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r25] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Aisha's wording that Muhammad returned bearing the revelation and Khadija asked Waraqa to hear him shows he understood and recited the verses after the third command; Gabriel's repetitions familiarized him with an unlearned act.
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r26] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The object of “read” is omitted either because the verb is used intransitively to mean begin reading, or because context makes clear that the object is what will be revealed of the Qur'an.
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r27] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | One analysis begins the recitation with “In God's name”: bi marks assistance and attaches either to an omitted “begin” or to a second “read,” fronted to emphasize God's name; it implies Muhammad uttered the phrase on receiving the revelation.
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r28] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Alternatively, bi marks accompaniment: recite with awareness of God's majesty; fronting gives the phrase exclusivity and rejects invoking idols such as al-Lat and al-Uzza, establishing Islam's first principle of monotheism.
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r29] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | A third analysis takes bi as “upon,” as in 3:75: read upon your Lord's name, meaning at His permission, since the angel was sent by Him. | mentions 3:75
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r3] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · البخاري | reports | Al-Bukhari titles the surah with the full opening phrase, “Read in the name of your Lord who created.”
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r30] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Calling God “your Lord” rather than using the proper name Allah evokes care for the one nurtured and, with the possessive pronoun, rejects rivals claimed as lords; the verse grounds Islamic monotheism.
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r31] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The relative clause signals why God's name should be invoked: He is Creator, whereas idols are not; polytheists themselves acknowledged that God created the heavens and earth, so creation is the clearest proof of uniqueness. | mentions 31:25
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r4] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · الكواشي | reports | Al-Kawashi calls it Surat Iqra wa-l-Alaq; the source also notes the shorter title Surat Iqra.
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r5] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · ابن عطية، أبو بكر بن العربي | reports | Ibn Atiyya and Abu Bakr ibn al-Arabi call this surah Surat al-Qalam; those using that name call the other Qalam surah Surat Nun, though al-Itqan does not list this surah among those with multiple names.
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r6] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The surah is Meccan by consensus.
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r7] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · عائشة، أبو موسى الأشعري | reports | Sound reports from Aisha establish that these first five verses were first, delivered to Muhammad in Hira on Ramadan 17, year 40 after the Elephant; Abu Musa and most exegetes agree.
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r8] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · جابر | reports | Jabir reports that al-Muddaththir was the first surah revealed.
[IBNASHUR:96:1/r9] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Ibn Ashur reconciles Jabir's report by taking al-Muddaththir as the first surah after the interruption in revelation, just as al-Duha followed a second interruption, as al-Itqan explains.
[IBNASHUR:96:2/r3] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The first “created” may be treated as intransitive, with its object omitted for generality (“all creation”), or with “the human” anticipated from the following clause.
[IBNASHUR:96:5/r7] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The command to read followed by teaching through the pen hints that Muhammad remained unlettered, a feature completing the Qur'an's miraculous character, as 29:48 says. | mentions 29:48
[IBNASHUR:96:5/r8] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | These are the final verses of the five that were first revealed to Muhammad in the cave of Hira.

[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p87/r10] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · عبد الله بن مسعود | reports | In Bukhari, Ibn Masʿud describes surah pairs the Prophet recited in each rakʿa; Alqama identifies twenty surahs at the start of the Mufassal in Ibn Masʿud's order, ending with al-Dukhan and al-Nabaʾ.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p87/r11] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · الجمهور | reports | The majority hold that many surahs were already arranged during the Prophet's lifetime.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p87/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The last revealed verse is variously placed in al-Baqara, al-Nisaʾ, or Baraʾa, all of which precede many later surahs in the present arrangement.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p87/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Companions' personal codices differed in surah order; Ibn Masʿud and Ubayy had codices of their own.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p87/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | Ibn Ashur cites an unidentified report that Salim, the freedman of Abu Hudhayfa, was the first to collect the Quran in a codex.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p87/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · السيوطي | reports | Al-Suyuti reports that some Companions ordered their codices by revelation as best they knew; Ali's began with Iqra, then al-Muddaththir, al-Muzzammil, and al-Takwir, continuing through the Meccan surahs before the Medinan ones.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p87/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Others ordered codices from longer surahs to shorter ones: Ubayy and Ibn Masʿud began with al-Baqara, al-Nisaʾ, and Aal ʿImran, and Uthman used this method for the official codex.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p87/r7] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · عبد الله بن عباس | reports | Al-Tirmidhi and Abu Dawud transmit Ibn Abbas's question to Uthman about pairing al-Anfal and Baraʾa, omitting a basmala between them, and placing them among the seven long surahs.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p87/r8] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · عثمان بن عفان | reports | Uthman said the Prophet directed scribes where to place new revelations; Anfal was among the first Medinan surahs, Baraʾa among the last, and their similar stories led him to pair them, while the Prophet left their relation unclear.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p87/r9] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | This account shows that the basmala marked boundaries between surahs; Uthman omitted it between Anfal and Baraʾa because he was not certain they were separate, though that remained the likelier view.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p433/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · عائشة | reports | Aisha reports that the first Qur'anic surah revealed was named by its opening words, Iqra bi-smi rabbika; several Successors transmit the same title, used by al-Tirmidhi.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p433/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | It is also commonly called al-Alaq because that word occurs near the beginning; Bukhari titles it Iqra bi-smi rabbika alladhi khalaq, and others call it Iqra or Iqra wa-l-Alaq.
