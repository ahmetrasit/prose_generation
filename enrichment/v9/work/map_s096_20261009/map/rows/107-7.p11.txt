[ASAD-NOTES:107:1#n4/r2] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | More broadly, al-ma'un means aid or assistance in any difficulty.
[ASAD-NOTES:107:intro/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | The surah was revealed in the mission's early years, probably after surah 102; its title comes from al-ma'un in the last verse.

[BINTSHATI:v2p190/r1] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | Ma'un can mean water or rain, useful borrowed household items, especially a vessel, or zakat as a small required share withheld from someone in need.

[BINTSHATI:v2p190/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · أكثر المفسرين | reports | Most commentators, as al-Razi reports, understand ma'un as ordinary tools people lend each other, such as an axe, bucket, fire striker, salt, water, or fire.

[BINTSHATI:v2p190/r8] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الطبري | reports | Al-Tabari combines the readings: people withhold ordinary shared necessities and the needy's required rights in their wealth, all of which are mutual benefits.

[CORPUSCORANICUM:107:4-7:kommentar/r3] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | holds | The closing verse adds refusal of assistance, generalizing the earlier failure to support orphans and the poor, who represent socially vulnerable people in the Hebrew Bible.
[CORPUSCORANICUM:107:4-7:kommentar/r4] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | holds | The two groups cross-correspond: verse 1 and verses 4–6 concern religious omissions, while verses 2–3 and verse 7 concern social omissions; the first group stresses social failings and the second religious ones.
[CORPUSCORANICUM:107:4-7:kommentar/r5] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | holds | Māʿūn and manaʿa are near-anagrams built from permutations of the same consonants, m-n-ʿ and m-ʿ-n.
[CORPUSCORANICUM:107:4-7:kommentar/r6] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | holds | The rare hapax māʿūn was likely chosen instead of a commoner word for its assonance with manaʿa; the sound pattern sharpens the contrast between expected assistance and its denial, a figure later rhetoricians call reversed paronomasia.
[CORPUSCORANICUM:107:6:anmerkung/r2] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | holds | If verses 6–7 are combined despite the rhyme potential at yurāʾūn, the sura has a symmetrical structure of two triads.
[CORPUSCORANICUM:107:7:anmerkung/r1] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | reports | Q 68:12 and 70:21 provide parallels for withholding good or help. | mentions 68:12, 70:21
[CORPUSCORANICUM:107:7:anmerkung/r2] CORPUSCORANICUM · Geiger | reports | Geiger derives māʿūn from Hebrew māʿôn, meaning dwelling or refuge, and interprets the verse as denying shelter to people seeking help.
[CORPUSCORANICUM:107:7:anmerkung/r3] CORPUSCORANICUM · Nöldeke | rejects | Nöldeke accepts Geiger's derivation but rejects the assumption that Arabic must preserve the Hebrew word's original meaning.
[CORPUSCORANICUM:107:7:anmerkung/r4] CORPUSCORANICUM · Nöldeke | reports | Nöldeke cites al-Aʿshā Maimūn's verse praising someone as more generous with his benefits than a foaming Euphrates channel, supporting the sense benefit or gift.
[CORPUSCORANICUM:107:7:anmerkung/r5] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | holds | The Hebrew loan māʿūn apparently already meant benefit, gift, or donation in pre-Qur'anic Arabic.
[CORPUSCORANICUM:107:7:anmerkung/r6] CORPUSCORANICUM · Rhodokanakis | reports | Rhodokanakis agrees with Nöldeke and explains the Arabic semantic development through interference from maʿūna, from root ʿ-w-n.
[CORPUSCORANICUM:107:7:anmerkung/r7] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | holds | A deliberate Qur'anic echo of Hebrew māʿôn remains possible despite the Arabic word's changed meaning.
