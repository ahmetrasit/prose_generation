[ALUSI:96:11/r33] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Although revealed about Abū Jahl, the warning applies to everyone who stops prayer. It does not forbid objecting to prayer in usurped places or disliked times, since the objection concerns an accompanying attribute rather than prayer itself.
[ALUSI:96:11/r34] ALUSI, d. 1270 · علي | reports | A report says ʿAlī saw people praying before the ʿĪd prayer; when asked why he did not forbid them, he feared the warning in this verse. Another version says he preferred to tell them what he had seen the Prophet do rather than forbid a worshipper.
[ALUSI:96:11/r35] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبو حنيفة | reports | Al-Ālūsī says Abū Ḥanīfa likewise avoided a direct prohibition: asked by Abū Yūsuf about a prayer formula while rising from bowing, he stated the formula he favored and continued to prostration without explicitly forbidding it.
[ALUSI:96:11/r36] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | The warning extends by analogy to forbidding other worship, whether by words or conduct; distracting someone from worship also counts as a practical prohibition, a widespread affliction.
[ALUSI:96:11/r4] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبو حيان | reports | Abū Ḥayyān relates that the prayer in question was the noon prayer.
[ALUSI:96:11/r5] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبو حيان | reports | A further account calls it the first congregational prayer in Islam: the Prophet prayed with Abū Bakr and ʿAlī; Abū Ṭālib passed with Jaʿfar, urged him to join his cousin, and left pleased.
[ALUSI:96:11/r6] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبو طالب | reports | The cited poem attributed to Abū Ṭālib calls ʿAlī and Jaʿfar trusted kin in hardship and urges them not to abandon the Prophet but to support their maternal and paternal cousin.
[ALUSI:96:11/r7] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | rejects | Al-Ālūsī questions the Abū Ṭālib account: prayer was prescribed on the Night Journey, usually dated a year before the Hijra, whereas Abū Ṭālib died about three years before it. Reports place the Journey five years after the mission, a chronology criticized by others.
[ALUSI:96:11/r8] ALUSI, d. 1270 · ابن حزم | reports | Ibn Ḥazm claims consensus that the Night Journey was one year before the Hijra; Ibn Fāris gives one year and three months, and al-Suddī one year and five months.
[ALUSI:96:11/r9] ALUSI, d. 1270 · الزهري | reports | Al-Qāḍī ʿIyāḍ reports al-Zuhrī's view that the Night Journey occurred five years after the prophetic mission; al-Nawawī and al-Qurṭubī favor it, though al-Ālūsī notes objections to that dating.
[ALUSI:96:12/r1] ALUSI, d. 1270 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the commentators agree that the worshipper is the Messenger and the person forbidding him is Abu Jahl.
[ALUSI:96:12/r10] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | rejects | Al-Ālūsī objects that Abū Ṭālib died about three years before Hijra, shortly before Khadīja, so on the usual chronology he could not have witnessed obligatory prayer.
[ALUSI:96:12/r11] ALUSI, d. 1270 · القاضي عياض | reports | Qāḍī ʿIyāḍ transmits from al-Zuhrī that the Night Journey occurred five years after the mission began, a dating endorsed by al-Nawawī and al-Qurṭubī; al-Ālūsī notes objections to it.
[ALUSI:96:12/r12] ALUSI, d. 1270 · بعضهم | reports | Some understand the prohibition as prevention; its wording hints that Abu Jahl lacked the power to do more than forbid.
[ALUSI:96:12/r13] ALUSI, d. 1270 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās reports an explicit verbal prohibition while the Prophet prayed; al-Tirmidhī authenticated the report.
[ALUSI:96:12/r14] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | The present or future form evokes the past scene vividly because the incident is strange.
[ALUSI:96:12/r15] ALUSI, d. 1270 · بعضهم | reports | Some take the vision in the first and third questions as intellectual rather than visual; grammarians hold two views on this construction. | mentions 96:13
[ALUSI:96:12/r16] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Here arayta means Tell me: because seeing leads to knowledge, the question functions as an inquiry into what is seen.
[ALUSI:96:12/r17] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | In one parsing, the first question’s object is the relative clause, while those of the second and third are omitted pronouns or demonstratives; the third question’s second object is ألم يعلم بأن الله يرى. | mentions 96:11, 96:13, 96:14
[ALUSI:96:12/r19] ALUSI, d. 1270 · بعض الأئمة | reports | Some authorities say the question construction works with both visual and intellectual seeing; grammarians differ over its analysis.
[ALUSI:96:12/r2] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبو هريرة | reports | Abu Hurayra reports that Abu Jahl swore to trample the Prophet if he prayed; when he approached, he recoiled, describing fire and terror between them. The Prophet said angels would seize him limb by limb, and 96:6 through the sūra’s end was revealed. | mentions 96:6
[ALUSI:96:12/r20] ALUSI, d. 1270 · جمع | reports | A group takes the address throughout as directed to anyone capable of understanding; another view makes it a generic address to humankind, as in 96:8. | mentions 96:8
[ALUSI:96:12/r21] ALUSI, d. 1270 · بعض | reports | Some take the indefinite form of ʿabdan as simple indefiniteness; another analysis uses it to magnify the Prophet and intensify the astonishment.
[ALUSI:96:12/r22] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Restricting the prohibition to the time clause suggests it occurred during prayer; separating the clauses gives each reproach and threat independent force.
[ALUSI:96:12/r23] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Al-Ālūsī reads three escalating reproaches: first for forbidding prayer without regard to the forbidder’s state, then on the state he claims, and finally on his actual denial and turning away, each ending in divine threat. | mentions 96:11, 96:13, 96:14
[ALUSI:96:12/r3] ALUSI, d. 1270 · الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan identifies the forbidding person as Umayya b. Khalaf, who stopped Salman from praying.
[ALUSI:96:12/r33] ALUSI, d. 1270 · البحر | reports | Earlier interpreters do not address the Prophet or the unbelieving forbidder, since context removes both from the address; al-Baḥr favors the Prophet, while another view permits addressing the unbeliever.
[ALUSI:96:12/r37] ALUSI, d. 1270 · بعضهم | reports | Another explanation says the first prohibition occurred during prayer but could concern prayer or something else; prayer perfects the worshipper and guides others, so forbidding during it could block both, hence the later mention of piety. | mentions 96:9
[ALUSI:96:12/r39] ALUSI, d. 1270 · عصام الدين | reports | ʿIṣām al-Dīn reads 96:9 as exposing tyranny born of self-sufficiency, 96:11 as reproaching lost guidance and piety, 96:13 as reproaching earned punishment and distance from God, and 96:14 as the threat after both reproaches. | mentions 96:9, 96:11, 96:13, 96:14
