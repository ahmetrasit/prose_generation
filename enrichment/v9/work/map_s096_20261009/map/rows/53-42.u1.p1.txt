Anas or Sufyan al-Thawri transmit 'when the Lord is mentioned, stop.' Abu Hurayra, Ibn Abbas, and Abu Dharr transmit the creation-versus-Creator instruction because thought cannot encompass Him, with Abu Dharr warning of ruin. Al-Thaʿlabi's Abu Hurayra report describes layers of earth, heavens, and a sea in the seventh heaven. Ibn Kathir says the wording of Abu Hurayra's raised report is not preserved; he cites a sound Satanic-question hadith requiring refuge and stopping, and a Sunan report including an angel's three-hundred-year earlobe-to-shoulder distance. Al-Qurtubi also links the Satanic-question hadith and cites a poem directing thought from God's essence to His works. Al-Durr transmits Yunus ibn Maysara's account of the companions discussing God's greatness and the Prophet redirecting them, and a Kufa imam's report of reflection on the sun and a radiant western land inhabited by beings unaware whether Adam or Iblis was created. Al-Biqai adds Ibn Abbas's inability-to-grasp-God's-measure report. Imam al-Sadiq applies the verse to stopping speech when it reaches God. Imam al-Baqir warns Ziyad that forbidden disputation brings doubt, void deeds, and confusion, or wandering in a variant. Al-Wahidi specifies that divine knowledge, essence, and modality cannot be comprehended; al-Alusi says most transmitted proofs indicate the essence cannot be known in its full reality. Al-Jishumi gives no grading for the creation report.

53:42/q08 How does the verse support God's existence? [* · theology]
  53:42/q08/p1 Reason's chain of contingent causes must end with God's necessary, uncaused existence. — reasons: Al-Razi says each contingent thing requires a cause, and another contingent thing cannot be the final explanatory terminus. He reads the reports against speculation about the Lord as the point where one reaches the uncaused necessary existent. He rejects indiscriminately transferring this explanation to every return or ascent verse, including 35:10, though he finds it clear here. Study Quran reports God as Necessary Being and source of all beings.
53:42/q09 What does the verse teach about God's unity and lordship? [* · theology] (turns on: The notes offer complementary proofs: al-Razi argues from necessary existence, while others describe the scope of divine governance.)
  53:42/q09/p1 The final necessary existent is one; two necessary beings could not each be the final terminus. — reasons: Al-Razi says the terminus is necessary existence precisely as necessary, making a plurality of necessary beings impossible.
  53:42/q09/p2 God's lordship embraces all creation, relations, affairs, and governance from origin through resurrection. — reasons: Al-Tabatabai says all systems of governance end with God, who creates beings and their relations, directly or through causes; he cites 39:62–63 and 7:54. Al-Bursevi cites 42:53, says there is no deity but God, and that all affairs return to Him. Al-Biqai says beginnings, destinations, deeds, knowledge, hopes, time, and place depend on Him. Al-Husayn, reported by al-Sulami and Bursevi, defines unity by God's encompassing or causal priority: contingent effects start and end with Him and vanish as He remains; al-Sulami cites 57:3.
53:42/q10 What cautions do commentators place on interpretations and titles for God? [* · theology] (turns on: Whether proposed theological interpretations and divine titles are acceptable to these commentators.)
  53:42/q10/p1 Reading the verse as union is heretical and must be rejected. — reasons: Al-Biqai rejects union and any other heretical reading, invoking a curse on its interpreter, defender, and silent supporter.
  53:42/q10/p2 The title 'cause of causes' should not be used to invoke or describe God. — reasons: Bursevi reports that some great authorities use it as a proof of unity for those lacking unveiling, but calls the title unscriptural and forbids it for invocation or description. Al-Razi uses 'cause of causes' in explaining the Prophet-directed address, without Bursevi's qualification.
53:42/q11 How is ending with God understood inwardly? [* · inward] (turns on: These reports develop different experiential images of reaching God: transformed will, converging paths, vanished effects, and boundless reality.)
  53:42/q11/p1 Knowing God's lordship removes temptation and leaves the servant willing only what God chooses. — reasons: Ibn ʿAtaʾ, reported by al-Sulami, says every trial turns away when a servant reaches that knowledge. Bursevi reports his formulation that whoever begins from God ends with Him and loses any will apart from God's choice.
  53:42/q11/p2 All paths to God converge in one end, despite the number of paths matching creatures' breaths. — reasons: Bursevi says their unity lies in the end and God brings the seeker after death to the aspiration sought.
  53:42/q11/p3 Contingent things begin and end in God, then disappear while their abiding cause remains. — reasons: Al-Husayn defines tawhid through God's encompassing presence in al-Sulami's account, citing 'He is the First' (57:3); Bursevi reports his cause-and-effect formulation.
  53:42/q11/p4 God's boundlessness dwarfs all other existences as a sea dwarfs streams and seas. — reasons: Bursevi quotes Rumi's poetic comparison.
53:42/q12 How does this verse advance the surrounding argument? [* · links] (turns on: The notes connect the clause to several parts of its context: recompense, witnessing deeds, and the earlier scriptures.)
  53:42/q12/p1 It locates the full recompense promised just before at the resurrection before God. — reasons: Al-Razi says it answers when the preceding recompense happens: the grateful receive reward and the ungrateful punishment. Kuranyolu says the judgment then gives each deed its full recompense, without the slightest injustice; grace and forgiveness remain subject to God's will.
  53:42/q12/p2 It closes the chain of proof by naming God as the ultimate witness over deeds. — reasons: Al-Biqai contrasts appointed angels who may see deeds with the supreme King himself, whose witnessing inspires greater awe.
  53:42/q12/p3 It continues the enduring teachings attributed to Moses' and Abraham's scriptures. — reasons: Al-Zajjaj and Ibn al-Jawzi reporting him tie the whole passage to 53:36–37. Ibn Ashur says this clause may share verse 40's place and cites Abraham's 'going to my Lord' in 37:99 as a possible parallel. Study Quran's introduction says the sura's third section moves from reflecting on creation to these enduring scriptural truths. Islahi says this challenges claims that descent from those prophets guarantees Paradise and calls attention to their teachings and sacrifices.
