20:130/q19 What do reports say about guarding the two prayers before sunrise and sunset? [قَبْلَ طُلُوعِ ٱلشَّمْسِ وَقَبْلَ غُرُوبِهَا · reports] (turns on: These are distinct reports about the same two prayer times, with different promised merits or wording.)
  20:130/q19/p1 The Prophet likened believers' vision of God to seeing the full moon, then urged guarding the two prayers and cited this verse. — reasons: Jarir transmits the report across Yahya, Tabari, Baghawi, Ibn Kathir, Biqai, Durr and Bursevi; Ibn Kathir calls it authentic and Biqai says both Sahih collections transmit it.
  20:130/q19/p2 Whoever performs the prayers before sunrise and sunset will not enter the Fire. — reasons: Ibn Kathir cites Muslim through Umara ibn Ruwayba; Durr repeats the report.
  20:130/q19/p3 The Prophet called them the two asr prayers and explained them as prayers before sunrise and sunset. — reasons: Durr and Alusi transmit the instruction to Fadala ibn Wahb.
20:130/q20 What further reports describe divine vision and reward? [لَعَلَّكَ تَرْضَىٰ · reports] (turns on: The reports describe different events or degrees of heavenly reward.)
  20:130/q20/p1 The highest-ranking person in Paradise sees God twice daily, unlike the lowest rank's thousand-year kingdom. — reasons: Ibn Kathir transmits a report from Ibn Umar.
  20:130/q20/p2 God asks the people of Paradise if they are content, then grants His abiding pleasure. — reasons: Ibn Kathir calls the report authentic.
  20:130/q20/p3 The lifting of the veil and vision of God surpass all prior gifts to Paradise's people. — reasons: Ibn Kathir reports the people recall whitened faces, heavy scales, rescue from Fire and admission to Paradise, then receive the increase of vision.
20:130/q21 What reports or rulings concern prayer practice beyond the fixed times? [وَسَبِّحْ فَسَبِّحْ · law] (turns on: Different reported legal allowances and merits concerning prayer practice.)
  20:130/q21/p1 Prayers may be combined during travel or rain, and advancing them is preferable. — reasons: Jishumi transmits al-Shafi'i's view.
  20:130/q21/p2 Prayers may be combined for occupation and a valid excuse. — reasons: Jishumi transmits al-Hadi's allowance.
  20:130/q21/p3 Combining is allowed generally, while Abu Hanifa distinguishes which prayers may be advanced. — reasons: Jishumi reports these further legal approaches without details of Abu Hanifa's distinctions.
  20:130/q21/p4 Isha and Fajr are hardest on hypocrites, though their reward would bring attendance even by crawling. — reasons: Bursevi transmits the hadith.
  20:130/q21/p5 Keeping the five prayers in congregation brings broad benefits; neglect brings losses. — reasons: Bursevi relays a report without enumerating all its benefits or losses in the note.
  20:130/q21/p6 The community receives mercy and calamities are averted through sincerity, prayer, supplication and weak members. — reasons: Bursevi transmits this hadith and a report that Daniel ascribed rescue from Noah's flood, Ad's wind and Thamud's blast to prayer like this community's.
20:130/q22 How many prescribed prayers does this verse encompass, and when? [وَسَبِّحْ قَبْلَ طُلُوعِ ٱلشَّمْسِ وَقَبْلَ غُرُوبِهَا وَمِنْ ءَانَآئِ ٱلَّيْلِ وَأَطْرَافَ ٱلنَّهَارِ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the verse is a fixed prayer timetable, includes extra devotions, or uses tasbih more generally; chronology of five prayers is also disputed.)
  20:130/q22/p1 It encompasses all five daily prayers. — reasons: Qatada's common mapping is Fajr, Asr, Maghrib, Isha and Dhuhr; Ibn Abbas and Razi assign Dhuhr with Asr before sunset and repeat dawn or sunset at the edges. Razi prefers the five-prayer interpretation among prayer readings; Ibn Ashur relies on mass-transmitted practice. Islahi says the verse rebuts claims that prayer times are absent from the Qur'an, while the Prophet defines each prayer's extent.
  20:130/q22/p2 It names four fixed prayers and omits noon. — reasons: Razi reports the four-prayer interpretation; Tabatabai compares the four-prayer pattern of 11:114 and argues both suras predate the institution of five after the Ascension, citing 17:78.
  20:130/q22/p3 It includes five obligatory prayers and also additional voluntary worship in night and day. — reasons: Razi reports this second prayer reading; Ibn Atiyya and Biqai allow voluntary rites. Islahi specifies tahajjud and ishraq and says extra worship should be maintained in collective or individual hardship.
  20:130/q22/p4 It primarily urges continuous glorification rather than legislating a count of obligatory prayers. — reasons: Tabatabai says the lexicon does not require prescribed prayer; Kur'an Yolu argues five were not yet obligatory in the sura's period and takes thought, word and deed as means of peace.
20:130/q23 What was the historical sequence of obligatory prayer? [وَسَبِّحْ · sciences] (turns on: How the verse's date relates to the development of the five obligatory prayers.)
  20:130/q23/p1 Prayer began as two cycles at dawn and evening; after the Ascension five two-cycle prayers except sunset were imposed, then completed after migration. — reasons: Muqatil reports the sequence.
  20:130/q23/p2 This command predates the obligation of five prayers. — reasons: Durr transmits al-Suddi; Tabatabai dates Ta-Ha and Hud before the post-Ascension al-Isra and suggests four daily prayers then, with five later at 17:78. Kur'an Yolu also uses the earlier date against a five-prayer reading.
20:130/q24 How does this verse connect to its immediate context and other verses? [* · links] (turns on: The sources connect different clauses to prior delay, prayer parallels and later promises.)
  20:130/q24/p1 The fixed but delayed judgment in 20:129 grounds patience in 20:130, followed by worship rather than preoccupation with worldly comfort. — reasons: Tabari and Ibn Ashur explain the prior decree and term; Qashani invokes 8:33 and the Prophet's mercy to explain why the community is spared; Bursevi and Alusi call delay respite. Ibn Ashur and Islahi connect patience and prayer to the warning against coveting comforts and to the God-fearing outcome; Kur'an Yolu reads the sura's end as encouragement under pressure.
  20:130/q24/p2 The command recalls seeking help through patience and prayer in 2:45 and 2:153. — reasons: Razi and Alusi explicitly compare those verses; Tabatabai compares them when explaining contentment with decree.
  20:130/q24/p3 The day-end expressions and repeated prayers are clarified by 11:114, 2:238 and related prayer-time verses. — reasons: Farra, Kashshaf, Razi, Ibn Ashur, Tabatabai and Asad invoke Hud 11:114 or the middle prayer in 2:238; Bursevi notes parallel discussions at Hud and Qaf.
  20:130/q24/p4 The promised contentment recalls 93:5, with some readings also compared with 19:55 or the high station of 17:79. — reasons: Farisi, Wahidi, Baghawi, Razi, Ibn Kathir, Bursevi and Islahi invoke these promises and prophetic descriptions in arguing over reward, intercession and passive reading.
20:130/q25 Was verse 20:130 revealed in Mecca or Medina? [* · sciences] (turns on: Reports of exceptions to the sura's Meccan classification versus the local style and context.)
  20:130/q25/p1 It belongs with the generally Meccan sura. — reasons: Ibn Ashur reports the majority classify all of Ta-Ha as Meccan; Kur'an Yolu doubts the Medinan reports because the style is Meccan; Study Quran says context supports Mecca.
  20:130/q25/p2 Verses 20:130–131 are Medinan exceptions to the Meccan sura. — reasons: Ibn Ashur reports al-Sakhawi's exception for 20:130 and possible 20:131; Study Quran notes this classification.
