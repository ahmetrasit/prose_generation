## THE CURRENT MAP
22:40/q01 Who were the people expelled from their homes? [ٱلَّذِينَ أُخْرِجُوا۟ · referent] (turns on: Whether the verse describes the emigrants generally, Muhammad with his companions, or extends to his family.)
  22:40/q01/p1 Believers expelled by Quraysh from Mecca, especially the Emigrants. — reasons: Al-Tabari and Baydawi name Mecca and Quraysh; Razi uses 22:41 to distinguish the emigrants from the Helpers. Al-Tabataba'i says the whole community is described by some members' ordeal.
  22:40/q01/p2 Muhammad and his companions specifically. — reasons: Sa'id ibn Jubayr relates the alarm at the Prophet's departure; Ibn Abbas names the Prophet and companions and their move to Medina.
  22:40/q01/p3 The emigrants, with the Prophet's family also included by application. — reasons: Abu Ja'far says the family of Muhammad too were expelled and intimidated.
22:40/q02 How were the believers driven out, and where did they go? [أُخْرِجُوا۟ مِن دِيَٰرِهِم · reports] (turns on: The reports differ in destination and in whether expulsion means direct removal or persecution that forced departure.)
  22:40/q02/p1 Quraysh tortured, threatened, and harassed believers until they left Mecca for Medina. — reasons: Muqatil and Tabari describe torture and threats; Ibn al-Arabi says some were tempted from faith. Tabataba'i says compelled departure, not physical deportation.
  22:40/q02/p2 Some fled to Abyssinia, others to Medina, and some endured in Mecca. — reasons: Tabrisi treats Medina as implying a Medinan revelation and Abyssinia as implying a Meccan one; Biqa'i also names the mountain valley.
  22:40/q02/p3 Specific Meccan emigrants were prevented from migrating to Medina. — reasons: Mujahid says fighting was permitted against the unbelievers who blocked their migration.
22:40/q03 How does 'those expelled' attach grammatically to the previous verse? [ٱلَّذِينَ أُخْرِجُوا۟ · grammar] (turns on: The relative may continue an earlier relative in the genitive or stand in the nominative or accusative by ellipsis.)
  22:40/q03/p1 It is genitive, qualifying or replacing the preceding relative expression. — reasons: Tabari says the second relative resumes those permitted to fight; Ibn Ashur ties it to 22:39. Kashshaf calls the genitive most apparent.
  22:40/q03/p2 It is accusative with an implied 'I mean' or as praise. — reasons: Tabrisi and Samin give an omitted verb of specification or praise.
  22:40/q03/p3 It is nominative with an implied subject such as 'they'. — reasons: The construction begins a nominal clause with an understood subject.
22:40/q04 How is the expulsion attributed to Quraysh if believers left under pressure? [أُخْرِجُوا۟ · rhetoric]
  22:40/q04/p1 A forced departure is attributed to the coercer, assigning blame to Quraysh. — reasons: Qurtubi compares 9:40, 'when the unbelievers expelled him'; Tabataba'i details persecution forcing departure.
22:40/q05 What spiritual failings can betray the way of those defended by God? [ٱلَّذِينَ أُخْرِجُوا۟ · inward] (turns on: Qushayri distinguishes motives by spiritual station.)
  22:40/q05/p1 Ascetics, worshippers, gnostics, lovers and unifiers each risk betrayal at their own station. — reasons: Qushayri names seeking afterlife compensation, falling back on concessions, seeking rank, craving relief from longing, and renewed self-choice after effacement. Biqa'i says sincere devotion attracts plots and harm.
  22:40/q05/p2 God protects the common through the elect as a lasting divine custom. — reasons: Qushayri says He pardons lesser people for the honor of the great.
22:40/q06 What does diyār denote and how is dār derived? [دِيَٰرِهِم · meaning]
  22:40/q06/p1 It is their dwellings, homes, or country; dār denotes an enclosed circuit. — reasons: Bursevi says the plural can denote countries because inhabitants move within them; al-Raghib derives dār from a wall's circuit.
22:40/q07 May houses in Mecca be sold or leased? [دِيَٰرِهِم · law] (turns on: Whether residents own their houses despite pilgrimage access.)
  22:40/q07/p1 Sale and lease are permissible. — reasons: Baghawi prefers this: the verse calls the homes theirs; the Prophet called Abu Sufyan's house his, and Umar bought a Meccan house for a prison for four thousand dirhams. Tawus, Amr ibn Dinar, and al-Shafi'i allow sale.
  22:40/q07/p2 Sale and lease are impermissible because pilgrims have common access. — reasons: Abd al-Rahman ibn Sabit says pilgrims had equal entitlement to houses; Umar forbade closing doors during pilgrimage.
22:40/q08 What makes the expulsion 'without right'? [بِغَيْرِ حَقٍّ · interpretation] (turns on: The sources distinguish the absence of a lawful offense from the inherent right to remain in one's homeland.)
  22:40/q08/p1 They committed no wrong; their sole alleged offense was affirming God's oneness. — reasons: Al-Hasan says they shed no blood, stole no property, and severed no kinship, citing 85:8; Ibn Kathir cites 60:1 and 85:8; Razi cites 5:59. Ibn Ashur says a declaration of faith harms no one. Al-Jubba'i says no action merited exile; Ibn Ashur notes the religious cause.
  22:40/q08/p2 People have a natural right to live in their homeland unless a valid law or custom warrants exile. — reasons: Ibn Ashur traces the right to settlement or conquest; legitimate banishment punishes wrongdoing, and Zuhayr's verse illustrates old customary grounds.
22:40/q09 How does 'without right' work grammatically? [بِغَيْرِ حَقٍّ · grammar] (turns on: Circumstantial qualification versus an omitted verbal noun.)
  22:40/q09/p1 It is a circumstantial phrase describing the exiles' unjust state. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says no valid ground existed for imposing removal.
  22:40/q09/p2 It qualifies an omitted cognate verbal noun: an unjust expulsion. — reasons: Tabrisi supplies an understood 'expulsion'.
22:40/q10 How is the exception 'except that they say' parsed? [إِلَّآ أَن يَقُولُوا۟ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the construction is disconnected, connected to ḥaqq, or uses an adjectival 'other than'; Abu Hayyan disputes the proposed apposition.)
  22:40/q10/p1 It is connected to the supposed 'right' or cause, grammatically genitive through bāʾ or appositive to ḥaqq. — reasons: Al-Farra and Zajjaj allow understood bāʾ; Wahidi cites 92:20, al-Mubarrad's connected pretext, and al-Nabigha's ironic sword nicks. Alusi rejects objections to the apposition.
  22:40/q10/p2 It is a disconnected accusative exception, with illā meaning 'but' or 'because'. — reasons: Sibawayh gives Qur'anic parallels 11:43, 10:98, 11:116 and poets; Abu Hayyan says a positive governor cannot reach the exception and Arabs agree on accusative here. Tabataba'i prefers the contrast. Nahhas transmits Sibawayh's non-connected accusative and Abu Hayyan's objection. Qurtubi transmits Farra and Zajjaj's genitive cause.
  22:40/q10/p3 Illā is an adjective meaning 'other than' (siwā) of ḥaqq. — reasons: Alusi offers this alternative; Samin distinguishes it from the rejected apposition.
  22:40/q10/p4 The exception is elliptic: 'except that they say the truth'. — reasons: Abu Ubayda supplies the omitted truth.
  22:40/q10/p5 It is appositive to ghayr, implying they were only maintained in their homes by profession. — reasons: Alusi reports this parse but calls it weak.
22:40/q11 Why present their confession as an exception to wrongdoing? [إِلَّآ أَن يَقُولُوا۟ رَبُّنَا ٱللَّهُ · rhetoric] (turns on: The paradox treats a virtue as a supposed fault.)
  22:40/q11/p1 The ironic exception intensifies innocence and praises them by feigning blame. — reasons: Wahidi, Baydawi, Bursevi and Ibn Ashur invoke al-Nabigha's 'only fault' of battle-nicked swords; Kashshaf, Nasafi and Razi compare 5:59. Islahi says Quraysh treated the greatest truth as the greatest sin.
  22:40/q11/p2 It states the sole actual cause of persecution: affirmation of one God. — reasons: Qatada says monotheism distressed Satan and his troops; Tabataba'i says polytheists perversely treated truth as grounds for eviction.
22:40/q12 What did Quraysh understand by the confession? [رَبُّنَا ٱللَّهُ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether they viewed it as a harmless private belief or understood its social force.)
  22:40/q12/p1 They recognized its far-reaching implications and opposed it. — reasons: Islahi says their opposition to the greatest truth was deliberate.
  22:40/q12/p2 They harassed Muslims for an innocuous belief. — reasons: Some interpreters see profession of one God as no offense.
22:40/q13 How does 'were it not for' work here? [وَلَوْلَا · grammar]
  22:40/q13/p1 Lawlā introduces a counterfactual: destruction does not occur because God's defense does occur. — reasons: Ibn Ashur explains the consequent is withheld by the existence of the condition.
22:40/q14 How is 'God's repelling' read and formed? [دَفْعُ ٱللَّهِ · readings] (turns on: Whether the verbal noun is Form I dafʿ or difāʿ with alif and how it relates to the corresponding verb.)
  22:40/q14/p1 Difāʿ with alif is read with yudāfiʿ and suggests mutual or sustained defense. — reasons: Nafi, Abu Ja'far and Ya'qub are reported among its readers. Abu Ali compares qitāl from qātala; Jishumi calls difāʿ reciprocal.
  22:40/q14/p2 Dafʿ without alif is read, including with yadfaʿ without alif. — reasons: Ibn Kathir and Abu Amr use the simple form; Asim is reported with dafʿ. Qiraat-ER says Form I may indicate one act. Jishumi calls dafʿ the verbal noun of dafaʿa; he explains al-madfaʿ as a poor man pushed away.
  22:40/q14/p3 Yudāfiʿ with dafʿ combines the derived verb with the simple verbal noun. — reasons: Abu Ali says fāʿala may have the meaning of faʿala, as in ʿāqaba and ṭābaqa.
  22:40/q14/p4 Yadfaʿ with difāʿ is also possible; difāʿ may be a Form I verbal noun. — reasons: Abu Ali compares kitāb from kataba, qiyām and ghiyāth.
