[NASHR:v1p456/r3] NASHR, d. 833 · author | rejects | Al-Makki’s prohibition on pausing at هاوم, based on an assumed original waw that would conflict with either spelling or etymology, is a clear error; every reader may pause on mīm.

[NASHR:v2p140/r3] NASHR, d. 833 · جميع القراء | reports | At the four words whose waw is omitted in the written form before a quiescent letter, all readers pause according to the rasm.
[NASHR:v2p140/r4] NASHR, d. 833 · مكي وغيره | reports | Makki and others advise against pausing on these forms or their analogues because either rasm or the underlying form would be violated.
[NASHR:v2p140/r5] NASHR, d. 833 · author | rejects | The author rejects that restriction: these are emergency stops, and where a transmitted reading is sound it is acceptable even if it departs from rasm or the underlying form.

[BIQAI:96:18/r1] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | In answer to the implied question about the result of summoning his gathering, God promises to summon the zabāniya, the guards who drag him to Hell.
[BIQAI:96:18/r2] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | Zabāniya originally refers to guards or police, singular zabniya, from zabn meaning to push; alternatively it is a nisba form, with tāʾ replacing yāʾ; they are large and powerful.
[BIQAI:96:18/r3] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | The ʿUthmānic codices all omit the wāw in the written form of the verb, although Arabic gives no phonetic reason for its omission.
[BIQAI:96:18/r4] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | reports | Biqāʿī tentatively explains the omitted wāw as preventing any belief that the angels have independent elevated power; all strength belongs to God, the Mighty.
[BIQAI:96:18/r5] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | reports | He also suggests that the spelling resembles an imperative, signaling an inevitable summons and response, perfectly matching script and meaning, with the prefixed sīn reinforcing certainty and establishment.
[BIQAI:96:18/r6] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | reports | Alternatively, the lightest summons suffices to unleash an irresistible force; the angels would be even more overwhelming if their summons were strengthened.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p487/r3] BIQAI-FULL, d. 885 · author | holds | God will summon the zabāniya, Hell's appointed helpers who drag offenders there; the term originally refers to guards and those who push, and describes powerful, imposing beings.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p487/r4] BIQAI-FULL, d. 885 · author | holds | The singular zabniya is explained through the root zabn, pushing, or as a nisba form whose tāʾ replaces yāʾ.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p487/r5] BIQAI-FULL, d. 885 · author | holds | The Uthmānic codices omit the wāw in yadʿu; the author speculates this avoids attributing independent elevated strength to the angels, or makes their summoning certain and irresistible, or shows that the slightest call suffices.
