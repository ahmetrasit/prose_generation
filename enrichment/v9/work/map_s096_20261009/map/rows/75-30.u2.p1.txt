75:30/q10 How does the preceding image of entwined legs relate to the journey? [إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ يَوْمَئِذٍ ٱلْمَسَاقُ · links] (turns on: Whether the leg image is physical incapacity, joined worldly and otherworldly hardships, or the severing of worldly ties.)
  75:30/q10/p1 The legs physically twist together, leaving the dying person unable to move toward any destination by himself. — reasons: Bayḍāwī describes the bodily immobility. Samarrāʾī contrasts the person's former ability to go home at will with now being driven against his will to his Lord and master.
  75:30/q10/p2 The hardship of leaving this world meets the terror or hardship of the hereafter. — reasons: Bayḍāwī gives this alternative meaning. Ibn ʿAbbās's report in Qurṭubī says the last worldly day meets the first hereafter day and death's agony meets encounter's terror, unless God has mercy. Qurṭubī reports a view in which the disbeliever's soul is punished when it leaves and later faces resurrection hardship. Al-Bāqir's report says the world entwines with the hereafter as the dying person leaves loved ones.
  75:30/q10/p3 The entwining depicts the severing of worldly ties and passage into the afterlife and divine presence. — reasons: Kuran Yolu takes the deceased's entwined legs as a sign of separation from worldly life and movement toward the next life.
75:30/q11 How does al-masāq here relate to al-mustaqarr in 75:12? [ٱلْمَسَاقُ · links]
  75:30/q11/p1 Al-masāq is movement of the soul at death; al-mustaqarr is the final abode after Resurrection. — reasons: Samarrāʾī says the grave is only visited during the intervening journey, while Paradise or Hell is the settled end; he cites 75:12, 102:1–2, 25:24, and 39:71–73. Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 75:12 while describing the compelled course that begins at death.
75:30/q12 How does this forced journey contrast with the person's former movement in the world? [إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ يَوْمَئِذٍ ٱلْمَسَاقُ · links]
  75:30/q12/p1 He once chose when and where to go home; now he is driven to his Lord on a fixed day without choice. — reasons: Samarrāʾī says worldly destinations were open, so their phrase was not fronted, whereas the final destination is one Lord and the appointed day is named; the contrasted phrases frame opposite scenes. He likens the person to a slave driven to his master.
75:30/q13 What does the verse's shift in address do in the passage? [إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ · rhetoric]
  75:30/q13/p1 It varies a previously generic, unspecified plural address. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts this address with the plural 'you love the immediate life' in 75:20; because that earlier audience is generic and unspecified, variation is fitting.
75:30/q14 How does a following fa connect back to this verse? [* · links]
  75:30/q14/p1 It can attach the later statement to the person's being taken to God without provisions, implying awareness of loss and regret. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this alternative connection: the person has left life and is being taken to God unprepared for the meeting.
75:30/q15 What do the reported accounts say happens to the soul around this journey? [* · reports] (turns on: The accounts emphasize different events: the assigned station, return to earth, mercy toward the soul, or the dying person's realization and plea.)
  75:30/q15/p1 It is carried to the high or low degrees appointed for it. — reasons: Qushayrī says angels carry it to the degrees of ʿIlliyyīn or Sijjīn. Ṭabrisī reports an angel taking the soul to ʿIlliyyūn for a person of Paradise or Sijjīn for a person of Hell.
  75:30/q15/p2 It rises to heaven, then is ordered back to earth for burial and later raising. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr ties ascent and return to 6:61–62. Study Quran transmits a hadith qudsī in which God orders servants returned to the earth from which they were created, into which they return, and from which they will be brought forth again.
  75:30/q15/p3 God clothes, cleanses, and prays over the soul in mercy. — reasons: Qushayrī gives this alternative merciful image alongside the angelic journey.
  75:30/q15/p4 The dying person asks for treatment, realizes separation, and then journeys to the Lord of the worlds. — reasons: Al-Qummī's report says someone asks who can treat or recite when the soul reaches the collarbone. In al-Kāfī, Abū Jaʿfar al-Bāqir answers Jābir that the dying person asks for a doctor, knows he is leaving loved ones, and sees the world and hereafter entwine. Study Quran also reports the challenge 'Who will heal him?' from Q and Ṭ.
75:30/q16 What practical or spiritual point does the verse draw from the journey? [* · interpretation] (turns on: Which consequence of the compelled return is foregrounded: accountability, lack of choice, hope in mercy, or the different experience of death according to attachment.)
  75:30/q16/p1 Its unavoidable reckoning urges obedience and preparation before death. — reasons: Jishumī says being driven to God's judgment urges obedience and warns against disobedience. Iṣlāḥī says pleasure-seekers lose their vigor at death, Judgment is certain, and they must prepare for return before dying; he rebukes the luxury-seeker who can no longer reach the Lord in that condition. Kuran Yolu says the dead cannot act freely and God judges their worldly deeds, sending believers to Paradise and disbelievers to Hell. Biqāʿī says presentation before God will expose the sinner like an absconding slave, for whom 'I will die and rest' gives no refuge. Elmalılı says the person is seized for reckoning and recompense.
  75:30/q16/p2 The inevitability and force of the journey show that no one can escape it or choose another course. — reasons: Muqātil says there is no departure from the final destination. Ṭabāṭabāʾī finds lack of choice and escape in the wording. Iṣlāḥī says all undergo death's agony helplessly. Samarrāʾī says a former free traveler is now driven like a slave to one Lord.
  75:30/q16/p3 It gives hope to those who trust the Lord's mercy. — reasons: Ālūsī calls it glad tidings for those who think well of God and know His mercy precedes His wrath, illustrating hope with a cited poem. Qushayrī pictures God clothing, cleansing, and praying over the soul in mercy.
  75:30/q16/p4 Death brings painful exile to lovers of this world but joyful release and union to those devoted to the hereafter. — reasons: Elmalılı contrasts the two spiritual orientations at the same moment of death.
