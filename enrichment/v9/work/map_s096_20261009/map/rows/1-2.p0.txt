[AKHFASH:v1p12/r1] AKHFASH, d. 215 · author | reports | Al-Akhfash cites a rajaz couplet about every living being meeting death and every pair ending in separation, then says this usage is scarcely known; the witnessed word is ithnayn.
[AKHFASH:v1p12/r2] AKHFASH, d. 215 · author | holds | The lām makes li-llāh genitive, and rabb al-ʿālamīn al-raḥmān al-raḥīm is also genitive as an attribute of Allāh.
[AKHFASH:v1p12/r3] AKHFASH, d. 215 · author | holds | The open nūn in the sound masculine plural is an added marker that disappears in the singular; its fatḥa distinguishes it from the dual's always-kasra nūn. | mentions 5:23, 36:14
[AKHFASH:v1p12/r4] AKHFASH, d. 215 · author | holds | Sound masculine plurals use wāw for nominative and yāʾ for accusative and genitive, while the dual uses alif for nominative and yāʾ for the other two cases.
[AKHFASH:v1p12/r5] AKHFASH, d. 215 · author | holds | The plural nūn falls in annexation like the dual nūn; unlike the root nūn in broken plurals such as shayāṭīn, dahāqīn, and masākīn, which remains.
[AKHFASH:v1p12/r6] AKHFASH, d. 215 · author | holds | Al-ladhīna has an added open nūn because it disappears in singular alladhī; the text also cites the nominative form alladhūn.
[AKHFASH:v1p7/r1] AKHFASH, d. 215 · author | holds | In words beginning with the added alif-lām, the alif drops in connected speech and is always fatḥa when starting; the two letters function together like qad or bal.
[AKHFASH:v1p7/r2] AKHFASH, d. 215 · author | holds | The alif-lām is recognized as added because another alif-lām cannot be prefixed to words such as al-ḥamd, al-ʿālamīn, alladhī, and Allāh.
[AKHFASH:v1p7/r3] AKHFASH, d. 215 · author | holds | A preceding interrogative alif preserves and lengthens the article alif to distinguish a question from a statement; only a few Arabs leave it unlengthened. | mentions 10:59, 27:59, 10:91
[AKHFASH:v1p7/r4] AKHFASH, d. 215 · author | holds | With an interrogative alif before aṣṭafā or aftarā, the interrogative is open and the following hamzat waṣl drops. | mentions 37:153, 34:8
[AKHFASH:v1p8/r1] AKHFASH, d. 215 · author | holds | An initial alif is hamzat qaṭʿ when another article can be added, as with al-ilāh, alwāḥ, ilhām, and ilqāʾ. | mentions 7:59
[AKHFASH:v1p8/r2] AKHFASH, d. 215 · author | holds | The alif in ilā is also cut, but it is not an added article: ilā is a preposition rather than a noun, and its alif is kasra rather than fatḥa.
[AKHFASH:v1p8/r3] AKHFASH, d. 215 · author | holds | Al-ḥamdu is nominative as a subject begun without a preceding governing verb; the beginning raises subject and predicate, an explanation al-Akhfash says is more analogical. | mentions 48:29
[AKHFASH:v1p8/r4] AKHFASH, d. 215 · بعضهم | reports | Some grammarians say that the beginning raises both the subject and the predicate.
[AKHFASH:v1p8/r5] AKHFASH, d. 215 · بعضهم | reports | Other grammarians say that the predicate raises the subject; al-Akhfash accepts both accounts but prefers the first as more analogical.
[AKHFASH:v1p8/r6] AKHFASH, d. 215 · بعض العرب | reports | Some Arabs put al-ḥamdu in the accusative as a verbal noun in place of the verb, equivalent to saying a praise to God or I praise with praise.
[AKHFASH:v1p8/r7] AKHFASH, d. 215 · بعض العرب | reports | Some Arabs give al-ḥamdu a fixed kasra by treating it like indeclinable nouns; before and after are likewise fixed unless annexed, as the cited Qurʾanic examples show. | mentions 30:4, 57:10, 9:69, 59:10, 57:22, 12:100
