## THE CURRENT MAP
20:94/q01 Who is speaking? [قَالَ · referent]
  20:94/q01/p1 Aaron speaks to Moses after Moses returns to the calf worship. — reasons: Muqātil identifies the brothers; Yaḥyā places the rebuke on Moses' return.
20:94/q02 How is يَبْنَؤُمَّ read and analyzed? [يَبْنَؤُمَّ · readings] (turns on: The mīm vowel, compounding and omission of a possessive ending.)
  20:94/q02/p1 The mīm has fatḥa, explained as a fused vocative or shortened yā ibn ummā. — reasons: Ibn Mujāhid and Fārisī list Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr and Ḥafṣ; Jishumī adds Abū Jaʿfar. Naḥḥās compares a fused noun; Fārisī, Jishumī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya compare khamsata ʿashar or lost alif; Bursevī traces ummī through ummā.
  20:94/q02/p2 The mīm has kasra, a genitive construction with omitted possessive yāʾ. — reasons: Ibn Mujāhid and Fārisī assign this to Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim, Ḥamza, Kisāʾī and Ibn ʿĀmir; Naṣafī says Syrian and Kufan readers except Ḥafṣ. Fārisī also permits a compound; Jishumī cites ibn ʿamm and yā ghulām; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the yāʾ loss irregular. QIRAAT-ER notes overt genitive inflection.
  20:94/q02/p3 A form with explicit possessive yāʾ is also attested. — reasons: Abū Isḥāq permits non-Quranic yā ibna ummī; QIRAAT-ER records a possessive variant.
20:94/q03 Why does Aaron say 'son of my mother'? [يَبْنَؤُمَّ · rhetoric]
  20:94/q03/p1 He appeals to kinship and maternal tenderness to calm Moses. — reasons: Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Ibn Kathīr, Biqāʿī, Bursevī, Ālūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī give this motive; Biqāʿī notes maternal pain. Burhān and Itqān call it affectionate address; Burhān links the renewed vocative to emotional distance.
  20:94/q03/p2 In an inward reading it recalls mercy and Moses' Sinai encounter. — reasons: Bursevī reports Sufi masters linking maternal nurture to Aaron's merciful prophethood (19:53); Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya describes Moses' longing, collapse and angels dragging his head, with Ḥāfiẓ's Sīmurgh image of a lofty station.
20:94/q04 What was the brothers' parentage? [يَبْنَؤُمَّ · referent] (turns on: Literal genealogy versus rhetorical maternal appeal.)
  20:94/q04/p1 They shared both parents. — reasons: Often called the majority view; the maternal address is then tender rather than a literal distinction.
  20:94/q04/p2 They shared only a mother. — reasons: Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Rāzī and Bayḍāwī report it; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it weak and Ālūsī rejects it as the explanation.
  20:94/q04/p3 They shared a father but not a mother. — reasons: Māwardī reports this contradictory alternative.
  20:94/q04/p4 Motherhood is literal, fatherhood a legal attribution. — reasons: Thaʿlabī transmits this further account.
20:94/q05 What is notable about the written vocative and its parallel? [يَبْنَؤُمَّ · rhetoric] (turns on: Orthography and degree of narrative detail.)
  20:94/q05/p1 The connected spelling varies in retained alifs. — reasons: Itqān describes joined ibn umm, hamza written as wāw and omitted ibn hamza; Nashr says al-Dānī saw both alifs lost while the author saw codices retaining one.
  20:94/q05/p2 The fuller Ṭāhā wording uses a vocative particle for narrative detail or emphasis. — reasons: Sāmarrāʾī contrasts the concise al-Aʿrāf report; his notes phrase the direction inconsistently but cite clarity, detail and emphasis.
20:94/q06 What do the beard and head designate, and how is 'beard' read? [بِلِحْيَتِى بِرَأْسِىٓ · meaning] (turns on: Anatomical reference and dialectal vocalization.)
  20:94/q06/p1 Beard means chin hair; head means its hair or forelock. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī define beard; Thaʿlabī calls these honored parts; Wāḥidī, Baghawī and Biqāʿī identify head hair. Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn note variant plural vowels.
  20:94/q06/p2 Head can stand for Aaron's ear. — reasons: Māwardī reports a view that Moses held ear and beard.
  20:94/q06/p3 The beard's lām has the usual kasra. — reasons: Samīn calls it the eloquent majority form.
  20:94/q06/p4 The beard's lām has fatḥa in a Hijazi reading. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn and Ālūsī attribute it to ʿĪsā ibn Sulaymān.
20:94/q07 What is the bāʾ in 'by my beard' doing? [بِلِحْيَتِى · grammar] (turns on: Whether the particle is grammatically extra.)
  20:94/q07/p1 It is extra for emphasis. — reasons: Jishumī compares bi-junūdihi and 17:1; Samīn rejects this as overreach, comparing 2:195.
  20:94/q07/p2 It contributes to the construction, or follows an omitted object 'me'. — reasons: Samīn supplies both analyses and rejects the extra-bāʾ view.
20:94/q08 Did Moses seize Aaron, and for what purpose? [لَا تَأْخُذْ بِلِحْيَتِى وَلَا بِرَأْسِىٓ · interpretation] (turns on: What occurred and what Moses intended.)
  20:94/q08/p1 He grasped head hair and beard in anger over idolatry or Aaron's apparent lapse. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās, Kalbī and reports specify right hand on hair and left on beard; Ibn Kathīr says he cast tablets and dragged Aaron. Māwardī prefers reproach for insufficient condemnation; Ibn al-Anbārī says Moses thought Aaron disobeyed; Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ālūsī and Islāḥī emphasize religious zeal. Ṭabāṭabāʾī suggests an intended strike.
  20:94/q08/p2 He drew Aaron near without contempt, as one handles oneself in agitation. — reasons: Wāḥidī and Qurṭubī compare holding one's own beard; Rāzī compares twisting fingers and stresses brotherly identification.
  20:94/q08/p3 He wished to whisper about the tablets or investigate privately. — reasons: Māwardī reports the whispering account; Rāzī says Israel's suspicion, even an accusation that Moses killed Aaron, framed the investigation.
  20:94/q08/p4 He suspected Aaron supported the calf worshipers. — reasons: Māwardī reports and rejects this as impermissible of prophets.
  20:94/q08/p5 The prohibition alone does not prove a completed seizure. — reasons: Rāzī cites prohibitions at 33:48 and 39:65; he distinguishes the separate report of head-pulling.
20:94/q09 Why did Aaron ask Moses to stop? [لَا تَأْخُذْ بِلِحْيَتِى وَلَا بِرَأْسِىٓ · interpretation] (turns on: How the gesture appeared to Aaron and Israel.)
  20:94/q09/p1 Observers might mistake it for contempt, punishment or rejection. — reasons: Naḥḥās, Qurṭubī's Ibn ʿAbbās and Rāzī cite possible misreading by a suspicious people.
  20:94/q09/p2 He sought to end an angry or threatening gesture. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Bayḍāwī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī describe angry seizure; Ṭabāṭabāʾī suggests a blow.
  20:94/q09/p3 The local custom treated beard-taking like taking a hand. — reasons: Wāḥidī says it was not then humiliating and that custom determines assessment.
20:94/q10 Does the episode entail sin by Moses or Aaron? [لَا تَأْخُذْ بِلِحْيَتِى وَلَمْ تَرْقُبْ قَوْلِى · theology] (turns on: Prophetic immunity and the reading of Moses' motive.)
  20:94/q10/p1 No: an ambiguous episode can reflect differing judgments about the better course or a conditional order. — reasons: Rāzī lists dilemmas over reproach, seizure and prohibition, cites prophetic immunity at 2:36, allows leaving the best course and conditions following on avoiding disorder. He rejects anger alone as an excuse.
  20:94/q10/p2 Moses' religiously motivated discipline is compatible with infallibility. — reasons: Ālūsī rejects Rāzī's anger objection and sees Aaron's apparent lapse as disciplinable; Māwardī rejects the thought that Moses suspected Aaron of idolatry.
  20:94/q10/p3 If minor prophetic sins are allowed, the objection does not arise. — reasons: Rāzī distinguishes this theological starting point.
20:94/q11 What division did Aaron fear? [فَرَّقْتَ بَيْنَ بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether departure, fighting or existing belief was the decisive cause.)
  20:94/q11/p1 Leaving with believers for Moses would separate them from the calf's remaining followers. — reasons: Ibn Zayd and Ibn ʿAbbās report this; Ṭabarī prefers it because Aaron urged the people and they refused (20:90–91). Naḥḥās, Wāḥidī, Qurṭubī and Ibn Kathīr give similar accounts; Islāḥī adds Sāmirī's sway and Aaron's fear.
  20:94/q11/p2 Forceful confrontation would split factions and cause killing. — reasons: Muqātil, Ibn Jurayj and many later authors give this; Wāḥidī describes believers, calf followers and Sāmirī's party; Biqāʿī cites few weak allies and retaliation; Ālūsī fears lasting separation. Qatāda recalls earlier righteous people disliking division.
  20:94/q11/p3 Different beliefs had already divided the people. — reasons: Māwardī reports this account.
  20:94/q11/p4 Moses would himself be blamed for splitting them through fighting or separate followings. — reasons: Māwardī mentions fighting; Nasafī's wording attributes separation to Moses' action.
20:94/q12 What was Moses' earlier instruction? [قَوْلِى · interpretation] (turns on: Departure versus stewardship under changed circumstances.)
  20:94/q12/p1 Remain deputy, reform, preserve unity and await Moses' return. — reasons: Muqātil and later authors invoke 7:142; Biqāʿī says it did not require the sword and compares gentle resistance; Ālūsī cites Aaron's near killing (7:150). Rāzī makes following conditional on avoiding disorder; Asad reports Zamakhsharī on unity.
  20:94/q12/p2 Follow Moses with believers. — reasons: Ṭabarī prefers this account of Moses' reproach; Aaron feared abandoning the rest.
20:94/q13 What does 'you did not heed my word' mean? [وَلَمْ تَرْقُبْ قَوْلِى · meaning] (turns on: Preserving versus awaiting, and whose words are meant.)
  20:94/q13/p1 You did not observe, preserve or enact my instruction. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās and Ṭabarī derive it from watchful attention; Muqātil and others tie it to care for the people. Jishumī notes raqīb and mirqab; Asad glosses qawlī as what Moses said; Nasafī sees scope for juristic reasoning.
  20:94/q13/p2 You did not wait for my instruction, return or decision. — reasons: Abū ʿUbayda, Ibn Jurayj and al-ʿUyūn give waiting; Taʾwīlāt says Aaron waited obediently; al-ʿUyūn also offers consideration of Moses' situation.
