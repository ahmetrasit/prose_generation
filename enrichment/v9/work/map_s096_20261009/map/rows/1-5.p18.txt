[ALUSI:1:5/r22] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | The prayer formula must express worship of God Himself; al-Ālūsī says a prayer would be invalid if its intention were merely to gain reward or the honor of worshipping Him.

[ALUSI:1:5/r27] ALUSI, d. 1270 · القدرية | reports | The Qadariyya argue that asking for aid makes sense only if the servant already has some capacity to act; without it, aid would be pointless.
[ALUSI:1:5/r28] ALUSI, d. 1270 · البيضاوي | reports | Al-Bayḍāwī distinguishes necessary aid, such as power, understanding, tools, and material, from nonessential aids that ease or encourage action; the latter are not required for liability.
[ALUSI:1:5/r29] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | rejects | On al-Bayḍāwī’s distinction, neither faction can use this verse to establish its doctrine: necessary aid permits action and liability, while helpful extras merely ease action.

[ALUSI:1:5/r30] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | prefers | If the verse bears on created acts, al-Ālūsī takes it to affirm that people have real capacity to act, effective by God’s permission but not independent of Him.
[ALUSI:1:5/r31] ALUSI, d. 1270 · الجبرية | rejects | Al-Ālūsī rejects the Jabriyya claim that people have no capacity and all their acts resemble the involuntary twitch of a trembling person; necessary capacity contradicts this.
