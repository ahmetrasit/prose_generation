## THE CURRENT MAP
11:112/q01 What does istiqāma mean as a word? [فَٱسْتَقِمْ · meaning]
  11:112/q01/p1 Continuing straight on one course without turning aside. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī contrasts a single direction with turning right or left; Jishumī derives it from q-w-m and compares aqāma; Ibn Qutayba glosses it as continuing; Biqāʿī and Qurṭubī stress constancy. Ṭabāṭabāʾ explains standing as stable capacity to bring about intended effects, with examples of justice, prayer, testimony, law, and religion; iqāma makes that functioning available and istiqāma sustains it.
11:112/q02 What does the command to stand firm require? [فَٱسْتَقِمْ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the command is taken comprehensively or identified with a particular part of religious constancy.)
  11:112/q02/p1 Steady observance of the whole commanded religion, in belief, conduct, worship, and conveying it. — reasons: Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Biqāʿī, Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Ṭabāṭabāʾ, and Kuryolu give overlapping comprehensive accounts. Al-Ṭabrisī specifies performing commands and avoiding prohibitions; Rāzī includes creed, practice, and preaching after promise and threat, with belief balanced between anthropomorphism and negation and anger and desire between extremes. Bayḍāwī includes faithful transmission and worship without excess or neglect. Biqāʿī adds endurance of mockery and demands to conceal revelation. Ibn ʿĀshūr says complete law after faith prevents factional alteration and cites the hadith on earlier peoples ruined by excessive questions and disagreement. Ṭabāṭabāʾ invokes 41:6, 41:30, and 30:30 for inner and outward monotheism; he rejects reducing the command merely to moderation, because full servitude is required. Al-Ālūsī prefers this breadth over narrower glosses and likens the difficult balance to the line between sun and shade, citing 17:74. Rāzī uses the similarly imperceptible edge of shadow and light. ʿUmar compares constancy in commands and prohibitions to avoiding a fox's twisting; Sufyān ibn ʿAbd Allāh reports the Prophet's concise instruction, believe in God, then stand firm. Ibn ʿAbbās advises God-consciousness, precedent, and avoidance of innovation. Jishumī makes obligations and avoiding prohibitions necessary for valid repentance; Qushayrī adds avoiding unlawful wealth and approval or companionship of the unjust, forbidding wrong, and valuing each moment of worship.
  11:112/q02/p2 Remain firm upon monotheism without mixing it with disbelief or doubt. — reasons: Muqātil frames the command for Muhammad and those who left shirk; Abū Ḥayyān reports his gloss. Bursevī ties steadfast monotheism and its call to the earlier prophets' endurance; Ṭabāṭabāʾ joins monotheism with servitude.
  11:112/q02/p3 Remain firm upon the Qur'an. — reasons: Sufyān ibn ʿUyayna gives the gloss in Ṭabarī and Abū Ḥayyān; the Study Quran reports it. Ālūsī reports Ibn ʿAṭiyya's related construal of kāf as upon.
  11:112/q02/p4 Persevere in jihad. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān attributes this gloss to al-Ḍaḥḥāk.
  11:112/q02/p5 Report truthfully about God with firm resolve. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī attribute this to Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq.
11:112/q03 Why command the already upright Prophet to stand firm? [فَٱسْتَقِمْ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether istifʿāl here asks for divine establishment or commands continued practice.)
  11:112/q03/p1 He must continue and preserve the uprightness he already has. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares telling a person already walking or eating to continue; Abū Ḥayyān transmits him, and al-Zamakhsharī's gloss in Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn calls for maintained straightness. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the comparison effectively means remain as you are.
  11:112/q03/p2 He is to ask God to establish him firmly in religion. — reasons: Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān take the istifʿāl prefix as seeking, like asking forgiveness; Ālūsī reports this as a more remote reading with kāf meaning upon.
11:112/q04 How does this command relate to its surrounding passage and sūra? [فَٱسْتَقِمْ · links] (turns on: Which part of the preceding discourse explains the command's force.)
  11:112/q04/p1 It draws a practical conclusion from the preceding disputes, promises, threats, and reassurance. — reasons: Rāzī and Bayḍāwī connect it to the preceding promise and threat and disputes over unity and prophecy. Ālūsī likewise sees a conclusion after those matters. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it follows consolation and reassurance in 11:109-110; Ṭabāṭabāʾ says preceding accounts of destroyed nations intensify the address, citing 17:74-75. Bursevī sees continuity with earlier prophets and believers who bore harm.
  11:112/q04/p2 It sums up Sūrat Hūd's sustained guidance through the hardships of calling people. — reasons: Al-Shihāb says the sūra guides prophetic methods and trials from beginning to end and that this concluding command concentrates its burden of accountability.
11:112/q05 Who is addressed by the singular command? [فَٱسْتَقِمْ · referent] (turns on: Whether the Prophet is an intended recipient as well as the direct addressee.)
  11:112/q05/p1 It directly addresses the Prophet and also extends to his companions and community. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya takes the companions who left disbelief and the whole community by implication; Qurṭubī includes others, and Abū Ḥayyān reports Ibn ʿAṭiyya. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the direct address honors the first recipient and makes him a model. Ṭabāṭabāʾ ties this honor to 2:285 and 66:8. The Study Quran notes the direct prophetic address and companions' inclusion.
  11:112/q05/p2 The Prophet is addressed in form, but his community is intended. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Suddī say this in al-Wāḥidī; Qurṭubī transmits al-Suddī.
11:112/q06 How is kāmā umirta construed? [كَمَآ أُمِرْتَ · grammar] (turns on: Whether kāf expresses comparison or the sense upon.)
  11:112/q06/p1 It compares the commanded uprightness with the manner prescribed to the Prophet. — reasons: Samīn reports kāmā as accusative, either qualifying an omitted verbal noun or circumstantial from its pronoun. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats the comparison as a circumstantial phrase equating uprightness with other commands. Ālūsī reports a comparison likened to the idiom one like you is not miserly, but objects that self-comparison is needless; Abū Ḥayyān supplies the like of what the command seeks.
  11:112/q06/p2 Kāf means upon, so the phrase says to remain upon what was commanded. — reasons: Ālūsī attributes this to Ibn ʿAṭiyya and others, with the Qur'an as its object; he reports a further version combining this kāf with an istifʿāl of seeking establishment.
11:112/q07 What command does umirta recall? [أُمِرْتَ · referent] (turns on: Whether an earlier Qur'anic command supplies the antecedent or another revelation must be inferred.)
  11:112/q07/p1 It recalls prior Qur'anic commands to uphold religion. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾ points to the earlier command to face religion in 30:30 and rejects positing an unmentioned revelation, since Qur'anic instructions already suffice.
  11:112/q07/p2 It implies another command, perhaps revelation outside the recited Qur'an. — reasons: Ālūsī suggests this from the wording after the discussion of monotheism and prophecy; Ṭabāṭabāʾ objects that an earlier Qur'anic command already exists.
11:112/q08 Who are those who repented with the Prophet? [وَمَن تَابَ مَعَكَ · referent] (turns on: Whether repentance is specified as leaving shirk, left general for believers, or read as surrendering selfhood.)
  11:112/q08/p1 Believers who turned from shirk or disbelief and joined him, including his companions. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās specifies the companions in al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī; Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Bursevī, Ālūsī, Elmalılı, and Ṭabāṭabāʾ identify faith as turning from shirk. Qurṭubī includes later followers; Ibn ʿĀshūr says faith itself is repentance from associating partners and treats with you as circumstantial, not attached to repent. Bursevī says they share faith with the Prophet without implying he shared their unbelief; Ālūsī says shared repentance need not have the same object. Biqāʿī says these believers should not lose heart over delayed help.
  11:112/q08/p2 All believers who turn back to God and His Messenger. — reasons: Al-Ṭabrisī reports this broader alternative and a distribution in which Muhammad continues conveying while believers continue receiving.
  11:112/q08/p3 In the inward sense, people who leave self-assertion and independent selfhood. — reasons: Qāshānī describes multiplicity within unity and abiding after annihilation; Ālūsī calls them monotheists reaching that station.
11:112/q09 How is wa-man tāba attached grammatically? [وَمَن تَابَ مَعَكَ · grammar] (turns on: Which preceding pronoun or implied construction governs man, and whether the separator permits conjunction with a hidden subject.)
  11:112/q09/p1 It is nominative, coordinated with the hidden subject of istāqim. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān say the intervening kāmā umirta licenses conjunction without an explicit pronoun; Samīn prefers this and cites 2:35, though he describes an implied clause, you and your followers be upright. Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Bursevī, Ṭabāṭabāʾ, and Ālūsī also give the coordination. Burhān says the separator must precede the conjunction; it rejects using mā ashraknā wa-lā ābāʾunā as a counterexample. Burhān notes that singular address can prevail over the absent persons joined to the Prophet.
