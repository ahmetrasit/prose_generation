[IBNASHUR:89:28/r21] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Mutmaʾinna is an active participle of itmaʾanna, meaning calm, untroubled, and undisturbed.
[IBNASHUR:89:28/r22] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | reports | One possible sense is a soul settled by belief in the Quran without doubt or hesitation; this praises its faith.
[IBNASHUR:89:28/r23] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | reports | Alternatively, it may describe the soul's quiet security in the afterlife, without fear or trial.
[IBNASHUR:89:28/r24] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أبو عمرو | prefers | The author regards Abu Amr's analysis as more correct: the first radical is hamzated and mīm is the root's second radical.
[IBNASHUR:89:28/r25] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · سيبويه | reports | Sibawayh derives the verb from ṭaʾmana, with metathesis placing mīm before hamza; this gives muṭaʾminna and verbal noun iṭmiʾnān. | mentions 2:260, 4:103
[IBNASHUR:89:28/r26] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The adjective need not identify or restrict the addressees, who would not know they were tranquil until admitted; it instead praises them, explains the good news, and assures them of safety.
[IBNASHUR:89:28/r27] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | reports | Alternatively, God may inspire the souls to recognize their tranquility, making the adjective identifying or restrictive.
[IBNASHUR:89:28/r28] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Tranquility is figurative for contentment and certainty about one's fate: believers trusted the messengers in life and see signs of favor at resurrection, unlike the doomed.
[IBNASHUR:89:28/r29] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Certainty in God's existence and oneness, trust in His promise, and sincerity in deeds are intended constituents of tranquility, individually and together.
[IBNASHUR:89:28/r30] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | reports | Another interpretation is that the souls received glad tidings of Paradise before being called to enter it.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p335/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The passage turns from threats of worldly punishment to unavoidable punishment in the next life, when compelled remembrance and regret will be useless.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p335/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The preceding description of the earth's crushing and Hell is staging that builds suspense and magnifies the Day; the chief messages are the severe torment and the address to the reassured soul.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p340/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The passage ends the warnings with glad tidings for believers guided by the Quran, following the Quran's pattern of joining promise to warning to encourage good and deter evil.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p341/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Alternatively, the address may begin a new passage, prompted by the mention of punishment for the polytheist, and God may be speaking directly to the believers' reassured souls.
