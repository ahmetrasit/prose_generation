36:14/q10 How is the verb read, and what does each reading imply? [فَعَزَّزْنَا · readings] (turns on: Transmission of doubling versus lightening, and whether lightening changes the meaning or only the verb form.)
  36:14/q10/p1 Most readers double the zāy: fa-ʿazzaznā, usually explained as reinforcing or strengthening. — reasons: Al-Tabari prefers it by the readers' consensus and because 'overcame' fits poorly. Ibn al-Jawzi lists Ibn Kathir, Nafi', Abu Amr, Ibn Amir, Hamza, al-Kisa'i and Hafs from Asim. Abu Ubayd instead explains his doubled recitation as overcoming; al-Farisi reports a similar reversed semantic distinction. Al-Zajjaj records both forms without excluding either.
  36:14/q10/p2 A light-zāy reading, fa-ʿazaznā, is transmitted, chiefly from Abū Bakr and al-Mufaḍḍal from ʿĀṣim; its meaning is disputed. — reasons: Al-Tha'labi also attributes it to Talha ibn Musarrif and Hafs from Asim, while other sources put Hafs with the doubled reading. Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi add al-Hasan, Abu Haywa and Aban; al-Durr cites Mujahid. Al-Farra, al-Baghawi, al-Jawhari and al-Alusi allow the same strengthening sense; Abu Ali, al-Mubarrad, al-Nahhas, al-Tabrisi and others understand 'overcame,' supported by 38:23, Jarir and the proverb. Ibn Atiyya phrases its result as their prevailing over the messengers, whereas most overcomer explanations make God overcome the opponents. Qiraat-er says Form I may signify simple strength, victory, or rarity.
36:14/q11 What does the omitted object of fa-ʿazzaznā refer to? [فَعَزَّزْنَا · grammar] (turns on: Whether the verb describes support of the two, support of the message, or victory over those who denied it.)
  36:14/q11/p1 It refers to the first two messengers, supplied by the preceding clause. — reasons: Al-Zamakhshari and al-Nasafi say omission draws attention to Shimon and the subtle strategy by which truth prevailed; al-Zamakhshari compares omitting those judged when saying a ruler judged justly. Al-Baydawi says the context supplies the object and focuses on the helper. Abu Hayyan says the third reinforced the two and all prevailed through argument and tactful invitation. Bursevi and al-Alusi likewise recover the pair; al-Samin extends the omission to both readings.
  36:14/q11/p2 The mission or call, rather than the messengers personally, is what was strengthened. — reasons: Al-Zajjaj says the message was reinforced; al-Samarrai explicitly distinguishes the call from the persons. Al-Wahidi prefers this explanation for the passage.
  36:14/q11/p3 On the overcoming sense, the understood object is the opponents or the disbelievers. — reasons: Al-Zamakhshari and al-Baydawi treat the third as the means of victory; Abu Ali says his proof defeated the opponents. Al-Samin's general statement instead calls the omitted object the two messengers even for the 'overcome' reading.
36:14/q12 Was the third sent after the pair or had he been sent earlier? [فَعَزَّزْنَا بِثَالِثٍۢ · reports] (turns on: Narrative order and whether 'third' can designate a man dispatched earlier but introduced after the pair.)
  36:14/q12/p1 The third was sent after the first two were rejected, to support them. — reasons: The sequence is explicit for Yahya ibn Sallam, Qatada, al-Tabari, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi and others; Wahb says the third received news of the imprisoned pair and set out. Al-Samarrai explains why the verse does not simply say three were sent at once. Ibn al-Jawzi objects that a messenger sent before both would be called first, and al-Nahhas says the apparent wording favors a later third; al-Tabataba'i rejects transmitted versions that jar with Quranic sequence.
  36:14/q12/p2 The third had been sent before the pair; the narration mentions him later and speaks retrospectively of his strengthening. — reasons: Al-Farra identifies this earlier messenger as Shimon and says the strengthening came from what he taught the pair. Al-Nahhas reports his view but says an earlier man should have been introduced as 'the third' with the article. Ibn al-Jawzi rejects it because exegetes place the third later and because one sent first would not be third.
36:14/q13 Who is the third? [بِثَالِثٍۢ · referent] (turns on: Incompatible name traditions and, for Islahi and al-Qashani, different identifications of the entire parable.)
  36:14/q13/p1 He was Shimʿūn/Simon, also called al-Ṣafā, al-Ṣakhra or al-Ṣaffār, Jesus's disciple or successor. — reasons: Muqatil's form Shamʿūn/Shaman and many other reports identify Simon; Ibn Abbas calls him al-Ṣafā. Al-Qurtubi calls him chief of the apostles and says he entered the royal court in disguise. Bursevi calls him their leader and Jesus's successor after the ascension. Al-Durr mentions a messengerless interval of 434 years. In one report God inspired him with the pair's plight; in others Jesus sent him. Al-Farra instead puts Simon before the pair. Elmalili's reported John–Paul pair makes Simon Peter third.
  36:14/q13/p2 He was Salūm/Shalūm. — reasons: The Ibn Abbas–Ka'b–Wahb or Ka'b reports pair him with Ṣādiq and Ṣadūq; spelling varies.
  36:14/q13/p3 He was Būluṣ/Paul. — reasons: Shuʿayb al-Jubbāʾī, Shu'ba and other reports pair him with Simon and John; al-Alusi notes a spelling with ṣād or sīn. Sa'id ibn Jubayr also names Paul, though he calls Simon son of John the helper and says all three were killed.
  36:14/q13/p4 He was Yūnus/Jonas. — reasons: Al-Mawardi attributes Yūnus to Shuʿayb; Study Quran reports Jonas as another identification; al-Alusi says Wahb and Ka'b also transmit Yūnus.
  36:14/q13/p5 He was Pharaoh's believing kinsman who defended Moses, though not a prophet in the technical sense. — reasons: Islahi identifies him with the believer of 40:28 and the speaker in 40:26–45. He hid his faith until Pharaoh proposed killing Moses, then defended Moses before the court; his family status gave his plea force. His arguments and courage earn him collective inclusion with Moses and Aaron, as a member of one embassy can speak with the others; Islahi compares him with Abu Bakr among Muhammad's followers.
  36:14/q13/p6 In an inward reading, the third is intellect, joining spirit and heart. — reasons: Al-Qashani says intellect shares the self's interests and succeeds in influencing it toward what heart and spirit seek, whereas the body initially rejects spirit and heart because of its darkness and lack of affinity.
36:14/q14 How does Arabic form and use ordinal expressions such as 'a third'? [بِثَالِثٍۢ · grammar]
  36:14/q14/p1 Ordinals use the fāʿil pattern, with count, gender and compound-number forms governed by Arabic usage. — reasons: Sibawayh cites ثاني اثنين (9:40) and ثالث ثلاثة (5:73) for ordinals two through ten in annexation; feminine fāʿila takes a feminine ending, while feminine counted numbers three through ten omit hāʾ. خامس أربعة means making four into five and is rare though analogical; Arabic does not say ثاني واحد. Eleven through nineteen have open compound parts, as حادي عشر and ثالث عشر; feminine حادية عشرة follows its feminine number pattern. He treats خامس خمسة عشر and حادي أحد عشر, explaining when حادي declines, and notes a less common expanded ثالث عشر ثلاثة عشر whose final count is often omitted. Masculine ordinals dominate mixed-gender counts; خامس أربع for four women plus a man is scarcely used; higher patterns include رابع ثلاثة عشر and بضعة عشر/بضعة عشرة inflect like تسعة عشر/تسع عشرة.
36:14/q15 Is 'with a third' also read with a definite article? [بِثَالِثٍۢ · readings]
  36:14/q15/p1 The transmitted wording is 'with the third' (bi-l-thālith). — reasons: Al-Farra attributes it to Abd Allah and explains definiteness by prior mention of the messengers, comparing 'the two dirhams' after an earlier reference. Ibn Atiyya identifies Ibn Masud's codex; al-Samin and al-Alusi also report Abd Allah's reading. Al-Nahhas says this would have been the natural wording for al-Farra's theory that the third had been sent first.
36:14/q16 What purpose or effect did sending a third serve? [فَعَزَّزْنَا بِثَالِثٍۢ · interpretation] (turns on: The literal mission, Islahi's Moses reading, or al-Qashani's inward reading determines the kind of support.)
  36:14/q16/p1 It reinforced the first two, increased their number, and made the proof and mission stronger. — reasons: Abu al-Aliya explicitly calls the third a stronger proof against the town; Ibn Zayd says making three is reinforcement. Ibn Atiyya says it completed the proof. Al-Biqa'i says God brought might to His cause after their weakening by denial. Al-Samarrai says rejection explains why support came later rather than all three at once. Al-Jishumi says one messenger may permissibly be strengthened by another; messengers sent together may share a law, while successively arriving messengers each require a new miracle and renewed law.
