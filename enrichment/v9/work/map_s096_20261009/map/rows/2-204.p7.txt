<<2:204 notes, part 7 of 0..11>>
Abbas glosses ألد الخصام as someone skilled in disputation.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p327/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan glosses the phrase as a liar who argues falsely.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p327/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · قتادة | reports | Qatada says he is fiercely obstinate in disobeying God.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p327/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · السدي | reports | Al-Suddi interprets him as crooked in disputation.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p327/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · مجاهد | reports | Mujahid says he does not remain on the truth when disputing.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p327/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The opening nominal clause is apparently coordinated with the relative clause of من; grammarians also allow it as a circumstantial clause coordinated with ويشهد or attached to its implied subject.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p327/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The syntax of the genitive depends on whether الخصام is plural, verbal noun, active-participle sense, or an intensive adjective; each reading yields a different account of the إضافة.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p327/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | rejects | Al-Zamakhshari treats الخصام as المخاصمة and the genitive as meaning في, but Abu Hayyan says this is a weak grammatical position because a superlative is normally annexed only to something it partly is.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p327/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The verse counsels care in religious and worldly matters and cautions judges not to rely on outward faith or righteousness without examining people's inner states.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p358/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The passage contrasts apparent verbal piety with inward sincerity: this person's polished, pleasing words display outward agreement and obedience, then he invokes God to swear his heart matches his speech, though he is fiercely contentious.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#2/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The clause may coordinate with yuʿjibuka; it is then a relative clause without syntactic position, or an adjective in the nominative if man is indefinite.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Alternatively, the clause is circumstantial, attached either to the concealed subject of yuʿjibuka or to the genitive pronoun in qawlihi; this restricts the admiration and speech unnaturally, and a positive imperfect seldom serves as a circumstantial clause without an implied huwa.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الجمهور | reports | The majority read yushhidu in the active, with ḍamma on the prefix and kasra on hāʾ, taking Allah as its object.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#2/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو حيوة وابن محيصن | reports | Abū Ḥaywa and Ibn Muḥayṣin read with fatḥa on both letters, making Allah the subject.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبي | reports | Ubayy reads yastashhidu Allāh; istifʿāl can mean ifʿāl and match the majority reading, or it can mean the bare verb شهد with Allah as object after an omitted preposition.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#2/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الجمهور | reports | The majority explanation takes the phrase as swearing by God and calling Him witness that the speaker is truthful; the author says the actual sense is that God knows the concealed contents of his heart.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#2/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The author rejects taking quadriliteral ashhada as an oath verb: oath usage belongs to triliteral shahida, so the mainstream reading means that God sees what is hidden in the man's heart.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#2/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | To make the reported oath interpretation fit, an omitted phrase must say that he swears against his heart: what he privately holds is disbelief, so he swears to its opposite, the pleasing claim he presents.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#2/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو حيوة | reports | Abū Ḥaywa's reading supports the sense that God sees the disbelief concealed in the man's heart.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The syntax of wa-huwa aladdu al-khiṣām follows the alternatives given for the preceding clause, and it may additionally be circumstantial from the pronoun in yushhidu.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#3/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A further proposal takes afʿal as noncomparative, meaning ladhīd al-khiṣām, and analyzes the phrase as an adjective annexed to its complement.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#3/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī says al-khiṣām means disputation and the annexation of al-add has the sense of fī, like thabata al-ghadr.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#3/r12] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | rejects | The shaykh rejects al-Zamakhsharī's fī-genitive analysis as contrary to the usual rule that afʿal is annexed to something of which it is a part, and notes that annexation meaning fī is weak.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#3/r13] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | One proposal takes huwa not as the pronoun of man, but as referring to the quarrel: his quarrel is the fiercest quarrel.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#3/r14] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abū al-Baqāʾ takes huwa as referring to the verbal source qawlihi, which is construed as khuṣām.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Al-add means fiercely contentious, from lidad, intense disputation; an unattributed poem uses alad for a fierce opponent.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#3/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The author gives lidad verb patterns for prevailing in disputation; an unattributed line uses talid for outdoing stubborn opponents.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#3/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | A man may be called alad, alandad, or yalandad; the feminine is ladda and the plural is lud, like ḥumur.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#3/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjāj derives alad from the two lidadī of the neck, its sides.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#3/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Another derivation is from the two sides of a valley, also called lidadī because they curve.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#3/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A third derivation connects the word with ladda, to confine: the disputant holds his opponent back from conducting the argument.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#3/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | One parse treats al-khiṣām as the plural of khaṣm, like kaʿb/kiʿāb, so no adjustment is needed for the predicate.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204#3/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | If al-khiṣām is a verbal noun, the predicate needs an interpretation: omit a first complement (his quarrel is the fiercest), a second complement (fiercest among disputants), or use the verbal noun as an agent adjective.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Man may be a relative pronoun or an indefinite noun qualified by the following clause, as in a parallel earlier in the sūra.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Iʿjāb is finding something pleasing, inclining toward it, and esteeming it; the hamza makes the verb transitive.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الراغب | reports | Al-Rāghib defines wonder as perplexity caused by ignorance of a thing's cause, relational rather than an intrinsic quality; a thing that amazes me appears in a way whose cause I do not know.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشاعر | reports | An unattributed poem uses ʿajibtu for wondering at a goat that insulted the speaker, though he had not struck it.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Fī al-ḥayāt may modify qawlihi: he impresses the listener with claims about worldly affairs because he seeks worldly advantage through false professions of love.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Alternatively, fī al-ḥayāt modifies yuʿjibuka: his speech is pleasing and eloquent in worldly life, but not in the afterlife because of speech difficulty there or because he is not allowed to speak.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:204/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | prefers | The shaykh prefers attachment to yuʿjibuka, meaning the man speaks pleasing, polished words throughout his life and never switches to harsh speech; he rejects al-Zamakhsharī's construal.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p562#2/r1] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · السدي | reports | Al-Suddi says the verse was revealed about al-Akhnas ibn Shurayq, who professed Islam to the Prophet while concealing the opposite.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p562#2/r2] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says it concerned hypocrites who disparaged Khubayb and his companions, killed at al-Raji; the verses condemn them and praise Khubayb's companions.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p562#2/r3] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · قتادة، ومجاهد، والربيع ابن أنس وغير واحد | prefers | They take the contrast as general: the hypocrites' traits apply to hypocrites broadly and the self-sacrifice to believers broadly; Ibn Kathir calls this correct.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p562#2/r4] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · نوف البكالي | reports | Nauf says scripture describes people who pursue worldly gain through religion, with honeyed tongues and bitter hearts, sheep-like exteriors and wolf-like hearts; God warns of a trial that confounds the wise.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p562#2/r5] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · القرظي | reports | Al-Qurazi identifies those described in the earlier scripture report as the hypocrites and finds their description in this verse.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p562#2/r6] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · سعيد المقبري ومحمد بن كعب القرظي | reports | Sa'id al-Maqburi recounts a saying from earlier books about religious opportunists; Muhammad ibn Ka'b says the description is in the Quran, at this verse.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p562#2/r7] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · محمد بن كعب | reports | Muhammad ibn Ka'b says a verse may be revealed about one man and thereafter have general application.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p562#2/r8] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | Ibn Kathir judges al-Qurazi's explanation sound and correct.
