[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p765/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · وهب | reports | Wahb reports that Job was asked what was hardest in his affliction and answered: the enemies’ gloating.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p765/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · السدي | reports | Al-Suddi identifies the burden imposed on earlier people as Israel’s restrictive prohibitions and heavy restraints.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p765/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · قيل | reports | Other explanations of the burden are hellfire or the whispers of the soul.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p765/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | These explanations are examples, not restrictions on the general meaning of the request.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p765/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The request for anything beyond capacity is broader than the earlier request for a burden like that imposed on predecessors; the intensified verb also underscores the request.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p765/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | Abu reads the verb in an intensive form, comparable to the difference between جرح and جرّح.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p765/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | Another interpretation distinguishes a first request to be spared difficult duties from a second request to be spared punishments inflicted on earlier communities for failing to keep them.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p765/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Ṭāqa is an anomalous verbal noun, not a productive pattern; it can mean absolute inability or a burden that is possible but severely difficult, mapping respectively to punishment or duties.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p765/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن الأنباري | reports | Ibn al-Anbari explains the plea as asking not to bear a load that is heavy to discharge even if one could endure it, like saying one cannot bear to look at someone one dislikes. | mentions 11:20
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p766/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Pardon removes punishment; forgiveness conceals the sin and protects from humiliation; mercy bestows beneficence, with each petition increasing in scope and intensity.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p766/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib similarly defines pardon as removing sin’s punishment, forgiveness as covering it and replacing it with kindness, and mercy as an outpouring of beneficence.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p766/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · قيل | reports | Specific readings assign pardon to protection from metamorphosis or actions, forgiveness to sinking or speech, mercy to the weighing of deeds, or each plea to death, the grave, and Judgment; Abu Hayyan says these restrictions lack evidence.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p766/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Mawla derives from waliya and can function as a verbal, temporal, or locative noun; in the sense of ruler, master, helper, or kinsman it is a lexicalized noun.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p766/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The ف in فانصرنا signals causation: because God is their master and manager, they ask Him to help them against enemies and instill courage while weakening the enemy.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p766/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The closing passage uses antithesis, semantic contrast, repetition, paronomasia, and ellipsis for rhetorical effect.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#2/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Lahā mā kasabat is an independent explanatory sentence: being answerable only for one's own deeds illustrates that God does not burden a soul beyond its capacity.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Some distinguish the verbs: kasaba can mean earning for oneself or another, whereas iktasaba is narrower; Arabic says “he earns for his family” but not “he acquires his family,” and al-Ḥuṭayʾa's line uses kāsib.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī says evil takes the intensive iktasaba because the desirous self exerts effort to reach it, while good is described by the unmarked kasaba because it follows the path.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#2/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya first suggests the different forms simply vary the wording elegantly, as in 86:17; the author instead sees a semantic distinction in the effort involved in good and evil. | mentions 86:17
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The author prefers a distinction: good is earned without strain along God's prescribed way, while sin requires the intensified effort of breaking His prohibition.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#2/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Others find no difference, noting that the Qurʾān uses both forms for evil: 74:38, 6:164, and 33:58. | mentions 74:38, 6:164, 33:58
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#2/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abū al-Baqāʾ reports both the view that the forms are synonymous and the view that iftaʿala signals effort, making sin weighty in its consequences.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#2/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الواحدي | reports | Al-Wāḥidī says the sound linguistic view treats kasb and iktisāb as equivalent, citing Dhū al-Rummah's line that uses both forms together.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Tuʾākhidhnā is read with hamza from taking someone to task; the wāw reading either eases hamza to wāw by regular phonology or derives from wākhadhahu, as Abū al-Baqāʾ says.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The reciprocal form is used for one action because the sinner exposes himself to punishment by his deed, as if helping the punisher act against him; it may also be a conventional reciprocal such as sāfartu.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#3/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبي | reports | Ubayy reads rabbanā wa-lā tuḥammil ʿalaynā iṣran with a doubled mīm; al-Zamakhsharī explains this as intensifying “lay a burden upon.”
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#3/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī distinguishes the two doubled forms: the first intensifies carrying upon someone, while the second causatively shifts ḥamala from one object to two.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#3/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · النابغة | reports | Iṣr fundamentally means weight or severity; al-Nābigha's verse calls it a burden borne after the people have been exhausted.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#3/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Because a covenant is heavy, iṣr also means covenant or compact, as in God's covenant taken from the prophets in 3:81. | mentions 3:81
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#3/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The lām in lahā conveys ownership and the pleasure of good; ʿalā in ʿalayhā conveys evil's burden weighing upon its bearer.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#3/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | One view says even a slight good is credited graciously by God, while punishment follows only deliberate striving; the author says this depends on distinguishing the verb forms and finds that distinction strongest.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#4/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Iṣr in 7:157 means burdensome legal duties; it broadens to anything heavy, and some reportedly interpret it here as enemies' gloating, citing an unnamed poet. | mentions 7:157
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#4/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الحطيئة | reports | Iṣr can also mean affection or kinship; al-Ḥuṭayʾa says that no tie of kinship draws him to the person.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#4/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Iṣr may be the thing by which objects are bound; iṣār is the cord securing loads, from the verb aṣara, “to bind.”
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#4/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The noun iṣr is also transmitted with ḍamma on its hamza in a rare reading.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#4/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبي | reports | Ubayy's doubled mīm in lā tuḥammil ʿalaynā intensifies the action.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#4/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ṭāqa means ability and is an irregularly shortened verbal noun of aṭāqa; the expected form is iṭāqa, like other exceptional maṣdars such as ghāra, jāba, and nabāt, and it is not productive.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#4/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The author supports the exceptional verbal-noun pattern with Qurʾān 71:17 and a poetic example of ʿaṭāʾ. | mentions 71:17
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#5/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Mawlā is a mafʿal from wali/yali; here it is a verbal noun used for an agent, possibly with an omitted “possessor of our guardianship,” and it may also denote place or time.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#5/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The fāʾ marks causation: because God is their protector and manages their affairs, they ask Him to grant victory over their enemies.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#5/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The author illustrates this causal appeal with “You are generous, so grant me” and “You are a hero, so protect your sanctuary.”
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286#5/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The sūra contains many branches of learning; most have already been indicated, and an intelligent reader can infer what is suggested without explicit detail.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Wusʿahā is the second object of yukallifu. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says another object is omitted, but the Shaykh says that is true only at the verb's underlying valency, not in the surface syntax.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | rejects | The Shaykh rejects an omitted object in the grammatical parse: wusʿahā itself is the second object, like dirhaman after an exceptive in “I gave Zayd only a dirham.”
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Wusʿ is what a person can contain or bear without constriction or exceeding capacity.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:286/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن أبي عبلة | reports | Ibn Abī ʿAblah reads the phrase as the past verb wasiʿahā; one parse supplies a deleted relative mā, making it the second object, a construction Basrans reject and Kufans allow, though the omission is weak.
