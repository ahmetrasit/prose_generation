[ELMALILI:87:1#2/r16] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Şeyh Abduh | reports | Abduh defines God's name here as whatever enables people to know Him: minds recognize Him through attributes such as knowledge, power, and wisdom as disclosed in creation.
[ELMALILI:87:1#2/r17] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Şeyh Abduh | reports | Abduh links this sense of “name” with the divine “face” in 55:27–78 and Adam being taught the names in 2:31; these are the forms by which beings or God are known. | mentions 55:27, 55:78, 2:31
[ELMALILI:87:1#2/r18] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Şeyh Abduh | reports | On Abduh's reading, sanctifying God's name excludes likeness to creation, indwelling in a creature, partners, offspring, and other unworthy defects; the next verses identify Him as Creator and Guide. | mentions 87:2, 87:3
[ELMALILI:87:1#2/r19] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Abduh's account broadens a name's conventional linguistic indication to rational and natural signs, a valid metaphor when it does not sanctify the world itself; nevertheless, leaving the apparent meaning is remote.
[ELMALILI:87:1#2/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Names unique to God's essence or attributes belong only to Him; names describing divine acts must also be understood free of low, creaturely implications and recalled reverently. | mentions 42:11
[ELMALILI:87:1#2/r20] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | In the Sufi account, essence is described by attributes, names are the attributes, and effects are consequences of names, not the names themselves; the sanctified referent is God's essence.
[ELMALILI:87:1#2/r21] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Sanctifying God's essence through His name is apt because speech expresses its purity by pure names, while human minds know the divine essence only through names, attributes, or effects; direct manifestation is an afterlife matter. | mentions 87:14, 87:15, 87:17
[ELMALILI:87:1#2/r22] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Although “name” can broadly include verbal, rational, and natural indications that turn the mind toward God, its clearest ordinary sense is the linguistic names and attributes conveyed in revelation.
[ELMALILI:87:1#2/r23] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The choice “your Lord, the Most High” signals why He must be sanctified; the following attributes explain and establish this lordship and exaltedness. | mentions 87:2, 87:3, 87:4, 87:5
[ELMALILI:87:1#2/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | After 86:15–17, recalling the Lord's supremacy assures the Prophet that God's promise will prevail over the disbelievers' plotting and delay, and calls him to gratitude. | mentions 86:15, 86:16, 86:17
[ELMALILI:87:1#2/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Some call “name” redundant, mean the named referent, or identify name and referent; Elmalılı says these views all intend sanctification of the referent through its name.
[ELMALILI:87:1#2/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Sâhib-Keşşâf | reports | Al-Zamakhshari explains glorifying God's name as freeing it from invalid meanings such as anthropomorphism and protecting its mention from casual, irreverent use.
