TABAI:7:172/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The verse's covenant concerning God's lordship is among the Qur'an's most subtle in meaning and remarkable in composition.
[TABATABAI:7:172/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | “Taking” something from another thing implies separation, but its precise sense varies by context; here it means extracting offspring from backs while the source remains whole, then making each child an independent person through successive generations.
[TABATABAI:7:172/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The phrase “from their backs” specifies this mode of extraction; individuals become distinct from their parents and from one another, each with an independent soul and personal accountability.
[TABATABAI:7:172/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Witnessing people against themselves means showing them the reality of their own selves so they can experientially know it and testify when called upon; the question specifies that they witness God's lordship over them.
[TABATABAI:7:172/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Human beings cannot own or govern their existence independently: they cannot protect themselves from death and suffering, and the cosmic causes on which they depend are themselves governed by an unseen authority.
[TABATABAI:7:173#2/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The address and reply are real: the people understood God’s intended request for acknowledgment from what they witnessed; speech is any communication conveying an intended meaning.
[TABATABAI:7:173#2/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The addressees of ‘lest you say’ are those who witnessed and acknowledged; worldly distractions veil the event, but resurrection removes the veils and restores their direct recollection.
[TABATABAI:7:173#2/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | prefers | Alternatively the warning may address the present hearers, but the author prefers the witnessed people as addressees, supported by the third-person reading ‘that they say.’
[TABATABAI:7:173/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | A fourth objection says a single collective existence in the primordial world would leave no parents whose earlier polytheism descendants could cite.
[TABATABAI:7:173/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | A fifth view accepts the primordial reports as a pre-birth event that fosters an innate disposition toward knowledge of God, but argues the verse itself cannot mean that because descendants could plead that God made them forget the witness and left them to fallible reasoning.
[TABATABAI:7:173/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | A sixth objection says the verse is not explicit and could be figurative, while the reports are either raised or stopped traditions and carry no probative force.
[TABATABAI:7:173/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · المثبتون لنشأة الذر | reports | Defenders answer that forgetting the occasion and details of the covenant does not invalidate proof; the essential pledge and knowledge of God's oneness remain even if the scene is forgotten.
[TABATABAI:7:173/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · المثبتون لنشأة الذر | reports | They answer that the alleged impossibility of mass forgetting is only an unsupported intuition; essential knowledge of lordship remains, and transmigration has separate refutations, so forgetting a prior world is not logically impossible.
