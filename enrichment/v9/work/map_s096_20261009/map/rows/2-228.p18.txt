[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#8/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · رسول الله | reports | Malik’s intention-based rule cites the Prophetic report that deeds are judged by intentions and each person receives according to what he intended.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#8/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · مالك | reports | When intercourse occurs without intending revocation, Malik says the husband may revoke during the waiting period but must not have intercourse again until she has completed an interval to establish that his unlawful semen is cleared.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#8/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن القاسم | reports | Ibn al-Qasim says that after the waiting period she cannot marry her former husband or another man during the remaining clearance period; a marriage then is annulled, but does not create permanent prohibition because the semen was his.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#8/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · سعيد بن المسيب | reports | Saʿid ibn al-Musayyab, al-Hasan al-Basri, Ibn Sirin, al-Zuhri, Ata, Tawus, and al-Thawri hold that intercourse itself is revocation; witnesses should be used, as held also by the Hanafi school, al-Awzaʿi, and Ibn Abi Layla.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#8/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو عمر | reports | Abu Umar reports a view among some Maliki authorities that intercourse revokes in every case, whether intended or not; al-Layth adopted it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#8/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Scholars agree that a seller with an option to cancel a slave-girl sale can have intercourse during the option period, thereby reclaiming her and undoing the sale; the author says a revocable wife has the same status.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#8/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | A kiss or other intimate touch with intent to revoke counts as revocation; without that intent, kissing or touching is sinful but does not constitute revocation.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#9/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The Sunnah is to witness revocation before intercourse, kissing, or intimate touching.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#9/r10] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو حنيفة | reports | Abu Hanifa and his companions agree that the revocable wife may adorn herself for her husband with perfume, jewelry, and dress.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#9/r11] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · سعيد بن المسيب | reports | Saʿid ibn al-Musayyab says she should ask permission, may wear clothes and jewelry, and if they share one house they should separate with a curtain; Qatada also recommends announcing entry with a greeting and throat-clearing.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#9/r12] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الشافعي | reports | Al-Shafiʿi treats a revocably divorced wife as forbidden to her former husband like an irrevocably divorced woman until he revokes, and holds that revocation requires spoken words.

[QURTUBI-FULL:2:228#9/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · مالك | reports | Malik and al-Shafiʿi do not allow the husband to travel with a revocable wife until he revokes; Abu Hanifa and his companions allow travel before revocation.
