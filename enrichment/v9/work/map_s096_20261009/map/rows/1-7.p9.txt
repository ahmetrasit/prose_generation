[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p541/r2] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · ورش عن نافع | reports | Warsh from Nāfiʿ pronounces plural mīm with ḍamma and links a following initial hamza with wāw; dropping hamza and moving its vowel requires the mīm to recover its original ḍamma and lengthen.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p541/r3] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | Warsh lengthens before an open hamza rather than transferring its fatḥa to mīm, which could make the plural resemble the dual; he follows the same pattern before other hamza vowels.

[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p541/r5] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · أبو عمرو | reports | Abū ʿAmr uses kasra for mīm as the usual vowel when breaking two sukūns and also gives hāʾ kasra to avoid a heavy kasra-to-ḍamma transition.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p541/r6] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | reports | Readers who keep hāʾ kasra but give mīm ḍamma restore mīm to its original vowel without restoring hāʾ, which was not forced by the context.

[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p543/r4] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | Ghayr can be genitive as an appositive to alladhīna or as its adjective; the adjective is valid because the relative phrase here is not a particular, singled-out group.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p543/r5] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | Ghayr may instead be accusative as a circumstantial expression or an exception meaning except those under wrath; as a circumstantial phrase it means not those under wrath.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p543/r6] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · ابن السراج | reports | Ibn al-Sarrāj additionally allows accusative as an implied aʿnī, I mean; al-Khalīl reportedly allowed it as a detached form of praise.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p543/r7] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | prefers | The author chooses genitive: although ghayr and mithl are usually indefinite even when annexed to a definite word, a known thing with one known opposite makes ghayr definite.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p543/r8] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | If a known thing has one known opposite, ghayr annexed to that opposite is definite, as in movement versus stillness.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p546/r1] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · صاحب الحجة | reports | The author of al-Ḥujja says genitive ghayr is either apposition or adjective; apposition implies repeating the governing factor, as the repeated prepositions in 7:75 show. | mentions 7:75
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p546/r2] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · صاحب الحجة | reports | Apposition differs from an adjective because an indefinite can replace a definite and an expressed noun can replace a pronoun, neither of which is allowed for modifiers.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p546/r3] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · صاحب الحجة | reports | Apposition also repeats the understood governing case factor, while both apposition and adjective clarify the first noun.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p546/r4] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · صاحب الحجة | holds | As apposition, ghayr has a clear meaning whether construed definite or indefinite, since both definite and indefinite apposition are accepted.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p546/r5] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · صاحب الحجة | reports | As an adjective, ghayr can modify alladhīna here only because the relative phrase does not single out a particular group, making it like an indefinite description.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p546/r6] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · صاحب الحجة | reports | A parallel is the definite al-rajul described by mithluka: the article does not identify a particular man, so the description remains nonspecific.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p546/r7] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | In the verse, ghayr al-maghḍūb describes the favored people and is definite because one favored with faith is not under wrath.

[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p553/r1] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | Before an indefinite, lā may be extra; if not extra, it either joins the noun as one genitive compound or contributes negation without affecting the noun's case.
