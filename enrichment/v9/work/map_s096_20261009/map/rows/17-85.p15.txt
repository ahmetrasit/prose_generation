/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İbn Abbas | reports | In Ibn Abbas’s sira account, Quraysh sent Nadr ibn al-Harith and Uqba ibn Abi Mu‘ayt to Medina; Jews advised asking about the Cave Companions, Dhul-Qarnayn, and spirit, which Torah left obscure. The Prophet answered two stories and gave a concise answer about spirit, so they regretted asking.
[ELMALILI:17:85/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Because humans remain ignorant of much while knowing something, the spirit is a source breathed into the self’s spiritual life as air, light, or heat is breathed into bodily life; its degree shapes disposition toward guidance or error.
[ELMALILI:17:85/r11] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Many commentators take min as explanatory or partitive and amr as affair: the spirit is a matter only God knows, an answer suited to asking why souls differ by God’s will. | mentions 46:23, 67:26
[ELMALILI:17:85/r12] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | If the question concerns spirit’s nature, the answer gives a definition: God’s command is His unconditioned creative act, saying be, an instantaneous act like a blink; some commentators choose this reading. | mentions 36:82, 54:50
[ELMALILI:17:85/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İbn Abbas | reports | Another report says Quraysh asked the Jews for a question to pose, they chose spirit, and this verse was revealed; this account treats the verse as Meccan.
[ELMALILI:17:85/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Abdullah b. Mes‘ud | reports | A report in Bukhari and Muslim places the question in Medina: Jews asked the Prophet about spirit, he fell silent as revelation came, and then recited the verse.
[ELMALILI:17:85/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | The conflicting Meccan and Medinan reports led some to say that this verse was revealed twice, once in each place.
[ELMALILI:17:85/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Spirit can mean the source of movement, broadly a force contrasted with matter; in this sense even electricity or any motive force may be called spirit.
[ELMALILI:17:85/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Spirit can also mean the source of life: broadly it includes plant life, while its familiar narrower sense is animal life culminating in humans and includes plant life.
[ELMALILI:17:85/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | In its most distinctive sense, spirit is the source of perception and consciousness, from sensation through knowledge, reason, will, and speech; its clearest manifestation is the human spirit.
[ELMALILI:17:85/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The human spirit distinguishes human knowledge from animal life and lets one know oneself and others and reach truth; Qur’an calls it God’s spirit breathed into Adam. | mentions 15:29
[ELMALILI:17:85/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | We know this spirit through hearing and through its effects in conscience, will, reasoning, and inner speech; all humans have a spark, although whether spirit and human self are identical is disputed.
