ntions 1:2
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r66] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · عائشة | reports | ʿĀʾisha reports that the Prophet began prayer with the takbīr and began recitation with al-ḥamdu lillāh. | mentions 1:2
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r67] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · عبد الله بن مغفل | reports | Ibn Mughaffal reports that he heard none of the Prophet, Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, or ʿUthmān say the basmala, and advises beginning with al-ḥamdu lillāh. | mentions 1:2
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r68] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أهل المدينة | reports | The author calls continuous Medinan mosque practice decisive: the Prophet, caliphs, Companions, and scholars did not recite the basmala aloud in public prayers, a practice he considers mass-transmitted.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r69] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · عائشة | reports | Ibn ʿĀshūr adds a reliable marfūʿ report from ʿĀʾisha on the first revelation: the angel said Read, then recited al-ʿAlaq without first saying the basmala. | mentions 96:1
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r7] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · التفتزاني | reports | Al-Taftazani says the feminine suffix shifts Fātiha from a description to a noun; Fātiha and Khātima can stand independently of any feminine noun.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r70] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | An Arabic-style argument against making the basmala a Fātiha verse is the close repetition of al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm in a short passage, which weakens eloquence.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r71] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · الرازي | reports | Al-Rāzī answers that Qur’anic repetition can emphasize meaning and that affirming God’s mercy is especially important.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r72] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | rejects | Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects the emphasis reply: the context has no reason for repetition, certainty of mercy is not denied, and close repetition is disfavored in eloquent speech.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r73] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · البيضاوي | reports | Al-Bayḍāwī says repeating the two attributes explains why God deserves praise; al-Salkūtī presents this as a reply to the Hanafi repetition objection.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r74] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | rejects | Ibn ʿĀshūr finds al-Bayḍāwī’s answer inadequate: if the repeated attributes are needed to explain praise, removing them from the basmala would avoid repetition more directly.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r75] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | prefers | For the view that the basmala opens every sura as a verse, Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers the argument that identical openings everywhere violate the rhetorical art of varying openings.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r76] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أبو هريرة | reports | A report that Fātiha has seven verses, the first being the basmala, is cited for the Shāfiʿī position; it appears in al-Ṭabarānī, Ibn Mardawayh, and al-Bayhaqī, below Ṣaḥīḥ status.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r77] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أم سلمة | reports | A report says the Prophet recited Fātiha and counted the basmala with al-ḥamdu lillāh as a verse; al-Ṭaḥāwī objects that Ibn Abī Mulayka did not hear Umm Salama, and another report from him conflicts. | mentions 1:2
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r78] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · زكرياء شيخ الإسلام | reports | Zakariyyāʾ notes the cited wording is not the transmitted wording; other versions only show that the basmala is a verse by itself, not that it belongs to Fātiha.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r79] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Agreement that everything between the muṣḥaf’s covers is Qur’an proves the basmala is Qur’anic, which is uncontested; it does not prove it must be recited at each written opening.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r8] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | By frequent use, Fātihat al-Kitāb became the conventional proper name of this sura.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r80] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | rejects | If early Muslims also wrote sura names and Meccan or Medinan labels, writing is no proof that the basmala belongs at each opening; the single-color ink evidence defeats a colored-ink theory.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r81] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أنس بن مالك | reports | Anas’s report in al-Bukhārī that the Prophet elongated the basmala does not establish verse status: the reciting and elongating pronouns refer to Anas, who gives it as a familiar example.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r82] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أنس بن مالك | reports | Ibn al-Mubārak and al-Shāfiʿī cite Muslim’s report that the Prophet recited the basmala before Sūrat al-Kawthar after God revealed it. | mentions 108:1
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r83] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | rejects | Ibn ʿĀshūr argues this report does not say the basmala is part of the sura: it can be the opening formula, understood as an omitted request for refuge in God’s name.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r84] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | rejects | If this interpretation is refused, Anas contradicts his other report that he never heard the Prophet say the basmala in prayer, so the reports would conflict fatally.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r85] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Ibn ʿĀshūr’s preferred account is that the basmala was written to separate suras, make the muṣḥaf’s transitions fitting, and avoid a separator made of non-Qur’anic wording.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r86] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · عثمان بن عفان | reports | In a report authenticated by al-Tirmidhī, Ibn ʿAbbās asks ʿUthmān why al-Anfāl and Barāʾa were joined without a basmala; ʿUthmān says their similar subject and the Prophet’s silence led him to join them.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r87] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Ibn ʿĀshūr infers that the basmala was added between suras only when the Qur’an was assembled in ʿUthmān’s single muṣḥaf, not in Zayd’s separate sheets under Abū Bakr.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r88] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Readers agree to say the basmala when beginning any sura except Barāʾa, regardless of whether their juristic school counts it as a verse.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r89] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Readers who do not count it as a verse may recite it at a sura opening as a recommended act of blessing that imitates the muṣḥaf scribes; this practice does not prove their legal position.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r9] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Fātiha is first in the Prophet’s reading order, not the first revelation; sound, widespread reports make al-ʿAlaq the first revealed passage, after which God ordered Fātiha to lead recitation. | mentions 96:1
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r90] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · ورش وابن عامر وأبو عمرو وحمزة ويعقوب وخلف | reports | Warsh in his best-known transmission, Ibn ʿĀmir, Abū ʿAmr, Ḥamza, Yaʿqūb, and Khalaf omit the basmala between suras; they treat its script as marking the beginning, not separation.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r91] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · قالون وابن كثير وعاصم والكسائي وأبو جعفر | reports | Qālūn, Ibn Kathīr, ʿĀṣim, al-Kisāʾī, and Abū Jaʿfar recite the basmala between suras except between al-Anfāl and Barāʾa, following inherited recitation practice.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r92] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · مؤرج السدوسي | reports | Al-Jāḥiẓ tells of al-Muʾarrij al-Sadūsī restoring the basmala before Barāʾa after hearing a phrase about the Commander returning rights; if true, Ibn ʿĀshūr treats it as a joke.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r93] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Recitation traditions do not settle juristic rulings: readers transmit how teachers read, while prayer practice follows legal reasoning; regional alignment between readers and jurists reflects shared local learning.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r94] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Although Ibn ʿĀshūr follows Mālik’s view that the basmala is not a verse at the start of al-Baqara, he explains it here because earlier commentators made this customary. | mentions 27:30
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r95] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The governed element after bi is omitted, here understood as I read; omission is conventional because the basmala accompanies the beginning of good deeds and the intended action supplies the cue.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r96] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The phrase is a mobile adverbial dependent on an omitted verb, not a stable predicative adverb; the specific action, rather than a generic begin, is understood.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r97] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The omitted verb should name the action being begun, so all of that action is associated with God’s blessing; a generic begin would cover only its first instant.
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r98] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Qur’anic and Arabic examples show context supplies omitted actions: Pharaoh’s magicians act by his might, while wedding greetings and travel formulas omit their intended verbs. | mentions 26:44
[IBNASHUR:1:1/r99] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | rejects | The omitted verb precedes the basmala as in normal word order; al-Zamakhsharī’s postposed estimate, which could suggest restriction, is unjustified because no exclusivity is intended.

[IBNASHUR-FULL:v1p108/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The hadith shows al-Fatiha is arranged in three sections, an instance of the rhetorical art of balanced division.
