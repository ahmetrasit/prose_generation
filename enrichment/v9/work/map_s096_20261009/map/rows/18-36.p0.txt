[MUQATIL:18:34-37/r9] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | He claimed that if returned to God in the afterlife he would find a better destination than his garden; munqalab means return.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذاباباختلافالعربفيتحريكالآخرلأنهلايستقيمأنيسكنهو#647/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · العرب | reports | Some non-Hijaz speakers vocalize the final geminate in verbs to match the vowel before it: fatḥa with fatḥa, ḍamma with ḍamma, and kasra with kasra.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذاباباختلافالعربفيتحريكالآخرلأنهلايستقيمأنيسكنهو#647/r10] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · العرب | reports | Some tribes use kasra for the geminate in all contexts, as in اضرب الرجل and اضرب ابنك, even without alif-lām; they do not apply this treatment to هلم.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذاباباختلافالعربفيتحريكالآخرلأنهلايستقيمأنيسكنهو#647/r11] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | All dialects keep the consonant before feminine plural nūn quiescent: unlike jussive forms, it stays quiescent in every mood, so ارددن is not treated as a command-induced cluster.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذاباباختلافالعربفيتحريكالآخرلأنهلايستقيمأنيسكنهو#647/r12] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Non-geminate verbs also keep the consonant before feminine nūn quiescent in indicative, subjunctive, and jussive forms, as in ضربن and يضربن.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذاباباختلافالعربفيتحريكالآخرلأنهلايستقيمأنيسكنهو#647/r13] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | Khalil reports that some of Bakr ibn Wāʾil say ردن, مدن, and ردت, treating them like رد and مد.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذاباباختلافالعربفيتحريكالآخرلأنهلايستقيمأنيسكنهو#647/r14] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The same dialect differences apply across geminate verbs, including forms like ردد and يردد, which remain unassimilated because two quiescent consonants cannot meet and voicing the first would require two tongue movements.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذاباباختلافالعربفيتحريكالآخرلأنهلايستقيمأنيسكنهو#647/r15] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · قعنب بن أم صاحب | reports | Poets may undo assimilation for metrical necessity even where Hijazi and other dialects all assimilate; Qanab ibn Am Sahib's lines use the unassimilated forms ضننوا and أظلل.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذاباباختلافالعربفيتحريكالآخرلأنهلايستقيمأنيسكنهو#647/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | They always open before هاء followed by alif because هاء is weak, as if the verb ended in alif, as in ردها understood like ردا.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذاباباختلافالعربفيتحريكالآخرلأنهلايستقيمأنيسكنهو#647/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | When the following هاء is ḍamma, they make the preceding verb vowel ḍamma, as if forms like مده and عضه were مدوا and عضوا.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذاباباختلافالعربفيتحريكالآخرلأنهلايستقيمأنيسكنهو#647/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · العرب | reports | Before alif-lām or light nūn, this dialect uses kasra, restoring the underlying jussive form whose final consonant was made mobile to avoid a consonant cluster.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذاباباختلافالعربفيتحريكالآخرلأنهلايستقيمأنيسكنهو#647/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In forms such as مذ اليوم and ذهبتم اليوم, the sukūn reflects deletion like the lost ياء in قاض, not a consonant built on original sukūn.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذاباباختلافالعربفيتحريكالآخرلأنهلايستقيمأنيسكنهو#647/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | Another dialect opens the verb whenever two quiescent sounds meet, except before alif-lām or light nūn; Khalil explains this as analogy with fixed-fatḥa words and locates it among Banū Amd and other Tamim groups.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذاباباختلافالعربفيتحريكالآخرلأنهلايستقيمأنيسكنهو#647/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · العرب | reports | Some speakers do not harmonize the final vowel with the preceding one, as seen in their differing forms امرؤ, امرئ, امرأ and ابنم.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذاباباختلافالعربفيتحريكالآخرلأنهلايستقيمأنيسكنهو#647/r8] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · العرب | reports | A further dialect keeps fatḥa even before alif-lām, treating the form like أين in every setting; Yunus heard غض الطرف إنك من نمير as evidence.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذاباباختلافالعربفيتحريكالآخرلأنهلايستقيمأنيسكنهو#647/r9] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · العرب | reports | Even speakers who use conjugated forms هلما and هلمي never break هلم; as an indeclinable imperative particle it follows the Hijazi treatment of رويد rather than finite verbs.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالنونالثقيلةوالخفيفةفيفعلالاثنينوفعلجميعالنساء)#643/r10] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Heavy nūn is kasra after that added alif, like the dual nūn; elsewhere it is fatḥa because the first of its two consonants is quiescent.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالنونالثقيلةوالخفيفةفيفعلالاثنينوفعلجميعالنساء)#643/r11] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Light nūn with a feminine plural leaves the verb as if no light nūn were intended; the extra separating alif is omitted because it was needed only to avoid adjacent nūns.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالنونالثقيلةوالخفيفةفيفعلالاثنينوفعلجميعالنساء)#643/r12] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · يونس وناس من النحويين | rejects | Yunus and some grammarians propose اضربان and اضربنان; Sībawayh rejects these forms as unheard in Arabic and without a parallel, since an unassimilated consonant does not follow alif.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالنونالثقيلةوالخفيفةفيفعلالاثنينوفعلجميعالنساء)#643/r13] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | rejects | Their pause lengthening and treatment before alif-lām create further problems: the forms should lose the light nūn and then the alif, as in the ordinary singular form.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالنونالثقيلةوالخفيفةفيفعلالاثنينوفعلجميعالنساء)#643/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The dual alif can precede a consonant because a geminated consonant's two parts occur together, rather than the second being added after the first has formed a word; heavy nūn is likewise two nūns together.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالنونالثقيلةوالخفيفةفيفعلالاثنينوفعلجميعالنساء)#643/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Light nūn is separated from heavy nūn because it behaves like tanwīn at pause and is deleted before a following alif or article, while heavy nūn remains distinct.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالنونالثقيلةوالخفيفةفيفعلالاثنينوفعلجميعالنساء)#643/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Heavy nūn is comparable to the paired consonants in قب and قط; similar quiescent consonants occur after alif or a soft letter, though wāw and ياء have different preceding vowels.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالنونالثقيلةوالخفيفةفيفعلالاثنينوفعلجميعالنساء)#643/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | Khalil says light nūn with the dual has the same form in connected speech and pause as the dual without it: اضربا; deleting the alif would confuse singular and dual.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالنونالثقيلةوالخفيفةفيفعلالاثنينوفعلجميعالنساء)#643/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Do not restore a light nūn after its deletion before following material such as نعمان: it would assimilate to another nūn, and forms whose deletion is fixed stay deleted in speech and pause.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالنونالثقيلةوالخفيفةفيفعلالاثنينوفعلجميعالنساء)#643/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The same non-restoration applies to forms like جئني: even where a following consonant is assimilated, the omitted nūn is not restored in connected or pausal speech.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالنونالثقيلةوالخفيفةفيفعلالاثنينوفعلجميعالنساء)#643/r8] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In a raised dual with light nūn intended, the stable رفع nūn remains as in هل تضربان; light nūn cannot co-occur with it, so its deletion is secure.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالنونالثقيلةوالخفيفةفيفعلالاثنينوفعلجميعالنساء)#643/r9] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | With heavy nūn on the feminine plural, an extra alif separates the nūns, while the feminine plural nūn stays to prevent confusion with the singular.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالوقفعندالنونالخفيفة)#642/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | At pause, light nūn after a fatḥa becomes alif, like tanwīn, because both are extra, quiescent sounds from the same articulation; light nūn marks emphasis while tanwīn marks declension.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالوقفعندالنونالخفيفة)#642/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | When the light nūn had suppressed a pronoun ending, pausing restores that ending, as in feminine and plural imperatives such as اضربي, اضربوا, وارموا.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالوقفعندالنونالخفيفة)#642/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | holds | After a preceding kasra or ḍamma, Khalil does not substitute ياء or واو at pause, treating the light nūn like tanwīn after genitive or nominative endings.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالوقفعندالنونالخفيفة)#642/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · يونس | reports | Yunus instead inserts ياء or واو after a preceding kasra or ḍamma at pause, as in اخشيي and اخشوو.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالوقفعندالنونالخفيفة)#642/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · العرب | prefers | Sībawayh says Arab usage follows Khalil's pausal forms, not Yunus's; Yunus's forms match only speakers who also say عمرو and عمري in case endings.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالوقفعندالنونالخفيفة)#642/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In a raised plural verb at pause, the ordinary رفع nūn returns with the light nūn intended: هل تضربين, هل تضربون, and هل تضربان, but not هل تضربونا.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالوقفعندالنونالخفيفة)#642/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | On Yunus's account, the ending should instead behave like a jussive: plural رفع nūn is already absent in connected speech, and the raised dual parallels the raised plural.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالوقفعندالنونالخفيفة)#642/r8] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Heavy nūn does not change at pause because it is unlike tanwīn.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالوقفعندالنونالخفيفة)#642/r9] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Light nūn is deleted before alif-lām or hamzat al-waṣl to avoid two quiescent sounds; unlike tanwīn, it is verbal, and nouns are stronger and more declinable.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابتحقيركلحرفكانفيهبدل)#618/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Diminution removes a substitute letter and restores the original radical, as broken plurals do.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابتحقيركلحرفكانفيهبدل)#618/r10] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | عطاء, قضاء, and رشاء diminish as عطي, قضي, and رشي; final hamza is a nonfixed substitute.
