[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r35] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Ibn Kathir distinguishes the Muwatta's Abu Saʿid, a Successor and client of Khuzāʿa, from the Companion Abu Saʿid ibn al-Muʿalla; the former report appears disconnected unless he heard it from Ubayy ibn Kaʿb.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r36] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report through Abu Hurayra from Ubayy repeats that the Fatiha has no equal in the Torah, Gospel, Psalms, or Qur'an; al-Tirmidhi calls it hasan sahih.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r37] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Another report from Ubayy calls Umm al-Qur'an the seven oft-repeated, divided between God and His servant; al-Tirmidhi grades it hasan gharib.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r38] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · عبد الله بن جابر | reports | The report that the Prophet called the Fatiha the best surah has a good chain according to Ibn Kathir; the Companion's identity is uncertain between al-ʿAbdi and al-Ansari al-Bayyadi.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r39] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · إسحاق بن راهويه وأبو بكر بن العربي وابن الحصار | reports | These scholars infer that some verses and surahs are superior to others from the superiority reports.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r4] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · الحسن | reports | Hasan also objects to calling the surah Umm al-Qur'an.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r40] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · الأشعري وأبو بكر الباقلاني وابن حبان ويحيى بن يحيى | reports | Another group denies ranking Qur'anic passages, since all are God's speech and ranking could imply deficiency in what is less preferred; a report from Malik agrees.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r41] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · أبو سعيد الخدري | reports | In a report judged by the Prophet, a companion recited the Fatiha as ruqya over a stung man who recovered; the Prophet approved the reward of thirty sheep and asked how he knew it was ruqya.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r42] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbas reports that Gabriel announced two lights given to Muhammad alone: the Fatiha and the closing verses of al-Baqara; each letter brings a response. | mentions 2:285, 2:286
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r43] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet says prayer without Umm al-Qur'an is deficient, and the divine hadith answers the servant's praise verse by verse as the Fatiha is divided between God and servant. | mentions 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r46] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · أبو حنيفة وأصحابه | reports | Abu Hanifa and his followers hold that Fatiha is not specifically required in prayer; any Qur'an passage suffices, citing 73:20 and “recite what is easy.” | mentions 73:20
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r47] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · مالك والشافعي وأحمد والجمهور | reports | The other major view requires the Fatiha in prayer, using the deficient-prayer hadith and reports that prayer does not count without it.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r48] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · الشافعي وجماعة من أهل العلم | reports | Al-Shafiʿi and a group require the Fatiha in every prayer unit; others require it in most units, while Hasan and most Basrans require it in one unit.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r49] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · الحسن وأكثر البصريين | reports | Hasan and most Basrans require the Fatiha in only one unit, relying on the unrestricted wording, “There is no prayer without Fatiha.”
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r5] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | In a hadith graded sound by al-Tirmidhi, the Prophet calls it Umm al-Qur'an, Umm al-Kitab, the seven oft-repeated, and the great Qur'an.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r50] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · أبو حنيفة والثوري والأوزاعي | reports | Abu Hanifa, al-Thawri, and al-Awzaʿi again hold that another Qur'anic passage can replace the Fatiha, citing 73:20. | mentions 73:20
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r51] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | reports | A report that each prayer unit needs al-Hamd and another surah is questionable in authenticity.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r52] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | reports | Scholars have three views on whether a follower must recite: that the follower must recite the Fatiha just like the imam.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r53] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · مالك | reports | A second view says a follower recites nothing, whether prayer is audible or silent, based on “one with an imam has the imam's recitation”; the marfuʿ routes are weak, and Malik transmits it as Jabir's own statement.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r54] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · الشافعي في قوله القديم وأحمد في رواية | reports | A third view requires the follower's recitation in silent prayer but not audible prayer, combining the general requirement with the command to listen when the imam recites.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r55] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Al-Bazzar reports that reciting the Fatiha and Surat al-Ikhlas before sleep protects from everything except death. | mentions 112:1
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r6] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | Al-Hamd is another name for the surah.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r7] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | It is also called al-Salah because the divine hadith names recitation of al-Hamd as the servant's praise, and the surah is a condition of prayer.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r8] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report from Abu Saʿid calls the Fatiha a healing from every poison.
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r9] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The surah is called a ruqya because the Prophet affirmed its use in healing a stung man and asked how the companion knew it was a ruqya.
[BURHAN:v1p100/r2] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | holds | The basmala is counted differently because it came as part of a verse in some readings; readers of those modes count it, while others omit it and count a replacement after اهدنا to keep al-Fatiha seven verses. | mentions 27:30, 1:6
[BURHAN:v1p194#2/r4] BURHAN, d. 794 · ابن عباس والضحاك ومقاتل وعطاء | reports | Ibn Abbas, al-Dahhak, Muqatil, and Ata classify al-Fatiha as Meccan, while Mujahid classifies it as Medinan.
[BURHAN:v1p198/r7] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | reports | Other reports say al-Fatiha descended with eighty thousand angels, the Throne Verse and Yunus with thirty thousand each, and “Ask those We sent before you” with twenty thousand. | mentions 1:1, 2:255, 10:1, 43:45
[BURHAN:v1p207/r1] BURHAN, d. 794 · أبو ميسرة | reports | An account through Abū Isḥāq from Abū Maysara says al-Fātiḥa was the first revelation; it describes the Prophet hearing a voice, fleeing, and then the angel's descent and the opening of the sūra. | mentions 96:1
[BURHAN:v1p207/r2] BURHAN, d. 794 · القاضي أبو بكر | rejects | Al-Qāḍī Abū Bakr calls the report that al-Fātiḥa came first disconnected.
[BURHAN:v1p207/r4] BURHAN, d. 794 · القاضي أبو بكر | holds | The views can be reconciled: اقرأ was the first verses, المدثر the first preaching commands, and al-Fātiḥa the first complete sūra revealed. | mentions 74:1, 1:1
[BURHAN:v1p251/r5] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | holds | The basmala is counted in readings where it descended with the sūra and omitted in readings where it did not.
[BURHAN:v1p267/r3] BURHAN, d. 794 · ابن العربي | reports | Ibn al-Arabi cites reports that al-Fatiha has seven verses and al-Mulk thirty, and that the Prophet recited Aal Imran's final ten; verse counts should follow early Muslims' practice.
[BURHAN:v1p29/r1] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | holds | An ayah may descend twice to magnify its importance and recall it when a new cause arises; the Fatiha is said to have descended once in Mecca and once in Medina, and this resolves the report about the Makkan Hūd verse.
[BURHAN:v1p348/r6] BURHAN, d. 794 · أم سلمة | reports | A report from Umm Salama says the Prophet divided his recitation verse by verse; some later scholars therefore objected to stopping mid-verse for meaning.
[BURHAN:v1p390/r1] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | holds | The omitted alif in bism signals the name’s elevated, unique rank and its connection to God, whose name gathers all names; the spelling also evokes manifestation and hiddenness.
[BURHAN:v1p390/r2] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | holds | The omitted alif in al-Rahman reflects that people know mercy’s effects but entrust knowledge of its full reality to God.
[BURHAN:v1p390/r3] BURHAN, d. 794 · علماء الظاهر | reports | The outward scholars explain the omission as abbreviation from frequent use: the alif of ism is dropped only when appended to Allah and preceded by ba according to the majority.
[BURHAN:v1p390/r4] BURHAN, d. 794 · الكسائي | reports | Al-Kisaʾi does not require the preceding ba and permits deletion in forms such as bism al-malik; the majority requires ba.
[BURHAN:v1p390/r5] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | holds | Alif is also omitted from some active-participle forms such as qadara and ʿalima because it lies inside the word.
[BURHAN:v1p390/r6] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | holds | In sound and broken plurals, a surplus alif is deleted when the detailed meaning is latent and retained when it is manifest.
[BURHAN:v1p438/r3] BURHAN, d. 794 · ابن حبان | reports | Ibn Hibban reads the report comparing al-Fatiha with the Torah and Gospel as a difference in reward granted by God’s favor, not as intrinsic superiority within the Quran.
[BURHAN:v1p438/r4] BURHAN, d. 794 · ابن حبان | reports | Ibn Hibban says calling al-Fatiha the greatest surah means greatest in reward, not that one part of the Quran is intrinsically superior.
[BURHAN:v1p439/r1] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | holds | Abu Said reports the Prophet calling al-Fatiha the greatest surah in a Bukhari narration; al-Zarkashi says this does not conflict with Ayat al-Kursi’s rank among verses, since the comparisons concern surahs and verses respectively.
[BURHAN:v1p73/r3] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | prefers | Al-Shafiʿi counts the basmala and makes the rest of 1:7 one verse; Abu Hanifa omits the basmala and divides its two phrases. Both count seven verses, but al-Shafiʿi's division better preserves the endings because anʿamta ʿalayhim does not match prior endings.
