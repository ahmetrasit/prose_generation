[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالهمز)#649/f1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Sībawayh distinguishes three treatments of hamza: full realization, softening, and replacement.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالهمز)#649/f10] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Frequent forms from رأيت are commonly softened because of their frequent use; Arabs treat the hamza as alternating in these forms.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالهمز)#649/f11] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · أبو الخطاب | reports | Abū al-Khaṭṭāb heard trusted Arabs retain the original hamza in قد أرآهم despite the widespread softened forms.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالهمز)#649/f12] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A moving hamza after alif is not deleted, since changing the alif would turn it into another letter; it may instead be softened between-between.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالهمز)#649/f13] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A moving hamza after a long added wāw or yāʾ is replaced by the matching letter rather than softened or deleted, preserving the preceding glide.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالهمز)#649/f14] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Root consonants, derivational additions, and meaningful suffixes behave differently from long added glides; root wāw and yāʾ remain, as in يدعو and يرمي.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالهمز)#649/f15] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Hamza is difficult to articulate because it is produced deep in the throat with force, making it the farthest articulation point among the letters.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالهمز)#649/f16] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | When two hamzas from separate words meet, speakers ease one because fully articulating both together is avoided; each word can otherwise occur independently.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالهمز)#649/f2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | An open hamza after fatḥa may be softened to a sound between hamza and a long alif, retaining its measure while weakening the sound.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالهمز)#649/f21] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · أهل الحجاز | reports | The Hijazis soften both hamzas in a two-word sequence, as in their pronunciation of اقرأ آية.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالهمز)#649/f22] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In اقرأ آية, the result depends on which hamza is softened: a vowelless hamza is replaced, while a moving hamza after a consonant is deleted and transfers its vowel.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالهمز)#649/f23] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | reports | Sībawayh reports that Abū ʿAmr prefers a Hijazi pronunciation inserting an alif between the interrogative alif and a following softened hamza; insertion prevents two hamzas from meeting.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالهمز)#649/f24] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · بنو تميم | reports | Some Tamimis insert an alif between interrogative alif and hamza to avoid adjacent hamzas; other Tamimis fully realize both without insertion.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالهمز)#649/f25] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | When two hamzas occur in one word, the second must be replaced; the word-internal sequence cannot be handled like two separate words.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالهمز)#649/f26] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In derived forms, hamza replacement follows the preceding vowel: the form from جئت uses yāʾ, while آدم uses alif after fatḥa.

[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالهمز)#649/f28] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The form خطايا has two hamza adjustments: the final yāʾ becomes alif after kasra, and the preceding hamza becomes yāʾ opened before that alif.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالهمز)#649/f29] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Replacement of a realized hamza with alif, yāʾ, or wāw is generally lexical rather than a fully productive analogy; poets may extend it under metrical necessity.

[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالهمز)#649/f35] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Across word boundaries, open hamza may be replaced by the matching glide, as in أو أنت becoming أونت; within one word, forms such as سوأة may instead delete it.

[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالهمز)#649/f6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A vowelless hamza is replaced with the long-vowel letter matching the preceding vowel: alif after fatḥa, wāw after ḍamma, and yāʾ after kasra.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالهمز)#649/f7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A vowelless hamza cannot undergo between-between softening or deletion, so the matching long-vowel letter must replace it.
