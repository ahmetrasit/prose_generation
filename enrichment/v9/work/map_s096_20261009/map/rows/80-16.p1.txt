[ALUSI-FULL:v30p43/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Barara is plural only of barr, while abrār can be plural of barr or bār; some say the Qur’an and Prophetic usage reserve the first for angels and the second for humans.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p43/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · قيل | reports | One explanation reserves barara for angels and abrār for humans because abrār is a paucity plural, which supposedly suits the greater number of pious angels.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p43/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الراغب | reports | Al-Rāghib says barara is specific to angels because it is more emphatic than abrār, comparing barr to the more emphatic verbal-source adjective ʿadl.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p43/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | rejects | Alusi notes abrār can also be plural of barr and disputes that angels invariably deserve the more emphatic form; another view makes abrār more emphatic and sees human excellence as hard-won through struggle against innate impulses.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p43/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · عائشة | reports | Aisha reports the Prophet saying that a skilled Qur’an reader is with the noble, obedient angels, while one who struggles with recitation receives two rewards.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p103/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · مالك عن هشام بن عروة عن أبيه | reports | In a mursal report, Ibn Umm Maktum came asking to be brought near while the Prophet addressed a Meccan notable; the opening was revealed when the Prophet turned toward the notable. Al-Tirmidhi transmits a similar report from Urwa via Aisha and grades it hasan gharib.
