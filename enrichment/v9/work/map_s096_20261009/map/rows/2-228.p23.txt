lds | The degree first denotes an advantage or distinction in men's rights.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p461/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · مجاهد، وقتادة | reports | Mujahid and Qatada explain the degree as men's advantage in inheritance and jihad.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p461/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · زيد بن أسلم، وابنه | reports | Zayd ibn Aslam and his son identify the degree as the wife's duty to obey her husband, while he is not bound to obey her.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p461/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الشعبي | reports | Al-Shaʿbi locates the degree in the dowry, the husband's right to liʿān if he accuses her, and her punishment if she accuses him.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p461/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن إسحاق | reports | Ibn Ishaq says the degree is the husband's responsibility to maintain and otherwise care for her, although both share sexual enjoyment.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p461/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · قتادة، وابن زيد | reports | Qatada and Ibn Zayd explain the degree as the husband's control of the marital bond and his possession of divorce.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p462/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | The exegetes' summary indicates that men's degree over women entails preference.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p462/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | “A degree” is an indefinite subject, “for men” its predicate, and “over them” attaches to the predicate's implied state.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p462/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | Alternatively, “over them” may be a circumstantial phrase describing the indefinite “degree,” attached to an omitted governor.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p462/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | It is invalid to make “over them” the predicate and “for men” a circumstantial phrase, because its semantic governor cannot precede both sentence parts.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p462/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · أبو الحسن | reports | Abu al-Hasan allows a circumstantial phrase between subject and predicate; other grammarians prohibit it.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p462/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Might and wisdom close the verse because it contains command, prohibition, permission, and obligation; dominance and wise ordering suit these legal duties.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#2/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | For numerals three through ten, the counted noun normally takes a paucity plural; since aqraʾ exists, the use of the abundance plural qurūʾ needs explanation.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#2/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الأخفش | reports | A woman’s qarʾ can denote her time of menstruation or purity; al-Akhfash distinguishes aqraʾat, becoming menstruating, from qaraʾat, menstruating.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#2/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Other definitions are menstruation together with purity, or the interval between two menstruations.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#2/r12] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Qarʾ may originally mean gathering, as in gathering water in a basin and reciting the Qurʾān.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | One explanation counts all the divorced women together: each waits three periods, making the aggregate number of periods large.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Another explanation allows one plural pattern to stand broadly in place of the other.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#2/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A third explanation derives qurūʾ from qarʾ with fatḥa; aqraʾ would be irregular because afʿāl is not a regular plural for this faʿl pattern.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · المبرد | reports | Al-Mubarrad estimates “three of periods,” with min omitted; Abu al-Baqāʾ's expanded estimate is the same analysis.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#2/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | One proposed root sense is a recurring appointed time: the star's qarʾ is its rising or setting, and aqraʾa al-najm means it rose or set.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#2/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشاعر | reports | An unnamed poet uses qāriʾ for the appointed time when winds blow, supporting the time sense.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#2/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Other proposed origins are transition from purity to menstruation or the reverse, and gathering; the gathering-water etymology is rejected because that verb has a weak y-root while qarʾ is hamzated.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#2/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Linguists disagree whether menstruation and purity are homonyms or share an underlying common meaning such as gathering, time, or transition.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · عمرو بن كلثوم | reports | Ibn Kulthūm's verse uses taqraʾ for gathering a fetus; on the gathering derivation, menstruation gathers blood in the womb and purity gathers blood in the body.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · جمهور أهل اللسان | reports | Most linguists, including Abū ʿAmr, Yūnus, and Abū ʿUbayda, take menstruation and purity as homonyms/opposites rather than senses sharing one meaning.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#3/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الأعشى | reports | Al-Aʿshā uses qurūʾ for periods of purity, as the context refers to the loss of the women's pure intervals.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#3/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشاعر | reports | An unnamed poet uses qurūʾ for menstruation, likening a man's flowing blood to a menstruating woman's blood.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#3/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الأصمعي | reports | Al-Aṣmaʿī transmits qarʾ with ḍamma, while Abū Zayd transmits it with fatḥa; both forms mean the same.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#3/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan reads thalāthata qarū, with fatḥa on qāf, silent rāʾ, light wāw, and no hamza; he treats the number as annexed to a generic noun and uses a dialect form of qarʾ.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#3/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزهري | reports | Al-Zuhrī, also reported from Nāfiʿ, reads qurūww with doubled wāw, easing the hamza to wāw and assimilating the preceding wāw.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#4/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The lām in lahunna is attached to yaḥillu and is the lām of communication, as in “I said to you.”
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#4/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The stronger parsing makes mā a relative pronoun meaning “that which”; its resumptive pronoun is omitted, estimated mā khalaqahu.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#4/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Alternatively, mā is an indefinite noun described by the following clause; like the relative analysis, it has an omitted resumptive pronoun.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#4/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Mā may refer to the fetus, treated grammatically as nonrational, or to menstrual blood.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#4/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Fī arḥāmihinna can attach directly to khalaqa, or modify the omitted resumptive pronoun circumstantially: what God created, existing in their wombs.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#4/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abū al-Baqāʾ calls the circumstantial reading prospective, since at the time of creation the fetus is not yet a thing until its creation is complete.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#4/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · مبشر بن عبيد | reports | Mubashshir ibn ʿUbayd reads both hāʾ pronouns with ḍamma, the original form and a Hijazi usage; kasra harmonizes with a preceding yāʾ or kasra.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#5/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The conditional's answer may be omitted and recovered from the preceding prohibition: if they believe, they must not conceal.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#5/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الكوفيون | reports | The Kufans and Abū Zayd allow the answer to precede the conditional, with the earlier prohibition serving as its answer.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#5/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | Taking in as meaning idh, “when,” is a weak analysis.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#5/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · مسلمة بن محارب | reports | The majority pronounce the tāʾ of baʿūlatuhunna with fatḥa; Muslimah ibn Muḥārib silences it to avoid consecutive vowel movements, as in a reading of 43:30. | mentions 43:30
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#5/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو زيد | reports | Abū Zayd reports silencing the nominative ending in rusulunā; Abū ʿAmr reports that Tamīm similarly silences nominatives such as yaʿlamuhum. | mentions 43:30
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#5/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Aḥaqqu is the predicate of baʿūlatuhunna and means “entitled,” not comparative: outsiders have no claim, and a wife's refusal does not invalidate a husband's revocation.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#5/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Baʿūla is the plural of baʿl, husband; its tāʾ marks a feminine collective plural like fuhūla and dhukūra, a pattern that is not productive.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#5/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Baʿūla is also a verbal noun of baʿala; ḥasanat al-tabāʿul describes a good wife, while bāʿalahā is a euphemism for intercourse.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#6/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Bi-raddihinna is attached to aḥaqqu.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#6/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | If fī dhālika attaches to aḥaqqu, dhālika means the waiting period: the husband has the right to revoke during it, restoring her to the marriage.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#6/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Alternatively, fī dhālika attaches to the act of revocation, and dhālika refers to marriage.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#6/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The pronoun in baʿūlatuhunna refers to some divorced women, specifically those whose divorces are revocable.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#6/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | prefers | The Shaykh prefers an understood annexed noun, “husbands of their revocably divorced women,” which makes the pronoun refer to all divorced women.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228#6/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The clause has a fronted predicate; its balanced ellipsis removes “against their husbands” where “against them” appears, and “for their husbands” where “for them” appears.
