[MUQATIL:89:15-22/r1] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | reports | The passage was revealed about Umayya b. Khalaf and ʿAbd Allāh b. Nufayl: after Nufayl advised him to do good and avoid evil, Umayya cited 47:11, asked why God enriched him but impoverished Nufayl, then claimed wealth proved his honor; Ibn Khaṭal said God might indeed have done so. | mentions 47:11
[MUQATIL:89:15-22/r2] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | Wealth does not show God's honor or poverty His contempt: God meant to give the rich man ease in this life and make the poor man's reckoning lighter in the Hereafter.
[MUQATIL:89:15-22/r3] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | The repeated promise that with hardship comes ease means every difficulty is followed by relief, and every ease by later difficulty. | mentions 94:5, 94:6

[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابآخرمنأبوابأو)#517/r12] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | أعاقل عمرو أو عالم and أتضرب عمرا أو تشتمه make two predicates or actions alternatives for one person, paralleling one action for one of two people.

[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابآخرمنأبوابأو)#517/r14] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In أتجلس أم تذهب and أتضرب زيدا أو تقتل خالدا, أم and أو can be equivalent because their functions cannot be separated by the pronoun marker.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابآخرمنأبوابأو)#517/r15] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | When asking which paired action applies, use أم, as in أتضرب زيدا أم تقتل خالدا.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابآخرمنأبوابأو)#517/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Fronting alternatives such as أزيدا لقيت أو عمرا remains grammatical and can be natural when asking which person was involved.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابآخرمنأبوابأو)#517/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | أزيد أفضل أم عمرو requires أم because it asks which is better; it means أيهما, and أزيد أفضل cannot stand alone in this sense.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابآخرمنأبوابأو)#517/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | ما أدري أزيد أفضل أم عمرو and ليت شعري أزيد أفضل أم عمرو likewise mean which of the two is better.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابآخرمنأبوابأو)#517/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In ما أبالي أضربت زيدا أم عمرا, only أم is suitable because it asks which alternative; fronting the names is preferable.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابآخرمنأبوابأو)#517/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | أتجلس أو تذهب أو تحدثنا asks whether any of those actions will happen; to ask which one, use أم.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابآخرمنأبوابأو)#517/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | With different actions and objects, أو asks whether any alternative occurs, while أم asks which; the same distinction appears in examples with ضرب.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابآخرمنأبوابأو)#517/r8] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | reports | Ḥassān ibn Thābit uses أم in a question meaning the speaker does not mind which action occurred.

[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابتسميتكms477الحروفبالظروفوغيرهامنالأسماء)#546/r13] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Alif-lām makes a form definite, just as a person is not definite without the definite article.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابتسميتكms477الحروفبالظروفوغيرهامنالأسماء)#546/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | أين، كيف and متى are treated as masculine adverbs like ما and من; diminutive evidence shows most adverbs are masculine.

[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابتسميتكms477الحروفبالظروفوغيرهامنالأسماء)#546/r9] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | If ثم، أين or حيث become a person's, letter's or word's name, they change like زيد and عمرو; quotations may keep their original form.
