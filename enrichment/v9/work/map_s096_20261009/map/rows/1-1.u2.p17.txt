1:1/q10 From what is ٱللَّهِ derived? [ٱللَّهِ · meaning] (turns on: Whether Allāh is an underived proper name or a contracted or otherwise derived form.)
  1:1/q10/p1 Allāh derives from al-ilāh or ilāh through loss of hamza and assimilation. — reasons: TAB (author): Allah’s form comes from al-ilāh: the initial hamza is dropped, then the two lām sounds meet and assimilate into one doubled lām. TAB-FULL (author): Ṭabarī proposes that Allāh derives from al-ilāh: the initial hamza is dropped and the adjacent lām sounds assimilate into one doubled lām. THALABI (أهل الكوفة): The Kufans derive Allah from ilāh: the definite article magnified the name, then the hamza dropped through frequent use and the two lām letters assimilated. MAWARDI-FULL (author): A second view derives Allah from ilāh: the hamza is dropped and the pronunciation intensified. JISHUMI (سيبويه): One derivation takes Allah from ilāh, dropping the hamza and making the alif-lām an obligatory substitute so the name becomes a proper name. KASHSHAF (author): Allah derives from al-ilah, with hamza removed; al-nas likewise derives from al-unas, with the hamza replaced by the article. IBNATIYYA (author): Three accounts explain the change from ilah to Allah: irregular loss of hamza before alif-lam, alif-lam added to ilah with hamza-vowel transfer and lam assimilation, or an original lah; Ibn Atiyya favors the first. IBNATIYYA (الخليل): Al-Khalil says the original was ilah or walah, with hamza substituted for waw as in ishaḥ/wishaḥ and isadah/wisadah. IBNATIYYA-FULL (author): Several analyses explain the development from ilah to Allah through deletion or movement of the hamza and attachment of the article; the author judges the first explanation strongest. IBNATIYYA-FULL (الخليل): Al-Khalil reports that ilah/walah began with wāw, with hamza substituted as in ishaḥ/wishaḥ and isādah/wisādah.
