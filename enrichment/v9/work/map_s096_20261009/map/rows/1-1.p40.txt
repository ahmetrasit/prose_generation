[NASAFI:1:1/r30] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | Rahman is uniquely used as a name but broad in meaning; rahim can describe others but is specific to believers. Rahman therefore precedes the more intense form despite the usual progression from lesser to greater.
[NASAFI:1:1/r31] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | God’s mercy is His beneficence toward servants, while its root sense is affection. The poet’s use of rahman for Musaylima is treated as a taunting excess in unbelief.
[NASAFI:1:1/r32] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | prefers | Whether rahman declines depends on the proposed condition for non-declension: those requiring absence of fa‘lanah leave it indeclinable, while those requiring an existing fa‘la decline it; Nasafi prefers the first analysis.
[NASAFI:1:1/r4] NASAFI, d. 710 · الله تعالى | reports | A divine report calls the Fatiha a treasure from the treasures of God’s Throne, explaining its name Sura of the Treasure.
[NASAFI:1:1/r5] NASAFI, d. 710 · النبي عليه السلام | reports | A Prophetic report calls the Fatiha a healing for every illness except al-sam; this grounds its names al-Shifa and al-Shafiya.
[NASAFI:1:1/r6] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | It is called al-Mathani because it is repeated in every prayer; it is also called the Prayer based on reports and its obligatory status, and al-Hamd and al-Asas because it grounds the Qur’an.
[NASAFI:1:1/r7] NASAFI, d. 710 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas advises a person who falls ill or complains of pain to turn to the Fatiha, calling it al-Asas.
[NASAFI:1:1/r8] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | The Fatiha has seven verses by consensus.
[NASAFI:1:1/r9] NASAFI, d. 710 · قراء المدينة والبصرة والشام وفقهاؤها | reports | Medinan, Basran, and Syrian reciters and jurists, including Abu Hanifa and his followers, hold that the basmala is no verse of any sura, but is written to separate suras and for blessing; they therefore do not recite it aloud in prayer.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r1] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | reports | Medina, Basra, and Syria's readers and jurists, with Abu Hanifa, hold that the basmala is not a verse of al-Fatiha or other surahs; it separates surahs and is blessed at the opening, so they do not recite it aloud.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r10] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | The ba is kasra-marked because it is invariably a preposition governing genitive; the noun's initial hamza is added only when it begins speech, while medial pronunciation drops it.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r11] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | The noun ism has a prosthetic hamza for initial pronunciation, can be shortened to sim, and derives from deleted-final form samw, “elevation”: naming raises and publicizes the named one's mention.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r12] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · عمر بن عبد العزيز | reports | Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz instructed his scribe to lengthen the ba, make the sin strokes clear, and round the mim; the written alif is omitted here through frequent use.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r13] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | Allah is uniquely the name of the true God, not an attribute; the source derives it from al-ilah with hamza loss, while Khalil, al-Zajjaj, Muhammad ibn al-Hasan, and al-Husayn ibn al-Fadl deny derivation and other accounts link it to bewilderment or worship.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r2] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | reports | Meccan and Kufan readers, with al-Shafi'i and his companions, count it as a verse of al-Fatiha and every surah and recite it aloud; they cite its inclusion in the early codices despite the instruction to exclude non-Qur'anic material.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r3] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says that whoever omits the basmala has omitted 114 verses from God's Book.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r4] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · أبو هريرة | reports | A Prophetic report presents al-Fatiha as divided between God and His servant: God answers the praise, glorification, and magnification, shares the response to worship and aid, and grants the servant's request in the closing supplication.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r5] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | prefers | The report begins God's response when the servant says “Praise belongs to God,” which indicates the basmala is not part of al-Fatiha; if it is outside al-Fatiha, consensus excludes it from other surahs too.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r6] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | In the Hanafi account the basmala remains a Qur'anic verse revealed to separate surahs; this does not contradict its exclusion from the surahs themselves.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r7] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | The initial ba attaches to an omitted verb such as “I read” or “I recite”; the act named is understood from the basmala that precedes it, as in a traveler saying it on departure or arrival.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r8] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | The omitted verb follows the basmala because the act and its object matter most; placing the divine name first gives it exclusive priority over the polytheists' invocations of al-Lat and al-Uzza.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r9] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | In “Read in the name of your Lord,” the verb is brought forward because reading was the central command of the first-revealed surah; “read” may also mean perform reading absolutely, or take the following phrase as its object. | mentions 96:1
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p6/r1] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | The lām of Allah is generally emphatic after fatḥa or ḍamma and light after kasra; some readers lighten it in every position, others always emphasize it, while the first pattern is the majority practice.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p6/r2] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | Rahman and Rahim are both derived from mercy, but Rahman is more emphatic because it has two augmentative patterns versus one in Rahim; added form signals added meaning.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p6/r3] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | Rahman describes mercy encompassing all creation, believer and unbeliever, especially in this world; Rahim describes mercy particular to believers in the hereafter, explaining the supplicatory usage and the order of the names.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p6/r4] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | Rahman is restricted as a name to God but universal in meaning, while Rahim can describe others and is particular in its referent; Rahman is nevertheless placed first despite its greater force.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p6/r5] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | reports | A hostile poet applied “Rahman” to Musaylima as a taunt in unbelief, not as a genuine divine description.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p6/r6] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | prefers | Grammarians differ on whether Rahman declines: those requiring the absence of a faʿlān feminine form leave it indeclinable, while those requiring a faʿlā feminine form decline it; the first analysis is preferable.

[QASHANI:1:1#2/r2] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | Three hidden alifs complete the twenty-two letters and signify the divine world in respect of essence, attributes, and acts: three in detailed consideration, one in realization.
[QASHANI:1:1#2/r3] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | The three written alifs signify those worlds appearing in the supreme human manifestation while the divine world remains veiled.
[QASHANI:1:1#2/r4] QASHANI, d. 736 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Asked where the alif of the basmala went, the Messenger replies that Satan stole it; he orders the bāʾ lengthened in its place.
[QASHANI:1:1#2/r5] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | Lengthening bāʾ compensates for the hidden alif: divinity is concealed in diffusive mercy and appears in human form, knowable only to its people, hence the indefinite form.
[QASHANI:1:1#2/r6] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | reports | A hadith says God created Adam in His image; the author explains a hierarchy of veils from essence by attributes, from attributes by acts, and from acts by beings and effects.
[QASHANI:1:1#2/r7] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | Disclosure of acts beyond the veil of created effects yields trust; disclosure of attributes beyond acts yields contentment and surrender; disclosure of essence beyond attributes yields annihilation in unity and absolute unification.
[QASHANI:1:1#2/r8] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | Unification of acts precedes unification of attributes, which precedes unification of essence; the three are reflected in the Prophet’s prostration supplication seeking refuge in pardon, pleasure, and God Himself.
[QASHANI:1:1/r1] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | Names are the determinate forms by which divine attributes and essence are known: their identities signify attributes and essence, existence signifies His face, and their individuation His unity.
[QASHANI:1:1/r2] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | Allah names the divine essence absolutely, without reference either to its being qualified by attributes or to its lack of attributes.
[QASHANI:1:1/r3] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | The All-Merciful grants existence and perfection universally according to wisdom and each recipient’s capacity at the beginning.
[QASHANI:1:1/r4] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | The Especially Merciful grants the human kind its particular spiritual perfection at the end; the perfected human form gathers universal and special mercy and manifests the divine essence and all attributes.
[QASHANI:1:1/r5] QASHANI, d. 736 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet says he was given comprehensive speech and sent to complete noble character; existent things are realities, while noble character’s states and sources of action are gathered in the universal human being.
