ULL:v1p62/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | The diminutive samiyy and plurals asmāʾ and asāmī support the proposed original form of ism.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p62/r2] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · الكوفيون | reports | Kufans derive ism from wasm, “mark,” because a name marks its bearer, but this would predict forms that the attested diminutive and plurals contradict.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p62/r3] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | rejects | The Kufan derivation has a good semantic fit, but its predicted forms fail because diminutives and plurals restore words to their roots.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p62/r4] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · الطبري | reports | Al-Tabari says this is not the place for debating whether a name and its named referent are identical, criticizing theologians who pursue such questions.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p62/r5] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | A name such as Zayd can refer either to its bearer or to the name as a word; in the first use name and named are identical in reference, in the second they are distinct.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p62/r6] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | Arab usage can make ism itself mean the essence; this reading underlies interpretations of several verses and the hadith of God's ninety-nine names. | mentions 87:1, 55:78, 12:40, 2:31
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p62/r7] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | reports | Malik reportedly answered that the name is neither the named referent nor other than it in every possible context, matching the author's contextual distinction.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p62/r8] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | The written name Allah is the most striking and most frequently used of God's names, usually preceding the others, which tend to function as attributes.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p63#2/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · العرزمي | reports | Al-ʿArzamī distinguishes Rahman as merciful to all creation through rain, senses, and universal favors, and Rahim as merciful to believers through guidance and kindness.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p63#2/r11] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · ابن عباس وموسى بن جعفر عن أبيه وعلي بن الحسين وقتادة وأبو العالية ومحمد بن يحيى بن حبان | reports | These authorities say the sura was revealed in Mecca; its linkage with Meccan al-Hijr and the identification of the seven oft-repeated verses support that view. | mentions 15:87
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p63#2/r12] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | The author adds that prayer was prescribed in Mecca and no prayer in Islam is known to have been performed without al-hamdu.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p63#2/r2] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · أبو سعيد الخدري وابن مسعود | reports | Abu Saʿid al-Khudri and Ibn Masʿud report the Prophet's distinction: Rahman is merciful in this world and the next, while Rahim is merciful in the next.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p63#2/r3] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · أبو علي الفارسي | reports | Abu ʿAli al-Farisi says Rahman covers all kinds of mercy and is unique to God, while Rahim concerns believers, as in 33:43; the author says these views reinforce one another. | mentions 33:43
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p63#2/r4] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · عطاء الخراساني | reports | ʿAta al-Khurasani claims Rahman was paired with Rahim after Musaylima adopted the name Rahman, making the pairing exclusive to God.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p63#2/r5] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | rejects | The author rejects ʿAta's chronology because the basmala predates Musaylima's rise and his naming was not firmly established.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p63#2/r6] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · قوم | reports | Some say Arabs did not know the word Rahman, citing their question about the one to whom they were commanded to prostrate.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p63#2/r7] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | rejects | The author says the Arabs' question showed ignorance of which deity was intended, not ignorance of the word Rahman itself.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p63/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · فرقة من أهل العلم | reports | One group holds that Allah is an un-derived name specially coined for God, with the article integral to its form.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p63/r2] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · كثير من أهل العلم | reports | Many scholars derive Allah from alaha, “worship,” and taʾallaha, “worship devotedly”; the author adduces a poetic use and a reading of 7:127 glossed by Ibn ʿAbbas as worship. | mentions 7:127
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p63/r3] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | reports | Several analyses explain the development from ilah to Allah through deletion or movement of the hamza and attachment of the article; the author judges the first explanation strongest.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p63/r4] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · الخليل | reports | Al-Khalil reports that ilah/walah began with wāw, with hamza substituted as in ishaḥ/wishaḥ and isādah/wisādah.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p63/r5] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | reports | Another explanation derives walah from bewilderment: minds are bewildered by the realities of God's attributes and by thought about knowing him.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p63/r6] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | reports | Explanations for omitting the final alif in Allah include avoiding confusion with al-Lat, lightening, or dialect; a verse by Ibn al-Aʿrabi illustrates the form.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p63/r7] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | Rahman marks mercy at its utmost limit, is used only of God, and is more emphatic than rahim, which is more emphatic than rāḥim.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p63/r8] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · بعض الناس | reports | Some say Rahman and Rahim have one meaning, like nadmān and nadīm, with one form more emphatic than the other.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p65/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · عطاء بن يسار وسوادة بن زياد والزهري وعبد الله بن عبيد بن عمير | reports | ʿAta ibn Yasar, Sawada ibn Ziyad, al-Zuhri, and ʿAbd Allah ibn ʿUbayd ibn ʿUmayr say al-Fatiha was revealed in Medina.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p65/r10] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · رسول الله | reports | The Prophet says no equivalent to al-Fatiha was revealed in the Torah, Gospel, or Qur'an.

[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p65/r4] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · ابن عباس وغيره | reports | Ibn ʿAbbas and others do call al-Fatiha the Mother of the Book.
