Al-Biqāʿī says harms may be immediate or delayed and differ in severity, while the repetition marks the enormity and lasting desert of the offense.
  75:34/q06/p3 The four warnings across 75:34–35 answer four faults: no faith, no prayer, denial, and turning away. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī lists failure to believe the Messenger, failure to pray before God, denial, and turning away from prayer, noting strutting came before denial; another reported view distributes threats over the four deeds. Samarrāʾī says the later denial and turning away restate the two earlier faults, so each gets an initial and repeated warning.
  75:34/q06/p4 The repetitions distribute threatened sufferings over successive stages of death, grave, resurrection, and Fire. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī gives death, resurrection, and Hell; al-Ṭabrisī reports Badr, grave, Judgment, then Fire, with thumma marking sequence. Al-Qurṭubī reports life, death, resurrection, and Hell but calls that assignment weak. Kāshifī in al-Burṣawī specifies hard death, painful grave, resurrection's horrors, eternal Hell. Samarrāʾī mentions death, resurrection, and Hell; he also says fa joins closely spaced harms while thumma marks a longer interval involving grave and afterlife, comparing Pharaoh's people in 40:46. The Study Quran reports Badr, grave, resurrection, and Hell for Abū Jahl.
  75:34/q06/p5 The later warning is greater in rank; thumma need not express elapsed time. — reasons: Samarrāʾī compares 90:11–17, where faith stands beyond freeing and feeding in rank, to argue for a stronger later threat.

75:34/q09 What is reported about the warning's occasion and first speaker? [* · reports] (turns on: Reports differ on whether the Prophet spoke before revelation or used revealed words, who directed the warning, the encounter's place and gesture, and which later boast or death detail is included.)
  75:34/q09/p1 The Prophet first said the warning to Abū Jahl, and revelation then repeated his words. — reasons: Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and Ibn ʿAbbās explicitly say the Prophet spoke first, with the latter specifying his own initiative. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Rāzī say the revelation confirmed or matched his warning; al-Qaffāl offers this as one possibility. Ibn ʿĀshūr cautions that reports of an occasion need not establish that the event first caused revelation.
  75:34/q09/p2 God first directed the Prophet to confront Abū Jahl with these words. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī transmits al-Ṭabarī's report that Abū Jahl had mocked the nineteen keepers of Hell; Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Suyūṭī links this to the revelation of 74:30. Al-Māwardī says the encounter at Mecca's Baṭḥā followed Abū Jahl's swaggering. Al-Ṭabrisī says God revealed the words as foretold.
  75:34/q09/p3 The Prophet confronted him at al-Baṭḥā, seized his garment or lapels, and Abū Jahl boasted before his defeat at Badr. — reasons: Muqātil says Abū Jahl had threatened to kill the Prophet, who shoved his chest; al-Māwardī adds a poke and says verses followed. Qatāda's versions have a garment seizure and Abu Jahl's boast that neither Muhammad nor God could harm him; al-Thaʿlabī says the sons of ʿAfrāʾ and Ibn Masʿūd killed him, comparing 3:12. Al-Thaʿlabī also reports his claim that he would never follow a Quraysh youth even if Muhammad were God's messenger; Ibn ʿĀshūr places the gesture at a mosque door in one transmission. Ibn Kathīr's Qatāda version reverses the grip, making Abū Jahl seize the Prophet by his clothes.
  75:34/q09/p4 At the mosque entrance, the Prophet took Abū Jahl's hand, warned him, and he entered shaken. — reasons: Al-Jishumī locates this encounter outside the mosque; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports the mosque door and says the revelation answered Abū Jahl's boast.
  75:34/q09/p5 At the Banū Makhzūm gate, the Prophet took and shook Abū Jahl's hand before the words were revealed. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī records this distinct location and hand shaking, followed by the boast of status and revelation in the spoken wording.
  75:34/q09/p6 The Prophet took Abū Jahl's hand, and Abū Jahl was later killed at Badr after another boast. — reasons: ʿAbd al-Razzāq's Qatāda report has Abū Jahl at Badr boast that God would no longer be worshipped. Al-Rāzī's report names Qatāda, al-Kalbī, and Muqātil and says revelation confirmed the warning. No location is specified in these notes.
  75:34/q09/p7 The verse came about Abū Jahl's killing at Badr, and he was called this community's Pharaoh. — reasons: Al-Jishumī reports a terrible death; al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, and al-Durr record the Prophet's saying that every community has a Pharaoh and Abū Jahl was this one's.
  75:34/q09/p8 The verse is a fresh divine warning to unbelievers, rather than a repetition of the Prophet's earlier utterance. — reasons: This is al-Qaffāl's first possibility as reported by al-Rāzī.
  75:34/q09/p9 God commands the Prophet to warn his enemy of an unbearable, near calamity. — reasons: This is al-Qaffāl's third possibility as reported by al-Rāzī.
  75:34/q09/p10 The Prophet used an already revealed threat when addressing Abū Jahl. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr makes this reading of the encounter, explaining that early occasion language can be loose.
75:34/q10 How does the warning connect with the passage and other verses? [* · links] (turns on: The notes relate the warning to the preceding faults, the sūra's haste motif, and the different circumstances of the similar formula in 47:20.)
  75:34/q10/p1 It answers the man's preceding denial, failure to pray, and arrogant departure. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr calls the taunting threat apt for a swaggering rejecter and compares 44:49, 77:46, 39:15, and 41:40. Al-Qurṭubī enumerates the faults in 75:31–33. Al-Biqāʿī sees the threat as a fitting substitute for his family's greeting. Elmalılı describes the dire fate, broken back, and dark distressed face rather than a laughing face. Iṣlāḥī says the deniers lack prayer and spending, their only useful provisions for an exacting journey.
  75:34/q10/p2 Its immediate warning matches the sūra's theme of hastening sin while delaying obedience. — reasons: Samarrāʾī says they chose immediate pleasures; ruin therefore comes quickly. He relates immediate fa to 75:5 and 75:20. The ḥalaqāt account cites preference for present life over the next at 75:20–21.
  75:34/q10/p3 Its repetition and strength contrast with the single awlā warning in 47:20 because the situations differ. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba and Ibn ʿAṭiyya cite 47:20 as a parallel threat or rebuke; al-Samīn refers its discussion to the end of Sūrat Muḥammad. Samarrāʾī says Muḥammad concerns living people hesitant before fighting, still able to repent, and names one fault, cowardice; Qiyāma concerns a denier after death with multiple faults and realized punishment, so its warning is repeated. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also compares 47:20 to the consequence of the present state.
75:34/q11 What spiritual consequence does the divine warning impose? [* · theology]
  75:34/q11/p1 It is God's word stamped on the person's heart, barring faith and piety and decreeing the Fire. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī explains the warning as a divine sealing with this effect.
