[SAMIN-DURR:2:228/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Al-muṭallaqātu is a subject with yatarabbaṣna as predicate; the sentence may be declarative in form but command divorced women to wait, or it may be literal description.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The emphasis requires the women themselves to observe the waiting period, not have someone else observe it for them, heightening the intended force.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الكوفيون | reports | The Kufans say the wording is itself an imperative with an omitted imperative lām.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | A literal declarative reading reconstructs ḥukm al-muṭallaqāti an yatarabbaṣna, omitting ḥukm and the maṣdar particle an; the author calls this very remote.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Tarabbaṣa takes a direct object because it means intazara, to wait for.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The apparent analysis omits the object of tarabbaṣa, understood as marriage or husbands, and treats three quruʾ as a temporal adverb.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Alternatively, three quruʾ itself is the direct object: they wait for three cycles to pass.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The apparent attachment makes bi-anfusihinna modify yatarabbaṣna, with bāʾ marking cause, because of themselves; this construction requires an explicit detached self-reference rather than a bound pronoun.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Alternatively, anfusihinna emphatically restates the attached subject nun, with an extra bāʾ allowed before nafs or ʿayn; on this parse the phrase has no syntactic governor.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:228/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The author answers that ordinary emphasis would require a separate nominative pronoun first, but the extra bāʾ makes this emphasis adjunct-like; he cites ba- phrases and al-Akhfash's report qāmū anfusahum.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p57#2/r1] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Abū Mūsā reports the Prophet’s comparison: a Qur’an reader resembles a citron with pleasant taste and scent, while a nonreader resembles a date with pleasant taste but no scent; a wicked reader is like fragrant but bitter basil, and a wicked nonreader like a bitter, scentless colocynth.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p57#2/r2] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | The chapter fits the report because fragrance tracks the presence of Qur’an recitation, showing the Qur’an’s superiority over speech from both righteous and wicked people.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p57#2/r3] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Ibn ʿUmar reports that the Muslim community’s allotted time is like the span from afternoon prayer to sunset, after the Jews’ morning-to-noon span and the Christians’ noon-to-afternoon span; Muslims receive two qirāṭs per worker while each earlier community received one.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p57#2/r4] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | Although its era is shorter, the Muslim community surpasses earlier communities by God’s favor, as 3:110 states; the workers’ extra reward is grace, not an injustice to the earlier communities. | mentions 3:110
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p57#2/r5] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Bahz ibn Ḥakīm reports from his father and grandfather that the Prophet said Muslims complete seventy nations and are the best and most honored before God.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p57#2/r6] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | The community’s distinction comes through the blessed Qur’an, which God made superior to, authoritative over, abrogating, and final among the revealed books.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p57#2/r7] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | Earlier scriptures came down all at once, but the Qur’an came in stages according to events; each descent was like the arrival of one earlier book, reflecting special care for it and its Messenger.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p57#2/r8] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | The Jews’ period ran from Moses to Jesus, the Christians’ from then until Muḥammad, and the Muslim community’s until the Hour; its doubled reward is God’s favor, supported by 57:28–29. | mentions 57:28, 57:29
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p606#2/r1] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | Divorced wives who have consummated marriages and menstruate must wait three quruʾ before they may marry again.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p606#2/r2] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · الأئمة الأربعة | reports | The four imams hold that an enslaved woman waits two quruʾ, half the free woman's term; a quruʾ cannot be divided, so the count is completed as two.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p606#2/r3] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · عائشة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report says an enslaved woman's divorce count is two and her waiting period is two menstruations; Ibn Kathir calls its transmitter Mzahir extremely weak, and al-Daraqutni says the correct version is al-Qasim's own statement.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p606#2/r4] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · ابن عمر وعمر بن الخطاب | reports | Ibn Majah transmits a raised report from Ibn Umar, but al-Daraqutni prefers Salim and Nafi's version as Ibn Umar's own view, similarly reported from Umar; they say no Companion's disagreement was known.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p606#2/r5] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · ابن سيرين وبعض أهل الظاهر | rejects | Some early scholars say enslaved women have the free woman's waiting period because the verse is general and menstruation is a natural bodily process common to both; Ibn Abd al-Barr reports and weakens this view.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p606#2/r6] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · أسماء بنت يزيد بن السكن الأنصارية | reports | Asma says she was the first divorced woman for whom a waiting period was established when this verse was revealed; Ibn Abi Hatim calls this report strange by this chain.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p607/r1] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · أبو بكر بن عبد الرحمن | reports | Abu Bakr ibn Abd al-Rahman says every jurist he knew held Aisha's view that quruʾ are intervals of purity.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p607/r2] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · ابن عمر | reports | Ibn Umar says a woman is clear of her husband once she enters the blood of her third menstrual period; Malik says this is the established practice in his community.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p607/r3] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · ابن عباس، وزيد بن ثابت، وسالم، والقاسم، وعروة، وسليمان بن يسار، وأبو بكر بن عبد الرحمن، وأبان بن عثمان، وعطاء، وقتادة، والزهري وغيرهم | reports | These authorities, and Malik, al-Shafi'i, Dawud, Abu Thawr, and one report from Ahmad, count quruʾ as purity; the purity in which divorce is pronounced counts among the three. | mentions 65:1
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p607/r4] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | For the purity-view jurists, the waiting period ends when the third menstruation begins, and the shortest time a woman can truthfully report is 32 days and two moments.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p607/r5] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · الأعشى | reports | Abu Ubayd and others cite al-Aʿsha's poem as evidence for quruʾ meaning intervals of purity, describing wives' purity days lost to their husbands' campaigning.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p607/r6] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · الثوري ومن وافقه | reports | The second view takes quruʾ as menstruations: the waiting period ends only when the third period finishes, with some requiring a ritual bath; the shortest asserted duration is 33 days and one moment.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p607/r7] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · علقمة عن عمر وابن مسعود | reports | A woman whose husband had divorced her asks about remarriage after her bleeding stopped but before she could pray; Ibn Masud says she remains his wife until prayer is lawful, and Umar agrees.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p608/r1] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · أبو بكر وعمر وعثمان وعلي وابن عباس ومجاهد وغيرهم | reports | Many early authorities count quruʾ as menstruations; this is the Hanafi position, the stronger report from Ahmad, and the views of al-Thawri, al-Awza'i, and others; senior Companions are cited in support.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p608/r2] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · فاطمة بنت أبي حبيش عن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report tells Fatima to stop praying during her quruʾ, which would plainly mean menstruation if authentic; Abu Hatim calls its transmitter Mundhir ibn al-Mughira unknown, though Ibn Hibban lists him among trustworthy narrators.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p608/r3] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · ابن جرير | reports | Ibn Jarir defines quruʾ as the appointed time for a regularly recurring thing to arrive or depart; this allows the word to cover both menstruation and purity, a shared-word analysis adopted by some legal theorists.
