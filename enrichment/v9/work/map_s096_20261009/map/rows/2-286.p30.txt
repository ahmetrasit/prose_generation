[ELMALILI:2:286/r15] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Divine commands require good deeds, while prohibitions forbid evil; performing or abandoning each kind brings benefit or harm respectively to the person charged.
[ELMALILI:2:286/r16] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The benefits and harms of obedience or sin accrue to those charged, not to God.
[ELMALILI:2:286/r17] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The idea that God's mercy would be greater if He imposed no duties is mistaken: mercy does not permit harmful desires to go unchecked or withhold obligations that lead to lasting benefit.
[ELMALILI:2:286/r18] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | An obligation beyond given capacity would be torment rather than a duty; God appoints such torment as just punishment for refusing His law, as in the command to enter Hell at 40:76, with worldly examples among lawbreaking peoples and individuals. | mentions 40:76
[ELMALILI:2:286/r19] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The supplications that follow are connected to the wisdom that duties are proportionate to capacity and direct people away from harm.
[ELMALILI:2:286/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | God's obligations leave capacity beyond what is required, allowing rest and voluntary good deeds after duties; the five daily prayers do not exhaust a person's ability to act.
[ELMALILI:2:286/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Taklīf means laying a burden upon the will; every burden requires enabling power, and the duty must not strain that power.
[ELMALILI:2:286/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Individual obligations scale with differing capacities, which is justice and equality of rights: zakat is absent for the poor and varies by wealth while remaining at one rate, unlike a flat per-person levy.
[ELMALILI:2:286/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The same capacity principle governs how communal obligations fall on individuals, and the total duties assigned to one person must not exceed that person's capacity.
[ELMALILI:2:286/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Some duties require not only enabling capacity without hardship but an additional, facilitative capacity grounded in greater ease.
[ELMALILI:2:286/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Elmalılı summarizes the greatest principle of legislative wisdom as duties being proportionate to the capacity of the person charged.
[ELMALILI:2:286/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Al-Qāmūs glosses kesb, iktisāb, and tekassub as seeking provision or something beneficial even without obtaining it; in Turkish, kesb here is working or exerting power.
[ELMALILI:2:286/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | If kesb and iktisāb are synonymous, the contrast between benefit and liability comes from their objects; the first mā is read as commanded good and the second as forbidden evil.
