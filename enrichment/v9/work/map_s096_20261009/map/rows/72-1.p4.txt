[RAZI-FULL:v30p134/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says the Prophet did not see the jinn: after heaven was guarded and meteors drove them away, investigators reached Tihāma, heard the Prophet reciting at dawn in ʿUkāẓ, recognized the cause, and returned to warn their people.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p134/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās infers that the Prophet had not seen them because, had he witnessed the event, he would not have attributed his knowledge of it to revelation.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p134/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | One reconciliation is that jinn were accompanying the devils when the latter were struck by meteors while spying on heaven.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p134/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | Alternatively, the ones struck by meteors were themselves jinn, called devils because a shayṭān is any rebellious being far from obedience to God.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p134/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · عاصم عن ذر | reports | A report from ʿĀṣim via Dharr identifies the visitors as a group led by Zawbaʿa and his companions, who heard the Prophet recite in Mecca and returned; Rāzī links this to 46:29. | mentions 46:29
[RAZI-FULL:v30p134/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · قيل | reports | Another view identifies them as the Shayṣabān, the most numerous jinn and the main body of Iblīs’s troops.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p135/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masʿūd reports that the Prophet was ordered to go to the jinn and recite the Qurʾān; he accompanied him to al-Ḥajūn, where a throng of jinn gathered, heard the recitation, and remained unseen to him.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p135/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · ابن مسعود | reports | In another version, the jinn ask who the Prophet is and for a witness; a tree comes, testifies that he is God’s Messenger, then returns to its place.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p135/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · ابن مسعود | reports | The jinn later ask for provisions, and the Prophet gives them bones and dung; he forbids people to use either as food.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p135/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Rāzī says the reports about Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Masʿūd cannot be rejected and offers several ways to reconcile them.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p135/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | The event Ibn ʿAbbās describes may have happened first, with revelation of this sūra, followed later by the Prophet’s instructed visit described by Ibn Masʿūd.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p135/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | If there was one event, the Prophet may have visited and recited without knowing what the jinn said or did, and God then revealed it to him.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p135/r7] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | Alternatively, the Prophet saw and heard the believing jinn; after they returned and told their people what had happened, God revealed their account to him.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p136/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The command “Say” tells the Prophet to disclose to his companions what God revealed about the jinn episode; Rāzī lists its benefits: the Prophet’s mission includes jinn, Quraysh see the Qurʾān’s inimitability, jinn are morally responsible, understand human speech, and believing jinn call their kin to faith.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p136/r10] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Rāzī answers that taking āmannā to mean “we affirmed and testified” resolves both objections.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p136/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Wahy is the discreet casting of meaning into the soul, as inspiration or an angel’s descent, and is done rapidly.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p136/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · يونس وهرون عن أبي عمرو | reports | The common reading is awḥā with an alif; Yūnus and Hārūn report that Abū ʿAmr read waḥā with ḍamma on wāw and no alif.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p136/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · قُرِىء | reports | Another reading is aḥyā, with hamza instead of the original wāw, by analogy with aʿada and azana.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p136/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | They agree that anna in “that a group of jinn listened” takes fatḥ because the clause is the passive subject of awḥā; Rāzī compares 6:19. | mentions 6:19
[RAZI-FULL:v30p136/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | They agree on kasr for innā in “we heard,” since it begins a quoted independent clause after the verb of saying.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p136/r7] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | One reading pattern gives fatḥ to clauses that belong to revelation and kasr to the jinn’s speech; the final clauses about mosques and the Prophet’s standing are exceptions to the jinn’s speech.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p136/r8] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | A second pattern opens all the following clauses by supplying “we believed in it and believed that…,” including the claims about the Lord’s greatness and the fool’s speech.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p136/r9] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | This second parsing faces two objections: attaching belief to clauses such as “our fool used to say” sounds improper, and Arabic does not coordinate onto a genitive pronoun without repeating the preposition.
[QURTUBI-FULL:72:1-2/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Muhammad is told to tell his community that God revealed through Gabriel that a group of jinn listened; Ibn Abbas and others say he had not known before the revelation.
[QURTUBI-FULL:72:1-2/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن أبي عبلة | reports | Ibn Abi ʿAbla reads aḥiya, preserving the original form; the source compares the change of wāw to hamza with 77:11. | mentions 7, 7, :, 1, 1
[QURTUBI-FULL:72:1-2/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Changing a wāw to hamza is generally permitted when the wāw bears ḍamma; al-Mazini extends the allowance to kasra and gives examples.
[QURTUBI-FULL:72:1-2/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The apparent wording, especially istamaʿa, indicates that the Prophet did not see the jinn; the source links this to 46:29. | mentions 4, 6, :, 2, 9
[QURTUBI-FULL:72:1-2/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas reports that the Prophet neither recited to the jinn nor saw them: a group at Nakhlah heard his dawn recitation and returned to warn their people.
[QURTUBI-FULL:72:1-3#2/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · عكرمة | reports | Ikrima says the Prophet was reciting Read in the name of your Lord (96:1); the jinn's names were already given in al-Ahqaf and need not be repeated. | mentions 9, 6, :, 1
[QURTUBI-FULL:72:1-3#2/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · عامر الشعبي عن علقمة عن ابن مسعود | reports | Al-Shaʿbi asked Alqama about Ibn Masud's encounter with the jinn. Ibn Masud said he was not with the Prophet that night; later the Prophet told him a jinn caller had taken him to recite, showed him their traces and fires, and the jinn asked for food and fodder, which he supplied with a warning against using bones or dung for cleansing.
[QURTUBI-FULL:72:1-3#2/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن العربي | prefers | Ibn al-Arabi says Ibn Masud's account is more authoritative than Ibn Abbas's because Ibn Masud witnessed the event, whereas Ibn Abbas heard about it; eyewitnessing is not like report.
[QURTUBI-FULL:72:1-3#2/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · قيل | reports | One reconciliation says the jinn came twice: first at Mecca in Ibn Masud's account, then at Nakhlah in Ibn Abbas's.
[QURTUBI-FULL:72:1-3#2/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · البيهقي | reports | Al-Bayhaqi reconciles the reports by saying Ibn Abbas describes the first hearing, when the Prophet neither recited to nor saw them, and Ibn Masud describes a later summons when he recited to them.
[QURTUBI-FULL:72:1-3#2/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · البيهقي | reports | Al-Bayhaqi says sound hadiths show Ibn Masud was not with the Prophet on the night of the jinn, but went with him later to see their traces and fires; other routes place him there.
[QURTUBI-FULL:72:1-3#3/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · النبي | reports | In Ibn Masud's report, the Prophet asks who will accompany him to recite to the jinn; Ibn Masud volunteers, waits within a marked boundary, and is told they listened and returned as warners. They request provisions, receive bones and dung, and Muslims are forbidden to use them for cleansing.
[QURTUBI-FULL:72:1-3#3/r10] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · مجاهد | reports | Mujahid's reported view is that believing jinn descended from Iblis do not enter Paradise, though they are kept from Hell.
[QURTUBI-FULL:72:1-3#3/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · عكرمة | reports | Ikrima says the group numbered twelve thousand and came from the Island of Mosul.
[QURTUBI-FULL:72:1-3#3/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | Another report describes jinn like men of Zutt with magpie-like faces; a tree came at the Prophet's call, testified to his prophethood, then returned to its place.
[QURTUBI-FULL:72:1-3#3/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | Another report says the Prophet rested his head on Ibn Masud's stone, awoke and asked for water to perform ablution; he used date water from Ibn Masud's vessel.
[QURTUBI-FULL:72:1-3#3/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The source says the discussion of water and cleansing materials was already covered in al-Hijr and al-Tawbah.
[QURTUBI-FULL:72:1-3#3/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الحسن البصري | reports | Al-Hasan says jinn descend from Iblis and humans from Adam; each kind includes believers and unbelievers, sharing in reward and punishment, with believers God's allies and unbelievers devils.
[QURTUBI-FULL:72:1-3#3/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says jinn descend from al-Jann, not the devils; they include believers and unbelievers, while devils descend from Iblis and die only with him.
[QURTUBI-FULL:72:1-3#3/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Whether believing jinn enter Paradise depends on their origin: if from al-Jann, they enter through faith; if from Iblis, two views follow.
[QURTUBI-FULL:72:1-3#3/r9] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الحسن | reports | If jinn descend from Iblis, al-Hasan's view is that believing jinn nevertheless enter Paradise.
