20:12/q23 How does Ṭuwā function grammatically? [طُوًۭى · grammar] (turns on: Whether Ṭuwā is a place-name appositive, an elliptical clarification, a verbal noun, or an adjective; its inflection reflects its analysis.)
  20:12/q23/p1 It is an apposition or explanatory apposition to الوادي. — reasons: Al-Tabari makes the proper name genitive by apposition; al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir and al-Alusi agree. Al-Samin lists apposition or badal.
  20:12/q23/p2 It is supplied as a nominative subject or accusative clarification. — reasons: Al-Samin permits a nominative with implied subject or an accusative with implied أعني.
  20:12/q23/p3 It is a verbal noun or adjective of repeated sanctification, or attached to the call. — reasons: Al-Hasan uses a verbal noun from folding twice; al-Farisi allows an adjective like thanā and sawā; al-Alusi attaches it to المقدس or نودي. Al-Nahhas cites a hadith against double collection as part of the double-folding analysis.
  20:12/q23/p4 Its declension depends on masculine name, feminine locality, shifted form, foreignness or regional status. — reasons: Al-Zajjaj gives four forms and contrasts masculine fu'al, feminine place-name and shifted name; Ibn Khalawayh questions the proposed unattested Ṭāwin, while al-Farisi allows analogy without one. Al-Farra compares Hunayn and Hira. Al-Samin and al-Alusi add foreignness; tanwīn may also fit a verbal noun.
20:12/q24 Is the final yāʾ of الوادي pronounced? [بِٱلْوَادِ · readings]
  20:12/q24/p1 It may be restored or omitted without changing the meaning. — reasons: Qiraat-ER notes a ten-reader restored yāʾ and a shortened form following the rasm. Ibn al-Jazari documents deletion and restoration at both بالواد المقدس occurrences, الواد الأيمن and وادي النمل, with routes differing for al-Kisai.
20:12/q25 What connects this wording to other Quranic accounts? [* · links]
  20:12/q25/p1 It recalls the call from the tree at 28:30 and the staff sign at 7:117. — reasons: Al-Tabarsi marks those links; Study Quran also reports God called from a tree. Al-Wahidi and Ibn al-Jawzi refer back to discussion of the sacred land at 5:21. Al-Farisi compares 79:16–17 for Ṭuwā's reading; the direct-address wording is contrasted with al-Naml and al-Qasas by al-Samarrai.
20:12/q26 What is reported about the tree and the call's visible setting? [* · reports] (turns on: The distinct reports about the tree, fire, voice and Moses' experience.)
  20:12/q26/p1 A green tree blazed with white fire without burning and angels glorified God. — reasons: Al-Zamakhshari adds great light, Moses' fright and calm, and calls the tree buckthorn; al-Tabarsi stresses that green and fire did not extinguish one another. Study Quran reports lycium or boxthorn.
  20:12/q26/p2 The tree moved away as Moses approached, then nearer when he turned back. — reasons: Ibn Ishaq's report says God spoke after this movement.
  20:12/q26/p3 The caller came from a tree. — reasons: Al-Tabarsi connects this to 28:30; Study Quran reports this identification.
  20:12/q26/p4 The sight or call is associated with the fire, which consumed the tree. — reasons: Study Quran transmits this alternate identification and cites 20:10.
  20:12/q26/p5 The call enveloped Moses from every direction. — reasons: Ja'far says each hair perceived it and divine majesty stunned him; al-Zamakhshari records hearing with every limb. Wahb says God was above, with, before and behind Moses and nearer than himself.
20:12/q27 How is the fiery encounter understood inwardly? [* · inward]
  20:12/q27/p1 Divine light and love consumed Moses' selfhood and revealed God through a fiery veil. — reasons: Qashani calls the fire a curtain of majesty and the declaration a veiled manifestation. Bursevi reads light as love and fire as ardent love, burning away selfhood. Ja'far says Moses was annihilated before an address borne and answered by God.
20:12/q28 What does the verse show about God and place? [* · theology]
  20:12/q28/p1 The divine speaker is not contained by the valley or by time. — reasons: Biqai says the purified courtly setting also signals transcendence of spatial and temporal containment.
  20:12/q28/p2 The divine manifestation may be veiled in created fiery form. — reasons: Qashani treats 'I am your Lord' as manifestation behind fiery curtains of majesty.
20:12/q29 What is the scope of figurative interpretation here? [* · interpretation]
  20:12/q29/p1 A metonymic reading can convey the whole sentence's gist beyond its apparent words. — reasons: Al-Burhan describes this rhetorical mode; al-Razi objects that it could turn the real sandal order into mere total devotion, opening boundless esoteric reinterpretation.
20:12/q30 What is said here about the preceding divine istiwāʾ? [* · theology] (turns on: Whether istiwāʾ denotes power or an independently named act or attribute.)
  20:12/q30/p1 The sounder interpretation is divine power. — reasons: Al-Jishumi prefers Abu Ali's view and calls the Kullabi account of an act or attribute named istiwāʾ unintelligible.
  20:12/q30/p2 It is a divine act or attribute called istiwāʾ. — reasons: Al-Jishumi reports this Kullabi position only to argue against it.
20:12/q31 Who identifies himself to Moses in this address? [إِنِّىٓ أَنَا۠ رَبُّكَ · interpretation]
  20:12/q31/p1 God himself is the speaker addressing Moses. — reasons: Ibn Kathir takes the words as God's explicit self-identification.
20:12/q32 What legal status does untanned carrion leather have? [نَعْلَيْكَ · law]
  20:12/q32/p1 It is impure, with two noted dissenters. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi says jurists generally agree that untanned dead-animal leather is impure, except al-Zuhri and al-Layth.
20:12/q33 What court custom is used to explain taking off the sandals? [فَٱخْلَعْ نَعْلَيْكَ · interpretation]
  20:12/q33/p1 Shoes are removed when entering a king's court. — reasons: Al-Qushayri reports this as an analogy for Moses' presence before God.
20:12/q34 How does the ruling on tanning affect the carrion-hide explanation? [نَعْلَيْكَ · law]
  20:12/q34/p1 The later Prophetic ruling purifies tanned hide and would abrogate an earlier ban if that ban explained Moses' removal. — reasons: Al-Razi cites the Prophet's saying that any tanned hide becomes pure.
20:12/q35 How has the sandal image appeared in Sufi literature? [نَعْلَيْكَ · inward]
  20:12/q35/p1 It became a recurring image in poetry and named works. — reasons: Study Quran notes Ibn Qasi's book Taking Off the Sandals among its literary afterlives.
20:12/q36 What root is Ṭuwā derived from? [طُوًۭى · meaning]
  20:12/q36/p1 A root meaning to fold or become enwrapped. — reasons: Study Quran gives the etymology; it informs interpretations of the valley as folded or doubled.
