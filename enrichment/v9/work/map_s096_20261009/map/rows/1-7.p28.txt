[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p52/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī argues that “ghayr” allows an object before it, just as lā does, and uses this to establish their grammatical and semantic similarity.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p52/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | Abū Ḥayyān calls al-Zamakhsharī’s argument very weak: the construction is disputed, similarity in meaning does not transfer syntax, and “أنا زيدا غير ضارب” is unattested.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p52/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | Supplying an omitted “path” before “ghayr” yields weak analyses, including an adjective before its apposition or repeated apposition.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p52/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · أيوب السختياني | reports | Ayyūb al-Sakhtiyānī reads “wa-laḍ-ḍāllīn” with a hamza in place of the long vowel to avoid two adjacent vowelless letters.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p52/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The hamza substitution occurs in words such as dāba and shāba, but it is too rare to be treated as a productive rule, despite Abū al-Fatḥ’s dialect argument.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p52/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · أبو زيد | reports | Abū Zayd records dāba and shāba as dialect examples; he first thought ʿAmr ibn ʿUbayd had misread until he heard the forms from Arabs.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p53/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | Some analyses posit omitted “path” before “not angered,” while other syntactic choices create many further ellipses in the sūra.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p54/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The sūra uses fronting and delay in “we worship, we seek help” and in “those angered, those astray.”
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p54/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The apposition “the path of those whom You favored” is explicit clarification after the initially vague “straight path.”
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p54/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الجمهور المكيون والكوفيون | reports | The Meccan and Kufan majority count the basmala as a verse and do not count “whom You favored” separately; other counters reverse those choices.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#2/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Alladhīna is genitive through annexation, a relative plural requiring a clause and resumptive; it usually has yāʾ in all cases, though some raise it with wāw, as in the cited verse.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A poetic form uses alladhūna with nominative wāw, and another deletes the relative's nūn to lengthen its clause; the plural is restricted to rational beings unlike singular alladhī.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Anʿamta is the relative clause: tāʾ is its attached nominative second-person subject, and ʿalayhim is governed by the verb and supplies the resumptive pronoun.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#2/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The causative hamza in anʿamta normally makes the object directly governed, but here the verb is cast with the prepositional government of tafaḍḍala.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The hamza pattern afʿala has many meanings: causation, abundance, becoming, assistance, removal, offering, finding, reaching number, time or place, and lexical relations to other patterns.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#2/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Further afʿala meanings include naming, prayer, deserving, conveying, greeting, arriving with something, distinguishing forms, and attacking; the passage illustrates each with examples.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | ʿAlā primarily signals literal or figurative elevation; it can also mean ʿan, bi, fī, accompaniment, causation, min, or be augmentative, with Qur'anic and poetic examples. | mentions 7:105, 2:102, 2:177, 2:185, 23:5-6
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | ʿAlā can be a noun meaning above when preceded by a preposition, and may be nominal where treating it as a preposition would produce an impermissible link between detached and attached pronouns.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#3/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The claim that ʿalā is also a verb because ʿalā Zayd means Zayd rose confuses unrelated words: the verb derives from ʿuluww, unlike the noun or preposition.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#3/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The pronoun hāʾ is normally damma, but non-Hijazis use kasra after a silent yāʾ or kasra; mīm is usually silent before a vowel and kasra before silence when hāʾ is kasra.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#4/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو بكر ابن الأنباري | reports | Ibn al-Anbārī records ten pronunciations of ʿalayhim, including variations in hāʾ and mīm vowels and added wāw or yāʾ; some were read in Qur'anic recitation.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#4/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ghayr is an indefinite apposition to alladhīna; calling it an adjective is difficult because ghayr is indefinite and the relative is definite.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#4/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · some grammarians | reports | Two defenses of adjectival ghayr are offered: it becomes definite between opposites, per Ibn al-Sarrāj's weak view, or the relative resembles an indefinite through ambiguity.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#4/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · مكي | reports | Makkī says ghayr replaces the attached pronoun in ʿalayhim; the author objects that deleting the replaced pronoun would leave the relative clause without its resumptive.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#4/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Al-maghḍūb is genitive by annexation and a passive participle; the prepositional phrase supplies its agent, and the relative al means those who were angered.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#4/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ghayr remains masculine singular, though its verb may be feminine when it refers to a woman; it is inherently adjectival and does not become definite through annexation.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#4/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ghayr can function like illā for exception, as an adjective by analogy with illā, or like lā for negation, permitting its governed object to precede it.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#5/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The poetic line places the object before negative ghayr, meaning the speaker is not unremembered by the beloved despite distance.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#5/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Object-fronting is not permitted when ghayr is non-negative; grammarians also differ between a general ban and a distinction for oath responses.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#5/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ghayr must be annexed overtly or implicitly, so putting the definite article on it is incorrect.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#5/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Ghayr is also read in the accusative as a circumstantial from alladhīna or ʿalayhim, or as a disjoint exception; al-Farrāʾ rejects the latter because lā lacks a preceding negation.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#5/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ argues lā is only augmentative after an earlier negation; the cited lines are offered in reply as cases without one.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#5/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Calling lā augmentative does not by itself answer al-Farrāʾ; the reply should show examples where it is augmentative without prior negation, while another analysis makes lā the object of abā.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#6/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الخليل | reports | Al-Khalīl is reported to parse accusative ghayr with an omitted aʿnī, meaning I mean those other than the angered ones.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#6/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | An omitted ṣirāṭ after ghayr is proposed, but this adjective analysis is weak because when an adjective and apposition coexist, the adjective ordinarily comes first; ghayr can instead modify ṣirāṭ alladhīna.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#6/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Accusative ghayr may also replace either ṣirāṭ, despite a possible repeated-apposition problem, or serve as a circumstantial from either one.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#6/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | If ghayr is adjectival, the analysis must either treat ghayr as definite or treat its relative antecedent as indefinite-like through ambiguity.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#6/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | Lā reinforces the negation already carried by ghayr and blocks reading al-ḍāllīn as coordinated with those blessed; Kufans instead equate it with ghayr, and ʿUmar reportedly read that way.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#6/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A rare hamzated reading of al-ḍāllīn is supported by dialectal examples; al-Zamakhsharī says it avoids two quiescent consonants, but examples without that environment suggest a regular dialect.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#6/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The past-tense relative clause presents God's blessing as established, while the noun after al generalizes across times; passive wording tactfully avoids attributing anger directly to the deity.
[SAMIN-DURR:1:7#7/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Inʿām is delivering benefit to another and is said only of rational recipients, not horses or donkeys.
