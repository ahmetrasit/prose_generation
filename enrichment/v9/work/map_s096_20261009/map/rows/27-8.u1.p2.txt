27:8/q18 What does calling God 'Lord of the worlds' contribute? [رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the title explains this call's universal authority or the different emphases of parallel scenes.)
  27:8/q18/p1 It shows universal lordship, power, providence and fitness for glorification. — reasons: Biqāʿī connects the perfect Lord's acts with raising Moses. Iṣlāḥī says Moses can trust the Lord of all worlds; Kur'an Yolu stresses God is Lord of all humanity, not only Israel, and relates the next verse's Mighty and Wise names to Moses' struggle.
  27:8/q18/p2 In the parallel narratives, the divine names fit each scene's level of exaltation. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī says Naml also names the Lord of the worlds and adds Mighty, Wise in the next verse, whereas Qaṣaṣ uses Lord of the worlds in its direct identification.
27:8/q19 What report about God's veil is linked to this fire? [ٱلنَّارِ · reports]
  27:8/q19/p1 God does not sleep, governs the balance and night and day deeds, and His veil of fire or light would burn all reached by His sight if removed. — reasons: Abū Mūsā transmits the Prophetic report, and Abū ʿUbayda recites 27:8 after it. Versions say fire, light, or light or fire; Muslim, Ibn Mājah and al-Bayhaqī are cited. Qurṭubī says Ibn Mājah supplies one wording and Muslim a fuller one. Abū ʿUbayd explains the radiances as the majesty of God's face; Ibn Kathīr locates the underlying report in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim through ʿAmr ibn Murra. Study Quran gives no grading.
27:8/q20 What other reports are attached to the call or its aftermath? [* · reports] (turns on: Which supplementary report is being related.)
  27:8/q20/p1 A Bedouin learned glorification and other devotional words from the Prophet. — reasons: Saʿd reports monotheism, magnification, praise and the verse's glorification formula, then pleas for forgiveness, mercy, guidance and provision. Abū Ḥātim says a believer's request for guidance asks for steadfastness and increase.
  27:8/q20/p2 Wahb says Moses did not touch a woman after God spoke to him. — reasons: Māwardī transmits Wahb's report.
  27:8/q20/p3 God's eschatological call is equally audible at every distance. — reasons: Ālūsī cites al-Bukhārī's report that God gathers people and calls: 'I am the King, the Judge.'
27:8/q21 How does this call compare with the parallel Moses narratives? [* · links] (turns on: Which detail in the parallel telling carries each account's rhetorical emphasis.)
  27:8/q21/p1 Naml gives the blessing and glorification, while Qaṣaṣ specifies where the call came from. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī says Naml's added formula suits its exalted setting; Qaṣaṣ supplies geographical detail for its fuller mode. He also notes Naml's short definite family promise versus Qaṣaṣ's tentative and detailed one.
  27:8/q21/p2 Naml's direct naming of Moses honors him and suits its weightier commission; Qaṣaṣ uses explanatory an. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the Qaṣaṣ call may convey meaning by words or sign, comparing 37:104–105 and 11:46. Its expansive mode permits an, while Naml's direct summons conveys honor; its divine names add exaltation.
  27:8/q21/p3 The narratives vary in the fire object, the hand sign, number of signs and addressees. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts shihāb qabas with jadwa, 'insert' with broader sulūk, nine signs with two proofs, Pharaoh's people with his elite, and Qaṣaṣ's wing gesture. He says shihāb suggests firmer benefit, idkhāl is narrower and harder, while sulūk can connote easy movement as in 16:69 and 39:21; recurring d-kh-l forms in Naml support its diction.
  27:8/q21/p4 The accounts can be read together as Moses' encounter, commission and signs. — reasons: Al-Bāqillānī says the call flows without break into lordship and the staff and hand signs. Ibn Kathīr says Moses' prediction of news and light was fulfilled and the passage recalls his mission to Pharaoh's arrogant elite. Iṣlāḥī says these verses prepare him for the signs; Asad compares 20:17–20 and points to an earlier note on staff symbolism.
27:8/q22 How does this episode function in the sūra? [* · links]
  27:8/q22/p1 It begins the promised pattern of prophetic election and signs, later balanced by warnings. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the five stories illustrate the sūra's opening promise and warning: promise predominates in Moses, David and Solomon, warning in the final two. Ibn Kathīr recalls Moses' commission and Pharaoh's elite rejecting the signs; Iṣlāḥī says the encounter prepares Moses for his mission.
27:8/q23 What inward significance is given to Moses' encounter? [* · inward] (turns on: How visible light relates to spiritual disclosure and inward faculties.)
  27:8/q23/p1 The outward fire was a veil for divine lights that drew Moses from attachment into intimacy and prophetic speech. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says God removed attachment to the visible fire, restrained Moses' speech and singled him out among messengers. ʿArāʾis al-Bayān says tree and fire mirrored majesty and beauty in a humanly bearable disclosure. Bursevī adds that God may manifest through fire, tree or mountain without direction.
  27:8/q23/p2 Moses' heart and faculties receive the fire of divine attributes, unveiling and wisdom. — reasons: Qāshānī calls the heart the blessed inner participant and the faculties, with celestial angels, its illuminated surroundings; glorification entails shedding bodily and psychic veils.
  27:8/q23/p3 God's manifestation can be perceived in a form while His transcendence remains absolute. — reasons: Ālūsī invokes mystical disclosure without confinement and the sacred hadith of God becoming the servant's hearing, within 42:11. Bursevī invokes a hadith about God being seen in a form He wills.
27:8/q24 What is said about tazkiya and zakāh in the ancillary note? [* · meaning]
  27:8/q24/p1 Tazkiya is the Messenger's taking alms; zakāh derives from growth and increase. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba cites 'he purifies you' at 2:151 and says alms grow wealth like blessed crops. This note offers an analogy for blessing language rather than an explanation of a word in 27:8.
