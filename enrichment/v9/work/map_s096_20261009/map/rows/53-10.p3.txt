[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:53:5/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The mysterious, vivid scene points to faith about what senses cannot reach and warns against straining reason beyond its limits.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:53:5/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The passage condemns pagan attempts to make divinity concrete through material or spiritual intermediaries and to ascribe godhood to beings besides God.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:53:5/r29] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | If Gabriel is the subject, after approaching he brings and teaches Muhammad the revelations God sent to His servant.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:53:5/r30] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | If God is the subject, Muhammad’s nearness removes the intermediaries and God gives revelation directly to His servant.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:53:5/r31] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | prefers | If these verses describe the Miʿrāj, the direct-revelation reading is stronger.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:53:5/r32] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The commentary also proposes that Gabriel appeared in his true form and approached closely, after which God revealed through Gabriel whatever He willed.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:53:5/r33] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | If the scene is the Miʿrāj, appearance, horizon, Sidra, descent, and nearness are figurative descriptions of divine acts and attributes, understood without asking how or how much.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:53:5/r61] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | Elmalılı reads verses 9–10 as Muhammad approaching God so closely after the horizon that every intermediary, even Gabriel, disappeared and God revealed directly to him.

[STUDYQURAN:53:10/r3] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | A third view is that God revealed to Gabriel, His servant, what Gabriel later transmitted to the Prophet.
[STUDYQURAN:53:10/r4] STUDYQURAN · JJ, Ṭb | reports | The revelation's content is left unnamed to honor its exalted status.

[STUDYQURAN:53:intro/r11] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The sūrah is famous for its rhetorical power and the dispute over the meaning of its opening section.
[STUDYQURAN:53:intro/r12] STUDYQURAN · many commentators | reports | Many commentators identify the passage as the Prophet’s two visions of Gabriel in his true form, unlike Gabriel’s usual human appearance.
[STUDYQURAN:53:intro/r13] STUDYQURAN · many others | reports | Many others read the passage as the Prophet seeing God, by heart alone or by both eye and heart, during the Night Journey and Ascension.
