[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p505#2/r1] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · الخليل | reports | Al-Khalīl analyzes iyyā as a pronoun with kāf attached in genitive; Abū ʿUthmān is also said to hold this view.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p505#2/r2] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · أبو الحسن | reports | Abū al-Ḥasan treats iyyā itself as a pronoun whose ending changes with the number and person addressed; kāf merely marks address, as in dhālika.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p505#2/r3] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · أبو الحسن | reports | According to the transmitted account, Abū al-Ḥasan disallows combinations such as iyyā-ka with iyyā of Zayd or falsehood.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p505#2/r4] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · سيبويه | reports | Sibawayh reports hearing an Arab say that after sixty a man should beware of himself and young women.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p505#2/r5] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · ابن كيسان | reports | Ibn Kaysān attributes to some grammarians the view that the full form iyyāka is a noun; others take its final yāʾ, kāf, or hāʾ as the pronoun and iyyā as support.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p505#2/r6] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · أبو إسحاق | reports | Abū Isḥāq says kāf is genitive by adding it to iyyā, which can also take the other attached pronouns.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p505#2/r7] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | prefers | The author accepts only Abū al-Ḥasan's account: a pronoun is already maximally definite and has no need for the specificity of annexation.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p505#2/r8] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | rejects | The author rejects the full-word analysis as weak: like anta, iyyā is the pronoun, while the kāf's vowel marks masculine or feminine address.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p511/r1] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | Pronouns divide into independent, attached, and hidden forms; they may be nominative, accusative, or genitive, though genitive pronouns have no independent form.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p511/r2] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | A hidden pronoun is understood in a verb such as sat or stood because a verb cannot stand without a subject.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p511/r3] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | Iyyā is built on sukūn because it resembles a particle; its final alif is original, like the alifs in lā, mā, ḥattā, and kallā.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p511/r4] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · أبو إسحاق | reports | Abū Isḥāq reportedly glosses iyyā-ka naʿbud as we worship Your reality and derives iyyā from āya, a sign.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p511/r5] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · أبو الفتح | rejects | Abū al-Fatḥ rejects that derivation: pronouns are built forms, not derived words; since iyyā is a pronoun it should not be considered derived.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p511/r6] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · صاحب النظم | reports | The author of al-Naẓm explains iyyā as exclusivity: iyyā-ka ḍarabtu directs the act specifically to you, which also explains its use in warnings such as beware the lion.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p516/r1] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | Worship is obedience joined to submission and humility, a form of abasement due only to God; ʿabd reflects a servant's lowliness and a well-trodden road is muʿabbad.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p516/r2] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · أبو بكر | reports | Abū Bakr says iyyā-ka is repeated for emphasis, like repeating bayna in the phrase between Zayd and ʿAmr.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p516/r3] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · أبو بكر | reports | He also notes that each verb independently selects an object: if placed after the verbs the phrase would be naʿbuduka wa-nastaʿīnuka, and both take the fronted iyyā-ka.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v1p516/r4] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | prefers | The author prefers emphasis as the reason for repeating iyyā-ka, since Arabic can leave the shared object unspoken after the first coordinated verb.
