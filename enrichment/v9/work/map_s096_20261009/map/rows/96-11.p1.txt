[BAYDAWI:96:11/r1] BAYDAWI, d. 685 · author | holds | The challenge considers whether the worshipper is rightly guided or commands piety; the repeated “Have you considered?” echoes the earlier question.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:96:18-19/r4] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report promises the reciter a reward as though he had recited the entire set of shorter suras.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:96:9-14/r3] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | The repeated أَرَأَيْتَ construction has the same grammatical function; its conditional is the second object, and its answer is omitted but supplied by the later condition. God sees the person's guidance and error.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:96:9-14/r4] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | reports | One reading asks whether the forbidding person is himself guided and commands piety as he understands idol worship, or whether he is a denier who turns from truth; God sees all his states.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:96:9-14/r5] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | reports | Another interpretation marvels that the worshipper is guided and commands piety while the forbidding person denies and turns away.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:96:9-14/r6] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | reports | A further reading shifts the second question to the unbeliever, as if a judge addresses each of two litigants in turn.
[NASAFI:96:11/r1] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | The question asks whether the forbidding person is on a sound path in what he forbids: worship of God.
[NASAFI:96:14/r2] NASAFI, d. 710 · author | holds | “The one who forbids” and the conditional clause are the two objects of the first “Have you seen?”; its omitted answer is supplied from the second condition, while the repeated question adds emphasis.
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p349#5/r1] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | The hypothetical asks whether the one doing the forbidding follows a sound path in the worship of God that he is trying to prevent.
[QASHANI:96:9-14/r2] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | The hypothetical question about whether the worshipper is guided or commands piety voices the arrogant denier’s own misguided claim and invitation to associationism.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:11/r11] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ reads the servant as guided and enjoining piety, and the forbider as denying and turning away; Abū Jahl is rebuked for ignoring God’s sight. Prayer reforms the Prophet himself and enjoining piety reforms others, both of which troubled Abū Jahl.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:11/r13] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī assigns the relative clause and conditional sentence as the two objects of the first “Have you seen?”
[ABUHAYYAN:96:11/r14] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī treats “did he not know?” as the omitted conditional answer, supplied by the second condition; he argues it may answer without fāʾ, by analogy with two interrogative examples.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:11/r15] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī regards the second “Have you seen?” as an extra repetition for emphasis.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:11/r16] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | prefers | Abū Ḥayyān rejects al-Zamakhsharī’s object analysis: the first object is the relative clause, while the second must be an interrogative clause, as in Qurʾānic parallels. | mentions 53:33, 53:35, 19:77, 19:78, 56:58, 56:59
[ABUHAYYAN:96:11/r17] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | prefers | Abū Ḥayyān analyzes the following “Have you seen?” forms as requiring omitted objects; their pronouns may refer to the forbider or, in the second instance, the servant, according to the disputed antecedent.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:11/r18] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The three governing verbs do not form a case of syntactic competition, because clauses cannot be pronominalized; the omissions are analyzed separately.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:11/r19] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | rejects | Abū Ḥayyān rejects an interrogative clause answering a conditional without fāʾ: grammarians require it after constructions that entail a request, except in poetic necessity.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:11/r5] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | The apparent addressee of both instances of “Have you seen?” is the Messenger; the pronoun sequence supports this reading.
[ABUHAYYAN:96:11/r6] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · قيل | reports | Another view addresses the unbeliever: would he forbid the worshipper if the prayer were guidance, calling to God, and commanding piety?
[ABUHAYYAN:96:11/r7] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · قيل | reports | On the alternative address reading, the pronouns in both conditional clauses refer to the forbider.
