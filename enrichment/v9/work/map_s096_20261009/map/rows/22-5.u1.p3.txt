22:5/q43 What other grammatical and orthographic details are noted? [مِّنَ ٱلْبَعْثِ لِكَيْلَا هَامِدَةًۭ مِن كُلِّ زَوْجٍۭ · grammar] (turns on: Attachment of min, circumstantial hāmidah, joined kaylā, and min in the plant phrase.)
  22:5/q43/p1 Min al-baʿth attaches to rayb or qualifies it through an omitted adjective. — reasons: Al-Samīn states both parses; Ālūsī favors the partitive adjective analysis.
  22:5/q43/p2 Hāmidah is an accusative circumstance of visible earth. — reasons: Al-Samīn says tarā denotes visual perception; Ālūsī also insists on physical sight.
  22:5/q43/p3 Li-kaylā attaches to 'is returned' and is written joined here. — reasons: Al-Samīn supplies the syntax; al-Nashr counts four joined occurrences; Samarrāʾī relates the joined form to immediate loss after knowledge, while Zarkashī compares parallel wording.
  22:5/q43/p4 Min kulli zawj qualifies an omitted object, or min is extra. — reasons: Al-Samīn reports the Kufan and Akhfash allowance of extra min, leaving 'it brought forth every kind.'
22:5/q44 What further wording choices shape the verse's argument? [* · rhetoric] (turns on: Specific particles, passives, word order, and direct address.)
  22:5/q44/p1 The wording guides the hearer through ordered stages while foregrounding the decisive sign. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr takes early thumma as real succession; Biqāʿī emphasizes passive old-age verbs and swift loss; Ālūsī says disclosure is placed first as the essential aim and maturity is marked as responsibility.
  22:5/q44/p2 The plant scene is chosen and worded for resurrection rather than merely natural description. — reasons: Samarrāʾī says hāmidah suits al-Ḥajj's resurrection setting; Ibn ʿĀshūr says beautiful growth adds grace beyond the proof; Ṭabāṭabāʾ links the growth to the womb's stages.
22:5/q45 How is the verse linked to surrounding passages? [* · links] (turns on: Opening warnings, subsequent conclusions, and parallel creation passages.)
  22:5/q45/p1 It supplies proofs for the following resurrection conclusions and continues the opening warning. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr calls 22:6–7 a concluding tailpiece and 22:8 a return to disputants; Study Quran connects the opening's destruction and resurrection to the life cycle; Rāzī cites 36:79 and 17:51.
  22:5/q45/p2 The verse connects with other descriptions of creation, aging, and revived earth. — reasons: Qurṭubī cites 23:12–13, 64:2, 40:64, 95:4 and 96:2; Study Quran lists many dead-land parallels; Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts 40:67's mention of old age.
22:5/q46 What broader sūra and Qurʾān background do these notes supply? [* · sciences] (turns on: Different sūra datings and general Qurʾān virtues.)
  22:5/q46/p1 Sūrat al-Ḥajj is described as Makkan, Madinan, or mixed, with a report of varied revelation settings. — reasons: Study Quran records the three dating views and the night/day, travel/home, war/peace report.
  22:5/q46/p2 Qurʾān recitation and teaching receive special merit in attached reports. — reasons: Al-Nashr reports prophetic and later statements favoring recitation and teaching, and a dream attributed to Aḥmad about reciting with or without understanding.
22:5/q47 What ancillary linguistic examples accompany citations of this verse? [* · meaning] (turns on: Lexicographic and poetic digressions preserved in the extracted notes.)
  22:5/q47/p1 The sources provide additional Arabic word distinctions and poetic terminology. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba contrasts paired Arabic words and explains poetry's reliability, rhyme faults, and usage; these examples support his general lexical method rather than a separate interpretation of the verse.
22:5/q48 What other nuances do the sources give to the embryo's gradual formation? [مُّضْغَةٍۢ مُّخَلَّقَةٍۢ غَيْرِ مُخَلَّقَةٍۢ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the formed stage is a discrete result, an ongoing process, or one of multiple outcomes.)
  22:5/q48/p1 The morsel develops from the drop and clot into visible organs, with some growth incomplete. — reasons: Muqātil traces the formed morsel to the drop; Ibn Qutayba glosses fully formed; al-Irshād says limbs gradually become distinct. Abū Ḥayyān says the intensive form reflects many organs; Jishumī gives blind or deaf children as examples.
  22:5/q48/p2 The text describes kinds of semen and stages of creation, even though miscarried matter does not itself produce the addressed people. — reasons: Ālūsī distinguishes generic from literal ancestry and contrasts this verse's starting-point sequence with 23:14, where each stage makes the next.
  22:5/q48/p3 The formation contrast may mark gradual stages within one development rather than two birth outcomes. — reasons: Kur'an Yolu finds sequence through the conjunction uncertain but prefers the embryological flow; it reports Asad's positive-negative rendering as logically plausible.
22:5/q49 What do the sources say about the stages after fetal formation? [ثُمَّ نُخْرِجُكُمْ طِفْلًۭا ثُمَّ لِتَبْلُغُوٓا۟ أَشُدَّكُمْ · interpretation]
  22:5/q49/p1 God brings surviving pregnancies to birth as weak infants and gradually nurtures them to strength. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr names initially weak senses, reason, and body, with parental care; Biqāʿī says small size protects mothers in childbirth and traces nursing, youth, and puberty; Rāzī says God eases nourishment.
22:5/q50 What additional readings concern the life-stage clauses? [وَمِنكُم مَّن يُتَوَفَّىٰ أَرْذَلِ ٱلْعُمُرِ · readings] (turns on: Different readings of العمر and an alternative codex wording.)
  22:5/q50/p1 The ordinary العمر has a vowel on its middle consonant. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it the majority reading; some transmissions dispute Nāfiʿ's short form.
  22:5/q50/p2 Abū ʿAmr, Nāfiʿ, and others read العمر with a still or shortened middle consonant. — reasons: Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, al-Samīn, Ālūsī and Abū Ḥayyān name the readers; al-Samīn calls it regular lightening.
  22:5/q50/p3 ʿAbd Allāh's codex has 'some of you become old men' in place of the usual return clause. — reasons: Rāzī transmits the alternative wording without naming an argument.
22:5/q51 What other transmitted wording is attached to resurrection and the creation clause? [ٱلْبَعْثِ لِّنُبَيِّنَ لَكُمْ · readings] (turns on: Unattributed readings and a minor vowel variant.)
  22:5/q51/p1 An unattributed reading has 'and that He is a raiser' (وأنه باعث). — reasons: Rāzī records the variant without naming its reader.
  22:5/q51/p2 A reading varies the vowel of the tāʾ in the reported form. — reasons: Jishumī says the manifest reading has fatḥa while some use ḍamma; the excerpt does not identify the term further.
  22:5/q51/p3 A report from ʿĀṣim reads nuqir with fatḥa and coordination with li-nubayyina. — reasons: Jishumī contrasts the majority nominative, understood with an implicit 'we'.
22:5/q52 What more is said about the purpose clause's syntactic link? [لِّنُبَيِّنَ لَكُمْ وَنُقِرُّ · grammar] (turns on: Whether disclosure attaches to created, and how retention relates to purpose.)
  22:5/q52/p1 The purpose clause attaches to 'We created you'; the following retention can be resumptive. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān reports Ibn ʿAṭiyya's parenthesis and al-Kirmānī's guidance gloss; Ālūsī reports the nominative critics, while al-Kashf makes retention a scene rather than an aim.
  22:5/q52/p2 Explaining embryo formation as proof of unrestricted divine choice is questioned. — reasons: Ālūsī calls the view that unformed embryos prove elective creation far-fetched.
22:5/q53 How do thumma and fa express the timing of stages? [ثُمَّ · grammar] (turns on: Whether they view the beginning or end of the preceding event.)
  22:5/q53/p1 Apparent delay reflects the time a process takes, so thumma can coexist with a parallel fa account. — reasons: The author of al-Basīṭ says developmental stages take time; Zarkashī reports that thumma looks to an event's beginning while fa looks to its end.
22:5/q54 What does being 'in' doubt convey? [فِى رَيْبٍۢ · rhetoric]
  22:5/q54/p1 The preposition pictures doubt as an enclosing condition. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the use of fī figurative, portraying the doubters as immersed in it.
22:5/q55 What does 'resurrection' itself mean in this argument? [ٱلْبَعْثِ · meaning]
  22:5/q55/p1 It is the dead being brought out of earth and conveyed to the gathering, bodies and souls restored. — reasons: Bursevī gives emergence and gathering; Biqāʿī explicitly includes bodies and souls.
22:5/q56 What further report describes the embryo's material gathering? [نُّطْفَةٍۢ عَلَقَةٍۢ · reports]
  22:5/q56/p1 A drop is gathered through a forty-day spread beneath the woman's nails and hair, then becomes blood in the womb. — reasons: Qurṭubī transmits Ibn Masʿūd's explanatory account; Bursevī also transmits it with ensuing clot and morsel periods.
22:5/q57 What legal conclusions are drawn from recognizing pregnancy or fetal movement? [مُّضْغَةٍۢ إِلَىٰٓ أَجَلٍۢ مُّسَمًّۭى · law] (turns on: Maintenance and paternity issues versus determining an empty womb.)
  22:5/q57/p1 Movement after the reported ensoulment period can inform paternity and support for a pregnant divorced woman. — reasons: Qurṭubī draws these rulings from the certainty of fetal movement after four months.
  22:5/q57/p2 A widow's four-month-and-ten-day wait can disclose whether the womb is empty. — reasons: Qurṭubī reports this proposed wisdom, linked to the fetal timetable.
