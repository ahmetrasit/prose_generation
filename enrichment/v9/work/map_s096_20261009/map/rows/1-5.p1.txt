[TAB-FULL:v1p159/r1] TAB-FULL, d. 310 · author | prefers | The worship clause means humbling, submitting, and lowering ourselves to God in acknowledgment of His lordship, exclusively; al-Ṭabarī prefers this sense to fear and hope because servitude is rooted in abasement.

[TAB-FULL:v1p161/r1] TAB-FULL, d. 310 · author | holds | The supplication seeks God's help alone for worship, obedience, and every affair; unlike idolaters who seek aid from their idols, believers serve God sincerely.
[TAB-FULL:v1p161/r2] TAB-FULL, d. 310 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas glosses the request as seeking God's help in obeying Him and in all affairs.
[TAB-FULL:v1p161/r3] TAB-FULL, d. 310 · author | holds | Help is requested for the remaining lifetime and future obedience, not completed past deeds; enabling the limbs and granting success are divine favor and لطف, whose unequal bestowal is neither mismanagement nor injustice.
[TAB-FULL:v1p161/r4] TAB-FULL, d. 310 · author | rejects | The prayer for help refutes Qadariyyah proponents of tafwīḍ, who claim God must grant each commanded person the assistance needed to act or refrain; otherwise asking help would amount to asking God not to commit injustice.
[TAB-FULL:v1p161/r5] TAB-FULL, d. 310 · author | holds | Worship may precede the request for help because worship and its enabling aid necessarily accompany one another, like saying either that someone fulfilled a need and thereby helped, or helped and thereby fulfilled it.
[TAB-FULL:v1p161/r6] TAB-FULL, d. 310 · author | rejects | He rejects the suggestion that the sequence uses a fronted phrase whose intended meaning comes later, a proposal attributed to some inattentive readers and illustrated by Imruʾ al-Qays.
[TAB-FULL:v1p163/r1] TAB-FULL, d. 310 · author | rejects | The Imruʾ al-Qays analogy fails: having enough money does not entail giving up desire for more, whereas worship and the aid enabling it necessarily coexist, so their order is not semantic reversal.
[TAB-FULL:v1p163/r2] TAB-FULL, d. 310 · author | holds | The repeated second-person object is idiomatic and more eloquent: Arabic repeats a pronoun with each verb, whether attached after the verb or fronted within iyyāka, though omission is possible when attached.

[TAB-FULL:v1p164/r1] TAB-FULL, d. 310 · author | rejects | The analogy to paired phrases with bayna is mistaken: bayna requires two complements, so omitting one makes the sentence defective, while iyyāka nعبد is already a complete clause.
[TAB-FULL:v1p164/r2] TAB-FULL, d. 310 · author | holds | Each clause independently needs its object pronoun, just as every verb may repeat its attached object; therefore iyyāka properly recurs before nastaʿīn.
