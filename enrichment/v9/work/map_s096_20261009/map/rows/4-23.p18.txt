[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p578/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Mothers and daughters have been forbidden since Adam; intercourse is degrading, so mothers are protected from it in return for their immense beneficence to children.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p578/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | A prohibited daughter is any female descended from you through birth at any degree; whether a granddaughter is literally or figuratively a daughter parallels the grandmother question.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p578/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · النضر بن شميل | reports | Al-Naḍr ibn Shumayl records that pre-Islamic Ḥājib ibn Zurārah married his daughter and became a Magian.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p578/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | A prohibited sister is anyone sharing with you a paternal backbone or womb; paternal and maternal aunts mean the father's and mother's sisters.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p578/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The text names aunts but not their daughters; grand-aunts are also prohibited, with some collateral aunt relations allowed when they are unrelated to the brother's descendants.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p578/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Nieces remain prohibited however far they descend; singular brother and sister are used because a plural follows, while the singular conveys the whole gendered class.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p578/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Seven women are permanently forbidden by lineage: mother and daughter through birth, plus sister, aunt, and brother's or sister's daughter through sibling relations.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p578/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The verse names nursing mothers and sisters to treat milk kinship like lineage: it gives one example from each of the birth and sibling categories to indicate the rest.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p579/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The Prophetic rule that nursing forbids what lineage forbids confirms the analogy indicated by the verse.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p579/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Milk kinship maps through the wet nurse and her husband: his parents are grandparents, his sister an aunt, his other children paternal siblings, and her children by him full siblings.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p579/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Two exceptions to the nursing rule are marrying one's nursing son's sister or nursing brother's mother, since the intercourse that creates the corresponding lineage bar is absent.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p579/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The verse does not settle nursing age, number of feeds, milk-father, or ways of administering milk into the stomach; jurists disagree on these matters.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p579/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | Most read اللاتي أرضعنكم; ʿAbd Allāh reads اللاي, Ibn Hurmuz التي, and Abū Ḥaywah reads الرضاعة with a kasra on rāʾ.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p579/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الجمهور | reports | Most authorities take mothers of wives as an unrestricted prohibition, whether or not the marriage to the daughter was consummated.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p579/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · علي ومجاهد | reports | ʿAlī, Mujāhid, and others allow marrying a wife's mother if the wife was divorced before consummation, treating her like a stepdaughter.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p579/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · علي وداود وأهل الظاهر | reports | They read “stepdaughters in your care” as a condition: if the daughter was not in his care, a man who consummated with her mother may marry her; both custody and consummation must be present for prohibition.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p580/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الطحاوي وغيره | reports | They explain the stepdaughters' association with the household as describing the usual case; stepdaughters remain forbidden even when they are not actually in the man's custody.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p580/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari takes “in your households” as explaining the prohibition: the girls are or may be raised by you, and after intercourse with their mothers they function like your own daughters.
