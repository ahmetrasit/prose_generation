# 87:7: 18 questions

## 87:7/q01 How does the exception in “except what God wills” function?  [إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the reservation names actual, divinely willed forgetting, a hypothetical divine power, an emphatic idiom, a rare lapse, or an exception attached to an earlier clause.
- 87:7/q01/p1 It is a real exception to the promise of retention: God may make the Prophet forget what He wills. — Al-Ṭabarī calls this the clearest sense; Abū Ḥayyān calls a substantive exception the apparent reading and objects that an empty exception is unsuitable to eloquent divine speech. Al-Ālūsī says it ranges over all objects taught, rather than a restricted class. Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies the understood object, 'what God wills you to forget.' Qatāda says no forgetting occurred except by God's will; the generic wording need not itself specify an occasion. [31 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس,مقاتل); BAGHAWI; IBNKATHIR(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN; DURR(ابن عباس,قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); IBNASHUR; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES(classical commentators); ABUHAYYAN+; IBNKATHIR(ابن جرير)+; TAB-FULL+; TAB+; ASAD-NOTES-]
- 87:7/q01/p2 It is a conventional qualification with no actual exception; God did not will the Prophet to forget. — Al-Farrāʾ compares oath language and the exception for eternity in 11:107–108: a speaker may say 'except what you wish' while intending to give all. Al-Qurṭubī says oath exceptions follow the swearer's intention. Makkī makes the comparison to both Hūd passages. Al-Farrāʾ's temporal gloss makes the exception a possible time that never comes. Al-Rāzī, al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı say the qualification displays God's power and that retention is His gift; al-Rāzī cites 17:86 and 39:65, al-Ālūsī also 20:114, and Elmalılı cites 39:65. Abū Ḥayyān rejects the empty-exception account; al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı reject his criticism. [35 notes: TAB(بعض أهل العربية); TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية); NAHHAS(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-QT(قيل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(قيل); IBNATIYYA(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفراء); TABRISI(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI(الفراء,الكلبي); QURTUBI(ابن عباس,الفراء,قتادة,قيل); ABUHAYYAN(الفراء وجماعة); SAMIN-DURR(الفراء,مكي); ALUSI(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,الفراء); ELMALILI(Ferrâ,İsam); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazı âlimler); ELMALILI+; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)+; ABUHAYYAN-; ALUSI(أبو حيان)-]
- 87:7/q01/p3 The tiny or unspecified exception rhetorically intensifies a total denial of forgetting. — Al-Kashshāf likens this to a friend's all-inclusive grant with a nominal reservation; al-Bayḍāwī says rarity can express negation. Al-ʿIṣāmī says the phrase reinforces universal negation. Elmalılı gives the poetic example of praising a fighter by excepting only sword nicks, a form of praise through seeming blame. Al-Samīn says such an exception has a meaningful emphatic force, rather than being mere surplus. [11 notes: KASHSHAF; RAZI; BAYDAWI; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI(العصامية,قيل); ELMALILI(Bazıları,İsam); ABUHAYYAN-]
- 87:7/q01/p4 The exception permits a small number of real, temporary lapses. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī cite the report of a verse omitted in prayer: Ubayy wondered whether it had been abrogated, and the Prophet said he had forgotten it. Al-Ālūsī distinguishes 'nearly nothing' from figurative total negation. [10 notes: KASHSHAF; RAZI; QURTUBI; BAYDAWI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(صحيح البخاري وغيره,قيل); ALUSI-FULL(أبي); ELMALILI]
- 87:7/q01/p5 The exception is attached to the earlier pasture becoming dark debris, sparing useful growth. — Al-Naḥḥās gives this as a fifth syntactic possibility. Al-Qurṭubī says pasture useful to people and animals would be exempt. Al-Samīn reports Makkī's transmission but declares this analysis impermissible. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(مكي)-]
- 87:7/q01/p6 The exception expresses the general principle that all human faculties and their limits operate by God's will. — Kuran Yolu's authors cite 76:30: God equips people for their task with thought, speech, memory and forgetting. They say the Prophet retains all that was recited because God wills it, while God could will otherwise. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]

## 87:7/q02 What may God cause the Prophet to forget or set aside?  [إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'forget' describes abrogated recitation, an ordinary but corrected lapse, cessation of practice, revelation not yet sent, or fading human knowledge.
- 87:7/q02/p1 Revelation whose recitation, and often its ruling, God abrogates. — Al-Ṭabarī favors divine removal as the clearest sense; al-Ḥasan and Qatāda repeatedly say both recitation and ruling are lifted. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Rāzī relate forgetting to 2:106. Al-Biqāʿī grounds it in benefit and easing the community; al-Rāzī says God knows people's interests. Al-Ālūsī argues abrogation may cause disuse or make removal from memory permissible without making 'forgetting' figurative; ʿĀʾishah's ten and five sucklings show that abrogated texts can remain remembered by scholars. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers this kind, describing cessation of recitation followed by forgetting; he cites ʿUmar's stoning and disavowal reports and the 'cause it to be forgotten' reading of 2:106. [46 notes: TAB(قائلو هذه المقالة); TAB-FULL(قائلو هذه المقالة); NAHHAS; MAWARDI(الحسن وقتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن، قتادة); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI(الحسن، قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وقتادة); RAZI(مقاتل); QURTUBI(قيل); BAYDAWI; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الحسن وقتادة وغيرهما); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI(الحسن وقتادة وغيرهما,الكشاف,عائشة); ALUSI-FULL(الكشاف,عائشة); IBNASHUR(عمر); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazı müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(Muslims); IBNASHUR+; QURTUBI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+; TAB+; ASAD-NOTES-]
- 87:7/q02/p2 Whatever portion God wills, without a specified cause in the wording. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Muqātil and Qatāda transmit the unrestricted explanation. Al-Ālūsī says the exception ranges over all the objects God teaches, rather than being restricted by grammar to abrogation. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes later kinds of divinely willed loss; the Study Quran stresses that such forgetting is no fault of the Prophet. [15 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس,مقاتل); IBNKATHIR(قتادة); DURR(ابن عباس,قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); ALUSI; IBNASHUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 87:7/q02/p3 A passage may slip from memory temporarily and later be recalled. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq distinguish a lapse from permanent forgetting. Ibn ʿAṭiyya limits this to after the Prophet has conveyed the material. Reports of a man's or ʿAbbād ibn Bishr's recitation bringing verses back support this. Al-Biqāʿī says a member of the community or another means may remind him. Al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı say no lapse is left uncorrected; Ibn ʿĀshūr makes it his second kind. Al-Ālūsī accepts eventual correction but says this verse alone does not establish it. [27 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحق); IBNATIYYA(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,بعض المتأولين); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); RAZI(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN(قيل); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قيل); ALUSI-FULL(أبي); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عائشة); ALUSI-]
- 87:7/q02/p4 A lapse may occur to establish a practice for the community. — Ibn ʿAbbās cites the Prophet's saying that he forgets or is made to forget in order to establish a Sunnah. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān include this among permitted lapses after conveyance. Elmalılı says fleeting lapses teach rulings for errors in recitation and prayer. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN(ابن عباس); ELMALILI]
- 87:7/q02/p5 God may delay sending down some revelation, which the Prophet has therefore not yet recited. — Al-Zajjāj, Ibn ʿĪsā through al-Māwardī, al-Ṭabrisī and an unnamed explanation in al-Qurṭubī give this reading. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI(ابن عيسى); TABRISI; QURTUBI(قيل)]
- 87:7/q02/p6 The Prophet leaves practice of a teaching only where God abrogates its ruling. — Al-Ṭabarī reports this interpretation of nisyān as leaving action. Al-Naḥḥās relates it to reading verse 6 as 'do not leave.' Al-Qurṭubī distinguishes ceasing recitation from ceasing practice. Al-Ālūsī argues that the apparent verb means literal forgetting and sees no need for this paraphrase. [5 notes: TAB(آخرون); TAB-FULL(آخرون); NAHHAS; QURTUBI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ALUSI-]
- 87:7/q02/p7 Humanity may let obsolete empirical and rational knowledge fade as knowledge advances. — Asad extends the address from Muhammad to humanity, connects it to 96:1–5, and describes observation and learning accumulated across generations. He says rational inquiry remains valuable yet cannot by itself reveal ultimate truths. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ASAD-NOTES+]

## 87:7/q03 What does the promise of not forgetting guarantee?  [إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · theology]
turns on: Whether the assurance excludes all lapses, lasting loss of conveyed teaching, or only loss outside God's chosen purposes.
- 87:7/q03/p1 The Prophet is protected from losing revelation that he must convey; any later lapse is corrected or serves a stated purpose. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says protection applies to what he was ordered to transmit; after learning and conveying it, he may temporarily forget, establish Sunnah, or experience abrogation. Abū Ḥayyān agrees. Al-Rāzī says a lapse in obligatory religion would damage the law. Al-Ālūsī says the verse bars lasting, uncorrected forgetting of essentials, while lesser etiquette or Sunnah can be forgotten, citing al-Rāzī; correction may come by revelation or a listener. Elmalılı says continuing non-forgetfulness is miraculous. [14 notes: RAZI; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ALUSI(الإمام الرازي); ALUSI-FULL(الرازي); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR]
- 87:7/q03/p2 God guarantees preservation, collection, recital, following, and explanation of the Qurʾān for the Prophet. — Al-Ṭabarī relates the assurance to 75:16–17 and the Prophet's hurried recitation. Islahi cites 75:16–19 for the several divine responsibilities. Al-Ṭabrisī calls the unlettered Prophet's retention of long passages after one hearing a proof of prophecy. [6 notes: TAB(بعضهم); TAB-FULL(بعضهم); TABRISI; NASAFI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES-]
- 87:7/q03/p3 The Prophet forgot nothing after this verse was revealed. — Al-Kalbī, Ibn ʿAbbās through Abū Ṣāliḥ, and other reports make the claim explicitly. Al-Farrāʾ's hypothetical interpretation also says God never willed actual forgetting. Qatāda's formulation retains the verse's qualification without quantifying any actual lapse. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI(مجاهد والكلبي); RAZI(الكلبي); QURTUBI(ابن عباس,قتادة,قيل); ALUSI(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazı âlimler)]
- 87:7/q03/p4 Only God can preserve or withdraw the granted memory, according to the interests of religion. — Al-Bursevī says God preserves what should remain and lets what should be forgotten pass away. Elmalılı says complete withdrawal remains within divine power and invokes 2:106. Qutb says the exception keeps hearts attached to God's unrestricted will. Kuran Yolu says memory and forgetting are among abilities God grants for human purposes. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 87:7/q04 Is “you will not forget” a statement or a prohibition?  [إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether lā is negating a future occurrence or prohibiting neglect, and whether the final alif can be explained as verse-end spelling.
- 87:7/q04/p1 It is a statement promising non-forgetfulness. — Al-Qurṭubī prefers negation: an exception to a prohibition is ordinarily time bounded, and the final alif appears in every codex and reading. Abū Ḥayyān calls the prohibitive reading weak. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that involuntary forgetting cannot be commanded and the written alif resists the proposed rhyme explanation. Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr explain it as God's promise. [9 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL(بعضهم); IBNKATHIR(ابن جرير); ABUHAYYAN; IBNASHUR; QURTUBI-FULL+; QURTUBI+]
- 87:7/q04/p2 It is a prohibition against neglecting recitation or leaving action, with the final alif retained for the verse ending. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī compare the extra alif to 33:67; the exception then permits omission where recitation is abrogated. Ibn Kathīr reports permission to leave aside abrogated material. Ibn Kaysān and Junayd's reported exchange glosses non-forgetting as not failing to act. Al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr object to the construction. [12 notes: TAB(آخرون); NAHHAS; KASHSHAF; QURTUBI(الفرغاني); NASAFI(ابن كيسان النحوي,جنيد); NASAFI-FULL(جنيد); IBNKATHIR(قيل); ABUHAYYAN(قيل); QURTUBI-; ABUHAYYAN-; IBNASHUR-; ALUSI-]

## 87:7/q05 What is the grammatical scope and placement of the exception?  [إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · grammar]
turns on: The syntactic antecedent of the exception: the Prophet's forgetting, an earlier position in that promise, or the pasture clause.
- 87:7/q05/p1 It excepts what God wills from the objects of 'forget'; mā refers to the thing willed. — Al-Naḥḥās calls the exception accusative. Al-Ālūsī says the scope is unrestricted among all the objects of forgetting. Ibn ʿĀshūr says mā is a relative pronoun and supplies 'that God wills you to forget.' [4 notes: NAHHAS; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR]
- 87:7/q05/p2 It is understood before the promise not to forget. — Elmalılı records this alternate placement of the exception. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 87:7/q05/p3 It attaches to the earlier description of pasture becoming dry or dark debris. — Al-Naḥḥās calls it a fifth syntactic possibility; al-Qurṭubī describes useful pasture as exempt. Al-Samīn says Makkī transmitted it and rejects it categorically. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(مكي)-]

## 87:7/q06 Why mention a divine exception after promising retention?  [إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the qualification conveys an actual reserved possibility, an emphatic idiom, instruction, vigilance, or the wisdom behind selective removal.
- 87:7/q06/p1 It reserves God's absolute power and makes the Prophet's retention visibly dependent on divine favor. — Al-Rāzī cites 17:86 and 39:65 to show that God could remove revelation or alter the Prophet's state; al-Ālūsī cites 17:86, 20:114 and 75:16. Elmalılı compares the hypothetical warning in 39:65. Qutb says the exception keeps hearts attached to God's will; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees the third-person turn as pointing to divine power. [14 notes: RAZI(الفراء); ALUSI(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); ELMALILI(Ferrâ); QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 87:7/q06/p2 It is an idiomatic way of emphatically affirming that none will be forgotten. — Al-Farrāʾ compares a complete oath and the exceptions in Hūd; al-Kashshāf likens a nominally excepted gift to giving everything. Al-ʿIṣāmī and Elmalılı say the exception reinforces, rather than restricts, negation. Abū Ḥayyān objects that this makes the exception empty; al-Samīn says it still serves emphasis. [12 notes: TAB(بعض أهل العربية); TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية); KASHSHAF; QURTUBI; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(العصامية,قيل); ELMALILI(Bazıları,İsam); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)+; ABUHAYYAN-]
- 87:7/q06/p3 It teaches the practice of saying 'if God wills' even when an outcome is known. — Al-Rāzī compares 18:24, where the future is qualified by God's will. [1 notes: RAZI]
- 87:7/q06/p4 It keeps the Prophet attentive to every piece of revelation. — Al-Rāzī says an unspecified possible exception would prevent carelessness, because any amount might have been excluded. [1 notes: RAZI]
- 87:7/q06/p5 It marks the place of beneficial abrogation or a corrective lapse. — Al-Biqāʿī says abrogation eases the community or a lapse displays the Qurʾān's greatness even over the greatest creature. Al-Bursevī says divine choice serves religion's interests. Al-Rāzī says God knows where abrogation benefits people. [5 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; RAZI]

## 87:7/q07 What was happening when the Prophet received and recited revelation?  [إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · reports]
turns on: Whether the hidden response to Gabriel's recitation was inner rehearsal or fear, and how his outward recitation proceeded.
- 87:7/q07/p1 He hurried or repeated Gabriel's recitation because he feared forgetting, and God reassured him of preservation. — Mujāhid describes inner rehearsal; al-Baghawī's report from Mujāhid and al-Kalbī says he began each verse before Gabriel had finished. Al-Kashshāf and al-Ālūsī say Gabriel would repeat it until it was memorized. Al-Ṭabarī and Islahi connect the reassurance to 75:16–19. Ibn ʿAbbās's report through al-Durr adds Gabriel's assertion that other prophets also forgot or had revelation lifted. [14 notes: TAB(بعضهم,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(بعضهم,مجاهد); BAGHAWI(مجاهد والكلبي); KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس); ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:7/q07/p2 He sometimes recited aloud alongside Gabriel while inwardly fearing that the passage would escape him. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān connect the manifest and hidden knowledge to this pair of outward recitation and inward fear. Al-Baghawī says the next promise of ease follows it. [15 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 87:7/q07/p3 He silently rehearsed or recited the passage within himself. — Mujāhid says the Prophet rehearsed internally for fear of loss; al-Nasafī gives a reading in which the hidden matter is the silent recitation itself, and al-Biqāʿī includes private repetition in the heart. [6 notes: TAB(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; NASAFI; BIQAI]

## 87:7/q08 Which reported lapses or reminders illustrate the exception?  [إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · reports]
turns on: The reported event or textual example used to show an actual lapse, its correction, or abrogation.
- 87:7/q08/p1 In prayer the Prophet omitted a verse; Ubayy thought it abrogated, but the Prophet said he had forgotten it. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Samīn, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite versions of this report. Al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı place it in dawn prayer and cite Bukhārī or the Ṣaḥīḥayn. Its point in these notes is a real, corrected lapse rather than permanent loss. [9 notes: KASHSHAF; RAZI; QURTUBI; BAYDAWI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(صحيح البخاري وغيره); ALUSI-FULL(أبي); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:7/q08/p2 A man's recitation, identified in some accounts as ʿAbbād ibn Bishr, reminded the Prophet of omitted verses. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya names ʿAbbād, while Ibn ʿĀshūr reports ʿĀʾishah's Bukhārī account of a nighttime reciter. Al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı transmit the reminder through al-Baḥr. The notes vary between a verse and verses, and between an unnamed man and ʿAbbād. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ALUSI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR(عائشة)]
- 87:7/q08/p3 Ibn ʿAbbās reports Gabriel mentioning earlier prophets' losses and Moses losing four of thirteen scrolls when emerald tablets broke. — Al-Durr's two summaries give these details as part of Gabriel's reassurance to the Prophet, who feared losing revelation; one says the lost or lifted verses carried no legal ruling. [2 notes: DURR(ابن عباس)]
- 87:7/q08/p4 ʿĀʾishah reports ten known sucklings were revealed and later replaced by five. — Al-Ālūsī cites the Ṣaḥīḥayn report to argue that abrogation of recitation does not entail the Prophet's forgetting every withdrawn text, since some hearers preserved such material. [2 notes: ALUSI(عائشة); ALUSI-FULL(عائشة)]
- 87:7/q08/p5 ʿUmar says passages on stoning and disavowing one's fathers were once recited. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites this as evidence for recitation later set aside. [1 notes: IBNASHUR(عمر)]

## 87:7/q09 Why use the name Allāh and shift to third person?  [ٱللَّهُ إِنَّهُۥ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the shift primarily foregrounds divine majesty and all attributes or the power behind the exception.
- 87:7/q09/p1 The divine name and pronoun heighten majesty and awe, locating the will in the one who has every divine attribute. — Al-Biqāʿī says the third-person turn and name invoke a majesty beyond explicit mention of an instrument of greatness; he sees the pronoun as exceeding creatures' grasp. Al-Ālūsī says the name signals a will encompassing all attributes. Elmalılı says the all-comprehensive name cultivates awe and relates the gift to the totality of names and attributes, with 'Lord Most High' confirming it. Corpus Coranicum refers its Allah note to Q 95:8 without a further claim. [6 notes: BIQAI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 87:7/q09/p2 The third-person turn points to divine power as the ground of the exception. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī gives this explanation. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 87:7/q10 What does al-jahr mean here?  [ٱلْجَهْرَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term is confined to voiced speech or denotes anything manifest to perception.
- 87:7/q10/p1 It is audible, raised speech, contrasted with quiet whispering. — Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī explicitly give raised voice as the lexical sense, the opposite of a whisper. [2 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI]
- 87:7/q10/p2 It is the manifest or openly perceptible, including what can be seen or heard. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says jahr can mean what is fully available to sight or hearing, and here contrasts manifest with hidden; he cites 4:153 and 21:110. Other commentators apply it to public deeds as well as speech. [7 notes: TABATABAI; MUQATIL(مقاتل); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; QURTUBI; ALUSI]

## 87:7/q11 What are the manifest and hidden things God knows?  [ٱلْجَهْرَ وَمَا يَخْفَىٰ · referent]
turns on: The scope of God's knowledge: all people's public and inward states or a specific contrast involving the Prophet, revelation, charity, future acts, or human perception.
- 87:7/q11/p1 People's public and private speech, deeds, and inward states in general. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr and others apply the pair broadly. Qatāda specifies inner whispering; Ibn ʿAbbās and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr name what lies in the heart or self. Al-Ālūsī says the overt includes actions and other manifestations, not speech only; Asad calls the pair what humans perceive and what they cannot perceive. Al-Bursevī adds that the unlettered Prophet received knowledge of writing, rules and crafts. [37 notes: MUQATIL(مقاتل); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير,قتادة); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(سعيد بن جبير,قتادة); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); TABATABAI]
- 87:7/q11/p2 The Prophet's audible recitation with Gabriel and his inward fear or silent rehearsal. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī and others give this contextual reading. The accounts vary: some identify the hidden as fear, others as private rehearsal or unspoken thoughts prompting aloud reading. Al-Ṭabrisī says God's knowledge preserves whatever he wishes to retain, voiced or concealed. Elmalılı says he may recite publicly or privately as appropriate. [22 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:7/q11/p3 The Qurʾān retained or recited in the Prophet's breast versus recitation erased from it. — Al-Māwardī, al-Qurṭubī and their fuller parallels give this contrast. Ibn ʿĀshūr describes the spoken verse as manifest and an omitted, forgotten verse as hidden in memory. Al-Rāzī also suggests known recitation versus what God abrogates. [7 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:7/q11/p4 What is known now versus what a person will know or put into practice later. — The abbreviated al-Māwardī attributes to Ibn ʿAbbās what one knows and later practices; the full note gives what God already knows and what the person will later learn. The two versions differ on the future term. [2 notes: MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 87:7/q11/p5 What a person displays versus acts of obedience left undone. — Al-Māwardī and his full version report this third explanation. [2 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL]
- 87:7/q11/p6 Charity given openly versus charity concealed. — Muḥammad ibn Ḥāmid or Ḥātim is named in the transmitted versions; the names vary in the notes. [4 notes: SULAMI(محمد بن حامد); THALABI(محمد بن حامد); QURTUBI(محمد بن حاتم); QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن حاتم)]
- 87:7/q11/p7 Perfection already manifest in the Prophet versus perfection still latent in him. — Al-Qāshānī gives this inward reading. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 87:7/q11/p8 The Prophet's needs and prayers, together with enemies' open tricks and hidden plots. — Islahi cites 20:6–7 and says God's comprehensive knowledge and power ensure needed aid. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:7/q11/p9 What humans can perceive versus realities hidden from human perception. — Asad connects limited empirical and rational knowledge with need for revelation to disclose ultimate truths. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]

## 87:7/q12 How does God's knowledge explain the promise and exception?  [إِنَّهُۥ يَعْلَمُ ٱلْجَهْرَ وَمَا يَخْفَىٰ · links]
turns on: Whether the knowledge clause chiefly explains selective revelation, relieves the Prophet's fear, warns against sins, or reinforces a wider sequence.
- 87:7/q12/p1 God knows what to preserve, erase, or abrogate for people's benefit. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī say comprehensive knowledge includes what benefits or harms religion; al-Bayḍāwī mentions the benefit of preserving or causing forgetting. Al-Biqāʿī says one who determines what can be forgotten must know all, invoking 16:40 for the efficacy of God's will. Al-Ālūsī and Qutb relate preservation and exception to wise decisions grounded in complete knowledge. [14 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; ELMALILI]
- 87:7/q12/p2 God knows the Prophet's audible recitation and private anxiety, so He reassures and reminds him. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says comprehensive knowledge underwrites God's reminding of a forgotten passage. Al-Ṭabrisī says God preserves what the Prophet wishes to retain. Al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān emphasize relief from his fear; al-Ālūsī reports the clause as emphasis for the recitation promise. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats it as a parenthetical explanation of protection from accidental loss, because even an unspoken, omitted verse remains known to God. [20 notes: KASHSHAF; BAGHAWI; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 87:7/q12/p3 The clause warns people to avoid open and secret wrongdoing. — Al-Ṭabarī applies the knowledge to Muhammad's public and concealed deeds, warning against contravening God's command; al-Naḥḥās applies the warning to everyone's public and private sins. [3 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS]
- 87:7/q12/p4 It generally reinforces both the preceding assurance and the following promise. — Al-Ālūsī reports this broader reading, which includes what follows in 87:8, while also offering the narrower connection to the Prophet's audible recitation. [2 notes: ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 87:7/q12/p5 Awareness of God's knowledge and power grounds trust, worship and confidence in needed help. — Elmalılı says God's omniscience makes the preservation promise trustworthy and merits glorification. Islahi says it includes the Prophet's worries and enemies' plots; firm awareness of knowledge and power generates faith and trust. [3 notes: ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 87:7/q13 Is the knowledge clause parenthetical or attached to another clause?  [إِنَّهُۥ يَعْلَمُ ٱلْجَهْرَ وَمَا يَخْفَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Its discourse attachment: a parenthesis explaining preservation, a broader reinforcement, or an explanation of glorification.
- 87:7/q13/p1 It is parenthetical and explains the promise of preserved recitation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr describes an interruption: God knows spoken recitation and even the verse hidden by an omission. Elmalılı says the clause is parenthetical while also grammatically attached to the promise to make the Prophet recite. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 87:7/q13/p2 It can reinforce what precedes and what follows, or particularly the recitation assurance. — Al-Ālūsī reports both broad and narrow connections; the latter rests on God's knowledge of the Prophet's aloud recitation and fear. Al-Kashshāf's full note similarly gives both contextual reassurance and general public and hidden states. [4 notes: ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 87:7/q13/p3 It should not be attached as an explanation of the command to glorify. — Al-Ālūsī explicitly rejects that attachment to 87:1. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 87:7/q14 Why is the manifest mentioned before the hidden?  [ٱلْجَهْرَ وَمَا يَخْفَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 87:7/q14/p1 The manifest recitation is the immediate focus; mentioning it first does not limit God's knowledge. — Al-Biqāʿī says knowledge of the hidden would include it if disclosed, and the explicit pair shows that public and concealed recitation are equally known. [2 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 87:7/q15 How should mā yakhfā be parsed?  [وَمَا يَخْفَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā denotes a hidden thing or nominalizes 'being hidden,' and whether the verb then has a subject.
- 87:7/q15/p1 Mā is a substantive pronoun referring to what is hidden. — Al-Samīn prefers this: treating mā as a verbal noun would leave yakhfā without a subject. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR+]
- 87:7/q15/p2 Mā may be analyzed as a verbal noun, giving neater coordination with al-jahr. — Al-Samīn mentions the syntactic attraction but rejects it because yakhfā would lack a subject. [0 notes: SAMIN-DURR-]

## 87:7/q16 How does the promise of recitation relate to the command to glorify?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether recitation is a necessary part of glorification or an independent promise that explains and prepares it.
- 87:7/q16/p1 Reciting revealed Qurʾān is part of glorifying God, so reassurance about memory follows the command. — Abū Ḥayyān says glorification entails recitation and the Prophet feared forgetting; al-Ālūsī reports this link but finds it weak. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN; ALUSI(أبو حيان); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-]
- 87:7/q16/p2 The independent recitation promise explains why glorification is fitting or prepares the Prophet for divine effusion. — Al-Ālūsī prefers the explanatory connection; alternatively glorification prepares reception of revelation, showing its merit, with reports cited from al-Thaʿlabī and Ibn al-Shaykh. He rejects making the later knowledge clause an explanation of the command. [4 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+]

## 87:7/q17 Who is addressed by the promise, and what knowledge does it concern?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the promise is confined to Muhammad's Qurʾānic memory or also addresses humanity's acquired knowledge.
- 87:7/q17/p1 Muhammad is promised retention of Qurʾānic revelation. — Classical commentators as summarized by Asad read this as addressed to Muhammad; al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr and Islahi connect it to preservation of the recited Qurʾān. Al-Ṭabrisī finds a prophetic sign in the unlettered Prophet's retention after a single hearing. [5 notes: ASAD-NOTES(classical commentators); TAB; IBNKATHIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABRISI; ASAD-NOTES-]
- 87:7/q17/p2 The address to Muhammad also reaches humanity and concerns cumulative knowledge that God teaches across generations. — Asad links the passage to the first revelation in 96:1–5 and says experience can render parts of empirical and rational learning obsolete. He argues that observation and speculation are limited, while realities beyond perception require revelation. [4 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ASAD-NOTES+]

## 87:7/q18 What role does this verse play in the sūrah and its surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the verse is read as part of the sūrah's original reassurance and thematic sequence or as a later harmonizing insertion.
- 87:7/q18/p1 It belongs to the sūrah's reassurance of the Prophet through preservation and ease. — Qutb says the sūrah fills the cosmos with praise and gives the Prophet good news of divine preservation and ease. Islahi says the address shifts from obstinate Quraysh in the previous sūrah to the Prophet, with the next sequence dispelling his preaching fears; 87:6–8 promises matured revelation, preservation except what God sets aside, and ease. Islahi says prior deniers had only temporary respite. [4 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:7/q18/p2 It contributes to themes of creation, revelation, final recompense, and the easy character of the message. — Qutb says these foundations are rooted in earlier scriptures, citing 87:18–19. He describes al-Aʿlā as Meccan with nineteen verses and says the Prophet loved and recited it at Eid and Friday prayers with al-Ghāshiyah, sometimes on the same day. [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]
- 87:7/q18/p3 It appears to be a later insertion reconciling the preservation promise with 2:106. — Corpus Coranicum proposes this literary history, saying verse 7 harmonizes verse 6 with the possibility of God making Muhammad forget verses in 2:106. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
