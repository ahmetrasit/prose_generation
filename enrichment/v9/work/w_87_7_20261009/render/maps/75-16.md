# 75:16: 16 questions

## 75:16/q01 What does moving the tongue mean here?  [تُحَرِّكْ لِسَانَكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the phrase denotes the organ's motion or the recitation accomplished with it.
- 75:16/q01/p1 It is the physical motion of the speech organ in reciting revelation. — Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī define taḥrīk as moving something between directions; al-Jishumī places motion and rest among spatial states. Al-Ṭabrisī calls the tongue the instrument of speech. Ibn al-Jawzī gives lisān as the organ, lasin as skilled speech, and lasan as eloquence; he also reports the simple sense 'tongue itself'. [6 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 75:16/q01/p2 Moving the tongue stands for reciting or rehearsing the words. — Al-Thaʿlabī explicitly glosses tongue movement as recitation to preserve the revelation; al-Ṭabrisī gives the same metonymy. [3 notes: THALABI; TABRISI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 75:16/q01/p3 Moving the tongue means opening one’s mouth to ask for knowledge of the Day’s timing. — Al-Ālūsī reports this idiomatic explanation in the alternative Judgment Day reading. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:16/q02 Which broader facts about the articulation and form of speech are cited?  [لِسَانَكَ · grammar]
turns on: Which articulatory or morphological environment governs the cited speech forms.
- 75:16/q02/p1 Identical consonants may assimilate, conceal, or remain distinct according to movement, word boundaries, and word structure. — Sībawayh favors assimilation when at least five moving segments would follow, citing poetic meter; clear Hijazi articulation remains good. More movements strengthen the preference, and a preceding long vowel counts as movement, as his rhyme example with yāʾ shows. A preceding matching moving consonant and following quiescent consonant also favor assimilation. Across words, distinct articulation remains possible; a quiescent predecessor bars silencing, though concealment with moving weight is possible. Ibn Nūḥ and ism Mūsā preserve word form, unlike intra-word رد and قل. Qurādd/lahāmm cannot be silenced; poems of an unnamed poet and Ghaylān b. Ḥurayth witness concealment. Meccans reportedly do not fully pronounce both tāʾs in 58:9; thawb Bakr favors clarity. Ṣuwaym permits concealment or silence. Final long wāw/yāʾ remain unassimilated before matching initials; speakers can conceal or articulate but cannot silence without losing length, as rhyme confirms. Imperative ikhshi/ikhshū can assimilate because their glides are not long vowels. Two hamzas are normally distinct, although Ibn Abī Isḥāq's assimilation is called poor but permissible. Iftaʿala tāʾs can be shown or concealed, unlike fixed iḥmarra; some Arabs quiesce the first or move the predecessor. Al-Ḥasan's 37:10 reading and Meccan murdifīn reported by al-Khalīl and Hārūn attest alternatives; muqtalīn is rarer and radif dialectal. Initial alif is retained in ألْحمر as hamzat qaṭʿ-like. Assimilation in iqtatalū is acceptable like jaʿal laka. اردد permits no concealment between two still letters. [26 notes: SIBAWAYH(أهل مكة,الحسن,الخليل وهارون,بعض العرب,بعضهم)]
- 75:16/q02/p2 Hamza at pause permits several realizations determined by its preceding sound and case. — Sībawayh records hinted or applied case vowels. Tamīm and Asad often move the predecessor to clarify faint hamza; some Tamīm avoid a faʿl sequence in al-radiʾ. Other Arabs replace final hamza by wāw, yāʾ, or alif by case, as in al-kalʾ/al-wathʾ. The choice should remain consistent. Once the predecessor moves, ordinary pause rules apply; final hamza is not doubled. Hijazi lightening after fatḥa yields alif without ishām; after ḍamma/kasra yields wāw/yāʾ. After a still consonant, softened hamza is deleted and its vowel transferred. [11 notes: SIBAWAYH(أهل الحجاز,من العرب,ناس من بني تميم)]
- 75:16/q02/p3 Weak letters change form according to position, vowel, suffix, and pattern. — Sībawayh cites taʿayyaṭat and rajaz ʿawṭaṭ for quadriliteral yāʾ becoming wāw. Final radicals are weakest because endings bear inflection and suffixes. Wāw-final imperfects use yafʿulu, yāʾ-final yafʿilu; faʿula occurs with wāw, not yāʾ. Final wāw after ḍamma and yāʾ after kasra resist heavy endings but accept fatḥa. After fatḥa they become alif; before consonantal suffixes they retain their original identity. Declensional final wāw after ḍamma shifts to yāʾ/kasra; consonant-preceded glides behave like sound radicals. Some plurals prefer yāʾ, while wāw after kasra always becomes yāʾ, with ʿAbd Yaghūth's verse cited. An added alif hamzates a final weak radical. Dialectal ghazi/shaqi retain yāʾ; raḍiyū/sarūw depend on vowel retention and two-sukūn avoidance. Lightened hamzated jīʾ/jūyi turns to jī. Deleted weak radicals remain deleted in singular jussives; dual long-vowel markers remain while jussive nūn drops. [18 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 75:16/q02/p4 Weak-medial noun plurals vary by vowel, number, gender, and analogy. — Sībawayh gives afʿāl for small counts of wāw-medial faʿl to avoid heavy afʿul, and fiʿāl/fiʿlān for larger counts; qaws/aqwus and rajaz athwāb preserve rarer afʿul. Yāʾ-medial faʿl likewise takes afʿāl in small counts, often fuʿūl in larger ones. Hollow qāʿ/tāj/jār favor afʿāl and fiʿlān. Yūnus reports feminine dār/sāq/nār with afʿul, but Sībawayh doubts gender alone explains it; dār/sāq also take fuʿul, dīrān, diyār. Nīb avoids yāʾ plus ḍamma. Fīl nouns use afʿāl/fuʿūl, sometimes -ah. Arwāḥ/riyāḥ preserve wāw class patterns. Feminine weak-medial nouns can take -āt or sound-like larger plurals, while nūbah/jawbah/dawlah/qaryah take broken fuʿul. Ḍayʿah/khaymah have nonregular fuʿul by analogy. Qīmah/rībah/dīmah permit sound and broken patterns; nāqah-type favors fiʿāl with fuʿul also; ʾaynūq/qiyam and the poetic tārāt/tīr are exceptional. [18 notes: SIBAWAYH(يونس)]
- 75:16/q02/p5 First-person suffix and pausal pronunciation depend on host structure and articulatory pressure. — Sībawayh distinguishes verb-object -ni from genitive -ī; frequent particles may drop nūn, especially laʿallī, whereas Zayd al-Khayl's poetic laytī is exceptional. ʿAnnī/qadnī/qaṭnī/minnī/ladunnī preserve the nūn-yāʾ marker; ilā/ladā/ʿalā cannot add nūn because their endings cannot move; qaṭ/ʿan/min are fixed like verbs. Some Arabs move a prefinal consonant to avoid adjacent still letters at pause; a Saʿdī poet's al-naqri witnesses this. Vowel harmony or pattern avoidance explains ʿudl/fisl, al-busr, and al-ʿukm; long vowels in Zayd/ʿawn differ. Qalqala qāf/jīm/ṭāʾ/dāl/bāʾ release audibly, sometimes strongly; zāy/ẓāʾ/dhāl/ḍād release softer breath, and other voiceless sounds breathe. Lām/nūn/mīm/ʿayn/ghayn/hamza lack this release. Connected speech suppresses pausal releases, illustrated by ayqiẓ ʿumayran and aḥriz mālan. [21 notes: SIBAWAYH(الراجز من بني سعد,بعض العرب)]

## 75:16/q03 Who is addressed by the prohibition?  [لِسَانَكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the scene is Gabriel delivering revelation, the resurrection record, an individual's inward knowledge, or inquiry about Judgment.
- 75:16/q03/p1 The Prophet Muhammad is addressed while receiving revelation. — Al-Ṭabarī and many commentators connect the command to Gabriel's delivery. Abū Ḥayyān calls this the apparent account supported by the sound occasion report. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it is etiquette during revelation. Al-Ālūsī invokes the sound majority transmission for the prophetic address and favors Zamakhsharī’s account over the proposed Judgment Day address; al-Ṭībī depicts Gabriel delivering the instruction to the Prophet at 75:15. [34 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الحسن,الشعبي,الضحاك,سعيد بن جبير,قتادة,مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي,القفال); TABATABAI; ELMALILI(İbn Abbas); ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAbbās); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+; ELMALILI+]
- 75:16/q03/p2 A person confronted with a record of deeds at resurrection is addressed. — Al-Qaffāl ties this to 75:13–15 and 17:14: God gathers and reads deeds, then commands confession and explains punishment. Al-Balkhī appeals to the surrounding judgment passage. Al-Jishumī says this fits what a person sent ahead better, though he says the worldly address is more apparent. Al-Suyūṭī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reject the account because of the sound revelation report and 20:114 parallel. Al-Balkhī and his followers favor al-Qaffāl’s address to the person shown the record, taking the record as implied by 75:13; al-Ālūsī notes that this differs from the sound majority transmission. [14 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI(البلخي); RAZI-FULL(القفال); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القفال); ITQAN(القفال,بعض الرافضة); TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(القفال); ALUSI-FULL(البلخي)+; JISHUMI+; ITQAN-; TABATABAI-]
- 75:16/q03/p3 Humanity generally is addressed about its inward knowledge or conscience. — Elmalılı reports this possible universal reading, based on 75:14–15 and the implied inner knowledge; he prefers the specific prophetic address because Qurʾān is later named. [2 notes: ELMALILI; ELMALILI-]
- 75:16/q03/p4 The addressee is a person asking prematurely about Judgment Day. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this proposal: God will gather those destined for Judgment, reveal its description, and clarify it with the trumpet blast; he rejects it. Al-Ālūsī reports that this alternative can address the Prophet, a generic addressee, or any suitable listener, and concerns asking prematurely for the Day’s date; he rejects the alternative as difficult. [3 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 75:16/q03/p5 The instruction extends to every believer studying the Qurʾān. — Asad extends it to reading, listening, and studying; Study Quran says students should reflect before drawing conclusions. [4 notes: ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]

## 75:16/q04 What does the pronoun “it” refer to?  [بِهِۦ بِهِۦٓ · referent]
turns on: The unstated antecedent, identified from the revelation occasion or the surrounding resurrection passage.
- 75:16/q04/p1 The Qurʾān or revelation, though its antecedent is implicit. — Al-Wāḥidī compares the unintroduced pronoun with 97:1; Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares 38:32. Al-Rāzī allows revelation, its descent, or Qurʾān. Elmalılı prefers this because Qurʾān is named later. Al-Ālūsī likewise cites 97:1. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says concision suits the sūra's haste motif. Asad specifies the contents of revelation; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports a narrower reading of what had already been recited. [19 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); ASAD-NOTES; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; ELMALILI+]
- 75:16/q04/p2 The person's record of deeds or the deeds read from it. — Al-Qaffāl links the pronoun to 75:13–15 and 17:14; al-Jishumī and al-Bayḍāwī describe an anxious reader. Al-Suyūṭī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reject the interpretation by the revelation report and parallel 20:114. Al-Balkhī and his followers derive the implied record from 75:13 and favor this referent. [13 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI(البلخي); RAZI-FULL(القفال); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القفال); ITQAN(القفال,بعض الرافضة); TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(القفال); ALUSI-FULL(البلخي)+; ITQAN-; TABATABAI-]
- 75:16/q04/p3 What is within a person, namely conscience. — Elmalılı says this follows if the address is universal and the implicit antecedent derives from the preceding self-witness passage; he prefers the Qurʾān reading. [2 notes: ELMALILI; ELMALILI-]
- 75:16/q04/p4 The coming Judgment and its description. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this interpretation and calls it untenable. Al-Ālūsī reports the Day of Resurrection as the antecedent in an alternative about its timing, but rejects that explanation. [3 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:16/q05 What action does the verse prohibit?  [لَا تُحَرِّكْ بِهِۦ لِسَانَكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether tongue movement is simultaneous recitation, later review, premature delivery, or a resurrection scene.
- 75:16/q05/p1 Reciting along with Gabriel before his revelation is complete. — The Prophet should listen with heart and ear and repeat only after Gabriel finishes. Al-Ṭabarī prefers Ibn ʿAbbās via Saʿīd: review for memory would be after collection, so the prohibition concerns premature recitation. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares a listener finishing a speaker's words too soon. Al-Ṭībī says the Prophet had habitually hurried ahead of Gabriel while hearing the earlier verses; Gabriel conveyed this instruction at 75:15. - 75:16/q05/p2 Compulsive or continuous review after receiving revelation, from fear of forgetting. — Al-Ṭabarī records Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, and al-Ḥasan on repeated review; he argues against taking that as the immediate occasion, since memory review follows collection. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk says it became burdensome. Al-Ālūsī says al-Ḍaḥḥāk’s account of burdensome review is unestablished and unsupported by the verse’s apparent sense. [12 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); TABATABAI(بعضهم); TAB-FULL-; TABATABAI-; ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك)-]
- 75:16/q05/p3 Speaking or delivering revelation before its connected parts have arrived. — Al-Shaʿbī says love and the sweetness of recitation prompted this; the instruction is to wait for the connected whole. - 75:16/q05/p4 Rushing the recitation to people, who need measured repetition for the message to settle. — Al-Jishumī reports a worldly-life coherence reading: repeated, slow instruction establishes God's proofs in hearts distracted by love of the world. [2 notes: JISHUMI]
- 75:16/q05/p5 Rushing through one's record of deeds in distress at resurrection. — Al-Qaffāl says God reads, gathers, and explains the deeds and asks for confession. Al-Jishumī finds the preceding self-accounting context favorable; al-Suyūṭī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī dispute the report's fit. Al-Qaffāl says fear makes the person stumble as he rushes through the record, before God reads the deeds and explains their consequences as a severe threat. [12 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI(البلخي); RAZI-FULL(القفال); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القفال); ITQAN(القفال,بعض الرافضة); TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(القفال); JISHUMI+; ITQAN-; TABATABAI-]
- 75:16/q05/p6 Voicing one's inner knowledge or asking too soon about Judgment. — Elmalılı reports the conscience interpretation. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports a question about Judgment and rejects it. Al-Ālūsī reports the alternative as a ban on opening one’s mouth to demand the Day’s date; demanding it before its divinely wise time is misguided. [5 notes: ELMALILI; TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI-; TABATABAI-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:16/q06 What does “to hasten with it” mean?  [لِتَعْجَلَ بِهِۦٓ · meaning]
turns on: What activity is premature and what 'it' denotes.
- 75:16/q06/p1 To try to take in or memorize revelation before its delivery is complete. — Al-Ṭabrisī distinguishes haste, action before its due time, from speed at the first due moment. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī gloss the phrase as hastening to receive it; 20:114 is their parallel. Al-Ālūsī says the bāʾ transitively marks what the tongue moves with or one hastens to learn. [8 notes: JISHUMI; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:16/q06/p2 To deliver or speak the revelation prematurely out of eagerness. — Al-Shaʿbī gives eagerness to convey and sweetness of speech as the occasion; al-Jishumī's worldly reading calls for slow repetition to people. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(الشعبي); MAWARDI-FULL(عامر الشعبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الشعبي); QURTUBI-FULL(عامر الشعبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشعبي); JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL(الشعبي)]
- 75:16/q06/p3 To hurry through a record of deeds, or to hurry a question about Judgment. — Al-Qaffāl links the former to the fearful reading of the record; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports and rejects the latter. Al-Qaffāl describes fearful haste and stumbling over the record; the other alternative concerns pressing for knowledge of the Day’s timing, which al-Ālūsī rejects. [9 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(القفال); ITQAN(القفال); TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(القفال); TABATABAI-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:16/q07 How does the purpose phrase function?  [لِتَعْجَلَ بِهِۦٓ · grammar]
- 75:16/q07/p1 It explains why the Prophet moved his tongue; the bāʾ marks the object of his recitation or learning. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the purpose clause gives the cause of the motion and matches the sūra's atmosphere of haste. Al-Ālūsī calls the bāʾ transitive on both accounts, movement with it or haste to learn it. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; ALUSI-FULL]

- 75:16/q08/p4 In a worldly coherence reading, he was urged to repeat slowly so listeners could absorb the proof. — Al-Jishumī says listeners' attachment to worldly life made repeated reminders necessary. [2 notes: JISHUMI]

## 75:16/q10 What does the instruction teach about receiving and preserving revelation?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the lesson is specific to the Prophet's reception or also extends to later Qurʾān students.
- 75:16/q10/p1 Listen until the reading is complete; God undertakes collection, preservation, recitation, and explanation. — Al-Khaṭṭābī says the unlettered Prophet should attend to understanding; al-Kashshāf adds heart and ear, then repetition and study. Ibn Kathīr identifies three stages: reception, recitation, explanation. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says inna causally grounds the prohibition in the promise, comparing 9:103. Iṣlāḥī adds arrangement and divinely appointed timing; 20:114–115 exhorts patience. [30 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الحسن,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; KHATTABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURHAN; ITQAN(الأئمة); BURSEVI(أهل التفسير); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; STUDYQURAN]
- 75:16/q10/p2 Patient study should precede conclusions; students must attend to meaning rather than mere fluent sound. — Asad applies the instruction to every believer, linking 20:114 to hasty conclusions from isolated verses and urging study of the whole message. Study Quran likewise urges reflection on all teachings. [4 notes: ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]

## 75:16/q11 Does the prohibition imply a prophetic sin?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the act was permitted before the command, a sin, or a good act refined by instruction.
- 75:16/q11/p1 The earlier haste could have been permitted until this prohibition. — Al-Rāzī answers the sin argument by analogy with a ruling changed over time, as in abrogation. Al-Ālūsī finds the Imam’s suggestion of express permission until the prohibition problematic. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)-]
- 75:16/q11/p2 The haste was either unauthorized sin or inexplicable if already permitted. — Al-Rāzī attributes this dilemma to those who permit prophetic sin. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(من جوز الذنب على الأنبياء)]
- 75:16/q11/p3 The haste sprang from a praiseworthy motive, and the command moved him to a more complete calm. — Al-Biqāʿī sees love of God and message as its motive and compares Moses' haste to please God at 20:84. Iṣlāḥī says noble motives still required patience under gradual revelation. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 75:16/q12 What does this episode show about prophecy and divine agency?  [* · theology]
- 75:16/q12/p1 God fixed revelation in the Prophet without ordinary study, demonstrating divine preservation and authorship. — Al-Jishumī calls this a miracle contrary to ordinary experience. Al-Biqāʿī says the command unveils divine giving rather than Muhammad's composition and cites 76:30 for action under God's will. [4 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 75:16/q12/p2 The Prophet had exceptional perception, yet receiving Gabriel's words still strained him. — Al-Biqāʿī says he was complete and continually advancing, not distracted from knowledge; he cites the Prophet's report of seeing companions behind his back, and describes sight in darkness and complete unveiling. Despite that, reverence and fear of loss prompted haste. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 75:16/q12/p3 The divine timing of revelation supplied the Prophet's mission, spiritual renewal, and answers to opponents. — Iṣlāḥī says he depended on guidance at every step, while opponents challenged piecemeal revelation and demanded replies. Revelation strengthened resolve, soul, and intellect; the Prophet was to leave collection, arrangement, explanation, and timing to God. [13 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Ḥamīd al-Dīn Farāhī)]

## 75:16/q13 How is this instruction connected to other Qurʾānic passages?  [* · links]
- 75:16/q13/p1 It parallels 20:114, which forbids hastening the Qurʾān before revelation is complete. — Al-Thaʿlabī also cites 87:6. Al-Rāzī says 20:114 redirects reliance from repetition to God and asks Him for knowledge. Ibn Kathīr notes the instruction to ask for more knowledge. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the parallel supports reports of one-time as well as staged descent. Iṣlāḥī adds 20:115 for patience. [22 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(ابن عباس، وسعيد بن جبير، والضحاك); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشعبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ITQAN; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 75:16/q13/p2 Its divine guarantee is comparable to promises of preserving, reciting, or clarifying revelation. — Al-Wāḥidī reports Abū ʿAlī's comparison to 20:114; 87:6 is also invoked for assured memory. Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares the causal use of inna with 9:103. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; THALABI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:16/q14 How does the instruction fit the sūra's surrounding argument?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the command is an occasion-driven aside, a thematic transition, or part of the judgment scene.
- 75:16/q14/p1 It is a timely aside during revelation, after which the resurrection discourse resumes. — Al-Khaṭṭābī and al-Rāzī compare a teacher or speaker briefly correcting a distracted listener. Al-Suyūṭī says the opening reached 75:15 as the Prophet hastened, and 75:16–19 intervened. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it etiquette interrupting the resurrection account. Al-Rāzī says six contextual explanations answer an allegation of textual loss. Al-Ṭībī says Gabriel inserted the instruction at 75:15 and the recitation resumed at 75:20, like a teacher correcting a rushed pupil; al-Ālūsī calls this interruption account far-fetched and unsupported. [8 notes: KHATTABI; RAZI-FULL(قوم من قدماء الروافض); BURHAN; ITQAN(الفخر الرازي); TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); KHATTABI-; ITQAN(بعض الرافضة)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 75:16/q14/p2 It is linked thematically by haste, conscience, speech, and the need for divine guidance. — Al-Rāzī ties unbelievers' pursuit of immediate happiness to religious haste, fear of forgetting, knowledge of their own falsity, and reliance on God; Abū Ḥayyān contrasts denial of signs with the Prophet's eager preservation. Al-Bayḍāwī says even haste in religion is reproved. Elmalılı integrates conscience and revelation; al-Suyūṭī moves from the human soul to the Prophet's soul. Al-Sāmarrāʾī links the reproaching soul, spoken excuses, 75:20, omitted elements, and gathering bones and Qurʾān; he also notes plural and singular divine forms. Al-Biqāʿī ties self-reproach to the accusation of human speech at 74:25–26. Iṣlāḥī relates the instruction to pressure from opponents. Abū Ḥayyān contrasts a denier absorbed in desire with a believer preserving, studying, and presenting God’s signs in hope of acceptance; al-Ālūsī objects that the denier’s discussion is still in progress when this instruction appears. [24 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الإتقان); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 75:16/q14/p3 The verses continue the resurrection record scene rather than introducing a prophetic aside. — Al-Balkhī and al-Qaffāl invoke the surrounding self-witness and record passage; al-Jishumī finds this contextual fit stronger while conceding the prophetic reading is more apparent. Al-Suyūṭī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reject it on the revelation report and 20:114. Al-Balkhī and his followers prefer continuity with 75:13: an implied saying introduces a circumstantial clause when the book is presented. Al-Qaffāl sees the ensuing detailed account and punishment as a severe threat. [12 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI(البلخي); RAZI-FULL(القفال); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القفال); ITQAN(القفال); TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(القفال); ALUSI-FULL(البلخي)+; JISHUMI+; ITQAN-; TABATABAI-]
- 75:16/q14/p4 The seeming break prompted an allegation of missing or altered text. — Al-Khaṭṭābī records the objection that 75:16–19 separates 75:14–15 from 75:20–21. Al-Rāzī attributes alteration allegations to early Rāfiḍīs; al-Suyūṭī records a Shiʿite allegation of dropped material. Abū Ḥayyān repeats al-Rāzī's report. Al-Khaṭṭābī, al-Rāzī, and Abū Ḥayyān answer with contextual connections. [4 notes: KHATTABI; RAZI-FULL(قوم من قدماء الروافض); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الرازي); ITQAN(بعض الرافضة); KHATTABI-; RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 75:16/q14/p5 The command interrupts the threat to heighten the terror of the Day and warn against demanding its date. — Al-Ālūsī reports that the Day is the pronoun’s referent and that God will assemble creation, describe the Day in the Qurʾān, and reveal it at the blast; he finds this account seriously difficult. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:16/q15 How are the following verbs about loving immediate life and leaving the Hereafter read?  [* · readings]
- 75:16/q15/p1 The following verbs yuḥibbūna and yadharūna are read in the third person or second person. — Al-Jishumī reports the third-person reading from Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr, Ibn ʿĀmir, and Yaʿqūb, and the second-person reading from the others. [1 notes: JISHUMI(ابن كثير، وأبو عمرو، وابن عامر، ويعقوب)]

## 75:16/q16 How is the command syntactically attached in the record reading?  [* · grammar]
- 75:16/q16/p1 It is a circumstantial sentence at the presentation of the record, introduced by an implied saying. — Al-Balkhī and his followers derive the record from 75:13 and favor this construction. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(البلخي)+]
