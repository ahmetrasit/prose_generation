# 17:86: 14 questions

## 17:86/q01 How do the oath and conditional in 'If We willed, We would surely take away' work?  [وَلَئِن شِئْنَا لَنَذْهَبَنَّ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the first lām is described as introducing an unspoken oath or simply as emphasis; both accounts make the later emphatic clause the oath's answer.
- 17:86/q01/p1 The first lām introduces an implicit oath; the emphatic second clause answers the oath and also supplies the conditional answer. — Al-Bayḍāwī calls the second lām the oath lām in the answer. Al-Nasafī says the first prepares the omitted oath and the answer stands for the conditional response. Al-Biqāʿī says the emphatic construction makes removal certain if God wills. Al-Būrsawī and Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise say no separate conditional answer is needed; al-Ālūsī says this allows the object after 'if We willed' to be omitted. Elmalılı describes God as swearing to this capacity. [8 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 17:86/q01/p2 The first lām is emphatic rather than the answer; the second lām receives and answers the oath. — Abū al-Fatḥ al-Mawṣilī distinguishes the function of each lām. He adds that the first lām can be omitted while the second answers the oath, citing 5:73 as an example. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو الفتح الموصلي)]

## 17:86/q02 How can God take away what He revealed?  [لَنَذْهَبَنَّ بِٱلَّذِىٓ أَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَيْكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'taking away' removes the already revealed material from people and books or takes the recipient in death and thus ends further revelation.
- 17:86/q02/p1 He could erase the revelation from memory and written copies, leaving no trace or accessible scripture. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām says nothing at all would remain. Al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī citing Abū Isḥāq, al-Jishumī, Ibn al-Jawzī citing al-Zajjāj, and al-Ṭabrisī's three authorities specify hearts and books; al-Ṭabrisī includes the Prophet's and community's memories. Al-Rāzī calls such complete removal extraordinary and links it to the little knowledge of 17:85. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Būrsawī, al-Ālūsī, Asad, and Study Quran specify manuscripts and memories; al-Būrsawī calls it a hypothetical supposition, leaving the Prophet as before revelation, and al-Ālūsī distinguishes written forms from stored mental images. Al-Zamakhsharī, reported by Abū Ḥayyān, says the Prophet would know nothing of the Book. Elmalılı stresses the Qurʾān revealed through the Holy Spirit and preserved against trials. Kur'an Yolu extends possible forgetting to later generations; earlier scriptures' forgetting or alteration illustrates human inability to secure divine knowledge. Ibn Masʿūd's interpretation reported by al-Ṭabarī applies the verse to lifting it from reciters' hearts and copies. [20 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن مسعود); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; TABRISI(الحسن وأبو مسلم والأصم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 17:86/q02/p2 He could take Muhammad in death, ending the revelation sent to him. — Al-Māwardī transmits this as an alternative to erasure from hearts and written copies; on this view the next clause means no one can protect Muhammad from God. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 17:86/q03 What is the force of the bā in the phrase for taking it away?  [بِٱلَّذِىٓ · grammar]
- 17:86/q03/p1 The bā makes the verb causative, like a causative hamza. — Abū Ḥayyān compares the construction with one discussed at 2:20 near the start of al-Baqara. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 17:86/q04 What is the thing described as revealed to the Prophet?  [بِٱلَّذِىٓ أَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَيْكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the contextual reference to spirit identifies the revealed object as the Qurʾān itself or as the divine Spirit that carries it; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī disputes that the next verse proves the Qurʾān reading.
- 17:86/q04/p1 It is the Qurʾān revealed to Muhammad. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, and al-Ālūsī explicitly identify it as the Qurʾān. Al-Baghawī compares its possible removal with God's withholding knowledge of the spirit. Al-Ālūsī includes its healing, mercy, and its steadying of the Prophet against opponents. Al-Ṭabrisī says the erasure reading supports taking the preceding question about spirit to concern the Qurʾān; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī replies that the next verse does not uniquely support that interpretation because it also fits other meanings of spirit. [4 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABRISI; TABATABAI-]
- 17:86/q04/p2 It is the heavenly or divine Spirit sent to the Prophet carrying the Qurʾān. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the phrase in the context of the spirit question and cites 42:52 for the Spirit sent to the Prophet. He says the following warning fits meanings of spirit other than the Qurʾān too. [3 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]

## 17:86/q05 Why describe the object as 'what We revealed to you'?  [بِٱلَّذِىٓ أَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَيْكَ · rhetoric]
- 17:86/q05/p1 The relative expression magnifies the revelation and draws attention to it as God's own speech and gift. — Al-Ālūsī says the description establishes the Qurʾān's rank at the outset and indicates it is not created speech. Al-Biqāʿī says the phrasing emphasizes revelation as the sentence's subject and that God alone can restore or replace it by mercy. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 17:86/q06 Who receives the warning in the singular address?  [إِلَيْكَ لَكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular form addresses Muhammad alone, includes the community, or rhetorically threatens other questioners.
- 17:86/q06/p1 The Prophet is addressed directly, though the lesson extends to others. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Prophet is addressed because his knowledge is greatest, while the caution extends to other scholars. The al-Taḥrīr explanation reported by Abū Ḥayyān connects the address with Muhammad's distress over delayed revelation about spirit. Muqātil connects the warning with his being invited to his forefathers' religion. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(صاحب التحرير); MUQATIL]
- 17:86/q06/p2 The warning is directed to everyone, or to the Prophet together with the community, despite the singular form. — Study Quran reports this as the view of most commentators and distinguishes the last sentence of 17:87 as addressed specifically to the Prophet. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(most commentators)]
- 17:86/q06/p3 The warning is directed to others whose knowledge could be removed. — Abū Sahl, reported by Abū Ḥayyān, says it deters questions about knowledge not granted, such as the spirit or the Hour. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو سهل)]

## 17:86/q07 What does 'then' add between loss of revelation and finding no advocate?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
- 17:86/q07/p1 It orders the two losses by rhetorical gravity: inability to recover revelation is more grievous than its removal. — Ibn ʿĀshūr treats the sequence as one of rank rather than merely elapsed time. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 17:86/q08 What does wakīl mean here, and what could no such agent do?  [وَكِيلًا · meaning]
turns on: Whether wakīl is conceived as an agent to restore what is gone, a defender against its removal, or a replacement guide; the first two also track erasure versus the Prophet's death in al-Māwardī.
- 17:86/q08/p1 An advocate, guarantor, or trusted agent who could recover and restore revelation; none can do so against God's will. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Būrsawī citing al-Kāshifī, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, Study Quran, and Kur'an Yolu describe retrieval of all or any part, memorized or written. Al-Baghawī stresses that no entrusted person can bring it back against God's will; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses no advocate can demand or compel its return. Al-Ālūsī calls wakīl figurative. Al-Jishumī glosses the word as one entrusted with affairs and relied upon, with alternatives defender, guarantor, keeper; Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives manager of an affair, including advocacy, dispute, and benefit; Ibn ʿĀshūr says entrusted defender, intercessor, or advocate. Abū Ḥayyān reports possible preservation or guarantees either of its return to hearts or of God's restoring it. Al-Māwardī says this sense fits the erasure interpretation. Al-Biqāʿī says God alone restores it by mercy. [27 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr); STUDYQURAN]
- 17:86/q08/p2 A protector or supporter who could shield the Prophet or the Qurʾān from God's act of withdrawal; none could prevent it. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām and al-Ṭabarī explain the impossibility of a guardian preventing God. Al-Ḥasan, reported by al-Thaʿlabī, speaks of protecting the Prophet from God; Abū Sulaymān al-Dimashqī, reported by Ibn al-Jawzī, says defending him against the decision. Al-Māwardī connects protection of Muhammad from God with the death interpretation. Al-Ṭabarī calls wakīl a caretaker or protector; al-Thaʿlabī also says supporter, while al-Jishumī includes defender or keeper. [8 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; THALABI(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي)]
- 17:86/q08/p3 No other guide could replace divine revelation as a source of ethical direction. — Asad says divine revelation is the sole absolute source of ethics, so no one could provide an alternative guide to the Prophet. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 17:86/q09 Does the power to remove the Qurʾān establish that it is created?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the removable thing is the Qurʾān itself or only human knowledge and written signs of it.
- 17:86/q09/p1 Yes: what can be removed cannot be eternal. — Al-Kaʿbī makes this inference from God's stated power to remove the Qurʾān. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الكعبي); RAZI-FULL-]
- 17:86/q09/p2 No: removal of people's knowledge and written signs does not prove the known or signified Qurʾān is created. — Al-Rāzī calls al-Kaʿbī's inference remote because the threatened erasure concerns hearts and inscriptions, not necessarily what those signs signify. Al-Ālūsī says the revelation's relative description signals speech that is not a created being's speech. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]

## 17:86/q10 What setting or incident is associated with this warning?  [* · reports]
turns on: The notes give different circumstances for invoking the warning: ancestral religion, delay over spirit, or the Yemen delegation's encounter.
- 17:86/q10/p1 It warned Muhammad after he was invited to his forefathers' religion. — Muqātil says God could take the Qurʾān away and no protector could prevent Him. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 17:86/q10/p2 It followed the delayed answer about spirit and addressed the Prophet's distress and need for decorum. — The al-Taḥrīr explanation reported by Abū Ḥayyān says the verse consoled and reassured Muhammad; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says it rebukes his promise to answer the next day and teaches submission to God's choice of what to reveal or withhold. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(صاحب التحرير); IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 17:86/q10/p3 After a Yemen delegation tested his prophethood, the Prophet recited the warning while expressing fear of straying. — The report in al-Durr has the delegation pose a riddle and test him through speaking pebbles; they hear his recitation and weep, and he describes his assigned path as razor edged before citing this verse. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 17:86/q11 What do reports say about the future lifting of the Qurʾān?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports vary in agent and setting (red wind from Syria, Gabriel, or no agent), timing and destination, cause, and what remains afterward; al-Dimashqī contests sudden extraction by citing a Prophetic statement about knowledge.
- 17:86/q11/p1 It will be withdrawn overnight from hearts and written copies, leaving no verse accessible. — Ibn Masʿūd is reported by al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Durr, and Study Quran; al-Thaʿlabī adds that trust disappears first, prayer and praying people last, and people are left like beasts. Al-Durr gives shorter variants emphasizing every codex, every inner memory, or no verse on earth or in a Muslim's body; a Prophetic report commands adherence to the Book after his departure. Shamr ibn ʿAṭiya says night worshippers cannot recite from memory or copies and gather to tell each other of the loss. The Prophet, as reported through Ḥudhayfa and Abū Hurayra, says no verse remains on earth. Ibn Masʿūd in Study Quran similarly frames the loss between trust and prayer. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن مسعود); THALABI(عبد الله بن مسعود); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,شمر بن عطية); STUDYQURAN(ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي)-]
- 17:86/q11/p2 A red wind from Syria will erase every verse from codices and hearts at the end of time. — Ibn Masʿūd gives the red wind's place of origin in reports in al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr; al-Ṭabarī says even a collector of the Qurʾān loses it. Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites the same report and this verse to establish that the event is possible. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 17:86/q11/p3 Gabriel will take the Qurʾān from people's breasts and homes in one night. — The report in Ibn al-Jawzī says people wake unable to recite or even sense a verse; the named agent here is Gabriel. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عبد الله بن مسعود); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي)-]
- 17:86/q11/p4 Reports of sudden Qurʾān extraction are unsound or may concern other knowledge; Qurʾān removal would come last. — Abū Sulaymān al-Dimashqī, reported by Ibn al-Jawzī, invokes the Prophet's statement that knowledge is not seized by sudden extraction; even sound chains for Ibn Masʿūd's report could refer to knowledge other than Qurʾān. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي)+]
- 17:86/q11/p5 At the end of time the Qurʾān is lifted because people recite without obeying it. — ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr says this in al-Thaʿlabī. In al-Durr, he adds that it sounds like bees around the Throne and protests that it was recited but not acted on. Ibn ʿUmar in Study Quran says it returns to its place of descent and complains for the same reason before the Hour. [3 notes: THALABI(عبد الله بن عمرو); DURR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); STUDYQURAN(ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿUmar)]
- 17:86/q11/p6 The Qurʾān is lifted when people abandon it for other books. — Al-Layth ibn Saʿd gives that cause in al-Durr. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(الليث بن سعد)]
- 17:86/q11/p7 The Book is lifted to heaven with the Torah, Gospel, and Psalms. — Abū Hurayra says this in al-Durr and adds that people will not know what they do in prayer. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة)]
- 17:86/q11/p8 The Qurʾān will be lifted from earth before the Hour, along with remembrance. — An Abū Hurayra Prophetic report says a time comes when it is sent for and lifted; Jābir reports the Prophet saying the Hour awaits the lifting of remembrance and Qurʾān. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 17:86/q11/p9 After overnight withdrawal, those favored by God will still retain the testimony of faith. — The Prophet says 'There is no god but God' remains in the heart when other written material is removed, in al-Thaʿlabī. Another Prophetic report in al-Durr says Islam wears away like a garment, leaving elderly people who repeat the testimony without knowledge of prayer, fasting, or rites. Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn ʿUmar transmit a warning against writing other books with God's Book and the same exception for the testimony. Ḥudhayfa says Qurʾān loses its sweetness and hearts forget the rites when it is removed overnight. [4 notes: THALABI(رسول الله); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,حذيفة)]
- 17:86/q11/p10 People should recite and perform pilgrimage before the Qurʾān is withdrawn, since afterward they forget and revert to older customs or poetry. — A report from ʿAbd Allāh in al-Thaʿlabī urges abundant pilgrimage and recitation before overnight removal and says people forget the testimony and follow pre-Islamic customs. Ibn Masʿūd in al-Durr urges recitation before night lifting and says people forget knowledge and turn to poetry. [2 notes: THALABI(عبد الله); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 17:86/q12 What is the warning's teaching and purpose?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes emphasize different implications of the same threat: God's control, gratitude for a favor, restraint in questioning, and dependence on revelation for ethics.
- 17:86/q12/p1 Revelation is wholly God's gift and under His control; its recipients should depend on His mercy and submit to what He discloses. — Al-Wāḥidī says God can take back what He gave and links it to humanity's little knowledge; al-Ṭabrisī says God mercifully supplies what the Prophet needs but withholds unneeded details even when people think them necessary. Ibn ʿAṭiyya applies it to the Prophet's next-day promise. Abū Ḥayyān says the power to reveal entails the power to withdraw, though God did not withdraw it. Islāḥī says the Prophet can neither acquire, refuse, nor retain revelation at will. Kur'an Yolu says all knowledge comes from God, no other power can protect, restore, or replace it; its example of earlier scriptures illustrates this dependence. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 17:86/q12/p2 It recalls a great favor and calls for gratitude and humility about knowledge. — Ibn Kathīr calls the revealed Qurʾān a great favor and joins it to 17:88: even cooperating humans and jinn cannot produce its like because created speech cannot match the Creator. Al-Nasafī says the gift consoles the Prophet through argumentative questioning. Ibn ʿĀshūr connects Qurʾānic healing and mercy to gratitude rather than conceit; the explicit threat indirectly declares favor, as the next verse clarifies, and admonishes scholars too. Study Quran says the warning and mercy exception express the favor of revealing and preserving Qurʾān in hearts and writing, and cites the pairing of mercy and bounty at 2:64, 4:83, 4:113, 4:175, 24:10, 24:14, and 24:20. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 17:86/q12/p3 It cautions against improper questions about withheld knowledge. — Abū Sahl says it threatens removal of what people do possess if they press for knowledge not granted, such as the spirit or Hour. The al-Taḥrīr explanation says it teaches the Prophet decorum after delayed disclosure about spirit. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو سهل,صاحب التحرير)]
- 17:86/q12/p4 It shows that only revelation supplies an absolute ethical guide. — Asad says, if it were removed, no alternative guide could perform its role. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 17:86/q13 How does the verse connect with the surrounding verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources attach the warning to different adjacent themes: little knowledge and spirit, divine mercy after the warning, or the Qurʾān's inimitability.
- 17:86/q13/p1 It follows 17:85's statement that people were given little knowledge: God could withdraw even that gift. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, al-Biqāʿī, and Study Quran make this link. Al-Wāḥidī also reports a reading in which the preceding address includes the Prophet and believers, whose knowledge of spirit was withheld; the threat to withdraw revelation supports it. Al-Ṭabrisī says the erasure reading suggests the spirit question concerned Qurʾān, whereas al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the warning fits other readings of spirit too. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 17:86/q13/p2 It follows accounts of revelation as healing, mercy, and a force that steadied the Prophet, and points toward mercy's preservation of it. — Al-Ālūsī recalls both healing and the thwarted effort to divert the Prophet; al-Biqāʿī mentions attempts to alter revelation and expel the Messenger. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the next verse makes the implied favor explicit. Study Quran says mercy may mean God declines to remove the Qurʾān or restores it afterward. Elmalılı says the Lord who revealed it preserves it and sustains Muhammad through special grace intended to spread universal mercy; al-Ṭabrisī says God gives necessary revelation while withholding needless detail. [8 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI; TABRISI]
- 17:86/q13/p3 The next challenge shows that the Qurʾān is a unique divine gift beyond human and jinn imitation. — Ibn Kathīr says no coalition of humans and jinn can produce its like, since created speech cannot match the Creator. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 17:86/q14 How is withdrawal of revelation read inwardly?  [* · inward]
- 17:86/q14/p1 Revelation can be effaced in annihilation or veiled after disclosure as spiritual states change; only merciful manifestation sustains revelation and the self. — Al-Qāshānī says no agent can restore revelation if veiled. A direct manifestation of the divine essence would leave neither revelation nor the Prophet's self, while manifestation through mercy brings both into being and majesty can veil revelation and knowledge. [2 notes: QASHANI]
