# 17:87: 11 questions

## 17:87/q01 How is the mercy exception construed against the preceding warning?  [إِلَّا رَحْمَةًۭ مِّن رَّبِّكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mercy is outside the preceding class, belongs among possible advocates, gives the purpose or verbal force of the clause, or supplies an omitted statement; the connected reading is challenged over the sense of advocate and what counts as the further favor.
- 17:87/q01/p1 It is a disconnected exception: God's mercy instead prevents the loss of revelation or keeps it with the Prophet. — Al-Farra compares the exception in 12:68; al-Zajjaj and al-Wahidi's Abu Ishaq say God fixed revelation in the Prophet's and believers' hearts. Al-Nahhas also allows mercy to restore lost knowledge. Ibn Atiyya stresses that mercy is outside the preceding category and disputes the view attributed to Ibn Khuwayz Mindad that the Qur'an lacks disconnected exceptions. Al-Tabrisi relates it to the preceding statement. Al-Samin says Basrans supply lakin and Kufans bal. Al-Kashf, preferred by al-Alusi, takes it to correct the claim that no advocate could restore the Qur'an; al-Alusi says this reading makes preservation a further favor after revelation. Ibn Ashur calls it a qualification of a hypothetical removal. Al-Razi, al-Baydawi, and al-Nasafi also report a connected alternative. [21 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الأنباري,ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن الأنباري وابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف)+]
- 17:87/q01/p2 It is connected to the missing guardian or advocate: divine mercy itself can secure the Qur'an's return. — Al-Zamakhshari includes mercy among the possible protectors against loss. Al-Samin likewise takes it under guardian. Al-Bursevi prefers a personified mercy advocating restoration. Al-Alusi reports this parse but says making mercy an advocate strains the expression; he also says it obscures the additional favor of preservation. Al-Razi, al-Baydawi, and al-Nasafi offer restoration as an alternative. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); BURSEVI+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 17:87/q01/p3 Mercy is an accusative purpose complement: God preserved revelation for mercy. — Al-Kawwashi supplies this purpose construction in al-Bursevi; Abu al-Baqa supplies it in al-Alusi. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الكواشي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]
- 17:87/q01/p4 Mercy is an accusative verbal noun or absolute object: God showed mercy. — Al-Jishumi calls rahmatan a verbal noun; Abu al-Baqa, as reported by al-Alusi, calls it an absolute object. [2 notes: JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]
- 17:87/q01/p5 An implicit clause says that revelation was sent, received, or retained only by the Lord's mercy. — Yahya ibn Sallam supplies God's sending of the Qur'an to the Prophet. Al-Tabatabai supplies the Prophet's receiving and retaining the Spirit. [2 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TABATABAI]

## 17:87/q02 What does the Lord's mercy accomplish here?  [رَحْمَةًۭ مِّن رَّبِّكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether mercy primarily prevents loss, restores a loss, grants revelation initially, or, in al-Qashani's inward account, sustains existence and revelation.
- 17:87/q02/p1 It keeps the Qur'an or its knowledge with the Prophet and believers, preventing its threatened erasure. — Muqatil calls the Qur'an a special grant; al-Zajjaj, al-Wahidi's Abu Ishaq, al-Wahidi al-Wajiz, and al-Tabrisi stress its establishment in hearts. Al-Tabari, al-Mawardi, al-Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, al-Razi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, al-Bursevi's Kashifi, al-Alusi, and Ibn Ashur connect preservation to favor. Al-Kashshaf and al-Razi add that scholars should thank God for retained knowledge. Ibn Ashur cites 15:9; Kur'an Yolu adds transmission without corruption, while Study Quran says preservation is in hearts and writing and cites 2:64, 4:83, 4:113, 4:175, 24:10, 24:14, and 24:20 for the pairing of mercy and bounty. Elmalili calls this special grace the basis for spreading universal mercy. [30 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الأنباري,ابن عطية); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن الأنباري وابن عطية); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف)+]
- 17:87/q02/p2 It can restore revelation or knowledge after God takes it away. — Al-Nahhas allows restored knowledge even while calling the exception detached. Al-Razi, al-Baydawi, and al-Nasafi give restoration as an alternative; al-Nasafi pictures mercy as its guardian. Al-Zamakhshari includes mercy among protectors, and al-Bursevi prefers mercy personified as its advocate. Al-Alusi reports this connected reading and objects that it strains advocate. [7 notes: NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); BURSEVI+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 17:87/q02/p3 It gives revelation or knowledge to the Prophet in the first place. — Yahya ibn Sallam supplies an implied clause about sending the Qur'an. Al-Jishumi calls both descent and preservation mercy. Al-Tabatabai speaks of receiving and retaining the Spirit. Study Quran pairs the revealing and preserving of the Qur'an. [5 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; JISHUMI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 17:87/q02/p4 It is the special divine manifestation that sustains the Prophet's existence, perfection, knowledge, and revelation. — Al-Qashani contrasts the mercy of the Merciful with direct manifestation of the divine essence, which would leave neither the Prophet's self nor revelation; majesty can veil knowledge. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 17:87/q03 What does 'from your Lord' modify?  [مِّن رَّبِّكَ · grammar]
- 17:87/q03/p1 It modifies mercy directly, or an omitted adjective describing mercy. — Al-Samin reports both grammatical attachments without choosing between them. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 17:87/q04 What is God's great favor upon the Prophet?  [إِنَّ فَضْلَهُۥ كَانَ عَلَيْكَ كَبِيرًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause identifies the gift of revelation and its preservation, the Prophet's rank and promised station, the wider gift of knowledge, or an inward or guidance-centered account; several sources combine these.
- 17:87/q04/p1 It includes granting prophethood and the Qur'an, teaching it, and preserving it. — Yahya ibn Sallam names prophethood and revelation; al-Tabari adds the choice of Muhammad for the message and countless revelations. Al-Kashshaf, al-Razi, Abu Hayyan, al-Baydawi, al-Alusi, and Ibn Ashur treat continued preservation as a favor beyond the original gift. Ibn Atiyya adds protection from polytheists; al-Bursevi adds the Seal of the Prophets. Al-Biqai extends the favor to the community. Kur'an Yolu says revelation is transmitted without alteration. Al-Jishumi says divine favor gives what best serves people's religion, not what they wish for. [21 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; THALABI; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 17:87/q04/p2 It is Muhammad's precedence over humanity, final prophethood, and the Praiseworthy Station. — Ibn Abbas is cited by al-Wahidi and al-Tabrisi; al-Wahidi al-Wajiz, al-Razi, and al-Alusi also give this account. Ibn Atiyya mentions prophethood and protection from polytheists; al-Bursevi includes final prophethood. Study Quran points to 17:79 and 33:40 for excellence and being the Seal. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 17:87/q04/p3 It grants scholars accessible knowledge and keeps what they learn in memory. — Al-Razi counts access to knowledge and its preservation as two favors; al-Kashshaf urges scholars to thank God for both. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 17:87/q04/p4 Its eternal basis is necessary existence, followed by revelation and divine teaching. — Al-Qashani orders the gifts as existence, then revelation and teaching. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 17:87/q04/p5 It does what most conduces to people's guidance and acceptance and best serves their religion. — Al-Jishumi contrasts this with doing what people merely wish for. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 17:87/q05 In what sense is the favor great?  [كَبِيرًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Al-Mawardi distinguishes abundance of favor from the greatness of its significance.
- 17:87/q05/p1 The favor is abundant or beyond comparison in extent. — Al-Mawardi gives abundance as one explanation. Al-Biqai stresses its immense scale for the Prophet and community; Elmalili says it exceeds favor given to others. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 17:87/q05/p2 The favor is immense in significance. — Al-Mawardi gives importance, rather than amount, as the other explanation. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 17:87/q06 Who is addressed by the warning and the favor?  [عَلَيْكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular address chiefly cautions the Prophet with wider application or chiefly targets other questioners; al-Alusi rejects the latter.
- 17:87/q06/p1 The Prophet is addressed directly; the warning also extends to others and their knowledge. — Study Quran reports that most commentators extend the singular warning of verse 86 to everyone or to the Prophet and community while taking the last clause of verse 87 as addressed to the Prophet. Ibn Ashur says the Prophet is addressed because his knowledge is greatest and extends the caution to scholars. Al-Kawwashi says the warning concerns others despite its address to the Prophet. Elmalili says the Prophet's grace is singular and enables confident speech. [5 notes: BURSEVI(الكواشي); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(most commentators)]
- 17:87/q06/p2 The warning chiefly threatens other people with loss for asking about withheld matters. — Abu Sahl, as reported by al-Alusi, mentions questions about the spirit and Hour. Al-Alusi rejects this interpretation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو سهل); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 17:87/q07 What do the reports say happens when revelation is lifted?  [إِلَّا رَحْمَةًۭ مِّن رَّبِّكَ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in sequence, what is removed, what remains, who takes it, and where it goes; named transmitters also vary.
- 17:87/q07/p1 The Qur'an disappears from manuscripts and hearts in one night, leaving no verse. — Ibn Masud is cited by Yahya ibn Sallam, al-Baghawi, al-Kashshaf, and al-Alusi; al-Baghawi adds his call to recite before the lifting and that people will recite poetry afterward. Al-Kashshaf records Ibn Masud answering the objection that books, memories, and children's instruction should secure it. Al-Alusi says several transmitters report this and adds al-Bahja's view that manuscripts are emptied first. Al-Biqai says the verse implies the reported end-time possibility. Al-Baghawi also glosses threatened removal as erasure from both media. [6 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(عبد الله بن مسعود); BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن مسعود); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 17:87/q07/p2 The Qur'an returns to its source, buzzing like bees around the Throne because it was recited but not obeyed. — Al-Baghawi attributes this to Abdullah ibn Amr ibn al-As. Al-Alusi gives a version attributed to the Prophet through Ibn Umar, in which it declares it came from God and returns to Him, and mentions a similar transmission by Muhammad ibn Nasr from Abdullah ibn Amr ibn al-As. The named transmitters and exact descriptions vary. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عمر,عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص)]
- 17:87/q07/p3 Trust is lost first, prayer last, and people pray without religion before the Qur'an disappears. — Al-Kashshaf reports Ibn Masud's sequence of lost religious qualities. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 17:87/q07/p4 Islam fades; the Qur'an is lifted in a night, while the elderly retain the declaration of God's oneness. — Al-Alusi cites a report of Hudhayfa authenticated by al-Hakim; it says no verse remains on earth. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(حذيفة)]
- 17:87/q07/p5 After a warning against writing alongside the Qur'an, every Qur'anic letter can vanish overnight while favored people keep the declaration of God's oneness. — Al-Alusi attributes this prophetic warning jointly to Ibn Abbas and Ibn Umar; it specifies paper and hearts. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن عمر)]
- 17:87/q07/p6 The Qur'an and other revealed books are lifted to heaven, leaving no verses or guidance in hearts. — Al-Alusi cites an Abu Hurayra report authenticated by al-Hakim; its scope includes books besides the Qur'an. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة)]
- 17:87/q07/p7 Gabriel takes the Qur'an away. — Al-Alusi cites this transmission from Ibn Abi Hatim. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن أبي حاتم)]

## 17:87/q08 Does the warning predict a real removal, and how does that fit preservation?  [إِلَّا رَحْمَةًۭ مِّن رَّبِّكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the conditional removal is merely hypothetical, predicts end-time events, or promises preservation for a limited span; al-Alusi explicitly reconciles the latter with removal reports.
- 17:87/q08/p1 Removal is a hypothetical threat; mercy in fact prevents it and secures ongoing preservation. — Al-Alusi says the surface sense makes removal hypothetical and avoids any need for a recovery agent; Ibn Ashur calls the exception a qualification and cites 15:9 on guarding the Reminder. Kur'an Yolu says the Prophet theoretically could forget revelation but in practice will not; earlier scriptures' forgetting and alteration show human inability to secure knowledge, while divine favor preserves Islamic revelation across generations. Al-Biqai takes the wording as implying possibility. [8 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 17:87/q08/p2 The Qur'an will later be removed from hearts and copies before the Hour. — Ibn Masud and other reports describe its lifting. Al-Biqai says the verse implies this possibility. Al-Alusi says if the verse predicts removal, those reports agree with it. [15 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(عبد الله بن مسعود); BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص,عبد الله بن مسعود); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن مسعود); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن أبي حاتم,ابن عباس وابن عمر,ابن عمر,ابن مسعود,حذيفة,عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص)]
- 17:87/q08/p3 Preservation lasts until shortly before the Hour, when the reported removal may occur. — Al-Alusi offers this reconciliation if the verse is read as a preservation promise. Kur'an Yolu distinguishes the Prophet's secure reception from people's later voluntary forgetting or straying, for which they remain responsible. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 17:87/q09 What role does the warning and favor play in this passage?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes variously read the passage as a lesson in gratitude, an emphasis on preservation, a reply to a boast, consolation, restraint on questioning, guidance, or confidence; al-Alusi rejects the consolation and question-restraint readings.
- 17:87/q09/p1 The threat teaches gratitude for revealed and preserved knowledge and cautions against conceit. — Ibn Ashur connects it to the Qur'an's preceding description as healing and mercy and extends the caution from the Prophet to scholars. Al-Kashshaf and al-Razi distinguish receiving knowledge from retaining it as two grounds for scholars' gratitude. Al-Tabrisi says the immense favor calls for thanks. Ibn Ashur says the warning states removal explicitly while implying favor, made clear by the next clause. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 17:87/q09/p2 The exception and following clause emphasize the additional favor of preserving the Qur'an after revealing it. — Al-Alusi prefers the disconnected reading because the connected reading would make favor only the first revelation. Ibn Ashur says the favor clause explains why mercy prevents removal. Al-Biqai sees strong emphasis on greatness, and Study Quran cites other verses pairing mercy and bounty. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 17:87/q09/p3 It answers al-Nadr's boast that he could produce something like the Qur'an. — Al-Nasafi connects preservation and favor to that boast. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]
- 17:87/q09/p4 It comforts the Prophet after delayed answers about the spirit, Dhul-Qarnayn, and the People of the Cave. — Al-Tahrir, reported by al-Alusi, says the delay distressed him and the reminder of God's ability to remove all revelation consoled him. Al-Alusi finds this unpersuasive. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(صاحب التحرير); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 17:87/q09/p5 It discourages people from pressing for withheld knowledge such as the spirit or the Hour. — Abu Sahl, reported by al-Alusi, takes it as a threat to others; al-Alusi finds this unpersuasive. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو سهل); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 17:87/q09/p6 God gives what best guides people rather than whatever they wish to receive. — Al-Jishumi says divine favor chooses what most benefits people's religion and acceptance. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 17:87/q09/p7 The Prophet can speak confidently because his Lord's favor to him exceeds that to others. — Elmalili ties this confidence to the incomparable greatness of divine grace. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 17:87/q10 What does the missing advocate imply for the mercy exception?  [إِلَّا رَحْمَةًۭ مِّن رَّبِّكَ · interpretation]
- 17:87/q10/p1 No one but God can protect against loss or obtain the return of withdrawn revelation. — Al-Zamakhshari, as quoted by Abu Hayyan, says God could erase the Qur'an from hearts and manuscripts and leave no guarantor of restoration. Ibn Ashur distinguishes wakil, an advocate seeking a withdrawn favor, from nasir, a defender against punishment in the preceding warning; Kur'an Yolu reports this reading and says no other power can protect, restore, or replace divine knowledge. Elmalili says the Prophet relies on the same Lord who first revealed the Qur'an and now preserves it. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr)]

## 17:87/q11 How does the wording emphasize the great favor?  [إِنَّ فَضْلَهُۥ كَانَ عَلَيْكَ كَبِيرًۭا · rhetoric]
- 17:87/q11/p1 The favor clause explains mercy's prevention of removal and emphasizes the greatness of that favor. — Ibn Ashur says kana and the emphatic particle strengthen the explanation. Al-Biqai says the continued gift of revelation to Prophet and community is strongly emphasized. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
