# 7:205: 29 questions

## 7:205/q01 Who is addressed by “remember your Lord”?  [وَٱذْكُر رَّبَّكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular imperative continues the Prophet’s address or addresses listeners and believers more broadly.
- 7:205/q01/p1 The Prophet is addressed, with guidance extending to the community or all accountable people. — Al-Wāḥidī, Mawardī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ṭabrisī, Rāzī, and Study Quran extend the address generally. Abū Ḥayyān calls the Prophet the apparent addressee and says protection from error makes the community the intended recipients of the final warning. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the community emulates his obligations unless specifically barred. Iṣlāḥī notes the singular can denote a plural audience, comparing 7:199. [16 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; JISHUMI(قيل); MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; TABRISI; WAHIDI-BASIT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 7:205/q01/p2 The Qur’an listener is addressed, including one listening in prayer or at a sermon. — Ibn Zayd connects the command to the preceding order to listen (7:204); Ṭabrisī reports listeners raised supplicatory voices upon hearing Paradise or Hell. Ibn Kathīr objects that this would conflict with attentive listening; Ālūsī calls the congregant reading remote. [7 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); STUDYQURAN; TABRISI(ابن زيد ومجاهد وابن جريج,قيل); ALUSI-FULL-; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير وعبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم)-]
- 7:205/q01/p3 Every person is directly addressed. — Jishumī reports reflection on signs and proofs to know God's attributes; Abū Ḥayyān records it as an alternative. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; JISHUMI(قيل)]

## 7:205/q02 What remembrance is commanded?  [وَٱذْكُر · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the speech wording confines remembrance to prayer or invocation, or encompasses inward reflection and all acts of remembering.
- 7:205/q02/p1 Recitation in prayer, sometimes specifically a follower’s quiet recitation. — Ibn ʿAbbās ties it to prayer and 7:204's account of companions confusing the Prophet by reciting behind him; Qatāda specifies silent recitation behind an imam. Al-Shāfiʿī places a congregant’s recitation after the imam finishes. Jishumī prefers this because the verse specifies speech below loudness; Qatāda’s identification of dawn and afternoon can limit it to those prayers. Wāḥidī extends it across silent and audible prayers, citing 17:110. [34 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL(الشافعي); IBNABIHATIM(الحكم بن عتيبة,زيد بن أسلم,عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم,قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة,قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); MUQATIL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(أحدهما,ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن عباس والأصم)+; ALUSI-FULL-; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير وعبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم)-]
- 7:205/q02/p2 Inward awareness, reflection on God's signs, blessings, attributes, and commands, joined to obedience. — Ṭabarī requires heeding Qur’anic admonitions and limits; Thaʿlabī adds faith in signs and obedience. Māwardī specifies reflection on favors. Rāzī requires understanding uttered words, comparing a meaningless sale formula. Ālūsī reports that bare tongue recitation may earn no reward; Ghazālī says love and intimacy are required. [33 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,جمع); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); JISHUMI(أبو علي,قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Gazzâlî); MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI; THALABI(أهل المعاني,مجاهد وابن جريج); TUSTARI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 7:205/q02/p3 Every form of remembrance, including Qur’an, supplication, praise, glorification, the testimony of unity, and other invocations. — Zayd b. Aslam lists glorification, unity, and praise; Kashshāf and Nasafī explicitly broaden the scope. Ibn ʿĀshūr lists testimony, ḥawqala, tasbīḥ, takbīr and thanks. Kur’an Yolu distinguishes general dhikr from fixed Sufi formulas and cites 13:28 and 24:37. [20 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNABIHATIM(زيد بن أسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; MAWARDI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; TABRISI(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 7:205/q02/p4 Supplication to God. — Ṭabarī explicitly calls it quiet supplication; Ibn Jurayj and others require humble, subdued prayer. Qurtubī invokes Naḥḥās and 7:55. Ibn ʿAbbās in Thaʿlabī distinguishes aloud supplication and inward fear. [14 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد وابن جريج); JISHUMI(أبو علي); MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); TABRISI(الجبائي); THALABI(أهل المعاني,ابن عباس,مجاهد وابن جريج); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد وابن جريج)]
- 7:205/q02/p5 Obedience or memory opposed to forgetting. — Jishumī lists these among alternative interpretations; Ṭabarī, Thaʿlabī and Biqāʿī make remembered commands and practice part of remembrance. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو علي); TAB-FULL; THALABI(أهل المعاني)]

## 7:205/q03 Why does the verse say “your Lord”?  [رَّبَّكَ · rhetoric]
- 7:205/q03/p1 It evokes divine care, nearness, beneficence, and the servant’s lowliness, stirring hope alongside fear. — Rāzī invokes God’s uncountable favors at 14:34 and sees hope foregrounded before awe; he quotes a saying that believers’ fear and hope balance. Abū Ḥayyān says “Lord” honors the servant while fitting humility before a master. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; RAZI-FULL(النبي)]

## 7:205/q04 What is meant by remembering God “within yourself”?  [فِى نَفْسِكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether “within yourself” distinguishes a mental act from quiet tongue speech, or denotes the private setting of remembrance.
- 7:205/q04/p1 Inward remembrance takes place in the heart or inner cognition, distinct from speech. — Mujāhid commands heart remembrance. The theologians infer inward speech; Rāzī rejects reducing it to knowledge, because known concepts cannot be sought anew and absent ones cannot be conceived. Abū Ḥayyān rejects Ibn ʿAṭiyya’s tongue only reading: coordination distinguishes inward from verbal remembrance. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes nafs may mean soul, embodied self, cognition, or private self, comparing 5:116. [21 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(المتكلمون); STUDYQURAN; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI; THALABI(مجاهد وابن جريج); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد وابن جريج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)-]
- 7:205/q04/p2 It means quiet verbal remembrance with tongue movement, audible to oneself. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites “below loud speech” as proof; Bursevī says quiet speech protects sincerity. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats dhikr as verbal, including Qur’an and praise. Abū Ḥayyān disputes the inference. [13 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,الإمام); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; MAWARDI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 7:205/q04/p3 It means remembrance in solitude or privately, as well as in company. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the Bukhārī sacred saying contrasting private remembrance with a gathering; Abū Ḥayyān calls a private state unseen by others the noblest. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الله تعالى); STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭabarī)]

## 7:205/q05 Why is inward remembrance urged?  [فِى نَفْسِكَ · interpretation]
- 7:205/q05/p1 Concealment protects sincerity, prevents display, aids reflection, and increases receptivity. — Jishumī and Jubbaʾī stress distance from ostentation; Kashshāf, Nasafī, Biqāʿī, and Study Quran say quiet favors reflection. Sulamī’s Ḥusayn warns against publicizing even to oneself for recompense. Rāzī calls inward humility most beneficial. Bursevī links quiet speech to an answered prayer. [14 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; SULAMI(الحسين); TABRISI]
- 7:205/q05/p2 The servant realizes God sees and is near, and remembrance transforms inner and outer life. — Tustarī calls true dhikr certainty of divine sight, modesty, and choosing God; he glosses the world-curse report as avoiding sin under that awareness. Study Quran says it can lead to presence and unveiling. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(a ḥadīth); TUSTARI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 7:205/q06 What is promised to one who remembers God privately or publicly?  [فِى نَفْسِكَ · reports]
- 7:205/q06/p1 God reciprocates private remembrance privately and public remembrance in a better, nobler assembly. — Reported from ʿUbayd b. ʿUmayr across several collections; Ibn ʿĀshūr names Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī for the sacred report. Ālūsī gives no source or grading. [7 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(عبيد بن عمير); ALUSI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عبيد بن عمير); IBNABIHATIM(عبيد بن عمير); IBNASHUR-FULL(الله تعالى); STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭabarī); TAB-FULL(عبيد بن عمير)]

## 7:205/q07 What do “humbly and fearfully” require?  [تَضَرُّعًۭا وَخِيفَةًۭ · interpretation]
- 7:205/q07/p1 The rememberer humbles and submits to God while fearing Him or His punishment. — Ṭabarī compares 6:63; Jishumī cites the idiom “the fever humbled me” and links abasement to unmet duties. Bursevī distinguishes humble entreaty from earnest ibtihāl. Ibn ʿĀshūr says humility can connote raised speech, whereas fear evokes soft speech; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ contrasts believers’ worldly humility with unbelievers’ abasement on the promised Day. [35 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; JISHUMI(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; MAWARDI-FULL; MUQATIL; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; TAB-FULL; TABATABAI; TABRISI; THALABI(أهل المعاني,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)]
- 7:205/q07/p2 Humility and awe correspond to spiritual stations and the interplay of divine beauty and majesty. — Rāzī distinguishes beginners’ fear of punishment from knowers’ fear of majesty; unveiled beauty sustains while majesty overwhelms. Qāshānī associates humility with differentiation within unity and fear with the self claiming a share. Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees loving attraction and majestic repulsion; Ālūsī says the degree varies by station. Rāzī reads Wāsiṭī’s “gratitude is shirk” as criticizing the claim that thanks independently repay divine gifts, while humble gratitude shows servitude. [10 notes: ALUSI-FULL; QASHANI; RAZI-FULL(أبو بكر الواسطي); TABATABAI]

## 7:205/q08 How are “humbly and fearfully” parsed?  [تَضَرُّعًۭا وَخِيفَةًۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the accusatives state a manner, the motive of remembrance, or a supplied verbal action.
- 7:205/q08/p1 They are circumstantial accusatives or verbal nouns functioning adverbially: remember humbly and fearfully. — Naḥḥās, Qurtubī, Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn and Bursevī give this construction. Ālūsī also allows an omitted noun, “possessing humility and fear,” and rejects a purpose parse. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; TABRISI]
- 7:205/q08/p2 They are purpose complements: remembrance is motivated by humility and fear. — Abū Ḥayyān connects them to hope of reward and fear of punishment; Samīn explicitly prefers this because those motives cause remembrance. Ālūsī objects. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 7:205/q08/p3 They are semantic verbal nouns supplied from an unmentioned verb. — Samīn reports Abū al-Baqāʾ’s root-independent semantic derivation but calls it remote. Ālūsī rejects a verbal noun supplied from an omitted verb. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-]

## 7:205/q09 What supplication customs accompany the discussion of humble remembrance?  [تَضَرُّعًۭا · reports]
- 7:205/q09/p1 Hands are raised in supplication and wiped over the face afterward. — Bursevī reports ʿUmar’s statement that the Prophet wiped his face after prayer, relates it to the prayer mark in 48:29, and interprets the gesture as returning blessing and uniting outward and inward orientation. Asrār Muḥammadiyya assigns palm positions for requests, distress, and imprecation; Abū Yazīd’s dream favors an uncovered hand. Bursevī compares the petitioner to someone pointing toward a sultan’s treasury and cites provision in heaven at 51:22. [7 notes: BURSEVI(أبو يزيد البسطامي,الأسرار المحمدية,عمر)]
- 7:205/q09/p2 Supplication is an earnest bodily devotion sustained by hope of divine generosity. — Bursevī quotes a gnostic who praises prayer, fasting, and supplication and a poem arguing that God’s teaching the request signals willingness to grant it. Mathnawī urges lamenting invocation and thirst for divine grace. [3 notes: BURSEVI(المثنوي,بعض العارفين)]

## 7:205/q10 What does “khīfatan” mean and how is it formed?  [وَخِيفَةًۭ · meaning]
- 7:205/q10/p1 It means fear; the underlying wāw in khawfah changes to yāʾ after kasra. — Majāz says the wāw drops; Zajjāj, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurtubī and Bursevī describe the vowel-conditioned replacement. Naḥḥās calls khīfah singular and khawf collective of the same sense, with al-Farrāʾ recording khīf. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the noun does not by itself specify a posture. Samarraʾī also allows a manner or inward-state noun by comparison with other paired verbal forms. [11 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; JISHUMI(العامة); MAJAZ; NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج)]

## 7:205/q11 What variant is read for “khīfatan”?  [وَخِيفَةًۭ · readings]
turns on: Whether the word derives from fear or from hiding.
- 7:205/q11/p1 The common reading is khīfatan, a noun of fear. — Jishumī calls it the common reading; Rāzī and others explain the wāw-to-yāʾ formation. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; JISHUMI(العامة); MAJAZ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج)]
- 7:205/q11/p2 Some read khifyatan or khafiyatan, indicating concealment. — Jishumī gives the faʿla pattern from the verb “hide”; Samīn notes reversal of fāʾ and yāʾ. Rāzī distinguishes beginners concealing worship to avoid display from perfected lovers concealing it through protective jealousy, citing a saying that one who knows God has a silent tongue. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; JISHUMI(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج,النبي); SAMIN-DURR(قارئ)]

## 7:205/q12 What fear can accompany remembrance?  [وَخِيفَةًۭ · interpretation]
turns on: The servant's station and the feared object: punishment and outcome, divine majesty, or self-appropriation.
- 7:205/q12/p1 Fear of punishment, failures in duties, an adverse ending, or the prior decree. — Ṭabarī fears punishment for neglecting admonitions and limits. Rāzī lists deficient works and inability to repay blessings, then prior decree, citing the saying that the pen has dried. Bursevī reports Ibn al-Shaykh on the same fears but restricts fear of ending and decree to ordinary believers rather than prophets or perfected saints. [10 notes: BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(النبي); TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 7:205/q12/p2 Awe of divine majesty, heightened by knowledge and spiritual realization. — Rāzī distinguishes this from beginners’ fear of punishment; Ṭabāṭabāʾī links it with majestic names; Ālūsī’s poem describes lowering the gaze through reverence. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 7:205/q12/p3 Fear that the self takes credit or claims a share in remembrance. — Qāshānī locates this fear in the self and its pretensions. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 7:205/q13 How loud should spoken remembrance be?  [وَدُونَ ٱلْجَهْرِ مِنَ ٱلْقَوْلِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether “below loudness” requires audible self-hearing, permits silent inner remembrance, or sets recitation levels for particular prayers.
- 7:205/q13/p1 Quiet yet audible to oneself, at a moderate volume below shouting. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Rāzī require self-hearing; Rāzī links 17:110 and 19:3. Qurtubī calls loudness prohibited. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the middle volume permits differing levels of energy in speech. Bayḍāwī places it above a whisper. Bursevī says the imam needs only enough volume for the congregation; reports of ʿUmar and Abū Bakr being told to lower and slightly raise their voices set a middle course. [45 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,الإمام,قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(أنوار المشارق,الكشف,عمر وأبو بكر); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KASHSHAF-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī); TABATABAI; TABRISI(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)+]
- 7:205/q13/p2 In prayer, quiet prayers are read silently and audible prayers low enough to avoid excessive volume. — Wāḥidī invokes 17:110 for the middle course; Baghawī says the imam should remain audible to those behind. Zayd reports both a ban on dawn audibility and a further report allowing it. [14 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNABIHATIM(زيد بن أسلم,عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; MUQATIL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
- 7:205/q13/p3 Keep remembrance wholly inward, with no audible speech. — Ibn Zayd forbids audibility; Mujāhid commands heart remembrance. Ibn Kathīr separates inward remembrance from quiet speech. Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise distinguishes two modes. [13 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد,قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد وابن جريج); IBNKATHIR-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,مجاهد); TABATABAI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد وابن جريج)]

## 7:205/q14 Why avoid loudness, and when may vocal remembrance be raised?  [وَدُونَ ٱلْجَهْرِ مِنَ ٱلْقَوْلِ · law]
turns on: Whether quiet is an absolute norm or a contextual rule governed by sincerity, disturbance, and devotional benefit.
- 7:205/q14/p1 Avoid shouting because God is near and hearing, and quiet protects humility, sincerity, and reflection. — Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Kathīr, and Biqāʿī cite the Prophet telling loudly supplicating companions that they were not calling one deaf or absent. Study Quran and Ibn Kathīr cite the question about a near or distant Lord answered by 2:186. Abū Ḥayyān compares etiquette before the Prophet in 49:2 and 49:4; Study Quran adds 31:19 and 4:142. Ibn Kathīr says loud recitation provoked polytheists’ insults. [31 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(النبي); BURSEVI(الكشف); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(the Prophet (sws)); KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(the Companions); TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); TABRISI(الجبائي); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد وابن جريج)]
- 7:205/q14/p2 Moderate audibility is permitted or useful when it benefits listeners, focuses the reciter, dispels sleep, or helps a beginner. — Nawawī permits it absent display or disturbance; Anwar al-Mashāriq reports agreement on moderate volume. A Kashshāf commentator and Suhrawardī use vocal repetition to interrupt thoughts. Bursevī notes purified practitioners of loud dhikr have arguments. Ibn ʿAjībah rejects banning communal dhikr, citing later public mosque practice in Medina. [8 notes: ALUSI-FULL(السهروردي); BURSEVI(أنوار المشارق,النووي,شارح الكشاف,عمر وأبو بكر); STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAjībah); STUDYQURAN(some)-]
- 7:205/q14/p3 The verse can be applied to keeping the two testimonies quiet in the call to prayer or to banning loud communal dhikr. — Burhān applies it to the call to prayer. Study Quran reports an attempted prohibition of loud Sufi dhikr, opposed by Ibn ʿAjībah on historical grounds. [2 notes: BURHAN; STUDYQURAN(some); STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAjībah)-]

## 7:205/q15 How is “below loud speech” construed grammatically?  [وَدُونَ ٱلْجَهْرِ مِنَ ٱلْقَوْلِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the coordinated phrase is an implied speaking manner, a direct adverbial coordination, or a second state beside inward remembrance.
- 7:205/q15/p1 It modifies an omitted circumstantial expression: remember while speaking below loudness. — Zamakhsharī and Ālūsī supply a speaking state; Samīn favors this over treating dūn as an inflected coordinated noun. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)+]
- 7:205/q15/p2 It coordinates with “within yourself,” adding quiet tongue speech to inward remembrance. — Abū Ḥayyān prefers this because coordination implies difference; Ālūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguish inward and quiet spoken states. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 7:205/q15/p3 It coordinates directly with the circumstantial accusatives, meaning “humbly and moderately.” — Abū al-Baqāʾ offers this; Samīn calls it weak because dūn normally stays adverbial. Ālūsī records a version needing no ellipsis. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-]
- 7:205/q15/p4 Dūn is a locative adverbial noun governing its genitive complement. — Sībawayh classifies dūn among nouns locating things, contrasting genitive government by particles or ordinary nouns. His related examples discuss case after bāʾ and ʿalā; he rejects treating bifa wqih as an idiom and cites Kaʿb’s “wa-dūna Maʿadd.” [11 notes: SIBAWAYH; SIBAWAYH+]

## 7:205/q16 What time does “ghudū” denote?  [بِٱلْغُدُوِّ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the time word names a stretch of morning or points to the dawn prayer.
- 7:205/q16/p1 The morning or early part of the day. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the first half of daylight; Ibn Kathīr says its beginning. ## 7:205/q23 Who are “the heedless,” and what is forbidden?  [وَلَا تَكُن مِّنَ ٱلْغَٰفِلِينَ · interpretation]

- 7:205/q23/p1 Those who neglect remembrance, recitation, reflection, or the commanded supplication. — Muqātil and Wāḥidī Wajīz specify neglecting prayer recitation; Ṭabarī means ignoring Quranic lessons and divine limits. - 7:205/q23/p2 Those who neglect obedience to God's commands and prohibitions more broadly. — Māwardī attributes this to the majority; Tustarī says heedlessness among the elite is settling into a thing, among ordinary people pride in it. - 7:205/q23/p3 Heedlessness can appear as failure to know Friday, neglect of morning devotion, or neglect of one's religious self. — Bukayr b. al-Akhnas says arriving at Friday without recognizing it counts. - 7:205/q23/p4 On the inward reading, heedlessness is forgetting essential unity or treating oneself as the true rememberer. — Qāshānī urges vigilance when self traits dominate; Ibn Ḥusayn says only God truly remembers God. ## 7:205/q24 Why say “do not be among the heedless”?  [وَلَا تَكُن مِّنَ ٱلْغَٰفِلِينَ · rhetoric]
- 7:205/q24/p1 It reinforces the command and warns against joining a settled class marked by heedlessness. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the group expression is stronger and clearer than simply “do not be heedless.” Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes rooted heedlessness from a momentary lapse; Abū Ḥayyān calls the ban reinforcement. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 7:205/q25 What practices and reports are linked to remembrance amid heedlessness?  [وَلَا تَكُن مِّنَ ٱلْغَٰفِلِينَ · reports]
- 7:205/q25/p1 A person remembering God among the heedless is likened to a fighter defending the two groups. — Durr transmits this Prophetic comparison. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 7:205/q25/p2 Regular devotional practice keeps the soul turned to God and should be maintained for God's right. — Ibn Malik says habit shapes the soul; Bursevī warns not to abandon litanies for extraordinary gifts. A knower says litany’s reward ends with the worldly practice; Bursevī cites a report that mercy grants Paradise and deeds distribute its rewards, and Hafiz’s verse prefers divine intimacy to houris. [5 notes: BURSEVI(ابن ملك,الحافظ,بعض العلماء بالله)]
- 7:205/q25/p3 Remembrance has rewards and divine companionship beyond the garden's ordinary rewards. — Bursevī reports a saying that remembrance exceeds battlefield encounter and a sacred saying, “I am the companion of whoever remembers Me”; the promised intimacy and witnessing exceed Paradise as usually sought. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 7:205/q28 What supporting grammatical and rhetorical examples accompany discussion of the verse?  [* · rhetoric]
- 7:205/q28/p1 Poetic similes show how a later detail can delimit an earlier image precisely. — Jurjānī’s startled crow example conveys swift flight driven by fear or release; his hawk-beak comparison selects only the curved upper stroke of a left-handed jīm, clarified by later letters and a mention of thought.
