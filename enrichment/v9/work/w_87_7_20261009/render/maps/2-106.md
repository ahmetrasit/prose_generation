# 2:106: 31 questions

## 2:106/q01 What does naskh mean in Arabic?  [نَنسَخْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether removal, replacement, transfer, or copying is primary.
- 2:106/q01/p1 Removal or cancellation, with or without a successor. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Rāzī cite wind erasing traces and 22:52; al-Zajjāj and others cite sun removing shade. Al-Rāzī argues transfer is a narrower instance of removal; Ibn ʿĀshūr denies mere disappearance counts. Al-Ālūsī describes removal and re-establishment; al-Rāghib treats the usages as related figurative extensions. Al-Rāzī answers the objection that sun and wind are only metaphorical agents by noting their effective role and Arabic usage. [43 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,العلماء من أهل اللغة والتفسير); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(معترض); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي); IBNASHUR-FULL(الراغب); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); BAGHAWI-FULL; BURHAN; IBNABIHATIM(السدي,مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي,مجاهد عن ابن جريج); JISHUMI(الحسن وأكثر أهل العلم); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); SAMIN-DURR(unspecified); RAZI-FULL+]
- 2:106/q01/p2 Replacement or succession. — Al-Zajjāj's sun and shade image, Ibn Durayd's generalization, and Muqātil's verse replacement support this sense. Al-Qaffāl calls it transfer and transformation. [20 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء وأبو سعيد,ثعلب عن ابن الأعرابي,غيره); JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج,مقاتل وابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(القفال,معترض); TABRISI(ابن دريد); ELMALILI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس، والزجاج); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN]
- 2:106/q01/p3 Copying or transferring an original. — Al-Ṭabarī derives legal usage from copying a book; Ibn ʿAbbās relates the whole Qurʾan to the Preserved Tablet. Al-Wāḥidī discusses 45:29. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this figurative imitation of transfer. Al-Biqāʿī and al-Ḥarālī describe transfer of benefit or trace to a successor. [20 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(العلماء من أهل اللغة والتفسير); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن فارس); RAZI-FULL(عطاء وسعيد بن المسيب,معترض); NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; BURHAN(قيل); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; JISHUMI(عطاء وسعيد بن المسيب)]

## 2:106/q02 How is ننسخ read and explained?  [نَنسَخْ · readings]
turns on: The first nūn's vowel and whether Form IV is synonymous, causative, or means finding a thing abrogated.
- 2:106/q02/p1 The common open-nūn Form I means We abrogate. — Al-Fārisī prefers its clarity; the majority reading is listed by Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nashr, and others. [8 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن عامر); THALABI(ابن عامر); BAGHAWI-FULL(قراءة العامة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); TABRISI(ابن عامر); QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور); NASHR(ابن عامر); FARISI-HUJJA+]
- 2:106/q02/p2 Ibn ʿĀmir's ḍamma-nūn Form IV means finding it abrogated. — Al-Fārisī rejects an unattested synonym and a causative implying every verse is abrogated; Abū Ḥātim calls the reading erroneous but al-Samīn rebukes him. [18 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن عامر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عامر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو علي,ابن عامر); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عامر,مكي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي,ابن عامر); SAMIN-DURR(أبو حاتم,أبو علي,ابن عامر); ALUSI-FULL(طائفة وابن عامر)]
- 2:106/q02/p3 Ibn ʿĀmir's form is synonymous with Form I or makes a verse abrogated. — Al-Ṭabrisī prefers the dialectal synonym; Ibn Khālawayh offers both causative analyses. Al-Zamakhsharī supplies Gabriel as recipient of God's command; Ibn ʿAṭiyya supplies the Prophet. [28 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن عامر); THALABI; JISHUMI(أبو عبيد,ابن عامر); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عامر); RAZI-FULL(ابن عامر); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن عامر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية,بعضهم); QIRAAT-ER; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI(İbn Âmir); TABRISI+]
- 2:106/q02/p4 Form IV means copying or revealing from the Tablet, or delaying revelation. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya proposes these parses; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī object that the consequent pronoun then lacks a proper antecedent. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية,الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:106/q03 What is meant by an āya here?  [ءَايَةٍ · referent]
turns on: Whether āya denotes a Quranic verse, its ruling, an earlier message, or signs generally.
- 2:106/q03/p1 A Qurʾanic verse or its ruling. — Al-Ṭabarī supplies an omitted 'ruling' and compares the omitted love in 2:93; al-Jishumī allows ruling, recitation, or preservation. Most exegetes take the āya as Qurʾanic. Al-Zarkashī explains 'any verse' as accommodating supersession of earlier books, with no later abrogator of Qurʾan. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(بعض المفسرين,جميع المفسرين); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; BURHAN(قيل)]
- 2:106/q03/p2 Earlier-scripture message or law. — Abū Muslim cites Sabbath and earlier prayer directions; Asad reads 2:105 as the contextual clue, and al-Biqāʿī allows earlier laws. ʿAṭāʾ's omitted Qurʾanic material is clarified by Abū Muḥammad as never revealed to Muhammad. [15 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم,جميع المفسرين); BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(many Muslim theologians); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Abū Muslim al-Iṣfahānī); IBNABIHATIM(عطاء); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن أبي حاتم,عطاء)]
- 2:106/q03/p3 Any divine sign, message, event, or miracle. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes a Qurʾanic passage, rule, existent, prophet, or saint; the Study Quran lists miracles and natural signs. Ibn al-Jawzī records āya as a sign, group of letters, or bounded verse. [10 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن قتيبة,الزجاج,الشيباني,بعض المفسرين); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Abū Muslim al-Iṣfahānī)]

## 2:106/q04 What constitutes legal abrogation?  [نَنسَخْ ءَايَةٍ · sciences]
turns on: Whether it is a later proof ending an apparently continuing rule, merely a replacement, or copying.
- 2:106/q04/p1 A later revealed proof ends a previously binding rule that otherwise would continue. — The Qāḍī, Sunni jurists, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr define it this way; stated expiry or a specification is excluded. Abū Hāshim treats legal naskh as analogous to its lexical sense; another view calls it a transferred legal term. [22 notes: JISHUMI(أبو هاشم,القاضي,علي بن عيسى); TABRISI(علي بن عيسى); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(القاضي عبد الوهاب والقاضي أبو بكر); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL(علماء الأصول)]
- 2:106/q04/p2 It changes a ruling to its opposite or another worship. — Al-Ṭabarī lists lawful to forbidden and the reverse; al-Thaʿlabī says it transfers worship for a benefit. Al-Sunnī definitions regard the established ruling itself as removed. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أئمتنا); QURTUBI-FULL(أهل السنة,النحاس); NASAFI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير)]
- 2:106/q04/p3 The Muʿtazilite account ends future equivalents of an earlier rule. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya rejects its premises concerning intention and intrinsic goodness; al-Qurṭubī contrasts it with the Sunni view. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(المعتزلة); QURTUBI-FULL(المعتزلة)]

## 2:106/q05 Which parts of a verse can abrogation remove?  [نَنسَخْ ءَايَةٍ · sciences]
turns on: Whether ruling, recitation, both, or only the legal effect are at issue.
- 2:106/q05/p1 The ruling can end while recitation remains. — Ibn Masʿūd's circle and many later sources report this; examples include 60:11, the changed battle ratio, widow's waiting period, and pre-consultation charity. Abū Ḥayyān allows removal of ruling, wording, or both. Al-Ālūsī gives the recitation and ruling examples and excludes a specified endpoint. Ibn ʿAṭiyya treats recitation and ruling as independently removable, including leaving either side while lifting the other. [43 notes: MUJAHID(أصحاب ابن مسعود); TAB-FULL(أصحاب عبد الله بن مسعود,مجاهد عن أصحاب ابن مسعود); THALABI(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; FARISI-HUJJA(مجاهد); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو العالية ومحمد بن كعب القرظي,مجاهد عن أصحاب ابن مسعود,مجاهد عن ابن أبي نجيح); STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله، وابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأصوليين); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد عن أصحاب ابن مسعود وأبو العالية); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; KASHSHAF-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود); NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; TABATABAI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
- 2:106/q05/p2 Recitation can end while its ruling remains. — ʿUmar's stoning wording is the recurring example; the five-suckling report is another. Some sources distinguish the retained legal force from vanished written text. [10 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(عمر); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(عائشة,عمر); QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(many jurists (IK, R, Ṭ),ʿUmar)]
- 2:106/q05/p3 Both recitation and ruling can end. — Abū Bakr's former recitation on disowning fathers, vanished sūras, and ten sucklings are cited. Al-Tabataba'i accepts the abstract categories but rejects reports of textual alteration as explanations of 2:106. [20 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو بكر الصديق,أبو بكر الصديق وأبي بن كعب,ناس من أهل النظر); THALABI(النبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو أمامة بن سهل بن حنيف,قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو بكر الصديق); TABRISI(أبو بكر); RAZI-FULL(عائشة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو أمامة بن سهل بن حنيف,أبو بكر,أبو عبيد,أبي بن كعب وعائشة); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(ʿĀʾishah); ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأصوليين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي وابن عباس)]
- 2:106/q05/p4 Naskh removes a sign's effect while its underlying existence remains. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says a ruling may cease while the verse's eloquence and inimitability remain; he extends naskh to events and prophetic succession. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī treats reported pairings of worship and mercy, universal arrival at Hell and exemption, Jonah's deliverance, and succession of Imams as broader naskh; he rejects reducing the Hell pairing to simple general-particular wording. [14 notes: TABATABAI(أئمة أهل البيت,أمير المؤمنين عليه السلام,الباقر عليه السلام)]

## 2:106/q06 To what kinds of statement can abrogation apply?  [نَنسَخْ · law]
turns on: Whether reports, doctrine, and immutable moral rules can be abrogated.
- 2:106/q06/p1 Commands and prohibitions can be abrogated, but truthful reports cannot. — Al-Thaʿlabī says a report's repeal would make its informer a liar; jurists extend the distinction to creedal and historical propositions. The Study Quran names universal prohibitions and kindness to parents as stable. Ibn ʿAṭiyya answers an objection from an imperative sounding like a report by reading an implicit 'unless I abrogate it.' [16 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المعترضين); QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير)]
- 2:106/q06/p2 A report containing an implicit legal command may be abrogated. — Al-Qurṭubī gives 16:67 as an example and distinguishes qualification of a general report such as 2:186 by 6:41. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN(الجمهور)]

## 2:106/q07 Did abrogation occur in the Qurʾan?  [نَنسَخْ ءَايَةٍ · sciences]
turns on: How to read the conditional verse and how to treat apparent conflicts and examples.
- 2:106/q07/p1 Yes, some Qurʾanic rulings were superseded. — Majority examples: 2:240/2:234, 8:65/8:66, 58:12/58:13, and qibla change. Al-Rāzī notes this conditional verse alone proves possibility, using 16:101 and 13:39 for occurrence. Al-Zarkashī says the number is small. Al-Ālūsī says the conditional introduces a possibility and most communities acknowledge abrogation, with Jewish exceptions. Al-Zarkashī rejects a definition limited to removal of both text and rule, citing Torah's continued recitation; abrogation clarifies a duration. Kur'an Yolu cites Shawkānī's acceptance of repeal and the changed grave-visiting rule. Al-Rāzī rejects an alleged declaration that a permanent law can later end; an explicit qualification of prior law would have been widely transmitted. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says 2:109 and 4:15 hint at coming replacement. He cites reports from the Prophet's family and a reported ʿAlī interpretation of 51:56 and 11:118. [39 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(جمهور العلماء,جمهور المسلمين); BURHAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو مسلم الأصبهاني); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî); TABATABAI(أمير المؤمنين,النبي والصحابة وأئمة أهل البيت)]
- 2:106/q07/p2 No technical abrogation occurred within Qurʾanic verses. — Abū Muslim assigns the verse to earlier scripture, Tablet copying, or a merely hypothetical condition; Asad cites lack of a reliable Prophetic declaration and inconsistent verse counts. Kur'an Yolu favors continuing application in distinct circumstances absent an explicit final replacement. [28 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم,بعض المسلمين,معترض,منكرو النسخ); ASAD-NOTES(many Muslim theologians); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Abū Muslim al-Iṣfahānī); BURHAN(قيل)]
- 2:106/q07/p3 Earlier dispensations are superseded by Islam. — Abū Muslim and Asad focus the verse on Torah and Gospel; Ibn ʿĀshūr also includes replacement between laws, and al-Biqāʿī describes the Qurʾan superseding earlier burdens. [9 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:106/q07/p4 Context bound rules can recur when their causes recur. — Al-Zarkashī and al-Ḥarālī call this postponement, citing restraint in weakness, fighting in strength, and the temporary meat storage ban; Kur'an Yolu likewise retains both context-specific rules. Al-Ḥarālī compares recurring rules to seasons. [13 notes: BURHAN(الشافعي); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 2:106/q08 Can a rule be abrogated without a replacement?  [نَنسَخْ بِخَيْرٍۢ مِّنْهَآ · law]
turns on: Whether 'bring better or like it' requires a successor ruling.
- 2:106/q08/p1 Yes; lifting a duty may itself be better. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī cite pre-consultation charity or the emigrant women's test; al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī distinguish a promised benefit from the abrogating rule. [11 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:106/q08/p2 No; the verse promises something better or equal. — Some jurists require a substitute; Ibn al-Ḥaṣṣār says extant mutawātir text replaces removed recitation. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(قوم); ITQAN(ابن الحصار); BURHAN(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 2:106/q09 Can a replacement ruling be more burdensome?  [بِخَيْرٍۢ مِّنْهَآ · law]
turns on: Whether better means easier or more beneficial and rewarding.
- 2:106/q09/p1 Yes; greater burden can yield greater reward or benefit. — Al-Ṭabarī cites Ramadan fasting; al-Rāzī lists Ramadan, adultery penalties, and prayer cycles. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites wine's categorical ban, and al-Ālūsī compares changing rules to medicine. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:106/q09/p2 No; the promise requires the successor to be easier or equal. — Some infer this from 'better or equal'; al-Rāzī rejects the inference because benefit is not identical to lightness. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(قوم); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 2:106/q10 Can Sunnah and Qurʾan abrogate one another?  [نَنسَخْ ءَايَةٍ · law]
turns on: Whether the verse's divine speaker and 'better' require a Qurʾanic replacement.
- 2:106/q10/p1 Definitive Sunnah may abrogate Qurʾan, and Qurʾan may abrogate Sunnah. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī cite no bequest to an heir, stoning, qibla, and 60:10; al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ālūsī answer that Sunnah is divinely given. Solitary-report application at Qubāʾ remains disputed. [26 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو المعالي وغيره,حذاق الأئمة); QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي وأبو الفرج المالكي,حذاق الأئمة); RAZI-FULL(جمهور العلماء); TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; BURHAN(ابن حبيب النيسابوري,ابن عطية,قيل); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 2:106/q10/p2 Qurʾan may be abrogated only by Qurʾan. — Al-Shāfiʿī argues the replacement must be same-kind and God's direct speech, and Sunnah is not superior to Qurʾan; the ʿUmar stoning wording can recast that example as Qurʾanic abrogation. [14 notes: RAZI-FULL(الشافعي,عمر); QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي وأبو الفرج المالكي); TABRISI(من زعم أنه لا يجوز نسخ القرآن بالسنة المعلومة); BURHAN(الشافعي,قيل)]
- 2:106/q10/p3 Sunnah abrogation depends on its being revealed, not independent reasoning. — Ibn Ḥabīb al-Nīsābūrī draws this distinction. [1 notes: BURHAN(ابن حبيب النيسابوري)]

## 2:106/q11 How is abrogation distinguished from specification and expiry?  [نَنسَخْ · law]
turns on: Whether a later statement removes a previously included case or clarifies its original scope or duration.
- 2:106/q11/p1 Specification, stated expiry, and recurring causes do not permanently remove a ruling. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says specified cases were never included; al-Rāzī requires a time gap and excludes stated endpoints. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes reconciliation through wording from changing time-bound interests. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; BURHAN]
- 2:106/q11/p2 Adding a condition or description can count as abrogation. — Al-Nasafī treats addition to a text as abrogating its earlier description, contrary to al-Shāfiʿī. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 2:106/q12 When does an abrogating rule take effect and how is it known?  [نَنسَخْ · law]
turns on: Whether notice, performance, or the Prophet's lifetime limits its force.
- 2:106/q12/p1 Before notice, the earlier rule still binds; repeal can precede performance. — Leading scholars cite Qubāʾ for notice and Abraham's sacrifice or fifty prayers for repeal before action. After the Prophet's death there is no new abrogation; explicit statement, chronology, or consensus can reveal an earlier repeal. Al-Nasafī requires ability to form inward commitment, not ability to perform, against the Muʿtazila. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(حذاق الأئمة); QURTUBI-FULL(حذاق الأئمة); NASAFI-FULL]
- 2:106/q12/p2 The later rule binds everyone even before they hear it. — Some theologians hold this view, opposed by leading scholars. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المتكلمين)]

## 2:106/q13 Why must interpreters know abrogation?  [نَنسَخْ · sciences]
- 2:106/q13/p1 It is needed to distinguish binding law from superseded law. — ʿAlī warned or expelled an untrained preacher; al-Thaʿlabī and al-Qurṭubī treat naskh as essential to exegesis and lawful/unlawful judgments. [7 notes: THALABI(علي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,علي); STUDYQURAN]
- 2:106/q13/p2 Detailed legal-theory debates can obscure this verse's exegesis. — Abū Ḥayyān deliberately leaves the technical disputes to legal theory and criticizes al-Rāzī's expansion. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:106/q14 How is ننسها read?  [نُنسِهَا · readings]
turns on: Whether hamza indicates deferral, vowel forms indicate forgetting or leaving, and how the codex variants address the Prophet.
- 2:106/q14/p1 The unhamzated ḍamma-nūn reading is the majority form. — Abū ʿUbayd and Abū Ḥātim prefer it; transmitted variants include direct address, passive and doubled-sīn forms, and codices explicitly supplying 'you'. A dream report is offered for its preference. Al-Samīn also considers reading an unhamzated form as hamza changed to alif. Shujāʿ's dream report has the Prophet approving Abū ʿAmr's readings except this hamzated reading and another; Qāsim ibn Sallām calls Shujāʿ reliable. ## 2:106/q20 What do the pronouns in 'better than it or its like' refer to?  [بِخَيْرٍۢ مِّنْهَآ مِثْلِهَآ · referent]
turns on: Whether the two pronouns divide between abrogated and left verses or refer to the same antecedent.
- 2:106/q20/p1 'Than it' refers to the abrogated verse and 'its like' to the one left. — Al-Ṭabarī distributes the two clauses this way; al-Suddī likewise pairs better with abrogated and like with left. Abū Ḥayyān rejects Ibn ʿAbbās's rearrangement as disturbing Qurʾanic order. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس)-]
- 2:106/q20/p2 Both compare with what has been abrogated. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says when the other verse is left intact, both pronouns refer to the abrogated item; Ibn ʿĀshūr makes each conditional branch eligible for either better or equal. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:106/q20/p3 They refer back to a deferred and unrevealed verse. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya's causative-copying parse makes this antecedent; Abū Ḥayyān rejects its missing answer-link. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية,الشيخ)]

## 2:106/q21 How do ما and من آية work grammatically?  [نَنسَخْ ءَايَةٍ · grammar]
turns on: Conditional object versus verbal noun, and partitive versus specification or extra min.
- 2:106/q21/p1 ما is a fronted conditional object; من آية specifies the verses partitive or by tamyīz. — Al-Ṭabrisī and Abū Ḥayyān explain fronting; al-Samīn prefers partitive min, while al-Ālūsī prefers accusative specification. Singular āya may stand for any verses. [16 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL; BURHAN; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 2:106/q21/p2 من is extra and آية a circumstantial accusative or ما a verbal noun. — Abū al-Baqāʾ offers these analyses; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn reject the circumstantial parse, and the verbal-noun parse lacks a pronoun in the conditional answer. [7 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL; BURHAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعضهم)-; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 2:106/q22 What is the syntax of the conditional verbs and أو?  [نَنسَخْ نُنسِهَا · grammar]
- 2:106/q22/p1 The first verbs are jussive conditions, أو joins alternatives, and نأت is their answer. — Al-Naḥḥās explains dropped yāʾ or hamza vowel in the jussive; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn consider imperfect condition and answer especially apt. Abū ʿAmr is reported to change the hamza of naʾti to alif. Al-Naḥḥās notes lam's jussive force in the final question and the nominal position of anna. [14 notes: AKHFASH; NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو عمرو)]

## 2:106/q23 Is خير comparative or a noun meaning good?  [بِخَيْرٍۢ مِّنْهَآ · grammar]
turns on: Whether من expresses comparison or origin, and whether مثلها can coordinate.
- 2:106/q23/p1 Comparative: more beneficial than the earlier verse. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Samīn favor this; coordination with 'or its like' is natural. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قوم); SAMIN-DURR; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 2:106/q23/p2 A good or benefit from the verse, with من marking origin. — Some compare 2:105 or 27:89, but Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Samīn find coordination of 'its like' strained. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قوم); SAMIN-DURR; STUDYQURAN(Q); IBNATIYYA-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 2:106/q24 What does the opening question do rhetorically?  [أَلَمْ تَعْلَمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Confirmation, admonition, rebuke, or a genuine request for knowledge.
- 2:106/q24/p1 It confirms what the addressee knows about God's power. — Al-Wāḥidī cites Arabic rhetorical usage; al-Baghawī calls it interrogative in form and affirmation in force. Abū Ḥayyān rejects making it need an omitted alternative. Al-Ālūsī says replacing a pronoun with Allah's proper name evokes awe and gathers the divine attributes. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BURHAN(ابن السراج,الزمخشري)]
- 2:106/q24/p2 It admonishes or rebukes denial of abrogation. — Al-Ṭabarī rejects the simple affirmation analysis; al-Zarkashī prefers rebuke directed to a denier, while another explanation treats it as commanding recognition. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; BURHAN; BURHAN+]

## 2:106/q25 Who is addressed by 'do you not know'?  [أَلَمْ تَعْلَمْ · referent]
turns on: Prophet alone, his community through him, any listener, or deniers.
- 2:106/q25/p1 Muhammad is addressed, with his companions or community intended. — Al-Ṭabarī cites 2:104, 2:107, and 2:108 and Arabic singular-to-plural shifts; al-Kumayt and Jamīl's poems illustrate indirect address. Bursawī adds the Prophet's special knowledge. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(جميل بن معمر); MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو علي); TABRISI(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:106/q25/p2 Any accountable listener or especially the deniers of abrogation. — Abū ʿAlī and al-Ālūsī transmit these possibilities; the scope of God's power may be general or specifically support Muhammad against enemies. [5 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي); TABRISI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:106/q26 What does qadīr and 'everything' mean here?  [ٱللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍۢ قَدِيرٌ · theology]
turns on: Whether the scope is absolutely general or understood to exclude impossibilities and God's attributes.
- 2:106/q26/p1 God has power to change rules and provide whatever benefit He promises. — Al-Ṭabarī distinguishes ability from measuring; al-Jishumī calls qadīr intensive, and Ibn ʿAṭiyya says impossibilities and eternal attributes are not 'things' within the scope. The claim also supports divine sovereignty. Abū Ḥayyān says repetition of Allah marks independent clauses; the final denial of protector or helper and mention of power match the promised change. Elmalılı says divine unity reserves legislative repeal to God and grounds the Prophet's knowledge of omnipotence. [26 notes: IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; RAZI-FULL]
- 2:106/q26/p2 The conclusion specifically reassures the Prophet of help against enemies. — Some link God's unrestricted power to support and vengeance as well as legal replacement. [2 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI(قيل)]

## 2:106/q27 Does abrogation imply divine change of mind?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether changed law reflects changed divine knowledge or changed human circumstances.
- 2:106/q27/p1 No; God knew each ruling's duration and changes people's duties wisely. — Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Qurṭubī cite Abraham, Moses, Noah, Adam, and changing interests; al-Nasafī says repeal clarifies the endpoint to humans. Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Biqāʿī appeal to time-dependent benefit. Biqāʿī distinguishes divine repeal from scholars' unauthorized changes to scripture. [40 notes: THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير); BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,اليهود); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 2:106/q27/p2 Yes or it is impossible for divine law to be abrogated. — Jewish objectors equate naskh with badāʾ or late knowledge; some later Muslim groups deny possibility. Abū Muslim allows possibility while disputing Qurʾanic occurrence. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(اليهود); QURTUBI-FULL(طوائف من المنتمين للإسلام المتأخرين,طوائف من اليهود); BIQAI-FULL(اليهود); ALUSI-FULL(اليهود); TABATABAI(المعترض,اليهود)]

## 2:106/q28 Does the verse imply that the Qurʾan is created?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether changes in words and relations reach eternal divine speech itself.
- 2:106/q28/p1 Yes; abrogation and differing merit imply originated speech. — The Muʿtazila reason that succession, removal, and divine power over replacement cannot characterize an eternal thing; al-Ṭabrisī agrees with createdness. [7 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); BAYDAWI-FULL(المعتزلة); ALUSI-FULL(المعتزلة)]
- 2:106/q28/p2 No; change concerns expressions or relations, not eternal inner speech. — Rāzī's side distinguishes words from meaning; al-Bayḍāwī distinguishes relations from essence. Al-Ālūsī challenges Rāzī's formulation because abrogation targets rulings. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(الأصحاب); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام الرازي)]

## 2:106/q29 Why is this verse placed after the preceding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether it answers envy of revelation, objections to changed commands, or the ban on rāʿinā.
- 2:106/q29/p1 It answers criticism of revelation and changed commands. — Polytheists or Jews accused Muhammad of contradictory commands; reports connect this verse with 16:101. Some tie the accusation to the qibla change. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Muslim instruction is primary while the Jewish objection is implicitly refuted. [22 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-ASBAB(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم,علي بن عيسى); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(رُوي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(اليهود); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(اليهود); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(المشركون أو اليهود); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(أبو مسلم)]
- 2:106/q29/p2 It follows 2:105's denial that unbelievers wish believers any good. — ʿAlī ibn ʿĪsā and al-Ṭabrisī take the verse as reassurance that God continues to send what is best; al-Ālūsī adds an answer to the claim that refraining from repeal would be more generous. Abū Ḥayyān notes the switch from third-person mention of God to first-person plural. [6 notes: JISHUMI(علي بن عيسى); TABRISI(علي بن عيسى); ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:106/q29/p3 It exemplifies the immediately preceding ban on rāʿinā. — Al-Biqāʿī takes that newly forbidden expression as a recent legal change; al-Ālūsī transmits the link. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:106/q29/p4 It frames later changes in the sūra's detailed rulings. — Al-Ḥarālī sees a programmatic introduction, and al-Kashshāf links 2:107's dominion and the warning against Israelite demands to trust in God's prescription. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 2:106/q30 What inward lesson is drawn from changing states?  [* · inward]
- 2:106/q30/p1 Successive changes raise a person to intimacy and direct address. — Al-Sulamī invokes 53:8–10. [1 notes: SULAMI]

## 2:106/q31 What additional textual and methodological context do the notes give?  [* · sciences]
turns on: How the surrounding source material classifies suras or transmits recitation.
- 2:106/q31/p1 Sura groupings and recitation chains contextualize Qurʾanic study. — Ibn Qutayba and al-Suyūṭī classify the miʾūn, mathānī and mufassal with competing starting points; al-Baghawī records reader chains and accepts their agreed readings. Ibn al-Jinnī discusses unrelated phonetic examples; al-Nashr details recitation connection and another reading. [42 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(ابن أبي إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عمرو,ابن عامر,ابن كثير,الأعرج,الكسائي,حمزة,عاصم,يعقوب); NASHR(ابن الباذش,ابن عامر); ITQAN(أوس,ابن أبي الصيف اليمني,ابن السيد,ابن الفركاح,ابن عباس,الأكثرون,الحاكم,الخطابي,الراغب,الفراء,القاضي عياض,الكمال الذماري,النكزاوي,النووي,جماعة,جمال القراء,سعيد بن جبير); BURHAN]
- 2:106/q31/p2 An illustrative interpretation need not limit a verse's meaning. — Al-Zarkashī distinguishes an example from restriction and records varying counts and classifications of abrogation. [2 notes: BURHAN(قيل)]
- 2:106/q31/p3 Only the Prophet's judgment is wholly authoritative. — Ibn Kathīr uses ʿUmar's dispute with Ubayy to note that respected reciters can err; Mālik's maxim accepts or rejects everyone's view except the Prophet's. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عمر,مالك)]
