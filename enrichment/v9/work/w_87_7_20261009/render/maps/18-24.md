# 18:24: 18 questions

## 18:24/q01 What is meant by an “exception” in this usage?  [إِلَّآ · meaning]
- 18:24/q01/p1 It removes an implication of an earlier utterance and can qualify speech by God's will or another condition. — Al-Sīrāfī says Arabic uses istithnāʾ for conditional qualification. Al-Rāghib defines removal of what general or particular speech entails, citing 6:145 and conditional divorce. Al-Ālūsī cites the Prophetic description of “if God wills” after an oath as an exception. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الراغب,السيرافي)]

## 18:24/q02 How does “unless God wills” attach to the preceding prohibition?  [إِلَّآ أَن يَشَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · grammar]
turns on: The scope of the exception and whether an omitted saying, permission, time or condition is supplied.
- 18:24/q02/p1 It directs the speaker to add “if God wills”; a verb of saying or equivalent circumstance is understood. — Al-Akhfash says a long utterance allows one part to indicate the whole. Al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and others supply saying; al-Naḥḥās calls it a disconnected accusative. Al-Samīn reports omitted qawl, waqt and bāʾ analyses; al-Kashshāf and al-Ālūsī attach the condition to the prohibition. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the previous verse's causal lām concerns an intended act, and “tomorrow” figuratively covers the future; Zuhayr's today/yesterday/tomorrow verse supports this. [25 notes: FARRA; AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(الأخفش); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الأخفش); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,ابن عطية,الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); IBNASHUR-FULL(الكسائي والأخفش والفراء); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 18:24/q02/p2 It means do not make the statement unless God permits or wills its utterance. — Al-Kashshāf gives this parse; Abū al-Baqāʾ and al-Ālūsī describe a time of divine permission. Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya reject this apparent prohibition-scope reading, whereas al-Ālūsī defends it. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري); TAB-FULL-; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري)-; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية)-]
- 18:24/q02/p3 It is an emphatic “never” or an indefinitely postponed exception under an unknowable divine time. — Al-Kashshāf compares 7:89; al-Ālūsī says it need not imply that the speech is intrinsically bad. Ibn ʿAṭiyya objects. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية)-]
- 18:24/q02/p4 The exception qualifies the promised action: speak only of acts God wills. — Al-Ṭabrisī attributes a surface reading to al-Farrāʾ and explains that the resulting restriction to obedience does not ban reports about permissible acts. Al-Kashshāf and al-Ālūsī object that whether the action happens is not the prohibition's target. [4 notes: TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); TABRISI(الفراء)+; KASHSHAF-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)-; ALUSI-FULL(الخفاجي)-]

## 18:24/q03 Why should a future act be qualified by God's will?  [أَن يَشَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · interpretation]
- 18:24/q03/p1 Fulfillment depends on God, and illness, death or another obstacle may make an unqualified promise false. — Al-Ṭabarī says nothing happens apart from God's will. Al-Māwardī and Ibn al-Jawzī cite Moses' qualified promise at 18:70; al-Jubbāʾī lists illness, incapacity and changed intention. Ibn Kathīr stresses God's knowledge of unrealized possibilities. Islahi says wisdom may delay an answer; 31:34 and 18:69, 28:27, 37:102 are cited. [32 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; IBNHISHAM; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس,الأخفش,الجبائي,السيد المرتضى); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(القاضي); NASAFI-FULL(الزجاج); QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(الإمام); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 18:24/q03/p2 The rule is counsel in prophetic courtesy; forgetting it can be an omission of what is better rather than sin. — Al-Ṭabrisī invokes consensus that omission for permitted acts is not sinful; Rāzī defends the Prophet on this ground; Ibn ʿĀshūr treats the delayed rebuke as gentle etiquette and cites counsel to Ḥakīm ibn Ḥizām. [8 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(القاضي); IBNASHUR-FULL(حكيم بن حزام); ASAD-NOTES; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 18:24/q04 What theological account of divine will is argued from the exception?  [أَن يَشَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · theology]
turns on: Whether God wills disobedience and how the qualification operates for permissible and commanded acts.
- 18:24/q04/p1 Everything God wills occurs, including faith and unbelief; His command and will may differ. — Rāzī argues a qualification excuses an unfulfilled promise only if divine will can prevail over a servant's. A debtor can fail to pay without breaking a qualified oath although repayment was commanded. Bursawī says frustrated independent intentions reveal divine control. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; QASHANI]
- 18:24/q04/p2 God wills obedience but servants can will sin; qualification can instead suspend legal force or seek facilitation. — Rāzī reports the Muʿtazilite view. Al-Murtaḍā distinguishes suspension in reports, contracts and oaths; a request for ability, continuation or divine grace; and simple acknowledgment of dependence. These senses answer the debt example. Al-Ālūsī rejects both the objection and reply as unpersuasive. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); TABRISI(السيد المرتضى); ALUSI-FULL(الاعتراض على المعتزلة,المرتضى); RAZI-FULL-]
- 18:24/q04/p3 A future act may be called a “thing” in anticipation, without implying nonexistent things already exist. — Rāzī answers the contrary argument by comparison with 16:1. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القائلون بأن المعدوم شيء)]

## 18:24/q05 When can a later “if God wills” affect an oath?  [إِلَّآ أَن يَشَآءَ ٱللَّهُ إِذَا نَسِيتَ · law]
turns on: The time allowed for a legally effective exception, and whether late remembrance merely repairs neglect.
- 18:24/q05/p1 A forgotten qualification may be added even after a year and, on some reports, can avert breach. — Ibn ʿAbbās is reported with a year or unlimited delay; Mujāhid with two years and Abū al-ʿĀliya indefinitely. Ibn ʿAbbās argues the verse gives no time limit. Some jurists infer cancellation of expiation; al-Wāḥidī rejects application to oaths. The one-year limit is attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās; reports of day, week and month occur under Saʿīd ibn Jubayr. - 18:24/q05/p2 Only a qualification joined to the oath prevents breach or expiation. — Al-Ḥasan, Mālik, al-Shāfiʿī and most jurists require continuity. Al-Wāḥidī reasons that a completed oath is binding; Rāzī cites 5:1 and 17:34. Abū Ḥanīfa says a late qualification would destabilize allegiance, and others cite acknowledgments, divorce and emancipation. A Shāfiʿī nuance requires intent at the outset; Rāzī rejects a weaker public-doubt argument because even a whispered exception counts. - 18:24/q05/p3 A late qualification may be spoken even after breach, but removes blame or renews devotion without changing the oath's legal effect. — Al-Ṭabarī distinguishes relief from omission's sin from expiation; al-Wāḥidī applies delayed reports to promises rather than oaths. Ibn Kathīr calls this correct. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ālūsī say completed speech cannot be amended retroactively. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls thunyā an exception and leaves whether it alters a binding oath to jurisprudence. [20 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزهراوي,الطبري); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير الطبري); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(القرطبي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Z); TABATABAI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 18:24/q05/p4 It counts while the speaker remains in the same sitting. — Al-Ḥasan and Ṭāwūs give this limit; a report from al-Ḥasan distinguishes intentional omission from forgetfulness. A similar view is reported from Aḥmad. [15 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن وعطاء); WAHIDI-BASIT(طاووس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن,طاوس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(طاوس والحسن); TABRISI(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وطاووس); RAZI-FULL(طاوس); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); NASAFI-FULL(الحسن); DURR-FULL(طاوس); ALUSI-FULL(طاوس); STUDYQURAN(JJ, Z)]
- 18:24/q05/p5 It counts before conversation breaks off, during continued engagement, or within a short interval. — The limits are attributed to unnamed jurists, Ibn Ḥanbal and Ibn Rāhawayh. ʿAṭāʾ specifies the time needed to milk a plentiful camel; Qatāda permits anticipation of the oath. [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(عطاء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن حنبل وابن راهويه,عطاء,قتادة); RAZI-FULL(عطاء); DURR-FULL(عطاء); ALUSI-FULL(عطاء)]
- 18:24/q05/p6 The late-qualification dispensation belongs only to the Prophet; others must attach it to an oath. — Al-Ṭabarānī identifies the route through al-Walīd ibn Muslim and ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz ibn al-Ḥusayn as unique; Ibn ʿAbbās is also cited. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(الطبراني عن ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 18:24/q05/p7 It may be supplied whenever one remembers, with no fixed deadline. — Abū al-ʿĀliya and ʿAmr ibn Dīnār are cited for an unrestricted remembrance rule. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العالية,عمرو بن دينار); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(أبو العالية)]
- 18:24/q05/p8 It may be added after two years. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Mujāhid's two-year limit; al-Qurṭubī also records a two-year Ibn ʿAbbās report, treating its benefit as devotional. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 18:24/q05/p9 A month is permitted for a forgotten exception. — Ibn Jubayr is reported with a month limit, distinct from reports allowing a day, week, month or year. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جبير)]
- 18:24/q05/p10 Ibn Jubayr permits a forgotten exception within four months. — Ibn Jubayr appears with month and four-month limits; ʿAlī's report gives forty days and links it to the Prophet's delayed answer. These are separate reported lengths. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جبير)]
- 18:24/q05/p11 A report allows the exception for forty days. — ʿAlī connects this limit with the Prophet's delayed answer after omitting the formula. [1 notes: TABATABAI(علي بن أبي طالب عليه السلام)]

## 18:24/q06 What effect does a connected qualification have, and which commitments can it qualify?  [إِلَّآ أَن يَشَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · law]
turns on: Whether the condition affects only oaths or also divorce, manumission and vows; whether verbal and inward qualifications count.
- 18:24/q06/p1 A connected exception lets the oath-taker act or refrain without breach or expiation. — Prophetic reports through Ibn ʿUmar and Abū Hurayra establish the option. Solomon's vow to visit wives illustrates the rule; the reported number varies among seventy, ninety and one hundred, and only one woman bore half a child after he omitted the exception. [14 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن عباس,رسول الله); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو هريرة عن النبي,ابن عمر عن النبي); JISHUMI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي); RAZI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن عمر); BURSEVI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL(البخاري)]
- 18:24/q06/p2 After an unqualified oath, one should take a better course and expiate. — Al-Ḥasan and Prophetic reports say so; Ibn ʿAṭiyya uses this against erasing expiation by a late phrase. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(النبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري)]
- 18:24/q06/p3 A legal exception must be spoken; inward reservation alone is insufficient. — Yaḥyā endorses Ibrāhīm; Ḥammād requires hearing oneself; al-Ḥasan accepts moving the tongue. Ibn ʿAṭiyya requires verbal speech. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(إبراهيم); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن,حماد); IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 18:24/q06/p4 Qualification can also suspend divorce, emancipation and other commitments. — Abū Ḥanīfa, al-Shāfiʿī, Ṭāwūs and Ḥammād are cited for broader application; Rāzī offers a circularity argument concerning conditional divorce. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(مالك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو حنيفة والشافعي); RAZI-FULL(الفقهاء)]
- 18:24/q06/p5 Qualification affects oaths by God and future expiatory vows, while divorce and manumission wording can still take effect. — Mālik and Aḥmad distinguish operative formulas from future oath acts; Ibn al-Jawzī includes ẓihār and vows. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(مالك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أحمد ومالك)]
- 18:24/q06/p6 The soul must share the exception; a merely verbal formula lacks genuine effect. — Bursawī cites a hadith and Mawlānā's couplet on inward submission. [2 notes: BURSEVI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,مولانا)]
- 18:24/q06/p7 A knowingly false oath is not saved by a verbal exception. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives this limit. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 18:24/q07 What happened when Solomon omitted “if God wills” from his oath?  [إِلَّآ أَن يَشَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · reports]
turns on: The transmitted number of wives differs; the omitted qualification and outcome agree.
- 18:24/q07/p1 He intended to visit one hundred wives, but only one bore half a child. — Bursawī and Ibn ʿĀshūr give one hundred; the intended children were to fight for God's cause, and the oath would not have been broken with the exception. [4 notes: BURSEVI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL(البخاري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي)]
- 18:24/q07/p2 He intended to visit ninety wives, but only one bore a half-formed child. — Al-Durr gives ninety; Ibn Kathīr lists it as a narration. Each wife was expected to bear a fighter for God's cause. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي)]
- 18:24/q07/p3 He intended to visit seventy wives, but only one bore half a child. — Ibn Kathīr transmits this number alongside ninety and one hundred; the angel urged the exception, and the Prophet says it would have fulfilled the wish without breach. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي)]

## 18:24/q08 What does “remember your Lord when you forget” require?  [وَٱذْكُر رَّبَّكَ إِذَا نَسِيتَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the command continues the preceding exception instruction or begins an independent topic.
- 18:24/q08/p1 On remembering an omitted “if God wills,” say it or restore awareness that God controls the act. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī allows appending it before finishing, repeating the statement or later acknowledging God's authority. Al-Ālūsī supplies the omitted genitive “His will” and says recovery is after forgetfulness, whether soon or late. Ibn Kathīr cites two al-Ṭabarānī routes; Rāzī says this reading preserves continuity. Al-Ālūsī rejects an analysis that supplies a recurrent future statement as remote from the apparent sense. - 18:24/q08/p7 The verse begins a general supplication for the Prophet and community. — Al-Qurṭubī and the majority say this is not a special oath formula; Ibn ʿAṭiyya extends it to common human need. Abū Ḥayyān stresses that the verse is about a promise, not an oath. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(كثير من المفسرين); JISHUMI(أبو علي وجماعة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور)+; RAZI-FULL-]
- 18:24/q08/p8 Praise God when a forgotten matter returns to mind. — Al-Jishumī records this additional explanation. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 18:24/q09 Why does the prayer's wording differ from Moses' prayer in 28:22?  [يَهْدِيَنِ رَبِّى · rhetoric]
- 18:24/q09/p1 The shortened yāʾ and verb-first order fit this brief request for direction; Moses' fearful flight foregrounds his Lord and a fuller form. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares the fuller Moses narrative and first-person pronouns, with twelve guidance mentions in al-Qaṣaṣ against five in al-Kahf. Al-Burhān distinguishes spiritual guidance here from Moses' physical route to Midian. [6 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR; BURHAN(أبو العباس)]

## 18:24/q10 Is the following prayer a required formula after a forgotten exception?  [وَٱذْكُر رَّبَّكَ إِذَا نَسِيتَ وَقُلْ عَسَىٰٓ أَن يَهْدِيَنِ رَبِّى لِأَقْرَبَ مِنْ هَٰذَا رَشَدًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: A special expiation formula or a general supplication.
- 18:24/q10/p1 The prayer for nearer guidance is said as repentance or expiation for forgetting the exception. — Muḥammad al-Kūfī is cited by al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī; one account calls it required. Al-Wāḥidī says it asks not to forget again. Al-Ālūsī says the report has no evidence. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(محمد رجل من أهل الكوفة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(بعضهم); JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(محمد الكوفي المفسر); QURTUBI-FULL(محمد الكوفي المفسر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(محمد الكوفي المفسر); DURR-FULL(أبو الحارث); STUDYQURAN(Z); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور)-; QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور)-; ALUSI-FULL(رجل من أهل الكوفة)-; TABATABAI-]
- 18:24/q10/p2 The prayer is a general appeal for guidance after remembrance. — Most interpreters do not restrict it to repair of the omitted exception; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees progression toward constant remembrance. [4 notes: TABATABAI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور)+; QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور)+; TABATABAI+]

## 18:24/q11 How is the final yāʾ of “yahdiyanī” read?  [يَهْدِيَنِ · readings]
turns on: The reciters differ in connected and paused recitation.
- 18:24/q11/p1 Nāfiʿ and Abū ʿAmr retain it in connection but drop it at pause. — Ibn Mujāhid records their connected form; Ibn al-Jawzī specifies the pause. Qiraat-ER notes a nūn sākina in pause. Al-Nasafī compares Meccan, Abū ʿAmr and Medinan handling of analogous first-person forms. [4 notes: IBNMUJAHID; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(نافع وأبو عمرو); QIRAAT-ER; NASAFI-FULL]
- 18:24/q11/p2 Ibn Kathīr retains yāʾ in connection and pause. — Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī calls this the original analogical form and compares 18:39, 18:40, 18:64 and 18:66. [3 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير); QIRAAT-ER]
- 18:24/q11/p3 Ibn ʿĀmir, ʿĀṣim, Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī omit it in connection and pause. — Al-Fārisī compares omission even in non-rhyme forms, citing 11:105. [3 notes: IBNMUJAHID; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن عامر وعاصم وحمزة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عامر وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي)]

## 18:24/q12 To what does “this” refer, and what nearer guidance is requested?  [لِأَقْرَبَ مِنْ هَٰذَا رَشَدًۭا · referent]
turns on: Whether “this” is the Cave story, the promised answer, a forgotten matter, or present remembrance.
- 18:24/q12/p1 A clearer proof of prophethood than the Cave youths' story. — Al-Zajjāj and many followers say God subsequently disclosed unseen histories of messengers and future events. The defiant questioners made the Cave story a test; Bursawī and al-Ālūsī say the wording reduces its apparent wonder, as does 18:9. Al-Biqāʿī prefers broad signs of oneness and resurrection. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects this fit with context. [21 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الأصم); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج); TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج,الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(سعدي المفتي); ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج); STUDYQURAN(Bḍ, JJ, Z); TABATABAI-]
- 18:24/q12/p2 Something better than a forgotten matter, whether restored memory or a more beneficial substitute. — Al-Jubbāʾī and Abū ʿAlī give this alternative; al-Kashshāf and Abū Ḥayyān cite 2:106 for beneficial forgetting. Abū Ḥayyān prefers this antecedent; al-Ālūsī finds its syntactic link close but other aspects remote. [12 notes: THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(بعضهم); JISHUMI(أبو علي,قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(السمرقندي); ALUSI-FULL(الجبائي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 18:24/q12/p3 The answer he promised, or a better and earlier answer before the promised deadline. — Muqātil and Ibn al-Anbārī take the hope as accelerated revelation; al-Ṭabarī and others seek guidance beyond the future statement. Al-Ālūsī says the early-deadline sense is not derivable from the wording. [9 notes: MUQATIL; IBNHISHAM; TAB-FULL; THALABI; JISHUMI(قيل); RAZI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الأنباري); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL(ابن الأنباري)-]
- 18:24/q12/p4 Continued, more direct right guidance in future affairs, extending to the community. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers the broad sense; al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī describe steadiness. Elmalılı compares the Cave youths' success; Qāshānī gives an inward account of steadfast witness. [6 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; ELMALILI; QASHANI; RAZI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 18:24/q12/p5 Constant remembrance and awareness of divine control, beyond merely remembering after a lapse. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues repeated recollection builds uninterrupted remembrance and cites 7:205. [2 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 18:24/q12/p6 A better course in revealed law beyond answering the challengers' extra questions. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Prophet should seek more important guidance after the sleepers' answer; the prayer may continue the warning against disputing their number at 18:22. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:24/q12/p7 Guidance more beneficial than the preceding commands and prohibitions. — Al-Ālūsī reports this link and calls it strained. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 18:24/q12/p8 A more prompt answer to the questioners. — Islahi says the Prophet can tell them God may guide him before the promised time. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 18:24/q13 What does “aqrab” mean and how is it formed?  [لِأَقْرَبَ · meaning]
- 18:24/q13/p1 It can mean more correct or sounder. — Al-Dāmghānī gives this lexical sense; al-Biqāʿī derives the comparative from intransitive qaruba, distinct from the transitive root used at 17:32 and 17:34. [2 notes: DAMGHANI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 18:24/q14 What does “rushdan” mean and how does it function?  [رَشَدًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Its lexical sense and whether it specifies the comparative or acts as an absolute object.
- 18:24/q14/p1 It denotes correctness and specifies the nearer relation or describes the kind of guidance sought. — Al-Dāmghānī gives the lexical sense; Ibn ʿĀshūr gives the two grammatical analyses. [2 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:24/q15 How is “rashadan” read here, and why?  [رَشَدًۭا · readings]
- 18:24/q15/p1 All readers open both rāʾ and shīn here and at 18:10. — Ibn al-Jazarī suggests rhyme with endings such as ʿajaban, ʿadadan and aḥadan. [2 notes: NASHR(القراء)]

## 18:24/q16 What inward meaning is given to remembrance and guidance?  [وَٱذْكُر رَّبَّكَ إِذَا نَسِيتَ لِأَقْرَبَ مِنْ هَٰذَا رَشَدًۭا · inward]
turns on: Distinct descriptions of spiritual forgetfulness, annihilation and God's prior remembrance.
- 18:24/q16/p1 Remember God after forgetting self and creation, so nothing besides Him seems independently real. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ, Jaʿfar, Dhū al-Nūn and others describe forgetting everything else; al-Qushayrī adds forgetting one's share. Al-Ālūsī says pure remembrance follows forgetting the universe. [6 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,جعفر); THALABI(أهل الإشارة); QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 18:24/q16/p2 True remembrance transcends awareness of remembering through annihilation in the Remembered. — Al-Junayd and al-Wāsiṭī stress witness rather than self-observation; al-Qushayrī says noticing one's own remembrance is a flaw. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ invokes remembrance after separation. [6 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الجنيد,الواسطي); QUSHAYRI; ALUSI-FULL(الجنيد)]
- 18:24/q16/p3 Heedlessness arises with the ego; nearer guidance is steadfast witness free of changing attributes. — Qāshānī gives this sequence. Bursawī says remembrance and forgetfulness are heart states; al-Qushayrī says the youths lost track of time while God counted their breaths, citing 72:28. [4 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI; QUSHAYRI]
- 18:24/q16/p4 A higher station is God's prior remembrance of the servant, or constant presence beyond human forgetfulness. — Al-Junayd describes God's remembrance stripping the servant's attributes. Al-Shiblī says realized people do not forget the Real; Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says overwhelming presence can appear as forgetfulness. [4 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الجنيد,الشبلي); ALUSI-FULL(الجنيد)]
- 18:24/q16/p5 Remember God by repenting after sin. — Al-Sulamī transmits this spiritual gloss; it parallels a reading attributed to ʿIkrima. [2 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); JISHUMI(عكرمة)]

## 18:24/q17 What occasion prompted the instruction?  [* · reports]
turns on: Reports differ on the duration of withheld revelation and whether the promise was an oath.
- 18:24/q17/p1 After the Prophet promised answers to the Cave youths and other questions for the next day without “if God wills,” revelation was withheld for fifteen days. — Reports variously name the Spirit, Dhū al-Qarnayn or al-Khiḍr among the questions, with Quraysh prompted by Jews. The wait distressed him and caused hostile rumors; Gabriel explained the omission. Ibn ʿĀshūr also notes a three-day report. [27 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNHISHAM; TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(جبريل); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن إسحاق والطبري والواحدي); ASAD-NOTES(almost all of the commentators); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL(القاضي)-]
- 18:24/q17/p2 The delay was forty days or forty nights after an unqualified promise or oath. — Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān report forty as an alternative; Ibn ʿAbbās says the verse came forty nights after an oath; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmits a forty-day report. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 18:24/q17/p3 An unqualified promise occasioned delayed revelation, with no duration fixed. — Muqātil expects revelation on the Cave companions; al-Ṭabrisī says the delay troubled the Prophet. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees subtle reproach. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TABRISI; ASAD-NOTES(almost all of the commentators); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 18:24/q17/p4 Another report says revelation was delayed three days. — Ibn ʿĀshūr records this alongside fifteen days. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن إسحاق والطبري والواحدي)]

## 18:24/q18 Which reports or links accompany the verse?  [* · links]
- 18:24/q18/p1 The Cave answer is linked to the sura's wider correction of the Prophet's concerns and to fuller guidance. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links the prayer to 18:22 and more important revealed law; Bursawī notes the diminished stress on the Cave story at 18:9. The Study Quran links the delayed answer to 18:6. An alternative syntactic link makes the prayer follow the command to recall the youths entering the cave at 18:10. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI(سعدي المفتي); STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأفاضل)]
- 18:24/q18/p2 The verse is cited with passages on remembrance, abrogation and lasting good deeds. — Saʿd invokes 87:6 and this verse to argue that the Prophet was protected from forgetting. Al-Burhān discusses prayer, expiation and lasting good deeds. [2 notes: TABATABAI(سعد بن أبي وقاص); BURHAN]
- 18:24/q18/p3 A long Dajjāl report accompanies the opening passages of al-Kahf. — Al-Durr says every prophet warned of him; he emerges between Iraq and Syria, claims prophethood, bears kāfir between his eyes, displays misleading fire and paradise, kills and revives one person, and has days of unequal length. Believers recite the sura's opening and seek God's help. [6 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو أمامة,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 18:24/q18/p4 Reports add details of the Jewish questioners, Gabriel's delay and reaction to revelation. — Al-Durr reports Quraysh's claim about al-Raḥmān, Jewish recognition of the expected prophet, the Prophet's lack of prior knowledge and a puppy preventing Gabriel's entrance. Quraysh later called Torah and Quran collaborating sorceries, citing 28:48. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,مجاهد)]
- 18:24/q18/p5 The donkey-buyer anecdote illustrates reliance on God in future plans. — Bursawī recounts a man who refused to say the phrase, lost his money, then repeated the phrase while recounting the loss. [1 notes: BURSEVI(روضة الخطيب)]
- 18:24/q18/p6 A Baghdad anecdote challenges a year-late exception by citing Job's oath. — A traveler heard a vegetable seller argue that Job would have been told to except rather than use the remedy in 38:44 if Ibn ʿAbbās were right. Al-Ālūsī doubts the anecdote and calls the objection excessive. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
