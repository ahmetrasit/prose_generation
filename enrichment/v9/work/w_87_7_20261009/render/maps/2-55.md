# 2:55: 37 questions

## 2:55/q01 How does this recollection connect with the surrounding account?  [وَإِذْ قُلْتُمْ · links]
- 2:55/q01/p1 It continues the dialogue with Israel and recalls another favor after the calf episode. — Abu Hayyan links it to Moses' speech in 2:54; Razi calls the death and revival a sixth favor permitting repentance and reward. Kur'an Yolu says this passage condenses the longer history in 7:141–162 while adding these details. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:55/q01/p2 It is a warning and consolation through the ancestors' repeated resistance. — Tabari compares their earlier rebellions (5:24; 7:161); Wahidi, Jishumi, Tabarsi, Razi and Biqai apply the pattern to Jews resisting Muhammad, and Razi says it consoles him, explains why signs are withheld, and why scriptural learning need not lead to belief. Razi adds that Muhammad's accurate unstudied narration proves revelation; Zajjaj and Biqai connect resurrection to disputants who denied it. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 2:55/q02 Who said this to Moses, and why are later Israelites addressed?  [قُلْتُمْ · referent]
turns on: The competing identifications give different numbers and scope to the speaking group; later collective address may reflect ancestral or communal responsibility.
- 2:55/q02/p1 Moses' seventy selected companions spoke; the later community is addressed through its ancestors. — Ibn Masud, Ibn Abbas, Qatada, al-Rabi and many commentators identify the seventy; Ibn Kathir says this is the dominant contextual account. Jishumi says the addressees followed and approved their ancestors; Elmalılı grounds collective attribution in communal responsibility. Abu Hayyan rejects a claim of unanimity because other identifications exist. [29 notes: TAB-FULL(الربيع بن أنس,السدي,محمد بن إسحاق); IBNABIHATIM(الربيع بن أنس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود وابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(السدي,محمد بن إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن مسعود وقتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس عن أبي جعفر); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; DURR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); STUDYQURAN(IK, R); IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 2:55/q02/p2 All Israelites except those whom God protected spoke. — Ibn Zayd's report is repeated by Ibn al-Jawzi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi; it differs from identification with the selected seventy. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 2:55/q02/p3 Ten thousand Israelites spoke. — Al-Kashshaf and Baydawi transmit this number alongside the seventy report; Alusi repeats it. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 2:55/q02/p4 Those who stayed with Aaron and did not worship the calf spoke. — Abu Hayyan records this further identification. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]

## 2:55/q03 When and why were the men selected for the mountain meeting?  [وَإِذْ قُلْتُمْ · reports]
turns on: Reports differ over whether the selection and demand preceded or followed the calf worshippers' self-killing, and whether an earlier appointed meeting is intended.
- 2:55/q03/p1 They were selected after the calf episode to seek pardon, before the self-killing repentance was completed. — Ibn Ishaq says Moses destroyed the calf, took seventy to hear God, and only after their restoration received the command that repentance required mutual killing. Tha'labi, Mawardi, Wahidi, Baghawi and the dominant chronology in Ibn Atiyya associate selection with seeking pardon; Ibn Atiyya prefers this chronology. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أكثر المفسرين); RAZI-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 2:55/q03/p2 They were selected after Israel had repented by mutual killing. — Al-Suddi says seventy survivors sought pardon, demanded vision, were revived, then asked to be prophets and were made prophets; Ibn Zayd places the demand after repentance and before rejection of the tablets and the raised mountain. Bursevi distinguishes this second selection from an earlier group after Pharaoh drowned. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,السدي); RAZI-FULL(السدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي,عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI]
- 2:55/q03/p3 They were selected after the sea crossing for the appointed meeting. — Al-Naqqash and others place the selection then; Ibn Atiyya prefers the later calf chronology. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النقاش); IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 2:55/q03/p4 The verse leaves the chronology and whether the seekers worshipped the calf unresolved. — Razi says neither of the two reported chronologies, nor the seekers' prior calf worship, is settled by this verse; Elmalılı also leaves timing open. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 2:55/q04 What did the group do and experience before making the demand?  [قُلْتُمْ · reports]
- 2:55/q04/p1 At Sinai they prepared themselves, heard God address Moses, then demanded open sight. — Tha'labi has seventy fasting and purifying themselves, asking to hear God, prostrating in the cloud, and hearing the command to worship God alone. Baghawi adds a screen against Moses' light and a reminder of deliverance from Egypt. Ibn Atiyya reports sixty elders plus ten youths who matured overnight, seventy chosen from seventy-two with Joshua and Talut left behind at equal reward, three days' abstinence and bathing, Aaron left in charge, then cloud, light and prostration. Al-Suddi says some later altered what they heard (2:75). [9 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي,النقاش); RAZI-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق)]

## 2:55/q05 What does calling Moses by his bare name convey?  [يَٰمُوسَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 2:55/q05/p1 It is a singular vocative. — Nahhas and Qurtubi state the grammatical form. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 2:55/q05/p2 It shows discourtesy toward the prophet. — Abu Hayyan contrasts 24:63's instruction about addressing Muhammad; Biqai says they used a bare name despite witnessing God's honor to Moses. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:55/q06 What belief were they withholding?  [لَن نُّؤْمِنَ لَكَ · interpretation]
turns on: The disputed object of belief is Moses' prophethood, the particular revelation he brought, or the completeness of existing faith.
- 2:55/q06/p1 They refused to affirm Moses as a truthful commissioned prophet. — Tha'labi, Wahidi Wajiz, Tabarsi and Razi explain this as withholding trust in Moses himself; al-Asamm and Tabarsi specify his prophetic commission. Baydawi includes it as one option. [7 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الأصم); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; TABRISI+]
- 2:55/q06/p2 They withheld acceptance of Moses' account of God, Torah, or divine commands. — Jishumi and Tabarsi mention God's attributes; Ibn Zayd makes the Torah's authenticity decisive. Bursevi combines the book, Moses hearing speech, and the command to follow. Abu Hayyan cites 12:17 for trusting what Moses brought, and Biqai distinguishes detailed teachings from basic faith by 9:61. Alusi says they already believed Moses. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); JISHUMI; TABRISI(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:55/q06/p3 They denied complete faith until the further sign. — Abu Hayyan compares a hadith requiring supreme love of the Prophet for full faith; Alusi cites loving for one's fellow believer what one loves for oneself. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 2:55/q06/p4 Their refusal conditions true faith on direct witnessing, in an inward reading. — Qashani reads Moses' guidance as leading toward direct witnessing. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 2:55/q07 How does the lām in 'believe you' work?  [لَكَ · grammar]
turns on: The construction may supply acknowledgment as the verb's sense or make Moses' claim the cause or purpose of belief.
- 2:55/q07/p1 It marks affirmation or acknowledgment of Moses. — Qurtubi glosses believe/affirm; Baydawi gives this sense; Abu Hayyan and Samīn derive it from acknowledgment, including the unstated claim about Torah or speech, with 3:81 cited. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:55/q07/p2 It is causal or purposive: belief because of Moses' claim. — Baydawi gives 'because of what you say'; Abu Hayyan and Samīn likewise allow causation, while Alusi allows purpose. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:55/q08 What does īmān mean here?  [نُّؤْمِنَ · meaning]
- 2:55/q08/p1 It means assent or belief. — Jishumi cites the brothers' statement in 12:17 as a parallel. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 2:55/q09 How does ḥattā govern the condition?  [حَتَّىٰ · grammar]
- 2:55/q09/p1 It marks the endpoint of their refusal and makes 'we see' subjunctive. — Tabarsi says an implicit an follows it and the governed phrase is construed as a noun in genitive relation; Abu Hayyan notes it can elsewhere be causal and cites Hudhayl's ʿayn substitution and slight imāla. Biqai contrasts its bounded endpoint with purposive kay; Alusi states the endpoint sense. [5 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:55/q10 What does seeing denote in this wording?  [نَرَى · meaning]
- 2:55/q10/p1 Its basic sense is visual perception, though it can extend to inward knowledge, opinion, or dream vision. — Jishumi notes mirʾāt is named for visual seeing; Tabarsi distinguishes these usages and cites Jarir for raʾy as appearance; Biqai explains dream forms as presenting inward realities through outward forms. [4 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 2:55/q10/p2 Here it is actual sight by the eyes. — Tabari, Qatada, al-Rabi, Wahidi, Tabarsi, Ibn Kathir, Razi and Abu Hayyan reject merely inward or dream sight in this request; the accompanying jahratan supplies the clarification. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(الربيع,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة والربيع بن أنس); BURSEVI; ELMALILI]

## 2:55/q11 What are the verb forms behind narā?  [نَرَى · grammar]
- 2:55/q11/p1 The hamza of raʾā is lost in certain 'see' forms, with dialectal and semantic exceptions. — Jishumi cites poetry preserving yarʾā. Abu Hayyan describes the imperfect and imperative losses, retention in nouns, Tamīm's conservative form, and the different verb meaning 'injure the lung.' [3 notes: JISHUMI(الشاعر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:55/q12 How is narā Allāh read with imāla?  [نَرَى ٱللَّهَ · readings]
turns on: The disagreement is whether temporary loss of the alif also removes the phonetic basis of its imāla, and which route is transmitted.
- 2:55/q12/p1 Imāla of the rāʾ is transmitted in connected reading. — Abū ʿAmr inclines the hamza's fatḥa toward yāʾ to incline the alif; al-Fārisi says an open rāʾ or emphatic consonant does not prevent it, citing saqā and ṣafā. Al-Sūsī's routes include narā Allāh; al-Dānī chooses them by transmitted performance. Ibn al-Jazarī refers back to those routes. [5 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو); NASHR(أبو القاسم الهذلي); NASHR(الداني)+]
- 2:55/q12/p2 When liaison elides the alif, the known reading leaves the rāʾ uninclined. — Al-Fārisi reports the standard Abū ʿAmr treatment; Aḥmad ibn Mūsā says it is the known one. Sibawayh supports restoring fatḥa when the target of imāla disappears. [3 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو,أحمد بن موسى)]
- 2:55/q12/p3 Imāla may remain even when the alif drops temporarily. — Ibn Mūsā transmits this from ʿAbd al-Wārith and ʿAbbās for Abū ʿAmr; Abū al-Ḥasan accepts both Arabic treatments. Ibn al-Jazarī discusses the first yāʾ-derived alif and rejects an unattested qirāʾāt inference from the grammatical dispute over pause on accusative tanwīn. [5 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو الحسن,أحمد بن موسى); NASHR]

## 2:55/q13 How is the divine-name lām pronounced after imāla?  [ٱللَّهَ · readings]
turns on: The preceding imāla is neither a pure kasra nor a pure fatḥa.
- 2:55/q13/p1 Both heavy and light lām are transmitted. — The performance authorities reason respectively that no pure kasra or no pure fatḥa precedes; Ibn al-Ḥājib prefers the light original pronunciation because neither pure fatḥa nor ḍamma precedes and compares the light rāʾ at pause. Ibn al-Jazarī accepts both. [2 notes: NASHR(أئمة الأداء); NASHR(أبو عمرو بن الحاجب)+]

## 2:55/q14 What does jahratan add to the requested sight or speech?  [جَهْرَةً · meaning]
turns on: The adverb may attach to 'see' or to 'said'; the former specifies bodily vision and the latter publicity of speech.
- 2:55/q14/p1 It demands open, unobstructed ocular sight of God. — Ibn Qutayba excludes sleep; Tabari derives the disclosure from cleaning a muddy well and cites al-Farazdaq, Ibn Abbas, Qatada, al-Rabi and Ibn Zayd; Tha'labi cites poetry; Mawardi compares loud recitation and public sin; Wahidi cites Abu Zayd and al-Aʿsha. Tabarsi, Qurtubi, Razi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi exclude dream and inward perception. Biqai contrasts public unveiling with private nearness. [57 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الربيع,الفرزدق بن غالب,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,قتادة); NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الأعشى,قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(القفال); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة والربيع بن أنس); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الرغب); STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; TABRISI(بعضهم)+]
- 2:55/q14/p2 It describes the demand being voiced openly. — Ibn Abbas and Abu Ubayda take it with the utterance; Tabarsi and Abu Hayyan report this, Qurtubi says it requires reordering, while Samīn disputes that syntactic objection. Alusi emphasizes that the speakers showed no hesitation. [8 notes: JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وأبو عبيدة); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وأبو عبيدة); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وأبو عبيدة); STUDYQURAN; TABRISI(بعضهم)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 2:55/q15 What is the accusative construction of jahratan?  [جَهْرَةً · grammar]
turns on: The analyses differ over which verb or participant governs the accusative and whether the noun names a kind of sight.
- 2:55/q15/p1 It is a verbal noun denoting a kind of seeing or an adverbial circumstance of vision or seers. — Nahhas, Wahidi, Jishumi, Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Samīn, Bursevi and Alusi describe these analyses. Wahidi calls it faʿla from jahr used for an object; Abu Hayyan prefers manner or kind; Samīn prefers the verbal noun. The plural jāhir analysis supports a circumstance of the seers. [19 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)+]
- 2:55/q15/p2 It is circumstantial to 'you said' or to the speakers. — Ibn Atiyya also mentions attachment to 'we believe'; Samīn calls that senseless. Abū al-Baqāʾ takes it with qaltum; Samīn says ordinary order can support that without Qurtubi's transposition. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI(بعضهم); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية)-]
- 2:55/q15/p3 It is circumstantial to God as the object seen: manifest and uncovered. — Baydawi, Samīn and Bursevi state this alternative. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]
- 2:55/q15/p4 It is a cognate accusative with an omitted verb 'you disclosed openly'. — Abū al-Baqāʾ supplies the omitted verb. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]

## 2:55/q16 How is jahratan vocalized?  [جَهْرَةً · readings]
turns on: They are dialectal vowel forms, with an additional possible plural analysis for the open-hāʾ form.
- 2:55/q16/p1 The general reading leaves hāʾ quiescent. — Tha'labi and Jishumi identify the common form; other commentators treat it as the base alongside the variant. [2 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(العامة)]
- 2:55/q16/p2 Ibn Abbas and others open the hāʾ with fatḥa. — Tha'labi, Jishumi, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Samīn and Alusi report it; Sahl ibn Shuʿayb and Humayd ibn Qays are also named. It may be a dialectal faʿala verbal noun like ghalaba or a plural of jāhir. Ibn Atiyya says Basrans attest the fatḥa near a guttural after an open syllable, while Kufans allow it by analogy. [12 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(سهل بن شعيب); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وسهل بن شعيب وحميد بن قيس); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس,ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL(سهل بن شعيب وغيره)]

## 2:55/q17 Can God be seen, and in what manner or time?  [نَرَى ٱللَّهَ جَهْرَةً · theology]
turns on: The central dispute is whether condemnation of this demand proves vision impossible, or punishes the circumstances and manner of a request for a possible afterlife vision.
- 2:55/q17/p1 Divine vision is impossible; visual perception would entail bodily quality or direction. — Jishumi reasons that anything seen has body, color or likeness and says even a bare request is condemned; Zamakhshari says Moses refuted directed vision. Al-Balkhi adduces 4:153; Qadi Abd al-Jabbar and Abu al-Husayn argue punishment for the request proves impossibility, citing 2:61, 4:153 and 25:21. These arguments are attributed to Muʿtazila and other sects. [17 notes: JISHUMI(أبو القاسم); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(أبو القاسم البلخي); RAZI-FULL(أبو الحسين,القاضي عبد الجبار); QURTUBI-FULL(أكثر المبتدعة); NASAFI-FULL(المعتزلة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,القدرية والمعتزلة والنجارية والجهمية والخوارج); JISHUMI+; RAZI-FULL-; NASAFI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 2:55/q17/p2 God can be seen without likeness or modality, especially by believers in the afterlife. — Qurtubi invokes Moses' own request; Abu Hayyan cites abundant sound hadith and describes sight without direction, facing, color, form or corporeality. Razi says condemnation of a possible request proves no impossibility, since the paired book and angel requests in 4:153 and 25:21 are possible. Baydawi and Bursevi allow afterlife vision and some prophetic worldly vision; Elmalılı says God can create capable eyes but present eyes cannot encompass Him. Biqai cites the Prophetic promise and 75:22–23. [14 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي وأبو هاشم); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أكثر المسلمين,أهل السنة); BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL(أهل السنة والسلف)+]
- 2:55/q17/p3 Vision without anthropomorphic likeness is itself unbelief. — Abu al-Qasim states this stricter judgment; Jishumi also says vision with anthropomorphic likeness is unbelief, while Abu Ali and Abu Hashim distinguish the nonanthropomorphic case. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو القاسم); JISHUMI(أبو علي وأبو هاشم)-]
- 2:55/q17/p4 God would be seen as a body from above or in an image. — Abu Hayyan attributes bodily direction to Karramiyya and image to anthropomorphists. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكرامية,المشبهة)]
- 2:55/q17/p5 It remains unsettled whether the punishment condemns vision itself. — Razi explicitly rejects the inference from punishment to impossibility; Tabarsi connects Moses' request in 7:143 to his people's request, saying Torah adherents agree he requested vision only once and on their behalf. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABRISI]

## 2:55/q18 Does God see Himself?  [نَرَى ٱللَّهَ · theology]
turns on: Abu Hayyan reports three positions on divine self-perception.
- 2:55/q18/p1 Most Muʿtazilites say He does not. — Abu Hayyan reports this view. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أكثر المعتزلة)]
- 2:55/q18/p2 Another Muʿtazilite group says He does. — Abu Hayyan reports this alternative. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أكثر المعتزلة)]
- 2:55/q18/p3 Al-Kaʿbi and al-Najjar say He sees neither Himself nor anything else. — Abu Hayyan distinguishes this from the other two Muʿtazilite views. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أكثر المعتزلة)]

## 2:55/q19 Why was the demand punished?  [لَن نُّؤْمِنَ لَكَ حَتَّىٰ نَرَى ٱللَّهَ جَهْرَةً · interpretation]
turns on: The explanations distinguish the object and possibility of the requested vision from the group's defiance after established proof and from alternative prior offenses.
- 2:55/q19/p1 It was obstinate refusal of a proven messenger and a demand for needless further proof. — Wahidi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan and Razi say a messenger must be believed once miracles establish him; Jishumi calls a post-proof refusal unbelief. Moses requested from faith and longing, unlike the group's doubting denial. Elmalılı connects their sensory insistence to calf worship and inability to believe unseen truth. Biqai likewise notes clear signs were already enough. [22 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 2:55/q19/p2 They demanded an impossible bodily or worldly sight of God. — Jishumi makes the bare request culpable and says punishment deterred others. Kashshaf says a body in a direction would follow; Baydawi distinguishes such sight from vision without modality. Ibn Kathir calls open vision impossible for them; Bursevi and Elmalılı confine the impossibility to their earthly condition or encompassing bodily sight. Biqai says public unveiling is for selected nearness, not rejecters. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(بعضهم); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]
- 2:55/q19/p3 They improperly asked Moses to show what was outside his power. — Ibn Furak connects the wording to 4:153; Qurtubi repeats his proposal. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن فورك); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن فورك)]
- 2:55/q19/p4 The request sought something reserved for the afterlife or would end obligation. — Razi lists afterlife-only vision, necessary knowledge ending responsibility, and a divine benefit in withholding sight; Abu Hayyan repeats the first two. Qurtubi later disputes the supposed end of duty after an extraordinary event. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:55/q19/p5 The punishment was for calf worship, rejection of Torah, or another disbelief. — Ibn Atiyya reports that Moses thought calf worship prompted the destruction, relating 7:155 to the fools' act; Abu Hayyan lists these alternatives. Ibn Zayd's report has them rejecting the tablets. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم)]
- 2:55/q19/p6 The demand was meant as an impossible condition for never believing. — Wahidi reports the comparison with 7:40's impossible condition. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(بعضهم)]

## 2:55/q20 What was the ṣāʿiqa that seized them?  [ٱلصَّٰعِقَةُ · interpretation]
turns on: Reports identify different physical causes; other interpreters use the term for the destructive effect or a broader class of overwhelming events.
- 2:55/q20/p1 It was death or a deadly punishment. — Ibn Qutayba, Zajjaj, Wahidi and Ibn al-Jawzi cite the raising after death in 2:56 or 39:68. Tustari gives death and destructive punishment. Ibn Abbas uses Rabi'a's verse for a deadly calamity. Razi rejects equating the thing seen with death itself. [22 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الحسن); NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL-]
- 2:55/q20/p2 It was fire or lightning from heaven that burned them. — Al-Suddi, Tha'labi, Wahidi and others give fire; Jishumi cites 13:13 and his root sense of heavenly fire. Biqai prefers fire because they watched it. Bursevi adds a sound. [22 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); BIQAI-FULL+]
- 2:55/q20/p3 It was a heavenly cry, sound, or force heard by the group. — Al-Rabi reports a sound; Marwan calls it a heavenly cry; Wahb says a heavenly force killed them for a day and night; Kashshaf, Baydawi and Alusi list heard hosts or a cry. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(الربيع,الربيع بن أنس); IBNABIHATIM(الربيع بن أنس,مروان بن الحكم); THALABI(وهب); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس عن أبي جعفر,مروان بن الحكم); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 2:55/q20/p4 It was a quake or tremor. — Ibn Ishaq calls the killing tremor the thunderbolt; Razi and Abu Hayyan cite the rajfah parallel in 7:155 for an external cause, and Study Quran notes ṣāʿiqa has broader range than rajfah. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المحققون)+; RAZI-FULL(المحققون)+]
- 2:55/q20/p5 It names any overwhelming event or cause of death, including sound, fire, quake, terror, or loss of faculties. — Tabari's broad definition includes bodily harm, loss of reason or a faculty; Jishumi extends heavenly fire to punishment and cites 41:13. Razi says the object named is the cause, not death itself; Abu Hayyan prefers that view. Asad says the Qur'an specifies no physical form, though lexicographers agree on force and suddenness. Study Quran notes calamity, destruction and the analogous afflictions of ʿĀd and Thamūd. [13 notes: TUSTARI; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المحققون)+; RAZI-FULL(المحققون)+]

## 2:55/q21 Did the strike kill them or merely stun them?  [فَأَخَذَتْكُمُ ٱلصَّٰعِقَةُ · reports]
turns on: The texts use death and raising for the group but waking for Moses; some reports interpret the group's episode as extreme terror or swooning.
- 2:55/q21/p1 They died, then were restored to complete their allotted lives. — Qatada and al-Rabi explicitly say this; 2:56 is cited as proof of death. Baghawi says Moses pleaded with 7:155 and they revived one by one after a day and night; Ibn Kathir and al-Suddi explain his fear of returning without them. Ibn Atiyya says their spirits departed and they were good when they left. The alternating groups report varies sequence. [34 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق,ابن زيد,الربيع,الربيع بن أنس,السدي,قتادة,محمد بن إسحاق); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الربيع بن أنس,عدوة بن رويم,قتادة); THALABI(وهب); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي,قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(السدي,محمد بن إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس عن أبي جعفر,السدي,عروة بن رويم,محمد بن إسحاق); DURR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK, R)]
- 2:55/q21/p2 They fainted or entered a near-death state and later recovered. — Tabari cites Moses waking in 7:143 and Jarir's comparison of a living Farazdaq; Kashshaf says Moses' own ṣaʿq was swooning. Abu Hayyan reports a view that the group fainted like Moses. Wahb describes terror and loss of senses, with 2:56 using revival for recovery; Elmalılı makes the calamity a communal death in effect, with survivors unable to move. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(جرير بن عطية); KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(جرير,قيل); ELMALILI(Vehb)]
- 2:55/q21/p3 Moses was struck unconscious but did not die, unlike the Israelites. — Abu Hayyan and Alusi distinguish 'awoke' for Moses in 7:143 from 'raised' for the others in 2:56; another report says he too was struck, without settling whether he died. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL+]

## 2:55/q22 How is ṣāʿiqa read?  [ٱلصَّٰعِقَةُ · readings]
turns on: The two noun forms are transmitted as linguistic variants.
- 2:55/q22/p1 The general reading is al-ṣāʿiqa with an alif. — Tha'labi and Jishumi distinguish it from the shorter variant. [2 notes: THALABI(عمر وعثمان وعلي); JISHUMI(العامة)]
- 2:55/q22/p2 Umar, Uthman, Ali and others read al-ṣaʿqa without an alif. — Tha'labi, Jishumi, Qurtubi, Ibn Atiyya and Alusi report the named readers; Qurtubi adds Ibn Muhaysin throughout the Qur'an, Abu Hayyan names ʿAmr, and Ibn Atiyya says the short noun is what comes upon a person from a thunderbolt. Kashshaf also records Ali's fa-adhkhatkum al-ṣaʿqa. [7 notes: THALABI(عمر وعثمان وعلي); JISHUMI(عمر وعثمان وعلي); KASHSHAF-FULL(علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عمر); QURTUBI-FULL(عمر وعثمان وعلي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(عمر وعلي)]

## 2:55/q23 What does the thunderbolt's 'taking' convey?  [فَأَخَذَتْكُمُ · meaning]
- 2:55/q23/p1 It seized, overtook, and surrounded them by force. — Abu Hayyan gives grasping by hand as the root; Alusi says it overcame and encompassed them; Biqai emphasizes whole and violent grasp. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:55/q24 What were they looking at as the strike came?  [وَأَنتُمْ تَنظُرُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: The interpretations identify the object as the approaching event, one another, its aftermath, knowledge, or an awaited vision; Elmalılı stresses the timing of the clause.
- 2:55/q24/p1 They watched the strike, fire, or its approaching signs and effects. — Tustari says they witnessed it while others learned a warning; Mawardi says they saw death; Wahidi Wajiz, Nasafi, Biqai and Bursevi say they watched descending lightning or fire. Tabarsi says signs leading to death; Baydawi allows event or effects; Razi says this made punishment more severe, and Kashshaf says they were struck by what they sought to see. Abu Hayyan and Alusi include effects visible after revival. [19 notes: TUSTARI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 2:55/q24/p2 Some watched others being struck, died, or revived in turn. — Urwa ibn Ruwaym says a first group was struck while others watched, then the sequence reversed; Ibn Zayd says they watched one another. Baghawi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi also allow mutual looking; Abu Hayyan includes watching individual revivals and cites Ezra. [10 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عدوة بن رويم,عروة بن رويم); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عروة بن رويم); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 2:55/q24/p3 Looking means knowing what was happening. — Baghawi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi allow mental knowledge in place of ocular observation. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 2:55/q24/p4 They were awaiting the requested vision. — Abu Hayyan allows an Arabic sense of naẓara as waiting; Alusi calls this possible but unattested here and cites a poetic tanẓurāni only for its Arabic usage. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]
- 2:55/q24/p5 They stared helplessly while struck but did not view death after death. — Elmalılı says looking and striking are concurrent, supported by 7:155's trembling calamity, and rejects an interpretation of dead people still looking. [3 notes: ELMALILI(Birçokları); ELMALILI+]

## 2:55/q25 How is 'while you looked' constructed?  [وَأَنتُمْ تَنظُرُونَ · grammar]
- 2:55/q25/p1 It is a circumstantial clause concurrent with the seizing. — Nahhas, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi and Elmalılı agree on the clause's basic syntax; Elmalılı also permits coordination of simultaneous events. [5 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 2:55/q26 What is the range of naẓar?  [تَنظُرُونَ · meaning]
- 2:55/q26/p1 It is directing the eye toward a visible object, and can extend to thought or waiting. — Jishumi gives visual search with a sound faculty and mental extension; Alusi cites the poem for 'wait for me' as an Arabic usage. [2 notes: JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:55/q27 What happened afterward, and how long did the affliction last?  [* · reports]
turns on: Accounts disagree over literal death and recovery; the narrated duration is a day and night.
- 2:55/q27/p1 God revived them after Moses pleaded, so they could finish their remaining lives. — Qatada and al-Rabi state the life-span purpose; Baghawi and Ibn Ishaq report Moses' fear of returning alone and his prayer in 7:155–156; Ibn Zayd places later rejection of the tablets and the raised mountain after revival. Al-Suddi says they then requested prophethood and were made prophets. [16 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,الربيع بن أنس,السدي,قتادة,محمد بن إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(السدي,محمد بن إسحاق); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي,عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم,محمد بن إسحاق); DURR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس,قتادة); ZAJJAJ]
- 2:55/q27/p2 The death or thunderstruck interval was one day and one night. — Wahb, Baghawi, Kashshaf, Baydawi and Abu Hayyan transmit this duration; the latter says exegetes agree. [5 notes: THALABI(وهب); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المفسرون)]
- 2:55/q27/p3 Some recovered from severe terror rather than literal death. — Wahb's alternative has Moses pray and their senses return; Elmalılı says the event amounted to a people's death and rescue to renewed life. [3 notes: ELMALILI(Vehb)]

## 2:55/q28 Did moral obligation remain after their revival?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether extraordinary direct knowledge ends the conditions for accountable faith.
- 2:55/q28/p1 They remained obligated. — Al-Qurtubi prefers this view ascribed to Mawardi, arguing that Israel saw the falling mountain and surrounding fire yet remained accountable, as did Jonah's people; otherwise no rational person would owe worship. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي)+]
- 2:55/q28/p2 Their obligation ended after direct extraordinary knowledge. — The rejected view says accountability depends on inferential proof, not unavoidable knowledge after witnessing such events. [0 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي)-]

## 2:55/q29 What does the episode teach about faith and resurrection?  [* · interpretation]
- 2:55/q29/p1 Resurrection after the blow is a mercy, a warning, and evidence for resurrection. — Zajjaj compares 2:259 and 2:243 and says scriptural reports answer Arab deniers; Razi stresses repentance and reward; Biqai directs deniers to People of the Book, and Elmalılı says the miracle taught faith without sensory sight. The thunderbolt illustrates punishment of ʿĀd and Thamūd elsewhere. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 2:55/q29/p2 The sudden punishment did not force their knowledge and accountability. — Jishumi says otherwise their subsequent restoration and accountability would be improper. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 2:55/q30 How does the demand relate to the other Qur'anic accounts?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the different descriptions and sequences refer to one meeting or separate incidents.
- 2:55/q30/p1 It is the same episode as Moses' seventy in 7:155. — Ibn Ishaq, al-Suddi and others link the thunderbolt with the quake and Moses' prayer; Tabarsi and Study Quran point to 4:153's demand to see God openly. Study Quran notes ṣāʿiqa and rajfah can describe the same event despite different ranges. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي,محمد بن إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي,قوم); TABRISI(أبو القاسم البلخي); RAZI-FULL(السدي,المحققون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المحققون); IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); STUDYQURAN(IK, R)]
- 2:55/q30/p2 The events in 2:55 and 7:155 are distinct. — Study Quran reports this alternative. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(JJ)]
- 2:55/q30/p3 The relationship is left unresolved. — Razi says the verse does not settle chronology or group identity; Study Quran records commentators who suspend judgment. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 2:55/q31 Why is the punishment's agent left unspoken?  [فَأَخَذَتْكُمُ ٱلصَّٰعِقَةُ · rhetoric]
- 2:55/q31/p1 The passage foregrounds God's blessings, so punishment is attributed to the thunderbolt. — Abu Hayyan says God is the actual sender, but explicit divine naming at this point could overwhelm the audience's joy; encouragement predominates over warning. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:55/q32 What inward meaning is drawn from the demand and strike?  [* · inward]
- 2:55/q32/p1 Direct witnessing surpasses mediated faith, and the strike is annihilation in divine self-disclosure. — Qashani reads refusal as remaining short of true faith until direct witnessing, and the thunderbolt as death in disclosure of the Essence while they observe. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 2:55/q32/p2 Reverent gaze at the Divine Essence leads to majesty and then confession of helplessness. — Al-Sulami reports a Baghdadi saying the gazer is clothed in might and majesty and seeks refuge in repentance. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعض البغداديين)]

## 2:55/q33 How is the nūn of nuʾmina pronounced before laka?  [نُّؤْمِنَ لَكَ · readings]
- 2:55/q33/p1 Abū ʿAmr assimilates the nūn into the lām. — Samīn says the two sounds are close. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو)]

## 2:55/q34 What does the divine name Allah convey here?  [ٱللَّهَ · rhetoric]
- 2:55/q34/p1 It conveys divine greatness and the impropriety of their demand. — Biqai contrasts the name with 'Lord' in 75:22–23, which accompanies attained vision; Allah here evokes the unseen and the group's loss. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:55/q35 Did the seventy actually see God?  [نَرَى ٱللَّهَ · reports]
- 2:55/q35/p1 They did not see Him. — Ibn Kathir rejects the People of the Book's claim that they did, since Moses himself requested vision and was denied. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 2:55/q36 How is al-ṣāʿiqa parsed?  [ٱلصَّٰعِقَةُ · grammar]
- 2:55/q36/p1 It is the nominative subject of the verb. — Nahhas gives this iʿrāb. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 2:55/q37 Where else does Alusi direct the reader for this episode?  [* · links]
- 2:55/q37/p1 To his later discussion in Sūrat al-Aʿrāf. — Alusi points ahead to his treatment around 7:155. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
