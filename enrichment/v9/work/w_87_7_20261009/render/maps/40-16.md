# 40:16: 31 questions

## 40:16/q01 What is the Day of Meeting to which this day is linked?  [يَوْمَ · links]
turns on: Which meeting supplies the name; al-Kashf appeals to literal breadth and the sequence of gathering then exposure.
- 40:16/q01/p1 It is the gathering of all creatures, with several kinds of meeting encompassed. — Ibn ʿAbbās in Ālūsī calls it the gathering of all creatures; al-Kashf prefers this literal sense because the gathering and subsequent exposure each inspire awe. Elmalılı includes spirits and bodies, heaven and earth, deeds and doers, worshipped beings and worshippers. Ṭabāṭabāʾī relates meeting God to removal of distracting causes, citing 32:10, 11:29, 84:6. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(صاحب الكشف)+]
- 40:16/q01/p2 It is the meeting of Creator and created. — Muqātil's explanation is preferred by some scholars in Ālūsī because other verses speak of meeting the Lord, including 18:110, 10:7 and 25:21. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(مقاتل)]
- 40:16/q01/p3 It is the meeting of heaven's people and earth's people. — Al-Suddī is reported by Ālūsī. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(السدي)]
- 40:16/q01/p4 It is the meeting of oppressor and oppressed. — Maymūn ibn Mihrān is reported by Ālūsī. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ميمون بن مهران)]
- 40:16/q01/p5 It is each person's meeting with their deeds. — Al-Thaʿlabī's report is transmitted by Ālūsī. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الثعلبي)]

## 40:16/q02 How is 'the day they are exposed' attached and inflected?  [يَوْمَ هُم بَٰرِزُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Which preceding or understood expression governs yawm; Ālūsī prefers apposition over governance by lā yakhfā.
- 40:16/q02/p1 The day is appositional to the preceding Day of Meeting; the nominal clause specifies it. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī permit apposition; Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī, Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr identify that day as the Day of Meeting. Ālūsī calls this the apparent parse; Ibn ʿĀshūr judges the nominal clause without ellipsis stronger. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 40:16/q02/p2 The day is an adverb governed by 'nothing is hidden.' — Ibn ʿAṭiyya argues for inflection and cites poetry and 5:119; al-Nābigha supplies an inflected temporal example. Al-Qurṭubī allows the clause to govern the day. Al-Samīn notes this requires allowing lā to govern a preceding expression, apart from an oath's answer. Ālūsī reports this analysis but finds apposition more apparent. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النابغة الذبياني); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL]
- 40:16/q02/p3 The day is governed by 'meeting': the meeting occurs when they appear. — Al-Samīn lists this construction alongside apposition. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 40:16/q02/p4 An omitted 'remember' governs the accusative day. — Al-Samīn gives this as a fourth parse. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 40:16/q03 Can a future 'day' be annexed to the nominal clause 'they are exposed'?  [يَوْمَ هُم بَٰرِزُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether future yawm may directly take a nominal sentence; Sībawayh requires ellipsis, al-Akhfash permits it.
- 40:16/q03/p1 Yes: the nominal clause follows yawm directly, with hum as subject and bārizūn as predicate. — Al-Naḥḥās treats the entire clause as genitive after yawm and its tanwīn as omitted when the day means idh, not idhā. Al-Akhfash permits direct annexation, as al-Samīn notes with 84:1. Abū Ḥayyān calls this Abū al-Ḥasan's rare allowance; Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise give the direct clause parse. [6 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 40:16/q03/p2 No: an implied event or verb must mediate a future time word and a nominal clause. — A Basran analysis in al-Ṭabarī supplies their trial or another event, working when yawm means idh but awkward otherwise; it cites 51:13 and 77:35. Sībawayh supplies a verb before the noun, unlike al-Akhfash, as al-Samīn illustrates with 84:1; al-Samīn can take hum as raised by an omitted verb explained by bārizūn, itself predicated of an omitted subject. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي البصرة); SAMIN-DURR(سيبويه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 40:16/q04 Is the temporal expression inflected or built?  [يَوْمَ هُم بَٰرِزُونَ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical conditions under which an annexed time adverb can be built rather than declined.
- 40:16/q04/p1 It is inflected here, as an adverb or noun bearing its syntactic case. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers time adverbials taking the case of their position and ordinary inflection when nouns; another grammarian explains that time words act like idh and idhā but become full nouns with tanwīn when a pronoun returns, citing 11:66. Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites grammar and al-Nābigha's poetry; al-Samīn says this is the general treatment. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(غيره); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النابغة الذبياني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR; TAB-FULL+]
- 40:16/q04/p2 Both inflection and building are allowed when an adverb is annexed to a nominal clause. — Abū Ḥayyān prefers the Kufan allowance, citing many built examples. He rejects Ibn ʿAṭiyya's implication that building is confined to annexation to an indeclinable form; Basran and Kufan treatment of 5:119 differs. Al-Samīn reports both forms. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 40:16/q04/p3 It must be built when annexed to the nominal clause. — Abū Ḥayyān calls this claim of some in his school mistaken and says no grammarian held it; al-Samīn also calls the insistence wrong. [0 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 40:16/q04/p4 The Basran rule builds a day adverb only when annexed to a past verb. — Al-Samīn cites the separate writing of yawm hum here and in al-Dhāriyāt as its original form. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 40:16/q05 Why is 'yawm hum' written as two words?  [يَوْمَ هُم · grammar]
- 40:16/q05/p1 Hum opens an independent nominal sentence annexed to yawm, so the two words are separate. — Al-Burhān contrasts joined yawmuhum, whose possessive pronoun belongs to a single compound; al-Nashr records two occurrences written separately. Al-Samīn calls separation the original form here and in al-Dhāriyāt. [3 notes: BURHAN; NASHR; SAMIN-DURR]

## 40:16/q06 Whom does 'they' identify?  [هُم · referent]
turns on: Whether the local antecedent in 40:14 restricts the group or the scene denotes humanity generally.
- 40:16/q06/p1 The people raised and gathered from their graves. — Most accounts speak of people or all humanity at resurrection: Muqātil, al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Ibn Kathīr, Elmalılı and Study Quran. Muqātil pictures them spread over earth like a stretched hide. [9 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 40:16/q06/p2 The pronoun refers specifically to the disbelievers mentioned in 40:14. — Ibn ʿĀshūr traces the third-person pronoun to that antecedent. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:16/q07 What does their being 'exposed' describe?  [بَٰرِزُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether exposure names bodily emergence, unobstructed visibility, or disclosure of inner realities; Ālūsī objects to a purely disembodied account.
- 40:16/q07/p1 They emerge from their graves into open view. — Muqātil likens the spread earth to a stretched hide; al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Elmalılı and Study Quran explicitly mention graves. Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī report emergence as one sense. Bursevī defines baraza as coming into open space after concealment. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 40:16/q07/p2 They stand plainly visible, with no terrain, building, or cover shielding them. — Qatāda says no mountain or mud building screens them, reported by ʿAbd al-Razzāq, al-Ṭabarī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Durr. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī and Ālūsī describe the level earth without hill or building; Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds that sight and a caller's voice reach them. Al-Biqāʿī includes inward and outward exposure. [16 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 40:16/q07/p3 Their souls, deeds, inner states, and secrets become manifest, including to one another. — Al-Ṭabrisī says hidden states are disclosed and people see one another; al-Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī report disclosure of souls, deeds and secrets. Al-Biqāʿī calls exposure outward and inward; Iṣlāḥī says every inner and outer self is exposed. [5 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 40:16/q07/p4 Their souls stand exposed without bodily veils. — Ālūsī reports this proposal but rejects it for lacking support from an infallible authority. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]

## 40:16/q08 How are the gathered described as clothed or unclothed?  [بَٰرِزُونَ · reports]
turns on: Different reports qualify the general naked gathering by personal merit or by community.
- 40:16/q08/p1 People gather barefoot, naked, and uncircumcised. — Al-Zamakhsharī and Bursevī cite a hadith; Abū Ḥayyān adds bare feet and nakedness. Ālūsī transmits Ibn ʿAbbās's Prophetic report. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(النبي ﷺ)]
- 40:16/q08/p2 Believers who died away from home without fornication receive garments from Paradise. — Bursevī states this exception to the naked gathering. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 40:16/q08/p3 The Prophet's community rises in shrouds, while other communities are barefoot and naked. — Bursevī quotes the Prophet for the community distinction. [1 notes: BURSEVI(رسول الله عليه السلام)]

## 40:16/q09 What does 'nothing of them is hidden from Allah' encompass?  [لَا يَخْفَىٰ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ مِنْهُمْ شَىْءٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase primarily names unrestricted divine knowledge, knowledge for repayment, or the visible emergence of everyone.
- 40:16/q09/p1 No person, deed, condition, inner secret, or temporal detail escapes God's knowledge. — Al-Ṭabarī adds worldly deeds; Ibn ʿAṭiyya specifies thoughts in breasts, al-Qushayrī existing, absent, past, future, impossible and hypothetical events, and al-Biqāʿī outward and inward, now or otherwise. Bursevī cites 69:18; Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes remembered and forgotten things. Ibn ʿĀshūr says minhum embraces the group and their circumstances and shayʾ makes the denial maximally general; Ālūsī expressly chooses this inclusive reading. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; SULAMI(الواسطي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن مسعود رضي الله عنه); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 40:16/q09/p2 None of their deeds is hidden, so recompense takes account of every act. — Ibn ʿAbbās in Ibn al-Jawzī names deeds; al-Māwardī offers God's repayment in knowledge of every deed. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī and al-Ṭabrisī also identify deeds and circumstances; al-Wāḥidī adds wealth. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 40:16/q09/p3 No physical screen can hide them, and God brings every person forth. — Qatāda in al-Durr and Ibn al-Jawzī specifies mountains or dwellings; al-Māwardī and Ibn al-Jawzī give the alternative that God brings all forth because none is hidden. [6 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي,قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 40:16/q10 Why emphasize that nothing is hidden when God already knows all?  [لَا يَخْفَىٰ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ مِنْهُمْ شَىْءٌۭ · rhetoric]
turns on: The point of specifying the resurrection scene despite God's timeless knowledge.
- 40:16/q10/p1 It explains their exposure and dispels the worldly illusion that concealment from people conceals anything from God. — Al-Zamakhsharī and Bursevī point to imagined walls and screens; al-Bayḍāwī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say the sentence confirms their being exposed. Al-Zamakhsharī links 41:22, 4:108 and 14:48; Ālūsī says this is a resumed explanation; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says imagined causes that veiled them are removed. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 40:16/q10/p2 It threatens certain recompense, even though God's knowledge is already complete. — Ibn al-Jawzī explicitly calls the wording a threat; al-Māwardī speaks of repayment and Iṣlāḥī says comprehensive exposure leaves no need for witnesses, evidence, excuses, lies or recommendations. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; MAWARDI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 40:16/q10/p3 God's knowledge is complete at every time, beyond exposed or hidden appearances. — Al-Qushayrī includes even counterfactual possibilities; Ibn Kathīr says all are equally present in God's knowledge; al-Biqāʿī expressly extends knowledge and power to every day, and Qatāda in al-Durr says nothing is hidden even now. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 40:16/q11 How can 'nothing is hidden' function syntactically?  [لَا يَخْفَىٰ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ مِنْهُمْ شَىْءٌۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause resumes, describes a circumstance, or continues the predicate.
- 40:16/q11/p1 It is an independent resumed sentence explaining their exposure. — Ālūsī reports explanatory resumption; al-Samīn permits independence. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 40:16/q11/p2 It is a circumstantial clause attached to the pronoun in 'exposed.' — Al-Samīn and Ālūsī both list this possibility. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 40:16/q11/p3 It is a second predicate. — Al-Samīn and Ālūsī both list this possibility. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 40:16/q12 What variant wording of 'nothing is hidden' is reported?  [لَا يَخْفَىٰ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ مِنْهُمْ شَىْءٌۭ · readings]
- 40:16/q12/p1 Ubayy's codex adds 'upon Him,' reading that nothing concerning them is hidden from Him. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya transmits the codex wording. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبي بن كعب)]

## 40:16/q13 Why name Allah explicitly and say 'of them, anything'?  [ٱللَّهِ مِنْهُمْ شَىْءٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 40:16/q13/p1 Allah clarifies a remote antecedent, while minhum and shayʾ extend the negation to all their circumstances. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the antecedent of Allah lies back in the phrase about whom He wills among His servants, with the subject of warn probably the relative pronoun; minhum permits an understood contextual modifier, and shayʾ is maximally general, broader than merely saying no person is hidden. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:16/q14 When does the question about sovereignty occur?  [لِّمَنِ ٱلْمُلْكُ ٱلْيَوْمَ · reports]
turns on: Whether the surrounding exposure and reckoning require a living audience; Rāzī challenges speech addressed to no one, while extinction reports place the declaration before resurrection.
- 40:16/q14/p1 After creation perishes, between the two trumpet blasts, before resurrection. — Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb in al-Māwardī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī says only the Creator remains and He answers Himself. Ibn ʿUmar calls it the end of worldly time before resurrection; Ibn Kathīr's Trumpet report has God take every soul and ask three times. Al-Baghawī, al-Nasafī and Ibn ʿAbbās in Abū Ḥayyān give the extinction setting; al-Qurṭubī favors the disappearance of every claimant and lineage. [12 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(محمد بن كعب); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(محمد بن كعب القرظي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عمر,محمد بن كعب); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL+; NAHHAS(أبو وائل عن ابن مسعود)-; RAZI-FULL(المفسرون)-; TABRISI-]
- 40:16/q14/p2 At the resurrection gathering, when living people appear before God for judgment. — Al-Māwardī gives this alternative. Al-Naḥḥās accepts Ibn Masʿūd's white-plain report and rejects an empty scene. Al-Ṭabrisī prefers it because the verse describes servants leaving graves. Rāzī appeals to the Day of Meeting and adjacent 40:15 and 40:17, saying a speech without a hearer lacks a purpose; believers and unbelievers answer with contrasting feelings. Ibn al-Jawzī says the accounts differ in timing. [16 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(أبو وائل عن ابن مسعود); MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن مسعود); BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭs); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس)+; TABRISI+]
- 40:16/q14/p3 Before the Hour, a herald announces it to living and dead, and God descends to the lowest heaven for the proclamation. — Ibn ʿAbbās is transmitted by Ibn Abī Ḥātim, Ibn Kathīr and al-Durr; al-Durr says al-Ḥākim authenticated it. Abū Saʿīd's Prophetic report in al-Durr locates the herald between blasts and says the voice reaches living and dead. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 40:16/q14/p4 After humanity perishes, angels may still be present to hear it. — Rāzī offers this accommodation even if the Ibn ʿAbbās extinction report is soundly transmitted. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 40:16/q15 Who asks and who answers the sovereignty question?  [لِّمَنِ ٱلْمُلْكُ ٱلْيَوْمَ لِلَّهِ ٱلْوَٰحِدِ ٱلْقَهَّارِ · referent]
turns on: The reports assign the question and answer to God, a herald, people, angels, or Idrīs; the timing and whether creatures remain to reply affect the identification.
- 40:16/q15/p1 God asks and answers Himself when creatures are silent or gone. — Muqātil says no one replies after God takes heaven and earth; al-Ṭabarī, al-Baghawī, al-Ḥasan in Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī, and Ibn ʿAbbās in Abū Ḥayyān report divine self-answer. Jaʿfar says God silences animate creatures because only He can rightly answer; Ibn ʿĀshūr allows a single divine utterance, while the Study Quran lists this interpretation. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); SULAMI(جعفر); MAWARDI-FULL(عطاء,محمد بن كعب); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن بن أبي الحسن); TABRISI(محمد بن كعب القرظي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطاء); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن كعب); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK, Q, Ṭs); QURTUBI-FULL+; TABRISI-; RAZI-FULL(المفسرون)-]
- 40:16/q15/p2 A herald asks, and all gathered people answer. — Ibn Masʿūd in Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān sets it on a white plain like silver; al-Naḥḥās accepts that report and says believers answer gladly but unbelievers reluctantly, as al-Qurṭubī reports. Al-Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, al-Nasafī and modern commentaries also report this exchange. Rāzī does not prove a unique speaker. [12 notes: NAHHAS(أبو وائل عن ابن مسعود); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن مسعود); BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭs); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس)+]
- 40:16/q15/p3 God asks, and all creatures answer. — Ibn Jurayj in al-Māwardī and Ibn al-Jawzī says believers and unbelievers answer; Ibn Masʿūd in Ibn ʿAṭiyya says assembled people answer God; al-Ṭabrisī prefers the creatures' answer because they have emerged from graves. Ibn ʿĀshūr permits separate omitted speech for the people's answer. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جريج); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABRISI+]
- 40:16/q15/p4 A herald both asks and supplies the answer. — Al-Zamakhsharī reports the first speech on an unblemished white plain as the herald's question and answer before the declaration of each soul's recompense; Bursevī gives the same caller-and-respondent reading. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 40:16/q15/p5 One group of angels asks and another group answers. — Rāzī allows differing angel groups with no decisive proof; Kuranyolu gives this as a view. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 40:16/q15/p6 God asks, the world falls silent, He answers, and then people answer. — Abū Ḥayyān reports the sequence; the initial silence is from awe. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 40:16/q15/p7 Idrīs gives the answer. — Bursevī reports this alternative without further support. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 40:16/q15/p8 A herald asks and the people of Paradise answer. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this through al-Zamakhsharī and leaves the matter to God. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري)]

## 40:16/q16 What happens on the white gathering plain?  [لِّمَنِ ٱلْمُلْكُ ٱلْيَوْمَ · reports]
turns on: Whether the people or the herald provide the answer in the white-plain accounts.
- 40:16/q16/p1 A caller asks, and the gathered answer, on a white earth like silver. — Ibn Masʿūd's account in al-Naḥḥās and Abū Ḥayyān says the white plain resembles silver; al-Naḥḥās accepts it over a question with no creation present. Al-Qurṭubī relays his preference and says believers answer with joy, unbelievers with grief. [4 notes: NAHHAS(أبو وائل عن ابن مسعود); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس)+]
- 40:16/q16/p2 A herald gives both the first question and answer on an untouched white plain, then declares each soul's recompense. — Al-Zamakhsharī compares the plain to a silver ingot on which God has never been disobeyed. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 40:16/q17 What does 'today' mean and how does it attach to the question?  [ٱلْيَوْمَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether al-yawm remains in the question or begins the answer after a pause.
- 40:16/q17/p1 It marks the time sovereignty is established or attaches to 'sovereignty' within the question. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports both parses; Ibn ʿĀshūr says today is relative to the time of the narrated speech. [2 notes: TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 40:16/q17/p2 The reciter may pause after 'sovereignty,' starting a statement 'Today belongs to God.' — Al-Ṭabrisī records this segmentation as an alternative. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 40:16/q18 What is omitted in 'to Allah'?  [لِلَّهِ · grammar]
- 40:16/q18/p1 The subject 'sovereignty' is understood: sovereignty belongs to Allah alone. — Al-Burhān supplies the omitted al-mulk after the question. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 40:16/q19 Why frame exclusive sovereignty as a question and answer?  [لِّمَنِ ٱلْمُلْكُ ٱلْيَوْمَ لِلَّهِ ٱلْوَٰحِدِ ٱلْقَهَّارِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the device primarily forces confession, prepares the hearer, or voices what the scene itself displays.
- 40:16/q19/p1 It compels acknowledgment and humiliates false claimants, while believers rejoice in a truth they confessed. — Al-Biqāʿī compares a victorious king confronting rebels, answered by speech or state, and cites an unnamed poet's cry after long silence that makes rebels weep blood. Al-Naḥḥās, Rāzī and Bursevī distinguish joyful believers from reluctant or regretful unbelievers. Ibn ʿĀshūr names tyrants, Pharaoh's claims in 28:38 and 43:51, royal titles and gods assigned to peoples or domains; Iṣlāḥī asks where expected gods and intercessors have gone. [10 notes: NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(بعض من قال); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 40:16/q19/p2 It builds anticipation so the answer settles more firmly. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the question in 2:186 addressed to the Prophet's servants. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 40:16/q19/p3 It narrates speech or manifests what the disappearance of causes already shows. — Al-Bayḍāwī says the scene itself indicates God's exclusive dominion. Ālūsī calls the question and answer narrated speech attached to the preceding negation or a reply raised by people's appearance; Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies an omitted 'Allah says,' as a circumstance or rhetorical restart, and says following clauses remain connected whichever party answers. Rāzī leaves the identity of speakers unproved. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:16/q20 Why single out that day for God's sovereignty?  [ٱلْمُلْكُ ٱلْيَوْمَ · theology]
turns on: Whether the day dramatizes the exposure of false claims at judgment or the literal disappearance of every creature before resurrection.
- 40:16/q20/p1 God owns sovereignty always, but worldly pretensions and delegated control vanish or become visibly false that day. — Al-Qushayrī says divine dominion never depends on a time; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says believers already know that creatures lack independent power, while unbelievers then cannot deny it. Al-Ṭabrisī says people held some worldly control but nobody then owns anything without a grant; he answers the objection of prophets' and believers' afterlife possessions by distinguishing unqualified kingship or placing the proclamation before Paradise grants. Bursevī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ṭabāṭabāʾī say the day exposes exclusive dominion as causes, intermediaries and claims fail. [12 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن عطاء); BAYDAWI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 40:16/q20/p2 All rival claimants, kingdoms and lineages have perished, displaying God's solitary rule. — Al-Qurṭubī favors this timing; Muqātil, al-Baghawī and al-Nasafī link God as sole respondent to creation subdued by death. Ibn Kathīr's report says God remains alone after taking every soul. [5 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL+]

## 40:16/q21 What does 'the One' affirm?  [ٱلْوَٰحِدِ · theology]
- 40:16/q21/p1 God has no partner, second, component, or division in His sovereignty and essence. — Muqātil says without partner; al-Ṭabarī says without equal; al-Biqāʿī excludes partnership and division and grounds divine perfection in God alone; Elmalılı denies parts or constituent units. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the name fits a scene manifesting unity and mastery of tyrants. [6 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 40:16/q22 What does 'the Subduer' affirm?  [ٱلْقَهَّارِ · theology]
- 40:16/q22/p1 God overpowers all creation, including through death and resurrection, by unrestricted might and will. — Muqātil applies overpowering to resurrection; al-Baghawī, al-Nasafī and Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb apply it to death. Al-Ṭabarī invokes 14:48 and 12:39 for unrivaled might. Al-Biqāʿī says continuous subduing follows self-sufficiency and oneness; Elmalılı cites 28:88, where all but God's face perishes. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the name answers the domination of tyrants. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(محمد بن كعب); BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 40:16/q23 Why follow 'the One' with 'the Subduer'?  [ٱلْوَٰحِدِ ٱلْقَهَّارِ · rhetoric]
- 40:16/q23/p1 Oneness excludes rivals and subduing power makes all claims and effects yield to God. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ in Bursevī says the One nullifies numerical multiplicity and the Overpowering forces acknowledgment of servitude; Bursevī's shaykh says oneness removes multiplicity and overpowering removes effects. Al-Biqāʿī derives continual subduing from perfect self-sufficiency grounded in oneness; Ibn ʿĀshūr says both names suit the collapse of tyrants' power. [6 notes: BURSEVI(ابن عطاء); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 40:16/q24 What reports depict God's taking creation and challenging earthly kings?  [لِّمَنِ ٱلْمُلْكُ ٱلْيَوْمَ · reports]
- 40:16/q24/p1 God takes or folds heaven and earth, declares Himself King, and challenges tyrants and kings. — Muqātil says God takes both in His right hand before the question; al-Qurṭubī cites God taking earth and souls, folding heavens and calling Himself King, and Ibn ʿUmar's report specifies earth in the left hand, heavens in the right, then 'where are tyrants?' Ibn Kathīr cites Ibn ʿUmar's folded heavens and earth in His hand and the challenge to kings. Ibn ʿĀshūr reads the report as frightening rulers by loss of grandeur. [5 notes: MUQATIL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عمر); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عمر); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:16/q25 How is exposure read inwardly?  [يَوْمَ هُم بَٰرِزُونَ لَا يَخْفَىٰ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ مِنْهُمْ شَىْءٌۭ · inward]
turns on: Whether inward exposure centers disclosure of deeds and veils or annihilation of separate existence.
- 40:16/q25/p1 They emerge from selfhood or bodily veils; hidden deeds become visible in their records. — Al-Qāshānī cites 58:6 and 18:49 for deeds passing from latency to appearance; he also proposes emerging from veils of attributes into the Essence. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 40:16/q25/p2 Annihilation effaces their separate existence until only God appears and answers Himself. — Al-Qāshānī says the unity of gathering annihilates all, with the Subduer doing the effacing; Najmī interpretations in Bursevī say nothing of their own existence remains and only God owns being. Junayd describes the station as nothing existing but God; Jāmī calls beginning and return unity and intervening multiplicity illusion. The Najmī source has God both caller and respondent through manifestation of overpowering. [7 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,الجنيد,جامى)]

## 40:16/q26 What do recompense and grace mean for those exposed?  [لَا يَخْفَىٰ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ مِنْهُمْ شَىْءٌۭ · inward]
- 40:16/q26/p1 Those who survey deeds and worship receive just recompense, while those who behold grace receive mercy beyond recompense. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ in al-Sulamī cites 10:58; Abū Bakr ibn Ṭāhir says God first shows the recompense owed for deeds so people recognize the greater standing of grace and generosity. [2 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر,ابن عطاء)]

## 40:16/q27 What hidden failings are exposed at judgment in the inward description?  [بَٰرِزُونَ · inward]
- 40:16/q27/p1 Ungrateful wealth, impatient poverty, inactive learning, and ostentatious recitation are publicly shamed; some frightened people seek the way to God. — Kashf al-asrār as quoted by Bursevī names the ungrateful rich, poor stripped of hypocrisy, scholars without deeds, and showy reciters, then depicts terrified people asking where God's way lies. [2 notes: BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار)]

## 40:16/q28 How does this verse fit the surrounding warning and the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the link is the immediate Day-of-Meeting sequence or the larger argumentative movement of the sūra.
- 40:16/q28/p1 It unfolds the warning about the Day of Meeting into exposure, divine knowledge, and sole sovereignty, then judgment. — Al-Bāqillānī traces this progression; Rāzī uses 40:15 and 40:17 to locate living people at judgment; al-Ṭabarī links power to 14:48 and 12:39. Al-Zamakhsharī invokes 41:22, 4:108 and 14:48 for the exposure and false concealment. Ibn ʿĀshūr keeps the next three clauses in the divine utterance whether God or people answer. [5 notes: BAQILLANI; TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 40:16/q28/p2 Verses 13–20 teach God's control and exclusive worship; verses 10–20 warn disbelievers before the Moses and Pharaoh account. — Iṣlāḥī emphasizes control of mercy and calamity, obedience and lack of an idolaters' intercessor; Study Quran says the Hereafter warning sets up the believer of Pharaoh's House who challenges opposition to Moses, citing 40:28 and 40:45. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 40:16/q29 How does al-Bāqillānī use the sovereignty proclamation in discussing Qur'anic eloquence?  [لِّمَنِ ٱلْمُلْكُ ٱلْيَوْمَ لِلَّهِ ٱلْوَٰحِدِ ٱلْقَهَّارِ · rhetoric]
- 40:16/q29/p1 It exemplifies the majestic badīʿ of God's possession of all things within the Qur'an's fitting eloquence across subjects. — Al-Bāqillānī asks if inimitability is recognized through badīʿ and catalogs examples, including the proclamation and 16:53 about blessings; he extends the catalogue to compact sayings of the Prophet, Abū Bakr and ʿAlī. He says every Qur'anic verse, sūra, narrative, law, proof and instruction has fitting eloquence, whereas human masters vary: poets may fail in ascetic verse and legal stylists change register. [6 notes: BAQILLANI]

## 40:16/q30 How is the earlier 'Day of Meeting' read and written?  [يَوْمَ · readings]
- 40:16/q30/p1 The muṣḥaf omits the yāʾ of al-talāqī; Nāfiʿ and one Abū ʿAmr transmission read without it even in connected speech. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says other readers retain the yāʾ in connection; dropping it there can be explained only by treating connection like pause, rare in prose, while the transmissions attest the form. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:16/q31 Does the excerpt decide every speaker and purpose of the proclamation?  [لِّمَنِ ٱلْمُلْكُ ٱلْيَوْمَ · interpretation]
- 40:16/q31/p1 The question of why this day is singled out is raised without an answer in Rāzī's excerpt, and the speaker cannot be established decisively. — Rāzī allows God or groups of angels as speaker and respondent with no identifying proof; he ends by asking why the day is particular but the excerpt gives no answer. Al-Qurṭubī reports the Paradise people's answer and leaves the matter to God. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري)]
