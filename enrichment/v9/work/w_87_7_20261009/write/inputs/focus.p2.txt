# 87:7/q05 What is the grammatical scope and placement of the exception? [إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · grammar]
turns on: The syntactic antecedent of the exception: the Prophet's forgetting, an earlier position in that promise, or the pasture clause.
- 87:7/q05/p1 It excepts what God wills from the objects of 'forget'; mā refers to the thing willed.
  reasons: Al-Naḥḥās calls the exception accusative. Al-Ālūsī says the scope is unrestricted among all the objects of forgetting. Ibn ʿĀshūr says mā is a relative pronoun and supplies 'that God wills you to forget.'

# 87:7/q06 Why mention a divine exception after promising retention? [إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the qualification conveys an actual reserved possibility, an emphatic idiom, instruction, vigilance, or the wisdom behind selective removal.
- 87:7/q06/p1 It reserves God's absolute power and makes the Prophet's retention visibly dependent on divine favor.
  reasons: Al-Rāzī cites 17:86 and 39:65 to show that God could remove revelation or alter the Prophet's state; al-Ālūsī cites 17:86, 20:114 and 75:16. Elmalılı compares the hypothetical warning in 39:65. Qutb says the exception keeps hearts attached to God's will; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees the third-person turn as pointing to divine power.
