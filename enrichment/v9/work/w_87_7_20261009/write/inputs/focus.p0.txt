# 87:7/q01 How does the exception in “except what God wills” function? [إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the reservation names actual, divinely willed forgetting, a hypothetical divine power, an emphatic idiom, a rare lapse, or an exception attached to an earlier clause.
- 87:7/q01/p1 It is a real exception to the promise of retention: God may make the Prophet forget what He wills.
  reasons: Al-Ṭabarī calls this the clearest sense; Abū Ḥayyān calls a substantive exception the apparent reading and objects that an empty exception is unsuitable to eloquent divine speech. Al-Ālūsī says it ranges over all objects taught, rather than a restricted class. Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies the understood object, 'what God wills you to forget.' Qatāda says no forgetting occurred except by God's will; the generic wording need not itself specify an occasion.

- 87:7/q01/p2 It is a conventional qualification with no actual exception; God did not will the Prophet to forget.
  reasons: Al-Farrāʾ compares oath language and the exception for eternity in 11:107–108: a speaker may say 'except what you wish' while intending to give all. Al-Qurṭubī says oath exceptions follow the swearer's intention. Makkī makes the comparison to both Hūd passages. Al-Farrāʾ's temporal gloss makes the exception a possible time that never comes. Al-Rāzī, al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı say the qualification displays God's power and that retention is His gift; al-Rāzī cites 17:86 and 39:65, al-Ālūsī also 20:114, and Elmalılı cites 39:65. Abū Ḥayyān rejects the empty-exception account; al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı reject his criticism.

- 87:7/q01/p3 The tiny or unspecified exception rhetorically intensifies a total denial of forgetting.
  reasons: Al-Kashshāf likens this to a friend's all-inclusive grant with a nominal reservation; al-Bayḍāwī says rarity can express negation. Al-ʿIṣāmī says the phrase reinforces universal negation. Elmalılı gives the poetic example of praising a fighter by excepting only sword nicks, a form of praise through seeming blame. Al-Samīn says such an exception has a meaningful emphatic force, rather than being mere surplus.

# 87:7/q02 What may God cause the Prophet to forget or set aside? [إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'forget' describes abrogated recitation, an ordinary but corrected lapse, cessation of practice, revelation not yet sent, or fading human knowledge.
- 87:7/q02/p1 Revelation whose recitation, and often its ruling, God abrogates.
  reasons: Al-Ṭabarī favors divine removal as the clearest sense; al-Ḥasan and Qatāda repeatedly say both recitation and ruling are lifted. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Rāzī relate forgetting to 2:106. Al-Biqāʿī grounds it in benefit and easing the community; al-Rāzī says God knows people's interests. Al-Ālūsī argues abrogation may cause disuse or make removal from memory permissible without making 'forgetting' figurative; ʿĀʾishah's ten and five sucklings show that abrogated texts can remain remembered by scholars. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers this kind, describing cessation of recitation followed by forgetting; he cites ʿUmar's stoning and disavowal reports and the 'cause it to be forgotten' reading of 2:106.
