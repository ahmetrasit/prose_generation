# 87:7/q14 Why is the manifest mentioned before the hidden? [ٱلْجَهْرَ وَمَا يَخْفَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 87:7/q14/p1 The manifest recitation is the immediate focus; mentioning it first does not limit God's knowledge.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says knowledge of the hidden would include it if disclosed, and the explicit pair shows that public and concealed recitation are equally known.
  holders (2 notes): BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL
  notes: BIQAI:87:7/r8 BIQAI-FULL:v8p397/r2
sources: BIQAI = al-Biqāʿī, d. 885; BIQAI-FULL = al-Biqāʿī, d. 885

# 87:7/q15 How should mā yakhfā be parsed? [وَمَا يَخْفَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā denotes a hidden thing or nominalizes 'being hidden,' and whether the verb then has a subject.
- 87:7/q15/p1 Mā is a substantive pronoun referring to what is hidden.
  reasons: Al-Samīn prefers this: treating mā as a verbal noun would leave yakhfā without a subject.
  holders (1 notes): SAMIN-DURR+
  notes: SAMIN-DURR:87:7#2/r1
- 87:7/q15/p2 Mā may be analyzed as a verbal noun, giving neater coordination with al-jahr.
  reasons: Al-Samīn mentions the syntactic attraction but rejects it because yakhfā would lack a subject.
  holders (0 notes): SAMIN-DURR-
  notes:  | against: SAMIN-DURR:87:7#2/r1
sources: SAMIN-DURR = al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756

# 87:7/q16 How does the promise of recitation relate to the command to glorify? [* · links]
turns on: Whether recitation is a necessary part of glorification or an independent promise that explains and prepares it.
- 87:7/q16/p1 Reciting revealed Qurʾān is part of glorifying God, so reassurance about memory follows the command.
  reasons: Abū Ḥayyān says glorification entails recitation and the Prophet feared forgetting; al-Ālūsī reports this link but finds it weak.
  holders (3 notes): ABUHAYYAN; ALUSI(أبو حيان); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-
  notes: ABUHAYYAN:87:7/r12 ALUSI:87:7/r24 ALUSI-FULL:v30p106/r4 | against: ALUSI:87:7/r25 ALUSI-FULL:v30p106/r9
- 87:7/q16/p2 The independent recitation promise explains why glorification is fitting or prepares the Prophet for divine effusion.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī prefers the explanatory connection; alternatively glorification prepares reception of revelation, showing its merit, with reports cited from al-Thaʿlabī and Ibn al-Shaykh. He rejects making the later knowledge clause an explanation of the command.
  holders (4 notes): ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+
  notes: ALUSI:87:7/r25 ALUSI:87:7/r26 ALUSI-FULL:v30p106/r10 ALUSI-FULL:v30p106/r12
sources: ABUHAYYAN = Abū Ḥayyān, d. 745; ALUSI = al-Ālūsī, d. 1270; ALUSI-FULL = al-Ālūsī, d. 1270

# 87:7/q17 Who is addressed by the promise, and what knowledge does it concern? [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the promise is confined to Muhammad's Qurʾānic memory or also addresses humanity's acquired knowledge.
- 87:7/q17/p1 Muhammad is promised retention of Qurʾānic revelation.
  reasons: Classical commentators as summarized by Asad read this as addressed to Muhammad; al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr and Islahi connect it to preservation of the recited Qurʾān. Al-Ṭabrisī finds a prophetic sign in the unlettered Prophet's retention after a single hearing.
  holders (5 notes): ASAD-NOTES(classical commentators); TAB; IBNKATHIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABRISI; ASAD-NOTES-
  notes: ASAD-NOTES:87:6#n4/r1 TAB:87:7/r1 IBNKATHIR:87:7/r1 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p277/r2 TABRISI:87:7-9#2/r3 | against: ASAD-NOTES:87:6#n4/r2
- 87:7/q17/p2 The address to Muhammad also reaches humanity and concerns cumulative knowledge that God teaches across generations.
  reasons: Asad links the passage to the first revelation in 96:1–5 and says experience can render parts of empirical and rational learning obsolete. He argues that observation and speculation are limited, while realities beyond perception require revelation.
  holders (4 notes): ASAD-NOTES; ASAD-NOTES+
  notes: ASAD-NOTES:87:6#n4/r3 ASAD-NOTES:87:6#n4/r4 ASAD-NOTES:87:6#n4/r5 ASAD-NOTES:87:6#n5/r1
sources: ASAD-NOTES = Muhammad Asad, d. 1412; IBNKATHIR = Ibn Kathīr, d. 774; ISLAHI-TADABBUR = Amin Ahsan Islahi (d. 1997); TAB = al-Ṭabarī, d. 310; TABRISI = al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548

# 87:7/q18 What role does this verse play in the sūrah and its surrounding passage? [* · links]
turns on: Whether the verse is read as part of the sūrah's original reassurance and thematic sequence or as a later harmonizing insertion.
- 87:7/q18/p1 It belongs to the sūrah's reassurance of the Prophet through preservation and ease.
  reasons: Qutb says the sūrah fills the cosmos with praise and gives the Prophet good news of divine preservation and ease. Islahi says the address shifts from obstinate Quraysh in the previous sūrah to the Prophet, with the next sequence dispelling his preaching fears; 87:6–8 promises matured revelation, preservation except what God sets aside, and ease. Islahi says prior deniers had only temporary respite.
  holders (4 notes): QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR
  notes: QUTB-ZILAL:87:1/r3 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p270/r1 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p270/r2 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p270/r4
- 87:7/q18/p2 It contributes to themes of creation, revelation, final recompense, and the easy character of the message.
  reasons: Qutb says these foundations are rooted in earlier scriptures, citing 87:18–19. He describes al-Aʿlā as Meccan with nineteen verses and says the Prophet loved and recited it at Eid and Friday prayers with al-Ghāshiyah, sometimes on the same day.
  holders (2 notes): QUTB-ZILAL
  notes: QUTB-ZILAL:87:1/r1 QUTB-ZILAL:87:1/r4
- 87:7/q18/p3 It appears to be a later insertion reconciling the preservation promise with 2:106.
  reasons: Corpus Coranicum proposes this literary history, saying verse 7 harmonizes verse 6 with the possibility of God making Muhammad forget verses in 2:106.
  holders (1 notes): CORPUSCORANICUM
  notes: CORPUSCORANICUM:87:1-8:kommentar/r9
sources: CORPUSCORANICUM = Nicolai Sinai (Frühmekkanische Suren), with Nora K. Schmid, using preparatory work by Angelika Neuwirth; later parts Dirk Hartwig, Angelika Neuwirth et al. (author line per sura in segments); ISLAHI-TADABBUR = Amin Ahsan Islahi (d. 1997); QUTB-ZILAL = Sayyid Quṭb, d. 1386
