# 87:7/q08 Which reported lapses or reminders illustrate the exception? [إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · reports]
turns on: The reported event or textual example used to show an actual lapse, its correction, or abrogation.
- 87:7/q08/p1 In prayer the Prophet omitted a verse; Ubayy thought it abrogated, but the Prophet said he had forgotten it.
  reasons: Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Samīn, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite versions of this report. Al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı place it in dawn prayer and cite Bukhārī or the Ṣaḥīḥayn. Its point in these notes is a real, corrected lapse rather than permanent loss.

# 87:7/q09 Why use the name Allāh and shift to third person? [ٱللَّهُ إِنَّهُۥ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the shift primarily foregrounds divine majesty and all attributes or the power behind the exception.
- 87:7/q09/p1 The divine name and pronoun heighten majesty and awe, locating the will in the one who has every divine attribute.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says the third-person turn and name invoke a majesty beyond explicit mention of an instrument of greatness; he sees the pronoun as exceeding creatures' grasp. Al-Ālūsī says the name signals a will encompassing all attributes. Elmalılı says the all-comprehensive name cultivates awe and relates the gift to the totality of names and attributes, with 'Lord Most High' confirming it. Corpus Coranicum refers its Allah note to Q 95:8 without a further claim.
  holders (6 notes): BIQAI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; CORPUSCORANICUM
  notes: BIQAI:87:7/r4 BIQAI:87:7/r6 ALUSI:87:7/r23 ALUSI-FULL:v30p106/r3 ELMALILI:87:6/r23 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:7:anmerkung/r1
- 87:7/q09/p2 The third-person turn points to divine power as the ground of the exception.
  reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī gives this explanation.
  holders (1 notes): TABATABAI
  notes: TABATABAI:87:1-19#3/r5

# 87:7/q10 What does al-jahr mean here? [ٱلْجَهْرَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term is confined to voiced speech or denotes anything manifest to perception.
- 87:7/q10/p1 It is audible, raised speech, contrasted with quiet whispering.
  reasons: Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī explicitly give raised voice as the lexical sense, the opposite of a whisper.
  holders (2 notes): JISHUMI; TABRISI
  notes: JISHUMI:v10p7373/r3 TABRISI:87:7-9#2/r5
- 87:7/q10/p2 It is the manifest or openly perceptible, including what can be seen or heard.
  reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says jahr can mean what is fully available to sight or hearing, and here contrasts manifest with hidden; he cites 4:153 and 21:110. Other commentators apply it to public deeds as well as speech.
  holders (7 notes): TABATABAI; MUQATIL(مقاتل); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; QURTUBI; ALUSI

# 87:7/q11 What are the manifest and hidden things God knows? [ٱلْجَهْرَ وَمَا يَخْفَىٰ · referent]
turns on: The scope of God's knowledge: all people's public and inward states or a specific contrast involving the Prophet, revelation, charity, future acts, or human perception.
- 87:7/q11/p1 People's public and private speech, deeds, and inward states in general.
  reasons: Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr and others apply the pair broadly. Qatāda specifies inner whispering; Ibn ʿAbbās and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr name what lies in the heart or self. Al-Ālūsī says the overt includes actions and other manifestations, not speech only; Asad calls the pair what humans perceive and what they cannot perceive. Al-Bursevī adds that the unlettered Prophet received knowledge of writing, rules and crafts.
