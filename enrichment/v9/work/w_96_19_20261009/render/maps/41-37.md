# 41:37: 22 questions

## 41:37/q01 What do the night, day, sun, and moon show as God's signs?  [وَمِنْ ءَايَٰتِهِ ٱلَّيْلُ وَٱلنَّهَارُ وَٱلشَّمْسُ وَٱلْقَمَرُ · theology]
- 41:37/q01/p1 Their creation, regular changes, courses, light, and service to people disclose God's existence, oneness, power, wisdom, and governance. — Muqatil says workmanship makes the unseen God knowable. Al-Tabari cites 36:40, non-overtaking and separate orbits; al-Mawardi cites measured alternation and distinct light; al-Qushayri cites courses. Ibn Atiyya notes changing lengths, overlap and equality across places. Al-Tabrisi defines night as the sun's departure from the earth's surface and day as its rising, and cites ordered light and motion. Al-Razi links created parts to the Creator and points to his discussions of 1:2 and 6:1. Al-Nasafi cites fixed light and assigned courses. Ibn Kathir adds that phases measure days, months, years, rights, worship and transactions. Al-Biqa'i infers resurrection and all possible things; his quoted poem calls creation the King's message and all besides God vain. Bursevi adds existence, knowledge, wisdom, the revival of earth (30:50), lengthened shade (25:45), and hope of seeing God (75:22). Al-Tabataba'i sees one governing order and hence one Lord. Islahi describes subordinate cycles and assigned service as signs of power, wisdom, mercy and providence. Study Quran compares 31:29, 16:12, 35:13, 39:5, and 36:37-40. Asad says God alone is the source of existence, so ascribing real power to a created phenomenon, force, circumstance or idea is unreasonable and blasphemous. [30 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 41:37/q02 Why are night and day named in this order, followed by sun and moon?  [ٱلَّيْلُ وَٱلنَّهَارُ وَٱلشَّمْسُ وَٱلْقَمَرُ · rhetoric]
- 41:37/q02/p1 Night precedes day because darkness, viewed as absence, precedes light, viewed as existence; the sun then accompanies and illuminates day, and the moon accompanies night. — Al-Razi says the order hints that these phenomena began to exist. Abu Hayyan adds that the sun illuminates more than the moon, whose light was said to derive from it. Al-Biqa'i calls sun and moon the spirits of the night and day as bodies. Bursevi states the day and night pairings; al-Alusi reports precedence of night and the sun as day's luminous sign and cause. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإمام المرزوقي); ALUSI-FULL]

## 41:37/q03 Where does the sun's and moon's light come from?  [ٱلشَّمْسُ وَٱلْقَمَرُ · interpretation]
turns on: Al-Alusi reports competing accounts of whether sunlight is intrinsic or borrowed, including the philosophers' claimed observation.
- 41:37/q03/p1 The moon's light is said to derive from the sun, whose light is not borrowed from another body. — Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi report the moonlight explanation in accounting for the order of sun after day. Al-Alusi calls unborrowed sunlight the common view. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 41:37/q03/p2 The sun's light is attributed to the Throne. — Al-Alusi reports this as one account beside the common view. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 41:37/q03/p3 The sun's light may derive from another body. — Al-Alusi reports contemporary philosophers' suspicion, supported by their claim of a small dark spot at the sun's edge. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 41:37/q04 What does the definite singular “the moon” convey?  [وَٱلْقَمَرُ وَلَا لِلْقَمَرِ · rhetoric]
- 41:37/q04/p1 The one moon is naturally definite; an indefinite singular moon suggests multiple moons and can impair the intended image. — Al-Jurjani contrasts the plural crescents in 2:189, praises al-Buhturi's subtle 'two moons,' and faults Abu Tammam's image for implying moons both distant and near in light. Al-Buhturi's definite moon conveys lofty position yet light near travelers. Forcing a sentence break after 'a moon' does not repair the wording. Sa'id b. Humayd's night visit and the opposed dawn version play on the customary rising times of moon and sun. [9 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR(سعيد بن حميد)]

## 41:37/q05 Why repeat the prohibition and name the moon explicitly?  [لَا تَسْجُدُوا۟ لِلشَّمْسِ وَلَا لِلْقَمَرِ · rhetoric]
- 41:37/q05/p1 The repeated negation separately forbids each prostration, and the repeated definite celestial noun refers back to the same known object. — Al-Biqa'i says the moon is named rather than replaced by a pronoun, stressing each prohibition. Al-Burhan gives the general rule that repeated definite nouns refer to the same thing, comparing 94:5-6 and the path in the Fatiha. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN]

## 41:37/q06 Who worshipped the sun and moon, and whom does the prohibition address?  [لَا تَسْجُدُوا۟ لِلشَّمْسِ وَلَا لِلْقَمَرِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the audience is confined to Sabaeans, and what historical and archaeological evidence establishes Arabian celestial worship.
- 41:37/q06/p1 Celestial worship concerned polytheists beyond the Sabaeans, including ancient and Arabian worshippers. — Abu Hayyan cites the Queen of Sheba's people in 27:24. Al-Biqa'i calls the sun a leading idol. Al-Tabataba'i says polytheists generally venerated sun and moon; Islahi says idolatrous peoples did so in every age. Kuran Yolu cites archaeology for a South Arabian moon-sun-Venus triad and its spread into the Hijaz; it cites Lat and Uzza as sun goddesses alongside Manat (53:19), Wadd as a moon god named in North Arabian inscriptions and 71:23, and servant-of-Wadd/sun names. It says Ibn Ashur's inability to establish contemporary Arab worship is no proof of its absence. Al-Burhan describes rising stars' splendor attracting idolaters, the setting sun's retreat, its rising and setting between Satan's horns, the people exposed to it, and repentance open until it rises from the East; it links 18:90. [11 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 41:37/q06/p2 The addressees are specifically the sun- and moon-worshipping Sabaeans. — Kuran Yolu reports this attribution to Zamakhshari, Razi, and Ibn Ashur but rejects limiting the audience to them; al-Tabataba'i says the restriction is reported. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Zemahşerî, Râzî, İbn Âşûr); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 41:37/q06/p3 Worship of sun and moon among Arabs of the Qur'an's time could not be established. — Kuran Yolu attributes this evidentiary judgment to Ibn Ashur but argues it does not establish absence, adducing archaeological, inscriptional, divine-name, and personal-name evidence. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr)]

## 41:37/q07 Why must prostration go to God instead of the sun and moon?  [لَا تَسْجُدُوا۟ لِلشَّمْسِ وَلَا لِلْقَمَرِ وَٱسْجُدُوا۟ لِلَّهِ · interpretation]
- 41:37/q07/p1 The luminaries are created, dependent servants and signs, while only their Creator deserves worship. — Muqatil and al-Tabari say creation and directed motion make them unable to benefit or harm independently; al-Tabari says God could extinguish their light. Al-Qushayri says loftiness, beauty and human benefit confer no divinity and God is jealous for devotion. Ibn Atiyya says benefit results from God's subjection; al-Tabrisi says they are not creators. Al-Razi calls prostration supreme reverence. Al-Qurtubi says God could erase them. Al-Baydawi, Bursevi, al-Alusi and al-Tabataba'i say they are creatures subject to command like humans. Ibn Kathir calls them the finest visible bodies yet servants; associating partners is unforgiven. Al-Biqa'i says God alone has perfection without defect and is self-sufficient; human dignity is belittled by bowing to a creature, while angels bowed to Adam and Iblis refused. Al-Qushayri likewise contrasts angels bowing to Adam with the prohibition to sinful humans and extends it to abasement before base desires and other people. The Mathnawi, quoted by Bursevi, calls the sun God's servant cooking for people, unable to cure its own darkness or provide refuge at night. Elmalili calls them ornaments of the lower heaven. Islahi says worshipping servants belittles both Creator and worshipper; Kuran Yolu says all besides God is created and unworthy. Asad says assigning real power to such phenomena is blasphemous. [26 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(المثنوي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 41:37/q08 Can worship through celestial or idol intermediaries count as worshipping God?  [لَا تَسْجُدُوا۟ لِلشَّمْسِ وَلَا لِلْقَمَرِ وَٱسْجُدُوا۟ لِلَّهِ · theology]
turns on: Whether a worship act directed to a created intermediary can be credited as worship of God; the verse's prohibition is used against the idolaters' claim.
- 41:37/q08/p1 No; claimed mediation still directs worship to another, whereas God requires undivided worship. — Al-Razi, al-Nasafi and Bursevi mention star-worshipping Sabaeans who claimed that prostrating to luminaries intended God. Al-Biqa'i cites idolaters' claim of nearness in 39:3 and their exclusive appeal to God at sea. Islahi reports Arabian idolaters' similar rationale, then says the wording refutes it and reserves distinctive acts to God. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 41:37/q08/p2 Yes; worshipping celestial bodies or idols was claimed to be a means of worshipping or approaching God. — Al-Razi, al-Nasafi and Bursevi report the Sabaean claim; al-Biqa'i cites the idolaters' statement in 39:3; Islahi reports Arabian idolaters treating such worship as equivalent to worship of God. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(idolaters of Arabia); ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]

## 41:37/q09 Could the sun serve as a direction for prayer?  [لِلشَّمْسِ · law]
- 41:37/q09/p1 No; its radiance and stature make prostration toward it liable to be mistaken for sun worship, while a stone can provide orientation without that suggestion. — Al-Razi proposes the possibility then rejects it on the risk of habituating sunward prostration; Bursevi repeats the distinction and adds the sun's benefits. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 41:37/q10 What does prostration signify in the command?  [وَٱسْجُدُوا۟ لِلَّهِ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes describe worship both by its prostrating outward act and by attribution of power.
- 41:37/q10/p1 It is worship's highest or clearest bodily expression, expressing humble obedience and exclusive devotion to God. — Al-Tabari defines worship as humbled obedience and dedication to God alone, comparing 2:172. Al-Razi calls prostration highest reverence; al-Baydawi and Elmalili call it worship's most specific form. Bursevi and al-Alusi call it its highest degree. Islahi calls it worship's most prominent outward manifestation. Al-Biqa'i says even one prostration to another makes worship shirk. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 41:37/q10/p2 Adoration here also means ascribing real power to a thing. — Asad says God alone is the source of existence, so conferring independent causal power on created phenomena, forces, circumstances or ideas is unreasonable and blasphemous. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 41:37/q11 How does “if you worship Him” qualify the command?  [إِن كُنتُمْ إِيَّاهُ تَعْبُدُونَ · interpretation]
- 41:37/q11/p1 True worship of God requires worshipping and prostrating to Him alone; worship of another is incompatible with it. — Al-Tabrisi and al-Qurtubi read the condition as an exclusive demand. Al-Nasafi says worshipping another beside God means one is not truly worshipping God. Al-Tabataba'i calls the two incompatible. Ibn Ashur reads the paired prohibition and command as restriction to God. Study Quran emphasizes the outward command. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 41:37/q12 To what does the feminine plural in خَلَقَهُنَّ refer?  [خَلَقَهُنَّ · referent]
turns on: The antecedent's scope and the grammatical warrant for feminine plural agreement, especially with four coordinated nouns rather than a single plural.
- 41:37/q12/p1 To the four named signs: night, day, sun, and moon. — Al-Majaz, al-Tabari, al-Zajjaj, al-Tha'labi, al-Baghawi, al-Tabrisi, al-Qurtubi and others invoke feminine agreement for nonrational or broken plurals, rather than masculine predominance; al-Majaz says mixed collective groups may take it. Ibn Atiyya cites 9:36 and a line of Hassan ibn Thabit and says a nonrational plural below ten may take feminine plural agreement. Al-Tabrisi illustrates with sheep; al-Nasafi with pens; al-Alusi reports al-Zamakhshari's logs. Al-Baydawi says the joint mention marks lack of knowledge or choice. Abu Hayyan and the Sheikh qualify al-Zamakhshari's account: four separate coordinated nouns are not literally one plural; the Sheikh treats them as functioning like one and distinguishes paucity and abundance plurals. Al-Samin replies that al-Zamakhshari was explaining why feminine agreement occurs despite three masculine nouns. Al-Alusi's report gives Abu Hayyan's small/large plural agreement distinction in the opposite stated direction from al-Samin's report. Bursevi describes a feminine singular pronoun for the four, while the cited form is feminine plural; his alternative is signs. Al-Biqa'i sees the feminine as lowering the luminaries' divine pretensions. [30 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,الشيخ); BURHAN(البغوي,قيل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,الزمخشري); ELMALILI(Zemahşerî); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 41:37/q12/p2 To the implied feminine plural āyāt, the signs. — Al-Zajjaj takes the listed entities as signs. Al-Tabrisi, al-Qurtubi, al-Nasafi, al-Zamakhshari as reported by Abu Hayyan and al-Samin, and Bursevi present this alternative; al-Samin says it naturally explains the feminine form. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(الزجاج); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 41:37/q12/p3 To the sun and moon treated collectively as a plural. — Ibn Atiyya notes the Arabic plurals shumūs and aqmār; al-Qurtubi reports the dual treated as plural. Abu Hayyan mentions their differing cycles; al-Samin and al-Alusi cite their collective forms. Al-Biqa'i proposes counting the moon as multiple moons through its crescent, full and dark phases. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 41:37/q12/p4 To the days mentioned earlier. — Ibn Atiyya reports this as one group's view. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]
- 41:37/q12/p5 To night, day, and moon. — Al-Razi states these three and appeals to feminine agreement for nonrational plurals and the feminine sense of 'among His signs.' [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 41:37/q12/p6 To the four signs and the other stars together. — Elmalili appeals to the statements that all swim in an orbit, citing 21:33 and 36:4 as recorded in the note. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 41:37/q13 Why call all four things created when the sun and moon are the prohibited objects?  [ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَهُنَّ · rhetoric]
- 41:37/q13/p1 Including all four underscores their complete dependence on God and their inability to merit worship. — Bursevi makes this point explicitly. Al-Biqa'i says God's creation of the four signs supports His sole claim to prostration and the nonrational feminine form underscores their lowliness as deities. [2 notes: BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 41:37/q14 Why is إِيَّاهُ placed before تَعْبُدُونَ?  [إِيَّاهُ تَعْبُدُونَ · rhetoric]
- 41:37/q14/p1 The placement preserves the cadence and sound pattern of the verse endings. — Al-Burhan gives the verse-ending pattern as the reason. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 41:37/q15 Where is the recitation prostration made?  [وَٱسْجُدُوا۟ لِلَّهِ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَهُنَّ إِن كُنتُمْ إِيَّاهُ تَعْبُدُونَ · law]
turns on: Whether the explicit prostration command and its condition complete the recitation point in verse 37, or the meaning completes in verse 38; one later-point argument invokes precaution.
- 41:37/q15/p1 At the end of verse 37, after تَعْبُدُونَ. — Al-Mawardi names Ibn Masud and al-Hasan; al-Tabrisi adds Abu Amr and the Imams. Al-Razi and al-Alusi attribute it to al-Shafi'i. Al-Qurtubi cites Malik, Ali and Ibn Masud and says the phrase remains connected to the command. Ibn Ashur specifies Malik's companions except Ibn Wahb, and reports Ali, Ibn Masud and al-Shafi'i. Al-Alusi reports the later point as precaution: delaying works if the earlier point is intended, while advancing does not work if the later is intended. Al-Qurtubi says consensus affirms a prostration verse, and Ibn al-Arabi calls the location dispute minor. [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود والحسن); TABRISI(ابن مسعود والحسن); RAZI-FULL(الشافعي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); ALUSI-FULL(الشافعي,علي وابن مسعود); IBNASHUR-FULL(الشافعي,علي بن أبي طالب وابن مسعود,مالك وأصحابه عدا ابن وهب); QURTUBI-FULL(مالك)+]
- 41:37/q15/p2 At the end of the following verse, where the sense is complete. — Al-Baydawi reports this as Abu Hanifa's placement and gives completion of meaning as its ground. Al-Qurtubi records a consensus that the passage contains recitation prostration while acknowledging dispute over location; Ibn al-Arabi treats the disagreement as minor. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)]

## 41:37/q16 Does this verse include the eclipse prayer?  [ٱلشَّمْسُ وَٱلْقَمَرُ · law]
- 41:37/q16/p1 Yes; it encompasses prayer at solar and lunar eclipses. — Ibn Khuwayz Mandad says Arabs linked eclipses to a great person's death, occasioning the Prophet's prayer. Al-Qurtubi confirms eclipse prayer in Bukhari and Muslim, notes variations in its reported forms, and treats Sahih Muslim as the authority on the issue. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن خويزمنداد)]

## 41:37/q17 What happened when the Prophet and believers prostrated?  [وَٱسْجُدُوا۟ لِلَّهِ · reports]
- 41:37/q17/p1 Meccan polytheists responded that they would worship al-Lat, al-Uzza, and Manat instead. — Muqatil reports this response after the Prophet and believers prostrated. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 41:37/q18 What reports are attached to the night, day, sun, and moon here?  [ٱلَّيْلُ وَٱلنَّهَارُ وَٱلشَّمْسُ وَٱلْقَمَرُ · reports]
turns on: The notes recount different events involving the named signs rather than variant versions of one event.
- 41:37/q18/p1 The Prophet forbade cursing night, day, sun, moon, and winds, since they may bring mercy to some and punishment to others. — Ibn Kathir transmits Jabir's report. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(جابر)]
- 41:37/q18/p2 At sunset the sun enters a sea beneath the Throne, glorifies God, and asks not to rise because people worship it. — Bursevi transmits Ikrima's report; God orders it to rise, and Hell is led against idolaters by thirteen thousand angels. [1 notes: BURSEVI(عكرمة)]

## 41:37/q19 What hadith connects prostration to freedom from pride?  [وَٱسْجُدُوا۟ لِلَّهِ · reports]
- 41:37/q19/p1 Placing one's forehead in prostration to God frees one from pride. — Bursevi quotes a hadith also saying faith in the Prophet's community remains firm even if deeds are shown off; he supplies no grading. [1 notes: BURSEVI(النبي)]

## 41:37/q20 Do the sun and moon obey God's command, and in what sense?  [ٱلشَّمْسُ وَٱلْقَمَرُ · theology]
turns on: Whether celestial obedience is only the created order's subjection or is also understood as universal spiritual obedience.
- 41:37/q20/p1 Their subjection is creational, since they lack knowledge and choice. — Bursevi attributes this explanation to outward interpreters; al-Baydawi says the joint mention shows the four are without knowledge or choice. [2 notes: BURSEVI(أهل الظاهر); BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 41:37/q20/p2 All creation is obedient in spiritual reality, and this obliges human seekers to obey too. — Bursevi attributes the alternative to the people of spiritual reality and invokes Sa'di's verse. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أهل الحقيقة)]

## 41:37/q21 What inward reading is given to these signs and the command?  [* · inward]
turns on: The inward readers give different symbolic mappings and practical emphases.
- 41:37/q21/p1 The night is the veiled lower self, day the spirit's light, sun the spirit, moon the heart, and true prostration annihilation in God's essence. — Al-Qashani says the self's darkness invites sins and satanic whispers; the spirit's light fosters good deeds and receives divine breezes. Stopping at the spirit veils the Real; stopping at the heart's virtues inclines one to the Paradise of attributes. Unveiled unity reserves worship for God. [4 notes: QASHANI]
- 41:37/q21/p2 The signs should lead the seeker to their Manifestor rather than become objects of attention in themselves. — Ibn Ata, reported by al-Sulami, says attention to signs distracts from Him, while attending to Him averts other distractions. Bursevi urges seeing God's workmanship with the heart's eye now, hoping by grace to see Him in the hereafter; he cites 30:50, 25:45 and 75:22. [2 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); BURSEVI]
- 41:37/q21/p3 The command calls one to overcome the lower self's turn toward worldly distraction and remember God. — Study Quran reports this inward interpretation alongside the outward command to worship God alone. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Aj, Q)]

## 41:37/q22 How does verse 37 connect to its surrounding passage and sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The commentators identify different levels of connection: the immediately preceding call, the earlier creation argument, and the syntax running into verse 38.
- 41:37/q22/p1 It follows the call to God and good action by adducing signs of God's essence, power, and wisdom. — Al-Razi says calling to God requires proof of His essence and attributes; Abu Hayyan similarly connects the best speech and action to cosmic signs; Asad reports al-Razi's link with 41:33. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Razi)]
- 41:37/q22/p2 It resumes the sūra's earlier creation and monotheism arguments, including resurrection, after intervening warnings and assurances. — Al-Biqa'i links prior idol criticism and the virtue of prayer to cosmic proofs dispelling rivals. Al-Tabataba'i says the passage returns to the original invitation after commending the call and answering well, arguing unity and resurrection through three signs. Islahi relates it to the sūra's opening and specifically to creation in 41:9-12; his section overview includes warnings against misleading others and the Qur'an's divine origin. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 41:37/q22/p3 Its prohibition and command interrupt the connection between the signs in verse 37 and the conditional clause of verse 38. — Ibn Ashur describes the insertion and cites 41:38. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
