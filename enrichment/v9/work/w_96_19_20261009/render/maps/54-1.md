# 54:1: 23 questions

## 54:1/q01 What does اقتربت mean?  [ٱقْتَرَبَتِ · meaning]
- 54:1/q01/p1 It means 'has drawn near', with the derived form intensifying the sense. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares iqtadara/qadara; Jishumī and Ibn ʿĀshūr explain the iftaʿala form. al-Māwardī cites poetic usage. [9 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 54:1/q02 What does ٱلسَّاعَةُ refer to?  [ٱلسَّاعَةُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the threat names final Judgment alone or also this community's nearer reckoning.
- 54:1/q02/p1 The Resurrection or final end of the world. — Muqātil lists Muhammad's coming, smoke and the moon's split as signs. Ibn Kathīr and Study Quran compare 16:1 and 21:1. [14 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(IK,JJ)]
- 54:1/q02/p2 The promised worldly punishment of the deniers. — Ibn ʿĀshūr suggests the familiar promised hour of destruction by Muslim swords; Iṣlāḥī relates it to al-Najm's near warning. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 54:1/q02/p3 Both the near worldly seizure and final Judgment. — Iṣlāḥī says each messenger's people face a worldly prelude to the final punishment. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 54:1/q03 How near is the Hour, and can its date be calculated?  [ٱقْتَرَبَتِ ٱلسَّاعَةُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether nearness is relative admonition or a measurable chronology, and the reliability of chronology reports.
- 54:1/q03/p1 Its time is unknown, but the remaining age is short relative to what has passed. — The Prophet compares his mission and the Hour to adjacent fingers, a day nearly spent, and a depleted pool. Ḥudhayfa urges preparation as a race. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ibn ʿĀshūr doubt world-age reports; Ibn Kathīr discusses chains and corroboration. Bursevī also cites warnings of false claimants and the Dajjāl, treating earlier impostors as precursors; his announcement urges obedience. [45 notes: TUSTARI(حذيفة بن اليمان); TAB-FULL(حذيفة,رجل); ZAJJAJ(حذيفة); SULAMI(عبد العزيز المكي); MAWARDI-FULL(النبي); QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أنس عن النبي,النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة عن أنس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس بن مالك عن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,أنس عن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,ابن عمر,حذيفة,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,سهل بن سعد عن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عتبة بن غزوان عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,وهب السوائي عن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام,حذيفة); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ELMALILI(Huzeyfe); STUDYQURAN(Prophet); THALABI(حذيفة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(حذيفة)]
- 54:1/q03/p2 Every coming event is near to God, and the cosmic sign makes the end intellectually near. — Bursevī cites 70:6–7; Asad distinguishes divine from human time; Ibn ʿĀshūr treats possibility as a form of nearness. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 54:1/q03/p3 Reports or calculations assign the world a lifespan of thousands or hundreds of thousands of years. — Kaʿb and Wahb say six thousand, with 5,600 elapsed; Bursevī relays seven- and seventy-thousand-year schemes and proposes 360,000 from a Virgo cycle, while conceding the age is not agreed. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(كعب ووهب); BURSEVI(ابن عباس,النبي عليه السلام)]

## 54:1/q04 Why is the Resurrection called the Hour?  [ٱلسَّاعَةُ · meaning]
turns on: The feature of an hour that motivates the name.
- 54:1/q04/p1 Its events or reckoning move swiftly. — al-Māwardī and Bursevī give speed as the naming motive. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 54:1/q04/p2 It comes in the final hour of its day or of the world. — al-Māwardī gives an hour of the Day; Bursevī the world's final hour. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 54:1/q04/p3 A brief interval contains an immense event. — Bursevī explains the time-word by the great event it contains. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 54:1/q05 How do the feminine ending and two clauses work?  [ٱقْتَرَبَتِ وَٱنشَقَّ · grammar]
turns on: Phonology of the feminine suffix and whether conjunction encodes event order.
- 54:1/q05/p1 The feminine tāʾ takes kasra to avoid adjacent sukūns. — al-Naḥḥās attributes this to the Basrans, who treat it as a normally silent particle. [1 notes: NAHHAS]
- 54:1/q05/p2 The feminine third-person tāʾ is silent because other person forms occupy the available vowels. — al-Naḥḥās attributes the account to Kufans. [1 notes: NAHHAS]
- 54:1/q05/p3 The moon split first, and the Hour drew near afterward; wāw does not impose temporal order. — Ibn Kaysān and al-Farrāʾ infer an inverted event sequence; Ibn ʿĀshūr says wāw alone does not order the events. [5 notes: FARRA; THALABI(ابن كيسان); JISHUMI(ابن كيسان); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن كيسان)]
- 54:1/q05/p4 The clauses are coordinated, with no change of sense from their order. — al-Naḥḥās calls the second clause coordinated; al-Farrāʾ says reversal does not alter sense. [2 notes: NAHHAS; FARRA]
- 54:1/q05/p5 The feminine verbal form itself signals God's great power over the Hour. — al-Biqāʿī reads the form rhetorically. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 54:1/q06 Is there a reading with qad before ٱنشَقَّ?  [وَٱنشَقَّ · readings]
- 54:1/q06/p1 Ḥudhayfa reads wa-qad inshaqqa al-qamar, explicitly presenting the split as completed. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya also notes a version without wāw; Ibn Jinnī says qad confirms an anticipated event. al-Ālūsī construes the clause circumstantially. [13 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(حذيفة); JISHUMI(حذيفة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(حذيفة); QURTUBI-FULL(حذيفة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(حذيفة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(حذيفة); IBNASHUR-FULL(حذيفة بن اليمان); ELMALILI(Huzeyfe); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل)]

## 54:1/q07 What does the verb ٱنشَقَّ describe?  [ٱنشَقَّ ٱلْقَمَرُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether splitting strictly entails two detached halves.
- 54:1/q07/p1 The moon divided into visible portions. — al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī glosses two segments; Abū Ḥayyān calls it a widening fissure. [2 notes: TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 54:1/q07/p2 Strictly, a literal split is a surface fissure; two detached halves are a metonymic extension. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares cracks in wood and walls, and treats loss of light as metaphor. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

Ibn Kathīr calls the reports sound and widespread. Ibn ʿĀshūr accepts the historical miracle while exploring another referent for this verse. - 54:1/q08/p2 It predicts a future split at the Resurrection, with past tense expressing certainty. — Attributed to ʿUthmān ibn ʿAṭāʾ from his father, al-Ḥasan, ʿAṭāʾ and al-Balkhī. al-Ḥasan locates it after the second trumpet. ## 54:1/q09 How many times did the moon separate?  [وَٱنشَقَّ ٱلْقَمَرُ · reports]

## 54:1/q11 What prompted the sign, and did the verse precede or follow it?  [وَٱنشَقَّ ٱلْقَمَرُ · reports]
turns on: Chronology of revelation and whether the request named the moon.
- 54:1/q11/p1 Meccan unbelievers requested a sign, saw the split, called it magic, heard travelers confirm it, and then the verse was revealed. — Some requested the moon's split specifically and promised belief; Rāzī says the Prophet prayed. - 54:1/q11/p2 The verse came first, forecasting a lunar sign as the Hour approached. — Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers this on 54:2's wording, while accepting a historical miracle. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 54:1/q11/p3 The sign need not have been specifically requested. — Abū Ḥayyān mentions a request for a sign generally; Iṣlāḥī says signs can appear without an announced challenge. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 54:1/q13 What does the split teach?  [وَٱنشَقَّ ٱلْقَمَرُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the sign confirms prophethood, foretells cosmic change, or symbolizes the deniers' fate.
- 54:1/q13/p1 It confirms the Messenger and shows God's power over celestial bodies and the coming Hour. — Qatāda invokes God's will; Biqāʿī says Arabs depended on lunar signs and the effortless rupture warns them; Iṣlāḥī says no cosmic body is immortal. Ibn ʿĀshūr links it with 75:7–8 and the world's end; Elmalılı calls the Hour intellectually near. [25 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(حذيفة); TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Elmalılı,İbn Âşûr)]
- 54:1/q13/p2 It principally portends the Resurrection; foretelling it is the prophetic miracle. — Kuranyolu reports Shāh Walī Allāh's distinction. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şah Veliyyullah)]
- 54:1/q13/p3 It symbolizes Arab polytheism's fracture and doom. — Kuranyolu reports Doğrul's symbolic addition to a historical sighting. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Ömer Rıza Doğrul)]

## 54:1/q14 Can the physical and scriptural objections to the miracle be answered?  [وَٱنشَقَّ ٱلْقَمَرُ · theology]
turns on: Whether physical rarity or verses on withheld signs exclude the event.
- 54:1/q14/p1 A real split is possible by God's will; lack of global records or a known mechanism proves no impossibility. — Ṭabrisī and Fennī mention clouds, sleep, horizons and inattention; Rāzī invokes Qurʾanic testimony and astrologers' eclipse explanations. al-Ālūsī answers sound, gravity and reunion objections; Elmalılı critiques immutable celestial matter and lists possible mechanisms; Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes brief visibility and absent observatories. [43 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الفلاسفة); ELMALILI(Fennî,İbn Sînâ); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(gelecekteki yoruma karşı çıkanlar); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 54:1/q14/p2 A cosmic event should have been universally seen and recorded, and physical rupture and reunion are implausible. — al-Ālūsī reports objections from philosophers and skeptics about records, devastating sound and lost attraction; future-reading advocates cite young transmitters and disputed chains. [7 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الفلاسفة,بعض الملاحدة); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bu görüşü savunanlar); ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 54:1/q14/p3 Qurʾan 17:59 and 17:90–93 indicate requested signs were withheld. — The objection recalls previous peoples destroyed after denying signs. Ṭabāṭabāʾī replies that the Prophet's presence and believers delayed punishment until after Hijra and Badr, and a messenger produces signs only by God's leave. [3 notes: TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bu görüşü savunanlar); TABATABAI-]
- 54:1/q14/p4 The withholding verses allow the sign and delayed punishment; only God grants miracles. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī uses 8:33–35, 17:76, 48:25 and 13:38, reviewing Meccan believers, Hijra, conversions and Badr; Elmalılı cites 13:38 and 40:78. [18 notes: TABATABAI; ELMALILI]

## 54:1/q15 How well attested is the splitting report?  [وَٱنشَقَّ ٱلْقَمَرُ · reports]
turns on: Whether multiple sound routes satisfy technical mass transmission.
- 54:1/q15/p1 It has many sound routes, which some judge mass transmitted. — Sources name Ibn Masʿūd, Jubayr, Anas, Ibn ʿUmar, Ibn ʿAbbās, Ḥudhayfa and ʿAlī; Ibn Ḥajar says converging routes bring certainty; Sharḥ al-Mawāqif and al-Subkī use tawātur. Some reporters were young or absent but transmit validly. [30 notes: ZAJJAJ; QUSHAYRI(أنس وابن عمر وحذيفة وابن عباس وجبير بن مطعم,أهل التفسير,ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود وجبير بن مطعم وعبد الله بن عمر وأنس وابن عباس وحذيفة بن اليمان); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(المفسرون بأسرهم,جمع من الصحابة); QURTUBI-FULL(أنس,ابن مسعود وابن عمر وأنس وجبير بن مطعم وابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(ابن مسعود وأنس,القشيري); BURSEVI(ابن حجر,الطيبي,شرح الشريف للمواقف); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Ashâb, tâbi‘în ve müteahhirîn müfessirlerinin umûmu,Seyyid Şerîf,Sübkî); TABATABAI(السيد الشريف وابن السبكي)]
- 54:1/q15/p2 Its technical tawātur and the certainty of particular details remain disputed. — Saʿdī al-Muftī compares the report with the better-attested warning against fabrication; al-Ālūsī notes disagreement, while Kuranyolu and Elmalılı resist making contested details a definitive article of faith. [5 notes: BURSEVI(سعدي المفتي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bu görüşü savunanlar)]

## 54:1/q16 Which added miracle details are contested?  [وَٱنشَقَّ ٱلْقَمَرُ · reports]
turns on: Authenticity of details beyond the core splitting.
- 54:1/q16/p1 The parts briefly separated and rejoined while staying in the sky. — Muqātil says the halves returned; al-Ālūsī rejects the sleeve tale as fabricated and the finger-pointing detail as lacking a sound report. [4 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); ALUSI-FULL(بدر الدين الزركشي عن شيخه العماد بن كثير)]
- 54:1/q16/p2 The moon entered the Prophet's sleeve and emerged from his garment. — al-Ālūsī and Ibn Kathīr, as cited by al-Zarkashī, dismiss it as baseless fabrication. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بدر الدين الزركشي عن شيخه العماد بن كثير)-]
- 54:1/q16/p3 The Prophet pointed at the moon to split it. — Kuranyolu and Study Quran report the gesture; al-Ālūsī finds no sound report of it. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İslâm âlimlerinin çoğunluğu); STUDYQURAN(most commentators); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 54:1/q16/p4 Two moons appeared over al-Ṣafā and al-Marwa after Jews requested the sign. — al-Ālūsī calls this al-Ḍaḥḥāk route unreliable, in conflict with reports naming Meccan opponents. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)-]

## 54:1/q17 What inward readings are offered?  [وَٱنشَقَّ ٱلْقَمَرُ · inward]
turns on: Whether the symbolic moon is the heart, body, spiritual epoch or a field of perception.
- 54:1/q17/p1 The moon is the two-sided heart, split by spiritual light as witnessing and annihilation in unity approach. — Qāshānī contrasts its dark soul-facing and bright spirit-facing sides. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 54:1/q17/p2 The moon is the body, receiving sensation and life from spirit. — Qāshānī compares it with lunar light from the sun for the minor resurrection. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 54:1/q17/p3 The split figures Muhammad's manifestation in the Mahdī's era or the Perfect Human's movement between unseen and witnessed realms. — Qāshānī gives the era reading. Ibn ʿArabī's crescent, full moon and hidden moon symbolize stations of spiritual perception; Elmalılı says he also narrates an actual split. [8 notes: QASHANI; ELMALILI(Muhyiddîn-i Arabî,Şeyh)]
- 54:1/q17/p4 The opening invocation illuminates hearts and eyes and gives spirits joy. — al-Qushayrī reads it as divine majesty, favor and kindness. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 54:1/q17/p5 The basmala introduces divine knowledge, power, mercy and favor. — al-Biqāʿī reads its language as encompassing both the wretched and the fortunate, with perfected blessing for God's chosen. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 54:1/q18 How does the opening connect with al-Najm and the sūra's argument?  [* · links]
- 54:1/q18/p1 It continues 53:57's near warning and extends celestial imagery from stars to moon. — Jishumī, Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, Biqāʿī, Asad, Iṣlāḥī and Study Quran make the link; Biqāʿī traces mounting warnings in nearby sūras and earlier prophetic stories, while Elmalılı contrasts knowledge in al-Najm with divine will in the moon sign. [19 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر ابن الزبير); ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES(Razi); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Āl, Sy)]
- 54:1/q18/p2 The opening launches a warning that unfolds through rejected signs, destroyed peoples, Judgment and reward. — al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Study Quran outline the progression; Iṣlāḥī contrasts sensory demands with the Book's warning. [4 notes: TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 54:1/q19 Where was the sūra revealed and how many verses does it contain?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether Badr or selected later verses modify the Meccan classification.
- 54:1/q19/p1 It is Meccan and contains fifty-five verses. — Jishumī, Bayḍāwī, al-Suyūṭī, Bursevī and others give the count; Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn al-Zubayr transmit Meccan origin; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī appeals to context. [8 notes: JISHUMI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس وابن الزبير); BURSEVI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(most commentators)]
- 54:1/q19/p2 It was revealed at Badr. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects it from context. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 54:1/q19/p3 It is Meccan apart from verse 45 or verses 44–46. — Abū Ḥayyān attributes the latter to Muqātil; Study Quran reports both exceptions; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī dismisses them. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل); STUDYQURAN(most commentators); TABATABAI-]

## 54:1/q20 Why is it called al-Qamar or Iqtarabat al-sāʿah?  [* · sciences]
- 54:1/q20/p1 The name comes from the moon in verse 1, and the opening words form another title. — Biqāʿī connects al-Qamar with al-Najm's foreshadowed sign and says a plain 'Splitting' title would suggest the sky. Ubayy's codex places al-Qamar after al-Muddaththir and before al-Dukhān. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ITQAN(أبو جعفر الكوفي)]
- 54:1/q20/p2 When اقتربت is a proper title, its initial alif becomes hamzat al-qaṭʿ. — Sībawayh compares a man named iḍrib; his wider discussion covers omitted sūrah, feminine proper names, indeclinable letter names, compounds and poetic usage. [11 notes: SIBAWAYH(بعضهم)]

## 54:1/q21 What virtues and liturgical uses are reported?  [* · sciences]
- 54:1/q21/p1 Recitation is said to yield a bright full-moon face and other protection or rank. — Jishumī reports Ubayy's morning/night promise without grading. al-Suyūṭī includes a Torah 'whitening' report graded munkar by al-Bayhaqī, every-other-night reports, and a report grouping four sūras. [5 notes: JISHUMI(النبي); DURR-FULL(إسحق بن عبد الله بن أبي فروة,البيهقي عن ابن عباس,عائشة,ليث عن معن عن شيخ من همدان)]
- 54:1/q21/p2 The Prophet recited al-Qamar with Qāf at Eid and other gatherings. — al-Suyūṭī cites Muslim; Study Quran mentions both feasts and morning prayer. A Muʿādh prayer report advises shorter sūras, naming al-Shams. [4 notes: ITQAN; DURR-FULL(بريدة); STUDYQURAN(Prophet,the Prophet)]

## 54:1/q22 What formal features does the opening illustrate?  [* · rhetoric]
- 54:1/q22/p1 It is a declarative opening with balanced medium cadence and matching end sound. — al-Zarkashī uses it for sūra openings, cadence, sound pattern and word order; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ compares agent-omitting reflexive constructions for cosmic events. [6 notes: BURHAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 54:1/q22/p2 Its finite verb is compared with other conditional and temporal constructions. — al-Zarkashī and al-Suyūṭī compare 84:1's idhā with this verse and 16:1. The wider discussion covers conditional divorce, jussives, repetition, immediacy, and an omitted apodosis. [11 notes: BURHAN(أبو جعفر ابن الزبير,ابن عصفور,ابن مالك); ITQAN(قيل)]
- 54:1/q22/p3 Prophetic recitation of sūras supports a fixed verse order within them. — al-Suyūṭī names many recited sūras and Ibn Khuzayma's mass-transmission claim, while noting the report of al-Tawba's closing verses as a complication. [12 notes: ITQAN(الحارث بن خزيمة,حذيفة)]

## 54:1/q23 What order or relative dating is reported for the sūra?  [* · sciences]
- 54:1/q23/p1 Reported codex and revelation orders differ. — Ubayy's codex puts it after al-Muddaththir and before al-Dukhān; al-Jaʿbarī places it after al-Ṭāriq among Meccan revelations. al-Suyūṭī questions another sequence; Asad guesses al-Najm and this sūra arose near one another, perhaps the fourth year. [5 notes: ITQAN(أبو جعفر الكوفي,البرهان الجعبري); ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]
