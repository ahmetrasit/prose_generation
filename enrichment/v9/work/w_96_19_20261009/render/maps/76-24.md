# 76:24: 22 questions

## 76:24/q01 What patience does the Prophet's command require?  [فَٱصْبِرْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the decree is explained as submission to divine judgment, sustained duty and suffering, delayed victory, or an inward trial; several explanations overlap.
- 76:24/q01/p1 Patiently accept God's decree and submit to it. — Muqatil returns to the earlier command to be patient with God's judgment; al-Thaʿlabi, al-Baghawi, and al-Qurtubi gloss it as patience with His decree. Al-Qushayri adds acceptance and submission, with obedience to the Lord alone. Ibn Kathir relates it to God's gift of the Qur'an and trust in His protection. Al-Biqaʿi includes what God has decreed for opponents and for the Prophet, explaining that a compassionate nurturer educates through hardship and ease. Al-Biqaʿi defines patience as steady restraint of the self against desire and blameworthy pleasures. [8 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 76:24/q01/p2 Persevere in the imposed duties, worship, delivery of revelation, and the hardships and opposition that accompany them. — Al-Tabari lists tests, obligations, delivery of messages, and revelation, comparing 68:48. Al-Nahhas emphasizes enduring opponents' harm, noting Qatada's Abu Jahl report. Al-Razi lists worship, preaching, and conveying the message; al-Qurtubi reports patience in prescribed obedience. Al-Nasafi joins message delivery, harm, and waiting for victory. Elmalılı adds toil and struggle, with trust in a good outcome. Iṣlāḥī couples perseverance with prayer and remembrance; Kur'an Yolu says worship and obedience fortify resolve against deniers. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 76:24/q01/p3 Wait patiently for God's judgment or promised victory, without rushing while success and permission to fight are delayed. — Al-Razi compares waiting until God judges in 7:87 and reports delayed permission to fight. Al-Qurtubi says promised victory will surely come. Al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Bursavī, and al-Ālūsī mention delayed victory over Meccan unbelievers; Bursavī says retaliation should not be rushed and each matter has its appointed time. Elmalılı adds awaiting future judgment and a good end. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 76:24/q01/p4 In an inward reading, endure the disclosure of divine unity through annihilation while the ego's residue is tried. — Al-Qāshānī says that at the descent of attributes the Lord is the Essence alone. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 76:24/q02 What range of meaning does حُكْمِ رَبِّكَ carry?  [لِحُكْمِ رَبِّكَ · meaning]
- 76:24/q02/p1 It encompasses God's judgment or decree and His wisdom. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says حكم can signify wisdom or judgment, making this wording deliberately broad; he cites 26:21, 19:12, and 40:48. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 76:24/q03 What conduct does the prohibition on obedience cover?  [وَلَا تُطِعْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the wording is understood as forbidding compliance with sinful demands specifically or as a practical refusal of these polytheists' authority generally.
- 76:24/q03/p1 Do not yield to calls to sin, disbelief, or abandoning the mission; obedience in a neutral matter is outside this ban. — Al-Tabari says the Prophet must not disobey God at a polytheist's bidding. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī, and Bursavī tie the ban to what the person invites him to do, expressly allowing a request that is neither sinful nor unbelieving. Al-Zamakhsharī, as reported by al-Samīn, distinguishes invitations to sin, disbelief, and neither although all the addressees disbelieved. Ibn Kathīr specifies attempts to turn him from revelation while he conveys it under God's protection. Qushayrī says obey the Lord alone. Al-Ālūsī says the attributes supply the reason for the ban. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 76:24/q03/p2 Its practical effect is to obey none of the polytheists. — Ibn ʿĀshūr derives this practical sense from the partitive منهم and its reference to the polytheists. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 76:24/q04 Who are 'them' in مِنْهُمْ?  [مِنْهُمْ · referent]
- 76:24/q04/p1 The Meccan polytheists and other unbelieving opponents who press the Prophet to yield. — Muqatil depicts people devoted to worldly life, possessions, and material goods. Al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī identify the Meccan polytheists; al-Biqāʿī and Bursavī call them unbelievers, contrasted with the grateful. Ibn ʿĀshūr derives the polytheist referent from preaching or implied opposition in the preceding passage, citing 76:23; he calls من partitive. [7 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAGHAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 76:24/q05 How does مِنْهُمْ attach grammatically?  [مِنْهُمْ · grammar]
- 76:24/q05/p1 It is a circumstantial adjunct to آثِمًا, formed by advancing a phrase that would describe that indefinite noun. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls من partitive and links its pronoun to the polytheists inferred from the surrounding preaching context. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 76:24/q06 What does آثِمًا describe?  [ءَاثِمًا · meaning]
- 76:24/q06/p1 A person who commits sin or wrongdoing, including one who calls another to sin. — Ibn Zayd calls him a wrongdoing offender. Al-Tabari contrasts the sinner's wrongdoing with denial of blessings. Al-Razi cites 4:48, 2:283, 6:120, and 2:219 for idolatry, concealed testimony, hidden or open sin, and wine and gambling. Al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, Bursavī, and Elmalılı emphasize the invitation to sin; al-Biqāʿī says the sin need not be joined to disbelief. Al-Sāmarrāʾī includes outward and inward sin, citing 6:120. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 76:24/q07 Why use آثِمًا here rather than the intensive أَثِيم?  [ءَاثِمًا · rhetoric]
- 76:24/q07/p1 The active participle includes even the lesser sinner, so a more intensely sinful person is excluded a fortiori. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts the active participle آثم with intensive أثيم. He says al-Qalam's 68:10–13 uses أثيم amid several intensive traits: each act makes its doer sinful and their combination makes the intensive form fitting. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 76:24/q08 What does كَفُورًا describe?  [كَفُورًۭا · meaning]
- 76:24/q08/p1 An unbeliever who denies God's favor, often with an intensive sense, and calls others to disbelief. — Al-Tabari says he denies favors and worships another; al-Māwardī also contrasts denial of blessings with sin. Al-Razi says every disbeliever is sinful but not every sinner disbelieves, and worshiping another combines both descriptions. Al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, Bursavī, and Elmalılı describe invitation to disbelief; al-Biqāʿī calls him emphatically unbelieving and says worldly greatness cannot outweigh truth. Ibn Kathīr places this ingratitude in the heart and sinful acts in deeds. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the form intensifies disbelief or denial of blessings, citing 22:66, 43:15, and 76:3. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 76:24/q09 Why mention both the sinner and the ingrate?  [ءَاثِمًا كَفُورًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the labels are read as moral categories or as inward states of veiling.
- 76:24/q09/p1 The two descriptions cover sin and the graver or more emphatic disbelief, and implicitly censure those so described. — Al-Razi says kafūr is the wickedest kind of sinner, named to mark the extreme and distance from God; elsewhere he notes that all unbelievers are sinful, but not conversely. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the pair bars both classes. Bursavī distinguishes ordinary sin from the specific worst sin of disbelief, and observes that a sinner can be a follower and an unbeliever a leader. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the names censure the men and allude to their offers and stories; Ibn Kathīr contrasts deeds and heart. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 76:24/q09/p2 In an inward reading, the sinner is veiled by qualities or self, and the unbeliever by acts and their effects. — Al-Qāshānī says following such people perpetuates their veils; Bursavī reports the same distinction and says agreement would veil the seeker. [2 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(القاشاني)]

## 76:24/q10 Who are the sinner and the ingrate in this verse?  [ءَاثِمًا كَفُورًۭا · referent]
turns on: Whether the indefinite labels are general, denote one person, or point to two specific people; named traditions reverse the assignment of ʿUtba and al-Walīd, and al-Razi disputes the hypocrite division.
- 76:24/q10/p1 Any sinner and any unbeliever; the descriptions are general rather than confined to named men. — Al-Razi calls this closer to the apparent wording, although another note of his prefers the single-person identification on Qur'anic evidence. Al-Nasafī likewise prefers the apparent general sense. Al-Bayḍāwī describes any person urging sin or unbelief; Study Quran reports this wider possibility. Al-Razi says the sinner is a broad class and the unbeliever a narrower one. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(JJ); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 76:24/q10/p2 Abu Jahl bears both labels, or is the unbeliever singled out by the warning. — Qatāda links him to a threat to trample the Prophet's neck for praying; Ibn Jurayj transmits the same threat. Ibn Zayd and Qatāda take the two words as one person's descriptions. Al-Razi prefers a single-person identification for Qur'anic evidence, though elsewhere prefers the general reading. Al-Ālūsī reports the narrower version that calls Abu Jahl the unbeliever. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 76:24/q10/p3 ʿUtba ibn Rabīʿa is the sinner and al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra the ingrate. — Muqatil and later reports connect ʿUtba with an offer of a daughter or marriage, sometimes expressly without dowry, and al-Walīd with money or enough wealth to satisfy the Prophet. Al-Kashshāf, as cited by al-Razi, calls ʿUtba sinful for many transgressions and al-Walīd extremely unbelieving. Ibn ʿĀshūr connects the wealthy al-Walīd to 74:12. Study Quran identifies the two leaders without adding their roles in its first note. [14 notes: THALABI(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي، مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(JJ,JJ, Ṭs); IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل)]
- 76:24/q10/p4 Al-Walīd is the sinner and ʿUtba the ingrate. — Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Razi report the reversal. Al-Qaffāl supports al-Walīd as sinner because 68:10 and 68:12 call him athīm. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL(القفال)]
- 76:24/q10/p5 The sinner is a hypocrite and the ingrate an openly unbelieving Arab polytheist. — Al-Ḥasan's division is reported by al-Razi; al-Qurtubi and al-Ālūsī also report hypocrite versus open unbeliever. Al-Razi rejects the division as weak because sinner is general and disbeliever is narrower. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]
- 76:24/q10/p6 Al-Walīd is the sinner and Abū al-Bakhtarī ibn Hishām the ingrate, with ʿUtba also associated with the latter label. — This is Muqatil's named identification; his accompanying Dar al-Nadwa report assigns wealth to al-Walīd and a bride to Abū al-Bakhtarī. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 76:24/q11 What does أَوْ contribute to the prohibition?  [أَوْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether أو keeps its ordinary alternative sense with universal scope supplied by prohibition, or is construed as واو or لا; the notes debate whether واو would forbid each individual or only the pair together.
- 76:24/q11/p1 It retains ordinary alternation; under a prohibition each alternative is independently barred, hence neither alone nor both may be obeyed. — Ibn al-Jawzī lists ordinary uses for doubt (Zayd or ʿAmr) and choice (a coin or its silver value). Sībawayh says the negative command binds both objects, while repeating لا would change the sense. Al-Zajjāj and many careful scholars say أو is stronger than واو here, since conjunction could suggest only jointly obeying both is forbidden; al-Wāḥidī uses Zayd, ʿAmr, al-Ḥasan, and Ibn Sīrīn to explain. Al-Samīn reports Abū al-Baqāʾ's speech analogy and al-Zamakhsharī's a fortiori analogy with saying uff or striking parents. Al-Burhān explains that negation of an indefinite supplies universality, not an altered lexical meaning; Abū al-Baqāʾ says doing both doubles the breach, and Sībawayh contrasts permission. Al-Ṭībī and al-Ālūsī prefer this account. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the single member coordinated by أو has contextual senses such as choice, permission, division, and doubt; doing both entails doing one. Al-Itqān says pronoun agreement remains singular with أو, unlike واو. Al-Razi disputes one inference from banning one to banning both, giving a master's servant example; Ibn al-Ḥājib raises the objection that avoiding only one might seem enough. Al-Sāmarrāʾī raises the wording question and answers that أو bars either separately. [32 notes: SIBAWAYH; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج وأكثر المحققين); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,الزمخشري,سيبويه); BURHAN(أبو البقاء,سيبويه,قيل); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(أبو البقاء); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن الحاجب,الزجاج,جار الله); IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; ALUSI-FULL+; BURHAN+; ITQAN(الطيبي)+; NASAFI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL-; BURHAN(ابن الحاجب)-]
- 76:24/q11/p2 أو functions like واو: the command says not to obey the sinner and the ingrate. — Abū ʿUbayda, the Kufans, and several commentators take it as conjunction rather than choice. Al-Farrāʾ's poetic example ends in an أو meaning nor; his other example promises to give whether asked or not, showing an inclusive use. Al-Thaʿlabī treats the alif as added. Ibn al-Jawzī compares 6:146; his lexicon cites 'perhaps he will remember and fear.' Al-Burhān records the argument that avoiding just one would not comply, while al-Khaṭībī there prefers the ordinary sense. Al-Nahhas expressly rejects identifying أو with واو because their scope differs in a prohibition concerning two people. [16 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(الفراء); THALABI(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عبيدة); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAGHAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الكوفيون); BURHAN(قيل); ITQAN(غيره); ALUSI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); NAHHAS-]
- 76:24/q11/p3 In this negative construction, أو functions like لا or ولا: obey neither one who sins nor one who disbelieves. — Al-Farrāʾ says this sense occurs in negations, interrogations, and conditions and cites an unnamed poet's final أو meaning ولا. Al-Nahhas's Abū Jaʿfar accepts this analysis in those contexts and rejects the واو substitution. Al-Farrāʾ also illustrates with a promise to give whether asked or silent. Al-Razi reports his paraphrase as obey none of them whether sinful or unbelieving. [10 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(الفراء); NAHHAS(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; NAHHAS+]

## 76:24/q12 Why state the duties and prohibition separately, and warn a protected Prophet?  [* · rhetoric]
- 76:24/q12/p1 Explicit positive duties followed by the prohibition clarify guidance already implicit in patience, and repetition warns others against desires and doubts. — Al-Razi says patience already includes the later ban and remembrance by implication, but spelling out duties then prohibitions adds clarity. He and Bursavī say the Prophet would not obey, yet people need repeated guidance against corrupt desires, heedlessness, doubts, and appetites. Al-Ālūsī says the Prophet's protection from sin makes this a lesson for others to seek God's help and refuge. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the two labels allude to the men's offers and censure them. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 76:24/q13 Does this verse rule out prayer behind a sinful imam?  [وَلَا تُطِعْ ءَاثِمًا · law]
- 76:24/q13/p1 No; it forbids following someone in sin, while following him in a neutral act such as leading prayer is outside its scope. — Al-Ālūsī explicitly applies his distinction between sinful invitations and neutral acts to prayer behind a sinful imam. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says more generally that the wording also forbids a leader to obey a sinner or unbeliever. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 76:24/q14 What occasions are reported for the command?  [* · reports]
turns on: Different reported events are attached to the passage: Abu Jahl's prayer threat, opponents' offers, and feeding narratives; the offers accounts themselves vary in people and terms.
- 76:24/q14/p1 Abu Jahl threatened to trample Muhammad's neck if he saw him praying, and the verse followed. — Qatāda transmits this account in ʿAbd al-Razzāq, al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Durr; Ibn Jurayj also transmits it. Al-Nahhas links enduring their harm to this threat. Al-Durr places it after prayer was prescribed in Mecca; some abbreviated reports say only that the verse concerns Abu Jahl. [12 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); NAHHAS; THALABI(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL]
- 76:24/q14/p2 Meccan opponents offered marriage and wealth if Muhammad abandoned his mission or preaching; he refused, and the verse followed. — Muqatil places a meeting at Dar al-Nadwa: al-Walīd offered half his wealth, Abū al-Bakhtarī a bride, and a third man protection; the Prophet laughed and left. Other reports put ʿUtba's daughter or marriage, sometimes without dowry, opposite al-Walīd's money. Al-Razi says the Prophet recited the opening of Fuṣṣilat and 41:13, leaving them fearful; Kur'an Yolu cites his refusal and 41:1–3, and adds offers of status and prestige. Al-Biqāʿī reports wealth, control, and marriage to eminent women amid severe harm; the accounts vary in names and offers. [16 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي، مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(عتبة بن ربيعة); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN(JJ, Ṭs); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(tefsirlerde)]
- 76:24/q14/p3 A report connects the verse with Ibn al-Dahdah's act of feeding. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this as another view and leaves the matter to God. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 76:24/q14/p4 The passage is connected with ʿAlī's successive meals for a needy person, an orphan, and a prisoner. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes this narrative alongside the view that verse 24 is Meccan and the rest Medinan; his note does not equate the meals with the Abu Jahl or offers reports. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن، عكرمة)]

## 76:24/q15 Where was this verse or its surrounding sūra revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the sūra came wholly in one locale or has a Meccan exception, and whether the exception is verse 24 alone or the closing section; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī disputes using patience alone to date it.
- 76:24/q15/p1 The whole sūra is Meccan, including this verse. — Al-Jishumī reports a majority view and the qāḍī's Meccan judgment while noting disagreement; ʿAṭāʾ is cited by al-Baghawī, and Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes this to some commentators. [3 notes: JISHUMI(القاضي); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المفسرين)]
- 76:24/q15/p2 The whole sūra is Medinan, including this verse. — Mujāhid and Qatāda are repeatedly cited for a wholly Medinan sūra; al-Jishumī also notes a general Medinan view. [5 notes: JISHUMI(القاضي,مجاهد، وقتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد، قتادة); ITQAN]
- 76:24/q15/p3 This verse is Meccan while the rest of the sūra is Medinan. — Al-Ḥasan and ʿIkrima hold this in al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, and Abū Ḥayyān. Ibn ʿAṭiyya also mentions the narrative of ʿAlī's meals. Al-Suyūṭī's Itqān notes the exception without settling the sūra's overall classification. [5 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن وعكرمة); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن وعكرمة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن، عكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن وعكرمة); ITQAN]
- 76:24/q15/p4 The closing section beginning at verse 24 is Meccan, while the earlier sūra is Medinan. — Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Māwardī's formulation from 'be patient' onward; Ibn ʿĀshūr attributes a similar closing-passage view to al-Ḥasan, ʿIkrima, and al-Kalbī, but says they name no earlier sūra in which it was recited. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes a later self-contained section possibly Meccan and the household passage Medinan; he rejects the inference that patience by itself proves a Meccan date even if the sūra descended at once. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الماوردي); IBNASHUR-FULL(الحسن وعكرمة والكلبي); TABATABAI; TABATABAI-]

## 76:24/q16 Was this sūra revealed in installments?  [* · sciences]
- 76:24/q16/p1 Yes; this sūra and its verses came in installments, unlike other scriptures. — Al-Wāḥidī reports al-Kalbī's statement and says Muqatil agrees. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي)]

## 76:24/q17 Was the patience command abrogated?  [فَٱصْبِرْ · sciences]
turns on: Whether the instruction to bear hostile harm ceased with permission to fight; Ibn al-Jawzī disputes that reported abrogation.
- 76:24/q17/p1 Yes; patience with polytheist abuse was later abrogated by the fighting verse. — Al-Qurtubi reports al-Ḍaḥḥāk transmitting Ibn ʿAbbās's view that this was patience with harm as decreed until the fighting verse. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون)-]
- 76:24/q17/p2 No; calling this verse abrogated by the sword verse is unsound. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports the abrogation claim and rejects it. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون)+]

## 76:24/q18 How many verses does Sūrat al-Insān contain?  [* · sciences]
- 76:24/q18/p1 Thirty-one. — Al-Jishumī gives the count. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 76:24/q19 What merit is reported for reciting this sūra?  [* · reports]
- 76:24/q19/p1 Its reciter receives Paradise and silk from God. — Al-Jishumī reports Ubayy ibn Kaʿb transmitting this from the Prophet. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبي بن كعب، النبي)]

## 76:24/q20 How does this command connect to nearby verses and the sūra's argument?  [* · links]
turns on: Which contextual link is foregrounded: the nearby descent and divine outcome, or continuity with the preceding sūra's creation and resurrection proof.
- 76:24/q20/p1 The revealed Qur'an, its weight, and the preceding promise of divine outcomes ground the call to patience and steadiness. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says descent of the Qur'an precedes patience because its weighty message, duties, and coming harm require it, citing 73:5 and 20:132. Al-Biqāʿī connects the command with al-Muddaththir's warning, al-Muzzammil's weighty message, al-Qiyāma's instruction not to move the tongue, and al-Jinn's treatment of the Qur'an, citing 74:54, 73:5, and 75:16. He says the Qur'an's miraculous greatness establishes the rest of the law and a wise speaker repeats a resisted truth indirectly. Asad links patience to the earlier promise of bliss for the righteous and suffering for evildoers. Ibn al-Jawzī points back to explanations at 52:48 and 68:48.
