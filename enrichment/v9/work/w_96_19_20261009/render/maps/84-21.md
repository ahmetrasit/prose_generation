# 84:21: 19 questions

## 84:21/q01 How is the conditional sentence attached to what precedes?  [وَإِذَا قُرِئَ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلْقُرْءَانُ لَا يَسْجُدُونَ · grammar]
- 84:21/q01/p1 The recitation clause gives the circumstance or condition of their failure, and their not prostrating parallels their not believing. — Al-Samīn identifies إذا قرئ as the condition and لا يسجدون as its answer, and treats the conditional clause as a circumstantial accusative coordinated with the preceding clause. Bursawī also calls it circumstantial and coordinated with what precedes. Ibn ʿĀshūr coordinates لا يسجدون with لا يؤمنون. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 84:21/q02 How is قُرِئَ read?  [قُرِئَ · readings]
- 84:21/q02/p1 Ḥamza eases the final hamza, reading quriya, with the passive meaning unchanged. — The reading note attributes the hamza easing to Ḥamza. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(hmz)]

## 84:21/q03 Why is the recitation time clause placed first?  [وَإِذَا قُرِئَ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلْقُرْءَانُ · rhetoric]
- 84:21/q03/p1 Its fronting emphasizes and honors the Qur’an. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives this as the effect of placing the introductory time clause first. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 84:21/q04 What is meant by reciting the Qur’an to them?  [قُرِئَ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلْقُرْءَانُ · interpretation]
- 84:21/q04/p1 It is recitation addressed to them to convey the message and invite faith, whether by the Prophet or another Muslim. — Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies conveying the message and inviting them to faith. Bursawī names the Prophet, a Companion, or another Muslim as possible reciters and says Arabic speakers should recognize the recitation’s divine origin, affirm Muhammad’s prophethood, and obey him. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 84:21/q05 What encounters with Qur’anic recitation are cited?  [قُرِئَ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلْقُرْءَانُ · reports]
turns on: The notes describe different encounters and addressees.
- 84:21/q05/p1 Ibn Ubayy told the Prophet not to provoke or deceive people with Qur’anic recitation in their gatherings. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ubayy ibn Salūl’s words. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(عبد الله بن أبي بن سلول)]
- 84:21/q05/p2 The Prophet recited Qur’an to al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra. — Ibn ʿĀshūr refers to his discussion of this encounter under Sūrat ʿAbasa. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 84:21/q06 How is ٱلْقُرْءَانُ read?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانُ · readings]
- 84:21/q06/p1 With hamza easing it is al-qurānu, referring to the same Qur’an. — The reading note gives hamza easing without a change of referent. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(hmz)]

## 84:21/q07 Why is the Qur’an not classified as poetry?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانُ · interpretation]
- 84:21/q07/p1 Its opponents did not recognize it as poetry, and incidental meter does not constitute deliberately composed poetry. — Abū Bakr argues that eloquent opponents would have challenged accessible poetry if they had considered the Qur’an poetry; their motive to discredit it also makes it implausible that later readers discovered poetry they missed. Most Arabic specialists require at least two metrical lines, with mismatched meters and rhymes failing the test. Some exclude rajaz, especially a fragment or half-line; others demand four rajaz lines, absent from the Qur’an. Al-Zarkashī says poetry requires deliberate poetic composition, since ordinary speech can accidentally scan. Abū Bakr prefers this defense as reliable or mostly so and adds that contemporaries would naturally try to rival poetry. [6 notes: BURHAN(أكثر أهل صناعة العربية,القاضي أبو بكر,قوم); BURHAN(القاضي أبو بكر)+]

## 84:21/q08 What about the Qur’an warrants recognition and prostration?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانُ لَا يَسْجُدُونَ · interpretation]
- 84:21/q08/p1 Its divine message, signs, and distinctive eloquence call for recognition, gratitude, humility, and obedience. — Al-Biqāʿī cites its comprehensive worldly and afterlife benefits, lawful and unlawful distinctions, and self-evident divine origin and miracle. Bursawī says Arabic speakers should recognize its inimitability and Muhammad’s prophethood. Al-Zarkashī describes inimitable shifts of register and apt word choice whose elegance disappears with a synonym. Study Quran calls it another divine sign and miracle for people who overlook created signs. Asad points to its stress on God’s law of unceasing change and progression. Iṣlāḥī says its news of humanity’s journey toward meeting the Lord deserves grateful prostration, while arrogant denial reverses the proper response; he cites Arab and Egyptian prostration before impressive discourse, Pharaoh’s magicians, and poets hearing Labīd. Qāshānī says its reminder of these states should induce submission, whereas those veiled from truth remain veiled from religion. Kuranyolu says the disbelievers are rebuked despite the evidence. [9 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; BURHAN; STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QASHANI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 84:21/q09 What does their failure to prostrate mean?  [لَا يَسْجُدُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether sujūd here names inward submission, prayer, or the bodily act at recitation; some interpreters include more than one sense.
- 84:21/q09/p1 They refuse humility and submission to God, the Qur’an’s truth, and its commands. — Al-Naḥḥās reports exegetical agreement on failure to humble themselves in obedience. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī’s other interpreters, and a view cited by al-Jishumī give this sense. Al-Jishumī’s report from al-Kalbī and Muqātil adds humility to prayer. Mālik, as reported by al-Qurṭubī, takes it as failure to submit and obey the required actions. Al-Biqāʿī says inward refusal also entails refusal of ritual prostration. Bursawī includes bowing or submitting; Elmalılı includes submission to its truth and commands. Ibn ʿĀshūr includes rejection of God, Qur’anic meanings and proof, and its truth, contrasting 84:22 and citing 55:6 and 16:48. Study Quran reports the humility gloss; Qāshānī connects refusal with being veiled from truth. [12 notes: NAHHAS(أهل التفسير); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(غيرهم); JISHUMI(الكلبي ومقاتل,قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(مالك); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Others); QASHANI]
- 84:21/q09/p2 They do not pray to God. — Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī report al-Kalbī and Muqātil; al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī report ʿAṭāʾ and al-Kalbī. Al-Jishumī’s report from al-Kalbī and Muqātil also adds failure to humble themselves. Al-Qurṭubī states the prayer gloss, and Study Quran reports it as one interpretation. [7 notes: THALABI(الكلبي ومقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء والكلبي); JISHUMI(الكلبي ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل); TABRISI(عطاء والكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Some)]
- 84:21/q09/p3 They fail to perform a bodily prostration in reverence when the Qur’an, particularly its prostration verse, is recited. — Al-Ṭabrisī asks what prevents prostration to God at recitation. Al-Biqāʿī includes the ritual prostration required then alongside inward humility. Bursawī offers the narrower reading of failure to prostrate at the specific prostration verse, rather than at any recitation. Elmalılı says they neither prostrate nor submit. Iṣlāḥī describes gratitude expressed by prostration, and Kuranyolu describes reverent prostration. [6 notes: TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 84:21/q10 What event is reported as the occasion of this rebuke?  [وَإِذَا قُرِئَ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلْقُرْءَانُ لَا يَسْجُدُونَ · reports]
- 84:21/q10/p1 The Prophet recited 96:19 and prostrated with the believers while Quraysh mocked them, after which this verse was revealed. — Al-Bayḍāwī reports Quraysh clapping overhead. Elmalılı reports clapping and whistling. Both link the event to 96:19. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 84:21/q11 What prostrations at this sūrah are reported from the Prophet and his Companions?  [لَا يَسْجُدُونَ · reports]
turns on: The reports name different participants and prayer settings; Ibn ʿĀshūr also notes reports conflicting with the Prophet’s reported prostration.
- 84:21/q11/p1 The Prophet prostrated at Sūrat al-Inshiqāq, as Abū Hurayra witnessed or transmitted. — Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī cite Abū Hurayra’s report of the Prophet’s recitation and prostration. Al-Qurṭubī calls the report sound and says Abū Hurayra prostrated on reciting the passage, then told people the Prophet had done so. Al-Bayḍāwī says Abū Hurayra prostrated only after seeing the Messenger do so; Elmalılı says he swore to this. Ibn ʿĀshūr says reports of the Prophet’s prostration here conflict with other reports and permits that he sometimes left it or did not emphasize it. [6 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI(أبو هريرة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 84:21/q11/p2 The Companions prostrated with the Prophet during recitation of al-ʿAlaq and al-Inshiqāq. — Al-Baghawī relates Abū Hurayra’s report and states no grading; the report also names Sūrat al-ʿAlaq. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي)]
- 84:21/q11/p3 Abū Hurayra prostrated after al-Inshiqāq in night prayer, citing his earlier prostration behind the Prophet. — Al-Baghawī reports Abū Rāfiʿ’s eyewitness account: Abū Hurayra said he would continue that practice until meeting the Prophet. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو رافع)]
- 84:21/q11/p4 Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, ʿUthmān, and ʿAlī prostrated during prayer at this passage. — Elmalılı attributes this report to Anas. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 84:21/q12 Is this verse counted among Qur’anic recitation prostration passages?  [لَا يَسْجُدُونَ · law]
turns on: Whether the rebuke of nonprostration institutes a recitation rite, and how to weigh the Prophet’s reported practice against contrary reports and the broader sense of submission.
- 84:21/q12/p1 Yes, a recitation prostration is prescribed or recognized here. — Al-Jishumī says the verse establishes a required prostration and cites Abū Hurayra’s report. Al-Biqāʿī includes the ritual prostration required when recited. Ibn al-ʿArabī, reported by al-Qurṭubī, calls it obligatory and cites the Medinese report from Mālik, Qur’an, and Sunna. Ibn Wahb counts it among fourteen; Ibn al-ʿArabī, as Ibn ʿĀshūr reports, attributes prostration at al-Inshiqāq to Medinese Mālikīs. Study Quran says most commentators include it among fifteen, with other Mālikīs requiring it. Reports of the Prophet’s practice underlie the legal views summarized by Kuranyolu. [8 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن العربي,ابن وهب); STUDYQURAN(Imam Mālik and other Mālikī jurists,Most commentators); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)+; QURTUBI-FULL(مالك)-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 84:21/q12/p2 No, this wording does not establish a special Qur’anic prostration here. — Mālik, as reported by al-Qurṭubī, denies its status as an obligatory prostration passage because it speaks of failure to submit and obey. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the wording establishes no Qur’anic prostration and prefers the stronger Mālikī view excluding it. Elmalılı reports Ibn ʿAbbās’s denial of prostrations in the Mufaṣṣal sūrahs; Kuranyolu reports a view prescribing neither obligation nor sunna. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مالك); IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)-]

## 84:21/q13 What legal force is assigned to prostration at this verse?  [لَا يَسْجُدُونَ · law]
turns on: Whether the rebuke makes the act obligatory, reported prophetic practice supports a Sunnah act, or the passage establishes no required rite.
- 84:21/q13/p1 It is obligatory. — Al-Jishumī says this verse establishes a required prostration and cites Abū Hurayra. Ibn al-ʿArabī, preferred in al-Qurṭubī’s report, relies on Qur’an, Sunna, and the Medinese report from Mālik. Al-Bayḍāwī says Abū Ḥanīfa uses the rebuke of listeners who do not prostrate as proof. Elmalılı and Ibn ʿĀshūr also report Abū Ḥanīfa’s ruling; Kuranyolu lists obligation among the three views drawn from reports of prophetic practice. [6 notes: JISHUMI; BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو حنيفة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)+; QURTUBI-FULL(مالك)-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 84:21/q13/p2 It is recommended or Sunnah. — Elmalılı reports al-Shāfiʿī’s Sunnah ruling, and Ibn ʿĀshūr reports his recommendation. Kuranyolu lists Sunnah as a view arising from the Prophet’s reported practice. [3 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الشافعي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 84:21/q13/p3 It is not required, and some deny a prescribed prostration here altogether. — Mālik says this is not an obligatory prostration passage, taking the wording as failure to submit and obey. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the wording does not establish such a prostration and prefers the stronger Mālikī exclusion. Elmalılı reports Ibn ʿAbbās denying prostrations in the Mufaṣṣal. Kuranyolu lists neither obligation nor Sunnah. Study Quran says Mālik does not require it, though other Mālikīs do. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مالك); IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Imam Mālik and other Mālikī jurists); IBNASHUR-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)-]

## 84:21/q14 How many recitation prostrations are counted in the cited views?  [لَا يَسْجُدُونَ · law]
turns on: The cited counts differ over how many passages qualify and how sound or consistent the reports are.
- 84:21/q14/p1 Fifteen verses, including this one. — Study Quran attributes this count to most commentators and mentions 19:58. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Most commentators)]
- 84:21/q14/p2 Fourteen verses, including this one. — Ibn ʿĀshūr attributes this count to the Mālikī Ibn Wahb. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن وهب)]
- 84:21/q14/p3 Eleven prostrations are established by sound Companion reports; three more, including this verse, rest on conflicting reports. — Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers the eleven-report assessment. He says reports of the Prophet’s prostration at three further verses conflict with other reports and allows that the Prophet left the prostration or did not emphasize it. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 84:21/q15 How is recitation prostration fulfilled when this sūrah is read in prayer?  [لَا يَسْجُدُونَ · law]
- 84:21/q15/p1 If the sūrah is completed before bowing, the prayer’s bowing and prostration suffice. — Elmalılı explains al-Ḥasan’s report that it is not obligatory as referring to this sufficiency within prayer. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 84:21/q16 How did Ibn al-ʿArabī handle this passage when leading prayer?  [لَا يَسْجُدُونَ · reports]
- 84:21/q16/p1 He avoided reciting it while leading others and recited it only when praying alone. — As al-Qurṭubī reports, Ibn al-ʿArabī feared people would reject his prostration, while leaving it out would be a shortcoming. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)]

## 84:21/q17 Does the rebuke imply that unbelievers can choose faith and prostration?  [لَا يَسْجُدُونَ · theology]
- 84:21/q17/p1 Yes; faith and prostration are their own possible acts. — Al-Jishumī argues against determinists that one cannot ask why someone failed to do what was impossible for them. Al-Ṭabrisī says a wise speaker would not demand an act someone could not perform. [2 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI]

## 84:21/q18 Are unbelievers addressed by duties of worship?  [لَا يَسْجُدُونَ · law]
- 84:21/q18/p1 Yes; the rebuke shows that religious obligations address unbelievers too. — Al-Jishumī draws this inference along with the requirement of prostration here. Al-Ṭabrisī likewise says they are addressed by worship obligations. [2 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI]

## 84:21/q19 How does this verse connect with the sūrah’s surrounding argument?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes connect the rebuke either with this sūrah’s cosmic signs or with the preceding sūrah’s judgment contrast.
- 84:21/q19/p1 It follows signs of cosmic change and humanity’s progression toward meeting its Lord, which make denial and refusal to prostrate foolish. — Asad says the Qur’an consistently stresses God’s law of unceasing change and progression. Iṣlāḥī says physical phenomena change gradually under God’s control, as humanity moves toward its Lord; hearing the announcement should prompt grateful prostration. Study Quran says people who miss created signs should recognize the Qur’an itself as a sign and miracle. [4 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 84:21/q19/p2 It continues the close of al-Muṭaffifīn, contrasting reward for allies with punishment for deniers of resurrection and judgment. — Al-Biqāʿī connects al-Inshiqāq with the preceding sūrah and grounds the contrast in judgment before the Creator-King. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
