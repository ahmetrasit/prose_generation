# 21:97: 27 questions

## 21:97/q01 What does 'has drawn near' convey?  [وَٱقْتَرَبَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb merely equals qaruba or intensifies it, and whose measure of nearness is used.
- 21:97/q01/p1 The form emphasizes a greater or intensified nearness. — Abū Ḥayyān and Biqāʿī read iftaʿala as intensified; Biqāʿī compares the Hour to a woman about to give birth and cites a hadith. Ālūsī records this alternative. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:97/q01/p2 The event has come near, with no distinct force beyond qaruba. — Jishumī equates qaruba and iqtaraba; Ālūsī reports this plain gloss. [2 notes: JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:97/q01/p3 It is near by God's measure despite people's sense that Judgment is remote. — Iṣlāḥī contrasts brief human measures with God's measure and cites 70:6–7. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 21:97/q02 What is the true promise that approaches?  [ٱلْوَعْدُ ٱلْحَقُّ · referent]
turns on: Whether waʿd names the final Resurrection, its second trumpet blast, or the individual's death.
- 21:97/q02/p1 The Resurrection or Hour, including the ensuing reckoning and recompense. — Muqātil stresses its certainty; Ṭabarī resurrection from graves for reward and punishment; Bursevī and Ālūsī place resurrection, reckoning, and recompense after the second blast, not the first. Rāzī calls it unquestionable; Ibn ʿĀshūr treats these events collectively. Rāzī calls waʿd a figurative name for the appointed event. Ibn Kathīr links its imminence to terrors and upheavals; Kuranyolu to Gog and Magog as a sign. [27 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 21:97/q02/p2 Specifically the second blast of the Trumpet. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām identifies the approaching promise with that blast. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM]
- 21:97/q02/p3 The minor resurrection at each person's death. — Qāshānī reads the veiled person's horrified stare and admission as occurring at death. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 21:97/q03 What does 'true' contribute and what senses of its root are given?  [ٱلْحَقُّ · meaning]
turns on: The notes distinguish this sense from other attested uses of the root.
- 21:97/q03/p1 It is sound, correct, and certain, the opposite of falsehood. — Ibn al-Jawzī gives ḥaqq as sound/correct, ḥaqīqa as a matter's true outcome, and ḥaqqa as becoming due; Biqāʿī says the promised event matches what will happen. Ibn al-Jawzī lists ḥāqqa for rival truth-claims, ḥaqqahu/aḥaqqahu for prevailing, and iḥtaqqū for mutual claims. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; BIQAI-FULL]
- 21:97/q03/p2 The root also names the all-befalling Resurrection and several concrete things. — Ibn al-Jawzī records al-ḥāqqa for Resurrection, ḥiqqa for a rideable young camel and ḥuqāq its plural, ḥuqqa/ḥuqaq for a box, and ḥaqḥaqa for exhausting travel; Muṭarrif's poem contrasts that pace with moderation. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(مطرف بن عبد الله)]

## 21:97/q04 How does the preceding 'until' introduce this scene?  [وَٱقْتَرَبَ · links]
- 21:97/q04/p1 It links the statements and advances to a new purpose, without requiring a literal temporal endpoint. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares Labīd's Muʿallaqah and 2:214, where ḥattā introduces speech arising from distress rather than the endpoint when the Messenger spoke. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 21:97/q05 How is the clause joined to the preceding 'until when'?  [وَٱقْتَرَبَ ٱلْوَعْدُ ٱلْحَقُّ فَإِذَا · grammar]
turns on: Which clause supplies the apodosis, and whether the wāw can be extra.
- 21:97/q05/p1 The wāw is semantically extra; 'the promise drew near' answers the preceding conditional. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Kisāʾī and others take Gog and Magog's release as condition. Ibn Qutayba cites the extra conjunction. Ṭabarī compares 37:104 and Imruʾ al-Qays's successive verbs; later sources cite 37:103, 39:73 and poetry. Al-Kisāʾī generalizes to ḥattā idhā, lammā and ḥīna clauses. Ālūsī reports this analysis. [15 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI(الفراء وجماعة من العلماء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,الكسائي,امرؤ القيس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الفراء وجماعة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء,الفراء والكسائي وغيرهما); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء)]
- 21:97/q05/p2 The wāw is retained, and an omitted 'they said' before 'Woe to us' gives the answer. — Al-Zajjāj and the Basrans reject an extra wāw; Zajjāj's objectors worry about its omission before reported speech, while his reply supplies qālū. Naḥḥās calls Abū Isḥāq's account good and cites common Qurʾānic speech ellipsis, including 39:3; Qurṭubī calls it sound. Ālūsī notes the cry may be circumstantial or a new sentence under another parse. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ(بعضهم); THALABI(الزجاج); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج وغيره); TABRISI(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج); NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق)+; QURTUBI-FULL(البصريون والزجاج)+]
- 21:97/q05/p3 The approach is coordinated with the opening; 'then suddenly the eyes stare' answers both. — Al-Kisāʾī coordinates wa-iqtaraba with idhā futiḥat. Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers this response because it depicts the unavoidable return they denied. Bursevī joins the opening and approach and treats the short interval before Resurrection as effectively no interval. Naḥḥās cites al-Kisāʾī's poetic syntactic parallel. [6 notes: NAHHAS(الكسائي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي); BURSEVI; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]

## 21:97/q06 How does the preceding emergence of Gog and Magog relate to this promise?  [وَٱقْتَرَبَ ٱلْوَعْدُ ٱلْحَقُّ · links]
turns on: Whether the previous verse depicts Gog and Magog as a sign or people leaving graves.
- 21:97/q06/p1 Their emergence is a sign that the Hour approaches, though intervening events may occur. — Zajjāj's tafsīr, Wāḥidī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn Kathīr, Ṭabāṭabāʾ and Study Quran link the opening to the Hour. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the scene conveys rapid shock. Bursevī counts the interval as short; Kuranyolu connects 21:96 and 21:104. Ṭabrisī reports Qatāda, Ibn Masʿūd, al-Jubbāʾī and Abū Muslim that Gog and Magog spread rapidly from every elevation. [14 notes: ZAJJAJ(التفسير); WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI(قتادة وابن مسعود والجبائي وأبو مسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 21:97/q06/p2 The preceding emergence refers to people coming out of graves. — Ṭabāṭabāʾ rejects this: opening, approaching promise, circumstantial syntax and emergence from every height do not fit people leaving graves. [0 notes: TABATABAI-]

## 21:97/q07 What sequence of end-time events is reported around this promise?  [ٱلْوَعْدُ ٱلْحَقُّ · reports]
turns on: The reports supply distinct narrators, sequences and details; the exact onset of the Hour remains God's knowledge.
- 21:97/q07/p1 The prophetic report of Nawwās places the Dajjāl before Jesus's descent and killing of him. — Ibn Kathīr reports a curly-haired Dajjāl with a floating eye emerging between Syria and Iraq, staying forty days (year, month, week, then ordinary days), and commanding believers to estimate prayer times. He travels like wind-driven rain, rewards followers with rain and livestock, starves opponents, draws treasures from ruins, and kills and revives a man. Jesus descends at the white minaret east of Damascus and kills him at Ludd's eastern gate. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 21:97/q07/p2 An Ibn Masʿūd report describes Jesus killing the Dajjāl, the destruction and cleansing afterward, and an unknown Hour. — Al-Durr reports the Night Journey account: Abraham, Moses and Jesus defer exact knowledge of the Hour to God; Jesus says stones and trees expose hiding unbelievers; God destroys the Dajjāl's people, foulness covers the earth, and rain washes bodies to sea. Ibn Masʿūd relates the sequence to these verses. [4 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 21:97/q07/p3 A long hadith places Gog and Magog after Jesus kills the Dajjāl, followed by cleansing and a wind taking believers. — Ālūsī cites Muslim, Abū Dāwūd, al-Tirmidhī, al-Nasāʾī and Ibn Mājah: divine affliction kills Gog and Magog; birds and rain cleanse the earth, abundance returns, and the Hour reaches the wicked after believers die. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:97/q07/p4 Kaʿb relates a vanguard of seven or eight hundred after news of Dhū al-Suwayqatayn, then a fragrant wind. — Jesus sends the vanguard; the wind takes every believer, and those left mate like beasts. Ibn Kathīr prefers this as one of Kaʿb al-Aḥbār's best accounts because authentic reports corroborate it. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(كعب); IBNKATHIR-FULL+]

## 21:97/q08 How soon does the Hour follow Gog and Magog in the reports?  [وَٱقْتَرَبَ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in descriptions of proximity and intervening activity.
- 21:97/q08/p1 A foal acquired after their emergence will not be ridden before the Hour. — Ḥudhayfa's report appears in Ṭabarī, Thaʿlabī, Baghawī, al-Durr and Bursevī; Ibn ʿAṭiyya has a similar unnamed report. Ālūsī cites Ibn Jurayj's Prophetic wording about a horse's surviving foal. The wording varies between acquisition and preparation of a foal. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(حذيفة); THALABI(حذيفة); BAGHAWI-FULL(حذيفة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(رُوي); DURR-FULL(حذيفة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 21:97/q08/p2 Its onset is as unpredictable as delivery from a full-term pregnancy. — The Ibn Masʿūd account says a family cannot know whether labor starts by night or day; Biqāʿī cites the pregnancy simile without grading; Ālūsī attributes it to Aḥmad and others. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:97/q08/p3 Pilgrimage and minor pilgrimage will still be performed after their emergence. — Ibn Kathīr cites Abū Saʿīd's Prophetic report and notes al-Bukhārī alone transmits it; al-Durr gives the same saying. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 21:97/q08/p4 The Hour has already come upon them. — Al-Rabīʿ is reported with this formulation. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(الربيع)]

## 21:97/q09 What does the sudden 'then behold' do in the result?  [فَإِذَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether idhā is the sudden result particle or an adverb governed by the following predicate.
- 21:97/q09/p1 Sudden idhā supplies the conditional result and can replace its linking fāʾ; both together reinforce the connection. — Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī and Nasafī compare 30:36 and allow either idhā hiya or fa-hiya alone. Biqāʿī, Bursevī and Ālūsī agree; Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses immediate surprise and the absence of an interval. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 21:97/q09/p2 Idhā is a locative adverb governed by 'staring' in an alternate parse. — Ṭabrisī gives this analysis with hiya as situation-pronoun and eyes as delayed subject. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 21:97/q10 What is 'she/it' (hiya) and how is the eye clause parsed?  [هِىَ شَٰخِصَةٌ أَبْصَٰرُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether hiya anticipates eyes, supports a predication, introduces an affair-clause, or completes an independent clause; Basran and Kufan rules differ.
- 21:97/q10/p1 It is a pronoun of the affair or story, explained by the full clause with 'staring' fronted and 'eyes' delayed. — Sībawayh and al-Mubarrad distinguish masculine affair and feminine story (20:74, 72:3); Wāḥidī, Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn, Bursevī, Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr use the event/story analysis. Samīn prefers the full-sentence Basran parse; Ālūsī calls it standard. Itqān says the pronoun anticipates a sentence, takes no dependent, remains singular and magnifies later information. Wāḥidī compares 14:42. [15 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد، سيبويه); IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(علي بن أحمد النيسابوري); RAZI-FULL(سيبويه); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 21:97/q10/p2 It refers to the eyes in advance, which the following noun names explicitly. — Al-Farrāʾ cites a poem that names a woman or she-camel after her pronoun; Ṭabarī and Qurṭubī repeat the parallel. Wāḥidī calls it explanatory ellipsis and points to 12:77. Ibn Mālik and others allow the antecedent to follow; Ālūsī gives poems about an eye and Mālik ibn Abī Kaʿb. [18 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء)]
- 21:97/q10/p3 It is a supporting or separating pronoun, replaceable by masculine huwa. — Al-Farrāʾ cites Qurʾān 27:9 and 22:46, Arab speech and poetry; Ṭabarī and later commentators repeat this. Abū Ḥayyān says it depends on al-Kisāʾī's contested allowance for a separator before a fronted predicate. Samīn cites هو خير منك زيد and disputes the Shaykh's two-definite-noun example, proposing the separator travels with the fronted predicate. [18 notes: FARRA(بعضهم); TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ,الفراء,الكسائي); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء)]
- 21:97/q10/p4 It is a vague or unspecified pronoun, made definite by 'eyes'. — Zamakhsharī compares the wrongdoers' whispered speech in 21:3; Nasafī and Samīn repeat it. Bayḍāwī allows either this or a whole-situation reference. Study Quran records an alternative rendering of the eye clause. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 21:97/q10/p5 The first clause ends at hiya: the Hour is present; a new clause says the eyes stare. — Thaʿlabī and Baghawī give this with a fronted predicate. Abū Ḥayyān calls the syntax forced; Samīn calls it remote and tangled; Ālūsī calls it strained and discordant. [5 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الثعلبي); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الثعلبي)-; SAMIN-DURR(الثعلبي)-; ALUSI-FULL(الثعلبي)-]
- 21:97/q10/p6 It is the affair-pronoun, but a single predicate 'staring' is explained by 'eyes' as its agent. — Abū Ḥayyān notes Kufans allow this while Basrans require a sentence; Samīn gives the Kufan subject-like agent parse. Ālūsī reports some Kufans but rejects it under the usual rule. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الكوفيون); ALUSI-FULL(بعض الكوفيين)-]

## 21:97/q11 Why retain the pronoun before naming the disbelievers' eyes?  [هِىَ أَبْصَٰرُ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes give both suspense and particularization as effects of the pronoun.
- 21:97/q11/p1 It gives suspense and then clarifies the important scene. — Itqān says the affair-pronoun first leaves the matter vague and then explains it, magnifying the following information; Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise sees advance notice resolved by the following words. [2 notes: ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 21:97/q11/p2 It singles out disbelievers as those whose eyes are fixed. — Zarkashī says dropping hiya would lose this particularizing force. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 21:97/q12 What does 'staring' mean as a word?  [شَٰخِصَةٌ · meaning]
turns on: The physical root senses explain the figurative description of an eye fixed outward.
- 21:97/q12/p1 The eyes are fixed open toward an object, without blinking. — Jishumī lists a gaze fixed outward among the root's senses; Ṭabrisī explains it as if the eye travels toward the object; Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Rāghib define an immobile, unblinking gaze, often signaling sudden evil or intense attention. [7 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب)]
- 21:97/q12/p2 The root also describes traveling, displacement, disturbance, or an arrow overshooting. — Jishumī lists a departing traveler, movement between towns, a troubling event, and an arrow passing over its target; Ṭabrisī records travel from home or city as the source image. [2 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI]

## 21:97/q13 What makes the disbelievers' eyes stare, and where do they look?  [شَٰخِصَةٌ أَبْصَٰرُ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the gaze is specified by its cause, Resurrection's terror, or by its direction toward the caller.
- 21:97/q13/p1 Terror at the Resurrection fixes their open eyes, scarcely blinking. — Muqātil says they see wonders denied in life; al-Kalbī, commentators, Rāzī, Nasafī, Bursevī and Ālūsī describe horror, raised lids and shock. Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes severe fear, illness or similar causes; Study Quran cites 14:42 and 80:34–37. Iṣlāḥī says those who mocked the Hour are horrified; Kuranyolu stresses astonishment and helplessness. [18 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ)]
- 21:97/q13/p2 Their eyes stare toward answering the caller to Jerusalem. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām supplies that direction. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM]

## 21:97/q14 How is the final tāʾ of 'staring' read when pausing?  [شَٰخِصَةٌ · readings]
- 21:97/q14/p1 In this ṣād-class word the preferred pause uses fatḥ rather than imāla. — Ibn al-Jazarī lists ten letters: ḥāʾ, ʿayn, alif and seven emphatics. Fatḥ after alif is agreed and after the other nine preferred; شاخصة belongs to ṣād. He excludes Tawrāt, taqāt, marḍāt, mazjāt and mishkāt because their alif takes imāla in connection and pause. For hamza, kāf, hāʾ and rāʾ he gives imāla after silent yāʾ or kasra, even across an intervening sukūn, and fatḥ otherwise. [4 notes: NASHR]

## 21:97/q15 Who are 'those who disbelieved' in this scene?  [ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ · referent]
- 21:97/q15/p1 Those who denied the truth and its warnings. — Asad reads the participle as those deliberately bent on denial, comparing his treatment of 2:6; Elmalılı identifies rejecters of the signs who harmed communal unity. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ELMALILI]

## 21:97/q16 How is 'Woe to us' introduced syntactically?  [يَٰوَيْلَنَا · grammar]
turns on: Its role depends on which preceding clause is treated as the conditional answer.
- 21:97/q16/p1 An omitted verb of saying is understood; the speech can be circumstantial to the disbelievers. — Ṭabarī infers 'they say' from the staring description. Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Ṭabrisī, Bursevī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Iṣlāḥī supply it; Samīn and Ālūsī allow either conditional answer, circumstantial clause or new sentence. The Qurʾān frequently omits reported-speech verbs, as Naḥḥās and Qurṭubī note with 39:3. [16 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 21:97/q17 What does their cry of 'Woe to us' express?  [يَٰوَيْلَنَا · interpretation]
- 21:97/q17/p1 Terror, regret and a call for woe when calamity has arrived. — Ṭabarī places the lament amid the Hour's horrors and compares 21:14. Biqāʿī says woe is now their companion and no helper remains; Bursevī and Ālūsī describe summoning woe when its time arrives; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a self-directed imprecation. Ṭabāṭabāʾ has them call woe upon themselves as the Hour appears. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 21:97/q18 What had they been heedless of?  [قَدْ كُنَّا فِى غَفْلَةٍۢ مِّنْ هَٰذَا · interpretation]
turns on: The demonstrative may emphasize the Day, one's duties, or the realities now witnessed.
- 21:97/q18/p1 The Resurrection or Judgment Day and the warnings about it during worldly life. — Yaḥyā specifies denial of the Hour; Ṭabrisī says worldly affairs distracted them from reflecting. Wāḥidī and others stress worldly inattention or denial; Bayḍāwī says they did not know the promise was true. Ibn ʿĀshūr points to evidence for resurrection and recompense. Ālūsī reports the narrower view that they did not know this day was real. [20 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 21:97/q18/p2 They were heedless of the truths they now see and recognize. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān emphasize the confrontation with previously ignored reality. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 21:97/q18/p3 They were blind to the obligations required of them. — Ibn ʿAbbās explains the confession as worldly blindness to what was required. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)]

## 21:97/q19 What does the preposition 'in' add to 'heedlessness'?  [فِى غَفْلَةٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 21:97/q19/p1 It depicts heedlessness as enveloping them. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares fī to a container surrounding its contents. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 21:97/q20 What does 'min' mean in 'heedless of this'?  [مِّنْ هَٰذَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether min is used for ʿan or retains a source meaning.
- 21:97/q20/p1 It means 'about' or 'away from' (ʿan). — Ibn al-Jawzī glosses 'from this' as 'about this'; Zarkashī and Suyūṭī explicitly give ʿan. Zarkashī also records the Basran constraints on an extra min: indefinite following noun and a negative, prohibitive or interrogative setting, though some include a positive conditional clause. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURHAN; ITQAN]
- 21:97/q20/p2 It marks the starting point or source. — Zarkashī records this alternative for both this phrase and the verse about hearts hardened from God's remembrance. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 21:97/q21 What do they admit by correcting themselves with 'Rather, we were wrongdoers'?  [بَلْ كُنَّا ظَٰلِمِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the line emphasizes specific wrongs or the correction of claimed heedlessness; the accounts can overlap.
- 21:97/q21/p1 They wronged themselves by denying the messengers, persisting in unbelief and directing worship to others. — Muqātil says messengers warned them; Wāḥidī names denial of Muḥammad and other deities; Thaʿlabī, Ṭabrisī, Qurṭubī and Nasafī stress misdirected worship and disobedience. Ibn ʿAṭiyya describes persistence in unbelief; Rāzī includes idols. Kuranyolu says repeated prophetic warning leaves no excuse. [17 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 21:97/q21/p2 Bal retracts an excuse of mere inadvertence: they knowingly rejected clear warnings and evidence. — Abū Ḥayyān calls the unbelief deliberate; Biqāʿī says signs and messengers made the evidence unmistakable. Bursevī and Ālūsī say they denied warnings or abandoned reflection, exposing themselves to lasting punishment; Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses stubborn opposition. Ṭabāṭabāʾ says worldly preoccupations drove their injustice; Asad cites 14:44–45 and says prophetic warning makes their neglect inexcusable. Iṣlāḥī says desire, rather than lack of evidence, led them to postpone preparation. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 21:97/q22 Can their late confession or faith benefit them?  [يَٰوَيْلَنَا بَلْ كُنَّا ظَٰلِمِينَ · interpretation]
- 21:97/q22/p1 No; the recognition comes only when the Hour's signs or punishment make it too late. — Zamakhsharī says those decreed for destruction persist until punishment and confess only at Resurrection. Ibn Kathīr says the confession then brings no benefit, citing 54:8; Zarkashī says belief at that sign is useless. His separate discussion of negation notes that denial can concern a complete or beneficial state rather than every outward act. Iṣlāḥī says late faith will not help. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 21:97/q23 How does this warning fit the sūra's argument?  [* · links]
- 21:97/q23/p1 The closing warning returns to the sūra's opening concern with the near Hereafter. — Iṣlāḥī links the close to 21:1 and calls this a common coherence pattern that restores the sūra's central purpose. He says the conclusion reassures the Prophet and companions of the prophets' one message, calls for persistence, leaves dividers to God, and ends with prayer for judgment; only pious servants succeed. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 21:97/q23/p2 The opening of Gog and Magog and the near promise are future events predicted by revelation. — Ṭabāṭabāʾ lists this alongside believers' succession in the land and punishments from above or below. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 21:97/q24 What practical warning is drawn from their heedlessness?  [* · interpretation]
- 21:97/q24/p1 Reflect on the warnings while opportunity remains, since people have no excuse after God has warned them. — Bursevī says God advised jinn and humans, recorded deeds, and directs each person to praise Him for good or blame himself otherwise. He cites a sage urging mourners to pity themselves before death's agony, and Saʿdī's body-as-cage and soul-as-bird image urging wakefulness; Saʿdī says to seize each remaining moment. Iṣlāḥī says indifference and desire postpone preparation. [6 notes: BURSEVI(الله,بعض الحكماء); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 21:97/q25 What do Gog and Magog symbolize in the inward reading?  [* · inward]
- 21:97/q25/p1 Gog and Magog symbolize impressions and preoccupations that corrupt the soul. — Study Quran reports the symbolic reading: tumult assaults the soul and exposes it to satanic influence. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Aj)]

## 21:97/q26 What is said about Qur'anic inimitability in relation to this expression?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether a learnable individual device suffices or the sustained composition is decisive.
- 21:97/q26/p1 Some locate it in isolated rhetorical devices or recognized eloquent forms. — Bāqillānī reports this view but argues learnable badiʿ cannot prove inimitability. Simile is eloquent but Ibn al-Muʿtazz also made striking similes. He rejects oath, simile, paronomasia or antithesis taken alone. [1 notes: BAQILLANI; BAQILLANI-]
- 21:97/q26/p2 It can lie in sustained supreme eloquence and exceptional connection of the whole discourse. — Bāqillānī prefers this account across every subject, distinguishing qualities inaccessible through practice from learnable devices. [2 notes: BAQILLANI; BAQILLANI+]

## 21:97/q27 What does the nearby word ḥasīs mean?  [* · meaning]
- 21:97/q27/p1 It means movement. — Ṭabrisī equates ḥasīs and ḥass. [1 notes: TABRISI]
