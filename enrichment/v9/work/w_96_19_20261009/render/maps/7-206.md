# 7:206: 42 questions

## 7:206/q01 Who are those with your Lord?  [ٱلَّذِينَ عِندَ رَبِّكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase specifies angels, includes all spiritually near servants, or receives an inward reading.
- 7:206/q01/p1 The angels, sometimes specified as the angels of the highest assembly. — Ibn ʿAbbās and other exegetes are cited by al-Wāḥidī; al-Ṭabrisī cites al-Ḥasan. Al-Qurṭubī calls the identification unanimous; Ibn ʿĀshūr relates their model role to the Messenger's rank. Elmalılı cites 39:75. [24 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن وغيره); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirlerin çoğunluğu); STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI-]
- 7:206/q01/p2 All who are brought near to God, not angels alone. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says remembrance lifts the veil of absence although all things are otherwise equally related to God; limiting the phrase to angels would defeat the exhortation to human remembrance. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 7:206/q01/p3 Prophets, messengers, and Imams. — Reported by Ṭabāṭabāʾī from Tafsīr al-Qummī. [1 notes: TABATABAI(تفسير القمي)]
- 7:206/q01/p4 Those annihilated in divine unity and abiding through God. — Al-Qāshānī describes steadfast spiritual subsistence; Najmiyya as transmitted by Bursevī describes effacement of actions, traits, and selves. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمة); ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:206/q01/p5 A metaphor for utter God-consciousness. — Asad's formulation. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 7:206/q02 What does ʿind convey here?  [عِندَ رَبِّكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether nearness describes rank or favor, an honored place or mission, proximity to the Throne, or spatial proximity to God.
- 7:206/q02/p1 Figurative nearness in rank, honor, mercy, and favor, without spatial proximity. — Al-Zajjāj cites mercy and favor while God is in heaven and earth (6:3); al-Rāzī compares minister and doorkeeper and cites the divine report about broken hearts. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, and others deny bodily location; al-Burhān cites 3:169. Al-Ṭabrisī says God transcends place. [21 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج,قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:206/q02/p2 An honorific association with their distinguished dwelling and the place where divine commands issue. — Al-Wāḥidī cites people of meaning; al-Rāzī describes the realm of lights, spirits, obedience, and blessings. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); RAZI-FULL]
- 7:206/q02/p3 Their location is wholly governed by God's decree. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī report a place where nothing occurs save His decree; al-Ṭabrisī likens their relation to soldiers under a prince's authority. [3 notes: NAHHAS(غيره); QURTUBI-FULL(غيره); TABRISI]
- 7:206/q02/p4 They are God's messengers to creation, as troops can be said to be with a distant ruler. — The analogy is reported by al-Naḥḥās, al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, and al-Qurṭubī. [4 notes: NAHHAS(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 7:206/q02/p5 Near the Lord's Throne. — Al-Ālūsī reports this as another interpretation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 7:206/q02/p6 Physical proximity implying God occupies a place and direction. — Al-Rāzī attributes this to anthropomorphists and rejects it by rational and transmitted proofs discussed under 7:54 and 10:3. [0 notes: RAZI-FULL(المشبهة)-]

## 7:206/q03 What range of meaning does ʿind have in Arabic?  [عِندَ · meaning]
- 7:206/q03/p1 It primarily conveys nearness, literal or figurative, and context can make it mean before or behind. — Al-Burhān compares ladā and waraʾ and cites 18:79, 14:16, 2:91, and 59:14 for contextual direction. [2 notes: BURHAN]

## 7:206/q04 Why is the group described by a relative clause instead of named as angels?  [ٱلَّذِينَ عِندَ رَبِّكَ · rhetoric]
- 7:206/q04/p1 The description signals their exalted station and urges people to emulate them. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the wording invites competition in their conduct. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:206/q05 What does their lack of istikbār mean?  [لَا يَسْتَكْبِرُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the absence of pride is explained as obedient humility or inward effacement of ego.
- 7:206/q05/p1 They do not act proudly or deem themselves too exalted for worship. — Al-Jishumī derives istikbār from the form istifʿāl, seeking greatness by rejecting truth. Abū Ḥayyān says pride imagines superiority and obstructs obedience; al-Biqāʿī says angels neither show nor seek pride despite purity and rank. [7 notes: JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 7:206/q05/p2 They have no ego veil or independent self-claim. — Al-Qāshānī speaks of witnessing differentiation in unity; Bursevī's Najmiyya account says effacing one's acts removes pride. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:206/q06 What is their ʿibāda?  [عِبَادَتِهِۦ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ʿibāda is general obedience and reverence or specifically prayer.
- 7:206/q06/p1 Obedient submission and humble reverence to God. — Al-Ṭabarī identifies humility as worship; Ibn al-Jawzī reports obedience; al-Ālūsī and Bursevī stress doing what was commanded. Kuran Yolu defines worship as acknowledging Lordship and showing respect by specified acts. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 7:206/q06/p2 Prayer with humility within it. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this alternative. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 7:206/q07 What does their tasbīḥ mean?  [وَيُسَبِّحُونَهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether tasbīḥ is transcendence in thought and speech, a formula, prayer, or inward self-negation.
- 7:206/q07/p1 They declare God transcendent above evil, imperfection, and all unfitting attributes. — Al-Jishumī stresses inadmissible attributes; Ibn ʿĀshūr includes speech and belief; al-Biqāʿī links angelic purity to freedom from desire; Kuran Yolu adds exaltation. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 7:206/q07/p2 They say Subḥān Allāh and remember God. — Al-Baghawī supplies the utterance; Ibn al-Jawzī reports it as a distinct gloss; Muqātil says remembrance. [3 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 7:206/q07/p3 Their glorification is prayer. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports this from al-Suddī. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي)]
- 7:206/q07/p4 It negates association by effacing selfhood. — Al-Qāshānī and al-Ālūsī's inward interpretation. [2 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:206/q08 What does the imperfect verb suggest about glorification?  [وَيُسَبِّحُونَهُۥ · grammar]
- 7:206/q08/p1 Its action recurs and continues. — Ibn ʿĀshūr notes renewed continuous praise; al-Sāmarrāʾī says tasbīḥ and istighfār commonly take a verb for renewed action, citing 62:1. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 7:206/q09 What does their sujūd mean?  [يَسْجُدُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether sujūd denotes the bodily act, prayer, humble submission, or inward self-effacement.
- 7:206/q09/p1 Actual prostration to God, including prostration in prayer. — Al-Ṭabrisī attributes the prayer detail to al-Ḥasan; Kuran Yolu describes lowering the head to the ground before God alone. [3 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 7:206/q09/p2 Prayer. — Muqātil, Ibn al-Jawzī, and al-Qurṭubī use prayer as the gloss. [3 notes: MUQATIL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 7:206/q09/p3 Humble submission, especially exclusive servitude to God. — Al-Naḥḥās contrasts sinners; al-Ṭabrisī gives humility among alternatives; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī, and al-Ālūsī stress worship without association. [7 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:206/q09/p4 Complete inward annihilation of selfhood. — Al-Qāshānī says God alone remains after creation perishes; al-Ālūsī repeats the inward reading. [2 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:206/q10 Why is lahu placed before yasjudūn?  [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether fronting principally creates exclusivity or serves the surrounding verse endings.
- 7:206/q10/p1 To restrict prostration to God alone and censure those who worship others. — Al-Rāzī notes the restriction; Abū Ḥayyān reports this view; Bursevī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say the fronting censures polytheists, and Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that the imperfect conveys continuity. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:206/q10/p2 To make the clause fit the verse ending. — Abū Ḥayyān prefers this over restriction; al-Ṭībī also mentions the verse ending alongside the moral rationale. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]

## 7:206/q11 Why are glorification and prostration mentioned in that order?  [وَيُسَبِّحُونَهُۥ وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · rhetoric]
- 7:206/q11/p1 Inward knowledge and purification of God precede bodily worship, which grows from them. — Al-Rāzī identifies heart then body; Abū Ḥayyān says freedom from pride produces both, with prostration the servant's closest state. Al-Biqāʿī says speech and action both return to the heart and cites the saying on nearness in prostration. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 7:206/q12 How can exclusive prostration to God fit the angels' prostration to Adam?  [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Which angels were commanded to prostrate, or whether a particular command qualifies the verse's general wording.
- 7:206/q12/p1 Only earthly angels prostrated to Adam, not the great heavenly angels. — Al-Rāzī cites al-Ghazālī and 15:30, 38:73. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 7:206/q12/p2 The command concerning Adam is a particular exception to a general statement. — Al-Rāzī reports that the particular prevails over the general, citing 15:30 and 38:73. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 7:206/q13 Why are the angels' worship and humility cited here?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the emphasis falls on emulation and rebuke, misplaced hopes for intercession, or spiritual joining through remembrance.
- 7:206/q13/p1 Their greater rank and constant devotion urge humans to worship and rebuke arrogant unbelievers. — Muqātil contrasts Meccans; al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, and Study Quran reason from more honored angels to humans. Al-Rāzī cites 19:31 and 15:99; al-Kashshāf stresses exclusive worship; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls rebuke of polytheists the primary hint and cites Ibn Dāra's poem for emulation. Kuran Yolu says worship lifts humans beyond animal limits. [26 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن وغيره); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:206/q13/p2 Their own worship challenges idolaters' expectation of angelic intercession. — Iṣlāḥī says idolaters slight God and the Prophet while expecting angels to intercede; the angels themselves glorify and prostrate. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 7:206/q13/p3 It calls the servant to join the company of those near God through remembrance. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects the verse to the previous command; Iṣlāḥī says earthly rememberers join the angelic assembly; Elmalılı says remembrance and prostration open the way from the Heights to Paradise. [3 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI]

## 7:206/q14 Does citing angels as models establish that they outrank prophets or humans?  [ٱلَّذِينَ عِندَ رَبِّكَ · theology]
turns on: Whether exhortation by an angelic model entails an absolute ranking.
- 7:206/q14/p1 Yes, since the Messenger is urged to worship by appeal to their worship. — Al-Rāzī reports Abū Bakr al-Aṣamm's argument. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو بكر الأصم); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 7:206/q14/p2 No; acquired human imitation of angelic traits can be more meritorious. — Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects the Muʿtazilite inference and says the human achievement is more remarkable. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:206/q15 What incident is reported behind the verse?  [* · reports]
- 7:206/q15/p1 Meccan unbelievers refused or questioned prostrating at the Prophet's command; the verse answered them with the angels' example. — Muqātil, al-Māwardī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and al-Kāshifī cite the objection in 25:60; al-Kāshifī says aversion increased. [4 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(كفار مكة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]

## 7:206/q16 How does the verse connect to the preceding commands and the sūra's story?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the link is chiefly to nearby remembrance commands or to the sūra's opening narrative.
- 7:206/q16/p1 It explains and motivates the preceding commands to listen, remember, and supplicate by the angels' example. — Al-Jishumī cites 7:204; Ibn ʿĀshūr says emphatic in marks a rationale rather than doubt and links 7:203–204 to believers' prostration; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says remembrance brings one among the near; Iṣlāḥī reads the ending as help to persevere against objections. [7 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 7:206/q16/p2 The ending recalls the opening Adam story: angels prostrate humbly whereas Iblīs refused proudly. — Al-Biqāʿī also relates it to the opening command to follow the Qurʾan; al-Sāmarrāʾī traces pride and prostration across the sūra. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 7:206/q17 What other Qurʾanic descriptions illuminate the angels here?  [* · links]
turns on: Which parallel passage or report supplies the connection.
- 7:206/q17/p1 Other verses describe their continuous glorification, standing, and prostration. — Al-Rāzī cites 37:165–166 and 39:75; Study Quran cites 13:13, 39:75, 42:5 and prostration at 16:49; the sky-groaning report has angels standing, bowing, and prostrating in every handspan. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI]
- 7:206/q17/p2 It echoes 4:172, where Jesus and angels do not disdain God's service. — Study Quran identifies the parallel. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 7:206/q18 What level of remembrance does this verse call for?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the emphasis is degree of continuity or inward as well as outward form.
- 7:206/q18/p1 Regular but intermittent remembrance below the angels' uninterrupted glorification. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares 21:19–20 and 41:38, where the near glorify without weariness. [2 notes: TABATABAI]
- 7:206/q18/p2 Remembrance includes inward awareness as well as speech and bodily action. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites creation's glorification and prostration at 17:44, 55:6, 13:15. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 7:206/q19 Why is this a place of recitation prostration?  [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law]
- 7:206/q19/p1 Reciters prostrate here to imitate the angels and oppose unbelievers' refusal; it is the first such place in muṣḥaf order. — Al-Māwardī, Ibn Kathīr, and Ibn ʿĀshūr call it first. Al-Ṭabrisī reports consensus; Ibn Kathīr extends consensus to listeners. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Ibn ʿAbbās that sajdah sites frustrate polytheists or emulate prophets, including David at 38:24 under 6:90. Bursevī adds remediation of deficiency as another general purpose. [16 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN]

## 7:206/q20 May one bow instead of prostrating at this marker?  [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law]
- 7:206/q20/p1 Yes, either bowing or prostration is allowed. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports al-Nakhaʿī via al-Naqqāsh. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النخعي)]

## 7:206/q21 How many recitation prostrations are counted in the Qurʾān?  [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · sciences]
turns on: Which sites are included, especially the second al-Ḥajj prostration, Ṣād, al-Ḥijr, and the short sūras; al-Qurṭubī cites varying reports and disagreement over commands.
- 7:206/q21/p1 Fifteen, including two in al-Ḥajj and the short sūras. — Ibn Ḥabīb, a report from Ibn Wahb, and Isḥāq are cited; Study Quran says fifteen. The report to ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ gives fifteen but ʿAbd al-Ḥaqq doubts Ibn Munayn; ʿUqbah's report for two in al-Ḥajj contains very weak Ibn Lahīʿah. ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ reports that the Prophet taught him fifteen prostration sites, specifying three in al-Mufaṣṣal and two in al-Ḥajj. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن حبيب وابن وهب وإسحاق,عبد الله بن منين,عقبة بن عامر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن وهب); STUDYQURAN; DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 7:206/q21/p2 Fourteen, counting Ṣād but omitting the second al-Ḥajj prostration. — Ibn Wahb and the Ḥanafīs; al-Qurṭubī prefers omitting second al-Ḥajj because its report is unsound. Abū Ḥayyān compares Abū Ḥanīfa's locations with al-Shāfiʿī's. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن وهب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حنيفة,ابن وهب); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 7:206/q21/p3 Fourteen, counting the second al-Ḥajj prostration but omitting Ṣād. — Al-Shāfiʿī's count; Abū Ḥayyān says the two fourteen-count positions differ in those locations. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حنيفة)]
- 7:206/q21/p4 Sixteen, adding al-Ḥijr 15:98. — Al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān report the addition. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(بعض العلماء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 7:206/q21/p5 Eleven, omitting second al-Ḥajj and three in al-Mufaṣṣal. — The famous Mālikī count, also attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn ʿUmar; Abū al-Dardāʾ reports eleven with the Prophet and lists the sites. Abū al-Dardāʾ specifies the eleven places where he prostrated with the Prophet: al-Aʿrāf, al-Raʿd, al-Naḥl, Banī Isrāʾīl, Maryam, al-Ḥajj, al-Furqān, al-Naml, al-Sajdah, Ṣād, and one of the Ḥawāmīm; none was in al-Mufaṣṣal. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الدرداء,مالك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مالك); DURR-FULL(أبو الدرداء)]
- 7:206/q21/p6 Ten, omitting second al-Ḥajj, Ṣād, and three in al-Mufaṣṣal. — Attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās. Ibn ʿAbbās lists the ten sites as al-Aʿrāf, al-Raʿd, al-Naḥl, Banī Isrāʾīl, Maryam, one in al-Ḥajj, al-Naml, al-Furqān, al-Sajdah, and Ḥā Mīm al-Sajdah, with none in al-Mufaṣṣal. ʿAṭāʾ reports a ten-site list from ʿAlī ibn ʿAbbās naming the first al-Ḥajj prostration and the same other sūras. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عطاء)]
- 7:206/q21/p7 Four: al-Sajdah, Fussilat, al-Najm, and al-ʿAlaq. — Reported by al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 7:206/q21/p8 Twelve. — Al-Kāshifī as reported by Bursevī; he notes al-Shāfiʿī and Aḥmad accept the last al-Ḥajj sajdah while Abū Ḥanīfa excludes it, and schools differ on Ṣād because its wording mentions bowing though the Prophet prostrated. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]

## 7:206/q22 At which words in Fussilat is its recitation prostration?  [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law]
turns on: Whether precaution favors the later phrase or the report locating it at the earlier phrase.
- 7:206/q22/p1 At 41:38, lā yasʾamūn. — Bursevī follows ʿUmar and Ibn Masʿūd as precaution: delaying is allowed while doing it too early is not. [1 notes: BURSEVI(عمر وابن مسعود)+]
- 7:206/q22/p2 At 41:37, in kuntum iyyāhu taʿbudūn. — Bursevī reports this from ʿAlī and says al-Shāfiʿī follows it. [1 notes: BURSEVI(علي)]

## 7:206/q23 Is recitation prostration obligatory?  [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law]
turns on: Whether the command, Satan report, and prophetic practice imply obligation or recommendation; al-Qurṭubī relies on ʿUmar's public statement.
- 7:206/q23/p1 No, it is a confirmed sunnah or recommended. — Al-Ṭabrisī names al-Shāfiʿī and his companions; al-Qurṭubī cites Mālik, al-Shāfiʿī, and ʿUmar's public statement before Companions without objection, which he treats as consensus. He says the Satan report concerns an obligatory prostration elsewhere and prophetic practice indicates recommendation. The majority and Study Quran agree. [8 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(عمر,مالك والشافعي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 7:206/q23/p2 Yes, it is immediately obligatory on reciter and listener and made up if missed. — Abū Ḥanīfa cites the unqualified command, Satan's grief at Adam's child's obedience and reward, and the Prophet's practice; Bursevī states its immediate and make-up consequences. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 7:206/q24 Who is required or invited to prostrate when the verse is heard?  [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law]
- 7:206/q24/p1 The reciter and attentive listener prostrate; an incidental hearer is not obligated. — Ibn Kathīr says there is consensus for reciter and listener; Abū Ḥanīfa requires it for one who intended to listen; ʿUthmān limits it to attentive listening. ʿImrān's answer implies no duty for an unprepared hearer; Salmān and al-Sāʾib declined in relevant settings. Ibn ʿAmr reports that when the Prophet recited a sūra containing a prostration, he prostrated and the Companions followed, sometimes so crowded that they could find no room for their foreheads. [7 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حنيفة); QURTUBI-FULL(السائب,سلمان,عثمان,عمران بن حصين); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 7:206/q25 What conditions apply to recitation prostration?  [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law]
turns on: Whether recitation prostration takes the purity conditions of prayer.
- 7:206/q25/p1 Prayer-like conditions apply: purity, intention, qibla, and permissible time. — Al-Qurṭubī lists the conditions; Abū Ḥayyān adds freedom from impurity and ritual defilement. Al-Zuhrī requires purity and qibla at home but allows a mounted traveler any direction. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الزهري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 7:206/q25/p2 Purity is not required. — Al-Bukhārī reports Ibn ʿUmar prostrating without purity; Ibn al-Mundhir and Ibn al-Munkadir report al-Shaʿbī likewise. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عمر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عمر)]

## 7:206/q26 What takbīr and hand raising accompany the prostration?  [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law]
turns on: Whether rules inside prayer carry over outside it, especially hand raising and takbīr.
- 7:206/q26/p1 Say takbīr and raise the hands for it. — Al-Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad, and Isḥāq are cited by al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān; Ibn ʿUmar reports the Prophet saying takbīr on descent and ascent. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عمر,الشافعي وأحمد وإسحاق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشافعي)]
- 7:206/q26/p2 Say takbīr on descent and ascent within prayer; hand raising applies within prayer, with differing reports outside. — Mālik's well-known position; al-Qurṭubī adds most jurists require takbīr outside prayer, and Abū Ḥayyān notes the varying reports. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مالك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مالك)]
- 7:206/q26/p3 For a seated worshipper, stand with takbīr, prostrate with takbīr, then sit. — Bursevī says this is fuller and unnecessary delay is disliked. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 7:206/q27 Does recitation prostration end with salām?  [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law]
turns on: Whether analogy to prayer requires an exit salām; the two compilers give opposite majority attributions.
- 7:206/q27/p1 No salām is required. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this as majority practice; Abū Ḥayyān instead attributes no salām to a group of early authorities and Isḥāq. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 7:206/q27/p2 Salām is required. — Ibn al-ʿArabī favors it because an act entered with takbīr needs an exit as prayer does, more so than funeral prayer. Al-Qurṭubī attributes this to early authorities and Isḥāq, while Abū Ḥayyān calls it the majority view. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)+]

## 7:206/q28 At what times may recitation prostration be performed?  [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law]
turns on: How a cause-specific sajdah interacts with bans on prayer after dawn and ʿAṣr, and why those bans apply.
- 7:206/q28/p1 At any time, as prayer with a specific cause. — Al-Shāfiʿī and others are reported by al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي وجماعة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشافعي)]
- 7:206/q28/p2 Until dawn brightens or until the sun yellows after ʿAṣr. — One Mālikī view reported by al-Qurṭubī. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 7:206/q28/p3 Not after dawn and not after ʿAṣr. — One Mālikī view reported by al-Qurṭubī; Abū Ḥayyān also reports bans after dawn and afternoon prayers. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 7:206/q28/p4 Allowed after dawn but not after ʿAṣr. — Another Mālikī view in al-Qurṭubī. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 7:206/q28/p5 Not after sunrise until the sun is high. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this among Mālikī views. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 7:206/q28/p6 Not after dawn only. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this among Mālikī views; al-Qurṭubī says these debates turn on the general prayer ban and its rationale. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 7:206/q29 May one perform it during an obligatory prayer?  [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law]
turns on: Whether an extra sajdah is intrinsically excluded or only avoided to prevent congregational confusion.
- 7:206/q29/p1 No, in any obligatory prayer, whether alone or together and whether recited quietly or aloud. — Mālik's well-known ruling regards it as an added prostration. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مالك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مالك)]
- 7:206/q29/p2 Yes where the congregation will not be confused, especially when alone. — Another rationale in al-Qurṭubī is avoiding confusion. The Abū Rāfiʿ report describes Abū Hurayra prostrating during ʿIshāʾ at al-Inshiqāq, citing the Prophet's practice. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو رافع,قيل)]

## 7:206/q30 May it be performed in voluntary congregational prayer when it could confuse worshippers?  [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law]
turns on: How much weight to give possible disruption in voluntary group prayer.
- 7:206/q30/p1 Yes, even if confusion is feared; certainly when it is not feared. — Al-Qurṭubī states this as the ruling, including solitary voluntary prayer. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 7:206/q30/p2 No when it may confuse the congregation. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this alternative. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]

## 7:206/q31 What should be said during recitation prostration?  [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · law]
turns on: Which transmitted supplication or glorification is used.
- 7:206/q31/p1 Recite glorification, especially subḥāna rabbī al-aʿlā three times. — Bursevī prefers this and also gives alternatives asking the Merciful for forgiveness or the Turner of hearts for steadfastness; al-Asrār al-Muḥammadiyya favors the latter. His cited Persian scholar gives site-specific requests at al-Isrāʾ, al-Aʿrāf or al-Sajdah, and al-Najm. [3 notes: BURSEVI(ابن فخر الدين الرومي)]
- 7:206/q31/p2 Use the Prophet's supplication about one's face prostrating to its Creator, with requests for reward and pardon. — Bursevī reports creation, hearing, sight, provision, and acceptance; ʿĀʾisha reports repeated use at night, graded authentic by al-Tirmidhī. [2 notes: BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام); ALUSI-FULL(عائشة)]
- 7:206/q31/p3 Ask God to lift burden, record reward, and store the sajdah as treasure. — Ibn ʿAbbās transmits this Prophetic supplication in al-Qurṭubī. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 7:206/q31/p4 Ask for useful knowledge and deeds that raise one's rank. — Ibn ʿUmar's Prophetic report in al-Ālūsī. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عمر)]

## 7:206/q32 What merit or warning is reported for prostrating?  [وَلَهُۥ يَسْجُدُونَ · reports]
turns on: Which Prophetic report is being cited.
- 7:206/q32/p1 Satan withdraws weeping when a human prostrates: the human obeys and gains Paradise, while he refused and faces Fire. — Reported from Abū Hurayra and in multiple commentaries; al-Qurṭubī says its commanded prostration concerns an obligation, while the Prophet's regular practice supports recommendation here. [7 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(النبي); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة); ELMALILI(Aleyhissalâtü vesselâm)]
- 7:206/q32/p2 Each prostration raises a degree and removes a sin, or grants both. — Al-Baghawī reports Thawbān from the Prophet; al-Ālūsī reports ʿĀʾisha. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(ثوبان); ALUSI-FULL(عائشة)]

## 7:206/q33 What virtue is reported for reciting Sūrat al-Aʿrāf?  [* · reports]
- 7:206/q33/p1 It brings a screen from Iblīs on Judgment Day and Adam's intercession. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Bayḍāwī report this from the Prophet. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 7:206/q34 What spiritual counsel is drawn from prostration here?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether counsel focuses on chosen human prostration or universal submission.
- 7:206/q34/p1 Prostration is the proper response to divine command and a source of human honor. — Bursevī's Persian verses shame refusal, the Shaykh al-Islām's maxim censures a head absent from prostration, and another couplet couples generosity with nobility and prostration with honor. [3 notes: BURSEVI(شيخ الإسلام)]
- 7:206/q34/p2 All beings already submit to God's power before and during existence. — Bursevī says pre-eternal nonexistence bowed to God's power to create; existing beings surrender to His decrees of annihilation, creation, and preservation. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 7:206/q35 What theological limits are set on inward readings?  [* · theology]
- 7:206/q35/p1 God remains transcendent; talk of annihilation cannot imply indwelling, union, fusion, or identity of God and servant. — Bursevī explicitly rejects these claims and says God remains as in eternity. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 7:206/q36 Is quiet remembrance preferable?  [* · interpretation]
- 7:206/q36/p1 Yes, quiet remembrance is best. — Al-Ālūsī draws the inference and cites Aḥmad's report, 'The best remembrance is silent.' He also condemns contemporary ignorant Sufis' religiously and rationally objectionable practices. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:206/q37 How does this sūra's ending suit its theme?  [* · links]
- 7:206/q37/p1 It closes by urging worship through the angels' example. — Al-Itqān compares distinctive endings of al-Māʾida, al-Anʿām, al-Anfāl, al-Tawba, Yūnus, Hūd, Yūsuf, and al-Raʿd; Kuran Yolu says the close calls for praise by heart and tongue, humble prostration, and preferring God's pleasure to desire. [2 notes: ITQAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 7:206/q38 How are al-Anfāl and Barāʾa arranged after al-Aʿrāf?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Length, thematic and opening links, codex order, and whether arrangement was fixed by revelation.
- 7:206/q38/p1 They form or function as a pair among the seven long sūras. — Al-Ālūsī reports early authorities treating the two as one and al-Naḥḥās's report from ʿUthmān that they were called twin sūras in the Prophet's time. He lists divergent seventh-sūra identifications: Yūnus, al-Kahf, Barāʾa, or the pair. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(جماعة من السلف,عثمان)]
- 7:206/q38/p2 Barāʾa's great length warrants placement here. — Al-Ālūsī reports its affinity to the preceding long sūras and says shifting it after six shorter ones weakens the connection. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:206/q38/p3 Their placement reflects judgment because their order was not fixed by revelation. — A reported view says placing the pair among the seven long sūras shows the Prophet died before clarifying the order. Al-Ālūsī calls this far-fetched: a Jewish scholar's question and ʿUthmān's reply are not explicit evidence; he also rejects an earlier rationale that conflicts with the question's apparent implication for Barāʾa's missing basmala. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 7:206/q38/p4 Yūnus belongs with the next five sūras rather than immediately after al-Aʿrāf. — Al-Ālūsī favors their shared stories, alif-lām-rā openings, Book references, Meccan origin, comparable length except al-Ḥijr, and prophet names; he notes al-Raʿd's angelic name and qualified opening-pattern parallels. An alternative would move Yūnus and Hūd as in Ubayy's codex. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 7:206/q38/p5 Other codex ordering can follow descending sūra length and local thematic links. — Al-Ālūsī says Ibn Masʿūd placed al-Anfāl after al-Nūr, whose promise of inheriting land is fulfilled by al-Anfāl's recall of earlier weakness (24:55, 8:26). He says he has an additional explanation for the link to the previous sūra. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:206/q39 What background reports about the sūra are appended here?  [* · sciences]
turns on: These are separate appended reports on historical setting, commentary completion, and future communal trouble.
- 7:206/q39/p1 Al-Aʿrāf is classed as late Meccan and reportedly followed Ṣād and preceded al-Anʿām. — Study Quran's introduction also summarizes its warning about rejecting signs and messengers through earlier prophetic stories and their consequences. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 7:206/q39/p2 Bursevī completed the commentary in early Ṣafar 1101 AH before proceeding to al-Anfāl. — His colophon records the first ten days of the month. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 7:206/q39/p3 Gabriel warned the Prophet of later discord, unjust rulers, and misguided reciters, with restraint and patience as protection. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmits ʿUmar's report about the Prophet. [1 notes: TABATABAI(عمر بن الخطاب عن النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]

## 7:206/q40 How does Ibn Kathīr urge believers to emulate the angels?  [* · interpretation]
- 7:206/q40/p1 Imitate their abundant, constant worship and orderly prayer rows. — Ibn Kathīr says their unceasing glorification and prostration invite devotion; he cites a hadith urging Muslims to fill first rows and stand close together as angels do. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 7:206/q41 Why do counts of Qurʾanic prostration sites differ?  [* · sciences]
- 7:206/q41/p1 Reports and practice vary, as does interpretation of prostration commands. — Al-Qurṭubī says some read commands as recitation prostration and others as required prayer prostration. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 7:206/q42 Why do rulings on permitted times differ?  [* · law]
- 7:206/q42/p1 Scholars weigh the recitation sajdah against general bans on prayer after dawn and ʿAṣr and differ over those bans' rationale. — Al-Qurṭubī's explanation. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
