# 83:28: 11 questions

## 83:28/q01 What is the spring called Tasnim?  [عَيْنًۭا · referent]
- 83:28/q01/p1 A spring or drink of Paradise called Tasnim, esteemed as its finest or highest drink. — Al-Damghani says ʿayn may denote a Paradise drink or spring and compares 76:6. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kur'an Yolu say this verse identifies the Tasnim of the preceding verse as a spring. Abu Salih calls it the noblest drink; Ibn ʿAbbas calls it the highest, and Ibn ʿAtiyya's report calls it the finest. Study Quran cites most commentators for its purity and nobility. Al-Nahhas reports Mujahid's derivation from elevation: elevated water, a raised grave, and a camel's hump; Elmalılı derives the name from raising like a hump and applies it to a high fountain. [10 notes: DAMGHANI; TAB-FULL(أبو صالح); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI(İbn Abbas); NAHHAS(مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Most commentators)]

## 83:28/q02 Where does the spring originate?  [عَيْنًۭا · reports]
turns on: The reported origin: Eden, Paradise's upper reaches, or beneath the Throne.
- 83:28/q02/p1 It comes from Eden, the core of Paradise. — Muqatil describes Eden as Paradise's core. Hudhayfa locates the spring in Eden; al-Qurtubi associates the foremost drinkers with Eden. [3 notes: MUQATIL; DURR-FULL(حذيفة بن اليمان); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 83:28/q02/p2 It descends from the upper or highest part of Paradise. — Al-Dahhak says it flows from Paradise's highest part and no spring there is more honored. Al-Zajjaj describes its descent from the chambers; al-Tabari and al-Kalbi say it flows down upon the Garden's people. Elmalılı transmits al-Kalbi's account. [5 notes: NAHHAS(الضحاك); ZAJJAJ; TAB-FULL; DURR-FULL(الكلبي); ELMALILI]
- 83:28/q02/p3 It flows from beneath the Throne. — Ibn Zayd says this is what they were told; al-Qurtubi adds that Hasan's mursal reports say the same. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد)]

## 83:28/q03 How does the spring reach the drinkers?  [عَيْنًۭا · reports]
turns on: Whether the account merely describes descent or specifies an airborne, measured delivery into vessels.
- 83:28/q03/p1 It descends or flows down to them from above. — Muqatil says it descends from Eden. Al-Zajjaj says it wafts toward the people from the chambers. Al-Tabari concludes that it flows down from above. Al-Dahhak and al-Kalbi place its downward flow above the drinkers, as Elmalılı also reports. [6 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(الكلبي); ELMALILI]
- 83:28/q03/p2 It flows through the air and fills each vessel in a measured amount. — Qatada says God's power suspends the flow in the air: it stops when a vessel fills, spills no drop on the ground, and removes the need to draw water. Elmalılı reports a similar aerial flow that pours enough into each vessel and elevates the elevated drinkers. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI(adı verilmeyen görüş)]

## 83:28/q04 Why is ʿaynan accusative?  [عَيْنًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether an omitted verb of praise or giving drink, circumstantial relation to a definite name, Tasnim's verbal derivation and tanwin, or reversed construction governs the accusative.
- 83:28/q04/p1 It is an accusative of praise or specification, with an understood 'I mean a spring'. — Basran grammarians, Muhammad ibn Yazid as transmitted by Ali ibn Sulayman, al-Mubarrad, al-Zamakhshari, and later authors supply an omitted aʿni. Muhammad ibn Yazid says only this analysis works. Ibn ʿAtiyya and al-Qurtubi call it praise; Bursevi and Tabataba'i include specification. Alusi contrasts this with al-Zajjaj's circumstantial analysis. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(نحويّو البصرة); NAHHAS(محمد بن يزيد); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(المبرد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 83:28/q04/p2 It is a circumstantial accusative from the definite Tasnim. — Al-Zajjaj makes Tasnim definite and ʿaynan indefinite. Al-Nahhas treats Tasnim as a feminine proper water name given tanwin by construing it as masculine water, and calls ʿaynan jāriyan circumstantial; he prefers the proper-name premise. Al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, and al-Samin report the analysis. Alusi says it can work if specified by the following clause or interpreted adjectivally. Ibn ʿAtiyya calls this far-fetched; al-Samin objects that Tasnim is a proper name for a concrete thing. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج); ALUSI-FULL; NAHHAS+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأخفش)-]
- 83:28/q04/p3 It is the object of an implied yusqawn, 'they are given a spring to drink'. — Basran grammarians posit an omitted yusqawn; al-Zajjaj glosses this as drinking from a spring. Al-Akhfash is reported by al-Nahhas, al-Wahidi, al-Qurtubi, and al-Samin. Ibn ʿAtiyya calls the proposed yusqawn analysis far-fetched. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(نحويّو البصرة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الأخفش); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج والأخفش); QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأخفش)-]
- 83:28/q04/p4 It is governed by Tasnim taken as a verbal noun, or by an implied construction involving an indefinite spring. — Al-Farra offers an indefinite spring apposed to Tasnim and a welling-water construction, both yielding accusative; he cites 90:14, 77:25, and 5:95. Kufan grammarians posit an indefinite ʿayn after min Tasnim, then the accusative after tanwin, citing 90:15 and 77:25. Al-Nahhas reports al-Farra's 90:14–15 analogy. Ibn ʿAtiyya allows Tasnim as governing verbal noun; al-Qurtubi reports al-Farra's governing-object analysis. Al-Wahidi, on derivation from sanam, calls it causal and cites the hungry-day orphan verse. [9 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(نحويّو الكوفة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج,الفراء)]
- 83:28/q04/p5 It is explained by reversing the phrase as 'Tasnim, a spring'. — Al-Nahhas records this fourth parsing without further argument. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 83:28/q05 What does the bāʾ in yashrabu bihā convey?  [يَشْرَبُ بِهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether bāʾ is partitive, extra, causal or instrumental, or locative; also whether yashrabu carries an added sense of quenching or mixing.
- 83:28/q05/p1 They drink from the spring; bāʾ has the sense 'from'. — Ibn Qutayba gives this as one reading; al-Thaʿlabi, al-Baghawi, and al-Nasafi gloss it explicitly. Al-Wahidi's direct drinking and al-Biqaʿi's reported direct spring drinking fit this sense. Al-Baydawi compares 76:6. Ibn ʿAshur attributes partitive bāʾ to al-Asmaʿi, al-Farisi, Ibn Qutayba, Ibn Malik, and Kufan grammarians, but finds the partitive explanation unclear since Arabic normally uses bāʾ with drink. Islahi rejects this as vague and redundant because Tasnim has already been named as the source. [13 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(بعضهم); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الأصمعي والفارسي وابن قتيبة وابن مالك); IBNASHUR-FULL-; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 83:28/q05/p2 The bāʾ is extra, so the verb means 'they drink it'. — Ibn Qutayba also allows the spring as direct object. Ibn ʿAtiyya cites Abu Dhuʾayb's line with sharibna bi-māʾ al-baḥr; al-Biqaʿi? No: al-Samin, Bursevi, and Alusi state the extra-bāʾ option. Ibn ʿAshur cites the same Abu Dhuʾayb line about clouds drinking sea water and rising. Elmalılı proposes the fountain itself as the nearest's wine. [9 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو ذؤيب الهذلي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 83:28/q05/p3 They are refreshed, satisfied, or delighted by the spring. — Al-Akhfash includes the sense of being refreshed; al-Samin reads yashrabu with yurwa, quenching. Alusi lists satisfaction, enjoyment, and sufficiency. Elmalılı proposes satiety and delight through Tasnim. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 83:28/q05/p4 The bāʾ marks mixing: they drink nectar by means of Tasnim as its blend. — Al-Biqaʿi takes bāʾ as instrument or cause of the mixture. Ibn ʿAshur gives it causal force, with yashrabu including 'mix', and cites 83:27. Alusi lists mixing but questions it. Elmalılı notes the blended-drink rendering may appear natural, yet says it obscures the nearest's distinction and special tie to Tasnim. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI-]
- 83:28/q05/p5 They drink the nectar beside the spring; bāʾ marks accompaniment or location. — Ibn ʿAshur supplies an omitted object, the nectar, and has the drinkers gathered around the spring; he suggests this is what the grammarians meant by bāʾ. Islahi makes bāʾ locative, comparing 76:6: favored wine is drunk beside a river, in contrast to worldly wine consumed anywhere. He prefers this to the 'from' gloss. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 83:28/q06 How do the brought-near drink Tasnim compared with others?  [عَيْنًۭا يَشْرَبُ بِهَا ٱلْمُقَرَّبُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the nearest receive Tasnim unmixed or as a blend, and whether those below them share any Tasnim.
- 83:28/q06/p1 They drink Tasnim pure and unmixed; the righteous or other Garden dwellers receive it blended into their drink. — This is transmitted from Abdullah or Ibn Masʿud, Ibn Abbas, Abu Salih, Qatada, Masruq, Hudhayfa, Hasan, Malik ibn al-Harith, and Muqatil; al-Tabari calls it the view of most exegetes and adopts it. Variants name recipients the companions of the right, the righteous, those below the nearest, or all other Garden people. Masruq says the blend sweetens their cups; Hudhayfa says it flows from Eden to them. Al-Baydawi says pure drink suits those occupied only with God; al-Tabatabaʿi sees Tasnim's superiority in 83:27. Elmalılı prefers this transmitted distinction; Samarraʾi says reward matches devotion. [35 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو صالح,ابن عباس,سائر أهل التأويل,عبد الله,قتادة); SULAMI(بعضهم); WAHIDI-BASIT(عبد الله ومالك بن الحارث ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن مسعود وابن عباس,قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود); TABRISI(ابن مسعود وابن عباس,مسروق); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود); QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن مسعود، ابن عباس، الحسن، أبو صالح); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن مسعود، ابن عباس، مسروق، قتادة); BIQAI-FULL(بعضهم); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود,حذيفة بن اليمان); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود,الجمهور); ELMALILI(İbn Abbas); TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; STUDYQURAN(Others,Some); ELMALILI(İbn Abbas, İbn Mes‘ûd, Hasan, Ebû Sâlih)+]
- 83:28/q06/p2 The brought-near drink wine or nectar blended with Tasnim. — Muqatil says the close ones of Eden drink wine mixed with its water. Ibn ʿAshur takes the near ones as mixing nectar with Tasnim under the causal bāʾ, as 83:27 says; this does not specify whether others have access. Elmalılı reports the reading that they drink from Tasnim while blending it with sealed nectar, but finds that blended rendering unable to explain their special distinction. [4 notes: MUQATIL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; ELMALILI-]
- 83:28/q06/p3 Even blended Tasnim is reserved for the nearest; lower-ranking righteous people cannot drink it. — Al-Biqaʿi says the nearest drink the spring's blend while others have no access to the spring itself. Elmalılı reports a view under which the nearest cannot drink it pure either. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI(başka bir görüş)]

## 83:28/q07 Who are the muqarrabūn?  [ٱلْمُقَرَّبُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether nearness denotes a higher human rank, all the righteous, or angels.
- 83:28/q07/p1 They are the foremost people brought near to God, a rank above the righteous companions of the right. — Most commentators distinguish the two groups, as Abu Hayyan and Alusi report. Elmalılı invokes the hierarchy of 56:10–11, the nearest witnessing the record in 83:21, and a saying contrasting the two ranks; he rejects treating the labels as synonyms. Alusi says the nearest are absorbed in God's love; al-Biqaʿi says God draws them near. Qashani characterizes the perfected, arrived near ones as steadfast in detailed realization. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); BIQAI-FULL; QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور); ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; ELMALILI(bazıları)+]
- 83:28/q07/p2 The near ones and the righteous are synonymous labels for all the blessed in Paradise. — Abu Hayyan and Alusi report this minority identification. Ibn ʿAshur identifies the brought-near here with the righteous, saying those who drink the water are themselves brought near. Elmalılı rejects synonymy because it misses the distinction and force of the verse. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL(قوم); IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI(bazıları)-]
- 83:28/q07/p3 They are angels, the servants nearest to God's honor. — Al-Jishumi explicitly identifies the muqarrabun with angels brought near to divine honor. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 83:28/q08 What spiritual meaning is drawn from their drink?  [عَيْنًۭا يَشْرَبُ بِهَا ٱلْمُقَرَّبُونَ · inward]
turns on: The spiritual contrast may concern pure vision versus mixed attention, the scene of intimacy, or distinctions among stations.
- 83:28/q08/p1 Pure Tasnim represents unalloyed knowledge, love, and vision of God; mixed drink represents attention also to created delights. — Bursevi calls Tasnim knowledge and love of God and joy in seeing His face, contrasting pure with mixed love. Elmalılı reports Razi's graded rivers: Tasnim is knowledge and vision of God, while sealed nectar is contemplation of creation. Study Quran reports Razi's view that the near ones see only God, whereas the people of the right also attend to created pleasures. Al-Baydawi says the nearest occupied themselves only with God; Alusi similarly cites absorption in divine love. Al-Sulami reports that attributes alone do not bring all to witness the Essence, whereas the near ones bear Essence and attributes. [6 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور); ELMALILI(Râzî); STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)]
- 83:28/q08/p2 Their drinking depicts intimate nearness to and beholding of God. — Al-Wasiti says they drink pure while witnessing the beloved. Al-Jurayri locates them on the carpet of nearness, amid intimacy and gardens of holiness, drinking a cup of contentment while beholding God. Al-Thaʿlabi relays al-Hariri and al-Wasiti's image of closeness, intimacy, contentment, and witnessing. [3 notes: SULAMI(الجريري,الواسطي); THALABI(الحريري والواسطي)]
- 83:28/q08/p3 The different drink names mark distinct spiritual stations even among the near ones. — Qashani says Tasnim's near ones stand by God in detailed steadfastness and realize unity of Essence, while 'servants of God' and kafur signal subjugation, annihilation, and undifferentiated unity. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 83:28/q09 How does this verse connect to other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes point to different textual connections: the immediate Tasnim clause, an analogous spring, unseen delights, or the hierarchy of ranks.
- 83:28/q09/p1 It clarifies the Tasnim named as the blend in 83:27. — Ibn ʿAshur and Kur'an Yolu say 83:28 identifies that spring. Tabataba'i calls the drinking clause an explanation of Tasnim and uses 83:27 to infer the superior pure drink. Ibn ʿAshur also cites 83:27 for the alternative blending parse. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]
- 83:28/q09/p2 The spring recalls the drink of God's servants in 76:5–6. — Al-Baydawi applies the same discussion of bāʾ as in 76:6. Asad connects the two drinks; al-Damghani uses 76:6 to illustrate ʿayn as a Paradise spring. Islahi compares the riverside drinking image with 76:6. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; DAMGHANI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 83:28/q09/p3 The spring belongs with the Garden's unseen delights in 32:17. — Al-Qurtubi makes this explicit connection. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 83:28/q09/p4 The higher drink follows the verse's and al-Waqiʿa's distinction between the nearest and the righteous. — Elmalılı cites 83:21, where the nearest witness the record, and 56:10–11 for their higher rank. Study Quran lists 56:8, 27, 38, 90–91, 74:39, and 90:18 for the companions of the right. Samarraʾi compares 83:18, 25, 27 and says the higher reward matches greater devotion. [5 notes: ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(Some); SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 83:28/q10 What does the spring scene contribute to the passage's account of reward?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The comments either explain the graded drink or describe the quality of Paradise's blessings generally.
- 83:28/q10/p1 The scene shows a higher reward for the brought-near, fitting their higher rank and devotion. — Elmalılı finds an ascending sequence from sealed nectar to Tasnim, parallel to the nearest above the righteous. Tabataba'i says pure Tasnim demonstrates the spring's superiority and the nearest's rank. Samarraʾi says the mixed and pure drinks match their devotion. Islahi says the pure wine is properly enjoyed beside a river and contrasts its etiquette with worldly wine. [4 notes: ELMALILI; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 83:28/q10/p2 The Garden's blessings form a life of peace, happiness, and virtue without worldly harms. — Kur'an Yolu specifies the absence of envy, conflict, unhappiness, injustice, illness, grief, and similar harms. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 83:28/q11 How does this passage move between the righteous and the wicked?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes emphasize either the passage's transition or the explanation of the criminals' fate.
- 83:28/q11/p1 After describing the faithful's afterlife fate, it turns to worldly and final conditions of the faithful and fraudulent. — Islahi says 83:18–28 describes the loyal, faithful, and God-fearing. Elmalılı says that after giving the fraudulent and righteous their due afterlife fates, the passage turns to their conditions in this life and the next. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI]
- 83:28/q11/p2 The criminals' punishment includes believers' satisfaction at their enemies' fate. — Al-Biqaʿi says the criminals wasted worldly life, lost what truly benefits them, and become the object of believers' satisfaction. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
