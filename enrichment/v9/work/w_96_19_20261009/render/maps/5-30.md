# 5:30: 29 questions

## 5:30/q01 What does ṭawwaʿat say the soul did?  [فَطَوَّعَتْ نَفْسُهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the stress is on ease, compliance, impulse, or gradual persuasion.
- 5:30/q01/p1 It made the murder easy, attractive, or permissible. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Azharī derive ease from yielding; Qatāda says adorned, al-Akhfash says permitted. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Bursevī, Ālūsī and Elmalılı compare spacious pasture. Rāzī says it made a grave act seem possible. [54 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(قتادة); NAHHAS; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة,يمان); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة,يمان); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو الحسن الأخفش,قتادة); RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش,الزمخشري,قتادة); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور,الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN(commentators); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأزهري,الزجاجي)+]
- 5:30/q01/p2 It complied with, followed, assisted, or consented to Cain. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Mubarrad and Ibn Qutayba trace the verb to willing compliance; al-Zajjāj cites branches coming within reach. Jishumī cites huwa ṭawʿa. Al-Naḥḥās calls compliance ordinary Arabic; al-Ṭabrisī distinguishes spontaneous ṭāʿa from command-obedience aṭāʿa. [34 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ(أبو العباس محمد بن يزيد المبرد); NAHHAS(أبو جعفر); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,الكلبي,المبرد,عبد العزيز بن يحيى); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,ابن قتيبة,المبرد); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الهروي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن قتيبة,المبرد); TABATABAI(الراغب,قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 5:30/q01/p3 It encouraged, impelled, or emboldened him. — Mujāhid supplies the gloss repeatedly; Ibn ʿAbbās describes enticement. Abū ʿUbayd explains the encouragement as the soul answering Cain's wish. [19 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مجاهد); JISHUMI(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عباس ومجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(commentators)]
- 5:30/q01/p4 It gradually overcame moral resistance. — Al-Biqāʿī says the intensive form marks strenuous persuasion because murder is awe-inspiring; Ibn ʿĀshūr depicts resistant murder as subdued. Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers repeated whispering in tafaʿʿul. Samarrāʾī contrasts stronger ṭawwaʿat with sawwalat in 20:96 and 12:18. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; TABATABAI+]

## 5:30/q02 How is faṭawwaʿat read?  [فَطَوَّعَتْ · readings]
turns on: Whether form III is reciprocal or shares the simple or causative sense.
- 5:30/q02/p1 Al-Ḥasan and others read faṭāwaʿat: the murder called and the soul yielded. — Ibn Jinnī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn list al-Ḥasan, Zayd ibn ʿAlī, al-Jarrāḥ, al-Ḥasan ibn ʿImrān and Abū Wāqid. Al-Zamakhsharī and Abū Ḥayyān read reciprocal interaction and initial resistance. [12 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن بن أبي الحسن والجراح والحسن بن عمران وأبو واقد); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن وزيد بن علي والجراح والحسن بن عمران وأبو واقد,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن وزيد بن علي,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); TABATABAI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 5:30/q02/p2 Faṭāwaʿat can carry a form-I or form-II sense without reciprocal action. — Al-Zamakhsharī offers this alternative; Abū Ḥayyān cites Sībawayh's examples and faults Ibn ʿAṣfūr and Ibn Mālik for omitting the pattern. Al-Samīn and Ālūsī concur. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]
- 5:30/q02/p3 The majority reads faṭawwaʿat with doubled wāw, a causative making murder yield. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Samīn describe the majority form and its causative object; Elmalılı calls it taṭwīʿ from ṭawʿ. [6 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور); ELMALILI]

## 5:30/q03 Why narrate persuasion before the actual killing?  [فَطَوَّعَتْ فَقَتَلَهُۥ · rhetoric]
turns on: The rhetorical effect of sequencing and kinship.
- 5:30/q03/p1 It reveals temptation, hesitation and the causal sequence. — Al-Biqāʿī says Abel's warning ought to deter Cain but hardened him; persuasion caused murder and murder loss. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees an elided interval of hesitation and purposeful elaboration; Ālūsī proposes renewed resolve or confidence in Abel's nonresistance. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 5:30/q03/p2 Calling Abel his brother heightens condemnation and cruelty. — Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr stress kinship and owed mercy; al-Qurṭubī draws the envy lesson. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 5:30/q04 Who creates the motives behind the soul's persuasion?  [فَطَوَّعَتْ نَفْسُهُۥ · theology]
turns on: Whether attribution to a human soul conflicts with divine creation.
- 5:30/q04/p1 The beautifying should be attributed to God if He creates all things. — Rāzī reports the Muʿtazilī objection to attributing the easing to Cain's soul. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة)]
- 5:30/q04/p2 Acts are attributed to their motives, and God creates those motives and acts. — Rāzī replies that ordinary attribution to the soul is compatible with divine creation. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 5:30/q05 What does lahu add?  [لَهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether lām is connective or compensates for an omitted preposition.
- 5:30/q05/p1 An added connective strengthens or clarifies the relation. — Al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and Bursevī compare 'I guarded Zayd his property.' Ālūsī compares 94:1 and rejects the claim that it excludes another persuader. [7 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 5:30/q05/p2 It replaces an omitted ʿalā. — Abū al-Baqāʾ says the underlying meaning is compliance with him regarding the killing, and rejects direct transitivity without lām. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]

## 5:30/q06 What does nafsuhu refer to?  [نَفْسُهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether the broad self or its particular passion is foregrounded.
- 5:30/q06/p1 Cain's soul or self, the source of immoral desire. — Study Quran notes the moral self and the passions, citing 12:53; al-Qushayrī says corrupt intentions mute admonition. Kuran Yolu says jealousy overwhelmed advice. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(al-Qushayrī)]
- 5:30/q06/p2 His passionate determination. — Asad prefers 'passion' among soul, mind and self, citing Qāmūs and Asās. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES+]

## 5:30/q07 How is qatla akhīhi construed?  [قَتْلَ أَخِيهِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the verb directly governs the act or requires an implicit relation.
- 5:30/q07/p1 It is the direct object of making murder easy or attractive. — Al-Azharī explicitly denies an omitted preposition; al-Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn derive direct transitivity from the causative. [5 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI(بعضهم); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأزهري)+]
- 5:30/q07/p2 It is accusative after a deleted preposition: complying in or with murder. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Mubarrad posit deletion; al-Baghawī supplies 'in'; al-Ṭabrisī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī report it. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والمبرد); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 5:30/q07/p3 It stands for obedience to the command to kill. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says an act may substitute for the command to perform it. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 5:30/q08 How did Cain kill Abel?  [فَقَتَلَهُۥ · reports]
turns on: Reports differ in weapon, setting and Abel's conduct.
- 5:30/q08/p1 He used stones after Satan demonstrated crushing a bird or animal's head. — Ibn Jurayj and Mujāhid report ignorance of how to kill; Iblīs appears as a bird in some versions. The demonstration victim varies between bird, snake and other animal. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); THALABI(ابن جريح); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); JISHUMI(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن جريج); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جريج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج ومجاهد وغيرهما); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير); BIQAI-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد وابن جريج); TABATABAI(علي بن الحسين); STUDYQURAN(commentators)]
- 5:30/q08/p2 He crushed Abel's head with a rock while Abel slept. — Al-Suddī says Cain pursued him among sheep in the mountains and left the body exposed. Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd and others agree broadly; Study Quran calls this the common report. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); THALABI(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); JISHUMI(ابن عباس وابن مسعود وأبو مالك); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي); BIQAI-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود وناس من الصحابة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود وناس من الصحابة); STUDYQURAN(commentators)]
- 5:30/q08/p3 Abel submitted during the attack. — Al-Baghawī, Bursevī and Ālūsī preserve the detail. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد وابن جريج)]
- 5:30/q08/p4 The verse establishes the killing but no definite method. — Al-Ṭabarī allows variant methods without deciding; Abū Ḥayyān says the verse omits the method. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 5:30/q08/p5 He pelted Abel with stones. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites Abū Ṣāliḥ from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس)]
- 5:30/q08/p6 He used an iron implement. — Ibn Kathīr attributes this to Abū Jaʿfar al-Bāqir. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو جعفر الباقر)]
- 5:30/q08/p7 He strangled and bit Abel like a predatory beast. — Ibn Kathīr attributes this to some People of the Book. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض أهل الكتاب)]
- 5:30/q08/p8 He stalked and killed by stealth. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers Abū Jaʿfar's account as better fitting reflection; Abel did not enable the attack. [2 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر); TABATABAI+]

## 5:30/q09 Did Cain already know a method of killing?  [فَقَتَلَهُۥ · reports]
turns on: Whether the satanic demonstration supplies the very concept of killing or a method.
- 5:30/q09/p1 No; Iblīs taught him by crushing a creature's head. — Ibn Jurayj and Mujāhid report a bird or other animal, sometimes with Iblīs as a bird. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); THALABI(ابن جريح); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); JISHUMI(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن جريج); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جريج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج ومجاهد وغيرهما); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير); BIQAI-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد وابن جريج); TABATABAI(علي بن الحسين); STUDYQURAN(commentators)]
- 5:30/q09/p2 He grasped that life could be ended; uncertainty concerned the method. — Al-Qurṭubī reports intuitive knowledge; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the prior threat makes literal ignorance inconsistent, so the report may mean choosing a method. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI+]

## 5:30/q10 Where was Abel killed?  [فَقَتَلَهُۥ · reports]
turns on: Reports name mutually different places and the verse names none.
- 5:30/q10/p1 Near the pass at Ḥirāʾ. — Al-Ṭabarī is named by Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī; others preserve the site as an alternative. [10 notes: THALABI(بعضهم); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير الطبري); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن جرير الطبري); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 5:30/q10/p2 In Basra at the future Great Mosque. — Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq is cited by al-Thaʿlabī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī; others repeat the site. [10 notes: THALABI(جعفر الصادق); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(جعفر الصادق); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(جعفر الصادق); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 5:30/q10/p3 On Mount Thawr or Nawd near Mecca. — Ibn ʿAbbās is cited for Nawd, Thawr, and a 'mountain bull' in Mecca; Bursevī lists Thawr. [5 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود); BURSEVI]
- 5:30/q10/p4 On Mount Qāsiyūn in Damascus. — Al-Durr transmits a Prophetic report; Ṭabāṭabāʾī deems it acceptable. Kaʿb points to a blood streak, though Ṭabāṭabāʾī doubts the physical trace. [5 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,كعب); ALUSI-FULL(عمرو الشبعانى عن كعب الأخبار); TABATABAI(كعب الأحبار); TABATABAI(النبي)+; TABATABAI-]
- 5:30/q10/p5 On Mount Dayr al-Murrān. — Kaʿb identified a blood streak there as the murder's sign; Ālūsī repeats the report. Ṭabāṭabāʾī questions the trace. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(كعب الأحبار); ALUSI-FULL(عمرو الشبعانى عن كعب الأخبار); TABATABAI(عمرو بن خبير الشعباني); TABATABAI-]
- 5:30/q10/p6 In India. — Al-Qurṭubī places a stone-killing there; Ibn ʿAbbās says Adam found Abel's body there. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس)]
- 5:30/q10/p7 The verse provides no location. — Abū Ḥayyān notes it omits the disputed place; al-Ṭabarī limits certainty to the killing. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TAB-FULL]

## 5:30/q11 How old was Abel when killed?  [فَقَتَلَهُۥ · reports]
turns on: Whether the age report is accepted.
- 5:30/q11/p1 Twenty years old. — Ibn Jurayj and later commentators transmit twenty, often beside the Ḥirāʾ and Basra reports. [9 notes: THALABI(ابن جريح); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جريج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 5:30/q11/p2 The verse gives no age. — Abū Ḥayyān says the age is among disputed extra-Qur'anic details. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 5:30/q12 Was Abel the first human killed?  [فَقَتَلَهُۥ · reports]
turns on: The first-killing claim derives from reports.
- 5:30/q12/p1 Yes; Cain first instituted murder. — Al-Māwardī, Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī give the claim, reinforced by the Prophetic inherited-guilt reports. [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI(قيل); THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(عبد الله); IBNATIYYA-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 5:30/q12/p2 The verse establishes only Abel's murder. — Al-Ṭabarī treats the killing as certain without settling narrative detail. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]

## 5:30/q13 What happened to Abel's body?  [فَقَتَلَهُۥ · reports]
turns on: The body is variously left, carried or buried after a raven's lesson.
- 5:30/q13/p1 Cain left it exposed, sometimes after stripping it. — Al-Suddī reports exposure; al-Zajjāj adds stripping and a desolate plain; Ālūsī says Cain did not know burial. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); ZAJJAJ; IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود وناس من الصحابة)]
- 5:30/q13/p2 A raven taught Cain burial. — Al-Qurṭubī and al-Durr describe one raven killing and burying another; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this illustrates learning through observation. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود وناس من الصحابة); TABATABAI(علي بن الحسين)]
- 5:30/q13/p3 He carried it in a sack. — Al-Wāḥidī says Cain did not know what to do with the first corpse. [1 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ]

## 5:30/q14 What followed the killing in Adam's family and nature?  [فَقَتَلَهُۥ · reports]
turns on: The reports offer differing consequences and incompatible dates for Seth.
- 5:30/q14/p1 Adam mourned for a century or ceased laughing. — Salim reports a hundred-year mourning; another account has an angel explain bayyaka as 'make you laugh'. Study Quran reports he never laughed again. Ibn Kathīr reports Ḥawwāʾ's first wailing. [7 notes: THALABI(سالم بن أبي الجعد); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد وسالم بن أبي الجعد); ALUSI-FULL(سالم بن أبي الجعد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); STUDYQURAN(commentators)]
- 5:30/q14/p2 The earth drank Abel's blood and was cursed thereafter. — Reports vary on whether God, Adam or Cain uttered the curse; al-Awzāʿī adds seven days of earthquakes and blood crying. [5 notes: THALABI(الأوزاعي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(خيثمة,وهب)]
- 5:30/q14/p3 Seth replaced Abel. — Salim says Seth, 'God's gift', was born when Adam was 130, five years after murder; Muhyī al-Sunna says fifty years later, adds fifty scriptures, and says Seth inherited Adam. Other reports describe his instruction in worship. [5 notes: THALABI(سالم بن أبي الجعد); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(محيى السنة); TABATABAI(علي بن الحسين)]
- 5:30/q14/p4 Cain's white body blackened and Adam questioned him. — Al-Baghawī, Rāzī and Ālūsī give the darkening; some include Cain's denial and Adam's grief. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 5:30/q14/p5 Vegetation, food, fruit, water, earth and animal behavior changed. — Ibn ʿAbbās describes the environmental changes; Muqātil says formerly tame animals and birds fled. [3 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,مقاتل)]
- 5:30/q14/p6 Iblīs mocked Adam's lament in verse. — Al-Thaʿlabī preserves the poem blaming Iblīs's schemes for Paradise's loss. [1 notes: THALABI(إبليس)]

## 5:30/q15 Did Adam compose the reported elegy?  [فَقَتَلَهُۥ · reports]
turns on: Authenticity and original language of the elegy.
- 5:30/q15/p1 Adam lamented in verses about a changed, dusty land. — Al-Thaʿlabī continues the elegy to Abel's burial. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī transmit it; the latter notes the accusative bashāsha. Ālūsī cites ʿAlī and Ibn ʿAbbās transmissions. [7 notes: THALABI(آدم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(آدم عليه السلام); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية,الثعلبي وغيره); ALUSI-FULL(علي); TABATABAI(روايات)]
- 5:30/q15/p2 Adam lamented in Syriac; Yaʿrub later made it Arabic verse. — Ibn ʿAbbās denies Adam composed Arabic poetry, citing 36:69; the lament passed through Seth to Yaʿrub, who ordered or translated it into meter. [4 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ميمون بن مهران عن الحبر)]
- 5:30/q15/p3 The poem is forged or too defective to attribute to Adam. — Al-Zamakhsharī and Rāzī cite its poor quality and Adam's God-given knowledge; Ālūsī lists grammatical and rhyme defects. Ṭabāṭabāʾī doubts related reports. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف)+; TABATABAI+]

## 5:30/q16 Does the first murderer share guilt for later murders?  [فَقَتَلَهُۥ ٱلْخَٰسِرِينَ · law]
turns on: How founding a practice and repentance affect liability.
- 5:30/q16/p1 Yes; each unjust killing adds a share because he instituted murder. — Many sources cite the Prophet; Ibn Kathīr notes broad hadith transmission, al-Qurṭubī cites Muslim. Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī also names Satan. ʿAlī links Cain and Iblīs to 41:29. [16 notes: THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(عبد الله,علي بن أبي طالب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن عمرو); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hz. Peygamber)]
- 5:30/q16/p2 Founding any good or bad practice carries followers' reward or burden. — Al-Qushayrī and al-Qurṭubī cite the Prophetic maxim; al-Qurṭubī applies it to Iblīs, innovators and misleading leaders. Ālūsī suggests Cain bears later fire-worship guilt. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL]
- 5:30/q16/p3 Repentance or forgetfulness prevents inherited liability. — Al-Qurṭubī says Adam repented and was forgiven, and prefers that his eating was forgetful, hence not sinful; he points to his Baqara discussion. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL+]

## 5:30/q17 On what day is the murder reported to have happened?  [فَقَتَلَهُۥ · reports]
- 5:30/q17/p1 Tuesday. — A report from Anas has the Prophet call Tuesday a day of blood because Eve menstruated and Adam's son killed his brother then. [2 notes: THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(أنس)]

## 5:30/q18 Does aṣbaḥa date the murder?  [فَأَصْبَحَ · meaning]
turns on: General change of state versus a literal time reference.
- 5:30/q18/p1 It means 'became' and fixes no time of day. — Al-Ḍamghānī cites 18:45 and 3:103; Abū Ḥayyān says several time verbs generalized to ṣāra. Al-Ṭabrisī, Ālūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī reject the night inference. [7 notes: DAMGHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; TABATABAI(بعضهم)+; TABRISI+]
- 5:30/q18/p2 Morning evokes the beginning of a continuing state or expected relief. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites al-Rabīʿ ibn Ḍabʿ and Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ for continuing use; Abū Ḥayyān rejects the rationale. Al-Biqāʿī says morning normally promises relief. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الربيع بن ضبع,سعد بن أبي وقاص); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); BIQAI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 5:30/q18/p3 It implies Cain killed at night. — Al-Ṭabrisī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī report the inference, then reject it. [1 notes: TABRISI(قيل); TABRISI-; ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 5:30/q18/p4 It may retain literal morning, when murder occurred. — Ibn ʿĀshūr pairs this alternative with Cain's visible worldly misery. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 5:30/q19 What happened to Cain later?  [فَأَصْبَحَ مِنَ ٱلْخَٰسِرِينَ · reports]
turns on: Reports describe distinct later fates and origins.
- 5:30/q19/p1 He fled to Aden and instituted fire worship after Satan deceived him. — Al-Thaʿlabī adds fugitive status and his sister Hibat Allāh; several sources say Satan misexplained the accepted offering as fire worship. [6 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 5:30/q19/p2 God removed his reason or heart and he wandered until death. — ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Faḍāla gives this report. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن فضالة); ALUSI-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن فضالة)]
- 5:30/q19/p3 A blind grandson accidentally killed him with a stone. — Al-Thaʿlabī says the grandson then killed his accusing son with a slap. [1 notes: THALABI]
- 5:30/q19/p4 His descendants fell into vice and perished in Noah's flood. — Al-Thaʿlabī lists instruments, wine, fire worship and adultery. [1 notes: THALABI(قالوا)]
- 5:30/q19/p5 His conception came from a poisonous remnant of Adam's vomit. — Bursevī cites a master: the same tree made a snake deadly, and Cain a corrupter. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الكبار)]

## 5:30/q20 What was Cain's loss?  [ٱلْخَٰسِرِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Worldly, moral, spiritual and otherworldly scopes of loss.
- 5:30/q20/p1 Loss in this world and the next. — Ibn ʿAbbās specifies parents' anger and brotherlessness here, God's wrath and Hell hereafter. Al-Bayḍāwī adds exile; Bursevī and Elmalılı say loss of religion and worldly life. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 5:30/q20/p2 Loss of good deeds. — Al-Zajjāj's gloss recurs in Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج)]
- 5:30/q20/p3 Worldly agony, agitation or disappointment. — Ibn ʿĀshūr connects it with a morning reading; Ibn ʿAṭiyya conditionally includes Mujāhid's torment account. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 5:30/q20/p4 He simply became one of the losers. — Muqātil and al-Nasafī leave it general; Ālūsī says the plural 'one of' intensifies the point. [3 notes: MUQATIL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 5:30/q20/p5 Loss of the afterlife through a bad bargain. — Al-Ṭabarī says he sold the afterlife for worldly life; Ibn ʿĀshūr gives an otherworldly reading. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 5:30/q20/p6 Destruction of his own self. — Qāḍī Abū Yaʿlā's gloss is transmitted by Ibn al-Jawzī and Abū Ḥayyān. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(القاضي أبو يعلى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القاضي أبو يعلى)]
- 5:30/q20/p7 God and other humans grew angry with him. — Al-Biqāʿī connects it to assaulting God's creation and a fellow human. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 5:30/q21 Was Cain an unbeliever or a sinner?  [ٱلْخَٰسِرِينَ · theology]
turns on: How the reports bear on Cain's faith.
- 5:30/q21/p1 He was an unbeliever, at least at death. — Ibn ʿAbbās calls him unbelieving; Ālūsī infers this from reports of his wretched end. Al-Māwardī notes this view. [3 notes: MUJAHID(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 5:30/q21/p2 He was a grave sinner rather than an unbeliever. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers this as the apparent reading; al-Māwardī records it. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]

## 5:30/q22 What punishment is reported for Cain?  [ٱلْخَٰسِرِينَ · reports]
turns on: Conflicting bodily scenes, places and durations.
- 5:30/q22/p1 A fixed leg, sun-facing fire in summer and ice in winter. — Mujāhid's account differs on which leg and duration; Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds seven angels. Ibn ʿAṭiyya conditions its relevance on authenticity. [6 notes: THALABI(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI(روايات)]
- 5:30/q22/p2 Half the punishment of Hell's inhabitants. — Ibn ʿUmar or ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr are cited; some say equal sharing. [5 notes: THALABI(عبد الله بن عمر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); DURR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عمر)]
- 5:30/q22/p3 He is Hell's most wretched, bearing later bloodshed. — Ibn ʿAmr calls him most wretched; a Prophetic report lists him among three such people. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن عمرو)]
- 5:30/q22/p4 He hangs upside down, unable to reach water. — One report gives a dead town and pool; another a sea island until resurrection. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(عبد الله); TABATABAI(روايات)]
- 5:30/q22/p5 He hangs by his horns in the sun until resurrection. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī lists this among reports he regards as weak or corrupted. [2 notes: TABATABAI(روايات)]

## 5:30/q23 What broader lesson does the killing teach?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The ethical and anthropological lesson drawn.
- 5:30/q23/p1 Envy and the lower soul can overcome warning and destroy kin. — Al-Qurṭubī stresses the victim's closeness; al-Qushayrī says corrupt intentions mute truth and regret cannot repair harm. Rāzī describes the initial moral resistance. Kuran Yolu notes jealousy overcame advice. [8 notes: QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(al-Qushayrī)]
- 5:30/q23/p2 Follow Abel's example and avoid Cain's. — Prophetic reports in al-Māwardī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Durr present Abel as the model for the community and threatened victims. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 5:30/q23/p3 The raven illustrates practical learning provided by God. — Al-Qushayrī describes divine help when a practical solution is unclear; Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes sensory and intellectual human learning. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; TABATABAI]
- 5:30/q23/p4 The brothers knew God, sacrifice, judgment, Hell and piety. — Kuran Yolu infers this from the wider story. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 5:30/q24 What other verses illuminate this account?  [* · links]
turns on: Different cross-references serve different points.
- 5:30/q24/p1 The killing leads to 5:32 and recalls the warning of 4:93. — Mujāhid connects both passages. [1 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد)]
- 5:30/q24/p2 Cain and Iblīs are the two misleaders in 41:29. — ʿAlī gives this identification. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(علي بن أبي طالب)]
- 5:30/q24/p3 The evil soul parallels Joseph's statement in 12:53. — Study Quran cites Joseph. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 5:30/q24/p4 Ṭawwaʿat is stronger than sawwalat in 20:96 and 12:18. — Samarrāʾī says murder required greater effort to overcome resistance. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 5:30/q25 How is the murder read inwardly?  [* · inward]
turns on: Which inner faculties and losses the figures portray.
- 5:30/q25/p1 The lower self kills the heart and loses spiritual goods in both worlds. — Bursevī identifies Cain with self and Abel with heart, listing lost unveiling, gnosis, intimacy, gardens and divine vision and citing 103:1–2. Qāshānī says estimation suppresses intellect and stirs greed and animal pleasure. [6 notes: BURSEVI; QASHANI]
- 5:30/q25/p2 Inward corruption is punished in spiritual perception. — Al-Qushayrī distinguishes outward legal punishment from inward loss of inspiration, gnosis, intimacy and worship. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 5:30/q26 How do the Qur'an and Torah accounts compare?  [* · theology]
turns on: Authority of extra-Qur'anic reports and the portrayal of God.
- 5:30/q26/p1 The Qur'an teaches human learning without the Torah's anthropomorphic God. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares Genesis 4's offerings, mark, curse and departure to Nod; he rejects its picture of God as an earthly person who withdraws or hides. The raven shows gradual learning. [5 notes: TABATABAI(التوراة)]
- 5:30/q26/p2 Traditional names and extra-Qur'anic details have uncertain authority. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts Qābīl with Torah Qayin, questions the Torah chain and altered text, notes related reports in Sunni and Shiʿi routes, and rejects many as weak or corrupt. [4 notes: TABATABAI(روايات)]

## 5:30/q27 What became of Abel's accepted offering?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports disagree on whether the ram replaced Isaac or Ishmael.
- 5:30/q27/p1 The ram became the ransom sacrificed in place of Isaac. — Ibn ʿAbbās's account in al-Ṭabarī includes twin births, a marriage dispute, the two offerings, and the end of sibling marriage after four generations. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 5:30/q27/p2 The ram was kept in Paradise forty years before replacing Ishmael. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this distinct version. [1 notes: TABATABAI(روايات)]

## 5:30/q28 What does the corpse's saw'ah signify in the related burial account?  [* · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term names exposed private parts or the whole body.
- 5:30/q28/p1 The genitals. — Al-Qurṭubī transmits this gloss. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 5:30/q28/p2 The murdered man's corpse. — Al-Qurṭubī gives this alternative. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 5:30/q29 What other benefits followed the introduction of death?  [* · reports]
- 5:30/q29/p1 Wind enabled burial and death in old age eased weariness. — A report from Anas names three benefits: wind so relatives could bury their dead, worms in grain so rulers would not hoard it as money, and death after old age to ease families' burden. [1 notes: THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
