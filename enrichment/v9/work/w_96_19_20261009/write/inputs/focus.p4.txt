# 96:19/q18 How does the ending complete the sūra's argument? [* · links]
- 96:19/q18/p1 It counters the tyrant's worldly authority and returns to the opening call to worship.
  reasons: Corpus Coranicum says the imperatives turn from the accused to the Messenger, summarize the sūra, contrast human and divine authority, and echo 96:1,3. Neuwirth likens the close to a psalmic hallelujah (Psalm 149:9). Al-Biqāʿī says the ending returns to the beginning. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says nearness defeats the illusion of independence; Ibn ʿĀshūr links it to the forbidding man introduced after human transgression.
  holders (8 notes): BIQAI; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM(Neuwirth); IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: BIQAI:96:19/r14 BINTSHATI:v2p35/r3 CORPUSCORANICUM:96:15-18:kommentar/r5 CORPUSCORANICUM:96:15-18:kommentar/r6 CORPUSCORANICUM:96:15-18:kommentar/r7 CORPUSCORANICUM:96:19:anmerkung/r3 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p443/r7 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p452/r5
- 96:19/q18/p2 Prayer, recitation and devotion shield from distraction and sustain spiritual state.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says required recitation and devotion veil distractions and increase purity; he cites Abraham's scroll advising attention to one's affairs, tongue and time.
  holders (3 notes): BIQAI(إبراهيم عليه الصلاة والسلام); BIQAI-FULL
  notes: BIQAI:96:19/r12 BIQAI:96:19/r13 BIQAI-FULL:v8p489/r1

# 96:19/q19 When and in how many stages was the sūra revealed? [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether internal tone and cohesion support one episode or an interval after the opening five verses.
- 96:19/q19/p1 The opening five verses came first and the rest followed after a pause.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says Gabriel remained visible and non-Qur'anic revelation continued; he finds a two-year pause unlikely, treats its exact duration as unimportant, and puts the rest after a short interval when prayer and news of revelation had spread. Consecutive recitation normally reflects consecutive revelation.
  holders (2 notes): IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p443/r1 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p443/r6
- 96:19/q19/p2 The pause lasted two years.
  reasons: Al-Suhaylī reports this duration; Ibn ʿĀshūr considers it unlikely.
  holders (1 notes): IBNASHUR-FULL(السهيلي)
  notes: IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p443/r2
- 96:19/q19/p3 The sūra is a coherent single revelation unit.
  reasons: Islahi finds the first five verses as severe in tone as the rest, no indication of two episodes, and uses tone, addressee and style to assess period.
  holders (2 notes): ISLAHI-TADABBUR
  notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p391/r6 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p392/r4
sources: IBNASHUR-FULL = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393; ISLAHI-TADABBUR = Amin Ahsan Islahi (d. 1997)

# 96:19/q20 Where does al-ʿAlaq stand in reported revelation order? [* · sciences]

# 96:19/q23 How are the attached religious and tribal group nouns used and derived? [* · grammar]
turns on: Whether naṣārā rests on unattested naṣrī or attested naṣrāna.
- 96:19/q23/p1 Majūs and Yahūd are tribal nouns, with feminine gender prevailing.
  reasons: Sībawayh cites Imruʾ al-Qays's 'fire of Majūs'; a man called Majūs is diptote like ʿAmmān. Al-Yahūd and al-Majūs resemble nisba adjectives with deleted yāʾ, whereas bare majūs is indefinite.
