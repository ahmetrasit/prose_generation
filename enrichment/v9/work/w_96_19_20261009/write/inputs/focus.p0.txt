# 96:19/q01 What does kallā do here? [كَلَّا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether kallā negates Abū Jahl's confidence or serves as an alert.
- 96:19/q01/p1 It rebukes and negates the forbidding man's claim or expectation.
  reasons: Al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj and later commentators apply it to Abū Jahl's ban or boast. Al-Rāzī adds that his assembly cannot help and he cannot end prayer. Al-Biqāʿī denies the threat, reform and protection of the gathering. Ibn ʿĀshūr links it to 96:17; Būrsawī calls the pause after it absolute; Corpus Coranicum notes recurrent kallā at 96:6–8 and 15.

# 96:19/q02 Whom does 'him' designate? [تُطِعْهُ · referent]
turns on: Whether to identify the preceding denier specifically as Abū Jahl.
- 96:19/q02/p1 Abū Jahl, who forbade the Prophet's prayer.
  reasons: Abū Hurayra is cited by al-Māwardī. Other sources treat him as the historical opponent.

# 96:19/q03 What does 'do not obey him' require? [لَا تُطِعْهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether obedience is literal compliance, answering his speech, or caution induced by fear.
- 96:19/q03/p1 Keep praying and worshipping despite his ban and threats.
  reasons: Muqātil and most commentators specify refusal to abandon prayer. Al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī cite 68:8. Ibn Kathīr says the Prophet may pray anywhere under divine protection; Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies the Sacred Mosque; Elmalılı adds continued recitation. Al-Biqāʿī includes devotion generally.

# 96:19/q04 What does sujūd mean in its root usage? [وَٱسْجُدْ · meaning]
- 96:19/q04/p1 Lowering and bending, extended to humility and obedience.
  reasons: Ibn Qutayba cites a camel lowering itself, a bending palm, and a forehead on earth. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites the angels before Adam and Joseph's family at 12:100 as submission.
