# 96:19/q05 What action does 'prostrate' command here? [وَٱسْجُدْ · interpretation]

- 96:19/q05/p1 Continue prayer and worship, with prostration naming prayer.

# 96:19/q06 Why is prayer expressed through prostration? [وَٱسْجُدْ · rhetoric]
- 96:19/q06/p1 Prostration is prayer's noblest and most humble posture, so naming it emphasizes prayer and nearness.
  reasons: Abū Ḥayyān calls it the finest posture; al-Biqāʿī the noblest component. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees reinforcement of the prayer command. Elmalılı says naming it separately stresses its importance because 'draw near' spans other worship. Islahi calls it prayer's greatest component.
  holders (7 notes): ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR

# 96:19/q07 What kind of prayer or prostration lay behind this early scene? [وَٱسْجُدْ · reports]
- 96:19/q07/p1 Prostration was known before Islam, including among idol worshippers.
  reasons: Muqātil says idolaters began with prostration before bowing and standing, whereas God later changed the Prophet's order to standing, bowing, then prostrating. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites Abraham and Ishmael's sanctuary (2:125; 22:26) and pre-Islamic idol prostration. Neuwirth, following Uri Rubin, sees pre-Islamic ritual in the command.
  holders (3 notes): MUQATIL; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM(Neuwirth; Uri Rubin)
  notes: MUQATIL:96:9-19/r7 BINTSHATI:v2p34/r2 CORPUSCORANICUM:96:19:anmerkung/r1
- 96:19/q07/p2 The Prophet practiced a public, still unregulated prayer involving prostration before the five daily prayers.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says it was distinct from the later obligation, visible enough to provoke Abū Jahl; he places the Night Journey and five-prayer obligation a year before Hijra.
  holders (2 notes): IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR+
  notes: IBNASHUR:96:6/r11 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p443/r5
- 96:19/q07/p3 Before the Night Journey he prayed once before sunset and once before sunrise.
  reasons: Al-Ḥarbī gives the two times and Yaḥyā b. Sallām concurs. Ibn ʿĀshūr's full account identifies them as ʿAṣr and Fajr.
  holders (4 notes): IBNASHUR(الحربي، يحيى بن سلام); IBNASHUR-FULL(الحربي، يحيى بن سلام)

# 96:19/q08 Is supplication during prostration legally required? [وَٱسْجُدْ · law]
turns on: Whether the exhortation establishes obligation or encouragement.
- 96:19/q08/p1 It is strongly urged because likely to be answered.
  reasons: Abū Hurayra's report urges abundance; al-Qurṭubī cites a sound hadith contrasting bowing's glorification with prostration's supplication. These reports state encouragement without an explicit ruling on obligation.
  holders (4 notes): BAGHAWI(أبو هريرة); IBNATIYYA(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI(النبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)

- 96:19/q08/p2 It is obligatory.
  reasons: Al-ʿIzz b. ʿAbd al-Salām's opinion is described by al-Ālūsī as unusual.

# 96:19/q09 Is prostration the best pillar of prayer? [وَٱسْجُدْ · law]
- 96:19/q09/p1 Several scholars hold it to be the best pillar.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī grounds this in reports of nearness and Thawbān's report that each prostration raises a degree and erases a sin.

# 96:19/q10 What is the status of recitation prostration at this verse? [وَٱسْجُدْ · law]
turns on: Whether established recitation prostration also has obligatory status.
- 96:19/q10/p1 A prostration of recitation is recognized here.
  reasons: Al-Māwardī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya's group recognize it. Sound reports from Abū Hurayra in Muslim and both Ṣaḥīḥ collections attest the Prophet prostrating at Iqraʾ and al-Inshiqāq (84:1 or 84:21). Mālik prostrated privately. Al-Qurṭubī prefers the sound report despite Ibn al-ʿArabī's contextual prayer reading. Study Quran counts it among fifteen prostration verses.
