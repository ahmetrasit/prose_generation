# 96:19/q13 What force does the form iqtirāb add? [وَٱقْتَرِب · grammar]
turns on: Whether the form stresses effort to gain nearness or direct nearness achieved in prostration.
- 96:19/q13/p1 The iftiʿāl form conveys exertion and seeking closeness through prayer.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr derives this from the morphology. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects Abū Ḥayyān's similar devotional reading.
  holders (2 notes): IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI-
  notes: IBNASHUR:96:19/r5 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p452/r7 | against: BINTSHATI:v2p35/r2
- 96:19/q13/p2 It conveys direct nearness, the worshipper's highest aspiration.
  reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the ending breaks the proud man's vanity and counters imagined independence; 92:13 recalls God's ownership of both lives.
  holders (2 notes): BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI+
  notes: BINTSHATI:v2p35/r2 BINTSHATI:v2p35/r3

# 96:19/q14 What does prostration bring, and what practice follows from its nearness? [وَٱسْجُدْ وَٱقْتَرِب · interpretation]
- 96:19/q14/p1 The servant is nearest to God while prostrating, so abundant supplication is urged and likely answered.
  reasons: Mujāhid and many Prophetic reports give the closest-state formula; Abū Hurayra adds frequent supplication, reported sound by Ibn Kathīr and al-Ālūsī. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says prayers then are likely answered. Al-Qurṭubī contrasts glorification in bowing with supplication in prostration. Būrsawī parses mā as verbal noun, aqrab as subject with omitted predicate, and yakūn as complete in the hadith.

# 96:19/q15 What inward approach do these commands describe? [وَٱسْجُدْ وَٱقْتَرِب · inward]
- 96:19/q15/p1 Prostration is annihilation of acts, attributes and essence, ending in abiding in God.
  reasons: Al-Qāshānī reads attentive monotheistic prayer and the Prophet's refuge formula as three stages. Al-Ghazālī, cited by al-Biqāʿī, reads it as ascent through witnessing God's acts, attributes and essence until God alone praises Himself. The refuge prayer names pardon from punishment, pleasure from anger, and God from God.
  holders (5 notes): QASHANI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI(الإمام الغزالي، النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL(الغزالي)
  notes: QASHANI:96:15/r2 QASHANI:96:15/r3 BIQAI:96:19/r5 BIQAI:96:19/r6 BIQAI-FULL:v8p487/r9
- 96:19/q15/p2 Bodily servanthood is joined to inner witnessing of Lordship.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAṭāʾ speaks of entry to Lordship's court after release from servanthood's court. Al-Qushayrī keeps the self on servanthood's ground while the heart witnesses Lordship, or body prostrates as the secret draws near. Al-Ḥusayn says limbs display worship while one approaches only when drawn near, citing 56:88.
  holders (3 notes): SULAMI(ابن عطاء، الحسين); QUSHAYRI
  notes: SULAMI:v2p407#3/r1 SULAMI:v2p407#3/r3 QUSHAYRI:v3p436/r18
- 96:19/q15/p3 Nearness involves continual ascent through spiritual stations and gratitude.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says earlier stations appear deficient beside later ones; the Prophet sought forgiveness seventy times a day and ceased self-observation. ʿĀʾisha's question about his weeping despite forgiven sins elicited his wish to be grateful; 14:7 links thanks with increase.
  holders (8 notes): BIQAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم، عائشة رضي الله عنها); BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم، عائشة)
  notes: BIQAI:96:19/r7 BIQAI:96:19/r8 BIQAI:96:19/r9 BIQAI:96:19/r10 BIQAI-FULL:v8p487/r10 BIQAI-FULL:v8p487/r11 BIQAI-FULL:v8p487/r12 BIQAI-FULL:v8p487/r13
- 96:19/q15/p4 The elect witness divine qualities beyond common people's limited capacity.
  reasons: Al-Wāsiṭī says the elect see no other attributes; commoners' inner secrets are too weak and distant from truth's source.
  holders (1 notes): SULAMI(الواسطي)
  notes: SULAMI:v2p407#3/r2
- 96:19/q15/p5 Permission to prostrate signals that God has already drawn the servant near.
  reasons: Dhū al-Nūn urges an inward approach in light of the report that prostration is the nearest state.

# 96:19/q16 What did Abū Jahl's confrontation with the praying Prophet involve? [* · reports]
- 96:19/q16/p1 He threatened to trample the Prophet, recoiled before a fiery trench and wings, and was warned of angels' seizure.
  reasons: Abū Hurayra reports the threat, retreat, awe, trench and wings; the Prophet said angels would tear him apart. Al-Ṭabarī's short note flags uncertain attribution of the quoted revelation. Qatāda ties revelation to the threat; the zabāniya would snatch him if he acted. Būrsawī says summoning his council would reveal torment angels.
  holders (6 notes): TAB(أبو هريرة، النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم، قتادة); TAB-FULL(أبو هريرة); BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام)

# 96:19/q17 What general lesson follows from the final commands? [* · interpretation]
- 96:19/q17/p1 One must obey God rather than a creature who commands disobedience, and persevere in worship.
  reasons: Al-Jishumī states the maxim. Sayyid Quṭb extends the warning to all believers who pray and call to God against aggressors, leaving the tyrant to the zabāniya. Study Quran extends it to deniers of religion or prayer. Islahi adds forbearance, perseverance and divine help through prayer.
  holders (6 notes): JISHUMI; QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR
