# 96:13/q08 Whose actions do the conditional pronouns describe? [كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the preceding guidance clause shares the forbider as subject or shifts to the worshipper; the denial clause consistently points back to the forbider in these analyses.
- 96:13/q08/p1 The verbs of denial and turning, as well as knowing in verse 14, describe the prohibiter.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī's principal parsing carries the unstated subjects of the surrounding verbs back to the forbider. Al-Farrāʾ instead assigns the earlier 'was' and 'commanded' to the praying servant while keeping denial and turning for the prohibiter. Ibn ʿĀshūr says context directs the pronouns to different antecedents.

# 96:13/q09 What contrast do the linked questions make? [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the conditions compare worshipper and forbider, test the forbider's professed state, or mark an escalation of rebuke.
- 96:13/q09/p1 They marvel that the guided, pious worshipper is forbidden by a denier who turns away.
  reasons: Al-Farrāʾ presents the conditional form as an astonishing juxtaposition. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Bayḍāwī describe the worshipper's prayer and call to piety against the opponent's denial. Abū Ḥayyān adds that prayer reforms the Prophet and enjoining piety reforms others, both troubling Abū Jahl. The omitted conclusion can be 'How astonishing this is'; aw can function as waw. Al-Bayḍāwī says prayer alone is an active call, or the ban may cover other matters, contrasting self-perfection in worship with perfecting others through invitation.

# 96:13/q10 What warning does denial and turning away carry? [كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes emphasize either divine surveillance and repayment or the moral absurdity of opposing clear guidance.
- 96:13/q10/p1 God sees the forbider and will requite or punish him for denial, turning away, and obstructing worship.
  reasons: Al-Jishumī connects threatened punishment to following truth. Al-Zamakhsharī says God sees and repays both parties; if Abū Jahl persists, God seizes his forelock and drags him to Hell. Ibn Kathīr adds God hears. Al-Qāshānī says God sees either state. Al-Rāzī argues evident proofs and sound reason should deter stubborn obstruction. Islāhī cites 92:15–16 on the wretched entering Hell; al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr stress the threat.
  holders (14 notes): JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR

- 96:13/q10/p2 The question exposes the strangeness and moral evil of opposing evident guidance.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says denial against clear evidence is plainly evil and follows desire, while turning away damages disposition and conduct. Al-Ṭabarī concludes the prohibiting denier must know God sees him. Al-Rāzī calls the conduct stubborn.
