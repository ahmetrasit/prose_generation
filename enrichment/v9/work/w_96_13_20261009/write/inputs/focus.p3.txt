# 96:13/q11 What occasion involving Abū Jahl is reported for this passage? [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in the narrated encounter and detail of angelic intervention.
- 96:13/q11/p1 Abū Jahl forbade or threatened to trample Muhammad while he prayed; the passage was revealed concerning this confrontation.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās reports Abū Jahl's boast of his council and says angels would seize him if summoned. Qatāda identifies Muhammad as guided and Abū Jahl as denier, with his council his clan. Al-Suyūṭī transmits Ibn ʿAbbās's report through the lying forelock. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the sequence suggests public preaching and prayer, while allowing general application.
  holders (10 notes): MUSNAD-AHMAD(ابن عباس); DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); BINTSHATI
  notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD:3044/r1 DURR:96:10/r8 DURR:96:15/r8 DURR:96:16/r8 DURR:96:6/r8 DURR:96:7/r8 DURR:96:9/r8 DURR-FULL:v8p565/r3 SUYUTI-LUBAB:v1p232#2/r3 BINTSHATI:v2p27/r3
- 96:13/q11/p2 Abū Jahl approached to trample the Prophet but recoiled from a trench of fire, terror, and wings.
  reasons: Abū Hurayra's report says the Prophet declared that angels would seize him limb by limb. Al-Baghawī notes uncertainty whether the revelation reference belongs to Abū Hurayra's narration or another source.
  holders (1 notes): BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة)
  notes: BAGHAWI-FULL:v8p479#2/r11
sources: BAGHAWI-FULL = al-Baghawī, d. 516; BINTSHATI = ʿĀʾisha ʿAbd al-Raḥmān (Bint al-Shāṭiʾ), d. 1419; DURR = al-Suyūṭī, d. 911; DURR-FULL = al-Suyūṭī, d. 911; MUSNAD-AHMAD = Aḥmad b. Ḥanbal, d. 241; SUYUTI-LUBAB = al-Suyūṭī, d. 911

# 96:13/q12 When and how was this passage placed in the sura? [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the passage was later occasioned by Abū Jahl and inserted after the opening, or continues the sura's generic description of humanity.
- 96:13/q12/p1 The remainder of the sura came later about Abū Jahl, and the Prophet was directed to place these verses after the opening.
  reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports this as al-Rāzī's first solution; she finds both his accounts possible and takes the sequence as following public preaching and prayer.
  holders (2 notes): BINTSHATI
  notes: BINTSHATI:v2p27/r1 BINTSHATI:v2p27/r3
- 96:13/q12/p2 The generic human in the opening continues into the warnings about transgression and forbidding worship.
  reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports al-Rāzī's second solution: despite creation and blessings, people forget their return, transgress, and forbid worship when they should follow guidance and enjoin piety; she considers it possible.
  holders (2 notes): BINTSHATI(الفخر الرازي)
  notes: BINTSHATI:v2p27/r2 BINTSHATI:v2p27/r3
sources: BINTSHATI = ʿĀʾisha ʿAbd al-Raḥmān (Bint al-Shāṭiʾ), d. 1419

# 96:13/q13 What theological point is drawn from these acts? [كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ · theology]
- 96:13/q13/p1 Denial and turning away are acts of human beings, not acts created by God.
  reasons: Al-Jishumī draws this from the threat directed to the disbeliever.
  holders (1 notes): JISHUMI
  notes: JISHUMI:v10p7482/r9
sources: JISHUMI = al-Ḥākim al-Jishumī, d. 494

# 96:13/q14 How broadly does the ban on forbidding prayer apply? [* · law]
- 96:13/q14/p1 The warning reaches everyone who prohibits prayer itself, but an objection to prayer in a usurped place or disliked time can concern that accompanying circumstance.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī distinguishes prayer as such from a concomitant quality, even though the occasion concerns Abū Jahl.
  holders (1 notes): ALUSI
  notes: ALUSI:96:13/r47
sources: ALUSI = al-Ālūsī, d. 1270

# 96:13/q15 Which other verses are linked to the paired acts of denial and turning? [كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ · links]
- 96:13/q15/p1 The pair recurs at 92:16, 79:21–22, and 75:32; 92:15–16 also supplies the denier's fate in Hell.
  reasons: Corpus Coranicum notes the recurring verbs and related discussions at 95:7, 73:11, and 88:23. Islāhī cites 92:15–16 for the wretched person's fate.
  holders (2 notes): CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR
  notes: CORPUSCORANICUM:96:13:anmerkung/r1 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p390/r1

# 96:13/q17 Can a conditional clause be the second object of أَرَءَيْتَ? [أَرَءَيْتَ إِن · grammar]
turns on: Whether the second object must itself be interrogative or oath-like, or may be supplied by a conditional and its question together.
- 96:13/q17/p1 No: its second object must be an interrogative or oath clause.
  reasons: Abū Ḥayyān and other grammarians give this objection; al-Irshād includes an oath-like clause among the permitted forms.
  holders (3 notes): ALUSI(أبو حيان)
  notes: ALUSI:96:11/r21 ALUSI:96:12/r30 ALUSI:96:13/r33
- 96:13/q17/p2 Yes: the conditional and following question can together occupy or indicate that second-object position.
  reasons: Al-Khafājī replies to the objection in al-Ālūsī's account; al-Raḍī and al-Damāmīnī cite Sībawayh's preferred analysis against the restriction.
  holders (4 notes): ALUSI(أبو حيان، الخفاجي، الرضي)
  notes: ALUSI:96:13/r33 ALUSI:96:12/r31 ALUSI:96:12/r32 ALUSI:96:13/r34
sources: ALUSI = al-Ālūsī, d. 1270
