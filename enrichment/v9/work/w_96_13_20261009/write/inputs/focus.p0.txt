# 96:13/q01 How is أَرَءَيْتَ read? [أَرَءَيْتَ · readings]
turns on: The notes distinguish elision, facilitation, and vowel lengthening in pronunciation.
- 96:13/q01/p1 The medial hamza is dropped in the canonical reading among the Seven.
  reasons: QIRAAT-ER says the omission eases pronunciation without changing meaning.
  holders (1 notes): QIRAAT-ER
  notes: QIRAAT-ER:96:13:1#1/r1
- 96:13/q01/p2 The hamza is facilitated with a brief pause.
  reasons: QIRAAT-ER says this retains a trace of the hamza without full articulation.
  holders (1 notes): QIRAAT-ER
  notes: QIRAAT-ER:96:13:1#2/r1
- 96:13/q01/p3 The canonical madd lengthens the ā before the hamza.
  reasons: QIRAAT-ER says the lengthening gives the question extra rhythmic weight.
  holders (1 notes): QIRAAT-ER
  notes: QIRAAT-ER:96:13:1#3/r1
sources: QIRAAT-ER = QIRAAT-ER

# 96:13/q02 What does أَرَءَيْتَ do here? [أَرَءَيْتَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether seeing is an attention-getting challenge, intellectual apprehension, or initially literal sight.
- 96:13/q02/p1 It prompts consideration or an answer and conveys rebuke, warning, or astonishment rather than seeking information.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it an interrogative challenge without the two objects of cognitive vision. Al-Ālūsī explains 'tell me' from sight leading to knowledge and notes two grammatical accounts. Elmalılı describes admonition and amazement. Al-Rāghib compares it to akhbirnī. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ points to Meccan parallels at 107:1, 19:77, 25:43, 45:23, 53:33 and questions in al-Wāqiʿa at 56:58, 63, 68, 71, as well as 10:59, 26:75, 35:40, 39:38, 53:19, 46:4.
  holders (6 notes): IBNATIYYA; ALUSI; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI(الراغب)
  notes: IBNATIYYA:96:13/r1 ALUSI:96:13/r16 ELMALILI:96:9-19/r1 BINTSHATI:v2p28/r3 BINTSHATI:v2p29/r1 BINTSHATI:v2p30/r2
- 96:13/q02/p2 The seeing is inward or intellectual.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports this for the opening and third questions, and notes grammarians' two analyses.
  holders (2 notes): ALUSI(بعضهم، قيل)
  notes: ALUSI:96:10/r20 ALUSI:96:12/r15
- 96:13/q02/p3 The first seeing is visual, while the later questions are intellectual.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports that the visual first use needs no second object, whereas the later conditional clauses occupy second-object positions.
  holders (4 notes): ALUSI(بعضهم، قيل)
  notes: ALUSI:96:10/r38 ALUSI:96:11/r18 ALUSI:96:12/r25 ALUSI:96:13/r28
sources: ALUSI = al-Ālūsī, d. 1270; BINTSHATI = ʿĀʾisha ʿAbd al-Raḥmān (Bint al-Shāṭiʾ), d. 1419; ELMALILI = Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi Yazır, d. 1361; IBNATIYYA = Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542

# 96:13/q03 Whom does أَرَءَيْتَ address? [أَرَءَيْتَ · referent]
turns on: The pronoun sequence and whether the discourse questions an observer, the forbider, or both disputants in turn.
- 96:13/q03/p1 The Prophet is addressed throughout.
  reasons: Abū Ḥayyān prefers the apparent address to the Messenger on the pronoun sequence; al-Baḥr also favors him. Al-Rāzī says the proofs are plain to sound reason. Elmalılı says the opening address clearly reaches the Prophet and extends to others.
  holders (11 notes): RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI(البحر); ELMALILI; ABUHAYYAN+
  notes: RAZI:96:13/r1 RAZI-FULL:v32p22#2/r1 ABUHAYYAN:96:11/r5 ABUHAYYAN:96:12/r5 ABUHAYYAN:96:13/r5 ABUHAYYAN:96:14/r5 ALUSI:96:10/r47 ALUSI:96:12/r33 ALUSI:96:13/r35 ELMALILI:96:9-19#2/r13 ELMALILI:96:9-19/r2
- 96:13/q03/p2 The unbelieving forbider is addressed.
  reasons: Al-Rāzī asks whether Muhammad, if lying, would need the forbider's intervention when God sees him. Al-Bayḍāwī compares a judge addressing opposing litigants. Elmalılı asks whether the servant should obey the forbider and abandon prayer.
  holders (10 notes): RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ELMALILI
  notes: RAZI:96:13/r2 RAZI-FULL:v32p22#2/r2 BAYDAWI:96:13/r4 BAYDAWI:96:14/r4 BAYDAWI-FULL:96:9-14/r6 ABUHAYYAN:96:11/r6 ABUHAYYAN:96:12/r6 ABUHAYYAN:96:13/r6 ABUHAYYAN:96:14/r6 ELMALILI:96:9-19#2/r14
- 96:13/q03/p3 The addresses alternate between the unbeliever and the Prophet, like a judge questioning litigants.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports that the second question faces the unbeliever and the third the Prophet; 'or' then divides like 'and.' Elmalılı finds such turn-taking more meaningful.
  holders (4 notes): ALUSI(بعضهم، قيل); ELMALILI+
  notes: ALUSI:96:10/r50 ALUSI:96:12/r35 ALUSI:96:13/r37 ELMALILI:96:9-19/r2
- 96:13/q03/p4 The address is general, to any understanding person or humanity.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī compares the address to humanity at 96:8; he also reports that some exclude both immediate parties on contextual grounds. Elmalılı allows all three questions to address humanity.

# 96:13/q04 Who denies and turns away? [كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronouns name the historical forbider, express a hypothetical about the worshipper, or extend the warning to all such actors.
- 96:13/q04/p1 The forbider of prayer, Abū Jahl, is the denier who turns away.
  reasons: Muqātil contrasts the Prophet alone with Abū Jahl's company. Qatāda names him; Ibn ʿAṭiyya identifies him with the forbider. Ibn ʿĀshūr says context sends these pronouns back to the man in 96:9–10. The Qatāda report identifies Muhammad as guided, Abū Jahl as denier, his council as his clan and zabāniya as police.

# 96:13/q05 What does he deny and turn away from? [كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: The unexpressed object of 'denies' and the sphere of 'turns away': God, the Book, the Messenger, truth generally, or the worshipper's guidance.
- 96:13/q05/p1 He denies God and turns from obedience to Him.
  reasons: Al-Naḥḥās adds denial of God's Messenger and prevention of prayer. Al-Jishumī links denial of God and His Messenger, turning away, and preventing worship with punishment and care to follow truth.
  holders (4 notes): NAHHAS; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI
  notes: NAHHAS:v5p261/r14 MAWARDI:96:13/r2 MAWARDI-FULL:v6p307/r5 JISHUMI:v10p7481/r12
- 96:13/q05/p2 He denies the Qur'an or God's Book and turns away from faith.
  reasons: Muqātil says Abū Jahl rejects the Qur'an. Al-Ṭabarī says he denies the truth Muhammad brought and does not believe. Al-Baghawī and al-Qurṭubī specify faith or belief.
  holders (9 notes): MUQATIL; TAB; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL
  notes: MUQATIL:96:9-19/r3 TAB:96:13/r1 MAWARDI:96:13/r3 MAWARDI-FULL:v6p307/r6 BAGHAWI:96:13/r1 BAGHAWI-FULL:v8p480#2/r3 QURTUBI:96:13/r1 QURTUBI:96:14/r1 QURTUBI-FULL:96:13-14/r1
- 96:13/q05/p3 He denies the Messenger and refuses to accept him.
  reasons: Al-Māwardī gives this as a further possibility. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the unexpressed object of denial as the preceding praying servant and predicts Abū Jahl's denial when the Prophet calls him to Islam.
