# 96:13/q06 How do the repeated أَرَءَيْتَ clauses work grammatically? [أَرَءَيْتَ إِن · grammar]
turns on: Whether arayta governs two objects, whether later occurrences repeat or govern independently, and whether the rhetorical question needs supplied syntax.
- 96:13/q06/p1 The later questions substitute for or repeat the first; the conditionals and the statement that God sees complete the ellipsis.
  reasons: Al-Akhfash calls the second clause appositional and 96:14 its predicate. Al-Ṭabarī treats the three questions as substitutions about the one forbidding, denying, and turning. Al-Qurṭubī reports the later questions standing for the first. Al-Zamakhsharī says the repetition is emphatic, with the first relative and conditional as objects and one conditional's answer supplied by the other. Al-Bayḍāwī treats the condition as second object and later answer as supply.

- 96:13/q06/p2 The first question takes the relative clause as object; the later first objects are omitted pronouns referring back, with the final question about God's seeing as the third question's second object.

Ibn ʿĀshūr calls verse 13 a fresh threat.

# 96:13/q07 How does the condition in إِن connect to the next question? [إِن كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the apodosis is implicit, expressed as the following question without fa, or replaced by an independent warning; grammarians dispute the required fa.
- 96:13/q07/p1 Its answer is omitted; the question that God sees indicates the omitted response.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the closing question supplies broad completions and one threat suffices. Al-Ālūsī's principal parsing infers both omitted answers from 96:14, though he notes a view using arayta as cue. Al-Biqāʿī's iḥtibāk infers being astray from earlier guidance and divine sight from its later mention, rather than tanāzuʿ. Islāhī says suppressed apodosis conveys unspeakable severity in classical Arabic. Elmalılı prefers omission and independent resumption.

- 96:13/q07/p2 The answer is indicated by the repeated 'Have you seen?' itself.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī records the cue as prospective reference to the statement still to come.
  holders (3 notes): ALUSI(قيل)
  notes: ALUSI:96:10/r24 ALUSI:96:10/r23 ALUSI:96:13/r19
- 96:13/q07/p3 The question 'Does he not know that God sees?' directly answers the condition without fa.
  reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī uses 6:47 for this construction; al-Raḍī explicitly permits an interrogative answer without fa and compares al-Anʿām. Al-Khafājī says conditional and interrogative can jointly fill object and answer slots, directly or by implication; al-Raḍī and al-Damāmīnī connect this with Sībawayh's preferred analysis. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites 6:40, 6:46–47, 28:71–72, 11:88, 41:52 and 10:50 as other such Qur'anic questions.
