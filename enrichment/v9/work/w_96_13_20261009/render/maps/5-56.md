# 5:56: 15 questions

## 5:56/q01 What does taking God, His Messenger, and believers as allies entail?  [يَتَوَلَّ ٱللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُۥ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether wilaya denotes patronage and authority, religious support, or the concrete acts of obedience and solidarity, and how 5:55 is read.
- 5:56/q01/p1 It entails obeying God and supporting or following the Messenger and believers. — Al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, and al-Tabrisi distinguish obedience to God from support of the Messenger and believers; al-Tabrisi adds following the Messenger's command. Al-Qurtubi reports this formulation. Al-Tabataba'i argues that support is given to God's religion, which the Messenger himself upholds; he cites 61:14, 47:7, 3:81, 22:40, 40:51, and 30:47, and says the Qur'an does not call the Prophet a supporter of believers. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 5:56/q01/p2 It is taking them as patrons, guardians, or protectors and joining their side. — Al-Tabataba'i glosses tawalla as taking a wali and says the single singular wali governing God, Messenger, and believers implies one sense of wilaya; he adds that the promised victory fits authority, love, and support, citing 58:21 and 37:171–73. Al-Zamakhshari says their patron has joined an unconquerable party. Al-Biqa'i stresses seeking alliance with the source of all might. The Study Quran relates this to 5:51 and 5:55. [13 notes: THALABI(أبو جعفر); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 5:56/q01/p3 It is entrusting one's affairs to God, obeying the Messenger, and allying with Muslims. — Al-Qurtubi gives the three relationships separately. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 5:56/q01/p4 In a contextual reading, wilaya here means support limited to God, the Messenger, and true believers. — Al-Tabataba'i reports that some exegetes link this sense to 3:68, 33:6, 8:72, and 9:71. He argues against making these verses part of the prior passage's support theme, because their content and occasions differ; the Prophet cannot be made a mere supporter of believers. [1 notes: TABATABAI(جماعة من مفسري القوم); TABATABAI-]
- 5:56/q01/p5 On an alternative reading of the preceding bowing phrase, the allies are faithful worshippers who submit in obedience or give alms despite poverty. — Al-Tabataba'i presents the consequences of reading bowing in 5:55 in this broader way. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 5:56/q02 How can alliance with God be attained inwardly?  [يَتَوَلَّ ٱللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُۥ · inward]
turns on: The notes offer different spiritual applications of alliance rather than an explicit dispute.
- 5:56/q02/p1 True repentance is the alliance with God and His Messenger described here. — Sahl places this alliance among repentance's stations and states. [1 notes: SULAMI(سهل)]
- 5:56/q02/p2 Alliance with God passes through alliance with the Messenger and exemplary elders, who must be honored. — Al-Qasim gives the sequence; al-Sulami adduces two Prophetic sayings equating honor or reverence for an aged Muslim with reverence for God or His majesty. [3 notes: SULAMI(القاسم,النبي)]

## 5:56/q03 What event is reported as the occasion for this verse?  [وَمَن يَتَوَلَّ ٱللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُۥ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · reports]
turns on: Whether the verse's reported occasion is ʿUbādah's broken Jewish pact or the ring gift, and whether the latter report includes a succession declaration.
- 5:56/q03/p1 It followed ʿUbādah ibn al-Ṣāmit's renunciation of Banū Qaynuqāʿ and allegiance to God, the Messenger, and believers. — Ibn Ishaq connects 5:55–56 with ʿUbādah's disavowal of their pact; the report through ʿUbādah's son specifies the tribe's war with Muhammad. Ibn Kathir says the preceding verses concerned the same act. [4 notes: IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); TAB-FULL(عبادة بن الوليد بن عبادة بن الصامت); IBNABIHATIM(عبادة بن الصامت); IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 5:56/q03/p2 It followed ʿAlī's giving a ring to a petitioner while bowing, after ostracized converts complained to the Prophet. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Salām reports name ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām and other converts and say the petitioner identified ʿAlī; some have the Prophet recite 5:56, others both verses or the passage ending there. The noon caller's question establishes the act in al-Durr's account. Ḥassān's poem praises Abū Ḥasan's bowing gift and says God established allegiance for him. Al-Tabataba'i says Sunni and Shia reports are numerous, their wording varies, major tafsir and hadith authorities accepted them, and rejecting so many occasions would imperil reports of other verses. [9 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن عباس); TABRISI(حسان بن ثابت); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI(ابن سلام,ابن عباس); TABATABAI+]
- 5:56/q03/p3 In a fuller ring report, the Prophet declared ʿAlī his successor in wilaya, those present assented, and then 5:56 was revealed. — Al-Tabataba'i quotes the report's continuation: the Prophet questioned the petitioner about the ring before making the declaration. [1 notes: TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]

## 5:56/q04 Who are the believers named as allies?  [ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase points to ʿAlī particularly, the first Muslim communities, or believers generally, and whether it resumes the description in 5:55.
- 5:56/q04/p1 They are ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib in the immediate reference. — Muqātil identifies ʿAlī directly and then extends the party beyond him. The ring reports identify him as the believer who gave while bowing. [4 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن عباس); TABATABAI]
- 5:56/q04/p2 They are the Emigrants, the Helpers, and later believers. — Ibn ʿAbbās gives this wider identification in al-Wāḥidī. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)]
- 5:56/q04/p3 They are the Emigrants and the Helpers. — Al-Baghawī transmits this identification from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 5:56/q04/p4 They are the believing community, who protect one another. — Abū Jaʿfar speaks of mutual protection; al-Tabataba'i says the definite phrase points back to the believers described in 5:55. Al-Biqāʿī says repeating the allies states the referents and brings blessing, while describing the Messenger as formed by the Qur'an. Al-Ālūsī says repeating believers rather than a pronoun preserves God's primacy in guardianship. [4 notes: THALABI(أبو جعفر); TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 5:56/q05 Why repeat the believers instead of using a pronoun?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · rhetoric]
turns on: The sources give two rhetorical functions for repeating the noun.
- 5:56/q05/p1 The repetition clarifies the intended allies and invokes blessing. — Al-Biqāʿī adds that the Messenger is characterized by the Qur'an. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 5:56/q05/p2 The repetition maintains God's primary place in guardianship. — Al-Ālūsī ties it to the preceding declaration of guardianship. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 5:56/q06 How is the conditional construction parsed?  [وَمَن يَتَوَلَّ ٱللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُۥ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ فَإِنَّ حِزْبَ ٱللَّهِ هُمُ ٱلْغَٰلِبُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the fa clause itself answers the condition or explains an omitted answer, and whether a conditional subject needs a resumptive pronoun.
- 5:56/q06/p1 The following fa clause is the conditional answer, explicitly stating the result. — Al-Wāḥidī says its meaning returns to man and fa introduces the response; al-Ṭabrisī makes man nominative, its verb's pronoun jussive, and the fa clause jussive in position. Al-Samīn notes this analysis allows no overt resumptive pronoun; al-Akhfash accepts repetition of the subject's meaning, with an understood pronoun. Abū Ḥayyān also permits an explicit noun in place of the pronoun. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 5:56/q06/p2 The conditional answer is omitted; the following clause supplies evidence for it. — Al-Naḥḥās supplies 'is in God's party'; Abū Ḥayyān supplies joining that party and prevailing; al-Samīn supplies being joined to or helped by it and says the explanatory clause then has no syntactic position. Al-ʿĀlūsī says many grammarians treat it as evidence. Ibn ʿĀshūr says membership in God's party explains the implied result. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes an unstated major premise: taking them as wali yields victory because it joins God's party. [7 notes: NAHHAS(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 5:56/q06/p3 Man is the subject and the following clause its predicate, implying that whoever allies with God is victorious. — Al-Jishumī gives this analysis; al-Samīn also identifies man as a nominative conditional subject. [2 notes: JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR]

## 5:56/q07 How is the hamza in ءامنوا read or eased?  [ءَامَنُوا۟ · readings]
turns on: Whether easing is productive or restricted to transmitted words, and whether the preceding weak letter is an original glide or a long vowel.
- 5:56/q07/p1 Iraqi transmitters ease medial hamza after a long vowel, using replacement or between-between pronunciation. — Ibn al-Jazarī says open hamza after damma may become wāw and after kasra yāʾ, or be pronounced between; in the remaining seven suras the between-between form is used. [1 notes: NASHR]
- 5:56/q07/p2 Some readers substitute and assimilate hamza after original wāw or yāʾ. — Yūnus and al-Kisāʾī report Arab usage; Sībawayh transmits it but limits it to attested cases. Ibn al-Jazarī reports it from Ḥamza. He rejects a ban on assimilation after a long vowel: for an augmentative vowel, assimilation is estimated, as in Abū ʿAmr's examples نودي يا موسى and هو والذين آمنوا. [3 notes: NASHR(حمزة,يونس والكسائي)]
- 5:56/q07/p3 Substitution and assimilation are limited to شيء, هيئة, and موئلا. — Ibn Bulayma limits them to these transmitted words rather than a productive analogy. [1 notes: NASHR(ابن بليمة)]
- 5:56/q07/p4 Only transmitted instances license assimilation; the productive analogy keeps original wāw and yāʾ. — Ibn Mujāhid and the majority favor this transmission-only rule. [1 notes: NASHR(ابن مجاهد)+]
- 5:56/q07/p5 Assimilation is allowed after a weak-letter glide but forbidden after a long vowel. — Abū al-ʿAlāʾ says a long vowel cannot assimilate; Ibn al-Jazarī counters that augmentative long vowels require estimated assimilation. [1 notes: NASHR(أبو العلاء); NASHR-]

## 5:56/q08 What does ḥizb mean in this wording?  [حِزْبَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the lexical emphasis is common purpose, support, strength, or the separate usage of a recitation portion.
- 5:56/q08/p1 It is a group or companions gathered around a common cause, view, or pressing matter. — Al-Wāḥidī reports al-Farrāʾ's 'class' and Ibn al-Aʿrābī's 'group' and calls them companions sharing a position. Al-Jishumī says shared hearts and deeds suffice without physical meeting; he and al-Ṭabrisī connect the root to an affliction. Al-Qurṭubī gives companions, a class, gathered factions fighting prophets, and the idiom hisbahu amr. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, and Elmalılı emphasize assembling for a cause or matter that confronts them. [15 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأعرابي,الفراء); JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 5:56/q08/p2 A ḥizb is a supporter or helper. — Al-Ṭabarī cites a rajaz line using 'my party' for 'my supporter' alongside aḍwā, being weakened or oppressed; al-Thaʿlabī cites a line about Bilāl, and al-Qurṭubī cites a poem using ḥizbī in the same sense. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 5:56/q08/p3 A ḥizb is a distinct group marked by strength or severity. — Al-Samīn states this; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ quotes al-Rāghib's strength or toughness, and al-Ālūsī allows either general group or this narrower sense. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL]
- 5:56/q08/p4 A ḥizb may mean a nightly or Qur'anic recitation portion. — Al-Qurṭubī cites the hadith about missing one's nightly ḥizb and also notes the senses Qur'an portion, faction, and gathering as taḥazzub. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 5:56/q09 Who are God's party?  [حِزْبَ ٱللَّهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether ḥizb Allāh names all loyal believers, their function as helpers or army, a narrower set of believers, or the Messenger with them.
- 5:56/q09/p1 They are those allied with God, His Messenger, and believers: the faithful adherents of God's side. — Muqātil begins with ʿAlī and includes other Muslims and believing People of the Book such as ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām. Al-Wāḥidī contrasts believers as God's party with unbelievers as Satan's; al-Ṭabarī contrasts God's with Satan's party. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says 58:22 describes their refusal to love opponents of God even among kin and their strengthening by the Spirit. The Study Quran notes later Islamic groups, including Hezbollah in Lebanon, adopted the term. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI(أبو جعفر); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI]
- 5:56/q09/p2 They are God's supporters and helpers, especially supporters of His religion. — Al-Ṭabarī says God's helpers prevail over Satan's party. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, Abū ʿUbayda as cited by Ibn al-Jawzī, and al-Qurṭubī offer this gloss; a poem is cited for ḥizb as supporter. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(غيره); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); QURTUBI-FULL; ELMALILI(المفسرون)]
- 5:56/q09/p3 They are God's army or troops. — Al-Ḥasan gives the army gloss in al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and al-Qurṭubī; al-Wāḥidī and Elmalılı also give troops among their glosses. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); ELMALILI(المفسرون)]
- 5:56/q09/p4 They are God's friends or adherents who follow His religion and obey Him. — Abū Rawq says friends, Abū al-ʿĀliya says adherents, and al-Akhfash says obedient followers whom God supports; Elmalılı reports friends and partisans too. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العالية,أبو روق,الأخفش); ELMALILI(المفسرون)]
- 5:56/q09/p5 The phrase can refer particularly to the Messenger and the believers, whose patron joins their unconquerable side. — Al-Zamakhsharī permits this narrower referent; al-Nasafī repeats it. Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī's inclusion of God with them but calls that parsing syntactically strained. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 5:56/q09/p6 The party is God, the Messenger, and believers collectively. — Abū Ḥayyān attributes the parsing to al-Zamakhsharī and objects that it strains syntax. [0 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-]
- 5:56/q09/p7 They are the Emigrants and the Helpers. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس)]
- 5:56/q09/p8 They are the Helpers specifically. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports Abū Sulaymān's identification. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان)]
- 5:56/q09/p9 They are God's elect, steadfast with Him under uprightness. — Al-Sulamī reports this inward definition from unnamed interpreters. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]

## 5:56/q10 Why say God's party explicitly rather than use a pronoun?  [فَإِنَّ حِزْبَ ٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
- 5:56/q10/p1 The explicit name proves their victory by identifying them as God's party and honors their affiliation. — Al-Zamakhsharī says it marks their defining affiliation; al-Bayḍāwī adds an implied contrast with Satan's party. Abū Ḥayyān says the association distinguishes them. Al-Biqāʿī says it encourages choice of God's allegiance; Bursevī makes the proof explicit; al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ state that joining this party is the unstated reason they prevail. Al-Naṣafī reports the pronoun-replacement analysis. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 5:56/q11 What syntactic role does hum have?  [هُمُ ٱلْغَٰلِبُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether hum serves as a predicate, a separator, or the subject of an embedded sentence.
- 5:56/q11/p1 Hum is the first predicate of inna, and al-ghālibūn is a second predicate. — Al-Naḥḥās reports this analysis. [1 notes: NAHHAS(قيل)]
- 5:56/q11/p2 Hum is a separator pronoun emphasizing the identification. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn allow this parsing; Abū Ḥayyān notes that man is generic despite its singular form. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 5:56/q11/p3 Hum begins a new subject-predicate sentence, with al-ghālibūn its predicate. — Al-Samīn says this inner sentence predicates the inna clause; Abū Ḥayyān also allows hum as subject. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 5:56/q12 In what sense does God's party prevail?  [ٱلْغَٰلِبُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the victory is historical over Jews, military or political over opponents, inward over desire, or ultimate success in both worlds.
- 5:56/q12/p1 They defeat their opponents and are assured victory over Satan's party. — Al-Ṭabarī says they win and rule over enemies, while al-Suddī says they need not fear loss of power, reversal, or calamity; Ibn Abī Ḥātim and al-Durr transmit the reassurance. Al-Ḥasan says God's soldiers overcome enemies. Al-Biqāʿī says opponents are defeated and gathered for Hell; Abū Ḥayyān transmits Ibn ʿAṭiyya's 'every opponent'. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); THALABI; TABRISI(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 5:56/q12/p2 They overcame the Jews through killing, captivity, expulsion, and jizya. — Muqātil names expulsion from Medina to Syria, Adhriʿat, and Jericho. Al-Kalbī says killing and expelling Jews from their homes while ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām and companions remained loyal. Al-Wāḥidī also reports their continued loyalty. Al-Qurṭubī adds captivity and jizya. [5 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 5:56/q12/p3 They overcome the desires, wishes, and aims of their own souls. — Sahl supplies this inward victory in al-Tustarī and al-Sulamī. [2 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل)]
- 5:56/q12/p4 They succeed in this life and the next, with ultimate happiness, truth, and nearness to God. — Ibn Kathīr explicitly includes both worlds. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ explains falāḥ as attaining an aim, including control, and reads unqualified triumph as overcoming misery and falsehood in both worlds, citing 23:1. Elmalılı says other victories are merely outward or temporary because ultimate guardianship is God's; al-Qāshānī says their victory is through God. [5 notes: QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]

## 5:56/q13 How does this verse relate to its surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether adjacency and the preceding alliance warnings determine this verse's context, or its reported occasion and content require a separate reading.
- 5:56/q13/p1 It gives the positive counterpart to warnings against alliance with others. — Al-Ṭabarī addresses both those who renounced Jewish alliances and those afraid to do so. Al-Biqāʿī says the prior implication placed other allies in Satan's losing party, while this verse names the converse. The Study Quran places 5:51–57 within political boundaries about taking Jews and Christians as protectors over one's community. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 5:56/q13/p2 Verses 5:55–56 continue the account of ʿUbādah's renunciation of Banū Qaynuqāʿ. — Ibn Isḥāq connects the two verses with his disavowal. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ denies that adjacency establishes one occasion: though the sura was revealed late, its verses came at different times, shown by content and reported occasions. [1 notes: IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); TABATABAI-]
- 5:56/q13/p3 The verses have their own occasion and do not carry forward the earlier support theme. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ cites different contents and occasions within a late-revealed sura and says close reading defeats the proposed contextual objection. [2 notes: TABATABAI+]
- 5:56/q13/p4 The passage continues the bans on alliance and construes wilaya as support. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ attributes this reading to some exegetes and notes their links to 3:68, 33:6, 8:72, and 9:71. [1 notes: TABATABAI(جماعة من مفسري القوم); TABATABAI-]

## 5:56/q14 Which other Quranic passages illuminate God's party and its victory?  [* · links]
turns on: The cited passages illuminate different aspects of party identity, divine victory, and ultimate success.
- 5:56/q14/p1 58:21–22 connects God's decreed victory for His messengers with His successful party. — Ibn Kathīr cites both verses; the Study Quran adds 58:19's contrasting Satan's party and 58:22's contentment between God and His people. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says 58:22 describes believers who refuse love to God's opponents, even relatives, and are strengthened by the Spirit. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI]
- 5:56/q14/p2 23:53 illustrates groups dividing into parties and rejoicing in their own faction. — Al-Jishumī sees an echo in the description of parties. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 5:56/q14/p3 58:21 and 37:171–73 show that promised victory accords with authority, love, and support. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ cites the passages to say religious victory follows connection to God and His Messenger by any means. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 5:56/q14/p4 23:1 helps explain victory as complete success. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ uses falāḥ and ultimate happiness to explain the unqualified promise. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 5:56/q15 What practical boundary does the passage place on alliances?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether the warned-against protection excludes one's own religious community; the treaty note distinguishes interstate agreements.
- 5:56/q15/p1 Muslims are warned against taking Jews and Christians as protectors to the exclusion of their religious community. — The Study Quran describes 5:51–57 as setting political boundaries; al-Ṭabarī addresses fear of losing power after disavowing Jewish alliances. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN; TAB-FULL]
- 5:56/q15/p2 Treaties of alliance between Muslim states and Christian or Jewish political entities are allowed. — The Study Quran states this qualification of Islamic law. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
