# 2:142: 50 questions

## 2:142/q01 What does the future form of 'will say' indicate?  [سَيَقُولُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the objection was foretold before it arose or was already being repeated.
- 2:142/q01/p1 It predicts an objection before it occurs. — Zamakhshari, Razi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Biqai and Alusi say forewarning cushions its impact, prepares an answer and confirms prophecy when fulfilled; Ibn Ashur says it forestalls a claim that criticism caused the change. Jishumi calls it consolation and a sign. Bursevi says readiness lessens shock; Alusi reports an alternative that advance notice marks foolishness but replies that the later exchange would show this too. [13 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BURSEVI; ITQAN(ابن هشام)+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 2:142/q01/p2 It marks persistence or recurrence of an objection already voiced. — Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Qaffal, Abu Hayyan, Burhan, Suyuti and Study Quran cite reports of earlier speech; Abu Hayyan favors this chronology from al-Bara and resists making sin itself past-tense. Ibn Hisham says the imperfect carries continuity while sin remains future. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القفال); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BURHAN; ITQAN(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(البراء بن عازب,القفال); STUDYQURAN(R); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ITQAN(ابن هشام)-]
- 2:142/q01/p3 The future particle assures fulfillment of the prediction. — Zamakhshari makes sin strengthen the promised event; Suyuti calls this confirmation of a promise or threat. Tibī's equation with inna in negative speech is rejected because it shifts emphasis from promise to event. [3 notes: BURHAN(الزمخشري,الطيبي); ITQAN(الزمخشري)]
- 2:142/q01/p4 Sin marks the nearer future, compared with sawfa. — Grammarians cited in Burhan say sawfa is more delayed; Suyuti says sin attaches to an affirmative ungoverned imperfect and expands time. [2 notes: BURHAN(الزمخشري وابن الخشاب وابن يعيش وابن أبان وابن بابشاذ وابن عصفور); ITQAN]

## 2:142/q02 How is the plural 'the foolish' formed?  [ٱلسُّفَهَآءُ · grammar]
- 2:142/q02/p1 Sufahāʾ is the plural of safīh. — Wahidi Basit says the singular is one who lightly rushes toward what should be avoided. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 2:142/q03 What does 'the foolish' mean?  [ٱلسُّفَهَآءُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word names ordinary intellectual lightness or a sharper moral fault.
- 2:142/q03/p1 People of light or defective judgment, hence ignorant or foolish. — Zajjaj and Nahhas give its plural morphology; Mawardi compares thin cloth and a quick spear; Jishumi, Ibn Atiyya and Ibn Jawzi cite loose weave, wind and Dhu al-Rumma's poetry; Razi says religious error is most harmful; Biqai says acting without evidence; Kuranyolu adds crude or aggressive usage. [27 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن فارس,الزجاج,بعض المفسرين,ذو الرمة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعض أرباب الحقيقة); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:142/q03/p2 A knowingly slanderous liar. — Al-Muarrij says the safih deliberately contradicts known truth. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(المؤرج)]
- 2:142/q03/p3 An extremely unjust and ignorant person. — Qutrub's gloss combines moral wrongdoing and ignorance. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قطرب)]
- 2:142/q03/p4 In other Qur'anic usage it can denote dependents or self-ruin. — Ibn Jawzi reports women and children in 4:5 and ruin of oneself in 2:130; this distinguishes other contexts from 2:142. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين)]

## 2:142/q04 Who are the foolish speakers?  [ٱلسُّفَهَآءُ مِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ · referent]
turns on: Reports name Jews, Meccan polytheists, hypocrites, combinations, or an unrestricted set.
- 2:142/q04/p1 Jews, especially Medina's Jews or Jewish scholars. — Mujahid, al-Bara, Ibn Abbas and others name Jews; Tabari describes rejection of an Arab messenger. Ibn Abbas names Rifa'a, Qardam, Ka'b al-Ashraf and other leaders; their opposition to abandoning Jerusalem and to abrogation explains the objection. Kuranyolu favors this historical setting. [34 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,البراء,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(البراء); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); WAHIDI-ASBAB(البراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن عباس,ابن عباس، ومجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL(اليهود); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(البراء بن عازب، مجاهد، سعيد بن جبير); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس، مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(البراء بن عازب، مجاهد، ابن جبير); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,البراء); DURR-FULL(البراء); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî,müfessirler); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 2:142/q04/p2 Hypocrites. — Al-Suddi and others say they mocked the change as arbitrary; Razi and Elmalili cite the earlier designation of hypocrites as fools in 2:13. Elmalili prefers this link. [25 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); JISHUMI(السدي); KASHSHAF-FULL(المنافقون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي); TABRISI(السدي); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين,قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي عن ابن مسعود وابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي، عن ابن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البراء بن عازب,السدي); ELMALILI(Yehûd veya münafıkīn); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazı müfessirler,Münafıklar,müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); ELMALILI+]
- 2:142/q04/p3 Meccan polytheists or Quraysh. — Muqatil, Hasan, Zajjaj and others report their taunt that Muhammad returned to his ancestral qibla and would return to their religion; Ibn Ashur's contextual reading specifically expects this objection before the Kaaba command. [21 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الزجاج); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن,السدي); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(المشركون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(طائفة); TABRISI(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس، البراء بن عازب، الحسن، الأصم); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو صالح، عن ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن,القفال); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 2:142/q04/p4 All three groups, or all unbelieving objectors. — Thalabi, Baydawi, Baghawi, Ibn Jawzi, Jishumi and Tabatabai combine groups; Razi and Alusi argue the definite plural is general, while Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi include anyone who asked. Qadi observes that the text only proves some group spoke, without requiring universality. [17 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(القاضي); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي، عن ابن مسعود); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 2:142/q04/p5 Jews and hypocrites. — Tabari names both; Ibn Atiyya reports al-Suddi's pairing, and Elmalili allows both. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي); ELMALILI(Yehûd veya münafıkīn)]
- 2:142/q04/p6 Jews and Meccan polytheists. — Wahidi Wajiz, Baghawi and Tabatabai include these two groups. [3 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 2:142/q04/p7 People of the Book, possibly including polytheists. — Abu Muslim names the People of the Book while allowing others; Ibn Ishaq's al-Bara report says People of the Book. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(البراء)]

## 2:142/q05 Why say 'among people'?  [مِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether it chiefly qualifies human speakers, compares them with humanity, or restricts a subset.
- 2:142/q05/p1 It specifies human fools because foolishness can be ascribed to things and animals. — Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Samin cite cloth, spears and animals; Samin calls it a circumstantial phrase governed by sayaqūl, and Tabrisi likewise gives circumstantial syntax. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); TABRISI]
- 2:142/q05/p2 It marks comparison with humanity generally and heightens their disgrace. — Alusi parses an accusative circumstantial phrase with generic al-nas and prefers this; Biqai says it broadens censure to all who side with them. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 2:142/q05/p3 It specifies the worst among unbelievers, rather than every member. — Alusi reports this restricted reading of al-nas and rejects it in favor of generic humanity. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 2:142/q06 What does 'what turned them' mean?  [مَا وَلَّىٰهُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the question asks what caused a turn, or names the agent and its rhetorical force.
- 2:142/q06/p1 What diverted or redirected them from the former direction. — Mujahid, Tabari, Zajjaj, Ata and others gloss turning away; Jishumi contrasts turning away with toward; Tabrisi traces walī to succession without gap; Razi cites 8:16; Tabatabai describes putting a place before the face or turning one's back. [18 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد، عطاء); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 2:142/q06/p2 Who caused them to turn away? — Thalabi and Kashshaf phrase mā as asking the agent; Bursevi calls it emphatic and Biqai sees a contemptuous choice of mā instead of man. [4 notes: THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 2:142/q06/p3 The question expresses mockery or astonishment. — Razi and Alusi call it reproachful; Suyuti permits either astonishment or a genuine inquiry; Qushayri says objectors doubt that the first direction was true if later abandoned. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; ALUSI-FULL; ITQAN]

## 2:142/q07 How is 'what turned them' parsed?  [مَا وَلَّىٰهُمْ عَن قِبْلَتِهِمُ · grammar]
- 2:142/q07/p1 Mā is an interrogative subject, wallāhum its predicate, and ʿan qiblatihim depends on the verb. — Nahhas, Tabrisi and Samin agree on the subject-predicate construction and complement. [3 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:142/q08 Whom do the pronouns in 'turned them' and 'their qibla' denote?  [وَلَّىٰهُمْ قِبْلَتِهِمُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronouns refer to Muslims or the critics and their forebears.
- 2:142/q08/p1 The Prophet and believers. — Abu Hayyan says it is called theirs because they faced it long; Wahidi Basit prefers the majority view. Tabatabai says attributing it to Muslims heightens the objection. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 2:142/q08/p2 The critics and their inherited directions or ancestral qibla. — Abu Muslim and Abu Hayyan allow critics' Jewish and Christian directions; Ata via Ibn Abbas takes the ancestors Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob and tribes as the pronoun's referent. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT-]

## 2:142/q09 What does 'qibla' mean?  [قِبْلَتِهِمُ · meaning]
- 2:142/q09/p1 The posture or direction of facing, conventionally the place faced in prayer. — Tabari notes faʿla like jalsa and qaʿda; Wahidi, Jishumi, Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi, Baydawi, Bursevi, Alusi and Elmalili derive it from facing. Razi cites Qutrub's idiom of direction/refuge, reciprocity between two who face, and a poetic use. Harali calls it what is opposite the face. [16 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(بعض المحدثين,غيره,قطرب); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:142/q09/p2 The Kaaba is the Muslims' qibla as a term of worship. — Jishumi and Kuranyolu explicitly identify the Kaaba as the canonical prayer direction and say facing it is a condition of valid prayer. [2 notes: JISHUMI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 2:142/q10 What is the 'qibla they had been on'?  [قِبْلَتِهِمُ ٱلَّتِى كَانُوا۟ عَلَيْهَا · referent]
turns on: The main reports identify Jerusalem, while alternatives identify the Kaaba or critics' directions.
- 2:142/q10/p1 Jerusalem, the former Muslim prayer direction. — Tabari, Rabi, Ata, Wahidi and many later writers identify Jerusalem; Wahidi Basit prefers this as the exegetical majority and Razi accepts transmitted reports over Abu Muslim's hypothetical alternative. It could be called the Rock or Syrian direction. [32 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(الربيع,علي بن أبي طلحة عن ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(عطاء بن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(جماعة من المفسرين، وأبو مسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(البراء بن عازب,المفسرون); IBNKATHIR-FULL(البراء); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 2:142/q10/p2 The Kaaba, originally faced before a move to Jerusalem. — Some cited by Abu Hayyan say the Meccans reproached the Prophet for leaving Abraham's qibla. Ibn Ashur prefers this reading for the local sequence of Abraham and the House; Biqai says leaving the former direction unnamed can evoke the Kaaba. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قوم); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 2:142/q10/p3 The critics' own Jewish and Christian directions. — Abu Muslim suggests this without reports; Jishumi says Jews and Christians faced west and east respectively, and Razi says their established directions make facing the Kaaba surprising. Abu Hayyan records this pronoun reading. [4 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:142/q11 What does 'they had been on it' convey?  [ٱلَّتِى كَانُوا۟ عَلَيْهَا · rhetoric]
- 2:142/q11/p1 It stresses sustained attachment and sharpens the rebuke. — Abu Hayyan calls ʿalā figurative as if bearing the orientation; Samin supplies an omitted word such as orientation or belief; Alusi says the relative clause after 'their' intensifies criticism. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:142/q11/p2 On one account the expression is understood as the qibla now being faced. — Qurtubi records the interpretation of kunt ʿalayhā as anta ʿalayhā in an account of abrogation twice. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 2:142/q12 Why were opponents troubled by the change?  [مَا وَلَّىٰهُمْ عَن قِبْلَتِهِمُ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes give several motives, sometimes assigned to different groups.
- 2:142/q12/p1 Polytheists claimed a return to the ancestral direction vindicated their religion. — Muqatil, Hasan, Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya and others report the taunt that longing for birthplace would lead back to their religion. Jishumi notes this was to make their own practice appear true. [15 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(المشركون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(طائفة); TABRISI(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس، البراء بن عازب، الحسن، الأصم); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:142/q12/p2 Hypocrites mocked a seemingly arbitrary shift. — Al-Suddi, Razi, Alusi and Kuranyolu depict cynicism or the claim that directions are equivalent and no rational reason distinguishes them. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); JISHUMI(السدي); KASHSHAF-FULL(المنافقون); RAZI-FULL(السدي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي، عن ابن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Münafıklar); STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 2:142/q12/p3 Jews denied abrogation or feared loss of their religious distinction. — Kashshaf, Nasafi, Tabatabai, Alusi and Ibn Ashur say Jerusalem's replacement affronted Jews; Tabatabai says they thought it honored by earlier prophets and doubted that God could change it. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(اليهود); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; JISHUMI(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس)]
- 2:142/q12/p4 Jews offered to believe if the Prophet returned, seeking to tempt him. — Ibn Abbas names Jewish leaders; the offer is reported by Tabari, Ibn Abi Hatim, Mawardi, Ibn Atiyya and others; Alusi suspects insincerity. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:142/q12/p5 The critics' judgment was veiled by habitual belief or envy. — Qushayri says unbelievers object to each new affair; Qashani says restricted orientation and attachment to prior beliefs block recognition of comprehensive unity; Kuranyolu calls the roots ignorance and envy. Biqai says they spoke indirectly to followers after arguments failed. [8 notes: QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 2:142/q13 Why is the question phrased 'their qibla' rather than 'the Jews' qibla'?  [قِبْلَتِهِمُ · rhetoric]
- 2:142/q13/p1 It preserves surprise and sharpens the challenge. — Tabatabai says explicitly naming the Jews' qibla would make the reason for departure obvious; Alusi says the additional clause about following it intensifies rebuke. [2 notes: TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:142/q14 Why is the former qibla left unnamed?  [قِبْلَتِهِمُ · rhetoric]
- 2:142/q14/p1 The ambiguity can evoke both Jerusalem and the Kaaba. — Biqai says either way the critics fault God's command and expose desire. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:142/q15 What force does the command 'Say' have here?  [قُل · rhetoric]
- 2:142/q15/p1 It supplies a divine reply before or upon the objection. — Kashshaf, Baydawi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi emphasize a ready answer that silences a challenge; Biqai notes the unaddressed qul contrasts with direct challenges in 2:80 and 2:111 because critics would not face believers openly. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:142/q16 What are 'east and west' here?  [ٱلْمَشْرِقُ وَٱلْمَغْرِبُ · referent]
turns on: The pair may encompass every direction or evoke specific historical prayer directions.
- 2:142/q16/p1 The two horizons stand for all lands and directions. — Tabari describes the world between extremes of sunrise and sunset; Kashshaf and Nasafi take the lands, Biqai the horizons, and Tabatabai notes relative solar and stellar orientations. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 2:142/q16/p2 The pair evokes eastward Christians and westward Jews. — Some shaykhs relate Jewish orientation to Moses and 28:44, Christian orientation to Mary and 19:16; Bursevi says they chose those directions by preference. Ibn Ashur reports and interprets this allusion. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعض المشايخ); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(المفسرون)]
- 2:142/q16/p3 East is the Kaaba from Medina and west is Jerusalem. — Wahidi Basit reports this geographic gloss. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(قيل)]
- 2:142/q16/p4 East is Jerusalem and west the Kaaba. — Abu Hayyan gives the Kaaba as west of Jerusalem. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:142/q17 What does God's ownership of east and west answer?  [لِّلَّهِ ٱلْمَشْرِقُ وَٱلْمَغْرِبُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ownership itself suffices or direction changes should be explained by changing benefit.
- 2:142/q17/p1 No direction has intrinsic authority; God appoints whichever He wills and obedience is decisive. — Tabari, Zajjaj, Mawardi, Razi, Baydawi, Ibn Kathir, Alusi, Tabatabai and Kuranyolu state sovereignty over all places. Tabatabai relates legislation to individual and collective perfection. Ibn Kathir links obedience to 2:177; Tabrisi rejects priority for prophets' Holy Land. [29 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 2:142/q17/p2 The change tracks wisdom or the benefit appropriate to a time. — Jishumi prefers changed benefit; Tabrisi says God sanctifies places for people's interests; Baydawi and Alusi make welfare the straightness of successive orientations. [8 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI(القاضي)+]
- 2:142/q17/p3 God's knowledge and presence are not bounded by a direction. — Zajjaj cites 6:3, 57:4 and 58:7 to say God knows and is present wherever worship is commanded; Qashani says the Real is in no direction though all directions relate to Him, citing 2:115. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; QASHANI]

## 2:142/q18 How is 'He guides whom He wills' understood?  [يَهْدِى مَن يَشَآءُ · theology]
turns on: Whether guidance is indication, God's effective creation of knowledge, or a person's willing receptivity.
- 2:142/q18/p1 God grants and directs guidance to whomever He wills. — Tabari applies it to believers receiving Abraham's qibla while others are left astray; Muqatil calls the destination Islam; Qushayri says divine light; Elmalili says foundational grace and prophethood precede human acquisition and cites 2:286. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية,البراء بن عازب); BAGHAWI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 2:142/q29 Why institute a fixed bodily prayer direction?  [* · interpretation]

## 2:142/q30 Do God's rulings require purposes or benefits?  [* · theology]
turns on: Razi contrasts Sunni sovereignty with Mu'tazili wisdom; other sources warn against speculation.
- 2:142/q30/p1 Every wise divine act has a purpose, perhaps hidden benefit. — Razi reports the Mu'tazila: qibla change may carry concealed good without compromising wisdom. Jishumi and Tabrisi explain changes by altered welfare. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); JISHUMI(القاضي); TABRISI]
- 2:142/q30/p2 No purpose is required; divine sovereignty is sufficient. — Razi reports Sunni objections: a purpose benefiting God implies lack; intermediary goals imply futility or incapacity; eternal or created purposes lead to impossible regress, including time of creation, divine will and evil. - 2:142/q30/p3 Only revealed or soundly reasoned wisdoms should be accepted. — Abu Hayyan rejects speculative rationales; Razi states his listed wisdoms are possible, not proofs. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; RAZI-FULL]

## 2:142/q31 What does the change prove about abrogation?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The accounts disagree on what was abrogated and whether any earlier divine command existed.
- 2:142/q31/p1 The qibla change establishes permissible abrogation, often called the first. — Ikrima and Hasan cite 2:115; Jishumi cites 2:106 and 2:142; Ibn Abbas calls qibla first abrogated; Qurtubi says the community agrees except an outlier and speaks of the first Qur'anic ruling, perhaps twice. Study Quran calls it a paradigm. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(عكرمة والحسن البصري); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن فورك عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 2:142/q31/p2 Qur'an abrogated an earlier Sunna or non-recited practice. — Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan say the Jerusalem direction was not set by recited Qur'an; Study Quran repeats the paradigm. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 2:142/q31/p3 No earlier divine qibla ordinance was abrogated, and the passage is coherent. — Asad says no command preceded 2:142-150; Tabatabai rejects reports of disorder or abrogation against the sequence's apparent meaning. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI+]
- 2:142/q31/p4 An earlier prediction was itself abrogated by a later recited command. — Zarkashi adds the qibla passage to cases where recitation order and supersession differ: 2:142's prediction is called abrogated by 2:144. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 2:142/q31/p5 Opponents deny abrogation as divine ignorance or purposelessness. — Razi records Jewish and Christian dilemmas about temporary versus permanent commands and equally created directions; his explanation of the objection sets up the reply. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(اليهود والنصارى)]

## 2:142/q32 When does a changed ruling bind someone unaware of it?  [* · law]

- 2:142/q32/p1 Only after notice reaches the person; prior acts stand. — Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir cite Quba's continued Jerusalem prayer and lack of repetition. - 2:142/q32/p3 A lone report cannot itself abrogate definitive knowledge. — Opponents say conjectural evidence cannot replace certainty; Qurtubi says Quba and governor cases carried additional indications that produced knowledge. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(من منع ذلك)]
- 2:142/q32/p4 A worshipper finding water during tayammum prayer completes it. — Qurtubi gives this as Malik, Shafi'i and others' view; he groups other changing circumstances where current prayer continues. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 2:142/q32/p5 A worshipper finding water during tayammum prayer interrupts it. — Qurtubi reports Abu Hanifa's dissent. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة)]

## 2:142/q33 What is required for facing the Kaaba in prayer?  [* · law]
turns on: Exact axis and general direction differ for distant worshippers.
- 2:142/q33/p1 Distant worshippers need only face its general direction. — Most Malikis, Abu Hanifa, Ahmad and others favor an attainable direction; Ibn Ashur prefers it to remove hardship. - 2:142/q33/p2 Facing the Kaaba is required when able, with exceptions for need and travel. — Ibn Ashur says obligatory and voluntary prayer require orientation, with necessity and journey concessions; Kuranyolu permits convenient direction when illness, disability, fear or uncertainty prevents it, and advises an onboard traveler to face as far as possible at the start. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:142/q33/p3 Those who see the Kaaba must face its visible structure. — Ibn Ashur says interior and visible worshippers face the building; long rows curve. Kuranyolu reports agreement on exact facing when it is visible. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İslâm bilginleri)]
- 2:142/q33/p4 Distant worshippers must aim at the Kaaba's exact axis. — Ibn al-Qassar and a transmitted Shafi'i view require precision, sometimes needing astronomical instruments. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن القصار)]
- 2:142/q33/p5 Medinans can use the Prophet's prayer place as an orientation. — Ibn al-Qasim reports Malik saying Gabriel fixed the Prophet's mosque qibla. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن القاسم عن مالك)]
- 2:142/q33/p6 A sincere best estimate suffices if direction cannot be learned. — Kuranyolu advises celestial signs, wind or a marker; later discovery of an error does not invalidate completed prayer, while discovery during prayer requires turning without interruption. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 2:142/q34 How has the qibla been determined at long distances?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Historic and modern geometric methods can point differently.
- 2:142/q34/p1 Sector methods extend the Kaaba's walls and use cardinal and celestial markers. — Study Quran describes summer sunrise and Canopus as examples and notes older mosques' variable bearings. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 2:142/q34/p2 Great-circle calculation uses the shortest surface path. — Study Quran says North American paths begin northeast and curve southeast; this differs from older sector southeast bearings and both are used. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 2:142/q34/p3 A rhumb line keeps a constant compass bearing toward Mecca. — Study Quran names this as another method. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 2:142/q34/p4 Local nonmathematical methods and later instruments produced differing mosque axes. — Study Quran says mathematical work sometimes led to adjusted walls. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 2:142/q35 How does this passage connect with its surrounding verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Writers emphasize several different local and long-range links.
- 2:142/q35/p1 It continues the Abraham and Kaaba argument and anticipates opponents. — Asad links Abraham's building of the earliest temple; Tabatabai links Abraham and Ishmael's prayers, construction and purification; Ibn Ashur sees the sequence as prompting a Meccan objection. Biqai lists five prior arguments including 2:115 and mosque sanctity. [10 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 2:142/q35/p2 It echoes 2:115 and al-Fatiha's request for guidance. — Study Quran repeats 'east and west belong to God'; Elmalili and Ibn Ashur connect the straight path to 1:6. Biqai contrasts definite and indefinite paths. [4 notes: STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 2:142/q35/p3 It links foolish opposition to earlier rebukes and the qibla test. — Biqai cites 2:130 and 2:13; Ibn Kathir and Durr connect the change to 2:143's testing and concern for earlier prayers; Samarrai describes the extended sequence through 2:150. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن إسحاق); DURR-FULL(قتادة); SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 2:142/q35/p4 It follows criticism of Jewish faults or disputes over fundamentals. — Jishumi says the passage supplies arguments on unity then prophethood and consoles the Prophet; Alusi says lack of conjunction makes criticism of a subsidiary matter independently blameworthy. [2 notes: JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:142/q35/p5 The whole sequence coherently announces the Kaaba direction. — Tabatabai rejects disorder and abrogation claims and treats verses 2:142 onward as a connected proclamation. [1 notes: TABATABAI+]

## 2:142/q36 What distinctive inward reading does the verse invite?  [* · inward]
- 2:142/q36/p1 True orientation witnesses God's unity beyond competing physical directions. — Qushayri says the veiled seek causal explanations for saints' states; Qashani says sound insight recognizes the Real beyond directions and cites 2:115; Bursevi reports spiritual masters' account of veiled minds. [8 notes: QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; BURSEVI(بعض أرباب الحقيقة)]
- 2:142/q36/p2 The Kaaba can symbolize nested qiblas and inward direction toward God. — Razi reports ranks from Throne, Kursi and Frequented House to Kaaba and God; he contrasts bodily orientation with the heart and compares the poor as direction of divine mercy, citing 6:52. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 2:142/q37 What broader Arabic interrogative usages inform reading questions?  [* · grammar]
- 2:142/q37/p1 An interrogative may coordinate alternatives and a renewed question in different ways. — Sibawayh distinguishes aw after one question from am hal for a new clause; hal lacks the presupposition an interrogative alif can carry and survives indirect inquiry. He cites poetry by Zuhayr, Malik ibn al-Rayb, Zafar and Alqama. [5 notes: SIBAWAYH(العرب)]

## 2:142/q38 What principles govern responsible lexical interpretation of Qur'an?  [* · interpretation]
- 2:142/q38/p1 Arabic usage, Sunnah and early reports guard against arbitrary interpretation. — Wahidi urges learning Qur'anic address and Sunnah; early authorities encouraged Arabic. He reports Umar asking a Hudhali elder about takhawwuf in 16:47, which poetry explains as gradual diminution, and includes a report on teaching good manners. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(النبي,سعيد بن المسيب)]
- 2:142/q38/p2 Words name apprehensible intended meanings, especially those needed in discourse. — Razi argues infinite or obscure essences lack common names, practical meanings merit names, and 'movement' names transition rather than hidden causes. Meanings are intended mental images; uncoined sweetness distinctions and private states require relational description or newly shared terms. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 2:142/q38/p3 Eloquent verbal composition must be explained by speakers' aims. — Jurjani urges readers to move from repeated unexplained expressions to understanding composition; he distinguishes God's own reciprocal clauses from quoted speech followed by assertion. [2 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 2:142/q39 How can east–west imagery work outside this verse?  [* · rhetoric]
- 2:142/q39/p1 A poetic sun may be imagined as literally visiting while staying in its orbit. — Jurjani reads Bashshar's conceit as the actual celestial sun coming down. [1 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 2:142/q39/p2 An indefinite sun can mean a sunny day, though that strains the conceit. — Jurjani prefers this reading for Ashja's change from definite to indefinite and acknowledges tension with east-west imagery. [1 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR+]

## 2:142/q40 What other abrogation examples and rationales are adduced?  [* · sciences]
- 2:142/q40/p1 The Witr qunut surahs are reported as recitation lifted while wording was memorized. — Zarkashi cites Munadi on al-Khal' and al-Hafd in Ubayy's codices, and al-Funun says retained rulings test obedience on probable evidence like Abraham's dream in 37:102. [3 notes: BURHAN(أبو الحسين أحمد بن جعفر المنادى,صاحب الفنون)]

## 2:142/q41 What stopping and beginning rules are illustrated near 'will say'?  [سَيَقُولُ ٱلسُّفَهَآءُ · readings]
turns on: Stop taxonomies differ in scope.
- 2:142/q41/p1 An absolute stop can precede self-contained fresh speech. — Suyuti uses sayaqūlu al-sufahāʾ as an independent verb example; he lists questions, negations, conditions and omitted objects as other contexts but bars a stop between subject and predicate or condition and consequence. [3 notes: ITQAN]
- 2:142/q41/p2 Stops can be classified in eight grades. — Suyuti reports another scholar's complete, near-complete, deficient, near-deficient, good, near-good, ugly and near-ugly scheme. [1 notes: ITQAN(غيره)]
- 2:142/q41/p3 Voluntary and forced stops are more defensible divisions. — Ibn al-Jazari finds conventional categories unbounded and says complete speech permits a voluntary stop, while a truly complete stop lacks lexical or semantic tie to following text. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن الجزري)+]

## 2:142/q42 What legal lesson about sincerity appears in nearby discussion?  [* · law]
turns on: Reports distinguish devotional sacrifice from social celebration.
- 2:142/q42/p1 Worship begun sincerely follows its initial intention, but display has no added merit. — Bursevi cites Tatarhaniyya on ostentatious prayer, a sage's pebbles-to-market simile, and a report that God accepts only sincere deeds. [3 notes: BURSEVI(التتارخانية,الحديث,بعض الحكماء)]
- 2:142/q42/p2 Sacrifice devoted to other than God is forbidden. — Bursevi cites a report cursing such sacrifice and Nawawi's explanation of slaughter in another's name, such as an idol or Moses. Marwadhi applies this to slaughter honoring a ruler as devotion. [3 notes: BURSEVI(أحاديث المشارق,إبراهيم المراودي,النووي)]
- 2:142/q42/p3 Slaughter celebrating a ruler's arrival can be permissible. — Rafi'i likens it to an aqiqah; Bursevi prefers a charitable interpretation of Muslims' acts to avoid charges of unbelief. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الرافعي); BURSEVI+]

## 2:142/q43 What other descriptions of the Qur'an appear in related notes?  [* · meaning]
