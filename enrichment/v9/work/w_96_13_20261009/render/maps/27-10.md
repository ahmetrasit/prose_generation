# 27:10: 34 questions

## 27:10/q01 What was Moses' staff?  [عَصَاكَ · reports]
- 27:10/q01/p1 An angel gave it to Moses on his way to Midian. — Ibn ʿAbbās says it lit the night, brought up plants on struck ground, and shook leaves for sheep. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 27:10/q01/p2 It was named Masha. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr names it; al-Māwardī's Ibn ʿAbbās report repeats the name. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 27:10/q01/p3 It was made of boxthorn. — Ibn Jubayr identifies ʿawsaj as its wood. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جبير)]

## 27:10/q02 How did Moses take the command to cast his staff?  [وَأَلْقِ عَصَاكَ · reports]
- 27:10/q02/p1 He thought he was to abandon it. — Wahb says he threw it as if rejecting it; al-Qurṭubī transmits the same understanding. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(وهب); QURTUBI-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]

## 27:10/q03 Why was Moses told to cast the staff?  [وَأَلْقِ عَصَاكَ · interpretation]
- 27:10/q03/p1 To show a sign, establish his commission and train him for its use. — Al-Qushayrī says the familiar staff yields certainty; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Nasafī stress training and reassurance; Ibn Kathīr sees God's free power; al-Biqāʿī, might and wisdom; al-Qurṭubī says each prophet needs a personal sign; Iṣlāḥī calls it the main sign among nine. [8 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 27:10/q03/p2 To teach reliance on God alone. — Elmalılı says Moses should no longer rely on another support at this station. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 27:10/q04 How is 'cast your staff' connected to the preceding speech?  [وَأَلْقِ عَصَاكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether unlike declarative and imperative clauses coordinate, and how the parallel أن bears on it.
- 27:10/q04/p1 It coordinates with the blessing announcement as part of the divine call. — Al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī cite repeated explanatory أن in al-Qaṣaṣ. Al-Zamakhsharī supplies 'he was told.' Al-Fārisī and Abū al-Baqāʾ alternatively construe būrika as prayer so both clauses are performative. Al-Ālūsī replies that renewed 'O Moses' can continue the call or be parenthetic. [12 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURHAN(الفارسي); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 27:10/q04/p2 It coordinates directly with the declaration 'I am God'. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn cite Sībawayh's permission to coordinate unlike sentence forms; al-Samīn rejects a parallel-form requirement. Study Quran links the opening to verse 9. Al-Naḥḥās says the utterance to Moses ends here. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); STUDYQURAN; NAHHAS; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 27:10/q04/p3 An unspoken 'he was told' connects the declaration and imperative. — The Shaykh objects that Zamakhsharī's explanation needs this extra speech clause. Al-Ālūsī says an omitted command would favor fa-alqi. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 27:10/q05 Why is the command direct here but introduced by 'wa-an' in al-Qaṣaṣ?  [وَأَلْقِ عَصَاكَ · rhetoric]
- 27:10/q05/p1 The direct imperative gives God's utterance and honors Moses; al-Qaṣaṣ gives the call's sense. — Al-Burhān notes the wording difference. Sāmarrāʾī links direct address, command and ladayya to divine nearness. [6 notes: BURHAN; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 27:10/q06 What events are omitted between the command and seeing the staff move?  [فَلَمَّا رَءَاهَا تَهْتَزُّ · rhetoric]
- 27:10/q06/p1 Moses cast it and it became a moving serpent. — Al-Wāḥidī says al-Aʿrāf and al-Shuʿarāʾ state the steps; al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī say fa compresses a quick sequence. Abū Ḥayyān differently locates the omitted casting after trembling. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 27:10/q07 How is 'raʾāhā' read?  [رَءَاهَا · readings]
turns on: The placement of imāla.
- 27:10/q07/p1 Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim, al-Kisāʾī and Ḥamza incline both vowels. — Al-Fārisī reports kasra on rāʾ and hamza and points to earlier grounds for imāla; Ibn ʿAṭiyya mentions imāla. Ibn Khālawayh calls kasra on both the rāʾ and hamza imālat al-imāla. [5 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو بكر عن عاصم وحفص عن عاصم,أبو عمرو والكسائي وحمزة وابن عامر وابن كثير ونافع); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض القراء); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA]
- 27:10/q07/p2 Abū ʿAmr opens rāʾ and inclines the hamza. — Al-Fārisī reports this throughout. [1 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو والكسائي وحمزة وابن عامر وابن كثير ونافع)]
- 27:10/q07/p3 Ḥafṣ, Ibn ʿĀmir, Ibn Kathīr and Nāfiʿ open both. — Al-Fārisī contrasts Ḥafṣ with Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim. [2 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو بكر عن عاصم وحفص عن عاصم,أبو عمرو والكسائي وحمزة وابن عامر وابن كثير ونافع)]

## 27:10/q08 What does 'tahtazzu' mean?  [تَهْتَزُّ · meaning]
- 27:10/q08/p1 It moves, shakes or writhes vigorously. — Muqātil and al-Wāḥidī say it moves; Ibn Jurayj says it had become a moving serpent; al-Bursawī says it darted violently; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī defines ihtizāz as vigorous motion. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); BURSEVI; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 27:10/q09 What is the grammatical role of 'tahtazzu'?  [تَهْتَزُّ · grammar]
- 27:10/q09/p1 It is a circumstantial clause from the seen object pronoun. — Al-Nasafī, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī attach it to raʾāhā; the latter two specify visual seeing. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 27:10/q10 What does 'jānn' mean?  [جَآنٌّ · meaning]
turns on: Lexical usage, size and the force of the simile.
- 27:10/q10/p1 A small, light, swift snake. — Ibn Qutayba says not large; al-Layth says white, Ibn Shumayl smooth and harmless, and the house-snake hadith uses plural jinān. Al-Māwardī, al-Jishumī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Rāzī derive the name from concealment. Al-Qāmūs gives dark-eyed and harmless; Elmalılı notes doubled nūn. [23 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الليث وابن شميل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(القاموس); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 27:10/q10/p2 A snake between small and large. — Al-Kalbī and al-Farrāʾ give the intermediate size. [4 notes: THALABI(الكلبي); JISHUMI(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي)]
- 27:10/q10/p3 A large serpent. — Al-Ṭabarī says a large snake of this kind, citing a rajaz poet's jinnān; al-Naḥḥās invokes Arab usage. Al-Zajjāj calls thuʿbān the great snake. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(الراجز); NAHHAS; ZAJJAJ]
- 27:10/q10/p4 A devil of the jinn, likened for its frightfulness. — Al-Māwardī cites 37:65; Sāmarrāʾī says jinn are hidden yet may appear in forms, citing Abū Hurayra. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 27:10/q11 How is 'jānn' read?  [جَآنٌّ · readings]
turns on: How the dialect avoids adjacent sukūns.
- 27:10/q11/p1 Al-Ḥasan, al-Zuhrī and ʿAmr ibn ʿUbayd replace alif with hamza. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī explain avoidance of adjacent sukūns, with 1:7 and daʾba/shaʾba parallels. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن والزهري وعمرو بن عبيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن، والزهري، وعمرو بن عبيد); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن والزهري وعمرو بن عبيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن والزهري وعمرو بن عبيد)]
- 27:10/q11/p2 Al-Ḥasan omits tanwīn. — Al-Rāzī attributes it to a dialect avoiding two sukūns, citing shaʾba and daʾba. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الحسن)]

## 27:10/q12 How is the jān comparison reconciled with the great serpent elsewhere?  [كَأَنَّهَا جَآنٌّ · interpretation]
turns on: One body and motion, successive changes, or distinct occasions.
- 27:10/q12/p1 One great serpent moved with a small snake's speed and lightness. — Al-Zajjāj, Abū Isḥāq and Thaʿlab distinguish shape from motion; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says small snakes move faster. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 7:107 and 26:32. Al-Biqāʿī describes twisting and speed. [20 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ثعلب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; TABATABAI+]
- 27:10/q12/p2 It first appeared as a small snake, then grew into a great serpent. — Al-Thaʿlabī says it swelled from staff size; al-Qurṭubī gives a variant with an intermediate female serpent before a male thuʿbān. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says staging fails to explain the comparison. [6 notes: THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قول غير مسمى)-]
- 27:10/q12/p3 It was a small jān at the mountain and a great serpent before Pharaoh. — Al-Ṭabrisī and Abū al-Layth distinguish the occasions; Sāmarrāʾī says jān fits private fear and thuʿbān public might. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that this fails to resolve the simile. [4 notes: TABRISI; BURSEVI(أبو الليث); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; TABATABAI(قول غير مسمى)-]

## 27:10/q13 What happened to the staff in the transformation?  [عَصَاكَ كَأَنَّهَا جَآنٌّ · theology]
turns on: The nature of the change and the witnessed form.
- 27:10/q13/p1 God made it alive and changed its attributes while preserving its substance. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says God created life and changed properties; Abū Ḥayyān denies destruction and recreation of the staff's substance. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 27:10/q13/p2 It became a huge terrifying serpent. — Wahb says it swallowed a boulder, tore up a tree, and had fiery eyes, hair-like ridges and fangs; Ibn Kathīr stresses immediate change. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(وهب); IBNKATHIR-FULL; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); YAHYA-SALLAM]

## 27:10/q14 How does 'kaʾannahā jānn' function grammatically?  [كَأَنَّهَا جَآنٌّ · grammar]
- 27:10/q14/p1 It is a circumstantial clause, either second from the object or nested under 'tahtazzu'. — Al-Naḥḥās identifies a ḥāl; al-Nasafī attaches it to the pronoun in tahtazzu; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī give both parses. [4 notes: NAHHAS; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 27:10/q15 What does 'wallā mudbiran' describe?  [وَلَّىٰ مُدْبِرًۭا · interpretation]
- 27:10/q15/p1 Moses turned his back and fled from the serpent. — Yaḥyā says in fright; al-Ṭabarī and al-Ṭabrisī describe retreat, and Kashf al-asrār says he put the snake behind him. Al-Itqān says mudbiran adds meaning, since merely turning need not be flight, citing the command to turn toward the Sacred Mosque. [15 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ITQAN]
- 27:10/q15/p2 Inwardly, Moses turned toward God and away from the self. — Al-Qāshānī takes the serpent as the sanctified self. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 27:10/q16 What is the grammatical role of 'mudbiran'?  [مُدْبِرًۭا · grammar]
- 27:10/q16/p1 It is a circumstantial accusative. — Al-Naḥḥās marks it as another ḥāl. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 27:10/q17 Why did Moses flee?  [وَلَّىٰ مُدْبِرًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Natural fright, a feared divine threat, or initial unfamiliarity.
- 27:10/q17/p1 He naturally feared harm from the terrifying serpent. — Al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān stress normal human reaction without advance reassurance. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says his only staff had turned frightening, leaving him unable to repel danger; flight was not blameworthy. Ibn Kathīr stresses its size and speed. [24 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(وهب); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 27:10/q17/p2 He feared that God might have directed the portent against him. — Al-Qushayrī says God could punish as He wished; al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Zamakhsharī infer this from reassurance. Al-Bursawī specifies fear of death. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 27:10/q17/p3 The unfamiliar first prophetic experience frightened him until God trained him. — Iṣlāḥī says God gradually made such experiences familiar; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says taking the serpent became habitual. Ibn Zayd says he turned only after restoration to a staff. Wahb reports that Moses ran far, stopped in shame on remembering God, and was called back. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد,وهب)]

## 27:10/q18 What does 'lam yuʿaqqib' mean?  [وَلَمْ يُعَقِّبْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether it describes return, looking, lingering, or a further step.
- 27:10/q18/p1 He did not return or retrace his steps. — Mujāhid supplies the common gloss; al-Ṭabarī derives it from returning to the start. Al-Zajjāj cites Labīd and a fighter's renewed charge; al-Wāḥidī cites Shammar for every returner as muʿaqqib. Some derive it from the heel; al-Muḥkam and al-Qazzāz confirm return after departure. Several also add that he never looked back. [37 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(أهل النظر); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد وأهل اللغة وشمر,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(عمر); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة والزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(القزاز,المحكم); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI]
- 27:10/q18/p2 He did not look back or turn his head. — Qatāda repeatedly gives this gloss. Al-Wāḥidī calls it a gloss rather than full exegesis; al-Qāmūs gives looking back as taʿqīb. Study Quran's variant says he did not glance sideways, emphasizing no hesitation. [25 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); NAHHAS(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL(القاموس); DURR-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 27:10/q18/p3 He did not stop, wait or linger. — Al-Suddī, Ibn ʿAbbās, Sufyān and others give these variants; al-Wāḥidī treats such glosses as partial. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,سفيان); TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي); BIQAI-FULL]
- 27:10/q18/p4 He stayed put rather than taking another step. — Al-Māwardī derives it from walking's next heel movement following the first step. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 27:10/q19 How does 'wa-lam yuʿaqqib' attach grammatically?  [وَلَمْ يُعَقِّبْ · grammar]
- 27:10/q19/p1 It coordinates with 'wallā' or is another circumstantial clause. — Al-Samīn gives both alternatives. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 27:10/q20 Why does the weak ending disappear in 'lam yakhaf'?  [يَخَافُ · grammar]
- 27:10/q20/p1 It is weak-letter loss distinct from deletion before a following consonant. — Sībawayh contrasts it with dropping final alif, yā after kasra and wāw after ḍamma in ḥublā, maʿzā, yarmī, yaqḍī, yaghzū and yadʿū. Duals and akhshaw/ramaū retain endings to avoid number or gender ambiguity; opening yā could mimic accusative. [7 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 27:10/q21 What does 'ladayya' mean?  [لَدَىَّ · meaning]
turns on: Presence generally or the particular revelatory setting.
- 27:10/q21/p1 With Me or in My presence. — Qatāda and al-Zajjāj give ʿindī. Al-Khafājī says 'at My meeting' and Ibn al-Shaykh 'under My judgment'; Sāmarrāʾī stresses nearness. [7 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 27:10/q21/p2 At the extraordinary place and time of revelation. — Al-Biqāʿī specifies this setting; al-Māwardī and others restrict the assurance because messengers fear God at other times. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(بعض المفسرين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 27:10/q22 What is Moses told not to fear?  [يَٰمُوسَىٰ لَا تَخَفْ · interpretation]
turns on: The immediate snake, all creatures, all harm, or the earlier killing.
- 27:10/q22/p1 The snake and its harm. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī identify the immediate object; al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī say prophetic security from punishment removes reason to fear the snake. Ibn Kathīr ties it to the coming commission. [5 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 27:10/q22/p2 Any creature besides God. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Bursawī add trust in God; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Nasafī say chosen messengers fear none but Him. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 27:10/q22/p3 Anything harmful in God's presence. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 28:31 and divine proximity as safety. Al-Ālūsī says this may be reassurance rather than a literal ban, or may forbid the suspicion causing fear. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 27:10/q22/p4 Also his lingering fear over killing the Egyptian. — Ibn Qutayba says the serpent fear comes first, then repentance removes fear over the killing; al-Bursawī sees the allusion too. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; BURSEVI]

## 27:10/q23 When and why do messengers not fear in God's presence?  [إِنِّى لَا يَخَافُ لَدَىَّ ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · theology]
turns on: Which circumstance ladayya restricts and which fear is excluded.
- 27:10/q23/p1 While receiving revelation, though they fear God at other times. — Al-Māwardī, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı restrict the time; absorption in divine speech excludes other fear. Al-Ālūsī cites 35:28. Sāmarrāʾī cites the Prophet's declaration of being most God-fearing. [14 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(بعض المفسرين); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 27:10/q23/p2 When commanded to display a miracle. — Al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī transmit this narrower occasion, allowing fear otherwise. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 27:10/q23/p3 Because God grants His chosen messengers safety from punishment, while reverence remains. — Al-Ḥasan says secure in both lives as God's friends; al-Baghawī cites the Prophet's greater fear of God; al-Ṭabrisī connects safety to avoiding grave wrong and neglected duty; al-Biqāʿī says only a wrongdoer fears a just ruler. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees divinely taught security rather than innate immunity. [14 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 27:10/q23/p4 They do not fear a bad end with God. — Al-Bayḍāwī gives the alternative; al-Ālūsī says it means the hereafter, since some messengers were killed. A marginal Ashʿarī view says fear would imply distrust of assurance. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الأشعري)]

## 27:10/q24 Can prophets fear a bad final outcome despite assurance?  [لَا يَخَافُ لَدَىَّ ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether promise forecloses hidden conditions and rational possibility of sin.
- 27:10/q24/p1 Yes, divine devising or an undisclosed condition can still be feared. — Al-Ālūsī cites the Prophet's prayer about hearts, 7:99, fearful promised Companions, and protected angels; Gabriel and Michael reportedly feared Iblīs's fate. He concludes sin is legally barred but rationally possible, and God owes no act. Al-Ālūsī allows God to create certain knowledge of safety but sees no evidence that He grants it in this life. [7 notes: ALUSI-FULL(جبريل وميكائيل,عائشة); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 27:10/q24/p2 No, God has secured them from a bad end. — A marginal Ashʿarī reading says such fear would imply distrust of God; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī report the interpretation. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الأشعري)]

## 27:10/q25 How is prophetic infallibility understood here?  [ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether immunity is intrinsic, contingent, or choice preserving.
- 27:10/q25/p1 God does not create sin in prophets, though He could. — Al-Ālūsī quotes Ashʿarī doctrine in al-Mawāqif: God is not obligated to withhold sin. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الأشاعرة)]
- 27:10/q25/p2 Knowledge creates a restraining disposition that can cease. — The philosophers say knowledge of sin's harms and obedience's merits, strengthened by revelation, forms the disposition. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الحكماء)]
- 27:10/q25/p3 An innate property makes sin impossible. — Some infer security from this; al-Ālūsī says the argument fails. Al-Jurjānī says compelled abstention merits no praise and invokes prophetic humanity. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قوم); ALUSI-FULL(الشريف الجرجاني)-]
- 27:10/q25/p4 A created restraint preserves choice; impossibility is legal, not rational. — Ibn al-Humām describes lack of power with a restraint that does not compel; al-Ālūsī relates Māturīdī teaching and Ibn Ḥajar's legal impossibility. Al-Jurjānī cites 18:110 and reward for choice. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن الهمام,ابن حجر,الشريف الجرجاني); ALUSI-FULL+]

## 27:10/q26 How is the exception after 'messengers' construed?  [ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the wrongdoer is a messenger, a separate person, or part of implied clauses.
- 27:10/q26/p1 A disconnected exception about someone other than the messengers. — Abū Ḥayyān prefers this because prophets are protected; al-Qurṭubī reports a sentence break and al-Burhān says it is the majority reading. Ibn Qutayba relates the next verse to Moses' former killing. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 27:10/q26/p2 A connected exception through implied clauses: others fear, except a repentant wrongdoer. — Al-Farrāʾ supplies omitted others. Ibn Qutayba rejects it because nothing expressed cues the ellipsis; al-Naḥḥās calls it impossible and gives a reductio about 'I strike people except Zayd'. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النحاس)-]
- 27:10/q26/p3 A connected exception allowing a minor prophetic lapse. — Reports from al-Ḥasan, Muqātil, Ibn Jurayj and al-Ḍaḥḥāk imply this. Al-Zamakhsharī lists Adam, Jonah, David, Solomon, Joseph's brothers and Moses' Copt killing, citing Moses' confession in 28:16. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports protection from grave and vile minor sins, with disagreement on others. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الحسن، ومقاتل، وابن جريج، والضحاك,الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 27:10/q26/p4 The particle means 'and' or 'and neither'. — A group gives conjunctive illā; Abū Ḥayyān objects that conjunction includes while exception excludes. Asad renders 'and neither' and refers to 4:29. [2 notes: BURHAN; ASAD-NOTES; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فرقة)-]

## 27:10/q27 What happens to a wrongdoer who reforms?  [ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether the clause addresses ordinary wrongdoers or prophets.
- 27:10/q27/p1 Repentance and good action bring forgiveness and safety. — Al-Wāḥidī says God forgives the repentant; Qatāda and Maymūn say there is no safety before repentance; Mujāhid describes replacing evil with good. Al-Ḥasan relates Moses' repentance after the Egyptian killing. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DURR-FULL(زيد بن أسلم,قتادة,مجاهد,ميمون)]
- 27:10/q27/p2 Prophets may commit minor lapses outside delivery but need not persist. — Al-Qushayrī holds this; those denying prophetic sin place Moses' fear before prophethood. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says major and vile minor sins are excluded, other cases disputed. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري)]

## 27:10/q28 What inward lesson is drawn from the staff and serpent?  [وَأَلْقِ عَصَاكَ تَهْتَزُّ كَأَنَّهَا جَآنٌّ · inward]
turns on: The symbol is the disciplined self, worldly support, or divine majesty.
- 27:10/q28/p1 The staff is the sanctified self released under God's command. — Al-Qāshānī sees shaking as the self's renewed life after discipline; independent action becomes a veil and trial, while divine command and love do not. Messengers subsist after annihilation. [6 notes: QASHANI]
- 27:10/q28/p2 The staff is any support besides God, which appears as a serpent. — Al-Bursawī cites Najmī taʾwīl and a Mathnawī couplet rejecting reliance on anything but grace; another couplet says fantasies become visible at resurrection. [4 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,المثنوي)]
- 27:10/q28/p3 The serpent is divine majesty, witnessed safely by messengers. — ʿArāʾis al-bayān says they know lordship's secrets; Najmī taʾwīl says those fleeing to God from all else are secure. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,عرائس البيان)]

## 27:10/q29 How did the staff sign establish Moses' commission?  [وَأَلْقِ عَصَاكَ ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · interpretation]
- 27:10/q29/p1 It proved the divine speaker and prepared him to face Pharaoh. — Al-Qushayrī says God's informing could give necessary or inferential knowledge, verified by staff and white hand; al-Qurṭubī says each prophet needs a personal sign. Iṣlāḥī says appointment is unexpected, accepted anxiously after conviction; the staff becomes like a sword against enemies. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 27:10/q30 How does this account relate to other Moses passages?  [* · links]
- 27:10/q30/p1 Parallel verses supply omitted acts and the staff's restoration. — Yaḥyā cites 20:20; al-Wāḥidī cites al-Aʿrāf and al-Shuʿarāʾ; Ibn Zayd describes restoration and later confidence from 28:31 and 20:21. Asad points to 20:17–20 and a tentative symbolism note on 20:20–21. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 27:10/q30/p2 This concise account selects staff and white hand from nine miracles. — Kuryolu cites al-Aʿrāf and al-Isrāʾ for others. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 27:10/q31 Why does al-Qaṣaṣ add a command to come forward?  [وَلَّىٰ مُدْبِرًۭا يَٰمُوسَىٰ لَا تَخَفْ · links]
- 27:10/q31/p1 Its longer narrative and recurring fear require an explicit call back. — Sāmarrāʾī traces fear from Moses' mother through the Egyptian killing, flight, Midian and Pharaoh; he may have fled farther. Al-Qaṣaṣ directly assures Moses while al-Naml is concise. [8 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 27:10/q32 Why say messengers do not fear instead of directly saying Moses is safe?  [إِنِّى لَا يَخَافُ لَدَىَّ ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · rhetoric]
- 27:10/q32/p1 It implicitly identifies Moses as a messenger and honors him with closeness. — Sāmarrāʾī contrasts al-Qaṣaṣ's direct assurance amid a stronger fear setting; ladayya and the direct address and command suggest proximity. [7 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 27:10/q33 Why include the reforming wrongdoer exception here but omit it in al-Qaṣaṣ?  [ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · links]
- 27:10/q33/p1 It suits the Queen of Sheba and would disrupt the direct assurance to Moses. — Sāmarrāʾī cites her confession and submission in 27:44. He notes Moses confessed wrong in 28:16 but distinguishes his Egyptian killing from her change from sun worship. [6 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 27:10/q34 How does this episode fit the wider sūra?  [* · links]
- 27:10/q34/p1 It begins Moses' commission after mental and emotional preparation. — Iṣlāḥī relates the earlier verses to preparation; Study Quran places the fire encounter before the mission to free Israel. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 27:10/q34/p2 Its fear at the staff echoes terror at the sūra's end. — Sāmarrāʾī notes the opening and ending correspondence. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 27:10/q34/p3 The word jān fits private fear while thuʿbān fits Pharaoh's public confrontation. — Sāmarrāʾī says Ṭā Hā uses ḥayya for divine power and omits flight; jān occurs with Moses and the great serpent term before Pharaoh. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
