# 92:8: 13 questions

## 92:8/q01 Who is the miserly, self-sufficient person?  [مَنۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether reported names restrict a general description; al-Alusi objects specifically to Abu Sufyan because of his later Islam.
- 92:8/q01/p1 A general human type; named people are examples rather than limits. — Al-Razi cites 92:4 and 92:14 for the sūra's general scope. Al-Alusi says the wording is general despite reported occasions and treats named people as instances; Kuran Yolu likewise says its message and rulings remain general. Ibn Ashur describes the traits as those of a polytheist; Qutb and Kuran Yolu describe a selfish disbeliever. [8 notes: RAZI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد وابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 92:8/q01/p2 Umayya and Ubayy, the sons of Khalaf. — Ibn Masud names both; al-Durr's report sets Abu Bakr's purchase and freeing of Bilal against their efforts and identifies al-husna as the testimony of faith and the hard path as Hell. [4 notes: MAWARDI(ابن مسعود); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 92:8/q01/p3 Umayya ibn Khalaf. — Ata and Ibn Abbas name Umayya; other reports contrast him with Abu Bakr, including the account of Bilal's purchase for a garment and ten coins. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء); QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI; BAGHAWI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد وابن عباس)]
- 92:8/q01/p4 Abu Sufyan ibn Harb. — Muqatil and al-Kalbi name him, and reports contrast him with Abu Bakr. Al-Alusi questions this identification because Abu Sufyan later became a strong Muslim in Sunni accounts. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل والكلبي); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس عن أبي صالح); ALUSI(ابن عباس)-; ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد وابن عباس)-]
- 92:8/q01/p5 The Ansari owner of a palm tree. — Ata's account says he refused a palm in Paradise when his tree's dates fell into a poor neighbor's yard; Abu al-Dahdah acquired and gave the tree to the neighbor. Ibn Abbas's version says the believer traded forty palms for it; the owner is set against the giver. [7 notes: THALABI(عطاء); BAGHAWI(عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطاء); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس)]
- 92:8/q01/p6 Abu Jahl. — Tustari applies the traits to Abu Jahl; a narration via al-Tusti from Ibn Abbas names him as the occasion. [3 notes: TUSTARI; ALUSI(الطستي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد وابن عباس)]

## 92:8/q02 What does بَخِلَ mean here?  [بَخِلَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether withholding names wealth, a specified religious due, or the broader refusal of obedience and self.
- 92:8/q02/p1 He withholds wealth or charitable spending. — Ibn Abbas and al-Hasan say wealth that will not last; Tabari specifies God's bestowed bounty withheld from commanded uses. Al-Wahidi, Baghawi, and others specify good works or charity. Ibn Atiyya favors wealth because 92:11 asks how wealth helps when he falls. Bint al-Shati cites 92:11 and 92:18 and includes alms, charity, generosity and rightful recipients. Alusi rejects extending it to required actions. [39 notes: TAB(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس، قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI(ابن عباس والحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس، الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); QURTUBI(ابن عباس عن عكرمة); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة عن ابن عباس); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR(عكرمة عن ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس عن عكرمة); ELMALILI; BINTSHATI; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNATIYYA+]
- 92:8/q02/p2 He withholds what is due to God, especially required spending. — Qatada and al-Mawardi say God's due; Tabarsi speaks of seeking sufficiency by withholding it. Biqai broadens it to what God commanded or encouraged; Bint al-Shati includes dues to rightful recipients. Elmalili says avoiding what is due. [18 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس، قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); MAWARDI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI; BINTSHATI]
- 92:8/q02/p3 He withholds zakat after God enriched him. — Ibn Abbas states this narrower application in al-Tabari, Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Durr; al-Durr's same report glosses al-husna as affirmation of monotheism. [6 notes: TAB(ابن عباس); DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 92:8/q02/p4 He withholds obedience, faith, speech, or acts that religion requires. — Tustari specifies Abu Jahl's withheld obedience to God and Messenger. Ibn Atiyya extends miserliness beyond wealth to faith and required speech or acts. Al-Alusi finds withholding required actions unconvincing. [4 notes: TUSTARI; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 92:8/q02/p5 He withholds both himself and his wealth. — Qutb treats both forms of withholding as part of the corrupting rejection of God and guidance. [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]

## 92:8/q03 How does the unexpressed object of بَخِلَ work?  [بَخِلَ · grammar]
- 92:8/q03/p1 The object is omitted because stinginess with wealth is intended. — Ibn Ashur explicitly supplies wealth as the omitted object. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 92:8/q04 Why does the passage place withholding at the head of this list?  [بَخِلَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the ordering explains the traits' causal role or aims to exhort generosity.
- 92:8/q04/p1 Giving and withholding ground the later traits of faith and piety or denial and self-sufficiency. — Al-Irshad, quoted by al-Alusi, says they open their clauses despite being lower in rank because each is foundational to what follows. [2 notes: ALUSI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL(الإرشاد)]
- 92:8/q04/p2 Naming withholding beside self-sufficiency urges Muslims to give. — Ibn Ashur says giving and miserliness are included within piety and self-sufficiency yet singled out to encourage giving. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 92:8/q05 What does وَٱسْتَغْنَىٰ mean?  [وَٱسْتَغْنَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the implied source of sufficiency is God, His reward, wealth and self, or worldly life; Corpus Coranicum also records a translation choice.
- 92:8/q05/p1 He considers himself independent of God, His mercy, commands, or guidance. — Ibn Abbas and Qatada say independent of his Lord. Tabari says he therefore does not seek obedience or more of God's bounty. Tustari calls Abu Jahl's independence a show; Ibn Atiyya says it claims no need of God or mercy. Al-Kashshaf and Nasafi say he fails to fear God. Ibn Ashur says a persistent unbeliever relies instead on idols and his people. Qutb includes guidance; Study Quran calls the independence an illusion, referring to 96:6–7. [43 notes: TUSTARI; TAB(ابن عباس,قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس، قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,قتادة); MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI(ابن عباس عن عكرمة); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة عن ابن عباس); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; IBNKATHIR(عكرمة عن ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR(ابن عباس,قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس عن عكرمة,قتادة); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; STUDYQURAN+]
- 92:8/q05/p2 He feels no need of God's promised reward and does not seek it. — Al-Nahhas and al-Wahidi make reward the object. Kashshaf says he disdains what is with God and thus fails to fear Him. Thalabi and Baghawi tie this to denial of promised good; Biqai adds vain hopes. [14 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 92:8/q05/p3 He relies on his wealth, possessions, power, or self as sufficient. — Al-Hasan says wealth; Ibn Atiyya pairs wealth with miserliness to intensify blame. Qashani describes love and hoarding of wealth veiling virtue and God. Elmalili adds imagined freedom from future need and protection. Islahi cites Qarun's boast in 28:78 and says all gain is from God. Kuran Yolu includes one's own power. [9 notes: MAWARDI(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QASHANI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس)]
- 92:8/q05/p4 He treats worldly pleasures or life as sufficient instead of afterlife bliss. — Kashshaf and Nasafi set worldly desires against Paradise, the opposite of God-consciousness in 92:5. Baydawi and Alusi agree. Islahi describes the one who makes this life ultimate and loses resolve for afterlife reward; Samarrai contrasts the seekers of this life and the next. [11 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 92:8/q05/p5 He seeks independence from people and promised reward by vain hopes. — Biqai says this leaves him neither helping people nor working for the afterlife. [2 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 92:8/q05/p6 The phrase means self-sufficiency, rather than acting arrogantly. — Corpus Coranicum prefers consistent 'self-sufficient' across three occurrences, including 96:7, over Paret's 'acting arrogantly'. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM+]
- 92:8/q05/p7 It means acting arrogantly. — Corpus Coranicum attributes this rendering to Paret and prefers 'self-sufficient' for consistency. [0 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM-]

## 92:8/q06 What force do the added sīn and tāʾ have in ٱسْتَغْنَىٰ?  [وَٱسْتَغْنَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the augmented form intensifies the verb or expresses seeking.
- 92:8/q06/p1 They intensify the action. — Ibn Ashur compares istajāba, 'answered'. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:8/q06/p2 They mark seeking, so the miser seeks more wealth by withholding it. — Ibn Ashur reports this analysis as an alternative. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 92:8/q07 How do the two sets of traits and fates correspond?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the comparison concerns paired traits within 5–10 or their larger arrangement across the sūra.
- 92:8/q07/p1 They form antitheses: giving/withholding, piety/self-sufficiency, affirmation/denial, and ease/hardship. — Abu Hayyan pairs giving with stinginess and piety with self-sufficiency. Ibn Ashur calls the pattern الجمع مع التقسيم. Corpus Coranicum also notes symmetry, behavior-to-consequence sequences and Neuwirth's cross-rhyme through vowel variation; the divine first-person plural authorizes the promises alongside the oath. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; BINTSHATI]
- 92:8/q07/p2 The final stanza mirrors and reverses the earlier treatment of saved and damned. — Corpus Coranicum says earlier behavior precedes outcome, whereas the final warning and promise precede conduct; the outer blessed sections share ittaqa/atqa. Neuwirth places verse 11 with theological statements in 12–13. [3 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM(Neuwirth)]

## 92:8/q08 What do the contrasting descriptions teach about people's paths?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the paths describe present moral formation, withdrawal of grace, or their final destinations; Abu Hayyan objects to the grace explanation.
- 92:8/q08/p1 Human striving divides into generous and miserly paths with opposed outcomes. — Razi says deeds culminate in reward or punishment. Ibn Ashur reports the two groups as those enabled to ease and hardship or as the two triads in 5 and 8–9; 99:6–8 illustrates even tiny deeds' outcomes. Qutb calls them two ranks and says each receives facilitation. Islahi says 8–10 describe the character and belief ending in Hell. Bint al-Shati places diverse earthly endeavors under the oath of night, day and creation; Corpus Coranicum says virtue and vice receive eschatological outcomes. [12 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR; QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 92:8/q08/p2 The miser's chosen course brings evil deeds and ultimately Hell. — Thalabi and Baghawi say he is turned toward disapproved deeds and punishment, and al-usra may name Hell itself. Qurtubi points to 3:180 for wealth's worldly consequence. Qashani says wealth veils God; Qutb says this rejection brings the greatest corruption. Islahi says God lets worldliness continue smoothly until death and then comes 74:17's steep hardship. [11 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; QASHANI; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:8/q08/p3 God withdraws grace, making obedience especially hard. — Abu Hayyan reports Zamakhshari's argument from the constricted heart in 6:125 but rejects its Mu'tazili insinuation. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-]
- 92:8/q08/p4 The paths are named for their outcomes or are afterlife roads to Paradise and Hell. — Abu Hayyan reports Zamakhshari's alternative explanation of ease and hardship. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري)]

## 92:8/q09 What events or sayings are reported as occasions for these verses?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on the event: the date palm, Bilal's emancipation, or the angels' appeal.
- 92:8/q09/p1 A palm owner's refusal and Abu al-Dahdah's gift occasioned the contrast. — Ata says the owner's dates fell into a neighbor's home; he rejected the Prophet's offer of a palm in Paradise. Abu al-Dahdah bought the tree with his garden and passed it to the neighbor. Ibn Abbas's version says the buyer exchanged forty palms and the poor household was deprived of fallen dates; the giver received a promised palm in Paradise. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(عطاء); BAGHAWI(عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطاء)]
- 92:8/q09/p2 Abu Bakr's buying and freeing Bilal occasioned the contrast with Umayya and Ubayy. — Baghawi's anonymous report specifies a garment and ten coins; Ibn Masud's report says Abu Bakr did it for God and contrasts the brothers' striving. [2 notes: BAGHAWI(قيل); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 92:8/q09/p3 Two angels' daily prayer over spenders and hoarders occasioned the verses. — Reports vary between sunset and sunrise. Abu al-Darda's version says the two angels address all but humans and jinn and ask God to replace the spender's gift and destroy the hoarder's wealth. Al-Mawardi gives a chain via Qatada and Khulayd without grading. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI(أبو الدرداء عن النبي); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو الدرداء); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 92:8/q10 What does the Prophet's decree report say about acting?  [* · theology]
- 92:8/q10/p1 Destinations and deeds are decreed, yet people must act, for each is facilitated toward the work or path appointed. — Ali's funeral report says each place in Paradise or Hell is written. Other wordings ask whether work is new or settled, and the Prophet insists on action. Al-Tirmidhi's version is graded hasan sahih by Qurtubi. The report of two young men says they resolved to strive; reports via al-Sulami invoke 54:49. Ibn Hibban's Shu'ba notes Mansur corroborating Sulayman al-A'mash. Jabir's version names Suraqa; al-Nazzal's cites an Arab questioner. Burhan says the Prophet cited the verses among Paradise descriptions alongside 32:17 and 56:30. [25 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي ﷺ عن النزال بن سبرة,النبي ﷺ عن بشير بن كعب,النبي ﷺ عن جابر بن عبد الله,النبي ﷺ عن سعد بن عبيدة وأبي عبد الرحمن السلمي,النبي ﷺ عن علي); IBNHIBBAN(شعبة,علي بن أبي طالب); QURTUBI-FULL(الغلامان,علي عن النبي,غلامان شابان عن النبي); DURR(أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي,علي بن أبي طالب); DURR-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي بن أبي طالب عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet); BURHAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 92:8/q11 How does the verse connect to other passages and the sūra's theme?  [* · links]
turns on: Which textual relation is being traced: the preceding sūra, parallel language, wealth and fate, the oath, or coordination.
- 92:8/q11/p1 It expands the prior sūra's success and failure into traits, warning, and fate. — Abu Hayyan connects 91:9–10 to this sūra's traits and its warning of Fire. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 92:8/q11/p2 Its claim of self-sufficiency echoes other Qur'anic uses and contrasts with piety. — Asad and Study Quran cite 96:6–7; Corpus Coranicum calls istaghna the antonym of ittaqa and compares 96:7 and 80:5, 80:9. Alusi says al-husna follows interpretations of 92:6. [4 notes: ASAD-NOTES; CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI]
- 92:8/q11/p3 The fate of the miser and spender is clarified by other verses about wealth, ease, and divine ownership. — Qurtubi refers to 3:180 for wealth's consequence; Corpus Coranicum compares 94:5–6 and 87:8 with ease and hardship. Samarrai says the emphatic lam of God's ownership fits this wealth-centered sūra, unlike 53:25's context without wealth. Ibn Kathir recalls the maxim that a good deed breeds good and a sin breeds sin. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; QURTUBI; CORPUSCORANICUM; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNKATHIR(بعض السلف)]
- 92:8/q11/p4 The sūra's oath frames divergent lives and afterlife fates. — Bint al-Shati says night, daylight, and creation set up the denier's fire and the pious giver's satisfaction. Corpus Coranicum notes the divine plural authenticates the paired promises with the oath. [2 notes: BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 92:8/q11/p5 The surrounding clauses coordinate as one despite an omitted conjunction. — Burhan cites the lack of waw in coordinated clauses here and compares 3:118 and 9:92. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 92:8/q12 How does this verse turn from the giver to the miser?  [* · links]
- 92:8/q12/p1 The passage turns from the one who purifies himself and receives reward to the one who corrupts himself and faces a wretched fate. — Biqai links the transition to the preceding promise in 92:7. [1 notes: BIQAI]

## 92:8/q13 What does the majestic plural signal in the two paths?  [* · rhetoric]
- 92:8/q13/p1 It stresses how unlikely either facilitated course is. — Biqai says good is difficult because surrounded by aversions, whereas reason and primordial nature warn against evil. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
