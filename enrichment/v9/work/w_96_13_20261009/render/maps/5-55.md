# 5:55: 28 questions

## 5:55/q01 What does innamā restrict?  [إِنَّمَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether restriction is exhaustive, and whether its object is support or governing authority.
- 5:55/q01/p1 It confines the stated guardianship to God, the Messenger, and the described believers. — Al-Zamakhsharī calls the restriction obligatory; al-Ṭabrisī gives the dirham, Ḥātim, and al-Aʿshā examples; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the affirmative report acts as a command to maintain allegiance; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers qasr al-ifrād against imagined additional guardians, while allowing qasr al-qalb. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 5:55/q01/p2 The particle need not exclude every other description or restrict walī to authority. — Al-Rāzī cites 10:24 and 47:36, where the descriptions of worldly life are not exhaustive; he says even restricted named allies can be a particular supporting group. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+; TABRISI-; RAZI-FULL(الشيعة)-]

## 5:55/q02 How is the main sentence constructed?  [إِنَّمَا وَلِيُّكُمُ ٱللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُۥ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: How the singular predicate relates grammatically to the coordinated names.
- 5:55/q02/p1 The sentence is nominal, with God as predicate and Messenger and believers coordinated with Him. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Samīn give this parse; al-Burhān calls coordination with God a mode of magnification, comparing 3:18 and 4:69; Qāshānī sees an inward unity then outward distinction, comparing 3:18. [4 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; QASHANI]
- 5:55/q02/p2 The later names may be understood through an implicit 'likewise' or a plural predicate. — Al-Ālūsī says either parse shows the Messenger and believers become allies by God's appointment. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 5:55/q03 What does walī mean here?  [وَلِيُّكُمُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether walī denotes mutual support or delegated authority, and whether one use can carry multiple senses.
- 5:55/q03/p1 A religious ally, friend, protector, or helper who gives support and mutual loyalty. — Al-Ṭabarī excludes Jewish and Christian allies; al-Rāzī invokes 5:51, 5:54, and 5:57, contemporaneous support and affection, and the rule against combining homonymous senses; al-Ālūsī says the verse consoles believers and its opponents were never imams; 9:71 confirms mutual allegiance. Al-Rāghib derives the senses from proximity; al-Dāmghānī calls it religious allegiance. [50 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(أبو جعفر); WAHIDI-BASIT; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب,جماعة من مفسري القوم); STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭabarī); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; ALUSI-FULL+; NAHHAS(أبو جعفر)+; RAZI-FULL+; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)+; TABRISI-; TABATABAI-]
- 5:55/q03/p2 One entitled to manage affairs and owed obedience, with divine authority delegated to the Messenger and the identified believer. — Al-Mubarrad defines the root as awlā; al-Kumayt uses walī al-amr for a successor. Al-Ṭabrisī cites marriage guardians, blood claimants, rulers, and heirs and says ordinary mutual friendship cannot be exclusive under 9:71. The Shīʿī argument cites the marriage-guardian usage and the shared singular walī; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī derives wilāya from proximity, distinguishes ontological and legislative authority, and cites 33:6, 4:59, and 33:36 for the Prophet's delegated governance. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says believers support God or the Messenger in the Qurʾan (61:14, 47:7, 3:81, 7:157, 59:8, 8:72), while the Prophet shares religious distinction with them and is never called their supporter in that sense. [39 notes: TUSTARI; TABRISI(الكميت,المبرد); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; RAZI-FULL(الشيعة,القوم); BAYDAWI-FULL(الشيعة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL(الشيعة,صاحب إظهار الحق); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Shiites); TABRISI+; RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)-]
- 5:55/q03/p3 Walī has a broad family of senses rooted in nearness, including protection, affection, aid, and management. — Al-Rāghib traces walāʾ and tawālī from spatial succession to kinship, faith, affection, support and taking charge; al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn al-Jawzī enumerate concrete holders of protective or managing rights; Elmalılı uses guardians, authorities, friends and helpers together. Al-Rāghib observes tawallā with a direct object means turning toward, but with ʿan means turning away (2:144); al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī applies the idiom to battle and course. [14 notes: TABRISI(المبرد); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(الراغب); STUDYQURAN]
- 5:55/q03/p4 The verse can convey support and authority together. — The reported Shīʿī reasoning allows the senses to coexist; Elmalılı lists both, while al-Rāzī says a single homonymous expression cannot carry both at once. [2 notes: ELMALILI; RAZI-FULL(الشيعة)+; RAZI-FULL-]

## 5:55/q04 Why is walī singular for several guardians?  [وَلِيُّكُمُ ٱللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُۥ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the singular marks divine priority or follows generic and morphological usage.
- 5:55/q04/p1 God is the original guardian; the Messenger and believers share that role derivatively. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Ālūsī, al-Biqāʿī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī say the plural would hide the priority; al-Ālūsī offers an implied 'likewise' or plural predicate as syntactic explanations and stresses that conjunction does not equate rank. [19 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(المفسرون); STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI]
- 5:55/q04/p2 Walī is a generic noun or a faʿīl form usable for singular or plural. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān call it generic; al-Samīn notes faʿīl's invariant form and cites 11:89 by analogy. Al-Ālūsī says grammatical possibility alone does not explain the rhetorical choice. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 5:55/q05 What variant wording is reported for walīyyukum?  [وَلِيُّكُمُ · readings]
- 5:55/q05/p1 A wording attributed to Ibn Masʿūd or ʿAbd Allāh has mawlākum instead. — Al-Kashshāf, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān record it; al-Samīn treats it as an explanatory gloss rather than a reading. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(عبد الله); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود); RAZI-FULL(عبد الله); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله); SAMIN-DURR(ابن مسعود)]

## 5:55/q06 Does the verse establish ʿAlī's imamate or succession?  [وَلِيُّكُمُ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · theology]
turns on: The sense of walī, scope of innamā, referent of the plural, and whether succession timing is supplied.
- 5:55/q06/p1 It teaches loyalty and support, and does not prove ʿAlī's immediate exclusive succession. — Al-Rāzī argues the adjacent bans concern support, authority did not belong to ʿAlī during the Prophet's life, the plural is literal, 5:54 supports Abū Bakr, and ʿAlī did not cite this verse at the council. Al-Ālūsī adds that exclusivity would exclude later Imams and 9:71 makes allegiance general; Ibn Kathīr rejects report chains and the Study Quran notes Sunni resistance to the legitimist inference. Al-Rāzī's claim that ʿAlī never cited the verse at the council conflicts with the council appeal reported by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī. [56 notes: NAHHAS(أبو جعفر); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(أهل السنة,الشيخ إبراهيم الكردى,الطبرسى,المرتضى وابن المطهر,بعض منا أهل السنة,محمد الباقر); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية,ابن كثير); STUDYQURAN(Sunni authors,al-Rāzī); ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; TABRISI-; TABATABAI(المعترضون)-]
- 5:55/q06/p2 Yes: it appoints ʿAlī to governing or spiritual authority and succession after the Prophet. — Al-Ṭabrisī argues exclusive walī means obligatory obedience and the ring description identifies ʿAlī; Shīʿī arguments say general love would include all believers and 9:71, so restriction selects authority. The Study Quran describes spiritual authority; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī links 5:55 with 4:59, Ghadīr, Thaqalayn, and reports of ʿAlī's challenge at the council and to Abū Bakr. In one reported appeal Abū Bakr himself answers ʿAlī that the ring verse's joined authority belongs to ʿAlī; another report says the council candidates agreed. Other reports say God required love of ʿAlī, Abū Dharr, Salmān and Miqdād, only three knew the authority matter, and the Imams form one household with a unified affair. [36 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الشيعة,القوم); BAYDAWI-FULL(الشيعة); ALUSI-FULL(الشيعة,صاحب إظهار الحق); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر,أبو جعفر عليه السلام,أبو ذر,أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,أحدهما عليهما السلام,حسان بن ثابت,علي بن أبي طالب,علي بن محمد الهادي,عمار بن ياسر,عمرو بن العاص); STUDYQURAN(Shiites); TABATABAI+; TABRISI+; RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 5:55/q06/p3 Even if it implies ʿAlī's imamate, it does not date it and can apply after the first three caliphs. — Al-Rāzī and Ibrāhīm al-Kurdī say imamate is deputyship after the Prophet; a claim of immediate succession needs a timing premise not in the verse. The Sunni answer accepts ʿAlī's authority during his own imamate. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أهل السنة); ALUSI-FULL(الشيخ إبراهيم الكردى)+; RAZI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(الشيعة)-]
- 5:55/q06/p4 The exclusive authority applies to ʿAlī's own period as Imam. — The reported Shīʿī answer says restriction in that time need not disqualify Ḥasan, Ḥusayn, or later Imams; the Sunni response says this admits the verse alone does not establish authority during prior caliphs. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الشيعة)+; ALUSI-FULL(أهل السنة)-]

## 5:55/q07 What is reported about proclaiming the authority indicated here?  [وَلِيُّكُمُ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · reports]
turns on: Whether later proclamation and succession questions are linked to this verse.
- 5:55/q07/p1 The Prophet declared ʿAlī mawla after his ring gift or named him minister and deputy. — ʿAmmār's reports attach the mawla declaration to the gift; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites declarations of ʿAlī as successor, debt-settler, and deputy like Aaron, except prophethood. Abū Dharr's report parallels Moses' prayer. [9 notes: DURR-FULL(عمار بن ياسر); TABATABAI(أبو ذر,عمار بن ياسر); THALABI(أبو ذر); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو ذر)]
- 5:55/q07/p2 The Prophet later proclaimed ʿAlī's wilāya at Ghadīr after fearing rejection. — Al-Bāqir says 5:67 ordered proclamation and 5:3 marked completion of religion; another Imam says Quraysh's possible rejection distressed the Prophet. The report calls wilāya the final obligation. [2 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر,أحدهما عليهما السلام)]
- 5:55/q07/p3 Reports of requested succession do not establish that this verse resolved a known dispute. — Al-Ālūsī cites Ḥudhayfa's report of a request for an appointee and ʿAlī's report naming Abū Bakr and ʿUmar; he says chronology, connection to revelation, and later dispute are unproved; a solitary report cannot supply the needed uncertainty if it cannot prove imamate. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL(حذيفة,على)]

## 5:55/q09 How can plural believer wording relate to a single person?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ ٱلَّذِينَ يُقِيمُونَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether plural language is a literal group, honorific singular, or general description instantiated by one person.
- 5:55/q09/p1 The plural can honor the single actor or invite others to imitate the deed. — Al-Ṭabrisī calls the usage common magnification; al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī and al-Bayḍāwī say it makes charity a model; al-Ālūsī cites Abraham called a nation in 16:120. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes a collective rule with one valid instance from calling one person a plural, cites 3:61 and single-person occasions in 60:1, 63:8, 2:215, 2:274. [13 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 5:55/q09/p2 The literal plural denotes the community and cannot establish a sole referent. — Al-Rāzī counts seven plural forms and says a singular application is only figurative; al-Naḥḥās, al-Qurṭubī and Elmalılı invoke the plural's general reach; objectors say Arabic does not permit the sole-person reading. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(المرتضى وابن المطهر,محمد الباقر); TABATABAI(المعترضون); ELMALILI+; NAHHAS(أبو جعفر)+; TABATABAI-]

## 5:55/q10 What is reported about ʿAlī's merits and the verse?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · reports]
- 5:55/q10/p1 Reports praise ʿAlī as specially honored by this revelation and connect him to other Qurʾanic merits. — Aḥmad says no Companion has as many reports of merit. Al-Bāqir identifies ʿAlī as the witness and one with knowledge of the Book (11:17, 13:43); ʿAmr cites 76:7, 33:23 and 42:23. The Prophet's reported prayer and Ḥassān's poem celebrate the ring gift. Anas says the Prophet assured ʿAlī Paradise and release from sin after the ring gift. [8 notes: THALABI(أبو ذر,أحمد بن حنبل); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام,أبو ذر,أنس بن مالك,حسان بن ثابت,عمرو بن العاص)]

## 5:55/q12 Did the revelation precede or follow the ring gift?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَهُمْ رَٰكِعُونَ · reports]
turns on: The sequence of revelation and the Prophet's inquiry at the mosque.
- 5:55/q12/p1 It came after ʿAlī gave the ring, or Gabriel brought it as the Prophet prayed for him. — The common ring accounts place the revelation after the gift; Abū Dharr's version adds the Prophet's Moses-like prayer before Gabriel came. ## 5:55/q17 Why are the believers described by prayer and almsgiving?  [ٱلَّذِينَ يُقِيمُونَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَيُؤْتُونَ ٱلزَّكَوٰةَ · interpretation]

## 5:55/q25 What occasion is reported for the revelation?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which historical episode explains the address, and whether the ring gift is its specific occasion.
- 5:55/q25/p1 ʿAlī gave a ring or charity to a petitioner while bowing, and the verse was revealed. — The accounts name a mosque petitioner and often the Prophet asking who gave the ring. ## 5:55/q26 How does this verse connect to the adjacent alliance passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether adjacency fixes the sense as support or the verse's distinct occasion permits authority.
- 5:55/q26/p1 It continues the ban on Jewish and Christian alliances by naming the proper allies. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Rāzī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, al-Ālūsī, al-Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr link it to 5:51; Ibn ʿĀshūr says 5:54 is parenthetical and the sentence resumes 5:51, praising opposite loyalty. The Study Quran reads 5:51–57 as political boundaries. [21 notes: TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(جماعة من مفسري القوم); STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭabarī); IBNASHUR-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 5:55/q26/p2 The next verse's victory promise fits allegiance to God and His party. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Thaʿlabī connect taking the named guardians to God's victorious party in 5:56; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says victory can fit authority, affection, or support and cites 58:21 and 37:171–73. [5 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(أبو جعفر); TABRISI(ابن عباس عن أبي صالح); TABATABAI]
- 5:55/q26/p3 The passage discourages seeking honor from outside the faithful community. — Elmalılı says seek God's guardianship for honor and independence; the Study Quran frames 5:51–57 as community boundaries and notes Islamic law allows treaties with Jewish and Christian political entities. [3 notes: ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 5:55/q26/p4 The verse has its own occasion and need not share the surrounding passage's support theme. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues sūra verses came at different times, notes the shift from criticizing hypocrites to directly addressing forbidden groups, and says 5:51 concerns social assimilation rather than a mere pact; adjacent placement does not prove a common occasion. [5 notes: TABATABAI(المعترضون); TABATABAI+; RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 5:55/q27 How is the verse read inwardly?  [* · inward]
turns on: How ritual and alliance language is transposed into spiritual states.
- 5:55/q27/p1 Guardianship is divine care, and spiritual allegiance requires opposition to the lower self and loyalty to believers. — Al-Tustarī defines God's guardianship as His choice of those in His care; Bursawī's cited Taʾwīlāt says loyalty to God opposes all besides Him (26:77), loyalty to the Messenger resists desire and loves him above self, family and wealth, and loyalty to believers means fraternity (49:10). [6 notes: TUSTARI; BURSEVI]
- 5:55/q27/p2 Prayer is witnessing, zakāt subsistence, and bowing the attribution of all perfections to God. — Qāshānī interprets the sequence through divine presence, the remnant's subsistence, humility and the Commander's profession of unity after annihilation. [5 notes: QASHANI]
- 5:55/q27/p3 A prefatory Persian couplet counsels accepting what has been given and setting aside a frown. — Bursawī says neither party had been granted choice; the couplet frames his reflection on divine care. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
