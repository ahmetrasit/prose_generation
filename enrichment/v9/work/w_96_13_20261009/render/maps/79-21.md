# 79:21: 7 questions

## 79:21/q01 How does the fāʾ connect the denial to what preceded it?  [فَكَذَّبَ · grammar]
- 79:21/q01/p1 Pharaoh's denial followed Moses' invitation and the displayed sign, with the sequence emphasizing his swift rejection after proof. — Naḥḥās says fāʾ marks the second action as following the first, whereas wāw basically conjoins or permits simultaneity. Rāzī says this rejection came after Moses' invitation and miracle, unlike Pharaoh's earlier rejection (79:20). Bayḍāwī and Biqāʿī place it after the sign established the matter; Biqāʿī stresses that the sight should have prompted belief. Būrsawī says Pharaoh called it magic immediately, without further inspection, reflection, or consulting reason and conscience, and that the conjunction links the sign's effect to culpable denial. Ibn ʿĀshūr reads the quick sequence as stubborn refusal to pause over the proof. [7 notes: NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 79:21/q02 Whom or what did Pharaoh deny?  [كَذَّبَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the denial is directed at Moses and his sign or at God's lordship.
- 79:21/q02/p1 He denied Moses' truth and mission and the divine or evidentiary force of his sign, commonly dismissing it as magic. — Muqātil, Baghawī and Ṭabrisī specify denial that the sign or two signs came from God; Muqātil calls it magic. Wāḥidī and Bayḍāwī name Moses; Ṭabrisī adds rejection of his prophethood, Ibn al-Jawzī of the message from God, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī of his mission. Nasafī names Moses and the great sign, calling Moses a sorcerer and the sign sorcery; Qāshānī names the sign. Abū Ḥayyān, Būrsawī, Ālūsī, Elmalılı and Iṣlāḥī also mention the charge of magic or calling Moses a liar. Rāzī says Pharaoh denied that the miracle proved Moses truthful: possible objections to miracles include imitation, a nondivine agent, no divine intention to authenticate, and no necessary entailment of a claimant's truth; the subsequent action suggests Pharaoh thought this sign could be matched (79:23; 26:53). Biqāʿī stresses denial despite established proof. Study Quran says even the sorcerers recognized Moses' superiority and the staff and white hand as true signs, yet Pharaoh dismissed them as sorcery (7:111–126). [19 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 79:21/q02/p2 He denied God's lordship, even daring to deny the Lord of the worlds. — Būrsawī describes extreme rebellion despite knowledge of the truth, reaching denial of the Lord of the worlds. Ālūsī likewise says Pharaoh dared to deny that Lord. Elmalılı cites his taunt, ‘Who is the Lord of the worlds?’ (26:23), as he spurned purification. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 79:21/q03 What did Pharaoh's disobedience involve, and whom did it oppose?  [عَصَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: The implicit object and content of ‘disobeyed’: God's demand for faith and worship, Moses' instruction, or the release of Israel.
- 79:21/q03/p1 He defied God by refusing faith, worship, purification, and God's command despite the proof, in arrogance and rebellion. — Muqātil makes this refusal of faith; Baghawī adds turning from faith and spreading corruption. Bayḍāwī and Nasafī explicitly name God as the one disobeyed. Abū Ḥayyān says Pharaoh rebelled despite knowing the miracle's truth. Biqāʿī calls it major arrogant refusal joined to denial after proof. Būrsawī calls it extreme rebellion despite knowing the command's truth. Ālūsī says Pharaoh and his people were commanded to worship God and abandon Pharaoh's claimed divinity, beyond merely releasing Israel. Elmalılı adds spurning purification with the taunt of 26:23. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī report disobedience to God as a possible reading; Ālūsī favors God as the object over Moses as the object of both verbs. Būrsawī reports the Moses alternative but finds the God-directed interpretation more condemnatory. [14 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+; BURSEVI+]
- 79:21/q03/p2 He disobeyed Moses, God's prophet, by rejecting or defying what Moses instructed. — Wāḥidī says Pharaoh rejected and disobeyed Moses; Ṭabrisī calls Moses God's prophet, and Ibn al-Jawzī God's messenger. Qāshānī links defiance of Moses to Pharaoh's transgression and self-aggrandizement. Būrsawī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī report this as a possible reading. Ālūsī argues that making God the object of disobedience is more condemnatory than making Moses the object of both verbs. [6 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QASHANI; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 79:21/q03/p3 His disobedience may mean refusing to release the Israelites from enslavement and forced service. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives this as an alternative to rejecting God's command to worship Him alone. Ālūsī says Pharaoh and his people were commanded to worship God and abandon his claimed divinity, not merely to release Israel. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 79:21/q04 What does pairing denial with disobedience emphasize?  [فَكَذَّبَ عَصَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the pairing highlights inner versus outer action, knowingly arrogant rejection after proof, or the force of assigning different objects to the verbs.
- 79:21/q04/p1 The verbs distinguish denial in heart and speech from outward defiance and tyranny. — Rāzī explicitly divides the two verbs this way. Būrsawī describes denial as verbal rejection despite inner certainty that the person deserved belief; denial of someone not entitled to belief would not be disobedience. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 79:21/q04/p2 Together they expose Pharaoh's culpable arrogance after clear proof, even where he knew the truth. — Abū Ḥayyān says he rebelled against God despite knowing the miracle's truth. Biqāʿī joins denial to major arrogant refusal after the message's truth was established. Būrsawī describes immediate rejection without reflection out of arrogance and rebellion, and extreme disobedience despite knowing the truth. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees stubborn haste after the sign. Qāshānī traces his defiance to transgression and self-aggrandizement. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; QASHANI]
- 79:21/q04/p3 Making Moses the object of denial and God the object of disobedience makes the condemnation stronger. — Ālūsī expressly prefers this distribution over making Moses the object of both verbs. Būrsawī reports disobedience of Moses as possible but says the God-directed reading condemns Pharaoh more forcefully. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL+; BURSEVI+]

## 79:21/q05 What claim about human action is drawn from Pharaoh's conduct?  [* · theology]
- 79:21/q05/p1 Human acts originate from the servants themselves. — Jishumī argues this from Pharaoh's own transgression, denial, turning away, and calling out. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 79:21/q06 How does Pharaoh's episode serve the sūra's larger argument?  [* · links]
- 79:21/q06/p1 It contributes to the sūra's demonstration of humanity's final fate within its argument for resurrection and gathering. — Biqāʿī says the sūra establishes resurrection and the gathering and displays humanity's final fate after the preceding sūras' fuller exposition; its oaths mark the matter's decisive climax. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 79:21/q07 What inward lesson is drawn from Pharaoh's rejection?  [* · inward]
- 79:21/q07/p1 Seeing signs without the lights of divine attributes left Pharaoh veiled from love and submission. — Būrsawī reports some spiritual knowers: witnessing divine attributes yields humility, whereas a vision of the essence produces insolence. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعض أهل المعرفة)]
