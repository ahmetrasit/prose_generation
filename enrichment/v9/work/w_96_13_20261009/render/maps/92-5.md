# 92:5: 23 questions

## 92:5/q01 What does fa-ammā do in the argument?  [فَأَمَّا · grammar]
- 92:5/q01/p1 It introduces a conditional division of the preceding statement that human efforts differ. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says tafṣīl separates a shared group by a relevant difference; the account moves eloquently from efforts to their agents. He adduces a poet's comparison with a watchful ibex and 2:177 and 9:19 for describing a quality through its bearer. [6 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 92:5/q02 Who is the giver described here?  [مَنْ أَعْطَىٰ وَٱتَّقَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether the reports identify a particular occasion or the unrestricted relative pronoun is taken generally.
- 92:5/q02/p1 The description applies to everyone who has these traits, including the named examples. — Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses the general force of man; al-Ālūsī says general wording governs despite particular occasions. Rāzī cites the general statements about varied striving and the Fire at 92:4,14. Jishumī says the passage's point is general. Kur'an Yolu applies both message and rulings generally. [8 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد وابن عباس); IBNASHUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 92:5/q02/p2 The giver is Abū Bakr. — Ibn Masʿūd and most commentators identify him; reports describe freeing weak enslaved Muslims or Bilāl, seeking God's reward rather than strong protectors. Ibn Abī Awfā and Ibn ʿAbbās pair him with Abū Sufyān; other reports contrast him with Umayya or Abū Jahl. Al-Ālūsī questions the Abū Sufyān pairing because he later became a strong Muslim; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls substituting him for Umayya an error. [44 notes: FARRA; TUSTARI(أبو بكر الصديق); TAB-FULL(أبو بكر الصديق); MAWARDI(ابن مسعود); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA(عبد الله بن أبي أوفى,مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI(ابن مسعود وعامة المفسرين,عامر بن عبد الله بن الزبير); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود وعامة المفسرين,عامر بن عبد الله بن الزبير); ABUHAYYAN(عبد الله بن أبي أوفى,قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله بن أبي أوفى); BAGHAWI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس,عامر بن عبد الله بن الزبير); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,عامر بن عبد الله بن الزبير,عامر بن عبد الله بن الزبير عن أبيه); ALUSI(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود,الحاكم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو قحافة,ابن زيد وابن عباس,عبد الله بن مسعود); ELMALILI(Hâkim,İbn Cerîr); IBNASHUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); IBNASHUR(بعض المفسرين)-]
- 92:5/q02/p3 The giver is Abū al-Daḥdāḥ. — Accounts tell of his buying a palm whose fruit reached a poor family or orphans, then giving it away; the owner is variously called an Anṣārī or hypocrite. A further Bukhārī report concerns date clusters hung in the mosque. The stories differ over the exchange: a garden, forty palms, or forty trees. [12 notes: THALABI(عطاء); BAGHAWI(عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA(السدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(السدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي); IBNASHUR; ALUSI]
- 92:5/q02/p4 The traits describe a believer or the generous Muslim as a type. — Ibn ʿĀshūr adduces the denizens' admission that they neither prayed nor fed the poor at 74:43–44; Qur'an Yolu contrasts the generous Muslim with a miserly disbeliever. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 92:5/q03 What does giving mean here?  [أَعْطَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the gift is wealth in general, required financial dues, wider obedience and rights, or an inward gift.
- 92:5/q03/p1 Giving wealth in good causes, both required and voluntary. — Al-Ṭabarī specifies God's commanded ways and what God bestowed; Ibn ʿAbbās sometimes says one's available or surplus wealth. Rāzī names manumission, captive ransom and support for Muslims, with 2:3, 8:3, 76:8 and 92:17–20; Elm alılı adds feeding the needy. Abū Ḥayyān includes obligatory, recommended and honorable giving; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ points to 92:11,18 for the wealth contrast. [42 notes: TAB(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس، قتادة، الضحاك); MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(أهل التفسير,ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(أهل التفسير,ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN(ابن زيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة وابن زيد); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس عن عكرمة); ALUSI(ابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد); ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI; STUDYQURAN(most)]
- 92:5/q03/p2 Giving means discharging the financial dues owed to God. — Qatāda and others specify God's due; al-Ḥusayn ibn Wāqid specifies alms. Al-Ālūsī understands these as financial obligations. Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī state wealth's rights. [22 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس، قتادة، الضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); NAHHAS(الحسين بن واقد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); QURTUBI(قتاده); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة وابن زيد); DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); STUDYQURAN(most)]
- 92:5/q03/p3 Giving includes obedience, every due right in word or deed, or oneself as well as wealth. — Qushayrī includes fairness to others without demanding it for oneself. Rāzī cites Arabic use of 'give obedience.' Al-Sāmarrāʾī says two omitted objects open the gift and recipient; 93:5 and 108:1 each state one object. Al-Ālūsī rejects the obedience reading as contrary to conventional money-giving and the contrast with withholding. [8 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ALUSI-]
- 92:5/q03/p4 Giving means offering truth to God from the heart. — This reading is attributed to al-Ḥasan by Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); QURTUBI(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 92:5/q03/p5 Giving is an inward relinquishment for God's pleasure. — Al-Sulamī says the generous give up both abodes, avoid frivolity and doubtful matters and persist toward nearness; al-Qāshānī adds purifying attachment to what was relinquished. [2 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); QASHANI]

## 92:5/q04 Why are the objects of 'gave' omitted?  [أَعْطَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the omission highlights the giver or leaves the kind and recipient of giving open.
- 92:5/q04/p1 The omission makes the deed and giver the focus of praise. — Abū Ḥayyān says both gift and recipient are omitted because praise concerns the giver; al-Samīn includes omissions after 'was mindful' and 'affirmed,' and parallel omissions in the opposing clause. Al-Itqān gives this as an example of an omitted verbal object. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN]
- 92:5/q04/p2 The omission leaves the gift and recipient unrestricted. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the range includes wealth, obedience and self; he distinguishes ellipsis where an object is recoverable from treating a transitive verb intransitively, citing 2:48 and 74:11. The sura also leaves oath, answer and later promise broad, unlike al-Shams. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 92:5/q04/p3 The omitted object is understood as money, specifically alms. — Ibn ʿĀshūr argues that unqualified giving without return conventionally means giving money. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]

## 92:5/q05 What does being mindful mean here?  [وَٱتَّقَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether its scope is God and disobedience generally, stinginess specifically, the Day or reckoning, or inward detachment.
- 92:5/q05/p1 Fearing God and guarding against disobedience or His punishment. — Al-Biqāʿī connects protection with obedience and avoiding sin. Rāzī says all improper conduct, referring to the question of minor sins at 2:2. Elm alılı cites 53:32 on major sins. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ gives the root sense of protection from harm, adduces 16:81 and verses locating piety in the heart (22:32,37; 49:3; 58:9), and its contrast with wickedness (91:8; 38:28). Corpus Coranicum compares biblical fear of God and notes Andrae's late antique monastic parallel, while distinguishing predominant secular pre-Qurʾānic protection-seeking. [60 notes: TAB(ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس، قتادة، الضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,قتادة); NAHHAS(الحسين بن واقد); THALABI; MAWARDI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,مقاتل); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,قتادة); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(ابن عباس,قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس,قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس عن عكرمة,قتادة); ALUSI(ابن عباس,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM(Andrae); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 92:5/q05/p2 Guarding against stinginess. — Mujāhid offers the gloss; al-Sāmarrāʾī says the objectless form especially allows it. Al-Ālūsī dismisses it, while Bint al-Shāṭiʾ argues that giving already opposes stinginess and verses 8–9 oppose the combined traits to it. [9 notes: MAWARDI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة); ALUSI(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); BINTSHATI(ابن عباس); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BINTSHATI-]
- 92:5/q05/p3 Fearing the Day of Judgment, reckoning, or punishment. — Corpus Coranicum prefers to understand the absolute verb as shortening 'fear the Day,' comparing explicit 73:17. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports God and reckoning or punishment as closely related objects; Iṣlāḥī links the fear to the Day when deeds alone avail, citing 76:7–10. [4 notes: BINTSHATI(المفسرون); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; CORPUSCORANICUM+]
- 92:5/q05/p4 Renouncing distractions and cleansing the self of attachment. — Al-Qāshānī pairs relinquishment with freedom from attachment; al-Sulamī includes avoiding doubtful or frivolous things. [2 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); QASHANI]

## 92:5/q06 What is understood as the object of 'was mindful'?  [وَٱتَّقَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the omitted object is God, the Day or reckoning, or left open.
- 92:5/q06/p1 God is the understood object. — Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies God; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says that when the Qurʾān states the object of taqwā it is God. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI]
- 92:5/q06/p2 The Day of Judgment is understood. — Corpus Coranicum compares the explicit construction in 73:17 and prefers it for the absolute verb here. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM+]
- 92:5/q06/p3 The omitted object keeps protection general, with stinginess especially in view. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī distinguishes an unspecified use from an ellipsis that requires recovering a particular object. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 92:5/q07 Why are giving and piety named before belief in the good promise?  [أَعْطَىٰ وَٱتَّقَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the order reflects a reported life sequence, foundational deeds, cause and effect, or a progression from specific action to comprehensive belief.
- 92:5/q07/p1 The order moves from concrete deeds and piety toward faith, making the traits comprehensive. — Al-Ālūsī prefers financial worship, other worship, then tawḥīd or comprehensive faith; he rejects an obedience reading whose order remains unexplained. Al-Sāmarrāʾī moves from the specific to the broad and says giving builds solidarity, piety prevents harm and belief protects rights. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ALUSI+]
- 92:5/q07/p2 Giving heads the clause because it is foundational to the ensuing traits. — Al-Irshād says giving and withholding lead their clauses though lower in rank than what follows, as foundations for faith and piety or their opposites. [1 notes: ALUSI(الإرشاد)]
- 92:5/q07/p3 The acts come first because causes and practice precede recompense. — Elm alılı says faith precedes the deeds in being, but affirmation must be practiced: speech without giving and piety does not earn ease. Deeds are named before their resulting goal because effects follow causes. [3 notes: ELMALILI]
- 92:5/q07/p4 Belief follows piety as a specific case of a general trait and to preserve rhyme. — Al-Ālūsī rejects the alternative that learning tawḥīd itself is part of giving obedience and avoiding shirk part of piety. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 92:5/q07/p5 The order reflects the sequence of deeds in a reported person's life. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī reports that some identify that person as Abū Bakr or ʿAlī, but himself argues for a progression of qualities. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 92:5/q08 How do these descriptions explain the sura's contrast?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the division emphasizes agents and traits, outcomes, or the structure joining both.
- 92:5/q08/p1 They unpack the preceding claim that human striving differs into opposed paths and outcomes. — Rāzī pairs deeds with reward or punishment; al-Bayḍāwī says the descriptions explain divergent efforts. Al-Ālūsī rejects the alternative that effort seeks the night or day or male or female. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the prior summary builds suspense; 99:6–8 also distinguishes even minute deeds. Corpus Coranicum sees paired conduct and recompense, symmetrical stanza halves, cross-rhyme and divine first-person plural; al-Biqāʿī connects the oath to its answer and 91:10. [18 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BIQAI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:5/q08/p2 The passage uses mirrored opposing traits and consequences. — Al-Burhān says the shared condition for giving, piety and belief is mirrored by withholding, self-sufficiency and denial. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this الجمع مع التقسيم and four antitheses; Corpus Coranicum adds ease versus hardship and notes that the final stanza reverses the earlier saved/damned order, linking ittaqā in verse 5 with atqā in verse 17. The majestic plural marks the difficulty of either path, says al-Biqāʿī. [8 notes: BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 92:5/q08/p3 Giving and miserliness receive separate mention to encourage generosity. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says they are already included under piety and self-sufficiency but are named to urge Muslims to give; the three positive traits mark believers and their opposites idolaters. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 92:5/q09 What occasion is reported for this passage's revelation?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports connect it to Abū Bakr's manumission, a disputed palm, or the prophetic saying about spenders and misers; individual accounts differ in their actors and exchanges.
- 92:5/q09/p1 Abū Bakr freed vulnerable enslaved Muslims for God's reward despite his father's preference for strong protectors. — Abū Bakr reportedly freed elderly women; some accounts say nine enslaved men. Abū Qūḥāfa's advice and Abū Bakr's answer are reported by Ibn Isḥāq, ʿĀmir and others. Al-Ḥākim grades one report sound, and Ibn Jarīr is said to call another authentic; the claimed revealed span varies. Ibn ʿAṭiyya links these accounts with Meccan or partly Medinan dating. ## 92:5/q11 How do the verses relate divine facilitation to choice and decree?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether facilitation means God makes each side more likely, or the language is understood as rhetorical enabling, causal attribution, or judgment of the outcome.
- 92:5/q11/p1 God makes obedience more likely for believers and sin more likely for disbelievers, so the favored course becomes necessary. — Rāzī presents the companions' inference and rejects three replies as contrary to the apparent wording and rational proof; he says the prophetic report concerns what accords with unchangeable divine foreknowledge. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(الأصحاب); RAZI-FULL+]
- 92:5/q11/p2 Facilitation of hardship means withholding aids to good, by rhetorical opposition. — Al-Qaffāl treats the two opposed outcomes as parallel expressions and cites 42:40 and 84:24. Rāzī rejects it as departing from the apparent meaning. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); RAZI-FULL-]
- 92:5/q11/p3 The result is attributed to its cause. — Al-Qaffāl compares 14:36, where idols are said to have led many astray; Rāzī rejects the response. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); RAZI-FULL-]
- 92:5/q11/p4 The wording is God's judgment and report of a person's end. — Al-Qaffāl proposes this; Rāzī rejects it. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); RAZI-FULL-]
- 92:5/q11/p5 All people were created to worship, so facilitation need not establish divergent created purposes. — Al-Qaffāl cites 51:56; Rāzī calls the reply weak because facilitation follows God's unchangeable prior knowledge. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); RAZI-FULL-]
- 92:5/q11/p6 God makes obedience attractive and grants success until it becomes easiest. — The Study Quran reports al-Zamakhsharī's explanation and compares the opening of a guided person's breast in 6:125. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(al-Zamakhsharī)]

## 92:5/q12 What ethical or social lesson does the giving and piety pairing teach?  [أَعْطَىٰ وَٱتَّقَىٰ · interpretation]
- 92:5/q12/p1 Giving must seek God's pleasure rather than display or worldly advantage. — Iṣlāḥī cites 76:7–10: the giver fears the Day when deeds avail. He says faith in afterlife reward motivates virtue, while giving for worldly gain lacks blessing, comparing 107:1–3. Elm alılı says the believer gives gladly, resists selfishness, and trusts the better afterlife, citing 59:9 and 64:16. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI]
- 92:5/q12/p2 The passage promotes generosity against a Meccan pattern of hoarding and neglecting the poor. — Kur'an Yolu notes unequal wealth and wealthy pagans' refusal to feed the poor, citing 36:47, 4:37 and 57:24; it sees Meccan revelation cultivating shared hardship and blessing alongside monotheism. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 92:5/q13 How does this verse connect with the rest of the sura and neighboring suras?  [* · links]
- 92:5/q13/p1 It begins the detailed contrast of conduct and outcome that the sura later reprises. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the summary of differing efforts at 92:4 builds suspense; the final section reverses saved and damned, with ittaqā at verse 5 echoed by atqā at verse 17. Corpus Coranicum describes the first behavior-to-result pair and its mirrored final stanza. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 92:5/q13/p2 It explains the previous sura's successful and failed self-purification. — Abū Ḥayyān links the traits here to success and failure at 91:9–10, followed by warning of Fire and differentiation of those entering and spared. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 92:5/q13/p3 The sura contrasts believers and idolaters to affirm guidance and accountability. — Ibn ʿĀshūr summarizes its themes: praise and blame, differing rewards, divine guidance and the Messenger's reminder; he also notes signs of creation. Al-Biqāʿī says different ends refute claims that outcomes are all alike. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI]

## 92:5/q14 How is the sura's revelation place and order described?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether it is wholly Meccan or has Medinan material inferred from the Abū al-Daḥdāḥ report.
- 92:5/q14/p1 The whole sura is Meccan. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this view alongside the Abū Bakr occasion; Ibn ʿĀshūr says most authorities hold it. He gives the revelation order as ninth, after al-Aʿlā and before al-Fajr. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 92:5/q14/p2 The sura is Medinan or contains Medinan revelation. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya associates this with occasion reports; Ibn ʿĀshūr says especially the reported Abū al-Daḥdāḥ purchase from a hypocrite prompts this classification. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور)]

## 92:5/q15 What other sura facts are recorded with this verse?  [* · sciences]
- 92:5/q15/p1 Its titles include al-Layl, wa-al-Layl, and wa-al-Layl idhā yaghshā; a count of twenty verses is given. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says al-Bukhārī and al-Tirmidhī use the last title and places the sura ninth in revelation order. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 92:5/q16 How does the open wording of this passage extend beyond verse 5?  [* · rhetoric]
- 92:5/q16/p1 Unspecified objects and nouns keep deeds, belief, and outcomes broad. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says al-ḥusnā may denote Paradise, good life, good word, future outcome or sound creed, and prefers all these since no noun is supplied, citing 7:180. Likewise yusrā embraces helping others, ease in one's own affairs, and Paradise, with 65:2–4 and 13:18; the contrasting hardship culminates in Hell. He says the sūra leaves oath, answer, actions, agents and outcomes unrestricted. Rāzī reports future wording as certainty (2:21), room for repentance, or deferred afterlife reward; al-Sāmarrāʾī sees the prefixed sīn as assurance, comparing 87:8. [11 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(قسم من المفسرين); RAZI-FULL]
- 92:5/q16/p2 The opposing clauses map virtues and vices to different forms of ease and hardship. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī maps giving to easing others, piety to one's circumstances and faith to Paradise; self-sufficiency instead breeds tyranny and hardship for self and others, citing 96:6–7. Al-Burhān treats the opposite traits as a shared condition. [4 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BURHAN]

## 92:5/q17 What non-final vowel treatment is reported in comparable recitation routes?  [فَأَمَّا · readings]
- 92:5/q17/p1 Some routes open such forms, while others read between vowels where yāʾ is written. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports Abū ʿAmr's usual treatment on imāla routes and al-Azraq from Warsh opening in routes through Ibn Ghalbūn, while al-Taysīr and related routes use the intermediate pronunciation; the cited comparison is fa-ammā man ṭaghā. [2 notes: NASHR(أبو عمرو,الأزرق عن ورش)]

## 92:5/q18 How is the passage cited in descriptions of Paradise?  [* · links]
- 92:5/q18/p1 The Prophet cited the promise attached to human work among verses describing Paradise. — Al-Burhān reports that he also cited 32:17 for unseen delights and 56:30 for the tree's long shade. [1 notes: BURHAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 92:5/q19 What later usage of taqwā-related terms is noted?  [وَٱتَّقَىٰ · meaning]
- 92:5/q19/p1 The plural muttaqūn emerges later alongside growing mention of disbelievers and believers. — Corpus Coranicum places it in groups IIIa and IIIb and cites 78:31, 77:41, 69:48, 68:34, 52:17 and 51:15. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 92:5/q20 How do the giver's deeds relate to later facilitation?  [* · interpretation]
- 92:5/q20/p1 Giving, piety, and belief cause God to enable continued good conduct. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the relative clause makes facilitation a reward for those deeds and helps the person persist; describing their deeds as easy praises them, as at 87:8. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 92:5/q21 Whom do the opposing three traits characterize?  [* · referent]
- 92:5/q21/p1 The combined opposing traits characterize a polytheist. — Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts them with the positive traits that mark a believer. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]

## 92:5/q22 How does the sura frame wealth and God's ownership?  [* · interpretation]
- 92:5/q22/p1 Givers seek the Hereafter while misers seek the present world, though both lives belong to God. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says this calls people to God's guidance in both lives. He says the emphatic lām in the ownership statement suits this sura's repeated concern with wealth, unlike the wealth-free context of 53:25. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 92:5/q23 How can 'easing toward hardship' be expressed?  [* · rhetoric]
- 92:5/q23/p1 It may mirror the phrase for ease rhetorically, or the verb may mean 'prepare' for either outcome. — Al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī offers both ways of understanding the wording. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
