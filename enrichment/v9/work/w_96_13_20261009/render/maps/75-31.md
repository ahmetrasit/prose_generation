# 75:31: 15 questions

## 75:31/q01 What does the opening fā connect this account to?  [فَلَا · links]
turns on: Whether fā resumes the earlier resurrection challenge or the immediately preceding death scene.
- 75:31/q01/p1 It follows the earlier sinner's skeptical question about resurrection and exposes the conduct behind it. — Al-Zamakhsharī connects it to 75:6; al-Biqāʿī says the omitted object broadens the denial. Elmalılı links the sinner's worldly absorption and return to God. Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī call the proposed syntactic attachment to yasʾalu strained or remote, although al-Ālūsī says the Abū Jahl revelation report does not exclude it. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 75:31/q01/p2 It follows 75:30, where a dying person is taken to God without provision for the meeting. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the context implies awareness of loss and regret; al-Bayḍāwī says the person is driven to God and His judgment. Iṣlāḥī likewise describes an exacting journey without prayer or spending as provisions. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 75:31/q02 How does lā work before the past verbs here?  [فَلَا وَلَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether a past-tense lā needs a second negative, and how the apparently single lā of 90:11 is explained.
- 75:31/q02/p1 It negates completed actions with the force of lam; the paired negative supports ordinary past negation. — Ibn Qutayba cites two anonymous poems, lā afaʾnā and lā alamma. Abū ʿUbayda and al-Mubarrad cite 90:11, a hadith and Ṭarafa; Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites Ṭarafa and Abū Khirāsh. Al-Kisāʾī says Arabs pair or imply a second negation; al-Rāzī reads the freeing and feeding after 90:11 as its delayed completion. Al-Qurṭubī cites Zuhayr; Abū Ḥayyān cites poetry and rajaz; al-Samīn adds Imruʾ al-Qays. Al-Burhān, al-Itqān, Bursawī and Ibn ʿĀshūr stress repetition, with unpaired past lā rare or optative. Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts optative lā zāla with factual mā zāla and cites an unnamed poet's optative lā. [27 notes: AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الشاعر); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة، المبرد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن قتيبة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL(أهل العربية,الكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي,زهير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(امرؤ القيس); BURHAN; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الكسائي); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(الشاعر)]
- 75:31/q02/p2 Past lā is negative without needing a paired lā; 90:11 can exemplify this. — Al-Akhfash gives lā dhahaba, 'he did not go,' and treats 90:11 similarly. Al-Samīn says Makkī calls the second lā independently negative, though al-Samīn asks why its coordination must be denied. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); SAMIN-DURR(مكي)]
- 75:31/q02/p3 The apparent past lā in 90:11 is an elided interrogative exhortation, not an independent parallel to this pair. — Al-Qurṭubī supplies an omitted interrogative alif, afalā; al-Rāzī reports 'would he not?' or 'why did he not?' Ibn ʿĀshūr instead finds an implied repeated negation in the obstacle's freeing, feeding and believing. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 75:31/q03 Who is the person described?  [فَلَا صَدَّقَ وَلَا صَلَّىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether the passage singles out the reported occasion concerning Abū Jahl, the sūra's generic human, or ʿAdī ibn Rabīʿah.
- 75:31/q03/p1 Abū Jahl ibn Hishām. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Qatāda and many exegetes are cited; Abū Ḥayyān notes that his swaggering gait in 75:34 was characteristic of him and Banū Makhzūm. Al-Zajjāj reports the saying that each community has a Pharaoh and Abū Jahl was this community's Pharaoh. Al-Qurṭubī prefers the identification attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās. [20 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، المفسرون,الكلبي,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور المتأولين,مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ITQAN; STUDYQURAN(Q); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 75:31/q03/p2 The human being spoken of earlier in the sūra, understood generally. — Al-Rāzī cites 75:3, 75:6 and 75:36; al-Zamakhsharī and Abū Ḥayyān link 75:3 and 75:6. Al-Nasafī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī point to the human of 75:3; al-Bayḍāwī points to the human of 75:36. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); TABATABAI]
- 75:31/q03/p3 ʿAdī ibn Rabīʿah. — Study Quran reports this from Q as an alternative identification. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Q)]

## 75:31/q04 What does ṣaddaqa deny of the person?  [صَدَّقَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ṣaddaqa denotes affirmation, charitable giving, or an expression that carries both.
- 75:31/q04/p1 He did not affirm the truth: God, His Book, the Messenger, the message, or the Hereafter. — Qatāda specifies God's Book; al-Kalbī specifies the message and its sender; Muqātil specifies the Qurʾān. Al-Kashshāf ties denial of Messenger and Qurʾān to resurrection. Al-Nasafī, Bursawī, al-Ālūsī and Study Quran specify the Messenger and revelation. Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers this sense; Elmalılı calls it apparent. Al-Samīn objects that this can repeat wa-lākin kadhdhaba, while al-Ālūsī questions that objection because the contrast follows the paired clauses. [34 notes: MUQATIL; AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(قتادة); NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI(ekser müfessirîn); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(فتح القدير); STUDYQURAN; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ELMALILI-]
- 75:31/q04/p2 He did not give charity or the alms due on his wealth. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Bayḍāwī supply mālahu; al-Qurṭubī speaks of wealth stored with God. Abū Ḥayyān prefers charity since it pairs alms with prayer and avoids repeating 'denied' in 75:32, citing 74:43–46; Elmalılı agrees and cites 74:43–44. Bursawī explains placing alms first by Meccan failure to feed the needy, with prayer later for verse endings. Iṣlāḥī says spending presupposes confidence in the Hereafter's lasting recompense and cites 92:5–7. Al-Ālūsī questions Abū Ḥayyān's contrast argument. [18 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,قوم); ELMALILI(Ebû Hayyân); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Q, Z); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ELMALILI+; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 75:31/q04/p3 The compressed expression embraces both failure to affirm the Hereafter and failure to spend for God. — Iṣlāḥī supplies a suppressed element from the linguistic context and 92:5–7: denying the Hereafter's good outcome makes giving difficult. Elmalılı's overview similarly says the sinner neither affirmed the Hereafter nor gave alms or prayed. Al-Biqāʿī hears both rejection of the Messenger's warning and refusal of the call to spend; omission of the object broadens the scope. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 75:31/q05 What does ṣallā deny of the person?  [صَلَّىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether prayer means prescribed worship, supplication for the Messenger, or a plea for enlightenment.
- 75:31/q05/p1 He failed to pray to God, especially the prescribed ṣalāt. — Al-Biqāʿī extends this to obligatory and voluntary prayer, a severed bond with the Creator. Al-Bayḍāwī, Bursawī and al-Ālūsī specify obligatory prayer. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls ṣalāt religion's central pillar; Iṣlāḥī counts prayer with spending as the two chief deeds. [32 notes: MUQATIL; AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(قتادة); NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن,قتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة,قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(فتح القدير)]
- 75:31/q05/p2 He did not supplicate God or pray for His Messenger. — Al-Qurṭubī gives both parts of this gloss. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 75:31/q05/p3 He did not pray for enlightenment. — Asad links this to his failure to accept truth while alive. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 75:31/q06 What human failure do the two negations portray together?  [فَلَا صَدَّقَ وَلَا صَلَّىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the paired failures are read chiefly as inward and outward conduct, as effects of denying the Hereafter, or as a broader portrait of the denier.
- 75:31/q06/p1 He lacked inward faith and outward obedience or bodily action. — Al-Māwardī offers this synthesis; al-Qurṭubī and al-Sāmarrāʾī report it. Ibn Kathīr says there was no good in either inner or outward state. Al-Sāmarrāʾī distinguishes inward assent, public denial and bodily turning away; the verse negates two acts of obedience. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the addition of prayer makes his condition repugnant and unlike Muslims. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(قيل)]
- 75:31/q06/p2 Failure to believe in the Hereafter explains the lack of prayer and giving. — Iṣlāḥī says belief in recompense motivates spending, and that both prayer and spending depend on the promised reward; al-Sāmarrāʾī ties failure to pray to denial of resurrection in 75:6. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:31/q06/p3 The verse condemns a person who rejected revelation and worship despite proofs and faces God's judgment. — Kur'an Yolu cites proofs of divine unity, resurrection and punishment; Elmalılı includes denial of God, Messenger, Qurʾān and afterlife. Al-Rāzī adds swaggering return to family. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 75:31/q07 Why is prayer singled out alongside belief or giving?  [وَلَا صَلَّىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes give a substantive reason for naming prayer and, on the charity reading, a formal reason for its order.
- 75:31/q07/p1 It is the chief bodily act of worship, so naming it exposes the full severity of his failure. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says its addition makes the person unlike Muslims; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls prayer a pillar of religion. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says it is the most important worship; Iṣlāḥī calls prayer and spending the two primary deeds of the sharīʿah. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 75:31/q07/p2 Prayer is placed after almsgiving to preserve the verse endings. — Bursawī says the Meccans' failure to aid or urge feeding the needy explains mentioning alms first. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 75:31/q08 What is omitted from the wording?  [فَلَا صَدَّقَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Which unspoken element the interpreter supplies or explains.
- 75:31/q08/p1 The object of ṣaddaqa is omitted, widening denial to the Messenger's warning and the call to spend. — Al-Biqāʿī reads the ellipsis as opening the statement to all such good deeds. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 75:31/q08/p2 An understood act of spending is supplied after 'affirmed.' — Iṣlāḥī infers it from the strong linguistic context and 92:5–7, where giving, piety and belief in the Hereafter occur together. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 75:31/q08/p3 The acting subject is omitted. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī treats the omission as a further mark of the sūra's haste. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:31/q09 What does the verse imply about accountability for prayer and other duties?  [وَلَا صَلَّىٰ · law]
turns on: Whether missed prayer is punishable for a disbeliever, and how that failure differs from unbelief.
- 75:31/q09/p1 Disbelievers are addressed by subsidiary obligations and liable for neglecting prayer as well as unbelief. — Al-Jishumī includes almsgiving; al-Rāzī says neglect of prayer merits blame and punishment alongside unbelief. Al-Biqāʿī includes obligatory and voluntary prayer and duties to other people. Bursawī distinguishes liability in punishment from requiring worldly performance. [4 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 75:31/q09/p2 Failing to perform an obligation can itself merit punishment. — Al-Jishumī reports Abū Hāshim's view and says Abū ʿAlī disagrees. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو هاشم)]
- 75:31/q09/p3 A Muslim who neglects prayer from laziness while affirming its duty remains within Islam. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī cites most jurists and says the neglect is less grave than rejecting faith; repentance and forgiveness remain possible. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(أكثر الفقهاء); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 75:31/q10 How are these two failures set against the next verse?  [فَلَا صَدَّقَ وَلَا صَلَّىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the later wording simply restates, intensifies, or formally counterpairs the first two failures.
- 75:31/q10/p1 The next verse repeats them as denial and turning away. — Al-Qurṭubī lists disbelief, prayerlessness, denial and turning from prayer as four faults. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the wrongdoer does wrong and neglects good, while Iṣlāḥī contrasts what he should have done with what he did. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 75:31/q10/p2 The following verbs intensify the faults: public denial exceeds lack of assent, and turning away exceeds merely missing prayer. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says this is why the restatement is harsher; his comparison with 47:20 finds the Qiyāma figure worse because he openly denies, turns away and struts. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 75:31/q10/p3 The clauses form a muqābala: ṣaddaqa opposes kadhdhaba, and ṣallā, understood as approaching, opposes tawallā. — Al-Burhān defines muqābala as paired counterparts that may extend beyond strict opposites; he distinguishes analogical, contradictory and divergent types, and cites Ibn al-Athīr's treatment of ṭibāq as a kind of muqābala. Al-Qalʿī claims the whole Qurʾān displays such relations. Al-Burhān ranks divergent pairing as richest in ambiguity, then contradictory, then analogical. [4 notes: BURHAN(أبو الفضل يوسف بن محمد النحوي القلعي)]

## 75:31/q11 How do the later warnings correspond to these failures?  [فَلَا صَدَّقَ وَلَا صَلَّىٰ · links]
turns on: Whether the four warnings map to faults, different degrees of fault, or stages of the person's fate.
- 75:31/q11/p1 Each fault receives its own warning twice, yielding four threats. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī maps lack of belief and prayer, then denial and turning away, to the four awlā warnings. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 75:31/q11/p2 The warning for unbelief is stronger than that for failing to pray. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the first awlā includes laka and the second omits it; the different gravity explains this, with verse ending also helping explain the omission. The Halaqāt version cites 18:67 and 18:72. [4 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 75:31/q11/p3 The four awlā warnings follow the person through life, death, resurrection and entry into Hell. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī assigns one warning to each state; he says fā binds the close pairs of death and grave, then resurrection and Hell, while thumma conveys the long interval from grave to resurrection. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 75:31/q12 How does the verse connect with other parts of the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Which earlier motif or scene the verse is read as fulfilling.
- 75:31/q12/p1 It exposes the resurrection-denier foreshadowed by the questions about bones, the Hour and being left aimless. — Al-Zamakhsharī cites 75:3 and 75:36; al-Rāzī adds 75:6. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says denial of the Last Day explains his prayerlessness and connects to the bones question. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:31/q12/p2 It belongs to the Resurrection scene announced by the opening oath and the preceding death scene. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī cites 75:1 and 75:29–30: the denunciation reports the person's condition in the afterlife. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:31/q12/p3 It identifies the deeds sent ahead or left behind in 75:13. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī identifies disbelief and turning away as one side, delayed faith and prayer as the other. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:31/q12/p4 It explains the person's self-witness and attempted excuses in 75:14–15. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī links lack of inward belief and outward action to 75:14, and failure to pray and turning away to the later offered excuses in 75:15. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:31/q12/p5 His preference for immediate life in 75:20–21 explains the denial and neglect. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says love of worldly life and abandonment of the afterlife cause these faults. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:31/q12/p6 His conduct places him with the grim-faced people, not the radiant faces of 75:22–25. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says belief and prayer would have joined him to the radiant group. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:31/q13 What consequence does the verse imply for repentance?  [* · interpretation]
- 75:31/q13/p1 His repentance at this stage is useless because he did not accept truth or pray for enlightenment in life. — Asad says the supplied statement completes the sequence and grounds it in 4:17–18. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 75:31/q14 How is the lām of ṣallā pronounced in the transmitted readings?  [صَلَّىٰ · readings]
turns on: Whether the preceding emphatic sound, the imāla, or the verse ending governs lām's pronunciation.
- 75:31/q14/p1 It is pronounced emphatically after the vowelless ẓāʾ or other triggering preceding letter. — Ibn al-Jazarī attributes the emphatic lām after vowelless ẓāʾ to Warsh via al-Azraq; some transmitters retain emphasis before an imāla-bearing alif because of the preceding trigger. [2 notes: NASHR(رواة التغليظ,ورش من طريق الأزرق)]
- 75:31/q14/p2 It is softened in the specified environments, including before an imāla-bearing alif. — Some Warsh transmitters soften after ẓāʾ with ṭāʾ as in the common reading; al-Hidāya distinguishes open and vowelless ẓāʾ. Al-Mujtabā and al-Taysīr report softness before imāla because of the imāla. [2 notes: NASHR(بعض الرواة عن ورش,رواة الترقيق)]
- 75:31/q14/p3 Both emphatic and softened lām are transmitted before an imāla-bearing alif. — Ibn al-Jazarī says several sources preserve both modes. [1 notes: NASHR(رواة الوجهين)]
- 75:31/q14/p4 It is softened at verse endings but emphatic elsewhere. — Ibn al-Jazarī prefers this distinction: euphony favors softness at endings, the preceding trigger emphasis elsewhere. He lists 75:31, 87:12, 87:15 and 96:10 among verse-final examples, and yaslā al-nār as non-final. [2 notes: NASHR; NASHR(أصحاب التفريق بين رؤوس الآي وغيرها)+]

## 75:31/q15 Is lā here a negative particle or a conjunction?  [فَلَا وَلَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the negative clauses can also be treated as coordinated.
- 75:31/q15/p1 It is negative, and the second lā is independently negative. — Al-Naḥḥās says a conjunctive reading would resemble supplication, citing grammarians' rejection of 'I struck Zayd, not I struck Amr.' Makkī likewise calls the second lā independently negative. [2 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR(مكي)]
- 75:31/q15/p2 The clauses may be coordinated while retaining their negative force. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya paraphrases them as fa-lam yuṣaddiq wa-lam yuṣall and says fa-lā coordinates the verbs. Al-Samīn asks why coordination must be denied. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(مكي)]
