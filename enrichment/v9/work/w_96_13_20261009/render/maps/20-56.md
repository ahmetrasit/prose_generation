# 20:56: 21 questions

## 20:56/q01 What force does the opening oath give the account?  [وَلَقَدْ · rhetoric]
- 20:56/q01/p1 It stresses the importance of this brief account and amazement at Pharaoh's persistent disbelief despite the signs. — Alusi calls the clause oath-marked and a concise recap of the Moses–Pharaoh encounter. Ibn Ashur says the oath lam and qad express wonder at Pharaoh's tenacity. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:56/q02 Who is the one shown the signs?  [أَرَيْنَٰهُ · referent]
- 20:56/q02/p1 Pharaoh. — Wahidi-Wajiz, Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi and Ibn Kathir identify him; Ibn Kathir says the proofs were plainly established before him. [4 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 20:56/q03 How did God show Pharaoh the signs?  [أَرَيْنَٰهُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether arayna denotes direct visual showing, imparted knowledge, or both; the timing of what Pharaoh had seen and the verb's object structure are cited.
- 20:56/q03/p1 He made Pharaoh see them directly. — Nahhas stresses direct sight rather than reports. Abu Hayyan derives arayna from visual seeing; Samin says its causative hamza supplies two objects, Pharaoh and the signs. Ibn Ashur says God made them manifest for him to witness. Qurtubi grounds Pharaoh's obstinacy in firsthand sight. Abu Hayyan and Samin object that a report of former prophets' miracles is not ordinarily called seeing. Alusi allows visual perception as the verb's base. [7 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 20:56/q03/p2 He made Pharaoh know or recognize the signs and their truth. — Baydawi offers knowing their validity. Abu Hayyan and Samin report the argument that Pharaoh had then seen only the staff and shining hand, so arayna must mean cognition. Bursevi says God clarified their evidentiary force. Asad attributes this reading to Zamakhshari, Razi and Baydawi. Alusi permits recognition as a base of ara but rejects treating it as a knowledge/reporting verb that needs an omitted third object. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari, Razi and Baydawi); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:56/q03/p3 Both physical sight and inward recognition are involved. — Samin reports that interpreters who allow literal and figurative senses, or both senses of an ambiguous word, can combine them. Biqai says Pharaoh perceived the signs by eye and heart. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL]

## 20:56/q04 Which signs are meant by 'Our signs'?  [ءَايَٰتِنَا · referent]
turns on: The identity and number of Moses' signs, whether proofs of oneness and reported miracles of other prophets count as shown, and how broadly 'Our signs' is used.
- 20:56/q04/p1 The familiar miracles God gave Moses and displayed before Pharaoh, especially the staff and hand, with further signs over time. — Mawardi and Qurtubi say these proved Moses' prophethood; Abu Ali, reported by Jishumi, adds that they prove God's unity and belong to God because Moses displayed them by His power. Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Abu Hayyan and Samin exclude every divine sign from the possessive. Abu Hayyan includes afflictions; Bursevi says Moses showed the signs over time. Biqai says their extraordinary nature shows God's power. Tabatabai includes signs before the drowning, not necessarily at the first meeting. Alusi aligns the staff and hand with Pharaoh's demand for proof in 7:106–108. Islahi says the signs were many and their details appear in the Torah. [14 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 20:56/q04/p2 Moses' nine signs, without specifying their members here. — Wahidi-Basit refers to details given earlier; Ibn al-Jawzi adds that all God's signs cannot be counted. Tabrisi calls the nine proofs of Moses' prophethood. Study Quran cites 17:101. Alusi attributes this view to Ibn Abbas but objects that most signs appeared after the magicians' contest, some decades later or after the escape, and some destroyed rather than guided. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(JJ, Z); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:56/q04/p3 All proofs and arguments establishing the mission entrusted to Moses and Aaron. — Tabari calls them proofs and arguments of the mission. Jishumi says God showed every proof given to Moses and additional evidence. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI]
- 20:56/q04/p4 Proofs of God's oneness and guidance, including signs in creation and the heavens and earth. — Mawardi and Qurtubi report proofs of divine oneness. Abu Muslim, reported by Jishumi, adds justice and guidance for seekers. Abu Hayyan reports signs of oneness in the heavens and earth, visually perceived. Asad includes Moses' entrusted messages and intangible signs of creation from the preceding passage. Kuranyolu reports that commentators combine proofs of oneness with miracles proving prophethood. Alusi objects to including Moses' description of God's deeds proving exclusive lordship. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:56/q04/p5 Moses' signs and the miracles of earlier prophets that he recounted to Pharaoh. — Al-Zamakhshari, as reported by Abu Hayyan and Samin, treats truthful accounts as forceful as witnessed proof; Baydawi and Alusi report the broader inclusion. Abu Hayyan and the Sheikh in Samin object that hearing a report is not ordinarily seeing; Alusi says earlier prophets' signs are not mentioned here and the coming magic challenge disfavors the reading. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:56/q04/p6 Flood, locusts, lice, frogs, blood, blotting, years, staff and hand. — Muqatil gives this particular nine-item list; its blotting differs from other lists. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 20:56/q04/p7 Hand, staff, flood, locusts, lice, frogs, blood, famine years and reduced produce. — Yahya ibn Sallam cites 7:130 for the famine years and reduced produce. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM]
- 20:56/q04/p8 Staff, hand, sea split, stone, locusts, lice, frogs, blood and uprooting the mountain. — Nasafi gives this nine-item list, including the stone and uprooted mountain. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]
- 20:56/q04/p9 Staff, white hand, famine years, locusts, lice, frogs, blood, flood and parting of the sea. — Ibn Ashur identifies these familiar signs performed through Moses and cites 27:12. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:56/q04/p10 The staff and hand displayed when Pharaoh demanded proof. — Alusi connects these to 7:106–108; Arabic may use a plural for two, or each miracle may contain several remarkable details. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:56/q04/p11 Every kind of miracle, represented by the staff and hand. — Baydawi offers all categories or individuals. Bursevi says every kind is included. Alusi reports this view and al-Sakhawi's categories: creating the absent, destroying the existent, and changing what remains. Alusi concedes staff and hand show these three but questions whether they exhaust miracles and finds the proposal far-fetched. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(السخاوى,بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:56/q04/p12 The removal of Moses' speech impediment is among the signs. — Alusi reports this inclusion and relates it to God's command to Moses and Aaron to go with His signs in 20:42 and Moses' prayer in 20:27. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 20:56/q04/p13 Proofs that matter can be transcended and that lights exist. — Qashani gives this inward reading of the signs. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 20:56/q04/p14 The signs were Ali shown to Pharaoh on a horse. — Alusi reports the Kashfiyya appeal to plural wording in 3:97 and calls the claim manifestly false. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الكشفية)-]

## 20:56/q05 Why are the signs attributed to God as 'Our signs'?  [ءَايَٰتِنَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the possessive is explained by honor and divine agency or by reference to signs already familiar to the audience.
- 20:56/q05/p1 The divine possessive honors and magnifies the signs and underscores that Moses showed them by God's power. — Abu Ali, reported by Jishumi, grounds the attribution in God's power. Ibn Atiyya calls the majestic pronoun honorific; Abu Hayyan reports the possessive as honorific, and Alusi says the majestic plural magnifies the signs and Pharaoh's persistence. [4 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:56/q05/p2 The possessive makes the known signs definite, such as the staff and hand. — Samin says the possessive supplies prior definiteness; Bursevi likewise refers it to the familiar signs, and Ibn Ashur specifies those performed through Moses. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:56/q06 What is the force of 'all' in 'all Our signs'?  [كُلَّهَا · interpretation]
turns on: The relevant domain of 'all': each familiar sign, all categories or details, or an idiom for abundance.
- 20:56/q06/p1 It includes every sign in the relevant known set, without claiming every sign God has. — Ibn Atiyya restricts 'all' to signs shown to Pharaoh; Tabrisi and Ibn al-Jawzi exclude all God's signs. Samin says the emphatic word rules out a mere subset and fits inward perception of proofs of oneness and prophecy. Baydawi says it can cover every individual. Bursevi says it confirms the familiar kinds. Ibn Ashur says this totality intensifies wonder and compares 54:41–42. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:56/q06/p2 It confirms every kind or category of sign. — Baydawi offers categories as well as individuals; Bursevi says every kind was included. Alusi reports an all-kinds proposal but objects that its proposed categories may not exhaust miracles. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:56/q06/p3 It emphasizes every implication and detail of the displayed signs, leaving Pharaoh no excuse. — Alusi says many particulars are not enumerated yet every implication and detail was before Pharaoh; his account also allows plural wording for the many details within the staff and hand. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:56/q06/p4 Arabic idiom permits 'all' here to mean many or very many signs. — Kuranyolu explicitly prefers this reading over taking the phrase as literally every sign. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]

## 20:56/q07 When were the signs shown and the denial described?  [أَرَيْنَٰهُ فَكَذَّبَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the verse summarizes the whole confrontation or is read in strict sequence before the magicians' summons.
- 20:56/q07/p1 The verse surveys signs shown across Moses' mission and Pharaoh's continuing pattern of denial. — Bursevi says the staff, hand and other signs came over time; Tabatabai includes those before Pharaoh's drowning and says denial was a general pattern, not only the first encounter. Ibn Ashur says Pharaoh denied even after the signs and entered the parted sea seeking the Israelites; he rejects taking the verse as proof that all signs preceded the summoning of magicians, since many came later and waw need not imply sequence. Alusi says many of the nine signs appeared much later. [6 notes: BURSEVI; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL(المفسرين); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:56/q07/p2 The focus is the initial staff and hand shown in response to Pharaoh's demand. — Alusi cites 7:106–108 and argues that the later emergence of most of the nine signs makes their inclusion at this point strained. Abu Hayyan and Samin report the argument that only the staff and hand had yet been seen. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL(المفسرين)-]

## 20:56/q08 What does the 'fa' before 'he denied' convey?  [فَكَذَّبَ · grammar]
- 20:56/q08/p1 Pharaoh denied immediately after the showing, without hesitation. — Alusi says the fa marks immediate sequence; Bursevi likewise stresses denial without pause. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 20:56/q09 What did Pharaoh deny?  [فَكَذَّبَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the unstated object is the signs and their divine origin, Moses and his message, a report, or the proof.
- 20:56/q09/p1 He denied the signs and their truth, branding them magic. — Muqatil says he rejected their divine origin and claimed magic. Yahya, Nahhas, Wahidi-Wajiz, Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Nasafi, Tabrisi, Biqai, Bursevi and Study Quran describe denial of the signs. Wahidi-Basit says he treated them as lies or accused Moses of lying. Abu Hayyan reports denial of divine origin and the magic charge; Alusi says the omitted object can be the signs. Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir relate denial to firsthand sight and cite 27:14 for knowingly unjust rejection. [16 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Z)]
- 20:56/q09/p2 He called Moses a liar and denied his message. — Wahidi-Basit gives this as an alternative to treating the signs as lies. Jishumi says Pharaoh called Moses a liar, refused the call to worship God, then accused him of sorcery to mislead his people. Abu Hayyan says the unspoken object may be Moses and his message; Baydawi describes denial of Moses. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية وأبي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:56/q09/p3 He denied the report brought to him. — Mawardi offers this wording together with refusal of obedience. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 20:56/q09/p4 He rejected the proof presented to him. — Mawardi offers this as an alternative to denial of a report; Tabrisi also gives rejected proof as an alternative formulation. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI]

## 20:56/q10 Why did Pharaoh deny despite the signs?  [فَكَذَّبَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether rejection is described as knowingly arrogant or, in the inward reading, as incapacity rooted in attachment to matter.
- 20:56/q10/p1 He knowingly rejected them through arrogance, obstinacy and transgression. — Tabari, Baydawi, Ibn Kathir, Bursevi and Alusi stress deliberate persistence; Qurtubi says direct sight sharpened the obstinacy and compares those who inwardly knew but rejected unjustly in 27:14. Abu Hayyan rejects confusion because 27:14 says Pharaoh knew the signs. Ibn Kathir cites the same verse. Wahidi-Basit says Pharaoh called Moses a magician; Alusi says even a cognitive reading of arayna must retain knowing denial. Ibn Ashur emphasizes his persistence despite many signs. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:56/q10/p2 His attachment to matter prevented him from perceiving immaterial truth and made him call the proof magic. — Qashani's inward explanation says Pharaoh resisted departure from his bodily dwelling. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:56/q11 Why is the object of 'denied' left unspoken?  [فَكَذَّبَ · rhetoric]
- 20:56/q11/p1 The omission magnifies what he denied while leaving the object understood. — Samin gives this rhetorical explanation. Abu Hayyan and Alusi name the signs as the apparent object and Moses or his message as a possible object. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية وأبي); ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:56/q12 What does 'abā' mean here and what did Pharaoh refuse?  [وَأَبَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: The unstated object of refusal: faith and acceptance generally, worship and obedience specifically, or release of the Israelites.
- 20:56/q12/p1 He refused faith, submission, obedience and acceptance of the truth or signs. — Jishumi glosses iba as refusing or refraining; Bursevi calls it intense refusal, stronger than ordinary refusal. Yahya, Nahhas, Wahidi-Wajiz, Baghawi, Tabrisi, Qurtubi, Nasafi, Baydawi, Alusi and Study Quran specify belief, acceptance, submission or obedience. Mawardi pairs refusal of obedience with denial of a report and refusal of proof with rejecting it. Muqatil says Pharaoh denied divine origin and refused belief. [17 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Z)]
- 20:56/q12/p2 He refused the call to worship and obey God. — Jishumi explicitly identifies the call, after Pharaoh branded Moses a liar; Wahidi-Basit specifies monotheism. [2 notes: JISHUMI; WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 20:56/q12/p3 He refused to send the Israelites. — Biqai gives this as the concrete refusal in the Moses–Pharaoh confrontation. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 20:56/q13 What does Pharaoh's refusal imply about responsibility for his actions?  [وَأَبَىٰ · theology]
- 20:56/q13/p1 His refusal was his own acquired act, making reward and punishment applicable. — Ibn Atiyya says wa-aba establishes deliberate acquisition; Abu Hayyan reports Ibn Atiyya and Abi giving acquisition as the basis of recompense. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية وأبي)]

## 20:56/q14 What did the serpent sign reportedly look like and cause?  [ءَايَٰتِنَا · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in the serpent's size, actions and the consequences for the crowd.
- 20:56/q14/p1 It was an eighty-cubit terrifying serpent; Pharaoh fled, a crowd crush killed twenty-five thousand, and he begged Moses to remove it. — Alusi reports this from Wahb ibn Munabbih; Moses removed it and it became a staff again. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]
- 20:56/q14/p2 It rose a mile, swooped at Pharaoh, spoke to Moses, and returned to a staff after Pharaoh pleaded. — Alusi reports this separate account, with a different scale and sequence. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:56/q15 How was the hand sign reportedly seen?  [ءَايَٰتِنَا · reports]
- 20:56/q15/p1 Moses' hand shone with luminous whiteness brighter than the sun, attracting onlookers' wonder. — Alusi gives this report of the hand. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:56/q16 How does this sentence fit the surrounding Moses–Pharaoh narrative?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the verse is a retrospective summary or a strict chronological explanation of Pharaoh's earlier question.
- 20:56/q16/p1 It resumes and summarizes the confrontation before the ensuing dialogue. — Alusi calls it a brief recap. Ibn Ashur says it introduces the dialogue, may coordinate with 20:49 after ellipsis or stand parenthetically, and generalizes the signs here unlike al-Araf's successive enumeration in 7:133. Biqai says this concise account is more forceful than al-Araf's fuller list. Tabatabai treats denial as the whole pattern. Ibn Ashur objects to commentators who join it to 20:49 as an explanation requiring all signs before the magicians; waw need not impose sequence. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(المفسرين); TABATABAI]
- 20:56/q16/p2 It explains Pharaoh's challenge in 20:49 as his response after the signs. — Ibn Ashur reports this common coordination by commentators but objects that many signs came much later and waw does not require chronological sequence. [0 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(المفسرين)-]

## 20:56/q17 Who speaks this sentence, and whom does it address?  [* · links]
- 20:56/q17/p1 It is God's direct speech to Muhammad, interrupting Moses' conversation and addressing Quraysh through the past account. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan say the announcement to Muhammad supports taking preceding first-person speech as God's. Alusi says the majestic plural likewise supports this. Islahi says the inserted four verses are divine speech rather than Moses' and call present-day Quraysh to recognize the true Lord and rightful object of worship and obedience; the first person confirms the shift. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:56/q18 What setting and purpose explain the coming contest with magicians?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:56/q18/p1 Egyptian political anxiety and magic's public religious influence shaped the contest, which gave Pharaoh and his elite another test. — Kuranyolu says Israelite presence and Moses' wish to take them home alarmed Pharaoh's government; magic strongly influenced the people, so Pharaoh's elite used and rewarded magicians as a weapon against monotheism. God gave Moses miracles that defeated their skills, establishing his prophethood and leadership and offering another opportunity to be tested. [4 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 20:56/q19 What other accounts are readers directed to compare with this episode?  [* · links]
- 20:56/q19/p1 The Qur'anic account in 7:103–126 and the Biblical account. — Kuranyolu explicitly directs comparison with both accounts; Islahi says details of the signs are given in the Torah. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:56/q20 What does a cited poetic example show about deliberate grammatical relations?  [* · grammar]
- 20:56/q20/p1 A poet intentionally orders words by their grammatical relations rather than uttering them without syntactic awareness. — Al-Jurjani says Imru al-Qays knew nabki answers the imperative qifa, dhikra is annexed to habib, and manzil is coordinated with it. [1 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 20:56/q21 How is 'kullahā' parsed grammatically?  [كُلَّهَا · grammar]
- 20:56/q21/p1 It is accusative as an adjective qualifying āyāt. — Jishumi gives this iʿrāb. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
