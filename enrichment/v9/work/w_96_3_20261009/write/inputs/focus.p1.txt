# 96:3/q04 Why say 'your Lord' here? [رَبُّكَ · rhetoric]
- 96:3/q04/p1 The title stresses God's care for Muhammad and recalls the command to read in His name.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says rabbuka replaces the expected pronoun to emphasize divine care and to connect the Lord-title with the earlier command. Al-Biqāʿī says the Lord nurtured and trained Muhammad; Ibn ʿAṭiyyah and Abū Ḥayyān contrast Him with alleged pagan lords, assuring the Prophet of support.

# 96:3/q05 What does 'al-Akram' mean here? [ٱلْأَكْرَمُ · meaning]
turns on: The semantic scope of generosity and whether the definite elative compares God with givers or denotes unrestricted supremacy.
- 96:3/q05/p1 God is generous beyond every giver, the source of gifts that no other can originate.
  reasons: Al-Māwardī and al-Qushayrī give the ordinary sense 'generous'; al-Qurṭubī prefers it because preceding favors prove generosity. Al-Khaṭṭābī allows an elative equal to the positive. Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī say God initiates every blessing, directly or by its means. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bayḍāwī and others cite countless gifts, forbearance, repentance and forgiveness. Ibrāhīm al-Yashkurī says God feeds a servant who worships another. Rāzī adduces a poem on increased kindness after offense and distinguishes divine gifts from human gifts sought for praise, reward or protection. Ibn ʿĀshūr defines karam as giving beneficial gifts, including existence and sustenance. Study Quran says Qur'anic application of karīm to God introduced a new divine attribute to pagan Arabs. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ objects to restricting the definite unqualified form to finite comparison.

- 96:3/q05/p2 Al-Akram expresses God's absolute, unbounded generosity and perfection, without specifying another giver for comparison.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr treats the form as intensity rather than comparison; al-Biqāʿī includes perfection of essence, attributes and acts without defect. Elmalılı calls gifts limitless and gratuitous. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ argues that definite, unqualified afʿal and fuʿlā express an unrestricted maximum, citing 87:1, 92:20 and images of the greatest sign, blow, calamity, fire and model; explicit min or a named object limits a true comparison, as in 2:191. She contrasts 49:13's human scope and the genuinely comparative 'best of plotters' in 27:50 and 3:54; she rejects an argument that 'the Highest' implies a lesser lord and rejects explaining the form as rhyme. Her Quranic survey uses karīm for God, throne, messenger, angel, scripture and rewards, and notes ironic 'mighty, noble' at 44:47.

# 96:3/q06 How does the generous Lord encourage the Prophet to read and proclaim? [ٱقْرَأْ وَرَبُّكَ ٱلْأَكْرَمُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether generosity assures the ability to recite, reward for recitation, support for proclamation, or a sincere response.
- 96:3/q06/p1 God can teach the unlettered Prophet to recite and retain revelation.
  reasons: Al-Qurṭubī says the Lord will help and teach one who cannot read. Ibn ʿĀshūr uses the Prophet's 'I cannot read' in ʿĀʾishah's report: knowledge arrives by dictation, instruction or inspiration, as Adam learned names without literacy. He says the relative clause is placed between subject and predicate to answer the writing objection. Kur'an Yolu allows direct teaching without an ordinary teacher or instrument; al-Ṭabrisī says God helps retain the Qur'an.
  holders (9 notes): QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(قيل); ALUSI; IBNASHUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR
  notes: QURTUBI:96:3/r4 QURTUBI-FULL:96:3/r3 TABRISI:96:3-10/r3 ALUSI:96:3/r2 IBNASHUR:96:3/r2 IBNASHUR:96:3/r3 IBNASHUR:96:3/r4 IBNASHUR:96:3/r11 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:96:1/r6
- 96:3/q06/p2 God will reward recitation, including tenfold reward for each letter.
  reasons: Al-Naḥḥās says the Lord will not withhold the reward for reading. Rāzī and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ transmit the ten-rewards-per-letter explanation; al-Ṭabrisī says the Prophet will be rewarded.
