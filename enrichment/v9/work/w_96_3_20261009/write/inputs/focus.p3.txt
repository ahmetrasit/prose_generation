# 96:3/q12 Which part of this sura was revealed first? [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether 'first sura' fixes the entire sura's timing or the five-verse opening; several reports explicitly stop at verse five.
- 96:3/q12/p1 The opening five verses, through 'what he did not know,' came first at Hira; the rest came later.
  reasons: ʿĀʾishah's first encounter and Ibn ʿAbbās's account end at verse five. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Durr relay scholars heard by Muḥammad b. ʿAbbād who distinguish the later remainder. Al-Baghawī ascribes the five-verse view to most exegetes. Al-Burhān says al-Ḥākim authenticated ʿĀʾishah's Bukhārī report and Muslim gives it through 96:5. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites al-Rāzī's account that later opposition preceded the rest, and says al-Ṭabarī's reports and the biography stop at five. Ibn ʿĀshūr marks 96:6–10 as a new subject.

# 96:3/q15 What does the mention of teaching by the pen imply about writing? [* · links]
- 96:3/q15/p1 God taught people writing; reports and poetry trace its presence among Arabs.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies the two omitted objects of 'taught by the pen' as writers or people and the act of writing. He reports a chain from Murāmir ibn Murrah through Aslam ibn Sidrah to Ḥarb ibn Umayyah and from al-Anbār to the Ḥijāz, while Ḥimyar had an earlier Musnad script. Abū Ḥayyah al-Numayrī's verse about a Jew's writing hand associates writing with the People of the Book.
  holders (3 notes): IBNASHUR(أبو حية النميري)
  notes: IBNASHUR:96:3/r8 IBNASHUR:96:3/r9 IBNASHUR:96:3/r10
sources: IBNASHUR = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393

# 96:3/q18 What do analogies elsewhere in the Qur'an show about literal number and comparative forms? [* · rhetoric]
- 96:3/q18/p1 A stated comparator makes an elative comparative, while unqualified forms denote an unrestricted maximum; the dual of two gardens is literal.
  reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites 'best of plotters' in 27:50 and 3:54 as a true response to human plots. She rejects al-Farrāʾ's claim that the two gardens of 55:46 are one chosen for cadence, since 55:48–49 continue the dual address to humans and jinn. This supports her refusal to explain al-Akram as finite comparison or rhyme.
