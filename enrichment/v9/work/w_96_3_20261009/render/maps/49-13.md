# 49:13: 26 questions

## 49:13/q01 Who is addressed as people?  [ٱلنَّاسُ · referent]
turns on: How far the universal opening extends into the genealogical description.
- 49:13/q01/p1 All humanity, believers and others. — Al-Damghānī gives the general sense; al-Biqāʿī explicitly includes unbelievers. [3 notes: DAMGHANI; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 49:13/q01/p2 The opening is general, but the following tribal detail specifically concerns Arabs. — Al-Naḥḥās distinguishes address from genealogy; ʿUmar calls the verse addressed to Arabs. [2 notes: NAHHAS; DURR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب)]

## 49:13/q02 Who or what are the male and female?  [مِّن ذَكَرٍۢ وَأُنثَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pair denotes common ancestors, each person's parents, or only male biological material.
- 49:13/q02/p1 Adam and Eve, the shared first parents. — Al-Qurṭubī compares 4:1; common descent refutes lineage pride. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and al-Ālūsī find this stronger when that is the point. [16 notes: ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI(المفسرون); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Q); JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 49:13/q02/p2 Each person has a father and mother and is made from both parents' fluids. — Mujāhid cites this verse for both fluids. Al-Qurṭubī uses 86:6–7, resemblance from both, and the meeting waters of 54:12; verses mentioning one drop do not make it male only. Asad stresses equal human dignity. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers the broader reading where all class distinctions are addressed. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports an objection that wider class equality does not follow from a specific condemnation of lineage. [20 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI(قيل); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); ASAD-NOTES+; QURTUBI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 49:13/q02/p3 The fetus derives only from male fluid. — Al-Qurṭubī reports the earlier claim from 77:20, 32:8 and 75:37, and rejects exclusivity in light of this verse. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 49:13/q03 What follows from shared creation?  [خَلَقْنَٰكُم مِّن ذَكَرٍۢ وَأُنثَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the main inference is equal status or social fraternity.
- 49:13/q03/p1 People share lineage and human dignity, so ancestry, color and birth confer no superiority. — Al-Jurjānī invokes Adam's dust and a poetic maxim requiring earned merit; Rāzī notes neither birth nor tribal assignment is chosen. ʿAlī's poem contrasts common clay with knowledge and action. Al-Biqāʿī says the sex-specific strengths and weaknesses he mentions confer no status; Ibn ʿAṭiyya stresses equality. Al-Jishumī says human descent antedates religious law. [28 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-ASBAB; WAHIDI-BASIT(رسول الله); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JURJANI-ASRAR; QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(علي); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL; QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; JISHUMI]
- 49:13/q03/p2 Common origin establishes brotherhood and should stop mockery and backbiting. — Al-Ālūsī finds this link weaker than the direct rebuttal of lineage pride; Elmalılı recalls the image of eating a brother's flesh. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 49:13/q04 What do peoples and tribes denote?  [شُعُوبًۭا قَبَآئِلَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the terms mark nested lineage, region, or distinct ethnic populations.
- 49:13/q04/p1 Peoples are broader, more remote lineages; tribes are nearer subdivisions. — Muḍar and Rabīʿa are common examples over Tamīm and Bakr; genealogy descends through ʿimāra, baṭn, fakhdh, faṣīla and sometimes ʿashīra. Ibn al-Aḥmar's and al-Kumayt's poetry support the usage; versions differ in lesser ranks. Al-Ṭabarī also transmits the simple gloss 'lineages'; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Zajjāj state the large-to-small order. [66 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; FARRA; MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(آخرون,أبو بكر بن عياش,ابن عباس,الضحاك,سعيد بن جبير,قتادة,مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة والفراء وأهل اللغة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الفراء,الليث); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أكثر المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجمهور من المفسرين وأهل اللغة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد، ابن الكلبي,ابن عباس,مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(البغوي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو عبيدة عن ابن الكلبي عن أبيه,بعض الأدباء,بعضهم,قتادة ومجاهد والضحاك); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة ومجاهد والضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ]
- 49:13/q04/p2 Peoples are smaller branches below tribes. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Ibn ʿAbbās in one report, al-Ḥasan and Abū Sulaymān reverse the usual ranking. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the report opposes the cited linguists' consensus. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون,ابن عباس,بعضهم,سعيد بن جبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جبير); TABRISI(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد، قتادة); DURR-FULL(الضحاك); TABATABAI(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس)-]
- 49:13/q04/p3 Peoples are non-Arabs or their divisions, tribes Arabs; asbāṭ are Israelite tribes. — Abū ʿUbayd gives the non-Arab gloss; al-Ālūsī cites Masrūq's jizya report and the Shaʿūbiyya association. Al-Bayḍāwī likewise distinguishes non-Arab subdivisions from Arab tribes. [18 notes: THALABI(قيل); MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو عبيد); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الثعلبي وقيل); TABRISI(الصادق); IBNKATHIR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(مسروق); ELMALILI(müfessirîn); TABATABAI(الصادق عليه السلام,قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 49:13/q04/p4 Peoples are clients or people without known ancestry; tribes are Arab lineages. — The Ibn ʿAbbās–ʿAṭāʾ report says clients; al-Qushayrī's examples include Indians and Turks. Abū Razīn specifies mountain dwellers. [6 notes: TABRISI(عطاء عن ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو رزين,عطاء عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 49:13/q04/p5 Peoples identify by towns, regions or valleys; tribes identify by paternal descent. — Abū Rāzin and Abū Rawq give towns and villages, al-Māwardī cites poetry, and Sufyān contrasts Himyar with Hijazi Arabs. Rāzī distinguishes dispersed peoples with unknown common ancestor from tribes joined by one known ancestor. [9 notes: THALABI(أبو رزين وأبو روق); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو روق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو رزين); QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو روق); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سفيان الثوري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو روق); RAZI-FULL]
- 49:13/q04/p6 Peoples are Yemen's Qaḥṭān descendants; tribes are Rabīʿa, Muḍar and other ʿAdnān descendants. — Al-Māwardī reports this as a second classification. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 49:13/q05 What are the roots and forms of peoples and tribes?  [شُعُوبًۭا قَبَآئِلَ · meaning]
turns on: The cited vowel and root usages vary.
- 49:13/q05/p1 Shaʿb evokes branching and gathering, but its root can also mean separating or repairing. — A tree and joining needle illustrate gathering; death is Shuʿūb because it separates. Al-Jishumī quotes ʿĀʾisha about Abū Bakr mending the community. Al-Rāghib compares a valley. Qabīla evokes facing parts of a head. [15 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI(عائشة); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الجوهري); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(الراغب)]
- 49:13/q05/p2 Shuʿūb is plural of shaʿb with fatḥa; shiʿb with kasra is a mountain path, plural shiʿāb. — The Hamdān subclan al-Shaʿb and the nisba Shaʿūbiyya are also noted; Ibn ʿAṭiyya favors an explanation of the latter from broad non-Arab peoples. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ELMALILI; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 49:13/q05/p3 Qabāʾil is the regular faʿāʾil broken plural of qabīla. — Sībawayh compares ṣaḥīfa and katība and surveys related feminine, weak, geminate, small-count and larger-count plural rules; al-Dāmghānī glosses tribes. [24 notes: SIBAWAYH(بعض العرب); DAMGHANI]

## 49:13/q06 Why name only peoples and tribes?  [شُعُوبًۭا قَبَآئِلَ · rhetoric]
- 49:13/q06/p1 They are the furthest lineage divisions people readily recognize; narrower ranks are clearer. — Al-Biqāʿī gives six levels and cites al-Baghawī on the last named ʿashīra. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL(البغوي)]

## 49:13/q07 Why make peoples and tribes?  [وَجَعَلْنَٰكُمْ شُعُوبًۭا وَقَبَآئِلَ لِتَعَارَفُوٓا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the purpose is genealogical, cooperative, theological or extended by a variant construction.
- 49:13/q07/p1 To recognize ancestry and degrees of kinship and manage relationships, without boasting. — Mujāhid gives the example of knowing whose son someone is. Al-Ṭabrisī adds transactions and hadith transmission; al-Ālūsī adds inheritance; Ibn Kathīr cites a gharīb report about learning kinship; al-Kāshifī distinguishes people with the same name by tribal name. [36 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; SULAMI(بعضهم); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة,مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(المفسرون); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 49:13/q07/p2 To foster mutual support, cooperation and social cohesion. — Rāzī reports mutual support as another purpose and prefers recognition over estrangement because of 49:11–12. Elmalılı adds affection and protection; Asad stresses appreciation of shared humanity. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(قيل); ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari); STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL+]
- 49:13/q07/p3 Recognition of one another points to recognition of God. — Rāzī argues God, rather than a noble ancestor, is the noblest source of honor. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 49:13/q07/p4 The open-hamza reading extends the purpose to knowing that the pious are noblest. — Al-Farrāʾ says li-taʿrifū is more direct, but allows li-taʿārafū; al-Zajjāj and others connect the clauses. [6 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(العامة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 49:13/q08 How is li-taʿārafū read?  [لِتَعَارَفُوٓا۟ · readings]
turns on: Deletion, preservation or assimilation of the prefix, or a different verbal stem.
- 49:13/q08/p1 The majority elide one original tāʾ. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn identify the common form; Bursevî spells out contraction. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور والأعمش ومجاهد وابن كثير وابن محيصن وابن عباس وأبان); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]
- 49:13/q08/p2 Al-Aʿmash and others preserve both tāʾs. — The full li-tataʿārafū is reported repeatedly. [5 notes: THALABI(الأعمش وابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعمش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور والأعمش ومجاهد وابن كثير وابن محيصن وابن عباس وأبان); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الأعمش)]
- 49:13/q08/p3 Others assimilate the tāʾs. — Mujāhid, Ibn Kathīr in one transmission, Ibn Muḥayṣin and al-Bazzī are named; the qirāʾāt note says assimilation is phonetic and leaves sense unchanged. Al-Bayḍāwī lists assimilated and unassimilated forms. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد وأبو المتوكل وابن محيصن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور والأعمش ومجاهد وابن كثير وابن محيصن وابن عباس وأبان); SAMIN-DURR; NASHR(البزي); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد وابن كثير في رواية وابن محيصن); QIRAAT-ER; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 49:13/q08/p4 Li-taʿrifū lacks the alif and derives from ʿarifa. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Abān from ʿĀṣim are named, with Ubayy and others in Ibn al-Jawzī; Ibn Jinnī supplies an omitted object such as kinship. [8 notes: THALABI(الأعمش وابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبي بن كعب وابن عباس والضحاك وابن يعمر وأبان عن عاصم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور والأعمش ومجاهد وابن كثير وابن محيصن وابن عباس وأبان); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جني,ابن عباس وأبان عن عاصم)]
- 49:13/q08/p5 Further forms have doubled rāʾ or Ibn Masʿūd's expanded wording. — The Kashshāf includes li-tataʿarrafū; Ibn al-Jawzī names Abū Nuhayk and al-Aʿmash for two tāʾs and doubled rāʾ; Ibn Masʿūd adds 'among yourselves' and 'the best of you'. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو نهيك والأعمش); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 49:13/q09 How does li-taʿārafū work grammatically?  [لِتَعَارَفُوٓا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether lām is purposive or imperative and whether the verb has an implicit object.
- 49:13/q09/p1 Its lām expresses purpose and attaches to 'We made you'. — Al-Samīn states the attachment; al-Majāz says it completes the prior clause. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; MAJAZ]
- 49:13/q09/p2 With li-taʿrifū and open anna, an imperative lām makes anna the object. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī prefer this semantically; al-Samīn calls it unlikely. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن عباس); SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 49:13/q09/p3 If the lām is purposive with li-taʿrifū, its object is omitted. — Authors supply 'the truth' or kinship; Ibn Jinnī compares poetic ellipsis. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(ابن جني,ابن عباس)]

## 49:13/q10 How is inna akramakum read and connected?  [إِنَّ أَكْرَمَكُمْ · readings]
turns on: Hamza vowel and whether the clause is independent or governed by the preceding wording.
- 49:13/q10/p1 The majority use kasra and begin a new sentence. — Al-Farrāʾ says honor is outside the purpose clause; al-Majāz says connection would use open anna; al-Thaʿlabī notes the pause. [5 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; THALABI(العامة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور وابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR]
- 49:13/q10/p2 A reported open anna connects the honor criterion backward. — Ibn ʿAbbās is cited; explanations include a reason for not boasting, knowing that the pious are noblest, or the object of imperative li-taʿrifū. [14 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI(العامة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي ومجاهد وأبو الجوزاء,الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور وابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 49:13/q11 What does akram mean?  [أَكْرَمَكُمْ · meaning]
- 49:13/q11/p1 Most noble, honored or excellent in divine rank. — Ibn Qutayba compares karīm in other uses; Ibn al-Jawzī catalogs excellence and superiority. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]

## 49:13/q13 What does taqwā involve?  [أَتْقَىٰكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Minimum legal obedience versus moral and inward degrees.
- 49:13/q13/p1 Its baseline is guarding against shirk and sin by obeying and avoiding prohibitions. — ʿUmar singles out idolatry; Elmalılı derives the root from wiqāya, distinguishes broad and conventional legal senses, and notes debate about minor sins. [16 notes: TAB-FULL; SULAMI(أبو عثمان); QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); ELMALILI(Birgivî,Resûlullah sallallâhu aleyhi ve sellem,Râgıb); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 49:13/q13/p2 Fuller taqwā joins knowledge, fear, good character and caution about doubtful things. — Rāzī cites 35:28 and distinguishes minimum salvation from highest nearness; Elmalılı cites the doubtful-things hadith and limitations of capacity. Some reports name recitation, commanding right and kinship. [21 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان,جعفر); RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي ﷺ); DURR-FULL(رسول الله); ELMALILI(Birgivî,Resûlullah sallallâhu aleyhi ve sellem,Râgıb); STUDYQURAN]
- 49:13/q13/p3 Its inward summit is release from the self, desire and attachment to creation. — Al-Qushayrī describes nearness through self-release; al-Qāshānī proceeds from virtues through trust and witnessing divine acts to annihilation of separate selfhood. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; SULAMI(أبو عثمان,جعفر)]

## 49:13/q14 Does piety lead to honor or honor to piety?  [أَكْرَمَكُمْ أَتْقَىٰكُمْ · theology]
turns on: Direction of implication in the two elatives.
- 49:13/q14/p1 Greater piety yields greater divine honor. — Rāzī calls this better known, though the reverse more apparent in wording; his sweetness analogy makes the ranks proportional. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 49:13/q14/p2 Being more honored by God brings greater piety. — Rāzī presents this alternative direction. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 49:13/q15 Can lineage retain legitimate distinction?  [أَكْرَمَكُمْ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether rejection of boasting also excludes qualified genealogical or social distinctions.
- 49:13/q15/p1 Pedigree alone cannot confer divine rank; one's acquired faith and conduct decide. — Al-Jurjānī's poetic maxim requires one's own merit. Rāzī's learned Black freedman surpasses a profligate ʿAlid; al-Ālūsī repeats it and cites Noah's son (11:46) and Salmān's inclusion in the Prophet's house. Abū Ḥayyān criticizes descendants of Sufi shaykhs living on fathers' virtue. [25 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR(النبي); QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الشريف,الشيخ,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قيل)]
- 49:13/q15/p2 Prophetic ancestry and some collective lineages retain honor, but must be joined to piety. — Rāzī allows exceptional Prophetic descent but values scholarly inheritance. Al-Ālūsī cites selection of Kināna–Quraysh–Hāshim, reports on Fāṭima's children and continuing Prophetic lineage, challenges to some chains, and distinguishes collective rank from individual superiority. Al-Sulamī recounts the Prophet's 'lord of Adam's children, without boasting': true pride concerns the One who gave the rank. Bursevî locates honor in servanthood rather than office. [25 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(النبي ﷺ); TABRISI(ابن عباس عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الرحمن); ALUSI-FULL(ابن حجر,الذهبي,الشريف السمهودي,المناوي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,جلال السيوطي,جمع من الفقهاء,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI; SULAMI(عبد الله المعلم,عبد الله بن محمد)]
- 49:13/q15/p3 Lineage may have customary or legal standing without determining divine honor. — Rāzī reports the noblewoman/Nabatian objection and answers that religion overwhelms lesser rank. Al-Ālūsī notes public disgrace can affect social compatibility, while questioning blanket disparagement of Bahila. [14 notes: RAZI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL(عربي)]

## 49:13/q16 What does the closing description of God's knowledge add?  [إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ عَلِيمٌ خَبِيرٌۭ · interpretation]
- 49:13/q16/p1 God knows outward deeds and inward piety, so He alone knows who deserves honor. — Al-Ṭabarī cites 31:34; al-Nasafī stresses heart's inclinations; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts God's informed choice with imaginary worldly honor; Islahi says an unknown pious person may outrank a prominent Qurashī. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 49:13/q17 What event occasioned this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: Different occasion reports and, within them, differing names, actions and gradings.
- 49:13/q17/p1 Bilāl's call from the Kaaba at Mecca's conquest provoked racial and status objections. — Muqātil names ʿAttāb, al-Ḥārith, Suhayl and Abū Sufyān; Gabriel reportedly disclosed their remarks. - 49:13/q17/p5 It was also said to concern Salmān al-Fārisī and four Quraysh men. — Muqātil names ʿAttāb, al-Ḥārith, Suhayl and Abū Sufyān in this alternative. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 49:13/q18 What did the Prophet proclaim at the conquest when citing this verse?  [* · reports]
- 49:13/q18/p1 He abolished pre-Islamic ancestral pride: all descend from Adam, while piety or wickedness determines divine rank. — Ibn ʿUmar's route is called gharīb by al-Tirmidhī, and Yaḥyā ibn Maʿīn weakened ʿAbd Allāh ibn Jaʿfar. Ibn Hishām and al-Bāqillānī include Quraysh's amnesty, annulment of old claims, exceptions for Kaaba custodianship and pilgrim water, and aggravated blood payment of 100 camels, 40 pregnant, for whip/stick killing. ## 49:13/q20 Does ancestry count in marriage compatibility?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether this verse supplies the complete legal compatibility test.
- 49:13/q20/p1 Religion and piety suffice; clients may marry Arab women. — Mālik's transmission permits it. Al-Qurṭubī cites Sālim/Hind, Ḍubāʿa/al-Miqdād, Bilāl, Zayd/Zaynab, Abū Hind and a poor righteous suitor; he prefers this view. A report tells families to accept a religious, trustworthy suitor. [18 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,النبي ﷺ,عائشة,مالك); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(رسول الله); ALUSI-FULL(أبو أمامة); TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 49:13/q20/p2 Lineage and wealth may count in compatibility while piety remains primary. — Al-Wāḥidī claims lineage's legal relevance for Prophetic connection or inherited knowledge, excluding rulers' pedigree. Abū Ḥanīfa and al-Shāfiʿī count lineage and wealth; al-Qushayrī uses lineage among equally pious people. Al-Ālūsī reports most jurists considered it but Mālik, al-Thawrī and al-Karkhī opposed. ## 49:13/q21 What other legal rules concerning lineage are discussed?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether Qurashī descent is a necessary qualification.
- 49:13/q21/p1 Falsely denying actual lineage can incur the slander penalty. — Al-Qurṭubī includes falsely calling an Arab non-Arab or the reverse. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 49:13/q21/p2 The supreme imam must be Qurashī. — Al-Māwardī reports consensus; a Shāfiʿī fallback ranks Kinānī, Ismāʿīlī, then Jurhumī when no qualified Qurashī is available. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الشافعية,الماوردي)]
- 49:13/q21/p3 The supreme imamate can belong to any lineage. — Ḍirār and al-Bāqillānī are reported to allow it. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ضرار وأبو بكر الباقلاني)]

## 49:13/q22 How does the verse connect to the preceding social prohibitions?  [* · links]
- 49:13/q22/p1 Common origin and piety undercut mockery, contempt, backbiting and group arrogance. — Rāzī links 49:11–12 but allows censure for religious difference; al-Biqāʿī recalls religious brotherhood (49:10). Islahi traces the social evils to race and tribal superiority and compares 48:29; Asad stresses dignity. ## 49:13/q23 How does the verse fit the sūra's larger argument?  [* · links]
- 49:13/q23/p1 It marks the movement from social discipline to true faith and human brotherhood. — Elmalılı links taqwā to 49:1 and 49:3, and later Islam/faith/iḥsān; Islahi recalls 49:7; the Study Quran sees it as the final section's criterion of honor. [7 notes: ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 49:13/q24 Where and when was this verse revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether a Mecca revelation after the Hijra is classified by location or chronology.
- 49:13/q24/p1 At Mecca's conquest, but classified Medinan because it was after the Hijra. — Al-Burhān and al-Itqān give this as chronological classification; al-Itqān compares 5:3. [2 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN]
- 49:13/q24/p2 Al-Ḥujurāt is Medinan except this Meccan verse. — Ibn ʿAbbās gives the exception and counts eighteen verses; ʿUmar and a minority agree on the verse's Meccan label; others say the whole sūra is Medinan. [4 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عباس,الحسن وقتادة وعكرمة ومجاهد وغيرهم); DURR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); STUDYQURAN(a minority)]
- 49:13/q24/p3 It is a late Medinan verse. — The Study Quran relates it to the recasting of Arabia's moral order. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 49:13/q25 What other sūra notices occur with this verse?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Distinct notices, not competing answers.
- 49:13/q25/p1 In one Medinan chronology al-Ḥujurāt follows al-Mujādila and precedes al-Taḥrīm. — Al-Burhān counts 85 Meccan and 29 Medinan sūras but notes disagreement. [2 notes: BURHAN]
- 49:13/q25/p2 A report promises ten good deeds per obedient and disobedient person to its reciter. — Al-Jishumī transmits this from Ubayy ibn Kaʿb. [1 notes: JISHUMI(النبي)]
- 49:13/q25/p3 A neighboring notice calls Sūrat Qāf Meccan with forty-five verses. — Elmalılı includes it adjacent to this verse's discussion. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 49:13/q26 What theological inferences are drawn from its criterion?  [* · theology]
turns on: Different implications drawn from connected texts.
- 49:13/q26/p1 Guidance and piety involve human acquisition, unlike inherited lineage. — Rāzī answers the objection from 76:3 and 42:52 with 76:29–30 and says worship (51:56) precedes genealogy. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(قيل)]
- 49:13/q26/p2 Combined with 92:17 it supports Abū Bakr's rank after the Messenger. — Al-Itqān reports Rāzī's argument. [1 notes: ITQAN(فخر الدين الرازي)]
