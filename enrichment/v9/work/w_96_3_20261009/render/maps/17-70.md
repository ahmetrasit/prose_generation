# 17:70: 29 questions

## 17:70/q01 What does karramnā mean and convey?  [كَرَّمْنَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word denotes qualities and distinction alone or includes material wealth.
- 17:70/q01/p1 God conferred dignity, nobility, excellence, and manifold merits on humans. — Abū ʿUbayda and Majāz say the doubled form is stronger than akramnā; Jishumī says takrīm suggests repeated or abundant honor. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn derive the causative from karuma, comparing a noble garment or horse; they exclude a merely monetary sense. Ibn Qutayba and Ibn al-Jawzī survey karīm as noble, excellent, forgiving or beautiful; Damghānī relates it to divine favor, citing 89:15 and 17:62. [13 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,ابن قتيبة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 17:70/q01/p2 Honor includes both spiritual dignity and material wealth or blessings. — Kuranyolu explicitly includes material wealth alongside nobility and spiritual qualities; Bursevī describes dignity and generosity. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BURSEVI]

## 17:70/q02 Which of Adam's children receive this honor?  [بَنِىٓ ءَادَمَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the dignity is common to the human kind, collective because of some members, or restricted to believers.
- 17:70/q02/p1 All humanity, including believers and unbelievers, receives a common worldly dignity. — Wasitī distinguishes universal honor from Adam's election. Ibn al-Jawzī says abundant blessings apply even to disgraced unbelievers. Ṭabrisī lists form, subjected things, and messengers. Bursevī and Abū al-Suʿūd, Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī read the statement of humankind as a kind; Bursevī's inward source distinguishes common bodily and spiritual honors from prophethood, faith, and sainthood. [10 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 17:70/q02/p2 The group is called honored because some members possess the quality. — Ibn al-Jawzī and Ṭabrisī cite 'best community' at 3:110 as a collective description grounded in qualified members. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI]
- 17:70/q02/p3 The intended children are believers. — Qushayrī cites 22:18: one God humiliates cannot be honored; Bursevī reports that believers' faith and deeds constitute outward honor. [2 notes: BURSEVI(بحر العلوم,قشيري)]

## 17:70/q03 In what does God's honoring of humanity consist?  [كَرَّمْنَا بَنِىٓ ءَادَمَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether a single distinctive faculty or bodily feature defines honor, or the verse gathers multiple gifts.
- 17:70/q03/p1 It comprises many bodily, intellectual, spiritual, and practical gifts rather than one exclusive trait. — Zajjāj adds guidance and provision to bodily traits; Ṭabrisī prefers the whole range. Bayḍāwī and Nasafī list form, temperament, reason, speech, writing, guidance and crafts. Rāzī calls the human soul and body the noblest in the lower world, analyzes its vegetative, animal and rational powers and the upper origin of spirit, and cites 24:35, 96:1–4 and 82:6; Abū Ḥayyān objects that this philosophical idiom is foreign to Arabic expression. Biqāʿī says the omitted object of honoring generalizes it. Bursevī gives bodily and spiritual strata; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds cleanliness, dress, self-awareness, craft and civilization. Ālūsī rejects restricting honor to reason alone; Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes intellectual products from particular comparative advantages. [26 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); ALUSI-FULL+; TABRISI+]
- 17:70/q03/p2 The chief distinction is intellect, discernment and knowledge of God. — Ibn ʿAbbās reports reason; Junayd, Jaʿfar and Abū ʿUthmān stress understanding or gnosis. Rāzī calls rational knowledge higher than animal powers and references twenty arguments at 24:35. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī prefer reason because it enables knowledge of God, revelation and bliss; Qurṭubī compares reason to the eye and law to the sun. Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes animals can exceed people in speed, strength, courage or senses. Biqāʿī analyzes the soul's faculties; Asad invokes conceptual thought and 2:31. [29 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); SULAMI(أبو عثمان,الجنيد,السياري,جعفر,فضيل بن عياض); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عطية); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Fahreddin er-Râzî); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI(صاحب روح المعاني)-]
- 17:70/q03/p3 Humans eat and drink with hands or fingers, unlike animals that use their mouths. — Ibn Jurayj adds work and manipulation; Jābir's report names fingers. Rāzī and Nasafī relate Abū Yūsuf's citation to al-Rashīd, who then put away his spoon. Ibn ʿĀshūr includes lifting water or utensils. Ibn al-Jawzī contrasts human clean food with jinn eating bones and dung. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says hand eating alone is inadequate because animals excel elsewhere. [33 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); ZAJJAJ(أهل التفسير); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مكحول عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جماعة); TABRISI(جابر بن عبد الله,ميمون بن مهران عن ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ميمون بن مهران عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(أبو يوسف,ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس والكلبي ومقاتل,جماعة); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,جابر بن عبد الله); ITQAN(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(زيد بن علي); STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 17:70/q03/p4 Humans have speech, expressive communication and discernment. — Rāzī says humans can define and convey inward experiences; a mute person can gesture or write, whereas a parrot only imitates. Biqāʿī notes the progression from life to speech. Ḍaḥḥāk's reports sometimes name speech alone and sometimes speech with discernment. [16 notes: THALABI(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك); TABRISI(ابن عباس والضحاك); RAZI-FULL(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك,قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك); QASHANI]
- 17:70/q03/p5 Humans have upright, proportioned stature and bodily beauty. — Rāzī cites 40:64, 23:14 and 2:138, including the arrangement of the eye; he says height alone fails since trees are taller. Reports also single out men's beards and women's tresses or forelocks. Biqāʿī adds dexterous reach; 95:4 supports Ibn Kathīr's account; Jābir's report contrasts upright humanity with bent creatures. [32 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); THALABI(عطاء,يمان); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء,يمان); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء,قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الثعلبي,عطاء,يمان); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جماعة); TABRISI(عطاء); RAZI-FULL(بيان,عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL(عطاء,يمان); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عطاء,قيل,يمان); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL(عطاء,يمان); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام); RAZI-FULL-]
- 17:70/q03/p6 God gave people mastery over other creatures and subjected creation to their use. — Ṭabarī and Ibn Jarīr relate mastery to transport and lawful food; Rāzī details earth, water, air, fire, minerals, plants, animals and sea with 20:55, 2:22 and 16:14, plus a winter poem. Ibn Kathīr and Study Quran cite Adam's creation by God's Hands, divine breath, and taught names (38:75; 15:29; 2:31). [23 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(محمد بن جرير); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(محمد بن جرير); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(محمد بن جرير); TABRISI(ابن جرير); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن جرير الطبري); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جرير,قيل); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جرير,قيل); STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 17:70/q03/p7 God honored humanity by placing Muhammad among them or the best community within them. — Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb is repeatedly named for the Prophet; Māwardī and Baghawī mention the best community, whose collective description Ṭabrisī links to 3:110. [10 notes: THALABI(محمد بن كعب); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(محمد بن كعب); TABRISI(محمد بن كعب); QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن كعب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(محمد بن كعب); BURSEVI(بحر العلوم); ALUSI-FULL(محمد بن كعب); TABATABAI]
- 17:70/q03/p8 Honor lies in writing and accumulated learning. — Rāzī says writing preserves earlier scholarship and expands science, virtue and religion, citing teaching by the pen at 96:3–5. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 17:70/q03/p9 Humanity is honored by divine address, moral commands and prohibitions, messengers and revelation. — Abū Bakr ibn Ṭāhir stresses direct divine commands; al-Ḥusayn adds being held in God's grasp and addressed; Sulamī also reports the gift of messengers. Bursevī distinguishes covenant, fitra and revelation shared by all from special faith or prophethood. [7 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر,الحسين,بعضهم); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL]
- 17:70/q03/p10 Honor is divine kindness and provision preceding human request or obedience. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says God meets needs before prayer, giving and answering first. Māwardī speaks of God's beneficence; Zayd ibn Aslam singles out foods and pleasures. [5 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(زيد بن أسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم)]
- 17:70/q03/p11 Honor is a beautiful human voice. — Dhu al-Nūn gives this specific answer. [1 notes: SULAMI(ذو النون)]
- 17:70/q03/p12 Honor consists in God creating Adam by His Hand. — Rāzī says this implies greater care than creation by 'Be'; Study Quran connects the Hands with the breathed Spirit and extraordinary knowledge, citing 38:75, 15:29 and 2:31. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 17:70/q03/p13 Honor lies in Adam's inherited divine form. — Ālūsī reports the proposal that Adam was created in the form of the Merciful and transmitted this inheritance. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 17:70/q04 What does being carried on land and sea describe?  [وَحَمَلْنَٰهُمْ فِى ٱلْبَرِّ وَٱلْبَحْرِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether carriage names mounts and ships, protection from sinking, or an inward passage.
- 17:70/q04/p1 People ride animals on land and ships at sea. — Ibn ʿAbbās names camels, horses, mules and donkeys; Ibn al-Jawzī and Ālūsī call these soft backs and ships dry boards. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls ship carrying a metaphor from riding, cites 69:11, and adds God-taught sails and oars. Rāzī adds travel, defense, war and livelihood; Kuranyolu extends the benefit to trade and modern transport. Ālūsī prefers this because it uniquely distinguishes humans. [29 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,غيره); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Fahreddin er-Râzî); STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 17:70/q04/p2 God keeps people settled upon land and water instead of allowing them to sink or drown. — Bayḍāwī offers this alternative; Ālūsī reports and rejects its distinctiveness, since other creatures also avoid sinking. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 17:70/q04/p3 Land and sea signify body or self and spirit or heart in an inward journey. — Sulamī says self and heart need the light of divine support, otherwise planning fails; Wasitī calls them manifest attributes and hidden realities, another saying separation and union. Qāshānī reads bodily and spiritual travel toward perfection. Bursevī's Najmī source makes the destination the shore of lordship and reports knowledge and unity. [6 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي,بعضهم); QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 17:70/q05 Why does the verse mention travel on land and sea?  [فِى ٱلْبَرِّ وَٱلْبَحْرِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the phrasing is explained by the historical reach of transport, its surrounding context, or its moral effect.
- 17:70/q05/p1 They are the forms of travel shared across humanity and fit the surrounding land and sea passage. — Sāmarrāʾī says no air travel existed at revelation, the past verbs address historic gifts, saying only air would exclude many, and 17:66–68 concerns land and sea. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 17:70/q05/p2 The familiar means of transport are recalled to invite gratitude and reflection on divine power. — Kuranyolu says everyday familiarity hides the significance of trade and travel; Study Quran connects mounts and ships with gratitude. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 17:70/q06 What are the ṭayyibāt provided to humanity?  [ٱلطَّيِّبَٰتِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ṭayyib describes taste, lawfulness, useful benefit, or an inward gift.
- 17:70/q06/p1 Pleasant, choice foods and drinks, especially prepared human fare. — Examples vary: bread, meat, honey, clarified butter, butter, dates, sweets, fruit, grains, livestock, crops, milk and drinks. Damghānī cites 40:64 and 16:114; Ibn Kathīr adds fine sights and clothes, including goods brought from afar. Rāzī stresses purification and cooking; Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses knowledge of appealing varieties and human invention. [29 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(بعضهم); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(مقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 17:70/q06/p2 They are lawful or permitted provisions. — Abū ʿUthmān and Ibrāhīm al-Khawwāṣ say lawful; Māwardī, Ibn al-Jawzī and Abū Ḥayyān transmit the same sense. Kuranyolu combines lawfulness with appeal and advises healthy, clean, aesthetically good lawful provision. [7 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان,إبراهيم الخواص); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 17:70/q06/p3 They encompass all useful resources, including earnings, clothes, furnishings and possessions. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says provision is anything beneficial and contrasts human earning and dress with animals. Sahl names beneficial worker's earnings; Nasafī offers what one's hands earned. Ālūsī includes desirable goods obtained by work or otherwise. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(سهل بن عبد الله); IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 17:70/q06/p4 They are sincere deeds recorded for the worker. — Ibn al-Mubārak specifies written deeds done sincerely. [1 notes: SULAMI(عبد الله بن المبارك)]
- 17:70/q06/p5 They are things arriving without request or expectation. — Yaḥyā ibn Muʿādh gives this inward criterion. [1 notes: SULAMI(يحيى بن معاذ)]
- 17:70/q06/p6 For the elect they are witnessing, unveiling, knowledge and love. — Bursevī's Najmī source pictures food of witnessing and drink of unveiling in vessels of gnosis and cups of love, surpassing even near angels. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 17:70/q07 Should one renounce pleasant food because of this verse?  [وَرَزَقْنَٰهُم مِّنَ ٱلطَّيِّبَٰتِ · law]
turns on: Whether deprivation follows a sound report and serves health and worship.
- 17:70/q07/p1 No; suitable pleasant food may be eaten and the body cared for. — Qurṭubī says the report attributed to ʿĀʾisha about Satan and tasty food has no basis and authentic Sunnah contradicts it. His scholars call severe deprivation contrary to wisdom, law and reason: wheat is given to people, husks to livestock; bran, barley and coarse salt may harm health. He cites 57:27 against invented monasticism, and examples of Ibn Adham and al-Thawrī eating fine food then worshipping. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(إبراهيم بن أدهم,الثوري,علماؤنا)]
- 17:70/q07/p2 Ascetic refusal of ordinary or refined food is practiced or commended. — Some Sufis invoke the ʿĀʾisha report; stories say Sahl lived on nabq and fig leaves, Dhu al-Nūn rejected finely ground salt and bread for barley, and Abū Yazīd avoided others' food for forty years. Qurṭubī disputes the practice. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو يزيد,الصوفية,ذو النون,سهل); QURTUBI-FULL(علماؤنا)-]

## 17:70/q08 What kind of preference over creatures is asserted?  [وَفَضَّلْنَٰهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether preference concerns worldly advantage, reward and virtue, or inward perfection.
- 17:70/q08/p1 It is observable human mastery, use, and advantage over earthly creatures. — Ṭabarī emphasizes dominance over subjected beasts; Ibn ʿĀshūr limits this to reason and ingenuity in the visible world. Qurṭubī adds reward, protection, discernment and insight; Māwardī and Bayḍāwī list dominance as one possible sense. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kuranyolu deny that the statement decides unseen ranks. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(محمد بن جرير); BAYDAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr)+]
- 17:70/q08/p2 It is reward, spiritual rank, or virtue attained through faith and obedience. — Māwardī gives reward; Abū Ḥayyān reports afterlife reward. Ibn al-Jawzī's Ibn ʿAbbās report restricts the favored humans to believers. Nasafī says reason mastering desire elevates a human above angels, while surrender to desire lowers one below beasts. Biqāʿī names the pious elect. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(بحر العلوم)]
- 17:70/q08/p3 It is preservation, discernment and penetrating insight. — Māwardī gives separate formulations: protection and discrimination, or acquired insight or physiognomic perception; Sulamī reports discernment, self-knowledge and accurate spiritual insight. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; SULAMI(أبو حفص,الجنيد,فضيل بن عياض)]
- 17:70/q08/p4 It is nobility or honor itself. — Bayḍāwī offers nobility alongside dominance; Ālūsī reports an identification of preference with the already mentioned honor. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 17:70/q08/p5 It is the human capacity to combine intellect and appetite and grow toward perfection. — Rāzī divides beings into intellect without appetite, appetite without intellect, neither, and both; humans surpass beasts. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says developing humans may reach beyond angels, citing Adam's knowledge at 2:30. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 17:70/q08/p6 It is an inward perfection of unity and direct receptivity to divine light. — Qāshānī says prophets' superiority stems from entrusted divine knowledge rather than mere species membership, citing 2:30. Bursevī's Najmī commentary cites 33:72 and 24:35 for the perfect human as bearer of trust; Kashifī likens that human to a mirror collecting divine attributes. [4 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,الكاشفي)]

## 17:70/q09 Who are the 'many' over whom humanity is preferred?  [كَثِيرٍۢ مِّمَّنْ خَلَقْنَا · referent]
turns on: Whether kathīr is literal or rhetorical, whether min is partitive or explanatory, and whether exclusion entails rank.
- 17:70/q09/p1 Many means animals and sometimes jinn within the created material order; angels are outside the comparison. — Suddi names beasts; Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes animals and jinn. Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites animal communities at 6:38 and calls angels noncosmic beings. Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī say exclusion alone leaves angelic rank undetermined. Ibn ʿĀshūr says revelation, not this observable comparison, establishes unseen ranks. [20 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr)+; TABATABAI+]
- 17:70/q09/p2 Many is literal and excludes all angels from human superiority. — Zajjāj and Naḥḥās argue angels exceed people in obedience and freedom from sin, with 4:172 cited. Zamakhsharī attacks a universal reading; some infer even prophets fall below angels. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurṭubī, Rāzī, Ṭabrisī and Abū Ḥayyān reject the inference from mere exclusion. [22 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; BAGHAWI-FULL(قوم); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الزمخشري,فرقة); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(الزمخشري,المعتزلة,بعضهم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; TABRISI-; RAZI-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 17:70/q09/p3 Many is literal, but only selected leading angels are excepted. — Al-Kalbī names Gabriel, Michael, Israfil, the Angel of Death/Azrail and peers; one Ibn ʿAbbās report also exempts some leading angels. Bayḍāwī leaves open whether all or only elect angels are outside the group. [7 notes: THALABI(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(طائفة); BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 17:70/q09/p4 Many functions rhetorically as all creation, including angels. — Ṭhaʿlabī and Ibn al-Jawzī cite 'most' for all devils at 26:223; Baghawī cites 26:221–222; Nasafī attributes the universal reading to al-Ḥasan. Ṭabrisī allows this in rebuttal; Study Quran invokes angels' prostration to Adam (2:34 and parallels). Zamakhsharī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bayḍāwī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī object that this is strained or inelegant. [6 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قوم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; TABRISI; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); KASHSHAF-FULL-; BAYDAWI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 17:70/q09/p5 Min is explanatory: humanity exceeds all intelligent creatures, including angels and jinn. — Ṭībī says a kind cannot exceed itself. Ālūsī objects that explanatory min is remote and abandons the usual partitive reading. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 17:70/q09/p6 The many may itself be the numerous angels among rational creatures. — Al-Taqrīb proposes this; Ālūsī accepts the possibility that the preferred-over group is strong, not an automatic exception. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(صاحب التقريب)]
- 17:70/q09/p7 Min is partitive, but mentioning many does not determine the remainder's size or rank. — Ṭībī compares 2:253, where preferring over many honors the many. Ālūsī contrasts contextual 'some guided, many disobedient' at 57:26 with unopposed 'many' at 27:15, which need not mean a majority; he rejects arguments based solely on an alleged angelic exclusion. [8 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); IBNASHUR-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 17:70/q10 How do humans and angels rank in relation to one another?  [وَفَضَّلْنَٰهُمْ عَلَىٰ كَثِيرٍۢ مِّمَّنْ خَلَقْنَا · theology]
turns on: Whether the verse compares spiritual rank at all, and how nature, free choice, prophethood and angelic classes bear on rank.
- 17:70/q10/p1 The verse does not settle the human–angel ranking. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says angels can be superior or equal without being among the many, while 4:172 gives separate evidence. Rāzī calls proof from the verse's implication weak. Ṭabrisī says worldly blessings differ from reward and angelic classes may outrank humanity while individual prophets excel. Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān likewise leave the comparison open; Ālūsī disputes the probative force of implication. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kuranyolu limit the verse to observable dominion. [21 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فرقة,قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr)+]
- 17:70/q10/p2 Angels generally outrank human beings. — Zajjāj and Naḥḥās point to sinless obedience and 4:172. Zamakhsharī reads kathīr as excluding angels and calls contrary reports fabricated; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says angels' pure nature and sincere worship exceed mixed human nature and rejects reducing their obedience to mechanical behavior, citing 21:26–28, 66:6, 41:38 and 7:205. Rāzī recognizes the argument but finds this verse weak proof. [21 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(الزجاج وابن عباس,الزمخشري,المعتزلة)]
- 17:70/q10/p3 Believers or righteous humans surpass angels. — Abū Hurayra's report says a believer is dearer than near angels; Baghawī chooses ordinary believers over ordinary angels and elite believers over elite angels, citing 98:7. Nasafī reasons that reason mastering desire raises humans, while vice lowers them below beasts. Ashʿarī and Shiʿi advocates cite choice against temptation; Ṭabāṭabāʾī concedes impediments affect merit but rejects an analogy that empties angelic obedience of worth. Sāmarrāʾī specifies the righteous, faithful and God-fearing. [16 notes: THALABI(أبو هريرة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو هريرة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو هريرة عن رسول الله); RAZI-FULL(أبو هريرة); NASAFI-FULL(النبي); BURSEVI(الكاشفي,بحر العلوم); TABATABAI(الأشاعرة); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BAGHAWI-FULL+; KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو هريرة)-]
- 17:70/q10/p4 Humanity as a kind outranks even angels. — Ibn Kathīr cites the verse for this; Nasafī and Study Quran report a universal reading and Adam's prostration. Rāzī and Biqāʿī emphasize human potential. Zamakhsharī rejects this as forced. [7 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قوم); IBNKATHIR-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SULAMI(أبو عثمان); QASHANI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); KASHSHAF-FULL-]
- 17:70/q10/p5 Human messengers rank first, then angelic messengers, then ordinary angels above ordinary humans. — Hanafis, many Shāfiʿīs and Ashʿarīs are reported for this layered order; Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports a similar ranking. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أصحاب الإمام أبي حنيفة); TABATABAI(بعضهم)]
- 17:70/q10/p6 Cherubic angels rank first, then human messengers, perfected humans, and ordinary angels above ordinary humans. — Ālūsī attributes this to Rāzī, with Ghazālī similar; Ṭabāṭabāʾī repeats it. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الإمام الرازي); TABATABAI(الرازي والغزالي)]
- 17:70/q10/p7 All perfected humans, including saints, outrank angels generally. — Ālūsī reports this third position. Kashifī refines it to human progeny over angelic messengers, human saints over angelic saints, good believers over common angels, and common angels over sinful believers. Bursevī invokes Adam's knowledge and the prostration command, 2:31 and 3:33, plus a report of an angel standing for Muhammad. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI(الأسئلة المقحمة,التأويلات النجمية,الكاشفي,النبي,بحر العلوم)]
- 17:70/q10/p8 One should refrain from ranking angels against humans or prophets against one another. — A group cites reports against prophetic comparisons and preferring Muhammad to Jonah; Qurṭubī counters that the Qur'an itself ranks prophets. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قوم); QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 17:70/q11 What does the report of angels requesting worldly or afterlife gifts say, and is it accepted?  [وَفَضَّلْنَٰهُمْ · reports]
turns on: The report's chains and whether angels could meaningfully seek bodily pleasures.
- 17:70/q11/p1 Angels asked about human worldly pleasures; God distinguished righteous descendants of hand-created Adam from beings made by 'Be'. — Zayd ibn Aslam's form is transmitted by ʿAbd al-Razzāq, Ṭabarī, Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī and Rāzī. Baghawī gives Jābir's prophetic version. Ibn Kathīr calls Zayd's form mursal and supplies connected versions through ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr and Anas; one adds human food, clothing, marriage, riding and sleep. Durr has a variant about riding horses omitting the breathed Spirit. Ṭabāṭabāʾī lists Ibn ʿUmar, Anas, Zayd and Jābir transmitters. [11 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(زيد بن أسلم); TAB-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); THALABI(زيد بن أسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(زيد بن أسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL(جابر); RAZI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم عن أنس بن مالك,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم عن عبد الله بن عمرو,زيد بن أسلم); DURR-FULL(عروة بن رويم اللخمي); TABATABAI(ابن عمر وأنس بن مالك وزيد بن أسلم وجابر بن عبد الله الأنصاري)]
- 17:70/q11/p2 The angels' request report is implausible or fabricated. — Zamakhsharī calls it a manufactured prop for human superiority; Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues bodily needs are survival means for humans while angels already possess the perfection humans seek. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 17:70/q12 How do honoring and favoring differ?  [كَرَّمْنَا وَفَضَّلْنَٰهُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the two verbs separate innate from comparative gifts, worldly from afterlife gifts, means from outcomes, or simply clarify one another.
- 17:70/q12/p1 Honor is an innate unique dignity, while preference is comparative abundance in gifts shared with other creatures. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī makes intellect and its derivatives intrinsic honor, and more varied food, clothing, homes, family and society comparative preference; he rejects distinctions unsupported by ordinary language. [8 notes: TABATABAI(صاحب روح المعاني)]
- 17:70/q12/p2 Honor is natural endowment; preference is the acquired true belief and virtue made possible by it. — Rāzī and Ālūsī say intellect, speech, writing, beauty and stature enable salvation through acquired beliefs and character. Ālūsī says simple identity of the two clauses would repeat the point; Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects restricting the Arabic terms so sharply. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); TABATABAI(الرازي); TABATABAI-]
- 17:70/q12/p3 Honor names worldly gifts, and preference names afterlife or spiritual gifts. — Jishumī, Ṭabrisī, al-Majmaʿ and Study Quran transmit this solution to apparent repetition; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says evidence for it is lacking. [4 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); TABATABAI-]
- 17:70/q12/p4 Honor supplies the means for moral obligation; preference is the higher rank obligation enables. — Jishumī and Ṭabrisī offer this; Ṭabāṭabāʾī quotes al-Majmaʿ and Ālūsī reports a similar account. [4 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); TABATABAI-]
- 17:70/q12/p5 Honor is beneficence; preference is superior giving. — Al-Majmaʿ's formulation is quoted by Ṭabāṭabāʾī, who rejects making preference necessarily an undeserved gift. [2 notes: TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); JISHUMI; TABATABAI-]
- 17:70/q12/p6 Dignity is worth in itself, while superiority consists in reason, judgment, knowledge and sciences. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives this distinction between intrinsic worth and achieved or displayed distinction. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 17:70/q12/p7 The clauses form an ascending sequence of honors, transport, provision and culminating preference. — Ṭībī says the angels' prostration explains Adam's honor, with intervening verses a digression. Rāzī insists the two expressions are not identical; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says carriage and provision are concrete instances of honor. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,الطيبي); TABATABAI]
- 17:70/q12/p8 The second clause explains or repeats the first distinction. — Jishumī says karam alone signals blessing but not comparison, so faḍḍalnā makes the comparison explicit. Ālūsī reports identifying preference with the prior honor. [2 notes: JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 17:70/q13 What does the final tafḍīlan add grammatically and rhetorically?  [تَفْضِيلًۭا · grammar]
- 17:70/q13/p1 It is an emphatic verbal noun intensifying the preceding verb. — Naḥḥās identifies the cognate object; Ibn ʿĀshūr says its indefinite form conveys a great degree of preference. [2 notes: NAHHAS; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 17:70/q14 How does the verse connect with its surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the immediate link is rescue and warning, Adam's story, the afterlife, resurrection, or the later prophetic episode.
- 17:70/q14/p1 It follows danger at sea and warnings by recalling God's gifts and calling for gratitude. — Abū Ḥayyān connects ships and rescue to honor and says the threat of drowning makes gratitude and obedience urgent. Qurṭubī says blessing follows warning. Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees a reproach for forgetting God after rescue; Kuranyolu and Study Quran stress natural power and punishment as sura themes. [7 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 17:70/q14/p2 It recalls Iblīs's complaint that Adam was honored and enumerates Adam's descendants' gifts. — Ṭabrisī links the passage to the complaint and following honors; Asad sees continuity with 17:61 and Adam's knowledge at 2:31. [2 notes: TABRISI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 17:70/q14/p3 It leads from worldly honors to the following account of the afterlife. — Abū Ḥayyān makes this transition; Burhān reports interpreting blindness in the next passage as inability to see afterlife bliss after denial of these worldly gifts. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN]
- 17:70/q14/p4 Human distinction after the signs points to God's power to resurrect. — Biqāʿī argues that God differentiated creatures that began alike. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 17:70/q14/p5 The later verses describe the Prophet's steadfastness when idolaters tried to divert him. — Study Quran summarizes 70–76 as nearly yielding to pressure before God made him firm. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 17:70/q15 What practical response does the verse call for?  [* · interpretation]
- 17:70/q15/p1 Gratitude, obedience, sound belief and rejection of idolatry. — Bursevī says discernment should lead to thanks and rejecting shirk; Ālūsī's al-Kashf says remembered care rebukes idolatry; Kuranyolu says familiar means of travel are divine favors. Islāḥī warns against Satan-like arrogance and assuming unlimited human superiority. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 17:70/q16 Was the verse revealed after a journey involving roadside pools?  [* · reports]
- 17:70/q16/p1 Some said it was revealed when the Prophet told companions to wash hands and drink from roadside pools rather than dip their heads. — Al-Furāt ibn Salmān relates the journey instruction; an unnamed group connects the verse's revelation to it. [2 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الفرات بن سلمان,بعضهم)]

## 17:70/q17 Does human honor affect the ritual status of a dead person?  [كَرَّمْنَا بَنِىٓ ءَادَمَ · law]
- 17:70/q17/p1 A human corpse does not become ritually impure. — Ālūsī reports al-Shāfiʿī using the verse as evidence. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الشافعي)]

## 17:70/q18 What other reports illustrate divine favor or human selection?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports describe different events and degrees of favor.
- 17:70/q18/p1 Praise for being spared an affliction protects the observer from it. — Durr transmits ʿUmar's report: protection applies whatever the observed affliction. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(عمر)]
- 17:70/q18/p2 God selected humanity within a sequence ending in Muhammad as the best of the best. — The reported prophetic selection runs highest heaven, humanity, Arabs, Muḍar, Quraysh, Banū Hāshim, then Muhammad. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 17:70/q18/p3 An unusual report makes Adam's child dearest to God on Judgment Day. — Ibn Kathīr calls the report very unusual; it says angels are compelled like sun and moon. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم عن عبد الله بن عمرو)]

## 17:70/q19 Are the Qur'an's matching verse endings sajʿ?  [تَفْضِيلًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether deliberate matching sound should bear the genre name sajʿ and what that implies for inimitability.
- 17:70/q19/p1 The Qur'an has intentional matching endings called fawāṣil, without being called sajʿ. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says early scholars avoided the term because of soothsayers; Ashʿarites deny Qur'anic sajʿ while recognizing sound design. [4 notes: BAQILLANI(أصحابنا); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الأشاعرة)]
- 17:70/q19/p2 Its recurrent patterned rhyme is sajʿ and contributes to eloquence. — Opponents compare Moses–Aaron order at 20:70 and 7:122 and distinguish repeated rhyme from accidental meter; lexicographers define sajʿ as successive speech in one measure, and Ibn Durayd cites a dove's repeated sound and poetry. [6 notes: BAQILLANI(أهل اللغة,ابن دريد,كثير ممن يخالفهم); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(مخالفو الأشاعرة)]
- 17:70/q19/p3 Calling the Qur'an sajʿ undermines its inimitability and evokes soothsayers. — Bāqillānī argues a familiar Arab form alone could not prove miraculousness, any more than poetry; a prophetic report rebukes a rhymed compensation claim as pre-Islamic or soothsayer speech. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ recounts his argument. [4 notes: BAQILLANI(النبي); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الباقلاني); BAQILLANI+; BAQILLANI(كثير ممن يخالفهم)-]

## 17:70/q20 How does delayed placement create exclusivity in an Arabic utterance?  [* · rhetoric]
- 17:70/q20/p1 With innamā the delayed constituent receives the exclusive focus; a detached pronoun permits the intended word order. — ʿAbd al-Qāhir contrasts restricting who struck Zayd with restricting whom ʿAmr struck. He reads al-Farazdaq as saying he alone defends the clan, not that he defends only it; the detached pronoun serves focus rather than meter or redundancy. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 17:70/q21 When should Qur'an be read from a mushaf rather than memory?  [* · law]
- 17:70/q21/p1 Choose the mode yielding more attention; when equal, the mushaf aids accuracy and adds the act of looking. — Ibn Kathīr says writing catches omissions and altered words, but oral teaching gives accurate performance. Some scholars and Nawawī apply attention as criterion; a possibly non-prophetic report and Bakīr's saying say an angel records faulty recitation as revealed. Ibn Kathīr rejects a general memory rule inferred from Bukhārī's marriage report and cautions against untrained mispronunciation when a teacher is available, permitting necessity without one. [6 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي,بكير بن الأخنس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض العلماء)+]

## 17:70/q22 How long may study of a complete reading take?  [* · sciences]
- 17:70/q22/p1 There is no fixed maximum; recorded completions varied from one day to eighteen months. — Nashr gives Abū Jaʿfar over four months, Yaʿqūb over eighteen months, Abū ʿAmr in a day, all readings in seventeen days, and Ibn Kathīr and Kisāʾī in four and seven days. [2 notes: NASHR]

## 17:70/q23 How do inward and poetic accounts picture humanity's special rank?  [* · inward]
- 17:70/q23/p1 It is knowledge, unity and love exceeding angelic devotion. — Qāshānī quotes couplets about a son of Adam bearing a divine meaning and seeing with God's eye. Jāmī says Adam received an outpouring of love beyond angels' devotion; Ḥāfiẓ says angels know nothing of love's story. [3 notes: QASHANI(قيل); BURSEVI(جامي,حافظ)]

## 17:70/q24 Are the cited examples of human honor exhaustive?  [كَرَّمْنَا بَنِىٓ ءَادَمَ · interpretation]
- 17:70/q24/p1 They are particular examples of a broader divine honor and preference. — Abū Ḥayyān says exegetical examples do not exhaust human distinctions. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the details in two reports are particular instances, as one report itself concludes. Ṭabrisī says God treated humanity as honored, an emphatic description of His dealings. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; TABRISI]

## 17:70/q25 How many favors does this verse enumerate?  [* · rhetoric]
- 17:70/q25/p1 Five: dignity, land transport, sea transport, good provision, and superiority over many creatures. — Ibn ʿĀshūr counts land and sea transport separately. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 17:70/q26 Can good provisions arise through human work or without it?  [ٱلطَّيِّبَٰتِ · interpretation]
- 17:70/q26/p1 They include pleasant goods obtained by human work or otherwise. — Bursevī lists butter, dates, honey and sweets as examples. Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī also include both earned and unearned desirable things. [3 notes: BURSEVI(بحر العلوم); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 17:70/q27 How does Abū Ḥayyān assess Rāzī's account of human honor?  [كَرَّمْنَا · interpretation]
- 17:70/q27/p1 He objects that its philosophical mode is foreign to Arabic expression. — Abū Ḥayyān says Rāzī borrows from people called philosophers. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 17:70/q28 What does the cited poetic praise show about apparent restriction?  [كَثِيرٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 17:70/q28/p1 Praising someone as free of immediate indecency or impatience does not imply later vice. — Ṭabrisī quotes Suwayd ibn Abī Kāhil to deny that an apparently limited praise necessarily entails the contrary beyond its terms. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 17:70/q29 What did the angels' prostration to Adam signify?  [بَنِىٓ ءَادَمَ · theology]
- 17:70/q29/p1 It acknowledged the perfection of humanity represented by Adam. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says Adam was their qibla and represented humankind, citing 15:30. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
