# 31:10: 33 questions

## 31:10/q01 What does the creation of the heavens entail?  [خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ · interpretation]
- 31:10/q01/p1 God originated the seven heavens. — Muqātil specifies seven; Ṭabrisī says God brought them into being; Bursevī adds the Throne and Footstool. [4 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; TABRISI; BURSEVI]
- 31:10/q01/p2 The heavens exist by God's power and choice, not by necessity or nature. — Al-Rāzī says this holds whether they are spherical or flat; their place in limitless space depends on divine choice. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 31:10/q02 Are the heavens flat or spherical?  [ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether reports require flatness or observation and 21:33 favor circularity.
- 31:10/q02/p1 The heavens are spherical. — Al-Rāzī reports astronomers' agreement; al-Ghazālī prefers this on sensory evidence, interpreting contrary reports and citing 21:33 for circularity. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(جميع المهندسين); RAZI-FULL(الغزالي)+]
- 31:10/q02/p2 The heavens are spread flat like a plate. — Al-Rāzī attributes this to most commentators. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(أكثر المفسرين)]

## 31:10/q03 Do the heavens have pillars, and what does تَرَوْنَهَا describe?  [بِغَيْرِ عَمَدٍۢ تَرَوْنَهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether tarawnahā describes the heavens or restricts ʿamad, and what unseen support denotes.
- 31:10/q03/p1 The heavens have no pillars; people see them standing without supports. — Muqātil and al-Ḥasan read fronting. Ibn ʿAṭiyya makes tarawnahā circumstantial accusative of the heavens or an independent nominative clause, preferring this majority view. Al-Kashshāf calls visibility evidence. Ṭabrisī says physical supports would be visible, need support themselves, and cause regress. Al-Qurṭubī reports ʿAlī ibn Sulaymān and Makkī preferring a new sentence. Al-Ālūsī says vision is visual. Al-Qushayrī invokes God's decree, will and governance; al-Biqāʿī sees sheer power. [23 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); TAB-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن والناس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ, Z); IBNATIYYA-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL(علي بن سليمان ومكي)+; TABRISI+]
- 31:10/q03/p2 The heavens have unseen pillars or supports. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid and ʿIkrima report them. Tarawnahā can qualify ʿamad in the genitive; hidden support may be God's power and will. Al-Biqāʿī sees subtle wisdom. Al-Riḍā likens joined heaven and earth to interwoven fingers, citing 51:7 and 13:2. Iṣlāḥī explicitly makes 'you can see' qualify 'pillars,' leaving the pillars invisible; he identifies the universe's unseen bonds with gravitational laws known fully to God. [26 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة ومجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وابن عباس); TABRISI(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس وعكرمة ومجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI(الرضا عليه السلام); STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ, Z); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 31:10/q03/p3 God's special people are metaphysical supports of the heavens and world order. — Bursevī says their departure without successors near Resurrection ends visible world order; al-Ālūsī says the perfect-human claim has no Qurʾānic or Sunna basis. [1 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 31:10/q04 What does عَمَدٍ mean?  [عَمَدٍۢ · meaning]
- 31:10/q04/p1 Pillars or supports, the plural of ʿimād. — Al-Nashr describes a tall firm structure preventing an elevated object from leaning. Bursevī and al-Ālūsī say the plural suits multiple heavens; al-Ālūsī compares أهب/إهاب. [4 notes: NASAFI-FULL; NASHR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 31:10/q05 How is عَمَدٍ read here?  [عَمَدٍۢ · readings]
- 31:10/q05/p1 All readers give both letters fatḥa. — Al-Nashr identifies the form as plural of ʿimād. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 31:10/q06 Do the heavens occupy a place?  [ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ · theology]
turns on: What counts as place.
- 31:10/q06/p1 They occupy a chosen position within limitless space without physical support. — Al-Rāzī says divine power keeps them there. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 31:10/q06/p2 The heavens as a whole have no place if place means a surface on which they rest. — Al-Rāzī reports this distinction between position in air and resting on earth. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 31:10/q07 Does the verse establish that the heavens are immobile?  [ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ · interpretation]
- 31:10/q07/p1 It does not establish heavenly immobility. — Al-Ālūsī rejects the inference attributed to Pythagoras's followers and their further doctrines. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 31:10/q08 Who is addressed by تَرَوْنَهَا and بِكُمْ?  [تَرَوْنَهَا بِكُمْ · referent]
- 31:10/q08/p1 The polytheists. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the direct address in both expressions. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 31:10/q09 What are رَوَٰسِىَ?  [رَوَٰسِىَ · meaning]
- 31:10/q09/p1 Firmly fixed mountains. — Bursevī derives rawāsī from rāsiya and rasa, become fixed; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī add loftiness. Al-Qushayrī recognizes this outward meaning. [12 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 31:10/q10 What does أَلْقَىٰ mean?  [وَأَلْقَىٰ · meaning]
- 31:10/q10/p1 Cast or place where something can be found and seen; later, simply place. — Bursevī explains the lexical development. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 31:10/q11 Why describe mountains as cast into the earth?  [وَأَلْقَىٰ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ رَوَٰسِىَ · rhetoric]
- 31:10/q11/p1 Their casting makes even vast mountains easy before God's greatness. — Al-Biqāʿī remarks that familiar mountains are below; Bursevī compares tossing pebbles and cites God's command 'Be'. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 31:10/q12 What does أَن تَمِيدَ بِكُمْ mean, and how is it construed?  [أَن تَمِيدَ بِكُمْ · grammar]
- 31:10/q12/p1 The mountains were placed lest earth sway, shake, or move with inhabitants. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya defines mayd as side-to-side movement; Bursevī says it describes a great body's motion and bāʾ makes it transitive. Al-Ṭabarī cites anonymous rajaz 'أبى أن يزال ملهبا', glossed lā yazāl. Ṭabrisī and al-Rāzī allow aversion or purpose; al-Qurṭubī calls it accusative aversion and reports the Kūfan 'lest'. Al-Naqqāsh says move away; Yaḥyā says move with. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 16:15. [15 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(النقاش,يحيى بن سلام); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 31:10/q13 How do the mountains stabilize the earth, and why does that matter?  [رَوَٰسِىَ أَن تَمِيدَ بِكُمْ · interpretation]
- 31:10/q13/p1 They anchor earth so creatures can live and grow on stable ground. — Al-Ṭabarī says they stop sideward shifts; Qatāda says creatures otherwise lack a settled surface. Ibn Kathīr says earth lies over water. Al-Rāzī says heaviness resists water and winds, while shifting land defeats crops, travel and grazing; seeds need stable ground. Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursevī say earth's similar parts would change positions. Al-Ālūsī adds innumerable other purposes, waters and winds, and reports theories of minute center-of-gravity shifts from moving heavy bodies or large seas formed by water and windblown sand. Bursevī quotes Saʿdī's water-and-pegs poem; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ sees a direct earthquake link. [30 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(سعدى); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 31:10/q14 What happened when earth first moved and God fixed it?  [وَأَلْقَىٰ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ رَوَٰسِىَ · reports]
turns on: The number of mountains and sequence of stronger things differ.
- 31:10/q14/p1 Mountains anchored the moving earth; angels learned stronger things ending with a human. — Yaḥyā's al-Ḥasan report gives mountain, iron, fire, water, wind, human. Al-Māwardī's al-Ḥasan report has angels fear no creature could survive and names wind and Adam's child after mountains, omitting middle links. Bursevī notes great prior upheaval. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); BURSEVI]
- 31:10/q14/p2 Nineteen mountains branching through earth stabilized it like pegs. — Al-Māwardī likens the rocking to a ship; Bursevī cites al-Ḍaḥḥāk through al-Muwaḍḍiḥ, naming Qāf, Abū Qubays, Jūdī, Lebanon and Sinai. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BURSEVI(ضحاك)]
- 31:10/q14/p3 Some reports count more than nineteen mountains. — Bursevī refers to his discussion under al-Ḥijr. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 31:10/q15 What inward meaning do the fixed mountains have?  [رَوَٰسِىَ · inward]
turns on: Different inward analogies.
- 31:10/q15/p1 They are spiritual substitutes and pillars aiding creation and warding off calamity. — Al-Qushayrī sets this beside the outward sense. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 31:10/q15/p2 They are the earth's bones and veins in a cosmic body. — Bursevī reports some saying bones and veins; Sufis make sun and moon its eyes and stars lights in subtle veins, known by unveiling rather than philosophy. [2 notes: BURSEVI(أهل السلوك,بعضهم)]

## 31:10/q16 Is the earth spherical?  [ٱلْأَرْضِ · interpretation]
- 31:10/q16/p1 Earth is spherical. — Al-Ālūsī accepts al-Ghazālī's view, mentions Ibn ʿAbbās and philosophers, and cites sense and inference. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 31:10/q17 What does بَثَّ mean?  [وَبَثَّ · meaning]
- 31:10/q17/p1 Create, bring into view, scatter, and spread. — Yaḥyā and al-Suddī gloss create; al-Kalbī extends; al-Māwardī, al-Nasafī and Bursevī emphasize dispersal. Bursevī compares wind scattering dust and a soul disclosing grief; al-Ālūsī cites 56:6 and 101:4. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL(السدي,الكلبي); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 31:10/q18 What does دَآبَّةٍ encompass?  [دَآبَّةٍۢ · meaning]
- 31:10/q18/p1 Every kind of moving animal or creature on earth. — Al-Māwardī derives the name from motion; Bursevī says dabb/dabīb is light walking, especially insects but used generally. Ibn Kathīr notes forms and colors known only to God; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ specifies crawlers; Study Quran includes all animals. [13 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 31:10/q19 What does spreading animals show or accomplish?  [وَبَثَّ فِيهَا مِن كُلِّ دَآبَّةٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Provision, knowledge, and manifestation are emphasized.
- 31:10/q19/p1 It manifests mercy and provision. — Al-Biqāʿī places dispersal after stable settlement; al-Rāzī says stability enables travel and grazing; Study Quran reports al-Ṭabarī on nourishment with 2:164; Ibn ʿĀshūr lists food, milk, wool, hides, transport and sea creatures. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 31:10/q19/p2 It displays God's knowledge and power. — Study Quran reports Ibn Kathīr; Ibn Kathīr says only the Creator knows animal variety. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 31:10/q19/p3 It reveals God bringing absent things into existence. — Bursevī draws this implication from bathth. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 31:10/q20 What are the مَآءً from ٱلسَّمَآءِ?  [وَأَنزَلْنَا مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ مَآءًۭ · meaning]
- 31:10/q20/p1 Rain descending from above or from clouds. — Bursevī says heaven is what is above and shades; al-Ālūsī says upper direction, allowing a figurative canopy only if observation demands it. Al-Biqāʿī says no creature can produce rain. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 31:10/q21 Why shift to first-person plural in أَنزَلْنَا and أَنۢبَتْنَا?  [وَأَنزَلْنَا فَأَنۢبَتْنَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Emphasis, divine agency, and pronoun limits are given.
- 31:10/q21/p1 It emphasizes divine care in recurring rain and growth. — Al-Rāzī calls this refreshing iltifāt, illustrating with speakers and Bakr. Bursevī calls the plural majestic; al-Ālūsī stresses recurrence sustaining animals; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls blessing widely felt; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ sees concern; Elmalılı relates it to majesty, wisdom and oneness. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 31:10/q21/p2 Attributing rain directly to God discourages a merely natural account and prompts gratitude. — Al-Rāzī says water neither chooses nor naturally rises, unlike animals moving; recurrent rain elicits gratitude and increased mercy. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 31:10/q21/p3 Divine pronouns accommodate human speech about infinite God. — Asad says finite pronouns cannot contain God. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 31:10/q22 What grows through rain in فَأَنۢبَتْنَا?  [فَأَنۢبَتْنَا فِيهَا مِن كُلِّ زَوْجٍۢ كَرِيمٍ · referent]
turns on: Vegetation, all natural kinds, or humans from earth.
- 31:10/q22/p1 Every kind of plant, vegetation, tree, or crop. — Al-Ṭabarī ties rain to plants; al-Māwardī names trees and crops; Yaḥyā adds every color; Ibn ʿĀshūr includes grain, fruit, fodder, truffles. [14 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 31:10/q22/p2 Growth encompasses animals, plants, and minerals. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya names all three. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 31:10/q22/p3 It can refer to people grown from earth, noble if destined for Paradise, base if for Hell. — Al-Shaʿbī's view occurs in al-Māwardī, al-Qurṭubī and Ibn Kathīr. Al-Qurṭubī adds semen from soil, supported by apparent Qurʾānic wording. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الشعبي); QURTUBI-FULL(الشعبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الشعبي)]

## 31:10/q23 What does فِيهَا refer to?  [فِيهَا · referent]
- 31:10/q23/p1 Earth. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives the antecedent; Asad renders it 'thereon'. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 31:10/q24 What does زَوْجٍ mean here?  [زَوْجٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Species classification or pairing.
- 31:10/q24/p1 A kind, class, or species. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya rules out opposite of an individual; al-Rāzī says species divide in pairs, trees/non-trees and fruit-bearing/non-fruit-bearing; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 20:53 and 22:5; Asad cites 26:7. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 31:10/q24/p2 It alludes to pairing in plants and in all things. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ refers to plant pairing; Bursevī says everything pairs by opposite, likeness or composition, such as substance/accident or matter/form. [2 notes: TABATABAI; BURSEVI]

## 31:10/q25 What makes a زَوْجٍ كَرِيمٍ noble?  [زَوْجٍۢ كَرِيمٍ · meaning]
turns on: Beauty, benefit, ripeness, or worth.
- 31:10/q25/p1 It is beautiful, fine, or excellent in appearance and ordered formation. — Yaḥyā mentions colors; Qatāda and Ibn ʿAbbās say good or beautiful. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān distinguish every kind's ordered craft from select beautiful things. Bursevī quotes al-Mufradāt on excellence in one's field; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 27:29's precious book. [13 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(المفردات); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 31:10/q25/p2 It is useful, beneficial, or generous in yield. — Ibn ʿĪsā says good fruit, Ṭabrisī pleasant fruit, al-Bayḍāwī plentiful benefit; Bursevī, al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ say useful; al-Rāzī and Study Quran allow production without reckoning or return; al-Biqāʿī adds freshness and animal sustenance. Iṣlāḥī calls 'beneficial' the precise linguistic sense of 'noble' here and connects that benefit to God's mercy and providence. [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(المفردات); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 31:10/q25/p3 It is ripe. — Al-Māwardī reports Ibn Kāmil. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن كامل)]
- 31:10/q25/p4 It is valuable or esteemed. — Al-Māwardī suggests high price; al-Rāzī says honored, opposite despised; Ibn ʿĀshūr says precious within its kind and cites 27:29. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 31:10/q26 What do rain and plant growth provide?  [مَآءًۭ فَأَنۢبَتْنَا · interpretation]
- 31:10/q26/p1 Nourishment and care for inhabited earth. — Al-Biqāʿī says rain feeds and plants sustain animals; al-Rāzī says stable seeds germinate; Ibn ʿĀshūr lists grain, fruit, fodder and truffles. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 31:10/q27 What inward meaning is given to rain?  [وَأَنزَلْنَا مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ مَآءًۭ · inward]
- 31:10/q27/p1 Rain from inward heaven nourishes gardens of people near and present with God. — Al-Qushayrī contrasts outward rain feeding verdant gardens. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 31:10/q28 Where is the heavens-without-visible-pillars wording paralleled?  [بِغَيْرِ عَمَدٍۢ تَرَوْنَهَا · links]
- 31:10/q28/p1 At 13:2 in Sūrat al-Raʿd. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Samīn, Elmalılı, Asad and Ibn ʿĀshūr refer there. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ uses it for both construals; Study Quran for pronoun dispute; al-Riḍā joins it with 51:7. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الرضا عليه السلام); ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ, Z)]

## 31:10/q29 Where is the mountain-stabilizing wording paralleled?  [رَوَٰسِىَ أَن تَمِيدَ بِكُمْ · links]
- 31:10/q29/p1 At 16:15 in Sūrat al-Naḥl. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Asad and Study Quran point there. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]

## 31:10/q30 Where is the dispersal of creatures paralleled?  [وَبَثَّ فِيهَا مِن كُلِّ دَآبَّةٍۢ · links]
- 31:10/q30/p1 At 2:164 in al-Baqara. — Ibn ʿĀshūr notes the paired mention of creatures and wind patterns; Study Quran cites it on provision. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]

## 31:10/q31 Where are the rain and plant descriptions paralleled?  [وَأَنزَلْنَا مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ مَآءًۭ مِن كُلِّ زَوْجٍۢ كَرِيمٍ · links]
- 31:10/q31/p1 Rain resembles 2:164 and 13:17; plants recall 20:53, 22:5, and 26:7. — Ibn ʿĀshūr names the first four; Qatāda and Asad cite 26:7. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ASAD-NOTES]

## 31:10/q32 What do the signs in this verse establish about God?  [* · theology]
- 31:10/q32/p1 Creation shows power, wisdom, knowledge, mercy, and God's sole right to divinity. — Al-Bayḍāwī says might and wisdom show perfect power and knowledge; al-Ālūsī rebukes idolatry; Ibn ʿĀshūr says idols create nothing and lack wisdom, and useful creatures show mercy. Ibn Kathīr stresses God's knowledge of animals; al-Biqāʿī cites precise acts. Iṣlāḥī specifically takes the noble, beneficial growth as a sign of God's mercy and providence. [13 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]

## 31:10/q33 How does the verse fit its sūra's surrounding argument?  [* · links]
turns on: Connections differ among attributes, Qurʾān rejection, Luqmān, and 31:27.
- 31:10/q33/p1 Creation demonstrates the preceding divine might and wisdom and prepares oneness. — Al-Biqāʿī cites precision; Elmalılı cites direct divine speech; al-Ālūsī rebukes idolatry; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it 'proof of proof': thing, evidence, and evidence for evidence. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 31:10/q33/p2 Creation supports God's promise against the buyer who derides the Qurʾān. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ links arrogant recitation and promises of Paradise or punishment; Iṣlāḥī challenges alleged deities to show their creation. [2 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 31:10/q33/p3 If Quraysh asked about Luqmān and his son, these verses through 31:12 introduce his wisdom. — Ibn ʿĀshūr presents this conditionally; Iṣlāḥī warns fathers who pressure sons from Islam, echoing 29:8. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 31:10/q33/p4 A preceding Jewish claim about the Torah's small share of divine knowledge led to the trees-as-pens verse. — Abū Ḥayyān reports the exchange with the Prophet and connects it to 31:27. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
