# 87:9/q07 How should reminders be repeated after an initial call? [فَذَكِّرْ إِن نَّفَعَتِ ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · law]
turns on: The distinction between first delivery and repetition, and whether lack of future benefit can be known.
- 87:9/q07/p1 The first call is obligatory; repetition can cease after proof and entrenched refusal.
  reasons: Rāzī distinguishes an obligatory universal first call from further repetition; Ālūsī says God disclosed sealed hearts to the Prophet; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the first reminder completes proof; Kur'an Yolu cites 53:29 for turning away from mockers; Elmalılı says harmful repetition may be left.
  holders (13 notes): RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); ISLAHI-TADABBUR

- 87:9/q07/p2 Keep calling universally because humans cannot know that someone will never benefit.
  reasons: Biqāʿī invokes unseen spiritual opportunities and the Prophet's refusal to despair; he reads turning away as ending grief, not the call. Ibn ʿĀshūr says even Abū Jahl was invited.

  reasons: Rāzī and his fuller version both explicitly defer the measure to custom.

# 87:9/q08 What teaching practice does the verse support? [فَذَكِّرْ إِن نَّفَعَتِ ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · law]
- 87:9/q08/p1 Teach what each hearer can grasp and needs.
  reasons: Ibn Kathīr cites two sayings of ʿAlī: incomprehensible teaching can be a trial, and people may then accuse God and His Messenger of falsehood. ʿIṣām al-Dīn says call disbelievers first to faith and remind a believer neglecting prayer about prayer.

# 87:9/q09 How is the conditional sentence attached grammatically? [إِن نَّفَعَتِ ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the imperative supplies the response or the clause is an aside.
- 87:9/q09/p1 The command supplies the omitted answer: if it benefits, remind.
  reasons: Ṭabrisī and Nasafī explicitly analyze the response as supplied by the preceding imperative.
  holders (2 notes): TABRISI; NASAFI
  notes: TABRISI:87:7-9/r14 NASAFI:87:8/r5
- 87:9/q09/p2 The clause is parenthetical and does not restrict the general command.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Prophet cannot know beforehand who benefits and objects only to explicitly supplying al-Farrāʾ's omitted words.
  holders (2 notes): IBNASHUR
  notes: IBNASHUR:87:9/r4 IBNASHUR:87:9/r8
sources: IBNASHUR = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393; NASAFI = al-Nasafī, d. 710; TABRISI = al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548

# 87:9/q10 Why use إن and the feminine verb? [إِن نَّفَعَتِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the form marks uncertain reception or emphasizes the value of reminding.
- 87:9/q10/p1 In hints some will not benefit; the feminine verb marks even a weak chance.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr points to 87:10 as the answer to the hint; Biqāʿī says faint hope is enough and links the feminine form with weakness.
  holders (3 notes): IBNASHUR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL
  notes: IBNASHUR:87:9/r7 BIQAI:87:9/r5 BIQAI-FULL:v8p397/r7
- 87:9/q10/p2 The condition presents the favorable case to strengthen or ease the call.
  reasons: Rāzī says certainty of attaining an end motivates action and naming the better case can imply its opposite; Elmalılı says the condition emphasizes the command while easing it.

# 87:9/q13 What does the report about Moses and Pharaoh illustrate? [* · reports]
- 87:9/q13/p1 The duty to invite is distinct from God's knowledge that the hearer will not heed.
  reasons: Rāzī reports that God commanded Moses to speak gently to Pharaoh while affirming that Pharaoh would neither remember nor fear, citing 20:44; he says the command does not imply uncertainty.
