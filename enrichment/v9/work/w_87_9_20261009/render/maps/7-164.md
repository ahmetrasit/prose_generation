# 7:164: 20 questions

## 7:164/q01 How does the opening 'and when' connect grammatically to the preceding account?  [وَإِذْ · grammar]
- 7:164/q01/p1 It is coordinated with the earlier 'when they transgressed,' continuing the Sabbath episode. — Al-Razi and al-Nasafi give it the same construction as the earlier idh. Al-Baydawi, Bursevi, Elmalılı and Ibn Ashur connect it to 7:163; Ibn Ashur takes idh as a past-time noun in 'ask them about the time.' Al-Tibi and al-Taftazani object to attaching it to the nearer 'when they came,' since that would count the questioners among transgressors. Al-Alusi says treating it as a substitute strains the chronology: the exchange followed the violation, and stretching 'time' to a year is forced. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي والتفتازاني)]

## 7:164/q02 To whom does 'among them' refer?  [مِّنْهُمْ · referent]
- 7:164/q02/p1 It refers to the Israelites whom Muhammad is told to question. — Ibn Ashur says the passage rebukes Israelites generally; the town is only the episode's setting. He says its exact location is incidental and that Israelite scholars and righteous people knew the story. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:164/q03 Who were the group asking why the people were admonished?  [أُمَّةٌۭ مِّنْهُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'perhaps they may fear' addresses the offenders indirectly as a third party, and whether those who ceased warning count as a distinct faction.
- 7:164/q03/p1 They were a third group who kept the Sabbath but ceased or never undertook admonition; the warners and violators were separate groups. — Ibn Abbas, Qatada and many commentators enumerate three factions, though Qatada describes the abstainers as revering God and one Ibn Abbas account places questioners within the single spared, warning faction. Al-Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, al-Qurtubi, al-Baghawi and Abu Hayyan argue from 'your Lord' followed by 'they may fear': the questioners cannot be the addressed violators. Elmalılı and Ibn Ashur say these formerly admonished, then despaired; al-Tabataba'i says they continued associating with offenders. Asad calls them passive bystanders. Al-Wahidi transmits al-Kalbi's variant count of about 70,000 offenders and 12,000 warners. [46 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل التفسير); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور المفسرين); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(community); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي)+; THALABI+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 7:164/q03/p2 The questioners were the Sabbath offenders themselves, challenging or mocking the people who warned them; there were two factions. — Al-Kalbi and al-Zajjaj transmit two groups, and al-Kalbi estimates roughly 70,000 offenders against 12,000 admonishers. Al-Tabari, al-Tha'labi, al-Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, al-Razi and Abu Hayyan report this alternative. Al-Alusi finds it unlikely: a switch of addressee or mimicking the offenders' self-description is needed to explain 'they may fear.' [12 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(الكلبي); TAB-FULL(الكلبي); THALABI(آخرون); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,الكلبي والزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن الكلبي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 7:164/q03/p3 They were righteous people who had warned the sinners earlier and now asked fellow warners why to continue. — Al-Razi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, al-Biqa'i, Bursevi, Elmalılı and Ibn Ashur describe righteous questioners who had exhausted admonition and despaired; al-Alusi reports an exchange among righteous preachers. Ibn Abbas's one-faction report has some spared warners asking the question. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:164/q04 Where was the episode set, and how is its setting described?  [أُمَّةٌۭ مِّنْهُمْ · reports]
turns on: The transmitted place lists and the differing accounts of the assigned worship day.
- 7:164/q04/p1 It took place at Aylah, where fish appeared on Sabbaths and disappeared afterward. — Ibn Abbas locates Aylah between Egypt and Medina; Ikrima names two stone idols there, Laqim and Laqmana, to which fish came, and says the fish were reachable. Another Ibn Abbas report says they were white and fat like pregnant camels. The account was already known to Jews of Muhammad's time, according to Kur'an Yolu. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 7:164/q04/p2 The Sabbath fish test involved Aylah, Midian and al-Tur. — Ibn Abbas says God had assigned Israel Friday, but they chose Saturday; fish appeared visibly at these places on Saturdays and vanished afterward. Another Ibn Abbas report instead describes an assigned Monday changed to Saturday, after which Moses forbade work and a woodcutter was stoned. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 7:164/q05 How did Sabbath fishing begin and spread?  [قَوْمًا · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on the first violator's device and on Satan's proposed distinction between catching and eating.
- 7:164/q05/p1 A man tied or tethered fish on the Sabbath, recovered it later, and others copied the loophole. — Ibn Abbas, Ibn Zayd and Ibn Ruman report concealment or retrieval the next day; Ibn Ruman says the man taught others. Ibn Kathir reports one man openly eating the fish later while most failed to object. Only a remnant continued warnings after the practice became public, according to Ibn Abbas. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن رومان,ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن رومان,ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 7:164/q05/p2 Satan induced people to catch fish on Saturday and eat it later by claiming only eating on Saturday was banned. — Ibn Abbas reports both Satan's suggestion and an opposing group who said eating, frightening, and catching fish were all forbidden. Al-Baqir's report from Ali's book likewise says some exploited catching now and eating later; Ikrima says villagers adopted that plan and catches increased. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,طائفة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام)]
- 7:164/q05/p3 The offenders were repeatedly warned of God's prohibition and punishment but did not heed. — Muqatil says warnings concerned fish and punishment; Ibn Abbas reports warners saying God forbade the practice, then condemning the persistent offenders. Al-Suddi says warnings had already failed. [3 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس)]

## 7:164/q06 How is the interrogative 'why' formed?  [لِمَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā is the interrogative whose alif is deleted or a prepositional form analysed with a pause hāʾ.
- 7:164/q06/p1 It is li plus interrogative mā, whose alif is dropped after the preposition. — Al-Zajjaj compares famā and ʿam and cites 15:54 and 78:1; al-Tabrisi compares mim, fim, ʿalām and ʿam. Al-Nahhas says the deletion marks the interrogative. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; TABRISI]
- 7:164/q06/p2 An alternative analysis takes mā as a preposition with a hāʾ added at pause to preserve its vowel. — Al-Nahhas records this secondary parsing. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 7:164/q07 Why did the questioners ask about continued admonition?  [لِمَ تَعِظُونَ قَوْمًا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the questioners had become pessimistic about reform or were the violators mocking advice.
- 7:164/q07/p1 They judged warnings futile after repeated failure and expected the offenders to reject them and meet punishment. — Al-Zajjaj calls this their predominant estimate, not certain knowledge; Ibn Atiyya appeals to likelihood and God's earlier treatment of sinful nations. Al-Jubba'i says they despaired of acceptance. Al-Tabataba'i says the offenders were openly defiant. Ibn Ashur calls the question a censure denying a reason for admonition. Al-Alusi says its indirect interrogative form can express astonishment at apparent futility or ask about wisdom and benefit; either faction might speak before sinners to instil fear. Bursevi says the questioners neither approved sin nor rejected admonition itself. [23 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 7:164/q07/p2 The offenders mocked or challenged the warners themselves. — The two-faction reports put the question in the sinners' mouths; al-Tha'labi says they had already heard warnings of certain punishment, and Ibn Atiyya calls it mockery. Al-Alusi considers this identity and motive unlikely because of the third-person pronoun. [7 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(آخرون); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 7:164/q08 What does the threatened destruction or severe punishment mean?  [ٱللَّهُ مُهْلِكُهُمْ أَوْ مُعَذِّبُهُمْ عَذَابًۭا شَدِيدًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the contrast is between worldly destruction and afterlife punishment, or total destruction and a lesser punishment.
- 7:164/q08/p1 Destruction is in this world, and the alternative severe punishment is in the Hereafter. — Al-Tabari, al-Zajjaj, Ibn Atiyya, al-Tabrisi and al-Baydawi contrast the two realms; al-Baydawi attributes the latter to persistent disobedience. Al-Alusi reports this interpretation, adding that 'or' leaves either possible and the active participles portray future punishment as virtually present. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:164/q08/p2 Destruction means eradicating the offenders, while the alternative is severe punishment without total eradication. — Al-Razi and al-Baydawi gloss muhlikuhum as removing or annihilating them; Bursevi says cleansing the land. Abu Hayyan reports both senses, and al-Alusi gives non-exterminating severe punishment as the alternative. Bursevi takes the questioners to mean a worldly punishment. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:164/q08/p3 The alternatives leave which of two certain future punishments will occur undetermined. — Ibn Ashur says the active participles refer to the future. Al-Alusi similarly says they present the outcome as certain or imminent. Al-Baghawi offers the wording as a question about whether the hearers know God will punish severely. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL]

## 7:164/q09 What does maʿdhira mean?  [مَعْذِرَةً · meaning]
- 7:164/q09/p1 It is an excuse or exculpation, an act or proof offered to remove blame. — Abu Zayd lists ʿadhartuhu, aʿdhiruhu, ʿudhr, maʿdhira and ʿudhrā; Ibn al-A'rabi calls these an exit from blame or sin. Al-Wahidi describes upholding or accepting an excuse; al-Azhari treats maʿdhira as a mafʿila noun for apology, and Ibn Ashur an irregular mimetic verbal noun from iʿtadhara taking ilā to convey it. Al-Tabrisi distinguishes the truly excused muʿdhir from the negligent pretender muʿadhdhir and notes that a muʿtadhir may or may not truly have an excuse. Bursevi describes denial, explanation or promise to stop as modes of excusing oneself; a promise to stop is repentance, but not every excuse is repentance. Al-Sa'di's poem contrasts a stern summons with an opportunity for excuse and forgiveness. [16 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو زيد,ابن الأعرابي,الأزهري); RAZI-FULL(أبو زيد,الأزهري); TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR(الأزهري); BURSEVI(السعدي); ALUSI-FULL(الأزهري); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:164/q10 How is maʿdhira read and construed in the answer?  [قَالُوا۟ مَعْذِرَةً · readings]
turns on: Whether the answer asserts that the admonition itself is an excuse, or expresses its purpose or a newly offered apology; the transmission of Asim varies by route.
- 7:164/q10/p1 It is nominative: 'our admonition is an excuse,' usually a predicate of an omitted subject. — Ibn Mujahid and Abu Ali list Ibn Kathir, Nafi', Abu Amr, Ibn Amir, Hamza and al-Kisa'i, with a route from Abu Bakr from Asim; most Hijazi, Kufan and Basran readers also have nominative. Sibawayh and al-Farra prefer it as the actual answer: the speech itself is the excuse, unlike a new personal apology. Al-Farra compares 'illa sa'atun min nahar balagh'; Abu Ali compares the nominative hanan in poetry; Ibn Khalawayh permits an omitted demonstrative, like 24:1. Qiraat-ER also permits nominative as a subject, 'an excuse is our motive.' Al-Farra notes that ordinary Arabic often has accusative here; al-Qurtubi explicitly prefers nominative. [29 notes: SIBAWAYH; FARRA; TAB-FULL(عامة قراء الحجاز والكوفة والبصرة); IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير، نافع، أبو عمرو، ابن عامر، حمزة، الكسائي,عاصم عن أبي بكر، حفص عن عاصم); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي); TABRISI; JISHUMI(باقي القراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير، نافع، أبو عمرو، ابن عامر، حمزة، الكسائي); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج,حفص عن عاصم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور,زيد بن علي وعاصم وعيسى بن عمر وطلحة بن مصرف,سيبويه); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قرّاء); NASHR(الباقون); ALUSI-FULL(من عدا حفص والمفضل); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور وحفص عن عاصم); QIRAAT-ER; FARRA+; NAHHAS(سيبويه)+; QURTUBI-FULL(سيبويه)+; SAMIN-DURR(سيبويه)+]
- 7:164/q10/p2 It is accusative: 'we admonished as an excuse,' parsed as purpose, cognate verbal noun, or object of 'said.' — Hafs from Asim reads accusative; reports also name al-Mufaddal, Zayd ibn Ali, Isa ibn Umar and Talha ibn Musarrif, and a Husayn al-Ju'fi route from Abu Bakr. Al-Kisa'i, al-Zajjaj and others supply a verb such as 'we did this' or 'we apologize'; al-Samin prefers a purpose complement among the accusative analyses, and also reports an object of 'said' since apology entails speech. Sibawayh's accusative example is a fresh direct apology; Ibn Atiyya distinguishes it from the verse. Al-Farra accepts the accusative as valid, despite preferring nominative. ## 7:164/q11 Why did the admonishers continue warning as an excuse to God?  [قَالُوا۟ مَعْذِرَةً إِلَىٰ رَبِّكُمْ · interpretation]
- 7:164/q11/p1 Warning discharges their duty to command right and forbid wrong, so they can answer God and avoid blame for silence. — Al-Tabari, al-Zajjaj, al-Wahidi and later commentators state the duty; Ibn Abbas defines the excuse as disapproval of the deeds. Al-Biqa'i says they could report making every effort. Elmalılı calls it a communal obligation while sinners live, which abandonment would make all culpable. Al-Tabataba'i says they thereby showed God they did not approve the sin; Kur'an Yolu says people are answerable for effort, whereas guidance belongs to God (2:272, 16:37, 28:56). Ibn Ashur says continued warning was a precaution even when another group despaired. Muqatil adds the hope of deferment or rescue. Al-Umari says enjoining right remains an excuse even when the hearer will not accept. [39 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الناهون); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير، نافع، أبو عمرو، ابن عامر، حمزة، الكسائي); RAZI-FULL(أبو زيد); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عبد الله بن عبد العزيز العمري,عكرمة); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(admonishers)]

## 7:164/q12 Why does the reply say 'your Lord'?  [رَبِّكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 7:164/q12/p1 It addresses the questioners as fellow people accountable to God and indirectly rebukes their failure to warn. — Al-Tabataba'i says the duty belongs to the questioners too; al-Alusi calls the answer a wise redirection from the expected reply. Al-Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan use this second-person address together with 'they may fear' to distinguish the audience from the sinners. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)]

## 7:164/q13 What did the admonishers hope 'they may fear' would accomplish?  [وَلَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَّقُونَ · interpretation]
- 7:164/q13/p1 They hoped the offenders would fear God, repent and desist from Sabbath sin, perhaps escaping or delaying punishment. — Ibn Zayd glosses the clause simply as leaving the current practice; Muqatil says avoiding sins and possibly obtaining deferment or rescue. Al-Tabari adds return to obedience and repentance; Ibn Kathir mentions mercy. Al-Wahidi and Ibn al-Jawzi allow even a possible benefit; al-Baydawi says despair becomes certain only upon destruction. Ibn Atiyya calls la'alla genuine human hope; Bursevi says the hope is coordinated with the excuse and can concern even some piety because clear truth may persuade rational people. Elmalılı says future response is unknown and despair impermissible. Ibn Ashur says repetition might work; Study Quran relates fear to reverence for God and Mosaic Law. [39 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,السدي); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(admonishers)]

## 7:164/q14 What happened to the people who questioned further warnings?  [أُمَّةٌۭ مِّنْهُمْ · reports]
turns on: Whether their objection counts as resistance to sin or as culpable silence, and whether 7:165's silence about them establishes survival, doom or uncertainty.
- 7:164/q14/p1 They survived along with the admonishers; only Sabbath offenders were punished. — Ikrima argues that questioners disliked and opposed the sin; reports say Ibn Abbas accepted this and gave him a cloak or two coarse garments. Al-Tha'labi argues the third-person 'they may fear' places the exchange among believers; al-Qurtubi cites 7:165 and 2:65, which name wrongdoers alone as punished. Al-Hasan, Ibn Kathir and others accept their survival; Ibn Kathir prefers Ibn Abbas's later acceptance despite a good chain for the contrary report. Bursevi says certain despair would be needed to condemn the questioners. Al-Tabataba'i rejects Ikrima's inference: nonparticipation does not cancel the graver neglect of forbidding evil. [22 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,بعضهم,عكرمة); THALABI(ابن عباس,بعضهم,يمان بن رئاب); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الإسلام); TABATABAI(عكرمة); IBNKATHIR-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; THALABI+; TABATABAI-]
- 7:164/q14/p2 They perished with the offenders, although they had not themselves taken the fish. — One Ibn Abbas report says only warners escaped, linking the event to 2:66; another warns that silence at wrongdoing may merit the same fate. Al-Qurtubi records punishment for abandoning warning. Al-Tabataba'i argues that this omission was a graver sin and notes the silent group remained with the offenders while warners withdrew. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون,ابن عباس); THALABI(آخرون); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI]
- 7:164/q14/p3 Their fate is not stated, so it remains unknown. — Ibn Abbas repeatedly says 7:165 names the saved warners and punished wrongdoers but omits the third faction; he wept, feared for later silent witnesses, and wished to know their fate. Ikrima's counterargument subsequently moved him in other reports. Al-Qurtubi calls the unknown-fate reading apparent. Al-Tha'labi simply records disagreement. [17 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة)]
- 7:164/q14/p4 The questioners were turned to stone. — Layth ibn Abi Sulaym transmits this distinct outcome. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ليث بن أبي سليم)]

## 7:164/q15 What happened after the warners withdrew from the Sabbath offenders?  [قَوْمًا · reports]
- 7:164/q15/p1 The offenders became apes; their former neighbors recognized them, and some apes recognized and answered the warners. — Ibn Zayd reports separation by a wall and David's curse; Ibn Abbas and Ikrima report shut doors the next morning, apes of all ages and sexes, weeping, nodding or recognizing named people before fleeing into the wilderness. Al-Baqir says the warners ultimately left town for fear of shared punishment. Ibn Ruman says the town was enclosed and the apes approached former neighbors. One Ibn Abbas report also mentions swine. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن رومان,ابن زيد,ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن رومان,ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام)]

## 7:164/q16 What duty concerning wrongdoing does this verse teach?  [* · law]
turns on: The scope of the duty when warnings appear unlikely to succeed, and the legal import of the fishing loophole.
- 7:164/q16/p1 Believers must forbid wrong and continue sincere guidance even if success seems unlikely; their responsibility is the effort. — Al-Jubba'i qualifies the obligation as applying only while acceptance is not hopeless. Al-Tabataba'i says the warners had not fully despaired, so the duty remained, and charges the silent group with a graver failure. Elmalılı says abandoning a communal duty would implicate everyone and counsel may slow or soften wrong and its punishment. Kur'an Yolu makes the persistent admonishers the ideal, calls especially on learned believers to guide others, and says genuine educators must not assume vice incurable; prophets too answer for effort, while God grants guidance. Al-Umari says admonition remains an excuse even if rejected. [11 notes: TABRISI(الجبائي); DURR-FULL(عبد الله بن عبد العزيز العمري); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 7:164/q16/p2 The episode supports the legal principle of blocking the means to a prohibited act. — Al-Qurtubi refers back to his discussion in al-Baqara. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 7:164/q17 How does this dialogue connect with the surrounding verses and other passages?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connection concerns the episode's immediate continuation or parallel language and legal themes elsewhere.
- 7:164/q17/p1 It continues the Sabbath transgression account and shows persistent Israelite disobedience despite warnings. — Al-Biqa'i and al-Alusi connect it to 7:163; Elmalılı describes the righteous minority's circumstances; al-Tabataba'i situates the episodes in Israel's repeated covenant-breaking and later lax observance. Ibn Ashur says 7:165 supplies the outcome omitted in the compressed exchange. Study Quran sees the return to Israel's later disobedience and future punishment. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:164/q17/p2 Its language or lessons are cross-referenced to other verses on excuse, admonition, rescue and forbidding wrong. — Al-Wahidi points to 9:66 for excuse; Ibn Ashur cites earlier uses of admonition at 4:63 and 7:145. Al-Qurtubi refers to 2:65 on transformation, 3:104 and 5:63 on forbidding wrong, and 4:140 on avoiding corrupt company. Asad compares the true admonishers with 7:159.
