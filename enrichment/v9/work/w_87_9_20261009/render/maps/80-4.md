# 80:4: 19 questions

## 80:4/q01 How does “or” relate remembering to purification?  [أَوْ يَذَّكَّرُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the two clauses name equal routes to benefit or distinguish complete purification from a lesser benefit.
- 80:4/q01/p1 Remembering is an alternative, equally valuable outcome alongside purification. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says purification brings new knowledge through guidance, while remembrance recalls forgotten knowledge; both outcomes matter equally. Al-Suddī says the blind man may be purified and remember, taking the alif as connective or extra. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes purification through good action after reflection and remembrance as awakening to true belief. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); TABATABAI]
- 80:4/q01/p2 Remembrance can yield some benefit even if complete purification is not attained. — Al-Biqāʿī allows even a small step out of unbelief toward purification and says no good should be dismissed. Bursawī says exhortation may help without complete purification. Al-Ālūsī reports that purification can mean total cleansing from sin while remembrance still helps short of that; he calls a proposed obligatory-versus-optional learning distinction weak. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 80:4/q02 Who might remember and benefit here?  [يَذَّكَّرُ فَتَنفَعَهُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun continues to refer to the blind seeker or shifts to the unbeliever whom the Prophet was addressing; al-Kashf adduces the accusative reading for the latter.
- 80:4/q02/p1 The blind visitor, Ibn Umm Maktūm. — Al-Kashshāf says he might learn religious law, shed sin, and heed admonition toward obedience; the Prophet could not foresee this. Al-Ṭabrisī specifies good works, learning, and benefit to his religion. Ibn Kathīr says admonition could deter forbidden acts. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says faith and righteous action purify him. Al-Ālūsī reports that the accusative can reinforce qualified purification on this reading. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; JISHUMI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف)]
- 80:4/q02/p2 The unbeliever whose conversion the Prophet hoped for. — Al-Kashshāf says Islam and admonition might draw him toward truth, though this was unknown. Al-Suhaylī identifies the pagan as al-Walīd or Umayya and says his conversion might bring many others; Ibn Umm Maktūm was already Muslim and had learned the essentials. Al-Kashf says the accusative favors this referent because hope then approaches a wish for an unlikely outcome. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BURSEVI(السهيلي); ALUSI-FULL(الكشف)+]

## 80:4/q03 What does يَذَّكَّرُ mean?  [يَذَّكَّرُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the explanation emphasizes taking admonition or the more specific return of forgotten knowledge.
- 80:4/q03/p1 He may take heed, reflect, and respond to admonition. — Al-Ṭabarī joins remembering with reflection and consideration. Muqātil says presentation of Islam may lead to belief and beneficial admonition. Al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī specify what the Qurʾān teaches; Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Zayd say taking heed. Al-Qurṭubī refers to what the Prophet says. Al-Biqāʿī allows even a small step out of unbelief. Bursawī derives the contracted form from yatadhakkaru. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 80:4/q03/p2 A known matter returns to mind after being forgotten. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says tadhakkur is the effect of reminding and derives it from dhikr, with ḍamma on the dhāl; he contrasts recalling forgotten knowledge with gaining new knowledge through guidance. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 80:4/q04 How is يَذَّكَّرُ formed and read?  [يَذَّكَّرُ · readings]
turns on: Assimilation in yatadhakkaru versus the ungeminated present of dhakara.
- 80:4/q04/p1 With assimilated tāʾ and doubled dhāl and kāf. — Al-Naḥḥās derives it from yatadhakkaru, assimilating tāʾ to nearby dhāl. Abū Ḥayyān attributes doubling of dhāl and kāf to the majority. [3 notes: NAHHAS; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور؛ الأعرج، عاصم في رواية); BURSEVI]
- 80:4/q04/p2 With silent dhāl and light, rounded kāf, as the present of dhakara. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes this to al-Aʿraj and ʿĀṣim; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī specify one transmission from ʿĀṣim. Abū Ḥayyān calls the kāf rounded. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعرج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور؛ الأعرج، عاصم في رواية); SAMIN-DURR(عاصم في رواية والأعرج); ALUSI-FULL(الأعرج وعاصم في رواية)]

## 80:4/q05 How is فَتَنفَعَهُ read and construed?  [فَتَنفَعَهُ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted case ending and, for the accusative, whether fāʾ answers hope, implies an, or permits coordination through aw; Basrans reject the Kufan response-to-laʿalla analysis.
- 80:4/q05/p1 Indicative, coordinated with the preceding indicative verb. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers this and coordinates with yadhdhakkar; al-Zajjāj, Ibn al-Jawzī reporting him, and al-Qurṭubī coordinate with yuzakkā. Others coordinate with yadhdhakkar, or both preceding verbs. Al-Farrāʾ says readers agree on indicative, while later sources report a marked ʿĀṣim exception. Al-Fārisī lists Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, probably Ibn ʿĀmir; other sources call this the common or majority reading. Al-Nashr specifies ḍamma on ʿayn. QIRAAT-ER says the raised final vowel makes the benefit clause sound more independent. Al-Ṭabarī also reports an indicative transmission from ʿĀṣim. [26 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(عاصم); ZAJJAJ; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA(عاصم); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي وأحسب ابن عامر); WAHIDI-BASIT(قراءة العامة); JISHUMI(عاصم); BAGHAWI-FULL(عاصم، والعامة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور السبعة); TABRISI(أبو علي,عاصم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج,حفص عن عاصم); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(غيره); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور، عاصم، الأعرج، أبو حيوة، ابن أبي عبلة); SAMIN-DURR(عاصم); NASHR(الباقون); ALUSI-FULL(الأكثر وعاصم والأعرج وأبو حيوة وابن أبي عبلة والزعفراني); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); QIRAAT-ER(CE); TAB-FULL+]
- 80:4/q05/p2 Subjunctive, as the fāʾ response to hope expressed by laʿalla. — ʿĀṣim is the principal reported reader, though reports differ: Ibn al-Jawzī specifies Ḥafṣ; al-Ṭabrisī excepts al-Aʿshā and al-Barjumī; al-Qurṭubī adds Ibn Abī Isḥāq, ʿĪsā, al-Sulamī, and Zirr; Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds al-Aʿraj; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī add Abū Ḥaywa and Ibn Abī ʿAbla, and al-Ālūsī adds al-Zaʿfarānī. The Kufan analysis answers a nonassertive laʿalla; Makkī compares a wish or question. Al-Farrāʾ allows it though he had not heard it recited thus. Al-Ṭabarī cites a rajaz with tanqaʿ in both cases; al-Farrāʾ cites an anonymous poem. Comparisons include 40:36–37, 19:78, and 37:55. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it an implied wish; Abū Ḥayyān objects that this is hope, and al-Samīn says he meant an implied wish, citing Abū al-Baqāʾ. Al-Naḥḥās reports ʿAlī ibn Sulaymān's objection that he knows no basis for this subjunctive, despite ʿĀṣim's authority. [36 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(عاصم); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الفراء,الكوفيون,عاصم); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA(عاصم); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي وأحسب ابن عامر); WAHIDI-BASIT(من نصب); JISHUMI(عاصم); BAGHAWI-FULL(عاصم، والعامة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عاصم والأعرج); TABRISI(أبو علي,عاصم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج,حفص عن عاصم); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(عاصم وابن أبي إسحاق وعيسى); BAYDAWI-FULL(عاصم); NASAFI-FULL(عاصم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الجمهور، عاصم، الأعرج، أبو حيوة، ابن أبي عبلة); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية,عاصم,مكي); NASHR(عاصم); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الأكثر وعاصم والأعرج وأبو حيوة وابن أبي عبلة والزعفراني,الكشف); IBNASHUR-FULL(عاصم); NAHHAS(علي بن سليمان)-]
- 80:4/q05/p3 Subjunctive through an implicit an after fāʾ, licensed by a nonpositive clause. — Al-Fārisī compares faʾ after negation, command, prohibition, question, or offer, and cites 40:36–37 and 19:78. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī attribute the implied an analysis to Basrans, in contrast with the Kufan response to hope. [4 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور، عاصم، الأعرج، أبو حيوة، ابن أبي عبلة); ALUSI-FULL(الأكثر وعاصم والأعرج وأبو حيوة وابن أبي عبلة والزعفراني)]
- 80:4/q05/p4 Subjunctive by coordination with the element governed by أو. — ʿAlī ibn Sulaymān allows coordination governed by aw while saying he knows no basis for the response-to-hope account. Al-Naḥḥās illustrates with a poet's fanaʿdhira after aw namūta. [2 notes: NAHHAS(علي بن سليمان)]

## 80:4/q06 How does the reminder benefit him?  [فَتَنفَعَهُ ٱلذِّكْرَىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the notes describe the benefit generally or stress partial benefit short of complete purification.
- 80:4/q06/p1 Admonition leads him through reflection to faith, obedience, and religious benefit. — Muqātil says he benefits if Islam is offered and he believes. Al-Ṭabarī says reflection and admonition bring benefit; al-Kashshāf says he learns law, sheds sin, and obeys. Al-Ṭabrisī says Qurʾānic admonition benefits his religion. Ibn Kathīr says it deters forbidden acts. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it invites purification through faith and righteous deeds. Al-Biqāʿī says recollection benefits him immediately and causally, and hints that the Prophet turned aside in hope of another man's purification and recollection. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 80:4/q06/p2 It can help even if complete purification does not occur. — Bursawī says prophetic exhortation can still benefit him; al-Ālūsī reports the possibility that remembering gives benefit short of total cleansing, and al-Biqāʿī values even a small move from unbelief. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL]

## 80:4/q07 What is ٱلذِّكْرَىٰٓ?  [ٱلذِّكْرَىٰٓ · meaning]
turns on: Al-Māwardī gives understanding of religion as an alternative to admonition; other notes specify the admonition's source as the Prophet or Qurʾān.
- 80:4/q07/p1 The admonition or reminder delivered through the Prophet's teaching and the Qurʾān. — Al-Māwardī offers admonition; al-Wāḥidī calls it beneficial admonition. Al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī specify the Prophet's exhortation or preaching. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the Qurʾān, which recalls what people neglect, citing 68:52 and the blind man's question about verses. Iṣlāḥī says a later masculine pronoun referring back to dhikr in verse 4 points to its real meaning, the Qurʾān, with similar usage in earlier sūrahs. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls dhikrā the verbal noun of reminding. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 80:4/q07/p2 Understanding of religion. — Al-Māwardī gives this as an alternative sense alongside admonition. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 80:4/q08 Why is the benefit of purification left unstated?  [فَتَنفَعَهُ · rhetoric]
- 80:4/q08/p1 Both purification and reminder plainly benefit him, so only the reminder's benefit is expressed. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the omitted parallel clause and says the benefit of both outcomes is obvious. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 80:4/q09 Why was the visitor's possible response unknown?  [* · interpretation]
- 80:4/q09/p1 The Prophet could not know whether he would purify himself or heed admonition. — Al-Kashshāf says knowledge of what might follow was unavailable; al-Nasafī and Bursawī say knowing this prospect would have prevented neglect. The uncertainty applies on al-Kashshāf's reading of either the blind man or the unbeliever. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكشاف)]

## 80:4/q10 Who was reproached for turning away?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the unnamed frowning subject may be identified as the Prophet in light of his character and the reported occasion.
- 80:4/q10/p1 The Prophet was reproached for turning from Ibn Umm Maktūm. — Al-Ḥasan and others say the Prophet returned to the Meccan leaders and God rebuked him; al-Ṭabrisī and al-Majmaʿ transmit the encounter. Bursawī says his intended greater benefit still outwardly suggested favoring the rich and hurting the poor. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes the third-person opening followed by direct address as intensified reproach; he also reports a view that the indirection and later intimacy honor the Prophet. [7 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABRISI(قيل); BURSEVI; TABATABAI(قيل,مجمع البيان); TABRISI(المرتضى علم الهدى)-]
- 80:4/q10/p2 The passage does not identify the frowning person as the Prophet. — Al-Murtaḍā says he is unnamed and that frowning at a believing seeker or favoring the rich over the poor conflicts with the Prophet's noble character. [1 notes: TABRISI(المرتضى علم الهدى)+]

## 80:4/q11 What happened when the blind visitor came?  [* · reports]
- 80:4/q11/p1 Ibn Umm Maktūm interrupted the Prophet's appeal to Meccan leaders, asked to be taught, and the Prophet turned back to them. — Al-Ḥasan and others say this occasion drew God's rebuke. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Majmaʿ name the visitor ʿAbdallāh ibn Shurayḥ ibn Mālik ibn Rabīʿa al-Fihrī; the leaders are ʿUtba, Abū Jahl, al-ʿAbbās, Ubayy, and Umayya ibn Khalaf. They say he asked repeatedly and the Prophet feared elites would scorn blind or enslaved followers. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that he asked about Qurʾānic verses. Al-Suhaylī identifies the hoped-for pagan as al-Walīd or Umayya and says his conversion might bring many followers; he contrasts the visitor's prior Islam and basic learning. [8 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABRISI(قيل); TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI(السهيلي)]

## 80:4/q12 What happened to Ibn Umm Maktūm afterward?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports describe distinct later events and effects.
- 80:4/q12/p1 The Prophet honored him and welcomed his requests. — Ibn ʿAbbās says the Prophet spread his cloak for him to sit on. Al-Ḥasan and others say he welcomed the man for whom his Lord reproached him and asked his needs; al-Ṭabrisī reports the same greeting. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABRISI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 80:4/q12/p2 The Prophet appointed him to lead or serve in Medina twice. — Qatāda says he led the people's prayers during two expeditions. Al-Ṭabrisī says he was appointed deputy in Medina twice. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 80:4/q12/p3 He was seen at al-Qādisiyya armored and carrying a black banner. — Anas reports the sighting in both al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أنس); TABRISI(أنس بن مالك)]
- 80:4/q12/p4 This encounter gave him a lasting reputation. — Al-Biqāʿī contrasts Ibn Umm Maktūm's enduring renown with Pharaoh, who was turned away and neither purified himself nor benefited from reminder. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 80:4/q13 What does this verse teach about whom a preacher should attend to?  [* · interpretation]
- 80:4/q13/p1 Earnest seekers who may purify themselves or benefit from reminders deserve attention. — Iṣlāḥī says the messengers' mission is spiritual purification and a seeker's desire for reform marks receptivity; students may ask a question or listen to answers to others. Worldly importance and desire for pampering do not advance that mission. Bursawī says even an aim at greater benefit did not remove the outward impression of favoring wealth and hurting the poor. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the sūra as rebuking preference for privileged people over poor believers. [6 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 80:4/q14 What does remembering imply about human agency?  [* · theology]
- 80:4/q14/p1 People can will remembrance and act on it. — Al-Jishumī calls remembrance worship and takes the wording that whoever wills may remember as evidence that people are capable agents and their acts are theirs. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 80:4/q15 What does the shift in address accomplish?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the shift chiefly expresses courtesy followed by reproach or honor through distance and restored intimacy.
- 80:4/q15/p1 It softens the initial frown's attribution, then makes the reproach direct. — Al-Ṭabrisī says God courteously avoids directly saying 'you frowned' and addresses the Prophet only afterward. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the third-person opening distances the rebuke and direct address intensifies accountability. [2 notes: TABRISI; TABATABAI]
- 80:4/q15/p2 Both third-person distance and later direct address honor the Prophet. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports a view that third person implies someone else acted and direct address restores intimacy after estrangement. [1 notes: TABATABAI(قيل)]

## 80:4/q16 How does this passage connect with its sūra and the preceding sūra?  [* · links]
- 80:4/q16/p1 The receptive seeker's purification links the warning to resurrection and God's proofs of power. — Al-Biqāʿī connects the call to warn those who fear in 79:45 with ʿAbasa's purification of receptive hearts; human creation and food show divine power and make rejection astonishing. Iṣlāḥī says ʿAbasa pairs with al-Nāziʿāt in theme, discourse, and reasoning, though its topic order is distinct. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī traces the sūra from rebuke of wealth preference through human dependence and ingratitude to resurrection, recompense, and warning. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]

## 80:4/q17 What is the grammatical role of the surrounding 'what will make you know' clause?  [* · grammar]
- 80:4/q17/p1 It is circumstantial to the subject of 'frowned and turned away.' — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī gives this syntactic analysis. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 80:4/q18 What report is made about concealing this passage?  [* · reports]
- 80:4/q18/p1 Had the Messenger concealed revelation, he would have concealed this passage. — Ibn Zayd transmits this saying about the rebuke. [1 notes: JISHUMI(ابن زيد)]

## 80:4/q19 Where was the sūra revealed?  [* · sciences]
- 80:4/q19/p1 It is Meccan. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls this unquestionable. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
