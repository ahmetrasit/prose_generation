# 21:66: 15 questions

## 21:66/q01 Had the people understood Abraham's proof when he spoke?  [قَالَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the evident proof had actually been grasped by the people; al-Razi cites Abraham's subsequent questions for the second possibility.
- 21:66/q01/p1 They had already recognized that his argument was sound. — Al-Zamakhshari presents this as one possible timing after the failed excuse and the truth's disclosure. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); RAZI-FULL-]
- 21:66/q01/p2 The proof was apparent, but they had not understood it. — Al-Zamakhshari gives this alternative; al-Razi favors it because Abraham still asks about their worship and reasoning. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); RAZI-FULL+]

## 21:66/q02 What force does the opening af convey?  [أَفَتَعْبُدُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes give different glosses for af: an expression of annoyance or a word for filth and stench.
- 21:66/q02/p1 It voices Abraham's annoyance and displeasure. — Al-Zamakhshari calls af an uttered sound of annoyance: Abraham was exasperated that they persisted in worship after their excuse failed and the truth became clear. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف)]
- 21:66/q02/p2 It signifies filth or stinking, directed at them and their idols. — Al-Qurtubi gives this gloss alongside Abraham's cutting off their argument. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 21:66/q03 What does Abraham's question accomplish?  [أَفَتَعْبُدُونَ · interpretation]
- 21:66/q03/p1 It rebukes idol worship as unreasonable because its objects can neither benefit nor harm. — Muqatil, al-Tha'labi, al-Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, al-Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, al-Biqai, al-Bursevi, al-Alusi, Ibn Ashur, al-Tabatabai and Asad describe the rebuke. Al-Tha'labi, al-Wahidi, al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Ashur and al-Tabatabai stress that their admission made the proof decisive; al-Nasafi calls it an argument. Al-Tabrisi adds that idols capable of help or harm would have defended themselves. Al-Bursevi and al-Alusi say knowing their condition requires giving up worship. Al-Tabatabai says worship seeks good or fears harm; Asad says the people are urged to reason. Ibn Ashur and al-Tabatabai say Abraham turns their admission against them rather than defending himself. [19 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 21:66/q04 What is worshipped besides God here?  [مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ مَا · referent]
- 21:66/q04/p1 Their idols, described also as images or statues. — Yahya ibn Sallam identifies the powerless objects as their idols; Ibn Atiyya calls them images and Abu Hayyan statues. The other named sources likewise specify idols when explaining the question. [22 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي,الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 21:66/q05 Who has the power and right that their objects of worship lack?  [مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ مَا لَا يَنفَعُكُمْ شَيْـًۭٔا وَلَا يَضُرُّكُمْ · theology]
- 21:66/q05/p1 God alone merits worship and controls benefit and harm; mere ability to affect either would not by itself suffice for worship. — Al-Wahidi specifies reward for worship and punishment for neglect; Ibn al-Jawzi urges worship of the One who owns benefit and harm. Al-Biqai calls God the supreme sovereign with complete attributes. Al-Qashani says He alone is the beneficial and harmful agent. Al-Tabrisi adds that only the creator of life, desire, power and reason, who can reward and punish, deserves worship; power to benefit or harm alone is insufficient. Ibn Ata, as reported by al-Sulami and al-Bursevi, also names God as controller of harm, benefit and the return. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; SULAMI(ابن عطاء); BURSEVI(ابن عطاء)]

## 21:66/q06 In what practical sense can the idols neither benefit nor harm?  [مَا لَا يَنفَعُكُمْ شَيْـًۭٔا وَلَا يَضُرُّكُمْ · interpretation]
- 21:66/q06/p1 They give no benefit when worshipped and inflict no harm when ignored. — Muqatil and al-Baghawi give the worshipped versus abandoned contrast. Al-Suddi says they neither provide nor give anything and do not harm nonworshippers; al-Kalbi says they do no good when worshipped and no harm when abandoned. Ibn al-Jawzi repeats the giving and abandonment contrast; al-Bursevi says awareness of this should end worship. Al-Nasafi specifically describes the lack of harm to nonworshippers. [7 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي,الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 21:66/q07 How is 'anything' construed with 'benefit'?  [يَنفَعُكُمْ شَيْـًۭٔا · grammar]
turns on: Whether 'anything' intensifies the negated benefit itself or marks the domain in which no benefit occurs.
- 21:66/q07/p1 It is an emphatic verbal noun: they do not benefit you at all. — Al-Nasafi says 'anything' is a verbal noun meaning 'benefit'; al-Alusi offers the equivalent sense that the idols benefit you not at all. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:66/q07/p2 It means that they do not benefit you in anything. — Al-Alusi reports this as an alternative construal of the phrase. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 21:66/q08 Why is benefit mentioned before harm?  [يَنفَعُكُمْ يَضُرُّكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 21:66/q08/p1 The order follows what this context foregrounds, especially the worshippers' hoped-for help. — Al-Burhan says harm normally leads because worshippers fear punishment before hoping for reward, whereas benefit leads when foregrounded. It counts seven benefit-first passages, three using nouns and four verbs; it cites 7:188, 6:71, 10:106, 21:66 and 25:55, and explains 7:188 by earlier guidance and good before evil. Its comparison with 13:15 and 34:39 says each passage's order coheres with its own context rather than repeating mechanically. Al-Biqai says the people urgently sought help after their idols were smashed, so anticipated benefit precedes feared harm. [4 notes: BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL]

## 21:66/q09 How does the idols' inability to speak lead into this challenge?  [* · links]
- 21:66/q09/p1 Their admission that the idols cannot speak exposes their incapacity, enabling Abraham to challenge their worship. — Al-Tha'labi and al-Wahidi say the admission made his argument decisive; al-Baydawi says worship contradicted their admission. Al-Burhan links the preceding exchange to this question, and Islahi says the helplessness was already evident (21:65). Ibn Ashur reasons that lack of speech entails no mind, knowledge, will or power, and thus no ability to benefit or harm. Al-Tabatabai says Abraham turned their admission into proof that idols neither know nor act; Kuran Yolu says their own confession exposed the absurdity of their faith. Abu Hayyan says the rebuke follows the proof. [10 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 21:66/q10 What wider lesson is drawn about relying on other creatures?  [* · inward]
- 21:66/q10/p1 Seek God rather than help from equally needy creatures or anything besides Him. — Ibn Ata, in al-Sulami and al-Bursevi, says God calls servants to Himself and cuts them off from all else: a powerless creature cannot replace the One who controls benefit, harm and return. Hamdun al-Qassar, in al-Sulami, al-Bursevi and al-Alusi, likens one creature seeking another's help to a prisoner asking another prisoner. Al-Bursevi's spiritual masters say seeking another shows distance from God; all besides Him is play and falsehood, while the Lord suffices. Al-Alusi draws the same warning about needy creatures seeking other needy creatures. Al-Qashani says worship and reverence belong to God alone as the sole agent of benefit and harm. [8 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,حمدون القصار); QASHANI; BURSEVI(ابن عطاء,بعض الكبار,حمدون القصار); ALUSI-FULL(حمدون القصار)]

## 21:66/q11 What account illustrates reliance on God for provision?  [* · reports]
- 21:66/q11/p1 Habib al-Ajami worshipped God instead of earning wages, and God supplied food and gold after his wife demanded payment. — Al-Bursevi relates that his wife first urged him to earn; he worshipped all night and said he was ashamed to ask the Generous One for pay. After three days she demanded wages or divorce; he returned from another night of worship to find food and a bag of gold sent by God, and she repented. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 21:66/q12 How did the idolaters react to Abraham's criticism?  [* · reports]
- 21:66/q12/p1 Bigotry led them to decide to burn him and defend their gods themselves. — Kuran Yolu says they could not endure his criticism and protected their gods instead of expecting their gods to protect them. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 21:66/q13 How does Abraham's episode fit the sura's sequence of prophets?  [* · links]
- 21:66/q13/p1 It belongs to a sequence of earlier prophetic guidance that confronts Muhammad's opponents with the fate of past deniers. — Islahi links the section to 21:24's earlier prophets: from verses 48–77 it lists celebrated prophets in ascending order, beginning with Moses and Abraham, whose message matches the Quran's. Moses comes first as the last preceding law-bearing messenger, Aaron assists him, then Abraham and his descendants' two branches appear, followed by Noah. Abraham is founder and leader of the Israelite and Ishmaelite branches, explaining Isaac, Jacob and Lot. The series evokes the whole history of guidance and warns persistent opponents of earlier deniers' fate. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 21:66/q14 Where else does the Quran make the point that idols cannot help or harm?  [* · links]
- 21:66/q14/p1 Several other verses pose comparable questions about powerless idols. — The Study Quran lists 5:76, 6:71, 10:18, 10:106, 13:16, 20:89, 22:12 and 25:55. Al-Burhan also compares 7:188, 6:71, 10:106 and 25:55 when discussing benefit before harm, and 13:15 and 34:39 when discussing contextual word order. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; BURHAN]

## 21:66/q15 What does this verse's ending show about the sura's rhyme?  [* · rhetoric]
- 21:66/q15/p1 Its ending departs from the sura's usual rhyme because the intended meaning governs the wording. — Al-Samarrai points to 21:66 as an instance of a differing verse ending. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH]
