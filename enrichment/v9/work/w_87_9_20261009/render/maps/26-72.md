# 26:72: 28 questions

## 26:72/q01 How does قال introduce Abraham's next question?  [قَالَ · links]
- 26:72/q01/p1 It resumes the exchange, answering the question prompted by the idolaters' previous reply. — Bursevī calls it a resumption explaining the preceding exchange; al-Ālūsī says the detailed reply prompts an implicit question, to which this fresh clause responds. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 26:72/q02 How does هل frame this inquiry?  [هَلْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether هل itself shares the hamza's report-and-rebuke force; Abū ʿAlī rejects that equation.
- 26:72/q02/p1 It asks one question covering the coordinated alternatives of hearing, benefiting, and harming. — Sībawayh cites this wording as an example of هل followed by coordinated alternatives within a single inquiry. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 26:72/q02/p2 It can convey denial and rebuke with a force like the interrogative hamza. — Al-Kindī reports this view of many scholars and considers Abū ʿAlī's objection understandable since the expression is a form of denial. [1 notes: BURHAN(الكندي); BURHAN(أبو علي)-]

## 26:72/q03 Who are the plural subjects of يَسْمَعُونَكُمْ?  [يَسْمَعُونَكُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether rational plural agreement reflects the idolaters' treatment of images or includes rational objects of worship.
- 26:72/q03/p1 They are the idols, treated grammatically as rational beings because their worshippers credit them with power. — Al-Jishumī and Bursevī explain the masculine rational plural by the worshippers' belief that idols benefit and harm like rational beings. Ibn ʿĀshūr says they worship images for benefit or protection from harm. [3 notes: JISHUMI; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 26:72/q03/p2 The worshipped group includes rational beings alongside idols, so rational agreement prevails. — Al-Jishumī reports this alternative and points to 26:77; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that some worshipped devils. [2 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:72/q04 How is يَسْمَعُونَكُمْ read?  [يَسْمَعُونَكُمْ · readings]
turns on: Whether the verb is the simple form 'hear' or the causative form 'make hear,' with an unspoken second object.
- 26:72/q04/p1 The common reading is yasmaʿūnakum, from samiʿa, with fatḥa on yāʾ and mīm. — Al-Jishumī calls this the common reading; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān call it the majority reading. Abū Ḥayyān relates its object structure to whether the object itself is audible. [3 notes: JISHUMI(قراءة العامة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 26:72/q04/p2 Another reading is yusmiʿūnakum, from asmaʿa, with ḍamma on yāʾ and kasra on mīm. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī attribute it to Qatāda; Ibn ʿAṭiyya likewise names Qatāda. Ibn al-Jawzī names Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Ibn Yaʿmar, and ʿĀṣim al-Jaḥdarī; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī name Qatāda and Yaḥyā ibn Yaʿmar. Its second object is omitted: the idols would make worshippers hear their voice, speech, or an answer, as these sources variously phrase it; al-Ālūsī says it may support the answer interpretation. Al-Jishumī reports unspecified readers. [9 notes: NAHHAS(قتادة); JISHUMI(بعضهم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير وابن يعمر وعاصم الجحدري); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة ويحيى بن يعمر); SAMIN-DURR(قتادة ويحيى بن يعمر); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة ويحيى بن يعمر); BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 26:72/q05 What does يَسْمَعُونَكُمْ ask whether the idols do?  [يَسْمَعُونَكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'hearing' denotes actual perception, a response by metonymy, or causing a response to be heard in the causative reading.
- 26:72/q05/p1 Hear the worshippers' invocation or audible voices. — Ibn ʿAbbās mentions hearing the invocation as well as answering; ʿIkrima asks literally whether they hear the worshippers' voices. Al-Ālūsī prefers actual hearing because it best suits the context. Al-Bayḍāwī allows hearing their prayer or hearing them praying. Al-Ṭabrisī includes hearing supplications among the idols' missing powers. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عكرمة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 26:72/q05/p2 Answer the worshippers when they invoke the idols. — Qatāda is reported by Yaḥyā ibn Sallām, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and al-Durr; al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī phrase it as hearing and answering. Muqātil equates hearing with responding and invokes the prayer formula 'God hears whoever praises Him'; al-Ālūsī cites 'a prayer that is not heard' and 40:60 as figurative parallels. Abū Ḥayyān reports this interpretation. Ibn ʿAbbās mentions answering alongside hearing. [10 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL(قتادة); RAZI-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); MUQATIL]
- 26:72/q05/p3 Make their worshippers hear a voice or answer when called. — This is the sense assigned by al-Naḥḥās, al-Qurṭubī, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī to the causative reading; the omitted second object is the idols' sound, speech, or response. [6 notes: NAHHAS(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); SAMIN-DURR(قتادة ويحيى بن يعمر); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة ويحيى بن يعمر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة ويحيى بن يعمر); BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 26:72/q06 What is the object structure of يَسْمَعُونَكُمْ?  [يَسْمَعُونَكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether hearing a person requires a second object, or the act heard is an omitted noun or a circumstantial modifier.
- 26:72/q06/p1 An omitted 'your invocation' supplies what is heard; the following clause makes the omission clear. — Al-Akhfash and al-Naḥḥās also allow 'hear from you,' citing a Basīṭ verse about reins of abaq, meaning flax, with an understood noun; al-Qurṭubī repeats the line and notes al-Ṣiḥāḥ also glosses abaq as hemp. Abū ʿAlī and other grammarians say hearing a person means hearing their speech. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Burhān cite 35:14; al-Jishumī compares the omitted genitive in 12:82. Al-Ṭabrisī says إذ تدعون supplies the cue; Bursevī says a person is not itself audible. Al-Rāzī transmits al-Kashshāf; al-Ālūsī reports that the audible object دعاءكم can be restored. [17 notes: AKHFASH; NAHHAS(الأخفش); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي، النحويون); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 26:72/q06/p2 With a person as first object, the verb governs a second object denoting that person's audible speech or act of invocation. — Al-Ṭabrisī says the second object must be audible speech or recitation, never an inaudible action. Al-Fārisī, as reported by Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī, supplies the second object from تدعون; al-Samīn gives 'hear you invoking' as this analysis. Al-Ālūsī says the following clause supplies the omitted action. [4 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفارسي); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الفارسي)]
- 26:72/q06/p3 The verb takes one personal object, while the audible act is a circumstantial clause or related modifier. — Abū Ḥayyān prefers a single object with the following action circumstantial. Al-Samīn reports that some grammarians make the supplied clause circumstantial. Al-Ālūsī says it may be a circumstantial clause for a definite object, an adjective for an indefinite one, or apposition. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(غيره); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]

## 26:72/q07 What is the time sense of إِذْ with present-tense تَدْعُونَ?  [إِذْ تَدْعُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: How a past-time particle combines with present verbs: historical present, shifted verbal time, a sense like إذا, or an extra particle.
- 26:72/q07/p1 The present verb vividly reenacts repeated past invocations and sharpens the rebuke. — Al-Zamakhsharī says the audience is made to recall each past call and ask whether idols ever heard or answered; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn transmit this. Al-Bayḍāwī calls it vivid reenactment; al-Biqāʿī says it evokes their whole span of worship. Al-Ālūsī prefers this, adding that هل's usual future force poses no difficulty because hearing follows calling. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)+]
- 26:72/q07/p2 إذ is a past-time adverb; the present forms are understood with past reference. — Al-Samīn says the preceding verb governs إذ and إذ governs the following verb, making both past in meaning. Abū Ḥayyān allows the present verb to occupy a past-tense position. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 26:72/q07/p3 إذ can be understood with the sense of إذا, 'when.' — Abū Ḥayyān and some grammarians reported by al-Samīn give this analysis; al-Ālūsī recounts Abū Ḥayyān's proposal. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]
- 26:72/q07/p4 إذ is an extra particle. — Abū ʿUbayda is reported by al-Burhān to treat it as زائد. [1 notes: BURHAN(أبو عبيدة)]

## 26:72/q08 How is إِذْ تَدْعُونَ pronounced at the consonant boundary?  [إِذْ تَدْعُونَ · readings]
turns on: Whether adjacent dhāl and tāʾ remain separate, undergo the attested dhāl-to-tāʾ assimilation, or admit Ibn ʿAṭiyya's unattested analogy.
- 26:72/q08/p1 The dhāl and tāʾ are pronounced distinctly. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports some readers do this; al-Zajjāj, reported by Ibn al-Jawzī, allows it. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn also record distinct pronunciation. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 26:72/q08/p2 The dhāl changes to tāʾ and assimilates into the following tāʾ. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls this the majority pronunciation; al-Zajjāj judges it better Arabic because the sounds are close. Abū Ḥayyān says this is the actual assimilated form, and al-Ālūsī describes the same change. [7 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج)+]
- 26:72/q08/p3 An analogical doubled-dāl assimilation has been proposed but is unattested here. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares forms like mudakkir, yielding a doubled dāl before the root dāl, but says no one reads it; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Shaykh object that this replacement applies to the infix tāʾ of certain derived forms or a pronoun suffix, not the imperfect prefix tāʾ. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]

## 26:72/q09 What does إِذْ تَدْعُونَ describe?  [إِذْ تَدْعُونَ · interpretation]
- 26:72/q09/p1 The time they call upon the idols with needs and expect a response. — Bursevī explicitly explains the phrase in terms of petition and expected answer. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 26:72/q10 Why does Abraham ask whether the idols hear, benefit, or harm?  [* · interpretation]
- 26:72/q10/p1 He exposes their inability to meet a worshipper's needs and therefore their lack of claim to worship. — Muqātil spells out answer, benefit in worship, and harm on neglect; al-Ṭabrisī repeats the three tests. Al-Zamakhsharī says Abraham knew they worshipped idols and compares his staged inquiry to asking a trader about his property before showing slaves are not property. Al-Rāzī and Ibn ʿĀshūr explain that worshippers seek benefit and protection; Ibn ʿĀshūr says Abraham leaves them no proof. Abū Ḥayyān says these attributes belong only to God; al-Biqāʿī says lack of even one divine attribute defeats the claim, and lack of all makes it still worse. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says worship entails need, humility, and invocation, requiring an object that hears and knows the request. Iṣlāḥī says their only response is ancestral custom. [10 notes: MUQATIL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:72/q11 What principle about religious argument does Abraham's inquiry show?  [* · theology]
- 26:72/q11/p1 Religion must rest on proof. — Al-Ṭabrisī infers this from Abraham's argument against the idols instead of accepting their worship on custom. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 26:72/q12 What did the idolaters concede and why did they persist?  [* · reports]
- 26:72/q12/p1 They conceded that their idols could neither hear nor benefit nor harm, and kept their forefathers' practice. — Ibn Kathīr reports the concession and ancestral explanation; Iṣlāḥī says they had no answer to Abraham's questions and would not abandon inherited tradition. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:72/q13 At what stage in Abraham's preaching did this exchange occur?  [* · reports]
- 26:72/q13/p1 After he had conclusively conveyed the truth through scriptural and intellectual arguments. — Iṣlāḥī places the reported events after both forms of argument. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:72/q14 How does this Abraham account fit the sūra's sequence?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes identify different literary connections rather than dispute one another.
- 26:72/q14/p1 Like the preceding anecdote, it serves the sūra's preaching purpose and closes with its recurring verses. — Iṣlāḥī explicitly compares the two narratives and their endings. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:72/q14/p2 It fills the interval after Noah in the previous sūra, since Abraham was the chief messenger between Noah and Moses. — Iṣlāḥī relates the narrative's placement to the previous sūra's sequence. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:72/q14/p3 The surrounding exchange recalls the Israelites' devotion to an idol and Moses' confrontation in 20:91 and 20:97. — The Study Quran explicitly draws the two links. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 26:72/q15 What does ʿukūf mean in the surrounding exchange?  [* · meaning]
- 26:72/q15/p1 Steadfast adherence to something. — Al-Jishumī compares iʿtikāf. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 26:72/q16 What do benefit and ḍarr mean in the surrounding question?  [* · meaning]
- 26:72/q16/p1 Benefit is good, and ḍarr is harm that befalls. — Al-Jishumī gives the opposed glosses. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 26:72/q17 Why is the object of harm omitted in the following question?  [* · rhetoric]
- 26:72/q17/p1 The omission suits the cadence and makes possible harm general, unlike the specified benefit sought for themselves. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the idols cannot harm the worshippers or their enemies. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 26:72/q18 What does qidam mean in the surrounding speech, and to whom literally applies?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether qidam is literal only for beginningless divine existence or also for relative precedence among created things.
- 26:72/q18/p1 It is beginningless or ancient existence; applied literally only to God and metaphorically to others. — Al-Jishumī equates aqdam, asbaq, and awwal and describes qidam as existence without a beginning or in the eternal past; he reports Abū ʿAlī's restriction of literal usage. [3 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي); TABRISI]
- 26:72/q18/p2 It applies literally to God and to others. — Al-Jishumī reports Abū Hāshim's view. Al-Ṭabrisī glosses aqdam as what existed before another, like awwal and asbaq, while describing qadar as beginningless existence. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أبو هاشم); TABRISI]

## 26:72/q19 What does khizy mean in the surrounding passage?  [* · meaning]
- 26:72/q19/p1 Disgrace that exposes sin. — Al-Jishumī also lists the related verbal forms. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 26:72/q20 What does tabrīz mean in the surrounding passage?  [* · meaning]
- 26:72/q20/p1 Emergence from concealment or making something manifest. — Al-Jishumī traces it to appearing; al-Ṭabrisī glosses it as displaying or making manifest. [2 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI]

## 26:72/q21 What does ghāwī mean in the surrounding passage?  [* · meaning]

## 26:72/q22 What does naṣr mean in the surrounding passage?  [* · meaning]

## 26:72/q26 What does shāfiʿ mean in the surrounding passage?  [* · meaning]
- 26:72/q26/p1 One who seeks benefit or protection from harm for another. — Al-Jishumī connects the root to addition. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 26:72/q27 What does ṣadīq mean in the surrounding passage?  [* · meaning]

## 26:72/q28 What does ḥamīm mean in the surrounding passage?  [* · meaning]
- 26:72/q28/p1 A close relative or beloved close friend. — Al-Jishumī says close relative; al-Ṭabrisī says a beloved friend with mutual affection. [2 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI]
