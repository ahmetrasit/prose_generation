# 87:17: 14 questions

## 87:17/q01 What does al-ākhirah designate here?  [ٱلْءَاخِرَةُ · referent]
turns on: Whether al-ākhirah is taken broadly as the next abode, specifically as Paradise, or inwardly as spiritual life.
- 87:17/q01/p1 The life or abode after this world. — Muqātil, Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Nasafī and others contrast it with worldly life; al-Biqāʿī calls it the exalted abode and goal of creation and command. [6 notes: MUQATIL; TAB; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; NASAFI; BIQAI]
- 87:17/q01/p2 Paradise or the Garden. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī explicitly gloss the afterlife as Paradise; Ibn al-Jawzī specifies the Garden for the Prophet. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 87:17/q01/p3 The true spiritual life with God. — Al-Qāshānī contrasts it with sensory life and its ornaments, linking worldly preference to neglected remembrance and prayer and lack of purification. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 87:17/q02 In what sense is the Hereafter better than worldly life?  [ٱلْءَاخِرَةُ خَيْرٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes variously state a general comparison or specify its beneficiary as believers, the obedient and Paradise's people, or the Prophet.
- 87:17/q02/p1 It surpasses worldly life in goodness, pleasure, and reward. — Ṭabarī and Qatāda say better in goodness; al-Kashshāf, al-Nasafī and Qutb stress intrinsic or qualitative superiority. Al-Jishumī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī say its pleasure is pure or unspoiled whereas worldly pleasure is slight, interrupted or mixed with pain; al-Rāzī adds bodily and spiritual happiness; Ibn Kathīr calls the world low and the next abode noble. Al-Biqāʿī calls its bliss the goal of creation and command. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says relative superiority suffices for the comparison. [31 notes: MUQATIL; TAB(قتادة); MAWARDI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 87:17/q02/p2 It is better in its recompense for believers or those who seek it. — Al-Māwardī specifies believers and enduring recompense; al-Qushayrī specifies believers who seek it. [3 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI]
- 87:17/q02/p3 It is better for the obedient, and its permanence belongs to the people of Paradise. — Al-Māwardī presents this as a synthesis or further possibility, assigning the two predicates to those two groups respectively. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; MAWARDI+]
- 87:17/q02/p4 The Garden is better and more lasting for the Prophet. — Ibn al-Jawzī's formulation singles out the Prophet as beneficiary. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 87:17/q03 What does abqā say about duration?  [أَبْقَىٰٓ · meaning]
- 87:17/q03/p1 The afterlife lasts longer than the passing world, ultimately without end. — Ṭabarī and Qatāda contrast this life's ending with the next life's permanence. Al-Rāzī and al-Biqāʿī argue that permanence outranks transience; al-Biqāʿī says even a prolonged world is only figuratively lasting. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies abqā as a comparative of longer duration and cites the Prophet's garment saying as parallel usage. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes the comparative claim from the afterlife's intrinsic eternity; al-Samīn identifies worldly life as the omitted thing surpassed. Al-Itqān compares another omitted comparative, 'what is more hidden.' [19 notes: TAB(قتادة); MAWARDI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ITQAN; IBNASHUR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 87:17/q04 How is this clause grammatically construed?  [* · grammar]
turns on: These are compatible analyses of sentence form, its relation to 87:16, and comparative ellipsis.
- 87:17/q04/p1 It is a nominal sentence with a subject and predicate. — Al-Naḥḥās analyzes al-ākhiratu khayrun wa-abqā this way. [1 notes: NAHHAS]
- 87:17/q04/p2 It functions as a circumstantial clause attached to the subject of 'you prefer.' — Al-Ālūsī says this construction reinforces the rebuke of 87:16. [2 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 87:17/q04/p3 The comparatives leave worldly life unstated as what is surpassed. — Al-Samīn supplies worldly life for abqā; al-Itqān gives this verse and 'knows the secret and what is more hidden' as examples of an omitted comparative term. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN]

## 87:17/q05 Why does this wording leave parts of the contrast implicit?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The authors explain the implied worldly contrast through rhetorical pairing or through the obviousness of worldly defects.
- 87:17/q05/p1 The paired clauses imply rejection and elevation after worldly preference, and the world's defects after the Hereafter's goodness; the order first deters, then attracts. — Al-Biqāʿī explains both sides of the ellipsis and the sequence of deterrence followed by attraction. [2 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 87:17/q05/p2 The world's troubled pleasure and short span are omitted because they are obvious. — Al-Ālūsī says the listeners already know these defects. [2 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 87:17/q06 Why do people prefer worldly life despite this comparison?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes emphasize present visibility, inward disposition, or the disbelievers' concrete worldly interests.
- 87:17/q06/p1 Its pleasures are present and visible, while the promised afterlife is hidden or deferred. — Ibn Masʿūd names immediate food, drink, women, pleasure and adornment, contrasted with an unseen promised afterlife; the reports vary between his confession at the verse and a fuller list of pleasures. Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds ignorance of the afterlife's permanence; Qatāda says only those God protects escape this choice. [11 notes: TAB(ابن مسعود,قتادة); BAGHAWI(ابن مسعود); BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); IBNATIYYA; DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(عرفجة الثقفي,قتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 87:17/q06/p2 Low aspiration, attachment to appearances, and lack of purification veil the lasting life. — Abū al-ʿAbbās al-Dīnawarī contrasts low and elevated ambition; al-Qāshānī links attachment to sensory ornaments with neglected remembrance and prayer; al-Bursawī describes outward-looking people as veiled from the Hereafter. [3 notes: SULAMI(أبو العباس الدينوري); QASHANI; BURSEVI]
- 87:17/q06/p3 Disbelievers, especially Quraysh, protect worldly interests even at the expense of eternal reward. — Iṣlāḥī says their attachment makes the Prophet's message fail to take root; Quraysh invent doubts rather than risk their interests. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 87:17/q07 What choice and conduct does the comparison call for?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The sources prescribe choosing the lasting reward, name its preparatory acts, or describe the highest aspiration as seeking God beyond both worlds.
- 87:17/q07/p1 Choose and seek the enduring Hereafter over worldly life. — Al-Rāzī reasons that what is better and more enduring deserves preference; Ibn Kathīr calls such a choice rational. Al-Biqāʿī urges rejecting sensory attractions and imagined pleasures. Al-Bursawī says preferring mixed, temporary pleasure merits rebuke; Qutb calls the reverse foolish judgment. Mālik ibn Dīnār's illustration values even lasting pottery over passing gold. Al-Māwardī and al-Qushayrī specify faithful seekers; Kur'an Yolu warns unbelievers and believers, citing 7:169, 12:109 and 93:4. [9 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; BIQAI; BURSEVI; QUTB-ZILAL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QURTUBI(مالك بن دينار); QURTUBI-FULL(مالك بن دينار)]
- 87:17/q07/p2 Work for purification, charity, remembrance and prayer in preparation to meet God. — Elmalılı says worldly preference ends in loss, while afterlife seekers can bear hardship without despair, seek escape from pain and God's pleasure rather than mere survival, and give obligatory charity, remember the Lord and pray. [3 notes: ELMALILI]
- 87:17/q07/p3 Seek the Lord above both worldly life and the afterlife. — Abū al-ʿAbbās al-Dīnawarī distinguishes noble states and sound realities from the merely elevated ambition that chooses the afterlife. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو العباس الدينوري)]

## 87:17/q08 What reported images convey the world's value beside the Hereafter?  [* · reports]
turns on: These are distinct reported analogies or sayings, with different speakers and images.
- 87:17/q08/p1 The world is like a rabbit's sneeze or puff of breath beside the Hereafter. — Al-Kashshāf and Ibn ʿAṭiyya report ʿUmar's image; wording varies between sneeze and puff of breath. [3 notes: KASHSHAF(عمر); IBNATIYYA(عمر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عمر)]
- 87:17/q08/p2 The world is like water brought back by a finger dipped in the sea. — Al-Qurṭubī reports the Prophet's comparison and calls the hadith sound or authentic. [2 notes: QURTUBI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:17/q08/p3 Even passing gold would be inferior to lasting pottery, while in truth the Hereafter is lasting gold and the world passing pottery. — Al-Qurṭubī reports Mālik ibn Dīnār's counterfactual comparison. [2 notes: QURTUBI(مالك بن دينار); QURTUBI-FULL(مالك بن دينار)]
- 87:17/q08/p4 The world is home and wealth for those lacking real home and wealth, amassed by the mindless. — Ibn Kathīr attributes this to the Prophet through ʿĀʾisha and says Aḥmad transmits it; al-Durr likewise reports it. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR(عائشة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:17/q08/p5 The world is a prison for the believer and a garden for the disbeliever. — The Study Quran cites a Prophetic hadith and mentions 93:4. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]
- 87:17/q08/p6 God created nothing more hated to Him than the world and has never looked at it. — Al-Durr reports this through Mūsā ibn Yasār. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 87:17/q09 What reported counsel addresses love of the two lives?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in whether they give reciprocal counsel about the two lives or a caveat about testimony and worldly attachment.
- 87:17/q09/p1 Love of either life harms one's share in the other, so choose what endures. — Al-Ṭabrisī transmits the reciprocal warning; Ibn Kathīr reports Abū Mūsā's Prophetic version and notes Aḥmad alone transmits it, also through another route; al-Durr repeats the counsel. [5 notes: TABRISI; IBNKATHIR(أبو موسى الأشعري عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:17/q09/p2 The testimony leading to Paradise is compromised by love, preference, accumulation and contentment with worldly life and tyrannical conduct. — Al-Durr gives this as a Prophetic report in the notes on both 87:16 and 87:17. [2 notes: DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 87:17/q10 How is the preceding verb of preference read?  [* · readings]
turns on: Whether 87:16 addresses 'you' or speaks of 'they'; Ṭabarī cites reader consensus and Ubayy's antum for the second person.
- 87:17/q10/p1 In the second person, addressing the hearers. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers the readers' consensus on this reading and cites Ubayy's recitation with antum before the verb as support. [2 notes: TAB(أبي); TAB+]
- 87:17/q10/p2 In the third person with yāʾ, referring to the wretched. — Al-Ṭabarī reports Abū ʿAmr's reading; al-Durr reports that Ibn Masʿūd also read the verb with yāʾ. [2 notes: TAB(أبو عمرو); DURR-FULL(عرفجة الثقفي)]

## 87:17/q11 Which teaching here belongs to the earlier scriptures?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether 'this' in the next verses points to the nearby passage as a whole or specifically to 87:17; Ṭabarī appeals to the nearest referent.
- 87:17/q11/p1 The nearby passage from purification and prayer through the afterlife comparison belongs to them. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers 87:14–17 as the nearest referent of 'this' and says ṣuḥuf means the books or scrolls of Abraham and Moses. Al-Kalbī takes it from 'successful is one who purifies' through the sūra's end; al-Bursawī includes reward, soul purification, knowledge, obedience and warning against worldly distraction. A report says the Prophet recited this passage when asked what he received from the scrolls. [5 notes: THALABI(الكلبي); BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TAB-FULL+; TAB+]
- 87:17/q11/p2 The specific teaching is that the afterlife is better and more enduring than worldly life. — Ibn Zayd limits 'this' to the comparison; Qatāda says God's books repeatedly or successively teach it, including the earlier scrolls. Iṣlāḥī says the Prophet's message continues earlier prophetic teaching. [7 notes: THALABI(الكلبي,قتادة); DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 87:17/q12 How does this verse work in the sūra's argument?  [* · links]
turns on: These notes trace different connections: immediate rebuke, purification and prophetic teaching, or the sūra's broader movement.
- 87:17/q12/p1 It reinforces the rebuke of worldly preference by stating the better, lasting alternative. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says it exposes what the audience ignored; al-Ālūsī describes its circumstantial force. Corpus Coranicum sees a shift from address to the messenger into direct rebuke of hearers and a choice with eschatological consequences, mentioning 102:1. Kur'an Yolu connects 7:169, 12:109 and 93:4; Corpus Coranicum points to 93:4 for this vocabulary. [8 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 87:17/q12/p2 It connects the warning against worldly preference with purification and the prophetic path. — Al-Biqāʿī says the sūra proceeds to warn against turning from purification through routes other than the prophets' method; Qutb says the sūra grounds afterlife recompense in earlier scripture, beside God's oneness and revelation, and portrays an easy message, messenger and community. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 87:17/q12/p3 The sūra's wider movement contrasts good and bad and brings hearers to a consequential choice. — Corpus Coranicum says the contrast turns from an earlier typology to the listeners and argues that second-person rebukes and the parallel growth of text and community do not presuppose a formed community or only follower meetings. Qutb describes the cosmos as a sanctuary of praise and divine support for the Prophet; Iṣlāḥī says worldly attachment keeps the message from taking root. [4 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 87:17/q13 What sūra setting and recitation are reported alongside this verse?  [* · sciences]
- 87:17/q13/p1 Al-Aʿlā is Meccan, has nineteen verses, and the Prophet loved and recited it at Eid and Friday prayers with al-Ghāshiya, sometimes on the same day. — Qutb gives this sūra information and mentions 88:1. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]

## 87:17/q14 How do inward readings describe the lasting life?  [* · inward]
turns on: The inward notes emphasize true spiritual life, the inner kernel, or devotion to the Lord beyond both worlds.
- 87:17/q14/p1 True spiritual life, gained through purification, remembrance and prayer, outlasts sensory life. — Al-Qāshānī connects the sensory world's ornaments with neglect of remembrance and prayer. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 87:17/q14/p2 Inner reality is the lasting kernel; outward appearance is a quickly discarded shell. — Al-Bursawī uses the kernel and shell analogy and says people of inner truth choose the lasting life with God while outward-looking people are veiled. [2 notes: BURSEVI]
- 87:17/q14/p3 The highest aspiration prefers the Lord over both worlds. — Abū al-ʿAbbās al-Dīnawarī ranks low worldly ambition, elevated afterlife ambition, and noble states or sound realities that seek God. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو العباس الدينوري)]
