# 88:22: 16 questions

## 88:22/q01 What does muṣayṭir mean?  [بِمُصَيْطِرٍ · meaning]
turns on: The sources choose different aspects of control: authority, tyranny, mastery, oversight, or conquest.
- 88:22/q01/p1 One who has authority, control, or dominance over others. — Al-Farrāʾ, Ibn Qutayba, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī and others give power, control, or domination as the sense. Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites the Arabic idiom tasyṭara ʿalaynā fulān. Al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Kashshāf, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī and Corpus Coranicum compare 50:45; al-Rāzī also cites 10:99. Al-Rāghib, reported by Bursawī, likens it to standing over and maintaining something, with 13:33 and 6:107. Asad gives the literal sense as having power over them. [30 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); MAWARDI(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; TABRISI; RAZI(صاحب الكشاف); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI(الصحاح); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; DURR(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(الضحاك); ITQAN; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 88:22/q01/p2 A tyrant or coercive ruler who subdues and compels people. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid repeatedly gloss it as a tyrant; Ibn ʿAbbās adds pardon and forbearance in Ibn Abī Ḥātim and al-Durr. Qatāda speaks of subduing or compelling. Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds arrogant domination. Al-Kashshāf and others compare the denial of tyranny in 50:45. The contextual force is coercive power, especially over faith. [28 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB(ابن عباس,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR(ابن عباس ومجاهد وغيرهما); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); DURR(ابن عباس,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); ITQAN; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 88:22/q01/p3 A master, lord, or king over them. — Al-Ḥasan gives lord or master; Muqātil says the Prophet has no kingly authority; al-Kashshāf also says master. [4 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); KASHSHAF]
- 88:22/q01/p4 An overseer or recorder who watches and keeps account. — Al-Ṣiḥāḥ as reported by al-Qurṭubī includes overseeing, tending affairs and recording deeds; Bursawī calls this monitoring, and al-Qummī negates the Prophet's being a keeper or scribe. [4 notes: QURTUBI(الصحاح); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI(القمي)]
- 88:22/q01/p5 A conqueror over them. — Qatāda gives this gloss in both al-Durr versions. [2 notes: DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 88:22/q02 What is the derivation of muṣayṭir?  [بِمُصَيْطِرٍ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the proposed s-ṭ-r connection explains the word through writing, domination, or remains uncertain.
- 88:22/q02/p1 It derives from s-ṭ-r, writing or a bounded line. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Ṣiḥāḥ explain that a written line stays within bounds, and the overseer preserves matters in writing according to al-Jishumī. Al-Naḥḥās says sīn becomes ṣād before ṭāʾ; Bursawī likewise calls sīn original and ṣād assimilated for the following ṭāʾ. Corpus Coranicum attributes the writing link to Jeffery, while leaving the origin uncertain. [6 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI; QURTUBI(الصحاح); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 88:22/q02/p2 Its root expresses domination or overpowering. — Al-Ālūsī says saṭara ʿalayhi means he dominated him, and describes original sīn replaced by ṣād. Al-Qurṭubī adds that saṭarahu can mean knocking someone down. [4 notes: QURTUBI; ALUSI(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL]
- 88:22/q02/p3 Its etymology is uncertain. — Corpus Coranicum says the etymology is unclear even though Jeffery proposes s-ṭ-r, writing; it still accepts the contextual sense on comparison with 50:45. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM+]

## 88:22/q03 How is the form of muṣayṭir analyzed in Arabic?  [بِمُصَيْطِرٍ · grammar]
turns on: How many comparable Arabic forms count and whether the apparently similar names are diminutives.
- 88:22/q03/p1 It is a rare participial pattern shared with four named forms. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān list musayṭir, mubayṭir, mubayqir and muhaymin. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān suggest the names Mujaymir and Mudaybir could instead be diminutives. Abū Ḥayyān uses the verb sayṭara and its derivative or passive to support transitivity; al-Samīn says Hārūn's open-ṭāʾ form is a passive participle of this transitive verb. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(هارون)]
- 88:22/q03/p2 Only muṣayṭir and mubayṭir exemplify this pattern. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports Abū ʿUbayda's two-word inventory. [1 notes: TABRISI(أبو عبيدة)]
- 88:22/q03/p3 The proposed diminutive analysis of similar names is unsound. — Al-Samīn questions treating Mujaymir and Mudaybir as diminutives, recalling his judgment that a diminutive analysis of muhaymin at 5:48 was a grave error. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR+; IBNATIYYA-; ABUHAYYAN-]

## 88:22/q04 How is bi-muṣayṭir recited?  [بِمُصَيْطِرٍ · readings]

- 88:22/q04/p1 With initial ṣād and kasra on ṭāʾ. — Al-Farrāʾ notes that ṣād matches the written form and is valid; al-Nāshʾr details ṣād routes for Qunbul, Ibn Dhakwān, Ḥafṣ and Khallād, including split readings against 52:37. Al-Bursawī says the following ṭāʾ favors emphatic ṣād. ## 88:22/q05 Whom does ʿalayhim restrict the denial of control to?  [عَلَيْهِم · referent]
- 88:22/q05/p1 The people being addressed or warned, specifically these hearers. — Al-Biqāʿī says the preposition marks domination and limits the denial to those people; he expects the Prophet to gain authority over others later. Muqātil identifies the hearers as Mecca's people. Corpus Coranicum calls them the Messenger's hearers. [5 notes: MUQATIL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 88:22/q06 Why is ʿalayhim placed before bi-muṣayṭir?  [عَلَيْهِم بِمُصَيْطِرٍ · rhetoric]
- 88:22/q06/p1 To preserve the verse ending and cadence. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the prepositional complement precedes its predicate for the rhyme or cadence. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 88:22/q07 What task and limit does this statement give the Prophet?  [لَّسْتَ عَلَيْهِم بِمُصَيْطِرٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the limit refers to the lasting inability to produce inward belief or also to a temporary prohibition of fighting and killing.
- 88:22/q07/p1 He must remind, warn, and convey; he cannot force belief or hearts, and the outcome and reckoning belong to God. — Muqātil compares earlier prophets and Mecca. Al-Ṭabarī says leave them to God's judgment; Qatāda says God's servants belong to Him. Ibn Zayd denies forcing people back into faith. Al-Rāzī cites 50:45 and 10:99; al-Ṭabrisī adds patient invitation, not forcing faith into hearts. Ibn Kathīr says his commission is to convey; al-Qushayrī calls reminder his entrusted mission. Elmalılı says he cannot compel reflection, thoughts or hearts, citing 28:56. Quṭb says hearts remain under God's power and the preacher is freed from carrying the outcome. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the statement reassurance that continued disbelief is not his responsibility. Kur'an Yolu says faith and worship need inward conviction, citing 3:20, 16:82, 28:56 and 42:48; Study Quran lists 13:40, 3:20, 5:92, 5:99, 16:35, 16:82, 29:18, 36:17, 42:48 and 64:12. Corpus Coranicum also compares reminders in 51:55, 80:3-4 and 87:9-10. [53 notes: MUQATIL; TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن زيد); SULAMI(الواسطي); THALABI; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL(all exegetes); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR(ابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن زيد); BIQAI-FULL; DURR(قتادة,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 88:22/q07/p2 He was then barred from killing or coercing the rejecters. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī explicitly give a pre-fighting limit on killing as well as coercion, usually as the rule later abrogated. Al-Rāzī reports the same account of the commentators. [11 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL(all exegetes); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 88:22/q08 How does the clause relate to the preceding command to remind?  [لَّسْتَ عَلَيْهِم بِمُصَيْطِرٍ · links]
turns on: Whether the explanatory clause is described as apposition or interruption.
- 88:22/q08/p1 It restates and explains the restriction: the Prophet's charge is reminder, not control. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it an appositive elaboration of the preceding restriction's negative side; the command is to persist in reminder, and its omitted object is supplied by this clause. Innamā explains why he must continue despite inattention and reassures him that acceptance is not his charge. Al-Ālūsī says the clause confirms the warning's limit; al-Qushayrī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also make it the explanation of his mission. [9 notes: QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 88:22/q08/p2 It is an interrupting clause within the surrounding reminder and punishment passage. — Al-Qāshānī explicitly calls the reminder and non-control clause an interruption and cites 28:56 and 50:45. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 88:22/q09 Was the denial of control abrogated by fighting commands?  [لَّسْتَ عَلَيْهِم بِمُصَيْطِرٍ · sciences]
turns on: Whether the negation concerned a historical ban on fighting or the continuing inability to cause faith, and how later fighting is understood.
- 88:22/q09/p1 Yes, the earlier restraint was abrogated by the fighting or sword verse. — Muqātil, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī hold this; al-Rāzī attributes it to the commentators. Ibn Zayd cites 9:73 and 9:5 but says the duty of reminder in 51:55 remains; Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Naḥḥās's reported view specify killing polytheists wherever found at 9:5. Al-Wāḥidī describes a time before command to fight. Al-Biqāʿī locates the statement in the early Meccan period of pardon and expects authority over others later. [18 notes: TAB(ابن زيد); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); NAHHAS(بعضهم); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI(جميع المفسرين); RAZI-FULL(all exegetes); BIQAI-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL+; BAGHAWI+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; MUQATIL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; QURTUBI+; THALABI+; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون)+; TABRISI-; IBNASHUR-; IBNASHUR-FULL-; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazı müfessirler)-]
- 88:22/q09/p2 No, fighting does not annul the inability to compel inward faith. — Al-Naḥḥās reports that professing Muslims are left as they are, with lives and property protected except for due rights. Al-Ṭabrisī says jihad is not coercion, and rejection remains outside the Prophet's responsibility. Quṭb says later jihad removed barriers to hearing the message and persecution of converts. Ibn ʿĀshūr says combat followed polytheist aggression and expulsion, so the statement reassures rather than imposes a temporary ban. Kur'an Yolu says the Prophet never forced belief, while punitive action targeted harmful agitators. [6 notes: NAHHAS(بعضهم); QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNASHUR+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazı müfessirler)+; TABRISI+]

## 88:22/q10 What does the report recited with this verse say about fighting and reckoning?  [* · reports]
- 88:22/q10/p1 The Prophet was ordered to fight until profession of God's oneness; then life and property are protected except by right, while God judges the account. — Jābir transmits this wording with the verse recitation in al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās, Ibn Kathīr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī; al-Durr gives the substance. Ibn Kathīr names Aḥmad, Muslim, Tirmidhī and Nasāʾī for the recitation version, and Bukhārī and Muslim from Abū Hurayra without the verse. Al-Naḥḥās uses the report for the view that the verse is not abrogated. [9 notes: TAB(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TAB-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله عن رسول الله); NAHHAS(بعضهم,جابر); IBNKATHIR(جابر عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(جابر); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]

## 88:22/q11 How is the following exception, 'whoever turns away,' attached?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Which neighboring clause the exception in verse 23 qualifies.
- 88:22/q11/p1 It excepts those rejecters from the command to remind because reminder will not benefit them. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports this connection to the imperative 'remind.' [1 notes: THALABI(بعضهم)]
- 88:22/q11/p2 It excepts rejecters from 'you are not a controller,' allowing fighting until they submit. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports this connection. Corpus Coranicum rejects treating illā as giving Muhammad power over unbelievers because verses 21-22 deny coercion. [1 notes: THALABI(قيل); CORPUSCORANICUM-]
- 88:22/q11/p3 It attaches to the following statement that God will punish whoever turns away and disbelieves. — Al-Thaʿlabī gives this third connection; his next note cites Ibn Masʿūd's reading that explicitly says God will punish the rejecter. [2 notes: THALABI(قيل)]

## 88:22/q12 What does the adjoining punishment and return passage add?  [* · interpretation]
- 88:22/q12/p1 The rejecter faces God's greater punishment and final reckoning. — Al-Thaʿlabī identifies the greater punishment as Fire, beyond worldly hunger, drought, killing and captivity, and says Ibn Masʿūd's reading expressly attributes the punishment to God. Al-Qāshānī interprets the great Fire inwardly as absolute veiling at all levels of existence after turning away from ascent toward God. [2 notes: THALABI; QASHANI]

## 88:22/q13 What other reading of the adjoining return passage is reported?  [* · readings]
- 88:22/q13/p1 Abū Jaʿfar doubles the yāʾ of iyābah. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports the reading from Ibn Jaʿfar and Abū Ḥātim's grammatical rejection. [1 notes: THALABI(ابن جعفر)]

## 88:22/q14 What are the limits of invoking this verse for freedom within the Muslim community?  [* · law]
- 88:22/q14/p1 It does not guarantee that freedom to a Muslim who repudiates the faith or a necessary tenet. — Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes relations with polytheists from the Muslim community. He says an apostate is asked to repent for three days and, if unrepentant, killed; if inaccessible, expelled and treated as a combatant. The same applies after an explained denial of a necessary tenet, if the person persists without an acceptable interpretation or desisting. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNASHUR+]

## 88:22/q15 How does this address function in the sūra and neighboring passages?  [* · links]
- 88:22/q15/p1 It consoles the Prophet while closing a sūra about resurrection, signs, and return to God. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the sūra contrasts punishment and reward, rebukes neglect of creation's evidence for oneness and resurrection, then strengthens the Prophet against rejection and warns of recompense. Corpus Coranicum says this direct address is integrated into broader literary context and typical of group-II sūra endings, unlike shorter consolations at 93, 94 and 108. Elmalılı says the next sūra extends these reflections through changing time and final return. Al-Rāzī points back to the word's discussion under the controllers in 52:37. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; ELMALILI; RAZI]

## 88:22/q16 What merit is reported for reciting al-Ghāshiya?  [* · reports]
- 88:22/q16/p1 A report promises an easy reckoning to its reciter. — Al-Bayḍāwī transmits this report from the Prophet. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
