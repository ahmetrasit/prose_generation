# 7:165: 17 questions

## 7:165/q01 What does their forgetting of the reminder mean?  [نَسُوا۟ ذُكِّرُوا۟ · interpretation]
- 7:165/q01/p1 They deliberately abandoned or disregarded the warning, acting as though it had been forgotten. — Muqātil specifies the ban on fishing; Ibn Jurayj connects the reminder to the believers in 7:164. Al-Zajjāj and al-Bayḍāwī allow comparison with literal forgetting. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls forgetting the strongest form of leaving; al-Qurṭubī compares 9:67. Abū Ḥayyān stresses the pronoun refers to the warned offenders. Al-Biqāʿī says they ignored the threatened consequence. Al-Ālūsī prefers a metaphor over a causal metonymy and excludes innocent literal lapse; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 6:44. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says repeated sin weakens the reminder until it ceases to affect them. Al-Itqān glosses dhikr as admonition. [32 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن جريج); TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن جريج,ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 7:165/q02 What does mā in 'what they were reminded of' refer to?  [مَا ذُكِّرُوا۟ بِهِۦٓ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā names the reminded object or can stand for the reminding or its contents.
- 7:165/q02/p1 It is a relative pronoun referring to the thing they were reminded about. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn call this the apparent construction; al-Ālūsī agrees while allowing a verbal noun. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:165/q02/p2 It may denote the act of reminding or admonition itself. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya offers the verbal-noun reading; Abū Ḥayyān and the Shaykh find it unpersuasive, while al-Ālūsī allows it. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 7:165/q02/p3 It may denote the subject matter or contents of the reminder. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes this from the reminder itself; Abū Ḥayyān and the Shaykh say the analysis is not apparent. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]

## 7:165/q03 Which groups were saved or seized, and what became of the group that questioned further warning?  [أَنجَيْنَا ٱلَّذِينَ يَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ ٱلسُّوٓءِ وَأَخَذْنَا ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the questioners' objection counted as condemning evil, and whether the verse's silence about them implies rescue, punishment, or no conclusion.
- 7:165/q03/p1 The warners and the group that questioned further warnings were saved; the fishers alone were punished. — ʿIkrima says the questioners condemned the sin, and Ibn ʿAbbās accepted his inference and gave him cloaks. Al-Ḥasan says their mention of threatened punishment was itself warning, and killing a believer is graver than eating fish. Ibn Kathīr prefers Ibn ʿAbbās's later view despite a sound alternative report; the verse names only fishers as seized. Al-Rāzī invokes the communal nature of forbidding evil. Al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr count both groups as warners; some had stopped when warnings seemed futile. Al-Nasafī includes questioners among the saved. [28 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن جريج عن عكرمة,الحسن,يمان); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس وعكرمة,يمان بن رباب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عكرمة والحسن وغيرهما); TABRISI(الحسن,السدي); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); NASAFI-FULL(الحسن); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); BURSEVI(الحسن)+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 7:165/q03/p2 Only the active forbidders were saved; both the offenders and the silent or questioning group perished. — Ibn Zayd calls this the strongest warning against neglecting the duty. Ibn ʿAbbās in one account says silence about wrong brings destruction. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says failure to admonish or separate from wrongdoers makes the questioners complicit; some reports add that abstainers were transformed into tiny particles. The variants are judged weak and inconsistent by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī. [19 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد,ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن زيد); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما); ALUSI-FULL(الطبرسي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,جعفر بن محمد عن أبيه عليهما السلام); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Abdullah b. Abbas,başka bir rivayet,bir görüş); BURSEVI(الحسن)-; TABATABAI-]
- 7:165/q03/p3 The verse states the warners' rescue and the fishers' punishment but leaves the questioners' fate unstated. — Ibn ʿAbbās says he cannot determine their fate from the wording; al-Jubbāʾī prefers restraint. Ibn Kathīr says they are neither praised nor condemned expressly, though he ultimately favors their survival. The Turkish commentary and Study Quran similarly note the open question. [14 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس عن عطاء بن السائب); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس وعكرمة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,الطبرسي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Abdullah b. Abbas); STUDYQURAN(commentators); TABRISI(عكرمة)+]
- 7:165/q03/p4 The active warners were saved, silent abstainers were transformed, and active sinners perished. — Reports from Abū ʿAbd Allāh and Abū Jaʿfar distinguish three punishments. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the related versions weak in chain and confused in wording. [2 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام,أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); TABATABAI-]
- 7:165/q03/p5 The text explicitly says God saved those forbidding evil and seized the offending wrongdoers. — The sources often state this without deciding the third group's fate; Muqātil and al-Ṭabarī identify the wrong as making prohibited fishing lawful. Al-Biqāʿī emphasizes continued forbidding. Asad notes the literal wording. [20 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABRISI; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI(علي عليه السلام); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:165/q04 How many factions did the townspeople form?  [ٱلَّذِينَ يَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ ٱلسُّوٓءِ ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا۟ · reports]
turns on: Whether the questioners in 7:164 are a separate group or belong with the warners.
- 7:165/q04/p1 Three: fishers, active admonishers, and abstainers who questioned or did not continue admonishing. — Al-Wāḥidī prefers this because the admonishers say 'perhaps they will fear God' in the preceding verse, speaking of the sinners in the third person. Ibn Kathīr describes withdrawal of the warners. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports a version with those who desisted and withdrew, those who stayed without sinning, and sinners. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام,جعفر بن محمد عن أبيه عليهما السلام); RAZI-FULL(الواحدي)+]
- 7:165/q04/p2 Two: Sabbath offenders and believers who refrained and warned, including the concerned questioners. — The two-faction account treats the question to the warners as coming from within the righteous faction; al-Wāḥidī argues against this from its third-person phrasing. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ; RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي)-]

## 7:165/q05 What is the evil that was forbidden?  [ٱلسُّوٓءِ · meaning]
turns on: General lexical scope versus the particular Sabbath act in this story.
- 7:165/q05/p1 It is disobedience and sin, especially making what God forbade lawful. — Muqātil calls it sins; al-Ṭabarī mentions disobedience and legalizing the forbidden. Al-Māwardī says the reminder enjoined right and forbade wrong. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān say this is the word's general scope. [6 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 7:165/q05/p2 In this incident it specifically means fishing for Sabbath fish. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān distinguish the story's particular offense from the general sense of evil; Bursevī says the forbidders forbade fishing. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 7:165/q05/p3 The precise wrong forbidden is left unstated in one Ibn ʿAbbās query. — Al-Ṭabarī transmits Ibn ʿAbbās asking what specific evil the admonishers had forbidden. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 7:165/q06 Who are the 'wrongdoers,' and why are they called that?  [ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether omission of the silent group from the explicit seizure excludes it or its failure to intervene counts as wrongdoing.
- 7:165/q06/p1 They are the Sabbath fishers who disobeyed God and treated the ban as lawful. — Al-Ṭabarī identifies fishing and eating prohibited fish; al-Thaʿlabī and Abū Ḥayyān say the title points to the cause of seizure. Ibn ʿĀshūr says disobedience wrongs one's own soul by denying God's right to obedience. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:165/q06/p2 They may include those who stayed silent and failed to forbid or leave wrongdoing. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the verse explicitly names only the fishers as seized, yet silent complicity may fall within 'wrongdoers'; he argues failure to admonish or shun makes one liable. [4 notes: TABATABAI]

## 7:165/q07 What does baʾīs describe the punishment as?  [بَعَذَابٍۭ بَـِٔيسٍۭ · meaning]
turns on: Which sense of the baʾs/buʾs root is foregrounded: severity, vileness, poverty, or destruction.
- 7:165/q07/p1 Severe, painful, grievous punishment. — Mujāhid and Qatāda give severe and painful; Ibn ʿAbbās says aching or without mercy. Al-Zajjāj, al-Naḥḥās and al-Thaʿlabī derive it from baʾs or buʾs, intensity. Al-Fārisī distinguishes a severe adjective from a verbal noun for distress; Abū Zayd distinguishes formidable baʾs from poverty forms. Al-Rāghib notes hardship and punitive calamity. Ibn Kathīr regards the painful and severe glosses as close; Ibn ʿĀshūr says all readings signify severe harm. Dhū al-Aṣbaʿ's verse witnesses the term. [38 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,مجاهد); NAHHAS; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو زيد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL(أبو علي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,الراغب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 7:165/q07/p2 Bad or vile punishment. — Al-Akhfash gives bad or vile; ʿAlī ibn Sulaymān cites banāt bīs for worthless things. The blame-verb readings yield 'bad is the punishment.' [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الأخفش); NAHHAS(علي بن سليمان); QURTUBI-FULL(علي بن سليمان); THALABI(الحسن); JISHUMI(الحسن)]
- 7:165/q07/p3 A punishment marked by poverty or misery. — Al-Māwardī reports this gloss. Abū Zayd and al-Rāghib distinguish poverty and hardship senses of buʾs and baʾsāʾ; Elmalılı understands a punishment that wrings them with poverty. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(أبو زيد); ELMALILI; RAZI-FULL(أبو علي); ALUSI-FULL(الراغب)]
- 7:165/q07/p4 A destructive punishment. — Abū Ḥayyān attributes this gloss to al-Akhfash. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش)]

## 7:165/q08 How is baʾīs read?  [بَـِٔيسٍۭ · readings]
turns on: Differences in hamza, vowel, yāʾ, and pattern; authorities test forms against attested Arabic morphology, poetic witnesses, and transmission chains.
- 7:165/q08/p1 Baʾīs, with open bāʾ, hamza kasra and following yāʾ, on faʿīl. — Ascribed to Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr, Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, Ḥafṣ and many others. Al-Ṭabarī prefers it because exegetes agree on severity; Abū ʿUbayd and Abū Ḥātim prefer its familiar adjectival shape. Dhū al-Aṣbaʿ's line supports it. It can be an intensive adjective or a verbal noun used adjectivally. Abū Bakr doubted his earlier attribution to ʿĀṣim and took this from al-Aʿmash; later routes preserve both. - 7:165/q08/p2 Biʾīs, the faʿīl form with kasra on bāʾ. — Attributed to some Meccans or Ibn Kathīr; grammarians explain first-radical kasra before a guttural second radical, and Abū Ḥātim calls it a dialect. Abū Ḥātim says it requires an overt subject or complement; al-Naḥḥās cites ellipsis such as fa-bihā wa-niʿmat to supply 'punishment.' Al-Samīn nevertheless favors the normal requirement. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(آخر منهم); NAHHAS(الحسن); THALABI(الحسن); JISHUMI(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن,الحسن بن أبي الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن ونافع وأبو بكر,النحاس); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن,النحاس); NAHHAS(أبو حاتم)-; TABRISI(أبو حاتم)-; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حاتم)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حاتم)-; SAMIN-DURR(أبو حاتم)-]
- 7:165/q08/p9 Biʾs with open bāʾ, kasra hamza and open sīn. — Yaʿqūb transmits it from some readers; comparable readings are attributed to ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar and Zayd ibn ʿAlī, with patterns shahid or kabid. [5 notes: NAHHAS(يعقوب القارئ); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة عن يعقوب); QURTUBI-FULL(يعقوب القارىء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(يعقوب القارئ); SAMIN-DURR(يعقوب وعيسى بن عمر وزيد بن علي)]
- 7:165/q08/p10 Bāʾis, with long ā and hamza, on fāʿil. — Attributed to Mujāhid, Abū Rajāʾ and Abū al-Mutawakkil; al-Ālūsī says the form may mean one possessing strength. [7 notes: THALABI(مجاهد); JISHUMI(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو رجاء); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو المتوكل وأبو رجاء); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:165/q08/p11 Bīs or bays with open bāʾ and unhamzated yāʾ, either still or open. — Reported from Khārija from Nāfiʿ, Ubayy, Abū Iyās, Ṭalḥa and Naṣr routes. Some derive it by hamza easing and deletion, or by transferring hamza's vowel to yāʾ; the open yāʾ may remain because its vowel is incidental. Reports differ about final case vowel. [12 notes: IBNMUJAHID(نافع,نافع عن أبي قرة، خارجة); FARISI-HUJJA(نافع); THALABI(أبو أياس); JISHUMI(أبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة,مالك بن دينار عن نصر بن عاصم,نافع فيما يروي عنه خارجة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(خارجة عن نافع); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,طلحة وخارجة عن نافع)]
- 7:165/q08/p12 Bīyyis, without hamza and with a doubled yāʾ. — Attributed to Naṣr ibn ʿĀṣim and to al-Ḍaḥḥāk and ʿIkrima; it may derive directly from a nonhamzated root or by turning hamza to yāʾ and assimilating. A report from Naṣr through ʿĀṣim compares mayyit. [8 notes: NAHHAS(نصر بن عاصم); THALABI(نصر بن عاصم); JISHUMI(نصر بن عاصم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو الفتح,نصر عن عاصم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك وعكرمة); QURTUBI-FULL(نصر بن عاصم); SAMIN-DURR(نصر بن عاصم)]
- 7:165/q08/p13 A form with kasra bāʾ, still hamza and open yāʾ. — Transmitted by Yaʿqūb and attributed to al-Ḥasan and al-Aʿmash; Abū Ḥātim weakens it. Al-Samīn cites patterns ḥudhīm and ʿuthayr. [3 notes: NAHHAS(يعقوب القارئ); QURTUBI-FULL(بعض القراء); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن والأعمش); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن والأعمش)-]
- 7:165/q08/p14 Baʾs or biʾs as a short form or past verb, with several hamza and final-vowel variants. — Mālik ibn Dīnār and other groups transmit both declinable and indeclinable forms. Naṣr and al-Aʿmash have past-verb interpretations; al-Samīn explains a doubled hamza by assimilation, while another group lightens it. Reports also vary between alif and hamza. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعمش,فرقة,مالك بن دينار); QURTUBI-FULL(الأعمش); SAMIN-DURR(الأعمش ومالك بن دينار,طائفة,طائفة أخرى,نصر بن عاصم,نصر في رواية مالك بن دينار)]
- 7:165/q08/p15 Rare additional forms include bayās, biʾyas, baʾyas and biyās. — Ibn Khālawayh lists bayās among five forms. Al-Samīn records Abū al-Baqāʾ's baʾyas as remote because its pattern is unattested and biyās on rare fiyʿāl; Ibn ʿAbbās, Abū Razīn and Ayyūb are linked to biyās. The variants retain differing root analyses and vocalizations. [7 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وأبو رزين وأيوب); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:165/q08/p16 The authorities differ in how many forms to count: five, eleven, twenty-two, or twenty-six. — Ibn Khālawayh lists five; al-Jishumī counts eleven, five canonical and six beyond; the Shaykh classifies twenty-two by three- or four-letter form and noun or verb; Abū al-Baqāʾ's additions bring al-Samīn's total to twenty-six. Other sources note many variants without counting them. [9 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المدينة وابن عامر وأبو جعفر ونافع وعاصم والآخرون); NASAFI-FULL(شامى ومدنى وأبو بكر); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,الشيخ); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABRISI]

## 7:165/q09 What punishment was inflicted, and how does it relate to the transformation in 7:166?  [وَأَخَذْنَا بَعَذَابٍۭ بَـِٔيسٍۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 7:166 explains the torment named here or narrates a later, harsher stage.
- 7:165/q09/p1 The severe punishment was the transformation into apes, and in some reports swine; 7:166 explains it. — Muqātil, al-Wāḥidī, al-Dāmghānī and Ibn al-Jawzī identify the torment with metamorphosis. One Ibn ʿAbbās account says both apes and swine; Qatāda says young became apes and old swine. Ibn ʿĀshūr says 7:166 restates the offense while specifying the punishment. Abū ʿUbayda's gloss of khāsiʾīn is driven away or expelled. [10 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); MAJAZ]
- 7:165/q09/p2 A severe punishment came first; continued defiance brought the later transformation. — Al-Jubbāʾī explicitly puts torment before metamorphosis. Al-Rāzī calls them distinct. Al-Bayḍāwī says the surface sequence supports two stages; Bursevī suggests the first did not exterminate them, so they increased in error. Ibn ʿĀshūr says later witnesses persisted and then received the harsher transformation. [5 notes: TABRISI(الجبائي); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل)]
- 7:165/q09/p3 God's seizure destroyed or punished the persistent Sabbath offenders for their transgression. — Al-Ṭabarī describes destruction for making forbidden fish lawful; al-Thaʿlabī and Ibn Kathīr connect the punishment with repeated rejection of counsel. Ibn ʿAbbās says disobedience brought divine abandonment; al-Zajjāj says persistence intensified the trial. Al-Biqāʿī says the promised warning was fulfilled. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:165/q10 What does fisq mean, and what caused the seizure?  [بِمَا كَانُوا۟ يَفْسُقُونَ · interpretation]
- 7:165/q10/p1 Fisq is habitual disobedience, departure from obedience, and transgression; it caused their punishment. — Muqātil glosses it as disobedience; al-Ṭabarī specifies leaving obedience for sin. Bursevī adds injustice and aggression; al-Biqāʿī emphasizes renewed acts. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the punishment requital, and al-Bayḍāwī attributes it to transgressing God's command. Al-Zajjāj describes a defiant person as unresponsive to admonition. [11 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 7:165/q11 How does the final causal phrase attach grammatically?  [بِمَا كَانُوا۟ يَفْسُقُونَ · grammar]
- 7:165/q11/p1 It modifies 'We seized'; the two causal or accompanying prepositions can coexist because their meanings differ. — Bursevī and al-Ālūsī say the earlier bi introduces the severe punishment, whereas bi-mā supplies the cause; al-Ālūsī adds that wrongdoing itself compounds the wrong. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:165/q12 Why is 'punishment' indefinite and why does the passage expand the account?  [بَعَذَابٍۭ بَـِٔيسٍۭ · rhetoric]
- 7:165/q12/p1 Indefiniteness magnifies the punishment and its horror. — Al-Ālūsī explicitly gives this rhetorical effect. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:165/q12/p2 The expanded wording intensifies the account while presenting the reminder and prohibition as the same matter. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the differing phrases multiply the forms of punishment and compares Labīd's repeated simile, judging the Qur'anic phrasing more eloquent and beneficial. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(لبيد)]

## 7:165/q13 What duty to oppose wrongdoing does this verse teach?  [ٱلَّذِينَ يَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ ٱلسُّوٓءِ · law]
turns on: Whether abstaining and disapproving suffices once active warners have acted, or whether one must admonish or withdraw.
- 7:165/q13/p1 Forbidding wrong is a communal duty; performance by some discharges it for the others. — Al-Rāzī gives the communal-obligation rule; the Turkish commentary extends forms of opposition to force, speech and writing. Al-Ālūsī quotes al-Zamakhsharī that when warning surely has no effect one is relieved and may need to stop, illustrating with extortionists; those who kept warning may have been less despairing or especially persistent. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 7:165/q13/p2 Failure to forbid or shun wrongdoers can make an abstainer complicit and liable to punishment. — Ibn Zayd calls the verse the strongest warning against neglecting this duty; al-Baghawī repeats the warning. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī states the rule even where direct restraint cannot work, requiring admonition or withdrawal. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABATABAI(علي عليه السلام)]

## 7:165/q14 Where was the Sabbath town and who were its inhabitants?  [* · reports]
turns on: Conflicting reports on the settlement and the people's lineage.
- 7:165/q14/p1 It was Aylah, inhabited by a Jewish or Israelite clan. — Ibn ʿAbbās places Aylah between Egypt and Medina and says fish appeared there on Saturdays; other reports identify the people as a Jewish clan from Aylah. His fishing report says Satan suggested taking fish on Saturday and eating later. [4 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(ابن عباس)]
- 7:165/q14/p2 They were people of Thamud, perhaps Arabs who had adopted Judaism near Aylah. — A report from Abū Jaʿfar names Thamud; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī proposes conversion to Judaism as a reconciliation with the Aylah account. [2 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام)]
- 7:165/q14/p3 The town was Midian. — Reported as an unnamed alternative. [1 notes: TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 7:165/q14/p4 The town was Tiberias. — Reported as an unnamed alternative. [1 notes: TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 7:165/q14/p5 The town was Maqna, between Midian and Aynuna. — Reported as an unnamed alternative. [1 notes: TABATABAI(قيل)]

## 7:165/q15 What happened in the Sabbath violation and discovery of the transformed people?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on whether transformation was bodily or inward and on recognition and subsequent events.
- 7:165/q15/p1 A group took Sabbath fish through a loophole, ate them later, and was transformed; the apes recognized former human kin. — Ibn ʿAbbās says Satan suggested only eating was banned; others said catching was banned too. One man tethered a fish and openly ate it later; Ibn Rūmān says the tactic spread. Warners withdrew, later found the city shut, and apes approached their relatives, wept or nodded when asked whether they had been warned. Ibn Zayd adds David's curse and a separating wall. Ibn Kathīr mentions related reports in al-Baqara and al-ʿAwfī's parallel account. [15 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن رومان,ابن عباس,مجاهد); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام)]
- 7:165/q15/p2 The transformed apes recognized the humans but were unrecognized by them and died after three days. — Al-Bayḍāwī reports a wall and gate, the missing offenders, their circling the admonishers, weeping and sniffing clothes, then dying three days later; this differs in recognition and ending from other accounts. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 7:165/q15/p3 Their hearts, rather than bodies, became like apes. — Al-Bayḍāwī attributes this account to Mujāhid. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 7:165/q16 How does this verse relate to the preceding warning and the later Israelite narrative?  [* · links]
- 7:165/q16/p1 It fulfills the preceding admonishers' warning and briefly identifies the warned, rescued and punished groups. — Al-Bayḍāwī calls the outcome what the righteous had warned about. Ibn ʿĀshūr says 7:164 shows the questioners reacted after advice failed, while 7:165 summarizes the prior history. Ibn ʿĀshūr says 7:166 then expands the punishment. Al-Biqāʿī similarly calls it fulfillment of warning. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL]
- 7:165/q16/p2 The episode illustrates Israel's recurrent breach of covenant and anticipates later generations' neglect and punishment. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī links these breaches to descendants who sold scripture's signs cheaply and relaxed observance, with a faithful few remaining. Study Quran says the narrative returns to later Israelite disobedience and future punishments. [2 notes: TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:165/q17 Was this passage revealed in Mecca or Medina?  [* · sciences]
- 7:165/q17/p1 The section containing this verse is an exception to the sura's Meccan revelation. — Al-Burhān says al-Aʿrāf is Meccan except three verses starting with the command to ask about the town and ending at the raised mountain; Study Quran cites Al and IJ for 7:163–167 as Medinan, a differing span. [2 notes: BURHAN; STUDYQURAN(Al, IJ)]
