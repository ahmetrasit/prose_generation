# 80:7: 15 questions

## 80:7/q01 How is mā and the clause constructed?  [وَمَا عَلَيْكَ أَلَّا يَزَّكَّىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā negates the clause or asks an incredulous question; the negative analysis also specifies the predicate, subject, and circumstantial attachment.
- 80:7/q01/p1 Mā negates responsibility; ʿalayka is the predicate and the purification clause its subject. — Al-Samīn identifies the subject and predicate. Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies that ʿalayka is fronted and the subject delayed. Būrsawī makes the clause circumstantial to taṣaddā and takes an implied fī as linked to an omitted noun; Ālūsī also makes it circumstantial to the subject of taṣaddā. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this negative parse. [6 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 80:7/q01/p2 Mā introduces an incredulous question about what burden would fall on the Prophet if the man did not purify himself. — Biqāʿī proposes the interrogative wording; Ālūsī treats it as a repudiating question with the same practical import as the negative parse. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports it as the second reading and ranks it after his preferred contextual reading. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]

## 80:7/q02 What is the Prophet's stake in the man's failure to purify himself?  [وَمَا عَلَيْكَ أَلَّا يَزَّكَّىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the wording is read as a negation of accountability, an incredulous question about obligation, or a contextual call to stop troubling over the rich man's refusal; Ṭabāṭabāʾī explicitly ranks these.
- 80:7/q02/p1 He bears no fault or responsibility for the man's refusal; his charge is to convey the message. — Muqātil includes failure to believe or repair corruption. Al-Zajjāj frames the refusal as declining an invitation to Islam. Al-Qushayrī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī and others restrict the Prophet's duty to communication. Al-Bayḍāwī invokes 42:48. Al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, Ālūsī and others say zeal for conversion must not divert him from a believer. Al-Biqāʿī says even minimal purification is outside the Prophet's responsibility. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this divine gentleness and reassurance: no exertion beyond God's charge is required. Elmalılı says efforts toward unbelievers remain permissible, while ignoring a Muslim seeker entails responsibility. Islāḥī specifies warning of the Hereafter as part of his charge. Asad gives the literal gloss that the man's purity is not upon the Prophet. [24 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 80:7/q02/p2 His concern for an unreceptive wealthy person's purification should cease; its failure cannot harm or grieve him. — Al-Jishumī reports the alternative wording as a question about grief, adding that unbelief cannot hurt the Prophet and his duty is conveyance. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the man may neither purify himself nor prosper, so the Prophet should not concern himself with such people. Abū Ḥayyān says the phrase diminishes the unbeliever's importance. Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this contextual thrust over the interrogative and negative readings. [5 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI+]
- 80:7/q02/p3 The verse asks incredulously what obligation or harm could fall on him if the man remains impure. — Al-Biqāʿī gives the question of burden. Ālūsī says its force is the same as the negative construction. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports that purification here means cleansing disbelief and sin, and that the messenger is only charged with conveyance; he ranks this reading second. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]

## 80:7/q03 What does yazzakkā mean here?  [يَزَّكَّىٰ · meaning]
- 80:7/q03/p1 To attain religious purification by accepting faith or Islam, leaving unbelief, and finding guidance. — Muqātil adds mending what the man corrupted. Al-Baghawī speaks of belief or guidance; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān also express the outcome as prospering. Al-Ṭabrisī specifies leaving disbelief, Ṭabāṭabāʾī mentions cleansing disbelief and sin, and Būrsawī says purification by Islam. Al-Biqāʿī includes even a minimal degree of purification. [24 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ASAD-NOTES]

## 80:7/q04 Who is the person whose purification is at issue?  [يَزَّكَّىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular wording is applied to the wealthy man individually or to the group of men being addressed.
- 80:7/q04/p1 The self-sufficient, wealthy, disbelieving man whom the Prophet was addressing. — Ibn Kathīr identifies him as a rich man whom the Prophet hoped to guide. Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī identify the wealthy man; al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān call him an unbeliever. Al-Biqāʿī calls him proud, and Būrsawī self-satisfied. Biqāʿī describes a Quraysh notable whose conversion was hoped to benefit his kin. [13 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 80:7/q04/p2 The group of men whom the Prophet was trying to bring to faith. — Muqātil applies the verse to the group, expressing their possible refusal as failure to believe or amend corruption. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 80:7/q05 How should the Prophet direct his attention after this rebuke?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes distinguish neglect of a ready seeker, attention to a person with no receptivity, and continued guidance efforts where acceptance can reasonably be expected.
- 80:7/q05/p1 Give the receptive believer and humble seeker their due attention instead of letting hope for a wealthy person's conversion draw him away. — Al-Qushayrī says the passage reproaches the Prophet on behalf of the poor. Al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, Būrsawī and Ālūsī warn against neglecting an already believing person for hoped-for conversion. Al-Biqāʿī says Ibn Maktūm sought instruction while the Prophet hoped a notable's conversion would save the man and his kin; he cites the command to remain with those who seek God and not drive them away (18:28, 39:65, 38:88, 31:18). Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts the wealthy who scorn truth with the earnest seeker who fears God. Elmalılı distinguishes the Prophet's responsibility to the Muslim seeker from permitted guidance efforts toward unbelievers. [9 notes: QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 80:7/q05/p2 Do not press an unreceptive worldly person; the one who turns away is belittled by this wording. — Al-Wāsiṭī reads contempt for the one who turns away. Abū Ḥayyān says the wording diminishes the unbeliever's importance and urges attention away from him. Būrsawī specifies someone who loves this world, is blind to the next, and shows no receptivity to Islam. [3 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 80:7/q05/p3 Continue trying to purify a follower when there is reason to expect acceptance of guidance. — Al-Biqāʿī expressly retains this duty. Elmalılı says attempts to guide unbelievers are permissible in principle, though they do not excuse neglecting a Muslim seeking instruction. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 80:7/q06 What inward point is made about addressing one whom God has not guided?  [* · inward]
- 80:7/q06/p1 The Prophet gained no honor by being directed toward one whom God had not honored with guidance or gnosis. — Jaʿfar al-Khuldī makes the distinction depend on God's conferral of guidance and knowledge. [1 notes: SULAMI(جعفر الخلدي)]

## 80:7/q07 Who is the subject of the passage's rebuke?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the unnamed subject may be identified as the Prophet given his noble character and the narrated occasion.
- 80:7/q07/p1 The Prophet is rebuked for turning from Ibn Umm Maktūm while seeking to convert Quraysh leaders. — Al-Jishumī reports the account from al-Ḥasan and others; al-Ṭabrisī gives the encounter and motive, and ʿĀʾisha's account in al-Durr says the cited passage was revealed then. Al-Qushayrī describes the reproach as on behalf of the poor. Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes the rebuked pattern as the Prophet attending to wealthy scorners while neglecting the fearful seeker. [7 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); QUSHAYRI; TABRISI(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عائشة); TABATABAI; TABRISI(المرتضى علم الهدى)-]
- 80:7/q07/p2 The text does not establish that the Prophet was the one who frowned and preferred the rich. — Al-Murtaḍā argues that the Prophet is not named; frowning at a believing seeker or preferring wealthy people to the poor would conflict with his noble character. [1 notes: TABRISI(المرتضى علم الهدى)]

## 80:7/q08 What encounter occasioned the admonition?  [* · reports]
- 80:7/q08/p1 Ibn Umm Maktūm interrupted the Prophet's appeal to Quraysh leaders, asking for instruction, and the Prophet turned away. — Al-Jishumī attributes the occasion to al-Ḥasan and a group. Al-Ṭabrisī also names Ibn Umm Maktūm as ʿAbd Allāh ibn Shurayḥ ibn Mālik ibn Rabīʿa al-Fihrī; his list of leaders is ʿUtba ibn Rabīʿa, Abū Jahl, al-ʿAbbās, Ubayy, and Umayya ibn Khalaf. He says the repeated requests drew a turn away because the Prophet feared the leaders would scorn followers who were blind or enslaved. ʿĀʾisha's report in al-Durr specifically includes Abū Jahl and ʿUtba and quotes 80:5–10. Al-Biqāʿī identifies the visitor as the Prophet's muezzin and says the hoped-for conversion of a Quraysh notable might have helped his kin. [6 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABRISI(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عائشة)]

## 80:7/q09 How did the Prophet treat Ibn Umm Maktūm afterward?  [* · reports]
- 80:7/q09/p1 He honored and welcomed him as the man over whom God reproached him, asked after his needs, and, in one account, deputized him in Medina twice. — Al-Jishumī reports the greeting and inquiry. Al-Ṭabrisī repeats them and adds the two appointments as deputy. [2 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABRISI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 80:7/q10 What does Anas report about Ibn Umm Maktūm at al-Qādisiyya?  [* · reports]
- 80:7/q10/p1 Anas saw him there wearing armor and carrying a black banner or standard. — Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī transmit Anas's report; their words for the black flag vary slightly. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أنس); TABRISI(أنس بن مالك)]

## 80:7/q11 What does the reported saying claim about concealment of this passage?  [* · reports]
- 80:7/q11/p1 If the Messenger had concealed revelation, he would have concealed this reproaching passage. — Al-Jishumī attributes this saying to Ibn Zayd. [1 notes: JISHUMI(ابن زيد)]

## 80:7/q12 What is the force of the switch to direct address?  [* · rhetoric]
- 80:7/q12/p1 The direct address continues a stronger rebuke, without honorific or conciliatory intimacy. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the proposed honorific reading of the third-to-second-person switch because the direct address that follows is more reproachful. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 80:7/q13 How does the surrounding passage imply an unspoken contrast?  [* · rhetoric]
- 80:7/q13/p1 It uses ḥtibāk: the rich person's mention implies the poor counterpart, while the seeker's coming and fear imply their opposites in the first case. — Al-Biqāʿī identifies this pattern of reciprocal omission. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 80:7/q14 Why are humble and afflicted people more receptive than the self-sufficient?  [* · interpretation]
- 80:7/q14/p1 Luxury and self-sufficiency favor rejection, while affliction can humble and purify hearts, making the overlooked receptive to fear. — Al-Biqāʿī contrasts readiness for disbelief and wrongdoing in luxury with the softening effect of trials. Ibn al-Zubayr says the overlooked humble receive special divine care and cites the prior calls to heed and fear (79:26, 79:45). A reported saying about a disheveled, dusty person whom others ignore, yet whose oath God would fulfill, illustrates hidden rank. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 80:7/q15 How does this admonition connect with the surrounding sūra and other passages?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources draw different contextual links: the prior sūra's warning, commands concerning humble seekers, the messenger's duty, and divine mercy.
- 80:7/q15/p1 It develops the warning to those who fear, linking this sūra with al-Nāziʿāt and placing Ibn Umm Maktūm among receptive hearers. — Al-Biqāʿī relates the call to warn those who fear (79:45) to resurrection, human creation, and the food cycle as proofs of divine power; rejection despite them is astonishing. Ibn al-Zubayr connects the opening to 79:26 and 79:45 and God's care for humble people. Al-Biqāʿī applies the warner description specifically to Ibn Umm Maktūm. Islāḥī says ʿAbasa and al-Nāziʿāt share theme, discourse style, and reasoning, but differ in the order of topics. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 80:7/q15/p2 Other verses enjoin keeping company with sincere seekers and support the rebuke on behalf of Ibn Umm Maktūm. — Al-Biqāʿī invokes 18:28, 39:65, 38:88, and 31:18 in urging patience with seekers and forbidding contempt. He says the command in 18:28 belongs to the same pattern of revelation as Ibn Umm Maktūm's case. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 80:7/q15/p3 The messenger-duty teaching agrees with the statement that he is charged only with conveyance. — Al-Bayḍāwī explicitly cites 42:48. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 80:7/q15/p4 A preceding citation of 6:52 expresses divine kindness and a prayer not to be severed from mercy. — Al-Biqāʿī supplies this connection as the lead-in to his discussion of the verse. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
