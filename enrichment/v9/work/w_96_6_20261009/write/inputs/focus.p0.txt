# 96:6/q01 What does kallā do here? [كَلَّآ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether a rebuke has an implicit or subsequent target, or the particle affirms or opens a new statement.
- 96:6/q01/p1 It rebukes or restrains transgression and ingratitude toward God's favors.
  reasons: Al-Farrāʾ calls it repudiation; al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī supply the implicit object from God's preceding favors. Ibn ʿĀshūr instead points forward to the man prohibiting prayer and cites an opening negative oath of Imruʾ al-Qays. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ points to the following warning; Samarrāʾī notes kallā's concentration in the Qurʾān's second part and its fit with Meccan judgment themes.

# 96:6/q02 Who is al-insān here? [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular denotes the immediate historical offender or humanity as a class despite that occasion.
- 96:6/q02/p1 Humanity generally, with Abu Jahl a prominent instance.
  reasons: Al-Rāzī prefers the surface context of creation, blessings and return to judgment (96:2, 96:8); al-Ālūsī allows a later Abu Jahl occasion without narrowing the noun; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the generic covers most people of that time except those restrained by religion or character. Kur'an Yolu extends it to every age.

# 96:6/q03 What does yaṭghā mean here? [لَيَطْغَىٰٓ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the expression describes moral boundary crossing, social ascent, or a possible Semitic parallel.
- 96:6/q03/p1 To exceed proper bounds in disobedience, arrogance, rebellion or tyranny.
  reasons: Ibn Shajara and Corpus Coranicum derive the moral sense from water overflowing at 69:11; Corpus Coranicum also cites 69:5, 53:17 and early Meccan human uses including 91:11 and 79:17. Al-Ālūsī includes following desire; Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses pride when secure enough to overstep others; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ invokes despots. The shades 'disobey', 'arrogance', and 'insolence' differ in wording but share this boundary crossing.
