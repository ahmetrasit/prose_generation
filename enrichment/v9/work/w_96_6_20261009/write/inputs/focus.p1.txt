# 96:6/q05 Why are inna and the lām emphatic here? [إِنَّ لَيَطْغَىٰٓ · rhetoric]

- 96:6/q05/p1 They reinforce a surprising claim that might otherwise seem doubtful.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says human transgression after receiving favor is unexpected to the listener.

# 96:6/q06 How can the following 'he sees himself self-sufficient' construction work? [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَيَطْغَىٰٓ · grammar]
- 96:6/q06/p1 The seeing is cognition, so its subject and object can refer to the same person and 'self-sufficient' supplies the second object.
  reasons: Al-Farrāʾ contrasts complete verbs needing nafs in reflexive expression with cognition verbs such as aẓunnunī, and says raʾā nafsahu is not used here; poetry permits some complete-verb direct objects, but ʿadimtunī is not standard. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Ṭabrisī explain both pronouns and the second object.
  holders (6 notes): FARRA; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI

# 96:6/q07 What brings humans to the transgression described here? [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَيَطْغَىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the decisive sense of self-sufficiency is material prosperity, imagined independence from God through knowledge, or both.
- 96:6/q07/p1 Wealth and the feeling of self-sufficiency breed pride, desire and ingratitude.
  reasons: Tustarī says seeing oneself wealthy breeds felt independence, then tyranny; al-Ḥasan says expanded worldly life always brings transgression and one should fear divine guile in abundance, while deprivation may be God's regard. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says wealth harms one whose proper state is poverty. Al-Qushayrī says witnessing need prevents arrogance. Al-Rāzī adds status, prosperity and power as causes of blindness to divine signs. Ibn Kathīr says God will ask how wealth was acquired and spent. The Prophet's prayer seeks refuge from both forgetful poverty and transgressive wealth.

# 96:6/q08 What lesson does the warning draw from God's favors? [* · interpretation]
turns on: Which aspect of the warning is drawn out: gratitude for gifts or the contrasting outcomes of knowledge and worldly pursuit.
- 96:6/q08/p1 Creation, teaching and provision call for gratitude and humility; transgression is an ungrateful misuse of them.
  reasons: Al-Ṭabarī names sound creation, knowledge and unparalleled favor. Al-Rāzī says origin from a clot should have checked arrogance. Sayyid Quṭb says humanity should thank God for creating, teaching and honoring it, and God also gave wealth. Kur'an Yolu adds that human control over resources and fate is illusory. Al-Naḥḥās says reflection on creation reveals divine oneness.
  holders (7 notes): TAB; NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN

- 96:6/q08/p2 The worldly seeker remains insatiable and deepens transgression, while the seeker of knowledge gains God's approval.
  reasons: Ibn Masʿūd or ʿAbd Allāh connects the warning to learned fear in 35:28; Ibn Kathīr also transmits a version raised to the Prophet stating that both seekers are insatiable.
