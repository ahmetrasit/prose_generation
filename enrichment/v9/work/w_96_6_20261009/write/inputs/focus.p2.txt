# 96:6/q09 How does this warning connect with the surrounding verses? [* · links]
turns on: Whether the verse looks back to the favors, forward to Abu Jahl's act, or continues the command to read.
- 96:6/q09/p1 It turns from God's gifts in the opening to humanity's ungrateful response.
  reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī and later commentators derive the omitted ingratitude from the preceding creation and teaching; al-Ālūsī reports an implied call to thank God after verse 5. Biqāʿī describes a turn from divine matters to the human soul; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ sees pride in knowledge and status.

# 96:6/q10 Which portion of the sūra is tied to Abu Jahl? [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the occasion applies from verse 6, from the prohibition scene at verse 9, or to the whole sūra.
- 96:6/q10/p1 The passage from verse 6 through the end was revealed concerning Abu Jahl.
  reasons: Al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Nasafī and others give this scope; al-Qurṭubī sometimes reports the whole sūra as an alternative. The Abu Hurayra transmission leaves uncertain whether the revelation attribution belonged to his report.

# 96:6/q11 When were these verses revealed relative to the opening five? [* · sciences]
turns on: How much time elapsed after the opening revelation, especially in light of the prayer scene.
- 96:6/q11/p1 The opening five came first; the later passage followed after a pause and was joined to them.
  reasons: Al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī compare the later placement of 2:281 in al-Baqara. Ibn ʿĀshūr favors a short pause, says adjacent recited verses normally follow revelation order absent clear proof, and points to reports of Gabriel's non-Qurʾānic revelation during the pause and its memory in al-Ḍuḥā; he rejects al-Suhaylī's two-year estimate as unlikely. The prayer reference need not require a post Night Journey date.

# 96:6/q12 What is reported about Abu Jahl's challenge to the Prophet at prayer? [* · reports]
turns on: The reports vary in the threat, intervention, boasting and outcome.
- 96:6/q12/p1 He threatened to trample the Prophet while praying, then recoiled before fire, terror and wings; angels would have seized him.
  reasons: Abū Hurayra's report is transmitted by Muslim and others; Ibn Kathīr names Aḥmad, Muslim, al-Nasāʾī and Ibn Abī Ḥātim through Muʿtamir and notes doubt whether the revelation attribution was in the report. Versions say the angels would seize him, sometimes limb by limb; al-Durr identifies his council and the guards of 96:17–18.
