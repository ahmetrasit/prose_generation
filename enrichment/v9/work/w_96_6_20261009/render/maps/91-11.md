# 91:11: 18 questions

## 91:11/q01 What does Thamūd's denial through bi-ṭaghwāhā mean?  [كَذَّبَتْ ثَمُودُ بِطَغْوَىٰهَآ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ṭaghwāhā denotes the people's culpable conduct, the calamity they denied, or the collective scope of the act; the punishment analysis also differs over an omitted genitive.
- 91:11/q01/p1 Their rebellion and disobedience drove them to deny the truth or their messenger. — Mujāhid, Qatāda, and Ibn Zayd gloss it as disobedience or transgression; al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, al-Rāzī, and others make that rebellion the cause of denial. Ibn Kathīr says it fixed rejection of guidance and certainty in their hearts. Al-Biqāʿī specifies disbelief, sins, and oppression; Elmalılı says their strength as rock-cutters fed arrogance. Islahi says they rebelled after truth was clear. Corpus Coranicum contrasts 95:7 and 82:9, where the preposition supplies the object, and distinguishes the punishment at 69:5 from their disobedience here. Al-Qurṭubī says they exceeded bounds in disobedience; Qutb says tyranny hid guidance and defiled them. Al-Ṭabarī reports this view alongside his punishment interpretation. [57 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB(ابن زيد,قتادة,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI(مجاهد وقتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(جمهور المتأولين); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور المتأولين); TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(مجاهد وقتادة وغيرهما); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد، قتادة); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); ALUSI(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNKATHIR+]
- 91:11/q01/p2 They denied the promised, overwhelming punishment that struck them. — Ibn ʿAbbās names the punishment Ṭaghwā; al-Ṭabarī links it to al-ṭāghiya in 69:5. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī say an exceptional blast exceeds ordinary bounds and can thus be called transgressive, with al-Rāzī also citing 69:4. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says they denied it until it arrived. Al-Naḥḥās explains an omitted genitive, 'punishment of their ṭaghwā'; al-Ālūsī instead allows direct attachment without ellipsis and takes the verbal noun as intensifying the torment. Sources variously call it promised punishment, extraordinary cry, overpowering calamity, or immediate destruction. [36 notes: TAB(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 91:11/q01/p3 The expression embraces all Thamūd's misdeeds or transgression collectively. — Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb's gloss varies among all reported meanings, all misdeeds together, all Thamūd collectively, and their collective transgression. Ibn Kathīr quotes 'all of them together.' These reports preserve the scope of 'all' without specifying a single particular act. [7 notes: TAB(محمد بن كعب); TAB-FULL(محمد بن كعب); MAWARDI(محمد بن كعب); MAWARDI-FULL(محمد بن كعب); QURTUBI(محمد بن كعب); QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن كعب); IBNKATHIR(محمد بن كعب)]

## 91:11/q02 What is the object of kadhdhabat?  [كَذَّبَتْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the suppressed object is Ṣāliḥ's message or the threatened punishment.
- 91:11/q02/p1 The object is unstated and understood as their messenger. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the following clause supplies the messenger; his full account connects the referent to Ṣāliḥ. Al-Wāḥidī's concise works say they denied the messengers; Ibn Qutayba, Ibn Kathīr, and the Study Quran identify the messenger as Ṣāliḥ or the prophet sent to them. [7 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNKATHIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 91:11/q02/p2 They denied the truth, including the sign and warning brought by Ṣāliḥ. — Al-Baghawī describes denial of truth. Elmalılı says they rejected both the miraculous camel and the truthful warning; denying a true report is sin and denying a prophet's report is disbelief in the God who sent him. [3 notes: BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 91:11/q02/p3 They denied the punishment threatened against them. — Ibn ʿAbbās calls ṭaghwā the name of that punishment; al-Ṭabarī relates it to 69:5. Al-Rāzī and al-Wāḥidī explain that an extraordinary blast exceeded ordinary bounds. [25 notes: TAB(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI; ABUHAYYAN(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI]

## 91:11/q03 Why is the verb feminine with Thamūd?  [كَذَّبَتْ ثَمُودُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the feminine form is taken as expressive rhetoric or agreement with a tribe name.
- 91:11/q03/p1 The feminine form portrays the weak effect of Thamūd's denial. — Al-Biqāʿī says listeners can plainly recognize its injustice from the clear sign and ugly outcome. [3 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 91:11/q03/p2 Thamūd is treated grammatically as the name of a tribe. — Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts the feminine reference here with later plural pronouns referring to the people collectively. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 91:11/q04 How is the bāʾ in bi-ṭaghwāhā construed?  [بِطَغْوَىٰهَآ · grammar]
turns on: Whether bāʾ expresses cause, means, or the object of denial; the last analysis also turns on whether a genitive is omitted.
- 91:11/q04/p1 It marks the cause of denial: their rebelliousness. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān attribute this to most interpreters. Ibn ʿĀshūr equates the cause with extreme arrogance and compares Quraysh's pride, citing 2:15 and 43:31. Corpus Coranicum contrasts object-marking uses at 95:7 and 82:9. Al-Ālūsī judges the difference from the instrumental reading slight. [11 notes: KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA(جمهور المتأولين); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور المتأولين); ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 91:11/q04/p2 It is instrumental or metaphorically instrumental: they denied by means of rebellion. — Al-Zamakhsharī compares 'I wrote with a pen'; al-Samīn reports this as his first analysis. Al-Ālūsī says it differs little from causation. [5 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)+]
- 91:11/q04/p3 It attaches to 'denied' as the punishment's complement. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī relate this to the overwhelming torment at 69:5. Al-Naḥḥās supplies 'punishment of their ṭaghwā' by ellipsis and compares asking a town for its people; al-Ālūsī says direct attachment requires no omitted genitive. [5 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 91:11/q05 What does ṭaghwā mean as an Arabic word?  [بِطَغْوَىٰهَآ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the root's excess describes human disobedience or the exceptional punishment.
- 91:11/q05/p1 It means transgression or tyranny beyond bounds, including disobedience. — Al-Layth gives ṭughyā and ṭughwān as dialectal verbal nouns beside verbs ṭaghaytu and ṭaghawtu. Al-Zajjāj derives it from ṭughyān. Jishumī includes corruption and disobedience; al-Ālūsī calls it exceeding the limit in disobedience. Islahi specifies rebellion after truth is clear. - 91:11/q05/p2 Here it is a name or description of the overwhelming punishment. — Ibn ʿAbbās names the punishment Ṭaghwā. Al-Rāzī says a cry exceeding normal bounds fits the root; al-Ṭabarī and others compare al-ṭāghiya at 69:5. [19 notes: TAB(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 91:11/q06 How is the noun ṭaghwā formed?  [بِطَغْوَىٰهَآ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the wāw is a change from yāʾ, an original dialectal root letter, or left unanalyzed.
- 91:11/q06/p1 It is a faʿlā verbal noun with yāʾ changed to wāw to distinguish noun from adjective. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq compare taqwā, khazyā, and daʿwā; al-Zamakhsharī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī likewise explain a yāʾ-root noun shifted to wāw. Al-Qurṭubī gives underlying ṭaghyāhā. Jishumī proposes derivation from ṭāghiya. [14 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 91:11/q06/p2 Its wāw may be original in a dialect using ṭaghawtu. — Al-Layth records both ṭaghaytu and ṭaghawtu. Abū al-Baqāʾ makes the wāw original for the latter speakers; al-Ālūsī cites al-Rāghib for both roots and says this removes the supposed irregularity of the ḍamma form. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الليث); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI(الحسن ومحمد بن كعب وحماد بن سلمة); ALUSI-FULL]
- 91:11/q06/p3 It is simply a verbal noun equivalent to ṭughyān. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Ṭabarī treat both as verbal nouns; Ibn ʿAṭiyya does so without a root derivation. Al-Naḥḥās calls them synonyms. [11 notes: FARRA; TAB; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); IBNATIYYA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 91:11/q07 Why use ṭaghwā rather than ṭughyān or a yāʾ form?  [بِطَغْوَىٰهَآ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether verse-ending correspondence or the expressive force of wāw explains the chosen form.
- 91:11/q07/p1 The form fits the sūra's verse endings. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī, and al-Qurṭubī connect the form to rhyme. They compare daʿwā from supplication and 10:10; al-Farrāʾ also cites final prayer there. Müller likewise calls it rhyme driven, while Corpus Coranicum notes other Quranic ṭughyān occurrences postdate this sūra. [11 notes: FARRA; TAB; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM(Müller)]
- 91:11/q07/p2 The stronger wāw form underscores the extremity of denial. — Al-Biqāʿī prefers the wāw for force and says it marks the extreme degree of their denial; he also distinguishes the fatḥa noun and ḍamma verbal noun. [3 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 91:11/q10 Who were Thamūd and what did their denial involve?  [ثَمُودُ · reports]
turns on: The accounts differ over the divine warner, explicit camel command, named culprit, and aftermath.
- 91:11/q10/p1 An ancient Arabian people rejected Ṣāliḥ and his she-camel sign and warning. — Elmalılı says their story and destruction were famous, Ṣāliḥ showed the camel after a demanded sign, and warned them not to harm it, citing 7:73; the camel had an alternating drinking day and grazed freely, with painful punishment threatened for harm, citing 26:155. Study Quran calls them a northern Arabian tribe and points to 7:73–79, 11:61–68, and 54:23–31. Ibn ʿĀshūr says their peak arrogance was selecting their wicked man to hamstring the camel. [6 notes: ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 91:11/q10/p2 In a likely pre-Islamic poem, a camel's foal's curse follows cultic disrespect and a culprit called Aḥmar. — Corpus Coranicum says Umayya ibn Abī al-Ṣalt's likely authentic poem has neither divine warner nor explicit command; Aḥmar is elsewhere linked to ʿĀd, perhaps from overlapping traditions. It also records a surviving lame servant who reports the event then dies. In its comparison, the Quran adds the messenger and command and omits the servant's aftermath to focus the lesson. [3 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 91:11/q11 Why single out Thamūd here?  [ثَمُودُ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes emphasize complementary reasons: clarity of the sign, familiarity of the history, and the warning supplied by their fate.
- 91:11/q11/p1 Their clear sign and well-known remains made them a compelling example for Arab listeners. — Al-Biqāʿī adds that their sign particularly indicated the Hour and that Arabs knew their ruins and stories. Islahi and his teacher Farāhī say Quraysh knew the history, settlements, and traditions, so a brief allusion evoked the whole fate; Islahi cites an Arabic couplet naming Qidār and says their leaders' deviance was proverbial. Study Quran infers familiarity from the account's presence in early and later sūras. [8 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 91:11/q11/p2 Their ruin shows the failure of a community choosing rebellion despite the capacity for good. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, and Kuran Yolu connect the example to the soul's preceding purification/corruption contrast. Qutb says tyranny obscured guidance and led to anger and destruction; Asad says it illustrates human wickedness. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 91:11/q11/p3 Historical examples reach ordinary hearers and warn Quraysh of the same fate. — Islahi says cosmic and inward signs chiefly aid reflective intellectuals, whereas historical incidents can move ordinary people. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Quraysh knowingly defied the Messenger like Thamūd and could face similar punishment. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 91:11/q12 How does Thamūd's story connect to the preceding verses and the sūra's warning?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the story chiefly illustrates the preceding soul contrast, warns Quraysh of parallel punishment, or explains destruction through rejection; these functions can coexist.
- 91:11/q12/p1 It exemplifies the failure of a soul corrupted by rebellion. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān connect it to 91:10; al-Ālūsī calls the account a fresh sentence reinforcing that failure. Al-Biqāʿī says the warning from al-Balad and the preceding corruption aims to urge purification. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the story through the end demonstrates the contrast between purification and corruption. Study Quran calls it an example of obscuring the soul; Kuran Yolu emphasizes human choice. Ibn ʿĀshūr says, if 91:9–10 answer the oath, this story is evidence for failure. Corpus Coranicum says this old punishment story is recast around the choice of good and evil. [15 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; QUTB-ZILAL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 91:11/q12/p2 It threatens Quraysh with Thamūd's fate if they persist in arrogant rejection. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares Quraysh's pride toward a supposedly inferior messenger, citing 43:31, and says their punishment came by the swords of people they had opposed and expelled. Islahi connects al-Balad's warning about Mecca's prosperity from Abraham's prayer and the House to al-Shams' threat; he says persistent arrogant disorder ends in destruction when a nation's existence threatens the world. Corpus Coranicum suggests the preacher's rejection may be mirrored in the story. [10 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 91:11/q12/p3 The story makes rejection of the messenger the immediate cause of destruction. — Corpus Coranicum says 'denied' in 91:11 and its repetition in 91:14 foreground ethical judgment rather than narrative detail, and suggests a Syriac cognate kaddeb. Elmalılı says denial of a prophet's true report entails disbelief in the sender. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; ELMALILI]

## 91:11/q13 What is the answer to the sūra's opening oath?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether 91:9–10 answer the oath or are parenthetical, and whether the response is the narrated fate or an omitted threat.
- 91:11/q13/p1 The purification and failure statements answer the oath; Thamūd's story supplies evidence. — Ibn ʿĀshūr frames this as one possibility. Al-Ālūsī defends omitting the oath-response lām as common after a long oath, easier than omitting a whole sentence; he cites al-Zamakhsharī's acceptance of an omitted answer at 23:1. He rejects the objection that purification is merely practical, explaining that growth is itself an end. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 91:11/q13/p2 The purification statements are parenthetical; an omitted oath answer threatens Mecca with Thamūd's devastation. — Al-Zamakhsharī says God will devastate Mecca for rejecting Muḥammad as He devastated Thamūd for rejecting Ṣāliḥ. Al-Ālūsī reports the analysis but argues an omitted whole answer is less economical than omitting lām and says purification is central, not merely practical. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 91:11/q13/p3 If the purification statements are parenthetical, Thamūd's denial and destruction answer the oath. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says verse 11 with verse 14 supplies the response; the lām is omitted to shorten a long oath, as in al-Burūj 85:1–4. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 91:11/q14 How does the next verse follow this summary of Thamūd's denial?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether fāʾ indicates temporal succession or moves from a general summary to its particulars.
- 91:11/q14/p1 The fāʾ sequences denial and Ṣāliḥ's challenge and warning. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says Ṣāliḥ displayed the she-camel as a sign and warned them not to harm her or deprive her of her drinking turn, citing 11:64. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 91:11/q14/p2 The fāʾ introduces details after a general statement. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the ensuing report elaborates the arrogant denial when the wicked man arose; no fronting of the temporal clause is needed. He compares 2:36 and 7:4. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 91:11/q15 What broader rule about nations does Thamūd's example teach?  [* · interpretation]
- 91:11/q15/p1 A rebellious nation receives warning and time, then collective worldly punishment if it persists. — Islahi says historical evidence shows clear warning before destruction; the nation is punished together in this world, while individuals receive separate judgment in the Hereafter. He says God tolerates disorder only while expedient and then removes a community that threatens the world. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 91:11/q16 How does this account relate to other early Thamūd traditions?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the comparison concerns other Quranic allusions or a proposed pre-Islamic poetic account.
- 91:11/q16/p1 It is an early compact Quranic punishment story later expanded and cross-referenced. — Corpus Coranicum groups Q 91, 89, and 85 with early Thamūd, ʿĀd, and Pharaoh allusions, alongside Q 105, and calls them the nucleus of later punishment legends. It calls Q 54:23–31 the fuller later early Meccan Thamūd account and notes shorter allusions in Q 89, 69, and 51. Asad and Kuran Yolu direct readers to al-Aʿrāf and Hūd; Qutb directs them especially to al-Fajr. Study Quran cites early and later retellings as evidence of familiar history. [7 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN]
- 91:11/q16/p2 It reworks a likely pre-Islamic Thamūd story into an ethical warning. — Corpus Coranicum compares a likely authentic Umayya poem without a divine warner or explicit command: the camel's foal curses the offenders, and a lame servant later reports their end. It says the Quran adds a messenger and explicit command, omits the servant's aftermath, and makes the choice between good and evil central. [4 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 91:11/q17 What merit is reported for reciting Sūrat al-Shams?  [* · sciences]
- 91:11/q17/p1 Its recitation is likened to giving charity for everything lit by the sun and moon. — The report is attributed to the Prophet by al-Kashshāf and al-Bayḍāwī; these notes offer no grading. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 91:11/q18 What meanings are reported for expressions in the following verses?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: These notes explain several distinct expressions in the surrounding verses.
- 91:11/q18/p1 Damdama means that God became angry with them. — Al-Māwardī reports this possibility. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 91:11/q18/p2 Damdama means that God encompassed or enclosed them. — Al-Māwardī reports this possibility. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 91:11/q18/p3 Damdama means that God destroyed them. — Al-Māwardī adds that the word came from Abyssinian into Arabic. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 91:11/q18/p4 Fa-sawwāhā means making them equal in destruction. — Al-Māwardī attributes it to al-Suddī and Yaḥyā ibn Sallām. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(السدي ويحيى بن سلام)]
- 91:11/q18/p5 Fa-sawwāhā means leveling them with the earth. — Al-Māwardī attributes it to Ibn Shajara. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة)]
- 91:11/q18/p6 Fa-sawwāhā means leveling later nations after them. — Al-Māwardī records this further possibility. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 91:11/q18/p7 The one unafraid of the consequence is God. — Al-Māwardī attributes this to Ibn ʿAbbās; Mujāhid likewise says God does not fear their aftermath. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 91:11/q18/p8 The one unafraid is the camel's hamstringer. — Al-Māwardī attributes this to al-Ḥasan. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 91:11/q18/p9 The one unafraid is Ṣāliḥ. — Al-Māwardī says he warned them and God rescued him at their destruction. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 91:11/q18/p10 Inbaʿatha means being roused or sent forth. — Al-Ṭabrisī contrasts baʿatha as the cause of that response. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 91:11/q18/p11 Thamūd's most wretched man was Uḥaymir Thamūd. — Al-Durr reports Qatāda's identification. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 91:11/q18/p12 Purification improves the soul and concealment leads it astray. — Al-Durr reports Mujāhid's gloss of the preceding verses. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
