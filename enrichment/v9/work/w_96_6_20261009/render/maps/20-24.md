# 20:24: 14 questions

## 20:24/q01 What is Moses commanded to do by going to Pharaoh?  [ٱذْهَبْ فِرْعَوْنَ · interpretation]
- 20:24/q01/p1 Approach Pharaoh with God's signs, call him to worship God alone, warn him, and seek his reform. — Muqatil, al-Tha'labi, al-Baghawi, al-Baydawi, al-Razi, and al-Alusi explicitly make the mission a summons to worship; al-Wahidi's People of Meanings supply the call to affirm divine oneness as understood wording. Al-Razi's Wahb report and al-Alusi add warning and gentle speech; Ibn Kathir adds fair treatment of the Israelites. Al-Qurtubi grounds the commission in the staff and hand having shown Moses to be a messenger; al-Baydawi, Bursevi, and al-Alusi say he goes with the two signs, Bursevi identifying staff and hand from 28:32. Bursevi includes Pharaoh's chiefs and preaching; al-Biqa'i describes turning Pharaoh back from rebellion. Al-Tabrisi and Islahi also describe a call or warning; al-Qushayri stresses the summons despite known refusal. Kuranyolu notes the later instruction to address him gently. [22 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(وهب); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(وهب بن منبه); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(وهب بن منبه); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 20:24/q02 What action is understood after the command to go?  [ٱذْهَبْ إِلَىٰ فِرْعَوْنَ · grammar]
- 20:24/q02/p1 An omitted command directs Moses to call Pharaoh to affirm God's oneness. — Al-Wahidi reports this ellipsis from the People of Meanings. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني)]

## 20:24/q03 How did Moses respond to the command to go?  [ٱذْهَبْ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ over Moses' hesitation, its duration, and whether the prompting angel is identified; al-Alusi questions the seven-day account.
- 20:24/q03/p1 He accepted the difficult commission and sought the breadth of heart needed to face Pharaoh. — Al-Qushayri says Moses accepted an ordeal against his own wish after being honored by direct divine speech, despite knowing Pharaoh would not respond. Al-Kashshaf says the tyrant's confrontation required steadfastness and a broad, patient heart, explaining Moses' request that God expand his chest and ease the task. Al-Nasafi likewise introduces the need for an expanded heart. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 20:24/q03/p2 He worried that Pharaoh's bound heart made the mission impossible, and a wind-keeper angel urged obedience. — Muqatil identifies the visiting angel as keeper of the wind storehouses. Al-Tha'labi gives Moses' question about approaching Pharaoh with a bound heart and has the angel who keeps the winds urge compliance. [2 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI]
- 20:24/q03/p3 He remained silent for seven days, then an angel told him to answer God's command. — Al-Razi attributes the seven days and angel to Wahb. Al-Alusi reports seven days or longer through an unspecified account and doubts that account; neither note identifies the angel as keeper of the winds. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(وهب); ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:24/q04 Why send Moses when Pharaoh would not believe?  [ٱذْهَبْ إِلَىٰ فِرْعَوْنَ · theology]
- 20:24/q04/p1 Moses must carry out God's summons despite Pharaoh's divinely known or bound refusal. — Muqatil says God had bound Pharaoh's heart, yet told Moses to call him to worship. Al-Tha'labi reports Moses asking how to approach him under that condition and an angel urging obedience. Al-Qushayri says God knew Pharaoh would neither believe nor respond, but Moses accepted the ordeal. Wahb's account in al-Alusi says God could destroy Pharaoh but delays for the proof and excuse established for creation. [4 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; ALUSI-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]

## 20:24/q05 Who is the Pharaoh addressed here?  [فِرْعَوْنَ · referent]
turns on: Only an unnamed group supplies the personal name; Ibn Atiyya says the identifications lack support.
- 20:24/q05/p1 Egypt's king from whom Moses had fled. — Ibn Kathir identifies him this way. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 20:24/q05/p2 Mus'ab ibn al-Rayyan. — Ibn Atiyya transmits this identification from an unnamed group, while judging it and other proposed identifications unsupported. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]

## 20:24/q06 Why is Pharaoh singled out as the addressee?  [فِرْعَوْنَ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes explain the named addressee through Pharaoh's own offense, his leadership of followers, and the wider effect of addressing leaders first.
- 20:24/q06/p1 His transgression makes him the immediate object of the summons. — Al-Razi and Abu Hayyan read the command as following from Pharaoh's transgression; al-Alusi calls that transgression the explanation of the command or its obligation. Islahi says Moses' first campaign is to warn this tyrant, and al-Biqa'i says the mission seeks to turn him back from rebellion. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:24/q06/p2 Pharaoh is the leading claimant to divinity whose followers will be reached through him. — Al-Razi says Moses was sent to all, but Pharaoh is named for claiming divinity, arrogance, and leadership. Abu Hayyan calls him the head of disbelief and says his people follow him. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 20:24/q06/p3 Messengers first address rulers because their reform can reform society and their denial exposes weak reasoning. — Islahi says rulers administer society, so reform can spread quickly. Their rejection exposes hollow reasons, while informed later acceptance can persuade others; public campaigns based on emotional propaganda depend on feeling and lack a role for reason. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:24/q07 What does approaching this tyrant teach about preaching?  [ٱذْهَبْ إِلَىٰ فِرْعَوْنَ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw distinct lessons about gentle tone and about the audience and reasoning of the summons.
- 20:24/q07/p1 Even a great transgressor should be addressed gently; method and tone matter to preaching. — Kuranyolu draws this lesson from the later gentle-speech command despite Pharaoh's offenses. Wahb's charge, reported by al-Razi, Ibn Kathir, and al-Alusi, includes a gentle summons, warning, and trust in God's protection; al-Alusi cites 20:44 and says God hastens to pardon more than punish. [4 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL(وهب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(وهب بن منبه); ALUSI-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]
- 20:24/q07/p2 Address leaders with reasons so their reform or exposed denial can guide the wider public. — Islahi says leaders administer society, their reform spreads, and their hollow arguments become clear when they deny. He contrasts informed acceptance with emotional public propaganda that lacks a role for reason. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:24/q08 What does Pharaoh signify in an inward reading?  [فِرْعَوْنَ · inward]
- 20:24/q08/p1 Pharaoh signifies the soul's self-assertion, which veils itself and exceeds servitude's limit. — Al-Qashani gives this identification and ties Pharaoh's transgression to the soul's self-assertion. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:24/q09 What does Pharaoh's transgression mean?  [طَغَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: The sources describe the offense at different levels: exceeding bounds generally, unbelief, a divinity claim, oppression, and attachment to worldly life; Yahya equates disobedience and disbelief.
- 20:24/q09/p1 He exceeded the limit in disobedience, rebellion, corruption, pride, or tyranny. — Muqatil and al-Suddi as reported by Yahya gloss it as disobedience; Yahya says that amounts to disbelief. Al-Wahidi stresses extreme transgression beyond others. Al-Damghani says tughyān can mean disobedience, comparing 79:17 and 20:81; al-Jishumi compares baghy and the paired forms tughat and bughat. Ibn Atiyya specifies corruption; al-Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Baghawi, and al-Qurtubi give exceeding bounds in disobedience, the latter also listing tyranny. Al-Tha'labi adds self-exaltation; al-Baydawi says rebellion and arrogance; al-Alusi gives pride and tyranny. Al-Biqa'i speaks of rebellion beyond servitude's bounds. [14 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; THALABI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:24/q09/p2 He disbelieved, arrogantly rejecting God's blessings and worship. — Yahya glosses the verb as disbelief and says al-Suddi's disobedience gloss amounts to the same thing. Al-Nahhas makes it exceeding bounds in disbelief. Al-Tha'labi combines disobedience, arrogance, and unbelief; al-Wahidi al-Wajiz specifies ingratitude for blessings and arrogance toward worship. Al-Tabrisi calls him arrogant and tyrannical in disbelief; al-Qurtubi includes unbelief, and al-Baydawi adds arrogance. Al-Alusi calls him proud and tyrannical. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:24/q09/p3 He exceeded servanthood by claiming divinity for himself. — Al-Razi and Abu Hayyan cite his divinity claim as why he is singled out. Al-Nasafi and al-Biqa'i call it crossing servitude's bounds. Bursevi says Pharaoh claimed divinity independently, not as God's partner, citing 'I am your highest lord' at 79:24. Al-Alusi also mentions the claim. Asad calls it his greatest sin and cites 28:38 and 79:24. Islahi says Egyptians treated the claimant as an avatar of their sun deity. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:24/q09/p4 He oppressed the Israelites, killing or drowning boys and preserving girls as slave-maidens. — Islahi specifies these acts and says Moses knew the rebellion through personal suffering. Ibn Kathir's description of Moses' charge to treat the Israelites well identifies their welfare as part of the mission. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 20:24/q09/p5 He preferred worldly life and forgot God. — Ibn Kathir states these as aspects of Pharaoh's transgression. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 20:24/q10 Why is Pharaoh's transgression stated in this form?  [إِنَّهُۥ طَغَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: The explanations concern the clause's causal force, its emphasis, or the brevity of its description.
- 20:24/q10/p1 The clause gives the reason for the command or for its obligation. — Al-Alusi says Pharaoh's pride, tyranny, and claim to divinity explain why Moses is commanded to go. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:24/q10/p2 Its emphasis reflects how extraordinary rebellion against the Supreme King is. — Al-Biqa'i explains the emphatic statement by the gravity of anyone's rebellion against God. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:24/q10/p3 The offense is left undescribed because Moses already knew and had suffered it. — Islahi says Moses' personal experience of Pharaoh's rebellion made further explanation unnecessary. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:24/q11 Why is the staff fitting for this mission?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Bursevi gives one rationale from Moses' past and another from the correction he thinks Pharaoh needs.
- 20:24/q11/p1 It is a shepherd's tool appropriate to Moses, who had been a shepherd. — Bursevi explains the staff's fit from Moses' former occupation. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 20:24/q11/p2 It suits warning and correcting Pharaoh, whom Bursevi likens to a donkey needing a staff. — Bursevi gives this crude comparison and cites Rumi's verses elaborating the image of correction and warning. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 20:24/q12 What does Moses' commission teach about the spiritual stage of a messenger or guide?  [* · inward]
turns on: Al-Qashani stresses a mission before essential disclosure, while Bursevi speaks of appointment and return at a wayfarer's completion.
- 20:24/q12/p1 Messengership can begin at disclosure of the attributes before essential disclosure or annihilation of the Essence. — Al-Qashani places Moses' mission before his forty-day retreat and the essential disclosure after Pharaoh's destruction. He supports the point by saying most of the Prophet's spiritual journey followed prophethood, revelation, and scriptural guidance. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 20:24/q12/p2 At completion, the sincere wayfarer is appointed to guide others and returns among people to endure their harm. — Bursevi says the mature guide trains other servants and that mixing with them tests forbearance and forgiveness. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 20:24/q13 What does Wahb's report add to the commission of Moses?  [* · reports]
- 20:24/q13/p1 God reassured Moses of protection, then ordered a gentle summons to worship and warning while showing forbearance to Pharaoh. — Al-Razi reports Wahb's charge to deliver the message, summon Pharaoh to worship, warn him, speak gently, and trust God's protection and knowledge; he gives no grading. Ibn Kathir's Wahb report adds divine sight, hearing, support, and strength, prohibition of fear or envy of worldly display, and lasting honor for God's friends. Al-Alusi's Wahb report calls Pharaoh an arrogant denier of God's lordship; says God could destroy him but waits to establish proof and excuse for creation; portrays him as small before God; and names four hundred years of respite with rain, crops, health, youth, wealth, and power. Al-Alusi also says God hastens to pardon more than punish and connects gentle speech to 20:44. The reported details vary across sources. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(وهب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(وهب بن منبه); ALUSI-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]

## 20:24/q14 How does this command connect to the preceding signs and other Moses passages?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes trace preparation by signs, the command's place in the passage, and repeated wording elsewhere.
- 20:24/q14/p1 The staff and hand signs prepare and equip Moses for the ensuing mission. — Al-Razi, Abu Hayyan, and al-Tabataba'i say the displayed signs precede the order; al-Tabataba'i says they prepare it. Al-Qurtubi says the signs establish Moses as a messenger before he is sent. Al-Baydawi has Moses go with both signs; Islahi says Moses was first taught monotheism and the Hereafter and equipped with miracles. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:24/q14/p2 The command marks the move from preliminary instruction to the passage's central mission. — Al-Alusi says the separated command marks the mission's importance; al-Tabataba'i calls it the initiation of Moses' mission after preparation by the signs. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:24/q14/p3 The command repeats or parallels other accounts of Moses' call. — Study Quran notes identical wording at 79:17, a parallel at 20:43, and links to 26:10-11, 27:12, and 28:32. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
