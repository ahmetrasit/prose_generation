# 42:27: 27 questions

## 42:27/q01 What does basata mean here?  [بَسَطَ · meaning]
- 42:27/q01/p1 To widen, spread, or make provision abundant. — Damghani cites 13:26, 2:245, and 29:62 for expansion of provision; Qurtubi gives the sense 'widen or spread'; Bursevi calls it abundance. [3 notes: DAMGHANI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 42:27/q02 Why may basata be written with sad?  [بَسَطَ · grammar]
- 42:27/q02/p1 Sin can shift to sad before ta because their sounds are close. — Al-Khalil, reported by Nahhas, supplies the phonetic explanation; Qurtubi notes the alternate spelling. [2 notes: NAHHAS(الخليل); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 42:27/q03 What kinds of provision does rizq include?  [ٱلرِّزْقَ · referent]
turns on: Whether provision is confined to livelihood, identified with rain, or extended to nonmaterial gifts.
- 42:27/q03/p1 Material livelihood and wealth, distributed in varying amounts. — Muqatil, Tabari, and others discuss wealth, sufficiency, and the rich and poor; Kuran Yolu also includes intelligence, health, and ability in worldly means. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 42:27/q03/p2 Rain and its effects. — Qurtubi reports the rain reading, with unceasing rain distracting from prayer; Mujahid glosses rizq as rain. Razi and Baydawi report Arab warfare after rain and abundant pasture. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل)]
- 42:27/q03/p3 Material gifts, offspring, religious knowledge, revelation, and duties. — Tabatabai treats guidance, knowledge, law, children, and the capacity to receive revelation as apportioned provision; he invokes gradual revelation at 17:106 and the rain and valleys parable at 13:17. Study Quran's introduction includes wealth, offspring, and revelation. [8 notes: TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 42:27/q04 Who are 'His servants' in the conditional?  [لِعِبَادِهِۦ · referent]
turns on: Whether the universal wording or the context of God's care for believers controls the referent.
- 42:27/q04/p1 Servants generally, including believers and others. — Ibn Ashur says the universal address states a cosmic rule, not a separate worldly system for God's allies; Alusi prefers the general reading since the elect are unlikely to be intoxicated by wealth, though some believers can still be warned. Biqai includes all servants, repentant or otherwise. [7 notes: MUQATIL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي,محيي السنة)-]
- 42:27/q04/p2 God's specially honored friends, or some believing servants. — Al-Tibi takes the closing knowledge of their welfare as pointing to honored friends; Muhyi al-Sunna is also credited with this reading. Alusi allows some believing servants as an alternative but prefers generality. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي,محيي السنة); IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 42:27/q05 What event is reported behind the verse?  [وَلَوْ بَسَطَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلرِّزْقَ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ over named participants and setting; Ibn Ashur also disputes treating a Medinan event as the occasion of a Meccan verse.
- 42:27/q05/p1 Khabbab and his group coveted the wealth of Qurayza, Nadir, and, in many versions, Qaynuqa. — The names vary: Wahidi-Asbab and Ibn al-Jawzi mention only Qurayza and Nadir; most add Qaynuqa. Some tellings identify Khabbab's group with the Suffa. Ibn Ashur disputes this as an occasion: if authentic, he treats it as a later application because he regards the verse as Meccan. [12 notes: THALABI(خباب بن لادن); WAHIDI-ASBAB(خباب بن الأرت); JISHUMI(خباب بن الأرت); BAGHAWI-FULL(خباب بن الأرت); KASHSHAF-FULL(خباب ابن الأرت); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(خباب بن الأرت); RAZI-FULL(خباب بن الأرث); QURTUBI-FULL(خباب بن الأرت); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(خباب بن الأرت); ALUSI-FULL(خباب بن الأرت); IBNASHUR-FULL(خباب بن الأرت); STUDYQURAN(Bg, Q); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 42:27/q05/p2 People of the Suffa or Bench wished for worldly abundance, sometimes asking the Prophet to enrich them. — Amr b. Hurayth, Ali, and anonymous reports transmit this; al-Hakim authenticated Ali's report according to Durr. Some identify the group as homeless emigrants; Ibn Ashur grades the anonymous report weak. Thalabi links their wish to the wealth of the three Jewish tribes. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(عمرو بن حريث); THALABI; WAHIDI-ASBAB(عمرو بن حريث); JISHUMI(قيل); RAZI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قوم من أهل الصفة); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عمرو بن حريث); ALUSI-FULL(عمرو بن حريث); DURR-FULL(علي); ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL(أهل الصفة); STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 42:27/q05/p3 Poor early believers more generally longed for wealth and ease. — Study Quran reports the broader identification; Durr explains the verse by people wishing for worldly life. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); DURR-FULL]
- 42:27/q05/p4 A Prophetic warning about worldly bloom preceded this verse. — Durr relays Qatada's report that, after the Prophet warned against worldly adornment and was asked whether good can bring evil, the verse was revealed. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله)]

## 42:27/q06 What does baghaw mean in this verse?  [لَبَغَوْا۟ · meaning]
turns on: The word is explained as disobedience, interpersonal injustice, pride, insatiable seeking, or the concrete act of raiding.
- 42:27/q06/p1 They would transgress God's limits, disobey, and act corruptly. — Tabari says they would commit what God forbade; Baghawi calls it tyranny and insolence. Shafiq connects unearned leisure with corruption; Biqai describes killing and plunder when desires gain power. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI(شقيق بن إبراهيم); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 42:27/q06/p2 They would wrong, dominate, or oppress one another. — Zamakhshari cites Qarun; Nasafi also cites Pharaoh. Ibn Ashur says wealth joined to corrupt character produces aggression; Razi cites Arab raiding as a narrower version. Poems about rain and rivalry illustrate the mechanism. [15 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري,شاعر من العرب); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(بعض العرب,بعض بني جرم من طيء); TABATABAI]
- 42:27/q06/p3 They would become proud, arrogant, and extravagant. — Qurtubi and Kashshaf report this as an alternative; Baydawi distinguishes excess and oppression. Qarun is an example, and Alusi calls pride an overstepping of the limits proper to servants. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 42:27/q06/p4 They would keep seeking more in quantity, quality, status, houses, mounts, and clothing. — Jishumi and Baydawi derive baghy from seeking or exceeding moderation; Ibn Abbas's illustrations vary between dwellings, rank, mounts, vehicles, and clothes. Qurtubi and Study Quran cite the valley-of-gold report for insatiability. [11 notes: JISHUMI; THALABI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 42:27/q06/p5 They would raid one another after abundant rain and crops. — Razi and Baydawi describe Arabs fighting in fertile times, and Qurtubi reports the reading 'raiding.' [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل)]

## 42:27/q07 Why would expanded provision lead to transgression?  [بَسَطَ لَبَغَوْا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes offer different causal mechanisms: personal desire, idle leisure, lost economic interdependence, and particular pastoral warfare.
- 42:27/q07/p1 Wealth supplies means and stirs pride, desire, rivalry, and oppressive power. — Kashshaf invokes Qarun, the Prophet's warning, and a rain poem; Tabatabai cites 96:6–7; Asad cites greed at 102:1 and imagined self sufficiency at 96:6. Biqai likens universal excess to powerful kings pursuing desires by force. Bursevi's Kashifi adds Pharaoh, Haman, and Qarun, and compares different people to soils responding differently to rain. [25 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,شاعر من العرب); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي,النبي); ALUSI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL(بعض العرب,بعض بني جرم من طيء); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 42:27/q07/p2 Unearned abundance would free people from necessary work and leave leisure for corruption. — Shafiq calls livelihood work a mercy; Bursevi quotes a poem that youth, leisure, and wealth ruin a person, and al-Saib's poem on comfort making a laborer idle. [3 notes: THALABI(شقيق بن إبراهيم); BURSEVI(الصائب,بعضهم)]
- 42:27/q07/p3 Uniform abundance would undo mutual service and the social order. — Razi and Qurtubi say equal wealth would end service and crafts; Kuran Yolu extends the point to equal intelligence, health, ability, and wealth, undermining work and civilization. Ibn Ashur adds escalating conflict among affluent aggressors and their victims. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 42:27/q07/p4 Among Arabs, rain and plentiful pasture enabled raiding. — Razi and Baydawi contrast raids in fertile times with migration for pasture in drought; Qurtubi reports abundant crops as the trigger. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل)]

## 42:27/q08 Does the conditional say every wealthy person transgresses or every poor person refrains?  [وَلَوْ بَسَطَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلرِّزْقَ لَبَغَوْا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: How to reconcile a general causal conditional with wealthy people who remain upright and poor people who transgress.
- 42:27/q08/p1 It states a prevailing tendency under general abundance, allowing individual exceptions. — Kashshaf, Nasafi, Bursevi, and Jar Allah say poverty makes wrongdoing less common, not impossible. Kashifi cites wealthy Uthman without visible transgression and compares wealth to rain on different soils. Ibn Ashur says character, religion, law, courts, and communal zeal restrain some wealthy people; hunger may exceptionally drive a poor person to force. Tabatabai adds other rules: wealth as trial (64:15), gradual reprieve (7:182–183), and changed treatment following ingratitude (13:11; 3:154). [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL(جار الله); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 42:27/q08/p2 Some rich transgressors would have behaved as badly or worse without the added provision. — Tabrisi proposes this answer to the objection that wealthy transgressors exist; God knows each case. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 42:27/q08/p3 Those given wealth who transgress were known to transgress in either condition. — Al-Jubbai's answer, reported by Razi, is rejected by Razi: 96:6–7 links wealth to transgression, and wealth gives an evil inclined person more means to harm. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الجبائي); RAZI-FULL-]

## 42:27/q09 Why is 'in the earth' placed as it is?  [فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ · rhetoric]
- 42:27/q09/p1 The phrase is fronted in the sentence. — Muqatil explicitly notes its placement. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 42:27/q10 What work does 'but' do?  [وَلَٰكِن · rhetoric]
- 42:27/q10/p1 It corrects the thought that God either gives everyone everything desired or withholds all provision. — Qushayri says the contrast introduces measured apportionment between those imagined extremes. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 42:27/q11 Why does God give provision in measured amounts?  [وَلَٰكِن يُنَزِّلُ بِقَدَرٍۢ مَّا يَشَآءُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the emphasis falls on each person's welfare, social order, worldly testing, or divine will and nonobligation.
- 42:27/q11/p1 He allots each person's needs and circumstances according to His knowledge and wisdom. — Tabari and Thalabi mention sufficiency; many say wealth helps some and poverty helps others. Razi and Baydawi stress temperaments and outcomes; Biqai adds individual and collective measures. Ibn Ashur distinguishes explaining measured provision from explaining poverty itself. Tabatabai calls welfare the baseline rule, while Kuran Yolu says people cannot grasp the measure from their perspective. [35 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(القاموس,كشف الأسرار); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 42:27/q11/p2 Different allotments preserve the world's order and people's cooperation. — Razi and Qurtubi say equal wealth ends service and crafts; Nasafi and Bursevi warn universal poverty would ruin people too. Alusi says a mixed distribution restrains rich and poor through fear, dependence, and cooperation; Kuran Yolu says undifferentiated abilities and means would obstruct work and civilization. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL(صاحب الكشف)+]
- 42:27/q11/p3 Distribution is a test of gratitude and patience rather than a reward matched to virtue. — Islahi says believers and disbelievers receive wealth under a test; Kuran Yolu says giving strictly by moral status would remove life's trial. Tabatabai allows wealth and children as trials and some prosperity as gradual reprieve, not a refutation of the usual welfare rule. [5 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 42:27/q11/p4 God chooses freely and wisely without being obliged to maximize each person's worldly benefit. — Qurtubi's scholars hold that His acts have wisdom yet He is not bound to choose the most beneficial; wealth and poverty signify neither merit nor disgrace. Bursevi reports al-Kashifi's eternal-decree language. Ibn Ashur says only God encompasses the competing causes and interests. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(علماؤنا); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 42:27/q12 How is yunazzilu read?  [يُنَزِّلُ · readings]
turns on: The recited form is Form II with gemination or Form IV without it.
- 42:27/q12/p1 With doubled zay, the majority reading from Form II. — Razi and Alusi contrast the majority with Ibn Kathir and Abu Amr. Qiraat-ER says Form II may suggest staged or intensive descent, though both forms mean causing descent. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); QIRAAT-ER]
- 42:27/q12/p2 With light zay, the reading of Ibn Kathir and Abu Amr from anzala. — Nasafi calls this the reading of Meccans and Abu Amr. Qiraat-ER describes Form IV as the simpler causative sending down. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); QIRAAT-ER]

## 42:27/q13 What does sending down provision convey?  [يُنَزِّلُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the verb is given a distributive gloss or explained as a spatial metaphor.
- 42:27/q13/p1 God distributes or expands provision in measured amounts. — Damghani glosses yunazzilu by distribution or expansion; the widespread explanation is apportionment. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]
- 42:27/q13/p2 Calling provision a descent images the exalted God's gift as coming from above. — Ibn Ashur identifies the verb as metaphorical and says the expression recurs in the Quran. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 42:27/q14 What does bi-qadar mean?  [بِقَدَرٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether qadar is taken as a specified measure or as constriction in contrast to expansion.
- 42:27/q14/p1 By a determined measure or apportionment. — Kashshaf, Razi, Nasafi, al-Qamus, Bahr al-Ulum, Alusi, and Ibn Ashur give measuring or specification. Tabatabai cites 77:23 for assigning a determined measure and notes qadr/qadar; al-Kashifi calls the apportionment an eternal decree. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(القاموس,الكاشفي,بحر العلوم,كشف الأسرار); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 42:27/q14/p2 By restriction, as the opposite of expanding provision. — Tabatabai takes the phrase against 42:12's expansion and constriction of livelihood. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 42:27/q15 What grammatical forms and complements occur in the apportionment clause?  [يُنَزِّلُ بِقَدَرٍۢ مَّا يَشَآءُ · grammar]
turns on: The notes discuss the noun's vocalization, the verb's object, and an external pronunciation analogy.
- 42:27/q15/p1 The measuring verbal noun occurs as qadr or qadar. — Kashshaf says the verb accepts two verbal nouns; Razi, Nasafi, Bahr al-Ulum, and Tabatabai record the two forms, while Ibn Ashur specifies fatḥa on both consonants for qadar. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(بحر العلوم); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 42:27/q15/p2 'What He wills' is the direct object of yunazzilu. — Bursevi gives the syntactic analysis. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 42:27/q15/p3 In the cited lunar-month hadith, fa-qdurū is read with kasra on dal. — Bursevi reports the Prophet's instruction to estimate or complete thirty days when the crescent is obscured; he rejects a damma pronunciation. This is an analogy for the root, not a reading of this verse. [2 notes: BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام); BURSEVI+]

## 42:27/q16 What does the closing statement say about God's knowledge and sight?  [إِنَّهُۥ بِعِبَادِهِۦ خَبِيرٌۢ بَصِيرٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the paired names give a general claim of full knowledge or distinct relations before and after realization.
- 42:27/q16/p1 He knows hidden and manifest conditions, outcomes, and what benefits or harms each servant. — Kashshaf and Razi stress future outcomes; Baydawi, Bursevi, Alusi, and Biqai add hidden and outward states; Tabrisi states benefits and harms. This knowledge grounds wise allotment rather than stinginess or inability, as Jishumi argues. [17 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 42:27/q16/p2 Khabir knows conditions before their causes occur; basir knows them when realized. — Ibn Ashur describes two relations of the same divine knowledge. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 42:27/q17 How do the words of the verse support its explanation?  [لِعِبَادِهِۦ يُنَزِّلُ إِنَّهُۥ بِعِبَادِهِۦ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes explain the repeated noun, causal ending, conditional form, and ellipsis differently.
- 42:27/q17/p1 Repeating 'His servants' instead of a pronoun grounds God's knowledge and provision in His ownership and care. — Tabatabai says a servant receives provision from his master and cannot be hidden from his creator; Biqai says the wording embraces all servants. [3 notes: TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 42:27/q17/p2 The final inna clause signals the cause of measured distribution rather than simply emphasizing a fact. — Ibn Ashur says inna draws attention and acts like a causal fa; Biqai says naming God here heightens the issue's demand for perfect knowledge and power. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 42:27/q17/p3 The conditional presents a universal wisdom about the created order. — Ibn Ashur says addressing all servants makes a conclusive coda; Burhan notes that transgression explains withholding abundance. Qushayri likens the outward wording to a human apology for not expanding a poor servant's wealth. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN; QUSHAYRI]
- 42:27/q17/p4 The ellipsis points to human greed and imagined self-sufficiency. — Asad supplies 102:1 and 96:6 as parallels. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 42:27/q18 How is the final clause parsed?  [إِنَّهُۥ خَبِيرٌۢ · grammar]
- 42:27/q18/p1 The pronoun is the subject of inna and khabir is its predicate. — Jishumi gives the iʿrab. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 42:27/q19 What does the verse imply about human acts and divine willing?  [بَسَطَ لَبَغَوْا۟ · theology]
turns on: Whether a servant's inclination and act arise from the servant's choice or God's creation.
- 42:27/q19/p1 Human agents can choose obedience or rebellion; God varies provision as gracious aid toward obedience. — Jishumi argues that otherwise different allotments would be pointless; ability alone does not compel rebellion, and divine لطف presupposes both courses are possible. Al-Jubbai argues God cannot will expanded provision that would cause rebellion, much less will rebellion itself. Jishumi says God does not rebel and cannot be blamed for acts occasioning it. [9 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(الجبائي)]
- 42:27/q19/p2 God creates the inclination that leads to transgression. — Razi rejects a self-originating inclination: a new inclination needs a cause, while appeal to an earlier inclination regresses infinitely; rational people would not create their own deficiency. He also rejects al-Jubbai's answer about wealthy transgressors, citing 96:6–7 and the means wealth gives to harm. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL(الجبائي)-; JISHUMI-]

## 42:27/q20 Is God obliged to choose whatever maximizes benefit?  [بِقَدَرٍۢ مَّا يَشَآءُ · theology]
turns on: Whether wisdom and benefit impose a binding rule on God's choices.
- 42:27/q20/p1 The differentiated provision is لطف serving human welfare and moral responsibility. — Jishumi says God's knowledge and sight show gracious aid, not incapacity or stinginess; he ties it to genuine alternative human choices. [2 notes: JISHUMI]
- 42:27/q20/p2 God acts wisely but is not obligated to choose the maximally beneficial condition in each case. — Qurtubi's scholars say wealth may be withheld to prevent corruption, yet divine action follows will rather than a binding benefit rule; Ibn Ashur says the school dispute is largely verbal and no one denies beneficence. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(علماؤنا); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 42:27/q21 What transmitted sayings illustrate provision's benefit and danger?  [ٱلرِّزْقَ بِقَدَرٍۢ · reports]
turns on: The reports give different illustrations and applications of abundance, restraint, and care.
- 42:27/q21/p1 The best provision neither makes a person transgress nor distracts from God. — Qatada's maxim appears in Tabari, Thalabi, Ibn Kathir, and Durr, with Durr also wording it as 'best life.' [5 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 42:27/q21/p2 The Prophet especially feared worldly bloom and abundance for his community. — Thalabi, Kashshaf, Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, Bursevi, and Alusi report the warning. Qatada's fuller account asks whether good can produce evil; the Prophet compares lush growth that can kill a grazing animal. [9 notes: THALABI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(رسول الله); BURSEVI(النبي); ALUSI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 42:27/q21/p3 Wealth used in God's approved path is good; indulgence, withheld dues, and wrongful acquisition are harmful. — Tabari and Durr give the distinction. Abu Said's report commends kinship and God's path, while wrongful acquisition leaves its owner unsatisfied and testifies against him; the growth analogy says good itself only brings good. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(رسول الله)]
- 42:27/q21/p4 God may assign wealth, poverty, illness, health, or even worship to protect different servants' faith. — The long divine report through Anas, the Prophet, and Gabriel also tells of war on God's friend's aggressor, nearness through duties and voluntary worship, answered prayer, and reluctance to take a believer's soul. Versions differ in detail; Baghawi's extract ends mid-sentence and gives no grading. Durr's version grounds all assignments in knowing hearts. ## 42:27/q22 How does this verse explain the preceding promise to answer believers?  [* · links]
turns on: How worldly hardship or prosperity is reconciled with the nearby promise of divine response.
- 42:27/q22/p1 An unanswered request for wealth may itself be a wise response, since abundance can harm the petitioner. — Razi links it to 42:26; Biqai says divine response need not grant each worldly request as worded, and cites three forms of answered prayer. - 42:27/q22/p2 The promise directs attention to the Hereafter, where God's response becomes fully clear. — Asad notes righteous suffering and wrongdoer prosperity in this short worldly stage; Study Quran reads 42:26 with 42:23 as directing increase to the lasting Hereafter. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 42:27/q22/p3 The verse follows a warning with a promise and then guards against mistaking that promise for unlimited worldly wealth. — Jishumi notes the passage's paired promise and warning, God's usual encouragement and deterrence; Razi and Biqai explain why the promise does not imply every desired grant. ## 42:27/q23 How does the verse connect with the surah's wider argument?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources connect the verse to provision, revelation, afterlife contrast, the next surah, and the surah's broad themes.
- 42:27/q23/p1 It continues the surah's provision theme and links to signs of God's unity. — Tabatabai traces provision to 42:12 and 42:19, then to creation, creatures, ships, and offspring at 42:29, 32, 49–50; Ibn Ashur calls worldly provisions among the surah's signs of unity. Study Quran's introduction likewise joins wealth, children, and revelation. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 42:27/q23/p2 Provision includes guidance, so measured distribution echoes gradual revelation and differing capacities. — Tabatabai says the surah's subject of revelation returns through spiritual provision; 17:106 supports gradual disclosure, 13:17 illustrates receptive capacities, and duties likewise vary by trial. [7 notes: TABATABAI]
- 42:27/q23/p3 The surrounding passage contrasts worldly and lasting provision and warns against treating wealth as favor. — Ibn Ashur relates 42:18–19 to believers' afterlife focus and says nearby clauses are left without conjunction because of that close relation; Tabatabai traces the subsequent contrast and summary of rulings and admonitions. Study Quran points to 42:23 and 42:26. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 42:27/q23/p4 The next surah elaborates differentiated livelihoods and ranks. — Islahi says al-Zukhruf parallels this surah; he cites 43:33–35 for mutual service, temporary riches, and the virtuous end. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 42:27/q23/p5 The surah's larger introduction connects God's ownership, revelation, prophecy, judgment, and worldly signs. — Ibn Ashur describes divine ownership and legislative choice, human messengers, the idolaters' lack of proof, the approaching Hour, the Prophet's truthfulness, and the Quranic challenge of disjointed-letter surahs (2:1). He relates revelation to previous messengers and warning to Mecca; verse 27 is among its provision signs. [8 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 42:27/q24 What is reported about this verse's place of revelation and the surah's chronology?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The principal disagreement concerns whether the Suffa reports make verse 27 Madinan.
- 42:27/q24/p1 Verse 27 is Madinan because it concerns the People of the Bench. — Ibn Ashur quotes Muqatil via Ibn al-Faras for this claim, and Study Quran reports that some add the verse to the Madinan exceptions, referring to 2:273. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(مقاتل); STUDYQURAN(Āl)]
- 42:27/q24/p2 Verse 27 is Meccan; the Bench reports are weak or later applications. — Ibn Ashur rejects Khabbab's report as an occasion even if authentic and grades the anonymous Suffa report weak. He notes proposed Madinan exceptions at 42:23–24 and 42:39–41, yet considers the surah otherwise Meccan and allows revelation to have continued near the Aqaba pledge. - 42:27/q24/p3 The surah was the sixty-ninth revealed, after al-Kahf and before Ibrahim, in a reported order. — Ibn Ashur transmits al-Ja'bari's order from Jabir b. Zayd. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الجعبري عن جابر بن زيد)]
- 42:27/q24/p4 The surah has fifty verses in four regional counts and fifty-three in the Kufan count. — Ibn Ashur names Madinan, Meccan, Syrian, and Basran counts as fifty. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 42:27/q25 What practical lesson is drawn from differing wealth?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw distinct lessons about status, gratitude, effort, and the risk of abundance.
- 42:27/q25/p1 Wealth and poverty do not prove virtue, disgrace, or divine favor. — Qurtubi's scholars reject that inference; Study Quran says a reduction is not humiliation and increase is not necessarily favor; Islahi says the affluent cannot claim rightness or superiority over the Prophet and poorer believers. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(علماؤنا); STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 42:27/q25/p2 Recipients should thank God rather than credit their ability or ancestry. — Islahi says God can grant wealth and remove it; the distribution tests patience and gratitude. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 42:27/q25/p3 People should strive for good means in this life and the next and use what is given well. — Kuran Yolu draws this practical obligation despite the measure being beyond an individual's full grasp. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 42:27/q25/p4 Abundance itself can harm a person. — Jishumi states the warning; Qushayri presents it as the outward reason for withholding from a poor servant. [2 notes: JISHUMI; QUSHAYRI]

## 42:27/q26 What account of meaningful metaphor is supplied in the cited literary discussion?  [* · rhetoric]
- 42:27/q26/p1 A useful metaphor conveys extra meaning through an underlying simile. — Jurjani says 'lion' for a brave man evokes force and daring; 'sea' evokes generosity, and sun or moon beauty and radiance; 'drawn sword' can mean powerful support or penetrating judgment. Calling a human nose by a horse-specific word adds nothing, so is not a meaningful metaphor. He says such simile is shared across languages and its forms are innumerable. [5 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]

## 42:27/q27 How does the earlier divine naming frame this verse's distribution of provision?  [* · links]
- 42:27/q27/p1 God's power and might confirm that His gentleness and provision reflect wisdom, not weakness or scarcity. — Ibn Ashur says the earlier 'Powerful, Mighty' can modify the attribute لطيف or the sentence about providing whom God wills. Its definite predicate emphatically makes strength and might exclusive to God; his allocation follows wisdom about creatures. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
