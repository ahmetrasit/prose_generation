# 89:17: 15 questions

## 89:17/q01 What does kallā reject in the claims about wealth and poverty?  [كَلَّا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether kallā primarily corrects a false inference about divine standing, censures the unequal response to provision, or exposes the conduct that actually disgraces them.
- 89:17/q01/p1 Wealth does not prove divine honor, and poverty does not prove divine humiliation. — Qatāda, al-Ḥasan, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ṭabarī, and many later authors reject both inferences. Al-Ṭabarī prefers this because 89:17–18 identify conduct as the cause of disgrace. Al-Bayḍāwī notes the text says provision was 'measured,' not that God humiliated the person: expansion remains a benefit without proving approval. Ibn ʿĀshūr says revelation, not appearances, discloses divine standing (18:103–105); actual worldly benefits are compatible with the rebuttal, and if unbelief caused worldly deprivation every unbeliever would be poor. Biqāʿī cites the sound report of the bald, leprous, and blind Israelites and says prosperity can be a snare while restriction can expiate sin. Ibn Kathīr says God gives and withholds from both loved and unloved people. [47 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); NAHHAS; BAGHAWI; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI(حديث قدسي); QURTUBI-FULL(الله عز وجل); BAYDAWI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; IBNKATHIR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(الحسن); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+; TAB-FULL+; TAB+]
- 89:17/q01/p2 The human should praise God in both prosperity and poverty rather than praise only for wealth and complain at want. — Al-Farrāʾ gives this admonition; al-Ṭabarī transmits it as another explanation, and Ibn al-Jawzī cites al-Farrāʾ while also rejecting the inference that wealth and poverty prove honor or disgrace. [7 notes: FARRA; TAB(آخرون); TAB-FULL(آخرون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء)]
- 89:17/q01/p3 The rebuke also exposes the person's actual neglect of orphans as the disgrace hidden by the claim. — Al-Ṭabarī makes the following neglect of orphans and the poor the decisive evidence. Al-Ḥasan and later authors juxtapose the false claim with the conduct; Ibn ʿĀshūr says earlier nations' fate in 89:13–14 already supplies the broader conclusion. [7 notes: TAB; JISHUMI; TABRISI; DURR(الحسن); IBNASHUR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs)]

## 89:17/q02 On what basis are people honored or humiliated, and why is provision varied?  [كَلَّا · theology]
turns on: The notes contrast decree without stated cause, hidden benefit, and testing, while also identifying obedience and treatment of the vulnerable as measures of standing.
- 89:17/q02/p1 Honor is through obedience or piety and disgrace through disobedience or sin. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers Qatāda because the ensuing neglect of the orphan and poor shows the ground of humiliation. The divine saying cited by al-Qurṭubī contrasts obedience and sin. Al-Nasafī phrases honor as being enabled to obey and humiliation as abandonment. Biqāʿī says treatment of orphans shows the real measure; love or aversion to worldly life signals disposition to sin or obedience. [16 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI(حديث قدسي); QURTUBI-FULL(الله عز وجل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 89:17/q02/p2 Provision follows divine decree, power, will, and judgment beyond human causal explanation. — Al-Rāzī presents this as the Sunni account, and Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Bursevī say neither wealth nor poverty is assigned to prove personal merit or contempt. Al-Qurṭubī also attributes both conditions to decree. [7 notes: RAZI(أهل السنة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 89:17/q02/p3 Provision follows hidden beneficial purposes known only to God. — Al-Rāzī presents this as the Muʿtazilite account. Ibn ʿĀshūr says divine dealings have purposes beyond ignorant conjecture and calls for reasoning guided by revelation. [4 notes: RAZI(المعتزلة); RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR]
- 89:17/q02/p4 Both abundance and restriction are tests of gratitude, patience, and conduct toward others. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes gratitude and obedience for the rich from gratitude and patience for the poor. Al-Nasafī cites 21:35 on testing with good and evil, and 28:78 against entitlement. Iṣlāḥī says favors test service to others while hardship tests contentment; the peaceful soul succeeds. Qur'an Yolu adds sharing and organized solidarity. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 89:17/q02/p5 Restriction can itself honor by turning a person toward God, while abundance can entrap or ruin. — Biqāʿī says constriction can expiate sin or restrain desire and prosperity can be gradual enticement. Al-Bursevī reports that deprivation may keep blessings from distracting from their Giver; his report of seventy impoverished Companions of the Bench illustrates poverty without humiliation. A spiritual master sees the claim 'my Lord humiliated me' as a veil over merciful movement from nature to spirit, self to heart, and separation to union. [8 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(أبو هريرة,بعض اكبار,بعضهم)]
- 89:17/q02/p6 Honoring, urging, eating, and loving are human acts for which people are praised or blamed. — Al-Jishumī infers human agency from the praise and blame attached to the four actions. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 89:17/q03 How should a person respond to prosperity and hardship?  [كَلَّا · interpretation]
turns on: The sources stress either praise and patience in both conditions or the social duties that wealth makes possible.
- 89:17/q03/p1 Give thanks and obey in prosperity; remain grateful and patient in hardship. — Al-Farrāʾ and those reporting him stress praise in both states. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ibn Kathīr add obedience and patience. Al-Bursevī cites a hadith that faith is half patience and half gratitude. [11 notes: FARRA; TAB(آخرون); TAB-FULL(آخرون); IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR; QURTUBI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); BURSEVI]
- 89:17/q03/p2 Use prosperity to fulfill others' rights, particularly those of orphans and the poor; endure deprivation without despair. — Iṣlāḥī describes proud Makkan and Ṭāʾif leaders whose wealth should have led to gratitude and service. Qur'an Yolu joins sharing and organized solidarity with patience and reverence for God's command. Al-Māwardī is not in these notes. [7 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL]

## 89:17/q04 How does kallā function as a stop or opening?  [كَلَّا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether kallā is heard as closing the prior claim or emphatically opening the next sentence.
- 89:17/q04/p1 It can close the preceding false claim, and stopping here is preferred. — Al-Burhān reports Makkī's first category: denial of what precedes permits a stop at kallā, preferred at eleven locations including al-Fajr; it cites 19:15, 23:100, 34:27, 70:39, 74:32, 83:14, and 104:4. [1 notes: BURHAN(مكي)]
- 89:17/q04/p2 Restarting at kallā can make it an emphatic opening. — Al-Burhān reports this as the alternative to stopping on the denial. [1 notes: BURHAN(مكي)]

## 89:17/q05 What does bal do in the move to the orphan rebuke?  [بَل · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the transition emphasizes escalation to worse acts, correction of the supposed cause of disgrace, or sentence structure.
- 89:17/q05/p1 It moves from a false statement to worse conduct: wealthy people neglect the orphan's due. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, and al-Ālūsī stress the escalation; abundance creates a duty to give, and their failure is worse than their mistaken words. Al-Ālūsī says direct address intensifies censure. Ibn ʿĀshūr connects the shift by contrast to their boast of divine favor and generosity while they hoard surplus. [18 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI]
- 89:17/q05/p2 It turns from the claim of humiliation by restricted provision to the actual fault, neglect of orphans. — Al-Ṭabarī points to 89:18 as confirming this cause; al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī put the fault on the people's own behavior. [3 notes: TAB; JISHUMI; TABRISI]
- 89:17/q05/p3 It introduces a new sentence, either opening discourse or parenthetically separating the two kallā statements. — Ibn ʿĀshūr describes both discourse opening and parenthesis as syntactic possibilities. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]

## 89:17/q06 How does the plural address relate to the earlier singular human?  [لَّا تُكْرِمُونَ · grammar]
turns on: The choice between a direct turn to humanity, an implied 'say,' and continued third-person reference to collective al-insān.
- 89:17/q06/p1 The discourse shifts to direct plural address of humanity as a collective, making the rebuke more forceful. — Al-Ṭabarī observes the address; al-Fārisī explains agreement by grammatical form versus collective meaning and cites 7:4. Al-Samīn, Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr describe the genus-to-plural shift; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it confrontation of the offenders. [11 notes: TAB; FARISI-HUJJA; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR(الجمهور); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 89:17/q06/p2 The second person may be an implied command to the Prophet: 'Say this to them.' — Al-Fārisī and al-Rāzī give this rationale; al-Ālūsī reports that supplying qul removes a grammatical shift while retaining censure. [4 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI(قيل)]
- 89:17/q06/p3 The third person continues the generic collective 'human' and informs the Prophet and believers about their conduct. — Al-Fārisī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, and al-Samīn connect the plural to al-insān. Al-Fārisī and al-Wāḥidī cite 7:4 for shifting by sense; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 90:6–7. [10 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عمرو); RAZI(أبو عمرو); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(أبو عمرو ويعقوب); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); IBNASHUR(أبو عمرو ويعقوب); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 89:17/q07 How are 'you do not honor' and the following verbs read?  [لَّا تُكْرِمُونَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the verbs confront listeners with tāʾ or report their conduct with yāʾ; the notes give reader lists and prophetic transmission for the alternatives.
- 89:17/q07/p1 They are read with second-person tāʾ, confronting the audience. — Al-Ṭabarī names Abū Jaʿfar and most Kufans; other lists name Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, ʿĀṣim, Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, or the non-Basrans. The variant covers the four verbs of honoring, urging, eating, and loving, though al-Fārisī lists three. Al-Durr reports ʿĀṣim with lengthened unhamzated taḥāḍḍūn and geminated lamman. Al-Nashr excepts al-Zubayrī from Rawḥ from the Basran third-person group. [21 notes: TAB(أبو جعفر وعامة قراء الكوفة); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وعاصم وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عمرو); JISHUMI(أبو عمرو ويعقوب); BAGHAWI(أهل البصرة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل البصرة); KASHSHAF; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أهل البصرة); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(الباقون); BAYDAWI(أبو عمرو); NASAFI(البصري); SAMIN-DURR; NASHR; DURR(عاصم); IBNASHUR(الجمهور); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); QIRAAT-ER]
- 89:17/q07/p2 They are read with third-person yāʾ, reporting 'they do not honor' and continuing the earlier collective referent. — Abū ʿAmr and Yaʿqūb or the Basrans are named most often; Bayḍāwī adds Sahl. Al-Ālūsī lists al-Ḥasan, Mujāhid, Abū Rajaʾ, Qatāda, and al-Jaḥdarī. Reports ascribe yāʾ to prophetic recitation through ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf (authenticated by al-Ḥākim), Abū Hurayra, and Ibn ʿUmar. The listed verbs usually run through love in 89:20. Qiraat-ER notes that prefix and person change while form and object remain. [30 notes: TAB(عامة قراء البصرة); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن عوف); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عمرو); JISHUMI(أبو عمرو ويعقوب); BAGHAWI(أهل البصرة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل البصرة); KASHSHAF; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أهل البصرة); RAZI(أبو عمرو); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(أبو عمرو ويعقوب); BAYDAWI(أبو عمرو); NASAFI(البصري); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); NASHR; DURR(النبي); DURR-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن عوف); ALUSI(الحسن ومجاهد وأبو رجاء وقتادة والجحدري وأبو عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR(أبو عمرو ويعقوب); IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); QIRAAT-ER]
- 89:17/q07/p3 The established regional forms are all valid in meaning. — Al-Ṭabarī permits recitation with any of three established regional readings; Study Quran says either person reading is accepted. [2 notes: TAB; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ)]

## 89:17/q08 What does 'the orphan' denote here?  [ٱلْيَتِيمَ · meaning]
- 89:17/q08/p1 A child who lost his father before adulthood; the definite singular denotes orphans collectively. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds that for an animal, orphan denotes loss of its mother. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the generic definite and compares his discussion at 4:2. Al-Bursevī likewise specifies a human orphan who lost a father before maturity. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 89:17/q09 Which individual is reported as the orphan behind this verse?  [ٱلْيَتِيمَ · reports]
- 89:17/q09/p1 Qudāma ibn Maẓʿūn was an orphan under Umayya ibn Khalaf, who withheld his right. — Muqātil is named as the reporter in al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī, and al-Qurṭubī. Name spellings vary slightly; some reports say this was the occasion of revelation. [9 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); TABRISI(مقاتل); RAZI(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(مقاتل); QURTUBI(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل)]

## 89:17/q10 What does failing to honor the orphan mean?  [لَّا تُكْرِمُونَ ٱلْيَتِيمَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether honor focuses on care and social dignity, protected inheritance, or the specific absorption of orphan property; several sources include more than one.
- 89:17/q10/p1 Failing to give the orphan kindness, care, adequate support, and dignity. — Al-Ṭabrisī emphasizes enough support to avoid degrading need. Al-Jishumī includes care of person and property because of the orphan's vulnerability. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes loss of provider, property exposure, and the child's feelings. Iṣlāḥī adds respected social status and protection from hardship beyond mere spending. Biqāʿī says people neglect the weak child partly because no return benefit or praise is expected. Al-Rāzī relates this to not urging feeding of the poor in 89:18. [28 notes: JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 89:17/q10/p2 Denying the orphan the inheritance or due owed to him, often by consuming his property. — Al-Kalbī offers this alongside neglect of kindness and cites 89:19 for inheritance. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, and Ibn al-Jawzī connect it to pre-Islamic denial of inheritance to children or women. Al-Qurṭubī adds hasty and wasteful consumption before the orphan matures. Al-Rāzī distinguishes withholding inheritance and joining the child's property to one's own, linked respectively to 89:19 and 89:20. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says younger children are left destitute. Study Quran cites 2:220, 4:2–3, 4:8–10, 4:127, 6:152, and 107:1–3. [17 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 89:17/q10/p3 The abuse includes taking orphan wealth and merging it with one's own. — Al-Rāzī gives this as a further form, relating it to the love of wealth in 89:20. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 89:17/q11 What duty and wider moral lesson does the orphan rebuke teach?  [لَّا تُكْرِمُونَ ٱلْيَتِيمَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the emphasis is the immediate duty to an orphan or the social and spiritual failure the neglect reveals.
- 89:17/q11/p1 People must honor vulnerable orphans and safeguard their persons and property. — Al-Jishumī infers the positive duty from the prohibition of dishonor. Al-Biqāʿī says their inability to repay favor exposes selfish motive. Al-ʿĀshūr adds material support, kind treatment, and protection of feelings. Al-Malı emphasizes using present opportunity for good before the afterlife. [9 notes: JISHUMI; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 89:17/q11/p2 Failure toward orphans and the poor exposes greed, neglect of the Hereafter, and failed stewardship of wealth. — Muqātil connects neglect to disbelief in the Hereafter. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ links oppression and corruption to greedy individualism dulling conscience and insight. Qur'an Yolu criticizes polytheist arrogance, selfishness, and attachment to wealth while presenting compassion as a religious duty. [6 notes: MUQATIL; BINTSHATI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QUTB-ZILAL]

## 89:17/q12 What prophetic reports commend honoring or caring for an orphan?  [ٱلْيَتِيمَ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ between the worth of a household's treatment and the caregiver's promised closeness to the Prophet.
- 89:17/q12/p1 The most beloved or best home honors an orphan; the worst home mistreats one. — The 'most beloved home' wording is reported from ʿUmar and elsewhere without a named transmitter; al-Suyūṭī marks it sound in Ibn ʿAṭiyya's note, while Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī give no grading. Abū Hurayra's report in Ibn Kathīr contrasts best and worst Muslim homes. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA(عمر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN(الحديث); IBNKATHIR(النبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة); BURSEVI; ALUSI(حديث); ALUSI-FULL(النبي)]
- 89:17/q12/p2 The Prophet and an orphan's caregiver will be close together in Paradise like the index and middle fingers. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports the gesture; Ibn Kathīr gives transmissions from Abū Hurayra and Sahl ibn Saʿd, naming both fingers. [5 notes: TABRISI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR(النبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة,سهل بن سعد)]

## 89:17/q13 How do the neighboring poor and inheritance clauses explain this rebuke?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the next verses chiefly clarify omitted duties, identify inheritance abuses, or tie the social rebuke back to the false divine-honor claim.
- 89:17/q13/p1 Failure to urge feeding the poor accompanies failure to honor the orphan, and can imply failing to feed the poor oneself. — Al-Ṭabarī cites 89:18 as evidence that disgrace comes from conduct. Al-Bayḍāwī says the wealthy do not even urge family to feed. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees reciprocal ellipsis: failing to honor also means failing to urge guardians, while failing to urge feeding implies failing to feed from one's wealth. Rāzī distinguishes Muqātil's 'not feeding' from his own 'not urging others.' [8 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 89:17/q13/p2 The inheritance and wealth verses expose the material form of mistreating orphans and greed. — Al-Kalbī and al-Rāzī use 89:19 for withholding inheritance; al-Rāzī uses 89:20 for merging orphan wealth with one's own. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reads greed as dulling moral perception. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI]
- 89:17/q13/p3 The sequence links false claims about divine honor to social failures against vulnerable people. — Corpus Coranicum observes the ʾakrama–ʾahāna opposition and verse 17's 'honor' echo, judging 89:15–16 likely later additions and finding a less stable relation to God alongside social sins; elsewhere the positive counterpart is ṣ-b-r. Ibn ʿĀshūr links the transition to their boast of generosity. [3 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 89:17/q14 What expressions and readings in the neighboring clauses bear on this passage?  [* · grammar]
turns on: The notes treat separate forms in adjacent verses as contextual evidence for the orphan rebuke.
- 89:17/q14/p1 The phrase about 'food of the poor' means feeding the poor through ellipsis or use of food for feeding. — Al-Ṭabrisī supplies an omitted verbal noun as one analysis and treats 'food' itself as standing for the act of feeding as another. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 89:17/q14/p2 The reciprocal verb for urging can reflect loss of one initial tāʾ; its reading varies in vocalization and length. — Al-Rāzī derives the reciprocal from tatafāʿalūn and notes a reading with ḍamma on tāʾ. Al-Zajjāj gives lā taḥāḍḍūn and lā taḥāḍḍūna; al-Ṭabarī reports Kufan alif versus Basran no alif. Al-Durr calls ʿĀṣim's form lengthened and unhamzated. [7 notes: TAB(أبو جعفر وعامة قراء الكوفة,عامة قراء البصرة); ZAJJAJ; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; RAZI-FULL; DURR(عاصم)]
- 89:17/q14/p3 Some readings omit the final yāʾ of 'honored me' and 'humiliated me'; Ibn ʿĀmir also doubles the dāl of qaddara. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports omission in continuation and pause, comparing a night-oath reading. Al-Bayḍāwī names Ibn ʿĀmir, Kufans, and Abū ʿAmr for omission in both settings and Nāfiʿ in pause; he separately reports Ibn ʿĀmir's doubled dāl. [3 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI(ابن عامر)]
- 89:17/q14/p4 The inheritance wording evokes gathering, consuming, and attachment to wealth. — Al-Rāzī derives turāth from wirāth by replacing initial wāw with tāʾ, like wijāh. Al-Layth glosses lamm as forceful gathering (troop or stones); al-Rāzī says 'intense eating' is only a paraphrase. Al-Zajjāj reads voracious gathering of orphan inheritance; al-Ḥasan says heirs merge their own and the orphan's shares; another view includes lawful, doubtful, and unlawful estate parts. Al-Kashshāf instead focuses on idle heirs wasting unearned inheritance on lavish food. Rāzī glosses jamm as abundance and excessive love of wealth; Abū ʿAmr explains it as 'increases.' [9 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو عمرو,الحسن,الزجاج,الليث,صاحب الكشاف)]

## 89:17/q15 How does this orphan rebuke connect with other passages and the sūra's earlier charges?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the links are retrospective to earlier tyrants, parallels in other texts, or the sūra's closing moral trajectory.
- 89:17/q15/p1 The social sins specify the earlier charges against ʿĀd, Thamūd, and Pharaoh. — Corpus Coranicum reads 89:17–20 as concrete examples of neglected vulnerable people and excessive love of possessions. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 89:17/q15/p2 The orphan, poor, and feeding pattern echoes other Qur'anic passages. — Corpus Coranicum cites 107:2–3 and 90:11–16 as early and middle Meccan parallels; Study Quran cites 107:1–3 alongside passages about orphan property. Corpus Coranicum adds that similar motifs in biblical prophetic books may reflect a literary genre familiar to the audience. [3 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN]
- 89:17/q15/p3 The broader passage teaches that watchfulness before God and preparation for the Hereafter must shape care for the weak. — Muqātil says neglect stems from not expecting the Hereafter. Elmalılı urges good while there is opportunity; Iṣlāḥī connects the trial to the peaceful soul. [3 notes: MUQATIL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
