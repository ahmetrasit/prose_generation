# 69:11: 24 questions

## 69:11/q01 What does the water's transgression mean?  [طَغَا ٱلْمَآءُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ṭaghā describes physical excess, loss of the keepers' measured control, simple appearance, or an inward flood.
- 69:11/q01/p1 The floodwater rose, grew abundant, and exceeded its customary bounds, covering the land. — Ibn ʿAbbās locates the abundance in the nights Noah's people drowned; Mujāhid, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, and ʿAṭāʾ gloss it as rising or increasing. Al-Jishumī defines ṭughyān as crossing a limit. Ibn Kathīr says this occurred by God's permission. Al-Biqāʿī says people could neither control nor resist it and it exceeded ordinary causes. Al-Bayḍāwī relates this flood to the prior destroyed peoples; the Study Quran identifies Noah's Flood and Ark. [30 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; RUMMANI; MAWARDI-FULL(عطاء); WAHIDI-BASIT(سائر المفسرين); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(سائر المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 69:11/q01/p2 The water exceeded its angelic keepers' measure and control; the exception accompanied divine anger. — ʿAlī, Ibn ʿAbbās, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and transmitted prophetic reports say rain and wind ordinarily arrive by known measure, but Noah's water and ʿĀd's wind overwhelmed their keepers (69:6–7). Saʿīd describes the water's anger at God's anger. Al-Qurṭubī notes a raised version earlier in the sūra; al-Ālūsī says the Ṣaḥīḥayn and others corroborate ʿAlī's report. Al-Rāzī says the keepers could not tell how much escaped; Ibn Kathīr describes release beyond reservoirs. Al-Burṣawī offers this as an alternative level the angels could not contain. [21 notes: TAB-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,علي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي عن ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جبير); IBNKATHIR-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,سعيد بن جبير,قبيصة بن ذؤيب); BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب)]
- 69:11/q01/p3 The water appeared. — Al-Māwardī transmits this gloss from Ibn Abī Najīḥ. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن أبي نجيح)]
- 69:11/q01/p4 The overflowing water is the flood of prime matter. — Al-Qāshānī offers an inward reading. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 69:11/q02 How high did the floodwater rise?  [طَغَا ٱلْمَآءُ · reports]
turns on: The reports give different heights and different reference points.
- 69:11/q02/p1 It rose fifteen cubits above everything, described also as fifteen above the highest mountain. — Qatāda is repeatedly named; al-Wāḥidī's broader report names Qatāda and Muqātil. Al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Burṣawī and al-Ālūsī specify the highest mountain rather than 'everything.' [12 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(سائر المفسرين); JISHUMI(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL]
- 69:11/q02/p2 It rose forty cubits above everything. — Muqātil gives this measurement. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 69:11/q02/p3 It rose more than fifteen cubits. — Al-Jishumī cites Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid for this alternative. [1 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عباس، ومجاهد)]
- 69:11/q02/p4 It rose five hundred cubits above everything. — Al-Burṣawī cites this report. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 69:11/q02/p5 It rose above the highest mountain, without a specified distance. — Al-Biqāʿī says it drowned people there and could not be resisted. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 69:11/q03 Why is the floodwater described as transgressing?  [طَغَا ٱلْمَآءُ · rhetoric]
- 69:11/q03/p1 Its rising is pictured as overpowering arrogance or tyranny, intensifying the force of the flood. — Al-Rummānī says literal 'rose' becomes a scene of overpowering force. Al-Burhān locates the metaphor in arrogance and water, united by excessive elevation. Al-Itqān describes borrowing abstract arrogance for perceptible abundance. Ibn ʿĀshūr likens the unprecedented force to a tyrant overstepping against people. [5 notes: RUMMANI; BURHAN; ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:11/q04 What caused Noah's flood in this account?  [طَغَا ٱلْمَآءُ · interpretation]
- 69:11/q04/p1 God punished Noah's people for persistent unbelief, sin, and rejection of his message; Noah's supplication preceded the flood. — Ibn Kathīr mentions their worship of other gods and Noah's prayer. Al-Burṣawī and al-Ālūsī specify denial of revealed teaching on resurrection. Kur'an Yolu cites 7:59–64 and 11:25–49 for the rebellious people drowned and believers saved. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 69:11/q05 What does 'We carried you' mean?  [حَمَلْنَٰكُمْ · meaning]
- 69:11/q05/p1 It means putting people aboard a vessel and rescuing them through its movement. — Al-Dāmghānī lists boarding and saving, citing Noah's vessel (54:13) and carrying on land and sea (17:70). Ibn ʿĀshūr extends the basic sense of placing one body on another to passengers in a moving vessel. Al-Burṣawī and al-Ālūsī stress raising them above floodwater until it receded, beyond mere boarding. [4 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 69:11/q06 Who is addressed as 'you' in the carrying?  [حَمَلْنَٰكُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the address is to the saved passengers themselves or to descendants by ancestral identification; al-Ālūsī disputes indwelling as its mechanism.
- 69:11/q06/p1 Later descendants are addressed through their ancestors who were carried in Noah's ark, often described as present in their forefathers' loins. — Muqātil, al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī and others give the loins formulation. Al-Naḥḥās and Asad call the address figurative or metonymic; al-Jishumī compares ancestral praise and blame (2:91). Al-Māwardī cites al-ʿAbbās's poem tracing the Prophet through ancestral loins to Noah's ship and Nusr's drowning. Al-Wāḥidī and Study Quran compare 36:41. Al-Burṣawī says ancestral rescue made descendants' existence possible. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī invokes humanity as one kind and group. Al-Ālūsī also offers an omitted possessive, 'your ancestors'; al-Elmalılı adds preservation of the believing line. Al-Iṣlāḥī specifies Quraysh as Noah's descendants. [32 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(العباس بن عبد المطلب); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(classical commentators); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; TABRISI(ابن عباس)]
- 69:11/q06/p2 The 'you' are the original believers rescued aboard the ark, who became later generations' ancestors. — Kur'an Yolu says they came ashore after the water receded. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 69:11/q06/p3 Descendants are addressed as their ancestors through indwelling. — Al-Ālūsī reports and rejects this explanation as remote. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 69:11/q07 What inward meanings are given to being carried?  [حَمَلْنَٰكُمْ · inward]
turns on: The two reports locate the carrying in different spiritual processes.
- 69:11/q07/p1 The Real flows upon spirits according to appointed divisions and reveals a mystery at spiritual stations. — Al-Qāsim argues bodies did not yet exist and spirits cannot literally bear a current. [1 notes: SULAMI(القاسم)]
- 69:11/q07/p2 God carried humanity by His signs and allotted its times by His measures. — Al-Wāsiṭī relates this to God's touching Adam's side and bringing out his descendants. [1 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي)]

## 69:11/q08 What is al-jāriya?  [ٱلْجَارِيَةِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the definite expression denotes Noah's singular ark, sailing ships generally, or an inward ark.
- 69:11/q08/p1 It is Noah's sailing ark, a ship called jāriya because it moves over water. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Zayd, Mujāhid and al-Suddī gloss it as ship. Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī also note jāriya for a young woman, explaining it by flowing youthful beauty. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite 55:24; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn cite 42:32. Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī give the riddle of ninety 'maidens' inside one ship; al-Elmalılı cites the same poetic line and 11:42. Al-Biqāʿī sees an exceptional vessel sailing where water would normally drown. Al-Qurṭubī says Noah, his children and the ancestors of all earth's people were aboard. [25 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(ابن عباس، وابن زيد); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; DURR-FULL(السدي,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 69:11/q08/p2 It refers broadly to sailing ships as a divine favor to those addressed. — Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Mahdawī's broader reading. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المهدوي)]
- 69:11/q08/p3 The ark signifies religious law, comprising scientific and practical perfection. — Al-Qāshānī's inward reading. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 69:11/q09 Why use the word al-jāriya for the ark?  [ٱلْجَارِيَةِ · rhetoric]
- 69:11/q09/p1 The expression evokes the ship's motion and has become a conventional name for a vessel. — Al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī and al-Jishumī derive it from sailing on water. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn say usage is established. Ibn ʿĀshūr says an omitted safīna is described by its motion and the epithet nearly became a name; al-Biqāʿī says the quality substitutes for its noun. Al-Elmalılı says the definite singular points to Noah's ark and hints at descendants preserved in the current of ancestral loins. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:11/q10 How does 'in the vessel' attach grammatically?  [فِى ٱلْجَارِيَةِ · grammar]
- 69:11/q10/p1 The preposition belongs to an omitted circumstantial phrase describing the people as safe aboard the moving ship. — Al-Burṣawī and al-Ālūsī say fī is not governed directly by ḥamalnā. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 69:11/q11 Who truly carried and protected the passengers?  [حَمَلْنَٰكُمْ · theology]
turns on: Whether direct divine protection or God's command through Noah explains the attribution.
- 69:11/q11/p1 God alone protected and saved them; the ark was an outward means. — Al-Burṣawī says rescue depended solely on divine protection. Al-Ālūsī calls the ship an outward means. Al-Elmalılı says its use displayed divine power and faith was the spiritual reason for boarding. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 69:11/q11/p2 The carrying is attributed to God because He instructed Noah to build the ark and put the passengers aboard. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the attribution a grammatical metaphor and cites 23:27. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:11/q12 What is reported about the ark's form?  [ٱلْجَارِيَةِ · reports]
turns on: The reports describe different physical features.
- 69:11/q12/p1 It was 1,300 cubits long and 600 wide, and sailed between waters. — Al-Jishumī transmits this from al-Ḥasan. [1 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن)]
- 69:11/q12/p2 It was shaped like a bird's breast so travel in water resembled motion through air. — Al-Biqāʿī says God gave Noah this instruction. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 69:11/q13 Who first built a ship?  [ٱلْجَارِيَةِ · reports]
- 69:11/q13/p1 Noah was the first, building it by God's revelation and protection. — Al-Biqāʿī makes this claim. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 69:11/q14 What happened to life outside the ark?  [* · reports]
- 69:11/q14/p1 The flood engulfed the earth; all humans and nonhumans outside the ark drowned, while its believers survived. — Al-Biqāʿī says none outside survived. Ibn Kathīr says humanity descends from Noah; Kur'an Yolu says the believers survived and cites 7:59–64 and 11:25–49. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 69:11/q15 What lesson does the rescue give later people?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the stress falls on gratitude for rescue or warning from the drowned.
- 69:11/q15/p1 It calls descendants to gratitude for their ancestors' rescue and their own continued existence. — Al-Qurṭubī says God favors them as descendants of the saved; Ibn ʿĀshūr extends the gratitude to all people descended from the rescued group. Al-Iṣlāḥī says Quraysh indirectly received the favor through believing ancestors. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 69:11/q15/p2 It warns the present community to obey its messenger, or it may suffer punishment like Noah's deniers. — Al-Qurṭubī relates the destroyed peoples' stories to this warning; al-Iṣlāḥī applies it to Quraysh. Al-Suddī says an attentive ear preserves the story of Noah's people punished for disobedience. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; DURR-FULL(السدي)]

## 69:11/q16 How does Noah's episode fit the preceding sequence?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources explain either the account's link to 'those before him' or its placement at the end.
- 69:11/q16/p1 It completes the destroyed peoples' examples by showing all opponents destroyed and those agreeing with messengers saved. — Al-Biqāʿī says this answers the question raised by 'those before him' (69:9–10). Al-Bayḍāwī says the flood supports the preceding account. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 69:11/q16/p2 Noah's people come last though historically first; their earthwide story closes the sequence and recalls the messengers' history. — Al-Biqāʿī says Thamūd comes first as the closest destroyed people to Mecca and thus the stronger nearby warning, while Noah's people were known to all later people. Al-Iṣlāḥī says the brief closing mention evokes prophetic history. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 69:11/q17 What general grammatical distinctions appear in the cited discussion?  [* · grammar]
turns on: The discussion distinguishes kinds of predicate and adjective.
- 69:11/q17/p1 Predicates may be words or clauses; a clause needs a subject reference, which context may leave unspoken. — Al-Jurjānī distinguishes single-word predicates with or without a returning pronoun and four clause forms. [2 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 69:11/q17/p2 Adjectives can restrict, clarify, reinforce, or praise, each a distinct function. — Al-Jurjānī separates generality from ambiguity, distinguishes adjective, predicate and circumstantial attribution, and gives ams al-dābir and 69:13's 'one blast' as reinforcement. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 69:11/q18 What role does poetry study have in interpretation?  [* · interpretation]
- 69:11/q18/p1 It gives access to linguistic wisdom and breadth needed by interpreters. — Al-Jurjānī challenges opponents of poetry study to test their mastery of the disciplines they already accept as necessary. [1 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 69:11/q19 What is said about the Quran's challenge and eloquence?  [* · rhetoric]
- 69:11/q19/p1 The Quran's unmatched speech proved Muhammad's message through the Arabs' highest art. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says listeners could identify poets' styles, yet eloquent Arabs facing the challenge of 2:23 recognized the Quran as neither poetry, soothsaying nor madness; al-Walīd is an example. Some accepted it, while Abū Jahl and others resorted to fighting; eventually no tribe openly remained unbelieving. The miracle suited the age's eloquence, as Moses' suited magic and Jesus' medicine. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 69:11/q20 What senses of qatl are recorded in the ancillary lexical discussion?  [* · meaning]
- 69:11/q20/p1 The root spans killing, enemies, figurative thorough knowledge or mixing, and expressions of amorous yielding or a heart struck by love. — Ibn al-Jawzī distinguishes qatl, qitāl and maqātil; cites a poem using aqtāl for enemies and Imruʾ al-Qays for a heart felled by love; explains 'killing' wine with water as mixing it. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 69:11/q21 What does the ancillary discussion say about derivation and the word kalima?  [* · meaning]
- 69:11/q21/p1 Greater derivation tests root-letter permutations; kalima can also name extended linked speech by part-for-whole metaphor. — Al-Rāzī says triliteral roots have six permutations, though few use all, and larger roots are harder. He groups five k-l-m permutations around force or intensity, with examples of speech striking mind, wound, rough ground, completion, punch, low-water well, and firming dough or power. He says a poem, testimony, or good utterance can be called one word because connected speech forms a unity. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 69:11/q22 What accounts of divine oaths appear in the ancillary discussion?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The ancillary authorities classify divine oaths by object, purpose, and expression.
- 69:11/q22/p1 An oath by created things implies their Creator, while God may swear by what He wills and people only by God. — Ibn Abī al-Iṣbaʿ argues an effect presupposes an agent; al-Ḥasan states the distinction between divine and human oaths. [2 notes: ITQAN(ابن أبي الإصبع,الحسن)]
- 69:11/q22/p2 God's oath by the Prophet's life marks his exceptional rank. — The scholars and Ibn ʿAbbās cite 15:72; Ibn ʿAbbās says no other person's life was sworn by. [2 notes: ITQAN(ابن عباس,العلماء)]
- 69:11/q22/p3 Divine oaths can emphasize excellence or benefit, take different objects, and appear explicitly or implicitly. — Al-Qushayrī gives Sinai and the secure city for excellence and fig and olive for benefit (95:1–3). Another classification lists God's essence, acts and creation (91:5–7; 53:1; 52:1–2). Al-Itqān notes implicit oaths signaled by lām or inferred (3:186; 19:71); Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī distinguishes ambiguous report/oath forms from forms taking an oath response (57:8; 2:63; 58:18; 3:187; 24:53). [4 notes: ITQAN(أبو القاسم القشيري,أبو علي الفارسي)]

## 69:11/q23 How are Quranic verse endings described in the ancillary discussion?  [* · rhetoric]
- 69:11/q23/p1 Their patterned sounds serve meaning and mark pauses while adding rhythmic beauty. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports al-Rummānī's distinction between identical and neighboring final letters, with examples at 20:2, 52:2, 1:3 and 50:1. Al-Rummānī says meaning governs sound. Abū Hilāl al-ʿAskarī says Quranic patterned prose surpasses human sajʿ in meaning, clarity and elegance; most rhetoricians resisted a sharp separation, with 100:1 cited. [3 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(أبو هلال العسكري,الرماني)]

## 69:11/q24 How does the flood's time function in the sentence?  [لَمَّا طَغَا ٱلْمَآءُ · grammar]
- 69:11/q24/p1 The familiar time of Noah's flood supplies the temporal condition for God's carrying the people in the vessel. — Ibn ʿĀshūr treats the well-known flood as the time indicated by lammā. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
