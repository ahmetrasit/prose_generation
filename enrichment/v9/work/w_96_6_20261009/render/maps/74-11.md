# 74:11: 13 questions

## 74:11/q01 What does the command 'Leave Me' mean?  [ذَرْنِى · interpretation]
- 74:11/q01/p1 The Prophet should entrust the man to God, who will deal with and punish him Himself. — Muqatil and those citing him say God will destroy al-Walid alone, between Himself and the man. Tabari, Tabrisī, Ibn ʿAtiyya, Biqāʿī and others take it as a threat and consolation to the troubled Prophet. Rummānī says the literal image is ceasing intercession and leaving punishment to God; its metaphor magnifies a threat that no one can restrain. Tabrisī calls it an idiom, 'leave me and him'; Burhān illustrates the idiom by leaving a she-camel with her foal. Zajjāj, Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Damghānī and Samīn refer to the parallel at 73:11. Asad infers that humans must leave punishment of deniers to God. Qurʾan Yolu says the Prophet need not be involved. Samarrāʾī says the singular form can suit an individual punishment. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; RUMMANI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 74:11/q02 How is 'and the one whom I created' construed?  [وَمَنْ خَلَقْتُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether waw coordinates objects or gives the idiomatic sense 'leave the man to Me'; the relative clause also contains an understood object.
- 74:11/q02/p1 The waw makes the person a comitative object: leave Me with the one I created. — Naḥḥās and Nasafī allow the comitative accusative. Burhān insists that the idiom means leaving the person to God, comparing 'I left the she-camel with her foal'; Samīn refers to the two analyses of the corresponding expression at 73:11. [6 notes: NAHHAS; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(أبو البقاء)+]
- 74:11/q02/p2 The person is coordinated with the object pronoun of 'leave Me.' — Naḥḥās and Nasafī permit coordination; Abū al-Baqāʾ allows it. Burhān objects that this would order the Prophet to leave both God and the denier, obscuring the intended 'leave him to Me' sense. [3 notes: NAHHAS; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(أبو البقاء)-]
- 74:11/q02/p3 The relative clause has an omitted object: 'the one whom I created.' — Wāḥidī supplies the object of khalaqtu; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the relative clause also folds the man's ingratitude into the warning. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:11/q03 Who is 'the one whom I created'?  [مَنْ خَلَقْتُ · referent]
turns on: Whether reports naming al-Walīd specify the sole occasion and target, or name an instance of a general description.
- 74:11/q03/p1 Al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra al-Makhzūmī is the man meant. — Muqātil calls him uniquely prominent among his people. Ibn ʿAbbās also links him to 15:92; Ibn Zayd extends the subject through 74:26. Mujāhid says all humans begin alone though this concerns him. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr names him ibn Hishām and counts thirteen sons; Zajjāj and al-Qummī give ten. Māwardī and Qurṭubī say ingratitude and hostility singled him out. Ibn ʿAtiyya and Rāzī report exegetical agreement; Ibn ʿAtiyya doubts proof of the nickname report. Ibn ʿĀshūr names his son Khālid. The transmitted identification is repeated across the reports and later commentaries. [42 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(عكرمة); IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير,مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(المفسرين); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(قيل); DURR-FULL(أبو مالك,ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير,قتادة); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(تفسير القمي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); STUDYQURAN]
- 74:11/q03/p2 The wording addresses the general type of wealthy, arrogant denier, with al-Walīd as one application. — Iṣlāḥī says Meccan and Ṭāʾif leaders mistook wealth for divine favor; early 'revealed about' reports can mark an application rather than the occasion. The word for the person can have singular or plural antecedents. Elmalılı says a specific occasion leaves the warning generally applicable by the described traits. Mujāhid and Māwardī note that everyone starts in the same solitary state. [7 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 74:11/q04 What does 'alone' describe and mean here?  [وَحِيدًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: The grammatical attachment of the circumstantial accusative to God, the created person, or the speaker's pronoun; whether an epithet is recalled, mocked, or understood as blame.
- 74:11/q04/p1 The man was created alone, initially without wealth, children, or support. — Ibn Qutayba, Mujāhid and many others specify no wealth or sons; Wāḥidī's majority also says no wife. Tabarī, Thaʿlabī, Bagha wī and others put him in his mother's womb. Qushayrī says without a supporter; Iṣlāḥī adds status, companions and confidants; Biqāʿī says from nothing, through stages, with no property. Grammatically waḥīdan is the circumstantial accusative of the created man, the relative man, or its omitted object, as Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, Nasafī, Samīn and Ibn ʿĀshūr explain; Naḥḥās and Zajjāj identify a circumstantial accusative. Abū Ḥayyān calls this the apparent parse; Ibn ʿAtiyya prefers it if the nickname is unproved. Kashshāf, Rāzī, Elmalılı and Iṣlāḥī compare the solitary first creation at 6:94. God later gave him wealth and sons, intensifying his ingratitude; Muqātil, Qatāda, Māwardī, Kashshāf, Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr and others make that contrast. [50 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أكثر المفسرين وأهل المعاني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 74:11/q04/p2 God alone created the man, without any partner in creation. — Zajjāj gives this as the other referent of the circumstantial accusative. Wāḥidī attributes the construction to al-Farrāʾ, al-Kisāʾī and Zajjāj: God knows the man and has power over him. Kashshāf, Rāzī, Samīn, Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī add that the sole Creator can own, avenge or destroy him without help. Biqāʿī draws a contrast with the man's shirk. Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this parse, because God alone created and managed the man and can be entrusted with him. Elmalılı and Asad emphasize the Creator's uniqueness. [22 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والكسائي والزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI+]
- 74:11/q04/p3 God is alone with the man to avenge and punish him. — Waḥīdan is circumstantial from the first-person object of dharnī, as Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī and Samīn explain. Mujāhid, cited by Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī, says God suffices as the man's avenger; Kashshāf says no other avenger is needed. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this parse without preferring it. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI]
- 74:11/q04/p4 It alludes, often derisively, to al-Walīd's epithet 'the Unique One' or his boast of being without equal. — Ibn ʿAbbās says he was called al-Waḥīd; ʿAṭāʾ reports his boast 'unique son of the unique,' unequaled among Arabs. Reports explain the title by unmatched wealth, sons, rank and leadership; Ibn ʿĀshūr says he was older than Abū Jahl and Abū Sufyān. Al-Qummī instead says he claimed sole responsibility for one year's Kaʿba covering while Quraysh shared the next. Kashshāf says the title may postdate the verse; otherwise the verse mocks worldly distinction by recalling destitution. Rāzī and Samīn parse it as an accusative of disparagement, with Ṭabāṭabāʾī reporting an implied 'I condemn.' Ibn ʿĀshūr says the wording shifts from his boast to dependence at birth, citing 16:78. Wāḥidī rejects the nickname as the intended sense because God would appear to affirm a false boast; Ibn ʿAtiyya finds its report unproved. Rāzī answers that a proper name need not assert its quality, or means unique in his own estimation, citing 44:49; Qurṭubī likewise says a boast can be repeated without endorsement. [27 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(المفسرين); TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(تفسير القمي); STUDYQURAN(Bg); WAHIDI-BASIT-; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 74:11/q04/p5 It disparages him as uniquely wicked, lowly, or insignificant. — Rāzī says uniqueness need not denote rank: it can mark unbelief, vileness and lowliness. Bayḍāwī, Samīn, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursawī and Ālūsī similarly turn the boast into blame. Abū Ḥayyān cites the proper-name response to an objection over insult; Bursawī cites 44:49. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 74:11/q04/p6 It disparages him as having unknown paternity or being born outside marriage. — Abū Saʿīd al-Ḍarīr takes waḥīdan as a second object of 'created' and relates the insult to 68:13. Qurṭubī and Elmalılı mention the same link. Abū Jaʿfar, through al-ʿAyyāshī, says claiming to be 'son of the unique' would shame someone without a known father. Reports from Abū ʿAbd Allāh and Abū Jaʿfar gloss al-waḥīd as a child born outside marriage. Bayḍāwī, Bursawī and Ālūsī also report this sense, the last describing uncertain lineage or adoption. Abū Ḥayyān raises the difficulty of using it as an insult and replies with the proper-name or uniquely evil readings. [10 notes: TABRISI(أبو جعفر); RAZI-FULL(أبو سعيد الضرير); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر,أبو عبد الله وأبو جعفر)]
- 74:11/q04/p7 His solitary beginning also points to his solitary return at resurrection. — Māwardī and Qurṭubī say he will be raised alone as he was created. Elmalılı treats this as an individual phase of resurrection and cites 6:94. Asad and Study Quran add 6:94 and 19:80, 19:95 for life's solitary end. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]

## 74:11/q05 What report explains the revelation about al-Walīd?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ over whether the decisive setting was his private hearing followed by Abū Jahl's pressure or a Quraysh council preparing a public pilgrimage charge; recited passages and details also vary.
- 74:11/q05/p1 He was impressed by a recitation, but Abū Jahl pressed him into calling it transmitted magic. — ʿIkrima and Ibn ʿAbbās say he softened and praised Qurʾan as sweet, radiant, fruitful, elevated, powerful and unlike poetry or human and jinn speech. Abū Jahl used Quraysh's fear that his conversion would attract the tribe, alleging he sought food or money from the Prophet or Abū Bakr. Al-Walīd rejected the allegation, reflected, then said it was inherited magic. Muqātil says the Prophet had recited Ghāfir 40:1–3 in the Sacred Mosque and repeated it when al-Walīd listened: God is sovereign and all-knowing, forgives repentant associators, severely punishes unrepentant shirk, is independent of unbelievers, affirms unity and receives all creatures in the hereafter. Al-Qummī names 41:13 instead. Al-Ḥākim authenticated the Ibn ʿAbbās report, also transmitted as ʿIkrima's mursal report; Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites al-Bayhaqī too. Durr preserves a variant in which al-Walīd said he would avoid Abū Bakr, ʿUmar and Muhammad. Muqātil says the accusation distressed the Prophet, whom 51:52 consoles by recalling earlier prophets accused of sorcery or madness. [17 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(عكرمة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الوليد,عكرمة عن ابن عباس); TABATABAI(ابن عباس,تفسير القمي); STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAbbās)]
- 74:11/q05/p2 At pilgrimage season, Quraysh sought one charge against Muhammad; al-Walīd rejected others and chose 'sorcerer' because the message divided families. — Ibn Hishām's Ibn Isḥāq report has Quraysh request a single story; al-Walīd rejects soothsayer, madman, poet and sorcerer as accurate, yet settles on sorcerer for the family-division allegation. Muqātil says he also rejected 'liar' because Muhammad was known as trustworthy, then pondered, grimaced (basara), changed color, and said magic. Muqātil also records his allegation that the magic came from Musaylima through Yāsar Abū Fukayha. Jishumī and Ibn ʿĀshūr give the council account; Mujāhid places it at Dār al-Nadwa, says Quraysh spread the verdict, and connects revelation through 74:25. Ibn Isḥāq calls him a contentious adversary. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ sets such claims among repeated Meccan allegations of human fabrication, borrowed ancient lore and dictated prior writings, citing 68:10–15, 68:44–47, 25:4–10, 17:87–96, 6:7–9, 6:25–26 and 34:43–45. [10 notes: IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق,الوليد بن المغيرة); MUQATIL; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن إسحاق); BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 74:11/q06 How much of the passage was revealed about al-Walīd, and when?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The extent assigned to the reported occasion and the chronology of al-Walīd's statement relative to the sūra's opening.
- 74:11/q06/p1 The verses from 74:11 through the threat of Saqar at 74:26 concern him. — Ibn Zayd specifies the passage beginning here and ending at the threat of being cast into Saqar. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد)]
- 74:11/q06/p2 The revelation prompted by Quraysh's charge runs through 74:25. — Mujāhid's Dār al-Nadwa account says Quraysh spread the sorcery charge and the sūra was revealed through 74:25. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد)]
- 74:11/q06/p3 This verse and the following nineteen verses were revealed about him. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports abundant accounts for this extent. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 74:11/q06/p4 Al-Walīd's later conduct means the whole sūra cannot have come at the very beginning of the mission. — Ālūsī draws this chronological consequence from identifying his later conduct as the occasion. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 74:11/q06/p5 The likely opening chronology places the pilgrimage deliberation after a forty-day hiatus, near the end of Dhū al-Qiʿda. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says revelation began in Ramadan and this was the strongest chronology for the later Quraysh preparation. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:11/q07 What does the verse teach through the man's solitary creation and later blessings?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes develop complementary lessons: the duty of gratitude and the threat against arrogant denial.
- 74:11/q07/p1 God's gifts require gratitude; al-Walīd's pride and denial make his ingratitude especially grave. — Muqātil and Qatāda contrast the empty beginning with wealth and sons. Kashshāf says he repaid gifts with disbelief, shirk and religious mockery; Ibn Kathīr notes denial of signs and a claim that they were human speech. Qurʾan Yolu grounds gratitude in reason and justice, especially for life and material and spiritual gifts, and condemns its conversion to denial, polytheism and injustice. Iṣlāḥī criticizes the belief that wealth and rank prove divine favor or guarantee more in the Hereafter; 6:94 evokes departure without them. Study Quran and Asad stress human dependence on the Creator under either reading. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 74:11/q07/p2 The warning promises severe judgment for rejecters who trust their worldly standing. — Iṣlāḥī reads 74:11–17 as warning those expecting further advantage from wealth and rank. Ṭabāṭabāʾī extends the threat to disparagers of the Qurʾan who call it magic and mock its truths. Ibn Kathīr says God threatens the wicked man for ingratitude and false attribution of revelation to human speech. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 74:11/q08 Why use this wording and single out the man?  [* · rhetoric]
- 74:11/q08/p1 Naming God as his Creator exposes his ingratitude and contrasts his original helplessness with later wealth and pride. — Māwardī and Qurṭubī say his hostility to the Messenger singled him out although everyone begins similarly. Kashshāf and Ibn ʿĀshūr show how the relative clause and 'alone' recall God's favor; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 16:78 for birth without knowledge. Samarrāʾī says singular wording can also serve an individual punishment. Ibn ʿĀshūr defines waḥīd from waḥada as being alone in place or condition or distinguished in fame or story, with context determining the sense. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 74:11/q09 How does this verse connect with surrounding verses and the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the al-Walīd statement occasions the sūra's opening or follows it determines the immediate link to 74:7.
- 74:11/q09/p1 It consoles the Prophet and continues the call to patience while God undertakes judgment. — Biqāʿī connects it to the preceding warning of judgment and the Prophet's grief over arrogant opponents. Ibn ʿĀshūr says al-Walīd's fabrication troubled him, so 'leave Me' clarifies 74:7; if the statement occasioned the sūra, intervening verses are parenthetical, whereas a later statement makes this a new section. He compares the threat to 68:44. Muqātil cites 51:52 to console the Prophet with earlier messengers accused of sorcery or madness. Damghānī and Iṣlāḥī compare 73:11, where prosperous deniers are left to God for a little respite. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; MUQATIL; DAMGHANI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:11/q09/p2 Al-Muddaththir parallels the preceding al-Muzzammil in theme and title. — Iṣlāḥī calls them counterparts with essentially the same central themes and names sharing a meaning. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:11/q10 Was the command abrogated by the sword verse?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The status of an exegetical claim of abrogation; Ibn al-Jawzī gives no further proof here.
- 74:11/q10/p1 Some exegetes said that it was abrogated. — Ibn al-Jawzī records their sword-verse claim but calls it unsound. [0 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(بعض المفسرين)-]
- 74:11/q10/p2 The abrogation claim is unsound. — Ibn al-Jawzī rejects the reported claim. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(بعض المفسرين)+]

## 74:11/q11 What inward reading is given to the solitary man?  [* · inward]
- 74:11/q11/p1 Al-Walīd figures the solitary evil soul, whose worldly following and traits are overcome by spiritual light. — The Najmī allusion reported by Bursawī makes wealth, bad traits, followers and prestige symbols of sins and dark natural powers. It makes Abū Bakr the hidden, ʿUmar the spirit, ʿUthmān the secret and ʿAlī the heart, whose light erases the self's darkness, defeats its works and following, and ends its dominion. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 74:11/q12 Does calling al-Walīd 'unique' affirm his boast?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether an epithet asserts a true quality or can function as a name, quotation, or ironic condemnation.
- 74:11/q12/p1 Taking it as praise would wrongly affirm his claim of unequaled status. — Wāḥidī rejects that meaning on this ground; Rāzī reports that many later scholars make the same objection. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(كثير من المتأخرين); WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 74:11/q12/p2 The verse can invoke his title without endorsing the boast. — Rāzī replies that a proper name need not assert the quality, that uniqueness may be only in al-Walīd's belief, or that he is unique in evil and lowliness; he cites 44:49. Qurṭubī likewise says God can repeat a boast without endorsing it. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT-; RAZI-FULL(كثير من المتأخرين)-]

## 74:11/q13 What specific wealth is reported for al-Walīd?  [* · reports]
- 74:11/q13/p1 He had ten slaves, each trading with a thousand dinars. — Al-Qummī's report says this of al-Walīd; it adds that a qinṭār could mean a bullhide filled with gold. [1 notes: TABATABAI(تفسير القمي)]
