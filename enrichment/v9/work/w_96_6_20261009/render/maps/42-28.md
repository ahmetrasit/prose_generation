# 42:28: 37 questions

## 42:28/q01 Who sends rain, and what does that imply about its cause?  [وَهُوَ يُنَزِّلُ ٱلْغَيْثَ · theology]
- 42:28/q01/p1 God alone sends rain by choice and controls provision. — Al-Biqāʿī rejects attribution to time's habits. Ibn ʿĀshūr reads the restriction against attribution to stars and cites Zayd ibn Khālid's report: the Prophet calls attribution to God's grace faith and attribution to a star's phase disbelief. Iṣlāḥī denies idols or human means control livelihood; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says blessings prove God's unity and sole right to worship. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(زيد بن خالد الجهني عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 42:28/q02 How is يُنَزِّلُ read?  [يُنَزِّلُ · readings]
turns on: Doubling or lightening zāy; the sources vary in their enumeration of readers.
- 42:28/q02/p1 With doubled zāy, Form II. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it the majority reading; al-Rāzī and al-Bayḍāwī name Nāfiʿ, Ibn ʿĀmir, ʿĀṣim; al-Nasafī names Medinans, Syrians, ʿĀṣim; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds Abū Jaʿfar and fatḥa on nūn. Qirāʾāt-ER says it suggests repeated, intensified, or staged descent. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); RAZI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر وعاصم); QURTUBI-FULL(الباقون); BAYDAWI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر وعاصم); NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وابن عامر وعاصم وأبو جعفر); QIRAAT-ER]
- 42:28/q02/p2 With light zāy, Form IV. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya names Ibn Waththāb and al-Aʿmash, also Abū ʿAmr, recording Abū Ḥātim's preference. Al-Qurṭubī adds Ibn Kathīr, Ibn Muḥayṣin, Ḥumayd, Mujāhid, Yaʿqūb, al-Kisāʾī. Al-Ālūsī calls it majority. Ibn al-Jazarī puts Ibn Kathīr and Basrans in the general imperfect class and joins Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, Khalaf here and in 31:34, noting other exceptions. Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies nūn sukūn; Qirāʾāt-ER says causation has less overt staging force. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن وثاب والأعمش); RAZI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر وعاصم); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن كثير، ابن محيصن، حميد، مجاهد، أبو عمرو، يعقوب، ابن وثاب، الأعمش، الكسائي); NASHR; ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور); IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وابن عامر وعاصم وأبو جعفر); QIRAAT-ER; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حاتم)+]

- 42:28/q03/p1 Rain is sent repeatedly and anew. — Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts it with the past tense of their already enduring despair. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 42:28/q04 Why use an unseen third-person pronoun?  [وَهُوَ · rhetoric]
- 42:28/q04/p1 Sending rain belongs to the keys of the unseen. — Al-Biqāʿī explains the pronoun by rain's unseen sending. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 42:28/q05 What does ٱلْغَيْثَ mean?  [ٱلْغَيْثَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term is general rain, rain that brings succor, or an extended usage.
- 42:28/q05/p1 Beneficial rain that relieves need or drought, especially in its proper season. — Al-Māwardī and al-Ṭabrisī distinguish maṭar, which may help or harm in or out of season; al-Qurṭubī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī relay this. Al-Bayḍāwī, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr derive the word from succor; the last cites 12:49. Ibn al-Jawzī says rain comes when needed. Al-Biqāʿī compares rain's outward relief with revelation's inward and outward relief. Al-Rāghib distinguishes ghayth, rain, from ghawth, aid. Sāmarrāʾī contrasts good ghayth with punitive amṭara, citing 12:49 and punitive rain verses; Sufyān ibn ʿUyayna says Qurʾanic maṭar denotes punishment. Al-Aṣmaʿī reports a woman's phrase for rain as wished; Dhū al-Rumma praises its eloquence. [18 notes: THALABI(الأصمعي); MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(الأصمعي,الماوردي,ذو الرمة); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عيينة); TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 42:28/q05/p2 Rain generally. — Al-Baghawī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī gloss it simply as rain. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 42:28/q05/p3 Clouds and vegetation may also be called ghayth. — Al-Qurṭubī records these extended usages. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 42:28/q06 What does قَنَطُوا۟ mean?  [قَنَطُوا۟ · meaning]
- 42:28/q06/p1 They despaired or gave up hope of rain. — Mujāhid, Qatāda, al-Jishumī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and others gloss qunūṭ as despair; al-Bayḍāwī specifies rain, Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 15:55. [9 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); JISHUMI; BAYDAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 42:28/q07 How is قَنَطُوا۟ read?  [قَنَطُوا۟ · readings]
turns on: The nūn vowel; both readings mean despair.
- 42:28/q07/p1 With fatḥa on the nūn. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Samīn call this common or majority; al-Samīn compares the canonical open vowel in 39:53. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 42:28/q07/p2 With kasra on the nūn. — Al-Zajjāj says both qaniṭa yaqniṭu and qanaṭa yaqnaṭu mean despair. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī name Ibn/Yaḥyā ibn Waththāb and al-Aʿmash; both are Arabic variants, though al-Samīn calls past-tense kasra rare. Al-Jishumī notes two patterns in past and present. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(يحيى بن وثاب والأعمش); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن وثاب، الأعمش); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الأعمش وابن وثاب)]

## 42:28/q08 How does مِنۢ بَعْدِ مَا قَنَطُوا۟ work grammatically?  [مِنۢ بَعْدِ مَا قَنَطُوا۟ · grammar]

## 42:28/q09 Who are 'they' in قَنَطُوا۟?  [قَنَطُوا۟ · referent]
- 42:28/q09/p1 God's servants mentioned in verse 25. — Ibn ʿĀshūr traces the pronoun to 42:25. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 42:28/q10 When and why had people despaired of rain?  [مِنۢ بَعْدِ مَا قَنَطُوا۟ · interpretation]
- 42:28/q10/p1 Drought and apparent failure of rain had exhausted hope. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Qushayrī, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn Kathīr, and Study Quran describe relief at need; Ibn Kathīr cites 30:49 and Study Quran 41:39. Ibn ʿĀshūr says rain signs had vanished and planting season was ending; the past tense implies duration. Muqātil applies this to a seven-year Meccan drought. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 42:28/q11 Why mention rain after despair?  [مِنۢ بَعْدِ مَا قَنَطُوا۟ · rhetoric]
- 42:28/q11/p1 Relief after hopelessness makes the blessing vivid and prompts fuller joy, gratitude, and recognition of the giver. — Al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī, Bursevī, and al-Ālūsī make this point; al-Rāzī ties hardship to greater joy. Al-Baghawī urges gratitude. Al-Biqāʿī adds that despair reveals no one else can send rain. Bursevī and al-Ālūsī note rain also falls at other times. [7 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 42:28/q12 What did ʿUmar say when told of drought and despair?  [مِنۢ بَعْدِ مَا قَنَطُوا۟ · reports]
- 42:28/q12/p1 He expected or declared imminent rain and invoked this verse. — Versions say 'they will receive rain,' 'they have been rained upon,' or 'you have been rained upon'; Qatāda has a man report drought and ʿUmar recite the verse, sometimes with 'God willing.' Ibn ʿAṭiyya treats this as relief amid hardship. [12 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(عمر بن الخطاب); TAB-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); ZAJJAJ(عمر); THALABI(قتادة عن سعيد); MAWARDI-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); RAZI-FULL(عمر); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); NASAFI-FULL(عمر); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(عمر)]

## 42:28/q13 What occasion of revelation is reported for this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: Mecca's seven-year drought or the Medinan Friday rain appeal; versions differ on the petitioner.
- 42:28/q13/p1 Rain was withheld from Mecca or Quraysh for seven years, then relief came. — Muqātil and transmitters give the Meccan drought. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports the Prophet's prayer lifted it after carrion and bones were eaten, with 44:15 an allusion; the Ibn Masʿūd account says the Prophet prayed for Joseph-like years after rejection and Abū Sufyān sought relief, while another Ṣaḥīḥ version records the prayer against Quraysh. Ibn ʿĀshūr infers a beginning around year eight, extending perhaps into nine. [7 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNASHUR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود,قيل,مقاتل)]
- 42:28/q13/p2 The verse was revealed at the Prophet's Friday rain appeal prompted by a Bedouin. — Al-Qushayrī gives the Bedouin report. Ibn ʿĀshūr locates it in Medina during a Friday sermon; another version names the petitioner Kaʿb ibn Murra. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري); IBNASHUR-FULL(كعب بن مرة)]

## 42:28/q14 What does يَنشُرُ mean?  [وَيَنشُرُ · meaning]
- 42:28/q14/p1 To spread, scatter, expand, or extend. — Al-Jishumī contrasts nashr with folding, cites dispersed people and explains spread provision. Bursevī says spread or scatter. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the sense metaphorical expansion, citing 17:13. ʿĀʾisha's report of Abū Bakr restoring Islam's dispersed unity illustrates the usage. [4 notes: JISHUMI(عائشة); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 42:28/q15 What is the mercy God spreads?  [رَحْمَتَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Rain itself, its effects, all-encompassing mercy, or sun after rain.
- 42:28/q15/p1 The benefits of rain, including fertility, plants, fruit, and blessings across land and living creatures. — Al-Ṭabrisī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī name plants and fruit; al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī fertility; al-Bayḍāwī, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī plains, mountains, plants, animals. Al-Biqāʿī adds pools, rivers, trees, grain and fruit. Ibn Kathīr describes region and inhabitants; al-Jishumī says each person's benefit in each land. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it mercy through water; al-Māwardī notes general and particular reach. Abū Ḥayyān puts rain's benefits within broader mercy. [14 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 42:28/q15/p2 The rain itself, repeating and reinforcing the blessing. — Al-Thaʿlabī cites 30:48, al-Baghawī 7:57; al-Dāmghānī groups 7:57, 25:48, 30:50, 30:36, 30:46 as rain usages. Ibn ʿAṭiyya explicitly calls this reinforcement. Al-Ālūsī finds the repeated blessing far-fetched. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); ALUSI-FULL(السدي)-]
- 42:28/q15/p3 God's broad mercy in all things, beyond this rain. — Al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī give this alternative; Abū Ḥayyān says mercy is broader than rain; al-Ālūsī makes rain's effects one primary instance. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 42:28/q15/p4 Sunlight after rain, a separate welcome blessing. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya's group, Abū Sulaymān, al-Mahdawī, and Fatḥ al-Raḥmān say long rain becomes tiresome and sun welcome. Ibn al-Jawzī calls sun a seventeenth sense of raḥma. Al-Ālūsī rejects it as unsound. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعضهم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي); QURTUBI-FULL(المهدوي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(فتح الرحمن); IBNASHUR-FULL(المهدوي,قيل); ALUSI-FULL(المهدوي)-]

## 42:28/q16 Whose mercy is meant by the pronoun in رَحْمَتَهُۥ?  [رَحْمَتَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether the antecedent is God or, with one interpretation, rain.
- 42:28/q16/p1 God's mercy. — Al-Ālūsī states this primary antecedent. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 42:28/q16/p2 The rain's mercy or benefits. — Al-Ālūsī allows this antecedent under his rain-effects interpretation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 42:28/q17 What is conveyed by describing mercy as spread?  [وَيَنشُرُ رَحْمَتَهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 42:28/q17/p1 The breadth and distribution of divine benefit. — Al-Jishumī says it shows complete power and subtle provision in each land; al-Biqāʿī extends its reach to water, landforms, and growth. [2 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 42:28/q18 What does ٱلْوَلِىُّ mean here?  [ٱلْوَلِىُّ · meaning]
turns on: General care, ownership, special alliance, rain control, or spring rain itself.
- 42:28/q18/p1 The caring protector or benefactor who manages servants through grace. — Al-Ṭabarī stresses beneficence; al-Ṭabrisī management and interests; al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī gracious care. Ibn Kathīr extends direction to this life and next. Ibn ʿAṭiyya contrasts beneficial protection with powerless idols. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls an ally one who does good to those under care; al-Biqāʿī says God is nearest in every matter. [14 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 42:28/q18/p2 The owner or master. — Al-Māwardī lists this; al-Ṭabrisī includes ownership within guardianship; Bursevī calls Him master. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; BURSEVI]
- 42:28/q18/p3 The ally, patron, or friend of believers and obedient servants. — Muqātil and al-Baghawī specify believers or obedient; al-Qurṭubī says He supports His friends; al-Kāshifī says He repairs believers' affairs by rain and kindness. [4 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 42:28/q18/p4 The controller who repeatedly sends rain. — Bursevī reports that wise rain reveals need, strengthens resolve, and answers distress. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
- 42:28/q18/p5 Spring rain itself is called walī. — Al-Burhān reports a nondivine reading of both names. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 42:28/q19 What does ٱلْحَمِيدُ mean here?  [ٱلْحَمِيدُ · meaning]
turns on: Worthy of praise, actually praised, praising obedience, or spring rain itself.
- 42:28/q19/p1 God deserves praise for His acts, favors, and mercy. — Al-Māwardī gives 'deserving praise'; al-Ṭabrisī says all acts are kindness and benefit. Bursevī says He alone deserves praise, al-Biqāʿī every kind, Ibn ʿĀshūr connects it to deliverance. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the names praise of beautiful action; Iṣlāḥī says rain displays His worthiness of praise and gratitude; Ibn Kathīr calls every outcome of decree praiseworthy. [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 42:28/q19/p2 God is praised by creation, the obedient, or every tongue for His favors. — Muqātil singles out rain; al-Ṭabarī names favors; al-Baghawī creatures; al-Rāzī forms of mercy; al-Nasafī obedient servants; al-Qurṭubī every tongue. Al-Māwardī pairs 'praised' with benefactor. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 42:28/q19/p3 God praises obedient servants. — Al-Burhān offers this divine-name construal. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 42:28/q19/p4 Spring rain itself is praiseworthy. — Al-Burhān reports that both closing descriptions may refer to spring rain. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 42:28/q20 What is the form and voice of ٱلْحَمِيدُ?  [ٱلْحَمِيدُ · grammar]
- 42:28/q20/p1 The intensive faʿīl form with passive mafʿūl sense. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives this morphology. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 42:28/q21 Why end with ٱلْوَلِىُّ ٱلْحَمِيدُ?  [ٱلْوَلِىُّ ٱلْحَمِيدُ · rhetoric]
- 42:28/q21/p1 The names fit deliverance: God cares for His charges and His gift merits praise. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the pairing; al-Bayḍāwī connects care and mercy with praise; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it praise of beautiful action. Al-Biqāʿī connects patronage in 42:9 to rain as evidence of care. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 42:28/q22 What spiritual reading is given to rain and renewed life?  [يُنَزِّلُ ٱلْغَيْثَ وَيَنشُرُ رَحْمَتَهُۥ · inward]
- 42:28/q22/p1 Mercy or inspiration revives hearts and renews repentance, remembrance, knowledge, intimacy, and nearness. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ takes rain as mercy on saints' hearts, establishing repentance, return, watchfulness, care. Al-Qushayrī pictures withered spiritual time, obscured love and renewed inner life; his poem hopes estrangement ends, covenants revive, and a dry branch greens. Study Quran's Aj says inspiration revives hearts through remembrance and knowledge. [4 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); QUSHAYRI; STUDYQURAN(Aj)]

## 42:28/q23 What does the move between hope and despair teach inwardly?  [مِنۢ بَعْدِ مَا قَنَطُوا۟ · inward]
- 42:28/q23/p1 God exposes limitations in hope and comforts servants through His attributes in despair. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ describes this movement and reassurance; Bursevī cites the Mathnawī's calamity, supplication, intercession, and divine relief. [2 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); BURSEVI(المثنوي)]

## 42:28/q24 What is reported about supplication during rain?  [ٱلْغَيْثَ · reports]
- 42:28/q24/p1 It is answered or not rejected. — Thābit reports this and cites the verse; a Prophetic report pairs prayer under rain with prayer at the call to prayer. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(ثابت,رسول الله)]

## 42:28/q25 What reports describe rain's amount, source, and distribution?  [ٱلْغَيْثَ · reports]
turns on: Heavenly route and measured drops versus constant annual total and moral redirection.
- 42:28/q25/p1 Rain is measured and descends from a sea beneath the Throne through heavens, with angels placing drops. — Bursevī relays Ibn ʿAbbās: the sea supplies animals; rain is strained; each drop has an angel, except the unmeasured Flood. [1 notes: BURSEVI(ابن عباس)]
- 42:28/q25/p2 The annual amount is known to angels and unchanged, but sins divert rain to others or to deserts and seas. — Bursevī reports angelic knowledge and a hadith about redirection when some or all people sin. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 42:28/q26 What does rain demonstrate about divine power and life?  [ٱلْغَيْثَ رَحْمَتَهُۥ · interpretation]
- 42:28/q26/p1 Its benefits and growth display God's power, sole divinity, and possibility of resurrection. — Al-Biqāʿī points to soft plants from hard soil and delicate branches from rough trees against resurrection denial. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees a sign of wondrous making and sole divinity; Iṣlāḥī calls it an everyday sign of God's action; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says blessings prove unity. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 42:28/q27 How does God provide amid need or resistance?  [* · theology]
- 42:28/q27/p1 He gives needed sustenance wisely and sustains even those who challenge Him. — Al-Rāzī connects rain with measured provision: excess may harm faith, yet God gives at need. Iṣlāḥī says challengers live on His provision. Study Quran presents God as Provider owning heaven and earth's keys and wisely distributing wealth, offspring, revelation. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 42:28/q28 How does the verse connect to measured provision before it?  [* · links]
- 42:28/q28/p1 Rain exemplifies due-measure provision and grace because livelihood depends on the amount God knows. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links 42:27 and calls rain major sustenance; Asad calls it life-giving grace in measure. Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees a move from provision to signs of unity; al-Rāzī says God gives when needed without harmful excess. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL]

## 42:28/q29 How does the rain image lead to the next creation sign?  [* · links]
- 42:28/q29/p1 Rain sustains food and life, preparing living creatures and further signs in 42:29. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls rain universally acknowledged and foundational for people and animals; Asad connects the next verse with existence, purposeful activity, and life's continuation in the hereafter. Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes further signs of unity. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI]

## 42:28/q30 How is hope, the opposite of despair, used in Arabic and the Qurʾan?  [قَنَطُوا۟ · meaning]
turns on: Ordinary hope or contextual fear and lack of concern.
- 42:28/q30/p1 Rajāʾ normally means hope, including hope for God's mercy. — Ibn Fāris distinguishes long-ā rajāʾ from short rajā, an edge or side, plural arjāʾ, citing 69:17. Exegetes cite 2:218 and 17:57 for hope. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,ابن فارس)]

## 42:28/q31 What makes praise truthful or excellent in poetry?  [ٱلْحَمِيدُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Verified truth and benefit or imaginative range.
- 42:28/q31/p1 Truthful wisdom, counsel, and praise only for actual qualities make the finest poetry. — Al-Jurjānī reports moral distinctions and Zuhayr's truthful praise. He prefers this for sound reasoning, lasting effect, clear benefit, richer yield, and verification. [4 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR; JURJANI-ASRAR+]
- 42:28/q31/p2 Imaginative untruth makes the finest poetry through figurative invention and exaggeration. — Al-Jurjānī reports the opposing view's broader craft in praise, blame, description, though he favors truthful poetry. [1 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR; JURJANI-ASRAR-]

## 42:28/q32 What conventions of Qurʾanic wording and pairing are reported?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Stable diction or paired concepts.
- 42:28/q32/p1 Recurring diction assigns stable senses or reference to certain words and addresses. — Ibn ʿĀshūr urges learning such patterns. Ibn ʿAbbās says Qurʾanic kaʾs means wine, also reported from al-Ḍaḥḥāk, and 'O people' means Meccan polytheists. Sufyān ibn ʿUyayna says Qurʾanic maṭar is punitive whereas ghayth here names rain. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عيينة)]
- 42:28/q32/p2 The Qurʾan recurrently pairs related or opposed concepts. — Al-Jāḥiẓ lists prayer/almsgiving, hunger/fear, paradise/hell, hope/dread, emigrants/helpers, jinn/humans. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds benefit/harm and heaven/earth. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الجاحظ)]

## 42:28/q33 Which passages of this sūra are reported Meccan or Medinan, and when was it revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Passage-level places and a tentative chronology from drought and Anṣār references.
- 42:28/q33/p1 The sūra is otherwise Meccan, with 42:23–24 reportedly about the Anṣār and 42:39–41 reportedly Medinan. — Ibn ʿĀshūr attributes both exceptions to Muqātil and says the first belongs among Ibn ʿAbbās's four cited verses. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(مقاتل)]
- 42:28/q33/p2 Revelation may have begun around year eight and continued into year nine. — Ibn ʿĀshūr infers this from Muqātil's Meccan drought occasion for verse 28; he also suggests a post-ʿAqaba continuation since the Anṣār might be intended in 42:38 before Hijra. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(مقاتل)]

## 42:28/q34 Where is this sūra placed in a reported revelation order?  [* · sciences]
- 42:28/q34/p1 Sixty-ninth, after al-Kahf and before Ibrāhīm. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives al-Jaʿbarī's order from Jābir ibn Zayd. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الجعبري عن جابر بن زيد)]

## 42:28/q35 How many verses are counted in this sūra?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Regional verse-counting traditions.
- 42:28/q35/p1 Fifty. — Ibn ʿĀshūr attributes this to Medinan, Meccan, Syrian, and Basran counts. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 42:28/q35/p2 Fifty-three. — Ibn ʿĀshūr attributes this to the Kufan count. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 42:28/q36 What broader sūra themes frame this verse?  [* · links]
- 42:28/q36/p1 The sūra affirms revelation, divine rule and unity, provision, and judgment against idolatry. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says its disjointed-letter opening challenges deniers to match the Qurʾan; Muhammad's revelation resembles earlier revelation and warns Mecca; God owns heaven and earth, chooses human messengers, legislates. Idolaters have only imitation; the sūra warns of Judgment, defends the Prophet from fabrication charges, and points to navigation and provision as signs. Study Quran describes wise distribution of wealth, offspring, revelation. [9 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 42:28/q37 Where are the closing names explained elsewhere?  [ٱلْوَلِىُّ ٱلْحَمِيدُ · links]
- 42:28/q37/p1 Al-Walī at 4:45 and al-Ḥamīd at 2:267. — Ibn al-Jawzī directs readers to his earlier explanations without restating them here. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
