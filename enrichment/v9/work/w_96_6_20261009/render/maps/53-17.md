# 53:17: 18 questions

## 53:17/q01 What does zāgha mean here?  [زَاغَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether zaygh names a change of direction, loss or deficiency of vision, or error in perception.
- 53:17/q01/p1 To deviate or turn aside from the intended course. — Majāz and Ibn Qutayba give the basic sense; Mwardī reports deviation; Bursevī specifies deviation from uprightness; Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies the intended course; Sāmarrāʾī says either side. Jishumī applies it to turning from sought truth; Ṭabāṭabāʾī gives deviation from uprightness as the root sense. [8 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 53:17/q01/p2 For the sight to go away. — Māwardī attributes this explanation to Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 53:17/q01/p3 Deficiency or falling short. — Māwardī attributes this explanation to Ibn Baḥr, who then denies deficient comprehension in the Prophet's vision. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر)]
- 53:17/q01/p4 In the case of sight, to perceive inaccurately. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes the visual application from the general sense of deviation; Rāzī also denies seeing things contrary to reality. [2 notes: TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL]

## 53:17/q02 What does ṭaghā mean here?  [طَغَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the excess is of a limit, of truth, of status over another, or of visual perception.
- 53:17/q02/p1 To increase or cross a limit. — Ibn Qutayba says increase or exceed; Ibn Baḥr says increase; Ibn ʿĀshūr and Sāmarrāʾī specify crossing the limit or looking beyond what is allowed; Ṭabāṭabāʾī gives exceeding a limit as the basic sense. [5 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 53:17/q02/p2 To rise above or go beyond the truth. — Māwardī gives the first wording and transmits the second from Ibn ʿAbbās. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 53:17/q02/p3 To seek unjust elevation over another. — Jishumī compares ṭāghī with bāghī. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 53:17/q02/p4 For sight, to perceive something unreal. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī applies the sense of excess to vision; Rāzī similarly denies imagining a nonexistent thing as real. [2 notes: TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL]

## 53:17/q03 Whose sight is al-baṣar?  [ٱلْبَصَرُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the definite article singles out the Prophet's eye or gives the negated noun generic scope.
- 53:17/q03/p1 Muhammad's sight. — Rāzī reads the article as identifying his sight; Ṭabāṭabāʾī states the referent explicitly. The Ibn ʿAbbās reports and the common explanations likewise attach the gaze to the Prophet; Ibn ʿĀshūr says he saw Gabriel. [15 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس عن النبي,عطاء عن ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 53:17/q03/p2 Sight generically: no eye veered under the awe. — Rāzī reports the alternative and observes that a definite noun under negation can have generic scope, citing 6:103. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:17/q04 What did the gaze not turn away from?  [مَا زَاغَ ٱلْبَصَرُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the point is lateral motion, attention to the shown object, exclusive attention to God or truth, or fidelity of perception.
- 53:17/q04/p1 It did not wander right or left from what was before it. — Farrāʾ, Naḥḥās, Ibn ʿAbbās as reported by Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Kathīr and Durr describe lateral movement; Wāḥidī calls it a sideways glance. ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās adds that God enabled him to look without fear or panic; Elmalılı and Kur'an Yolu stress the astonishing scene. [17 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس عن النبي,عطاء عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 53:17/q04/p2 It did not turn from the particular object, signs, or wonders shown to him. — Zajjāj identifies Gabriel filling the horizon. Qushayrī, Wāḥidī, Ṭabrisī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān transmitting Zamakhsharī, Bursevī, Ālūsī and Study Quran refer to the permitted or commanded signs; Ibn ʿĀshūr speaks of Gabriel. Rāzī says moths or locusts at the Sidra did not divert him; on the lights reading, he did not turn from those lights. [21 notes: ZAJJAJ; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 53:17/q04/p3 It did not turn from God toward anything else, even himself. — Tustarī says absorption in the Lord and His manifest attributes excluded self-witnessing; Qāshānī excludes another object and selfhood. Rāzī's moths reading excludes anything besides God; al-Kāshifī speaks of everlasting divine beauty, al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya of the divine names and attributes instead of worldly or otherworldly attractions, and al-Baqlī of refusing even a noble distraction in the second vision. [7 notes: TUSTARI; QASHANI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(البقلي,التأويلات النجمية,الكاشفي)]
- 53:17/q04/p4 It did not turn from the truth sought. — Jishumī defines zaygh as turning aside from sought truth; Ṭūsī, reported by Study Quran, applies the line to the Prophet's pursuit of truth. [2 notes: JISHUMI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭū)]
- 53:17/q04/p5 It did not perceive the seen thing falsely. — Rāzī denies perception contrary to reality; Ṭabāṭabāʾī denies inaccurate sight; Elmalılı says it did not see falsely. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI]

## 53:17/q05 What did the gaze not exceed?  [وَمَا طَغَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the boundary is the seen object, a commanded allowance, truthful perception, or selfless attention.
- 53:17/q05/p1 It did not look past the object it actually saw. — Farrāʾ, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī speak of stopping at the seen object; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān say this confirms the event and removes doubt. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the sight did not exceed what was described. [11 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:17/q05/p2 It did not look beyond what God commanded or permitted him to see. — Naḥḥās says it did not pass what he could discern; Ibn ʿAbbās via Wāḥidī and Durr says what was commanded. Others specify permitted signs or bounds; Ibn Kathīr extends the restraint to asking for nothing more than was given. Ṭūsī says it held whether the vision concerned Gabriel or God; Sāmarrāʾī says the scene's pull did not move it. [21 notes: NAHHAS; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس عن النبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(غير مسمى); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN(Ṭū)]
- 53:17/q05/p3 It did not exceed the truth by making the vision larger or unreal. — Māwardī gives going beyond truth and Ibn Baḥr excludes excess imagined in perception; Rāzī denies imagining the nonexistent. Elmalılı and Ṭabāṭabāʾī connect the limit with sound perception. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر,ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 53:17/q05/p4 It did not turn inward to selfhood. — Qāshānī makes the excess an inward turning toward oneself that would veil the vision. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 53:17/q06 What does the verse affirm about the accuracy of the vision?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the stress falls on objective truth of what was seen or on his capacity to see it steadily.
- 53:17/q06/p1 The Prophet saw truly and with certainty, without deficiency, excess, illusion, or exaggeration. — Ibn Baḥr denies deficient comprehension and imagined excess; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān say the fixed gaze confirms the event and dispels doubt; Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī stress certainty. Rāzī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī deny false perception, Elmalılı precise observation, and Ibn ʿĀshūr says the verse restates 53:13–18 and denies exaggeration. Iṣlāḥī says revelatory experience of Gabriel is no illusion. [13 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 53:17/q06/p2 The verse marks the Prophet's ability to observe the signs without becoming bewildered. — ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās says God strengthened him against fear; Rāzī contrasts possible unconsciousness before divine lights; Ibn Kathīr cites a poet saying he saw the Garden of Abode and what was above it, and another sight would have bewildered him. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الناظم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 53:17/q07 What quality of the Prophet does the verse praise?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw distinct, compatible inferences from a steady gaze: manners, strength, accuracy, and inward devotion.
- 53:17/q07/p1 Proper etiquette and obedient restraint before the divine presence. — Qushayrī, Wāḥidī, Wāḥidī's Wajīz, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Qurṭubī, Bursevī via Kāshifī, Elmalılı, Kur'an Yolu and Sāmarrāʾī call this decorum; Ibn Kathīr says he did only what was commanded and sought no more; Biqāʿī calls it just, chaste moderation amid wonders. [14 notes: QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(غير مسمى); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 53:17/q07/p2 Unshaken strength and composure before overwhelming sights. — Rāzī compares steady sight before divine lights to composure before a king and says he did not lose consciousness; Elmalılı and Kur'an Yolu contrast Moses' collapse. Kur'an Yolu reads the second verb as strength of gaze. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI(Râzî); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 53:17/q07/p3 Exact, certain perception. — Ibn Baḥr denies perceptual shortfall and addition; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says accurate sight confirms the event; Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī emphasize certainty; Rāzī calls the certainty unsurpassed. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 53:17/q07/p4 Complete devotion, free from self and lesser attractions. — Tustarī says absorption in the Lord steadied the Prophet; Qāshānī rejects attention to others or self; Kāshifī praises high aspiration; al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya says bodily and spiritual sight ignored worldly and afterlife rewards; al-Baqlī calls this the beloved's steadfast wish to behold his Lord. [6 notes: TUSTARI; QASHANI; BURSEVI(البقلي,التأويلات النجمية,الكاشفي)]

## 53:17/q08 What was the object of the vision in this passage?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the nearby descriptions identify Gabriel, the great signs, or a vision of God.
- 53:17/q08/p1 Gabriel in his true form or the divine signs shown through him. — Zajjāj identifies Gabriel filling the horizon; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Prophet saw Gabriel and that 53:13–18 restates the sight. Study Quran reports a common interpretation of two visions of Gabriel in true form, unlike his usual human appearance; Ṭabāṭabāʾī observes that the passage names signs and revelation, not God. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن مسعود (على ظن محمد)); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(many commentators)]
- 53:17/q08/p2 God was seen. — Bursevī infers a waking sight of God with physical eyes; al-Baqlī treats this as the second vision of the beloved looking to his Lord. Study Quran reports that many locate a vision of God during the Night Journey and Ascension, by heart alone or by eye and heart. [3 notes: BURSEVI(البقلي); STUDYQURAN(many others)]
- 53:17/q08/p3 The verse leaves the seen object open between Gabriel and God. — Ṭūsī says the prohibition on seeking more holds with either object. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says even a hypothetical vision of God would be heart-sight, while the passage itself names God's great signs; the poet cited by Ibn Kathīr specifies the Garden of Abode and what lay above it as sights. [4 notes: STUDYQURAN(Ṭū); TABATABAI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الناظم)]

## 53:17/q09 Was this sight physical eye-sight or heart-vision?  [ٱلْبَصَرُ · theology]
turns on: Whether baṣar and the surrounding wording signal bodily waking sight or the inner vision linked to 53:11–13.
- 53:17/q09/p1 The Prophet saw God with physical eyes while awake. — Bursevī prefers this: naming the eye as steady suggests waking vision; a heart-vision would have named the heart. Study Quran reports that some readers combine eye and heart sight. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(many others); BURSEVI+]
- 53:17/q09/p2 It was heart-vision. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī links this seeing to 53:11 and 53:13 and says that even if God were the object, seeing God would be heart-sight rather than bodily sensory vision; Study Quran reports readers of a heart-only vision. Bursevī acknowledges the inner-sight construal as possible in isolation but rejects it for lack of contextual evidence. [3 notes: TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(many others); BURSEVI-]
- 53:17/q09/p3 Baṣar could mean inner sight, though the context does not support it here. — Bursevī records the lexical possibility only to reject it because the passage gives no contextual evidence. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 53:17/q10 What does the definite article in al-baṣar imply?  [ٱلْبَصَرُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the definite noun points to the Prophet specifically or has universal force under negation.
- 53:17/q10/p1 It identifies Muhammad's particular sight. — Rāzī ties the individual eye to the Sidra scene: whether insects or lights covered it, his sight stayed on its purpose. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+]
- 53:17/q10/p2 It gives generic scope: no sight veered under the awe. — Rāzī reports this alternative and replies that a definite noun under negation can be generic, citing 6:103. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:17/q11 How can the second clause be construed syntactically?  [وَمَا طَغَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the second clause has independent force or depends on an understood subject.
- 53:17/q11/p1 It may stand as an independent sentence. — Rāzī gives this as one construction. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:17/q11/p2 Its subject may be omitted and supplied from the first clause. — Rāzī gives this as the alternative construction. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:17/q12 Why mention sight and choose these two verbs?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the wording chiefly emphasizes the faculty that endured the encounter or the moral force of the two verbs.
- 53:17/q12/p1 Sight signals an immense encounter met by steady composure. — Rāzī contrasts sight with heart and likens the Prophet to one composed before a king; Bursevī uses the mention of eye to infer waking sight. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 53:17/q12/p2 The verbs make looking aside or beyond blameworthy there. — Rāzī says the choice of zaygh and ṭughyān carries censure and marks unsurpassed certainty; Kur'an Yolu assigns courtesy to the first verb and strength of gaze to the second. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 53:17/q13 How is the scene compared with Moses' experience?  [* · links]
- 53:17/q13/p1 Muhammad and the Sidra remained steady where Moses collapsed and the mountain was shattered. — Rāzī compares the divine lights with Moses' loss of consciousness; Elmalılı and Kur'an Yolu cite Moses at 7:143 and contrast the standing Sidra and composed Prophet. Kashf al-Asrār adds that Moses asked to see God, heard 'You will not see Me', and repented, whereas Muhammad's divinely guarded gaze is praised, invoking 7:143 and 20:131. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار); ELMALILI(Râzî); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 53:17/q14 When is this vision placed in the Prophet's life?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The reported dates of the Ascension differ, although both place it in Meccan time.
- 53:17/q14/p1 It belongs to the Night Journey and Ascension toward the end of the Meccan period. — Wāḥidī's Wajīz sets the conduct on the Night Journey; Kur'an Yolu says the sūra is early Meccan but 53:13–18 are generally tied to the later Miʿrāj. Study Quran says they may be later than the sūra's early setting, and many date the Ascension most likely to 619. [6 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(many commentators); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 53:17/q14/p2 The Ascension occurred in 614, seven years before the hijrah. — Study Quran reports a minority dating. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(a minority)]

## 53:17/q15 What does the Gabriel report say happened at the ascent?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether to state the general two-vision identification or the detailed, unusually graded Ibn Masʿūd report.
- 53:17/q15/p1 Muhammad saw Gabriel in his true form a second time; Gabriel then prostrated. — A report tentatively attributed to Ibn Masʿūd says the first true-form sight followed Muhammad's request and filled the horizon; the second was at the ascent, when Gabriel resumed his form and prostrated. It calls Gabriel's creation the great signs. Aḥmad transmits it, and Ibn Kathīr calls it strange. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن مسعود (على ظن محمد))]
- 53:17/q15/p2 The passage describes two visions of Gabriel in his true form. — Study Quran says many commentators distinguish these visions from Gabriel's usual human appearance. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(many commentators)]

## 53:17/q16 How does this passage support the sūra's account of revelation?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the passage's main polemical force is authentication of revelation, restraint of speculation, or denial of intermediary gods.
- 53:17/q16/p1 It validates revelation through the trustworthy angel against claims of invention, soothsaying, and illusion. — Iṣlāḥī says 53:1–18 establish the Qur'an as revelation from God's dependable angel and deny soothsaying and fortune-telling; he also denies illusory revelatory experiences. Study Quran relates the opening to the disbelievers' allegation in 52:33 that the Prophet invented revelation and notes the opening's rhetorical power and disputed interpretation. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 53:17/q16/p2 The scene directs faith beyond the senses and limits speculative reason. — Kur'an Yolu draws this lesson from the mysterious, vivid scene. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 53:17/q16/p3 It undercuts pagan attempts to assign divinity to intermediaries. — Kur'an Yolu says the passage condemns making divinity concrete through material or spiritual beings and attributing godhood besides God. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 53:17/q17 What additional scriptural parallel is drawn with Joseph?  [* · links]
- 53:17/q17/p1 Muhammad's pardon of his Meccan kin echoes Joseph's pardon of his brothers. — Biqāʿī cites Joseph's words in 12:92 as the model for the pardon at the conquest. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 53:17/q18 What does the ancillary report say about the Prophet's speech?  [* · reports]
- 53:17/q18/p1 A phrase or two of his speech can gather many matters from earlier books. — Ibn Abī Shihāb, reported in al-Itqān, explains the Prophet's description of his speech as comprehensive. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن أبي شهاب)]
