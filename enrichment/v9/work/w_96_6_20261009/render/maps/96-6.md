# 96:6: 14 questions

## 96:6/q01 What does kallā do here?  [كَلَّآ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether a rebuke has an implicit or subsequent target, or the particle affirms or opens a new statement.
- 96:6/q01/p1 It rebukes or restrains transgression and ingratitude toward God's favors. — Al-Farrāʾ calls it repudiation; al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī supply the implicit object from God's preceding favors. Ibn ʿĀshūr instead points forward to the man prohibiting prayer and cites an opening negative oath of Imruʾ al-Qays. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ points to the following warning; Samarrāʾī notes kallā's concentration in the Qurʾān's second part and its fit with Meccan judgment themes. [30 notes: MAWARDI(الفراء); MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ITQAN(ابن هشام)-]
- 96:6/q01/p2 It affirms the following statement: 'indeed' or 'truly'. — Al-Jurjānī says neither side provides an object for repudiation and compares 74:32; al-Qurṭubī says no prior statement calls for a literal 'no'. Elmalılı reports this reading as keeping the address to the Prophet. Samarrāʾī explicitly reads ḥaqqan. [17 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); WAHIDI-QT; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI(الجرجاني); RAZI-FULL(الجرجاني صاحب النظم); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ELMALILI; BINTSHATI(بعض المفسرين); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BINTSHATI-]
- 96:6/q01/p3 It means 'except'. — Abū Ḥātim al-Sijistānī compares 78:4 (kallā sawfa yaʿlamūn). [2 notes: MAWARDI(أبو حاتم السجستاني); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو حاتم السجستاني)]
- 96:6/q01/p4 It opens a new statement with the force of 'behold'. — Abū Ḥātim adduces Gabriel's first five verses, the pause after 96:5, and the later opening at 96:6; Ibn Hishām likewise treats it as a new opening, challenging a rebuke-only reading. [2 notes: BURHAN(أبو حاتم); ITQAN(ابن هشام)]
- 96:6/q01/p5 It corrects the human's failure to recognize God's creation and teaching. — Muqātil says wealth diverts the human from recognizing creation from a clot and teaching after ignorance. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); RAZI(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(مقاتل)]
- 96:6/q01/p6 It warns the Prophet not to cease repeating the command to read. — Elmalılı prefers continuity with the opening address to Muhammad; Abū Jahl exemplifies the self-sufficient transgressor against whom reading must continue. [2 notes: ELMALILI; ELMALILI+]

## 96:6/q02 Who is al-insān here?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular denotes the immediate historical offender or humanity as a class despite that occasion.
- 96:6/q02/p1 Humanity generally, with Abu Jahl a prominent instance. — Al-Rāzī prefers the surface context of creation, blessings and return to judgment (96:2, 96:8); al-Ālūsī allows a later Abu Jahl occasion without narrowing the noun; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the generic covers most people of that time except those restrained by religion or character. Kur'an Yolu extends it to every age. [19 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; BIQAI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(الفخر الرازي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI; ALUSI+; RAZI-FULL+; RAZI+]
- 96:6/q02/p2 Abu Jahl ibn Hishām in particular. — Muqātil links his growing wealth to better clothes, mounts, food and drink; Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds his large council, hostility to Muhammad and ban on mosque prayer. Al-Rāzī says most exegetes identify him, although he prefers the generic sense. Al-Suyūṭī identifies this as the third al-insān, contrasting earlier occurrences. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI(أكثر المفسرين); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ITQAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirlerin çoğunluğu)]

## 96:6/q03 What does yaṭghā mean here?  [لَيَطْغَىٰٓ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the expression describes moral boundary crossing, social ascent, or a possible Semitic parallel.
- 96:6/q03/p1 To exceed proper bounds in disobedience, arrogance, rebellion or tyranny. — Ibn Shajara and Corpus Coranicum derive the moral sense from water overflowing at 69:11; Corpus Coranicum also cites 69:5, 53:17 and early Meccan human uses including 91:11 and 79:17. Al-Ālūsī includes following desire; Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses pride when secure enough to overstep others; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ invokes despots. The shades 'disobey', 'arrogance', and 'insolence' differ in wording but share this boundary crossing. [33 notes: TAB; MAWARDI(ابن شجرة,الكلبي,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة,الكلبي); THALABI; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; CORPUSCORANICUM+]
- 96:6/q03/p2 To rise from one rank or social level to another. — Al-Suddī gives rank advancement; al-Kalbī specifies clothing, food and similar signs of status. Al-Ālūsī rejects al-Kalbī's reading as unsound. [4 notes: MAWARDI(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); THALABI(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); ALUSI(الكلبي)-; ALUSI-FULL(الكلبي)-]
- 96:6/q03/p3 Its moral sense may have an Aramaic parallel, meaning straying from right belief. — Corpus Coranicum reports Luxenberg's proposed Aramaic ṭʿā and preference for 'forgetting', but favors straying from right belief and rejects his denial of an Arabic root. It allows possible Syriac influence while retaining the Arabic image of exceeding measure. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM(Luxenberg)]

## 96:6/q04 Why does yaṭghā take this verb form?  [لَيَطْغَىٰٓ · grammar]
- 96:6/q04/p1 Its imperfect follows faʿala yafʿalu because the root contains the guttural ghayn. — Al-Naḥḥās gives the guttural as the cause. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 96:6/q05 Why are inna and the lām emphatic here?  [إِنَّ لَيَطْغَىٰٓ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the emphasis addresses doubt about the general claim or the severity of Abu Jahl's conduct.
- 96:6/q05/p1 They reinforce a surprising claim that might otherwise seem doubtful. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says human transgression after receiving favor is unexpected to the listener. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]
- 96:6/q05/p2 The stronger emphasis consoles and honors Muhammad amid Abu Jahl's hostility. — Al-Rāzī contrasts 20:24, said before Moses confronted Pharaoh, with Muhammad's warning after harsh rejection; Abu Jahl sought physical harm despite lesser power, envied Muhammad from youth and died hostile, while Pharaoh initially treated Moses well and professed faith at 10:90. He likens beloved Muhammad and Moses to eye and hand, the eye guarded more carefully. [9 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 96:6/q06 How can the following 'he sees himself self-sufficient' construction work?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَيَطْغَىٰٓ · grammar]
- 96:6/q06/p1 The seeing is cognition, so its subject and object can refer to the same person and 'self-sufficient' supplies the second object. — Al-Farrāʾ contrasts complete verbs needing nafs in reflexive expression with cognition verbs such as aẓunnunī, and says raʾā nafsahu is not used here; poetry permits some complete-verb direct objects, but ʿadimtunī is not standard. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Ṭabrisī explain both pronouns and the second object. [6 notes: FARRA; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI]

## 96:6/q07 What brings humans to the transgression described here?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَيَطْغَىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the decisive sense of self-sufficiency is material prosperity, imagined independence from God through knowledge, or both.
- 96:6/q07/p1 Wealth and the feeling of self-sufficiency breed pride, desire and ingratitude. — Tustarī says seeing oneself wealthy breeds felt independence, then tyranny; al-Ḥasan says expanded worldly life always brings transgression and one should fear divine guile in abundance, while deprivation may be God's regard. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says wealth harms one whose proper state is poverty. Al-Qushayrī says witnessing need prevents arrogance. Al-Rāzī adds status, prosperity and power as causes of blindness to divine signs. Ibn Kathīr says God will ask how wealth was acquired and spent. The Prophet's prayer seeks refuge from both forgetful poverty and transgressive wealth. [18 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TUSTARI(الحسن); TAB; SULAMI(ابن عطاء); THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QUSHAYRI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI-]
- 96:6/q07/p2 Claiming independence from the Creator, especially through acquired knowledge, is the decisive cause. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ argues that the text points to imagined independence from God, not absorption in money or status. Al-Qāshānī says the Prophet was taught knowledge before firm abiding so he would not claim independent perfection through selfhood; Biqāʿī warns the learned person's error weighs more. Kur'an Yolu stresses dependence on God and society and God's ability to remove resources. [6 notes: BINTSHATI; QASHANI; BIQAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 96:6/q07/p3 Both knowledge and wealth can produce tyranny; asceticism and hunger restrain it. — Yaḥyā ibn Muʿādh says asceticism protects worship from tyranny and persistent hunger cuts sinful desire. Biqāʿī likens human excess to volatile blood humor, whose disorder can be quickly remedied by a stronger, correctly applied treatment. [3 notes: SULAMI(يحيى بن معاذ); BIQAI]

## 96:6/q08 What lesson does the warning draw from God's favors?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Which aspect of the warning is drawn out: gratitude for gifts or the contrasting outcomes of knowledge and worldly pursuit.
- 96:6/q08/p1 Creation, teaching and provision call for gratitude and humility; transgression is an ungrateful misuse of them. — Al-Ṭabarī names sound creation, knowledge and unparalleled favor. Al-Rāzī says origin from a clot should have checked arrogance. Sayyid Quṭb says humanity should thank God for creating, teaching and honoring it, and God also gave wealth. Kur'an Yolu adds that human control over resources and fate is illusory. Al-Naḥḥās says reflection on creation reveals divine oneness. [7 notes: TAB; NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 96:6/q08/p2 The worldly seeker remains insatiable and deepens transgression, while the seeker of knowledge gains God's approval. — Ibn Masʿūd or ʿAbd Allāh connects the warning to learned fear in 35:28; Ibn Kathīr also transmits a version raised to the Prophet stating that both seekers are insatiable. [12 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن مسعود,عبد الله); IBNKATHIR(عبد الله); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن مسعود); DURR(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 96:6/q09 How does this warning connect with the surrounding verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the verse looks back to the favors, forward to Abu Jahl's act, or continues the command to read.
- 96:6/q09/p1 It turns from God's gifts in the opening to humanity's ungrateful response. — Al-Zamakhsharī and later commentators derive the omitted ingratitude from the preceding creation and teaching; al-Ālūsī reports an implied call to thank God after verse 5. Biqāʿī describes a turn from divine matters to the human soul; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ sees pride in knowledge and status. [12 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; ALUSI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); BINTSHATI; STUDYQURAN]
- 96:6/q09/p2 The general warning in verses 6–8 introduces the particular man who forbids prayer from verse 9 onward. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reconciles the general noun with the Abu Jahl reports: the scene leads through his challenge to summon his assembly at 96:17. [5 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 96:6/q09/p3 The address continues the command to the Prophet to read and warns him to persist. — Elmalılı prefers this connection because making Abu Jahl the immediate addressee disconnects the opening; he reports that rival connection as well. [4 notes: ELMALILI; ELMALILI+]
- 96:6/q09/p4 The clause closes a complete stop in recitation. — Al-Naḥḥās says the pause after the statement is complete throughout the Qurʾān. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 96:6/q10 Which portion of the sūra is tied to Abu Jahl?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the occasion applies from verse 6, from the prohibition scene at verse 9, or to the whole sūra.
- 96:6/q10/p1 The passage from verse 6 through the end was revealed concerning Abu Jahl. — Al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Nasafī and others give this scope; al-Qurṭubī sometimes reports the whole sūra as an alternative. The Abu Hurayra transmission leaves uncertain whether the revelation attribution belonged to his report. [18 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; RAZI(منهم); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(İbn Abbas); SUYUTI-LUBAB(أبو هريرة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirlerin çoğunluğu); IBNKATHIR; MUSLIM(أبو هريرة)]
- 96:6/q10/p2 The direct Abu Jahl account begins at verse 9; verses 6–8 are its general introduction. — Al-Rāzī reports this boundary; Ibn ʿĀshūr adopts it to reconcile the historical report and general wording. [4 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 96:6/q10/p3 The entire sūra was revealed concerning Abu Jahl. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this as an alternative and notes that it would make the entire sūra later than the first revelations. [3 notes: QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]

## 96:6/q11 When were these verses revealed relative to the opening five?  [* · sciences]
turns on: How much time elapsed after the opening revelation, especially in light of the prayer scene.
- 96:6/q11/p1 The opening five came first; the later passage followed after a pause and was joined to them. — Al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī compare the later placement of 2:281 in al-Baqara. Ibn ʿĀshūr favors a short pause, says adjacent recited verses normally follow revelation order absent clear proof, and points to reports of Gabriel's non-Qurʾānic revelation during the pause and its memory in al-Ḍuḥā; he rejects al-Suhaylī's two-year estimate as unlikely. The prayer reference need not require a post Night Journey date. [13 notes: RAZI(منهم); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(أبو حاتم); ALUSI; IBNASHUR; ELMALILI(İbn Abbas); IBNASHUR+]
- 96:6/q11/p2 The prayer scene requires intervening revelation, possibly a much later attachment. — Some cited by Ibn ʿĀshūr reason that obligatory prayer was prescribed at the Night Journey years later; al-Suhaylī estimates a two-year first pause, which Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects. Others deny that this was the first revealed sūra. [5 notes: IBNASHUR(السهيلي,بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI(منهم); RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-]
- 96:6/q11/p3 The sūra is Meccan and has seventeen verses. — Al-Jishumī gives the place and count. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 96:6/q12 What is reported about Abu Jahl's challenge to the Prophet at prayer?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports vary in the threat, intervention, boasting and outcome.
- 96:6/q12/p1 He threatened to trample the Prophet while praying, then recoiled before fire, terror and wings; angels would have seized him. — Abū Hurayra's report is transmitted by Muslim and others; Ibn Kathīr names Aḥmad, Muslim, al-Nasāʾī and Ibn Abī Ḥātim through Muʿtamir and notes doubt whether the revelation attribution was in the report. Versions say the angels would seize him, sometimes limb by limb; al-Durr identifies his council and the guards of 96:17–18. [17 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(أبو هريرة); IBNATIYYA(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN(أبو جهل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNASHUR(أبو هريرة); DURR(أبو هريرة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirlerin çoğunluğu); MUSLIM(أبو هريرة)]
- 96:6/q12/p2 He threatened to trample the Prophet and boasted of his large council; the Prophet warned that angels or zabāniya would seize him. — Ibn ʿAbbās relates the boast to 96:17–18; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān report the Prophet's rebuke and Abu Jahl's claim that no one in the valley had a larger following. Al-Rāzī also reports Abu Jahl's claim to be Mecca's most honored man, perhaps answering 96:3. [16 notes: IBNATIYYA(أبو جهل,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN(أبو جهل,رسول الله وأبو جهل); RAZI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR(ابن عباس); ELMALILI(İbn Abbas)]
- 96:6/q12/p3 He vowed to tread on the Prophet's neck, but saw the sky blocked as the Prophet recited the sūra and prostrated. — Al-ʿAbbās ibn ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib gives this variant, including the Prophet's prostration at the end. [6 notes: DURR(العباس بن عبد المطلب)]
- 96:6/q12/p4 He considered forbidding prayer but refrained. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this alternative. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN(أبو جهل)]
- 96:6/q12/p5 He demanded mountains of gold and silver, hoping riches would make people abandon their religion. — Al-Zamakhsharī reports that Gabriel offered this, but the Prophet declined. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو جهل)]
- 96:6/q12/p6 He died still hating Muhammad. — Al-Rāzī reports Abu Jahl's final message to the Prophet and uses it to sharpen his contrast with Pharaoh. [1 notes: RAZI]

## 96:6/q13 What reward is reported for reciting this sūra?  [* · sciences]
- 96:6/q13/p1 Its reciter receives reward as though reciting all the short sūras. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Bayḍāwī transmit this report. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 96:6/q14 What did Abu Jahl deny about Muhammad's revelation?  [* · reports]
- 96:6/q14/p1 He denied that God had taught Muhammad the revelation and prophethood he brought. — ʿAṭāʾ gives this explanation. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء)]
