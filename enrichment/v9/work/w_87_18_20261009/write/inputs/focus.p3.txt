# 87:18/q18 What recitation practices and merits are reported for Sūrat al-Aʿlā? [* · reports]
- 87:18/q18/p1 The Prophet recited it in the two rakʿas before witr, with al-Kāfirūn; then al-Ikhlāṣ, al-Falaq, and al-Nās in witr.
  reasons: ʿĀʾisha gives this sequence.
  holders (1 notes): BAGHAWI-FULL(عائشة)
  notes: BAGHAWI-FULL:v8p403#2/r3
- 87:18/q18/p2 He recited it in the first of three witr rakʿas, followed by al-Kāfirūn and al-Ikhlāṣ.
  reasons: The report is from ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Abī Shabrah.
  holders (1 notes): DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)
  notes: DURR:87:18/r13
- 87:18/q18/p3 He recited it in the first Maghrib rakʿa and al-Kāfirūn in the second.
  reasons: ʿAbd Allāh ibn al-Ḥārith reports this.
  holders (1 notes): DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)
  notes: DURR:87:18/r14
- 87:18/q18/p4 He used it at Eid and Friday prayers with al-Ghāshiya, sometimes both on one day.
  reasons: Quṭb also says al-Aʿlā is Meccan, has nineteen verses, and was loved by the Prophet.

# 87:18/q20 What is said about loss or abrogation of recited scripture? [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether solitary reports establish Qurʾānic wording, and whether ʿUmar's account shows continued recitation.
- 87:18/q20/p1 God can erase script and recitation by causing forgetfulness and commanding their abandonment, as with earlier scriptures; Qurʾānic abrogation ends with the Prophet's death.
  reasons: Abū Bakr al-Rāzī gives this explanation in al-Burhān and al-Itqān.
  holders (2 notes): BURHAN(أبو بكر الرازي); ITQAN(أبو بكر الرازي)
  notes: BURHAN:v2p38/r13 ITQAN:v3p69/r2
- 87:18/q20/p2 Solitary reports cannot establish Qurʾānic text and its subsequent abrogation.
  reasons: Al-Itqān reports this objection and Ibn Ẓafar's view that the stoning text is al-munsaʾ rather than abrogated recitation, though its wording may be known.
  holders (3 notes): ITQAN(ابن ظفر، قوم)
  notes: ITQAN:v3p69/r1 ITQAN:v3p69/r6 ITQAN:v3p69/r7
- 87:18/q20/p3 ʿUmar's stoning report does not securely establish that its recitation remained.
  reasons: Al-Burhān infers writability from ʿUmar's statement; a counterargument says criticism would not have stopped him writing a still-recited verse. Al-Suyūṭī finds the continued-recitation inference problematic but rejects calling ʿUmar's transmission solitary, since he received it from the Prophet. Kathīr ibn al-Ṣalt reports Zayd quoting the wording during codex writing and ʿUmar explaining lashes versus stoning.
  holders (5 notes): ITQAN(البرهان، قول، كثير بن الصامت)
  notes: ITQAN:v3p69/r3 ITQAN:v3p69/r4 ITQAN:v3p69/r5 ITQAN:v3p69/r8 ITQAN:v3p69/r9
sources: BURHAN = al-Zarkashī, d. 794; ITQAN = al-Suyūṭī, d. 911

# 87:18/q21 What reading of the sūra's moral drama is tied to this verse? [* · inward]
- 87:18/q21/p1 Its truths describe receptivity to reminders, purification, and the ruin of seekers of sensory life, preserved on luminous tablets disclosed to the two prophets.
  reasons: Qāshānī gives this inward account of the ancient scriptures.
  holders (1 notes): QASHANI
  notes: QASHANI:87:16-18/r2
sources: QASHANI = ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736

# 87:18/q22 What does the verse imply about the Qurʾān's distinctive scope? [* · interpretation]
- 87:18/q22/p1 Its eloquence and range exceed poets and soothsayers while continuing the prophets' message.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī cites praise, love verse, heroic boasting, satire, exhortation, warning, narrative, law, and unseen past and future information.
  holders (2 notes): BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL
  notes: BIQAI:87:18/r3 BIQAI-FULL:v8p402/r2
