# 87:18/q09 What do reports say Moses's scrolls taught? [ٱلصُّحُفِ · reports]
- 87:18/q09/p1 Admonitions about mortality, worldly change, destiny, and judgment.
  reasons: Abū Dharr's prophetic account challenges joy despite death, trust amid change, striving as though destiny were unknown, and failure to act despite certain judgment.
  holders (2 notes): DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)
  notes: DURR:87:18/r11 DURR-FULL:v8p488/r5

# 87:18/q10 What is said about the survival of Abraham's and Moses's teachings? [ٱلصُّحُفِ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the earlier teachings survived as scrolls, in later writings, or as enduring principles across laws.
- 87:18/q10/p1 The central teachings persisted through every revealed law without abrogation.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān apply this to purification and the afterlife; Rāzī says reason and transmission show these truths do not vary; al-Biqāʿī says the aim of all laws cannot be abrogated. The prophetic saying about acting without shame is cited as inherited from the first prophecy.
  holders (10 notes): IBNATIYYA(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI(بعض المفسرين); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL
  notes: IBNATIYYA:87:18/r5 IBNATIYYA:87:18/r6 IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p470#2/r17 IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p470#2/r18 RAZI:87:18/r4 RAZI-FULL:v31p135#3/r2 ABUHAYYAN:87:18/r5 ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p459/r1 BIQAI:87:18/r1 BIQAI-FULL:v8p402/r1
- 87:18/q10/p2 Mosaic and Israelite writings survive in the Bible despite alteration, retaining monotheism and the afterlife.
  reasons: Islāhī says the surviving teaching can revive faith.

- 87:18/q10/p3 Abraham left oral exhortations later written down by Israelite descendants, and later revived for the Ishmaelites.
  reasons: Islāhī says Israelite prophets renewed the teaching, while unlettered Ishmaelites kept it orally before neglect and innovations; Muhammad revived and completed it. Muqātil says Abraham's scrolls were later taken away.

# 87:18/q11 What does al-ūlā add to ṣuḥuf? [ٱلْأُولَىٰ · meaning]
- 87:18/q11/p1 It marks the scrolls as earlier or ancient and can magnify their standing.
  reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports that calling them ancient before naming Abraham and Moses honors them; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the concluding claim dignifies enduring teaching.
  holders (3 notes): TABATABAI(قيل); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL

# 87:18/q12 How is al-ūlā formed and related grammatically to ṣuḥuf? [ٱلْأُولَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: The three reported analyses differ over the root and the status of the initial hamza.
- 87:18/q12/p1 It is a feminine adjective agreeing with the broken plural ṣuḥuf, treated as feminine.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr gives this agreement.
  holders (1 notes): IBNASHUR
  notes: IBNASHUR:87:18/r8

  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this as one of three root analyses; ūlā's derivation follows the analysis.
  holders (1 notes): IBNASHUR
  notes: IBNASHUR:87:18/r9
- 87:18/q12/p3 The related awwal derives from wāw-wāw-lām with an added hamza.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this as another root analysis.
  holders (1 notes): IBNASHUR
  notes: IBNASHUR:87:18/r9
- 87:18/q12/p4 The related awwal derives from wāw-hamza-lām with metathesis.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this as a third root analysis.

# 87:18/q13 Why is the statement phrased and emphasized this way? [إِنَّ لَفِى ٱلْأُولَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 87:18/q13/p1 It dignifies enduring guidance and confirms it against deniers.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the emphatic particles as directed at deniers familiar with Abraham and Moses. Al-Biqāʿī says ancient acceptance makes hearers more receptive and confirms Muhammad's continuity with earlier prophets; Corpus Coranicum says the ending authorizes the preceding rebuke and its psalm-like hymn reinforces the claim.
  holders (4 notes): IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI; CORPUSCORANICUM
  notes: IBNASHUR:87:18/r1 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p290/r3 BIQAI:87:18/r2 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:16-19:kommentar/r3
- 87:18/q13/p2 The wording announces one divine source for prophetic truth, with changing details and shared core principles.
  reasons: Quṭb says the creed is one though eras differ; Islāhī says the Prophet's teaching is not new and includes the afterlife and individual responsibility. Kuran Yolu invokes 26:196 for shared faith, worship, and morality; Islāhī says worldly preference prevents the message taking root.
  holders (7 notes): QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR
  notes: QUTB-ZILAL:87:1/r4 QUTB-ZILAL:87:18/r1 QUTB-ZILAL:87:18/r2 QUTB-ZILAL:87:19/r1 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p271/r3 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p280/r3 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:87:18/r3
- 87:18/q13/p3 The general mention of earlier scrolls is specified by Abraham and Moses in the next verse.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it summary followed by detail and apposition; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the preliminary antiquity magnifies the scrolls.
