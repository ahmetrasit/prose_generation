# 26:196: 11 questions

## 26:196/q01 What does the pronoun refer to, and what is present in earlier scriptures?  [وَإِنَّهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether the antecedent is the Qur’an’s announcement, the Messenger, shared content, preceding reports, his community, or warnings; 26:197 and the nearby pronouns are cited to assess the choice.
- 26:196/q01/p1 The Qur’an, especially news of its revelation, name, description, or coming, was mentioned in earlier scriptures. — Qatāda and most commentators are cited for its mention. Al-Wāḥidī says God announced its revelation to the final Prophet (7:157); al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī specify news or glad tidings, not a prior revelation of the Qur’an to another prophet. Al-Burūsawī and al-Ālūsī take the pronoun as its mention. Ibn ʿĀshūr excludes verbatim earlier sūras and verses and adduces 7:157, Deuteronomy 18's promised prophet and words put in his mouth, and Matthew 24's worldwide proclamation, linked to 14:1 and 17:89. Asad cites 2:42 and 61:6 for this popular reading; Israelite scholars know the prophecies, says Iṣlāḥī. Kuran Yolu allows it together with shared message; al-Zajjāj also includes Muhammad. [35 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI(أكثر المفسرين); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(أكثر المفسرين); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأكثرون); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(عيسى عليه السلام); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî, İbn Âşûr, Esed); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); TABATABAI+]
- 26:196/q01/p2 Muhammad himself, his description and prophetic mission, was recorded in earlier scriptures. — Muqātil is repeatedly cited for this reading. Al-Suddī names the mission; 7:157 locates the written description in the Torah and Gospel, as al-Zajjāj and al-Qurṭubī note. Abū Ḥayyān says it better keeps nearby pronouns on one referent. Ibn Salām recounts Quraysh meeting Jews reading Torah and their disavowal of those who slandered it for gain, followed by revelation of the Prophet's description and mission. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees Deuteronomy 18's prophet from Israel's brethren as an Arab descendant of Ishmael, comparable to Moses in law, and reads Matthew 24's universal proclamation as relevant. Al-Ālūsī calls this reading unclear. [23 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(مقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(عبد الله بن سلام); IBNASHUR-FULL(عيسى عليه السلام); STUDYQURAN(Th); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]
- 26:196/q01/p3 The Qur’an’s essential meanings and teachings were present in earlier scriptures. — Al-Ṭabrisī names monotheism, justice, resurrection, and the fate of nations. Al-Ālūsī adds divine essence and attributes, admonitions, and stories, while allowing that some Qur’anic material was absent. Al-Biqāʿī names foundations, narratives, and principal teachings; Kuran Yolu calls these basic outlines in the Psalms, Torah, and Gospel and allows a concurrent prediction of the Qur’an. Asad prefers this as consonant with the Qur’an’s repeated doctrine, citing Zamakhsharī and Bayḍāwī. Ibn ʿĀshūr excludes verbatim text. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that the polytheists did not accept earlier books and that this reading poorly fits 26:197. [13 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ITQAN(الأصبهاني); ASAD-NOTES+; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 26:196/q01/p4 The preceding accounts of prophets and nations were already in earlier scriptures. — Al-Rāzī says the seven narratives agree with earlier accounts without discrepancy. Al-Ṭabrisī mentions earlier prophets' accounts of nations; al-Biqāʿī and al-Ālūsī include many narratives and stories among what earlier books contain. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 26:196/q01/p5 Muhammad’s community and religion were described in earlier scriptures. — Al-Suddī mentions Muhammad and his community; al-Ḍaḥḥāk adds the Prophet's religion and a description of the community. [2 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 26:196/q01/p6 The warnings already given are what is in the earlier scriptures. — Al-Rāzī lists the warnings alongside earlier reports and descriptions of the Qur’an or Muhammad as possible antecedents. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 26:196/q02 Why does the wording use a third-person pronoun after direct address?  [وَإِنَّهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 26:196/q02/p1 It shifts from direct address to third person. — Al-Samīn says continuation of the prior address, on the Muhammad reading, would have been wa-innaka, 'and you are in the earlier scriptures'. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 26:196/q03 What does zubur mean here?  [زُبُرِ · meaning]
- 26:196/q03/p1 Books or written scriptures; zabūr is its singular. — Abū ʿUbayda, al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Burūsawī explain the singular-plural pair like rasūl/rusul. Al-Jishumī derives it from collecting and writing; Ibn al-Jawzī gives zabartu al-kitāb, 'I wrote the book', and reports al-Zajjāj's sense of a book containing wisdom. Al-Dāmghānī compares 21:105; Study Quran compares 3:184. Qatāda and Ibn Zayd gloss the phrase as the books of earlier peoples. [17 notes: MAJAZ; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 26:196/q04 How is zubur read?  [زُبُرِ · readings]
turns on: The vowel on the bāʾ, with the still form explained as lightening.
- 26:196/q04/p1 With ḍamma on the bāʾ: zubur. — Al-Thaʿlabī contrasts this vowelled form with al-Aʿmash's still bāʾ; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it the majority pronunciation. Abū Ḥayyān says it is the underlying form. [4 notes: THALABI(الأعمش); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور والأعمش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأعمش); SAMIN-DURR(الأعمش)]
- 26:196/q04/p2 With sukūn on the bāʾ: zubr. — Al-Aʿmash is the named reader in all reports. Al-Samīn calls it a lightening of the standard form; Abū Ḥayyān says underlying ḍamma is suppressed. [6 notes: THALABI(الأعمش); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور والأعمش); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأعمش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأعمش); SAMIN-DURR(الأعمش); ALUSI-FULL(الأعمش)]

## 26:196/q05 Which are the books of the earlier peoples?  [زُبُرِ ٱلْأَوَّلِينَ · referent]
- 26:196/q05/p1 Revealed books of earlier prophets and peoples, including the Torah, Gospel, Psalms, and other scriptures. — Ibn Zayd says God sent them to earlier peoples. Al-Māwardī names the Torah, Gospel, and other revealed books; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds the Psalms and describes a collective category. Kuran Yolu also names Psalms, Torah, and Gospel. Al-Dāmghānī invokes 21:105; the Prophet's written description in Torah and Gospel is linked to 7:157. [11 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); MAWARDI-FULL; DAMGHANI; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 26:196/q06 How does the correspondence with earlier scriptures support the revelation?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether corroboration lies in agreement of past accounts or in earlier predictions of a future prophet and scripture.
- 26:196/q06/p1 The matching prophetic narratives indicate revelation because Muhammad had not learned them from earlier communities. — Al-Rāzī says the seven accounts match earlier scripture without discrepancy though Muhammad had neither studied nor prepared them. Al-Biqāʿī says he had no contact with those communities from which to learn their recognized divine books. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 26:196/q06/p2 Earlier predictions of the Prophet and Qur’an corroborate their coming. — Al-Zajjāj and Ibn ʿĀshūr invoke 7:157. Ibn ʿĀshūr interprets Deuteronomy 18's prophet from Israel's brethren and the words placed in his mouth as Muhammad and his recitation, and Matthew 24's worldwide proclamation as the Qur’an addressed to all peoples (14:1; 17:89). Iṣlāḥī says Israelite scholars know these prophecies. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNASHUR-FULL(عيسى عليه السلام); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:196/q07 Why are prophetic stories repeated in this sūra?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes give complementary purposes: independent lessons, retention, and reaching resistant listeners.
- 26:196/q07/p1 Each story functions as a self-contained revelation with its own lesson. — Al-Zamakhsharī says each therefore merits the same opening and conclusion. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 26:196/q07/p2 Repetition fixes the meanings in memory and strengthens understanding. — Al-Zamakhsharī likens it to repeated review, making knowledge harder to forget. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 26:196/q07/p3 Repetition may open listeners' ears and minds despite their resistance. — Al-Zamakhsharī says listeners are deaf to truth and closed to reflection; reiterated stories polish understanding dulled by neglect. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 26:196/q08 How does this verse fit the sūra’s concluding discourse?  [* · links]
- 26:196/q08/p1 It returns to the opening warning and makes prophetic histories signs for stubborn Quraysh. — Iṣlāḥī says the warnings are true, signs of punishment can come, and the earlier histories already serve as signs. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:196/q09 Does the Qur’an remain Qur’an when its meanings are expressed in another language?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether Qur’an names its transferable content, its revealed verbal composition, or has different lexical and religious uses.
- 26:196/q09/p1 Its meanings can count as Qur’an in another language. — Some followers of Abū Ḥanīfa infer this from 26:196 because Qur’anic content is in earlier books. Al-Ālūsī objects to the categorical Arabic-only naming claim by citing 41:44's hypothetical non-Arabic Qur’an. [2 notes: JISHUMI(بعض أصحاب أبي حنيفة); ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:196/q09/p2 The Qur’an comprises specific wording and meaning, so a translation does not have its status. — Al-Jishumī says earlier scriptures contain its content, not its form. Al-Kashf argues a translated miracle loses the revealed composition, and God's inner meaning is not recitable; differing names for shared meanings prove wording matters. Al-Iṣfahānī likens composition to the form of gold made into a ring, earring, or bracelet, which changes its name and status; its levels begin with letters forming words and words forming meaningful sentences. [5 notes: JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); ITQAN(الأصبهاني); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 26:196/q09/p3 Indefinite qur’ān may mean any recited text, whereas the definite Qur’an in religious usage is the Arabic text. — Al-Ālūsī applies the distinction to 73:20 and rejects a partitive reading of 'from' that treats a meaning as a part of the Qur’an. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 26:196/q10 May Qur’anic content be recited in a non-Arabic language during prayer?  [* · law]
turns on: The reports differ on permitted language, content, worshipper's Arabic ability, and Abu Ḥanīfa's later view.
- 26:196/q10/p1 Yes, without language or ability restriction. — Attributed to Abū Ḥanīfa, famously for Persian and in one report for Turkish, Indian, and other languages; some followers infer the rule from 26:196. Al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān report the use of the earlier-scripture meanings reading for Persian prayer recitation. Al-Jishumī rejects the inference because Qur’an includes wording and meaning; al-Ālūsī calls the verse weak evidence and cites a report of Abu Ḥanīfa's retraction. [4 notes: JISHUMI(بعض أصحاب أبي حنيفة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); JISHUMI-; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف,جمع من الثقات المحققين)-]
- 26:196/q10/p2 Persian alone is permitted. — Al-Ālūsī reports an attribution to Abū Ḥanīfa that calls Persian the noblest language after Arabic and cites a report that Paradise speaks Arabic and Persian Duri. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو حنيفة)]
- 26:196/q10/p3 Persian is permitted only for praise, such as Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ. — Al-Ālūsī reports this as another attribution to Abū Ḥanīfa, excluding other Qur’anic content. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو حنيفة)]
- 26:196/q10/p4 Persian is permitted in prayer only for one unable to recite Arabic, and only for remembrance or glorification. — Al-Ālūsī reports this position from the two companions; it excludes use outside prayer, by an Arabic-capable person, and for narrative or legal passages. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الصاحبان)]
- 26:196/q10/p5 Unrestricted non-Arabic recitation is not a valid settled permission. — Al-Ālūsī reports that reliable authorities authenticate Abū Ḥanīfa's later retraction; al-Jishumī rejects the premise that translated content remains Qur’an, and al-Kashf argues translation cannot be the revealed miracle. [4 notes: JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف,جمع من الثقات المحققين); JISHUMI(بعض أصحاب أبي حنيفة)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حنيفة)-]

## 26:196/q11 What makes the Qur’an inimitable in light of shared words or meanings?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the miracle inheres in composition, divine diversion of challengers, or unseen information.
- 26:196/q11/p1 Its particular composition and arrangement are intrinsically inimitable. — Al-Iṣfahānī distinguishes this from divine diversion and says Arabic words and meanings found in earlier scriptures are shared elements, while composition is the form. His gold-jewelry analogy and levels from letters to words to sentences explain that form. Al-Suyūṭī says Arabs failed to match it despite Arabic being their language; reflection on composition gives detailed proof and points to an all-knowing source. Al-Ālūsī reports arrangement proponents citing ordinary Arabic words and earlier meanings, with 43:3 and 26:196. [5 notes: ITQAN(الأصبهاني); ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:196/q11/p2 God's diversion of challengers is a proposed explanation of inimitability. — Al-Iṣfahānī distinguishes this theory from the intrinsic one; the note reports the contrast without endorsing diversion. [1 notes: ITQAN(الأصبهاني)]
- 26:196/q11/p3 Unseen knowledge is miraculous irrespective of language or form. — Al-Ālūsī reports this argument from proponents of the arrangement theory alongside their claim that words are ordinary Arabic and many meanings occur in earlier books. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
