# 20:133: 19 questions

## 20:133/q01 Who are the speakers?  [وَقَالُوا۟ · referent]
- 20:133/q01/p1 Meccan or Quraysh polytheists and unbelievers. — Muqātil and Ibn al-Jawzī identify the Meccan opponents; Bursevī specifies Quraysh. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says groups of disbelievers. [11 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; TAB-FULL]

## 20:133/q02 What does lawlā mean here?  [لَوْلَا · meaning]
- 20:133/q02/p1 “Why not?”: a prompt or demand to bring a sign. — Yaḥyā gives the direct gloss. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says lawlā/lawmā prompts when followed by a verb, but expresses impossibility after a noun. [2 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 20:133/q03 What sort of sign do they demand?  [يَأْتِينَا بِـَٔايَةٍۢ مِّن رَّبِّهِۦٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether their challenge seeks any proof, a prescribed tangible miracle, or the threatened punishment.
- 20:133/q03/p1 A sign of their own choosing, like the earlier prophets' visible miracles. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, and Ibn al-Jawzī cite Ṣāliḥ's she-camel; al-Wāḥidī and al-Qurṭubī cite Moses' staff. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī say they wanted a compelling sign; Asad compares 6:109. Al-Iṣlāḥī says the People of the Book encouraged this demand. [19 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:133/q03/p2 A sign proving Muhammad's prophetic mission, without a specified form. — Muqātil compares signs granted earlier messengers; Ibn Kathīr glosses it as a mark that he is God's messenger. Al-Rāzī observes that the demand insinuates there was no proof and cites 21:5. [10 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:133/q03/p3 The threatened punishment, as an alternative demanded sign. — Al-Iṣlāḥī says opponents demanded a miracle or the threatened punishment when cornered in argument. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:133/q04 Why do they ask for a further sign despite existing evidence?  [وَقَالُوا۟ لَوْلَا يَأْتِينَا بِـَٔايَةٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes distinguish willful dismissal, spiritual incapacity, and the immediate polemical occasion.
- 20:133/q04/p1 They stubbornly disregard signs already given. — Al-Zajjāj says signs had come but the deniers wanted to dictate them. Al-Baghawī, Bursevī, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Ālūsī say many witnessed signs were discounted; al-Ālūsī calls them powerful enough to shake mountains. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the demand taunts the Prophet by denying the Qur'an's force. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 20:133/q04/p2 They are blind to proof and would grow only more rebellious without God's willing belief. — Al-Qushayrī says the failure is perception rather than evidence and that even every requested sign would increase tyranny, disbelief and loss if God did not will belief. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 20:133/q04/p3 They resort to the demand when cornered, encouraged by People of the Book. — Al-Iṣlāḥī assigns special responsibility to the People of the Book, who urged idolaters to request signs like earlier messengers' signs. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:133/q05 What does bayyina mean?  [بَيِّنَةُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether bayyina denotes evidence that attests or an explanation that clarifies.
- 20:133/q05/p1 A clear proof, evidencing witness, or indication. — Bursevī says it can be rational or sensory. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī supplies “evidencing witness” and “clear proof.” [2 notes: BURSEVI; TABATABAI]
- 20:133/q05/p2 An explanatory clarification. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this alternative sense and applies it to the Qur'an's warning stories. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:133/q06 What is the clear proof answering their demand?  [بَيِّنَةُ مَا فِى ٱلصُّحُفِ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether proof is the Qur'an's agreement with earlier books, their prophecies, their warnings about rejected signs, or the earlier books as witnesses.
- 20:133/q06/p1 The Qur'an, whose agreement with and explanation of earlier revelation proves its divine source. — Al-Wāḥidī attributes this to al-Kalbī and others; Abū Muslim, al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Biqāʿī and others stress Muhammad's lack of schooling. Bayḍāwī cites shared beliefs and universal law, earlier books' need of authentication, and the Qur'an's lasting superior miracle of knowledge; Ibn Kathīr says it confirms truth and exposes additions, compares 29:50–51, and reports the Prophet's statement about revelation as his chief gift. Al-Biqāʿī cites 20:9, 20:115 and inimitable arrangement; Bursevī and al-Ālūsī stress foundational rulings and independent proof. Ṭabāṭabāʾī uses bayyina as witness; Asad, Kur'an Yolu and Study Quran cite shared truths or stories. [29 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:133/q06/p2 Earlier scriptures' predictions and descriptions of Muhammad. — Yaḥyā cites 7:157; al-Wāḥidī attributes this to some exegetes. Al-Iṣlāḥī calls the final Messenger's advent the fulfillment of their prophecies. Asad adduces Deuteronomy 18:15, 18 and Jesus' Comforter sayings, referencing 2:42 and 61:6; Kur'an Yolu cites 7:157. [11 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT(بعض أهل التفسير); JISHUMI(قيل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:133/q06/p3 Earlier scriptures' and the Qur'an's accounts of peoples destroyed after demanding and rejecting signs. — Al-Ṭabarī warns another granted sign could hasten the Meccans' punishment; Qatāda notes settlements they pass. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī ask why the present demanders would fare differently. Al-Ālūsī reports al-Imām and al-Ṭabrisī for this view but rejects it. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قول آخر); JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن جرير والقفال); QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام والطبرسي)-]
- 20:133/q06/p4 The earlier scriptures themselves, especially the Torah, witness to Muhammad. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya singles out the Torah as the greatest witness. Al-Qurṭubī says the books are a greater sign since Muhammad related their contents; Mujāhid identifies the Torah and Gospel. [6 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 20:133/q07 How does the question's wording rebut the demand?  [أَوَلَمْ تَأْتِهِم بَيِّنَةُ · rhetoric]
turns on: The first point explains the interrogative and conjunction; the second explains why proof is the grammatical arrival.
- 20:133/q07/p1 The interrogative denies that proof failed to come; the conjunction links an omitted premise. — Bursevī reads an omitted preceding clause; al-Ālūsī supplies that other signs had come and this proof especially had come despite obstinate denial. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:133/q07/p2 Saying that the proof itself came marks it as distinct, original and self-sufficient. — Al-Ālūsī says the phrasing suits bayyina and makes denial impossible. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:133/q08 What does ṣuḥuf mean?  [ٱلصُّحُفِ · meaning]
- 20:133/q08/p1 Written pages, plural of ṣaḥīfa. — Bursevī also says separate alphabet letters were among pages sent down to Adam. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 20:133/q09 Which earlier scriptures are meant?  [ٱلصُّحُفِ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether “earlier” points to specific named books, the wider prior corpus, or an inward heavenly source.
- 20:133/q09/p1 The Torah, Gospel, Psalms and other earlier revealed books. — Al-Biqāʿī explicitly adds books of Abraham, Moses, Jesus and David, connecting the sūrah's Adam and Moses accounts to 20:9 and 20:115. Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī stress common beliefs and universal rules. [10 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAYDAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI]
- 20:133/q09/p2 The Torah and Gospel in particular. — Mujāhid gives this identification repeatedly; Yaḥyā cites 7:157 and says Qatāda's wider phrase amounts to the same answer. [7 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 20:133/q09/p3 The books or pages of Abraham and Moses. — Muqātil says these preceded Muhammad's book. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 20:133/q09/p4 Heavenly tablets and upper spirits containing certain truths, wisdom and knowledge. — Al-Qāshānī gives this inward referent. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:133/q10 How is the verb in awalam taʾtihim read?  [تَأْتِهِم · readings]
turns on: Whether agreement follows the feminine form of bayyina or its masculine sense, with distance and nonliteral gender also cited.
- 20:133/q10/p1 With feminine tāʾ, matching the feminine bayyina. — Al-Fārisī names Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr and Ḥafṣ; later lists add Medina/Basra, Abū Jaʿfar, Yaʿqūb, Qutayba, Ibn Jammāz and variant Ibn Wardān transmission. Al-Fārisī and others argue formal feminine agreement. [13 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(نافع وأبو عمرو وحفص عن عاصم); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر، نافع، أبو عمرو، يعقوب، حفص عن عاصم، قتيبة عن الكسائي); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المدينة والبصرة وحفص عن عاصم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع وأبو عمرو وحفص عن عاصم); TABRISI(أهل المدينة والبصرة وقتيبة وحفص); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(نافع وأبو عمرو وحفص عن عاصم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جعفر وشيبة ونافع وأبو عمرو ويعقوب وابن أبي إسحاق وحفص); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(نافع وأبو عمرو وحفص); SAMIN-DURR(نافع وأبو عمرو وحفص); NASHR(نافع والبصريان وابن جماز وحفص وابن وردان)]
- 20:133/q10/p2 With masculine yāʾ, treating bayyina by sense as bayān or burhān, or permitting nonagreement. — Al-Fārisī compares mawʿiẓa/waʿẓ and ṣayḥa/ṣawt. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says semantic agreement; al-Qurṭubī reports Abū ʿUbayd and Abū Ḥātim prefer it. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī cite the subject's separation from the verb; al-Samīn cites nonliteral femininity; Qirāʾāt-ER allows an implied other subject. Ibn Wardān's transmitters differ. [17 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(الباقون وعاصم برواية أبي بكر); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر، نافع، أبو عمرو، يعقوب، حفص عن عاصم، قتيبة عن الكسائي); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المدينة والبصرة وحفص عن عاصم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الباقون وأبو بكر عن عاصم); TABRISI(أهل المدينة والبصرة وقتيبة وحفص); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي وأبو بكر عن عاصم); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(نافع وأبو عمرو وحفص); SAMIN-DURR(نافع وأبو عمرو وحفص); NASHR(نافع والبصريان وابن جماز وحفص وابن وردان); ALUSI-FULL(أكثر السبعة وأبو بحرية وابن محيصن وطلحة وابن أبي ليلى وابن منذر وخلف وأبو عبيد وابن سعدان وابن عيسى وابن جبير الأنطاكي); QIRAAT-ER(Imp); QURTUBI-FULL(الباقون وأبو عبيد وأبو حاتم)+]

## 20:133/q11 How is bayyinatu mā read and construed?  [بَيِّنَةُ مَا · readings]
turns on: Case and tanwīn of bayyina change whether mā is genitive, appositive, subject, an omitted predicate, or negation.
- 20:133/q11/p1 Bayyinatu mā is a nominative construct phrase. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Samīn report the majority or general reading; Abū Ḥayyān likewise calls it the majority reading. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]
- 20:133/q11/p2 Bayyinatun is nominative with tanwīn, and mā is appositional. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Ālūsī give apposition; al-Samīn reports Abū Zayd from Abū ʿAmr and permits this construction; Abū Ḥayyān reports a group. [6 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي والنحاس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو فيما رواه أبو زيد); ALUSI-FULL(أبو زيد عن أبي عمرو)]
- 20:133/q11/p3 Bayyinatun is nominative with tanwīn, and mā is subject or predicate of an omitted subject. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya takes mā as subject of taʾtī; al-Samīn allows mā as predicate of an omitted subject. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو فيما رواه أبو زيد)]
- 20:133/q11/p4 Bayyinatun has tanwīn and mā is negative, pointing to Quranic matter absent from earlier books. — Al-Samīn raises the possibility; the author of al-Lawāmiḥ applies it to abrogation and decisive rulings or merit. Al-Ālūsī rejects the proposal. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(صاحب اللوامح); ALUSI-FULL(صاحب اللوامح)-]
- 20:133/q11/p5 Bayyinatan is accusative with tanwīn as a circumstantial accusative, with mā the subject. — Al-Naḥḥās says this means earlier-scripture content came manifestly; al-Samīn notes feminine agreement by sense; al-Ālūsī reports the group reading. [5 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي والنحاس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(جماعة); ALUSI-FULL(فرقة)]

## 20:133/q12 How is the ḥāʾ of al-ṣuḥuf read?  [ٱلصُّحُفِ · readings]
turns on: Whether the ḥāʾ is rounded or still.
- 20:133/q12/p1 With ḍamma, the majority reading. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya contrasts it with a group reading sukūn; Abū Ḥayyān also identifies the majority. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 20:133/q12/p2 With sukūn or a lightened consonant. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports a group; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī attribute it to Ibn ʿAbbās, the latter saying it eases pronunciation. Al-Samīn's note says “ḥāʾ of bayyina” while giving the same still-ḥāʾ report. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 20:133/q13 What does the answer imply about divine guidance and people's excuse?  [* · theology]
turns on: The notes locate the decisive factor in the provision of an effective warning, foreknown response, or God's willing belief.
- 20:133/q13/p1 Sending messengers and proof removes people's excuse, showing an obligation of divine luṭf. — Al-Jishumī argues that without a messenger people would have a case against God; he rejects determinism because it would make messengers and admonition useless and give an even stronger excuse. Bursevī says the Qur'anic proof removes excuse for neglecting revealed laws. [3 notes: JISHUMI; BURSEVI]
- 20:133/q13/p2 Messengers provide reflectable signs and rational support; people follow the guidance or straying foreknown for them. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says signs remain open to reflection rather than overwhelming compulsion. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 20:133/q13/p3 Even every sign would fail without God's will for the hearers' belief. — Al-Qushayrī says more signs would then increase tyranny, disbelief and loss. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 20:133/q14 How does this verse connect to its sūrah and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Different notes connect the verse to its sūrah, another Qur'anic argument, and the following verse.
- 20:133/q14/p1 It caps the sūrah's stories and admonitions by exposing Quraysh's continued refusal. — Al-Biqāʿī connects ancient peoples, answers to Jewish challenges, wisdom and piety, and the stories of Adam and Moses at 20:9 and 20:115. Asad describes shared prophetic guidance and Moses' story as the sūrah's main theme, citing 20:9, 20:98 and 20:133. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:133/q14/p2 Its answer parallels 29:50–51, where the recited Qur'an suffices as mercy and reminder. — Ibn Kathīr cites both verses. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 20:133/q14/p3 The next verse develops how the Qur'anic proof removes excuse for abandoning revealed law. — Bursevī points to 20:135. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 20:133/q15 What name origins for prophets and their kin are recorded alongside this passage?  [* · reports]
- 20:133/q15/p1 The names are explained through their bearers' origins, experiences or qualities. — Muqātil derives Adam from earth's surface, Ḥawwāʾ from a living being, Noah from lament, Abraham from father of nations or a merciful father, Isaac from Sarah's laughter, Jacob from grasping Esau's heel, Joseph from increased beauty, and Yaḥyā from life granted to elderly barren parents. [3 notes: MUQATIL]

## 20:133/q16 What dreams about Muhammad and his community are reported alongside this passage?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports describe distinct dreams.
- 20:133/q16/p1 Gabriel promised him his community on Judgment Day and more if he asked. — Muqātil reports that the Prophet rejoiced. [1 notes: MUQATIL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 20:133/q16/p2 Gabriel said Paradise longed for him and his community. — Muqātil reports that the Prophet laughed joyfully. [1 notes: MUQATIL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 20:133/q16/p3 His community carried rods of light reaching east to west. — Gabriel identified the rods as Islam in Muqātil's report. [1 notes: MUQATIL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 20:133/q16/p4 He saw Paradise's pearl and ruby palaces prepared for him and his community. — Muqātil says they were adorned two thousand years before his creation. [1 notes: MUQATIL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 20:133/q17 What does the cited work say about anomalous readings and its own method?  [* · sciences]
- 20:133/q17/p1 Ibn Jinnī defends anomalous readings as strong and divinely intended for acceptance and application. — The introduction says they are not used in recitation; it explains the book's motive as predecessors' lack of a dedicated defense. Al-Sharīf al-Raḍī reports Abū al-Fatḥ wrote such a book late in life. It follows al-Ḥujja, identifies each reader, and uses linguistic witnesses, analogy, dialect and interpretation; the title al-Muḥtasab is linked to his afterlife concern. [6 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(الشريف الرضي)]

## 20:133/q18 What principles of Qur'anic rhetoric are stated in the cited discussion?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether Arabic rhetorical styles account for the Qur'an's special construction.
- 20:133/q18/p1 Interpreters should preserve eloquence, composition, contextual fit and inimitability through Arabic rhetorical knowledge. — Al-Zarkashī names truth/metaphor and freedom from discord, points to Ibn al-Naqīb, Ḥāzim and al-Kashshāf; al-Sakkākī compares inimitability to felt metrical correctness or charm, developed through semantics and rhetoric; al-Zamakhsharī warns against deficient command of Arabic usage. [4 notes: BURHAN(الزمخشري,السكاكي)]
- 20:133/q18/p2 Some features are outside Qur'anic rhetoric because the Qur'an differs from Arab styles. — Al-Zarkashī reports Abū al-Ṭayyib's argument and says he will discuss it later. [1 notes: BURHAN(أبو الطيب)]

## 20:133/q19 Why did early scholars leave Qur'anic rhetoric largely undiscussed?  [* · rhetoric]
- 20:133/q19/p1 Revelation's teaching centered on law, ordinances and faith; eloquence served as a miracle and early scholars already knew Arabic style. — Al-Zarkashī says rhetorical knowledge, unlike legal derivation, needed no explanation and was not itself the intended teaching. [2 notes: BURHAN]
