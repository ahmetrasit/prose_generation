# 74:54: 9 questions

## 74:54/q01 What does kallā do here?  [كَلَّآ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether kallā is taken as an affirmative particle or as a rebuke, and, for the rebuke, whether its immediate target is neglect of the reminder or the demand for individual scriptures.
- 74:54/q01/p1 It rebukes their turning away from and neglect of the reminder. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the repeated rebuke introduces the whole statement and exchange as a reminder. Kashshāf says the reminder itself rebukes their rejection of admonition. Abū Ḥayyān says they neglect it; Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Būrsawī, and Ālūsī likewise identify their turning away as the target. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 74:54/q01/p2 It rejects their demand for individual scriptures and their claims about what should happen. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports the alternative that matters are not as they wish and claim. Abū Ḥayyān specifies their demand for scriptures and says their true motive is lack of fear of the Hereafter. Ibn ʿĀshūr says no individual scrolls will come because the Qurʾān already admonishes them. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it a second rebuke refusing individual books. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 74:54/q01/p3 It means 'truly' or 'indeed,' affirming that the Qurʾān is a reminder. — Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī give the affirmative sense; Ṭabrisī applies it to the Qurʾān's being an admonition. Study Quran reports Q's judgment that this rendering suits verse 54 better than verse 53. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; STUDYQURAN(Q)]

## 74:54/q02 What is called a reminder by innahu tadhkira?  [إِنَّهُۥ تَذْكِرَةٌۭ · referent]
turns on: The antecedent and scope of the pronoun: the Qurʾān, the earlier reminder, revelation, or the whole discourse.
- 74:54/q02/p1 The Qurʾān, including its verses, is the reminder and admonition. — Qatāda identifies it as the Qurʾān in Ṭabarī and Durr, and Study Quran reports Ṭ's same gloss. Ṭabarī calls it God's reminder to creation against the allegation of transmitted magic or human speech; Wāḥidī al-Wajīz also rejects sorcery. Wāḥidī al-Basīṭ calls it admonition; Kuru Yolu specifies the Qurʾān's verses. Kashshāf, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, and Nasafī stress its power and sufficiency; Qurtubī says one may take heed, and Biqāʿī says it requires adherence and removes any excuse of lacking a guide. Ālūsī allows the Qurʾān as antecedent, while Būrsawī also offers it as an alternative to the prior reminder. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Qurʾān's identity explains the refusal of individual scrolls. [29 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(قتادة); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 74:54/q02/p2 The pronoun resumes the earlier tadhkira, the reminder from which they turn away in 74:49. — Kashshāf and Rāzī connect both this pronoun and the later 'remember it' pronoun to the reminder in 74:49. Ālūsī also offers the preceding reminder in the question about their turning away. Būrsawī offers tadhkira as the antecedent and explains it through its sense of remembrance. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 74:54/q02/p3 It refers to revelation. — Jishumī offers revelation as an alternative antecedent. Study Quran describes revelation as the reminder that reawakens knowledge of God and the primordial covenant, citing 7:172 and 30:30. [2 notes: JISHUMI; STUDYQURAN]
- 74:54/q02/p4 The entire preceding statement, explanation, and exchange is the reminder. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reads the repeated rebuke as asserting this broad scope. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 74:54/q03 How is the masculine pronoun in innahu explained beside tadhkira?  [إِنَّهُۥ تَذْكِرَةٌۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether agreement is explained by the masculine sense or expressed with a grammatically possible feminine pronoun.
- 74:54/q03/p1 The masculine form follows the sense of the antecedent as dhikr or Qurʾān, despite the feminine noun tadhkira. — Kashshāf and Rāzī apply this to both the present pronoun and the later pronoun in 'remember it.' Būrsawī says tadhkira may mean dhikr or Qurʾān and compares mawʿiẓa meaning admonition and ṣayḥa meaning sound. Ālūsī likewise points to the masculine gender of Qurʾān and dhikr. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 74:54/q03/p2 The feminine form innahā tadhkira would also be grammatical. — Farrāʾ compares the feminine wording in Sūrat ʿAbasa, 80:11. [1 notes: FARRA]

## 74:54/q04 What does calling it a reminder say about its effect and audience?  [تَذْكِرَةٌۭ · interpretation]
- 74:54/q04/p1 It is a powerful, sufficient admonition to heed and follow; people need no further guide, signs, or individual book. — Kashshāf says it itself rebukes their refusal of admonition. Rāzī says whoever wishes may keep it before his eyes for his own benefit. Qurtubī says whoever wishes may take heed; Biqāʿī says it demands following and leaves no excuse of lacking guidance. Būrsawī says it reminds people of truth, citing al-Burhān. Ālūsī calls it of great consequence. Ibn ʿĀshūr says its recitation suffices as mercy and reminder for believers, paralleling 29:50–51. Kuru Yolu says it is God's warning, that no further book will come, and that it suffices for happiness in this world and the next. Wāḥidī al-Basīṭ, Baghawī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, and others call it admonition or a powerful reminder. [16 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 74:54/q04/p2 It is God's reminder and warning to creation, contrary to claims that it is sorcery or human speech. — Ṭabarī names both allegations, transmitted magic and human speech. Wāḥidī al-Wajīz rejects sorcery. Kuru Yolu calls the verses God's warning; Būrsawī, citing al-Burhān, says they remind people of truth. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 74:54/q04/p3 Revelation reawakens innate knowledge of God and the primordial covenant. — Study Quran contrasts reminder with a list of demanded proofs and cites the covenant in 7:172 and primordial nature in 30:30. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 74:54/q04/p4 Its admonition is made effective through the recompense prepared for obedience and disobedience. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects the Qurʾān's warning to the prepared outcomes for both groups. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 74:54/q05 What does the indefiniteness of tadhkira convey?  [تَذْكِرَةٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 74:54/q05/p1 It magnifies the reminder as powerful and of great consequence. — Būrsawī takes the indefinite form to signal a powerful, sufficient reminder. Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise says the indefinite noun magnifies it. [2 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:54/q06 Why is the feminine form tadhkira used?  [تَذْكِرَةٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 74:54/q06/p1 Its feminine ending suits the verse ending and rhyme. — Burhān says the Qurʾān may use a feminine form where a masculine form might otherwise be expected to suit the verse ending, citing this phrase. Būrsawī reports al-Burhān's explanation that this word was chosen for the rhyme ending. [2 notes: BURHAN; BURSEVI]

## 74:54/q07 How does this declaration answer the demand for further scripture or signs?  [* · links]
- 74:54/q07/p1 The Qurʾān already provides the needed reminder, so the demand for individual scrolls or additional proofs is refused. — Abū Ḥayyān says their demand masks failure to fear the Hereafter. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the statement explains the refusal of scrolls and parallels the answer to sign seekers in 29:50–51, where recited Qurʾān suffices as mercy and reminder. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the second rebuke refuses personal books. Kuru Yolu says no further book will be sent and this one suffices for happiness here and in the afterlife. Study Quran contrasts revelation's reminder with a list of demanded proofs. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 74:54/q08 What nearby reading variants affect how the reminder is addressed?  [* · readings]
- 74:54/q08/p1 Nearby readings vary the person of address in fearing and remembering and whether letters are assimilated. — Abū Ḥayyān reports Abū Ḥaywa's second-person reading of the verb 'fear,' a shift to direct address. He also reports readings by Nāfiʿ, Sallām, Yaʿqūb, Abū Jaʿfar, al-Aʿmash, Ṭalḥa, ʿĪsā, al-Aʿraj, and Abū Ḥaywa varying listener address versus third person in reminder and remembering forms and assimilation of letters. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حيوة,نافع وسلام ويعقوب وأبو جعفر والأعمش وطلحة وعيسى والأعرج وأبو حيوة)]

## 74:54/q09 What reward report is given for reciting this sūra?  [* · reports]
- 74:54/q09/p1 A report promises ten good deeds per person in Mecca, believer or unbeliever, to one who recites al-Muddaththir. — Kashshāf attributes the promise to the Prophet and states no grading; the count includes each person in Mecca who believed in Muhammad or disbelieved in him. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله)]
