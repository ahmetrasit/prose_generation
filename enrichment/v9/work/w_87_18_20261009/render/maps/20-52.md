# 20:52: 20 questions

## 20:52/q01 What was Pharaoh asking about the earlier generations, and why?  [* · links]
turns on: The reported readings differ over whether Pharaoh sought history, resurrection, fate, a challenge to omniscience, or a distraction from Moses' proof.
- 20:52/q01/p1 He challenged why earlier peoples had not recognized or worshipped the Creator despite the evident proof, or had denied revelation. — Al-Tabari says Pharaoh pressed the implication of Moses' description of the creator and sustainer. Al-Alusi reports variants focused on failure to infer the Creator from creation or guidance, and on denial of revelation followed by punishment; the answer refers the matter to divine knowledge or decree. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 20:52/q01/p2 He diverted Moses from a persuasive argument by raising ancestral history or fate. — Razi prefers fear that Moses would expose Pharaoh publicly. Al-Biqa'i says the discussion was unproductive. Ibn Ashur compares the appeal to ancestors in 10:78 and says either answer on their punishment could alienate descendants or aid Pharaoh; Moses' answer frustrates the diversion. Kuranyolu reports Razi's reading. Al-Tabataba'i rejects the diversion-and-history account as disconnected from the preceding argument; al-Burhan describes the tactic of turning a bigoted opponent to another subject before returning to the premise. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(فرعون); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); BURHAN; RAZI-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 20:52/q01/p3 He asked about the prior nations' resurrection or the time of their raising. — Al-Tabrisi connects it to Moses' call to believe in resurrection. Ibn al-Jawzi and Abu Hayyan report that this reading takes the pronoun in 'their knowledge' to refer to the Resurrection. Al-Alusi dismisses this proposal, especially that pronoun reference; al-Hasan's gloss of the time of resurrection presupposes it. [3 notes: TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]
- 20:52/q01/p4 He asked the destiny, happiness, or misery of earlier people, perhaps suggesting the powerful escape consequences. — Al-Alusi reports a link to 'peace upon whoever follows guidance' in 20:47 but rejects it because the general rule of salvation and punishment was already stated. Ibn Ashur says discussion of creation may prompt the dead's fate. Asad stresses that only God can assess motives and spiritual merits; Kuranyolu suggests Pharaoh implied powerful people escape consequences. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:52/q01/p5 He challenged whether God could know all the details of ancient multitudes and assign their forms and properties. — Baydawi links the challenge to God's power over particulars. Al-Alusi reports a test using generations Pharaoh knew, or a quick trap that avoided a broad answer, but calls this interpretation remote; he says naming all things would strengthen the supposed trap. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:52/q01/p6 He tested whether Moses was a prophet or only a teller of old stories. — Al-Alusi transmits this as another proposed motive. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 20:52/q01/p7 He had heard the believer of Pharaoh's family warn of earlier nations and wanted their histories. — Ibn al-Anbari reports Muqatil and cites 40:31; al-Naqqash cites the warning of the confederates in 40:30. Both say Moses had not yet received the Torah. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); ALUSI-FULL(النقاش)]

## 20:52/q02 To what does the pronoun in 'its knowledge' refer?  [عِلْمُهَا · referent]
turns on: Whether Pharaoh's preceding question concerns the past generations themselves or their resurrection.
- 20:52/q02/p1 The earlier generations and their affairs. — Abu Hayyan calls this apparent. Al-Samin prefers it. Ibn Ashur explains that the collective noun 'generations' can take a feminine pronoun and is the object of the verbal noun in the possessive construction. The answer embraces their persons, deeds, and circumstances. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 20:52/q02/p2 The Resurrection or Day of Judgment implied by Pharaoh's question. — Ibn al-Jawzi and Abu Hayyan transmit the alternative. Al-Samin calls it second to the reference to the generations. Al-Alusi particularly dismisses this pronoun reference. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]

## 20:52/q03 Why does Moses leave this knowledge with his Lord?  [عِلْمُهَا عِندَ رَبِّى · interpretation]
turns on: The notes emphasize either Moses' limited revelation and refusal of a fruitless inquiry, or the substantive assurance of divine knowledge and judgment; both can coexist.
- 20:52/q03/p1 The unseen details belong to God; Moses knows only what revelation has taught him and keeps to his mission. — Al-Tabari says he did not know why some strayed. Al-Tha'labi, Muqatil through al-Mawardi and Ibn al-Anbari, al-Baghawi, and al-Biqa'i say the Torah telling their histories came only after Pharaoh perished. Al-Zamakhshari, Baydawi, Nasafi, Bursevi, and Alusi distinguish a prophet's mission from unrestricted unseen knowledge. Ibn Ashur says Moses knew their general circumstances, not each person's details; he compares the Prophet's answer concerning polytheists' children. Study Quran calls the matter unfathomable. [18 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); BAGHAWI-FULL(unnamed view); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(النقاش); IBNASHUR-FULL(محمد); STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
- 20:52/q03/p2 The reply affirms that God fully knows the earlier peoples and will judge their deeds. — Abu Ishaq says their deeds are preserved for repayment. Al-Baghawi, al-Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Ibn Kathir, al-Tabataba'i, and Islahi emphasize full knowledge, record, and recompense. Baydawi says the answer includes all people's parts and states. Asad says God alone can assess inner motives and errors. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES]

## 20:52/q04 How are 'its knowledge,' 'with my Lord,' and 'in a book' parsed?  [عِلْمُهَا عِندَ رَبِّى فِى كِتَٰبٍ · grammar]
turns on: Which phrase functions as predicate, whether a circumstance may precede an implied governor, and whether two independently predicative phrases can form one predicate.
- 20:52/q04/p1 ‘With my Lord’ is the first predicate and ‘in a book’ the second, or is attached to the first predicate's implied existential verb. — Al-Samin also permits 'in a book' as a circumstantial modifier of the hidden subject. Al-Alusi prefers the two-predicate analysis and says a verbal noun cannot govern across its predicate. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:52/q04/p2 ‘In a book’ is the predicate, while ‘with my Lord’ attaches to its implied verb or serves as a circumstantial phrase or modifier. — Al-Tabrisi gives this as a principal analysis and mentions other attachments. Al-Samin allows 'with my Lord' to modify the knowledge or the genitive pronoun; al-Akhfash supports fronting the circumstance with 39:67 and poetry, and other grammarians readily front an adverbial circumstance. Al-Samin rejects making 'in a book' modify 'knowledge' once 'with my Lord' has become the predicate, because the verbal noun and its complement cannot be split. [5 notes: TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش,بعض النحويين); SAMIN-DURR-]
- 20:52/q04/p3 The two prepositional phrases together form one semantic predicate. — Abu al-Baqa compares the combination to 'sweet and sour.' Al-Samin objects that each phrase can predicate independently, unlike that example. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR-]

## 20:52/q05 What is the book containing this knowledge?  [كِتَٰبٍ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'book' denotes a real heavenly or angelic record, or figures the permanence and certainty of God's knowledge.
- 20:52/q05/p1 The Preserved Tablet or Preserved Record. — Muqatil, al-Tabari, al-Tha'labi, al-Kalbi, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, al-Zamakhshari, al-Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Ibn Kathir, al-Biqa'i, Bursevi, and Study Quran identify it thus; Study Quran cites 85:22. Ibn Atiyya allows this alongside an angelic record. Baydawi and Alusi present it as one possibility; Alusi says the actual inscriptions stand for recorded knowledge. [18 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:52/q05/p2 A record written or kept by angels of people's deeds and circumstances. — Qatada speaks of deeds and lifespans. Ibn Atiyya permits the record angels wrote; al-Tabrisi reports this view, and al-Qurtubi says a book may be with one angel. Al-Biqa'i notes angels assigned to recording servants' affairs. Ibn Kathir mentions the record of deeds alongside the Preserved Tablet. [6 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:52/q05/p3 A metaphor or simile for God's firm, detailed, permanent knowledge, like an established written register. — Razi offers this second interpretation and argues the following denial of error and forgetfulness supports it; he objects that a literal book could suggest to Pharaoh that God needs it. Baydawi likens it to a scholar's written information. Alusi says an ordinary register used as simile may be preferable. Ibn Ashur adds the possibility that writing conveys certainty and cites al-Harith ibn Hilliza's verse. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]

## 20:52/q06 Why is knowledge said to be in a book?  [فِى كِتَٰبٍ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes distinguish the record's benefit to angels, its honor and judgment, and its assurance of precision without divine dependence on writing.
- 20:52/q06/p1 The record displays God's knowledge and decree to angels so they can recognize its accuracy in due time. — Al-Mawardi says angels learn at the appointed time. Razi says they gain a further proof of God's knowledge and freedom from heedlessness, though he objects that speaking of a book can wrongly suggest divine need. Nasafi, al-Biqa'i, Bursevi, Abu Hayyan reporting Razi, and Alusi similarly describe angels witnessing events agree with the record. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]
- 20:52/q06/p2 It honors the people concerned and records a judgment concerning them. — Al-Mawardi reports this alternative. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 20:52/q06/p3 It secures or illustrates an exact and enduring record for recompense, without implying that God needs a memory aid. — Al-Mawardi explicitly denies that God writes from fear of error or forgetting. Al-Biqa'i invokes familiar human record keeping. Al-Tabataba'i says the record confirms unchanging precision; Islahi says every small and great detail is kept, citing 18:49. Ibn Ashur says writing suggests established certainty; Razi and Alusi stress that knowledge does not fade. Abu al-Malih and Qatada cite this verse in defense of believing in the Book. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو المليح,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:52/q07 What does the indefinite 'a book' convey?  [كِتَٰبٍ · rhetoric]
- 20:52/q07/p1 It magnifies the record's immense scope and exact detail. — Al-Tabataba'i says indefiniteness conveys vastness and precision. Islahi says this is no ordinary register, preserving every detail as 18:49 illustrates. [2 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:52/q08 What does 'my Lord does not stray' mean?  [لَّا يَضِلُّ رَبِّى · meaning]
turns on: The verb's range covers mistake, absence or loss, neglect, and a reported sense of perishing; proposed objects include the book, its contents, the generations, and the resurrection time.
- 20:52/q08/p1 God does not err or mistake anything in knowledge or judgment. — Ibn Abbas, al-Tabari, al-Tha'labi, al-Kalbi, al-Farra as understood by al-Wahidi, al-Tabrisi, al-Nasafi, Ibn Ashur, and others gloss error. Al-Tabari and Ibn Jarir through Razi and Abu Hayyan specify that God never judges a wrong act right; hastening or delaying punishment is wise. Baydawi and Bursevi define error as missing an object's place, impossible for the One who knows essentially. Nasafi applies it to felicity and misery. Al-Biqa'i contrasts fallible makers and argues that provisions fitting all creatures prove God's precision. [25 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن جرير); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جرير); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:52/q08/p2 Nothing escapes, goes missing from, or is absent from God's knowledge. — Al-Wahidi and Abu Amr derive loss from absence; Ibn al-A'rabi, Ibn Isa, Abu Hayyan, al-Tabrisi, al-Qurtubi, and Ibn Kathir use the same sense. Al-Zajjaj permits God not losing track of the generations. Al-Suddi adds no heedlessness or neglect. Al-Qurtubi says al-Zajjaj and al-Nahhas favor an explanation in which God needs no book; he relates it to Ibn al-A'rabi's view. Arabic analogies include failing to find a fixed home or road versus a wandering animal: al-Farra, Abu Ishaq, Abu Amr, al-Rummani, Ibn al-Sikkit, and Ibn Arafa distinguish forms and objects; al-Zajjaj notes two vowelings. Ibn Atiyya cites the man who asked to be burned after death using 'perhaps I will be lost to God.' [26 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو عمرو,الفراء); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(أبو عمرو); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الأعرابي,ابن عرفة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عيسى,الزجاج,السدي,الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(الرماني,الفراء); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن السكيت); TABATABAI; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج، النحاس)+]
- 20:52/q08/p3 God neither loses the book nor forgets what it contains. — Ibn Abbas as reported by al-Mawardi, Qatada in al-Durr, Muqatil in Razi and Abu Hayyan, and Abu Ishaq in al-Wahidi construe an implied book object. Al-Zajjaj allows this. Abu Ishaq's damma reading expresses not finding God straying from the record. Ibn al-Anbari rejects the gloss 'the book does not stray from Him' because Arabic cannot simply omit 'from,' likening it to 'the dirham fell your sleeve.' [8 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ(أبو إسحاق); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); RAZI-FULL(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل); DURR-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري)-]
- 20:52/q08/p4 God does not lose sight of the earlier generations themselves or their affairs. — Al-Zajjaj and Abu Ishaq allow the generations rather than the book as the object; the phrase assures that vanished people are not beyond God. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); TABATABAI]
- 20:52/q08/p5 God does not neglect what is due, leaving no disbeliever unpunished or believer unrewarded. — Al-Suddi says nothing is overlooked or left. Ibn Abbas is reported by al-Zamakhshari, Abu Hayyan, and Alusi. Al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, and al-Tabrisi likewise connect this to recompense. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(unnamed view); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(السدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 20:52/q08/p6 God does not err concerning the time of resurrection. — Al-Hasan's reported gloss presumes that Pharaoh asked about resurrection. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 20:52/q08/p7 The verb means that God does not perish. — Al-Qurtubi reports al-Zajjaj's view and cites 32:10 for the sense; the clause is then a new sentence after 'in a book.' [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج)]

## 20:52/q09 What does 'and does not forget' mean?  [وَلَا يَنسَى · meaning]
turns on: Whether the subject is God or the book, and whether 'forgetting' denotes loss of knowledge or neglect of due action.
- 20:52/q09/p1 God never loses what He knows, whether earlier people's deeds, the record's contents, or the time of resurrection. — Al-Hasan and Qatada say nothing written is lost or forgotten. Baydawi and Bursevi distinguish forgetting as knowledge leaving awareness. Al-Qaffal stresses the permanence of knowledge; al-Tabataba'i says forgetting is loss after knowing. Muqatil focuses on the book's contents, al-Hasan on the resurrection time, and Ibn Ashur on recall. Ibn Kathir contrasts human forgetfulness with divine knowledge. [24 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن,قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الحسن,مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن,القفال,مقاتل); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(القفال وبعض المحققين)+; RAZI-FULL(القفال)+; TABATABAI+]
- 20:52/q09/p2 God does not fail to perform a right act or omit just recompense. — Al-Tabari interprets forgetting as leaving a wise act undone. Al-Mawardi reports that He does not leave the matter; al-Wahidi and al-Baghawi specify punishment for unbelievers and reward for monotheists or believers. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(unnamed view); NASAFI-FULL]
- 20:52/q09/p3 The book itself 'does not forget' metaphorically: it omits nothing. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan prefer God as the apparent subject but permit the book metaphor, comparing 18:49. Al-Samin explicitly gives the book as an alternative subject; Alusi reports that it leaves nothing uncounted. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 20:52/q10 Do 'does not stray' and 'does not forget' say the same thing or distinguish kinds of knowledge?  [لَّا يَضِلُّ رَبِّى وَلَا يَنسَى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the pair is synonymous, separates the scope from the permanence of knowledge, separates two forms of later loss, or contrasts error in judgment with forgetting.
- 20:52/q10/p1 They express one meaning: God neither errs nor forgets, and nothing escapes Him. — Mujahid is transmitted by al-Tabari, al-Tha'labi, al-Wahidi, Razi, Abu Hayyan, and al-Durr. Al-Farra and Abu Ishaq gloss straying as forgetting. Al-Alusi says Mujahid's synonymy is unsound. [8 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); FARRA; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد)-]
- 20:52/q10/p2 The first denies anything being outside God's knowledge; the second denies losing knowledge once possessed. — Al-Qaffal says the two jointly affirm universality and permanence without change. Razi prefers and Alusi approves this distinction. Abu Hayyan transmits it. Al-Tabataba'i reports an initial-ignorance versus subsequent-ignorance distinction but prefers both verbs as kinds of loss after knowledge. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(الأكثرون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القفال); TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(القفال وبعض المحققين)+; RAZI-FULL(القفال)+; TABATABAI-]
- 20:52/q10/p3 Both deny loss after knowledge: straying misplaces what is known, while forgetting removes it from awareness. — Al-Tabataba'i prefers this against the split between initial ignorance and ignorance after knowing; the clauses together make future ignorance and confusion in recompense impossible. [2 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 20:52/q10/p4 The first concerns error in divine governance, and the second failure to recall or carry out what is right. — Al-Tabari says punishment now or at Judgment is wise, and no wise act is left undone. Ibn Jarir, as transmitted by Razi and Abu Hayyan, similarly distinguishes wrongful judgment from forgetting what is known. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL(ابن جرير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جرير)]

## 20:52/q11 How is 'my Lord does not stray or forget' attached and parsed?  [لَّا يَضِلُّ رَبِّى وَلَا يَنسَى · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clauses stand independently, modify 'book' with a missing object, or invert the grammatical roles of book and Lord.
- 20:52/q11/p1 It is an independent statement about God, with God the subject of both verbs. — Ibn Atiyya reports a fresh sentence; Abu Hayyan calls it the apparent syntax. Al-Samin and al-Qurtubi report it; the latter notes a pause after 'in a book.' Alusi says the verbs may be intransitive or have an omitted object and objects to strained alternatives. Al-Tabrisi permits an omitted 'from it,' as with omitted 'in it' in 2:48. Al-Farra supplies an omitted pronoun object in the coordination and calls 'my Lord' nominative. [8 notes: FARRA; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:52/q11/p2 The clauses qualify 'book': a book God does not lose or forget, with an omitted return pronoun. — Al-Qurtubi calls this a fifth grammatical view and says there is no pause after 'book.' Al-Samin specifies a genitive adjective. Ibn Atiyya reports the written record never lost from God. Alusi reports the same analysis; al-Wahidi transmits Ibn al-Anbari's objection that omitting 'from' is ungrammatical. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:52/q11/p3 The book is the subject of 'strays' and 'my Lord' is an honorific accusative: it cannot lead God astray. — Al-Samin reports this parsing and, separately, a damma form where none leads God from knowledge of the book. Alusi reports Abu al-Baqa's object parsing. Alusi regards such alternatives as strained. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 20:52/q12 What readings of 'does not stray or forget' are reported?  [يَضِلُّ يَنسَى · readings]
turns on: The reported vowels change voice and causative force, and some analyses change whether 'my Lord' is subject or object.
- 20:52/q12/p1 The reading with open yāʾ and broken ḍād means the Lord does not stray or err. — Abu Ishaq explains the open-yāʾ reading as 'He does not stray from my Lord.' Ibn Atiyya describes it as a new sentence exalting God above confusion and forgetting. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]
- 20:52/q12/p2 A reading with rounded yāʾ and broken ḍād gives a causative sense: God does not let the matter or book be lost. — Ibn al-Jawzi names Abd Allah ibn Amr, Asim al-Jahdari, Qatada, and Ibn Muhaysin. Al-Qurtubi adds al-Hasan, Isa ibn Umar, and Ibn Kathir through Shibl; Abu Hayyan and al-Samin also name al-Jahdari, Hammad ibn Salama, and Isa al-Thaqafi. Al-Zamakhshari and Alusi report the causative meaning; al-Zajjaj says God is not found straying from the record. Ibn Arafa's distinction between wrong path and causing loss supports it. Alusi reports Abu al-Baqa's subject and object alternatives. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ(أبو إسحاق); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عبد الله بن عمرو وعاصم الجحدري وقتادة وابن محيصن); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عرفة,الحسن، قتادة، عيسى بن عمر، ابن محيصن، عاصم الجحدري، ابن كثير عن شبل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن، قتادة، الجحدري، حماد بن سلمة، ابن محيصن، وعيسى الثقفي); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن وقتادة والجحدري وعيسى الثقفي وابن محيصن وحماد بن سلمة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء,الحسن وقتادة والجحدري وحماد بن سلمة وابن محيصن وعيسى الثقفي)]
- 20:52/q12/p3 A reading has rounded yāʾ and open ḍād. — Ibn al-Jawzi names Abu al-Mutawakkil and Ibn al-Sumayfa' as its readers. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو المتوكل وابن السميفع)]
- 20:52/q12/p4 Al-Sulami reads both verbs passively. — Abu Hayyan says the passive denies error and forgetfulness of God; Alusi says the agent is unspecified. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السلمي); ALUSI-FULL(السلمي)]

## 20:52/q13 Why say 'my Lord' here and repeat it?  [رَبِّى · rhetoric]
turns on: The explanations focus on theological implication of lordship or the relation and parallelism in Moses' phrasing.
- 20:52/q13/p1 Calling Him Lord grounds perfect knowledge in His ownership and governance. — Al-Tabataba'i argues that an owned, governed thing cannot be present through God yet absent from His knowledge. Alusi says repeating the divine title emphasizes that lordship entails freedom from error and forgetting and gives pleasure in naming God. [3 notes: TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:52/q13/p2 ‘My Lord’ directly expresses Moses' relation to the generations in his answer, and the second occurrence parallels the first. — Al-Tabataba'i says 'our Lord' would express that relation less directly and doubts that either occurrence clearly counts as replacing a pronoun with an explicit noun, since the clauses are separated. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:52/q14 What does this answer imply about recompense?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:52/q14/p1 Every deed remains known and recorded, so God will give its due reward or punishment. — Al-Wahidi connects deeds and their recompense to 2:134 and applies the rule to Moses' hearers. Al-Kalbi says good receives good and evil evil. Al-Baghawi, al-Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi, and Ibn Kathir make the same inference; al-Tabataba'i says even vanished people's persons and deeds remain distinguishable. Islahi says Moses leaves accountability implicit because insinuation can say more. Asad says God knows motives and spiritual merits. [19 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(unnamed view); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(أبو مسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 20:52/q15 How does the final clause complete the answer and connect to its setting?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources relate the verse to the lost traces of earlier people, God's reason for recording, the prophetic exchange, and the following discourse.
- 20:52/q15/p1 It answers the objection that vanished generations and erased traces could be confused or forgotten. — Al-Tabataba'i says the clause stands without a conjunction as an answer to the anticipated objection; no person's deeds are lost, altered, or confused. Ibn Ashur says Moses keeps to the living mission while giving an answer fitting Pharaoh's attempted diversion. [3 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:52/q15/p2 It rules out the thought that God writes to compensate for a deficient memory. — Al-Mawardi, Razi, al-Biqa'i, Bursevi, and Alusi make this implication explicit; Razi argues that the verse's next words favor understanding the book as a comparison for enduring knowledge. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:52/q15/p3 The dialogue closes here, with verses 53–55 turning into direct address to humanity. — Asad reports Razi's view of the discourse transition. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Razi)]
- 20:52/q15/p4 Moses' inability to detail past peoples rebukes demands that every prophet answer every question. — Al-Biqa'i says Jews applying such a test would have to doubt Moses himself. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:52/q15/p5 Pharaoh's word for the generations' state carries a rhetorical nuance in the preceding question. — Ibn Ashur says bāl means an important condition (47:2) or a thought or opinion. Al-Burhan says the Moses account's calling and intimate speech both convey nearness despite their normal distant and near uses. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN]

## 20:52/q16 What does the verse suggest about writing and preserving knowledge?  [فِى كِتَٰبٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether writing protects knowledge or risks confusion with Qur'an and neglect of memory and action; some combined memorizing and writing.
- 20:52/q16/p1 Writing knowledge is beneficial and generally preferable, especially as memory and transmission chains weaken. — Al-Qurtubi says writing aids study, review, memorization, discussion, comparison, questions, transmitter checking, and confidence; elapsed time, longer chains, and varied routes increase its value. He cites 7:145, 21:105, 7:156, 54:52–53, a Prophet's instruction to bind knowledge by writing, Qatada's appeal to God's writing, the divine book of mercy reported by Abu Hurayra in Muslim, the Prophet's direction to an Ansari to use his right hand, and the sermon written for Abu Shah. Muawiya ibn Qurra says unwritten knowledge has not been secured. [10 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي,قتادة,معاوية بن قرة); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 20:52/q16/p2 Early authorities avoided writing hadith lest it resemble Qur'an or weaken memorization and practice. — Abu Sa'id discouraged turning hadith into another Qur'an; al-Sha'bi, Yunus ibn Ubayd, Khalid al-Hadhdha, Ibn Awn, and al-Zuhri disapproved. Early proximity and short chains explain the practice, al-Qurtubi says. A report from Abu Sa'id attributes a writing ban and erasure order to the Prophet; al-Qurtubi says it was early and superseded, also preventing confusion with Qur'an. He conditionally dates another refusal before the Hijra. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سعيد,الشعبي، يونس بن عبيد، خالد الحذاء، ابن عون، الزهري,النبي); QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 20:52/q16/p3 Some wrote temporarily, erased after memorizing, or memorized first and wrote later as insurance. — Khalid erased his sole written hadith; Ibn Sirin and Asim ibn Damra are named among erasers. Hisham ibn Hassan erased the al-A'maq report about Romans descending at al-A'maq or Dabiq before the Hour. Al-A'mash, Abd Allah ibn Idris, and Hushaym memorized before writing. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الأعمش، عبد الله بن إدريس، هشيم,خالد الحذاء,محمد بن سيرين، عاصم بن ضمرة,هشام بن حسان)]

## 20:52/q17 What writing materials and marks did scholars commend?  [كِتَٰبٍ · reports]
- 20:52/q17/p1 Durable black ink protects a record and is a fitting adornment of scholarship. — Al-Khatib recommends lasting black ink rather than ordinary liquid ink. Al-Shafi'i calls ink on Ahmad's shirt a mark of dignity, dark to the eye and bright in insight. Khalid ibn Yazid compares it to bridal perfume; Abu Abd Allah al-Balawi's poem pairs men's ink with women's saffron. Al-Mawardi reports Abd Allah ibn Sulayman covering a yellow stain with ink; the accompanying poem calls saffron the fragrance of girls and inkwell ink that of men. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو بكر الخطيب,أبو عبد الله البلوي,الشاعر,الشافعي,الماوردي,خالد بن يزيد)]

## 20:52/q18 What authority does Arabic language and poetry have for explaining this wording?  [* · meaning]
- 20:52/q18/p1 Classical Arabic usage, grammar, and poetry help establish Qur'anic meanings and guard against error. — Umar calls pre-Islamic poetry the Arabs' register; Ibn Abbas advises resort to poetry and himself cites it. Al-Shafi'i praises Arabic scholars, Abd al-Malik calls Arabic visible refinement, and al-Wahidi says literature, meter, and grammar aid learning while ignorance of Arabic fuels innovations. Al-Shafi'i attributes much Iraqi heresy to ignorance of Arabic and dialects. The lexical distinction in this verse between a missing fixed place and a wandering animal illustrates the method. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الشافعي,عبد الملك بن مروان,عمر)]

## 20:52/q19 What makes a transmitted reading reliable?  [* · readings]
- 20:52/q19/p1 A qualified recitation imam combines transmitted readings with grammar, languages, meanings, and assessment of variants. — Ibn Mujahid calls differing Companion and Successor recitations a merciful breadth like legal disagreement and plans to describe imams, regional readings, agreements, and ranks. He says regional memorizers rely on qualified imams. A mere native grammarian cannot vary his idiom; a performer without grammar may forget vowel and parsing distinctions. A forgetful memorizer can misattribute and spread error; such a reading is no proof. A grammarian without transmission may invent an unattested, innovative reading. [8 notes: IBNMUJAHID]

## 20:52/q20 How should verbal ornament relate to meaning?  [* · rhetoric]
- 20:52/q20/p1 Rhyme, pun, and assonance should follow and serve a secure meaning, never obscure or damage it. — Al-Jurjani warns against seeing only outward beauty and compares overdecorated prose to a bride weighed down with jewelry. Al-Jahiz's opening of the Book of Animals chooses semantic fit over forced pairings such as doubt and perplexity or disagreement and fairness. The finest wordplay arises unforced or is chosen because it fits; al-Shafi'i's answer on nabidh exemplifies this. [4 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
