# 53:33: 12 questions

## 53:33/q01 How is the hamza in 'Have you seen' read?  [أَفَرَءَيْتَ · readings]
turns on: Different pronunciations of the hamza: elision, lengthening, or an intermediate realization.
- 53:33/q01/p1 The hamza of raʾā is omitted to ease pronunciation. — QIRAAT-ER says the grammar remains unchanged. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
- 53:33/q01/p2 The hamza is lengthened to ā by madd badal. — QIRAAT-ER says the resulting stretch gives the question rhetorical emphasis. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
- 53:33/q01/p3 The hamza is realized between a full glottal stop and omission. — QIRAAT-ER describes this as a dialectal variant. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 53:33/q02 What does 'Have you seen' mean and how is it construed?  [أَفَرَءَيْتَ · grammar]
- 53:33/q02/p1 It means 'tell me'; the relative expression is its first object and the following question is its second. — Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī give both objects. Al-Samīn also glosses the neighboring arāʾayta as 'tell me.' [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 53:33/q03 How does the opening fāʾ connect this question with what precedes?  [أَفَرَءَيْتَ · links]
- 53:33/q03/p1 It draws the question from the preceding teaching and makes the man's conduct startling in that light. — Rāzī relates it to God's knowledge, power, recompense, and people's need for the Prophet's guidance after 53:29–32. Ibn ʿĀshūr relates the astonishment to 53:31's promised recompense and the rule that each has only what he strives for; he also notes the intervening warning against pride in good deeds and others' praise. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the fāʾ branches from the preceding discussion. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 53:33/q04 Who is the one who turned away?  [ٱلَّذِى تَوَلَّىٰ · referent]

- 53:33/q04/p1 Al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra. — Muqātil, Mujāhid, Ibn Zayd, and others identify him. Accounts say he heard or followed the Prophet, sometimes with Abū Bakr; a pagan shamed him for leaving his fathers' religion and offered to carry his punishment for payment. He returned to shirk, paid only part, then withheld the rest. One report says he had pledged faith; others say only that he neared Islam. Study Quran reverses the payment direction, saying al-Walīd offered a follower money to return to Quraysh religion. Rāzī reports the identification but prefers a general referent. Islāḥī says the account lacks a sound chain and fits the verse poorly; Kuranyolu also disputes the reports. - 53:33/q04/p2 A representative person or class who turns from reminder, belief, or good works, rather than one necessarily named individual. — Rāzī connects the referent to the self-sufficient people of 53:29; Arabic can resume a general man in singular form, as in 28:84. ## 53:33/q05 What does 'turned away' mean here?  [تَوَلَّىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the omitted object of turning is faith or truth, remembrance, the Uḥud position, or charitable spending; the following verse supports the spending reading for Ṭabāṭabāʾī.
- 53:33/q05/p1 He turned away from truth, Islam, or following and remaining steadfast in it. — Muqātil, Ibn ʿAbbās, and others gloss it as leaving truth or faith. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says he turned from God's command. Abū Ḥayyān notes unqualified tawallā often metaphorically means not entering faith; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the man stopped considering Islam after nearly embracing it. Al-Thaʿlabī's account says he returned from belief; Biqāʿī broadens the wording to apostates, hypocrites, and backsliders. [17 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; QUSHAYRI+]
- 53:33/q05/p2 He turned from revelation or remembrance to worldly pursuits. — Rāzī links the person to those in 53:29 who neglect reminder out of self-sufficiency and seek only worldly life; Biqāʿī says the person had formerly been devoted to remembrance and confident of his family's faith. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 53:33/q05/p3 He left his post at Uḥud. — Al-Zamakhsharī takes the verse as concerning ʿUthmān, who later returned to better conduct; the ʿUthmān report in al-Thaʿlabī and al-Ṭabrisī also connects his turning with Uḥud. Abū Ḥayyān presents this as al-Zamakhsharī's alternative to turning from Islam. [4 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس والسدي والكلبي والمسيب بن شريك); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس والسدي والكلبي وجماعة من المفسرين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 53:33/q05/p4 He turned away from spending in God's cause and stopped giving. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the next verse as specifying the turning; the man first gave a little and then ceased altogether. Kuranyolu describes a brief feeling of compassion overcome by attachment to wealth and lasting stinginess. [3 notes: TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 53:33/q07 How dependable are the named occasion reports?  [* · reports]
turns on: Historical establishment, the character of ʿUthmān, the chain of the al-Walīd report, and how its details fit the Qurʾānic censure.
- 53:33/q07/p1 The ʿUthmān account is false or unestablished. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya declares ʿUthmān innocent. Rāzī says the account is unestablished and contrary to his character. Ibn ʿĀshūr transmits Ibn ʿAṭiyya's reason that he would not heed Ibn Abī Sarḥ. Abū Ḥayyān attributes a rejection of the accounts to Ibn ʿAṭiyya. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الثعلبي عن قوم); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 53:33/q07/p2 The al-Walīd story is unsound or ill-fitted to these verses. — Islāḥī says it has no sound chain and, even if true, its connection is unclear because the Qurʾān does not rebuke him for breaking a promise; treating al-ladhī as a proper name led exegetes to invent an ill-fitting incident. Kuranyolu says polytheist reports are unreliable and their details conflict with Qurʾānic principles. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 53:33/q08 What conduct does the verse rebuke or warn against?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the main censure falls on abandoned faith and the imagined transfer of punishment, on stinginess after limited giving, or on complacency about earlier deeds.
- 53:33/q08/p1 Turning from obedience or truth and believing another can carry one's punishment. — Ibn Kathīr links the censure to 75:31–32, which describes neither believing nor praying but denying and turning away. Bursevī says the false transfer of punishment and turning away are the chief rebukes; the stopped gift narrates the episode ironically. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the man acted as if payment could erase the consequences of abandoning Islam, although his action presupposed both its guilt and transferability of afterlife sins. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI+]
- 53:33/q08/p2 His turning is blame, while the astonishment also prepares rebuke for giving little and then stopping. — Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes the blame for turning from the astonishment; the small gift leads to a further charge of stinginess even beyond disbelief. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:33/q08/p3 A person who trusts past good deeds or self-purity and ceases good works may lose even the fruit of earlier deeds. — Study Quran extends the warning beyond the reported person to anyone who stops doing good. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 53:33/q08/p4 Wealthy claimants to Abrahamic or Mosaic standing expect Paradise while withholding charity, despite those prophets' teachings about personal deeds and burdens. — Islāḥī says claimed descent cannot secure Paradise; no one carries another's burden, and each receives the fruit of his own deeds. He presents a miserly idolater as representative of wealthy Quraysh and notes sarcastic disparagement of those who give grudgingly yet act certain of Paradise's luxuries. Kuranyolu likewise stresses wealth attachment and enduring stinginess. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 53:33/q08/p5 The listener should consider a man who gave only a little toward God's cause and then stopped. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the following verse as clarifying that the turning was from spending. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 53:33/q09 How does this passage fit the surrounding argument of the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The commentators connect the example to different surrounding themes: recompense, the scriptural case for personal responsibility, ancestral pride, parallels elsewhere, or the sūra's recitation.
- 53:33/q09/p1 It moves from God's knowledge and recompense to a pointed example of one person's mistaken conduct. — Al-Qurṭubī says the passage singles out one idolater after exposing the idolaters' ignorance. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the question is astonishing after 53:31's promise of recompense, and contrasts its particular man with 53:29's group. Rāzī connects the warning to God's knowledge and human need for prophetic instruction in 53:29–32; Asad bases his rendering on Rāzī's return to an earlier theme, although the excerpt breaks before naming the verses. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Razi)]
- 53:33/q09/p2 The example introduces the scriptures' truth about personal responsibility against the polytheists' reliance on idols and intercession. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says citations from Moses' and Abraham's scriptures answer the claim that idols portray God's angel daughters who intercede. Creation and governance imply a second life of recompense, revelation, and duties; the destroyed peoples of Noah, ʿĀd, Thamūd, and the overturned cities testify. The sūra then identifies this warner with earlier warners, announces the near Hour, and commands worship and prostration. Study Quran calls this its third section, from creation to enduring truths in those scriptures. [4 notes: TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 53:33/q09/p3 The scriptures of Abraham and Moses expose the error of expecting Paradise through ancestry while refusing charity. — Islāḥī contrasts boastful Abrahamic and Mosaic association with the prophets' teaching and sacrifices: no one bears another's burden, and each meets the outcome of his own acts. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 53:33/q09/p4 The verse's turning away parallels the denial and turning in 75:31–32. — Ibn Kathīr connects the condemnation of disobedience with that passage's sequence of neither believing nor praying, then denying and turning away. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 53:33/q09/p5 The framing recalls apostasy after the Night Journey and the unexpected prostration by all groups at this sūra's end. — Biqāʿī cites Bukhārī for Muslims, polytheists, jinn, and humans prostrating together, even some who later apostatized. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 53:33/q10 Were the verses beginning here revealed in Medina?  [* · sciences]
- 53:33/q10/p1 Some except the nine verses beginning here from al-Najm's Meccan classification. — Al-Itqān reports a view extending the exception through 53:41; Study Quran says some extend the proposed Medinan dating to verse 33. [2 notes: ITQAN; STUDYQURAN(some)]

## 53:33/q11 What inward path does the turning away portray?  [تَوَلَّىٰ · inward]
- 53:33/q11/p1 A seeker begins to spend life's capital on the spiritual path, then tires, returns to the sensual ego, and spends the rest on worldly pleasure. — Bursevī says the relapse shows a lack of readiness for union. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 53:33/q12 What image explains the stopping of the gift in the continuation?  [* · meaning]
- 53:33/q12/p1 Akdā pictures a well digger striking hard rock and being stopped. — Study Quran derives the verb from al-kudyah, the hard rock met while digging a well. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
