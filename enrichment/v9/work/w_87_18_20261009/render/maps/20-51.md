# 20:51: 12 questions

## 20:51/q01 How does the interrogative mā work?  [فَمَا · grammar]
- 20:51/q01/p1 Mā marks an interrogative itself, so an added interrogative alif is redundant. — Sibawayh includes mā with ayyu, man and matā among interrogative nouns that already mark a question. He says am can precede man and hal, but not the interrogative alif: am itself corresponds to that particle and signals a shift between alternatives, whereas alif only opens a question. [3 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 20:51/q02 What does bāl mean here?  [بَالُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word's original sense of thought or its extended sense of condition is in view.
- 20:51/q02/p1 A state, condition, affair, or matter of concern. — Ibn Qutayba cites the idiom asking God to set one's bāl right; Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites the Prophetic prayer for guidance and a sound condition. Jishumī equates bāl with ḥāl and shaʾn. Al-Samīn and Bursevī specify a consequential condition one cares about; al-Samīn cites mā bālaytu bāla and says its ending was dropped for ease. Al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ derive this sense from an original sense of thought; al-Ālūsī says its plural is normally unused, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ calls bālāt anomalous. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Qurṭubī, and others apply the sense to these generations' fate. [9 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:51/q02/p2 Thought or something that comes to mind, the word's original sense. — Al-Samīn cites khaṭara bi-bālihi and calls the plural bālāt rare. Bursevī says the expression can mean what occurs to a person or comes to mind. Al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ describe the move from thought to a noteworthy state; the latter calls bālāt anomalous. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 20:51/q03 What does qurūn mean?  [ٱلْقُرُونِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether shared time alone defines a qarn or an exemplary religious figure is required.
- 20:51/q03/p1 Generations: people living together in one era. — Al-Māwardī says qarn is singular and explains the name by people being joined in an era; Jishumī likewise derives it from joining. Bursevī defines it as people living at the same time. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; BURSEVI]
- 20:51/q03/p2 A qarn is an era with a prophet or exemplary learned leader, excluding an age of ignorant prophetic hiatus. — Al-Māwardī attributes this to al-Zajjāj and excludes an age of widespread ignorance without a prophet. Al-Ṭabrisī likewise reports al-Zajjāj's criterion of a prophet, imam, or scholar to emulate. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الزجاج); TABRISI(الزجاج)]

## 20:51/q04 Which generations are meant by 'the former'?  [ٱلْقُرُونِ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · referent]
turns on: How broadly the earlier peoples are delimited and whether Pharaoh includes himself with them.
- 20:51/q04/p1 Past peoples such as Noah's people, ʿĀd, and Thamūd. — Maqātil's believer and al-Thaʿlabī cite the warning about these peoples in 40:30–31. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Baghawī identify them as idol worshippers; al-Ṭabrisī calls them nations that rejected Moses' message and worshipped idols; Bursevī asks about the detailed events that befell them. [6 notes: MUQATIL(مؤمن آل فرعون); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; BURSEVI]
- 20:51/q04/p2 Generations before Pharaoh, though he does not separate himself from them. — Elmalılı says these earliest generations end with Pharaoh's own destruction in the Qur'anic usage of al-Qaṣaṣ. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 20:51/q05 How does al-ūlā agree with qurūn?  [ٱلْقُرُونِ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · grammar]
- 20:51/q05/p1 A feminine singular adjective can describe a plural noun; al-ūlā is the feminine of al-awwal. — Al-Burhān compares al-qurūn al-ūlā to al-samāwāt al-ʿulā and al-asmāʾ al-ḥusnā. Bursevī compares the singular pattern al-kubrā beside al-akbar. [2 notes: BURHAN; BURSEVI]

## 20:51/q06 Who speaks the question?  [قَالَ · referent]
turns on: The identity of the subject of qāla.
- 20:51/q06/p1 Pharaoh asks it. — This is explicit in Maqātil, Yaḥyā ibn Sallām, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī, Jishumī, al-Baghawī, and the subsequent explanations of his motive and Moses' reply. [10 notes: MUQATIL(مؤمن آل فرعون); YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:51/q06/p2 Moses asks Pharaoh about the earlier generations. — Al-Zajjāj states this assignment of the speaker. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]

## 20:51/q07 What does the speaker ask about the earlier generations?  [فَمَا بَالُ ٱلْقُرُونِ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the question seeks historical information, posthumous fate and recompense, the earlier peoples' worship, their earthly punishment, or God's knowledge of them.
- 20:51/q07/p1 Their general condition or fate. — Ibn ʿAbbās, as reported by al-Wāḥidī, asks after the passed generations' condition; Qatāda is reported similarly by al-Durr. Al-Wāḥidī says their deeds are preserved for recompense. Al-Baghawī names Noah's people, ʿĀd, and Thamūd, who worshipped idols and denied resurrection. Al-Qurṭubī places their condition among the unseen matters. Al-Nasafī speaks of past and vanished generations; Bursevī asks what detailed events befell them; the Study Quran asks what God did with them. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:51/q07/p2 Why earlier idol worshippers did not recognize or worship the true God, if Moses' proof is clear. — Al-Māwardī frames this as whether they shared Moses' faith. Ibn al-Jawzī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Qurṭubī, and Abū Ḥayyān mention idol worship. Al-Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān explain the appeal to inherited belief against a supposedly obvious proof of the Creator; Elmalılı says the question seeks to deny guidance and prophecy. Ibn Kathīr explicitly prefers this reading. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 20:51/q07/p3 Why dead generations have not been raised, judged, and repaid. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām and al-Māwardī tie this to Moses' call to believe in resurrection; Jishumī cites the warning of punishment in 20:48 and, in another formulation, a threat of hell after long discussion. Al-Ṭabrisī reports the same link. Ibn al-Jawzī and Abū Ḥayyān explicitly include judgment and recompense. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says death seems to leave only reports and legends, and compares the objection to 32:10 on recreation after becoming dust. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI]
- 20:51/q07/p4 What becomes of them after death: who is blessed and who is wretched. — Al-Zamakhsharī says the happy and wretched are unseen to Moses. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī frame the question as their posthumous states; the Study Quran asks what God did with them. Al-Qāshānī reads this as the self's concealment from resurrection and afterlife happiness and misery. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:51/q07/p5 The deeds of earlier generations. — Qatāda gives this paraphrase in Yaḥyā ibn Sallām; al-Wāḥidī says Pharaoh asks after Moses' preceding answer. [2 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
- 20:51/q07/p6 Their sins and the punishment they received, or why earlier deniers were not punished. — Al-Māwardī asks about their sins and punishment. Al-Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān relate the challenge to Moses' warning that deniers are punished. Kur'an Yolu takes it as a question whether the powerful can escape consequences; Moses answers that they remain recorded with God. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 20:51/q07/p7 The histories and circumstances of vanished peoples. — Ibn al-Jawzī attributes this to Maqātil; Abū Ḥayyān says Pharaoh may be testing whether Moses and Aaron are prophets or storytellers, before the Torah's revelation. Bursevī names the past events of Noah's people, ʿĀd, and Thamūd. Iṣlāḥī calls the question sarcastic: a messenger ought to know those histories. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ reports this from many commentators but objects to the interpretation as a distraction. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; TABATABAI(كثير منهم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI-]
- 20:51/q07/p8 What blinded those earlier generations. — Qatāda adds that their lifespans and appointed terms are with God, referring to 20:52 and 7:187. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة)]
- 20:51/q07/p9 Whether past polytheists were irretrievably doomed. — Asad supplies this as Pharaoh's implied challenge about generations who worshipped multiple gods. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:51/q07/p10 How God knows every distant generation and all its parts. — Abū Ḥayyān attributes this possibility to al-Zamakhsharī: Moses replies that every being is known and recorded, unlike Pharaoh's fallible knowledge. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]

## 20:51/q08 Why does Pharaoh raise this question now?  [فَمَا بَالُ ٱلْقُرُونِ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Which prior claim prompts Pharaoh and whether he seeks a substantive challenge, a prophetic test, or a diversion; Bursevī contests who voiced the earlier warning, while al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ contests the diversion reading.
- 20:51/q08/p1 He diverts Moses from a compelling proof to confuse the audience and avoid answering. — Al-Māwardī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya call it evasion; Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī compare the tactic to innovators or heretics changing subjects when proof appears. Al-Rāzī prefers this: Pharaoh feared his falsehood would be exposed, and Moses returned to the proof of divine oneness. Abū Ḥayyān calls it sophistry after Moses silenced him; al-Biqāʿī calls the new dispute broad and unprovable. Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, Iṣlāḥī, and Kur'an Yolu stress protecting Pharaoh's influence or making Moses seem uninformed. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ reports the history-diversion reading from many commentators but rejects it as strange because the questions connect to the preceding argument. [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(كثير منهم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)+; RAZI-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 20:51/q08/p2 He challenges Moses' proof through the ancestral failure to recognize God. — Al-Rāzī calls it an appeal to inherited belief; Abū Ḥayyān says Pharaoh asks why past peoples missed an allegedly obvious rational proof. Elmalılı says he seeks to deny divine guidance and prophethood. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports a direct contradiction reading but finds it ill fitted: generations before Moses' claim could not have rejected that claim. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 20:51/q08/p3 He challenges the promise of resurrection and punishment for deniers. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām, Jishumī, and al-Ṭabrisī connect the move to Moses' call for resurrection and warning of punishment. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says 20:48 warned of punishment, idolaters reject it, and the apparent disappearance of the dead makes recompense seem incredible; the parallel is 32:10. He adds that guidance through prophecy and resurrection is needed for judgment to distinguish good from evil and make God's commands and approval known. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; JISHUMI; TABRISI(قيل); TABATABAI]
- 20:51/q08/p4 He asks after hearing a warning about the fate of earlier nations. — Maqātil assigns the warning about Noah, ʿĀd, Thamūd and later peoples to the believer from Pharaoh's household, citing 40:30–31. Al-Thaʿlabī says it followed Moses' warning. Al-Naqqāsh, reported by Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān, connects it to that believer. Bursevī reports al-Asʾila al-Muqḥima's view that Moses voiced the warning, then rejects that attribution: the fa fits, but the reply does not, and the speaker was the believer, whom the context does not identify with Moses. [5 notes: MUQATIL(مؤمن آل فرعون); THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(النقاش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النقاش); BURSEVI(الأسئلة المقحمة); BURSEVI-]
- 20:51/q08/p5 He tests whether Moses, claiming to be a messenger, can recount what happened long before him. — Al-Ālūsī says fa connects the question to the messenger claim. Iṣlāḥī calls Pharaoh's request sarcastic; Abū Ḥayyān says it tests whether Moses and Aaron are prophets or storytellers. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:51/q09 Why does Moses leave knowledge of those generations to God?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the relevant limit is the Torah's timing, revelation of unseen reports generally, or the contrast between human and divine knowledge.
- 20:51/q09/p1 Their histories were not yet revealed to him, because the Torah came after Pharaoh's destruction. — Maqātil says Moses could not tell their fate for this chronological reason; al-Naqqāsh is reported by Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān, and Ibn al-Jawzī attributes a similar account to Maqātil. Abū Ḥayyān also connects it with Pharaoh's test of Moses before the Torah. [5 notes: MUQATIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(النقاش); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النقاش,قيل)]
- 20:51/q09/p2 Their unseen fate and histories belong to God's knowledge; a messenger knows only what God reveals. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports another view that a messenger does not know unseen reports; al-Rāzī contrasts Moses' rational answer to a question about God with his referral of transmitted history to the Knower of the unseen. Al-Qurṭubī states the limit of revelation, and al-Kashshāf locates happiness and wretchedness in the unseen. Al-Wāḥidī says deeds are preserved for recompense; Kur'an Yolu says God neither errs nor forgets. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ links Moses' reply to the apparent erasure of the dead and their deeds. [7 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(غيره); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]
- 20:51/q09/p3 God's eternal, errorless knowledge encompasses and records their states, grounding knowledge of the afterlife in His attributes. — Al-Qāshānī reads the question as the self's concealment from resurrection and afterlife happiness or misery; Moses' response refers to the Preserved Tablet. Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī's suggestion that Pharaoh tests God's encompassing knowledge of remote generations and their parts, to which Moses contrasts divine record with Pharaoh's fallibility. [3 notes: QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]

## 20:51/q10 What other grammatical observations occur among the notes?  [* · grammar]
- 20:51/q10/p1 Demonstratives and relatives have distinctive diminutive patterns and endings. — Sibawayh says demonstratives retain their initial vowels and add final alifs; he gives hādhā to hadhayyā, dhāka to dhayyāka and alā to alayyā. He locates the diminutive yāʾ of dhā in third position and deletes one crowded yāʾ; tayyā is from tā. He cites Kaʿb al-Ghanawī and ʿImrān ibn Ḥiṭṭān for tamānī and hātā. Extended ulāʾ becomes ulayyāʾ, with feminine forms avoiding ambiguity. Relative alladhī and allatī yield alladhayyā and allatayyā, supported by al-ʿAjjāj; final alifs drop in duals through frequent use. Relative man and ayyu resist diminution because they remain interrogative forms, unlike alladhī; allātī needs no diminutive because allatiyyāt supplies it, compared to masiyyān and ʿashiyyān instead of a diminutive of qaṣr. [9 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 20:51/q10/p2 Feminine diminutives take or omit tāʾ according to word structure, established usage, and whether the name is a shared adjective. — Sibawayh adds tāʾ to three-letter feminine nouns; al-Khalīl says it marks femininity but ʿanāq omits it for heaviness. Shortened samāʾ gives sumayya; a woman named Siqāʾ gives siqīqī without suffix. He reports two analyses of ḥubārā, one preserving a feminine marker and another retaining a four-letter form; a late-position feminine alif yields lughayghīza from lughayzā. A shared masculine adjective such as niṣf, riḍā or khalīq remains masculine for a woman. Al-Khalīl analyzes she-camel al-nāb as nuyayyib on its established masculine name. Pure female proper names Ḥajar and Faras give Ḥujayra and Furaysa. Sibawayh omits tāʾ for a man named ʿAyn or Udhun, while Yūnus adds it and cites Udhayna. [10 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]
- 20:51/q10/p3 Feminine double-alif endings are diptote, while superficially similar hamzas and reduplicated forms have other analyses. — Sibawayh says the second alif becomes hamza to avoid adjacent vowelless letters and retains the feminine marking effect in definite and indefinite use. The pattern does not merely lengthen triliterals; ʿalbāʾ and ḥirbāʾ instead replace yāʾ, with alif plus yāʾ lengthening. Some Arabs use masculine declinable qūbāʾ like fusṭāṭ. Ghawghāʾ can be feminine diptote like ʿawrāʾ or masculine declinable with reduplicated sounds like qaḍqāḍ or qamqām. [6 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 20:51/q10/p4 Weak-letter substitution and reduplication account for several long verbal and nominal forms. — Al-Khalīl says five-letter verbs such as aghzaytu and ghāzaytu turn wāw to yāʾ after the imperfect loses wāw under kasra; taghāzaynā and tarjaynā then show alif replacing that yāʾ, with tāʾ added to ghāzaytu and rajaytu. Sibawayh treats ḍawḍaytu and qawqaytu as paired reduplication parallel to ḥayaytu and quwwa; ḥāḥaytu and ʿāʿaytu similarly substitute alif for yāʾ, supported by al-ḥayḥāʾ and al-ʿayʿāʾ. Al-Khalīl derives dahdaytu al-juʿal from dahdahtu, yāʾ replacing light hāʾ. Ghawghāʾ has feminine diptote or masculine declinable reduplicated analyses; related forms analogize to qamqām, ghaṣaṣ and ghaṣṣa. Mūmāh has root mīm like dūdāh and marmar, since an added initial mīm is rare; fīfāh has extra alif because al-fayyif has the same meaning. Qayqāʾ and zayzāʾ follow ʿalbāʾ, while qalqāl is only a verbal noun; added fourth-position yāʾ behaves like a root in salqaytu and jaʿbaytu. Al-murūrāh follows al-shujūjāh and ṣamḥamaḥ as the more common pattern, not ʿathawthal. Qayqāʾa and zayzāʾa are singulars; some say qayqāʾa and qawqāq, with substitution like qīl. Athfiyya is fiʿliyya for speakers of athaftu, but afʿūla for speakers of thafaytu. [14 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]
- 20:51/q10/p5 A relative clause after ayyu-hum is regular with huwa; omission is awkward or, in a longer construction, rare. — Sibawayh accepts ayyu-hum huwa afḍal and similar clauses, but judges ayyu-hum ʿāqil awkward. Al-Khalīl reports an Arab's rare mā anā bi-lladhī qāʾil laka shayʾan; Sibawayh rejects a shortened version with munṭaliq, saying few use even the longer possibility. [3 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 20:51/q11 What other lexical observation occurs among the notes?  [* · meaning]
- 20:51/q11/p1 Faraṭ is going ahead; ifrāṭ is overshooting and tafrīṭ is falling behind an obligation. — Al-Ṭabrisī compares faraṭ to a person going ahead toward water and derives excess and neglect from the respective directions of departure from what is right. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 20:51/q12 What other observations about Qur'anic interpretation occur among the notes?  [* · sciences]
- 20:51/q12/p1 The community could preserve and read one of seven revealed aḥruf; the other six were neither abrogated nor forgotten. — Al-Ṭabarī distinguishes the Prophet's reports about seven wordings from the present written Qur'an and compares the permitted choice to selecting one of three expiations for a broken oath. [2 notes: TAB-FULL]
- 20:51/q12/p2 Study of rhetorical artistry is essential for explaining Qur'anic inimitability and coherence. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ criticizes earlier neglect and singles out al-Rāzī for brief treatment in al-Nihāya despite his theological command. She reports Ibn Ḥamza al-ʿAlawī's al-Ṭirāz as an answer to that need and Ḥājjī Khalīfa's praise of al-Biqāʿī's Naẓm al-Durar for unprecedented precision in coherence, Qur'anic secrets, and difficult meanings. [4 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن حمزة العلوي,حاجي خليفة)]
