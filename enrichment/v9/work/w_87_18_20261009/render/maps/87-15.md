# 87:15: 17 questions

## 87:15/q01 What does remembering the Lord's name mean?  [وَذَكَرَ ٱسْمَ رَبِّهِۦ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether name indicates spoken invocation, confession, inward recollection, a liturgical formula, or Eid practice.
- 87:15/q01/p1 Affirming God's oneness, including the testimony of faith. — Ibn ʿAbbās gives the unity gloss; Muqātil places it in prayer. Al-Ālūsī links inward assent, spoken confession, and bodily prayer, and says the two testimonies may be included. [33 notes: TAB(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(رسول الله); QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس,النبي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,جابر بن عبد الله); ITQAN(جابر بن عبد الله); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+; TAB+]
- 87:15/q01/p2 Calling on God and seeking His favor, including supplication, forgiveness, and repentance. — Al-Ṭabarī includes invocation alongside confession because the verse does not restrict remembrance to one kind; al-Māwardī also records a repentance and forgiveness gloss. Corpus Coranicum argues that dhakara with God's name as object and the pairing with prayer favor audible invocation; it compares 87:1, 108:2, 76:25, and 73:8. [10 notes: TAB(آخرون); TAB-FULL(آخرون); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; TAB-FULL+; TAB+]
- 87:15/q01/p3 Recalling one's return and standing before God, which moves one to prayer. — Reported from Ibn ʿAbbās through ʿAṭāʾ; al-Rāzī prefers it and al-Ālūsī finds it apparent. Elmalılı adds that this recollection leads to opening prayer with Allāhu akbar. [16 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); KASHSHAF(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(عطاء عن ابن عباس); NASAFI(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس)+; RAZI+]
- 87:15/q01/p4 Inward awareness of God, His majesty, names, qualities, reward, and punishment. — Al-Māwardī and al-Ṭabrisī say fear and hope foster full, humble prayer. Al-Rāzī identifies inward remembrance with knowledge of God's essence, attributes, and names. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats 'name' as pointing to divine affairs and perfect qualities; al-Ālūsī says an exclusively inward sense departs from the apparent wording. [21 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); QASHANI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض); QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI(بعض)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 87:15/q01/p5 Remembrance with heart and tongue together. — Al-Bayḍāwī compares 20:14. Al-Ālūsī and Study Quran say heedless speech yields no reward. Al-Biqāʿī describes the heart delighting in divine beneficence and the tongue expressing it; Ibn ʿĀshūr allows spoken names, testimony, takbīr and tasbīḥ, comparing 87:1. [11 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(others); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 87:15/q01/p6 Saying the opening takbīr before prayer. — Al-Māwardī and al-Ṭabrisī say prayer does not begin without God's name. The Ḥanafīs take this as the prayer's opening; al-Rāzī objects that the verse does not identify remembrance as takbīr. [15 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI(بعض الحنفية); ALUSI-FULL(بعض الحنفية); ELMALILI; RAZI-; RAZI-FULL-]
- 87:15/q01/p7 Saying the basmala at the beginning of a sūra or prayer. — Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī specify each sūra; Study Quran specifies each prayer. Al-Ālūsī dismisses the pre-prayer basmala interpretation. [6 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); ALUSI(قيل)-; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]
- 87:15/q01/p8 The Eid takbīrs, especially while walking to the prayer ground. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and a further Ibn ʿAbbās report place the takbīr on the way to Eid; ʿAlī connects it to fitr alms and Eid prayer. ## 87:15/q02 What does 'the Lord's name' point to?  [ٱسْمَ رَبِّهِۦ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'name' designates God, the uttered divine designation, or a particular attribute.
- 87:15/q02/p1 God Himself, whose name calls its bearer to mind. — Al-Thaʿlabī records 'God Himself' versus actual pronunciation. Al-Biqāʿī says uttering the name inscribes its bearer in the heart; Ibn ʿĀshūr says 'name' may be inserted to indicate God's affairs and qualities. [6 notes: THALABI; QURTUBI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:15/q02/p2 God's spoken name, including His attributes of beneficence and perfection. — Al-Biqāʿī says attributes bring delight to heart and tongue. Ibn ʿĀshūr lists testimony, takbīr, and tasbīḥ and compares the sūra's opening. [6 notes: THALABI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 87:15/q02/p3 The particular divine name matching the seeker's needed perfection. — Al-Qāshānī gives Knower for ignorance, Guide for error, and Forgiver for sin; the name discloses the Real once the person sees the destined perfection. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 87:15/q04 How does remembrance relate in time to prayer?  [وَذَكَرَ فَصَلَّىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the coordinating fāʾ enforces chronological sequence.
- 87:15/q04/p1 Remembrance precedes and prompts prayer. — Al-Naḥḥās says reversing the conjunction's order violates normal syntax. Abū Ḥanīfa insists the fāʾ has sequential force. Ibn ʿĀshūr says recollection naturally produces reverence and prayer. [10 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; NAHHAS+; RAZI-FULL(أبو حنيفة)+; RAZI(أبو حنيفة)+]
- 87:15/q04/p2 The wording need not impose the actual order; prayer can precede or contain remembrance. — Al-Qurṭubī illustrates with visiting and honoring in either verbal order. Al-Rāzī's school suggests 'prayed, then remembered'; al-Ṭabarī reads remembrance through praise, glorification and supplication within prayer, which al-Naḥḥās rejects as reversing the conjunction. [8 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(محمد بن جرير); RAZI(أصحابنا); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NAHHAS-; RAZI(أبو حنيفة)-; RAZI-FULL(أبو حنيفة)-]

## 87:15/q05 What does the wording prove about the opening takbīr's legal status?  [وَذَكَرَ ٱسْمَ رَبِّهِۦ فَصَلَّىٰ · law]
turns on: Whether the verse's remembrance is specifically the opening takbīr.
- 87:15/q05/p1 It proves the opening invocation is obligatory. — The Kashshāf, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Nasafī tie the invocation to success. Al-Ālūsī reports the Ḥanafī argument that it stands between purification from shirk and prayer, both obligations. [11 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI(الفقهاء); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI(بعض الحنفية); ALUSI-FULL]
- 87:15/q05/p2 The verse does not establish that legal inference. — Al-Rāzī replies that remembrance is not identified as takbīr and may be reflection on reward and punishment. Abū Ḥayyān calls the combined argument weak; al-Ālūsī questions the inference that takbīr is a pillar. [8 notes: RAZI; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+; RAZI+]

## 87:15/q06 Is the opening takbīr outside prayer or a part of it?  [وَذَكَرَ ٱسْمَ رَبِّهِۦ فَصَلَّىٰ · law]
turns on: Whether conjunction separates the invocation from prayer and whether a whole can follow its part rhetorically.
- 87:15/q06/p1 It precedes prayer as a distinct condition. — Abū Ḥanīfa, the Kashshāf, al-Qurṭubī and al-Nasafī infer separation from the conjunction; Abū Ḥanīfa insists the fāʾ's order cannot be discarded without evidence. [12 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI(أبو حنيفة); RAZI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 87:15/q06/p2 The verse does not prove separation; takbīr may be a component or pillar of prayer. — Al-Rāzī says a preceding inward remembrance can prompt prayer with takbīr inside it. His school allows rhetorical reverse order. Al-Ālūsī says treating prayer as the whole following its part needs a rhetorical purpose, so he challenges a pillar inference as well. [10 notes: RAZI(أصحابنا); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+; RAZI+]

## 87:15/q07 May prayer be opened with any divine name?  [ٱسْمَ رَبِّهِۦ · law]
turns on: Whether 'His name' is a legal specification of the prayer-opening formula.
- 87:15/q07/p1 Yes; the general expression permits any of God's names. — The Kashshāf, Abū Ḥanīfa, al-Qurṭubī and al-Nasafī infer this from 'His name'; al-Qurṭubī notes a juristic dispute, and al-Ālūsī says this follows on the takbīr interpretation. [10 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI(أبو حنيفة); RAZI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 87:15/q07/p2 The verse is too general to settle that permission. — Abū Ḥayyān calls the combined Ḥanafī proof weak; al-Rāzī says remembrance need not be the opening formula at all. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 87:15/q08 What is the order and point of purification, remembrance, and prayer?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the order describes spiritual stages, means of purification, a practical charity sequence, or esoteric realization.
- 87:15/q08/p1 They trace belief's purification, knowledge or confession of God, then obedient bodily worship. — Al-Rāzī describes removing false belief, knowing divine names and attributes, then humble service. Al-Ālūsī maps the sequence to heart, tongue and body; Ibn ʿĀshūr says a cleansed soul receives guidance readily. Al-Biqāʿī sees removal of defects and acquisition of perfections. [27 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإمام); ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:15/q08/p2 Remembrance and prayer are foremost means and expressions of purification and success. — Muqātil contrasts Meccan unbelievers who do neither. Al-Biqāʿī calls prayer the greatest bodily worship and remembrance; Qutb says they bring sweetness now and escape from Hell later. Iṣlāḥī calls prayer the manifestation and guard of belief, citing 23:1–2. [16 notes: MUQATIL; QUSHAYRI; IBNKATHIR(قتادة); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 87:15/q08/p3 Charity before prayer is an application of the sequence. — Ibn Masʿūd, Abū al-Aḥwaṣ and others commend charity before prayer; Abū al-Aḥwaṣ also proposes two units after a donation. ʿAbd Allāh says prayer without zakāt is no prayer. [22 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو الأحوص,ابن مسعود); THALABI(ابن مسعود); BAGHAWI(ابن مسعود); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن مسعود); KASHSHAF(ابن مسعود); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI(عبد الله); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله); IBNKATHIR(أبو الأحوص); BIQAI(ابن مسعود,الرازي); BIQAI-FULL(ابن مسعود,الرازي في اللوامع); DURR(أبو الأحوص,ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(أبو الأحوص,ابن مسعود)]
- 87:15/q08/p4 It describes a divine-name practice in which worship follows the seeker's realized perfection. — Al-Qāshānī's inward interpretation says each person's innate readiness seeks a particular perfection through the matching name; he invokes 59:19 on forgetting God and self. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 87:15/q09 Can the verse apply specifically to fitr alms and Eid prayer despite the sūra's date?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: How a Meccan date relates to practices instituted later, and whether word order is decisive.
- 87:15/q09/p1 Yes; general wording may anticipate a later rule or these verses may have been revealed in Medina. — Al-Baghawī compares 90:2 and 54:45, fulfilled at the conquest and Badr; ʿUmar recognized the Badr prediction later. Al-Wāḥidī says God can praise foreknown acts. Ibn al-Ḥaṣṣār cites Meccan zakāt language at 6:141 and 73:20. Al-Ālūsī says the unusual order may urge payment before Eid; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī locates the closing verses in Medina. [16 notes: BAGHAWI(عمر بن الخطاب); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(ابن الحصار,البغوي); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(family of the Prophet)]
- 87:15/q09/p2 No; a Meccan sūra predates Eid and fitr alms, and the Qurʾān normally mentions prayer before zakāt. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Rāzī and Ibn al-Jawzī use the Meccan date; al-Rāzī also objects to reversing the usual order of prayer and alms. Some in al-Baghawī and al-Itqān state the same objection; al-Ālūsī records replies that verbal order can vary. [10 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI(بعضهم); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI(الثعلبي); RAZI-FULL; ITQAN(بعضهم); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; BAGHAWI-; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي)-; BIQAI-; ITQAN(ابن الحصار,البغوي)-; ALUSI-; TABATABAI-]

## 87:15/q10 What reported conduct was associated with reciting these verses?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which transmitted event or practice the verse was applied to.
- 87:15/q10/p1 The Prophet or early Muslims gave or directed fitr alms before going to Eid prayer. — Reports name the Prophet, ʿAlī, Ibn ʿUmar, Abū Saʿīd, Abū al-ʿĀliya, Ibn Sīrīn, ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz and al-Nakhaʿī. Ibn ʿUmar checked payment before departing; Abū al-ʿĀliya praised fitra and providing water. One raised report through Kathīr b. ʿAbd Allāh is called weak by al-Suyūṭī, though al-Tirmidhī graded him ḥasan and others weakened him. ## 87:15/q11 Were verses 14–15 revealed in Mecca or Medina?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the Eid-related closing verses share the opening's date.
- 87:15/q11/p1 The whole sūra, including these verses, is Meccan. — Al-Thaʿlabī and Ibn al-Jawzī call this consensus; opponents invoke it against Eid and fitr applications. Qutb calls the sūra Meccan and says it has nineteen verses. [7 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI(بعضهم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI(الثعلبي); RAZI-FULL; ITQAN(بعضهم); QUTB-ZILAL]
- 87:15/q11/p2 The opening is Meccan, but the closing verses about fitr alms and Eid came in Medina. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this from Ibn ʿUmar and Ibn ʿAbbās. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says reports calling the sūra Meccan can designate its opening; Study Quran reports the same family view. [3 notes: IBNASHUR(ابن عمر وابن عباس); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(family of the Prophet)]
- 87:15/q11/p3 The Meccan date is stronger but not unanimous; a later legal realization remains possible. — Al-Ālūsī questions a claim of consensus and permits a later rule even if the sūra is Meccan. [2 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 87:15/q12 How does this verse fit the sūra's surrounding argument?  [* · links]
turns on: Which neighbouring lines and themes frame the promise of purification and prayer.
- 87:15/q12/p1 It promises success to those who respond to the Prophet's reminder, balancing the warning to those who avoid it. — Corpus Coranicum maps 87:10–11 to 87:12–15 as a chiasm and compares Q 92:5–21; it sees the messenger as exemplary to believers. Qutb and Iṣlāḥī connect the response to the reminder with divergent outcomes. [9 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 87:15/q12/p2 It reprises the sūra's opening call to praise the Lord and removes defects before acquiring perfection. — Al-Biqāʿī links it to 87:1 and an interwoven omission contrasting fire and purification. Corpus Coranicum says the warning implies fear, purification, invocation and prayer, returning to 87:1. [7 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 87:15/q12/p3 Its acts belong to the earlier scrolls of Abraham and Moses. — Al-Ṭabarī takes 'this' in 87:18–19 to refer to the nearer 87:14–17 and explains ṣuḥuf as books; reported answers to questions about the scrolls recite this passage. [4 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI(أبي ذر); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:15/q12/p4 Prayer and remembrance reinforce each other and connect this teaching with other Qurʾānic calls. — Al-Bayḍāwī compares 20:14; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 29:45; Corpus Coranicum compares 87:1, 108:2, 107:4, 76:25, and 73:8 for glorification, invocation, and prayer. [7 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 87:15/q12/p5 The sura's wider scene combines cosmic praise, revelation, divine ease, and afterlife recompense. — Qutb says the Prophet loved the sūra, recited it at Eid and Friday prayer with al-Ghāshiya, and sees its themes of oneness, revelation and earlier scriptures behind this closing promise. [3 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]

## 87:15/q13 What variant pronunciations affect words in this verse?  [فَصَلَّىٰ وَذَكَرَ · readings]

- 87:15/q13/p1 The final lām in fa-ṣallā may be softened at verse end, while nonfinal counterparts are pronounced emphatically. — Ibn al-Jazarī cites this preferred distinction for euphony against a preceding emphatic trigger; verse-final examples are 75:31, 87:15 and 96:10, contrasted with nonfinal yaslā at 87:12. - 87:15/q13/p2 The noun for remembrance is pronounced with either kasra or ḍamma on its first consonant. — Al-Biqāʿī records the two pronunciations. [1 notes: BIQAI]

## 87:15/q14 What purification is presupposed before this remembrance and prayer?  [* · interpretation]
- 87:15/q14/p1 Freedom from associating partners with God. — Al-Suyūṭī glosses the preceding purification as removal of polytheism before the confession and five prayers. [1 notes: ITQAN]
