## 2:180/q01 What does كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمْ mean?  [كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمْ · meaning]
turns on: the sense of kataba and the force of ʿalaykum
- 2:180/q01/p1 Prescribed and established as an obligation — Muqātil compares 2:216 and 57:27; Ibn Qutayba cites 2:178 and 4:77; Ibn al-Jawzī lists fasting, retaliation, wills, and fighting. Rāzī argues that both kataba and ʿalaykum indicate obligation, as in 2:183 and 3:97. QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author: Kataba means the ruling was prescribed and established. NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author: كتب means prescribed as an obligation. [14 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 2:180/q01/p2 Decreed or settled — Ibn Qutayba adduces 58:21, 9:51, and 3:154; al-Wāḥidī adds 59:3. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 2:180/q01/p3 Made or placed — Ibn Qutayba cites 58:22, 3:53, and 7:156; al-Wāḥidī cites 58:22, 5:83, and 7:156. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 2:180/q01/p4 God wrote future events on the Preserved Tablet — Ibn Qutayba traces kitāb to what God wrote on the Preserved Tablet. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]
- 2:180/q01/p5 Writing fixes an obligation against forgetfulness — Al-Wāḥidī derives the obligatory sense from writing something down to secure it from forgetting. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 2:180/q01/p6 The root denotes gathering and joining — Ibn al-Jawzī derives kitāba from gathering letters, citing fastening a she-mule and stitching. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 2:180/q01/p7 A mukātaba is an emancipation contract — Ibn al-Jawzī notes the agreed payment by which a slave is freed. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 2:180/q01/p8 God is the understood agent of the passive verb — SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author: كتب is passive; its omitted agent is God, understood from context and left out for brevity. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

- 2:180/q03/p1 The plural address applies individually to each believer — ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author: The plural address means each believer individually, then the clause switches to one of you; the expected plural was avoided by following the universal sense of upon you, a rare construction illustrated by poetry. QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · عمرو بن العاص: Near death, Amr ibn al-As began dictating a will; his son Abdullah said it consumed property belonging to both and suggested asking siblings' approval. QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · جرير: Jarir calls himself the death people have heard about, likewise illustrating the expression. BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author: “When death comes” means when its causes have arrived and its signs have appeared.
