# 2:86: 26 questions

## 2:86/q01 Who are 'those' in this verse?  [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the demonstrative is identified from the preceding account of Israel or only from its own relative clause.
- 2:86/q01/p1 The Jews or Israelites just described as believing part of the Book while violating other covenant commands. — Al-Tabari specifies ransoming captives while killing and expelling protected people; al-Tabrisi points to 2:85. Ibn Abbas is reported by Ibn al-Jawzi and Abu Hayyan; Ibn Kathir says their selective observance and concealment make their textual testimony unreliable. Abu Hayyan says the demonstrative gathers all their listed traits, as in 2:5; Elmalili adds others like them. Study Quran calls expulsion and ransom hypocrisy, immorality, and a degree of unbelief. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 2:86/q01/p2 They are simply the people God mentions in this verse. — Sa'id ibn Jubayr gives this identification without further specification. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير)]

## 2:86/q02 How do the opening demonstrative and relative clause work grammatically?  [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ ٱلَّذِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether a second-subject analysis has a required link and whether this specific, past relative clause licenses a conditional fa.
- 2:86/q02/p1 أولئك is the subject and الذين, with its relative clause, is the predicate. — Al-Nahhas states the subject-predicate construction. Abu Hayyan and al-Samin identify the relative as predicate; al-Samin calls this the correct opening parse. [3 notes: NAHHAS; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 2:86/q02/p2 أولئك is the first subject; الذين is a second subject whose predicate is the punishment clause. — Abu Hayyan reports the proposal, and al-Alusi mentions identity of the two referents as its proposed substitute for a linking pronoun but calls it weak. Abu Hayyan and al-Samin object that the nested clause has no link back to أولئك; the analogy هذا زيد منطلق yields separate predicates or a substitute, not an unlinked clause. They add that the specified relative and its past clause lack conditional force to license the fa. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 2:86/q03 How is the fa and the punishment clause attached to what precedes?  [فَلَا يُخَفَّفُ عَنْهُمُ ٱلْعَذَابُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the fa coordinates clauses within the relative, marks an implied consequence, or can begin a second predicate through conditional force.
- 2:86/q03/p1 The fa clause is coordinated with the relative clause, so it remains within the description of those people. — Al-Wahidi makes it part of their relative description. Al-Jishumi prefers this because it follows the text without positing omitted words. Abu Hayyan says two relative-clause complements may have actions in different times; al-Samin agrees. Al-Alusi lists it. Al-Itqan notes the causal fa favors connecting across a permitted stop. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ITQAN; JISHUMI+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 2:86/q03/p2 The fa introduces a consequence of their purchase, with an implied clause. — Al-Jishumi lists this alternative but prefers direct coordination; Ibn Ashur calls the fa consequential because so grave a crime warrants severe punishment and no helper. [2 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:86/q03/p3 The punishment clause is a second predicate of أولئك, with fa licensed by the relative's conditional sense. — Al-Alusi reports the parse. Abu Hayyan objects that conditional force in a relative clause does not pass to the demonstrative it predicates; al-Alusi likewise objects. Al-Samin says fa cannot modify that second predicate. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 2:86/q04 What does buying worldly life with the Hereafter mean?  [ٱشْتَرَوُا۟ ٱلْحَيَوٰةَ ٱلدُّنْيَا بِٱلْءَاخِرَةِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the exchange is read through outward worldly benefits or through the heart's desires and divine approval.
- 2:86/q04/p1 They chose transient worldly gains and surrendered faith, reward, or eternal bliss in the Hereafter; the transaction is figurative. — Muqatil names food gained from the lower classes; al-Tabari names leadership over the weak and ignorant and poor food, and Qatada contrasts little worldly gain with much afterlife gain, also transmitted by Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Durr. Al-Wahidi contrasts small and great gain. Al-Jishumi names abandoning religion and the Messenger for disbelief and adornment, explicitly calling the sale metaphorical. Al-Tabrisi names leadership and lowly benefits. Ibn Atiyya and al-Alusi stress they could have kept the Hereafter; Abu Hayyan says actual trade exchanges goods and price, extended in Arabic to choices, and contrasts transient adornment with lasting bliss. Al-Baghawi glosses exchange; al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Ibn Kathir and others gloss preference. Al-Nasafi compares the buyer's choice. Al-Wahidi al-Wajiz is not the source here; al-Qurtubi says he discussed this expression earlier without repeating it. Bursevi says their rejection of scripture preserved allies' benefits. Elmalili says they forfeited their future share; Ibn Ashur parallels 2:16's purchase of misguidance with guidance. Kur'an Yolu connects worldly advantage to their punishment and earlier disgrace. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:86/q04/p2 In an inward reading, worldly life is the heart's proximate desires, and the Hereafter is what is joined to the Lord's approval. — Abu Hayyan attributes this to some interpreters of inner meanings. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعض أرباب المعاني)]

## 2:86/q05 What is the force of the ba in the exchange expression?  [بِٱلْءَاخِرَةِ · grammar]
- 2:86/q05/p1 The ba marks what is given up as the price in a purchase. — Bint al-Shati says this is the usual Quranic construction and adduces 2:175's exchange of guidance for error and forgiveness for punishment; her note also mentions 9:9. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 2:86/q06 What does dunyā mean and derive from?  [ٱلدُّنْيَا · meaning]
- 2:86/q06/p1 It is the near or lower life, from nearness or lowliness, contrasted with the higher or later Hereafter. — Abu Hayyan derives it from d-n-w, nearness, and says it is the feminine of adnā. Al-Harali, quoted by al-Biqa'i, derives it from lowliness and stresses lower rank and immediacy. Elmalili allows dunū or danāʾa, meaning nearest or very low, and says the Quranic phrase uses it adjectivally even though it can be a noun. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ELMALILI]

## 2:86/q07 What forms and uses are recorded for dunyā?  [ٱلدُّنْيَا · grammar]
turns on: Dialectal treatment of the original final waw.
- 2:86/q07/p1 Dunyā is an adjective and noun; as an adjective its ya replaces an original waw, and its final alif marks feminine form. — Abu Hayyan derives it from d-n-w, cites Quranic worldly-life phrases in 10:24 and 18:45, and says omission of its article is confined to poetry, citing a line. Elmalili notes its nominal use against the Hereafter but says al-hayāt al-dunyā is adjectival. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 2:86/q07/p2 Najd and Tamim pronounce or write dunyā with a final alif, while Hijaz and Banu Asad use dunwā. — Ibn al-Sarraj, as reported by Abu Hayyan, likens the latter form to waw-final verbal nouns. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن السراج)]

## 2:86/q08 Why pair worldly life with the Hereafter in this wording?  [ٱلْحَيَوٰةَ ٱلدُّنْيَا بِٱلْءَاخِرَةِ · rhetoric]
- 2:86/q08/p1 The pair has reciprocal ellipsis: worldly life implies a higher life, and the Hereafter implies an immediate life. — Al-Biqa'i explains each expressed term by its omitted counterpart. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:86/q09 What does the verse teach about this choice?  [* · interpretation]
- 2:86/q09/p1 Trading the Hereafter for this life is a ruinous, blameworthy choice that causes their sins and loss. — Abu Hayyan calls the chooser cheated in judgment and unsound in reason, and contrasts a tarnished transient life with a pure enduring one and eternal bliss with fire fueled by people and stones. Bursevi says pursuing one world's pleasures forfeits the other's and urges resisting Satan, seeking the Hereafter and keeping primordial religion. Elmalili traces covenant breaking and disbelief to loss of faith in the Hereafter: for a few days they alter God's books for coins and wrong His servants; their wish for a few numbered days stems from unbelief, while evil-enveloped souls remain in Hell. Al-Qushayri says choosing anything over God loses both worlds and cites a poem about self-sufficient people turning away. Al-Jishumi says the threat benefits morally responsible hearers by deterring punishment-earning acts. Ibn Ashur says deviation brings punishment in both worlds. [12 notes: QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:86/q10 What does lightening the punishment mean?  [يُخَفَّفُ ٱلْعَذَابُ · meaning]
- 2:86/q10/p1 It means making punishment less heavy, severe, or difficult, whether by reducing it or stopping it. — Al-Wahidi relates khiffa to a decrease of weight. Al-Tabrisi calls lightness the opposite of heaviness and equates easing with reduced severity. Abu Hayyan glosses takhfif as making easier or lighter. Al-Jishumi says reduction may end the punishment or ease its parts; Abu Hayyan says negating the genus excludes every interruption or reduction, temporary or permanent. Ibn Kathir specifies not even an hour of respite, and al-Jishumi adds no delay. Abu Hayyan says denying even mitigation more strongly denies removal. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]

## 2:86/q11 How forcefully is relief from punishment denied?  [فَلَا يُخَفَّفُ عَنْهُمُ ٱلْعَذَابُ · interpretation]
- 2:86/q11/p1 Their punishment is not eased; denying even its mitigation emphatically excludes relief. — Al-Baghawi states that it will not be made lighter. Al-Alusi says that, on the first interpretation of the punishment and help clauses, denying any relief follows especially emphatically from denying lightening. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:86/q12 How can physical lightness and heaviness be explained?  [يُخَفَّفُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether weight follows components, internal forces, directed dependence, or a heavy body's elevation.
- 2:86/q12/p1 By the constituent parts of a thing, or the increase and decrease of bodies. — Abu Ali, reported by al-Jishumi, explains weight through constituents; al-Tabrisi reports an account in terms of bodily decrease or increase. Al-Jishumi favors Abu Hashim instead because things with equal parts can weigh differently. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي); TABRISI(قيل); JISHUMI(أبو هاشم)-]
- 2:86/q12/p2 By the forces inherent in an object. — Abu Hashim advances this view; al-Jishumi calls it correct because equal quantities of parts can differ in weight. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو هاشم)+]
- 2:86/q12/p3 Heaviness is downward-directed dependence, and lightness upward-directed dependence. — Al-Tabrisi reports this as another account. [1 notes: TABRISI(قيل)]
- 2:86/q12/p4 Alleviation makes a heavy, low thing buoyant and elevated, like a stone rising in air. — Al-Harali gives this illustration as quoted by al-Biqa'i. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]

## 2:86/q13 Does the unlightened punishment concern this world, the Hereafter, or both?  [فَلَا يُخَفَّفُ عَنْهُمُ ٱلْعَذَابُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether al-adhab names the final promised punishment alone or also worldly disgrace and levy; some argue from the order of denying relief and help.
- 2:86/q13/p1 It concerns punishment in both this world and the Hereafter. — Al-Wahidi al-Wajiz states both. Al-Baydawi specifies no easing of jizya in this life and punishment hereafter; Abu Hayyan reports al-Zamakhshari's similar reading. Al-Biqa'i, Bursevi, Ibn Ashur and Kur'an Yolu speak of both abodes. Al-Alusi includes this as general punishment. Bursevi says neither intercession nor force rescues them. [8 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:86/q13/p2 It concerns punishment in the Hereafter. — Al-Wahidi al-Wajiz reports this alternative; Ibn Atiyya states it. Al-Tabrisi links unrelieved punishment to having no share in the Hereafter's bliss. Al-Alusi allows the promised Judgment Day punishment, and some prefer this reading of both punishment and help because, on the two-world reading, rescue would more naturally be denied before reduction. [5 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)+]

## 2:86/q14 Why is no relief from punishment available to them?  [فَلَا يُخَفَّفُ عَنْهُمُ ٱلْعَذَابُ · theology]
- 2:86/q14/p1 They have no share in the Hereafter's bliss, whereas punishment may be eased for those who do have a share in its reward. — Al-Tabari states the comparison; al-Tabrisi likewise makes their absence of reward the ground of punishment without reduction or mitigation. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI]

## 2:86/q15 What is the force of the negation in 'will not be lightened'?  [فَلَا يُخَفَّفُ · grammar]
- 2:86/q15/p1 The apparent, usual force of lā is future negation, excluding all instances of mitigation. — Abu Hayyan states the future force and argues that negating the genus excludes any interruption or diminution, briefly or permanently. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]

## 2:86/q16 What does it mean that they will not be helped?  [وَلَا هُمْ يُنصَرُونَ · interpretation]
- 2:86/q16/p1 No one will ward off, prevent, or rescue them from God's punishment. — Muqatil, al-Jishumi, al-Baghawi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir and Ibn Ashur express protection or rescue. Ibn al-Jawzi reports nasr as protection or prevention and cites 26:93, 40:29 and 37:25 asking who can shield people from divine torment. Ibn Kathir calls the punishment everlasting. Ibn Abi Hatim reports Abu al-Aliya's comparison with 77:35–36, where people cannot speak or offer excuses. [10 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:86/q17 When and from what are they denied help?  [وَلَا هُمْ يُنصَرُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the denial tracks punishment in both abodes, the final punishment, or continuing worldly disgrace.
- 2:86/q17/p1 They receive no protection in either this world or the Hereafter. — Ibn Atiyya states both. Al-Baydawi says neither worldly jizya nor afterlife punishment is repelled; Abu Hayyan reports al-Zamakhshari's two-world view. Al-Biqa'i and Bursevi include both, Bursevi ruling out intercession or force. Al-Alusi gives lack of worldly protection from poll tax and no escape after death as one interpretation. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:86/q17/p2 They receive no help against the Hereafter's punishment. — Al-Tabrisi specifies the Hereafter. Ibn Kathir speaks of their everlasting punishment. Al-Alusi pairs a Judgment Day reading of the first clause with this reading; some prefer that pairing because the alternative order of denying reduction before rescue seems less apt. [4 notes: TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)+]
- 2:86/q17/p3 Their worldly disgrace will not be removed through the end of this life. — Al-Alusi gives this as another possible referent of denied help. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:86/q18 How is 'nor will they be helped' parsed?  [وَلَا هُمْ يُنصَرُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether هم is an independent subject or governed by an omitted explanatory verb; the preceding verbal clause favors ishtighal, while the claimed preference of lā for verbs is contested.
- 2:86/q18/p1 هم is an initial subject and ينصرون its predicate, so a nominal clause joins the preceding verbal clause. — Abu Hayyan and al-Samin report this parse; al-Samin notes that ينصرون is nominative as predicate. Al-Samin resists Ibn al-Sayyid's preference for an omitted verb, saying lā does not favor verbal syntax; Abu Hayyan likewise rejects that premise and says initial-subject syntax would otherwise be stronger. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن السيد)]
- 2:86/q18/p2 هم is raised by an omitted verb explained by ينصرون, an ishtighal construction coordinating verbal clauses. — Abu Hayyan prefers it because the preceding clause is verbal, while acknowledging initial-subject syntax elsewhere. Al-Samin calls it stronger and says the explicit verb then has no syntactic position; he cites the poem وإن هو لم يحمل على النفس ضيمها for the detached pronoun. Ibn al-Sayyid argues lā favors verbal syntax; Abu Hayyan and al-Samin reject that particular reason, and al-Samin calls Ibn al-Sayyid's preference disfavored despite its strength as an argument. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو محمد بن السيد); SAMIN-DURR(ابن السيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 2:86/q19 Why use this passive ending and subject-first order?  [وَلَا هُمْ يُنصَرُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Different features of the wording are explained: passive voice and the placement of the pronoun.
- 2:86/q19/p1 The passive is brief and general, and preserves the established verse endings. — Abu Hayyan says an active verb with an unspecified subject would explicitly mean nobody helps, but the passive already conveys that. Ibn al-Sayyid, reported by Abu Hayyan, also favors passive voice for generality. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو محمد بن السيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 2:86/q19/p2 The subject-first order serves the verse ending and the meaning of taqwa, without implying exclusivity. — Al-Alusi states these functions of the order. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:86/q20 Does the denial of help also exclude intercession?  [وَلَا هُمْ يُنصَرُونَ · theology]
- 2:86/q20/p1 Yes; they have no intercessor who can relieve their punishment. — Al-Jishumi infers that an intercessor would lighten punishment. Bursevi says neither intercession nor force can ward it off. Ibn Ashur connects the ending to 2:48, which denies intercession, ransom, and help. [3 notes: JISHUMI; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:86/q21 Is divine support necessarily a reward reserved for believers?  [يُنصَرُونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether support is intrinsically reward or can be a commanded act toward a sinner.
- 2:86/q21/p1 God's support is given to believers as a reward. — Abu Ali holds this; al-Jishumi calls it correct. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي)+]
- 2:86/q21/p2 Divine support can be reward or something else, and even a sinner may be supported because support is commanded. — Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn Ali gives this alternative, as reported by al-Jishumi. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو بكر أحمد بن علي)]

## 2:86/q22 How is this exchange image applied in sales law?  [ٱشْتَرَوُا۟ · law]
- 2:86/q22/p1 Malik disallows a purchase allowing selection among unequal individual units, or unequal exchange of such goods. — Ibn Atiyya reports Malik's application to sales involving items of differing qualities, such as slaughtered partridges. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(مالك)]

## 2:86/q23 How does this verse connect to the surrounding account and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: These are complementary links: the local context and verbal parallels elsewhere.
- 2:86/q23/p1 It gathers the preceding accusations and explains their worldly disgrace and coming punishment. — Al-Tabari recalls ransoming fellow Jews while killing and expelling others in breach of covenant; Abu Hayyan likens the summary demonstrative to 2:5. Ibn Kathir sees knowing selective observance and concealment of prophetic descriptions. Elmalili traces the sins to the sale of the Hereafter and says the next discussion of hostility to the Messenger further explains eternal punishment. Kur'an Yolu links the verse to prior worldly disgrace; Study Quran calls the expulsion and ransom hypocritical and immoral. Al-Samarrai relates no help to the fighting and war of 2:84–85, where combatants seek victory. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 2:86/q23/p2 Its purchase image and ending echo other Quranic verses and parallel formulations. — Ibn Ashur compares the demonstrative with 2:5, buying with 2:16, and the ending with 2:48's denial of intercession, ransom, and help. Abu al-Aliya, transmitted by Ibn Abi Hatim, compares 77:35–36 on inability to speak or excuse oneself. Al-Burhan catalogs differences among parallel passages in particles and forms, including after/from, to/for, and then/fa, without explaining each variation. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية); BURHAN]

## 2:86/q24 What warning about spoils does the verse carry?  [* · interpretation]
- 2:86/q24/p1 The lack of help warns against seeking booty through campaigns or embezzling spoils. — Al-Biqa'i draws this warning. He transmits a Prophetic report from Ibn Abbas through Malik: when embezzlement appears among a people, God puts fear in their hearts. He also cites the Joshua story: Israel stole devoted spoils at Jericho, lost at Ai, Achan was exposed by lot and stoned and burned with his household and goods, and Ai then fell. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 2:86/q25 Why is a stop before the punishment clause permitted?  [فَلَا يُخَفَّفُ · grammar]
- 2:86/q25/p1 The following fa favors connection, while the verb begins an independent sentence that permits a stop. — Al-Itqan gives both the causal-resulting fa and independent sentence structure as grounds for a reasoned stop. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 2:86/q26 What does khizy in the preceding warning mean?  [* · meaning]
- 2:86/q26/p1 It means humiliation, affliction, or shame. — Abu Hayyan cites al-Jawhari's verb khazi and Ibn al-Sikkit's gloss of falling into affliction; he also records forms for a shamed man, group, and woman. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
