# 75:21: 15 questions

## 75:21/q01 How are the verbs of 75:20–21 read, and what does each reading do?  [وَتَذَرُونَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the verbs turn to direct address or continue the earlier third-person reference; the notes differ in their attribution of particular readers.
- 75:21/q01/p1 Second-person plural: ‘you love’ and ‘you leave,’ directly addressing and rebuking the audience. — Al-Farrāʾ reports this from ʿAlī; Ibn Khālawayh, Abū ʿAlī through al-Wāḥidī, and al-Fārisī explain it as an address Muhammad is to deliver, with an implied ‘tell them.’ Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Nasafī assign it to Medinan and Kufan readers; al-Fārisī names Ibn Kathīr and Abū ʿAmr, while Ibn al-Jawzī instead assigns these two to yāʾ. Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr call it the majority reading; the latter says the turn to direct address sharpens admonition of the polytheists. Abū ʿUbayd selects it; al-Qurṭubī says direct confrontation strengthens blame. Al-Farrāʾ and al-Wāḥidī compare a shift between address and absence at 10:22; al-Ālūsī says this reading includes the listener. Al-Qirāʾāt-ER notes that the alternate prefix changes person without changing root w-dh-r or object. [19 notes: FARRA(علي بن أبي طالب); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي,الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المدينة والكوفة، والآخرون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(مدني وكوفي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); QIRAAT-ER(Imp); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد)+]
- 75:21/q01/p2 Third-person plural: ‘they love’ and ‘they leave,’ continuing reference to collective humanity or the earlier denier. — Ibn Khālawayh traces yāʾ to ‘the human’ of 75:13; al-Fārisī and Abū ʿAlī take that singular generically and compare 70:19 and 70:22; Abū Ḥātim prefers this return to al-insān, with 76:27 cited. Ibn ʿĀshūr connects it to the third-person pronouns and collective ‘human’ of 75:14. Al-Fārisī calls it the other readers’ reading; al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī likewise contrast it with Medinan and Kufan tāʾ. Ibn al-Jawzī names Ibn Kathīr and Abū ʿAmr, while al-Bayḍāwī adds Ibn ʿĀmir and Basrans and Ibn ʿĀshūr adds Yaʿqūb; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī list Mujāhid, al-Ḥasan, Qatāda, al-Jaḥdarī, Ibn Kathīr, and Abū ʿAmr. Al-Zamakhsharī calls this more emphatic; al-Ālūsī finds it more eloquent because it gently turns the Prophet away from direct reproach. Study Quran also reports it; al-Qirāʾāt-ER specifies ya- for ta-. [17 notes: FARRA(علي بن أبي طالب); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المدينة والكوفة، والآخرون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن كثير وابن عامر والبصريون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد والحسن وقتادة والجحدري وابن كثير وأبو عمرو); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن كثير وابن عامر وأبو عمرو ويعقوب); QIRAAT-ER(Imp); STUDYQURAN(some other schools of recitation); ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو ومجاهد والحسن وقتادة والجحدري)+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حاتم)+]

## 75:21/q02 Why use تَذَرُونَ rather than another verb for leaving?  [وَتَذَرُونَ · rhetoric]
- 75:21/q02/p1 Its form and unfavorable associations suit a hurried, blameful context. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says تذرون comes from توذرون with its wāw dropped, a shortening suited to the sūra’s hurried tone. He says وذر generally bears blame: وذرة can mean foul-smelling like meat, يا ابن شامة الوذر is an insult for a slanderer, and a hadith calls some women الوذرة المذرة. By comparison, ودع can evoke ease, comfort, and calm, and رجل وديع is a settled, quiet man; this context therefore favors وذر over such alternatives as تتركون or a ودع form. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:21/q03 What does leaving the Hereafter mean here?  [وَتَذَرُونَ ٱلْءَاخِرَةَ · interpretation]
- 75:21/q03/p1 They prefer the present world and neglect, abandon work for, or deny the life to come. — Muqātil and al-Wāḥidī specify Meccan unbelievers who seek and work for worldly life instead; al-Ṭabarī says they trust the present and deny future reward. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī gloss it as preferring the world. Al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, and al-Bursevī make lack of deeds for the next life explicit; al-Bursevī adds denial. Al-Nasafī mentions its abode and delights. Asad describes giving no thought to the Hereafter and Judgment; al-Qummī simply glosses it as abandoning the hereafter. Qatāda qualifies the general tendency: God protects some people from choosing the present. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المدينة والكوفة، والآخرون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(القمي); ASAD-NOTES]

## 75:21/q04 Does ٱلْءَاخِرَةَ denote the next life generally or something more specific?  [ٱلْءَاخِرَةَ · referent]
turns on: Whether ‘the Hereafter’ names the future life as a whole or uses it for its desired reward, Paradise.
- 75:21/q04/p1 The afterlife, including its abode, reward, and delights. — Al-Ṭabarī calls it the later life's reward; al-Nasafī specifies its abode and delights; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it the life to come. This is the general sense in the other explanations of neglecting it. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 75:21/q04/p2 Paradise specifically. — Al-Wāḥidī transmits this gloss from al-Kalbī; al-Qurṭubī attributes it to an unnamed exegetical opinion. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]

## 75:21/q05 Who is rebuked by this plural accusation?  [وَتَذَرُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural rebuke stays focused on the deniers being discussed or extends the human disposition and warning to the species.
- 75:21/q05/p1 The Meccan or Quraysh unbelievers and deniers of Judgment. — Muqātil, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and al-Qurṭubī identify Meccan disbelievers; Abū Ḥayyān says the preceding discussion returns to the denier and the majority's direct address rebukes Quraysh unbelievers. Al-Ṭībī connects the person’s false excuses and persistence in sin to expecting no resurrection; Iṣlāḥī says the discourse returns to deniers of Judgment after the aside. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the direct reading admonishes polytheists. [10 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 75:21/q05/p2 Human beings generally, whose natural haste exposes them to this fault, with divinely protected exceptions. — Al-Bayḍāwī says singular ‘the human’ denotes the species and the plural generalizes rebuke. Al-Biqāʿī likewise takes the plural by the collective sense of human and excepts isolated people protected by God; Qatāda makes the same mercy exception. Al-Qāshānī, al-Ālūsī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, Asad, al-Sāmarrāʾī, and Study Quran describe a general human address or tendency. Al-Ālūsī adds that even the Prophet should guard against human impulse; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says he is not the continuing addressee of this accusation. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الزمخشري); STUDYQURAN]

## 75:21/q06 What drives people to leave the Hereafter?  [وَتَذَرُونَ ٱلْءَاخِرَةَ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes explain abandonment through immediate desire and haste, disbelief in the future, or aversion to the consequences of one's deeds; some combine these causes.
- 75:21/q06/p1 Attachment to immediate pleasure and human haste displace thought and work for the Hereafter, and can fuel denial. — Al-Biqāʿī likens the two lives to rival co-wives: love of one blinds and deafens one to the other; he contrasts the Prophet's soul, urging obedience, with theirs, which urges fleeting gain. Al-Qāshānī, al-Ālūsī, al-Sāmarrāʾī, and al-Zamakhsharī as reported by al-Sāmarrāʾī connect this to human haste; al-Sāmarrāʾī cites 21:37. His report of al-Ālūsī adds delayed repentance, false hope, indulgence, and the wish to sin ahead (75:5). Ibn Kathīr says preoccupation with this world explains heedlessness, opposition to revelation, and denial of resurrection; Iṣlāḥī says conscience already testifies to Judgment, but luxuries and unwillingness to forgo present pleasure produce excuses. Al-Sāmarrāʾī likewise says the preference accounts for denial and turning away. [14 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد); QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الألوسي,الزمخشري)]
- 75:21/q06/p2 They believe in what is present but deny the future resurrection and its reward. — Al-Ṭabarī explicitly pairs belief in the present with denial of the future. Ibn Kathīr and Abū Ḥayyān describe a denier who rejects resurrection and therefore disregards its reward; the latter says he is concerned instead with perishable gain. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 75:21/q06/p3 They hate the Hereafter because their own deeds will harm them there. — Al-Biqāʿī makes their anticipated harmful outcome the reason for aversion and abandonment. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 75:21/q07 What conduct does the verse condemn?  [وَتَذَرُونَ ٱلْءَاخِرَةَ · interpretation]
- 75:21/q07/p1 Worldly attachment becomes blameworthy when it displaces obedience and the Hereafter; worldly enjoyment with obedience is allowed. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the two acts together deserve censure and cites 28:77 for enjoying worldly life while obeying revelation. Kur’an Yolu says blessings were created for people and cites 7:31–33; abandonment of the Hereafter because of love for them is censured. Junayd says pursuing and turning toward the world costs a person a share of the Hereafter. Al-Biqāʿī's rival co-wives image says love of one drives actions that distance one from the other; Qatāda notes exceptions whom God protects. [5 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد); TAB-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 75:21/q08 How does the verse's wording sharpen the reproach?  [وَتَذَرُونَ ٱلْءَاخِرَةَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes identify different rhetorical devices in the wording rather than opposing interpretations.
- 75:21/q08/p1 The plural and direct address broaden and intensify the rebuke. — Al-Bayḍāwī says plural address generalizes the charge despite the earlier singular human; al-Biqāʿī says the plural follows the collective sense of human. Al-Qurṭubī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say direct confrontation makes blame or admonition more forceful. Al-Ālūsī cites the proverb that a hide is reproached only while sound to underline how deeply worldly love resists admonition; he also says the second-person reading includes the listener. Al-Farrāʾ points to the Qurʾān's address shifts and compares 10:22. [7 notes: FARRA; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 75:21/q08/p2 The paired clauses use iḥtibāk, each supplying what the other leaves unstated. — Al-Biqāʿī says loving the present implies hatred of the Hereafter, while leaving the Hereafter implies turning to and taking the present. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 75:21/q09 How does this accusation relate to the preceding instruction to the Prophet in 75:16–19?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether 75:16–19 is only a parenthetical change of addressee or also supplies a thematic comparison about haste; al-Ālūsī separately rejects an allegation of textual alteration.
- 75:21/q09/p1 The instruction is an aside; the discourse now returns to deniers or humanity, and the charge is not addressed to the Prophet as a continuation. — Abū Ḥayyān says the discourse returns from addressing the Prophet to the earlier denier. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the instruction parenthetical and unrelated to the verses on either side. Iṣlāḥī calls it an aside about patience in piecemeal revelation before the original sequence resumes; Kur’an Yolu likewise sees a return to humanity and its denial of resurrection. Study Quran notes the shift from singular Prophet to plural humanity; Elmalılı notes a general address after the Prophet. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 75:21/q09/p2 The insertion forms a purposeful link: haste in receiving revelation makes haste toward worldly ruin still more blameworthy. — Al-Ālūsī says the Prophet is lovingly guided away from haste and told to wait, then the address generalizes to humanity; if haste is barred in receiving the healing, merciful Qurʾān (75:16), it is still less fitting in pursuit of depravity. Al-Sāmarrāʾī reports al-Zamakhsharī linking the tongue instruction to humanity's haste and rebuke, and includes that instruction among the sūra's haste scenes. Al-Ālūsī rejects a hostile inference from the passage's placement that the Qurʾān was altered, saying scholars answered it. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الزمخشري)]
- 75:21/q09/p3 The intervening tongue prohibition is irrelevant and proves textual alteration. — Al-Ālūsī attributes this claim to some heretics and early extremist Rāfiḍīs, rejects it, and says scholars have answered it. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:21/q10 What earlier claim or excuse does this verse answer?  [* · links]
- 75:21/q10/p1 It exposes worldly desire as the real reason behind denial of resurrection and false excuses. — Al-Ālūsī says the explicit charge develops 75:5 after 75:16–19 interrupts, making reproach progressively stronger. His report from al-Ṭībī links kallā bal tuḥibbūn to 75:15: the sinner persisted while expecting no resurrection or judgment. Ibn ʿĀshūr reads it as rebutting their excuses and also reports a new-clause analysis that invalidates the excuses of 75:15 for the same reason. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī relates the shift to the denied resurrection of 75:3 and the desire to sin ahead in 75:5; Iṣlāḥī says the deniers' excuses contradict conscience's testimony to Judgment. Al-Sāmarrāʾī likewise relates love and abandonment to earlier denial and turning away. [7 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:21/q11 What does kallā do at the start of this accusation?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether kallā is anchored in the worldly preference and excuses or the intervening instruction against the Prophet's haste; Elmalılı also reports an affirmative sense.
- 75:21/q11/p1 It rebukes or rejects their worldly haste, preference, or excuses. — Al-Zamakhsharī, as reported by Abū Ḥayyān, calls it a rebuke; Abū Ḥayyān's majority-reading account says it rebukes Quraysh for letting desire overcome the Hereafter. Al-Bursevī says it rebukes preference for the present and urges attention to death. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the rebuttal to cancel excuses; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it displaces their assumption against resurrection with the real explanation. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور,الزمخشري); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 75:21/q11/p2 It rejects haste in the preceding prophetic instruction, or can mean ‘indeed.’ — Elmalılı gives both possibilities after the direct address to the Prophet. Al-Ālūsī says it guides the Messenger away from haste and emphasizes patient waiting out of divine love. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 75:21/q12 How does the warning develop after this verse?  [* · links]
- 75:21/q12/p1 The passage turns to death and the good or evil consequences of these preferences. — Al-Ālūsī says the death scene at 75:26 reinforces the warning about loving the present and neglecting the next; al-Bursevī says death ends one's tie to worldly immediacy. Al-Naḥḥās briefly notes that the following verse is later than the first. [3 notes: NAHHAS; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 75:21/q13 How does leaving the Hereafter fit the sūra's larger pattern?  [* · links]
- 75:21/q13/p1 The sūra contrasts haste and omission with self-reproach, records deeds, and repeatedly pairs opposing outcomes. — Al-Biqāʿī connects God encompassing and counting all things at 72:27–28 to the preceding preservation of deniers' deeds, and contrasts their worldly-driven souls with the Prophet's reproaching soul. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says neglect of the Hereafter relates to the self-reproach of 75:2: one regrets both acting hastily and postponing a duty until its good is lost. He lists the desire to sin ahead, hurrying the tongue, and preferring the immediate life as haste scenes; he also lists paired themes across 75:1–2, 9, 13–15, and 20–21. The two final groups, those working for the Hereafter and those abandoning it, fulfill the opening oath at 75:1. [9 notes: BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:21/q14 Where else is this worldly preference expressed in the Qurʾān?  [* · links]
- 75:21/q14/p1 The wording or warning parallels 76:27 in Sūrat al-Insān. — Muqātil points to its opening phrase; al-Thaʿlabī, al-Qurṭubī, and Study Quran also cite it as a close parallel. Abū Ḥātim's preference for third-person forms is reported with the same passage mentioned. [5 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حاتم); STUDYQURAN]

## 75:21/q15 What inward contrast is drawn from leaving the Hereafter?  [وَتَذَرُونَ ٱلْءَاخِرَةَ · inward]
- 75:21/q15/p1 Love of worldly sensual pleasure causes neglect of the afterlife's blessing of obscurity. — Al-Bursevī transmits this inward interpretation from al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyyah. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
