# 14:44: 20 questions

## 14:44/q01 How does this warning connect to its surrounding passage?  [* · links]
- 14:44/q01/p1 It resumes the warning after 14:42 and calls the Prophet to persist while God delays judgment. — Al-Wāḥidī says the description of disbelievers and their resurrection state ends and address returns to the Prophet; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the reminder that God is not heedless consoles him. Jishumī says respite establishes proof, not divine heedlessness. Ibn ʿAṭiyyah says the passage threatens wrongdoers and consoles the wronged, and Muhammad is addressed for the benefit of others. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 14:44/q01/p2 It belongs to the sūrah's closing sequence of warnings and assurance. — Iṣlāḥī says 14:42–52 completes the sūrah's discourse with reassurance to the Prophet and threat to deniers; if people do not heed him, this verse indicates his next task. Study Quran notes divine chastisement in this world and the next throughout the sūrah, citing 14:16–17, 29–31, 42–44, 48–50. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 14:44/q01/p3 It leads into a rebuke supported by the fate of earlier peoples. — Al-Qushayrī addresses later hearers who imitate punished predecessors and are fooled by respite; he also reports the warning that proximity to sinful people shares their ruin. Asad says the next verse recalls earlier peoples and parables. Kuranyolu says deniers lived near the lands of Noah, ʿĀd and Thamūd; prophets' examples showed that the Creator can resurrect and punish, but they ignored them. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî)]

## 14:44/q02 Who are the people Muhammad is told to warn?  [ٱلنَّاسَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the general word al-nās denotes everyone warned or the disbelievers identified by the subsequent punishment.
- 14:44/q02/p1 All people, with the wrongdoers singled out for punishment. — Al-Biqāʿī and Bursawī say all people. Bursawī says unbelievers are the primary audience, believers the secondary audience although they will not be punished. Al-Jubbāʾī and Abū Muslim include all morally responsible people, comparing 36:11. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows all people with disbelievers specified afterward. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the address is universal. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الجبائي وأبو مسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 14:44/q02/p2 The disbelievers or polytheists, especially the Meccans, are the intended people. — Muqātil identifies Meccan unbelievers. Ibn ʿAbbās identifies Mecca's inhabitants or Makkan idolaters; al-Wāḥidī adds that their following Muhammad would have ended disagreement and al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra's belief would have brought all along. Abū Ḥayyān says the subsequent punishment of wrongdoers identifies the people. Ibn ʿĀshūr also allows the familiar polytheists. [6 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAbbās); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 14:44/q02/p3 Those to whom Muhammad was sent to call to Islam. — Al-Ṭabarī frames the warning as addressed to his mission's audience. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]

## 14:44/q03 What does the command to warn seek to do?  [وَأَنذِرِ · interpretation]
- 14:44/q03/p1 It frightens people with coming harm so they turn before punishment arrives. — Al-Baghawī and al-Rāzī gloss warning as making afraid or mentioning harms. Qatāda says it is delivered in worldly life before punishment. Kuranyolu says it lets people prepare for the afterlife before reaching the wrongdoers' plight. Al-Ālūsī reports that naming them people gives the warning a compassionate aim of restraining wrongdoing. [6 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الإسلام)]

## 14:44/q04 How does yawma function grammatically?  [يَوْمَ · grammar]
- 14:44/q04/p1 It is the second object of 'warn,' meaning warn them of that day or its punishment. — Al-Wāḥidī, Jishumī, Ibn ʿAṭiyyah, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say it cannot be a temporal adverb: warning happens now, not on the day of Judgment, death or destruction. Al-Rāzī reports al-Zamakhsharī's second-object analysis. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this an extension from the sense of cautioning and cites a hadith that every prophet warned of the Dajjāl. Al-Baghawī paraphrases it as 'by a day.' Al-Samīn objects to Abū al-Baqāʾ's gloss 'punishment of a day' as redundant. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL]

## 14:44/q05 When is the day on which punishment comes?  [يَوْمَ يَأْتِيهِمُ ٱلْعَذَابُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the plea presupposes return after death, extension at death, or delay of punishment within earthly life; al-Rāzī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī invoke different contextual evidence.
- 14:44/q05/p1 It is the Day of Resurrection or Judgment. — Mujāhid, al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Māwardī, al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyyah, al-Qurṭubī and others identify it so. Al-Rāzī reports broad agreement and prefers this: a return request and rebuke for denying transfer fit Resurrection. Al-Ālūsī prefers the familiar Day of Resurrection. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and Bursawī give this alongside other possibilities. Al-Biqāʿī allows it. Study Quran affirms it. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 14:44/q05/p2 It is the moment of death, when agony begins and the dying meet angels. — Al-Zamakhsharī compares the dying person's plea in 63:10; Abū Muslim uses the same parallel. Al-Rāzī reports but rejects Abū Muslim's timing. Al-Bayḍāwī calls death the first day of punishment; al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān mention death throes and angels without glad news; Bursawī says death is the first punishment. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم); BIQAI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]
- 14:44/q05/p3 It is immediate worldly punishment or extermination of a rejecting community. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī mention destruction amid torment; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī also report it, though al-Ālūsī notes an objection from the preceding restriction. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows threatened worldly punishment. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī holds that this extermination strikes the unjust group rather than every individual (10:103), is the recurring judgment between messengers and rejecting communities, and purges idolatry until the righteous inherit the earth (21:105). He argues that limiting the warning to Resurrection overrides context and verses threatening this community. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 14:44/q06 What does the wording about punishment convey?  [يَأْتِيهِمُ ٱلْعَذَابُ · rhetoric]
- 14:44/q06/p1 The definite punishment recalls the punishment already described. — Al-Rāzī relates the article to the fixed eyes and raised heads of the preceding passage (14:42). [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 14:44/q06/p2 Punishment is singled out to threaten sinners, though the day also brings reward. — Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī say obedient people receive encouragement or reward. Abū Ḥayyān similarly distinguishes the warning to wrongdoers from glad tidings for believers. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 14:44/q06/p3 The 'coming' of punishment figuratively means its occurrence. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the verb metonymic. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 14:44/q07 Who are 'those who did wrong'?  [ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase names a historical set of polytheists, a wider moral class, or the group overtaken by worldly destruction.
- 14:44/q07/p1 They are the unbelievers, especially polytheists who wronged themselves through shirk and denial. — Muqātil specifies Meccan polytheists; al-Ṭabarī says disbelievers wronged themselves; Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Bursawī and Ibn ʿĀshūr specify polytheists or deniers. Al-Ālūsī reports al-Jubbāʾī and Abū Muslim's disbelievers among the warned, and another view includes idolatrous and denying earlier nations. Kuranyolu adds deniers of afterlife and judgment. [13 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الجبائي وأبو مسلم,قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 14:44/q07/p2 The general expression also warns that any wrongdoing can lead to this distress. — Al-Ālūsī says the label records guilt and need not imply continued wrongdoing; al-Qurṭubī identifies the speakers as those wrongdoers on that day. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 14:44/q07/p3 They are the unjust among a community overtaken by extermination. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says punishment removes that subset, while other members remain. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 14:44/q08 How is fa-yaqūlu construed?  [فَيَقُولُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether fa introduces a clause coordinated with 'comes' or an answer to the imperative; the poem and al-ʿAlāʾ's objection address the latter construction.
- 14:44/q08/p1 It is nominative and coordinated with 'comes to them.' — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Ṭabarī deny that it is a subjunctive response to 'warn'; al-Ṭabarī says that a response could itself be nominative as a new sentence, or accusative. Both cite anonymous poetry illustrating the accusative after fa. Al-Farrāʾ reports his teacher al-ʿAlāʾ ibn Siyyābah rejected that accusative, while al-Farrāʾ accepts resumed fa; al-Ṭabarī also reports the teacher's rejection and that al-Farrāʾ said he taught Muʿādh and companions. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, Jishumī and Ibn ʿAṭiyyah also coordinate it with 'comes.' [10 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(العلاء بن سيابة,الفراء); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 14:44/q08/p2 A construction after fa as response to the command is grammatically possible, though not the preferred parsing here. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Ṭabarī cite an anonymous poetic example of accusative 'so we may rest'; al-ʿAlāʾ ibn Siyyābah objected to that construction. [3 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; FARRA-; TAB-FULL(العلاء بن سيابة)-]

## 14:44/q09 What are the wrongdoers asking for in their plea?  [رَبَّنَآ أَخِّرْنَآ إِلَىٰٓ أَجَلٍۢ قَرِيبٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether they speak after entering the Hereafter, at death, or as worldly punishment starts; and whether 'return' denotes earthly life or a renewed state of obligation.
- 14:44/q09/p1 They ask to return to worldly life briefly to repair neglected belief and obedience. — Muqātil says they ask in the Hereafter for life again; Mujāhid asks for worldly time for deeds. Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and others make the same return explicit. Al-Māwardī says truth has become plain, but the afterlife accepts no new repentance or duties. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Biqāʿī include this possibility. Al-Kāshifī paraphrases postponed punishment and return. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 23:99–100; Ibn Kathīr compares rejected pleas in 23:99–100, 63:9–10, 32:12, 6:27–28 and 35:37. Asad points to 6:27; Study Quran cites 6:27–28, 32:12 and 40:49–50; Kuranyolu adds 6:23. Al-Ṭabarī says they seek a short respite to answer truth and worship God alone. [29 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك ومجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN]
- 14:44/q09/p2 They ask for a short extension of their present earthly life before death or punishment. — Al-Zamakhsharī relates this to 63:10. Abū Muslim's death interpretation yields extra days of life; al-Bayḍāwī offers prolonged life. Bursawī says they seek time to believe. Ibn ʿĀshūr says delay is literal if the plea comes at worldly punishment. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says those overtaken by extermination request earthly extension to make up their losses. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 14:44/q09/p3 They ask to return to a state of religious responsibility and obligation. — Al-Rāzī reports this alternative from some, evidenced by their promise to respond and follow. Al-Ālūsī reports that punishment would be lifted and they would briefly regain responsibility to do what pleases God. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 14:44/q10 How should akhkhirnā be construed?  [أَخِّرْنَآ · grammar]
- 14:44/q10/p1 Give us respite or delay us from punishment. — Al-Qurṭubī glosses the verb as respite. Al-Ālūsī allows either delaying the people from punishment or delaying their punishment, with an omitted noun or figurative attribution. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 14:44/q11 What does 'a near term' mean and how is it expressed?  [إِلَىٰٓ أَجَلٍۢ قَرِيبٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the words directly measure the requested time or elliptically identify the world to which they seek return.
- 14:44/q11/p1 It is a short span of time, only enough to answer the call. — Bursawī glosses a short time or limit; Ibn ʿĀshūr says time is likened to distance. Al-Suddī says a short fixed term. [3 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي)]
- 14:44/q11/p2 It elliptically refers to the world as the possessor of a near term. — Saʿdī al-Muftī supplies 'return us to the possessor of a near term,' while postponing punishment. [1 notes: BURSEVI(سعدي المفتي)]

## 14:44/q12 What do they promise by 'we will answer Your call'?  [نُّجِبْ دَعْوَتَكَ · interpretation]
- 14:44/q12/p1 They promise to accept God's call to faith, monotheism and obedience, making up their former refusal. — Muqātil specifies monotheism; al-Ṭabarī says belief without partners; al-Qurṭubī says Islam. Bursawī says God's oneness; al-Ālūsī says the call to God and His oneness, or through the messengers, whose divine commission they now acknowledge. Al-Biqāʿī glosses ijābah as committing to the caller's request. Al-Bayḍāwī says the promise is a response to the imperative, paralleling 63:10. Al-Samīn parses nujib as the imperative's response. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 14:44/q13 What do they promise by following 'the messengers,' and why the plural?  [وَنَتَّبِعِ ٱلرُّسُلَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the speakers represent many nations and their messengers or Muhammad's immediate opponents facing worldly punishment.
- 14:44/q13/p1 They promise obedience to God's messengers and their teachings to make up former neglect. — Muqātil specifically names Muhammad; al-Ṭabarī mentions obedience; al-Qurṭubī states the promise. Bursawī adds following their teachings. Al-Biqāʿī notes eagerness but says repentance is too late; al-Ālūsī says the clauses reinforce each other. [6 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 14:44/q13/p2 The plural reflects a shared summons or each nation's messenger. — Al-Ālūsī says all messengers share a call to monotheism and rejecting them is one act, or each community's wrongdoers promise their own messenger. Ibn ʿĀshūr says in an afterlife scene they are all bearers of God's call. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says each messenger's judgment against rejecters follows the same principle (10:47). Al-Burhān says the article in 'the messengers' carries the possessive sense 'Your messengers.' [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BURHAN]
- 14:44/q13/p3 At the onset of worldly punishment the plural can stand figuratively for Muhammad alone. — Ibn ʿĀshūr proposes this to fit the immediate worldly scene. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 14:44/q14 Who speaks the reproach after their plea?  [أَوَلَمْ تَكُونُوٓا۟ أَقْسَمْتُم · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the implied speech is an external divine or angelic answer or attributed to the wrongdoers themselves.
- 14:44/q14/p1 An omitted 'it will be said to them' introduces God or the angels as the speakers. — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah infers the omitted words from context; Abū Ḥayyān identifies God or angels and rejects making the wrongdoers speak the rebuke. Al-Samīn prefers the Sheikh's external speaker. Al-Ālūsī coordinates the implied speech with 'they say'; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the reply is supplied after their plea. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also describes an implied speaker. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)+]
- 14:44/q14/p2 The rebuke can be construed as words the wrongdoers themselves utter. — Abū Ḥayyān records this construction only to reject it in favor of God or angels as speaker. [0 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 14:44/q15 What does the interrogative rebuke accomplish?  [أَوَلَمْ تَكُونُوٓا۟ أَقْسَمْتُم · rhetoric]
- 14:44/q15/p1 It recalls their worldly oath and refuses their request for more time. — Al-Ṭabarī places the reproach among Quraysh in Hell; al-Qurṭubī says the oath was made in life. Ibn Kathīr says they now taste the result. Al-Biqāʿī calls their earlier posture ignorance, folly, arrogance or insolence. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the question rejects the plea without direct yes or no; an opening conjunction hints at omitted 'No!' Kuranyolu says the reprieve will not be granted. Asad says God answers with their denial of resurrection and retribution. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES]
- 14:44/q15/p2 It exposes the impossibility of repairing neglected duties after truth is unveiled. — Al-Māwardī says the afterlife accepts no repentance or fresh obligation. Al-Qushayrī says earlier failures cannot be repaired or excused once the criminal is exposed and judged. Study Quran invokes 6:28: if returned, they would repeat forbidden acts and their promise is false. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; STUDYQURAN]

## 14:44/q16 Was the earlier oath spoken, implied by conduct, or shared across the group?  [أَقْسَمْتُم مِّن قَبْلُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether 'swore' describes an actual utterance, a categorical claim, or conduct that implied the same claim; and how collective speech is attributed.
- 14:44/q16/p1 It was an arrogant spoken claim or forceful assertion made in worldly life. — Al-Zamakhsharī gives the spoken boast as one reading, due to arrogance and ignorance; al-Bayḍāwī similarly says it may arise from arrogance or delusion. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī explains that oath invokes something honored for maximal emphasis and may figuratively denote any categorical assertion. Al-Qurṭubī says these oaths were in worldly life. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 14:44/q16/p2 Their durable buildings and distant hopes implied the claim even without a spoken oath. — Al-Zamakhsharī, as also quoted by Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn, associates construction and long hopes with assurance of permanence. Al-Ālūsī says ambitions and conduct reveal an expectation of no transfer and long delay. Ibn ʿĀshūr says some or most may have sworn while others implicitly held the same belief. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 14:44/q16/p3 The oath may be attributed to all generations, or to a majority whose belief the rest shared. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says later polytheists inherited the teaching from predecessors; alternately only most or some actually swore it. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes their confidence that defenses would preserve a dominant community. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 14:44/q17 What did 'you have no departure' deny?  [مَا لَكُم مِّن زَوَالٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether zawāl means death, movement to resurrection, escape from punishment, change of faith or residence, or loss of worldly power.
- 14:44/q17/p1 They denied leaving this world or the grave for resurrection, reckoning and the Hereafter. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, Mujāhid and many others relate it to the oath in 16:38 that God would not raise the dead. Al-Rāzī rejects mere ordinary changes of age, wealth or death: the denial is transfer to recompense. Ibn ʿAṭiyyah says it is departure from earth after death; Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies leaving graves for reckoning and says the complement is implicit. Asad notes the literal image of no going down or removal. Al-Suddī glosses resurrection after death. Kuranyolu adds denial of punishment. Nuances between world, earth, graves and recompense are stated by these sources. [36 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج,مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(many commentators)]
- 14:44/q17/p2 They claimed they would never die or cease to live in this world. — Mujāhid reports Quraysh's oath that they would not die or pass away; al-Ṭabarī preserves another report from him. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Bayḍāwī allow this permanence reading. Abū Ḥayyān objects that wrongdoers admitted death and explicitly rejects the reading he attributes to Mujāhid. Bursawī recalls an arrogant oath never to leave worldly life. Al-Ālūsī allows denial of departure from worldly enjoyment. [7 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 14:44/q17/p3 They thought they could not escape or leave punishment. — Al-Ḥasan gives this interpretation, and al-Qurṭubī reports it as a second reading. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 14:44/q17/p4 They asserted they would not leave disbelief for faith. — Al-Biqāʿī includes this among three senses of resisting a changed status quo. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 14:44/q17/p5 They asserted they would not leave their homes or settled lands. — Al-Biqāʿī includes remaining at home. In the related account Kināna tells Kaʿb that his people will remain in their inherited gardens and vineyards, backed by enduring ancestors, armor and swords, and will make opponents yield. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNHISHAM(كنانة بن عبد ياليل بن عمرو بن عمير)]
- 14:44/q17/p6 They would not leave their present condition for the state they now request. — Ibn ʿAbbās gives this gloss, transmitted by al-Durr. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 14:44/q17/p7 Their power and defenses made their community permanently dominant. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the implied speaker exposes their earlier confidence and asks why they now beg for time. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 14:44/q18 How does 'mā lakum min zawāl' relate grammatically to the oath?  [مَا لَكُم مِّن زَوَالٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause supplies the oath's answer or is a separate divine response to the plea.
- 14:44/q18/p1 It is the answer or content of their oath, recast in second person for direct rebuke. — Al-Bayḍāwī calls it reported speech matching the grammatical form of 'you swore.' Al-Nasafī, al-Samīn and Ibn ʿĀshūr say a literal oath would use 'we,' while the rebuke keeps 'you.' Al-Ālūsī adds that min strengthens negation. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the second clause explains the oath without a separate implicit quotation. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 14:44/q18/p2 It is God's answer to their current plea; the oath's answer is omitted. — Al-Ālūsī reports this as a less apparent construction: 'what means have you to depart this state?' He favors the direct oath-answer reading. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 14:44/q19 What report places this plea within the requests of Hell's inhabitants?  [رَبَّنَآ أَخِّرْنَآ · reports]
- 14:44/q19/p1 The people of Hell ask to obey if delayed, and are rebuked by their oath and predecessors' dwellings. — Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb al-Quraẓī reports the scene and God's answer extending into 14:45–46. Al-Durr says the account reached him; Abū Ḥayyān places the address while they are in Hell. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(محمد بن كعب); DURR-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي)]
- 14:44/q19/p2 This is one of five pleas; four receive answers and the fifth ends in silence. — Al-Qurṭubī and al-Ālūsī cite al-Bayhaqī's transmission from Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb. The related pleas and replies cite 40:11–12, 32:12–14, this verse, 35:37, and, in al-Ālūsī's account, 23:106–108; afterward they gasp or groan without hope. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي); ALUSI-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي)]

## 14:44/q20 What effect can verbal repetition and similar endings have?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: How strongly sound similarity sets up an expectation of repetition, and whether the repeated forms serve a useful meaning.
- 14:44/q20/p1 Useful meaning makes wordplay effective; mere strained repetition does not. — Al-Jurjānī criticizes Abū al-Fatḥ al-Bustī's strained examples and praises repetitions that serve an apt thought. [1 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 14:44/q20/p2 Repeated endings create expectation, then surprise when the word changes. — Al-Jurjānī says the delayed realization brings a sense of gain; where words differ from their beginnings, as in al-Buḥturī's example, the effect remains but is weaker. [2 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
