# 100:8: 16 questions

## 100:8/q01 To whom does the pronoun in wa-innahu refer?  [وَإِنَّهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun can shift subjects across verses 6–8; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ objects to breaking their unity.
- 100:8/q01/p1 The human being of the preceding verses. — Māwardī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya identify the human; Qurṭubī says there is no disagreement. Biqāʿī relates the pronoun to the human's self-witness and deserved loss of blessings. [8 notes: MAWARDI; BAGHAWI; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; BIQAI; BINTSHATI-]

## 100:8/q02 What does al-khayr mean here?  [ٱلْخَيْرِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether khayr is confined to wealth, extends to worldly advantage or chosen desire, or retains its broader moral sense; context and other Qurʾanic usages are cited.
- 100:8/q02/p1 Wealth or money. — Reported from Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Qatāda, ʿIkrima, and Ibn Zayd; ʿIkrima or Qatāda generalize this sense to every Qurʾanic khayr. Many cite 2:180, Rāzī also 70:21. Qurṭubī adduces ʿAdī's poetry. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ accepts wealth in this context because an ungrateful witness would love money rather than virtue, while qualifying broad lexical claims. Kur'an Yolu specifies abundant wealth; al-Ālūsī reports that limitation for 2:180. Ibn al-Jawzī lists worldly wealth as a fifteenth sense. - 100:8/q02/p2 Worldly life and its desired advantages, including wealth, health, status, power, or pleasures. — Ibn Zayd is reported as taking khayr as worldly life. Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds health and favor with rulers because disbelievers and the ignorant recognize only worldly goods; Study Quran points to 3:14. Quṭb traces worldly wealth, power, and pleasure to self-love without faith. Ṭabarī's Ibn Zayd still cites the bequest verse for wealth. [11 notes: TAB(ابن زيد); MAWARDI(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); QUTB-ZILAL; BURSEVI; CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN]
- 100:8/q02/p3 Whatever the person chooses for himself. — Māwardī offers chosen preference as a third possibility, so the person strongly loves his own selection. [2 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL]
- 100:8/q02/p4 Good deeds or benefits from God, toward which the person remains constricted. — The Kashshāf's further reading makes good deeds the object; al-Ālūsī says these come from God and describes an unresponsive person. Elmalılı prefers the reading of aversion to good works that cost money or bodily effort. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ELMALILI(صاحب الكشاف)+]
- 100:8/q02/p5 Good in a broad sense, which people naturally love but mistake worldly ornaments for. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this wider sense and connects the mistake with forgetting gratitude. Rāghib, as reported by Kur'an Yolu, includes reason, justice, virtue, and benefit. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ surveys about 125 comparative uses of better/best, contrasts with evil in 17:11, 10:11, 21:35 and 70:21, and with harm in 7:188, 6:17 and 10:107; she also notes horses in 38:32 and wealth in 23:55–56. [7 notes: BINTSHATI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râgıb el-İsfahânî); TABATABAI+]
- 100:8/q02/p6 Worldly loves falsely called good by their adherents; true good is freedom from creation and obedience to God. — Tustarī contrasts the conventional label for love of self, the world, and desire with neediness toward God and fulfilling His command. [2 notes: TUSTARI]

## 100:8/q03 Why is wealth called good?  [ٱلْخَيْرِ · rhetoric]
- 100:8/q03/p1 It follows people's customary valuation of wealth, even when the wealth is illicit or harmful in the final outcome. — Ibn Zayd compares 2:180 and people's naming of battle or its wounds as evil despite 3:174. Wāḥidī says the label reflects ordinary usage rather than ultimate outcome. Rāzī cites 70:21 too. Bursawī and al-Ālūsī stress that illicit or afterlife-harmful wealth can still be called good; Elmalılı says the false belief that wealth is wholly good is condemned. Biqāʿī points to its immediate visible benefit, which distracts from unseen service of God. [17 notes: TAB(ابن زيد); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد,جميع); TABRISI(ابن زيد); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(ابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 100:8/q04 How does li-ḥubb al-khayr relate grammatically to shadīd?  [لِحُبِّ ٱلْخَيْرِ لَشَدِيدٌ · grammar]
turns on: Whether lām is causal, marks a domain of strength, or participates in an omitted and reordered 'love'; opponents of inversion say the surface construction is sound.
- 100:8/q04/p1 The lām is causal: because of love of wealth, the person is miserly or severe. — Al-Khaṭṭābī, Ibn al-Anbārī, al-Samīn, al-Suyūṭī, Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr give the sense 'because of'; Ibn al-Anbārī says this plain wording makes inversion unnecessary. Burhān glosses min ajl. Abū Ḥayyān explicitly rejects an ellipsis on this analysis. Al-Samīn says there is no need to make the lām mean ʿalā. [26 notes: FARRA(الكلبي); MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB(بعض البصريين); TAB-FULL(بعض البصريين); KHATTABI; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري,المفسرون); IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN; BURHAN; BIQAI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 100:8/q04/p2 The lām marks the domain or capacity of strength: he is strong in loving wealth. — Al-Samīn says shadīd li-shayʾ can mean capable of it; Abū Ḥayyān says a strong person can manage and master an affair. Al-Ālūsī prefers the sense 'in' for the lām with strong love; Quṭrub compares 'much striking for Zayd.' Ibn al-Āshūr's Biqāʿī notes that shadīd cannot simply govern the following love phrase across the lām and requires an understood complement. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(قطرب); RAZI(قطرب); RAZI-FULL(قطرب); BIQAI; ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); ALUSI+]
- 100:8/q04/p3 The order is understood as 'strong in love of wealth,' with a repeated 'love' omitted after shadīd. — Al-Farrāʾ and Kufan grammarians say the first ḥubb supplies the omitted second occurrence; this also preserves verse endings. They compare 14:18, where 'wind' is understood after a 'stormy day'; al-Ālūsī's fuller citation gives 41:16. Ibn Qutayba and Burhān report inversion. Al-Khaṭṭābī records the objection that ordinary speakers say they are intense in love of someone or something. Ibn Qutayba himself objects to imposing inversion without grounds, noting poetic reversals may arise from error, rhyme, meter, or necessity; Ibn al-Anbārī objects that the surface wording already works. [23 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(آخر); TAB(بعض نحويي الكوفة); TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي الكوفة); NAHHAS(الفراء); KHATTABI; THALABI(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة,الفراء); RAZI(الفراء); QURTUBI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(الفراء); BURHAN(بعضهم); ALUSI(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); FARRA+; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL-; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري)-]
- 100:8/q04/p4 An analysis shifts an adjective connected to a raised verbal noun and fronts its predicate complement. — Al-Ālūsī reports this proposal but objects that the predicate complement cannot precede it. [0 notes: ALUSI-]
- 100:8/q04/p5 Some take the lām to mean ʿalā. — Al-Samīn reports this but sees no need for it. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 100:8/q05 What does shadīd say about the person's love and conduct?  [لَشَدِيدٌ · meaning]

- 100:8/q05/p1 He is miserly, tightly withholding wealth because he loves it. — Arabic calls a miser shadīd, mutashaddid, or shadīda; Ṭarafa's line depicts death selecting a grasping miser's choice wealth. Thaʿlabī and Qurṭubī also cite fāḥish/fāḥisha for a miser in 2:268. Al-Ḥasan specifies withholding God's due. Al-Ālūsī gives passive 'held back from generosity' and active 'tying the purse shut.' Ibn ʿĀshūr adds Abū Dhuʾayb's elegy (with jawl meaning earth), 59:9 on protection from stinginess, and charity withheld despite ostentatious spending; Ibn Kathīr accepts this with the strong-love reading. ## 100:8/q06 What do the word's root and Qurʾanic usage add to shadīd here?  [لَشَدِيدٌ · meaning]
- 100:8/q06/p1 Its basic force is tight binding and strength; frequent Qurʾanic use in threats adds menace to the rebuke of stinginess. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ traces shadd to a knot or binding, citing 47:4, 10:88, 76:28, 20:31, 28:35, and 38:20; maturity formulas appear in 6:152, 17:34, 12:22, 28:14, 40:67, 46:15, 18:82, and 22:5. She counts roughly forty shadīd uses for punishment, divine might, seizure, or afterlife retribution; exceptions involve iron (57:25), Lot's hoped-for support (11:80), Solomon's threat (27:21), and fighting forces (17:5, 27:33, 48:16, 72:8). Ashadd denotes severity or force, and only 2:165 uses it for love. Against the prior self-witness, the threatening association intensifies the indictment. [6 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 100:8/q07 Why place li-ḥubb al-khayr before la-shadīd?  [لِحُبِّ ٱلْخَيْرِ لَشَدِيدٌ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the fronting belongs to a plain causal phrase or to a reordered phrase with a repeated word omitted; both explanations mention the verse ending.
- 100:8/q07/p1 Fronting highlights the surprising motive, love of wealth, and preserves the verse's cadence. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the causal phrase is fronted for emphasis and rhyme; a governed genitive phrase can precede a predicate with lām, as in verse 6, despite lām al-ibtidāʾ's usual precedence. Biqāʿī says shadīd cannot govern the following phrase across the lām and points to an omitted governed element. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI]
- 100:8/q07/p2 The rearrangement and omission of repeated ḥubb keep the verse endings aligned. — Al-Farrāʾ and Kufan grammarians compare the understood 'wind' of a stormy day in 14:18; the first ḥubb licenses the omitted repeat. Ibn Qutayba warns against assuming inversion without sound grounds, since poets alter order for meter, rhyme, mistake, or necessity. [14 notes: FARRA; TAB(بعض نحويي الكوفة); TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي الكوفة); THALABI(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); QURTUBI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(الفراء); ALUSI(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL-]

## 100:8/q08 How does this clause relate to the witness clause before it?  [وَإِنَّهُۥ لِحُبِّ ٱلْخَيْرِ لَشَدِيدٌ · links]
turns on: The referent of the witness in verse 7 and whether that clause interrupts the syntax or completes the description of human self-testimony.
- 100:8/q08/p1 The human's ingratitude and love of wealth are stated together, with God's witness to that condition placed between them. — Ṭabarī says verse 7 is semantically delayed or parenthetic between verses 6 and 8; Qatāda says God witnesses specifically the human's intense love of wealth. Qushayrī reports that God may witness his condition; Durّ reports Mujāhid for God as witness. Al-Suyūṭī notes a rare shift from human to Lord and back. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ objects to interpretations that switch the pronoun to the human in the next verse and break the unity of the three verses. [7 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); QUSHAYRI; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ITQAN(ابن أبي الإصبع); BINTSHATI-]
- 100:8/q08/p2 The human himself witnesses his ingratitude or his hoarded possessions, and the new clause exposes its motive. — Qushayrī reports the human's own hoards as witness; Ibn ʿAbbās is reported for the human in verse 7. Biqāʿī finds it astonishing that a person denies the benefactor while testifying against himself; Corpus Coranicum reads public witness to ingratitude and possessiveness. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the clauses reveal variable ingratitude in speech, intention, conduct, or neglect. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 100:8/q09 What conduct or motive does love of wealth expose?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Which practical or spiritual consequence the verse's attachment to wealth is used to explain.
- 100:8/q09/p1 It drives ingratitude, hoarding, withheld charity, and preference for immediate possessions over service of God. — Biqāʿī says the visible present benefit eclipses unseen afterlife benefit. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 59:9 and Sūrat al-Fajr 89:18–20: Arabs condemned stinginess yet neglected the poor and orphans while spending for reputation, drinking, and gambling. Elmalılı urges spending for good works and preparing force for God's cause (8:60); Bursawī's Persian verses urge giving rather than leaving regretted treasure to heirs. Islahi compares the obedient horse returning spoils and accepting feed with man boasting like Qārūn (28:78). Corpus Coranicum links love of possessions with 89:20, 76:8, and 75:20. [12 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 100:8/q09/p2 For hypocrites, greed can explain outward profession of faith to protect property and gain spoils. — Bursawī takes the placement after ingratitude as a hint at this motive. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 100:8/q09/p3 Without faith, excessive self-love fixes ambition on wealth, power, and pleasures; faith makes generosity and higher values possible. — Quṭb says unbelieving ambition is earthbound and limited by lifespan; connection to God and the Hereafter joins earthly life to the eternal Origin and End, replacing ingratitude and greed with gratitude, generosity, and mercy. Islahi says God the benefactor is worthier of love, and believers prefer Him over self-interest, as in 2:165. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says love of afterlife good is praiseworthy and may lead to success; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says mistaking worldly ornaments for good causes forgotten gratitude. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 100:8/q10 What inward reading is drawn from the attachment to good?  [* · inward]
turns on: Which divine gift is mistakenly treated as one's own and hoarded.
- 100:8/q10/p1 The self is veiled by divine favors, stopping at gifts instead of using them to reach God. — Bursawī reports al-Qāshānī on ingratitude; Qāshānī also sees love of wealth as hoarding and veiling or constriction toward good leading to truth. Tustarī says worldly loves are conventionally called good while genuine good is independence from creatures, need for God, and obedience. [4 notes: TUSTARI; QASHANI; BURSEVI(القاشاني)]
- 100:8/q10/p2 Ingratitude means claiming independent possession of existence and divine attributes and hoarding guidance. — Bursawī reports al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya: one misuses these gifts or refuses to share guidance. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 100:8/q11 What rhetorical pattern does this verse make with its neighbors?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Different commentators identify distinct sound and discourse devices rather than dispute a single device.
- 100:8/q11/p1 Balanced verse endings and the near sounds shahīd and shadīd form paronomasia. — Bāqillānī calls the passage tarsīʿ with tajnīs and compares 7:201, 68:3, 52:1–2, and 79:3–4. Burhān defines a close-letter type by one changed letter and cites 75:22, 6:26, 44:29, and 4:83. Itqān calls shahīd/shadīd jinās al-lāḥiq, whose differing letters are non-neighboring, with hamaza/lamaza and other pairs. [3 notes: BAQILLANI; BURHAN; ITQAN]
- 100:8/q11/p2 The forceful rhythm suits the dust, opened graves and hearts, and indictment of ingratitude and greed. — Quṭb describes the sūrah's rough, rumbling, cracking sound and rapid jumps before its final settling, like a runner reaching the finish. [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]
- 100:8/q11/p3 A shift of pronouns moves from the human to his Lord and back to the human. — Itqān transmits Ibn Abī al-Iṣbaʿ's classification as a rare pronoun shift after mentioning two subjects. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن أبي الإصبع)]

## 100:8/q12 What role does the verse play in the sūrah's sequence?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the focus is structural placement, cultural characterization, or the contrast with the opening horses.
- 100:8/q12/p1 Verses 6–8 form the central human indictment, pausing the energetic oath before the final eschatological reckoning. — Corpus Coranicum says these are the sūrah's only nominal sentences, contrasting with surrounding verbal and participial clauses; the ʾinsān saying delays resolution of the enigmatic oath. Neuwirth calls the saying a motif later becoming a topos. Quṭb marks a second scene moving from the charge to ingratitude, denial, selfishness, and miserliness. Corpus Coranicum compares the subsequent accusation-to-doom sequence with Q104 and Old Testament doom prophecy; it sees similar riddling tension in Q90, Q97, Q101 and Q104. [6 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 100:8/q12/p2 The human accusation can be read against pre-Islamic human-centered values. — Corpus Coranicum reports Neuwirth's view, while its author says the text itself does not explicitly attack particular pre-Islamic Arab virtues. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM(Neuwirth); CORPUSCORANICUM-]
- 100:8/q12/p3 The obedient horse's return of spoils exposes the human's refusal to acknowledge his benefactor. — Islahi contrasts the horse accepting its master's feed with man hoarding blessings as self-achieved and boasting like Qārūn in 28:78. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 100:8/q13 What is reported about the sūrah's place of revelation?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The competing reports of Meccan origin and a Medinan raid setting.
- 100:8/q13/p1 It is Meccan. — Jishumī transmits this from al-Ḥasan, ʿIkrima, and Qatāda; Quṭb describes the sūrah as Meccan and counts eleven verses. [2 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن وعكرمة وقتادة); QUTB-ZILAL]
- 100:8/q13/p2 It is Medinan and connected with a raid. — Jishumī reports this alternative without further details. [1 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن وعكرمة وقتادة)]

## 100:8/q14 What reward is reported for reciting this sūrah?  [* · reports]
- 100:8/q14/p1 Ten good deeds per person who spent the night at Muzdalifah and witnessed Jamʿ. — Jishumī and the Kashshāf transmit the prophetic report; Rāzī's fuller text attributes it to Ubayy and raises it to the Prophet, describing the reward by that number. [3 notes: JISHUMI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); RAZI-FULL(أبي عن النبي)]

## 100:8/q15 What pilgrimage interpretation is reported for the opening scene?  [* · reports]
- 100:8/q15/p1 It depicts departure from ʿArafa, fires, dawn departure from Muzdalifah, a valley floor, and arrival at Mina's Jamʿ. — Al-Durr reports this sequence from Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb al-Quraẓī. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي)]

## 100:8/q16 What is said about the following sūrah's revelation?  [* · sciences]
- 100:8/q16/p1 Sūrat al-Qāriʿah is Meccan according to all authorities cited. — Māwardī's full text reports this for the next sūrah. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
