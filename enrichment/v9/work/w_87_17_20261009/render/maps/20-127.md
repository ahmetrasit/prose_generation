# 20:127: 15 questions

## 20:127/q01 What earlier punishment does ‘and thus’ point back to?  [وَكَذَٰلِكَ · links]
turns on: The antecedent of ‘thus’ and the point at which the narrative turns to its audience.
- 20:127/q01/p1 It applies the preceding punishment of those who turned from God's signs to others who exceed the bounds and disbelieve. — Jishumi calls this an analogy to the warning about turning from the Qur'an. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the narrow life of 20:124 as the comparison; Ṭabāṭabāʾī points to those who forgot the signs after warning in 20:124 and 20:126. Al-Ālūsī says a general class of punishment is compared with the particular punishment just described. Al-Samīn says ‘thus’ means in the manner of that punishment. Al-Qurṭubī includes turning from reflection on created things. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes that the preceding dialogue leaves implicit the person's being forgotten and earlier blindness to the signs as well as present blindness. [10 notes: JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 20:127/q01/p2 It carries the lesson of the Adam and Satan account into the address to the Prophet and his community. — Jishumi reports Abū Muslim: the account of Satan ends here and its lesson is applied to the Prophet's people. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم)]

## 20:127/q02 Is this clause continued divine rebuke or a concluding statement to listeners?  [وَكَذَٰلِكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the conjunction carries on the divine address or introduces a parenthetical conclusion.
- 20:127/q02/p1 It is a concluding lesson addressed to listeners, introduced parenthetically by the conjunction. — Ibn ʿĀshūr presents this as an alternative to continuing God's address to the resurrected man; on this reading the afterlife comparison concludes the story by referring back to the worldly hardship of 20:124. Ṭabāṭabāʾī also takes the wāw as opening a new statement, without specifying its addressee. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:127/q02/p2 It continues God's rebuke addressed to the person resurrected blind. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the conjunction then links this clause to the previous sentence; the man's afterlife punishment is worse and longer than worldly punishment. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:127/q03 What requital does God promise in ‘We requite’?  [نَجْزِى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the statement is specified by the earlier punishment or described across both lives.
- 20:127/q03/p1 God gives comparable punishment to those who transgress and reject His signs. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī in al-Wajīz, al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Nasafī, al-Samīn, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī apply the preceding warning to this class. Al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī describe the response as corresponding to the offense; al-Biqāʿī includes arrogant defiance by denial or by acting like a denier. Al-Ṭabarī identifies the constricted grave-life as the requital. Al-Wāḥidī reports Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Kalbī, and the exegetes: the one who abandoned the signs is left in the Fire today. [15 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، الكلبي، أهل التفسير); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:127/q03/p2 The deniers are requited in this life and the next. — Ibn Kathīr cites 13:34 for punishment in both lives. Asad describes the eventual recompense as severe, enduring punishment in the life to come. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 20:127/q04 What does ‘exceeded’ mean here?  [أَسْرَفَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether isrāf is specified as shirk and unbelief, taken broadly as crossing a limit in conduct, or characterized by pursuit of desire.
- 20:127/q04/p1 It means associating partners with God, unbelief, or stubborn denial of His signs. — Muqātil, Yaḥyā ibn Sallām, Ibn ʿAbbās as reported by al-Wāḥidī, al-Wāḥidī's al-Wajīz, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and Sufyān as reported by al-Durr specify shirk. Al-Rāzī reports this as one interpretation and takes the following denial of signs as explaining the excess. Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies false belief, unbelief, and stubborn denial; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says rejecting signs transgresses the bounds of servitude. Al-Ṭabrisī joins shirk to disobedience. [13 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); DURR-FULL(سفيان); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:127/q04/p2 It means going beyond limits through disobedience or sin, including misuse of one's capacities. — Al-Rāzī reports disobedience as another interpretation. Al-Ṭabarī says disobeying God; al-Qurṭubī says transgressing through sin. Al-Bursawī defines isrāf as exceeding the limit in any human action, though it is most familiar in spending. Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Iṣlāḥī define it as exceeding a limit; Iṣlāḥī says people waste talents on forbidden things and neglect obligations. Asad refers to his discussion of the cognate musrif in sūra 10 and renders the offenders as wasting themselves; Study Quran refers readers to 10:12. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:127/q04/p3 It means plunging into desires and turning away from the signs or God's commands. — Al-Bayḍāwī speaks of appetites; al-Biqāʿī of following desire and arrogantly rejecting commands; al-Ālūsī of plunging into desires and denying or turning away from signs. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:127/q05 What are ‘the signs of his Lord’?  [بِـَٔايَٰتِ رَبِّهِۦ · referent]
turns on: Whether ‘signs’ is restricted to the Qur'an or includes other divine proofs and communications.
- 20:127/q05/p1 They are the Qur'an. — Muqātil identifies the signs as the Qur'an; al-Qurṭubī includes turning from the Qur'an in the offense. [2 notes: MUQATIL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 20:127/q05/p2 They include God's proofs, scriptures, messengers, and miracles, including the Qur'an. — Al-Ṭabarī names messengers and books; al-Ṭabrisī names proofs, scriptures, and messengers; al-Bursawī names the Qur'an and other miracles. Al-Qurṭubī also includes reflection on created things in what the sinner turns away from. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; BURSEVI; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 20:127/q06 What does failing to believe the signs involve?  [وَلَمْ يُؤْمِنۢ بِـَٔايَٰتِ رَبِّهِۦ · interpretation]
- 20:127/q06/p1 It is refusing to affirm the signs, denying or abandoning them, and potentially acting against them. — Al-Qurṭubī says failing to affirm them as true; al-Bayḍāwī adds denial and acting contrary to them. Al-Bursawī says denial and turning away. Al-Ṭabarī specifies rejection of messengers and books. Al-Wāḥidī reports Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Kalbī, and the exegetes on abandoning the signs and being left in the Fire in return. Al-Biqāʿī includes denial or behavior resembling denial; al-Ālūsī includes disbelief, denial, or turning away. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، الكلبي، أهل التفسير); TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:127/q07 How is unbelief in the signs related to excess?  [أَسْرَفَ وَلَمْ يُؤْمِنۢ بِـَٔايَٰتِ رَبِّهِۦ · interpretation]
- 20:127/q07/p1 The unbelief explains or exemplifies the excess. — Al-Rāzī expressly says the clause about not believing explains transgression. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says rejection of the signs exceeds the bounds of servitude. Al-Thaʿlabī joins shirk and unbelief as the excess punished here. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; THALABI]

## 20:127/q08 What does ‘the punishment of the Hereafter’ refer to?  [وَلَعَذَابُ ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase names the whole next-life punishment, the Fire, or the blindness at resurrection.
- 20:127/q08/p1 The punishment of the next life in general. — Al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī offer this general sense. Al-Biqāʿī says the comparison holds whatever form that punishment takes. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:127/q08/p2 Specifically the Fire that follows resurrection. — Al-Bayḍāwī gives the Fire as one possibility; al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī also report it as a specific sense. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:127/q08/p3 It can mean resurrection while blind. — Al-Bayḍāwī offers this as an alternative to the Fire; his further comparison allows that blindness may end in Hell, when the person sees his place and condition. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 20:127/q09 What earlier suffering is the Hereafter's punishment compared with?  [أَشَدُّ وَأَبْقَىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Where the preceding constricted life and blindness occur, and which of them supplies the comparison.
- 20:127/q09/p1 It surpasses suffering in this world, including the narrow life of 20:124. — Muqātil specifies being killed at Badr and says the contrast warns Meccan unbelievers. Al-Nasafī, al-Bursawī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and others specify constricted life or livelihood; al-Biqāʿī notes that this world is transient and a place of departure. Ibn Kathīr cites the Prophet's statement to spouses involved in mutual imprecation that worldly punishment is easier than the next life's. Al-Bayḍāwī allows narrow worldly life alone as the comparator; al-Ālūsī allows the first punishment alone. Study Quran reports that perpetual afterlife blindness exceeds any worldly constriction. [22 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:127/q09/p2 It surpasses the constricted life or punishment of the grave. — Al-Ṭabarī takes the prior constricted life as grave-life. Al-Wāḥidī says this comparison supports that reading of 20:124; al-Qurṭubī also names grave punishment. Other commentators compare the Hereafter with both worldly and grave punishment. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:127/q09/p3 It surpasses blindness, either blindness at resurrection alongside worldly constriction or earlier blindness from turning from the signs. — Al-Bayḍāwī allows worldly constriction together with blindness as the comparison and says resurrection blindness may end when the person enters Hell and sees his place. Al-Ālūsī includes blind resurrection among the possible comparators. Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī's comparison of permanent blindness with temporary hardship. Study Quran reports one view that the Hereafter's blindness is more intense and lasting than the blindness caused by turning from God's signs. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; STUDYQURAN; NASAFI-FULL]
- 20:127/q09/p4 If the previous constricted life is already in the Hereafter, the verse reiterates its superiority to imaginable worldly pain. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya explicitly makes the comparison depend on whether the narrow life is in this world or the barzakh, or already in the Hereafter; in the first cases it adds a further threat. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 20:127/q10 Why is that punishment more severe and more lasting?  [أَشَدُّ وَأَبْقَىٰٓ · interpretation]
- 20:127/q10/p1 It is greater in intensity and endures without end. — Al-Rāzī names its magnitude and endlessness; Yaḥyā ibn Sallām, al-Ṭabarī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, and al-Bursawī explicitly say it does not cease. Al-Biqāʿī contrasts this with the world's transience. Al-Qāshānī calls it spiritual and permanent; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it encompasses both inner and outer being. Al-Bursawī says separation from God, the Giver, is the severest torment. Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī's contrast of permanent blindness with temporary hardship. [16 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 20:127/q11 What does recompense for excess imply about human responsibility?  [نَجْزِى مَنْ أَسْرَفَ · theology]
- 20:127/q11/p1 Punishment follows a person's own act of excess, so the verse counts against compulsion and against attributing that excess to God's creation. — Jishumi says recompense is for deeds and that the verse attributes extravagance to people themselves; he explicitly argues against the doctrine of compulsion and the claim that God creates their extravagance. [1 notes: JISHUMI+]

## 20:127/q12 What does this reasoning imply about children of polytheists?  [نَجْزِى مَنْ أَسْرَفَ · theology]
- 20:127/q12/p1 They lack an act of excess for which they could be punished. — Jishumi extends the verse's link between punishment and one's act of excess to the children of polytheists. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 20:127/q13 How should a person respond to this warning?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:127/q13/p1 Avoid sin and desire, endure hardship in obedience, and seek to escape punishment and blindness. — Al-Bursawī says a sensible person strives not to be resurrected blind and treats separation from God as the severest torment. He invokes the saying that Paradise is surrounded by hardships and Hell by desires, and reports Gabriel's inspection of both: once surrounded respectively by hardships and desires, Gabriel fears few will enter Paradise and few will avoid Hell. [3 notes: BURSEVI]

## 20:127/q14 What reports illustrate the severity or causes of the punishment?  [وَلَعَذَابُ ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ أَشَدُّ · reports]
turns on: These are distinct narrated illustrations rather than competing accounts of one event.
- 20:127/q14/p1 The Prophet told spouses involved in mutual imprecation that worldly punishment is easier than the next life's. — Ibn Kathīr relates the Prophet's warning in that setting. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 20:127/q14/p2 Even the least punished person in Hell wears sandals that make his brain boil. — Al-Bursawī cites this hadith as an illustration of Hell's severity. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 20:127/q14/p3 The people of Hell are shackled, dragged face-down, struck by angels, and returned whenever they try to escape. — Al-Bursawī reports this narration describing the punishment. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 20:127/q14/p4 Gabriel feared few would enter Paradise or avoid Hell after seeing their surrounding hardships and desires. — Al-Bursawī reports that God sent Gabriel to inspect Paradise and Hell, then each after it was surrounded by hardships or desires respectively. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 20:127/q15 How does this verse fit the passage that follows?  [* · links]
- 20:127/q15/p1 It begins a sequence of threats, promises, proofs, rulings, and consolation for the Prophet, all arising from the preceding discussion. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes the ensuing passage in these terms. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
