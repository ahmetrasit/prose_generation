# 87:17/q12 How does this verse work in the sūra's argument? [* · links]
turns on: These notes trace different connections: immediate rebuke, purification and prophetic teaching, or the sūra's broader movement.
- 87:17/q12/p1 It reinforces the rebuke of worldly preference by stating the better, lasting alternative.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says it exposes what the audience ignored; al-Ālūsī describes its circumstantial force. Corpus Coranicum sees a shift from address to the messenger into direct rebuke of hearers and a choice with eschatological consequences, mentioning 102:1. Kur'an Yolu connects 7:169, 12:109 and 93:4; Corpus Coranicum points to 93:4 for this vocabulary.
  holders (8 notes): ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR
  notes: ALUSI:87:17/r1 ALUSI-FULL:v30p111/r1 IBNASHUR:87:17/r1 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p290/r1 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:16-17:anmerkung/r1 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:16-19:kommentar/r1 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:16-19:kommentar/r2 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:87:16/r1
- 87:17/q12/p2 It connects the warning against worldly preference with purification and the prophetic path.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says the sūra proceeds to warn against turning from purification through routes other than the prophets' method; Qutb says the sūra grounds afterlife recompense in earlier scripture, beside God's oneness and revelation, and portrays an easy message, messenger and community.
  holders (2 notes): BIQAI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL
  notes: BIQAI-FULL:v8p404#2/r1 QUTB-ZILAL:87:1/r4
- 87:17/q12/p3 The sūra's wider movement contrasts good and bad and brings hearers to a consequential choice.
  reasons: Corpus Coranicum says the contrast turns from an earlier typology to the listeners and argues that second-person rebukes and the parallel growth of text and community do not presuppose a formed community or only follower meetings. Qutb describes the cosmos as a sanctuary of praise and divine support for the Prophet; Iṣlāḥī says worldly attachment keeps the message from taking root.
  holders (4 notes): CORPUSCORANICUM; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR
  notes: CORPUSCORANICUM:87:16-19:kommentar/r2 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:9-15:kommentar/r3 QUTB-ZILAL:87:1/r3 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p271/r3

# 87:17/q14 How do inward readings describe the lasting life? [* · inward]
turns on: The inward notes emphasize true spiritual life, the inner kernel, or devotion to the Lord beyond both worlds.
- 87:17/q14/p1 True spiritual life, gained through purification, remembrance and prayer, outlasts sensory life.
  reasons: Al-Qāshānī connects the sensory world's ornaments with neglect of remembrance and prayer.
  holders (1 notes): QASHANI
  notes: QASHANI:87:16-18/r1
- 87:17/q14/p2 Inner reality is the lasting kernel; outward appearance is a quickly discarded shell.
  reasons: Al-Bursawī uses the kernel and shell analogy and says people of inner truth choose the lasting life with God while outward-looking people are veiled.
  holders (2 notes): BURSEVI
  notes: BURSEVI:v10p318/r2 BURSEVI:v10p318/r3
- 87:17/q14/p3 The highest aspiration prefers the Lord over both worlds.
  reasons: Abū al-ʿAbbās al-Dīnawarī ranks low worldly ambition, elevated afterlife ambition, and noble states or sound realities that seek God.
  holders (1 notes): SULAMI(أبو العباس الدينوري)
  notes: SULAMI:v2p390#4/r1
