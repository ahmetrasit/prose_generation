# 87:17/q01 What does al-ākhirah designate here? [ٱلْءَاخِرَةُ · referent]
turns on: Whether al-ākhirah is taken broadly as the next abode, specifically as Paradise, or inwardly as spiritual life.
- 87:17/q01/p1 The life or abode after this world.
  reasons: Muqātil, Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Nasafī and others contrast it with worldly life; al-Biqāʿī calls it the exalted abode and goal of creation and command.
  holders (6 notes): MUQATIL; TAB; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; NASAFI; BIQAI
  notes: MUQATIL:87:14-19/r3 TAB:87:17/r1 WAHIDI-QT:87:17/r1 WAHIDI-WAJIZ:87:17-19/r1 NASAFI:87:17/r1 BIQAI:87:17/r1
- 87:17/q01/p2 Paradise or the Garden.
  reasons: Al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī explicitly gloss the afterlife as Paradise; Ibn al-Jawzī specifies the Garden for the Prophet.
  holders (5 notes): WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL
  notes: WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p448/r8 TABRISI:87:10-17/r20 IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p92#2/r1 QURTUBI:87:17/r1 QURTUBI-FULL:87:17/r1
- 87:17/q01/p3 The true spiritual life with God.
  reasons: Al-Qāshānī contrasts it with sensory life and its ornaments, linking worldly preference to neglected remembrance and prayer and lack of purification.
  holders (1 notes): QASHANI

# 87:17/q02 In what sense is the Hereafter better than worldly life? [ٱلْءَاخِرَةُ خَيْرٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes variously state a general comparison or specify its beneficiary as believers, the obedient and Paradise's people, or the Prophet.
- 87:17/q02/p1 It surpasses worldly life in goodness, pleasure, and reward.
  reasons: Ṭabarī and Qatāda say better in goodness; al-Kashshāf, al-Nasafī and Qutb stress intrinsic or qualitative superiority. Al-Jishumī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī say its pleasure is pure or unspoiled whereas worldly pleasure is slight, interrupted or mixed with pain; al-Rāzī adds bodily and spiritual happiness; Ibn Kathīr calls the world low and the next abode noble. Al-Biqāʿī calls its bliss the goal of creation and command. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says relative superiority suffices for the comparison.

# 87:17/q03 What does abqā say about duration? [أَبْقَىٰٓ · meaning]
- 87:17/q03/p1 The afterlife lasts longer than the passing world, ultimately without end.
  reasons: Ṭabarī and Qatāda contrast this life's ending with the next life's permanence. Al-Rāzī and al-Biqāʿī argue that permanence outranks transience; al-Biqāʿī says even a prolonged world is only figuratively lasting. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies abqā as a comparative of longer duration and cites the Prophet's garment saying as parallel usage. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes the comparative claim from the afterlife's intrinsic eternity; al-Samīn identifies worldly life as the omitted thing surpassed. Al-Itqān compares another omitted comparative, 'what is more hidden.'

# 87:17/q04 How is this clause grammatically construed? [* · grammar]
turns on: These are compatible analyses of sentence form, its relation to 87:16, and comparative ellipsis.
- 87:17/q04/p1 It is a nominal sentence with a subject and predicate.
  reasons: Al-Naḥḥās analyzes al-ākhiratu khayrun wa-abqā this way.
  holders (1 notes): NAHHAS
  notes: NAHHAS:v5p207/r13
- 87:17/q04/p2 It functions as a circumstantial clause attached to the subject of 'you prefer.'
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī says this construction reinforces the rebuke of 87:16.
  holders (2 notes): ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL
  notes: ALUSI:87:17/r1 ALUSI-FULL:v30p111/r1
- 87:17/q04/p3 The comparatives leave worldly life unstated as what is surpassed.
  reasons: Al-Samīn supplies worldly life for abqā; al-Itqān gives this verse and 'knows the secret and what is more hidden' as examples of an omitted comparative term.
  holders (2 notes): SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN
  notes: SAMIN-DURR:87:17/r1 ITQAN:v3p268/r2

# 87:17/q05 Why does this wording leave parts of the contrast implicit? [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The authors explain the implied worldly contrast through rhetorical pairing or through the obviousness of worldly defects.
- 87:17/q05/p1 The paired clauses imply rejection and elevation after worldly preference, and the world's defects after the Hereafter's goodness; the order first deters, then attracts.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī explains both sides of the ellipsis and the sequence of deterrence followed by attraction.
  holders (2 notes): BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL
  notes: BIQAI:87:17/r4 BIQAI-FULL:v8p401/r4
- 87:17/q05/p2 The world's troubled pleasure and short span are omitted because they are obvious.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī says the listeners already know these defects.
  holders (2 notes): ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL
  notes: ALUSI:87:17/r3 ALUSI-FULL:v30p111/r2
