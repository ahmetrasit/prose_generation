re: the immediate two-group contrast and the larger turn to public proclamation.
- 87:11/q08/p1 It contrasts fearful hearers with avoiders and pairs each response with its outcome.
  reasons: Qutb says the Prophet delivers reminder and God deals with hearers according to response. Corpus Coranicum says verses 10–11 contrast good and bad responses, with Hell in verses 12–13 balanced by promise in 14–15; its fuller structural account calls verses 10–11 and 12–15 a chiasm, comparing Q 92:5–11 and 14–21. Biqāʿī describes reciprocal ellipsis: fear and happiness imply the second group's hard-heartedness and misery, while their cause and outcome imply the first group's opposites. Al-Mulāwī illustrates such cause-outcome correspondence with planning and operating a waterwheel. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the comparison implies the avoider does not fear.

  reasons: Corpus Coranicum says the second stanza again addresses the messenger: after praising God, perhaps with close followers, he turns to a wider audience, making the revelation of verse 6 public in purpose.

# 87:11/q09 What does the verse imply for the Prophet's practice of reminding? [* · interpretation]
- 87:11/q09/p1 He should give the reminder without pursuing or forcing those who persistently evade it.
  reasons: Islāḥī says the fearful will heed it, while the obdurate face their own fate; their rejection does not show a defect in the message or preacher. Ṭabāṭabāʾ says one reminder establishes proof for the wretched, further reminders do not help, and cites Q 53:29 for turning away. Qutb says the Prophet fulfills his duty by reminding, after which people diverge.

# 87:11/q10 What other Qur'anic usage illuminates ٱلْأَشْقَى? [ٱلْأَشْقَى · links]
- 87:11/q10/p1 Early parallels use the elative for the damned or a threatened sinner; later passages use the positive shaqiyy.
  reasons: Corpus Coranicum points to the damned in Q 92:15 and the Thamūd compatriot in Q 91:12, perhaps with an eschatological threat; it notes that the elative disappears from later texts, with the positive in Q 19:4, 19:32, 19:48 and 11:105. Rāzī compares Q 25:24 to show the superlative need not entail a separate comparison class, and Q 30:27 plus poetry for an elative used positively.

# 87:11/q11 What wider sūrah setting frames this warning? [* · sciences]
- 87:11/q11/p1 Al-Aʿlā is a Meccan sūrah of nineteen verses, moving from cosmic praise and assurance of the Prophet to warning and recompense.
  reasons: Qutb says the Prophet loved the sūrah and recited it with al-Ghāshiya at Eid and Friday prayers, sometimes on the same day. He describes the cosmos as a sanctuary of praise, the Prophet's promised preservation and ease, and the sūrah's foundations of divine oneness, revelation, afterlife recompense, and roots in earlier scriptures (87:18–19).
