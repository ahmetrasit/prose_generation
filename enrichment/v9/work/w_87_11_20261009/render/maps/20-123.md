# 20:123: 20 questions

## 20:123/q01 How does the opening divine speech follow the preceding account?  [قَالَ · links]
- 20:123/q01/p1 It answers the implied question about the consequence of Adam's lapse and what God then commanded. — Al-Ālūsī says the preceding account raises what God commanded; Ibn ʿĀshūr says Adam's disobedience raises what consequence followed; al-Biqāʿī says the mention of Eve and Iblīs arouses curiosity about the rest of their story, and the holy royal abode cannot contain the disobedience, although Adam had been prepared through election. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:123/q01/p2 The shift from first-person plural to third-person singular marks God's sole prerogative to decree and judge. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 40:20 and 12:40 for judgment belonging to God alone. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:123/q02 What does the command to descend convey?  [ٱهْبِطَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether hubūṭ is treated as literal descent, its disparaging usage, or an inward account of Adam's elected earthly test.
- 20:123/q02/p1 It is descent from the garden to earth. — Bursevī glosses hubūṭ as descending after the lapse. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this a creative command: leaving the garden for earth required God's act of creation. [2 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:123/q02/p2 Hubūṭ suggests compelled downward movement and humiliation. — Al-Rāghib, quoted by Bursevī, compares it to a falling stone and contrasts the disparaging hubūṭ of persons with the honorific inzāl of the Qurʾān, angels, and rain; he cites 2:74, 2:36, and 7:13. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الراغب)]
- 20:123/q02/p3 Outward reproach in the descent inwardly completes Adam's honor. — Bursevī reads the command's outward blame and inward completion together; the Najmī interpretations say Adam's election made his earthly descent a test accompanied by guidance for his descendants. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 20:123/q03 Who are the two commanded to descend?  [ٱهْبِطَا · referent]
turns on: The dual's referents, the plural wording, and whether mutual enmity can describe Adam and Eve.
- 20:123/q03/p1 Adam and Eve are the dual addressees. — Abū Sulaymān al-Dimashqī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī, and al-Ālūsī give this reading. Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes Iblīs and the serpent in the joint descent. Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Bursevī explain plural language through the couple as humanity's progenitors, Bursevī citing 7:190. Al-Ālūsī relies on this reading. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL-; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 20:123/q03/p2 Adam and Iblīs are the dual addressees, each also representing his descendants. — Muqātil and Ibn ʿAbbās identify the pair; Abū Muslim explains that each is a kind with many descendants, accounting for later plural address. Al-Qurṭubī argues Adam and Eve were not enemies; Eve's descent is included in Adam's. He notes 7:18 could mean Iblīs was first expelled to a heavenly place; al-Ālūsī says Iblīs entered the garden to whisper after expulsion, citing 15:34. Iṣlāḥī cites the plural parallel, jamīʿan, and Adam and Eve's natural love, with 2:36 and 7:24. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Eve followed her husband. [15 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو مسلم الأصبهاني); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; QURTUBI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:123/q03/p3 The descent embraces Adam, Eve, Iblīs, and their descendants. — Ibn Kathīr names Adam, Eve, and Iblīs as descending from Paradise. Study Quran takes the address as Adam, Eve, and future descendants, citing 2:38–39 and 7:24–25; its report from al-Ṭabarī includes Iblīs. The precise set varies across these notes. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]
- 20:123/q03/p4 A further proposal includes Adam, Iblīs, the serpent, and their descendants. — Al-Ālūsī reports this proposal but rejects it in favor of Adam and Eve. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]

## 20:123/q04 Who is included by the plural address after the dual command?  [جَمِيعًۢا بَعْضُكُمْ يَأْتِيَنَّكُم · referent]
turns on: Which original pair the dual denotes and how progenitors can represent later generations.
- 20:123/q04/p1 The founders stand for their descendants, who are included in the plural address. — Al-Kashshāf treats Adam and Eve as humanity's origins and ascribes the effect's act to its cause; al-Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān repeat this. Abū Muslim treats Adam and Iblīs as two kinds, each with many descendants. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the founders of humankind and the satanic kind include their descendants; for the guidance address he singles out Adam as humanity's ancestor. Bursevī cites 7:190 for the couple's offspring. [13 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم,صاحب الكشاف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو مسلم الأصبهاني,الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:123/q04/p2 The plural addresses Adam, Eve, and their future descendants. — Study Quran takes the address to announce their banishment from Paradise, comparing 2:38–39 and 7:24–25. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 20:123/q05 What does 'all together' do in the descent command?  [جَمِيعًۢا · grammar]
turns on: Whether jamīʿan adds Satan and the serpent to the couple's descent or describes the couple together.
- 20:123/q05/p1 It is an accusative circumstantial qualifier expressing the whole group. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says jamīʿan is a singular noun expressing every member of a plural set, invariant in gender and number; he cites 11:55. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:123/q05/p2 It indicates that Iblīs and the serpent descend together with Adam and Eve. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says their hostility continues among descendants to the end of time; Abū Ḥayyān reports his interpretation. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 20:123/q05/p3 It describes Adam and Eve's joint descent only. — Abū Ḥayyān explicitly excludes Satan and the serpent from what jamīʿan implies. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]

## 20:123/q06 Who are the parties in the mutual enmity?  [بَعْضُكُمْ لِبَعْضٍ عَدُوٌّۭ · referent]
turns on: Which parties the plural includes and whether the hostility is across species, within humankind, or both.
- 20:123/q06/p1 Humans and Iblīs with his descendants are enemies to each other. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr, and others name the opposing lineages. Al-Qurṭubī includes Adam's hostility to the serpent; al-Qāḍī says the wording is satisfied by reciprocal human–devil hostility. Iṣlāḥī says regarding Satan as enemy is needed to recover Paradise. [12 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(القاضي); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,قول أول); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:123/q06/p2 Enmity also occurs among Adam's descendants. — Al-Nasafī attributes it to worldly envy and religious disagreement; Abū Ḥayyān reports divergent religions and opinions; Bursevī and al-Bayḍāwī mention struggle over livelihood. Al-Qāḍī allows hostility within each side, and al-Biqāʿī also names internal opposition. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قول ثان); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:123/q06/p3 Adam and the serpent, as well as humans and snakes, are enemies. — Al-Qurṭubī names Adam's mutual hostility with the serpent and Iblīs. Wahb reports the snake carried Iblīs; God cursed it, its legs withdrew into its belly, it ate earth, and people were told to split its head when encountered. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); DURR-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]
- 20:123/q06/p4 Enmity can arise because each kind disrupts the other's condition. — Al-Bayḍāwī gives this beside conflict over livelihood and says what follows favors livelihood rivalry. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 20:123/q07 How does the enmity clause function grammatically?  [بَعْضُكُمْ لِبَعْضٍ عَدُوٌّۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause is a circumstantial qualifier or an independent sentence.
- 20:123/q07/p1 It is a circumstantial qualifier describing the parties as they descend. — Al-Ālūsī calls it a circumstantial clause on either addressee reading; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a second circumstantial qualifier. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:123/q07/p2 It begins a new sentence after jamīʿan. — Al-Jishumī places the sentence break after jamīʿan. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 20:123/q08 What does the word 'enemy' allow grammatically?  [عَدُوٌّۭ · grammar]
- 20:123/q08/p1 The same form may describe one, two, or many. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya states that ʿaduww is invariant across these numbers. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 20:123/q09 What purpose or experience does the enmity express?  [بَعْضُكُمْ لِبَعْضٍ عَدُوٌّۭ · interpretation]
- 20:123/q09/p1 Earthly life after the Fall entails continuing trials and a struggle against hostile forces. — Al-Qushayrī names separation from Paradise, Iblīs and the serpent, worldly trials, and desires. Al-Biqāʿī says enmity renews determination after forgetfulness: jinn mislead people, people guard themselves with charms and incantations, and both camps have internal opponents. Ibn ʿĀshūr describes entry into a mixed world of good and evil, reality and illusion, unlike the pure garden; this belongs to human nature. Asad links lost innocence and mutual hostility to the need for guidance. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:123/q09/p2 God placed enmity among people so that none would be loved except Him. — The Najmī inward interpretation, reported by Bursevī, invokes Abraham's statement that all are enemies except the Lord of the worlds (26:77). [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 20:123/q10 How does the conditional expression introduce guidance?  [فَإِمَّا يَأْتِيَنَّكُم · grammar]
- 20:123/q10/p1 It is an emphatic conditional whose answer is the promise to followers. — Al-Jishumī treats the mīm as connective, equivalent to fa-in, and identifies the emphatic nūn and answer beginning fa-man ittabaʿa. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the answer runs through this and the second category. Bursevī calls the mīm augmentative and says the nūn functions like an oath lām. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says mā and the nūn augment 'if guidance comes' to signal certain occurrence. [4 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 20:123/q11 Does the conditional form imply that God must send revelation?  [فَإِمَّا يَأْتِيَنَّكُم · theology]
- 20:123/q11/p1 No; God may guide or leave people, although the emphatic condition indicates revelation is likely to occur. — Bursevī takes the conditional offer of book and messenger as denying any obligation upon God. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 20:123/q12 What is the guidance that comes from God?  [هُدًۭى · referent]
turns on: Whether hudā identifies particular bearers or texts, all divine indications, or inward and natural forms of direction.
- 20:123/q12/p1 It comes through a revealed Book and a messenger, with law and clarification. — Muqātil speaks of messengers bearing books with clarification; al-Thaʿlabī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Bayḍāwī, Bursevī, and al-Ālūsī name Book and Messenger. Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī include religious law; Abū Ḥayyān says God's law; al-Biqāʿī adds intellectual support for the Messenger. Abū al-ʿĀliya names prophets, messengers, and clear explanation; Ibn ʿĀshūr charges people to follow messengers and revelation. [16 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو العالية); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:123/q12/p2 It is divine indication, right direction, and clarification broadly, embracing messengers, proofs, and scripture. — Al-Ṭabarī defines clarification of God's chosen religion and path; Ibn ʿAṭiyya a prescribed summons; al-Qurṭubī right direction and true speech; al-Rāzī includes all indications, messengers, proofs and Qurʾān. Al-Ālūsī prefers this general scope: dhikr in 20:124 can be any divine scripture and signs in 20:126 proofs generally; the specific reports name honored instances. Ibn ʿĀshūr glosses guidance as directing to good. Al-Jishumī insists indication and explanation, not faith itself, against compulsion. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 20:123/q12/p3 The promised guidance is the Qurʾān specifically. — Al-Rāzī reports this view; al-Ālūsī reports its appeal to 'My remembrance' in 20:124 and God's signs in 20:126, as well as Ibn ʿAbbās's Qurʾān report, but prefers a general meaning. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:123/q12/p4 It is the messengers specifically. — Al-Rāzī reports the narrower view among some interpreters. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 20:123/q12/p5 It is proofs and indications specifically. — Al-Rāzī reports this alternative and then includes it in his broader synthesis. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 20:123/q12/p6 Inwardly it is divine light in prophets' and saints' hearts, outwardly leading scholars and shaykhs. — The Najmī interpretations, reported by Bursevī, say the light leads its recipients to God after the prophets and messengers. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 20:123/q12/p7 It is the natural religion of beliefs and acts fitted to human constitution. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 30:30: each creature's happiness matches its created endowment; guidance after divine election brings religious felicity in exclusive servitude to God and cannot fail as worldly benefits through fallible causes can. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:123/q13 To whom is the promised guidance addressed?  [يَأْتِيَنَّكُم · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural promise reaches Satan or is directed to Adam's human descendants.
- 20:123/q13/p1 It addresses Adam and his descendants as future recipients. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls Adam humanity's ancestor and excludes Iblīs because his enduring evil is disclosed, unlike Abū Jahl. Bursevī names the descendants of Adam and Eve; the Najmī reading says Adam was sent to earth for a test and guidance promised to his descendants. Al-Rāzī sees descendants in the plural address. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 20:123/q13/p2 The speech promises guidance to Adam, Eve, and Satan. — Al-Ṭabarī includes Satan with Adam and Eve in the promise. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]
- 20:123/q13/p3 The report about each person's jinn companion does not make Iblīs a recipient of this invitation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the al-Dāraquṭnī report that everyone, including the Prophet, has a jinn companion and that his became Muslim through God's aid; he says God inspired it to command good, without the Prophet inviting it to Islam. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي ﷺ)]

## 20:123/q14 What does following God's guidance require?  [ٱتَّبَعَ هُدَاىَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether following is described through text and messengers, inference and practice, or inward submission and resistance to desire.
- 20:123/q14/p1 It requires following the Book's teaching and God's messengers in belief and action. — Ibn ʿAbbās repeatedly speaks of reading or reciting the Qurʾān and acting on or following it. Muqātil, al-Suddī, and Ibn ʿAbbās name messengers and books; Sahl and al-Tustarī add the Prophet's example and Sunna. Al-Ṭabarī says act on the clarification without deviating; al-Nasafī specifies obeying commands and avoiding prohibitions; Bursevī specifies belief in the Book and affirmation of the Messenger. [27 notes: MUJAHID(ابن عباس); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); SULAMI(سهل); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,الشعبي عن ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:123/q14/p2 It includes reasoning from evidence as well as acting on it. — Al-Rāzī says following evidence must combine inference and practice; Abū Ḥayyān reports his formulation. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي)]
- 20:123/q14/p3 It means leaving personal desire and resisting the enemy's whisper. — Al-Qushayrī says this brings every good and averts harm; al-Biqāʿī says God's guidance alone protects from the enemy, and Iṣlāḥī calls the Book the only shield from devils among jinn and people. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:123/q14/p4 Inwardly it is submission, acceptance, and imitation of a good example. — The Najmī interpretation says this keeps one on truth and prevents deprivation and estrangement. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 20:123/q15 Why is guidance described as something followed, and why this wording?  [ٱتَّبَعَ هُدَاىَ · rhetoric]
- 20:123/q15/p1 The wording stresses complete, effortful following and honors God's guidance. — Al-Biqāʿī says form VIII ittabaʿa conveys strenuous completeness because guidance matters greatly; al-Ālūsī says the repeated explicit noun rather than a pronoun, attached to God, honors guidance and intensifies duty. Al-Burhān notes ittabaʿa here accords with the later yattabiʿūna al-dāʿī. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURHAN]
- 20:123/q15/p2 Calling guidance itself the thing followed is metaphor for following its guide. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī supplies the underlying sense: follow the guide who guides by God's guidance. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:123/q16 When and in what sense will the follower neither stray nor suffer?  [فَلَا يَضِلُّ وَلَا يَشْقَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clauses divide this life and the next, both apply to one domain, or have unrestricted scope; the next verse and transmitted reports inform the choice.
- 20:123/q16/p1 There is no religious error in this world and no misery or punishment in the afterlife. — This is the repeated Ibn ʿAbbās explanation. Some versions say protection from evil reckoning or uncertainly from Resurrection's terror; al-Ṭabarī adds entry to Paradise and escape from torment. Al-Kashshāf links afterlife misery to worldly religious error. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the negation of misguidance is universal and contrasts revelation from the all-knowing God, supported and protected messengers, with human systems vulnerable to oversight, conflicting evidence, custom, and appeasing leaders; he illustrates with Socrates' tales and rooster offering to Hermes. He links misery to 20:124. Al-Ālūsī prefers this reading because of transmitted reports, despite finding the reversed reading attractive. [51 notes: MUJAHID(ابن عباس); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,الشعبي عن ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو الطفيل,ابن عباس,بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:123/q16/p2 The promise covers freedom from both error and misery in both worlds. — Al-Tustarī and Sahl include this life and the next; al-Biqāʿī extends it to every undertaking in both lives. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the unrestricted wording covers error and misery in both worlds; Asad speaks of neither straying nor unhappiness. Al-Qushayrī says all good comes and harm is avoided. [7 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل); MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:123/q16/p3 Both the absence of error and of misery concern the afterlife. — Al-Rāzī reports that God guides the follower to the Garden and enables him to dwell there; Abū Ḥayyān transmits the same inference and al-Ālūsī attributes it to the Imam. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)]
- 20:123/q16/p4 Both promises concern this world, with religious error and religion-caused hardship excluded. — Al-Rāzī says guidance followers can suffer other worldly hardship; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī relay that qualification. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قول آخر); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)]
- 20:123/q16/p5 No straying means reaching Paradise later, while no misery means no exhausting livelihood struggle now. — Al-Ālūsī reports that this reverses the usual domains: afterlife orientation answers resurrection blind in 20:124, and worldly ease answers constricted livelihood there; the reversed order reflects the guided person's focus on the afterlife. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 20:123/q17 What does not suffering or shaqāʾ mean here?  [وَلَا يَشْقَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether shaqāʾ is afterlife punishment, worldly toil, or inward estrangement.
- 20:123/q17/p1 It means freedom from afterlife misery, punishment, and bad reckoning. — Ibn ʿAbbās reports vary between misery and an evil reckoning, while Saʿīd ibn Jubayr's transmission of his report uncertainly mentions Resurrection's terror. Al-Ṭabarī specifies Paradise rather than torment; Ibn ʿĀshūr links the opposite misery to 20:124. [9 notes: MUJAHID(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:123/q17/p2 Shaqāʾ can mean toil or hardship. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports interpreters' lexical sense, citing the Prophet not being burdened by the Qurʾān as well as this promise. Al-Rāzī excludes only hardship caused by religion in his worldly reading; al-Ālūsī reports a livelihood reading. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 20:123/q17/p3 Inward misery is separation, deprivation, and estrangement from God. — Al-Biqāʿī reports al-Rāzī's al-Lawāmiʿ defining happiness as reaching God and misery as separation; the Najmī reading describes freedom from deprivation and estrangement. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الرازي); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 20:123/q18 What does the verse teach about human action, reason, and divine guidance?  [* · theology]
- 20:123/q18/p1 Human acts originate from human agents, and guidance indicates the way rather than creating faith. — Al-Jishumī invokes these verses against the doctrine of compulsion and identifies guidance as indication and explanation, not faith itself. [2 notes: JISHUMI]
- 20:123/q18/p2 People have natural reason, yet revelation supplies the secure route against error and Satan. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports many scholars' belief that knowledge of the one God is innate or widespread and that polytheists without messengers are still liable to punishment. Iṣlāḥī says revelation joins nature and intellect as a weapon against Satan, and argues Adam, as a prophet, received a fitting sharīʿah, citing directives around Abel and Cain in 5:31. Study Quran says guidance allows humanity to recover its original nature after the Fall. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(علماء الإسلام,كثير من علماء الإسلام); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:123/q18/p3 Divinely selected guidance reaches exclusive servitude to God without failure. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts direct religious felicity with worldly benefits through fallible causes, citing 16:37; he also identifies the natural religion with the created human constitution, citing 30:30. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:123/q19 What specific transmitted reports accompany the promise?  [فَمَنِ ٱتَّبَعَ هُدَاىَ · reports]
- 20:123/q19/p1 A Prophetic recitation and saying apply the clause to followers of God's Book. — Al-Durr reports Abū al-Ṭufayl's hearing of the Prophet reciting the phrase and a Prophetic saying promising freedom from worldly error and evil afterlife reckoning. Al-Ālūsī cites al-Ṭabarānī and others for a recitation with indefinite hudan, without grading; he also gives an Ibn ʿAbbās Prophetic report without grading. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī quotes the report from al-Durr. [5 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو الطفيل,ابن عباس); TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]
- 20:123/q19/p2 Reports applying this guidance and 'My remembrance' to the Prophet's household's wilāya are applications, not lexical definitions. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī classifies these reports as Qurʾānic jary. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:123/q20 How does this verse relate to other accounts and its surrounding passage?  [* · links]
- 20:123/q20/p1 It revisits the earlier accounts of descent and guidance in al-Baqara and al-Aʿrāf. — Yaḥyā, al-Wāḥidī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī point back to those accounts, with references including 2:36, 2:38, and 7:24. Study Quran compares the commands in 2:36, 7:24, and 20:123 and notes that 7:24 addresses Adam and wife after Iblīs's descent. Kur'an Yolu recalls the related episodes in Ṣād and al-Aʿrāf and the Qurʾān's contextual retellings; it cites 2:30 and 7:11. Iṣlāḥī draws on 2:36 and 7:24 for the two-group reading. [11 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:123/q20/p2 Here the promise is stronger than the corresponding promise in al-Baqara. — Al-Biqāʿī argues that since misery is error's penalty, denying misery also denies fear and grief; this passage urges fearful striving against enmity and neglect, in relation to 2:38, 20:3, 20:100, and 20:115. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:123/q20/p3 The following warning about turning away helps explain the promise. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reads afterlife misery against 20:124; al-Ālūsī reports the reversed reading's appeal to blindness and constricted livelihood in 20:124, while his general guidance reading treats 20:124 and 20:126 as compatible with all scripture and proofs. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
