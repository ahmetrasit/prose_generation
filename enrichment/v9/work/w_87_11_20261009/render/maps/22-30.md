# 22:30: 26 questions

## 22:30/q01 What does 'that' refer to and how does it introduce this statement?  [ذَٰلِكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the demonstrative resumes the preceding rites, supplies an elliptical predication or command, or marks a discourse transition.
- 22:30/q01/p1 It sums up the prescribed pilgrimage acts just mentioned. — Al-Tabari specifies removing dishevelment, fulfilling vows, and circumambulating the Ancient House; Tabataba'i extends the summary back to Abraham's rites, prayer, sacrifice and pure worship. Islahi reads a close to Abraham's directions and an address to negligent heirs. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 22:30/q01/p2 It is a discourse transition, 'the matter is thus,' with an omitted subject or predicate. — Al-Zajjaj and Abu Ishaq make it nominative; Zamakhshari compares an author's transition, and Samin prefers an omitted subject. Ibn Ashur instead makes 'that' a fronted subject whose predicate is omitted. Zuhayr's line and 38:55 exemplify the usage; the remote demonstrative dignifies the prior matter. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(زهير); SAMIN-DURR+]
- 22:30/q01/p3 It means 'your prescribed obligation is that.' — Ibn Atiyya supplies 'your obligation' or 'the obligation' as the omitted element; Qurtubi also allows this nominative construction. [4 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية)]
- 22:30/q01/p4 It is an accusative object of an implied command to perform or obey what preceded. — Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, and Alusi record the omitted command as 'carry out,' 'comply with,' or 'follow.' [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 22:30/q01/p5 It is a nominative topic with an omitted predicate referring back to the previous matter. — Samin and Abu Hayyan report this alternative; Nasafi permits 'let them do that' alongside 'the matter is that.' [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; NASAFI-FULL]
- 22:30/q01/p6 It sums up pilgrimage's great worldly and otherworldly benefits. — Al-Biqa'i takes the pointing word to refer to the great pilgrimage and its benefits. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 22:30/q02 What are God's inviolable things and what does honoring them require?  [حُرُمَٰتِ ٱللَّهِ يُعَظِّمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the term covers all divine limits, pilgrimage duties or prohibitions, a list of sacred places and states, or inward disciplines.
- 22:30/q02/p1 They include all divine obligations, commands, prohibitions, and sacred things; honoring them means obeying and preserving them. — Al-Layth defines hurma as what may not be violated, and al-Zajjaj as what must be upheld. Wahidi prefers Ibn Abbas's obligations and Sunna as most comprehensive; Abu Hayyan prefers the general sense. Qurtubi includes recommended acts; Razi reports theologians excluding supererogatory acts. Zajjaj includes Hajj and Umra. Elmalili includes sacred places. Tabari and Durr transmit Mujahid's combined list; Ibn Jawzi repeats the lexical rule that violation is unlawful. [44 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,الزجاج,الليث,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الزجاج,الليث); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(المتكلمون); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الليث); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)+]
- 22:30/q02/p2 They are specifically the rites of Hajj and Umra, honored by performing them. — Ibn Qutayba names pebble casting and standing at Jamʿ or Muzdalifa. Surrounding 22:29 motivates the narrower sense for Baghawi, Tabarsi and Qurtubi. Al-Kalbi emphasizes performing prescribed rites; Ibn Ashur adds sacrificial animals, garlands and the sacred site and prefers this breadth over a fixed list. [19 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن قتيبة); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(أكثر المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكلبي); ALUSI-FULL(جمع); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 22:30/q02/p3 They are God's forbidden acts or sins, especially prohibitions during ihram; honoring means refraining. — Ata and Ikrima gloss sanctities as sins. Ibn Abbas specifies intercourse, sin, quarrels and hunting in consecration. Tabari, Ibn Jawzi, Ibn Kathir and Tabataba'i stress not crossing divine limits. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(عطاء وعكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 22:30/q02/p4 They are named sacred sites, times and states: the House, Mosque, territory, month, and pilgrim in consecration. — Ibn Zayd's lists vary: some include Sacred Station or Landmark and some omit month or ihram. Zayd ibn Aslam's five include the Kaaba, Mosque, land, month, and person or site in consecration. Wahidi links the list to 2:194, as does Tabarsi. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن زيد); KASHSHAF-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); TABRISI(ابن زيد); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNASHUR-FULL(زيد بن أسلم)]
- 22:30/q02/p5 Inwardly, sanctities include spiritual practice and reverence preserving faith. — Qushayri stresses jealous care for faith and warns that neglect of service brings punishment, while neglected reverence risks separation and disturbed oneness. Bursevi says obedience reaches Paradise but reverence brings nearness to God. Qashani includes self-offering, purification, virtue, disclosures and stations. [7 notes: QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 22:30/q03 What does 'it is better for him with his Lord' mean and what does 'it' resume?  [فَهُوَ خَيْرٌۭ لَّهُۥ عِندَ رَبِّهِۦ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether khayr is comparative or absolute good; all connect the gain to reverence.
- 22:30/q03/p1 Honoring the sanctities earns good or reward with God in the afterlife. — The pronoun resumes the verbal noun of 'honor,' as Wahidi, Samin and Ibn Ashur explain; Nahhas parses subject and predicate. Ibn Abbas calls the benefit increased obedience and fear. Razi and Ibn al-Shaykh read 'with his Lord' as stored reward. Alusi says the possessive 'his Lord' honors the person. [27 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; QASHANI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 22:30/q03/p2 'Better' is comparative: reverence benefits one more than neglect or taking sanctities lightly. — Al-Asamm explicitly contrasts veneration with treating sanctities lightly; Samin accepts the apparent comparative sense with interpretive qualification. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(الأصم); QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 22:30/q03/p3 'Good' is a promise of good, without an implied comparison. — Qurtubi reports this alternative; Abu Hayyan says no comparator need be supplied, and Alusi transmits that observation. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]

## 22:30/q04 What livestock does God make lawful and under what basic rule?  [وَأُحِلَّتْ لَكُمُ ٱلْأَنْعَٰمُ · law]
turns on: Whether livestock is read as actual sacrificial and edible animals or inwardly as sound souls.
- 22:30/q04/p1 Camels, cattle, sheep and goats—the eight male and female pairs—may be slaughtered and eaten; pagan dedicated or taboo categories remain lawful. — Tabari and Ibn Kathir name bahira, saiba, wasila, hami, and idol-dedicated beasts. Bursevi excludes horses, mules and donkeys. Alusi notes lawful action is slaughter or eating rather than the animal itself. Asad says what revelation does not explicitly prohibit is lawful. Ibn Jawzi points to the livestock discussion at 5:1. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 22:30/q04/p2 Inwardly, lawful cattle are sound souls whose moral qualities and actions may be used on the path. — Qashani says the wayfarer uses the soul's rights rather than personal interest. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 22:30/q05 What does 'except what is recited to you' exclude?  [إِلَّا مَا يُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْكُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the recited exception names forbidden food categories, game in consecration, or an inward analogue.
- 22:30/q05/p1 It excludes the scripturally forbidden foods, especially carrion, blood, pork, offerings to other than God and animals dying by strangling, blows, falls, goring or predators. — Most cite 5:3 or 5:1; Ibn Ashur cites prior Meccan prohibitions in 6:145 and al-Nahl, whereas Tabataba'i cites al-An'am, al-Nahl and al-Baqara. Qatada singles out carrion and animals over which God's name was not pronounced. Tabataba'i says pilgrimage context especially targets pagan offerings at the Kaaba, Safa, Marwa and Mina. This exception never reinstates pagan bahira or saiba bans. [33 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 22:30/q05/p2 It excludes hunting or game during ihram. — Yahya links the restriction to 5:1. Thalabi, Mawardi, Ibn Jawzi and Qurtubi transmit the alternative; Razi says the clause prevents confusing livestock with game. [6 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 22:30/q05/p3 Inwardly, the exceptions are vices disguised as virtues. — Qashani calls acts of the soul in an improper mode forbidden to wayfarers. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 22:30/q06 How is the exception grammatically attached?  [إِلَّا مَا يُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether prohibited cases remain members of the livestock class or the recited list extends beyond it.
- 22:30/q06/p1 The exception is accusative and connected: some livestock become forbidden by an incidental cause. — Nahhas and Zajjaj call mā accusative. Samin and Bursevi give death as a contingent cause; Alusi says this is the majority analysis and cites carrion and offerings to another. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 22:30/q06/p2 The exception is disconnected because livestock are not intrinsically forbidden or some listed foods are not livestock. — Samin refers to the argument at 5:1; Alusi reports that the list of 5:3 includes non-livestock items. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 22:30/q07 Why say 'is recited' in the present tense?  [يُتْلَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the imperfect evokes a past recitation, recurrence, or an already current recitation rather than a future reference.
- 22:30/q07/p1 The present evokes an already recited prohibition with emphasis, not necessarily future revelation. — Alusi says the prohibition verse had already been recited. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 22:30/q07/p2 The present marks continuing and repeated recitation. — Alusi reports the contextual reading; Ibn Ashur covers earlier recitation continuing into the present and later revelation such as 5:103. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 22:30/q07/p3 It means prohibitions already being recited, with no need to anticipate the later Ma'idah verse. — Tabataba'i prefers this because food bans appear in al-An'am, al-Nahl and al-Baqara; Ibn Ashur likewise points to earlier Meccan verses. [2 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 22:30/q08 Why is livestock permission inserted among the pilgrimage commands?  [وَأُحِلَّتْ لَكُمُ ٱلْأَنْعَٰمُ · links]
turns on: Whether its chief link is correcting animal law in Hajj or preparing the return to prohibition.
- 22:30/q08/p1 It corrects pagan animal taboos and clarifies that ihram does not forbid livestock as it forbids game. — Zajjaj cites 6:139 and 5:103; Razi, Bursevi and Alusi describe possible confusion in consecration. Qurtubi connects the rule to animals sacrificed at Hajj; Ibn Ashur to 6:145. Samarra'i notes 22:28 and 22:34 specify bahimat al-an'am after general remembrance, while this verse names livestock directly. [14 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 22:30/q08/p2 It prepares the later commands by distinguishing God's limits from idols and false claims about animals. — Tabataba'i prefers the sanctities sentence as preparation; Alusi says the exception eases transition to the idol ban. Bursevi says permission to consume leads to clarification of what must be avoided. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 22:30/q09 What does 'avoid' require?  [فَٱجْتَنِبُوا۟ · meaning]
- 22:30/q09/p1 Keep well away, leave aside, and do not approach the prohibited thing. — Jishumi gives the lexical sense of distance; Ibn Jawzi says to leave the abomination; Razi and Bursevi extend avoidance to every form of false speech. [4 notes: JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 22:30/q10 What does rijs mean here, and why are idols so called?  [ٱلرِّجْسَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether impurity belongs to idols as moral or legal filth, a disparaging figure, blood on the objects, or related gambling.
- 22:30/q10/p1 It means foulness or impurity; idols are figuratively or legally filthy because their worship is sinful, causes punishment, or deserves revulsion. — Ibn Qutayba contrasts rijs, stench or filth, with rijz, torment, citing 9:125, 10:100, 7:134 and 8:11. Wahidi says an idol is not physical dirt; Razi rejects physical impurity. Kashshaf compares wine, gambling and divining arrows at 5:90. Qurtubi reports impurity as legal status removed by faith; Bursevi distinguishes natural, rational and legal impurity. [26 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الأخفش); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 22:30/q10/p2 Idols are called rijs to disparage and belittle them. — Razi prefers this explanation to Al-Asamm's blood account; Baydawi says comparison to repulsive filth intensifies the ban. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 22:30/q10/p3 Rijs is sacrificial blood smeared on idols. — A reported account says polytheists covered idols in blood. Al-Asamm offers this rationale; Razi rejects it as far-fetched. [1 notes: TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL(الأصم)-]
- 22:30/q10/p4 Imami reports extend the prohibited filth to chess, backgammon and other gambling. — Tabrisi and Tabataba'i transmit these reports. [2 notes: TABRISI(أصحابنا); TABATABAI(أصحابنا)]

## 22:30/q11 What does awthan mean, and how is the word explained?  [ٱلْأَوْثَٰنِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term denotes erected worship objects only or also whatever the self treats as an object of worship.
- 22:30/q11/p1 Awthan are erected objects worshipped as idols, made of stone, wood or metal; a cross can be called a wathan. — Shammar lists wood, stone, gold, silver and copper. Al-A'sha calls the Christian cross a wathan; reports from Adi ibn Hatim say the Prophet called his gold cross an idol. Bursevi transmits a distinction between a human-shaped sanam and a less fashioned wathan, while Mufradat says worshipped stones. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأعشى,النبي,شمر); QURTUBI-FULL(عدي بن حاتم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(رسول الله,شمر); BURSEVI(المفردات,بعضهم); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 22:30/q11/p2 Wathan derives from standing fixed in place. — Wahidi, Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan cite the related word wathin; Ru'ba's line illustrates steadfastness in an old bond or covenant. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(رؤبة); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 22:30/q11/p3 In a wider reading, idols include false beliefs, values, practices and enslaving desires. — Asad extends concrete idols to worshipped values; Qushayri says one's followed desire and self become one's idol; Qashani cites 45:23. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 22:30/q12 What does 'the filth of idols' command one to avoid?  [ٱلرِّجْسَ مِنَ ٱلْأَوْثَٰنِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the object of avoidance is idols as such, their worship or other attached idolatrous practices.
- 22:30/q12/p1 It forbids worshipping idols and obeying Satan in that worship. — Tabari says all idols are rijs and rejects the claim that the phrase singles out only some; Ibn Abbas emphasizes not obeying Satan. Wahidi reports the worship brings curse, punishment or sin. The wording extends avoidance to the object to intensify the ban, as Tabataba'i notes. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الأخفش); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن جريج); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî)]
- 22:30/q12/p2 It commands avoiding the idols themselves as impure objects. — Zajjaj's and Tabarsi's explanatory reading identifies the rijs as the idol; Ibn Ashur says it clarifies what lies outside God's sanctities. Al-Kur'an Yolu attributes this view to Ibn Ashur. [17 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr)]
- 22:30/q12/p3 It includes cleansing the House and keeping away from tangible as well as spiritual filth. — Elmalili connects slaughter in God's name with removal of idols and distinguishes carrion's tangible filth from idolatry's spiritual filth. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 22:30/q12/p4 It may cover offerings dedicated to other than God as an expression of ingratitude. — Alusi reports a causal reading tied to livestock permission and the later 'not associating'; he objects that lawful status itself does not prove monotheism. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 22:30/q13 What is the force of min in 'the filth of idols'?  [مِنَ ٱلْأَوْثَٰنِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether min identifies the idol as the kind of rijs, a defiling aspect of it, its source, or adds no semantic relation.
- 22:30/q13/p1 It explains or specifies the kind: the filth is idols. — Zajjaj, Abu Ishaq, grammarians, and most exegetes support this; Kashshaf compares specifying dirhams, Mubarrad cites 48:29 and Sibawayh. Razi says it cannot imply only some idols are filth. Burhan tests substitution with alladhi. Tabataba'i prefers this and calls alternatives strained. [21 notes: NAHHAS; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,المبرد); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(المعربون); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN(التراس); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 22:30/q13/p2 It is partitive: the filth arising from idols is the forbidden act of worshipping them. — Al-Akhfash calls this rare but good, and Ibn Abbas and Ibn Jurayj allow the idols' use for building while prohibiting worship. Ibn Abi al-Rabi prefers it and cites 39:17. Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi object that it corrupts the sense; Alusi rejects their objection but finds the reading strained. [10 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش); JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس وابن جريج); BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عطية); TABATABAI(بعضهم); BURHAN(ابن أبي الربيع)+; QURTUBI-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية)-; ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 22:30/q13/p3 It marks the origin or starting point of filth in idol worship. — Qurtubi says idol worship gathers all corruption; later Maghribi grammarians say impurity is broader than idols. Alusi considers this reading strained. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(متأخري المغاربة); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 22:30/q13/p4 It is an extra particle. — Jishumi reports the analysis without further support. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 22:30/q13/p5 The explanatory and source senses can be combined here. — Abu al-Baqa proposed the blend; Samin rejects the conflation. [0 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-]

## 22:30/q14 What is meant by 'false speech'?  [قَوْلَ ٱلزُّورِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the expression remains a general ban on falsehood or is focused on shirk, a pilgrimage formula, false testimony, fabricated food law, or inward and other speech.
- 22:30/q14/p1 It is all lies and falsehood, including slander and fabricated speech against God. — Mujahid glosses lying. Zajjaj accepts lying, shirk and false testimony together. Tabari names claims that idols mediate at 39:3 and that angels are daughters. Biqa'i includes falsehood deviating from proof. Alusi and Razi treat specific applications as included without narrowing the general ban; Alusi prefers this breadth. [33 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT; ZAJJAJ; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 22:30/q14/p2 It is associating partners with God or idolatrous speech. — Yahya says lying about God is shirk; Ibn Abbas calls it fabrication and denial.
