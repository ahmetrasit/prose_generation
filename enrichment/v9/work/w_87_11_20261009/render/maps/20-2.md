# 20:2: 16 questions

## 20:2/q01 How does this clause relate syntactically to Ṭā Hā?  [مَآ · grammar]
turns on: Whether Ṭā Hā is letter names, a sūra name, an oath, a vocative, or an elliptical construction.
- 20:2/q01/p1 It begins an independent statement after the disconnected letter names. — Al-Majāz treats Ṭā Hā as introductory disconnected letters; al-Nasafī and al-Samīn say this parse makes the clause independent. Al-Ālūsī calls the consolation independent. [5 notes: MAJAZ; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:2/q01/p2 It is the predicate of Ṭā Hā understood as the sūra or Qur'an, with al-Qur'an replacing a pronoun. — Al-Nasafī, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī explain the overt noun by the identity of Ṭā Hā and Qur'an; al-Ālūsī says it emphasizes comforting mercy and permits reference to this sūra or the Qur'an generally. Some object that this newly revealed sūra could not have caused prior toil; al-Ālūsī finds that reasonable. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]
- 20:2/q01/p3 It answers an oath expressed by Ṭā Hā. — Muḥammad b. Kaʿb makes the oath by God's stature and guidance; al-Zajjāj allows the oath form; al-Ālūsī notes an omitted oath particle. Al-Qāshānī's inward oath invokes two divine names. [8 notes: THALABI(محمد بن كعب القرظي); TABRISI(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); QASHANI]
- 20:2/q01/p4 It answers a vocative Ṭā Hā. — Al-Bayḍāwī lists the vocative answer among the possible parses; the gloss of Ṭā Hā as 'O man' supplies its address. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; YAHYA-SALLAM; MAJAZ]
- 20:2/q01/p5 Ṭā Hā has an omitted predicate or is governed by an implied 'recite'. — Al-Ālūsī reports both ellipses as other syntactic parses. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 20:2/q02 How is anzalnā read here?  [أَنزَلْنَا · readings]
turns on: Active first-person revelation or passive revelation with the Qur'an as grammatical subject.
- 20:2/q02/p1 The majority read active mā anzalnā, 'We did not send down'. — Abū Ḥayyān calls this the majority reading; al-Samīn calls it common. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(طلحة والجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(طلحة)]
- 20:2/q02/p2 Ṭalḥa reads passive mā nuzzila, with al-Qur'an nominative as the passive subject. — Al-Ālūsī specifies the doubled zāy; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn give the grammatical consequence. Al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī also record the passive wording. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(طلحة والجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(طلحة); ALUSI-FULL(طلحة)]

## 20:2/q03 Why is the Qur'an called Qur'an?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانَ · meaning]
- 20:2/q03/p1 Its name expresses its conjunction with its divine speaker without separation. — Al-Wāsiṭī compares Qur'an and God to sunlight and heat reaching people while remaining joined to the sun's disk. [1 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي)]

## 20:2/q04 How is the word Qur'an pronounced here?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانَ · readings]
- 20:2/q04/p1 A reading omits the hamza, producing qurān without changing the meaning. — Qirāʾāt-ER attributes the simpler pronunciation to Hamza and explicitly denies a semantic change. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(hmz)]

## 20:2/q05 What does the lām and its construction express?  [لِتَشْقَىٰٓ · grammar]
turns on: How the purpose lām and any omitted preposition govern their complements; whether toil can syntactically lead to the later reminder.
- 20:2/q05/p1 The lām expresses the denied purpose: revelation was not sent in order to make him suffer. — Ibn Kaysān calls it the lām of lowering; Abū Ḥayyān notes its shift from God as sender to the Messenger as addressee. Al-Naḥḥās records labels 'lām of negation' and 'lām of denial' for the same negative construction. [3 notes: NAHHAS(ابن كيسان,بعض النحويين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 20:2/q05/p2 An tashqā may appear without the lām after deletion of the preposition. — Al-Zamakhsharī compares 49:2; al-Samīn calls the accusative secondary after preposition deletion, unlike the basic object tadhkira, and also cites 7:155. Abū Ḥayyān says grammarians dispute whether the verb governs accusative or the omitted preposition still governs. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)]
- 20:2/q05/p3 The later 'reminder' may be related to litashqā as toil leading to reminder. — Al-Zamakhsharī argues that toil can be the route to a reminder; Abū al-Baqāʾ rejects making litashqā govern tadhkira as semantically unsound. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]

## 20:2/q06 What does tashqā mean here?  [لِتَشْقَىٰٓ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word denotes immediate fatigue or the broader state of misery opposed to happiness; several sources allow both.
- 20:2/q06/p1 It means to toil, tire, or reach exhaustion through effort. — Al-Wāḥidī defines exhaustion at the limit of effort; al-Dāmghānī compares 20:117 and 20:123; al-Baghawī calls toil its basic sense; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Samīn and Bursevī cite the proverb about labor training a colt; al-Ālūsī adds a poem contrasting suffering rational and ignorant persons. Al-Naḥḥās says shaqāʾ admits long and short forms and a wāw root. [11 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 20:2/q06/p2 It means continuing misery or unhappiness, opposite felicity. — Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī describe a continuing burden on the soul; al-Ālūsī says opponents may have used it literally in this sense and God echoed their word. Kur'an Yolu also allows 'become unhappy'. [4 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 20:2/q07 What hardship does the verse relieve?  [لِتَشْقَىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether shaqāʾ addresses bodily toil in worship, emotional distress and responsibility for rejection, opponents' taunt, worldly humiliation, or enduring hardship.
- 20:2/q07/p1 Excessive night prayer and severe worship should not exhaust the Prophet. — Mujāhid compares 73:20 and ropes tied during prayer; al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq mention one-legged prayer; al-Kalbī says the Prophet later divided night between prayer and sleep. Other accounts describe swollen feet, standing on toes, shifting weight, or all-night vigil. Al-Bursevī and al-Ālūsī invoke the easy, upright religion. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ adds that grateful service gives gnostics repose and cites the Prophet's grateful-servant reply. Al-Qāḍī objects that commanded worship would be happiness, not denied hardship. - 20:2/q07/p2 He should not grieve, strain in debate, or blame himself for people's refusal to believe; his duty is to convey and remind. — Abū Muslim cites 18:6 and 4:80; others compare 18:6, 10:65, 88:22 and 6:107. Al-Qāshānī attributes rejection to hard veils and lack of receptivity, not faulty guidance. Al-Biqāʿī ties his fear to Quraysh's resistance and the prospect of long labor. Iṣlāḥī says the Prophet feared his own effort inadequate; the reminder serves receptive God-fearing people. [26 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:2/q07/p3 The verse refutes unbelievers who claimed that revelation or leaving their religion made him wretched. — Muqātil names Abū Jahl, al-Walīd, al-Naḍr and al-Muṭʿim; other transmitters name smaller groups or Quraysh generally. Al-Ḥasan says God answered their accusation, and al-Ṭabrisī says the Quran instead prepares and honors him in both lives. Al-Ālūsī sees rhetorical reuse of their word shaqāʾ. [26 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(أبو جهل بن هشام والنصر بن الحرث); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-ASBAB(الضحاك,مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل والضحاك); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي والضحاك ومقاتل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(أبو جهل والنضر بن الحارث); ALUSI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 20:2/q07/p4 He will not remain humiliated under hostile Meccan power; revelation promises later honor and strength. — Al-Rāzī relates this to an early Meccan sūra and enemy dominance. Kur'an Yolu reports later power, prestige and recognition. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bazıları)]
- 20:2/q07/p5 The faithful follower is relieved from ultimate hardship, especially in the afterlife. — Al-Jishumī says adherence prevents suffering; some connect relief to abrogation of night prayer, while al-Ḥasan and Abū ʿAlī make it broad relief from afterlife hardship. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 20:2/q08 For what benefit was the Qur'an sent?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانَ لِتَشْقَىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the benefit is chiefly guidance for hearers, the Prophet's nearness and gratitude, or the happiness radiating through his character.
- 20:2/q08/p1 It is a reminder and guide for those who fear God, bringing mercy, light and happiness. — Muqātil contrasts suffering with reminder from the Creator of earth and seven heavens; Qatāda calls it mercy, light and a guide to Paradise. Al-Jishumī notes happiness through conveying and practicing it. Ibn Kathīr cites the Bukhārī-Muslim report that God gives religious understanding to one intended for good and a graded-good report from Thalaba about forgiving scholars; Thalaba is identified as al-Laythī, resident of Basra then Kufa, narrated by Samāk. Elmalılı adduces reports of angels descending for Usayd's recitation, the Prophet's exhortation at the Farewell Pilgrimage to hold to Qur'an, and fear of God marking the best reciter, then prays for illuminated hearts. Kur'an Yolu says revelation guides, reminds and warns responsible people without extinguishing life's joy. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(أبو جهل بن هشام والنصر بن الحرث); QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; TABRISI(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ELMALILI(Resûl-i Ekrem,Ukbe b. Âmir,Üseyd b. Hudayr); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 20:2/q08/p2 It opens nearness and grateful service rather than imposing suffering. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ explains intense worship as gratitude for nearness and communion, citing the Prophet's grateful-servant reply. Al-Qushayrī says the Quran prepares the ground for connection and nearness. [4 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,النبي); QUSHAYRI(النبي)]
- 20:2/q08/p3 It makes the Prophet happy through embodying its character and spreads happiness through him. — The Taʾwīlāt Najmiyya says his noble character gladdens inhabitants of heaven and earth. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 20:2/q09 What event prompted the verse according to the reports?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in speakers and named opponents, and in whether the triggering toil was all-night prayer, ropes, swollen feet, one-foot standing, toes, or shifting weight.
- 20:2/q09/p1 Four named Quraysh leaders mocked Muhammad as wretched for abandoning his fathers' religion. — Muqātil names Abū Jahl, al-Walīd b. al-Mughīra, al-Naḍr b. al-Ḥārith and al-Muṭʿim b. ʿAdī; after the Prophet answered that he was sent as mercy they repeated the charge. Al-Rāzī preserves the four-name form. [2 notes: MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL(مقاتل)]
- 20:2/q09/p2 Abū Jahl and al-Naḍr mocked his strenuous worship and departure from their religion. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī and Bursevī give the two names; al-Ālūsī adds al-Muṭʿim. Their details vary between long worship, leaving ancestral religion and receiving Qur'an. [4 notes: THALABI(أبو جهل بن هشام والنصر بن الحرث); WAHIDI-ASBAB(مقاتل); BURSEVI(أبو جهل والنضر بن الحارث); ALUSI-FULL(مقاتل)]
- 20:2/q09/p3 Quraysh or unnamed polytheists said the Qur'an made him suffer after seeing prayer or austerity. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk locates the charge after Muhammad and Companions stood praying with the newly revealed Qur'an; Ibn Kathīr names Juwaybir as transmitter. Al-Ḥasan reports the general polytheist charge; Ibn ʿAṭiyya stresses austere living and much worship. The reported wording varies between 'suffer with his Lord' and suffering caused by Qur'an. [13 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-ASBAB(الضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل والضحاك); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي والضحاك ومقاتل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(الضحاك); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 20:2/q09/p4 People remarked without named speakers that he had made himself wretched for his Lord. — Ibn ʿAbbās reports this remark in al-Ṭabarī, al-Durr and al-Suyūṭī. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس)]
- 20:2/q09/p5 After revelation in Mecca he prayed through the whole night, then began both sleeping and praying. — Al-Kalbī says revelation intensified worship; this verse ended uninterrupted night prayer. Al-Baghawī also describes transferring weight during the long standing. [4 notes: THALABI(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي)]
- 20:2/q09/p6 The Prophet or Companions tied ropes to their chests to sustain night prayer. — Mujāhid links the practice to the easing of recitation in 73:20; versions differ on whether the Prophet alone or his companions joined him and whether ropes held them from sleep. Al-Qurṭubī says the practice was superseded by the obligation. Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid give variants, including placing one leg over the other. [11 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد)]
- 20:2/q09/p7 He prayed until his feet swelled and was told by Gabriel or the verse to spare his body. — Al-Rāzī says Gabriel reminded him of his body's right; al-Nasafī says his soul's right. ʿAlī links the swollen feet to night-vigil command and Gabriel's instruction to place them on the ground, followed by 73:20. Al-Thaʿlabī cites Mughīra's grateful-servant dialogue. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ prefers ʿAlī's combined account as the likely original but notes that its fit with surrounding verses is unclear. [7 notes: THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(علي); TABATABAI(علي); TABATABAI+]
- 20:2/q09/p8 He prayed on one raised foot until told to plant both feet. — Al-Ḥasan, al-Rabīʿ and Abū ʿAbd Allāh report the raised foot; Ibn ʿAbbās adds Qur'an recitation on one foot. Qāḍī ʿIyāḍ calls the instruction an honor and kindness. The report's details vary as to the speaker and whether Ṭā Hā means 'tread the earth'. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); TABRISI(أبو عبد الله,الحسن,النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الأنباري,الحسن,الربيع بن أنس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الربيع بن أنس); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله,الربيع بن أنس)]
- 20:2/q09/p9 At revelation's beginning he stood praying on his toes or the balls of his feet until they swelled. — Ibn ʿAbbās reports the toes or balls of feet; the Imams' report through Abū Baṣīr says they swelled and glosses Ṭā Hā in Tayy speech as 'O Muhammad'. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(أبو بصير عن أبي جعفر وأبي عبد الله)]
- 20:2/q09/p10 He alternated his weight between his feet during prolonged prayer. — ʿAlī's report is graded ḥasan by al-Bazzār in al-Durr; al-Rabīʿ gives a similar version in al-Suyūṭī. Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Baghawī describe the motion; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ integrates it with swollen feet and Gabriel's intervention. [6 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي والضحاك ومقاتل); DURR-FULL(علي); SUYUTI-LUBAB(الربيع بن أنس); TABATABAI(علي)]
- 20:2/q09/p11 Anas saw a woman's rope between mosque pillars; the Prophet ordered alert prayer and sleep when overcome. — Yaḥyā b. Sallām also reports the Prophet's advice to pray at night only while understanding and sleep when recitation becomes difficult. [2 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(أنس بن مالك,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 20:2/q10 How does the verse connect with its surrounding passages?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the main connection is Maryam's closing warning, parallel consolation elsewhere, or the following description of the revealer.
- 20:2/q10/p1 It answers the Prophet's grief after Maryam's warning about destroyed or corrupt generations and anticipates receptive hearers. — Al-Biqāʿī cites 19:58-59 and 19:98; Ibn al-Zubayr says the preceding warning could alarm Muhammad. Al-Biqāʿī says the sūra assures him that patient gentleness will grow his community, mentioning 19:96, 10:65 and the following throne statement in 20:5; early Muslims were calmed and ʿUmar became a defender rather than the community being destroyed like Noah's and Hūd's peoples. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير)]
- 20:2/q10/p2 It parallels other reassurances that prophets need not consume themselves over rejection. — Al-Jishumī, al-Bayḍāwī and others cite 18:6; al-Qushayrī connects the reaction to 15:88. Study Qur'an places it with 2:214, 11:36, 15:97-99, 21:83-84 and 26:3 on prophets' human reactions. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the ending echoes the opening and closes the debate with the Prophet's mission fulfilled. [9 notes: QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:2/q10/p3 Revelation from the Creator of earth and heavens dignifies the Qur'an and invites reflection and obedience. — Al-Rāzī says honoring the sender's message encourages attention to its meanings; Muqātil mentions the Creator of earth and seven high heavens. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; MUQATIL]

## 20:2/q11 What is the force of the exception after this denial?  [* · grammar]
- 20:2/q11/p1 The following 'except' means 'rather': the Qur'an was sent as a reminder. — Al-Burhān treats the exception as corrective rather than a literal exception from causing suffering. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 20:2/q12 What does Ṭā Hā mean in this opening?  [* · meaning]
turns on: Whether the letters function as unexpanded opening letters, a vocative, an imperative, a symbol of felicity, or divine names; one note's gloss is missing.
- 20:2/q12/p1 It is disconnected introductory letters. — Abū ʿUbayda compares other sūra openings; al-Sāmarrāʾī says these openings commonly precede mention of Qur'an and the best-known account challenges Arabs to match speech made from their own letters, citing 2:2, 3:3, 7:2, 36:2, 50:1 and 38:1. Another reported approach combines letters into words. [3 notes: MAJAZ; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(بعضهم)]
- 20:2/q12/p2 It is a vocative meaning 'O man' or, in Tayy speech, 'O Muhammad'. — Abū Ṭufayla is cited for 'O man'; Yaḥyā takes it as the renewed address. The Imams' report via Abū Baṣīr glosses it as 'O Muhammad' in Tayy speech. Abū ʿUbayda argues against treating it as an indeclinable proper name because a name would inflect. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAJAZ; TABATABAI(أبو بصير عن أبي جعفر وأبي عبد الله); MAJAZ-]
- 20:2/q12/p3 It commands the Prophet to tread or press the earth with both feet during prayer. — Al-Ḥasan, Ibn al-Anbārī and al-Rabīʿ connect it to one-foot prayer or swollen feet. Al-Qurṭubī reports forms deriving from ṭaʾa: either hamza lightened to silent alif with hāʾ and alif referring to ground, or hamza omitted and hāʾ of pause added. Ibn Masʿūd replies to an objector that he recites exactly as the Messenger taught him. [11 notes: TABRISI(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الأنباري,الحسن,الربيع بن أنس,زر بن حبيش,طائفة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الربيع بن أنس); TABATABAI(علي)]
- 20:2/q12/p4 It expresses blessedness for whoever is guided. — Al-Qurṭubī ascribes this sixth gloss to Mujāhid and Muḥammad b. al-Ḥanafiyya. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد ومحمد بن الحنفية)]
- 20:2/q12/p5 Its ṭāʾ signifies the joy of Paradise's people and its hāʾ the humiliation of Hell's people. — Al-Qurṭubī records this as an anonymous gloss. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 20:2/q12/p6 Its two letters represent divine names of purification and guidance. — Al-Qāshānī says they free the Prophet from residual selfhood and defective guidance; discipline made him perfected and perfecting. He alternatively reads an oath by the two names disclosed for refinement. [4 notes: QASHANI]
- 20:2/q12/p7 The preserved note gives parallels of terror cast in disbelievers' hearts, but leaves its Ṭā Hā gloss unspecified. — Al-Qurṭubī cites 3:151 and 33:26; the preceding gloss is not preserved in this note. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 20:2/q13 How is Ṭā Hā pronounced?  [* · readings]
turns on: Whether the two sounds undergo imāla, partial imāla, or full opening, and whether Arabic phonology permits imāla here.
- 20:2/q13/p1 Abū ʿAmr and Abū Isḥāq incline hāʾ and open ṭāʾ. — Al-Qurṭubī lists this recitation among the dialect forms that al-Thaʿlabī regards as correct and eloquent. ## 20:2/q14 What inward meaning does this reassurance bear?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the inward point is the perfected guide's freedom from blame or the soul's recollection of former union.
- 20:2/q14/p1 The Prophet's prior discipline perfected him; hardened hearts, not a defect in him, explain their resistance. — Al-Qāshānī says mercy made him fear a shortcoming when revelation did not move his people; the names Pure and Guide exclude residual selfhood or failed guidance, while absent receptivity prevents effect. [5 notes: QASHANI]
- 20:2/q14/p2 The reminder recalls prebodily union and either promises return or stirs longing, tears and effort to regain it. — Al-Ālūsī reports both versions of the esoteric reading. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:2/q15 What does the verse imply for a believer's practice and life?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: How the denial of hardship applies to practical limits in worship and to the broader quality of life.
- 20:2/q15/p1 Prayer and Qur'an recitation should stop when weariness prevents alertness or understanding. — Anas reports the Prophet telling a woman to sleep rather than rely on a rope to stay upright; another prophetic report says to sleep when night recitation becomes difficult. [2 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(أنس بن مالك,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 20:2/q15/p2 Qur'anic discipline guides and enriches life without imposing disabling hardship. — Asad says ethical discipline deepens awareness of right and wrong; Kur'an Yolu says worship does not exceed capacity and revelation guides without extinguishing joy. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bazı müfessirler)]

## 20:2/q16 What rhetorical sound pattern appears in the opening?  [* · rhetoric]
- 20:2/q16/p1 Its linked endings exemplify identical final letters across an opening. — Al-Rummānī compares the linked endings at the opening of Ṭā Hā with those at the opening of al-Ṭūr. [1 notes: RUMMANI]
