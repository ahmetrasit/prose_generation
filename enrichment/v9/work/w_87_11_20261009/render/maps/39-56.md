# 39:56: 30 questions

## 39:56/q01 How does 'an taqula' depend on the preceding commands?  [أَن تَقُولَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the prevention sense is expressed by purpose syntax, an omitted noun, or an omitted negative.
- 39:56/q01/p1 It is a purpose clause: return, submit, and follow revelation lest a soul say this. — Al-Farra, Tabari, Baghawi and others compare 16:15; Tha'labi also cites 2:184. Ibn Atiyya attaches it to anibu, Ibn Ashur to 39:54-55 with an omitted purpose lam. Al-Mubarrad and Zajjaj paraphrase it as acting before remorse or fearing the utterance. [33 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(الزجاج,المبرد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج,المبرد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); BURHAN(الكوفيون); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية,بعض النحاة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 39:56/q01/p2 An omitted noun of aversion, caution, or fear supplies the negative purpose. — Nahhas gives karahata an taqula; Kufans say li-alla, Basrans posit karaha or hidhār. Zamakhshari supplies aversion; Hawfi and Abu al-Baqa supply 'we warned you for fear.' Al-Alusi prefers aversion over fear and says it denotes lack of approval, not God's will. Islahi posits an omitted governing noun. [13 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء والحوفي,الزمخشري); BURHAN(البصريون); ALUSI-FULL(الحوفي,الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 39:56/q01/p3 A negative la is omitted after an. — Ibn Ashur prefers this account to al-Kashshaf's fear paraphrase, citing 6:155-157 and 4:135 as parallels. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 39:56/q02 Which soul does the indefinite 'nafs' designate?  [نَفْسٌۭ · referent]
turns on: Whether the indefinite is partitive, generic, or singles out an exceptional sinner.
- 39:56/q02/p1 Some souls, particularly unbelieving or polytheist souls. — Nasafi and Zamakhshari say the partitive indefinite can mean some unbelievers. Abu Hayyan prefers this; Al-Alusi says warning any possible member is enough. Ibn Ashur calls it a category of polytheist souls and cites 81:14 and Labid. [8 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)+]
- 39:56/q02/p2 Many or every soul subject to this warning. — Razi relates generic regret after neglected obedience; Qurtubi, Baydawi, Samin and Al-Alusi cite al-A'sha's indefinite noun for a group. Bursevi says the warning may cover all; Biqai says anyone might be intended. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري); BAYDAWI-FULL(الأعشى); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:56/q02/p3 One unusually persistent sinner or one facing exceptional punishment. — Razi, Zamakhshari, Nasafi and Samin report this possibility; Al-Alusi finds it unpersuasive. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 39:56/q03 What does the word 'nafs' itself mean here?  [نَفْسٌۭ · meaning]
- 39:56/q03/p1 A person or self. — Jishumi equates nafs with dhat and connects it with preciousness, the self being an animal's most precious part. Asad says nafs normally means a human being and renders its pronouns masculine despite Arabic feminine gender. [2 notes: JISHUMI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 39:56/q04 Is the soul's saying an audible cry or inward speech?  [أَن تَقُولَ نَفْسٌۭ · interpretation]
- 39:56/q04/p1 It may be an audible lament or inward speech. — Ibn Ashur says the audible cry adds to hidden regret, while the wording also permits inner speech. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:56/q05 What is the meaning of 'hasrata'?  [يَٰحَسْرَتَىٰ · meaning]
- 39:56/q05/p1 Intense sorrow or regret over irrecoverable neglect and loss. — Suddi glosses it as regret; Tha'labi, Baghawi, Ibn Jawzi, Tabarsi, Bursevi, Ibn Ashur and al-Majma' stress loss beyond recovery. Jishumi links its root to a camel exhausted at journey's end; Bursevi cites sorrow leaving the soul exhausted, and says ignorance is stripped away. Al-Farra calls it the most intense remorse. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(السدي); THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN(الفراء); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(المجمع); STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]

## 39:56/q06 Why address regret with 'ya'?  [يَٰحَسْرَتَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 39:56/q06/p1 The speaker summons personified regret because its moment has arrived, making the warning and lament forceful. — Zajjaj says regret has seized the speaker; Azharī, Sibawayh, Baghawi, Bursevi and Ibn Ashur say 'come, now is your time.' Jishumi says regret cannot literally be summoned, so the cry alerts listeners. Basrans compare 'O wonder'; Ibn Jinni says this would be the time to call regret if it could be called. Nahhas says vocative is stronger than a report. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; JISHUMI(الأزهري); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سيبويه); BURHAN(ابن جني,البصريون); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(سيبويه); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:56/q06/p2 It expresses a plea for aid or an exclamation of utmost grief. — Farra says Arabic changes the possessive ending in appeals into a plea form; Bursevi calls it an appeal for help. Al-Farra elsewhere glosses it 'what regret!'. [4 notes: FARRA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); BURSEVI; BURHAN(الفراء)]

## 39:56/q07 How is 'ya hasrata' read and analyzed?  [يَٰحَسْرَتَىٰ · readings]
turns on: Whether the ending is possessive ya, its alif substitute, both together, a dual, or a pausal ha; connected ha is disputed.
- 39:56/q07/p1 The common form ends in alif replacing the speaker's possessive ya. — Tabari compares ya waylata and ya nadama; Farra, Tha'labi, Mawardi, Jishumi, Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, Ibn Jawzi, Qurtubi, Nasafi, Samin, Bursevi and Ibn Ashur explain the alif as easier and allowing a prolonged cry. Abu Hayyan reports the majority reading. [25 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; THALABI(الفراء); MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(الباقون,الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(سيبويه); IBNASHUR-FULL; NASHR(أبو جعفر وابن جماز)]
- 39:56/q07/p2 The original possessive ya is retained: ya hasrati. — Zajjaj permits it; Ibn Atiyya calls it the form of most readers in his account. Ibn Jawzi names Hasan, Abu al-Aliya, Abu Imran and Abu al-Jawza; Samin and Al-Alusi report Abu Ja'far. Baydawi and Nasafi note the variant. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وأبو العالية وأبو عمران وأبو الجوزاء); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو جعفر); ALUSI-FULL(أبو جعفر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 39:56/q07/p3 Abu Ja'far also reads ya hasratay, retaining both alif and added possessive ya. — Ibn Atiyya reports open and silent ya and Ibn Jammaz's kasra-ta/silent-ya variant; Ibn Jinni objects to combining substitute and original, but Tabarsi cites Farazdaq and Abu Zayd as parallels. Nashr says Ibn Jammaz has open ya, Ibn Wardan both open and silent, and al-Jazari defends the silent transmission. Samin offers dialectal defense and weaker alternative analyses; Biqai says it intensifies grief. [29 notes: THALABI(أبو جعفر,الفراء); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر,الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جعفر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو جعفر بن القعقاع,ابن جماز); TABRISI(أبو جعفر والباقون,أبو زيد,ابن جني,الفرزدق); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جعفر,الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(معاذ القارئ وأبو جعفر); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(أبو جعفر); NASHR(أبو جعفر وابن جماز,ابن وردان); BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر); ALUSI-FULL(أبو جعفر); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو جعفر); QIRAAT-ER(Cs|Prn,skn); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو الفتح)-]
- 39:56/q07/p4 Ya hasratay may be a dual, expressing repeated or two regrets. — Abu al-Fadl al-Razi gives loss of Paradise and entry into Fire as the two; Samin notes expected contracted morphology as an objection, and cites the dialect retaining dual alif. Sahib al-Kashf prefers an emphatic dual like labbayk; Al-Alusi prefers intensity because Judgement Day has many sorrows. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو الفضل الرازي); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو الفضل الرازي,صاحب الكشف)+]
- 39:56/q07/p5 A final ha may be appended in lament or at a pause. — Farra adduces Abu Fuq'as/Banu Asad's ya rabbah and ya marhabah, with kasra, damma or fatha; Zajjaj rejects the poetic proof as irrelevant to a Quranic reading. Nahhas rejects connected-speech ha because grammarians, text and readings do not allow it. Ibn Kathir reads silent ha at pause; Qiraat-ER describes its pausal sound. Some grammarians derive the form from ya hasratah with dropped ha. [14 notes: FARRA(أبو ثروان العكلي,أبو فقعس); TAB-FULL(الفراء); ZAJJAJ(الفراء,رجل من بني أسد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن كثير); SAMIN-DURR(ابن كثير); BURHAN(قوم); ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير); QIRAAT-ER(Cs); ZAJJAJ(الكوفيون,النحويون أجمعون)-; NAHHAS(الفراء)-; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(النحويون أجمعون)-]

## 39:56/q08 What does 'ala' add to the lament?  [عَلَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 39:56/q08/p1 It depicts regret as pressing firmly upon what was lost. — Ibn Ashur says 'upon' is the usual link with hasra and makes the regret cling to its object; Al-Alusi treats it as causal. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:56/q09 How does 'ma' function in 'ala ma farrattu'?  [مَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether ma makes an action noun or refers to neglected things.
- 39:56/q09/p1 It is a verbal noun marker: 'for my neglect.' — Nasafi compares 9:25; Abu Hayyan, Samin, Bursevi, Al-Alusi and Ibn Ashur agree. Al-Alusi compares 2:185. [6 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:56/q09/p2 It can be a relative pronoun: 'what I neglected.' — Ibn Ashur supplies a deleted object pronoun after farraṭt and makes 'in God's side' describe that object. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:56/q10 What does 'farrattu' mean?  [فَرَّطتُ · meaning]
- 39:56/q10/p1 I neglected or fell short of what was due before its opportunity passed. — Muqatil says neglect; Ibn Atiyya says falling short of what was required. Tabarsi and al-Majma' define omission before time passes; Raghib contrasts tafrit with excess in pressing ahead. Biqai stresses broken trust and lost order; KuranYolu prefers Ibn Ashur's required-duty sense. [13 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(المجمع); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr)+]

## 39:56/q11 What does 'fi janb Allah' mean?  [فِى جَنۢبِ ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Which figurative relation 'side' denotes—command, duty, remembrance, reward, path, nearness, essence, or application to the Imams—and whether a bodily sense is possible.
- 39:56/q11/p1 God's command or affair, including the commanded path of faith. — Mujahid and Suddi say command; Ibn Arafa and others use affair. Zajjaj specifies monotheism and the Messenger's prophethood. Tabari says failure to act on commands in worldly life; Ibn Atiyya includes law and faith. Arabic idiom and Kathir's poetry attest janb as an affair. [30 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(السدي,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(أهل المعاني,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد والسدي); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(مجاهد والسدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد والزجاج); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عرفة,مجاهد); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد والسدي); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(اللغويون); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,مجاهد); ITQAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî)]
- 39:56/q11/p2 God's obedience, duty or right is what was neglected. — Hasan repeatedly says obedience; Sa'id ibn Jubayr says God's due. Baydawi cites Sabiq al-Barbari's verse for the right owed a beloved; Samin derives right from side. Burhan says neglect cannot apply to a bodily flank. Tabatabai specifies worshipping God alone without disobedience. KuranYolu prefers obedience. [42 notes: TUSTARI; TAB-FULL; THALABI(الحسن,سعيد بن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن,سعيد بن جبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن,سعيد بن جبير); RAZI-FULL(الحسن,سعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL(سابق البربري); NASAFI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(اللغويون); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,الحسن,الراغب,سابق البربري,سعيد بن جبير); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 39:56/q11/p3 Remembrance of God, especially the Quran and acting on it. — Muqatil, Dahhak, Ikrima and some Mujahid reports say remembrance; Nahhas and Qurtubi specify the Quran and practice. Abdullah's variant reads fi dhikr Allah. [10 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عكرمة والضحاك); RAZI-FULL(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مقاتل)]
- 39:56/q11/p4 God's reward, particularly Paradise, was forfeited. — Ibn Abbas and Abu Ubayda give reward; Naqqash specifies Paradise. The nearness interpretation also points to Paradise. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(النقاش); TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 39:56/q11/p5 The path or side leading to God's pleasure and reward. — Arabic uses janb for a side, means or road. Tha'labi quotes the poet contrasting ruler and people and one enduring bitterness for another's sake; Zajjaj connects the path with faith. Ibn Ashur's master-and-preserve picture lets the side remain concrete within a tableau. [29 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI(الشاعر,قيل); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(الزجاج,كثير); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); NASAFI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الراجز,الشاعر); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI(الراغب,كشف الأسرار); ALUSI-FULL(الراغب,زياد الأعجم,سابق البربري); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 39:56/q11/p6 Seeking nearness to God, ultimately Paradise. — Al-Farra explains janb by neighborliness and 4:36's companion at one's side; Mawardi speaks of seeking proximity. Study Quran includes bodily, emotional and spiritual closeness. Al-Alusi rejects this reading as needing an extra metaphor and reducing to obedience. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 39:56/q11/p7 God's essence is meant, with an omitted related noun in some accounts. — Mawardi reports Hasan; Baghawi records the view, Baydawi adds omitted annexation, Nasafi lists it. Al-Alusi rejects it as awkward even figuratively. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 39:56/q11/p8 The Imams are God's side as an application of the verse. — Tabrisi transmits Abu Ja'far via al-Ayyashi and Abu al-Jarud. Tabatabai says reports apply the verses to the Imams' Shi'a and calls them application rather than primary exegesis. [2 notes: TABRISI(أبو جعفر); TABATABAI(أئمة أهل البيت)]
- 39:56/q11/p9 The phrase is a figure for what belongs to God, not a divine bodily limb. — Razi rejects physical parts; Abu Hayyan says bodily attribution is impossible. Raghib traces bodily side to neighboring direction; Razi and al-Imam extend part/side to a necessary attendant, while Al-Alusi rejects al-Imam's explicit-metaphor analysis. Baydawi and Abu Hayyan compare putting virtues in a man's dome; Ibn Ashur pictures a master's neglected preserve. [24 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(اللغويون); ITQAN; BURSEVI(الراغب,كشف الأسرار); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,الراغب,زياد الأعجم); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب)]

## 39:56/q12 Is there a variant reading for 'fi janb Allah'?  [فِى جَنۢبِ ٱللَّهِ · readings]
- 39:56/q12/p1 A reading attributed to Abdullah has 'fi dhikr Allah' ('in God's remembrance'). — Nasafi and Al-Alusi report this wording; it supports the remembrance interpretation. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله)]

## 39:56/q13 Why say 'Allah' rather than use a pronoun?  [ٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
- 39:56/q13/p1 Naming God heightens the warning about the Supreme King's right. — Biqai says this right was neither forgotten nor treated lightly. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 39:56/q14 How is 'wa-in kuntu lamina' parsed?  [وَإِن كُنتُ لَمِنَ ٱلسَّٰخِرِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause is a circumstantial modifier or a separate report; the particle and plural also need parsing.
- 39:56/q14/p1 In is lightened emphatic inna; the lam distinguishes it from negative in. — Bursevi, Al-Alusi and Tabatabai give this parsing; Ibn Ashur calls the lam differentiating. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 39:56/q14/p2 The clause is circumstantial: the person neglected duty while mocking. — Razi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Zamakhshari, Bursevi and Ibn Ashur say the simultaneous mockery aggravates neglect. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 39:56/q14/p3 It is an independent account of the speaker's former state. — Abu Hayyan and al-Bahr reject the circumstantial parse; it reports worldly mockery and intensifies regret. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(البحر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 39:56/q14/p4 The masculine plural means 'among the people or group of mockers' although nafs is feminine. — Nahhas answers the grammatical objection by supplying group, people or folk. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جعفر النحاس)]

## 39:56/q15 Whom or what did the speaker mock?  [ٱلسَّٰخِرِينَ · referent]
- 39:56/q15/p1 God's religion, scripture, Messenger, command, and believers. — Tabari lists command, scripture, Messenger and believers; Suddi specifies Prophet, Book and message. Qatada stresses a further wrong beyond neglect: mocking obedient people. Tabrisi also names callers to faith. Biqai calls the mockery habitual arrogance; Ibn Kathir contrasts scoffing with conviction. [32 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(السدي,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(النقاش,يحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة والسدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); NASAFI-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 39:56/q15/p2 The phrase describes a life of mockery, play, falsehood, or worship of others. — Qurtubi reports the broader explanation; Ibn Kathir says the former conduct was scoffing rather than certain faith. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 39:56/q15/p3 Sakhir means one who mocks; one gloss takes the mockers as foolish or senile. — Damghani groups 2:212 and 49:11 under mockery; Ibn Atiyya and Tabatabai derive the active participle from sakhira. Itqan supplies the foolish/senile gloss. [5 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ITQAN]

## 39:56/q16 Why say 'among the mockers' rather than simply 'a mocker'?  [لَمِنَ ٱلسَّٰخِرِينَ · rhetoric]
- 39:56/q16/p1 Membership in the group gives the admission extra emphasis. — Ibn Ashur compares 2:67's wording about being among the ignorant. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:56/q17 What does the verse warn people to do before remorse comes?  [* · interpretation]
- 39:56/q17/p1 Repent, submit and follow revelation now; regret after the chance passes is futile. — Rummani, Baqillani and Burhan call the cry the strongest warning against neglect. Ibn Kathir locates the regret on Judgment Day; Islahi says worldly pleasure distracted people and later wishes to believe come too late. Study Quran urges repentance before delay; KuranYolu describes missed submission and obedience. Itqan cites it as self-reproach beside 25:27; Biqai stresses benefit recognized too late. [16 notes: RUMMANI; BAQILLANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 39:56/q17/p2 Neglect of vigilance and reliance on God yields inward regret over missing Him. — Tustari names immediate worldly life, appetite and ego; Sahl describes lost service; Faris says those who flee to self regret missing God when they see the knowers' stations, a theme Bursevi repeats. [4 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل,فارس); BURSEVI(الفارسي)]

## 39:56/q18 What report links a neglectful Israelite scholar to this lament?  [* · reports]
- 39:56/q18/p1 He abandoned learning or worship for vice after Satan promised time to repent, then died in sin and lamented obeying Satan. — Abu Salih's versions in Tha'labi and Qurtubi say God revealed his story; Abu Hayyan retells it. Details vary about final deeds determining the end, squandered wealth, and death amid pleasure. No grading is stated. [3 notes: THALABI(أبو صالح); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو صالح); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 39:56/q19 What did God foretell about the regretful speakers and their return?  [* · reports]
- 39:56/q19/p1 God disclosed their future speech and acts; returned sinners would repeat their former conduct. — Ibn Abbas in Ibn Abi Hatim mentions 6:28 and 6:110; Ibn Kathir and Durr cite 35:14 for foreknowledge. Durr's damaged Ibn Abbas fragment leaves a blank before al-mahluqin. Durr also cites 6:28 and 6:110 for failed guidance on return. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 39:56/q20 What do reports say about remembering God and future regret?  [* · reports]
- 39:56/q20/p1 Sitting, walking or lying without remembering God becomes regret on Judgment Day. — Nahhas gives an Abu Hurayra Prophetic report; Qurtubi cites Abu Dawud and calls the regret tirah. [2 notes: NAHHAS(أبو هريرة); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 39:56/q20/p2 The resurrection has specific regrets over lost wealth, rank and sight. — Ibrahim al-Taymi says one sees another benefit from one's former wealth, a former slave nearer God, and a former blind acquaintance seeing while oneself is blind; Nahhas and Qurtubi give variants. [2 notes: NAHHAS(إبراهيم التيمي); QURTUBI-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي)]

## 39:56/q21 What are the three later sayings and how do they relate?  [* · links]
- 39:56/q21/p1 They proceed from regret over neglect, to a guidance excuse, to a wish to return and act well. — Tabari and Razi name the three; Ibn Atiyya says the two later aw taqulu clauses coordinate with the first. Zamakhshari and Ibn Ashur say the order reflects the movement of thought, with Ibn Ashur citing 23:99-100. Qatada assigns the sayings to different groups; Tabatabai says moving the reply would disrupt the arrangement. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); DURR-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 39:56/q21/p2 The divine reply answers the guidance excuse and distributes answers to the other claims. — Zamakhshari says it answers the one needing an answer; Ibn Ashur says guidance is met by rejected signs, neglect by arrogance, and wishing to fear God by disbelief; the mockery was already confessed. Tabatabai says God rebuffs the first and third, citing 23:106-111. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 39:56/q22 What does the passage's placement in the sura show?  [* · links]
- 39:56/q22/p1 The call to repent prepares the contrast between saved believers and lost unbelievers. — Tabatabai says 39:53's ban on despair opens one connected discourse of repentance, submission and action; Islahi says neither intercessors nor polytheism avert ruin. Study Quran says 39:54-67 leads into the final contrast. [4 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 39:56/q23 Do these regrets presuppose human ability to obey?  [* · theology]
- 39:56/q23/p1 Yes; reproach, guidance requests and wishes to return presuppose capacity to obey and change. — Al-Qadi argues that blame and wishes make no sense if God gave only an unchangeable capacity for unbelief. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي)]

## 39:56/q24 What reported occasion concerns verses 53–59?  [* · reports]
- 39:56/q24/p1 One reported view says the verses were revealed about Wahshi. — Study Quran attributes this to other commentators without further detail. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Other commentators)]

## 39:56/q25 How does Aisha's exoneration narrative enter the material?  [* · reports]
- 39:56/q25/p1 Reports recount the slander, her distress, revelation of innocence, and consequences for the accusers. — Ibn Hisham says Aisha hoped for a dream rather than revelation, then the Prophet publicly recited her vindication and ordered penalties for Mistah, Hassan and Hamna. Bukhari links 24:11 and Abu Bakr's renewed aid after 24:22; Muslim recounts Safwan's escort and Abdullah ibn Ubayy's role, and notes an echo of 39:56. These reports do not otherwise explain this verse. [5 notes: IBNHISHAM(عائشة); BUKHARI(عائشة); MUSLIM(عائشة)]

## 39:56/q26 What recitation conduct is discussed alongside this verse?  [* · sciences]
- 39:56/q26/p1 A reader should complete and separate suras, pause at verses, and recite with gratitude, dignity and self-scrutiny. — Burhan says use a pause or basmala between suras; Bayhaqi prefers the Prophet's verse pauses even across incomplete syntax. It also warns against boasting or worldly gain, quotes Halimi on markets and bathhouses, and Nawawi on quiet roadside recitation. [5 notes: BURHAN(الحليمي); BURHAN(البيهقي)+]

## 39:56/q27 What Qur'anic notation and pronunciation conventions are noted here?  [فَرَّطتُ · sciences]
- 39:56/q27/p1 Vowel, assimilation and concealment signs follow established notation; the ta after ta in farraṭtu is marked with sukun. — Itqan contrasts early colored dot vowels with al-Khalil's modern letter-derived signs, doubled tanwin, red omitted alif and hamza, red mim for iqlab, and assimilation marking. It singles out ṭāʾ before tāʾ in farraṭtu. [4 notes: ITQAN]

## 39:56/q28 How do cited discussions frame interpretation of divine expressions?  [فِى جَنۢبِ ٱللَّهِ · theology]
- 39:56/q28/p1 Interpret the apparent Arabic sense as God's right or obedience while affirming divine transcendence. — Itqan cites Ibn al-Salah on early practice, Ibn Burhan's preference for interpretation, Ibn Daqiq al-Id's allowance of interpretations close to Arabic and suspension on remote ones, then applies the apparent sense here to God's due. [5 notes: ITQAN(ابن الصلاح,ابن برهان,ابن دقيق العيد)]
- 39:56/q28/p2 An estimated aversion in the purpose clause does not settle the doctrine of divine will. — Al-Alusi says aversion here is the opposite of approval and does not entail the Mu'tazilite position. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:56/q29 What general Qur'anic study methodology is recorded in these notes?  [* · sciences]
- 39:56/q29/p1 Al-Itqan arranges Qur'anic studies into eighty kinds, potentially more than three hundred if separated. — Itqan compares earlier works by Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Sakhawi, Abu Shama and Shidhila, judging them smaller samples. [2 notes: ITQAN]

## 39:56/q30 Why use declarative wording for a directive?  [* · rhetoric]
- 39:56/q30/p1 A declarative prohibition can be stronger because a prophetic report is treated as unable to fail. — Burhan quotes Nawawi's examples of 'he does not propose' and 'bid,' and notes that 'do not ask' may be read as statement or direct prohibition. [1 notes: BURHAN(النووي)]
