# 74:49: 13 questions

## 74:49/q01 How does the preceding passage set up this rebuke?  [فَمَا · links]
- 74:49/q01/p1 The prior account supplies reasons to heed the reminder, making their continued rejection astonishing and baseless. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya points to the judgment and intercession just described; Abū Ḥayyān says they turn away while awaiting that situation. Biqāʿī says the saved and damned dialogue is recounted as a moral lesson; the believers ask about the damned to learn their condition, not to intercede, and had earlier been too occupied by that day's immense concerns to know others' fate. Bursawī points to the dreadful fate and ample reasons to heed the Qur'an. Ālūsī says the fāʾ orders the rebuke after reasons to accept and reflect. Ibn ʿĀshūr links it to the Qur'an's description as a reminder for humankind in 74:31. Ṭabāṭabāʾī points to the preceding threat and promise and says they seek individual revelation despite manifest truth. Iṣlāḥī mentions advance warning, a revealed Book, and a messenger. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:49/q02 What force does the question فما لهم have?  [فَمَا لَهُمْ · rhetoric]
- 74:49/q02/p1 It rebukes and expresses amazement that they turn away without good cause despite what they have received. — Sībawayh says mā shaʾnuka/mā laka carries the sense 'why?' Al-Wāḥidī's Wajīz asks why they reject the Prophet's admonition; Ṭabrisī asks why they reject the Qur'an's admonitions. Qurṭubī frames it as why Mecca's people turn from the brought message. Bursawī asks what could make them turn from the Qur'an after so many reasons to heed. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies an interrogative of amazement at a strange state; Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise says it expresses astonishment. Iṣlāḥī stresses their advance warning, Book, and messenger. [9 notes: SIBAWAYH; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:49/q03 Who are the people referred to by لهم?  [لَهُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun is anchored to the original Meccan audience or extended to people who refuse truth generally.
- 74:49/q03/p1 The Meccan disbelievers, especially the polytheists of Quraysh. — Ṭabarī calls them polytheists; al-Wāḥidī's Basīṭ and Ibn al-Jawzī specify Quraysh's disbelievers. Qurṭubī calls them Mecca's people. Their rejection of the Qur'an and admonition is explicit in Ṭabarī and Ibn al-Jawzī. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 74:49/q03/p2 People who refuse to listen to the truth more generally. — Asad speaks of the many people who refuse to listen to truth, without restricting the question to Quraysh. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 74:49/q04 How is the construction فما لهم عن التذكرة معرضين parsed?  [فَمَا لَهُمْ عَنِ ٱلتَّذْكِرَةِ مُعْرِضِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether مُعْرِضِينَ is read as the accusative state of the pronoun in لهم or as an expression of their having become heedless or turned away.
- 74:49/q04/p1 مُعْرِضِينَ is an accusative circumstantial expression describing the pronoun in لهم; عن التذكرة depends on it. — Sībawayh compares mā shaʾnuka qāʾiman and mā laka qāʾiman; his analogies man dhā qāʾiman bi-l-bāb, hādhā ʿAbd Allāh qāʾiman, and li-man al-dāru maftūḥan bābuhā illustrate the circumstantial accusative. He distinguishes man dhā khayrun minka, where no identifiable person is indicated and the description is relative-clause-like, from an indicated person, where khayra minka is accusative circumstantial. Al-Naḥḥās, Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Bursawī and Iṣlāḥī also give the circumstantial parse; al-Nasafī compares 'why are you standing?' Qurṭubī says the lām has verbal force licensing the state. Al-Samīn calls it a necessary circumstantial expression from the pronoun in mā's prepositional predicate and makes ʿan al-tadhkira governed by muʿriḍīn. Bursawī calls mā interrogative and the pronoun part of a locative predicate. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes lahum as the predicate of interrogative mā, with an understood 'is established'; he compares 12:11, 84:20, 37:154 and 68:36. Ṭabāṭabāʾī supplies an understood kāna (mā kāna lahum), with the same state and dependence of ʿan al-tadhkira. Ālūsī likewise attaches the state to the pronoun in the prepositional predicate. [21 notes: SIBAWAYH; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:49/q04/p2 The expression can instead be read as saying that they have become turned away or heedless of the reminder. — Thaʿlabī mentions an alternative parse in which they have become heedless of it; al-Baghawī likewise gives the alternative 'they became turned away.' Neither supplies further grounds. [2 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL]

## 74:49/q05 Why is عن التذكرة placed before معرضين?  [عَنِ ٱلتَّذْكِرَةِ مُعْرِضِينَ · rhetoric]
- 74:49/q05/p1 The fronting emphasizes the reminder and preserves the verse ending. — Ālūsī says ʿan al-tadhkira is governed by muʿriḍīn despite its earlier position; its placement gives emphasis and maintains the verse ending. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 74:49/q06 What is التذكرة from which they turn away?  [ٱلتَّذْكِرَةِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether التذكرة specifies the Qur'an, covers every admonition, or is described through the Prophet's delivered message; Ālūsī favors the Qur'an from the following context.
- 74:49/q06/p1 The Qur'an, including its admonitions and warnings. — Qatāda identifies it as this Qur'an in Ṭabarī and al-Durr. Al-Wāḥidī's Basīṭ, al-Baghawī, al-Nasafī and Ṭabrisī emphasize admonition within the Qur'an; al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī gloss the noun as reminder or admonition. Al-Bayḍāwī reports the Qur'an reading. Ālūsī prefers this specification because of the following context, while noting that the noun literally means reminding and is applied emphatically to what is remembered. The Study Quran cites Q and Ṭ for the Qur'an identification. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 74:49/q06/p2 Reminder or admonition broadly, including exhortations beyond the Qur'an. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī explicitly allow the Qur'an or other exhortations. Al-Bayḍāwī reports reminder in its broader sense. Ālūsī acknowledges all admonition as possible, though he prefers the Qur'an. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the explicit noun makes the rebuke cover every reminder, especially the Qur'an, and compares 81:27. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:49/q06/p3 The Prophet's admonition or the message he brought to them. — Al-Wāḥidī's Wajīz calls it the Prophet's admonition; Qurṭubī describes the message brought to Mecca's people. [2 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 74:49/q07 Why name التذكرة explicitly instead of using a pronoun?  [ٱلتَّذْكِرَةِ · rhetoric]
- 74:49/q07/p1 It broadens the rebuke to rejection of every reminder, especially the Qur'an. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the explicit noun in place of a pronoun this way and mentions 81:27. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:49/q08 What conduct does معرضين describe?  [مُعْرِضِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Muqātil, Kashf al-Asrār and Q expressly distinguish denying the reminder from failing to follow it; other notes stress the refusal to listen or reflect.
- 74:49/q08/p1 They reject or deny the reminder and refuse belief. — Ṭabarī describes polytheists turning from God's Qur'anic reminder. Al-Wāḥidī's Basīṭ says their rejection is without belief; Ibn al-Jawzī similarly says Quraysh refuse belief. Muqātil, reported by Qurṭubī, makes denial one of two forms of turning away. Kashf al-Asrār, reported by Bursawī, and Q, reported by the Study Quran, also make denial one form. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار); STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 74:49/q08/p2 They refuse to listen, take heed, or reflect on the reminder. — Ṭabarī names refusal to listen, take admonition and reflect. Al-Zamakhsharī describes avoidance of Qur'an and admonition. Bursawī's Persian gloss says they flee listening because they lack a listening ear and awakened heart; his Mathnawī verses contrast them with the living message and its spiritual secret. Asad extends this to many who refuse to hear truth. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 74:49/q08/p3 They fail to act on or follow the reminder. — Muqātil, reported by Qurṭubī, identifies failure to act as the other form of turning away. Kashf al-Asrār, reported by Bursawī, says refusal to follow; Q, reported by the Study Quran, likewise says failing to act according to the Qur'an. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار); STUDYQURAN(Q)]

## 74:49/q09 How does the following wild-ass simile portray their response?  [* · links]
- 74:49/q09/p1 Their flight from the reminder resembles startled wild asses bolting in panic and exposes their foolish response. — Al-Zamakhsharī says the comparison censures foolishness and lack of sense, comparing the donkey carrying books in 62:5. Ibn al-Jawzī links their flight from the Qur'an to the donkeys in the next verse. Bursawī describes frantic flight from what frightens the animals and adds a Persian gloss about their failure to listen with an awakened heart, with Mathnawī verses on the living message and its spiritual secret. Kur'an Yolu says their response to the Prophet and his message is abnormally thoughtless, foolish, coarse and indecorous. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 74:49/q10 What consequence does turning away have for them?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the consequence is stated as having nothing in the afterlife or as failing to escape the fate already warned of.
- 74:49/q10/p1 Rejecting the Qur'an without belief leaves them with nothing for themselves in the afterlife. — Al-Wāḥidī's Basīṭ states this consequence; Ṭabrisī says rejection leaves nothing awaiting them in the afterlife; Ibn al-Jawzī says the same of Quraysh's refusal of belief. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 74:49/q10/p2 Turning away cannot help them escape the warned fate. — Elmalılı says evading the Qur'anic reminder gives deniers no escape from the fate it warns of. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 74:49/q11 What does the verse imply about responsibility for rejection?  [* · theology]
- 74:49/q11/p1 People are answerable for their own rejection and required to reflect on the signs. — Al-Jishumī calls turning away a major sin deserving threat and says blame attaches because rejection is their act. He argues that the question about what prevents belief contradicts claims that God creates their rejection or removes their ability to reflect; such alleged obstacles would conflict with the verse and God's commands. [3 notes: JISHUMI]

## 74:49/q12 What should the Prophet do about those who flee the reminder?  [* · interpretation]
- 74:49/q12/p1 He should ignore their flight and continue reminding; only those made receptive under God's laws will benefit. — Iṣlāḥī says the fleeing audience has an indicated underlying motive, and gives this instruction and condition for benefit. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:49/q13 What reward is reported for reciting al-Muddaththir?  [* · sciences]
- 74:49/q13/p1 A report promises ten good deeds for each person in Mecca who believed in Muhammad or disbelieved in him. — Al-Zamakhsharī attributes the report to the Prophet and states no grading. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله)]
