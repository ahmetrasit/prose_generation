# 23:106: 13 questions

## 23:106/q01 In what exchange do they make this reply?  [* · links]
- 23:106/q01/p1 They answer God's rebuke for denying His recited signs in worldly life, then plead to leave Hell. — Al-Ṭabarī identifies the rebuke as the rejected recited verses and cites 40:49. Al-Qurṭubī's report connects it to 23:105 and their expectation of mercy. Kur'an Yolu says denial of revelation is singled out because other evils followed it. Iṣlāḥī notes the order: divine response, confession, plea. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; THALABI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 23:106/q02 What does غَلَبَتْ عَلَيْنَا mean?  [غَلَبَتْ عَلَيْنَا · meaning]
- 23:106/q02/p1 Their wretchedness prevailed over, mastered, or took possession of them. — Al-Jishumī defines ghalaba as prevailing by force. Al-Kashshāf likens it to taking ownership of a thing; Rāzī reports his gloss as mastery by a settled character. The Study Quran reports possession, ownership, or dominion. Ibn ʿĀshūr says its literal sense is overpowering. [10 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R and Z); BURSEVI]

## 23:106/q03 How is غَلَبَتْ عَلَيْنَا constructed?  [غَلَبَتْ عَلَيْنَا · grammar]
- 23:106/q03/p1 The omitted object is happiness; misery prevailed over happiness, while على marks what was overcome. — Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies the object from شقوتنا and compares the use of على to the hadith phrase 'the men have prevailed over us.' [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:106/q04 What image does the wording غَلَبَتْ عَلَيْنَا convey?  [غَلَبَتْ عَلَيْنَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether mastery is represented as an inner contest or personified as a settled agent.
- 23:106/q04/p1 It pictures an inner contest in which misery arising from their own choices gained the upper hand. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explains that they freely chose causes of misery over causes of happiness. Al-Ālūsī prefers a representational metaphor for an affair originating in them, rather than treating misery as a settled agent. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 23:106/q04/p2 It pictures misery as a settled character that mastered them. — Rāzī reports al-Kashshāf's comparison with someone taking and owning something from another. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 23:106/q05 How is شِقْوَتُنَا read and formed?  [شِقْوَتُنَا · readings]
turns on: The vowel of shīn and the presence of alif after qāf vary; the sources regard the principal nouns as equivalent while differing in their reader attributions.
- 23:106/q05/p1 Shiqwatunā: kasra on shīn, still qāf, and no alif. — Al-Ṭabarī attributes it to most Medinans and Basrans and some Kufans; Ibn Mujāhid names Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, Ibn ʿĀmir, and ʿĀṣim. Later sources call it the majority or Hijazi form. Ibn Khālawayh compares its pattern to fidya; al-Fārisī to ridda and fiṭna; al-Samīn to fiṭna and niʿma. Al-Farrāʾ cites a line with shaqwatihi as evidence. The two principal forms are synonymous verbal nouns; al-Ṭabarī accepts either, and a report says ʿĀṣim allowed both. [27 notes: TAB-FULL(قراء المدينة والبصرة وبعض أهل الكوفة); ZAJJAJ; IBNMUJAHID(أبان عن عاصم,ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وعاصم); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(الباقون); THALABI(أهل الكوفة غير عاصم); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); TABRISI(أبو علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير وعاصم ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أهل المدينة وأبو عمرو وعاصم والكوفيون وابن مسعود والحسن); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله); SAMIN-DURR(الأخوان,الفراء); NASHR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); QIRAAT-ER(Lvw); TAB-FULL+]
- 23:106/q05/p2 Shaqāwatunā: fatḥa on shīn and qāf, with an alif after qāf. — Most Kufans or Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī are named; al-Nashr and Ibn ʿĀshūr add Khalaf. Reports also name Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan, al-Aʿmash, and others; transmissions from ʿĀṣim vary and permit both principal forms. It is a synonymous verbal noun, compared to saʿāda, salāma, or qasāwa; Qiraat-ER calls faʿāla common. Al-Farrāʾ cites Abū Tharwān's line for a hardship sense of this form. [31 notes: TAB-FULL(قراء أهل الكوفة); ZAJJAJ; IBNMUJAHID(أبان عن عاصم,ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وعاصم); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(حمزة والكسائي); THALABI(أهل الكوفة غير عاصم); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); TABRISI(أبو علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود وابن عباس وأبو عبد الرحمن السلمي والحسن والأعمش وحمزة والكسائي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أهل المدينة وأبو عمرو وعاصم والكوفيون وابن مسعود والحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء,عبد الله); SAMIN-DURR(الأخوان); NASHR; DURR-FULL(إسحق,الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله والحسن وقتادة وحمزة والكسائي والمفضل عن عاصم وأبان والزعفراني وابن مقسم); IBNASHUR-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وخلف); QIRAAT-ER(Lvw); TAB-FULL+]
- 23:106/q05/p3 Shaqwatunā: fatḥa on shīn with no alif and a still qāf. — Ibn al-Jawzī names ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ, Abū Razīn, and Abū Rajāʾ; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī report it from Shibl. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عمرو بن العاص وأبو رزين وأبو رجاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(شبل); SAMIN-DURR(شبل); ALUSI-FULL(شبل); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 23:106/q05/p4 Shiqāwatunā: kasra on shīn with an alif after qāf. — Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī report this variant from al-Ḥasan and Qatāda; al-Ālūsī specifies al-Ḥasan's transmission through Khālid ibn Ḥawshab. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وقتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله); SAMIN-DURR(قتادة والحسن); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة والحسن عن خالد بن حوشب)]
- 23:106/q05/p5 Related nouns include shaqāʾ and shaqā, with long or short final vowel. — Al-Qurṭubī records both usages; Abū Muslim compares shaqwa to the relation of jarya to flowing, so the noun can name a condition. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم)]

## 23:106/q06 What is the شِقْوَة that overcame them?  [شِقْوَتُنَا · interpretation]
turns on: The accounts identify different referents for misery: a prior decree, its final outcome, causes within desire and deeds, a moral attitude, or bad luck.
- 23:106/q06/p1 The wretched fate already decreed or written for them. — Mujāhid, Muqātil, al-Quraẓī and others identify it with what was written. Al-Ṭabarī specifies prior knowledge and the Mother of the Book; Study Quran notes 43:4. Ibn Masʿūd says wretchedness is written in the womb, and Yaḥyā recalls a Prophetic report on happiness and wretchedness written there. The Ashʿarites identify the written matter as their disbelief and sins. Al-Ṭabarī's sinners cite it after rejected recitation. [18 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن مجاهد,عبد الله بن مسعود); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(القرظي، مجاهد، مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(الأشاعرة); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); NASAFI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 23:106/q06/p2 Their desires and forbidden pleasures, which brought misery as their consequence. — Al-Jubbāʾī says they name the cause by its effect and admit responsibility. Al-Qurṭubī prefers this and cites 4:10, where consuming orphans' wealth is called eating fire. Al-Ṭabrisī calls neglecting God for idols and desire the gravest misery; al-Biqāʿī says desires led to evil deeds. Kur'an Yolu adds appetite, self-interest, and weakness rather than reasoned inquiry. [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الجبائي); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(القرطبي); ALUSI-FULL(الجبائي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 23:106/q06/p3 Their own evil deeds or acquired sinful disposition, which produced the bad end. — Al-Kashshāf says God knew their evil deeds deserved an evil outcome; al-Ṭabrisī says the deeds caused misery. Al-Nasafī calls their sinful deeds its cause, al-Būrsawī their bad choices, al-Ālūsī their bad disposition, and al-Ṣādiq their own deeds. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī stress voluntary choices; Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites their promise to do good if returned. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:106/q06/p4 Misery as the opposite of happiness: an evil outcome, harm, or lost allotted good. — Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Jishumī define it by eventual harm versus benefit; al-Ṭabrisī says harm overtakes well-being. Al-Kashshāf says the bad end was known by God as deserved by their deeds, a gloss Rāzī repeats; al-Ālūsī attributes this view to most Muʿtazilites. Ṭabāṭabāʾī also offers the evil that characterizes them or lost allotted good. Abū Ḥayyān calls an evil outcome its ordinary sense. [9 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 23:106/q06/p5 Thinking well of oneself and badly of other people. — Abū Turāb gives the inward moral gloss; al-Māwardī, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Būrsawī transmit the same explanation. The reports vary slightly between 'bad opinion' generally and bad opinion of others. [4 notes: SULAMI(أبو تراب); MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(أبو تراب)]
- 23:106/q06/p6 The state of misery itself. — Abū Muslim derives shaqwa from shaqāʾ by analogy with jarya and flowing: the noun can denote a condition. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم)]
- 23:106/q06/p7 Misfortune that overwhelmed them during worldly life, leaving them unguided. — Al-Kalbī specifies the time and result without specifying whether this misfortune was written or chosen. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي)]
- 23:106/q06/p8 Abstract bad luck, invoked as an excuse for their own failings. — Asad treats this contextual sense as the sinners' futile pretext. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 23:106/q07 Does their wording make divine decree the cause of their error?  [شِقْوَتُنَا · theology]
turns on: Whether a determining motive must be divinely created or God's writing follows foreknown voluntary choices.
- 23:106/q07/p1 Yes: God creates the determining motives toward good or evil, and the sinners invoke a written cause. — Rāzī argues that uncaused choice would permit preference without cause and weaken proof of a Creator; a further chooser produces regress. Humans lack knowledge of the quantity and manner of acts needed to originate them. Since people seek knowledge yet some become ignorant, God creates the motives. Treating deviation as identical with denial would be circular, so an antecedent motive is needed. He says the appeal acknowledges this cause without excusing them; God's order to be silent bars dispute over His acts. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الأشاعرة); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); NASAFI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 23:106/q07/p2 No: foreknowledge records their freely chosen acts, so their own disposition and deeds cause their error. — Al-Nasafī says God writes what He knows a person will choose, without compulsion. Al-Ālūsī says knowledge follows its object, including their bad disposition and choices. Al-Qāḍī argues that if they knew God willed their unbelief they would cite that as a stronger excuse; Rāzī counters that they already cite causation while admitting no excuse. Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes their promised good action if returned, and Asad reads the exchange as stressing free will. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL(الجبائي,القاضي); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL-]

## 23:106/q08 What do they mean by saying they were ضَآلِّينَ?  [ضَآلِّينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'astray' is glossed by loss of guidance and truth or by ignorance.
- 23:106/q08/p1 They had strayed from guidance, truth, and right conduct. — Muqātil and others gloss it as departure from guidance. Al-Nasafī says truth and right; al-Būrsawī says this explains denial and sins. Ibn Kathīr says proof had been established but misery kept them from submitting. Al-Biqāʿī pictures them steeped in causes of misery, captive to them and driven by devils. Abū Ḥayyān says the admission precedes their promise to abandon unbelief and idolatry. [13 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 23:106/q08/p2 They were ignorant. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr gives this gloss of 'astray.' [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير)]

## 23:106/q09 Why do they say قَوْمًا ضَآلِّينَ?  [قَوْمًۭا ضَآلِّينَ · rhetoric]
- 23:106/q09/p1 Calling themselves a people astray makes misguidance a defining collective trait. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares Qurʾānic descriptions of peoples at 2:164 and 10:101. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:106/q10 Is their statement a confession, excuse, or plea?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The sources differ over whether naming misery functions as admission, pretext, or a factual causal statement.
- 23:106/q10/p1 It confesses culpability and the established proof against them, then leads into their plea for release. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls confession an apology and disavowal before the decreed command to be silent. Al-Jubbāʾī says naming their culpable desires by the resulting misery is no excuse. Al-Qurṭubī cites their promise not to return to sin. Ibn Kathīr says the proof had been established. Al-Ālūsī says they acknowledge no injustice in punishment; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says their own bad choices complete the proof. Kur'an Yolu says a plea offered only now goes unanswered. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الجبائي); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 23:106/q10/p2 It shifts blame to their misery as a futile excuse, despite responsibility for their failings. — Asad calls 'bad luck' the excuse exposed by the allegorical dialogue. Al-Nasafī describes the sinful deeds as an excuse for neglect without absolution. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says they imagine neutral selves overcome by misery, though they acquired it; their speech expresses a settled disposition. [6 notes: ASAD-NOTES; NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 23:106/q10/p3 On the decreed-fate interpretation, it is a statement of fact rather than a plea. — The Study Quran reports this account from Q; Rāzī says citing the causal account still does not excuse them, as God's command to silence them shows. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(Q); RAZI-FULL]

## 23:106/q11 What sequence of appeals is reported around this confession?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in the number and duration of the appeals and in the order or detail of petitions.
- 23:106/q11/p1 They petition Hell's keepers, Mālik, and God in succession, receiving delayed refusals before God silences them. — Ibn Jurayj cites 40:49, 43:77, and 23:107. The Prophetic report through Abū al-Dardāʾ adds requests for food, drink, and relief, followed by choking fare and scalding water. Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb says they then try patience, seek death or return, repent, cite written misfortune, and are silenced, linking 43:77, 14:21–22, 40:11–12, 32:12–14, 14:44, 35:37, 23:106, and 77:35. A further report says Paradise's people deny Hell's people water and provision, citing 7:50. Al-Qurṭubī notes Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb's fuller account in al-Tadhkira. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو بكر بن عبد الله,ابن جريج,محمد بن كعب القرظي); DURR-FULL(أبو الدرداء عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي)]
- 23:106/q11/p2 They make six appeals, each lasting a thousand years, each answered by refusal or rebuke. — Ibn ʿAbbās's cited sequence includes 32:12, 40:11–12, 43:77, 14:44, 35:37, and 23:99,108. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 23:106/q11/p3 They call Mālik for forty years; after he replies, God leaves their later prayer unanswered for twice the world's span. — Ibn ʿAmr reports Mālik's answer that they will remain there; after the long silence God orders them driven away. Al-Qurṭubī notes al-Tirmidhī's similar Prophetic report from Abū al-Dardāʾ. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الدرداء,عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص)]

## 23:106/q12 What happens after they are told to be silent?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports give differing descriptions of their sounds and breathing after speech ends.
- 23:106/q12/p1 They cease intelligible speech and supplication; only gasping, wailing, or howling remains. — Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb says hope ends and only howling remains, with Hell shut over them. Al-Azhar connects the silence with 77:35, the Day without speech or permission to make excuses. Abū Bakr ibn ʿAbd Allāh swears this wordless gasping and wailing never ends. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو بكر بن عبد الله,الأزهر بن أبي الأزهر,محمد بن كعب القرظي); QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي)]
- 23:106/q12/p2 Their sounds resemble a donkey's bray: an exhalation followed by an inhalation. — Ibn ʿAmr's account gives the sequence after unanswered prayer and God's command; Qatāda gives the same comparison. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص,قتادة)]
- 23:106/q12/p3 They bark like dogs. — Ibn ʿAbbās supplies the comparison; Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb's report also says they bark at one another. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,محمد بن كعب القرظي)]
- 23:106/q12/p4 The people of Hell do not breathe. — Zayd ibn Aslam tells Abū Jaʿfar al-Qāriʾ this in the context of the aftermath. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(زيد بن أسلم)]

## 23:106/q13 How does this exchange relate to the sūra's wider themes?  [* · links]
- 23:106/q13/p1 The final warning reverses the worldly fortunes of believers and deniers: the condemned wish to return and do good after it is too late. — The Study Quran notes the recurring opposition of affluent and powerful people to prophets, citing 7:59–62 and 7:136; its introduction says the conclusion portrays a futile wish to return. Kur'an Yolu contrasts the worldly prayers and preparation of sincere Muslims with the disbelievers' mockery and the believers' happy outcome. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
