# 96:1/q14 Why is God called 'your Lord' here? [رَبِّكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether it foregrounds nurturing, reassurance, unique allegiance, or active lordship.
- 96:1/q14/p1 The title recalls God's nurture and care for Muhammad and reassures him amid fear.
  reasons: Rāzī argues the frightened Prophet is reassured that the one who began caring for him will complete that care; creation from nonexistence is itself nurturing. Abū Ḥayyān stresses protection and welfare. BIQAI: Rather than the all-encompassing Greatest Name, the verse uses the nurturing Lord's attribute, gathering the other attributes and comforting the Prophet as the first revelation came while he loved solitude in worship at Ḥirāʾ. BIQAI-FULL: The Lord's nurturing attribute, rather than the encompassing divine name, comforts the Prophet and recalls the favors recalled in al-Sharḥ; the command came when he loved retreat for worship in Ḥirāʾ.; cites 94:1-8 BIQAI-FULL: Both names point to the command: the Lord who nurtures deserves thanks, while reading signals the command to worship and implies salvation at the final judgment. ALUSI: Calling God the Prophet's Lord evokes nurturing and gradual development toward fitting perfection; the possessive pronoun signals that revelation brings him to the highest human completion. ALUSI: The description recalls God's first favor to the Prophet and signals God's power to teach him to read in the gentlest way. ALUSI-FULL: Who created recalls God's original favor and ability to teach reading; it may mean the one who creates or the one who created everything, and it rules out idols as true lords because Arabs did not ascribe creation to them. ALUSI-FULL: Calling God Lord and adding the possessive for Muhammad recalls divine nurturing and his gradual attainment of human perfection through recurring revelation. IBNASHUR: Calling God “your Lord” rather than using the proper name Allah evokes care for the one nurtured and, with the possessive pronoun, rejects rivals claimed as lords; the verse grounds Islamic monotheism. BINTSHATI (الفخر الرازي): Al-Razi says Rabbika comforts the Prophet: God has nurtured him from an embryo into a knowing believer, so he should neither fear nor expect abandonment; the command also obliges him to act. BINTSHATI (أبو حيان): Abu Hayyan says Rabbuka comforts Muhammad and identifies his exclusive Lord; the Creator attribute follows because Arabs called idols lords, though idols cannot create.
  holders (17 notes): RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI(أبو حيان، الفخر الرازي)

# 96:1/q15 Who or what is denoted by 'the one who created'? [ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ · referent]
turns on: Whether creation is unrestricted, refers to humanity, or denotes ownership of creation.
- 96:1/q15/p1 God, Creator of all creatures and things.
  reasons: Al-Kalbī, reported by al-Wāḥidī and al-Baghawī, says all creatures; al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī say the unqualified wording includes everything. ALUSI: Khalaqa can be intransitive, meaning the one who creates, or have a general omitted object, meaning the one who created everything; either way its force is universal. ALUSI-FULL: Who created recalls God's original favor and ability to teach reading; it may mean the one who creates or the one who created everything, and it rules out idols as true lords because Arabs did not ascribe creation to them. STUDYQURAN: The first mention of creation may mean that God creates all existence from nothing.
