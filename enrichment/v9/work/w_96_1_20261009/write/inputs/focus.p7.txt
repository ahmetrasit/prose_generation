# 96:1/q18 Why identify the Lord as Creator? [ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the description rules out idols, recalls divine favor, proves teaching, or serves cadence.
- 96:1/q18/p1 It distinguishes God from idols called lords, since they create nothing.
  reasons: Al-Māwardī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, and Abū Ḥayyān note that Arabs called idols lords but conceded none of them created; the clause therefore removes ambiguity. BURSEVI (فتح الرحمن): Fath al-Rahman says the Creator attribute answers Arabs who called idols lords, since idols cannot share in creating. ALUSI (قيل): Another explanation says the creator description confirms that the intended Lord is God, since Arabs called idols lords but did not attribute creation to them. ALUSI-FULL: Who created recalls God's original favor and ability to teach reading; it may mean the one who creates or the one who created everything, and it rules out idols as true lords because Arabs did not ascribe creation to them. IBNASHUR: The relative clause signals why God's name should be invoked: He is Creator, whereas idols are not; polytheists themselves acknowledged that God created the heavens and earth, so creation is the clearest proof of uniqueness.; cites 31:25 IBNASHUR-FULL: Calling God 'the Creator' implies exclusive divinity: because He alone created, people should invoke Him rather than al-Lat or al-Uzza, though the polytheists acknowledged His creation of heaven and earth.; cites 31:25 BINTSHATI: The command is to recite in the name of the Creator, the rightful Lord rather than the idols the Arabs had fashioned and then worshiped. BINTSHATI: She centers Rabbika on the Prophet’s long search for guidance: the Creator revealed Himself as the Lord worthy of worship over created Arabian idols. BINTSHATI (بعض المفسرين): Al-Razi transmits an argument that naming God as Creator leads idolaters gradually to acknowledge Him: they know they did not create themselves and that their handmade idols did not create them. BINTSHATI: The context plainly asserts the Creator’s lordship; humans are singled out because they read, learn, bear obligations, and receive the revealed words. BINTSHATI (أبو حيان): Abu Hayyan says Rabbuka comforts Muhammad and identifies his exclusive Lord; the Creator attribute follows because Arabs called idols lords, though idols cannot create.

ALUSI: The description recalls God's first favor to the Prophet and signals God's power to teach him to read in the gentlest way. KURANYOLU-TEFSIR: Creation is stressed because God made both human capacity for reading and the objects studied; denying God or gratitude while using these gifts is profound ingratitude.
