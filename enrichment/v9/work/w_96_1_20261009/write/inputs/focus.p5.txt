# 96:1/q10 Is the basmala itself part of the earliest revelation or of each sūra? [بِٱسْمِ رَبِّكَ · sciences]
turns on: Whether reports omit it, place it first, or allow a later descent.
- 96:1/q10/p1 The basmala was the first revelation, followed by Iqraʾ.
  reasons: ALUSI (عكرمة والحسن): A report from ʿIkrima and al-Ḥasan says the first Qurʾānic revelation was bismi Allāh al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm, followed by the opening of Iqraʾ.; cites 1:1, 96:1 ALUSI (ابن عباس): Al-Ḍaḥḥāk's report from Ibn ʿAbbās says Gabriel first instructed the Prophet to seek refuge, then to say the basmala. ALUSI-FULL (عكرمة، الحسن): Ikrima and Hasan say the basmala was the first Quranic revelation; al-Dahhak's report from Ibn Abbas says Gabriel first instructed Muhammad to seek refuge and say it. Al-Suyuti counts this as the first revelation, but al-Alusi objects that this does not follow.
  holders (3 notes): ALUSI(ابن عباس، عكرمة والحسن); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة، الحسن)
  notes: ALUSI:96:1/r28 ALUSI:96:1/r29 ALUSI-FULL:v30p178#2/r5
- 96:1/q10/p2 It may be Qurʾān but descended later than the opening passage.
  reasons: ALUSI (المثبتون): Those who affirm the basmala as Qurʾān answer that it did not descend first but at another time, as other portions of the sūra also did. ALUSI-FULL (النووي): Some argue that the basmala is not Quran at the beginnings of surahs because it is absent from sound accounts of the first revelation; al-Nawawi reports this argument, while proponents answer that it descended later.

# 96:1/q11 What is the origin and form of ism? [بِٱسْمِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether ism comes from elevation or marking, and what its forms show.
- 96:1/q11/p1 Ism derives from sumū, elevation; its initial alif is hamzat waṣl.
  reasons: Al-Zajjāj derives ism from sumū, elevation, with original sumw and plural asmāʾ; samī and the Arab forms ism/sim, a rajaz line with simah, and comparable plurals support hamzat waṣl. He notes bi's kasra marks its governing genitive.

  reasons: Al-Zajjāj rejects derivation from wasama: the dropped initial wāw and expected diminutive wasīm conflict with actual forms.

# 96:1/q12 Why is the alif of ism written or omitted? [بِٱسْمِ · grammar]
- 96:1/q12/p1 Frequent use and spoken elision omit the alif in bism Allāh, while bi-smi rabbika retains it.
  reasons: Al-Zajjāj says bism Allāh drops the alif because spoken elision and repeated use made it familiar, but bi-smi rabbika retains it. BURHAN (علماء الظاهر): The outward scholars explain the omission as abbreviation from frequent use: the alif of ism is dropped only when appended to Allah and preceded by ba according to the majority.
  holders (2 notes): ZAJJAJ; BURHAN(علماء الظاهر)
  notes: ZAJJAJ:v1p39/r9 BURHAN:v1p390/r3
sources: BURHAN = al-Zarkashī, d. 794; ZAJJAJ = al-Zajjāj, d. 311

# 96:1/q13 What does invoking the divine name do? [بِٱسْمِ رَبِّكَ · interpretation]
turns on: The name's relation to its bearer and the invoker.
- 96:1/q13/p1 Honoring God's name honors God and His attributes, praising and glorifying Him.
  reasons: Al-Zajjāj says a name points to its meaning; Jishumī says honoring a name honors its uniquely signified bearer, and al-Ṭabrisī links invocation with God's beautiful names (17:110; 87:1). BIQAI: The commentators say this indicates that recitation is not complete without invoking God's name; honoring the name honors its bearer and all that is attributed to Him. BIQAI-FULL (قالوا): They say recitation is not complete without naming God; naming Him honors both the name and its bearer and all His attributes.
