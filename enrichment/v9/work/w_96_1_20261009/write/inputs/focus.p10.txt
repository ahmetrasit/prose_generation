- 96:1/q20/p2 Al-Fātiḥa was first.
  reasons: Abū Maysara's report is cited by Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī; al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī attribute this to a majority. Jishumī rejects it as contrary to consensus; Abū Ḥayyān judges the report unsound. ALUSI (صاحب شرح صحيح مسلم): The commentary on Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim says Iqraʾ was first absolutely and al-Muddaththir first after the pause; it calls the Fātiḥa-first view clearly invalid. ALUSI (أبو ميسرة عمرو بن شرحبيل): A mursal report with trustworthy narrators, transmitted by al-Bayhaqī and al-Wāḥidī, supports the view that al-Fātiḥa came first. BINTSHATI (الزمخشري والفخر الرازي): An alternative view in al-Kashshaf and al-Razi’s commentary makes the Fatiha the first revealed surah, followed by al-Alaq. ISLAHI-TADABBUR (Zamakhsharī): Zamakhsharī records the view that al-Fātiḥah was revealed first and then Sūrah al-Qalam; exegetes both accept and dispute it. Against—ALUSI: The Fātiḥa-first report could describe what was revealed after Iqraʾ and al-Muddaththir, and the reports supporting the other order are stronger. Against—ALUSI-FULL (أبو ميسرة عمرو بن شرحبيل): A trustworthy-transmitters but mursal report argues that al-Fatiha came first; the response is that it could refer to what followed Iqra and Muddaththir, and other reports are stronger. Against—ALUSI-FULL (الكشف): Al-Kashf takes Jabir's claim to concern the first complete surah, while Sahih Muslim's commentary distinguishes Iqra as first overall from Muddaththir as first after the hiatus; the claim that al-Fatiha came first is plainly false. Against—QUTB-ZILAL: Qutb says the sura's opening was the first revelation by consensus; accounts assigning that primacy elsewhere are unreliable. Against—BINTSHATI (أبو حيان): Al-Bahr al-Muhit says the surah is Meccan and its opening was the first revelation at Hira, as established in Bukhari and elsewhere; it judges the claims that al-Muddaththir or the Fatiha came first unsound.

- 96:1/q20/p3 Al-Muddaththir was first.
  reasons: Jābir's report is cited by Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī; al-Wāḥidī answers that Jābir knew the later encounter after the pause. ALUSI (جابر بن عبد الله): Relating the pause in revelation, Jābir says the angel appeared seated between heaven and earth; the Prophet returned frightened, and God then revealed al-Muddaththir. ALUSI (صاحب الكشف): Al-Kashf reconciles Jābir's report by taking his claim to mean the first complete sūra, while al-Muddaththir was first after the interval. ALUSI (جابر بن عبد الله): In a report in both Ṣaḥīḥ collections, Jābir ibn ʿAbd Allāh answers that Yā ayyuhā al-muddaththir was first; he responds to those who cite Iqraʾ by narrating the Prophet's account. ALUSI-FULL (جابر بن عبد الله): A report from Jabir calls Ya ayyuhal-Muddaththir the first revelation, but the earlier group offers several responses to it. ALUSI-FULL (جابر بن عبد الله): Jabir's account of Muddaththir explicitly concerns the period after revelation paused and then resumed, undermining its use as the first revelation overall. ALUSI-FULL (الكشف): Al-Kashf takes Jabir's claim to concern the first complete surah, while Sahih Muslim's commentary distinguishes Iqra as first overall from Muddaththir as first after the hiatus; the claim that al-Fatiha came first is plainly false. IBNASHUR (جابر): Jabir reports that al-Muddaththir was the first surah revealed. IBNASHUR: Ibn Ashur reconciles Jabir's report by taking al-Muddaththir as the first surah after the interruption in revelation, just as al-Duha followed a second interruption, as al-Itqan explains. IBNASHUR-FULL (جابر): Jabir says al-Muddaththir was first; Ibn Ashur reconciles this as the first revelation after the pause, like al-Duha after the second pause. ISLAHI-TADABBUR (the proponents of this view): A third view says Sūrah al-Muddaththir was first; its proponents probably mean only its opening verses because the rest has a severity resembling al-‘Alaq. Against—ITQAN (الكرماني): Al-Kirmani argues that Jabir inferred his view rather than narrating it, so Aisha's transmitted report should take precedence. Against—QUTB-ZILAL: Qutb says the sura's opening was the first revelation by consensus; accounts assigning that primacy elsewhere are unreliable. Against—BINTSHATI (أبو حيان): Al-Bahr al-Muhit says the surah is Meccan and its opening was the first revelation at Hira, as established in Bukhari and elsewhere; it judges the claims that al-Muddaththir or the Fatiha came first unsound. Al-Ālūsī reports Jābir ibn ʿAbd Allāh's sound narration: after retreat at Ḥirāʾ and the angel's sudden appearance, Muhammad returned frightened and al-Muddaththir was revealed, which Jābir takes to imply its priority over al-ʿAlaq.
