- 96:1/q02/p3 The phrase 'in the name of your Lord who created' itself.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the possibility that the words to be read are this opening phrase itself; Abū Ḥayyān reports a comparable reading as the grammatical object. ALUSI: The command does not impose an impossible task: the Prophet knows that whatever is revealed as Qurʾān is what he is commanded to recite, and the command can include its own wording. ALUSI-FULL: The command is not impossible even if immediate: the Prophet knows that whatever is revealed as Quran is what he must read, so the command itself can be part of the material to read. ALUSI-FULL (الآمدي): Al-Amidi cites the repeated command Read followed by this passage as an example of clarification delayed until after the original command; al-Alusi accepts the passage as the clarification but rejects making the whole passage or basmala the object of the earlier command.

# 96:1/q03 How could the unlettered Prophet obey the command to read? [ٱقْرَأْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the command prepares him for dictation, miraculously enables recitation, or concerns visible writing.
- 96:1/q03/p1 Gabriel's repeated command prepares him to receive and recite the words about to be dictated.
  reasons: Qushayrī reports Muhammad twice saying he was not a reader; Jishumī reports his asking what he should read before Gabriel supplied the words. ELMALILI: At Hira the angel first commanded only “read” twice; because Muhammad had not read before, he answered that he was not a reader, so these were preparatory prompts to begin recitation. ELMALILI: The third command began the Qur'an and simultaneously miraculously made the unreading Prophet able to read, fixed the text to be recited, and taught him to introduce the Creator, as related to 29:48 and 42:52.; cites 29:48, 42:52 IBNASHUR: The imperative is literal, asking for reading now or in the near future; the Prophet is to say what will shortly be dictated, since no text had yet been dictated or given to him. IBNASHUR: The command resembles a teacher telling a student to write before dictating: it prepares the student for the text that is about to be supplied. IBNASHUR (عائشة): Aisha's report in the two Sahih collections describes Gabriel pressing the Prophet three times; each time he answered that he could not read, and at the third command Gabriel recited the opening verses through “what he did not know.” IBNASHUR: Aisha's wording that Muhammad returned bearing the revelation and Khadija asked Waraqa to hear him shows he understood and recited the verses after the third command; Gabriel's repetitions familiarized him with an unlearned act. IBNASHUR-FULL: The imperative asks for immediate or near-future recitation of words about to be dictated, since no text had previously been read or handed to him. IBNASHUR-FULL (عائشة): Aisha narrates in the two Sahihs that Gabriel came to Muhammad in Hira, commanded him three times, embraced him forcefully each time, then recited the opening through 'what he did not know.' IBNASHUR-FULL: Aisha's narration is directly from the Prophet; her account that he returned trembling with the passage means he retained and recited what had been dictated. IBNASHUR-FULL: Khadija's request that Waraqa hear him further indicates Muhammad recited the revealed passage immediately; Gabriel's repeated command repeated God's wording to accustom him to reading.

- 96:1/q03/p2 God enabled the unlettered Prophet to retain, recite, and teach the revelation without prior reading or writing.
  reasons: ELMALILI: A book need not already be written to be a book, and reading does not require writing; the fullest recitation is from memory. ELMALILI: The Prophet's own recitation was the fullest kind, from memory by divine instruction, whether for himself, prayer, teaching others, or dictation. ELMALILI: The command miraculously gave the unlettered Prophet a book to recite, teach, and dictate without writing himself; beginning with the basmala accords with God's lordship. ELMALILI: The attachment to “your Lord” explains the command through God's role as creator and caretaker, leaving no doubt that He can teach an unlettered recipient. ELMALILI: The Prophet needed no writing to recite, while his unlettered reading establishes prophethood and the Qur'an's divine origin; 29:48 states this explicitly.
