# 96:1/q41 What biblical or earlier-scripture parallels are proposed? [* · links]
turns on: Whether the Arabic command evokes Hebrew naming or Isaiah's call.
- 96:1/q41/p1 The phrase may echo calling on the divine name in Hebrew scripture.
  reasons: CORPUSCORANICUM (Hirschfeld 1902): Hirschfeld recognizes a correspondence between iqraʾ bi-smi rabbika and the Hebrew formula qārāʾ be-šem YHWH, then proposes proclaim the name of your Lord. CORPUSCORANICUM: Despite rejecting Hirschfeld's translation, the author allows that the phrase deliberately imitates a biblical expression, as 112:1 may echo Deuteronomy 6:4.; cites 112:1
  holders (2 notes): CORPUSCORANICUM(Hirschfeld 1902)
  notes: CORPUSCORANICUM:96:1-2:anmerkung/r2 CORPUSCORANICUM:96:1-2:anmerkung/r4
- 96:1/q41/p2 The command may cast the preacher as a second Isaiah.
  reasons: CORPUSCORANICUM: Isaiah 40:5 also addresses the prophet with qrā, corresponding to iqraʾ, so the Quranic preacher may be implicitly cast as a second Isaiah.
  holders (1 notes): CORPUSCORANICUM
  notes: CORPUSCORANICUM:96:1-2:anmerkung/r5
- 96:1/q41/p3 Earlier scriptures are said to predict speaking in God's name and accountability for rejection.
  reasons: ISLAHI-TADABBUR: Earlier scriptures predict that Muhammad would speak in God's name and that God would hold accountable those rejecting his message.
  holders (1 notes): ISLAHI-TADABBUR
  notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p385/r4
sources: CORPUSCORANICUM = Nicolai Sinai (Frühmekkanische Suren), with Nora K. Schmid, using preparatory work by Angelika Neuwirth; later parts Dirk Hartwig, Angelika Neuwirth et al. (author line per sura in segments); ISLAHI-TADABBUR = Amin Ahsan Islahi (d. 1997)

# 96:1/q42 What is distinctive about this sūra's opening form? [* · rhetoric]
- 96:1/q42/p1 It is one of the Qurʾānic sūras opening with an imperative.
  reasons: Al-Burhān lists six sūras opened by commands, including Iqraʾ. ITQAN (أبو شامة): Abu Shama counts six sūras that open with a command, including those beginning qul, iqraʾ, and sabbiḥ. CORPUSCORANICUM: The opening imperative follows hymn openings in Q 87, 73, and 74, and likely addresses the preacher; such commands also apply by example to the wider community of hearers.; cites 87:1, 73:1, 73:2, 73:3, 73:4, 74:1, 74:2, 74:3, 74:4, 74:5, 74:6, 81:1, 93:1, 94:1, 108:1
  holders (3 notes): BURHAN; ITQAN(أبو شامة); CORPUSCORANICUM
  notes: BURHAN:v1p180/r1 ITQAN:v3p283/r2 CORPUSCORANICUM:96:1-2:anmerkung/r7
- 96:1/q42/p2 Its opening previews the Qurʾān's recitation, divine name, teaching, and principles.
  reasons: ITQAN: The opening of “Read” similarly previews the Qur’an: it commands reading, begins in God’s name, signals legal knowledge and the principles of religion, and reports God’s teaching of what humans did not know; it can therefore be called the Qur’an’s title. IBNASHUR: Opening with “Read” foretells that the Messenger will recite a book despite having recited no book before the Qur'an, as 29:48 states; he told Gabriel, “I am not a reader.”; cites 29:48 IBNASHUR: This opening is a fine instance of barāʿat al-istihlāl, an apt opening that anticipates the Qur'an's significance. IBNASHUR-FULL: Opening with 'Recite' announces that the Messenger will become a reciter of scripture although he had not recited scripture before revelation; it is a fitting opening for the Qur'an.; cites 29:48
  holders (4 notes): ITQAN; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: ITQAN:v3p284/r3 IBNASHUR:96:1/r18 IBNASHUR:96:1/r19 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p434#2/r2
sources: BURHAN = al-Zarkashī, d. 794; CORPUSCORANICUM = Nicolai Sinai (Frühmekkanische Suren), with Nora K. Schmid, using preparatory work by Angelika Neuwirth; later parts Dirk Hartwig, Angelika Neuwirth et al. # 96:1/q43 What is reported of Waraqa after his death? [* · reports]
- 96:1/q43/p1 The Prophet saw Waraqa in Paradise wearing silk because he believed.
  reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Ṭabrisī report the Prophet seeing Waraqa in Paradise clothed in silk because he believed.
  holders (2 notes): THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI(رسول الله)
  notes: THALABI:v10p244/r5 TABRISI:96:2#2/r6
- 96:1/q43/p2 Waraqa's poem affirms Muhammad's mission and angelic revelation.
  reasons: Waraqa's poem in al-Thaʿlabī and al-Ṭabrisī names Aḥmad, Gabriel, and Michael and contrasts the saved and doomed.
  holders (2 notes): THALABI(ورقة بن نوفل); TABRISI(ورقة بن نوفل)

# 96:1/q44 What report connects this passage to ʿUmar's conversion? [* · reports]
- 96:1/q44/p1 ʿUmar heard his sister recite al-ʿAlaq, then accepted Islam.
  reasons: DURR (ثوبان): Thawbān reports the Prophet’s prayer for God to strengthen Islam through ʿUmar; after hearing his sister recite al-ʿAlaq, ʿUmar sought the message and embraced Islam. DURR-FULL (عمر بن الخطاب): Thawban's report says Umar heard his sister reciting the opening passage, came to Muhammad, and accepted Islam after the Prophet invited him to testify to God's oneness and Muhammad's messengership.
  holders (2 notes): DURR(ثوبان); DURR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب)
  notes: DURR:96:1/r21 DURR-FULL:v8p563/r2
sources: DURR = al-Suyūṭī, d. 911; DURR-FULL = al-Suyūṭī, d. 911
