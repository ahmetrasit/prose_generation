# 96:1/q01 What does iqraʾ mean? [ٱقْرَأْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether it denotes voicing text, attentive reading, public proclamation, or gathering words.
- 96:1/q01/p1 Read or recite specified words, from memory or writing; ordinary Arabic recitation.
  reasons: Rāzī cites Qurʾānic use of unqualified recitation for the Qurʾān (75:18; 17:106); his full account also invokes 73:20. ELMALILI: Recitation is audibly voicing connected words in sequence, whether from memory or a page and aloud or quietly; silent study and recollection are figurative senses. ELMALILI: A book need not already be written to be a book, and reading does not require writing; the fullest recitation is from memory. ELMALILI: A prophetic report says reading from a page is rewarded because it supports accurate retention and memorization. ELMALILI: The Prophet's own recitation was the fullest kind, from memory by divine instruction, whether for himself, prayer, teaching others, or dictation. IBNASHUR: Qara'a means to pronounce specified speech, whether it is written or memorized by heart. IBNASHUR-FULL: Iqra literally commands reading aloud of specified written or memorized words; the grammatical discussion parallels the command in al-Nahl.; cites 16:98 TABATABAI: Qaraʾa can mean mentally combining written words, uttering them aloud, or reading them to another person; in the last sense it is also tilāwa.; cites 98:2 BINTSHATI (الطبري): Al-Tabari did not allegorize the command because its ordinary Arabic meaning, reciting from a written or unwritten text, was clear; Arabs knew rabb as owner and object of worship. CORPUSCORANICUM (Nöldeke and Schwally): Nöldeke and Schwally object that qaraʾa elsewhere in the Quran means recite from a text or memory, supporting the usual translation recite in the name of your Lord.; cites 16:98, 17:93, 69:19, 73:20, 87:6 ISLAHI-TADABBUR: Iqraʾ can be a teacher's instruction to read, but it can also direct someone to read or recite aloud to others. ISLAHI-TADABBUR: Qur'an 7:204 and 17:45 show that iqraʾ can mean reciting the Qur'an aloud to an audience; the usage is indicated by these verses.; cites 7:204, 17:45

- 96:1/q01/p2 Its root sense is gathering letters or words in sequence.
  reasons: Jishumī links qirāʾa with gathering, comparing water collected in a basin. TABATABAI (الراغب): Raghib defines recitation as joining letters and words in ordered reading, not merely gathering people or pronouncing a single letter. CORPUSCORANICUM: Qurʾān derives from qaraʾa, and the divine title rabb is treated in the note to 95:8.; cites 75:16, 75:17, 75:18, 75:19, 95:8

- 96:1/q01/p3 Recite the revelation aloud to an audience.
  reasons: CORPUSCORANICUM (Hirschfeld 1902): Hirschfeld recognizes a correspondence between iqraʾ bi-smi rabbika and the Hebrew formula qārāʾ be-šem YHWH, then proposes proclaim the name of your Lord. # 96:1/q02 What is Muhammad commanded to read? [ٱقْرَأْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the omitted object is the Qurʾān, God's name, the phrase itself, or all signs for study.
- 96:1/q02/p1 The Qurʾān being revealed to him.
  reasons: Al-Ṭabarī explains it as reciting in remembrance of the Creator; Rāzī cites 75:18 and 17:106, while al-Qurṭubī supplies the omitted object as what has been revealed. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn likewise say the object is omitted; al-Biqāʿī says the Qurʾān comprehends every good. BIQAI: The verb is placed before its prepositional phrase for greater impact, especially as this was the first revelation and reading was the urgent command; its object is omitted to indicate that the reading commanded is the Qurʾān, comprehensive of all good. BIQAI-FULL: Omitting the object of “read” signals that the reading is the comprehensive Qur'an, containing every good; God alone enables the Prophet to read it. BIQAI-FULL: The Qur'an is the healing, clarifying guide to humanity's beginning and end, so the command is to read it reflectively; its description as guidance and mercy reinforces this role.; cites 25:1
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p480/r4] BIQAI-FULL, d. 885 · author | holds | Humanity was created in the best form and then reduced to the lowest, producing opposing outcomes by God's prior wisdom and will; He could have guided every soul if He willed. | mentions 95:5, 76:30 ALUSI: The object of iqraʾ is omitted but understood from context as the Qurʾān being revealed; the verb is not intransitive. ALUSI: The command does not impose an impossible task: the Prophet knows that whatever is revealed as Qurʾān is what he is commanded to recite, and the command can include its own wording. ALUSI-FULL: Read has an implied object, what is revealed of the Quran; the verb is not intransitive and باسم ربك is not its object with an extra ba, contrary to Abu Ubayda. ALUSI-FULL: The command is not impossible even if immediate: the Prophet knows that whatever is revealed as Quran is what he must read, so the command itself can be part of the material to read. ALUSI-FULL (الآمدي): Al-Amidi cites the repeated command Read followed by this passage as an example of clarification delayed until after the original command; al-Alusi accepts the passage as the clarification but rejects making the whole passage or basmala the object of the earlier command. ELMALILI: The Prophet's own recitation was the fullest kind, from memory by divine instruction, whether for himself, prayer, teaching others, or dictation. ELMALILI: The command miraculously gave the unlettered Prophet a book to recite, teach, and dictate without writing himself; beginning with the basmala accords with God's lordship. IBNASHUR: The object of “read” is omitted either because the verb is used intransitively to mean begin reading, or because context makes clear that the object is what will be revealed of the Qur'an. IBNASHUR-FULL: The imperative asks for immediate or near-future recitation of words about to be dictated, since no text had previously been read or handed to him. TABATABAI: The apparent sense of iqraʾ is to receive and read what the angel reveals; the command is embedded within the revealed book, like an instruction at a book’s opening. TABATABAI: The wording supports this being the first revelation and implies an omitted object, ‘the Qur’an’ or its equivalent. TABATABAI: The command does not simply use read intransitively with its object omitted, nor does it mean ‘recite to people’ with an omitted complement.; cites 17:106 BINTSHATI: The opening command to recite is striking because it begins revelation to an unlettered Prophet among an unlettered, pagan Bedouin people, with the Book as his miracle. BINTSHATI (الطبري): Al-Tabari did not allegorize the command because its ordinary Arabic meaning, reciting from a written or unwritten text, was clear; Arabs knew rabb as owner and object of worship. BINTSHATI: She prefers the straightforward context: the command to recite is reiterated, and revelation specifies what the Prophet should read in his Lord’s name. ISLAHI-TADABBUR: Qur'an 7:204 and 17:45 show that iqraʾ can mean reciting the Qur'an aloud to an audience; the usage is indicated by these verses.; cites 7:204, 17:45 ISLAHI-TADABBUR: Reciting in the Creator's name presents the Qur'an as God's binding directive, not a preacher's words or a request; people must obey and not mock or trivialize it. ISLAHI-TADABBUR: The Qur'an is uniquely composed entirely of words revealed by God, so its opponents should recognize its exaltedness and fear its wrath.
