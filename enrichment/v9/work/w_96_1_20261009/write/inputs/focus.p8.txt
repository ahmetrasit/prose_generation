# 96:1/q19 Why mention creation generally before creating humanity? [خَلَقَ · rhetoric]
- 96:1/q19/p1 General creation comes first, then humanity is singled out for its remarkable origin and responsibility.
  reasons: Al-Burhān says the general creative act is followed by the specific creation of a human from a clot. BURHAN: The command first states that God created generally, then singles out the human being's creation from a clot. BINTSHATI: The first verse states creation generally, while the next specifies the human, recipient of revelation and the one uniquely able to read, learn, and bear religious responsibility. BINTSHATI: The context plainly asserts the Creator’s lordship; humans are singled out because they read, learn, bear obligations, and receive the revealed words.
