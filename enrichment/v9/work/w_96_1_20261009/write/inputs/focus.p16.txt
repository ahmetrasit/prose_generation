# 96:1/q26 Which sūras followed the first revelation? [* · sciences]
turns on: The reported sequences differ.
- 96:1/q26/p1 Al-Qalam or Nūn, then al-Muddaththir, then al-Ḍuḥā.
  reasons: Aisha's report lists Nūn or al-Qalam, al-Muddaththir, then al-Ḍuḥā; the sources cite 68:1, 74:1, and 93:1. DURR (مجاهد): Mujāhid says Sūrat al-ʿAlaq was the first revelation, followed by “Nūn wa-l-qalam.” DURR (عبيد بن عمير): ʿUbayd b. ʿUmayr says al-ʿAlaq was first, followed by Sūrat Nūn. # 96:1/q27 What names are given to this sūra? [* · sciences]
turns on: Use of Iqraʾ, al-ʿAlaq, and al-Qalam.
- 96:1/q27/p1 Al-ʿAlaq, named from its early mention of a clot.
  reasons: ALUSI: Sūrat al-ʿAlaq, also called Sūrat Iqraʾ, is Meccan by consensus; verse counts differ: twenty in the Ḥijāzī count, nineteen Iraqi, and eighteen Syrian. IBNASHUR: Mushafs and most commentaries call it Surat al-Alaq because that word occurs near its beginning; some tafsirs use the name too. IBNASHUR-FULL: It is also commonly called al-Alaq because that word occurs near the beginning; Bukhari titles it Iqra bi-smi rabbika alladhi khalaq, and others call it Iqra or Iqra wa-l-Alaq. BINTSHATI: The familiar name is Surat al-Alaq; al-Tabari calls it Surat Iqra or Iqra bi-smi rabbik, while al-Razi calls it Surat al-Qalam, a name easily confused with the following surah, which he calls Nun.; cites 68:1
  holders (4 notes): ALUSI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI
  notes: ALUSI:96:1/r1 IBNASHUR:96:1/r2 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p433/r2 BINTSHATI:v2p13/r1
- 96:1/q27/p2 Iqraʾ or Iqraʾ bi-smi rabbika, from its opening words.
  reasons: ALUSI: Sūrat al-ʿAlaq, also called Sūrat Iqraʾ, is Meccan by consensus; verse counts differ: twenty in the Ḥijāzī count, nineteen Iraqi, and eighteen Syrian. IBNASHUR (أبو سلمة بن عبد الرحمن، أبو رجاء العطاردي، مجاهد، الزهري، الترمذي): The surah was commonly called Surat Iqra bi-smi rabbik among Companions and Successors; these authorities report the name, and al-Tirmidhi titles it that way. IBNASHUR (البخاري): Al-Bukhari titles the surah with the full opening phrase, “Read in the name of your Lord who created.” IBNASHUR (الكواشي): Al-Kawashi calls it Surat Iqra wa-l-Alaq; the source also notes the shorter title Surat Iqra.
