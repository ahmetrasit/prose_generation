# 96:1/q34 What refuge formula or practice is linked with this first recitation? [* · law]
turns on: The formula and timing of seeking refuge before Qurʾān recitation.
- 96:1/q34/p1 Gabriel taught refuge before the basmala and first command.
  reasons: Rāzī reports Ibn ʿAbbās that Gabriel taught refuge, then basmala, then Iqraʾ; Ibn al-Jazarī calls one chain weak and disconnected and cites al-Dānī's alternative. NASHR (عبد الله بن عباس): A report attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās says Gabriel first told Muhammad to seek refuge; he replied with a formula seeking refuge in the All-Hearing, All-Knowing, then came the basmalah and the command to read. NASHR (ابن كثير): Ibn Kathīr calls the Ibn ʿAbbās report unusual and says its chain is weak and disconnected, so it cannot serve as proof. NASHR (أبو عمرو الداني): Al-Dānī gives what the author calls the correct version from Abū Rūq through al-Ḍaḥḥāk to Ibn ʿAbbās, saying Gabriel first taught the Prophet; the excerpt ends before the account is completed. BIQAI: The command to recite also alludes to seeking refuge from devils, as the Qurʾān interposes a hidden barrier and God commands refuge before recitation.; cites 17:45, 16:98 BIQAI-FULL: The order to read the Qur'an also alludes to seeking refuge from devils when reciting.; cites 17:45, 16:98 BIQAI-FULL: The cited commands to seek refuge and the protective veil apply against human and jinn devils when reading the Qur'an.; cites 17:45, 16:98 IBNASHUR: Ibn Ashur points to the earlier discussion of reciting the Qur'an and seeking refuge from Satan in 16:98.; cites 16:98

# 96:1/q36 How is this early sūra positioned in codices and sūra order? [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether arrangement follows revelation or codex compilation.
- 96:1/q36/p1 The received codex order reflects companion or prophetic arrangement rather than chronology.
  reasons: Ibn Kathīr reports debate on companion judgment and prophetic arrangement; Rabiʿa invokes compiler consensus, Mālik what companions heard, and Ibn Masʿūd's early sūras are described as old possessions. IBNASHUR-FULL: Quran 16:118 recalls the prohibition in 6:146, showing al-Anʿam was revealed before al-Nahl, in agreement with their current mushaf order.; cites 16:118, 6:146 IBNASHUR-FULL (عبد الله بن مسعود): In Bukhari, Ibn Masʿud describes surah pairs the Prophet recited in each rakʿa; Alqama identifies twenty surahs at the start of the Mufassal in Ibn Masʿud's order, ending with al-Dukhan and al-Nabaʾ. IBNASHUR-FULL (الجمهور): The majority hold that many surahs were already arranged during the Prophet's lifetime. IBNASHUR-FULL: The last revealed verse is variously placed in al-Baqara, al-Nisaʾ, or Baraʾa, all of which precede many later surahs in the present arrangement. IBNASHUR-FULL: Companions' personal codices differed in surah order; Ibn Masʿud and Ubayy had codices of their own. IBNASHUR-FULL: Ibn Ashur cites an unidentified report that Salim, the freedman of Abu Hudhayfa, was the first to collect the Quran in a codex. IBNASHUR-FULL: Others ordered codices from longer surahs to shorter ones: Ubayy and Ibn Masʿud began with al-Baqara, al-Nisaʾ, and Aal ʿImran, and Uthman used this method for the official codex. IBNASHUR-FULL (عبد الله بن عباس): Al-Tirmidhi and Abu Dawud transmit Ibn Abbas's question to Uthman about pairing al-Anfal and Baraʾa, omitting a basmala between them, and placing them among the seven long surahs. IBNASHUR-FULL (عثمان بن عفان): Uthman said the Prophet directed scribes where to place new revelations; Anfal was among the first Medinan surahs, Baraʾa among the last, and their similar stories led him to pair them, while the Prophet left their relation unclear. IBNASHUR-FULL: This account shows that the basmala marked boundaries between surahs; Uthman omitted it between Anfal and Baraʾa because he was not certain they were separate, though that remained the likelier view.

- 96:1/q36/p2 Some personal codices began with Iqraʾ in revelation order.
