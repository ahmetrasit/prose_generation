  reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī reports Abū Jaʿfar's light hamza; Abū Ḥayyān records al-Aʿshāʾ via Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim dropping it, explained from the imperfect verb. Al-Samīn adds a poetic parallel. # 96:1/q06 Why does 'Read' precede 'in your Lord's name'? [ٱقْرَأْ بِٱسْمِ رَبِّكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the command is foregrounded or the prepositional phrase belongs to the later repetition.
- 96:1/q06/p1 The verb comes first to emphasize the urgent, initial command to read.
  reasons: Al-Burhān gives the urgency of the act in the first revealed sūra as reason to put the verb first. BURHAN: Two explanations are offered for placing the verb before “in the name of your Lord”: the act is foremost because this was the first revealed sūrah, or the phrase attaches to the second “recite.”; cites 96:1 BIQAI: The verb is placed before its prepositional phrase for greater impact, especially as this was the first revelation and reading was the urgent command; its object is omitted to indicate that the reading commanded is the Qurʾān, comprehensive of all good. BIQAI-FULL: The active verb is placed before its prepositional phrase because this is more forceful, especially in the first revelation, where the command to read is paramount. ALUSI: Al-Ālūsī prefers attaching “in your Lord’s name” to the adjacent verb; placing the verb first suits this opening surah, where recitation is especially important. ALUSI-FULL: The fronted command suits the importance of reading in the first surah revealed; even if another passage preceded it, the sense is read beginning with God's name, so other openings would not comply, while moving the name first changes the focus.
