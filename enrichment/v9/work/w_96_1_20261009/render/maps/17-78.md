# 17:78: 31 questions

## 17:78/q01 What does it mean to establish prayer here?  [أَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · interpretation]
turns on: The cited sources differ over lexical scope, reports, grammatical construal or legal inference, as set out in the positions.
- 17:78/q01/p1 Perform the prescribed prayers fully and regularly. — Jishumi says perform them fully; Biqaʿi specifies conditions, pillars, preliminaries and ends; Ibn ʿAshur and Islahi stress regular observance. [9 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(المفسرون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 17:78/q01/p2 Prayer is the foremost worship after faith and aid in hardship. — Razi and Abu Hayyan call it the noblest act after faith; Biqaʿi says it aids against enemies and hardship. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 17:78/q02 Whom does the prayer command address?  [أَقِمِ · referent]
turns on: The cited sources differ over lexical scope, reports, grammatical construal or legal inference, as set out in the positions.
- 17:78/q02/p1 The Prophet is addressed, and his community is included. — Razi contrasts the next verse's expressly personal night vigil; Ibn ʿAshur invokes the normal scope of legislative address. [4 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 17:78/q02/p2 It addresses the Prophet personally. — Abu Hayyan says the address is to the Messenger; Jishumi also reports a personal reading. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 17:78/q03 What does the lām in li-dulūk mean?  [لِدُلُوكِ · grammar]
turns on: The cited sources differ over lexical scope, reports, grammatical construal or legal inference, as set out in the positions.
- 17:78/q03/p1 It marks the time, at the sun's decline. — Ibn al-Anbari and Ibn ʿAshur explain it as 'at'; Islahi also uses this temporal sense. [7 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 17:78/q03/p2 It means after the sun's decline. — Abu Hayyan, Samin and Alusi cite Mutammim ibn Nuwayra's elegy and the idiom for days elapsed; Burhan and Itqan give the same sense. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(متمم بن نويرة); BURHAN; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL]
- 17:78/q03/p3 It marks the cause of the prayer's obligation. — Al-Wahidi: the sun's decline causes prayer to become due; Ibn Aban calls this the apparent sense. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأزهري); RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الواحدي); SAMIN-DURR(الواحدي); BURHAN(ابن أبان)]
- 17:78/q03/p4 It is emphatic. — Ibn al-Anbari compares 27:72. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري)]

## 17:78/q04 What is the sun's dulūk?  [دُلُوكِ ٱلشَّمْسِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether dulūk denotes the first westward inclination at noon, setting, or the whole interval; reported Prophetic practice, Arab usage and poetry are cited.
- 17:78/q04/p1 Its decline from the zenith at noon. — Tabari argues that dulūk means inclination and its first occurrence is after the zenith; Razi agrees. Reports cite the returned shadow, the Prophet's noon prayer, and Gabriel's instruction. Alusi notes noon was the first daytime prayer performed after the Isra; Durr records ʿUmar's Prophetic gloss, though the Ibn ʿUmar route is weak. Reports from Anas and Abu Barza locate the Prophet's Ẓuhr there. - 17:78/q04/p2 The sun's inclination through the period from noon decline to sunset. — Mubarrad, Abu Ubayda and Ibn ʿAtiyya extend the word over the interval; Mawrdi and others say the root denotes inclination at either end. Poetry describes shielding the eyes with a palm; variant barāḥ can name the sun. [39 notes: MAJAZ; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عبيدة والأصمعي وأبو عمرو الشيباني,ابن مسعود); TABRISI(الزجاج,المبرد,ثعلب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة,الأزهري,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الأزهري,القفال); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية,الماوردي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(الراغب,الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(المبرد,قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr,Şevkânî)]
- 17:78/q04/p3 Its setting. — Farra reports Arab usage and poetry; Ibn Qutayba cites Dhu al-Rumma's setting stars; Ibn Masud swore the verse marked sunset. ## 17:78/q05 Which prayers does the sun-to-night span encompass?  [لِدُلُوكِ ٱلشَّمْسِ إِلَىٰ غَسَقِ ٱلَّيْلِ · interpretation]
turns on: The starting sense of dulūk and whether a continuous span names every prayer time.
- 17:78/q05/p1 Decline to nightfall covers Ẓuhr, ʿAṣr, Maghrib and ʿIshāʾ; dawn adds Fajr. — Azhari, Baghawi, Tabarsi, Razi and others prefer noon because it encompasses all five; Gabriel's teaching supplies the individual times. [29 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(أبو هريرة); TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عمر وابن عباس وأبو بردة والحسن والجمهور,جابر); TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عمر وابن عباس وأبو بردة والحسن والجمهور); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Fahreddin er-Râzî,İbn Âşûr); STUDYQURAN(most commentators); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 17:78/q05/p2 From sunset it covers Maghrib and ʿIshāʾ, then separately Fajr. — Ibn Masud's sunset report grounds this reading; Ibn ʿAtiyya notes noon and afternoon then remain unmentioned. Mujahid's concise report identifies the interval with sunset and Maghrib; Ibn ʿAtiyya notes this reading leaves the noon and afternoon prayers outside the verse. [5 notes: MUJAHID; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود وابن عباس وزيد بن أسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن مسعود وابن عباس وزيد بن أسلم)]
- 17:78/q05/p3 The span specifies Maghrib alone, from sunset until darkness. — Ibn Zayd and some jurists explain the start and end of Maghrib's time this way. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 17:78/q05/p4 The wording itself gives one prayer interval, and does not specify all five times. — Abu Hayyan objects to extracting three or five distinct times from a single interval. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 17:78/q06 What is ghasaq al-layl?  [غَسَقِ ٱلَّيْلِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ghassaq is first darkness, accumulated darkness after twilight, or the midnight endpoint in transmitted Imami reports.
- 17:78/q06/p1 Night's accumulated darkness, especially after twilight disappears. — Al-Azhari and Razi identify twilight's disappearance; Razi prefers it as covering the four earlier prayers. The root is compared to tears, blood or liquid flowing and darkness pouring over the world. Abu Zayd, Ibn al-Aʿrabi, Tabarsi and Samin compare the root to fluid flowing from an eye or wound; al-Kisaʾi distinguishes verbal and noun forms. Bint al-Shati lists related forms in 113:3, 38:57 and 78:25. [44 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,الضحاك); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(أبو عبيدة,ابن قيس الرقيات); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو زيد,ابن شميل,ابن عباس,الفراء والزجاج وأبو عبيدة وابن قتيبة,ثعلب عن ابن الأعرابي); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الأزهري,الكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة,ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(الفراء); BIQAI-FULL(الرازي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; MUQATIL; STUDYQURAN(some Sunni schools of law); ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأزهري)+]
- 17:78/q06/p2 The arrival or first darkness of night, around sunset. — Ibn Abbas, ʿAta, al-Nadr and others describe darkness entering or mixing; Zuhayr's verse is cited. [34 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن عباس,قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(عطاء,قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة,مجاهد); THALABI(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة,الفراء وابن عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن جريج عن عطاء); JISHUMI(أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس,النضر بن شميل,عطاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النضر بن شميل); SAMIN-DURR(ابن شميل); BIQAI-FULL(الرماني); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن عباس,ابن مسعود,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن السكيت); STUDYQURAN(some Sunni schools of law)]
- 17:78/q06/p3 Midnight. — Al-Baqir and al-Sadiq are reported to set the endpoint at midnight; Shiʿi reports divide four prayers between noon and midnight. [2 notes: TABRISI(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(أئمة أهل البيت عليهم السلام)]

## 17:78/q07 Which prayer is associated with ghasaq?  [غَسَقِ ٱلَّيْلِ · referent]
turns on: Whether ghasaq points to sunset, the dark time of ʿIshāʾ, or the whole evening interval.
- 17:78/q07/p1 ʿIshāʾ, the final night prayer. — Ibn Masud and several commentators associate darkness with ʿIshāʾ. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبو جعفر الطبري); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 17:78/q07/p2 Maghrib, at the beginning of night. — Qatada, Mujahid and Ibn Abbas associate the onset or sunset with Maghrib; Tabari prefers this over ʿAsr. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة والضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,القاضي أبو يعلى); MUJAHID; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL+]
- 17:78/q07/p3 Maghrib and ʿIshāʾ. — Hasan and other commentators distribute both evening prayers within the interval. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); MUQATIL]
- 17:78/q07/p4 ʿAṣr. — Tabari reports this minority view through Abu Jaʿfar and rejects it because ʿAṣr lies before nightfall. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو جعفر); TAB-FULL-]

## 17:78/q08 How long does Maghrib's time last?  [غَسَقِ ٱلَّيْلِ · law]
turns on: Whether Gabriel's two sunset demonstrations fix the only valid time or show the preferred start of a longer interval.
- 17:78/q08/p1 It may be prayed until twilight disappears. — Qurtubi reconciles Gabriel's preferred prompt time with reports allowing delay; Abu Musa and Jabir are cited. Some define the limit as red twilight, others white. Alusi notes that red twilight and the later white twilight lead to different starts for ʿIshāʾ. Qurtubi calls the extended permissible time the famous Maliki view, despite a well-known short-time report. [12 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(أبو هريرة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,الربيع بن خثيم,جابر بن عبد الله,مالك وأبو حنيفة وأصحابه والحسن بن حي وإسحاق وأبو ثور وداود); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البيهقي، النووي); TABRISI(الشيخ أبو جعفر); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 17:78/q08/p2 Its fixed time is sunset. — The two-day Gabriel report places Maghrib at sunset both days, cited for this view. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مالك والشافعي والثوري)]

## 17:78/q09 When does ʿAṣr time begin?  [دُلُوكِ ٱلشَّمْسِ · law]
turns on: The cited sources differ over lexical scope, reports, grammatical construal or legal inference, as set out in the positions.
- 17:78/q09/p1 When an object's shadow is twice its height beyond the noon shadow. — Abu Hanifa's rule is favored by leading verifiers cited by Alusi. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); ALUSI-FULL(المحققون)+]
- 17:78/q09/p2 When its shadow equals its height beyond the noon shadow. — Abu Hanifa's two disciples take this position. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو حنيفة)]

## 17:78/q10 Does this verse permit joining the paired prayers?  [لِدُلُوكِ ٱلشَّمْسِ إِلَىٰ غَسَقِ ٱلَّيْلِ · law]
turns on: Whether a shared interval permits combining, and how the Medina combining report is explained.
- 17:78/q10/p1 Combining is allowed for travel, rain, illness or comparable need. — Razi restricts combination without excuse; Nawawi selects illness, and others allow a nonhabitual need. Alusi notes the no-rain wording in another report. Alusi reports Ibn Abbas said the Prophet joined prayers to spare the community hardship, with no illness specified, while Nawawi favors illness and others allow nonhabitual need. [7 notes: TABRISI(الشيخ أبو جعفر); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أحمد، القاضي حسين، الخطابي، المتولي، الروياني,ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Fahreddin er-Râzî); ALUSI-FULL(النووي)+]
- 17:78/q10/p2 The interval gives shared times for Ẓuhr with ʿAṣr and Maghrib with ʿIshāʾ, allowing their combination. — Tabarsi explains two ordered pairs; Shiʿi reports set noon to sunset and sunset to midnight. Ibn Abbas reports the Prophet joined eight and seven units in Medina. [8 notes: TABRISI(أبو عبد الله,المرتضى,بعض أصحابنا); ALUSI-FULL(جماعة من الشيعة,عبيدة، زرارة عن أبي عبد الله); TABATABAI; TABRISI(الشيخ أبو جعفر)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 17:78/q10/p3 Combining is confined to ʿArafat and Muzdalifa. — Abu Hanifa's view; Alusi says authentic reports do not support this restriction, though he favors prayer in undisputed times. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو حنيفة,ابن الهمام,الترمذي)]
- 17:78/q10/p4 The Medina report may describe apparent combination at adjacent prayer times or obscured times. — Alusi reports clouds and end/beginning-time explanations, but says these fail to explain both pairs or conflict with the plain report. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الشافعي,قيل)]

## 17:78/q11 How is Qurʾān al-fajr construed grammatically?  [وَقُرْءَانَ ٱلْفَجْرِ · grammar]
turns on: The implied governor, if any, of the accusative Qurʾān.
- 17:78/q11/p1 It is coordinated with prayer as an object of 'establish'. — Farra and many later grammarians supply 'establish the dawn recitation'. [9 notes: THALABI(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج); JISHUMI(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 17:78/q11/p2 It is an accusative exhortation: attend to the dawn recitation. — The Basrans and Akhfash report this; Samin objects under Basran principles. [9 notes: NAHHAS(الأخفش سعيد); THALABI(أهل البصرة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل البصرة); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش); BIQAI-FULL; JISHUMI(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 17:78/q11/p3 An omitted verb governs it: recite, increase, or adhere to dawn recitation. — Zajjaj and Tabarsi supply aqim; Akhfash and Samin report other implied commands. [7 notes: AKHFASH; NAHHAS(أبو اسحاق); TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء,الأخفش); SAMIN-DURR; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 17:78/q11/p4 It is an appositional explanation of the prayer command. — Thaʿlabi reports this alternative. [1 notes: THALABI]
- 17:78/q11/p5 It is accusative by specification, emphasizing dawn. — Islahi adopts this analysis. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 17:78/q12 What does Qurʾān al-fajr designate?  [قُرْءَانَ ٱلْفَجْرِ · referent]
turns on: Whether Qurʾān names the whole dawn prayer by one act or retains its literal sense of recitation in that prayer.
- 17:78/q12/p1 The Fajr prayer itself. — Most early exegetes identify it as morning prayer; it is named by its recitation as a constituent act. [33 notes: MUJAHID(محمد بن كعب القرظي); YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الضحاك,علي بن أبي طالب وأبي بن كعب,قتادة,كعب,مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(أبو إسحاق الزجاج); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABDURRAZZAQ(طاوس,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(الجصاص)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي)-]
- 17:78/q12/p2 The Qurʾān recited during the Fajr prayer. — Majaz, Ibn Qutayba and others retain the literal sense; the recitation is normally audible and prolonged. Abu ʿUbayda illustrates the root as gathering with a poem on a she-camel carrying a fetus and calls Qurʾān a verbal noun like qirāʾa; another poem uses Qurʾān for recitation. [9 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(أبو عبيدة); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو جعفر الطبري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL(الجصاص)]

## 17:78/q13 Why call the dawn prayer Qurʾān?  [قُرْءَانَ ٱلْفَجْرِ · rhetoric]
turns on: The cited sources differ over lexical scope, reports, grammatical construal or legal inference, as set out in the positions.
- 17:78/q13/p1 Its recitation is especially long and audible. — Ibn ʿAtiyya, Qurtubi, Biqaʿi and others point to dawn's longer public recitation and the benefit of hearing it. Qurtubi says Medinan practice gives substantial Fajr recitation without burdening the congregation, while Kashshaf urges length so more people hear and gain reward. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ASAD-NOTES; ELMALILI]
- 17:78/q13/p2 Its recitation is essential to prayer. — Zajjaj, Zamakhshari and others take naming by recitation to mark a constituent pillar, like naming prayer by bowing or prostration. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 17:78/q14 Is Qurʾān recitation obligatory in prayer?  [قُرْءَانَ ٱلْفَجْرِ · law]
turns on: Whether naming the prayer for recitation proves a pillar or could merely highlight a recommended act.
- 17:78/q14/p1 Recitation is required, at least in the dawn prayer, and by extension other prayers. — Zajjaj and Zamakhshari infer a pillar from naming; al-Jassas keeps Qurʾān literal and cites the command, arguing no other dawn recitation is obligatory. [14 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأوزاعي وأيوب والشافعي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الجصاص); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BAYDAWI-FULL-]
- 17:78/q14/p2 The wording alone does not prove recitation is a pillar. — Baydawi says metaphorical naming could rest on recommendation; Alusi records objections to the whole-for-part inference. Baydawi distinguishes metaphorical naming from a literal command to recite, and says analogy could extend a literal dawn obligation to other prayers. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الكشاف)]
- 17:78/q14/p3 Recitation is not a pillar of prayer. — Attributed to Ibn ʿUlayya and al-Aṣamm; Zamakhshari and Nasafi argue against it. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الجصاص); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 17:78/q15 How much al-Fātiḥa is required in prayer?  [قُرْءَانَ ٱلْفَجْرِ · law]

## 17:78/q16 How long should the dawn recitation be?  [قُرْءَانَ ٱلْفَجْرِ · law]
- 17:78/q16/p1 Longer than in other prayers, while avoiding hardship for the congregation. — Razi cites jurists; Qurtubi cites Medinan practice and the Prophet's instruction to Muʿadh to keep communal prayer easy. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]

## 17:78/q17 Is early darkness or later brightness preferred for Fajr?  [ٱلْفَجْرِ · law]
turns on: The timing indicated by dawn's onset, the angelic handover and differing reports of Prophetic and Companion practice.
- 17:78/q17/p1 Pray at dawn's first light or begin in darkness. — Razi infers preference after consensus removes immediate obligation; Qurtubi says both angel watches then attend. Aisha reports women returned unrecognized in darkness. Razi says prolonged Fajr starts in darkness under night angels and ends in daylight under day angels; Alusi replies the same logic would wrongly require delaying ʿAṣr. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,عائشة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 17:78/q17/p2 Pray when the dawn is brighter, or combine darkness and brightness. — Abu Hanifa and Alusi cite reports of greater reward in brightness and a report of Companion practice; Alusi rejects deriving early timing solely from angelic attendance. Alusi cites a Tirmidhi report that brighter dawn increases reward and an authenticated Tahawi report from Ibrahim on Companion practice, while discussing Aisha's contrary report of women returning unrecognized in darkness. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); ALUSI-FULL(إبراهيم)]
- 17:78/q17/p3 Begin in darkness and finish in brighter light. — Al-Tahawi's proposed reconciliation; Alusi questions its fit with Hanafi practice. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الطحاوي)]

## 17:78/q18 Who or what witnesses the dawn recitation?  [مَشْهُودًۭا · referent]

- 17:78/q18/p2 The worshippers who attend, or should attend, in large numbers. — Ibn Bahr, Abu Muslim and others read the wording as describing or urging a well-attended congregation; Razi describes spiritual reflection among worshippers. - 17:78/q18/p3 God together with the night and day angels. — Abu al-Darda and other reports add God; Ibn ʿAtiyya calls one route weak. - 17:78/q18/p7 Angels and jinn witness it. — ʿAtaʾ is reported with this additional witness. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(عطاء)]

## 17:78/q19 When and how do the angel watches change?  [مَشْهُودًۭا · reports]
