# 19:65: 24 questions

## 19:65/q01 What does God's lordship over the heavens and earth include?  [رَّبُّ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَا · interpretation]
- 19:65/q01/p1 He creates, owns, preserves, governs, and has power over all beings. — Muqatil includes all creatures. Zajjaj includes knowledge of everything in them. Qushayri argues that existence within His power makes Him owner; even the weak draw value from their Owner. Wahidi, Tabrisi, Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, Tabatabai and others stress creation, ownership and governance; Ibn Kathir adds that none reverses His decree. Qashani gives an inward account: He nurtures each realm by its fitting name. Ibn Ashur calls heaven the upper world, earth the lower, and what is between the skies and horizons; together they comprise all beings. Study Quran lists other occurrences of the divine title (13:16; 17:102; 18:14; 21:56; 26:24; 37:5; 38:66; 43:82; 44:7; 45:36; 78:37). [12 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 19:65/q02 Why does the verse name God Lord of all creation here?  [رَّبُّ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَا · links]
turns on: The notes dispute which preceding premise directly grounds the command.
- 19:65/q02/p1 Universal lordship explains why God cannot forget or neglect anyone. — Tabari says creation would perish without continuous preservation. Razi says its order and dependent existence would collapse. Baydawi and Alusi apply this to God's care for His messenger; Bursevi says forgetfulness is impossible for the Owner. Abu Muslim in Tabrisi adds that He does not forget anyone and fail to resurrect them. Tabatabai connects lordship to unfailing governance; Biqai says the formulation strengthens the resurrection argument. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; TABRISI(أبو مسلم); BIQAI-FULL]
- 19:65/q02/p2 The phrase establishes the sole authority behind the call to worship and the angels' message. — Tabatabai says the message is God's own summons and the repeated 'your Lord' shows the angels' Lord is the Prophet's, matching the surah's opening purpose. Ibn Ashur says it concludes that angels act only by leave and all creation is under God. Alusi also reports lordship and God's not abandoning or forgetting as proposed immediate grounds for the imperative. [4 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)-]

## 19:65/q03 How is 'Lord of the heavens' parsed?  [رَّبُّ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the phrase continues the preceding syntax, begins an omitted-subject clause, or heads a clause with an imperative predicate.
- 19:65/q03/p1 It is apposition to the preceding 'your Lord.' — Ibn Atiyya holds this; Kashshaf, Tabrisi, Razi citing Zamakhshari, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Samin and Alusi permit it. Tabrisi says it may appose kāna's predicate. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الأخفش); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:65/q03/p2 It predicates an omitted 'He is.' — Kashshaf, Razi citing Zamakhshari, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, Bursevi and Alusi give this alternative. Ibn Ashur chooses it and cites a verse attributed uncertainly to al-Suli or Ibn al-Zubayr; he says usage omits the subject before a final distinguishing description. Kashshaf cites a Khawlan marriage line for the ensuing imperative construction. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(الأخفش); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 19:65/q03/p3 It is a subject whose predicate is the imperative 'worship Him.' — Tabrisi attributes this to al-Akhfash rather than Sibawayh. Samin explains al-Akhfash permits the added fāʾ in such a predicate. Alusi reports Abu al-Baqa's allowance but calls this parse implausible. [3 notes: TABRISI(الأخفش); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء، الأخفش)]

## 19:65/q04 What does 'what is between them' refer to?  [وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَا · referent]
- 19:65/q04/p1 The intervening realms and all their creatures. — Ibn Ashur identifies skies and horizons between upper and lower worlds. Muqatil includes all creatures; Zajjaj includes everything in heaven and earth; Qashani speaks of each intervening realm. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; QASHANI]

## 19:65/q05 What follows the ownership of everything for human acts?  [رَّبُّ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَا · theology]
- 19:65/q05/p1 Human acts or acquisitions are created by God. — Razi reports his school's inference from God's lordship over everything between heaven and earth. Qurtubi says the verse proves human acquisitions are created by Him and links His sole ownership to His exclusive right to worship. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 19:65/q06 What does the command to worship require?  [فَٱعْبُدْهُ · interpretation]
- 19:65/q06/p1 Obey God alone by carrying out His commands and prohibitions with humility. — Tabari says persist and submit, seeking approval; Bursevi and Islahi interpret worship broadly as obedience, Islahi comparing 19:44. Qushayri calls for standing where God commands and abandoning personal inclination, opinion and planning. Wahidi and Ibn al-Jawzi say idolatrous worship is no worship. Qurtubi defines worship as obedience with utmost humility. Ibn Ashur says it addresses Muhammad and condemns association. [21 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 19:65/q06/p2 Worship according to one's state creates readiness for spiritual disclosure. — Qashani says the servant's preparation for effusion and revelation must be continuous, not merely once or twice. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 19:65/q07 Why is the Prophet commanded to worship Him?  [فَٱعْبُدْهُ · links]
turns on: The disputed immediate premise is universal lordship, the preceding assurance, or God's own messenger-delivered call.
- 19:65/q07/p1 God's universal lordship and uniqueness warrant exclusive worship. — Wahidi, Qurtubi, Tabrisi, Razi, Baydawi, Ibn Ashur and others infer worship from His creation, ownership and incomparable bounty. Razi specifies bodies, life, intellect and other basic blessings. Biqai connects the command to God's encompassing knowledge. Alusi reports this as one possible direct basis. Tabatabai says the message is God's own call from the sole Lord. [16 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 19:65/q07/p2 Because God does not forget the Prophet or workers' deeds, he should work and expect reward. — Kashshaf links the preceding assurance to the Prophet's work and reward like the pious. Alusi reports the assurance that God has not abandoned him, or forgotten deeds, as alternative premises. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)-]

## 19:65/q08 What does it mean to persevere for worship?  [وَٱصْطَبِرْ لِعِبَٰدَتِهِۦ · interpretation]
- 19:65/q08/p1 Remain patiently constant in obedience through worship's obligations, hardships and opposition. — Muqatil adds without haste. Tabari and Wahidi specify commands and prohibitions; Ibn Atiyya calls the legal obligations demanding. Qushayri calls for utmost patience. Kashshaf, Razi, Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi mention delayed revelation, sophistries, mockery and enemies' delight; Nasafi adds envy. Biqai emphasizes full effort; Ibn Ashur cites the prophetic saying that ʿishāʾ is heaviest for hypocrites. Tabatabai says constancy is implicit in worship itself. Islahi stresses waiting for God's decree and help, likening the Prophet to a soldier awaiting guidance and citing 20:114. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 19:65/q08/p2 Keep directing oneself toward God and renewing purification. — Qashani interprets patience as continuous Godward attention and says worship creates readiness for effusion and revelation only with continuing purification. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 19:65/q09 How is 'iṣṭabir' formed?  [وَٱصْطَبِرْ · grammar]
- 19:65/q09/p1 It comes from iṣtabir or iṣtabara, with tāʾ changed to ṭāʾ beside ṣād. — Nahhas and Qurtubi cite the heavy tāʾ-ṣād cluster and compare a form from ṣawm. Ibn Ashur says the form intensifies patience; Islahi says it magnifies the command. [4 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 19:65/q10 Why does 'iṣṭabir' take lām here?  [وَٱصْطَبِرْ لِعِبَٰدَتِهِۦ · grammar]
- 19:65/q10/p1 The lām conveys standing firm or contending through worship's many hardships. — Kashshaf, Razi and Alusi compare worship to an opponent whom a fighter withstands. Abu Hayyan, Samin, Bursevi and Ibn Ashur say the verb usually takes ʿalā (as in 20:132), but includes the sense of steadfastness here. Islahi also hears waiting, difficult to translate. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 19:65/q11 How is the rāʾ before the lām in 'iṣṭabir li-ʿibādatihi' read?  [وَٱصْطَبِرْ لِعِبَٰدَتِهِۦ · readings]
turns on: Transmission routes and the treatment of adjacent quiescent rāʾ and lām.
- 19:65/q11/p1 The rāʾ is assimilated into the following lām. — Ibn al-Jazari transmits this for Abu Amr through al-Susi, alongside examples at 18:28 and 52:48, and also through al-Duri from Ibn Shurayh and Abu al-Izz. Qalun has it in many transmissions, al-Dani through Abu al-Hasan; several transmit it for Khallad, and isolated routes give it to Warsh through al-Asbahani. Both options for Qalun and Khallad are sound. [5 notes: NASHR(أبو عمرو من رواية السوسي,الدوري,خلاد,صاحب المبهج وأبو العلاء,قالون)]
- 19:65/q11/p2 The rāʾ is pronounced distinctly. — Ibn al-Jazari gives this for Ibn Amir, Abu Jafar, Khalaf, Warsh and Khalaf from Hamza; some authorities report it for Yaqub outside Rawh and Ruways. He calls it established practice and his own reading. Most sources give Khallad clear pronunciation; Qalun has it via al-Dani from Abu al-Fath. Most assign Qalun's two forms to Abu Nashit and al-Hulwani respectively, though al-Mubhij reverses them. Al-Mubhij and Abu al-Ala's isolated Warsh assimilation differs from other al-Asbahani transmitters. [4 notes: NASHR(ابن عامر وأبو جعفر وخلف وورش وخلف عن حمزة,خلاد,صاحب المبهج وأبو العلاء,قالون)]

## 19:65/q12 How is the boundary 'li-ʿibādatihi hal' treated in large assimilation?  [لِعِبَٰدَتِهِۦ هَلْ · readings]
turns on: Whether removing the intervening vowel and glide is permitted in large assimilation.
- 19:65/q12/p1 Assimilate across this class of boundaries. — Ibn al-Jazari says the hāʾ's lengthened vowel has no independent status, is omitted in writing, and cannot block assimilation. He rejects Ibn Mujahid's restriction; performance authorities agree. Muhammad ibn Shuja al-Balkhi and others transmit analogous forms such as ilāhihi hawāhu and innahu huwa al-tawwāb. Al-Karazini alone reports clear pronunciation in jāwazahu huwa, which Ibn al-Jazari sets aside. [4 notes: NASHR(الكرزيني,محمد بن شجاع البلخي); NASHR+]
- 19:65/q12/p2 Leave the consonants distinct in this class. — Ibn Mujahid says assimilation should only remove the first consonant's vowel, while these examples would also lose an intervening wāw and the hāʾ's vowel. Al-Karazini uniquely gives an analogous clear reading from alleged students of Ibn Mujahid. [2 notes: NASHR(ابن مجاهد,الكرزيني); NASHR-]

## 19:65/q13 What does the resumptive wāw do?  [وَٱصْطَبِرْ · grammar]
- 19:65/q13/p1 It introduces an independent clause, preventing it from being mistaken for a coordinated single word. — Burhan says the resumptive wāw can introduce nominal or verbal clauses without a shared grammatical slot or dependent sense. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 19:65/q14 How is the lām of 'hal taʿlamu' pronounced?  [هَلْ تَعْلَمُ · readings]
turns on: Proximity of lām and tāʾ permits assimilation, while clear pronunciation preserves the original consonants.
- 19:65/q14/p1 Assimilated into the tāʾ, yielding 'hat-taʿlamu.' — Majaz and Abu Ubayda call this an Arabic dialect; Majaz cites the heaviness of successive tāʾs. Abu Amr is reported with assimilation by Ibn Mujahid, Farisi, Ibn Atiyya and others; reports also name the two readers, Hisham, Ali ibn Nasr, Harun, Hasan, Amash, Isa and Ibn Muhaysin. Sibawayh permits assimilation into nearby tongue-tip consonants; Farisi compares Abu Amr at 83:36 and 87:16. The line of Muzahim al-Uqayli with hataʿīn witnesses usage. Ibn Mujahid and Farisi report Harun saying Abu Amr later returned to clarity. [19 notes: MAJAZ; IBNMUJAHID; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مزاحم العقيلي); TABRISI(أبو عمرو,سيبويه); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة,سيبويه,هارون عن أبي عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيدة,الأخوان، هشام، علي بن نصر، هارون عن أبي عمرو، الحسن، الأعمش، عيسى، ابن محيصن); SAMIN-DURR(الأخوان، هشام,مزاحم العقيلي); ALUSI-FULL(الكسائي، هشام، علي بن نصر، هارون عن أبي عمرو، الحسن، الأعمش، عيسى، ابن محيصن)]
- 19:65/q14/p2 The lām is pronounced clearly. — Ibn Mujahid and Farisi report Abu Amr's later return to this form. Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi and Abu Hayyan call it the majority form. Ibn Khalawayh grounds it in the original separateness of the consonants; Abu Ubayda calls both forms dialects. [10 notes: MAJAZ; IBNMUJAHID; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو عمرو); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيدة,الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL(الكسائي، هشام، علي بن نصر، هارون عن أبي عمرو، الحسن، الأعمش، عيسى، ابن محيصن)]

## 19:65/q15 What does 'samiyyan' deny of God?  [سَمِيًّۭا · meaning]
turns on: The Arabic word can be taken as an equal, a literal sharer of a particular divine name or title, one genuinely deserving divine attributes, or a child; the context and cited usage decide the preferred sense.
- 19:65/q15/p1 He has no equal, likeness, peer, rival, or counterpart. — Qatada, Yahya, Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Said ibn Jubayr, Ibn Jurayj and many others give this sense. Tabari adds no equal in generosity and favor and Qatada cites 43:87 as creatures' acknowledgment of their Creator. Ibn Atiyya approves the derivation from musāmāh or mutual elevation and says it suits this verse rather than John's in 19:7; Ibn Ashur prefers it as equality in all attributes. Abu Hayyan cites a poem distinguishing the mere sharing of al-Zubayr's name from matching his merits. Qushayri says even imagined likeness would entail equality, which is impossible; Qashani and Alusi say no peer could be turned to for gifts. Bint al-Shati notes related roots for matching and distinguishing marks and tabulates uses of ism, asmāʾ, tasmiyah and musammā, including 53:23. Kuranyolu records but does not prefer this sense. [52 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة,يحيى); MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(المفسرون); TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); THALABI(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومجاهد وسعيد بن جبير وابن جريج); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس ومجاهد وابن جريج وسعيد بن جبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن المسيب,ابن عباس ومجاهد,مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، ابن جبير، قتادة، ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس، مجاهد، ابن جبير، قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب,الطبري); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭabarī); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس)+; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 19:65/q15/p2 No one else bears the proper name Allah. — Al-Hasan says Allah and al-Rahman are reserved names. Ibn Abbas, Qatada, Kalbi and others say no idol is called Allah. Muqatil says God prevented such naming; Wahidi notes idolaters used al-Lat and al-Uzza instead, Kashshaf says they called idols gods, and Bursevi says divine majesty protected the name. Ibn Ashur compares 19:7 and says their failure to claim creative power acknowledges God (31:25). Baydawi and Kuranyolu distinguish Allah from ilāh. Bint al-Shati notes that divine names used of people have different meanings. Ibn Khalawayh allows a literal shared-name reading by comparison with the earlier John verse (19:7). [33 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ(التفسير); SULAMI(بعض المفسرين); THALABI(الكلبي); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس والكلبي,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة والكلبي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب); STUDYQURAN; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; RAZI-FULL-; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 19:65/q15/p3 No one else is truly named al-Rahman. — Reports from Ibn Abbas via Simak/Ikrima supply this sense; Wahidi says Ibn Masud's reading supports it and Qurtubi cites Nahhas's judgment that the chain is the strongest known. Zajjaj, Kashshaf, Razi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, Durr, Bursevi and Study Quran also give it. Al-Hasan reserves Allah and al-Rahman. Bursevi reports al-Fanari's distinction: Allah is exclusive in designation and use, Rahman in use, Rahim not exclusive; calling Musaylima Rahman was deliberate unbelief. [16 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عباس ومجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما,المولى الفناري); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 19:65/q15/p4 No one else shares the designation 'Lord of the heavens and earth' or its absolute lordship. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan report that people do not apply the whole title to another. Tabatabai prefers 'Lord' because the argument has established absolute, universal lordship, and the absence of another such lord grounds exclusive worship. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 19:65/q15/p5 He has no child or offspring. — Dahhak and a report from Ibn Abbas give this sense. Mawardi, Durr, Itqan, Alusi and Bint al-Shati cite Arabic poetry for samiyy or related musammā; Durr's report explicitly addresses Muhammad. Bint al-Shati rejects this interpretation for both Maryam verses, noting walad and awlād occur forty-six times without samiyy meaning son. Ibn Khalawayh also reports the earlier wording can describe a child not previously born to the same parents. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبو طالب,الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; BINTSHATI-IJAZ-; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 19:65/q15/p6 No one else truly deserves God's divine names or deity, creatorhood and power. — Zajjaj names Creator, Powerful and Knower of past and future. Wahidi's Abu Ishaq says a genuine namesake would merit these attributes; Tabrisi includes provider, giver and taker of life, rewarder and punisher. Mawardi and Kalbi ask who deserves to be called a god; Sulami's Husayn ibn al-Fadl and Alusi require real entitlement, not a false label. Kashshaf says false naming counts as none; Bint al-Shati cites Raghib: people's shared names differ in meaning from God's. Tabatabai explicitly rejects this gloss. [16 notes: ZAJJAJ; SULAMI(الحسين ابن الفضل); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الحسين بن الفضل); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب); STUDYQURAN(Ṭabarī); TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 19:65/q15/p7 No one else answers every call or receives every turning toward him. — Sulami reports this from Muhammad ibn al-Fadl as the practical import of God's unrivalled name. [1 notes: SULAMI(محمد بن الفضل)]

## 19:65/q16 How does the final question make its point?  [هَلْ تَعْلَمُ لَهُۥ سَمِيًّۭا · rhetoric]
- 19:65/q16/p1 It is a rhetorical denial of any match and a reason for exclusive worship and perseverance. — Yahya, Tabari, Qurtubi and others take the question to mean none exists. Alusi says denying knowledge emphatically denies reality. Ibn Ashur says absence of a known equal entails that no other being merits worship. Islahi says no peer can thwart God's intention, so the Prophet should continue and expect relief. Study Quran likewise calls it rhetorical. [14 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة والكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 19:65/q16/p2 Its indefinite noun makes the denial comprehensive: any namesake is in question. — Burhan applies the grammatical rule that an indefinite noun in an interrogative context is general. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 19:65/q17 Who speaks these verses and how do they connect to the preceding speech?  [* · links]
turns on: The continuity of speakers and the syntax of 'Lord of the heavens' determine the proposed transitions.
- 19:65/q17/p1 They are Gabriel's revealed message to the Prophet, addressing delay and God's sole control. — Tabatabai says the two verses interrupt the surrounding passage but their Quranic style indicates Gabriel's speech revealed by God. His king-and-messenger analogy explains how Gabriel's words remain God's message, and he prefers this coherent continuation to varying weak occasion reports. Ibn Ashur calls it a new divine address after the angels' explanation. Islahi says Gabriel and all angels have no independent agency and prefers him as speaker of the final question, though a divine insertion gives the same meaning. [7 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; TABATABAI+]
- 19:65/q17/p2 The preceding 'your Lord was not forgetful' may belong to the pious in Paradise, followed by God's speech. — Kashshaf reports this only with the omitted-subject parsing of 'Lord'; Alusi repeats that limitation. Tabatabai rejects placing the verses in the pious people's mouths on contextual and rhetorical grounds. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 19:65/q17/p3 The verses can be attached to Gabriel's earlier speech to Mary or require an implied insertion by Gabriel. — Tabatabai reports and rejects these proposed connections because the context and eloquence disfavor them. [0 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]

## 19:65/q18 What report explains the delay behind Gabriel's words?  [* · reports]
turns on: The notes give distinct reported events, with different functions in explaining the verse.

## 19:65/q19 How does this verse fit the surah and neighboring verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes disagree about the exact preceding premise and speaking context.
- 19:65/q19/p1 It brings the angelic explanation back to the Prophet's patient worship under God's sole rule. — Alusi reports al-Kashf: angelic obedience in movement and stillness sets up the worship command, contrasting ignorant and unjust humanity. Al-Kashf rejects an omitted condition about knowing Paradise's state as weakening the obvious cause. Tabatabai says the interlude confirms that Gabriel delivered the surah only on command; Islahi ties patience to awaited guidance. Ibn Ashur says the passage moves to monotheism. Study Quran describes the surah's arc from commanded revelation through criticism, denial of God's child, and destroyed generations (19:64, 19:65, 19:88, 19:95, 19:98). [10 notes: ALUSI-FULL(صاحب الكشف); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 19:65/q19/p2 The verse's demand for worship follows God's assurance that He remembers deeds and rewards workers. — Kashshaf applies the preceding statement to the Prophet's work and reward like the pious; Alusi lists not forgetting deeds as a possible link. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)-]

## 19:65/q20 What inward practices are read in the worship command?  [فَٱعْبُدْهُ وَٱصْطَبِرْ لِعِبَٰدَتِهِۦ · inward]
- 19:65/q20/p1 Worship spans body, self, heart, secret and spirit, each surrendering at its own level. — Bursevi quotes the Najmiyya: the body follows the Law; the self resists desires; the heart turns from world to afterlife; the secret detaches from both for God; the spirit yields its existence in witnessing. Qashani says continuing purification permits effusion and revelation and patience keeps attention toward God. [3 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); QASHANI]

## 19:65/q21 What does divine naming mean for names used of people?  [سَمِيًّۭا · theology]
- 19:65/q21/p1 The supreme names are reserved or have a distinct divine meaning when shared. — Bursevi reports al-Fanari's distinction between Allah, Rahman and Rahim, an accusation that a Yamama teacher called Rahman taught Muhammad (13:30; 13:33), and the Prophet's changing al-Aziz. Bint al-Shati cites Raghib that names used for humans have different meanings, suggests examples Ali, Aziz, Rauf and Karim as shortened Abd names, and notes early honorifics al-Ali bi-Allah and al-Zahir bi-amr Allah. Bursevi's Persian couplet praises the divine name as charm of heart and remembrance of soul. [8 notes: BURSEVI(ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما,المولى الفناري); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب)]

## 19:65/q22 How does the noun's annexation bear on literal and figurative predication?  [لِعِبَٰدَتِهِۦ · rhetoric]
- 19:65/q22/p1 A noun's annexation functions like verbal predication and presupposes established usage. — Jurjani says literal or figurative judgment of a verbal noun's annexation follows that of verbal predication; language alone cannot establish a genitive relation apart from knowledge of noun and complement. A metaphor may transfer a source noun such as 'life' before deriving 'revive'; he rejects splitting one inseparable word so its action is literal while its necessary result is metaphorical. [3 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR; JURJANI-ASRAR-]
