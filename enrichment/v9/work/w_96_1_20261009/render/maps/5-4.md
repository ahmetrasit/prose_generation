# 5:4: 42 questions

## 5:4/q01 What prompted the question or revelation?  [يَسْـَٔلُونَكَ · reports]
turns on: Which occasion report fits the broad wording and the preceding carrion prohibition.
- 5:4/q01/p1 ʿAdī ibn Ḥātim and Zayd al-Khayl asked about dogs and falcons catching prey, some dying before slaughter. — Saʿīd b. Jubayr supplies the two hunters; reports vary on prey species and number/names of dogs. Al-Baghawī calls this the sounder occasion. Al-Biqāʿī links their concern to carrion in 5:3. Al-Māwardī gives five dog names: al-Mukhtalis, Ghallāb, al-Ghānim, Sahlīb and al-Mutaʿāṭī; al-Qurṭubī instead lists Ṣalhab, Ghallāb, al-Mukhtalis, al-Mutanaʿis and an uncertain Akhṭab or Waththāb, while al-Ṭabrisī reports six dogs. Zayd is also called Ibn al-Muhalhil. [25 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن جبير); BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BIQAI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BURHAN(قيل); DURR-FULL(الطائيين,عامر); ELMALILI(Sa‘îd b. Cübeyr); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عدي بن حاتم، زيد بن المهلهل الطائي، سعيد بن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,زيد الخير); MUQATIL; QURTUBI-FULL(السهيلي,عدي بن حاتم); SUYUTI-LUBAB(سعيد بن جبير); TAB-FULL(عدي بن حاتم); TABRISI(أبو حمزة الثمالي، الحكم بن ظهيرة); THALABI(سعيد بن جبير,عدي بن حاتم وزيد الخير); WAHIDI-ASBAB(سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT(سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(عدي بن حاتم); BAGHAWI-FULL+]
- 5:4/q01/p2 Dogs were ordered killed after Gabriel refused entry; people asked what of them was lawful. — Abū Rāfiʿ reports the puppy, Gabriel's refusal, the Medina/al-ʿAwālī order and the people's question; al-Ḥākim authenticated his report. ʿIkrima names ʿĀṣim ibn ʿAdī, Saʿd ibn Khaythama and ʿUwaymir ibn Sāʿida; Muḥammad b. Kaʿb reports the same occasion more generally. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī questions the story's coherence. - 5:4/q01/p3 ʿAdī alone asked about dog hunting and revelation followed the Prophet's initial silence. — ʿĀmir and al-Shaʿbī report the Prophet awaited an answer. [3 notes: SUYUTI-LUBAB(الشعبي); TAB-FULL(عدي بن حاتم); TABATABAI(عامر)]
- 5:4/q01/p4 The question can be an anticipated or recurring question about food rather than one historical event. — Ibn ʿĀshūr appeals to Qurʾānic present-tense يسألونك; Elmalılı also permits a general possible question. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī treats occasion reports as imperfect applications of the general wording. [7 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 5:4/q01/p5 People asked whether trained hunters counted among the predators prohibited in 5:3. — Kuranyolu ties the question to the prior prohibition of torn prey. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 5:4/q02 What did the question ask about?  [مَاذَآ أُحِلَّ لَهُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the question is comprehensive or specifically about game or dogs.
- 5:4/q02/p1 It asks which foods and meals are lawful after the prohibitions. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān connect it to the previous catalogue of forbidden food; Ibn ʿĀshūr allows an actual or anticipated request for a comprehensive account. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI]
- 5:4/q02/p2 It asks specifically about game and slaughtered animals. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ālūsī report this narrower scope; Abū Ḥayyān says the answer expands beyond it. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABRISI; ZAJJAJ]
- 5:4/q02/p3 It asks about uses of dogs ordered killed. — Abū Rāfiʿ reports this wording; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says 'this kind' denotes dogs and suggests the verse gives a broader criterion. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 5:4/q03 How is ماذا parsed?  [مَاذَآ · grammar]
turns on: Whether ذا is relative, predicative, or extra.
- 5:4/q03/p1 ماذا is one interrogative noun, with أحل as its predicate. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī and Abū Ḥayyān offer this equivalent parse. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); TABRISI; WAHIDI-BASIT; ZAJJAJ]
- 5:4/q03/p2 ما is the interrogative subject and ذا a relative or predicate; أحل describes it. — Al-Akhfash, al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī and others give this analysis; the relative ذا means 'that which'. [6 notes: AKHFASH; BURSEVI; JISHUMI; NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; ZAJJAJ]
- 5:4/q03/p3 ذا is superfluous, so ما alone is the subject of أحل. — Al-Akhfash cites ماذا بال نسوتكم: replacing it with ما الذي makes the poetic clause ill formed; al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī also mention an extra ذا. [2 notes: AKHFASH(الشاعر)]

## 5:4/q04 Why is the question phrased in the third person?  [لَهُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether لهم is indirect wording or direct quotation.
- 5:4/q04/p1 The third person follows يسألونك and reports the situation indirectly. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn note that لنا would quote the speakers directly; al-Rāzī objects to treating لهم as their verbatim words. [12 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); BAYDAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن الخطيب); STUDYQURAN]
- 5:4/q04/p2 It is a verbatim question understood through an implicit verb of saying. — Al-Zamakhsharī invokes the question's sense of speech; al-Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān object that questioners would have said 'for us'. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي)-; RAZI-FULL-]

## 5:4/q05 How does the interrogative clause follow يسألونك?  [يَسْـَٔلُونَكَ مَاذَآ · grammar]
turns on: Whether asking governs the interrogative directly or through implied speech.
- 5:4/q05/p1 The interrogative suspends the verb's action and serves as its clause complement without an omitted speech verb. — Abū Ḥayyān compares 68:40; al-Samīn says asking leads to knowledge and can take an interrogative complement. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); SAMIN-DURR]
- 5:4/q05/p2 The question implies saying, or an omitted saying verb introduces the clause. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Ālūsī give this account; al-Ālūsī rejects Abū Ḥayyān's objection. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 5:4/q05/p3 An omitted noun such as 'the answer to what is lawful' supplies the object. — Al-Ālūsī reports this grammatical alternative. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]

## 5:4/q06 Why is the answer verbal although the question is nominal?  [قُلْ أُحِلَّ لَكُمُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the verbal answer follows meaning, an elided verb, or a rhetorical change.
- 5:4/q06/p1 The answer matches the question's meaning, not its nominal form. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn explain the verbal response this way; Ibn al-Muraḥḥal proposes a purposeful change of form. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR]
- 5:4/q06/p2 An omitted verb should be understood before the answer. — Al-Zarkashī and al-Suyūṭī use this verse as an example of a full verbal answer that reveals the elided verb in shorter answers. [4 notes: BURHAN(ابن مالك,ابن يعيش,شهاب الدين بن المرحل); ITQAN]
- 5:4/q06/p3 The different form is a deliberate rhetorical answer. — Ibn al-Muraḥḥal compares the reply about lunar phases in 2:189; al-Zarkashī questions the analysis. [1 notes: BURHAN(شهاب الدين بن المرحل)]

## 5:4/q07 What are الطيبات?  [ٱلطَّيِّبَٰتُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether goodness denotes legal permission, natural pleasantness, slaughter, or freedom from harm.
- 5:4/q07/p1 All lawful things, whether pleasant to eat or not. — Muqātil, Mujāhid's successors, al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās, Mālik and others gloss it as licit provision; al-Wāḥidī prefers its breadth. Al-Māwardī says legal goodness may be analogical to pleasantness. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats permission as a practical sign of goodness. [17 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو علي الجبائي، أبو مسلم); DAMGHANI; IBNASHUR-FULL(مالك); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مقاتل بن حيان); JISHUMI; MAJAZ; MAWARDI-FULL; NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; SULAMI(سهل); TAB-FULL; TABRISI(أبو علي الجبائي، أبو مسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ELMALILI(İbn Cerîr Taberî)-]
- 5:4/q07/p2 Pleasant, clean foods that sound human nature finds wholesome. — Al-Shāfiʿī, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, Elmalılı and others cite 7:157's contrast with خبائث. They use cultivated taste rather than necessity-driven appetite; Ibn ʿĀshūr objects that local taste cannot govern a universal law, citing the Prophet's distaste for but permission of monitor lizard. [30 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشافعي); ALUSI-FULL(البلخي); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإمام في تفسيره); ELMALILI(İbn Cerîr Taberî); IBNASHUR-FULL(الشافعي,فخر الدين); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الشافعي); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; TABRISI(البلخي); WAHIDI-BASIT; ELMALILI+]
- 5:4/q07/p3 Lawfully slaughtered animals, especially named slaughter. — Muqātil, al-Kalbī, Saʿīd b. Jubayr and others specify slaughtered meat in this context. [9 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-ASBAB; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل والكلبي)]
- 5:4/q07/p4 Whatever no scripture, Sunna, consensus or sound analogy prohibits. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī articulate original permission with legal exceptions. [7 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قائلون); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ZAJJAJ; TABRISI+]
- 5:4/q07/p5 Food that brings no worldly or otherworldly harm. — Al-Naḥḥās reports this formulation; Ibn ʿĀshūr says harmful pleasure is not truly good. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; NAHHAS(قيل)]
- 5:4/q07/p6 Divine aid or provision free from greed is the deeper good. — Yūsuf b. al-Ḥusayn says good provision comes without greedy striving; al-Rūdhbārī calls divine aid the best provision of gnostics. [2 notes: SULAMI(الروذباري,يوسف بن الحسين)]

## 5:4/q08 What food-law principle follows from الطيبات?  [أُحِلَّ لَكُمُ ٱلطَّيِّبَٰتُ · law]
turns on: Whether general permission is limited by taste, text, or particular reports.
- 5:4/q08/p1 Food and earthly benefits are presumed lawful until evidence forbids them, while foul or harmful things are excluded. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, Elmalılı, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kuranyolu invoke 2:29, 7:157 and 7:32; Asad limits Qurʾānic prohibitions to injury. This corrects pagan bans on baḥīra, sāʾiba, waṣīla and ḥām. [27 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; DURR-FULL(الفجيع العامري,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,سمرة بن جندب,عقبة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL; THALABI(أكثر العلماء,ابن عمر,الزهري ومكحول,الشعبي وعطاء)]
- 5:4/q08/p2 Palatability of Arabs, especially cultivated urban Hijazis, controls unlisted animal foods. — Al-Wāḥidī cites lizards, jerboas and rabbits; Muḥammad al-Kākī describes comparison with Hijazi tastes. Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects making a regional custom universal, citing Khālid's monitor lizard report. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(محمد الكاكي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل العلم); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)-]
- 5:4/q08/p3 Horse meat is lawful as pleasant food. — Al-Shāfiʿī applies the general permission; Abū Ḥanīfa disagrees. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الشافعي وأبو حنيفة); RAZI-FULL(الشافعي)]
- 5:4/q08/p4 Horse meat is impermissible. — Abū Ḥanīfa's contrary ruling is reported by al-Rāzī and Ibn ʿĀshūr. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو حنيفة)]
- 5:4/q08/p5 Domestic donkey meat is lawful under the general permission. — Mālik and Bishr al-Marīsī are reported so; al-Rāzī invokes the Prophet's Khaybar prohibition against them. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(مالك)]
- 5:4/q08/p6 Domestic donkey meat is forbidden by the Khaybar report. — Al-Rāzī takes the Prophet's prohibition to specify the general verse. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 5:4/q08/p7 Specific things such as therapeutic urine or edible clay are not wholesome. — Ibn Kathīr reports al-Zuhrī excluding urine as treatment and Mālik excluding sale of clay eaten by people. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(الزهري,مالك)]

## 5:4/q09 What slaughter by People of the Book is permitted?  [ٱلطَّيِّبَٰتُ · law]
turns on: Whether scripturalist slaughter is covered when another name is pronounced.
- 5:4/q09/p1 Their slaughter is lawful, even if they invoke Christ or prepare a church offering. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports the majority, al-Shaʿbī, ʿAṭāʾ, al-Zuhrī and Makḥūl; they read the permission for scripturalists' food broadly and take prohibitions on idolatrous naming as directed at polytheists. Al-Thaʿlabī distinguishes Jews, Christians and pre-mission adherents from later converts in one report. [4 notes: THALABI(أكثر العلماء,الزهري ومكحول,الشعبي وعطاء)]
- 5:4/q09/p2 Slaughter naming Christ is forbidden or disliked, especially when heard. — Ibn ʿUmar cites 6:121; al-Ḥasan and al-Ḥārith al-ʿAklī distinguish hearing another name from absence. [2 notes: THALABI(ابن عمر,الحسن والحرث العكلي)]
- 5:4/q09/p3 Only a Muslim may slaughter a Muslim ritual sacrifice. — ʿAlī distinguishes the special offering from ordinary food. [1 notes: THALABI(علي)]

## 5:4/q10 How is وما علمتم connected syntactically to the answer?  [وَمَا عَلَّمْتُم · grammar]
turns on: Whether ما is coordinated, conditional, or a new relative construction.
- 5:4/q10/p1 It is coordinated with الطيبات and implies 'the game or catch of what you trained'. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and others infer the omission from فكلوا; al-Qurṭubī says eating the predators themselves is not intended. [18 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; TAB-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); TABRISI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج وغيره); ZAJJAJ]
- 5:4/q10/p2 ما is conditional and فكلوا is its answer, with no implied catch needed. — Al-Wāḥidī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefer avoiding ellipsis; al-Ālūsī says permissions attach to actions. [10 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; ALUSI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)+; TABATABAI+]
- 5:4/q10/p3 ما is a relative subject whose predicate is فكلوا, with فاء by analogy with a condition. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī report this less apparent parse. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 5:4/q10/p4 The clause starts a new sentence, and فكلوا supplies its result. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī report this explanation without requiring coordination with الطيبات. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 5:4/q11 What does الجوارح mean and why are they so named?  [ٱلْجَوَارِحِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the name comes from earning prey or wounding it.
- 5:4/q11/p1 Hunting creatures called earners or catchers because they procure prey for their owners. — Abū ʿUbayda, al-Akhfash, Ibn Qutayba, al-Ṭabarī, al-Māwardī and many later authors use the root for acquisition; 6:60 and 45:21, al-Aʿshā's line, and a family's provider substantiate the sense. The singular is جارحة. [36 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; AKHFASH; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); BAGHAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; JISHUMI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; MAJAZ; MAWARDI-FULL; NAHHAS(الأخفش); NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; TAB-FULL(الأعشى); TABRISI; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 5:4/q11/p2 They are predators named for wounding prey. — Al-Māwardī reports this etymology; al-Rāzī links it to requiring a bleeding wound. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī call it weak; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Biqāʿī allow it alongside earning. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); RAZI-FULL]
- 5:4/q11/p3 The word denotes trained dogs. — Al-Farrāʾ, Ibn Qutayba and Muqātil gloss it this way in context; other authorities extend it to birds and beasts. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB]

## 5:4/q12 Which hunting animals are covered?  [ٱلْجَوَارِحِ مُكَلِّبِينَ · law]
turns on: Whether jawāriḥ includes birds and beasts despite مُكَلِّبِينَ.
- 5:4/q12/p1 Every trainable hunting beast and bird, including dogs, cheetahs, falcons and hawks. — Mujāhid, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan and most commentators cite general wording; al-Ṭabarī prefers it. The falcon report to ʿAdī and analogy with dogs support it, though al-Qurṭubī notes al-Tirmidhī's falcon chain has weak Mujālid. [58 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور,قوم); ASAD-NOTES; BAGHAWI-FULL(جمهور أهل العلم); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عدي بن حاتم,مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الأمة,قوم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,خيثمة، طاوس، مجاهد، مكحول، يحيى بن أبي كثير,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي بن الحسين); ITQAN; KASHSHAF-FULL; MUJAHID(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(جمهور الأمة); RAZI-FULL(الليث); STUDYQURAN; TAB-FULL(أبو جعفر,ابن عباس,الحسن,خيثمة,طاوس,عبيد بن عمير,عدي بن حاتم,علي بن حسين,مجاهد); TABATABAI; THALABI(سائر العلماء); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,ابن عباس,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; TAB-FULL+]
- 5:4/q12/p2 Only dogs may kill game without subsequent slaughter; bird and other beast catches must be slaughtered alive. — Ibn ʿUmar, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Suddī and reports from the Prophet's family invoke مُكَلِّبِينَ and dogs' distinctive holding for owners. Al-Ṭabarī and al-Wāḥidī reject this restriction; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī adopts it. [30 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك والسدي وابن جبير وعطاء); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عمر، السدي، الضحاك، أئمة أهل البيت); BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك والسدي); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عمر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عمر,الضحاك والسدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عمر,الضحاك، السدي,سعيد بن جبير,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عمر); MUQATIL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جعفر,ابن عمر,الضحاك); RAZI-FULL(ابن عمر); TAB-FULL(ابن عمر,السدي,الضحاك); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله,أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); TABRISI(أبو عبد الله,أهل البيت,ابن عمر، الضحاك، السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عمر والضحاك); TAB-FULL-; WAHIDI-BASIT-]
- 5:4/q12/p3 Falcons are included but other nondog hunters are not established. — Some defenders cite only the ʿAdī falcon report, distinguishing this narrower concession from a general extension. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم)]

## 5:4/q13 What does مكلبين mean and why use this term?  [مُكَلِّبِينَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word denotes training, ownership, eagerness, or a broad use of dog.
- 5:4/q13/p1 Skilled trainers who set hunting animals on prey; dogs name the craft because they are its commonest subjects. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Ṭabarī, al-Kashshāf and others say the term qualifies the handlers and stresses expertise. Poems of Ṭufayl, an unnamed hunter, and the common dog-hunting usage support it. [24 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو سليمان الدمشقي,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي); ITQAN; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; TABRISI; THALABI(الشاعر); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ZAJJAJ]
- 5:4/q13/p2 Dog owners or handlers. — Al-Farrāʾ, Ibn Qutayba, Abū ʿUbayda and reported authorities gloss it thus; the light form can mean having many dogs. [12 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح، ابن عمر، سعيد بن جبير، عطاء، الضحاك، السدي، الفراء، الزجاج، ابن قتيبة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; JISHUMI; MAJAZ; QURTUBI-FULL(الرماني); TABATABAI; TABRISI; THALABI(الحسن والقتيبي)]
- 5:4/q13/p3 The word can use dog for any predator. — Al-Zamakhsharī and followers cite the Prophet's prayer that one of God's dogs attack a man, followed by a lion attack; al-Ālūsī says that report does not establish inclusion of birds. [7 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو نوفل); BAYDAWI-FULL(رسول الله); ELMALILI; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-]
- 5:4/q13/p4 Hunters avid or persistent in pursuing game. — Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan and Mujāhid are reported with the persistent-hunting gloss; al-Zamakhsharī derives it from kalb, eagerness. Abū Ḥayyān objects that training is the human act, not a predator's nature. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس، الحسن، مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]

## 5:4/q14 What is the grammatical role of مكلبين?  [مُكَلِّبِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the circumstance qualifies people or animals.
- 5:4/q14/p1 It is an accusative circumstantial qualifier of the people who trained the hunters. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Naḥḥās, al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī and others emphasize human skill. Al-Kashshāf says it is not redundant with عَلَّمْتُم. [18 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; FARRA; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; NAHHAS; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); TAB-FULL; THALABI]
- 5:4/q14/p2 It qualifies the hunting animals themselves as trained. — Al-Māwardī reports this third analysis; Ibn Kathīr says it can describe the animals rather than the human subject. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 5:4/q15 What variant readings occur here?  [عَلَّمْتُم مُكَلِّبِينَ · readings]
turns on: Active versus passive verb and heavy versus light lām.
- 5:4/q15/p1 مُكْلِبِينَ has a light lām, from أكلب; it can mean trainers or owners of many dogs. — Ibn Masʿūd, al-Ḥasan, Abū Zurayn and others are cited; Form IV may overlap with doubled Form II, though al-Zajjāj distinguishes trainer from dog owner. [11 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وابن الحنفية); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس، ابن مسعود، الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن وأبو زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن، أبو رزين، ثعلب); JISHUMI(ابن مسعود والحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); SAMIN-DURR; TABRISI; THALABI(ابن مسعود وأبو زرين والحسن)]
- 5:4/q15/p2 عُلِّمْتُم is passive, referring to the instruction people received about hunting. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Muḥammad b. al-Ḥanafiyya transmit this reading; al-Samīn notes that God is the understood agent. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وابن الحنفية); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس ومحمد بن الحنفية); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس وابن الحنفية)]
- 5:4/q15/p3 عَلَّمْتُم is active and مُكَلِّبِينَ has doubled lām. — Most readers use these forms; Jishumī and al-Qurṭubī explain the ordinary subject and intensive trainer. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس)]

## 5:4/q16 What does teaching the animals entail?  [تُعَلِّمُونَهُنَّ · interpretation]
turns on: Which behaviors prove learned obedience, especially for birds.
- 5:4/q16/p1 Train them to pursue at release, obey and return, and secure prey for their owners. — Al-Ṭabarī, Saʿīd b. Jubayr, al-Shāfiʿī and later authorities enumerate obedience, response to call, no fleeing and owner-directed holding; Ibn ʿĀshūr says release can be hand or understood command. [27 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن); IBNASHUR-FULL(العلماء,مالك وأبو حنيفة والشافعي وأحمد وأبو يوسف ومحمد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير); IBNKATHIR-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي); RAZI-FULL; TAB-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس، عدي بن حاتم، عطاء، الشعبي، طاوس، السدي,سعد بن أبي وقاص، سليمان، ابن عمر); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(العلماء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ZAJJAJ]
- 5:4/q16/p2 Training also requires the animal not to eat its catch. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Kalbī, Ibn ʿAbbās and others treat abstention as proof that it holds for the owner; al-Ṭabarī calls eating proof of deficient training. [9 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); FARRA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي,الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي); STUDYQURAN(JJ and Ṭ); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي)]
- 5:4/q16/p3 For birds, responding to a call or command suffices; they may eat. — Rabīʿa and Ibn Ḥabīb say birds cannot practically be made to desist like dogs; Ibn ʿAbbās and others distinguish bird training from beating dogs. [13 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن حبيب,ربيعة,قوم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ربيعة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي، الشعبي، حماد بن أبي سليمان,ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن حبيب,ربيعة); TAB-FULL(بعض قائلي هذه المقالة)]
- 5:4/q16/p4 God-given intellect, inspiration or taught methods enable humans to train animals. — Al-Kashshāf, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr credit divine perception and acquired skill; Ibn ʿĀshūr links human teaching to Adam at 2:33. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 5:4/q16/p5 Training can include eating because predators learn through it. — Al-Ṭabarī reports this contrary account, applying release, pursuit and response criteria to all hunters without abstention. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون); TABRISI(سلمان، سعد بن أبي وقاص، ابن عمر)]

## 5:4/q17 How many successful trials establish training?  [عَلَّمْتُم مُكَلِّبِينَ · law]
turns on: Whether custom or one, two, or three demonstrations fixes the threshold.
- 5:4/q17/p1 Three successes are required. — Abū Yūsuf and Muḥammad b. al-Ḥasan require three demonstrations; some allow the third catch, others the fourth. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أصحابنا); BAGHAWI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أصحاب أبي حنيفة); QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي); RAZI-FULL(أبو يوسف ومحمد); TAB-FULL(أبو يوسف ومحمد بن الحسن); TABRISI(أبو يوسف، محمد)]
- 5:4/q17/p2 No fixed number; repeated behavior or hunters' custom establishes it. — Al-Shāfiʿī, Abū Ḥanīfa in a principal report, al-Biqāʿī and others appeal to habitual obedience; al-Biqāʿī notes no text or consensus fixes a count. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حنيفة); BAGHAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الشافعي); IBNASHUR-FULL(مالك وأبو حنيفة والشافعي وأحمد وأبو يوسف ومحمد); RAZI-FULL(الشافعي); TAB-FULL(بعض المتأخرين); TABRISI(أصحابنا)]
- 5:4/q17/p3 One successful hunt suffices. — Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī and some jurists count the first success, making the second catch edible in one formulation. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غيرهم); ELMALILI(حسن-ı Basrî); RAZI-FULL(الحسن البصري)]
- 5:4/q17/p4 Two successes suffice. — Another Abū Ḥanīfa report and Aḥmad require two repetitions. [2 notes: ELMALILI(أحمد بن حنبل); RAZI-FULL(أبو حنيفة)]

## 5:4/q18 How is من in مما علمكم الله understood?  [مِمَّا عَلَّمَكُمُ ٱللَّهُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether مِن expresses source or comparison.
- 5:4/q18/p1 It is partitive or marks the source/cause of human training knowledge. — Al-Ṭabarī insists من has meaning and rejects substituting كاف; Ibn ʿAṭiyya permits a portion or origin, and al-Ālūsī cause or portion. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TAB-FULL; TABRISI(السدي)]
- 5:4/q18/p2 It means 'as God taught you'. — Al-Suddī gives this paraphrase; al-Ṭabarī and al-Ṭabrisī reject equating partitive من with comparative كاف. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); TAB-FULL(السدي); THALABI(السدي); TAB-FULL-; TABRISI(السدي)-]

## 5:4/q19 How is تعلمونهن construed?  [تُعَلِّمُونَهُنَّ · grammar]
turns on: Which clause teaching modifies.
- 5:4/q19/p1 It is a second circumstantial clause emphasizing the handlers' teaching. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān and others attach it to the people or the hidden pronoun in مكلبين; al-Samīn reports a nested حال. [11 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); TABRISI]
- 5:4/q19/p2 It cannot directly qualify الجوارح across مكلبين. — Al-Samīn rejects that separation; Abū al-Baqāʾ also objects to two circumstantials governed by one verb. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 5:4/q19/p3 It begins an independent sentence. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Samīn permit this, especially if ما is not conditional. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 5:4/q19/p4 It is parenthetical between condition and answer. — Al-Samīn reports this if ما is conditional or relative with فكلوا as predicate. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 5:4/q20 May trained hunters be kept, trained, or sold?  [وَمَا عَلَّمْتُم مِّنَ ٱلْجَوَارِحِ · law]
turns on: Which benefits justify keeping dogs after the killing order.
- 5:4/q20/p1 Useful hunting dogs and other service dogs may be kept; training and sale are permitted. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī and al-Qurṭubī exempt hunting, livestock and field dogs from the earlier killing order; al-Ālūsī includes guard dogs by analogy and permits useful corrective training. Elmalılı permits buying and selling. [11 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QURTUBI-FULL(مالك); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ and W); TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-ASBAB]
- 5:4/q20/p2 Unneeded dogs are prohibited or reduce daily reward by one or two qīrāṭs. — Reports from Ibn ʿUmar and Abū Hurayra give different deductions; explanations include barking at guests, keeping angels away and impurity. Al-Qurṭubī proposes differences by dog harm or place. Ibn ʿUmar reports two qīrāṭs, Abū Hurayra one in another version; al-Qurṭubī relates the difference to harm or city. [17 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن عمر,الزهري وابن عمر); THALABI(أبو هريرة,ابن عمر,بعض شعراء البصرة,عبد الله بن معقل,عمرو بن عبيد)]
- 5:4/q20/p3 Harmful or rabid dogs should be killed, harmless useful dogs spared. — Reports from the dog-killing occasion and al-Thaʿlabī draw the distinction; the solid-black dog is singled out in some reports. [2 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-ASBAB]
- 5:4/q20/p4 A dog may protect a house as well as livestock or crops. — Some jurists extend the benefit rule to rural houses; Mālik confines the livestock dog to accompanying animals to pasture. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(غير مالك)]

## 5:4/q21 Can a wholly black dog be used for hunting?  [ٱلْجَوَارِحِ مُكَلِّبِينَ · law]
turns on: Whether the black-dog reports restrict the general wording.
- 5:4/q21/p1 No, or it is disliked, because the black dog is called a devil and ordered killed. — Aḥmad, Isḥāq, some literalists, al-Ḥasan, Qatāda and al-Nakhaʿī object; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the prayer-crossing proof weak and figurative. [10 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أحمد وجماعة من أهل الظاهر,الحسن وقتادة والنخعي); IBNASHUR-FULL(أحمد بن حنبل وإسحاق بن راهويه,الحسن وقتادة والنخعي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن وقتادة وإبراهيم النخعي وأحمد بن حنبل وابن راهويه); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(القاضي أبو يعلى); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الإمام أحمد,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(أحمد,النبي); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 5:4/q21/p2 Yes, any trained dog is covered by the verse's general wording. — Jurists of Medina and Kufa and the majority permit it; Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr stress the unrestricted text. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); IBNASHUR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(عوام أهل العلم)]

## 5:4/q22 Does the trainer's religion affect the catch?  [عَلَّمْتُم · law]
turns on: Whether non-Muslim training affects the catch.
- 5:4/q22/p1 A Magian-trained dog or bird is disfavored or its catch unlawful even if a Muslim invokes God. — Jābir, al-Ḥasan, ʿAṭāʾ, Mujāhid, al-Thawrī and Ibn ʿAbbās are reported so; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects Ibn ʿAbbās's premise that only Muslims receive God-given training. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله والحسن وعطاء ومجاهد والنخعي والثوري وإسحاق); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله والحسن وعطاء ومجاهد وإبراهيم النخعي والثوري وإسحاق بن راهويه); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الثوري); QURTUBI-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله والحسن وعطاء ومجاهد والنخعي والثوري وإسحاق); TABATABAI(ابن عباس); TABATABAI-]
- 5:4/q22/p2 A Muslim may use a dog trained or owned by a non-Muslim, even a Magian. — Mālik, al-Shāfiʿī, Abū Ḥanīfa and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī compare the trained animal to a borrowed knife; God's teaching is not exclusive to Muslims. [9 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مالك وأبو حنيفة والشافعي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مالك والشافعي وأبو حنيفة وأصحابهم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أحمد، الجمهور، الثوري); QURTUBI-FULL(مالك والشافعي وأبو حنيفة); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,ابن عباس)]
- 5:4/q22/p3 A Jewish or Christian trainer's dog is disfavored. — Al-Ḥasan bases his dislike on the verse's address to believers. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن البصري); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن البصري)]

## 5:4/q23 Whose own hunting is lawful?  [فَكُلُوا۟ مِمَّآ أَمْسَكْنَ عَلَيْكُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether hunting follows slaughter rules and who qualifies as hunter.
- 5:4/q23/p1 A Muslim or scripturalist may hunt, but a Magian's game is generally forbidden. — The majority treats hunting as a kind of ritual slaughter; Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers this principle. Abū Thawr has a minority allowance for Magians as People of the Book. [12 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور,عطاء وابن جبير والنخعي ومالك وأبو حنيفة والليث والشافعي); IBNASHUR-FULL(أشهب وابن وهب,مالك); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الأمة,مالك والشافعي وأبو حنيفة وأصحابهم وعطاء وابن جبير والنخعي والليث بن سعد وجمهور الناس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن وهب وأشهب,جمهور الأمة,مالك والشافعي وأبو حنيفة وأصحابهم وجمهور الناس); TAB-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور)+]
- 5:4/q23/p2 A Magian's game is lawful because Magians count as People of the Book. — Abū Thawr's alternate view supplies the exception. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو ثور); IBNASHUR-FULL(أشهب وابن وهب); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو ثور)]
- 5:4/q23/p3 A Sabian or intoxicated hunter's catch is invalid. — Muḥammad's legal text excludes Sabians; al-Qurṭubī says intoxication prevents the intention required for slaughter. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(محمد); QURTUBI-FULL(كتاب محمد)]
- 5:4/q23/p4 Only Muslims may hunt under this concession; even scripturalist game is excluded. — Mālik reads the hunting allowance as Muslim specific and cites 5:94; Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects using that sanctuary verse here. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مالك); IBNASHUR-FULL(مالك)-]

## 5:4/q24 What makes a killed catch lawful without ordinary slaughter?  [فَكُلُوا۟ مِمَّآ أَمْسَكْنَ عَلَيْكُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether a trained hunter's action counts as slaughter.
- 5:4/q24/p1 A released, trained hunter's wound substitutes for slaughter when its owner invoked God; live prey must be slaughtered. — The Prophet's reports to ʿAdī and Abū Thaʿlaba distinguish trained from untrained animals; al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr, Elmalılı and others extend the rule to trained beasts, birds and piercing arrows. Ibn ʿĀshūr says release supplies slaughter intent. [27 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL; TAB-FULL(الضحاك,عطاء,قتادة); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); THALABI(ثعلبة الخشني); WAHIDI-BASIT(العلماء); ZAJJAJ; BIQAI-FULL+]
- 5:4/q24/p2 Only prey reached alive for ritual slaughter is lawful in the relevant case. — A restricted view reported by al-Thaʿlabī is not acted upon; dog-only authorities apply it to nondog catches. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عمر والضحاك); THALABI-]

## 5:4/q25 What if caught prey remains alive or dies before slaughter?  [فَكُلُوا۟ مِمَّآ أَمْسَكْنَ عَلَيْكُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether live prey could be slaughtered before death.
- 5:4/q25/p1 Prey found alive must be slaughtered if the hunter can do so; neglect makes it unlawful. — The Prophet's ʿAdī report commands slaughter of live prey; al-Qurṭubī says an accessible animal dying while the hunter negligently delays is not edible, though reaching for a knife already present is excused. Al-Ṭabrisī counts movement of eye, ear or tail as life and then requires cutting windpipe and jugulars. [7 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI]
- 5:4/q25/p2 If slaughter cannot be performed before it dies, the trained hunter's kill remains lawful. — Mālik, al-Shāfiʿī and several commentators treat hunting as slaughter where access is impossible; al-Ḥanafī reports vary. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو حنيفة,مالك، الشافعي، أبو حنيفة)]
- 5:4/q25/p3 Even an animal dying when slaughter was impossible is disallowed. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports a stricter Abū Ḥanīfa view in the disputed live-catch case. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مالك، الشافعي، أبو حنيفة)]

## 5:4/q26 May one eat prey killed by choking or blunt impact without a wound?  [فَكُلُوا۟ مِمَّآ أَمْسَكْنَ عَلَيْكُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether a bleeding wound is required or capture suffices.
- 5:4/q26/p1 No; it is strangled or struck carrion and a bleeding wound is required. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Qurṭubī compare the broad side of a projectile and a dog's crushing to 5:3. Ibn Kathīr prefers prohibition, citing the Rāfiʿ b. Khadīj blood-flow report, al-Muzanī's arrow analogy, legal specification and retained blood. [22 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعضهم); ELMALILI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الشافعي، المزني، ابن الصباغ، أبو يوسف، محمد، أحمد بن حنبل,المزني,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم (حديث رافع بن خديج)); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QURTUBI-FULL(أشهب,الشافعي وابن القاسم); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 5:4/q26/p2 Yes; the general permission covers a trained animal's catch even if its impact kills without bloodshed. — Some Shāfiʿī authorities, al-Nawawī and al-Rāfiʿī are reported so; al-Wāḥidī says owner-directed holding, not wounding, is decisive. Ibn Kathīr contests attributing a firm position to al-Shāfiʿī. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو حنيفة,ابن جرير,الشافعي، النووي، الرافعي); RAZI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(كثير من العلماء); ELMALILI-; IBNKATHIR-FULL-]

## 5:4/q27 What if the hunting animal ate part of the prey?  [أَمْسَكْنَ عَلَيْكُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether eating proves the animal caught for itself and how contrary reports are reconciled.
- 5:4/q27/p1 The prey is unlawful unless found alive and slaughtered; eating shows it held the prey for itself. — Ibn ʿAbbās, the sound ʿAdī report, al-Ṭabarī, the predominant Shāfiʿī view and many authorities apply the words 'for you' strictly. Some distinguish a trained bird; al-Ṭabarī extends the bar to every predator. [69 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(عدي بن حاتم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو هريرة وابن جبير وعطاء وقتادة وعكرمة والشافعي وأحمد وإسحاق وأبو ثور,النبي); ALUSI-FULL(عدي بن حاتم,علي، الشعبي، عكرمة); BAYDAWI-FULL(أكثر الفقهاء,رسول الله); BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عدي بن حاتم); ELMALILI; FARRA; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,جماعة من الصحابة والتابعين); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وأبو هريرة والشعبي وإبراهيم النخعي وسعيد بن جبير وعطاء بن أبي رباح وقتادة وعكرمة والشافعي وأحمد وإسحاق وأبو ثور والنعمان وأصحابه); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس، عطاء); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة، ابن عباس، الحسن، الشعبي، النخعي، أبو حنيفة، صاحباه، أحمد، الشافعي,ابن جرير,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); TAB-FULL(آخرون,ابن عباس,ابن عمر,السدي,الشعبي,سعيد بن جبير، الشعبي، إبراهيم,طاوس,عدي,عدي بن حاتم,عطاء); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,رسول الله); TABRISI(ابن عباس، عدي بن حاتم، عطاء، الشعبي، طاوس، السدي,عدي بن حاتم,علي، الشعبي، عكرمة); THALABI(ابن عباس,الحسن وطاوس والشعبي وعطاء والسدي,عدي بن حاتم); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; TAB-FULL+]
- 5:4/q27/p2 The uneaten remainder remains lawful, even if little remains. — Salmān, Saʿd, Ibn ʿUmar and Abū Hurayra report allowances of two-thirds eaten or only a morsel left. Al-Qurṭubī deems an ʿAdī permission version unsound, accepts Abū Thaʿlaba's report as permission and treats the ʿAdī prohibition as caution; Ibn Kathīr rates two Abū Thaʿlaba chains good, while al-Ṭabarī criticizes an alleged Salmān-to-Prophet chain because Saʿīd b. al-Musayyib's hearing is unproven. - 5:4/q27/p3 Dogs and land beasts must abstain, but predatory birds may eat. — Al-Shaʿbī, al-Nakhaʿī, Abū Ḥanīfa and others say dogs can be beaten into abstention whereas hawks cannot; Ibn Kathīr notes ʿAdī's falcon wording omits the dog's condition. [27 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس والشعبي والنخعي وحماد بن أبي سليمان وأبو جعفر محمد بن علي الثوري وأبو حنيفة وأصحابه); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حنيفة وأصحابه,إمام الحرمين,ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL(بعض الفقهاء); BURSEVI(بعضهم ومنهم أبو حنيفة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس وحماد والنخعي وأبو حنيفة وأبو ثور); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النخعي وأصحاب الرأي والثوري وحماد بن أبي سليمان); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي، الشعبي، حماد بن أبي سليمان,ابن عباس,طائفة من العلماء); KASHSHAF-FULL(العلماء); MAWARDI-FULL(الشعبي); NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(النخعي والثوري وأصحاب الرأي وحماد بن أبي سليمان وابن عباس,النعمان); RAZI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); TAB-FULL(إبراهيم,إبراهيم وعطاء,ابن عباس,الشعبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الشعبي، النخعي، السدي)+]
- 5:4/q27/p4 A bird that eats also invalidates its catch. — ʿAlī, Saʿīd b. Jubayr, ʿIkrima and some reports from al-Shaʿbī and ʿAṭāʾ apply the same 'for itself' test to falcons. [10 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي); KASHSHAF-FULL(علي); RAZI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); TAB-FULL(آخرون,الشعبي,سعيد بن جبير,عطاء,عكرمة,علي)]
- 5:4/q27/p5 If the dog ate only after first holding prey for its owner and becoming hungry, the remainder is lawful. — Ibn Kathīr prefers this reconciliation of contrary reports; some Shāfiʿī colleagues allow a hungry dog's lapse. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(الأستاذ أبو المعالي الجويني,طائفة من الأصحاب); QURTUBI-FULL(أصحاب الشافعي); IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 5:4/q27/p6 The reports conflict; the permissive report abrogates the restrictive one. — Abū ʿUmar proposes chronology from the more explicit Abū Thaʿlaba answer; al-Qurṭubī objects that the dates are unknown and prefers reconciliation. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمر); QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 5:4/q28 Does drinking blood count as eating from prey?  [أَمْسَكْنَ عَلَيْكُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether drinking blood counts as eating.
- 5:4/q28/p1 No; the prey remains lawful. — Most jurists and ʿAṭāʾ distinguish drinking blood from eating flesh. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور من العلماء,عطاء)]
- 5:4/q28/p2 Yes, or the prey is disliked. — Al-Shaʿbī and Sufyān al-Thawrī disapprove; Abū Ḥayyān says the apparent rule equates it with eating. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سفيان الثوري); QURTUBI-FULL(الشعبي وسفيان الثوري)]

## 5:4/q29 What if the catch disappears before it is found dead?  [أَمْسَكْنَ عَلَيْكُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether the fatal cause remains certain after disappearance.
- 5:4/q29/p1 Unseen dead prey is not eaten because another cause of death is possible. — Al-Zajjāj cites the instruction to eat a witnessed kill and leave what disappeared; al-Qurṭubī cites Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Thawrī and al-Shāfiʿī. The words iṣmāʾ and inmāʾ distinguish witnessed and lost quarry, illustrated by Imruʾ al-Qays. [18 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(مالك وبعض أصحابه); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس والثوري,الشافعي,مالك); ZAJJAJ(الحرث بن وعلة الشيباني,امرؤ القيس)]
- 5:4/q29/p2 It may be eaten if a sole mortal wound or arrow mark proves the hunter's cause, even after a day or three. — Reports from Abū Thaʿlaba, al-Awzāʿī, Ashhab and others allow delayed recovery without another wound or drowning; Abū ʿUmar infers a similar Mālik position. Stench causes dislike or danger. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو ثعلبة,أبو عمر); QURTUBI-FULL(الأوزاعي وأشهب وعبد الملك وأصبغ)+]
- 5:4/q29/p3 An unseen arrow kill is allowed, but an unseen dog kill is barred. — A reported position says an arrow has one clear mechanism whereas a dog can kill several ways. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(علماؤنا)]
- 5:4/q29/p4 Any unseen prey is allowed if the hunter's arrow or dog killed it. — One of al-Qurṭubī's reported positions allows both forms. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(علماؤنا)]

## 5:4/q30 What confirms the cause of death for arrow or dog game?  [فَكُلُوا۟ مِمَّآ أَمْسَكْنَ عَلَيْكُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether a fatal mark establishes cause over water or a rival hunter.
- 5:4/q30/p1 A penetrating arrow or certain trained-hunter wound suffices; a flat-side blow or drowning does not. — The ʿAdī report distinguishes a piercing miʿrāḍ from its blunt side, and excludes water or another arrow mark. Al-Qurṭubī demands certainty about the fatal cause. The same reports warn against a second uninvoked dog, an unknown arrow mark, and a body found in water. [6 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QURTUBI-FULL; THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 5:4/q30/p2 A lost catch can still be eaten if a sole fatal mark identifies the hunter's weapon. — Abū Thaʿlaba's and other reports permit delayed retrieval where no rival cause appears; al-Qurṭubī juxtaposes the contrary visible-kill instruction. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو ثعلبة,الأوزاعي وأشهب وعبد الملك وأصبغ)]

## 5:4/q31 What if another dog joins the hunt?  [أَمْسَكْنَ عَلَيْكُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether an unknown dog may have killed the prey.
- 5:4/q31/p1 Do not eat if an unnamed or untrained dog may have killed the prey. — The Prophet's ʿAdī report and later authorities stress uncertainty of the killer and that God's name was invoked only over the hunter's own dog; al-Qurṭubī presumes a stray joined on its own. [6 notes: DURR-FULL(عدي بن حاتم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 5:4/q31/p2 If another hunter sent a dog, the hunters share the catch; if the first dog inflicted the fatal wound, its owner takes it. — Al-Qurṭubī distinguishes jointly caused catches from a preexisting mortal wound. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 5:4/q32 Must the hunter release the animal intentionally?  [أَمْسَكْنَ عَلَيْكُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether the hunt belongs to the owner's dispatch.
- 5:4/q32/p1 Yes; self-started hunting is excluded because it was not sent for the owner. — The majority, Rabīʿa, Abū Ḥanīfa, Mālik and al-Shāfiʿī require release or urging; al-Qurṭubī grounds it in intention, and Ibn ʿĀshūr allows an understood command. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ربيعة وأبو حنيفة ومالك والشافعي وأبو ثور); IBNASHUR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(جمهور العلماء)]
- 5:4/q32/p2 Taking the animal out to hunt is enough even if it starts the chase itself. — ʿAlī, al-Awzāʿī and ʿAṭāʾ are reported to allow this. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي والأوزاعي); QURTUBI-FULL(عطاء)]

## 5:4/q33 What does من in مما أمسكن express?  [مِمَّآ أَمْسَكْنَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether مِن is partitive, extra, or relevant to a partially eaten catch.
- 5:4/q33/p1 It is partitive, either idiomatic with eating or because only edible parts of prey are eaten. — Al-Ṭabarī distinguishes flesh from blood and offal; al-Bursawī names hides and bones. Ibn ʿĀshūr says ordinary partitive usage does not forbid the whole catch; al-Qurṭubī rejects the offal rationale as unnatural. [17 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(بعض أصحابنا); JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL(البصريون); SAMIN-DURR; TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية); TABRISI; WAHIDI-BASIT(النحويون); SAMIN-DURR+; TAB-FULL+]
- 5:4/q33/p2 It is an extra or emphatic particle: eat what they caught. — Al-Akhfash and some Basrans compare 2:271, 24:43 and 6:141; al-Ṭabarī and other grammarians object that من contributes meaning or is not extra in an affirmative statement. [8 notes: AKHFASH; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأخفش); JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); TAB-FULL(نحويي البصرة); TABRISI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش); TAB-FULL-; WAHIDI-BASIT-]
- 5:4/q33/p3 It permits eating what remains after an animal has eaten. — Some Maliki colleagues infer this; Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects using the common من for that inference. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(بعض أصحابنا)-]

## 5:4/q34 What does عليكم mean in أمسكن عليكم?  [عَلَيْكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether على denotes benefit or control.
- 5:4/q34/p1 It means for the hunter's benefit or on the hunter's behalf. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites ʿAlqama's poem and 33:37 for causal على; al-Samīn calls the literal spatial relation figurative. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR]
- 5:4/q34/p2 It marks the prey as held under the hunter's direction rather than for the animal. — Al-Ṭabarī and many jurists derive the owner-directed condition from it. [2 notes: ELMALILI; TABATABAI]

## 5:4/q35 To what does عليه refer, and when is God's name mentioned?  [وَٱذْكُرُوا۟ ٱسْمَ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun denotes the hunter, live prey, or eating.
- 5:4/q35/p1 It refers to the released hunter or its dispatch; invoke God at release. — Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Suddī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn Kathīr and most jurists cite the Prophet's command to name God when sending a dog or arrow. [31 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي,قيل); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس، الحسن، السدي); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس، السدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أحمد بن حنبل,ابن عباس,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL; MUQATIL; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; STUDYQURAN; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,الكلبي وغيره من المفسرين); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI(ابن عباس، الحسن، السدي)+]
- 5:4/q35/p2 It refers to eating; invoke God over the meal. — Al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān prefer this and cite the Prophet's meal instruction; al-Ālūsī deems it less likely. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض الناس); SAMIN-DURR; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 5:4/q35/p3 It refers to prey reached alive; name God when slaughtering it. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī and others offer this antecedent; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the compact wording covers release and slaughter. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 5:4/q36 Is naming God legally required, and what if it is omitted?  [وَٱذْكُرُوا۟ ٱسْمَ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ · law]
turns on: Whether naming is obligatory and how omission affects the catch.
- 5:4/q36/p1 It is obligatory; deliberate omission bars the catch, but forgetfulness is excused. — Mālik, Abū Ḥanīfa, al-Ṭabrisī and others distinguish intent; Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ḥasan invoke the believer's standing when forgetful. The formula بسم الله الله أكبر is recommended by many. [24 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفقهاء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حنيفة، الشافعي); BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(أنس رضي الله عنه); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أكثر أهل العلم,مالك وجمهور أهل العلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أحمد بن حنبل,ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QURTUBI-FULL(النبي,جمهور العلماء,مالك); TABRISI(ابن عباس، الحسن، السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الحسن)]
- 5:4/q36/p2 It is recommended; a Muslim's catch remains lawful even if omitted deliberately without contempt. — Al-Shāfiʿī and some Mālikīs rely on the broader permission and a report that a believer slaughters in God's name whether uttered or not; Ashhab distinguishes ignorance from contempt. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أشهب); QURTUBI-FULL(جماعة من أصحابنا); RAZI-FULL(الشافعي); WAHIDI-BASIT(العلماء,عائشة)]
- 5:4/q36/p3 One should say God's name at the meal, including when remembered late. — The Prophet's meal instructions, the Umayya b. Makhshī story, and reports that Satan eats when God is not named support this practice. Ibn Kathīr notes one ʿĀʾisha meal chain is disconnected, while another through Umm Kulthūm is graded ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ by al-Tirmidhī; transmitters dispute Jābir b. Ṣubḥ's strength. [15 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أم كلثوم,أمية بن مخشي,ابن معين، النسائي,الترمذي,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(أمية بن مخشي,حذيفة,عمر بن أبي سلمة); QUSHAYRI]
- 5:4/q36/p4 Verbal naming is indispensable even when omission was forgetful. — Al-Naḥḥās prefers the spoken tongue over metaphorical heart remembrance; al-Shaʿbī, Ibn Sīrīn, Nāfiʿ and Abū Thawr hold the stricter omission view. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الشعبي وابن سيرين ونافع وأبو ثور); NAHHAS+]
- 5:4/q36/p5 Inward remembrance suffices. — Al-Naḥḥās reports this alternative and argues that verbal speech is the literal meaning. [1 notes: NAHHAS(قيل)]

## 5:4/q37 What does واتقوا الله require here?  [وَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the warning targets legal care or excessive hunting.
- 5:4/q37/p1 Observe all the food and hunting limits, especially training, owner-directed catch and invocation. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, al-Ṭabrisī and others apply the general command to the preceding rulings; al-Biqāʿī asks hunters to scrutinize fine distinctions. [13 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI]
- 5:4/q37/p2 Avoid excessive or recreational killing and keep worship in view. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī warns against cruelty and arrogance; al-Ālūsī notes hunting's distraction from prayer and purity, while the ʿUrfuṭa report permits hunting for livelihood. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 5:4/q38 What does سريع الحساب mean?  [سَرِيعُ ٱلْحِسَابِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether swiftness describes knowledge, near Judgment, or prompt requital.
- 5:4/q38/p1 God knows and counts every deed and can reckon all creatures simultaneously without calculation. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Qushayrī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī and al-Biqāʿī emphasize complete knowledge and no distraction; 21:47 is cited. ## 5:4/q39 How does the verse connect with surrounding passages?  [* · links]
turns on: How permission specifies prohibitions and expresses the sūra's themes.
- 5:4/q39/p1 It resumes and explains 5:3's food prohibitions, especially carrion and the slaughter exception. — Al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Biqāʿī, Elmalılı, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kuranyolu connect permitted game to forbidden carrion; some distinguish actual and anticipated questions. 5:94 concerns sanctuary game. [10 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN(أبو إسحاق الإسفرابيني); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI+]
- 5:4/q39/p2 The food rule fits the sūra's themes of God's legislative authority, covenant and blessings. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī emphasizes God alone declares lawful and the naming command counters polytheist omission; Study Quran links the opening rulings to blessings and covenant, and comparison with Moses' final sermon. [12 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN]
- 5:4/q39/p3 Good food is a divine favor whose lawful enjoyment calls for gratitude. — Study Quran connects 5:4 with manna, Israelite food changes, Jesus' relief and Muhammad's permission, citing 2:57, 3:50, 3:93, 4:160, 7:32 and 16:114. [6 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 5:4/q39/p4 The verse's concise sequence of food and hunting rules exhibits Qurʾānic eloquence. — Al-Bāqillānī cites its wisdom and composition as an example of inimitability alongside legal and theological passages. [5 notes: BAQILLANI]

## 5:4/q40 What inward lessons are drawn?  [* · inward]
turns on: How the verse is read as a lesson about faculties and intentions.
- 5:4/q40/p1 Trained faculties should acquire virtues for God, not their own appetites. — Al-Qāshānī reads good things as divine realities and true knowledge, hunting animals as senses and faculties, training as moral sciences, owner-directed holding as selfless intention, naming as inward orientation, and swift reckoning as immediate imprint on the soul. [6 notes: QASHANI]
- 5:4/q40/p2 A trained dog's surrender models the servant's complete dedication; lawful food preserves the heart. — Al-Qushayrī says forbidden food hardens and alienates the heart while lawful food brightens it; good manners raise the lowly, and heedless eating is disapproved. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 5:4/q40/p3 Spiritual goodness is whatever does not interrupt the path to God. — Al-Bursawī contrasts ego-directed consumption, clothing, speech and thought with obligations purified by witnessing; his immediate reckoning yields intimacy or estrangement, and he cites a maxim and al-Sāʾib's couplet. [8 notes: BURSEVI(الصائب,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 5:4/q41 What other legal or textual details are attached to this verse?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: How ancillary reports, manuscript details, and general examples are attached.
- 5:4/q41/p1 Knowledge gives the trained animal and knowledgeable person higher standing. — Al-Qurṭubī quotes ʿAlī that one's value lies in what one knows; al-Kashshāf advises students to seek expert teachers even by long travel, a point repeated by later authors. [10 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام,صاحب الكشاف); IBNASHUR-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب)]
- 5:4/q41/p2 The passage permits hunting for livelihood and frames it as lawful support for dependents. — The ʿUrfuṭa report says earlier messengers hunted and earning for one's family is striving; al-Qurṭubī nonetheless forbids purposeless killing. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL(صفوان بن أمية); BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(عرفطة بن نهيك التميمي); QURTUBI-FULL(الليث,مالك)]
- 5:4/q41/p3 The sūra contains many distinctive legal obligations, including trained game. — Abū Maysara counts eighteen; al-Qurṭubī as reported by Ibn ʿĀshūr counts nineteen, with food, purity, theft and testimony among them. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو ميسرة); IBNASHUR-FULL(القرطبي)]
- 5:4/q41/p4 The verse's hunting exception qualifies the carrion ban but sanctuary restrictions still govern sanctuary game. — Al-Zarkashī describes specification and reconciliation, citing game caught inside versus outside the sanctuary. [3 notes: BURHAN]
- 5:4/q41/p5 This verse was included in the Prophet's letter to ʿAmr ibn Ḥazm. — Ibn Shihāb reports the letter from the command to fulfill contracts through swift reckoning. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن شهاب)]
- 5:4/q41/p6 The word مما is written joined in the codex. — Ibn al-Jazarī lists this instance in his discussion of joined spellings; broader rasm principles govern regional readings and pauses. He notes joined wording for مما in 5:4, regional codex alignment, written rather than inferred omission at pauses, Hamza's eased pause, and assimilation in joined spellings. [5 notes: NASHR]

## 5:4/q42 What broader discussions appear in the collected passages?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Which collected passages are direct commentary and which are general excursuses.
- 5:4/q42/p1 General literary theory explains common poetic themes and analogies without treating them as theft. — Al-Jurjānī distinguishes common praise and demonstrations by lion or sea analogy from borrowing. [5 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 5:4/q42/p2 A prologue says this sūra can yield ten thousand questions and surveys refuge, sects and doctrine. — Al-Rāzī cites the seventy-three-sect report as well known and reasons that understanding evils sought refuge from requires extensive knowledge. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(النبي)]
- 5:4/q42/p3 An excursus analyzes verbal and mental speech and divine speech. — Al-Ālūsī distinguishes act and product, internal speech, and eternal divine speech, citing a report about inward thought and a sacred hadith. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أم سلمة)]
- 5:4/q42/p4 The grammatical discussion of مِن compares a separate hail verse. — Al-Akhfash discusses two readings of 24:43: mountains of hail sent down, or mountains of hail existing in the sky. [2 notes: AKHFASH(بعضهم)]
