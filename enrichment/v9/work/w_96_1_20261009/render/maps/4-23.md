# 4:23: 41 questions

## 4:23/q01 What act does the opening prohibition forbid?  [حُرِّمَتْ عَلَيْكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ordinary usage supplies an omitted marriage act or leaves the object ambiguous.
- 4:23/q01/p1 Marriage to the listed women is forbidden. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, and others supply marriage from the preceding verse or ordinary use of prohibitions attached to persons; food prohibitions similarly imply eating. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL(الكرخي)-; ITQAN-]
- 4:23/q01/p2 The construction is ambiguous until the prohibited act is supplied. — Al-Karkhī says prohibition attaches to acts, not persons, and the object is unstated; al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī reject ambiguity because context supplies marriage. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(الكرخي); ITQAN; RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 4:23/q01/p3 The omitted act covers sexual enjoyment generally. — The alternative in al-Itqān says customary language points to intercourse and similar acts; al-Biqāʿī speaks of marriage and ownership. [2 notes: ITQAN; BIQAI-FULL]

## 4:23/q02 How are the prohibited relations classified?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the seventh nonblood category is married women, fathers’ wives, or combining sisters.
- 4:23/q02/p1 Seven blood relations and seven other prohibitions are counted. — Ibn ʿAbbās and later commentators count mothers through nieces as seven by lineage; the others involve nursing and affinity. Al-Baghawī counts married women, while others include fathers’ wives and sister combination. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,عمرو بن سالم); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عابس,عمرو بن سالم مولى الأنصار); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; TABATABAI]
- 4:23/q02/p2 The later list is contingent, unlike permanently barred blood kin. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, and al-Ṭabrisī distinguish blood prohibition from causes arising through nursing, marriage, or combination. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون وأهل العلم); RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); TABRISI]

## 4:23/q03 Whom do 'your mothers' include?  [أُمَّهَٰتُكُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether the word itself literally reaches grandmothers or their prohibition rests on consensus or implication.
- 4:23/q03/p1 Mothers and grandmothers through either parent at every generation are forbidden. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, and others extend the line through birth. Al-Ālūsī argues 'mother' denotes an origin; he also adduces a fortiori reasoning from prohibited aunts. [16 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 4:23/q03/p2 Grandmothers are forbidden, but the verse may name only the immediate mother literally. — Al-Rāzī lays out univocal, homonymous, and figurative analyses, with consensus supplying the rule if the word is restricted. Abū Ḥayyān repeats these alternatives. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:23/q04 What does the plural 'ummahāt' derive from?  [أُمَّهَٰتُكُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the hāʾ is original or added.
- 4:23/q04/p1 The hāʾ is an augment, often distinguishing human mothers. — Al-Wāḥidī favors augmentation because hāʾ is added rather than deleted; al-Rāʿī uses amāt for animals. Al-Samīn notes human exceptions, and Ibn ʿĀshūr cites rare singular and plural forms. [19 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الراعي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن كيسان,الراعي,جرير,قصي بن كلاب); RAZI-FULL(الراعي)]
- 4:23/q04/p2 The hāʾ belongs to an older singular ummah or umahah. — Al-Naḥḥās, Abū Bakr, al-Wāḥidī, and later authorities cite ummahatī and the verb taʾammah as evidence; al-Wāḥidī balances the contrary noun umūma. [14 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر,أبو علي); JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(شاعر)]

## 4:23/q05 Whom do 'your daughters' include?  [وَبَنَاتُكُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether descendant daughterhood is literal or legally extended.
- 4:23/q05/p1 Every female descendant through sons or daughters, however remote, is included. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī, and others extend the birth line both ways. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses equal legal descent through daughters; al-Ālūsī adds inference from nieces. [20 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 4:23/q05/p2 Some restrict literal sonship through daughters to the paternal line. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the linguistic claim and two poems; he rejects treating social kinship as merely lexical. [7 notes: TABATABAI(الآخر,بعضهم,قائل)]

## 4:23/q06 Is a daughter conceived through fornication forbidden to her biological father?  [وَبَنَاتُكُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether biological descent or legally established lineage governs the word daughter.
- 4:23/q06/p1 She is forbidden as a biological daughter. — Most jurists, including Abū Ḥanīfa, Mālik, and Aḥmad, include her. Al-Ālūsī distinguishes real filiation from judicial proof and inheritance, invoking part-whole bodily relation. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL(الشافعي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(جمهور العلماء); ALUSI-FULL(البحر); STUDYQURAN(all other schools of law); ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL(الشافعي,النبي)-]
- 4:23/q06/p2 She is not legally a daughter and may be married under this rule. — Al-Shāfiʿī cites the marital-bed hadith, denial of inheritance and guardianship, and the inability to claim legal lineage. Al-Ālūsī reports and rebuts these arguments. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL(الشافعي,النبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الشافعي); ALUSI-FULL(الشافعي,بعض الشافعية,بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(al-Shāfiʿī); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 4:23/q07 Which sisters are forbidden?  [وَأَخَوَٰتُكُمْ · law]
- 4:23/q07/p1 Full, paternal half, and maternal half-sisters are included. — The listed commentators take the shared parent in either line to establish sisterhood. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 4:23/q08 Which aunts are forbidden?  [وَعَمَّٰتُكُمْ وَخَٰلَٰتُكُمْ · law]
turns on: How auntship follows male or female ancestors, including cross-line cases.
- 4:23/q08/p1 Sisters of male ancestors are paternal aunts; sisters of female ancestors are maternal aunts, however remote. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī expressly include maternal grandfathers' sisters and paternal grandmothers' sisters; al-Ālūsī distinguishes an aunt of an aunt by the connecting blood line. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الخانية والمحيط,المحيط); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 4:23/q09 Which nieces are forbidden?  [وَبَنَاتُ ٱلْأَخِ وَبَنَاتُ ٱلْأُخْتِ · law]
- 4:23/q09/p1 Daughters and further female descendants of every brother or sister, including half-siblings, are forbidden. — Al-Wāḥidī compares their scope to daughters; Abū Ḥayyān explains the singular sibling nouns as generic. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 4:23/q10 What does nursing establish between the child and a family?  [وَأُمَّهَٰتُكُمُ ٱلَّٰتِىٓ أَرْضَعْنَكُمْ وَأَخَوَٰتُكُم مِّنَ ٱلرَّضَٰعَةِ · law]
turns on: Whether the milk-producing husband is included, and how far the lineage analogy extends.
- 4:23/q10/p1 Nursing reproduces the seven lineage prohibitions through nurse and milk father. — The Prophetic rule is repeatedly transmitted; al-Rāzī sees mothers and sisters as birth and sibling examples that imply the rest. Commentators trace grandparents, aunts, and siblings through the nurse and husband. [46 notes: IBNABIHATIM(رسول الله); THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أولو التحقيق من ذوي العلم,عائشة); BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(النبي); TABATABAI(النبي); STUDYQURAN]
- 4:23/q10/p2 The milk father does not generate kinship prohibition. — Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib, al-Nakhaʿī, and Abū Salama deny transmission through the husband. Al-Qurṭubī says milk comes from the woman, while the father only caused conception. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب وإبراهيم النخعي وأبو سلمة بن عبد الرحمن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض السلف)]
- 4:23/q10/p3 The milk father does generate corresponding paternal kinship. — The majority attribute milk to the husband whose child caused it; the four Sunni schools and several commentators apply the lineage hadith. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور); IBNKATHIR-FULL(جمهور الأئمة الأربعة); WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 4:23/q11 What exceptions are claimed to the nursing-lineage analogy?  [وَأُمَّهَٰتُكُمُ ٱلَّٰتِىٓ أَرْضَعْنَكُمْ وَأَخَوَٰتُكُم مِّنَ ٱلرَّضَٰعَةِ · law]
turns on: Whether apparent exceptions concern affinity rather than lineage.
- 4:23/q11/p1 A man may marry his milk son's sister or his milk brother's mother. — Al-Zamakhsharī and Abū Ḥayyān say the blood prohibitions in these cases come from sexual affinity absent in nursing; al-Bayḍāwī makes the same distinction. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 4:23/q11/p2 The analogy has no true lineage exceptions. — Ibn Kathīr rejects lists of four or six purported exceptions because their examples involve affinity or already exist in blood kinship; investigators cited by al-Ālūsī agree. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(كثير من المحققين)]
- 4:23/q11/p3 Numerous exceptions exist. — Al-Baḥr is reported by al-Ālūsī to count eighty-one; the note does not list them. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(البحر)]

## 4:23/q12 How much nursing creates a marriage bar?  [وَأُمَّهَٰتُكُمُ ٱلَّٰتِىٓ أَرْضَعْنَكُمْ وَأَخَوَٰتُكُم مِّنَ ٱلرَّضَٰعَةِ · law]
turns on: The verse's unqualified nursing wording against reports about suckling counts and abrogation.
- 4:23/q12/p1 Any confirmed nursing, even one swallowed feed, suffices. — Mālik and others appeal to the verse's general wording and minimum nursing sense. Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn ʿUmar, Ibn Masʿūd, and al-Ālūsī treat one-or-two denial as superseded; al-Qurṭubī cites Medinan practice. [21 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(حنبل عن أحمد، عمر، علي، ابن عباس، ابن عمر، الحسن، طاووس، الشعبي، النخعي، الزهري، الأوزاعي، الثوري، مالك، أبو حنيفة وأصحابه); TABRISI(أبو حنيفة وابن عمر وابن عباس ومالك والأوزاعي); RAZI-FULL(أبو بكر الرازي,ابن عمر); QURTUBI-FULL(أئمة الفتوى,الليث بن سعد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مالك); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عمر,طاوس,علي وعبد الله بن مسعود,قيل); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عمر,ابن مسعود,النووي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müctehidlerin çoğu); ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 4:23/q12/p2 At least three feeds are required. — The Prophetic denial of one or two sucks supports Aisha, Ibn al-Zubayr, Dāwūd, Aḥmad, and hadith authorities; al-Qurṭubī favors the report while noting uncertainty over milk reaching the stomach. [11 notes: THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(محمد بن العباس عن أحمد); QURTUBI-FULL(داود,عائشة وابن الزبير; أحمد وإسحاق وأبو ثور وأبو عبيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أم الفضل,عائشة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو ثور وإبن المنذر وداؤد وأبو عبيد,قيل); STUDYQURAN(a more widely transmitted report); QURTUBI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL(الطحاوي)-]
- 4:23/q12/p3 Five separate feeds are required. — Aisha's account reduces ten revealed feeds to five; al-Shāfiʿī and some Aḥmad reports adopt it. Al-Ṭabrisī's school differs with its fifteen-feed or day-and-night measure. [22 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI(الشافعي وعائشة وسعيد بن جبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو الحارث عن أحمد، الشافعي); RAZI-FULL(الشافعي); QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي,حفصة; الشافعي; إسحاق,عائشة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سهلة بنت سهيل,عائشة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عائشة); ALUSI-FULL(أحمد,أصحاب الشافعي,الشافعي,النبي,عائشة); STUDYQURAN(al-Shāfiʿī,ʿĀʾishah)]
- 4:23/q12/p4 Ten or fifteen sessions, or nutritive effect, are required. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports ten, fifteen or a full day and night; Tawus and other reports preserve ten, while a seven-feed report is also ascribed to Aisha. [8 notes: TABRISI(أصحابنا,بعض أصحابنا); QURTUBI-FULL(حفصة; الشافعي; إسحاق,طائفة,عائشة); DURR-FULL(طاوس,عائشة); STUDYQURAN(Twelver Shiite school)]

## 4:23/q13 At what age does nursing still create a marriage bar?  [وَأُمَّهَٰتُكُمُ ٱلَّٰتِىٓ أَرْضَعْنَكُمْ وَأَخَوَٰتُكُم مِّنَ ٱلرَّضَٰعَةِ · law]
turns on: Whether nourishment before weaning, a fixed two years, later months, or adult nursing controls.
- 4:23/q13/p1 The limit is two full years or infancy before effective weaning. — The majority cite 2:233 and reports that nursing is from hunger; Ibn ʿĀshūr makes nutritive dependence central and treats the Salim case as special. [25 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(النبي); TABRISI(أصحابنا والشافعي وأبو يوسف ومحمد); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(جمهور العلماء); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود وابن عباس وابن عمر وأبو هريرة,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو موسى الأشعري وإبن مسعود,النبي,صاحبا أبي حنيفة,عمر بن الخطاب); IBNASHUR-FULL(أمهات المؤمنين,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عمر بن الخطاب، وابن مسعود، وابن عباس، والزهري، ومالك، والشافعي، وأحمد، والأوزاعي، والثوري، وأبو يوسف); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müctehid ve müfessirlerin çoğu,Peygamber’in diğer hanımları (ümmehâtü’l-mü’minîn) ve müctehidlerin çoğunluğu)]
- 4:23/q13/p2 Abū Ḥanīfa allows thirty months. — Reports in al-Thaʿlabī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Ālūsī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr give two and a half years. [5 notes: THALABI; TABRISI(أبو حنيفة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة; مالك; زفر; الأوزاعي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو حنيفة)]
- 4:23/q13/p3 Mālik is reported to allow some period beyond two years. — Versions range from days or one month to two or three months or continued need for milk; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports distinct transmissions. [7 notes: THALABI; TABRISI(مالك); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة; مالك; زفر; الأوزاعي); ALUSI-FULL(مالك); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن القاسم,ابن عبد الحكم,الوليد بن مسلم)]
- 4:23/q13/p4 Adult nursing can establish the relation. — Aisha, al-Layth, and Dāwūd cite the adult Salim dispensation; other wives and the majority treat it as specific, while Abū Mūsā reportedly retracted. [14 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو موسى الأشعري,الليث بن سعد; عائشة,عائشة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سهلة بنت سهيل); ALUSI-FULL(أم سلمة وسائر أزواج النبي,داؤد,عائشة,كثير من العلماء); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو موسى الأشعري,الليث بن سعد,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عائشة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hz. Âişe)]

## 4:23/q14 What manner of milk delivery counts as nursing?  [وَأُمَّهَٰتُكُمُ ٱلَّٰتِىٓ أَرْضَعْنَكُمْ وَأَخَوَٰتُكُم مِّنَ ٱلرَّضَٰعَةِ · law]
turns on: Whether milk must pass from the breast by the mouth, or other delivery reaching the stomach suffices.
- 4:23/q14/p1 Milk reaching the infant's stomach by mouth or nose counts even without sucking. — Al-Ālūsī and al-Khāniya define the operative cause as human milk reaching the stomach and exclude ear, wound, or enema delivery and uncertain intake. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الخانية)]
- 4:23/q14/p2 Only ordinary breast-to-mouth feeding counts. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports his school's exclusion of poured, nasal, enema, and dead-woman's milk. [1 notes: TABRISI(أصحابنا)]

## 4:23/q15 When does a wife's mother become forbidden?  [وَأُمَّهَٰتُ نِسَآئِكُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether the later consummation condition reaches mothers-in-law as well as stepdaughters.
- 4:23/q15/p1 A valid marriage contract with her daughter forbids her, regardless of consummation. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, and most commentators cite the unqualified clause, syntax, consensus, and the Prophetic distinction between mother and stepdaughter; Ibn Masʿūd reportedly withdrew his contrary view. [79 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن والزهري,طاوس,عمران بن حصين,مسروق بن الأجدع); TAB-FULL(النبي,عطاء,علي); ZAJJAJ(أبو العباس محمد بن يزيد,ابن عباس,بعضهم); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,علي); WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم,عبد الله بن مسعود,عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عمر وعمران بن الحصين,مسروق); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور أهل العلم,زيد بن ثابت); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الأكثرين من الصحابة والتابعين); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,جمهور السلف,زيد بن ثابت,عطاء); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عمر); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,زيد بن ثابت,عطاء,عمران بن حصين); BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام); ALUSI-FULL(إبن عباس,إبن مسعود,النبي,زيد بن ثابت); TABATABAI(الصادق عن منصور بن حازم,النبي عن عمرو بن شعيب عن أبيه عن جده); STUDYQURAN(community consensus); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن المنذر)+; RAZI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+; TABATABAI+; WAHIDI-BASIT(النبي)+; RAZI-FULL(جمع من الصحابة,علي وزيد وابن عمر وابن الزبير وجابر وابن عباس)-]
- 4:23/q15/p2 Consummation with the daughter is required. — Reports attribute this to ʿAlī, Zayd, Ibn ʿUmar, Ibn al-Zubayr, Jābir, Ibn ʿAbbās, and Mujāhid; they apply the one later condition to both clauses. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers it on parallel rationale. - 4:23/q15/p3 Zayd distinguishes divorce before consummation from death. — In reports through al-Ṭabarī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr, and others, divorce permits the mother but death with inheritance bars her; al-Rāzī analogizes death to consummation for waiting-period rules. ## 4:23/q16 How does tanwin behave in compound vocatives?  [* · grammar]
- 4:23/q16/p1 It remains inside an incomplete vocative phrase, but may drop at a completed name's end. — Sībawayh compares compound numerals with participial and comparative vocatives, and distinguishes annexation to an indefinite noun. [5 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 4:23/q17 How do verbal nouns govern their arguments?  [* · grammar]
- 4:23/q17/p1 Verbal nouns can govern objects, agents, and adverbials with verbal force, while tanwin and annexation affect case. — Sībawayh cites 90:14-15, Ruʾbah, al-Murār al-Asadī, and other verse and poetry. He rejects one adjectival analogy that al-Khalīl accepts. [10 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]

## 4:23/q18 How can an accusative verbal noun emphasize an assertion?  [* · grammar]

## 4:23/q25 Does illicit intercourse establish marriage prohibitions by affinity?  [ٱلَّٰتِى دَخَلْتُم بِهِنَّ · law]

## 4:23/q26 When may a man marry the daughter of an unconsummated former wife?  [فَإِن لَّمْ تَكُونُوا۟ دَخَلْتُم بِهِنَّ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ · law]

- 4:23/q31/p1 The infinitive clause is nominative and coordinated with the prohibited list. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Zajjāj, al-Naḥḥās, al-Wāḥidī, and many later grammarians paraphrase it as the act of combining sisters being forbidden. ## 4:23/q33 May a man have intercourse with two owned sisters?  [وَأَن تَجْمَعُوا۟ بَيْنَ ٱلْأُخْتَيْنِ · law]
turns on: Whether the marriage prohibition restricts the owned-women permission of 4:24.
- 4:23/q33/p1 No; joint ownership is possible but sexual combination is forbidden. — Most jurists apply the verse's general ban or its rationale to ownership; after intercourse with one, the other is barred until the first is effectively relinquished. ## 4:23/q34 How must an owner make the first sister unavailable before approaching the second?  [وَأَن تَجْمَعُوا۟ بَيْنَ ٱلْأُخْتَيْنِ · law]
turns on: Whether intention or transfer, and what happens if ownership returns.
- 4:23/q34/p1 Dispose of ownership or create a firm bar before switching. — Sale, emancipation, gift, manumission contract, or marriage are cited. Mālik initially trusts the owner's abstention, but suspends both after he approaches both; al-Qurṭubī prefers a valid immediate bar despite possible return. [17 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب وابن عمر والحسن البصري والأوزاعي والشافعي وأحمد وإسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL(علي وابن عمر والحسن البصري والأوزاعي والشافعي وأحمد وإسحاق,مالك); IBNKATHIR-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); ALUSI-FULL(علي); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله,علي بن أبي طالب); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 4:23/q34/p2 Intention, abstention, and completion of a waiting period suffice. — Qatāda permits approaching the second after intending the first forbidden and waiting out her period. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 4:23/q34/p3 Neither may be approached while both remain owned. — Al-Ḥakam, Ḥammād, and a report from al-Nakhaʿī take joint ownership itself to suspend access. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الحكم وحماد والنخعي)]

## 4:23/q35 May a man marry his former wife's sister during her waiting period?  [وَأَن تَجْمَعُوا۟ بَيْنَ ٱلْأُخْتَيْنِ · law]
turns on: Whether irrevocable divorce ends the combining bar immediately.
- 4:23/q35/p1 After irrevocable divorce, he may marry her sister immediately. — ʿAṭāʾ, Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyab, al-Shāfiʿī and others allow it; al-Qurṭubī says this is the adopted position. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن المنذر,عطاء وسعيد بن المسيب والحسن والقاسم وعروة بن الزبير وابن أبي ليلى والشافعي وأبو ثور وأبو عبيد); BIQAI-FULL]
- 4:23/q35/p2 He must wait even after irrevocable divorce. — ʿAlī, Zayd, Mujāhid, al-Thawrī, Aḥmad, and legal rationalists require her waiting period to end; all agree in a revocable divorce. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(علي وزيد بن ثابت ومجاهد وعطاء والنخعي والثوري وأحمد وأصحاب الرأي)]

## 4:23/q36 What does 'except what has passed' cover?  [إِلَّا مَا قَدْ سَلَفَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether it pardons earlier acts, preserves pre-Islamic contracts, or points to earlier law.
- 4:23/q36/p1 Earlier forbidden combinations are pardoned but may not continue. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, and most commentators treat the exception as disconnected. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes that legitimate children and kinship from past unions remain recognized. [32 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(عطاء بن أبي رباح); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو بكر,ابن عباس,ابن عباس في رواية عطاء والكلبي وأبو عبيدة وقطرب وابن الأنباري,الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(النبي); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو يعلى)-]
- 4:23/q36/p2 A pre-Islamic union may stand with the husband choosing one sister. — Abū Yaʿlā and al-Qurṭubī report a grandfathering construction; Fayrūz al-Daylamī's hadith orders choosing or divorcing one, while al-Samīn rejects treating the exception as connected. [12 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو يعلى); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو خراش الرعيني,الديلمي,الضحاك بن فيروز); SAMIN-DURR; DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL(القرطبي); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet); SAMIN-DURR-]

- 4:23/q36/p4 The exception's grammar is 'after' or implies exemption from accounting. — Al-Mufaḍḍal construes illā as after, al-Ḥasan al-Jurjānī compares 44:56, and al-Akhfash posits an omitted accountability noun. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش,الحسن بن يحيى الجرجاني,المفضل)]

## 4:23/q37 What do 'forgiving, merciful' explain here?  [إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ كَانَ غَفُورًۭا رَّحِيمًۭا · interpretation]
- 4:23/q37/p1 God pardons conduct preceding revelation and shows mercy to those who cease it. — Al-Ṭabarī and others connect the close to the past exception; Ibn Abī Ḥātim records separate glosses of forgiveness for grave or past sins and mercy toward servants. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير,قتادة); TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]

## 4:23/q38 Why is the list ordered as it is?  [* · rhetoric]
- 4:23/q38/p1 The sequence moves from closest blood kin to nursing and then affinity. — Al-Bāqillānī begins with mothers for their unique inviolability, sets milk sisters after milk mothers, and locates eloquence in arrangement and legal wisdom; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī describe the transition. [9 notes: BAQILLANI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 4:23/q40 Where may a reciter pause in this long list?  [* · readings]
- 4:23/q40/p1 A pause at an internal blood-kin boundary is possible, while verse-end or fuller stops may be preferable. — Al-Zarkashī and al-Suyūṭī distinguish complete and sufficient stops and place a boundary after the nieces; Ibn al-Jazarī allows a licensed stop within a long passage.
