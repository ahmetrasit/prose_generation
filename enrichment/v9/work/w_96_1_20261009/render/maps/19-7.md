# 19:7: 24 questions

## 19:7/q01 Who delivered the announcement to Zachariah?  [يَٰزَكَرِيَّآ إِنَّا نُبَشِّرُكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether direct divine speech, the angelic report of 3:39, or both are intended.
- 19:7/q01/p1 God addressed Zachariah directly. — Al-Razi cites the surrounding direct address and says 'your Lord has said' can be God's own speech; the majority take this view. Ibn Ashur introduces the words as God's speech. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(الأكثرون); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭs)]
- 19:7/q01/p2 An angel or angels delivered God's announcement. — Muqatil names Gabriel during prayer. Al-Razi and others cite 3:39 and 'your Lord has said'; Bursevi reads the opening as angelic speech; Alusi says an angel conveys divine wording, comparing 39:53. [7 notes: MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL(آخرون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭs)]
- 19:7/q01/p3 Both God and the angels may have announced it. — Al-Razi reconciles Maryam's apparent divine call with the explicit angelic announcement in 3:39 by allowing both. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 19:7/q02 How does this announcement follow Zachariah's prayer?  [يَٰزَكَرِيَّآ إِنَّا نُبَشِّرُكَ · links]
- 19:7/q02/p1 An unspoken clause supplies God's answer to the prayer, fulfilled by the promise of a son. — Majaz supplies 'so We said'; Jishumi an answer and call; Ibn Kathir compares 3:38–39, Tabatabai 21:90, and Kuranyolu 21:90. Alusi says the promise itself counts as a prompt answer despite any delay. Mawardi and Qurtubi find honor in the answer, son, and God's naming. [19 notes: MAJAZ; MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 19:7/q03 When did the announcement and birth occur after the prayer?  [إِنَّا نُبَشِّرُكَ · reports]
turns on: Reports differ on the interval between prayer, announcement, and birth.
- 19:7/q03/p1 The call came promptly and the birth within months. — Abu Hayyan reports an immediate call; Alusi calls birth within months the common account. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:7/q03/p2 The child came forty years later. — Abu Hayyan and Alusi relay this alternative delay. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:7/q03/p3 The child came sixty years later. — Abu Hayyan and Alusi relay this second alternative delay. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:7/q04 How is 'O Zachariah' read and construed?  [يَٰزَكَرِيَّآ · readings]
turns on: The reports differ in hamza treatment and connection to the following word.
- 19:7/q04/p1 The name has an extended form Zakariyyāʾ, a final-yāʾ-sukūn form Zakariyyā, and a shortened Zakrā form. — Majaz compares the shortened form with Bukhtā. One recitation softens the first hamza and realizes the second; Abu Amr follows that pattern. Other Arabs reverse the treatment, according to speakers heard by Sibawayh. [3 notes: MAJAZ; SIBAWAYH(العرب)]
- 19:7/q04/p2 Adding a hamza supplies case inflection and continuity with 'innā'. — Qiraat-ER also records a form with a marked pause or phonological break before 'innā'. [2 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 19:7/q05 How is 'nubašširuka' read, and what does its form convey?  [نُبَشِّرُكَ · readings]
turns on: Form I versus doubled Form II and the rhetorical force of intensification.
- 19:7/q05/p1 The doubled Form II reading intensifies the glad tidings. — Most readers use the doubled sh, according to Ibn Atiyya and the Nashr; Biqai says it suits news extraordinary enough to be denied. Nashr lists its occurrence alongside other loci. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASHR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 19:7/q05/p2 The lighter Form I reading gives the same announcement without intensification. — Ibn Atiyya associates it with Ibn Masud's readers, with still b and damma on sh; Ibn Jawzi and Nasafi name Hamza. Nashr names Hamza and al-Kisai for the relevant occurrences; Qiraat-ER describes the Form I substitution. Nashr also notes a separate five-locus Prophet-address dispute and the Maryam exception for Hamza, al-Kisai, Khalaf, and Hafs. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(حمزة); NASAFI-FULL(حمزة); NASHR(نافع); QIRAAT-ER]

## 19:7/q06 Who is the promised 'ghulām' and what does the word denote?  [بِغُلَٰمٍ · referent]
- 19:7/q06/p1 It is a male child, Yahya son of Zachariah. — Baghawi, Abu Hayyan, and Alusi gloss ghulam as male child; Abu Hayyan and Alusi note feminine ghulama also exists. Damghani identifies the boy, and Jishumi says he will be born to Zachariah. [6 notes: DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 19:7/q07 How does 'his name is Yahya' function grammatically and as speech?  [ٱسْمُهُۥ يَحْيَىٰ · grammar]
- 19:7/q07/p1 'Yahya' is the predicate of 'his name'; the nominal clause describes the boy. — Nahhas gives the predicate analysis; Tabrisi and Samin say 'ismuhu Yahya' modifies ghulam. Damghani notes ism can denote the act of naming. Ibn Ashur sees the report as an implicit command to give this name. [5 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; DAMGHANI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 19:7/q08 What is the linguistic origin and form of the name Yahya?  [يَحْيَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the proper name is borrowed or transferred from an Arabic present-tense verb.
- 19:7/q08/p1 It is probably a foreign proper name without Arabic derivation. — Baydawi and Samin prefer this; Samin says properness and foreignness make it diptote. Abu Hayyan calls it apparently non-Arabic; Alusi considers this strongest because Arabs did not usually name children with Arabic words. Razi warns that proper names need only identify. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 19:7/q08/p2 If Arabic, it is a verb transferred into a proper name. — Nahhas says its origin as an imperfect verb explains indeclinability and yāʾ spelling distinguishes it from the verb. Kashshaf, Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, and Samin compare Yaʿmar, Yaʿīsh, Yaʿmal, and Yamut. Alusi says Arabic and foreign analyses have different meters and diminutives. [7 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:7/q09 Why was the boy called Yahya?  [يَحْيَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Which aspect of life, if any, the proper name is taken to describe.
- 19:7/q09/p1 God gave him spiritual life through faith, obedience, knowledge, and wisdom. — Qatada speaks of faith or prophethood and faith; Zajjaj and Wahidi speak of knowledge and wisdom. Razi invokes 6:122 and 8:24; Jishumi adds 8:24 and 3:169 for the obedient as living. Qushayri mentions the household's worship and prophecy. Asad says he is spiritually alive and remembered. [14 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); RAZI-FULL(قتادة); JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); RAZI-FULL-]
- 19:7/q09/p2 His birth brought life to a barren womb or came from aged and infertile parents. — Muqatil names the aged father and barren mother; Ibn Khalawayh says both past ninety and despairing. Qushayri extends revival to lineage and memory. Qurtubi objects that the mother was barren, not merely old. Razi reports Ibn Abbas via Thalabi. Baydawi and Abu Hayyan mention the revived womb. [12 notes: MUQATIL; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ); RAZI-FULL-]
- 19:7/q09/p3 Religion or other people came alive through his mission. — Jishumi says faith would live through him and uses 6:122 and 8:24; Ibn Atiyya says his guidance enlivened people. Razi says Zachariah sought him for religion; Baydawi names the revival of God's religion; StudyQuran mentions Zachariah's faith. [10 notes: QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); RAZI-FULL-]
- 19:7/q09/p4 The name points to his life without sin. — Jishumi says the obedient are living and the disobedient dead. Junayd says Yahya lived by obedience without blame; one report says he never sinned or intended sin. [2 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد); JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL-]
- 19:7/q09/p5 His martyrdom leaves him alive with his Lord. — Abu al-Qasim ibn Habib and later reports apply the life of martyrs to Yahya. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو القاسم بن حبيب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]
- 19:7/q09/p6 It predicts a long life or survival past infancy. — Jishumi mentions survival past infancy; Abu Hayyan gives long life among proposed senses. [3 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]
- 19:7/q09/p7 The name recalls that he first believed in Jesus. — Razi relays a story of the unborn Yahya bowing toward Jesus in Mary's womb. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]
- 19:7/q09/p8 The name derives from a change to Sarah's name or a letter transferred from it. — Amr al-Maqdisi tells of Sarah lending a letter to the servant Hayy; an Ibn Abbas report changes Yisara's yāʾ to ḥāʾ. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(عمرو بن عبد الله المقدسي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL-]
- 19:7/q09/p9 A descriptive etymology need not be assigned to a proper name. — Razi rejects the preceding explanations because a proper name can simply identify its bearer. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+]

## 19:7/q10 What honor or lesson lies in God naming Yahya before birth?  [ٱسْمُهُۥ يَحْيَىٰ · interpretation]
- 19:7/q10/p1 God's choice of his name before birth specially honors Yahya and Zachariah. — Mawardi counts the name with answered prayer and son as divine election; Wahidi says parents usually name children. Tabatabai compares Jesus at 3:45; Asad calls it a divine promise. Biqai says the name was reserved; Bursevi's inward source adds the direct call by name. [15 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس عن عكرمة وقتادة والكلبي وابن جريج والسدي وابن زيد والقتبي); TABRISI(قتادة وابن جريج والسدي وابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة وابن أسلم والسدي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭs,Ṭs)]
- 19:7/q10/p2 Distinctive, refined names deserve preference. — Kashshaf cites a poem praising striking names and red robes and Ru'ba's answer to al-Bakri about his lineage. Qurtubi says such names are memorable and avoid mocking nicknames. Abu Hayyan adds Ru'ba's verse and al-Salt ibn Ata's account of advancing among the Barmakids. Ibn Ashur says the novel name distinguishes the child and becomes a model. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رؤبة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,الصلت بن عطاء,رؤبة); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 19:7/q10/p3 A name can be newly assigned without prior convention. — Jishumi infers this from the unprecedented name. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 19:7/q10/p4 Naming before birth is possible for several figures. — Amr ibn Murrah lists Muhammad/Ahmad, Yahya, Jesus, Isaac, and Jacob, citing 61:6, 3:45, and 11:71. Bursevi's inward source says God inspires each person's name before creation and cites Jesus's announcement of Ahmad. [2 notes: ITQAN(عمرو بن مرة); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 19:7/q10/p5 Naming Yahya before he existed shows the nonexistent can be known. — Jishumi draws this theological inference from the prior announcement. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 19:7/q11 What does 'no samiyyan before him' mean?  [لَمْ نَجْعَل لَّهُۥ مِن قَبْلُ سَمِيًّۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether samiyy is the literal bearer of the same name, a counterpart, a child, or a distinction in naming; counterpart claims also turn on whether special merit implies overall prophetic rank.
- 19:7/q11/p1 No one before him had borne the name Yahya. — Qatada, Ibn Abbas, Ibn Zayd, al-Suddi and many others give the literal namesake reading; Ibn Jarir, Razi, and commentators reported by Kuranyolu prefer it. Tabari derives samiyy from being named; Razi says 19:65 needs the figurative peer sense because of its worship context but this verse does not. Some say no prior prophet bore it; Nahhas says nobody had been instructed to give it. A report has the Prophet name a later child Yahya. [42 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,السدي,قتادة); ZAJJAJ(ابن عباس); NAHHAS; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; THALABI(قتادة والكلبي); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس عن عكرمة وقتادة والكلبي وابن جريج والسدي وابن زيد والقتبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(قتادة، السدي، ابن جريج، ابن زيد، الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة والكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة وابن عباس وابن أسلم والسدي); TABRISI(قتادة وابن جريج والسدي وابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن وسعيد بن جبير وعكرمة وقتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة وابن أسلم والسدي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة والسدي وابن أسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة وابن جريج وابن زيد); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عكرمة,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الطبري); STUDYQURAN(Ṭs); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; RAZI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+]
- 19:7/q11/p2 No one before him was his equal or likeness. — Mujahid, Said ibn Jubayr, Ata, and others compare 19:65; some derive the sense from elevation or matching. Proposed singular traits include never sinning or intending sin, leadership, continence, childhood wisdom (19:12), miraculous parentage, announcing Jesus, and his novel name. Ibn Ashur says no earlier prophet combined them and prefers this reading. Razi, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi and Kuranyolu object to any implication that Yahya outranks Abraham, Moses, or earlier prophets; Ibn Ashur answers that a special merit does not establish overall rank. Qushayri speaks of total virtues or sinlessness before and after prophethood. Bint al-Shati notes the Arabic counterpart sense. - 19:7/q11/p3 No barren woman before had borne a child like Yahya. — Ibn Abbas reports this exceptional parentage; Ibn Kathir stresses that Zachariah had no children and his wife was barren from youth, unlike Abraham and Sarah, citing 15:54 and 11:72–73. Some place the birth among Yahya's incomparable merits. [16 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(علي بن أبي طالب عن ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس عن الوالبي); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(علي بن أبي طلحة عن ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن أبي طلحة عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس)]
- 19:7/q11/p4 Zachariah had no son before Yahya. — Mujahid is cited for this by Mawardi. Ibn Khalawayh says the phrase can mean no child previously born to his parents; a later Ibn Abbas report pairs no prior son with the unused name and reads 19:65's samiyy as child. Bint al-Shati says she finds no such son sense in either Maryam verse. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BINTSHATI-IJAZ-]
- 19:7/q11/p5 His unique namesake status is that God named him before he existed. — Razi contrasts divine naming before birth with usual human naming after birth; StudyQuran relays the same explanation. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 19:7/q12 Why does the verse specify 'before'?  [مِن قَبْلُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the temporal limit anticipates someone later or only restricts an earlier comparison.
- 19:7/q12/p1 A greater figure, Muhammad, would be created afterward. — Thalabi and Qurtubi relay this explanation; Jishumi agrees Muhammad came later and was superior. [3 notes: THALABI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; JISHUMI]
- 19:7/q12/p2 It limits the claim to earlier namesakes or earlier matching qualities, without ranking every prophet. — Thalabi says only some qualities may be meant because earlier patriarchs were greater; Baghawi names Abraham and Moses. Jishumi says a name reading poses no rank issue. Ibn Ashur says special merit does not entail overall superiority. [5 notes: THALABI(قيل); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 19:7/q13 How are 'lam najʿal ... samiyyan' and samiyy formed grammatically?  [لَمْ نَجْعَل لَّهُۥ مِن قَبْلُ سَمِيًّۭا · grammar]
- 19:7/q13/p1 'Samiyyan' is the direct object and the clause describes the boy. — Jishumi identifies the object; Samin says the whole clause is a genitive adjective of ghulam. Samin derives samiyy on the pattern of radiyy from sumuww, supporting the Basran origin of ism; a derivation from wasm would yield wasim. [3 notes: JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR]
- 19:7/q13/p2 The negative 'lam najʿal' belongs to a Qur'anic rhetorical pattern about what God did not make. — Zarkashi compares ma jaʿala clauses and says these patterns signal whether renewal of the named matter is expected. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 19:7/q14 What reports describe Yahya's freedom from sin?  [يَحْيَىٰ سَمِيًّۭا · reports]
- 19:7/q14/p1 Yahya alone neither committed nor even intended sin. — Sulami quotes the Prophet without a grade; Razi reports it through Ikrima from Ibn Abbas, and Alusi says such reports are numerous. These reports support his claimed lack of a peer. [3 notes: SULAMI(النبي); RAZI-FULL(عكرمة عن ابن عباس عن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 19:7/q15 What reports connect Yahya with Husayn?  [يَحْيَىٰ سَمِيًّۭا · reports]
- 19:7/q15/p1 Husayn likewise had an unprecedented name and shared a martyr's fate. — Tabrisi reports Abu Abd Allah's saying that the sky reddened morning and evening for forty mornings and the killers of both men were illegitimate; StudyQuran describes the Shiite tradition of Husayn's name given by the Prophet and martyrdom under Yazid. Ali Zayn al-Abidin says Husayn recalled at every stop that Yahya's head was presented to an adulteress. [3 notes: TABRISI(أبو عبد الله,علي بن زيد عن علي بن الحسين); STUDYQURAN(Ṭs)]

## 19:7/q16 What does the Gospel account report about John's announcement and naming?  [إِنَّا نُبَشِّرُكَ ٱسْمُهُۥ يَحْيَىٰ · reports]
- 19:7/q16/p1 Gabriel announced John to the elderly, righteous priest Zechariah and Elizabeth; after birth both parents affirmed the name John. — Tabatabai reports the Gospel: Elizabeth is Aaronite and childless; the child will avoid wine, receive the Spirit, and prepare Israel in Elijah's spirit. Relatives object to the name, Zechariah writes it, regains speech, praises God and prophesies. [2 notes: TABATABAI(الإنجيل)]

## 19:7/q17 How does the Qur'an's account of Zachariah and Mary lead to this promise?  [* · links]
- 19:7/q17/p1 Zachariah's care of Mary and the provision he saw with her prompted his prayer for a righteous child despite barrenness. — Tabatabai cites 3:37–41 and 6:85, 6:87: Zachariah is a prophet and guardian of Mary after Imran's death. A report specifies out-of-season fruit. Tabatabai says even if accepted, the fruit explains hope, and inheritance cannot mean nonhereditary spiritual stations without evidence. Angels announced John during prayer and a three-day inability to speak became a sign. [5 notes: TABATABAI]
- 19:7/q17/p2 The two birth stories display divine creative power beyond ordinary bodily limits. — StudyQuran connects Mary, Zachariah, John and Jesus with 3:35, 3:62, 21:89, and 21:91. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 19:7/q18 What lesson does the answered prayer teach about supplication?  [* · interpretation]
- 19:7/q18/p1 Sincere need and prayer can be answered despite apparently hopeless circumstances. — Bursevi says God opens prayer as a sign mercy opens, wholly or partly; a report speaks of mercy's doors. Abu Yazid says poverty, helplessness, and need reach God; a gnostic calls prayer a weapon, fidelity a mount, and weeping an intercessor. Islahi stresses right timing, objective, feelings, and wording. Razi says a person may request without permission, or receive permission while the timing remains unknown; Biqai says Zachariah seeks circumstances and assurance rather than doubting. [7 notes: BURSEVI(أبو يزيد البسطامي,بعض أهل المعرفة,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 19:7/q18/p2 The best prayer seeks a righteous heir of knowledge and religious service. — Bursevi says worldly order needs knowledge, action, piety, justice and fairness; the perfect person in every age manifests divine light, and a seeker should request an enduring, worthy child. Razi says Zachariah's request served religion. [3 notes: BURSEVI; RAZI-FULL]
- 19:7/q18/p3 A supplicant should address God as 'My Lord'. — Razi draws this practice from Zachariah's address. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 19:7/q19 How does John's birth fit the sura's argument about Jesus?  [* · links]
- 19:7/q19/p1 John's birth to an old man and barren woman introduces Mary's story and refutes the inference that Jesus's miraculous birth makes him divine. — Islahi says John neither claimed divinity nor was made a god; the sura also presents prophets' monotheism, prayer, and zakah against later followers' innovations, counsels Muhammad to persevere against opponents mistaking respite for vindication, and addresses the People of the Book amid their support of Quraysh. Abu Hayyan links this sura's wondrous births with the previous sura's Cave, Moses-Khidr and Dhu al-Qarnayn stories. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 19:7/q19/p2 The parallel in Al Imran gives a fuller announcement and correspondingly fuller gratitude and sign. — Samarrai says 3:39 has more attributes; 3:41 specifies three days, with clearer daytime remembrance, versus Maryam's three nights at 19:10. In Al Imran Zachariah himself is told to remember God, while in 19:11 he asks his people to praise. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 19:7/q20 What inward meanings are drawn from the promised boy and his name?  [* · inward]
- 19:7/q20/p1 The boy is the heart, born of spirit and soul, and Yahya names its enduring spiritual life. — Qashani says Zachariah seeks a sign to reach this heart; it receives wisdom and the book of discriminating intellect. Silence avoids sensory concerns; faculties continue disciplined worship. Divine tenderness, purity and piety signify attribute manifestation, detachment and avoidance of soul traits; peace, birth, death and resurrection signify release from material entanglement, manifestation, annihilation and life in God. [10 notes: QASHANI]
- 19:7/q20/p2 The written shape and symmetry of Yahya's name symbolize divine enabling, tenderness, verification and sinlessness. — Al-Subayh reads its two yāʾs around the ḥāʾ and says the name reads in either direction. Junayd says obedience keeps Yahya praiseworthy and without blame. [2 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد,الصبيح)]
- 19:7/q20/p3 God's personal address, prior naming and singular name are inward honors. — Bursevi's Najmi reading says John never desired or intended sin. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 19:7/q21 What other literary or scholarly observations are made about the verse?  [* · rhetoric]
- 19:7/q21/p1 The verse exemplifies rare Qur'anic vocabulary and the rhetorical design of divine non-making clauses. — Zarkashi lists these phrases in works on gharib al-Qur'an and says Qur'anic styles disclose the inimitable composition, arrangement, sweetness and fluency. [2 notes: BURHAN]
- 19:7/q21/p2 The sura's opening letters can symbolically summarize its figures. — Elmalili tentatively maps Kāf to Zachariah, Hā to his wife, Yā to John, ʿAyn to Jesus, and Ṣād to Mustafa. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 19:7/q22 Where was this sura revealed?  [* · sciences]
- 19:7/q22/p1 The sura is Meccan, with a reported exception for its prostration verse. — Abu Hayyan says Muqatil places that verse in Medina after the migration to Abyssinia. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 19:7/q23 What additional report compares Ahmad's name with the other prenatally announced names?  [* · reports]
- 19:7/q23/p1 Ahmad in Jesus's prophecy indicates Muhammad is more praiseworthy than Jesus and predecessors. — Al-Raghib is cited on 61:6 in the context of names announced before birth. [1 notes: ITQAN(الراغب)]

## 19:7/q24 What does the report say of Gabriel announcing Yahya and his name?  [يَحْيَىٰ · reports]
- 19:7/q24/p1 Gabriel announced Yahya, whose name is linked to living and counted among God's names. — The Ibn Abbas report gives these details. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
