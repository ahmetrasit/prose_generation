# 17:106: 28 questions

## 17:106/q01 What does “Qurʾān” mean and name?  [قُرْءَانًۭا · meaning]
- 17:106/q01/p1 It denotes recitation, and as a proper name the Arabic revelation to Muhammad. — Jishumī derives it from qaraʾa and also calls it a collection, comparing a basin gathering water. Elmalılı calls it a verbal noun like ghufrān named for recitation, and defines the transmitted Arabic Book by tawātur; revealed Qurʾānic wording distinguishes it from hadith and ḥadīth qudsī. Ibn ʿĀshūr connects the contextual name to recitability, while noting Book, Criterion, Reminder and Sending as other contextual names; Shidhla lists fifty-five names and al-Jāḥiẓ compares its divisions to poetry. Elmalılı connects slow tilāwa and tartīl to its name (13:30; 73:4), and says its Arabic revelation came through Gabriel to the Prophet's heart (2:97; 16:102; 26:193–194), in truth (17:105), without crookedness (39:28). [12 notes: JISHUMI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ITQAN(أبو المعالي عزيزي بن عبد الملك المعروف بشيذلة,الجاحظ)]

## 17:106/q02 How is “Qurʾānan” pronounced or written?  [قُرْءَانًۭا · readings]
turns on: Pronunciation through hamza transfer or omission, and rasm abbreviation, are separate transmitted features.
- 17:106/q02/p1 The hamza may be eased or omitted in pronunciation; the sense remains the same. — Ibn Kathīr transfers the hamza vowel in this form and comparable Qurʾān expressions. QIRAAT-ER describes qurānan without a glottal stop. [2 notes: NASHR(ابن كثير); QIRAAT-ER]
- 17:106/q02/p2 Some written forms omit an alif for lightness. — Ibn al-Jazarī describes qāf-rā-nūn abbreviation here and in Qurʾānan ʿarabiyyan (39:28). [1 notes: NASHR]

## 17:106/q03 How is “Qurʾānan” construed grammatically?  [قُرْءَانًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether an omitted verb governs it, it coordinates with the previous sending, or it functions as a fronted state or resumed object; the analyses differ on the case of the following clause.
- 17:106/q03/p1 It is accusative under an omitted verb explained by faraqnāhu, an ishtighāl construction. — Sībawayh's analysis is reported by Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī. Zamakhsharī, Nasafī, Bursevī and Ālūsī adopt it; Samīn says the following verb has no syntactic case in this parse, and Abū Ḥayyān prefers it despite the indefinite noun needing an understood qualifier. Al-Ṭabarī prefers his accusative analogy to 36:39 over the Kufan reading. Zajjāj and Naḥḥās posit an omitted verb, while al-Jishumī and Samīn offer 'We gave you a Qurʾān,' the latter citing 17:101. [15 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه); QURTUBI-FULL(سيبويه); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; TAB-FULL+]
- 17:106/q03/p2 It is governed by an earlier or understood verb of sending or revealing. — Al-Wāḥidī allows the object of anzalnāhu; al-Jishumī likewise makes it depend on anzalnāhu. Abū al-Baqāʾ posits a verb indicated by ātaynā or arsalnāka, ranked by Ālūsī below ishtighāl but above further alternatives. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]
- 17:106/q03/p3 It coordinates with the Prophet's sending, treating the Qurʾān as mercy or as sharing that mission. — Al-Farrāʾ offers a second description of what the Prophet was sent with; the Kufan explanation calls it mercy in 17:105. Ibn ʿAṭiyya coordinates it with the object pronoun of arsalnāka because the sendings have one purpose. Abū ʿAlī's deleted annexation is awkward because it indefinitizes a proper name despite 39:28. Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī call al-Farrāʾ's version strained; Samīn suggests an omitted 'bearer of a Qurʾān' instead, and Ālūsī calls Ibn ʿAṭiyya's parse astonishing. [6 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية من أهل الكوفة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية,الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء)-; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية,الفراء)-]
- 17:106/q03/p4 It is a fronted circumstantial accusative describing the recitable Book. — Ibn ʿĀshūr coordinates the verse with 17:105 and treats Qurʾān as an advanced state of the object pronoun; Abū Muslim allows coordination with the preceding circumstantial position of wa-bi-l-ḥaqq nazal. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم)]
- 17:106/q03/p5 It can be an accusative object resumed by its pronoun, with the following clause adjectival or predicative. — Al-Farrāʾ offers an object of faraqnāhu resumed after the conjunction. Al-Jishumī calls the following clause an adjective or predicate; Samīn says it is an accusative adjective under the non-ishtighāl parses. Ālūsī also allows an indefinite nominative start when licensed by context. [4 notes: FARRA; JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 17:106/q04 What does “faraqnāhu” say God did to the Qurʾān?  [فَرَقْنَٰهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether farq refers chiefly to successive descent, explanation, discrimination of truth, arrangement into units and subjects, or the expression of unified truth in human language.
- 17:106/q04/p1 God revealed it successively in separate portions. — Muqātil gives twenty years, Yaḥyā twenty-three; Qatāda and Ibn Zayd contrast this with revelation all at once, and Ibn Zayd cites 25:32–33. Ibn ʿAbbās says neither one nor two days and also explains successive verses or stars. Ibn Qutayba reports al-Ḥasan's response to questions and the Prophet, citing 25:32. Al-Kashshāf, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ṭabrisī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Ālūsī and later authors connect installments to events. Ibn ʿĀshūr holds both division and recitability; Asad deliberately preserves this and the clarity sense. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers it from the surrounding demand for one book (25:32; 4:153). [54 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن بن دينار); FARRA(ابن عباس); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة,مجاهد); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الحسن); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-ASBAB; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,السدي,قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو مسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(المجمع); ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(JJ, Ṭ); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI+]
- 17:106/q04/p2 God made it clear, firm, detailed and distinct. — The light reading is glossed by al-Ṭabarī, Ubayy, Ibn ʿAbbās, Abū ʿAmr and later lexicographers as explanation; al-Farrāʾ cites 44:4 for making it firm and distinct. Al-Wāḥidī says farq includes clarification whereas tafrīq does not. Asad preserves both senses and Ibn ʿĀshūr permits both. [26 notes: FARRA; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(أبي بن كعب,ابن عباس,عامة قراء الأمصار); NAHHAS; THALABI(ابن عباس والحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس عن علي بن أبي طلحة,الأئمة,الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور من الناس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك عن ابن عباس,الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الأئمة); QURTUBI-FULL(جمهور الناس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الفراء); DURR-FULL(أبي بن كعب); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(some authorities quoted by Razi)]
- 17:106/q04/p3 God distinguished truth from falsehood through it, including lawful from unlawful and believer from unbeliever. — Yaḥyā reports truth/falsehood and lawful/unlawful; al-Ḥasan, Abū ʿAmr, Ibn ʿAbbās and others give these distinctions. Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī note an omitted preposition in this analysis. Al-Biqāʿī adds that the text is distinct from falsehood and miraculous. [18 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(الحسن); NAHHAS(أبو عمرو بن العلاء); THALABI(ابن عباس والحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور من الناس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن,الضحاك عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن,جمهور الناس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 17:106/q04/p4 God organized it into distinct textual units or kinds of teaching. — Ibn Baḥr describes sūras and verses. Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn and Ālūsī list reports, commands, prohibitions, promises, warnings, rulings, admonitions, parables and unseen matters. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees diverse teachings drawn from a unified source; Study Quran notes unequal verse and sūra length. [8 notes: NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); TABRISI(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(IK, Ṭ)]
- 17:106/q04/p5 The unified divine truths were rendered into Arabic expressions people could receive gradually. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī invokes 43:3–4 and relates expression to human comprehension and memory. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 17:106/q05 How is “faraqnāhu” read, and what does each form imply?  [فَرَقْنَٰهُ · readings]
turns on: The majority light form and reported doubled form differ in intensity and sometimes gloss; some codices add a prepositional phrase.
- 17:106/q05/p1 The common light-rāʾ reading expresses explanation, distinction or precise separation. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers the communal authoritative reading and al-Ḥasan corrects Abū Rajāʾ's heavy reading. Ubayy and Ibn ʿAbbās also transmit it. Al-Farrāʾ, al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī cite 44:4. Abū ʿUbayd favors it because division implies clarification. Al-Wāḥidī contrasts its explanatory sense with the heavy form; al-Kashshāf calls it close gradual separation. Al-Rāzī cites Ibn al-Aʿrābī's distinction between separation of speech and bodies and the buyers' option hadith. [38 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; TAB-FULL(أبي بن كعب,ابن عباس,الحسن,عامة قراء الأمصار); NAHHAS(أبو عمرو بن العلاء); THALABI(ابن عباس والحسن,الباقون); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأعرابي عن ثعلب,الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور من الناس); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن,الضحاك عن ابن عباس,الفراء,علي وسعد بن أبي وقاص وأبي بن كعب وابن مسعود وابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ثعلب عن ابن الأعرابي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,جمهور الناس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة); DURR-FULL(أبي بن كعب); ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الأئمة)+; TAB-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأئمة)+]
- 17:106/q05/p2 The doubled-rāʾ reading emphasizes repeated division or installment revelation. — Reported from ʿAlī, Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd, Ubayy, al-Shaʿbī, Qatāda and other listed readers. Ibn ʿAbbās says verse by verse, not one or two days; Naḥḥās says it may intensify the same meaning. Ālūsī prefers frequency over a near/far separation contrast. Samīn questions how securely Ibn ʿAbbās's twenty-year gloss is tied to his reading. [28 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن بن دينار); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(ابن عباس، الشعبي، عكرمة، قتادة); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة وأبو رجاء وعلي بن أبي طالب وابن مسعود وأبي بن كعب والشعبي وحميد وعمرو بن فائد); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(علي وسعد بن أبي وقاص وأبي بن كعب وابن مسعود وابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وعلي وابن مسعود وأبي بن كعب وقتادة وأبو رجاء والشعبي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبي وعبد الله وعلي وابن عباس وأبو رجاء وقتادة والشعبي وحميد وعمرو بن قائد وزيد بن علي وعمرو بن ذر وعكرمة والحسن); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,علي وأبي وعبد الله وابن عباس والشعبي وقتادة وحميد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(المجمع); ALUSI-FULL(علي وابن عباس وأبي وعبد الله)+; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 17:106/q05/p3 Some early codices add ʿalayka or ʿalayhi after the verb. — Ibn Masʿūd and Ubayy are named by al-Māwardī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī. Al-Thaʿlabī reports Ubayy's 'upon you'; the pronoun wording varies in the reports. [6 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود وأبي بن كعب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة وأبو رجاء وعلي بن أبي طالب وابن مسعود وأبي بن كعب والشعبي وحميد وعمرو بن فائد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وعلي وابن مسعود وأبي بن كعب وقتادة وأبو رجاء والشعبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبي وعبد الله); ALUSI-FULL(أبي وعبد الله)]

## 17:106/q06 What route did revelation take before reaching the Prophet?  [فَرَقْنَٰهُ وَنَزَّلْنَٰهُ · reports]
turns on: The reported location of the first whole descent differs: the lowest heaven or the Prophet's heart.
- 17:106/q06/p1 The whole Qurʾān first descended to the lowest heaven, then came to Muhammad in portions. — Ibn ʿAbbās locates the first descent on Laylat al-Qadr, in Ramadan, often at Bayt al-ʿIzza, from the Preserved Tablet or highest heaven; Gabriel conveys later portions in response to words, deeds or new questions. Yaḥyā mentions portions of three, four, five or other numbers. Al-Suyūṭī calls this the soundest view and grades its chains sound or acceptable, citing 2:185 and 97:1. Al-Burhān cites 2:185, 97:1 and 15:9. Reported periods vary; Ibn Kathīr gives twenty-three years. Al-Durr gives a second route through the noble scribes in the lowest heaven, then Gabriel over twenty nights and the Prophet over twenty years. [20 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس عن سعيد بن جبير); RAZI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة); BURHAN(أبو بكر بن الأنباري); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(IK, Ṭ); ITQAN+]
- 17:106/q06/p2 The whole Qurʾān entered the Prophet's heart on that night, then reached his awareness gradually. — Study Quran attributes this to some commentators, citing 26:192–194. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]

## 17:106/q07 How long did the successive revelation last?  [فَرَقْنَٰهُ وَنَزَّلْنَٰهُ · sciences]
turns on: Reports differ about years of the Prophet's mission or Makkan residence; Ibn ʿAṭiyya also challenges the eighteen-year report.
- 17:106/q07/p1 About twenty years, sometimes ten in Mecca and ten in Medina. — Muqātil, Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Shaʿbī and others use twenty or roughly twenty. Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Kashshāf contrast this with one or two days. Al-Wāḥidī reports al-Shaʿbī praising its proofs and defeat of falsehood. Qatāda alternatively says 'as God willed.' Later authors explain discrepancies by different accounts of the Prophet's age and residence. [18 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-ASBAB(الشعبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ITQAN(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 17:106/q07/p2 Twenty-three years. — Yaḥyā, al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAbbās as cited by Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Biqāʿī, Ālūsī, Elmalılı and Asad give this span. Ālūsī calls it the relied-on span; Ibn ʿAṭiyya lists other estimates. [12 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)+]
- 17:106/q07/p3 Eighteen years: eight in Mecca and ten in Medina. — Al-Ḥasan's report is preserved by al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī and al-Durr; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it unsound, echoed by Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-ASBAB(الحسن); DURR-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن)-; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)-]
- 17:106/q07/p4 Twenty-five years. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī report this estimate among varying accounts tied to the Prophet's age or years in Mecca. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 17:106/q08 In what size portions was the Qurʾān brought or taught?  [فَرَقْنَٰهُ وَنَزَّلْنَٰهُ · reports]
turns on: Whether five verses describes a regular pattern or the size varied with occasion.
- 17:106/q08/p1 Gabriel commonly brought five verses at a time; learning and teaching could follow that unit. — ʿUmar advises five at a time. Abū Saʿīd taught five morning and evening. Ālūsī and Elmalılı qualify the reports: sound evidence shows larger and smaller revelations too, so five describes a usual pattern. [7 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري,عمر); ALUSI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري,عمر); ELMALILI(Ebû Sa‘îd-i Hudrî,Ömer)]
- 17:106/q08/p2 A verse, two or several verses came as occasions required. — Al-Ḥasan describes answers to questions and replies to the Prophet (25:32). Yaḥyā reports Ibn ʿAbbās's groups of three, four, five or other counts. Ibn ʿAbbās also says one verse at a time (25:33); Ālūsī notes reliable reports above and below five. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن عباس); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الحسن); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري)]

## 17:106/q09 What does “li-taqraʾahu” attach to grammatically?  [لِتَقْرَأَهُۥ · grammar]
- 17:106/q09/p1 It depends on faraqnāhu, giving the purpose of division. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn state the attachment directly. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 17:106/q10 How is the pronominal hāʾ of “taqraʾahu” sounded?  [لِتَقْرَأَهُۥ · readings]
- 17:106/q10/p1 After a long vowel, shortening the following yāʾ or wāw in connected speech is preferred, while full realization is Arabic. — Sībawayh adduces other Qurʾānic cases (7:176; 12:20). [1 notes: SIBAWAYH+]

## 17:106/q11 Whom does “al-nās” address?  [ٱلنَّاسِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase marks the universal audience or the immediate Quraysh hearers.
- 17:106/q11/p1 All people the Prophet could reach, consistent with a mission to all humanity. — Al-Biqāʿī gives this scope. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 17:106/q11/p2 The Quraysh among whom the Prophet recited. — Yaḥyā transmits Mujāhid's gloss of deliberate recitation among Quraysh. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد)]

## 17:106/q12 How is “al-nās” read with imāla in connection and at pause?  [ٱلنَّاسِ · readings]
turns on: At pause, whether the incidental sukūn removes the kasra's effect or the connecting form continues to govern.
- 17:106/q12/p1 Fatḥ is the majority transmission for Abū ʿAmr, although imāla is also sound. — Ibn al-Jazarī says al-Yazīdī's unqualified fatḥ was his own choice, not proof against other transmission. Al-Ḥarbī calls genitive imāla a Hijazi dialect used by Abū ʿAmr; al-Hudhalī transmits it through al-Dūrī. Most routes give fatḥ, only two Yazīdī routes explicitly give imāla, and Ibn al-Jazarī read both. [3 notes: NASHR(عبد الله بن داود الحربي); NASHR+]
- 17:106/q12/p2 At pause, fatḥ follows from loss of the kasra that triggered imāla. — Al-Shadhāʾī, Ibn al-Munādī and Ibn Ḥabash report this, including Basran and specific Warsh and Ḥamza routes; Ibn al-Jazarī confines fatḥ-only transmission to certain routes. [2 notes: NASHR(أبو بكر الشذائي، وأبو الحسن بن المنادي، وابن حبش)]
- 17:106/q12/p3 At pause, imāla or its between-between form is retained because pause is incidental. — The majority teach and practice this; Ibn al-Jazarī accepts both pause forms from al-Sūsī and has read both, while calling imāla prevalent. [2 notes: NASHR(الجمهور)+]

## 17:106/q13 What does “ʿalā mukth” mean?  [عَلَىٰ مُكْثٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether mukth describes the manner of reciting each passage or the span between and across successive passages.
- 17:106/q13/p1 The Prophet should recite slowly, deliberately, clearly and with measured tartīl. — Mujāhid and Ibn ʿAbbās repeatedly gloss it as tartīl or unhurried reading. Ṭāwūs says clarity enables understanding; Ibn Zayd cites 73:4, while ʿAṭāʾ contrasts measured recitation with casting words away. Al-Ṭabarī and others link measured articulation to listener comprehension; al-Qurṭubī includes a ban on melody that alters text. Mālik also gives deliberate reading. Al-Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr relate pace to words settling in hearts. ## 17:106/q14 How does “ʿalā mukth” attach syntactically?  [عَلَىٰ مُكْثٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the phrase modifies recitation, the act of division, or the people; the last is rejected on semantic grounds.
- 17:106/q14/p1 It describes the reader or recitation as a circumstance of the reading. — Abū Ḥayyān attaches it to li-taqraʾahu despite repeated ʿalā. Al-Samīn reports an implicit circumstance of reader or text, but objects that a circumstantial analysis needs an implicit governor if described that way. Ālūsī reports Abū al-Baqāʾ's state reading. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)+; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 17:106/q14/p2 It attaches directly to faraqnāhu, describing division in stages. — Al-Samīn lists this as a third parse, and Ālūsī notes the disagreement between attachment to faraqnāhu and li-taqraʾahu. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 17:106/q14/p3 It is an apposition to ʿalā al-nās. — Al-Ḥawfī proposes this; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and Ālūsī reject it because delay characterizes the reader or recitation, not the people. [0 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي)-; SAMIN-DURR(الحوفي)-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 17:106/q15 How is the mīm of “mukth” vocalized?  [مُكْثٍۢ · readings]

## 17:106/q16 What practice follows from measured recitation?  [عَلَىٰ مُكْثٍۢ · law]
turns on: Whether tartīl is specifically measured delivery or also observance of textual order.
- 17:106/q16/p1 Deliberate recitation with full articulation and reflection has priority over speed. — Mujāhid prefers al-Baqara recited slowly to a faster greater quantity when other prayer acts are equal; Ibn ʿAbbās prefers slow al-Baqara to a quick full Qurʾān. Ibn Masʿūd advises at least three days and recommends seven. Qurṭubī says melodic alteration of the text is forbidden. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(ابن عباس,عبد الله بن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد وابن عباس وابن جريج)]
- 17:106/q16/p2 Tartīl can mean reciting in the established Qurʾānic order. — Al-Burhān reports some who condemn reversing order and construe an oath to read in order that way, citing 73:4. [1 notes: BURHAN(بعضهم)]

## 17:106/q17 What does the final “tanzīlan” add?  [وَنَزَّلْنَٰهُ تَنزِيلًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the verbal noun stresses the actual staged descent or itself denotes clarification.
- 17:106/q17/p1 The cognate accusative intensifies the assertion of staged descent. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it reinforcement of the preceding sense. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the doubled verb and cognate object signal staged revelation, and argues that the heavier transitive form pragmatically suggests abundance; he reports al-Zamakhsharī's pairing of anzal and nazzala. Al-Rāzī says the final clause restates the described manner and quality. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 17:106/q17/p2 Tanzīl means clarification here. — Al-Dāmghānī gives this gloss. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]

## 17:106/q18 Why was the Qurʾān revealed and recited in stages?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes give complementary purposes: ease of learning, responses to events, steadiness for the Prophet, gradual uptake of duties, and unity across installments.
- 17:106/q18/p1 Stages make reception, memorization, understanding and reflection easier. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī, Bursevī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Study Quran and others give this rationale. Rāzī answers an objection that staged delivery could look like composition by the Messenger; instead it aids memory and grasp of details. Al-Biqāʿī stresses sustained teaching; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī pairs learning with practice. Elmalılı says even a verse may be a complete lesson. Al-Jaʿbarī adds short-sūra milestones and rest. [21 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ, Ṭ); BURHAN(الجعبرى)]
- 17:106/q18/p2 Stages meet new events, questions and people's changing needs. — Al-Ḥasan says verses answered questions and replies to the Prophet. The Qurʾān came day by day as circumstances unfolded. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 25:32 and the demand for one book; al-Suyūṭī reports Ibn ʿAbbās that each new polytheist challenge drew an answer (25:33). Elmalılı describes movement from foundations to subsidiary matters. [18 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الحسن); TAB-FULL(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL; ITQAN(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 17:106/q18/p3 Stages strengthened the Prophet's heart and renewed divine reassurance. — Al-Durr links the reply to the polytheists' demand at 25:32; Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies this implicit wisdom from measured recitation and 25:32. Al-Biqāʿī says repeated news from the beloved was like a daily celebration. Study Quran says the staged descent was divinely intended. [5 notes: TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 17:106/q18/p4 Stages let obligations and social change take hold gradually. — Qurṭubī says a simultaneous arrival of all duties would repel people. Al-Burhān says abrogating and abrogated material cannot descend together. Elmalılı and Kuranyolu describe people leaving entrenched beliefs and habits step by step, including wine prohibition. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]
- 17:106/q18/p5 Each passage should still be understood as part of a unified whole. — Asad says gradual revelation does not break the Qurʾān's unity and cites 20:114; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes the diverse teachings as issuing from one unified source. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI]

## 17:106/q19 How does 17:106 connect with its surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: How the separated clause relates to the truthful descent, the challenge narrative, and its rhetorical joining.
- 17:106/q19/p1 It explains the preceding truthful descent and the opponents' demand for a single complete revelation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr coordinates it with 17:105 and points to 25:32 for the omitted wisdom. Al-Durr likewise connects 25:32. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ places it after the sūra's challenge at 17:88 and demands at 17:89–96; 17:105–109 then affirm truthful descent and believers' response. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds the demand of the People of the Book at 4:153. [8 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; DURR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 17:106/q19/p2 The clause separation has rhetorical force beyond labeling it a fresh start. — ʿAbd al-Qāhir al-Jurjānī says joining and separating clauses is subtle and merely calling an unjoined clause a new start fails to explain the choice. [1 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 17:106/q20 What do the verse's divisions imply about sūras and verses as units?  [فَرَقْنَٰهُ · sciences]
- 17:106/q20/p1 A sūra is a bounded, purposefully arranged unit and an āya a distinguishable recitable unit. — Al-Jaʿbarī defines a sūra as an opening and closing around at least three verses; each division is an art and sign, and even al-Kawthar's three verses are miraculous like al-Baqara. He gives teaching, memory, rest and theme as benefits. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī defines verse units by separable recitation, from one word to long passage, and sūras as basmala-prefaced speech organized around a purpose, with reported counting exceptions. Umm Salama's reports of the Prophet pausing at verse ends and traditional counts for al-Fātiḥa and al-Mulk support verse units. [6 notes: BURHAN(الجعبرى); TABATABAI(أم سلمة)]

## 17:106/q21 How is “sūra” derived and used for the Qurʾān's divisions?  [فَرَقْنَٰهُ · meaning]
- 17:106/q21/p1 It can denote an elevated, ranked or enclosing section of discourse. — Jishumī contrasts unhamzated elevated dwelling with hamzated Qurʾānic section and suʾr, remainder. Al-Qutaybī allows hamza from vessel remainder or an unhamzated sense; others compare building levels, city wall or bracelet enclosure, rank and ordered verses, or elevation and rising (Ibn Jinnī). Al-Burhān distinguishes the plural suwar from the building wall plural and says naming individual sūras is permitted, citing Ibn Masʿūd. [8 notes: JISHUMI; BURHAN(ابن جني,القتيبي,بعضهم)]

## 17:106/q22 What is meant by āya, tafsīr and taʾwīl in discussions of the Qurʾān's units?  [فَرَقْنَٰهُ · meaning]
- 17:106/q22/p1 Āya can mean a sign bounding discourse or a group; tafsīr uncovers meaning, while taʾwīl has several accounts. — Jishumī gives āya as a beginning and end marker or collection of letters and reports faʿla/fāʿila analyses; Sībawayh favors medial wāw because that root pattern is common. Abū al-ʿAbbās equates tafsīr, taʾwīl and meaning; another account makes tafsīr explanation of obscure wording and taʾwīl a choice fitting apparent sense; a third derives taʾwīl from final outcome, citing 7:53. [6 notes: JISHUMI(سيبويه)]

## 17:106/q23 What divisions of sūras are reported?  [فَرَقْنَٰهُ · sciences]
- 17:106/q23/p1 The seven long sūras, mathānī, miʾūn and mufassal classify Qurʾānic sections. — Jishumī reports a Prophetic comparison with Torah, Gospel and Psalms. The long sūras run from al-Baqara to al-Tawba, or through Yūsuf. Mathānī may mean sūras over one hundred verses with recurring rulings, or al-Fātiḥa; miʾūn are around one hundred verses; mufassal are short sūras with frequent breaks. [4 notes: JISHUMI(النبي)]

## 17:106/q24 How fixed are verse and sūra counts?  [فَرَقْنَٰهُ · sciences]
- 17:106/q24/p1 Verse endpoints and total counts vary across regional traditions and are not fixed by a reliable mutawātir text. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī lists Meccan, two Medinan, Syrian, Basran and Kufan lines of transmission. He says a researcher may weigh contextual evidence where boundaries are unclear. He challenges the Kufan count attributed to ʿAlī against basmala reports and notes 114 sūras in the familiar Uthmanic codex versus Ahl al-Bayt joining Barāʾa to the preceding sūra and pairing al-Ḍuḥā/al-Inshirāḥ and al-Fīl/Quraysh. [4 notes: TABATABAI]

## 17:106/q25 Who arranged and compiled the Qurʾān's sūras and verses?  [فَرَقْنَٰهُ · sciences]
turns on: Whether Prophetic instruction established both sūra and verse order or only verse order, leaving sūra arrangement to the Companions.
- 17:106/q25/p1 The Prophet set the arrangement; Companions collected the written and remembered text without addition or loss. — Al-Burhān says material lay on palm stalks, stones and in memories and was gathered before memorizers died; the Companions wrote what they heard. Gabriel showed the Prophet placements, says Ibn al-Anbārī, so changing a sūra's place disrupts order. Al-Sulamī says Zayd witnessed the final review with Gabriel, hence Abū Bakr and ʿUthmān chose him. Present order in the Preserved Tablet and whole descent to the lowest heaven are also adduced. [5 notes: BURHAN(أبو بكر بن الأنباري,أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي)]
- 17:106/q25/p2 The Prophet fixed verse order, but Companions arranged the sūras. — Ibn Fāris distinguishes the two compilations. Makkī allows Companion judgment in sūra order while attributing placement of basmala to the Prophet. Al-Qāḍī Abū Bakr reports differing early codex orders, including ʿAlī's chronological start with Iqraʾ, Ibn Masʿūd's al-Fātiḥa/al-Baqara/al-Nisāʾ, and Ubayy's al-Fātiḥa/al-Nisāʾ. [3 notes: BURHAN(ابن فارس,القاضي أبو بكر بن الطيب,مكي)]

## 17:106/q26 How can revelation order and Meccan or Medinan status be established?  [فَرَقْنَٰهُ · sciences]
- 17:106/q26/p1 Transmitted chronologies exist but conflict, so contextual and internal evidence is needed. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports Ibn ʿAbbās's sequence from Iqraʾ through early Meccan sūras; Medinan lists differ and may omit al-Fātiḥa because of double revelation. Ibn Ḥaṣṣār counts twenty agreed Medinan and twelve disputed. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses the value of order for the prophetic mission but questions conflicting reports. Bint al-Shāṭiʾī places ten letter-opened sūras before al-Qaṣaṣ and al-Isrāʾ fiftieth, in the context of the sign challenge. [6 notes: TABATABAI(ابن حصار,ابن عباس); BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 17:106/q27 What inward counsel is drawn from the Qurʾān's division and address?  [* · inward]
turns on: The counsel addresses different spiritual conditions.
- 17:106/q27/p1 Holding to the Qurʾān brings nearness and recognition; neglect brings deprivation. — Al-Bursevī reports the Najmī interpretations on the Qurʾān's rope and consequences of severing it. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 17:106/q27/p2 The sinful should hope in mercy and the righteous should fear pride in their deeds. — Al-Bursevī reports Sulamī, a Ḥāfiẓ verse on mercy for a sinful believer, and another verse warning that ascetic pride fails while humility reaches peace. [3 notes: BURSEVI(سلمى)]

## 17:106/q28 How does furqān relate to this Qurʾānic separation?  [فَرَقْنَٰهُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether furqān names the act of discrimination or its saving result.
- 17:106/q28/p1 It denotes distinction between truth and falsehood. — Jishumī attributes this to Ibn al-ʿAbbās, citing 8:29. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 17:106/q28/p2 It can denote salvation or escape. — Jishumī also reports this alternative, citing 8:29. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
