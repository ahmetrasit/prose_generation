# 2:27: 32 questions

## 2:27/q01 Who are the covenant breakers?  [ٱلَّذِينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the immediate scriptural or hypocritical audience limits a general description.
- 2:27/q01/p1 Jewish scholars and People of the Book who concealed or denied Muhammad. — Muqatil includes rejection of Jesus and selective belief. Tabari cites the prior disbelievers and hypocrites and following address to Israel at 2:40; Ibn Atiyya sees this as a particular application in a broad context. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(بعضهم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); RAZI-FULL(القفال); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; STUDYQURAN; NASAFI-FULL; TAB-FULL+]
- 2:27/q01/p2 All who share the conduct, including unbelievers, polytheists, and hypocrites. — Tabari extends the warning beyond its first audience; Abu Hayyan and Alusi prefer the general wording over a restricted occasion. Razi and Ibn Kathir favor wide application. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم); MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مقاتل بن حيان); ASAD-NOTES; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 2:27/q01/p3 Hypocrites whose six traits appear when they hold power. — Abu al-Aliya and Rabi list lying, broken promises, betrayal, covenant breach, severance, and corruption; the first three alone appear when weak. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو العالية); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; MAWARDI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 2:27/q01/p4 The Haruriyya or Kharijites exemplify the traits. — Sa'd applies both covenant breach and severance to them. Ibn Kathir says they arose after revelation, so the report, if sound, applies the verse rather than predicting them. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعد بن أبي وقاص); DURR-FULL(سعد بن أبي وقاص)]
- 2:27/q01/p5 People who believed in the Prophet and then disbelieved. — Qatada describes the reversal; Abu Hayyan reports apostasy after faith. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:27/q02 How is ٱلَّذِينَ parsed?  [ٱلَّذِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Attachment to the preceding fāsiqīn or a separate condemnatory sentence.
- 2:27/q02/p1 It is accusative as an adjective of the preceding fāsiqīn. — Abu Hayyan and Ibn Ashur prefer syntactic continuity; Baydawi says the description confirms their transgression. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 2:27/q02/p2 It is a new subject whose predicate is 'those are the losers'. — Alusi calls this almost parenthetical; al-Samin gives the resulting final-clause parse. [6 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:27/q02/p3 It is detached for censure or predicate of an omitted pronoun. — Nahhas and Qurtubi supply huwa/hum; Abu Hayyan also allows accusative censure. [5 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:27/q03 What does 'breaking' mean?  [يَنقُضُونَ · meaning]
- 2:27/q03/p1 Undoing a firmly secured bond, then abandoning or opposing its terms. — The sources compare untying rope and dismantling a building. Wahidi cites counter-poetry by Jarir and Farazdaq; Jishumi extends it to refuting proof. Ibn Ashur says untying conveys deeper destruction than cutting; Tabatabai notes a preceding bond. [17 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 2:27/q04 Why describe breach as untying?  [يَنقُضُونَ عَهْدَ ٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
- 2:27/q04/p1 The covenant is an imagined cord binding two parties; breach loosens its strands. — Baydawi and Bursevi explain the image. Alusi distinguishes explicit from implicit metaphor. Ibn Ashur discusses representational and imaginative features, citing Abu Firas, Labid, and poetry on severed bonds; classifications can overlap. Burhan describes the rope and its links as the bond between the covenanting parties; Itqan identifies 'undo' as the cue to an unspoken rope comparison, a metaphor by insinuation. [11 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN]

## 2:27/q05 What can ʿahd mean in Arabic?  [عَهْدَ · meaning]
turns on: The word has several attested senses; the verse's referent is a separate question.
- 2:27/q05/p1 A charge, instruction, testament, or entrusted duty. — Ibn Jawzi and Ibn Faris cite the charge in 36:60 and revelation to Abraham and Ishmael in 2:125; some group revelation with instruction. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن فارس,ابن قتيبة,الحسن,بعض المفسرين,بعضهم); SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:27/q05/p2 A binding pact, security guarantee, oath, or agreement. — Ibn Faris gives binding security; 9:4 illustrates treaty protection, while Ibn Qutayba reads 16:91 as oath and another reading calls it agreement. [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن فارس,ابن قتيبة,بعض المفسرين,غيره); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:27/q05/p3 In other uses it means keeping faith, safeguarding, monotheism, revelation, or prophethood. — Ibn Jawzi cites 7:102, 19:87, 2:125, and 2:124; a prophetic saying makes faithful keeping part of faith. [7 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن قتيبة,الحسن,النبي,بعض المفسرين,بعضهم)]
- 2:27/q05/p4 Related forms name a period, first rain, refuge, covenanting person, sale document, unresolved matter, or renewed care. — Ibn Faris catalogs the uses; Ibn Jawzi distinguishes one-sided taʿahhad from reciprocal taʿāhad. [7 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن فارس)]

## 2:27/q06 Which covenant is broken here?  [عَهْدَ ٱللَّهِ · referent]
turns on: The rival grounds are transmitted obligations, rational proof, primordial testimony, or a specific historical pledge.
- 2:27/q06/p1 God's general commands and prohibitions transmitted in revelation and through messengers. — Breaking means not acting on them. Qurtubi prefers this inclusive view despite an unbeliever context. Asad includes the moral duty to use endowed gifts as intended. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(قول); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض المفسرين); ASAD-NOTES; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 2:27/q06/p2 The Torah or prior scriptures' duty to affirm Muhammad, follow him, and disclose his description. — Muqatil also names belief in Jesus and all prophets. Tabari cites 3:187; Wahidi prefers an obligation made known by revelation. Ibn Kathir transmits Ibn Jarir and Muqatil ibn Hayyan favoring this. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(بعضهم); THALABI; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(القفال); QURTUBI-FULL(قول); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير ومقاتل بن حيان)+; TABRISI(الطبري)+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 2:27/q06/p3 The primordial pledge of Adam's descendants to acknowledge God as Lord and worship Him alone. — The supporters cite 7:172. Wahidi says revelation made an unremembered pledge known; Ibn Jawzi invokes truthful report. Jishumi, Tabarsi, and Razi's theologians object that forgotten obligations cannot fairly bind. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); QURTUBI-FULL(قول); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مقاتل بن حيان); QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK); ZAJJAJ+; JISHUMI-; TABRISI-; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم)-]
- 2:27/q06/p4 Rational and innate evidence of God and prophecy in creation, reason, miracles, and scripture. — Zajjaj grounds obligation in discerning a Creator; Ibn Atiyya cites cosmic signs. Razi favors repeated accessible proofs; Asad reasons from human dependence. Ibn Kathir and Alusi encompass all unbelievers. [18 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم); ZAJJAJ(قوم); MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(قول); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مقاتل بن حيان); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 2:27/q06/p5 The pledge of prophets and followers to affirm and support a confirming messenger. — Zajjaj cites 3:81 and glosses iṣr as God's covenant; Wahidi favors this known proof over the unremembered Adam pledge. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 2:27/q06/p6 Three covenants: primordial lordship, prophetic conveyance and unity, and scholars' disclosure of truth. — Razi cites 3:81 and 3:187; Nasafi adds 33:7. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 2:27/q06/p7 The Quranic charge accepted before its recipients disbelieved. — Al-Suddi reports acceptance followed by repudiation. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدى); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي)]
- 2:27/q06/p8 An oath to follow a warner if one came. — Razi and Study Quran cite 35:42; Razi objects that it only binds oath makers. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R); RAZI-FULL-]
- 2:27/q06/p9 Israel's pledge not to kill, expel, oppress, or sever one another. — Abu Hayyan cites 2:84 for Israelite addressees. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:27/q06/p10 The trust humanity carried after heaven and earth declined it. — Reported by Abu Hayyan and Alusi. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:27/q06/p11 Any confirmed pledge made in worldly life, including one invoked in God's name. — Kuranyolu pairs such promises with the primordial pledge; Ibn Ashur cites 16:91 and includes prior or subsequent confirmation. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:27/q07 What duty does this verse establish toward covenants?  [عَهْدَ ٱللَّهِ · law]
- 2:27/q07/p1 A lawful covenant must be fulfilled; feared betrayal must be openly declared instead of secretly committed. — Ibn Atiyya includes Muslim agreements. Qurtubi cites 5:1 and 8:58. Qatada stresses threat. Prophetic sayings connect covenant and trust to faith and religion. Malik ibn Dinar's story and the Mathnawi warn that repeated breach incurs rebuke or curse. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(النبي); DURR-FULL(ابن عمر,النبي,عبادة بن الصامت وأبو أمامة,قتادة); BURSEVI(المثنوي)]

## 2:27/q08 How does مِنۢ بَعْدِ work?  [مِنۢ بَعْدِ · grammar]
turns on: Temporal starting point or emphatic particle.
- 2:27/q08/p1 Min marks the start of breach after confirmation, suggesting swift disregard. — Abu Hayyan attaches it to yanqudun; Samin and Alusi reject emptiness. Baydawi and others allow this force. [8 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 2:27/q08/p2 Min is extra or merely emphatic after baʿd. — Wahidi calls its mim emphatic; others report an extra particle. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 2:27/q09 Why use بَعْدِ here?  [بَعْدِ · rhetoric]
- 2:27/q09/p1 It locates breach after the covenant in time and cannot be replaced by khalfa. — Samarrai compares 2:52, 2:64, 2:109, 2:120, 2:230, and 3:8. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 2:27/q10 What is مِيثَٰق and how is it formed?  [مِيثَٰقِهِۦ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the form names the confirming thing or functions as a verbal noun.
- 2:27/q10/p1 A firm confirmation, bond, or undertaking securing a covenant. — Wahidi traces yāʾ to wāw; Farra and others give plurals miyāthiq and mawāthiq with poetry. Baydawi includes signs, books, and acceptance. Abu Hayyan notes this instance need not signify an oath. [16 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:27/q10/p2 A noun substituting for a verbal noun of binding or confirming. — Akhfash compares nabat/inbat and ata/iʿta, citing 71:17; Nahhas cites Ibn Kaysan and Qutami; Ibn Atiyya uses a line of Amr ibn Shayyim. Abu Hayyan and Samin find substitution unnecessary. [7 notes: AKHFASH; NAHHAS(ابن كيسان,القطامي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء,ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية)-]
- 2:27/q10/p3 A verbal or source noun expressing the act of confirmation. — Tabari says tawaththuq; Abu Hayyan compares miʿad and milad and notes missing mifʿal from Ibn al-Hajj and Ibn Malik's lists. Ibn Ashur favors this over Khafaji's intensive form. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL(الخفاجي)+]
- 2:27/q10/p4 An intensive mifʿal form. — Khafaji's account is reported, but Ibn Ashur favors a source noun here. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الخفاجي)]

## 2:27/q11 What is the antecedent of the pronoun in مِيثَٰقِهِۦ?  [مِيثَٰقِهِۦ · grammar]
turns on: Whether confirmation is annexed to its object, agent, or undertaking.
- 2:27/q11/p1 The covenant: its own ratification. — Abu Hayyan and Ibn Ashur favor the topical antecedent; Abu al-Baqa makes the verbal noun object-related. Salikuti objects to a divine antecedent. [15 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الزمخشري); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء,الساليكوتي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 2:27/q11/p2 God, who confirmed and bound His covenant. — Tabari chooses this. Abu al-Baqa makes the verbal noun agent-related; Alusi replies that such genitives are common. [14 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء,الساليكوتي)]
- 2:27/q11/p3 The accepted undertaking or its ratification. — Razi reports Zamakhshari's three alternatives, including the covenant undertaken and God as confirmer through books and signs. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(الزمخشري); BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 2:27/q12 What does يَقْطَعُونَ mean lexically?  [يَقْطَعُونَ · meaning]
- 2:27/q12/p1 Severing or dividing, extended to crossing, bird migration, waning water, and broken breath. — Nahhas and Qurtubi list the uses; Jishumi says a doubled form can intensify or repeat cutting. Tabarsi extends physical cutting to speech and abstract matters. Damghani and Ibn Jawzi's Nuzha give severing relatives' rights and kinship ties as an abstract use of qatʿ. [6 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 2:27/q13 What connection does God command to be joined?  [مَآ أَمَرَ ٱللَّهُ بِهِۦٓ أَن يُوصَلَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause names one concrete relation or all commanded connections.
- 2:27/q13/p1 Kinship and family ties, including relatives' rights and kindness. — Tabari cites 47:22 and maternal relation; Wahidi specifies Quraysh's hostile break with Muhammad. Study Quran cites 4:1, 8:75, 33:6 and notes the Jews' shared ancestry. Bursevi reports warnings and blessings for kinship. [27 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(السدى); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قول); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazı tefsirciler); STUDYQURAN; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)+; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة وابن جرير)+]
- 2:27/q13/p2 The Messenger and believers, whom rejection or hostile alliance cuts off. — Hasan names the Messenger; Razi mentions alliance with unbelievers. Abu Hayyan objects that a person would be called man rather than ma. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن البصري); JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); TABRISI(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); TAB-FULL+; TABRISI+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن)-]
- 2:27/q13/p3 Belief in Muhammad and all prophets and scriptures without selective acceptance. — Thalabi cites 4:150 and 2:285; Ibn Abbas says believers join their faith by distinguishing none; Nasafi says the prophets agree in truth. [13 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(قول); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:27/q13/p4 Profession joined to action, which hypocrites leave apart. — Abu Hayyan cites 2:8 and hypocrites' tongue and heart; Bursevi reports neglect of known truth. [5 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(قول); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 2:27/q13/p5 All divinely commanded ties, worship, law, good conduct, and the bond between God and servant. — Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi include kinship as one part of religion. Baydawi lists believer alliance, prophets, congregational prayer, and any good. Kuranyolu adds unified humanity and proper order; Biqai adds completion of innate gifts. [17 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور أهل العلم); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(IK); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(القاضي أبو محمد)+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور)+; TAB-FULL+; TABRISI+]
- 2:27/q13/p6 Higher spiritual kin and paths by which the soul journeys to God. — Qashani names the Holy Spirit, higher principles, and celestial spirits. Najmi interpretation cites 73:8 for spiritual wayfaring and withdrawal toward God. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 2:27/q13/p7 Reconciliation between spouses as one particular bond. — Kuranyolu reports this alongside kinship. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazı tefsirciler)]

## 2:27/q14 How are مَا and أَن يُوصَلَ parsed?  [مَآ أَمَرَ ٱللَّهُ بِهِۦٓ أَن يُوصَلَ · grammar]
turns on: The attachment of an yusala and the grammatical status of ma.
- 2:27/q14/p1 An yusala is genitive apposition to the pronoun in bihi; ma is the severed object. — Akhfash compares 'I passed the people, some of them.' Abu Hayyan cites poetry and prefers this; Baydawi and Alusi find it stronger in wording and force. [10 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+]
- 2:27/q14/p2 An yusala is accusative apposition to ma. — Ibn Atiyya, Mahdawi, Abu al-Baqa, and Bursevi allow it; Abu Hayyan calls it weaker. [8 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(القاضي أبو محمد); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المهدوي، وابن عطية، وأبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 2:27/q14/p3 An yusala gives purpose or aversion: lest it be joined. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu al-Baqa allow estimates such as 'disliking that it be joined'; Abu Hayyan rejects them. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(القاضي أبو محمد); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المهدوي وابن عطية وأبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 2:27/q14/p4 An yusala predicates an omitted huwa. — Abu al-Baqa proposes it; Samin calls it remote. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المهدوي وابن عطية وأبو البقاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-]
- 2:27/q14/p5 Ma is a relative pronoun with 'God commanded it' as its clause. — Nahhas makes it the object of yaqtaun; Samin and Nasafi give the relative analysis. [3 notes: NAHHAS; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:27/q14/p6 Ma is indefinite and qualified by its following clause. — Abu al-Baqa allows it; Abu Hayyan argues that an unrestricted indefinite cannot ground such severe blame. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 2:27/q14/p7 Ma can be a verbal noun despite the returning pronoun. — Samin attributes this only to Abu al-Hasan and Ibn al-Sarraj. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو الحسن وابن السراج)]

## 2:27/q15 What is an amr?  [أَمَرَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether command is only verbal or also names the act or affair.
- 2:27/q15/p1 A superior's verbal request for an act, though context can yield permission, threat, challenge, or creation. — Jishumi and Tabarsi give request as the core; Baydawi links affairs with intended purposes. Mu'tazilites require superiority, Abu al-Husayn only claimed superiority; Pharaoh's words in 7:110 challenge that distinction. [7 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(المعتزلة)]
- 2:27/q15/p2 The word can also name an act or affair, not just speech. — Jurists cite Pharaoh's command in 11:97; Baydawi and Zamakhshari link a 'thing' to what is commanded. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الفقهاء)]

## 2:27/q16 Does a bare command imply obligation?  [أَمَرَ · law]
turns on: The default legal force of the command form.
- 2:27/q16/p1 Usually it implies obligation. — Jishumi reports the majority while recognizing contextual uses. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 2:27/q16/p2 It indicates recommendation. — Jishumi reports Abu Ali and Abu Hashim; he rejects suspension of judgment. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي وأبو هاشم)]

## 2:27/q17 What is corruption on earth here?  [وَيُفْسِدُونَ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether corruption denotes particular public offenses or all harmful sin.
- 2:27/q17/p1 Sins, idolatry, injustice, and evil deeds. — Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi specify worship of other than God and following desire. Kuranyolu includes material and spiritual disorder, pollution, unrest, pain, and calamity; Biqai pictures arrested bodily and moral development. [19 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(السدى,سعد,مقاتل); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); DURR-FULL(السدي); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:27/q17/p2 Inviting unbelief or obstructing faith in Muhammad and the Quran. — Ibn Abbas identifies calling to unbelief; Ibn Jawzi reports intercepting migrants. Baydawi adds mockery of truth and damaged world order; Razi cites Pharaoh's fear at 40:26. [15 notes: THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 2:27/q17/p3 Intimidating travelers, blocking roads, and creating insecurity. — Mawardi and others list road violence; Alusi refers to routes blocked to prevent migration. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:27/q17/p4 Any sin whose harm extends to others. — Tabarsi, Razi, and Alusi prefer this scope; Alusi says 'on earth' signals harm beyond the sinner. Razi says observance of law sustains order. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; TABRISI+]
- 2:27/q17/p5 Breaking the covenant and fragmenting the ties of social order. — Tabarsi and Abu Hayyan report covenant breach itself; Baydawi and Kuranyolu connect severance to worldly disorder. [5 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:27/q17/p6 Quarrelling and stirring discord. — Razi reports this distinct account. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 2:27/q17/p7 Spoiling the innate seed of unity by shirk and rejection of prophets. — Najmi interpretation says faith and deeds should water the seed, citing Sura 103; Qashani describes wasting luminous substance for perishable darkness. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); QASHANI]

## 2:27/q18 What does 'losers' mean here?  [ٱلْخَٰسِرُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Lexical diminished share versus particular worldly and final consequences.
- 2:27/q18/p1 They forfeit their share of mercy, reward, and lasting good, like traders losing capital. — Tabari cites Jarir for diminished share and honor. Wahidi adds missed reward and punishment; Qaffal speaks of labor without return. Baydawi, Biqai, Alusi, and Kuranyolu develop loss of eternal good for transient gain. [30 notes: TAB-FULL(جرير بن عطية); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(القفال); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القفال); SAMIN-DURR(جرير); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير,جرير بن عطية); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI]
- 2:27/q18/p2 They are destroyed or doomed, ultimately inhabitants of the Fire. — Tabari says doom is a consequence rather than the lexical definition; Jawhari gives ruin senses. Muqatil connects final loss to 13:25. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مقاتل بن حيان); DURR-FULL(مقاتل); MUQATIL]
- 2:27/q18/p3 They lose their allotted home, family, and portion in Paradise to believers. — Razi cites 23:10–11; Bursevi gives a home, family, and servants for each person. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:27/q18/p4 Unbelief nullifies their earlier good deeds and reward. — Razi applies this to Jews and hypocrites. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:27/q18/p5 Immediate pleasures sought through unbelief vanish through fighting or death. — Razi reports this worldly account. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 2:27/q18/p6 For non-Muslims loss language denotes unbelief; for Muslims it denotes sin or worldly loss. — Ibn Abbas's transmitted formula says sin in several sources but worldly loss in Tabarsi. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 2:27/q19 How is أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلْخَٰسِرُونَ parsed?  [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلْخَٰسِرُونَ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical role of hum.
- 2:27/q19/p1 Ulaika is subject, al-khasirun predicate, and hum an emphatic separator. — Zajjaj calls the separator imad in Kufan terminology; Nasafi stresses emphasis. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:27/q19/p2 Hum starts a second nominal clause whose predicate completes the first. — Zajjaj, Nahhas, and Qurtubi allow the nested clause. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 2:27/q19/p3 Hum is extra; al-khasirun alone predicates ulaika. — Nahhas reports this; Qurtubi permits redundancy. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 2:27/q20 What force does the sequence and diction carry?  [* · rhetoric]
- 2:27/q20/p1 Escalating acts, recurring verbs, passive joining, and a distant demonstrative intensify the condemnation. — Abu Hayyan orders breach, broader severance, and broader corruption; imperfect verbs renew a settled fasiq trait. The passive includes ties others must join. Biqai and Alusi read distance from good; Alusi sees exclusive loss in a trade image. Samin notes the echo of 2:5. [9 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:27/q21 How are the three verbs connected syntactically?  [يَنقُضُونَ وَيَقْطَعُونَ وَيُفْسِدُونَ · grammar]
- 2:27/q21/p1 Yaqtaun and yufsidun are coordinated with yanqudun in the relative clause. — Nahhas makes ahd Allah the first verb's object and ma the second's; fī l-arḍ attaches to the third. [5 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:27/q22 How does this verse connect to its surroundings?  [* · links]
turns on: Contextual explanation and verbal parallel.
- 2:27/q22/p1 It describes the fasiqin of the preceding parable and is echoed by covenant breakers in 13:25. — Ibn Kathir cites 13:19–25. Tabatabai contrasts fulfillers and breakers there. Abu Hayyan links parables to guidance or misguidance. Elmalili connects bad parable interpretation with practical wrongdoing and the next shift to direct address; Baghawi sees a turn to Arab polytheists. Abu Hayyan cites reports that children are born in a natural state and those dying before maturity enter Paradise; Asad connects the innate bond to divine nearness in 50:16 and 13:25. [11 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 2:27/q22/p2 The verse's reversals and final wording echo nearby contrasts and 2:5. — Abu Hayyan pairs small and great, belief and unbelief, guidance and misguidance, and breach after confirmation; Samin notes the final-clause echo. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:27/q23 What event occasioned this passage?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on objectors and examples.
- 2:27/q23/p1 Hypocrites objected to God's fire and rainstorm parables as beneath divine majesty. — Suddi and the companion report cite 2:17 and 2:19 and say the response runs through 'the losers'. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود وناس من الصحابة)]
- 2:27/q23/p2 Polytheists objected to the fly and spider examples. — Ibn Abbas's report cites 22:73 and 29:41. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 2:27/q24 What does fisq mean for the people described?  [ٱلَّذِينَ · theology]
turns on: The general meaning of disobedience versus the specific status of a sinner.
- 2:27/q24/p1 Fisq is departure from obedience, and the people here are unbelievers. — Ibn Kathir compares the date leaving its skin and mouse leaving its hole and cites 13:19–25. Elmalili ties the judgment to combined breach, severance, and corruption. [6 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 2:27/q24/p2 Fisq has degrees; only denial that sin is ugly amounts to unbelief. — Elmalili describes occasional acknowledged sin, habitual sin, and denial; Sunnis retain believer status before the third, Mu'tazilites say an intermediate rank, Kharijites call all three unbelief. Ibn Kathir cites the Sahih report on five harmful creatures including mice. [4 notes: ELMALILI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عائشة)]

## 2:27/q25 What is the primordial covenant in Qashani's inward reading?  [عَهْدَ ٱللَّهِ · inward]
- 2:27/q25/p1 Innate knowledge of unity planted by the holy intellect, later obscured by bodily desire. — Qashani reports without grading that God drew Adam's descendants like particles. He reads God's hand as holy intellect and first spirit, Adam as universal rational soul, and offspring as individual potential souls. The primordial Yes is innate assent and proof appears when veils lift. [5 notes: QASHANI]

## 2:27/q26 What does Qashani's prophetic report say about aims?  [وَيَقْطَعُونَ مَآ أَمَرَ ٱللَّهُ بِهِۦٓ أَن يُوصَلَ · reports]
- 2:27/q26/p1 God loves elevated aims and hates base ones. — Qashani cites the Prophet without grading. [1 notes: QASHANI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 2:27/q27 How can divine will and human choice coexist?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether necessity relative to the complete cause negates choice relative to the agent.
- 2:27/q27/p1 God owns all existence and capacity, while humans choose and own their acts within His ownership. — Tabatabai distinguishes existence from moral attribution and complete from partial causes; a fully caused event is necessary, but possible relative to its human agent. Ali, al-Rida, and al-Sadiq reject both coercion and total delegation. Commands and recompense assume choice; planning and education assume human possibility. [46 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر وأبو عبد الله عليهما السلام,أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,الحجاج بن يوسف,الرضا عليه السلام,جعفر بن محمد الصادق عليه السلام,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي بن أبي طالب عليه السلام); TABATABAI+]
- 2:27/q27/p2 Compulsion makes deeds fixed by divine will and voids effective choice. — Tabatabai states the fatalist inference and rejects its effects on responsibility, justice, reward, and punishment. He rejects materialist determinism's confusion of complete and partial causes. [4 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر وأبو عبد الله عليهما السلام,أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,الرضا عليه السلام,علي بن أبي طالب عليه السلام)-]
- 2:27/q27/p3 Delegation makes humans independent creators outside divine will. — Tabatabai reports Mu'tazilites affirming choice but accepting the fatalist premise that divine will cancels it; he objects that God's ownership remains. [1 notes: TABATABAI(المعتزلة); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)-]

## 2:27/q28 How are sin and divine misguidance attributed?  [* · theology]
- 2:27/q28/p1 God creates existence; evil as deprivation and blame attach to human acts, while misguidance follows freely chosen evil. — Tabatabai cites 9:3, 39:62, 32:7, 4:79, 4:78, and 7:28. Al-Rida says God withholds help and seals hearts after persistent rejection; the Prophet and Ali distinguish divine gifts from blameworthy choices. Misfortune is evil relative to a lost blessing. [13 notes: TABATABAI(أبو الحسن الثالث عليه السلام,الرضا عليه السلام,جعفر بن محمد الصادق عليه السلام,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي بن أبي طالب عليه السلام)]

## 2:27/q29 What reality underlies deeds and final recompense?  [ٱلْخَٰسِرُونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether the descriptions name real states or figures.
- 2:27/q29/p1 Deeds become embodied recompense, already present but veiled; spiritual labels name real states. — Tabatabai cites 50:22 and many recompense verses; embodied deeds and recompense are compatible accounts. Distinct effects reveal underlying realities, and a hidden happy or wretched life becomes manifest. [6 notes: TABATABAI]
- 2:27/q29/p2 Prior life and afterlife language is figurative. — Tabatabai reports most exegetes reading prior life as disposition and afterlife as metaphor; he says many verses resist this. [1 notes: TABATABAI(الجمهور من المفسرين)]
- 2:27/q29/p3 Human life has a real prior stage, a worldly interval, and a later stage. — Tabatabai says each stage follows the one before it. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 2:27/q30 How is the preceding ماذا parsed?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā and dhā form one noun or a subject-predicate expression.
- 2:27/q30/p1 Mādhā is one accusative noun meaning 'what?'. — Zajjaj applies this to what God intended by the example. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]
- 2:27/q30/p2 Mā is the subject and dhā a relative predicate. — Zajjaj reports this alternative analysis. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]

## 2:27/q31 How is the preceding ٱلْفَٰسِقِينَ governed?  [* · grammar]
- 2:27/q31/p1 It is accusative after an ellipsis: He misguides none by it except the fāsiqīn. — Zajjaj supplies the implied verb and exception construction. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]

## 2:27/q32 How does the following كَيْف function grammatically?  [* · grammar]
turns on: The category assigned to kayfa and whether analogy licenses a jussive.
- 2:27/q32/p1 It is chiefly interrogative; conditional use is rare and its jussive is unattested. — Abu Hayyan rejects the Kufan and Qutrub analogical jussive. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:27/q32/p2 It may be an adverb or a non-adverb noun. — Abu Hayyan reports Sibawayh for the adverb and Akhfash and Sirafi for the noun; its answer's case follows the verb. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سيبويه)]
