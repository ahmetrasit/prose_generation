# 18:103: 22 questions

## 18:103/q01 What does the command قُلْ signal, and who carries it out?  [قُلْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the command is understood as the Prophet's delivery alone or as also involving believers.
- 18:103/q01/p1 Muhammad is commanded to deliver an important warning to the heedless and unbelievers. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says opening with 'say' marks an utterance worthy of attention; Biqāʿī describes recipients whose eyes and ears fail to heed remembrance. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 18:103/q01/p2 The Prophet and the believers who follow him announce the warning. — Bursevī extends the command's delivery to believers alongside the Prophet. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 18:103/q02 What force does هَلْ have in this question?  [هَلْ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes distinguish an inviting offer, rebuke, ironic offer, and several reported uses of hal.
- 18:103/q02/p1 It is an inviting prompt meaning 'shall we' or 'would you like us to'. — Ibn al-Jawzī's lexicon applies the 'alā' use to 18:103 and compares 20:10, 26:221, 34:7, and 61:10. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 18:103/q02/p2 It is a rebuking or emphatic question. — Ibn Qutayba likens hal to the interrogative hamza, citing 27:60; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls this question a taunting rebuke of unbelievers. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن قتيبة); IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 18:103/q02/p3 It offers to inform the hearers ironically, since the disclosure does not depend on their assent. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the speaker will inform them regardless; Ālūsī calls the supposed request for leave deeply sarcastic. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:103/q02/p4 In ordinary use, it can be a genuine question. — Ibn al-Jawzī's lexicon illustrates this with 7:53, 10:35, 30:28, and 50:30; it does not explicitly select this use for 18:103. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 18:103/q02/p5 It can mean qad, introducing a report. — The lexicon cites questions introducing Moses, the disputants, Abraham's guests, the human being, and the Overwhelming Event: 20:9, 38:21, 51:24, 76:1, 88:1. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 18:103/q02/p6 It can mean mā, with a negative or exclusive implied answer. — The lexicon compares 2:210, 6:158, 7:53, 16:35, 43:66, and 55:60. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 18:103/q02/p7 It is said to mean illā here. — Al-Burhān lists 18:103 as an example of this usage. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 18:103/q03 Who is meant by the plural speaker in نُنَبِّئُكُم?  [نُنَبِّئُكُم · rhetoric]
turns on: The nūn may be divine majesty, a shared speaker, or a signal about prior knowledge.
- 18:103/q03/p1 It is God's majestic plural, with a shift from the expected third-person 'He informs you'. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this explanation of the plural nūn. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:103/q03/p2 It refers to God together with the Messenger. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this as one alternative. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:103/q03/p3 It refers to the Messenger together with the Muslims. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this as the other alternative; Bursevī includes believers among those told to deliver the announcement. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 18:103/q03/p4 The plural either identifies the speaker at once or intimates that believers already know the news. — Ālūsī gives both rhetorical explanations. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:103/q04 To whom is the address كُم directed?  [نُنَبِّئُكُم · referent]
turns on: The notes specify the audience at different levels: unbelievers, contextual polytheists, and the heedless.
- 18:103/q04/p1 It addresses unbelievers, particularly the polytheists in the surrounding passage. — Bursevī names unbelievers; Ālūsī says the question addresses unbelievers; Ibn ʿĀshūr says polytheists are rebuked and alerted to their failed efforts; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says their identity is confirmed by the later denial of God's signs and meeting. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 18:103/q04/p2 It warns people heedless of remembrance, whose eyes and ears miss the warning. — Biqāʿī takes the addressees from the preceding description. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 18:103/q05 Why ask before naming the greatest losers?  [هَلْ نُنَبِّئُكُم بِٱلْأَخْسَرِينَ · rhetoric]
- 18:103/q05/p1 The question rebukes the hearers and builds curiosity before exposing them as the losers. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it a taunting rebuke; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the shift from second to third person delays the reveal that the listeners themselves are meant, and calls the offer ironic; Ālūsī notes its sarcasm. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:103/q06 Who are the greatest losers in deeds?  [بِٱلْأَخْسَرِينَ أَعْمَٰلًا · referent]
turns on: The disagreement concerns whether named communities are exclusive referents, illustrative cases, or displaced by the immediate polytheist context; reports from ʿAlī and Saʿd differ.
- 18:103/q06/p1 They are Jews and Christians, or the disbelieving People of the Book. — Al-Thaʿlabī cites 88:3–4; Saʿd says Jews denied Muhammad and Christians denied food and drink in Paradise, while calling the Kharijites covenant breakers; Biqāʿī adds that both deny bodily resurrection. Al-ʿAyyāshī's report describes scriptural communities that disbelieved and innovated. Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya treat specific reports as applications, not the original referent. [19 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); THALABI(سعد بن أبي وقاص وابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(سعد بن أبي وقاص); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس رواية عطاء,الكلبي); JISHUMI(سعد بن أبي وقاص، ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس وسعد بن أبي وقاص); TABRISI(العياشي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعد بن أبي وقاص); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، author); BIQAI-FULL(سعد بن أبي وقاص); TABATABAI(علي بن أبي طالب); NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(سعد)+; IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعد بن أبي وقاص)+; QURTUBI-FULL(سعد)+; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية)-; TABATABAI-]
- 18:103/q06/p2 They are monks and priests, especially Christian ascetics confined in cells or monasteries. — ʿAlī is repeatedly cited; al-Ḍaḥḥāk also appears. Abū Ḥayyān compares the 'toiling, weary' language of 88:3. Reports vary between cells, monasteries, and pillars; Jishumī gives this as an occasion of revelation. Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects its status as the original contextual referent. [12 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(علي بن أبي طالب); MAWARDI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); WAHIDI-BASIT(علي); JISHUMI(علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(علي والضحاك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي); QURTUBI-FULL(علي); DURR-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); TABATABAI(علي بن أبي طالب); NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية)-; TABATABAI-]
- 18:103/q06/p3 They are the polytheists who worship idols or other beings and think that worship benefits them. — Qurṭubī says the question rebukes those worshipping other than God; Ibn ʿĀshūr connects it to reliance on useless idol patrons; Ṭabāṭabāʾī appeals to 18:106 and the surrounding passage. Biqāʿī lists angels, stars, prophets, idols, and other unbelief; Kur'an Yolu says idolaters deem shirk obedience. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the next verse fits Meccan idolaters. [9 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI+]
- 18:103/q06/p4 They include the Harurites or Kharijites. — Reports attribute this to ʿAlī, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, and Abū ʿAlī; one al-ʿAyyāshī report says they are 'not far' from the People of the Book. Jishumī would accept ʿAlī's report if authentic, arguing that kufr can include rejection of some signs. Al-Aṣamm objects that the verse describes unbelievers while the Harurites are Muslims; Saʿd explicitly names another group. Ibn Kathīr calls them included but not exclusive; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the Jewish-to-Kharijite report unsound. [13 notes: THALABI(علي); MAWARDI-FULL(علي); WAHIDI-BASIT(علي); JISHUMI(أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); TABRISI(العياشي); QURTUBI-FULL(علي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، author); IBNKATHIR-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب والضحاك); DURR-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); TABATABAI(علي بن أبي طالب); IBNKATHIR-FULL+; JISHUMI+; JISHUMI(الأصم)-; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية,سعد)-; IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعد بن أبي وقاص)-; DURR-FULL(سعد)-; TABATABAI-]
- 18:103/q06/p5 The label broadly covers anyone whose unapproved religious path seems sound to its follower. — Abū Ḥayyān says named groups are examples rather than an exhaustive definition, including outsiders to Islam, show-offs, and heretics whose innovations reach unbelief. Ibn Kathīr says the Meccan date precedes Kharijites and the relevant Jewish-Christian address, and cites 88:2–4, 25:23, and 24:39. He says these people believe their deeds accepted and beloved. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، author); IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 18:103/q06/p6 They are the sinful or transgressive people of Quraysh. — Two reports attribute this answer to ʿAlī when Ibn al-Kawwāʾ asked him; the second cites 18:103–104. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); TABATABAI(علي بن أبي طالب)]
- 18:103/q06/p7 They are adherents of religious whims or heresies. — Māwardī records the first description; Abū Ḥayyān includes innovators whose heresy leads to unbelief within the broad warning. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، author)]
- 18:103/q06/p8 They are show-offs or people who boast of good deeds. — Māwardī reports boasters; Abū Ḥayyān includes show-offs as an application. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، author)]
- 18:103/q06/p9 They are hypocrites whose deeds contradict belief. — Māwardī records this possible referent. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 18:103/q06/p10 They are people who seek this world and abandon the next. — Māwardī gives this possibility twice, once alongside hypocrites. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 18:103/q06/p11 They are the Sabians. — Abū Ḥayyān records this among the named groups. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، author)]
- 18:103/q06/p12 They are Christians specifically. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes a report naming Christians without a wider group. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 18:103/q07 What degree or kind of loss does ٱلْأَخْسَرِينَ express?  [بِٱلْأَخْسَرِينَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the explanation stresses the comparative degree or the practical image of fruitless investment.
- 18:103/q07/p1 It marks the greatest or more severe loss among losers or among creation. — Sāmarrāʾī says akhsar exceeds khāsir and ranks some losers below others; Wāḥidī says these deeds make them the greatest losers among creation; Bursevī similarly says greatest loss among creatures. [4 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; WAHIDI-BASIT; BURSEVI]
- 18:103/q07/p2 It portrays exhausting effort that ends in ruin, like a merchant who expects profit but loses the investment. — Thaʿlabī and Baghawī use the failed merchant analogy; Wāḥidī al-Wajīz says their deeds are disastrously lost. [3 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ]

## 18:103/q08 How is أَعْمَٰلًا parsed, and why is it plural?  [بِٱلْأَخْسَرِينَ أَعْمَٰلًا · grammar]
turns on: The main analysis takes aʿmālan as tamyīz; alternatives differ over its underlying singular and whether it denotes workers.
- 18:103/q08/p1 It is an accusative specification of the loss; its plural reflects diverse deeds, desires, or agents. — Sībawayh notes that the definite plural adjective retains nūn, preventing annexation; al-Akhfash likewise says the article and plural ending require accusative specification. Al-Zajjāj and Ibn al-Jawzī say it clarifies what is lost. Bayḍāwī, Samīn, Bursevī, and Ālūsī explain the plural by diverse actions or agents; Nasafī contrasts expected singular specification and refers to many desires. Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses varied losses; Biqāʿī names worship of angels, stars, prophets, idols, and other disbelief. [12 notes: SIBAWAYH; AKHFASH; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 18:103/q08/p2 It is a rare plural of ʿāmil, 'worker'. — Ālūsī reports this analysis and says some grammarians deny such plurals except for a few established forms. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:103/q08/p3 It is a plural of ʿamal used for people possessed of deeds. — Ālūsī attributes the proposal to al-Qāmūs and finds it strained. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(القاموس)]

## 18:103/q09 What do ʿamal and the related work terms imply here?  [أَعْمَٰلًا · meaning]
- 18:103/q09/p1 ʿAmal denotes work without itself requiring the skill and intent conveyed by ṣunʿ. — Sāmarrāʾī distinguishes saʿy as running or brisk walking, iḥsān as mastery, fiʿl as applicable even to inanimate things, ʿamal as ordinary work, and ṣunʿ as finer intentional work of a rational agent. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 18:103/q10 Why does the verse attach the strongest loss to deeds?  [بِٱلْأَخْسَرِينَ أَعْمَٰلًا · rhetoric]
- 18:103/q10/p1 The passage joins lost striving to supposedly skillful action, and deeds frame the surrounding discussion. — Sāmarrāʾī says this is the Qur'an's sole attribution of loss to work and compares 18:30; Ibn Kathīr says they follow a way not approved by God while expecting acceptance. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 18:103/q11 What makes these people's work a loss?  [بِٱلْأَخْسَرِينَ أَعْمَٰلًا · interpretation]
turns on: The notes differ on whether failure lies in unbelief, mistaken evaluation of evil acts, corruption of good acts, or the aim toward which effort is directed.
- 18:103/q11/p1 They labor expecting gain or reward, but their worldly effort comes to nothing while they suppose it good. — Yaḥyā reports al-Suddī's description; Thaʿlabī and Baghawī compare a failed trader; Ṭabrisī says the disbelieving Jews and Christians thought their worship virtuous; Ibn Kathīr says the work is invalid under an approved religious way though they think it accepted; Study Quran connects futility to denial of God's signs and meeting Him, citing 7:147. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; WAHIDI-BASIT; STUDYQURAN]
- 18:103/q11/p2 Disbelief or corrupt worship voids even acts good in themselves, although their doers expect reward. — Qurṭubī names corrupt belief, with disbelief specific here; al-Irshād gives charity and freeing slaves as intrinsically good examples; Ālūsī relays the contrast with intrinsically bad acts; Jishumī says worship of others leads to the Fire; Ibn ʿĀshūr says idol patrons are useless. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL(إرشاد العقل السليم); JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:103/q11/p3 They treat intrinsically bad acts, including shirk, as good worship. — Ālūsī relays this contrast from Irshād al-ʿAql al-Salīm; Kur'an Yolu says polytheists believe idols draw them near God and deem shirk obedience, yet their deeds receive no value in either life; Biqāʿī details varied forms of object worship. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(إرشاد العقل السليم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 18:103/q11/p4 Good deeds can be ruined by boastfulness, reproaching beneficiaries, or worship for display. — Abū Bakr al-Warrāq specifies demanding thanks and performing worship for reputation; Māwardī mentions boasters; Qurṭubī names ostentation as a possible cause of nullification. [3 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر الوراق); MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 18:103/q11/p5 Choosing worldly gain over God's pleasure and the afterlife redirects effort toward a different kind of work. — Kur'an Yolu contrasts aims; Māwardī identifies those seeking this world and abandoning the next. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; MAWARDI-FULL]

## 18:103/q12 What duty or warning does the question teach?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw both a duty to examine one's striving and a warning of its final consequence.
- 18:103/q12/p1 One must pursue truth seriously, since earnest effort can still go astray. — Jishumī expressly draws this duty from 'shall We inform you'; Qurṭubī warns that people can think nullified work good. [2 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 18:103/q12/p2 Those whose deeds fail through these evils face Hell. — Yaḥyā links the question to Hell prepared for unbelievers; Jishumī says worshippers of others exhaust themselves and end in the Fire; Kur'an Yolu says the offenders described here will be punished in Hell. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; JISHUMI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 18:103/q13 How is the lām of هَلْ pronounced before نُنَبِّئُكُم?  [هَلْ نُنَبِّئُكُم · readings]
turns on: The reading disagreement turns on lām assimilation before nūn and the scope of Hishām's transmitted routes.
- 18:103/q13/p1 Al-Kisāʾī assimilates the lām into the following nūn. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports that al-Kisāʾī assimilates lām of hal or bal before all eight listed consonants, including this nūn; al-Naḥḥās says assimilation is possible at a distance because the articulation points are close. [2 notes: NASHR(الكسائي); NAHHAS(حمزة)]
- 18:103/q13/p2 Ḥamza and Hishām make the lām distinct before this nūn. — Al-Naḥḥās judges Ḥamza's reading more correct on Sībawayh's concern about lām-to-nūn assimilation. Ibn al-Jazarī says Ḥamza assimilates before tāʾ, thāʾ, and sīn but not this nūn; Hishām shows lām before ḍād and nūn and assimilates before six other consonants, which the author calls the sound majority view. [3 notes: NASHR(حمزة,هشام); NAHHAS(حمزة)+]
- 18:103/q13/p3 Hishām's assimilated readings differ by transmission route. — Some confine his assimilation to al-Ḥalwānī; al-ʿAlāʾ, al-Dānī, and al-Hudhalī give both al-Ḥalwānī and al-Dājūnī; al-Dānī and al-Hudhalī explicitly report complete assimilation without a dispute. [2 notes: NASHR(أبو عمرو الداني وأبو القاسم الهذلي,أهل الأداء)]

## 18:103/q14 What pronunciation principles are recorded with the recitation material?  [* · readings]
turns on: These are distinct pronunciation and madd topics in the cited material.
- 18:103/q14/p1 Each letter's point of articulation and qualities must be learned in isolation and in context. — Ibn al-Jazarī says practice makes precision natural and neighbouring letters alter sounds. He compares hamza with hāʾ, ʿayn with ḥāʾ, ghayn with khāʾ, jīm with shīn and yāʾ, ḍād with ẓāʾ, ṭāʾ with dāl and tāʾ, ẓāʾ with dhāl and thāʾ, and ṣād with zāy and sīn; he specifies the relevant differences in voicing, stoppage, openness, emphasis, frication, spreading, and istiṭāla. [11 notes: NASHR]
- 18:103/q14/p2 Madd lengths follow the stronger operative cause and vary with continuation, pause, and reader. — Ibn al-Jazarī says lexical madd before hamza outranks semantic madd; at a stop on words like yashāʾ qasr is excluded, and a reciter using full madd in continuation cannot use tawassuṭ. Warsh keeps full madd on al-samāʾ; al-Azraq's pause options on yastahziʾūn, muttakiʾīn, and al-maʾāb track connected reading; Warsh's examples raʾā aydiyahum, jāʾū abāhum, and al-sūʾā an kadhdhabū allow three pause options, while barāʾ and āmīn al-bayt stay full. [5 notes: NASHR]
- 18:103/q14/p3 Madd levels may be counted in alifs, but the count describes a continuum; temporary madd allows three lengths. — Al-Itqān reports al-Hudhalī's five-alif sixth level for Ḥamza and six-alif seventh level for Warsh; Ibn al-Jazarī calls such level counting verbal because small increases create the next level. For temporary madd, the author allows extension, intermediate length, or shortening for every reciter. [3 notes: ITQAN(ابن الجزري)]

## 18:103/q15 What occasion of revelation is reported for the losers?  [* · reports]
turns on: The competing reports name different groups; one is challenged by the description of unbelief.
- 18:103/q15/p1 The verse was revealed concerning monks and priests confined in monasteries. — Jishumī attributes the report to ʿAlī. [1 notes: JISHUMI(علي)]
- 18:103/q15/p2 The verse was revealed concerning the people of Harūrāʾ. — Jishumī attributes the report to Abū ʿAlī; al-Aṣamm objects that the following language calls them unbelievers though they are Muslims, while Jishumī would accept an authentic report and permits kufr for denial of some signs. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي); JISHUMI+; JISHUMI(الأصم)-]

## 18:103/q16 How does this warning fit the surrounding sūra and verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The cited links emphasize different elements of the surrounding account.
- 18:103/q16/p1 It turns prior warnings about idolatrous reliance and failed perception into an account of futile works and leads toward the close of the sūra. — Yaḥyā places it after Hell is prepared for unbelievers; Biqāʿī connects heedlessness of remembrance to this warning; Ibn ʿĀshūr links idol patrons and their hoped-for favor to futile effort; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the six verses conclude themes of worldly seduction, protectors besides God, spiritual blindness, and bad ends before 18:110. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 18:103/q16/p2 The surrounding account also affirms Gog and Magog and the trumpet blast as signs of the Hour. — Jishumī records these earlier passage themes. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 18:103/q17 How does the term for greatest loss relate to other Qur'anic passages?  [بِٱلْأَخْسَرِينَ · links]
turns on: The comparative explanations turn on which offences and emphatic wording each passage contains.
- 18:103/q17/p1 Hūd's offenders are worse than Naḥl's because they obstruct others as well as themselves. — Sāmarrāʾī compares 11:22, 16:109, and 27:5, observing the added obstruction of God's path. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 18:103/q17/p2 Hūd uses stronger emphasis than Naml because it adds charges of obstructing God's path and lying against God. — Sāmarrāʾī compares 11:18–22 with 27:4–5. He reports grammarians' analyses of lā jarama as an oath or as 'truly/rightfully'; either reinforces the Hūd assertion. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(النحاة)]

## 18:103/q18 What report about works and the Qur'an accompanies this verse?  [* · reports]
- 18:103/q18/p1 The Qur'an appears as a fragrant companion to one who acted by it, comforts him, intercedes, and secures his honor. — Kaʿb says it identifies itself as the person's good deeds, recalls his thirsty days and wakeful nights to God, and he is crowned and robed. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(كعب)]

## 18:103/q19 How does the following alladhīna clause attach to this question?  [* · grammar]

- 18:103/q19/p2 Alladhīna may be accusative for censure. — Abū Ḥayyān gives this alternative; the following clause can be its description or apposition. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 18:103/q20 Which groups does Saʿd report as having strayed?  [* · reports]
- 18:103/q20/p1 Monastic ascetics and the Kharijites strayed, and God caused their hearts to stray. — Al-Durr transmits this as a second report from Saʿd alongside his separate identification of Jews and Christians. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(سعد)]

## 18:103/q21 How does the wording of نُنَبِّئُكُم here compare with the corresponding verb in al-Māʾida?  [نُنَبِّئُكُم · links]
- 18:103/q21/p1 Al-Māʾida uses أَنَبِّئُكُم with hamza, while al-Kahf uses نُنَبِّئُكُم with nūn. — Al-Burhān contrasts the opening letters of the two forms. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 18:103/q22 What does نُنَبِّئُكُم imply compared with a verb for ordinary news?  [نُنَبِّئُكُم · meaning]
- 18:103/q22/p1 The verb anbaʾ conveys more momentous news than akhbara. — Sāmarrāʾī says nabaʾ is weightier than khabar and illustrates the distinction with broadcasts: nashrat al-anbāʾ for major news, nashrat al-akhbār for ordinary news. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
