# 41:35: 15 questions

## 41:35/q01 What does yulaqqaha mean here?  [يُلَقَّىٰهَآ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb emphasizes reception of a granted quality or the person's effort to cultivate it.
- 41:35/q01/p1 To receive or be granted the virtue, and thus accept and practice it. — Jishumi explains talaqqi as receiving a report from another person and cites al-Mu'arrij's gloss 'accepting.' Bursevi says talqiya can mean bringing something before someone; here it is receiving the virtue that restrains revenge. Ibn Kathir stresses overcoming the soul's resistance to accept and practice difficult counsel. Damghani says liqa can mean being given something and cites 76:11 for this sense. [4 notes: JISHUMI; BURSEVI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DAMGHANI]
- 41:35/q01/p2 To make oneself attain the virtue, as though seeking and meeting someone. — Ibn Ashur takes the meeting as a metaphor for cultivation and compares 76:11. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 41:35/q02 What does the feminine pronoun in yulaqqaha refer to?  [يُلَقَّىٰهَآ · referent]
turns on: Which preceding idea supplies the feminine antecedent; the conduct in 41:34 is explicit in context, whereas some alternatives require a more remote referent.
- 41:35/q02/p1 The virtue or better response of repelling evil with good, described in the previous verse. — Zajjaj allows the act itself. Jishumi calls it the stated virtue or way of life; Bagha wi, al-Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi's report from Zajjaj, al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi and al-Alusi identify the conduct or disposition. Al-Alusi also allows the better response itself. Ibn Ashur says ordinary good deeds need no such patience, so the antecedent is specifically best conduct in repelling. Islahi says the antecedent is unstated, a common Arabic construction, and compares 2:45. Nahhas describes the expression as an indirect reference to the state just mentioned. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; NAHHAS; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 41:35/q02/p2 The recompense for the virtuous response. — Zajjaj offers receiving the reward for the act as an alternative to receiving the act itself. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]
- 41:35/q02/p3 Righteous deeds. — Jishumi transmits this alternative without further grounds. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 41:35/q02/p4 Paradise itself. — Al-Qurtubi says the sense remains close because the patient attain Paradise. Al-Samin lists the Garden among possible antecedents; al-Alusi reports but dismisses it. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 41:35/q02/p5 The testimony that there is no god but God. — Makki gives this explanation in Abu Hayyan, who finds it far-fetched. Al-Samin lists the testimony of divine unity; al-Alusi reports it as construing the testimony as the best response but finds it unpersuasive. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مكي)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 41:35/q02/p6 The word mentioned previously. — Al-Samin lists 'the word' alongside the quality, Garden and testimony, without further identification. Nahhas also describes it as an indirect reference to the expression just mentioned. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; NAHHAS]

## 41:35/q03 How is yulaqqaha read?  [يُلَقَّىٰهَآ · readings]
turns on: Whether the verb is the passive of talqiya or derives from mulaqat, meeting.
- 41:35/q03/p1 The common passive reading means that the trait is received or granted. — Abu Hayyan attributes this to the majority; al-Samin calls it the passive of talqiya. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR]
- 41:35/q03/p2 Talha ibn Musarrif and Ibn Kathir in one transmission read yulaqaha, meaning to meet or encounter it. — Abu Hayyan gives the meaning and names both readers; al-Samin derives it from mulaqat; al-Alusi reports the same variant. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(طلحة بن مصرف وابن كثير); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 41:35/q04 What is the syntax of the clause beginning wa-ma yulaqqaha?  [وَمَا يُلَقَّىٰهَآ · grammar]
turns on: How this clause attaches grammatically to the command in 41:34.
- 41:35/q04/p1 It is coordinated with the preceding command. — Ibn Ashur gives this as one grammatical analysis and restricts the pronoun to the best way of repelling, since good deeds in general need not require patience. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 41:35/q04/p2 It is circumstantial to 'that which is best' in the preceding command. — Ibn Ashur gives this as his other grammatical analysis. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 41:35/q05 Who are the patient people able to receive this response, and why?  [ٱلَّذِينَ صَبَرُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes specify patience as control of anger and harm, wider obedience and desire-restraint, inward endurance, or a strong soul; Biqa'i stresses divine enabling.
- 41:35/q05/p1 Those who endure harm, restrain anger and forgo retaliation can answer evil with good. — Zajjaj and Ibn al-Jawzi stress restraining anger; al-Zajjaj as reported by al-Razi adds swallowing distress, enduring hardship and leaving revenge. Al-Wahidi, al-Tabrisi, al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Bursevi, Elmalili, Ibn Ashur and Study Quran connect patience with bearing injury and controlling the urge to retaliate. Al-Tabrisi reports Abu Abd Allah's formulation as enduring worldly harm. Ibn Ashur says this must be trained with resolve and links the patient exception to 103:2-3. Islahi calls patience necessary to acquire enduring wisdom; Kuran Yolu calls it the first condition of a high ethical aim. Ibn Kathir says the soul resists this difficult counsel. Baghawi, Tha'labi, Biqa'i and Tabataba'i likewise restrict this virtue to patient people. [24 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(أبو عبد الله); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs)]
- 41:35/q05/p2 Those steadfast in obedience and in turning away from desires receive it; patience encompasses many good qualities. — Al-Mawardi offers this as an alternative under the first restriction, connecting their patience with Paradise. Ibn Atiyya says patience in obedience and restraint from desires gathers goodness; Ibn Abbas as reported by Abu Hayyan describes the same steadfastness and calls the verse praise. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 41:35/q05/p3 Those whose souls are strong and pure can bear external harm without seeking revenge. — Al-Razi reasons that a strong inner substance is not shaken or hurt by events; Bursevi calls revenge a sign of weakness, says purification makes sweetness and bitterness alike, and regards inner strength as making injury easy to bear. Elmalili describes a high soul and spiritual virtue. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]
- 41:35/q05/p4 Those patient with God, trusting and imitating His forbearance, receive the trait. — Qashani describes remaining unchanged by enemies' slips, trusting God, taking on His forbearance or obeying His command, and calls the station a great gift. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 41:35/q05/p5 Those who endure the hardships of spiritual knowledge without self-regard enter its company. — Al-Sulami transmits this inward explanation, which makes endurance of spiritual knowledge and lack of self-valuation the qualifications. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 41:35/q05/p6 God grants the difficult virtue to those already patient. — Biqa'i draws this from the repeated passive 'is granted,' says the better response is contrary to human disposition and should be sought from God, and ranks it beyond forgiveness and forbearance. Junayd in Bursevi describes the station as special divine care. Damghani says patience is given only to the steadfast. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الجنيد); DAMGHANI]

## 41:35/q06 What does a great share mean here?  [حَظٍّ عَظِيمٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether hazz names the present person's qualities and fortune, a future reward, Paradise specifically, or an inward allotment.
- 41:35/q06/p1 Paradise, or a destined share of it. — Qatada is reported by Abd al-Razzaq, al-Tabari, Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Tabrisi and others; Ibn Atiyya says Qatada chooses it. Zajjaj says only those to whom Paradise is due receive it; Ibn Abbas in al-Tabari speaks of people prepared for Paradise; al-Hasan in al-Mawardi, al-Qurtubi and al-Nasafi says no share exceeds Paradise. Mujahid is named with Qatada by al-Qurtubi. Al-Baydawi, al-Alusi, Kuran Yolu and Study Quran also report the view. [18 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن,قتادة ومجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Atıyye); STUDYQURAN(IJ, Ṭ, Ṭs); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة)+]
- 41:35/q06/p2 A great share of goodness, good deeds, virtue, or general prosperity. — Al-Tabari adds a prior record of good deeds; Zajjaj and Ibn Abbas in al-Mawardi and al-Qurtubi speak of abundant goodness. Tha'labi and Baghawi add reward; al-Mawardi also offers excellent character. Al-Baydawi adds perfection of soul; Ibn Ashur specifies virtue, right guidance and piety, with patience necessary but only part of the qualification. Tabataba'i calls it human perfection and good qualities, especially belonging to the patient; Biqa'i adds standing with God and people in this life and the next. Ibn Ashur says the 'possessor' construction presents a settled disposition. Kuran Yolu says supporting virtues adorn the patient. Study Quran reports virtue and inner purity. [19 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Atıyye,İbn Âşûr); STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 41:35/q06/p3 A large share of afterlife reward, making the clause a promise. — Al-Razi contrasts this with the first clause's praise of patience. Ibn Atiyya, al-Tabrisi, Abu Hayyan, al-Alusi and Kuran Yolu report the reward reading; Qatada is named by Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi, while Ibn Abbas in al-Nasafi calls it reward. Ibn Kathir describes abundant happiness in this life and the next. [10 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Atıyye)]
- 41:35/q06/p4 Intelligence, reason, and insight. — Ibn Atiyya allows great intelligence and merit; al-Tabrisi says abundant insight and reason. Abu Hayyan reports intelligence; Kuran Yolu relays Ibn Atiyya's view; Study Quran reports abundant intellect and vision. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Atıyye); STUDYQURAN(Ṭs)]
- 41:35/q06/p5 Good fortune or prosperity. — Al-Suddi is reported by al-Tabari and Ibn al-Jawzi; al-Mawardi renders it great prosperity. Study Quran reports the good-fortune gloss. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); STUDYQURAN(IJ)]
- 41:35/q06/p6 Noble character, spiritual strength, or a perfected soul. — Al-Mawardi suggests excellent character; Abu Hayyan reports good character. Ibn Abbas in al-Alusi says excellent traits and a perfected soul, so the clause praises the person. Bursevi and Elmalili explain the allotment through inner strength and a high soul; Study Quran reports elevated virtue, strength and purity. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 41:35/q06/p7 Simply a share, without identifying its object. — Tha'labi transmits the bare gloss 'a person with a share.' Ibn Ashur defines hazz lexically as a share, sometimes particularly a share of good. [2 notes: THALABI(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 41:35/q06/p8 Knowledge of God and His days, or special divine favor. — Ibn Ata in Bursevi identifies the person by knowledge of God and His days; Junayd says only someone favored by God's special care receives the station. [2 notes: BURSEVI(ابن عطاء,الجنيد)]

## 41:35/q07 What wording is transmitted for the final restriction?  [إِلَّا ذُو حَظٍّ عَظِيمٍۢ · readings]
- 41:35/q07/p1 A report gives 'only everyone with a great share.' — Al-Tabrisi transmits this wording from Abu Abd Allah without further explanation. [1 notes: TABRISI(أبو عبد الله)]

## 41:35/q08 Why repeat 'none receives it' in the second clause?  [وَمَا يُلَقَّىٰهَآ وَمَا يُلَقَّىٰهَآ · rhetoric]
- 41:35/q08/p1 The repetition emphatically praises and elevates the rare moral act. — Abu Hayyan says the repetition emphasizes the trait's excellence; al-Alusi says it reinforces praise. Ibn Ashur says repeating the full clause rather than coordinating leaves none of the statement subordinate to a conjunction. Biqa'i says the repeated passive emphasizes that God enables the patient to acquire it. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 41:35/q09 Why shift from plural patient people to singular possessor of a great share?  [ٱلَّذِينَ صَبَرُوا۟ ذُو حَظٍّ عَظِيمٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 41:35/q09/p1 The singular marks the recipient's rarity and high rank. — Biqa'i explicitly reads the shift from the plural patient people to the singular possessor this way; Ibn Ashur adds that the possessor construction presents goodness as a settled disposition and warns that the counsel is not easy for everyone. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 41:35/q10 Why say 'those who were patient' rather than simply 'the patient'?  [ٱلَّذِينَ صَبَرُوا۟ · rhetoric]
- 41:35/q10/p1 The past form marks patience as an established prior trait, while the present verbs depict later cultivation. — Ibn Ashur contrasts the two present-tense verbs with the past 'were patient' and says this explains the choice over a simple adjective. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 41:35/q11 What rhetorical force does the wording around the better response have?  [وَمَا يُلَقَّىٰهَآ · rhetoric]
turns on: How to account for the force of the wording as reported by al-Nasafi.
- 41:35/q11/p1 It may answer an imagined question about how one should act. — Al-Nasafi offers this analysis of the wording. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]
- 41:35/q11/p2 Emphatic la and the stronger comparative intensify the command. — Al-Nasafi alternatively takes la as emphatic and says a comparative replaces an expected simple adjective to heighten the response. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 41:35/q12 What follows logically from the two restrictions?  [* · rhetoric]
- 41:35/q12/p1 Every patient person has a great share. — Salih Efendi treats the clauses as universal premises; al-Alusi reconstructs a first-figure syllogism: every patient person receives the act, and everyone who receives it has great fortune. The positive universal conclusion, called the noblest categorical form, helps a patient person recognize his good lot and remain patient. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(صالح أفندي)]

## 41:35/q13 What incidents or persons are connected with this counsel?  [* · reports]
turns on: Different transmitted identifications and illustrative events.
- 41:35/q13/p1 Abu Bakr bore an insult until he replied; an angel then left and Satan arrived, so the Prophet left. — Ibn Atiyya reports the episode and the Prophet's explanation; al-Tabari transmits Qatada's version, specifying that the Prophet would not sit with Satan. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 41:35/q13/p2 The Prophet was told to forgive and bear Abu Jahl's harm. — Muqatil says the Prophet disliked Abu Jahl, and patience in anger might turn an enemy into a close ally; he adds that Muhammad must seek refuge from Satan. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 41:35/q13/p3 A report relates it to Abu Sufyan ibn Harb, who harmed the Prophet and later became a close friend. — Al-Nasafi reports this as an occasion of revelation. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 41:35/q14 How is this difficult counsel applied in conduct and preaching?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the note draws out a universal ethical aim or its application to the Prophet's preaching.
- 41:35/q14/p1 It is a high, universal moral aim: answer wrong with the better response despite natural resistance. — Biqa'i says it exceeds mere forgiveness, forbearance and patience, serves religious and worldly interests, contradicts human disposition and must be sought from God. Kuran Yolu calls it a universal moral principle with patience as its first condition. Ibn Ashur urges training in it through resolve and patience. Islahi calls it lasting wisdom to cultivate. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 41:35/q14/p2 The Prophet should meet ignorant opposition with decency and forgiveness so the message can proceed. — Islahi says the Prophet bears a noble call; patient pardon of naive opponents who disrupt it benefits preaching. Muqatil applies the counsel to Abu Jahl's harm and the possibility that an enemy becomes a close ally. [3 notes: MUQATIL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 41:35/q15 What inward examples describe the share and demands of nearness to God?  [* · inward]
turns on: The notes give different inward illustrations: love's allotment and the discipline of divine service.
- 41:35/q15/p1 Spiritual love grants different shares; its perfected drink is inexhaustible. — Bursevi says some have a share of love but not fullness, while others never reach satiation, the state of perfection. Ibn al-Farid's cited verse says wasting life leaves no share of its wine-like blessing. Yahya ibn Muadh describes intoxication by love's cup; Abu Yazid replies that the strong continue drinking without exhausting it or becoming satiated even after seven seas. [3 notes: BURSEVI(ابن الفارض,يحيى بن معاذ الرازي)]
- 41:35/q15/p2 Nearness to God requires finer conduct and brings stricter accountability. — Ibn Ata in Bursevi contrasts proper conduct on entering and leaving divine service with bad conduct there, says poor manners nearby are graver than at a distance, and says God may pardon the ignorant for major sins while holding the elect to account for a glance or turning aside. [2 notes: BURSEVI(ابن عطاء)]
