# 40:55: 24 questions

## 40:55/q01 How does the command to be patient follow the surrounding account?  [فَٱصْبِرْ · links]
- 40:55/q01/p1 The fate of Moses and Pharaoh illustrates God's promised support, so Muhammad and the believers should persevere. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says Moses' outcome foreshadows Muhammad's. Qurṭubī and Bayḍāwī invoke Moses and the Children of Israel. Biqāʿī connects Moses' guidance, scripture and victory over Pharaoh with the promise to messengers. Bursawī likewise cites Moses. Alūsī says the stories console the Prophet. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Moses account interrupts the promise of 40:51, and the change from subjection to rule parallels the Muslims and hints at the Qur'an's endurance. Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects the true mission, opponents' false arguments, past peoples in 40:21, and victory to 40:51. Iṣlāḥī says the story warns Quraysh, encourages the believers, profiles the believer in Pharaoh's family for hesitant sympathizers, and shows worldly grandeur cannot save rejecters. Kur'an Yolu says the passage came in Mecca's difficult days; Study Quran notes earlier prophets' triumph. [15 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 40:55/q01/p2 The command resumes the explicit promise to help messengers and believers in 40:51. — Abū Ḥayyān grounds the command in 40:51. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it follows from the same promise; Alūsī and Kur'an Yolu identify the earlier assurance. Iṣlāḥī calls verse 55 a summary of the argument. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 40:55/q02 What does the command to be patient require?  [فَٱصْبِرْ · interpretation]
- 40:55/q02/p1 Muhammad should endure opponents' abuse, keep preaching and carrying out his mission, and await God's promised aid without weakening or haste. — Ṭabarī specifies delivery of revelation and trust in victory. Wāḥidī and Baghawī connect endurance with God's aid and destruction of enemies; Baghawī adds establishment of the religion. Ibn ʿAṭiyya stresses waiting for the promise. Ṭabrisī includes worldly victory and next-world reward. Al-Biqāʿī says events unfold through worldly causes and in their proper settings. Ibn ʿĀshūr warns against hastening victory. Iṣlāḥī adds Muhammad's companions and their continued duty. [23 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 40:55/q02/p2 Patience in obedience is a truthful refuge leading to the stations of the truthful and the afterlife. — Al-Nabbāḥī says fear of the afterlife motivates it; another report in Sulamī says patience in well-being is harder than seeking safety during fear. Al-Qushayrī says patience while awaiting the promise grows with faith and certainty. [4 notes: SULAMI(النباحي,بعضهم); BIQAI-FULL(القشيري)]

## 40:55/q03 Was the command to be patient abrogated?  [فَٱصْبِرْ · sciences]
- 40:55/q03/p1 The fighting or sword verse abrogated the command to endure. — Baghawī, Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān transmit al-Kalbī; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says al-Kalbī extended this to every patience command. Ibn al-Jawzī reports the view anonymously. [5 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكلبي)]

## 40:55/q04 What is the inward meaning of patience?  [فَٱصْبِرْ · inward]
- 40:55/q04/p1 The sincere seeker's heart endures the lower self, desire and Satan, trusting God's promise of victory over its unbelieving self. — Bursawī applies the verse to the heart's struggle. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 40:55/q05 What does God's promise encompass?  [وَعْدَ ٱللَّهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the promise is limited to worldly aid, extends to reward after death, or includes all divine promises.
- 40:55/q05/p1 God will support Muhammad, the believers and their message, vindicating them against opponents in this world. — Ṭabarī says the promise gives victory over deniers; Wāḥidī and Baghawī include destruction of enemies. Nasafī says God will exalt Muhammad's word; Ibn Kathīr says his message and followers gain the final outcome. Bursawī adds Islam's supremacy and the conquest of Mecca. Alūsī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Iṣlāḥī and Kur'an Yolu connect it to 40:51; Study Quran also cites 40:52. Muqātil says the mockers' promised punishment includes killing at Badr. [24 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 40:55/q05/p2 The promise includes victory or aid in this life and reward in the Hereafter. — Ṭabrisī explicitly names both. Iṣlāḥī calls it success for messengers and followers in both worlds; Study Quran reports the same dual scope. [3 notes: TABRISI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs)]
- 40:55/q05/p3 The phrase can embrace all divine promises, with victory especially in view. — Alūsī gives this as an alternative to the specific promise of victory. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 40:55/q06 How is the certainty of God's promise expressed?  [إِنَّ وَعْدَ ٱللَّهِ حَقٌّۭ · rhetoric]
- 40:55/q06/p1 The emphatic inna supplies the reason for patience: God does not break His promise, so the promised outcome is sure. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls inna emphatic and causal and says the Prophet need not hasten the result. Alūsī says victory must occur because God never breaks a promise. Iṣlāḥī says the verse summarizes the assurance. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 40:55/q07 What historical events are given as fulfillments of the promise?  [وَعْدَ ٱللَّهِ · reports]
turns on: Whether the account describes broad military vindication or the specific punishment of the mockers.
- 40:55/q07/p1 Badr, the conquest of Mecca, Ḥunayn and other campaigns realized the victory. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says Uḥud was a temporary setback testing believers and warning against disobeying the order to hold position, before the final outcome favored them. Bursawī also names the conquest of Mecca and Islam's supremacy. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 40:55/q07/p2 Badr brought the mockers' punishment through killing, angelic blows and souls rapidly sent to the Fire. — Muqātil reports the Meccans' mocking question about when punishment would come and gives these details of its arrival. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 40:55/q08 Who is addressed by the commands?  [فَٱصْبِرْ وَٱسْتَغْفِرْ وَسَبِّحْ · referent]
- 40:55/q08/p1 Muhammad is addressed directly, and believers are instructed through him. — Asad reports classical commentators and refers to his note on 24:31. Ibn Kathīr takes the forgiveness command as urging the wider community. Iṣlāḥī says Muhammad represents his people. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the community has even greater reason to seek forgiveness. [4 notes: ASAD-NOTES(all classical commentators); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:55/q09 What does 'your sin' mean and why seek forgiveness?  [لِذَنۢبِكَ · interpretation]
turns on: How prophetic protection from sin bears on the verse's direct wording and whether 'sin' is his, his community's, or a rank-relative lapse.
- 40:55/q09/p1 Seeking forgiveness is devotional worship that raises Muhammad's rank and gives later believers an example, without implying his own sin. — Baghawī and Ṭabrisī give rank and example; Ṭabrisī prefers this view. Qurṭubī says it fits those denying prophetic minor sins and cites 3:194. Abū Ḥayyān says worship can be commanded even when its result is guaranteed. Bursawī reports the view. Study Quran attributes it to the majority. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds continued protection, proper conduct and the stronger lesson for the community. Kur'an Yolu cites sound reports that the Prophet sought pardon seventy or one hundred times daily. [9 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(majority of Muslims); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABRISI+]
- 40:55/q09/p2 It refers to lapses relative to the Prophet's high station, such as leaving the better course, while he attends to and repairs his religious duties. — Bayḍāwī and al-Rāzī describe leaving what is more fitting. Alūsī says such lapses are not actual sins. Bursawī calls them occasional omissions of the better and distinguishes the Prophet's lapses of attention from ordinary forgetfulness. Elmalılı says forgiveness covers shortcomings one could not remedy. Ṭabāṭabāʾī rules out disobedience to a binding command. [7 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 40:55/q09/p3 Even a complete deed can be called a sin at the Prophet's station and occasion seeking forgiveness. — Biqāʿī says the Prophet is already forgiven; his example teaches the community. He cites the maxim that righteous people's good deeds count as faults for those drawn near to God. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 40:55/q09/p4 If prophets can commit minor sins, he is asked to repent for a minor sin of his own. — Ṭabrisī, Qurṭubī and Study Quran make the reading conditional on the possibility of minor prophetic sins. [3 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(those who maintain that prophets do commit minor sins)]
- 40:55/q09/p5 The 'sin' is the community's, and the Prophet is commanded to seek forgiveness for them. — Nasafī states the view; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān say the address can teach the community. Qurṭubī posits an omitted possessed noun. Ṭabāṭabāʾī's reported view adds intercession. Iṣlāḥī reads the attribution through Muhammad's representative role; Ibn Kathīr says the command urges the wider community. Bursawī rejects making it community-only because 47:19 separately commands forgiveness for believing men and women. Kur'an Yolu rejects exempting the Prophet from worship. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI(بعضهم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); BURSEVI-; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 40:55/q09/p6 It is the community's sin against the Prophet, with the genitive understood objectively. — Abū Ḥayyān offers this variant. Samīn says li-dhanbika can be an objective verbal noun; Alūsī calls the genitive relation objective. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 40:55/q09/p7 It refers to an error or sin before prophethood. — Qurṭubī reports the view; Kur'an Yolu mentions pre-prophethood errors in light of prophetic protection from sin. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 40:55/q09/p8 The command is conditional: seek pardon if a sin should issue from the Prophet. — Bursawī attributes this wording to ʿAyn al-Maʿānī. [1 notes: BURSEVI(عين المعاني)]
- 40:55/q09/p9 God knows what He means by the attribution, though people cannot ascribe sin to the Prophet. — Samīn states the apparent wording; Bursawī reports Ibn al-Shaykh and strongly endorses him. Bursawī reasons that prophethood exceeds sainthood, no saint can know a prophet's state or the attributed sin's reality, and only God knows it, comparing the secret of blessings on the Prophet in 33:56. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); BURSEVI+]

## 40:55/q10 How is the command to seek forgiveness related to later assurances of forgiveness?  [وَٱسْتَغْفِرْ لِذَنۢبِكَ · links]
turns on: Whether the chronological order explains the command or seeking forgiveness remains a continuing act after assurance.
- 40:55/q10/p1 The command may precede the later assurance of forgiveness in Sūrat al-Fatḥ. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reasons that this sūra is Meccan and al-Fatḥ later; Abū Ḥayyān transmits him and cites 48:2. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 40:55/q10/p2 A later assurance of forgiveness does not remove the Prophet's continuing duty of seeking pardon. — Kur'an Yolu says this command cannot be used to date the sūras, since the later Sūrat al-Naṣr also commands pardon at 110:3; the Prophet repeatedly sought forgiveness. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it continued protection and worship. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:55/q11 What is the inward meaning of seeking forgiveness?  [وَٱسْتَغْفِرْ لِذَنۢبِكَ · inward]
- 40:55/q11/p1 The heart seeks cleansing from lower-self traits; obedience and remembrance polish its clouded mirror. — Bursawī says the body belongs to the visible realm and the heart to the unseen; influences run both ways, so outward deeds repair and illuminate the heart. He reports ʿArāʾis al-Baqlī's description of the temporal entering the eternal realm as sin. [3 notes: BURSEVI(عرائس البقلي)]

## 40:55/q12 Why do forgiveness and glorification follow patience?  [فَٱصْبِرْ وَٱسْتَغْفِرْ وَسَبِّحْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the following commands are understood as means of patient endurance or anticipatory gratitude.
- 40:55/q12/p1 They cultivate the patience needed while awaiting the promised outcome. — Iṣlāḥī says seeking forgiveness, prayer and praise form the method for acquiring patience. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 40:55/q12/p2 They express gratitude for an assured victory even before it arrives. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says their placement indirectly confirms the victory and makes worship and conduct fitting while it is awaited. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:55/q13 What is commanded by 'glorify your Lord'?  [وَسَبِّحْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether tasbīḥ names ritual prayer, spoken praise, or permits both.
- 40:55/q13/p1 Pray at the named times. — Muqātil calls it the dawn and afternoon prayers; Qatāda says Quranic glorification refers to prayer. Ṭabarī says pray while thanking God. Wāḥidī says pray gratefully at both ends of the day. Ibn al-Jawzī and Ṭabrisī transmit or hold the prayer sense. [6 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 40:55/q13/p2 Declare God free of defect and praise Him in remembrance. — Ṭabrisī describes transcendence, attribution of blessings, denial of likeness, and an alternative purification of God's attributes or deeds from created or unjust qualities. Qurṭubī stresses gratitude. Biqāʿī connects God's perfection with growing awareness of human limits. Ṭabāṭabāʾī praises God's beautiful acts and blessings. Nasafī reports repeating 'Glory be to God and praise belongs to Him.' Kur'an Yolu prefers praise and glorification over a prayer-time reading. [6 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; NASAFI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr)+]
- 40:55/q13/p3 The words may signify either literal glorification and thanks or prayer. — Bayḍāwī offers both; Alūsī says literal glorification or prayer in the two periods, and observes that tasbīḥ need not be ritual prayer. Qurṭubī also reports praise within and outside prayer. Ibn ʿĀshūr makes the ordinary sense an argument against a revelation-date claim. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:55/q14 What does 'with praise' add to glorification?  [بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ · interpretation]
- 40:55/q14/p1 Glorification is joined with thanksgiving for God's blessings and praise of His perfection. — Ṭabarī says pray while thanking God; Wāḥidī says pray gratefully. Ṭabrisī adds attributing blessings to God, denying likeness and negating defect. Qurṭubī calls it gratitude; Biqāʿī says it affirms perfection. Ṭabāṭabāʾī praises beautiful acts. Ibn ʿĀshūr says praise completes inner and outer gratitude. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:55/q15 How do the prepositions in the time phrases work?  [بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ بِٱلْعَشِىِّ وَٱلْإِبْكَٰرِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the construction uses bi- simply as 'in' or distributes bi- and fī over both periods.
- 40:55/q15/p1 Bi- before 'evening' has the sense 'in', yielding 'in the evening and in the morning'. — Ṭabarī reports a Basran grammarian who says bi- can mean fī. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي البصرة)]
- 40:55/q15/p2 Bi- and implied fī are distributed over both coordinated periods: pray with praise at and in both times. — Ṭabarī reports another grammarian's account of the coordination. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي البصرة)]

## 40:55/q16 What do 'evening' and 'early morning' denote as times?  [بِٱلْعَشِىِّ وَٱلْإِبْكَٰرِ · meaning]
turns on: Where the two named periods begin and end, especially whether ibkār continues beyond sunrise.
- 40:55/q16/p1 Evening begins at the sun's decline and lasts until night; early morning is from second dawn until sunrise. — Ṭabarī defines both and prefers the dawn-to-sunrise limit as familiar Arab usage. Ṭabrisī attributes the same bounds to Mujāhid. Ibn ʿAṭiyya transmits Ṭabarī's morning boundary. Ibn ʿĀshūr describes evening as late day into night and ibkār as the morning's first part. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL; TAB-FULL+]
- 40:55/q16/p2 Early morning extends after sunrise into midmorning or forenoon. — Ṭabarī reports some speakers extend it beyond sunrise; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the sunrise-to-midmorning definition. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 40:55/q16/p3 Eventide is sunset and dawn the time before sunrise. — Study Quran transmits this timing with Bayḍāwī's two-cycle practice. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Bḍ)]
- 40:55/q16/p4 Evening is late day or early night, and morning is the beginning of day or late night. — Ibn Kathīr gives these broader descriptions. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 40:55/q16/p5 Ibkār and bukr have the same meaning. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya records the lexical equivalence. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 40:55/q17 Why are both evening and morning named?  [بِٱلْعَشِىِّ وَٱلْإِبْكَٰرِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the pair stands for the whole day, specifies favored times, or evokes their recurring cycle.
- 40:55/q17/p1 Together they express continuing praise and remembrance through all times. — Abū Ḥayyān gives all times as an alternative to special hours. Bayḍāwī, Bursawī, Alūsī, Elmalılı and Ibn ʿĀshūr take the pair as continuous praise; Bursawī says the terms span the day, and Elmalılı says neither heart nor tongue should grow heedless. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says praise can be continual. Qurṭubī reports praise inside and outside prayer to occupy the Prophet instead of hastening victory. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 40:55/q17/p2 They designate the two specified periods for glorification or prayer. — Abū Ḥayyān says these are hours of important human work; Alūsī permits a literal two-period reading. Ṭabāṭabāʾī allows morning and evening praise. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 40:55/q17/p3 Their recurring cycle points to God's power to restore the perished at Resurrection. — Biqāʿī draws this lesson from the alternation of evening and morning. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 40:55/q18 Which prayers are associated with evening and morning?  [وَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ بِٱلْعَشِىِّ وَٱلْإِبْكَٰرِ · interpretation]
turns on: Which acts the time words designate: two named daily prayers, all five, an earlier two-period regimen, or ʿIshāʾ and Fajr.
- 40:55/q18/p1 They are the ʿAṣr and Fajr prayers. — Muqātil, Qatāda and al-Ḥasan identify them thus; Biqāʿī adds that angels of night and day witness these prayers. The view is also reported by Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Qurṭubī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Alūsī, Kur'an Yolu and Ṭabāṭabāʾī; Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects to the related claim that the verse is Medinan while all five prayers were prescribed in Mecca. [13 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); TABATABAI(بعضهم)]
- 40:55/q18/p2 They indicate all five daily prayers. — Ibn ʿAbbās is cited by Baghawī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Abū Ḥayyān, Biqāʿī and Alūsī; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports that al-Ḥasan connected the verse to the five prayer times, though he placed their prescription in Medina. [7 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(الحسن)]
- 40:55/q18/p3 They were two morning and two evening prayer cycles before the five obligatory prayers. — Ibn al-Jawzī attributes two dawn and dusk cycles to al-Ḥasan. Qurṭubī reports al-Māwardī's Meccan practice and says it may have been abrogated. Bayḍāwī says two cycles in each period were obligatory in Mecca; Study Quran gives sunset and before sunrise. Alūsī reports al-Ḥasan; Kur'an Yolu notes the pre-five-times view. [7 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(Bḍ)]
- 40:55/q18/p4 They are the night ʿIshāʾ and morning Fajr prayers. — Study Quran reports this separate interpretation. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Q)]

## 40:55/q19 What does the prophetic report say to do after morning and afternoon?  [بِٱلْعَشِىِّ وَٱلْإِبْكَٰرِ · reports]
- 40:55/q19/p1 Remember God for an hour after each time, and He will take care of one's concerns. — Ṭabrisī transmits a prophetic report quoting God to this effect. [1 notes: TABRISI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 40:55/q20 What is the inward practice of continuous devotion?  [وَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ · inward]
- 40:55/q20/p1 Keep worshipping inwardly and outwardly: pray, recite, remember with tongue and heart, maintain watchfulness, and rest when overwhelmed. — Bursawī says move among these practices as receptivity and fatigue change. He defines murāqaba as the heart's awareness that God sees it and calls this the finest remembrance. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 40:55/q21 Was this verse revealed in Mecca or Medina?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the prayer reference requires a Medinan date, and when the timed obligatory prayers began.
- 40:55/q21/p1 The verse belongs to the Meccan sūra. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the sūra is Meccan by consensus apart from al-Ḥasan's exclusion. Alūsī favors the majority view and says five daily prayers and their times were prescribed in Mecca; Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise rejects al-Ḥasan's premise, and both say tasbīḥ need not mean ritual prayer. Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites reliable Ascension reports that all five were prescribed together in Mecca. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 40:55/q21/p2 Verse 55, or its command to glorify, is Medinan. — Al-Ḥasan reasons that the five timed prayers were prescribed in Medina while Meccan prayer consisted of two untimed cycles. Study Quran reports both a classification extending Medina to verse 55 and another classifying only verse 55 as Medinan with morning and sunset prayers obligatory in the last Meccan years. Alūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī argue against the prayer chronology used for this position. ## 40:55/q22 What does comparison with Sūrat al-Naṣr show?  [* · links]
- 40:55/q22/p1 Here patience and gratitude accompany an awaited victory; after victory Sūrat al-Naṣr emphasizes gratitude and pardon. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 110:1–3. Kur'an Yolu uses 110:3 to show that seeking pardon remains commanded after the later promise of forgiveness and compares the praise in both passages. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr)]

## 40:55/q23 How does this verse lead into the next discussion?  [* · links]
- 40:55/q23/p1 The assured aid to messengers leads to a warning about baseless dispute and arrogant refusal to worship. — Alūsī says 40:56 concerns disputants challenging signs of unity, books and miracles without God-given proof; the rule extends to all false disputants though Meccan polytheists are the likely immediate audience. Study Quran says the discussion moves from divine aid to condemnation of the arrogant, then to God's power over creation. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 40:55/q24 Where else does the sūra repeat this counsel?  [فَٱصْبِرْ إِنَّ وَعْدَ ٱللَّهِ حَقٌّۭ · links]
- 40:55/q24/p1 The same counsel appears again at 40:77. — Ibn al-Jawzī notes the repetition while explaining patience under abuse and certainty of victory. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
