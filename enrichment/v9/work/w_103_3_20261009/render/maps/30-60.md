# 30:60: 19 questions

## 30:60/q01 What patience does the Prophet's command require?  [فَٱصْبِرْ · interpretation]
turns on: The explanations emphasize different objects and practices of patience: hostile conduct, continued proclamation, restraint from haste, or the inner experience of affliction.
- 30:60/q01/p1 Endure the opponents' rejection, abuse, and obstinacy while trusting the promised outcome. — Yaḥyā ties endurance to victory and the religion's triumph; al-Ṭabarī adds bearing abuse and delivering the message, with establishment for the Prophet and followers in the land. Al-Wāḥidī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr connect patience to rejection after sealed hearts or persistent unbelief; al-Biqāʿī says even hostility falls within God's will. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī specifies taunts; al-Nasafī hostility, al-Bursawī verbal and physical abuse, and al-Iṣlāḥī stubborn rejection. The Turkish commentary includes both Prophet and believers. Al-Iṣlāḥī says the closing command assures the Prophet to persist despite mockers trying to uproot him; those mockers will themselves fail and be humiliated. [18 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 30:60/q01/p2 Keep inviting people to faith and proclaiming the message firmly. — Al-Rāzī says silence would let unbelievers claim the Prophet changed position and lacked firmness. Al-Biqāʿī forbids slackening or compromise; al-Ṭabarī requires carrying out God's command to deliver the message. Kur'an Yolu extends the instruction to the caller, who must withstand belittlement, doubt, and anxiety. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 30:60/q01/p3 Do not let opponents provoke haste, anger, or premature action. — Al-Jubbāʾī says anger could make the Prophet violate the command to patience and gentleness; al-Nasafī mentions rushing to ask for punishment. Al-Biqāʿī mentions hastening victory; Derveze relates the warning to preparing for migration before conditions matured. [4 notes: TABRISI(الجبائي); NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Derveze)]
- 30:60/q01/p4 Patience is giving up complaint. — Al-Sulamī transmits Ruwaym's definition. [1 notes: SULAMI(رويم)]
- 30:60/q01/p5 Patience is exposing oneself to the arrows of affliction. — Al-Sulamī transmits al-Ḥārith al-Muḥāsibī's image. [1 notes: SULAMI(الحارث المحاسبي)]
- 30:60/q01/p6 Confidence in the promised reward makes affliction easy to endure and removes its pain. — Al-Sulamī transmits Abū ʿUthmān's explanation concerning the generous reward for the patient. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان)]

## 30:60/q02 What is God's true promise here?  [وَعْدَ ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
turns on: The sources supply different promised outcomes; al-Māwardī explicitly offers support and victory or vengeance, while others combine them.
- 30:60/q02/p1 God will support the Prophet, give him victory over enemies, and establish or manifest his religion. — Yaḥyā, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī give victory or religious triumph; al-Ṭabarī includes followers established in the land. Al-Nasafī says Islam will triumph over every religion. Al-Biqāʿī adds victory for followers of originally revealed scripture over those without scripture and says time will disclose the promise. Ibn Kathīr adds the good end; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 30:47, with help promised at both ends of the sūrah. Study Quran reports this from IJ, Ṭ, and Z. Al-Iṣlāḥī describes the promised success as extending to this world and the next, with the opponents failing and being humiliated. [20 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(IJ, Ṭ, Z); TABRISI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 30:60/q02/p2 God will punish or avenge the Prophet's enemies. — Al-Māwardī gives vengeance as an alternative to support and victory; al-Ṭabrisī combines punishment of enemies with support for Prophet and religion; Ibn ʿĀshūr mentions retribution alongside victory. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 30:60/q02/p3 The Prophet's truthfulness will become clear. — Al-Rāzī says the certain promise consoles the Prophet by disclosing his veracity; Study Quran reports the interpretation from R. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 30:60/q02/p4 The patient will receive generous reward. — Abū ʿUthmān, transmitted by al-Sulamī, says confidence in that reward eases hardship and removes its pain. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان)]
- 30:60/q02/p5 For a sincere seeker, whoever seeks God will find Him. — Al-Bursawī transmits Najm al-Dīn's inward application of the promise. [1 notes: BURSEVI(نجم الدين)]

## 30:60/q03 What does ḥaqq say about the promise?  [حَقٌّۭ · meaning]
- 30:60/q03/p1 It is established and certain, expressed without exaggeration. — Ibn ʿĀshūr treats al-ḥaqq as the substantive of ḥaqqa yaḥuqqu. Study Quran links the promise's truth to 30:6 and cross-references 4:122, 10:4, 10:55, 18:21, 28:13, 31:33, 35:5, 40:55, 40:77, 45:32, and 46:17. Al-Iṣlāḥī says the promise of success in this world and the next is certain. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 30:60/q04 What does 'do not let them yastakhiffannaka' mean?  [وَلَا يَسْتَخِفَّنَّكَ · meaning]
turns on: The root's lightness is applied to agitation or haste, and some glosses extend its effect to foolish error or departure from religion.
- 30:60/q04/p1 Do not let them unsettle, distress, agitate, or dislodge your steadiness. — Al-Azharī, as reported by al-Wāḥidī, names judgment and forbearance; al-Baghawī reports judgment and patience; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says remain unmoved despite lack of insight. Al-Nasafī cites what opponents say and do; al-Rāghib, via al-Bursawī, says their doubts must not dislodge belief. Al-Ālūsī mentions denial and abuse, including charges that messengers lie; Kur'an Yolu adds belittlement, doubt, and anxiety. Study Quran translates the verb 'disquiet.' Al-Iṣlāḥī describes the mockers as trying to uproot the Prophet, while the closing command assures him that they will fail. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأزهري); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IJ, Q); BAYDAWI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 30:60/q04/p2 Do not let them hurry or provoke you into haste or angry reaction. — Ibn Shajara gives 'make you hurry'; Yaḥyā 'provoke.' Al-Jushumī explains istikhfāf as seeking lightness: making a people light prompts haste, while overwhelming delight impairs judgment. Al-Jubbāʾī warns anger would violate patience and gentleness. Al-Nasafī mentions a premature request for punishment; Ibn ʿĀshūr says figurative lightness denotes panic or visible anger. Study Quran also gives rushing and unsteadiness. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة,يحيى بن سلام); JISHUMI; TABRISI(الجبائي); NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 30:60/q04/p3 Do not let them lead you into foolishness, error, or transgression. — Al-Azharī says istakhaffa can mean treating someone as foolish and inducing error; al-Baghawī gives that gloss as well as impaired judgment. Al-Qurṭubī says making someone foolish enough to follow error; Study Quran reports from Q a sense of making someone heedless or ignorant, leading to error and transgression. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأزهري); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 30:60/q04/p4 Do not let them draw you down from your position or away from your religion. — Al-Naqqāsh gives the first gloss; al-Zajjāj and his later transmitters explain the warning as preventing opponents from provoking or unsettling the Prophet away from religion. Yaḥyā says he must not follow their call to abandon it. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(النقاش); YAHYA-SALLAM; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IJ, Q)]
- 30:60/q04/p5 Do not let them dismiss your views as flimsy and weak. — Al-Iṣlāḥī glosses istikhfāf as treating someone's views as flimsy and weak. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 30:60/q05 How is yastakhiffannaka read?  [يَسْتَخِفَّنَّكَ · readings]
turns on: The variants change the root from istikhfāf to istihqāq, or change the emphatic nūn's weight while retaining the prohibition.
- 30:60/q05/p1 The common reading has khāʾ and fāʾ, from istikhfāf, with an emphatic heavy nūn. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya contrasts the majority form with istihqāq; al-Samīn derives it from istikhfāf. Al-Biqāʿī says its emphatic prohibition is stronger than a bare prohibition and guards the foundations of religion against conceding to opponents. Al-Ālūsī says the majority geminate the nūn. The Qirāʾāt note specifies a third-person plural subject. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق ويعقوب); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة); QIRAAT-ER]
- 30:60/q05/p2 The nūn is light or unvowelled, while the prohibition keeps its meaning. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the two alternative readers also transmit silent nūn in the istikhfāf form. Ibn al-Jawzī assigns sukūn to Yaʿqūb except Rawḥ and Zayd; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī assign it to Yaʿqūb. Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī name Ibn Abī ʿAbla and Yaʿqūb; Qirāʾāt says lightening reduces emphasis. Al-Nashr points to its earlier treatment of Ruways's reading at Āl ʿImrān. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق ويعقوب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(يعقوب إلا روحا وزيدا); BAYDAWI-FULL(يعقوب); NASAFI-FULL(يعقوب); SAMIN-DURR; NASHR; ALUSI-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة); QIRAAT-ER]
- 30:60/q05/p3 An alternative reads yastaḥiqqannaka with ḥāʾ and qāf, from istihqāq. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya names Ibn Abī Isḥāq and Yaʿqūb; al-Samīn agrees. Al-Bayḍāwī glosses it as not letting opponents turn the addressee away. Al-Biqāʿī attributes it to Ruways from Yaʿqūb, connects ḥaqq with not oneself seeking a just settlement from opponents, and warns that worldly concessions invite demands for favoritism. Al-Ālūsī says not to let them win the addressee over until they are more entitled to him than believers; he applies this reading to the community because the Prophet is protected. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق ويعقوب); BAYDAWI-FULL(يعقوب); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(رويس عن يعقوب); ALUSI-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق)]

## 30:60/q06 How does the prohibition work grammatically?  [وَلَا يَسْتَخِفَّنَّكَ · grammar]
- 30:60/q06/p1 It is a prohibition with an emphatic nūn, formally directed at the opponents but functionally telling the Prophet not to be affected. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī say the jussive prohibition with heavy nūn is built on fatḥa. Al-Samīn compares the idiom 'do not let me see you here.' Al-Bursawī says the apparent ban on unbelievers indirectly forbids the Prophet from yielding to contempt; al-Ālūsī similarly invokes the idiom and glosses it as not grieving fearfully over them. Al-Biqāʿī says emphasis strengthens the ban beyond a bare prohibition. [6 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 30:60/q07 What is the case and form of alladhīna?  [ٱلَّذِينَ · grammar]
- 30:60/q07/p1 Alladhīna is nominative; some Arabs use alladhūna in the nominative. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī state the case; al-Naḥḥās reports the Arab usage الذون, also given by al-Qurṭubī as اللذون. [3 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 30:60/q08 Who lack certainty, and what do they doubt?  [ٱلَّذِينَ لَا يُوقِنُونَ · referent]
turns on: The sources variously locate the missing certainty in revelation generally, the afterlife, imminent worldly punishment, divine help, or the promise itself; al-Qurṭubī also reports a named individual.
- 30:60/q08/p1 They are unbelievers or polytheists who reject the Prophet's message and remain doubtful or astray. — Yaḥyā calls them polytheists urging the Prophet to leave his religion. Ibn ʿAbbās, as reported by al-Wāḥidī, says they disbelieve what the Prophet brought. Al-Wāḥidī's Abū Isḥāq calls them astray and doubtful; Ibn al-Jawzī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Bayḍāwī, and al-Bursawī give doubt, lack of insight, or denial of signs. Al-Ālūsī names their accusations that messengers lie; al-Biqāʿī includes wavering doubters. [13 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 30:60/q08/p2 They deny resurrection, judgment, or recompense. — Al-Ṭabarī specifies deniers of resurrection; al-Māwardī offers this as an alternative to unbelievers generally. Al-Wāḥidī reports another interpretation; al-Baghawī and Ibn al-Jawzī mention resurrection and reckoning or recompense. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(غيره)]
- 30:60/q08/p3 They doubt punishment will descend on them in this world. — Al-Wāḥidī reports Muqātil's explanation and identifies these people as those punished at Badr. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)]
- 30:60/q08/p4 They doubt divine support and confidently expected Persia to defeat Rome. — Al-Biqāʿī says their prediction risked their credibility because God had promised Roman victory; he also includes wavering doubters. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 30:60/q08/p5 They are specifically al-Naḍr ibn al-Ḥārith. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this identification. Muqātil elsewhere recounts al-Naḍr demanding immediate punishment and later being killed at Badr, with ʿAlī beheading him. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 30:60/q08/p6 They are people who lack belief in God's promise itself. — Al-Ālūsī mentions this alternative only to dismiss it. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 30:60/q09 Who receives the closing warning?  [وَلَا يَسْتَخِفَّنَّكَ · referent]
turns on: The prophetic address can be taken as direct, extended to the community, or applied to the community particularly under the istihqāq reading.
- 30:60/q09/p1 It directly addresses the Prophet Muhammad. — Most explanations address his patience and steadiness amid opponents; al-Ālūsī says the prohibition, although formally cast with a plural subject, is directed to him. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 30:60/q09/p2 The address to the Prophet also intends his community. — Al-Qurṭubī says the warning is worded for him but intended for the community; Study Quran reports this interpretation from Q. Kur'an Yolu extends the closing encouragement to believers and callers. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 30:60/q09/p3 On the istihqāq reading it addresses the community, because the Prophet is protected. — Al-Ālūsī explains that the alternate reading warns against opponents becoming more entitled to the addressee than believers. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق)]

## 30:60/q10 How does the conclusion connect to the sūrah's argument?  [* · links]
turns on: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī explicitly favors the sūrah-wide promise of victory over a reading centered on distress at rejected signs.
- 30:60/q10/p1 The close follows obstinate rejection and sealed hearts, so the Prophet must persevere. — Al-Wāḥidī says God has just reported their sealed hearts. Ibn Kathīr cites the many examples given to clarify truth, the dismissal of signs as magic including the moon's splitting, and 10:96–97 on those who would not believe even after every sign. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the omitted object of patience as their obstinacy after 30:58. Al-Ālūsī and Study Quran likewise connect the exhortation to sealed hearts; al-Iṣlāḥī says only people of intellect receive the historical proofs in 46–60. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 30:60/q10/p2 The close returns to the sūrah's promise of divine help and victory. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 30:47 and says the sūrah begins and ends with promised help; he rejects making distress at denied signs the main reason for the patience command. Al-Biqāʿī connects the Romans' promised victory, which exposed doubters' error, with the promised Hour's justice for wrongdoers and reward for the virtuous. Al-Iṣlāḥī reads 46–60 as historical proofs of help. Study Quran links the promise's truth to 30:6 and lists 4:122, 10:4, 10:55, 18:21, 28:13, 31:33, 35:5, 40:55, 40:77, 45:32, and 46:17. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI+]
- 30:60/q10/p3 The patience command responds chiefly to the Prophet's distress when disbelievers deny clear signs. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this explanation only to reject it, appealing instead to the victory promise at both ends of the sūrah. [0 notes: TABATABAI-]

## 30:60/q11 What happened when ʿAlī recited this verse in prayer?  [* · reports]
- 30:60/q11/p1 A Kharijite insinuated ʿAlī's shirk by reciting 39:65 during dawn prayer, and ʿAlī answered with 30:60. — Al-Ṭabarī says Qatāda also transmits the episode. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says ʿAlī recognized the insinuation. Al-Ālūsī names Ibn Abī Shayba and others as transmitters and judges the response apt from the Prophet's brother and gate of the city of knowledge. Al-Māwardī, al-Durr, and al-Itqān likewise report the prayer exchange. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(علي); MAWARDI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); DURR-FULL(علي); ITQAN(علي); ALUSI-FULL(علي)]

## 30:60/q12 What occasion involving al-Naḍr is reported?  [* · reports]
- 30:60/q12/p1 After al-Naḍr demanded immediate punishment, Muhammad was told not to be provoked; al-Naḍr was later killed at Badr. — Muqātil says ʿAlī beheaded al-Naḍr and the dead are displayed to the Fire morning and evening. Al-Wāḥidī transmits Muqātil's identification of doubters in worldly punishment with those punished at Badr; al-Qurṭubī reports naming al-Naḍr as the one lacking certainty. [3 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 30:60/q13 What other experiences illustrate endurance of opposition?  [* · reports]
- 30:60/q13/p1 After Abū Ṭālib died, abuse intensified; someone threw dust on the Prophet, and he assured his weeping daughter of God's protection. — Al-Bursawī says the daughter wiped the dust from his head and adds that the Companions also endured abuse and ultimately prevailed. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 30:60/q14 What is reported about reciting Sūrat al-Rūm in dawn prayer?  [* · reports]
- 30:60/q14/p1 The Prophet stumbled during its recitation and attributed the confusion to some worshipers' poor ablution. — Ibn Kathīr reports his instruction to perform ablution well, grades the chain and text ḥasan, and infers that a follower's prayer is connected to the imam's. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(صحابي من أصحاب النبي)]

## 30:60/q15 What virtue is reported for reciting Sūrat al-Rūm?  [* · sciences]
- 30:60/q15/p1 The reciter receives ten rewards per angel praising God between heaven and earth, compensating for missed deeds by day and night. — Al-Bayḍāwī transmits this as a prophetic report. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي)]

## 30:60/q16 Was this verse abrogated?  [* · sciences]
- 30:60/q16/p1 Some exegetes say it was abrogated. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports the claim without further argument in this note. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(بعض المفسرين)]

## 30:60/q17 May Qur'anic wording be used in other speech, as in ʿAlī's reply?  [* · law]
turns on: The jurists differ over whether adapting Qur'anic wording into other expression is disliked or permitted.
- 30:60/q17/p1 Using Qur'anic expressions in poetry or other writing is permissible. — Al-Suyūṭī states this without doubt in al-Itqān, alongside the report of ʿAlī answering a Kharijite with the verse during prayer. [1 notes: ITQAN+]
- 30:60/q17/p2 Some jurists dislike making parables from Qur'anic wording. — Al-Itqān transmits this from al-ʿImād al-Bayhaqī through al-Ṣalāḥ. [1 notes: ITQAN(العماد البيهقي)]

## 30:60/q18 How does the Qur'an present proof to deniers?  [* · rhetoric]
- 30:60/q18/p1 It uses every kind of clear proof, argument, distinction, and warning in familiar Arab speech. — Al-Suyūṭī says plain arguments convince ordinary listeners without obscure technical disputation while teaching specialists too; Ibn Abī al-Iṣbaʿ counters al-Jāḥiẓ's claim that kalām argument is absent from the Qur'an and defines such argument as evidence decisively defeating an opponent. [4 notes: ITQAN(ابن أبي الإصبع)]

## 30:60/q19 How is the verse applied to seekers on the inward path?  [* · inward]
turns on: The inward notes apply patience both to enduring critics and to the seeker's own self-discipline and detachment.
- 30:60/q19/p1 Seekers must endure mockery from people who belittle the quest for God. — Al-Bursawī says even holders of inherited faith may lack certainty, deny that seeking God is obligatory, and scorn those who leave worldly life, family, and children. Al-Ālūsī applies the warning to heirs of the Muhammadan way and spiritual guides whose states, words, and deeds are rejected by veiled deniers. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 30:60/q19/p2 The seeker patiently detaches from worldly ties and inward attachment until the self gives way to union. — Al-Bursawī gives outward detachment then inward separation from happiness in either world, citing 64:14 and the saying of two steps followed by arrival. He reports ʿAṭṭār's clod dissolving in the sea and the Mathnawī's grammarian and boatman: grammar cannot prevent drowning, whereas self-effacement lets the sea carry the dead. Najm al-Dīn defines patience as disciplining desire, guarding the heart from ego traits, and surrendering the spirit's separate existence to divine generosity; Ḥāfiẓ urges steadfast love because it is rewarded. [7 notes: BURSEVI(الحافظ,العطار,نجم الدين)]
