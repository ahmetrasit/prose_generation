# 31:17: 34 questions

## 31:17/q01 How is the final yāʾ of yā bunayya read?  [يَٰبُنَىَّ · readings]
turns on: Transmission and whether the final yāʾ preserves possessive inflection, opens after contraction or is lightened.
- 31:17/q01/p1 Doubled with kasra. — Jishumi relates kasra to the possessive form; Ibn Atiyya names Nafi, Abu Amr, Ibn Amir, Hamza, al-Kisai and Abu Bakr from Asim. Ibn Abi Barra and Qunbul report kasra in the second occurrence; Qiraat-ER notes explicit possessive inflection. [5 notes: JISHUMI(حفص عن عاصم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن أبي برة عن ابن كثير وقنبل,نافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي وأبو بكر عن عاصم); BAYDAWI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]
- 31:17/q01/p2 Doubled with fatha. — Jishumi explains dropping the possessive after three yāʾs; Ibn Atiyya names Hafs and al-Mufaddal. Nashr gives Hafs in six occurrences, Abu Bakr at Hud and al-Bazzi at the last Luqman occurrence; Ibn Abi Barra gives Ibn Kathir fatha at the third. [5 notes: JISHUMI(حفص عن عاصم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن أبي برة عن ابن كثير وقنبل,نافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي وأبو بكر عن عاصم); NASHR; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 31:17/q01/p3 Lightened with sukun. — Jishumi calls sukun original; Ibn Abi Barra reports it at the first occurrence, Qunbul at first and third, and Nashr gives Qunbul at the final Luqman occurrence. Qiraat-ER says it shortens and lightens the diminutive. [4 notes: JISHUMI(حفص عن عاصم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن أبي برة عن ابن كثير وقنبل); NASHR(قنبل); QIRAAT-ER]

## 31:17/q02 What does the address yā bunayya convey?  [يَٰبُنَىَّ · rhetoric]
- 31:17/q02/p1 Tenderness and compassion that invite the son to obey. — Tabrisi rejects contempt; Baydawi calls the diminutive affectionate; Alusi says it inclines the son to obedience. [3 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 31:17/q03 What does establishing prayer require?  [أَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · interpretation]
turns on: Outward observance or prayer's transforming effect on conduct.
- 31:17/q03/p1 Perform it fully with obligations, limits, conditions and appointed times. — Nahhas includes every right of the entrusted work; Tabari its bounds; Tabrisi and Ibn Kathir its time; Biqai its conditions; Ibn Ashur regularity. Alusi reports prompt prayer and congregation even atop a spearhead. Islahi includes praying oneself and urging others. [8 notes: NAHHAS; TAB-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 31:17/q03/p2 Persist inwardly in avoiding indecency and wrongdoing. — Najm al-Din says abstention can be prayer without outward form, while outward form without restraint is not truly prayer. [1 notes: BURSEVI(نجم الدين)]

## 31:17/q04 Why is prayer singled out?  [أَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · interpretation]
- 31:17/q04/p1 It perfects and purifies the worshipper after belief and is the chief worship directed to God. — Razi calls it sincere worship; Razi, Baydawi, Elmali, Bursevi and Alusi contrast perfecting self with helping others. Abu Hayyan calls it noblest turning to God. Biqai says it restrains wrongdoing and follows monotheism in rank. Bursevi says it reforms the soul more deeply than fasting and disciplines, which chiefly improve temperament; desire is especially hateful to God. Ibn Ashur calls it the pillar of deeds acknowledging God's authority; Tabatabai the pillar of religion; Islahi the foremost gratitude; Kuranyolu representative of worship. [15 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 31:17/q05 Was prayer taught in earlier communities?  [أَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · interpretation]
- 31:17/q05/p1 Yes; prayer and these acts were taught or required before, though prayer's form varied. — Razi notes different earlier forms; Nasafi and Abu Hayyan infer duties across nations; Islahi says all prophets and religions taught prayer; Biqai says no community may abandon this counsel. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL]

## 31:17/q06 Why does prayer precede enjoining and forbidding?  [أَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَأْمُرْ بِٱلْمَعْرُوفِ وَٱنْهَ عَنِ ٱلْمُنكَرِ · rhetoric]
- 31:17/q06/p1 One perfects one's own worship, then helps perfect others. — Razi calls the latter prophetic work; Baydawi, Alusi, Elmali and Study Quran contrast self and others. Abu Hayyan calls prayer the noblest turning to God. Islahi contrasts connection to God with connection to people. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(R); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 31:17/q07 Why is almsgiving omitted after prayer?  [أَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · rhetoric]
- 31:17/q07/p1 It signals Luqman and his son's detachment from worldly provision. — Biqai makes prayer second only to monotheism and connects the omission with their asceticism. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 31:17/q08 What good and wrong are meant?  [بِٱلْمَعْرُوفِ عَنِ ٱلْمُنكَرِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the nouns specify faith and shirk, justice and injustice, or the full scope of good and wrong.
- 31:17/q08/p1 All divinely, rationally or legally approved good and condemned evil. — Tabari includes obedience and prohibitions; Tabrisi rational and legal vileness; Wahidi reports obedience and disobedience. Qushayri says good leads to God and evil distracts; Bursevi combines this with reason and law. Alusi says no specific acts are singled out. Islahi lists rights, charity, aid to needy and neighbours versus stinginess, usurpation, injustice, broken promises and conceit. Ibn Ashur includes one's own conduct. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل,الكلبي); TABRISI; QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 31:17/q08/p2 Monotheism and worship are enjoined; shirk and idols are forbidden. — Abu al-Aliya, al-Suddi, Ibn Abbas, Muqatil and Ibn Jubayr are reported. Ibn Jawzi groups 9:71 and 9:112 with 31:17. Ibn Abbas calls shirk a wrong against oneself; Muqatil specifically says hidden or unrecognized shirk. [9 notes: MUJAHID(أبو العالية); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,ابن عباس ومقاتل,مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جبير)]
- 31:17/q08/p3 Justice is made manifest and injustice denounced. — Wahidi reports al-Kalbi's gloss. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي)]

## 31:17/q09 Must the adviser first practice what he teaches?  [وَأْمُرْ بِٱلْمَعْرُوفِ وَٱنْهَ عَنِ ٱلْمُنكَرِ · interpretation]
- 31:17/q09/p1 Yes, one should correct and govern oneself before directing others. — Sulami reports beginning with oneself; Qushayri makes self-rule prerequisite. Qurtubi and Biqai cite poetry saying to restrain one's own wrong before claiming wisdom. Abu Hayyan says embody the advice; Ibn Ashur includes one's own conduct. [7 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو الأسود الدؤلي); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 31:17/q10 What is the social purpose of enjoining and forbidding?  [وَأْمُرْ بِٱلْمَعْرُوفِ وَٱنْهَ عَنِ ٱلْمُنكَرِ · interpretation]
- 31:17/q10/p1 They guide others and protect shared life by promoting good and restraining harm. — Razi associates them with prophets and learned successors. Biqai says enjoining is broader than forbidding, which alone does not enact good, and compares society to a vessel imperiled by wrongdoing. Elmali says they guide society; Islahi makes enjoining social gratitude; Kuranyolu calls it constructive social conduct. Study Quran cites 3:104, 3:110, 3:114, 7:157, 9:71, 9:112 and 22:41. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 31:17/q11 What duty and limits does enjoining and forbidding impose?  [وَأْمُرْ بِٱلْمَعْرُوفِ وَٱنْهَ عَنِ ٱلْمُنكَرِ · law]
turns on: Whether bearing harm for this work is binding within capacity or commended courage.
- 31:17/q11/p1 It is a duty within one's ability, including bearable harm. — Wahidi says fear excuses it only if harm exceeds one's ability; Ibn Kathir says as far as able. Jishumi links the command to patience. Study Quran calls it a major Quranic injunction with seven parallels. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNKATHIR-FULL; JISHUMI; STUDYQURAN]
- 31:17/q11/p2 Changing wrong despite harm is recommended courage. — Qurtubi treats the encouragement to face injury as recommended rather than obligatory. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL+]

## 31:17/q12 What affliction does patience address?  [وَٱصْبِرْ عَلَىٰ مَآ أَصَابَكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether context limits the harm to opposition in public duty or includes life's wider trials.
- 31:17/q12/p1 Injury and opposition incurred while enjoining and forbidding. — Muqatil, Tabari, Ibn Abbas and Muqatil in Wahidi, Ali in Tabrisi, Baghawi, Razi, Ibn Kathir, Ibn Jurayj in Durr, Ibn Jubayr in Alusi and Elmali connect injury to this work. Razi says it is inevitable; Ibn Ashur says it could cause abandonment and mentions reward and the following verse. Qushayri says those standing for God's right are tested. Umayr counsels preparation and trust in reward, which lightens hurt. Islahi says hardship meets every step. [21 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(علي); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,سعيد بن جبير,عمير بن حبيب); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن جبير); ELMALILI(Umeyr b. Habîb); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 31:17/q12/p2 All personal and worldly calamities, especially injury during this work. — Mawardi allows injury to person or property; al-Jubbai names illness; Qurtubi calls the inclusive interpretation good. Abu Hayyan includes all calamities; Bursevi names illness, poverty, grief and abuse; Alusi adds prayer's burden and 2:45. Nasafi says trials can become gifts. Tabatabai calls adversity a noble trait; Kuranyolu mentions material and social trials. [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SULAMI(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL+]

## 31:17/q13 What conduct counts as patience?  [وَٱصْبِرْ عَلَىٰ مَآ أَصَابَكَ · interpretation]
- 31:17/q13/p1 Endure without complaint, despair or sin while persisting in duty. — Nahhas says grief must not turn to disobedience and includes resisting sin. Wasiti says cease complaint at trials but stay alert at sudden blessings; Sulami also reports trials becoming gifts. Qurtubi excludes despair leading to sin; Bursevi includes restraining the self from what law or reason forbids. Biqai calls patience key to action and abstention; Islahi calls for wholehearted perseverance. [8 notes: NAHHAS; SULAMI(الواسطي,بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 31:17/q14 Why is patience important when religious work brings harm?  [وَٱصْبِرْ عَلَىٰ مَآ أَصَابَكَ · interpretation]
- 31:17/q14/p1 It sustains the work and carries divine reward. — Qushayri says patience for God cannot lose; Razi predicts injury; Umayr urges confidence in reward to lighten hurt. Ibn Ashur says injury could make one abandon duty and says reward is the immediate point. Islahi calls for perseverance; Ibn Abbas calls patience a core reality of faith. [7 notes: QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(عمير بن حبيب); ELMALILI(Umeyr b. Habîb); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 31:17/q15 What does dhālika refer to?  [إِنَّ ذَٰلِكَ · referent]
turns on: The range of the singular demonstrative and Quranic parallels about patience.
- 31:17/q15/p1 The preceding commands and prohibitions together. — Wahidi prefers inclusion over al-Kalbi's narrower reading and cites 2:68; Abu Hayyan says the demonstrative gathers them. Alusi construes its singular as one unified matter. Bursevi names enjoining, forbidding and patience; Biqai the counsel, especially patience. Qurtubi's Ibn Jurayj names prayer, enjoining and forbidding. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي)+; TABATABAI-]
- 31:17/q15/p2 Patience specifically, especially through harm from enjoining and forbidding. — Muqatil and Ibn Kathir highlight endurance. Ibn Jubayr says singular dhālika suits this; Tabatabai cites repeated Quranic descriptions of patience as resolve and rejects the all-duties reading. [6 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن جبير); TABATABAI+]
- 31:17/q15/p3 Enjoining and forbidding specifically. — Wahidi reports this from al-Kalbi but prefers the inclusive reading. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي)]

## 31:17/q16 What are ʿazm al-umūr?  [عَزْمِ ٱلْأُمُورِ · interpretation]
turns on: Divine binding determination or human strength and moral excellence.
- 31:17/q16/p1 Matters God firmly determined and commanded as duties. — Tabari and Ibn Jurayj say God decisively enjoined them; Muqatil and Baghawi include patience through injury. Nasafi likens a binding decree to cutting off; Razi calls it obligatory. Qurtubi prefers Ibn Jurayj; Abu Hayyan prefers necessity because dhālika gathers all commands. Bursevi allows obligations; Biqai says no community may abandon them. Wahidi Wajiz calls doing right obligatory; Study Quran reports Tusi's divine determination. [21 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جريج); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جريج); BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,سعيد بن جبير); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي)+]
- 31:17/q16/p2 Weighty good matters requiring human resolve and perseverance. — Ibn Abbas says matters of resolve or reality of faith; Muqatil in Thalabi calls them right. Tabrisi says sound intention to do good rather than wavering; Elmali and Islahi stress effort. Kuranyolu makes them mature worship with further hard virtues; Study Quran renders a course to resolve on and cites 3:186 and 42:43. Alusi reports noble morals of prudent people; Qurtubi offers virtues of resolute good people. [10 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس,مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 31:17/q17 What does ʿazm mean in Arabic usage?  [عَزْمِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether resolve and caution are synonyms or distinct stages and qualities.
- 31:17/q17/p1 Firm settled commitment and preparation for action. — Damghani calls it firmness; Jishumi adds self-preparation and contrasts fakhara, recounting merit, with ikhtiyal, swagger. Tabrisi contrasts wavering. Bursevi calls it settled determination; Abu Hayyan adds controlling and preserving a matter. Mawardi cites proverbs distinguishing caution as vigilance or preparation from resolve as strength or execution, though they may be synonyms. [8 notes: DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 31:17/q17/p2 In Hudhayli usage it means ḥazm, firmness or prudence. — Muarrij reports the dialect gloss. Mawardi considers synonymy or distinctions of vigilance, preparation, strength and action; Tabrisi reports strength and caution. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مؤرج); ALUSI-FULL(المبرد)]

## 31:17/q18 Did ʿazm derive from ḥazm by consonant change?  [عَزْمِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether shared meaning and dialect usage imply an altered root.
- 31:17/q18/p1 Yes, initial ʿayn and ḥāʾ alternate. — Al-Mubarrad proposes this, as reported by Samin and Alusi. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(المبرد); ALUSI-FULL(المبرد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المبرد)-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 31:17/q18/p2 No; the roots are independent despite similar senses. — Abu Hayyan and Samin reject al-Mubarrad's sound change; Alusi also rejects it while reporting his dialect gloss. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المبرد)+; ALUSI-FULL(المبرد)+; SAMIN-DURR+]

## 31:17/q19 How does ʿazm al-umūr work grammatically?  [عَزْمِ ٱلْأُمُورِ · grammar]
turns on: Passive-object use of the verbal noun or figurative attribution to the matters.
- 31:17/q19/p1 The verbal noun denotes its object: matters resolved upon. — Razi compares 'my eating' for what is eaten; Nasafi, Alusi, Samin, Abu Hayyan and Bursevi allow the same object use. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 31:17/q19/p2 The matters themselves are figuratively called resolute. — Samin calls it a strong metaphor and cites 47:21; Alusi takes ʿazm as active participle by figurative attribution rather than an 'in' genitive; Abu Hayyan and Bursevi also allow it. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 31:17/q20 What work does the closing clause perform?  [إِنَّ ذَٰلِكَ مِنْ عَزْمِ ٱلْأُمُورِ · rhetoric]
- 31:17/q20/p1 It gives reason to obey and stresses the counsel's weight. — Alusi calls it the rationale. Islahi demands wholehearted perseverance; Biqai stresses decisive commitment; Kuranyolu makes these examples of mature worship and further demanding virtues. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 31:17/q21 Why does 42:43 strengthen this wording?  [إِنَّ ذَٰلِكَ مِنْ عَزْمِ ٱلْأُمُورِ · links]
- 31:17/q21/p1 Al-Shura adds forgiveness to patience, a harder act, so emphasizes it with both inna and lām. — Samarrai contrasts patience alone here with patience plus forgiveness in 42:43 and its la-min ʿazm al-umūr. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 31:17/q22 How does this counsel connect with Sūrat al-ʿAṣr?  [وَٱصْبِرْ عَلَىٰ مَآ أَصَابَكَ · links]
- 31:17/q22/p1 Both join upholding truth with steadfastness and patience. — Islahi cites 103:3 because enjoining and forbidding demand perseverance. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 31:17/q23 Why is prayer ordered differently from patience and prayer in 2:45?  [أَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَٱصْبِرْ عَلَىٰ مَآ أَصَابَكَ · links]
- 31:17/q23/p1 The audiences differ: worldly engrossed unbelievers there, a believing ascetic here. — Biqai gives the contrast; Alusi cites 2:45 for the burden of prayer. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 31:17/q24 How does the verse fit Luqman's wider counsel?  [* · links]
- 31:17/q24/p1 It moves from belief and God's knowledge to prayer, social duty and patience, then rejects pride. — Razi says the son already recognized God and first needed warning against shirk; Bursevi says this implies monotheism before action. Ibn Ashur surveys warning against shirk, kindness to parents, awareness of God, these acts, then humble walk and speech. Samarrai sees companionship across father, parents and others. Islahi says Arab sages taught this Quranic counsel, opposing blind ancestor imitation and punishment of children who follow it. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 31:17/q25 What is reported about the son's response to earlier counsel?  [* · reports]
- 31:17/q25/p1 He was a polytheist whom Luqman admonished until he accepted Islam. — Razi reports the commentators' account while explaining the order. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 31:17/q26 For whom is this counsel especially pertinent?  [* · interpretation]
- 31:17/q26/p1 It benefits everyone, especially political leaders. — Islahi says it prepares Luqman's son for leadership and describes Luqman as sage and ruler rather than prophet, with general resemblance to David. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 31:17/q27 What range of senses can divine commands have?  [وَأْمُرْ · meaning]
- 31:17/q27/p1 Obligation, creation, transformation, threat, guidance, decree, mockery, custom, public duty, benevolence and sufficiency. — Sulami reports al-Wasiti's list with Quranic illustrations at 2:43, 2:117, 7:166, 41:40, 4:135, 17:23, 17:64, 33:53, 9:71, 58:11 and 9:5. [1 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي)]

## 31:17/q28 How much wisdom is reported from Luqman?  [* · reports]
- 31:17/q28/p1 Twelve thousand branches absorbed into sayings, judgments and maxims. — Baghawi reports this from Wahb. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(وهب)]

## 31:17/q29 What did Luqman call a person's best and worst parts?  [* · reports]
- 31:17/q29/p1 The tongue and heart, good or bad according to their condition. — Baghawi reports Khalid al-Rabi's story: Luqman's master requests the best, then worst, two morsels and receives the same two. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(خالد الربعي)]

## 31:17/q30 What further counsel is reported from Luqman?  [* · reports]
turns on: These are separate pieces of advice.
- 31:17/q30/p1 Wisdom seats the poor among kings. — Ibn Kathir reports Luqman's saying. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(لقمان)]
- 31:17/q30/p2 Greet gatherings, listen quietly, join remembrance of God and leave other talk. — Ibn Kathir reports Luqman's gathering etiquette. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(لقمان)]
- 31:17/q30/p3 Guard secrets, shun temptation and excess, remember death, avoid burdening others and harmful women. — Bursevi transmits Kashf al-Asrar, including funerals and a satanic snare. [1 notes: BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار)]
- 31:17/q30/p4 Eat enough while fasting to retain strength for prayer. — Bursevi transmits Kashf al-Asrar: God values prayer more than fasting. [1 notes: BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار)]

## 31:17/q31 How did Luqman illustrate his admonitions' force?  [* · reports]
- 31:17/q31/p1 He removed a mustard seed at each admonition until the bag was empty; the counsel would split a mountain. — Ibn Kathir reports that his son did as urged. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(لقمان)]

## 31:17/q32 Who are named as distinguished Black figures?  [* · reports]
- 31:17/q32/p1 Luqman, the Negus and Bilal. — Ibn Kathir reports attribution to the Prophet; al-Tabarani explains Abyssinians are intended. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 31:17/q33 What is reported about unrecognized pious people?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports describe poor and wealthy hidden piety.
- 31:17/q33/p1 An unnoticed poor person in worn clothes may have an oath fulfilled by God. — Ibn Kathir transmits from Anas; Ibn Masud adds that God grants Paradise but no worldly status.
