# 39:36: 20 questions

## 39:36/q01 What does the opening question assert?  [أَلَيْسَ ٱللَّهُ بِكَافٍ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the question alone or also the predicate's bāʾ gives the assertive force.
- 39:36/q01/p1 It emphatically affirms God's sufficiency. — Jishumi calls the negated question a demand for acknowledgment, comparing 2:75 and 75:40. Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi, Alusi and Tabatabai explain affirmation through interrogation over negation. Biqai relates it to the idol threats; Ibn Ashur says it rebukes idolaters. [10 notes: JISHUMI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI]
- 39:36/q01/p2 The bāʾ in the negated predicate helps turn the question into binding affirmation. — Bint al-Shati says interrogatives without bāʾ may remain genuine questions or express rebuke, warning, mockery or expectation; she notes the regular bāʾ here and rhetoric's broader functions. [4 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 39:36/q02 How do the bāʾ and final yāʾ work in بِكَافٍ?  [بِكَافٍ · grammar]
- 39:36/q02/p1 The bāʾ can attach to the predicate, and the yāʾ of kāfī drops before tanwīn. — Qurtubi says the yāʾ may return at pause but commonly stays absent; some Arabs say kāfī. Burhan cites al-Zajjaj's account that kafā bears the sense of iktafā. Ibn Asfur treats augmentative bāʾ as rare and non-analogical, citing 75:40 and 17:99 as parallels without it. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN(ابن عصفور)]

## 39:36/q03 How is بِكَافٍ عَبْدَهُۥ read?  [بِكَافٍ عَبْدَهُۥ · readings]
turns on: The letters and vocalization, number of servants, and the alternate verb's derivation.
- 39:36/q03/p1 The common reading has singular ʿabdahu. — Farra, Ibn Mujahid, Farisi, Tabarsi, Qurtubi, Samīn, Nashr and Ibn Ashur contrast it with the plural. Farisi and Abu Ubayd favor its fit with following singular address. [15 notes: FARRA(يحيى بن وثاب وأبو جعفر المدني); IBNMUJAHID(حمزة، الكسائي); FARISI-HUJJA(حمزة والكسائي); MAWARDI-FULL(بعضهم); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(أبو علي,أهل الكوفة غير عاصم وأبو جعفر); QURTUBI-FULL(عامة القراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(العامة); NASHR; IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد)+]
- 39:36/q03/p2 A recognized reading has plural ʿibādahu. — The named readers vary: Yahya ibn Waththab, Abu Ja'far, Hamza, al-Kisa'i, Khalaf, Mujahid, Talha and al-A'mash appear in different sources; Tabarsi says Kufans except Asim plus Abu Ja'far. Farra deems both readings sound. Qiraat-Er says this broadens sufficiency to a community. [19 notes: FARRA(يحيى بن وثاب وأبو جعفر المدني); IBNMUJAHID(حمزة، الكسائي); FARISI-HUJJA(حمزة والكسائي); MAWARDI-FULL(الباقون); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جعفر وحمزة والكسائي); TABRISI(أبو علي,أهل الكوفة غير عاصم وأبو جعفر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(حمزة والكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); NASAFI-FULL(حمزة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو جعفر ومجاهد وابن وثاب وطلحة والأعمش وحمزة والكسائي); SAMIN-DURR(الأخوان); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم); NASHR; BIQAI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وأبو جعفر); ALUSI-FULL(أبو جعفر ومجاهد وابن وثاب وطلحة والأعمش وحمزة والكسائي); IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]
- 39:36/q03/p3 An explicit yāʾ appears in bi-kāfī with singular ʿabdihi. — Ibn al-Jawzi attributes it to Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas and Abu Imran al-Jawni, with specified kasras and no alif. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعد بن أبي وقاص وأبو عمران الجوني)]
- 39:36/q03/p4 An explicit yāʾ appears in bi-kāfī with plural ʿibādihi. — Ibn al-Jawzi attributes it to Ubayy, Abu al-Aliya, Abu al-Jawza and al-Sha'bi; the plural retains alif. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبي بن كعب وأبو العالية وأبو الجوزاء والشعبي)]
- 39:36/q03/p5 A reading has tanwīn on bi-kāfin with plural ʿibādahu. — Ibn al-Jawzi attributes it to al-Sulami, Abu Ja'far, Shayba and al-A'mash. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي وأبو جعفر وشيبة والأعمش)]
- 39:36/q03/p6 A reading annexes bi-kāfī to ʿibādihi. — Abu Hayyan, Samīn and Alusi report the genitive construction. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو جعفر ومجاهد وابن وثاب وطلحة والأعمش وحمزة والكسائي)]
- 39:36/q03/p7 A reading has the verb yukāfī with ʿibādahu as its object, meaning emphatic sufficiency. — Ibn al-Jawzi attributes it to Ibn Mas'ud and Abu Raja. Abu Hayyan and Samīn parse its object; they and Alusi compare the intensive form with yujāzī/yujzī. Alusi favors sufficiency as the Qur'an often uses it. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود وأبو رجاء العطاردي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو جعفر ومجاهد وابن وثاب وطلحة والأعمش وحمزة والكسائي)]
- 39:36/q03/p8 The alternate verb can mean God recompenses His servants. — Abu Hayyan gives the reciprocal reading; Samīn derives it from softened hamzated yukāfiʾ. Alusi says requital fits rewards in 39:33–35 and the supposed counter-requital of idols, yet he considers the common reading's sufficiency sense stronger. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:36/q04 Who is God's servant?  [عَبْدَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether singular servant is specific or generic, and who the plural includes.
- 39:36/q04/p1 The servant is Muhammad. — Most commentators identify the addressed Prophet; Farisi, Abu Ubayd via Qurtubi, Tabarsi and Ibn Ashur point to the following direct address. Tabatabai links him to the preceding praised believer; Study Quran's Tabari source compares earlier prophets. [18 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; MAWARDI-FULL(بعضهم); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(أبو علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد,عامة القراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(السدي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 39:36/q04/p2 The plural includes earlier prophets whom God protected from their peoples. — Farra cites Shu'ayb at 11:54; Baghawi cites 40:5. Tabarsi names Abraham saved from fire, Noah from drowning and Jonah from his plight. Ibn al-Jawzi says Muhammad will likewise be protected. [7 notes: FARRA; MAWARDI-FULL(الباقون); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جعفر وحمزة والكسائي); TABRISI(أبو علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(حمزة والكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 39:36/q04/p3 The plural includes prophets and believers, especially Muhammad and his followers. — Qurtubi gives prophets and believers as an option; Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Samīn, Biqai, Alusi and Ibn Ashur include faithful followers. Alusi says the singular too can cover followers' worldly and religious needs, citing 39:33. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); NASAFI-FULL(حمزة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو جعفر ومجاهد وابن وثاب وطلحة والأعمش وحمزة والكسائي); SAMIN-DURR(الأخوان); BIQAI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وأبو جعفر); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:36/q04/p4 The singular can mean any devoted believer, with Muhammad foremost. — Qurtubi compares generic al-insān at 103:2; Ibn Kathir specifies worship and reliance; Biqai pure devotion; Bursevi true servitude. Baydawi and Alusi see support in the plural reading. Study Quran's Ibn Kathir source specifies sole worship and trust. [7 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 39:36/q04/p5 Both believers and unbelievers can be called servants here, with different outcomes. — Al-Jurjani, reported by Qurtubi, says the believer receives reward and the unbeliever punishment. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الجرجاني)]
- 39:36/q04/p6 An inward reading calls the servant one who worships and believes, or who realizes the divine identity. — Bursevi reports the alternatives from unnamed great Sufis. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الكبار)]

## 39:36/q05 What sufficiency does God promise?  [بِكَافٍ عَبْدَهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: The scope of sufficiency: protection, provision, recompense, or causal power.
- 39:36/q05/p1 God protects His servant against enemies and idols. — Muqatil, Wahidi, Tabarsi, Qurtubi, Ibn Ashur and Islahi stress protection; Nasafi compares 15:95, Ibn Ashur and Tabatabai 2:137. Biqai says God aids pure devotees until His religion prevails. Ibn Kathir says one relying on God cannot be overpowered. Tabatabai calls it an implicit promise; Study Quran compares protection of earlier prophets. [18 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(حمزة والكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 39:36/q05/p2 God supplies the worshipper's religious and worldly needs. — Ibn Kathir links worship and trust; Alusi names religious and worldly needs; Bursevi glosses sufficiency as meeting need and desired end. Islahi stresses that harm depends on God alone; Study Quran says the one master frees the believer from divided aspirations. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 39:36/q05/p3 God suffices believers with reward and unbelievers with punishment. — Al-Jurjani's explanation is reported by Qurtubi. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الجرجاني)]
- 39:36/q05/p4 God's sufficiency is not limited by ordinary causes, though using means remains appropriate. — Tabatabai compares unrestricted promises of answered supplication at 2:186 and 40:60. Biqai says God may attach effects to worldly means or render them ineffective, and using them accords with wise ordering. [2 notes: TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 39:36/q06 Why call Muhammad 'His servant' and then address him directly?  [عَبْدَهُۥ وَيُخَوِّفُونَكَ · rhetoric]
- 39:36/q06/p1 The title honors and reassures him; the shift focuses the threat on him and rebukes the idolaters. — Abu Hayyan calls servanthood an honor. Ibn Ashur says third person directs the statement to polytheists and assures God will not abandon Muhammad; iltifāt to direct address then singles out the Prophet. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:36/q07 How is وَيُخَوِّفُونَكَ connected to the opening?  [وَيُخَوِّفُونَكَ · grammar]
turns on: Circumstantial clause versus independent continuation.
- 39:36/q07/p1 It is a circumstantial clause around God's reassurance. — Samīn permits this parse; Ibn Ashur says the first sentence may be independent while the threat clause is circumstantial. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:36/q07/p2 It is a new sentence placed after the reassurance for effect. — Samīn permits a fresh sentence. Ibn Ashur says ordinary order would mention threats before protection, but the opening was advanced to comfort the Prophet and dismay opponents. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:36/q08 Who or what are 'those besides Him'?  [بِٱلَّذِينَ مِن دُونِهِۦ · referent]
turns on: Idol gods, human force, or a broader set of imagined powers.
- 39:36/q08/p1 They are the idols or deities worshipped by Quraysh. — Mujahid, al-Suddi, Ibn Zayd, al-Kalbi and many authors identify idols; Muqatil names al-Lat, al-Uzza and Manat. Ibn Ashur explains the rational relative as conventional and an implied clause of taking or worshipping them. Wahidi observes the idolaters admitted God is Creator at 39:38; Qurtubi compares Abraham at 6:81. [23 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL(كفار مكة); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,السدي); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي والسدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة والسدي وابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(السدي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 39:36/q08/p2 The intimidation can involve the polytheists' own threats, numbers and strength. — Mawardi offers their personal threats; Qurtubi cites 54:44 for their numbers and strength. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 39:36/q08/p3 The false powers can include saints, wealth, status, race and self-sufficiency. — Asad includes living and dead saints and imagined abstract powers; people fear practical harm if they ignore them. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 39:36/q09 What threat or revelation occasion is reported?  [وَيُخَوِّفُونَكَ بِٱلَّذِينَ مِن دُونِهِۦ · reports]
turns on: Meccan threats versus Khalid's later mission; whether the latter was occasion or illustration.
- 39:36/q09/p1 Quraysh threatened that their gods would hurt, maim, sicken or madden Muhammad for criticizing them. — Farra and Ibn al-Jawzi say the verse responded. Muqatil names al-Lat, al-Uzza and Manat; the unnamed man to Ma'mar says Quraysh would order their gods to afflict him. Jishumi records both a question about fear and a demand to stop. Qurtubi mentions mutilation; the report versions vary on the exact harm. [20 notes: MUQATIL(كفار مكة); FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(رجل); TAB-FULL; JISHUMI(المشركون); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قريش); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قريش); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية,قريش); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 39:36/q09/p2 A warning to Khalid at al-Uzza was a threat to the Prophet who sent him. — Baydawi, Qurtubi and Ibn Ashur give this connection; Ibn Ashur says it would make the occasion Medinan. Tabarsi reports the incident. [4 notes: TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL(البيضاوي); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 39:36/q09/p3 The verse was revealed twice, for Muhammad and later for Khalid. — Bursevi reports unnamed exegetes' analogy with al-Fatiha in Mecca and Medina. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعض أهل التفسير)]
- 39:36/q09/p4 The Khalid story merely illustrates a familiar threat, not a Medinan occasion. — Ibn Ashur objects that a Medinan occasion does not fit the context. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 39:36/q10 What happened when Khalid went against al-Uzza?  [بِٱلَّذِينَ مِن دُونِهِۦ · reports]
turns on: The object destroyed and the report's outcome differ.
- 39:36/q10/p1 He ignored the keeper's warning and smashed the idol's nose or face with an axe. — Qatada's reports in Tabari, Ibn Abi Hatim and Qurtubi specify its nose. Jishumi adds Khalid's declaration of humiliation; Abu Hayyan says face. Tabarsi and Baydawi recount the warning and blow. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); JISHUMI; TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(البيضاوي)]
- 39:36/q10/p2 He uprooted an al-Uzza tree and killed the demon beneath it. — Bursevi says Arab idolaters worshipped the tree and that the Prophet then declared al-Uzza would never again be worshipped. [1 notes: BURSEVI(خالد بن الوليد)]

## 39:36/q11 Why are the idol threats false or blameworthy?  [وَيُخَوِّفُونَكَ بِٱلَّذِينَ مِن دُونِهِۦ · interpretation]
turns on: Idols' impotence, the creed implied by fear, and historical parallel.
- 39:36/q11/p1 Idols cannot benefit or harm, so fear of them is ignorance or mockery. — Jishumi says imitation attributes power to inanimate things; Abu Hayyan calls the threat mockery. Ibn Kathir calls it ignorance. Islahi says only the Creator harms or benefits. Ibn Ashur notes their evident impotence. [6 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:36/q11/p2 Fearing harm independent of God's leave implies disbelief and association. — Islahi says that fear implies God is insufficient; Asad says the godless magnify imagined powers and frighten themselves and others. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES]
- 39:36/q11/p3 The threat echoes earlier threats to prophets. — Jishumi, Abu Hayyan and Biqai compare 11:54, where Ad say a god harmed Hud. Ibn Ashur compares Abraham's opponents urging his burning at 21:68; Biqai notes later feminizing idols humiliates them. [4 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:36/q12 What does God's misguidance and the absence of a guide mean?  [وَمَن يُضْلِلِ ٱللَّهُ فَمَا لَهُۥ مِنْ هَادٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the loss is access to Islam, Paradise, further guidance, sound judgment, or the result of prior deeds.
- 39:36/q12/p1 One God misguides has no one to lead him to Islam or right conduct. — Muqatil specifies Islam; Baghawi and Nasafi state the maxim. Bursevi says no one guides the person to good. Study Quran gives parallels at 4:88, 4:143, 42:46, 7:186, 13:33, 39:23, 40:33, 17:97, 18:17, 42:44 and 7:178, describing loss of path and protector. [7 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 39:36/q12/p2 Disbelief and sin take one from the path to Paradise, and no one leads him there. — Tabarsi specifies the destination. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 39:36/q12/p3 Once someone strays from truth, no one may call him a guide. — Tabarsi reports this alternative construal. [1 notes: TABRISI(قيل)]
- 39:36/q12/p4 God withholds further increments of guidance, which none else can give. — Tabarsi reports this further alternative. [1 notes: TABRISI(قيل)]
- 39:36/q12/p5 The misguided forgets God's sufficiency and fears powerless things, lacking guidance to good. — Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi connect misguidance to such fear; Bursevi mentions loss of sound understanding. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:36/q12/p6 Misguidance is punishment for previous conduct, not an initial act of leading astray. — Tabatabai calls it retribution; Islahi invokes God's established practice in response to deeds. [2 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 39:36/q13 What does the verse teach about God's control of guidance?  [وَمَن يُضْلِلِ ٱللَّهُ فَمَا لَهُۥ مِنْ هَادٍۢ · theology]
turns on: God's agency, retributive misguidance, and divergent effects of causes.
- 39:36/q13/p1 God alone controls guidance and misguidance; none overturns His decree. — Razi says proofs benefit only those singled out for guidance; his Ash'ari associates cite the paired statements for divine creation of acts and willing all events. Qurtubi and Biqai reject the Qadariyya. Abu Hayyan attributes both outcomes to God. Tabatabai grounds the maxims in might and requital; Islahi says they rebuke polytheists. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:36/q13/p2 The same cause may misguide some and guide others; God may still guide opponents. — Biqai says differing outcomes bring punishment or blessing according to God's will. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 39:36/q13/p3 Divine misguidance is retribution for unbelief and deeds. — Tabatabai denies initial divine deception; Islahi cites the divine practice tied to deeds; Qurtubi calls it abandonment. [3 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 39:36/q13/p4 Seeing good or evil as independent of God is misguidance. — Al-Ta'wilat al-Najmiyya, quoted by Bursevi, says God alone guides and misleads. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 39:36/q14 Why does the closing clause deny any guide in this form?  [فَمَا لَهُۥ مِنْ هَادٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 39:36/q14/p1 It figuratively denies attainment of guidance; min makes the negation generic. — Ibn Ashur compares 7:186, whose lā plus accusative also gives generic negation. He says fronting lahu stresses their plight and idol fear, a more severe error than the failure in 7:185; the next verse assures guided people remain firm. He sees deep inherited misguidance as exceptionally hard to remove. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:36/q14/p2 Naming God explicitly makes the paired guidance clauses universal maxims. — Tabatabai says the apparent noun replaces a possible pronoun; placed here, the maxims indicate that idol-threateners cannot attain their aims against the guided Prophet. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 39:36/q15 How is هَادٍۢ pronounced at pause?  [هَادٍۢ · readings]
- 39:36/q15/p1 Ibn Kathir and Ibn Muhaysin retain final yāʾ at pause; others omit it. — Qurtubi records both locations. Nashr notes a rasm-based pause rule; Qiraat-Er says yāʾ realization at a boundary preserves the active participle. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن كثير وابن محيصن); NASHR; QIRAAT-ER]

## 39:36/q16 How does this verse connect to surrounding verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Links to preceding promises, next verse, following address, and earlier scriptural parallels.
- 39:36/q16/p1 It extends the promises to truthful believers and reassures the Prophet after idol threats. — Tabarsi links the preceding truthful and truth-affirming. Alusi traces 39:32–35 from denunciation to recompense and favor. Ibn Ashur says the parable of one master at 39:29 provoked the threat; Study Quran says monotheism frees one from divided masters. Islahi places reassurance in section IV, 39:36–52. [5 notes: TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 39:36/q16/p2 The guidance maxims explain the idolaters' failure and the faithful's security. — Tabatabai says the threateners will not attain their aims, since God's might and requital underlie guidance. Kuran Yolu says verse 37 assures the guided. Study Quran notes recurring contrasts of believers and unbelievers. [4 notes: TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 39:36/q16/p3 The next singular address favors identifying the servant as Muhammad. — Farisi and Abu Ali via Tabarsi make this argument; Abu Ubayd prefers singular for it; Alusi notes the adjoining threat. [4 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(حمزة والكسائي); TABRISI(أبو علي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد); ALUSI-FULL(السدي)]
- 39:36/q16/p4 Other verses give parallel assurance or idol threats. — Nasafi cites 15:95; Tabatabai and Ibn Ashur cite 2:137. Jishumi, Abu Hayyan and Biqai compare Hud at 11:54; Ibn Ashur compares Abraham at 21:68. [7 notes: NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 39:36/q17 What inward demand follows from God's sufficiency?  [بِكَافٍ عَبْدَهُۥ · inward]
turns on: Exclusive reliance, self-purification, or contemplation of God's care.
- 39:36/q17/p1 Seek sufficiency from God alone, not hope or fear from creatures. — Abu Bakr ibn Tahir says discontent with divine sufficiency means ruin; Ibn Ata says hoping in, fearing or coveting creatures forfeits servitude. Ja'far al-Sadiq praises a poor person's independence from the rich. Bursevi reports a hadith about one concern being sufficient for God's care. [6 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر,ابن عطاء); BURSEVI(أبو بكر بن طاهر,ابن عطاء,النبي,جعفر الصادق)]
- 39:36/q17/p2 Surrender, purified worship and scrupulous attention bring divine acceptance. — Kashf al-Asrar says abandoning scheming and asking only God frees concern. Abu Sa'id says the lower self has no share in God's trust. The Muadh report says purified religion makes a little deed suffice. David's anecdote concerns lawful food; Utba's concerns absorption in the Creator amid a market. [5 notes: BURSEVI(أبو سعيد أبو الخير,النبي,داود,عبد الواحد زيد,كشف الأسرار)]
- 39:36/q17/p3 God suffices the servant against all things, whereas nothing suffices against God. — Al-Ta'wilat al-Najmiyya says heavenly treasures did not distract Muhammad from God, citing 53:17–18. Ara'is al-Baqli gently reproaches imagining that God will abandon one under His care. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,عرائس البقلي)]

## 39:36/q18 What Prophetic teachings illustrate sufficiency and reliance?  [بِكَافٍ عَبْدَهُۥ · reports]
turns on: The reports give different practical forms of sufficiency.
- 39:36/q18/p1 Rely on God alone; people cannot benefit or harm beyond His decree. — Ibn Abbas transmits counsel to obey God, ask and seek help only from Him; patience brings relief and ease follows hardship. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس)]
- 39:36/q18/p2 Success joins guidance to Islam, sufficient livelihood and contentment. — Ibn Kathir reports the saying; al-Tirmidhi graded it sahih. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 39:36/q18/p3 Purified religion makes a little deed sufficient. — Bursevi reports the Prophet's advice to Muadh. [1 notes: BURSEVI(النبي)]
- 39:36/q18/p4 God suffices the worldly and afterlife concerns of one who wakes with a single concern. — Bursevi reports this hadith. [1 notes: BURSEVI(النبي)]

## 39:36/q19 What figurative examples clarify compressed and extended likenesses?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the intended comparison is immediately apparent or requires explanation.
- 39:36/q19/p1 A familiar simple likeness can be compressed into metaphor. — Al-Jurjani illustrates with a brave man called a lion and a beautiful woman called the sun. [1 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 39:36/q19/p2 A complex likeness needs explanatory clauses rather than a compressed metaphor. — Al-Jurjani says calling a man 'night' alone suggests darkness or misses that a fugitive cannot escape his reach. [2 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]

## 39:36/q20 Does the oath prohibition bear on a construction cited here?  [* · grammar]
- 39:36/q20/p1 Al-Zajjaj invokes the ban on oaths by other than God. — Burhan reports this objection to the preceding construction but calls it strange because the ban concerns created beings. [1 notes: BURHAN(الزجاج)]
