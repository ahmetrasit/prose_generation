# 17:110: 35 questions

## 17:110/q01 What prompted the command to call on Allah or al-Raḥmān?  [قُلِ ٱدْعُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ أَوِ ٱدْعُوا۟ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنَ · reports]
turns on: The reported hearers and names spoken differ.
- 17:110/q01/p1 Polytheists heard the Prophet invoke both names and accused him of calling on two gods. — Ibn ʿAbbās and reports through al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Thaʿlabī and others locate the exchange in Mecca, often at night in prostration; some name Abū Jahl. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers the report with Allah and al-Raḥmān because those are the verse's two names. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī finds this occasion best suited to the wording. [26 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(أبو جهل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî; Kurtubî); STUDYQURAN(Bḍ, IK); TABATABAI(ابن عباس)+]
- 17:110/q01/p2 Jews or other People of the Book asked why the Prophet seldom used al-Raḥmān although Torah used it often. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk supplies the Torah comparison; al-Māwardī names converts including Ibn Salām. Bayḍāwī, reported by al-Ālūsī, prefers this occasion because the reference to beautiful names addresses a perceived ranking; al-Ṭībī distinguishes Jewish preference for one name from pagan objection to combining names. [14 notes: THALABI(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); WAHIDI-ASBAB(الضحاك); JISHUMI(الضحاك); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(الضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(اهل الكتاب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(البيضاوي)+]
- 17:110/q01/p3 The Prophet invoked al-Raḥmān and al-Raḥīm in prostration; pagans mistook the names for two gods. — Ibn ʿAbbās's report, as conveyed by al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Jišumī and others, specifies a Meccan night prayer and these two mercy names; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the command also implicitly licenses al-Raḥīm. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,مكحول); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مكحول); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Bḍ, IK)]
- 17:110/q01/p4 A hearer associated al-Raḥmān with Musaylima or another man of Yamāma. — Makḥūl's reports, as cited by al-Ṭabarī and al-Qurṭubī, name a man in Yamāma; Ibn ʿAbbās reports in al-Thaʿlabī and Ibn al-Jawzī name Musaylima. Al-Bursawī relays al-Suhaylī's claim that Musaylima adopted the title before the Prophet's father's birth and was killed by Waḥshī. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(مكحول); THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مكحول); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(مكحول); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(السهيلي)]
- 17:110/q01/p5 Arabs questioned al-Raḥmān after the Prophet began writing the full basmala. — Maymūn b. Mihrān says the Prophet changed from 'In Your name, O God' to the basmala after 27:30; some reports say Quraysh knew al-Raḥīm but not al-Raḥmān. [7 notes: THALABI(ميمون بن مهران); WAHIDI-ASBAB(ميمون بن مهران); JISHUMI(ميمون بن مهران); TABRISI(ميمون بن مهران); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ميمون بن مهران); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ميمون بن مهران)]
- 17:110/q01/p6 Opponents treated the name al-Raḥmān as evidence of outside religious influence. — Iṣlāḥī says both names were known to Arabs, with Raḥmān perhaps more common among the People of the Book; Quraysh alleged Jewish assistance in making the Qurʾān (25:4) and feared imposition of their tradition. Kur'an Yolu judges concern over use of such names likelier than a two-god rumor; Ibn al-ʿArabī in al-Burhān reads 'what is al-Raḥmān?' (25:60) as ignorance of an attribute, not the name. [5 notes: BURHAN(ابن العربي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 17:110/q01/p7 A hearer associated al-Raḥmān with a man in Yemen. — Al-Suyūṭī's Makḥūl report locates the man in Yemen after the Prophet invoked al-Raḥmān and al-Raḥīm in Meccan night prostration. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(مكحول)]
- 17:110/q01/p8 Jews questioned the name while the Prophet stood in a field with a palm branch. — Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī's account in al-Durr adds the field, palm branch, Jewish questioners and a priest in Yamāma bearing the title. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي)]

## 17:110/q02 How does the naming command connect to its context?  [قُلِ ٱدْعُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ أَوِ ٱدْعُوا۟ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنَ · links]
- 17:110/q02/p1 It answers surrounding challenges and summons readers to the Qurʾān's revealer. — Abū Ḥayyān says opponents, unable to answer the Qurʾān's challenge, accused the Prophet of the plurality he condemned. Al-Biqāʿī ties the command to learned believers' response, repeated mercy language across sūra 17 and prostration; the names meet the imagined question which name to use. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the prior verse occasioned the objection. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 17:110/q03 What does 'call upon' mean in the opening command?  [ٱدْعُوا۟ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb is naming with an omitted object or direct invocation.
- 17:110/q03/p1 Name God Allah or al-Raḥmān, with an understood object of the naming verb. — Al-Jurjānī argues that ayyan requires two alternative designations of one object and reconstructs 'call Him Allah or call Him al-Raḥmān'; al-Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī say a second object is omitted. Abū Ḥayyān records a construction with bāʾ and a poetic parallel. Ibn Hishām's distinction, cited in al-Itqān, separates genuine ellipsis from a verb used without an intended object; misunderstanding the ellipsis could imply shirk. [21 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURHAN; ITQAN(ابن هشام); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,الطيبي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 17:110/q03/p2 Invoke God vocatively by either name. — Abū Ḥayyān finds this suited to the occasion reports and distinguishes God's name from His attribute; al-Ālūsī reports and favors his reading against al-Zamakhsharī, while allowing the two designations to be coordinated because their concepts differ. Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Itqān leave both invocation and naming possible. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)+]
- 17:110/q03/p3 Worship God under either name. — Al-Dāmghānī notes duʿāʾ can mean worship, adducing 6:71, 10:106, 26:213 and 28:88. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]

## 17:110/q04 How is idʿū read?  [ٱدْعُوا۟ · readings]
- 17:110/q04/p1 Readers vary the linking vowels without changing the meaning. — Ibn Mujāhid reports disagreement among Abū ʿAmr transmitters over lām and wāw, while ʿĀṣim and Ḥamza use kasra on both; Ibn Khālawayh gives ḍamma or kasra on idʿū. Qirāʾāt Er identifies the final lām and another vowel as phonetic liaison. [4 notes: IBNMUJAHID; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; QIRAAT-ER]

## 17:110/q05 What related practices of supplication are reported?  [ٱدْعُوا۟ · reports]
turns on: Which transmitted practices guide petition.
- 17:110/q05/p1 Call with certainty, attention and requests worthy of divine power; use prophetic prayers. — Ibn al-Jazarī transmits Abū Hurayra reports urging certainty of response, an attentive heart and large petitions, naming al-Tirmidhī, al-Ḥākim, Muslim and others as transmitters; at Qurʾān completion he prefers the Prophet's comprehensive supplications over private fixed formulas. Al-Biqāʿī cites the servant's nearness in prostration. [4 notes: NASHR(أبو هريرة); BIQAI-FULL; NASHR+]
- 17:110/q05/p2 Raise the palms for supplication and wipe the face afterward. — Ibn al-Jazarī cites Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Sāʾib and ʿUmar through Abū Dāwūd, al-Ḥākim and al-Tirmidhī, and adds a personal vision in 792 AH; he says Ibn ʿAbd al-Salām's rejection may reflect not seeing these reports. [4 notes: NASHR(ابن عباس,السائب بن يزيد عن أبيه,عمر); NASHR(ابن عبد السلام)-]
- 17:110/q05/p3 Wiping the face after supplication is rejected. — Ibn ʿAbd al-Salām rejects the practice in his fatwas; Ibn al-Jazarī counters that he may not have seen the reports from Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Sāʾib and ʿUmar. [1 notes: NASHR(ابن عبد السلام)]

## 17:110/q06 What do the two divine names imply about the One invoked?  [ٱللَّهَ أَوِ ٱدْعُوا۟ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنَ · theology]
turns on: Whether the names are equivalent in reference, efficacy, or rank.
- 17:110/q06/p1 Both names designate the one God, so either may be used without multiplying deities. — Muqātil points also to the names in 59:24; al-Zajjāj says al-Raḥmān occurs in earlier revelation; Mujāhid extends the permission to every divine name. Al-Biqāʿī distinguishes Allah's majesty from al-Raḥmān's beneficence. Bayḍāwī prefers equal goodness and efficacy in invocation while also reporting equality of referent. Al-Ālūsī relates the answer to 17:111's denial of partner; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses that names guide worship toward their single bearer. [32 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق,الأخفش سعيد,الحسن); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,المبرد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري وابن عطية); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 17:110/q06/p2 The names are equally good or efficacious in invocation, even if their aspects differ. — Bayḍāwī prefers equal success in attaining the supplicant's aim because the clause names all of God's beautiful names; al-Ālūsī connects this with the Jewish preference for Raḥmān and relays the view that one good name suffices against it. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي,صاحب الكشف); BAYDAWI-FULL+]

## 17:110/q07 What distinguishes al-Raḥmān and Allah as names?  [ٱللَّهَ أَوِ ٱدْعُوا۟ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنَ · theology]
turns on: Whether al-Raḥmān is a proper name, attribute, or uniquely universal mercy.
- 17:110/q07/p1 Al-Raḥmān is uniquely divine and denotes encompassing mercy, while Allah gathers divine majesty or the essence. — Yaḥyā reports al-Ḥasan's exclusivity claim; al-Burhān notes faʿlān is more emphatic than faʿīl and records Ibn Mālik's answer to a poet's indefinite raḥmānan: the poet meant mercy, not the established proper name. Al-Ḥarālī contrasts al-Raḥmān's encompassing creative mercy with al-Raḥīm's particular mercy. Asad calls the grace unconditional; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī derives Allah from al-ilāh, 'worshipped'. [12 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); BURHAN(ابن مالك,الزمخشري,بعضهم); BIQAI-FULL(أبو الحسن الحرالي); ITQAN; BURSEVI(المولى الفناري); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 17:110/q07/p2 Al-Raḥmān chiefly connotes mighty, just kingship rather than mercy alone. — Al-Barzabādhānī in al-Burhān adduces 25:26, 25:60, 19:18, 19:92, 20:108, 21:42 and 19:93 for majesty, power, protection, self-sufficiency and universal sovereignty. [1 notes: BURHAN(البرزاباذاني)]

## 17:110/q08 Which divine names are ranked greatest or preferred?  [ٱللَّهَ أَوِ ٱدْعُوا۟ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنَ · theology]
turns on: What property or word order establishes precedence.
- 17:110/q08/p1 Allah ranks highest; al-Raḥmān is near it. — Al-Ghazālī says Allah uniquely denotes the essence containing every attribute while other names usually denote single qualities; Raḥmān is also exclusive to God but derives from mercy. Al-Rāzī says these two surpass other names and the order puts Allah first; al-Ālūsī notes that this can address the Jewish ranking claim. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الرازي,الغزالي)]
- 17:110/q08/p2 Some Jews regarded al-Raḥmān as most beloved or greatest. — Al-Ālūsī infers this from the Torah's frequent use of the name and allows that Muslims too distinguish ranks among names. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 17:110/q08/p3 The greatest name is implicated in invoking the One, Eternal Refuge, who neither begets nor is begotten. — Al-Ālūsī cites a Prophetic report promising response to a man who invoked God by that formula. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 17:110/q08/p4 The greatest name appears in 2:163 and in the opening of Āl ʿImrān. — Al-Ālūsī cites a Prophetic report joining the One Merciful in 2:163 with the Living, Sustaining in 3:1–2. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 17:110/q09 How does 'or' relate the two invocations?  [أَوِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the verse merely permits alternatives or also explicitly permits combining them.
- 17:110/q09/p1 It offers a choice between two equally valid names. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī read aw as choice; al-Ṭībī in al-Ālūsī says this particularly suits the Jewish objection to preferring one name. The name chosen does not alter the one referent. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي)]
- 17:110/q09/p2 It permits either name and also permits combining both. — Al-Ṭabrisī explicitly allows one or both. Al-Jalabī argues that the fronted predicate makes all beautiful names God's alone and answers the pagan plurality charge; al-Ālūsī favors permissive aw because nonconflicting names can rationally be joined, while finding al-Ṭībī's expanded conditional reconstruction strained. Al-Ṭībī says using both may be better under 7:180. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الجلبي,الطيبي); ALUSI-FULL+]

## 17:110/q10 How does 'ayyan mā tadʿū' work?  [أَيًّۭا مَّا تَدْعُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the following mā is syntactically extra, emphatic, or another condition.
- 17:110/q10/p1 Mā is an added or emphatic particle; the conditional answer is introduced by fāʾ. — Al-Farrāʾ and Ibn Qutayba compare 23:40; al-Wāḥidī also cites 38:11 and 71:25. Al-Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī say tanwīn replaces the genitive complement and mā strengthens generality. Al-Burhān favors calling it a connecting particle because no Qurʾānic particle is meaningless; al-Itqān distinguishes emphatic mā that does and does not cancel governance, with al-Fārisī and Abū al-Baqāʾ emphasizing its force. [26 notes: FARRA; AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ITQAN(أبو البقاء,ابن الحاجب,الفارسي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 17:110/q10/p2 Ayyan is a conditional object, with an omitted genitive understood; tadʿū is jussive. — Sībawayh treats free ayy like man and illustrates annexed and distributive forms with lines of al-ʿAbbās b. Mirdās and Khidāsh; Yaḥyā reports the usage as Kalb dialect. Al-Wāḥidī distinguishes interrogative, conditional and declarative ayy and cites Zuhayr's feminine ayatan. Al-Samīn makes ayyan the object; Ibn ʿĀshūr says mā turns original interrogative ayy conditional. [16 notes: SIBAWAYH(خداش بن زهير); YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 17:110/q10/p3 Mā repeats or shares conditional force with ayy. — Al-Khalīl's analysis in Sībawayh compares mahmā to two mā particles and the patterns matā mā, in mā and aynamā. Al-Jišumī says mā can mean 'any thing'; al-Samīn cites a poem for doubled conditionals, and al-Ālūsī another for rare paired particles. [4 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل); JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الشاعر)]

## 17:110/q11 How does 'ayyan mā tadʿū fa-lahu' form its answer?  [أَيًّۭا مَّا تَدْعُوا۟ فَلَهُ · grammar]
- 17:110/q11/p1 The fāʾ clause supplies, or stands in place of, the conditional answer and refers back to the named God. — Al-Samīn rejects an omitted 'it is permissible' followed by a new clause. Al-Ālūsī says the clause supplies the rationale where the consequence is expected, while al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī similarly calls cause substituted for effect; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn reject a pause after ayyan with independent mā because mā cannot denote rational persons and the scope fails. Al-Ṭabrisī explains singular dhālika by reference to the act (2:68). [11 notes: TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(البحر); TABATABAI]

## 17:110/q12 How is ayyan mā read?  [أَيًّۭا مَّا تَدْعُوا۟ · readings]
- 17:110/q12/p1 Ṭalḥa b. Muṣarrif reads ayyan man tadʿū. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī transmit the reading. Al-Kisāʾī treats man as extra; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn also consider an exceptional pair of conditionals. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(طلحة بن مصرف); QURTUBI-FULL(طلحة بن مصرف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(طلحة بن مصرف); SAMIN-DURR(الكسائي,طلحة بن مصرف)]

## 17:110/q13 Where may a reader pause in ayyan mā?  [أَيًّۭا مَّا · readings]
turns on: The relation between the written separate words and syntactic attachment.
- 17:110/q13/p1 Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī are reported to stop on ayyan. — Al-Dānī, Ibn Ghalbūn and Ibn Shurayḥ in al-Nashr report this, with disagreement over Ruways; al-Samīn explains that two readers change ayyan's tanwīn to alif at the stop. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; NASHR(الداني، وطاهر بن غلبون، وابن شريح)]
- 17:110/q13/p2 Other readers stop on mā; some consider this preferable. — Ibn Saʿdān in al-Nashr prefers mā because it augments ayyan; al-Samīn reports the other readers stopping there. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; NASHR(الداني، وطاهر بن غلبون، وابن شريح); NASHR(ابن سعدان)+]
- 17:110/q13/p3 Both stopping points are permissible. — Ibn al-Jazarī notes most authorities make no special ruling and favors both because the words are separate in the rasm. [1 notes: NASHR+]

## 17:110/q14 Who is meant by 'to Him' in the beautiful-names clause?  [فَلَهُ · referent]
- 17:110/q14/p1 The pronoun refers to God's single essence, not to either spoken name. — Al-Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī distinguish bearer from title; al-Biqāʿī sees emphatic pronoun repetition and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls names paths toward the transcendent referent. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 17:110/q15 Why are God's names called the Most Beautiful?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · theology]
- 17:110/q15/p1 They express divine perfection, praise, holiness, majesty and good attributes. — Al-Dāmghānī lists knowledge, power, hearing, sight, will, speech and life; al-Ṭabrisī groups names of essence, good action and servants' turning to God. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī and al-Bursawī stress praise and sanctification. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says each name denotes pure perfection free of defect, unlike creaturely traits; Asad glosses 'most perfect or goodly' (7:145). [13 notes: DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]

## 17:110/q16 What are divine names in relation to God and spoken words?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · theology]
turns on: Whether 'name' denotes the divine reality or the human utterance.
- 17:110/q16/p1 The names' realities point to the divine essence and attributes; spoken labels are created signs distinct from their bearer. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes essential positive names, identical with the essence, from actional or negative names that are additional; he calls words names of names. Reports from al-Ṣādiq reject worshipping a name alone or pairing name and meaning as two objects; a report through Ibrāhīm b. ʿUmar describes a hidden name and three manifested parts. Worship is directed to God, not to angelic or jinn manifestations. [10 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); TABATABAI(ابن رئاب,هشام بن الحكم)]
- 17:110/q16/p2 The spoken name is identical with the named God. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this early theological claim only to reject it through the distinction between names and spoken labels. [1 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI(هشام بن الحكم)-]
- 17:110/q16/p3 The name is identical with the named God. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports that some early theologians said this because they conflated the spoken label with the divine-name reality, and rejects the equation. [1 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI(هشام بن الحكم)-]

## 17:110/q17 How many divine names are reported?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · reports]
turns on: Which count and what it promises.
- 17:110/q17/p1 A report assigns God ninety-nine names. — Abū Hurayra's report in al-Ṭabarī says these are in the Qurʾān and enumerating them leads to Paradise; Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites the list, and al-Ghazālī in al-Ālūsī compares Allah and Raḥmān among the ninety-nine. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 17:110/q17/p2 A report describes 360 beautiful names derived from twelve pillars, with one hidden name. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmits al-Ṣādiq's four pillars each for Allah, Tabārak and Subḥān, thirty names per pillar, with the three revealed forms veiling a hidden name. [2 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله,إبراهيم بن عمر)]

## 17:110/q18 How are divine names established?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · theology]
- 17:110/q18/p1 A divine name requires revelation or consensus, not mere rational coinage. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī state this restriction; their concern is that the name truly ascribes a revealed perfection. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 17:110/q19 Does the beauty of God's names rule out His creating injustice?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · theology]
turns on: Whether creating an act makes it an attribute and name of the creator.
- 17:110/q19/p1 No: creating an act does not make God bear its description. — Al-Rāzī replies that creating movement or color does not make God movable or colored; al-Ālūsī likewise denies the inference. Both recommend reverent names such as Creator of heaven and earth or Creator of all instead of distasteful qualified descriptions. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الجبائي)]
- 17:110/q19/p2 Yes: if God created unjust acts, He would acquire an ugly name. — Al-Jubbāʾī argues this in al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī, taking the claim that every divine name is beautiful as the premise. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الجبائي); RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(الجبائي)-]

## 17:110/q20 What does the verse establish about divine action and worship?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · theology]
- 17:110/q20/p1 It establishes one God to whom petitions are directed and from whom evil or injustice is excluded. — Al-Ṭabrisī uses the unity of names and attributes to support invocation by beautiful names before requests and argues that He does no evil or injustice; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says supposed divine offspring and subordinate causes remain servants under God's power. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 17:110/q21 How is the voice command read?  [وَلَا تَجْهَرْ بِصَلَاتِكَ وَلَا تُخَافِتْ بِهَا · readings]
- 17:110/q21/p1 A reading attributed to Ibn ʿUmar or ʿAbd Allāh says not to lower or excessively raise 'your voice'. — Abū Razīn's transmission in al-Durr and al-Ālūsī puts the voice explicitly in the wording; al-Durr attributes it to Ibn ʿUmar while al-Ālūsī says ʿAbd Allāh. ## 17:110/q23 How should prayer volume be moderated?  [وَلَا تَجْهَرْ بِصَلَاتِكَ وَلَا تُخَافِتْ بِهَا وَٱبْتَغِ بَيْنَ ذَٰلِكَ سَبِيلًۭا · interpretation]

## 17:110/q24 Was the voice instruction abrogated?  [وَلَا تَجْهَرْ بِصَلَاتِكَ وَلَا تُخَافِتْ بِهَا · sciences]

- 17:110/q24/p1 Yes, by the command to remember God inwardly in 7:205. — Some Medinan authorities in ʿAbd al-Razzāq and Ibn ʿAbbās in Ibn al-Jawzī transmit this; Ikrima and al-Ḥasan in al-Ṭabarī link the two verses, as does a report in al-Durr. - 17:110/q33/p1 Reciting it before sleep protects a house from theft. — A report in al-Durr and al-Itqān tells of a thief unable to leave with belongings; al-Ālūsī notes a route through Nahshal and al-Ḍaḥḥāk whose excerpt is cut off, then gives the story of a migrant's protected house. [4 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ITQAN(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 17:110/q34 What recitation practice is reported for this sūra?  [* · reports]
- 17:110/q34/p1 The Prophet recited this sūra and al-Zumar nightly. — Elmalılı cites ʿĀʾisha's report. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 17:110/q35 How was this sūra valued among early revelations?  [* · sciences]
- 17:110/q35/p1 The Prophet called this and following sūras his earliest treasured possessions. — Elmalılı relays Ibn Masʿūd and glosses the saying as early Meccan revelation treasured in memory and devotion. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
