# 11:87: 29 questions

## 11:87/q01 What does their address to Shuayb convey?  [يَٰشُعَيْبُ · rhetoric]
- 11:87/q01/p1 A harsh and mocking rhetorical address. — Al-Biqai notes their use of his name instead of kinship; al-Tabatabai calls the question a shaped reproach. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; TABATABAI]

## 11:87/q02 What does 'your prayers' mean?  [أَصَلَوٰتُكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ṣalāt names his ritual act, religion, recitation, supplication, or place.
- 11:87/q02/p1 Shuayb's ritual prayer or worship. — Ibn Abbas and al-Ahnaf report his frequent prayer. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan favor prescribed prayer; Hasan says prayer and almsgiving were required of every prophet, but al-Alusi says this does not prove they were required of Shuayb's people. [17 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن)+; ALUSI-FULL+; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس)+; DURR-FULL(الأحنف)+; ELMALILI+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن,فرقة)+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; MAWARDI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن)+; RAZI-FULL+; TABRISI(ابن عباس)+; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)+]
- 11:87/q02/p2 His religion or faith, named for its prominent practice. — Ata, Hasan and Abu Muslim give this sense; al-Mawardi and al-Razi compare the following racehorse al-musalli. [12 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن,عطاء عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI(الحسن وعطاء وأبو مسلم); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطاء); RAZI-FULL; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî)]
- 11:87/q02/p3 His recitation. — Repeatedly transmitted from al-A'mash; Ibn Kathir calls it his Qur'an. [14 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الأعمش); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(الأعمش); IBNABIHATIM(الأعمش); MAWARDI-FULL(الأعمش); BAGHAWI-FULL(الأعمش); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأعمش); QURTUBI-FULL(سفيان عن الأعمش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الأعمش); DURR-FULL(الأعمش); ALUSI-FULL(الأعمش); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî)]
- 11:87/q02/p4 His supplications. — Ibn Atiyya favors this alongside ritual prayer; al-Alusi calls it a lexical sense. Al-Biqai traces prayer to connection with God. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(غيره)]
- 11:87/q02/p5 His mosques. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan report this possibility. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى)]

## 11:87/q03 Why speak of prayer as commanding?  [أَصَلَوٰتُكَ تَأْمُرُكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Hostile ridicule, figurative moral effect, or affirmative answer.
- 11:87/q03/p1 They ridicule his frequent devotion and its moral authority. — Several commentators say they winked and laughed as he prayed. Al-Nasafi and others cite 29:45, where prayer restrains indecency; al-Alusi reads ongoing command from the imperfect. Their taunt portrays the call as delusion. [26 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,الأحنف); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Some commentators)]
- 11:87/q03/p2 Prayer figuratively commands by urging right conduct. — Sahib al-Nazm, al-Zamakhshari and others invoke 29:45; prayer is part of faith that restrains wrongdoing. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:87/q03/p3 Prayer really did enjoin abandoning idols. — Al-Hasan answers the people's question affirmatively. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن)]

## 11:87/q04 How can prayer command Shuayb concerning their action?  [تَأْمُرُكَ أَن نَّتْرُكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether a complement is omitted or the awkward attribution is intentional.
- 11:87/q04/p1 An omitted term means it moves Shuayb to command or charge them. — Sahib al-Nazm supplies a repeated command; al-Zamakhshari, al-Baydawi and Abu Hayyan supply a charge because one person cannot perform another's act. Al-Alusi reports omitted taklif and preposition. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:87/q04/p2 Direct attribution to prayer is itself part of the taunt. — Al-Tabatabai locates mockery in assigning the command to prayer; al-Alusi considers a reading without ellipsis but objects to its contextual motivation. [26 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,الأحنف); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Some commentators)]

## 11:87/q05 What did their fathers worship and why cite them?  [مَا يَعْبُدُ ءَابَآؤُنَآ · interpretation]
- 11:87/q05/p1 Idols and images, defended by inherited custom. — Al-Zajjaj and Ibn Atiyya infer worship of other gods; al-Razi and al-Biqai call this blind ancestral imitation. [15 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 11:87/q06 Why is their fathers' worship in present tense?  [يَعْبُدُ · grammar]
- 11:87/q06/p1 It expresses dead ancestors' past practice in present form. — Al-Wahidi compares 2:102. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 11:87/q07 What governs the first 'that we leave'?  [أَن نَّتْرُكَ · grammar]
- 11:87/q07/p1 It is genitive after an implicit bi. — Al-Kisa'i's parsing is transmitted by al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi. [2 notes: NAHHAS(الكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي)]

## 11:87/q08 How does 'or that we do' attach?  [أَوْ أَن نَّفْعَلَ · grammar]
turns on: The verb persons, an omitted prohibition, and whether the received reading would command fraud.
- 11:87/q08/p1 It coordinates with what the fathers worship: leave worship and chosen wealth practices. — Al-Tabari's Basrans, al-Nahhas, al-Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, al-Samin and Ibn Hisham give this parse. Ibn Atiyya notes a shift in 'leave' or an omitted noun; al-Alusi rejects coordination with the first an for the received verbs, since it would make Shuayb command fraud. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض البصريين,بعض الكوفيين); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN(ابن هشام); ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:87/q08/p2 An omitted 'forbid' makes the second clause parallel to the first. — Al-Farra compares the implied opposite to heat implying cold at 16:81; al-Razi and al-Jishumi also give command and prohibition. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض الكوفيين); NAHHAS(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء)]
- 11:87/q08/p3 With second-person verbs, the second an can coordinate with the first. — Ibn Atiyya says the mixed reading must attach here; grammarians also allow attachment to ma for both ta verbs. Abu Hayyan and al-Samin note both alternatives. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض النحويين); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 11:87/q09 What force does 'or' have?  [أَوْ · grammar]
turns on: Combination of both duties versus variation over occasions.
- 11:87/q09/p1 It functions as 'and,' combining two obligations. — Al-Tabrisi compares 4:135; al-Bursevi joins monotheism with fair trade. [3 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 11:87/q09/p2 It varies demands made on different occasions. — Abu Hayyan and al-Samin present this option. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

turns on: Transmitted singular, plural, and lengthened forms.

- 11:87/q10/p3 With long ā in ṣalawāt. — Al-Zajjaj reports this variant. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]

## 11:87/q11 How are the two financial verbs read?  [نَّفْعَلَ نَشَٰٓؤُا۟ · readings]
turns on: Which persons the two imperfect verbs mark.
- 11:87/q11/p1 Both use first-person plural nun. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan call it the majority reading. [4 notes: JISHUMI(بعضهم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(زيد بن علي)]
- 11:87/q11/p2 Both use second-person ta. — Reported from al-Dahhak ibn Qays, Ibn Abi Abla, Zayd ibn Ali and others; al-Jishumi calls the construction apt while cautioning against reading both forms. ## 11:87/q12 What conduct with wealth are they defending?  [فِىٓ أَمْوَٰلِنَا مَا نَشَٰٓؤُا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the protest concerns commercial fraud, coinage, almsgiving, or unrestricted ownership.
- 11:87/q12/p1 Cheating in weights, measures, and other transactions. — Al-Zajjaj and Ibn Abbas mention mutual agreement to fraud; Muqatil and al-Nasafi contrast short and full measure. Al-Biqai adds gambling and ruined transactions. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 11:87/q12/p2 Clipping, breaking, debasing, or misusing coins. — Zayd ibn Aslam, Ibn Zayd and Muhammad ibn Ka'b supply forms of cutting. Ibn Zayd says they claimed liberty even to burn coins. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan describe passing clipped coins as sound; others include changing currency type. [28 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,زيد بن أسلم,محمد بن كعب القرظي); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); IBNABIHATIM(زيد بن أسلم); THALABI(أهل التفسير); MAWARDI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس عن عطاء، القرظي، زيد بن أسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب,محمد بن كعب وغيره); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد,القرظي); QURTUBI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن المسيب,محمد بن كعب); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد,زيد بن أسلم,سعيد بن المسيب,محمد بن كعب القرظي); BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(محمد بن كعب)]
- 11:87/q12/p3 Refusing zakat or almsgiving. — Sufyan al-Thawri gives this; al-Mawardi also names Zayd ibn Aslam. Al-Alusi says Hasan's general report does not establish this specific claim. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سفيان الثوري); MAWARDI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم وسفيان الثوري); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سفيان الثوري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الثوري); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن,سفيان الثوري)]
- 11:87/q12/p4 General liberty to do what they wish with owned property. — Qatada glosses it broadly; al-Tabatabai explains their assertion of owner control. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة)]

## 11:87/q13 What happened concerning their coins?  [أَمْوَٰلِنَا · reports]
- 11:87/q13/p1 Clipping circulating coins was corruption and caused punishment. — Muhammad ibn Ka'b connects their punishment to clipping; Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib calls clipping corruption. Qurtubi describes passing clipped and sound coins by differing units, while Zayd links 27:48's nine corrupt men to coin breaking. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي); QURTUBI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم,سعيد بن المسيب,عمر بن عبد العزيز,مالك); DURR-FULL(ربيعة بن أبي هلال,سعيد بن المسيب,محمد بن كعب القرظي)]
- 11:87/q13/p2 Authorities punished coin clippers. — Ibn al-Zubayr reportedly amputated; Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz whipped, shaved and paraded one, sparing his hand for lack of prior warning. [13 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,النبي,زيد بن أسلم,سعيد بن المسيب,عبد الرحمن بن القاسم,علماؤنا المالكية,عمر بن عبد العزيز,مالك)]

## 11:87/q14 What legal consequences follow coin clipping?  [أَمْوَٰلِنَا · law]
turns on: Public harm, major sin, and analogy to theft.
- 11:87/q14/p1 It is grave public harm, defeats probity, and warrants discipline. — The Prophet forbade breaking valid Muslim coinage except necessity. Ibn al-Arabi distinguishes clipping value from damaging form; Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Qasim denies a clipper credible testimony, though truthful or obscure excuses can count. [13 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,النبي,زيد بن أسلم,سعيد بن المسيب,عبد الرحمن بن القاسم,علماؤنا المالكية,عمر بن عبد العزيز,مالك)]
- 11:87/q14/p2 Amputation can apply. — Ibn al-Arabi says he imposed it as judge; Ibn al-Zubayr reportedly did likewise. Umar's analogy treats clipping as theft because coin value is protected. [13 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,النبي,زيد بن أسلم,سعيد بن المسيب,عبد الرحمن بن القاسم,علماؤنا المالكية,عمر بن عبد العزيز,مالك)]

## 11:87/q15 What claim about freedom and property is exposed?  [أَمْوَٰلِنَا · interpretation]
- 11:87/q15/p1 Ownership gives no license to wrong others; fair dealing secures everyone's rights. — Elmalili distinguishes disposing of one's own goods from injuring others; Kuran Yolu links life and property security with freedom. Asad mentions the poor's needs; Qushayri favors a little lawful gain over much ruinous gain, citing 24:15. [11 notes: QUSHAYRI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 11:87/q16 What do the closing words mean as speech?  [إِنَّكَ لَأَنتَ ٱلْحَلِيمُ ٱلرَّشِيدُ · interpretation]

- 11:87/q16/p2 Sincere recognition of his wisdom, sharpened into reproach. — Ibn Kaysan compares Salih's people's words at 11:62. Al-Razi, Qurtubi and al-Tabatabai favor variants of this: a wise man should not divide the people or restrict their property. Al-Tabatabai rejects ironic inversion. - 11:87/q16/p3 He is wise only according to his own claim. — The phrase may imply 'as you claim'; al-Kawashi says Shuayb merely pretends to the virtues. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(الكواشي); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN(Q)]

## 11:87/q17 What do 'forbearing' and 'right-minded' mean?  [ٱلْحَلِيمُ ٱلرَّشِيدُ · meaning]
- 11:87/q17/p1 Patient under provocation and sound in guidance or financial judgment. — Al-Tabari describes calm restraint; al-Tabrisi says delay of deserved punishment and guiding. Ibn Ashur gives rational for halim and prudent in money for rashid. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 11:87/q18 Why this emphatic ending?  [إِنَّكَ لَأَنتَ ٱلْحَلِيمُ ٱلرَّشِيدُ · rhetoric]
- 11:87/q18/p1 Emphasis sharpens the taunt, and the virtues fit worship and wealth. — Ibn Ashur counts inna, oath lam and la-anta; al-Burhan and al-Itqan say halim fits worship and rashid financial conduct, a prepared ending. [3 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]

- 11:87/q19/p1 He responds with a gentle, persuasive appeal. — Abu Hayyan calls it drawing in the addressee and reports the Prophet's title 'orator of the prophets'; al-Alusi says 11:88 works with taunting speech and ordinary prayer. - 11:87/q20/p1 It answers the summons to monotheism and fair measure. — Al-Razi pairs each objection with one prior demand; Abu Hayyan cites 11:84–85. Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi compare a report of Banu Qurayza responding to the Prophet that they had not known him ignorant. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 11:87/q21 How is 'we wish' written?  [نَشَٰٓؤُا۟ · readings]
