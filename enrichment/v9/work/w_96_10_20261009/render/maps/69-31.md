# 69:31: 14 questions

## 69:31/q01 What does ثُمَّ signal between the punishments?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
- 69:31/q01/p1 It marks an increase in the severity or terror of successive punishments, without requiring a delay between them. — Al-Kashshāf and Bursawī explicitly contrast escalation with elapsed time; Bursawī says the threatening scene allows no pause. Al-Bayḍāwī says the punishments differ in severity. Al-Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr specify that entry into the Fire is harsher than seizure and shackling. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:31/q02 How can ثُمَّ and the following ف connect the commands?  [ثُمَّ · grammar]
- 69:31/q02/p1 Unspoken successive orders to the wardens allow ثُمَّ to link the orders while ف marks their immediate execution. — Ibn al-Shaykh, as reported by Bursawī, supplies the omitted commands to avoid coordinating both particles on a single clause. [1 notes: BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ)]

## 69:31/q03 What is ٱلْجَحِيمَ here?  [ٱلْجَحِيمَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether al-Jaḥīm denotes Hell's great fire generally or a specific level or gate; the notes supply no argument deciding its precise location.
- 69:31/q03/p1 Hell as an immense, fiercely blazing fire, described by some as its greatest or most severe fire. — Al-Jishumī calls it a name for Hell and a great fire, near in sense to al-saʿīr and al-waqūd. Al-Rāzī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı stress its immensity or fierce blaze; al-Biqāʿī says it overwhelms the onlooker. Al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and Elmalılı call it the greatest fire or most severe layer. [8 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 69:31/q03/p2 It names a particular level of Hell. — Al-Ālūsī reports this as a view of some authorities; Elmalılı identifies the Jahīm as Hell's most severe layer. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI]
- 69:31/q03/p3 It is Hell's sixth gate, where he is to be roasted. — Muqātil specifies the sixth gate. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 69:31/q04 Why is this fierce fire assigned to the condemned person?  [ٱلْجَحِيمَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the rationale is the condemned person's disbelief generally or an arrogant ruler's conduct specifically; al-Ālūsī treats the latter as a possible subset, while Abū Ḥayyān disputes the ruler limitation.
- 69:31/q04/p1 It fits his arrogance and oppressive conduct toward other people, described by some as the conduct of a ruler. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī and Bursawī connect the greatest fire with his self-exaltation. Al-Biqāʿī adds his effort to burn advisers' hearts with words and his exaltation over the weak. Saʿdī al-Muftī, reported by Bursawī, limits this rationale to the arrogant. Al-Ālūsī reports the ruler explanation and replies that it may describe a subset of those given the book in the left hand: Qatāda's report does not exclude that, just as 107:3 concerns a subset. Abū Ḥayyān rejects the kingly-status explanation as weak and says Ibn ʿAbbās's proof-reading fits all the damned, not kings alone. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(سعدى المفتي); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 69:31/q04/p2 It is fitting because of his grave disbelief in God. — Al-Ālūsī gives disbelief as the grave sin behind this punishment, while allowing that an arrogant ruler may be among the condemned subset. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]

## 69:31/q05 What does putting ٱلْجَحِيمَ before صَلُّوهُ achieve?  [ٱلْجَحِيمَ صَلُّوهُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether fronting establishes restriction, serves the verse endings, emphasizes the torment, or brings the frightening object forward; Abū Ḥayyān challenges the grammatical basis for restriction, while al-Samīn says grammar permits it.
- 69:31/q05/p1 It restricts the destination or burning to al-Jaḥīm alone. — Al-Zamakhsharī's reading is reported by Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn; al-Kashshāf, Bursawī and Elmalılı give the same exclusive sense. Al-Bayḍāwī speaks of particularity. Al-Burhān reports exclusivity as one explanation. Abū Ḥayyān objects that such restriction from fronting is neither Sībawayh's nor expert grammarians' doctrine. Al-Samīn reports that objection from al-Shaykh but says grammar does not rule out al-Zamakhsharī's analysis. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURHAN(بعضهم); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 69:31/q05/p2 It preserves the aligned endings and cadence of the verses. — Al-Burhān says the unfronted wording would convey the sense but lose the joined cadence; verse-ending alignment can motivate multiple frontings, with the stronger motive governing when motives conflict. Ibn ʿĀshūr also gives rhyme as a reason for fronting the Fire and then the chain. [3 notes: BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 69:31/q05/p3 It singles out the Fire for emphasis and draws attention to this kind of torment. — Al-Bayḍāwī says fronting the Fire and then the chain signals particularity and draws attention to the kinds of punishment. Al-Burhān reports emphasis as another explanation of the order. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURHAN(بعضهم)]
- 69:31/q05/p4 It hastens the hearer's distress. — Ibn ʿĀshūr applies this to fronting both Hell here and the chain in the next command; he also mentions rhyme. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:31/q06 How is ٱلْجَحِيمَ governed in the accusative?  [ٱلْجَحِيمَ · grammar]
- 69:31/q06/p1 An implied verb governs it, and the following صَلُّوهُ explains that verb. — Al-Nasafī gives this as an alternative syntactic analysis. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 69:31/q07 What does صَلُّوهُ command the wardens to do?  [صَلُّوهُ · interpretation]
turns on: The explanations emphasize entry, the burning suffered there, or continued confinement; the notes do not present these as mutually exclusive.
- 69:31/q07/p1 Put or send him into Hell, immersing him in its fire. — Al-Kalbī, al-Mubarrad, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Kashshāf, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Zajjāj, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Kathīr, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Asad express entry, casting or immersion. Ibn Kathīr describes forcible transfer from the gathering after shackling. Al-Jishumī adds that he is made to remain there. [15 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,المبرد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(المبرد); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 69:31/q07/p2 Roast or burn him in Hell, making him suffer its blazing fire. — Muqātil locates the roasting at the sixth gate. Al-Kashshāf and al-Bayḍāwī specify the greatest Fire; al-Qurṭubī says he suffers in the blaze. Al-Biqāʿī likens the repeated, intense immersion to roasting a sheep again and again. Al-Ālūsī speaks of fierce blazing, and Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the verb as suffering the Fire's burning. [9 notes: MUQATIL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 69:31/q07/p3 Make him remain in the Fire, bound to it. — Al-Jishumī defines taṣliya as making someone stay in the fire and glosses this command as putting him in and making him remain. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise joins entry into the tremendous Fire with remaining bound to it. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABATABAI]

## 69:31/q08 What root senses help explain صَلُّوهُ?  [صَلُّوهُ · meaning]
- 69:31/q08/p1 Its root evokes contact with fire, including warming oneself; it can also evoke following someone closely. — Al-Jishumī gives warming oneself and following a predecessor closely as related root uses alongside making someone stay in fire. Ibn ʿĀshūr gives suffering the Fire's burning or warming oneself by it. [2 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:31/q09 Which transitive verb forms express the action of صَلُّوهُ?  [صَلُّوهُ · grammar]
- 69:31/q09/p1 Both aṣlā and ṣallā can take an object and express sending or exposing someone to fire. — Al-Mubarrad, reported by al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī, compares aṣlaytuhu and ṣallaytuhu to akramtuhu and karramtuhu; al-Rāzī specifies exposure or bringing to fire. Al-Kashshāf cites ṣalā al-nār and ṣalāt al-nār for putting someone into fire. Ibn ʿĀshūr gives aṣlāhu nāran and ṣallāhu nāran as transitive forms. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(المبرد); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:31/q10 What happens to the wāw in the form صَلُّوهُ?  [صَلُّوهُ · grammar]
- 69:31/q10/p1 The original wāw can be retained; its deletion is explained by its weak boundary with the following hāʾ. — Al-Naḥḥās reports the retention and explains the deletion through the adjacent wāw and hāʾ. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 69:31/q11 Who receives the command صَلُّوهُ?  [صَلُّوهُ · referent]
- 69:31/q11/p1 The angels or keepers who govern Hell receive God's command. — Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn Kathīr address the sequence of seizure, shackling and casting to the infernal guards. Elmalılı presents God's orders as an implied utterance, as though addressed to the wealth and power the condemned man found useless. Kur'an Yolu names the angels and follows their handling of the left-handed person into Hell and the chain. The Study Quran, citing al-Qurṭubī and al-Ṭabarī, identifies the angels who govern Hell. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ)]

## 69:31/q12 How does this verse continue the surrounding scene?  [* · links]
- 69:31/q12/p1 After the account of the left-handed person's book, the orders move from seizure and shackling to Hell, then to binding him in a chain. — Al-Jishumī says the two groups described through their books lead to a warning of punishment for sinners. Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn Kathīr detail seizure, a neck collar or shackling, and transfer to Hell; Ibn Kathīr specifies forcible seizure from the gathering. Asad places entry into Hell after shackling. Kur'an Yolu adds hands bound to neck and the criminals' long chain. [5 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 69:31/q13 How does the clause fit the passage's cadence?  [* · rhetoric]
- 69:31/q13/p1 The command illustrates a cadence in which balanced wording is followed by a longer third clause. — Al-Burhān contrasts a longer second clause, exemplified by mā ḍalla ṣāḥibukum wa-mā ghawā, with the longer third clause in the commands to take and bind the condemned man; the note mentions 69:30–31. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 69:31/q14 What inward meaning is given to this punishment?  [* · inward]
- 69:31/q14/p1 It is deprivation's inferno, with suffering through an unending chain of events and varied torments. — Al-Qāshānī gives this inward account of the next punishment. [1 notes: QASHANI]
