# 96:10/q05 What happened around this act of forbidding prayer? [إِذَا صَلَّىٰٓ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in the attempted act and what Abu Jahl perceived; some describe verbal confrontation, others a thwarted physical approach.
- 96:10/q05/p1 Abu Jahl threatened to trample Muhammad at prayer and recoiled from a fiery trench, terror, and wings; angels would have seized him.
  reasons: Abu Hurayra supplies the full threat, retreat and angelic warning; Abu Jahl swears by al-Lat and al-Uzza in Mawardi, Alusi and Elmalili. Baghawi and Ibn Kathir note uncertainty whether the revelation notice belongs to Abu Hurayra's report. Mawardi cites Tirmidhi's grade hasan sahih gharib; Ibn Kathir lists Ahmad, Muslim, Nasa'i and Ibn Abi Hatim through Mu'tamir. Some accounts say neck and face; Bursevi says Abu Jahl saw wings but not angels' persons. Variants place him at the Maqam or Kaaba, and describe his attempted action as approach, threat, or physical harassment.

# 96:10/q06 Was the prayer a first congregation, and who attended? [إِذَا صَلَّىٰٓ · reports]
turns on: The attendance list differs, and Alusi questions Abu Talib's presence because of the date of obligatory prayer.
- 96:10/q06/p1 It may have been Islam's first congregational prayer, with Muhammad and Ali; Abu Talib brought Jaʿfar to support them.
  reasons: Jaʿfar ibn Muhammad's account in Mawardi has Muhammad praying with Ali; Abu Talib passes with Jaʿfar, urges him to join, leaves rejoicing, and composes verses praising Ali and Jaʿfar's loyalty.
  holders (4 notes): MAWARDI(أبو طالب، جعفر بن محمد)
  notes: MAWARDI:96:10/r5 MAWARDI:96:10/r6 MAWARDI:96:9/r5 MAWARDI:96:9/r6
- 96:10/q06/p2 It was Islam's first congregation, attended by Muhammad, Abu Bakr, Ali, and other early believers; Abu Talib and Jaʿfar passed by.
  reasons: The Abu Hayyan, Alusi, and Tabrizi accounts add Abu Bakr and sometimes other early Muslims. Abu Talib urges support or Jaʿfar's joining and recites verses vowing not to abandon the Prophet; versions differ as to who rejoiced.

# 96:10/q07 What is the force and form of forbidding in this verse? [عَبْدًا إِذَا صَلَّىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the narrated threat itself or explicit speech constitutes the prohibition.
- 96:10/q07/p1 The forbider attempts to stop prayer, including an explicit verbal prohibition.
  reasons: Jishumi glosses forbidding as refraining from an act; Alusi says the wording suggests Abu Jahl had power only to forbid, while Ibn Abbas's authenticated report has him explicitly say he had forbidden the Prophet. Ibn Ashur says the prohibited act is omitted because prayer supplies it.
  holders (9 notes): JISHUMI; ALUSI(ابن عباس، قيل); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES
  notes: JISHUMI:v10p7479#2/r1 ALUSI:96:10/r17 ALUSI:96:10/r18 ALUSI:96:11/r10 ALUSI:96:11/r11 ALUSI-FULL:v30p183/r7 IBNASHUR:96:10/r2 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p446/r6 ASAD-NOTES:96:9#n5/r1
- 96:10/q07/p2 The forbidding is a threat meant to reach Muhammad, even without face-to-face prohibition.
  reasons: Ibn Ashur describes Abu Jahl's threatened trampling at the Kaaba as a communicated threat rather than direct speech.

# 96:10/q08 What does the phrase 'when he prays' add? [إِذَا صَلَّىٰٓ · rhetoric]
- 96:10/q08/p1 It locates the prohibition precisely while the servant is engaged in prayer.
  reasons: Alusi says the temporal restriction suggests interruption of an ongoing prayer.
  holders (1 notes): ALUSI
  notes: ALUSI:96:10/r30
- 96:10/q08/p2 It emphasizes the evil of obstructing the servant's highest act of worship.
  reasons: Biqai says prayer joins nearness to God and withdrawal from creation, giving God His due; obstructing it can arise from envy or rebellion. Islahi calls prayer a person's foremost duty to God.
  holders (3 notes): BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR
