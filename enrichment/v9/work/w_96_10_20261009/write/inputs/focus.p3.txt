# 96:10/q15 How is the repeated 'have you seen?' construction parsed? [* · grammar]
turns on: Whether 'seeing' is visual or cognitive, whether a second object is required, and whether a conditional interrogative answer requires fa.
- 96:10/q15/p1 The first relative clause is an object, with later objects and final interrogative supplied by ordinary ellipsis.
  reasons: Abu Hayyan makes the relative clause the first object's content, recovers omitted later objects from the servant or forbider, and says omission of clauses is not syntactic competition. Alusi similarly recovers pronouns or demonstratives and the final 'Does he not know?' as second object, calling it semantic government rather than tanazuʿ. Abu Hayyan argues the second object must be interrogative, citing 53:33–35, 19:77–78 and 56:58–59.
  holders (9 notes): ABUHAYYAN; ALUSI; BINTSHATI; ABUHAYYAN+; ALUSI+; ALUSI-
  notes: ABUHAYYAN:96:10/r14 ABUHAYYAN:96:10/r15 ABUHAYYAN:96:10/r16 ABUHAYYAN:96:9/r14 ABUHAYYAN:96:9/r15 ABUHAYYAN:96:9/r16 ALUSI:96:10/r21 ALUSI:96:10/r22 BINTSHATI:v2p28/r1 | against: ALUSI:96:10/r46
- 96:10/q15/p2 The relative and conditional clauses fill two objects, one repeated question is emphatic, and a later clause supplies the conditional answer.
  reasons: Zamakhshari offers this syntax; Bint Shati reports his treatment of the verse 13 conditional as the second object and verse 14 as answer. Abu Hayyan and Bint Shati object that an interrogative conditional answer requires fa except in poetic necessity; Alusi rejects the claim that the second object must always be interrogative, citing Sibawayh's reported view.
  holders (4 notes): ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); BINTSHATI(الزمخشري); ALUSI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-; BINTSHATI-
  notes: ABUHAYYAN:96:10/r13 ABUHAYYAN:96:9/r13 BINTSHATI:v2p27/r4 ALUSI:96:10/r40 | against: ABUHAYYAN:96:10/r14 ABUHAYYAN:96:10/r17 ABUHAYYAN:96:9/r14 ABUHAYYAN:96:9/r17 BINTSHATI:v2p27/r5
- 96:10/q15/p3 Interrogative 'have you seen?' need not have an express or inferred second object.
  reasons: Bint Shati cites Meccan examples at 107:1, 19:77, 25:43, 45:23 and 53:33: the question directs attention to a lesson or warning. She urges attention to Quranic usage even when school grammar would force a conjectural object.
  holders (3 notes): BINTSHATI
  notes: BINTSHATI:v2p28/r3 BINTSHATI:v2p29/r2 BINTSHATI:v2p30/r3
- 96:10/q15/p4 The first seeing is visual and takes no second object, while the later two are inward and take conditional objects.
  reasons: Alusi reports this alternate analysis; another reported view takes the opening and verse 13 as inward seeing. Ibn Atiyya calls the opening an interrogative challenge rather than two-object knowledge vision.

# 96:10/q16 How does this scene fit the sura's revelation sequence and earlier verses? [* · links]
turns on: The dating of the Night Journey and what kinds of prayer existed before the five daily prayers.
- 96:10/q16/p1 The Abu Jahl episode follows the opening revelation, while the preceding account of human transgression prepares for it.
  reasons: Ibn Ashur says a short pause followed the first verses and that adjacent recited verses normally preserve revelation order absent clear proof otherwise. He sees 96:6–8 as preparation for the Abu Jahl scene at 96:9. Asad places the later passage at least a year after 96:1–5; Bint Shati says later public preaching and prayer suit the episode.
  holders (5 notes): IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; BINTSHATI; IBNASHUR+
  notes: IBNASHUR:96:6/r12 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p442/r4 ASAD-NOTES:96:9#n5/r1 BINTSHATI:v2p27/r1 BINTSHATI:v2p27/r3
- 96:10/q16/p2 The prayer reference creates a chronological difficulty for immediate revelation after the first five verses.
  reasons: Some reason from the later Night Journey obligation of five prayers to an intervening revelation or to denying that this sura was first. Bint Shati says the Abu Jahl occasion presupposes public preaching and threats. Ibn Ashur reports both alternatives without deciding for them.
  holders (3 notes): IBNASHUR(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI
  notes: IBNASHUR:96:6/r5 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p442/r5 BINTSHATI:v2p26/r6
- 96:10/q16/p3 Earlier non-five-daily prayer could explain the episode before the Night Journey.
  reasons: Elmalili objects to Alusi's chronology: the Night Journey established five daily prayers, but a night vigil or noon prayer could have been prescribed earlier. Ibn Ashur prefers an unregulated earlier prayer including prostration; al-Harbi and Yahya ibn Sallam describe prayer before sunset and sunrise.

- 96:10/q16/p4 Abu Talib's presence in a purported first congregation conflicts with common Night Journey and death chronologies.
  reasons: Alusi says obligatory prayer began on the Night Journey, usually placed one year before Hijra, while Abu Talib died roughly three years before it, three or five days before Khadija. Ibn Hazm claims consensus at one year; Ibn Faris gives one year three months and al-Suddi one year five months. Al-Zuhri instead dates it five years after the mission, favored by Nawawi and Qurtubi but contested. Elmalili rejects the inference that no earlier prayer existed.
