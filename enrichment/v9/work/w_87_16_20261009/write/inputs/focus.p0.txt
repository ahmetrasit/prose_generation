# 87:16/q01 How does بل redirect the discourse here? [بَلْ · links]
turns on: Whether bal cancels an implied conclusion or moves to a new explanation or address, and how the second- or third-person reading relates to the preceding verses.
- 87:16/q01/p1 It overturns the implied expectation that people will pursue the purification and success just described.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr connect it to 87:14: instead of following the path to success, people prefer the world. Elmalılı similarly contrasts worldly indulgence with purification, charity, remembrance, and prayer.
  holders (5 notes): ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; ELMALILI
  notes: ALUSI:87:16/r1 ALUSI-FULL:v30p110/r1 IBNASHUR:87:16/r9 ELMALILI:87:16-17/r1 ELMALILI:87:16-17/r2
- 87:16/q01/p2 It moves from the contrast between those who heed or avoid the reminder to why the rebellious avoid it.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative. Ibn ʿĀshūr says a clause after bal may change topic without cancelling the previous claim; this transition is especially clear with Abū ʿAmr's third-person reading. He cites 23:62–63 for such non-cancelling movement.
  holders (4 notes): ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR
  notes: ALUSI:87:16/r2 ALUSI-FULL:v30p110/r2 IBNASHUR:87:16/r7 IBNASHUR:87:16/r6
- 87:16/q01/p3 It turns from narration to direct rebuke, renewing the hearer's attention.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr ties this to the majority second-person reading. Biqāʿī says the shift signals extreme anger. Al-Ālūsī says it heightens rebuke of unbelievers, and also admonishes Muslims if the address is general. Corpus Coranicum calls the turn abrupt and effective, confronting hearers with a choice and its consequences.
  holders (6 notes): IBNASHUR; BIQAI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM
  notes: IBNASHUR:87:16/r8 BIQAI:87:16/r1 ALUSI:87:16/r5 ALUSI-FULL:v30p110/r5 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:16-19:kommentar/r1 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:16-19:kommentar/r2
- 87:16/q01/p4 In general, بل redirects discourse; between clauses it may cancel the previous assertion or merely begin another report.
  reasons: Al-Burhān distinguishes sentence-to-sentence transition from cancellation after an affirmative followed by a single word; al-Mubarrad also allows transfer after a negative or prohibition. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes the two clause uses by context, citing 23:70 for cancellation and 23:62–63 for a simple move.
  holders (7 notes): BURHAN(المبرد); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: BURHAN:v4p259/r1 BURHAN:v4p259/r2 BURHAN:v4p259/r3 IBNASHUR:87:16/r3 IBNASHUR:87:16/r4 IBNASHUR:87:16/r5 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p288/r5

# 87:16/q02 How is the lām of بل pronounced before تُؤْثِرُونَ? [بَلْ تُؤْثِرُونَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the adjacent lām and tāʾ are assimilated or kept distinct.
- 87:16/q02/p1 The lām is assimilated into the following tāʾ.
  reasons: Sībawayh records the resulting batūthirūna. Al-Naḥḥās permits the assimilation; Ibn Khālawayh acknowledges assimilated articulation.
  holders (3 notes): SIBAWAYH; NAHHAS; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA

- 87:16/q02/p2 The lām is pronounced distinctly.
  reasons: Ibn Khālawayh acknowledges clear as well as assimilated articulation, with arguments given earlier.
  holders (1 notes): IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA
  notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p369/r2

# 87:16/q03 Which person is read in تُؤْثِرُونَ? [تُؤْثِرُونَ · readings]
turns on: The tāʾ of direct address versus the yāʾ that resumes third-person reference to the wretched.
- 87:16/q03/p1 Second-person plural: 'you prefer.'
  reasons: Al-Farrāʾ describes agreement; al-Ṭabarī calls this the authoritative or majority reading and prefers it, as does al-Zajjāj. Ubayy's expanded 'you' supports it. Ibn Jawzī reports al-Farrāʾ and al-Zajjāj preferring it. Biqāʿī says the direct address marks anger. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it abruptly addresses polytheists. Other reports call it the majority or the reading of the other reciters.
