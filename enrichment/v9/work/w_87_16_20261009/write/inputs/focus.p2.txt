# 87:16/q08 Why do people choose ٱلْحَيَوٰةَ ٱلدُّنْيَا? [ٱلْحَيَوٰةَ ٱلدُّنْيَا · interpretation]
turns on: The sources point to immediacy and sense perception, denial of the next life, or desire and differing spiritual aspiration.
- 87:16/q08/p1 Its pleasures are immediate and visible, while the Hereafter is unseen and deferred.
  reasons: Ibn Masʿūd names adornment, food, drink, women and pleasure; al-Ṭabarī and later transmitters report his explanation. Abū Mūsā says sight of the Hereafter would remove hesitation. Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds love of what is near and ignorance of what lasts. Biqāʿī calls sense-bound creatures captivated by immediacy.

# 87:16/q10 What conduct does the verse commend or condemn? [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether worldly pursuit actually displaces salvation, and whether the point is renunciation, moral correction, or a conditional warning.
- 87:16/q10/p1 Worldly attachment diverts people from remembrance, purification and the deeds that bring success.
  reasons: Al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī connect preference with neglect of success. Biqāʿī says appetite and wealth consume time and lead to evil deeds. Quṭb calls worldly preference the root of neglect and urges accounting for the afterlife.
  holders (11 notes): KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QASHANI; QUTB-ZILAL
  notes: KASHSHAF:87:16/r1 KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p742/r7 BAYDAWI:87:16/r1 BAYDAWI-FULL:87:13-18/r5 NASAFI:87:16/r1 NASAFI-FULL:v4p333#5/r1 BIQAI:87:16/r3 BIQAI-FULL:v8p401/r1 BIQAI-FULL:v8p401/r2 QASHANI:87:16-18/r1 QUTB-ZILAL:87:16/r1
- 87:16/q10/p2 Worldly benefits are permissible when pursuit of them does not neglect the means of salvation.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 28:77 and says blame tracks neglect of one's afterlife responsibilities under the law.
  holders (1 notes): IBNASHUR
  notes: IBNASHUR:87:16/r14
- 87:16/q10/p3 The believer should renounce worldly dependence and seek God.
  reasons: Al-Tustarī likens the believer to a person on a log at sea pleading for rescue; he says God sends a wise angel to plant wisdom in the heart of a renouncer. Al-Dīnawarī ranks choosing the Lord above both worlds as the noblest state.
  holders (3 notes): TUSTARI; SULAMI(أبو العباس الدينوري)
  notes: TUSTARI:87:16/r1 TUSTARI:87:16/r2 SULAMI:v2p390#4/r1
- 87:16/q10/p4 The reproach is also a stage in the sūra's moral treatment, purging vices and urging lasting good.
  reasons: Biqāʿī calls it a fourth treatment and says the pairing deters from the base world while attracting to the enduring Hereafter. Elmalılı contrasts festival pleasures with charity and prayer.
  holders (3 notes): BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI
  notes: BIQAI:87:16/r4 BIQAI-FULL:v8p401/r4 ELMALILI:87:16-17/r2
