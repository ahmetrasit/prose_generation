# 87:16/q12 How does this verse connect to its surrounding passage and the sūra? [* · links]
turns on: Whether the emphasis falls on the immediate contrast of 87:14–17, the transition to earlier scriptures, or the sūra's broader address and mission.
- 87:16/q12/p1 It contrasts the earlier call to purification and prayer with preference for the world, then the better and lasting Hereafter.
  reasons: Al-Ṭabarī takes 'this' in 87:18–19 to refer to nearby 87:14–17, including this verse, because the demonstrative points to the nearest passage; ṣuḥuf means Abraham's and Moses' books or scrolls. Biqāʿī describes an implicit contrast: rejecting the Hereafter follows from preferring the world, and 87:17 implies the world's inferiority. Corpus Coranicum points to the vocabulary of 93:4.
  holders (4 notes): TAB; TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM
  notes: TAB:87:19/r6 TAB-FULL:v24p323/r6 BIQAI-FULL:v8p401/r4 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:16-17:anmerkung/r1
- 87:16/q12/p2 The worldly rebuke prepares a warning against abandoning purification outside the prophets' way.
  reasons: Biqāʿī reads the subsequent development as warning against turning from purification by a path other than the prophets' method.
  holders (1 notes): BIQAI-FULL
  notes: BIQAI-FULL:v8p404#2/r1
- 87:16/q12/p3 The sūra shifts from addressing the Messenger to confronting hearers with the core choice and its consequences.
  reasons: Corpus Coranicum links the abrupt rebuke with 102:1 and says an earlier contrast of good and bad now becomes a direct choice. It argues second-person rebukes and parallel growth of text and community make a presupposed formed community or follower-only devotional meetings unlikely.
  holders (3 notes): CORPUSCORANICUM
  notes: CORPUSCORANICUM:87:16-19:kommentar/r1 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:16-19:kommentar/r2 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:9-15:kommentar/r3
- 87:16/q12/p4 The sūra frames worldly preference within its themes of revelation, divine oneness and final recompense.
  reasons: Quṭb calls al-Aʿlā Meccan with nineteen verses, loved and recited by the Prophet at Eid and Friday prayer alongside al-Ghāshiya, sometimes the same day. He describes cosmic praise, promised preservation and ease for the Messenger, earlier scriptures in 87:18–19, and the easy nature of the message and community.
  holders (3 notes): QUTB-ZILAL
