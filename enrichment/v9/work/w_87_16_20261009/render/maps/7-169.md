# 7:169: 30 questions

## 7:169/q01 Who are the later successors?  [فَخَلَفَ مِنۢ بَعْدِهِمْ خَلْفٌۭ · referent]
turns on: Whether Israelite context limits the group to Jews or extends to other communities.
- 7:169/q01/p1 Later Jewish descendants, particularly Jews of the Prophet’s era. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers Jews because the surrounding passage concerns Israel and no Christian name appears. Al-Ālūsī favors contemporaries of the Prophet; Ibn ʿAbbās calls them descendants of the dispersed, and Kuran Yolu rejects a Moses-era setting. Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports a sixty-year interval; al-Wāḥidī calls them children of those dispersed. Al-Qurṭubī also reports a specific Yathrib identification. [23 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن سعيد الخدري); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,ابن عباس عن عطاء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(المفسرون,قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وابن زيد,الزمخشري,الطبري); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî,Zemahşerî); STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+]
- 7:169/q01/p2 Christians succeeding Jews. — Mujāhid gives this identification in several transmissions. Al-Māwardī connects it with Christians possessing the Gospel; al-Ṭabarī and al-Ālūsī object from the Israelite context. [10 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bazı rivayetler); TAB-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد)-; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî)-]
- 7:169/q01/p3 Members or counterparts of Muhammad’s community. — Mujāhid is also reported for this community. Al-Suddī includes the Murjiʾa as counterparts; another report likens successive people to heedless cattle. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي,مجاهد,مجاهر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 7:169/q01/p4 Jews and Christians. — Qatāda names Jews and Christians together. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة)]
- 7:169/q01/p5 Jews who returned from exile under Cyrus. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reads the preceding dispersion as exile, places the return under Cyrus about 530 BCE, mentions Nehemiah and Ezra’s scroll, and says the first returnees included prophets and righteous people. His other reading includes later Medina Jews. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:169/q02 What does خَلْفٌ mean, and how do its vowels affect praise or blame?  [خَلْفٌۭ · meaning]
turns on: Whether sukūn itself signals blame and whether the two forms exchange meanings.
- 7:169/q02/p1 Both forms can describe bad successors; open lām more often describes good ones. — Al-Naḍr and Basrans allow both vowels for evil but usually open lām for good; al-Ṭabarī notes exceptions. Ḥassān supports good still-lām, Labīd bad still-lām. Abū ʿUbayda and al-Farrāʾ allow overlap, while al-Ālūsī reports reversed assignments. [70 notes: THALABI(النضر بن شميل,محمد بن جرير الطبري); WAHIDI-BASIT(أحمد بن يحيى,ابن السكيت,الأزهري عن الفراء,النضر,حسان); JISHUMI(ابن الأعرابي,الفراء,حسان,علي بن عيسى,لبيد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن الأعرابي,النضر بن شميل,محمد بن جرير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عبيدة والزجاج,حسان,لبيد); TABRISI(الزجاج,الفراء,حسان,علي بن عيسى,لبيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة,ابن الأنباري,ابن قتيبة,الجوني والجحدري,قوم); RAZI-FULL(أحمد بن يحيى,الزجاج,لبيد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الأعرابي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,لبيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الأنباري,ابن السكيت,الزجاج,الفراء,النضر بن شميل,ثعلب); SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج,الفراء,النضر,ثعلب,حسان); BURSEVI(ابن الأعرابي,محمد بن جرير); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حاتم,البصريون,حسان,لبيد); IBNASHUR-FULL(البصريون,النضر بن شميل,كثير من أهل اللغة)]
- 7:169/q02/p2 The still-lām form commonly means a bad successor. — Ibn Qutayba uses it for inferior people or speech. Labīd’s scabbed-skin verse and a pejorative proverb support blame; al-Māwardī and Ibn al-Anbārī prefer the common vowel distinction. [14 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري)+; MAWARDI-FULL+]
- 7:169/q02/p3 It means a succeeding generation or replacement without inherent blame. — Al-Farrāʾ calls it a following generation; al-Zajjāj allows replacement of a loss. Ibn ʿĀshūr derives succession from what is behind and denies inherent evil. Ḥassān’s line praises still-lām successors. [10 notes: FARRA; AKHFASH; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:169/q02/p4 Still-lām means offspring; open-lām means a replacement, child or stranger. — Abū Ḥātim distinguishes offspring, singular or plural, from a replacement that may be a child or stranger. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو حاتم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حاتم)]
- 7:169/q02/p5 The pejorative sense may derive from corruption or changed smell. — Al-Ṭabarī proposes sour milk or fasting breath; Arabic also uses the root for spoiled wine, bad speech, and changed mouth odor, say al-Wāḥidī’s grammarians. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أصحاب العربية); SAMIN-DURR]

## 7:169/q03 How does singular خَلْفٌ describe plural heirs?  [خَلْفٌۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the noun is collective or an adjectival verbal noun.
- 7:169/q03/p1 It is an adjectival verbal noun usable of one or many. — Ibn al-Anbārī says a verbal noun can cover two or more and any gender; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Bursevī use this to explain the plural verb. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن الأنباري); BURSEVI]
- 7:169/q03/p2 It is a collective noun, like ركب for riders. — Ibn al-Anbārī compares خالف/خلف with راكب/ركب and invokes 19:59; Abū Ḥayyān objects that خلف behaves as a singular, not a collective. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); SAMIN-DURR(ابن الأنباري)]

## 7:169/q04 What book did they inherit, and what does inheritance mean?  [وَرِثُوا۟ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · referent]
turns on: The book varies with the successors identified.
- 7:169/q04/p1 They inherited the Torah and its knowledge and duties from predecessors. — Most exegetes say Torah, inherited from fathers with its commands, prohibitions, study and covenant. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls inheritance metaphorical succession in possession and recalls Ezra’s renewed public reading. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI(المفسرون); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(المفسرون); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:169/q04/p2 For Christian successors the book is the Gospel. — Al-Māwardī and Ibn al-Jawzī make this depend on the Christian identification. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 7:169/q04/p3 For Muslim successors the book is the Qurʾān. — Ibn al-Jawzī and Abū Ḥayyān list Qurʾān if Muhammad’s community is the referent. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 7:169/q04/p4 The divine writ is called the Bible. — Asad calls both occurrences of divine writ in the passage the Bible. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 7:169/q05 How is وَرِثُوا۟ read?  [وَرِثُوا۟ · readings]
- 7:169/q05/p1 Al-Ḥasan reads it with ḍamma on wāw and doubled rāʾ. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī attribute ḍamma and shadda to al-Ḥasan; al-Samīn says يأخذون can then start a new sentence. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن البصري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن البصري); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 7:169/q05/p2 Most read it in the ordinary active form. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the majority active reading; al-Zamakhsharī lists a related variant. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور)]

## 7:169/q06 How are inheriting and taking linked grammatically?  [وَرِثُوا۟ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ يَأْخُذُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether taking is circumstantial or a fresh statement.
- 7:169/q06/p1 Taking is circumstantial to the heirs. — Al-Samīn makes ورثوا descriptive of خلف and taking a circumstance of the heirs. Ibn ʿĀshūr says juxtaposition of inherited knowledge and misconduct heightens blame; al-Nasafī concurs. [8 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:169/q06/p2 Taking independently describes what they did after inheritance. — Al-Ālūsī lists an independent description; al-Samīn allows a fresh يأخذون after the passive reading. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:169/q07 What payment or sale of a muṣḥaf is allowed?  [* · law]
turns on: Early reports differ over sale, purchase and copying.
- 7:169/q07/p1 Sale is disliked or forbidden, while some allow purchase. — Al-Suyūṭī prefers disliking sale while permitting purchase, citing al-Shāfiʿī. Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid permit purchase but not sale; al-Nakhaʿī also forbids inheritance, and Ibn al-Musayyib urges lending or gifting. [6 notes: ITQAN(ابن المسيب,ابن عباس,النخعي,عبد الله بن شقيق,مجاهد); ITQAN+]
- 7:169/q07/p2 Sale, purchase and copying payment are permitted. — Saʿīd b. Jubayr says payment compensates handiwork; Ibn al-Ḥanafiyya identifies paper, and al-Shaʿbī paper and labor. Al-Rāfiʿī also mentions covers or copying work. [5 notes: ITQAN(ابن الحنفية,الرافعي,الشعبي,سعيد بن جبير,مجاهد وابن المسيب والحسن)]
- 7:169/q07/p3 Buying, selling and paid copying are all disapproved. — Ibn ʿUmar and Ibn Masʿūd disapprove buying, selling and paid copying; Ibn ʿAbbās and Ayyūb disapprove copying payment. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن عمر وابن مسعود)]
- 7:169/q07/p4 Payment for copying a muṣḥaf is disapproved. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Ayyūb al-Sakhtiyānī are reported to dislike it, separately from the broader ban on trade. [2 notes: ITQAN(أيوب السختياني,ابن عباس)]

## 7:169/q08 What does عَرَضَ mean?  [عَرَضَ · meaning]
turns on: The vocalization changes the property sense in some accounts.
- 7:169/q08/p1 It denotes passing worldly goods, opportunities or benefits. — Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī connect transience with the philosophical accident; al-Ḥaddādī compares the cloud in 46:24. Al-Akhfash includes opportunities, trouble and benefits; Ibn ʿĀshūr allows desires. [14 notes: AKHFASH; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيدة); BURSEVI(الحدادي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 7:169/q08/p2 Vowelled and still-rāʾ forms distinguish broad goods from narrower property classes, though authorities reverse the distribution. — Abū ʿUbayd and others distinguish worldly goods from noncoin chattels. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn reverse the coin sense. Al-Akhfash gives poetry for encounter, spear-facing, cloud, direction, presentation and breadth versus length. Al-Akhfash names ʿayn for dirhams and dinars, ʿurūḍ for chattels, ʿāriḍ for a facing cloud, and contrasts still-vowel breadth with length; he cites a spear poem. [17 notes: AKHFASH; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيد); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو عبيدة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو عبيدة)]

## 7:169/q09 What gain did they take?  [يَأْخُذُونَ عَرَضَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether taking is literal bribery or a metaphor for indulgence in sin.
- 7:169/q09/p1 They took bribes for judgments or for altering Scripture. — Al-Suddī and most commentators specify bribery in judgments; al-Zajjāj distinguishes unjust and just judgments. Ibn Zayd reports forged books for an oppressor. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers literal bribes because a similar offer can recur. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ(قوم); THALABI(المفسرون); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); IBNASHUR-FULL(السدي ومعظم المفسرين)+]
- 7:169/q09/p2 They seized whatever worldly opportunity appeared, lawful or unlawful. — Mujāhid, Qatāda and Ibn ʿAbbās say they took any desired gain, lawful or unlawful. ʿAṭāʾ says whatever presented itself; Ibn ʿĀshūr allows literal taking or becoming involved in desire. Al-Thaʿlabī glosses وهف as readily available. Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Ṭabrisī says worldly enjoyment reaches righteous and wicked alike. [17 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,عطاء عن ابن عباس,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:169/q09/p3 The gain could be books they authored or possessed. — Ibn Zayd reports al-Mathnā, a book reversing Torah’s true and false, and an altered book used to rule for an unjust litigant; al-Qurṭubī preserves the latter account. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد)]
- 7:169/q09/p4 Taking gain figuratively means committing sins. — Saʿīd b. Jubayr and Ibrāhīm gloss the taking as sins; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a metaphor for indulgence. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); IBNABIHATIM(إبراهيم وسعيد بن جبير,سعيد بن جبير وإبراهيم); IBNASHUR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير ومجاهد وقتادة والطبري)]
- 7:169/q09/p5 The gain was unlawful or corrupt earnings generally. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk simply calls the gain unlawful. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك)]

## 7:169/q10 What does هَٰذَا ٱلْأَدْنَىٰ convey?  [هَٰذَا ٱلْأَدْنَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether أدنى stresses nearness, baseness, or both.
- 7:169/q10/p1 It can mean near and lowly, and هذا dismissively points to transient goods. — Al-Māwardī and al-Bayḍāwī permit temporal and moral senses. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Qays ibn al-Khaṭīm; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares Abraham’s masculine demonstrative at 6:78. [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 7:169/q10/p2 It means the nearer, immediately available world. — Al-Ṭabarī contrasts present proximity with future life; al-Wāḥidī’s Mujāhid interpretation makes it the present day. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī prefer nearness. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 7:169/q10/p3 The masculine adjective follows an omitted masculine thing or abode. — Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Wāḥidī explain a masculine adjective after omitted feminine dunyā; al-Ālūsī supplies an omitted masculine ‘thing’. [2 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 7:169/q10/p4 It marks the gain as morally base and low in worth. — Al-Zamakhsharī derives it from low rank, smallness and moral baseness; al-Nasafī says the wording belittles the offer. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]

## 7:169/q11 What did their claim سَيُغْفَرُ لَنَا mean?  [وَيَقُولُونَ سَيُغْفَرُ لَنَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether they assert certain pardon, promise repentance, or merely hope.
- 7:169/q11/p1 They presumptuously asserted certain pardon while continuing to sin. — Saʿīd, al-Ṭabarī and many call it presumptuous self-assurance. Night/day forgiveness appears in Muqātil and al-Wāḥidī. Al-Ālūsī takes س as emphatic certainty; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 2:80 and allows public speech or self-talk. [32 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(سعيد بن جبير); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير,قتادة,مجاهد); THALABI(المفسرون); MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس والمفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:169/q11/p2 They claimed they would seek forgiveness or repent, yet returned to sin. — ʿAṭāʾ says they promised to seek forgiveness and repent; Ibn Kathīr says they promised themselves repentance. Al-Bursevī distinguishes tongue-only seeking from heartfelt repentance. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عطاء); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(عطاء); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 7:169/q11/p3 Their assurance reflected conceit about chosen descent. — Asad invokes Abrahamic descent; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes chosen-tribe conceit; Kuran Yolu treats it as plausible rather than certain. [3 notes: TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 7:169/q11/p4 They regarded the sin as forgivable and hoped for mercy. — Al-Māwardī and Ibn al-Jawzī report a view that they acknowledged sin as forgivable and merely hoped for mercy. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 7:169/q12 How does the passive سَيُغْفَرُ لَنَا work?  [سَيُغْفَرُ لَنَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether لنا or an implied noun occupies the passive subject position.
- 7:169/q12/p1 The implied subject is their taking or sins. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Samīn supply the verbal noun of taking; al-Bursevī compares 5:8. Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies an unspoken ‘that’ or ‘our sins’. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:169/q12/p2 لنا is treated as the passive verb’s deputy subject. — Al-Zamakhsharī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn treat the prepositional phrase as the passive deputy subject. Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects it because lām expresses cause, comparing 94:1. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 7:169/q12/p3 The passive leaves the known forgiver implicit and broadens their assurance. — Al-Biqāʿī says the passive pursues assurance without naming the forgiver; Ibn ʿĀshūr says God is known and the claim is generalized. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:169/q13 Does forgiveness of grave or repeated sin require repentance?  [سَيُغْفَرُ لَنَا · theology]
turns on: Whether repentance is a necessary condition or pardon remains possible without it.
- 7:169/q13/p1 Forgiveness for persistent grave sin requires repentance. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Zamakhsharī say forgiveness of grave sin requires repentance and repetition disproves it; al-Wāḥidī reports that Torah promised no pardon during persistence. Study Quran cites 16:119. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,بعض المفسرين); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 7:169/q13/p2 Presuming pardon while following desire is self-deception rather than genuine hope. — The Prophet’s prudent-person report contrasts self-accounting with following desire and wishing. Mālik b. Dīnār and al-Qushayrī warn of delusion by delayed punishment; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says true hope includes fear and action, citing 18:110. [11 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال); BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL(مالك بن دينار); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو الجلد,الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(شداد بن أوس); TABATABAI]
- 7:169/q13/p3 God may forgive without repentance, but a sinner cannot claim certainty. — Al-Rāzī rejects inferring no pardon for major sin; Muslims hope for forgiveness without guaranteeing it. Al-Samīn states the Sunni permission of pardon without repentance; al-Ālūsī distinguishes this from categorical certainty. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]

## 7:169/q14 What does taking another similar gain show?  [وَإِن يَأْتِهِمْ عَرَضٌۭ مِّثْلُهُۥ يَأْخُذُوهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether it emphasizes persistence or greed.
- 7:169/q14/p1 They repeat the wrongdoing and do not repent. — Saʿīd, Mujāhid and Qatāda treat renewed taking as continued sin without repentance. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares 5:79; Muqātil gives day and night bribes and al-Wāḥidī another bribe next day. ## 7:169/q29 What wider recitation and textual observations occur in the notes?  [* · sciences]
- 7:169/q29/p1 Reading transmission depends on trusted authorities and mastered routes. — Ibn al-Jazarī gives biographies and reliability assessments for ʿAbd al-Wāḥid, Ibn Mujāhid, al-Sāmirī, Ṣāliḥ, Ibn Shanabūdh and Qāḍī Abū Faraj, including late memory criticism of al-Sāmirī. Ibn Mujāhid lived 245–324 AH and first canonized seven readings, with 300 senior pupils and 84 deputies; al-Sāmirī lived about 295–386 and weakened late; Ibn Shanabūdh died 328 and admitted sound readings beyond the written muṣḥaf; ʿAbd al-Wāḥid died 349, Ṣāliḥ about 380, and Qāḍī Abū Faraj at 85 in 390. [14 notes: JISHUMI; NASHR(أبو محمد عبد الباقي,الخطيب البغدادي,الداني)]
- 7:169/q29/p2 Licensed readings may be combined only after separate mastery. — Ibn al-Jazarī and al-Suyūṭī say one may use a licensed variant without reciting all forms, and combined recitation follows separate mastery. They report differing full-cycle requirements, Egyptian word-by-word and Syrian pause methods, and avoidance of mixed routes. Al-Kamāl al-Ḍarīr studied nineteen full cycles; Ibn al-Jazarī reports twenty-one required by his teachers and another forty-cycle precedent. Some allow one cycle per reader but separate Nāfiʿ and Ḥamza transmitters; mastery and authorization remove later separate-cycle requirements. [17 notes: NASHR(الكمال الضرير,عبد الوهاب القروي الإسكندري,مشايخ الأندلس); ITQAN(أبو الحسن القيجاطي)]
- 7:169/q29/p3 Nearby readings and assimilation rules are also noted. — Ibn Mujāhid reports Abū ʿAmr’s doubled يمسكون and ولا تمسكوا; al-Naḥḥās says rāʾ cannot assimilate into lām because of vibration, citing 83:14. [2 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو); NAHHAS]
- 7:169/q29/p4 Verse endings and pauses are established through transmitted practice. — Al-Zarkashī cites Umm Salama’s verse-by-verse recitation, distinguishes consistent from occasional stops and analogical verse boundaries, and compares endings to prose rhyme and rajaz. [4 notes: BURHAN(أم سلمة)]
- 7:169/q29/p5 Retained ghunna in assimilation is disputed between incomplete assimilation and concealment. — Ibn al-Jazarī prefers incomplete assimilation with retained ghunna because audible shadda remains; al-Sakhāwī calls it concealment, while Abū ʿAmr defines complete assimilation by no residual trace. [5 notes: NASHR(أبو الحسن السخاوي,أبو عمرو الداني,الحافظ أبو عمرو)]
- 7:169/q29/p6 Sūra transitions have rules for tahlīl, takbīr and basmala. — Ibn al-Jazarī retains original vowels at sūra transitions, allows devotional tahlīl lengthening, and distinguishes takbīr attached to the end from that at the beginning, including prostration and prayer. [5 notes: NASHR]
- 7:169/q29/p7 Verse order comes through prophetic instruction; sūra order is discussed. — Al-Suyūṭī cites Makkī, al-Qāḍī Abū Bakr, Mālik and al-Baghawī for prophetic instruction in verse arrangement; Ibn Ḥajar’s inferred companion discretion is answered by other reports. Al-Tawba lacks an opening basmala in Makkī’s account because no instruction placed one; al-Baghawī distinguishes revelation order from recitation order. [6 notes: ITQAN(ابن حجر,البغوي,القاضي أبو بكر,مالك,مكي وغيره)]

[3 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
