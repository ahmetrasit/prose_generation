# 89:24: 12 questions

## 89:24/q01 Who utters the lament?  [يَقُولُ · referent]
- 89:24/q01/p1 The human being facing judgment speaks it, particularly the wrongdoer who failed to prepare. — Al-Wāḥidī names al-insān as the speaker. Al-Ṭabrisī calls him the wrongdoer. Ibn Kathīr distinguishes the sinner's regret over disobedience from the obedient person's wish for more obedience. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 89:24/q02 Is the statement voiced or inward, and how does it explain remembrance?  [يَقُولُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the lament is audible speech attached syntactically to the preceding remembrance, or an unspoken account of its content.
- 89:24/q02/p1 It may be spoken aloud, as a circumstantial clause or inclusive substitute for the earlier remembrance. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives both syntactic analyses. Al-Ālūsī likewise allows an inclusive substitute or an independent answer to an implied question asking what the person says on remembering; his fuller note calls it an appositive spelling out the remembered matter. Both connect it with 89:23. [4 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 89:24/q02/p2 It may be an inward statement explaining what the person remembers. — Ibn ʿĀshūr presents this as the alternative to audible speech; his full note repeats both possibilities. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 89:24/q03 Why is the vocative yā used in the wish?  [يَٰلَيْتَنِى · rhetoric]
- 89:24/q03/p1 It draws attention to the wish's momentous importance on that day. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly assigns this force to the vocative particle. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 89:24/q04 What form of laytanī is reported in Arab usage?  [لَيْتَنِى · grammar]
- 89:24/q04/p1 Some Arabs say laytanī by analogy with annī. — Al-Naḥḥās reports the analogy without adding a further interpretation. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 89:24/q05 What does the wish express at judgment?  [لَيْتَنِى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the account concerns regret for neglected obedience alone or also an obedient person's wish for still more reward.
- 89:24/q05/p1 It expresses sincere but useless regret for neglected duties and lost chances to prepare. — Al-Ḥasan says God knows the regret is truthful. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Zamakhsharī's full account, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, and Iṣlāḥī say action or useful remembrance is now too late. Al-Jishumī specifies remembered evil, neglected obligations and wasted time, with a wish to ransom oneself. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says annā signals distance and layta an impossible or remote return to do good before punishment. Iṣlāḥī relates the failure to love of wealth; Kur'an Yolu adds pride and neglected duties to God and people. Study Quran says repentance and making up time are now impossible. [16 notes: TAB(الحسن); TAB-FULL(الحسن); THALABI; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 89:24/q05/p2 Even an obedient person wishes to have done more and gained more reward. — Ibn Kathīr distinguishes the sinner's regret from the obedient person's wish for additional obedience. The Companion Muḥammad ibn Abī ʿUmayra says a lifelong worshipper would still judge his service insufficient and wish to return; Ibn Kathīr also reports a route through ʿUtba ibn ʿAbd attributing the account to the Prophet. Al-Durr transmits the Companion account. Study Quran cites a hadith saying believers also regret each occasion of remembrance of God, which it presents as a source of regret on Judgment Day. [8 notes: IBNKATHIR(النبي,محمد بن أبي عميرة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن أبي عميرة); DURR(محمد بن أبي عميرة); DURR-FULL(محمد بن أبي عميرة); STUDYQURAN]

## 89:24/q06 What does the speaker wish he had sent ahead?  [قَدَّمْتُ · interpretation]
- 89:24/q06/p1 Righteous deeds, obedience, or useful provision for the life to come. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Nasafī, and others describe deeds performed in worldly life for enduring happiness, salvation from God's anger and His approval. Al-Qushayrī says obedience and care for one's future life. Al-Biqāʿī specifies beneficial effort acted upon. Al-Ālūsī calls the omitted object good works beneficial there; al-Qurṭubī and Study Quran stress rescue from the Fire. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says only provision for the next world remains useful; al-Qutb calls that world the only life worthy of preparation. Al-Jishumī gives useful work now for the enduring afterlife or preparation for life after death as equivalent explanations. [34 notes: FARRA; TAB; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); THALABI; MAWARDI(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(most commentators); ALUSI+; BINTSHATI+]

## 89:24/q07 Why is the object of qaddamtu unstated?  [قَدَّمْتُ · grammar]
- 89:24/q07/p1 It is omitted for concision and understood as good or righteous deeds. — Al-Wāḥidī supplies good deeds and righteous work; Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly calls the omission concise and supplies good deeds. Al-Ālūsī also identifies the unspoken object as good works that would benefit the speaker. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI]

## 89:24/q08 What other verse is cited for qaddamtu?  [قَدَّمْتُ · links]
- 89:24/q08/p1 The verb is referred to the commentary on 82:5. — Corpus Coranicum directs readers to its note on 82:5 but supplies no further explanation here. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 89:24/q09 What does 'for my life' mean?  [لِحَيَاتِى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether lām marks purpose or time, and whether life names the deathless Hereafter, a rescued pleasant life, denied grave life, former earthly lifetime, or the once-denied Quran.
- 89:24/q09/p1 For the enduring afterlife, the true life without death. — Al-Ḥasan and Qatāda stress that it is long or deathless; Mujāhid and al-Ḍaḥḥāk identify the Hereafter. Al-Wāḥidī names Ibn ʿAbbās, Muqātil and the exegetes and cites 29:64; al-Rāzī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and others cite that verse for the afterlife as true life. Al-Māwardī also reports Ibn ʿAbbās's variant wording, sending provision from one's present life for the return. Al-Ālūsī prefers the causal lām, 'for the sake of' the afterlife. Al-Biqāʿī says obedience had been possible before, and only self-diversion squandered the God-given capacity. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers this reading because worldly life passes and only preparation for the next remains. The notes variously call it enduring, useful, real, or without death. [55 notes: FARRA; TAB(الحسن,قتادة,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(الحسن,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); NAHHAS(الحسن,الضحاك); MAWARDI(ابن عباس,الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل والمفسرون,الحسن); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(جمهور المتأولين); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور المتأولين); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(الحسن,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(الحسن,مجاهد); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; ALUSI+; BINTSHATI+]
- 89:24/q09/p2 During my former worldly life, with the lām marking time or meaning fī. — Al-Kashshāf compares dating an event to nights of Rajab and calls this clearer in one note, though its full note calls the afterlife clearer. Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares 'I came at sunrise'; Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī and others and compares sunrise or a date. Al-Qurṭubī and al-Burhān take lām as 'in'; al-Burhān cites 21:47. Al-Itqān compares 75:12 and 41:47. Al-Ālūsī compares writing for a date or coming at sunrise. Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies the first life before death. Al-Biqāʿī calls it the days of earth. [24 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(بعض المتأولين); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المتأولين); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري وغيره); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURHAN; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; KASHSHAF+]
- 89:24/q09/p3 A full and pleasant life secured by escape from Hell's diminished existence. — Al-Rāzī says Hell's occupants are effectively lifeless although they do not die, citing 14:17, 20:74 and 87:11–13. Al-Qurṭubī stresses the absence of pleasant life in the Fire. Al-Bursawī and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite 87:13 and describe saving deeds and freedom from torment; Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives the figurative sense of a good living existence now. Al-Ālūsī reports this purpose reading but calls the wish for beneficial life far-fetched. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA(آخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(آخرون); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI(قيل)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 89:24/q09/p4 Life in the grave after being raised, which the denier had rejected as impossible. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this from a group of interpreters; Abū Ḥayyān reports another group saying it was the grave life they denied. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA(قوم من المتأولين); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم من المتأولين); ABUHAYYAN(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 89:24/q09/p5 The Quran that the person had once denied. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ lists this among exegetes' interpretations of 'my life' without naming a proponent; she herself prefers the afterlife reading. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 89:24/q10 Does the lament prove independent human choice?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether wishing one had acted establishes independent power to act; the counterargument says a person can wish for what was impossible.
- 89:24/q10/p1 Yes; regret and wishing show that people could choose obedience and were not compelled into sin. — Al-Zamakhsharī and the Muʿtazilites infer real intention and choice from the wish. Al-Biqāʿī says obedience was possible before and the person's own distraction kept him from using God's powers. Abū Ḥayyān and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ report the argument; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ declines the wider free-will dispute. [10 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI(المعتزلة); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI(الزمخشري); BINTSHATI(الزمخشري); RAZI-; RAZI-FULL-; BAYDAWI-; BAYDAWI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-; ALUSI(أهل الحق)-; ALUSI-FULL(أهل الحق,الزمخشري)-]
- 89:24/q10/p2 No; a wish can concern an act the person could not independently perform, without eliminating human choice entirely. — Al-Bayḍāwī observes that someone barred from an act may still wish for it. Al-Rāzī argues that intentions either depend on prior intentions without end or on God's intention, defeating the Muʿtazilite inference. Al-Ālūsī says the verse shows perceived capacity, not whether God creates the act or acquired capacity; al-Kashf compares a drowning person's impossible wish, while 'the people of truth' still affirm some human choice. Abū Ḥayyān labels al-Zamakhsharī's inference Muʿtazilite. [9 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ALUSI(أهل الحق); ALUSI-FULL(أهل الحق,الزمخشري)]

## 89:24/q11 How does this lament fit the surrounding verses and sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes offer either a direct explanation of verse 23 in the judgment scene or a proposed later compositional layer connecting several speech passages.
- 89:24/q11/p1 It explains the remembrance in verse 23 and exposes the futility of recognizing the warning only at judgment. — Iṣlāḥī explicitly calls verse 24 the explanation of verse 23 and ties regret to dismissed warnings. Abū Ḥayyān says remembrance then is useless, whereas heed in earthly life would have helped afterward. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the repeated 'that day' in the preceding text has different attachments; its fronting stresses the time and builds suspense after interruption. Kur'an Yolu places this alongside unseen scenes of God or His command and ranked angels, whose nature cannot be specified, and calls for belief in accountability. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 89:24/q11/p2 Verses 23–24 are later additions that bring an afterlife response to Hell into the sūra's structure. — Corpus Coranicum says additions in 23–24 and 27–30 bring in the afterlife and clarify the enigmatic oath. It calls 23–24 and 27–30 insertions expanding an original four-verse eschatological unit, forming an antithesis that enacts the oath's duality. The human response to Hell parallels worldly responses in 15–16, while God's address to the trusting soul and servants supersedes the negative human speeches. [4 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 89:24/q12 How does ḥayātī contribute to the surrounding rhyme?  [لِحَيَاتِى · links]
- 89:24/q12/p1 Its rhyme with dhikrā is approximate; imāla in some canonical readings of dhikrā makes them sound closer. — Corpus Coranicum relates the pair in verses 15–16 to the pair in 29–30 through the approximate dhikrā/ḥayātī rhyme, and notes that several of the seven canonical readers pronounce dhikrā with imāla. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
