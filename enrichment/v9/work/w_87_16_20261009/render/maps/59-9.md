# 59:9: 28 questions

## 59:9/q01 Who are those who settled the abode and faith?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ تَبَوَّءُو ٱلدَّارَ وَٱلْإِيمَٰنَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the description is confined to Medina's Helpers, also includes already settled emigrants, or extends by analogy to later believers.
- 59:9/q01/p1 The Anṣār, Medina's Helpers. — Mujāhid, Qatādah, Ibn Zayd, and many authors identify them; Qatādah says they hosted migrants and built mosques before the Prophet arrived. Bursevī identifies Aws and Khazraj and traces their ancestry. Al-Qurṭubī says there is no disagreement on the historical referent. Al-Baghawī says they accepted Islam at home and built mosques two years before the Prophet arrived. Bursevī cites an account that they embraced Islam at home and built mosques two years before the Prophet arrived. [33 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(عمر)]
- 59:9/q01/p2 The Anṣār and earliest emigrants already established in Medina. — Islāḥī reasons that both groups preceded later arrivals in residence and faith; Anṣār alone preceded them only in possessing homes. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 59:9/q01/p3 By extension, settled believers of any age who welcome faith-driven refugees. — Asad explicitly generalizes the historical Anṣār to believers who live securely in Islam and welcome those displaced for faith. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 59:9/q01/p4 Inwardly, people established in the original abode of primordial purity. — Qāshānī interprets the home and faith as primordial nature and the first covenant, contrasting residents who retained purity with spiritual emigrants returning from the soul's exile. [3 notes: QASHANI]

## 59:9/q02 How does this clause connect grammatically with the preceding emigrants?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether wāw coordinates the Anṣār into the preceding allocation or begins an independent sentence.
- 59:9/q02/p1 It coordinates the Anṣār with the preceding emigrants, sharing the grammatical allocation. — Al-Naḥḥās allows genitive coordination with circumstantial or detached 'they love'; al-Ṭabarṣī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī and others report it. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers it for sūrah coherence; Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues the three Anṣār who got fayʾ prove inclusion; Elmalılı reasons altruism presupposes a right to relinquish. Islāḥī disputes using this to grant an additional share. [20 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(إسماعيل بن إسحاق); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الشافعي); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(exegetes); ELMALILI+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; TABATABAI+; QURTUBI-FULL-; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 59:9/q02/p2 It begins a new sentence praising the Anṣār, with 'they love' as predicate. — Al-Naḥḥās gives the nominative parse; al-Qurṭubī prefers a new beginning because Banu Naḍīr fayʾ went separately to emigrants, and reads 59:10 similarly. Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects it as disrupting coherence and relying on weak reports. [11 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL(من المفسرين); ALUSI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 59:9/q02/p3 It is joined to the conditional's concluding 'those are the successful'. — Elmalılı says this makes sincerity explicit and a fayʾ share implicit. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 59:9/q03 What does tabawwaʾū mean?  [تَبَوَّءُو · meaning]
- 59:9/q03/p1 They made a suitable place their dwelling and became settled there. — Jishumī explains bawwaʾa as lodging someone and mabāʾa as dwelling. Bursevī derives it from parts fitting and leveling in a place, contrasting nubuww; Qurṭubī, Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Elmalılı add establishment, preparing, returning, and taking a home. Bursevī cites a report about the Prophet choosing a settled place even for relief. [9 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 59:9/q03/p2 It means preparing or securing the abode. — Elmalılı includes preparing among lexical senses; Kur'an Yolu chooses it for the phrase, citing the Anṣār's Aqaba pledge and avoiding the implication that faith first gave them the land. [5 notes: ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Yaygın yorum); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]

## 59:9/q04 What is al-dār?  [ٱلدَّارَ · referent]
turns on: Whether it names the historical city or an inward abode.
- 59:9/q04/p1 Medina, the home of migration and faith. — Maqātil, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Rāzī and others identify it as Medina. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the definite article singles out Yathrib and dār can mean a land, tribal territory, or town (7:78); Biqāʿī calls it the prepared, blessed seat of migration. Mālik contrasts Medina, founded through faith and migration, with cities conquered by force. A Medina-name report includes al-Dār. [20 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مالك); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(مالك); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 59:9/q04/p2 Inwardly, the heart or primordial home of truth. — Qāshānī calls it the original home of primordial nature and covenant; Bursevī takes it as the heart, home of truth and sincerity. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 59:9/q05 How are 'the abode and faith' grammatically and rhetorically joined?  [ٱلدَّارَ وَٱلْإِيمَٰنَ · grammar]
turns on: Faith is not literally a place; analyses supply a verb or place, use metaphor, or read the conjunction as accompaniment.
- 59:9/q05/p1 An omitted fitting verb governs faith: they settled the city and embraced, chose, or made sincere their faith. — Abū ʿAlī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and the majority cite Arabic coordination of unlike objects, including 'fed her straw and [gave] cold water'; Samarrāʾī cites drinking milk and eating dates, and 10:71 requires assembling partners beside gathering a plan. Al-Ṭabarṣī says coordination is in wording, not sense. Al-Baghawī says faith is not a place; Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports an omitted verb meaning they preferred faith. Qurṭubī also reports omitted words referring to faith's places. [32 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي,مقاتل); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو علي والزمخشري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو علي); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL(الراجز الذي لا يعرف,جمهور المفسرين,عبد الله بن الزبعرى); SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Yaygın yorum); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 59:9/q05/p2 Faith is a figurative dwelling in which the Anṣār were firmly established. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī say steadfast faith is like a home; Abū ʿAlī cites settling among a clan's elite. Ālūsī prefers the implicit metaphor, while al-Samīn notes the debated combination of literal and figurative senses. Biqāʿī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī emphasize abiding and making religious practice possible. Qurṭubī reports metaphorically settling faith. [15 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو علي); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(المفسرون); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 59:9/q05/p3 The wording implicitly means 'abode of migration and abode of faith,' or calls Medina 'faith'. — Al-Zamakhsharī offers ellipsis of abode or calling Medina faith as the place it appeared; al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī and others report these. Bursevī favors 'abode of faith'; al-Samīn challenges whether the article can replace a possessive complement, noting Kufan and Basran disagreement and 79:39. Ālūsī rejects both parses as strained. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,الكوفيون); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 59:9/q05/p4 Faith accompanies the abode as a comitative object: they settled there with faith. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the pairing eloquent; Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers the comitative waw and argues its noun need not share the verb's government, against the Basran restriction and Ibn Hishām's doubt that it occurs certainly in the Qur'an; Kufans, Akhfash, and ʿAbd al-Qāhir allow the waw itself to govern. Elmalılı likes avoiding ellipsis; Ālūsī rejects the parse. [16 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن هشام,الأخفش,البصريون,جمهور البصريين); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Elmalılı,İbn Atıyye,İbn Âşûr); ELMALILI(İbn Atıyye)+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 59:9/q05/p5 The verb takes an expanded sense of adhering to the abode and faith together. — Al-Samīn and Abū Ḥayyān allow tabawwaʾū to imply adherence; Bursevī reports this alongside treating spiritual state as place; al-Irshād sees observance of faith's public rights. Ālūsī notes metonymy of settling for staying. Qurṭubī reports taking the verb as adhering to abode and faith. [6 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو علي); BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 59:9/q05/p6 The Anṣār prepared the abode through faith. — Kur'an Yolu prefers the sense 'prepare' because the Anṣār already owned their land; the Aqaba pledge secured it as an Islamic abode. Elmalılı permits this lexical sense. [3 notes: ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]

## 59:9/q06 What does 'before them' refer to and what came first?  [مِن قَبْلِهِمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether it dates their settlement before migrants arrived, their belief before some migrants, or precedence in settled faith.
- 59:9/q06/p1 Before the emigrants arrived or migrated to Medina; the Anṣār had established abode and faith there. — Mujāhid identifies the pronoun; Maqātil distinguishes prior settlement from faith. The verse does not require Anṣār belief before all emigrants, say Qurṭubī, Bursevī, and Ālūsī. Biqāʿī restricts the comparison to migration, not the whole mission; Ibn ʿĀshūr attaches the phrase only to settlement on his comitative parse. Al-Zajjāj assigns the pronoun to emigrants. [35 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(عمر); ZAJJAJ]
- 59:9/q06/p2 They believed before many emigrants, or before the emigrants in view. — Ibn Kathīr limits priority to many, not all; Ṭabarṣī reports a view of the seventy at Aqaba believing before the emigrants and pledging to fight all peoples. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABRISI(قيل)]
- 59:9/q06/p3 Inwardly, before migration from the exile of the lower soul to the primordial home. — Qāshānī cites 'love of homeland is part of faith' and contrasts residents who kept purity with wayfarers who regained it. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 59:9/q07 Whom do they love and how is that love shown?  [يُحِبُّونَ مَنْ هَاجَرَ إِلَيْهِمْ · interpretation]
- 59:9/q07/p1 They love emigrant believers who came to Medina, welcoming and supporting them. — Maqātil, Ṭabarī and others identify the migrants; Ālūsī offers literal love for faith-driven migration or a figure for unworn support. Biqāʿī says choosing the Anṣār as destination deepens the claim; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites brotherhood, Saʿd ibn al-Rabīʿ's offer, and unusual tribal welcome. Reports recount date-palm sharing, Bahrain land refused without migrant parity, and migrants praising the Anṣār. Elmalılı includes the Prophet and rich or poor migrants. Al-Dāmghānī says ḥubb can mean preferring, citing 38:32, 14:3, 9:23, 16:107 and 41:17; al-Naḥḥās glosses hājara ilayhim as moving to them. [31 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة,أنس بن مالك); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة,أنس بن مالك); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الأنصار,سعد بن الربيع); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; DAMGHANI; NAHHAS]
- 59:9/q07/p2 Inwardly, it is affinity and true kinship among those sharing primordial purity. — Qāshānī invokes the first covenant and brotherhood of fellow wayfarers; Bursevī says lovers of God love whom God and His beloved love. [2 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 59:9/q08 What is the 'need' they do not find in their breasts?  [وَلَا يَجِدُونَ فِى صُدُورِهِمْ حَاجَةً · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ḥājah is desire for the goods, resentment about them, or a felt lack.
- 59:9/q08/p1 No envy, anger, resentment, or distress over the migrants' preference and gifts. — Al-Ḥasan glosses it as envy; al-Rāzī explains 'need' as metonym for these accompanying emotions. Al-Wāḥidī says a ḥājah is anything burdensome in the breast needing removal. Bayḍāwī includes requests; several authors point to the Anṣār's acceptance of the Banu Naḍīr distribution. Qāshānī ascribes it to purity and certainty of their own share. Anas reports a man promised Paradise for bearing no ill will or envy despite modest night worship; the Prophet warned Anṣār of later favoritism and urged patience until the Pool. [33 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(الحسن); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(بعضهم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس,الحسن البصري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 59:9/q08/p2 No desire or claim for the migrants' allotment arises in them. — Al-Zamakhsharī takes ḥājah as a needed object; al-Ālūsī prefers this over resentment and says 'find' conveys practical awareness in the chest. Biqāʿī says no inner request, much less an open request. Ibn ʿĀshūr explains 'find' as mental perception and cites a poet's negated perception; ḥājah can mean desired aim as in 40:80 and 12:68, and min marks origin or cause. Qāshānī and the Study Quran extend detachment to worldly shares. Al-Naḥḥās coordinates the clause with 'they love'; Study Quran cites renouncing the world for God's love and renouncing others' goods for people's love. [28 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الشاعر); TABATABAI; QASHANI; STUDYQURAN(Aj); NAHHAS; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 59:9/q08/p3 They felt no painful lack from what the migrants received. — Ibn ʿĀshūr retains literal need as an emotion and says love erased any sense of deprivation; Qurṭubī and Nasafī supply omitted nouns yielding distress at what they missed. Qurṭubī also offers contentment even if the Anṣār's own allotment was small. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 59:9/q09 To whom do the pronouns in 'their breasts' and 'they were given' refer?  [صُدُورِهِمْ أُوتُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether both pronouns refer to the Anṣār or the second to emigrants.
- 59:9/q09/p1 The breasts belong to Anṣār; those given things are emigrants. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya supplies Banu Naḍīr's villages and property as what the emigrants received. Ibn ʿĀshūr says contextual pronouns can change antecedent nearby, citing 30:9 and poetry; Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes the first plural tracks the Anṣār and second tracks the plural sense of 'who'. [10 notes: AKHFASH; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن زيد,عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم,قتادة); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 59:9/q09/p2 Both pronouns refer to the Anṣār, with an omitted possessive before 'need'. — Abū al-Baqāʾ supplies the omitted muḍāf; Qurṭubī and Nasafī similarly understand their distress over what they themselves lacked. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل)]

## 59:9/q10 What does 'breasts' imply for inward speech and feeling?  [فِى صُدُورِهِمْ · theology]
turns on: Whether inward wording and meaning count as speech literally or only figuratively in a tangential discussion cited among the notes.
- 59:9/q10/p1 Inward speech is a mental wording joined to meaning, and 'speech' can apply to it literally. — Ālūsī argues a mentally imagined sentence is a fifth element beyond spoken words and abstract meaning; recalled words can affect a person without sound. He challenges al-Ṭūfī's appeal to frequent verbal usage and defends al-Akhṭal's line, whether it reads bayān or kalām, as evidence of the heart's inward ordering of words. [13 notes: ALUSI-FULL(المخالفون)]
- 59:9/q10/p2 Speech is literal utterance; an inner meaning or the poem's 'speech' is figurative. — Al-Ṭūfī argues listeners immediately understand utterance and that only uttered expression actually affects them; he reads al-Akhṭal's line as bayān or treats kalām there as hyperbole. Ālūsī rejects those arguments. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(نجم سليمان الطوفي); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 59:9/q11 What was given, and to how many needy Anṣār?  [مِّمَّآ أُوتُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the gift is material fayʾ or status, and whether two or three Anṣār were exceptional recipients.
- 59:9/q11/p1 Primarily Banu Naḍīr's fayʾ and other wealth or gifts to emigrants. — Ṭabarī and others describe the Prophet's distribution. Biqāʿī says the passive can include any giver, even God and His Messenger, and gifts beyond fayʾ; Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies Banu Naḍīr. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,عبد الله بن أبي بكر); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 59:9/q11/p2 Three needy Anṣār received fayʾ, named Abū Dujāna, Sahl ibn Ḥunayf, and al-Ḥārith ibn al-Ṣimma. — Bursevī supplies all three names; other sources give the count of three without all names. Ṭabāṭabāʾī uses the exceptions as evidence that the Anṣār were legally included. [10 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI(Müfessirînin bir kısmı); TABATABAI]
- 59:9/q11/p3 Only two needy Anṣār received it: Sahl ibn Ḥunayf and Abū Dujāna. — Ṭabarī reports these two pleading poverty; Bursevī argues al-Ḥārith had died at Biʾr Maʿūna. Ṭabarṣī notes divergent counts. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن أبي بكر); TABRISI; BURSEVI]
- 59:9/q11/p4 The emigrants' rank, honor, closeness, and precedence. — Māwardī reports this alternative; Ibn Kathīr holds that the Anṣār did not envy their favored rank and precedence in mention. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 59:9/q12 What does it mean to prefer others over oneself here?  [وَيُؤْثِرُونَ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the omitted recipients are specifically emigrants or people generally, and what the preference comprises.
- 59:9/q12/p1 The Anṣār prefer emigrants by sharing homes, wealth, food, and the fayʾ, even while needy. — Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī and Qurṭubī identify the omitted object as emigrants; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the previous reference makes it recoverable and that this exceeds merely not coveting their gifts. Mujāhid says they received no booty themselves. Bayḍāwī and Nasafī report even an offer involving a wife. Ibn Kathīr compares 76:8 and 2:177 and quotes charity by one with little. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says this praise and the īthār clause describe the Anṣār. [40 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد,مجاهد وابن حيان); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 59:9/q12/p2 They put any other believers or people before themselves. — Biqāʿī leaves recipients general; Ālūsī says the omitted object need not restrict the virtue to emigrants. Elmalılı includes fellow believers more broadly; Asad extends it to refugees. Al-Wāḥidī says īthār is singling out and favoring; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a virtuous choice. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 59:9/q12/p3 Inwardly, altruism prefers God's pleasure or all companions' rights over one's own interest. — Tustarī says preference for God's pleasure proves love; Wahīb's divine oath promises freedom from worries and inner poverty for such preference. Sulamī's authorities define chivalry, companion-first conduct, refusing distinctions, afterlife preference, and seeing one's hand as a trustee of God's property. Qushayrī says true altruism may favor even the full while one hungers and sees one's own goods as a trust. Qāshānī makes it detachment from ego and love of path companions. [17 notes: TUSTARI(وهيب بن الورد); SULAMI(أبو الحسين البوشيخي,أبو حفص,ابن عطاء,القاسم,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,بعضهم,يوسف بن الحسين); QUSHAYRI(ويقال); QASHANI]

## 59:9/q13 What does īthār mean as a virtue, and when is it appropriate?  [وَيُؤْثِرُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether giving all at personal cost is always superior or depends on one's ability to endure hardship.
- 59:9/q13/p1 It is choosing another's benefit over one's own, a high form of generosity even in need. — Wāḥidī defines it as favoring and singling out; Qurṭubī traces it to certainty, love and patience, and Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls costly preference the noblest trait. Ibn Kathīr cites the best charity as the effort of one with little; Study Quran links 2:177 and 76:8. Giving one's life is an even higher form: Qurṭubī and Bursevī cite Abū Ṭalḥa shielding the Prophet at Uḥud and the sacrifice at Yarmūk. Elmalılı says occasion stories exemplify many such acts, including giving up a wife for an emigrant. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو طلحة); BURSEVI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI]
- 59:9/q13/p2 An inward virtue of placing God and others ahead of ego without distinctions. — Abū Ḥafṣ includes worldly and religious interests; Sulamī's other authorities place every person's rights first and see owned goods as trust. Qushayrī says one may even prefer someone already full and that distinguishing people weakens altruism. Qāshānī calls it restraint from ego's share after tasting universal desires. [11 notes: SULAMI(أبو حفص,ابن عطاء,بعضهم,يوسف بن الحسين); QUSHAYRI(ويقال); QASHANI]
- 59:9/q13/p3 Giving everything is excellent for one able to bear want; another should retain enough to avoid begging. — Qurṭubī reconciles praise of the Anṣār with the Prophet's rebuke of a man who gave away his entire gold piece then would beg, and cites 2:177. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 59:9/q14 What historical arrangement with the Anṣār and emigrants is linked to the fayʾ?  [وَيُؤْثِرُونَ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ · reports]
turns on: Which choice the Prophet offered and what the Anṣār chose; sources give differing terms.
- 59:9/q14/p1 The Prophet offered the Anṣār a fayʾ share if they shared homes and wealth, or their property if they took no fayʾ; they shared their property and left the fayʾ to emigrants. — Al-Farrāʾ, Ibn ʿAbbās reports, and others describe the choice at Banu Naḍīr and the Anṣār's selflessness. ## 59:9/q16 What does khaṣāṣah mean?  [خَصَاصَةٌۭ · meaning]
- 59:9/q16/p1 Need, poverty, or hunger, with an original image of gaps or openings. — Ṭabarī calls it a verbal noun and noun for need or a gap, with plurals khaṣāṣāt and khaṣāṣ; an anonymous rajaz line pictures eyes looking through wall openings. Others cite holes in walls, doors, sieves, clouds and veils, or a sickly person's poverty relieved by spring; Bursevī cites al-Rāghib on a gapped reed hut. Tustarī notes makhṣūṣ for an impoverished person; Qurtubī and Jishumī also derive it from being left without what one needs. ## 59:9/q17 How does 'even though they have need' work grammatically and rhetorically?  [وَلَوْ كَانَ بِهِمْ خَصَاصَةٌۭ · grammar]
- 59:9/q17/p1 It is a circumstantial, concessive clause intensifying the Anṣār's preference despite poverty. — Al-Zamakhsharī treats it as ḥāl; al-Samīn and Ālūsī identify the wāw as circumstantial. ## 59:9/q18 How is yūqa read and parsed?  [يُوقَ · readings]
turns on: The common light-qāf reading differs from geminated readings; grammarians also dispute retaining a weak final letter under jussive.
- 59:9/q18/p1 The common reading is yūqa with a light qāf, passive imperfect in a jussive condition. — Naḥḥās, Jishumī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya analyze the verb as conditional with the final weak letter deleted; Jishumī makes shuḥḥ its accusative object. Basrans bar retention of the weak alif; Kufans cite poetic 'alam yaʾtīka' and 20:77, but Naḥḥās says they confuse vowels and poetic license. - 59:9/q18/p2 Abū Ḥaywa and Ibn Abī ʿAblah read a doubled qāf, with the wāw opened. — Ibn al-Jawzī also names Ibn al-Sumayfaʿ and Abū Rajāʾ for yuwaqqa. The exact transmitters vary among the sources. Ālūsī and Elmalılı say total generosity in every matter is unnecessary. Ibn ʿUmar's disputed example of money he wished back and approving ʿUthmān's death shows how widely the charge could be applied. [18 notes: TAB-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن عمر); THALABI(أنس بن مالك); IBNATIYYA-FULL(رسول الله); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(رسول الله); QURTUBI-FULL(أنس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,خالد بن يزيد بن جارية,علي بن أبي طالب); ALUSI-FULL(النبي صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم عن مجمع بن يحيى,جابر بن عبد الله وأنس,علي); ELMALILI(Hazret-i Ali,Mücemmi‘ b. Yahyâ); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Birçok müfessir)]
- 59:9/q22/p3 Protection frees the heart from any claim beyond God and from ego's self-interest. — Tustarī and Sahl say the protected abide with God; al-Wāsiṭī warns that even the virtues of Moses, Abraham, and the Beloved cannot compensate for an innermost self that still sees its own stake. Al-Qushayrī distinguishes being protected from merely abstaining; al-Suhrawardī contrasts innate liberality with acquired giving. [11 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(الواسطي,سهل); QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL(القشيري); BURSEVI(السهروردي,النبي عليه السلام); QASHANI; STUDYQURAN]
- 59:9/q22/p4 The Anṣār exhibited protection by voluntarily leaving fayʾ to emigrants. — Maqātil, Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī give this concrete application; Anas swore there were no misers among the Anṣār, and Bursevī quotes a report making the same claim. [5 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); TABRISI(أنس بن مالك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام)]

## 59:9/q23 Why is protection from greed worded and placed as a conditional conclusion?  [وَمَن يُوقَ شُحَّ نَفْسِهِۦ فَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ · rhetoric]
- 59:9/q23/p1 It sums up the Anṣār's example as a general rule and emphatically praises those whom God protects. — Qushayrī stresses 'is protected' rather than simple avoidance; Biqāʿī says the passive evokes strong divine aid. Bursevī calls the close an aside about Anṣār; Ibn ʿĀshūr an epilogue explaining their altruism. Ālūsī says singular man followed by plural ulāʾika tracks form then sense, suggesting few attain this great rank. [7 notes: QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 59:9/q24 Who are the successful, and what is their success?  [فَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ · interpretation]
- 59:9/q24/p1 Those protected from greed attain their sought good and escape harm, especially Paradise and reward. — Ṭabarī says they remain in Paradise; Ibn ʿAṭiyya glosses mufliḥūn as attaining what they seek; Bayḍāwī names present praise and future reward; Bursevī calls it happiness in both abodes. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the demonstrative honors them and emphatic restriction magnifies their success, with partial protection bringing proportional success. [26 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Hasan,Râgıb,Âlûsî,İbn Mes‘ûd,İbn Ömer); TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 59:9/q24/p2 Inwardly, success is attaining the heart's perfections and life with God. — Qāshānī identifies the heart's perfections; Sahl and Tustarī say freedom from attachment yields abiding with God. [3 notes: QASHANI; TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل)]

## 59:9/q25 What legal consequence follows from resentment of the Companions?  [وَلَا يَجِدُونَ فِى صُدُورِهِمْ حَاجَةً · law]
- 59:9/q25/p1 One who disparages or resents a Companion has no entitlement to Muslim fayʾ. — Mālik cites the sequence of fayʾ distribution and the prayer for later believers in 59:7–10. [1 notes: THALABI(مالك بن أنس)]

## 59:9/q26 What rights to fayʾ and conquered land follow from these verses?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether the Anṣār and later Muslims share fayʾ, and whether an imam may retain conquered land for public benefit.
- 59:9/q26/p1 The fayʾ passages include emigrants, Anṣār, later believers, and ultimately Muslims generally. — ʿUmar cites the charity, booty and fayʾ passages, including 59:7–10, to say even a remote shepherd has a public-fund right. Ismāʿīl ibn Isḥāq coordinates all three groups; Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Ibn ʿĀshūr include Anṣār. Kur'an Yolu says land revenues, kharāj and jizya are treated as fayʾ for these beneficiaries. [16 notes: MUQATIL; QURTUBI-FULL(إسماعيل بن إسحاق,مالك بن أوس وعمر بن الخطاب); DURR-FULL(عمر); BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hz. Ömer,Yaygın görüş)]
- 59:9/q26/p2 The Anṣār's praise does not itself create an additional fayʾ allotment. — Qurṭubī prefers an independent sentence on Banu Naḍīr, and Islāḥī says the named needy categories already included poor Anṣār; the verse praises willingness to sacrifice, not a disputed claim. Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects a blanket denial of their share as disruptive and weakly reported. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(من المفسرين,منهم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 59:9/q26/p3 The imam may retain conquered land for public Muslim interests. — ʿUmar retained Iraq and Egypt lands for stipends and dependents despite requests from al-Zubayr, Bilāl and soldiers to distribute them; one account says he bought dissenters' shares, another says he did not compensate. Mālik permits retention, Abū Ḥanīfa gives a choice, and a report from al-Shāfiʿī gives the imam choice when fayʾ is land. [7 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة,عمر,مالك); IBNASHUR-FULL(الشافعي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hz. Ömer)]
- 59:9/q26/p4 The imam must distribute conquered land unless owners freely dedicate their shares. — Al-Shāfiʿī, as quoted by Qurṭubī, reads ʿUmar's action as relying on consent and purchasing withheld shares. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي)]

## 59:9/q27 How does the verse fit the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether it continues a distribution list or forms a parenthetic praise and ethical contrast.
- 59:9/q27/p1 It continues the fayʾ recipients and praises Anṣār after the emigrants. — Biqāʿī says allocation first honors emigrants, then consoles and thanks Anṣār; Ibn ʿĀshūr joins them for coherence and treats the closing condition as a general epilogue. Kur'an Yolu reads 59:8–10 as an ideal of selfless believers and respect across generations. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]
- 59:9/q27/p2 It is a fresh praise of Anṣār after an emigrant-only allotment. — Qurṭubī bases this on distinct Banu Naḍīr allocations; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports and rejects such a break as damaging coherence. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(من المفسرين,منهم)]
- 59:9/q27/p3 Verses 5–10 are a parenthesis answering Jewish and hypocrite objections to Banu Naḍīr events. — Islāḥī treats this as a digression from the main story and says Anṣār's contentment shames hypocrites who demanded booty; he warns that the hypocrites' selfishness threatens their fate. [7 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 59:9/q28 What conduct does the verse commend for later believers?  [* · interpretation]
- 59:9/q28/p1 Love of the Anṣār is a mark of faith; honor them and pardon their faults. — ʿUmar counsels the next caliph to safeguard early emigrants' rights and accept Anṣār good while forgiving faults. Bursevī quotes the Prophet that loving Anṣār signals faith and hating them hypocrisy, plus a prayer for them and their descendants. [7 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(عمر); BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام); BIQAI-FULL(أبو بكر الصديق,العنزي,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 59:9/q28/p2 Welcome and share with needy believers, seek God's pleasure, keep faith sincere, and ask divine help against selfishness. — Islāḥī says the historical sacrifice teaches mutual sympathy and generosity. Kur'an Yolu draws practical counsel: credit success to God, seek His pleasure, aid the Messenger, keep honest promises, welcome needy believers without display, ask protection from weakness, trust mercy and pray for others. Asad applies the Anṣār's welcome to refugees in every age. [15 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES]
- 59:9/q28/p3 Religion is love and the Anṣār's love illustrates it. — Imam al-Bāqir links this verse with 3:31 and 49:7 and concludes that religion and love are one. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام)]
