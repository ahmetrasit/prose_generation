# 36:12: 36 questions

## 36:12/q01 How is إنا نحن parsed?  [إِنَّا نَحْنُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether نحن completes the predication or reinforces the subject of نحيي.
- 36:12/q01/p1 نحن is emphatic, and نحيي is the predicate. — Al-Razi prefers this analysis. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+]
- 36:12/q01/p2 إنا نحن is a complete subject and predicate. — Al-Razi likens it to a famous person saying simply 'I': divine self-identification suffices because God is known by His attributes. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 36:12/q02 Why the emphatic divine 'We'?  [إِنَّا نَحْنُ · rhetoric]
- 36:12/q02/p1 It marks majesty, sole agency, and assurance of resurrection. — Al-Razi says God needs no name or lineage to distinguish Him and the wording signals oneness; al-Biqaʿi stresses protection of believers; al-Alusi says it answers resurrection deniers (45:24) and repeating نحن adds emphasis or exclusivity; Elmalılı gives majesty or 'We alone.' [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 36:12/q03 What is the form of إنا?  [إِنَّا · grammar]
- 36:12/q03/p1 A nūn has been omitted from إننا to avoid three consecutive nūns. — Al-Nahhas gives the phonological explanation. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 36:12/q04 What does reviving the dead mean?  [نُحْىِ ٱلْمَوْتَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether death is physical, figurative spiritual death, the earth's death, or an allusion to deeds.
- 36:12/q04/p1 God raises the physically dead for resurrection and recompense. — Muqatil, Yahya, Ibn Abbas and others give resurrection; al-Qurtubi prefers it as a reply to unbelievers; Abu Hayyan rejects the faith metaphor as remote. Al-Biqaʿi also sees gradual worldly revival before the collective Hour. [18 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 36:12/q04/p2 God brings people from disbelief or ignorance to faith. — Al-Dahhak and al-Hasan give this figurative reading; al-Kashshaf says from idolatry to faith; al-Biqaʿi allows spiritual rescue from obscurity. Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi find it remote. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك والحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazı ilk dönem müfessirleri); STUDYQURAN(Z); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن والضحاك)-; QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 36:12/q04/p3 God revives dead hearts and inner faculties through faith, truth, service, disclosure, and witnessing. — Muhammad b. Ali al-Tirmidhi distinguishes souls, hearts, secrets, and inmost hearts; Study Quran reports the knowledge and truth interpretation. [2 notes: SULAMI(محمد بن علي الترمذي); STUDYQURAN(Aj, IK)]
- 36:12/q04/p4 God revives the dead earth. — Study Quran attributes this reading to IK and cites 57:17. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 36:12/q04/p5 It refers to deeds. — Jishumi transmits this unusual gloss from Mujahid without further explanation. [1 notes: JISHUMI(مجاهد)]

## 36:12/q05 Why is نحيي inflected as it is?  [نُحْىِ · grammar]
- 36:12/q05/p1 Its ḍamma is omitted for heaviness, and the two yāʾs cannot assimilate because that would juxtapose two vowelless letters. — Al-Nahhas gives both morphological constraints. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 36:12/q06 Why does resurrection appear before recording?  [نُحْىِ ٱلْمَوْتَىٰ وَنَكْتُبُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the order reflects the aim of recording or the grandeur of resurrection.
- 36:12/q06/p1 Resurrection is the purpose that makes the record consequential. — Al-Razi says writing comes first in time, but without revival and reckoning it has no effect; al-Alusi says the effect of angelic writing appears then. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 36:12/q06/p2 Resurrection is the greater divine act, so its prominence magnifies it. — Al-Razi connects the order to the majestic emphasis. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 36:12/q07 What does God's writing of deeds signify?  [وَنَكْتُبُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether writing is an actual deed register, a figure for recompense or knowledge, or paired with prior inscription.
- 36:12/q07/p1 Deeds and traces are entered in angelic or individual deed records. — Al-Alusi says angelic writing is ascribed to God by His command; Tabatabai distinguishes personal books from the universal Imam; Elmalılı says pre-event Tablet inscription is followed by writing after action. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Zemahşerî ve Şevkânî)]
- 36:12/q07/p2 Writing stands for recompense. — Abu Hayyan gives recompense alongside comprehensive knowledge; al-Alusi prefers the metonym because writing follows resurrection in the wording. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 36:12/q07/p3 Writing means preserving deeds in God's knowledge. — Abu Hayyan connects the metaphor to exhaustive knowledge fixed by writing; al-Alusi transmits a view that God neither forgets nor neglects acts. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 36:12/q07/p4 Writing shows a prior record of what people will do, then a record as they do it. — Al-Razi reports the predetermination explanation; Elmalılı says things are known and written beforehand and recorded afterward for accountability. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 36:12/q08 How is ما قدموا construed grammatically?  [مَا قَدَّمُوا۟ · grammar]
- 36:12/q08/p1 It is the object of the active نكتب, with آثارهم coordinated with it. — Al-Samin describes the common active reading; al-Nahhas says a genitive complement stands in place of an omitted governing noun. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(العامة); NAHHAS]

## 36:12/q09 What have people sent ahead?  [مَا قَدَّمُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether قدموا denotes all acts, only good acts, intentions, or an implied pair including later effects.
- 36:12/q09/p1 Their deeds performed in worldly life, good and evil. — Mujahid, Qatada, Ibn Zayd and others give the general deed gloss. Al-Biqaʿi includes acts, states and words, recorded with ensoulment; Study Quran says these testify for or against people. Al-Razi calls them deeds brought into existence; Islahi includes afterlife-directed and worldly acts. [34 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(مجاهد وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة ومجاهد وابن زيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Zemahşerî ve Şevkânî); STUDYQURAN]
- 36:12/q09/p2 Their good works, contrasted with misguidance left behind. — Mujahid is reported this way by Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Wahidi, al-Durr and Ibn Kathir. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 36:12/q09/p3 Their intentions, preceding the resulting deeds. — Al-Razi reports the possibility; Tabatabai finds it contextually remote; al-Alusi says angels cannot see or record intentions. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); TABATABAI(قيل)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 36:12/q09/p4 Both what they did earlier and what they left for later. — Al-Razi sees one side of the pair as implying the other, comparing 16:81's clothing protecting from heat while also protecting from cold. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 36:12/q10 How do deeds and traces relate?  [مَا قَدَّمُوا۟ وَءَاثَٰرَهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the pair contrasts acts with enduring effects or intentions with acts.
- 36:12/q10/p1 Direct acts are distinguished from effects that endure or are followed afterward. — Abu Ali and al-Jubbaʾi contrast acts without and with continuing effects; Ibn Atiyya says the categories overlap but naming both stresses enduring remembrance; Islahi includes acts done for later generations. [5 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي); TABRISI(الجبائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 36:12/q10/p2 Intentions are sent ahead and actual deeds are their traces. — Al-Razi gives this alternative; Tabatabai rejects it as far from context, and al-Alusi notes reports that angels cannot record hidden intentions. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); TABATABAI(قيل)-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 36:12/q11 What are people's آثار?  [وَءَاثَٰرَهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the traces are literal tracks, continuing social effects, wealth, deeds as opposed to intentions, or specifically a legacy of error.
- 36:12/q11/p1 Good or evil legacies, practices, and consequences that continue after death. — Muqatil and the Prophetic precedent reports say originators share followers' reward or burden without reduction. Examples across al-Kashshaf, al-Qurtubi, al-Razi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, al-Alusi, Elmalılı and Kuran Yolu include knowledge, books, charity, endowments, mosques, bridges, buildings, harmful policies, debased coinage, falsehood and corruption. Al-Mawardi includes later repair or corruption through one's character; al-Zajjaj prefers this broad common sense; al-Baghawi is said to choose it. Masruq says every step counts good or evil; Qatada and Umar b. Abd al-Aziz stress even wind-erased tracks. Anas specifies Friday. - 36:12/q11/p6 Every former deed, good or evil. — Al-Kalbi makes the traces encompass all prior works. [1 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الكلبي)]

## 36:12/q12 What is the singular and vocalization of آثار?  [وَءَاثَٰرَهُمْ · meaning]
- 36:12/q12/p1 Its singular is أثر, also pronounced إثر or with another athar vocalization. — Al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi record the forms. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 36:12/q13 How is ونكتب ... وآثارهم read?  [وَنَكْتُبُ وَءَاثَٰرَهُمْ · readings]
turns on: Active and accusative object coordination versus passive morphology and nominative deputy-subject analysis.
- 36:12/q13/p1 A passive reading has يُكْتَبُ or ويكتب and nominative آثارهم. — Al-Zamakhshari notes passive yuktab and nominative kull; Ibn al-Jawzi attributes the passive and rafʿ of آثار to al-Nakhaʿi and al-Jahdari, while Abu Hayyan, al-Samin and al-Alusi cite Zirr and Masruq. Al-Samin makes آثارهم a deputy subject coordinated with ما قدموا; Ibn Atiyya separately attributes nominative آثارهم to Masruq. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مسروق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(النخعي والجحدري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زر ومسروق); SAMIN-DURR(زر ومسروق); ALUSI-FULL(زر ومسروق)]
- 36:12/q13/p2 The common active reading makes ما قدموا the object and آثارهم accusative by coordination. — Al-Samin describes the common construction; Ibn Atiyya notes a group with accusative آثارهم. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(العامة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]

## 36:12/q14 What happened when a distant group considered moving near the mosque?  [وَءَاثَٰرَهُمْ · reports]
turns on: Which group the report names and whether the episode is tied to revelation.
- 36:12/q14/p1 Banū Salima or the distant Ansar considered moving; the Prophet urged them to stay because their footsteps were recorded. — Reports vary in whether they complained, proposed selling homes, or received a prophetic response after the verse. Ibn Atiyya says the Prophet also disliked depopulating Medina. Jabir's version is in Muslim; Abu Saʿid's is graded hasan gharīb or hasan by al-Tirmidhi and authenticated by al-Hakim in later citations. Anas, Ibn Abbas, Abd Allah and al-Hasan supply variants. - 36:12/q14/p2 Banū ʿUdhra, whose distant homes made prayer attendance hard, occasioned the verse. — Al-Mughira and al-Dahhak name Banu Udhra in al-Thaʿlabi; Jishumi gives the same name and footstep explanation. [3 notes: THALABI(الضحاك,المغيرة بن شعبة); JISHUMI]

## 36:12/q15 Was the verse newly revealed for the mosque relocation?  [وَءَاثَٰرَهُمْ · sciences]
turns on: Whether descent reports outweigh the Meccan setting, the Sahihayn recitation account, and the passage context.
- 36:12/q15/p1 Yes: reports say it descended when Banū Salima or the distant Ansar contemplated moving. — Abu Saʿid, Ibn Abbas and others explicitly use revelation language; a group makes this a Medinan exception to otherwise Meccan Yā Sīn. Al-Alusi argues the reports plainly say revelation then and rejects al-Khafaji's counterargument from the Sahihayn recitation. [21 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); IBNABIHATIM(أبو سعيد الخدري); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); WAHIDI-ASBAB(أبو سعيد الخدري); WAHIDI-BASIT(أنس وابن عباس في رواية عكرمة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وجابر بن عبد الله وأبو سعيد الخدري,فرقة); TABRISI(أبو سعيد الخدري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري,ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري,ابن عباس); ITQAN(أبو سعيد); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو سعيد الخدري,ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazıları); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; IBNKATHIR-FULL-; TABATABAI-; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 36:12/q15/p2 No: the verse was Meccan and later invoked or recited in Medina. — Ibn Atiyya rejects the occasion because Yā Sīn was revealed in Mecca; Ibn Kathir finds a Medinan revelation strange; Abu Hayyan calls it unsound; al-Khafaji and Kuran Yolu point to the Sahihayn report in which the Prophet recites an already known verse. Tabatabai says the proposed occasion disrupts the passage's unified flow. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,الخفاجي); TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; TABATABAI+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 36:12/q15/p3 The wording is general, beyond Banū ʿUdhra or any one occasion. — Jishumi reports this response to his Banu Udhra account. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]

## 36:12/q16 What do the footstep reports teach about walking to prayer?  [وَءَاثَٰرَهُمْ · reports]
- 36:12/q16/p1 Steps to and from prayer are individually recorded and rewarded, with greater distance bringing greater reward. — Uqba's report assigns a good deed and erased sin per step in both directions; Zayd cautions Anas against hurrying; Abu Musa's far-walker report occurs in Bukhari and Muslim; Ubayy's distant worshipper sought full credit rather than a donkey. Anas specifies Friday footsteps; Masruq and Qatada extend registration to good and bad steps, even wind-erased tracks. One report measures a migrant's distance from birthplace as reward in Paradise. [27 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة,مسروق); ABDURRAZZAQ(عمر بن عبد العزيز,مسروق بن الأجدع); TAB-FULL(ثابت,قتادة); NAHHAS(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(عمر بن عبد العزيز,مسروق); BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI(أبو موسى); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أنس بن مالك,قتادة وعمر بن عبد العزيز); QURTUBI-FULL(أنس بن مالك,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(زيد بن ثابت,عبد الله بن عمرو,قتادة); DURR-FULL(أنس,رسول الله,قتادة,مسروق); ALUSI-FULL(أنس,بعضهم)]
- 36:12/q16/p2 Waiting for the imam after walking earns additional reward. — The Prophet's report cited by al-Baghawi says waiting for prayer exceeds praying then sleeping. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 36:12/q17 May one pass a local mosque for a farther one?  [وَءَاثَٰرَهُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether extra walking reward warrants passing one's own nearby mosque.
- 36:12/q17/p1 A farther mosque can yield greater reward. — Al-Qurtubi cites the footstep reports and Anas passing a new mosque for an old one; an Ibn Majah report gives one reward at home, twenty-five in a tribal mosque and five hundred in a congregational mosque. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 36:12/q17/p2 One should not leave a nearby mosque merely for distance. — Al-Hasan and others disapprove, and al-Qurtubi identifies this with Malik's view; he reports juristic disagreement over passing the local mosque for the principal mosque. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن وغيره)]

## 36:12/q18 How are 'your homes' and 'we record' parsed in the mosque report?  [وَنَكْتُبُ · grammar]
- 36:12/q18/p1 Diyārakum is accusative exhortation to remain at home; naktub is jussive as the command's response. — Al-Qurtubi parses the words of the cited prophetic report. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 36:12/q19 What other scripture explains deeds sent ahead and traces left behind?  [مَا قَدَّمُوا۟ وَءَاثَٰرَهُمْ · links]
- 36:12/q19/p1 75:13 and 82:5 give the parallel pair of what one sends ahead and leaves behind. — Muqatil, Ibn Qutayba, Yahya and later commentators make the comparison; al-Qurtubi also cites 59:18, and Study Quran cites 5:80. Ibn Kathir adds 17:71, 39:69 and 18:49 for complete personal records; Tabatabai cites 45:29 for truthful book testimony. [16 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس عن عطاء); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(ḥadīth); TABATABAI]

## 36:12/q20 What does كل شيء encompass?  [وَكُلَّ شَىْءٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether كل شيء is unrestricted or bounded by the finite material capacity assigned to the Tablet.
- 36:12/q20/p1 Absolutely everything, extending beyond human works. — Al-Tabari says past and future; al-Biqaʿi includes created and uncreated matters in eternal knowledge and writing; Bursawi includes human and nonhuman things; al-Razi says no word or act escapes, citing 54:52–53. Al-Alusi treats the universal as broad and considers Paradise's unending events separately. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 36:12/q20/p2 All events in this world or through the Resurrection, a finite span. — Al-Alusi prefers a finite through-Judgment span for what the Tablet contains, restricting broad reports by narrower ones; others argue a finite physical Tablet cannot simultaneously inscribe infinite future events. Tabatabai calls this restriction arbitrary. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL+; TABATABAI-]

## 36:12/q21 How is وكل شيء أحصيناه parsed and read?  [وَكُلَّ شَىْءٍ أَحْصَيْنَٰهُ · grammar]
turns on: Accusative through an implied or resumptive verb versus nominative as a new sentence.
- 36:12/q21/p1 Kull is accusative in an object-resumption or اشتغال construction. — Al-Farra says all readers choose accusative because the verb acts on its resumptive pronoun; al-Wahidi preserves his analogy to an earlier governing verb. Ibn Atiyya, al-Tabrisi, Bursawi and al-Alusi posit an understood verb explained by أحصيناه. Al-Nahhas, al-Khalil, Sibawayh and al-Samin prefer accusative, especially as it coordinates with prior verbal clauses. [11 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; NAHHAS+; QURTUBI-FULL(الخليل وسيبويه)+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 36:12/q21/p2 Kull can be nominative as a fresh subject, with أحصيناه its predicate. — Al-Farra says the negative sense makes kull resemble an indefinite: nothing escaped. Abu al-Sammal, Ibn al-Sumayfiʿ and Ibn Abi Abla are cited for nominative; al-Wahidi calls it a new sentence. [10 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السميفع وابن أبي عبلة); QURTUBI-FULL(الخليل وسيبويه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو السمأل)]

## 36:12/q22 What does أحصيناه mean?  [أَحْصَيْنَٰهُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether enumeration is literal counting, preservation, clarification, or comprehensive knowledge.
- 36:12/q22/p1 We preserved it, bounded it, or recorded it. — Ibn Abbas gives 'preserved'; al-Baghawi combines preserving with counting and clarification; al-Mawardi offers preservation; al-Razi says enumeration is stronger than scattered writing because it totals entries. [9 notes: MAJAZ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL]
- 36:12/q22/p2 We made it clear. — Muqatil says God clarified every deed recorded; Ibn al-Shaykh says counting metaphorically yields clarification as well as preservation. [8 notes: MAJAZ; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL]
- 36:12/q22/p3 We counted or enumerated it. — Jishumi derives it from aḥṣā yuḥṣī; Ibn al-Shaykh says counting is original; al-Raghib connects the noun with pebbles once used for counting. Al-Baghawi and al-Alusi include counting among the senses. [7 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ,الراغب); ALUSI-FULL]
- 36:12/q22/p4 We know everything. — Al-Mawardi and Jishumi offer knowledge as what enumeration conveys. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل)]

## 36:12/q23 Why say أحصيناه rather than simply 'wrote it'?  [أَحْصَيْنَٰهُ · rhetoric]
- 36:12/q23/p1 Enumeration adds the idea of collection and total reckoning. — Al-Razi says writing could remain scattered without a complete tally; al-Biqaʿi says the wording uses mutual ellipsis, with the verb implying enumeration and Imam implying writing. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 36:12/q24 What is an إمام in language?  [إِمَامٍۢ · meaning]
- 36:12/q24/p1 Something followed or guiding, whether person, book, or road. — Al-Zajjaj ties imam to intention and imitation; Ibn Qutayba says a road guides travelers. Jishumi, al-Raghib and Islahi extend it to a followed book or source. Ibn al-Jawzi lists exemplary person (2:124), book or prophet (17:71), Tablet here, and road (15:79). Al-Razi observes possible singular and plural forms, reading 17:71 as plural leaders. [15 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,ابن قتيبة,الزجاج,قيل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ITQAN]

## 36:12/q25 What is the clear إمام here?  [إِمَامٍۢ مُّبِينٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether Imam is the universal Tablet, deed-books, divine knowledge, a guiding book or path, or Ali in inner interpretation.
- 36:12/q25/p1 The Preserved Tablet or Mother of the Book, the comprehensive divine register. — Sources equate the two titles; al-Tabari says it records past and future; al-Wahidi says angels compare events to it; Tabatabai calls it the unchanged detailed decree and source of deed books; al-Alusi prefers it through Judgment, while avoiding claims about its substance. Study Quran links it to 85:22 and 68:1. [49 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن عباس,السدي); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); NAHHAS(مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(السدي,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,ابن عباس ومقاتل,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة وابن زيد); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة وابن زيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، وقتادة، وابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة وعبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة وابن زيد); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(تفسير القمي); STUDYQURAN(Aj, Ṭb, Ṭs); ALUSI-FULL+; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 36:12/q25/p2 A book or central authoritative register, without a more specific identity. — Yahya, Abu Ubayda, al-Tabari's Qatada, Ibrahim and Islahi give the generic book gloss; al-Qummi calls it a firmly established clear book. Some say it is followed as proof or model. [12 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(إبراهيم,قتادة); ITQAN; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI(تفسير القمي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Atıyye)]
- 36:12/q25/p3 The individual or collective records of people's deeds. — Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi report deed-pages; al-Tabrisi says their marks do not wear away; Study Quran cites 17:71 and 18:49. Al-Alusi calls this unconvincing; Tabatabai rejects it because personal and universal books differ. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، وقتادة، وابن زيد); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Atıyye); STUDYQURAN(Aj, IK, Q, Ṭs); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)-; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 36:12/q25/p4 Ali, endowed with knowledge of all things, in an inner allusion. — Reports ascribed to Ali and the Prophet say he clarifies truth and inherited knowledge from the Prophet; Shiite theologians use them for imamate. Tabatabai permits this as an inward allusion if sound; al-Alusi rejects a literal extremist claim but allows a Sufi technical understanding of the perfect human. [7 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(أمير المؤمنين,النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); STUDYQURAN(Shiite theologians,the Prophet,ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib); ALUSI-FULL(بعض غلاة الشيعة)-]
- 36:12/q25/p5 God's eternal, causal knowledge. — Al-Alusi reports a comparison to Umm al-Kitab in 13:39 but rejects it as a departure from the apparent sense; Tabatabai rejects mere knowledge but allows active causal knowledge. Ibn Ashur says events unfold according to divine will and knowledge. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr); ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 36:12/q25/p6 A straight path. — Al-Mawardi attributes the identification to al-Dahhak; Ibn al-Jawzi also lists a road as a lexical sense using 15:79, although he applies Tablet to this verse. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك)]

## 36:12/q26 Why is the register called an إمام?  [إِمَامٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 36:12/q26/p1 It is followed as guide, model, command, or authoritative proof. — Jishumi says angels follow the book or it precedes other books; al-Razi says angels follow instructions for lifespan, provision, life and death; Tabatabai says creation follows its inexorable decree and deed-books are copied from it; al-Nasafi calls it the model for other books. [10 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Aj, Ṭb, Ṭs)]

## 36:12/q27 What makes the register مبين?  [مُّبِينٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether مبين qualifies a written register's legibility and testimony or knowledge's disclosure.
- 36:12/q27/p1 It makes what it contains plain or reveals the truth of recorded matters. — Al-Tabari says it reveals the record's truth; al-Razi says it shows angels their work, people their fate, and separates Paradise from Fire; al-Alusi says it manifests past and future. Al-Tabrisi says the deed-books' marks do not wear away. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 36:12/q27/p2 On the eternal-knowledge reading, knowledge discloses its object and creation follows knowledge and will. — Al-Alusi reports this sense but objects to the overall eternal-knowledge identification. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 36:12/q28 How can a finite Tablet contain everything?  [وَكُلَّ شَىْءٍ إِمَامٍۢ مُّبِينٍۢ · theology]
turns on: Whether the Tablet is a finite body, whether everything includes an infinite future, and whether writing may occur successively.
- 36:12/q28/p1 Limit the total record to worldly events or events through Resurrection. — Al-Alusi prefers the finite through-Judgment reading, reconciling unrestricted reports with restricted ones; Tabatabai reports the finite-body argument and rejects the limitation as arbitrary. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 36:12/q28/p2 The Tablet's actual nature is unknown, so physical-capacity claims are insecure. — Al-Alusi avoids specifying its substance and does not accept ruby-and-emerald reports as certainly authentic; Tabatabai calls restriction arbitrary. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 36:12/q28/p3 Successive installments could be inscribed, like the higher account in the Jafr al-Jamiʿ. — Al-Alusi proposes this if endless Paradise events are included. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 36:12/q28/p4 Completed thousand-year sets could be erased and replaced. — Al-Alusi offers this further solution under the assumption of a finite physical Tablet. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 36:12/q29 Are the deed-books and clear Imam one record?  [وَنَكْتُبُ إِمَامٍۢ مُّبِينٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether two different registers are specified by the two clauses.
- 36:12/q29/p1 They are distinct: angelic personal books record deeds, while the Imam enumerates universal decree. — Tabatabai cites 6:59, 45:28 and 17:13 for universal, communal and personal registers, and says the verse contrasts writing with enumeration. Elmalılı likewise distinguishes prior Tablet inscription from later action recording. [5 notes: TABATABAI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI+]
- 36:12/q29/p2 Writing and enumeration describe the same encompassing divine record. — Al-Razi's preservation and generalization explanations and al-Biqaʿi's mutually implying writing and enumeration allow the expressions to converge; Tabatabai argues against this identification. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI-]

## 36:12/q30 What inward reading is given for the clear record?  [إِمَامٍۢ مُّبِينٍۢ · inward]
- 36:12/q30/p1 The perfected heart is a manifest tablet in which unseen lights and mysteries become legible after purification. — Bursawi says polishing the mirror of the heart yields complete witnessing; al-Taʾwilat al-Najmiyya reads acts of nearness as lights impressed in the beloveds' hearts. Al-Jami's dog-and-reflection parable cautions against pursuing an image and losing reality. [5 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,الجامي)]

## 36:12/q31 What practice and caution are attached to the divine name here?  [إِمَامٍۢ مُّبِينٍۢ · inward]
- 36:12/q31/p1 Twentyfold recitation over twenty pieces of bread is said to subdue hearts, but self-mastery and reliance on God's will are superior. — Bursawi says influence may avert harm or enable guidance, while some Sufis leave outward influence to God. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 36:12/q32 How does this verse connect with the preceding warning?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the explanation attends to the preceding promise and threat or to the larger passage's two-group structure.
- 36:12/q32/p1 It completes the warning and promise by affirming resurrection, divine unity, and exhaustive reckoning. — Al-Razi says the missing worldly recompense of 36:11 requires resurrection and confirms fear of the Unseen; Ibn Atiyya says it answers denial and warns that acts are counted; al-Biqaʿi sees consolation for suffering believers. Al-Alusi says it closes the response to disbelievers and warned believers; Islahi says judgment is left implicit; Kuran Yolu and Study Quran stress consolation and reckoning. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 36:12/q32/p2 The passage is one coherent account of two responses to the call. — Tabatabai says a piecemeal application to Abu Jahl's group and the distant Ansar disrupts its flow, while a real concealment episode remains possible. [3 notes: TABATABAI]

## 36:12/q33 What does the complete record teach people?  [* · interpretation]
- 36:12/q33/p1 It calls for conscious moral responsibility because no deed, word, movement, or breath is lost. — Bursawi says the record both warns and encourages; Kuran Yolu says writing meets no divine need but turns duty into constant awareness and connects physical acts to transcendent consequences. [4 notes: BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 36:12/q34 What other Yā Sīn reports are cited near this verse?  [* · sciences]
- 36:12/q34/p1 Reports call Yā Sīn the Qur'an's heart, an intercessor for reciter and listener, and a source of nighttime joy. — Ibn Atiyya cites reports through Anas, Aisha and Yahya b. Abi Kathir; the latter appeals to experience. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أنس بن مالك,عائشة,يحيى بن أبي كثير)]

## 36:12/q35 How are Qur'anic words and the uncreated Qur'an manifested?  [* · theology]
- 36:12/q35/p1 The eternal Qur'an appears in created voices, hearts and codices without indwelling or loss of eternity. — Al-Alusi contrasts Moses' direct hearing behind a veil with human recitation, explains reports of the Qur'an appearing as a pale man, dismisses polemical objections by named scholars, and recommends overlooking great scholars' faults. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 36:12/q36 What is reported about Ibn Abbas's lexical questioning by Nafiʿ?  [* · reports]
- 36:12/q36/p1 Several recensions preserve the Meccan encounter and differing question counts. — Bint al-Shatiʾ records a thirty-one-question Juwaybir–al-Dahhak version, a fifty-question Damascus manuscript attributed to al-Khutalli, a parallel Abu Shihab–Ikrima version, and a third fifty-question Uthman al-Harrani recension. She notes manuscript audition marks and al-Zarkashi's rule that rare vocabulary is essential to exegesis. [8 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس,البدر الزركشي)]
