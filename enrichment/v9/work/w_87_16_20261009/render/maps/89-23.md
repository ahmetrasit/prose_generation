# 89:23: 28 questions

## 89:23/q01 What does Hell's being brought mean?  [وَجِا۟ىٓءَ بِجَهَنَّمَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether bringing denotes physical movement, exposure without movement, figurative depiction, or an inward fire.
- 89:23/q01/p1 Hell is actually brought to the gathering, led or moved there by angels. — Al-Ṭabarī says God brings it. Al-Ālūsī prefers real movement because it is possible for Hell to move from its place and back; the Day exceeds ordinary imagination. Bursawī says places can be moved instantly by origination and annihilation, the expanded earth can hold Hell, and an imaginal form could appear, as Jerusalem was shown to the Prophet. Biqāʿī says angelic leading shows God's power and heightens terror. Jishumī reports moving its location nearer, while rejecting the claim of dragging it in chains. [8 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; BURSEVI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+]
- 89:23/q01/p2 Hell is made visible or exposed to the people. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Biqāʿī, and others compare 26:91 or 79:36. Jishumī's Abū Muslim and Abū ʿAlī say it was already there, not newly created. Rāzī's theologians say it does not leave its place and the unbeliever sees his destination. Ṭabrisī says its exposure both enables punishment and displays its terror. Ibn ʿĀshūr likens it to a king's arrival and cites 39:71 for its opened gates; he also accepts the supernatural dragging report. Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers appearance and cites 50:22 for unveiling; Elmalılı says the sight can no longer be denied or avoided. Corpus Coranicum cross-references 79:36. [21 notes: ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم وأبي علي); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI(الأصوليون); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI(قيل); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI+; ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 89:23/q01/p3 The verse personifies Hell's arrival to embody the terror of the afterlife. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ calls this Quranic animation and personification, comparing 99:1, 18:100, 79:36, 26:91 and 78:21; she declines to investigate reports whose particulars the Quran does not hint at. [1 notes: BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-]
- 89:23/q01/p4 In an inward reading, Hell is the fire of nature exposed to those punished. — Al-Qāshānī treats the scene as exposure of the fire of nature. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 89:23/q02 What does the written form of 'was brought' convey?  [وَجِا۟ىٓءَ · rhetoric]
- 89:23/q02/p1 Its extra alif marks an exceptional, awesome, threatening appearance. — Al-Burhān links the extraordinary visible coming to Hell's exposure in 79:36 and being seen from afar in 25:12. Al-Itqān lists the written form in al-Zumar and al-Fajr; al-Marrākushī says such extra letters, including those in nabāʾ and bi-aydin, can signal grandeur, awe, warning and divine power (39:69; 51:47). [3 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN(المراكشي)]

## 89:23/q03 Why is 'came' used here?  [وَجِا۟ىٓءَ · rhetoric]
- 89:23/q03/p1 Jāʾa depicts an arrival visible or treated as witnessed. — Al-Burhān contrasts atā for unseen crops or earth with jāʾa for a visible command. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 89:23/q04 How is the opening passive construction parsed?  [وَجِا۟ىٓءَ يَوْمَئِذٍۭ بِجَهَنَّمَ · grammar]
turns on: Which expression fills the deputy subject slot of the passive verb.
- 89:23/q04/p1 The prepositional phrase 'with Hell' occupies the passive deputy subject position; the first day expression is governed by 'was brought'. — Al-Naḥḥās says the verb is passive and the following noun is the deputy subject; al-Samīn specifies the first yawm's governing verb. [2 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR]
- 89:23/q04/p2 The day expression can itself serve as the passive deputy subject. — Al-Naḥḥās also allows a verbal noun analysis; al-Samīn attributes the day as deputy subject to Makkī. [2 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR]

## 89:23/q05 Why is the bringing expressed passively?  [وَجِا۟ىٓءَ بِجَهَنَّمَ · rhetoric]
- 89:23/q05/p1 It foregrounds the terrifying act of bringing Hell over the identity of those who bring it. — Biqāʿī connects this focus to Hell's exposure in 79:36 and also says angels do the leading under God's power. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BIQAI]

## 89:23/q06 What do reports say about Hell's reins and those leading it?  [وَجِا۟ىٓءَ بِجَهَنَّمَ · reports]
turns on: The reported number of reins and whether seventy thousand angels attend each rein or the whole set; some sources add a muzzle.
- 89:23/q06/p1 Hell has seventy thousand reins, each led or pulled by seventy thousand angels. — Ibn Masʿūd's report occurs both as a Prophetic report in Muslim and, in Tirmidhī and Ibn Jarīr, as his own statement; Ibn Kathīr distinguishes these transmissions. Muqātil adds that it comes from five hundred years away and depicts fearful eyes, fiery mouths, mountain-sized maces and seven snake-like heads, citing 6:158 and 2:210. Variants say it roars, is set left of the Throne, strains or bolts toward the gathering and would burn it if unrestrained; ʿAlī's version extends the danger to heaven and earth. Ibn ʿĀshūr says afterlife events transcend ordinary custom. Sources vary between reins, bridles and leashes; Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes varying report wording. Al-Jishumī rejects the Hashwiyya's chain-dragging account. - 89:23/q06/p2 Hell is pulled by a million reins, each held by seventy thousand angels. — Al-Zajjāj cites this as a tafsīr report while linking its exposure to 26:91. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]
- 89:23/q06/p3 Seventy thousand angels lead Hell on seventy thousand reins. — The Abū Saʿīd, ʿAlī and Zayd ibn Aslam narratives phrase the numbers this way; some also say it lurches or breaks loose and is caught. Their wording differs from the report of seventy thousand angels for each rein. - 89:23/q06/p4 Hell is led muzzled or bridled. — Abū Wāʾil says it is brought with a muzzle or bridle, and Qatāda says it is muzzled. [2 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(أبو وائل); TAB(قتادة)]

## 89:23/q07 What else happens as Hell is brought?  [وَجِا۟ىٓءَ بِجَهَنَّمَ · reports]
- 89:23/q07/p1 Angels restrain the raging Fire until it stands before creation, often left of the Throne. — Muqātil says angels restrain it from creatures until it stands upright. Ibn Masʿūd and Muqātil's versions place the roaring Fire left of the Throne. Ibn al-Jawzī's Muqātil report gives that location. Reports from ʿAlī and Zayd say it lurches or escapes and would burn the assembly, or even heaven and earth, without divine restraint. [19 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(عبد الله بن مسعود ومقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(جماعة المفسرين); BAGHAWI(عبد الله بن مسعود ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود ومقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); QURTUBI(عبد الله بن مسعود,علي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود ومقاتل,علي عن النبي); DURR(أبو سعيد,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,زيد بن أسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو سعيد,زيد بن أسلم عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي); ALUSI-FULL(علي); TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]
- 89:23/q07/p2 A neck extends from Hell and selects tyrannical unbelievers. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the long report's wording varies. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 89:23/q08 What is said to lie over or under Hell?  [بِجَهَنَّمَ · reports]
- 89:23/q08/p1 The known sea lies over seven seas of fire and seven seas of water. — ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr supplies these numbers; Saʿīd ibn Abī al-Ḥasan says the sea forms a covering over Hell. [2 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(سعيد بن أبي الحسن,عبد الله بن عمرو)]

## 89:23/q09 What does Jahannam encompass here?  [بِجَهَنَّمَ · referent]
- 89:23/q09/p1 It denotes the whole Fire. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya states this in both versions of his commentary. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 89:23/q10 What is reported about the Prophet when this verse was revealed?  [وَجِا۟ىٓءَ بِجَهَنَّمَ · reports]
- 89:23/q10/p1 The Prophet's color changed or he became distressed after Gabriel recited the verse; he described Hell's arrival. — Abū Saʿīd and related reports say he paled and sweated, companions noticed, and ʿAlī asked how Hell would be brought. Versions describe Hell's charge at the assembly; Hell declares Muhammad's flesh forbidden to it, while others plead for themselves and he pleads for his community. Some versions mention 89:21–22. Al-Zamakhsharī states no grading; Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes varying wording. [18 notes: THALABI(أبو سعيد عن النبي); KASHSHAF(النبي); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو سعيد الخدري); QURTUBI(أبو سعيد الخدري,علي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري عن النبي,علي عن النبي); DURR(أبو سعيد,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,زيد بن أسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو سعيد,زيد بن أسلم عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI(علي); ALUSI-FULL(علي)]

## 89:23/q11 How are Paradise, Hell and the divine coming situated in this scene?  [يَوْمَئِذٍۭ بِجَهَنَّمَ · reports]
- 89:23/q11/p1 Paradise and Hell flank the gathering when God descends from His Throne to His Footstool to reckon with creation. — Qatāda is the reported authority in al-Ṭabarī's two versions. [2 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة)]

## 89:23/q12 Why does the verse mention Hell here?  [بِجَهَنَّمَ · rhetoric]
- 89:23/q12/p1 The warning addresses people who ignored admonition, so Hell is named while Paradise is also brought near. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 26:90–91 for the paired scenes. Iṣlāḥī says the sūra chiefly addresses active adversaries of Islam and seeing Hell brings despair. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 89:23/q13 What time does the first 'that day' specify?  [يَوْمَئِذٍۭ · grammar]
- 89:23/q13/p1 It is the day when the earth is crushed, and it modifies 'was brought'. — Ibn ʿĀshūr ties the implied day to the preceding scene of repeated crushing in 89:21; al-Samīn makes the verb its governor. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 89:23/q14 How does the second 'that day' attach to the preceding time clause?  [يَوْمَئِذٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the appositive shares the earlier temporal phrase's governor, requires it repeated, or has a separate governor.
- 89:23/q14/p1 It substitutes or apposes the earlier 'when the earth is crushed', with 'remembers' governing both. — Al-Rāzī supplies the temporal condition: when the earlier events occur, then the human remembers. Al-Bayḍāwī speaks of both day expressions substituting for the earlier clause. Al-Zamakhsharī's analysis is attributed to Sībawayh; al-Ālūsī prefers the shared governor, and Ibn ʿĀshūr uses it. Corpus Coranicum points elsewhere for yawm idhin. [11 notes: RAZI; BAYDAWI; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; CORPUSCORANICUM; ALUSI+]
- 89:23/q14/p2 The second day expression is an appositive, but its governing verb is understood as repeated. — Abū Ḥayyān calls this the common analysis and al-Ālūsī reports it from al-Baḥr. Al-Ālūsī's full version calls it apparently stronger, though his shorter note prefers the shared governor. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI(البحر); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 89:23/q14/p3 The earlier 'when' is governed by 'says', while 'remembers' governs the second 'that day'. — Al-Samīn reports this parsing from Abū al-Baqāʾ. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]

## 89:23/q15 Why is 'that day' repeated and placed first?  [يَوْمَئِذٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 89:23/q15/p1 The repetition reconnects a distant temporal clause and heightens dread; fronting adds emphasis and suspense. — Biqāʿī says it clarifies the time after a long interval while people see what awaits the thankful and ungrateful. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it summary followed by detail after a long interruption. [4 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 89:23/q16 Who is 'the human' here?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the generic noun points to the unbelieving audience broadly or a named individual.
- 89:23/q16/p1 It is the unbeliever, especially the disbelieving Meccans, or one absorbed in worldly life. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Baghawī explicitly call him the unbeliever. Al-Qurṭubī includes anyone whose chief concern was worldly life. Ibn ʿĀshūr links the noun to the unbeliever in 89:15, repeated after a distant antecedent; he says al-insān is grammatically generic but customarily points to the Meccan disbelievers, comparing 96:6–7, 75:3, and 90:4–5. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 89:23/q16/p2 It is Umayya ibn Khalaf. — Al-Damghānī identifies the human in the earlier test passage this way; Ibn al-Jawzī reports Muqātil's identification here and says his repentance comes too late. [2 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل)]

## 89:23/q17 What does the human remember or realize?  [يَتَذَكَّرُ ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether remembering means recollection of deeds, taking warning from the revealed truth, or repentance.
- 89:23/q17/p1 The person recalls worldly deeds, sins, neglect of duty and missed preparation for the afterlife. — Al-Ṭabarī emphasizes neglected obedience and good works. Ibn Shajara speaks of deeds sent ahead; Ibn ʿAṭiyya of disobedience and missed good. Al-Rāzī says worldly effort was wrongly devoted to this life. Ibn Kathīr includes earlier and recent deeds. Biqāʿī says attachment to wealth brought loss but could not alter one's allotment. Bursawī and al-Ālūsī say the deeds' effects or embodied forms may make them visible; al-Ālūsī adds God may recall them to mind. Quṭb ties the regret to hoarding, oppression, orphans and the poor (89:17–20). Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses the forgotten test of good and evil; Iṣlāḥī links verse 24 to failure to prepare. [27 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI(ابن شجرة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 89:23/q17/p2 The person finally takes admonition, recognizes the truth of God's warnings and regrets former choices. — Al-Rāzī cites the wish to return in 6:27. Al-Bayḍāwī says the ugliness of sins becomes clear. Al-Ālūsī says signs of God's power and majesty prompt the admonition. Al-Ḥasan says the person recognizes the long, deathless hereafter as better than this life. Elmalılı says formerly ignored truths become clear. Abū Ḥayyān contrasts the helpful admonition possible in worldly life. [20 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); DURR(الحسن); DURR-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI]
- 89:23/q17/p3 The remembering is repentance or the display of repentance, too late to help. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk says the person remembers and seeks repentance; al-Ḥasan is also reported to interpret it thus. Al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī say repentance is displayed but cannot be obtained or accepted then. Al-Rāzī argues regret for wrongdoing itself, rather than only fear of punishment, counts as repentance. Ibn al-Jawzī's Muqātil report includes Umayya ibn Khalaf. Durr connects it with wishing one had prepared in this world (89:24). [24 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); NAHHAS(الضحاك); MAWARDI(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI(الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI(الحسن); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك); BURSEVI; ALUSI(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 89:23/q17/p4 In an inward reading, entrenched false beliefs are recalled but block any benefit from the truth. — Al-Qāshānī says people realize their beliefs contradicted reality; Bursawī reports him as saying God appears in majesty and angels in the punishment their denial merited. [2 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(القاشاني)]

## 89:23/q18 Why can remembrance no longer help?  [وَأَنَّىٰ لَهُ ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the unavailable thing is the benefit of recollection generally or repentance specifically.
- 89:23/q18/p1 The question denies benefit because the time for repentance, action and useful warning has passed. — Al-Ṭabarī says the human neglected obedience and good works. Ibn ʿAbbās glosses it as asking how he can benefit. Al-Zamakhsharī says the literal recollection and denied recollection require 'benefit' in sense. Jishumī says obligation has ended. Biqāʿī and Quṭb say the afterlife is for recompense, yielding regret, pain and lost opportunity, not deeds. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says there is no return to make good works. Elmalılı adds longing and loss. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ sees annā as remoteness and layta as an impossible or distant wish. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a parenthetical interruption before the person's speech; al-Itqān says the interrogation treats benefit as implausible. [52 notes: TAB(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الضحاك,قيل); THALABI; MAWARDI(ابن شجرة,الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة,الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI(الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI; QURTUBI(الزمخشري); QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 89:23/q18/p2 It asks where repentance could be obtained at that point. — Al-Zajjāj and those quoting him focus on the impossibility of repentance then. Al-Wāḥidī compares the analogous question in 44:13. This is a narrower gloss of the same rhetorical denial. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-QT; BAGHAWI(الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج)]

## 89:23/q19 How is 'and where for him is remembrance' parsed?  [وَأَنَّىٰ لَهُ ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · grammar]
- 89:23/q19/p1 'Where' is the fronted predicate, 'remembrance' its delayed subject, and 'for him' attaches to the predicate. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī state this analysis; Ibn ʿĀshūr also glosses annā as 'where'. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; IBNASHUR]

## 89:23/q20 Must an omitted word such as 'benefit' be supplied in the question?  [وَأَنَّىٰ لَهُ ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the implicit limitation to beneficial remembrance requires an ellipsis or follows directly from the lām.
- 89:23/q20/p1 Yes: supply 'benefit' to reconcile his remembering with the question's denial. — Al-Zamakhsharī says without an omitted genitive the two clauses conflict; al-Rāzī and al-Bayḍāwī agree, the latter comparing the command to remind. Al-Ālūsī reports this as an alternative; al-Burhān lists the verse among elliptical objects intended from context, alongside 38:81, 13:26, 11:43, 27:59, 6:22. [9 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; QURTUBI(الزمخشري); QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري); BAYDAWI; BURHAN; ALUSI(قيل); IBNASHUR]
- 89:23/q20/p2 No omitted noun is needed: the wording itself denies useful recollection. — Al-Ālūsī says restricting the lām to what is beneficial resolves the apparent conflict; his full note allows the omission as an alternative. He rejects reading the question as merely a description of failed reflection during worldly life. [3 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI+; ALUSI-]

## 89:23/q21 Does the verse bear on whether God must accept repentance?  [وَأَنَّىٰ لَهُ ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · theology]
turns on: Whether late remorse qualifies as repentance and whether any rational duty of acceptance extends past death or the end of moral obligation.
- 89:23/q21/p1 It supports the view that acceptance of repentance is not rationally obligatory. — Al-Rāzī says the sinner's genuine regret for wrong as wrong counts as repentance despite nonacceptance; fear alone would not suffice. Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursawī cite the unhelpful late repentance. Al-Ālūsī's counterargument defines repentance as regret for sin as sin and resolve not to repeat when able, with no worldly-life limit in the definition; al-Ḍaḥḥāk calls the recollection repentance. [10 notes: RAZI; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك,المعتزلة)]
- 89:23/q21/p2 Reason obliges God to accept repentance, within its applicable time and conditions. — Al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī report the Muʿtazilite doctrine, tied by al-Ālūsī to God's doing what is most beneficial. An objection limits the obligation to the period before legal duties end. [4 notes: RAZI(المعتزلة); ALUSI(المعتزلة,قيل); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 89:23/q21/p3 The verse does not prove anything about acceptance of repentance during worldly life because this recollection is not true repentance. — Al-Irshād argues the person knows repentance must occur before death, as the wish in 89:24 shows. Bursawī quotes the objection while arguing the contrary; al-Ālūsī reports the same response. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI(قيل); BURSEVI-]

## 89:23/q22 How is yatadhakkaru pronounced or analyzed in reading traditions?  [يَتَذَكَّرُ · readings]
- 89:23/q22/p1 The tāʾ may assimilate into dhāl; other variation is prosodic without changing root, voice or syntax. — Al-Naḥḥās allows assimilation. Al-Burhān lists the tāʾ form across 13:19, 20:3, 35:37, 38:29, 39:27, 40:13 and 79:35. Qirāʾāt-ER notes a rounded transition before long ī that leaves morphology unchanged. [3 notes: NAHHAS; BURHAN; QIRAAT-ER]

## 89:23/q23 How is al-dhikrā read?  [ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · readings]
- 89:23/q23/p1 Several canonical readers use imāla, bringing its sound closer to ḥayātī. — Corpus Coranicum relates this to the approximate rhyme of the paired passages. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 89:23/q24 What does the scene contribute to the sūra's warning?  [* · links]
- 89:23/q24/p1 The delayed remembrance answers the earlier test of wealth, status and treatment of others, urging heed before judgment. — Biqāʿī links ʿĀd and God's watchfulness (89:14) to the call to heed before 89:21–23. Ibn ʿĀshūr says status is no measure of merit: Ṭarafa's verse voices the old boast; Quraysh and Tamīm asked that poor believers be expelled, an error addressed in 6:52. Earlier peoples shared it and were punished. He notes most commentators leave the passage's connection unexplained apart from al-Zamakhsharī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya. Quṭb cites inheritance, wealth, orphans and the poor (89:17–20). Iṣlāḥī depicts the helpless wealthy and says 89:24 explains the realization. Study Quran adds a report that believers too regret times they remembered God, because that remembrance becomes a source of regret. [9 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(طرفة); QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 89:23/q24/p2 The judgment scene links to warnings in other passages. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites 40:32–33 on people turning away with no divine protector. Corpus Coranicum compares remembering with 79:35 and points to discussion of dhikr, dhikrā and tadhkira at 73:19 and Jahannam at 78:21; it also compares the bringing of Hell with 79:36. [4 notes: BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 89:23/q25 How does this verse relate to the sūra's proposed literary formation?  [* · links]
- 89:23/q25/p1 Verses 23–24 are later insertions that expand an earlier judgment unit and answer its paired human-response scenes. — Corpus Coranicum calls 23–24 and 27–30 later additions to the four-verse eschatological unit, framing the oath's duality as an antithesis. The first inserted clause explicitly names Hell to explain the punishment of 89:25–26; 23b–24 pairs the human's response to Hell with worldly responses in 89:15–16. Its discussion notes approximate rhymes involving dhikrā and ḥayātī. Another note says the three inserted sections use direct speech: human responses to allotment and Hell give way to God's address to the trusting soul and servants. [6 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 89:23/q26 Where is a verse division made?  [بِجَهَنَّمَ · sciences]
- 89:23/q26/p1 Damascus, Ḥimṣ, Mecca and Medina divide after Jahannam. — Neuwirth prefers this division because the verse is unusually long and has two independent main clauses. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; CORPUSCORANICUM(Neuwirth)+]

## 89:23/q27 What other reports describe the gathering surrounding this verse?  [* · reports]
- 89:23/q27/p1 Reports describe a widened earth, the gathering of nations and intercession through the prophets. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites Ibn ʿAbbās on earth stretched like hide and a herald calling those who praised God in every state; Ubayy ibn Kaʿb's transmission from Abū Hurayra describes the people seeking intercession until Muhammad prostrates and intercedes. She declines chain investigation because the Quran gives no hint of these details. Kür'an Yolu says the coming of God or His command and the angels' ranks are unseen in nature and calls for belief in accountability and action. [5 notes: BINTSHATI(أبي بن كعب,ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 89:23/q28 What does the later human lament imply about choice and regret?  [* · interpretation]
- 89:23/q28/p1 It voices remorse over a lost chance to choose good and prepare for life. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports al-Zamakhsharī's inference that the lament implies freedom to obey or sin rather than compulsion; she declines the wider debate. She says the speech recognizes that the past cannot return and that annā and layta mark a distant or impossible wish. [3 notes: BINTSHATI(الزمخشري)]
