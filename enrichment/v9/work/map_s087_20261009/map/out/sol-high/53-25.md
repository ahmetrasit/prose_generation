# 53:25: 16 questions

## 53:25/q01 What do 'the later' and 'the first' denote here?  [ٱلْءَاخِرَةُ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · referent]
- 53:25/q01/p1 The afterlife and this worldly life, including what each contains. — Rāzī says al-ākhirah qualifies an understood 'life' or 'abode.' Ibn ʿAṭiyyah calls them two abodes. Farrāʾ includes the reward of each; Ibn ʿĀshūr says their contents are included and compares the two easts and wests of 55:17. Elmalılı calls them the first and final stages of human life. [5 notes: FARRA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:25/q02 How is ٱلْءَاخِرَةُ related to the form and sense of 'last'?  [ٱلْءَاخِرَةُ · grammar]
- 53:25/q02/p1 It is the feminine of 'last,' whose apparent active participle has no attested simple verb and denotes true finality. — Rāzī illustrates a hypothetical base by أخرته فتأخر but says usage does not establish the expected form. He explains that completed pastness would need a true endpoint, while تأخر can express a reflexive effort or claim to being last. He treats آخر as a participial form intensified as أفعل; he distinguishes a connected final part from the separate thing that comes after it. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:25/q03 How is ٱلْأُولَىٰ formed and inflected?  [ٱلْأُولَىٰ · grammar]
- 53:25/q03/p1 It is the feminine فعلى of أول, an exceptional أفعل form; Arabic also has أولة, accounting for different inflection. — Rāzī says every أفعل/فعلى pair calls for a base, though the base here is unattested. أول has neither a verb nor a participle: an agent precedes its act, so an act cannot make the first thing first. His reply to سبق/سابق treats 'preceder' as resemblance rather than derivation; تقدم is answered through his discussion of تأخر. He rejects derivation from آل ('making another first') as far-fetched. Arabs use both أولة and أولى, the latter more common and the Qurʾanic form; أولة permits tanwin while أولى is diptote, explaining أولا with or without tanwin. He calls أولة etymological but weaker. He also reports a proposal that أفعل is diptote only if its feminine is exclusively فعلى; a ة feminine would remove that condition. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]

## 53:25/q04 How do 'first' and 'last' differ from 'before' and 'after'?  [ٱلْءَاخِرَةُ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · meaning]
- 53:25/q04/p1 First and last express endpoints, whereas before and after express a wider relation. — Rāzī says أول and آخر have أفعل/فاعل morphology unlike قبل and بعد. آخر demands an afterness beyond which nothing follows; something after the first may still be intermediate. Arriving after everyone explains being last, but being last does not explain every use of 'after.' [3 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:25/q05 What does placing 'for God' first convey?  [فَلِلَّهِ · rhetoric]
- 53:25/q05/p1 It marks God's exclusive ownership of the two lives. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the fronting makes explicit the further restriction implied by the previous verse, 53:24: the two lives belong to God rather than people. Qushayrī describes His sovereignty as complete and creatures as deficient; Ṭabāṭabāʾī denies any partner in either dominion. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; QUSHAYRI; TABATABAI]

## 53:25/q06 Why is the afterlife mentioned before this life?  [ٱلْءَاخِرَةُ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the ordering is primarily semantic, primarily a verse-ending choice, or serves both; Biqāʿī disputes the rhyme explanation.
- 53:25/q06/p1 To give the afterlife priority because the argument centers on hopes or consequences there. — Biqāʿī says pleasures, attained wishes, and deprivation come to full realization there, where idolaters expect their gods to secure prosperity; only followers of guidance who resist desire attain their wishes there, while nobody gets every worldly wish. He explicitly says the order is semantic because al-ukhrā could also have supplied the rhyme. Ālūsī stresses rebutting opponents' hope of winning in the afterlife, which leads into the next verse. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL+]
- 53:25/q06/p2 To preserve the verse ending. — Itqān calls the order reversed for the verse ending and compares 'We gathered you and the earlier peoples' in 77:38. Biqāʿī argues against rhyme as the explanation, observing that al-ukhrā would also rhyme. [1 notes: ITQAN; BIQAI-FULL-]
- 53:25/q06/p3 Both to emphasize the later life and to fit the verse ending. — Abū Ḥayyān says the afterlife comes later chronologically and that the ending fits, comparing 92:13. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the order directs believers' concern toward the more important afterlife while fitting the ending. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:25/q07 What does God's ownership of both lives entail?  [* · interpretation]
- 53:25/q07/p1 God alone owns and governs both worlds; what anyone receives or does there depends on His will and permission. — Muqātil relates this to resurrection and recompense (6:12; 53:31). Farrāʾ includes rewards. Naḥḥās, Thaʿlabī, Kashshāf, Qurṭubī, Bayḍāwī, and Abū Ḥayyān emphasize God's granting or withholding as He wills. Qushayrī adds that He creates both and has complete sovereignty while creatures lack it. Wāḥidī's Basīṭ and Baghawī say nobody possesses anything without permission; Wāḥidī's Wajīz and Ibn Kathīr say nothing occurs contrary to His will. Ibn ʿAṭiyyah adds power and authority; Nasafī adds His governance. Biqāʿī calls the entire dominion His; Elmalılı says human life begins by His will and the afterlife proceeds under His law. Ibn ʿĀshūr applies His governance to each world's inhabitants. Ṭabāṭabāʾī excludes a partner; Study Quran says pagans' conjectural picture effectively denies this mastery. [26 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; NAHHAS; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 53:25/q08 Does a person's wish determine what they receive in either life?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The explanations stress sovereign allocation, beneficial gifts to deserving believers, or recompense by deeds; Jishumī prefers Abū ʿAlī's more comprehensive account.
- 53:25/q08/p1 No; God grants or withholds according to His will and permission, so wishes alone secure nothing. — Wāḥidī's Basīṭ explicitly rebuts the unbeliever's wish. Kashshāf and Bayḍāwī say no one may dictate to God; Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān similarly set His will above desire. Biqāʿī cites the returning person's false hope for good in 41:50 as proof of subjection to the sole sovereign. Bursevī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī make ownership the reason wishes are not guaranteed. Ālūsī calls this an explanation of the previous denial. Islahi says the question of wishes expresses amazement at the preceding argument, while desire cannot replace truth or override divine law. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the clause adds the restriction implicit in 53:24. [14 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 53:25/q08/p2 God gives beneficial worldly and otherworldly goods, but afterlife success belongs to deserving believers. — Abū ʿAlī names religion, wealth, prophethood, and Paradise among the possible benefits; Jishumī explicitly prefers this comprehensive reading. Elmalılı says final good requires following revealed proof and rules, with practical grounding rather than bare ideals. Biqāʿī says followers of guidance who resist desire attain their wishes in the afterlife, unlike idolaters expecting intercession; worldly wishes are never all fulfilled. [4 notes: ELMALILI; BIQAI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو علي)+]
- 53:25/q08/p3 No; deeds are recompensed, so wishing does not cancel reward or punishment. — Jishumī reports this as another explanation: God rewards obedience and punishes disobedience. Rāzī reports punishment in this life or the next for one who chooses a desired deity and cites 53:26 against hoped-for intercession. Islahi says false hopes collapse when judgment follows the balance of good and evil deeds, ending in Paradise or Hell; he also says conjecture cannot defeat real reward and punishment. Muqātil links ownership to resurrection and recompense (6:12; 53:31). [5 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; MUQATIL]

## 53:25/q09 What does the verse imply about intercession?  [* · theology]
- 53:25/q09/p1 Intercession is subject to God's permission and acceptance; idols and other beings cannot provide it independently. — Muqātil places the declaration after people hoped angels would intercede. Nasafī says God grants intercession only to whom He wills and approves. Rāzī says 53:26 answers the claim that al-Lāt and al-ʿUzzā are angelic intercessors; the idols rest on conjecture and desire. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the idolaters' claims in 39:3 and 10:18, arguing even honored angels need God's leave and acceptance for a recipient, while earthly stone idols have still less claim. He says the transition makes the gravity of intercession clear. Islahi and Kur'an Yolu also deny independent intercession or change to God's decisions. Asad says omnipotence and omniscience leave God in need of no mediator. [11 notes: MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 53:25/q10 What does this ownership imply for prophethood and guidance?  [* · interpretation]
- 53:25/q10/p1 God gives prophethood and guidance to those He chooses, rather than to anyone who wishes for them. — Māwardī grounds God's choice of prophet in His ownership of heaven and earth. Nasafī includes prophethood among grants by divine will and approval. Rāzī reports an answer to unbelievers who inferred believers' inferior guidance from their lack of wealth: God distributes both wealth and guidance as He wills, citing 46:11. Abū ʿAlī includes religion and prophethood among beneficial gifts. Kur'an Yolu reports Ṭabarī's view that Muhammad's high rank came by grace rather than his wish. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو علي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî)]

## 53:25/q11 Whom does 'the human' in the prior verse mean on one reading of this passage?  [* · referent]
- 53:25/q11/p1 Muhammad, whose high rank was God's gift rather than the product of his own wish. — Kur'an Yolu reports this as Ṭabarī's reading of 53:24 in light of this verse and the next. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî)]

## 53:25/q12 How does the verse connect to the surrounding argument?  [* · links]
turns on: Rāzī offers several contextual readings: idol worship and intercession, punishment, consolation, proof of resurrection, and the wealth objection.
- 53:25/q12/p1 It answers wishes grounded in idols, conjecture, and hoped-for intercession. — Muqātil connects it to people's hope of angelic intercession. Rāzī relates al-Lāt and al-ʿUzzā's alleged authority to conjecture and desire and says 53:26 answers their supposed intercession. Ibn ʿĀshūr says 53:24's denial leads naturally to the idolaters' claims in 39:3 and 10:18 and then the serious matter of intercession. Study Quran describes the sūra's second section as rebuking Quraysh's idol worship, ignorance, conjecture, and appeal to angels. Islahi says idolaters follow baseless wishes despite clear guidance; Study Quran says their adapted god and hereafter effectively deny divine mastery. [8 notes: MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 53:25/q12/p2 It warns that selecting a desired deity cannot evade punishment in this life or the next. — Rāzī offers this explanation and reads the next verse, 53:26, as confirming that intercession will not avail. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:25/q12/p3 It consoles the Prophet that deniers remain under God's power despite rejecting his message. — Rāzī offers this after noting that Muhammad had explained his mission and divine unity without winning their belief. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:25/q12/p4 It concludes the preceding proof of revelation and divine unity by confirming resurrection. — Rāzī takes established revelation, including 53:4, as warrant for trusting Muhammad's report of resurrection; God's ownership of both lives closes the proof. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:25/q12/p5 It replies to the claim that believers' poverty shows they are not better guided. — Rāzī cites the unbelievers' claim in 46:11 and answers that God gives wealth and guidance as He wills in either life. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:25/q13 What is reported to have happened during the public recitation tied to this passage?  [* · reports]
- 53:25/q13/p1 Satan inserted praise of the exalted cranes; Meccan polytheists hoped for intercession, and both groups prostrated at the sūra's end for different objects. — Muqātil says the phrase was put on Muhammad's tongue, making polytheists rejoice over hoped-for angelic intercession. He says Muhammad and believers prostrated to affirm God, polytheists at their idols, and the elderly Walīd ibn al-Mughīra lifted dirt to his forehead in prostration. [2 notes: MUQATIL]

## 53:25/q14 What follows from God's superiority over creatures for the claim that He has children?  [* · theology]
- 53:25/q14/p1 If offspring were possible, God would have a stronger claim to sons than the polytheists, though He is exalted above offspring. — Māwardī reasons from God's greater power over created beings while expressly distancing Him from the claim. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 53:25/q15 What grounds temporal priority and posteriority?  [* · theology]
- 53:25/q15/p1 Temporal order has reality through God; without Him there would be no before or after. — Rāzī cites, without grading, the Prophet's saying 'Do not revile time, for time is God' and argues that God grounds the temporal order that people would otherwise revile. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 53:25/q16 Who is addressed by the warning that wishes cannot override God's rule?  [* · interpretation]
- 53:25/q16/p1 Every individual, including members of later religious communities. — Islahi says the general 'man' of 53:24 has no exception. He names Arabs, Jews, Christians, and Muslims; he connects Jewish and Christian amāniyyu to al-Baqarah and Āl ʿImrān and says Muslims too devised beliefs contrary to Qur'an and Sunnah by following them. Desires do not alter reality or bias divine law. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
