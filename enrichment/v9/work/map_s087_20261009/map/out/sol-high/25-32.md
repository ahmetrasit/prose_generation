# 25:32: 30 questions

## 25:32/q01 Who made the objection?  [ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the speech is attributed to Quraysh/polytheists or to Jews or Christians.
- 25:32/q01/p1 Quraysh or the Arab polytheists. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Zamakhsharī and Ibn ʿĀshūr identify Quraysh or polytheists; al-Ālūsī says the epithet condemns their disbelief, and Abū Ḥayyān calls this the apparent referent. [14 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); RAZI-FULL(الذين كفروا); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(المشركون); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 25:32/q01/p2 Jews. — Al-Māwardī and others report that Jews compared the Torah; Ibn ʿĀshūr says this would be slander, and Abū Ḥayyān calls this identification pointless. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(اليهود); ITQAN(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 25:32/q01/p3 Jews or Christians. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this wider alternative and rejects their alleged premise about their scriptures. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 25:32/q01/p4 The disbelievers, without a narrower identification. — The early reports put the demand in unbelievers' mouths; Ibn ʿAbbās relates the polytheists' challenge and Qatāda compares Moses and Jesus. [17 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); FARRA(الذين كفروا); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,السدي,قتادة); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الذين كفروا); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الذين كفروا); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ITQAN(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN]

## 25:32/q02 What does lawlā mean here?  [لَوْلَا · meaning]
- 25:32/q02/p1 Why not? or if only, expressing a demand. — Yaḥyā glosses it as why not; Biqāʿī gives why not or if only; Bursevī calls it exhortation. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 25:32/q03 What was the objectors' demand and its motive?  [لَوْلَا نُزِّلَ عَلَيْهِ ٱلْقُرْءَانُ جُمْلَةًۭ وَٰحِدَةًۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the demand is read simply as comparison with earlier scriptures, as an allegation of fabrication, or as a theological premise.
- 25:32/q03/p1 They demanded the entire Qur'an in one revelation, citing earlier books. — Reports variously name Torah alone, Torah and Gospel, or Torah, Gospel and Psalms; Ibn ʿAbbās specifies one verse, two verses, or a sūra at a time as what they resented. Al-Wāḥidī reports the verse's occasion as al-Kalbī does. [27 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); FARRA(الذين كفروا); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,السدي,قتادة); NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الذين كفروا); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الذين كفروا); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الذين كفروا); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(المشركون); STUDYQURAN]
- 25:32/q03/p2 They insinuated that gradual composition showed Muhammad invented the Qur'an. — Biqāʿī says they wanted a complete revelation as proof of divine origin; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says they called the event-tied discourse scattered and fabricated, while Asad and Iṣlāḥī report suspicions of personal or political convenience and other coauthors. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 25:32/q03/p3 The demand was hostile evasion rather than a genuine test. — Zamakhsharī says it reveals flight from truth; Biqāʿī sees concealed envy and denial of God's signs. Kuran Yolu says they protected beliefs and social interests, and modern analogues make innocent-looking demands; Naḥḥās says they lacked a proof against him. [9 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 25:32/q03/p4 They supposed a divine book should set out a whole ordered religion at once. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reconstructs their premise without supposing they believed in or invoked earlier scriptures; Gabriel could recite the entire Qur'an in one sitting, even though recitation takes time. [4 notes: TABATABAI]

## 25:32/q04 Was the objection cogent as a test of revelation?  [لَوْلَا نُزِّلَ عَلَيْهِ ٱلْقُرْءَانُ جُمْلَةًۭ وَٰحِدَةًۭ · theology]
turns on: Whether miraculousness requires the delivery pattern, or whether situational eloquence provides further evidence.
- 25:32/q04/p1 No: its miraculousness is independent of the speed of revelation. — Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī, Biqāʿī, Ālūsī and Elmalılı note that opponents failed to match even a short sūra, then fought; gradual challenge can make the proof clearer. Qurṭubī adds that God could teach all at once but His wisdom cannot be arraigned. [16 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 25:32/q04/p2 Matching each revelation to its circumstance is part of its inimitable proof. — An argument reported by Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr's grandfather says each passage fits its occasion; Ālūsī replies that a book revealed once could anticipate later events, and its descent to the lowest heaven would otherwise compromise inimitability. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الجد الوزير); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 25:32/q05 What does nuzzila imply here?  [نُزِّلَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the doubled verb carries necessary distributive force here.
- 25:32/q05/p1 Here the doubled form means simply 'was sent down,' like anzala. — Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Bursevī, Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr avoid a clash with 'all at once'; Abū Ḥayyān agrees the two forms can be synonymous but rejects making this a special necessity. Samīn questions the assumed intensive implication. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم)]
- 25:32/q05/p2 Tanzīl retains a sense of gradual descent, but 'all at once' means one complete reception. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes inzāl from tanzīl and says a full oral recital can be received in one session while its components arrive in sequence; Biqāʿī says the intensive form evokes the gradual mode they criticize. Grammarians cited by al-Sāmarrāʾī object to a rigid distinction using this verse. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(قسم)]

## 25:32/q06 Why is the verb passive?  [نُزِّلَ · rhetoric]
- 25:32/q06/p1 The objectors scarcely acknowledge divine revelation. — Biqāʿī says the passive avoids admitting that God sent the Qur'an. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 25:32/q07 How is 'jumlatan wāḥidatan' constructed?  [جُمْلَةًۭ وَٰحِدَةًۭ · grammar]
- 25:32/q07/p1 Jumlatan is an accusative circumstantial expression, meaning all together. — Ṭabrisī, Nasafī, Samīn and Ālūsī parse the phrase as a ḥāl of the Qur'an; Ālūsī calls wāḥidah emphatic and rejects construing it as one collective installment. [4 notes: TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 25:32/q08 How is qurʾān read without a hamza?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانُ · readings]
- 25:32/q08/p1 The hamza-less form is a phonetic simplification with the same meaning. — Qiraat-Er records the hamza-less qurān without semantic change. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(hmz)]

## 25:32/q09 Where does the objectors' speech end, and what does kadhālika point to?  [كَذَٰلِكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'thus' belongs to the quotation or to the divine reply, and whether its comparison is with prior books or the implied gradual descent.
- 25:32/q09/p1 It starts God's reply and points to the gradual revelation implied by the objection. — Al-Farrāʾ, Zajjāj, Naḥḥās, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Rāzī, Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn ʿĀshūr and others take the pause after wāḥidah; Zamakhsharī and Rāzī explain that the demand implies a complaint about portions. Naḥḥās objects that a pause after kadhālika would require an unmentioned comparison; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the other view weak. [20 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; NAHHAS+; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس)+]
- 25:32/q09/p2 It completes their speech, comparing the requested descent with earlier scriptures. — Al-Farrāʾ gives this parse; al-Wāḥidī says it requires elision, while Rāzī says it need not. Ibn al-Anbārī acknowledges transmitted support yet prefers the first pause. Biqāʿī rejects an earlier-scripture referent, saying the Torah also descended in stages. [8 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(قوم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; NAHHAS-; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الأنباري,النحاس)-; BIQAI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 25:32/q09/p3 Kadhālika refers to the Qur'an's good gradual manner, without settling the dialogue boundary. — Biqāʿī says the advantages of this manner expose the critics' narrowness. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 25:32/q10 What grammatical role does kadhālika have?  [كَذَٰلِكَ · grammar]
turns on: Its syntactic case and the reconstruction of the omitted governing clause.
- 25:32/q10/p1 It is an accusative manner expression attached to an implied 'We revealed it.' — Samīn allows a circumstantial accusative or qualifier of an omitted verbal noun; Bursevī makes it the adjective of an emphasized verbal noun; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it substitute for an absolute object; Ālūsī allows adverbial or circumstantial force. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 25:32/q10/p2 It is nominative: 'the matter is thus.' — Samīn gives this parse, with the following purpose attached to an elided act. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 25:32/q10/p3 It is an oath answer. — Samīn reports Abū Ḥātim's proposal and judges it weak, comparing al-Akhfash on 6:113. [0 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو حاتم)-]

## 25:32/q11 How does li-nuthabbita attach?  [لِنُثَبِّتَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the lām is causal or an oath particle.
- 25:32/q11/p1 The lām marks purpose and attaches to an implied act of revelation. — Naḥḥās, Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn and Ibn ʿĀshūr supply an omitted verb or action; when pausing after 'thus,' Naḥḥās relates it to the subsequent arrangement. [6 notes: NAHHAS; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 25:32/q11/p2 It is an oath lām promising to strengthen. — Abū Ḥātim reconstructs an oath with omitted emphatic nūn; Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī reject it as extremely weak, citing al-Akhfash's disputed treatment of 68:43; Samīn makes the parallel at 6:113. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حاتم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(أبو حاتم)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حاتم)-]

## 25:32/q12 Is there a variant reading of li-nuthabbita?  [لِنُثَبِّتَ · readings]
- 25:32/q12/p1 ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd reads li-yuthabbita with yāʾ, making God the subject. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn and Ālūsī transmit the reading. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله); SAMIN-DURR(عبد الله); ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله)]

## 25:32/q13 What do tathbīt and fuʾād mean?  [لِنُثَبِّتَ فُؤَادَكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether 'heart' here is construed broadly as the perceiving self or specifically as intellect.
- 25:32/q13/p1 Tathbīt makes firm or reassures; fuʾād is the heart, the perceiving self. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explains literal fixity and figurative certainty, citing 14:24 and 4:66; Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts thabāt with disappearance and says tathbīt expresses gradual establishing. [4 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 25:32/q13/p2 Fuʾād here means the intellect held steady in the Qur'an's words and meanings. — Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies freedom from confusion in comprehension. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 25:32/q14 Whose hearts benefit from gradual revelation?  [فُؤَادَكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the direct address carries a communal benefit as well.
- 25:32/q14/p1 The Prophet's heart is addressed; the benefit also reaches believers and learners. — Ibn Qutaybah says the address includes the believers; Ibn Kathīr says the response strengthens believers' hearts; Ibn ʿĀshūr and Iṣlāḥī describe willing learners; al-Naḥḥās says answers strengthen both his heart and theirs. [6 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; NAHHAS; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 25:32/q14/p2 The address is specifically to Muhammad. — The early glosses and Ibn ʿĀshūr's turn from the objectors to the Prophet take the second-person address literally. [7 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 25:32/q15 Why did the Qur'an come gradually?  [كَذَٰلِكَ لِنُثَبِّتَ بِهِۦ فُؤَادَكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Which wisdom is emphasized: retention and reassurance, practical instruction and law, renewed proof and angelic contact, or an inward account.
- 25:32/q15/p1 Successive revelation steadied the Prophet and made learning and retention possible. — Ibn ʿAbbās says each verse was learned before the next; Zamakhsharī and later commentators compare manageable portions with an overwhelming whole, stressing Muhammad's illiteracy. Reports add repeated comfort, Gabriel's visits, reassurance amid hostility, and benefit to the community. - 25:32/q15/p2 The divine answer highlights two linked ends: settling the Prophet and clarifying truth for people. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads this verse with the next answer as two reasons; the teaching is applied at the moment of need, illustrated by a doctor instructing beside a patient. [5 notes: TABATABAI]
- 25:32/q15/p3 Gradual revelation enabled abrogation, timely law, and responses to occasions. — Ibn Qutaybah says whole delivery would precede occasions, impose all duties at once, and prevent abrogation; al-Naḥḥās says earlier rulings can operate before replacement. Rāzī, Biqāʿī and others add paced legislation; Ibn Jurayj says God answered critics' words. 17:106 links division with public recitation over time. - 25:32/q15/p4 Its serial descent brought repeated angelic company and exceptional honor. — Māwardī mentions relief from loneliness; Rāzī connects Gabriel's repeated visits with courage amid harm and his role as envoy; Ibn Kathīr counts day/night, home/travel revelation as a higher prophetic station, and Burhān links Gabriel's visits to Ramadan generosity. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL]
- 25:32/q15/p5 Successive portions strengthen the miracle by repeated challenge and enduring coherence. — Rāzī, Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say opponents failed to answer individual portions; Asad finds consistent ordered teaching across twenty-three eventful years evidence of inspiration, comparing 4:82 and 39:23; Ibn ʿĀshūr says completed sūras retain order across time. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري); ASAD-NOTES]
- 25:32/q15/p6 Inwardly, stages of revelation match the Prophet's changing spiritual states and establish him on the complete path. — Qāshānī describes return from annihilation to subsistence, recurring clouding and repentance, divine discipline and attraction, diverse trials that perfect the Prophet and equip him to guide differing capacities; he cites reports of his Lord's education and seventy daily petitions for forgiveness. ## 25:32/q16 How does the Prophet's unlettered state bear on the explanation?  [لِنُثَبِّتَ بِهِۦ فُؤَادَكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the account of earlier scriptures supports the literate-versus-unlettered comparison.
- 25:32/q16/p1 It made oral, staged learning apt, unlike literate earlier prophets. — Thaʿlabī, Zamakhsharī, Ibn Fūrak and others compare unwritten Qur'an with the Torah's written delivery; Rāzī says an entire recital could invite error or forgetting. Burhān says God could grant instant memory, but need not; abrogation and questions add further reasons. [20 notes: THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مكي والرماني); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURHAN(ابن فورك,قيل); ITQAN(ابن فورك); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 25:32/q16/p2 That explanation fails because no prior scripture arrived as one complete book. — Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects the contrast with earlier prophets' literacy based on his account of the books' actual delivery. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 25:32/q17 How long did revelation span?  [كَذَٰلِكَ وَرَتَّلْنَٰهُ · sciences]
turns on: The reported length of the Meccan mission and whether dates are rounded.
- 25:32/q17/p1 About twenty-three years. — Yaḥyā, Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Ibn Kathīr, Asad and Kuran Yolu give twenty-three; some sources pair it with twenty or twenty-two. Burhān derives twenty-three from thirteen Meccan and ten Medinan years. [14 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI(النخعي والحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(ابن جريج); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 25:32/q17/p2 About twenty years. — Al-Ḥasan and others give twenty; Ibn Jurayj says the Prophet began at forty and died at sixty-two or sixty-three. Ibn ʿAbbās's heavenly report gives Gabriel twenty years. [19 notes: FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن); TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,الحسن); IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(النخعي والحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مكي والرماني); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL(ابن جريج); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن); ITQAN(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 25:32/q17/p3 Twenty-two years. — Ibn Jurayj and Study Quran include this estimate; al-Ṭabarī records death at sixty-two or sixty-three after a beginning at forty. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن جريج); STUDYQURAN; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج)]
- 25:32/q17/p4 Twenty-five years. — Burhān derives this from a fifteen-year Meccan period plus ten in Medina. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 25:32/q18 Did the Qur'an first descend whole to heaven?  [نُزِّلَ عَلَيْهِ ٱلْقُرْءَانُ · reports]
turns on: The reported celestial route and whether its intermediary stages are specified.
- 25:32/q18/p1 Yes: it reached the lowest heaven on the Night of Decree, then Gabriel brought portions to Muhammad. — Ibn ʿAbbās locates it at the stars or Bayt al-ʿIzza, citing 97:1, 44:3, 2:185 and 56:75; Ibn Kathīr cites al-Nasāʾī and 17:106; Study Quran refers to the Preserved Tablet or Mother of the Book. Qurṭubī reads the stars as Qur'anic portions. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 25:32/q18/p2 It went from the Preserved Tablet to heavenly scribes, then to Gabriel over twenty nights before his twenty-year delivery. — Ibn ʿAbbās's report in Qurṭubī and Itqān distinguishes those intermediary stages; Abū Shāma suggests this may reconcile accounts of one complete and gradual descent. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN(أبو شامة,ابن عباس)]
- 25:32/q18/p3 A prior complete descent does not erase the contextual eloquence of later delivery. — Ālūsī says complete descent to the lowest heaven would otherwise imply a non-inimitable section; anticipating later occasions could itself heighten the miracle. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 25:32/q19 Did earlier scriptures descend all at once?  [جُمْلَةًۭ وَٰحِدَةًۭ · sciences]
turns on: The status of historical reports and scriptural evidence for a one-time descent.
- 25:32/q19/p1 Yes, the Torah and commonly the Gospel and Psalms came as wholes. — The early objectors' comparison and the common scholarly position assume this; Suyūṭī argues God's answer would have denied the premise if false and cites analogous replies in 25:20, 21:7, 13:38. Ālūsī reports hadith and early reports and Suyūṭī's near-consensus claim; Bursevī says the Israelites struggled with the Torah's bulk. [38 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); FARRA(الذين كفروا); TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(السدي,قتادة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الذين كفروا); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره,مكي والرماني); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الذين كفروا); QURTUBI-FULL(اليهود); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الذين كفروا); BURHAN(ابن فورك,قيل); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ITQAN(ابن فورك); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(السيوطي); TABATABAI; ITQAN+]
- 25:32/q19/p2 No: Torah, Gospel and Psalms came in successive parts. — Biqāʿī cites the Torah's text; Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes tablets with Ten Commandments, Jesus's spoken sayings, and multiple Psalms. Iṣlāḥī adds prophets' teaching mission and says People of the Book scholars do not claim a whole descent; Ibn al-Kamāl argues the Torah took eighteen years, while Ālūsī criticizes his evidence. [12 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(ابن الكمال); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; ITQAN-]
- 25:32/q19/p3 The Torah apparently came as written tablets, without proving a single delivery of every prior book. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts written tablets with the Qur'an received orally and says the objectors did not appeal to earlier scriptures. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 25:32/q20 What does rattalnāhu tartīlā mean?  [وَرَتَّلْنَٰهُ تَرْتِيلًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause describes the spacing of revelation, the clarity and arrangement of its content, or an instruction for recitation.
- 25:32/q20/p1 God sent it in separated, ordered portions, one following another. — Al-Farrāʾ, Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibrāhīm, Mujāhid and many commentators use successive verses or passages; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says their links stay intact despite pauses, answering the worry that distance would make earlier lessons fade. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes coherent completed sūras. [40 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(إبراهيم); IBNABIHATIM(إبراهيم,ابن عباس,الحسن); THALABI(ابن عباس,النخعي والحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(إبراهيم,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(إبراهيم، الحسن,مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي,النخعي والحسن وقتادة,مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة,النخعي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي,ابن عباس,الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(النخعي والحسن وقتادة,قيل,مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قالوا)]
- 25:32/q20/p2 God made it clear, distinguished its parts, and explained it. — Qatāda says clarity upon clarity; Ibn Zayd says explanation; al-Suddī stresses detail. Wāḥidī records Ibn al-Aʿrābī's precise verification and al-Layth's ordered pacing. Study Quran reports interpretation explained to the Prophet. [27 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(السدي,عبد الرحمن بن زيد,قتادة); THALABI(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد,السدي,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأعرابي,ابن عباس,السدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي); TABRISI(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة,السدي); DURR-FULL(السدي,قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي); STUDYQURAN(IK and Ṭ)]
- 25:32/q20/p3 God delivered or recited it deliberately and calmly, rather than in haste. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq oppose tartīl to haste; Bayḍāwī and Bursevī describe measured recital over twenty or twenty-three years, while Elmalılı stresses beauty and Iṣlāḥī thoroughness. [24 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,الليث); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,قيل); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 25:32/q20/p4 God arranged its words and meanings into an internally consistent whole. — Asad compares 4:82 and 39:23; Ibn ʿĀshūr describes ordered sūras whose coherent speech, accumulated over time, shows divine origin. [5 notes: ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 25:32/q20/p5 God commanded the Prophet to recite it with deliberate articulation. — Zamakhsharī, Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite 73:4; Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies every letter and vowel. Prophetic instructions to Ibn ʿAbbās say not to chop words like dates or rush like poetry but to pause at marvels and move hearts. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 25:32/q20/p6 Inwardly, each interval lets revelation settle as a stable spiritual disposition. — Qāshānī distinguishes a settled station from a passing state. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 25:32/q21 What is the lexical image of tartīl?  [تَرْتِيلًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the etymological stress falls on spacing and ordered succession or exact clarification.
- 25:32/q21/p1 Orderly spacing, especially of teeth, extended to paced speech. — Zamakhsharī likens tooth whiteness to chamomile; Jishumī mentions moist white teeth; Ṭabrisī and Samīn say teeth neither crowd nor leave excessive gaps. Al-Layth defines ordered sequence; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes no poetry is cited for the derivation. [15 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأعرابي,الليث); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قالوا)]
- 25:32/q21/p2 Precise verification and clarification. — Ibn al-Aʿrābī's lexical gloss in Wāḥidī emphasizes exactness. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأعرابي)]

## 25:32/q22 How is wa-rattalnāhu tartīlā constructed?  [وَرَتَّلْنَٰهُ تَرْتِيلًۭا · grammar]
turns on: The suppressed governing act and the force of the cognate accusative.
- 25:32/q22/p1 The verb coordinates with an omitted 'We revealed it' or the act implicit in kadhālika. — Zamakhsharī, Nasafī, Bursevī, Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Iṣlāḥī supply the elided act; Naḥḥās says a pause at kadhālika yields 'We arranged it thus.' [7 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 25:32/q22/p2 Tartīlā is an accusative cognate object; indefiniteness magnifies the manner. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says it specifies a grand kind of arrangement; Ālūsī likewise notes the remarkable manner. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 25:32/q23 How should the Qur'an be learned and recited?  [وَرَتَّلْنَٰهُ تَرْتِيلًۭا · law]
turns on: Whether the emphasis is delivery, practical obedience, or preservation of the received text.
- 25:32/q23/p1 Read clearly and thoughtfully, with pauses that let meaning affect the heart. — Reports from Ibn ʿAbbās and the Prophet warn against chopping like dry dates, rushing like poetry, or merely seeking a sūra's end; ʿĀʾisha says listeners could count the Prophet's letters. Warsh's recitation is praised for clear articulation without tiring listeners. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL(عائشة رضي الله عنها); NASHR(يونس بن عبد الأعلى)]
- 25:32/q23/p2 Act on the clear teaching; memorizing or completing the whole Qur'an is not required of everyone. — Ibn Qutaybah says believe ambiguous passages, obey commands, avoid prohibitions and recite what one can in prayer; al-Ḥasan faults making recitation itself the sole action. He says few Companions memorized all; Anas says one who knew al-Baqara and Āl ʿImrān was esteemed; al-Shaʿbī says Abū Bakr, ʿUmar and ʿAlī died without collecting all. [5 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(أنس بن مالك,الحسن,الشعبي)]
- 25:32/q23/p3 Recite as transmitted, without paraphrase or inserted commentary. — Ibn al-Jazarī rejects attributing paraphrase to Ibn Masʿūd, who said readings are close and one should recite as taught; some Companions inserted explanatory glosses, while Masrūq reports Ibn Masʿūd objected to putting tafsīr into the text. [3 notes: NASHR(مسروق)]

## 25:32/q24 How do reports describe the Qur'an's written and annual review?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانُ · reports]
turns on: How the final presentation relates to early variants and codices.
- 25:32/q24/p1 The Prophet assigned each new verse its sūra and Gabriel reviewed the Qur'an annually, twice in the final year. — Burhān gives the writing instructions and annual Ramadan reviews; a sound Ibn ʿAbbās report through Zirr says Ibn Masʿūd witnessed the final review and knew the changed or abrogated readings. [3 notes: BURHAN; NASHR(ابن عباس)]
- 25:32/q24/p2 The Companions recorded verified, unabrogated Qur'an; codex differences reflect uncertainty about the final review. — Ibn al-Jazarī says some soundly transmitted material was not known to be in the final presentation; writing only that presentation would not yield additions and omissions. [2 notes: NASHR]
- 25:32/q24/p3 The seven dialects eased early recitation, then final presentation either gathered or displaced variants. — One view says readers converged when a single reading became easy; another says nonfinal readings were abrogated, including codex-conflicting readings attributed to Ubayy and Ibn Masʿūd. [3 notes: NASHR(بعضهم)]

## 25:32/q25 What do the cited reciter biographies and chains establish?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانُ · reports]
turns on: Whether particular links were full presentations or hearings of individual variants.
- 25:32/q25/p1 Medinan and Kufan recitation passed through named teachers and students. — Ibn Mujāhid says Muslim b. Jundab taught thirty verses morning and evening and introduced hamza usage; he lists Nāfiʿ's Medinan teachers, seventy Successors, and Ṣāliḥ b. Khawwāt. He describes Ḥamza as Kufa's leading transmitted reader, his pupils and Sufyān's repeated readings; one al-Zubayr Friday-prayer account is labeled mursal. Ibn al-Jazarī records Warsh's repeated reading to Nāfiʿ, Egyptian leadership, and al-Azraq's precise route. ## 25:32/q27 What spiritual analogy is drawn from gradual revelation?  [لِنُثَبِّتَ بِهِۦ فُؤَادَكَ · inward]
turns on: The inward model used for spiritual progress.
- 25:32/q27/p1 The soul ascends step by step through divine realities and continuing self-disclosure. — Ālūsī reports Sufi masters reading the 'wise Qur'an' as realized divine realities; Qāshānī correlates spaced revelation with the Prophet's changing inner attributes and with seekers' path. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ساداتنا الصوفية); QASHANI]
- 25:32/q27/p2 The Prophet's luminous heart assimilated the Qur'an through serial nourishment. — Bursevī describes gradual revelation as a privilege and likens it to rain that nourishes crops better when spread over time. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 25:32/q28 Can Qur'anic passages be ranked in merit?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانُ · theology]
turns on: Whether merit means intrinsic superiority or differences in reward attested by reports.
- 25:32/q28/p1 No intrinsic ranking: all passages are divine speech. — Al-Ashʿarī and Bāqillānī fear implying deficiency; Yaḥyā b. Yaḥyā calls preference an error and Mālik dislikes repeating one sūra to the exclusion of others. Ibn Ḥibbān reads al-Fātiḥa's 'greatest' as reward and special favor to this community. - 25:32/q28/p2 Yes, passages may differ in merit. — Isḥāq, Ibn al-ʿArabī, Ghazālī and Qurṭubī use the apparent hadiths; Ghazālī cites Yāsīn, al-Fātiḥa, Āyat al-Kursī and al-Ikhlāṣ, while Ibn al-Ḥaṣṣār cites explicit ranking texts. [4 notes: ITQAN(إسحاق بن راهويه وأبو بكر بن العربي والغزالي,ابن الحصار,الغزالي,القرطبي)]
