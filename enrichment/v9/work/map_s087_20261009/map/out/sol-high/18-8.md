# 18:8: 21 questions

## 18:8/q01 How does the making construction work?  [لَجَٰعِلُونَ صَعِيدًۭا · grammar]
- 18:8/q01/p1 Jaʿl denotes transformation, with ṣaʿīdan as its second object and a future sense in the active participle. — Jishumī calls ṣaʿīdan accusative as the object of jāʿilūn; al-Samīn specifies the second object because this use of jaʿala means making one thing into another. Al-Ālūsī also takes jaʿl as transformation. Ibn ʿĀshūr says jāʿilūn is an active participle referring to the future. Al-Burhān discusses other examples where the source and result of transformation are explicit and notes two possible analyses of maʿāsh in 'day a livelihood.' [5 notes: JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN]

## 18:8/q02 What does 'what is on it' include?  [مَا عَلَيْهَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase ranges over all earthly contents or specifically resumes the adornment of 18:7.
- 18:8/q02/p1 Everything placed on earth, including living beings and other contents. — Al-Ālūsī says this extends more broadly than the preceding adornment and that repeating the full phrase in place of a pronoun intensifies it; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī suggests the repetition stresses that the adornment is upon earth. Ibn Isḥāq and Ibn ʿAbbās speak of all that is on earth perishing; al-Biqāʿī extends the affliction from vegetation to human and other animals. The Study Quran reports al-Rāzī's view that humanity is the noblest of earth's creatures. [10 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق,ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن إسحاق,ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 18:8/q02/p2 The adornment or attractive things on earth mentioned in the preceding verse. — Abū Ḥayyān explicitly allows what adorned earth as the referent. Al-Samarrāʾī says the ornament belongs to earth and its growth, especially flowers that delight the viewer, comparing God's adornment of the sky in 15:16 and 37:6 and earthly beauty in 27:60. The Study Quran lists plants, animals, gold and metals; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes goods, children and status as worldly attractions. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN(R); TABATABAI; WAHIDI-BASIT; NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 18:8/q03 Does making everything soil imply literal conversion of elemental matter?  [مَا عَلَيْهَا صَعِيدًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the expression is taken as physical conversion, ordinary language about decay, or a philosophical account of composites.
- 18:8/q03/p1 Literal conversion of the created elements into earth is possible. — Al-Ālūsī says that, if the phrase is applied literally to every component, elements must change into earth; elements already change into one another. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:8/q03/p2 The idiom can describe decay until only earth remains without implying elemental conversion. — Al-Ālūsī gives the ordinary expression about a person's body decaying into earth and says hearers would not picture elemental conversion. He adds that the philosophers' account of enduring elemental forms in composite bodies is scarcely heard among early Muslims. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:8/q03/p3 Elemental forms persist within composite bodies. — Al-Ālūsī reports this as a philosophical view and notes its distance from early Muslim discourse. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:8/q04 What does ṣaʿīd mean here?  [صَعِيدًۭا · meaning]
turns on: The word's range in Arabic usage: material soil, earth's face, level or elevated ground, and roads.
- 18:8/q04/p1 Soil or dust. — Al-Farrāʾ, Abān ibn Taghlib, al-Samīn and Ibn ʿĀshūr identify it as soil; al-Baghawī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya report soil as one of several usages. Al-Māwardī transmits Abān's gloss, and Ibn ʿĀshūr points to 18:40 for the word's recurrence. [9 notes: FARRA; MAWARDI-FULL(أبان بن تغلب); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR; DURR-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سهل)]
- 18:8/q04/p2 The surface or face of the earth. — Ibn Qutayba explains the face as what rises or projects; al-Majāz, Ibn Hishām as quoted by al-Qurṭubī, al-Rāghib as quoted by al-Ālūsī, and Ibn al-Anbārī's lexicographers give surface or face. Ibn Hishām cites a line of Dhū al-Rumma. [7 notes: MAJAZ; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن قتيبة); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن هشام); ALUSI-FULL(الراغب وأبو عبيدة)]
- 18:8/q04/p3 Level or smooth ground. — Al-Akhfash and Muqātil are reported by al-Māwardī; Abū ʿUbayda is reported by al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī; al-Suddī is reported by Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī. Al-Majāz likewise allows level ground. [7 notes: MAJAZ; MAWARDI-FULL(الأخفش ومقاتل); RAZI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو عبيدة,الراغب وأبو عبيدة); YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن مجاهد)]
- 18:8/q04/p4 A road or path, specifically a path without vegetation. — Al-Zajjāj defines it as a plantless path, transmitted by Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī. Al-Qurṭubī cites a hadith warning against sitting on ṣaʿīd as roads; Abū Ḥayyān reports a hadith on ṣuʿud, elevated or open paths. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن هشام); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي); ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج)]
- 18:8/q04/p5 Ground without plants. — Abū Ḥayyān transmits Mujāhid's gloss; Qatāda's reports explain the resulting ṣaʿīd as land without trees or vegetation. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 18:8/q04/p6 Wholesome earth. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya lists this among the word's possible senses. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 18:8/q04/p7 Ground raised above a lower area. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya lists this among the word's possible senses. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 18:8/q05 What does juruz mean here?  [جُرُزًا · meaning]
turns on: Whether juruz simply describes barrenness, specifically prior growth stripped away, inherent failure to grow, the ground's shape, ruin, cutting, confinement, or a place name.
- 18:8/q05/p1 Barren, bare land with no vegetation or growth. — Mujāhid's barren plain is transmitted repeatedly; Qatāda describes no trees, plants or buildings; Ibn Zayd says nothing useful remains. Al-Majāz cites Dhū al-Rumma for infertile land and dry years; al-Ṭabarī cites rajaz on ajrāz years; al-Māwardī cites rajaz on years stripping land. Al-Zajjāj gives the plural ajrāz. Abū ʿUbayda, transmitted by Ibn al-Jawzī, calls the ground coarse and the year drought-struck. Al-Ālūsī treats Qatāda's no-vegetation sense as apparent. Al-Bāqir's report through al-Qummī agrees. [34 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); FARRA; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)+]
- 18:8/q05/p2 Land stripped of vegetation after it grew, by cutting, grazing, drought, locusts, or consumption. — Al-Farrāʾ describes animals and locusts eating growth; Abū Muslim derives the root from cutting, like a sharp sword or all-consuming eater. Al-Kisāʾī describes people consuming every crop. Al-Bayḍāwī traces it to cutting. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says jarazat is loss through drought or locusts and corrects al-Zajjāj: the land has not grown, rather than being unable to grow. Al-Zajjāj likens the land to something that devours plants, and Ibn al-Anbārī says it consumes or burns every plant. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn cite gluttonous-woman usage, with poetic or comic lines; al-Rāzī adds the destructive sword. [17 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سهل,الكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج)+]
- 18:8/q05/p3 Land that does not grow anything. — Al-Zajjāj states this, including the picture of land consuming plants; Ibn ʿAṭiyya explicitly corrects this wording to land that has not grown. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج)-]
- 18:8/q05/p4 Smooth or level ground. — Al-Māwardī reports Muqātil's gloss; other descriptions of the resulting plain also stress its smoothness. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 18:8/q05/p5 Ruined or desolate land. — Al-Ḥasan and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr are reported with the ruin gloss. Al-Ālūsī reports it through Ibn Abī Ḥātim but disputes its status as the genuine lexical sense. [2 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن أبي حاتم)-]
- 18:8/q05/p6 Cut down or harvested. — Jishumī reports this further explanation of the transformed earth. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 18:8/q05/p7 Enclosed or confined. — Al-Māwardī attributes this gloss to Ibn Baḥr. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر)]
- 18:8/q05/p8 Al-Jurz is a land's proper name. — Al-Suyūṭī records the name in al-Itqān. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 18:8/q07 What does the promised change do to the earth?  [لَجَٰعِلُونَ مَا عَلَيْهَا صَعِيدًۭا جُرُزًا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the image describes a physical barren earth, the severance of attachment, or the manifestation of divine reality and annihilation.
- 18:8/q07/p1 God removes the earth's adornment and life, leaving an actual level, dry, barren surface. — Muqātil says mountains and plants disappear as at first creation; al-Thaʿlabī says it cannot grow anything; al-Zamakhsharī pictures the green earth turning white. Al-Biqāʿī describes greenery stripped and exposed to sun and wind. Ibn Kathīr says no growth or benefit remains. Al-Ālūsī contrasts the bare soil with the scene that once delighted spectators. Abū Sahl likens it to plants cut away; al-Ṭabrisī says inhabited earth is ruined. This destruction is described as all earthly adornments or contents perishing by Ibn Isḥāq, Ibn ʿAbbās and later commentators. [35 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن مجاهد); IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق,ابن عباس); NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سهل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن إسحاق,ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 18:8/q07/p2 The barren plain figures the ending of people's attachment to worldly adornments when the trial ends. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this metaphor: attractive goods, children and status bring souls into the earthly trial, then their attractions are removed and people stand alone for judgment. He reports the literal level-dust reading as an alternative. [2 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 18:8/q07/p3 The world's qualities vanish through divine manifestation and death without injustice or loss. — Al-Qāshānī describes true or natural death and annihilation of qualities as the way God reduces worldly adornment to the barren plain. Al-Ālūsī reports an inward account in which worldly annihilation becomes manifest when true divine existence and the greater Resurrection appear. [2 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:8/q08 When does the earth become barren?  [لَجَٰعِلُونَ صَعِيدًۭا جُرُزًا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether to speak only of the final universal event or also of present local examples.
- 18:8/q08/p1 At the end of the world and resurrection, after earthly life and responsibility end. — Muqātil places it in the afterlife. The exegetes reported by Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī place the leveling at resurrection, without plants, water or shelter. Jishumī adds that moral responsibility ends, bodies perish and signs of the Hour occur. Bursawī says it is when worldly life ends; Ibn ʿĀshūr's future active participle confirms eventual ending. Kuran Yolu compares 14:48 and 55:26–27. [8 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 18:8/q08/p2 The process occurs in regions now and will encompass all earth at resurrection. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya explicitly distinguishes present local barrenness from its universal occurrence at resurrection; the image of drought, locusts and grazing offers present instances. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 18:8/q09 What lesson does the verse draw from earthly beauty passing away?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw complementary moral, consoling, evidential and grateful lessons from the same reversal.
- 18:8/q09/p1 People should detach from fleeting adornment and devote themselves to enduring good deeds and God. — Al-Rāzī calls adornment a trial before destruction and recompense. Al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Kāshifī as cited by Bursawī, Elmalılı, Iṣlāḥī and Kuran Yolu warn against attachment. Bursawī urges prayer to be among those turning wholly to God; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says only deeds endure. The Study Quran reports al-Ghazālī's view that adornment tests detachment and asceticism. Elmalılı specifies service to the everlasting Creator and the best deeds, not grief over what perishes; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī at 18:42–44 says God is better in reward and outcome because He endures and people need a reliable Lord. [15 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Abū Ḥāmid al-Ghazzālī,IK)]
- 18:8/q09/p2 The Prophet should cease grieving over rejection because all worldly goods and rejecters pass away before judgment. — Ibn Isḥāq, reported also by al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr, says people return to God for recompense according to deeds. Abū Ḥayyān calls the verse consolation. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says people absorbed in pleasure cannot defeat God's will, citing 6:93–94; Iṣlāḥī says their blindness comes from worldly lures and they will regret it. Elmalılı counsels striving rather than wasted grief. [8 notes: IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن إسحاق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI]
- 18:8/q09/p3 The reversal from flourishing to barrenness displays God's power, resurrection and the need to prepare for changing states. — Al-Biqāʿī says recurring restoration by rain proves resurrection and is omitted for readers of insight. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the earth is the most immediate sign to human perception, compares 88:17–20 and 51:20, and urges judging by virtue and good outcomes rather than trusting strength. Kuran Yolu cites 14:48 and 55:26–27 for the world's transformation. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 18:8/q09/p4 The wording invites gratitude for the earth's beauty and recognition of God's artistry. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the compact wording includes these purposes, comparing 16:6 and 3:14; al-Samarrāʾī notes flowers and other delights, and the adorned sky in 15:16 and 37:6. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 18:8/q10 How does this verse connect to nearby and parallel verses?  [* · links]
turns on: How the clause completes 18:6–7 and which parallel earthly transformations illuminate it.
- 18:8/q10/p1 It completes the account of earth's adornment as a temporary test before its removal and recompense. — Al-Ālūsī's first coherence reading makes 18:7 explain the concern of 18:6 and 18:8 finish it; al-Rāzī cites 55:26, 20:106, 84:3 and 14:48 for destruction and transformation. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the clause explains the consolation of 18:6 and connects with the warning in 18:2. Asad says his bracketed interpolation supplies an implied link to the longer passage. Iṣlāḥī points forward to 18:46–48. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 18:8/q10/p2 It consoles Muhammad after denial and warns that God will answer the rejecters. — Al-Ālūsī's second coherence reading links 18:6–7 with impending divine vengeance; the opening says the Prophet warns but cannot create faith. Ibn Isḥāq says he should not grieve over what he sees and hears; Iṣlāḥī likewise says rejection is neither a defect in the Book nor in preaching. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 18:8/q10/p3 It urges the Prophet to continue calling rebels because God continues sustaining them, while warning against worldly attachment. — Al-Ālūsī reports this proposed link to 18:7 but calls it strained. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]
- 18:8/q10/p4 The barren-land image parallels 32:27, where water makes dry land produce crops. — Yaḥyā, Ibn Zayd as transmitted by al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr, and al-Rāzī all cite 32:27. Their comparison shows that land can be barren and later productive. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(يحيى); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن زيد)]
- 18:8/q10/p5 The earth's ultimate destruction is echoed by 14:48 and 55:26–27. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 14:48 for the eventual transformation; Kuran Yolu compares both verses and says only God endures. The Study Quran cites 55:26 for adornments returning to God. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK)]

## 18:8/q11 Was the opening through 18:8 revealed in Mecca or Medina?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether reports of Medinan exceptions outweigh the reported general Meccan attribution.
- 18:8/q11/p1 The whole sūra, including its opening through 18:8, is Meccan. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the commentators' general view and prefers it. Ibn ʿĀshūr quotes his consensus and says proposed Medinan passages at 18:28–29 and 18:107 to the end are weak. Iṣlāḥī situates the sūra in late Makkan conflict; the Study Quran also calls it late Makkan. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جميع المفسرين)+]
- 18:8/q11/p2 The opening through the verse ending juruzan is a Medinan exception. — Muqātil, transmitted by Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī, also excepts the believers passage at the end; al-Suyūṭī lists the patient-company command as another exception. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the first-eight report Farqad's, while Ibn ʿAṭiyya and the Study Quran note the minority view. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(مقاتل); STUDYQURAN; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جميع المفسرين)-; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية)-]

## 18:8/q16 What purpose and setting do commentators give the sūra?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The commentators stress different narrative, historical and ethical purposes.
- 18:8/q16/p1 Its narratives teach rejection of association with God and piety. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī suggests the sūra presents three unusual narratives found nowhere else in the Qur'an: the Companions of the Cave, Moses and his companion, and Dhū al-Qarnayn. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 18:8/q16/p2 It warns Quraysh in the late Makkan conflict and steadies the Prophet and believers. — Iṣlāḥī says Quraysh sought to erase the mission with indirect Jewish and Christian backing, risked punishment through arrogant worldly success, and the Prophet and companions were urged to patience, steadfastness, possible migration and coming victories. The Prophet's role is warning and glad tidings; unbelief is no flaw in Book or preaching. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 18:8/q16/p3 It challenges Christians after the previous sūra exposed Jews, and warns Quraysh against following baseless opponents. — Iṣlāḥī pairs al-Kahf with Maryam in this purpose and says Quraysh risk losing God's great opportunity. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 18:8/q16/p4 It is among the Qur'an's most excellent chapters. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives this appraisal. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 18:8/q17 What does the Cave story teach in its place after this opening?  [* · links]
- 18:8/q17/p1 It is an ethical parable of resurrection and faith-driven withdrawal from a wicked world. — Asad says the cave episode is no more wondrous than other Qur'anic messages because it fits the Qur'an's ethical teaching; whatever its origin, it depicts withdrawal rewarded by spiritual awakening beyond time and death, with 18:9–26 as context. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 18:8/q18 What historical origin is proposed for the Cave story?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the story reflects a Christian episode, older Jewish tradition, or an Essene allegory.
- 18:8/q18/p1 The youths belong to early Christian history, especially persecution under Decius at Ephesus. — Asad says most commentators rely on the Christian legend of youths who slept for centuries; he reports the early Christian setting as their common placement. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES(most classical commentators,most commentators)]
- 18:8/q18/p2 The youths lived before Christianity. — Asad quotes Ibn Kathīr's reasoning that Jewish rabbis would not have preserved a Christian story after relations with Christians had broken down. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Ibn Kathir)]
- 18:8/q18/p3 An older Jewish oral tradition underlies the later Christian telling. — Asad argues that the Christian version developed from pre-Christian Jewish sources. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 18:8/q18/p4 The story's fundamentals allegorize the Essenes, especially the secluded Qumran community. — Asad cites the community's scriptural study, copying, preservation, seclusion and reputation for piety as likely inspiration, and notes that Jesus may have belonged to the Essenes. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 18:8/q19 What does al-raqīm in the Cave story mean?  [* · meaning]
- 18:8/q19/p1 A written thing, book or scripture. — Asad reports Ibn ʿAbbās and early authorities equating al-raqīm with marqūm and kitāb; he reports al-Rāzī's statement that Arabic rhetoricians and philologists agree on al-kitāb. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Ibn 'Abbas,Razi)]

## 18:8/q20 What is the cosmos beside God's power?  [* · theology]
- 18:8/q20/p1 It is in God's grasp and is mere dust beside His power. — Al-Sulamī transmits this saying of al-Wāsiṭī. [1 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي)]

## 18:8/q21 How do the criminals face the Fire in the later passage?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the verb describes expectation, rushing or descent, or encounter in al-Aʿmash's reading.
- 18:8/q21/p1 The polytheists expect to enter it when they see it. — Al-Thaʿlabī gives the identification as polytheists. A report from Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī attributes to the Prophet that a disbeliever sees Hell and thinks he will enter it from forty years' travel away. [2 notes: THALABI(أبو سعيد الخدري عن النبي,مجاهد)]
- 18:8/q21/p2 They rush into or descend into it. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports these alternative glosses. [1 notes: THALABI(مجاهد)]
- 18:8/q21/p3 They encounter it. — Al-Thaʿlabī records al-Aʿmash's reading with this sense. [1 notes: THALABI(مجاهد)]
