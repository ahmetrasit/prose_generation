# 19:22: 23 questions

## 19:22/q01 What does “she carried him” say Mary conceived?  [فَحَمَلَتْهُ · referent]
- 19:22/q01/p1 Mary became pregnant with Jesus. — Al-Damghani explains ḥaml as pregnancy ending at delivery; Ibn al-Jawzi identifies the object as Jesus. [2 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 19:22/q02 How did Mary conceive Jesus?  [فَحَمَلَتْهُ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ over the breather, the path of the breath, and what bodily process made conception possible.
- 19:22/q02/p1 Gabriel's breath entered through her garment opening or collar and reached her womb. — Yahya's report has Gabriel draw the opening with his finger; Wahb says he appeared as a man and breathed there, sometimes after Mary accepted God's command. Al-Suddi describes the front slit and chest; Ibn Jurayj names neckline and sleeve. Ibn Abbas says she felt pregnancy at once. Al-Baqir says the fetus instantly became complete and Mary felt its weight. Alusi reads the explanatory fāʾ as implying her trust. Ibn Kathir says conception occurred by God's permission; 21:91 and 66:12 are cited for the Spirit's breath. Reports vary between edge, collar, split neckline and the garment being put on afterward. [23 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,السدي,وهب بن منبه); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن جريج,الباقر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(آخرون,وهب); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(الإمام الباقر); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 19:22/q02/p2 Gabriel's breath entered through her sleeve. — Ibn Jurayj names both neckline and sleeve; al-Suddi says it entered Mary's chest through the sleeve. Other compilers list the sleeve alongside other reported openings. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(السدي); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 19:22/q02/p3 Gabriel's breath entered through the garment's lower hem. — Razi reports a view that the breath traveled from the hem to the womb; Alusi also lists the hem among the reported entry points. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:22/q02/p4 Gabriel's breath entered Mary's mouth. — Razi reports that it reached her belly and caused immediate conception; other compilers also list the mouth. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:22/q02/p5 Gabriel breathed from a distance and the breath reached Mary. — Baghawi says the wind reached her and conception followed immediately; Alusi reports that Gabriel did not approach. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:22/q02/p6 God Himself breathed the spirit. — Razi reports the appeal to wording about God's spirit and the analogy with Adam in 3:59 and 15:29; he notes that wording about breathing into Jesus can refer to Mary because Jesus was in her womb. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 19:22/q02/p7 The one who spoke to Mary was the child she carried, who entered through her mouth. — Ibn al-Jawzi reports this from Ubayy ibn Kaʿb. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبي بن كعب)]
- 19:22/q02/p8 Jesus formed bodily from Mary's fluid after Gabriel's human appearance stirred her. — Bursevi says the human appearance preserved a human pattern; Mary's pleasure and imagined fluid entered the womb. He invokes Jesus' reviving miracles as reflecting Gabriel's breath, and rejects naturalists who deny generation from one parent's fluid. He says a mother's state or sight at conception can shape her child; Mary's calm after the angelic good news, 3:45, made Jesus open-hearted. Breath need not enter by an opening, as Adam's spirit entered through his crown. [7 notes: BURSEVI]
- 19:22/q02/p9 Jesus' spirit joined semen only after it settled in the womb and became ready. — Qashani says the settled semen first united into a temperament able to receive the spirit. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 19:22/q03 What event is left unstated between the angel's speech and Mary's pregnancy?  [فَحَمَلَتْهُ · rhetoric]
- 19:22/q03/p1 God decreed it, Gabriel breathed into Mary, and she conceived. — Al-Wahidi says the omission is clear from other Quranic passages because the Quran is one book; Razi supplies the decree and Gabriel's breath. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL]

## 19:22/q04 How old was Mary when she conceived?  [فَحَمَلَتْهُ · reports]
turns on: Competing age reports; Razi says the Quran supplies no decisive age.
- 19:22/q04/p1 Thirteen years old. — Muqatil, Ibn Atiyya and Bursevi report thirteen; reports collected by Kashshaf, Razi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi, Nasafi and Baydawi include it. Razi cautions that the Quran establishes no age. [9 notes: MUQATIL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:22/q04/p2 Ten years old. — Muqatil's age appears in Thalabi and Baghawi; Baydawi couples it with two menstrual periods. Other compilers list ten among conflicting ages. [7 notes: THALABI(مقاتل بن سليمان); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل بن سليمان); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:22/q04/p3 Fifteen years old. — Abu Hayyan and Alusi attribute this report to Wahb and Mujahid. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:22/q04/p4 Fourteen years old. — Abu Hayyan and Alusi list this among the reports. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:22/q04/p5 Twelve years old. — Abu Hayyan and Alusi list this among the reports. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:22/q04/p6 Twenty years old. — Razi and Nasafi report it; Razi says the Quran fixes none of the reported ages. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); NASAFI-FULL]

## 19:22/q05 Had Mary menstruated before conceiving?  [فَحَمَلَتْهُ · reports]
turns on: The reports distinguish two prior periods, no period yet, and no menstruation ever.
- 19:22/q05/p1 She had menstruated twice. — Muqatil and later compilers pair two periods with differing ages, ten or thirteen; Razi says the Quran establishes none of these details. [10 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(مقاتل بن سليمان); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل بن سليمان); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:22/q05/p2 She had not yet menstruated. — Abu Hayyan lists this report against the two-period and never-menstruated accounts; Alusi notes the disagreement without specifying the alternative. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:22/q05/p3 She never menstruated. — Abu Hayyan reports this as a third account of Mary's menstrual history. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]

## 19:22/q06 How long did Mary carry Jesus before his birth?  [فَحَمَلَتْهُ · reports]
turns on: Conflicting transmitted durations and whether the consecutive fāʾ clauses imply immediate birth or allow ordinary stages; Abu Hayyan says the reports contradict one another and should be left aside.
- 19:22/q06/p1 Nine months, the ordinary duration. — Al-Hasan, Saʿid ibn Jubayr and Ibn al-Saʾib, the majority according to Ibn Kathir, and reports attributed to Ibn Abbas and al-Baqir support it. Ibn Kathir prefers ordinary development: the fāʾ permits intervals, stages of human creation are each forty days in sound reports and 23:12–14, and 22:63 likewise joins rain to later greening. Alusi inclines to nine months though he considers the reports unsound and the instantaneous argument inconclusive. Samarrai says each fāʾ event follows after its natural interval. Kuranyolu cites Ibn Kathir's majority report. [10 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير وابن السائب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 19:22/q06/p2 An hour or no extended interval separated conception and birth. — Ibn Abbas appeals to the uninterrupted Quranic sequence; Qurtubi calls that the apparent reading, and Bursevi cites both the fāʾ and Jesus' direct creation. Razi adduces the divine 'Be' analogy with Adam in 3:59 and says ordinary gestational intervals pertain to semen. Muqatil's shorter scheme differs: conception, formation and nursing each in an hour. Ibn al-Jawzi says immediate birth cannot be literal because Mary withdrew while carrying him; Ibn Kathir calls the report strange and Alusi finds the inference inconclusive. Alusi also says no duration report is sound. [15 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الثعلبي); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس,قيل); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس)+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس)-; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)-; ALUSI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 19:22/q06/p3 Six months. — Listed by Thalabi, Baghawi, Kashshaf, Ibn al-Jawzi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi and Tabatabai; Alusi says naturalists give six months as a minimum. [12 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عطاء وأبو العالية والضحاك); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(الإمام الصادق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 19:22/q06/p4 Eight months. — Several sources call Jesus the only surviving eight-month infant, making his survival a sign; Ikrima says an eight-month child ordinarily does not live. Alusi rejects astrological explanations based on planetary months as fables. [11 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عطاء وأبو العالية والضحاك); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 19:22/q06/p5 Three hours, one each for conception, formation, and birth. — Muqatil's sequence puts the birth at noon; reports vary as to whether the separate hours describe forming, nursing, or birth. Thalabi, Kashshaf, Ibn al-Jawzi and Abu Hayyan relay versions. [9 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(مقاتل بن سليمان); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل بن سليمان); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل بن سليمان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 19:22/q06/p6 Seven months. — Kashshaf and Abu Hayyan attribute seven months to ʿAtaʾ, Abu al-ʿAliya and al-Dahhak; Baydawi, Nasafi and Alusi also list it. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عطاء وأبو العالية والضحاك); ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 19:22/q06/p7 Nine hours, with delivery on the same day. — Ibn al-Jawzi attributes this to al-Hasan; Tabatabai reports it from Imam al-Sadiq. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); TABATABAI(الإمام الصادق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 19:22/q07 What does the sequence of fāʾ clauses imply about timing?  [فَحَمَلَتْهُ فَٱنتَبَذَتْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether consecutive clauses specify a short elapsed time or merely the order of events.
- 19:22/q07/p1 Each event follows the previous one, while the interval depends on the event. — Ibn Kathir compares human developmental stages in 23:12–14 and land greening after rain in 22:63; Samarrai says normal pregnancy can intervene without breaking succession. Ibn Ashur says conception followed the exchange closely, while the fāʾ expresses consequence. Ibn al-Jawzi says Mary still carried Jesus during withdrawal. Alusi finds immediate birth unproved. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 19:22/q07/p2 The immediate sequence suggests a very short interval. — Ibn Abbas draws immediate birth from the sequence; Tabrisi infers just an hour between Mary's withdrawal and conception; Qurtubi calls immediate birth the more apparent reading. Bursevi adds Jesus' direct creation and the fāʾ. Biqai reads the fāʾ as immediate withdrawal with the child in her womb. [6 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس)+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس)-; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 19:22/q08 When did Mary withdraw?  [فَٱنتَبَذَتْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the narrative's sequence places departure at awareness of pregnancy or near delivery.
- 19:22/q08/p1 She withdrew after recognizing she was pregnant. — Wahidi Wajiz says she sensed pregnancy; Nasafi and Ibn Kathir likewise make discovery of pregnancy precede departure. Biqai takes fāʾ to mark immediate withdrawal; Kuranyolu says she concealed the pregnancy for a time. [5 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 19:22/q08/p2 She withdrew as birth approached. — Wahidi Basit reads the sequence as departure at delivery; al-Suddi says she went east as delivery approached. Wahb's account has God direct her to leave near birth; Bursevi reports her leaving Zakariya's house at night then. Nawf describes heaviness and labor pains before flight. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(قصص الأنبياء); ALUSI-FULL(نوف,وهب); STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ)]

## 19:22/q09 What does “with him” describe in her withdrawal?  [بِهِۦ · grammar]
- 19:22/q09/p1 The bāʾ expresses accompaniment or state: she withdrew while carrying Jesus in her womb. — Kashshaf cites a poetic line and 23:20 on a plant bearing oil; Razi repeats the plant comparison. Baydawi and Samin cite poetry; Samin identifies the phrase as circumstantial. Bursevi calls it accompaniment or state; Alusi cites 6:99. Zajjaj glosses going away with the child, while Ibn Ashur says ḥamalat bihi can stress close attachment and cites Abu Kabir al-Hudhali and 46:15. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 19:22/q10 Why did Mary withdraw from her people?  [فَٱنتَبَذَتْ · interpretation]
turns on: Reports give fear of accusation, concern for others' embarrassment, and protection of the child; Razi says the text does not choose among them.
- 19:22/q10/p1 She feared blame or an accusation of wrongdoing for bearing a child without a husband. — Muqatil says she went beyond the hills, away from family. Qushayri says she had no trusted confidant. Wahidi says she feared reproach from Zechariah and her people; Baghawi identifies shame over no husband. Ibn Atiyya emphasizes modesty; Tabrisi says shame and accusation, including her aunt failing to recognize her. Ibn Kathir says she hid from people's sight; Ibn Ishaq says they accused her and Yusuf when pregnancy became visible. Abu Hayyan, Qurtubi, Nasafi, Kuranyolu and others give the same fear. [20 notes: MUQATIL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الباقر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); BURSEVI(قصص الأنبياء); ALUSI-FULL(نوف); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 19:22/q10/p2 She left to protect the child from danger. — Wahb says God told Mary to leave lest her people shame her and kill the child; Razi lists fear for the child's safety while saying the text chooses no reason. Alusi relays Wahb's command near birth. Study Quran includes protection of both Mary and the child. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(وهب); STUDYQURAN]
- 19:22/q10/p3 She wished to spare Zechariah embarrassment. — Razi lists this among reasons but says the text chooses none; Study Quran connects the concern with protecting Mary and the child from misunderstanding. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN]
- 19:22/q10/p4 She felt shame because of her known piety. — Razi lists this separately and says the verse itself does not choose a reason; Ibn Atiyya stresses modesty. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 19:22/q11 Where was the distant place?  [مَكَانًۭا قَصِيًّۭا · referent]
turns on: The reports identify different sites; the verse specifies remoteness but, as Ibn Ashur says, does not name a place.
- 19:22/q11/p1 Near Bethlehem, at or beyond the far end of its valley. — Ibn Abbas names the far end of the Valley of Bethlehem; Ibn Atiyya says Bethlehem is four miles from Jerusalem, while Kashifi gives about six miles from Iliya and Ibn al-Jawzi reports six miles walked. Bursevi cites the Isra report from Anas: Gabriel said he prayed at Bethlehem, where Jesus was born, a report Bursevi calls authentic or hasan and attributes to al-Nasaʾi and al-Bayhaqi. Ibn Ashur appeals to Luke's Bethlehem birth against an Egypt birth. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود وابن أبي عبلة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI(أنس,الكاشفي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 19:22/q11/p2 At the far eastern side of the sanctuary or near the Temple. — Al-Suddi locates it there as delivery approached; Nawf says Mary fled east in embarrassment. Study Quran mentions an eastern Temple identification but prefers a place where Mary was unknown. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); ALUSI-FULL(نوف); STUDYQURAN-]
- 19:22/q11/p3 Behind a mountain or beyond the hills. — Muqatil says away from family beyond hills; Kashshaf, Razi, Nasafi and Alusi mention a mountain. Kashifi locates a mountain or valley near Bethlehem; Abu Hayyan reports a birth behind a mountain. [7 notes: MUQATIL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:22/q11/p4 At the far end of her house or in its farthest room. — Kashshaf, Razi and Abu Hayyan report the house; Bursevi's cited Irshad and Alusi prefer the far side or farthest room because it fits a short pregnancy. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL+; BURSEVI(الإرشاد)+]
- 19:22/q11/p5 In Egypt or nearby, with Joseph helping her flee. — Wahb's report in Ibn Atiyya says Egypt or a nearby region; Alusi says Joseph carried her toward Egypt and labor began there. Study Quran reports a later flight with Joseph as delivery approached. Ibn Ashur rejects an Egypt birth because Luke places it at Bethlehem and 19:27 has Mary return carrying Jesus. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(وهب بن منبه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(وهب); STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 19:22/q11/p6 Outside Jerusalem after leaving Zechariah's house. — A story cited by Bursevi has her leave at night as birth approached, wishing Zechariah and others not to know. [1 notes: BURSEVI(قصص الأنبياء)]
- 19:22/q11/p7 A remote place away from family, without a specified site. — Ibn Ashur says the verse offers no grounds to choose a particular place; Study Quran prefers a place where Mary was unknown over the Temple vicinity. Tabatabai says she separated from her people. [16 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN+]

## 19:22/q12 What does qaṣiyyan mean?  [قَصِيًّۭا · meaning]
- 19:22/q12/p1 Far away or remote. — Al-Farra compares qaṣiyyan with qāṣiyan and cites an anonymous poem using al-qaṣiyy for distance; Tabari and Wahidi cite a rajaz line and the root meaning to become or make distant. Zajjaj says both forms mean distant. Tabrisi contrasts it with near; Ibn Ashur says it derives from distance. [18 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); FARRA; MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس، مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود وابن أبي عبلة); RAZI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 19:22/q13 How may qaṣiyyan be read?  [قَصِيًّۭا · readings]
- 19:22/q13/p1 Ibn Masʿud and Ibn Abi ʿAbla read qāṣiyan instead of qaṣiyyan. — Ibn al-Jawzi transmits the variant and al-Farra's judgment that both forms mean distant; Zajjaj likewise gives qaṣiyyan or qāṣiyyan with the same sense. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود وابن أبي عبلة); ZAJJAJ; FARRA]

## 19:22/q14 What grammatical role does “a distant place” have?  [مَكَانًۭا قَصِيًّۭا · grammar]
turns on: Al-Nahhas offers a locative and an object analysis of the destination phrase.
- 19:22/q14/p1 It is a locative expression of where she withdrew. — Al-Nahhas calls qaṣiyyan locative; Bursevi treats the phrase as a destination reached by going. [2 notes: NAHHAS; BURSEVI]
- 19:22/q14/p2 It is an object expressing withdrawal to a distant place. — Al-Nahhas states this alternative to the locative analysis. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 19:22/q15 What happened when Mary's pregnant relative met her?  [* · reports]
- 19:22/q15/p1 John, in his mother's womb, bowed toward Jesus in Mary's womb. — Al-Suddi and Wahb give the recognition scene; 3:39 is cited for John as confirmer. Ibn Kathir says Mary confided in her sister, Zakariya's wife, and both women and their family believed God; Malik calls the women cousins who conceived together and relates the bow to Jesus' special status. Ibn Kathir interprets the bow as honor and humility. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي,وهب بن منبه); RAZI-FULL(السدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مالك); STUDYQURAN(IK, Ṭ)]

## 19:22/q16 What ruling is drawn from John's bow in the womb?  [* · law]
- 19:22/q16/p1 Prostrating in greeting was permitted then but later forbidden, reserving devotion to God. — Ibn Kathir treats John's fetal bow as an act of honor and humility and says greeting prostration was lawful at that time. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 19:22/q17 What did Joseph the carpenter do when he learned of Mary's pregnancy?  [* · reports]
turns on: The Joseph narratives differ over whether his role was wary assistance or a flight interrupted by Gabriel's warning.
- 19:22/q17/p1 He doubted at first, then accepted God's ability to create without ordinary parents and helped Mary. — Wahb says Joseph was Mary's relative and fellow temple servant near Mount Zion; he balanced her visible pregnancy against her known purity, took over temple duties when she weakened, and in one account carried her toward Egypt. Ibn Kathir says Mary's analogies named trees, crops and Adam without parents. Study Quran says Joseph knew and helped. Alusi repeats Wahb's exchange about seed, rain and Adam. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(وهب); STUDYQURAN(Wahb ibn Munabbih)]
- 19:22/q17/p2 He fled with Mary after accusation and nearly killed her, until Gabriel explained the child was from the Holy Spirit. — Kashshaf attributes the story to al-Suddi; Abu Hayyan says some reports call Joseph her betrothed cousin. Ibn Ishaq says people accused both because Joseph was the only man with her in the church, after pregnancy signs appeared when she drew water. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(السدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق)]

## 19:22/q18 What other biographical details are reported about Mary and Jesus?  [* · reports]
turns on: These are separate transmitted biographical claims, not alternatives on a single event.
- 19:22/q18/p1 Mary lived with Jesus thirty-three years, lived six more after his ascension, and died at fifty-two. — Muqatil places conception and early formation at noon and says Mary had two menstrual periods before conceiving. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 19:22/q18/p2 Jesus was said to be the exception to the rule that newborns cry at birth. — Kashshaf reports the popular saying without further argument. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 19:22/q19 How is Jesus' creation compared with Adam's?  [* · theology]
- 19:22/q19/p1 Both are signs of creation through God's command or breath, outside ordinary parentage. — The letter to the Negus calls Jesus God's spirit and word cast to pure Mary and compares his conception with Adam made by God's hand and breath. Razi relates 66:12 to 15:29 and cites the 'Be, and it is' analogy of 3:59; Study Quran cites 15:29, 32:9 and 38:72 for God's breath and 3:59 for the likeness. [4 notes: BAQILLANI(محمد رسول الله); RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 19:22/q20 What did the Prophet's letter to the Negus say alongside its account of Jesus?  [* · reports]
- 19:22/q20/p1 It invited the Negus and his troops to worship God alone, follow Muhammad and believe his revelation. — Baqillani reports that the letter closes by affirming sincere delivery of the message; it calls Jesus God's spirit and word cast to Mary and compares his creation with Adam's. [2 notes: BAQILLANI(محمد رسول الله)]

## 19:22/q21 What inward sense is given to Mary's distant withdrawal?  [* · inward]
turns on: The two inward readings address different spiritual aspects of Mary's experience.
- 19:22/q21/p1 She moved from the eastern place into the western natural and bodily horizon with the child. — Qashani identifies the west with natural bodily existence. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 19:22/q21/p2 Her ordeal is a trial that burns away blameworthy dispositions and yields divine qualities through patience. — Bursevi presents this spiritual reading of her trial. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 19:22/q22 How does this surah connect to the previous surah?  [* · links]
- 19:22/q22/p1 Both recount astonishing events; this surah's unusual births follow the earlier surah's extraordinary stories. — Abu Hayyan names the Cave, Moses and Khidr, and Dhu al-Qarnayn in 18:9, 18:60 and 18:83, followed by John's birth to aged and barren parents and Jesus' fatherless birth. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 19:22/q23 Where and when was this surah revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the surah's Meccan classification has an exception for its prostration verse.
- 19:22/q23/p1 The surah is Meccan like the preceding surah. — Abu Hayyan states this general classification. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 19:22/q23/p2 Muqatil excludes the prostration verse, placing its revelation in Medina after the migration to Abyssinia. — Abu Hayyan reports Muqatil's exception to the surah's Meccan classification. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
