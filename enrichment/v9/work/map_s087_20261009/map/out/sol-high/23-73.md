# 23:73: 6 questions

## 23:73/q01 Who are the people Muhammad calls?  [تَدْعُوهُمْ · referent]
- 23:73/q01/p1 Muhammad's own people, identified as the Quraysh disbelievers or Meccan idolaters. — Al-Wahidi names the Quraysh disbelievers; Kur'an Yolu calls them Meccan idolaters; the dream reported by Ibn Kathir speaks of the Prophet and his people as travelers. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 23:73/q02 What is the straight path to which he calls them?  [صِرَٰطٍۢ مُّسْتَقِيمٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the path is identified with Islam and its practice, described through truth and straightness, or read as a spiritual way of following or attainment.
- 23:73/q02/p1 Islam, the upright and sound religion, embracing monotheism, sincere worship, and obedience to religious law. — Muqatil and al-Tha'labi identify it as Islam without crookedness; Yahya ibn Sallam calls it sound religion and the road to Paradise. Al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi call it upright religion and explain that religion is called a road because it leads to Paradise; al-Qurtubi also glosses sirat as road. Al-Wahidi says Muhammad invites the Quraysh disbelievers to Islam; al-Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, and al-Nasafi likewise identify Islam, with al-Nasafi adding that they should answer him. Al-Tabrisi specifies monotheism, sincere worship of God, and action according to religious law. Kur'an Yolu says the Prophet's sole aim is inviting to Islam, for which God will reward him better; it describes the Meccan idolaters as straying from right belief and conduct despite his efforts. Qatada's reported parable in al-Durr depicts invitation to Islam as a familiar guide leading someone from a rough route onto a broad, easy road. [14 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 23:73/q02/p2 A clear, true, consistently straight way without crookedness, leading its followers to their goal. — Al-Razi says proof establishes this way as sound and more fully straight than an ordinary road. Al-Baydawi, al-Bursevi, and al-Alusi say sound minds attest to its straightness and find no deviation that could justify accusing or suspecting the Messenger. Ibn Kathir restates that the Prophet calls to a straight path. Al-Biqa'i says the reminder offers no crookedness or valid flaw and that following it reaches the goal and every honor. Qatada, as reported by al-Durr, glosses it as a path without crookedness. Al-Tabataba'i says truth is one, its parts do not conflict, its judgment is clear and consistent, and it reaches the intended end; because the Prophet guides to truth he guides to this path. Islahi calls it the clear salvation path reaching the true destination, whose following requires belief in the Hereafter; Asad says those denying the afterlife deviate from it. [13 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 23:73/q02/p3 The direct and sound path of following, whose end is disclosed through correctness and truth. — Some authorities reported by al-Sulami describe it as the most direct route to uprightness and emphasize following. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 23:73/q02/p4 Paths of attainment fit for those steadfast with God, seeking none other and claiming no spiritual rank. — Ibn Ata, reported by al-Sulami, says the Prophet leads people on paths of attainment, but only those steadfast with God are fit for that way; they claim neither rank nor station for themselves. [1 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]

## 23:73/q03 What pronunciations of ṣirāṭ are reported?  [صِرَٰطٍۢ · readings]
turns on: Phonetic realization of the initial consonant of ṣirāṭ.
- 23:73/q03/p1 Pronounce it with sīn in place of ṣād. — Qiraat-ER calls this a phonetic alternation between non-emphatic and emphatic sibilants, with no change in meaning. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(ibdāl|sp)]
- 23:73/q03/p2 Pronounce ṣād with ishmām toward zāy, producing an intermediate sound. — Qiraat-ER attests this pronunciation for Hamza and Khalaf. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(ibdāl)]

## 23:73/q04 Why do the hearers turn away from the straight path?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes variously attribute refusal to hostility to truth, failure to discern or reflect, ancestral loyalty, and disbelief in the Hereafter.
- 23:73/q04/p1 They hate the truth and obstinately resist a call whose objections have been answered. — Al-Baydawi lists hatred of truth after the grounds for denial have been removed by 23:70-73. Al-Bursevi calls their refusal to follow or listen obstinate despite refutation from every angle. Al-Tabataba'i says those who hate truth turn away from the path to which the Prophet guides. Al-Zamakhshari's account, praised by al-Alusi, says they disliked the manifest truth and rejected the saving reminder despite miracles. Islahi says they acknowledge truth but contradict it and stray. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 23:73/q04/p2 They lack discernment or fail to reflect on the evidence. — Al-Baydawi presents lack of discernment as another explanation after the verses eliminate grounds for denial and accusation. Al-Zamakhshari, in the account praised by al-Alusi, exposes their failure to reflect although the Prophet was known to them, sought no payment or false distinction, and displayed truth and miracles. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 23:73/q04/p3 Blind loyalty to ancestral ways keeps them from the call. — Al-Zamakhshari, in the account praised by al-Alusi, includes blind ancestral loyalty among their failures. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 23:73/q04/p4 Disbelief in the Hereafter causes them to evade and stray from the salvation path. — Asad says those who deny the afterlife deviate from the straight way. Islahi says following the path to the true destination requires belief in the Hereafter; hearts lacking that belief evade it and inevitably go astray. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 23:73/q05 What reported illustrations explain the Prophet's call to this path?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports use different events and images to portray guidance and responses to it.
- 23:73/q05/p1 Two angels liken him to a guide leading stranded travelers from barren ground toward gardens and water; some follow and some stay. — Ibn Kathir reports Ibn Abbas's dream of the Prophet and his people, with two angels explaining the travelers and their guide. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 23:73/q05/p2 The Prophet restrains his community from the Fire, but they rush toward it and some arrive at his Basin bearing misappropriated livestock. — Ibn Kathir reports Umar's account of the Prophet's saying: he will disavow those with misappropriated livestock after delivering his message. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب)]
- 23:73/q05/p3 He invites a reluctant man to Islam by likening it to following a familiar guide from a rough route onto a broad, easy road. — Al-Durr reports this parable from Qatada. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 23:73/q06 How does the verse fit the passage and related Quranic themes?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes connect the verse to different surrounding arguments, the previous reward verse, other Quranic passages, and the surah's theme of opposition.
- 23:73/q06/p1 The preceding arguments remove excuses for rejecting Muhammad and establish the truth and nobility of his call. — Al-Razi says God establishes the Messenger's case after exposing his opponents' position. Al-Baydawi says 23:70-73 eliminate grounds for denial and accusation. Al-Biqa'i says all objections have been removed and the call comes from the King of kings through the most devoted and noble of creation; his reminder remains free of valid flaw. Al-Bursevi says the unbelievers have been refuted from every angle. Al-Zamakhshari, whose summary al-Alusi praises, adduces the Prophet's known character and fitness, his lack of a demand for payment or false distinction, his call to Islam, manifest truth and miracles, and the opponents' charges of madness and rejection of the saving reminder. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)+]
- 23:73/q06/p2 After the question of payment, it affirms that the Prophet's sole aim is to call to Islam and God will reward him better. — Kur'an Yolu relates the verse to 23:72 and its discussion of the Prophet's reward. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 23:73/q06/p3 It rebukes the straying disbelievers while reassuring the Prophet that his path is right and their fate will become clear. — Islahi reads the passage as a rebuke of carelessness followed by reassurance not to lose hope; he says its concluding challenge exposes people who affirm truth and then assert the opposite. Kur'an Yolu says the Meccan idolaters strayed from right belief and conduct despite the Prophet's efforts. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 23:73/q06/p4 Its straight path invites comparison with the straight path in 1:6. — The Study Quran directs readers to 1:6 for the straight path. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 23:73/q06/p5 It belongs to the surah's recurring theme of affluent and powerful people's opposition to prophets. — The Study Quran points to 7:59-62 and 7:136 as other Quranic examples of that opposition. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
