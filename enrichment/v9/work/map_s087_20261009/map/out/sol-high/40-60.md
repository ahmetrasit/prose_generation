# 40:60: 27 questions

## 40:60/q01 What does “Your Lord said” refer to?  [وَقَالَ رَبُّكُمُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the past verb denotes inward speech, earlier revelation, or a present saying.
- 40:60/q01/p1 God's inward speech or will regarding what He would say in creating. — Ibn ʿĀshūr offers this as one possible reference for the past tense. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 40:60/q01/p2 Earlier literal divine speech in revelation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the earlier command at 40:14 and notes 2:186 was revealed later. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 40:60/q01/p3 A figurative past tense meaning “Your Lord says.” — Ibn ʿĀshūr treats this as a third possibility. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:60/q02 Why does the verse say “your Lord”?  [رَبُّكُمُ · rhetoric]
- 40:60/q02/p1 Lordship signals the addressees' duty to obey. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the wording invokes neither favors nor divine attributes here. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:60/q03 Who is addressed by the command?  [رَبُّكُمُ ٱدْعُونِىٓ · referent]
turns on: Whether the address is localized, restricted to faithful callers, or extends to humanity and disbelievers.
- 40:60/q03/p1 The people of Yemen. — Muqātil identifies the addressees this way. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 40:60/q03/p2 Believers or God's servants who pray in faith. — Rāzī links God's servant address to believing prayer at 3:193 and 39:53; unbelievers are excluded because they did not invoke Him. Qushayrī presents the believer address as a conditional reading, with reward potentially deferred. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; QUSHAYRI(غير مسمى)]
- 40:60/q03/p3 People generally, including disbelievers and the first hearers. — Bursevī says God addresses people; Kuranyolu says the verse broadly calls disbelievers to prayer and response. Ibn ʿĀshūr connects the call with warning polytheists, while embracing believers' pure worship. [3 notes: BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:60/q04 What does “Call upon Me” ask people to do?  [ٱدْعُونِىٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether duʿāʾ names worship, literal asking, both together, or a specified practice or inward state.
- 40:60/q04/p1 Worship God alone, affirm His oneness, and obey Him. — Al-Ṭabarī, Zamakhsharī and others cite the following “My worship” and Qurʾānic usage; Ibn ʿAbbās glosses the call as tawḥīd, Jarīr as worship, and Biqāʿī includes prayer and other acts of submission. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this a less common figurative use of duʿāʾ; Elmalılı adds practical striving. [30 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,جرير بن عبد الله); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أكثر المفسرين); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي); ELMALILI(Ibn Abbas, Dahhâk ve Mücâhid); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî); STUDYQURAN(many commentators); RAZI-FULL-]
- 40:60/q04/p2 Ask or supplicate to God for needs, with a promised answer. — Al-Suddī, Mawardī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī state “ask Me and I will give”; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls asking the common literal sense. Rāzī argues the apparent sense needs no shift: supplication itself displays servitude, so refusing it can be pride. Al-Kashf and Ālūsī favor this sense for wording; the surah contrasts arrogant disputation with humble asking (40:69). [21 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(السدي,الكشف); ELMALILI(Süddî); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ITQAN; THALABI(بعضهم); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 40:60/q04/p3 The command encompasses both asking and worship. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says both senses of the shared term operate together, while response likewise covers granting needs and accepting worship. Biqāʿī includes worshipful submission and requests for victory; Elmalılı says prayer and worship entail one another. Ṭabāṭabāʾī uses the terms broadly. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 40:60/q04/p4 Remember God and ask Him. — Some interpreters reported by Thaʿlabī, Baghawī and Qurṭubī include remembrance alongside prayer and asking. [3 notes: THALABI(بعضهم); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 40:60/q04/p5 Leave sins, since abandoning disobedience is itself supplication. — Sufyān or al-Thawrī repeatedly answers a request for prayer this way; Ālūsī explains that persistent sin contradicts genuine inward seeking, even after many utterances. Qurṭubī reports it as another explanation; ʿĀʾisha reports prophetic gloss of supplication as seeking forgiveness. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(سفيان); THALABI(سفيان); KASHSHAF-FULL(الثوري); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الثوري); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الثوري); BIQAI-FULL(الثوري); DURR-FULL(عائشة); ALUSI-FULL(سفيان الثوري); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Süfyân-ı Sevrî)]
- 40:60/q04/p6 Obey God so that He grants the response. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk's gloss pairs obedience with divine granting. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 40:60/q04/p7 Seek God from God, finding Him rather than a requested object. — Elmalılı says finding God yields everything because all things obey His creative command at 36:82; he cites a report that whoever seeks God finds Him. Shiblī's outward exhortation is to invoke God alone, for finding Him is finding everything. [3 notes: ELMALILI; SULAMI(الشبلي)]
- 40:60/q04/p8 Respond to God while invoking Him. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ reads a reciprocal command: invoke and respond to Me, and I will answer you. [1 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
- 40:60/q04/p9 Invoke God from desperate need and selfless inward awareness. — Al-Warrāq requires utter need and no refuge but God; Shiblī says the inward self must disappear in the unseen and the outward call must be to God alone. [2 notes: SULAMI(الشبلي,الوراق)]

## 40:60/q05 How is the imperative and its response parsed?  [ٱدْعُونِىٓ أَسْتَجِبْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether udʿūnī is an uninflected imperative or jussive after an omitted lām.
- 40:60/q05/p1 The imperative is uninflected; the response is jussive through an implied condition. — Naḥḥās reports the Basran analysis of udʿūnī, with astajib jussive as the answer; Ālūsī paraphrases: if you call on Me, I answer. [2 notes: NAHHAS; ALUSI-FULL]
- 40:60/q05/p2 The imperative is jussive after a deleted lām; the response is jussive. — Naḥḥās attributes the first analysis to al-Farrāʾ and gives the conditional response analysis for astajib. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 40:60/q06 What is the initial hamza of “I will answer”?  [أَسْتَجِبْ · grammar]
- 40:60/q06/p1 A pronounced prefix hamza, not hamzat al-waṣl. — Al-Akhfash identifies it as the afʿal prefix, analogous to imperfect yāʾ and tāʾ, pronounced in connection and isolation; Naḥḥās calls it hamzat al-qaṭʿ, like nūn in nafʿal, while waṣl drops when unnecessary. [2 notes: AKHFASH; NAHHAS]

## 40:60/q07 What response does God promise?  [أَسْتَجِبْ لَكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the call is worship, a request, or both, and whether response must match the request.
- 40:60/q07/p1 He accepts worship, rewards obedience, forgives sins and shows mercy. — Al-Ṭabarī speaks of pardon and mercy; Ibn ʿAbbās pairs tawḥīd with forgiveness, Jarīr worship with answer; Mujāhid and Zamakhsharī say response means reward. Baghawī says acceptance is termed response. Ibn ʿĀshūr says repentance from shirk is assured; al-Ḥasan says God rewards believers who do good with increase. [29 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,جرير بن عبد الله); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,فرقة); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أكثر المفسرين); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,الضحاك,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(السدي); ELMALILI(Ibn Abbas, Dahhâk ve Mücâhid); IBNASHUR-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî)]
- 40:60/q07/p2 He grants what the petitioner asks. — Al-Suddī says “ask and I will give”; Abū Ḥayyān transmits the same, and Ālūsī calls it an alternative. Ibn ʿĀshūr says granting a request is istijāba's common literal sense. Bursevī cites God's abundant treasury. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي); ALUSI-FULL(السدي); ELMALILI(Süddî); IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 40:60/q07/p3 He responds in another beneficial form, through averted harm or stored reward. — Tustarī lists the exact request, an unknown answer, harm averted or a recorded good deed. Qushayrī says the requested thing or better, possibly deferred to the Hereafter. Qurṭubī cites an Abū Saʿīd report under 2:186; Biqāʿī adds worldly granting, equivalent harm removed, or afterlife reward; Bursevī and Study Quran give the same alternatives. [10 notes: TUSTARI; QUSHAYRI(غير مسمى); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(ويقال); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAjībah); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 40:60/q07/p4 He grants the seeker Himself, so finding God fulfills the prayer. — Elmalılı says the prayer seeking God is described as never rejected and cites a report meaning whoever seeks God finds Him; all things obey His creative command (36:82). [2 notes: ELMALILI]
- 40:60/q07/p5 Response can mean both granting requests and accepting worship. — Ibn ʿĀshūr treats astajib as literal and figurative at once, cued by the context, making a concise comprehensive promise; Ṭabāṭabāʾī treats calling and response broadly. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 40:60/q08 What qualifies or explains the promise to answer prayer?  [أَسْتَجِبْ لَكُمْ · theology]
turns on: Whether the unqualified promise is read at face value or in light of divine will, benefit, sincerity and prerequisites.
- 40:60/q08/p1 God answers according to His will, wisdom, benefit and the request's conditions. — Qushayrī, Rāzī, Qurṭubī, Ālūsī and Elmalılı cite 6:41 to qualify the open wording; Ṭabrisī says granting a harmful request would be wrong. Ibn ʿAṭiyya warns against overstepping in prayer, citing a person who requested a white palace in Paradise. Elmalılı cites good words and righteous deeds at 35:10. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes asking from assured acceptance of repentance from shirk; Islāḥī sees wise delay or denial. [16 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الكعبي); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 40:60/q08/p2 The wording promises an answer without an expressed condition. — Khālid al-Rabʿī contrasts conditional promises at 2:25 and 40:14 with this one and compares 10:2; Qurṭubī notes the unrestricted/qualified distinction. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says open-ended promises surpass ordinary causal expectations, while Elmalılı warns this is not universal quantification. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(خالد الربعي); TABATABAI; ELMALILI]
- 40:60/q08/p3 A sincere, wholly God-directed heart receives an answer. — Rāzī says reliance on wealth, rank, people or one's own work makes spoken prayer tongue-only; near death, detachment from all else makes acceptance likely. Yaḥyā b. Muʿādh adds sincere reliance; Tustarī speaks of prayer with marwa. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; SULAMI(يحيى بن معاذ); TUSTARI]
- 40:60/q08/p4 Repentance, lawful sustenance, Sunna observance and inward care prepare a prayer for acceptance. — Muḥammad b. ʿAlī and al-Ḥakīm al-Tirmidhī warn of rejection without repentance, lawful food, following the Sunna and guarding the inner self; Qushayrī, Biqāʿī and Bursevī cite lawful food as the key's teeth. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ adds humble heart, sincerity, dawn and blessings on Muḥammad. [6 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,محمد بن علي); QUSHAYRI(غير مسمى); BIQAI-FULL(القشيري); BURSEVI(الحكيم الترمذي,قيل)]
- 40:60/q08/p5 Whoever prays receives a response, and turning to prayer itself opens divine aid. — Anas reports that when God opens prayer He answers; Abū ʿAbd Allāh parallels prayer/answer with gratitude/increase and trust/sufficiency at 14:7 and 65:3. Kaʿb's report treats this response as a gift to the community. Ibn Kathīr calls the invitation and promised answer divine favor and generosity. [10 notes: DURR-FULL(أنس بن مالك); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); IBNABIHATIM(كعب الأحبار); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة عن كعب); KASHSHAF-FULL(كعب); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,كعب الأحبار); IBNKATHIR-FULL(كعب الأحبار); ELMALILI(Ka‘b)]

## 40:60/q09 How is the yāʾ of “Call upon Me” read?  [ٱدْعُونِىٓ · readings]
- 40:60/q09/p1 Ibn Kathīr opens the yāʾ before the following alif. — Ibn al-Jazarī singles out udʿūnī astajib and the parallel fadhkurūnī adhkurkum. Ibn Mujāhid discusses the rule for added yāʾ before a separate alif after a jussive verb, citing 2:152 and another parallel. [3 notes: NASHR(ابن كثير); IBNMUJAHID]

## 40:60/q10 What status does supplication have as worship?  [ٱدْعُونِىٓ عِبَادَتِى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the emphasis falls on supplication itself, its inward humility, or other devotion that can stand in its place.
- 40:60/q10/p1 Supplication itself is worship, and is among its greatest or best forms. — The Prophet states “supplication is worship” and recites the verse in the Nuʿmān reports; Tirmidhī calls it ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ and notes Dharr is trustworthy, while al-Ḥākim calls a chain sound. Anas says it is all, not half, of worship. Ibn ʿAbbās, Abū Jaʿfar and Abū ʿAbd Allāh rank it as best or greatest; Ṭabarī, Ṭabrisī, Ālūsī, Ibn Kathīr and others infer its merit from the verse. ʿĀʾisha reports the Prophet answering that the best worship is a person's supplication for himself. Peygamber); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet); TIRMIDHI(النبي)]
- 40:60/q10/p2 Supplication displays poverty, humility, need, refuge and reliance on God. — Tustarī and Sahl call it the best deed for these qualities; Qushayrī, Rāzī and Study Quran stress confession of servitude. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ describes its humble heart and sincerity. [7 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء,سهل); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 40:60/q10/p3 Obedience or remembrance can replace asking and receive an even better gift. — A Prophetic report in Kashshāf and a sacred report in Study Quran say one occupied by obedience or remembrance receives more than an asker. Al-Ḥasan's “act and rejoice” adds reward for believers doing good. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN(ḥadīth qudsī)]

## 40:60/q11 What practice does the verse encourage when asking God?  [ٱدْعُونِىٓ · law]
turns on: Whether the action stressed is ordinary asking, persistence, preparation, refusing fatalism, or context-sensitive restraint.
- 40:60/q11/p1 Ask God directly for every need, even a small one. — Anas transmits the sandal-strap example; Islāḥī says no deity-intermediary is needed and only God can accept requests; Bursevī cites al-Kāshifī on God's abundant treasury. Kuranyolu likewise calls for direct prayer. [7 notes: THALABI(أنس); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أنس); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 40:60/q11/p2 Pray persistently and seize occasions of mercy. — Anas says God loves persistent petitioners, and Ibn Kathīr cites Sufyān and a poet: God dislikes being left unasked, unlike people. A note found on Muḥammad b. Maslama's sword recalls one timely prayer that meets mercy; Wahb says affliction can elicit prayer. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(الشاعر,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,سفيان الثوري); DURR-FULL(أنس بن مالك,وهب بن منبه)]
- 40:60/q11/p3 Pray with attentive, candid, accurate words and appropriate timing. — Bursevī links attention, frankness and accuracy to promptness, fulfillment and giving; Ibn ʿAṭāʾ names heart-presence, sincerity, dawn and blessings on Muḥammad as pillars, wings, time and cause. [2 notes: BURSEVI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
- 40:60/q11/p4 Do not abandon prayer on the assumption that matters are already decided. — Abū ʿAbd Allāh cites both ends of the verse; Ṭabāṭabāʾī explains the worship argument supports praying and the promise rebuts fatalistic restraint. Biqāʿī says prayer remains necessary despite the promise; Elmalılı says the command establishes particular human will against compulsion. [4 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 40:60/q11/p5 Abstaining without arrogance may be blameworthy or apt according to circumstances. — Ālūsī cites the report that failure to call incurs anger, but tentatively gives Abraham's reported silence in the fire because God knew his condition as an apt exception. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة,إبراهيم)]

## 40:60/q12 Can supplication change a decreed calamity?  [ٱدْعُونِىٓ أَسْتَجِبْ لَكُمْ · theology]
turns on: Whether and how the prayer affects an outcome governed by God's knowledge, wisdom or decree.
- 40:60/q12/p1 Prayer can benefit against calamities already present or yet to come. — Muʿādh explicitly urges prayer for both; Ṭabāṭabāʾī takes open divine promises to transcend ordinary causal expectation. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(معاذ); TABATABAI]
- 40:60/q12/p2 Prayer draws the servant into reliance, while God's wise decree still governs outcomes. — Al-Kaʿbī poses the objection that God already does what is best and replies with the benefit of reliance; he says the deterministic objection applies to acts whether known to occur or not. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Muslims disagree with Muʿtazilites over repelling decree and this verse does not decide it because responses are interpreted differently. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(الكعبي); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:60/q13 What does “My worship” mean here?  [عِبَادَتِى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ʿibāda keeps its general sense or specifies supplication as an act of worship.
- 40:60/q13/p1 Exclusive worship, monotheism and obedience to God. — Muqātil identifies the rejected worship as monotheism; Ṭabarī, Thaʿlabī and Bursevī stress worship and oneness; Ibn ʿĀshūr says Qurʾānic worship acknowledges God alone. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI(أكثر المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 40:60/q13/p2 Supplication and asking God. — Al-Suddī and a Prophetic gloss make this specific referent explicit. Qushayrī, Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī say arrogant refusal of supplication is condemned; Bursevī calls this use of the general word for a particular act figurative, or sees refusal to ask treated as refusal to worship. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI(السدي); QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(الصحيفة السجادية)]
- 40:60/q13/p3 Both supplication and other forms of worship. — Ibn Kathīr says refusal to call on God and to affirm His oneness; Ālūsī says the offense is unbelief whether the refused act is prayer or another worship; Biqāʿī includes worshipful submission in any form. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 40:60/q14 What is the arrogance condemned here?  [يَسْتَكْبِرُونَ عَنْ عِبَادَتِى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the refusal targets worship generally, asking specifically, or apparent prayers directed to a false conception of God.
- 40:60/q14/p1 Proud refusal of exclusive worship and obedience, including turning to idols. — Ṭabarī, Muqātil and others associate it with rejecting God's divinity alone. Ibn ʿĀshūr says polytheists turned to God only when partners could not answer (17:67), and the warning counsels against idols. Kuranyolu includes refusal to submit to the Prophet's religion; Islāḥī says evasion stems from political ambition and fear of inferiority at 40:56. [11 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 40:60/q14/p2 Proud refusal to invoke or ask God. — Qushayrī, Ṭabrisī and Abū Ḥayyān interpret the refused worship as prayer. Biqāʿī calls pride a usual reason to stop asking; Ālūsī says affluent excess can breed this pride and stresses that asking is humbling. Study Quran likewise describes most noncallers as proud. [11 notes: QUSHAYRI; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI(الصحيفة السجادية)]
- 40:60/q14/p3 False prayer directed to a partner rather than God. — Some in Sulamī warn that one may think one prays to God while really worshipping another; Qushayrī and Bursevī say the polytheist does not truly call God. Bursevī adds deniers of attributes and anthropomorphists as misconceiving the incomparable, hearing and seeing God. [3 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); QUSHAYRI(غير مسمى); BURSEVI]

## 40:60/q15 Who are “those who are arrogant”?  [ٱلَّذِينَ يَسْتَكْبِرُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the relative clause generalizes the warning or identifies its first opponents.
- 40:60/q15/p1 All who arrogantly refuse God's worship. — Itqān says the relative clause is general, as with those who profess God and persevere (41:30) and those who strive (29:69). Ibn ʿĀshūr says the relative clause hints at the reason for the command. [2 notes: ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 40:60/q15/p2 Meccan unbelievers or the polytheist opponents. — Muqātil names Meccan unbelievers; Islāḥī links the warning with opponents of 40:56 and their political motives; Ibn ʿĀshūr discusses polytheists turning to God only when idols fail. [3 notes: MUQATIL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:60/q16 What does their entry into Hell teach?  [سَيَدْخُلُونَ جَهَنَّمَ دَاخِرِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether humiliation is described solely as the stated Hell entry or also as deprivation of God.
- 40:60/q16/p1 Pride ends in certain, fitting humiliation and punishment. — Muqātil, Ṭabarī, Zamakhsharī, Biqāʿī and others set humbled entry against former arrogance. Rāzī calls the severe threat itself kindness; Islāḥī links humiliation to earlier evasion. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the warning explains God's command and desire that servants avoid punishment. [12 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 40:60/q16/p2 The arrogant suffer separation from God as well as humiliation. — Elmalılı describes a Hell of separation and deprivation for those who refuse worship or seeking God through prayer. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 40:60/q17 How is “they will enter” read and construed?  [سَيَدْخُلُونَ · readings]
turns on: Active entry versus causative admission, with varied transmissions from several readers.
- 40:60/q17/p1 Active: they will enter Hell. — Al-Fārisī cites entrance commands at 15:46 and 16:29; readers including Ḥafṣ, Nāfiʿ, Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī and Ibn ʿĀmir are variously assigned this reading, and some transmissions of ʿĀṣim and Abū ʿAmr differ. Ibn al-Jazarī lists the remaining readers as active, with yāʾ fatḥa and khāʾ ḍamma. [12 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(الباقون وحفص عن عاصم وأبو عمرو في غير رواية عباس); THALABI(أبو عمرو وعاصم); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو جعفر وأبو بكر والآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو جعفر وابن كثير وأبو بكر غير الشموني وسهل؛ والباقون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير وأبو بكر عن عاصم وعباس بن الفضل عن أبي عمرو); QURTUBI-FULL(الباقون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(مكي وأبو عمرو); NASHR(الباقون); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 40:60/q17/p2 Passive: they will be admitted or made to enter Hell. — Ibn Kathīr, Abū Jaʿfar, Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim, and Ruways appear in several lists; transmissions from Abū ʿAmr and ʿĀṣim vary. Al-Fārisī relates the passive to causative adkhalū, whose active takes two objects as at 40:46; Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies punishment angels as agents. The reading has yāʾ ḍamma and khāʾ fatḥa; Qiraat-ER stresses imposed entry. [17 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وعاصم في رواية أبي بكر وأبو عمرو في رواية عباس بن الفضل); THALABI(أبو عمرو وعاصم,ابن كثير); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو جعفر وأبو بكر والآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو جعفر وابن كثير وأبو بكر غير الشموني وسهل؛ والباقون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير وأبو بكر عن عاصم وعباس بن الفضل عن أبي عمرو); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن كثير وابن محيصن ورويس عن يعقوب وعياش عن أبي عمرو وأبو بكر والمفضل عن عاصم); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو بكر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; NASHR(ابن كثير وأبو جعفر ورويس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو جعفر ورويس عن يعقوب); QIRAAT-ER(Pss)]

## 40:60/q18 What does “dākhirīn” mean and how does it function?  [دَاخِرِينَ · meaning]
- 40:60/q18/p1 Humbled, lowly, abased or subjugated as they enter. — Ibn ʿĀshūr parses it as a circumstantial accusative from the subject of “enter,” cites the verb patterns of manaʿa and fariḥa and 16:49; Bursevī adds the Arabic root sense of becoming small and a Persian gloss. Other sources give humbled and abject as equivalent glosses. [18 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(السدي); THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو جعفر وأبو بكر والآخرون); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 40:60/q19 Why are calling and worship paired in this wording?  [ٱدْعُونِىٓ عِبَادَتِى · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the later clause selects worship, identifies asking as worship, or invites both senses together.
- 40:60/q19/p1 The later mention of worship explains the earlier command as worship. — Ṭabarī's reading, Zamakhsharī, Baghawī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and Kuranyolu use the following clause as proof; Ṭabrisī says calling worship “supplication” makes reward a “response.” [9 notes: TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî)]
- 40:60/q19/p2 Supplication is itself worship, so proud refusal to ask counts as proud refusal to worship. — Rāzī argues no shift from literal asking is needed; Bayḍāwī and Bursevī explain the warning by treating supplication as a form of worship; Ālūsī prefers asking since it requires fewer figurative moves. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the clause promotes asking and warns against turning to idols. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 40:60/q19/p3 The pairing deliberately combines worship and asking by concise cross-reference. — Biqāʿī says each half implies the term explicit in the other; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls duʿāʾ a shared homonym and astajib both literal and figurative, and Elmalılı says worship and prayer entail each other. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:60/q20 How does this command connect with the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Which nearby theme is taken to explain the command and its placement.
- 40:60/q20/p1 After the Hour and resurrection proofs, the verse urges obedient prayer and monotheism. — Rāzī says resurrection's certainty makes obedience urgent. Ṭabāṭabāʾī recalls the believer from Pharaoh's family warning about the Hour; Islāḥī and Kuranyolu see a turn from the Hereafter to direct prayer and salvation through faith and good works (40:58–59). [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 40:60/q20/p2 Prior favors and proofs of God ground the call, which leads to warning and creation. — Rāzī says God granted greater unasked favors and furnished ten signs of His existence, power and wisdom. Biqāʿī says refusing His call and disputing His signs link to 40:69. Study Quran sees divine aid to messengers ending in this warning before divine creative power; Islāḥī says 56–85 argues for oneness and the Hereafter. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 40:60/q20/p3 The verse resumes earlier calls for exclusive prayer and contrasts believers with idolaters. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links 40:12, 13, 14, 18 and 50: sincere prayer, rebuke of polytheists and the failed prayer of disbelievers. Al-Kashf ties it to the surah's rebuke of disputants at 40:4 and 40:69; Ālūsī finds its wording clearer than its contextual case. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف)]
- 40:60/q20/p4 The surrounding emphasis on creation and blessings shapes the next wording. — Samarrai's three discussions point to 40:57, 61 and 62: “creator of everything” comes before “no deity but He” because this passage emphasizes creation, including night and day; Ibn ʿĀshūr links arrogance to ingratitude for those blessings. [4 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:60/q21 What other verses illuminate the promise of response?  [ٱدْعُونِىٓ أَسْتَجِبْ لَكُمْ · links]
turns on: Whether another promise supplies a parallel, a qualification, or an associated divine gift.
- 40:60/q21/p1 It parallels the promise to answer callers at 2:186. — Biqāʿī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Asad and Study Quran point to 2:186; Ṭabāṭabāʾī directs readers there for fuller treatment and Rāzī and Qurṭubī refer back to discussion in al-Baqara. [9 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري)]
- 40:60/q21/p2 The promise is qualified by the divine will expressed in 6:41. — Qushayrī, Rāzī, Qurṭubī, Ālūsī and Elmalılı read the unrestricted command together with 6:41; Rāzī also cites 27:62. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 40:60/q21/p3 It belongs with promises of sufficiency, increase and forgiveness. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī pairs it with 39:36 and 2:186; Abū ʿAbd Allāh pairs prayer/response with gratitude/increase at 14:7 and trust/sufficiency at 65:3. Anas reports divine forgiveness for one who calls and hopes, even with immense sins. [3 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); DURR-FULL(أنس بن مالك)]

## 40:60/q22 What report marks this promise as a gift to the community?  [ٱدْعُونِىٓ أَسْتَجِبْ لَكُمْ · reports]
turns on: The wording and transmitter of the privilege report, including which verses identify the other gifts.
- 40:60/q22/p1 Kaʿb lists answered prayer among three privileges earlier limited to prophets. — The other privileges are witness over peoples (2:143) and no religious hardship (usually 22:78; Kashshāf and Elmalılı give 5:6, and Elmalılı also 33:38). Qurṭubī says Kaʿb's statement is unlikely to be personal opinion. Earlier people reportedly brought needs to prophets as intermediaries. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(كعب الأحبار); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة عن كعب); KASHSHAF-FULL(كعب); QURTUBI-FULL(خالد الربعي,كعب الأحبار); IBNKATHIR-FULL(كعب الأحبار); ELMALILI(Ka‘b)]
- 40:60/q22/p2 A Prophetic report from ʿUbāda lists the same three gifts. — Qurṭubī cites al-Tirmidhī al-Ḥakīm's Nawādir al-uṣūl: invitation to pray for a response, no hardship and witness over humanity (2:143, 22:78). [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 40:60/q22/p3 Kaʿb states more simply that the chosen person receives a direct invitation to ask. — Al-Durr reports this formulation as a privilege denied other communities. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(كعب)]

## 40:60/q23 What reports warn against neglecting supplication?  [يَسْتَكْبِرُونَ عَنْ عِبَادَتِى · reports]
turns on: Whether the report states divine anger, describes the arrogant's punishment, or warns against asking others.
- 40:60/q23/p1 One who does not ask God incurs His anger. — Baghawī and Durr cite this Prophetic saying; Ibn Kathīr gives Ahmad's versions, calls one chain acceptable and names Abū al-Malīḥ and Abū Ṣāliḥ in another. Ālūsī lists Ahmad, Ibn Abī Shayba and al-Ḥākim. Ḥanān b. Sudayr reports no one dearer to God than the asker and none more hateful than the arrogant refuser. [6 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI(حنان بن سدير عن أبيه); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة)]
- 40:60/q23/p2 The arrogant will be raised tiny, humiliated and imprisoned in fiery Bulas. — Ibn Kathīr transmits this Prophetic warning, including a drink of the foul fluid of Hell's inhabitants. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 40:60/q23/p3 A report urges asking God rather than others, to avoid His anger. — Ibn Kathīr relates a man in Byzantine lands hearing a voice ask why someone who knows God seeks needs from another or risks divine anger to please people; the voice says it is human and urges attention to what matters. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(رجل)]

## 40:60/q24 What inward discipline makes the call and response real?  [ٱدْعُونِىٓ أَسْتَجِبْ لَكُمْ · inward]
turns on: Whether inward response depends on obedience, single-hearted need, divine enabling, or knowing the true God.
- 40:60/q24/p1 Prayer and answer form reciprocal spiritual pairs. — Qushayrī reports pairings of prayer with obedience, freedom from heedlessness, repentance, effort and asking; their answers are reward, immediacy, grace, relief and generosity. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ reads the command as respond to Me while invoking Me. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI(غير مسمى); SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
- 40:60/q24/p2 The supplicant must turn wholly to God in poverty, trust and self-effacement. — Al-Warrāq speaks of no refuge but God, Yaḥyā b. Muʿādh of sincere reliance, and Shiblī of absence of self inwardly and invocation of God alone outwardly. Rāzī says prayer only on the tongue while relying on means is not genuine. [4 notes: SULAMI(الشبلي,الوراق,يحيى بن معاذ); RAZI-FULL]
- 40:60/q24/p3 Prayer and divine answering are reciprocal appointments. — Al-Naṣrābādhī says God deputized creatures to pray and Himself to answer; those deprived of the appointment lack both prayer and response. [1 notes: SULAMI(النصرآباذي)]
- 40:60/q24/p4 An unknown God is not truly invoked. — Qushayrī and Bursevī say idolaters call a partner rather than God; Mūsā b. Jaʿfar transmits al-Ṣādiq's answer to those with unanswered prayers: they invoke one they do not know. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI(غير مسمى); BURSEVI; TABATABAI(موسى بن جعفر عليه السلام)]

## 40:60/q25 How does the verse's warning work rhetorically?  [إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ يَسْتَكْبِرُونَ عَنْ عِبَادَتِى · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the second clause chiefly gives the reason for the command or sharpens the equivalence of prayer and worship.
- 40:60/q25/p1 The warning explains and reinforces the command to call. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the relative clause hints at the command's reason and the Hell warning deters idol worship; Biqāʿī says pride often causes abandonment of prayer. Rāzī calls the threat a kindness. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 40:60/q25/p2 The shift from asking to worship emphasizes that refusal to ask is arrogant refusal of worship. — Bursevī says the second phrase replaces the first for emphasis; Ālūsī says the object “worship” signals humility, because asking God is submission; Biqāʿī sees mutual ellipsis of the two terms. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 40:60/q26 How do descriptions of Hell deepen the warning?  [جَهَنَّمَ · reports]
turns on: Distinct reported images of Hell's severity.
- 40:60/q26/p1 An iron shackle from Hell would crush a mountain to the black water. — ʿAbd al-Razzāq reports the statement from Ibn al-Taymī's father. [1 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(أبوه)]
- 40:60/q26/p2 Arrogant people are confined in Hell's prison and made to drink foul fluid. — Ibn Kathīr reports their resurrection as tiny human forms, covered in humiliation, then confinement in Bulas under fire. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 40:60/q27 What neighboring reports or technical discussions accompany this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which ancillary report or linguistic example a source includes in its discussion.
- 40:60/q27/p1 Reports about the Dajjāl's duration and warning are transmitted nearby. — Al-Durr reports Ibn Masʿūd's forty mornings and successive day lengths of a Friday, month and year; ʿUbayd reports wrath against followers who join for sustenance; Ashʿath's father reports Ibn Masʿūd warning against rumor as decree may arrive faster. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(أشعث بن أبي الشعثاء,ابن مسعود,عبيد بن عمير)]
- 40:60/q27/p2 A divine report combines exclusive worship, reward, answered prayer and reciprocal goodwill. — Ibn Kathīr says Abū Yaʿlā transmits the report of four matters from the Prophet's Lord. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 40:60/q27/p3 General grammatical examples of relative clauses, articles and genitives are discussed. — Itqān treats the relative clause here as general; in adjacent general examples it classifies definite articles by known referent, exhaustive inclusion and genus, and genitives by concision, exaltation and generality, citing 15:42, 39:7 and 24:63; Ibn ʿAbbās glosses chosen servants in the cited examples. [6 notes: ITQAN(ابن عباس)]
- 40:60/q27/p4 Adjacent verses have alternative conjunction and predication analyses. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the conjunction before lākin an embellishment; for the later “Allah” he offers apposition to “your Lord,” or several subject/predicate parses, preferring a fresh explanatory sentence because it suits the creation argument and exposes ingratitude (40:64). He describes “most people” as the numerically larger polytheists and disputes over signs as about oneness and resurrection (40:69–74). [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
