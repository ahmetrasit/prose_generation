# 17:19: 22 questions

## 17:19/q01 What is the Hereafter sought here?  [ٱلْءَاخِرَةَ · referent]
turns on: Whether desire names the ultimate life sought or is restricted to seeking its reward through particular deeds.
- 17:19/q01/p1 Paradise, its abode and delights, or its reward and good. — Ibn ʿAbbās names Paradise; al-Wāḥidī also reports reward or good of the Hereafter and compares 3:145. Ibn Kathīr specifies its abode and delights; al-Ālūsī calls them lasting. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 17:19/q01/p2 The next life itself as the person's ultimate goal. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects narrowing desire to intention in particular deeds; the world is a stage toward this goal. Al-Ālūsī reports a similar reading, arguing that the subsequent effort clause supplies the deed qualification without supplying it also in 17:18. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); TABATABAI+]

## 17:19/q02 What does seeking the Hereafter require as an intention?  [أَرَادَ ٱلْءَاخِرَةَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the verse is applied to the motive of each deed, a person's governing life-goal, or an inward predisposition.
- 17:19/q02/p1 Making the Hereafter one's sincere aim is necessary for deeds to benefit. — Al-Rāzī invokes 53:39 and the saying on intentions, explaining that worship must be intended to illuminate the heart through knowledge and love of God. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī infer intention and sincerity from the lām. Al-Burhān cites the contrast with 17:18 and verses on intended hearts and bound oaths. Kuran Yolu and Study Quran cite the prophetic saying on intentions, including emigration for God versus marriage or worldly benefit; the latter says an earnest attempt need not succeed or be completed. Abū Ḥafṣ excludes ostentation, reputation, self-regard and even desire for compensation. [21 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حفص,بعض المتقدمين); BURSEVI; BURHAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hz. Peygamber,Zemahşerî); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet); SULAMI(أبو حفص); KASHSHAF-FULL(بعض المتقدمين); NASAFI-FULL(بعض السلف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعض المتقدمين); BIQAI-FULL(بعض السلف)]
- 17:19/q02/p2 The aim is a settled orientation toward the next life; ordinary worldly work can serve it. — Kuran Yolu says honest worldly work done under God's rules, with accountability and hope of reward, can seek the Hereafter, while apparent worship can seek worldly gain. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the verses classify ultimate life-goals, not acceptance of each individual act. Al-Kashshāf depicts the believer choosing afterlife riches and accepting worldly poverty. [5 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 17:19/q02/p3 Genuine desire arises from a sound spiritual readiness and seeks latent perfection. — Al-Qāshānī says the desired perfection is potentially present in the decree and effort brings it into actuality; al-Ālūsī likewise describes a clear disposition and sound nature. [3 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 17:19/q03 Why is desire for the Hereafter expressed in the past tense?  [أَرَادَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the past tense emphasizes the seeker's settled state or the singularity of the desired Hereafter.
- 17:19/q03/p1 It portrays afterlife desire as settled, unlike renewed worldly desire. — Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts the past and omitted kāna here with the present tense of worldly desire in 17:18. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 17:19/q03/p2 Desire for the one Hereafter is a single act, unlike recurrent desires for reward with each deed. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares 3:145; he generally says present conditional verbs mark recurrence and past verbs mark actions that do not or should not recur, comparing gratitude and disbelief and accidental and deliberate killing. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 17:19/q04 What does saʿy mean?  [وَسَعَىٰ سَعْيَهَا · meaning]
turns on: The literal motion sense and its extended sense of purposeful deeds.
- 17:19/q04/p1 Effort, work, or action, especially deeds toward the Hereafter. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Dāmghānī and Ibn al-Jawzī give the sense of work or deeds; al-Dāmghānī compares 92:4, 22:51 and 34:5. Al-Ālūsī says it often denotes exertion in good deeds and cites a line by ʿAlqama ibn Saʿd. [6 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(المفسرون); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب)]
- 17:19/q04/p2 Literally brisk walking, slower than running; figuratively earnest striving toward good or evil. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports al-Rāghib's lexical definition. Al-Ālūsī similarly gives brisk walking, while Ibn ʿĀshūr says walking rather than running and compares good works to a traveler's route to the Hereafter. [3 notes: TABATABAI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 17:19/q05 What kind of effort is due to the Hereafter?  [وَسَعَىٰ لَهَا سَعْيَهَا · interpretation]
turns on: The notes describe either prescribed outward deeds or the corresponding inward mode of striving.
- 17:19/q05/p1 The fitting effort is sustained lawful good deeds: doing what God commands and avoiding what He forbids. — Muqātil and al-Ṭabarī specify obedience pleasing to God; Ibn ʿAbbās names obligations and God's rights. Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds good speech under the law; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī exclude invented devotions. Ibn Kathīr names following the Messenger. Al-Biqāʿī names Book and Sunna, requiring action when possible and capacity when action is not. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī requires full effort grounded in certain reason or legal proof. Ibn ʿĀshūr says wishing without the path is self-deception, citing Ibn al-Mubārak's poem about a ship sailing on dry land. Al-Rāzī rejects star or angel intermediaries, images of prophets or saints, and self-killing or burning as invalid devotional paths. [34 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL; SULAMI(القاسم); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(عبد الله بن المبارك); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 17:19/q05/p2 The effort has inward forms: the heart strives toward the Hereafter and aspiration strives toward God. — Some authorities reported by al-Sulamī and al-Ālūsī distinguish bodily striving for worldly life, heart-striving for the Hereafter, and spiritual aspiration toward God. Al-Bursawī describes the lower self inclining to Hell, the spirit to Paradise, and the heart turning by wrath or grace; grace turns it sincerely toward God. Al-Qāshānī makes effort actualize a perfection already latent. [5 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); BURSEVI; QASHANI]

## 17:19/q06 How is سعيها parsed?  [سَعْيَهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the accusative is the verb's object or a cognate accusative specifying its kind.
- 17:19/q06/p1 A direct object: the deeds appropriate to the Hereafter. — Al-Samīn gives this as one parse; al-Nasafī reports direct striving toward the Hereafter. Al-Ṭabarī identifies the feminine pronoun as referring to the Hereafter. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; NASAFI-FULL; TAB-FULL]
- 17:19/q06/p2 A cognate or absolute accusative specifying the kind of striving, with لها meaning for its sake. — Al-Samīn gives the cognate accusative analysis; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the possessive a verbal noun semantically related to its object and the accusative one specifying kind. Al-Ālūsī reports both an act for the Hereafter and an effort suited to it. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 17:19/q07 What does the lām in لها contribute?  [لَهَا · grammar]
- 17:19/q07/p1 It marks purpose or special relation, requiring intention and sincerity toward the Hereafter. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Bursawī infer sincerity from the preposition; al-Ālūsī gives purpose or special relation. Al-Samīn connects it to the cognate accusative parse, 'for its sake.' [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 17:19/q08 What faith must accompany the seeking and effort?  [وَهُوَ مُؤْمِنٌۭ · theology]
- 17:19/q08/p1 Sound, enduring faith is indispensable; it includes God's oneness, revelation and messengers, and belief in requital. — Muqātil stresses oneness; al-Ṭabarī reward; Ibn ʿAṭiyya certainty about God, the Hereafter and His messages; al-Ṭabrisī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Bursawī reject association and denial. Al-Nasafī specifies promises and threats, Ibn Kathīr inward conviction in reward, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī oneness, prophethood and resurrection, and Asad accountability and willing acceptance of revelation. Al-Qāsim warns outwardly upright seekers may lack lasting faith. Al-Ālūsī says faith accompanies both desire and effort; al-Elmalılı says truth and monotheism supply their power and explain the following commands' opening with tawḥīd. Al-Ḥasan reports that God accepts no deed before being pleased with the servant's utterance. [27 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); TAB-FULL; SULAMI(القاسم); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Zemahşerî)]

## 17:19/q09 What conditions together make effort accepted?  [أَرَادَ ٱلْءَاخِرَةَ وَسَعَىٰ لَهَا سَعْيَهَا وَهُوَ مُؤْمِنٌۭ · interpretation]
- 17:19/q09/p1 Afterlife intention, sound or prescribed action, and firm faith must come together. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī and al-Ālūsī transmit an early threefold saying: firm faith, sincere intent, correct action. Al-Kashshāf also adds turning from delusion and avoiding prohibitions; al-Biqāʿī links firm faith to the sūra's ideal of excellence. Al-Rāzī orders the three conditions as intention, valid worship and faith. Al-Jishumī argues against the Murjiʾa that faith plus good works is needed. Study Quran adds that sincere earnest effort need not reach completion or success. [17 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(بعض المتقدمين); JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(بعض السلف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعض المتقدمين); BIQAI-FULL(بعض السلف); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المتقدمين); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Zemahşerî); STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 17:19/q10 Are deeds with mixed worldly and afterlife motives accepted?  [أَرَادَ ٱلْءَاخِرَةَ · theology]
turns on: Whether a dominant afterlife motive preserves sincerity, and whether this verse addresses individual deeds at all.
- 17:19/q10/p1 Any worldly association spoils the deed, even when afterlife intent predominates. — Al-Rāzī reports a divine saying rejecting works with association and an analysis that mixed motives defeat purity; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the same debate from Rūḥ al-maʿānī. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI(روح المعاني)]
- 17:19/q10/p2 A deed can be accepted when afterlife intent predominates. — Al-Rāzī reports this alternative; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the disagreement over the dominant afterlife motive. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI(روح المعاني)]
- 17:19/q10/p3 A deed is unaccepted if worldly motive predominates or ties with afterlife motive. — Al-Rāzī calls this agreed, though he deems such a deed better than seeking worldly gain alone; the discussion reported by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also calls it agreed. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI(روح المعاني)]
- 17:19/q10/p4 The verse classifies people's ultimate goals, not the acceptance of every mixed-motive deed. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the reported application to individual acts and says the world is either the end or a stage to the Hereafter. [1 notes: TABATABAI+]

## 17:19/q11 Can a voluntary deed have no motive, and what follows?  [أَرَادَ · theology]
turns on: Whether a motive is necessary for any voluntary act.
- 17:19/q11/p1 A voluntary deed without a motive is impossible. — Al-Rāzī and the discussion quoted by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī attribute this to those who hold that every voluntary act requires a motive. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI(روح المعاني)]
- 17:19/q11/p2 Such an act is possible but inwardly ineffective and outwardly forbidden as pointless. — Al-Rāzī and the discussion quoted by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī attribute this to those who do not require a motive. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI(روح المعاني)]

## 17:19/q12 What is the grammatical force of وهو مؤمن?  [وَهُوَ مُؤْمِنٌۭ · grammar]
- 17:19/q12/p1 It is a circumstantial nominal clause describing the seeker during the effort, with faith shown as stable and accompanying desire and action. — Al-Samīn identifies the subject of سعى as the clause's referent. Al-Ālūsī extends the circumstance to desire and effort. Ibn ʿĀshūr says nominal form suggests enduring faith and compares 90:17. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 17:19/q13 Who does أولئك denote, and why is it plural?  [فَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ · grammar]
- 17:19/q13/p1 It denotes the previously described seekers; the plural follows the collective meaning of singular من. — Abū Ḥayyān explains the shift from singular relative to plural demonstrative. Al-Ālūsī adds collective receipt of reward and elevated status signaled by distance. Ibn ʿĀshūr says pointing them out marks their merit for reward. Asad renders the earlier pronouns in the plural to match normal Arabic usage and the final clause. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 17:19/q14 What does their final سعي refer back to?  [سَعْيُهُم · referent]
turns on: Whether the noun resumes all preceding qualifications or only the explicit striving.
- 17:19/q14/p1 All the qualifying deeds and conditions just described. — Al-Ālūsī reports this scope as one reading. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 17:19/q14/p2 The effort specifically named earlier, whose central place the wording highlights. — Al-Ālūsī reports this narrower scope and its emphasis. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 17:19/q15 What does God thanking the effort mean?  [مَّشْكُورًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether thanks is expressed as general acceptance and praise, specific multiplied reward and pardon, or attaining the inward goal.
- 17:19/q15/p1 God accepts, praises and rewards the effort and its doer, including Paradise. — Muqātil and Yaḥyā name Paradise; al-Suddī names repayment. Al-Rāzī analyzes gratitude as recognizing, praising and honoring the benefactor and says God treats the obedient in all three ways. Abū Ḥayyān says God praises and rewards, though God alone ultimately deserves gratitude; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says both reward and added praise are grace. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls 'thanked effort' a rational metaphor approving its worker. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Kashshāf and others equate gratitude with reward for obedience; Study Quran compares 2:200–201 and 3:145. [28 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); THALABI; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 17:19/q15/p2 God multiplies good deeds and forgives or overlooks sins, sometimes raising ranks. — Al-Ṭabarī and Qatāda pair reward with forgiveness; al-Wāḥidī adds raised ranks. Al-Ṭabrisī's Qatāda has multiplied good and overlooked sins; al-Durr's Qatāda says little good is thanked while many shortcomings are overlooked. Al-Qurṭubī reports ten-, seventy-, seven-hundred- or greater-fold multiplication, and Abū Hurayra reports the Prophet promising two millionfold when asked about a millionfold reward. Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites the report of a man forgiven after giving water to a thirsty dog. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي); TABRISI(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 17:19/q15/p3 God grants the sought object through the seeker's realized spiritual readiness. — Al-Qāshānī says sound original nature, faith and works bring the sought perfection from potentiality into actuality; al-Bursawī describes grace turning the heart upward until it finds God. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 17:19/q16 Why is kāna used with مشكورا?  [كَانَ سَعْيُهُم مَّشْكُورًۭا · rhetoric]
- 17:19/q16/p1 It shows that their effort was already worthy of thanks in worldly life, before later reward. — Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes its present worthiness from the ensuing recompense. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 17:19/q17 For whom was the verse revealed?  [* · reports]
- 17:19/q17/p1 Bilāl the muezzin and others. — Muqātil reports this occasion without specifying the others. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 17:19/q18 What does the contrast with the worldly seeker teach?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes draw different, compatible implications from the contrast and its wider context.
- 17:19/q18/p1 Seeking the Hereafter succeeds and can include worldly good; seeking only this world may achieve neither. — Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī's report from al-Ḥasan make this contrast; al-Iṣlāḥī says afterlife seekers receive their ordained worldly share. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts reliable afterlife-directed outcomes with worldly provision restricted by divine will. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the pairing implies that 17:18 means wanting only the immediate world. [5 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI(الحسن); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 17:19/q18/p2 Worldly provision does not measure honor; deeds determine standing before God. — Al-Biqāʿī notes that wealth was given to David and Solomon for favored work and withheld from others as honor. Al-Kashshāf says the afterlife-minded believer may benefit from worldly poverty. Kuran Yolu says worldly advantages are not true superiority. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 17:19/q18/p3 The passage contrasts ultimate human choices and their consequences in the sūra's wider moral sequence. — Ibn ʿĀshūr relates it to acquired deeds and personal accounting in 17:13 and 17:15. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the passage follows destruction after prophetic missions with afterlife punishment, reward and a general principle. Al-Iṣlāḥī describes Section III's invitation to Jews and Quraysh, contrasted fates, blindness to signs, demands for punishment and the punishment of nations. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 17:19/q19 How should the possible aims of human actions be classified?  [* · theology]
- 17:19/q19/p1 A person or act may aim at this world, the Hereafter, both, or neither; mixed aims differ by predominance or equality. — Al-Rāzī says the verse directly treats the first two; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī quotes Rūḥ al-maʿānī's fourfold taxonomy, then objects that the verses classify ultimate goals rather than each deed's acceptance. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI(روح المعاني)]

## 17:19/q20 Who creates or performs the human willing and faith mentioned here?  [أَرَادَ مُؤْمِنٌۭ · theology]
turns on: Whether gratitude for faith locates its act in God, the servant, or God's enabling and the servant's performance.
- 17:19/q20/p1 Faith must be created by God for thanks to Him to be proper. — Jaʿfar ibn Ḥarb argues that otherwise thanks to God would wrongly praise Him for another's act, citing 3:188. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(جعفر بن حرب)]
- 17:19/q20/p2 Humans perform faith while God supplies capacity, intellect, revelation and proofs. — Thumāma uses these gifts to explain why God is thanked while God thanks humans for their act. Al-Jishumī takes the human willing and disobedience in the passage as evidence for his doctrine of created acts. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(ثمامة بن الأشرس); JISHUMI]
- 17:19/q20/p3 God provides complete power and motive, and the servant performs faith, so thanks can belong to both. — Al-Rāzī reconciles the rival explanations. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+]

## 17:19/q21 How are vowel-state names explained in the grammatical discussion?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Whether the first four names apply only to built forms or also to inflection.
- 17:19/q21/p1 Their names come from articulation and meaning: closing or raising lips, opening mouth, lowering jaw, and cutting for jazm. — Al-Rāzī adds that waqf and sukūn have evident senses. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 17:19/q21/p2 Fatḥa, ḍamma, kasra and waqf name built states only; the other four name inflectional states. — Al-Rāzī reports this restriction from some grammarians. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 17:19/q21/p3 The first four terms name both built and inflected states; the second four are narrower inflectional terms. — Al-Rāzī reports this broader use from others, making the first set a genus to the second. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(آخرون)]

## 17:19/q22 Can the states of built words be called al-majārī?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Whether flowing describes a vocal sound that can vary at pause or specifically changing inflectional endings.
- 17:19/q22/p1 Yes: their sounds flow after consonants, and built forms vary between connected speech and pause. — Al-Rāzī explains the movement as voiced breath extending after a consonant. Responding to al-Māzinī, he says built forms are not wholly fixed. Sībawayh counts eight al-majārī. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(سيبويه); RAZI-FULL+]
- 17:19/q22/p2 No: only changing inflectional states truly 'flow.' — Al-Māzinī objects to Sībawayh's name for the states of built words. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(المازني); RAZI-FULL-]
