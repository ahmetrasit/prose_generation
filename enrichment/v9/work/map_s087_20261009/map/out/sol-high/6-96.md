# 6:96: 24 questions

## 6:96/q01 What does 'Splitter of dawn' mean?  [فَالِقُ ٱلْإِصْبَاحِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether splitting describes dawn's emergence, its creation, or the reported day-and-night lights.
- 6:96/q01/p1 God cleaves and reveals the column of dawn from surrounding darkness. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī and al-Ṭabrisī describe a column emerging from night. Al-Kashshāf allows cleaving the late darkness or dawn itself; al-Wāḥidī objects that night strictly parts for dawn. Al-Biqāʿī treats splitting as disclosure. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a metaphor and links 36:37. [40 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن مسلم); NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أكثر المفسرين); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكرماني); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; WAHIDI-BASIT(كثير من أهل التفسير)-]
- 6:96/q01/p2 God creates or brings forth dawn or daylight. — Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Zajjāj, Abū Isḥāq and al-Layth use creation or origination. Al-Wāḥidī favors a newly created dawn each day and connects falaq with 113:1 and 6:95; some reports extend this to night and day. Al-Zajjāj says splitting ultimately returns to creation. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,الضحاك); THALABI(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس والمحققون,الليث); JISHUMI(الضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 6:96/q01/p3 The phrase denotes sunlight by day and moonlight by night. — Transmitted from Ibn ʿAbbās by al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Māwardī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Durr and al-Bukhārī; al-Suyūṭī also holds it. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن أبي طلحة عن ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR(ابن أبي طلحة عن ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; BUKHARI(ابن عباس)]

## 6:96/q02 What does al-iṣbāḥ mean?  [ٱلْإِصْبَاحِ · meaning]
- 6:96/q02/p1 Al-iṣbāḥ is dawn or morning, originally a verbal noun for entering morning or illumination. — Al-Farrāʾ says it covers the morning; al-Akhfash, al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī and others derive it from aṣbaḥa. Ibn al-Sikkīt, al-Layth, cited poetry, and the parallel with evening support usage. Mujāhid and Qatāda stress its first light. Al-Samīn prefers the verbal noun later used as a dawn name. [41 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(الضحاك,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة,مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن السكيت,الليث والفراء والزجاج); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأخفش,الزجاج,مجاهد، ابن زيد); RAZI-FULL(الشاعر,الليث); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الليث والفراء والزجاج,مجاهد); SAMIN-DURR(الليث والزجاج والفراء,مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 6:96/q03 How is al-iṣbāḥ vocalized?  [ٱلْإِصْبَاحِ · readings]
turns on: The kasra verbal noun versus the fatḥa plural of mornings.
- 6:96/q03/p1 Al-aṣbāḥ is read with an open hamza, as a plural of ṣubḥ, successive mornings. — Reported from al-Ḥasan, ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar, Abū Rajāʾ and others. Al-Ṭabarī rejects it against reciters' consensus; poetry uses al-amsāʾ and al-aṣbāḥ as plurals. Al-Kashshāf allows both vowel forms as verbal nouns too. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن البصري); NAHHAS(الحسن وعيسى بن عمر); THALABI(الحسن والقيسي); JISHUMI(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن بن أبي الحسن وعيسى بن عمر وأبو رجاء); TABRISI(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيد,أنس بن ماله، الحسن، أبو مجلز، أيوب، الجحدري); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن وعيسى بن عمر); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن وعيسى وأبو رجاء); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن وأبو رجاء وعيسى بن عمر); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن,النخعي)]
- 6:96/q03/p2 Al-iṣbāḥ is read with a broken hamza, the established verbal noun. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers this on authoritative consensus; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Samīn call it the majority form. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور); TAB-FULL+]

## 6:96/q04 How is fāliq read and construed?  [فَالِقُ · readings]
turns on: Participle, past verb, accusative praise, or loss of tanwīn.
- 6:96/q04/p1 Falaqa is read as a past verb. — Attributed to al-Nakhaʿī, Abū Ḥaywa, Yaḥyā ibn Waththāb and others; some pair it with jaʿala. Al-Samīn treats transmitted recitation as decisive. [8 notes: NAHHAS(الأعمش عن إبراهيم النخعي); THALABI(النخعي); JISHUMI(النخعي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حيوة وإبراهيم النخعي ويحيى بن وثاب); QURTUBI-FULL(الأعمش عن إبراهيم النخعي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النخعي وابن وثاب وأبو حيوة); SAMIN-DURR(عبد الله,يحيى والنخعي وأبو حيوة)]
- 6:96/q04/p2 Fāliq is accusative as praise or an implied 'I mean the splitter'. — Al-Jishumī, al-Kashshāf, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī report this analysis, sometimes extending praise to jāʿil. [5 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن,قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قراءة غير مسماة)]
- 6:96/q04/p3 Fāliq loses tanwīn and governs accusative al-iṣbāḥ. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn report deletion at adjoining sukūns. Sībawayh permits it in poetry and al-Mubarrad in prose; cited verse and 22:35 and 37:38 are offered. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it anomalous. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فرقة); SAMIN-DURR(سيبويه والمبرد)]

## 6:96/q05 What causes dawn's light?  [فَالِقُ ٱلْإِصْبَاحِ · theology]
turns on: Direct creation, atmospheric sunlight, or a proposed link to Mount Qāf.
- 6:96/q05/p1 Dawn light, especially false dawn, is directly created by God. — Al-Rāzī says a narrow streak that vanishes cannot be sunlight, and the below-horizon sun cannot directly light the surface; transparent air cannot transmit light, while vapor predicts the wrong brightness. Al-Biqāʿī calls false dawn a stronger sign. Al-Ālūsī repeats objections and ranks creation plausible. [12 notes: RAZI-FULL(القائلون); ALUSI-FULL(أحمد بن حجر الهيثمي,الإمام الرازي)]
- 6:96/q05/p2 Dawn is sunlight visible through atmospheric vapor and Earth's shadow geometry. — Ibn al-Haytham proposes illumination through air. Astronomers cited by al-Ālūsī describe a shadow cone, vapor sphere, first vertical light and later horizontal spread; Aristarchus supplies geometry. Al-Ālūsī later says many favor this and answers al-Rāzī's meridian objection. Ibn Bashīr invokes eye adaptation. [19 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو علي بن الهيثم); ALUSI-FULL(أرسطوخس,أهل الهيئة,ابن بشير المالكي,التذكرة وشرح سيد المحققين); RAZI-FULL(القائلون)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 6:96/q05/p3 The false dawn is linked to Mount Qāf, through a ray or an opening. — Al-Ālūsī transmits the ray and reports from Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Burayda and Mujāhid about Qāf. He ranks the ray below direct creation but above a sun emerging through an opening; sun-size estimates disfavor the opening, and later travel casts doubt on the mountain report. [8 notes: ALUSI-FULL(آخرون,ابن عباس,عبد الله بن بريدة,غياث الدين جمشيد الكاشي,مجاهد)]

## 6:96/q06 How do the false and true dawn appear?  [ٱلْإِصْبَاحِ · sciences]
turns on: Whether the first streak vanishes or only seems to fade.
- 6:96/q06/p1 False dawn is a vertical streak; true dawn spreads horizontally and grows. — Al-Rāzī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī and a report from Aḥmad distinguish the two. Muslim's report says not to rely on the streak until it spreads. Al-Ālūsī describes seasonal, weather and perceptual variation; he suggests the first signal wakes sleepers. [19 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو جعفر البصري,أحمد,الأصبحي,القرافي والأصبحي,بعض أهل الهيئة,بعض المؤقتين,راصد غير مسمى,مسلم)]

## 6:96/q07 Why is night called a repose?  [ٱلَّيْلَ سَكَنًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Literal rest and dwelling, with inward applications.
- 6:96/q07/p1 Night is a time or place of quiet, dwelling, sleep and recovery from daily labor. — Qatāda says birds and beasts rest; Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Kalbī speak of all creatures and returning home. Al-Wāḥidī, Ibn Kathīr, Ibn ʿĀshūr and others cite 10:67 and related verses. Sakan can name a spouse, house, beloved or comforting fire. Suhayb's thoughts of Paradise and Hell kept him awake. [43 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس ومجاهد وأكثر المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد,قتادة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:96/q07/p2 Night figures inward rest from creation or a station where heart and faculties settle. — Al-Nasafī describes intimacy with the Real; al-Qāshānī reads soul-darkness in which the heart recovers or bodily faculties cease agitation. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 6:96/q08 How is the making of night read?  [وَجَعَلَ ٱلَّيْلَ · readings]
turns on: Past jaʿala with accusative night versus participial jāʿil with genitive night.
- 6:96/q08/p1 Jāʿil al-layli is an active participle with genitive night. — Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, Ibn ʿĀmir and the non-Kūfan majority read it. Abū ʿAlī and Ibn Khālawayh favor its parallel with fāliq; some construe it as past, others as continuing. Al-Ṭabarī accepts it. [22 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر); THALABI(الباقون); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأكثرون); JISHUMI(باقي القراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر); TABRISI(الباقون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير، نافع، أبو عمرو، ابن عامر); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أهل المدينة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(باقي السبعة); SAMIN-DURR; NASHR(الباقون); ALUSI-FULL(سائر السبعة إلا الكوفيين); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); QIRAAT-ER; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر)+; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA+]
- 6:96/q08/p2 Jaʿala al-layla is a past verb with accusative night. — Kūfan readers including ʿĀṣim, Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī are named. Preceding fāliq may convey past action; later verbs in 6:97–99 support it. Al-Ṭabarī accepts both widespread readings. [22 notes: TAB-FULL(قراء الكوفيين); NAHHAS(الحسن وعيسى بن عمر وحمزة والكسائي); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(عاصم وحمزة والكسائي); THALABI(أهل الكوفة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل الكوفة); JISHUMI(عاصم، وحمزة، والكسائي); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل الكوفة وإبراهيم النخعي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أهل الكوفة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عاصم، حمزة، الكسائي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن وعيسى بن عمر وحمزة والكسائي,مكي); BAYDAWI-FULL(قراءة الكوفيين); NASAFI-FULL(كوفي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكوفيون); SAMIN-DURR(الكوفيون والباقون); NASHR(الكوفيون); IBNASHUR-FULL(عاصم ، وحمزة ، والكسائي ، وخلف)]

## 6:96/q09 How is sakanan read and governed?  [سَكَنًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Second object, circumstantial accusative, direct participial government, or implied verb.
- 6:96/q09/p1 Sakanan is a second object governed by jaʿala, jāʿil or an implied verb. — Al-Jishumī gives making two objects. Basrans often supply a verb for a past participle; al-Kisāʾī, Hishām and al-Sīrāfī allow direct government under stated conditions. Al-Samīn distinguishes past from present or future. Other grammarians make it a circumstantial accusative when jaʿala means create. [24 notes: ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكسائي وهشام); SAMIN-DURR(أبو سعيد السيرافي,البصريون والكسائي,بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(الكسائي وبعض الكوفيين,المحققون,بعض النحاة,كثير)]
- 6:96/q09/p2 The word is read sākinan, 'still' or 'resting'. — Reported from Yaʿqūb through Ruways, but Abū ʿAmr al-Dānī calls the attribution unsound. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); QURTUBI-FULL(يعقوب في رواية رويس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(يعقوب)]

## 6:96/q10 How are sun and moon read and attached syntactically?  [وَٱلشَّمْسَ وَٱلْقَمَرَ · grammar]
turns on: Accusative ellipsis or position coordination, genitive form coordination, or nominative clause.
- 6:96/q10/p1 Accusative sun and moon depend on an understood jaʿala. — Sībawayh, Abū ʿUbayda, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Samīn supply it from jāʿil or sakanan. Basran limits on past participial government motivate ellipsis; poems cited by Sībawayh, al-Farrāʾ and Ibn al-Anbārī show the construction. [17 notes: SIBAWAYH; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق وأبو علي); JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(مكي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ,سيبويه); ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:96/q10/p2 Accusative sun and moon coordinate with the object position of al-layl. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās, al-Zamakhsharī and Ibn ʿĀshūr treat genitive night as accusative in meaning; intervening sakanan helps. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn reject al-Zamakhsharī's continuing-participle rationale under Sībawayh's rule about an unbounded participle. [25 notes: FARRA(بعضهم); TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري,الفراء); KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور,الزمخشري,الشيخ,غير سيبويه); BURHAN(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 6:96/q10/p3 Sun and moon are genitive, coordinated by form with al-layl. — Reported from Yazīd ibn Qutayb or Quʿnib, Abū Ḥaywa and others. Al-Farrāʾ prefers genitive without an intervening word; al-Naḥḥās considers separation a weakness. [9 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(يزيد بن قطيب السكوني); THALABI(يزيد بن قعنب); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); JISHUMI(عامة القراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حيوة); QURTUBI-FULL(يزيد بن قطيب السكوني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حيوة); SAMIN-DURR(أبو حيوة)]
- 6:96/q10/p4 Sun and moon are nominative, beginning a clause with an omitted predicate. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī report it; the implicit predicate is 'made' or 'subjected to reckoning'. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قراءة غير مسماة)]

## 6:96/q11 What does ḥusbānan mean here?  [حُسْبَانًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Reckoning, light, or the disputed fire sense.
- 6:96/q11/p1 Ḥusbānan means calculated, measured motion or reckoning. — Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Suddī, Qatāda, Mujāhid and most commentators relate it to courses and orbits; 55:5, 21:33 and 10:5 are cited. Al-Ṭabarī prefers it over light since dawn already supplied illumination. [31 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(السدي,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس، السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الكلبي,قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(ابن عباس والسدي وقتادة ومجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجمهور، ابن قتيبة,السدي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; TAB-FULL+]
- 6:96/q11/p2 Ḥusbānan means light or radiance. — Qatāda reports this; al-Māwardī analogizes the fire term in 18:40. Al-Ṭabarī rejects repetition of dawn's light. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); TAB-FULL-]
- 6:96/q11/p3 Ḥusbān means fire or a fire projectile, as elsewhere. — Ibn ʿAbbās's 18:40 gloss is cited. Al-Ṭabarī and al-Samīn reject applying it here. ## 6:96/q13 What is the sun and moon's reckoning for?  [ٱلشَّمْسَ وَٱلْقَمَرَ حُسْبَانًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Human timekeeping and benefit or an appointed cosmic end.
- 6:96/q13/p1 The sun and moon mark days, months, years, seasons and human schedules. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Muqātil, al-Ṭabrisī and others describe cycles and stations, sometimes 365¼ days for the sun and 28 for the moon. Measures support worship, transactions, debt deadlines, harvests, direction of prayer and livelihood. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes solar and lunar calendars. [26 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الزمخشري,تاج القراء); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:96/q13/p2 Their reckoned courses run only until an appointed end. — Ibn ʿAbbās says they run to a term; al-Rabīʿ says when their days expire the age ends and the great terror begins. Al-Kalbī stresses fixed stages and outer stations. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الربيع); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(العوفي عن ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس)]

## 6:96/q14 What does 'that' point to?  [ذَٰلِكَ · referent]
turns on: How far back the demonstrative reaches.
- 6:96/q14/p1 Dhālika encompasses dawn's splitting and the other acts, possibly back through 6:95. — Al-Ṭabrisī includes dawn, night and luminaries; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn extend the possible scope through split grain and kernels. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الطبرسي)]
- 6:96/q14/p2 Dhālika points chiefly to the preceding ordering of night, sun and moon. — Al-Nasafī singles out measured motion; the majority cited by al-Ālūsī points to the local arrangement and takes the distant demonstrative as elevating it. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور)+]

## 6:96/q15 What do 'the Mighty, the Knowing' explain?  [ٱلْعَزِيزِ ٱلْعَلِيمِ · theology]
- 6:96/q15/p1 The Mighty subdues cosmic order and the Knowing measures it for creatures' benefit. — Al-Rāzī sees exhaustive power and knowledge in specified motions; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr describe irresistible command and knowledge of every interest. Ibn ʿAbbās includes each era's provisions. [20 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 6:96/q16 What does this cosmic order prove?  [* · theology]
- 6:96/q16/p1 The phenomena demonstrate one purposive Creator governing by power and knowledge. — Al-Jishumī says alternation prevents harm and gives universal proof and blessing. Al-Rāzī argues the sun's special luminosity requires a willing agent; al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı oppose natural necessity. Islāhī draws out unity, judgment and obedience. [16 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 6:96/q17 How does this verse fit its surrounding passage?  [* · links]
- 6:96/q17/p1 The verse continues the sequence of earthly and celestial signs. — Al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī and Islāhī move from split seed in 6:95 to dawn and sky; al-Biqāʿī invokes Abraham and the sūra opening. Ibn Kathīr links 6:1 and night-day verses; Study Quran notes 6:95–97. [23 notes: MUQATIL; BAQILLANI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN]

## 6:96/q18 What supplication uses this verse's language?  [* · reports]
- 6:96/q18/p1 A prophetic or early-Muslim supplication invokes the verse's epithets. — Al-Qurṭubī transmits prayers asking the Splitter of dawn and Maker of night to settle debt, remove poverty and preserve hearing and sight; al-Durr gives similar wording. Al-Qurṭubī says 'inherit hearing and sight' means retaining them until death, rejecting a reference to Abū Bakr and ʿUmar. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(صهيب); QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(صهيب الرومي); DURR-FULL(مسلم بن يسار)]

## 6:96/q19 When may Qur'anic wording be quoted outside recitation?  [* · law]
turns on: Permission and scope of iqtibās.
- 6:96/q19/p1 Qur'anic wording outside recitation has qualified acceptance or restriction. — Al-ʿIzz permits iqtibās with prophetic prayers as precedent; Mālikīs are reported to prohibit it, while al-Qāḍī Abū Bakr allows prose and dislikes poetry. Ibn Ḥijja and Ibn al-Muqriʾ distinguish sermons and praise from appropriation of God's first-person speech or humor. [8 notes: BURHAN(الشيخ عز الدين); ITQAN(ابن حجة,الشرف إسماعيل بن المقرئ,القاضي عياض,عز الدين ابن عبد السلام)]

## 6:96/q20 What inward reading is given to these images?  [* · inward]
- 6:96/q20/p1 The verse also maps inward light, darkness, spirit, heart and gnosis. — Al-Sulamī reports hearts split by unseen lights and secrets illuminated by gnosis. Al-Rāzī allegorizes creation, life, reason and guidance emerging from darkness; al-Qāshānī reads sun and moon as spirit and heart and alternation as disclosure and veiling. [8 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL; QASHANI]

## 6:96/q21 What further cosmological reports are attached to this verse?  [* · reports]
- 6:96/q21/p1 Further accounts describe celestial bodies and afterlife. — Al-Durr reports a weak-chain account of a sea below the sky and eclipses. Aftādeh, quoted by Bursawī, describes moonlight from a realm of lights, reflected stars, shooting stars and otherworldly day-night signs. [5 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(حضرة الشيخ الشهير بأفتاده أفندي)]

## 6:96/q22 Why are these images phrased and ordered this way?  [* · rhetoric]
- 6:96/q22/p1 Word choice, order and paired omissions carry the verse's rhetorical force. — Al-Biqāʿī describes iḥtibāk: repose implies omitted motion and splitting omitted spreading. Bursawī puts the sun first for brightness and primacy. Al-Bāqillānī praises the four phrases' composition; al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts nominal divine attributes with verbal living movement. [14 notes: BAQILLANI(أبو عبيدة); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 6:96/q23 How are the verse's clauses grammatically related?  [* · grammar]
- 6:96/q23/p1 The clauses admit several predicate and coordination analyses. — Al-Naḥḥās calls fāliq definite and adjectival and the close a subject-predicate sentence. Al-Qurṭubī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ālūsī parse fāliq as predicate or attribute. Ibn ʿĀshūr coordinates the night clause with dawn; al-Sāmarrāʾī distinguishes wāw from causal fa. [14 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 6:96/q24 What response does the verse ask of people?  [* · interpretation]
- 6:96/q24/p1 The ordered cosmos grounds human accountability and faithful conduct. — Elmalılı says people answer for activity and rest and should not worship created forces. Islāhī sees purposeful design requiring judgment, prophetic guidance and obedience; Bursawī quotes Saʿdī on creation serving people while obeying God. [7 notes: BURSEVI(السعدي); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(الحسن بن علي بن بنت إلياس,علي بن عقبة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
