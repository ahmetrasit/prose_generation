# 20:126: 17 questions

## 20:126/q01 What does the first كَذَٰلِكَ point to, and how is it construed?  [كَذَٰلِكَ · grammar]
turns on: The antecedent of dhālika, the supplied expression, and whether the first kāf is extra or retains its ordinary comparative force.
- 20:126/q01/p1 It points to the addressee's blind resurrection or his former conduct, which explains the present treatment. — Ibn ʿAtiyya relates it to the blindness just mentioned. Al-Zamakhshari, as reported by al-Samin and al-Alusi, supplies 'you acted thus' and parses the kāf as a direct object; al-Alusi says it points to resurrection blind. Al-Nasafi likewise supplies the person's action. Ibn ʿAshur says the fate fits his refusal to consider the signs; al-Tabatabaʾi makes the reply an answer to his question about blindness, with recompense in kind (42:40). Al-Biqaʿi includes this as one alternative. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:126/q01/p2 It affirms that matters are just as the person now sees or says, before the reply explains why. — Al-Wahidi says God answers 'this is how things are, as you see.' Al-Baydawi says God treated him in just that way and then explains it. Al-Biqaʿi offers 'matters were as he said' as an alternative, on which the reply supplies the cause. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:126/q01/p3 It describes the way God's clear signs came to the person. — Al-Tabari glosses it 'thus they came to you.' Abu al-Baqa, reported by al-Samin, allows an arrival like that. Al-Alusi treats the kāf as extra, comparing mithluka lā yabkhal, and takes dhālika to indicate their striking manner of arrival; he contrasts al-Zamakhshari's nonextra kāf. Al-Samin says such estimates make the kāf accusative as verbal noun or direct object. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:126/q01/p4 An omitted noun makes it mean a gathering of this kind. — Abu al-Baqa, as reported by al-Samin, supplies an omitted noun for the manner of gathering; al-Samin allows an accusative kāf under these estimates. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]
- 20:126/q01/p5 It can indicate retribution matching the person's turning away or forgetting. — Abu al-Baqa, reported by al-Samin, includes this among possible estimates; al-Samin allows the accusative kāf as verbal noun or direct object. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]

## 20:126/q02 What are the ءَايَٰتُنَا that came to the person?  [ءَايَٰتُنَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the signs are specified as Qur'anic revelation, proofs of divine attributes, or the messenger's whole presentation of guidance.
- 20:126/q02/p1 They are God's revealed signs, especially the Qur'an and its clarifying proofs. — Muqatil identifies Qur'anic signs. Al-Tabari calls them proofs, evidences, and clarification in God's Book. Al-Bursevi includes revealed verses and says the signs were clear to everyone. Al-Zamakhshari, reported by al-Samin, calls them clear and illuminating; al-Nasafi, al-Kashshaf, and al-Alusi also stress their clarity. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; BURSEVI; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); NASAFI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:126/q02/p2 They are proofs of God's oneness and power. — Al-Qurtubi specifies proofs of oneness and power; al-Bursevi includes proofs of divine power and signs of oneness among the clear signs. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 20:126/q02/p3 They include the message and proofs brought by God's messengers, specifically Muhammad and the Qur'an. — Al-Tabrisi names Muhammad, the Qur'an, and the proofs. The Study Quran calls them proofs brought by God's messengers. [2 notes: TABRISI; STUDYQURAN]

## 20:126/q03 What does فَنَسِيتَهَا mean here?  [فَنَسِيتَهَا · meaning]
- 20:126/q03/p1 He deliberately neglected, rejected, or failed to act on the signs, treating them as though forgotten. — Muqatil, Yahya, al-Wahidi, and Ibn al-Jawzi stress abandoning belief; al-Tabari adds rejection and failure to act, while al-Suddi specifies abandoning the duty to act. Mujahid glosses it as leaving the signs behind. Al-Kashshaf, al-Nasafi, al-Baydawi, al-Qurtubi, and Abu Hayyan stress failure to reflect or consider, turning away, and blindness to clear signs. Ibn Kathir says they were conveyed but deliberately ignored. Al-Biqaʿi invokes veiled eyes (18:101); al-Alusi says the effect, forgetting, is named for its cause, turning away. Al-Tabrisi and Ibn ʿAtiyya distinguish culpable neglect from an involuntary mental lapse. Al-Islahi says ignored revelation leaves the eyes shut. [31 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ABDURRAZZAQ(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، الكلبي، أهل التفسير); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 20:126/q04 What does the second كَذَٰلِكَ say about the person's treatment?  [وَكَذَٰلِكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the second kāf expresses a matching comparison or causal 'because of'.
- 20:126/q04/p1 His treatment corresponds to his conduct: as he neglected the signs, God leaves him to the fitting consequence. — Al-Tabari, al-Zajjaj, al-Thaʿlabi, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, al-Biqaʿi, al-Bursevi, al-Islahi, and al-Tabatabaʾi express requital in kind. Al-Alusi says the second dhālika points to the neglect implied by nasiya and the kāf retains its ordinary comparative sense; Ibn Kathir compares 7:51 and explicitly calls it recompense matching the deed; al-Tabatabaʾi cites 42:40. Al-Tabari treats Qatada's withholding of good as close to the Fire gloss, since the Fire is the greatest evil. [19 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، الكلبي، أهل التفسير); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:126/q04/p2 The kāf can mean 'because of': he is left because he neglected the signs. — Al-Alusi reports this from some interpreters and compares a proposed understanding of 'remember Him as He guided you' in 2:198. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 20:126/q05 What does تُنسَىٰ mean for the person on that Day?  [ٱلْيَوْمَ تُنسَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes variously specify the resulting Fire, blindness, loss of good, or loss of mercy; al-Tabari explicitly treats the good/evil and Fire accounts as close.
- 20:126/q05/p1 He is abandoned in the Fire or enduring punishment, without release or relief. — Muqatil adds that he is never brought out and is no longer remembered by God. Yahya, Ikrimah, Mujahid, Abu Salih, al-Tabari, al-Zajjaj, al-Thaʿlabi, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Qurtubi, al-Durr, and Abu Hayyan identify the Fire or punishment. Al-Tabrisi says the punishment does not end. Al-Tabari says this is the greatest evil and close to Qatada's formulation. Al-Islahi says the complaint cannot reverse the deserved fate. [24 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(عكرمة); ABDURRAZZAQ(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(أبو صالح,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، الكلبي، أهل التفسير); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:126/q05/p2 He is left blind or without sight and guidance, along with punishment. — Al-Kashshaf, al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan's report of al-Zamakhshari, al-Biqaʿi, al-Bursevi, al-Alusi, and al-Tabatabaʾi connect the abandonment to blindness. Al-Nasafi says God leaves him to his deeds and does not lift the veil from his eyes. Al-Bursevi says blindness, muteness, and deafness are eventually removed so he sees the terrors and his place in Hell (19:38); al-Alusi similarly links later sight of the Fire to 18:53 and calls it punishment upon punishment. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:126/q05/p3 He is deprived of good while evil or punishment is not withheld from him. — Qatada says he is forgotten as to good, not evil; al-Baghawi phrases it as forgetting what is good but not the punishment. Al-Tabari relates this to the Fire gloss, the Fire being the greatest evil. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 20:126/q05/p4 He is deprived of mercy and its benefits, as though unattended to. — Al-Qushayri says neglect of reflection leads to punishment without mercy, according to God's practice of repayment by deeds. Ibn ʿAshur calls forgetting in both places metaphor or metonymy for deprivation of mercy's benefits; al-Tabrisi says he is left as though forgotten, and al-Biqaʿi likens him to an unseen object no one attends to. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 20:126/q06 Why is the person raised blind in the surrounding dialogue?  [كَذَٰلِكَ تُنسَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether blindness is a direct matching requital or the persistence and intensification of the soul's acquired spiritual state.
- 20:126/q06/p1 Resurrection blindness recompenses his former refusal to see or heed God's guidance. — Al-Razi gives this as one explanation. Al-Kashshaf, Ibn ʿAtiyya, al-Biqaʿi, Ibn ʿAshur, al-Tabatabaʾi, Asad, and al-Islahi relate worldly disregard to the blindness and the reply. Ibn ʿAshur says the blindness marks God's anger and exclusion from mercy; al-Islahi says ignored revelation shut their eyes and made their complaint useless. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:126/q06/p2 The soul remains ignorant and cut off from spiritual beings after leaving the body, and its spiritual pain intensifies. — Al-Razi offers this as an alternative: worldly rejection of evidence produced that state, which persists after death. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 20:126/q07 How do worldly and resurrection blindness relate?  [كَذَٰلِكَ تُنسَىٰ · interpretation]
- 20:126/q07/p1 Worldly blindness to guidance is figurative or unrecognized; resurrection blindness is real and recognized when reality is unveiled. — Ibn ʿAshur explicitly distinguishes figurative first blindness from real blindness at resurrection. Al-Islahi reconciles the passage with 50:22: people did not recognize their worldly blindness but do when reality is unveiled. Al-Qushayri cites a report that one meets God in the state in which one lived, so blindness of heart and ignorance persist. Al-Tabrisi also reports blindness of heart followed by torment. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; TABRISI(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:126/q08 How does the reply work as dialogue and ellipsis?  [قَالَ كَذَٰلِكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: These notes address different features of the same dialogue: the implied question and the omitted reciprocal terms.
- 20:126/q08/p1 The reply answers the person's question about blindness and implicitly asks what he did to merit it. — Yahya says God explains that the signs came in worldly life. Al-Alusi supplies the implicit question 'What did I do?' before the charge that signs came and were forgotten. Ibn ʿAshur says the question starts a new sentence and God's reply is separated because it continues the dialogue. Al-Tabatabaʾi says 'thus' answers the blindness question; Asad recounts the sinner's complaint that he is blind despite earlier sight. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:126/q08/p2 The wording leaves reciprocal blindness and abandonment implicit. — Ibn ʿAshur identifies three ellipses: the gathering leaves him forgotten, the reply implies earlier blindness to signs, and it implies present blindness. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:126/q09 Was this verse reported to have been revealed about a named person?  [* · reports]
- 20:126/q09/p1 A report says it was revealed concerning al-Murshi. — Ibn ʿAtiyya reports the attribution without further detail in the note. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 20:126/q10 What reported warnings concern forgetting memorized Qur'an?  [فَنَسِيتَهَا · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in the warned consequence and include differing transmitters and assessments of the Anas and ʿUbada routes.
- 20:126/q10/p1 A Qur'an learner who later forgets it meets God maimed; one version joins this to a warning about rulers over ten. — Ibn Kathir reports the Prophetic warning that a ruler over ten comes shackled until freed by justice and a forgetful Qur'an learner meets God ajdham. Ahmad transmits the latter from Saʿd ibn ʿUbada and also through ʿUbada ibn al-Samit. Ibn Kathir notes Abu ʿAwana's ʿUbada transmission through Yazid and disagreement in its chain; he accepts it for exhortation if corroborated, as Abu ʿUbayd says. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,سعد بن عبادة,عبادة بن الصامت)]
- 20:126/q10/p2 Forgetting an entrusted Qur'anic verse or surah is described as the community's greatest sin. — Ibn Kathir gives Anas's report that the Prophet saw his community's rewards, even removing dirt from a mosque, and saw no greater sin; a report attributed to Salman similarly names an entrusted surah. The fuller Anas version passes through Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi, Abu Yaʿla, al-Bazzar, and others; al-Tirmidhi calls it gharīb, al-Bukhari finds it strange, al-Darimi questions al-Mutallib's hearing from Anas, and Ibn Kathir notes an alternate al-Zuhri route. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 20:126/q10/p3 The Prophet commanded regular review because Qur'an slips from memory faster than camels escape. — Ibn Kathir cites the Prophetic command and comparison. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 20:126/q11 Does this verse condemn literal forgetting of memorized Qur'an as a major sin?  [فَنَسِيتَهَا · law]
turns on: Whether nasiya means culpable rejection or lost memorization, and whether the latter arose from negligence and disregard.
- 20:126/q11/p1 The verse targets abandoning and disbelieving in the signs, not mere loss of memorized wording while one understands and practices it. — Ibn Kathir says such loss is outside this particular threat, though another warning applies. Al-Alusi says the verse cannot establish that mere forgetting a Qur'anic verse is a major sin because nasiya means abandoning and not believing. Ibn ʿAtiyya and al-Tabrisi distinguish neglect from involuntary lapse, which is not punishable conduct. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; TABRISI+]
- 20:126/q11/p2 Neglecting recitation, allowing Qur'an to be forgotten, and failing to care for it are grave parts of turning away from remembrance. — Ibn Kathir reports this inclusion by some commentators and endorses it as a part, though not the whole, of the verse's intent. Abu Shama, as reported by al-Alusi, construes traditions condemning forgetfulness as failure to act on Qur'an. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو شامة); IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 20:126/q11/p3 Forgetting Qur'an or even a verse counts as a major sin, with literal forgetting qualified by laziness and disregard. — Al-Alusi reports al-Rafiʿi's major-sin ruling and says al-Nawawi's wording in al-Rawda seems to favor it. Al-Balqini and al-Zarkashi, in al-Alusi's account, condition literal forgetting on laziness and disregard. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو شامة,الرافعي); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 20:126/q12 What practical warning does the verse give its hearers?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:126/q12/p1 Reflect on God's signs, take heed, and stop heedless conduct lest the same fate follow. — Al-Qushayri urges reflection, taking heed, and turning back from bad conduct. Ibn ʿAshur says the Qur'an cites the fate to warn its hearers against turning from remembrance. Bint al-Shati notes that ḍank appears only here in the Qur'an in this warning, whose subjects are raised blind. Al-Razi says a worldly reading of constricted life pairs hardship in life with afterlife blindness for turning from remembrance. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; RAZI-FULL]

## 20:126/q13 How do decree and human effort relate to the warning?  [* · theology]
- 20:126/q13/p1 Happiness and misery are decreed and foreknown, yet striving and effort are also within the decree. — Al-Qushayri says divine knowledge encompasses what will occur and still urges effort. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 20:126/q14 What does the warning imply about guidance and accountability before revealed laws?  [ءَايَٰتُنَا · theology]
- 20:126/q14/p1 Humans were warned against error and polytheism from the beginning, with warning embedded in their nature; reason obliges knowledge of God and supports accountability. — Ibn ʿAshur states the original warning. He reports that many Muslim scholars punish polytheists among peoples between revealed laws, and that Ahl al-Sunna and all Muʿtazilites deem knowledge of God obligatory by reason. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(أهل السنة والمعتزلة,كثير من علماء الإسلام)]

## 20:126/q15 How does this warning connect to Adam's forgetfulness?  [فَنَسِيتَهَا · links]
- 20:126/q15/p1 Adam's garden story prefigures descendants who forget divine guidance and sin, though Adam's prohibition preceded religious law and was advisory. — Al-Tabatabaʾi relates the tree prohibition to later guidance and cites 20:115, 20:117, and 20:124; he distinguishes Adam's case from his descendants'. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:126/q16 What other passages illuminate being forgotten and later seeing punishment?  [تُنسَىٰ · links]
turns on: The cited parallels illuminate different aspects of the fate: reciprocal abandonment and eventual sight of punishment.
- 20:126/q16/p1 Other verses describe God forgetting those who forgot their meeting with the Day. — Ibn Kathir cites 7:51 for reciprocal forgetting. The Study Quran refers to 7:51 and 45:34 as parallels for God forgetting disbelievers on the Last Day. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:126/q16/p2 The condemned may later see the Fire, adding a further punishment. — Al-Alusi says blindness lasts until God shows the person Judgment's terrors and the Fire, and cites 18:53, where criminals see the Fire and realize they will fall into it. Al-Bursevi similarly cites 19:38 for later sight after blindness, muteness, and deafness are removed. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 20:126/q17 What general rhetorical observations are recorded alongside this verse?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: These are distinct rhetorical observations, not competing readings of the verse.
- 20:126/q17/p1 Readerly misapprehension ranges from firm false belief to a fleeting impression, depending on how strong the resemblance is. — Al-Jurjani distinguishes degrees of resemblance and their effects on a listener. [1 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 20:126/q17/p2 A phrase is empty padding only if it contributes nothing; an unexpected benefit makes it meaningful. — Al-Jurjani distinguishes condemned padding from a phrase that initially seems expendable but benefits the listener. [1 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 20:126/q17/p3 Metaphor is a form of simile and representation, an analogy grasped by intellect and heart. — Al-Jurjani says comparison is not apprehended by hearing alone. [1 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 20:126/q17/p4 Antithesis depends on opposed meanings rather than merely opposed word forms. — Al-Jurjani locates antithesis in the ideas expressed. [1 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 20:126/q17/p5 Word order can disrupt comprehension when it fails to follow the order of thought. — Al-Jurjani uses a line of al-Farazdaq: the hearer must rearrange it, disrupting the image like ill-assembled geometric parts. [1 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
