# 91:9: 15 questions

## 91:9/q01 What does qad add to aflaḥa?  [قَدْ · grammar]
- 91:9/q01/p1 It confirms the realization of success. — Al-Suyūṭī says qad enters affirmative finite ungoverned verbs and can bring a remote past closer to the present. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 91:9/q02 What does aflaḥa mean and what success does it promise?  [أَفْلَحَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether success names its general outcome or a particular spiritual realization.
- 91:9/q02/p1 It means success, felicity, or attaining one's aim, ultimately lasting good. — Al-Dāmghānī glosses happiness or blessing; al-Qurṭubī, success or felicity; al-Biqāʿī, attainment of all aims. Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts lasting afterlife good with transient gain. Elmalılı relates it to divine welcome and Paradise (89:29–30). [11 notes: WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAGHAWI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 91:9/q02/p2 Success is insight into the afterlife or one's appointed destiny. — Sahl, reported by al-Sulamī, speaks of insight into what is appointed; al-Tustarī specifies the affairs of the afterlife. [2 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل)]

## 91:9/q03 Who performs the purification in man zakkāhā?  [مَن زَكَّىٰهَا · referent]
turns on: The grammatical subject of zakkāhā and whether divine creation and human acquisition can both be affirmed.
- 91:9/q03/p1 The human person purifies his or her own soul. — Al-Ḥasan and Qatāda explain human obedience and good deeds. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the person acquires purification God created; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn cite the apparent pronoun of man. Al-Ālūsī calls this immediate though reports are inconclusive; Elmalılı stresses responsibility. Al-Zamakhsharī cites 87:14 and 20:111 and objects to attributing evil to God; Ibn Kathīr cites 87:14–15. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats self and soul as one being separated by intention and action. Al-Rāzī initially notes nafs is the explicit antecedent. - 91:9/q03/p2 God purifies the successful soul. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Zayd and al-Kalbī report God as agent; al-Ṭabarī says He cleanses disbelief and sins and enables obedience. Al-Naḥḥās proposes understanding man as soul or group to resolve its pronoun. Al-Rāzī favors this from 91:8 and the nearer antecedent 'inspiring it'; al-Wāḥidī invokes prior decree. Ṣāḥib al-Intiṣāf cites consistent divine subjects and 87:14. Prophetic prayers support the reading, though al-Ālūsī finds them inconclusive. Al-Qāḍī instead takes God as judging or naming it pure; al-Rāzī rejects this, citing the verb's causative form and immutable divine judgment. Abū Ḥayyān rejects al-Zamakhsharī's polemic with Ibn ʿAbbās and the prayer. - 91:9/q03/p3 Human striving and divine purification are attributable at different levels. — Elmalılı distinguishes acquisition by the person, causation by mentors, and decree, success and creation by God. Al-Ālūsī says servant participation suffices even when God creates the act, without proving independent power. The Study Quran cites 35:18, 76:30, 4:49, 24:21 and 13:11. ## 91:9/q04 What does zakkāhā mean?  [زَكَّىٰهَا · meaning]
turns on: The root's growth and purity senses, and whether the verse denotes moral reform or almsgiving.
- 91:9/q04/p1 It means cleansing or reforming the soul morally. — Mujāhid and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr gloss purification or setting right. Sources name disbelief, sins, ignorance, bad traits and false belief. Al-Naḥḥās compares cleansing wealth by giving the poor their share and zakī as pure. Corpus Coranicum cites 80:3, 80:7 and 87:14 against an exclusive almsgiving sense. [20 notes: TAB(مجاهد، سعيد بن جبير، عكرمة); TAB-FULL(مجاهد، سعيد بن جبير، عكرمة); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); NAHHAS; THALABI(سعيد بن جبير ومجاهد); MAWARDI(سعيد بن جبير,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير,مجاهد); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(IK); CORPUSCORANICUM+]
- 91:9/q04/p2 It means making the soul grow and rise through piety, knowledge and good action. — Ibn Qutayba cites thriving crops, blessed expenditure and zakat increasing wealth; tazkiya raises a witness's standing. He and al-Qurṭubī compare conspicuous hospitable Arabs with hidden misers, and a poet praises a visitor-feeding house. Al-Bayḍāwī links growth to knowledge and action, al-Ālūsī to piety in both, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī to blessed plant growth, and Ibn ʿĀshūr to increased goodness. [30 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الشاعر); NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI(الكشاف); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Sh, Z)]
- 91:9/q04/p3 It combines purification and growth, cleansing and cultivating the soul. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya describes purifying and nourishing through good deeds. Al-Biqāʿī pairs adorning virtue with removing vice. Elmalılı derives purity, growth, increase and blessing from the zakat root and distinguishes cleansing from cultivating faith, knowledge, benevolence and divine character. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 91:9/q04/p4 It means almsgiving, making a favorable afterlife bargain. — Corpus Coranicum reports Horovitz treating earliest z-k-w/y verbs as specialized almsgiving, Schulthess translating the verse by an afterlife bargain, and Neuwirth adducing wealth-giving with yatazakkā in 92:18. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM(Horovitz,Neuwirth); CORPUSCORANICUM-]
- 91:9/q04/p5 It can mean certifying or praising a soul as pure. — Elmalılı calls this a third sense but says people may not commend themselves before knowing their fate, citing 53:32; Ibn Qutayba's witness accreditation illustrates the related usage. [2 notes: ELMALILI; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]
- 91:9/q04/p6 It can signify purification from material dross. — Al-Ālūsī pairs it with the next verse's concealment as immersion in matter. [3 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 91:9/q05 How did the moral purity sense of zakkā arise?  [زَكَّىٰهَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the proposed Aramaic influence came specifically through Jewish usage or wider contact.
- 91:9/q05/p1 Arabic growth language converged with Aramaic purity language through contact before the Quran. — Corpus Coranicum distinguishes Arabic ṭ-h-r, cites Andrae's Syriac parallels, and prefers Bell's cultural-contact account, comparing ṣallā. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; CORPUSCORANICUM+]
- 91:9/q05/p2 Jewish usage caused the convergence with the Aramaic homonym. — Corpus Coranicum attributes this view to Torrey. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM(Torrey)]

## 91:9/q06 To what does the suffix hā in zakkāhā refer?  [زَكَّىٰهَا · grammar]
- 91:9/q06/p1 It refers to the soul mentioned in verse 7. — Al-Ālūsī gives man as subject and the soul as object. Corpus Coranicum identifies the feminine suffix's syntactic antecedent; Ibn ʿĀshūr explains that the person and soul are one being separated by intention and action. [6 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 91:9/q07 Are there variant ways of pronouncing the verse endings?  [قَدْ أَفْلَحَ مَن زَكَّىٰهَا · readings]
- 91:9/q07/p1 Readers vary between imāla and full pronunciation, applying imāla throughout or selectively. — Al-Rāzī records both patterns across this sūra's verse endings. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القراء)]

## 91:9/q08 Where may a reciter stop in this long oath passage?  [قَدْ أَفْلَحَ مَن زَكَّىٰهَا · readings]
- 91:9/q08/p1 A reciter may stop at verse endings through this clause. — Ibn al-Jazarī says the length of the oath passage allows those stops. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 91:9/q09 What is the answer to the preceding oath, and what role does this clause play?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the long oath licenses omission of lām or the whole answer, or syntactic inversion; the Thamūd narrative helps infer an omitted threat.
- 91:9/q09/p1 This success clause answers the oath, with an understood lām before qad. — Al-Khalīl and al-Zajjāj supply la-qad; al-Zajjāj says the long passage compensates for omitted lām. Ibn ʿAbbās counts eleven oaths; al-Rāzī reports seven sworn things. Al-Ṭabarī cites Qatāda. Al-Bayḍāwī says the oaths urge knowledge and gratitude. Al-Ālūsī argues lām omission is common and easier than omitting an entire answer, invokes 23:1, and says purification itself merits emphasis. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 92:4–5. - 91:9/q09/p2 The oath's answer is omitted: God will destroy Meccan deniers as He destroyed Thamūd; this clause is an aside. — Al-Zamakhsharī connects this to denial of Muḥammad and Ṣāliḥ and 91:11–14, placing the tangent after 91:8. Al-Nasafī calls it more evident; Qāshānī calls the doomed veiled deniers. Iṣlāḥī says suppression permits all implications of the oaths, prominently destruction of rebellious nations. - 91:9/q09/p3 The omitted oath answer is that the hearers will surely be resurrected. — Al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān report the supplied la-tubʿathunna. [4 notes: QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); SAMIN-DURR]
- 91:9/q09/p4 The success and failure clauses are placed before the oath by inversion. — Al-Jishumī and al-Qurṭubī report fronting and postponement rather than an omitted answer. [3 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 91:9/q09/p5 This clause interrupts the oath to explain moral choice, with the oath answer left open. — Ibn ʿĀshūr allows both oath answer and interruption explaining success by following divinely indicated piety. Iṣlāḥī favors a suppressed complement whose implications include judgment on rebellion. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 91:9/q10 What prophetic reports are connected to reciting this verse and asking for purification?  [* · reports]
turns on: The setting and wording of the prayer, and the strength of specific routes.
- 91:9/q10/p1 The Prophet paused at this verse and prayed for his soul's piety and purification. — Saʿīd ibn Abī Hilāl transmits the pause and prayer, calling God guardian and best purifier. Ibn Kathīr cites al-Ṭabarānī's Ibn ʿAbbās route and al-Haythamī's ḥasan grading; Abū Hurayra's route is absent from major collectors. Elmalılı and al-Ālūsī also cite Ibn ʿAbbās. [11 notes: TABRISI(سعيد بن أبي هلال); RAZI(سعيد بن أبي هلال); RAZI-FULL(سعيد بن أبي هلال); ABUHAYYAN(رسول الله); IBNKATHIR(أبو هريرة,ابن عباس); ALUSI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI(ابن عباس)]
- 91:9/q10/p2 The Prophet taught or recited a wider prayer asking God to grant the soul piety and purify it. — Zayd ibn Arqam's report includes refuge from incapacity, laziness, miserliness, cowardice, senility or worry, grave punishment, useless knowledge, an unsated soul, an unhumble heart, and unanswered prayer. Muslim transmits it; Aḥmad, Ibn Abī Shayba and al-Nasāʾī are also cited. ʿĀʾisha reports finding the Prophet praying in prostration; Ibn Kathīr says Aḥmad alone transmits it. [10 notes: BAGHAWI(زيد بن أرقم); BAGHAWI-FULL(زيد بن أرقم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR(زيد بن أرقم,عائشة); ALUSI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن أرقم); ELMALILI(زيد بن أرقم)]
- 91:9/q10/p3 A reported prophetic gloss says the soul God purifies succeeds and the one He deprives of good fails. — Ibn Kathīr questions the route through Juwaybir, abandoned in hadith, and al-Ḍaḥḥāk, who never met Ibn ʿAbbās. Al-Ālūsī cites other collections without grading. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(النبي)]

## 91:9/q11 What does the verse teach about human responsibility and divine decree?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether human action is independent, acquired under divine creation, or determined by prior decree.
- 91:9/q11/p1 People must choose and cultivate good and are responsible for corrupting the soul. — Quṭb describes a conscious discerning faculty; Iṣlāḥī says moral awareness obliges choice and compares neglect to shutting one's eyes near danger. Elmalılı cites 47:7 for striving that brings divine help. Al-Zamakhsharī says inspiration clarifies the difference and enables choice; Ibn Kathīr cites 87:14–15. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNKATHIR; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 91:9/q11/p2 God creates and enables acts, while human participation warrants attribution and responsibility. — Al-Ālūsī says attribution need not imply independent creation or autonomous choice. Elmalılı distinguishes God's creation and human acquisition; the Study Quran cites 35:18, 76:30, 4:49, 24:21 and 13:11 for will and change. Al-Biqāʿī says God enables each person's created path. [8 notes: BIQAI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 91:9/q11/p3 God's prior purification or abandonment determines the outcome outside independent human action. — Al-Wāḥidī invokes prior decree; al-Rāzī takes 91:8 as God granting piety or abandoning the wicked; al-Intiṣāf cites divine subjects in preceding oaths. Al-Zamakhsharī rejects attributing evil destiny to God. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); ALUSI-FULL(صاحب الانتصاف)+; ALUSI(صاحب الانتصاف)+; RAZI+; KASHSHAF-; KASHSHAF-FULL-]

## 91:9/q12 What inward forms of purification or success are drawn from the verse?  [* · inward]
turns on: Different spiritual practices or states are singled out as the inward realization of success.
- 91:9/q12/p1 Success is attending carefully to each moment. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ, reported by al-Sulamī. [1 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
- 91:9/q12/p2 Success is scrutinizing the source of one's food. — Abū ʿUthmān al-Maghribī opposes it to negligence. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان المغربي)]
- 91:9/q12/p3 Success is protecting the inner secret from worldly pollution. — Abū Bakr ibn Ṭāhir contrasts this with preoccupation with the world. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر)]
- 91:9/q12/p4 Success is knowing what God requires and addresses to oneself. — An unnamed authority contrasts it with neglect. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 91:9/q12/p5 Success is continual worship and service without another deity. — Reported by al-Sulamī from an unnamed authority. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 91:9/q12/p6 Success is turning toward the Lord. — An unnamed authority contrasts this with turning away. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 91:9/q12/p7 Purification restores the original fitra and perfection. — Al-Qāshānī describes this as the successful person's attainment. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 91:9/q13 How does this verse connect to nearby verses and the sūra's movement?  [* · links]
turns on: Which adjacent sequence is emphasized: inspired choice, Thamūd, neighboring sūras, or broader scriptural parallels.
- 91:9/q13/p1 It turns inspired piety and vice into the opposed outcomes of moral choice. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī invokes 91:8 and 2:197: innate recognition makes submission natural and piety provisions lasting life. Corpus Coranicum sees opposing cosmic phenomena moving through morality in verses 7–8 to contrasting outcomes; Iṣlāḥī calls the soul a mirror of the world and a witness to accountability. Al-Ālūsī says the promise and threat attest to God's knowledge, necessary existence, perfect attributes and beneficence. [12 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 91:9/q13/p2 The success and failure contrast prepares and explains Thamūd's ruin. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says success follows piety in order and failure precedes Thamūd; if the pair answers the oath, Thamūd is evidence for the failed corrupter. Corpus Coranicum says the sūra does not explicitly state afterlife recompense, though 91:12 may imply it. [5 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 91:9/q13/p3 It links al-Shams to adjacent sūras' accounts of moral paths. — Al-Ālūsī says the previous sūra's right and left groups are summarized here and 91:8 explains the two paths in 90:10. Al-Biqāʿī says al-Layl continues divine power and divergent paths despite shared sensual aims, with night and sleep frustrating desires. Iṣlāḥī says al-Layl details the soul's corruption and protection. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 91:9/q13/p4 Its blessing formula has Quranic and earlier scriptural parallels. — Corpus Coranicum compares Psalm 1:1 and especially 87:14, with related Quranic uses at 92:18, 80:3, 80:7, 79:18, 73:20 and 53:32. It says a pre-Islamic oath genre is repurposed with biblical creation ideas in verses 5–7. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 91:9/q13/p5 The oath exhorts completion of the soul through knowledge and gratitude. — Al-Bayḍāwī calls knowledge of the Creator and gratitude the highest theoretical and practical excellences. Al-Rāzī says oaths by beneficial creatures prompt reflection and gratitude and weigh on hearts. [3 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 91:9/q13/p6 The pair became a central statement of purification teaching. — The Study Quran says it inspired extensive writing on purification, which some regard as the Quran's entire purpose. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 91:9/q14 How is the divine-agent reading argued over?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the verb expresses actual divine causation, mere divine declaration, or an unacceptable attribution of evil to God.
- 91:9/q14/p1 God actually brings about purification, as the prayer asking Him to purify suggests. — Abū Ḥayyān cites the prophetic prayer that God is the best purifier. Al-Rāzī rejects al-Qāḍī's weaker account of God merely judging or naming the soul pure: the verb form denotes bringing about, while divine judgment cannot change without truth becoming falsehood or knowledge ignorance. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الرسول); RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 91:9/q14/p2 Attributing purification and defilement to God wrongly assigns evil destiny to Him. — Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī's attack on the divine-agent interpretation as Qadarite blame of God. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري)]

## 91:9/q15 What root is cited for the contrasting verb in the next verse?  [* · meaning]
- 91:9/q15/p1 Dassāhā derives from d-s-s, the root of concealment. — Al-Zamakhsharī compares the form cited for taqadada. [2 notes: KASHSHAF]
