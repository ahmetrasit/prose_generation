# 6:122: 25 questions

## 6:122/q01 How is the opening «أَوَمَن» read and parsed?  [أَوَمَن · readings]
turns on: The conjunction after the interrogative hamza is read as waw, quiescent waw, or faʾ; grammarians also differ over the implied coordinate clause.
- 6:122/q01/p1 The majority read awaman with a voiced waw: an interrogative hamza precedes a conjunction. — Al-Naḥḥās, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Samīn parse hamza before the conjunctive waw; al-Ṭabrisī calls it a question demanding acknowledgment. Al-Samīn also allows an omitted verb between the hamza and waw. Bursawī and al-Ālūsī supply a parallel clause implied by context; Ibn ʿĀshūr coordinates it with 6:121 and reads denial of equality. [12 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:122/q01/p2 Ṭalḥa ibn Muṣarrif reads afaman, with faʾ in place of waw. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the sense close to the waw reading; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn transmit the variant. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(طلحة بن مصرف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 6:122/q01/p3 Al-Musayyibī transmits from Nāfiʿ a reading with the waw quiescent. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī say it may join the question of seeking a judge other than God (6:114) to the revived person's case, with coordination understood through the sense. [2 notes: NAHHAS(المسيبي عن نافع بن أبي نعيم); QURTUBI-FULL(المسيبي عن نافع بن أبي نعيم)]

## 6:122/q02 Which people does the comparison concern, and what occasion is reported?  [أَوَمَن كَانَ مَيْتًۭا كَمَن مَّثَلُهُۥ فِى ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ · reports]
turns on: Whether reports identify a named convert and Abū Jahl, or the wording and context make the comparison universal.
- 6:122/q02/p1 The believer is ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb and the one in darkness is Abū Jahl. — Zayd ibn Aslam links the occasion to the Prophet's prayer that Islam be strengthened through Abū Jahl or ʿUmar: God guided ʿUmar and left Abū Jahl astray. Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Yamān, Abū Sinān, and others are cited in variant transmissions. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports but objects to restricting the verse to them. [26 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو سنان الشيباني,الضحاك,زيد بن أسلم); THALABI(الضحاك ويمان); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك، مقاتل); WAHIDI-ASBAB(زيد بن أسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك ويمان); BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك); TABRISI(الضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(زيد بن أسلم، الضحاك); RAZI-FULL(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم والسدي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم); DURR-FULL(أبو سنان,ابن عباس,الضحاك,زيد بن أسلم); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس، الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,زيد بن أسلم); STUDYQURAN(reports (R, Ṭ, Ṭs)); TABATABAI(ابن عباس وزيد بن أسلم)-; IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 6:122/q02/p2 The believer is Ḥamza and the one in darkness is Abū Jahl. — Ibn ʿAbbās's report says Abū Jahl insulted, harmed, or threw filth at the Prophet; Ḥamza returned from hunting, struck Abū Jahl with his bow, denounced idols and accepted Islam. The versions vary in the precise affront and conversion details. Al-Rāzī transmits it without grading. [16 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(one report); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 6:122/q02/p3 The believer is ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir and the one in darkness is Abū Jahl. — ʿIkrima, al-Kalbī and other transmissions identify the pair; some reports name only ʿAmmār. Al-Rāzī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī give no grading. [16 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عكرمة); THALABI(عكرمة والكلبي); MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة، الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(عكرمة والكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة والكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عكرمة والزجاج); TABRISI(عكرمة وأبو جعفر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس، عكرمة); RAZI-FULL(عكرمة والكلبي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم); DURR-FULL(عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة); TABATABAI(زيد بن أسلم); STUDYQURAN(reports (R, Ṭ, Ṭs)); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 6:122/q02/p4 It applies generally to every guided believer and persistent unbeliever. — Al-Ḥasan and others state the general comparison; al-Ṭabrisī prefers it while including named cases, and al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Ālūsī, and Study Quran prefer the same scope. Al-Rāzī says it fits everyone and that a sūra descending together makes specific occasions hard to assert without a prophetic specification. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says context bars restriction to ʿUmar; al-Wāḥidī says commentators agree on a believer and disbeliever, commonly identifying Abū Jahl in reports. [17 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن، غيره من أهل العلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن,الزجاج,المفسرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وآخرون); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; STUDYQURAN+; TABATABAI(ابن عباس وزيد بن أسلم)+; TABRISI(الحسن وجماعة)+]
- 6:122/q02/p5 The revived person is the Prophet and the one in darkness is Abū Jahl. — Muqātil and other reports pair them. Abū Jahl said Banū ʿAbd Manāf rivaled his clan in prestige and now claimed a prophet; he demanded comparable revelation and refused belief, linking the account to 6:124. Al-Zajjāj says the Prophet received Islam, prophecy and wisdom. Al-Rāzī gives no grading. [10 notes: MUQATIL(أبو جهل); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عكرمة والزجاج); STUDYQURAN(other reports); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 6:122/q02/p6 Abū Jahl is the person in darkness; the companion figure is left unnamed. — Ibn Kathīr reports the identification as Abū Jahl, ʿAmr ibn Hishām, without identifying the revived figure in that report. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]

## 6:122/q03 What is the syntax of the two compared clauses and «لَيْسَ بِخَارِجٍ»?  [أَوَمَن كَمَن مَّثَلُهُۥ فِى ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ لَيْسَ بِخَارِجٍۢ مِّنْهَا · grammar]
turns on: Which pronoun carries the circumstantial clause and what unspoken terms complete the comparison.
- 6:122/q03/p1 The first man is subject and kaman its predicate; the final clause is circumstantial, with attachment disputed. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī parse the first man as relative subject; al-Ālūsī alternatively allows both man words to be indefinite with descriptive clauses. Al-Bayḍāwī and Abū al-Baqāʾ attach the circumstance to an implicit locative pronoun, not the separated hāʾ in mathaluhu; Makkī attaches it to the pronoun implicit in 'darkness.' Ibn ʿĀshūr attaches it to the pronoun governed by 'likeness.' Al-Ālūsī prefers a complete implied clause over a bare locative. [10 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 6:122/q03/p2 The first clause and darker counterpart have several implied elements completing the comparison. — Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies 'is one who was dead equal?' or 'the condition of one who was dead'; walking with light implies a counterpart dead in darkness, and the third ellipsis compares the remaining figure to a dead person. Al-Ṭabrisī says the person's likeness stands for the person. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABRISI]

## 6:122/q04 What kind of comparison is the verse making, and why is it phrased as a question?  [أَوَمَن كَمَن مَّثَلُهُۥ فِى ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the two representational pictures count technically as similes or metaphors; all explain denial of parity.
- 6:122/q04/p1 The question denies equality and makes the guided person's superiority clear through two full scenes. — Al-Kashshāf shows a revived person with discernment against one lost in darkness. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the hamza and kāf deny equality; citing 13:16 and 32:18, he says denying resemblance indirectly asserts superiority. He calls each case a complete image, with components also open to comparison. Bursawī supplies the rebuke's implicit opposing clause; al-Burhān observes that this is a comparison stating both sides. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BURHAN; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 6:122/q04/p2 The two scenes are extended or representational similes. — Al-Quṭb al-Rāzī classifies them thus in al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(القطب الرازي); IBNASHUR-FULL(القطب الرازي)]
- 6:122/q04/p3 The two scenes are tableau or representational metaphors. — Al-Shihāb objects to the simile analysis: the represented subject is not explicit, and the first full image is tenor, the second vehicle. He compares saying 'is a lion like a fox?' for brave versus cowardly people. Al-Taftāzānī classifies the construction likewise; Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers him, saying kāf denies the likeness of the two clauses together rather than their internal comparisons. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الشهاب); IBNASHUR-FULL(التفتزاني); ALUSI-FULL(الشهاب)+; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 6:122/q05 What does the dead person's revival mean?  [كَانَ مَيْتًۭا فَأَحْيَيْنَٰهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether death and revival denote disbelief and faith, ignorance and knowledge, literal embryonic life, or stages of intellectual capacity.
- 6:122/q05/p1 A person dead in unbelief or error is guided into faith and spiritual life. — Muqātil, Ibn Qutayba and many exegetes equate death with disbelief or error and revival with guidance; faith gives life to hearts. Al-Wāḥidī cites 16:21; al-Rāzī cites 16:21, 36:70, 27:80, 35:19, 35:22 and says ignorance immobilizes while insight leads to salvation. Al-Ṭabrisī says unbelievers' lives neither benefit themselves nor others and may harm others, whereas believers benefit both. Ibn Kathīr adds following the messengers; Biqāʿī invokes the heart's effect on the whole body. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kur'an Yolu say Islam restores discernment and pursuit of good; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says faith and guidance coincide here. Asad reports broad commentator agreement on the metaphor. [50 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,مجاهد والسدي وأبو سنان); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,ابن عباس ومجاهد ومحمد بن كعب وجميع المفسرين); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JURJANI-ASRAR; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,بعض المفسرين); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وغيرهما); TABRISI(ابن عباس والحسن ومجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(all commentators); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:122/q05/p2 Death is ignorance; revival is knowledge and action. — Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī cite verses by a Basran poet for ignorance as death; al-Wāḥidī's semantic authorities likewise equate knowledge with life because it leads to truth and right conduct. Al-Jurjānī says life can signify knowledge or power, death their absence, and 'alive of heart' can mean discernment or vigorous action. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); JURJANI-ASRAR; SULAMI(سهل)]
- 6:122/q05/p3 A sperm drop, or a developing embryo, is physically brought to life by the spirit. — Ibn Baḥr specifies the sperm drop and breathing of spirit; Abū Ḥayyān records sperm, clot and morsel of flesh; al-Ṭabrisī cites 2:28. Al-Māturīdī instead emphasizes the unseen, unreasoning womb state before birth and subsequent sight of truth. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن بحر); TABRISI(قول غير مسمى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن بحر,الماتريدي)]
- 6:122/q05/p4 The first life is the soul's readiness for knowledge; revelation or reasoning supplies the following light. — Al-Rāzī lays out four stages: capacity, primary universal axioms, acquired theory, and sacred insight. He calls capacity death, axioms life, reasoning light, and active sacred insight walking; alternatively sound reason needs revelation as an eye needs sunlight. Abū Ḥayyān reports both accounts but rejects the elaboration as remote from ordinary Arabic; Study Quran reports the revelation formulation. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 6:122/q05/p5 One image begins with someone created upon Islam's natural disposition, then guided by clear signs. — Al-Ālūsī transmits this composite reading from Shaykh al-Islām. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الاسلام)]

## 6:122/q06 How is «مَيْتًا» read, and do the forms differ in sense?  [مَيْتًۭا · readings]
turns on: Whether the phonetic contraction changes meaning, and which readers use the doubled yāʾ.
- 6:122/q06/p1 Nāfiʿ, Yaʿqūb, and Abū Jaʿfar are reported with a doubled yāʾ; both forms have the same meaning. — Al-Fārisī and al-Wāḥidī report Abū ʿUbayda and language scholars that the light form loses the second yāʾ, originally wāw. Al-Ṭabrisī adduces Abū al-Raʿlā's poem; Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives the doubled form with kasra. Al-Nashr details the readers' agreements in 6:122 and related loci, including 36:33 and 49:12. [20 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(الباقون,نافع); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل اللغة,نافع); BAGHAWI-FULL(نافع والآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع); TABRISI(أبو عبيدة,أهل المدينة ويعقوب,الباقون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(نافع، أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL(نافع); BAYDAWI-FULL(نافع ويعقوب); NASAFI-FULL(مدنى); NASHR(أبو جعفر,نافع,يعقوب); ALUSI-FULL(نافع ويعقوب)]
- 6:122/q06/p2 A grammatical debate distinguishes doubled mayyit as dying from light mayt as already dead. — Qirāʾāt-ER notes both are canonical and reports the classical grammarians' debate; the other philologists above instead equate their sense. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(classical grammarians)]

## 6:122/q07 Is the life in the verse figurative or a real higher life?  [مَيْتًۭا فَأَحْيَيْنَٰهُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether familiar life and death are solely a comparison or indicate a distinct level of actual life.
- 6:122/q07/p1 Death and revival are figurative names for error and guidance. — Al-Jurjānī places the metaphor in the predicate 'life,' while attribution of the gift to God remains literal; he distinguishes metaphors in both predicate and attribution and describes disparagement by treating existence as nonexistence. Al-Suyūṭī calls revival a concordant metaphor. Abū Ḥayyān, Asad, and Study Quran state the spiritual metaphor; Ibn ʿĀshūr develops two complete figurative scenes. [11 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR; ITQAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(all commentators); STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:122/q07/p2 The believer's spiritual life and light are actual higher realities. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues from the good life promised in 16:97, the deficient hearts, eyes and ears of unbelievers in 7:179, and the spirit supporting believers in 58:22. He says faith yields higher perception and will, an enduring life protected by God and immersed in divine love. Ibn ʿArabī, as quoted by Bursawī, progressively denies created things independent acts, life, and existence; Bursawī says only God is unqualifiedly alive. [10 notes: TABATABAI; BURSEVI(الشيخ الأكبر)]

## 6:122/q08 What inward states do death, revival and light describe?  [مَيْتًۭا فَأَحْيَيْنَٰهُ نُورًۭا · inward]
turns on: The inward sources locate death in separation, ignorance, self-regard or desire, and distinguish several effects and signs of revived life.
- 6:122/q08/p1 Ignorance, separation or veiling is death; knowing, loving and drawing near to God is life and light. — Sahl calls ignorance death and knowledge life; Ibn ʿAṭāʾ makes separation death and connection light. Al-Qāshānī describes the soul veiled by its attributes and revived through knowledge and love of the Real or divine disclosure; graded light comes as guidance, divine attributes or sustaining presence. Bursawī's people of spiritual reality add self-following versus divine love, and gnosis that outlasts bodily life, supported by a Prophetic report and Ḥāfiẓ's verse. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes enduring love and higher perception; his treatment of Joseph says love purifies motives beyond fear and hope, and divine love guides beautiful acts. [12 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,سهل); QASHANI; BURSEVI(أرباب الحقيقة,الحافظ,النبي عليه السلام); TABATABAI]
- 6:122/q08/p2 Death is the self or reliance on one's own acts; life is the awakened, needy heart. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ contrasts a living self and dead heart with extinguishing the self and enlivening the heart by lights of nearness. Jaʿfar says reliance on obedience dies through supplication and apology; an unnamed authority contrasts seeing one's deeds with seeing one's need. Al-Qāsim compares awakening's light to bodily spirits; al-Qannāʾ calls it gnosis rather than biological life and describes annihilation from invocations and life in God. [6 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,القاسم,القناء,بعضهم,جعفر); BURSEVI(أرباب الحقيقة)]
- 6:122/q08/p3 Revival makes a person a guide to others and grants abiding life through God's light. — Jaʿfar says the one revived by God becomes a guide to strangers and the lost while the desire-following person lacks divine nearness. Al-Jurayrī says the servant enlivened by divine lights never truly dies; abandonment is never true life. Bursawī says one alive with love does not die and preserves the believer-alive-in-both-abodes report. [4 notes: SULAMI(أبو محمد الجريري,جعفر); BURSEVI(الحافظ,النبي عليه السلام)]
- 6:122/q08/p4 Spiritual life shows itself in remembrance, watchfulness, detachment and preparation for death. — Shāh al-Kirmānī gives intimacy after loneliness, fullness of the Real through constant remembrance, and awe from pure watchfulness. Shāh names transience of the world, preparation for death, and tears at the afterlife as signs of a heart's light. Bursawī's report from the King of Kerman gives seclusion from people, calling to God and remembrance by tongue and heart; these formulations differ in their signs. [3 notes: SULAMI(شاه,شاه الكرماني); BURSEVI(شاه كرمان)]

## 6:122/q09 What does the fāʾ in «فَأَحْيَيْنَٰهُ» do?  [فَأَحْيَيْنَٰهُ · grammar]
- 6:122/q09/p1 It is conjunctive. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reads the fāʾ as joining the revival to the prior state. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 6:122/q10 What theological point is drawn from God giving life and light?  [فَأَحْيَيْنَٰهُ وَجَعَلْنَا لَهُۥ نُورًۭا · theology]
turns on: How the divine act of giving guidance is described: creation of faith and motives, distinct modes of guidance, or the reported primordial distribution of light.
- 6:122/q10/p1 God creates faith, guidance and the motives behind belief or unbelief. — Al-Rāzī says life and light are knowledge and guidance explicitly given by God; a person seeking knowledge but receiving ignorance has the contrary produced in him. Explaining each mistaken judgment by an earlier mistaken judgment regresses unless some ignorance is created externally. His school's account of God-created motives also governs the adorned deeds. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا)]
- 6:122/q10/p2 God's guidance includes created direction and revealed direction, with different outcomes. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes created direction of each being toward its form, appointed acts and lifespan (20:50; 87:2–3) from legislative guidance to true belief and righteous action with promised reward and punishment; the latter can merely show the road (76:3) or bring one to it (7:176). [3 notes: TABATABAI]
- 6:122/q10/p3 A report says God created beings in darkness, then sprinkled His light upon them. — Ibn Kathīr cites Aḥmad's Musnad: whoever received the light was guided, while whoever missed it went astray. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 6:122/q11 What distinctive word choices and omissions sharpen the two scenes?  [فَأَحْيَيْنَٰهُ وَجَعَلْنَا لَهُۥ نُورًۭا فِى ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ كَذَٰلِكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Which asymmetries of attribution, number and ellipsis explain the comparison's force.
- 6:122/q11/p1 The illuminated believer is credited to God and benefits others; the dark counterpart is settled in varied darknesses. — Abū Ḥayyān observes explicit divine attribution of the believer's benefits, no equivalent agent attribution for the dark figure, plural darknesses for varied unbelief, and public illumination benefiting others. Biqāʿī says if even the ideal likeness is rooted in darkness, the represented person is worse; al-Ṭabrisī says physical light and knowledge alike guide on a road. Bursawī explicitly says God gives a great light alongside the life. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABRISI; BURSEVI]
- 6:122/q11/p2 The verse omits matching details on each side, inviting completion of the paired images. — Biqāʿī says darkness is mentioned with the second image but implied in the first, while adornment is said second and implied first. Ibn ʿĀshūr says light and walking imply death in darkness for the other side; the final clause supplies the reason for the persistent error. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:122/q12 What is the light given to the revived person?  [نُورًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the gift denotes faith itself, scripture, the understanding produced by faith, eschatological light, an Imam, or revelation.
- 6:122/q12/p1 It is faith, Islam, religion, or divine guidance. — Muqātil calls it faith guiding among people. Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, al-Kalbī and Ibn Zayd give guidance, religion, faith or Islam; al-Wāḥidī explicitly treats these as equivalent. Al-Baghawī cites 2:257, al-Dāmghānī 57:28 and 2:257, and Ibn al-Jawzī 2:257, 24:40 and 57:28. Ibn Kathīr accepts Islam alongside the Qurʾān reading. Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers faith; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the context requires divine guidance to true belief and action. [26 notes: ZAJJAJ; FARISI-HUJJA; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج وأبو علي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الاسلام); TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); RAZI-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

Iṣlāḥī connects God's Book to 6:114–119 as the criterion against innovations; Study Quran calls the Quran a possible specific sense. [21 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة والحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي,العوفي وابن أبي طلحة عن ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI(قيل)-]

- 6:122/q12/p7 It is acquired reasoning and finally present sacred insight, after the soul receives basic knowledge. — Al-Rāzī distinguishes capacity, axioms, theoretical reasoning and immediately illuminating sacred insight; he prefers this staged account to the simpler Qurʾān, religion or wisdom glosses, though calls them close. - 6:122/q12/p8 It is an Imam whom the believer follows. — Imam al-Bāqir, reported in al-Kāfī, glosses the dead as knowing nothing and the light as an Imam. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī accepts this as application to a case while reading the context as guidance to belief and righteous action. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام)]
- 6:122/q12/p9 It is the beauty of faith. — Al-Māwardī records this as a third gloss. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 6:122/q14 How do the words «يَمْشِى بِهِۦ فِى ٱلنَّاسِ» attach grammatically?  [يَمْشِى بِهِۦ فِى ٱلنَّاسِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the walking clause is descriptive or an answer to an implied question, and whether 'among people' belongs to walking or the prior death.
- 6:122/q14/p1 Yamshī qualifies the light, and fī al-nās attaches to the walking; the bāʾ marks the means or cause. — Al-Samīn makes yamshī an adjective of nūran; Ibn ʿAṭiyya ordinarily attaches fī al-nās to yamshī. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the bāʾ causal and says 'people' in the pictured scene are living members of society, corresponding to the convert's fellow Muslims. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:122/q14/p2 The walking clause answers an implied question about what one does with the light. — Al-Ālūsī offers this beside its adjectival attachment to nūran. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:122/q14/p3 Fī al-nās can attach to kāna maytan. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows this alternative, placing 'among people' with the former state. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 6:122/q15 What does the verse teach believers to do with the given light?  [يَمْشِى بِهِۦ فِى ٱلنَّاسِ · interpretation]
- 6:122/q15/p1 Act by God's guidance and carry it among people for their benefit. — Iṣlāḥī says God's light is for seeking guidance and public presentation, never concealment; 6:114–119 supply its scriptural setting. Qatāda says the believer acts by God's Book and returns to it; Abū Sinān says walking means acting. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says insight selects happiness-producing acts. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Islam restores discernment and avoidance of corruption. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNABIHATIM(أبو سنان الشيباني,قتادة); TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:122/q16 How does «كَمَن مَّثَلُهُۥ فِى ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ» work?  [كَمَن مَّثَلُهُۥ فِى ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mathal contributes a description, is semantically extra, or invokes a further implicit comparison.
- 6:122/q16/p1 Mathal means description, condition, or example: his description is that he remains in darkness. — Al-Kashshāf compares 47:15, where the following clause supplies Paradise's description. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Samīn make mathaluhu subject and fī al-ẓulumāt predicate. Al-Wāḥidī's grammarians call the person an example; Abū ʿAlī says the likeness's darkness means the person's darkness. Biqāʿī reads example exposing the inner state or a description; Bursawī and Ibn ʿĀshūr read remarkable condition or state. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī supplies an omitted pronoun returning to man. [16 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي,غيره من النحويين); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 6:122/q16/p2 Mathal or mithl is extra in sense: the phrase means 'like one in darkness.' — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Qurṭubī compare extra wording in 5:95 and 42:11; al-Baghawī and Ibn al-Jawzī agree. Al-Wāḥidī says mithl and mathal coincide here. Al-Jurjānī cautions that an added word is not itself a metaphor, though an unexpected case created by an added particle may be called figurative, and discusses aspect-dependent addition with 57:29 and Abū ʿAlī's lām example. [10 notes: THALABI(بعضهم); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; JURJANI-ASRAR(أبو علي)]
- 6:122/q16/p3 The sense is 'someone whose likeness, if likened, would be a person in darkness.' — Al-Ḥusayn ibn al-Faḍl offers this analysis, which al-Wāḥidī calls weakest. Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī report it; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī would accept it except for its excessive ellipsis. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسين بن الفضل)-]
- 6:122/q16/p4 One reported construal takes the phrase as an imperative 'be,' or likens the person to one wandering in unbelief, ignorance and error. — Al-Thaʿlabī transmits both formulations as an alternative view. [1 notes: THALABI(بعضهم)]

Al-Rāzī says a dispute over religion leads into the parable. Biqāʿī reads obedience as return to darkness. Al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭībī link 'if you obey them' to this deterrent. ## 6:122/q19 What are the darknesses, and why can the person not escape?  [فِى ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ لَيْسَ بِخَارِجٍۢ مِّنْهَا · interpretation]

Al-Rāzī says entrenched traits seem innate. Abū Ḥayyān says plural darknesses reflect varied unbelief; Bursawī and Kur'an Yolu qualify impossibility as persistence in that state. - 6:122/q19/p3 On the inward reading, they are the self, its attributes and acts, or separation from God. — Al-Qāshānī says these veils prevent emergence; Ibn ʿAṭāʾ describes the darkness of severance from God. [2 notes: QASHANI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]

- 6:122/q20/p1 It reinforces the settled denial that the person can leave darkness. — Abū Ḥayyān says it intensifies the image; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ distinguishes firm bāʾ-negation from a less fully committed bare laysa, citing numerous Qurʾānic examples. ## 6:122/q21 What does «كَذَٰلِكَ» point back to?  [كَذَٰلِكَ · interpretation]

- 6:122/q21/p1 It compares the unbelievers' adornment with the believers' faith being made attractive. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Bayḍāwī and Bursawī say each group's condition is likened in the attractiveness of its chosen course; al-Ṭabrisī cites 23:53. - 6:122/q21/p2 It points to bringing believers to life, or to the unbelievers' remaining in darkness. — Al-Samīn analyzes the kāf as adjective of an omitted verbal noun and reports both estimates. Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq put kāf in the nominative: 'like what was related to you was adorned.' Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the parable supplies a general model, comparing 13:17 and 29:43, and adornment imprisons them. Al-Ālūsī also allows reference to earlier adornment. [7 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:122/q21/p4 It recalls devils inspiring their allies in the preceding warning. — Al-Ālūsī explicitly offers this antecedent alongside prior adornment and the adornment of faith. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 6:122/q22 Who makes the unbelievers' deeds attractive?  [زُيِّنَ · theology]
turns on: Whether the passive is supplied with God, Satan, both at different causal levels, or kept deliberately unspecified.
- 6:122/q22/p1 Satan or human and jinn devils adorn the deeds by suggestion and deception. — Ibn ʿAbbās says Satan makes idol worship attractive. The Muʿtazila cite al-Ḥasan; al-Ṭabrisī joins Satan and the self, invoking 6:121. Ibn ʿĀshūr says human devils embellish directly and jinn prompt, invoking 6:137. Kur'an Yolu links the failure of judgment to 6:121. Al-Rāzī argues against Satan as sole source. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة والحسن); TABRISI(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL-]
- 6:122/q22/p2 God does so by creating the motive or by His decree. — Al-Rāzī's school argues every act rests on a judgment of greater benefit, whose motive God creates. He rejects Satan as sole agent: tracing Satan's unbelief to another satan gives regress; 6:108 and 6:123 attribute such acts to God. Al-Nasafī cites 6:108, Ibn Kathīr speaks of divine decree and wisdom, and Biqāʿī of a formed disposition. Al-Kashshāf and al-Samīn report the attribution, with 27:4 as evidence. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا)+]
- 6:122/q22/p3 Both attributions work: devils whisper; God creates what occurs. — Bursawī distinguishes creation from Satan's whispering. Elmālīlı says God creates the devils' promptings as they happen, while they mislead. Al-Ālūsī states both modes. Al-Ṭabrisī adds that the passive does not even require a distinct agent, citing idioms of self-admiration and devotion. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL; TABRISI]
- 6:122/q22/p4 The passive leaves the agent unspecified to emphasize the occurrence of adornment. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the focus is the occurrence, not naming its author; al-Samīn says either God or Satan can be supplied, citing 27:4 and 29:38. Al-Sāmarrāʾī observes that God explicitly claims adorning faith and heavens, whereas desires and evil deeds are usually passive and this verse does not say 'We adorned their evil deeds.' [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 6:122/q23 What deeds are made attractive, and what is their effect?  [لِلْكَٰفِرِينَ مَا كَانُوا۟ يَعْمَلُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the focus is specifically idolatry and sins, or the general self-approval that veils a person.
- 6:122/q23/p1 Their unbelief, idol worship and sins look good to them, so they persist in error. — Muqātil specifies polytheists, especially Abū Jahl. Ibn ʿAbbās names idol worship; al-Baghawī names disbelief and disobedience. Al-Ālūsī and Bursawī include many repeated forms of sin; Ibn ʿĀshūr says delight in them explains persistence despite intelligence and proofs. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Elmālīlı say attractive deeds imprison them in darkness and cannot bring escape. Iṣlāḥī relates this to 6:125 and 2:7 and offers reassurance to the Prophet. ## 6:122/q24 How is «مَا كَانُوا۟ يَعْمَلُونَ» parsed?  [مَا كَانُوا۟ يَعْمَلُونَ · grammar]
- 6:122/q24/p1 The clause occupies the passive subject's position; mā has three possible analyses. — Al-Samīn says mā can be a relative pronoun, a verbal-noun particle, or an indefinite noun qualified by the following clause; relative and indefinite analyses imply an omitted resumptive. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 6:122/q25 What ruling is drawn about offerings or acts directed to jinn?  [مَا كَانُوا۟ يَعْمَلُونَ · law]
- 6:122/q25/p1 Rituals or sacrifices performed to win jinn protection or imitate their offerings are shirk. — Bursawī names burning incense, sprinkling rosewater or sacrificing where jinn are suspected; he also condemns sacrifices imitating a rooster or guarding family and property when directed to jinn. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
