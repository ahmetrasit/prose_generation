# 74:55: 13 questions

## 74:55/q01 How does a person's will to remember relate to God's will?  [فَمَن شَآءَ · theology]
turns on: Whether the human choice expressed here is sufficient in itself, is conditioned by God's will, or whether God's willing in the next verse means coercion.
- 74:55/q01/p1 People have a real, accountable choice, but remembering and attaining what they seek depend on God's will and enabling. — Al-Tabari says God must grant ability; Ibn ʿAtiyya says guidance enables choosing and nothing occurs outside God's will. Ibn al-Jawzi says the next verse redirects willing to God. Ibn Kathir cites 76:30; al-Biqaʿi says God created human powers and choice. Al-Razi argues that non-remembrance shows God did not will remembrance, and that restricting His will to coercion departs from the apparent wording. Ibn ʿAshur cites 81:29: human will grounds duties and recompense, but God's supreme will cannot be resisted; Ashʿari kasb and Muʿtazili created capacity are close formulations. He names divine enabling tawfīq, abandonment to corrupt ideas and desires khidhlān, and rescue from error luṭf, exemplified by ʿUmar's conversion; he cites 6:125 for God's opening or constricting the breast and 4:78–79 for reconciling divine command and accountability, while acknowledging a mystery behind promise and warning. Kur'an Yolu says God wills and creates good for one seeking good and evil for one seeking evil. [16 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL+]
- 74:55/q01/p2 The invitation establishes a servant's power to choose and take heed without compulsion. — Al-Jishumi opposes compulsion theorists; al-Tabrisi says taking heed lies within a person's capacity. Al-Tabatabaʾi describes an invitation in a setting of choice. Islahi says hearers choose their fate while the Prophet only delivers the reminder and cannot force guidance. [5 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:55/q01/p3 God's willing means compelling and forcing people to remember. — Al-Razi reports this as the Muʿtazilite interpretation; he argues against it from the verse's unrestricted wording and the absence of remembrance when God does not will it. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); RAZI-FULL-]

## 74:55/q02 What does the invitation 'whoever wills' convey to its hearers?  [فَمَن شَآءَ · rhetoric]
- 74:55/q02/p1 It invites willing reflection, places responsibility on hearers, and leaves neglect without excuse; the Messenger delivers the warning but cannot force acceptance. — Ibn ʿAshur links willing reflection to 74:37 and 73:19 and says neglect has no excuse. Al-Tabatabaʾi stresses invitation without compulsion. Islahi says stubborn hearers choose their fate, the Prophet must deliver without bearing their tantrums, and God warns before seizing people to remove their excuse. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:55/q03 What does it mean to 'remember it'?  [ذَكَرَهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: What is remembered, and whether remembrance chiefly means taking admonition, retaining and understanding, or acting on it.
- 74:55/q03/p1 Take heed of the Qur'an or reminder: keep it in mind, understand it, and put its guidance into practice. — Muqatil includes understanding; al-Tabari specifies obeying commands and prohibitions; Ibn ʿAbbas, reported by al-Wahidi, glosses the verb as taking admonition. Al-Zamakhshari adds retention and making it one's concern; Ibn al-Jawzi adds understanding. Al-Nasafi stresses not forgetting; al-Biqaʿi adds retaining it in the heart, knowing its meanings, and taking on its character. Bursawi urges heed before the grave; Study Quran especially stresses acting according to God's Word. Al-Baghawi, al-Wahidi al-Wajiz, al-Tabrisi, al-Baydawi, al-Alusi, Ibn ʿAshur and al-Tabatabaʾi also explain it as taking heed; Ibn ʿAshur says this happens through the Qur'an. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 74:55/q03/p2 Remember the afterlife and work for salvation there. — Ibn ʿAtiyya adds that God's guidance enables this choice. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 74:55/q03/p3 Remember God. — Study Quran offers this as an alternative sense of the phrase. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 74:55/q04 To whom or what does the pronoun in 'remember it' refer?  [ذَكَرَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether the object of remembrance is the Qur'an or reminder, God understood from context, or the afterlife.
- 74:55/q04/p1 The Qur'an or the reminder mentioned in 74:49. — Al-Zamakhshari relates the pronouns in 'it is a reminder' and 'remember it' to al-tadhkira in 74:49; their masculine form follows its sense as dhikr or Qur'an. Ibn al-Jawzi and Ibn ʿAshur specify the Qur'an; Ibn ʿAshur construes the phrase as 'remember by it.' Kur'an Yolu calls the reminder and warning the Qur'an's verses. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 74:55/q04/p2 God, understood from the context. — Ibn ʿAshur notes God's name has not previously appeared in these verses and compares 73:19; Study Quran also offers remembering God as an alternative. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 74:55/q04/p3 The afterlife. — Ibn ʿAtiyya explains the act as remembering the afterlife and working for it. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 74:55/q05 What enables a seeker to benefit from the Qur'an's reminder?  [ذَكَرَهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes attribute receptivity to sincere seeking, the Qur'an's qualities, and divine disclosure; these explanations can coexist.
- 74:55/q05/p1 Sincere seeking, intent and resolve lead a person to its guidance and hidden meanings. — Al-Biqaʿi says sincere intent and resolve disclose the Qur'an's hidden secrets. Kur'an Yolu says seekers of guidance and truth find what they seek in it. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 74:55/q05/p2 The Qur'an's clear, attractive wording and deep meanings make it a powerful reminder. — Al-Biqaʿi says its wording and meaning were easy for its originally unlearned audience, while reflection continually yields more, like a fresh sea whose depths no one reaches. He also cites clear meanings, sweet wording, grand structure, smooth composition and lofty aims. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 74:55/q05/p3 God's will determines whether the Qur'an's disclosures reach someone. — Al-Biqaʿi says God may veil a person or unveil its beauties and treasures to a follower and adorn him with it, as with Muhammad, whose character was the Qur'an. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 74:55/q06 Who benefits from remembering, and how?  [ذَكَرَهُۥ · interpretation]
- 74:55/q06/p1 The benefit returns to the person who remembers, bringing happiness in this life and the next. — Al-Zamakhshari and al-Nasafi say the benefit returns to the rememberer. Bursawi says taking heed before the grave brings happiness in both worlds; al-Alusi also names happiness in both abodes. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 74:55/q07 How does this invitation follow the preceding reminder and echo another sūra?  [* · links]
- 74:55/q07/p1 It follows naturally from calling the Qur'an a reminder and resembles the invitation in Sūrat al-Muzzammil. — Ibn ʿAshur cites the corresponding line at 73:19. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:55/q08 How does the sūra's conclusion relate to its opening and overall aim?  [* · links]
- 74:55/q08/p1 The ending returns to the opening call to warn and magnify God, joining warning with the possibility of returning to obedience and receiving forgiveness. — Al-Biqaʿi cites 74:2, 74:3 and 74:5, including shunning impurity and having lapses covered. He says the sūra displays the greatness of the Prophet sent to warn through the greatness and complete power of his Sender, who disclosed knowledge to him. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 74:55/q09 How does the following statement about God's fear and forgiveness complete this invitation?  [* · links]
- 74:55/q09/p1 Remembrance should lead to reverent fear and obedience toward God, who merits that fear and can forgive past unbelief. — Al-Razi says forgiveness follows belief and obedience. Ibn ʿAshur says the closing statement explains the invitation: people should remember and fear God, citing 33:37. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:55/q10 What does the passage imply about the Qur'an's continuing role?  [* · interpretation]
- 74:55/q10/p1 The Qur'an is God's sufficient warning; no later book will be sent, and it suffices for happiness here and in the afterlife. — Kur'an Yolu reads these points from the verses. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 74:55/q11 What readings are reported for the remembrance verb in the following verse?  [* · readings]
turns on: The initial consonant (tāʾ or yāʾ) and whether the form is light or doubled by assimilation.
- 74:55/q11/p1 It is read with tāʾ: tadhakkarūn. — Ibn ʿAtiyya reports this from Nāfiʿ, the people of Medina, Sallām and Yaʿqūb. Al-Razi also reports a tāʾ form and says both lighter and doubled forms occur. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع وأهل المدينة وسلام ويعقوب); RAZI-FULL]
- 74:55/q11/p2 It is read with yāʾ: yadhakkarūn. — Ibn ʿAtiyya attributes it to Abū Jaʿfar, ʿĀṣim, Abū ʿAmr, al-Aʿmash, Ṭalḥa, Ibn Kathīr, ʿĪsā and al-Aʿraj. Al-Razi reports yāʾ and both lighter and doubled forms. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو جعفر وعاصم وأبو عمرو والأعمش وطلحة وابن كثير وعيسى والأعرج); RAZI-FULL]
- 74:55/q11/p3 Abū Jaʿfar is also reported with tāʾ and a doubled dhāl, assimilating an underlying tatadhakkarūn. — Ibn ʿAtiyya gives the attribution and explains the assimilation. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو جعفر وعاصم وأبو عمرو والأعمش وطلحة وابن كثير وعيسى والأعرج)]

## 74:55/q12 Where may reciters pause in this passage?  [* · grammar]
- 74:55/q12/p1 A stop may occur at either kallā, at munashshara, and at al-ākhira when the latter is taken in the sense of illā. — Al-Alusi states these pause locations and the condition for the last one. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 74:55/q13 What merit is reported for reciting Sūrat al-Muddaththir?  [* · reports]
- 74:55/q13/p1 A report promises ten good deeds for each Meccan believer in Muhammad and each Meccan disbeliever to one who recites the sūra. — Al-Zamakhshari attributes the report to the Prophet and supplies no grading. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله)]
