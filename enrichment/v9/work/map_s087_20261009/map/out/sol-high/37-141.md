# 37:141: 19 questions

## 37:141/q01 What does sāhama mean?  [فَسَاهَمَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the act is described as a drawing of lots or in the language of gambling.
- 37:141/q01/p1 Jonah participated in drawing lots. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Mubarrad, al-Nahhas's Muhammad ibn Yazid, al-Baghawi, al-Tabrisi, al-Samin, al-Bursevi, and Ibn Ashur derive the term from the sahm, an arrow used for lots; Ibn Ashur calls these arrows azlam. Mujahid's report specifies an arrow that fell on Jonah. Al-Samin describes the winning arrow as that which falls to the successful contestant. Al-Biqai says this followed seafarers' custom. [30 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي); NAHHAS(محمد بن يزيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس والسدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد,المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن، ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(المبرد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(البغوي); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 37:141/q01/p2 It describes gambling, with Jonah losing the gamble. — Ibn Uyayna gives both the gambling sense of sahama and the corresponding sense of al-mudhadin as losers. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(ابن عيينة)]

## 37:141/q02 Who drew lots with whom?  [فَسَاهَمَ · grammar]
- 37:141/q02/p1 Jonah joined the people aboard the ship in the draw. — Al-Bursevi and Kashifi explicitly make Jonah the subject; al-Baydawi says the draw was among the passengers. Nasafi allows one or three acts of drawing. Asad calls the other participants mariners. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TABRISI(الحسن، ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 37:141/q03 Why did the people aboard draw lots?  [فَسَاهَمَ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ over whether the ship stalled, a storm exposed a hidden offender, or its occupants sought to reduce the load by sacrificing one person.
- 37:141/q03/p1 A stalled ship led them to seek the guilty, fugitive, or ill-omened passenger. — Yahya, Qatada, Said ibn Jubayr, Wahb, Ibn Atiyya, Tawus, and others say the vessel would not move; Ibn Atiyya says other ships sailed on. Al-Bursevi says there was no wind or apparent cause, while merchants cited prior experience. The suspected fault is variously called wrongdoing, a grave sin, an escaped servant, an ill-omened man, or a criminal. Ibn Jawzi's Ibn Masud report says Jonah's people had repented after a three-day warning. The biblical Book of Jonah says the lot identified the cause of a calamity. [17 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(سعيد بن جير,وهب); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس ووهب); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(طاووس,عبد الله بن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري); BAYDAWI-FULL(روي); BURSEVI(الإمام); DURR-FULL(طاوس); ALUSI-FULL(رُوي); IBNASHUR-FULL(كتاب يونان); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 37:141/q03/p2 A storm or threatened sinking prompted a draw to identify its cause. — Al-Tabari's account reports a violent storm and suspected passenger; the Biblical narratives related by al-Biqai, Elmali, Tabatabai, Asad, and Study Quran likewise make the storm the occasion, with Jonah's flight identified by lot. Tabatabai says sailors first threw cargo overboard and Jonah was woken from sleep. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري); BIQAI-FULL(مترجمه); ELMALILI(Âlûsî); TABATABAI(الآلوسي); ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(the Biblical book of Jonah)]
- 37:141/q03/p3 Facing sinking, they drew lots to sacrifice one passenger and save the rest or lighten the vessel. — A report in al-Tabrisi explicitly gives the belief that throwing one overboard saves the rest. Ibn Kathir says waves threatened the ship and the passenger was to lighten it; al-Bursevi reports the merchants' calculation that one loss was better than all. Tabatabai says the lot selected someone for a great fish, allowing the ship to escape. Ibn Ashur says ancient seafarers used lots when passengers or cargo overloaded a ship. [5 notes: TABRISI(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 37:141/q04 What did Jonah and the sailors do before the draw?  [فَسَاهَمَ · reports]
turns on: Reports differ on Jonah's confession, prayer during the storm, and attempted jump before the lot.
- 37:141/q04/p1 Jonah identified himself as the fugitive or sinner and proposed that he be thrown overboard. — Muqatil says the sailors refused because Jonah was God's messenger. Yahya says they refused and proposed a lot; Wahb calls him the fugitive servant; Said ibn Jubayr records his confession of a grave sin. The account transmitted by al-Tabari adds a storm and a nighttime leap after three losing lots. [7 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT(سعيد بن جير,وهب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عبد الله بن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري); BAYDAWI-FULL(روي)]
- 37:141/q04/p2 The crew woke Jonah to pray during the wind, which stopped and then returned. — Yahya says Jonah then recognized his own sin as the cause. Another account in al-Qurtubi says he had slept covered through two near-sinkings, and his prayer calmed the wind after he was woken. [2 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 37:141/q04/p3 Jonah wrapped himself in his cloak and tried to jump when the ship stalled, but the crew stopped him and chose lots. — Yahya reports this as another account. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(بعضهم)]

## 37:141/q05 How many times was the lot drawn for Jonah?  [فَسَاهَمَ · reports]
turns on: Nasafi explicitly allows one draw or three; several narrative reports specify three.
- 37:141/q05/p1 It fell on Jonah three times. — Muqatil says names were drawn in water; Yahya says Jonah protested that he had warned them and they kept drawing. Ibn Masud's report says a sea creature then appeared on every side of the ship. Ibn Abbas and Wahb, Tawus, Ibn Jawzi's Ibn Masud account, the merchants in al-Bursevi, and al-Alusi's report also give three. Nasafi presents this as one of two possibilities. [14 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس ووهب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(طاووس,عبد الله بن مسعود); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); DURR-FULL(طاوس); ALUSI-FULL(رُوي); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس ووهب بن منبه)]
- 37:141/q05/p2 The draw may have occurred once. — Nasafi explicitly leaves open one time or three; several accounts give a single selection without specifying repeated draws. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 37:141/q06 What does al-mudḥaḍīn mean here?  [ٱلْمُدْحَضِينَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the participle describes the loser selected by lot, anyone assigned a lot, or the related idiom of a defeated argument.
- 37:141/q06/p1 Those defeated in the drawing of lots: Jonah's lot selected him as the loser. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Tabari, al-Farra, al-Jishumi, al-Tabrisi, al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, al-Biqai, al-Bursevi, and al-Alusi connect defeat with slipping or falling from victory; the root also describes an invalidated argument. Al-Tabari notes that the idiom of God making a proof slip is uncommon. Abu Qays's poem supports 'defeated'; Dhu al-Isba's verse, an unnamed camel poem, and al-Qurtubi's anonymous poetic line illustrate slippery ground or the vanquished. Ibn Faris and al-Burhan say the root usually denotes falsehood, while this verse uses defeat. Some glosses stress selection by lot or expulsion from the ship; Elmali says consent remains possible. Al-Suddī's and Ibn Abbas's reports use selection; Ibn Uyayna calls it losing the gamble. [35 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(ابن عيينة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي); NAHHAS(الفراء); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد,سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن، ابن عباس,مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء,المبرد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(ابن فارس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 37:141/q06/p2 Those who participated in or were assigned a lot. — Mujahid is reported as calling them those assigned a lot and elsewhere those who drew lots; the emphasis is on participation or assignment rather than defeat. [2 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 37:141/q06/p3 A refuted or invalidated argument. — Al-Suddi derives this gloss from the usage of making an argument fail. The cited root explanations use adḥaḍa ḥujjatahu; Ibn Qutayba calls the underlying sense slipping, and al-Tabrisi cites a poem about a camel swerving from a slippery place. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(السدي)]

## 37:141/q07 What is the root image behind al-mudḥaḍīn?  [ٱلْمُدْحَضِينَ · meaning]
- 37:141/q07/p1 Slipping or falling on wet, muddy, or otherwise slippery ground. — Abu Ubayda cites Dhu al-Isba's poem about daḥḍ ground and the idiom adḥaḍa ḥujjatahu. Al-Tabari describes slipping in water or mud and says the idiom involving God's invalidation of a proof is uncommon. Ibn Qutayba, al-Jishumi, al-Tabrisi, Ibn Jawzi's report of Ibn Qutayba, al-Farra, and al-Bursevi use the failing argument analogy; al-Tabrisi adds an unnamed poet's camel line. Al-Baydawi and Abu Hayyan describe losing a place of victory. Al-Burhan and Ibn Faris note that falsehood is the usual sense elsewhere. [18 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة); JISHUMI; TABRISI(شاعر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(ابن فارس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 37:141/q08 How did Jonah enter the sea after the lot?  [فَكَانَ مِنَ ٱلْمُدْحَضِينَ · reports]
turns on: Accounts differ over whether Jonah leapt voluntarily or the sailors cast him out; Elmali argues the wording favors the latter.
- 37:141/q08/p1 He jumped or threw himself into the water. — Qatada, Wahb, Ibn Abbas and Wahb in al-Baghawi, Ibn Masud, al-Tabari's account, Tawus, and some later reports make Jonah act himself; several add that the crew hesitated or resisted and he saw a waiting whale on each side. Ibn Kathir says he stripped to jump. Qushayri says two whales waited, one on either side. Study Quran reports he leapt into the fish's mouth when it rose by God's command. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(وهب); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس ووهب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري); BAYDAWI-FULL(روي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(طاوس); STUDYQURAN(IK); ELMALILI-]
- 37:141/q08/p2 The sailors cast or threw him overboard after the lot. — Muqatil, Said ibn Jubayr, al-Tabrisi, Elmali, Tabatabai, Islahi, and the Biblical accounts say the crew did this. Hamid ibn Hilal says Jonah requested to be tipped feet first. Elmali prefers this reading of the wording, while allowing Jonah may have consented. The Biblical account in Elmali says sailors first tried to reach shore. [13 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(سعيد بن جير); TABRISI(قيل); DURR-FULL(حميد بن هلال); ELMALILI(Âlûsî); TABATABAI(الآلوسي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Kitâb-ı Mukaddes); ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(the Biblical book of Jonah); ELMALILI+]

## 37:141/q09 What did the whale do when Jonah entered the sea?  [فَكَانَ مِنَ ٱلْمُدْحَضِينَ · reports]
turns on: The reports give different details of the whale's approach, divine instructions, and treatment of Jonah.
- 37:141/q09/p1 A waiting whale swallowed him under God's command without harming him. — Muqatil says God instructed it only when Jonah was two spans from the water: its belly became a worship space, with no injury to hair or skin and no food or drink. Said ibn Jubayr says it waited open-mouthed. A Prophet report through Abu Hurayra says God forbade flesh or bone injury; Jonah heard sea creatures glorifying God, and angels interceded. Al-Qurtubi says the belly was a container, not food. Qushayri and Ibn Masud's reports describe a creature stationed at the ship, while Hamid ibn Hilal calls it pale-mouthed and says a larger whale followed. [10 notes: MUQATIL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(سعيد بن جبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(حميد بن هلال); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 37:141/q09/p2 The whale swam beside the ship with Jonah breathing and praising from its raised head, then expelled him unharmed ashore. — Al-Qurtubi quotes this report through al-Zamakhshari. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري)]

## 37:141/q10 How long did Jonah remain in the fish?  [* · reports]
turns on: Narratives assign distinct durations; the notes do not decide between them.
- 37:141/q10/p1 Three days and nights. — Al-Biqai quotes the translator's Book of Jonah; Elmali's account from the People of the Book also gives three days and nights; Study Quran lists three days among the reported durations. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL(مترجمه); ELMALILI(Âlûsî); STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 37:141/q10/p2 Seven days. — Study Quran lists this among the variant reports. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 37:141/q10/p3 Forty nights. — Al-Qurtubi's continuation says Jonah prayed after forty nights and was rescued; Study Quran lists forty days among accounts. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 37:141/q10/p4 From morning until that evening. — Study Quran reports swallowing in the morning and expulsion the same evening. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(IK)]

## 37:141/q11 What happened after Jonah was cast into the sea?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports emphasize distinct outcomes and differ over his condition on reaching shore.
- 37:141/q11/p1 The sea calmed after he went overboard. — The accounts of the People of the Book transmitted by Elmali, Tabatabai, Asad, and Study Quran say this happened; Tabatabai also says the ship escaped. [5 notes: ELMALILI(Âlûsî); TABATABAI(الآلوسي); ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(the Biblical book of Jonah)]
- 37:141/q11/p2 The sailors believed. — Al-Mawardi states this consequence of Jonah's being thrown into the sea. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 37:141/q11/p3 Jonah prayed in the whale and was cast ashore sick or unharmed. — The Abu Hurayra report says angels interceded before he was cast ashore sick; al-Zamakhshari's report says expelled unharmed. Al-Biqai's and Elmali's versions say he prayed in the whale, and the latter has him expelled after three days. The Turkish Quran Yolu account says he was later returned ashore and recommissioned. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة,الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL(مترجمه); ELMALILI(Âlûsî); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Kitâb-ı Mukaddes)]

## 37:141/q12 What earlier events led Jonah to board the ship?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports give different precipitating events and levels of detail for his departure.
- 37:141/q12/p1 He left after warning his people, whose repentance or delayed punishment averted the warning. — Ibn Abbas and Wahb say he left when punishment was delayed; Ibn Masud's report says his people repented after a three-day warning. Al-Alusi's report says Jonah left before permission and was ashamed to return after the punishment was averted. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس ووهب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عبد الله بن مسعود); ALUSI-FULL(رُوي)]
- 37:141/q12/p2 After waves separated him from his wife and sons and a wolf took a son, he boarded a different ship. — Al-Baghawi presents this as another story. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل)]
- 37:141/q12/p3 He fled the divine commission to Nineveh by taking ship toward Tarshish. — The Book of Jonah as summarized by al-Biqai and reported through al-Alusi by Elmali and Tabatabai gives the Nineveh mission and flight; Elmali names Jaffa as the departure port and says Jonah was overwhelmed by Nineveh's wickedness. Asad and Study Quran report flight from God. Quran Yolu says the fuller Biblical account broadly matches the Quranic account. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL(مترجمه); ELMALILI(Âlûsî); TABATABAI(الآلوسي); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Kitâb-ı Mukaddes); STUDYQURAN(the Biblical book of Jonah)]

## 37:141/q13 Why was Jonah considered for the lot?  [فَسَاهَمَ · interpretation]
- 37:141/q13/p1 The passengers may have chosen him because they regarded him as especially respectable. — Islahi notes the irony of the respected passenger's own selection. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 37:141/q14 What events does the wording leave unstated?  [فَسَاهَمَ · rhetoric]
- 37:141/q14/p1 It omits the ship's crisis between Jonah's boarding and the lot. — Ibn Atiyya says the narrative is compressed and supplies the report that Jonah's ship stalled while others moved. Ibn Ashur takes the consequence marker to imply a rough sea and fear of drowning; he compares the ellipsis with Moses striking the sea and its splitting in 26:63. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 37:141/q15 When and for what may drawing lots settle a matter?  [فَسَاهَمَ · law]
turns on: The issue is the permitted scope of a lot: fair allocation of an uncertain entitlement, every ambiguity, or selecting a person for lethal sacrifice.
- 37:141/q15/p1 Drawing lots is lawful for uncertain allocations. — Ibn al-Arabi says lots existed in earlier law and Islamic law, citing 3:44; the Prophet drew among wives for travel, among six slaves for deathbed manumission, and directed inheritance disputants to draw. Two of the six slaves matched the one-third limit; subjective preference was unlawful. Lots prevent favoritism among wives and determine recipients of disputed objects. Al-Qurtubi prefers drawing among wives for campaigns because taking all is impossible and choosing one favors her; he says jurists across regions agree. Al-Alusi and jurists he reports cite Jonah's story; al-Sadiq says entrusting the draw to God yields the true, fairest lot. [9 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); ALUSI-FULL(من قال بمشروعية القرعة); TABATABAI(الصادق); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 37:141/q15/p2 Lots should govern every ambiguous case, including which wife is divorced. — Ibn al-Arabi argues lots most clearly resolve uncertainty and settle judgment; divorce among wives follows by analogy with selecting enslaved women for manumission. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)+]
- 37:141/q15/p3 Lots may not choose a person to be thrown into the sea, even to lighten a ship. — Al-Qurtubi treats Jonah's case as a unique preliminary to manifest proof and strengthen faith, not precedent for killing or throwing a sinner into fire or water. He says discarding people does not relieve a storm-tossed vessel and the passengers should bear God's decree. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 37:141/q16 What does Jonah's plight show about God's location?  [* · theology]
- 37:141/q16/p1 God is in no spatial direction and is no nearer to Muhammad in the ascent than to Jonah in the whale's deep sea darkness. — Ibn al-Arabi recounts al-Juwayni delaying his answer to a question about God's direction until a guest's debt was paid; al-Juwayni cites the Prophet's saying not to prefer him over Jonah ibn Matta and compares the ascent with Jonah's predicament, with 21:87 mentioned. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(إمام الحرمين الجويني,ابن العربي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 37:141/q17 How does this episode fit the surrounding prophetic narratives?  [* · links]
- 37:141/q17/p1 The prophets' stories illustrate divine peace and mercy, support for their missions, and reward for good deeds. — Islahi says the shared refrain sums up the stories. He observes that the prophets are listed chronologically through Aaron, then arranged by their attributes. Quran Yolu says these verses give the instructive lesson of Jonah's experience rather than bare history. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 37:141/q18 What other method for choosing people to throw overboard is reported?  [* · reports]
- 37:141/q18/p1 A leader counted passengers and cast out every ninth person. — Al-Safadi recounts a sinking ship with Muslims and non-Muslims. To avoid a dangerous direct lot, the leader counted every ninth passenger until the non-Muslims were gone; his diagram marks Muslims in red and starts counting from them toward the north. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الصفدي)]

## 37:141/q19 Where else is Jonah's story told in the Quran?  [* · links]
