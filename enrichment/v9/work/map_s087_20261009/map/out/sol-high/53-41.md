# 53:41: 15 questions

## 53:41/q01 What does thumma indicate here?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether thumma orders events in time or ranks the recompense above the preceding display.
- 53:41/q01/p1 It orders disclosure of a person's deeds before recompense. — Jishumī reports that people first learn their deeds, then receive recompense; this follows thumma's sequencing force. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 53:41/q01/p2 It marks a rise in importance from seeing the deed to its recompense, rather than elapsed time. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says payment matters more than showing the effort or its recipient. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:41/q02 What does yujzāhu mean, and what does its object pronoun refer to?  [يُجْزَىٰهُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the object is the previously mentioned striving or an anticipated recompense, and whether the latter construction is grammatically admissible.
- 53:41/q02/p1 The person is recompensed for his striving; the object pronoun refers to that striving. — Ibn Qutayba glosses the verb as being recompensed for it. Al-Wāḥidī cites a poem for recompensing effort. Al-Akhfash, as transmitted by al-Thaʿlabī and al-Qurṭubī, and al-Baghawī and Jishumī cite poetry using both jazaytuhu al-jazāʾ and jazaytuhu bi-l-jazāʾ. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī (citing 76:12), al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr explain direct attachment through omission of a preposition; al-Ālūsī also records recompense by, for, or directly with the work. Al-Samīn prefers this referent, with the human as passive subject. Al-Ṭabrisī says the effort itself is repaid on Judgment Day. Naḥḥās relates the pronoun to the effort and to God's fulfillment of promise and warning. [19 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; NAHHAS; THALABI(الأخفش); WAHIDI-BASIT(الشاعر); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش,الشاعر); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 53:41/q02/p2 The object pronoun anticipates the recompense named by al-jazāʾ al-awfā. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī give this alternative, taking the following phrase as explanation or apposition. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī compare 21:3; al-Samīn transmits that comparison. Al-Samīn also offers ʿaṭf bayān or an omitted aʿnī to explain the noun. The Shaykh objects that an explanatory accusative needs a governor and replacement of a pronoun by an explicit noun is disputed; al-Ālūsī reports Abū Ḥayyān's similar objection and says the correct view forbids that replacement. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-]

## 53:41/q03 What does al-jazāʾ mean?  [ٱلْجَزَآءَ · meaning]
- 53:41/q03/p1 It means recompense or reward, synonymous here with ajr. — Jishumī explicitly equates jazāʾ and ajr; he cites a poetic line showing recompense expressed with or without bi. [2 notes: JISHUMI]

## 53:41/q04 How is al-jazāʾ al-awfā parsed?  [ٱلْجَزَآءَ ٱلْأَوْفَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the accusative names the action's kind, fills an object slot, or explains the pronoun; the explanatory analyses face objections about grammatical government and substitution.
- 53:41/q04/p1 It explains or replaces the pronoun referring to recompense, as an appositive or explanatory noun. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and al-Samīn compare the construction in 21:3; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī also report it. The Shaykh objects that an explanatory accusative needs a governor and that replacing a pronoun with an explicit noun is disputed and disallowed in his view. Al-Ālūsī reports Abū Ḥayyān's objection and agrees that such replacement is forbidden. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-]
- 53:41/q04/p2 It is an accusative cognate or absolute object specifying the kind of recompensing. — Naḥḥās calls it a verbal noun; al-Bayḍāwī allows that analysis. Al-Samīn prefers this parse, with the human as passive subject and striving as object, and says al-awfā may describe the act metaphorically because even the reward bears the quality by association; only the rewarded person literally bears it. Al-Bursevī and Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly identify a kind-specifying cognate or absolute object. Al-Ālūsī says association with the complete reward licenses applying the adjective to the event. [7 notes: NAHHAS; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 53:41/q04/p3 Al-jazāʾ is the verb's second object, after its attached pronoun as first object. — Jishumī states this accusative analysis. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 53:41/q04/p4 Al-jazāʾ is an additional object denoting the thing given in recompense. — Abū al-Baqāʾ, reported by al-Samīn, argues that al-awfā describes the recompense rather than the act, so the noun is an object. Al-Ālūsī reports the resulting three-object analysis. Al-Samīn objects that the passive subject and pronoun already fill two roles, so a third object strains both syntax and meaning; he rejects the objection to describing the act as al-awfā. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 53:41/q04/p5 Al-jazāʾ is an object licensed by an omitted preposition. — Al-Ālūsī records this alternative to making it a further direct object. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 53:41/q04/p6 Al-jazāʾ is ʿaṭf bayān, an explanatory adjunct to the pronoun. — Al-Samīn answers the Shaykh's grammatical objection by calling ʿaṭf bayān an ordinary explanatory construction. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 53:41/q04/p7 Al-jazāʾ is an explanatory accusative governed by an omitted aʿnī, ‘I mean.’ — Al-Samīn gives this as another regular answer to the objection that an explanatory accusative lacks a governor. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 53:41/q04/p8 Al-jazāʾ replaces the pronoun as badal, avoiding an extra object. — Al-Samīn offers this way to rescue Abū al-Baqāʾ's object analysis, but calls it remote from Abū al-Baqāʾ's intent and obscure. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 53:41/q05 What makes the recompense al-awfā, and what is it?  [ٱلْجَزَآءَ ٱلْأَوْفَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether fullness describes the fitting payment itself, its unending duration, or a specific supreme reward.
- 53:41/q05/p1 It is the complete, fully fitting recompense for one's deeds, including good and evil. — Al-Wāḥidī calls it most complete and perfect and cites Abū Isḥāq's gloss of fully rewarding the deed; Ibn al-Jawzī and Ibn Kathīr gloss fullness or abundance. Ṭabāṭabāʾ derives wafā from a thing fulfilling all required qualities. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the comparative intensifies fullness rather than comparing rewards, so recompense matches good and evil deeds; he cites 4:173, 11:109, 24:39 and 17:63. Al-Rāzī says it suits righteous believers, yet Hell can also be called plentiful recompense in 17:63 because its harm exceeds the benefit of sins. Al-Biqāʿī says good is multiplied, evil repaid equivalently for whom God wills, and much is forgiven. The Study Quran transmits al-Ṭabarī's explanation that it is the full reward God promised. Naḥḥās connects its fullness to God's fulfillment of promise and warning. [19 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 53:41/q05/p2 For obedient or spiritually striving people, its fullness includes lasting reward in the everlasting abode, without interruption or withholding. — Jishumī calls the enduring reward of the eternal abode the reason it is complete. Al-Ṭabrisī specifies lasting reward for obedience. Al-Qushayrī calls it the greatest, most exalted reward that is neither cut off nor withheld. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; QUSHAYRI]
- 53:41/q05/p3 The fullest reward is the vision of God. — The Study Quran reports this interpretation from Rāzī. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 53:41/q06 How is al-awfā read in the reported recitation practice?  [ٱلْأَوْفَىٰ · readings]
- 53:41/q06/p1 Abū ʿAmr opens the listed non-final words on routes that incline verse endings. — Ibn al-Jazarī adds that for Mūsā Abū ʿAmr follows his usual between-vowels reading where imāla is transmitted; the note does not state a distinct pronunciation of al-awfā itself. [1 notes: NASHR(أبو عمرو)]

## 53:41/q07 Whose striving determines a person's recompense?  [* · interpretation]
- 53:41/q07/p1 Each person receives recompense for their own deeds; another person's promise or lineage cannot replace that account. — Al-Ṭabarī says no one is rewarded or punished for another's deeds and applies the rule to an apostate who relied on another person's promise to bear punishment. Al-Iṣlāḥī challenges those who assume descent from Abraham and Moses entitles them to Paradise, directing attention to those prophets' teachings and sacrifices. Elmalılı says one's future is the product of one's striving. Muqātil, Ibn Kathīr and the Turkish commentary speak of full recompense for each person's work. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 53:41/q08 How does recompense relate to the preceding disclosure of deeds?  [* · links]
- 53:41/q08/p1 Seeing or learning one's deeds leads to their full recompense on Judgment Day. — Al-Ṭabarī says each worker sees the work through its good or bad recompense. Al-Ṭabrisī says the servant sees the effort on Judgment Day and receives full recompense. Ibn Kathīr cites 9:105: God informs people of deeds and repays good with good and evil with evil. Jishumī reports a view that they first learn their deeds and are then recompensed. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this clause the point of the preceding statement about seeing effort. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:41/q09 What promise and warning does the verse convey?  [* · interpretation]
- 53:41/q09/p1 Full payment promises good to believers and warns that evil will be accounted for. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it a threat to unbelievers and promise to believers. Al-Iṣlāḥī says even tiny good deeds count and no small evil is ignored. Ibn Kathīr says good is repaid with good and evil with evil. Al-Biqāʿī adds that good is multiplied, evil paid equivalently for whom God wills, and God forgives much; he calls the warning a reminder. Naḥḥās links complete payment to fulfillment of divine promise and warning. [5 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:41/q10 What inward forms of striving and recompense are described?  [* · inward]
turns on: The notes distinguish kinds and stages of spiritual striving, with a distinct recompense for each.
- 53:41/q10/p1 Different aims of striving lead respectively to loss, profit, God's approval, or divine thanks and guidance. — Al-Qushayrī distinguishes worldly pursuit, pursuit of Paradise, disciplining the self, and spiritual aspiration in that order. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 53:41/q10/p2 A repentant sinner's striving for forgiveness receives reward, nearness, honor, and intimacy. — Al-Qushayrī describes the sinner as regretting the darkening of his record. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 53:41/q10/p3 Constant attention to God and one's shortcomings is thanked here and hereafter. — Al-Qushayrī describes one who counts his breaths with God, overlooks no command, and is directly addressed as forgiven and thanked. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 53:41/q10/p4 At the end of the spiritual path, annihilation receives witnessing and subsistence receives bodily nurture. — Al-Bursevī reports al-Warrāq's beginning, middle and end of striving; the final nurture includes lawful provisions deferred during the path. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الوراق)]

## 53:41/q11 What occasion is reported for this passage?  [* · reports]
- 53:41/q11/p1 It was revealed after a man unable to obtain a mount from the Prophet was offered transport by someone else to catch the army. — Al-Suyūṭī transmits this from Darrāj Abū al-Samḥ. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(دراج أبو السمح)]

## 53:41/q12 What did the Prophet reportedly do after reciting these verses?  [* · reports]
- 53:41/q12/p1 He said the istirjāʿ formula and became subdued. — Al-Suyūṭī transmits the report from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 53:41/q13 Is this verse within a reported exception to al-Najm's Meccan classification?  [* · sciences]
- 53:41/q13/p1 One reported view excepts the nine verses beginning at 53:33 from the sūra's Meccan classification. — Al-Suyūṭī records this alternative and mentions 53:41 within the passage. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 53:41/q14 How does this passage relate to the scriptures of Moses and Abraham?  [* · links]
- 53:41/q14/p1 It states enduring truths associated with their scriptures, calling readers to their teachings and sacrifices rather than descent from them. — The Study Quran places this third section after reflection on human creation and says it turns to perennial truths in the scriptures of Moses and Abraham. Al-Iṣlāḥī uses their teachings and sacrifices to answer claims that ancestry guarantees Paradise. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:41/q15 What other scriptural prayer echoes the promised final recompense?  [* · links]
- 53:41/q15/p1 Abraham's prayer not to be disgraced when people are raised echoes it. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 26:87. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
