# 17:18: 24 questions

## 17:18/q01 Who is the seeker of the immediate life?  [مَّن كَانَ يُرِيدُ ٱلْعَاجِلَةَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the warning is restricted to unbelief and a sole life goal, or extends to people doing ostensibly religious deeds for worldly ends; everlasting Hell and the contrast with 17:19 are cited.
- 17:18/q01/p1 One devoted to this world alone, denying or neglecting the Hereafter; the warning especially concerns unbelievers. — Yaḥyā identifies an idolater without belief in the Hereafter. Ṭabarī describes no belief in resurrection or hope of divine reward or punishment. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ibn Jarīr restrict the warning to unbelievers. Abū Ḥayyān infers the omitted contrast from 17:19. Al-Ālūsī argues everlasting Hell requires an unbeliever, although banishment can describe a believer's time in Hell. Ibn ʿĀshūr says worldly desire rests on seeing no life after death. Ṭabāṭabāʾī allows denial in deeds despite verbal acknowledgment. [20 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(قيل); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,الضحاك); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 17:18/q01/p2 The seeker includes anyone who directs deeds or apparent devotion toward worldly gain, including hypocrites, show-offs, sinners, migrants, and fighters seeking spoils or fame. — Al-Kashshāf distinguishes ordinary worldly pursuit from afterlife deeds done for worldly ends; its migration report ties recompense to the migrant's aim. Abū Ḥayyān reports both migration and the warning that afterlife deeds sought for the world yield no afterlife share. Al-Wāḥidī applies the rebuke to worship or Islam for gain. Al-Ālūsī objects that sinful believers cannot thereby be assigned everlasting Hell. Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that importing the deeds restriction from Hūd 11:15–16 confuses recompense for acts with ultimate life goals. [18 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قيل); SAMIN-DURR(قيل); DURR-FULL(الضحاك); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 17:18/q01/p3 The verse classifies a person's ultimate life goal; worldly work can serve the Hereafter and religious acts can serve the world. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the world can be a route to afterlife happiness and notes degrees of attachment and denial; he rejects making the verse a rule for each separate act. Kur'an Yolu likewise distinguishes aim from the kind of work. [4 notes: TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI+]

## 17:18/q02 What does ٱلْعَاجِلَةَ denote?  [ٱلْعَاجِلَةَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the adjective names worldly life itself or also the goods and aims sought within it.
- 17:18/q02/p1 The present, short-lived worldly life, contrasted with the delayed Hereafter. — Ibn Zayd and the commentators gloss it as this world. Al-Thaʿlabī and Ibn ʿĀshūr supply the understood 'abode' or 'life'; al-Ṭabrisī explains its name by transience. Al-Ālūsī cites 11:15 for the narrow life sense but prefers the wider sense. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 17:18/q02/p2 The world together with its immediate aims, gains, pleasure, or adornment. — Al-Ālūsī prefers this breadth, citing 42:20, 11:15, and 3:145. Islahi includes immediate gains. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports transient goods or blessings as alternative glosses and explains pleasure sought as an independent end. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 17:18/q03 What noun is understood with the adjective ٱلْعَاجِلَةَ?  [ٱلْعَاجِلَةَ · grammar]
- 17:18/q03/p1 An omitted noun such as the immediate abode or worldly life. — Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Qurṭubī supply 'abode'; Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī favor 'life' from the contrast with the Hereafter, and Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 11:15. [5 notes: THALABI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 17:18/q04 What force do كَانَ and the present يُرِيدُ have here?  [كَانَ يُرِيدُ · rhetoric]
- 17:18/q04/p1 They portray continuing or renewed desire for this world. — Al-Ālūsī says kāna adds continuing or exclusive desire and rejects a merely inward resolve or an equally mixed desire. Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes persistent devotion and cites 53:29–30. Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts recurring worldly desire in the present with settled afterlife desire in the past and the omitted kāna; al-Sāmarrāʾī compares repeated and nonrepeated conditions at 31:12 and 4:92–93. [4 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 17:18/q05 What worldly thing is hastened, and how much is received?  [عَجَّلْنَا لَهُۥ فِيهَا مَا نَشَآءُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'what We will' specifies God's selected amount alone or may identify the servant's desired object under divine permission.
- 17:18/q05/p1 God grants only a chosen share of worldly provision or enjoyment, sometimes plenty, little, hardship, or none. — Muqātil names wealth. Al-Farrāʾ names provision. Ṭabarī includes ease, hardship, and chastisement; al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, and Ibn al-Jawzī include expansion and restriction, with al-Baghawī also naming destruction. Al-Kashshāf notes some obtain less than sought and some nothing; al-Biqāʿī and al-Bursawī describe loss in both worlds. Al-Bayḍāwī says achievement and concern are gifts; al-Bursawī invokes wisdom. Islahi connects selective gifts with divine respite. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says causal conditions limit the grant, citing 43:33–35 to show wealth has no afterlife worth. [41 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 17:18/q05/p2 The hastened thing is the particular worldly thing the servant wants, insofar as God wills to give it. — Al-Zajjāj allows an implied servant as the subject of 'wishes,' conditional on God's will; al-Kashshāf likewise says the servant's wish may name the thing granted while God determines its recipient. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 17:18/q06 How is نَشَآءُ read, and whose wish can it express?  [نَشَآءُ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted nūn/yāʾ reading and whether the third-person subject is God or the recipient.
- 17:18/q06/p1 The majority read nashāʾ, 'We will,' making God the speaker and giver. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān identify the majority reading. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says its object can be what God or the seeker wishes, while 'for whom We will' qualifies either. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 17:18/q06/p2 Nāfiʿ also transmits yashāʾ, 'he wills,' with the pronoun referring to God. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Bayḍāwī say its sense then matches the common reading; Abū Ḥayyān describes a shift in person. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(نافع)]
- 17:18/q06/p3 The variant yashāʾ can instead make the seeker or recipient the one who wishes. — Al-Zajjāj reports the servant interpretation of the implied subject. Al-Kashshāf says the desired worldly object may be granted to one selected by God; Abū Ḥayyān cautions this is not universal. Al-Ālūsī suggests successful recipients may be enticed by the grant. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(نافع)]

## 17:18/q07 Who is meant by لِمَن نُّرِيدُ?  [لِمَن نُّرِيدُ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'intend' simply selects a beneficiary or implies that person's destruction; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī say the wording does not bear the latter.
- 17:18/q07/p1 Only those worldly seekers whom God chooses to receive a worldly grant. — Al-Bayḍāwī calls this a restriction to a person God specifically grants. Al-Kashshāf, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, and Abū Ḥayyān stress that choosing the recipient is a second limit, so seekers may receive nothing. Ibn al-Jawzī reports 'one for whom God intends to hasten something.' [20 notes: FARRA; MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 17:18/q07/p2 Those whose destruction God intends. — Abū Isḥāq al-Fazārī is reported by Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, and Ibn al-Jawzī. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī reject this because the wording does not support it. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو إسحاق الفزاري); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو إسحاق الفزاري); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو إسحاق الفزاري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو إسحاق الفزاري)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو إسحاق الفزاري)-]

## 17:18/q08 How does لِمَن نُّرِيدُ attach to the preceding لَهُۥ?  [لَهُۥ لِمَن نُّرِيدُ · grammar]
- 17:18/q08/p1 It is a partitive apposition or replacement of the preceding recipient, repeating the preposition because the first pronoun denotes a collective class. — Al-Kashshāf calls it an appositional subset; al-Ṭabrisī says the pronoun repeats because the apposition forms another clause. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī identify the earlier 'to him' or its pronoun; al-Samīn states the governor repeats; al-Bursawī says only some of the class receive the gift. Al-Ālūsī allows replacement of the phrase or its pronoun. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 17:18/q09 What is the syntax of the conditional opening?  [مَّن كَانَ يُرِيدُ عَجَّلْنَا · grammar]
- 17:18/q09/p1 مَن is conditional, and عَجَّلْنَا is its response, with مَا as the object. — Al-Samīn also parses لِمَن نُرِيدُ as a partitive apposition with its governing preposition repeated. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 17:18/q10 What happens to the worldly seeker after the worldly grant?  [ثُمَّ جَعَلْنَا لَهُۥ جَهَنَّمَ يَصْلَىٰهَا · interpretation]
- 17:18/q10/p1 God assigns the seeker Hell, where the person enters or suffers it in disgrace and expulsion. — Ṭabarī places this at resurrection and connects blame with ingratitude and misconduct toward favors. Ibn Kathīr describes Hell enveloping the person on all sides; al-Rāzī describes lasting harm with humiliation and no escape. Al-Ālūsī reports his definition and infers everlasting Hell for the unbeliever. Asad notes the literal force of assigning Hell. Al-Bursawī says Hell replaces the hoped-for accelerated gift. [28 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الرازي); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]

## 17:18/q11 Why is Hell assigned despite a worldly grant?  [ثُمَّ جَعَلْنَا لَهُۥ جَهَنَّمَ · interpretation]
- 17:18/q11/p1 Worldly gain is no sign of divine approval or afterlife reward; the seeker failed to intend God and chose fleeting life. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq say deeds without intention toward God explain Hell. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī deny afterlife reward for worldly-directed work. Al-Rāzī warns that worldly success can end in punishment, and al-Biqāʿī says both deprivation and partial success may leave the seeker a loser. Ibn Kathīr contrasts fleeting and lasting ends. Elmalılı says reward depends on the accepting employer, while citing Hūd 11:15 for full payment of works. Qur'an Yolu ties moral blame to choosing desire over God's pleasure. [14 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]

## 17:18/q12 How are جَعَلْنَا and يَصْلَىٰهَا construed?  [جَعَلْنَا يَصْلَىٰهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether جَعَلَ needs a supplied second object and whether the circumstantial clause attaches to the person or Hell.
- 17:18/q12/p1 جَعَلْنَا makes Hell the person's destination or recompense, with a second object understood. — Abū Ḥayyān states Hell is the first object and reports another grammarian supplying an omitted second object. Al-Ālūsī rejects the need to posit that ellipsis. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 17:18/q12/p2 No omitted second object after جَعَلْنَا is required. — Al-Ālūsī explicitly rejects supplying 'destination' or 'recompense.' [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]
- 17:18/q12/p3 يَصْلَىٰهَا is a circumstantial clause about the recipient. — Abū al-Baqāʾ attaches it to the pronoun in لَهُ; al-Samīn also allows this, and says مَذْمُومًا describes the subject of يَصْلَىٰهَا. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]
- 17:18/q12/p4 يَصْلَىٰهَا is a circumstantial clause about Hell. — Al-Ṭabrisī calls it circumstantial; Abū Ḥayyān makes it a circumstance of Hell as first object of جَعَلْنَا; al-Samīn permits either attachment. [3 notes: TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]

## 17:18/q13 What does يَصْلَىٰهَا mean?  [يَصْلَىٰهَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb emphasizes enduring heat or entering the fire; the cited lexicon distinguishes related forms.
- 17:18/q13/p1 To endure Hell's heat. — Al-Khalīl gives the heat gloss. The lexicon cited by Ṭabāṭabāʾī explains ṣaly as kindling or enduring fire's heat. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الخليل); TABATABAI(المفردات)]
- 17:18/q13/p2 To enter Hell, or be caused to enter it. — Al-Ālūsī reports entry as another gloss. The cited lexicon distinguishes ṣallā, entering, from aṣlā, causing someone to enter. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الخليل); TABATABAI(المفردات)]

## 17:18/q14 What does مَذْمُومًا express?  [مَذْمُومًۭا · meaning]
- 17:18/q14/p1 Blamed, reproached, or disgraced, including blame amid divine punishment. — Ibn ʿAbbās glosses blameworthiness. Al-Zajjāj equates it with مذءوم. Qatāda places blame in punishment and amid God's favor; Ṭabarī names ingratitude and bad treatment of favors. Abū Ḥayyān stresses humiliation. [17 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 17:18/q15 What does مَّدْحُورًا mean?  [مَّدْحُورًۭا · meaning]
- 17:18/q15/p1 Expelled or driven far away, especially from God's mercy and Paradise. — Abū ʿUbayda cites دحر and repelling Satan, with verbal noun الدحور. Yaḥyā specifies exile from Paradise into Hell; Qatāda speaks of torment. Al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī connect it to removal from mercy. Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds disgrace, humiliation, and displeasure; the cited Majmaʿ lexicon says expulsion and distance. [22 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(المجمع)]

## 17:18/q16 What occasion of revelation is reported for this warning?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports name different groups; al-Ālūsī tests the campaign report against the sūra's Meccan revelation.
- 17:18/q16/p1 It was said to concern three men of Thaqīf. — Muqātil names Farqad ibn Yamāma, Abū Fāṭima ibn al-Bukhturī, and Ṣafwān, then adds two unnamed placeholders. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 17:18/q16/p2 It concerned emigrants seeking booty instead of God's reward. — Al-Jishumī reports this without naming the emigrants. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 17:18/q16/p3 It concerned hypocrites joining Muslim campaigns for booty rather than religious reward. — Al-Bayḍāwī and Abū Ḥayyān report it; al-Ālūsī rejects this occasion because the sūra is Meccan and this verse is not among its exceptions, while adding that general wording outruns any specific occasion. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 17:18/q17 How do this verse and related teachings distinguish worldly and afterlife aims?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the verse primarily classifies ultimate life goals or supplies a rule for the acceptance of each mixed-motive deed.
- 17:18/q17/p1 The intended end governs a deed's value; an apparently pious deed may seek worldly reward, while worldly activity may serve the Hereafter. — Al-Kashshāf and Abū Ḥayyān cite migration for a worldly prize or woman and fighting for gain. Al-Burhān and Kur'an Yolu connect the contrast to the hadith that actions depend on intentions. Al-Rāzī classifies pursuit of this world, the Hereafter, both, or neither. Al-Wāḥidī rebukes professed Islam for worldly gain; al-Ṭabrisī says it receives no afterlife reward. [14 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قيل); BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hz. Peygamber)]
- 17:18/q17/p2 Mixed motives form further classes; scholars differ over accepting an act when afterlife intent dominates, but reject it when worldly intent dominates or ties. — A discussion reported by Ṭabāṭabāʾī from Rūḥ al-maʿānī divides agents into four classes and mixed motives by predominance. Its fourth class, a voluntary act with neither motive, is impossible to those who require every act to have a motive; others call it inwardly ineffective yet outwardly forbidden. Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects applying this act-by-act taxonomy to verses about a person's ultimate life-goal. [3 notes: TABATABAI(روح المعاني); TABATABAI-]

## 17:18/q18 How does the verse connect with its surrounding passage and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: How the conditional worldly promise relates to adjacent accountability and more general statements of worldly reward.
- 17:18/q18/p1 It contrasts the worldly seeker's uncertain allotment and fate with afterlife seeking, as part of the passage's account of divine judgment. — Al-Qaffāl and the Rāzī reading cited by Kur'an Yolu link it to 17:13 and personal responsibility: rejecting prophets for status makes one's deeds lead to Hell. Ibn ʿĀshūr draws the sole-world restriction from the next sentence. Ṭabāṭabāʾī places it after worldly destruction of nations and says afterlife effort is thanked while worldly provision depends on divine choice. Islahi situates the section's warning to Jews and Quraysh about rejection, signs, and national punishment; Study Quran notes earthly and afterlife warnings as sūra themes. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); STUDYQURAN]
- 17:18/q18/p2 Other verses describe worldly recompense more generally, while this verse qualifies its amount and recipient. — Al-Wāḥidī cites 3:145 for worldly reward. Ibn Kathīr says the double restriction qualifies more general promises. Al-Qurṭubī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī discuss Hūd 11:15–16; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it concerns recompense for deeds rather than this verse's life-goal contrast. Al-Rāzī invokes 18:104 for worldly losers who suppose they do well. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 17:18/q19 What inward account is given of the worldly seeker and Hell?  [* · inward]
- 17:18/q19/p1 Attachment to nature and appetite clouds spiritual readiness; Hell images deprivation and expulsion from mercy. — Al-Qāshānī invokes the allotted share in the Preserved Tablet and describes Hell as a dark well of nature, its fire as deprivation and its blame in both worlds. Al-Ālūsī describes a turbid disposition overwhelmed by desire. Al-Wāsiṭī says witnessing the Hereafter accompanies renouncing the world, divine support effaces self-regard, and contemplating divine attributes causes servant attributes to fall away. [4 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 17:18/q20 Was a broader promise of full worldly gain abrogated by this verse?  [* · sciences]
- 17:18/q20/p1 Ibn ʿAbbās says the earlier promise of full health, family joy, money, and children was abrogated by 17:18. — The report glosses the desired life as its reward and its adornment as wealth, then treats this verse's restriction as the abrogating rule. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 17:18/q21 What recitational assimilation or clear articulation is reported at this locus?  [* · readings]
turns on: The transmitted routes and whether adjacent sounds are assimilated, concealed, or clearly pronounced.
- 17:18/q21/p1 Assimilation is transmitted and practiced by most performance authorities. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports it across Ibn Farḥ's routes from al-Dūrī and Ibn Jarīr's from al-Sūsī, as read by al-Dānī and his students. Other al-Dūrī routes give assimilation at 2:158, 3:45, and 21:81. He says dāl assimilates into ten following letters, with a restricted case for open dāl after a consonant, while dāl before tāʾ always assimilates because of shared articulation. [3 notes: NASHR(القاسم بن عبد الوارث وصاحب التجريد,عامة أهل الأداء)]
- 17:18/q21/p2 Clear articulation, izhār, is transmitted and is the sounder working practice. — All Iraqi transmitters report izhār through Abū al-Zarʿāʾ from al-Dūrī and through al-Sūsī; the author accepts both practices but prefers izhār. His comparison with 43:89 says ḥāʾ-to-ʿayn assimilation is transmitted, not analogical. For dāl before tāʾ or jīm he says some apparent izhār reports reflect two sukūns and the actual distinction is concealment versus assimilation, favoring the experts' analysis supported by kasra. [3 notes: NASHR(جميع العراقيين); NASHR+]

## 17:18/q22 What ancillary grammatical parsing is cited alongside 17:18?  [* · grammar]

- 17:18/q22/p1 Al-Akhfash takes idhā as genitive under ḥattā. — The citation concerns an idhā construction that the note associates with 17:18. [1 notes: ITQAN(الأخفش); ITQAN(الجمهور)-]

## 17:18/q23 Does the written form of مَا نَشَآءُ differ in the codex?  [مَا نَشَآءُ · readings]
- 17:18/q23/p1 A parallel occurrence in Shuʿayb's verse has an extra written wāw, without a change in meaning. — Ibn Qutayba says the codex writes ما نشؤا there with wāw after alif, whereas the same form elsewhere lacks it. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]

## 17:18/q24 What can attachment to the world do to a person's judgment and obedience?  [* · interpretation]
- 17:18/q24/p1 It can obscure obedience and reflect a mistaken estimate of worldly life. — Al-Biqāʿī says God deals with people according to knowledge of inner good and evil, while attachment to leadership, wealth, and pleasure can prevent obedience to messengers. A report from ʿĀʾisha calls this world the home of one without a home and wealth of one without wealth, gathered by one lacking reason. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عائشة)]
