# 2:185: 58 questions

## 2:185/q01 What does shahr mean and why is it so called?  [شَهْرُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the root points to public visibility, the crescent, or the measured lunar period.
- 2:185/q01/p1 A month is the publicly visible or known lunar interval. — Al-Tabari invokes the drawn sword and appearing crescent; al-Wahidi notes debts and rituals timed by months; Ibn Ashur cites the twelve-month year in 9:36. [24 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; JISHUMI; MAWARDI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج,الفراء والثعلبي); TABRISI; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,الفراء,الليث وابن الأعرابي)]
- 2:185/q01/p2 Shahr may itself denote the visible new crescent. — Al-Zajjaj and Dhu al-Rumma's verse call the crescent shahr; Abu Hayyan describes its public appearance. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(بعضهم؛ ذو الرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR(ذو الرمة)]

## 2:185/q02 Why is the month named Ramadan?  [رَمَضَانَ · meaning]
turns on: Which suggested root or image explains the name.
- 2:185/q02/p1 It was named for scorching heat, hot stones, or the season of its naming. — Al-Tabari's Arab-language authority cites scorched camel feet; Baghawi prefers this root; Ibn Atiyya notes former name Nathir. Abu Hayyan objects to deriving a regular verbal noun from an intransitive verb, while Alusi answers him. [43 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض أهل المعرفة بلغة العرب); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الخليل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن فارس); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); MAWARDI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الجوهري,الماوردي); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,المفضل); TABRISI; THALABI(أبو عمرو); BAGHAWI-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 2:185/q02/p2 It burns away sins or heats hearts through admonition. — Tha'labi, Qurtubi, and Razi transmit variants; Qurtubi finds support in reports of Ramadan's rewards. [7 notes: THALABI(غيره); QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أنس وعائشة,ابن عمرو); TABRISI]
- 2:185/q02/p3 It comes from cleansing rain before autumn. — Al-Khalil compares rain cleansing dust with Ramadan purifying bodies and hearts. [4 notes: THALABI(الخليل); RAZI-FULL(الخليل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI(Halîl)]
- 2:185/q02/p4 It recalls hunger's heat and hardship in an older fasting practice. — Al-Kashshaf and Razi connect the name with the faster's burning hunger; the ancient name Natiq also evokes distress. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 2:185/q02/p5 It comes from tempering or thinning weapons between stones. — Al-Azhari says blades were prepared during Ramadan for later fighting. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(الأزهري); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن السكيت); ELMALILI(Ezherî)]

## 2:185/q03 Is Ramadan a divine name, and may the month be called Ramadan alone?  [رَمَضَانَ · law]
turns on: Authority of the divine-name report and authenticated bare usage.
- 2:185/q03/p1 Ramadan is a divine name, so one should say 'month of Ramadan'. — Mujahid and a reported Prophetic prohibition give this reason. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن كعب القرظي، سعيد بن أبي هريرة، مجاهد، محمد بن كعب); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,مجاهد); BURSEVI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة,مجاهد، ومحمد بن كعب); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI(قتادة); TABRISI(مجاهد); THALABI(أنس عن هشيم عن أبان,بعضهم)]

## 2:185/q04 What duration and recurrence does the named month denote?  [شَهْرُ رَمَضَانَ · law]
turns on: Lunar sighting versus a fixed thirty-day month, and whether each annual Ramadan is included.
- 2:185/q04/p1 It is a recurring lunar month of twenty-nine or thirty days. — Al-Harali describes one appearance of the moon to the next; Ibn Ashur says the recurring name renews the fast annually; the Prophet's report explicitly allows twenty-nine days. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); IBNASHUR-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI; TABATABAI]
- 2:185/q04/p2 It must always be a full thirty days. — Al-Sadiq says the Prophet always completed thirty; Tabrisi answers that a completed lunar count can be twenty-nine or thirty. [1 notes: TABATABAI(الصادق عليه السلام); TABRISI-]

## 2:185/q05 How is 'month of Ramadan' construed grammatically?  [شَهْرُ رَمَضَانَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the nominative opens a clause, supplies an omitted subject or predicate, or apposes the earlier fasting phrase; the accusative has its own parses.
- 2:185/q05/p1 Nominative: it explains the earlier counted days as Ramadan, often by an omitted subject. — Al-Akhfash, al-Tabari, al-Farra and Ibn Ashur link it to 2:184; Ibn Ashur rejects a disconnected new subject. [51 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL(أبو علي,الفراء والأخفش); IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس,مجاهد وشهر بن حوشب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأخفش); JISHUMI(الفراء والأخفش,الكسائي); KASHSHAF-FULL; NAHHAS(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش,الفراء); TABATABAI; TABRISI; THALABI(العامة,الفراء والأخفش والكسائي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش والفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ZAJJAJ]
- 2:185/q05/p2 Nominative: it is a subject whose predicate is the revelation clause or the fasting command. — Al-Zajjaj and others permit the relative clause; al-Wahidi and Abu Ali prefer making that clause an adjective so the command supplies the predicate and the fast becomes explicit. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL(أبو علي)+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 2:185/q05/p3 Nominative: it apposes the prior fasting obligation. — Al-Kisai supplies fasting Ramadan; Abu Hayyan and al-Samin object to the distance and reversed inclusion apposition, though an omitted siyam can rescue it. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL(الكسائي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكسائي); SAMIN-DURR(الأعشى,الكسائي)]
- 2:185/q05/p4 Accusative: an omitted imperative exhorts people to fast Ramadan. — Al-Samin prefers 'fast the month'; Abu Ubayd also favors the imperative, though al-Nahhas calls it remote because Ramadan was not previously named. [10 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو عبيدة والحوفي,ابن عطية والزمخشري); QURTUBI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 2:185/q05/p5 Accusative: it apposes the counted days or functions as a time adverb. — Al-Akhfash, al-Rummani and others link it to 2:184; al-Akhfash also allows fasting during Ramadan. The long separation is noted. [7 notes: AKHFASH(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش,الرماني); SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش والرماني); IBNASHUR-FULL(مجاهد); JISHUMI; TAB-FULL(بعض القراء)]
- 2:185/q05/p6 Accusative: it is governed by the prior 'that you fast' or 'fasting'. — Al-Kisai and al-Kashshaf propose it; al-Nahhas and Abu Hayyan reject splitting the relative construction from its antecedent. [3 notes: NAHHAS(الكسائي); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); NAHHAS-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 2:185/q06 How is shahr Ramadan pronounced or read?  [شَهْرُ رَمَضَانَ · readings]
turns on: Case of shahr and assimilation or transfer between the two r sounds.
- 2:185/q06/p1 The common reading gives shahr the nominative. — Al-Zajjaj calls this more common; al-Nahhas names the readers of the accusative. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; JISHUMI]
- 2:185/q06/p2 Mujahid, Shahr ibn Hawshab, and others read shahr accusative. — The accusative supports imperative, apposition, or adverbial analyses. [5 notes: THALABI(الحسن ومجاهد وشهر بن حوشب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد ومعاوية والحسن وزيد بن علي وعكرمة ويحيى بن يعمر); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد وشهر بن حوشب); SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:185/q06/p3 Abu Amr and others assimilate the adjacent r sounds. — Al-Nahhas objects that two quiescent consonants meet; Kufans transfer the vowel to ha, and Abu Hayyan and al-Samin prioritize sound transmission over analogy. [12 notes: THALABI(أبو عمرو); NAHHAS(الحسن وأبو عمرو,الكوفيون,امروء القيس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن وأبو عمرو)]

## 2:185/q07 Why was Ramadan singled out for fasting?  [شَهْرُ رَمَضَانَ أُنزِلَ فِيهِ ٱلْقُرْءَانُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether revelation's virtue, spiritual kinship, or a distinctive lunar practice explains the choice.
- 2:185/q07/p1 Its distinction is the Qur'an's revelation in it. — Tabrisi says faith depends on that revelation; Razi pairs God's greatest sign of lordship with fasting as servanthood; Ibn Kathir notes earlier scriptures too. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; JISHUMI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 2:185/q07/p2 Fasting purifies and prepares understanding of revealed guidance. — Razi says fasting removes bodily attachments; al-Harali says hunger illuminates the heart and pairs daytime fast with night recitation. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 2:185/q07/p3 The lunar month makes one unified fast distinctive to this community. — Al-Harali contrasts its thirty nights with Moses' added ten in 7:142. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]

## 2:185/q08 What is the name Qur'an derived from?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانُ · meaning]
turns on: Proper name versus recitation, gathering, joining, corroborating signs, or utterance.
- 2:185/q08/p1 It is a proper, un-derived name for God's Book. — Al-Shafi'i compares Torah and Gospel; Qurtubi favors derivation. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الشافعي,جماعة); RAZI-FULL(الشافعي); QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي); ELMALILI(İmam Şâfiî)]
- 2:185/q08/p2 It derives from reciting and originally means recitation. — Razi and Qurtubi cite poetry and 17:78; the verbal noun came to name the recited speech. [16 notes: RAZI-FULL(القائلون بالهمز); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمر); ELMALILI; SAMIN-DURR(حسان); TABATABAI]
- 2:185/q08/p3 It derives from gathering or joining its parts and teachings. — Baghawi cites the idiom of gathering water; Razi and Abu Hayyan distinguish qara'a and qarana derivations, while Abu Hayyan rejects the water-root argument. [11 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج وأبو عبيدة,القائلون بالاشتقاق من قرن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 2:185/q08/p4 It derives from qarā'in, mutually corroborating signs. — Al-Farra cites 4:82: a nondivine book would contain contradictions. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الفراء)]
- 2:185/q08/p5 It names what the reciter casts from the mouth. — Qutrub compares a camel expelling afterbirth. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(قطرب)]

## 2:185/q09 How is al-Qur'an read with respect to hamza?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانُ · readings]
turns on: Unhamzated lexical origin versus phonetic transfer and deletion.
- 2:185/q09/p1 Ibn Kathir reads it without hamza; many others pronounce hamza. — Al-Farisi and later sources report the distributions; Abu Hayyan and al-Samin prefer transferred hamza vowel and deletion over a separate q-r-n root. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن كثير); QIRAAT-ER(Ibn Kathīr); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن كثير والشافعي); ELMALILI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير); JISHUMI(ابن كثير); NASAFI-FULL(مكي); SAMIN-DURR(ابن كثير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 2:185/q09/p2 The unhamzated form can reflect q-r-n, joining. — Some grammarians treat its nun as original and link it to the joined suras and verses. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(آخرون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:185/q10 In what sense did the Qur'an descend in Ramadan?  [أُنزِلَ فِيهِ ٱلْقُرْءَانُ · interpretation]
turns on: A complete heavenly descent, annual installments, the start of revelation, revelation concerning Ramadan, or a single descent of an underlying reality.
- 2:185/q10/p1 The whole Qur'an descended at once to the lowest heaven, then gradually to Muhammad. — Ibn Abbas places it in Bayt al-Izza on Laylat al-Qadr; reports vary twenty, twenty-one, and twenty-three years and the destination's name. Razi prefers this because inzal suggests one descent; Qurtubi calls it consensus. [66 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الشعبي,سعيد بن جبير); THALABI(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,مقاتل); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن جبير والحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN(ابن عباس,الحاكم); BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); ELMALILI(Ekser müfessirîn); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن إسحاق وأبو سليمان الدمشقي,ابن عباس,مجاهد والضحاك); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Some); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله,ابن عباس,بعضهم,سعيد بن جبير); TABRISI(ابن إسحاق,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 2:185/q10/p2 Each year's destined portion descended on its Night of Decree, then reached Muhammad as needed. — Ibn Jurayj places each year's share with Gabriel in the lowest heaven; Muqatil, al-Halimi and al-Mawardi are cited for multi-year nights. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); BURHAN(مقاتل); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); IBNABIHATIM(ابن أبي نجيح); IBNKATHIR-FULL(القرطبي,فخر الدين); MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI(السدي)]
- 2:185/q10/p3 The Qur'an's revelation to Muhammad began in Ramadan. — Ibn Ishaq treats the first portion as the whole; Razi considers this figurative; Elmalili prefers the Hira beginning, while Tabatabai objects that the mission began in Rajab. [21 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن إسحاق); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURHAN(الشعبي); ELMALILI(Muhammed b. İshak); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI(بعض الباحثين); WAHIDI-ASBAB(أبو قتادة); ELMALILI+; RAZI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 2:185/q10/p4 The wording refers to revelation about Ramadan's fasting duty or merit. — Mujahid and al-Dahhak identify 2:183 or the duty; Sufyan ibn Uyayna and al-Husayn ibn al-Fadl identify its virtue. Ibn Kathir calls this strange. [13 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري,سفيان بن عيينة); RAZI-FULL(ابن الأنباري,سفيان بن عيينة والحسين بن الفضل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سفيان بن عيينة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سفيان بن عيينة,مجاهد والضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(الضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك); TABRISI]

## 2:185/q11 What dates do reports give for scriptures revealed in Ramadan?  [أُنزِلَ فِيهِ ٱلْقُرْءَانُ · reports]
turns on: The reported day and scripture sequence differ.
- 2:185/q11/p1 Scrolls first, Torah sixth, Gospel thirteenth, Qur'an twenty-fourth. — Prophetic chronologies from Wathila and others; some add Psalms on the eighteenth. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNABIHATIM(النبي); WAHIDI-ASBAB(واثلة); DURR-FULL(وائلة بن الأسقع); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(واثلة بن الأسقع); ALUSI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(النبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(واثلة بن الأسقع); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي); MAWARDI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(واثلة بن الأسقع); RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI(النبي); THALABI(أبو ذر الغفاري)]
- 2:185/q11/p2 Scrolls third, Torah sixth, Gospel thirteenth or eighteenth, Qur'an twenty-fourth. — Abu Dharr's variant has scrolls third and, in one version, Gospel eighteenth; Abu Hayyan says the variants might mark beginning and completion. [4 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو ذر); TABRISI(النبي)]
- 2:185/q11/p3 Psalms twelfth and Gospel eighteenth, with Qur'an twenty-fourth. — Jabir's chronology interchanges dates found in the Wathila reports. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله); DURR-FULL(أبو الجلد,جابر بن عبد الله)]
- 2:185/q11/p4 Gospel twelfth and Psalms eighteenth, with Qur'an twenty-fourth. — Aisha's chronology differs from both Jabir and Wathila. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(عائشة)]
- 2:185/q11/p5 Qur'an twenty-third, with scrolls first, Torah sixth and Psalms eighteenth. — The chronology attributed to the Prophet in Tabatabai gives the Qur'an a different night. [2 notes: TABATABAI(النبي)]

## 2:185/q12 What does the verse establish about Laylat al-Qadr?  [أُنزِلَ فِيهِ ٱلْقُرْءَانُ · links]
turns on: The conjunction of Ramadan with 97:1 and 44:3, and the reported dates of the night.
- 2:185/q12/p1 Laylat al-Qadr is a Ramadan night, identified with the blessed night. — Ibn Umar infers this from 2:185 and 97:1; Qurtubi links 44:3. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عمر); QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله)]
- 2:185/q12/p2 Some reports identify it as Ramadan's twenty-fourth night. — Ibn Abbas's descent reports and al-Hasan's reading of Wathila's chronology support this; other reports place the Qur'an at mid-Ramadan or the twenty-third. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(الحسن بن علي)]

## 2:185/q13 How does the Qur'an guide people here?  [هُدًۭى لِّلنَّاسِ · interpretation]
turns on: General direction, a restricted audience, or inward illumination.
- 2:185/q13/p1 It guides people toward truth, right action, and God's pleasure. — Muqatil and Tabari describe religious direction; Ibn Ashur adds public and private interests; Ibn Kathir says believers benefit through faith and reflection. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); ELMALILI; IBNABIHATIM(ابن جريج); MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
- 2:185/q13/p2 It guides ordinary people generally, while clear proofs address those mature in knowledge. — Tabatabai argues ordinary people need an explaining guide whereas the learned can distinguish truth through signs. [2 notes: TABATABAI]
- 2:185/q13/p3 Its intelligibility itself refutes claims that faith or an Imam alone supplies understanding. — Jishumi says guidance would add nothing if knowledge were necessary or apparent meanings inaccessible. [3 notes: JISHUMI(أصحاب المعارف,المجبرة)]

## 2:185/q14 What are the clear signs of guidance?  [وَبَيِّنَٰتٍۢ مِّنَ ٱلْهُدَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Legal clarifications, theological proofs, earlier history, or experienced insight.
- 2:185/q14/p1 They explain lawful and unlawful matters, limits, duties, and judgments. — Al-Tabari, al-Suddi and many later commentators list legal rulings. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT]

- 2:185/q14/p3 They include prophetic stories, prior laws, histories, and decisive admonitions. — Al-Asamm and al-Qadi distinguish this from initial knowledge; Ibn Atiyya also lists decisive verses and admonitions. [2 notes: TABRISI(الأصم); IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 2:185/q14/p4 They are inward signs disclosed to the fasting person. — Al-Harali names an illuminated heart, humbled soul and prepared thought. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]

## 2:185/q15 What is al-Furqan in this phrase?  [وَٱلْفُرْقَانِ · referent]
turns on: Criterion of truth, legal decisiveness, the Torah, or escape from doubt.
- 2:185/q15/p1 It distinguishes truth from falsehood. — Tabari and the majority give this gloss; Abu Hayyan notes its cadence with adjacent endings. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI]
- 2:185/q15/p2 It denotes decisive rulings within the Qur'an that must be followed. — Abu Abd Allah distinguishes the whole book from its binding clear judgments. [1 notes: TABRISI(أبو عبد الله)]
- 2:185/q15/p3 It denotes the Torah. — Abu Salih gives this identification. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو صالح)]
- 2:185/q15/p4 It is a way out of religious doubt and error. — Muqatil and Ibn al-Jawzi point to 3:4 and 8:29. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]

## 2:185/q16 Why is guidance stated twice with clear proofs and criterion?  [هُدًۭى مِّنَ ٱلْهُدَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Emphatic specification, distinct kinds of guidance, anaphora, or wider divine guidance.
- 2:185/q16/p1 The second phrase specifies or intensifies the first general guidance. — Al-Kashshaf and Razi read general then manifest best guidance; Bursevi calls it an honorific three-level description. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

- 2:185/q16/p4 The second huda and furqan are broader divine categories that include prior revelation. — Abu Hayyan and al-Samin prefer taking the Qur'an's guidance as one part of God's wider guidance and criterion. - 2:185/q19/p1 Being resident and present during Ramadan; al-shahr is a time adverb. — Tabari, Wahidi and most grammarians omit the object 'town'; travelers too encounter Ramadan, so literal month-as-object cannot by itself mean residence. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 2:185/q21 Who becomes obligated when Ramadan arrives, including those gaining legal capacity midmonth?  [فَمَن شَهِدَ مِنكُمُ ٱلشَّهْرَ · law]

## 2:185/q23 Whose crescent testimony suffices?  [ٱلشَّهْرَ · law]
turns on: One versus two witnesses, clear versus obscured sky, and private observation.
- 2:185/q23/p1 One upright observer can establish Ramadan; Shawwal generally needs two. — Al-Shafi'i's early view and Abu Hanifa cite Ibn Umar's report; Razi and Elmalili distinguish Ramadan precaution and the human interests of Eid. [17 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الدارقطني,الشافعي,الشافعي وأبو حنيفة,علي بن أبي طالب); RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; JISHUMI]
- 2:185/q23/p2 Two upright witnesses are needed for Ramadan too. — Malik analogizes Shawwal and Dhu al-Hijja; al-Shafi'i's later position agrees. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي,مالك); DURR-FULL(أبو مسعود الأنصاري,عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن الخطاب)]
- 2:185/q23/p3 With a clear sky, numerous observers are needed; obstruction permits one for Ramadan, two for Eid. — Elmalili explains the Hanafi concern that a few claims conflict with many careful observers under clear skies. [4 notes: ELMALILI(eimme-i Hanefiyye)]
- 2:185/q23/p4 A lone observer follows the personal sighting for both months. — Ibn al-Mundhir says both fasting and breaking bind the lone observer; Malik instead says fast personally but do not break on Shawwal. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن المنذر,مالك)]
- 2:185/q23/p5 A lone observer follows neither sighting without communal proof. — Ata and Ishaq hold this. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عطاء وإسحق)]

- 2:185/q26/p1 A fast needs intention and knowing abstention from food, drink, sex and other invalidators from dawn to sunset. — Razi invokes Prophetic statements on intentions and sunset; Ibn al-Jawzi distinguishes lexical abstention from the legal fast. ## 2:185/q28 Why is the illness and travel concession repeated?  [وَمَن كَانَ مَرِيضًا أَوْ عَلَىٰ سَفَرٍۢ · rhetoric]

## 2:185/q29 What illness permits postponing the fast?  [مَرِيضًا · law]
turns on: Any named illness, inability to pray standing, intolerable worsening, or personal strength.
- 2:185/q29/p1 Any condition that is called illness qualifies. — Ibn Sirin's painful finger illustrates the view; Zahiris and al-Hasan in Jishumi invoke the wording's unrestricted label. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون,محمد بن سيرين); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل الظاهر,طريف بن تمام العطاردي); JISHUMI(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن سيرين); THALABI(طريف بن تمام العطاردي)]
- 2:185/q29/p2 An illness that prevents standing for prayer qualifies. — Ibrahim and al-Hasan use standing prayer as their threshold. - 2:185/q29/p3 Fasting must likely cause intolerable harm, worsening or exhaustion. — Al-Shafi'i and most jurists favor this; al-Tabari phrases it as hardship beyond capacity, otherwise the duty remains. ## 2:185/q30 What journey qualifies for the concession?  [عَلَىٰ سَفَرٍۢ · law]
turns on: Any travel versus stated distances and the kinds of journeys accepted.
- 2:185/q30/p1 Any journey, short or long, qualifies. — Dawud follows the unrestricted word; Razi rejects inferring a three-day minimum from the footwear-wiping report. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(داود); RAZI-FULL]
- 2:185/q30/p2 About two days or sixteen farsakhs qualifies. — Al-Shafi'i and Wahidi give sixteen farsakhs; Razi cites the Mecca-to-Asfan four-station report. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(الشافعي); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس ومالك,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN]

- 2:185/q30/p5 A one-day journey suffices. — Baghawi reports this additional distance. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قوم)]

## 2:185/q31 When a resident departs during a fasting day, may that day's fast be broken?  [عَلَىٰ سَفَرٍۢ · law]

## 2:185/q35 What number of make-up days is due?  [فَعِدَّةٌۭ مِّنْ أَيَّامٍ أُخَرَ · law]

## 2:185/q37 When must make-up fasting occur, and what if it is delayed or interrupted?  [مِّنْ أَيَّامٍ أُخَرَ · law]

## 2:185/q38 How is the phrase 'a number of other days' parsed?  [فَعِدَّةٌۭ مِّنْ أَيَّامٍ أُخَرَ · grammar]

## 2:185/q50 Why does takbir take 'upon what He guided you'?  [عَلَىٰ مَا هَدَىٰكُمْ · grammar]

- 2:185/q50/p2 'Upon' means 'because of' guidance. — Abu Hayyan and al-Burhan allow causal force. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(بعضهم)]

## 2:185/q53 What does 'perhaps you will give thanks' imply?  [وَلَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ · rhetoric]

## 2:185/q54 What inward meanings are given to this verse?  [* · inward]

- 2:185/q54/p1 Ramadan and fasting describe purification from sin, ego and heedlessness. — Sulami says witnessing God means fasting every moment from disobedience; Qushayri says mercy burns sins and reality burns ego-forms. [6 notes: SULAMI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QUSHAYRI]

## 2:185/q58 If Eid is learned after the prayer time, may its prayer be held the next day?  [وَلِتُكَبِّرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ · law]

- 2:185/q58/p1 The Eid prayer may be held on the next morning when late crescent testimony made the original time impossible. — The report of Anas's uncles says the Prophet ordered breaking the fast and Eid prayer the next day; Qurtubi prefers following this established exception. - 2:185/q58/p2 The Eid prayer is not held after noon or on another day. — Malik, Abu Hanifa and one Shafi'i position say a non-obligatory prayer is not made up outside its prescribed time; Abu Umar advances this principle. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمر,الشافعي والمزني,مالك وأصحابه وأبو حنيفة)]
- 2:185/q58/p3 A next-day Eid prayer is allowed for Adha but not Fitr, or allowed for Fitr only if a sound report establishes it. — Hasan ibn Salih distinguishes the festivals; al-Buwayti conditions Fitr on hadith; Abu Yusuf extends Adha to the third day because its festival lasts longer. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمر,أبو يوسف,البويطي,الحسن بن صالح بن حي)]
