# 21:87: 29 questions

## 21:87/q01 Who is Dhū al-Nūn?  [وَذَا ٱلنُّونِ · referent]
turns on: The cited interpretations differ in report, language, or doctrinal inference.
- 21:87/q01/p1 Jonah son of Mattā, called the man of the fish or whale. — Muqātil and al-Ṭabarī identify Jonah son of Mattā; 68:48 calls him the fish's companion. Abū Ḥayyān gives the plural nīnān; al-Ālūsī adds anwān. Al-Qurṭubī records a separate usage of nūna for a child's chin dimple. [33 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(عثمان وثعلب عن ابن الأعرابي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 21:87/q01/p2 Inwardly, a spirit not yet perfected. — Al-Qāshānī's inward reading makes Dhū al-Nūn a spirit short of perfection. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 21:87/q02 Why this title rather than companion of the fish?  [وَذَا ٱلنُّونِ · rhetoric]
turns on: The cited interpretations differ in report, language, or doctrinal inference.
- 21:87/q02/p1 Dhū al-Nūn is more honorific in a passage of praise. — Al-Suhaylī, quoted by al-Burhān, al-Itqān and Bursevī, finds dhū and nūn more dignified in praise than ṣāḥib al-ḥūt in 68:48; nūn is also a surah initial beside the pen at 68:1. [6 notes: BURHAN(السهيلي); ITQAN(السهيلي); BURSEVI(السهيلي)]
- 21:87/q02/p2 It recalls the fish that swallowed Jonah. — Al-Rāzī says the epithet conveys information; al-Wāḥidī and Islāḥī connect it to the swallowing. Al-Qushayrī says the title endured despite a short stay and draws hope for lasting divine connection. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 21:87/q03 Was Mattā Jonah's father or mother?  [وَذَا ٱلنُّونِ · reports]
turns on: The cited interpretations differ in report, language, or doctrinal inference.
- 21:87/q03/p1 His father. — Kaʿb names Mattā as father; al-Ālūsī cites Ibn Ḥajar's authentication, gives Hebrew name variants and says no connected genealogy is known. [3 notes: BURSEVI(عطاء); ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:87/q03/p2 His mother. — Jāmiʿ al-Uṣūl and Ibn al-Athīr say Mattā was the mother; al-Ālūsī reports the exceptional maternal naming claim. [2 notes: BURSEVI(جامع الأصول); ALUSI-FULL(ابن الأثير)]

## 21:87/q04 Did the fish episode precede Jonah's mission to Nineveh?  [وَذَا ٱلنُّونِ · reports]
turns on: The cited interpretations differ in report, language, or doctrinal inference.
- 21:87/q04/p1 Yes: God sent him to Nineveh after he emerged. — Ibn ʿAbbās says his mission began after emergence. Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Rāzī cite 37:145–47: cast ashore, restored, then sent to a hundred thousand or more. The king assignment narrative also puts the fish before Nineveh. [8 notes: MUJAHID(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:87/q04/p2 No: he preached before leaving and being swallowed. — Al-Rāzī says most scholars put the episode after his preaching; al-Wāḥidī prefers this repeated account. 10:98 concerns the people's repentance. Some accounts have him return after rescue. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(أكثر العلماء); QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري); TABATABAI; WAHIDI-BASIT+]

## 21:87/q05 Where did Jonah and the fish travel?  [وَذَا ٱلنُّونِ · reports]
turns on: The cited interpretations differ in report, language, or doctrinal inference.
- 21:87/q05/p1 Nineveh was by Mosul on the Tigris; the fish traveled through river and sea. — Yahyā places Nineveh near Mosul on the Tigris and reports Ibn ʿAbbās's river-sea route; al-Thaʿlabī mentions al-Abulla, Ibn Kathīr a fish from the Green Sea. [7 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(عبد الله بن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 21:87/q05/p2 A Jewish account places departure at Jaffa for Tarshish. — Al-Ālūsī reports and rejects the Jewish Jaffa–Tarshish flight as unworthy of a prophet. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(اليهود)-]

## 21:87/q06 What happened when Nineveh repented?  [وَذَا ٱلنُّونِ · reports]
turns on: The cited interpretations differ in report, language, or doctrinal inference.
- 21:87/q06/p1 The people pleaded with children and animals, and punishment was lifted. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Yahyā report public pleading with mothers, children and animals, separation of young, and God lifting promised punishment. Other accounts mention the deadline and Jonah's shame. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن بن أبي الحسن وغيره); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الله تعالى); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(başka bir rivayet)]
- 21:87/q06/p2 They repented after Jonah returned from the fish and warned them anew. — Islāḥī and related reports say Jonah was sent back after recovery, preached anew, and the king and people put on sackcloth, fasted and repented. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

Al-Qurṭubī calls it the best gloss. - 21:87/q07/p5 Himself. — Al-Junayd says Jonah was angry at himself; al-Qushayrī makes this resistance to self and religious opponents. [3 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI]
- 21:87/q07/p6 No specified object; he felt affronted by seeming a liar. — Ibn Qutayba says Jonah felt affronted at appearing a liar; he cites a poem equating ghaḍab and anaf and another on resenting an insult. ## 21:87/q08 Why did Jonah depart?  [إِذ ذَّهَبَ مُغَٰضِبًۭا · reports]
turns on: The cited interpretations differ in report, language, or doctrinal inference.
- 21:87/q08/p1 Persistent rejection exhausted him and he left before permission. — Muqātil, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Kashshāf and Islāḥī describe prolonged denial and departure before permission; Islāḥī says a messenger cannot abandon his post at his own judgment. [19 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); TAB-FULL(إياس بن معاوية); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL-; RAZI-FULL-]
- 21:87/q08/p2 His people were spared and he feared being called a liar. — Ibn Qutayba cites long abuse and shame when the promised punishment failed to arrive; al-Wāḥidī prefers this post-mission account. Some reports add mockery or fear of a liar-killing custom. [11 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); THALABI(الآخرون,القتيبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(عروة بن الزبير وسعيد بن جبير وجماعة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(نجم الدين)+; WAHIDI-BASIT+; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL-]
- 21:87/q08/p3 Prophetic burdens overwhelmed his temperament. — Wahb describes a righteous but narrow-tempered servant overwhelmed by prophecy, like a young camel under a load; 46:35 and 68:48 urge greater endurance. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه); THALABI(وهب بن منبه اليماني); MAWARDI-FULL(وهب); BAGHAWI-FULL(وهب بن منبه); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(وهب بن منبه); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 21:87/q08/p4 A ruler or prophet assigned him without naming a divine order. — The Isaiah/Hezekiah account concerns Israelite captives and a mission to a conquering ruler; Jonah asked if God had appointed him. Ibn ʿĀshūr disputes the account. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو صالح,ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:87/q08/p5 He feared execution under a custom of killing liars. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī and others report the custom of killing proven liars; Ibn ʿAṭiyya judges this weak and unsuitable. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن بن أبي الحسن وغيره)-]
- 21:87/q08/p6 An urgent mission left no time to prepare. — Al-Ḥasan reports an urgent command that allowed not even time for sandals; Ibn ʿAbbās has a request for a mount. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن البصري); THALABI(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL-]
- 21:87/q08/p7 He believed he had discharged his duty and could migrate. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says Jonah thought God might send another messenger; Islāḥī says he judged his warning sufficient and hoped for relief. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(النقاش); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 21:87/q09 How does mughāḍiban function?  [مُغَٰضِبًۭا · grammar]
turns on: The cited interpretations differ in report, language, or doctrinal inference.
- 21:87/q09/p1 It is a circumstantial accusative; the form can mean simple or intense anger. — Ibn Qutayba says a reciprocal mufaʿala form can describe one actor, comparing sāfara and nāwala. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn parse it as ḥāl of dhahaba; Bursevī notes intensity. [8 notes: THALABI(القتيبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:87/q09/p2 It expresses reciprocal anger. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī read genuine mutual anger: Jonah at disbelief, the people at his frightening departure. [5 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:87/q09/p3 It denotes dignified resentment or wounded pride. — Ibn Qutayba adduces poems linking anger and anaf, including offence at insulting Tamīm; al-Qurṭubī argues indignation still entails anger. [4 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; THALABI(القتيبي)]

## 21:87/q10 How is mughāḍiban read?  [مُغَٰضِبًۭا · readings]
turns on: The cited interpretations differ in report, language, or doctrinal inference.
- 21:87/q10/p1 A passive mughḍaban or mughāḍaban. — Abū Sharaf's reported passive form varies with or without alif and in the ḍād vowel; al-Zamakhsharī derives the alifless form from aghḍaba. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو شرف); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو المتوكل وأبو الجوزاء وعاصم الجحدري وابن السميفع); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو شرف); SAMIN-DURR(أبو شرف,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو سرف)]
- 21:87/q10/p2 The common active participle. — Al-Samīn gives the general active participle. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]

## 21:87/q26 What inward lessons are drawn from Jonah?  [* · inward]
turns on: The cited interpretations differ in report, language, or doctrinal inference.
- 21:87/q26/p1 Past worship and knowledge remain a source of hope. — Al-Qushayrī draws hope from his enduring title after a short ordeal; angelic-recognition reports stress prior worship. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن); SULAMI(ذو النون); QUSHAYRI]
- 21:87/q26/p2 The spirit seeks God while enclosed by soul, body or world. — Al-Qāshānī and Najmiyya through Bursevī compare spirit, body and commanding soul with Jonah and fish, with divine preservation amid veils. [9 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,عرائس البقلى)]
- 21:87/q26/p3 One should resist retaliation and surrender to mercy. — Al-Wāsiṭī warns against retaliation and commends divine mercy; al-Junayd treats confession as humility before God. [4 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد,الواسطي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:87/q26/p4 The fish belly became a site of witnessing and ascent. — Al-Baqlī says Jonah witnessed below earth what Muḥammad saw above the Throne; Bursevī reports distress at veiling. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 21:87/q28 What should one infer about prophetic error and God's treatment?  [* · theology]
turns on: The cited interpretations differ in report, language, or doctrinal inference.
- 21:87/q28/p1 Prophets can commit faults and confess them. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Ṭabarī and al-Wāḥidī treat blame and confession as real fault, citing 37:142, 68:48 and parallels such as Joseph's confession at 12:53. [12 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة,الحديث); RAZI-FULL(القائلون بجواز الذنب); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 21:87/q28/p2 Prophets are protected from actual sin; apparent blame describes an implication or less good act. — Al-Rāzī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and others protect prophets from intrinsic or grave sin; the apparent supposition is an implication of the act, or the lapse is departure from a better course. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو المعالي); TABATABAI]
- 21:87/q28/p3 Do not infer inferiority of Jonah from his trial. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Prophetic reports prohibit using Jonah's ordeal to diminish his dignity, while allowing the Prophet's general rank. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
