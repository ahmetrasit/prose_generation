# 20:61: 23 questions

## 20:61/q01 Why does Moses' speech begin here?  [قَالَ لَهُم مُّوسَىٰ · links]
- 20:61/q01/p1 It resumes the scene after Pharaoh gathered his forces, leaving Moses' arrival understood and showing him open the contest with counsel. — Bursevi and al-Alusi say the wording answers the implicit question of what Moses did next; al-Alusi says his arrival needs no repetition. Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise calls it an explanatory beginning. Islāhī places the speech first in the public arena. [5 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:61/q02 Whom does 'them' address?  [لَهُم · referent]
turns on: Whether the implied magicians alone supply the plural antecedent, or the prior gathering and public audience include Pharaoh and his party.
- 20:61/q02/p1 The sorcerers assembled by Pharaoh. — Al-Wāḥidī links them to Pharaoh's gathering of his stratagem at 20:60; Ibn al-Jawzī points to his discussion at 7:114. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the promised matching magic implies them and says an actual imprecation could target them alone, preserving gentle speech to Pharaoh. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 20:61/q02/p2 Pharaoh, the sorcerers, and others present or assisting him. — Al-Qurṭubī names Pharaoh and magicians; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the court could hear and see and the assembled scheme implies them. Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes Pharaoh, his schemes, magicians, and helpers, arguing that magicians alone had not been mentioned previously. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 20:61/q03 How many sorcerers were gathered?  [لَهُم · reports]
turns on: The reports give different counts, while one view specifies only a large gathering.
- 20:61/q03/p1 Seventy-two, each carrying a rope and staff. — Al-Baghawī gives this report. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 20:61/q03/p2 Four hundred. — Al-Baghawī reports this as another account. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل)]
- 20:61/q03/p3 Twelve thousand or more. — Al-Baghawī attributes this to Kaʿb. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(كعب)]
- 20:61/q03/p4 They were numerous, without a specified count. — Al-Jishumī reports this as another view alongside the miracle and sleight-of-hand distinction. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 20:61/q04 What was Moses doing by this warning, and how did the sorcerers respond?  [قَالَ لَهُم مُّوسَىٰ وَيْلَكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes describe successive sides of the encounter rather than competing histories.
- 20:61/q04/p1 He admonished and intimidated them, calling them to God and the afterlife. — Al-Jishumī names admonition and intimidation. Al-Qushayrī says Moses exhausted gentleness, offered warning of punishment and promise of grace, and spoke of afterlife reward and punishment. [4 notes: JISHUMI; QUSHAYRI]
- 20:61/q04/p2 They dismissed his warning and miracle as falsehood and magic and alleged that he and Aaron sought their expulsion and the destruction of their way. — Al-Qushayrī contrasts Moses' counsel with Pharaoh's folly and their worldly response; his reminders increased their heedlessness. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 20:61/q05 What does 'woe to you' convey?  [وَيْلَكُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether waylakum functions chiefly as an imprecation and rebuke or an exclamation of astonishment.
- 20:61/q05/p1 A warning, rebuke, or invocation of ruin and punishment. — Al-Ṭabrisī says it threatens that God impose woe; Bursevī says the original invocation now restrains fabrication; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it a threat of punishment. Ibn ʿĀshūr says wayl names punishment or evil and has no verb. Al-Qurṭubī calls it an invocation of woe. [5 notes: TABRISI; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 20:61/q05/p2 An expression of astonishment at their strange conduct, with a warning. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the Prophet's astonished description of Abū Baṣīr as a fire-stirrer of war; he gives no grading for the report. Al-Ṭabrisī also reports an analysis as wonder. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI(قيل)]

## 20:61/q06 How is 'woe to you' parsed?  [وَيْلَكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether its accusative depends on an implied verb or an omitted vocative particle.
- 20:61/q06/p1 An accusative verbal noun or object governed by an understood verb imposing woe. — Al-Zajjāj, through al-Naḥḥās, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī, supplies the implied imposition of woe. Al-Qurṭubī calls it a verbal noun. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows an omitted warning or exhortation verb, referring to 2:79. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو إسحاق الزجاج); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:61/q06/p2 An accusative vocative with an omitted calling particle. — Al-Zajjāj and those transmitting him compare 36:52; he also cites 19:4, and al-Rāzī's account mentions 11:72. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 2:79. Al-Ṭabrisī reports an invocation of woe and says it may be analyzed as two words. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو إسحاق الزجاج); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:61/q07 How can the sharp 'woe' fit the command to speak gently to Pharaoh?  [وَيْلَكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether Pharaoh is addressed, whether the phrase curses, and whether the circumstances permitted forceful speech.
- 20:61/q07/p1 It may address only the sorcerers, leaving Pharaoh outside the imprecation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr makes this conditional on waylakum being a genuine imprecation. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:61/q07/p2 Moses may have received special leave to speak harshly after Pharaoh's settled disbelief resisted gentleness. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the restriction of a general command with Muhammad's permission to fight at 22:39. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:61/q07/p3 The sorcerers' public misleading made forceful denunciation fitting. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says they invoked Pharaoh's might as evidence of divine support before the crowd. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:61/q07/p4 The phrase can express astonishment rather than an imprecation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the Prophet's expression to Abū Baṣīr as a parallel, without grading the report. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 20:61/q08 What lie against God does Moses forbid?  [لَا تَفْتَرُوا۟ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ كَذِبًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Which act in the contest is the intended fabrication: denying the signs, presenting magic as divine, or claiming divinity for others; several authors include multiple acts.
- 20:61/q08/p1 Calling Moses' genuine signs, message, or proof sorcery and denying their divine origin. — Muqātil specifies the hand and staff; al-Wāḥidī reports the same. Al-Rāzī says this claim licenses opposition; Ibn ʿAṭiyya stresses knowingly opposing recognized truth. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes Pharaoh's political-intrigue label; Ibn ʿĀshūr includes calling Moses liar and magician. Ibn ʿĀshūr relates 5:103, and Study Quran 10:77. [20 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(آخرون); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:61/q08/p2 Presenting their sorcery or illusions as divine truth or a genuine miracle. — Al-Māwardī says they attribute magic to God. Ibn Kathīr says illusions falsely suggest real creation and thus attribute creation to God. Al-Taʾwīlāt, quoted by Bursevī, says they claim the prophetic gift for their magic. Ibn ʿĀshūr mentions ropes made to seem snakes and claimed divine backing. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes magic presented as mere magic. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:61/q08/p3 Associating partners with God or treating Pharaoh or other gods as divine. — Ibn ʿAbbās is reported by al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn al-Jawzī. Islāhī locates the debate in Pharaoh's claim to divinity: the true Lord creates and sustains, while alleged partners have no revealed proof. Ṭabāṭabāʾī specifies claims of divinity, intercession and control of the world, citing 7:89. Ibn ʿĀshūr includes Pharaoh and his gods. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:61/q08/p4 Deliberately attributing any falsehood to God or denying His messages. — Al-Ṭabarī states the general prohibition; Ibn ʿAṭiyya emphasizes knowing the truth. Asad construes it as deliberate denial of God's messages; Ibn ʿĀshūr lists the several forms their blasphemous claims took. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says Pharaoh's labels severed the signs from God. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 20:61/q09 What does the expression 'fabricate a lie' add?  [تَفْتَرُوا۟ كَذِبًۭا · rhetoric]
- 20:61/q09/p1 Iftirāʾ is deliberate invention of falsehood; adding 'a lie' emphasizes it. — Ibn Qutayba equates fabrication with lying; Bursevī stresses deliberateness. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the cognate sense emphatic and compares 5:103. [3 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:61/q10 What does 'He will eradicate you' mean?  [فَيُسْحِتَكُم · meaning]
turns on: Whether the gloss emphasizes total removal and death or the hardship inflicted by punishment.
- 20:61/q10/p1 God will destroy them utterly, removing every trace. — Ibn Zayd says no remnant remains; Qatāda says they are cut off completely. Al-Layth and language scholars relate the root to shaving hair close; Islāhī gives peeling meat from bone and later uprooting or exploitation. Abū ʿUbayda, Abū ʿUthmān, al-Zajjāj and others treat saḥata and asḥata as equivalent. Al-Farazdaq's depleted-wealth line, also cited with Suwayd by Abū ʿUbayda, illustrates removal; its transmitted spelling and case vary. Al-Farrāʾ says yusḥitu is common. Al-Rāzī, al-Biqāʿī and others stress complete extermination. [47 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,السدي,قتادة); ZAJJAJ(الفرزدق); NAHHAS(الفرزدق); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو الحسن وأبو عثمان,أبو عبيدة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة والأخفش والفراء والزجاج,أهل المعاني,ابن عباس,الفرزدق,الليث,قتادة وأبان بن تغلب); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة,مقاتل والكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس، الكلبي، مقاتل، الجبائي,الفرزدق,قتادة، السدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء,الفرزدق); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الفرزدق); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الرازي); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:61/q10/p2 God will wear them down to the utmost hardship by punishment. — Ibn ʿAbbās through ʿAṭāʾ uses 'wear down'; al-Layth gives utmost hardship and recognizes asḥatnāhum as a dialect form. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس في رواية عطاء,الليث)]

## 20:61/q11 How is 'He will eradicate you' read?  [فَيُسْحِتَكُم · readings]
turns on: The two accepted dialectal verb forms and reader transmissions; most sources equate their meanings, while Qirāʾāt-ER distinguishes direct action from causation.
- 20:61/q11/p1 With fatḥa on yāʾ and ḥāʾ, from the triliteral saḥata, the Hijazi form. — Ibn Mujāhid names Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim, Abū ʿAmr and Ibn ʿĀmir; reports also add Ibn ʿAbbās, Ruways and Ibn Abāʿī in their respective enumerations. Al-Ṭabarī prefers this as more eloquent highland usage; al-Naḥḥās also prefers the Hijazi form. Al-Farrāʾ is reported elsewhere as calling the other form more common. Both generally mean destruction; Qirāʾāt-ER distinguishes this as God's direct scraping or destruction. Some reports abbreviate the reader lists. - 20:61/q11/p2 With ḍamma on yāʾ and kasra on ḥāʾ, from quadriliteral asḥata, the Najdi or Tamimi form. — The usual list is Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī and Ḥafṣ from ʿĀṣim; later lists add Khalaf, Ruways, Yaʿqūb, al-Aʿmash, Ṭalḥa or Ibn Jarīr. Al-Rāzī's summary names ʿĀṣim without distinguishing transmitters. Al-Farazdaq's masḥatan is cited for this form, though its verse's parsing is disputed. ## 20:61/q12 Why is the verb after the prohibition in the subjunctive?  [لَا تَفْتَرُوا۟ فَيُسْحِتَكُم · grammar]
- 20:61/q12/p1 It gives the consequence of the prohibition, with an implicit an after fa. — Al-Naḥḥās, al-Qurṭubī and al-Nasafī call it the answer or result of 'do not fabricate'; al-Samīn explicitly supplies an elided an governing the accusative. [4 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 20:61/q13 How is al-Farazdaq's cited line parsed?  [فَيُسْحِتَكُم · grammar]
turns on: Different transmissions and explanations of the raised masḥat and mujallaf in the poem.
- 20:61/q13/p1 In one transmission, masḥatan denotes what remains after loss, and raised mujallaf is coordinated with it. — Al-Samīn describes masḥatan as quasi-subject of 'nothing remained'; he says al-Zamakhsharī cites the construction for raised qalīl at 2:249. Al-Farazdaq's text is transmitted with masḥatan or masḥat, with Abū ʿUbayda giving nominative case; al-Kashshāf and al-Qurṭubī note sustained dispute. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفرزدق); KASHSHAF-FULL(الفرزدق); QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 20:61/q13/p2 Raised mujallaf has an omitted verb: 'or some stripped remnant remained.' — Al-Samīn supplies baqiya from the preceding lam yadaʿ. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 20:61/q13/p3 Raised mujallaf is a subject with an omitted predicate. — Al-Samīn reports al-Farrāʾ's estimate 'or mujallaf likewise'. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الفراء)]
- 20:61/q13/p4 Mujallaf is coordinated with the concealed pronoun in masḥatan. — Al-Samīn reports al-Kisāʾī's view: separation is ordinarily expected, but he does not require it and poetry permits the coordination. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الكسائي)]
- 20:61/q13/p5 Mujallaf is a verbal noun shaped like a passive participle and coordinated with 'the bite of time'. — Al-Samīn reports al-Fārisī's analogy to mumazzaq at 34:19 and expressly prefers this fifth analysis. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 20:61/q13/p6 With kasra on dāl, masḥat is raised as subject of active wadaʿ, with mujallaf coordinated. — Al-Samīn reports a second transmission and an omitted pronoun meaning 'because of it'. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 20:61/q13/p7 With passive yudaʿ, masḥat is the deputy subject and mujallaf is coordinated. — Al-Samīn reports this third transmission; he says the wāw should remain in the passive because it is not between yāʾ and kasra, so its deletion follows analogy with the active form. He refers to fuller discussion under al-Baqara. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 20:61/q14 What punishment does the warning threaten?  [بِعَذَابٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Al-Rāzī leaves the threatened punishment open between worldly annihilation and severe punishment in the Hereafter.
- 20:61/q14/p1 A devastating annihilation or uprooting in this world. — The verb's eradication sense supplies the force; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the indefinite 'punishment' marks severity and shirk brings annihilation. Bursevī and al-Alūsī describe the torment as immense and beyond measure. Asad calls it grievous suffering; Islāhī says false attribution makes them liable, as wrongdoers are. [11 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:61/q14/p2 Severe punishment in the Hereafter may be intended. — Al-Rāzī offers this as an alternative to worldly annihilation. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 20:61/q15 Who is the failed fabricator?  [مَنِ ٱفْتَرَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether the relative man is taken in full generality, applied to shirk, or directed to Pharaoh as an immediate example.
- 20:61/q15/p1 Anyone who invents a lie against God; the rule is general. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says man is general, and al-Alūsī stresses any manner of fabrication. Bursevī says whoever and however. Al-Biqāʿī extends deliberate lying even to lies against others. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this is broader than 16:116 and recalls Joseph's falsely bloodied shirt at 12:18. The specific prohibited fabrication remains its immediate instance. [13 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:61/q15/p2 The person who claims another deity beside God. — Ibn ʿAbbās is quoted by al-Wāḥidī; al-Wāḥidī's concise commentary also makes the fabricator a claimant of another deity. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
- 20:61/q15/p3 Pharaoh in particular, whose fabrication failed to preserve his rule. — Al-Bayḍāwī applies it to Pharaoh's unsuccessful schemes. Al-Alūsī offers Pharaoh as an alternative intended referent, warning the sorcerers not to share his frustration. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:61/q16 What is the fabricator's failure?  [وَقَدْ خَابَ · meaning]
- 20:61/q16/p1 Loss of the desired outcome, ultimately deprivation of divine mercy, reward, and Paradise. — Al-Jishumī says loss and deprivation of God's reward; al-Ṭabrisī reports Qatāda's 'lost' and another account's lost hope of reward and Paradise. Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī specify lost mercy and reward. Abū Ḥayyān and Bursevī define khayba as missing one's aim; al-Rāzī adds deprivation of one's aim. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says false causes cannot yield true lasting good; Asad calls the liar already doomed. [19 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة,قيل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 20:61/q17 How does the closing sentence support Moses' warning?  [وَقَدْ خَابَ مَنِ ٱفْتَرَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 20:61/q17/p1 It gives a general reason for the ban and reinforces it as a parenthetical statement. — Al-Alūsī calls it parenthetical; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it circumstantial and the major premise of a categorical syllogism, with earlier fabricators' failure as evidence. Moses thereby signals he would not falsely claim revelation, knowing God would destroy a fabricator. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:61/q18 What rhetorical device links the verse's beginning and ending?  [تَفْتَرُوا۟ ٱفْتَرَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 20:61/q18/p1 Taṣdīr, or returning the ending to the beginning, repeats the fabrication root. — Al-Bāqillānī identifies the return and compares 17:21. Al-Suyūṭī defines taṣdīr and reports Ibn al-Muʿtazz's three forms: the end matches the last, first, or another opening word; he assigns this verse's final iftarā to the earlier taftarū. Al-Zarkashī lists further examples at 42:20, 21:37, 9:104, 9:70, 6:31, 71:10, 33:37, 5:12 and 9:112. [5 notes: BAQILLANI; BURHAN; ITQAN(ابن المعتز)]

## 20:61/q19 What role can poetic amplification play at a verse ending?  [وَقَدْ خَابَ مَنِ ٱفْتَرَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 20:61/q19/p1 Some seek īghāl at Qur'anic verse endings, as a rhyme may heighten a complete meaning. — Al-Bāqillānī cites poets' extreme praise, reaching beyond sun or sky, then notes that Imruʾ al-Qays's rhyme adds description although the sense stands without it; his discussion treats īghāl as a poetic device sought by some only at verse endings. [2 notes: BAQILLANI]

## 20:61/q20 What makes their denial especially blameworthy?  [لَا تَفْتَرُوا۟ · theology]
- 20:61/q20/p1 Rejecting a witnessed miracle after proof is graver and warrants greater punishment. — Al-Jishumī says the open daylight gathering displayed the miracle to all, whereas sleight of hand often works privately, at night, or on the ignorant; Moses' challenge exposed the difference. [3 notes: JISHUMI]

## 20:61/q21 What does accountability in this warning imply about human acts?  [لَا تَفْتَرُوا۟ بِعَذَابٍۢ · theology]
- 20:61/q21/p1 People produce their own acts and can justly be punished for denial and turning away. — Al-Jishumī argues otherwise God's signs, attributing denial and turning away to people, and punishing them for those acts would make no sense. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 20:61/q22 Why did the sorcerers fail to benefit from the warning?  [قَالَ لَهُم مُّوسَىٰ · inward]
- 20:61/q22/p1 Their inward blindness and divine exclusion left them heedless of faith, grace, and the afterlife. — Al-Qushayrī says open warning did not reach their inward perception; they answered only in worldly terms, and reminders increased ignorance. One marked by exclusion has no gnosis or faith, regrets nothing missed, and disbelieves what lies ahead. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 20:61/q23 What drew the sorcerers into the contest?  [لَهُم · reports]
- 20:61/q23/p1 They sought reward and rank from Pharaoh, with inducements or threats pressing them to face Moses. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī refers to Pharaoh's promise of payment and closeness in 26:41–42. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
