# 88:21: 15 questions

## 88:21/q01 How does the opening fā connect the command to what precedes it?  [فَذَكِّرْ · links]
turns on: Whether the consequence is drawn from the preceding proofs and refusal to reflect or from God's lordship and coming judgment.
- 88:21/q01/p1 After the signs and the account of resurrection, the Messenger must remind people even when they fail or refuse to reflect. — Al-Rāzī and his full version point to proofs of oneness and resurrection; Biqāʿī points to creation's proof of power, Paradise and Hell, and innate recognition; Ibn al-Zubayr, reported by Biqāʿī, traces the damned, blessed, created signs, then reminder. Al-Kashshāf, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī, Elmali̇li, and Ibn ʿĀshūr emphasize their failure or refusal to reflect; Ibn ʿĀshūr also connects the order to rejection of resurrection. [13 notes: KASHSHAF; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR]
- 88:21/q01/p2 The command follows from God's sole lordship and the judgment awaiting believers and unbelievers. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī gives these as the reason to remind without coercing. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 88:21/q02 What does the command to remind call the Messenger to communicate?  [فَذَكِّرْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'remind' is glossed as preaching, recalling favors and duties, presenting proofs, or conveying revelation; several notes combine these contents.
- 88:21/q02/p1 He should preach, admonish and warn people. — Muqātil specifies Mecca and comparison with earlier prophets. Al-Māwardī and his full version give preaching as one interpretation; Ibn ʿAbbās as reported by al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī describe admonition at that stage. Al-Qurṭubī and his full version say admonish and warn. Al-Dāmghānī gives dhikr the sense of admonition or exhortation with the Qurʾān. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, Elmali̇li and Corpus Coranicum also speak of preaching or warning. [13 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ELMALILI; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 88:21/q02/p2 He should explain divine proofs and signs, especially oneness, resurrection and the need to reflect. — Al-Naḥḥās supplies God's proofs and signs as the understood object; al-Wāḥidī includes proofs of oneness. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says this verse and like signs; al-Ṭabrisī reports al-Jubbāʾī and Abū Muslim urging reasoning from these proofs. Al-Rāzī adds resurrection and warning against neglect; al-Nasafī says the proofs invite reflection. Biqāʿī invokes creation and innate knowledge of divine power; Quṭb names the afterlife and creation signs. [15 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي، أبو مسلم); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 88:21/q02/p3 He should recall God's favors and people's duties so they respond with fear, gratitude and worship. — Al-Ṭabarī gives signs, proofs, favors, duties and admonition; his full version stresses favors and obligations. Al-Māwardī and his full version say recalling blessings brings fear of punishment. Al-Wāḥidī includes blessings with proofs of oneness; al-Ṭabrisī says reminder makes the favor and duty intelligible and calls for gratitude and worship. [7 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI]
- 88:21/q02/p4 He should convey the revelation and what he was sent with. — Ibn Kathīr and the parallel note on the next verse say to remind people of what was sent with him. Al-Kashshāf cites 42:48 for the duty of conveying; Quṭb names the afterlife and creation signs; Kuranyolu specifies conveying revelation. [5 notes: KASHSHAF; IBNKATHIR; QUTB-ZILAL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 88:21/q03 Whom is the Messenger told to remind?  [فَذَكِّرْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the addressees are unrestricted, narrowed by capacity to benefit, specified as Meccans or the elect, or limited by attachment of the following exception.
- 88:21/q03/p1 People or God's servants generally. — Al-Ṭabarī calls them God's servants; al-Naḥḥās says the omitted object is God's servants. Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, and Kuranyolu describe the people addressed by preaching or warning. Al-Māwardī likewise says 'them' without restricting the audience. [8 notes: TAB; NAHHAS; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 88:21/q03/p2 Anyone who might benefit or is prepared to rise toward God. — Biqāʿī and his full version say to awaken innate knowledge in anyone likely to benefit. Al-Qāshānī says reminder can benefit a soul prepared to ascend; he contrasts the veiled person who turns away and faces the great fire, understood as absolute veiling across all levels of existence. [3 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QASHANI]
- 88:21/q03/p3 The people of Mecca. — Muqātil specifies Mecca's people, comparing the Prophet's warning with earlier prophets. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 88:21/q03/p4 Discerning believers, especially the elect. — Abū al-ʿAbbās ibn ʿAṭāʾ, reported by al-Sulamī, distinguishes admonition for ordinary people, counsel for brethren and reminder for the elect; reminder to discerning believers remains obligatory lest the Sunnah and obligations lapse. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو العباس بن عطاء)]
- 88:21/q03/p5 The people, except those who turn away and cannot benefit. — Al-Jishumī reports this as an alternative syntactic attachment of the later exception to 'remind'. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 88:21/q04 Why is the object of 'remind' unspoken?  [فَذَكِّرْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the missing object is recovered from shared knowledge of the signs or from the following clause.
- 88:21/q04/p1 The hearer already knows the object: God's servants are to be reminded of His signs and proofs. — Al-Naḥḥās explicitly explains the omission through what is already understood. [1 notes: NAHHAS]
- 88:21/q04/p2 The following statement that he is no controller over them supplies the omitted object. — Ibn ʿĀshūr and his full version connect the unexpressed object to the next verse, 88:22. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 88:21/q05 Does the imperative call for sustained reminder?  [فَذَكِّرْ · grammar]
- 88:21/q05/p1 Yes, he must keep reminding despite inattention and obstacles. — Ibn ʿĀshūr and his full version read the imperative as continued effort. Al-Rāzī and his full version say the commission requires patient endurance of obstacles; Ibn ʿĀshūr links the continuation to refusal to listen. [5 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 88:21/q06 What response should reminder seek from hearers?  [فَذَكِّرْ · interpretation]
- 88:21/q06/p1 They should reason from clear signs and act on what reflection teaches. — Al-Ṭabrisī says explanation makes needed knowledge intelligible and that explicit religious proofs make blind imitation inadequate, linking 51:55 and 16:67; he reports al-Jubbāʾī and Abū Muslim urging inference from the proofs. Elmali̇li says reflection on creation leads to knowledge of divine ways, commands and useful deeds. [4 notes: TABRISI(الجبائي، أبو مسلم); ELMALILI]

## 88:21/q07 What does 'you are only a reminder' limit or explain?  [إِنَّمَآ أَنتَ مُذَكِّرٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 88:21/q07/p1 It confines the Messenger's mission to reminder and conveyance, without control over acceptance or blame for refusal, and explains why he should persist. — ʿAbd al-Qāhir al-Jurjānī says negation after innamā excludes a competing agent, whereas negation before it corrects the actor; the verse uses the former pattern, with 88:22. Al-Ālūsī says the clause explains the command. Ibn ʿĀshūr says its added restriction explains persistence despite inattention and removes responsibility for producing recollection. Al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Nasafī compare 42:48 on conveyance; Quṭb cites 88:17–20. Al-Qāshānī calls the reminder and no-control clause an interruption and cites 28:56 and 50:45. Al-Study Quran cites 13:40, 3:20, 5:92, 5:99, 16:35, 16:82, 29:18, 36:17, 42:48 and 64:12. [38 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; JURJANI-DALAIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR; BIQAI; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 88:21/q08 Can the Prophet compel belief or be held responsible when people do not respond?  [إِنَّمَآ أَنتَ مُذَكِّرٌۭ · interpretation]
- 88:21/q08/p1 He can deliver and warn, but cannot force faith, guarantee guidance, or bear blame for their refusal; guidance and reckoning belong to God. — Muqātil says he is no king over them; al-Qushayrī and al-Baghawī deny control or forced belief. Al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say he need not be distressed or blamed for refusal. Ibn Kathīr assigns reckoning to God; al-Bursevī assigns guidance and success to God. Kuranyolu argues faith and worship require inward conviction and cites 3:20, 16:82, 28:56 and 42:48. Corpus Coranicum rejects reading the later exception as giving power over unbelievers because 88:21–22 deny compulsion. [26 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR; BIQAI; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 88:21/q09 Was the no-compulsion directive superseded by permission to fight?  [إِنَّمَآ أَنتَ مُذَكِّرٌۭ · sciences]
turns on: Whether later fighting authorized compulsory belief or concerned harmful acts while faith still required free conviction.
- 88:21/q09/p1 Yes; the reminder-only direction belonged to the period before fighting was commanded and was abrogated by the fighting verse. — Al-Zajjāj says reminder was commanded before fighting. Ibn ʿAbbās as reported by al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī call the mission reminder-only at that time; Biqāʿī locates it in an early Meccan period of forbearance. Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī explicitly say the fighting verse abrogated the rule. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BIQAI]
- 88:21/q09/p2 No; authorization to fight did not end the ban on forcing belief. — Kuranyolu rejects the abrogation claim: after fighting was allowed, the Prophet still did not force belief, and punitive action was directed at harmful agitators. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazı müfessirler)+]

## 88:21/q10 What does the reported Prophetic statement say about fighting and the profession of faith?  [* · reports]
- 88:21/q10/p1 The Prophet said he was ordered to fight until people profess God's oneness; then their lives and property are protected except by due right, and God handles their reckoning. — Ibn Kathīr reports Jābir's version with recitation of the verse through Aḥmad, Muslim, al-Tirmidhī and al-Nasāʾī; he adds that al-Bukhārī and Muslim give Abū Hurayra's version without the verse. Al-Durr reports the passage recited and its full version names Jābir, though that note omits the recitation detail. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also reports Jābir and recitation of the reminder verses. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR(جابر عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]

## 88:21/q11 Who is addressed by the closing command?  [* · referent]
- 88:21/q11/p1 The Prophet Muhammad. — Iṣlāḥī says the address could be general, but internal evidence and the closing command to remind identify the Prophet. Corpus Coranicum observes the direct address to the Messenger. Numerous commentators name Muhammad or the Prophet as recipient. [6 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; CORPUSCORANICUM; MUQATIL; TAB; QUSHAYRI; IBNASHUR]

## 88:21/q12 How do inward readings understand the practice and benefit of reminder?  [* · inward]
turns on: The inward notes emphasize different conditions: the hearer's rank, the preacher's authority and adaptation, or the soul's preparedness.
- 88:21/q12/p1 Reminder especially addresses the elect and discerning believers, and must continue for them. — Abū al-ʿAbbās ibn ʿAṭāʾ, reported by al-Sulamī, grades admonition for ordinary hearers, counsel for brethren and reminder for the elect; neglect would cause the Sunnah and obligations to lapse. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو العباس بن عطاء)]
- 88:21/q12/p2 True preaching addresses each listener according to capacity and requires authority to counsel. — Al-Junayd, reported by al-Sulamī, describes luminous counsel, takes the Prophet as its model, and requires authorization for real preaching. [1 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد)]
- 88:21/q12/p3 Reminder may help a soul prepared to ascend, while one veiled from the cause by its effects faces the great fire. — Al-Qāshānī defines that fire as punishment of absolute veiling through every level of existence. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 88:21/q13 What inward correspondences does Biqāʿī draw from the earlier creation signs?  [* · inward]
- 88:21/q13/p1 The camel corresponds to tranquil souls, the sky to luminous spirits, mountains to firm intellects and knowledge, and earth to the body and its organs. — Biqāʿī offers these as inward counterparts to the created signs. [1 notes: BIQAI]

## 88:21/q14 How does the closing command fit the sūra and its neighboring sūras?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes connect the command to different scopes: its own passage, neighboring sūras, or a broader pattern of direct address.
- 88:21/q14/p1 It concludes a sequence of the damned, the blessed and created proofs that failed to persuade. — Ibn al-Zubayr, reported by Biqāʿī, says the passage follows divine transcendence and the magnification of resurrection, then moves through the damned's ordeal (88:6), the blessed, signs (88:17), and command (88:21). [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير)]
- 88:21/q14/p2 It completes the preceding sūra's call to purification and leads into the next sūra's lesson about time and the final return. — Elmali̇li says creation's signs disclose divine ways and useful deeds before the command; the next sūra draws attention to changes in time while explaining return to God. [2 notes: ELMALILI]
- 88:21/q14/p3 Its direct address to the Prophet forms part of a developing sūra-ending pattern. — Corpus Coranicum says earlier direct addresses occur only as short consolations in sūras 93, 94 and 108; in group II, such address enters broader literary settings and becomes a regular closing feature. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 88:21/q15 What virtue is reported for reciting Sūrat al-Ghāshiya?  [* · reports]
- 88:21/q15/p1 Its reciter is promised an easy reckoning. — Al-Bayḍāwī's full version reports this from the Prophet. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
