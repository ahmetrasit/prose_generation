# 20:14: 29 questions

## 20:14/q01 How is God's first-person declaration read and pronounced?  [إِنَّنِىٓ أَنَا · readings]
turns on: Reader transmission and whether the following sound is hamzat qaṭʿ.
- 20:14/q01/p1 The first-person yāʾ is pronounced open in connected recitation. — Ibn Mujāhid lists Nāfiʿ and Abū ʿAmr among readers opening listed yāʾs, with stated exceptions; Ibn Kathīr closes لذكري إن. Ibn al-Jazarī reports the two Medinese, Ibn Kathīr and Abū ʿAmr open the yāʾ of إنني أنا الله; the phonetic variant changes no meaning. Abū Jaʿfar's inferred opening has no explicit transmission. [6 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو,ابن كثير,نافع); NASHR(المدنيان وابن كثير وأبو عمرو); QIRAAT-ER(Y-iḍ)]
- 20:14/q01/p2 The listed first-person yāʾs are read with sukūn. — Ibn Mujāhid assigns this to Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, Ibn ʿĀmir and ʿĀṣim through Abū Bakr. [1 notes: IBNMUJAHID(حمزة، الكسائي، ابن عامر، عاصم عن أبي بكر)]
- 20:14/q01/p3 The final alif of أنا is omitted in connection and retained at pause. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports consensus on this general rule, but disagreement on omission when a following hamzat qaṭʿ occurs. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 20:14/q02 Why is the divine self-identification emphatic and in the singular?  [إِنَّنِىٓ أَنَا ٱللَّهُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether emphasis is explained by Moses' situation, divine self-disclosure, or the singular form.
- 20:14/q02/p1 The emphasis magnifies and confirms the divine speaker to Moses. — Al-Zarkashī says the explicit pronoun can magnify the speaker. Ibn ʿĀshūr says inna reassures Moses amid strange news; the separating pronoun emphasizes identity, not exclusivity, and compares 5:72. Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts singular speech for oneness with plural speech for majesty, citing Ṭā Hā and Maryam. [5 notes: BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 20:14/q02/p2 The naming discloses the present speaker, while keeping the divine Essence beyond description. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares ordinary self-introduction: God already knows Moses but gives Moses His name. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the named present one can say 'I am that one,' as Joseph does in 12:90; the earlier address prepared revelation and this is the message itself. Al-Qāshānī says the shift from Lord to Allah prevents stopping at names and attributes. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; QASHANI]

## 20:14/q03 What does the name Allah convey here, and how does it relate to Hebrew naming?  [ٱللَّهُ · meaning]
turns on: The name's theological force and the proposed cross-language relation.
- 20:14/q03/p1 Allah names the necessary existent, worthy of all praise and alone perfect in the divine names. — Ibn ʿĀshūr makes knowledge of God's proper name Moses' first revealed matter and the basis of later rulings. Al-Biqāʿī says it gathers the names' meanings and only God bears their perfection. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:14/q03/p2 The Arabic Allah corresponds to Hebrew divine names, with proposed verbal and letter affinities. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites YHWH and Ehyeh in Exodus 3 and 6, later occurrences in Exodus 31:18 and 32:16, and possible translators' wording; he compares their letters with ilāh and reports a proposal of Hebrew 'lāham' whose final mīm might underlie tanwīn. Al-Wāsiṭī says God spoke Hebrew to Moses and Arabic to Muhammad without changing the divine attribute. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; SULAMI(الواسطي)]

## 20:14/q04 How does the declaration connect grammatically to the preceding revelation?  [إِنَّنِىٓ أَنَا ٱللَّهُ لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّآ أَنَا۠ · grammar]
turns on: The scope of the apposition and the internal predication.
- 20:14/q04/p1 The declaration is an apposition explaining what Moses was ordered to hear. — Al-Bayḍāwī identifies monotheism as the highest knowledge beside worship as perfected action; al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī say the foundational creed is foregrounded, though al-Ālūsī says selection was not solely for it. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it unrestricted apposition to 'what is revealed.' [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:14/q04/p2 'There is no deity except Me' is a second predicate of inna. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies this syntactic relation. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:14/q05 What does the declaration of exclusive divinity teach?  [لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّآ أَنَا۠ · theology]
turns on: Doctrinal statement and inward implications of oneness.
- 20:14/q05/p1 God alone is worthy of worship; Moses and the messengers must establish monotheism against association. — Al-Ṭabarī excludes every rival object of worship; al-Ṭabrisī adds Moses' duty to convey this to his people. Ibn Kathīr calls this the first duty of responsible persons. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it establishes Moses' knowledge. Iṣlāḥī calls it central to every messenger's mission against polytheism. Asad emphasizes God's uniqueness; the Study Quran compares God's testimony in 3:18. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:14/q05/p2 Divine unity remains one amid the plurality of attributes and manifestations. — Al-Qāshānī says the one God bears every attribute without division of unity or selfhood; al-Qushayrī says God transcends defect and likeness through majesty and beauty. [2 notes: QASHANI; QUSHAYRI]
- 20:14/q05/p3 The declaration inwardly excludes every competing object of reverence. — Al-Ḥusayn says the formula removes other gods from people; once the inner secret is freed, the declaration is no longer needed. [1 notes: SULAMI(الحسين)]

## 20:14/q06 What is meant by 'worship Me'?  [فَٱعْبُدْنِى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the imperative is glossed chiefly as monotheistic confession, all obedience, or inward servitude.
- 20:14/q06/p1 Affirm God's oneness and worship Him alone without association. — Muqātil ties it to the preceding denial of other gods. Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Bursawī demand exclusive worship. Abū Ḥayyān reports exegetes' monotheistic gloss with 51:56; al-Ālūsī also reports it. Ibn Kathīr combines affirmation and devotional acts. [9 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المفسرون); ALUSI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:14/q06/p2 Submit to and obey every divine command, in words, deeds and sincere heart. — Al-Qushayrī includes submission to decree and dominion. Al-Nasafī combines oneness and obedience. Abū Ḥayyān prefers broad commanded worship and al-Ālūsī ultimate submission. Ibn ʿĀshūr includes glorification and inward sincerity. Iṣlāḥī makes servitude renewed obedience and says obeying a rival against God, including granting religious legislation, is association. [7 notes: QUSHAYRI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:14/q06/p3 Worship is directed inwardly to the Essence and makes creatures know their need. — Al-Qāshānī directs it beyond names and attributes toward annihilation of selfhood. Al-Wāsiṭī says the command reveals creatures' helplessness, not divine need. [2 notes: QASHANI; SULAMI(الواسطي)]

## 20:14/q07 Why does the command to worship follow the declaration of divinity?  [لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّآ أَنَا۠ فَٱعْبُدْنِى · rhetoric]
- 20:14/q07/p1 The fāʾ marks worship as the consequence of God's sole divinity and exclusive right. — Al-Rāzī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly draw the consequence. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds that all things begin, subsist and return through Him. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 20:14/q08 Can the unspecified commands to worship and pray precede explanation of their forms?  [فَٱعْبُدْنِى وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · law]
turns on: Whether the form was known, explained unreported, or legitimately left for later clarification.
- 20:14/q08/p1 Yes, clarification may be delayed until the time of need. — Al-Rāzī's camp notes that neither worship's modality nor prayer's form is specified here. Rāzī rejects the claim that an unreported immediate explanation is more fitting, since explicit explanation would be more informative; prior knowledge of an older prayer cannot answer the new command to worship. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 20:14/q08/p2 Moses may already have known prayer from Shuʿayb and earlier prophets. — Al-Qāḍī offers this answer; Rāzī replies that a new prayer would add benefit and known prayer would not be in doubt. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); RAZI-FULL-]
- 20:14/q08/p3 God may have explained the prayer at once, without the Qurʾān recounting it. — Al-Qāḍī proposes this; Rāzī argues a narrated explicit explanation would have been more informative and fitting. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); RAZI-FULL-]

## 20:14/q09 What does it mean to establish prayer?  [وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · interpretation]
- 20:14/q09/p1 Perform and maintain the prayer fully and diligently. — Al-Zarkashī says complete it as commanded; Ibn ʿĀshūr says maintain it without neglect. Iṣlāḥī demands diligence and says neglect threatens the whole religion, citing 19:59. Muqātil states Moses is commanded to establish it. [5 notes: BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; MUQATIL]

## 20:14/q10 Why is prayer singled out after the general command to worship?  [فَٱعْبُدْنِى وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes emphasize prayer's rank, its inclusive practice, and its place in the sequence.
- 20:14/q10/p1 Prayer has special excellence, gathers worship, and preserves remembrance. — Abū Ḥayyān calls it the best and most useful act; al-Bursawī its special merit; al-Ālūsī says it joins heart and tongue and cites prayer called faith in 2:143. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it gathers worship's states. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it the specific after the general and best expression of servitude. Al-Biqāʿī says remembrance wards off evil and joins majesty and beauty. Iṣlāḥī calls it the greatest safeguard and communal manifestation of remembrance; al-Nasafī ranks it highest after monotheism. [12 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; NASAFI-FULL]
- 20:14/q10/p2 Its position honors servitude and marks an ascent into divine presence. — Al-Ālūsī calls prayer the sign of servitude following the honored command to worship. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:14/q11 What does 'for My remembrance' mean as the purpose or time of prayer?  [لِذِكْرِىٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whose remembrance the genitive denotes; whether lām marks purpose or time; whether dhikr means God, an owed prayer, scriptural mention, appointed time, or exclusive devotion.
- 20:14/q11/p1 Pray in order to remember God within prayer. — Mujāhid says the praying servant remembers the Lord; al-Ḥasan, al-Zajjāj and Ibn Qutayba say prayer necessarily contains remembrance, glorification or magnification. Al-Ṭabarī prefers this as the apparent wording: a remembered prayer would be 'its remembrance.' Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this contextually, with purposive lām and object genitive. Al-Bursawī includes tongue, heart and limbs; Ibn ʿĀshūr includes intimate standing before God, mental or verbal remembrance, and piety through 29:45. Asad calls conscious oneness prayer's deepest purpose. Iṣlāḥī stresses preservation of remembrance; Study Quran says frequent remembrance in prayer. Al-Ālūsī says this is the apparent attachment to 'establish.' [43 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن مجاهد عن أبيه); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); THALABI(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(الحسن ومجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(قول); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî)+; TAB-FULL+; TABATABAI+; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة)+]
- 20:14/q11/p2 Pray when a missed prayer comes to mind, even outside its appointed time. — Ibrāhīm and many commentators take remembrance as recall of the owed prayer; al-Zajjāj says forgetfulness is excused unless deliberately distracted and prayer is performed even at sunrise or sunset. Al-Ṭabrisī attributes the view to most commentators and Abū Jaʿfar, even if its time has not arrived. Ibn Kathīr prefers it on hadith evidence. The lām can mean 'at,' as in 17:78; explanatory ellipses supply 'My prayer,' or make remembering prayer lead to remembering God. Al-Ṭabarī notes al-Zuhrī's reading could support it if widely transmitted, but rejects it as primary sense. Al-Ālūsī says the hadith does not compel this literal interpretation; some verifiers see make-up as an implication of prayer's general purpose. [27 notes: TAB-FULL(إبراهيم); ZAJJAJ(الناس); NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); THALABI(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(العامة); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(أكثر المفسرين وأبو جعفر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأكثرون); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو جعفر,بعض المحققين); TABATABAI(أكثر المفسرين وأبو جعفر,قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قيل)+; TAB-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:14/q11/p3 Pray so that God remembers the worshipper with praise or reward. — Al-Naḥḥās, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī report this. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī say the verbal noun can be construed actively or passively; al-Bursawī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī make its pronoun the subject. [13 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قول); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 20:14/q11/p4 Pray because God mentioned and commanded prayer in scripture. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī give this subject-genitive explanation. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 20:14/q11/p5 Pray at appointed times of remembrance, the prayer times. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī cite 4:103; al-Zamakhsharī supports temporal lām by ordinary speech and 89:24. Al-Ālūsī compares an expression for five nights passing; Ibn ʿĀshūr takes lām temporally and also connects verbal praise and the wisdom of set times. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 20:14/q11/p6 Pray with remembrance devoted solely to God, free of display or another aim. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī give exclusive sincerity. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports both a restriction even excluding hope of reward or fear of punishment and objections that the wording entails no such restriction. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 20:14/q11/p7 Sustain remembrance of God through prayer without heedlessness. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī cite the sincere whom trade does not distract in 24:37; al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī likewise require continuity. Al-Ālūsī distinguishes prayer as cause of continuing remembrance, citing 2:45; Iṣlāḥī says prayer protects remembrance. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:14/q11/p8 Pray to God alone, unlike idolaters. — Abū Muslim explicitly gives this alternative; al-Zamakhsharī relates divine remembrance to worship and prayer directed only to Him. [2 notes: TABRISI(أبو مسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 20:14/q12 What does 'for My remembrance' modify, and how is its genitive parsed?  [لِذِكْرِىٓ · grammar]
turns on: Attachment to establish, worship, or listen; and whether the pronoun acts as subject or object.
- 20:14/q12/p1 It modifies 'establish prayer'; My is the object of the verbal noun. — Al-Ālūsī calls attachment to aqim apparent; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers it and reads 'your remembrance of Me.' Al-Samīn explains object-genitive. Ibn ʿĀshūr's purposive reading concurs. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 20:14/q12/p2 It modifies prayer, but My is the subject: God mentions prayer or remembers the worshipper. — Al-Samīn and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī identify subject-genitive readings; al-Qurṭubī and al-Ālūsī permit subject or object according to sense. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:14/q12/p3 It attaches to the preceding 'worship Me' or governs worship and prayer together. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports attachment to faʿbudnī. Al-Ālūsī allows shared governance by extending remembrance to inward and bodily acts, but calls it less apparent. [2 notes: TABATABAI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:14/q12/p4 It refers back to the earlier command to listen to revelation and remembrance. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports this attachment. Al-Naḥḥās reports a wider purpose clause running from prayer to each soul's recompense. [2 notes: THALABI; NAHHAS(قيل)]

## 20:14/q13 Which alternative forms of 'for My remembrance' are transmitted?  [لِذِكْرِىٓ · readings]
turns on: Presence of the article, feminine alif versus masculine form, and vowel of the possessive yāʾ.
- 20:14/q13/p1 The definite feminine form li-l-dhikrā is read. — Al-Naḥḥās names Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān, Abū Rajāʾ and al-Shaʿbī; other sources name Ibn Masʿūd, Ubayy, Ibn al-Sumayfaʿ, al-Sulamī and al-Nakhaʿī. Ibn Shihāb/al-Zuhrī and a report attributed to the Prophet are also cited. The alif can mark feminine or substitute for yāʾ to suit verse endings; Ibn al-Jawzī describes two lāms and doubled dhāl. Its reading can mean 'when reminded.' [11 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(الزهري); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو عبد الرحمن وأبو رجاء والشعبي); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود وأبي بن كعب وابن السميفع); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السلمي والنخعي وأبو رجاء); SAMIN-DURR(أبو رجاء، السلمي); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة); ALUSI-FULL(السلمي والنخعي وأبو رجاء)]
- 20:14/q13/p2 The indefinite feminine form li-dhikrā is read. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī report this distinct form without the article. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); SAMIN-DURR(أبو رجاء، السلمي); ALUSI-FULL(فرقة)]
- 20:14/q13/p3 The definite masculine form li-l-dhikr is read. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī report this form. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); SAMIN-DURR(أبو رجاء، السلمي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:14/q13/p4 The ordinary possessive yāʾ is opened or left still in connection according to reader. — Ibn Mujāhid reports Ibn Kathīr's sukūn at لذكري إن, while al-Jazarī reports the two Medinese and Abū ʿAmr open it; the catalogued variant has no semantic effect. [3 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير); NASHR(المدنيان وأبو عمرو); QIRAAT-ER(Y-iḍ)]

## 20:14/q14 What is the rule for prayer missed through sleep or forgetfulness?  [وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ لِذِكْرِىٓ · law]
turns on: The scope of the Prophetic command and whether number of missed prayers limits liability.
- 20:14/q14/p1 Pray the missed prayer upon remembering or waking, as its required makeup and sole expiation. — Reports through Anas, Abū Hurayra and others quote the Prophet and cite this verse; some include sleep and say no other expiation. Al-Zajjāj and Ibrāhīm allow sunrise or sunset. Ibn ʿAbbās applies the verse to a missed night prayer; al-Durr also reports Abū Juḥayfa, Ibn Masʿūd, ʿUbāda and the post-Khaybar episode. Al-Qurṭubī says forgetting cancels sin, not duty, whether one prayer or many; Abū Hurayra's report says its time is remembrance. Ibn Kathīr prefers the phrase's missed-prayer interpretation on these reports. [35 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABDURRAZZAQ(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TAB-FULL(أبو هريرة عن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ZAJJAJ(الناس); THALABI(أنس); WAHIDI-BASIT(النبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(أنس عن النبي); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله); TABRISI(أنس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أنس عن النبي); RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة,أنس بن مالك,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو جحيفة,أبو هريرة,أنس,إبراهيم,ابن عباس,الشعبي,عبادة بن الصامت,عبد الله بن مسعود); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة); TABATABAI(أنس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Resûlullah)]
- 20:14/q14/p2 More than five forgotten prayers need not be made up. — Al-Qurṭubī calls this an anomalous view contrary to the Prophetic report requiring makeup for one or many. [0 notes: QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 20:14/q15 What does the report's 'no other expiation' mean?  [وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · reports]
turns on: Whether the phrase excludes substitutes for prayer or additional penalties for inadvertence.
- 20:14/q15/p1 Nothing clears the missed prayer except making it up. — Al-Khaṭṭābī's first reading of the report. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الخطابي)]
- 20:14/q15/p2 Forgetting adds no separate penalty beyond the prayer itself. — Al-Khaṭṭābī contrasts intentional missed Ramadan fasting and omitted pilgrimage rites, where compensation can be due. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الخطابي)]

## 20:14/q16 Does a deliberately abandoned prayer have to be made up?  [وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · law]
turns on: Whether a sinful missed obligation remains payable; how severe 'never makes it up' language is construed.
- 20:14/q16/p1 Yes; deliberate omission adds sin, but does not discharge the duty. — Al-Qurṭubī gives the majority view, argues a fortiori from sleeper and forgetful person, says the general imperative covers later performance, compares an unpaid human debt and an intentionally missed Ramadan day, and explains that God's 'forgetting' means abandoning, not literal loss of knowledge (9:67, 59:19, 2:106). He reads Mālik's contrary wording as impossibility of restoring the past or severe warning; the weak Abū al-Muṭawwis fasting report is likewise warning, while sound reports require makeup and repentance. Al-Ālūsī notes separate disagreement about whether lazy abandonment entails unbelief. [13 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو المطوس); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:14/q16/p2 No makeup is owed for deliberate abandonment. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this from Dāwūd and Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Ashʿarī via Ibn al-Qaṣṣār, and reports Mālik's severe formulation, which he construes as warning or impossibility of returning to past time. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 20:14/q17 Does makeup follow the original time-bound prayer command or a new command?  [وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · law]
turns on: Whether an expired appointed time ends the original command's scope.
- 20:14/q17/p1 It rests on the renewed Prophetic command to pray upon remembering. — Al-Qurṭubī says performance outside the appointed day or night does not fulfill the original command, citing 17:78; absent the Prophet's instruction, such prayer would gain no reward. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 20:14/q17/p2 The general command to establish prayer still entails makeup after its time. — Al-Qurṭubī also invokes the imperative's general obligation without distinguishing on-time and late performance. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 20:14/q18 Must a remembered missed prayer precede the current prayer?  [وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ لِذِكْرِىٓ · law]
turns on: Whether the verse, reports and Prophetic sequence make order obligatory; whether current time is narrow; and how many misses count as few.
- 20:14/q18/p1 Order is required for a small number of missed prayers, even at a tight current time. — Abū Ḥanīfa requires order until misses exceed a day and night; Mālik, al-Layth and al-Zuhrī begin with the missed prayer even if current time expires. Ḥanafīs take lām as 'at' in 17:78 and the report's fāʾ as immediate succession; Jābir's sound Trench report has missed ʿAṣr then Maghrib, and a four-prayer Trench report gives noon, afternoon, sunset, night. They treat Prophetic action as explaining 24:56 and analogize ordered combined prayers at ʿArafa/Muzdalifa. Al-Qurṭubī prefers Jābir over a weak contrary report, notes narrow Maghrib time, and says scholars order multiple missed prayers. Mālik counts at most five as few; some count at most four; six are many. [13 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو حنيفة,الحنفية,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,جابر بن عبد الله); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة بن عبد الله بن مسعود,مالك); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 20:14/q18/p2 Start with the current prayer when its time is tight; ordering missed prayers is recommended but not a validity condition. — Al-Shāfiʿī, al-Ḥasan, hadith jurists, al-Muḥāsibī and Ibn Wahb hold this. Al-Shāfiʿī says ample time favors makeup first, but current prayer may be finished if recollection occurs during it; repetition is only recommended. He cites Abū Qatāda's delayed Fajr makeup to show no narrow makeup window and argues a validity condition would not lapse through forgetfulness, unlike mistaken early Ẓuhr at ʿArafa. A report ascribed to Ibn ʿAbbās would support starting current prayer, but its transmitter is unknown. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو قتادة,الشافعي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن والشافعي)]
- 20:14/q18/p3 The worshipper may choose either current or missed prayer first. — Al-Qurṭubī attributes this third view to Ashhab. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أشهب)]

## 20:14/q19 What if a missed prayer is recalled during a current congregational prayer?  [وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · law]
turns on: Whether remembered order invalidates an ongoing prayer and whether repetition is obligatory.
- 20:14/q19/p1 The follower finishes with the imam, then makes up the missed prayer and repeats the current prayer when required. — Ibn ʿUmar gives this sequence; al-Qurṭubī says raising it to the Prophet was erroneous. Abū Ḥanīfa and Aḥmad require repetition unless more than five prayers intervene; Aḥmad waives repetition when the current time would expire. Al-Qurṭubī says all agree the follower first completes the imam's prayer. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عمر); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة وأحمد بن حنبل,أحمد بن حنبل,ابن عمر)]
- 20:14/q19/p2 Finish the current prayer, make up the missed one, and regard repetition as recommended only. — Al-Shāfiʿī holds the current prayer valid even when recollection occurs during it. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الشافعي)]
- 20:14/q19/p3 The current prayer is invalidated on recollection unless time is too short. — Abū Ḥanīfa gives this condition. Mālik's apparent view ends after two rakʿahs and invalidates an imam and congregation; al-Qurṭubī rejects that appearance because Mālik elsewhere has the worshipper complete three or four units as a voluntary prayer. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); QURTUBI-FULL(مالك); QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 20:14/q20 Must a prayer made up after sleep be repeated at its usual time the next day?  [وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · law]
turns on: How to reconcile the additional next-day wording with sound reports accepting one makeup.
- 20:14/q20/p1 No; one makeup suffices. — Al-Qurṭubī prefers this from the sound ʿImrān report: the Prophet's party prayed after sunrise and he said God accepts repayment; sound versions omit an extra instruction. Al-Khaṭṭābī knows no scholar requiring repetition and treats a contrary wording as possible recommendation for the virtue of the appointed time. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الخطابي,عمران بن حصين); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 20:14/q20/p2 Also pray the missed prayer at the corresponding time the next day. — Abū Qatāda's report distinguishes sleep from negligent delay and includes prayer next day at its time; an ʿImrān report adds makeup alongside the next morning prayer. Al-Qurṭubī regards Abū Qatāda's wording as ambiguous and the added ʿImrān instruction absent from sound versions. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو قتادة,عمران بن حصين); QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 20:14/q21 What reports describe the Prophet's missed prayers and their performance?  [وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · reports]
turns on: Different transmitted episodes and the number of prayers missed.
- 20:14/q21/p1 After the Trench, the Prophet made up ʿAṣr after sunset, then prayed Maghrib. — Jābir's report is in both Ṣaḥīḥ collections and is preferred by al-Qurṭubī as explicit evidence of order; ʿUmar also had missed ʿAṣr. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 20:14/q21/p2 At the Trench, the Prophet made up four prayers in order: noon, afternoon, sunset, night. — Al-Tirmidhī reports this through Abū ʿUbayda from his father. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة بن عبد الله بن مسعود)]
- 20:14/q21/p3 The Prophet's party slept past sunrise, moved, and made up dawn prayer. — Abū Qatāda reports moving the mounts before prayer; ʿImrān reports two rakʿahs and Fajr after travel. Abū Hurayra describes a post-Khaybar night when Bilāl slept on watch, then the party moved, performed ablution and prayed. Ibn Masʿūd gives a return-from-Hudaybiyya episode; Abū Juḥayfa reports God returning their souls. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(عمران بن حصين); DURR-FULL(أبو جحيفة,أبو هريرة,عبد الله بن مسعود); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة)]

## 20:14/q22 How does this declaration of oneness relate to salvation and ʿUmar's conversion?  [لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّآ أَنَا۠ · reports]
turns on: Distinct reports about the declaration's reception and virtue.
- 20:14/q22/p1 Sincere testimony to God's oneness promises safety from punishment. — Ibn ʿAbbās reports a gate of Paradise bearing the promise; ʿAlī transmits a divine report through Gabriel describing the testimony as entry to God's fortress. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,علي بن أبي طالب)]
- 20:14/q22/p2 ʿUmar embraced Islam after reading Ṭā Hā including this declaration. — Anas recounts the event and Khabbāb's recollection of the Prophet's prayer that Islam be strengthened through ʿUmar or ʿAmr ibn Hishām. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أنس)]

## 20:14/q23 What inward experience is sought through remembrance and prayer?  [وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ لِذِكْرِىٓ · inward]
turns on: Whether the experience is described as enduring presence or direct witnessing beyond attributes.
- 20:14/q23/p1 Remain wholly present with God in proper adab and intimate communion. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ warns against heedlessness in turning to God. Al-Wāsiṭī says no deed or word should displace Him from the heart and one should transcend passing times. Al-Qushayrī says witnessing another as the true establisher opens intimate nearness, while praying unaware of the Originator yields self-admiration. Al-Bursawī cites lasting converse, theophany and annihilation; Rūzbihān describes the servant becoming remembrance amid disclosure of divine secrets and beauty. [6 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الواسطي); QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); STUDYQURAN(Rūzbihān al-Baqlī)]
- 20:14/q23/p2 Prayer is spiritual witnessing of the Essence beyond the heart's remembrance of attributes. — Al-Qāshānī distinguishes essential witnessing from presence of heart with divine attributes; al-Qushayrī says forms and traces vanish before the Real's existence. [2 notes: QASHANI; QUSHAYRI]

## 20:14/q24 How is this verse ordered with the surrounding address and wider prophetic teaching?  [* · links]
turns on: How broadly the surrounding revelation is included in the sequence.
- 20:14/q24/p1 Knowledge of oneness comes first, then worship and prayer, then the Hereafter and mission. — Al-Rāzī says doctrine precedes practical branches and lists six charges to Moses: oneness, worship/prayer, Hereafter, wisdom of God's acts, signs and mission. Al-Bursawī makes creed prior to practice. Al-Ālūsī maps beginning, middle and return across 20:14–15; Ibn ʿĀshūr traces command, Hour, warning and ruin. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees monotheism and resurrection explicit, prophecy implicit, worship condensing practice; he locates the mission between the staff question and Pharaoh command and compares 19:51. Iṣlāḥī identifies three founding duties given to all messengers. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:14/q24/p2 The sequence begins in unity and ends in prayerful obedience. — Al-Wāsiṭī emphasizes the move from divine oneness to the language of obedience. [1 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي)]

## 20:14/q25 What is said about prayer in Moses' scriptural legacy?  [وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · links]
turns on: Whether Torah prayer references preserve Mosaic instruction or later editing.
- 20:14/q25/p1 Moses was directed first to prayer after monotheism, though later Jews neglected prayer and the Hereafter. — Iṣlāḥī aligns this with prophetic history, notes Torah reference to temple prayer only once in Deuteronomy 26, and says Jewish scriptures speak more often of sacrifice. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:14/q25/p2 Some Jewish jurists say Moses never commanded prayer and later Torah editors added its mention. — Iṣlāḥī reports this view without endorsing it. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(their jurists)]

## 20:14/q26 What adjacent reading and message introduce Moses' reception of this verse?  [* · readings]
turns on: Variant wording in the preceding address, with no substantive disagreement in meaning according to al-Ṭabarī.
- 20:14/q26/p1 The doubled-nūn, open-alif reading links selection to the earlier call to Moses. — Al-Ṭabarī attributes this reading of the preceding selection statement to the general Kufan readers and accepts it as sound. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(عامة قراء الكوفة); TAB-FULL+]
- 20:14/q26/p2 The light-nūn reading states God's selection of Moses. — Al-Ṭabarī attributes it to most Medinan and Basran readers and some Kufans, accepts both readings because their meanings agree, and says Moses was chosen for the message, then told to listen, retain and act. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(عامة قراء المدينة والبصرة وبعض أهل الكوفة); TAB-FULL+]

## 20:14/q27 What contextual linguistic principles do the cited discussions bring to this passage?  [* · links]
turns on: These notes discuss different linguistic and theological context rather than competing answers.
- 20:14/q27/p1 Figurative expressions retain a connection to their literal source. — Al-Jurjānī warns that forcing a literal hand into al-Shammākh's right-hand phrase makes it trivial; al-Khansāʾ's hand toward glory corresponds to receiving its banner. Severing figurative names from their source breeds strained readings; 50:37 depicts incomprehension as deprivation of a heart, not a bare synonym for intellect, as with sight and hearing. [4 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 20:14/q27/p2 The preceding divine istiwāʾ is best understood as power. — Al-Jishumī prefers Abū ʿAlī's interpretation and calls the Kullābī act-or-attribute account unintelligible. [1 notes: JISHUMI+]
- 20:14/q27/p3 A pronoun of affair needs explanation by a clause, and differing sentence moods can be conjoined. — Al-Zarkashī says an otherwise unexplained impersonal pronoun needs a following word or clause; the pronoun of affair takes a clause, unlike singular pronouns of niʿma and biʾsa. He reports Ibn Kharūf, al-Ṣaffār and Ibn ʿAmr permitting conjunction of imperative, report and prohibition. [3 notes: BURHAN(ابن خروف والصفار وابن عمرو)]

## 20:14/q28 Which early explanations of the prayer command are transmitted?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether remembrance denotes God during prayer or recollection of an owed prayer.
- 20:14/q28/p1 Mujāhid says prayer brings remembrance of the Lord. — Al-Suyūṭī transmits Mujāhid's concise formulation; Ibn Kathīr reports the same interpretation without naming its proponent. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قيل)]
- 20:14/q28/p2 Ibrāhīm says to pray upon remembering an omitted prayer. — Al-Suyūṭī transmits this early interpretation. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(إبراهيم)]

## 20:14/q29 How does divine reality relate to beginnings and passing time?  [* · inward]
- 20:14/q29/p1 Beginnings, ends, times and epochs limit creatures but not God's reality. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ draws this inward implication from the verse. [1 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
