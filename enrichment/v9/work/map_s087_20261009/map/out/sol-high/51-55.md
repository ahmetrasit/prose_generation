# 51:55: 12 questions

## 51:55/q01 What does the command to remind ask the Prophet to convey?  [وَذَكِّرْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the imperative is explained as general admonition, its Qur'anic medium, the things recalled, or instruction fitted to the hearer.
- 51:55/q01/p1 Admonish and exhort people, continuing the work of guidance. — Mujahid glosses the imperative as admonition (Tabari; Mawardi; Durr); Nahhas and Ibn al-Jawzi likewise say admonish. Muqatil, Tabari, Baydawi, Bursevi, Alusi, Elmalili, Asad and Ṭabaṭabaʾi stress that reminder continues. Biqaʿi adds gentleness. Islahi says to guide willing listeners after the Quraysh leaders proved immovable. [18 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 51:55/q01/p2 Remind through the Qur'an. — Qatada gives this medium in Mawardi and Qurtubi; Muqatil applies it to Meccan disbelievers in Wahidi and Baghawi; al-Kalbi applies it to believers in Tabrisi. Ali identifies the reminder as the Qur'an in Durr. Nasafi and Study Quran also identify Qur'anic admonition or preaching. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); TABRISI(الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(علي); STUDYQURAN; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)+]
- 51:55/q01/p3 Recall God's days, punishment, retribution and mercy, including reward and punishment. — Zajjaj specifies God's days and punishment, retribution and mercy; Ibn al-Jawzi transmits Zajjaj's punishment and mercy gloss; Qurtubi reports punishment and God's days. Mawardi says reward and punishment move believers by hope and fear. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 51:55/q01/p4 Recall God's generosity, favors and care that preceded human religious attainment. — Jaʿfar al-Sadiq says primordial care preceded faith, knowledge, certainty, obedience and protection from sin. [1 notes: SULAMI(جعفر الصادق)]
- 51:55/q01/p5 Shape the reminder to spiritual rank and duty. — Ibn Ata distinguishes ordinary people's admonition, companions' counsel and the elect's reminder, and says discerning believers need reminder lest Sunna and obligations lapse. Bursevi's cited Sufi glosses mention spared afflictions for gnostics, reward for the obedient, punishment for sinners and renewed witnessing of divine beauty and majesty for lovers. [3 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 51:55/q02 Whom is the Prophet told to remind?  [وَذَكِّرْ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'remind' retains a general audience after 51:54, is restricted to believers, or targets future believers and receptive listeners.
- 51:55/q02/p1 The people addressed by his mission, including believers and people who may yet respond. — Tabari says those to whom Muhammad was sent. Study Quran reports an audience of both believers and disbelievers. Muqatil and Asad speak generally of continuing to remind; Elmalili says some reminders bring people to faith. The reading preserves an open audience while specifying believers as beneficiaries. [5 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 51:55/q02/p2 The believers among his people. — Al-Kalbi gives this restriction in Wahidi, Baghawi and Tabrisi; Study Quran reports a believers-only reading. Bursevi says believers still need instruction despite unbelievers' obstinacy. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); TABRISI(الكلبي); BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)-]
- 51:55/q02/p3 Meccan unbelievers, especially those destined to believe, are to be warned with the Qur'an. — Muqatil gives this reading in Wahidi and Baghawi. Wahidi prefers it because it fits the preceding verse better; its benefit is for those God knows will believe. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)+]
- 51:55/q02/p4 Those willing to listen or seeking faith, while the obstinate Quraysh leaders are left alone. — Islahi says the Prophet had pursued the leaders because their conversion might strengthen the new faith and draw others; after conclusive delivery showed them immovable, God stopped that pursuit. He should guide responders patiently; God supports him and his companions. Tabatabai says disputing rebels increases transgression and disbelief. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]

## 51:55/q03 How is the object of 'remind' understood grammatically?  [وَذَكِّرْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the imperative is treated as intransitive or as taking a contextually omitted object.
- 51:55/q03/p1 The imperative is used intransitively to command persistent reminding and admonition. — Alusi explicitly treats it as intransitive while allowing an understood object; Bursevi also takes the imperative as continued reminder. Both connect it to not abandoning admonition. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 51:55/q03/p2 An obvious object, 'them,' is omitted or understood: remind them. — Bursevi gives this as an alternative and also mentions an implied pronoun in his continuing-admonition account; Alusi reports omission because the object is evident and allows it even alongside his intransitive analysis. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 51:55/q04 What is the relation between 'reminder' and 'remembrance' in Arabic?  [ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · grammar]
- 51:55/q04/p1 Al-dhikrā and al-dhikr are equivalent, allowing a masculine or feminine verb. — Nahhas supplies the equivalence and its grammatical consequence. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 51:55/q05 Who are 'the believers' who benefit?  [ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the designation describes present faith, faith known by God in advance, or openness to faith.
- 51:55/q05/p1 Those who already believe. — Mawardi and Qurtubi emphasize existing believers' hope, fear and receptivity; Nasafi says their deeds increase. Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi explicitly offer current believers as one referent. Biqaʿi describes people deeply rooted in faith by God's decree. [16 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); TABRISI(الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 51:55/q05/p2 Those God knows or has destined will believe, including future believers. — Muqatil specifies future Meccan believers; Ibn Atiyya includes those who join believers later. Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi state this alternative; Abu Hayyan says those God will guide, Biqaʿi those destined for felicity, and Kuran Yolu cites Zamakhshari for those God knows will believe. Elmalili notes that reminder sometimes brings people to faith. [10 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)+]
- 51:55/q05/p3 People receptive to guidance and seeking faith. — Islahi contrasts listeners with rebellious leaders; Kuran Yolu says receptive people may attain faith and believer-like conduct. Tabatabai contrasts believers who benefit with rebels who only increase in transgression through dispute. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]

## 51:55/q06 How does reminder benefit its hearers?  [ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ تَنفَعُ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes describe different beneficiaries and effects: growth in existing faith, entry into faith, moral motivation, recognition of faith, or rank-specific remembrance.
- 51:55/q06/p1 It renews and deepens believers' faith, knowledge, certainty and conduct. — Baydawi says insight grows; Nasafi says deeds grow; Bursevi and Alusi say insight and certainty increase and hard hearts soften. Biqaʿi says repetition overcomes desire, competing interests and forgetfulness. Elmalili lists renewed memory and faith, freedom from heedlessness, joy and teaching the uninformed. Kuran Yolu adds conduct; Study Quran adds spiritual insight, certainty and knowledge; Ibn Kathir specifies believing hearts. [11 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 51:55/q06/p2 It can bring those destined or receptive to believe to faith. — Muqatil says the Qur'anic warning benefits Meccans God already knew would believe. Ibn Atiyya includes later entrants; Abu Hayyan says God will guide some. Abu Jaʿfar ibn al-Zubayr, reported by Biqaʿi with 6:36, says those destined for felicity respond. Elmalili and Kuran Yolu describe conversion through reminder. [7 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)+]
- 51:55/q06/p3 News of reward and warnings of punishment prompt hope, fear and repentance. — Mawardi states hope and fear. Bursevi reports the obedient hear news of abundant reward and sinners return after warning of punishment. Zajjaj and Qurtubi include punishment and mercy among reminder's subjects. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
- 51:55/q06/p4 Its effect in the heart identifies a believer, who remembers after lapses. — Salman ibn Habib says someone who feels reminder work in the heart should recognize himself as a believer. Bursevi cites a hadith that believers repeatedly lapse into a familiar sin because they are forgetful and tested, yet remember when reminded. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(سلمان بن حبيب المحاربي); BURSEVI]
- 51:55/q06/p5 For spiritual ranks it recalls distinct gifts and experiences. — Bursevi reports reminders of spared afflictions for gnostics, reward for the obedient, return from sin for sinners, and renewed witnessing of divine beauty and majesty for lovers. Ibn Ata distinguishes admonition, counsel and reminder by rank and says it preserves Sunna and obligations. [3 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 51:55/q07 How does 'and remind' relate to the preceding command to turn away?  [وَذَكِّرْ · links]
turns on: Whether 51:55 qualifies withdrawal by preserving useful admonition or supersedes the withdrawal order.
- 51:55/q07/p1 The Prophet should continue admonition after turning away from obstinate opponents. — Baydawi and Bursevi say admonition must not be abandoned; Tabatabai says disputation with rebels increases transgression and disbelief while believers benefit. Islahi says ignore conceited Quraysh leaders after conclusive delivery, then guide willing listeners; he says God supports the Prophet and companions. Durr transmits Mujahid's sequence of withdrawal then admonition. Wahidi prefers Muqatil's Meccan warning because it fits the preceding verse. [11 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)+]
- 51:55/q07/p2 The reminder command reverses or abrogates the preceding withdrawal command. — Alusi reports Ibn Abbas: God had ordered Muhammad to withdraw from rejecters so they would be punished and had excused him; the subsequent reminder command abrogated that order. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 51:55/q08 What was reported to happen when the command to turn away was followed by this verse?  [* · reports]
- 51:55/q08/p1 The withdrawal command distressed believers or the Prophet; the command to remind reassured them. — Ali reports companions thought the Messenger had been ordered to leave them; Qatada says they feared revelation had ceased and punishment was imminent. Ibn Abbas, with Muqatil and Qatada in Wahidi, says the Prophet feared punishment for his people while companions feared cessation and punishment. Baghawi's exegetes add that the Prophet was saddened. Abu Hayyan and Alusi transmit Ali's fear that revelation had ended or destruction was certain; Alusi also transmits Qatada's fear that punishment had arrived. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(علي,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(المفسرون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي); ALUSI-FULL(علي,قتادة)]

## 51:55/q09 Is the instruction connected with abrogation?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The notes differ over whether the ongoing reminder stands, whether a later sword verse overrides an apparent truce, or whether this verse overrides 51:54.
- 51:55/q09/p1 This verse is not abrogated when reminder includes future believers. — Ibn Atiyya explicitly ties the continuing benefit to believers and later entrants. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 51:55/q09/p2 Its apparent truce was abrogated by the sword verse. — Abu Hayyan holds this; Alusi reports the same view from al-Bahr. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البحر)]
- 51:55/q09/p3 The reminder command abrogated the earlier order to withdraw. — Alusi reports Ibn Abbas's sequence: withdrawal from rejecters preceded this renewed reminder command. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 51:55/q10 What does human and divine remembrance imply here?  [ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · inward]
- 51:55/q10/p1 People forget, whereas God neither forgets nor is ignorant; God remembers a person who remembers Him. — Jaʿfar distinguishes human forgetfulness from divine knowledge and remembrance. [1 notes: SULAMI(جعفر)]

## 51:55/q11 How is this verse connected with the call to prayer?  [* · links]
- 51:55/q11/p1 The call's statement that prayer is better than sleep is associated with beneficial reminder. — Burhan pairs it with the warning not to turn away while hearing, citing 8:20. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 51:55/q12 Where does the Prophet's reward lie in this work?  [* · interpretation]
- 51:55/q12/p1 His reward lies in reminding people and calling them to God. — Abu Jaʿfar ibn al-Zubayr, reported by Biqaʿi, adds that reminders benefit believers and people destined for felicity respond; his discussion mentions 6:36. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير)]
