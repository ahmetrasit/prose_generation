# 21:32: 18 questions

## 21:32/q01 What does جعلنا mean in this construction?  [وَجَعَلْنَا · grammar]
- 21:32/q01/p1 It can express creation even with two objects. — Al-Zarkashi says Arabic جعل with one object means create, while with two it can mean create, name, or transform; the two-object form alone does not establish creation. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 21:32/q02 What does السماء designate here?  [ٱلسَّمَآءَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the sky is read cosmically or as an inward faculty.
- 21:32/q02/p1 The visible heaven above earth, including celestial space. — Kur'an Yolu understands the protected roof as both Earth's atmosphere and cosmic space containing galaxies, solar systems and stars formed from the nebula's fragmentation; Asad calls it a secure canopy. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES]
- 21:32/q02/p2 The rational faculty in an inward reading. — Al-Qashani reads the raised, protected sky as reason, guarded from change, forgetfulness and error, while people ignore its proofs. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 21:32/q03 Why is the sky singular?  [ٱلسَّمَآءَ · rhetoric]
- 21:32/q03/p1 The singular is generic and evokes the one roof visible to people. — Al-Biqa'i says most people observe only the lowest heaven; one protected roof also conveys its preservation. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 21:32/q04 Why call the sky سقفا, a roof?  [سَقْفًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the image emphasizes its raised roof shape or the wave imagery in transmitted descriptions.
- 21:32/q04/p1 It arches or stands over the earth as a house roof stands over a house. — Mujahid and al-Tabari call it raised. Al-Wahidi cites 52:5 and the house analogy; al-Razi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Bursawi, al-Biqa'i and the Study Quran likewise use roof or canopy comparisons. Abu Hayyan says anything raised and arched over something is a roof. Ibn Ashur specifies a roof laid on house walls, as a direct simile, and cites the absence of pillars in 13:2. Ibn Kathir describes a dome, citing 51:47, 91:5 and 50:6, and compares Arab tent construction and the Prophet's 'Islam built on five' image. Al-Biqa'i adds that this roof contains light, guidance signs and ornaments. Asad compares 13:2. [18 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 21:32/q04/p2 It is a restrained wave. — Qatada calls it a restrained or contained wave; transmitted wording varies. A reported Prophetic description likewise calls it a restrained wave running like an arrow. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 21:32/q05 Why is سقفا singular if the skies are plural?  [سَقْفًۭا · rhetoric]
- 21:32/q05/p1 The heavens together form one roof over earth. — Al-Farra' compares them with a house roof. [1 notes: FARRA]

## 21:32/q06 What does محفوظا grammatically modify?  [سَقْفًۭا مَّحْفُوظًۭا · grammar]
- 21:32/q06/p1 The masculine participle modifies سقف; a feminine form could instead modify السماء. — Al-Farra' compares the masculine/feminine variation تغشى/يغشى in 3:154; al-Nahhas states both agreement possibilities. Al-Jishumi adds that the later 'its signs' refers to the sky. [3 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS; JISHUMI]

## 21:32/q07 In what sense is the roof protected?  [مَّحْفُوظًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Which threat or quality 'protected' specifies: devils, falling, deterioration, human reach, moral defilement, or general/cosmic security; al-Razi also disputes the means of guarding against devils.
- 21:32/q07/p1 It is guarded from devils and their eavesdropping by stars or shooting meteors. — Muqatil says devils cannot overhear angels and relay it to people. Yahya, Ibn Abbas via al-Wahidi and Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Farra', al-Zajjaj, al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi, al-Nasafi, al-Jubba'i, and others cite 15:17 or 37:6–7. Al-Tabari calls them rejected devils. Al-Tabataba'i prefers this sense. Al-Alusi objects that the roof image then seems idle; the reply compares thieves climbing earthly roofs. Al-Razi favors angels instead, reasoning that stars need not stop harm from stolen hearing. [27 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ(قيل); MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، الكلبي، الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Some exegetes); STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI+; RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 21:32/q07/p2 God keeps it from falling upon earth, despite lacking supporting pillars. — Al-Zajjaj and al-Wahidi specify 'except by God's permission'; al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi and al-Nasafi cite 22:65. Al-Razi also cites 30:25, 35:41 and 2:255; al-Tabrisi cites 35:41. Al-Kashshaf adds quaking. Ibn Ashur stresses the blessing: celestial bodies or their parts do not fall and destroy people or land. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES]
- 21:32/q07/p3 It remains intact, free from decay, collapse, corruption or alteration until its appointed end. — Al-Wahidi contrasts ordinary roofs' long-term deterioration; al-Hasan says no breach, decay or collapse. Qatada says time does not alter it; al-Alusi says this accords with eventual folding at Resurrection. Al-Baydawi and al-Bursawi qualify the preservation as lasting until the appointed time. Elmalili cites 35:41. Islahi calls it ancient, flawless, free of fissures and cites 50:6, 67:2–4, 17:99 and 79:27–33. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(غيره); TABRISI(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 21:32/q07/p4 Its height keeps it beyond human reach, breach, demolition and other harms. — Mujahid glosses 'protected' as 'raised'; al-Wahidi explains that height keeps demolition and artifice from reaching it. Al-Hasan mentions attempted breach. Ibn Kathir says guarded against being reached. Ibn Atiyya takes preservation broadly, from devils, projectiles and other harms; al-Qurtubi lists human devices and breach. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 21:32/q07/p5 Angels guard it from devils. — Al-Razi reports both angel and star accounts and prefers angels because this magnifies divine guardianship and stars need not prevent damage from jinn stealing a hearing. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL+]
- 21:32/q07/p6 It is protected from idolatry and sins. — Al-Mawardi, al-Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan transmit this sense. Al-Alusi rejects it, invoking his objection to a prior interpretation. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 21:32/q07/p7 It is protected from those below it, thereby protecting them from it. — Al-Alusi reports this further interpretation and says it implies complete safety for people beneath the sky. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:32/q07/p8 Its ordered cosmic system is secured by centrifugal force and mutual gravitational attraction. — Kur'an Yolu describes space as created in divine order and balance, relates this to the preservation of heaven and earth in 35:41, and identifies atmosphere and cosmic space as the protected roof. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 21:32/q07/p9 Its preservation is general rather than limited to one danger. — Ibn Atiyya includes devils, projectiles and other harms. Al-Biqa'i combines divine prevention of falling with meteor protection from devils; Asad calls it a well-secured canopy. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 21:32/q08 What reports describe the roof as a restrained wave?  [سَقْفًۭا مَّحْفُوظًۭا · reports]
turns on: Whether the description is Qatada's report or the report attributed to the Prophet; its chain and force as an explanation are disputed.
- 21:32/q08/p1 Qatada calls it a protected raised roof and a restrained or contained wave. — Yahya's wording is 'protected roof and restrained wave'; al-Tabari's is 'raised roof and contained wave.' [2 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة)]
- 21:32/q08/p2 A report through Ibn Abbas attributes a raised, restrained, moving roof protected from devils to the Prophet. — Abu Hayyan says this directly explains the verse if sound. Ibn Kathir calls the chain unusual and summarizes it as a wave held back from people. Al-Alusi says that even if sound it is not explicit proof of the verse's meaning, against Abu Hayyan. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 21:32/q09 Who are وهم?  [وَهُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the third-person plural singles out unbelievers or describes the wider heedless audience.
- 21:32/q09/p1 The polytheists or unbelievers, especially the Meccan unbelievers. — Al-Wahidi reports Ibn Abbas's identification; Ibn al-Jawzi specifies Meccans. Al-Tabari says polytheists, while al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi, al-Nasafi and the Study Quran say unbelievers or idolaters. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 21:32/q09/p2 People generally who are heedless of the signs. — Yahya speaks of people who see without reflecting; al-Biqa'i says most people, while al-Alusi calls them heedless and Islahi says their eyes are shut. [5 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 21:32/q10 What are the sky's signs?  [ءَايَٰتِهَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the signs are named heavenly bodies, a wider range of phenomena, or the sky taken together as one sign.
- 21:32/q10/p1 The sun, moon and stars, including other celestial bodies and their motions. — Mujahid and many later transmitters give sun, moon and stars; al-Qurtubi reports Mujahid as sun and moon. Al-Kashshaf adds courses, risings and settings; al-Razi adds directions, conjunctions and separations. Abu Hayyan includes timed motions; Ibn Kathir mentions fixed and wandering stars and the sun's immense circuit. Al-Razi connects their orbits and seasonal effects to day, night and benefits. [28 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن مجاهد عن أبيه); FARRA; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي وغيره); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 21:32/q10/p2 The signs also include weather and all visible or investigated conditions of the sky. — Ibn Atiyya lists rain, thunder, lightning and thunderbolts; Ibn Ashur adds clouds, lightning and thunder. Al-Biqa'i includes winds and rains. Al-Baydawi says some conditions are sensed and others studied in natural philosophy and astronomy; al-Kashshaf and Abu Hayyan describe ordered heavenly motions. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 21:32/q10/p3 The sky as a created whole, or any thing in it, is itself a sign. — Al-Zajjaj says a coordinated set can count as one āya because it points to oneness. Singular-reading explanations by al-Farra', al-Zajjaj, Ibn al-Jawzi, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin and al-Alusi variously treat heaven and its contents as one sign, any part as sufficient, or singular as a generic collective. Al-Tabrisi emphasizes signs of createdness and dependence. [8 notes: FARRA(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد والفراء); TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(مجاهد وحميد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد وحميد)]

## 21:32/q11 How is آياتها read and understood?  [ءَايَٰتِهَا · readings]
turns on: Whether the word is plural or singular; the singular can name the whole sky or serve as a generic collective.
- 21:32/q11/p1 The plural آياتها names the sky's many signs. — Abu Hayyan says most reciters read the plural. Al-Farra' says both singular and plural readings are valid; the many bodies and phenomena described by the commentators fit the plural. [2 notes: FARRA(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 21:32/q11/p2 Mujahid and Humayd read singular آيتها, as one collective or generic sign. — Al-Farra' and Ibn al-Jawzi say the sky and its contents make one sign. Al-Zajjaj calls the sky the greatest sign and says a collection may be one āya. Al-Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, al-Razi and al-Samin allow a generic collective; al-Alusi says each thing suffices to prove a Creator. [10 notes: FARRA(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد والفراء); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(مجاهد وحميد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد وحميد)]

## 21:32/q12 Why are the signs attributed to the sky?  [ءَايَٰتِهَا · rhetoric]
- 21:32/q12/p1 They are located in it, though God is their maker. — Al-Qurtubi contrasts their attribution here to heaven with their attribution elsewhere to God. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; JISHUMI]

## 21:32/q13 How does the closing clause function grammatically?  [وَهُمْ عَنْ ءَايَٰتِهَا مُعْرِضُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether وهم begins a new sentence or gives a circumstantial state.
- 21:32/q13/p1 It is a new sentence. — Al-Samin says a prospective circumstantial analysis is weak. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 21:32/q13/p2 It is a circumstantial clause. — Ibn Ashur analyzes it as describing the state accompanying God's making the sky. Al-Samin judges a prospective circumstantial reading weak. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 21:32/q14 What does turning away from the signs involve, and what should they show?  [عَنْ ءَايَٰتِهَا مُعْرِضُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the failure is general lack of reflection, selective attention to worldly use, or a spiritual failure to remember God.
- 21:32/q14/p1 People fail to contemplate the signs that prove one Creator's existence, power, wisdom and exclusive divinity. — Muqatil, al-Tabari, al-Wahidi and al-Qurtubi stress monotheism; Yahya adds belief in the return, invoking 10:101. Al-Baydawi and al-Alusi include divine knowledge, will and wisdom; al-Tabrisi stresses createdness and dependence. Ibn Kathir cites 12:105. Al-Biqa'i says the signs prove resurrection. Islahi calls shut eyes the reason signs do not benefit, and Kur'an Yolu stresses God's power to recreate. [29 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI]
- 21:32/q14/p2 They use the sky's worldly benefits while overlooking its proof of the Creator. — Al-Kashshaf names light, navigation and life-sustaining rain; Abu Hayyan reports his explanation. Al-Razi connects the singular sign with visible worldly benefits and ignored proof of God's existence and oneness. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); RAZI-FULL]
- 21:32/q14/p3 Complete perception remembers God in every created thing; heedless unbelief is spiritual blindness. — Al-Bursawi lists reflection among the ten traits of the abdals and recounts David learning from a worm that thanked and remembered God more than he did. His Masnavi citation compares deniers who miss divine knowledge's value to asses overlooking a pearl; he says true perception marks complete believers. [4 notes: BURSEVI]

## 21:32/q15 What inward meanings are drawn from the sky and its protection?  [ٱلسَّمَآءَ مَّحْفُوظًۭا · inward]
turns on: Whether the inward sky is reason or the remembering heart.
- 21:32/q15/p1 The sky is the rational faculty, whose proofs people ignore. — Al-Qashani says reason is raised and protected from change, forgetfulness and error. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 21:32/q15/p2 The gnostic's heart is a heaven guarded by remembrance against human and jinn devils. — Al-Bursawi cites a Prophetic prayer for remembrance and protection from satanic whispers, and Masnavi verses where remembrance burns away whispers. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 21:32/q16 How does this verse connect with the neighboring passage and its argument?  [* · links]
turns on: Which neighboring or cross-verse link is being explained: the sequence of creation signs, the challenge for proof, or verbal parallels.
- 21:32/q16/p1 The discourse turns from earthly signs and benefits to the sky, then to celestial order. — Al-Biqa'i says the sura moves from familiar terrestrial features and mountains to the sky. Ibn Ashur contrasts stated earthly benefits with heaven's less obvious benefit, explaining the call to reflect. Al-Razi says the next verse details ignored signs through sun, moon, day, night and orbits. Al-Zarkashi says the sequence gives place priority over time in creation, citing 2:164. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURHAN]
- 21:32/q16/p2 The signs in 21:30–33 answer opponents' demand for proof of the Prophet's messengership. — Islahi says they mock his humanity and ask for proof, citing 21:8; the universe already attests to providence, mercy, wisdom, justice, monotheism and Judgment, so no new sign is needed. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 21:32/q16/p3 Other verses echo the roof, preservation, and neglected signs. — Yahya compares 15:17, where protection is attributed to the sky rather than the roof, and 10:101 on observing heaven and earth. Asad compares the first sentence of 13:2. The Study Quran cites 2:22, 40:64 and 79:28 for the canopy image. Ibn Kathir invokes 12:105 for signs people pass by. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 21:32/q17 What further material is recorded alongside these verse notes?  [* · reports]
turns on: The supplied notes include separate introductory, lexical and narrative material.
- 21:32/q17/p1 Al-Tabari's notes on al-Fatiha discuss its names, seven verses and disputed counting of the basmala. — The Prophet names it Umm al-Quran, Fatiha al-Kitab and the seven oft-repeated. Al-Tabari explains opening the codex and prayer, and umm as a leading encompassing source, citing Dhu al-Rumma, Humayd ibn Thawr and accounts of Mecca as Umm al-Qura. He says its frequent prayer recitation explains 'oft-repeated,' citing al-Hasan. Kufans count the basmala, Medinans instead count 'those upon whom You bestowed favor'; he defers fuller argument to a legal work. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(أعظم أهل الكوفة,أعظم قراء أهل المدينة ومتفقهيهم,النبي,قيل)]
- 21:32/q17/p2 Ibn Ashur describes the sources, aims and method of his tafsir. — He names al-Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, al-Razi, al-Baydawi, al-Alusi and other works; discusses Ibn Arfa's incomplete notes; often omits source attribution; disclaims originality; seeks meanings, coherence, rhetorical subtleties, lexical precision and inimitability, and explains the title and scope of his concise commentary. [17 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 21:32/q17/p3 Other supplied notes concern nearby or unrelated linguistic and narrative points. — Al-Tabrisi parses 'all swim in an orbit' as a circumstantial nominal clause with fī attached to yasbaḥūn. Abu Hayyan defines كلأ and its verbal noun. Al-Zarkashi discusses Arabic preference for placing night before day, and reports al-Tabari's Ibn Abbas and Abu Hurayra evidence about earth and light preceding night and day. Al-Suyuti reports Jews asking the Prophet about Friday and another Ibn Abbas report of the Preserved Tablet's pearl, ruby and luminous pen. [6 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(الطبري); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ITQAN(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 21:32/q18 What report illustrates failure to reflect on the sky?  [عَنْ ءَايَٰتِهَا مُعْرِضُونَ · reports]
- 21:32/q18/p1 An Israelite worshiper looked at the sky without reflection and so received no cloud. — Ibn Kathir relays Ibn Abi al-Dunya's story: after thirty years without a cloud, the worshiper's mother suggested his failure to reflect despite looking upward; he admitted it. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن أبي الدنيا)]
