# 21:105: 24 questions

## 21:105/q01 What does God's writing here mean?  [كَتَبْنَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the note explains this divine act or the Arabic root of kataba.
- 21:105/q01/p1 God recorded and decreed this promise in scripture and prior decree. — Qushayri says katabna is announcing and decreeing; Wahidi prefers recording the ruling in books after the Mother Book; Ibn Kathir says the recorded decree of happiness in both lives must occur, citing 7:128, 40:51 and 24:55; Tabari says the book-form means written; Samīn makes the an-clause its object. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TAB-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 21:105/q01/p2 Kataba originally means joining or gathering parts together. — Ibn Qutayba explains writing as gathering letters like beads strung together; he adds the usage for joining a mare's lips with a ring. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB]

## 21:105/q02 What does zabūr mean as an Arabic word?  [ٱلزَّبُورِ · meaning]
turns on: Root usage, morphological form and whether ordinary or special usage defines the term.
- 21:105/q02/p1 A written book or scripture, from zabara, to write. — Ibn Qutayba calls faʿūl passive, like jalūb and rakūb; Zajjaj, Nahhas, Qurtubi and Wahidi equate zabūr with kitāb; Thaʿlabi calls the fatḥa form a passive participle; Ibn al-Jawzi gives zubur as its plural; Razi compares halūb and rakūb; Ibn Ashur treats the singular as a passive form and plural as zubr/qishr-qushur; Study Quran and Asad note the general book sense. [13 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور,حمزة); ASAD-NOTES(Tabari); STUDYQURAN; BURSEVI(القاموس)]
- 21:105/q02/p2 Originally a book written heavily or in bold strokes, especially David's book in usage. — Raghib in Bursevi gives bold strokes; Alusi cites al-Muhkam for either vowel of the verb's ba and says common usage narrows the name to David; Bursevi notes the special usage. Alusi treats the name as Arabic and reports Hamza's Syriac claim. [4 notes: BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL(حمزة)]
- 21:105/q02/p3 A divine book of intellectual wisdom without legal rulings. — Bursevi and Alusi report a distinction from books combining law and wisdom; David's Zabur contains no rulings. Ibn al-Jawzi transmits Zajjaj's broader formula, any book containing wisdom. [3 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 21:105/q02/p4 Any divine book difficult to comprehend. — Bursevi reports this as another lexical view. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
- 21:105/q02/p5 The root has several other usages. — Ibn al-Jawzi lists chest, iron piece, calamity, rebuke and one's writing alongside book. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 21:105/q03 How is al-Zabūr read?  [ٱلزَّبُورِ · readings]
turns on: The vowel on zāy and whether the form is singular or plural; both retain a writing or book sense.
- 21:105/q03/p1 With fatḥa on zāy, singular zabūr, the majority reading. — Ibn Mujahid and Farisi contrast Hamza with other readers; Thaʿlabi describes the fatḥa form as passive; Razi and Jishumi say both readings mean book or are dialect forms; Ibn Ashur explains the passive singular; Nashr names Hamza and Khalaf for the other form. [7 notes: IBNMUJAHID(حمزة); FARISI-HUJJA(حمزة); THALABI(الأعمش وحمزة); JISHUMI(حمزة); RAZI-FULL(حمزة والباقون); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); NASHR]
- 21:105/q03/p2 With ḍamma on zāy, plural zubur, read by Hamza and, in later enumeration, Khalaf. — Aʿmash is also reported by Tabari and Thaʿlabi; Nahhas derives the plural from zubr; Qurtubi, Nasafi, Ibn Ashur and Qiraat-ER explain that it means writings or scriptures; Nashr cross-references its discussion at al-Nisaʾ. Jishumi calls the two forms dialects. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(الأعمش); NAHHAS; IBNMUJAHID(حمزة); FARISI-HUJJA(حمزة); THALABI(الأعمش وحمزة); JISHUMI(حمزة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(حمزة); RAZI-FULL(حمزة والباقون); QURTUBI-FULL(حمزة); NASAFI-FULL(حمزة); NASHR; IBNASHUR-FULL(حمزة); QIRAAT-ER(vw)]

## 21:105/q05 What does al-Dhikr mean as a word?  [ٱلذِّكْرِ · meaning]
turns on: The basic act of recall and its metonymic uses for instruction or honor.
- 21:105/q05/p1 Remembering, remembrance, or the remembered thing, in heart or speech. — Elmalili distinguishes heart and tongue, recall after forgetting and continuous awareness; Raghib and Qamus describe retaining knowledge inwardly, with hifz preserving it and dhikr bringing it to awareness. Asad gives the literal reminder sense. [7 notes: ELMALILI(Râgıb ve Kāmûs sâhibi); ASAD-NOTES]
- 21:105/q05/p2 A divine reminder giving limits, duties and wisdom, or honor and renown. — Tabari gives the instruction and honor senses; Elmalili includes name, fame, praise, prayer and supplication, and uses this verse for heavenly books teaching religion and law. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 21:105/q06 What is al-Dhikr here?  [ٱلذِّكْرِ · referent]

Alusi cites the Bukhari report of God's writing everything in al-Dhikr after creating heaven and earth, while Tabatabai rejects this identification as untenable. ## 21:105/q07 What does min baʿd al-Dhikr indicate?  [مِنۢ بَعْدِ ٱلذِّكْرِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the preposition expresses chronological sequence, the converse Arabic usage, or rank.
- 21:105/q07/p1 Later in time than the earlier record, Torah or admonition. — Al-Kalbi says Zabur followed Torah; Tabari and Zajjaj say revealed writing followed the heavenly record; Ibn Ashur says this distinguishes David's later promise from Moses' earlier political and Holy Land promises in 5:21 and 7:128; Biqai and Islahi locate counsel before the Psalmic promise. [9 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الكلبي); ZAJJAJ; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TAB-FULL+]
- 21:105/q07/p2 Here baʿd means before. — Thaʿlabi cites 18:79 and 79:30 for the usage; Baghawi cites 18:97 and 79:30; Tabarsi reports Shaʿbi; Ibn Khalawayh in Itqan calls this the only Qur'anic baʿd used as before. [4 notes: THALABI(بعضهم); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(الشعبي); ITQAN(ابن خالويه)]
- 21:105/q07/p3 After means later in rank, not chronology. — Tabatabai reports this for the view that the Reminder is the Qur'an. [1 notes: TABATABAI(قيل)]

## 21:105/q08 Where does min baʿd al-Dhikr attach, and what is the object of katabnā?  [كَتَبْنَا مِنۢ بَعْدِ ٱلذِّكْرِ أَنَّ · grammar]
turns on: The possible attachment of the prepositional phrase; the an-clause supplies the stated content of writing.
- 21:105/q08/p1 The phrase attaches to katabnā. — Samīn gives this syntactic possibility. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 21:105/q08/p2 The phrase qualifies al-Zabūr as a written text. — Samīn gives this attachment too; Ibn Ashur calls it a settled circumstantial qualifier distinguishing the later Zabur promise from the Torah promise. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 21:105/q08/p3 The an-clause is the object of katabnā. — Samīn gives the analysis and says it means God decreed inheritance for the righteous. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 21:105/q10 Who are My righteous servants?  [عِبَادِىَ ٱلصَّٰلِحُونَ · referent]

## 21:105/q11 What makes these servants righteous and fit to inherit?  [عِبَادِىَ ٱلصَّٰلِحُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Different explanations of the righteousness criterion, from inward sincerity to public obedience and a specified prayer practice.
- 21:105/q11/p1 Sincerity, repaired inward life and detachment from all besides God. — Sahl's formulation is transmitted by Tustari, Sulami and Bursevi; Biqai also explains the divine possessive and their remembrance, monotheism, fear, hope and humility. [4 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل); BURSEVI(سهل); BIQAI-FULL]
- 21:105/q11/p2 Faith, obedience and righteous action sustain the promise. — Tabari specifies divine commands and prohibitions; Razi says Paradise is reserved for the obedient while the unrighteous are only dependents there; Elmalili says communities last by being most fit, citing Pharaoh's fall, Israel's Holy Land and David in 38:26; Muslims retain inheritance by monotheism and avoiding division. Asad relates faith and conduct to 3:139. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وسعيد بن جبير وعكرمة والسدي وأبو العالية); ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 21:105/q11/p3 Performing the five daily prayers in congregation. — Ibn Abbas in Durr specifically identifies these people as inheritors of Paradise. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 21:105/q12 How should inheriting the earth be understood?  [يَرِثُهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether inheritance is worldly power, afterlife settlement, or both.
- 21:105/q12/p1 A transfer of earthly authority and benefits, with lasting rule for the fit. — Tabatabai defines inheritance as transfer without transaction and connects it to a society free of idolatry under 24:55; Bursevi says disbelievers are expelled; Elmalili says God takes land from corrupters, as with Pharaoh, Israel and David, and Kuranyolu describes tyrants' loss to prophetic communities. Alusi says most inhabited earth is in view, with ultimate worldwide rule possible under Mahdi and Jesus. [13 notes: TABATABAI; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 21:105/q12/p2 Entering and settling Paradise or the renewed eternal earth. — Jishumi contrasts this exclusive righteous inheritance with shared worldly blessings; Tabatabai says stations of nearness acquired in life become afterlife bliss; Islahi says wrongdoers have no share in the new realm. [4 notes: JISHUMI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 21:105/q12/p3 Both worldly and afterlife reward. — Ibn Kathir says happiness is decreed in both lives; Ibn Ashur says both senses have been fulfilled; Tabatabai insists the verse is unrestricted though its immediate context foregrounds worldly recompense; Kuranyolu includes Paradise and both worlds. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI+]

## 21:105/q13 What earlier scriptural text expresses this promise?  [ٱلزَّبُورِ أَنَّ ٱلْأَرْضَ يَرِثُهَا عِبَادِىَ ٱلصَّٰلِحُونَ · links]
turns on: The exact Psalm identification and whether the wording belongs particularly to David's book or to multiple divine books.
- 21:105/q13/p1 The Psalms, especially the repeated land-inheritance promise in Psalm 37. — Islahi reproduces Psalm 37, citing 37:9, 11, 28 and 29: wicked wither and perish while meek and righteous inherit and dwell forever. He notes counsel in distinct stanzas before the promise and David's authority as prophet-king. Biqai quotes a matching promise in Psalm 36 before its fourth section and a final Psalm where the meek inherit land. Foido supplies a corresponding Hebrew formulation. Kuranyolu says the wording is nearly identical. [12 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(فويدو); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Psalms); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 21:105/q13/p2 A broad sequence of Psalms shows the same pattern of righteousness, divine rescue and wickedness destroyed. — Biqai quotes Psalm 1's watered tree and chaff; Psalm 2's nations, Zion king and trust; Psalm 5's morning prayer and rejected falsehood; Psalm 6's tears and mercy; Psalm 9's just defeat of enemies; Psalm 12's trust amid distress; Psalm 14's blameless sanctuary dweller; Psalm 16's truthful petitioner and scattering of foes; Psalm 17's storm rescue, moral recompense, battle victory and foreign service. Later passages describe a mocked persecuted speaker with pierced hands and feet and divided clothes, whose rescue leads poor and nations to worship; subsequent Psalms oppose idols, commend courage, peace and fear of God, preserve righteous bones, and vindicate one falsely accused despite his fasting for foes. [27 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 21:105/q13/p3 The promise appears in earlier divine books generally. — Wahb says he read it in several books; Ibn Abbas names Torah, Zabur and prior knowledge; Asad describes books of divine wisdom. Ibn Ashur says a general earlier promise of this exact kind was absent from other scriptures, making David's wording notable. [6 notes: THALABI(وهب); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 21:105/q14 How does this promise connect with its sūra and other Qur'anic promises?  [* · links]
turns on: Which surrounding passage governs the promise: immediate afterlife context, earlier believers' reward, or broader prophet stories.

## 21:105/q18 What reports connect the promise to end-time rule?  [ٱلْأَرْضَ يَرِثُهَا عِبَادِىَ ٱلصَّٰلِحُونَ · reports]

## 21:105/q21 What other Qur'an and earlier-scripture relations do these notes report?  [ٱلزَّبُورِ ٱلذِّكْرِ · reports]
turns on: Different reported correspondences between Qur'anic divisions and earlier books, and whether earlier scriptures had suras.
- 21:105/q21/p1 The Prophet received groups of Qur'anic suras in place of earlier books. — Wathila's versions align seven long suras with Torah, and vary between miʾun and mathani for Psalms or Gospel; all give Mufassal an extra distinction.
