# 39:17: 26 questions

## 39:17/q01 What does avoiding the ṭāghūt entail?  [ٱجْتَنَبُوا۟ ٱلطَّٰغُوتَ أَن يَعْبُدُوهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether avoidance is explained as separation from false worship alone or as the paired rejection and affirmation of exclusive devotion.
- 39:17/q01/p1 It means keeping apart from worship of false deities and all that is worshipped besides God. — Al-Ṭabarī makes the object any worshipped rival. Al-Qurṭubī and Bursawī explain ijtināb as moving away and remaining apart. Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī stress avoidance of worship. The Study Quran compares 2:256–57, 4:51–52, 4:60, 4:76, 5:60 and 16:36. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 39:17/q01/p2 It means rejecting all servitude to other than God, including misleading desire, while affirming exclusive devotion to Him. — Al-Rāzī summarizes the paired negative and positive acts. Al-Biqāʿī says Satan embellishes desires that pull one toward the false deity. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says negation without sincere worship of God does not benefit. Bursawī's Bahr al-ʿUlūm compares 2:256 and the firm handhold. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(بحر العلوم); TABATABAI]

## 39:17/q02 What does ṭāghūt denote here?  [ٱلطَّٰغُوتَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the word points especially to idols, Satan, human misleaders, every rival object of service, or an inward/worldly object of attachment.
- 39:17/q02/p1 Idols, or the false deities worshipped besides God. — Muqātil calls them idols and notes the feminine form. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and al-Suddī are cited for idols. Al-Rāzī reports that idols receive the name metaphorically from their worshippers' transgression. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes them collectively, while al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes every false deity. Al-Dāmghānī compares 16:36. [15 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك والسدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(مجاهد والسدي وابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك والسدي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 39:17/q02/p2 Satan, or devils collectively, whose prompting makes idol worship service to them. — Mujāhid, al-Suddī and Ibn Zayd transmit this gloss. Ibn Zayd compares collective singular address at 82:6. Al-Zajjāj says the devils are avoided by not following them. Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī associate the emphatic source form with Satan; al-Ḥasan's plural reading supports devils. Al-Rāzī, Bursawī and al-Ālūsī explain that Satan commands and beautifies idolatry. [23 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,السدي,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد وابن زيد); JISHUMI(مجاهد والسدي وابن زيد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد والسدي وابن زيد); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد وابن زيد); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن,الزمخشري,مجاهد); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 39:17/q02/p3 Anything worshipped or obeyed apart from God, or anything that calls people toward such worship. — Al-Rāzī includes worship and obedience. Al-Rāghib includes every transgressor and object of worship; al-Qāmūs enumerates al-Lāt, al-ʿUzzā, soothsayers, Satan, misguiding leaders, rebellious People of the Book and idols. Al-Ṭabrisī includes callers to false worship; Abū Baṣīr reports that obedience to a tyrant is worship of him. Islahi includes idols, devils, jinn and misleading leaders, with 2:256 and 4:51. Kuran Yolu includes visible and invisible causes of disobedience, unbelief and irreligion; Study Quran includes false authorities and causes. [15 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو بصير عن أبي عبد الله,قيل); RAZI-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب,القاموس,كشف الأسرار); ALUSI-FULL(الجوهري,الراغب); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 39:17/q02/p4 A soothsayer. — Ibn al-Sāʾib gives this identification; al-Qurṭubī also reports it. Al-Jawharī and al-Qāmūs include it among the word's possible senses. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الجوهري); BURSEVI(القاموس)]
- 39:17/q02/p5 Worldly life as a false deity. — Sahl describes ignorance as its root, food and drink its branches, boasting its adornment, sins its fruit, and hardness and punishment its outcome; the three transmissions vary between measure and inheritance. [3 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل); BURSEVI(سهل)]
- 39:17/q02/p6 Each person's lower self or corrupting passions. — Al-Qushayrī and al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya prescribe resisting desire and choosing God's pleasure. Asad names ambitions for power, exploitative wealth and immoral advancement; Kuran Yolu accepts his sense alongside others. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Muhammed Esed)]

## 39:17/q03 How is the word ṭāghūt formed and what force does its form carry?  [ٱلطَّٰغُوتَ · meaning]
turns on: Arabic derivation and intensive metathesis versus a proposed foreign name, and whether the resulting name is exclusive to Satan.
- 39:17/q03/p1 It derives from Arabic ṭughyān, transgression, on an intensive faʿalūt pattern with metathesis. — Ibn Jinnī traces ṭaghiyūt to ṭayghūt to ṭāghūt, citing ṭughyān for a yāʾ radical and ḥānūt/ḥawānīt with ʿUmāra's poetry as analogy; he also allows plural ṭawāghīt by analogy to ʿāqūl/ʿawāqīl and sājūr/sawājīr, but favors his first account. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī and al-Itqān link source naming, intensive pattern and inversion to emphasis; al-Rāzī calls the inversion unusual. Bursawī reports tāʾ as added intensifier, and al-Ālūsī accepts either yāʾ or wāw root forms, citing al-Jawharī's ṭughyān/ṭughwān and unused alternative roots; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes both weak-letter forms and al-Qāmūs's preference for wāw. [25 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو علي,أبو مسلم,قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(الكشاف); BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار); ALUSI-FULL(الجوهري,الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL(القاموس); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB+]
- 39:17/q03/p2 It is a foreign name, comparable to Hārūt, Mārūt, Jālūt or Ṭālūt. — Al-Māwardī, Jishumī and al-Qurṭubī transmit this proposal. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports a foreign fāʿūl form and al-Itqān's claim that it means soothsayer in Abyssinian, included by Ibn Ḥajar among Quranic loanwords. Al-Ālūsī rejects a proposed fāʿūl derivation in favor of faʿalūt. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(من العلماء); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 39:17/q03/p3 The word began as a noun for transgression and came to name the utmost transgressor, with Satan its dominant but not exclusive use. — Al-Ālūsī says applying the general concept to Satan or another case may be literal or figurative and questions al-Zamakhsharī's exclusive use; al-Kashf tries to reconcile it as literal after customary transfer to Satan, figurative elsewhere. Al-Zamakhsharī himself uses it of Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf at 4:60, for rebellion or comparison with Satan. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 39:17/q03/p4 Its intensive source form has become specifically the name of Satan. — Al-Kashshāf says the source word names Satan as sheer transgression; al-Nasafī compares raḥamūt and malakūt. Al-Itqān and al-Rāzī report source naming, pattern and metathesis as emphatic signs of this exclusive use; al-Ālūsī reports and qualifies the claim. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ITQAN(الكشاف); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 39:17/q04 How can ṭāghūt take the feminine pronoun in 'worship it'?  [ٱلطَّٰغُوتَ يَعْبُدُوهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether feminine agreement follows a collective/plural sense or the noun's own singular feminine gender.
- 39:17/q04/p1 The noun is treated as a feminine collective or plural, including idols or devils. — Sībawayh cites 39:17 for a feminine noun denoting a group. Al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Zayd, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Ḥasan's interpreters and al-Baḥr note collective sense. Ibn ʿĀshūr says nonrational plural agreement contrasts with masculine agreement in 2:257, where it relates to masculine allies or is treated as rational. Al-Sāmarrāʾī points to feminine idols here. Abū Ḥātim and later lexica allow singular/plural and masculine/feminine uses. [20 notes: SIBAWAYH; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن زيد); NAHHAS(الأخفش); JISHUMI(أبو حاتم,قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد والسدي وابن زيد); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأخفش,مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); BURSEVI(الكواشي والمفردات والقاموس,بحر العلوم); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 39:17/q04/p2 Ṭāghūt itself can be a single feminine noun, so its pronoun is feminine. — Al-Akhfash gives this alongside the plural analysis; al-Naḥḥās, al-Qurṭubī and Ibn al-Jawzī transmit it. Jishumī and al-Samīn emphasize matching the feminine form. [6 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأخفش); QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); SAMIN-DURR]

## 39:17/q05 Is there a variant reading of ṭāghūt?  [ٱلطَّٰغُوتَ · readings]
- 39:17/q05/p1 Al-Ḥasan and other reported readers use plural al-ṭawāghīt. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī report the variant as evidence for a plural referent; al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ālūsī attribute it to al-Ḥasan, the latter taking it as support for devils. Abū Ḥayyān likewise reports al-Ḥasan's plural. Ibn Jinnī reports al-Ḥasan with the displayed singular wording and discusses morphology instead. [6 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن); NASAFI-FULL(قُرِئَ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]

## 39:17/q06 How is 'that they worship it' attached syntactically?  [أَن يَعْبُدُوهَا · grammar]
- 39:17/q06/p1 It is an accusative apposition of inclusion to ṭāghūt, specifying avoidance of its worship. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Samīn, al-Biqāʿī, Bursawī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr call it badal ishtimāl; Abū Ḥayyān calls it an explicative verbal noun in apposition. Al-Biqāʿī says the general avoidance is thereby specified. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:17/q07 What does turning back to God mean here?  [وَأَنَابُوٓا۟ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
turns on: The scope of ināba: worship and obedience, total orientation, uprightness, or repentance from sin.
- 39:17/q07/p1 Returning to worship, obedience and affirmation of God alone. — Muqātil contrasts former idol worship. Al-Ṭabarī adds repentance, monotheism and dissociation from rival gods; al-Suddī calls it responding to God. Al-Ṭabrisī stresses abandoning former conduct; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls ināba repentance from all sin, especially pre-Islamic shirk, comparing 11:75. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(السدي); THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:17/q07/p2 It is turning or returning wholly toward God, away from everything else. — Qatāda glosses the word as coming toward or back to God; Jishumī explains its root as return, akin to repentance and opposite to persistence. Al-Qushayrī says in every matter, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī, Bursawī and al-Ālūsī stress complete exclusive orientation. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:17/q07/p3 It means remaining upright toward God. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk's gloss transmitted by al-Māwardī. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 39:17/q07/p4 It means turning back to God specifically from sins. — Al-Māwardī offers this as a further possibility; Ibn ʿĀshūr extends it to all sins, especially prior shirk. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:17/q08 Why is avoiding ṭāghūt stated before turning to God?  [ٱجْتَنَبُوا۟ ٱلطَّٰغُوتَ وَأَنَابُوٓا۟ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
- 39:17/q08/p1 Rejection precedes affirmation, as disbelief in ṭāghūt precedes faith in God. — Bursawī compares the negation and affirmation of the testimony of unity and al-Baḥr al-ʿUlūm compares the firmest handhold at 2:256. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says sincere exclusive worship needs both acts. [3 notes: BURSEVI(بحر العلوم); TABATABAI]

## 39:17/q09 What does the promised bushrā signify?  [لَهُمُ ٱلْبُشْرَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the good announced is specified as Paradise, broader reward, a disclosure at death, divine encounter, or the honor of guidance.
- 39:17/q09/p1 Good news of Paradise and its lasting delights. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Māwardī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī and Ibn ʿĀshūr identify Paradise; some distinguish announcement in this world from fulfillment in the next. Al-Rāzī says it removes fear and grief and cites 41:30 and 43:71; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 10:64. [12 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل ويحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:17/q09/p2 It is divine reward, including further bliss beyond what people know. — Al-Kashshāf compares 10:64 and 57:12; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī call it reward. Al-Rāzī argues known rewards would repeat old news; 32:17 points to hidden bliss, and God's own announcement after a demanding condition implies indescribable greatness. Bursawī includes supreme divine approval. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:17/q09/p3 It includes the reward shown at death. — Al-Māwardī offers this additional timing and object of good news. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 39:17/q09/p4 It is the encounter with God granted to pure monotheists. — Al-Qāshānī links avoidance of every rival object and sincere turning to God with pure monotheism. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 39:17/q09/p5 It is the honor of belonging to those given God's guidance and grace. — Bursawī transmits this from unnamed spiritual masters as the greatest honor. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الكبار)]

## 39:17/q10 What does bushrā mean as a word?  [ٱلْبُشْرَىٰ · meaning]
- 39:17/q10/p1 Joyful news whose joy appears on the recipient's face. — Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī give this lexical explanation; al-Ṭabrisī links it to recompense for their conduct. [2 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI]

## 39:17/q11 Who conveys the good news and when?  [لَهُمُ ٱلْبُشْرَىٰ فَبَشِّرْ · interpretation]
- 39:17/q11/p1 Messengers announce it by revelation in life; angels announce it at death, gathering, resurrection or entry into Paradise, and God may announce it at meeting Him. — Al-Kashshāf cites 10:64 and 57:12. Al-Rāzī specifies near death, grave, gathering, division of the saved and damned, Paradise entry, and possible divine greeting, citing 16:32, 13:23–24 and 33:44. Bursawī distinguishes Messenger in life and angels thereafter, citing 10:64; al-Biqāʿī and al-Ālūsī add angels at death and later. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:17/q12 What emphasis does 'for them is the good news' convey?  [لَهُمُ ٱلْبُشْرَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 39:17/q12/p1 Its preposed 'for them' restricts the good news to those who shun false worship and turn to God. — Al-Rāzī reads fronting as exclusivity and the definite article as the entire category of glad tidings, leaving others no share. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the restrictive clauses in 39:18. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:17/q13 How is the clause 'for them is the good news' parsed?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ لَهُمُ ٱلْبُشْرَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the prepositional phrase alone or the entire good-news clause is taken as the predicate.
- 39:17/q13/p1 The relative phrase is the subject; 'for them' is the preposed predicate and 'the good news' its delayed subject. — Al-Ṭabrisī says the adverbial raises al-bushrā; al-Samīn prefers this analysis. [3 notes: TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 39:17/q13/p2 The whole 'for them is the good news' clause functions as predicate of 'those who avoided'. — Al-Samīn reports this alternative; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise treats the good-news phrase as the predicate. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI]

## 39:17/q14 Who are 'My servants' addressed with the good news?  [فَبَشِّرْ عِبَادِ · referent]
- 39:17/q14/p1 Those who avoided false worship and turned to God, further characterized by listening and following the best speech. — Al-Kashshāf, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī identify the repeated noun with the preceding believers; their next relative clause specifies the additional quality. Al-Qāshānī calls them those singled out by God's special care. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 39:17/q15 Why replace 'give them good news' with 'give good news to My servants'?  [فَبَشِّرْ عِبَادِ · rhetoric]
- 39:17/q15/p1 The explicit possessed noun honors them, generalizes their reward, and foregrounds devotion and following the best speech. — Al-Biqāʿī calls it honor and generalization. Al-Irshād says their distinction is discerning truth from falsehood. Al-Ālūsī says it renews their entitlement and completes the preceding thought. Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī say it praises their worship and anticipates the next relative clause; al-Samīn notes that it also makes their identity explicit. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 39:17/q16 How is the final possessive yāʾ of 'My servants' read?  [عِبَادِ · readings]
turns on: Transmission of the possessive yāʾ in connected recitation and pause, and whether pronunciation follows the written rasm or a verse-ending rule.
- 39:17/q16/p1 The common reading omits the yāʾ and leaves kasra on the dāl. — Qunbul is separately reported this way, as are the remaining readers in al-Fārisī. Ibn ʿĀshūr says nine of the ten readers use it, with the kasra indicating omitted yāʾ; al-Ṭabrisī and Bursawī explain the underlying ʿibādī. Ibn al-Jawzī notes variant readings. Al-Sūsī's other transmitters omit it both in connection and at pause. [7 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(الباقون,قنبل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; NASHR(الباقون عن السوسي); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(العشرة ما عدا السوسي)]
- 39:17/q16/p2 The yāʾ is retained and open in connected recitation, especially in al-Sūsī's transmission; pause may retain or delete it. — Al-Fārisī reports Abū ʿAmr, Ibn Kathīr and Meccan routes with open yāʾ and allows vocalizing or stilling it; ʿUbayd distinguishes verse-end pause and connection. Ibn al-Jawzī describes open final yāʾ with kasra on bāʾ. Al-Nashr identifies al-Sūsī's connecting route as Muḥammad ibn Ismāʿīl al-Qurashī and lists Iraqi corroborations through Ibn Ḥabash; most retain yāʾ at pause, others omit by rasm. Al-Dānī prefers retention in al-Mufradāt but permits omission, while al-Taysīr reports omission. Ibn ʿĀshūr says both pauses are grammatical though rasm omits yāʾ; Qiraat-ER reports the audible possessive. [14 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو,ابن كثير وأهل مكة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمرو); NASHR(أبو العلاء وأبو معشر الطبري وأبو عبد الله الحضري وأبو بكر بن مهران,ابن مهران وأبو حمدون وابن اليزيدي,السوسي,رواة السوسي,صاحب التجريد والداني في التيسير,عبيد بن عقيل); IBNASHUR-FULL(السوسي); QIRAAT-ER(Yz); NASHR(الداني في المفردات)+]

## 39:17/q17 How is the omission of the possessive yāʾ rhetorically explained?  [فَبَشِّرْ عِبَادِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the shortened form points to the small group addressed or to the mediation of the prophetic address.
- 39:17/q17/p1 The abbreviated form suits the smaller group who follow the best speech. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares 39:18 and 39:53, saying followers of the very best are fewer than sinners; the choice of aḥsan, rather than merely ḥasan, reinforces the restriction. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 39:17/q17/p2 The deletion reflects that the Prophet receives the address directly while the servants receive it through him. — Al-Burhān compares 'give good news to My servants' and 'say, My servants.' [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 39:17/q18 Does 'give good news to My servants' end a verse, and how does that affect the yāʾ?  [فَبَشِّرْ عِبَادِ · sciences]
turns on: Differences in verse counting and whether the omitted letter is classified as a disputed possessive or extra yāʾ.
- 39:17/q18/p1 It is counted as an ending in some traditions, allowing a pause and a new nominative construction at 'those who listen'. — Al-Burhān counts the ending because the following phrase can be supplied with both object and predicate. Al-Kashshāf reports the stop and takes 'those who listen' as nominative subject and 'those are' as predicate. Abū ʿAmr permits omitting yāʾ at a verse-end pause, retaining it otherwise and in connection. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BURHAN; NASHR(عبيد بن عقيل)]
- 39:17/q18/p2 Meccan and early Medinan counts leave it outside the verse count, explaining some retained-yāʾ practice. — Ibn Mujāhid associates Abū ʿAmr with early Medinan counting; Ibn al-Yazīdī objects that retaining yāʾ at pause contradicts Abū ʿAmr's rasm practice and thinks he overlooked the ending. Al-Dānī says Abū ʿAmr knew the rival counts; Ibn al-Jazarī prefers the account that he followed Hijazi counting first and Basran counting later. [4 notes: NASHR(أبو عمرو الداني,ابن اليزيدي,ابن مجاهد); NASHR+]
- 39:17/q18/p3 The disputed 'My servants' yāʾ is better classified among extra yāʾs omitted by the codex. — Al-Dānī and others count 212 disputed possessive yāʾs; adding this and ātānī Allāh yields 214, and adding two further instances yields 216. The two others are alā tattabiʿan in Ṭā Hā and in yuridni al-Raḥmān in Yā Sīn. Ibn al-Jazarī prefers extra-yāʾ classification despite variation between fatḥ and sukūn because the codex omits them. [3 notes: NASHR(آخرون,الداني وغيره); NASHR+]

## 39:17/q19 About whom was the passage reported to have been revealed?  [* · reports]
turns on: Different transmitted rosters and whether the group is pre-Islamic monotheists or converts reached through Abū Bakr.
- 39:17/q19/p1 Zayd ibn ʿAmr ibn Nufayl, Abū Dharr and Salmān, pre-Islamic monotheists. — Ibn Zayd or his father Zayd ibn Aslam is the recurrent transmitter. They professed lā ilāha illā Allāh, rejected idols and followed the best teaching that reached them, without scripture or prophet. Some reports call them three men and some Qurtubi wording says Zayd, Abū Dharr and others. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(زيد بن أسلم); THALABI(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد); WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن زيد); JISHUMI(ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد); TABRISI(عبد الرحمن بن زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد عن أبيه); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); DURR-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); SUYUTI-LUBAB(زيد بن أسلم); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن زيد)]
- 39:17/q19/p2 Early converts persuaded by Abū Bakr after his own conversion. — Ibn Isḥāq names ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf, Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ, Saʿīd ibn Zayd and al-Zubayr. The Ibn ʿAbbās/ʿAṭāʾ versions additionally name ʿUthmān and Ṭalḥa, with six followers; one version says Abū Bakr first affirmed the Prophet and the best speech came from him. The four-name and six-name reports differ in roster. [6 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB(عطاء عن ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن إسحاق); ALUSI-FULL(ابن إسحاق); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 39:17/q19/p3 Saʿīd ibn Zayd, Abū Dharr and Salmān were the three pre-Islamic hearers of the best speech. — Ibn ʿUmar names Saʿīd rather than Zayd ibn ʿAmr; he identifies the best speech as the testimony of God's oneness. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عمر)]

## 39:17/q20 Does a reported occasion limit whom the verse covers?  [* · interpretation]
- 39:17/q20/p1 No; the verse applies to everyone who rejects idol worship and turns to God through the Day of Resurrection. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān state general applicability despite reports. Ibn Kathīr explicitly prefers the general reading including the named men, with good news in both worlds; al-Ālūsī repeats generality after Ibn Isḥāq's report. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن إسحاق); IBNKATHIR-FULL+]

## 39:17/q21 How does the verse connect with the surrounding warning?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the link is the immediate warning and promise sequence or the wider Quranic glad-news motif.
- 39:17/q21/p1 It follows threats to idolaters with a promise to those who shun false worship, joining warning and encouragement. — Jishumī and al-Rāzī describe God's practice of pairing threat and promise. Ibn ʿĀshūr connects it with the punishment in 39:16 and the admonition to all people, citing 40:15. Islahi contrasts the believers' success with the losers and says the Prophet is not responsible for people whose deeds make them fuel for Hell. [5 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:17/q21/p2 Its glad tidings echo the Quran's promise of glad news in this world and the next. — Asad points to 10:62–64; Ibn ʿĀshūr and Bursawī cite 10:64 for the good news and its fulfillment. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 39:17/q22 What does wholehearted return to God imply about created causes and reliance on God?  [وَأَنَابُوٓا۟ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ · theology]
- 39:17/q22/p1 Exclusive devotion recognizes God as the sole necessary and ultimate agent while using the created means He assigned. — Al-Rāzī cites a Torah instruction to answer God with the whole heart. Created causes remain contingent upon God; He acts through intermediaries and without them. In this world outcomes usually have assigned means, while the higher world may not; seeking worldly ends without means contradicts divine order. His story of a youth who invoked decree to neglect reputation and wealth illustrates mistaken reliance. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 39:17/q23 What broad uses of ṭāghūt help explain the word here?  [ٱلطَّٰغُوتَ · meaning]
- 39:17/q23/p1 It can name any transgressor or anything worshipped besides God, in singular or plural usage. — Al-Rāghib's definition is reported by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī. [1 notes: TABATABAI(الراغب)]

## 39:17/q24 Why is the promised good news of exceptional magnitude?  [ٱلْبُشْرَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 39:17/q24/p1 God's own announcement after so demanding a condition indicates a reward beyond human description. — Al-Rāzī argues from the divine speaker and the difficulty of avoiding all rivals and returning wholly to God. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 39:17/q25 How does the preceding warning address prepare this promise?  [* · links]
- 39:17/q25/p1 It addresses all people as God's servants, urges them to fear the threatened punishment, then turns to believers' good news. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the earlier possessive 'servants' includes believers and unbelievers, with everlasting punishment as the extreme. Taqwā follows the threat because warning implies prepared punishment. The earlier vocative precedes the command to focus attention, unlike 2:197, and its possessive yāʾ is deleted according to one of five vocative forms. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:17/q26 How did idol worship reportedly begin?  [* · reports]
- 39:17/q26/p1 Anthropomorphists pictured God as a great light and angels as lesser lights, then made and worshipped corresponding images. — Al-Rāzī attributes this account to historical reports. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(في التواريخ)]
