# 18:23: 27 questions

## 18:23/q01 What force does the emphatic nūn give the prohibition?  [وَلَا تَقُولَنَّ · grammar]
- 18:23/q01/p1 An emphatic nūn can occur in prohibitions and commands, but is optional there. — Sībawayh contrasts commands and prohibitions with the oath-lām construction and cites Quranic examples. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 18:23/q02 What does لِشَا۟ىْءٍ designate here?  [لِشَا۟ىْءٍ · meaning]
- 18:23/q02/p1 It means concerning any matter or action a person resolves to do. — Al-Zamakhsharī relates it to a resolved action; al-Qurṭubī takes the lām as 'concerning' or 'for'; al-Nasafī includes any intended future thing, and al-Biqāʿī includes both trivial and important plans. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 18:23/q03 Can a future action already be called a 'thing'?  [لِشَا۟ىْءٍ · theology]
- 18:23/q03/p1 Yes: a nonexistent future action can be called a thing. — Al-Jishumī argues from this wording against restricting 'thing' to what already exists. Al-Zarkashī says the future thing exists mentally through imagining an existing counterpart, though it does not yet exist externally. [2 notes: JISHUMI; BURHAN]

## 18:23/q04 Why is 'thing' written with an extra alif?  [لِشَا۟ىْءٍ · rhetoric]
- 18:23/q04/p1 It marks a future thing known by imagining an existing counterpart. — Al-Zarkashī distinguishes its mental existence from its absence in external reality. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 18:23/q05 How does the lām relate saying to its object or hearer?  [لِشَا۟ىْءٍ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the lām marks the topic of speech or a person receiving it.
- 18:23/q05/p1 Here it can mean 'concerning' or 'for' the thing spoken of. — Al-Qurṭubī gives these two equivalent renderings. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 18:23/q05/p2 When attached to the addressed person, lām makes saying transitive to its recipient. — Al-Zarkashī reports Ibn Mālik's grammatical rule; this concerns a lām with a person addressed, rather than the verse's thing. [1 notes: BURHAN(ابن مالك)]

## 18:23/q06 What does 'tomorrow' mean here?  [غَدًا · meaning]
turns on: Whether 'tomorrow' itself denotes any future time or names the next day and extends by implication.
- 18:23/q06/p1 It stands for any future time, beyond the next calendar day. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī generalize it to the future. Al-Ālūsī says its primary sense is the next day but the verse covers any future resolution. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the broader sense fits the occasion and what followed; Study Quran likewise takes tomorrow generally. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN]
- 18:23/q06/p2 The wording first names the literal next day; broader periods are reached by implication. — Al-Ālūsī reports this limitation, but rejects an objection to generalizing: death remains an obstacle and grows more likely over time. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 18:23/q07 What is the form and original shape of غَدًا?  [غَدًا · grammar]
- 18:23/q07/p1 It is an adverb of time, originally ghuduw. — Al-Naḥḥās supplies both the grammatical function and proposed origin. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 18:23/q08 Why specify tomorrow when 'I will do' already looks forward?  [إِنِّى فَاعِلٌۭ ذَٰلِكَ غَدًا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the emphasis is grammatical futurity or the occasion's next-day promise.
- 18:23/q08/p1 The time word reinforces the futurity already conveyed by the active participle. — Al-Ālūsī says fāʿil already indicates a future action; ghadan underscores it, even though ghadan first means the following day. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:23/q08/p2 The word fits the Prophet's promise to answer the next day. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī links 'tomorrow' to the three questions, including the Cave youths; he says revelation then waited fifteen nights and rumors spread. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 18:23/q09 Who is addressed by the prohibition?  [وَلَا تَقُولَنَّ · referent]
turns on: Whether the Prophet's occasion confines the addressee or introduces a general rule.
- 18:23/q09/p1 The Prophet is addressed, and the instruction extends to the community and anyone making future plans. — Al-Jishumī prefers Prophet and community over Prophet alone. Asad calls it a principle for all believers; al-Sāmarrāʾī compares the occasion-specific address in 24:33 with its broader rule. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes anyone who plans and announces action; Study Quran reports application to any future promise. [8 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); ASAD-NOTES; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); BURHAN; JISHUMI+]
- 18:23/q09/p2 The address concerns the Prophet alone in its immediate setting. — Al-Jishumī reports this alternative without adopting its restriction; the many occasion accounts make the Prophet the immediate addressee. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); JISHUMI-]

## 18:23/q10 What event prompted the instruction, and how long was revelation delayed?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on the interval and on which questions were asked; al-Jishumī also disputes making the Prophet's failed promise the verse's restricted setting.
- 18:23/q10/p1 The Prophet promised to answer the questioners tomorrow without saying 'if God wills'; revelation then paused fifteen days or nights. — Ibn ʿAbbās via Abū Ṣāliḥ names Quraysh's questions on Dhū al-Qarnayn, the Spirit, and the Cave, Gabriel's fifteen-day delay and the Prophet's distress (Ibn al-Jawzī). Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Kathīr, al-Durr, al-Ālūsī, al-Bursawī, Kuran Yolu, al-Sāmarrāʾī, Study Quran, and Asad relay the occasion with different emphasis. Abū Ḥayyān includes al-Khiḍr instead of Dhū al-Qarnayn; al-Durr reports Jewish advice, Quraysh's rumors, and Gabriel's explanation; Study Quran says the delay raised doubts about the answer and prophethood; al-Bursawī reports the claim that his Lord had abandoned him. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī's separate account names Moses and his youth instead of the Spirit. Islahi treats the Cave question as a possible testing occasion and adds the Prophet's wish to vindicate his message. [21 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(جبريل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن إسحاق); ASAD-NOTES(almost all of the commentators); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI]
- 18:23/q10/p2 The Prophet's unqualified promise led to a forty-day delay. — An account from Abū ʿAbd Allāh says Quraysh mocked, the Prophet grieved and doubted, and Gabriel explained that revelation only comes by God's permission. Al-Ālūsī and Abū Ḥayyān also record forty as a variant; al-Suyūṭī reports Ibn ʿAbbās's forty nights after the Prophet swore an oath. [4 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); ALUSI-FULL(ابن إسحاق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس)]
- 18:23/q10/p3 A report makes the delay three days. — Al-Ālūsī lists three alongside the fifteen-day and forty-day reports. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن إسحاق)]
- 18:23/q10/p4 The correction followed an unqualified promise, with no exact interval specified. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Jishumī, al-Durr, al-Bursawī and others state the occasion without fixing a number. Al-Durr says Gabriel did not arrive until God willed; Islahi presents the Cave question as possible rather than certain. [6 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; JISHUMI(قيل); DURR-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; JISHUMI-]

## 18:23/q11 Can this command be confined to the Prophet's failed promise?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the reported occasion limits the verse despite the general wording and questions about an authorized prophetic prediction.
- 18:23/q11/p1 No; the occasion yields a rule for everyone who plans future action. — Al-Jishumī prefers the general rule: an authorized prophetic prediction would not fail, and a failed one could repel people. Al-Sāmarrāʾī uses 24:33 to show an individual occasion can teach more broadly. Asad and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise generalize. [5 notes: JISHUMI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI; JISHUMI+]
- 18:23/q11/p2 It is tied specifically to the Prophet's promise to answer the questioners. — Al-Jishumī reports this view, which he rejects as a restriction; many occasion reports give that event as the immediate setting. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); JISHUMI-]

## 18:23/q12 What should one say when announcing a future action?  [إِنِّى فَاعِلٌۭ ذَٰلِكَ غَدًا · interpretation]
- 18:23/q12/p1 Qualify the announced plan with God's will, commonly by saying 'if God wills'. — Yaḥyā, al-Thaʿlabī's Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Wāḥidī, al-Jishumī's Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Ṭabarasī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Biqāʿī, and others apply it to a future resolve. Al-Māwardī says attach divine will rather than make an unconditional commitment. Al-Qāshānī treats it as courtesy after the dispute prohibition. Islahi says God may delay an answer. Kuran Yolu calls it commendable and notes Turkish inşallah and 18:69, 28:27, 37:102; Study Quran notes customary Muslim usage. Al-Thaʿlabī also reports Abū Hurayra's saying that faith is incomplete until one qualifies all speech with an exception. [24 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; THALABI(ابن عباس,رسول الله); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس,الأخفش); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); JISHUMI+]

## 18:23/q13 How is the exception 'unless God wills' grammatically connected to this prohibition?  [وَلَا تَقُولَنَّ إِنِّى فَاعِلٌۭ ذَٰلِكَ غَدًا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the exception qualifies the act of saying, the intended act, the kind of action, or the possibility of speaking at all; whether an omitted saying or preposition is needed.
- 18:23/q13/p1 An omitted verb of saying gives 'unless you say: if God wills'. — Al-Farrāʾ states this sense; al-Akhfash supplies 'say' in al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī, and al-Ṭabarasī. Al-Wāḥidī says brevity indicates the longer wording; al-Mubarrad, reporting al-Kisāʾī and al-Farrāʾ, says deletion shifts 'wills' to a future form. Ibn al-Jawzī also supplies the verb. Al-Ṭabarasī says it averts a false promise or broken oath. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that it strains the wording and changes the meaning. [6 notes: FARRA; MAWARDI-FULL(الأخفش); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش,المبرد عن الكسائي والفراء); TABRISI(الأخفش); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABATABAI(الأخفش)-]
- 18:23/q13/p2 It means one may state an intended action only by God's will, without inserting a verb of saying. — Al-Farrāʾ as reported by al-Ṭabarasī prefers the surface wording; al-Wāḥidī also reports his 'unless He intends'. Abū Isḥāq supplies an implied preposition and treats an as accusative. Al-Ṭabarasī says the obedience restriction need not ban speech about permissible acts. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,الفراء); TABRISI; TABRISI(الفراء)+]
- 18:23/q13/p3 The exception qualifies the circumstances or time of saying: speak while attaching God's permission. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this construction and supports ordinary permission with Moses, Shuʿayb, Ishmael, and the Prophet in 18:69, 28:27, 37:102, 48:27. [2 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 18:23/q13/p4 The exception modifies the intended act, allowing God to prevent an otherwise independent act. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this Muʿtazilī account and rejects it, citing Quranic constructions in 6:80, 7:89, and 6:111. [0 notes: TABATABAI(المعتزلة)-]
- 18:23/q13/p5 The Prophet may announce a promised disclosure only if God first authorizes it. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects this proposed implied object as unsupported and as requiring an unknowable duty. [0 notes: TABATABAI-]
- 18:23/q13/p6 The exception is perpetual, so no future intention may ever be stated. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects this proposal because 11:108, 10:41, and 18:83 attribute future actions to the Prophet and others. [0 notes: TABATABAI-]

## 18:23/q14 Does the exception restrict promises to obedient acts?  [إِنِّى فَاعِلٌۭ ذَٰلِكَ غَدًا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'God wills' names His legislative approval or His creative permission.
- 18:23/q14/p1 Yes; on one reading, one should promise only actions God wills, namely obedience. — Al-Ṭabarasī presents this as a possible consequence of al-Farrāʾ's literal reading. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the legislative interpretation but objects that Quranic usage supports creative will instead; he cites 18:69. [1 notes: TABRISI(الفراء); TABATABAI-]
- 18:23/q14/p2 No; permissible future acts may also be mentioned with the qualification. — Al-Ṭabarasī says the prohibition discourages omission rather than banning such reports, and consensus does not treat omission as sin. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says a legislative sense of divine will lacks Quranic evidence and cites 18:69 for creative will. [2 notes: TABRISI; TABATABAI]

## 18:23/q15 Why must a future promise be made conditional?  [إِنِّى فَاعِلٌۭ ذَٰلِكَ غَدًا · interpretation]
turns on: The notes give complementary grounds: uncertainty about fulfillment and dependence on divine will.
- 18:23/q15/p1 An obstacle or changed circumstances may prevent fulfillment and make an unconditional assertion false or an oath broken. — Al-Māwardī invokes Moses' qualified promise about patience in 18:70; al-Jubbāʾī in al-Ṭabarasī names illness, inability, or changed intention. Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Ṭabarasī also links the exception to obstacles; al-Biqāʿī notes that a failed assertion repels listeners. Al-Qurṭubī and al-Jishumī stress avoiding falsehood; al-Ālūsī says even distant plans face death. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this rationale for the omitted-saying interpretation but disputes that construction. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI(ابن عباس,الأخفش,الجبائي); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(الأخفش)]
- 18:23/q15/p2 A person lacks foreknowledge and independent control; God may delay, withhold, or empower the intended act. — Al-Māwardī points to divine power and assistance. Al-Qushayrī says events issue from God's will. Ibn Kathīr says God knows all unseen outcomes and counterfactuals. Elmalılı and Kuran Yolu cite 31:34 about tomorrow's unknown. Islahi says divine wisdom may defer an answer despite the Prophet's wish to vindicate the message. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites God's permission throughout creation. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]

## 18:23/q16 What does the verse imply about human agency and God's will?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether agents have efficacy independent of God's enabling will.
- 18:23/q16/p1 People act through power and permission granted by God; they should acknowledge His will rather than claim independent efficacy. — Al-Māwardī says God governs actions by power and assistance; al-Qāshānī says people act through Him. Al-Qushayrī counsels inward renunciation of personal power while resolving obedience. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God empowers causes or withholds effects and cites cutting trees 59:5, calamity 64:11, growth 7:58, death 3:145, faith 10:100, and messenger-obedience 4:64; rely on Him in 3:159. He notes Quranic attribution of actions to people in 10:41 and 28:55 remains valid. Elmalılı and Kuran Yolu cite 31:34 for ignorance of tomorrow. [13 notes: SULAMI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 18:23/q16/p2 One Muʿtazilī interpretation treats the act as independently caused, with God able to prevent it. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports and rejects the reading; he refers to Quranic usage at 6:80, 7:89, and 6:111. [0 notes: TABATABAI(المعتزلة)-]

## 18:23/q17 Must the exception accompany every future claim, including certain outcomes?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the condition handles only actual contingency or also teaches a general manner of speech.
- 18:23/q17/p1 It applies to possible future events, while a certain event need not receive the same exception. — Al-Jishumī distinguishes contingencies from certainties; he explains the conditional promise of entry to the Sacred Mosque in 48:27 by saying some addressees might die first. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 18:23/q17/p2 It is established practice to qualify every matter, even where God's promised outcome is certain. — Al-Zarkashī uses the promised entry to the Sacred Mosque in 48:27: its fulfillment was certain, but the phrasing teaches the practice commanded here. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 18:23/q18 Is omitting the qualification itself sinful?  [وَلَا تَقُولَنَّ · law]
- 18:23/q18/p1 No; the prohibition discourages omission, and consensus does not make it a sin. — Al-Ṭabarasī says this allows reports about permissible acts even on al-Farrāʾ's literal reading. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 18:23/q19 How does saying 'if God wills' affect an oath?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether the exception is attached when swearing or only after the oath has become binding.
- 18:23/q19/p1 The exception can avert expiation when the oath is broken. — Al-Māwardī gives the legal effect; the Prophet's Solomon report in Ibn Kathīr says he would not have broken his oath and would have attained his wish if he had said it. Al-Ṭabarasī's Ibn ʿAbbās links qualification to avoiding a broken oath. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي); TABRISI(ابن عباس)]
- 18:23/q19/p2 Whether a later exception qualifies an already binding oath is a separate unresolved legal question. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī defines thunyā as an exception, notes that the related reports suggest a decisive oath, and leaves their legal effect to jurisprudence. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 18:23/q20 Can a forgotten qualification be supplied later?  [* · law]
- 18:23/q20/p1 Yes; say it upon remembering, even after a long interval. — Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Thaʿlabī permits it even after a year; Yaḥyā and Islahi say to say or remember it upon recall. Ibn ʿAbbās in two al-Ṭabarānī reports via Jābir ibn Zayd and Mujāhid explains the instruction as remembering to say it. An account from ʿAlī allows an oath's exception within forty days and recalls the Prophet's delayed answer; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī leaves the binding-oath question to law. [6 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI(علي بن أبي طالب عليه السلام)]

## 18:23/q21 What does Solomon's unqualified promise illustrate?  [* · reports]
- 18:23/q21/p1 Solomon promised to visit many women, omitted 'if God wills', and his intended result failed. — The Prophet's report in Ibn Kathīr gives seventy women, with variants ninety and one hundred; an angel advised the qualification, but Solomon omitted it and only one woman bore half a person. Had he said it, the report says, he would have kept his oath and all sons would have become horsemen for God's cause. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي)]

## 18:23/q22 How does this instruction connect to nearby accounts and other verses?  [* · links]
- 18:23/q22/p1 It follows the Cave account and the Prophet's testing question, correcting a promise about its answer. — Al-Qāshānī places the courtesy after the prohibition of dispute. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes that the Cave story, unlike Dhū al-Qarnayn's in 18:83, lacks an opening explicit question formula, but this closing command hints at one. Study Quran's introduction links verse 6's rebuke of concern for Quraysh's disbelief to verses 23–24's warning; its note on 18:60 recalls the same advice. [4 notes: QASHANI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 18:23/q22/p2 Other Quranic future commitments model conditioning speech on God's will. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites Moses 18:69, Shuʿayb 28:27, Ishmael 37:102, and the Prophet 48:27; Kuran Yolu cites the first three. Al-Zarkashī also compares 48:27, even though God's promised entry is certain. [3 notes: TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BURHAN]

## 18:23/q23 What happened around the questioners besides the delayed answer?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports provide different accompanying details rather than competing explanations of the command.
- 18:23/q23/p1 Quraysh consulted Jews, tested the Prophet, then rejected the revelations. — Al-Durr reports Quraysh saying they knew no al-Raḥmān except Musaylima of Yamama, while the Jews recognized the expected prophet's description and time. The Jews suggested the Cave, Dhū al-Qarnayn and Spirit questions; al-Durr links the Spirit to 17:85. When the third answer matched Jewish information, Quraysh called Torah and Quran cooperating sorceries and rejected both, relating this to 28:48. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 18:23/q23/p2 The Prophet knew none of the answers before revelation, and a puppy in his house also delayed Gabriel's entry. — Al-Durr reports the Prophet explaining his distress and naming the puppy as another reason Gabriel did not enter. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 18:23/q24 What does 'guessing at the unseen' mean in the Cave discussion?  [* · meaning]
- 18:23/q24/p1 It is conjecture without certainty. — Abū ʿUbayda compares Zuhayr's phrase al-ḥadīth al-murajjam, an uncertain report; this note concerns the preceding Cave discussion. [1 notes: MAJAZ]

## 18:23/q25 What inward picture does the Cave story furnish near this instruction?  [* · inward]
- 18:23/q25/p1 The Cave company symbolizes inner faculties sheltered in the body's cave, while Decianus is the commanding ego. — Al-Bursawī names the spirit, heart, innate and acquired intellect, sacred power, and secret faculties. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 18:23/q26 What account of saints is included alongside this passage?  [* · inward]
- 18:23/q26/p1 Their ranks come through scrupulous truthfulness, pure intent, sound hearts and mercy, with humble and generous conduct. — Al-Bursawī reports al-Khiḍr: their attainment is not through abundant worship or adornment; they curse no one, do not harm or belittle those below, envy no one above, and are gentle and pleasant. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الخضر)]

## 18:23/q27 What Dajjal report is transmitted with the passage?  [* · reports]
- 18:23/q27/p1 The Dajjal will test believers, who are directed to seek God's protection and recite al-Kahf's opening passages. — Al-Durr reports Abū Umāma saying every prophet warned of him, he will emerge in this community, and God protects Muslims. The Prophet places his emergence between Iraq and Syria, with devastation on both sides and a claim of prophethood despite no prophet after Muhammad. Kāfir appears between his eyes for believers to read. He kills and revives one person but cannot repeat it with another. His paradise is fire and his fire paradise; a tested believer should close his eyes and seek God's help, and the fire will be cool like Abraham's. His forty days include one like a year, one like a month, one like a week, and an ordinary final day. [6 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو أمامة,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
