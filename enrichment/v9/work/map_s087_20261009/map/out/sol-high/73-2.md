# 73:2: 15 questions

## 73:2/q01 What does the command to rise mean?  [قُمِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether qiyām names formal prayer, Qurʾān recitation, prophetic work, or spiritual rising.
- 73:2/q01/p1 Rise for night prayer. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, Bursevī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Biqāʿī explain rising as prayer; Thaʿlab calls standing one part used for the whole prayer. Al-Qurṭubī says this metaphor became conventional. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes the warning command of 74:2 and excludes Maghrib, ʿIshāʾ and their regular sunnas. Biqāʿī says prayer grounds outward and inward worship. [17 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURHAN(ثعلب); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 73:2/q01/p2 Rise to recite the Qurʾān, without prayer necessarily being meant. — Abū Muslim says 73:20 allows manageable recitation; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī records the disagreement between prayer and recitation outside prayer. [2 notes: TABRISI(أبو مسلم); TABATABAI(القوم)]
- 73:2/q01/p3 Rise to undertake and convey the prophetic mission. — ʿIkrima takes the wrapping as assuming prophecy, with effort by night and day (73:7). Biqāʿī links rising to leaving the wrapping and carrying the burden of prophecy. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(عكرمة); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(عكرمة); TAB-FULL-]
- 73:2/q01/p4 Rise inwardly from heedlessness and journey toward God. — Al-Qāshānī describes traversal of the lower soul and the heart's stages. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 73:2/q01/p5 Rise for intimate communion with God while others sleep. — Qushayrī's reported Sufi addresses speak of the prophet's burden, his special gift, God's appointment and support; Bursevī's knowers speak of divine longing and the delight of communion. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال); BURSEVI(بعض العارفين)]

## 73:2/q02 How is the mīm of قُمِ read?  [قُمِ · readings]
turns on: How the adjoining sukūns are resolved: original kasra, vowel harmony, or ease.
- 73:2/q02/p1 With kasra, the common reading. — Al-Naḥḥās, al-Jishumī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, Bursevī and al-Ālūsī say kasra separates two sukūns; al-Naḥḥās says the original wāw is not restored because its vowel is unstable. Al-Samīn favors kasra as the grammarians' original rule; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says it is more usual Arabic. [10 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI(قراءة العامة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو السمال); QURTUBI-FULL(قراءة العامة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو الفتح); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 73:2/q02/p2 With ḍamma, following the vowel of qāf. — Abū al-Sammāl is named by al-Jishumī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Ālūsī; al-Ṭabrisī calls it noncanonical and suggests assimilation. Al-Rāzī instead reports ḍamma for the other readers; Quṭrub cites analogous imperatives. [10 notes: JISHUMI(أبو السمال العدوي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو السمال); TABRISI(أبو السماك); RAZI-FULL(أبو السمال,قطرب); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو السمال); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو السمال)]
- 73:2/q02/p3 With fatḥa, for ease of pronunciation. — Al-Rāzī attributes fatḥ to Abū al-Sammāl, unlike sources assigning him ḍamm; al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī report it without that attribution. Ibn Jinnī says any vowel breaks the two sukūns; Quṭrub gives parallels. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو السمال,ابن جني,قطرب); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو السمال,عثمان بن جني); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو الفتح); ALUSI-FULL]

## 73:2/q03 How does ٱلَّيْلَ function grammatically?  [ٱلَّيْلَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the accusative marks the time of an intransitive act or treats the time as its object.
- 73:2/q03/p1 It is an accusative adverb of time. — Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr use this analysis, attributed to the Basrans; standing is intransitive. Bursevī supplies an implicit fī and compares fasting a month, warning a day and reviving Laylat al-Qadr (2:185; 40:15). Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports an implied goal after 'rise'. [9 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 73:2/q03/p2 It is a direct object or accusative by semantic extension. — The Kufan analysis is reported by Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn; Bursevī reports some grammarians reading the surface form as an object. Al-Ālūsī notes attribution also to Basrans. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares saying one entered the house. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 73:2/q04 Which span of night is in view?  [ٱلَّيْلَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether 'night' denotes the full sunset-to-dawn interval, the interval after ʿIshāʾ, or a later portion for this vigil.
- 73:2/q04/p1 Night runs from sunset to dawn. — Al-Qurṭubī refers to his discussion in al-Baqara; Bursevī gives the same bounds. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 73:2/q04/p2 The relevant dark period is after ʿIshāʾ until dawn. — Ibn ʿĀshūr's adverbial analysis gives these limits and allows necessary sleep and like needs. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 73:2/q04/p3 The options concern the last two-thirds of night, after the first third assigned to evening prayer. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this explanation; al-Tabrīzī says the first third is for evening prayer. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(التبريزي)]
- 73:2/q04/p4 Night prayer begins after sleep in the latter half of night. — Iṣlāḥī calls half the night especially blessed and notes latitude because waking is hard. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 73:2/q05 What does إِلَّا قَلِيلًۭا except?  [إِلَّا قَلِيلًۭا · grammar]
turns on: The antecedent and scope of the exception: a nightly portion, whole nights missed, or a part of half.
- 73:2/q05/p1 A small part of each night is excepted from standing. — Al-Ṭabrisī calls qalīlan an accusative exception; al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and Biqāʿī make the initial scope nearly the whole night, with rest necessary because constant standing is impossible. Biqāʿī says the whole can stand for a portion every night. Al-Ālūsī prefers this clear construction. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 73:2/q05/p2 A few nights may be missed for a valid excuse. — Imam ʿAlī gives illness, overpowering sleep and eye trouble as examples. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says this especially fits a recommended duty; Abū Ḥayyān regards it as remote, and al-Ālūsī calls a related total-nights and pronoun analysis far-fetched. [2 notes: TABRISI(الإمام علي بن أبي طالب); IBNATIYYA-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)-; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]
- 73:2/q05/p3 The small exception is from half of the night. — Al-Nasafī offers this alternative: pray half, except a little of that half. Al-Zamakhsharī's related analyses are reported by Abū Ḥayyān, who objects that the ensuing reduction repeats the exception. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 73:2/q06 How large is the little part, and how much standing results?  [إِلَّا قَلِيلًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: The size of qalīl and how the following half, decrease and increase distribute the night.
- 73:2/q06/p1 Leave about a third for sleep and stand for two-thirds. — Ibn Qutayba identifies the little sleep as one third; al-Kalbī and Muqātil concur. Al-Rāzī cites 73:20 for two-thirds as the maximum; other analyses make the decrease from half reach one third. Reports vary in how subsequent options are calculated. Ibn Qutayba's Gharīb directs readers to his Kitāb al-Mushkil for this night-portioning discussion. [11 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة,الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(وهب بن منبه); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,الكلبي ومقاتل); RAZI-FULL; MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB]
- 73:2/q06/p2 The little part is less than half of night. — Al-Qurṭubī calls it less than half, while reporting narrower estimates; Abū Ḥayyān also reports this view. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]
- 73:2/q06/p3 The little part is less than a tenth or a sixth. — Wahb ibn Munabbih gives these alternative small estimates; al-Ālūsī reports their use in the legal-theory debate about excepting half or more. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(وهب بن منبه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(وهب بن منبه); ALUSI-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]
- 73:2/q06/p4 Half can be called 'little' relative to the whole. — Al-Rāzī reports this as a second reading and says a little beyond the stipulated half would secure compliance. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 73:2/q06/p5 The command allows half, somewhat less, or somewhat more. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Wāḥidī specify reduction toward a third and increase toward two-thirds; al-Ṭabarī calls this latitude. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī makes the half the pivot of the pronouns; al-Iṣlāḥī relates flexibility to difficulty waking and the varying night. [10 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 73:2/q06/p6 The options reach a quarter as the minimum and three-quarters as the maximum. — Al-Rāzī gives half, quarter and three quarters in one construction and says the quarter is obligatory and additions recommended. Elmalılı likewise gives a quarter to three quarters, with changing nights and circumstances; another Rāzī construction makes a third obligatory and two thirds the ceiling. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 73:2/q06/p7 Only necessary bodily rest and needs are excluded. — Al-Qāshānī applies the exception inwardly to rest, food, drink and bodily needs, amounting to half the time in the natural station and a quarter of the full day each. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 73:2/q07 How does the following 'half, less, or more' explain this command?  [قُمِ ٱلَّيْلَ إِلَّا قَلِيلًۭا · grammar]
turns on: What half apposes, whether a conjunction is omitted, and which amount the pronouns modify.
- 73:2/q07/p1 Half specifies the amount to be stood, with latitude above or below it. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī makes half a substitute for 'the night except a little' and prefers this natural order of stating the commanded object before details. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj and others construe the later options around half. Al-Nasafī also records that most readers except ʿĀṣim and Ḥamza give the wāw of inquṣ ḍamma. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 73:2/q07/p2 An omitted 'or' introduces half as another choice. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Akhfash posit ellipsis: the alternatives are the whole night less a little, or half, or less or more; al-Akhfash compares giving one, two, or three dirhams. Abū Ḥayyān rejects a similar half/half/third reconstruction for lacking a textual signal; al-Ālūsī finds al-Rāzī's version unconvincing. [2 notes: FARRA; AKHFASH; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى)-; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)-]
- 73:2/q07/p3 Half is appositive to the night, with a little excepted from half. — Al-Nasafī and al-Zamakhsharī are cited for this. Abū Ḥayyān rejects it as needless repetition of the following reduction. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 73:2/q07/p4 Half explicates the excepted little or the remaining night, with the calculations complementary. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the excepted-half alternative but prefers the commanded-half reading. Elmalılı says either time spent standing or remainder yields the same calculation, yet favors standing as the action addressed; Bursevī calls half an apposition to the remainder. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī reject some little-replacement parses because the pronoun and emphasis become confused. [5 notes: TABATABAI(قيل); ELMALILI; BURSEVI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ELMALILI+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 73:2/q07/p5 The extended and intricate constructions are unsound or unhelpful. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya rejects al-Jurjānī's treatment. Abū Ḥayyān rejects al-Zamakhsharī's speculative alternatives on grounds of natural Arabic. Al-Ālūsī prefers the clearest construction and says the quarter/third option has only a genitive reading as support; al-Rāzī's two analyses are noted by Abū Ḥayyān. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 73:2/q08 Why is night standing worded this way and commended?  [قُمِ ٱلَّيْلَ إِلَّا قَلِيلًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: Which stated purpose of the command the reader emphasizes: preparation, communion, rest, or consolation.
- 73:2/q08/p1 Night prayer prepares the Prophet for revelation and the burden of his mission. — Biqāʿī relates this sūra to the previous account of protected revelation and to intimate strengthening amid misguidance. Ibn ʿĀshūr invokes 73:5 and the angel's exhausting pressure before 96:1, reported in al-Bukhārī. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī ties it to patience under abuse; Iṣlāḥī says it renews energy, faith and resolve. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(البخاري); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 73:2/q08/p2 The privacy of night fosters prayerful communion and wider worship. — Biqāʿī says prayer is named because it grounds outward and inward works; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the Prophet's special night duty an honor of intimate devotion. Qushayrī says the address honors his capacity and rank. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; QUSHAYRI(ويقال)]
- 73:2/q08/p3 The small exception gives needed bodily rest and portrays worship as greater than sleep. — Al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān say all-night standing cannot be sustained; Elmalılı says the normal smaller-exception form favors standing beyond half and makes sleep little in value beside remembrance. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 73:2/q08/p4 The address gently consoles the Prophet and leads toward patience and later relief. — Ibn al-Zubayr sees worship and remembrance after the jinn accepted while other hearers failed; Biqāʿī adduces 35:8, 70:5, 73:10–12 and 50:45 for patience amid opposition, then 73:20 for merciful relief. Qushayrī's mystical frame says all acts depend on God and worshippers recognize their insufficiency. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); QUSHAYRI]

## 73:2/q09 What did early observance of the command look like?  [* · reports]
turns on: The transmitted incident or practice being described.
- 73:2/q09/p1 The Prophet and companions maintained long vigils, sometimes all night until their feet or legs swelled. — Qatāda, al-Ḥasan, Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān, ʿĀʾisha and others report the hardship. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Rāzī say uncertainty about measuring portions made some stand to dawn. Ibn ʿAbbās compares the amount to Ramadan prayer; al-Ṭabarī and later transmitters link relief to 73:20. [29 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(أبو عبد الرحمن,ابن عباس,الحسن,قتادة); THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(عائشة); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,عائشة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو عبد الرحمن,ابن عباس,عائشة,قتادة); DURR-FULL(الحسن); IBNASHUR-FULL(عائشة); STUDYQURAN]
- 73:2/q09/p2 The Prophet prayed a measured night vigil and made up a missed one by day. — ʿĀʾisha reports eight rakʿahs, remembrance and supplication while sitting, a ninth and two seated; later seven plus two seated. After sleep, pain or illness he prayed twelve by day; he did not finish the Qurʾān or stay up all night, and did not fast an entire month outside Ramadan. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عائشة)]
- 73:2/q09/p3 He declined continued congregational prayer because he feared it would become binding. — ʿĀʾisha describes people gathering around his prayer on a mat or behind a screen; he stayed away, sometimes explaining only after dawn, and urged manageable lasting works. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the screened Ramadan report sound; Ibn Kathīr reports his compassionate displeasure. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(عائشة); THALABI(عائشة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(رسول الله); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عائشة); IBNASHUR-FULL(عائشة)]
- 73:2/q09/p4 Saʿīd ibn Hishām sought ʿĀʾisha's account of the Prophet's night prayer. — Ibn Kathīr's report says Saʿīd had planned to sell property and fight the Romans until death, his townsmen cited the Prophet's earlier prohibition to six men, ʿĀʾisha said the Prophet's character was the Qurʾān, and Ibn ʿAbbās affirmed her account and wished to hear it directly. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن هشام,عائشة)]
- 73:2/q09/p5 The first-revelation report depicts the Prophet asking to be wrapped and being reassured by Khadīja. — Biqāʿī reports his fear after Ḥirāʾ and her appeal to his care for kin, guests and those in need. Qatāda and al-Ḍaḥḥāk read the wrapping as literal preparation for prayer; al-Ṭabarī prefers this apparent sense over ʿIkrima's figurative one. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL(قتادة); TAB-FULL+]

## 73:2/q10 How long did the first night-vigil regime last?  [* · reports]
turns on: The differing transmitted interval between the opening command and its easing.
- 73:2/q10/p1 About one year. — Ibn ʿAbbās and ʿĀʾisha reports place roughly a year between the opening and ending of the sūra; Qatāda sometimes says twelve months. Ibn Kathīr notes multiple routes, and al-Ḥasan and Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān also report a year. [18 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو عبد الرحمن,ابن عباس,الحسن,قتادة); THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(عائشة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عائشة وابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,عائشة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو عبد الرحمن,ابن عباس,عائشة); DURR-FULL(الحسن)]
- 73:2/q10/p2 Eight months. — ʿĀʾisha's congregation account gives eight months; Ibn Kathīr says the route through Mūsā ibn ʿUbayda is weak and conflicts with Aḥmad's year report, while the sound report lacks the claim that the sūra was revealed at that gathering. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(عائشة); THALABI(عائشة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عائشة وابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عائشة); IBNASHUR-FULL(عائشة); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 73:2/q10/p3 A year or two. — Qatāda's wording varies; the vigil continued until legs and feet swelled. [5 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 73:2/q10/p4 Ten years. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr says the Prophet and some companions kept the commanded vigil until the closing passage relieved them. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(سعيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعضهم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 73:2/q10/p5 Sixteen months. — Al-Māwardī transmits this alternative; an ʿĀʾisha report says the ending was held in heaven for this time before it descended. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عائشة)]

## 73:2/q11 On whom was night vigil binding when this command was given?  [* · law]
turns on: The imperative's force, the singular addressee, the reports of communal practice, and whether 17:79 calls the vigil extra duty or supererogation.
- 73:2/q11/p1 It was obligatory for the Prophet and community. — Al-Qurṭubī calls the view of ʿĀʾisha and Ibn ʿAbbās correct, invoking the Muslim report and arguing that a merely recommended act would not fix an unspecified night portion. Al-Rāzī reports that the imperative's apparent force is obligation and a prophet's duty may extend to followers (7:158). Reports describe the companions' burden. [26 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,سعيد,عائشة,عكرمة والحسن,قتادة); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(عائشة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(آخرون,بعضهم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(جماعة من المفسرين); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس,بعض العلماء); QURTUBI-FULL(عائشة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عباس، وعكرمة، ومجاهد، والحسن، وقتادة,عائشة); DURR-FULL(الحسن); TABATABAI(القوم); IBNASHUR-FULL(عائشة); QURTUBI-FULL(عائشة وابن عباس)+]
- 73:2/q11/p2 It was obligatory only for the Prophet; the community followed voluntarily. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and others cite the singular address. Ibn ʿAbbās says earlier prophets also had this duty, citing 17:79; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the companions imitated a permitted practice praised by 32:16. Some say his duty lasted to death, with 17:79 an additional nearness. [17 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعضهم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قوم); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(القوم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 73:2/q11/p3 Night vigil was strongly recommended for everyone and never obligatory. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites most scholars and the Prophet's fear of future congregational obligation. Al-Ṭabrisī prefers this without needing abrogation. Al-Naḥḥās cites the freedom to reduce or increase. Al-Rāzī's non-obligation arguments cite 17:79 as nāfila, default permissibility of omission, and the imperative's shared sense of preference; he also reports a counter-reading of nāfila as additional duty. [11 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL(رسول الله); RAZI-FULL(القائلون بعدم الوجوب,بعض العلماء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); TABATABAI(القوم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور أهل العلم)+; TABRISI+; QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 73:2/q12 What later changed the night-vigil ruling?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the opening was obligatory, whether 73:20 itself removed it, whether five prayers or 17:79 changed it, and whether the Prophet retained a special duty.
- 73:2/q12/p1 The closing verse 73:20 eased or abrogated the initial obligation for the community, leaving voluntary night prayer. — Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿĀʾisha, al-Ḥasan and other reports cite manageable recitation and mercy after hardship. Ibn al-Jawzī reports that advocates called this the Qurʾān's only case of a sūra's end abrogating its beginning. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Prophet's duty could remain; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī records disagreement over abrogation versus alteration to manageable recitation and challenges a report of abrogation. [40 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(أبو عبد الرحمن,ابن زيد,ابن عباس,سعيد,عائشة,عكرمة والحسن,قتادة); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); THALABI(عائشة); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء الخراساني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(عائشة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عائشة وابن عباس,قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(جماعة من المفسرين,قوم); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وعائشة,عائشة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو عبد الرحمن,ابن عباس,ابن عباس، وعكرمة، ومجاهد، والحسن، وقتادة,عائشة); DURR-FULL(الحسن); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(عائشة); TABATABAI(ابن عباس وغيره,القوم); STUDYQURAN]
- 73:2/q12/p2 The five daily prayers superseded the night-prayer obligation. — Al-Farrāʾ analogizes zakāt replacing charity and Ramaḍān replacing other fasts. Muqātil, Ibn Kaysān and later reports locate the night command before the five; al-Wāḥidī also mentions 73:20 as relief before this supersession. One account makes the five replace the believers' duty while 17:79 replaces the Prophet's. [11 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(مقاتل وابن كيسان); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل وابن كيسان); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قوم); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 73:2/q12/p3 The duty remained for the Prophet after relief to the community. — Qushayrī and Ibn al-Jawzī report continuing obligation, while Ibn ʿĀshūr argues 17:79 gives him additional nearness and Kūran Yolu says most commentators maintain it. Another report says the Prophet's obligation too was lifted before death. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعضهم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قوم); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler)]
- 73:2/q12/p4 The original command was recommended, so no abrogation was needed. — Al-Ṭabrisī expressly favors this. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports majority recommendation; al-Rāzī says abrogation is contrary to default. The community's later sunnah status is reported by Kūran Yolu and the Study Quran cites 17:79 for supererogation. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور أهل العلم); RAZI-FULL(بعض العلماء); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; TABRISI+]

## 73:2/q13 When and where was this command revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The Meccan chronology of the opening and the dating of the closing relief and congregation report.
- 73:2/q13/p1 The command came in Mecca before the five daily prayers. — Muqātil, al-Zajjāj, Muqātil and Ibn Kaysān as cited by al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr give this chronology. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the sūra one of the earliest, reportedly second or third. [13 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(مقاتل وابن كيسان); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء الخراساني); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل وابن كيسان); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 73:2/q13/p2 The sūra's opening was Meccan and its easing passage later or Medinan. — Al-Zajjāj excepts the final two verses as Medinan. ʿAṭāʾ al-Khurāsānī says the command was given in Mecca and abrogated after the Prophet reached Medina; Ibn ʿAbbās has a similar narration. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء الخراساني); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 73:2/q13/p3 A congregational-night-prayer account associates the opening with a later gathering, but that chronology is weak. — Ibn Kathīr says the Mūsā ibn ʿUbayda route is weak, the sound ḥadīth omits the claimed revelation at that occasion, and its implied Medinan setting conflicts with a Meccan sūra and the stronger year interval. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عائشة); IBNASHUR-FULL(عائشة); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]

## 73:2/q14 When is prayer especially favored within the night?  [ٱلَّيْلَ · reports]
- 73:2/q14/p1 The latter part of night is favored for prayer and supplication. — Al-Qurṭubī says reports incline people to the end rather than beginning. Reports from Abū Hurayra and Abū Saʿīd put the divine call after the first third, half, two thirds, or when the last third remains; the Nasāʾī version authenticated by ʿAbd al-Ḥaqq has a caller after half, which al-Qurṭubī says clarifies midnight. Ibn ʿAbbās reports the Prophet rose around midnight. The Study Quran repeats the last-third report; Qushayrī leaves its interpretation uncertain and notes lovers' comforts before settling it. [10 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة,أبو هريرة وأبو سعيد,ابن عباس,علماؤنا); STUDYQURAN(a famous ḥadīth); QUSHAYRI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 73:2/q15 How does the night command connect with other Qurʾānic worship passages?  [* · links]
- 73:2/q15/p1 Recitation and remembrance accompany the night command, while remembrance extends through day and night. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the tartīl clause coordinates standing with distinct Qurʾān recitation. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the unqualified command to remember is likewise coordinated but extends to day as 76:25 does. [2 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 73:2/q15/p2 The Qurʾān elsewhere praises voluntary night worship. — The Study Quran cites 3:113, 11:114, 21:20, 20:130, 25:63–64, 50:40, 52:49, 73:6, 73:20 and 76:26, including among upright People of the Book. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 73:2/q15/p3 Prayer and patience jointly strengthen a person under distress. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares 2:45, directing the Prophet to prayer and patience instead of sleeping under the wrap. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
