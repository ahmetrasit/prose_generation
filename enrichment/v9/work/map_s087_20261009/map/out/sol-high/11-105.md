# 11:105: 27 questions

## 11:105/q01 What span of time does يوم name?  [يَوْمَ · meaning]
- 11:105/q01/p1 A time, period, or occasion rather than daylight alone. — Abū ʿAlī and al-Ṭabrisī distinguish the span from daylight; Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites ʿUthmān and Abū Bakr's usage; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites al-Nābigha and Tawba for moment or hour. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 11:105/q02 What comes in يَأْتِ, and what does its coming mean?  [يَأْتِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the subject is the prior Day, God, a suppressed event, the appointed term, or people; a self-referential day raises an annexation or temporal objection.
- 11:105/q02/p1 The previously mentioned Day of Gathering or Resurrection arrives. — Ṭabrisī and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite 11:103; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 43:66. Ibn Jurayj calls it the appointed day; al-Ālūsī treats the significant Day as an event distinct from its time. Al-Zamakhsharī says its terrors arrive, while al-Ṭabrisī says resurrection and recompense arrive. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جريج); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 11:105/q02/p2 God comes. — Al-Zamakhsharī adduces 2:210, 6:158 and the yāʾ reading of يؤخره; al-Bayḍāwī and Abū Ḥayyān relay it. Al-Rāzī objects that the quoted verses do not prove the claim and 89:22 admits interpretations. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); RAZI-FULL-]
- 11:105/q02/p3 A tremendous, terrifying event comes, with its subject suppressed. — Al-Rāzī prefers ellipsis for heightened warning; al-Bayḍāwī allows requital. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 11:105/q02/p4 The previously appointed term comes. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī refers to the term for which Resurrection was delayed and cites 29:5. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 11:105/q02/p5 The people come. — Al-Aʿmash's plural reading and Ibn Masʿūd's codex have يأتون; ʿUmar ibn Dharr's codex report adds 'no creature among them speaks.' [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأعمش); ALUSI-FULL(الأعمش); DURR-FULL(عمر بن ذر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عاصم وابن عامر وحمزة وابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو والكسائي والأعمش)]
- 11:105/q02/p6 The coming is Resurrection and recompense, referred to contextually. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports al-Majmaʿ's contextual pronoun. [1 notes: TABATABAI(المجمع)]
- 11:105/q02/p7 The day is an indefinite time within which a specified Day arrives. — Al-Manār proposes this; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that a time cannot contain itself. Al-Fārisī also disallows making an annexed day its verb's subject; tanwīn would remove annexation. [5 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; TABRISI(أبو عثمان المازني); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي); TABATABAI(صاحب المنار)-]

## 11:105/q03 How is يَأْتِ read and why?  [يَأْتِ · readings]
turns on: Whether the transmitted route preserves final yāʾ, drops it at pause, or drops it throughout; codex spelling, Hudhayli usage, and grammatical regularity are cited.
- 11:105/q03/p1 Retain the final yāʾ in connected reading and omit it at pause. — Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr and al-Kisāʾī are repeatedly named; a Basran and Kufan report agrees. Pause parallels verse endings or loss of a vowel, although al-Naḥḥās prefers connection and avoiding a stop. The Nāshir adds the Medinans. Reports differ on Ibn Kathīr. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض قراء أهل البصرة وبعض الكوفيين); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو والكسائي); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عاصم وابن عامر وحمزة وابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو والكسائي والأعمش); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو والكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي,النحاس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(نافع); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو والكسائي ونافع); NASHR(المدنيان وأبو عمرو والكسائي وابن كثير ويعقوب); ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جعفر النحاس)+]
- 11:105/q03/p2 Omit the final yāʾ in both connection and pause. — ʿĀṣim, Ibn ʿĀmir and Ḥamza are named; the Uthmanic spelling omits it. Al-Ṭabarī prefers this and cites Hudhayl and poetry; al-Farrāʾ allows a matching kasra to replace silent yāʾ, al-Zajjāj cites frequent lā adrī, and others cite taʿṭī. Abū ʿUbayd invokes the codex, but al-Naḥḥās disputes its evidentiary status and analogy. Al-Biqāʿī and al-Sāmarrāʾī read the shortened form as near arrival or little speech. [49 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(جماعة من أهل الكوفة); ZAJJAJ(الخليل وسيبويه,النحويون); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو والكسائي); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج وسيبويه والخليل,الفراء); JISHUMI(ابن عامر وعاصم وحمزة); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عاصم وابن عامر وحمزة وابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو والكسائي والأعمش); TABRISI(ابن عامر وأهل عرفة غير الكسائي,الزجاج,سيبويه والخليل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الخليل وسيبويه,الزجاج,عاصم وابن عامر وحمزة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأعمش وحمزة,الزجاج,القشيري,سيبويه والخليل); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن عامر وعاصم وحمزة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,باقي السبعة); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو والكسائي ونافع,ابن جرير,الزمخشري وابن جرير); NASHR(المدنيان وأبو عمرو والكسائي وابن كثير ويعقوب); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد)+; TAB-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس)-]
- 11:105/q03/p3 Retain yāʾ in both connection and pause. — Ibn Kathīr, Yaʿqūb and reports from Ubayy are cited; Ubayy's codex retains it. Grammarians call retention original and regular, since the yāʾ is a root letter. Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī prefer it; al-Zajjāj calls it grammatically better though he follows transmitted recitation. [21 notes: TAB-FULL(عامة قراء أهل المدينة); ZAJJAJ(النحويون); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو والكسائي); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عاصم وابن عامر وحمزة وابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو والكسائي والأعمش); TABRISI(أبو علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو والكسائي,الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جعفر النحاس,أبي وابن مسعود,الزجاج); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن كثير); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو والكسائي ونافع); NASHR(المدنيان وأبو عمرو والكسائي وابن كثير ويعقوب); QIRAAT-ER; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 11:105/q03/p4 A plural يأتون is read. — Al-Aʿmash and Ibn Masʿūd's codex are reported; al-Ālūsī interprets the subject as gathered people. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عاصم وابن عامر وحمزة وابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو والكسائي والأعمش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأعمش); ALUSI-FULL(الأعمش); DURR-FULL(عمر بن ذر)]
- 11:105/q03/p5 The hamza is eased or the tāʾ given sukūn in a pause. — Qirāʾāt ER says easing the hamza changes pronunciation without meaning, and pausing with sukūn has no semantic effect. [2 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 11:105/q04 What governs the accusative يَوْمَ?  [يَوْمَ يَأْتِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the time expression attaches directly to the speech verb or needs a supplied governor.
- 11:105/q04/p1 The following لا تكلم governs it. — Al-Ṭabrisī prefers this because it needs no ellipsis and time nouns attach to verbs; al-Samīn, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr also prefer it. Al-Zamakhsharī and others allow it. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+; TABRISI+]
- 11:105/q04/p2 An implied 'remember' governs it. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī offer this ellipsis. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:105/q04/p3 The omitted ending of the fixed term governs it. — The time is when the term in 11:104 reaches its end. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:105/q04/p4 An omitted repetition of لا تكلم governs it. — Al-Samīn reports this fourth proposal but says it is unnecessary. [0 notes: SAMIN-DURR-]

## 11:105/q05 How does لا تَكَلَّمُ نَفْسٌ relate syntactically to the Day?  [لَا تَكَلَّمُ نَفْسٌ · grammar]
turns on: Which clause relation and ellipsis are adopted.
- 11:105/q05/p1 It begins a new sentence whose fronted time adverb is governed by its verb. — Ibn ʿĀshūr describes the sentence as resuming the discourse; al-Ṭabrisī prefers direct government. [2 notes: TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 11:105/q05/p2 It is a circumstantial clause about the arriving Day. — Al-Wāḥidī, Abū ʿAlī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya supply a pronoun or 'in it'; al-Ḥawfī relates it to the Day of 'witnessed'. Ibn ʿAṭiyya excludes making it a circumstance of the self-annexed phrase. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو علي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الحوفي); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية,الحوفي)]
- 11:105/q05/p3 It is an adjective modifying an indefinite يوم. — Indefiniteness follows attachment to a verb; 'in it' is understood. Al-Wāḥidī notes that the adjectival clause can be omitted. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو علي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الحوفي); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية,الحوفي)]
- 11:105/q05/p4 It is the predicate of يوم يأتي. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya also allows this analysis. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 11:105/q06 Why does تَكَلَّمُ lack a second tāʾ?  [تَكَلَّمُ · grammar]
turns on: Deletion for ease versus a transmitted assimilated form.
- 11:105/q06/p1 One of the two initial tāʾs of تتكلم is regularly omitted for ease. — Al-Akhfash says the weaker second tāʾ is dropped because the sequence is heavy; a connecting hamza cannot replace it in this verb class. Al-Ṭabarī says the surviving tāʾ signals the omitted one; al-Thaʿlabī and al-Ṭabrisī cite analogous forms and poetry. Al-Biqāʿī makes its brevity signify very little speech. [11 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; THALABI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 11:105/q06/p2 A reading assimilates the tāʾ. — Qirāʾāt ER describes idghām with added phonetic gravity and unchanged meaning; al-Nashr lists al-Bazzī's doubling route among future verbs including this verse. [2 notes: QIRAAT-ER; NASHR(البزي)]

## 11:105/q07 What speech requires God's permission, and how do the speech passages fit together?  [لَا تَكَلَّمُ نَفْسٌ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِۦ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'speech' means all utterance, useful defense, good words, a particular station, or compelled disclosure.
- 11:105/q07/p1 No soul may speak at all unless God permits it. — The indefinite soul under negation includes all people and all speech, says Ibn ʿĀshūr; 78:38 and 20:108 are parallels cited by al-Kashshāf, Ibn Kathīr and others. Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports Saʿīd ibn Jubayr's formulation in God's presence; an intercession hadith says only messengers speak, praying for safety. Elmalılı connects permission to the fortunate. [17 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله); BAYDAWI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 11:105/q07/p2 Only useful speech, especially defense, accepted proof, or intercession, needs permission. — Al-Ṭabrisī prefers intercession or mediation; al-Māwardī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī report versions. Confession, blame and empty argument occur but count as no speech; al-Zajjāj and al-Ṭabrisī compare figurative deafness in 2:18 and 2:171 and cite poetry. Al-Ālūsī prefers saying excuses are not heard or accepted. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that the verse does not restrict speech to another person and that 'no speech' then lacks a coherent permission clause. [22 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ZAJJAJ(الشاعر); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية,السيد المرتضى); TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; TABRISI+; TABATABAI-]
- 11:105/q07/p3 Only good or truthful answers are permitted, with evil or false excuses barred. — Al-Māwardī and al-Jubbāʾī say creatures are compelled to leave evil speech; al-Bayḍāwī permits truthful answers. Al-Ālūsī allows false claims when God exposes them. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the moral restriction unsupported because this is a place of requital and excuses are not sinful acts. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 11:105/q07/p4 The long Resurrection has different stations or moments, some silent and others speaking by permission. — Al-Zajjāj finds this and the ineffective-speech account both sound; al-Ḥasan and later writers cite 77:35–36, 37:24, 55:39, arguments and bodily testimony. Al-Ālūsī defends differing locations and moments; Elmalılı says one station allows no excuses. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this still depends on permission and disputes its fit with the verse's scope. [18 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قوم,قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن,السيد المرتضى,بعض الفضلاء,قوم من المفسرين); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL+; ZAJJAJ(قوم)+; TABATABAI(المرتضى)-]
- 11:105/q07/p5 Afterlife speech is compelled disclosure, unlike voluntary worldly speech. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says inner states are manifest, records and limbs testify (36:65, 55:39–41), so ordinary sound based disclosure is unnecessary. Speech by permission reveals God's sovereignty already true in this life; lies can arise as ingrained habits but cannot deceive God. He links this to requital without new choice and takes 78:38 as reconciling affirmations and denials; he rejects al-Manār's bare reconciliation. [20 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم); TABATABAI(صاحب المنار)-]
- 11:105/q07/p6 No excuse is requested because there is no duty then and terror forces confession. — Al-Ālūsī reports this distinct explanation of 77:35–36. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض المفسرين)]
- 11:105/q07/p7 The words concern only the first moment when the Day arrives. — A scholar reported by al-Ālūsī confines the statement to that moment. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض الفضلاء)]
- 11:105/q07/p8 The exception may be disconnected: none speaks by independent power, but God can permit speech. — Al-Ālūsī offers connected and disconnected analyses; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the latter as true of every human act in every age. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]

## 11:105/q08 Whose permission is بِإِذْنِهِۦ, and what does its bāʾ do?  [بِإِذْنِهِۦ · grammar]
- 11:105/q08/p1 It is God's permission, accompanying each permitted utterance. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links the pronoun to God and 78:38; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says bāʾ marks accompaniment, so the exception qualifies speech rather than selecting speakers. Ibn ʿĀshūr says this refutes assumed idol intercession. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 11:105/q08/p2 Permission and sole control become manifest on that Day. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says divine permission governs all events always, but the veil lifts then; he compares 40:16, 40:33, 82:19, 50:22, 32:12 and 10:28–30. [3 notes: TABATABAI]

## 11:105/q09 To whom does مِنْهُمْ refer?  [مِنْهُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the antecedent is the earlier gathering, the generic soul, or Muhammad's community.
- 11:105/q09/p1 All gathered people, explicit in 11:103 or implicit in 'no soul'. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Samīn and others point to the earlier gathering; al-Samīn prefers its explicit الناس. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the division an elaboration after the postponement parenthesis. [13 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 11:105/q09/p2 The generic singular نفس supplies a collective antecedent. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Wāḥidī say it includes all souls; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it generic, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes the shift from singular negation to plural division. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 11:105/q09/p3 The community of Muhammad. — Ibn al-Anbārī reports it; al-Ālūsī calls the restriction very strange. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الأنباري); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 11:105/q10 What do شَقِىٌّ and سَعِيدٌ mean in language?  [شَقِىٌّ سَعِيدٌ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the terms are described as living conditions, enabling causes, or divine aid.
- 11:105/q10/p1 Wretchedness is hardship and distress; felicity is the contrary ease and pleasant good. — Al-Wāḥidī cites a camel poem for mutual hardship; al-Jishumī defines the strength of causes of harm and blessing; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the adjectives immersion in those states. Labīd's lines illustrate deprivation versus honor or fortune. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī gives a technical account of attained bodily and spiritual good versus privation. [17 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(شيخنا); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر,لبيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو نصر عبد الرحيم القشيري,الجوهري,الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البحر,الراغب); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 11:105/q10/p2 Divine aid to attain good, with its opposite as wretchedness. — Al-Ālūsī reports al-Rāghib's definition. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الراغب)]
- 11:105/q10/p3 The forms have distinct transitive and dialectal uses. — Abū Ḥayyān cites ḍamma readings and lexicographers; al-Farrāʾ reports Hudhayl سعده الله; al-Jawharī distinguishes سعد بالكسر فهو سعيد from سعد فهو مسعود; al-Qushayrī distinguishes مسعود from مسعد. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو نصر عبد الرحيم القشيري,الجوهري,الفراء)]

## 11:105/q11 Who are the wretched and happy here?  [شَقِىٌّ سَعِيدٌ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the terms name final destinations, faith, prior inscription, a temporary state, or the speech privilege.
- 11:105/q11/p1 Those destined respectively for Hell's punishment and Paradise's bliss. — Al-Kashshāf ties destinations to evil and good deeds; al-Bayḍāwī to threat and promise; Ibn Kathīr cites 42:7. Al-Ālūsī reports the religious usage. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says pardoned persons can be happy from the outset through good or intercession. [13 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI]
- 11:105/q11/p2 Unbelievers and believers. — Al-Dāmghānī and exegetes cited by Ibn al-Jawzī give this contrast. [2 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 11:105/q11/p3 Those whose misery or felicity was written beforehand. — Ibn ʿAbbās and later reports use this gloss; al-Biqāʿī adds that worldly conduct follows what was fixed. Al-Qāshānī takes the indefinite forms to exalt pre-eternal to eternal states. [8 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; QASHANI]
- 11:105/q11/p4 Major sinners among Muslims may be wretched during temporary punishment, then enter Paradise by intercession. — Ibn ʿAbbās says the categories are hidden matters; the exception describes release from Hell. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī formulates this extraction account generally, while rejecting a division restricted to selected sinners. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; TABATABAI-]
- 11:105/q11/p5 The silent, disgraced are wretched; those permitted to speak are fortunate. — Elmalılı draws this link between permission and the division. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 11:105/q11/p6 The groups can describe distinct respects or gradations in a person. — Al-Bayḍāwī says both states may coexist in different respects; Ibn ʿĀshūr says they have degrees. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 11:105/q12 Does the twofold division include everyone gathered?  [شَقِىٌّ سَعِيدٌ · theology]
turns on: Whether an apparently exhaustive division is logically exhaustive by wording or by surrounding context.
- 11:105/q12/p1 The context ultimately sends everyone gathered to one of the two outcomes. — Al-Qāḍī argues there is no equal third state or permanent People of the Heights; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says even incapable persons ultimately join a group, comparing 9:106 and 42:7–8. Al-Bayḍāwī says the assembly is covered. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); BAYDAWI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 11:105/q12/p2 The two nouns alone do not exclude a third or exempt group. — Al-Rāzī excludes children and the mentally ill from accounting and says naming two groups does not logically rule out a third, including equal recompense; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī agrees about the surface wording and names incapable people. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL+]

## 11:105/q13 Are wretchedness and happiness fixed by decree or reached through deeds?  [شَقِىٌّ سَعِيدٌ · theology]
turns on: Whether the inscription describes an unconditional fate, a recompense for action, or an outcome decreed through chosen causes.
- 11:105/q13/p1 They are inscribed by an already settled decree, with each person eased toward the deeds of that end. — ʿUmar's repeated report says pens have run; ʿAlī's funeral report commands work and cites 92:5–10. A womb report and Ibn Masʿūd say destiny is known before birth; Ibn ʿUmar's two unalterable books say ends match deeds. Al-Rāzī argues a change would falsify God's knowledge, while al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that a future report does not make its state present now. [27 notes: TAB-FULL(عمر); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الله بن مسعود,عمر); THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عمر); MAWARDI-FULL(عمر); JISHUMI(عمر); BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL(عمر); QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); DURR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عمر,عمر بن الخطاب); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر,الحسن,الرازي,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم,علي,عمر,عمر بن الخطاب,عمران بن الحصين); RAZI-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 11:105/q13/p2 Their own obedient or disobedient deeds are the cause of the outcomes. — Ibn Baḥr calls the states recompense; al-Ḥasan's reported formula says each is wretched or happy by deeds. Al-Jishumī says God knows in the womb but does not punish an innocent; cites 51:56 and natural disposition, and rejects prenatal wretchedness. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses faith and piety versus shirk and sin and cites 53:39–41. [17 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); JISHUMI(رسول الله,عمر); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الحسن); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI(علي); JISHUMI-]
- 11:105/q13/p3 God initiates wretchedness or happiness without recompense for deeds. — Al-Māwardī reports this second view; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects a deterministic reading that erases genuine choice and action. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 11:105/q13/p4 Decree fixes outcomes through complete causes that include voluntary human action. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes necessity relative to a complete cause from possibility relative to human will, using fire and wood. A truthful forecast does not bring future state into the present; prophetic facilitation presupposes a path and does not make deeds irrelevant. He says 11:105 itself does not settle intrinsic versus acquired status. [22 notes: TABATABAI]
- 11:105/q13/p5 The decree makes deeds irrelevant, or compels them with no real choice. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī presents these inferred deterministic claims in order to reject them; their premise severs the causal path from deeds to outcome. [2 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI-]

## 11:105/q14 What was reported when the verse raised questions about destiny?  [شَقِىٌّ سَعِيدٌ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in speaker and subject, while the decree reports share the settled writing and facilitation formula.
- 11:105/q14/p1 ʿUmar asked if deeds concern a settled decree; the Prophet said it was settled and each person is facilitated for their end. — The versions mention dried pens and flowing fates; al-Tirmidhī grades the route ḥasan or ḥasan gharīb. ʿAlī's funeral account adds the command to work and 92:5–10; ʿImrān ibn Ḥuṣayn asks a related question in Bukhārī. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(عمر); IBNABIHATIM(عمر); THALABI(عمر); MAWARDI-FULL(عمر); BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL(عمر); QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); DURR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); ALUSI-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم,علي,عمر,عمر بن الخطاب,عمران بن الحصين)]
- 11:105/q14/p2 A prophetic report says one is wretched or happy from the womb. — Ibn Masʿūd invokes womb status; al-Ālūsī explains the report as an angel learning the decree or disclosure of prior knowledge. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عبد الله بن مسعود); THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:105/q14/p3 The Prophet showed two complete books listing the people of Paradise and Hell. — Ibn ʿUmar's account says the books cannot be altered, each end matches deeds, and servants should continue acting. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عمر)]
- 11:105/q14/p4 People speak Syriac on Resurrection Day. — Al-Shaʿbī gives this report. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(الشعبي)]
- 11:105/q14/p5 Ibn ʿAbbās called the division and the messengers' reply in 5:109 hidden matters. — The notes connect the unseen division to the eventual release of major sinners. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 11:105/q14/p6 Abū Jaʿfar distinguished the exceptions for Hell and Paradise. — Abū Baṣīr reports that the Paradise exception is followed by 'an unsevered gift'. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر)]

## 11:105/q15 Why use these short or indefinite forms and this order?  [يَأْتِ تَكَلَّمُ شَقِىٌّ سَعِيدٌ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether brevity, indefiniteness, sequence, or structural division carries the rhetorical point.
- 11:105/q15/p1 Shortened forms evoke nearness, terror, and very little speech. — Al-Biqāʿī reads omitted yāʾ as two thirds of the Day's silence and omitted tāʾ as scant speech; al-Sāmarrāʾī invokes repeated demands for punishment in Hūd, earlier nations' swift downfall, and fewer 'come' derivatives here than in al-Anʿām and al-Aʿrāf. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 11:105/q15/p2 Indefinite singular forms magnify the states and portray each group as a unit. — Al-Qāshānī takes indefiniteness as exaltation; al-Ālūsī says singularity precedes successive plural entry into the two abodes. [2 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:105/q15/p3 The wretched appear first because the surrounding passage warns. — Al-Ālūsī states the warning explains this order. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:105/q15/p4 The division exemplifies warning and a gather, differentiate, divide pattern. — Al-Zarkashī names warning; al-Itqān and al-Ālūsī say generic soul gathers all, two adjectives separate, and the ensuing أما clauses subdivide, citing al-Sharīf al-Qayrawānī's poem. [4 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL]

## 11:105/q16 How does this verse connect to its surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: How the gathered assembly, postponed Day, intercession warning and ensuing descriptions fit together.
- 11:105/q16/p1 It explains the witnessed gathering of 11:103 and then introduces the two destinations detailed next. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the opening evokes gravity and the central division resumes after a postponement parenthesis; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes final reckoning from grave questioning; Kuran Yolu links outcomes to worldly faith and deeds. [9 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 11:105/q16/p2 It warns against reliance on intercession. — Kuran Yolu reports a context limited to idolaters after stories of idolatrous peoples, but prefers a general warning covering prophets, saints, angels and others; Ibn ʿĀshūr says idols have no right to intercede. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 11:105/q16/p3 The verse's general two groups govern the following أما clauses and exceptions. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects restricting the opening division to selected sinners; he says that leaves repeated subjects, durations and exceptions confusing. He reports an extraction account as a possible broad reading. [3 notes: TABATABAI]

## 11:105/q17 What does the preceding postponement wording contribute?  [يَوْمَ يَأْتِ · interpretation]
turns on: The referent, fixed term and person of the preceding verb.
- 11:105/q17/p1 The Day is postponed only until a fixed, unadvanceable term for a wise purpose. — Al-Ṭabrisī says duties benefit creatures until then and the counted limit signals nearness; the lām in لأجل marks purpose unlike إلى. Abū Ḥayyān says the limit was previously decreed. Al-Biqāʿī defines taʾkhīr, ajal and the lām. [4 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 11:105/q17/p2 The postponed referent is the Day, though recompense is also proposed. — Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Ḥawfī's recompense alternative; Ibn Jurayj takes it as the appointed Day. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جريج)]
- 11:105/q17/p3 The preceding verb is read with nūn or yāʾ, with the same sense. — Yaʿqūb and al-Aʿmash have yāʾ; al-Ṭabrisī says it relates to 'your Lord's seizing,' while nūn begins a new clause. Al-Zamakhsharī uses it for the divine subject of يأتي. [4 notes: TABRISI(يعقوب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأعمش); KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 11:105/q18 How is the paired clause فَمِنْهُمْ شَقِىٌّ وَسَعِيدٌ parsed?  [فَمِنْهُمْ شَقِىٌّ وَسَعِيدٌ · grammar]
- 11:105/q18/p1 The second 'among them' is omitted before سعيد. — Al-Samīn compares 11:100; al-Ālūsī says the first prepositional phrase is fronted and شقي is its subject, with the same construction supplied for سعيد. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 11:105/q19 What do cited examples establish about clitic object order?  [* · grammar]
- 11:105/q19/p1 Person hierarchy and the verbs' construction govern whether object clitics join or separate. — Sībawayh orders speaker, addressee, then absent person; he rejects reversed analogies, cites 11:28 for an absent clitic after an addressee, notes poetic support for two absent objects, and says copula-like verbs usually use separate إياه. [5 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 11:105/q20 What is a fāṣila and does it have to be a verse ending?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether technical fāṣila is a verse ending or any separated sentence ending, and how that boundary is established.
- 11:105/q20/p1 It is the final word of a verse, analogous to rhyme. — Al-Zarkashī and al-Suyūṭī report the standard technical definition; al-Jaʿbarī rejects treating Sībawayh's يوم يأت as a technical counterexample, since it is not a verse ending. [4 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN; BURHAN(الجعبرى)+; ITQAN(الجعبري)+]
- 11:105/q20/p2 It is the end of a separated stretch of discourse, whether or not a verse ends there. — Al-Dānī cites يوم يأت and ما كنا نبغ as non-verse endings and distinguishes all verse heads as fawāṣil. Al-Qāḍī Abū Bakr defines similarly patterned terminal sounds carrying meaning. [4 notes: BURHAN(أبو عمرو الدانى,القاضى أبو بكر); ITQAN(الداني,القاضي أبو بكر); BURHAN(الجعبرى)-; ITQAN(الجعبري)-]
- 11:105/q20/p3 Transmission or analogy can establish verse divisions. — Al-Jaʿbarī uses prophetic stopping or connection, then analogy to clear endings; poetic-rhyme defects do not invalidate Quranic fāṣila, whose endings can shift forms. [4 notes: ITQAN(الجعبري)]
- 11:105/q20/p4 Fawāṣil beautify and separate Quranic discourse and are distinguished from sajʿ. — Al-Zarkashī avoids unauthorized or lowly associations with a bird's sound; al-Rummānī says sajʿ bends sense toward sound while fawāṣil follow meaning, a distinction al-Bāqillānī and Ashʿarites adopt. [4 notes: BURHAN(الرمانى,القاضى أبو بكر الباقلانى)]

- 11:105/q24/p1 It can mark the unseen or afterlife. — Al-Zarkashī groups omitted final yāʾs with unseen matters; he includes the ships of 42:33 as signs whose meaning reaches the unseen. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 11:105/q25 What do the cited vocative forms show about lost yāʾ and alif?  [يَأْتِ · grammar]
- 11:105/q25/p1 Alif replacing possessive yāʾ is a regular vocative form, not a lament form. — Abū ʿUthmān rejects the lament analysis: without lamenting alif it would be ordinary vocative; this supports Ḥafṣ's singular يا بني reading throughout the Qurʾān, including 19:42 and 11:29. [2 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(حفص عن عاصم); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عثمان)+]

## 11:105/q26 What does an unspoken locative supply in the speech clause?  [لَا تَكَلَّمُ نَفْسٌ · grammar]
- 11:105/q26/p1 It means no soul speaks in that Day except by God's permission. — Al-Rāzī says the wording omits 'on that day'. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 11:105/q27 Why are sinners not asked to discover their sins?  [لَا تَكَلَّمُ نَفْسٌ · interpretation]
- 11:105/q27/p1 God already knows their deeds; questioning serves rebuke and proof, not discovery. — Al-Zajjāj cites 55:39; al-Ṭabrisī adduces 37:24 for reproach and proof. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ; TABRISI]
