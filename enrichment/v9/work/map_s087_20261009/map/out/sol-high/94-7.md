# 94:7: 27 questions

## 94:7/q01 What does faraghta mean?  [فَرَغْتَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the release concerns completion of an occupied task or solitude.
- 94:7/q01/p1 To become free by completing a task; literally, to become empty after fullness. — Ibn ʿĀshūr begins with an emptied vessel, then applies it metaphorically to someone formerly occupied. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ includes both a vessel and the mind and cites physical and figurative Qurʾānic uses (28:10, 7:126, 2:250). [5 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI]
- 94:7/q01/p2 To be alone or free from the presence of others. — Abū Muslim explicitly glosses farāgh as solitude in his interpretation of worship when alone. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم)]

## 94:7/q02 How is faraghta read?  [فَرَغْتَ · readings]
turns on: Whether Abū al-Sammāl's dialectal vowel is accepted as eloquent; the majority has fatḥa.
- 94:7/q02/p1 With kasra on the rāʾ, attributed to Abū al-Sammāl. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and others call it dialectal; al-Zamakhsharī calls it inelegant. The QURTUBI-FULL note instead says kasra on the ghayn, a source-level variation. [12 notes: KASHSHAF(أبو السمال); KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو السمال); IBNATIYYA(أبو السمال); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو السمال); QURTUBI(أبو السمال); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو السمال); ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور وأبو السمال,الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور وأبو السمال); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(أبو السمال); ALUSI-FULL(أبو السمال)]
- 94:7/q02/p2 With fatḥa on the rāʾ, the common or majority reading. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn identify this as the majority form. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور وأبو السمال); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور وأبو السمال); SAMIN-DURR]

## 94:7/q03 What does the omitted object of 'when you finish' cover?  [فَرَغْتَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether omission gives broad scope or a specific contextual completion.
- 94:7/q03/p1 It is deliberately omitted to cover whatever important duties the context supplies. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Prophet's known demanding mission supplies the contextual category, with multiple tasks ordered by priority; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ calls omission a Qurʾānic way to keep an already understood activity open. [7 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; IBNASHUR(السلف); IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI]
- 94:7/q03/p2 It is a particular finished activity: prayer, worldly work, campaign, mission, or relief. — Reported specifications supply particular tasks; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ lists prayer, jihad, and worldly work, while herself preferring release from distress in 94:1–4. [2 notes: BINTSHATI(مفسرون); BINTSHATI+]

## 94:7/q04 What does fanṣab mean as a word?  [فَٱنصَبْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the root points here to wearisome effort, upright setting, or allotment.
- 94:7/q04/p1 Exert yourself, toil, or tire yourself in sustained effort. — Al-Farrāʾ derives it from exertion; al-Wāḥidī gives naṣiba yanṣabu. Al-Ṭabrisī cites fatigue and poetry by an anonymous poet and al-Nābigha; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites Qurʾānic uses of fatigue in travel and labor (18:62, 9:120, 35:35, 15:48), chooses this sense, and names al-Nīshābūrī, ʿAbduh and al-Zamakhsharī as agreeing. [19 notes: FARRA; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; TABRISI(الشاعر,النابغة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM; BINTSHATI+]
- 94:7/q04/p2 Stand upright or set upright. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ recognizes this as an Arabic sense and cites the image of set-up mountains in 88:19, while choosing exertion in this verse. [2 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 94:7/q04/p3 Assign a fixed share or portion. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports that al-Rāghib seems to derive this from the notion of an allotted portion and points to 4:53 and 4:44. [1 notes: BINTSHATI(الراغب)]

## 94:7/q05 How is fanṣab read, and what changes with the variants?  [فَٱنصَبْ · readings]
turns on: Whether altered consonant vocalization or doubling is a transmitted reading, and whether an omitted object supports a succession claim.
- 94:7/q05/p1 A doubled consonant reading gives the sense of pouring oneself wholly into another act, or, in one account, going home. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī connect the doubled form to inṣibāb; ALUSI-FULL describes a doubled open yāʾ, while others specify bāʾ. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī also report a doubled-bāʾ reading construed as heading to Medina or returning home after jihad, which they reject as a reading. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA(قوم); ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور وقوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(قوم); ALUSI-FULL(قوم); IBNATIYYA(النقاش)-; IBNATIYYA-FULL(النقاش)-; QURTUBI-; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)-]
- 94:7/q05/p2 Fanṣib with kasra on ṣād is taken by some Imāmīs as 'appoint a successor,' especially ʿAlī. — Al-Zamakhsharī and followers call the reading fabricated or anomalous and say hostile readers could force the root into hating ʿAlī. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says no recognized scholar attests it. Ibn al-ʿArabī denies the Prophet appointed a successor. Al-Ālūsī says no object is named: a Sunni could instead supply Abū Bakr, citing his leadership of prayer in the final illness; he questions the Ghadīr Khumm claim and objects to a pilgrimage setting because the sūra is Meccan, but rejects al-Zamakhsharī's hate-ʿAlī analogy as flawed. Some supply 'caliph' as omitted object. [12 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض الإمامية); ABUHAYYAN(آخرون من الإمامية,ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الإمامية); ALUSI(ابن عطية,الزمخشري,السني,بعض الإمامية,بعض الناس); ALUSI-FULL(الكشاف,بعض الإمامية); KASHSHAF(بعض الرافضة)-; KASHSHAF-FULL-; IBNATIYYA(آخرون من الإمامية)-; QURTUBI(ابن العربي)-; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)-; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية,الزمخشري)-; BURSEVI-; ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية)-]
- 94:7/q05/p3 The common imperative fanṣab has an ungeminated, still bāʾ and commands exertion. — Abū Ḥayyān calls this the majority form; al-Samīn derives it from naṣaba. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور وقوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR]

## 94:7/q06 What does placing the completion clause first do?  [فَإِذَا فَرَغْتَ فَٱنصَبْ · rhetoric]
- 94:7/q06/p1 It prepares the main command and emphasizes doing a new task in sequence after each completed one. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says fanṣab is the main command and the initial time clause sets its sequence; he calls the expression concise yet rich. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the future marker and paired fa particles also signal sequence and connection to what precedes. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI]

## 94:7/q07 What is the force of the fa connecting the commands?  [فَإِذَا فَرَغْتَ فَٱنصَبْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the fronted elements supply a conditional sense explaining fa.
- 94:7/q07/p1 The fa links actions because the fronted complements imply restriction and thus a conditional sense. — Ibn ʿĀshūr adduces comparable Qurʾānic clauses (39:66, 74:3–5, 83:26, 10:58) and says this resolves al-Ṭībī's and Ibn al-Shajarī's puzzlement over fa with wāw. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL(الطيبي)]
- 94:7/q07/p2 The pairing of fa and wāw is syntactically astonishing. — Al-Ṭībī, citing Ibn al-Shajarī, finds it striking because fa usually coordinates, answers a condition, or accompanies a relative clause; Ibn ʿĀshūr disputes the difficulty. [2 notes: IBNASHUR(الطيبي); IBNASHUR-FULL(الطيبي); IBNASHUR-]

## 94:7/q09 How does this command relate to the preceding favors and promise of ease?  [* · links]
- 94:7/q09/p1 God's past favors and promised future relief call for grateful, persistent worship and renewed work. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, and others see the injunction as gratitude. Al-Biqāʿī adds gratitude brings increase; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 14:7 and reads 94:5–6 as encouraging endurance of the mission. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ and Corpus Coranicum trace a contrast between finishing and exerting after hardship and ease; Elmalılı says each ease should spur further progress. ## 94:7/q10 What does the command teach about idle time and recreation?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether the injunction to avoid idle time excludes permitted leisure and festival recreation.
- 94:7/q10/p1 Free time should be used for worship or useful worldly and religious work; pointless idleness is censured. — Shurayḥ rebukes wrestlers in many accounts; al-Shaʿbī censures a man moving stones. ʿUmar and Ibn Masʿūd dislike a person with neither worldly nor afterlife work. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Ālūsī call useless idleness foolish or heedless. Al-Nuḥḥās extends the point to any completed work. [20 notes: FARRA(شريح); NAHHAS(عبد الله بن مسعود); THALABI(الفراء,شريح); WAHIDI-BASIT(علي بن أبي طلحة); KASHSHAF(الشعبي,عمر); KASHSHAF-FULL(الشعبي); IBNATIYYA(شريح); IBNATIYYA-FULL(شريح); RAZI(شريح); RAZI-FULL(شريح); ALUSI(شريك,عمر); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 94:7/q10/p2 Ordinary people need not work without pause; festival play and song are permitted. — Ibn al-ʿArabī challenges Shurayḥ's objection to festival play: Abyssinians performed with shields and spears before the Prophet in the mosque, and the Prophet told Abū Bakr to let two girls sing on Eid. Al-Qurṭubī says uninterrupted application is disliked for ordinary people. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,شريح); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)+]

## 94:7/q11 What did Shurayḥ and others say on seeing idle people?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the reported incident concerns wrestling, pointless carrying, general idleness, or festival play.
- 94:7/q11/p1 Shurayḥ rebuked two men wrestling and invoked this verse. — The versions differ only in whether he says the command calls for worship, effort, or turning to God; one later report attributes a similar rebuke to Sharīk. [9 notes: FARRA(شريح); THALABI(الفراء,شريح); IBNATIYYA(شريح); IBNATIYYA-FULL(شريح); RAZI(شريح); RAZI-FULL(شريح); ALUSI(شريك); ALUSI-FULL]
- 94:7/q11/p2 ʿUmar or Ibn Masʿūd disliked seeing anyone idle in both worldly and afterlife work. — The attribution varies between sources: al-Naḥḥās gives Ibn Masʿūd; al-Kashshāf and al-Ālūsī give ʿUmar. [5 notes: NAHHAS(عبد الله بن مسعود); KASHSHAF(عمر); KASHSHAF-FULL(الشعبي); ALUSI(عمر); ALUSI-FULL]
- 94:7/q11/p3 Al-Shaʿbī rebuked a man carrying or moving stones as a misuse of free time. — The Kashshāf versions link the anecdote to the view that pointless activity reflects poor judgment. [2 notes: KASHSHAF(الشعبي); KASHSHAF-FULL(الشعبي)]
- 94:7/q11/p4 A report has Shurayḥ objecting to festival play, but Ibn al-ʿArabī challenges the objection. — Ibn al-ʿArabī cites the Prophet watching Abyssinian spear and shield play and permitting two girls to sing on Eid. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,شريح)]

## 94:7/q12 When may the prayer's concluding supplication be made?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether 'finishing prayer' includes the final sitting before salām or follows the salām.
- 94:7/q12/p1 Before the salām, after the bowing, prostration, or tashahhud, while still seated. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Ibn Masʿūd, al-Shaʿbī, and al-Zuhrī are cited. Ibn Masʿūd holds that bowing and prostrations complete prayer even if ritual impurity then occurs, leaving room to ask God while seated. Al-Aṣamm also directs supplication after bowing, prostration, and recitation. [21 notes: TAB(الضحاك); TAB-FULL(الضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(ابن مسعود); THALABI(الضحاك); BAGHAWI(الشعبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الشعبي); TABRISI(الزهري,الصادق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الشعبي والزهري); RAZI(الشعبي); RAZI-FULL(الشعبي); DURR(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود,مجاهد); JISHUMI(الأصم)]
- 94:7/q12/p2 After finishing the prayer and salām. — Al-Nasafī records that some authorities place the supplication after salutation; many reports say simply 'after prayer' without deciding the exact moment. [2 notes: NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 94:7/q13 What inward meaning is drawn from the command?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether free time denotes outward completion, inward emptying, or the end of a spiritual journey.
- 94:7/q13/p1 After the outward obligatory prayer, return beyond the natural self to a solitary secret station with God. — Al-Tustarī relates this to the Prophet's reported intimate time with God that admits no other. [2 notes: TUSTARI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 94:7/q13/p2 When free of everything besides God, remember Him. — Jaʿfar's reported saying makes inward freedom the condition for remembrance. [1 notes: SULAMI(جعفر)]
- 94:7/q13/p3 After worship of the self, devote the heart inwardly. — Al-Qushayrī reports this as another way to understand the move from one work to the next. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 94:7/q13/p4 After the inward journey through, with, and from God, turn outward in steadfast invitation. — Al-Qāshānī sees an inward journey preceding renewed work among people. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 94:7/q14 What reports illustrate moving from one demand to another?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which reported circumstance—campaign, prayer distractions, or travel—illustrates completion and renewed action.
- 94:7/q14/p1 The Prophet is reported to call return from a campaign a move from lesser jihad to greater jihad. — Ibn ʿĀshūr uses the saying as an example of following one task with another. Al-Ālūsī notes its use for struggle against the self, while raising the Meccan chronology issue. [4 notes: ALUSI(النبي); IBNASHUR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL(محمد)]
- 94:7/q14/p2 Reports about food and bodily need counsel clearing distractions before prayer. — Ibn Kathīr cites a report from ʿĀʾisha that prayer should not be begun with food present or while resisting bodily needs; Muslim transmits it and the fuller source calls it sound. Another report says to begin with dinner when it is served at the prayer call, transmitted by al-Bukhārī. [6 notes: IBNKATHIR(عائشة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 94:7/q14/p3 Travel is a piece of torment, so after one's need is done one should hurry back to family. — Al-Qurṭubī and Ibn al-ʿArabī cite this Prophetic report to support the meaning of returning home, while rejecting a fabricated doubled-bāʾ reading used to claim it. [2 notes: QURTUBI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 94:7/q15 What does the Bedouin's word lesson distinguish?  [* · reports]
- 94:7/q15/p1 An opening in a wall or stick has ḍamma, while an opening from distress has fatḥa. — Abū ʿAmr ibn al-ʿAlāʾ says he heard this after fleeing al-Ḥajjāj and listening to verses that patience lifts sorrow; a messenger then announced al-Ḥajjāj's death. The distinction pleased him. [2 notes: TUSTARI(أبو عمرو بن العلاء)]

## 94:7/q16 How should someone respond after completing the Qurʾān?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether the notes concern a prescribed practice at completion or reported benefits of completion.
- 94:7/q16/p1 Supplicate at the completion, often with raised hands in tarāwīḥ before bowing. — Aḥmad recommends completing in tarāwīḥ and praying before the bow with raised hands, including for those present; al-Faḍl reports practicing this with Aḥmad behind him. Makkans, Basrans, Medinans, and reports from ʿUthmān attest similar practice. Al-Nawawī strongly urges comprehensive prayers for the afterlife, Muslims, and leaders. [5 notes: NASHR(الفضل بن زياد,عباس بن عبد العظيم); NASHR(أحمد بن حنبل,النووي)+]
- 94:7/q16/p2 Gather family and others at a completion. — Anas gathered family and neighbors; Qatāda reports Ibn ʿAbbās arranging watchers so he could witness a man's completion. [2 notes: NASHR(أنس بن مالك,قتادة)]
- 94:7/q16/p3 The completion brings mercy, an answered supplication, or an angel's kiss. — Ḥabīb ibn Abī ʿAmra relates the angel's kiss, approved by Aḥmad; Ibn Masʿūd and Mujāhid report answered supplication, and Mujāhid also reports descending mercy. Al-Bukhārī reportedly completed frequently in Ramadan and spoke of answered supplication. [5 notes: NASHR(ابن مسعود,حبيب بن أبي عمرة,مجاهد,محمد بن إسماعيل البخاري)]

## 94:7/q17 Who should lead supplication at a Qurʾān completion?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether the completing reader or a respected leader voices the prayer.
- 94:7/q17/p1 The reader who completed the Qurʾān should supplicate. — Some teachers of Ibn al-Jazarī prefer this arrangement. [1 notes: NASHR(بعض شيوخنا)]
- 94:7/q17/p2 A shaykh or someone sought for blessing should supplicate. — Ibn al-Jazarī says most scholars he knew followed this practice; he accepts either because the one supplicating and the one saying amen share in the prayer, illustrated by Moses and Aaron in 10:89. [2 notes: NASHR(سائر من أدركناهم)]

## 94:7/q18 Does adding a letter necessarily make a recitation preferable?  [* · readings]
- 94:7/q18/p1 No; Aḥmad would not choose a form merely for an extra letter. — Ibn al-Jazarī rejects Ibn Mufliḥ's distinction between letter names and sounds as his own elaboration rather than Aḥmad's reason. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 94:7/q19 How does arrangement of meaning govern spoken wording?  [* · rhetoric]
- 94:7/q19/p1 Meanings are arranged in the mind, and words take their places as their expressions. — ʿAbd al-Qāhir al-Jurjānī compares composition to building, linking, weaving, embroidery, engraving, and craft; ordinary speech may not say meanings are composed, yet these analogies concern meaning-relations, not words alone. A word's location depends on its meaning. [4 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 94:7/q20 How does this command lead into the following call to desire God?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the next clause directs the worshipper's own desire or, in a variant, urges others.
- 94:7/q20/p1 Work should be directed to God alone, with sincere desire and requests for religious and worldly needs. — Ibn Kathīr stresses an undistracted mind and intention, al-Jishumī says to turn to God in every circumstance, and Elmalılı relates labor for God's sake to reaching Him and to deeds being judged by intentions. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the conjunction completes the movement from distress through ease to exclusive longing for God. [9 notes: TAB(قتادة,مجاهد); JISHUMI; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI]
- 94:7/q20/p2 A reported variant of the next imperative, faraghib, means urge others toward what God has. — Al-Ālūsī attributes it to Zayd ibn ʿAlī and Ibn Abī ʿAbla; it is from the doubled verb raghiba. [1 notes: ALUSI(زيد بن علي وابن أبي عبلة)]

## 94:7/q21 How is 'desire' construed in the following verse?  [* · grammar]
- 94:7/q21/p1 Raghiba normally takes the desired object with fī, the one petitioned directly, and an for turning away. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites ʿĀʾisha's reading of an omitted preposition as an in 4:127. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 94:7/q22 Are al-Ḍuḥā and al-Sharḥ one sūra or two?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether prayer recitation without a second basmala outweighs the transmitted separation by basmala.
- 94:7/q22/p1 They form one sūra, or were recited together without a second basmala. — Asad reports Ṭāwūs and ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz treating them as one in prayer, citing al-Rāzī; Study Quran reports a similar explanation involving Companions. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Ta'us ibn Kaysan and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz); STUDYQURAN; JISHUMI-]
- 94:7/q22/p2 They are two sūras. — Al-Jishumī rejects unity because the basmala separates them and transmitted agreement establishes two. [1 notes: JISHUMI+]

## 94:7/q23 How do al-Ḍuḥā and al-Sharḥ relate in sequence and theme?  [* · links]
- 94:7/q23/p1 Al-Sharḥ follows and continues al-Ḍuḥā's reassurance, moving from blessings and hardship to renewed striving. — Asad says the revelation followed almost immediately. Islāḥī says al-Ḍuḥā covers shelter and early favors whereas al-Sharḥ addresses blessings after Islam spread, with promised success beginning to materialize. Study Quran distinguishes external needs in 93 from spiritual relief in 94. The address reaches the Prophet and, through him, believers. [7 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]

## 94:7/q24 Where and when was the sūra revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the commonly accepted Makkan setting permits a literal post-jihad command, or later dating or delayed application is possible.
- 94:7/q24/p1 It is Makkan, commonly placed just after Sūra 93. — Study Quran reports the majority view. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Jishumī use the Makkan date to object to interpretations based on later jihad; al-Ālūsī notes the same issue. [8 notes: STUDYQURAN(most commentators); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن بن أبي الحسن); ABUHAYYAN(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI]
- 94:7/q24/p2 The whole sūra is Madinan, or only verses 5–6 are Madinan. — Study Quran reports Ibn ʿAbbās for the whole sūra and an unnamed view for verses 5–6; al-Ālūsī mentions Medinan dating or a delayed ruling as possible answers to the jihad chronology objection. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN(most commentators); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن، زيد بن أسلم)]

## 94:7/q25 What merit is reported for reciting al-Sharḥ?  [* · reports]
- 94:7/q25/p1 Reciting it is likened to visiting the distressed Prophet and relieving him. — The report appears in al-Kashshāf and al-Bayḍāwī without a grade in these notes. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 94:7/q26 What warning do the sources give about invented readings or reports?  [* · law]
- 94:7/q26/p1 A sound meaning must not be attached to a self-invented reading or hadith as if it were revelation. — Al-Qurṭubī and Ibn al-ʿArabī say this lies about God and the Messenger; the fuller note invokes 2:281. The doubled reading about returning from travel illustrates the distinction between a supported meaning and an invalid reading. [3 notes: QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)]

## 94:7/q27 What broad maxims does the sūra's ending teach?  [* · interpretation]
- 94:7/q27/p1 Hardship yields ease; effort and reward correspond; intention gives work its direction. — Elmalılı articulates these maxims and says labor for God's sake leads one to God. Ibn ʿĀshūr describes the assurance of accompanying ease as what keeps people from perishing under hardship. [4 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
