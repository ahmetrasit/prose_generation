# 30:39: 33 questions

## 30:39/q01 How is the first آتيتم read and understood?  [ءَاتَيْتُم · readings]
turns on: Whether the first verb is form IV giving or form I coming/doing.
- 30:39/q01/p1 Long ātiytum: you gave. — The majority read the long form; al-Fārisī compares God's giving at 3:148. Biqāʿī says it specifies donors; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes two hamzas. [15 notes: IBNMUJAHID(الباقون); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير); JISHUMI(ابن كثير); BAGHAWI-FULL(الآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); TABRISI(أبو علي); QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); NASHR(ابن كثير); BIQAI-FULL(ابن كثير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); QIRAAT-ER(Vr)]
- 30:39/q01/p2 Short ataytum: you came, did, brought, or intended. — Ibn Kathīr reads it; some add Mujāhid and Ḥumayd or Meccan readers. Al-Fārisī and al-Ṭabrisī cite 'ataytu alladhī yaʾtī al-safīh' for doing; al-Baghawī says both convey giving. Biqāʿī sees giver, receiver and intermediary. [21 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير,الشاعر); JISHUMI(ابن كثير); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن كثير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير); TABRISI(أبو علي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن كثير ومجاهد وحميد); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن كثير); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; NASHR(ابن كثير); BIQAI-FULL(ابن كثير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن كثير); QIRAAT-ER(Vr)]

## 30:39/q02 What does ribā mean in Arabic and law?  [رِّبًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verse uses the ordinary or legal sense, or applies it figuratively to a hoped-for gift return.
- 30:39/q02/p1 Its Arabic sense is increase; legal usage names a prohibited excess or transaction. — Yaḥyā, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurṭubī, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Asad give the lexical sense. Jishumī notes a deferred gold exchange can be legally ribā without added amount; Bursawī distinguishes excess and delay; Asad describes additions on loans. [10 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(most of the early Muslim jurists); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 30:39/q02/p2 The gift is called ribā figuratively for the increase sought. — Al-Fārisī and al-Ṭabrisī distinguish this from forbidden excess above principal; Ālūsī says the gift causes increase or seeks an unowed excess. Islahi rejects the gift gloss, arguing that interest and loan are named by consequence, comparing 12:36. [4 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; TABRISI(أبو علي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وسعيد بن جبير والضحاك ومحمد بن كعب القرظي وطاوس,النخعي وابن عباس); ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]

## 30:39/q03 How is ribā spelled and inflected?  [رِّبًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the spelling is explained by the root or by the verse's scope.
- 30:39/q03/p1 Its spelling and dual reflect a wāw root. — Sībawayh gives the dual ribawān and his companions allow no alternative; Naḥḥās sides with Abū Isḥāq against the Kufian form because yarbū reveals wāw. Qurṭubī cites the verse's wāw spelling; Bursawī offers emphatic spelling or the root rabā-yarbū. [4 notes: NAHHAS(سيبويه); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 30:39/q03/p2 The written alif here distinguishes this instance from universal prohibited ribā. — Burhān says the core convention writes alif as wāw in four words and derivatives, with emphatic pronunciation; here alif marks a nonuniversal category, unlike universal zakat written with wāw. [2 notes: BURHAN]

## 30:39/q05 What occasion is reported for the first clause?  [رِّبًۭا · reports]
turns on: The competing reports identify gift giving or named usurious dealings.
- 30:39/q05/p1 It concerned people among the Companions involved in gambling. — Muqātil associates the passage with their gifts hoping to obtain more. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 30:39/q05/p2 It concerned the interest dealings of Thaqīf and Quraysh. — Al-Suddī reports Thaqīf's usury, with Quraysh participating; Jishumī has Thaqīf alone. Ibn ʿĀshūr places it in a wider pre-Islamic and early Islamic practice. [7 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي); ALUSI-FULL(السدي); ELMALILI(Süddî); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 30:39/q05/p3 It was revealed about gifts expecting compensation. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Jubayr, Ṭāwūs and Mujāhid are cited by Qurṭubī. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن جبير وطاوس ومجاهد)]

## 30:39/q06 What is the status of a gift given for a larger return?  [رِّبًۭا · law]
turns on: Whether anticipated human compensation cancels only divine reward or also makes the gift impermissible; one report allows reward.
- 30:39/q06/p1 It is lawful for ordinary people but earns neither reward nor sin. — Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Ṭāwūs and others distinguish lawful gift increase from unlawful usury; Ibn ʿAṭiyya extends the rule to greeting for repayment. Al-Ṭabarī distinguishes the Prophet's special restriction. - 30:39/q06/p2 The Prophet was specifically forbidden to give expecting more. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and others invoke the command at 74:6, though Durr's note cites 73:6. Ordinary gift exchange continues; Naḥḥās and al-Wāḥidī make the restriction specific. [13 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الضحاك بن مزاحم); TAB-FULL(آخرون,الضحاك); FARISI-HUJJA; NAHHAS(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير ومجاهد وطاوس وقتادة والضحاك وأكثر المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(الضحاك); BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL]
- 30:39/q06/p3 A gift anticipating more can have some reward. — Some Successors say the generous gift may be rewarded while still requiring a larger return from a stranger. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض التابعين)]
- 30:39/q06/p4 Biqāʿī calls the exchange prohibited or at least disliked. — He says it is forbidden for the Prophet and disliked for others. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 30:39/q07 May the giver demand or revoke a gift given for recompense?  [رِّبًۭا · law]
turns on: Whether a gift implies a price, and whether return, value or continued possession sets the right to revoke.
- 30:39/q07/p1 Recompense can be claimed when the giver customarily expects it. — Mālik allows it for a poor giver to a rich recipient; one Shāfiʿī position concurs. ʿUmar says it remains the giver's until satisfactory return; ʿAlī distinguishes gifts for God, people and recompense, reclaimable without payment. Qurṭubī endorses ʿAlī. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(علي,عمر بن الخطاب,مالك)]
- 30:39/q07/p2 There is no recompense without an explicit condition. — Abū Ḥanīfa and another Shāfiʿī position call the return-seeking gift invalid as a sale for an unknown price; the Kufan argument says compulsory return destroys gratuity. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة والشافعي,الكوفي)]
- 30:39/q07/p3 The gift can be revoked until its value is repaid. — Ibn al-Qāsim sets the threshold at equal value; Ibn al-ʿArabī says value alone is due by agreement if the object no longer exists; another opinion analogizes an unstipulated dowry. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,ابن القاسم,قيل)]
- 30:39/q07/p4 The gift can be revoked until a larger or satisfying return arrives. — Qurṭubī takes this as the apparent import of ʿUmar and ʿAlī. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عمر وعلي)]
- 30:39/q07/p5 The gift can be revoked while the original object remains. — Muṭarrif allows it despite change in value or repayment above value; another view lets the giver demand any return while the object remains unchanged. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل,مطرف)]

## 30:39/q08 What do reports say about accepting and returning gifts?  [رِّبًۭا · reports]
- 30:39/q08/p1 The Prophet urged accepting gifts and reciprocating. — Yaḥyā transmits that a gift is God's provision and should be answered with something better, and that no Muslim's gift be refused. ʿĀʾisha says the Prophet accepted and repaid gifts without censuring expected return. A Thaqīf delegation report distinguishes gift from charity by intention. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(عائشة,عبد الرحمن بن علقمة)]

## 30:39/q09 How is ليربوا read, and who increases?  [لِّيَرْبُوَا۟ · readings]

## 30:39/q10 What is meant by increasing in people's wealth?  [لِّيَرْبُوَا۟ فِىٓ أَمْوَٰلِ ٱلنَّاسِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the sought increase belongs to a gift's donor, its recipient, or an interest lender.
- 30:39/q10/p1 The giver seeks to draw more of other people's wealth through a gift. — Al-Fārisī and al-Ṭabrisī supply attraction of wealth; al-Baghawī says the gift seizes property. Ibn al-Shaykh says the gift attracts a return; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the gift interpretation restates the aim. [8 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(أبو علي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن الشيخ); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 30:39/q10/p2 The recipient's wealth grows from the donor's gift. — Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Nakhaʿī emphasize the recipient's benefit; Ālūsī says on the ordinary reading the ribā or gift grows in that wealth; Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts it with seeking God's face. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي,ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(النخعي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس والنخعي); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 30:39/q10/p3 Interest takes apparent gain from other people's property. — Biqāʿī says the wording hints the usurer does not truly own what he takes. Ibn ʿĀshūr reads fī figuratively as 'from,' citing Ṣabr al-Faqʿasī's gambling verse and 4:5; Islahi stresses lender profit without risk. [7 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 30:39/q11 How does the first mā construction work?  [وَمَآ مِّن رِّبًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Conditional object and jussive answer versus relative subject and predicate.
- 30:39/q11/p1 Mā may be conditional; min explains its unspecified content. — Abū ʿAlī makes mā the object of ātaytum and falā yarbū its jussive answer; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it general conditional, min explanatory in both clauses. Ālūsī says min is explanatory on either ribā reading. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 30:39/q11/p2 Mā may be relative, with falā yarbū as its predicate. — Abū ʿAlī gives a nominative relative with omitted resumptive pronoun and predicate introduced by fāʾ, comparing 16:53. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي)]

## 30:39/q12 Why does the first giving not grow with God?  [فَلَا يَرْبُوا۟ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the loss reflects an unrewarded but lawful motive or a condemned transaction.
- 30:39/q12/p1 It earns no divine reward because its intent is human recompense. — Ṭabarī, al-Fārisī, al-Wāḥidī and others tie the absence of growth to failing to seek God's face. Qurṭubī invokes 'deeds by intentions,' 2:264 on display, and kin support that may instead fulfill kinship rights. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts devotional and nondevotional intent. [27 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الضحاك بن مزاحم); ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن طاوس,ابن عباس,قتادة); FARISI-HUJJA; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية,النبي); BAYDAWI-FULL; DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير,محمد بن كعب القرظي); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 30:39/q12/p2 Usurious gain is invalid, unblessed and deficient in God's judgment. — Naḥḥās and Qurṭubī say the excess belongs to the person taken from; Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds sin and lost blessing. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers this, citing 2:276 and 3:130; Bursawī invokes God's erasure of usury. [14 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(السدي والحسن); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 30:39/q13 How is the second آتيتم read?  [ءَاتَيْتُم زَكَوٰةٍۢ · readings]
- 30:39/q13/p1 All readers lengthen it: 'you gave' zakat. — Ibn Mujāhid, al-Fārisī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and others report agreement. ## 30:39/q14 What does zakāt denote here?  [زَكَوٰةٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether zakat is technical obligatory alms or the broader category of charity.
- 30:39/q14/p1 Charitable giving generally, including voluntary alms. — Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Suddī and others gloss it charity; Qurṭubī uses a ring gift to show voluntary alms called zakat and includes obligatory too. Biqāʿī derives its name from purification and increase of wealth, body and character; Islahi says it is nontechnical. [17 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); ZAJJAJ; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 30:39/q14/p2 The obligatory zakat. — Māwardī calls it the apparent sense; Ṭabāṭabāʾī links this with a Medinan dating. Bursawī and Qurṭubī include obligatory giving within a broader usage. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 30:39/q15 What is the zakāt of wealth and of the person?  [زَكَوٰةٍۢ · inward]
- 30:39/q15/p1 Bodily and inward zakat cleanse a person; financial zakat cleanses wealth. — Sahl calls bodily zakat purification from sins and monetary zakat removal of doubtful things; Qushayrī adds heart and secret and stresses the prescribed categories and characteristics of wealth. Taʾwīlāt Najmiyya reads spending as purging love of this world, citing Abū Bakr and 92:17–20. [6 notes: SULAMI; QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,سهل)]

## 30:39/q16 What does seeking God's face mean, and why mention it?  [تُرِيدُونَ وَجْهَ ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the question asks the devotional intention or the imagery of 'face'.
- 30:39/q16/p1 It is sincere pursuit of God's approval and reward. — Al-Suddī says pursuit of God; Māwardī, Bursawī and Bayḍāwī stress reward or pleasure rather than repayment or reputation. Sahl says this intention, not mere zakat payment, brings increase. Qushayrī recommends giving even to a hostile relative to avoid self-interest; Bursawī recalls Abū Bakr in 92:17–20. [16 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); SULAMI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(سهل); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 30:39/q16/p2 Face denotes God's essence and evokes reverence. — Biqāʿī explains the image by the face's nobility and the reverence and modesty it arouses. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 30:39/q17 How does the second mā construction work?  [وَمَآ زَكَوٰةٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Relative-clause ellipsis versus two parallel conditional clauses.
- 30:39/q17/p1 It may be a nominative relative with an omitted object and resumptive. — Abū ʿAlī supplies ātaytumūhu and a predicate with fāʾ and a deleted pronoun, comparing the idiom 'al-samn manwān bi-dirham.' Bayḍāwī supplies al-muḍʿifūna bihi or fa-muʾtūhu; Ālūsī discusses whether the referent is donors or those who gave. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]
- 30:39/q17/p2 It may be a conditional parallel to the first mā. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says min explains both general mā expressions and the final clause answers the alms condition. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 30:39/q18 Why does the verse shift from address to 'those'?  [فَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the shift chiefly broadens the addressees or heightens their praise.
- 30:39/q18/p1 The shift generalizes the promise to all such givers. — Ibn Qutayba notes Arabic and Qurʾanic second-to-third-person transition. Nasafī and Bursawī say it extends beyond the addressees through the Resurrection; Bayḍāwī offers this alongside praise. Qurṭubī and Abū ʿAlī compare 10:22; Itqān compares 49:7 and 43:70–72. [9 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN; BURSEVI]
- 30:39/q18/p2 It honors and publicly praises the distinguished givers. — Bayḍāwī and al-Zamakhsharī picture praise before angels and elect; Ālūsī invokes a poetic line and says the demonstrative honors them. Biqāʿī says the distant demonstrative marks their rarity; Ibn ʿĀshūr also sees honor. [8 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الكشاف); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 30:39/q19 What is the force of the nominal final clause?  [فَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلْمُضْعِفُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the wording is read for emphasis or resolved by supplying an omitted complement.
- 30:39/q19/p1 It emphasizes and confines true multiplication to sincere givers. — Ālūsī says the demonstrative, separating pronoun and nominal form intensify the promise; Ibn ʿĀshūr says they disregard transient worldly gains. Biqāʿī sees the singular address as honoring the Prophet's awareness before the shift. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 30:39/q19/p2 Its omitted complement can refer to the donors' multiplied reward. — Al-Zamakhsharī supplies the complement and sees speech addressed to angels; Abū Ḥayyān calls it easier but judges the earlier construal more beneficial. Samīn calls 'fa-muʾtūhu' easier, citing 2:97. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)]

## 30:39/q20 Who are al-muḍʿifūn, and what is multiplied?  [ٱلْمُضْعِفُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether multiplication describes divine recompense alone or also growth and blessing of property.
- 30:39/q20/p1 They possess multiplied good deeds or reward. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Kisāʾī, al-Zajjāj, Jishumī and others explain the form by Arabic labels for owners of strong, fat, thirsty or weak camels, or wealthy people. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the title excludes merely transient worldly gain. Qurṭubī distinguishes multiplied deeds and multiplied good or bliss. [27 notes: FARRA(الكسائي); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 30:39/q20/p2 Their property increases in this world as well as their reward. — Al-Kalbī says zakat does not diminish wealth; Ṭabrisī cites a Prophetic report to the same effect. Biqāʿī speaks of protection and blessing now, reward later. ʿAlī and Fāṭima say zakat brings provision; Ālūsī gives a causative analysis. [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); TABRISI(أمير المؤمنين); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبي); TABATABAI(أمير المؤمنين,الزهراء)]

## 30:39/q21 How is al-muḍʿifūn alternatively read?  [ٱلْمُضْعِفُونَ · readings]
turns on: Active possession or causal action versus passive multiplication.
- 30:39/q21/p1 The usual active form denotes possessors or makers of multiples. — Samīn and Ālūsī relate the form to possessing multiplied return, as with strength or wealth; Bayḍāwī notes the alternative vocalization. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبي); BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 30:39/q21/p2 Ubayy reads an open ʿayn, yielding a passive participle. — Abū Ḥayyān records the fatḥa; Ālūsī says it supports being multiplied through zakat, and Bayḍāwī finds emphasis in departure from parallel wording. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبي); SAMIN-DURR; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبي)]

## 30:39/q22 How great is the promised multiplication?  [ٱلْمُضْعِفُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the stated multiple is the tenfold baseline or unbounded divine generosity.
- 30:39/q22/p1 At least tenfold, with further increase as God wills. — Muqātil, Qatāda, al-Zajjāj and others say tenfold or more. Rāzī says it is the worth of the good act, not ten literal objects; his loaf and king examples distinguish ordinary compensation, mercy and extraordinary generosity. Qurṭubī cites 2:245 and Elmalılı 2:261. [19 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 30:39/q22/p2 The sincere can receive limitless increase beyond expectation. — Taʾwīlāt Najmiyya says human expectations are finite but God's timeless generosity is not; Bursawī says God guarantees replenishment, citing the Mathnawī's angels and the Būstān's appeal to give before death. [4 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 30:39/q23 What reports illustrate charity's growth?  [زَكَوٰةٍۢ ٱلْمُضْعِفُونَ · reports]
turns on: The reports describe reward growing or worldly provision being preserved.
- 30:39/q23/p1 God receives and grows even a small lawful charity. — Rāzī cites the Prophet saying it is placed in the Merciful's hand until mountain-sized; Ibn Kathīr calls sound the report of a date from lawful earnings growing greater than Mount Uḥud. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(النبي عليه الصلاة والسلام); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 30:39/q23/p2 Charity does not diminish wealth. — Ṭabrisī transmits a Prophetic report; al-Kalbī applies it to zakat, while ʿAlī says zakat brings provision. [4 notes: TABRISI(أمير المؤمنين); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); TABATABAI(أمير المؤمنين)]

## 30:39/q24 How does this verse connect to the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Which nearby theme frames the contrast: social support, the incentive to give, or divine power.
- 30:39/q24/p1 It contrasts the preceding duty of support with extraction through usury, then returns to God-directed giving. — Ibn ʿĀshūr connects 30:38's kin rights to zakat and says interest exploits needy borrowers; Islahi stresses wealth spent for rights versus interest drawn from others; Kuran Yolu traces provision, aid, usury and monotheism through 37–40. Qurṭubī notes the return to sincere giving. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 30:39/q24/p2 The contrast urges zakat by reversing the apparent advantage of gifts for gain. — Rāzī says people happily give one to obtain two; since that gain fails before God while charity grows, the verse encourages charity. Bursawī calls money a temporary loan to its holder and urges investing for the lasting life; Islahi says fear focuses people on worldly future. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 30:39/q24/p3 The next passage resumes proofs of God's unique power. — Al-Wāḥidī reports Muqātil relating it to abandonment of monotheism; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the next resumption a proof of divine control and resurrection, comparing 10:34. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 30:39/q25 How does it relate to other verses on usury and giving?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the comparison is with other economic verses or with the Prophet-specific gift command.
- 30:39/q25/p1 It parallels God's erasure of ribā and growth of charity at 2:276. — Abū Ḥayyān, Biqāʿī, Bursawī, Ālūsī, Asad and Kuran Yolu point to that contrast; Qurṭubī cites the sincere spending garden at 2:265; Burhān also cites 2:278–279. Study Quran contrasts ruinous compounding loans. [10 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 30:39/q25/p2 On the gift reading, 74:6 supplies the Prophet's special prohibition. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī and others connect the command against giving for more to this practice; Durr's parallel has 73:6. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الضحاك بن مزاحم); TAB-FULL(الضحاك); FARISI-HUJJA; NAHHAS(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير ومجاهد وطاوس وقتادة والضحاك وأكثر المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL]

## 30:39/q26 When was this verse revealed relative to ribā's prohibition?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether chronology favors an early warning or a Medinan legal setting.
- 30:39/q26/p1 It is an early mention that condemns ribā before the later explicit legal ban. — Asad calls it the first mention; he and Study Quran date 2:275–281 to the final revelation, with ʿUmar saying its implications were not explained. Kuran Yolu calls this a first signal; Elmalılı says it censured Thaqīf's interest before prohibition. [8 notes: ELMALILI(Süddî); ASAD-NOTES('Umar ibn al-Khattab); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 30:39/q26/p2 The verse and preceding verse appear Medinan. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī sets aside reports or claimed consensus for Meccan dating; on a Medinan setting he reads forbidden usury and obligatory alms, while a Meccan setting favors lawful gifts and voluntary charity. [3 notes: TABATABAI]

## 30:39/q27 What legal and moral scope does condemnation of ribā have?  [رِّبًۭا · law]
turns on: How far a moral condemnation can settle legal cases before or apart from explicit later prohibition.
- 30:39/q27/p1 Interest-bearing loans and forbidden exchanges fall under ribā. — Early jurists broadly prohibited interest loans regardless of rate or motive, according to Asad. Bursawī includes same-kind excess and deferred exchange and says renaming a loan gift does not legalize it; necessity may excuse a compelled borrower. Ālūsī says many infer prohibition here. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(كثير); ASAD-NOTES(most of the early Muslim jurists)]
- 30:39/q27/p2 The definition turns on exploitative, risk-free gain and changing economic conditions. — Asad notes no comprehensive agreed definition, cites Ibn Kathīr's difficulty and ʿUmar's uncertainty, and emphasizes weaker borrowers, lender-guaranteed gain and equitable risk sharing; he says generations must reinterpret cases. Islahi contrasts risk-bearing trade with gain from needy borrowers. [9 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Ibn Kathir,Islamic scholars); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 30:39/q27/p3 The verse implies prohibition even if the immediate phrase describes a gift. — Elmalılı says figurative gifts are censured by analogy and even more strongly imply prohibition of usury; Ālūsī objects that the same inference would also reach the usually permitted gift. Kuran Yolu notes the verse has no explicit ban. [3 notes: ELMALILI(İbn Abbas); ALUSI-FULL(كثير); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî, Şevkânî)]

## 30:39/q28 How is ribā pronounced at pause?  [رِّبًۭا · readings]
turns on: Transmitted recitation versus grammatical accounts of pause alif.
- 30:39/q28/p1 The transmitted pause is with yāʾ. — Ibn al-Jazarī cites al-Dānī's sole explicit Warsh report, claimed consensus and his teachers' practice; he prefers it as sound analogy. [3 notes: NASHR(الداني)]
- 30:39/q28/p2 Some grammarians permit opening tanwīn or a between-vowels pause. — Al-Shāṭibī and al-Sakhāwī transmit unrestricted fatḥ or intermediate sound, but Ibn al-Jazarī rejects it as analogy unattested in qirāʾāt manuals. Al-Māzinī says pause alif replaces tanwīn; al-Kisāʾī, Kufans and some Basrans say it restores the original final letter. [3 notes: NASHR(الشاطبي والسخاوي,الكسائي والكوفيون وبعض البصريين,المازني); NASHR-]

## 30:39/q29 Why is zakāt written with wāw?  [زَكَوٰةٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 30:39/q29/p1 It marks the universal category promised multiplication. — Burhān contrasts its universal scope in God's judgment with the local ribā written with alif. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 30:39/q30 What does the final report about the Prophet's family say?  [* · reports]
- 30:39/q30/p1 His family are called the word of piety and path of guidance. — Ibn ʿAbbās transmits a sermon describing them also as the highest example, greatest proof and firmest bond. [1 notes: TABATABAI(عبيد الله بن عباس)]

## 30:39/q31 Who gives to whom in the first address?  [ءَاتَيْتُم · referent]
- 30:39/q31/p1 Some among those addressed give to others. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the act of giving necessarily has both donor and recipient within the plural address. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 30:39/q32 What is the alif written after the wāw of ليربوا?  [لِّيَرْبُوَا۟ · grammar]
- 30:39/q32/p1 It is an authoritative written form, not a plural marker. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says readers agree it does not mark a plural, despite the letter's appearance after wāw. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 30:39/q33 What causes the charitable giver's reward to multiply?  [تُرِيدُونَ وَجْهَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلْمُضْعِفُونَ · interpretation]
- 30:39/q33/p1 God multiplies a gift directed to Him; resisting self-interest deepens its merit. — Ibn Kathīr contrasts return-seeking giving with zakat seeking God's face, for which God multiplies recompense. Qushayrī adds that mastering the self against its preferences earns divine recompense. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; QUSHAYRI]
