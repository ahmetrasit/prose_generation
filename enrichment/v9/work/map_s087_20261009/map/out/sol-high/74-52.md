# 74:52: 16 questions

## 74:52/q01 How does 'bal' relate their demand to their earlier recoil from the reminder?  [بَلْ · links]
- 74:52/q01/p1 Rather than accept the reminder, they recoil from it and demand private divine messages. — Al-Ālūsī supplies the omitted sense 'they are not satisfied with the admonition'; al-Bursevī calls the demand stubborn. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads a shift from flight to a demand made despite manifest truth and the preceding threat and promise. Elmalılı likens the expected invitation to a personal notice of duties; Iṣlāḥī says the Prophet should continue reminding those receptive under God's laws. [6 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:52/q02 Does 'wants' describe real willing when its object will not happen?  [يُرِيدُ · theology]
- 74:52/q02/p1 Yes, willing can genuinely aim at something that will not occur. — Al-Jishumī explicitly argues against opponents who reduce such desire to mere wishing. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 74:52/q03 Who are 'each of them' who make the demand?  [كُلُّ ٱمْرِئٍۢ مِّنْهُمْ · referent]
- 74:52/q03/p1 The Quraysh unbelievers or polytheists, including Abū Jahl, ʿAbd Allāh ibn Abī Umayya, and their associates. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes it to ʿAbd Allāh ibn Abī Umayya and others; al-Qurṭubī names Abū Jahl and Quraysh; al-Bursevī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Kur'an Yolu likewise name leading objectors. Al-Wāḥidī and Muqātil identify Quraysh; al-Ṭabarī says the refusal was not ignorance of the Qurʾān's divine source. [11 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(كفار قريش); TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون، ومجاهد، ومقاتل، وقتادة، والحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن أبي أمية وغيره); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(أبو جهل وعبد الله بن أمية وأصحابهما); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو جهل وعبد الله بن أبي أمية); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Ebû Cehil ve yandaşları)]

## 74:52/q04 What does 'imriʾ' denote and how does it inflect?  [ٱمْرِئٍۢ · meaning]
- 74:52/q04/p1 It means a human being or man. — Al-Bursevī cites al-Qāmūs for three forms of its initial hamza and case behavior, and says it has no plural from the same lexical form. [1 notes: BURSEVI(القاموس)]

## 74:52/q05 What pages did each person demand?  [أَن يُؤْتَىٰ صُحُفًۭا مُّنَشَّرَةًۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the requested pages are commands to follow Muhammad, guarantees of safety, records of deeds, prophet-like revelation, tangible proof, or a metaphor; the Israelite precedent and the force of 'spread pages' are disputed.
- 74:52/q05/p1 A separate heavenly letter or book addressed to each person, certifying Muhammad's mission and ordering him to believe and follow. — Muqātil adds that it would declare idols false, God true, and angels witnesses; Gabriel offered punishment like Israel's, which the Prophet disliked, and God says such books would not make them believe. Qatāda, Mujāhid, al-Ḥasan, Ibn Zayd and later commentators give the individually addressed order; al-Wāḥidī says it would arrive at each man's head. Al-Ālūsī calls this the principal interpretation; al-Ṭabarī says they already knew the Qurʾān was from God. Elmalılı compares a personalized invitation or notice of tasks and benefits. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the demand another tactic for denying revelation. [34 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة,مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون، ومجاهد، ومقاتل، وقتادة، والحسن); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الحسن وقتادة ومجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(المفسرون); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن وقتادة وابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجمهور); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI(أبو جهل وعبد الله بن أمية وأصحابهما); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو جهل وعبد الله بن أبي أمية); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Ebû Cehil ve yandaşları); STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة وابن زيد)+; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 74:52/q05/p2 A personal document guaranteeing acquittal and safety from the Fire, even without meritorious deeds. — Abū Ṣāliḥ, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Suddī, and other reports place a page at or under each person's head by morning; Matar al-Warrāq and Qatāda say they wanted acquittal without deeds. Al-Ṭabrisī adds promises of blessing while they persisted in disbelief. Al-Suyūṭī and al-Durr explicitly say this demand occasioned the verse. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو صالح); JISHUMI(ابن عباس,مطر الوراق); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,مطر الوراق); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(السدي عن أبي صالح); SUYUTI-LUBAB(السدي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو صالح); STUDYQURAN(some of the Quraysh); TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 74:52/q05/p3 Visible sheets recording each person's sins and, in some accounts, their expiation, like those attributed to Israelites. — Al-Farrāʾ and Ibn Qutayba report an objection that Israelites saw written sins, sometimes on a slip; al-Zajjāj locates the writing on the door; al-Kalbī and others put it at or above the sleeper's head and add the remedy or expiation. Al-Wāḥidī reports al-Farrāʾ and al-Zajjāj prefer this account because plural pages suit one page per man. Al-Jishumī says the Prophet disliked the demand. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī find it remote from 'spread pages' unless the writing was openly exposed; al-Ālūsī says that qualification brings it back to the basic idea. [10 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(كفار قريش); ZAJJAJ(قيل); MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج)+; KASHSHAF-FULL-; RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 74:52/q05/p4 Individual heavenly revelation of the Qurʾān's true call, like that received by God's messengers. — Mujāhid in Ibn Kathīr says each polytheist wanted a book revealed to him as the Prophet's was; Ibn Kathīr cites 6:124. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says each sought a separate scripture containing the Qurʾān's message, which reveals arrogance toward God and rejection of a truthful messenger despite clear signs; he rejects the more detailed proposals as contextually remote. Iṣlāḥī compares 6:124 and God's knowledge of whom to entrust. [7 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI+]
- 74:52/q05/p5 The already written records of each person's deeds presented for inspection. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports objectors who asked to inspect the sheets if deeds were written; al-Qurṭubī speaks specifically of recorded sins; Abū Ḥayyān says this request occasioned the verse. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]
- 74:52/q05/p6 Each wants to become a prophet, receive revelation and followers, and avoid following another. — Al-Ṭabrisī gives this as another interpretation; Elmalılı offers it as a possible reading of separate pages. [2 notes: TABRISI; ELMALILI]
- 74:52/q05/p7 A tangible book written on parchment that they could touch. — Al-Rāzī associates this account with 17:93 and 6:7. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 74:52/q05/p8 Public mention of each person with praise, with 'pages' standing for remembrance. — Al-Qurṭubī transmits this as an alternative metaphorical interpretation. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]

## 74:52/q06 How is the clause 'that he be given spread pages' parsed?  [أَن يُؤْتَىٰ صُحُفًۭا مُّنَشَّرَةًۭ · grammar]
- 74:52/q06/p1 The an-clause is the first object of yurīdu; the passive verb's pronoun is its first object, ṣuḥuf its second, and munashshara describes ṣuḥuf. — Al-Bursevī supplies this object and adjective analysis. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 74:52/q07 What does 'ṣuḥuf' mean, and what are its forms?  [صُحُفًۭا · meaning]
- 74:52/q07/p1 It means written sheets or books, the plural of ṣaḥīfa. — Al-Wāḥidī cites al-Layth: fuʿul for faʿīla is rare, like safīna/sufun, beside the expected ṣaḥāʾif and safāʾin. Al-Jishumī adds that muṣḥaf derives from ṣaḥīfa and pluralizes as maṣāḥif. Al-Ṭabrisī explains the sheet as written on and turnable from side to side. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الليث); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI]

## 74:52/q08 Why is 'pages' plural when each person receives them?  [صُحُفًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the plurality is within each person's instructions, across all recipients, or one page per individual in the Israelite analogy.
- 74:52/q08/p1 Each person's separate command or prohibition would require its own document. — Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the plural distributively across instructions addressed to an individual. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:52/q08/p2 The many individually addressed documents are collectively described as though every person receives all of them. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explains this as collective phrasing for a jointly stated demand. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:52/q08/p3 The plural fits one separate page for every man. — Al-Wāḥidī reports al-Farrāʾ and al-Zajjāj use this fit to prefer the Israelite sin-record account. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج)+]

## 74:52/q09 What does 'munashshara' convey about the pages?  [مُّنَشَّرَةًۭ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the adjective describes visibly opened pages, their fresh heavenly delivery, or a figurative enlivening; sources also differ on the verb form's usual usage.
- 74:52/q09/p1 They are spread open, unfolded, accessible and readable. — Al-Jishumī defines nashr as the opposite of folding. Al-Qurṭubī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī, Asad and others stress visibility or readability; Ibn ʿĀshūr says they would reject a folded document and cites opening the Torah in the stoning report. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Bursevī say the intensive form signals numerous distributions; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it stresses unfolding. Al-Naḥḥās explains the feminine singular adjective as agreement with the feminine collective and says singular is the more numerous form. Al-Jishumī and al-Ālūsī also note the verb for spreading cloth or writing. Al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn Jinnī compare unfolding with reviving a dead thing, supported by al-Taymī or al-Mutanabbī's poetry; Ibn Jinnī says anshara for cloth is unattested but the analogy licenses it. Asad says the pages would be open so everyone could understand. [26 notes: NAHHAS; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(التيمي); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(المتنبي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(تاج المصادر); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 74:52/q09/p2 Freshly written heavenly books brought by angels before they have been folded. — Al-Zamakhsharī and Abū Ḥayyān offer this alongside ordinary open letters. Al-Ālūsī transmits it but finds it far-fetched. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 74:52/q09/p3 The opened contents figuratively come alive when mentioned. — Abū al-Baqā, reported by al-Samīn, offers this as one explanation of Ibn Jubayr's light form, alongside pages commanded to spread or already spread. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]

## 74:52/q10 How is 'ṣuḥufan munashshara' read and explained?  [صُحُفًۭا مُّنَشَّرَةًۭ · readings]
turns on: The consonant lightening, the regional account of ḥāʾ sukūn, and whether the ordinary verb for spreading or the revival analogy explains the light adjective.
- 74:52/q10/p1 The common reading vowels ṣuḥuf and uses the doubled form munashshara. — Abū Ḥayyān attributes rounded vowels to the majority; al-Samīn calls the doubled form canonical and refuses the Shaykh's implied criticism that ordinary spreading normally uses the light triliteral verb. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور وابن جبير); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)]
- 74:52/q10/p2 Saʿīd ibn Jubayr reads with still ḥāʾ in ṣuḥuf and still nūn or a light shīn in munshara. — Ibn Jinnī calls the ḥāʾ sukūn Tamīmī, while Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it Yemeni; al-Qurṭubī calls the nūn form anomalous. Ibn Jinnī supports the light form through ordinary nashara for cloth and the verb for reviving the dead; al-Taymī's verse witnesses revival, and he permits analogy from a folded sheet to a dead thing. Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes nashara for a sheet, anshara for God reviving the dead, and nashara for the dead is also attested; he says al-Taymī reverses the comparison. Al-Ṭabrisī cites Ibn Jinnī and al-Mutanabbī's revived-dead line. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī liken anshara/nashara to anzala/nazzala. Abū al-Baqā suggests ordered-to-spread, already-spread, or figuratively enlivened contents; al-Samīn and al-Qurṭubī defend the canonical doubled form despite ordinary cloth usage. [22 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(التيمي,سعيد بن جبير); KASHSHAF-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); TABRISI(ابن جني,المتنبي,سعيد بن جبير); RAZI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور وابن جبير); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير)]

## 74:52/q11 How does the following 'kallā' answer their demand?  [* · links]
- 74:52/q11/p1 It rebukes and rejects the demand; the requested documents will not be given. — Ibn Qutayba gives kallā's broader use as rebuke, restraint, denial of a claim, or rejection of conduct, citing 70:39, 74:52, 75:20, 104:4, 82:9 and 83:7. Al-Zamakhsharī calls it rebuke for extraordinary signs; al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn al-Jawzī say the request will not happen; Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects its apparent terms. Al-Biqāʿī adds that their profession of willingness to follow is insincere whether or not documents come. [6 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:52/q12 How does this demand compare with demands elsewhere in the Qurʾān?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether 17:93's single book is close enough to the individually distributed pages; other sources compare the desire for prophetic revelation or direct address.
- 74:52/q12/p1 It parallels the demand for a heavenly book to read in 17:93 and, in some accounts, a touchable parchment in 6:7. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursevī and al-Ālūsī draw the comparison; Muqātil also mentions 17:90. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that 74:52 specifies separate pages for each person, whereas 17:93 asks for one book. [15 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون، ومجاهد، ومقاتل، وقتادة، والحسن); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(أبو جهل وعبد الله بن أمية وأصحابهما); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 74:52/q12/p2 It resembles 6:124, where objectors want what God's messengers received. — Ibn Kathīr, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Iṣlāḥī cite 6:124; Iṣlāḥī adds that God knows whom to entrust with revelation. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:52/q12/p3 It resembles 2:118's request for direct divine speech without a prophet's mediation. — Asad cites 2:118; Study Quran also compares 2:118 and 17:90–93. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ)]

## 74:52/q13 What motive or lesson does the demand reveal?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the demand chiefly expresses pride in wanting revelation, desire for unearned safety, or a wish for prophetic rank.
- 74:52/q13/p1 It exposes arrogance and rejection of clear revelation through a demand for personal divine address. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says their aversion is more than flight: seeking separate revelation refuses a truthful messenger despite clear signs. Asad calls it an early Qurʾānic illustration of false pride. Iṣlāḥī calls open scrolls an excuse; al-Biqāʿī says they would not follow even if given them, and Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a denial tactic. [6 notes: TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو جهل وعبد الله بن أبي أمية)]
- 74:52/q13/p2 They seek exemption from punishment without deeds. — Qatāda and Matar al-Warrāq explicitly mention acquittal without action; reports of guaranteed safety and blessing elaborate the wish. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); JISHUMI(مطر الوراق); QURTUBI-FULL(مطر الوراق); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI]
- 74:52/q13/p3 They refuse to be followers and instead seek prophecy and followers of their own. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports this reading; Elmalılı gives it as a possible intention behind a separate message. [2 notes: TABRISI; ELMALILI]

## 74:52/q14 What inward lesson is drawn from the opened pages?  [* · inward]
- 74:52/q14/p1 Those empty of Truth and heedless of standing before God cannot grasp the opened pages or their hidden secrets. — Al-Sulamī transmits al-Ḥusayn's question about people turned away from the concerns of Truth. [1 notes: SULAMI(الحسين)]

## 74:52/q15 How should multiple possible meanings in Qurʾānic wording be weighed?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether candidate meanings are compatible or conflicting, and whether evidence excludes one or merely strengthens another.
- 74:52/q15/p1 Compatible meanings with equal support can both be intended, unless evidence rules one out. — Al-Māwardī says combining compatible senses is permitted and more eloquent than separate wording; if neither is preferred by evidence, God would have specified a single sense had He meant only one. He uses an āḥād report attributed to the Prophet, that every verse has outward and inward senses and each letter a limit and ascent, as compatible with his principles. He presents this method for assessing possible meanings. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 74:52/q15/p2 If evidence disproves one possible meaning, it and its ruling fall away; if evidence only supports another, the first remains possible. — Al-Māwardī distinguishes decisive exclusion from positive support that leaves another wording-based sense possible; he reports scholars who give stronger status to the evidenced meaning. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(بعض أهل العلم)]
- 74:52/q15/p3 When two equally supported rulings conflict, a jurist may choose either. — Al-Māwardī reports this as one scholarly view for conflicting rulings or meanings. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(العلماء)]
- 74:52/q15/p4 When two equally supported rulings conflict, the stricter ruling must be chosen. — Al-Māwardī reports this as the other scholarly view. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(العلماء)]

## 74:52/q16 What recitation merit is reported for this sūra?  [* · sciences]
- 74:52/q16/p1 A report promises ten good deeds per believer or unbeliever in Mecca to one who recites al-Muddaththir. — Al-Zamakhsharī attributes the promise to the Prophet and states no grading. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله)]
