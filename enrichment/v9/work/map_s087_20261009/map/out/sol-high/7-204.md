# 7:204: 23 questions

## 7:204/q01 How is قُرِئَ pronounced?  [قُرِئَ · readings]
- 7:204/q01/p1 The hamza is softened to yāʾ. — QIRAAT-ER calls this a dialectal phonological variant with no semantic change. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 7:204/q02 What counts as recitation in قُرِئَ?  [قُرِئَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the sound is a fresh, intentional act of reading.
- 7:204/q02/p1 Intentional articulated reading by a human speaker counts as recitation; echoes, silent reading, and recordings do not. — Elmalılı requires rational intention, articulation, and understanding; Gabriel causes recitation, while God reveals and creates it. Echoes and radio or gramophone traces do not trigger recitation duties such as prostration or obligatory listening. [5 notes: ELMALILI]
- 7:204/q02/p2 The passive wording leaves the reciter unrestricted. — Ālūsī says anyone can be the reader because the verb is passive. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:204/q03 What is the grammatical role of ٱلْقُرْءَانُ?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانُ · grammar]
- 7:204/q03/p1 It is the nominative subject substitute of the passive verb. — Jishumī observes that the passive verb is predicated of it. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 7:204/q04 How is ٱلْقُرْءَانُ pronounced?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانُ · readings]
- 7:204/q04/p1 The hamza may be dropped in continuous pronunciation, giving Qurān. — QIRAAT-ER identifies a canonical variant without the glottal stop between rāʾ and alif. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 7:204/q05 What does ٱلْقُرْءَانُ mean or derive from?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانُ · meaning]
- 7:204/q05/p1 Its root relates to gathering, while qirāʾa is reading with an underlying sense of following. — Jishumī compares the gathering of stars, menstrual blood in the womb, and blood in the body during purity. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 7:204/q06 Why is ٱلْقُرْءَانُ named again instead of using a pronoun?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانُ · rhetoric]
- 7:204/q06/p1 The explicit name honors the Qur'an, stresses the instruction, and makes it stand on its own. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this an eloquent usage; al-Ṭībī says the name brings out why accepting and obeying it produces mercy. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي)]

## 7:204/q07 In which settings does the listening command apply?  [وَإِذَا قُرِئَ ٱلْقُرْءَانُ فَٱسْتَمِعُوا۟ لَهُۥ وَأَنصِتُوا۟ · law]

## 7:204/q08 Who is addressed by the commands?  [فَٱسْتَمِعُوا۟ لَهُۥ وَأَنصِتُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: The preceding promise of mercy to believers, the Meccan opposition, and which audience the conditional hope of mercy best fits.
- 7:204/q08/p1 Believers are addressed to accept and act on the Qur'an. — Al-Ṭībī finds believers explicit in the surrounding discourse: it contrasts rejection with their obedience, while al-Ṭabarī calls them to understand signs and admonition. Biqāʿī describes a rhetorical turn toward the Prophet as their leader. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي)+]
- 7:204/q08/p2 Disbelievers are directly addressed as the Prophet recites to them. — Rāzī appeals to 41:26, the demand for signs, the Qur'an's miracle, and the conditional hope of mercy; Ibn ʿĀshūr sees the Meccan opponents as the target of the warning. Ālūsī replies that believers can also hope for further mercy. [10 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 7:204/q08/p3 The address reaches both disbelievers and Muslims. — Abū Ḥayyān assigns possible faith to disbelievers and reward for obedience to believers. Ibn ʿĀshūr says polytheists are admonished and Muslims guided, with several ways to understand who is explicit or implicit in the Meccan sequence. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:204/q09 What does فَٱسْتَمِعُوا۟ require?  [فَٱسْتَمِعُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'listening' is a literal auditory command, a figure for compliance, or both.
- 7:204/q09/p1 Deliberately attend with the ears to understand, reflect on, and heed the recitation. — Al-Ṭabarī names signs, examples, duties and admonition; al-Biqāʿī says to direct the ears and avoid distractions. Bursawī distinguishes intentional listening from incidental hearing. Ibn ʿĀshūr derives intensity from the verb form. Qatāda distinguishes ear-listening from tongue-silence; STUDYQURAN and ISLAHI add reflection and action. [18 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(عبد الله); MAWARDI-FULL; THALABI(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:204/q09/p2 Heed the Qur'an by accepting it, acting on its commands, and not exceeding it. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq compare God's 'hearing' a prayer to answering it. Al-Ṭībī prefers this figurative sense because it fits the surrounding contrast and makes prayer silence an application a fortiori. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 7:198; KURANYOLU prefers both literal and figurative listening. [16 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI(الزجاج); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI(شيخنا أبو حامد); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج); TABRISI(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج وجماعة); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr); ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي)+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 7:204/q09/p3 Listening includes both literal hearing and active obedience. — Ibn ʿĀshūr and KURANYOLU combine attentive hearing with compliance; attentive listening becomes insight, faith, corrected conduct and mercy. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]

## 7:204/q10 To whom does the pronoun in لَهُۥ refer?  [لَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: The nearest named antecedent versus God as the speaker through the revelation.
- 7:204/q10/p1 It refers to the Qur'an. — Al-Samīn connects the phrase to استمعوا, 'for its sake.' [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 7:204/q10/p2 It refers to God, the speaker through the Qur'an. — Abū al-Baqāʾ permits the pronoun to mean 'for God,' which al-Samīn finds unlikely; Ālūsī gives the inward ground that God speaks through the Qur'an. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-]

## 7:204/q11 How does the lām in لَهُۥ work?  [لَهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the preposition retains purpose, substitutes for ilā, or is syntactically extra.
- 7:204/q11/p1 It indicates purpose: listen for the Qur'an's sake. — Al-Samīn attaches it to استمعوا; Ālūsī includes purpose among the possibilities. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:204/q11/p2 It stands for ilā, 'to'. — Abū al-Baqāʾ and Ālūsī allow this; al-Samīn finds it unnecessary. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-]
- 7:204/q11/p3 It is extra, equivalent to 'listen to it' as a direct object. — Abū al-Baqāʾ permits it, while al-Samīn notes most grammarians allow the extra lām only with an advanced object or a derived governing form; Ālūsī also lists it. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-]

## 7:204/q12 What does وَأَنصِتُوا۟ mean?  [وَأَنصِتُوا۟ · meaning]
turns on: Whether inṣāt entails total attention and no recitation, or only quieting the voice.
- 7:204/q12/p1 Be silent while listening attentively, without distracting speech or turning away. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī and others give naṣata, anṣata and intaṣata as equivalent forms. Al-Māwardī bars answering aloud or turning away. Bursawī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī say inṣāt includes listening, not mute stillness alone; Rāzī derives this from the joined commands. Al-Qumayt's poem uses anṣata transitively, 'silenced another'; al-Azharī and Ibn al-Aʿrāb list related forms. [21 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأصمعي,الكميت); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن الأعرابي,الأزهري); QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء,الكميت); BIQAI-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 7:204/q12/p2 Silence means refraining from speech, while the ears hear and retain. — Jishumī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya gloss simple silence; Qatāda explicitly divides silence by tongue and listening by ears. In prayer, this forbids greetings and discussions but al-Zajjāj's report exempts devotional speech. [5 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ]
- 7:204/q12/p3 Silence can mean not reciting aloud even if one reads silently. — Al-Wāḥidī says Arabs call a person anṣata when none hears his recitation; Rāzī objects that even silent recitation divides attention and cites God's command to Moses in 20:13. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); RAZI-FULL-]

## 7:204/q13 Why combine listening and silence?  [فَٱسْتَمِعُوا۟ لَهُۥ وَأَنصِتُوا۟ · rhetoric]
- 7:204/q13/p1 Silence completes attentive listening and emphasizes the Qur'an's claim on one's attention. — Al-Qurṭubī calls it focused silence, Ālūsī says the conjunction intensifies care for the Qur'an, and STUDYQURAN sees near-synonyms emphasizing sacred recitation. Al-Biqāʿī says silence removes inner resistance and allows reflection; Bursawī says it honors and completes listening. [9 notes: SULAMI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:204/q14 What must a follower recite while an imam leads prayer?  [فَٱسْتَمِعُوا۟ لَهُۥ وَأَنصِتُوا۟ · law]
turns on: Whether silence includes inaudible personal recitation, and how the general command relates to the specific Fātiḥa reports and the report that an imam's reading suffices.
- 7:204/q14/p1 A follower must not recite aloud or answer warning and mercy verses while the imam recites. — Ibn Masʿūd rebukes reciting along, Mujāhid forbids verbal responses to warning or mercy, and the Prophet's report commands following the imam and quiet during his recitation. STUDYQURAN explicitly bars speech and aloud recitation; this does not itself decide inaudible reading. - 7:204/q14/p2 The follower refrains from reciting in both audible and silent prayer; the imam's recitation suffices. — Abū Ḥanīfa and Aḥmad appeal to general silence, including when hearing is impossible; reports say the imam's reading counts for the follower. Ibn Masʿūd, Zayd, ʿAlī, ʿUmar, Saʿd, al-Shaʿbī's seventy Badr veterans, and ʿAlqama are invoked. Ālūsī defends the raised and mursal Jābir routes, names transmitters and their gradings, and favors a later stronger unrestricted prohibition; he doubts claims of literal Companion consensus and notes criticism of ʿAlī's report. Ibn Kathīr judges the Companion form of Jābir's report sounder than its Prophetic form. Al-Sarakhsī reports that some saw recitation as invalidating prayer; Abū Ḥanīfa's school deems it prohibitively disliked. Bayḍāwī calls the omission inference weak. Ibn ʿĀshūr says silence while an inaudible imam reads is not listening. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأوزاعي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(من لا يرى وجوب القراءة على المأموم); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 7:204/q14/p4 The follower recites in quiet prayer or when the imam pauses, but refrains during audible imam recitation. — Al-Zuhrī says the imam's audible reading suffices even when unheard but permits silent reading in quiet prayer. Mālik and early al-Shāfiʿī bar al-Fātiḥa only in audible prayer. ʿUrwa, al-Qāsim, Mālik and others combine listening with a Sunnah duty to read when the imam is silent. Ālūsī says that claimed duty lacks proof; some Shāfiʿīs recite if the loud reading cannot be distinguished. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(الزهري); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); RAZI-FULL(مالك والشافعي في قوله القديم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الزهري,الشافعي); ALUSI-FULL(الشافعية,عروة بن الزبير والقاسم بن محمد والزهري ومالك وابن المبارك وأحمد وإسحق); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some jurists); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 7:204/q14/p5 The follower still recites al-Fātiḥa, especially during the imam's pauses, while otherwise listening. — Al-Shāfiʿī and Abū Salama point to two pauses; the Prophetic report exempts al-Fātiḥa because prayer without it is invalid. Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers this with sound reports. Rāzī accepts specifying the verse with the more specific report, but criticizes the pause solution: pauses lack consensus, can be too short, or leave simultaneous recitation and make the imam subordinate. ʿUbāda's dawn prayer report exempts only the Opening; Ālūsī prioritizes the later unrestricted ban. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو سلمة,الشافعي,النبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); RAZI-FULL(النبي); ALUSI-FULL(الشافعي,عبادة بن الصامت); IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 7:204/q14/p6 The follower recites in both silent and audible prayers. — Al-Bukhārī selected this in a separate treatise; a Muzanī transmission gives it from al-Shāfiʿī. Another al-Shāfiʿī transmission specifies al-Fātiḥa and a sūra in silent prayer and al-Fātiḥa in audible prayer. ISLAHI notes jurists citing the verse for Fātiḥa in silent prayer. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(البخاري); ALUSI-FULL(الشافعي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some jurists); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 7:204/q15 How should one behave during the Friday sermon?  [وَأَنصِتُوا۟ · law]
turns on: Whether the verse governs all preaching or only the Qur'anic recitation within it.
- 7:204/q15/p1 Listen silently and attentively to the preacher. — ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz includes every preacher; Ṭabarī notes consensus on Friday-sermon listening; Ibn ʿAbbās treats it as prayer and says speech nullifies it. Hanafis call sermon silence obligatory with qualifications. Ibn ʿAṭiyya roots the general sermon rule in Sunna while this verse covers Qur'an recited in it. [13 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(جابر بن عبد الله); TAB-FULL(عطاء,عطاء بن أبي رباح,مجاهد); THALABI(عمر بن عبد العزيز); IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(عمر بن عبد العزيز); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير والطبري); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عطاء); ALUSI-FULL; TAB-FULL+]
- 7:204/q15/p2 The verse specifically calls for hearing the Qur'an recited during the sermon. — A reported view limits the Qur'anic wording to recitation within the sermon rather than all preaching; Ibn al-ʿArabī objects to using this as the verse's occasion because the Qur'anic part is brief. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي والنقاش)-]

## 7:204/q16 What etiquette follows when the Qur'an is recited outside prayer?  [فَٱسْتَمِعُوا۟ لَهُۥ وَأَنصِتُوا۟ · law]
turns on: How far the listening duty extends beyond prayer and how to honor recitation where attention is difficult.
- 7:204/q16/p1 Speaking during another's recitation outside prayer is permissible, though attentive hearing is recommended. — Mujāhid permits ordinary conversation outside prayer; ʿUbayd and ʿAṭāʾ continued talking while a storyteller spoke. Ibn ʿAṭiyya still makes honoring the Qur'an obligatory in every state. [9 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(عبيد بن عمير وعطاء بن أبي رباح,مجاهد); THALABI(عبيد بن عمير وعطاء بن أبي رباح); WAHIDI-BASIT(عبيد بن عمير وعطاء بن أبي رباح); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن,مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 7:204/q16/p2 Honor the recitation with attention, avoid distraction, and choose circumstances in which others can listen. — ʿUthmān b. Zāʾida covered his face to remove visual distraction; Ālūsī recommends fine clothes, turban, and facing the qibla, allows walking or working with attention, and discourages stretched legs, nakedness, and disruptive places. Al-Nawawī allows bathhouse and road recitation but dislikes the toilet and noisy mill; KURANYOLU discourages loud reading in markets, streets, cafés and mosque projection when listeners cannot attend. Elmalılı requires listening even if a reciter sins by reading in an unsuitable place. [10 notes: DURR-FULL(عثمان بن زائدة); ALUSI-FULL(النووي); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 7:204/q16/p3 One may listen to a recording respectfully, but it does not impose the recitation-specific duty. — Elmalılı calls gramophone or radio replay a trace, not fresh human recitation; listening is permissible and preferable, disrespect is forbidden, and a recording should be removed from unworthy places. He says Qur'an must not be used merely as an instrument. [3 notes: ELMALILI]
- 7:204/q16/p4 Reciting while reclining or otherwise occupied is allowed if attention and dignity remain. — Ālūsī cites 3:191 for remembrance standing, seated or lying; he permits work and walking with an attentive heart, but dislikes distraction. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:204/q17 What inward hearing do the commands invite?  [فَٱسْتَمِعُوا۟ لَهُۥ وَأَنصِتُوا۟ · inward]
turns on: Whether inward attention is described as disciplined reception or as divine audition.
- 7:204/q17/p1 Listen outwardly so the heart understands God's address, silencing self-talk and seeking no private gain. — Sulamī stresses etiquette and lessons; Qāshānī says hear God alone as speaker and quiet all besides Him. Ālūsī speaks of outer ears and inward senses. STUDYQURAN describes pure servitude and entry into the Speaker's presence. [7 notes: SULAMI(قيل); QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 7:204/q17/p2 Outer silence opens inner hearing in which God becomes the servant's hearing. — The Najmī interpretation moves from tongue and ears to their inward counterparts, cites 55:1–2, and describes divine audition through the Creator. Jāmī's bat and sunlight couplet warns against taking only letters from the Qur'an; Suhrawardī says synchronized tongue and heart recitation displaces inner speech. [5 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,المولى الجامي); ALUSI-FULL(السهروردي)]

## 7:204/q18 What does لَعَلَّكُمْ express?  [لَعَلَّكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether laʿalla marks the hearers' expectation or the command's purpose.
- 7:204/q18/p1 It expresses hope or expectation of receiving mercy. — Jishumī gives hope as a sense; Ibn ʿAṭiyya locates the hope with human hearers; Abū Ḥayyān permits hope for each audience. [3 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 7:204/q18/p2 It can express purpose, 'so that' mercy may be received. — Jishumī equates this use with kay; Abū Ḥayyān also allows purpose. [2 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 7:204/q19 What mercy is hoped for, and how does listening bring it?  [لَعَلَّكُمْ تُرْحَمُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the hoped-for fruit is understanding and action, conversion and reward, or inward encounter with the Speaker.
- 7:204/q19/p1 Understanding admonition and acting on the Qur'an lead to divine mercy. — Al-Ṭabarī identifies examples and prescribed duties; Ṭabrisī names exhortations. Biqāʿī and Elmalılı link reflection to action, faith, insight and blessing; ISLAHI and KURANYOLU extend its fruit to corrected life and the hereafter. STUDYQURAN connects this to 7:203 and reports Ibn ʿAjībah that mercy is nearest the listener. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; SULAMI(قيل); TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAjībah)]
- 7:204/q19/p2 The mercy can be a felt gift of God's presence through truly hearing His speech. — Qāshānī and the Najmī inward reading describe the Speaker manifesting attributes and acts and hearing through God; the latter links 55:1–2. Sulamī calls outward and inward hearing an etiquette of servanthood. [7 notes: SULAMI(قيل); QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 7:204/q19/p3 For disbelievers, listening may lead to faith and thus mercy; believers receive reward for listening and obedience. — Rāzī sees conditional mercy as especially fitting those not yet believing, because 7:203 already names Qur'an mercy for believers. Abū Ḥayyān assigns each audience its suitable mercy. Ālūsī says believers may still hope for another mercy, even if already promised one in 45:20. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL+]

## 7:204/q20 How is رَحْمَة written or construed in relation to this verse?  [تُرْحَمُونَ · grammar]
- 7:204/q20/p1 The Qur'anic word for mercy appears with extended tāʾ in seven other places; nearby agreement and 'traces' can mark an active effect. — Al-Zarkashī in al-Burhān notes the orthographic set and cites 7:56 and 30:50 alongside 7:204 as illustrations of mercy's grammatical and semantic effect. [1 notes: BURHAN]

- 7:204/q22/p2 It answers opponents' call to drown out the Qur'an, as in 41:26. — Rāzī and Ibn ʿĀshūr connect Meccan opposition to the command; Ibn Kathīr, ISLAHI and KURANYOLU say respectful listening reverses the condemned noisy response. ʿAbd al-Jabbār also compares the jinn's hearing in 46:29. - 7:204/q22/p4 The sūra closes by returning to the revealed Book announced at its opening. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī notes the opening Book and closing listening command; STUDYQURAN describes the sūra's ending devotional practices, including invoking God's names and listening. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:204/q23 What reward or healing is associated with hearing or reciting Qur'an?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports attach either eschatological reward or healing to Qur'anic hearing and recitation.
- 7:204/q23/p1 Listening to one verse earns multiplied good, and reciting it is light on Resurrection Day. — Abū Hurayra attributes this to the Prophet; Ibn Kathīr says Aḥmad alone transmits it, while al-Durr grades it ḥasan. Versions say 'multiplied' or 'doubled'. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 7:204/q23/p2 The Qur'an is a cure, and recitation can relieve illness. — ITQAN cites Ibn Masʿūd's 'honey and Qur'an are two cures' from Ibn Mājah and others, ʿAlī's 'best medicine,' Ṭalḥa's report of relief for a sick hearer, and Wāthila's report of the Prophet prescribing recitation for a sore throat. Al-Suyūṭī says books on Qur'anic properties mix hadith and pious experience. [5 notes: ITQAN(ابن مسعود,طلحة بن مصرف,علي,واثلة بن الأسقع)]
