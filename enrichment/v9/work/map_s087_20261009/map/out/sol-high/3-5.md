# 3:5: 25 questions

## 3:5/q01 How is the assertion with inna structured?  [إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يَخْفَىٰ عَلَيْهِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clauses continue inna's predication or stand independently.
- 3:5/q01/p1 The negative clause is inna's predicate, and repeated predication strengthens the assertion. — Al-Ālūsī identifies the negative clause as the predicate and notes the emphasis of reiterating the predication. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 3:5/q01/p2 The clauses can be read as independent statements of knowledge, power and will, and exclusive divinity. — Abū Ḥayyān prefers independent clauses but allows them to predicate the preceding inna; he assigns complete knowledge to the first, power and will to the second, and exclusive divinity to the third. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]

## 3:5/q02 What does the construction of shayʾ under negation express?  [شَىْءٌۭ · grammar]
- 3:5/q02/p1 The indefinite 'thing' under negation covers every thing without exception. — Abū Ḥayyān expressly derives universality from indefiniteness under negation, including particulars and universals. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kur’an Yolu call shayʾ comprehensive, encompassing all beings and events. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 3:5/q03 What does it mean that nothing is hidden from God?  [لَا يَخْفَىٰ عَلَيْهِ شَىْءٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether a particular Christian occasion limits the verse's unrestricted wording.
- 3:5/q03/p1 God knows all things and states, including inward and outward, past, future, possible, particular and universal matters. — Muqātil and Ibn Kathīr include heaven's and earth's inhabitants and their unseen. Al-Zajjāj adds that God made all things. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, Qāshānī, Bursawī and al-Ālūsī include faith and unbelief. Al-Qurṭubī includes what was, will be and would not be. Al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān and Elmalılı include universals and particulars; Elmalılı adds material and spiritual, inward and outward. Al-Rāzī explicates the negation literally; Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes sensed and unsensed matters. Ibn Isḥāq and Ibn Jaʿfar apply it to intentions, plotting and claims about Jesus; Abū Ḥayyān says such cases fall within the general wording. Al-Rāghib's verbal comparison belongs to the wording question. [19 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TAB-FULL(محمد بن جعفر بن الزبير); IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); DURR-FULL(محمد بن جعفر بن الزبير); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الماتريدي)+]
- 3:5/q03/p2 Its immediate target is what the Christians say about Jesus and what they conceal. — Abū Ḥayyān records a narrower reading focused on speech about Jesus; Ibn Isḥāq and Ibn Jaʿfar report the claims and schemes, while Abū Sulaymān al-Dimashqī identifies concealed Najrān plotting against the Prophet. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); TAB-FULL(محمد بن جعفر بن الزبير); DURR-FULL(محمد بن جعفر بن الزبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي)]

## 3:5/q04 Why say that nothing is hidden rather than simply that God knows?  [لَا يَخْفَىٰ عَلَيْهِ · rhetoric]
- 3:5/q04/p1 The negative form conveys emphatic, exhaustive knowledge in every knowable respect. — Al-Ṭabrisī says this wording covers every knowable aspect. Al-Rāghib calls it originally more emphatic than 'He knows,' though equivalent in ordinary use; al-Ālūsī also stresses its greater force. [3 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL]

## 3:5/q05 How is God's knowledge characterized?  [لَا يَخْفَىٰ عَلَيْهِ شَىْءٌۭ · theology]
- 3:5/q05/p1 It is intrinsic, eternal and prior to events, unlike acquired human knowledge. — Al-Ṭabrisī says God knows by His essence, with no knowable respect hidden. Elmalılı says existence in any state is identical with divine knowledge; God's eternal knowledge is the basis of existence, whereas human knowledge is acquired and representational. Presence and absence are relative to creatures; God creates knowers, known things, faculties, wills and acts. He knows hidden shirk before it occurs. [6 notes: TABRISI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi)]
- 3:5/q05/p2 Apparent texts about God not knowing or knowing later concern nonoccurrence or manifestation, not an absence of omniscience. — Al-Zarkashī says 'He does not know' can mean He knows an alleged event will not occur; 'until We know' the patient strivers is therefore compatible with 3:5. Al-Ṣayrafī defines genuine contradiction as opposition in the same respect, name, act, time and reality; abrogation across times is no contradiction. [3 notes: BURHAN(أبو بكر الصيرفي)]

## 3:5/q06 What rational evidence supports God's comprehensive knowledge?  [لَا يَخْفَىٰ عَلَيْهِ شَىْءٌۭ · theology]
- 3:5/q06/p1 The ordered formation of bodies in wombs demonstrates the maker's knowledge and power. — Al-Rāzī argues that testimony cannot establish God's knowledge before its truth is validated; reason must do so. Intricate, harmonious structures from a drop evidence knowledge and power to make every possible form, confirming His care over creation. Elmalılı also draws complete knowledge, power and freedom of will from human formation. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 3:5/q07 How does the verse explain God as Living?  [لَا يَخْفَىٰ عَلَيْهِ شَىْءٌۭ · links]
- 3:5/q07/p1 Complete knowledge demonstrates the perfection of God's life. — Al-Bayḍāwī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kur’an Yolu connect omniscience with al-Ḥayy; Kur’an Yolu says comprehensive knowledge requires everlasting life. Al-Ālūsī likewise says the knowledge reinforces this name. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); ALUSI-FULL]

## 3:5/q08 How does the verse explain God as Self-Sustaining?  [لَا يَخْفَىٰ عَلَيْهِ شَىْءٌۭ · links]
- 3:5/q08/p1 Sustaining creatures requires full knowledge of their needs and the power to meet them. — Al-Rāzī pairs 3:5's knowledge with 3:6's power as prerequisites for al-Qayyūm. Kur’an Yolu says creating, supervising and sustaining all beings requires knowing every need in kind and degree. Abū Ḥayyān links the womb's fashioning and divine care; al-Qushayrī adds God's governance of every attribute, breath and atom, and His meeting the needs of those who turn to Him. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QUSHAYRI]

## 3:5/q09 How does this knowledge bear on recompense and conduct?  [لَا يَخْفَىٰ عَلَيْهِ شَىْءٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the warning is taken universally or concentrated on Najrān.
- 3:5/q09/p1 God knows faith, disbelief and hidden acts, so people will answer for them. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, Qāshānī and Bursawī connect knowledge of belief and unbelief with requital. Al-Ṭabrisī stresses secret sins before the Judge; Abū Ḥayyān reports a warning against private and public disobedience and al-Māturīdī's inference from hidden matters to visible deeds. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says complete knowledge makes the preceding description of God as mighty and avenging effective: no denial can escape punishment. Al-Ālūsī calls it a reinforced warning. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,الماتريدي); TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 3:5/q09/p2 It specifically warns the Christians of Najrān about their claims and concealed plots. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, citing Ibn Jaʿfar ibn al-Zubayr and al-Rabīʿ, says the opening declaration warns Najrān. Abū Sulaymān al-Dimashqī sees an allusion to their plotting; Ibn Isḥāq and Ibn Jaʿfar describe their knowingly false claim that Jesus was Lord and God. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(محمد بن جعفر بن الزبير والربيع); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي); IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); TAB-FULL(محمد بن جعفر بن الزبير); DURR-FULL(محمد بن جعفر بن الزبير)]

## 3:5/q10 What does in the earth and in the heaven refer to?  [فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَلَا فِى ٱلسَّمَآءِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the locations name the entire cosmos or serve as figures for outward and inward acts.
- 3:5/q10/p1 Earth and heaven together denote the whole universe and all its locations. — Al-Zamakhsharī takes them for the whole domain, and al-Bayḍāwī says they bound human perception. Abū Ḥayyān calls them its greatest visible parts and adds that they are large creatures, dwellings of rational beings and sources of benefits. Al-Ālūsī calls them the whole universe. Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies earth as the globe including seas and heaven as the genus of distant celestial worlds; Kur’an Yolu similarly names the globe and realms beyond it. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 3:5/q10/p2 They can point to outward deeds of bodily members and inward acts hidden in hearts. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī offers this as an alternative, citing 2:284. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 3:5/q11 What figure of speech uses earth and heaven for the whole?  [ٱلْأَرْضِ وَلَا فِى ٱلسَّمَآءِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether 'all' has genuine composition and can perish with a part.
- 3:5/q11/p1 It is a metaphor naming parts while intending the whole. — Al-Ālūsī reports that many describe the paired terms this way. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الكثير)]
- 3:5/q11/p2 It is metonymy rather than metaphor. — Al-Ālūsī reports this classification from those who require a genuinely composite whole, diminished by losing a part, before calling this a whole-part relation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(من قال)]

## 3:5/q12 Why specify earth and heaven after denying all concealment?  [فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَلَا فِى ٱلسَّمَآءِ · rhetoric]
- 3:5/q12/p1 The visible locations make the unlimited claim concrete and intensify its warning. — Al-Ṭabrisī says naming both locations makes hidden deeds in either vivid and weighty. Al-Biqāʿī says the pair instructs by making boundless knowledge concrete and denies divine knowledge to Jesus. Abū Ḥayyān notes their size, inhabited character and benefits; al-Bayḍāwī says they mark the bounds of human perception. [4 notes: TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 3:5/q13 Why is earth named before heaven?  [ٱلْأَرْضِ وَلَا فِى ٱلسَّمَآءِ · rhetoric]
- 3:5/q13/p1 The order begins with familiar earthly affairs and ascends to the more remote heaven, focusing the moral warning on people. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī describe an ascent from lower to higher; both stress earthly conduct. Abū Ḥayyān says the order arouses shame over hidden wrongdoing despite heaven's greater signs. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kur’an Yolu move from nearby, partly known earthly things to distant, barely known heavens. Al-Ālūsī also reports an explanation involving the placement of the negation particle. Al-Zarkashī cites the same earth-then-heaven order. [7 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BURHAN]

## 3:5/q14 What does the locative phrase 'in the earth' modify?  [فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the locative qualifies the verb or an implicit adjective of 'thing'.
- 3:5/q14/p1 It attaches directly to the verb 'is hidden'. — Al-Samīn reports this parse; Abū al-Baqāʾ, reported by al-Ālūsī, also allows it. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]
- 3:5/q14/p2 It modifies an omitted adjective of 'thing'. — Al-Samīn gives this alternative. Al-Ālūsī holds it and says negation universalizes the phrase regardless of the kind of containment. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 3:5/q15 What do the opening verses report about the Najrān delegation?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the occasion of revelation restricts a general statement.
- 3:5/q15/p1 The opening eighty-odd verses addressed Najrān's Christian delegation after their encounter with the Prophet. — Al-Kalbī, Ibn Isḥāq and al-Rabīʿ describe sixty delegates with fourteen notables, including ʿĀqib ʿAbd al-Masīḥ, Sayyid al-Ayham and Bishop Abū Ḥāritha. They prayed eastward in the mosque with the Prophet's permission; his arguments that Jesus dies, depends on God and knows only what is taught silenced them before revelation. Ibn ʿAbbās identifies those rejecting God's signs as the delegation rejecting the Qur'an and Muhammad. [3 notes: TABRISI(الكلبي ومحمد بن إسحاق والربيع بن أنس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس)]
- 3:5/q15/p2 A specific occasion restricts the preceding general statement to Christians. — Al-Rāzī reports this view from some commentators. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعض المفسرين)]
- 3:5/q15/p3 The general wording covers everyone who turns away from God's signs despite a specific occasion. — Al-Rāzī prefers this judgment of the more rigorous exegetes: the occasion does not narrow the language. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(المحققون من المفسرين)+]

## 3:5/q16 Does Jesus' knowledge of hidden things establish divinity?  [لَا يَخْفَىٰ عَلَيْهِ شَىْءٌۭ · theology]
turns on: Whether knowing some unseen events entails unlimited intrinsic knowledge and independent power.
- 3:5/q16/p1 Christians argued that Jesus' disclosures of unseen private events showed divine knowledge. — Al-Rāzī reports their claim and their additional appeal to reviving the dead, healing the blind and leprous, and making a bird from clay by God's permission. Kur’an Yolu, reporting al-Rāzī, describes reports about what people did at home and their observational and logical case. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(النصارى); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]
- 3:5/q16/p2 Partial unseen knowledge cannot prove divinity; Jesus lacked omniscience and received any disclosures from God. — Al-Rāzī says God could inform Jesus by revelation; a god must know all its creation, and ignorance of even some unseen matters disproves divinity. He argues Jesus would have foreseen his captors and fled if omniscient. Al-Qurṭubī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī and Kur’an Yolu agree that finite knowledge defeats the claim. Abū Ḥayyān cites 3:49 and says healing, creation and revival were divinely empowered miracles, not independent power. Al-Biqāʿī cites Gospel reports in Mark and Luke where Jesus asks who touched him; Kur’an Yolu reports the Christians' account of his fear of death. Elmalılı says prophets' unseen reports are taught by God. [17 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)+; QURTUBI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]

## 3:5/q17 How does the verse connect with nearby descriptions of God and Scripture?  [* · links]
- 3:5/q17/p1 It resumes divine power and might, explains the Living and Self-Sustaining names, and supports the warning and guidance. — Al-Ālūsī sees a resumption of power and might. Al-Biqāʿī recalls the Throne Verse (2:255): knowledge demonstrates qayyūmiyya, answers Jesus's claim and confirms that books guide because God knows the obedient and disobedient. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says omniscience explains the preceding 'mighty, avenging' description. Kur’an Yolu reports commentators chiefly connect 3:5–6 to al-Ḥayy and al-Qayyūm at 3:2 and Christianity's creed. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 3:5/q17/p2 It recalls al-Baqara's account of people's deeds and is paralleled by other omniscience verses. — Ibn al-Zubayr asks how actions described in al-Baqara could be hidden from the One who formed people in wombs. The Study Quran cross-references 14:38 and 27:75. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(ابن الزبير); STUDYQURAN]

## 3:5/q18 What inward response follows from God's seeing everything?  [لَا يَخْفَىٰ عَلَيْهِ شَىْءٌۭ · inward]
- 3:5/q18/p1 Purify hidden thoughts of desires and doubts. — Al-Sulamī transmits this counsel on the ground that nothing inward is concealed from God. [1 notes: SULAMI(قيل)]
- 3:5/q18/p2 Allow only God to appear in the heart lest He be displeased. — Al-Sulamī transmits Jaʿfar's counsel, likewise grounded in God's knowledge of the heart. [1 notes: SULAMI(جعفر)]
- 3:5/q18/p3 Turn to God with needs and in crises, trusting His sufficiency. — Al-Qushayrī says God counts every breath, originates every atom and governs attributes; in particular He grants the needs brought to Him and suffices those who turn to Him. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 3:5/q19 What were early authorities' approaches to interpreting the Qur'an?  [* · reports]
- 3:5/q19/p1 Several early authorities cautioned against speaking without transmitted authority. — Jundub rebuked Ṭalq's verse question. Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib answered legal questions but stayed silent about interpretation and referred a questioner to ʿIkrima, who claimed broad knowledge. Al-Shaʿbī said interpretation is a transmitted report from God, despite having asked about every verse, and said he would not speak about the Qur'an, spirit or opinion. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(الشعبي,جندب بن عبد الله,سعيد بن المسيب,يزيد بن أبي يزيد)]
- 3:5/q19/p2 God alone knows some interpretations; the Prophet learned and explained others by revelation. — Al-Ṭabarī says God taught the Prophet through Gabriel or another messenger what people needed; he rejects taking a report of 'few verses' to mean the Prophet explained only a tiny portion, instead limiting it to verses requiring revealed explanation. [2 notes: TAB-FULL+]

## 3:5/q20 What nominal predication principles do the grammatical notes state?  [* · grammar]
- 3:5/q20/p1 A nominal subject and its predicate are nominative, and the predicate identifies or locates the subject. — Sībawayh defines mubtadaʾ as the noun with which speech begins and on which it is built; its predicate may identify it or locate it in place or time. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 3:5/q20/p2 A fronted adjectival predicate is sound when intended as predicate; verb-like use of a noun needs its licensing noun. — Al-Khalīl disfavors قائم زيد unless the fronted adjective is deliberately predicative. Sībawayh accepts deliberate fronting, but says treating it as a verb-like assertion is disfavored; a participial noun acts like a verb only with an attached described or other licensing noun. [3 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]

## 3:5/q21 What number and plural usages do the grammatical notes illustrate?  [* · grammar]
- 3:5/q21/p1 Arabic can use plural forms for two paired persons or things. — Al-Khalīl treats dual as a kind of plural; Yūnus cites plural verb forms for two owners and paired persons, including Ruʾbah. Sībawayh cites Ruʾbah's ظهراهما and poetry for distinct paired things. [3 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل,يونس)]
- 3:5/q21/p2 Broken plurals can mark abundance or emphasis beyond simpler plurals. — Sībawayh contrasts emphatic أقاويل with أقوال and أبيات, which suffice without the extra force. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 3:5/q21/p3 Dual nouns can designate two distinct kinds or two herds. — Sībawayh gives عقلان and تمران for two kinds, إبلان for two herds, and لقاحان سوداوان as a paired dual. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 3:5/q21/p4 A numeral with a plural noun can be licensed by analogy or a partitive sense. — Al-Khalīl allows poetic ثلاثة كلاب by analogy to ثلاثة قرود or as 'three of the dogs'; the examples illustrate numeral constructions. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]

## 3:5/q22 What does the cited account say about the Qur'an's arrangement and collection?  [* · sciences]
- 3:5/q22/p1 The Prophet directed verse placement; the final codex was gathered after recitational abrogation ended. — ʿUthmān says Anfāl was early Medinan, Barāʾa late; their narratives resembled one another, and no final instruction on joining them survived, so he put them together without a basmala. Al-Zarkashī infers arrangement began during the Prophet's life, while a single codex awaited the end of possible abrogation; God preserved it in hearts and guided the caliphs' compilation. [3 notes: BURHAN(عثمان)]

## 3:5/q23 How do these notes define true happiness and misery?  [* · interpretation]
- 3:5/q23/p1 True happiness is spiritual wellbeing rooted in turning to God, with bodily wellbeing when it does not distract. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes an immortal spirit joined to a changing body and returning to God, citing 3:196–197. Loss of bodily wellbeing for God can be spiritual happiness; bodily pleasure alone is brief. Training reliance on God, owner of all, exposes worldly attachment as false happiness, citing 2:284. [3 notes: TABATABAI]
- 3:5/q23/p2 Happiness and misery require awareness and vary with a person's spiritual formation. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says unperceived pleasure or pain is not happiness or misery. Those shaped by the Qur'an perceive and think differently, with less fully trained believers at intermediate degrees; unbelievers' pleasures remain clouded by grief and worry. [3 notes: TABATABAI]
- 3:5/q23/p3 Bodily suffering can be called punishment without being spiritual misery or a direct divine act. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites Job's complaint (38:41) for bodily punishment and Pharaoh's killing sons and sparing women (7:141) for punishment and trial from God even though the perpetrators carried it out. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 3:5/q24 What does 'intiqām' mean in the surrounding passage?  [* · meaning]
- 3:5/q24/p1 It means an intensification of punishment. — Ibn al-Jawzī gives this gloss. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 3:5/q25 What does reflection on human formation show about God's will?  [* · theology]
- 3:5/q25/p1 God is a free agent, subject to no external necessity. — Elmalılı draws complete power and will from human formation and says God is not subject to necessity; Kur’an Yolu reports his view that all beings and events depend on God's will. [2 notes: ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi)]
