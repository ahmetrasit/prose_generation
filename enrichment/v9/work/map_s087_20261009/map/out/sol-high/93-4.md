# 93:4: 16 questions

## 93:4/q01 What do al-ākhirah and al-ūlā denote here?  [ٱلْءَاخِرَةُ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether the paired nouns name the two abodes, phases of the mission, an encompassing sequence of later goods, or an inward progression; the notes appeal to common Quranic usage, the sūra's context, the definite articles, and the conquest report.
- 93:4/q01/p1 The Hereafter and this worldly life: what awaits the Prophet with God exceeds his present worldly portion. — Al-Ṭabarī says this includes all God prepared for Muhammad and relieves grief over what passed. ʿAṭāʾ and Muqātil specify Paradise. Ibn Isḥāq compares the Prophet's return to God with prompt worldly honors; Abū Isḥāq calls this the apparent transmitted sense. Al-Ālūsī cites Ibn ʿAbbās's conquest report as support. Corpus Coranicum cites early Meccan contrasts and 87:17, 92:13, 79:25, 53:25, 87:16, 79:38, 53:29; Study Quran adduces 16:30, 16:41, 28:60, 28:79-80, 29:64, 40:39, 42:36, 43:35, 57:20-21, 87:16-17, 13:26. Elmalılı accepts this contextually if worldly life remains blessed until death; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the pair usually names the two lives, citing 53:25, 79:25, 28:70, 92:13, while the context focuses on Muhammad's future. Corpus Coranicum notes a Jewish-Christian parallel and pre-Quranic poetry's use of dunyā. [27 notes: TAB; THALABI; MAWARDI(ابن شجرة,يحيى بن سلام); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة,يحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء); WAHIDI-QT; IBNATIYYA(ابن إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن إسحاق وغيره); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطاء,غيره); QURTUBI(ابن إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن إسحاق); ABUHAYYAN(ابن إسحاق وغيره); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن إسحاق); ALUSI(أبو إسحاق وغيره); ALUSI-FULL(أبو إسحاق); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İkinci yorum); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(بعض المفسرين); STUDYQURAN; CORPUSCORANICUM; BINTSHATI]
- 93:4/q01/p2 The later and earlier phases of the Prophet's earthly life or mission: its end brings rising strength, victory, and rank. — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah says the period after this sūra brings victory and prominence, and verse 5 can be read similarly. Rāzī proposes daily growth in honor. Al-Ālūsī reports that the definite articles may refer back or substitute for possessive forms. ʿAbduh expects renewed revelation to complete religion and blessing. Iṣlāḥī specifies Islam's supremacy, Mecca's conquest, enemy humiliation and mass conversion. Kuran Yolu prefers this in light of opponents' expectation that the mission would fail. [19 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); ALUSI(ابن عطية وجماعة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو إسحاق); ELMALILI(ابن عطية، müfessirîn); BINTSHATI(الرازي,الشيخ محمد عبده); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Birinci yorum); IBNASHUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 93:4/q01/p3 Every later good for the Prophet surpasses an earlier one, through his remaining earthly life and into the Hereafter. — Al-Biqāʿī speaks of unending ascent from one perfection to another. Elmalılı prefers this breadth because limiting the promise to death would omit later worldly good; he invokes 5:3. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the definite nouns generically and includes continued revelation and a better return after its pause. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ favors the hoped-for future; 38:7 shows ākhirah can have a contextual sense other than the next life. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says saying 'than the world' would foreclose better earthly developments. Ibn ʿĀshūr also allows the familiar two-life reading within this comprehensive promise. [19 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(بعض المفسرين); BINTSHATI+; ELMALILI+]
- 93:4/q01/p4 In an inward reading, the later state is disclosure after concealment and longing, free of remaining selfhood. — Al-Qāshānī describes disclosure after intensified longing and security from selfhood's residual variegation. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 93:4/q02 Why is the later state better for the Prophet?  [ٱلْءَاخِرَةُ خَيْرٌۭ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the superior later good is principally the reward and honors of the next life or the growing success of the Prophet's earthly mission.
- 93:4/q02/p1 The Hereafter's lasting, pure gifts and the Prophet's honors there exceed the mixed, passing benefits and honors of this world. — Sahl/Tustarī names the greater stored station of praise and intercession than earthly prophethood and messengerhood; another Sulamī report adds the obeyed intercessor licensed to speak when others cannot. Al-Qushayrī lists intercession, the Basin and robe of unity. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān reporting him, and al-Ālūsī list precedence over prophets, the community's testimony, elevated believer ranks through intercession, the praised station and Pool. Rāzī adds God's acting as He wills for him, reunion with his community as children with a father (33:6, 25:74), his possession of the Hereafter, and the change from disbelievers' taunts to witness for nations and prophets with God's witness for him (48:28-29). Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī contrast few, tainted, fleeting worldly pleasures with abundant, pure, lasting afterlife pleasures; al-Ālūsī notes even worldly prophetic distinction involves administrative burdens. Al-Biqāʿī cites the unimaginable lot of even Paradise's lowest entrant. Ibn Kathīr points to the Prophet's asceticism and choice of God's company; Qutb describes uninterrupted divine favor in both lives. Ibn Isḥāq also describes victory now and reward later. Study Quran cites 2:86 and 4:74 against exchanging the next life for this one. [37 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(بعضهم,سهل); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-QT; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(ابن إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن إسحاق); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR; BIQAI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN]
- 93:4/q02/p2 The Prophet's later mission will have greater success, with victory, a completed religion and expanding community. — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah and al-Ṭabrisī anticipate worldly victory. ʿAbduh expects renewed revelation to perfect religion and blessing. Iṣlāḥī specifies Mecca's conquest, Islam's supremacy, enemy humiliation and mass conversion. Kuran Yolu sees a reply to opponents' claim that his work would fail; Elmalılı says his later worldly life remained productive. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI(الشيخ محمد عبده); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR; ELMALILI+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]

## 93:4/q03 What does the comparative khayr imply about the first life?  [خَيْرٌۭ · meaning]
- 93:4/q03/p1 The first life has good in it, although the later good is greater. — Elmalılı infers this from the comparative itself and says the broader later-stage reading adds continual growth in good. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 93:4/q04 Why is the form khayr used?  [خَيْرٌۭ · grammar]
- 93:4/q04/p1 Frequent use shortened akhayr to khayr. — Al-Naḥḥās gives frequency of usage as the cause. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 93:4/q05 How do the lām and sentence opening function?  [وَلَلْءَاخِرَةُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the lām is an oath answer, an initial emphasis particle, or part of an independently coordinated assertion; the disputed grammatical evidence is the emphatic nūn with sawfa.
- 93:4/q05/p1 The lām continues the opening oath's answer, alongside the denial of abandonment and the next promise. — Al-Samīn counts four sworn assertions. He says omission of an emphatic nūn is allowed when sawfa or an object intervenes and cites 3:158; Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī calls it the oath-lām and says sawfa replaces one nūn. Al-Samīn says even a proposed hidden subject would not bar the oath-answer function; the interlocutor also allows both lāms as oath-lāms. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو علي الفارسي,الشيخ); SAMIN-DURR+]
- 93:4/q05/p2 The lām is an emphatic initial lām, with an omitted subject supplied in the following clause. — Al-Zamakhsharī supplies 'you' before sawfa because an oath-lām before a present verb normally takes an emphatic nūn. Al-Samīn disputes that the missing nūn rules out an oath answer; his interlocutor first identifies the lām as an initial emphatic one. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Zamakhsharī's reading of both particles as lām al-ibtidāʾ. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,الشيخ); IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR-]
- 93:4/q05/p3 The clause independently coordinates with the opening oath clauses and adds glad tidings after the denial. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says it is not the oath answer, and its generic definite nouns make a comprehensive closing assertion. Al-Ālūsī objects to a proposed circumstantial connection partly because of the paired lāms and oath. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 93:4/q06 What does the definite form of the two nouns contribute?  [ٱلْءَاخِرَةُ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the definite nouns generalize every later and earlier good or specify the Prophet's known earlier and later mission.
- 93:4/q06/p1 It makes the promise generic: each later or deferred good exceeds its earlier counterpart. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the general forms include continued revelation and every other good in both lives. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 93:4/q06/p2 On the mission-stage reading, the articles refer back to known phases or replace possessives. — Al-Ālūsī reports this from Ibn ʿAṭiyyah and others as a grammatical support for taking the beginning and end of the Prophet's mission. [1 notes: ALUSI(ابن عطية وجماعة)]

## 93:4/q07 Whom does 'for you' cover, and why is it singled out?  [لَّكَ · referent]
turns on: How far the second-person lām extends beyond the Prophet personally, given that some people do not fare better in the Hereafter.
- 93:4/q07/p1 The promise is addressed specifically to the Prophet, because it cannot truthfully be said of every companion or person. — Rāzī says some companions fare badly in the next life; specifying the obedient would expose sinners and hypocrites. Al-Biqāʿī likewise says upward progression is not everyone's lot. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says this sūra and al-Sharḥ address the Prophet (94:1-8). [4 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 93:4/q07/p2 It contrasts the Prophet with hostile mockers, while the saved and his believing followers share afterlife good. — Al-Ālūsī reports the first interpretation but argues exclusivity need not exclude all the saved: the Prophet's degree is higher and his praised station unique. Elmalılı says unbelievers also benefit from his worldly good and cites 14:36. [4 notes: ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 93:4/q07/p3 It extends to what is connected with the Prophet: his person, religion and community. — Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the lām as particularization and includes Islam's spread and the community's hoped-for good. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]

## 93:4/q08 Why say 'the first' rather than 'the world'?  [ٱلْأُولَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 93:4/q08/p1 The wording leaves room for the Prophet's later earthly life to improve as well as for the next life to be better. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says an explicit 'than the world' would limit the promise and wrongly deny later worldly good. Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise reads every later matter as better than its immediate predecessor. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNASHUR]

## 93:4/q09 How does the promise relate to the preceding denial of abandonment?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the verse develops the prior honor, explains a revelation pause as approaching death, promises success after present hardship, or gives a circumstantial rationale for non-abandonment.
- 93:4/q09/p1 After reassuring the Prophet of continued revelation and love, God promises a still greater future honor. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Ālūsī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ give this ascent; the preceding denial itself confers honor (93:3). Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says verse 5 completes the reassurance. Al-Ālūsī prefers this link to the death proposal; al-Bayḍāwī adds the triumph and elevation of religion among the gifts. [19 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI(الرازي); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ALUSI+; ALUSI-FULL(عصام الدين)-]
- 93:4/q09/p2 A pause in revelation could signal completed mission and imminent death, whose afterlife reward is better. — Al-Rāzī distinguishes this from removal from prophethood. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports him. Al-Ālūsī calls the proposal much weaker, preferring an independent continuation of reassurance; Elmalılı says immediate death would miss the good of later earthly life (5:3). [4 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI(قيل); BINTSHATI(الرازي); ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-; ELMALILI-]
- 93:4/q09/p3 The denial answers fears over the present hardship by promising the later success of the mission. — Iṣlāḥī sees the present hardship changing into victory. Kuran Yolu sets the promise against idolaters' claim that revelation had stopped and the mission would fail. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reads it as comfort after the pause. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BINTSHATI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 93:4/q09/p4 The preceding denial is circumstantial: because the Hereafter is better for him, his Lord would not abandon him. — Al-Ālūsī reports ʿIṣām al-Dīn's paraphrase, in which the next promise means God will give him the Hereafter; al-Ālūsī objects that the paired lāms and oath make it unlikely and the paraphrase is strained. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(عصام الدين)]

## 93:4/q10 How does the promise fit the sūra's wider movement?  [* · links]
- 93:4/q10/p1 It moves from reassurance and promised future good to reminders of earlier favors and duties arising from them. — Iṣlāḥī describes day and night as models of developmental trials, then better times, past divine help and obligations. Al-Ṭabrisī links the later reminder of need and gifts to the Giver and future favor. Al-Sāmarrāʾī traces darkness to light through orphanhood and shelter, lostness and guidance, need and wealth. Al-Biqāʿī reports Ibn al-Zubayr's view that a preceding predestination discussion could cause fear, so the sūra reassures God's dearest servant with favors in both lives. [5 notes: TABRISI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير)]

## 93:4/q11 What event is reported to have preceded this verse's revelation?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the reported occasion is a vision of the community's later conquests or the pause in revelation and opponents' taunts; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ disputes specifying the pause's cause or length.
- 93:4/q11/p1 The Prophet was shown his community's future conquests, rejoiced, and the verse was revealed. — Ibn ʿAbbās transmits this in al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Suyūṭī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr; al-Suyūṭī says al-Ṭabarānī's chain is good. Al-Biqāʿī also reports Anas's account of successive lands opening. Elmalılı calls the foreknowledge a miraculous vision and names Persia, Byzantium and Constantinople as later examples. Al-Ālūsī says the report especially supports the two-abode reading. [17 notes: MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(علي بن عبد الله بن عباس); QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI(أنس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); ALUSI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI(ابن عباس، رسول الله); IBNASHUR(ابن عباس)]
- 93:4/q11/p2 The sūra answered polytheists who said God had abandoned the Prophet during a pause in revelation. — Al-Bayḍāwī lists proposed causes: an omitted exception formula like 18:23-24, a rebuked questioner, a dead puppy under the bed, or another reason. Al-Biqāʿī reports the pause and disbelievers' taunt; Kuran Yolu adds their hope that his mission would fail. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ describes the pause as natural, like night after dawn, and says the Quran specifies no cause or duration: proposed causes and twelve-to-forty-day counts do not determine the central meaning. She dates the sūra to early Mecca, long before the Hijra. [5 notes: BAYDAWI; BIQAI-FULL; BINTSHATI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Putperestler); BINTSHATI-]

## 93:4/q12 How many pearl palaces were promised in the reports?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on the number: a thousand versus a million.
- 93:4/q12/p1 A thousand pearl palaces, with spouses and attendants or servants in each. — Al-Wāḥidī adds provisions in each palace. Al-Qurṭubī says the Ibn ʿAbbās report, raised by al-Awzāʿī, describes musk soil and suitable spouses and servants. [3 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن عباس); QURTUBI(ابن عباس)]
- 93:4/q12/p2 A million pearl palaces, with desired spouses and attendants. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports this number from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: TABRISI(ابن عباس)]

## 93:4/q13 What other reports and examples illustrate preferring the Hereafter?  [* · reports]
turns on: The notes supply separate prophetic reports and sayings rather than rival versions of a single event.
- 93:4/q13/p1 God chose the Hereafter over this world for the Prophet's household. — Al-Baghawī transmits Ibn Masʿūd's report of the Prophet's statement. [2 notes: BAGHAWI(عبد الله); BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 93:4/q13/p2 The Prophet compared himself in the world to a rider resting briefly beneath a tree. — Ibn Masʿūd tells of a rough mat marking the Prophet's side; Ibn Kathīr cites al-Tirmidhī's grading of the report as ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR(عبد الله بن مسعود)]
- 93:4/q13/p3 The Prophet chose to be with God rather than remain alive until the world's end before entering Paradise. — Study Quran reports the choice; Ibn Kathīr also cites the Prophet's choosing what is with God near death as evidence of his asceticism. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(Prophet); IBNKATHIR]
- 93:4/q13/p4 Al-ʿAbbās inferred from the verse that the Prophet would remain among them only a short time. — Al-Durr reports ʿIkrimah's account of al-ʿAbbās's reaction when the verse was revealed. [1 notes: DURR(عكرمة)]
- 93:4/q13/p5 Both worlds involve hardship, but the more lasting one is worth seeking. — Al-Sulamī reports Yaḥyā ibn Muʿādh's counsel. [1 notes: SULAMI(يحيى بن معاذ)]
- 93:4/q13/p6 Leaving this world is difficult, but losing the Hereafter is more difficult. — Al-Sulamī reports al-Junayd's comparison. [1 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد)]

## 93:4/q14 What does the promise imply about different people's worldly and final fortunes?  [* · interpretation]
- 93:4/q14/p1 People's outcomes fall into four patterns according to wealth or poverty and obedience or unbelief. — Al-Biqāʿī says obedient wealthy people fare well in both realms, poor unbelievers badly in both, wealthy unbelievers have apparent worldly good but final harm, and poor believers worldly hardship but final good; he illustrates the four with a poem. He also relates belief and disbelief to Paradise and Hell, citing 64:2, 42:7, 92:3, 51:49 and 51:50. [3 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 93:4/q15 What special favor to Muhammad's community is drawn from this comparison?  [* · interpretation]
- 93:4/q15/p1 God's mercy reaches sinners through the obedient, unlike a reported occasion when one sinner kept mercy from Moses's assembly. — Rāzī contrasts Moses's 'with me' (26:62) with Muhammad's 'with us' (9:40), since Abū Bakr was certainly blessed. He recounts a report of three unsuccessful days of drought prayer by Moses and thousands because of one talebearer; after God said that man had died, Moses found seventy biers. Rāzī says God conceals even enemies and invokes the saying about bowing elders to suggest one obedient person can bring mercy to sinners. [6 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 93:4/q16 What interpretive discipline and early assessments of interpreters are recorded with these notes?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes distinguish standards for claims from reported judgments of particular interpreters; the latter judgments concern different people.
- 93:4/q16/p1 Interpretations must be distinguished by what God alone knows, what the Prophet taught by revelation, and what Arabic usage establishes. — Al-Ṭabarī reserves the Hour and future events to God, calls for sound Prophetic transmission through widespread or reliable reports or clear evidence for revealed explanations, and asks for the clearest proof from circulating Arabic poetry or speech for language-based explanations. [3 notes: TAB-FULL]
- 93:4/q16/p2 Early assessments of individual interpreters diverge. — Al-Shaʿbī sharply rebuked al-Suddī and said a drum beating was preferable to his session; Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī said al-Suddī interprets according to people's interpretations. Qatādah said no one kept pace with al-Kalbī in Quran interpretation. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(إبراهيم,الشعبي,قتادة)]
