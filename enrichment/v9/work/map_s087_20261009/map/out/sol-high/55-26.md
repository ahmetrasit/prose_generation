# 55:26: 16 questions

## 55:26/q01 Who is included in كُلُّ مَنْ?  [كُلُّ مَنْ · referent]
turns on: Whether من generically includes non-rational beings by predominance, specifically names the rational addressees, or extends the mortality statement to all creation.
- 55:26/q01/p1 All earthly inhabitants or creatures, including animals and composite beings. — Muqatil, Wahidi-Wajiz, Baghawi, Tabrisi, and Alusi speak of living creatures or animals; Wahidi-Basit says creatures moving over earth. Baydawi allows animals and material beings through grammatical predominance. Samin and Abu Hayyan say the masculine rational form can include non-rational beings because rational beings predominate. Bursevi includes earthly animals and composites; Elmalili includes ship passengers. Study Quran reports earth, its face, or the world generally. [15 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; NASAFI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(most commentators); NAHHAS]
- 55:26/q01/p2 The rational inhabitants, humans and jinn, are specially intended. — Tab-Full identifies both peoples. Baydawi, Bursevi, and Alusi offer them as an alternative to generic inclusion. Tabatabai says the discourse counts favors to humans and jinn in both lives; Razi says rational beings are singled out as the beneficiaries of the warning, although all things perish. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL]
- 55:26/q01/p3 All created beings, including inhabitants of the heavens, perish. — Ibn Kathir excepts whom God wills and says only the ever-living Lord remains. Biqai includes land, sea, and heavens because all besides God is contingent. Asad reports Ibn Kathir as taking the pronoun for the universe and himself says beings of earth and heaven pass away. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Ibn Kathir)]
- 55:26/q01/p4 Humans are the intended subjects of the lesson. — Ibn Ashur says من specifically marks rational beings here. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 55:26/q01/p5 People of sound intellect, free from imaginative defects, are especially indicated. — Bursevi reads the predominance of من this way and says they pass beyond natural conditions into divine manifestations. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 55:26/q02 What does the pronoun in عَلَيْهَا refer to?  [عَلَيْهَا · referent]
turns on: Whether context recalls the earlier earth, supplies the wider world, or points back to the ships; Razi invokes 55:27 for earth.
- 55:26/q02/p1 The earth. — Wahidi-Basit, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, and Elmalili cite its earlier mention at 55:10; Nahhas relates it to the earth God spread out. Tabrisi, Ibn Jawzi, Razi, Burhan, Itqan, Bursevi, Ibn Ashur, and Asad allow context to supply an unnamed earth. Tabrisi compares a Medinan expression about two lava tracts; Razi cites 35:45; Samin reports 38:32 as an analogy but also the objection that 55:10 already names earth; Ibn Ashur likewise cites 38:32. Razi says 55:27 favors earth over ships. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(most commentators); RAZI-FULL+]
- 55:26/q02/p2 The world, inferred from a previously mentioned part. — Burhan attributes this to Ibn Malik; Itqan says context can supply 'earth or world' without a named antecedent and compares the unnamed deceased in an inheritance expression. [3 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN; STUDYQURAN(most commentators)]
- 55:26/q02/p3 The ships just mentioned. — Razi reports that everyone aboard is near destruction and cannot control benefit or harm, while judging the earth reading better because of 55:27. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 55:26/q02/p4 The whole universe. — Asad reports Ibn Kathir's extension to the heavens as implying this antecedent. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Ibn Kathir)]

## 55:26/q03 Why say عَلَيْهَا rather than 'in the earth'?  [عَلَيْهَا · rhetoric]
- 55:26/q03/p1 Being 'upon' it suggests no settled permanence at annihilation. — Burhan explicitly contrasts this wording with 'in the earth.' [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 55:26/q04 How can كُلُّ مَنْ عَلَيْهَا be grammatically construed?  [كُلُّ مَنْ عَلَيْهَا · grammar]
- 55:26/q04/p1 The relative من can be modified by either a single word or a clause. — Burhan gives one reading of the expression as 'every person established upon it.' [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 55:26/q05 In what sense and at what time is each being فَانٍ?  [فَانٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether فَانٍ anticipates death, describes present contingency, or denotes extinction versus transition; Razi disputes an inference about bodily persistence.
- 55:26/q05/p1 They are destined to die or pass away in the future. — Ibn Jawzi glosses the word as doomed to perish. Razi compares the living Prophet called dead at 39:30 and ordinary speech treating the near future as arrived. Ibn Ashur says the active participle has future sense and cites 39:30. Tabatabai says this marks the end of earthly life and onset of the next; Ibn Ashur adds reckoning and recompense. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 55:26/q05/p2 Created beings are already perishing through contingent, changing existence. — Razi says human existence is an accident alternating origination and nonexistence; he rejects the inference that a body cannot persist two moments, since man includes transient accidents even if the body remains. Biqai says every creature lies between nonexistence before creation and after extinction and its states, qualities, and powers are continually made and unmade, with hidden destructive causes like those around ship passengers. Bursevi reports that dependent existence is not truly self-subsisting; real existence belongs to the pre-eternal. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
- 55:26/q05/p3 Perishing is extinction or passage out of existence. — Tabrisi says earthly creatures pass out of existence. Abu Hayyan applies extinction to every created thing, cites 28:88, and says 'face' means reality without bodily limbs. [2 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 55:26/q05/p4 Perishing is departure from this world and return to God, not absolute annihilation. — Tabatabai says many verses explain it as return; the next life begins for earthly inhabitants. [2 notes: TABATABAI]
- 55:26/q05/p5 Bodies themselves cannot persist for two moments. — Razi rejects this inference from the verse: a human includes changing accidents, but the body may remain and is not literally the whole human. [0 notes: RAZI-FULL-]

## 55:26/q06 What accounts for the form فَانٍ?  [فَانٍ · grammar]
- 55:26/q06/p1 An original yāʾ was silenced for heaviness and then deleted before the still tanwīn. — Nahhas gives this morphological sequence. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 55:26/q07 What did the angels infer when this verse was revealed?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reported later verse differs, as does Bursevi's qualification about kinds of angelic spirit.
- 55:26/q07/p1 They thought earth's people had perished, then learned their own mortality from 28:88. — Wahidi-Basit transmits this from al-Kalbi and Muqatil; Qurtubi attributes it to Ibn Abbas and Muqatil. The first report says earth's people; the second says its inhabitants. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي ومقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس ومقاتل)]
- 55:26/q07/p2 They thought Adam's children had perished, then learned their own mortality from 3:185. — Bursevi reports that subtle angelic bodies and attached spirits perish, while lofty immaterial spirits do not. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الملائكة)]

## 55:26/q08 Should recitation stop after the statement of perishing?  [* · readings]
- 55:26/q08/p1 Continue through the statement that the Lord's Face remains in 55:27. — Al-Shaʿbi is reported by Wahidi-Basit, Ibn Kathir, Durr, Itqan, and Nashr; Itqan says Ibn Abi Hatim recorded it. Nashr explains that pauses and resumptions must preserve sense because a whole sura cannot be recited in one breath. It cites Ali's definition of tartil as knowing stops and proper articulation, Ibn Umar's report of Companions learning stops with each sura and the author's inference of consensus; it names early readers and later authorization practice as support. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الشعبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الشعبي); DURR-FULL(الشعبي); ITQAN(الشعبي); NASHR(ابن عمر,الشعبي,علي بن أبي طالب)]

## 55:26/q09 Which sakt and madd combinations does Nashr transmit or rule out?  [* · readings]
turns on: The transmitted reader, hamza environment, and whether madd entails sakt.
- 55:26/q09/p1 Ibn Dhakwan's sakt occurs with either long or medium madd. — Nashr says both combinations are transmitted. [1 notes: NASHR]
- 55:26/q09/p2 For Hafs, sakt requires madd, while qasr occurs only with idraj. — Nashr cites al-Ashnani from Ubayd for the former and al-Fil from Amr for the latter. [1 notes: NASHR]
- 55:26/q09/p3 For Hamza, the options vary by hamza setting and word. — Nashr says easing within one word cancels sakt or tahqiq; across words the separated hamza's easing rules apply. On al-ard and al-insan, naql or sakt occurs; on qad aflaha, man amana, qul uhiya, qalu amanna, fi anfusikum, and ma unzila, sakt, no sakt, and naql occur; ya ayyuha and ha'ula' allow tahqiq or easing without sakt. [2 notes: NASHR]
- 55:26/q09/p4 Hamza's madd must accompany sakt in the relevant settings. — Nashr rejects the apparent Khallad report of madd on shay' without sakt: madd there functions as sakt, whether on the definite article alone or also on separated madd. [1 notes: NASHR+]
- 55:26/q09/p5 Khallad permits madd on shay' without sakt. — Nashr reports this apparent transmission only to disallow it. [0 notes: NASHR-]

## 55:26/q10 What moral response does the verse call for?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The sources draw several compatible exhortations from universal mortality.
- 55:26/q10/p1 Use brief life for worship, obedience, and a life pleasing to God. — Razi urges worship while time remains; Kuranyolu says lasting happiness calls for pleasing God. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 55:26/q10/p2 Do not rely on enduring wealth or worldly prosperity; turn back to God. — Razi warns the prosperous against trusting possessions; the reminder follows enumerated blessings in Kuranyolu. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 55:26/q10/p3 Bear distress patiently because hardship will pass. — Razi warns distressed people against disbelief based on current suffering. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 55:26/q10/p4 Worship and rely on God alone, since rulers and all mortal objects of reliance disappear. — Razi rejects open and hidden idolatry and dependence on kings. He describes a courtier dying before the king as meeting God like a runaway slave, one surviving the king as facing shame and revenge, and both dying as facing a hard meeting with God. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 55:26/q10/p5 Recognize the coming judgment and recompense. — Ibn Ashur says death points beyond itself to reckoning; Islahi infers from created order, provision, divine wisdom, sovereignty, and harmony that creation ends and all are judged without challenging God or interceding without leave. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 55:26/q11 Why is mortality mentioned among divine favors?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the announcement's warning and transition to reward make death a favor, or the refrain attaches elsewhere.
- 55:26/q11/p1 Its warning and consequences are favors. — Baghawi says admonition and restraint deter sin and explain the refrain. Tabrisi says death equalizes creatures, leads to reward, warns of worldly limits, and preserves moral agency because immediate reward would compel obedience and erase merit. Qurtubi separately gives equality and transfer to recompense. Itqan adds relief from a troubled abode and from wicked people for the righteous. Tabatabai calls worldly life preliminary to afterlife and explicitly answers the objection that perishing cannot be a favor. [6 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ITQAN; TABATABAI]
- 55:26/q11/p2 Perishing itself is not a favor, and the refrain is not a tailpiece to this statement. — Ibn Ashur denies both connections. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 55:26/q12 How does this verse follow the ships and earlier blessings?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the immediate transition highlights danger at sea, the scope of earth's inhabitants, or the move from gifts to their end.
- 55:26/q12/p1 The sea exposes dependence on God that people forget on land. — Razi connects 55:24's ships to bodies, souls, and property held in God's grasp at sea; he says earth's heavy sinking in water is more astonishing than light heavens floating. Biqai likens unseen causes of destruction around creatures to danger surrounding ship passengers. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 55:26/q12/p2 Those aboard ships are included with everyone else on earth. — Elmalili explicitly includes ship passengers; Razi reports a ship-specific antecedent reading under the prior verse. [2 notes: ELMALILI; RAZI-FULL]
- 55:26/q12/p3 The passage moves from worldly blessings to mortality and the next life. — Study Quran places earthly blessings in 55:10–25 and blessings of the Hereafter after 55:26–27; Kuranyolu says finitude follows enumerated worldly blessings. Qatada, as reported by Ibn Kathir, says God first told people what He created and then that it would all pass. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 55:26/q13 How is the sura's refrain sequence arranged around this verse?  [* · links]
- 55:26/q13/p1 A central verse of shared mortality divides worldly blessings and warnings from two garden sequences. — An unnamed commentator reported by Burhan counts seven worldly blessings, seven warnings, this central verse, then two groups of eight garden verses, totaling thirty-one; the note cites 55:13, 55:31, 55:46, and 55:62. Study Quran more broadly sees a turn at vv. 26–27 from earthly blessings in vv. 10–25 to the Hereafter. [2 notes: BURHAN(غيره); STUDYQURAN]

## 55:26/q14 What does the contrast with the enduring Lord teach?  [* · theology]
- 55:26/q14/p1 Created things perish while God alone endures. — Tab-Full says His Face remains with majesty and honor; Ibn Kathir says the Lord is ever-living and never dies. Abu Hayyan says Face denotes divine reality, not a bodily limb. Burhan says the Lord's enduring Face does not conflict with earthly mortality. Islahi says all other beings perish; Study Quran cites 28:88 and says divine endurance offsets the passage's dualities. Biqai argues all besides God is contingent, and Islahi connects the created order to final judgment. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 55:26/q15 What rhetorical figure joins this verse to the next?  [* · rhetoric]
- 55:26/q15/p1 Iftinan combines consolation for mortal creatures with praise of God's abiding majesty. — Itqan says the two modes occur in ten words and cites 19:72, which joins gladness and consolation, as another example. [2 notes: ITQAN]

## 55:26/q16 What inward meanings are given to perishing here?  [* · inward]
turns on: The reported inward interpretations apply perishing to desire, spiritual faculties, voluntary transformation, or ontological dependence.
- 55:26/q16/p1 A person between the two ends of annihilation is perishing. — Sulami transmits this from Junayd. [1 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد)]
- 55:26/q16/p2 One who keeps following desire perishes without realizing it. — Sulami transmits this from Ibn Ata. [1 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
- 55:26/q16/p3 Knowing annihilation and abiding means knowing this world's passing and the afterlife's permanence. — Sulami attributes this definition to unnamed authorities. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 55:26/q16/p4 Those aboard sailing vessels reach God by annihilation in Him. — Qashani applies the preceding ship image inwardly. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 55:26/q16/p5 Every faculty and station on the body's earth vanishes upon reaching the goal. — Qashani names spirit, intellect, heart, soul, and their stations and ranks as the particulars. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 55:26/q16/p6 Perishing is natural death in sensual pleasures or voluntary death by shedding human traits. — Bursevi contrasts bodily immersion with taking on spiritual qualities; he also says sound intellect passes from natural conditions into divine manifestations. [2 notes: BURSEVI]
- 55:26/q16/p7 The universe is already perishing to deeper discernment because dependent being lacks self-subsistence. — Bursevi transmits this from unnamed authorities and reserves real existence for the pre-eternal. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
