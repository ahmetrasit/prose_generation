# 76:23: 18 questions

## 76:23/q01 What does the emphatic assertion of divine revelation establish?  [إِنَّا نَحْنُ نَزَّلْنَا · rhetoric]
- 76:23/q01/p1 God Himself and no one else truly sent the Qur’an to Muhammad; the repeated pronoun reinforces that certainty against denial and reassures him. — Kashshāf says the repeated pronoun doubles emphasis on God's exclusive action and assures the Prophet that staged revelation follows wisdom; its commands of patience now and fighting later come from the wise author of every act. Rāzī says the unbelievers' charges of soothsaying and magic made such confirmation and consolation necessary; the Lord of the heavens honors His Messenger despite slander. Qurṭubī denies that Muhammad invented the book and says it is truth rather than magic, soothsaying, or poetry. Bayḍāwī and Nasafī stress the doubled pronoun and God's sole agency; Nasafī links this to purposeful gradual revelation and perseverance. Qāshānī says God Himself, without another. Biqāʿī sees God's limitless greatness in the doubled assertion and says it answers strong rejection while followers await permission to fight. Bursawī cites Meccan charges of sorcery and soothsaying and urges the Prophet not to heed them. Elmalılı also stresses God's sole agency. Ālūsī says emphatic pronouns reinforce the claim of gradual revelation. Sāmarrāʾī counts three markers of emphasis that name sender, message, and recipient: Quraysh denied revelation strongly though they acknowledged God as creator (31:25; 39:38), and the worship-giving book serves creation's purpose (51:56). He compares Yā Sīn's stronger assertion and oath after the rejection of a third messenger (36:14, 36:16). [18 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 76:23/q02 How is نحن parsed in the sentence?  [نَحْنُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether نحن depends on the pronoun of إن, separates subject and predicate, or heads its own clause; Samīn disputes describing a pronoun by a pronoun.
- 76:23/q02/p1 It is an accusative dependent of the pronoun in إن, serving emphasis; Makkī calls it an adjective. — Naḥḥās allows an accusative adjective. Samīn reports emphasis of the noun of إن and reports Makkī's adjective analysis, but objects that one pronoun does not describe another and that Makkī's account amounts to emphasis. [2 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR; SAMIN-DURR(مكي)-]
- 76:23/q02/p2 It is a separating pronoun, while نزلنا supplies the predicate of إن. — Naḥḥās and Samīn both offer this parse; Samīn states that نزلنا itself is the predicate of إن on this analysis. [2 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR]
- 76:23/q02/p3 It is a nominative subject whose predicate is نزلنا, with the resulting clause serving as the predicate of إن. — Naḥḥās gives the subject-and-predicate parse; Samīn specifies that this complete clause predicates إن. [2 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR]

## 76:23/q03 Why say عليك here?  [عَلَيْكَ · rhetoric]
- 76:23/q03/p1 The address to the Prophet suits the commands that follow. — Sāmarrāʾī contrasts al-Ḥijr 15:9, which centers on the Qur’an without a surrounding address to him, with the direct commands in 76:24–26. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 76:23/q04 How is القرآن read with respect to its hamza?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانَ · readings]
- 76:23/q04/p1 The canonical Ḥamza reading elides the hamza, yielding Qurān without a change of meaning. — QIRAAT-ER describes this as simplifying the glottal stop. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(hmz)]

## 76:23/q05 Why is the revelation called القرآن here?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانَ · rhetoric]
- 76:23/q05/p1 This is the sūra's only use of Qur’an for the revelation; al-Ḥijr uses dhikr to answer Quraysh's own wording and fit its preceding verse. — Sāmarrāʾī compares 15:6 and 15:9. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 76:23/q06 What purpose or effect does the revealed Qur’an have?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانَ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes give different stated functions and effects of the revelation, without arguing that they exclude one another.
- 76:23/q06/p1 It gives people needed guidance, guards against deviation, and heals spiritual disorders. — Qurṭubī says the book contains what people need and is true. Biqāʿī attributes its comprehensive guidance to the revealer's encompassing knowledge and power and says its knowledge and practice heal spiritual disorders. Elmalılı says it guides humanity to the right path and offers the delight of the pure drink. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 76:23/q06/p2 God sent it as a trial and test. — Ṭabarī states this purpose. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]
- 76:23/q06/p3 It will bring about further events, and what is promised will certainly occur. — Elmalılı says the revelation has consequences beyond those already mentioned. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 76:23/q07 How did the Qur’an come down, and why in that manner?  [نَزَّلْنَا تَنزِيلًۭا · interpretation]
- 76:23/q07/p1 It came in successive portions rather than all at once, at appointed times governed by divine wisdom. — Qushayrī says over years; Ibn ʿAbbās, cited by Baghawī and Qurṭubī, says verse after verse, not all at once; Ibn al-Jawzī also says separate installments. Kashshāf relates stages to wisdom and the sequence of patience now, fighting and retribution later. Rāzī says every matter has its appointed time and the Prophet should accept the wise, faultless decree patiently. Bayḍāwī and Bursawī cite the wisdom requiring stages. Nasafī connects stages with purposeful perseverance. Biqāʿī says stages answer questions and ease people step by step into religious duties. Ālūsī and Elmalılı specify about twenty-three years; Elmalılı compares gradual revelation to the first human's stages of creation. Asad says the verbal form nazzalnā implies gradualness. Study Quran reports JJ and Ṭs's installment reading. [16 notes: QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(JJ, Ṭs)]

## 76:23/q08 What is the grammatical force of تنزيلا?  [تَنزِيلًۭا · grammar]
- 76:23/q08/p1 It is an emphatic verbal noun. — Naḥḥās explicitly calls tanzīlan emphatic. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 76:23/q09 How does this verse follow the earlier account of humanity and its outcomes?  [* · links]
- 76:23/q09/p1 The sūra moves from human creation, trial, guidance, and divergent outcomes to the gift and truth of revelation for the Prophet. — Rāzī traces the opening from nonexistence and the mixtures of 76:1, understood variously as elements, humors, parental fluids, body and spirit or soul, and embryonic stages; these point to a freely choosing Creator. He says humanity was made for trial, supplied with hearing, sight, and especially reason, and guided to a path (76:2–3). He notes punishment is brief but reward extensive, then says 76:22 completes the afterlife subject before worldly duties. Qurṭubī says the prior promises and threats lead to affirmation that the book is true and meets people's needs. Biqāʿī recalls guidance, grateful believers, deniers, and their outcomes, comparing the pure drink that revives dead earth to Qur’anic knowledge reviving dead hearts. Ālūsī says the account of obedient and disobedient people and mercy for the obedient leads to honor for the Prophet to ease loneliness. KURANYOLU describes the preceding afterlife outcomes as corresponding to worldly beliefs and deeds. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 76:23/q10 What does the different amount of space given to punishment and reward show?  [* · links]
- 76:23/q10/p1 Mercy is stronger and more prevalent. — Rāzī infers this from the brief treatment of punishment and lengthy description of reward through 76:22. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 76:23/q11 How does this verse prepare the commands that follow?  [* · links]
- 76:23/q11/p1 The honor and reassurance of revelation strengthen the Prophet for patient obedience, worship, and perseverance amid opponents. — Muqātil connects this to the earlier command to be patient with God's judgment. Rāzī says the Messenger, as head of the obedient community, is reassured before commands and prohibitions; removing harm precedes gaining benefit, so prohibitions come first. His account of the sūra's arrangement includes its opening, this reassurance, commands, and later rebels. He says accusations made the Prophet lonely and that patience is commanded despite his wish to fight tormentors until wise revelation permits it; after revelation and guidance he must obey God alone. Kashshāf and Nasafī likewise connect staged revelation to present patience and later action. Biqāʿī says followers await permission to fight and says revelation honors the Prophet now before the next life, in gratitude for his good conduct and avoidance of impurity before prophethood. Ālūsī says the gift eases loneliness and strengthens his heart. Iṣlāḥī urges trust, prayer, remembrance, and perseverance; KURANYOLU says the reminder of revelation's value steels worship and obedience against deniers. Sāmarrāʾī points to the direct commands in 76:24–26. [18 notes: MUQATIL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 76:23/q12 What can the gift of revelation lead people to know about God?  [* · theology]
- 76:23/q12/p1 People can know divine names and attributes and are called to obey God alone, but their minds cannot reach the divine essence. — Rāzī connects this distinction to the Prophet's command after the gift of revelation and guidance. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 76:23/q13 Why do deniers resist the revelation and its call?  [* · interpretation]
- 76:23/q13/p1 They are unwilling to give up immediate gains for the Hereafter and therefore fabricate objections. — Iṣlāḥī says the Hereafter is certain and that God can easily recreate them. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 76:23/q14 How is the sūra connected with the one before it?  [* · links]
- 76:23/q14/p1 Their relationship is clear. — Ālūsī describes it as perfectly clear and mentions 75:40 without further detail in this note. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 76:23/q15 What does the sūra's opening say about the division into grateful and ungrateful people?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether gratefulness and ingratitude are assigned to human choice or to God.
- 76:23/q15/p1 The division results from people's own choice. — Rāzī reports this as the Qadarī reading of 76:3, after the creation, trial, faculties, and guidance of 76:1–3. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القدرية)]
- 76:23/q15/p2 The division is attributed to God. — Rāzī reports this as the Jabrī reading of 76:3. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الجبرية)]

## 76:23/q16 Where was the sūra, or its final section, revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the whole sūra or only particular later verses are Meccan or Medinan; the two mixed reports differ in how much of the closing section they assign to Mecca.
- 76:23/q16/p1 The sūra is Meccan. — Nasafī classifies the whole sūra as Meccan; Ālūsī reports that most authorities cited in al-Baḥr do so. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 76:23/q16/p2 The sūra is wholly Medinan. — Ālūsī reports Mujāhid and Qatāda for this view; Ibn ʿĀdil calls an unrestricted Medinan classification the majority view and attributes it also to Shīʿa authorities. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عادل)]
- 76:23/q16/p3 The sūra is Medinan except 76:24, which is Meccan. — Ālūsī attributes this report to al-Ḥasan, ʿIkrima, and al-Kalbī. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الحسن وعكرمة والكلبي)]
- 76:23/q16/p4 The opening section is Medinan, while the closing patient command or commands are Meccan. — Ālūsī reports a version locating the closing command to be patient in Mecca. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the commands to patience and refusal to obey sinners or unbelievers form a later self-contained section that may have been revealed in Mecca, while the opening concerning the Prophet's household was Medinan; the reported extent of the Meccan closing material varies. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الحسن وعكرمة والكلبي); TABATABAI]

## 76:23/q17 What other names are given to this sūra?  [* · sciences]
- 76:23/q17/p1 It is also called al-Dahr, al-Abrār, al-Amshāj, and Hal Atā. — Ālūsī lists these names for Sūrat al-Insān. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 76:23/q18 How many verses does the sūra have?  [* · sciences]
- 76:23/q18/p1 Thirty-one verses. — Nasafī gives this count. Ālūsī, reporting Ibn ʿĀdil, says there is no disagreement about it. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عادل)]
