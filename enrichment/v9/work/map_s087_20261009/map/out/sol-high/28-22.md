# 28:22: 35 questions

## 28:22/q01 What does Moses' turning toward Midian mean?  [تَوَجَّهَ · meaning]
- 28:22/q01/p1 He turned his face and set his course toward Midian. — Jishumi defines tawajjuh as facing something; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Bursevī and Biqāʿī explain the physical turn as setting out. Wāḥidī says he departed; Ṭabarī adds that he left Pharaoh's city and power. Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq say he took the road facing Midian. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الزجاج); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق)]

## 28:22/q02 Did Moses already intend Midian as his destination?  [تَوَجَّهَ مَدْيَنَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'toward Midian' states a chosen destination or describes where divine guidance brought him; Rāzī argues from the directional wording and the prayer.
- 28:22/q02/p1 He intended Midian, but did not know the road. — Rāzī prefers this because tilqāʾ indicates direction and his prayer asks for the route, not a destination. Others cite kinship through Midian son of Abraham, familiarity with the place, or safety beyond Pharaoh's rule. Qurtubī mentions his kinship; Biqāʿī says he set out intending it. [11 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة والسدي); FARRA; TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL(آخرون); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNKATHIR-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 28:22/q02/p2 He had no chosen destination; God brought him toward Midian. — Ibn ʿAbbās says he surrendered himself to God and walked without knowing where; Qushayrī calls it reliance on a divine opening. Ibn ʿĀshūr says he wandered and happened upon a Midian road; Iṣlāḥī says he had no destination or contact. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 28:22/q03 What does tilqāʾ mean here?  [تِلْقَآءَ · meaning]
- 28:22/q03/p1 Toward or facing Midian. — Ibn Qutayba derives it from liqāʾ with an added tāʾ and cites a poet's talqāʾik for meeting; Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī relay this. Ṭabarī, Thaʿlabī, Jishumī and Baghawī compare houses facing each other. Ṭabarī, Jishumī and al-Majmaʿ also give 'from oneself'; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 7:47 for directional usage. [17 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(الشاعر); TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,الشاعر); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(المجمع); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 28:22/q04 How does tilqāʾ function grammatically?  [تِلْقَآءَ · grammar]
- 28:22/q04/p1 It is a verbal noun used as an adverb of place. — Abū Ḥayyān and Bursevī identify its adverbial use; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it an uncommon noun of meeting or proximity that became a place adverb, 'toward the place facing Midian.' [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:22/q05 What place or people does Midian denote?  [مَدْيَنَ · referent]
- 28:22/q05/p1 Shuʿayb's town or watering place and its people. — Qatāda calls it a watering place inhabited by Shuʿayb's people; Farrāʾ says Moses headed for its water. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites Marāṣid al-Iṭṭilāʿ for the well where Moses later watered Shuʿayb's flock. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the people as descendants of Abraham's son Midian (7:85). Damghānī compares 11:84 and 29:36; Asad identifies biblical Midian's inhabitants as Amorite Arabs related to Hebrews. [18 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); FARRA; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ(قيل); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); DAMGHANI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(مراصد الاطلاع); ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]

## 28:22/q06 Why was the town called Midian?  [مَدْيَنَ · reports]
- 28:22/q06/p1 It was named for Midian, son of Abraham. — Thaʿlabī adds that the sibling Madāʾin gave his name to Madāʾin; Bursevī names Midian's mother as Keturah and says he settled there. Baghawī, Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī and Ālūsī repeat the ancestry. [7 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 28:22/q07 Where was Midian and why could Moses seek refuge there?  [مَدْيَنَ · reports]
turns on: The notes offer compatible reasons for choosing Midian: safety outside Pharaoh's realm and possible help from related people.
- 28:22/q07/p1 It lay beyond Pharaoh's rule and was reachable from Egypt. — Thaʿlabī, Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Bursevī, Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī invoke its location outside Pharaoh's dominion. Qurtubī says another ruler held it. Bursevī places it by the Red Sea; Marāṣid places it opposite Tabuk, six stages away and larger than Tabuk; Kur'an Yolu places it near Maʿan north of Aqaba; Ibn ʿĀshūr puts it on the Red Sea's western shore and estimates about 850 miles or 45 walking days from Rameses or Memphis via Amalekite, Edomite and Nabataean lands. Asad refers elsewhere to a location note (7:67). [13 notes: THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جبير والناس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(مراصد الاطلاع); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 28:22/q07/p2 Its people were kin or culturally close to Moses and might help him. — Rāzī reports kinship through Midian son of Abraham, Qurtubī says he fled there on account of kinship, Ālūsī reports knowledge of the place or kinship. Asad says Amorite Arabs there were close to Hebrews; Kur'an Yolu says ethnic and linguistic closeness made help plausible. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(آخرون); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 28:22/q08 Why is Madyan indeclinable?  [مَدْيَنَ · grammar]
- 28:22/q08/p1 It is treated as a town name. — Farrāʾ and Ṭabarī cite poetic uses, including 'rəhbān Madyan'; Jishumī also cites an unattributed poem. Thaʿlabī calls it a known town and Abū Isḥāq a place name. Majāz specifically calls it a feminine name. [8 notes: FARRA(الشاعر); MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(الشاعر); THALABI; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI]

## 28:22/q09 What does ʿasā convey in Moses' prayer?  [عَسَىٰ · meaning]
- 28:22/q09/p1 It expresses hope with confidence that guidance is fitting and possible. — Biqāʿī glosses it as worthy, fitting and assuredly possible; Bursevī and Ālūsī connect Moses' words to trust and good expectation despite unfamiliar roads. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 28:22/q10 Why does Moses say 'my Lord' before asking for guidance?  [رَبِّىٓ · rhetoric]
- 28:22/q10/p1 His urgent dependence directs him first to his protector. — Biqāʿī attributes the order to Moses' special care and watchfulness toward God. Sāmarrāʾī contrasts 18:24, where guidance comes first amid differing views, with the fearful fugitive in Qasas. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 28:22/q11 How is the possessive yāʾ in rabbī read?  [رَبِّىٓ · readings]
turns on: The difference is the vowel or stillness of the first-person possessive yāʾ, without a change in meaning.
- 28:22/q11/p1 It is opened with fatḥa as rabbiya. — Qirāʾāt ER says the meaning and morphology are unchanged. Ibn Mujāhid reports Nāfiʿ opens the relevant possessive yāʾs except maʿī ridʾan; Ibn Kathīr and Abū ʿAmr open nine listed forms, with three exceptions. [3 notes: QIRAAT-ER(Y-iḍ); IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو,نافع)]
- 28:22/q11/p2 It remains still or is elided as rabbī. — Qirāʾāt ER contrasts this with rabbiya. Ibn Mujāhid records quiescence among disputed possessive yāʾs, though his listed exceptions concern other expressions. [3 notes: QIRAAT-ER(Y-iḍ); IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو,نافع)]

## 28:22/q12 What does 'guide me' mean here?  [يَهْدِيَنِى · meaning]
- 28:22/q12/p1 Direct or show Moses the right way. — Yaḥyā glosses it as directing; Ibn Qutayba gives the root sense and Arabic arshidnā. Damghānī compares 20:10, 38:22 and 1:6; Ibn al-Jawzī cites 38:22; al-Suyūṭī likewise gives showing the right course. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; ITQAN]

## 28:22/q13 Why is the first-person yāʾ retained in yahdīnī?  [يَهْدِيَنِى · rhetoric]
- 28:22/q13/p1 The full form fits Moses' fear, complete turning to God, and the extended Qasas telling. — Sāmarrāʾī contrasts the shorter form in 18:24: Kahf's request is less urgent and occurs briefly in the sleepers' story. He notes more yāʾ pronouns in Qasas, twelve guidance mentions there versus five in Kahf, and an explicit yāʾ here where Kahf uses kasra in place of it. [4 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 28:22/q14 What is the middle or straight way Moses seeks?  [سَوَآءَ ٱلسَّبِيلِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the straight way is only the physical road or also religious guidance; Ibn ʿAṭiyya explicitly favors Ḥasan's wider sense.
- 28:22/q14/p1 The direct, middle road to Midian that avoids losing the way. — Mujāhid names the Midian road. Qatāda, Suddī and Ḥasan gloss it as intended, middle or straight; Majāz cites poetry for sawāʾ as open and long. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it sound and straight; Biqāʿī says God shows a road without deviation. Ibn Kathīr says God guided Moses in this life and the next. The source glosses vary between the route's destination, middle, and straightness. [37 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة والسدي,مجاهد); FARRA; MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(الحسن,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الحسن,السدي,قتادة,مجاهد); NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(المجمع); STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 28:22/q14/p2 The path of religious guidance or the true religion. — Ḥasan gives 'path of guidance'; Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers this as more excellent, comparing Abū Bakr's description of the Prophet guiding him to the path. Ibn ʿĀshūr says a request for true religion is implicit beneath the straight road. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(القاضي أبو محمد)+]

## 28:22/q15 What is the ordinary meaning and wider range of sabīl?  [ٱلسَّبِيلِ · meaning]
- 28:22/q15/p1 Its basic Arabic sense is a road, with context allowing figurative uses. — Ibn al-Jawzī lists obedience (2:195; 4:76), pilgrimage means (3:97), exit (4:15; 17:48), a course (4:22; 17:32), pretexts (4:34), religion (4:115; 4:150; 16:125), argument or authority (4:90; 4:141), aggression or liability (42:42), and sin or liability (3:75; 9:91). For this verse he gives the road sense. Bursevī says a sabīl is a route habitually traveled; Zajjāj also glosses it as road. [13 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); ZAJJAJ; BURSEVI]

## 28:22/q16 What inward meaning is given to Moses' direction and prayer?  [* · inward]
turns on: The symbolic referent assigned to Midian and the guidance sought.
- 28:22/q16/p1 He faces Midian bodily but God in his heart, and guidance opens through that devotion. — Jaʿfar says wholehearted turning led to divine speech and fulfillment; Abū Saʿīd al-Kharrāz invokes lights of discernment and divine conversation leading to Shuʿayb and the start of blessings. Bursevī calls the journey longing for encounter. [3 notes: SULAMI(أبو سعيد الحراز,جعفر); BURSEVI]
- 28:22/q16/p2 Midian symbolizes the station of spirit on the path of unity. — Qāshānī says strong will lets hope overcome fear while spiritual lights and manifestations of attributes bring true guidance. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 28:22/q16/p3 Midian signifies the realm of pre-eternity and post-eternity, where a seeker senses reality and seeks encounter. — Bursevī reports this from some and invokes the saying about the Breath of the Merciful from Yemen, understood through Uways al-Qaranī's heart and the blessings of saintly places and meetings. He says fear of obstruction accompanies the seeker through trial. [3 notes: BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام,بعضهم)]

## 28:22/q17 How many days or nights did Moses' journey take?  [* · reports]
turns on: Differing estimates of the distance and route, and reports using days versus nights.
- 28:22/q17/p1 Eight days or nights, likened to Kufa–Basra distance. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Ibn ʿAbbās and later commentators give eight; the reports vary between days and nights. Thaʿlabī and Ṭabarī compare Kufa and Basra; Zajjāj says Egypt to Midian is that distance. Many later sources repeat it. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); ZAJJAJ(قيل); THALABI(المفسرون); WAHIDI-BASIT(محمد بن إسحاق وغيره); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL(ابن إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جبير والناس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 28:22/q17/p2 Ten days. — Muqātil gives ten days and says Moses had no guide. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 28:22/q17/p3 About forty-five days on foot across an approximately 850-mile route. — Ibn ʿĀshūr estimates this from a proposed route from Rameses or Memphis through Amalekite, Edomite and Nabataean lands, with outdoor sleeping. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:22/q18 In what condition did Moses travel?  [* · reports]
- 28:22/q18/p1 He fled alone, fearful, without supplies or transport, eating leaves and wearing out his feet. — Ibn Isḥāq and the commentators list no food, bread, money, mount, shoes or provisions. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr says he was barefoot and the soles wore through; Baghawī adds that the green leaves showed through his belly, and Ibn al-Jawzī mentions severe hunger. Qurṭubī says he lacked comfort; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī relays Jaʿfar's report of an exhausting flight and later watering for two young women. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق,سعيد بن جبير); THALABI(ابن جبير,المفسرون); WAHIDI-BASIT(محمد بن إسحاق وغيره); BAGHAWI-FULL(المفسرون); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL(ابن إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو مالك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]

## 28:22/q19 What did Moses know about the roads, and which did he take?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ about whether he had entered a road, how many roads appeared, and how he selected the route.
- 28:22/q19/p1 He did not know the road; he trusted God to bring him to the right one. — Ibn ʿAbbās repeatedly says he relied on good expectation of God. Muqātil says he feared losing the route without a guide; Ibn Isḥāq says God prepared it. Abū Ḥayyān prefers the apparent sense of direct divine guidance to safety. Ibn ʿĀshūr says he reached a Midian road after wandering. [22 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة والسدي); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن إسحاق,ابن عباس,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 28:22/q19/p2 Four roads appeared, and he chose the Midian road after praying. — ʿIkrima says he could not tell which of the four to take; Mawardī specifies he prayed when they diverged. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عكرمة); MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 28:22/q19/p3 Three roads appeared; he chose the middle, while pursuers took the two sides. — Bayḍāwī says he chose it in trust and good expectation; Bursevī says pursuers reasoned a fugitive would avoid the central route. Biqāʿī says they expected him on side roads or cross-country paths. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 28:22/q19/p4 He had already entered the Midian road and prayed to stay straight on it. — Mawardī reports this from Qatāda and al-Suddī; Ibn Kathīr calls the road well traveled. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 28:22/q19/p5 He deliberately chose a safe road that happened to lead to Midian. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this as another account. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]

## 28:22/q20 Did a being guide Moses to Midian, and who was it?  [* · reports]
turns on: Reports differ between an unnamed angel, Gabriel, and divine direction without an identified intermediary.
- 28:22/q20/p1 An angel guided him. — Al-Suddī says the angel found him lost, mounted on a horse and sometimes carrying a spear; Moses bowed or prostrated in fear or joy, then followed. Thaʿlabī and Zamakhsharī report a mounted angel with spear; Baghawī gives a staff. Other sources simply say God sent an angel. Qurṭubī's Abū Mālik adds that Pharaoh's searchers watched the passes. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); RAZI-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو مالك); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(السدي)]
- 28:22/q20/p2 Gabriel specifically guided him. — Rāzī reports this from some; Qurṭubī attributes it to Muqātil and al-Suddī but leaves the truth to God. Ālūsī and Abū Ḥayyān also relay the identification. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل والسدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 28:22/q20/p3 God prepared and showed the route without a named guide. — Ibn Isḥāq says God prepared the road; Biqāʿī says God directed him along the main road and foiled pursuers; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees the prayer answered in his arrival. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن إسحاق); BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 28:22/q21 When and how did Moses receive his staff?  [* · reports]
turns on: Qurṭubī judges the Midian shepherding account more common and sounder than the angel's gift.
- 28:22/q21/p1 The guiding angel gave it to him on the journey. — Qurṭubī gives this as a reported addition to Abū Mālik's account of the mounted angel with spear. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو مالك); QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 28:22/q21/p2 He took the staff in Midian for shepherding. — Qurṭubī prefers this account as more widespread and sounder. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL+]

## 28:22/q22 Whom did Moses meet in Midian?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the unnamed elder in the ensuing episode is identified as Shuʿayb.
- 28:22/q22/p1 He met a man associated with Shuʿayb's people and later an elder whose daughter he married. — Al-Ḥasan calls Moses and the man from Shuʿayb's people the two best people on earth that day. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says he later married an unnamed elder's daughter; Jaʿfar's report says two young women brought Moses to their father after he watered for them. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 28:22/q22/p2 The elder was the prophet Shuʿayb. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites some Imami and Sunni reports for the identification. Abū Saʿīd al-Kharrāz speaks of Moses' encounter with Shuʿayb as the beginning of blessings; Ibn ʿĀshūr says he would meet a prophet who taught prophetic conduct. [3 notes: TABATABAI(روايات أئمة أهل البيت وبعض روايات أهل السنة); SULAMI(أبو سعيد الحراز); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:22/q23 What does Moses' prayer teach about reliance on God?  [* · interpretation]
- 28:22/q23/p1 He entrusted himself to God despite fear and ignorance of the route. — Ibn ʿAbbās repeatedly mentions his good expectation; Ibn ʿAṭiyya sees knowledge of God and bestowed wisdom; Qushayrī describes reliance on divine opening. Iṣlāḥī says this flight, unlike his later migration as a prophet, had no divinely fixed stages, so it models guidance for truth seekers without clear promises. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says his sequence of prayers and commitments in 28:17 and 28:21 shows he sought God's approval even in natural needs. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 28:22/q24 How does this flight relate to Abraham's migration?  [* · links]
- 28:22/q24/p1 Moses' prayer echoes Abraham's departure and trust in divine guidance. — Rāzī compares Abraham's 'I am going to my Lord; He will guide me' at 37:99 and says Moses often echoes Abraham in argument, reply, prayer and supplication. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares Abraham's migration at 29:26 and says Moses was guided to Midian, where he would meet a prophet. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:22/q25 How does this scene connect to the surrounding story?  [* · links]
- 28:22/q25/p1 It bridges Moses' escape from Egypt to the Midian episode and God's answers to his prayers. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reads the opening clause as coordinating with omitted wandering after he left the city. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī summarizes the flight, arrival and marriage, then says God answered three prayers: safety, the Midian road, and provision through watering, livelihood and a wife. Baghawī reports Ibn ʿAbbās calling this journey Moses' first trial. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 28:22/q26 Why do tellings of Moses and Pharaoh vary across the Quran?  [* · links]
- 28:22/q26/p1 Each telling has a distinct nuance in wording, order and detail. — Burhān contrasts repeated Moses narratives, including Qasas 28:3–43, with Joseph's story collected in one sura; he says earlier scripture's single separate tellings differ from this Quranic pattern. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 28:22/q27 What broader lesson does the Midian episode offer its audience?  [* · interpretation]
- 28:22/q27/p1 Its virtues prepare Moses for revelation and invite emulation and recognition of prophetic character. — Ibn ʿĀshūr lists generosity, aid to the distressed, compassion, modesty, gratitude, chastity and fidelity; Shuʿayb shows hospitality, protection and gentleness. He says Meccan polytheists could compare Moses with Muhammad's known premission virtue, while Muslims should emulate prophetic conduct. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:22/q28 How does the verse's hope fit the rhetoric of al-Qasas?  [* · rhetoric]
- 28:22/q28/p1 Hope is characteristic of al-Qasas, compared with certainty in al-Naml. — Sāmarrāʾī counts ten expressions of hope in Qasas against two in Naml, citing 27:7 and 27:46. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 28:22/q29 Was Moses' earlier confession a change from unbelief?  [* · interpretation]
- 28:22/q29/p1 No; he was already a believer, and confessed the killing of the Egyptian. — Sāmarrāʾī contrasts the Queen of Sheba, who left sun worship and submitted to God (27:44), with Moses' admission concerning the killing (28:16, 28:21–22). [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 28:22/q30 Is the sura containing this verse Meccan?  [* · sciences]
- 28:22/q30/p1 All of al-Qasas is Meccan. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this from Ḥasan, ʿAṭāʾ and ʿIkrima. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وعطاء وعكرمة)]

## 28:22/q31 What does the Quran's rhetoric require of its hearers?  [* · rhetoric]
- 28:22/q31/p1 Its inimitability is recognized directly by those fully versed in eloquent speech; others infer it according to their knowledge. — Bāqillānī says the first Muslims accepted from insight; foreigners infer Arab incapacity, intermediate speakers infer the limits of top experts, and skill in poetry, sermons or letters alone is insufficient. A complete rhetor hears the proof immediately; requiring the Prophet to test all Arabs would misstate it. Ibn ʿĀshūr says verse endings challenged eloquent leaders, establishing proof for them and ordinary hearers. [5 notes: BAQILLANI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:22/q32 How do interrogative forms express rejection or rebuke?  [* · rhetoric]
- 28:22/q32/p1 Their force varies with tense, word order and whether the rejected act occurred. — Burhān says an interrogative imperfect can deny an act or warn against it (11:28); object fronting questions the actor's fitness (6:14, 6:40); rejection may imply a false claim (37:153, 52:35, 6:100, 43:19). He distinguishes denial of an unreal act from rebuke of a real blameworthy one (37:95; 10:106; 37:86; 26:165–166; 2:188). [4 notes: BURHAN]

## 28:22/q33 How should Quranic pause marks be evaluated?  [* · sciences]
- 28:22/q33/p1 Pauses are graded by meaning and no location has a universally obligatory or forbidden stop. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Ibn al-Jazarī and classifies pauses as emphatic, good or lesser. He notes preferences for stopping at completed speech and shorter pauses before completion; he finds Ibn al-Naḥḥās's appeal to an Ibn ʿUmar report for obligatory stops unclear. Later scholars marked pauses to ease reading as Islam spread; their choices proposed interpretations. He names Ibn al-Anbārī, Ibn al-Naḥḥās, al-Nakzawī and al-Hibṭī. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن الجزري)]

## 28:22/q34 How is the hāʾ in tawajjaha treated in imāla?  [تَوَجَّهَ · readings]
- 28:22/q34/p1 The original hāʾ itself is not inclined. — Ibn al-Jazarī says it has no yāʾ origin, unlike an alif; a pronoun hāʾ is likewise not inclined and the preceding letter already has kasra. He contrasts ṣalāh and zakāh, whose alif cannot be inclined without the prior consonant, with tawrāh and muzjāh, whose alif derives from yāʾ. Al-Dānī assigns inclination to the alif and prior letter, with secondary coloring of hāʾ; Makkī says the hāʾ itself is never inclined. [5 notes: NASHR(الداني,الداني ومكي)]

## 28:22/q35 How are possessive yāʾs read in the related recitation lists?  [رَبِّىٓ · readings]
turns on: Different readers' treatment of first-person yāʾs across the cited set of passages.
- 28:22/q35/p1 Nāfiʿ opens twelve disputed yāʾs except maʿī ridʾan. — Ibn Mujāhid records this as a general recitation pattern in the passage. [1 notes: IBNMUJAHID(نافع)]
- 28:22/q35/p2 Ibn Kathīr and Abū ʿAmr open nine, leaving three named yāʾs still. — Ibn Mujāhid names innī urīd, satajidunī in shāʾ Allāh, and maʿī ridʾan as exceptions. [1 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو)]
- 28:22/q35/p3 Yaʿqūb and the Kufans keep the yāʾ still in both occurrences of laʿallī. — Ibn al-Jazarī records this related pronoun reading. [1 notes: NASHR(يعقوب والكوفيون)]
