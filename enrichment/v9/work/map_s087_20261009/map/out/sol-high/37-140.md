# 37:140: 17 questions

## 37:140/q01 How does 'idh' relate grammatically to the preceding statement?  [إِذْ · grammar]
- 37:140/q01/p1 It is an adverb attached to the statement that Jonah was among the messengers. — Al-Samīn says Jonah remained a messenger even in this condition. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 37:140/q02 What does 'abaqa' mean and what usages or forms explain it?  [أَبَقَ · meaning]
- 37:140/q02/p1 It means to flee or go away, especially a slave's flight from his master. — Abū ʿUbayda, al-Zajjāj, Muqātil, al-Mubarrad, and others gloss it as fleeing or going away. Ibn Qutayba likens the runaway to an overburdened camel. Al-Mubarrad and Muḥammad ibn Yazīd specify moving far away. Al-Māwardī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Jishumī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, and an authority in al-Ālūsī add concealment beyond a pursuer's reach; al-Jishumī contrasts near synonyms ābiq, hārib, and fārr. Al-Samīn gives abaqa al-ʿabdu yaʾbiqu ibāqan, ābiq and its plural ābāq, another dialect with kasra in the past and fatḥa in the present, and use for a man who hides. Ibn ʿĀshūr gives ibāq as verbal noun and the ḍaraba and samiʿa verb patterns. Al-Samīn also cites a poetic occurrence of al-abqā meaning hemp, a different form. [27 notes: MAJAZ; NAHHAS(محمد بن يزيد); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد,عبد الله,مقاتل); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة,الزجاج,المبرد); QURTUBI-FULL(المبرد); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض الكمل); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 37:140/q03 Why is Jonah's departure called a slave's flight?  [أَبَقَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the narrower sense of escaping beyond pursuit makes the expression necessarily figurative, and how its comparison to a servant's unpermitted departure works.
- 37:140/q03/p1 The slave-flight expression compares Jonah's departure without God's permission to a servant running from his master and assigned task. — Al-Wāḥidī's scholars of meaning say he knew God could reach him by land or sea, yet headed to the ship as refuge. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the literal basis a servant's unpermitted departure; al-Nasafī calls the application figurative. Al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn al-Jawzī's linguists, Asad, Elmalılı, and al-Qurṭubī's reported explanation likewise stress the missing permission. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it an extended metaphor for abandoning the commissioned city and task; al-Biqāʿī stresses flight from the Master who honored him with prophethood. Al-Jishumī and Islahi say he should have stayed at his assigned mission until ordered away. Al-Ālūsī allows metaphor or metonymy from the restricted slave sense to flight generally, preferring metaphor for eloquence; he rejects the claim that the narrower sense of being beyond pursuit makes its application strained. [20 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,عبد الله); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(بعض أهل المعاني); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 37:140/q03/p2 His flight was figurative because a true runaway escapes beyond the master's reach, whereas God could reach Jonah. — An eminent scholar reported by al-Ālūsī applies the especially narrow definition of ibāq, flight so complete a pursuer cannot find the fugitive. Al-Ālūsī objects that this nuance does not bar using the word for Jonah and calls the explanation strained. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض الكمل); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 37:140/q04 Why and when did Jonah leave his people?  [أَبَقَ · interpretation]
turns on: The reports differ on the trigger and timing of his departure, what he knew about the people's repentance, and whether he was leaving an ongoing commission.
- 37:140/q04/p1 He left angry after warning of punishment, and its delay or removal made him reluctant to face his people. — Yaḥyā says he preached long, gave the date God disclosed, left weeping as it approached, and feared return when the city survived because liars faced having their tongues cut. Al-Ḥasan says his departure was the sign punishment was near and that he left angry at his people, without God's leave. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says their repentance and deliverance tested and angered him; an unnamed report says he feared being killed as a false claimant. Al-Wāḥidī's exegetes say the delay made him leave as though estranged. Tawus says he left angry after the people's terrified repentance. Al-Ālūsī's report gives a three-day warning and says shame at the averted punishment preceded boarding. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī instead says he apparently did not know of their belief and repentance when he learned punishment had lifted. Elmalılı rejects grieving over believers being spared as though his prophecy had proved false: punishment was conditioned on unbelief, and grief may date from his earlier anger and flight. 21:87 is cited for anger. [17 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); ABDURRAZZAQ(طاوس); THALABI(ابن عباس ووهب); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس ووهب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قائل غير مسمى); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(طاوس); ALUSI-FULL(رُوي); TABATABAI; ELMALILI-]
- 37:140/q04/p2 His people's resistance and his inability to bear the mission drove him to leave before permission. — Ibn Qutayba describes a constricted chest and prophethood's burden breaking him like a heavily laden camel. Al-Jishumī says his people exasperated him, though he should have kept preaching. Al-Biqāʿī says weakness under the mission led him to flee. Asad calls it angry abandonment of a divine trust. Islahi says indifference offended him and he thought he should not cast pearls before them; Kur'an Yolu reports long monotheist preaching, with one report specifying thirty-three years, and impatience after failure. [6 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Tefsirler)]
- 37:140/q04/p3 He fled to avoid the harm and power of Nineveh while still charged to preach there. — Ibn ʿĀshūr conjectures fear of Nineveh's power and doubt about his patience with expected harm; he describes its people as Israelites under Assyrian protection. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects to the biblical version of flight from the prophetic commission. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 37:140/q04/p4 He fled after resenting an Israelite king's order to preach at Nineveh. — Abū Ṣāliḥ says Isaiah commanded the king to send Jonah; Jonah resented the king's order, fled, was swallowed, and only afterward preached until his people believed. Al-Tirmidhī al-Ḥakīm says the king's demand served God's right and that Jonah sought what seemed right but missed God's intended course. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(أبو صالح); QURTUBI-FULL(الترمذي الحكيم)]
- 37:140/q04/p5 He departed certain his people would be destroyed, unaware that they had repented. — Ḥumayd ibn Hilāl says Jonah privately prayed for the resistant people, heard from an informant that punishment had arrived, and left certain of their destruction; God then showed them mercy. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise says he apparently did not know of their repentance when he learned punishment had lifted. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(حميد بن هلال); TABATABAI]
- 37:140/q04/p6 In the biblical account, he fled his call to Nineveh toward Tarshish by way of Jaffa. — Elmalılı reports al-Ālūsī's People of the Book version: Nineveh's wickedness overwhelmed him and his vessel later met a storm. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the biblical name Jonah son of Amittai, a huge Nineveh taking three days to cross, and the Tarshish and Jaffa route; he rejects that account's portrayal of flight from the commission and resentment despite knowing repentance. Kur'an Yolu says the fuller Bible story broadly matches the Qur'an; the Study Quran reports its phrase flight from God's presence. [4 notes: ELMALILI(Âlûsî); TABATABAI(الآلوسي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Kitâb-ı Mukaddes); STUDYQURAN(the Biblical book of Jonah); TABATABAI-]

## 37:140/q05 What happened to Jonah's people around the threatened punishment?  [أَبَقَ · reports]
- 37:140/q05/p1 They repented and believed as signs of punishment approached, so God averted it. — Yaḥyā says a hearer told the king, who assembled the people; they saw darkness and fierce wind, separated children and young animals from mothers, wore rough garments, dust, and ashes, humbled themselves and wept. Al-Ḥasan reports a black wind and prayer with children and animals. Tawus likewise reports terrified separation of mothers and young. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says God had disclosed the day to Jonah and accepted their repentance when its signs appeared. Kur'an Yolu cites 10:98 for the people's escape from destruction after abandoning unbelief. Yaḥyā adds the agreement to cut a liar's tongue. [12 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; ABDURRAZZAQ(طاوس); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL; DURR-FULL(حميد بن هلال,طاوس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 37:140/q06 What does 'al-fulk' denote here, and what are its grammatical possibilities?  [ٱلْفُلْكِ · grammar]
- 37:140/q06/p1 It denotes one ship here, though the noun may be masculine or feminine and singular or plural. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya specifies the singular here. Al-Farrāʾ, reported by al-Naḥḥās, permits masculine or feminine and a collective or plural of fulk like wathan. Al-Qurṭubī likewise gives both genders and numbers. Al-Zajjāj defines it as a ship, al-Biqāʿī a vessel sailing at sea. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers ship as its immediate sense: the Qur'an uses al-fulk of ships in three named verses and twenty others, while falak means orbit in 21:33 and 36:40; she contrasts safīna for Noah's vessel in 29:15 and twice for Moses and his companion in 18:71 and 18:79. [7 notes: NAHHAS(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; BINTSHATI-IJAZ+]

## 37:140/q07 What does 'al-mashḥūn' say the ship carried or how full it was?  [ٱلْمَشْحُونِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether fullness refers to load and passengers or also to the vessel's readiness and equipment.
- 37:140/q07/p1 The ship was full or heavily laden, with some accounts specifying people, animals, cargo, or goods. — Muqātil, the report attributed to Mujāhid in al-Wāḥidī, and al-Bursawī specify people and animals; al-Bursawī adds goods. Yaḥyā and Ibn ʿĀshūr specify people or passengers. Al-Ṭabarī says cargo, with Qatāda and al-Suddī calling it heavily laden; Ibn Kathīr's Ibn ʿAbbās report and Islahi specify goods. Al-Biqāʿī says there was no space for anything else. Al-Itqān and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cite ʿUbayd ibn al-Abraṣ's use of shaḥnā as poetic evidence for filling or loading; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ attributes a loaded-to-capacity gloss to Ibn ʿAbbās and notes mashḥūn accompanies fulk only three times in the Qur'an, including 26:119 and 36:41. The many other commentators gloss it simply as full or loaded; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the passenger sense was explained earlier in Noah's account. [28 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(السدي,قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 37:140/q07/p2 It can mean a ship fully equipped and ready to sail. — Al-Bursawī offers this alongside its being filled with people, animals, and goods. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 37:140/q08 What does the ship's laden or ready condition add to the scene?  [ٱلْفُلْكِ ٱلْمَشْحُونِ · rhetoric]
- 37:140/q08/p1 Its imminent departure suited Jonah's wish to leave at once. — Al-Biqāʿī infers that a fully loaded ship had no reason to delay for more passengers. Islahi says the expression underscores Jonah's disappointment and readiness to board without fully reflecting. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 37:140/q09 Where and how did Jonah board the ship?  [إِلَى ٱلْفُلْكِ · reports]
turns on: The reports give different circumstances and, in the biblical account, a named embarkation point and destination.
- 37:140/q09/p1 He went from his people toward the shore and boarded a ship. — Yaḥyā says the crew did not recognize him and he slept in a corner. Muqātil says he covered his head and slept at its side. Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī place boarding after the people's punishment was delayed; al-Wāḥidī's exegetes have him travel to the sea. Al-Bayḍāwī reports boarding before God ordered him. Al-Majāz says abaqa can mean taking refuge toward the ship. [10 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; MAJAZ; THALABI(ابن عباس ووهب); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس ووهب); BAYDAWI-FULL(روي)]
- 37:140/q09/p2 A report places his boarding after waves separated him from his wife and sons and a wolf took one son. — Al-Baghawī introduces this as another story and says he boarded another ship, which later stalled. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل)]
- 37:140/q09/p3 In the biblical account he boarded at Jaffa for Tarshish. — Elmalılı and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī report this via al-Ālūsī; the latter gives Jonah's name as son of Amittai and Nineveh's size. Kur'an Yolu also reports the Tarshish destination and says the fuller Bible account broadly matches the Qur'an. [3 notes: ELMALILI(Âlûsî); TABATABAI(الآلوسي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Kitâb-ı Mukaddes)]

## 37:140/q10 What endangered or stopped the ship, and how did the crew explain it?  [ٱلْفُلْكِ · reports]
turns on: Reports differ between a stalled vessel attributed to a fugitive, dangerous weather, and a whale; several accounts combine them.
- 37:140/q10/p1 The ship stopped because a fugitive or wrongdoer was aboard, according to the crew's belief. — Muqātil reports a whale called al-Lakhm assigned by God and the stopped ship's passengers fearing drowning. Yaḥyā says the crew suspected a wrongdoer. The reports of Ibn ʿAbbās and Wahb, al-Wāḥidī's Wahb, al-Nasafī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī specify a fugitive slave; al-Bursawī calls this a customary reaction of ships, while al-Biqāʿī cites a seafarers' belief that such a ship cannot sail steadily. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says other ships moved while this one stalled. Ḥumayd says it stopped; Tawus says the crew blamed an ill-omened passenger. [18 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ABDURRAZZAQ(طاوس); THALABI(ابن عباس ووهب); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(سعيد بن جير,وهب); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس ووهب,قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(روي); NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(حميد بن هلال,طاوس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(رُوي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 37:140/q10/p2 A storm, fierce wind, or waves threatened to sink the ship. — Yaḥyā says a wind nearly sank it. Ibn ʿAbbās, as reported by al-Māwardī, says the crew feared storm-driven waves. Ibn Kathīr says waves tossed it toward sinking, motivating a lot to lighten it. Al-Biqāʿī's Ibn ʿAbbās report joins a storm with seafarers' fugitive belief. Asad says a storm threatened the vessel. The biblical accounts reported by al-Biqāʿī, Elmalılı, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, Kur'an Yolu and the Study Quran likewise describe a storm; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī's biblical report adds sailors discarding cargo, Jonah asleep below and the captain waking him to pray. [11 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس,مترجمه); ELMALILI(Âlûsî); TABATABAI(الآلوسي); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Kitâb-ı Mukaddes); STUDYQURAN(the Biblical book of Jonah)]
- 37:140/q10/p3 The crew feared a whale confronting the ship. — Ibn ʿĪsā's report in al-Māwardī gives this explanation; Muqātil's account names the whale al-Lakhm and says God assigned it to the ship. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); MUQATIL]

## 37:140/q11 How was Jonah selected and put overboard?  [ٱلْفُلْكِ · reports]
turns on: Accounts vary in why the lot was held, how many times it was drawn, and whether the crew cast him, he jumped into the sea, or he entered the fish's mouth.
- 37:140/q11/p1 The crew cast lots, often three times, selecting Jonah, who confessed his flight and went into the sea. — Muqātil says Jonah first identified himself; the crew refused to cast God's messenger overboard, then drew names in water three times and found his name each time. Ibn ʿAbbās and Wahb in al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī say he admitted being the runaway and jumped; al-Wāḥidī's Wahb says he threw himself in after the lot. Tawus reports three lots and his jump. Ibn Kathīr says the crew resisted despite three losses and Jonah stripped to jump. Al-Wāḥidī's Saʿīd ibn Jubayr says Jonah confessed a grave sin and proposed a lot before they cast him overboard. Al-Māwardī says the crew suspected a sinner and chose him by lot. Al-Ālūsī reports three selections followed by casting him overboard. Islahi says the crew wanted the apparent sinner removed; Asad says Jonah agreed to the lot. In the biblical accounts reported by al-Biqāʿī, Elmalılı, Kur'an Yolu, and the Study Quran, the storm prompted lots, Jonah confessed or requested casting overboard, and the sea calmed afterward in the Study Quran's account. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the biblical sailors' cargo throwing and lot. [24 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ABDURRAZZAQ(طاوس); THALABI(ابن عباس ووهب); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(سعيد بن جير,وهب); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس ووهب); IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(روي); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس,مترجمه); DURR-FULL(حميد بن هلال,طاوس); ALUSI-FULL(رُوي); ELMALILI(Âlûsî); TABATABAI(الآلوسي); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Kitâb-ı Mukaddes); STUDYQURAN(the Biblical book of Jonah)]
- 37:140/q11/p2 Lots selected Jonah as an offering to a great fish so the vessel could escape. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the crew cast him into the sea, the fish swallowed him, and the ship escaped. Ḥumayd ibn Hilāl says Jonah asked to be tipped feet first; a pale-mouthed whale swallowed him and a larger whale followed. [2 notes: TABATABAI; DURR-FULL(حميد بن هلال)]
- 37:140/q11/p3 He jumped himself into the fish's mouth when it rose by God's command. — The Study Quran attributes this to some Quranic commentators reported by IK and says the sailors hesitated to throw him. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(IK)]

## 37:140/q12 What happened to Jonah in and after the fish?  [* · reports]
- 37:140/q12/p1 God preserved Jonah in the fish, which ultimately returned him unharmed to shore. — Muqātil says God had not instructed the whale before Jonah's fall; at two spans above the water God made its belly a place of worship, forbade harm to hair or skin, and forbade food or drink. Water and wind challenged Jonah's flight, affirmed their worship and fear of God, and he recalled his deeds until inspired to call. Al-Qurṭubī cites al-Zamakhsharī's report of a whale swimming beside the ship with Jonah breathing and praising from its raised head, then carrying him ashore unharmed. Al-Biqāʿī's translated Book of Jonah says he prayed after three days and nights in the great fish; Kur'an Yolu's biblical summary says he prayed, was returned ashore and recommissioned. Kur'an Yolu cites 21:87–88 for prayer and rescue; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the Quranic account proceeds through fish, rescue, renewed mission and belief. [7 notes: MUQATIL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL(مترجمه); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Kitâb-ı Mukaddes)]

## 37:140/q13 How long did Jonah remain in the fish?  [* · reports]
turns on: Different transmitted durations are reported without an argument selecting one.
- 37:140/q13/p1 Three days, or three days and nights. — The Study Quran lists three days among competing accounts; al-Biqāʿī's translated Book of Jonah says his prayer came after three days and nights. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(IK); BIQAI-FULL(مترجمه)]
- 37:140/q13/p2 Seven days. — Listed as one of the different accounts by the Study Quran. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 37:140/q13/p3 Forty days. — Listed as one of the different accounts by the Study Quran. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 37:140/q13/p4 From morning until evening of the same day. — The Study Quran gives a report of swallowing in the morning and expulsion that evening. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(IK)]

## 37:140/q14 What does Jonah's flight imply about prophetic fault and servitude?  [أَبَقَ · theology]
turns on: Whether the Quranic terms assign an actual fault to a prophet or describe only the apparent implication of his act.
- 37:140/q14/p1 He left a divine duty without leave and was blameworthy for a lapse of impatience or desire. — Al-Ḥasan says he opposed God's command. Al-Jishumī says he was obliged to continue calling until God ordered departure. Al-Tirmidhī al-Ḥakīm says genuine servitude abandons desire and gives itself for God's affairs; Jonah chose desire over self-sacrifice, and despite aiming for the right course missed God's intended one. Islahi says a prophet cannot leave his post even with a pious motive and describes being cast into the sea as punishment. Kur'an Yolu calls the regretted act a momentary angry lapse for which he suffered, cites 68:48–50 on impatience, and reports a long frustrated preaching period. [11 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL(الترمذي الحكيم); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Tefsirler)]
- 37:140/q14/p2 The descriptions of flight and anger convey how his conduct appeared, while the prophet committed no sin. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī holds that, unlike biblical scriptures allowing even major prophetic sins, the Quran exempts prophets from major and minor sin; descriptions of absconding, anger and supposing God could not constrain him in 21:87 therefore express the apparent implication of his action. He rejects the biblical account's account of fleeing the commission and resenting a saved people despite knowing their repentance. [3 notes: TABATABAI]

## 37:140/q15 What lesson does Jonah's departure teach about a prophet's mission?  [* · interpretation]
- 37:140/q15/p1 A prophet must persist until God releases him, since only God knows when a people have received conclusive warning. — Al-Jishumī says Jonah should have continued calling until commanded to leave. Islahi says prophets must endure their ordained trials, an attempted escape may bring a greater trial, the nation needs respite until truth is fully communicated, and only God knows when that is; passion for truth can make prophets exceed bounds, which God checks because they serve as a barometer of truth. Kur'an Yolu says extraordinary events trained prophets for difficult monotheist struggles. [6 notes: JISHUMI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 37:140/q16 How does this episode fit the sūra's telling of the prophets?  [* · links]
- 37:140/q16/p1 It illustrates the burdens of messengership and God's care and support for His chosen messengers. — Al-Biqāʿī says it consoles the Prophet against Quraysh's arrogance and shows that God does not abandon messengers even when they try to flee their charge. Islahi says the prophet stories share refrains of peace, mercy, support for the mission and reward for good deeds; prophets through Aaron are chronological, thereafter ordered by their attributes. Kur'an Yolu says these verses privilege the story's lesson over bare history. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says al-Ṣāffāt recounts selected stages: mission, departure, ship, fish, rescue, renewed mission and the people's belief. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 37:140/q17 How does this compressed account connect to other scriptural tellings?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether and how the fuller biblical narrative may be used alongside the Quranic account, especially on Jonah's knowledge and prophetic commission.
- 37:140/q17/p1 Other Quranic passages supply or recall parts of Jonah's story omitted here. — Al-Wāḥidī connects his departure with going away angry at 21:87 and notes that discussion of idh follows two earlier stories. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says events between departure and the ship are omitted for concision; Abū Ḥayyān says al-Anbiyāʾ fills gaps before the ship and before prayer in the darkness. Ibn al-Jawzī refers back to discussions at the end of Yūnus and al-Anbiyāʾ 86; Ibn Kathīr recalls al-Anbiyāʾ. Asad points to 21:83. Islahi says Jonah's account also occurs in Yūnus and Anbiyāʾ and alleges Torah narrators distorted it. Kur'an Yolu notes his inclusion among revelatory prophets in 4:163 and in the seventeen listed at 6:86, the people's repentance in 10:98, his prayer and rescue in 21:87–88, and rebuke and favor in 68:48–50. - 37:140/q17/p2 The biblical account offers a fuller parallel with both agreements and disputed features. — Al-Biqāʿī's translated Book of Jonah gives commission to Nineveh, ship flight, storm, lot, sea, great fish, and prayer after three days and nights. Elmalılı and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī report the Tarshish voyage via Jaffa through al-Ālūsī; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds discarded cargo, a sleeping Jonah and the captain waking him. Kur'an Yolu says the Bible broadly matches the Qur'an, ending with prayer, shore, recommission and Nineveh's repentance. The Study Quran adds the tempest, Jonah's identification by lot, and a calmed sea after he was thrown. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the biblical attribution of fleeing an ongoing commission and resenting spared believers despite knowledge of their repentance; Islahi also says Torah narrators distorted the account.
