# 43:12: 19 questions

## 43:12/q01 How is 'the One who' connected to the preceding wording?  [وَٱلَّذِى · grammar]
- 43:12/q01/p1 It is nominative, coordinated with the preceding phrase. — Al-Naḥḥās gives nominative coordination as its iʿrāb. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 43:12/q02 What are the 'pairs' God created?  [ٱلْأَزْوَٰجَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether zawj names a kind, a sexual pair, contrasting things, all contingent beings, human conditions, plants, or mental and external forms; al-Ālūsī questions whether every created thing has an opposite.
- 43:12/q02/p1 All kinds, classes, and varieties of created things. — Muqātil, al-Zajjāj, al-Thaʿlabī, Saʿīd b. Jubayr as reported by al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Zamakhsharī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī use the broad sense of kinds. Al-Wāḥidī includes varieties, colors, and male and female. Ibn ʿAbbās, transmitted by al-Rāzī, al-Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī, illustrates kinds with sweet/sour, white/black, and male/female. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports kinds of beings with those examples. Al-Bursawī adduces 36:36, naming plants, people, and unknown things. Ibn ʿĀshūr says each zawj is the other's partner, commonly male/female livestock, but the usage extends to classes (6:143; 13:3); he allows either sense and notes livestock's milk, wool, hair, meat, and offspring. Ibn Kathīr includes plants, crops, fruits, flowers, other growth, and diverse animal genera and species. Al-Biqāʿī includes complementary members of all created classes, which attain perfection through one another, and links their creation to God alone. Asad attributes the kinds reading to Baghawī, Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, and Ibn Kathīr. Elmalılı reports it alongside a universal-contraries reading; Study Quran reports all animal species alongside other readings. [26 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ASAD-NOTES(Baghawi, Zamakhsharl, Baydawi, Ibn Kathir); STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 43:12/q02/p2 Male and female animals are the pairs. — Al-Ṭabarī describes males and females as mutually paired; Ibn ʿĪsā is reported by al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī. Al-Ṭabrisī gives this as one explanation. Ibn ʿĀshūr says this is a common use of zawj, cites 6:143 and 13:3, and allows paired animals here; Study Quran reports the view as male and female members of animal kinds. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عيسى); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 43:12/q02/p3 Contrasting entities and qualities, including natural and cosmic opposites, are pairs. — Al-Ḥasan, transmitted by al-Māwardī, al-Ṭabrisī, and al-Qurṭubī, lists winter/summer, night/day, heaven/earth, sun/moon, and Paradise/Fire. Al-Ṭabrisī also reports animal and inanimate forms and sweet/bitter or wet/dry; Asad attributes a general-opposites reading to Ibn ʿAbbās via al-Rāzī and describes al-Ṭabarī's reading as polarity throughout creation. Elmalılı reports all contraries; Study Quran reports all opposites and cites 36:36. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI(الحسن,قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES(Ibn 'Abbas,Tabari); STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 43:12/q02/p4 Everything other than God is paired, whereas its maker is without counterpart. — Scholars reported by al-Rāzī give above/below, directions, past/future, substances/attributes, and seasons; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmit cognate views, the last naming above/below, right/left, and male/female. Al-Ālūsī questions whether universal oppositeness applies and offers composition or contingency, including immaterial beings, as a possible basis. Al-Bursawī says created contingency points to the one maker. Asad reports al-Rāzī's complementary-pairs account and God's lack of opposite, likeness, or complement. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعض المحققين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ELMALILI(bazı muhakkıkīn); TABATABAI(قيل); ASAD-NOTES(Razi)]
- 43:12/q02/p5 The pairs include the alternating conditions of human life. — Al-Māwardī names good/evil, faith/unbelief, wealth/poverty, and health/illness. Al-Qurṭubī explicitly prefers a comprehensive reading encompassing these preceding views and adds benefit/harm. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 43:12/q02/p6 They can refer to pairs of plants. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this explanation and cites the verses about every beautiful pair and every noble pair (50:7; 26:7). [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 43:12/q02/p7 Things are paired as mental and external forms. — Elmalılı proposes this account and contrasts created things with God, who is not bounded by a mental form, knows Himself immediately, and is unlike anything (42:11). [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 43:12/q03 What is the form and lexical range of azwāj?  [ٱلْأَزْوَٰجَ · grammar]
- 43:12/q03/p1 It is the plural of zawj, a partner in a pair, with an extended sense of kinds. — Al-Naḥḥās says the expected azwuj pattern was avoided because moving the wāw would be heavy and compares azmān. Ibn ʿĀshūr says either partner is the other's zawj; male/female livestock is common usage, extended to classes, with 6:143 and 13:3. Asad notes the literal sense 'all pairs.' [3 notes: NAHHAS; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 43:12/q04 What does created pairhood imply about God and creation?  [خَلَقَ ٱلْأَزْوَٰجَ كُلَّهَا · theology]
- 43:12/q04/p1 Pairhood marks created things as contingent and dependent; God alone is absolutely one and without counterpart. — Al-Rāzī says paired beings are originated and preceded by nonexistence; God has no rival, opposite, or helper. His five comparisons favor the unit: a pair needs two units; even numbers divide equally and thus submit to division; an odd number divides into an even and odd part whereas an even one's halves are even; counterparts share and so limit their perfections; and shared plus differing features make each member dependent. Abū Ḥayyān says a singular maker transcends opposites. Al-Bursawī draws the same inference from contingency. Al-Ālūsī reports the contrast but doubts universal oppositeness, suggesting God's uncomposed unity versus contingent composition, even for immaterial beings. Elmalılı transmits the singularity view and proposes mental/external pairing while God knows Himself immediately (42:11). Asad links the claim to 112:4. Study Quran reports al-Rāzī's contrast between relative, multiple creation and the Absolute Creator beyond duality. [16 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ELMALILI(bazı muhakkıkīn); ASAD-NOTES(Razi); STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)]

## 43:12/q05 What is the grammatical force of 'all'?  [كُلَّهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether kullahā is classified as emphasis or an adjective.
- 43:12/q05/p1 It emphasizes azwāj. — Al-Naḥḥās calls it emphasis and says kullahā is used for a large plural, whereas kullahunna is used for a small plural. [1 notes: NAHHAS]
- 43:12/q05/p2 It is an adjective modifying azwāj. — Al-Naḥḥās reports that some grammarians call it an adjective rather than emphasis. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 43:12/q06 Why use 'made' for ships and livestock?  [وَجَعَلَ لَكُم · rhetoric]
- 43:12/q06/p1 Making fits crafted ships, while created livestock are supplied and trained for people's use. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says ships are manufactured, livestock were already covered by 'created the pairs,' and human thought enables the devising of ships and training of animals. Al-Zajjāj says God created and subjected both sorts of mounts. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ZAJJAJ]

## 43:12/q07 What does min contribute before 'ships'?  [مِّنَ ٱلْفُلْكِ · grammar]
- 43:12/q07/p1 It is partitive: some ships and livestock are provided as mounts. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya explicitly calls min partitive. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 43:12/q08 What are al-fulk here?  [ٱلْفُلْكِ · referent]
- 43:12/q08/p1 Ships or vessels used at sea. — Muqātil calls them vessels; al-Ṭabarī, al-Māwardī, and al-Qurṭubī say ships. Al-Biqāʿī specifies large seagoing ships. Ibn ʿĀshūr sets them opposite the land-travel camels and cites 36:41–42. [6 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:12/q09 Why are ships named before livestock?  [ٱلْفُلْكِ وَٱلْأَنْعَٰمِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether sound and display of power, or avoidance of repetition after azwāj, governs the order.
- 43:12/q09/p1 Ships display God's power and wisdom, and the order preserves the ending sound. — Al-Bursawī says the explanatory phrase precedes what it explains to preserve the final n-sound; ships come before livestock because they show divine power and wisdom. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 43:12/q09/p2 Ships introduce a fresh gift after the mention of pairs, before livestock return as an anticipated topic. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says pairs already included livestock but not ships, so immediate mention of livestock would repeat. He compares Imru al-Qays's sequence from pleasure-riding a horse to embracing a woman rather than riding a war horse, because the rides differ. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:12/q10 Which anʿām are meant as mounts?  [ٱلْأَنْعَٰمِ · referent]
turns on: Whether anʿām here retains its broad livestock sense, denotes the camels and cattle named in reports, or is restricted by ordinary riding custom to camels; al-Qurṭubī invokes the cow report against cattle.
- 43:12/q10/p1 Camels are the livestock intended for riding here. — Muʿādh, as transmitted by al-Māwardī, and an unnamed view in al-Ṭabrisī specify camels. Al-Qurṭubī holds this and explicitly calls Abū Muʿādh's camel-only view correct: a prophetic report has a ridden cow say it was made for plowing, not riding; the Prophet, Abū Bakr, and ʿUmar affirm its speech, though the last two were absent. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says camels alone among livestock are ridden, while horses, mules, and donkeys enter the broader class of mounts. Abū Ḥayyān says custom restricts ridden livestock to camels. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the traveled mount as camels and says Arabs call them ships of the land, comparing 36:41–42. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(معاذ); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو معاذ)+]
- 43:12/q10/p2 Camels and cattle are the livestock named. — Muqātil and Saʿīd b. Jubayr as transmitted by al-Māwardī, al-Ṭabrisī, and al-Qurṭubī give camels and cattle. Al-Qurṭubī argues for camels alone instead: the cow in a prophetic report says it was created for plowing rather than riding. [4 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); TABRISI(سعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو معاذ,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)-]
- 43:12/q10/p3 Livestock or beasts of burden generally are among the mounts. — Al-Ṭabarī glosses anʿām as livestock including beasts of burden (3:14) and names camels, horses, mules, and donkeys among land mounts. Al-Biqāʿī calls them livestock used on land. Ibn Kathīr notes their riding and other uses; Study Quran describes cattle as transport and other benefits. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 43:12/q11 What is mā and what is understood after 'you ride'?  [مَا تَرْكَبُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā is a relative requiring an understood object or a verbal noun requiring none.
- 43:12/q11/p1 Mā is a relative pronoun meaning the thing or kind ridden, with an omitted object pronoun. — Ibn al-Jawzī gives the relative sense 'that which.' Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn restore mā tarkabūnahu; al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise describe the omitted object. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says mā denotes the kind of mount, to which the following pronoun refers. Al-Naḥḥās says the pronoun can be omitted because of the long separation, while masculine or collective feminine agreement could be explicit. Ibn ʿĀshūr says min al-fulk wa-l-anʿām explains the relative mā and calls deletion of its connected accusative pronoun common. Al-Ālūsī cites 29:65 and 16:8 in explaining the ship/mount constructions. [9 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 43:12/q11/p2 Mā can be a verbal noun, so no omitted pronoun is needed. — Al-Naḥḥās gives this as an alternative to the relative analysis. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 43:12/q12 How does one verb for riding apply to animals and ships?  [مَا تَرْكَبُونَ · grammar]
- 43:12/q12/p1 The direct construction used for animals is extended to ships and predominates over the ship construction with fī. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Samīn, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr contrast direct animal-riding with riding in ships; most cite the direct pattern's strength. Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī cite 29:65 for ships and 16:8 for animals. Al-Bayḍāwī adds possible preference for animals made for riding or common over rare usage. Al-Bursawī describes the same sea/land contrast. Abū Ḥayyān says rakiba can directly take an object where another construction takes fī, although he calls the verb intransitive with respect to its complement here. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the object is clear, so direct transitivity generalizes and the relative object pronoun is omitted; literally one mounts an animal, while ship boarding is a figurative extension through likeness of motion (11:41). [13 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 43:12/q13 How does agreement with mā explain the following verse's 'backs' and pronouns?  [مَا تَرْكَبُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether form, collective sense, alternation between them, or nontrue feminine gender accounts for the agreement.
- 43:12/q13/p1 Masculine agreement follows the grammatical form of the relative mā. — Abū ʿUbayda, as transmitted by al-Rāzī, explains the masculine by the understood 'what you ride'; al-Majāz likewise says the masculine follows mā. Abū Ḥayyān objects to using mā tarkabūn as a simple parallel, since agreement can shift from form to sense and back in the following verse. [2 notes: MAJAZ; RAZI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 43:12/q13/p2 Plural 'backs' follows the collective sense of singular mā. — Al-Farrāʾ, reported by al-Thaʿlabī and al-Rāzī, compares singular collectives such as army, host, and cavalry. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the pronoun can refer to the kind of mount expressed by mā. [3 notes: THALABI(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 43:12/q13/p3 Agreement can move from mā's form to its collective sense and back to singular form. — Abū Ḥayyān says the next verse can use plural 'backs' according to sense and then a singular pronoun according to form; he rejects mā tarkabūn as an opposing parallel (43:13). [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 43:12/q13/p4 The feminine gender here is not true grammatical gender, allowing forms to vary. — Al-Rāzī reports this third explanation and compares a phrase about one among women who agrees with you. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 43:12/q14 What benefits and lesson do the provided mounts convey?  [وَجَعَلَ لَكُم مِّنَ ٱلْفُلْكِ وَٱلْأَنْعَٰمِ مَا تَرْكَبُونَ · interpretation]
- 43:12/q14/p1 God supplies and makes sea and land transport usable for people's travel and livelihood as a divine blessing. — Al-Ṭabarī names sea travel and livelihood and travel by camels, horses, mules, and donkeys to destinations. Al-Māwardī, al-Baghawī, al-Rāzī, and al-Biqāʿī contrast land and sea journeys; al-Biqāʿī calls both gifts. Al-Zajjāj says God created and subjected both. Ibn Kathīr adds eating animal meat and drinking milk. Study Quran calls seafaring and cattle blessings, adding loads, warmth/clothing, food/drink, and beauty as signs of beneficence, with 14:32, 17:66, 22:65, 30:46, 6:142, 16:5, 16:66, 16:79, 23:21–22, 36:72, and 40:79. Kuranyolu says riding, load-carrying, guarding, plowing, and threshing depend on domestication; without it those services fail. Study Quran says subjection of creation reveals dependence on Divine Aid (31:20). [11 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]

## 43:12/q15 What does iqran in the rider's response mean?  [مَا تَرْكَبُونَ · meaning]
- 43:12/q15/p1 It means having the ability or capacity to manage the mount. — Al-Ṭabrisī illustrates it with saying one can handle a camel when able to manage it. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 43:12/q16 What travel remembrance or instruction is associated with these mounts?  [مَا تَرْكَبُونَ · reports]
- 43:12/q16/p1 Riders should invoke God's name and recognize that God carries and enables them. — Abū Lās al-Khuzāʿī reports the Prophet saying every camel has a devil on its hump: riders should name God, tame the camel for their needs, and remember God carries them. Ibn Kathīr gives a parallel report with a devil on every camel's back and the instruction to say God's name and then pursue one's needs without restraint; he identifies Abū Lās as Muḥammad b. al-Aswad b. Khalaf. Study Quran says the supplication is commonly used on land journeys, while 11:41 is advised for sea journeys, and cites 28:85. Its account of human dependence on Divine Aid cites 31:20. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو لاس الخزاعي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]

## 43:12/q17 How does this verse's sequence connect to its context?  [* · links]
- 43:12/q17/p1 The divine acts are blessings and proof against polytheism, moving from life's supports to livelihood and travel. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls these acts blessings for humanity and proof against every disbelieving polytheist. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the passage shifts from supports of life to production and trade travel and repeats the relative noun. Al-Biqāʿī says the widened notion of pairs includes contrasting transport, sea ships and land livestock. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 43:12/q18 Do the second-person pronouns in this passage constitute a rhetorical shift?  [* · rhetoric]
- 43:12/q18/p1 No; the eleven second-person pronouns across the four verses continue the expected address. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says they are not iltifāt. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:12/q19 How does the sūrah's larger theme frame this verse?  [* · links]
- 43:12/q19/p1 It belongs to a unified challenge to the Makkan idolaters' worldview and objections. — Iṣlāḥī says every part of Sūrah al-Zukhruf relates deeply to its central theme and the whole sūrah is harmonious. Study Quran says the sūrah answers Makkan idolaters' objections and addresses forms of disbelief. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
