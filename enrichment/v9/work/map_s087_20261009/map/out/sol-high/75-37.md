# 75:37: 14 questions

## 75:37/q01 How is ألم يك read, and whom does its verb address?  [أَلَمْ يَكُ · readings]
turns on: Whether the initial verb has yāʾ or tāʾ, and whether the latter is understood as direct address.
- 75:37/q01/p1 With yāʾ, it speaks of the human being in the third person. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls this the majority reading; al-Samīn calls it the common reading and takes its pronoun back to the human; Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise makes the subject of kāna an implicit pronoun referring to the human. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); TABATABAI]
- 75:37/q01/p2 With tāʾ, it addresses the person directly. — Al-Ḥasan is named for this reading. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls its tāʾ feminine; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī explain it as a shift to second-person address, with al-Samīn taking the turn as a rebuke. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]

## 75:37/q02 What is conveyed by the shortened يك without the nūn of يكن?  [يَكُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the omission is taken as a cue to haste, humble size, incomplete causation or merely as an attested form.
- 75:37/q02/p1 Its brevity suits the sūra's haste and urgency. — Al-Biqāʿī links the omission to urgency as well as the lowliness of the origin. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the verb finishes quickly and calls this question another instance of haste-shaped omission. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:37/q02/p2 The omission evokes the small and humble beginning of the human. — Al-Biqāʿī connects it with the insignificant origin. Al-Zarkashī, as reported by al-Sāmarrāʾī, contrasts the initial drop with the later manifest disputant, citing 36:77. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR(الزركشي)]
- 75:37/q02/p3 It hints that semen alone is only part of the cause of a human being. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says semen must meet the ovum before it makes a human. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:37/q02/p4 It matches the sperm's incomplete, unfertilized state and an unfinished event. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī ties the omitted nūn to that incompleteness. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 75:37/q02/p5 It is one occurrence in the Qur'anic inventory of nūn-less yak. — Al-Zarkashī's Burhān lists this occurrence as al-m yak nuṭfatan and records other occurrences across several sūras, without giving a reason for the shortening in this note. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 75:37/q03 Whose origin is invoked in ألم يك نطفة?  [أَلَمْ يَكُ نُطْفَةًۭ · referent]
turns on: Whether the passage is read as a general address to humanity, a targeted rebuke of a resurrection denier, or specifically about Abū Jahl.
- 75:37/q03/p1 The human being or person under discussion, in general. — Muqātil, al-Wāḥidī's Wajīz, al-Bayḍāwī, Ibn Kathīr and others describe the human's beginning as emitted semen; al-Wāḥidī's Basīṭ says it is the person just mentioned, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī makes that human the implicit subject of kāna. [7 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 75:37/q03/p2 The denier of resurrection is confronted with his own origin. — Al-Ṭabarī identifies the addressee by his denial and uses his beginning as evidence that God can recreate him after death; he mentions 16:4. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]
- 75:37/q03/p3 The passage concerns Abū Jahl ibn Hishām. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this as the view of most interpreters. Al-Būrsawī relates a confrontation between the Prophet and Abū Jahl at al-Baṭḥāʾ and Abū Jahl's later death at Badr. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور المتأولين); BURSEVI]

## 75:37/q04 What does نطفة denote here?  [نُطْفَةًۭ · meaning]
- 75:37/q04/p1 A drop or quantity of reproductive fluid or water from which a human forms. — Al-Jishumī says it originally means a small amount of water but can also be larger, and identifies it with male reproductive fluid; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Būrsawī likewise allow little or much, the latter specifying clear water. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī stress a little water; al-Qurṭubī derives it from natafa, 'to drip.' Al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī mention the man's loins and woman's ribs; al-Biqāʿī says loins and breastbones. Al-Qurṭubī calls it a drop of semen poured into the womb. [8 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 75:37/q05 What does مني denote here?  [مَّنِىٍّۢ · meaning]
- 75:37/q05/p1 Semen, the reproductive fluid from which a human or animal forms. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya identifies maniyy as semen. Al-Būrsawī says it is the reproductive fluid of man and woman from which an animal is formed. Al-Biqāʿī relates it to fluid from the man's loins and woman's breastbones; al-Wāḥidī calls it the source from which the human is formed. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 75:37/q06 What does يمنى mean here?  [يُمْنَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb describes discharge, predetermination, bringing into being or mixing the fluids.
- 75:37/q06/p1 The fluid is emitted or poured, especially into the womb. — Al-Māwardī gives this as one explanation and connects the name Minā with blood spilled there; al-Qurṭubī and al-Būrsawī also mention spilled sacrificial blood at Minā. Al-Wāḥidī points back to 53:46 and 56:58; al-Rāzī also cites those verses. Ibn ʿAṭiyya permits the passive of a man's ejaculation; al-Jishumī specifies emission from the male sexual organ. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says formation requires this emission. Al-Rāzī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, al-Baghawī, al-Būrsawī and others specify the womb. [18 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عامر، حفص، أبو عمرو، ابن محيصن، الجحدري، سلام، يعقوب,جمهور السبعة); TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; FARISI-HUJJA]
- 75:37/q06/p2 The human's creation or outcomes are measured, decreed or apportioned from the semen. — Al-Fārisī explains yumnā as determination from semen and quotes three unattributed poetic lines, two using منت and المنا (the latter glossed 'fate drove it'), and a further line beginning منت لك أن تلقى ابن هند منية. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says this may derive from God's creating and determining creatures. Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī give measured or decreed; al-Biqāʿī says God deliberately apportioned the drop. Al-Rāzī gives this sense for the yāʾ reading. [9 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عامر، حفص، أبو عمرو، ابن محيصن، الجحدري، سلام، يعقوب,جمهور السبعة); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 75:37/q06/p3 It means brought forth or created. — Al-Māwardī reports this from Yazīd ibn ʿĀmir and cites Yazīd's verse using the term for what a maker brings about. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(يزيد بن عامر)]
- 75:37/q06/p4 It means combined, referring to the mingling of male and female fluids. — Al-Māwardī reports this as a third explanation. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 75:37/q07 How is يمنى read, and what does its verb agree with?  [يُمْنَىٰ · readings]
turns on: Grammatical agreement with feminine nuṭfa or masculine maniyy, and whether a masculine pronoun can refer back to nuṭfa by sense.
- 75:37/q07/p1 With tāʾ, tumnā agrees with the feminine drop, nuṭfa. — Al-Ṭabarī assigns it to most Medinan and Kufan readers; al-Fārisī names Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim, Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī, and records varying Abū ʿAmr routes. Ibn al-Jawzī names the same five and allows both from Abū ʿAmr. Al-Fārisī supports feminine agreement by 53:46, من نطفة إذا تمنى. Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Qurṭubī report Abū Ḥātim's preference for it. Al-Ṭabarī accepts both readings as sound; Ibn Khālawayh calls this the readers' agreed form except for Ḥafṣ. Reports differ on Abū ʿAmr, Yaʿqūb and which readers are included among the rest. [23 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو,ابن كثير ونافع وعاصم في رواية أبي بكر وحمزة والكسائي); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(حفص عن عاصم، والحسن، والآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور السبعة); TABRISI(حفص ورويس والباقون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمرو,ابن كثير ونافع وحمزة والكسائي وأبو بكر عن عاصم); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور); QIRAAT-ER(Imp); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حاتم)+; THALABI(الباقون وأبو حاتم)+]
- 75:37/q07/p2 With yāʾ, yumnā agrees with the masculine semen, maniyy. — Al-Farrāʾ reports ʿAbd Allāh's companions and some Medinans for tāʾ; al-Ṭabarī assigns yāʾ to some Meccan and Basran readers. Ibn Khālawayh names only Ḥafṣ from ʿĀṣim, whereas al-Fārisī also names al-Mufaḍḍal and Ibn ʿĀmir and records varying Abū ʿAmr routes. Al-Thaʿlabī names al-Ḥasan, Ibn Muḥayṣin, Abū ʿAmr, Yaʿqūb, Sallām, al-Mufaḍḍal and Ḥafṣ; al-Baghawī names Ḥafṣ and al-Ḥasan. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn al-Jawzī give further lists, including disagreements over Yaʿqūb and Abū ʿAmr. Al-Ṭabrisī names Ḥafṣ and Ruways. Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Qurṭubī report Abū ʿUbayd's preference on agreement with maniyy; al-Naḥḥās adds that maniyy is nearer. Al-Fārisī allows poured or decreed as the sense. [23 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو,حفص عن عاصم وابن عامر); THALABI(الحسن وابن محيصن وأبو عمرو ويعقوب وسلام); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(حفص عن عاصم، والحسن، والآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عامر، حفص، أبو عمرو، ابن محيصن، الجحدري، سلام، يعقوب); TABRISI(حفص ورويس والباقون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمرو,ابن عامر وحفص عن عاصم ويعقوب); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عامر وحفص); SAMIN-DURR(حفص); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(حفص وأبو عمرو ويعقوب وسلام والجحدري وابن محيصن); QURTUBI-FULL(حفص)+; THALABI(أبو عبيد)+]
- 75:37/q07/p3 Even with yāʾ, its pronoun could refer to nuṭfa, construed as water despite its feminine form. — Abū al-Baqāʾ permits this because nuṭfa has nonliteral feminine gender and means water. Al-Samīn objects that it works only under Ibn Kaysān's view; other grammarians allow such agreement only as poetic necessity, illustrated by a line with أبقل. [0 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-]

## 75:37/q08 What grammatical role does the يمنى or تمنى clause have?  [يُمْنَىٰ نُطْفَةًۭ مَّنِىٍّۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the relative clause qualifies genitive maniyy or accusative nuṭfa.
- 75:37/q08/p1 When the pronoun refers to maniyy, the clause describes the genitive semen. — Al-Samīn gives this analysis for Ḥafṣ's yāʾ reading, taking the sense as 'semen is poured.' [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(حفص)]
- 75:37/q08/p2 When the pronoun refers to nuṭfa, the clause is an accusative adjective of the accusative drop. — Al-Samīn gives this for feminine tumnā and also for the proposed yāʾ analysis that refers to nuṭfa. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]

## 75:37/q09 Why is the emission expressed with an unstated agent?  [يُمْنَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the unstated agent foregrounds human lack of choice or the passage's pattern and pace.
- 75:37/q09/p1 It shows that the person has no choice in the semen's emission into the womb. — Al-Biqāʿī treats the passive as reflecting the person's lack of agency in his own origin. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 75:37/q09/p2 The omitted agent matches later passive verbs and the sūra's urgent tone. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī connects the omission with 'left aimless,' 'created,' 'fashioned' and 'made' in the surrounding sequence. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:37/q10 Why specify that the semen was emitted?  [مَّنِىٍّۢ يُمْنَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the specified emission points to human lowliness or to a necessary stage of generation.
- 75:37/q10/p1 The detail stresses a lowly, even indecent origin and rebukes human rebellion. — Al-Rāzī says the wording recalls the filthy outlet yet expresses the bodily function indirectly; he compares 5:75, where Jesus and Mary eating food alludes to their need to relieve themselves. Al-Būrsawī says it belittles the human while displaying God's power to fashion him, and reports another view using the same food analogy to ask how such a creature could rebel. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
- 75:37/q10/p2 The emission is a necessary step for formation. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says that without semen entering the womb, formation does not occur. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:37/q11 What does the interrogation do here?  [أَلَمْ يَكُ · rhetoric]
- 75:37/q11/p1 It rebukes the addressee for denying resurrection or accountability. — Ibn al-Shaykh, reported by al-Būrsawī, calls this a second resurrection proof and the question a rebuke. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the repeated question strengthens rebuke after the account of Judgment and reckoning, comparing 23:115; al-Samīn says the direct-address reading sharpens the rebuke. [3 notes: BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الحسن)]

## 75:37/q12 What does the human's first creation establish?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the origin is used to prove capacity to recreate, the wisdom of imposing duties, or the impropriety of arrogance.
- 75:37/q12/p1 God can resurrect a human after death, since He first created him from a weak drop. — Al-Ṭabarī cites 16:4; Ibn Kathīr stresses the insignificant drop from loins to womb; al-Būrsawī calls first creation an answer to the alleged difficulty of restoration, and reports Ibn al-Shaykh's second proof. Ibn ʿĀshūr follows successive stages from weak material through bodily decay to 75:40, with 21:104 as an echo. Al-Iṣlāḥī mentions perfection and granted abilities; al-Sāmarrāʾī traces the drop, clot, formation and male and female. Al-Zajjāj and al-Ālūsī frame it as the response to denial. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 75:37/q12/p2 Changing stages reveal a wise maker who would not leave mature humans without religious responsibility. — Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī say God gave maturity, reason, capacity and desire, so He would not leave the person without duties. Al-Elmalılı says the origin refutes the idea of being left purposeless. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; ELMALILI]
- 75:37/q12/p3 The lowly beginning calls for humility rather than rebellion against God. — Al-Rāzī points to the filthy route of emission; al-Būrsawī says the wording both belittles humans and shows God's power to shape them, and reports the challenge to human rebellion. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 75:37/q13 How does this verse connect to the surrounding argument and the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Which span of context is in view: the immediately preceding purpose question, the sūra's opening, or the following stages of creation.
- 75:37/q13/p1 It moves from the question of being left purposeless to material proof of resurrection and the final question. — Al-Zajjāj moves from rejection of aimlessness to God's power to initiate creation; al-Ālūsī calls it a new sentence rebutting denial after 75:36. Al-Elmalılı says the origin refutes aimlessness and prepares the concluding resurrection proof. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the question follows the Judgment account, and verses 37–40 supply material proof for 75:40; the wisdom argument was already included in sudā, and revival entails recompense. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 75:37/q13/p2 The conclusion returns to the opening concern with bones and resurrection for gathering. — Al-Biqāʿī connects the collected bones at the opening with resurrection for the Day of Gathering at the close. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 75:37/q13/p3 The passage names successive stages of human creation, starting with semen and then the clot. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī identifies the two stages here as the semen drop and clot; in another note he extends the sequence through formation and the creation of male and female before the resurrection conclusion. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 75:37/q14 What is reported about Abū Jahl in connection with this passage?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which reported saying or episode about Abū Jahl is being related.
- 75:37/q14/p1 The Prophet warned him at al-Baṭḥāʾ; he boasted of safety, then was struck down at Badr. — Al-Būrsawī reports that the Prophet seized and shook Abū Jahl; the sons of ʿAfrāʾ felled him at Badr and Ibn Masʿūd finished him. Al-Būrsawī explains aqaṣahu as killing on the spot and ajhaza as finishing a nearly dead wounded person. [2 notes: BURSEVI]
- 75:37/q14/p2 The Prophet called Abū Jahl the Pharaoh of this community. — Al-Būrsawī reports the Prophet's statement that every community has a Pharaoh. [1 notes: BURSEVI(رسول الله)]
