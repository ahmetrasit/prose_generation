# 7:180: 34 questions

## 7:180/q01 What occasion prompted the verse?  [وَلِلَّهِ ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · reports]
- 7:180/q01/p1 A man used Allah and al-Raḥmān in prayer; a Meccan, usually Abū Jahl, mocked the apparent invocation of two gods, so the verse affirmed the one God's many names. — Maqātil reports the Prophet authorizing either name; al-Thaʿlabī leaves the mocker unnamed. Al-Qurṭubī's version has Raḥmān and Raḥīm. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the mockery contrarian, not ignorance of mercy. [9 notes: MUQATIL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية وغيره)]

## 7:180/q02 What is the force of fronting 'to God'?  [وَلِلَّهِ ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 7:180/q02/p1 Fronting God's possession emphasizes or restricts true ownership of every beautiful name to Him. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says it rebuts the charge that several names mean several gods, citing 17:110, 20:8 and 59:24. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī infers generality from the definite plural and rejects a merely familiar known set; al-Ālūsī notes a tautology if the labels themselves are already exclusive. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:180/q03 What are the 'names' in this phrase?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether they are spoken designations, qualities, or the named reality.
- 7:180/q03/p1 They are words designating the one God, signifying varied qualities. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says this is the agreed exegesis; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn distinguish designations from the act of naming. Al-Rāzī reasons from plurality and genitive construction that name differs from named; al-Ghazālī distinguishes name, naming, named. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes proper, customary and descriptive expressions. Al-Qurṭubī cites the Prophet's five names as evidence that several designations can belong to one person. Al-Ālūsī rejects an argument that a missing pronoun proves mere human designations. [23 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(آخرون,ابن الحصار,ابن عطية,القاضي أبو بكر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); JISHUMI; BURSEVI(الإمام الغزالي); ALUSI-FULL(حجة الإسلام العزالي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور)+]
- 7:180/q03/p2 They mean God's attributes or qualities. — Al-Zamakhsharī specifies justice, beneficence and transcendence from creaturely likeness; al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī cite 'name' in the sense of celebrated description. Asad derives this from ism; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī uses it to explain exclusivity. [13 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(آخرون); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,بعض العلماء); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(most Islamic thinkers); ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور)-]
- 7:180/q03/p3 A divine name can be the named reality itself. — Ibn al-ʿArabī argues that otherwise God's names would belong to another; the Study Quran reports the esoteric view that God is present in His sanctified Names. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أهل الحق,ابن العربي); STUDYQURAN(Islamic esoterism)]
- 7:180/q03/p4 A divine name can be an attribute connected with the named God, distinct from the act of naming. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this as another account among the people of truth. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أهل الحق)]
- 7:180/q03/p5 The word name can be used for both God as named and for the designations. — Ibn al-Ḥaṣṣār says the pronouns refer respectively to God and the names; the Prophet's five-names report supports the distinction. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الحصار)]

## 7:180/q04 Why are the names called most beautiful, and which qualify?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · theology]
turns on: Whether all fitting names or especially appealing mercy names are intended, and whether beauty lies in meaning or authorization.
- 7:180/q04/p1 All God's names are beautiful because they convey true perfection, praise or freedom from defect. — Ibn ʿAbbās affirms all names; examples include Raḥmān, Raḥīm, Malik, Quddūs, Salām, ʿAzīz and Jabbār. Al-Rāzī says God alone has perfection independently. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Jabbār and Mutakabbir befit the self-sufficient God though blameworthy in needy creatures. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī excludes bodily-limited traits such as courage and chastity. Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds revealed authorization to beauty of meaning; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī disputes making revelation the exegetical test while advising it for formal naming. [50 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(حجة الإسلام العزالي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Ebû Bekir İbnü’l-Arabî); STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭabrisī,some commentators)]
- 7:180/q04/p2 The intended names are those especially pleasing to hearts, such as Mercy and Forgiveness rather than Wrath and Punishment. — Al-Māwardī supplies this alternative; later sources repeat it. The Study Quran says it encourages asking for mercy through Names of Beauty. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]

## 7:180/q05 How is al-ḥusnā formed and construed with the plural?  [ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Feminine superlative versus adjectival verbal noun.
- 7:180/q05/p1 It is the feminine of aḥsan, singular feminine with a nonrational plural. — Al-Thaʿlabī compares kubrā/akbar; Abū Ḥayyān notes plural agreement ḥusun is possible; al-Samīn prefers this and cites 2:184. Al-Zarkashī parallels 'upper heavens' and 'earlier generations.' Ibn ʿĀshūr reads intrinsic excellence, not private preference. [13 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); STUDYQURAN; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 7:180/q05/p2 It is a verbal noun used adjectivally. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya offers this alternative; al-Samīn cites poetry on the fuʿlā pattern, like rujʿā and buqyā. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(unidentified exegete); SAMIN-DURR(الشاعر)]

## 7:180/q06 How is the hamza of al-asmāʾ treated at pause?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ · readings]
- 7:180/q06/p1 At pause on al-asmāʾ al-ḥusnā, Ḥamza has naql or sakt combined with five final-hamza treatments. — Ibn al-Jazarī counts ten transmitted routes and rejects unsupported full realization without sakt; predecessors agree on naql at pause. [3 notes: NASHR]

## 7:180/q07 How many beautiful names are there?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · theology]
turns on: Whether the ninety-nine are an exhaustive set or a set carrying a special promise.
- 7:180/q07/p1 A recognized set numbers ninety-nine; enumerating them brings Paradise. — Prophetic reports through Abū Hurayra, Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn ʿUmar sometimes add that God is One and loves oddness. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭībī infer a known bounded set from the definite article; transmitted lists begin Allah, Raḥmān, Raḥīm, Malik, Quddūs. [27 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNABIHATIM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI(أبو هريرة); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة عن النبي); TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(الحديث); ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله جعفر بن محمد الصادق عليه السلام,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي عليه السلام); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hz. Peygamber); STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-; IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 7:180/q07/p2 God has more than ninety-nine names; the report marks ninety-nine with a special reward. — Ibn Kathīr and al-Ālūsī cite Ibn Masʿūd's supplication including names hidden in God's knowledge. Al-Nawawī reports consensus on the special promise. Ibn al-ʿArabī transmits one thousand, four thousand and nearly innumerable estimates; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes differing lists and a report of 360 names. Al-Qurṭubī documented over two hundred. Al-Ālūsī calls the inference of a known fixed set from definiteness awkward. [21 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو بكر بن العربي); BURSEVI(شيخنا); ALUSI-FULL(أبو بكر ابن العربي,النووي); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 7:180/q07/p3 The scope of names is limitless with God's acts, relations, wisdom and created signs. — Al-Rāzī says negative and relational predications have no bound; insight into creation opens further knowledge. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the Qurʾanic phrase generically. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 7:180/q08 What does enumerating the ninety-nine names entail?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · interpretation]
- 7:180/q08/p1 It entails counting and memorizing them, believing and revering them, and reflecting on their meanings. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds aspiring through the names; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says faith in all their meanings; Kuran Yolu adds internalizing their beliefs. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي); TABATABAI(علي عليه السلام); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hz. Peygamber)]

## 7:180/q09 What is the status of the detailed ninety-nine-name lists?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · reports]
turns on: Whether the itemized lists are part of the sound core saying and whether every entry occurs in the Qurʾan.
- 7:180/q09/p1 The core promise is sound; the Tirmidhī itemized list is solitary, anomalous in entries, and may be a narrator's insertion. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the list non-mutawātir; al-Tirmidhī calls it gharīb through trustworthy Ṣafwān. Abū Ḥayyān says even calling the core technically mutawātir is imprecise. Ibn Kathīr follows hadith specialists on insertion. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī finds a sura list repeating names and totaling 110; Ahl al-Bayt lists differ. Abū Ḥayyān lists explanatory books by al-Ghazālī, Ibn Barrajān, al-Rāzī and Ibn al-ʿArabī. [28 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الترمذي عن أبي هريرة,النبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الترمذي); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله جعفر بن محمد الصادق عليه السلام); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bazı âlimler,İbn Hacer el-Askalânî); STUDYQURAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 7:180/q09/p2 A transmitted list can serve as a traditional enumeration, although wordings and entries differ. — Al-Bursawī cites a list-bearing hadith; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports several chains; al-Ālūsī records different sets. [7 notes: BURSEVI(الحديث); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(علي عليه السلام); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 7:180/q09/p3 All ninety-nine are in the Qurʾan. — A report adds this phrase; al-Ṣādiq apportions names across suras. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects a literal claim because lists include non-Qurʾanic wording and one totals 110. Ibn Ḥajar compiled a new Qurʾanic list. [3 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله جعفر بن محمد الصادق عليه السلام,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Hacer el-Askalânî); TABATABAI-]

## 7:180/q10 How are the divine names classified?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · theology]
- 7:180/q10/p1 They divide into essential, active and negative attributes. — Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī, the Najmiyya and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī give these kinds; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes nonrelational self-attributes, self-attributes with relations, and purely relational terms. Malik and mulk denote absolute dominion. [12 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(most Islamic thinkers)]
- 7:180/q10/p2 Names may express real, relational or privative attributes, alone or in combinations. — Al-Rāzī gives existence, unity and life; mentioned and known; holy and peace; then knowledge, power, Eternal, First and Wise as combinations. His further division is necessary, possible and impossible; al-Ālūsī reports similar necessary, impossible and other terms, with some usable only in constructions such as Reviver of decayed bones. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض الناس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Ebû Bekir İbnü’l-Arabî)]
- 7:180/q10/p3 Names can be grouped by divine reality, their effects, and qualities humans should emulate. — Al-Nasafī places Eternal, Powerful and Knowing in the first class; Forgiving, Merciful and Forbearing in the second; generosity in the third. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says specific names nest under broader ones, as hearing and sight under knowledge. [5 notes: NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 7:180/q10/p4 They can also be grouped as Names of Beauty and Names of Majesty. — The Study Quran gives Compassionate and Merciful for Beauty, Judge and Compeller for Majesty, and notes that the science of names includes al-jafr, a symbolic numerology of letters. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 7:180/q11 Which is the Greatest Name, and what makes it greatest?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · theology]
turns on: Whether it is Allah as an all-comprehensive designation, a real divine manifestation, or an effective verbal formula.
- 7:180/q11/p1 Allah is the all-encompassing essence-name and is often called the Greatest Name. — Al-Rāzī locates the Greatest Name in the essence-name; al-Bursawī says Allah comprehends all perfections. Al-Ālūsī reports a majority; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī treats Allah as an exclusive proper name, outside a technical attribute-name count except as subject. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:180/q11/p2 The Greatest Name is a reality or manifestation, with names ordered by range of effect. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says a word or thought cannot exert cosmic power; genuine turning to God meets the suitable name's reality. He takes seventy-three 'letters' as apportioned parts rather than ordinary letters. Reports assign seventy-two to Muhammad and one to God's hidden knowledge. [14 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,الباقر عليه السلام); TABATABAI+]
- 7:180/q11/p3 Reports and practitioners treat the Greatest Name as an unknown verbal formula or letter composition. — Cited reports connect it to the basmala, Āṣif's transport of Bilqīs's throne, and seventy-three letters. Practitioners propose variable letters and extraction methods; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects sound as the cause. [6 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,أصحاب العزائم والدعوات,الباقر عليه السلام); TABATABAI-]

## 7:180/q12 What numerical scheme is given for the divine names?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · inward]
- 7:180/q12/p1 A symbolic scheme generates counts of names from root attributes and worlds. — Al-Bursawī's shaykh maps life, knowledge, will and power onto realms, deriving 16, 32, 33, 99 and 1,001 names; the count of 100 recalls thirty-threefold prayer remembrances and a final tahlīl. [5 notes: BURSEVI(شيخنا)]

## 7:180/q13 Are God's attributes identical with His essence?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · theology]
- 7:180/q13/p1 Divine attributes are identical with the indivisible essence in reality, differing only in concept. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says real difference would impose limits, composition and dependence, rejecting mere negations and additions to essence. Elmalılı and the Study Quran say plurality of names does not divide God. [10 notes: TABATABAI; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:180/q14 How are the names' perfections shared with creatures?  [وَلِلَّهِ ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · theology]
turns on: Whether shared terms indicate common basic meanings or exclusive perfected divine meanings.
- 7:180/q14/p1 God owns all perfections essentially; creatures possess only gifted lesser instances. — Al-Biqāʿī infers God's free choice, power, wisdom and unity from created contingency. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites verses on power, might, knowledge and life. Comparative and genitive expressions presuppose a broad shared sense, not equal perfection. [20 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:180/q14/p2 Some names are exclusive to God; others apply to creatures with a different or lesser meaning. — Al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī treat Allah and Raḥmān as exclusive, Raḥīm and Karīm as shareable. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī instead says Allah or Ilāh is the exclusive proper name and Raḥmān shares a general meaning. The perfected divine meaning remains His alone. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:180/q15 Are essential and active names eternal?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · theology]
- 7:180/q15/p1 Essential names are eternal; names from acts become applicable when their acts occur. — The Najmiyya account says God eternally has capacity to create and provide, though Creator and Provider apply upon creation. Al-Rāzī rejects names for parts of essence because composition contradicts necessity. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); STUDYQURAN(most Islamic thinkers)]

## 7:180/q16 How much of God can be known through the names?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · theology]
- 7:180/q16/p1 The names disclose genuine perfections, but God's essence and ultimate reality exceed human comprehension. — Tustarī and al-Sulamī liken the Real to inaccessible fire. Al-Rāzī describes ascending knowledge from contingent beings to power, ordered action, knowledge and life. Al-Ālūsī says awareness of inability is the fullest human knowledge and cites 'I cannot enumerate praise of You.' Al-Rāzī refers detailed study to his Lawāmiʿ al-bayyināt. [17 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(بعضهم,قيل); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Sufi commentators)]
- 7:180/q16/p2 The names enable graded experiential nearness and participation in their effects. — Al-Sulamī links Allah to love and Raḥmān/Raḥīm to mercy; al-Qāshānī applies names to needs and acts. Al-Bursawī invokes the sacred report of divine hearing and sight in the servant. Study Quran calls names ladders and the cosmos their theophany. [13 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); QASHANI; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Islamic esoterism)]

## 7:180/q17 May an untransmitted fitting name be applied to God?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ فَٱدْعُوهُ بِهَا · law]
turns on: Whether meaning alone licenses a name and what counts as authority.
- 7:180/q17/p1 Invocation names must be authorized by the Qurʾan, sound Sunna or genuine consensus. — Al-Zajjāj allows Allah, Raḥmān, Jawād, Raḥīm, Qawī but excludes unsupported substitutes. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr favor restriction because judging implications is difficult. Al-Rāzī distinguishes true description from reverent vocative use. Al-Āmidī rejects analogy; al-Khaṭṭābī rejects unsupported Yā Subḥān and Lord of Ṭā Hā. Ibn al-Ḥaṣṣār calls Clean unattested; al-Qurṭubī replies that Clean occurs in al-Tirmidhī and Good in Muslim. Ibn al-ʿArabī restricts prayer formulas to Qurʾan and five canonical hadith books including the Muwaṭṭaʾ. Al-Ālūsī lists Creator, Existing, Necessary and Eternal as consensual names, with dispute over Cause and other transmission. Al-Ṭībī permits Yā Karīm but disallows Yā Ḥayy despite a report calling God Living and Generous; al-Ālūsī notes that strict caution would allow only mass-transmitted or agreed names. [53 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أهل المعاني,المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المعاني); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الخطابي,الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو الحسن الأشعري); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا,جهم بن صفوان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو القاسم القشيري,إمام الحرمين,الآمدي,الطيبي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,بعض المحققين,جمع من المتأخرين,جمهور أهل الحق); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية); STUDYQURAN(several commentators); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الحصار,ابن العربي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو الحسن الأشعري)+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفقهاء والجمهور)+; TABATABAI-]
- 7:180/q17/p2 A term may be added if it is true pure praise with no defect or ambiguity. — Al-Bāqillānī permits this, and Muʿtazila permit fitting terms more broadly. Al-Ālūsī allows carefully vetted essential, active and negative names and any reverential wording in one's capacity, citing 2:286. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows conventional attribute or act names except figurative or defect-suggesting ones. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees no exegetical restriction but advises caution in formal naming. [14 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن الباقلاني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القاضي أبو بكر الباقلاني); ALUSI-FULL(القاضي أبو بكر,المعتزلة); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(other commentators); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفقهاء والجمهور)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو الحسن الأشعري)-; IBNASHUR-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 7:180/q17/p3 A truthful attribute may be predicated even where a new name may not be given. — Al-Ghazālī says descriptions make truth claims, while names require a rightful namer. Al-Ālūsī replies that implication may still bar a predicate; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī treats ordinary predication as freer than formal naming. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الغزالي); TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 7:180/q17/p4 God should not be invoked as 'thing' or by other abstract terms lacking reverent sanction. — Jahm says 'thing' applies even to base or nonexistent things, argues from 42:11 and creation verses, and notes no devotional precedent. Al-Rāzī rejects using 6:19 as proof that thing is a divine name; real existence does not license address as essence, reality or concept. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL(جهم بن صفوان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجهم)]
- 7:180/q17/p5 When revelation is silent, judgment should be suspended pending evidence. — Al-Āmidī says neither analogy nor general caution settles the matter; apparent scriptural evidence suffices for a legal ruling. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الآمدي)]

## 7:180/q18 May divine actions yield names such as plotter or mocker?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ فَٱدْعُوهُ بِهَا · law]
turns on: Whether the verb licenses an agent name, qualified or unqualified.
- 7:180/q18/p1 A divine verb or figurative predicate does not itself license an agent-name, even when qualified. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr report the forbidding group; Abū Ḥayyān prefers it for restricted forms. Al-Baghawī forbids deceiver and plotter from 4:142 and 3:54. Al-Ālūsī lists cultivator, planter, shooter, mocker and schemer. Unqualified mocking names are unanimously rejected. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); BAGHAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين,بعض الناس); IBNASHUR-FULL(فرقة); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 7:180/q18/p2 A derivative may be used if qualified by its just cause and the intended request fits. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this despite preferring prohibition. Al-Qurṭubī cites a prophetic prayer for favorable plotting, graded ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ, and permits Best of plotters. Study Quran cites Completer of His light and Best of plotters. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(فرقة); STUDYQURAN(other commentators); QURTUBI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 7:180/q19 Which names may be invoked alone or with a matching request?  [فَٱدْعُوهُ بِهَا · law]
- 7:180/q19/p1 Names conveying death or harm should be paired with names conveying life or benefit. — Al-Rāzī pairs Death-causer with Life-giver and Harmful with Beneficent; al-Ālūsī reports the same invocation distinction. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:180/q19/p2 A request should invoke a name suited to what is sought. — Al-Qurṭubī matches Merciful to mercy and Provider to provision; a general name can accompany a fitting request, and Allah encompasses all names. Ibn al-ʿArabī warns against mismatches and says fitting speech leads to sincerity; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī similarly matches names to need. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); TABATABAI]
- 7:180/q19/p3 Terms divide into those usable in invocation, those usable only in naming, and those usable in neither. — Al-Qurṭubī reports al-Ashʿarī's distinction. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الحسن الأشعري)]

## 7:180/q20 May private supplication use non-Arabic names?  [فَٱدْعُوهُ بِهَا · law]
- 7:180/q20/p1 Ritual invocation requires Arabic; private supplication outside canonical prayer or Sufi dhikr may use other languages. — The Study Quran gives Persian Khudā and English God as examples. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 7:180/q21 What does 'call upon Him by them' require?  [فَٱدْعُوهُ بِهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Petition, praise, naming, or broad worship.
- 7:180/q21/p1 Invoke God in petition using the names. — Al-Māwardī gives this reading; examples include Allah, Raḥmān, Raḥīm, Powerful, Knowing and Creator of heaven and earth. Al-Biqāʿī includes asking, naming and describing. - 7:180/q21/p2 Remember, praise or glorify God worshipfully by the names. — Al-Māwardī offers this alternative; al-Bursawī calls it dhikr and cites 40:50. - 7:180/q21/p3 Name or address God with the names. — Al-Zamakhsharī compares naming a son ʿAbd Allāh, al-Samīn a son Zayd. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī lists this as a possible reading. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(قيل); TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 7:180/q21/p4 Worship God as bearer of all perfections the names express. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this, adducing 17:110, 40:60, 46:5-6 and 40:65, and taking naming and petition as parts of worship. [5 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]

## 7:180/q22 What inward practice accompanies invocation?  [فَٱدْعُوهُ بِهَا · inward]
- 7:180/q22/p1 Invoke with knowledge, humility, sincerity and attention to the name fitting one's need. — Al-Rāzī says formula without these states has little benefit and illustrates Allahu akbar by contemplating creation and divine incomparability. Al-Qāshānī says inward appeal readies one for the name's effect; verbal Lord can mean Knower, Healer or Enricher. Early Sufis begin with vocative names and end with blessings. - 7:180/q22/p2 One also calls through conduct embodying a fitting attribute and a purified heart. — Al-Qāshānī describes healing others and surrendering knowledge; al-Bursawī describes marriage for offspring, provision through spending, heart-polishing, and the report of God becoming the servant's hearing and sight. [7 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:180/q27 What does 'leave those who deviate' instruct?  [وَذَرُوا۟ ٱلَّذِينَ يُلْحِدُونَ · interpretation]

- 7:180/q27/p1 Leave them to God's coming punishment and ignore their objections; the command is a threat. — Al-Ṭabarī compares 15:3 and 29:66; al-Qurṭubī compares 74:11 and 15:3, arguing from the recompense clause. Al-Ālūsī says the promise answers why believers can disregard them. Al-Bursawī says avoiding their heresy averts punishment; the warning consoles believers, as al-Ālūsī and Islahi explain. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURSEVI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجمهور)+; QURTUBI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+]
- 7:180/q27/p2 Avoid prolonged debate or confrontation with those uninterested in truth. — Ibn Zayd supplies no-debate reading; Ibn ʿĀshūr says their doubts may mislead ordinary believers. Al-Qurṭubī adds exclusive worship and avoidance of deviants. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:180/q27/p3 Abandon their misnaming rather than literally abandoning the persons. — Al-Bursawī says leaving people themselves makes no sense; the Najmiyya interpretation says deviants are left to nature and desire. [3 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 7:180/q28 Was the command to leave them abrogated?  [وَذَرُوا۟ ٱلَّذِينَ يُلْحِدُونَ · sciences]
turns on: Whether a no-confrontation order was superseded by fighting or the wording remains a threat.
- 7:180/q28/p1 Ibn Zayd says fighting abrogated the order to leave unbelievers without confrontation. — Al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn al-Jawzī and Abū Ḥayyān transmit it. - 7:180/q28/p2 The verse remains operative because the command is a threat, not a ban on confrontation. — Al-Ṭabarī explicitly rejects abrogation; the majority in Ibn al-Jawzī cite 74:11. Al-Qurṭubī points to the attached recompense. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجمهور)+; QURTUBI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+]

## 7:180/q29 When and how will the deviants be recompensed?  [سَيُجْزَوْنَ مَا كَانُوا۟ يَعْمَلُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Hereafter alone or also this world.
- 7:180/q29/p1 They will be punished in the Hereafter for habitual disbelief, distortion and other deeds. — Maqātil, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya specify the Hereafter. - 7:180/q29/p2 Requital may occur in both this world and the Hereafter. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports it; al-Biqāʿī says the passive highlights certainty of punishment rather than its agent. [3 notes: TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 7:180/q30 How does the recompense clause stress the warning?  [سَيُجْزَوْنَ مَا كَانُوا۟ يَعْمَلُونَ · rhetoric]
- 7:180/q30/p1 The recompense clause answers an implied question and marks punishment as certain and repeated conduct as habitual. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes the connective fa; Ibn ʿĀshūr interprets sa- and kānū yaʿmalūn,. [5 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 7:180/q31 How does the verse connect with its context and parallels?  [* · links]
- 7:180/q31/p1 After 7:179's heedlessness and Hell warning, remembrance through the names offers a way away from that fate. — Al-Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān contrast worldly cravings and darkness with remembrance and obedience; Abū Ḥayyān gives making up missed prayers as example. Al-Ālūsī, Asad and Study Quran link the heedless to this verse. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); STUDYQURAN]
- 7:180/q31/p2 It recalls the innate pledge at 7:172, the humble prayer command at 7:55, and other beautiful-name verses. — Islahi invokes the pledge; al-Biqāʿī cites 7:55. Al-Rāzī lists 17:110, 20:8 and 59:24, also cited by later writers. Kuran Yolu, citing Ibn al-ʿArabī, adds that the names awaken reverence, help prayer, comfort hearts, elevate the reciter, and teach sound belief about what is necessary, possible and impossible for God. [9 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Ebû Bekir İbnü’l-Arabî)]
- 7:180/q31/p3 The passage interrupts condemnation of polytheists to reaffirm divine oneness and reject their naming errors. — Ibn ʿĀshūr makes their rejection of Raḥmān the context; al-Biqāʿī says perfect names forestall blaming God for misguidance and lead to the group at 7:181. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts worship through the names with their distortion. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds that misguidance is privative but persists when God withholds success through respite and gradual enticement. [11 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:180/q31/p4 A related interpretive application extends requital to righteous recipients of revelation across communities. — Asad connects it to Moses' people in 7:159. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]

## 7:180/q32 What further inward insights are drawn from divine names?  [* · inward]
- 7:180/q32/p1 Names mediate creation, provision and transformation of the caller. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says created perfections point back to divine names and needs seek their sustaining attribute. Sufi dhikr cultivates virtues. The hidden-name report has three revealed names and one concealed; the Najmiyya account distinguishes eternal essential and created action names. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī interprets Allah, Tabāraka and Taʿālā as encompassing perfection, bestowal of blessing and transcendence from deficiency; a transmitted account even counts 'neither slumber nor sleep overtakes Him' as a broad descriptive name. [26 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); STUDYQURAN(Islamic esoterism,Sufi commentators)]
- 7:180/q32/p2 Spiritual helpers and truthful knowledge of the Messenger manifest divine nearness. — Al-Qushayrī says God's people sustain truth, bring relief and rain, and that experiential knowers perceive the Prophet's nearness; his character and its effects witness against fabrication. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 7:180/q32/p3 The Imams are called God's beautiful names as signs through whom servants know Him. — Abū ʿAbd Allāh reports that deeds are accepted through knowledge of the Imams; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī explains name broadly as verbal or nonverbal indicator and the prophets and successors as complete servants. [2 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]

## 7:180/q33 How do related discussions of names illuminate Qurʾanic initials and Allāhumma?  [* · links]
- 7:180/q33/p1 Some take disconnected opening letters as keys or fragments of divine names. — Ibn ʿAbbās connects alif, lām and mīm to Allah, Laṭīf and Majīd and glosses ALM, ALMS and ALR as divine declarations. Al-Rabīʿ adds favors, trials and lifespans; Saʿīd ibn Jubayr says an unknown arrangement would disclose the Greatest Name. Ibn Fāris praises this. [6 notes: BURHAN(ابن عباس,ابن فارس,الربيع بن أنس); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 7:180/q33/p2 Others treat the letters as unknown secrets, sura titles, Qurʾan names, scribal signs or oaths. — Al-Zarkashī reports Abū Bakr, al-Shaʿbī and Ibn Fāris on hidden knowledge, and known-meaning, oath, revelation-marker and sura-title views. Bint al-Shāṭi says later interpreters mostly elaborate earlier views. Al-Zarkashī also explains letter-form spelling and vocative alif omission. [14 notes: BURHAN(ابن فارس,الرازي,الشعبي); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الزمخشري)]
- 7:180/q33/p3 Qurʾanic sciences frame these issues through revelation, language, readings, exegesis, orthography and worship. — Al-Suyūṭī and al-Zarkashī list places and occasions, textual variants, parsing, virtues, abrogation, coherence and devotion; these notes provide taxonomy rather than another reading of this verse. [13 notes: ITQAN; BURHAN]
- 7:180/q33/p4 Allāhumma is a vocative or a condensed invocation of all God's names. — Al-Suyūṭī reports the usual replaced yā with doubled mīm, a compound etymology, Abū Rajāʾ's seventy names, Ibn Ẓafar's Greatest Name and ninety-nine attributes, and al-Ḥasan's and al-Naḍr's encompassing-prayer views. [6 notes: ITQAN(أبو رجاء العطاردي,ابن ظفر,الحسن البصري,النضر بن شميل)]

## 7:180/q34 What related notes concern adjacent wording and prayer reports?  [* · links]
- 7:180/q34/p1 Additional reports promise answered prayer by the names and cite a prayer for divine aid. — One report promises response to whoever calls by the ninety-nine; al-Qurṭubī cites a prophetic prayer for help, victory and favorable plotting, graded ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ. [6 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 7:180/q34/p2 Adjacent wording distinguishes pregnancy from carried loads and weight-bearing. — Al-Akhfash distinguishes ḥaml from ḥiml, explains athqalat by analogy with becoming possessed of dates or milk, and reports a vocalized reading. [3 notes: AKHFASH(بعضهم)]
