# 56:74: 13 questions

## 56:74/q01 What does the command to glorify involve?  [فَسَبِّحْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether tasbih is primarily repudiation of false claims, remembrance or spoken formula, prayer, inward realization, public proclamation, or reverence for the divine name; several sources allow more than one act.
- 56:74/q01/p1 Declare God transcendent above false descriptions, partners, imperfection, and denial of His power to resurrect. — Wahidi-Basit and Wahidi-Wajiz specify people's and polytheists' claims; Ibn Atiyya specifies the unbelievers' treatment of God's exalted names. Tabrisi purifies God of unfit descriptions; al-Zajjaj, reported by Ibn al-Jawzi, connects this to proofs of unity, power, and favor. Qurtubi identifies alleged partners and inability to resurrect. Nasafi says what does not befit Him; Biqai especially names denial of resurrection. Tabatabai specifies shirk and denial of recompense. Elmalili includes partners and imperfection. Study Quran defines tasbih as declaring transcendence beyond everything and says deficiencies perceived in God's works cannot be attributed to Him. Muqatil includes remembrance of divine oneness. [14 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 56:74/q01/p2 Remember and invoke God by His name, including His beautiful names. — Nahhas mentions remembrance and the beautiful names; Muqatil makes that remembrance one of unity. Ibn Jarir, reported by Ibn al-Jawzi, says to remember and name the Lord. Kashshaf and Baydawi allow 'name' to mean remembrance; Nasafi and Study Quran also give invocation by name. Alusi prefers the ordinary meaning of mentioning the Lord or His name. Study Quran's Aj says God's glory exceeds anything anyone can say of it. [9 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Aj); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 56:74/q01/p3 Utter a formula of praise, especially 'subḥān Allāh' or 'subḥāna rabbī al-ʿaẓīm.' — Kashshaf gives subḥān Allāh; Razi allows it or equivalent words. Tabrisi and Nasafi give subḥāna rabbī al-ʿaẓīm; Tabrisi relates it to the Prophet's instruction for bowing. Elmalili gives the latter formula alongside prayer. Baydawi permits uttering the name. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 56:74/q01/p4 Pray in the Lord's name, beginning prayer with the takbīr. — Al-Dahhak, reported by Ibn al-Jawzi, gives the prayer and its opening takbīr; Elmalili also allows prayer as the response. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك); ELMALILI]
- 56:74/q01/p5 Realize divine unity inwardly through belief, deep reflection, knowledge, and protection of faith from doubt. — Qushayri describes thinking deeply and using the strength of unity to find knowledge and guard faith. Razi includes inward conviction that God is one without partner and can resurrect; he says true unity excludes body, accident, location, and being in, on, or from anything, while power entails knowledge, will, life, and other attributes, with reference to al-Ikhlas (112:1). [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL]
- 56:74/q01/p6 Name and describe God aloud among one's people, continuing to preach despite their refusal. — Razi says purely inward tasbih would not communicate the proclamation; the Lord is named and described publicly. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 56:74/q01/p7 Honor and protect God's own name and its words from disrespect. — Ibn Atiyya says to clear God's exalted names of unbelievers' claims. Alusi argues literally that, if God's essence and attributes are free from defects, the words used for them deserve freedom from disrespect even more, comparing 87:1. Biqai says mentioning the name most firmly honors the One named. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 56:74/q02 How does 'by the name of your Lord' work?  [بِٱسْمِ رَبِّكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether bāʾ is instrumental, associative, transitive, introductory, additional, or expresses seeking aid, and whether 'name' means the uttered name, remembrance, the divine essence, or attributes.
- 56:74/q02/p1 The bāʾ marks means or accompaniment: glorify by naming, mentioning, or remembering the Lord. — Kashshaf and Baydawi allow name to mean remembrance. Razi explains that the real object is the Lord through the name, so the relation permits a preposition. Bursevi treats the glorifying verb as intransitive and the bāʾ as means or accompaniment. Alusi supplies 'by mentioning' or treats 'name' figuratively as mention; he accepts means or accompaniment but rejects Abu Hayyan's apparent transitive analysis. Tabatabai says naming is mentioning and the bāʾ can mean use or association. Wāsiṭī, reported by Sulami, says a thing's name is the thing itself. [8 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 56:74/q02/p2 The bāʾ or 'name' is additional, giving the direct sense 'glorify your Lord.' — An unnamed view reported by Ibn al-Jawzi makes bāʾ extra and name stand for the divine essence. Razi reports the common parsing that makes 'name' extra, while saying its mention still heightens reverence because the truly exalted are named by greatness. Alusi objects that a symbolic direct-Lord interpretation needs omission or an extra bāʾ and conflicts with apparent wording. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قيل); RAZI-FULL(المشهور); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 56:74/q02/p3 Sanctify the Lord's name itself, thereby sanctifying the Lord. — Tabatabai reports a transitive analysis: purify the name from association and from denial of resurrection and recompense. Alusi defends literal reverence for the words used of God because His essence and attributes are free from defects; he objects to readings that require dropping the bāʾ or treating it as extra. Ibn Atiyya speaks of clearing His exalted names from unbelievers' claims. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 56:74/q02/p4 Begin the glorification with the Lord's name. — Kashshaf offers beginning by uttering the name; Razi says this leaves bāʾ meaningful. Alusi reports the intransitive 'begin glorifying' construction but discusses its temporal force separately. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 56:74/q02/p5 Reserve the divine name for God alone rather than giving it to idols. — Razi says idolaters concede God created all things yet name idols gods, so the meaningful bāʾ prohibits that designation and admonishes any hearer. Alusi reports a related view directed to an idolater who admits God created them and the heavens and earth; Alusi says this particularized address is unnecessary. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 56:74/q02/p6 The bāʾ expresses seeking the Lord's help while glorifying Him. — Islahi says the Prophet seeks aid against the circumstances posed by stubborn rejecters. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 56:74/q02/p7 The name signifies God's noble attributes as the means of knowing and relating to Him. — Islahi says these attributes source true knowledge and deeds. Nahhas refers to God's beautiful names in the act of remembrance. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; NAHHAS]

## 56:74/q03 Why is the alif of 'ism' written here?  [ٱسْمِ · grammar]
- 56:74/q03/p1 The alif is retained because this spelling occurs less often than the basmala. — Biqai contrasts the frequent basmala, whose repeated use encourages an abbreviated spelling. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 56:74/q04 What does 'the Great' describe?  [رَبِّكَ ٱلْعَظِيمِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether al-ʿaẓīm is syntactically attached to the Lord or His name; Ibn Ashur supplies a metaphorical account of the latter, and Kuranyolu prefers the former.
- 56:74/q04/p1 It modifies 'Lord': the Lord is Great. — Kashshaf, Baydawi, Nasafi, Alusi, Tabatabai, Study Quran citing Bḍ, and Ibn Ashur all allow this attachment. Ibn Ashur says every divine perfection is established. Kuranyolu prefers it as the usual attachment and translates 'the name of your mighty Lord.' [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 56:74/q04/p2 It modifies 'name': the name is Great. — Kashshaf, Baydawi, Nasafi, Alusi, Tabatabai, Study Quran citing Bḍ, and Kuranyolu allow this attachment. Ibn Ashur says greatness of the name is a common metaphor, nearly literal, arising from its blessing and the greatness of the One named. Kuranyolu gives the alternative wording 'glorify your Lord by His mighty name.' [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ)]

## 56:74/q05 What greatness is conveyed by 'the Great'?  [ٱلْعَظِيمِ · theology]
- 56:74/q05/p1 God's greatness encompasses majesty and every perfection while transcending defect and perception. — Muqatil says He is the greatest. Biqai says His majesty fills the cosmos, He transcends every defect and lacks no perfection. Razi says perceptible greatness suggests nearness to all, yet God transcends perception and His positive attributes exceed knowledge; Ibn Ashur says every divine perfection is established. Wāsiṭī says He is the Great. [5 notes: MUQATIL; SULAMI(الواسطي); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 56:74/q06 How does 'the Great' compare with 'the Most High' in 87:1?  [ٱلْعَظِيمِ · rhetoric]
- 56:74/q06/p1 'The Most High' more directly expresses transcendence and is the more apt epithet, while 'the Great' combines affirmation and negation. — Razi compares the epithets with 87:1: perceptible greatness suggests nearness, absolute elevation remoteness, though God transcends perception and His positive attributes outrun knowledge. He explicitly prefers al-Aʿlā as more apt usage. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]

## 56:74/q07 Who is addressed by the command?  [رَبِّكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the second-person address remains the Prophet's, extends generally to hearers, or identifies a particular idolater; Alusi questions the need for particularization.
- 56:74/q07/p1 The Prophet is addressed, with his response standing against the deniers. — Ibn Atiyya calls it a command to the Prophet after the signs. Razi describes his task after deniers reject creation and provision proofs. Tabrisi, Tabatabai, Islahi, and Ibn Ashur explicitly identify the Messenger or Prophet; Ibn Ashur calls the worship a fresh recollection of majesty and gratitude. Biqai says the Lord specially benefited the Prophet by supreme clarification. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 56:74/q07/p2 The command addresses any hearer, including idolaters who acknowledge God as Creator. — Razi makes the ban on assigning the divine name to idols an admonition to any hearer. Alusi reports a proposed particular idolater but says it can be a generic preacher's 'O poor man'; a general address is possible and the particularization is not required. Study Quran treats the verse as one of many commands to human beings to glorify God. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 56:74/q07/p3 A particular idolater is addressed to acknowledge that no other deity shares God's name. — Alusi reports this view because idolaters admit that God created them and the heavens and earth. He objects that the particular addressee is unnecessary and could be understood generically. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 56:74/q08 What is the force of commanding glorification when the Prophet already does it?  [فَسَبِّحْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the imperative introduces an act, renews an already continuous act, or urges the Prophet to persevere publicly; al-Tibi disputes making 'begin' mean 'continue'.
- 56:74/q08/p1 The command calls for renewed, distinct glorification after fresh reflection on the signs. — Al-Tibi objects to explaining 'begin' as 'continue' and prefers renewal. Ibn Ashur says the Messenger always glorifies God, yet each renewed reflection produces a distinct worship state with its own merits and greater reward; the command is a fresh recollection of majesty and gratitude. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي)+]
- 56:74/q08/p2 The command says to begin glorification, effectively to continue it since the Prophet never neglected it. — Alusi reports an intransitive construal of a transitive verb; al-Tibi objects that taking 'beginning' as continuing reverses the force of the wording. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي)-]
- 56:74/q08/p3 The Prophet should hold his stance and keep glorifying despite stubborn rejection. — Islahi says the arguments support him against the rejecters; Razi says he must perfect knowledge and service and continue naming God publicly even when his people refuse. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; RAZI-FULL]

## 56:74/q09 Why does the command follow the preceding signs?  [* · links]
turns on: The cited purposes are inference and gratitude from the signs, repudiation of deniers, possible astonishment at ingratitude, or a transition through recitation to the following oath; Alusi disputes the astonishment reading.
- 56:74/q09/p1 The signs of creation, provision, divine power, and favor call for glorification and gratitude. — Al-Zajjaj in Ibn al-Jawzi ties it to proof of unity, power, and favor; Kashshaf and Baydawi name power and blessings. Tabrisi invokes signs of oneness and favors; Qushayri and Biqai say they prepare inference. Biqai adds wonder and magnification; Alusi says the command follows marvelous works and gifts and reports gratitude as praise of the true Benefactor. Bursevi thanks God despite deniers. Ibn Ashur finds fresh majesty and gratitude after resurrection examples and blessings. Tabatabai reads fa as making glorification follow the proof. Study Quran says God transcends the listed works and their perceived deficiencies in 56:58–73. [13 notes: QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(القشيري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 56:74/q09/p2 The command rejects deniers' claims about God's oneness, resurrection, and favors after the proofs establish them. — Wahidi-Wajiz and Ibn Atiyya name polytheists or unbelievers. Kashshaf and Baydawi contrast denial with the signs. Qurtubi names partners and alleged incapacity to resurrect; Biqai especially opposes resurrection denial. Razi links the deniers' rejection to perfecting knowledge and service, citing 20:130. Alusi reports denials of unity and gifts; Tabatabai says the command turns from uncomprehending deniers after signs of lordship; Islahi says self-evident arguments about Judgment sustain the Prophet despite Quraysh's resistance. [12 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 56:74/q09/p3 The response also expresses amazement at the disbelievers' ingratitude. — Kashshaf, Baydawi, and Biqai allow this purpose. Alusi reports it but objects that taking subḥān metaphorically as astonishment is distant and prefers repudiation or gratitude. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 56:74/q09/p4 The verse directs recitation of the Qur'an or this sūra, preparing the following oath. — Al-Kashf, reported by Alusi, says recitation establishes resurrection, recompense, and ranks before the oath at 56:75. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الكشف)]

## 56:74/q10 How do the preceding examples support the command?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes emphasize either the evidential force of creation and livelihood for resurrection and lordship, or a classification of opposing conditions, elements, and life-sustaining qualities.
- 56:74/q10/p1 The enumerated signs supply grounds for inference about God and resurrection. — Qushayri says their enumeration prepares reflection; Biqai transmits this and a report, without authority or grading, that an hour's reflection is better than sixty years' worship. Ibn Kathir notes sweet water could have become salty and fire benefits worldly life while warning of the Hereafter. Abu Hayyan in Biqai groups creation with food, drink, and fire, and treats fire's transformation as evidence of resurrection. Tabatabai names crops, water, and fire as necessities showing lordship as governance rooted in ownership after creation and allotted death. Islahi sees self-evident proofs of Judgment, insisting the world was not made merely for luxury or sport and a day must reward gratitude and punish ingratitude. [10 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو حيان,القشيري); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 56:74/q10/p2 The examples are organized around opposing conditions and the elements and qualities that sustain life. — Biqai reports Razi's richest opposing pair as becoming rich and poor. He reports Ibn Barjan's linkage of water and earth with creation of the elements, and air and fire with temperaments and qualities; Ibn Barjan says the sun's heat, as hellfire, produces gardens, fruit, crops, and livelihoods. Abu Hayyan groups the examples as creation and necessities of livelihood. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو حيان,ابن برجان,الرازي)]

## 56:74/q11 What was the Prophet's instruction when this verse was revealed?  [* · reports]
- 56:74/q11/p1 He told worshippers to say its glorification during bowing. — Tabrisi and Nasafi report the instruction at revelation; Tabrisi also identifies the formula as 'Glory be to my Lord, the Mighty.' [3 notes: TABRISI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASAFI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 56:74/q12 Why command servants to glorify God if He needs no human glorification?  [* · inward]
- 56:74/q12/p1 The command honors servants and lets them manifest and purify themselves through their glorification; God is beyond needing or being reached by it. — Ibn ʿAta, reported by Sulami, says it honors them by manifesting their act of declaring Him transcendent; Bursevi transmits a variant emphasizing purification of the servants themselves. Both say He is too great to need or be reached by their glorification. [2 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); BURSEVI(ابن عطاء)]

## 56:74/q13 What fire imagery is related alongside this command?  [* · inward]
turns on: These are two accompanying images of love as fire, one a report involving Hell and one a Persian couplet.
- 56:74/q13/p1 A report portrays God's fire of love in believers' hearts as greater than Hell itself. — Bursevi reports Junayd's account: Hell asks whether it will be punished by anything worse than itself, and God answers that this greater fire of love dwells in believing friends' hearts. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الجنيد)]
- 56:74/q13/p2 A Persian couplet portrays love as a fire that burns a seeker completely. — Bursevi quotes the couplet, calling love fire for lovers and saying it burns the seeker's very being. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
