# 7:201: 21 questions

## 7:201/q01 Who are those who have taqwa here, and what do they avoid?  [ٱلَّذِينَ ٱتَّقَوْا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether taqwa names general faith and obedience, only avoidance of shirk, protection from punishment, a special prophetic class, or an inward state.
- 7:201/q01/p1 They are believers or righteous people generally, guarding against disobedience and fulfilling God's commands. — Mujahid, Ibn Abbas, al-Baghawi and al-Wahidi identify them as believers. Al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir and al-Nahhas include obedience and avoidance of sins; al-Tabrisi says avoiding disobedience; Ibn Atiyya insists praise here requires avoidance of both shirk and sins, not shirk alone. Alusi says the relative phrase is unrestricted. Bursevi defines taqwa as protection from harm. Ibn Ashur includes messengers and righteous followers as exemplars, citing 2:2. [18 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 7:201/q01/p2 They are people who avoid shirk, or shirk and grave indecencies. — Muqatil specifies avoidance of polytheism; al-Kalbi adds unbelief and grave indecencies; Qashani and Alusi give the shirk-only sense in their inward gloss. [4 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 7:201/q01/p3 They are those guarding against God's punishment. — Abu Hayyan reports this third explanation without naming an authority. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]
- 7:201/q01/p4 They are resolute messengers if the preceding address is specific, or righteous servants if it is general. — An unnamed critical scholar makes the referent depend on the scope of 7:200. Alusi disputes the necessity of restricting it to messengers. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 7:201/q01/p5 They are non-prophets in contrast to Muhammad in the preceding address. — Some say a mere initial nazgh affects the Prophet whereas an established mass affects other pious people, as a sign of prophetic favor. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 7:201/q01/p6 They are people of the heart whose taqwa is a light that distinguishes truth and falsehood. — Al-Ta'wilat al-Najmiyya cites the Prophet's indication toward his chest as evidence that taqwa resides in the heart. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 7:201/q02 What does 'touches them' convey?  [مَسَّهُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether mass emphasizes minimal contact or a stronger stage than nazgh in 7:200.
- 7:201/q02/p1 Mass is literal contact by hand, used figuratively for affliction or even the slightest onset of temptation. — Jishumi and Ibn Ashur give the hand-contact sense; Jishumi notes water reached by hands and affliction by jinn, as does al-Tabrisi. Ibn Ashur stresses even slight contact and, with conditional idha, intervention before thought grows into resolve. Bursevi calls it the slightest prompting. [5 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:201/q02/p2 The touch is a stronger incursion than the preceding nazgh. — Abu Hayyan contrasts bodily contact with the lighter movement nazgh; Razi similarly says a ṭāʾif touching the pious is more forceful than an initial nudge. Others distinguish established contact for non-prophets from initial prompting for Muhammad; another account says Satan makes greater effort against the perfect. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 7:201/q03 How is ṭāʾif or ṭayf read?  [طَٰٓئِفٌۭ · readings]
turns on: Transmission of the named readers and whether the forms are equivalent, distinct, or an original doubled form.
- 7:201/q03/p1 Ṭāʾif, with alif and hamza. — Most Medinan and Kufan readers in al-Tabari; Nafi, Asim, Ibn Amir and Hamza in Ibn Mujahid and others; reported also from Jabir's hearing of the Prophet and from Sa'id ibn Jubayr. ## 7:201/q04 What does ṭāʾif or ṭayf mean in this verse?  [طَٰٓئِفٌۭ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verse names a general satanic incursion or a specific instance such as anger, a sin, fear or seizure.
- 7:201/q04/p1 It is a passing satanic visitation or prompting, including whispering and temptation. — Ibn Abbas calls it lamma, touch, whisper or incitement; Abu Amr calls it whispering. Al-Tabari generalizes anger and intrusion to any misguiding prompt. Al-Layth describes whispering enveloping a person; Taj al-Arus includes an elusive image or obsession, whence Asad's 'dark suggestion.' Study Quran links whispering to 114:4. Ibn Ashur likens it to a visitor circling for entry; Arab guests circled a house, and he cites al-Nabigha, the Kaaba and al-A'sha. Itqan records the Thaqif dialect sense 'sting.' [54 notes: TAB-FULL(آخر من الكوفيين,أبو عمرو بن العلاء,ابن عباس,بعض الكوفيين); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو زيد وأبو عبيدة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(أبو عمرو,ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو عمرو بن العلاء); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,ابن عباس في رواية عطاء,الليث); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس والسدي,الحسن); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(أبو عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مفسرون); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN(أبو بكر الواسطي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Taj al-'Arus); STUDYQURAN(some); TAB-FULL+]
- 7:201/q04/p2 It is anger. — Mujahid, Said and others repeatedly explain the touch this way. Al-Wahidi and Razi say anger resembles madness because reason deserts the angry person; some read the verse as anger accompanied by Satan's making wrongdoing attractive. Al-Tabari regards anger as one instance within the broader satanic prompting. [24 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(سعيد,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس من رواية عكرمة,مجاهد وسعيد بن جبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(سعيد بن جبير ومجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(الأزهري); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مفسرون); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد); STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 7:201/q04/p3 It is a sin or the temptation to commit one. — Al-Kalbi calls it sin; other reports place the pious either on the brink of an indecent act or after a lapse. Ibn Kathir lists intending and committing a sin among the reported explanations. [5 notes: THALABI(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مفسرون); IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك,العلاء بن بدر)]
- 7:201/q04/p4 It is fear or alarm. — Al-Mawardi transmits this gloss from Sa'id ibn Jubayr. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 7:201/q04/p5 It is madness or seizure. — Al-Azhari cites Abu Ubayd from al-Ahmar and a Hudhali poet for ṭayf as madness; Jishumi reports a distinction in which ṭayf is madness. Ibn Kathir includes seizure or like affliction in the exegetical range. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأزهري); JISHUMI(أبو عمرو وأبو عبيد); RAZI-FULL(الأزهري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مفسرون)]

## 7:201/q05 How do the forms ṭāʾif and ṭayf relate in sense and formation?  [طَٰٓئِفٌۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the two forms are synonymous, mark different kinds of visitation, or are related through verbal-noun formation and phonetic shortening.
- 7:201/q05/p1 They are equivalent forms for a visitor, apparition or satanic prompting. — Some Basrans compare mayyit/mayt; al-Farra, Ibn Abbas, al-Farisi and others treat the forms as synonymous or dialectal. Ṭāʾif can be a source noun on fāʿil, or a participle from either ṭāfa yaṭūfu or yaṭīfu. Poetic phantoms and jinn visitors are cited by al-Tabari, al-A'sha and Hassan. Al-Akhfash calls ṭayf the more common form. Al-Suhayli denies an active participle for an unreal dream visitor, but allows the real visitor of 68:19; Abu Hayyan also cites that verse. [51 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض البصريين,بعض البصريين وبعض الكوفيين); ZAJJAJ; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(الأعشى,نافع وابن عامر وعاصم وحمزة); THALABI(النخعي وابن كثير وأبو عمرو والأعمش وابن يزيد والجحدري وطلحة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو زيد,الأعشى,الزجاج,الفراء); JISHUMI(ابن فارس وابن عرفة); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,الفراء والأخفش); RAZI-FULL(أبو زيد,الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج,النحاس وأبو حاتم,حسان); NASAFI-FULL(أبو عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو زيد,أبو عمرو,ابن عباس,السهيلي,حسان,قيل); SAMIN-DURR(أبو زيد الأنصاري,ابن عباس,السهيلي,الفراء,حسان); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:201/q05/p2 Ṭāʾif circles as an active visitor; ṭayf is a fleeting touch, thought, apparition or madness. — Abu Amr, al-Kisai and others distinguish the circling agent from the touch or whisper. Al-Farisi calls ṭayf a passing thought and ṭāʾif a visitor or thought. Al-Suhayli makes ṭayf an unreal image, ṭāʾif Satan or a real visitor, citing 68:19. Jishumi transmits ṭayf as madness. Ibn Atiyya objects with al-A'sha's jinn visitation; Abu Hayyan and al-Samin defend al-Kisai, arguing a jinn affliction can circle and the poem need not concern a human target. [26 notes: TAB-FULL(آخر من الكوفيين,بعض الكوفيين); THALABI(أبو عمرو,بعض المكيين); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عمرو,بعضهم); JISHUMI(أبو عمرو وأبو عبيد); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عمرو); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمرو); RAZI-FULL(غير مسمى); QURTUBI-FULL(السهيلي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي,السهيلي,الكسائي,قيل); SAMIN-DURR(السدي,السهيلي,الفارسي,الكسائي); QIRAAT-ER; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الكسائي)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)-; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية)-]
- 7:201/q05/p3 Ṭayf is a verbal noun of ṭāfa yaṭīfu, or a shortened doubled-yāʾ form. — Abu Ubayda and al-Tabari cite yaṭīf in poetry. Ibn al-Anbari derives it from ṭayyif, with one yāʾ omitted like hayyin and mayyit; Sa'id's doubled reading supports him. Some derive it from the waw root; Abu al-Baqa proposes changing waw to ya, but al-Samin calls this remote and says the preceding vowel is not kasra. Al-Nahhas answers al-Asma'i's objection to the fīʿl verbal-noun pattern by taking the word as equivalent to ṭāʾif. [22 notes: NAHHAS(أبو جعفر,أبو حاتم); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو والكسائي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر,ابن الأنباري,سعيد بن جبير); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء والأخفش); RAZI-FULL(ابن الأنباري,سعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي,النحاس,النحاس وأبو حاتم); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو والكسائي ويعقوب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو عبيدة,ابن الأنباري); ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو والكسائي ويعقوب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفارسي)+; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-]

## 7:201/q06 Why depict the temptation as a circling visitor?  [طَٰٓئِفٌۭ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the image stresses harmless superficial contact or the agency of a circling visitor.
- 7:201/q06/p1 It suggests a fleeting, slight contact that fails to penetrate the pious. — Baydawi and Alusi liken it to circling without effect; Alusi adds that indefiniteness belittles the touch. Ibn Ashur develops the visiting guest who circles before permission to enter. Biqai adds that Satan sometimes surrounds them or affects them, but his circuit may also pass without effect. [8 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:201/q06/p2 It marks an active circling agent, unlike ṭayf's phantom or passing image. — Qiraat-ER contrasts the connotations of the two readings; Ibn Ashur says the familiar dream image is the sense of ṭayf. [2 notes: QIRAAT-ER; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:201/q07 Who or what is 'Satan' here?  [ٱلشَّيْطَٰنِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the definite article denotes the devil-kind or Iblis as commander of devils.
- 7:201/q07/p1 Satan means devils generically, rather than Iblis alone. — Baydawi and Alusi use the plural pronoun in the next verse as evidence for a collective species. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:201/q07/p2 The definite noun may refer to Iblis, whose army whispers by his command. — Ibn Ashur gives this alongside the generic possibility. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:201/q08 How does 'from Satan' attach to the visitation?  [مِّنَ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether ṭāʾif itself is Satan or his whisper.
- 7:201/q08/p1 Min explains the circling visitor if that visitor is Satan himself. — Tabatabai correlates this construction with the interpretation of Satan circling the heart to cast a whisper. [2 notes: TABATABAI]
- 7:201/q08/p2 Min marks origin if the circling visitor is the whisper sent by Satan. — Tabatabai correlates this construction with a whisper circling until it enters and settles, and says the two interpretations converge. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 7:201/q09 How is 'they remembered' read?  [تَذَكَّرُوا۟ · readings]
turns on: The reported variant wording and the grammatical objection to doubling dhāl.
- 7:201/q09/p1 Tadhakkarū, the verse's usual reading. — Al-Nahhas says the proposed doubled-dhal form has no grammatical basis. [2 notes: NAHHAS(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير والنحاس)]
- 7:201/q09/p2 Tadhdhakkarū, with doubled dhāl. — Attributed to Mujahid by al-Nahhas and to Sa'id ibn Jubayr by al-Qurtubi; al-Nahhas objects to its grammar. [0 notes: NAHHAS(مجاهد)-; QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير والنحاس)-]
- 7:201/q09/p3 Taʾammalū, 'they reflected,' replaces tadhakkarū. — Al-Tha'labi attributes a variant to Ibn al-Zubayr; Ibn Atiyya reports Ibn al-Zubayr's reading and a reordered wording in Ubayy's codex. [2 notes: THALABI(عبد الله بن الزبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن الزبير وأبي بن كعب)]

## 7:201/q10 What do the pious remember or do in remembrance?  [تَذَكَّرُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: What recollection is foregrounded: commands and consequences, reflection on proof, refuge, divine lordship, or God's power and favors.
- 7:201/q10/p1 They recall God's commands, prohibitions, reward, punishment, promises and threats. — Al-Tabari and Ibn Kathir name reward and punishment; Razi says blessings and severe punishment turn natural impulse toward divine law. Ibn Atiyya links remembrance to the prior refuge command and to commands and prohibitions; Ibn Ashur calls it bringing prior knowledge to mind and cites 3:135. Alusi also reports Satan's enmity and guile. Biqai includes whatever benefits action and restraint. [17 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس والسدي); RAZI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:201/q10/p2 They reflect on proof, recognize the mistake or danger, and take heed. — Ibn al-Zubayr glosses remembrance as reflection; Zajjaj and Abu Ishaq say they contemplate God's clear proof. Al-Mawardi transmits recognition of danger and a separate account of reflection leading to guidance. Tabatabai says reflection recovers a conclusion previously overlooked or unknown; Study Quran includes 'take heed.' [12 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(عبد الله بن الزبير); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:201/q10/p3 They seek refuge in God, pray, or rely on Him. — Ataa reports from Ibn Abbas that they seek God's protection then perceive His greatness. Abu Rawq says they supplicate earnestly; Ibn Bahr says remembrance is refuge. Abu al-Suud adds reliance, and Tabatabai explains refuge as recognizing God as sole support. Ibn Ashur includes refuge within the broader practice of remembrance and conjectures this community was especially given refuge, as it was given Friday. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو روق,ابن بحر); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(المولى أبو السعود); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 7:201/q10/p4 They remember God's lordship and oneness, seeing Him as the sole agent. — Tabatabai says they recall God as owner and nurturer, entrust affairs to Him and dispel heedlessness. Qashani and Alusi give the inward account in which attribution to another agent is the touch and recollection of tawhid restores sight. [3 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 7:201/q10/p5 They recall God's power and favors. — Al-Nahhas connects this recollection to abandoning the sin and recovering sight; Razi also mentions blessings and punishment. [2 notes: NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL]

## 7:201/q11 What does it mean that they become 'seeing'?  [مُّبْصِرُونَ · interpretation]
- 7:201/q11/p1 They regain insight into truth, guidance, error and Satan's schemes, then leave the wrong path. — Zajjaj calls it sight with insight. Al-Farra makes it stopping on seeing; al-Wahidi and al-Baghawi make it spotting the error. Ibn Atiyya and al-Tabrisi stress turning to truth and the right path. Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi add seeing Satan's snares; Tabatabai describes the veil of heedlessness lifting. Islahi and Study Quran emphasize clarified vision and sound judgment. [32 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,الفراء,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:201/q12 Why say mubṣirūn rather than yubsirūn?  [مُّبْصِرُونَ · rhetoric]
- 7:201/q12/p1 The noun indicates restoration of an enduring quality of insight, immediately and stably. — Al-Burhan and al-Samarrai contrast yubsirun, which would suggest newly acquired seeing. Al-Biqai says the nominal clause and suddenness particle portray immediate stable light of the heart. [3 notes: BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 7:201/q13 What is the force of the two occurrences of idhā?  [إِذَا فَإِذَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the focus is the two particles' syntax and sudden result or a contrast with in in the preceding verse.
- 7:201/q13/p1 The first is temporal and conditional; the second is suddenative, marking immediate insight. — Al-Wahidi compares the second with 'I went out, and behold Zayd'; al-Tabrisi and Razi give the same division. Al-Wahidi distinguishes temporal idha from in and cites al-Shafi'i's conditional-divorce example. Ibn Ashur points to the result fa and sudden idha as signs that firm remembrance promptly leads to action. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الشافعي); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:201/q13/p2 Idhā makes a satanic touch for the pious certain or more likely than the Prophet's possible touch expressed by in. — Abu Hayyan contrasts the conditional particles in 7:200 and this verse and explicitly favors this explanation. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]

## 7:201/q14 What course of action does the verse portray when temptation arrives?  [إِذَا مَسَّهُمْ طَٰٓئِفٌۭ مِّنَ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنِ تَذَكَّرُوا۟ فَإِذَا هُم مُّبْصِرُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the remembered sin was only contemplated, anger was restrained, or a sin had already been committed and required repentance.
- 7:201/q14/p1 A person considers sin or feels anger, remembers God, restrains the impulse and does not commit it. — Muqatil says they identify the suggestion as sin and recoil from fear; Mujahid, al-Dahhak, Abu Abdillah and others describe contemplated sin abandoned. Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Mujahid apply it to an angry person restraining anger. Ibn Atiyya cites the Prophetic report that anger is a jinn troop, with red eyes and swollen veins, and advises refuge. [22 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); THALABI(سعيد بن جبير,مجاهد,مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد,مجاهد وسعيد بن جبير,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير,مجاهد,مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI(سعيد بن جبير ومجاهد,مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جبير,مجاهد,مقاتل); DURR-FULL(الضحاك); TABATABAI(أبو بصير); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 7:201/q14/p2 They may slip into sin, but remember God, repent and return quickly. — Al-Suddi repeatedly explains remembrance as repentance after a lapse; al-Ala ibn Badr says the indecent act was committed. Ibn Kathir says they turn back and seek refuge. Al-Hasan says repeated repentance after sin draws the believer near God; Muhammad ibn Ka'b says God still calls the fallen believer God-fearing. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي,العلاء بن بدر); THALABI(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله بن الزبير والسدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن,السدي,محمد بن كعب)]
- 7:201/q14/p3 At any satanic prompting they recall God, see clearly and obey rather than follow Satan. — Al-Tabari explicitly broadens the verse beyond anger or one lapse. Ibn Abbas says they stop sinning, obey God and disobey Satan; other commentators describe prompt recovery of guidance. Ibn Ashur says action is needed at the first onset before resolve, and al-Zamakhshari calls this remembrance the regular habit of the reverent. [20 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI(ابن عباس والسدي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(al-Zamakhsharī); TAB-FULL+]

## 7:201/q15 How does this verse relate to the preceding command and following contrast?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connection being explained is the refuge command, the forgiveness setting, or the contrast with the next verse.
- 7:201/q15/p1 It confirms and explains the prior command to seek refuge from Satan, extending its example to the pious. — Biqai notes the emphatic general statement after the singular prophetic address in 7:200. Alusi says refuge is their settled practice, unlike the astray. Ibn Ashur says in marks importance, and the explanation is broader than the command because remembrance includes more than refuge. Tabatabai parallels 16:99 on Satan's lack of authority over believers who rely on God. Baydawi, Nasafi and others call it confirmation. [14 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 7:201/q15/p2 It helps the Prophet and believers withstand provocation after the command to forgive opponents. — Islahi situates the counsel amid opponents' baseless objections and says the singular command addresses all Muslims. Kuran Yolu ties 7:199 to anger and retaliation, calling for refuge, patience and tolerance. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 7:201/q15/p3 The next verse contrasts the pious with unbelievers extended in error by satanic brothers. — Al-Burhan says the description of the God-fearing ends at 'mubṣirūn'; the subsequent 'yamuddūnahum' refers to Meccan unbelievers. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 7:201/q15/p4 An earlier statement is connected, but the surviving excerpt does not identify it. — Al-Tabrisi's coherence note is cut off before the earlier statement is named. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 7:201/q16 What does the verse imply about prophetic and ordinary vulnerability to Satan?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the focus is susceptibility, prophetic protection, or the enduring status of a repentant believer.
- 7:201/q16/p1 Even the pious may be touched by Satan; prophets alone are infallible. — Ibn Atiyya argues this from the verse's praise of the pious despite a satanic touch. Razi says the Prophet can receive an initial nudge while the pious may receive a stronger touch; Abu Hayyan contrasts in and idha. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 7:201/q16/p2 The Prophet is specially protected or faces Satan's more intense effort. — Some distinguish the Prophet's initial nazgh from non-prophets' established mass as favor. Another explanation says Satan expends greater effort against the perfect. Ibn Ashur calls the Messenger pious and invites following earlier pious exemplars, citing 6:90. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:201/q16/p3 A believer remains God-fearing after a lapse if he repents. — Muhammad ibn Ka'b says God does not call such a person unbeliever; al-Hasan emphasizes nearness through repentance. A Prophetic comparison likens a believer to a stalk that bends and straightens, ending well. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(الحسن,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,محمد بن كعب)]

## 7:201/q17 How can remembering God dissolve anger?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether restraint is explained through a detailed analysis of anger's assumptions or through remembrance of divine anger and refuge.
- 7:201/q17/p1 It corrects the judgments that sustain anger and redirects the person toward restraint and forgiveness. — Razi says anger assumes the offender is blameworthy, retaliation possible and the target defenseless. Divine decree and foreknowledge undermine the first; God could punish the angry person, so the last two are false. A report attributed to the Prophet says knowing God's secret in decree eases affliction; Razi gives no grading. He adds that revenge resembles beasts and snakes, forgiveness prophets and saints, and the target might later retaliate; the considerations remove the satanic whisper. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 7:201/q17/p2 Recall God's anger against transgressors and seek refuge, then restrain one's own anger. — The 'people of meanings' counsel recollecting God's anger to avoid a ruinous act. Ibn Atiyya's Prophetic anger report recommends seeking refuge, and Kuran Yolu connects this with patience toward provocation. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 7:201/q18 What reports or examples illustrate the verse's response to temptation?  [* · reports]
turns on: These are distinct transmitted incidents and images, rather than competing versions of one event.
- 7:201/q18/p1 Hasan ibn Ali answered an insulter gently, leading the man to remorse and affection. — Asim ibn al-Mustaliq recounts insulting Hasan, who answered with Quran, offered help, and changed Asim's attitude; al-Qurtubi cites 7:199 and 12:92. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عصام بن المصطلق)]
- 7:201/q18/p2 A woman with seizures chose patience and Paradise, and the Prophet prayed she would remain covered. — Ibn Kathir transmits Abu Hurayra's report; al-Hakim graded it sound on Muslim's criteria while neither collection included it. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة)]
- 7:201/q18/p3 A young worshipper died after recalling the verse while tempted by adultery and was promised two gardens. — Ibn Asakir's story says Umar prayed at the grave, cited 55:46, and heard the youth answer that God granted the two gardens twice. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عساكر)]
- 7:201/q18/p4 A saint's vision portrayed remembrance driving a pig-shaped Satan away from the heart. — Bursevi reports a crystal-like human form, a dark mark between the shoulders and a trunk probing the heart. He separately says Satan whispers through a blood-like route, explains prophetic cupping and the seal, and contrasts the Prophet's submitted companion with Adam's. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 7:201/q19 How do inward readings apply the verse to thoughts and the heart?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the inner reading concerns immediate discernment, tawhid, tests for thoughts, or the contrasting condition of false hearts.
- 7:201/q19/p1 A pure heart recognizes satanic thoughts by the light of taqwa and removes them through remembrance. — Sulami describes inner warning and restraint, linking the latter to 79:40. Al-Ta'wilat al-Najmiyya says the heart sees a prompting as a threat to its clarity. Bursevi's teacher says angels continually cast truth into truthful hearts and a satanic touch makes them perceive and erase the intruder; the Merciful shows truth as truth, whereas Satan reverses it. [6 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,حضرة شيخنا الفريد)]
- 7:201/q19/p2 Satanic touch is attributing an act to other than God; recollection of tawhid restores vision of God's agency. — Qashani and Alusi identify this as the inward gloss, with protection from shirk understood at the level of perceived agency. [3 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:201/q19/p3 Angelic and satanic thoughts can be distinguished by their effects, though unlawful or doubtful nourishment obscures discernment. — Bursevi says angelic thoughts lead to good, stable, safe outcomes and delight in worship; contrary effects indicate Satan. Sufi masters say unlawful food prevents distinction; some extend this to food of unknown origin. Mathnawi verses contrast lawful nourishment's light and service with illicit food's envy, traps, ignorance and heedlessness; some warn Satan can disguise falsehood as truth. [4 notes: BURSEVI(المثنوي,المشايخ)]
- 7:201/q19/p4 False hearts receive continual satanic casting and fail to notice or erase it when a merciful touch interrupts. — Bursevi's teacher contrasts this with true hearts' angelic casting and recognition of satanic intrusion, attributing the false condition to divine abandonment. [1 notes: BURSEVI(حضرة شيخنا الفريد)]

## 7:201/q20 What wider guidance follows from the verse about refuge and conduct?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw distinct practical lessons about the manner of seeking refuge and the social conduct it supports.
- 7:201/q20/p1 Refuge must be frequent and inwardly understood to counter satanic incitement. — Razi connects the refuge command before recitation with 16:97–98 and says bare spoken words without understanding do not benefit. Ibn Ashur says refuge is repeatedly commanded here and in Prophetic sayings; Tabatabai equates refuge with remembrance and reliance. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 7:201/q20/p2 Piety calls for patient, peaceful conduct and refusing to answer evil with evil. — Kuran Yolu cites 49:13 for piety as supreme honor and connects 7:199 with generosity, peace and reconciliation rather than retaliation against the ignorant. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 7:201/q21 What reading of in waliyyi Allāh is noted in the adjacent discussion?  [* · readings]
turns on: Whether the transmitted deletion of the middle yāʾ is sound.
- 7:201/q21/p1 The standard form has three yāʾs. — Ibn Khalawayh counts the extra yāʾ of faʿīl, the original root yāʾ and the possessive yāʾ; the first assimilates and the suffix opens to resolve adjacent sukūns. [1 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA]
- 7:201/q21/p2 A reported doubled, open-yāʾ form deletes the middle yāʾ. — Ibn al-Yazidi transmits it from his father from Abu Amr; Ibn Khalawayh qualifies the report with 'if sound.' [1 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA(ابن اليزيدي عن أبيه عن أبي عمرو)]
