# 7:170: 15 questions

## 7:170/q01 Who are those who hold fast to the Book?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ يُمَسِّكُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the contextual Israelites are all scriptural adherents, only believers who accepted Muḥammad, or an example of a general class; some identify the addressees as Muslims.
- 7:170/q01/p1 Believers among the People of the Book, especially ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām and his companions. — Al-Ḥasan specifies believers of earlier communities (IBNABIHATIM, DURR); the majority of exegetes say believing People of the Book (WAHIDI-BASIT). Mujāhid's reports name Ibn Salām and companions, who neither distorted or concealed Moses' Book nor profited from it (BAGHAWI, BURSEVI, ALUSI); IBNJAWZI says the verse was revealed about them, and MUQATIL reports the same. IBNASHUR identifies Jews who awaited and accepted the foretold final messenger; ISLAHI calls them a small truth-preserving group who later converted. [13 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); THALABI(عطاء); WAHIDI-BASIT(عامة المفسرين); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; DURR-FULL(الحسن); BURSEVI(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد وابن زيد); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 7:170/q01/p2 Jews and Christians who adhere to scripture. — MUJAHID says Jews and Christians; TAB-FULL reports his identification as possible, and THALABI specifies preserving Moses' scripture without concealment or violating lawful and forbidden rulings. DURR reports that he meant some of them. [5 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد وابن زيد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 7:170/q01/p3 Muḥammad's community, with Muḥammad himself among them. — ʿAṭāʾ reports this from Ibn ʿAbbās (WAHIDI-BASIT); THALABI says Muḥammad is among them. IBNASHUR also allows an address to Muslims, assuring them they will not treat their Book as the Jews treated theirs. [8 notes: THALABI(عطاء); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء); TABRISI(عطاء); BURSEVI(عطاء); ALUSI-FULL(عطاء); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr)]
- 7:170/q01/p4 Jews who faithfully observe the Torah and their own religious duties. — QURTUBI and the Şevkânî report identify Torah-observant Jews; KURANYOLU prefers this contextual reading of prayer, citing prayer in all revealed religions via Rāghib. STUDYQURAN says Jews following Torah and Mosaic law receive reward, comparing 2:62 and 5:69. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Kurtubî,Râgıb el-İsfahânî,Şevkânî); STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 7:170/q01/p5 Anyone who fulfills the verse's conditions, though Israelites are the immediate setting. — TABATABAI calls the wording general and independent despite its Israelite setting; KURANYOLU says the promise applies beyond Jews to everyone fulfilling its condition. [2 notes: TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 7:170/q02 How is يُمَسِّكُونَ read?  [يُمَسِّكُونَ · readings]
turns on: The reported recitations differ in verb stem, consonant doubling and tense; arguments invoke Arabic government by bi, parallel Qurʾānic forms, the force of intensification, and agreement with aqāmū.
- 7:170/q02/p1 The common reading is yumassikūn, with open mīm and doubled sīn. — TAB-FULL derives it from massaka; IBNMUJAHID names Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī and Ḥafṣ from ʿĀṣim; Abū ʿAmr also uses it and alone doubles the related verb in 60:10. FĀRISĪ, WAHIDI-BASIT, RAZI and QURTUBI prefer its force of whole, persistent or repeated adherence; FĀRISĪ compares 3:119 and 4:150, QURTUBI cites Kaʿb's covenant-and-sieve poem. Abu Ubayd and Abu Hatim favor the bi construction and Ubayy's past variant; WAHIDI notes the common direct-object idiom amsaktuhu. IBNKHALAWAYH calls it renewed adherence; QIRAAT-ER contrasts intensive Form II with simple Form IV. IBNATIYYA, TABRISI and ABUHAYYAN say both have the same basic sense. [24 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون من القراء); IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو,ابن كثير، نافع، ابن عامر، حمزة، الكسائي,حفص عن عاصم); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; THALABI(أبو عبيد وأبو حاتم); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو بكر عن عاصم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير ونافع وحمزة والكسائي وعاصم وأبو عمرو); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمرو,ابن كثير ونافع وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي وحفص عن عاصم); RAZI-FULL(أبو بكر عن عاصم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور); NASHR(الباقون); QIRAAT-ER; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو بكر عن عاصم)+; JISHUMI(أبو عبيد وأبو حاتم)+; QURTUBI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي)+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 7:170/q02/p2 The light reading is yumsikūn, with still mīm and undoubled sīn, from amsaka. — Reported for ʿUmar, Abū al-ʿĀliya, Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim, and in NASHR for Abū Jaʿfar; ALUSI also names Ḥammād, while SAMIN reports Abū ʿAmr. IBNMUJAHID says Abū Bakr also lightens 60:10, unlike Abū ʿAmr. IBNKHALAWAYH cites 33:37; FĀRISĪ and WAHIDI cite 2:229, 33:37 and 5:4 for related forms. NAHHAS cites ordinary Arabic and 60:10; QIRAAT-ER calls Form IV simple retention. FĀRISĪ accepts light 60:10 for retaining a wife and says Forms IV and II may coincide. [25 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(بعض القراء); IBNMUJAHID(عاصم عن أبي بكر); NAHHAS(أبو العالية); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر عن عاصم); JISHUMI(أبو بكر عن عاصم وعمر بن الخطاب وأبو العالية); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو بكر عن عاصم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور وأبو عمرو,عمر بن الخطاب وأبو العالية وعاصم); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو بكر عن عاصم); RAZI-FULL(أبو بكر عن عاصم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو العالية وعاصم في رواية أبي بكر); BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو بكر); NASAFI-FULL(أبو بكر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عمر وأبو العالية وأبو بكر عن عاصم); SAMIN-DURR(أبو بكر عن عاصم وأبو عمرو وأبو العالية); NASHR(أبو جعفر); ALUSI-FULL(أبوبكر وحماد وابن مسعود وأبي); QIRAAT-ER]
- 7:170/q02/p3 A variant uses the past tense istamsakū, 'held fast.' — Reported for al-Aʿmash; ABUHAYYAN also attributes it to ʿAbd Allāh, and ALUSI names Ibn Masʿūd. Some reports give the fuller wa-lladhīna istamsakū bi-l-kitāb. [6 notes: THALABI(الأعمش); JISHUMI(الأعمش); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعمش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله والأعمش); SAMIN-DURR(الأعمش وأبي); ALUSI-FULL(أبوبكر وحماد وابن مسعود وأبي)]
- 7:170/q02/p4 Ubayy's variant uses a past verb, reported as masakū or tamassakū. — THALABI and BAGHAWI say its tense matches aqāmū; reports vary between masakū, musikū, and tamassakū bi-l-kitāb. IBNKHALAWAYH says Ubayy's form is related to the doubled reading; JISHUMI's wording is tamassakū bi-l-qirāʾa. [9 notes: THALABI(أبو عبيد وأبو حاتم,أبي بن كعب); JISHUMI(أبي بن كعب); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبي بن كعب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبي); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله والأعمش); SAMIN-DURR(الأعمش وأبي); ALUSI-FULL(أبوبكر وحماد وابن مسعود وأبي)]

## 7:170/q03 What does holding fast to the Book require?  [يُمَسِّكُونَ بِٱلْكِتَٰبِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the action is described as observance of the present scripture alone or as including acceptance of a later revelation it foretells.
- 7:170/q03/p1 It means believing scripture, acting by its commands, avoiding its prohibitions, and judging by it. — MUQATIL adds no distortion or legalizing forbidden things; TAB-FULL includes acting within God's bounds. FARRĀʾ says taking what the Book contains, ZAJJAJ says belief and judgment, and IBNKATHIR says scripture leads to Muḥammad because it foretells him. BAGHAWI, THALABI, QURTUBI, STUDYQURAN and others stress observance. WAHIDI-BASIT reports an image of clutching what promises deliverance; BIQAI stresses continuous firm adherence amid corruption and refraining from false claims about God. [18 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(مجاهد وابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,الفراء,غيره); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:170/q03/p2 True adherence to an earlier Book includes following its abrogating successor. — BIQAI says the earlier revelation itself witnesses to its successor, so clinging to it requires leaving it when abrogated. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 7:170/q04 Which Book is meant by ٱلْكِتَٰبِ?  [ٱلْكِتَٰبِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the definite Book continues the reference to Israel's inherited scripture in 7:169 or names scripture more broadly; identifying the adherents as Muḥammad's community yields the Qurʾān.
- 7:170/q04/p1 The Torah, the Book given to Moses or inherited by Israel. — Ibn Zayd identifies Moses' Book in TAB-FULL, IBNABIHATIM and DURR. Mujāhid and Ibn Zayd describe its preservation without alteration or concealment in TABRISI. WAHIDI-BASIT calls this the majority view; ABUHAYYAN prefers the same scripture as 7:169. TABATABAI says Torah in the Israelite setting, though also allows Torah with Gospel. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); THALABI(مجاهد وابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(عامة المفسرين); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(مجاهد وابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); BURSEVI(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد وابن زيد); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Kurtubî,Şevkânî); STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 7:170/q04/p2 The Qurʾān. — ʿAṭāʾ identifies the adherents as Muḥammad's community; WAHIDI-BASIT explicitly says their Book on his view is the Qurʾān. BURSEVI and ALUSI pair the same two identifications. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عامة المفسرين); TABRISI(عطاء); BURSEVI(عطاء); ALUSI-FULL(عطاء)]
- 7:170/q04/p3 All divine books. — ABUHAYYAN reports this alternative to the contextually inherited scripture. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 7:170/q04/p4 The Torah together with the Gospel. — TABATABAI offers this as another way to understand the Book in the Israelite setting. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 7:170/q05 How do the verb forms and بِ work in يُمَسِّكُونَ بِٱلْكِتَٰبِ?  [يُمَسِّكُونَ بِٱلْكِتَٰبِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the chosen stem directly takes bi or has an understood object; several sources also treat the stems as equivalent in basic sense.
- 7:170/q05/p1 The doubled form functions like tamassaka, taking bi as the instrument of adherence. — BAGHAWI, IBNATIYYA and Abu Ubayd with Abu Hatim contrast masaktu bi-shayʾ with direct-object amsaktuhu. ABUHAYYAN and SAMIN explain that faʿʿala may have tafaʿʿala's sense; Kaʿb ibn Zuhayr's covenant-and-sieve couplet is cited by NAHHAS, IBNATIYYA, SAMIN and QURTUBI. IBNJAWZI reports Ibn al-Anbārī's related Arabic forms. [12 notes: NAHHAS(كعب بن زهير); THALABI(أبو عبيد وأبو حاتم); JISHUMI(أبو عبيد وأبو حاتم); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(كعب بن زهير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور,كعب بن زهير)]
- 7:170/q05/p2 On the light form, amsaka has an omitted object, while bi marks circumstance or instrument. — ABUHAYYAN supplies their deeds as object and cites transitive usage at 22:65. SAMIN supplies their religion and deeds, with the Book's commands and prohibitions as instrument. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 7:170/q05/p3 Amsaka, masaka, tamassaka and istamsaka are equivalent Arabic ways to say 'hold fast.' — WAHIDI-BASIT, IBNATIYYA, TABRISI, RAZI, NASAFI, ALUSI and TABATABAI report this lexical equivalence; FĀRISĪ says Form IV and II can coincide in meaning. Kaʿb's poem illustrates more than one form. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(أبو بكر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(كعب بن زهير); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 7:170/q06 How is وَٱلَّذِينَ joined to the preceding verse?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the wāw begins a fresh promise or coordinates with alladhīna yattaqūn in the preceding verse; the latter changes the status of the promise to a separate reinforcing statement.
- 7:170/q06/p1 It begins a new nominative clause whose promise is its predicate. — ZAJJAJ states alladhīna is nominative; NAHHAS, most grammarians in WAHIDI-BASIT, RAZI, NASAFI, TABRISI and SAMIN take it as topic. ABUHAYYAN and SAMIN prefer this apparent syntax; RAZI says a warning followed by promise parallels 18:30. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأكثرون من النحويين,الزجاج); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 7:170/q06/p2 It is genitive, coordinated with 'those who fear' in 7:169; the Hereafter is better for both. — IBNATIYYA, BURSEVI and the grammarians reported by WAHIDI-BASIT give the connection; RAZI and NASAFI say the following promise reinforces it. BAYDAWI, ALUSI, ABUHAYYAN and SAMIN report it; SAMIN quotes Zamakhsharī's view that the promise is parenthetical, but objects that 'independent' is the precise term because it does not interrupt syntactically connected elements. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(قوم من النحويين); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري وابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 7:170/q07 On the new-clause reading, how is its predicate linked to 'those who hold fast'?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ إِنَّا لَا نُضِيعُ أَجْرَ ٱلْمُصْلِحِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the return to the nominative subject is implicit, expressed through the generic or overt noun, carried by the article, or supplied by an omitted predicate.
- 7:170/q07/p1 The predicate is the promise with an understood minhum, 'the reformers among them.' — ZAJJAJ prefers this because unbelievers' good acts are lost, citing 47:1 and 88:2–4; believers fulfill faith and duties. NAHHAS, TABRISI, Ibn al-Anbārī in IBNJAWZI, WAHIDI-BASIT and most Basrans in SAMIN and ALUSI supply the return; Ibn al-Anbārī notes some addressees did not reform, denying prophets and Muhammad's foretold coming. [9 notes: NAHHAS; IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج)+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 7:170/q07/p2 The overt phrase 'the reformers' replaces or semantically repeats a pronoun referring to those people. — ZAJJAJ reports a semantic-antecedent argument, illustrated by believer/one who fears God rather than a mere alternative name such as Zayd/Abū ʿAmr. WAHIDI-BASIT and IBNJAWZI cite usage and poetry for making an implied pronoun overt; WAHIDI reports Abū Bakr's preference for the first of two analyses. TABRISI says 'their reward' is the intended sense; ITQAN gives this as manifest noun replacing pronoun. SAMIN attributes semantic repetition to al-Akhfash, citing nominal and relative-clause analogies; ABUHAYYAN reports Abū al-Baqāʾ's acceptance. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ(قوم); WAHIDI-BASIT(بعض النحويين); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(بعض النحويين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر)+]
- 7:170/q07/p3 The general class 'reformers' includes those holding fast and thus itself links the clauses. — ABUHAYYAN prefers no deletion; SAMIN attributes this to Abū al-Baqāʾ, accepts it, and adduces ala layta shiʿrī and niʿma al-rajulu Zayd. IBNASHUR says the description already marks them as reformers and omission gives concise praise. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 7:170/q07/p4 The definite article in al-muṣliḥīn stands for a possessive pronoun, meaning 'their reformers.' — SAMIN gives the Kufan explanation and comparisons from 79:41, 38:50 and 30:3; ALUSI lists it among the predicate links. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:170/q07/p5 The subject has an omitted predicate such as 'We reward them,' and the promise explains or confirms it. — WAHIDI-BASIT reports grammarians inferring 'We give them their reward' from the causal force of the next sentence; TABRISI similarly says the cause stands for the result. Al-Ḥawfī and Abū al-Baqāʾ supply a link, but ABUHAYYAN and SAMIN object that deletion is unnecessary. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(قوم من النحويين); TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي وأبو البقاء)-; SAMIN-DURR(الحوفي)-]

## 7:170/q08 Why is prayer expressed by the past verb أَقَامُوا after a present verb?  [يُمَسِّكُونَ وَأَقَامُوا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the differing tenses signal timing or theological context, or are resolved by interpreted coordination; Ubayy's variant removes the mismatch.
- 7:170/q08/p1 The past has a future or conditional force in context, so tense coordination is valid. — BURHAN reports Ibn ʿUmarūn and others requiring coordinated verbs to match tense but allowing interpreted shifts where meaning remains clear; it compares 25:66 and 25:75. [1 notes: BURHAN(ابن عمرون وغيره)]
- 7:170/q08/p2 The past prevents an earlier community from using its former prayer to justify remaining in its old religion. — BIQAI ties the wording to the obligation to accept a successor revelation. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 7:170/q08/p3 The present marks continuous adherence, while prayer takes place at appointed times. — ALUSI contrasts the well-known verb's ongoing force with prayer's prescribed timing; BAYDAWI says prayer's distinct singular verb signals its superiority among adherent acts. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو بكر)]
- 7:170/q08/p4 A past-tense reading of 'held fast' gives direct tense agreement with 'established.' — THALABI praises Ubayy's reading and BAGHAWI says a past verb rarely coordinates with a present except by meaning. [2 notes: THALABI(أبي بن كعب); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبي بن كعب)]

## 7:170/q09 What does establishing prayer mean here?  [وَأَقَامُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ṣalāh marks specifically the Islamic prayer and acceptance of Muḥammad or prayer as observed under an earlier revealed religion.
- 7:170/q09/p1 Perform prayer faithfully, especially at its proper time and within its prescribed bounds. — Al-Zuhrī specifies proper time in IBNABIHATIM; TAB-FULL says do not neglect its times or boundaries. MUQATIL and THALABI include establishment in observance of scripture. [4 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(الزهري); THALABI]
- 7:170/q09/p2 It is the prayer prescribed by Muḥammad, a sign of accepting him and Islam. — WAHIDI-WAJIZ specifies Muḥammad's prayer. IBNASHUR treats prayer as a metonym for accepting Muḥammad, since Islam is marked by prayer and Muslims are people of the qibla; KURANYOLU reports his suggestion of Jews recognizing Jesus but rejecting distorted Christianity before believing Muḥammad, or of Muslims. [3 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr)]
- 7:170/q09/p3 It means prayer or ritual in the Jews' own religion. — KURANYOLU prefers its lexical sense in context; it cites Rāghib's observation that prayer exists in every revealed religion though practices differ, supporting a Jewish referent. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râgıb el-İsfahânî); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]

## 7:170/q10 Why is prayer singled out after holding fast to the Book?  [يُمَسِّكُونَ بِٱلْكِتَٰبِ وَأَقَامُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes give complementary emphases: prayer's rank among scriptural duties, a prior history of neglect, and its role as a visible sign of covenantal fidelity.
- 7:170/q10/p1 Prayer is included in scripture's commands but mentioned separately for its exceptional rank. — WAHIDI-BASIT and TABRISI stress its high rank or obligation; QUSHAYRI, RAZI and BURSEVI call it the greatest worship after faith or knowledge of God. NASAFI, ABUHAYYAN, BURHAN, ITQAN, ALUSI and TABATABAI call it religion's pillar; ABUHAYYAN adds the boundary from shirk, TABATABAI remembrance of God and submission. BURSEVI's Persian couplet pictures it supporting religion's house. ITQAN explains the general-to-specific device, comparing prayer, Gabriel and Michael at 4:103, 2:98 and 3:104; BURHAN calls it tajrīd and cites Ibn Mālik's requirements of wāw and a distinguishing merit. BURHAN also reports a dispute whether the preceding general expression includes or excludes the special item, with a poetry analogy and 26:148, where both readings are possible because 'gardens' is indefinite. [21 notes: QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو بكر); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(أبو جعفر بن الزبير,ابن مالك); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]
- 7:170/q10/p2 Prayer is emphasized because earlier people had abandoned it. — BIQAI cites 19:59 and also calls prayer one of religion's greatest rites. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 7:170/q10/p3 Persevering in prayer manifests adherence to scripture and guards God's covenant. — ISLAHI uses it to characterize the small group that preserves the truth. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 7:170/q11 Whose reward does God promise not to waste, and what is promised?  [إِنَّا لَا نُضِيعُ أَجْرَ ٱلْمُصْلِحِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the promise is restricted to faithful reformers among the addressed group or stated as a rule for every reformer; the notes also differ in how they describe reform.
- 7:170/q11/p1 The reformers among the believers who hold the Book receive the reward of their faithful deeds. — ZAJJAJ limits it to believers because disbelievers' works are lost, citing 47:1 and 88:2–4; he compares 18:30 and requires belief, duties, and practice. IBNABIHATIM reports al-Ḥasan's 'believers among them.' Ibn al-Anbārī says some of the group failed to reform, by disobeying, denying prophets, or rejecting Muhammad's foretold coming. IBNKATHIR and ELMALILI identify adherents who establish prayer as reformers. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; ZAJJAJ+]
- 7:170/q11/p2 The promise covers all reformers who fulfill its condition and assures full recompense. — TABRISI says each receives what work deserves. BIQAI says no recompense is lost in any way; BURSEVI and al-Kāshifī say their words and deeds are fully paid. STUDYQURAN compares 3:171, 9:120, 11:115, 12:56, 12:90 and 18:30; it also cites 2:62 and 5:69 for Jews, Sabeans and Christians who believe and act well. KURANYOLU says the general wording extends beyond Jews, rejects condemnation of an entire people as unjust, and compares Israel's conditional mercy at 17:4–8. SAMARRAI says the subgroup falls in the wider class of reformers. [18 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 7:170/q11/p3 Reform involves outward obedience and inward purification, by which the self becomes receptive to divine light. — BURSEVI says the self must be disciplined; TABATABAI links adherence to scripture with reform of human nature and social life, citing 30:30 and 3:19, and rejects the claim that divine law does not serve human welfare. [3 notes: BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 7:170/q12 Why say 'the reward of the reformers' rather than 'their reward'?  [أَجْرَ ٱلْمُصْلِحِينَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The stated effects of the overt noun are inclusion of a wider class, naming reform as the cause of reward, and stylistic explicitness.
- 7:170/q12/p1 The explicit class name identifies the adherents as reformers and extends the promise to every reformer. — SAMARRAI-ASRAR and both SAMARRAI-LAMASAT notes say the named subgroup belongs to the general class; BIQAI likewise says the reward is generalized. IBNASHUR sees concise praise because the earlier description already entails reform. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 7:170/q12/p2 The explicit noun makes reform the stated reason they are saved or rewarded. — BURHAN says the noun in place of a pronoun gives this cause; ALUSI includes the same implication in the analysis of the overt noun. [2 notes: BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:170/q12/p3 The verse uses a manifest synonym instead of repeating a pronoun or exact wording. — ITQAN says this is more eloquent than exact repetition and compares the reformers' reward here to the reward of one who does good in Hūd 11:115; it also notes a parallel manifest noun in al-Kahf. [2 notes: ITQAN]

## 7:170/q13 How does this verse relate to the surrounding account?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes connect the verse to the preceding corruption, the covenant setting, or the earlier mountain episode.
- 7:170/q13/p1 It contrasts corrupt Israelites with faithful reformers who preserve scripture and receive reward. — BIQAI opposes them to those who falsely spoke about God and spoiled the Book, linking the faithful to 7:159. IBNASHUR says covenant-breakers are followed by those upholding scripture and its prophetic message; KURANYOLU contrasts greed and abandoned commands in 7:169 with those who remain faithful. STUDYQURAN says the larger discussion returns to Israel's later disobedience and punishments. KURANYOLU says 7:168–170 confirms justice for Jews and others and rejects collective condemnation, citing 17:4–8. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:170/q13/p2 It recalls the covenant and divine promise; the following reminder addresses both Israel's covenant and humanity's primordial covenant. — QUSHAYRI emphasizes God's true promise and loving care in making servants know Him. ELMALILI identifies the two covenants in the next reminder. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; ELMALILI]
- 7:170/q13/p3 The preceding mountain-lifting episode means God raised the mountain above them. — MAJAZ explains nataqnā al-jabal through lines by al-ʿAjjāj and Ruʾba using nataqa for lifting or raising; QUSHAYRI says coerced assent to the Book was soon answered with distortion. [2 notes: MAJAZ; QUSHAYRI]

## 7:170/q14 What inward meanings are drawn from holding the Book, prayer and God's promise?  [* · inward]
turns on: These are complementary inward readings of adherence, worship, reward and divine address.
- 7:170/q14/p1 Holding the Book is faith and a means of eventual safety; prayer is excellence and present communion, leading to divine pleasure. — QUSHAYRI pairs faith with immediate safety and excellence with deferred pleasure, then pairs adherence with final salvation and prayer with present intimate communion. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال)]
- 7:170/q14/p2 Hope in God's generosity is never wasted and brings blessing, favor, honor and support. — QUSHAYRI says the hopeful bargain does not fail; a step toward God's door brings blessing in the next life, lifted aspirations win present favor, and reliance on His generosity brings honor in both worlds and sufficiency. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال)]
- 7:170/q14/p3 Voluntary loving obedience differs from coerced compliance, which does not recognize God's worth. — QUSHAYRI cites poems questioning affection requiring an advocate and comparing admonition of a fickle person to writing on water; forced affection is unnatural. Those coerced to accept the Book soon distorted it. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 7:170/q14/p4 God's prior care gives gnosis before created influence, while one address yields differing inward responses. — QUSHAYRI says gnosis precedes what others see, effects in hearts, and speech of kin; a poem depicts divine love finding the heart ready before it knows love. Some recognize God through a near address, others remain veiled; some affirm unity when unveiled, others deny despite witnessing. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال)]

## 7:170/q15 What accounts illustrate the danger of later neglecting scripture and inward reform?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reported examples differ in speaker and event but illustrate the same later spiritual decline.
- 7:170/q15/p1 Later people may abandon the Qurʾān for worldly adornment and need spiritual discipline to overcome doubt. — BURSEVI contrasts blessed predecessors with wretched successors and says only divine enabling and a skilled spiritual guide can train the self and remove doubt. An unattributed Persian couplet depicts living as though in a grave without concern for death or winter provision. [3 notes: BURSEVI]
- 7:170/q15/p2 Al-Ḥasan recalled seventy Badr veterans whose asceticism and joy in hardship surpassed later people. — He says they abstained from lawful things more than later people avoid unlawful ones, seemed mad to later observers, and feared lawful wealth would corrupt their hearts. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الحسن)]
- 7:170/q15/p3 Uways told Hurm that doubt had entered hearts so deeply that admonition no longer helped. — BURSEVI reports their conversation about where to live and Syria's livelihood. [1 notes: BURSEVI(هرم وأويس)]
