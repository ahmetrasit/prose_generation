# 13:10: 23 questions

## 13:10/q01 What does equality here say about God's knowledge?  [سَوَآءٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the paired descriptions are a general contrast or describe one person's concealed wrongdoing.
- 13:10/q01/p1 Secret and open speech, night concealment and daytime exposure are equally known to God. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, and others extend the contrast to hidden and witnessed deeds. Ibn Kathīr cites 20:7, 27:25, 11:5 and 10:61, and ʿĀʾisha's report that she missed part of a woman's plea which God heard (58:1). Al-Qurṭubī and al-Māwardī specify good and evil. Al-Rāzī and al-Bayḍāwī stress comprehensive knowledge; human sight is obscured by night (al-Māwardī, al-Ṭabrisī). [56 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(أبو رجاء,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الحسن,قتادة); NAHHAS(أبو جعفر); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو العباس,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,الأكثرون); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عائشة); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 13:10/q01/p2 The paired figures are sinful in secret at night and overt by day, yet neither state hides sin from God. — Muqātil says the same sin is hidden then spread. Mujāhid and al-Rāzī also describe sins committed openly by day. Al-Wāḥidī notes this requires supplying 'sins' in both clauses. Ibn ʿAṭiyya extends the analysis to deeds; al-Kāshifī includes speech and acts. Asad reports Ibn ʿAbbās, via al-Baghawī and al-Rāzī, as taking daytime going forth to be open evil deeds. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); ASAD-NOTES(Ibn 'Abbas)]
- 13:10/q01/p3 The verse specifically exposes a suspicious person who sins at night but presents himself as innocent by day. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid identify one person; Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers this reading and argues that omission of a second man otherwise occurs only in poetry. Al-Ālūsī dismisses Ibn ʿAṭiyya's three-class version. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(العوفي عن ابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس ومجاهد); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية)-]

## 13:10/q02 How does sawāʾ govern the sentence?  [سَوَآءٌۭ مَّنْ أَسَرَّ ٱلْقَوْلَ وَمَن جَهَرَ بِهِۦ · grammar]
turns on: Whether sawāʾ is predicate or subject, and whether its verbal-noun sense needs an omitted possessive.
- 13:10/q02/p1 Sawāʾ is a fronted predicate; the two relative expressions are its delayed subject in the nominative. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Zajjāj say equality requires two terms. Al-Ṭabarī makes the first man subject and the others coordinated. Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī say sawāʾ remains singular because it began as a verbal noun; Abū Zayd attests a dual form. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports the alternative ordering. [9 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 13:10/q02/p2 Sawāʾ is a verbal noun whose predication requires an omitted 'possessors of equality'. — Al-Zajjāj says verbal nouns cannot directly govern nouns as participles do; al-Khansāʾ's verse illustrates an omitted possessive. Al-Naḥḥās reports Abū Isḥāq's analogy with 'a man of justice'; al-Ṭabrisī and al-Rāzī repeat it. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ(الخنساء); NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الخنساء,فرقة); TABRISI(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL]
- 13:10/q02/p3 Sawāʾ functions as an adjective meaning 'equal' without ellipsis. — Al-Zajjāj says frequent use made the verbal noun participial; Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares ʿadl, zawr and ḍayf. Abū Bakr prefers overt syntax without an omitted noun. Sībawayh objects to beginning with an indefinite participle, a point reported by al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī. [17 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق وأبو بكر); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج,فرقة); TABRISI(سيبويه); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL(سيبويه,قائل); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,ابن عطية); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه)-]
- 13:10/q02/p4 Sawāʾ is the subject, qualified by minkum, with the following expression filling its predicate slot. — Al-Naḥḥās says the predicate is delayed. Al-Samīn and Abū Ḥayyān cite Sībawayh's example 'good and evil are equal to him'; al-Samīn disputes Ibn ʿAṭiyya's report that Sībawayh rejected this indefinite opening. Al-Naḥḥās cites Ḥassān's poem for the equality in standing before God of insult, praise and defense of the Messenger. [6 notes: NAHHAS(حسان); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,ابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 13:10/q03 How is minkum attached?  [مِّنكُم · grammar]
turns on: Which constituent the prepositional phrase limits.
- 13:10/q03/p1 Minkum is circumstantial to the implicit subject of sawāʾ. — Al-Samīn, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī use sawāʾ's sense 'equal'; Abū al-Baqāʾ and al-Ālūsī bar attaching it to the hidden subject of asarra or jahara because that would front an element of a relative clause. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 13:10/q03/p2 Minkum qualifies sawāʾ or attaches to it in the sense 'equal among you'. — Al-Qurṭubī reports both formulations; the latter compares the construction 'I passed by Zayd'. Al-Samīn reports the subject parse is licensed by this qualification. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 13:10/q03/p3 Minkum is understood separately after both speakers: the secret speaker among you and the open speaker among you. — Al-Qurṭubī supplies the repeated phrase after each clause. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 13:10/q04 What are secret and public qawl?  [أَسَرَّ ٱلْقَوْلَ وَمَن جَهَرَ بِهِۦ · meaning]
turns on: Whether concealed qawl is inward thought, a very quiet utterance, or an inward state of knowledge.
- 13:10/q04/p1 Secret speech is inward or self-addressed, and public speech is voiced to another. — Al-Māwardī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Zajjāj give this contrast. Al-Ālūsī also allows an utterance so quiet only its speaker hears it; public speech contrasts with both senses. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 13:10/q04/p2 Qawl can mean an unspoken idea or thought here. — Asad says the literal meaning is utterance, but figurative usage includes an idea merely conceived; al-ʿĀshūr's context and Abū Ḥayyān's specification concern hidden and spoken words. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 13:10/q04/p3 The contrast is between latent knowledge and knowledge brought from potentiality into actuality. — Al-Qāshānī reads inner speech and public speech as stages of manifestation. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 13:10/q05 What does mustakhfin by night mean?  [مُسْتَخْفٍۭ بِٱلَّيْلِ · meaning]

Al-Wāḥidī gives the forms akhfaytu, khafiya and istakhfa, with rare ikhtafa. Al-Biqāʿī says the hider falsely expects to escape divine power; Abū Ḥayyān cites two poems on concealed deeds. ## 13:10/q06 What does sārib by day mean?  [سَارِبٌۢ بِٱلنَّهَارِ · meaning]

Al-Ṭabarī, al-Māwardī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī cite poems by Qays or unnamed poets; al-Akhnas's stallion, camel and wolf examples stress free movement. Al-Māwardī distinguishes evening sarūb from morning sarūj. Ibn ʿĀshūr derives the verb from the concrete road noun. Arab linguists differ on fatḥa versus kasra for the sīn in āmin fī sirbihi, al-Ṭabarī reports. ## 13:10/q07 How are the night-hidden and day-traveling descriptions joined?  [وَمَنْ هُوَ مُسْتَخْفٍۭ بِٱلَّيْلِ وَسَارِبٌۢ بِٱلنَّهَارِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the final adjective coordinates with a person, an adjective, or an omitted relative.
- 13:10/q07/p1 Sārib coordinates with the whole man huwa mustakhfin phrase or with man, contrasting two people. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī give this parse. Al-Ālūsī says huwa makes the first description explicit and the matching description before sārib is omitted. The author of al-Kashf says the generic relative and descriptive forms coincide, with the descriptive form more explicit; a definite referent would suggest an unintended implication. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI(بحر العلوم); ALUSI-FULL(صاحب الكشف)]
- 13:10/q07/p2 Sārib coordinates with mustakhfin, while man covers two people by sense. — Al-Zamakhsharī illustrates a dual man with a line about two companions; al-Samīn says the wolf line coordinates on sense alone and criticizes al-Zamakhsharī's wording. Al-Ālūsī notes that sawāʾ needs two compared things. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(الشاعر); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]
- 13:10/q07/p3 A second relative man is omitted before sārib. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn say Basrans forbid such relative deletion but Kufans permit it. Al-Ālūsī cites Abū Firās and Ḥassān; al-Zamakhsharī objects to combining two omissions. Al-Burhān compares 37:164. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري,قيل)]
- 13:10/q07/p4 The absence of repeated man favors one person or one category who rests at night and travels by day. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says otherwise man would have to be deleted, a move he finds only in poetry; Abū Ḥayyān reports his view. Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid read the two moments as one person's conduct. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس ومجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 13:10/q07/p5 The wording may enumerate three classes: secret speaker, public speaker, and a person who changes conduct between night and day. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya supplies sin and apparent innocence for the third class. Al-Ālūsī rejects this extension. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية)-]
- 13:10/q07/p6 The comparison can concern two states regardless of whether one or two people occupy them. — Al-Ālūsī reports this nominalized construal but objects that Arabic syntax does not sustain it. The note preserves the proposed reading despite al-Ālūsī's objection. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]

## 13:10/q08 Why pair secrecy with night and open movement with day?  [مُسْتَخْفٍۭ بِٱلَّيْلِ وَسَارِبٌۢ بِٱلنَّهَارِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the pairing explains natural visibility or serves intensified rhetorical contrast.
- 13:10/q08/p1 Night is naturally more concealing and daylight makes movement more visible, sharpening the equal-knowledge contrast. — Al-Zajjāj, Abū Bakr, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ālūsī use this fit against the reversed gloss. Abū Ḥayyān aligns night with secret speech and day with open speech. Al-Biqāʿī calls the omitted counterparts iḥtibāk; al-Burhān identifies antithesis and cites 3:26 and 35:19–22. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الأخفش وقطرب); WAHIDI-BASIT+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 13:10/q08/p2 The contrast stretches knowledge beyond human plausibility, a rhetorical hyperbole only relative to listeners. — Al-Burhān defines hyperbole as intensification beyond ordinary mental expectation and says divine knowledge itself is not exaggerated. [2 notes: BURHAN]

## 13:10/q09 Does the wording carry a warning or practical lesson?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The moral application drawn from God's knowledge.
- 13:10/q09/p1 Awareness of God's knowledge calls for vigilance and self-accounting. — Study Quran urges scrutiny of actions, thoughts and heart, and reports ʿUmar's instruction to take oneself to account before judgment. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb)]
- 13:10/q09/p2 No wrongdoer can hide from God's grasp or make Him act hastily. — Islāḥī connects equal knowledge of deeds and thoughts with God's ability to seize wrongdoers at His chosen time. Al-Ṭabarī mentions sinners relying on guards against enforcement of divine limits. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 13:10/q10 To whom or what concrete event was this verse applied?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which narrated event the passage was revealed about.
- 13:10/q10/p1 It was revealed concerning ʿĀmir and Arbad's plot against the Prophet. — Ibn ʿAbbās identifies both men's intentions. Ibn Zayd details ʿĀmir's demand for power and territory, his threat, Arbad's failed sword draw under God's protection, lightning killing Arbad and plague killing ʿĀmir; the report links this verse with protecting angels and futile disbelievers' prayer. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد)]
- 13:10/q10/p2 The passage was revealed about an unnamed swollen man who returned to al-Sulūliyya's house and died on horseback. — Al-Wāḥidī describes a camel-like swelling and his repetition of his fate; the revealed span extends from hidden/public speech to the disbelievers' prayer. [1 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB]

## 13:10/q11 How does this verse relate to its surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the clause explains God's general knowledge or advances the passage's warning.
- 13:10/q11/p1 It spells out the preceding statement that God knows unseen and witnessed things. — Abū Ḥayyān says the general claim is specified for accountable people's words. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it an explanatory resumption; Study Quran identifies 13:9. Al-Ālūsī says the added enumeration marks special attention. Al-Biqāʿī links it to people's limited secret knowledge and reliance on guards. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 13:10/q11/p2 The shift to direct address instructs believers and unbelievers and implicitly threatens polytheists plotting against the Prophet. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the pronoun shift and its target; the ʿĀmir-Arbad reports provide a narrative application. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس)]
- 13:10/q11/p3 This belongs to a sequence showing God's knowledge, power and transcendence as grounds for resurrection, requested signs and punishment. — Al-Rāzī says delay of these is ascribed by one group to will and by another to wisdom. Islāḥī notes wrongdoers cannot compel haste. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

- 13:10/q12/p1 The wāw divides alternatives and has the force of 'or'. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the conjunction as distribution among the listed categories. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 13:10/q13 What inward contrast is read in night concealment and daytime travel?  [مُسْتَخْفٍۭ بِٱلَّيْلِ وَسَارِبٌۢ بِٱلنَّهَارِ · inward]
turns on: Whether the contrast is read as a movement of spiritual stations or equal fidelity.
- 13:10/q13/p1 The hidden person remains in the self's dark night, while the traveler moves from selfhood to the spirit's daylight. — Al-Qāshānī maps the two conditions to spiritual stations. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 13:10/q13/p2 Concealing or voicing what God entrusted inwardly can both be faithful wakefulness, whether motivated by concern or joyful love. — Al-Naṣrābādhī, reported by al-Sulamī, interprets the inner and outer voices as states of fidelity. [1 notes: SULAMI(النصرآباذي)]

## 13:10/q14 What supports the reading of akhfīhā in 20:15 as 'I reveal it'?  [مُسْتَخْفٍۭ · readings]
- 13:10/q14/p1 Arabic khafaytu can mean 'I disclosed'; the reading with ḍamma is preferred by al-Akhfash. — Al-Akhfash cites an anonymous mutaqārib poem with nukhfihi 'we reveal it'; the parallel 20:15 reading is used to support the reversed gloss of mustakhfin. [5 notes: AKHFASH(الشاعر); TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي أهل البصرة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); AKHFASH+]

## 13:10/q15 What can akādu mean in the cited comparison?  [مُسْتَخْفٍۭ · meaning]
- 13:10/q15/p1 Some take akādu as arīdu, 'I intend,' or 'I am about to'. — Al-Akhfash cites 18:77, where a wall 'intends' to collapse, and a kāmil poem juxtaposing kādat/kudtu and irāda. [2 notes: AKHFASH(الشاعر,زعموا)]

## 13:10/q16 Is seeking refuge obligatory before Qur'an recitation?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether the command and prophetic practice establish recurring obligation, one-time obligation or recommendation.
- 13:10/q16/p1 It is obligatory every time, inside or outside prayer. — ʿAṭāʾ invokes the Prophet's constant practice and 7:158, the command of 16:98 recurring with every recitation, the obligation to repel Satan, and precaution. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(عطاء)]
- 13:10/q16/p2 One recitation in a lifetime discharges the obligation. — Ibn Sīrīn gives this limited obligatory view. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن سيرين)]
- 13:10/q16/p3 It is recommended, not obligatory. — The majority cite the Prophet's omission of the formula in teaching a Bedouin prayer, though an objection says that teaching was not exhaustive. Mālik omits it in obligatory prayer but uses it in Ramadan night prayer. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(الأكثرون,الباقون,الجمهور)]

## 13:10/q17 Should the refuge formula be recited quietly or aloud?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether precedent or analogy to the opening supplication guides the choice.
- 13:10/q17/p1 Either manner is permitted, with loud recitation preferred in al-Shāfiʿī's al-Imlāʾ. — Al-Shāfiʿī cites Ibn ʿUmar's quiet practice and Abū Hurayra's aloud practice. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الشافعي)+]
- 13:10/q17/p2 Quiet recitation is favored. — Al-Rāzī compares the formula to the opening supplication rather than al-Fātiḥa and treats loudness as an added quality. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 13:10/q18 How may non-scriptural sciences relate to Qur'anic interpretation?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Which disciplines the Qur'an contains, assists, alludes to or leaves aside.
- 13:10/q18/p1 The Qur'an includes prophetic histories, ethics, jurisprudence, law, creed, legal theory, Arabic and rhetoric. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls these first-level sciences contained in it. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 13:10/q18/p2 Wisdom, astronomy and the properties of created things can broaden an exegete's knowledge. — Ibn ʿĀshūr's second level describes helpful background disciplines. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 13:10/q18/p3 Earth strata, medicine and logic may be alluded to by or support the Qur'an. — Ibn ʿĀshūr's third level admits supporting or alluded-to fields. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 13:10/q18/p4 Divination, augury, mythology, prosody and rhyme do not serve interpretation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr's fourth level includes invalid arts and fields without interpretive use. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 13:10/q18/p5 Philosophical sciences should not be reconciled with Qur'anic meanings because of doctrinal error. — Ibn al-ʿArabī argues against this reconciliation; Ibn ʿĀshūr says his contempt for philosophers goes too far. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن العربي)]

## 13:10/q19 Was Sūrat al-Raʿd wholly revealed in Mecca?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The classification of the sūra's place of revelation.
- 13:10/q19/p1 The whole sūra is Makkan, with no Madinan verse. — Islāḥī rejects the codices' Madinan classification. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 13:10/q20 What is the sūra's governing aim?  [* · links]
- 13:10/q20/p1 It portrays revelation as intrinsically true, while its heard force leads some to guidance and leaves others blind or astray. — Al-Biqāʿī likens this variation to thunder, lightning and rain benefiting, failing or harming according to where they fall. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 13:10/q21 Can one person possess both innate perfection and acquired excellence?  [* · interpretation]
- 13:10/q21/p1 Only figuratively: innate perfection and excellence won through effort and removal of vices are distinct. — Al-Uqlīshī distinguishes intellect, character and conduct from acquired moral development. [1 notes: BURHAN(الأقليشي)]

## 13:10/q22 What is the scope of divine knowledge compared with human knowledge?  [* · theology]
- 13:10/q22/p1 God's knowledge is infinite, complete and certain through every stage of life; human knowledge is limited and changeable. — Kuran Yolu traces the contrast from conception to death; al-Māwardī and al-Ṭabrisī mention human inability to see nighttime states. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI]

## 13:10/q23 What do appointed angels do in the surrounding passage?  [* · interpretation]
- 13:10/q23/p1 Angels appointed by God watch over people. — Islāḥī states their role; Ibn Zayd's ʿĀmir-Arbad story links their protection to 13:10–11. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد)]
