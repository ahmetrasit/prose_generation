# 59:24: 31 questions

## 59:24/q01 How does the opening pronoun and name function?  [هُوَ ٱللَّهُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether huwa is a pronoun of matter or resumes the preceding divine name.
- 59:24/q01/p1 He is a pronoun of matter, with the following sentence as its predicate. — Ibn ʿAshur refers to the preceding verse for the same analysis. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 59:24/q01/p2 He refers back to God's preceding name; Allah is its predicate and al-Khāliq its attribute. — Ibn ʿAshur gives this as the alternative parse. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 59:24/q01/p3 The pronoun truly applies to God alone, the necessary being. — Al-Biqāʿī contrasts God's existence through Himself with contingent beings that might not exist. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 59:24/q02 What does the name Allah signify here?  [ٱللَّهُ · theology]
turns on: The reported etymologies differ; the theological descriptions address the name's function.
- 59:24/q02/p1 Allah uniquely names the divine Essence, gathering all perfections; the other names describe it. — Al-Biqāʿī calls it the familiar true name without namesake; Elmalılı treats al-Raḥmān as exclusive too; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says its repetition in three verses reinforces the point. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 59:24/q02/p2 Allah's derivation is from ilāh, its article replacing the hamza. — Al-Zajjāj transmits al-Khalīl through Sībawayh. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ(سيبويه)]
- 59:24/q02/p3 Allah's original form is lāh, with an inseparable definite article. — Al-Zajjāj reports al-Khalīl's other derivation. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ(الخليل)]

## 59:24/q03 Why identify God as the Creator and repeat Allah?  [ٱللَّهُ ٱلْخَٰلِقُ · theology]
turns on: Whether sole creation itself establishes exclusive divinity or requires the premise of sovereign lordship.
- 59:24/q03/p1 God alone truly creates, so alleged partners have no creative power and no claim to worship. — Al-Ṭabarī excludes any co-creator; Ibn ʿĀshūr finds exclusivity in the definite subject and predicate and cites 16:20, 16:17. Al-Biqāʿī says all acts, visible and unseen, are His, opposing the Qadariyya; Elmalılı says human art cannot originate even an atom. Islahi draws the duty of worship. [7 notes: ELMALILI; BIQAI-FULL+; ELMALILI+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; TAB-FULL+]
- 59:24/q03/p2 God's unique creation alone does not establish exclusive worship, since idolaters admit His creation yet worship other lords. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes creation names here from the preceding verses' lordship and sovereign management, which entail worship. [2 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 59:24/q03/p3 The Qurʾān does not leave people merely to predestination. — Muṭarrif ibn al-Shikhkhīr is reported with this assertion in Muqātil's discussion of the verse. [1 notes: MUQATIL(مطرف بن الشخير)]

## 59:24/q04 What does al-Khāliq mean?  [ٱلْخَٰلِقُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether khalq stresses prior determination, origination without a model, or a broader process of formation.
- 59:24/q04/p1 The one who measures, determines, and plans what He brings into existence according to will and wisdom. — Al-Naḥḥās cites poetry for khalq as measuring; al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī cite 39:6 and successive states, and al-Thaʿlabī 71:14. Zuhayr's line, cited by Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī, contrasts executing a plan with empty wishes. Ibn Kathīr's cutter measures before cutting; al-Biqāʿī uses the shoemaker. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites al-Rāghib's sound proportioning and al-Kashshāf's gloss. Al-Ṭabrisī separately reports a wise determination followed by willing origination. [29 notes: NAHHAS(محمد بن إبراهيم بن عرفة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(شاعرهم); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الراغب,الكشاف); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(زهير); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الشاعر); TABRISI(قيل)]
- 59:24/q04/p2 The initiator who brings what did not exist into being without prior material or model. — Al-Khaṭṭābī calls Him inventor without a model. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls origination the dominant divine-name sense and cites 50:38, distinguishing it from image-making; al-ʿArabī's broader formulation includes qualities. Al-Ṭabrisī includes bodies and accidents. [9 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الخطابي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن العربي)]
- 59:24/q04/p3 Creation can mean producing one thing from another or forming a thing in a specific shape. — Elmalılı and al-Ālūsī record the broader material sense. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes Jesus' forming a clay bird at 3:49. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 59:24/q04/p4 In inward interpretation, He measures manifestations according to His names and attributes. — Al-Qāshānī relates creation to the appearance of divine names. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 59:24/q05 What does al-Bāriʾ mean, and what is its scope?  [ٱلْبَارِئُ · meaning]
turns on: The proposed roots and usage differ, especially whether the name denotes origination, differentiation, adjustment, or a restricted class of creatures.
- 59:24/q05/p1 The originator who brings creatures from nonexistence into existence. — Al-Ṭabarī ascribes it to divine power; al-Māwardī cites poetry; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Bursawī add freedom from disproportion, and Ibn Kathīr emphasizes carrying out the determined plan. Al-Naḥḥās reports Ibn ʿUrafa's origination without prior model, addition, or subtraction. Al-Ṭabrisī includes bodies and accidents; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Ghazālī's origination gloss but rejects it as contrary to usage. [19 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الشاعر); NAHHAS(محمد بن إبراهيم بن عرفة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الغزالي)]
- 59:24/q05/p2 The one who separates, differentiates, and distinguishes creatures or their forms. — Al-Māwardī appeals to separating oneself from someone; al-Kashshāf and Ibn ʿĀshūr use varied shapes, and the latter prefers this over other glosses. Al-Biqāʿī adds refining and repairing what was measured; limited makers leave defects. Ṭabāṭabāʾī and al-Qāshānī also stress differentiation. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الكشاف); TABATABAI; QASHANI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 59:24/q05/p3 The one who proportions and adjusts what was previously measured. — Al-Naḥḥās prefers this distinct act; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Bursawī stress freedom from disproportion and defect, and Elmalılı orderly completion. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; NAHHAS+]
- 59:24/q05/p4 The maker or originator specifically of bodies, not accidents such as color or taste. — Al-Rāzī cites the term bariyya for corporeal creatures; Elmalılı reports his distinction. Al-Ṭabrisī instead explicitly includes both bodies and accidents. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI(Mefâtîhü’l-Gayb)]
- 59:24/q05/p5 Creation especially of living beings or animals, without a prior model. — Bint al-Shāṭi prefers Ibn al-Athīr's distinction, citing 2:54 and 98:6–7, while 57:22 shows rare nonliving use. Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects restriction to humans with 98:6–7, a hadith and ʿAlī's saying; al-Rāghib glosses bariyya as creatures. [6 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن الأثير); IBNASHUR-FULL(الراغب); BINTSHATI-IJAZ+]
- 59:24/q05/p6 Creation specifically of humankind from dust. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Ibn al-ʿArabī's derivation from dust and interpretation of bariyya in 98:6–7 as people, then cites evidence that the term is wider. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن العربي); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 59:24/q05/p7 It is essentially synonymous with al-Khāliq, creator. — Al-Naḥḥās calls this loose usage, perhaps eased by the adjacent al-Khāliq. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qāmūs but prefers differentiation; Bint al-Shāṭi finds the gloss plausible. [5 notes: NAHHAS(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية); BINTSHATI-IJAZ; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 59:24/q05/p8 From the root's two principles, creation and separation or distance. — Bint al-Shāṭi reports Ibn Fāris; barāʾa and tabarruʾ illustrate distance. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن فارس)]
- 59:24/q05/p9 The divine name is specially applied to God. — Bint al-Shāṭi reports al-Rāghib's characterization. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب)]
- 59:24/q05/p10 It is the active participle of hamzated baraʾa. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives the morphological derivation. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 59:24/q05/p11 In inward interpretation, the Maker differentiates manifestations as distinct forms within His Essence. — Al-Qāshānī gives this inward reading. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 59:24/q06 What does al-Muṣawwir mean?  [ٱلْمُصَوِّرُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether form refers to visible outlines alone, inward qualities too, or the changing stages of living creatures.
- 59:24/q06/p1 The one who gives creatures their distinct forms and appearances as He wills. — Muqātil details sex, color and bodily soundness in the womb; al-Thaʿlabī and Ibn al-Jawzī stress recognizable differences. Al-Rāghib, cited by al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı, distinguishes sensible and intellectual forms and cites 7:11; al-Ṭabrisī includes animals and inanimate things. Elmalılı cites 3:6 and 82:7; Ibn Kathīr 82:8. [31 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الرازي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); ELMALILI(Râgıb); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 59:24/q06/p2 The shaper of developing humans through successive womb forms or stages of life. — Al-Māwardī cites al-Nābigha's line, from sperm to clot to flesh and age; Muqātil describes limbs emerging from the lump, while al-Qurṭubī gives clot, lump and recognizable form. [4 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 59:24/q06/p3 In inward interpretation, He forms the detailed manifestations of His attributes. — Al-Qāshānī links outward form to divine attributes. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 59:24/q06/p4 God's form-giving embraces both visible shapes and intellectual or spiritual qualities. — Al-Rāghib's division in al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı; Kuran Yolu stresses sensory and nonsensory, material and spiritual characteristics. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); ELMALILI(Râgıb); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 59:24/q07 What is the derivation of al-Muṣawwir?  [ٱلْمُصَوِّرُ · meaning]
turns on: The root consonant and required participial form.
- 59:24/q07/p1 It derives from ṣūra and ṣawwara, meaning image or form. — Al-Naḥḥās and Makkī reject a derivation from ṣāra/yaṣīru because that would require yāʾ or yield al-muṣīr, and it misses the meaning. [2 notes: NAHHAS(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(مكي)+]
- 59:24/q07/p2 It derives from ṣāra/yaṣīru. — Al-Naḥḥās transmits this alternative only to reject it on morphology; Makkī also rejects it. [1 notes: NAHHAS(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(مكي)-]

## 59:24/q08 How do the three creation names relate and why this order?  [ٱلْخَٰلِقُ ٱلْبَارِئُ ٱلْمُصَوِّرُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the three names denote causal phases, biological development, inseparable perspectives, or progressively narrower acts.
- 59:24/q08/p1 They mark determination, bringing into existence, and final shaping, in that sequence. — Al-Rāzī argues will's choice precedes power's effect and essences precede attributes. Ibn Kathīr uses a measured cut, al-Biqāʿī a building plan and prepared materials, and al-Ghazālī an ordered act. Al-Qurṭubī rejects equating creation with image-making; al-Nābigha's verse supports the order. Al-Ṭabarī's terms and subsequent sources follow it. [16 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(النابغة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL(الغزالي); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 59:24/q08/p2 They describe the successive development of creatures, especially the human embryo in the womb. — Muqātil names seed, clot, lump, limbs and chosen traits; al-Thaʿlabī gives staged completion; al-Qurṭubī and al-Nābigha describe womb stages. Study Quran transmits this as an alternative. [9 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(النابغة); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 59:24/q08/p3 The names distinguish inseparable perspectives on the same act of creating. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Ghazālī's account but says it does not fit the names' usage. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الغزالي); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 59:24/q08/p4 The names narrow from originating things, to making bodies, to visible shaping. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Ibn al-ʿArabī's hierarchy and says it blends two senses; his own staged account invokes 82:7–8 and 3:6. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن العربي)]
- 59:24/q08/p5 Al-Bāriʾ and al-Muṣawwir form one semantic unit. — Asad cites al-Bayḍāwī for reading Maker and Shaper together. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Baydawi)]

## 59:24/q09 How is al-Muṣawwir read and parsed?  [ٱلْبَارِئُ ٱلْمُصَوِّرُ · readings]
turns on: The vowels and case ending determine whether the word names God, the fashioned object, or a construct phrase.
- 59:24/q09/p1 The common reading has kasra on wāw and nominative rāʾ: an adjective or predicate of God. — Al-Samīn records the two parses. Al-Zajjāj rejects a transmitted accusative al-bāriʾa al-muṣawwira. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ZAJJAJ-]
- 59:24/q09/p2 With fatḥa on wāw and accusative rāʾ, it is the formed object governed by al-Bāriʾ. — Reports attribute it variously to ʿAlī, Ḥāṭib, al-Ḥasan, Ibn al-Sumayfaʿ/Ibn al-Sumayqiʿ; the object is Adam, descendants, forms generally, or in al-Kashshāf's wording a form-giver. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it good; al-Samīn and al-Burhān say al-Bāriʾ governs it and one must not pause after al-Bāriʾ. Al-Zajjāj rejects a related accusative. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(حاطب بن أبي بلتعة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وأبو الجوزاء وأبو عمران وابن السميفع); QURTUBI-FULL(حاطب بن أبي بلتعة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي وحاطب بن أبي بلتعة والحسن وابن السميقع); SAMIN-DURR(مكي); BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL(علي وحاطب بن أبي بلتعة والحسن وابن السمقيع); IBNATIYYA-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب)+; ZAJJAJ-]
- 59:24/q09/p3 With fatḥa on wāw and genitive rāʾ, an active participle is annexed to its object. — Reported from ʿAlī; Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares al-ḥasan al-wajh, al-Samīn says annexation lightens the phrase and disallows pausing at the word. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي); SAMIN-DURR(أمير المؤمنين); ALUSI-FULL(علي)]
- 59:24/q09/p4 An accusative with kasra on wāw can be parsed as a detached accusative of praise. — Makkī rejects its construction as object of the prior participle; al-Samīn parses a confirmed report by detachment and compares 1:1. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(مكي)]

## 59:24/q10 Does a reading of al-Muṣawwir with an open wāw affect prayer?  [ٱلْمُصَوِّرُ · law]
- 59:24/q10/p1 Al-Khāniyya says that it invalidates prayer. — Al-Ālūsī reports the ruling, allowing it if the word is wrongly construed as God's attribute but questioning it otherwise. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الخانية)]

## 59:24/q11 How is al-Bāriʾ pronounced in one transmitted reading?  [ٱلْبَارِئُ · readings]
turns on: Which transmission from al-Dūrī and al-Kisāʾī is followed.
- 59:24/q11/p1 Most Maghribī authorities report imāla for al-Dūrī from al-Kisāʾī. — Ibn Farḥ's route also transmits imāla, treating it like bāriʾukum. [1 notes: NASHR]
- 59:24/q11/p2 Abū ʿUthmān al-Ḍarīr and most reading sources report fatḥ. — Ibn al-Jazarī contrasts their transmission with the Maghribī imāla reports. [1 notes: NASHR(أبو عثمان الضرير)]

## 59:24/q12 Why are the creation names listed without conjunctions?  [ٱلْخَٰلِقُ ٱلْبَارِئُ ٱلْمُصَوِّرُ · rhetoric]
- 59:24/q12/p1 They are successive descriptors of one referent, functioning as praise without conjunction. — Al-Burhān calls this taʿdīd; al-Itqān says the sequence praises and glorifies; al-Biqāʿī says each name reveals what the prior implies and their totality conveys Allah. [4 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN; BIQAI-FULL]

## 59:24/q13 What do 'the most beautiful names' denote, and why are they beautiful?  [لَهُ ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase is explained through the names' qualities, their scope, or their relation to the single divine Essence.
- 59:24/q13/p1 God's names denote supremely beautiful and perfect attributes and meanings. — Al-Māwardī derives their beauty from attributes; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says these meanings subsist in God's essence. Al-Kāshifī invokes revelation and reason; al-Bursawī calls al-ḥusnā a feminine superlative of aḥsan. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the names are perfectly true of God and analogizes 2:31. Asad translates 'attributes of perfection'. [20 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 59:24/q13/p2 The phrase denotes God's highest exemplars or qualities. — Al-Māwardī reports al-Kalbī's explanation. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي)]
- 59:24/q13/p3 It includes all God's other beautiful names, beyond the names just listed. — Al-Ṭabarī relates the preceding two verses to 20:8; Elmalılı cites 7:180 and 20:8, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī finds totality in the definite plural. Kuran Yolu and Study Quran cross-reference 7:180 and 17:110. Ibn al-Jawzī directs readers to his discussions at 7:180 and 17:110. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 59:24/q13/p4 The names are one God's many descriptions, not multiple named beings. — Al-Bursawī compares one person described by relations as father, grandfather, uncle, and scholar. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 59:24/q13/p5 Examples of the names express distinct divine perfections and degrees of mercy. — Muqātil calls al-Raḥīm gentler than al-Raḥmān. Al-Zajjāj says al-Raḥmān is exclusive, intensive mercy; al-Raḥīm is an intensive active participle; al-Aḥad has no likeness; al-Ṣamad is the master to whom all turn, evident in His workmanship. [5 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ]

## 59:24/q14 What kinds of divine names and attributes does the phrase include?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · theology]
turns on: How the sources classify or count the names.
- 59:24/q14/p1 Names of the Essence and names of attributes, including negative, affirmative, and active attributes. — Elmalılı details transcendence and necessary existence; life, knowledge, hearing, sight, will, power, speech and creation; then their creative effects. He lists examples from the Qurʾān and Prophetic invocation, including exclusive names. [7 notes: ELMALILI]
- 59:24/q14/p2 The listed names sum up power and knowledge as the roots of perfection. — Al-Bayḍāwī says all perfections return to them; al-Biqāʿī notes wisdom in fashioning and the limits of human comprehension. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 59:24/q14/p3 The passage provides seventeen divine names, or eighteen if huwa is counted. — Study Quran calls it the Qurʾān's most extensive list. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 59:24/q15 What divine names are proclaimed in the reported gathering on Judgment Day?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · reports]
- 59:24/q15/p1 God gathers the seven heavens and earth in His grasp and declares a series of His names. — Ibn ʿUmar transmits a Prophetic report of God's words; the excerpt breaks off during the list. [1 notes: THALABI(عبد الله بن عمر)]

## 59:24/q16 May God be named with fitting terms not found in scripture?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · theology]
turns on: Whether divine naming requires transmitted wording or a meaning befitting God's majesty.
- 59:24/q16/p1 Only terms established in the Qurʾān or authentic hadith may be used as divine names. — Al-Bursawī reports the restrictive view, citing ʿĀlim, ʿAlīm, ʿAllām and excluding unattested ʿĀrif, Faqīh, Mutayaqqin. [2 notes: BURSEVI]
- 59:24/q16/p2 Any fitting term conveying divine perfection may be used. — The permissive side invokes accepted Persian, Turkish and Indian descriptions and 17:110's stress on beautiful meanings. Al-Bursawī says new terms with unsound implications are discourteous, but sound and beautiful ones are permissible. [4 notes: BURSEVI; BURSEVI+]

## 59:24/q17 What etiquette governs using and invoking particular names?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · law]
- 59:24/q17/p1 Names of acts should be used with scriptural decorum and some opposing attributes paired. — Elmalılı cautions against calling God simply creator of apes and pigs, and rejects Muʿtazilī denial that He creates evil while preferring 'creator of good and evil.' Al-Bayhaqī pairs al-Ḍārr with al-Nāfiʿ (22:13); Elmalılı similarly pairs al-Qābiḍ with al-Bāsiṭ (2:245) and notes might with retribution. [5 notes: ELMALILI(Beyhakī); ELMALILI(Mu‘tezile)+]
- 59:24/q17/p2 The names can be invoked with attention to their grammatical article and root. — Al-Bursawī reports that knowers attend to both, while the Malāmiyya omit the article as additional to the root; scholars distinguish name, named, and naming. [2 notes: BURSEVI(العلماء)]

## 59:24/q18 How many names does God have, and what does enumerating ninety-nine mean?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · reports]
turns on: Whether ninety-nine is exhaustive, and whether enumeration means counting, knowing, or observing the names.
- 59:24/q18/p1 A Prophetic report promises Paradise to whoever enumerates God's ninety-nine names, one fewer than one hundred. — Muqātil, al-Zajjāj, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and others transmit it, often through Abū Hurayra; Ibn Kathīr and Elmalılı cite the Ṣaḥīḥayn and add that God is One and loves odd numbers. Al-Tirmidhī, Ibn Mājah and al-Bayhaqī transmit lists with variant wording; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says no particular list is firmly established. [16 notes: MUQATIL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ZAJJAJ(أبو هريرة الدوسي,التفسير); NAHHAS(أبو هريرة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(رسول الله); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI(Beyhakī,Ebû Hüreyre); STUDYQURAN(ḥadīth)]
- 59:24/q18/p2 The ninety-nine are not an exhaustive limit; God has other, even unrevealed names. — The Prophet's supplication asks by names revealed, taught or reserved in unseen knowledge. Elmalılı cites Ibn Masʿūd and ʿĀʾisha's prayers and names in Qurʾān and Sunna beyond enumerated lists; al-Rāzī reports a dhikr book with four thousand. Elmalılı even infers al-Witr as a further name from the odd-number hadith. [14 notes: BURSEVI(الرازي,النبي عليه السلام); ELMALILI(Abdullah b. Mes‘ûd,Hazret-i Âişe,İbn Kesîr); STUDYQURAN(many scholars,the Prophet)]
- 59:24/q18/p3 Enumerating can mean learning and counting the knowable names or carefully observing their bounds in one's dealings with God. — Elmalılı links it to knowing God, answered prayer and the significance of odd numbers; the three after-prayer remembrances of 33 each total 99. [3 notes: ELMALILI]
- 59:24/q18/p4 God has four thousand names in a reported dhikr book. — Al-Bursawī quotes al-Rāzī: one thousand in Qurʾān and sound reports and one thousand each in Torah, Gospel and Psalms. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الرازي)]

## 59:24/q19 Where is God's greatest name identified in this passage?  [ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · reports]
turns on: The transmitted locations and identification of the greatest name differ.
- 59:24/q19/p1 It occurs in the final six verses of Sūrat al-Ḥashr. — Reported from the Prophet through Ibn ʿAbbās; al-Durr and Elmalılı also transmit this location. [4 notes: TABRISI(رسول الله); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(النبي صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم عن ابن عباس); ELMALILI(İbn Abbas)]
- 59:24/q19/p2 It is sought in the ending of Sūrat al-Ḥashr, to be recited often. — Abū Hurayra reports asking the Prophet and receiving this repeated direction; al-Kashshāf, al-Qurṭubī and al-Nasafī transmit it. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو هريرة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); NASAFI-FULL(أبو هريرة)]
- 59:24/q19/p3 The name Allah itself is the greatest name. — Al-Zajjāj reports this exegetical identification; Jābir ibn Zayd points to the present verse. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ(التفسير); QURTUBI-FULL(جابر بن زيد)]
- 59:24/q19/p4 The first ten verses of al-Ḥadīd together with al-Ḥashr's ending precede an invocation by the greatest name. — ʿAlī instructs al-Barāʾ in a prayer said to be taught by the Prophet through Gabriel; al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı transmit the report. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(علي عن البراء بن عازب); ELMALILI(Hazret-i Ali)]
- 59:24/q19/p5 Invocation by all known and unknown names, including the supreme name, is taught in a report from ʿĀʾisha. — After ablution and two cycles of prayer, the Prophet affirms her supplication. [1 notes: ELMALILI(Hazret-i Âişe)]

## 59:24/q20 What is said about invoking or embodying the creation names?  [ٱلْخَٰلِقُ ٱلْبَارِئُ ٱلْمُصَوِّرُ · inward]
turns on: The practices differ in the name, number, and promised effect.
- 59:24/q20/p1 A servant of al-Khāliq plans in accord with God's determination. — Al-Bursawī says the divine attribute of measure is disclosed to him. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 59:24/q20/p2 A servant of al-Bāriʾ works in balance and consistency. — Al-Bursawī relates this to the absence of disparity in creation. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 59:24/q20/p3 Specified recitations of al-Khāliq are said to bring illumination or recovery of what is lost. — Al-Bursawī recommends an hour or more at midnight for heart and face; al-Suhrawardī prescribes 5,000 repetitions of an Idrīsī invocation for a lost thing or distant person. [2 notes: BURSEVI(السهروردي)]
- 59:24/q20/p4 Specified invocations of al-Bāriʾ are said to bring protection, wealth, honor, or healing. — Al-Bursawī prescribes 100 repetitions daily for seven days; al-Suhrawardī describes an Idrīsī invocation and an inscription on pitch for illness or madness. [2 notes: BURSEVI(السهروردي)]
- 59:24/q20/p5 A name's abjad count is used as an exact recitation number in retreat. — Al-Bisṭāmī omits the article and calls for a quiet retreat; al-Bursawī compares exact number to a key's teeth, with excess wasteful and shortfall deficient. [2 notes: BURSEVI(عبد الرحمن البسطامي)]

## 59:24/q21 Who or what glorifies God?  [مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the clause is taken distributively of all created things or specifically of rational beings.
- 59:24/q21/p1 Everything in heaven and earth, including the heavens and earth themselves, glorifies Him. — Muqātil explains all as remembering and affirming oneness; al-Ṭabarī adds prostration willingly or unwillingly. Ibn Kathīr and Elmalılı cite 17:44. Ṭabāṭabāʾī explicitly includes heaven and earth. [12 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 59:24/q21/p2 Rational beings in heaven and earth glorify God on recognizing His attributes. — Ibn ʿĀshūr describes each kind's response as intentional or otherwise; even an atheist admiring order unknowingly praises the free agent. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 59:24/q22 How do creatures glorify God?  [يُسَبِّحُ لَهُۥ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether tasbīḥ is actual speech, disclosure through created form and order, or both.
- 59:24/q22/p1 Living beings speak praise, while inanimate things show God's transcendence through their state and form. — Al-Ṭabrisī divides living and nonliving; al-Naḥḥās says creation reveals a maker without equal. Al-Ālūsī mentions wisdom and benefit as its language of state; Ibn ʿĀshūr treats order, provision and subjugation as signs and says even naturalists indirectly praise. [6 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 59:24/q22/p2 Every created thing has actual glorifying speech in its own manner, though people cannot understand all of it. — Al-Ālūsī reports many mystics' view; Ibn Kathīr cites 17:44, and al-Ṭabarī says all prostrate willingly or unwillingly. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TAB-FULL]
- 59:24/q22/p3 Glorification is the verbal declaration that God is free of evil and deficiency. — Al-Musayyib and Muqātil gloss subḥān Allāh; ʿAlī says God approved the formula for Himself. Muqātil notes other Qurʾānic contexts where tasbīḥ conveys wonder (17:1, 36:36) or prayer (30:17). [5 notes: MUQATIL(المسيب,علي بن أبي طالب,مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 59:24/q23 How does the glorification clause work grammatically and rhetorically?  [يُسَبِّحُ لَهُۥ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ · grammar]
turns on: The notes address different features of the same clause.
- 59:24/q23/p1 It is circumstantial to 'to Him belong the most beautiful names'. — Ibn ʿĀshūr makes the following al-ʿAzīz clause coordinate with it. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 59:24/q23/p2 The imperfect verb suggests continually renewed praise. — Al-Biqāʿī calls it a reminder to deficient creatures of abiding divine perfection. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 59:24/q23/p3 The placement of 'to Him' restricts glorification exclusively to God. — Al-Biqāʿī cites the preposition and object restriction. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 59:24/q23/p4 One mā covers both heavens and earth, hinting that earth shares heaven's elevation. — Al-Biqāʿī reads the joining as a sign of perfection; al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts the single mā here with al-Ḥadīd's opening and its subsequent talk of earthly people. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 59:24/q24 Is there a variant wording of the glorification clause?  [مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ · readings]
turns on: Whether the reference to the heavens appears in this transmitted reading.
- 59:24/q24/p1 Ibn Masʿūd reads 'and whatever is in the earth,' omitting heavens. — Al-Kashshāf reports this reading. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 59:24/q25 What does al-ʿAzīz convey?  [ٱلْعَزِيزُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the emphasis falls on unconquerable sovereignty generally or its punitive effect.
- 59:24/q25/p1 God is invincible, overpowering, and unassailable in dominion. — Muqātil stresses dominion; Ibn Kathīr says unassailable; al-Biqāʿī adds no equal, difficult to reach and urgently needed. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls Him the unconquered victor; Elmalılı says He governs all causes. [8 notes: MUQATIL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 59:24/q25/p2 God has power to take severe vengeance on enemies and unbelievers. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Naḥḥās make retribution explicit; Elmalılı notes Qurʾānic pairings of might and vengeance. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; ELMALILI]

## 59:24/q26 What does al-Ḥakīm convey?  [ٱلْحَكِيمُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term is read as wise action, rulership, or the sole source of human wisdom.
- 59:24/q26/p1 God acts, governs, and commands with exact wisdom, free from defect and for creatures' benefit. — Muqātil connects it to command; al-Ṭabarī to wise governance; al-Naḥḥās to flawless acts; Ibn Kathīr to law and decree. Al-Biqāʿī describes firm irreversible judgment and al-Ḥarālī restraining for good, medicine as bodily analogy, and religious striving as another. Elmalılı and Kuran Yolu connect wisdom and proper command. Al-Biqāʿī also connects steadfast governance with the command in 35:6 to treat Satan as an enemy. [15 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 59:24/q26/p2 Al-Ḥakīm can mean al-Ḥākim, the ruler or judge. — Al-Naḥḥās reports this other sense. [1 notes: NAHHAS]
- 59:24/q26/p3 True wisdom and judgment belong to God; human wisdom is granted in proportion to knowledge and purification. — Al-Biqāʿī cites 9:122 for learning rulings, 62:2 for wisdom after purification, and 31:12 for Luqmān. Wisdom eases the burden of obligations; all knowledge is God's. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]

## 59:24/q27 Why does the verse end with the Mighty and the Wise?  [وَهُوَ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْحَكِيمُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the pair's immediate function is to summarize perfection and creation or to close the sūra's wider pattern.
- 59:24/q27/p1 The two names sum up all divine perfection as power and knowledge, fitting His ordered creation. — Al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı pair perfect power with wise knowledge; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the pair suits the order of creation. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the ending assures that the Qurʾān comes from One who cannot be defied and never wastes obedience or reward. [7 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 59:24/q27/p2 The pair recalls the sūra's opening while balancing mercy, judgment, and human choice. — Elmalılı sees it mirroring al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm; Islahi says God could compel worship but wisely tests free human choice. [2 notes: ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 59:24/q28 How does the verse connect with the surrounding verses and sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources draw different local and whole-sūra links.
- 59:24/q28/p1 Its ending returns to the sūra's opening glorification, making a ring. — Al-Nasafī, Elmalılı, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kuran Yolu explicitly note the return to 59:1; Islahi links the final attributes to the opening. [5 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 59:24/q28/p2 The creation names follow the preceding divine names to disclose God's acts and prompt gratitude and glorification. — Ibn ʿĀshūr groups the names in sets matching the sūra's subjects, with local order by analogy or completion; these names address both groups. Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes this verse's creation names from the preceding verses' lordship names. Al-Biqāʿī reads a new proof moving from unseen to manifest. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 59:24/q28/p3 The names complete the mountain example and the call to fear, remembrance, and worship. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī relates the names to the mountain's collapse and prayer through the names; Islahi says they teach fear, love, hope, trust and service after the call to God-consciousness. Kuran Yolu foregrounds mercy and compassion. [6 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 59:24/q28/p4 Universal glorification praises believers and implicitly rebukes unbelievers and hypocrites. — Ibn ʿĀshūr ties it to the Qurʾān's humbling power and the sūra's legislation about wealth and obedience; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says idols possess none of these attributes. Islahi says hypocrites are especially addressed. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 59:24/q28/p5 The final attributes lead into Sūrat al-Mumtaḥina's test. — Elmalılı links the prospect of being tested before the Mighty and Wise to the next sūra. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 59:24/q29 What inward reading is given to the divine names and praise?  [* · inward]
turns on: The inward notes focus on manifestation, purification of perception, or human response.
- 59:24/q29/p1 The names manifest the one Essence's attributes outwardly and inwardly, and creation thereby praises it. — Al-Qāshānī reads the creator, maker and shaper as stages of manifestation; al-Tawīlāt al-Najmiyya sees oneness, governance, transcendence, origination, preservation, honor, subjugation and majesty across the names. [5 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 59:24/q29/p2 Divine transcendence excludes associating partners even in vision and thought. — Al-Bursawī reports al-Baqlī's inward gloss. [1 notes: BURSEVI(البقلي)]
- 59:24/q29/p3 One should conform inner and outward life to the implications of God's attributes. — Islahi makes attributes the basis of religion and sharīʿa; Elmalılı calls for fear, piety, reflection on the verses, and hope in mercy. [6 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI(Abdullah b. Mes‘ûd)]

## 59:24/q30 What rewards or healing are reported for reciting al-Ḥashr's ending?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in recitation length, promised reward, and grading.
- 59:24/q30/p1 Three recitations of refuge and the final three verses morning or evening bring 70,000 angels' prayers and martyrdom if death comes that day or night. — Maʿqil ibn Yasār transmits it. Abū ʿĪsā/al-Tirmidhī calls it gharīb through one route; al-Nashr says ḥasan gharīb, while al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say ḥasan. [9 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عيسى,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(معقل بن يسار); NASHR(النبي); ALUSI-FULL(النبي صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم عن معقل بن يسار); ELMALILI(Hazret-i Peygamber); IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN]
- 59:24/q30/p2 Reciting the final verses day or night and dying then guarantees Paradise or a martyr's mark. — Abū Umāma reports Paradise without grading; al-Ḥasan says one is stamped among martyrs. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو أمامة); DURR-FULL(الحسن)]
- 59:24/q30/p3 Reciting Sūrat al-Ḥashr forgives past and future sins. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Qurṭubī and al-Bayḍāwī transmit Prophetic reports without a stated grading. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله); QURTUBI-FULL(أنس بن مالك); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 59:24/q30/p4 The passage from 59:21 to the end cures headache. — Al-Daylamī reports it from ʿAlī and Ibn Masʿūd; Elmalılı and Ibn ʿĀshūr transmit it. [2 notes: ELMALILI(Resûlullah); IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 59:24/q30/p5 Placing a hand on the head at 59:21 heals every illness except death. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the serial report via Ibn Masʿūd, reciters and Gabriel rare. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 59:24/q30/p6 A verse among the 'Glorifiers' surpasses a thousand verses, possibly 59:24. — Study Quran reports a Prophetic saying and notes an alternative identification as 57:3. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]
- 59:24/q30/p7 The ending has merit in prayer and devotional recitation. — Study Quran calls the passage a Jewel of the Quran and says it is recited in obligatory and voluntary prayers; Elmalılı says reports of its merit are numerous. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI]

## 59:24/q31 What practical response does this verse and its sūra's ending call for?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The response varies with the addressed audience.
- 59:24/q31/p1 Reflect on the names, act on the Qurʾān, worship and glorify God with fear, love, trust and hope. — Islahi says believers and hypocrites alike should align beliefs and deeds with the attributes; Elmalılı cites Ibn Masʿūd that action is the Qurʾān's aim, not mere recitation, and connects reflection to 59:18. Kuran Yolu emphasizes mercy in the response. [10 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI(Abdullah b. Mes‘ûd); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 59:24/q31/p2 The close strengthens confident believers, resolves weaker ones, and warns hypocrites. — Islahi treats the final names as different appeals to those audiences. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
