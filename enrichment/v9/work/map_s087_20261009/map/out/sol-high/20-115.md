# 20:115: 23 questions

## 20:115/q01 What does God's covenanting with Adam mean?  [عَهِدْنَآ · meaning]
turns on: Whether عهد is described as an issued instruction or an entrusted pact; the notes treat these as overlapping usages.
- 20:115/q01/p1 God charged, commanded, or enjoined Adam. — Al-Kashshaf, al-Nasafi, al-Razi, al-Baydawi, and al-Alusi compare عهد إلى with a king issuing orders. Bursevi distinguishes covenants planted in reason, delivered by revelation, and voluntarily undertaken. Islahi stresses an imposed responsibility. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; DAMGHANI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bazıları)]
- 20:115/q01/p2 It is a testament, entrusted charge, admonition, or pact to be guarded. — Abu Hayyan attributes bequest or admonition to the majority. Bursevi emphasizes continual care and guarding; KURANYOLU renders a covenant taken from Adam. Tabatabai says the term can also denote edicts and codes. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); BURSEVI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI]

## 20:115/q02 What was Adam charged to do?  [عَهِدْنَآ ءَادَمَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the opening charge names the tree prohibition, the later Satan warning, an inward obligation, or primordial wilaya.
- 20:115/q02/p1 Avoid the forbidden tree, whether expressed as not eating or not approaching it. — Al-Kalbi, al-Suddi, Muqatil and others say do not eat; Ibn Abbas and other reports say do not approach. Razi reports both. Study Quran cites 2:35 and 7:19; Tabatabai cites 7:19 and argues that this prohibition is indispensable to the single story of 20:115–126. [24 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الكلبي، السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس,المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI(تفسير القمي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:115/q02/p2 He was warned that Satan was his and his wife's enemy and must not expel them from the Garden. — Al-Tabari quotes 20:117; Ibn Ashur locates the charge in the Garden warning. KURANYOLU specifically makes the command a warning not to obey Satan. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bazıları)]
- 20:115/q02/p3 The inward covenant was exclusive devotion and obedience to God. — Ibn Ata forbids obeying another; Ja'far says never forget God in any state. The Ta'wilat Najmiyya forbids attachment to any other. [3 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,جعفر); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 20:115/q02/p4 A report applies the covenant to Muhammad and the Imams among Adam's descendants. — Al-Baqir's reports describe primordial testimony to divinity, prophethood and wilaya: resolute prophets affirmed, while Adam hesitated without denial. Tabatabai limits this to an inner meaning and argues the tree ban must remain the narrative charge. [3 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام); TABATABAI-]

## 20:115/q03 Before what did God charge Adam?  [مِن قَبْلُ · referent]
turns on: The omitted temporal complement of قبل and which surrounding event supplies it.
- 20:115/q03/p1 Before later people received warnings or existed; Adam's breach precedes his descendants' breaches. — Al-Wahidi and al-Baghawi point to 20:113; Abu Hayyan calls the omitted complement most likely the warned people. Nasafi says before Adam's descendants existed; Islahi treats earlier family history as guidance. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:115/q03/p2 Before Adam ate from the tree. — Al-Kalbi and Ibn Abbas relate the temporal phrase directly to the later eating. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قول آخر); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:115/q03/p3 Before Muhammad, the Qur'an, or the present time. — Al-Hasan says before Muhammad and the Qur'an; Baydawi and Bursevi say before this time; Study Quran offers the broad pre-revelation sense. [7 notes: MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:115/q03/p4 Before Moses was sent or before the previously mentioned Mosaic events. — Ibn Ashur notes that قبل is built on damma because its genitive complement is omitted; he compares Moses' commission and the covenant recalled on Moses' return. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:115/q03/p5 Before Aaron, whose failure to preserve Moses' trust closes the prior narrative. — Ibn Ashur offers this as an alternative structure: the Adam narrative begins separately after this sentence. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:115/q04 Why is the name Adam used?  [ءَادَمَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether Adam is an Arabic derivation and, if so, which root applies.
- 20:115/q04/p1 It derives from the earth's surface from which his body was made. [1 notes: BURSEVI(قيل)]
- 20:115/q04/p2 It derives from the brownness of his complexion. — Bursevi notes ādam can mean brown. [1 notes: BURSEVI(قيل)]
- 20:115/q04/p3 It points to the mixture of elements and powers within him. — Bursevi cites the Arabic expression for blending someone into a family. [1 notes: BURSEVI(قيل)]
- 20:115/q04/p4 It points to the spirit, intellect, understanding and vision distinguishing him. — Bursevi compares the word for what seasons food. [1 notes: BURSEVI(قيل)]
- 20:115/q04/p5 Adam is most likely a foreign word. — Bursevi judges this likeliest. [1 notes: BURSEVI+]

## 20:115/q05 What does Adam's 'forgetting' mean, and what did he forget?  [فَنَسِىَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether نسي denotes accountable abandonment, actual loss of memory, a specific missed warning or inference, or an inward lapse.
- 20:115/q05/p1 He left, neglected, or abandoned God's charge. — Muqatil compares 20:88, 32:14, 5:14; Ibn Qutayba and Damghani compare 32:14 and 2:237; Tha'labi and Qurtubi cite God's 'forgetting' in 9:67. Wahidi says the reports converge on abandonment. Al-Hasan and Ibn Atiyya reason that ordinary forgetfulness does not incur punishment; Ibn Ashur cites Adam's response to Satan's oath in 7:20–21 as evidence of deliberate neglect. [41 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ABDURRAZZAQ(مجاهد); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس,السدي,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس ومجاهد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية,مجاهد وأكثر المفسرين); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد والحسن); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن); BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî ve Râzî); STUDYQURAN; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد)-; TABATABAI(أحدهما عليه السلام)-]
- 20:115/q05/p2 He genuinely forgot the charge through weak attention or an inadvertent lapse. — Ibn Qutayba cites the fish and Moses in 18:63,73 for memory loss. Razi attributes it to poor care; Kashshaf to inadequate inward retention; Islahi to impatience. Baghawi, Qushayri and Ibn Zayd say forgetfulness was then accountable though lifted from this community. Alusi instead reproaches avoidable neglect preceding the lapse. [32 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(ابن زيد وجماعة,ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(الراغب,قيل); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Shams-i Tabrīzī,the Prophet); IBNATIYYA-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية)-; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام,أحدهما عليه السلام)-; DURR-FULL(الحسن)-]
- 20:115/q05/p3 He failed to infer that the prohibition covered the whole kind of tree, thinking it named one tree. — Jishumi calls the mistaken individual-tree interpretation sound. Qurtubi's Ibn Abbas and Atiyya say Satan offered the named tree for eternity; Adam refused that one but failed to reason about a similar tree; lost knowledge by faulty reasoning. Alusi lists the omitted inference as one possible object. [7 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي); TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وعطية العوفي); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); JISHUMI+]
- 20:115/q05/p4 He forgot the warning that eating would expel him from the Garden. — Abu Ali and Alusi give this as a possible specific object of the omitted verb. [3 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي); TABRISI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 20:115/q05/p5 He forgot the warning that Satan was his and his wife's enemy. — Tabrisi and Alusi cite the warning in 20:117. [3 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي); TABRISI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 20:115/q05/p6 He forgot God's punishment and thought the prohibition merely recommended restraint. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 20:115/q05/p7 Inwardly, he forgot God by turning to Garden delights, his deeds, or appetite. — Ibn Ata contrasts eye and inner secret; Ja'far says enjoyment hid its giver; al-Wasiti distinguishes forgetting in God's presence from forgetting in absence. Another says he forgot upon eating the fruit. Ta'wilat Najmiyya says desire darkened knowledge and gnosis. [6 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الواسطي,بعضهم,جعفر); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 20:115/q06 How does the clause 'so he forgot' work grammatically?  [فَنَسِىَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the fa marks temporal sequence or explanation and how to supply the unspoken object.
- 20:115/q06/p1 Its object is omitted and understood from the charge; the fa may mark sequence or explain that neglect led to forgetting. — Alusi supplies the charge as object, with other possible objects being expulsion, Satan's enmity, or the class-wide prohibition. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:115/q06/p2 The clause straightforwardly reports Adam's forgetting of the earlier charge. — Elmalili paraphrases the sequence. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 20:115/q07 How is 'he forgot' read?  [فَنَسِىَ · readings]
turns on: The verbal voice and vocalization, and whether the final ya remains vocalized.
- 20:115/q07/p1 The active reading has Adam as the subject of forgetting. — The standard form is presupposed by discussion of variants; al-A'mash also quiesces the final ya for ease, which Ibn Jinni calls pausal style in an accusative position. [5 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(الأعمش); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعمش); QURTUBI-FULL(الأعمش); KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(اليماني)]
- 20:115/q07/p2 The passive causative reading فَنُسِّي means Satan made Adam forget. — Ibn Jawzi names Mu'adh al-Qari, Asim al-Jahdari and Ibn al-Sumayfa; Abu Hayyan and Alusi name al-Yamani and al-A'mash. The reading has damma on nun and doubled sin. Razi says the resulting act might be deliberate or interpretive. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(معاذ القارئ وعاصم الجحدري وابن السميفع); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(اليماني والأعمش); SAMIN-DURR(اليماني); ALUSI-FULL(اليماني والأعمش)]
- 20:115/q07/p3 Al-A'mash reads the final ya with sukun. — Ibn Jinni identifies a pausal-style quiescent ya in an accusative position; Ibn Atiyya says it eases pronunciation. [3 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(الأعمش); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعمش); QURTUBI-FULL(الأعمش)]

## 20:115/q08 What does Adam's lack of ʿazm mean?  [عَزْمًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ʿazm is taken as firmness toward obedience, patience, retention, intention toward sin, careful reasoning, or an inward state.
- 20:115/q08/p1 He lacked firm resolve or steadfastness to fulfill the charge and resist Satan. — Tabari makes the root a heart's firm determination, from which patience and preservation follow, and prefers failure to fulfill the entrusted command. Jishumi describes prior willing that prepares action; Ibn Ashur says it overcomes contrary inclination, comparing 2:227, 3:159 and 46:35. Kashshaf and Baydawi say sufficient resolve would defeat Satan's temptation. Ibn Jawzi says the failure concerns this eating and does not remove Adam from the resolute prophets; Ibn Atiyya says patience and preservation are broader consequences rather than the precise sense. Atiyya is also reported to gloss ʿazm as considered judgment. [46 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; THALABI(الضحاك,عطية); MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(عبد الله بن مسلم,قيل); DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(قتادة، ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن قتيبة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المفسرين); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية,الضحاك,القشيري,النحاس); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,اليماني والأعمش); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bir yorum); STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI; TAB-FULL+]
- 20:115/q08/p2 He lacked patience to refrain from the forbidden act. — Qatada, Muqatil and al-Hasan apply patience specifically to resisting the tree; al-Suddi says patience before sins. Damghani and al-Nahhas cite 46:35. Qushayri in Qurtubi calls patient steadiness the best fit. [20 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة ومقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الحسن,السدي); DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(قتادة، ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة ومقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة,النحاس); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة); TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قول آخر); QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المفسرين)-]
- 20:115/q08/p3 He failed to retain, remember, or guard the entrusted command. — Ibn Abbas, Atiyya and Ibn Zayd express it as preserving the trust; Qurtubi's Ibn Abbas and Atiyya also connect it with failing to infer that similar trees fell under the ban. Abu Umama's report in Durr glosses the deficiency as retention. [22 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,عطية); THALABI(ابن زيد,ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(عطية); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطية العوفي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن زيد,عطية); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطية العوفي); TABRISI(عطية); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس وعطية العوفي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قول آخر); DURR-FULL(أبو أمامة الباهلي,ابن عباس,عطية); BURSEVI; TABATABAI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المفسرين)-]
- 20:115/q08/p4 Adam did not intend to disobey or persist in opposition; his error was inadvertent. — Qushayri says a contrary act arose through forgetfulness. Ibn Zayd and others say no deliberate intention; Study Quran cites Satan's deception in 7:21–22. Ibn Abbas's Ali comparison treats a passing thought as distinct from resolved offense. [11 notes: QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; TABRISI(ابن زيد وجماعة); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قول آخر); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد وجماعة,ابن عباس؛ عمر); TABATABAI(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 20:115/q08/p5 He had no resolve to return to or persist in the sin a second time. — Razi says this reading approaches praise; Ibn Kaysan says neither insistence nor intention to return. [8 notes: SULAMI(الحسين بن الفضل); MAWARDI-FULL; THALABI; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن كيسان); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî)]
- 20:115/q08/p6 He lacked resolve to abandon the sin. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 20:115/q08/p7 He lacked resolve to guard against heedlessness. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 20:115/q08/p8 He lacked resolve to reason with due care about the prohibition. — Razi conditions this on Adam's acting by personal reasoning; Ibn Abbas and Atiyya say he did not infer that a similar tree was covered. ## 20:115/q09 What is the syntax and force of 'We found for him resolve'?  [وَلَمْ نَجِدْ لَهُۥ عَزْمًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether وجد is cognitive with two objects or existential/transitive with one.
- 20:115/q09/p1 نجد means know and governs two objects, له and عزما. — Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi describe له as advanced; Samīn supplies both objects. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:115/q09/p2 نجد means find or encounter, opposite to absence, with عزما as its object and له attached to the verb or circumstantial. — Samīn says له may be an advanced circumstantial phrase, originally an adjective of عزما. Bursevi prefers this sense because the point is resolve's absence. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI+]

## 20:115/q10 Why the oath and image of finding?  [وَلَقَدْ وَلَمْ نَجِدْ · rhetoric]
turns on: The emphatic oath and the figurative search each account for a different element of the wording.
- 20:115/q10/p1 The emphatic lam answers an unexpressed oath. — Alusi supplies 'By God'; Ibn Ashur says the oath and lam foreground the parallel with Israel's broken covenant in 20:86. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:115/q10/p2 Finding depicts a search for Adam's resolve that fails. — Ibn Ashur says absence is pictured as an object sought. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:115/q11 Can Adam be blamed for forgetting or disobedience?  [فَنَسِىَ · theology]
turns on: Whether Adam's lapse was intentional or preventable, whether later exemption applied to him, and whether the Garden ban was legal.
- 20:115/q11/p1 He can be held accountable for neglect, deliberate abandonment, or preventable forgetfulness. — Wahidi says simple forgetfulness is not liable and Satan reminded Adam in 7:20. Jishumi says the account permits minor prophetic sins because an unchosen lapse cannot be an object of obligation. Nasafi allows blame where memory could have been protected. Alusi locates reproach in inadequate attention. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن زيد); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية); NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:115/q11/p2 Literal forgetting was accountable in Adam's time; exemption from error and forgetting was granted to Muhammad's community later. — Qushayri says the reproach is softened by lack of resolve. Baghawi and Ibn Zayd locate the exemption in the later community; al-Wasiti cites the Prophet's report lifting error and forgetfulness. [7 notes: QUSHAYRI; THALABI(ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); BURSEVI(قيل); SULAMI(النبي)]
- 20:115/q11/p3 The prohibition was advisory before religious law, so Adam suffered a natural consequence rather than a legal punishment. — Tabatabai places the Garden in a barzakhic, non-worldly life before legislation. [2 notes: TABATABAI]
- 20:115/q11/p4 A sin can require seeking forgiveness, or be something one is made to commit and for which one is excused. — Al-Nasrabadi makes this inward distinction. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو القاسم النصرآباذي)]

## 20:115/q12 Was Adam a messenger of firm resolve?  [عَزْمًۭا · theology]
turns on: Whether a single lack of resolve in this event defines Adam's prophetic rank or only his response to one charge.
- 20:115/q12/p1 The verse can imply Adam was not among the messengers designated resolute. — Ubayd ibn Umayr makes the exclusion explicit; other sources present it as an inference from the wording and 46:35. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عبيد بن عمير); STUDYQURAN]
- 20:115/q12/p2 Adam's one failure of firmness does not remove him from the resolute prophets; some say all messengers were resolute. — Ibn al-Anbari limits the deficiency to the eating. Qurtubi reports that most scholars count all messengers as resolute and cites a report that every prophet except Yahya erred or considered a sin. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب); QURTUBI-FULL(المعظم,النبي)]
- 20:115/q12/p3 Resolute prophets affirmed primordial wilaya while Adam hesitated without denying it. — Al-Baqir's report applies the epithet to affirmation of covenants to divinity, prophethood and the Imams; Tabatabai classifies it as inner meaning. [3 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام)]

## 20:115/q13 What do reports say about Adam's intellect or forbearance despite this verse?  [ءَادَمَ عَزْمًۭا · reports]
turns on: The reports vary in faculty, comparison group and whether Adam equals or outweighs them.
- 20:115/q13/p1 Adam's intellect, discernment, or forbearance outweighs that of his descendants combined. — Abu Umama's reports vary between intellect, understanding, discernment and forbearance, and between all humans and all descendants. Muhammad ibn Ka'b compares Adam with all scholars; al-Hasan says equal rather than greater. Bursevi dates the lapse early in Adam's life and says it was no deficiency of intelligence. Baydawi likewise suggests Adam's lapse occurred early, before experience of hardship and ease. [14 notes: MUJAHID(أبو أمامة الباهلي); TAB-FULL(أبو أمامة); THALABI(أبو أمامة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن أمامة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو أمامة الباهلي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو أمامة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو أمامة); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو أمامة الباهلي,الحسن,محمد بن كعب); BURSEVI]

## 20:115/q14 Why are human beings called insan or linked to forgetting?  [ءَادَمَ فَنَسِىَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the name reflects the first ancestor's forgetting or only a general human habit.
- 20:115/q14/p1 The human name is linked to Adam receiving a covenant and forgetting it, a trait inherited by his descendants. — Ibn Kathir notes a second Ibn Abbas transmission through Ali ibn Abi Talha. Ta'wilat Najmiyya links nas to nisyan and forgetting covenants. Abu al-Fath al-Busti's verse calls the first human the first forgetful one; another poem exhorts remembrance. Study Quran cites the Prophet on descendants forgetting, and Shams-i Tabrizi warns that one lapse suffices. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(أبو الفتح البستي,التأويلات النجمية,العلامة); STUDYQURAN(Shams-i Tabrīzī,the Prophet)]

## 20:115/q15 What causes or explains ordinary forgetfulness?  [فَنَسِىَ · reports]
turns on: The notes give different reported mechanisms and practical causes; they do not adjudicate their relation.
- 20:115/q15/p1 A cloud-like darkness covers the heart, which remembers again when it lifts. — Umar gives the image. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب)]
- 20:115/q15/p2 Ali lists ten practices or experiences said to cause forgetfulness. — Bursevi reports worry, cupping at the nape, urinating in stagnant water, sour apples, coriander, mouse leftovers, reading grave tablets, seeing a crucified person, walking between tethered camels, and releasing a live louse. [1 notes: BURSEVI(علي)]
- 20:115/q15/p3 Chewing gum is also said to cause it in men, unless needed medically. — Al-Maqasid al-Hasana distinguishes a nonfasting woman: gum cleans teeth and firms gums as a siwak, and her teeth are more delicate. [1 notes: BURSEVI(المقاصد الحسنة)]
- 20:115/q15/p4 Releasing a louse alive causes no harm, though killing it is more proper. — Qadi Khan qualifies the louse item of Ali's list. [1 notes: BURSEVI(قاضي خان)]
- 20:115/q15/p5 Adam's use of the left hand to eat passed forgetfulness to descendants. — Ibn Abbas forbids left-handed eating or drinking on this account. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 20:115/q16 How is Adam's lack of resolve used in the report about ʿAlī?  [عَزْمًۭا · reports]
turns on: Whether an unacted wish illustrates no firm intent, and whether that reading fits Adam's story.
- 20:115/q16/p1 Ibn Abbas compares Ali's passing wish to marry Abu Jahl's daughter with Adam's lack of resolved intent to offend. — Umar also fears Ali might become proud in office; Ibn Abbas says Ali would repent if alerted and fleeting thoughts do not amount to resolve, even for learned jurists. [4 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس؛ عمر); TABATABAI(ابن عباس); TABATABAI-]
- 20:115/q16/p2 The comparison is invalid, and reports of Ali's purity weigh against implying resolved sin. — Tabatabai says the gloss conflicts with 7:20 and the story's context, doubts its attribution to Ibn Abbas, and cites the Two Weighty Things, the Station, and 'Ali with the truth' reports. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:115/q17 How does Adam's story connect with the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Which nearby warning, narrative promise, preceding prophetic story, or broader sura theme supplies the link.
- 20:115/q17/p1 Adam's breach exemplifies the recurring neglect of Qur'anic warnings by later people. — Tabari and Tha'labi compare Adam with those disregarding the warnings of 20:113. Razi stresses that even direct warning about Satan did not avert error. Alusi defends this parallel against Ibn Atiyya; Asad and Razi take Adam as humanity's representative. Biqai extends the motif from Moses through the Reminder and repentance. [24 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري والطبري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,الطبري); TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ASAD-NOTES(Razi); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري)-; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)-]
- 20:115/q17/p2 It warns Muhammad against haste and inadequate care in receiving revelation. — Ibn Atiyya and Alusi link it to 20:114. Tabrisi says to trust God to preserve the Qur'an. Razi contrasts the Prophet's zealous excess with Adam's neglect, and says the need to ask for knowledge and protection from forgetting follows; his final variant reassures the Prophet that his haste came from devotion. Islahi compares Moses' haste and cites human hastiness in 21:37 and 17:11, urging gradual community training. [13 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL; JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية)+]
- 20:115/q17/p3 It fulfills the earlier promise to tell accounts of the past. — Abu Muslim and Razi point to 20:99; Biqai also mentions the Arabic judgment at 13:37. Alusi calls Abu Muslim's distant link weak. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABRISI(أبو مسلم); BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم)-]
- 20:115/q17/p4 It may open as a fresh, independently placed story. — Ibn Atiyya offers the option; Ibn Ashur's Aaron construction also makes the Adam account begin separately. Alusi dismisses the independent-beginning option as narrow. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية)+; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية)-]
- 20:115/q17/p5 It parallels Moses, Aaron, Israel, and the Samaritan in covenants, Satanic prompting, and forgetting. — Ibn Ashur compares Israel's breach in 20:86, the Samaritan's prompted act in 20:96 with Satan's whisper in 20:120, and failure to remember in 20:16; he considers Moses' commission or Aaron's trust as a link. Islahi sees haste as the shared danger. [9 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:115/q17/p6 It illustrates God's mercy, forgiveness and forbearance after a lapse. — Qushayri says descendants should not despair. Biqai says God pardoned Adam without removing his chosen rank; Tabatabai links repentance with the promise of forgiveness in 20:82. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:115/q17/p7 It shows an enduring human tendency to disobey or forget. — Baydawi invokes 46:27; Abu Hayyan says the retelling warns descendants about Satan's snares and follows the request for knowledge. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Razi); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler)]
- 20:115/q17/p8 The placement after Surat Maryam connects Adam's account with correction of beliefs about Jesus. — KURANYOLU says Maryam treats Jesus' birth and mistaken beliefs, preparing its discussion of Adam and inherited sin. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 20:115/q18 How does this telling of Adam relate to other Qur'anic tellings?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources compare repetition, differences of emphasis, and the role of later guidance.
- 20:115/q18/p1 This is another retelling of Adam's temptation and expulsion, shaped by this passage's context. — Razi calls it the sixth telling after al-Baqara, al-A'raf, al-Hijr, al-Isra' and al-Kahf. KURANYOLU mentions Sad and al-A'raf, and the conditional Garden stay, temptation and expulsion; repeated accounts vary in style and context. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 20:115/q18/p2 Unlike earlier tellings, this passage explicitly presents Adam's accepted repentance and chosen prophetic rank. — Attributed to Shawkani. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî)]
- 20:115/q18/p3 The close of the story makes Adam's forgetting a pattern for descendants who forget later guidance. — Tabatabai cites 20:124; the tree ban prefigures divine guidance, though Adam lived before religious law. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:115/q19 What does the Adam episode teach about human nature and divine response?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The sources draw lessons about weakness, repentance, freedom from inherited guilt, and subsequent guidance.
- 20:115/q19/p1 Human beings are prone to Satanic temptation, heedlessness, haste and attachment to worldly pleasure. — Tabatabai depicts a well-provisioned Garden and warns that imagining worldly means confer mastery and lasting pleasure leads to suffering. Islahi says natural emotion and haste need patience; Biqai pairs human deficiency with divine forbearance and prompt repentance. Bursevi's hadith says even Adam's superior intellect could be affected by Satan. Study Quran calls Adam a model for humanity, citing God's teaching him all the names in 2:31. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Razi); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:115/q19/p2 God forgives a repentant person; Adam's lapse does not mean inherited original sin. — KURANYOLU says each person is tested with capacities usable for good or evil and can seek forgiveness directly; it rejects inherited guilt and Jesus' sacrificial cleansing. Biqai says Adam's high rank made his lapse count as sin under the maxim that a righteous person's good deed can be a fault for one brought near. [9 notes: QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî)]
- 20:115/q19/p3 The Garden story explains religious guidance and the consequences of following or turning from it. — Tabatabai says the compact twelve-verse account chiefly explains the institution of religion, reward for guidance, and punishment for turning away; the original Garden prohibition was advisory and prelegal. [5 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:115/q20 What inward lesson is drawn from Adam's lapse?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the focus is inward attention to God, self-regard, appetite, or dependence on grace.
- 20:115/q20/p1 True bliss is closeness to God; delight in the Garden or pride in one's action veils the giver. — Ibn Ata sets inward attention against sight of the Garden and against self-regard; Ja'far contrasts God with food and drink. Al-Wasiti makes self-restraint and witnessing servitude central. Ta'wilat Najmiyya describes desire darkening gnosis, and divine manifestation eclipsing Adam's created qualities. [12 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,النبي,الواسطي,جعفر); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,الحافظ)]
- 20:115/q20/p2 Without God's grace, Satan's snare overcomes even Adam. — Bursevi quotes Hafez's couplet. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الحافظ)]

## 20:115/q22 What broader principle of Qur'anic coherence is recorded alongside this verse?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether successive passages share a real unifying relation despite varied occasions of revelation.
- 20:115/q22/p1 Coherence is a meaningful relation among verses and suras and can disclose subtleties of their ordering. — Burhan defines munasaba as nearness or likeness through rational, sensory, imaginative, causal, contrasting or external links, binding discourse like a building. Ibn al-Arabi treats connected verses as a great science; Razi locates many subtleties in arrangement. Itqan lists works by Ibn al-Zubayr, Biqai and Suyuti; Abu Bakr al-Nisaburi made the field prominent. Al-Malawi rejects dismissing coherence because revelation had separate circumstances. [10 notes: BURHAN(ابن العربي,الفخر الرازي); ITQAN(أبو بكر النيسابوري,ابن العربي,فخر الدين,ولي الدين الملوي)]
- 20:115/q22/p2 Coherence should be sought where subject matter is unified; links across separate occasions and rulings can be contrived. — Izz al-Din ibn Abd al-Salam argues from revelation across more than twenty years. [1 notes: ITQAN(عز الدين بن عبد السلام); ITQAN(ولي الدين الملوي)-]
