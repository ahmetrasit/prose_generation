# 19:33: 27 questions

## 19:33/q01 What does al-salām mean?  [ٱلسَّلَٰمُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether salām is explained as safety itself or as the divine name invoked for it.
- 19:33/q01/p1 Safety or protection. — Al-Farrāʾ reports the transmitted gloss 'safety be upon me'; al-Zajjāj derives salām as the verbal noun of salimtu and also offers the plural of salāma; al-Ṭabrisī likewise gives the verbal-noun derivation. Al-Qushayrī and al-Qāḍī explain it as safety; Abū Isḥāq calls it complete safety, and al-Qāḍī specifies safety from harms and afflictions. [6 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; TABRISI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); RAZI-FULL(القاضي)]
- 19:33/q01/p2 A name of God invoked as the source of peace. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Ṭabrisī list salām among God's names. Al-Burhān says the definite form evokes the prayer's source in God's name al-Salām; invoking a divine name exposes the speaker to what it denotes. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; TABRISI; BURHAN]

## 19:33/q02 Why is al-salām definite here?  [ٱلسَّلَٰمُ · rhetoric]
turns on: The article's antecedent or scope, the force of definiteness versus indefiniteness, and the hostile context.
- 19:33/q02/p1 It recalls the earlier peace granted to John. — Al-Zajjāj says repeating an earlier indefinite salām with the article is stylistically preferable, though both forms are possible; Abū Isḥāq agrees and calls the result complete safety. Al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī's al-Zajjāj report, and al-Qurṭubī repeat the earlier-wording explanation. Some cited by al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn identify John's greeting in 19:15 as antecedent. Ibn al-Anbārī answers the different-speaker objection: Jesus learned God's wording for John, or God paraphrased Jesus' wording when quoting it. Al-Bayḍāwī allows this reading; al-Ālūsī rejects it as narratively and contextually remote. [11 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; TABRISI+; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق)+; ZAJJAJ+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 19:33/q02/p2 It denotes peace comprehensively and insinuates a curse on Mary's accusers. — Al-Zamakhsharī, preferred by al-Rāzī and al-Samīn and reported by Abū Ḥayyān, makes the article generic: assigning peace to Jesus and his followers implies its opposite for opponents. Al-Nasafī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī likewise note the hostile setting. They compare 20:47, where peace on followers of guidance implies punishment for deniers. Al-Ālūsī calls the John antecedent unlikely. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the definite form comprehensive and contrasts John's indefinite wording. [11 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف)+; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)+]
- 19:33/q02/p3 The definite and indefinite forms are dialectal equivalents. — Ibn al-Anbārī, reported by Ibn al-Jawzī, says salāmun and al-salāmu can have the same meaning in different dialects. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري)]
- 19:33/q02/p4 The article points to God's name al-Salām. — Al-Burhān connects the definite invocation to God's name, the source of the requested peace. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 19:33/q02/p5 Its definiteness reflects the contrast between God's gift to John and Jesus' self-greeting. — Al-Bursawī reports that even a little from God is much, citing al-Ḥasan's reading of 'guide us to a straight path' as contentment with little. Al-Sāmarrāʾī instead calls the indefinite form broader and the definite restricted: John receives God's indefinite greeting, while Jesus chooses the definite out of etiquette before God. These explanations differ over which form is broader. [4 notes: BURSEVI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 19:33/q03 Who confers peace on Jesus?  [ٱلسَّلَٰمُ عَلَىَّ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether Jesus' first-person declaration is solely his own or speech authorized in God's place.
- 19:33/q03/p1 Jesus pronounces peace on himself, whereas God pronounces John's peace. — Al-Qushayrī contrasts Jesus' self-greeting with the Prophet's greeting on the Night Journey. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī explicitly contrasts John's gift from God with Jesus' first-person act; al-Ālūsī reports the same comparison. Al-Bursawī's sheikh notes John's third-person and Jesus' first-person wording, while saying both have annihilation and completeness. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; BURSEVI(الشيخ)]
- 19:33/q03/p2 Jesus speaks in God's place, so his self-greeting is also God's greeting. — Al-Ḥasan answers a report's comparison by identifying God's greeting to Jesus with Jesus' own. A view reported by Abū Ḥayyān says God made Jesus speak as His representative; al-Ālūsī reports that this can make Jesus' greeting stronger. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)+]

## 19:33/q04 Which greeting is stronger, Jesus' or John's?  [ٱلسَّلَٰمُ عَلَىَّ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether divine first-person authorization equates Jesus' declaration with God's direct greeting to John.
- 19:33/q04/p1 John's is stronger because God gives it to him. — A report in al-Rāzī has Jesus tell John that John is better on this ground; Abū Ḥayyān prefers greetings from God, and al-Ālūsī reports this side of the comparison. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 19:33/q04/p2 Jesus' is stronger because he speaks for God and receives exclusive peace. — Abū Ḥayyān reports the representative-speech argument; al-Ālūsī reports the same counterview and leaves the comparison for reflection. Al-Ḥasan, reported by al-Rāzī, answers that Jesus' greeting is God's own greeting. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)+]

## 19:33/q05 What protection does the peace grant at birth, death and resurrection?  [ٱلسَّلَٰمُ يَوْمَ وُلِدتُّ وَيَوْمَ أَمُوتُ وَيَوْمَ أُبْعَثُ حَيًّۭا · interpretation]
- 19:33/q05/p1 God's safety covers Jesus at all three stages, with differing explanations of each danger. — Muqātil and Yaḥyā take the days as birth, death and resurrection; al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qāḍī and others describe protection across them. Al-Kalbī maps them to worldly life, grave and resurrection. Al-Ṭabarī specifies Satan, death's terror and Resurrection fear; al-Baghawī says Satan's piercing, shirk and resurrection terrors; al-Bursawī adds death's hardships and Hell. Al-Wāḥidī cites the prophetic report that Satan touches newborns except Jesus, a barrier intervening. Ibn al-Jawzī and Study Quran transmit birth protection; Study Quran adds grave trials for John and Jesus' fearless death after his return and resurrection with humanity, citing 43:61 and 4:157–159. Muqātil's second gloss gives fatherless creation, grave pressure and proof against those calling Jesus Lord. Al-Māwardī reports an alternative with Satan's poke and no grave pressure because Jesus was not buried; he finds its resurrection gloss unsatisfactory and suggests no reckoning after God cleared Jesus of sin. Al-Qushayrī specifies protection from Christian excess and Jewish disparagement at birth and trials outside divine union later. Al-Biqāʿī notes Satan did not harm his birth. [22 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القاضي); QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ,Ṭ)]

## 19:33/q06 Why are these three days singled out?  [يَوْمَ وُلِدتُّ وَيَوْمَ أَمُوتُ وَيَوْمَ أُبْعَثُ حَيًّۭا · interpretation]
- 19:33/q06/p1 They are the three vulnerable turning points at which a person especially needs divine mercy or peace. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says these are moments of special need for mercy; al-Qāḍī calls them the most vulnerable moments and connects Jesus' request to John's peace. Al-Shaʿbī calls them three moments of human frailty; al-Riḍā calls them the most frightening moments for John and Jesus. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls them the comprehensive turning points of Jesus' existence and says the wording applies peace throughout his life. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القاضي); DURR-FULL(الشعبي); TABATABAI(علي موسى الرضا)]

## 19:33/q07 What does Jesus' reference to birth, death and resurrection establish about him?  [يَوْمَ وُلِدتُّ وَيَوْمَ أَمُوتُ وَيَوْمَ أُبْعَثُ حَيًّۭا · theology]
- 19:33/q07/p1 Jesus is a created human servant who was born, will die and will be raised. — Wahb says Jesus' words disclose that he had no father, would die and would rise alive, connecting this to 19:34. Ibn Kathīr says he shares creatures' life, death and raising, while God protects him at the hardest moments. Al-Biqāʿī says birth and resurrection show his humanity. Iṣlāḥī parallels John and rejects weak reports and contrary claims as unable to override these stages. Kuranyolu connects his birth, death and rising with his earlier claim to be God's servant and prophet rather than deity or part of a trinity; his duty of worship also implies prayer among post-Abrahamic prophets despite differing forms. Its passage summary adds blessing everywhere, respect for Mary, and freedom from tyranny and disobedience. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 19:33/q08 What bearing does Jesus' stated death have on predestination?  [وَيَوْمَ أَمُوتُ · theology]
- 19:33/q08/p1 It states his decreed course through death and troubles adherents of predestination. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Mālik's observation that this verse is difficult for adherents of predestination because Jesus declares this fixed course. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(مالك بن أنس)]

## 19:33/q09 Does being raised alive exclude life in the grave?  [أُبْعَثُ حَيًّۭا · theology]
turns on: Whether 'alive' specifies the final embodied state or denies intermediate life altogether.
- 19:33/q09/p1 It implies no life in the grave. — An objection quoted by al-Bursawī reads the resurrection phrase as proof against grave life. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الأسئلة المقحمة)]
- 19:33/q09/p2 It describes everlasting embodied resurrection life; intermediate spirit life still exists. — The answer in al-Bursawī distinguishes enduring resurrection life from grave life; he explains barzakh as life of spirit alone, resurrection as spirit joined to body, and says spirits do not cease living after creation. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الأسئلة المقحمة)]

## 19:33/q10 What does yawm mean in these phrases?  [يَوْمَ وَيَوْمَ وَيَوْمَ · meaning]
- 19:33/q10/p1 A time or occasion. — Al-Dāmghānī cites birth, death and resurrection alongside travel, residence and harvest as occasions described by yawm. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]

## 19:33/q11 How do the three day phrases function grammatically?  [يَوْمَ وُلِدتُّ وَيَوْمَ أَمُوتُ وَيَوْمَ أُبْعَثُ حَيًّۭا · grammar]
- 19:33/q11/p1 They are temporal adverbials governed by the predicate's implied stability in 'upon me'. — Al-Nasafī calls the days adverbials governed by the predicate. Al-Samīn locates their governor in the implied stability of ʿalayya and rejects dependence on al-salām because it would separate a verbal noun from its complement. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 19:33/q12 How is wulidtu read?  [وُلِدتُّ · readings]
- 19:33/q12/p1 Zayd ibn ʿAlī reads a past verb referring to Mary, with feminine tāʾ. — Abū Ḥayyān describes yawma wulidtu as 'the day I was born'; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī specify an active waladtu with feminine tāʾ agreeing with Jesus' mother. Their descriptions of the verb's form vary. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زيد بن علي); SAMIN-DURR(زيد بن علي); ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن علي)]

## 19:33/q13 What does being raised alive denote?  [أُبْعَثُ حَيًّۭا · meaning]
- 19:33/q13/p1 Life after death on Resurrection Day. — Al-Dāmghānī explains ḥayāt as life after death, comparing 46:33 and 75:40 on God's reviving the dead. Muqātil likewise places the raising alive in the Hereafter; Yaḥyā identifies it with Resurrection Day. [3 notes: DAMGHANI; MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM]

## 19:33/q14 What is the grammatical role of ḥayyan?  [حَيًّۭا · grammar]
- 19:33/q14/p1 It is a confirmatory circumstantial accusative reinforcing the already implied state. — Al-Samīn calls ḥayyan emphatic. Al-Burhān explains that such a ḥāl states a stable, already known condition rather than a changing one; his parallels are acting as corrupters, smiling while laughing, and being sent as a messenger (2:60, 27:19, 4:79). Al-Itqān lists 'alive' with doing corruption, a messenger, turning away and not far away as examples. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ITQAN]

## 19:33/q15 When did Jesus speak these words, and what followed?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the reported infant speech is historical speech of these words or a projection of mature teaching; one report also distinguishes womb speech from glorification.
- 19:33/q15/p1 He spoke miraculously as an infant, then fell silent until ordinary speaking age. — Muqātil says the speech ended and Jesus developed like other children; al-Naḥḥās calls speech in Mary's lap the sign. Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Māwardī, Ibn ʿAbbās and exegetes in al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bursawī and Study Quran report silence until boyhood or normal speech age. Study Quran calls the infant ability temporary. [9 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس والمفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(R, Q)]
- 19:33/q15/p2 The passage may present future maturity as past speech, or Jesus may have uttered it after maturity. — Asad reads the past tense in 30–33 as a trope projecting a later truth despite 3:46's cradle-speech wording. He considers revelation and prophethood before full intellectual and experiential maturity inconceivable. He also reports the alternative that the whole passage was said after maturity as a summary of Jesus' ethics and servant identity. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 19:33/q15/p3 Jesus glorified God in the womb when others were present, though Mary heard him speak to her alone. — Mujāhid's report in al-Durr distinguishes Jesus' speech to Mary from his glorification before other people while still in her womb. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 19:33/q16 What did Jesus' speech establish for Mary and the onlookers?  [* · reports]
- 19:33/q16/p1 It vindicated Mary's chastity and showed Jesus' servant and messenger status. — Muqātil says his naming his mother but no father prompted Zechariah to embrace him and testify he was God's servant and messenger. Al-Baghawī says people recognized Mary's innocence; al-Ṭabrisī reports the same purpose, and al-Bursawī says they knew she was chaste and innocent. [4 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; BURSEVI]

## 19:33/q17 Why was the infant-speech sign not given to the Prophet?  [* · interpretation]
- 19:33/q17/p1 His people might have confused it with soothsaying. — Al-Biqāʿī gives this reason and mentions other wonders involving speech by infants, animals and inanimate things. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 19:33/q18 How does Jesus' greeting relate to John's?  [* · links]
- 19:33/q18/p1 It parallels John's peace at the same three stages, though the wording and speaker differ. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī link it to 19:15; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Qāshānī, al-Bursawī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, Iṣlāḥī and Study Quran likewise mark the parallel. Al-Qāshānī grounds it in Jesus' sanctified abstract essence, unhidden by matter even in childhood. Al-Bursawī says John received the same protections. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes John's indefinite divine greeting versus Jesus' definite self-greeting. Al-Bursawī's sheikh contrasts third and first person while crediting both with annihilation and completeness. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI(الشيخ); TABATABAI(علي موسى الرضا); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]

## 19:33/q19 How do the surrounding surahs and narratives connect to this passage?  [* · links]
- 19:33/q19/p1 They pair surprising births and contrasting silences with Jesus' miraculous speech. — Abū Ḥayyān relates Maryam to the preceding surah through astonishing stories: the Cave, Moses and Khiḍr, Dhū al-Qarnayn (18:9, 18:60, 18:83), then John's birth to an old man and barren woman and Jesus' fatherless birth. Study Quran pairs Zachariah's and Mary's temporary silences with infant Jesus' miraculous speech. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 19:33/q20 Where was the surah revealed?  [* · sciences]
- 19:33/q20/p1 It is Meccan, with a reported exception for the prostration verse. — Abū Ḥayyān calls this surah Meccan like its predecessor and reports Muqātil's view that its prostration verse was revealed in Medina after the migration to Abyssinia. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 19:33/q21 What may Jesus' self-description of praiseworthy qualities teach?  [* · interpretation]
- 19:33/q21/p1 One may state praiseworthy qualities to inform others if it is not boasting. — Al-Ṭabrisī derives this permission from Jesus' self-description. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 19:33/q22 What does the count of Jesus' speech suggest?  [* · interpretation]
- 19:33/q22/p1 Its thirty-three words match a proposed lifespan of thirty-three years. — Al-Burhān counts from 'qāla innī ʿabd Allāh' through 'ubʿathu ḥayyan' and reports this inference about Jesus' lifespan. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 19:33/q23 What inward reading is given to Jesus and his peace?  [* · inward]
turns on: Different inward referents: a sanctified essence free of material defects or spiritual meaning threatened by the ego.
- 19:33/q23/p1 Peace frees a sanctified essence from defects of attachment to matter. — Al-Qāshānī says Jesus shares John's peace because his abstract essence remains unhidden by matter even in childhood; he glosses peace as freedom from defects produced by material attachment. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 19:33/q23/p2 Jesus symbolizes a meaning born of divine breath in the heart that can still die under egoic traits and practices. — The Najmī taʾwīl reported by al-Bursawī says even someone spiritually alive should not presume immunity from overpowering ego traits and practices. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 19:33/q24 What further reports about Jesus and John accompany the verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports recount different events rather than competing versions of one event.
- 19:33/q24/p1 Jesus redirected praise from his mother to people who read, follow and practice God's Book. — Al-Qurṭubī reports Qatāda's account of a woman's blessing on Jesus' womb and nursing mother and Jesus' reply. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 19:33/q24/p2 John warned against security from God's plan, and Jesus against despair of His mercy. — Al-Bursawī reports a prophetic saying that God loved the one of them with the better expectation of Him. [1 notes: BURSEVI(رسول الله)]
- 19:33/q24/p3 John and Jesus each attained perfection in different stations. — Al-Bursawī reports John's blood boiling until seventy thousand were killed in retaliation, whereas Jesus was raised to the highest heaven, manifesting beauty. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 19:33/q24/p4 One chronology gives Mary's age and Jesus' prophetic and earthly milestones. — Al-Bursawī's Takmila dates Jesus' birth to the Successor kings, Mary's age then to thirteen, Jesus' prophethood to thirty and raising to thirty-three, and Mary's life for six more years. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التكملة)]
- 19:33/q24/p5 Mary took Jesus to Egypt with Joseph to escape Herod. — Al-Bursawī's Takmila adds that Persian envoys came because of a star and an angel warned them not to inform Herod. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التكملة)]

## 19:33/q25 What does the cited Arabic ellipsis excursus illustrate?  [* · grammar]
- 19:33/q25/p1 Arabic omits complements or nouns recoverable from context. — Sībawayh explains an understood dhāk after laysa ghayr and laysa illā for brevity; an understood al-wāḥid after min is supported by common speech, 4:159 and poetry. Poetic min jimāl and mā fī qawmihā omit jamal and aḥad. He interprets mā al-dahr illā tāratān as one turn bringing death and the other hard-won life, comparing omission after ghayr with deletion of part of a compound name. [4 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 19:33/q26 What does the cited proper-name excursus explain?  [* · grammar]
- 19:33/q26/p1 Names identify particular individuals and take several forms, with rules for titles and kunyas. — Al-Rāzī says specific rulings require individual identification, and humans have more distinct individual needs than other animals. He divides names into personal names, titles and kunyas, citing Ibrāhīm, Isrāʾīl and Abū Lahab; Sībawayh's animal examples show name plus kunya, name alone and kunya alone. Al-Rāzī lists kunyas formed from father, mother, son or daughter; unknown or known lineage, including camel-age names Ibn/Bint Makhāḍ and Labūn; joining unannexed names to titles but keeping annexed names separate; and motives of fact, hope, opposite meaning, reciprocal father-son naming or a notable trait. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(سيبويه)]

## 19:33/q27 How can indefinite salām begin a sentence?  [ٱلسَّلَٰمُ · grammar]
- 19:33/q27/p1 Its frequent use and generic sense make it sufficiently definite in effect to begin a sentence. — Al-Zajjāj says this greeting is frequent and generic nouns can nearly convey the benefit of definiteness; al-Ṭabrisī gives the same explanation. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ; TABRISI]
