# 79:26: 12 questions

## 79:26/q01 Is this clause the answer to the opening oath?  [إِنَّ لَعِبْرَةًۭ · grammar]
- 79:26/q01/p1 The clause affirming a lesson for whoever fears is the explicit answer to the oath. — Jishumi reports this placement. Burhan reports the same view and compares a recoverable omission in the magicians' speech, 'we will never prefer you,' where earlier words supply what is unspoken. [2 notes: JISHUMI; BURHAN]

## 79:26/q02 What does 'that' refer to?  [ذَٰلِكَ · referent]
- 79:26/q02/p1 The just-told account of Moses and Pharaoh, especially Pharaoh's conduct, defeat, and punishment. — Bursevi names Pharaoh's story and God's treatment of him; Alusi includes both what Pharaoh did and what was done to him; Ibn ʿAshur points to Moses' account. Tabari ties it to God's seizure and worldly punishment in 79:25, and explains the preceding nakal as an accusative verbal noun derived in meaning from 'God seized him,' though not from that wording's root. Biqaʿi recalls Israel's rescue despite weakness, Pharaoh's defeat, the parted sea, and drowning. Islahi describes the resistant tyrant's destruction by a slight onslaught of wind. [27 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 79:26/q03 What does ʿibra mean?  [عِبْرَةًۭ · meaning]
- 79:26/q03/p1 An admonitory example or sign from which the mind passes to understanding truth and the outcomes of analogous cases. — Jishumi derives the sense from crossing over to truth and compares crossing a river and interpreting a dream. Biqaʿi describes a deliberate passage between meanings leading to knowledge. Ibn ʿAshur describes inference from a state to its outcome and comparable cases, derives the word from crossing a valley or river, and glosses it here as admonition. Bint al-Shati reports possible associations with weighing coins, crossing a valley, reflective reading, and a well-traveled camel; taking a lesson entails seeing, weighing, considering, and being admonished. Tabrisi says the evidence lets the discerning distinguish truth from falsehood. [8 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI]

## 79:26/q04 Why is ʿibra indefinite?  [عِبْرَةًۭ · rhetoric]
- 79:26/q04/p1 The indefinite form magnifies the many lessons contained in the account. — Ibn ʿAshur specifies lessons about deeds and outcomes, vigilance and fear of God, and the consequences of virtue and its opposite in this life and the next. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 79:26/q05 What does khashya mean here?  [يَخْشَىٰٓ · meaning]
- 79:26/q05/p1 It means fear. — Jishumi equates khashya with khawf. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 79:26/q06 What is feared in 'whoever fears'?  [يَخْشَىٰٓ · referent]
turns on: Whether the omitted object is supplied as God or left open to the broadly feared consequence.
- 79:26/q06/p1 God and His punishment or vengeance. — Muqatil specifies remembering God's punishment and avoiding association of partners; Tabari, Jishumi, and Tabrisi mention God's punishment; Abu Hayyan specifies punishment in this world and the next. Tabatabaʾi reports an explanation that supplies God as the omitted object, but judges his broader explanation more eloquent. [11 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 79:26/q06/p2 The object remains unstated, allowing fear of misery and punishment in general. — Tabatabaʾi grounds this in sound human nature and finds it more eloquent than supplying 'God' as the object. [2 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI(قيل)+]

## 79:26/q07 Who benefits from the lesson?  [لِّمَن يَخْشَىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'whoever fears' means present fear, the capacity to fear, or both; Alusi reports the argument that one already afraid would need no further lesson.
- 79:26/q07/p1 Those who fear God take the warning to heart. — Jishumi says they are singled out because they benefit from proofs and examples. Biqaʿi calls fear of God the foundation of good. Ibn ʿAshur calls the God-fearing people of knowledge who infer hidden consequences, citing 35:28, 29:43, and Moses' call in 79:19. Bursevi says knowing God and His attributes makes one fear and avoid rebellion. Alusi reports an explanation embracing present fear as well as capacity for it. [19 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI]
- 79:26/q07/p2 Anyone disposed or capable of reverent fear can benefit, even if not yet actively fearing. — Baydawi and Elmalili stress disposition; Elmalili adds understanding and says the spiritually insensitive gain nothing from Moses' arrival, Pharaoh's drowning, or news of resurrection. Tabatabaʾi roots the capacity in sound human nature. Alusi records the argument that a person already fearing needs no further lesson, while also reporting an inclusive explanation covering present fear and capacity. Bursevi links capacity to knowledge of God. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]

## 79:26/q08 What lessons are drawn from Pharaoh's fate?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw several compatible lessons from the same fate: warning, divine power and resurrection, prophetic vindication, reflective duty, and inward humility.
- 79:26/q08/p1 His punishment warns people to avoid disbelief, rebellion, and disobedience before they share his fate. — Muqatil adds avoiding shirk; Nahhas says to fear punishment seen when others disobeyed; Ibn ʿAtiyya calls it a threat to unbelievers; Razi contrasts Pharaoh's humiliation with Moses' triumph; Abu Hayyan includes punishment here and hereafter. Bursevi says the wise learn from another's fate before suffering it. Biqaʿi calls Pharaoh a byword for evil and filth whom all sects curse. Ibn Kathir and Bint al-Shati call the fate an admonitory example; Islahi says the wise learn from others rather than await calamity. [27 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 79:26/q08/p2 The signs show God's power to punish tyrants and to resurrect the dead. — Biqaʿi points to the rescue of weak Israelites, Pharaoh's defeat, the sea's splitting and drowning; he says denial of resurrection gathers evil and belief gathers good, and the account leads into the creation argument. Elmalili says news of resurrection benefits the spiritually receptive in the same way as Moses' story. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 79:26/q08/p3 The fate vindicates divine messengers and helps discern truth from falsehood. — Tabrisi explicitly calls the story evidence for discerning truth from falsehood. Razi says God supports His prophets and messengers and that those who behave like Pharaoh incur his punishment; Moses' victory displays the contrast. [3 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL]
- 79:26/q08/p4 The verse obliges reflection on proofs and examples. — Jishumi says the fearful are named because they are the ones who benefit from such reflection. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 79:26/q08/p5 The soul should humble, soften, and relinquish its claims. — Qashani draws this inward lesson from Pharaoh's punishment for the person who fears. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 79:26/q09 Whom does this warning address beyond Pharaoh?  [* · interpretation]
- 79:26/q09/p1 It addresses Muhammad's deniers, especially the Meccan idolaters and Quraysh, by analogy with Pharaoh. — Muqatil says rejecting Muhammad may bring Pharaoh's people's punishment; Ibn ʿAtiyya calls it a threat to unbelievers addressed through Muhammad. Razi says the same culpable conduct brings the same penalty, and Ibn Kathir says Muhammad is warned about opponents. Islahi says Quraysh should learn before disaster. Study Quran likens Pharaoh's disbelief to Makkan idolaters and calls this likely the earliest revealed account of Moses and Pharaoh. [6 notes: MUQATIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 79:26/q10 Why is Moses' account told so briefly here?  [* · rhetoric]
- 79:26/q10/p1 Its selected scenes direct attention to the lesson while recalling the fuller story. — Biqaʿi says the concise retelling avoids contradiction, first presents the story accessibly, and recalls its fuller form through the sura's arrangement. Bint al-Shati says Moses' upbringing, early dealings with Pharaoh, the date, the great sign, and exact punishment are omitted because they are not essential; the arresting question starts at the call in the holy valley, whereas the parallel in Taha starts with Moses seeing fire (20:9-12). [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BINTSHATI]

## 79:26/q11 How does this conclusion connect the story to the surrounding sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources emphasize either the passage's place in the resurrection argument or its consoling and warning function for Muhammad.
- 79:26/q11/p1 It closes the Moses account begun by the question at 79:15 and turns to the deniers of resurrection and the creation argument. — Ibn ʿAshur calls the conclusion the answer to the introductory 'Has Moses' story come to you?' Wahidi says the discourse then addresses resurrection deniers at 79:27. Biqaʿi says the sura establishes resurrection and gathering after preceding suras' fuller exposition, its oaths marking a decisive climax; the story proves total divine power and leads to creation. Elmalili compares reception of resurrection news to reception of Moses' story. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 79:26/q11/p2 The account consoles Muhammad, urges patience, and warns his people after earlier mention of deniers. — Tabrisi says Moses follows mention of those who denied prophets and resurrection and reinforces the proof; Ibn Kathir applies Pharaoh's fate to Muhammad's opponents. Islahi says Moses earnestly called Pharaoh away from arrogance before God destroyed him. [3 notes: TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 79:26/q12 What report connects this passage to a challenge about resurrection?  [* · reports]
- 79:26/q12/p1 Ubayy b. Khalaf challenged resurrection with a decayed bone; the Prophet affirmed return and punishment, Ubayy mocked the return, and the passage was revealed. — Jishumi reports the exchange and Ubayy's description of the return as a losing one. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبي بن خلف)]
