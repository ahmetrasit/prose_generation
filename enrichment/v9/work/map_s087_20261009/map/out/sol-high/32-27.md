# 32:27: 30 questions

## 32:27/q01 Who are those asked whether they see?  [يَرَوْا۟ · referent]
- 32:27/q01/p1 The polytheists, especially the disbelievers among Quraysh. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām identifies the addressees as polytheists; Study Quran specifies disbelievers among Quraysh. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; STUDYQURAN]

## 32:27/q02 Does 'see' here mean physical sight or knowledge?  [يَرَوْا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the observable water and crops make the verb a reference to eyesight or to intellectual recognition.
- 32:27/q02/p1 It means literal, perceptible sight. — Bursevī prefers this because the process is perceptible and the ending again asks about seeing; al-Ālūsī describes the question as asking whether they have witnessed the sign. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI+]
- 32:27/q02/p2 It means knowing. — Bursevī reports this explanation from al-Baḥr al-ʿulūm, while choosing literal sight. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 32:27/q03 What does 'drive' mean?  [نَسُوقُ · meaning]
- 32:27/q03/p1 To urge or move something onward. — Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī give the lexical gloss; al-Ṭabrisī adds sāqahu yasūquhu. Ṭabāṭabāʾī relays the gloss from Majmaʿ al-Bayān. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; TABATABAI]

## 32:27/q04 How is water driven to the barren land?  [نَسُوقُ ٱلْمَآءَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the water is viewed as rain carried in clouds, flood runoff, or water conveyed in channels and springs.
- 32:27/q04/p1 By driving rain-bearing clouds, with rain or snow falling on the land. — Yaḥyā says the rain travels in driven clouds. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says even river water originates in clouds. Bursevī distinguishes God's driving clouds from people's directing rivers. Al-Ālūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī also describe driven clouds; al-Māwardī and al-Ṭabrisī include rain and snow. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 32:27/q04/p2 By floods or torrents that bring water from elsewhere. — Ibn ʿAbbās identifies a torrent and ineffective local rain; al-Ṭabrisī mentions dry high settlements; Ibn al-Jawzī includes rain and flood. Al-Wāḥidī allows rain or torrent on the general reading. Al-Ālūsī reports floods as a separate explanation. [10 notes: MUJAHID(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 32:27/q04/p3 Through rivers and springs, including running water from mountains. — Al-Māwardī and al-Ṭabrisī give this alongside rain and snow. Al-Nasafī includes rivers with rain; Ibn Kathīr mentions rivers and descending mountain water; al-Biqāʿī allows water from the earth; al-Ālūsī reports rivers and springs. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 32:27/q05 What does al-jurz mean of the land?  [ٱلْأَرْضِ ٱلْجُرُزِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether barrenness means a present lack of plants after drought or grazing, or an inherent inability to grow despite the verse's crops.
- 32:27/q05/p1 Dry, barren land with no vegetation. — Muqātil calls it smooth; Abū ʿUbayda and Ibn Qutayba stress hard, dry ground, and the latter gives plural ajrāz and sunūn ajrāz for drought years. Al-Ṭabarī calls it hard and dry; Qatāda says dusty; ʿIkrima and al-Ḍaḥḥāk say thirsty or dead; Ibn ʿAbbās says rain is ineffective except floodwater. Several cite 18:8. Ibn ʿAṭiyya objects that simply saying it produces nothing is imprecise. [35 notes: MUJAHID(ابن عباس); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(مجاهد); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,الضحاك,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الفراء,عكرمة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,يحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(الأصمعي,الضحاك,الفراء,عكرمة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة، والضحاك، وقتادة، والسدي، وابن زيد); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد,مجمع البيان); TABATABAI; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; BAYDAWI-FULL-]
- 32:27/q05/p2 Land whose former vegetation was cut, consumed, or dried away, but can grow again. — Ibn Shajara says its crops and trees were consumed; Ibn ʿAṭiyya explains thirst consuming growth and cites poetry. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, Bursevī and al-Kashshāf mention drought or grazing, rejecting salt flats because crops emerge. Al-Bayḍāwī explicitly prefers this over land that never grows. Al-Biqāʿī adds drying and crushing leave a smooth surface. Al-Rāghib describes vegetation cut at the root or eaten bare. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري); NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب,الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL+]
- 32:27/q05/p3 Naturally infertile salt-flat land. — Al-Ālūsī attributes this sense to al-Rāghib but rejects it here because the verse says crops grow; al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī likewise exclude salt flats. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري)-; NASAFI-FULL-]

## 32:27/q06 What does the root of al-jurz evoke?  [ٱلْجُرُزِ · meaning]
- 32:27/q06/p1 Cutting or consuming everything until nothing remains. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, al-Naḥḥās, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Māwardī, al-Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Ālūsī compare a voracious camel or person and a cutting sword. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Māwardī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Ṭabrisī cite rajaz lines; al-Māwardī's eater consumes dates with pits. Al-Thaʿlabī adds locusts that consume crops. Al-Zajjāj distinguishes jazūz for a voracious woman and jarāz for a sword. Al-Rāghib adds a proverb of total consumption and severe coughing. [16 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(الراجز); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب,مجمع البيان)]

## 32:27/q07 Which land does al-arḍ al-jurz refer to?  [ٱلْأَرْضِ ٱلْجُرُزِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the definite expression denotes a named locality or describes the general class of barren land; transmitted place names vary.
- 32:27/q07/p1 Any barren land; the expression is general. — Al-Wāḥidī prefers al-Suddī's general reading because the resurrection argument names no place. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the sign applies wherever land has this quality. Abū Ḥayyān invokes 18:8. Ibn Kathīr includes Egypt without limiting it to Egypt and links 36:33–35. Al-Ālūsī favors the apparent general sense; Muḥammad ibn Yazīd allows it as an alternative. [10 notes: NAHHAS(محمد بن يزيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 32:27/q07/p2 A land in Yemen, sometimes specified as Abyan or Aden. — Ibn ʿAbbās is repeatedly cited for Yemen; al-Naḥḥās calls its chain sound and al-Qurṭubī favors allowing it. Ibn ʿAbbās describes no seas or rivers, with unexpected torrents enabling any grain. Mujāhid and reports from Ibn ʿAbbās place it at Abyan; Abū ʿUbayda says the Aden region. These local names vary across reports. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); NAHHAS(ابن عباس,مجاهد); THALABI(ابن عباس,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس، ومجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 32:27/q07/p3 Villages of Nabiya or dry high settlements between Yemen and Syria. — Al-Ḥasan names villages of Nabiya; al-Ṭabrisī cites Ibn ʿAbbās for floodwater reaching dry high settlements; Ibn Kathīr and al-Ālūsī also report al-Ḥasan. The village name is given only in al-Māwardī. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 32:27/q07/p4 The Nile land of Egypt. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this from Mujāhid. Ibn Kathīr says Egypt is included, describing its soft, heavy, saline, sandy soil, the Nile's rain-fed overflow and red silt that permits yearly crops; he does not limit the verse to Egypt. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 32:27/q07/p5 A land in Babel. — Al-Baghawī attributes this identification to Mujāhid. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 32:27/q08 Does the definite article in al-jurz allow a specific place-name?  [ٱلْجُرُزِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether al-jurz is functioning as an adjective, an inherited descriptive name, or an ordinary proper name.
- 32:27/q08/p1 It may mark a descriptive adjective or a place-name transferred from a common description. — Al-Naḥḥās says jurz is an adjective that can take the article. Al-Mubarrad allows transferred descriptive names such as al-Madīna and al-Baṣra, whose article retains the earlier descriptive sense. Al-Qurṭubī favors the Yemen identification because Ibn ʿAbbās's report is sound and jurz can be a definite adjective. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد); NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 32:27/q08/p2 The article suggests a particular land but is unexpected in a strict proper name. — Muḥammad ibn Yazīd observes that the article suggests a specific land while a general reading remains possible; al-Mubarrad questions a proper name with the article, though he allows descriptive names to retain it. Al-Qurṭubī reports his doubt. [3 notes: NAHHAS(محمد بن يزيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد); QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن يزيد)]

## 32:27/q09 What is the form of jurz and how is it vocalized?  [ٱلْجُرُزِ · readings]
turns on: Vowel or silence on the rāʾ, and whether the recorded forms count as readings or dialectal variants.
- 32:27/q09/p1 The usual reading has a vowel on the rāʾ, commonly ḍamma; several dialectal vocalizations are allowed. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the ḍamma reading the majority's. Al-Farrāʾ and al-Ṭabarī enumerate four forms, with al-Ṭabarī assigning fatḥ to Banū Tamīm. Al-Zajjāj reports several undiacritized forms and treats two as dialects; al-Naḥḥās lists jarz, jaraz and jurz. Comparisons include bukhl, raghb and rahb. Al-Jishumī counts three forms; al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī count four. [11 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء وغيره)]
- 32:27/q09/p2 A variant leaves the rāʾ silent as a lightened form. — Al-Zajjāj calls it a lighter form; Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes a report to him. Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī record the variant; al-Samīn points to discussion at the beginning of al-Kahf. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قرئ)]

## 32:27/q10 How does al-jurz work grammatically?  [ٱلْجُرُزِ · grammar]
- 32:27/q10/p1 It may be a verbal noun used as an adjective for land with plant-consuming force. — Al-Zajjāj proposes this analysis and describes the land as having plant-consuming activity. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]

## 32:27/q11 How is 'so We bring forth' connected to what precedes?  [فَنُخْرِجُ · grammar]
turns on: The syntactic connection of the raised verb and the causal implication that an imagined subjunctive would create.
- 32:27/q11/p1 It is coordinated with 'We drive'. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī list coordination as one possible syntax. Al-Zamakhsharī says the raised verb establishes actual greening; Ibn al-Khabbāz says the water, rather than seeing the water, causes it. [4 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN(ابن الخباز,الزمخشري)]
- 32:27/q11/p2 It begins a new clause disconnected from the preceding syntax. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī both list this analysis. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 32:27/q11/p3 A hypothetical subjunctive would tie bringing forth to the seeing, spoiling the intended meaning. — Al-Zamakhsharī says it would reverse the point and imply failure to give thanks. Ibn al-Khabbāz says seeing water does not cause greening; water does. [0 notes: BURHAN(ابن الخباز,الزمخشري)-]

## 32:27/q12 What does 'by it' refer to, and how does water bring crops forth?  [بِهِۦ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'by water' describes direct productive agency or the occasion on which God creates growth.
- 32:27/q12/p1 It refers to the water, with the verse presenting water as the means of growth. — Al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī identify the antecedent. Al-Biqāʿī says water mixes with earth and draws real plants from its depths, unlike a magician's illusion. Al-Ālūsī reports that early authorities take the apparent sense. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(السلف الصالح); BIQAI-FULL]
- 32:27/q12/p2 God creates the crop when water arrives, without water directly producing it. — Al-Ālūsī attributes this causal reading to the Ashʿarites. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الأشاعرة)]

## 32:27/q13 What does zarʿ include here?  [زَرْعًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the named principal crop stands for all plant growth or retains its familiar narrower sense.
- 32:27/q13/p1 Vegetation generally, including crops and other useful plants. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān say crops are singled out as honored and principal but stand for all vegetation, since livestock eat other growth. Al-Ālūsī includes trees, and reports al-Baḥr allowing the broader sense; al-Biqāʿī also treats the passage as vegetation generally. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البحر)]
- 32:27/q13/p2 Cultivated crops in the familiar sense, named for their importance and honor. — Al-Ālūsī reports al-Baḥr's alternative: cultivated crops are the main purpose of growth and thus singled out. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(البحر)]

## 32:27/q14 What is the grammatical form of zarʿ?  [زَرْعًۭا · grammar]
- 32:27/q14/p1 It is originally a verbal noun used for the thing planted. — Bursevī and al-Ālūsī state this; Ṭabāṭabāʾī repeats the explanation from Majmaʿ al-Bayān. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 32:27/q15 What do livestock and people eat from the growth?  [تَأْكُلُ مِنْهُ أَنْعَٰمُهُمْ وَأَنفُسُهُمْ · interpretation]
- 32:27/q15/p1 Livestock eat fodder, leaves, straw or grass, and sometimes grain; people eat grains, other staples, greens, roots and fruit. — Al-Baghawī and al-Nasafī contrast animal grass or stalks with human grain. Al-Qurṭubī adds herbage, greens and fruit; al-Bayḍāwī mentions figs and foliage. Al-Biqāʿī includes animal grain and people's green roots; Bursevī and al-Ālūsī add straw, legumes and fruit. Muqātil, al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn al-Jawzī state the shared benefit broadly. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 32:27/q16 Why are livestock named before people?  [أَنْعَٰمُهُمْ وَأَنفُسُهُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether sequence follows timing, dependence, rank, or the immediate crop context and its contrast with ʿAbasa.
- 32:27/q16/p1 Livestock eat the growth sooner, before it fruits or the grain is harvested. — Abū Ḥayyān says animals graze as soon as plants appear; al-Ālūsī says they eat before fruit and grain. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 32:27/q16/p2 Livestock depend more exclusively on this growth and sustain human livelihood. — Abū Ḥayyān reports animals eat fodder while people have other food. Al-Ālūsī says their food is limited to the growth; al-Biqāʿī says livestock sustain people's livelihoods and bodies. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 32:27/q16/p3 The order rises from lower creatures to the more noble humans. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this; al-Ālūsī also gives the progression to the children of Adam. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 32:27/q16/p4 The preceding mention of crops suits mentioning livestock feed first. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī gives this contextual reason in three notes. Al-Biqāʿī contrasts ʿAbasa, which follows food through to the human meal and names foods unsuitable for livestock; al-Sāmarrāʾī likewise says ʿAbasa discusses human food before fodder. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 32:27/q17 How is 'they eat' read?  [تَأْكُلُ · readings]
- 32:27/q17/p1 Abū Bakr ibn ʿAyyāsh and Abū Ḥaywa read yaʾkulu with masculine yāʾ instead of the verse's feminine tāʾ. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān report both readers; al-Ālūsī says Abū Bakr's reading is in one transmission. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو بكر بن عياش وأبو حيوة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حيوة، أبو بكر); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيوة وأبو بكر)]

## 32:27/q18 What is the syntax of 'their livestock eat from it'?  [تَأْكُلُ مِنْهُ أَنْعَٰمُهُمْ · grammar]
- 32:27/q18/p1 The clause is an accusative adjective qualifying 'crop'. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī give this analysis. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 32:27/q19 How does 'and themselves' work after the eating clause?  [وَأَنفُسُهُمْ · grammar]
- 32:27/q19/p1 An understood verb means that the people themselves eat from the crop. — Al-Naḥḥās supplies the elided verb. He also notes that nafs can denote an individual distinct from others or a thing's entire self or reality, citing a verse about God's knowledge for the latter. [2 notes: NAHHAS]

## 32:27/q20 What does alā contribute to the closing question?  [أَفَلَا · grammar]
- 32:27/q20/p1 Alā is an alerting particle. — Al-Naḥḥās gives this grammatical function. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 32:27/q21 How is the closing verb read?  [يُبْصِرُونَ · readings]
turns on: Third-person yāʾ versus second-person tāʾ, with the transmitters differing on which reading is common and which is Ibn Masʿūd's.
- 32:27/q21/p1 The common reading is third-person yubṣirūn. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn call it the majority or common reading; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the majority as reading second-person, so the sources disagree over which is majority. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود وجمهور الناس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(ابن مسعود)]
- 32:27/q21/p2 A second-person tabṣirūn reads the question as direct address or a shift of person. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī attribute this to Ibn Masʿūd; al-Samīn calls it iltifāt. Ibn ʿAṭiyya instead attributes the second-person reading to the majority and the third-person to Ibn Masʿūd. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود وجمهور الناس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن مسعود); SAMIN-DURR(ابن مسعود); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 32:27/q22 Why does this verse end by asking whether they see?  [أَفَلَا يُبْصِرُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the change from hearing to seeing follows the kind of evidence or intensifies the warning.
- 32:27/q22/p1 The revival and provision are visible, while the previous verse's destroyed nations are known by hearing. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Burhān, al-Ālūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī compare 32:26. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says both the opening and closing sight language fits the visible water and crops. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the closing question also warns and reproaches. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 32:27/q22/p2 The ending heightens the admonition and removes excuses. — Al-Ālūsī reports this as another explanation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 32:27/q23 What does the visible revival teach?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw several compatible lessons from the same visible sign: resurrection, divine power and provision, and reproach for ignoring it.
- 32:27/q23/p1 God can resurrect dead people as He revives dead land. — Yaḥyā, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, Bursevī, Asad and Study Quran give this analogy. Ibn Kathīr cites 36:33–35; Study Quran mentions 56:68–70. Al-Biqāʿī says denial despite visible evidence shows lack of sight and insight. Abū Ḥayyān says this follows the warning from destroyed nations. [15 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 32:27/q23/p2 The sign displays God's oneness, complete power, wise care, bounty and provision, calling for worship and gratitude. — Muqātil urges affirmation of oneness; al-Ṭabrisī and al-Bayḍāwī stress favor and power. Bursevī calls for worship of God alone and rejection of angelic, human or lifeless partners. Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses care for living beings. Study Quran says deniers fail to thank God; al-Ālūsī says to infer perfect power and bounty. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 32:27/q23/p3 The question admonishes and rebukes heedless deniers. — Muqātil calls it an admonition to be wary. Al-Biqāʿī says it rebukes resurrection denial; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it warning and reproach. Al-Rabīʿ ibn Sīra says the water image is a parable that brings meanings closer to mind; al-Ālūsī describes blindness to a witnessed sign. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الربيع بن سيرة); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 32:27/q24 Can the barren land also represent a heart?  [ٱلْأَرْضِ ٱلْجُرُزِ · inward]
- 32:27/q24/p1 Yes: admonitions revive hard hearts that turn away from the Real. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ reads God's bringing blessings of admonition to hardened hearts so they take heed; al-Sulamī and al-Māwardī transmit it. Study Quran also sees dead earth as an image of a dead heart spiritually revived, citing 25:48–49, 30:19, 36:33 and 50:9–11. [3 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عطاء); STUDYQURAN]

## 32:27/q25 Which other verses illuminate the water and barren-land image?  [* · links]
turns on: The cited verses supply lexical parallels, analogous revival images, or contextual contrasts rather than competing identifications.
- 32:27/q25/p1 Qur'an 7:57 likewise describes water driven to dead land. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām explicitly links this wording to 7:57. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM]
- 32:27/q25/p2 Qur'an 18:8 uses jurz of a bare, plantless earth. — Al-Ṭabarī cites Ibn Zayd's explanation of ṣaʿīdan juruz. Al-Wāḥidī says he treated the word and derivation there; Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn Kathīr invoke the same passage, and al-Samīn directs discussion of the variant there. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 32:27/q25/p3 Qur'an 80:24–32 likewise describes rain producing food for humans and livestock. — Ibn Kathīr cites the sequence of rain, earth splitting, crops and food. Al-Biqāʿī and al-Sāmarrāʾī compare the different order of humans and livestock in ʿAbasa; their detailed order explanations appear under that question. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 32:27/q25/p4 Qur'an 36:33–35 likewise makes revived earth and its food a sign. — Ibn Kathīr cites this image in support of the general resurrection reading. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 32:27/q25/p5 Qur'an 30:48 uses a present tense cloud-stirring verb amid past verbs to make the event vivid. — Al-Burhān says wind stirring clouds is more astonishing because its cause is hidden, while people readily recognize water's effect on crops. [2 notes: BURHAN]

## 32:27/q26 How does this verse fit the surrounding passage and sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes identify different levels of context: adjacent signs, the sūra's paired denials, and the closing warning.
- 32:27/q26/p1 It shifts from destroyed past peoples to a visible creation sign supporting resurrection. — Abū Ḥayyān contrasts the earlier destroyed nations with the new evidence of God's power. Study Quran says readers reflect on past civilizations and creation signs. Kuranyolu says verses 26–27 invite reflection through archaeology and empirical study, including geophysics and agriculture. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 32:27/q26/p2 The sūra answers denials of the Qur'an and resurrection with paired rejecting questions and distinct endings. — Al-Biqāʿī describes four interrogative forms built from two denials and their answers. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 32:27/q26/p3 The sūra frames creation's directing power and warns that persistent rejection ends painfully; the Prophet conveys the message. — Kuranyolu describes the opening and close in these terms. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 32:27/q26/p4 The later challenge about promised dominance says accepting it when it arrives will be useless, so the Prophet leaves mockers to wait. — Iṣlāḥī identifies Quraysh as mocking the promised dominance and asking its time. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 32:27/q27 What Nile incident is reported alongside the Egypt interpretation?  [ٱلْأَرْضِ ٱلْجُرُزِ · reports]
- 32:27/q27/p1 After Egypt's conquest, ʿAmr stopped drowning a dressed virgin annually; ʿUmar's letter invoking God was followed by a sixteen-cubit overnight Nile rise. — Ibn Kathīr transmits the account through Ibn Lahīʿa and Qays ibn Ḥajjāj; it says the custom ended afterward. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(قيس بن حجاج)]

## 32:27/q28 What is reported about the sūra's place of revelation?  [* · sciences]
- 32:27/q28/p1 Sūrat al-Sajdah is Makkan. — Study Quran states this in its introduction. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 32:27/q29 What are the annual waters and yields reported for a particular barren land?  [ٱلْأَرْضِ ٱلْجُرُزِ · reports]
- 32:27/q29/p1 A riverless land far from the sea receives a yearly Wadān and permits three plantings a year. — Al-Qurṭubī records the report without identifying its transmitter in the note. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 32:27/q30 How is the preceding verse parsed?  [* · grammar]
- 32:27/q30/p1 In 32:26, yahdi has an implicit subject supplied by “how many We destroyed”; kam is the object of ahlaknā, and yamshūn is circumstantial. — Al-Ṭabrisī gives this parsing of the verse immediately before 32:27. [1 notes: TABRISI]
