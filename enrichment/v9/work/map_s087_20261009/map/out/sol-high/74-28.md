# 74:28: 11 questions

## 74:28/q01 What does تُبْقِى mean?  [تُبْقِى · meaning]
- 74:28/q01/p1 To spare, preserve, or leave something remaining; it can also suggest prolonging life or showing compassion. — Jishumi defines ibqāʾ as leaving some of what was taken, cites abqāhu Allāh for God prolonging a life, and explains al-bāqī as what continues to exist. Bursevi's Persian glossary gives preservation and compassion. [2 notes: JISHUMI; BURSEVI]

## 74:28/q02 How is the form تذر explained?  [تَذَرُ · grammar]
- 74:28/q02/p1 Its initial wāw is dropped by analogy with يذر, a verb without a past form and equivalent in meaning to يدع. — Ibn Kaysān supplies the analogy, as reported by Nahhas. [1 notes: NAHHAS(ابن كيسان)]

## 74:28/q03 How is the final rāʾ of تذر pronounced?  [تَذَرُ · readings]
- 74:28/q03/p1 When made sākin after a fatḥa, its rāʾ is emphatic for all readers. — Ibn al-Jazari uses ولا تذر as the example. He contrasts a final sākin rāʾ after kasra, which stays light even before an emphatic letter because the letter is separated from the rāʾ. [2 notes: NASHR]

## 74:28/q04 How is the clause attached grammatically to the description of Saqar?  [لَا تُبْقِى وَلَا تَذَرُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause is predication or explanation, an independent statement, or a ḥāl governed by the preceding rhetorical question; Al-Alusi disputes the last analysis.
- 74:28/q04/p1 It describes or explains Saqar, including analysis as another predicate of Saqar. — Baydawi allows an explanation of Saqar's nature or a circumstantial description. Al-Alusi calls it explanatory; Ibn Ashur says it elaborates the terror of the preceding question and serves as another predicate. Ibn Ashur says the coordinated ولا تذر has circumstantial force. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 74:28/q04/p2 It is an independent sentence. — Samin reports this as a second syntactic analysis; Al-Alusi also allows an independent explanatory clause and says no omitted subject need be posited. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 74:28/q04/p3 It is a circumstantial clause governed by the sense of magnification in the preceding question about Saqar. — Abu al-Baqa gives this analysis in Samin. Baydawi also permits a circumstantial description. Al-Alusi reports the analysis but finds it unconvincing. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 74:28/q05 What does Saqar leave or spare?  [لَا تُبْقِى وَلَا تَذَرُ · interpretation]
turns on: What the unstated object and scope of the two negated verbs are: everything thrown in, every person, every body part, every form of torment, or relief from torment.
- 74:28/q05/p1 Nothing cast into Saqar escapes its consuming destruction. — Thalabi says it destroys whatever enters. Abu Muslim calls this the ultimate description of burning. Kashshaf and Al-Alusi stress inevitable destruction of all that is thrown in; Baydawi says it neither leaves anything nor spares it before destroying it. Samin supplies the omitted object, what is cast in. Nasafi allows that nothing remains intact. Biqai extends this to every condition, distinguishing destruction from alteration of those punished. Kuranyolu connects the verse to burning and scorched skin; Bursevi cites 4:56 for renewed burning. [15 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI(أبو علي,أبو مسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 74:28/q05/p2 No part of its victims' bodies remains unburned, especially flesh, bone, blood, skin, sinew, veins, or marrow. — Al-Suddi specifies flesh and bone in Thalabi, Wahidi, Jishumi, Baghawi, Qurtubi, and Al-Alusi. Qurtubi and the Ibn Abbas report through Ata add blood; the latter says renewed burning becomes more intense. Ibn Burayda and Abu Sinan name flesh, veins, nerves, skin, sinew, and marrow. Nasafi and Al-Alusi divide flesh and bone between the two verbs; Al-Alusi calls that explanation weaker. Ibn Ashur says the omitted object can include every body part. Study Quran reports the bones, skin, and blood interpretation. [18 notes: THALABI(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي,الكلبي,عطاء عن ابن عباس); JISHUMI(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(آخرون,عطاء عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن بريدة وأبو سنان); DURR-FULL(ابن بريد); ALUSI-FULL(السدي); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 74:28/q05/p3 No person deserving Saqar's punishment escapes being seized or tormented. — Ibn Isa says none is spared seizure or punishment; Jishumi reports that everyone in Hell is reached. Razi distinguishes the first verb as covering every deserving person and the second as covering every body part. Samin and Ibn Ashur give a person cast in, or no one at all, as the omitted object. Abu Hayyan says no one thrown in remains untouched. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); JISHUMI(أبو علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(آخرون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:28/q05/p4 Saqar withholds no type, degree, or full force of torment. — Ibn Atiyya says it leaves no kind or limit of torment untouched. Al-Jubba'i and Abu Ali say it burns all and spares none of the full range of torments; Abu Ali adds that the victims are exhausted. Abu Hayyan says it delivers every degree. Samin suggests any degree of torment as the omitted object. Razi distinguishes exhaustive bodily burning from using all the fire's power and intensity. [6 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي); RAZI-FULL(آخرون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 74:28/q05/p5 It grants no mercy, health, relief, or respite, and does not release its victim. — Mawardi's further possibility denies health and respite. Bursevi's alternative denies mercy, supported by his Persian gloss of ibqāʾ as preservation or compassion. Elmalili says the victim is never released; Islahi says Hell neither eases agony nor spares anyone and remains vigilant. Muqatil says it overtakes them even when they swear. [6 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:28/q06 What happens to the victims after Saqar consumes them?  [لَا تُبْقِى وَلَا تَذَرُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the notes describe the cycle of destruction and recreation or its resulting state of neither life nor death; several sources combine both.
- 74:28/q06/p1 They are recreated or given new skin or bodies, and Saqar burns them again without end. — Marthad, Muqatil, Al-Dahhak, Al-Kalbi, Ibn Abbas, and others describe renewed seizing after recreation; Muqatil and Ibn Abbas mention changed skins or bodies. Ata from Ibn Abbas describes burning to charcoal and a more severe renewed punishment; Razi's Ibn Abbas report adds blood, flesh, and bone and repeated intensification. Al-Dahhak also says Hell never tires. Kashshaf and Al-Alusi explicitly distinguish destruction from leaving them dead; Bursevi and Ibn Ashur cite 4:56, and Study Quran cites 87:12-13 and 4:56. Elmalili stresses that Saqar does not release them. [29 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مرثد); THALABI(الضحاك,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك,الكلبي,عطاء عن ابن عباس,مقاتل); JISHUMI(الضحاك,مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك,مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(عطاء عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن بريدة وأبو سنان); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Bg, Ṭ)]
- 74:28/q06/p2 The Fire neither lets its inhabitants live nor lets them die. — Mujahid is reported by Tabari, Thalabi, Mawardi, Jishumi, Baghawi, and Qurtubi; several of these explain that burning resumes after recreation. Tabari states that they are destroyed and renewed; Ibn Burayda and Abu Sinan associate the state with consumed and replaced bodily tissue. Asad and Study Quran also give this reading; Study Quran cites 87:12-13 and 4:56. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); JISHUMI(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن بريدة وأبو سنان); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(Bg, Ṭ)]

## 74:28/q07 What do the two near-parallel negations contribute rhetorically?  [لَا تُبْقِى وَلَا تَذَرُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the pair is emphatic synonymy, cumulative wording, or a division between persons, body parts, the intensity of torment, and successive destruction and restoration.
- 74:28/q07/p1 They repeat near-synonymous senses for emphasis and complete negation. — Razi compares paired synonyms to saying someone turned away and avoided him. Qurtubi calls the repetition an emphasis of total bodily burning. Suyuti lists coordinated near-synonyms as a rhetorical type. Al-Mubarrad disputes the existence of such Qur'anic synonym pairs, so his note argues against this analysis. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL; ITQAN; ITQAN(المبرد)-]
- 74:28/q07/p2 The paired terms together add meaning beyond either verb alone. — An unnamed view in Itqan compares added meaning from paired terms to meaning added by extra letters. Suyuti warns against alleging needless repetition and cites this phrase alongside 2:157. Al-Zarkashi says discourse structure, including metaphor, adds meaning and should stop an exegete from assuming simple synonymy. Al-Mubarrad also insists the paired words have distinct senses. [4 notes: ITQAN(الزركشي,المبرد,بعضهم); ITQAN+]
- 74:28/q07/p3 The first verb describes consuming or destroying; the second describes renewed punishment rather than leaving victims dead or alone. — Kashshaf and Al-Alusi explicitly distinguish destruction and restoration. Ibn Jawzi and reports from Muqatil, Al-Kalbi, Ibn Abbas, Al-Dahhak, and others divide consumption from pursuit after recreated skin or bodies; Bursevi and Ibn Ashur use 4:56 for renewal. [12 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك,الكلبي,مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:28/q07/p4 The first leaves no deserving person untormented; the second leaves no part of their bodies unburned. — Razi reports this as one distinction made by others. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(آخرون)]
- 74:28/q07/p5 The first leaves no body part; the second applies the Fire's full power and intensity. — Razi reports this as a further distinction. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(آخرون)]
- 74:28/q07/p6 The first consumes flesh; the second spares no bone. — Nasafi gives this division, and Al-Alusi reports it from Al-Suddi but ranks it weaker than the preceding explanations. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(السدي)]
- 74:28/q07/p7 The pair excludes both anything remaining unharmed and anything escaping destruction. — Kashshaf and Al-Alusi frame this as nothing spared and nothing released from destruction; Baydawi likewise says nothing is left before being destroyed. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 74:28/q08 Does Saqar tire or give its victims respite?  [لَا تُبْقِى وَلَا تَذَرُ · interpretation]
- 74:28/q08/p1 It never tires or weakens and allows no pause, relief, or respite from punishment. — Thalabi says everything has a pause and weariness except Hell. Al-Dahhak says the same in Jishumi and Baghawi, and Al-Alusi repeats that everything else slackens. Biqai says Saqar destroys or alters without fatigue; Mawardi denies respite, and Islahi says it remains wholly vigilant without easing agony. [7 notes: THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(الضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:28/q09 How does this description work as a warning in its passage?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes emphasize either the warning's general audience or the different responses to its terrifying presentation.
- 74:28/q09/p1 It warns humankind generally by portraying Saqar's terrifying destruction. — Farra says the description warns humankind and explains nadhīr as warning, citing analogous language at 54:16 and 67:18. Islahi says the general warning is not addressed to Muhammad and Hell's horror exceeds worldly imagination. Kuranyolu says verses 27–28 portray a terrifying place that spares nothing and scorches human skin. [3 notes: FARRA; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 74:28/q09/p2 Its astonishing scale exposes the folly of arrogant disbelief and benefits seekers of truth. — Ibn Atiyya says the preceding question expresses amazement at Saqar's enormity. Ibn Ashur says the clause elaborates the terror of that question. Islahi says disbelievers mock Hell from imagined omniscience, whereas the warning rests on divine wisdom: truth seekers benefit and arrogant people ruin their fate. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:28/q10 How should this portrayal of the hereafter be understood?  [* · interpretation]
- 74:28/q10/p1 Allegorically, as part of the Qur'an's descriptions of the hereafter. — Asad says the following verse and verses 28 onward indicate this reading. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 74:28/q11 What interpretive cautions accompany discussion of this wording?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the caution concerns overreading repetition or admitting extraneous and unreliable material; the notes do not present them as competing rules.
- 74:28/q11/p1 Respect how the paired words work together and avoid treating them as needless repetition. — Suyuti says the combined wording can add meaning beyond either word and cites 2:157. Al-Zarkashi calls for attention to discourse structure, including metaphor, before assuming synonymy. [2 notes: ITQAN(الزركشي); ITQAN+]
- 74:28/q11/p2 Keep unrelated disciplinary arguments, unreliable revelation and virtue reports, and extraneous stories out of exegesis. — Abu Hayyan criticizes tafsirs for including these materials and Israelite histories. [1 notes: ITQAN(أبو حيان)]
