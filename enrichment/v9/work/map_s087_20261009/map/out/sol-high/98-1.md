# 98:1: 32 questions

## 98:1/q01 How is lam yakun formed and why does its nūn remain?  [لَمْ يَكُنِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether nūn can be deleted once it bears a temporary vowel.
- 98:1/q01/p1 The jussive removes the wāw but retains nūn, whose vowel is temporary. — Al-Naḥḥās invokes adjacent sukūns; al-Khalīl, Sībawayh, al-Kisāʾī and al-Farrāʾ reject nūn loss. Al-Burhān relates retention to the stated endpoint. [3 notes: NAHHAS(الخليل وسيبويه والكسائي والفراء); BURHAN+; NAHHAS-]
- 98:1/q01/p2 Some grammarians allow dropping the nūn. — They cite poetic wa-lāk isqunī; al-Naḥḥās rejects deleting both wāw and ḍamma as jussive signs. [1 notes: NAHHAS(غيرهم); NAHHAS(الخليل وسيبويه والكسائي والفراء)-]

## 98:1/q02 What alternate wording or order is recorded at the opening?  [لَمْ يَكُنِ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ أَهْلِ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ وَٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ · readings]
turns on: Whether Ubayy's or Ibn Masʿūd's codex changes the verb, order, or case, and whether the variant is recited.
- 98:1/q02/p1 Ibn Masʿūd places polytheists before People of the Book. — Al-Qurṭubī and Ibn al-ʿArabī permit this only as exposition, not codex recitation. [7 notes: MAWARDI(ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA(ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); QURTUBI(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL; JISHUMI(الأعمش); THALABI(الأعمش)]
- 98:1/q02/p2 Ubayy's codex has mā kāna or fa-mā kāna and related case variants. — Reports vary between plural and singular alladhī and sometimes give rasūlan as a detached circumstance. [7 notes: THALABI(أبي,الأعمش); MAWARDI(أبي بن كعب); IBNATIYYA(أبي بن كعب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبي بن كعب); QURTUBI(أبي بن كعب); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 98:1/q02/p3 The standard wording is lam yakun alladhīna kafarū, with the scripturalists named before polytheists. — Al-Qurṭubī distinguishes the codex recitation from Ibn Masʿūd's explanatory order; Ibn al-ʿArabī compares an explanatory rendering of 65:1. [4 notes: QURTUBI(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)]

## 98:1/q03 Which people are described as disbelievers?  [ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the characterization concerns prior deviation or rejection of Muhammad.
- 98:1/q03/p1 The disbelievers include scriptural communities and polytheists, particularly those rejecting Muhammad's message. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 59:11; al-Islāḥī specifies Qurʾān and Messenger as the denied objects. Al-Ālūsī says scripturalist disbelief followed their earlier prophets. [16 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 98:1/q03/p2 The phrasing can include scripturalists who are themselves polytheists. — Rāzī and Qurtubī cite Trinity, Christian divinization of Jesus, claims about Ezra and Jewish anthropomorphism; Elmalılı favors the overlap by reference to the following verse. Al-Ālūsī rejects this construal. [7 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قوم); QURTUBI-FULL; ELMALILI; ELMALILI+; ALUSI(بعض)-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 98:1/q04 How does min work in the description?  [أَهْلِ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether it is explanatory or partitive, and how the prepositional phrase attaches.
- 98:1/q04/p1 Min is explanatory, identifying the named groups. — Rāzī compares 22:30; al-Ālūsī says the description may refer to their postmission state, so it need not imply every scripturalist had already disbelieved. [9 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI(بعض); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 98:1/q04/p2 Min is partitive, restricting the claim to some members. — Al-Māturīdī cites Christians including Melkites who retained true belief; al-Islāḥī notes believers and impartial people in both communities; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī limits it to rejecters of Islam. [6 notes: ALUSI(أبو منصور الماتريدي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو منصور الماتريدي); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; RAZI]
- 98:1/q04/p3 The phrase is circumstantial with the pronoun in kafarū. — Al-Ālūsī gives this parse. [2 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 98:1/q04/p4 Either partitive or explanatory syntax is possible. — Elmalılı lists several possible distributions of the two groups. [2 notes: ELMALILI]
- 98:1/q04/p5 The phrase is an omitted modifier of the subject. — Al-Samīn supplies the implied modifier. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 98:1/q05 Who are People of the Book here?  [أَهْلِ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the referent is generally Jews and Christians, specifically Medinan Jews, or includes Magians.
- 98:1/q05/p1 Jews and Christians generally. — Wāḥidī compares their relation to scripture with 9:30; al-Biqāʿī says their religion began in truth but was altered; Nasafī explains ahl as those closely attached to a community. [36 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ, IK, Q, Sh)]
- 98:1/q05/p2 Specifically the Jews of Yathrib, including Qurayẓa, Naḍīr and Qaynuqāʿ. — Ibn ʿAbbās names these tribes; Ibn ʿĀshūr includes neighboring settlements and individual Christians in the discussion. [11 notes: QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 98:1/q05/p3 Magians may count as scripturalists for this purpose. — Some cite the Prophetic instruction to treat Magians by scripturalist rules. [2 notes: RAZI(بعض العلماء); RAZI-FULL]
- 98:1/q05/p4 Magians are excluded. — Others cite the Arabian context and 6:156, which names the two earlier scriptural groups. [2 notes: RAZI(الآخرون); RAZI-FULL]

## 98:1/q06 Why are People of the Book named first and by this title?  [أَهْلِ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ · rhetoric]
- 98:1/q06/p1 Their knowledge of revelation makes their rejection especially grave and puts them first. — Rāzī says wāw does not itself signal order; he cites Medinan setting, foreknowledge, influence and social rank. Ibn ʿĀshūr says they supplied polytheists' objections. The title may also honor learned status. [12 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 98:1/q07 Who are the polytheists here?  [وَٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether local Arab idolaters, all who associate partners, or scripturalists too.
- 98:1/q07/p1 The local Arab idolaters, especially Quraysh and those around Mecca or Medina. — Ibn ʿAbbās localizes the reference; al-Islāḥī treats mushrikūn as Quraysh or Ishmaelites. Elmalılı rejects confining the general term to them. [25 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI(بعض); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 98:1/q07/p2 All who assign partners to God, including idol, fire, sun or desire worship. — Ibn Kathīr includes Arab and non-Arab fire worshippers; Elmalılı cites 79:24 and 28:38 and distinguishes divine attributes from plurality of divine essence. [15 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI; ELMALILI+]
- 98:1/q07/p3 The term can describe some People of the Book too. — Trinity, claims about Christ or Ezra, and anthropomorphism are cited; al-Ālūsī rejects making them the coordinate referent. [7 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قوم); QURTUBI-FULL; ELMALILI; ELMALILI+; ALUSI(بعض)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 98:1/q07/p4 The two groups differ in religious history: scripturalists believed before disbelieving, while polytheists began in natural disposition and disbelieved at maturity. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this historical distinction. [1 notes: QURTUBI]

## 98:1/q08 How is the case of al-mushrikīn read and parsed?  [وَٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ · readings]
turns on: Whether genitive coordinates with ahl or nominative with alladhīna.
- 98:1/q08/p1 The common genitive coordinates with ahl. — Naḥḥās says coordination with alladhīna would be nominative; Qurtubī prefers genitive because nominative can suggest both groups are outside the scripturalists. Al-Ālūsī denies mere adjacency explains the case. [14 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI(الأعمش); IBNATIYYA(الجمهور); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(قارئ); ALUSI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL+; QURTUBI(الأعمش وإبراهيم)+]
- 98:1/q08/p2 Al-Aʿmash, Ibrāhīm and others read nominative al-mushrikūn. — They coordinate it with alladhīna rather than ahl. [11 notes: THALABI(الأعمش); JISHUMI(الأعمش); IBNATIYYA(بعض الناس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI(الأعمش وإبراهيم); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(بعض القراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(قارئ); ALUSI-FULL]

## 98:1/q09 What legal consequence is drawn from grouping these communities?  [أَهْلِ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ وَٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ · law]
turns on: Whether unbelief is one legal community.
- 98:1/q09/p1 All unbelief is one community and the two may inherit from one another. — Some jurists infer this from the shared description 'those who disbelieved'. [2 notes: RAZI(العلماء); RAZI-FULL]
- 98:1/q09/p2 The conjunction marks legally distinct communities. — A protected scripturalist differs from a polytheist, as reflected in a report on marriage and slaughtered food. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 98:1/q10 What is the Arabic sense of munfakkīn?  [مُنفَكِّينَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the root denotes separation, continuity, or an ending.
- 98:1/q10/p1 It denotes separation, release, departure or ceasing after attachment. — Al-Qaffāl compares releasing a captive or pledge, opening a book, and a dislocated bone. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it the intransitive of separating. Poems and usage support the sense. - 98:1/q10/p2 Under negation, the defective idiom mā انفك can mean continuing or never ceasing. — Al-Farrāʾ contrasts it with independent separation. Dhū al-Rumma's camels prompted al-Aṣmaʿī to object, while al-Māzinī takes illā as a detached exception; Ṭarafa's sword illustrates persistence. [17 notes: FARRA(ذو الرمة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; NAHHAS(الأصمعي,المازني,ذو الرمة); THALABI(طرفة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,طرفة); JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL(ذو الرمة,طرفة)]

- 98:1/q10/p4 It can mark dying, vanishing or departing the world. — Qurtubī gives separate formulations: endpoint of life, expiry of allotted time, and departure. [10 notes: QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Diğer bir yorum)]

## 98:1/q11 Is munfakkīn from complete or defective infakka?  [مُنفَكِّينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether a predicate must be supplied to the participle.
- 98:1/q11/p1 It is the predicate of yakun from complete intransitive infakka; separation itself needs no second predicate. — Ṭabarī and Naḥḥās argue the independent verb needs no complement; Nasafī says 'from disbelief' is understood from the relative clause. Abū Ḥayyān permits predicate omission only as poetic necessity; al-Samīn challenges that principle by analogy to ẓanna. [22 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض النحاة); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 98:1/q11/p2 It can be construed as defective mā انفك with an omitted predicate such as knowing Muhammad's affair. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī reject ordinary omission; al-Samīn disputes their reasoning. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA(بعض النحويين); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(بعض النحاة); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ABUHAYYAN-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL(بعض النحاة)-]

## 98:1/q13 Why did the verse seem difficult beside 98:4?  [* · interpretation]
- 98:1/q13/p1 On an apparent reading, 'until' implies departure from disbelief upon proof, but 98:4 and subsequent history show rejection. — Wāḥidī calls the passage exceptionally difficult; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that a subjunctive seems to put an already arrived messenger in the future.
