# 94:4: 12 questions

## 94:4/q01 What does 'raised' convey here?  [وَرَفَعْنَا · rhetoric]
turns on: The image can describe honorable reputation or the strength of the public call.
- 94:4/q01/p1 It is a metaphor for causing noble fame and praise. — Ibn Ashur says God created the means of Muhammad's reputation; height evokes dignity beyond ordinary reach, grounded in rare virtues and his title al-Amīn. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 94:4/q01/p2 It evokes the Prophet's once weak call becoming powerful and widespread. — Islahi compares the call with a reverberating roar. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 94:4/q02 What does 'for you' add to the wording?  [لَكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The dispute concerns delayed clarification versus ordinary emphatic address and benefit.
- 94:4/q02/p1 Its fronting emphasizes personal benefit, exclusive address and prompt reassurance. — Bint al-Shati calls this ordinary, purposeful Arabic and quotes Abduh on hastening the good news. Elmalılı contrasts beneficial renown with harmful fame; Islahi hears God's support for him alone. Ibn Ashur says the parallel لك of 94:1 and عنك convey care. [7 notes: BINTSHATI(الشيخ محمد عبده); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI+]
- 94:4/q02/p2 It delays specification of the object, producing ambiguity then clarification. — Al-Zamakhshari, al-Baydawi and al-Nasafi compare صدرَك, ذكرك and وزرك; Ibn Ashur acknowledges this effect. Bint al-Shati objects that readers join لك to its object and صدرَك already identifies it. [8 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(مفسرون); BAYDAWI-FULL+; BAYDAWI+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; KASHSHAF+; NASAFI-FULL+; NASAFI+; BINTSHATI-]
- 94:4/q02/p3 It is an insertion that might mark specification, exclusivity or emphasis. — Bint al-Shati reports these alternatives from al-Nishaburi and rejects the idea that the pronouns are redundant. [1 notes: BINTSHATI(النيسابوري); BINTSHATI-]
- 94:4/q02/p4 Its place after removal of the burden continues the verse's rhetorical sequence. — Al-Alusi cites the conjunction and fronted لك after 94:3; Tabataba'i notes the poetic lift after the burden. [3 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 94:4/q03 What does 'remembrance' mean?  [ذِكْرَكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word here denotes praised renown alone or also hints at scripture.
- 94:4/q03/p1 It means praised reputation, dignity and honorable mention. — Asad gives primary remembrance and a figurative sense of eminence; Elmalılı calls it honor. Ibn Ashur connects praise with the title al-Amīn. Bint al-Shati cites al-Raghib: remembering may be inward or spoken, recalling the forgotten or retaining it; dhikr often accompanies God's name. [8 notes: ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(الراغب)]
- 94:4/q03/p2 It also evokes revelation, the Qur'an and thought. — Elmalılı cites 21:10. Bint al-Shati contrasts dhikr attached to God's name with al-dhikr for revelation, citing 18:10, 20:14, 20:42, 20:124, 23:11, 38:8, 15:6, 15:9, 54:25, 41:41, 16:44, 25:18 and 36:11. [3 notes: ELMALILI; BINTSHATI]

## 94:4/q04 How is the rāʾ in 'your remembrance' pronounced?  [ذِكْرَكَ · readings]
turns on: Rhyme matching versus the regular pronunciation rule.
- 94:4/q04/p1 It is emphatic to match the verse endings. — Ibn al-Jazari attributes this to Makkī and others for both ذكرك and وزرك; works permitting both say emphasis is more common, and al-Dānī learned it from Abū al-Fatḥ. [2 notes: NASHR(مكي)]
- 94:4/q04/p2 It is light according to the regular rule. — Ibn al-Jazari reports this from other authorities; al-Dānī chose it despite learning the emphatic form. [2 notes: NASHR(الآخرون); NASHR+]

## 94:4/q06 What follows from joining the Prophet's mention to God's?  [ذِكْرَكَ · theology]
turns on: The notes draw different consequences for testimony, obedience, communal honor and the benefit of fame.
- 94:4/q06/p1 Faith and worship require acknowledgment of Muhammad as God's messenger. — Ibn Ata and Tustari say faith is completed by naming him; Ibn Ata says Messenger implies Lord. Ibn Abbas says worship without the testimony brings no benefit and is unbelief. Dahhak says prayer and sermons lack validity; al-Sarsari says this of adhan. Qashani joins both declarations with Islam's completion. [15 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء); THALABI(ابن عطاء); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI(الضحاك,عطاء عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الصرصري); DURR(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 94:4/q06/p2 Obedience and allegiance to him count as obedience and allegiance to God. — Razi cites 4:80 and 48:10; Nasafi adds 47:33, 64:12, 4:13 and 9:62. Baydawi, Biqai, Alusi and Elmalılı list the same honor. [13 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; BIQAI; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 94:4/q06/p3 His community shares in honor through reverent remembrance. — Razi describes Sunna preservation, greetings and hope for intercession. Biqai cites Razi that the community receives a full share, and reports God's friends are those whose mention or sight recalls God, whose speech adds knowledge and whose conduct prompts detachment. [11 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI(الرازي,رسول الله); BIQAI-FULL(الرازي,النبي)]
- 94:4/q06/p4 Public renown benefits leaders, while obscurity may benefit private worshippers. — Ibn Atiyya says God apportions each according to need. A report about an obscure servant is unverified in one version; another says God raised a servant's name. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 94:4/q07 What did reports say God told the Prophet about his raised mention?  [* · reports]

- 94:4/q07/p1 Gabriel relayed that whenever God is mentioned, Muhammad is mentioned with Him. — The Abu Said report is widespread. ## 94:4/q08 When and where was the raised remembrance evident?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The timing of the Medinan adhan relative to Meccan revelation and the verse's reach beyond earthly life.
- 94:4/q08/p1 It was already spread in Mecca. — Ibn Atiyya says his name had become known through the earth then; Ibn Ashur and Biqai trace its causes to childhood virtues and al-Amin. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI]
- 94:4/q08/p2 The adhan is a future fulfillment relative to Meccan revelation. — Ibn Atiyya notes that the adhan began in Medina, so the verse anticipates this instance. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 94:4/q08/p3 The verse reflects the public spread of Islam through Arabia and beyond. — Islahi names hajj pilgrims and the Ansar; Kur'an Yolu also allows an early promise of later worldwide reach. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 94:4/q08/p4 Its scope includes this world, heaven and the Hereafter. — Qatada says both worlds. Qurtubi names angels, believers, the Praiseworthy Station and noble ranks; Razi says the honor continues until Resurrection. [23 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); JISHUMI(أبو علي); BAGHAWI(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الثعلبي,الماوردي); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); NASHR(قتادة); DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); QUTB-ZILAL]

## 94:4/q09 How does this blessing connect with the surrounding verses and sūrahs?  [* · links]
turns on: Sources relate the verse to prior favors, later ease, the preceding sūra or a unique distinction.
- 94:4/q09/p1 The sequence of gifts consoles the Prophet and promises support. — A divine reminder lists shelter, guidance and enrichment with expansion, burden removal and raised mention. Ibn Atiyya says this consoles him against Quraysh and implies victory; Abu Hayyan similarly sees victory and later ease against mockery. Ibn Ashur reads relief for distress: Hira, revelation, Abu Talib's protection, conversions and promises, citing 26:3, 2:272 and 93:5. Biqai places inward completion before public honor. [25 notes: IBNABIHATIM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI; DURR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عدي بن ثابت عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 94:4/q09/p2 Sūra 94 completes Sūra 93, with subsequent blessings after earlier favors. — Asad dates it almost immediately after al-Duha. Islahi distinguishes pre-Islamic and early prophetic favors from later success and materialized predictions. Study Quran contrasts Sūra 93's external needs with Sūra 94's spiritual relief and closing commands. [6 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL]
- 94:4/q09/p3 Some reciters treated the two sūrahs as one unit. — Asad reports Tawus and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz reciting without a second basmala, citing Razi; Study Quran reports a similar Companion practice. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Ta'us ibn Kaysan and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz); STUDYQURAN]
- 94:4/q09/p4 The verse's position after the burden's removal heightens its effect. — Al-Alusi cites the conjunction and لك after 94:3; Tabataba'i hears a poetic lift. Ibn Ashur says رفعنا and وضعنا coordinate with نشرح, which lam has shifted to the past. [4 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR]
- 94:4/q09/p5 Raised remembrance is a special distinction of the sūra. — Ibn al-Jazari suggests this and prefers the Anas report, which adds the Quran-bearing community and the Throne's treasure. [3 notes: NASHR(أنس عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 94:4/q09/p6 The address extends through the Prophet to every true follower. — Asad gives the broader address; Islahi traces inner expansion, relief from hardship, mission completion and disciplined success. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 94:4/q10 Where was this sūra revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether all, none, or only verses 5–6 are Medinan.
- 94:4/q10/p1 Most say Mecca, shortly after Sūra 93. — Study Quran reports the majority; Asad says it came almost immediately after Sūra 93. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(most commentators); ASAD-NOTES]
- 94:4/q10/p2 Ibn Abbas says Medina. — Study Quran attributes this view to him. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(most commentators)]
- 94:4/q10/p3 Some assign only verses 5–6 to Medina. — Study Quran reports this narrower assignment. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(most commentators)]

## 94:4/q11 What grammatical observations accompany the cited discussion?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Implied verb with accusative versus direct nominal predication.
- 94:4/q11/p1 An accusative verbal noun can stand for an omitted action verb. — Sibawayh analyzes ما أنت إلا سيرا this way in statements and questions.
