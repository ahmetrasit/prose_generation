# 56:73: 16 questions

## 56:73/q01 What does the emphatic “We” add?  [نَحْنُ · rhetoric]
- 56:73/q01/p1 It stresses that God alone made the fire a sign. — Al-Biqāʿī says the sign points to resurrection, the greater Fire’s punishment, Zaqqūm, and other realities discerned by the perceptive. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 56:73/q02 How is “We made it a reminder” parsed?  [جَعَلْنَٰهَا تَذْكِرَةًۭ · grammar]
- 56:73/q02/p1 The construction has two objects: God made the fire a reminder. — Al-Naḥḥās identifies the two-object construction. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 56:73/q03 What does the fire serve to remind people of?  [تَذْكِرَةًۭ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes differ over whether tadhkira points to Hell, resurrection, divine light, a general admonition, visual illumination, or an inward covenant; al-Ālūsī explicitly rejects visual illumination as a lexical sense.
- 56:73/q03/p1 It recalls Hellfire and its punishment. — Mujāhid, Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās and many later exegetes identify the greater Fire. Al-Ṭabarī says it invites reflection and admonition; al-Zajjāj, Abū Isḥāq and Ibn al-Jawzī say seeing it prompts fear, remembrance of God and refuge from Hell. Al-Rāzī says a sensible person should fear God. Al-Kashshāf, al-Nasafī, Bursawī, al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı explain that livelihood constantly depends on fire, keeping the warning before people. Al-Kashshāf, Bursawī and al-Ālūsī also call it a model of Hell; al-Ālūsī says this account, unlike simple recollection, concerns whether the two fires are of one kind, while tadhkira on either account means remembering rather than forgetting. Al-Ālūsī cites Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid and Qatāda. Elmalılı adds that it exemplifies life's struggle; Qushayrī links the reminder with provision on journeys. The one-seventieth reports support the comparison (mapped under the report question). Ibn Barrajān, cited by al-Biqāʿī, compares fire struck from wood with God creating bodies from the unseen and says the greater Fire is hidden beyond the visible world. [38 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,الكلبي,عكرمة ومجاهد ومقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة، مجاهد، مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL(ابن برجان); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 56:73/q03/p2 It signals resurrection and God’s power to restore life. — Al-Rāzī cites 36:80: the One who puts fire into green trees can put innate heat into a dead body. Al-Bayḍāwī calls fire a sign of resurrection. Al-Biqāʿī includes resurrection in the emphatic divine making. Bursawī and al-Ālūsī infer renewal from fire emerging from moist or green wood; al-Ālūsī says dispersed matter can be restored. Kuran Yolu chiefly reads the sign in context as reflection on divine blessings and resurrection, translating tadhkira as 'sign' in line with Mujāhid. The Study Quran allows Resurrection or Hell. Ibn Barrajān also compares fire struck from wood with God creating bodies from the unseen. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(ابن برجان); BURSEVI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 56:73/q03/p3 It is a general admonition to believers. — ʿAṭāʾ says a believer should heed it; al-Wāḥidī and al-Baghawī transmit this. Kuran Yolu reports that many read tadhkira as admonition, particularly of Hell. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 56:73/q03/p4 It is evidence for punishment in the grave. — Bursawī reports this explanation from ʿAyn al-Maʿānī as an answer to deniers of grave punishment. [1 notes: BURSEVI(عين المعاني)]
- 56:73/q03/p5 It is light by which people see in darkness. — Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī report this from Mujāhid. Al-Ālūsī rejects it as the meaning of tadhkira, because remembrance cannot mean visual perception derived from sight. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 56:73/q03/p6 As human light, it recalls God as the light of heaven and earth. — Asad cites 24:35; Kuran Yolu reports his interpretation. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 56:73/q03/p7 It recalls the primordial covenant. — Al-Qāshānī gives this inward explanation, coupled with provision for spiritual wayfarers lacking knowledge and practice. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 56:73/q04 What does “matāʿ” mean and what use does the fire provide?  [مَتَٰعًۭا · meaning]
- 56:73/q04/p1 It is benefit, enjoyment, or useful provision from fire. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Kashshāf, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Baghawī, al-Ālūsī, Bursawī and Elmalılı gloss it as benefit or provision; Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses temporary enjoyment and cites 4:77. Al-Biqāʿī lists light, warmth, cooking, processing, melting, hardening, protection from predators and access to aims. Mujāhid and al-Qurṭubī mention cooking, baking, light and warmth. Al-Baghawī adds guiding the lost; al-Ḥasan describes travelers carrying the provision in cloth and saddle-bags; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds guiding night travelers to shelter. Bursawī says making or carrying fire is difficult; Kuran Yolu connects controlled combustion and light to civilization. Ibn al-Jawzī also cites Ibn Qutayba's account of deserted houses serving as inns against heat and cold. [36 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك,مجاهد); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(أكثر المفسرين,الحسن,مجاهد، عكرمة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); QURTUBI-FULL(المهدوي,مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة ومجاهد); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); IBNKATHIR-FULL+]

## 56:73/q05 Who are “al-muqwīn” for whom fire is provision?  [لِّلْمُقْوِينَ · referent]
turns on: The competing derivations invoke empty wilderness, empty dwellings, empty stomachs or provisions, poverty and wealth as opposites, or tending the fire; some extend the designation to every user of fire.
- 56:73/q05/p1 People traveling or staying in barren, uninhabited country. — Al-Akhfash, Ibn Qutayba, al-Zajjāj, al-Farrāʾ, al-Layth, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabarī and others derive the term from qawāʾ, desolate land, or an emptied house; al-Naḥḥās, al-Ṭabarī, al-Nābigha, ʿAntara and al-Samīn cite poetry for empty dwellings. Al-Ṭabarī prefers travelers without provisions from the empty-dwelling expression; Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers barren-country usage as most idiomatic and says Ibn ʿAbbās's 'travelers' is close to it. Al-Ḥasan says travelers carry fire in cloths and bags. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabarī include desert dwellers, Bedouins or residents of barren land; Ibn Kathīr notes a derivation from qiy/qiwa, a distant barren place. Al-Biqāʿī says the designation can apply to someone suited to wilderness even elsewhere. Bursawī describes country without water, speech or buildings. Al-Ālūsī says aqwā parallels aṣḥara ('entered the desert'); al-Thaʿlabī calls this the majority view. [66 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); THALABI(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الفراء والزجاج,الكلبي,الليث,مقاتل والحسن والضحاك); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(أكثر المفسرين,الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وقتادة والضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(الثعلبي,الضحاك,الفراء,القشيري,النابغة,عنترة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,غيره); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن وقتادة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; TAB-FULL+; ASAD-NOTES-]
- 56:73/q05/p2 Everyone who uses fire, whether resident or traveler. — Mujāhid and ʿIkrima explicitly include residents and travelers; Khaṣīf says all creatures. Fire serves light, warmth, cooking and baking; Ibn Kathīr prefers this broader account for rich and poor, residents and nomads, and says God embedded fire in stones and pure iron so travelers could carry tools to kindle it. Al-Mahdawī says every class needs fire. Al-Ālūsī's general 'people in need' account overlaps this universal benefit. [17 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(خصيف,مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(عكرمة ومجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد، عكرمة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(المهدوي,مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة ومجاهد); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 56:73/q05/p3 Hungry people whose stomachs are empty. — Ibn Zayd invokes aqwaytu, 'I have eaten nothing'; al-Qurṭubī adds sleeping in qiwāʾ or qafr without a meal and an unnamed poet who prefers hunger to being called mean. The hungry cook food over fire. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls this weak; Abū Ḥayyān calls it very weak; al-Ālūsī objects that hunger can be relieved without cooked food. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kuran Yolu combine hunger with desert travel, deriving both from emptiness. [18 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); NAHHAS(ابن زيد); THALABI(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن زيد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد,الشاعر); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 56:73/q05/p4 The poor or those lacking provisions, wealth, or fire-starting supplies. — Abū ʿUbayda derives it from provisions running out and from an empty house; Ibn ʿĪsā says weakness and poverty. Al-Rabīʿ and al-Suddī say destitute people kindle fire to bake, roast game, or, in al-Qurṭubī's variant, lack fire-starting sticks; al-ʿAwfī also transmits the interpretation from Ibn ʿAbbās. Al-Māwardī cites al-Nābigha on travelers left with a fire drill and kindling. Al-Ālūsī says poor people need warmth and light; Elmalılı adds gathering and selling wood. Ibn Qutayba rejects lack of provisions as the defining sense because a provisioned traveler needs fire more. [14 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(أبو عبيدة); THALABI(الربيع والسدي); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,ابن عباس,الربيع والسدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); QURTUBI-FULL(الربيع); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن وقتادة); ELMALILI; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB-]
- 56:73/q05/p5 Both poor and wealthy people, since muqwī has opposite senses. — Quṭrub explains poverty as lack of property, wealth as strong animals or abundant possessions; al-Ṭībī and al-Ṭabarsī report the same opposition, linking wealth with power to get one's desires. Al-Ṭabarī notes that outside this context muqwī can mean a strong-horse owner or rich person; al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī also record the strong sense. Ibn ʿAṭiyya rejects deriving the desert sense from the distinct strong-mount verb, likening it to atraba; al-Ālūsī questions the opposites account and favors 'in need.' [7 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; THALABI(قطرب); BAGHAWI-FULL(قطرب); QURTUBI-FULL(قطرب); ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي والطبرسي); IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 56:73/q05/p6 One who kindles, strengthens, and feeds the fire. — Al-Rāzī bases this sense on the person adding fuel to the fire. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 56:73/q05/p7 Anyone in need of fire. — Al-Ālūsī prefers this sense as more plausible than the opposites account, because everyone who enjoys fire needs it. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]
- 56:73/q05/p8 People lost in a desolate place, including the lonely and spiritually hungry. — Asad derives a literal desolate or hungry sense from qawiya/qawāʾ/qiwa, then renders it tropically for lonely, unfortunate and confused people seeking human warmth and spiritual light. He rejects restriction to desert wayfarers. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ASAD-NOTES+]

## 56:73/q06 Why are desert travelers or dwellers singled out as fire’s beneficiaries?  [لِّلْمُقْوِينَ · rhetoric]
- 56:73/q06/p1 They have an especially strong practical need for fire. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Qushayrī, al-Ālūsī, Bursawī and Elmalılı cite night protection from predators and multiple needs; al-Baghawī and Ibn al-Jawzī add guiding lost people. Bursawī lists warmth, drying clothes and cooking, and says settled neighbors can obtain fire without kindling; al-Ālūsī likewise contrasts nearby residents or travelers. Ibn Qutayba argues a supplied traveler has greater need than one without provisions. Ibn Kathīr says travelers are named despite universal use; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds lighting and warming camps, guiding night travelers, and cooking at home or on journeys. [16 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل والحسن والضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(أكثر المفسرين); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وقتادة والضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 56:73/q07 What inward senses are given to the fire, its reminder, and its provision?  [تَذْكِرَةًۭ مَتَٰعًۭا لِّلْمُقْوِينَ · inward]
turns on: These readings transfer the verse's fire, warning, provision, or desolation to spiritual experience.
- 56:73/q07/p1 It warns the pious and conveys the gnostic saints. — Al-Sulamī reports Jaʿfar's explanation. [1 notes: SULAMI(جعفر)]
- 56:73/q07/p2 It recalls the primordial covenant and provisions spiritual wayfarers lacking knowledge and practice. — Al-Qāshānī pairs these two inward senses. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 56:73/q07/p3 The inward fire is love, and its provision is spiritual food for ascetic lovers. — Abū al-Ḥusayn al-Manṣūr calls the fire love kindled in a sincere lover's heart, its tree eternal divine care; without that care one would not know Book or faith. Bursawī describes lovers abstaining from food and drink; he cites Sahl al-Tustarī fasting thirty days, Abū ʿAqīl eating nothing for two years in Mecca, other ascetics, and a report of Idrīs ascending to the fourth heaven or sphere of the Sun after sixteen years without sleep, food or marriage. [6 notes: BURSEVI(أبو الحسين المنصور,أبو عقيل المغربي,سهل التستري)]
- 56:73/q07/p4 Desolate hunger also describes longing for human warmth and spiritual light. — Asad applies the literal desert and hunger wording to lonely, unfortunate and confused people. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 56:73/q08 Why is the afterlife reminder placed before worldly use?  [تَذْكِرَةًۭ وَمَتَٰعًۭا · rhetoric]
- 56:73/q08/p1 The afterlife benefit is more important and deserves priority. — Al-Rāzī calls it fuller and more worthy of attention; Bursawī and al-Ālūsī likewise read the order as directing attention to it. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 56:73/q09 What is reported about earthly fire compared with Hellfire?  [تَذْكِرَةًۭ · reports]
turns on: The reports measure different qualities: the familiar one-seventieth heat comparison and a separate darkness comparison.
- 56:73/q09/p1 Earthly fire is one part in seventy of Hellfire. — Al-Ṭabarī reports Qatāda and the Prophet; al-Kashshāf states no grading. Al-Qurṭubī reports that when Companions said earthly fire sufficed, the Prophet said Hell has sixty-nine further parts, each as hot. Ibn Kathīr labels Qatāda's route mursal; he also cites Aḥmad from Abū Hurayra, and Bukhārī and Muslim for the sixty-nine further parts, with Muslim's each-part wording. Al-Ālūsī cites the two Ṣaḥīḥ collections. The Study Quran and Bursawī repeat the ratio. In some versions God struck or cooled earthly fire twice with water so people could approach and use it; Ibn Kathīr's Aḥmad route says twice by the sea. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة,قتادة); BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام); ALUSI-FULL(النبي صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم عن أبي هريرة); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]
- 56:73/q09/p2 Hellfire is seventy times darker than earthly fire. — Ibn Kathīr cites al-Ṭabarānī from Abū Hurayra; al-Ḍiyāʾ al-Maqdisī notes Ibn Muṣʿab transmitted it from Mālik without raising it to the Prophet and considers it to meet sound-hadith conditions. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة)]

## 56:73/q10 What other reports illustrate the severity of Hellfire?  [تَذْكِرَةًۭ · reports]
turns on: The two narrations illustrate severity in different ways.
- 56:73/q10/p1 Michael has not laughed since the Fire was created. — Bursawī relays the Prophet asking Gabriel why Michael never smiles and Gabriel's answer. [1 notes: BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام)]
- 56:73/q10/p2 The least punished inhabitant of Hell wears sandals that boil his brain. — Bursawī transmits Anas; the report underlines Hell's excess over worldly fire, and the narrator prays for protection. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أنس)]

## 56:73/q11 What is reported about sharing the fire?  [وَمَتَٰعًۭا · law]
- 56:73/q11/p1 Fire is a shared resource and may not be withheld. — Ibn Kathīr gives a Prophetic report through a man of the Muhājirūn of Qarn that Muslims share fire, pasture and water, transmitted by Aḥmad and Abū Dāwūd. Abū Hurayra's version forbids withholding the three; Ibn Mājah transmits it with a good chain. Ibn ʿAbbās's version adds a ban on charging, but its chain has the weak ʿAbd Allāh ibn Kharrāsh ibn Ḥawshab. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 56:73/q12 How does this fire connect with the preceding signs of provision?  [* · links]
turns on: Al-Ālūsī disputes the culinary sequence and instead explains the order by the relative urgency and generality of water.
- 56:73/q12/p1 The passage moves from creation to grain, water, then fire needed to bake food. — Al-Nasafī relates human creation, grain, water used with food and cooking fire to 56:58, 63, 68 and 71; these sustain bodily life. Al-Ālūsī reports the same proposed order with references to 56:58, 64 and 69, but finds the rationale unconvincing. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 56:73/q12/p2 Water comes before fire because its need and benefit are more widespread and urgent. — Al-Ālūsī gives this explanation of the order. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 56:73/q13 Why does the passage describe fire without threatening its removal?  [* · rhetoric]
- 56:73/q13/p1 People can survive without fire, whereas food and water are indispensable. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts this with the earlier food and water passages. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 56:73/q14 What is reported about responding to the preceding rhetorical questions?  [* · reports]
- 56:73/q14/p1 ʿAlī answered them with 'Rather, You, Lord,' but scholars dispute doing so during prayer. — Al-Ālūsī reports Ḥujr al-Marwazī hearing ʿAlī respond three times after each question on procreation, crops, water and fire (56:58, 64, 69, 72); he notes disagreement over whether the answer is recommended in ritual prayer. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(حجر المروزي عن علي)]

## 56:73/q15 How does fire from wood answer mockery of Hell’s tree?  [* · links]
- 56:73/q15/p1 God’s joining fire and tree in this world shows He can create Zaqqūm in Hell. — Iṣlāḥī points to worldly markh and ʿafār as the analogy answering Quraysh's mockery of 17:60 and relates it to 56:52–53. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 56:73/q16 What theological response does God’s creation of fire demand?  [* · theology]
- 56:73/q16/p1 People should thank God and acknowledge His power to resurrect. — Al-Qurṭubī reasons that God is the inventor and creator, so denial of resurrection is unfitting. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
