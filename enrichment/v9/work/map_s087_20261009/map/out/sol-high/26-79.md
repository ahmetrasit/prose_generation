# 26:79: 19 questions

## 26:79/q01 How is the relative clause about feeding joined to Abraham's preceding description?  [وَٱلَّذِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether the new relative clause is coordinated, begins a sentence with an understood predicate, or serves as a description; al-Alusi disputes the eloquence of the omitted-predicate analysis.
- 26:79/q01/p1 It is coordinated with the first relative clause describing the one who created Abraham. — Al-Alusi prefers this as more fitting to Quranic eloquence; al-Samin permits the waw coordination and cites a poetic parallel. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 26:79/q01/p2 It is a new subject with a predicate omitted from the preceding clause; the following relative clauses are likewise construed. — Al-Baydawi reports this first syntactic analysis, and al-Samin allows it. Al-Alusi objects that supplying the predicates from the earlier clause weakens both expression and sense. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 26:79/q01/p3 The relative clauses describe the one who created Abraham. — Al-Samin offers this alongside the omitted-predicate construction. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 26:79/q02 Why is the relative pronoun repeated for this divine act?  [ٱلَّذِى · rhetoric]
- 26:79/q02/p1 Each repeated relative clause presents its divine attribute as independently great and sufficient to establish God's lordship and claim to worship. — Bursevi says each attribute independently establishes the Lord's claim; al-Alusi calls each set magnificent and worship-worthy; Ibn Ashur stresses the possessor of three great attributes; Tabatabai adds God's management, sustenance, and answering Abraham's prayer. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 26:79/q03 What does the emphatic pronoun huwa establish?  [هُوَ · rhetoric]
- 26:79/q03/p1 God alone is the true source of feeding and drink, excluding rival providers. — The author of al-Nazm and al-Qurtubi explicitly read the pronoun as exclusivity. Abu Hayyan says feeding and guidance can be claimed by others, unlike creation, so they receive emphasis. Ibn Ashur says fronting rebuts the claim that idols arrange sustenance or healing. Burhan says people failed to distinguish the true feeder and drink-provider. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:79/q04 What does the present tense of feeding and giving drink convey?  [يُطْعِمُنِى وَيَسْقِينِ · rhetoric]
- 26:79/q04/p1 It marks God's repeatedly renewed feeding and giving drink. — Burhan contrasts these continuing acts with a one-time event and also says the waw joins the paired acts because the people did not identify their one source. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 26:79/q05 What does God's feeding and giving drink mean here?  [يُطْعِمُنِى وَيَسْقِينِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether nourishment is ordinary bodily provision, extraordinary sustenance without food, or a distinct inward food and drink; al-Mawardi expressly rejects a speculative faith-and-acceptance reading.
- 26:79/q05/p1 God provides Abraham with ordinary food, water, nourishment, and all bodily means that make them beneficial. — Muqatil specifies hunger and thirst; al-Tabari, al-Thalabi, al-Baghawi, and others call this provision. Al-Tabrisi adds bodily health. Ibn Kathir names clouds, revived earth, fruit, and fresh water for people and livestock (25:49). Al-Razi includes appetite, strength, and discrimination; Bursevi includes chewing, swallowing, digestion, and elimination, citing Abu Hurayra's prayer for molar, stomach, and bowel. Abu Hayyan calls ordinary sustenance the apparent sense; al-Alusi says provision comes through means. Biqai notes God can remove what is consumed or prevent consumption. Islahi notes idolaters credited deities with sustenance despite admitting they did not create the universe. Al-Thalabi and Bursevi say God gives whatever food and drink He wills. [19 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; THALABI(أبو العباس بن عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عطاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(أبو هريرة); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:79/q05/p2 God can feed and quench without material food or drink. — Abu Bakr al-Warraq invokes the Prophet's night with his Lord; al-Shibli says bread satisfies through a subtle property that God could preserve without bread. Al-Thalabi's extraordinary abstinence reports illustrate the possibility. Al-Alusi explicitly calls al-Warraq's account a mystical reading. [5 notes: THALABI(أبو بكر الشبلي,أبو بكر الوراق); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو بكر الوراق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو بكر الوراق); ALUSI-FULL(أبو بكر الوراق)]
- 26:79/q05/p3 The food is the delight of faith and the drink is trust, reliance, and sufficiency in God. — Al-Tustari and one report in al-Sulami give the pairing; Study Quran reports faith with trust and contentment. Al-Mawardi rejects speculative departures from the apparent sense. [3 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(Su); MAWARDI-FULL-]
- 26:79/q05/p4 Food and drink signify the pleasure of faith and sweetness of acceptance. — Al-Qurtubi reports the esoteric interpreters' pairing. Al-Mawardi cites this very speculative construal while rejecting it in favor of the apparent, rationally accessible sense. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أهل الإشارات); MAWARDI-FULL-]
- 26:79/q05/p5 The food is the sweetness of remembrance and the drink comes from the cup of divine love. — Al-Nahrajuri gives this pairing in al-Sulami. [1 notes: SULAMI(النهرجوري)]
- 26:79/q05/p6 Feeding occurs in God's presence, while the drink discloses the blessing in the food and drink. — Al-Jurayri gives this account in al-Sulami. [1 notes: SULAMI(الجريري)]
- 26:79/q05/p7 God feeds the soul with service and gives the spirit the drink of intimacy. — An unnamed speaker in al-Sulami says service revives the soul. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 26:79/q05/p8 God's food nurtures the servant and His drink gives the servant life. — Ibn Ata speaks of divine nourishment and enlivening without naming a material or a distinct spiritual substance. [1 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
- 26:79/q05/p9 When divine identity overwhelms human essence, God gives the servant the righteous one's everlasting food without fatigue. — Ibn Ata says sensory categories of modality, quantity, and totality then dissolve. [1 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
- 26:79/q05/p10 The food is the food of divine love and the drink is love's drink. — Dhu al-Nun's poem calls love's drink the best and every other drink a mirage. [1 notes: THALABI(ذو النون المصري)]
- 26:79/q05/p11 The food is worship that revives hearts, and the drink is a holy manifestation of lordship refreshing spirits. — Bursevi transmits this from the author of Bahr al-Haqaiq. [1 notes: BURSEVI(صاحب بحر الحقائق)]
- 26:79/q05/p12 The food is knowledge of God and the drink is divine love. — Bursevi transmits this from Dhu al-Nun; al-Junayd likewise contrasts resurrection hunger and thirst with having eaten knowledge and drunk love. [3 notes: BURSEVI(ذو النون مصري); THALABI(الجنيد); STUDYQURAN(Junayd)]
- 26:79/q05/p13 The divine drink is love that intoxicates devoted servants and raises them toward the seat of truth. — Abu Yazid describes rapture and ascent to the Powerful King; Bursevi cites a couplet praising love's wine above all other drink. [2 notes: THALABI(أبو يزيد البسطامي); BURSEVI]
- 26:79/q05/p14 God nourishes the soul in the inward sense of the sequence of divine acts. — Study Quran pairs the soul's nourishment with healing its ailments and enlivening the spirit by causing the passionate ego to die. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 26:79/q06 What attitude toward causes does God's feeding teach?  [يُطْعِمُنِى وَيَسْقِينِ · interpretation]
- 26:79/q06/p1 Depend on the giver of blessings, with trust, contentment, submission, and reliance on God rather than material causes. — Al-Nasafi says dependence on material causes is characteristic of animals. Bursevi adds entrusting affairs to God and turning fully away from other dependencies. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 26:79/q07 How is the first-person yāʾ of the verse endings treated?  [يُطْعِمُنِى وَيَسْقِينِ · readings]
turns on: Whether the first-person suffix is suppressed for the verse-ending sound or affirmed in a transmitted reading; named reciters and pause practice vary.
- 26:79/q07/p1 The final yāʾ is omitted in recitation or writing to maintain the sound of the verse endings. — Al-Nahhas and al-Tabrisi cite uniform verse endings; Ibn Ashur adds lightness and rhyme at pauses, since most endings in this surah end in a still nun, mentioning 26:14. [3 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 26:79/q07/p2 The first-person yāʾ is retained in the listed verbs, including the form after yasqīn. — Al-Nahhas reports Ibn Abi Ishaq's retention and his argument that the yāʾ is the subject while nun entered for a grammatical reason. Abu Hayyan also reports Nafiʿ; al-Samin reports ʿAsim. Al-Nashr says Yaqub affirms sixteen extra yāʾs both in connection and at pause, eight quoted forms and أطيعون in eight places. Qiraat-ER says the fuller suffix alters prosodic weight without changing meaning. [5 notes: NAHHAS; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق); SAMIN-DURR(ابن أبي إسحاق); NASHR(يعقوب); QIRAAT-ER]

## 26:79/q08 Why are food and drink named at this point in Abraham's sequence?  [يُطْعِمُنِى وَيَسْقِينِ · links]
turns on: Whether the pairing chiefly marks life's continual maintenance or exemplifies the broader series of bodily and spiritual gifts.
- 26:79/q08/p1 They are ongoing provisions that maintain bodily life after creation and guidance and before illness and healing. — Ibn Atiyya calls the pair an enumeration of provision's blessing. Abu Hayyan notes life depends on them and that excess or deficiency can cause illness. Al-Alusi says they maintain human life and order more persistently than other needs, citing even Hell's inhabitants asking for water and provision (7:50). Ibn Ashur places food and water among acts tracing this life and the next. Al-Razi adds the bodily capacities that complete their benefit. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 26:79/q08/p2 Food, drink, and healing select the chief material gifts that meet worldly deficiencies. — Tabatabai says the most important instances summarize material blessings; Ibn Ashur says the five acts span bodily and intellectual creation, needs, death, and resurrection, with spiritual perfection prepared by the sequence. [3 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:79/q09 How do feeding and drinking relate to the guidance just mentioned?  [يُطْعِمُنِى وَيَسْقِينِ · links]
turns on: The scope of 'guidance' in the preceding verse: universal guidance or religious guidance.
- 26:79/q09/p1 If guidance is universal, feeding, drinking, and healing are particular instances of it. — Tabatabai explicitly conditions this relation on reading guidance universally. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 26:79/q09/p2 If guidance is religious guidance, feeding and drinking are distinct gifts following guidance, the highest gift after existence. — Tabatabai expressly gives this alternative to the universal reading. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 26:79/q10 What does the Prophet's report about spending the night with his Lord say?  [يُطْعِمُنِى وَيَسْقِينِ · reports]
- 26:79/q10/p1 The Prophet spends the night with his Lord, who feeds him and gives him drink. — Al-Jurayri cites the Prophet in al-Sulami; Ibn Atiyya quotes the Prophet directly; al-Warraq and others invoke the report when explaining nourishment without ordinary food and drink. Bursevi says it can be understood from this verse's deeper implication. [7 notes: SULAMI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI(أبو بكر الوراق); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو بكر الوراق,النبي عليه السلام); ALUSI-FULL(أبو بكر الوراق); BURHAN; BURSEVI]

## 26:79/q11 How is the Prophet's nightly feeding and drinking understood?  [يُطْعِمُنِى وَيَسْقِينِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the report's feeding and drinking name literal intake or inward states and worship.
- 26:79/q11/p1 It is literal food and drink received by the mouth. — Bursevi reports this opinion and notes that it preserves the ordinary sense of the words. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أحد القولين)]
- 26:79/q11/p2 It is spiritual nourishment through God's knowledge, intimate conversation, nearness, and love. — Bursevi reports these as states feeding hearts and spirits; Burhan specifies glorification, declaring God's oneness, and praise in place of food for special servants. [2 notes: BURSEVI(القول الثاني); BURHAN]

## 26:79/q12 Which reported cases illustrate sustenance without ordinary meals?  [يُطْعِمُنِى وَيَسْقِينِ · reports]
turns on: The reports describe distinct people, places, lengths of abstinence, and means of sustenance.
- 26:79/q12/p1 Sufyan al-Thawri in Mecca lived from one Sabbath to the next on only a handful of sand inhaled. — Muhammad ibn Kathir al-Abdi reports this in al-Thalabi. [1 notes: THALABI(محمد بن كثير العبدي)]
- 26:79/q12/p2 Ibrahim ibn Adham ate ash and dirt at a kiln and urged freedom from asking people for their possessions. — Al-Thalabi transmits the visitor's encounter; Ibrahim then warns that speech can bring ruin and faults, urging remembrance while speaking and treating the body as dead while silent. [2 notes: THALABI(إبراهيم بن أدهم,راوي خبر إبراهيم بن أدهم)]
- 26:79/q12/p3 A man discarded his waterskin after hearing that God provides every creature, then drank a cup of Paradise in a dream and lived over twenty years without customary food or drink. — Al-Thalabi relates the report and Anas's statement; the recited provision verse is 11:6. [1 notes: THALABI]
- 26:79/q12/p4 Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Nuʿm ate monthly and, after fifteen days confined by Hajjaj, was found praying. — Ali ibn Qadim reports that he said only those who eat and drink need ablution. [1 notes: THALABI(علي بن قادم)]
- 26:79/q12/p5 After a voice offered Abu Saʿid al-Khazzaz eternal satiety while starving in Tarsus, he had no appetite for years but could eat and drink at will. — Al-Thalabi transmits al-Khazzaz's account. [1 notes: THALABI(أبو سعيد الخزاز)]
- 26:79/q12/p6 A captive woman escaping toward Aleppo traversed two hundred farsakhs without food, drawing strength when hungry from reciting al-Ikhlas three times. — The woman places the journey in Sayf al-Dawla's time; al-Thalabi records her saying that the recitation restored strength, food, and drink, mentioning 112:1. [1 notes: THALABI(امرأة من حلب)]

## 26:79/q13 What does Ibn Ishaq's report say Abraham declared at birth?  [يُطْعِمُنِى وَيَسْقِينِ · reports]
- 26:79/q13/p1 After his mother fled to a cave in labor, Abraham declared that his creator, provider, feeder, and giver of drink was his sole Lord. — Ibn Abi Hatim transmits Ibn Ishaq's account. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن اسحاق)]

## 26:79/q14 What reward report attaches to reciting Abraham's prayer when going to obligatory prayer?  [* · reports]
- 26:79/q14/p1 One who recites it after ablution while setting out receives guidance, Paradise food and drink, healing and expiation, blessed life and martyrdom, forgiveness, wisdom, truthful renown, and Paradise homes. — Al-Durr transmits the Prophet's promise; al-Hasan adds a prayer for the parents. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 26:79/q15 How does Abraham use these divine acts in speaking to his people?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes emphasize either an argument from acknowledged acts or the personal mode of presenting that argument.
- 26:79/q15/p1 The acts known to his audience establish that God alone is Lord and worthy of worship. — Al-Mawardi says Abraham cites agreed favors as proof. Al-Tabrisi says only their performer deserves divinity. Kuranyolu says all substitute gods are fabricated and their devotees misguided; Study Quran makes capacity for these acts the test of worthiness. Bursevi and Tabatabai make each repeated attribute an independent ground for God's claim. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 26:79/q15/p2 He presents the fundamentals of preaching in logical order as personal impressions that may stir hearts. — Islahi says the acquittal and migration phase made intellectual persuasion useless, while this subtle presentation still offered an appeal. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:79/q16 How are divine and human agency worded across this sequence?  [* · rhetoric]
- 26:79/q16/p1 Abraham attributes creation, guidance, feeding, drink, and healing to God, but speaks of illness as his own condition. — Al-Samarrai contrasts the divine attributions with Abraham's words 'when I am ill' rather than attributing illness to God. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 26:79/q17 What literary devices do the consecutive clauses and their transition display?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes identify different features of the same speech: balanced sequence, visual wordplay, and transition to prayer.
- 26:79/q17/p1 The successive clauses exemplify al-tafwit, varied meanings set out in separate sentences of equal measure. — Al-Itqan defines the device as accommodating long, medium, or short sentences and identifies Abraham's guidance, provision, healing, death, and revival statements as a long-sentence example. [2 notes: ITQAN]
- 26:79/q17/p2 The verbs exemplify musahhaf, or line jinas, in which letter forms differ through dotting. — Al-Itqan names the device and points to the cited verbs. [1 notes: ITQAN]
- 26:79/q17/p3 Abraham moves gracefully from divine attributes to a fitting request for judgment and the company of the righteous. — Al-Tibi identifies the transition to 'My Lord, grant me judgment and join me with the righteous' as a good request. [1 notes: ITQAN(الطيبي)]

## 26:79/q18 How does the Abraham account fit this surah and the preceding one?  [* · links]
turns on: The two observations concern distinct aspects of narrative placement: preaching pattern and prophetic sequence.
- 26:79/q18/p1 Like the prior anecdote, it serves the surah's preaching purpose and ends with its recurring verses. — Islahi compares the narrative to the preceding anecdote. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:79/q18/p2 It fills the gap after Noah in the previous surah, since Abraham was the most prominent messenger between Noah and Moses. — Islahi explains the narrative placement across the two surahs this way. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:79/q19 What inward account of death, life, and resurrection accompanies this sequence?  [* · inward]
turns on: The reports apply the bodily acts to differing inward states and to resurrection.
- 26:79/q19/p1 God nourishes and heals the soul, then brings the passionate ego to death and the spirit to life. — Study Quran gives this symbolic reading of the divine acts, including feeding. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 26:79/q19/p2 Spiritual death and life can be justice and grace, disobedience and obedience, separation and meeting God, or ignorance and knowledge. — Study Quran attributes these paired formulations to al-Thalabi. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Al-Thaʿlabī)]
- 26:79/q19/p3 People may die outwardly yet live inwardly, dying through forgetfulness of God and living through remembrance. — Study Quran reports this as another interpretation. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Su)]
- 26:79/q19/p4 At resurrection, piety clothes, knowledge feeds, and love quenches those who acquired them. — Al-Junayd contrasts these servants with the naked, hungry, and thirsty resurrected. [2 notes: THALABI(الجنيد); STUDYQURAN(Junayd)]
