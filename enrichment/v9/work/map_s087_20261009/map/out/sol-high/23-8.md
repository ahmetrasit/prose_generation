# 23:8: 19 questions

## 23:8/q01 How is the word for trusts read?  [لِأَمَٰنَٰتِهِمْ · readings]
turns on: Whether the noun is expressed as one collective or verbal noun or as multiple individual trusts, with ʿahd and 4:58 offered as analogies; sources differ on the named singular readers.
- 23:8/q01/p1 It is read in the plural, amānātihim. — Al-Naḥḥās prefers the plural because God entrusted many duties, including purification, prayer, and fasting, but rejects Abū ʿUbayd's inference from 4:58: that verse concerns particular deposits, and singular ʿahd here permits either number. Ibn Khālawayh, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, Jishumī, Abū ʿAlī through al-Ṭabrisī, and al-Samīn cite 4:58 for the plural; al-Wāḥidī compares singular collective 6:108 with plural 23:63. Al-Samīn prefers the plural for its generality. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it explicitly covers numerous kinds and trustees. Nāfiʿ and Abū ʿAmr are included among singular readers in some accounts, despite accounts naming Ibn Kathīr alone; al-Naḥḥās reports Meccans and al-Nasafī Meccans and Sahl. Al-Baghawī's cited command is noted as 4:57. Qirāʾāt-ER says the plural individualizes obligations. [23 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير); NAHHAS(أبو عبيد); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(الباقون); THALABI(الباقون); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(الباقون); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن كثير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); TABRISI(أبو علي,ابن كثير); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن كثير); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); NASHR(ابن كثير,الباقون); ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); QIRAAT-ER(Lvw); NAHHAS(المكيون)+; SAMIN-DURR(ابن كثير)+]
- 23:8/q01/p2 It is read in the singular, amānatihim. — Ibn Kathīr is repeatedly named, here and at 70:32; Ibn Mujāhid and al-Fārisī call him the sole singular reader, while al-Rāzī also names Nāfiʿ, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī mention a transmission from Abū ʿAmr, al-Naḥḥās says Meccans, and al-Nasafī adds Sahl. Ibn Khālawayh, al-Thaʿlabī, Jishumī, and al-Baghawī point to singular ʿahd; al-Wāḥidī and Abū ʿAlī say the singular verbal or collective noun embraces multiple trusts; Ibn al-Jawzī calls it generic. Al-Bayḍāwī says it avoids ambiguity or retains the original verbal noun; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 23:2. Al-Ṭabrisī cites a Prophetic statement on a woman's womb and 2:228 as an example of singular usage. Qirāʾāt-ER says it treats trust as one abstract concept. [26 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير); NAHHAS(أبو عبيد,المكيون); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير); THALABI(ابن كثير); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن كثير); JISHUMI(ابن كثير); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن كثير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير); TABRISI(أبو علي,ابن كثير,النبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير); RAZI-FULL(نافع وابن كثير); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن كثير); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن كثير); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); SAMIN-DURR(ابن كثير); NASHR(ابن كثير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن كثير); QIRAAT-ER(Lvw)]

## 23:8/q02 What does amāna denote as a word here?  [لِأَمَٰنَٰتِهِمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether a verbal noun retains its abstract sense in this verse or names what is entrusted; 4:58 and 8:27 give evidence for the latter usage.
- 23:8/q02/p1 It denotes entrusted things or obligations, although it originates as a verbal noun. — Al-Wāḥidī says a verbal noun names its object; al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī call the entrusted object itself a trust and cite 4:58, while al-Rāzī adds 8:27. Al-Samīn agrees the word can name an object as in 4:58 and 8:27 and reports al-Zamakhsharī's argument that things, not abstractions, are returned or betrayed there. Abū Ḥayyān says delivery concerns the entrusted object or statement, not the act of entrusting. Al-Ālūsī favors concrete entrusted things, especially property, but says plural form does not rule out a verbal noun. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī favors entrusted property and similar things; al-Bursawī calls it what one is entrusted with. Ibn ʿĀshūr describes privately deposited valuables whose value and lack of witnesses tempt denial. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 23:8/q02/p2 It can denote the abstract act or quality of trust itself. — Al-Samīn says this verse admits either the abstract trust or the entrusted object, unlike the object-focused examples at 4:58 and 8:27. Abū Ḥayyān notes it can denote the act of entrusting but favors the delivered item in context. Qirāʾāt-ER says the singular treats trust as one abstract concept. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls amāna originally a verbal noun while preferring concrete property here. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; QIRAAT-ER(Lvw)]

## 23:8/q03 What trusts are meant?  [لِأَمَٰنَٰتِهِمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase is confined to people's deposits, hidden divine duties, bodily faculties or inward deposits, or extends across all of them; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī weighs the breadth against the wording and plural.
- 23:8/q03/p1 All trusts owed to God and people, including religious duties, bodily capacities, property, and social obligations. — Al-Wāḥidī names fasting, prayer, washing, deposits and goods; al-Baghawī names worship, deposits and services; al-Ṭabrisī adds loans, sales and testimony. Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes word, deed, dealings and promises; al-Rāzī includes hidden worship, ablution, prayer and ritual bathing, with emancipations and restrictions created by speech. Al-Qurṭubī includes belief. Al-Biqāʿī reports al-Rāzī's sexual integrity, sincerity and truthfulness. Al-Ālūsī reports the majority's broad reading and offers bodily members and faculties; al-Elmalılı adds agencies, guardianships and public offices. Iṣlāḥī adds blessings, nature, revelation and social responsibility; KURANYOLU includes material and spiritual things. Al-Sāmarrāʾī lists secrets, dependents, crops, offices, courts and religious law, cites 33:72, and reports al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ, al-Kalbī and al-Suddī for comprehensive duties. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the broad meaning semantically sound but unlikely from the wording. Ibn ʿĀshūr refers to earlier discussion at 4:58; Study Quran points to 2:283, 4:58, and 8:27. [29 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الرازي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أكثر المفسرين); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(البحر المحيط,السدي,الكلبي,قيل); STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 23:8/q03/p2 Entrusted property and other trusts between people are foremost or exclusively meant. — Ibn ʿAbbās names deposits and other entrusted things; al-Wāḥidī says most commentators prefer people's trusts over al-Kalbī's hidden duties. Al-Thaʿlabī and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr describe interpersonal trusts. Abū Ḥayyān lists this as the narrower alternative to God's broader charge. Al-Ālūsī reports an exclusively interpersonal interpretation and rejects its limitation. Ibn ʿĀshūr describes valuables deposited privately without witnesses. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers entrusted property and similar circulating trusts, inferring this from the plural and wording. [8 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI+; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي)+; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المفسرين)-]
- 23:8/q03/p3 They are duties between God and the servant that others may not see. — Al-Kalbī names ablution, ritual bathing and fasting; al-Rāzī stresses worship hidden in itself or in its performance and names fasting, bathing and complete ablution. The reported saying about incomplete prayer being the greatest betrayal illustrates this. Al-Wāḥidī says most commentators favor people's trusts instead. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); RAZI-FULL(رسول الله)]
- 23:8/q03/p4 Human organs and faculties are trusts to be used rightly. — Muḥammad ibn al-Faḍl through al-Sulamī and al-Bursawī assigns eyes, ears, tongue, feet, mouth, hands and heart their duties: avoiding forbidden sight, idle or obscene speech, slander and unlawful use; reflecting, remembering God, obeying, consuming lawful things, doing good, and attending only to God. Al-Ālūsī says body and faculties must not be used against God's command. Al-Sāmarrāʾī reports a view that body and divine gifts are betrayed when misused; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes this in the wider reading he finds unlikely from the wording. [7 notes: SULAMI(محمد بن الفضيل); BURSEVI(محمد بن الفضل); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(قيل)]
- 23:8/q03/p5 They include inward secrets and spiritual dealings entrusted by God. — Al-Qushayrī distinguishes outward duties, inward subtleties, spiritual dealings, encounters and communion. Al-Qāshānī calls them secrets deposited in the innermost self. Najmiyya through al-Bursawī describes a direct divine outpouring to humanity without intermediary, an honor requiring exclusive worship. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 23:8/q04 Why are trusts plural while covenant is singular, and why do trusts come first?  [لِأَمَٰنَٰتِهِمْ وَعَهْدِهِمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether singular ʿahd reflects fewer individual covenants, its verbal noun form, or one comprehensive covenant of faith.
- 23:8/q04/p1 The plural emphasizes the many kinds and instances of trust; the covenant is less numerous or a collective verbal noun. — Al-Ālūsī says each person has diverse trusts from God, while covenants are not comparably numerous; alternatively money and similar trusts are visibly multiple. Ibn ʿĀshūr says both varieties and trustees multiply. Al-Sāmarrāʾī reports Rūḥ al-Maʿānī's alternatives for singular ʿahd, then says trusts come first for importance and breadth. Qirāʾāt-ER says the plural individualizes duties. Al-Naḥḥās says plural better suits many duties, though the following singular ʿahd does not exclude singular amāna. [8 notes: NAHHAS(أبو عبيد,المكيون); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER(Lvw); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(روح المعاني)]
- 23:8/q04/p2 Singular covenant can encompass all duties undertaken through faith. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this broader sense, citing 2:100 and 33:15; one covenant of faith can include all binding obligations. [1 notes: TABATABAI+]

## 23:8/q05 What does their covenant refer to?  [وَعَهْدِهِمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether ʿahd is understood through ordinary human agreements, a legal covenant formula, the primordial pledge, or the comprehensive commitment of faith.
- 23:8/q05/p1 All commitments to God or people, including divine commands, contracts, oaths, vows, and promises. — Al-Ṭabrisī lists commands, vows and human agreements; al-Rāzī lists contracts, oaths and vows. Al-Ālūsī cites the majority for duties, oaths, vows and agreements and also God's commands in scripture and through the Messenger. Al-Elmalılı includes God, Prophet and people. Iṣlāḥī includes natural and revealed obligations plus express or customary promises. Al-Qurṭubī includes word, deed and belief; KURANYOLU adds the divine rulings accepted by professing faith. Study Quran sets ʿahd beside mīthāq and mīʿād as terms for humanity's relation to God and cites 2:40, 6:152, 36:60, 2:63, 5:70 and 57:8. [15 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أكثر المفسرين); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); STUDYQURAN]
- 23:8/q05/p2 It means agreements and commitments made with other people. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī and Ibn Kathīr stress human contracts or agreements; al-Wāḥidī reports this as one interpretation. Ibn ʿĀshūr defines a covenant as reciprocal commitment between two or more parties, named for pledging by God who oversees it; he previously discusses 2:27. [7 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:8/q05/p3 It means the oaths taken from them. — Al-Wāḥidī reports al-Kalbī's explanation of ʿahd as their oaths. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي)]
- 23:8/q05/p4 It is the primordial or covenant of exclusive worship and witness of God. — Al-Qāshānī identifies God's primordial pledge at the beginning of human nature. Najmiyya through al-Bursawī identifies the primordial pledge to worship God alone, citing 36:61, and says desire is the most hateful idol. Al-Qushayrī distinguishes pledging to worship only God from witnessing none but Him in either world; al-Sulamī makes covenant the bearing and preserving of the bodily trusts, with neglect characterized as unjust and ignorant. [5 notes: SULAMI; QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 23:8/q05/p5 It may mean all obligations bound by the single covenant of faith. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this over the narrower legal usage, citing 2:100 and 33:15. Iṣlāḥī includes obligations grounded in nature and revelation. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI+]
- 23:8/q05/p6 In legal usage it is an obligation undertaken with a covenant formula, like a vow or oath. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī gives this as the technical legal sense before preferring the broader sense of all obligations of faith. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 23:8/q06 What does ʿahd mean as a word?  [وَعَهْدِهِمْ · meaning]
- 23:8/q06/p1 It is a binding commitment or covenant to be maintained. — Al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī say the matter undertaken itself is called ʿahd. Al-Bursawī emphasizes attending to a thing over time, including a binding compact. Al-Ālūsī says the original verbal noun names a concrete undertaking. Ibn ʿĀshūr describes a reciprocal pledge under God's oversight and cites earlier discussion at 2:27; Study Quran compares mīthāq and mīʿād as relational Quranic terms. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 23:8/q07 How do trust and covenant relate, and why mention both?  [لِأَمَٰنَٰتِهِمْ وَعَهْدِهِمْ · rhetoric]
- 23:8/q07/p1 Trust is broader; every covenant is a trust, but some trusts need no earlier covenant. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya states the distinction while saying both equally count as God's charge. Al-Qurṭubī agrees. Al-Biqāʿī reports that the greatest trust, covenant, is singled out after the general term. Al-Sāmarrāʾī reports al-Fatḥ al-Qadīr and says breadth helps explain why trusts lead; Ibn ʿĀshūr says a covenant maker entrusts fulfillment to the other party. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(فتح القدير)]

## 23:8/q08 What does rāʿūn mean?  [رَٰعُونَ · meaning]
- 23:8/q08/p1 They actively preserve, tend, repair and fulfill what is in their care. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq through al-Wāḥidī derive it from responsible care that sets an entrusted matter right, exemplified by a shepherd's flock, ruler's subjects and human relations. Ibn al-Jawzī reports al-Zajjāj; al-Nasafī, al-Rāzī and al-Bursawī add safeguarding. Al-Ālūsī traces care of livestock through feeding and defense before broader preservation. Ibn ʿĀshūr includes preventing loss, repairing decay and metaphorical good treatment of people. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī accepts preservation but finds derivation of the animal-tending sense from general care more plausible. Al-Sāmarrāʾī reports al-Kashshāf on improving entrusted things. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الكشاف)]

## 23:8/q09 Why use rāʿūn rather than a simple verb of guarding?  [رَٰعُونَ · rhetoric]
- 23:8/q09/p1 Its active, continuing stewardship includes preserving, managing and checking entrusted things. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says raʿā adds ongoing pastoral care beyond ḥifẓ and that the agent noun can convey an owner or responsible manager, unlike yarʿawn. Al-Kashshāf as reported by him compares shepherd and governor. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes returning a trust to its owner from keeping a covenant in view whenever relevant. [4 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الكشاف); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:8/q10 How are trusts and covenants guarded in practice?  [لِأَمَٰنَٰتِهِمْ وَعَهْدِهِمْ رَٰعُونَ · interpretation]
- 23:8/q10/p1 Believers preserve trusts, deliver them to their owners, and fulfill covenants and promises without betrayal. — Muqātil, Yaḥyā, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Iṣlāḥī state this practical duty. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies faithful return and covenant keeping as two virtues, explaining that a trust is preserved until delivered and a covenant kept in view; al-Biqāʿī adds repair. Al-Rāzī includes commitments made by speech or deed and words of emancipation or marriage prohibition. Al-Wāḥidī names oaths; al-Durr reports Saʿīd ibn Jubayr. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says faith makes acceptance of a trust or pledge stable against wavering into treachery; al-Bursawī emphasizes sound protection. [24 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 23:8/q11 What does the fronted phrase with lām contribute?  [لِأَمَٰنَٰتِهِمْ وَعَهْدِهِمْ رَٰعُونَ · rhetoric]
- 23:8/q11/p1 It foregrounds the trusts and covenants and stresses their special claim on the guardians. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says fronting marks their importance and worthiness of care; the lām strengthens exclusive association and the guardians' stewardship. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the fronted phrase and repeated relative pronoun match the pattern of preceding qualities. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 23:8/q12 What does the verse imply about the scope of required duties?  [* · interpretation]
- 23:8/q12/p1 Its trusts and covenants comprehensively cover religious, moral, legal and social duties. — Jishumī says worship, contracts and liabilities are included, while sins fall under avoidance of laghw. Al-Rāzī says fasting, pilgrimage and purity need not be separately enumerated because these terms and preserving prayer cover them. Iṣlāḥī says the detail appears across the Qur'an and every sharīʿah honors these duties. Al-Sāmarrāʾī cites 33:72 for the trust and reports al-Suddī's view that faith accepts all rights of religious law. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says basic needs and laws close to nature demand priority and neglect causes greater harm than luxury neglected. [7 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(السدي); TABATABAI]

## 23:8/q13 What moral or spiritual weight does keeping them have?  [* · interpretation]
- 23:8/q13/p1 Faithful keeping marks believers and their success; betrayal marks failure or hypocrisy. — Jishumī says the listed traits identify a believer without ambiguity. Al-Rāzī counts this trait toward success and reports Ibn Masʿūd's saying that trust is religion's first loss and prayer its last. Ibn Kathīr reports the Prophet's three signs of a hypocrite: lying, breaking promises and betraying trust. Al-Sāmarrāʾī quotes the Prophet that faith itself is a trust and a person without trust has no religion or faith. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says living faith produces conduct and success, and fidelity to pledges is steady because of faith. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls return of privately held valuables a branch of faith; covenant keeping shows nobility and resolve against greed, and 17:34 says the covenant will be called to account. Iṣlāḥī describes faithfulness to God and people without rebellion or betrayal. [11 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 23:8/q14 What report connects the opening ten verses with Paradise?  [* · reports]
- 23:8/q14/p1 ʿUmar reports that whoever upholds the first ten verses enters Paradise. — Al-Wāḥidī includes ʿUmar's description of a bee-like sound accompanying revelation and the Prophet's supplication before the report. [1 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB(عمر بن الخطاب)]

## 23:8/q15 What Prophetic reports illustrate trust and betrayal?  [لِأَمَٰنَٰتِهِمْ · reports]
turns on: The notes give distinct sayings with different illustrations, not competing accounts of one event.
- 23:8/q15/p1 A hypocrite lies when speaking, breaks promises and betrays trusts. — Ibn Kathīr reports this three-trait Prophetic saying. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله)]
- 23:8/q15/p2 Incomplete prayer is described as the greatest betrayal. — Al-Rāzī reports the Prophetic saying while discussing hidden aspects of worship as trusts. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(رسول الله)]
- 23:8/q15/p3 A woman's womb is a trust. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports the Prophet's statement in connection with 2:228 and singular amāna used for many instances. [1 notes: TABRISI(النبي)]
- 23:8/q15/p4 Faith is a trust, and without trust there is no religion or faith. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī quotes the Prophet in support of the moral centrality of trust. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 23:8/q16 What does the report of trust being withdrawn say, and how is it assessed?  [لِأَمَٰنَٰتِهِمْ · reports]
turns on: The first account has substantive content but no stated grading; the second is a grading whose report content is absent.
- 23:8/q16/p1 Trust was planted in hearts, taught through revelation, then gradually withdrawn until reliable trustees became scarce. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Ḥudhayfa's account from the Prophet: withdrawal occurs during sleep, apparent competence no longer signals faith, and people rarely return trusts. No grading is given. He explains its mustard-seed measure as trust others put in a person rather than internal faith, so only a gullible person would entrust him. Separately, Ibn Kathīr calls an immediately preceding report gharīb with an unidentified narrator, but the note gives no content by which to identify that report. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(حذيفة بن اليمان)]
- 23:8/q16/p2 An immediately preceding, unspecified report is gharīb and has an unidentified narrator. — Ibn Kathīr supplies this grading without the report's content in the note; it cannot be equated with Ḥudhayfa's report on the present evidence. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 23:8/q17 What do the unusual image words in the withdrawn-trust report mean?  [لِأَمَٰنَٰتِهِمْ · meaning]
- 23:8/q17/p1 Wakt is a dark mark on a date skin; majal is a raised swelling on thin skin. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares majal to grape skin and says fire or repeated handwork can produce it. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:8/q18 How does this quality connect to the neighboring traits and the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes trace several different contextual links: the opening virtue list, immediately adjacent duties, prayer, and the sūra sequence.
- 23:8/q18/p1 It contributes to the opening portrait of active faith leading to lasting success. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the opening traits make faith living and effective; al-Rāzī counts fidelity toward success. Jishumī says the virtues identify the believer. Iṣlāḥī traces a progression through prayer, zakāh, restraint, fidelity and guarded prayer; Study Quran says the sūra opens with believers' virtues and actions. Asad connects keeping trusts and pledges with prayer protected from worldly intent. Iṣlāḥī elsewhere summarizes success through humble prayer, avoidance of frivolity, zakāh, chastity, trustworthiness and promises, citing 23:1. [7 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 23:8/q18/p2 It follows zakāh because wealth given is God's trust, and it relates to guarded chastity. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls zakāh God's right, wealth's right and the poor's right. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says chastity and trusts both require guarding and failure in either is blameworthy and transgressive. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 23:8/q18/p3 Prayer renews and protects the believer's covenants. — Iṣlāḥī calls prayer a covenant with the Lord renewed five times daily, reminding believers of other promises and responsibilities. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 23:8/q18/p4 The next sūra extends the picture of faith's effects in personal and collective life. — Iṣlāḥī says al-Nūr supplements al-Muʾminūn: faith lights inward and communal life, and its absence darkens both. Study Quran notes a wider sūra pattern of affluent, powerful opponents to prophets, also elsewhere in the Qur'an at 7:59–62 and 7:136. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 23:8/q19 Where else does this verse or its theme recur?  [* · links]
- 23:8/q19/p1 A nearly identical trust-and-covenant verse occurs at 70:32. — Study Quran identifies the parallel; Jishumī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Samīn and Ibn al-Jazarī discuss the singular or plural reading in both places. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says both sūras repeat chastity, its exceptions, and keeping trusts and covenants.
