# 23:71: 18 questions

## 23:71/q01 Who or what is al-ḥaqq here?  [ٱلْحَقُّ · referent]
turns on: Whether al-ḥaqq names God, the revealed message, or truth and reality itself; Ibn ʿAṭiyya objects to the divine referent, while al-Ālūsī defends its contextual fit.
- 23:71/q01/p1 God Himself. — Reported by Abū Ṣāliḥ, Ibn Jurayj, al-Suddī, Mujāhid, Qatāda and others; al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī call this the majority view. Al-Nasafī cites 21:22. Al-Ālūsī says legislating shirk would be a divine deficiency, rationally excluded according to al-Khafājī, and defends the contextual fit. Ibn ʿAṭiyya objects that God has permitted unbelief to continue without cosmic collapse, so the verb and consequence are difficult. [30 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(بعضهم); ABDURRAZZAQ(الكلبي); TAB-FULL(أبو صالح,ابن جريج); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الأكثرون); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو صالح، ابن جريج، مقاتل، السدي، الكلبي); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن جريج ومقاتل والسدي وجماعة,قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جريج وأبو صالح); TABRISI(أبو صالح، ابن جريج، السدي); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد وابن جريج والسدي); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس,مجاهد وابن جريج وأبو صالح); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد، وأبو صالح والسدي); DURR-FULL(أبو صالح); ALUSI-FULL(أبو صالح,الخفاجي); STUDYQURAN(Q); MUQATIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 23:71/q01/p2 The Qur’an, revelation, Islam, or the truth brought by Muḥammad. — Al-Zajjāj connects revelation to 23:70; al-Farrāʾ and al-Zajjāj are cited by al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, and Ibn al-Jawzī. Al-Wāḥidī says Qur’an calls to good whereas desires call to evil. Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers what Muhammad brought; al-Ālūsī prefers this contextual sense, with figurative attribution of following. Al-Burṣawī includes Islam, oneness and justice; Kur'an Yolu also includes revealed commands and prohibitions. [16 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء، الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الفراء والزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء والزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(غيرهما)+]
- 23:71/q01/p3 Objective truth, right order, or the reality of creation. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says what is right and sound best fits the verse; al-Ṭabarī says heavens and earth stand by truth. Ibn ʿĀshūr connects it to 23:70 and distinguishes existential facts, such as oneness and resurrection, from moral truths, such as the wrongness of infanticide, murder and gambling. Ṭabāṭabāʾī grounds truth in the cosmic order and fitrah (30:30); Asad takes all created reality; Kur'an Yolu includes universal law and balance. [27 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q,commentators); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(غيرهما)+]
- 23:71/q01/p4 Unity and justice as inward realities. — Al-Qāshānī calls truth the summons to Essence and attributes: unity sustains immaterial reality and its shadow, justice, orders the multiplicity of heaven and earth. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 23:71/q02 What does following their desires mean in this condition?  [ٱتَّبَعَ أَهْوَآءَهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes variously construe following as divine action or legislation, conformity of reality or revelation, or the Prophet's hypothetical response.
- 23:71/q02/p1 Truth or reality would agree with their wishes, especially their wish for shirk. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls following a familiar metaphor for agreement; al-Qurṭubī also gives metaphorical agreement, including impunity through supposed divine incapacity or ignorance. Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers this over God as subject. The Study Quran reports this wording; al-Ālūsī distinguishes truth in general from truth about divinity. [18 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Q,commentators); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(غيرهما)+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 23:71/q02/p2 God would act or legislate according to their desire for partners, offspring, shirk or sins. — Muqātil and al-Kalbī describe God taking a partner; al-Baghawī adds a son; al-Zamakhsharī describes commanding shirk and sin. Ibn Kathīr points to conflicting human opinions and God's perfect governance. Al-Ālūsī says this is impossible but contextually apt. [22 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(الكلبي); TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو صالح، ابن جريج، مقاتل، السدي، الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن جريج ومقاتل والسدي وجماعة,قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جريج وأبو صالح); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد وابن جريج والسدي); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس,مجاهد وابن جريج وأبو صالح); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد، وأبو صالح والسدي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو صالح,الخفاجي); STUDYQURAN(Q); IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 23:71/q02/p3 The Qur’an or revelation would descend endorsing their wishes. — Their wishes include partners, offspring and other deities, according to al-Wāḥidī and al-Baghawī; al-Wāḥidī says this would reverse its call to good. Al-Ālūsī also paraphrases it as the Prophet bringing shirk rather than his revealed message. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء، الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الفراء والزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء والزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 23:71/q02/p4 Their desires concern what they wish for. — Al-Māwardī offers this as one scope of ahwāʾihim, alongside their worship. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 23:71/q02/p5 Their desires concern what they worship. — Al-Māwardī offers this as the other scope of ahwāʾihim. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 23:71/q03 Whose desires are meant, and what counts as desire?  [أَهْوَآءَهُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word is limited to appetites or includes corrupt choices and wishes contrary to nature.
- 23:71/q03/p1 The desires of the Meccan polytheists and unbelievers. — Yaḥyā explicitly identifies the polytheists; Muqātil and al-Ṭabarī identify Meccan unbelievers or polytheists. Their particular wishes include partners, offspring, idolatry and denial of Muḥammad. [7 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 23:71/q03/p2 Hawā is desire for one's own purpose, especially an unwarranted or corrupt appetite. — Ibn ʿĀshūr excludes ordinary hunger and thirst as such; examples are injustice, humiliation, stolen food and illicit sex. Islahi says human desire and truth make incompatible demands. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 23:71/q04 Why would the heavens and earth be corrupted?  [لَفَسَدَتِ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتُ وَٱلْأَرْضُ · interpretation]
turns on: The causal account ranges from impossible multiple gods, through conflicting whims and moral disorder, to immediate destruction or loss of divinity.
- 23:71/q04/p1 Multiple gods or endorsing shirk would undo cosmic governance. — Muqātil, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī invoke 21:22; Islahi invokes 23:91, where each god takes its creation and attacks another. Al-Biqāʿī describes mutual obstruction. Al-Ālūsī reports the objection that Quraysh's shirk in worship did not imply rival lordship, citing 31:25. [23 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(الكلبي); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو صالح، ابن جريج، مقاتل، السدي، الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الفراء والزجاج,قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو صالح، ابن جريج، السدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء والزجاج,مجاهد وابن جريج والسدي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 23:71/q04/p2 Conflicting, vicious or unstable desires would destroy the true order that sustains creation. — Al-Ṭabarī says people lack foresight and favor falsehood; al-Qaffāl says their opinions contradict each other. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī say proofs, justified conclusions and trust in promises and threats would fail. Ibn Kathīr cites their disagreements, comparing God's perfect attributes and rule (43:31–32, 17:100, 4:53). Ibn ʿĀshūr includes moral wrongs such as infanticide, killing and gambling. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says fitrah, cosmic causes and relations would have to turn arbitrary. Kur'an Yolu gives the example that proper work succeeds even for an unbeliever, whereas poor work can fail even for a believer. Islahi lists vice, injustice and denial of Judgment. Al-Ālūsī dismisses one contradictory-desires formulation. [34 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL(القفال); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد، وأبو صالح والسدي); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(commentators); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 23:71/q04/p3 God would bring the Resurrection and end the world in anger if Muhammad's truth became shirk. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī offer this consequence; al-Ālūsī calls the supposition that revelation could be changed impossible. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 23:71/q04/p4 God commanding shirk and sin would cease to be God and could not sustain the cosmos. — Al-Zamakhsharī's explanation is repeated by Abū Ḥayyān; al-Bayḍāwī calls it a Muʿtazilite principle. Al-Khafājī, reported by al-Ālūsī, says reason establishes that such legislation is a divine deficiency. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(المعتزلة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو صالح,الخفاجي)]
- 23:71/q04/p5 A purposeless, meaningless universe such as deniers imagine could not endure. — Asad says the universe, especially human life, would perish in chaos under this assumption. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 23:71/q04/p6 The heavens' and earth's governance would be corrupted. — Al-Māwardī contrasts governance by truth with governance by whim; Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds that creation and command, existence and governance, are inseparable. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 23:71/q04/p7 Their conditions would be corrupted. — Al-Māwardī says cosmic conditions proceed by wisdom, not whim. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 23:71/q05 Who are those 'within them,' and how would they be affected?  [وَمَن فِيهِنَّ · referent]
turns on: Whether man singles out rational inhabitants or includes creatures between heaven and earth, including animals and inanimate things.
- 23:71/q05/p1 Rational inhabitants: angels in heaven, humans on earth, and sometimes jinn. — Al-Māwardī names angels and humans; al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Burṣawī include jinn. Al-Qurṭubī and al-Nasafī say nonrational things follow rational beings. Angelic corruption would be worshipping servants as lords; humans are ruined by desire or idolatry; jinn by being obeyed and becoming tyrannical. Ibn ʿĀshūr says inhabitants of heaven and earth. [13 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:71/q05/p2 All creatures between heaven and earth, including irrational animals and inanimate things. — Al-Kalbī gives this scope, supported by Ibn Masʿūd's reading with 'and what is between them.' Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī say nonrational things are affected through rational beings' management. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود,الكلبي); TABRISI(الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 23:71/q06 How is the wāw in 'walaw' read?  [وَلَوِ · readings]
turns on: The vowel is kasra in the common reading and ḍamma in the reported variant.
- 23:71/q06/p1 With kasra on wāw, the usual reading. — Al-Samīn says kasra resolves the meeting of two vowelless letters; Ibn Jinnī also calls it usual. [2 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(يحيى); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور)]
- 23:71/q06/p2 With ḍamma on wāw. — Yaḥyā or Ibn Waththāb is reported for this rare reading. Ibn Jinnī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Samīn explain analogy with the plural pronoun wāw, as in ishtaraw al-ḍalālah (2:16); Ibn Jinnī notes converse analogies too. [5 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(يحيى); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن وثاب); SAMIN-DURR(ابن وثاب); ALUSI-FULL(ابن وثاب)]

## 23:71/q07 How does the impossible condition connect to the preceding rebuke?  [وَلَوِ ٱتَّبَعَ ٱلْحَقُّ أَهْوَآءَهُمْ · links]
- 23:71/q07/p1 It follows the statement that most hate the truth and exposes what their wishes would entail. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links it to 23:70 and says it intensifies rebuke. Al-Biqāʿī says dropping hatred would lead them to love truth, follow it and reform. Al-Ālūsī defends the God-as-truth reading in this context: their hatred cannot change revelation. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:71/q08 How is the conditional clause joined syntactically?  [وَلَوِ ٱتَّبَعَ ٱلْحَقُّ أَهْوَآءَهُمْ · grammar]
- 23:71/q08/p1 It can be circumstantial after 'hating' or coordinated with an implied condition and result. — Al-Biqāʿī records both analyses and attributes discussion of the circumstantial construction to Shaykh Saʿd al-Dīn. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 23:71/q09 How is 'we brought them' read?  [أَتَيْنَٰهُم · readings]
turns on: The reported forms differ in person, addressee and vowel length.
- 23:71/q09/p1 Ataynāhum, first-person plural: 'We brought them.' — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Samīn call it the majority reading. Al-Samīn says God speaks and means His messengers brought it; Abū Ḥayyān reports the plural among the variants. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور، ابن أبي إسحاق، عيسى بن عمرو، يونس عن أبي عمرو); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور)]
- 23:71/q09/p2 Ataytuhum, first-person singular: 'I brought them.' — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī report ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar, Ibn Abī Isḥāq and sometimes Abū ʿAmr or Yūnus transmitting it; accounts vary. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عيسى بن عمر وابن أبي إسحاق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور، ابن أبي إسحاق، عيسى بن عمرو، يونس عن أبي عمرو); SAMIN-DURR(ابن أبي إسحاق وعيسى بن عمر وأبو عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق وعيسى بن عمر ويونس عن أبي عمرو)]
- 23:71/q09/p3 Ataytahum, second-person singular: 'You brought them,' addressing Muḥammad. — Ibn Abī Isḥāq, ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar and other named readers are reported by Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī; the named lists vary. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عيسى بن عمر وابن أبي إسحاق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق، عيسى بن عمرو، أبو البر هثيم، أبو حيوة، الجحدري، ابن قطيب، أبو رجاء); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البرهسم وأبو حيوة والجحدري وأبو رجاء); ALUSI-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق وعيسى بن عمر ووهب حيوة والجحدري وابن قطيب وأبو رجاء,ابن أبي إسحاق وعيسى بن عمر ويونس عن أبي عمرو)]
- 23:71/q09/p4 Ātaynāhum, with long initial ā: 'We gave them.' — A report from Abū ʿAmr is cited by Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī. Al-Samīn says its second object may be omitted or bidhikrihim with extra bāʾ; al-Ālūsī says this reading avoids ellipsis or figurative coming. Ibn Mujāhid lists many and some unnamed transmitters of Abū ʿAmr's readings without specifying this form in these notes. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عمرو); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(أبو عمرو); IBNMUJAHID]

## 23:71/q10 How does God's 'bringing' the reminder work?  [أَتَيْنَٰهُم · grammar]
- 23:71/q10/p1 Coming is figurative: God's Book or messenger came to them. — Abū Ḥayyān says literal coming attributed to God is unsuitable. Al-Samīn explains the common plural as God speaking of what His messengers brought. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور)]
- 23:71/q10/p2 An omitted genitive can be supplied, or bāʾ can mean accompaniment. — Al-Ālūsī records this analysis on the majority reading; the long-ā 'We gave' reading avoids the issue. Al-Samīn adds the possible second-object construction for 'We gave.' [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو عمرو,قتادة); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو)]

## 23:71/q11 What is 'their reminder'?  [بِذِكْرِهِمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether dhikr stresses honor, admonition and guidance, clarification, duties and recompense, or the book they once wished to receive.
- 23:71/q11/p1 The Qur’an as their honor, renown and distinction. — Al-Suddī and Sufyān link it to the messenger from their people and Qur’an in their language; 21:10 and 43:44 are repeatedly cited. Al-Ṭabarī says it is both clarification and honor. Al-Rāzī says messenger and proofs support one another. Al-Ālūsī and Kur'an Yolu prefer honor; the latter describes later Arab and wider Islamic honor. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī concedes the Qur’an honors several communities but prefers another sense of 'their.' [40 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي,سفيان الثوري); FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(آخرون); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(السدي وسفيان); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(السدي وسفيان); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد,التأويلات النجمية); TABATABAI(كثير منهم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; TABATABAI-]
- 23:71/q11/p2 The Qur’an as admonition, guidance or reminder of God and true religion. — Ḥasan and Qatāda say it teaches duties and prohibitions; al-Biqāʿī says it repairs ignorance and conduct. Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 21:50 and 43:44, and prefers remembrance of God's religion of belief and good works over the simpler reminder of God, given context. Asad refers to 21:10. Al-Ālūsī says rejecting admonition is serious because it teaches self-reform, but calls the contrary objection strained. [27 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî ve Kurtubî); STUDYQURAN(R); DURR-FULL(قتادة); TABATABAI+]
- 23:71/q11/p3 Clarification of truth and needed religious matters. — Ibn ʿAbbās supplies this gloss in al-Ṭabarī, al-Māwardī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī; al-Ṭabarī says it converges with honor because the Qur’an gives both. Abū Ḥayyān reports the majority reading and Ibn ʿAbbās's clarification gloss. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور، ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî ve Kurtubî)]
- 23:71/q11/p4 A reminder of duties and promised reward or punishment. — Al-Māwardī offers duties and reward; al-Qurṭubī reports Qatāda on reward and punishment. Yaḥyā reports Ḥasan and Qatāda on what to do and avoid, lawful and forbidden. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 23:71/q11/p5 The scripture or reminder they had earlier wished for. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī recall their claim in 37:168–69 that if they had a reminder from earlier peoples they would be sincere servants. Al-Ālūsī favors this alongside Qur’an-as-honor because rejection is especially blameworthy. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 23:71/q11/p6 This community's own religion, conveyed by the final Qur’anic revelation. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the true call differs in detail across peoples and this final revelation is the last stage of law; 'their' marks the religion proper to them, rather than an honorific possessive. [3 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]

## 23:71/q12 How is 'their reminder' read?  [بِذِكْرِهِمْ · readings]
turns on: The variants change dhikrihim to dhikrāhum or to a first-person verb.
- 23:71/q12/p1 Bi-dhikrihim, the common reading. — Abū Ḥayyān reports the majority and cites Ibn ʿAbbās for admonition and clarification. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور، ابن عباس)]
- 23:71/q12/p2 Bi-dhikrāhum or dhikrāhum, with long feminine ā. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and al-Bayḍāwī record it; Ibn al-Jawzī names Ibn Masʿūd, Ubayy, Abū Rajāʾ and Abū al-Jawzāʾ for both occurrences. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn name ʿĪsā; al-Ālūsī says it supports Ibn ʿAbbās's admonition gloss. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود وأبي بن كعب وأبو رجاء وأبو الجوزاء); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عيسى، قتادة); SAMIN-DURR(عيسى); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 23:71/q12/p3 Nudhakkiruhum, a first-person plural intensive imperfect: 'We remind them.' — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī attribute it to Qatāda; al-Samīn says it is a circumstantial clause replacing the prepositional phrase. Al-Ālūsī's note writes the form as nadhkuruhum. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عيسى، قتادة); SAMIN-DURR(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)]

## 23:71/q13 Why call the reminder 'theirs'?  [بِذِكْرِهِمْ ذِكْرِهِم · rhetoric]
turns on: The possessive may mark their honor or their particular religious dispensation.
- 23:71/q13/p1 It marks honor that belongs to them through their messenger and language. — Al-Suddī and Sufyān cite the local messenger and Arabic Qur’an; 43:44 supplies a parallel. Al-Ālūsī and Kur'an Yolu emphasize the rebuke of rejecting one's own dignity. [11 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي,سفيان الثوري); TAB-FULL(آخرون); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي وسفيان); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد); TABATABAI(كثير منهم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 23:71/q13/p2 It marks the true religion specifically addressed to this community. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues the final revelation gives the community its own detailed stage of law; he rejects honor as the primary force of the possessive here. [2 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]

## 23:71/q14 Why repeat 'their reminder' and attach fāʾ to their turning away?  [فَهُمْ عَن ذِكْرِهِم مُّعْرِضُونَ · rhetoric]
- 23:71/q14/p1 The repeated noun sharpens the rebuke, and fāʾ makes their refusal follow their receipt of the reminder. — Al-Ālūsī explains that an explicit dhikrihim in place of a pronoun intensifies the reproach. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 23:71/q15 Why does the wording move from 'truth' to 'reminder'?  [ٱلْحَقُّ بِذِكْرِهِمْ · rhetoric]
- 23:71/q15/p1 Truth suits the denial context; reminder suits God's act of giving, while the first-person plural honors the Prophet. — Al-Ālūsī offers this if the earlier haqq means the Qur’an, describing the shift from a Prophet pronoun to majestic 'We.' Ṭabāṭabāʾī says 'reminder' also counters the charge that its bearer was mad (15:6; 23:70). [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 23:71/q16 What are they turning away from, and what is the force of the rebuke?  [فَهُمْ عَن ذِكْرِهِم مُّعْرِضُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: The object is explained as the Qur’an, clarification or their own honor and religion; some notes stress their deliberate choice and its loss.
- 23:71/q16/p1 They reject the Qur’an and its guidance. — Muqātil says they refuse belief in it; Qatāda repeatedly identifies it as Qur’an. Yaḥyā says they turn from God's explanation. Al-Nasafī and al-Burṣawī stress their bad choice, the latter saying they forsake present reform and future honor. Islahi warns of earlier nations' fate. [19 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES]
- 23:71/q16/p2 They turn from their own honor and dignity. — Al-Suddī says both mentions mean honor. Al-Wāḥidī says it would honor them in this world and the next; al-Ṭabrisī says they choose humiliation; al-Biqāʿī says their rejection hurts them, not God. Al-Irshād, cited by al-Burṣawī, says the rejected thing especially merited attention. [12 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 23:71/q16/p3 They turn from clarified truth and the religion proper to them. — Yaḥyā says God explained it; Ibn ʿAbbās is repeatedly cited for clarification. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the religion proper to this community is rejected. [7 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI]

## 23:71/q17 What does the verse teach about following truth and desire?  [* · interpretation]
- 23:71/q17/p1 People must follow evidence and truth rather than reshape it to their desires. — Jishumī says a rational person follows evidence; al-Rāzī says abandon desire. Al-Qurṭubī says people must submit to truth; Islahi says salvation requires it even when unwelcome. Al-Biqāʿī says love and following truth reform the person. [5 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 23:71/q17/p2 It proves divine oneness by showing the ruin that competing gods entail. — Jishumī identifies the inference and calls it argument from consequences. Al-Wāḥidī cites 21:22; Islahi invokes 23:91. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 23:71/q17/p3 God's objective laws govern nature and human conduct, and religion follows fitrah. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects the human goal of happiness to cosmic and human nature and 30:30. Kur'an Yolu says objective laws do not bend to believers or unbelievers; sound action, right and justice depend on them. [7 notes: TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 23:71/q17/p4 The true path inwardly produces unity, justice, love and witnessing. — Al-Qāshānī says those veiled by sensation and multiplicity remain in injustice and hostility. Al-Sulamī reports al-Wāsiṭī: after God disclosed realities, means, calm and insight, creatures witnessed the Real through the Real and renounced their own will. [4 notes: QASHANI; SULAMI(الواسطي)]
- 23:71/q17/p5 One must oppose the ego to remain in servitude. — Al-Sulamī's unnamed authority says God's command to resist the self keeps one from abandoning obedience; al-Burṣawī repeats and adds that ungoverned desire leads to Hell. Al-Burṣawī also contrasts reluctant submission from fear or reward with willing submission through knowledge and love of the giver. [3 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
- 23:71/q17/p6 Reflecting on the Qur’an purifies faith and leads to certainty and witnessing. — Al-Burṣawī cites 10:32 for truth versus error; Jishumī calls the Qur’an a remembrance for reflection. [2 notes: BURSEVI; JISHUMI]

## 23:71/q18 How does this verse link with its surrounding passage and the sūra?  [* · links]
- 23:71/q18/p1 The condition intensifies the preceding charge that they dislike truth, and the reminder rebukes their neglect. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links 23:70; al-Burṣawī and al-Ālūsī describe the shift from dislike of truth to rejection of a benefit they should welcome. Islahi connects the reminder to 23:69 and calls it conclusive communication, not a tale. Al-Bayḍāwī says 23:70–73 remove excuses for denial, leaving hatred or lack of discernment. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 23:71/q18/p2 The sūra exposes affluent opponents' contradiction and reassures the Prophet. — Study Quran notes affluent, powerful opposition to prophets (7:59–62; 7:136); Islahi says the disbelievers affirm truth then deny it, and the Prophet should hold his right path without despair. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 23:71/q18/p3 The nearby kharj discussion concerns Quraysh's unbelievers. — Al-Durr reports Mujāhid on the prior account; al-Samīn says the kharj/kharāj discussion was treated earlier at 18:94. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(مجاهد); SAMIN-DURR]
