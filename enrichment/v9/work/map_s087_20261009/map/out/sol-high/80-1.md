# 80:1: 26 questions

## 80:1/q01 What does ʿabasa mean?  [عَبَسَ · meaning]
- 80:1/q01/p1 He frowned, grimaced, or contracted and darkened his face in displeasure. — Thaʿlabī, Jishumī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurṭubī, Elmalılı, and others describe facial contraction or a sour expression; Jishumī compares basara and taqṭīb and derives ʿAbbās from the root. Elmalılı compares Turkish ekşime to tasting something sour or bitter and notes that a named face can be omitted; the Kāmûs commentator also allows a transitive use. Elmalılı says nominative wajh gives 'his face frowned,' while accusative wajh gives 'he frowned his face.' [15 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI(Kāmûs şârihi); TABATABAI]

## 80:1/q02 How is ʿabasa read and inflected?  [عَبَسَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the bāʾ is light or doubled; the doubled form intensifies the action.
- 80:1/q02/p1 The usual reading has a light consonant. — Abū Ḥayyān attributes the light bāʾ to the majority; Jishumī calls ʿabasa a past verb. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور؛ زيد بن علي، الحسن، أبو عمران الجوني، عيسى); JISHUMI]
- 80:1/q02/p2 A reading doubles the consonant to intensify the frown. — Naḥḥās, Kashshāf, Rāzī, and Bayḍāwī explain the doubled form as intensive; Rāzī compares doubled kallaḥa. Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn attribute it to Zayd ibn ʿAlī. [6 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور؛ زيد بن علي، الحسن، أبو عمران الجوني، عيسى); SAMIN-DURR(زيد بن علي)]

## 80:1/q03 Who frowned and turned away?  [عَبَسَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ · referent]
turns on: Whether the unnamed third-person subject is identified by the occasion reports and subsequent address, or by the grammar and the Prophet's established character.
- 80:1/q03/p1 The subject is the Prophet Muhammad. — Farrāʾ, Wāḥidī, Māwardī, Nasafī, and others identify him; Rāzī reports exegetical agreement, and Iṣlāḥī says later direct address identifies the unstated subject. Sunni and some Shiʿi reports in Ṭabāṭabāʾī support this identification. [11 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI(طرق أهل السنة,طرق الشيعة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; JISHUMI(المرتضى)-; TABRISI(المرتضى علم الهدى)-]
- 80:1/q03/p2 The subject is another man, specifically an Umayyad in some reports. — Al-Ṣādiq's report in Ṭabrisī and Shiʿi reports in Ṭabāṭabāʾī name an Umayyad man with the Prophet; Burhān reports Umayya. Jishumī and al-Murtaḍā argue that the third person does not name the Prophet and that such conduct conflicts with his treatment of believers; Ṭabrisī cites 68:4 and 3:159 for his character. [7 notes: JISHUMI(المرتضى); TABRISI(الصادق,المرتضى علم الهدى); TABATABAI(بعض روايات الشيعة); BURHAN(بعضهم); JISHUMI+]
- 80:1/q03/p3 The third-person wording addresses the public without fixing an individual subject. — Burhān gives this as the author's resolution, consonant with revealed laws addressed generally. [1 notes: BURHAN+]

## 80:1/q04 Why did the frown occur in the reported encounter?  [عَبَسَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the relevant cause was an interrupted conversion effort, a preference for social rank, or the seeker's breach of etiquette.
- 80:1/q04/p1 The interruption threatened the Prophet's effort to convert influential leaders and their followers. — Farrāʾ says he disliked interruption; Thaʿlabī, Bagḥawī, Qurṭubī, Mujāhid, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī say he feared chiefs would disparage followers who were blind, poor, or enslaved. Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr stress the hoped-for conversion of many followers; Iṣlāḥī says poverty or blindness themselves did not cause the displeasure and adds concern that leaders might disrespect his companion. Qurṭubī transmits Ibn Zayd's account of the seeker pushing past a guide; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the leaders might disperse. [17 notes: FARRA; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some narratives)]
- 80:1/q04/p2 The deeper cause may have been an inward preference for wealth and status. — Rāzī offers this as an alternative to criticism of the outward act: kinship and rank drew the Prophet toward the nobles, while the seeker lacked them. Qāshānī reads the attention to wealthy leaders and slighting of the poor man's faith as a veil of outward status. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; QASHANI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 80:1/q04/p3 The blind man's discourteous interruption warranted displeasure. — Rāzī voices the objection that the man heard the discussion, disrupted a greater missionary good although already Muslim, and compares the censure of untimely calls in 49:4. Ṭabrisī reports the view that he deserved to be ignored, though God rebuked the response for its possible appearance of class preference. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABRISI]

## 80:1/q05 What does tawallā mean?  [وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ · meaning]
- 80:1/q05/p1 He turned away, especially turning his face from the seeker. — Thaʿlabī, Jishumī, Bagḥawī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and Qurṭubī specify the face. Elmalılı includes refusing or facing another direction. Jishumī identifies it as a past verb. [13 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]

## 80:1/q06 How does the wording specify the cause and object of turning away?  [عَبَسَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ · grammar]
turns on: Which of the two earlier verbs governs an jāʾahu in the following verse.
- 80:1/q06/p1 The coming of the blind man supplies the cause, and the omitted object of tawallā is that man. — Jishumī glosses an jāʾahu as 'because he came.' Ibn ʿĀshūr says the context makes the omitted object clear: the person whose arrival occasioned the turning. [2 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 80:1/q06/p2 The later causal phrase is governed by tawallā. — Kashshāf and Rāzī give the nearer verb as one possible governor of the accusative an jāʾahu. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 80:1/q06/p3 The later causal phrase is governed by ʿabasa. — Kashshāf and Rāzī give the farther verb as another possible governor, depending on grammatical rules about which verb acts. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL]

## 80:1/q07 Is the following arrival phrase read as a question?  [عَبَسَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ · readings]
- 80:1/q07/p1 A variant begins it with an interrogative hamza after a pause, making the rebuke explicit. — Kashshāf and Rāzī describe two hamzas with an intervening alif after pausing at 'he frowned and turned away'; Abū Ḥayyān reports interrogative readings with lengthening or two hamzas. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور؛ زيد بن علي، الحسن، أبو عمران الجوني، عيسى)]

## 80:1/q08 Why does the verse begin in the third person?  [عَبَسَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether indirect opening primarily tempers the address, heightens reproof, or delays identifying the actor.
- 80:1/q08/p1 Indirect speech softens and dignifies the Prophet before direct address. — Jishumī says 'you frowned' would be harsher; Qushayrī says the address is tactful. Qurṭubī, Bursevī via Suhaylī, Biqāʿī, Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and a view reported by Ṭabāṭabāʾī stress honor, reassurance, or gradual rebuke. Biqāʿī compares Saʿd's indirect address; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 9:43 and cites ʿIyāḍ's report of pardon before reproach. [10 notes: QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(السهيلي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(عياض ناقلا عن عون بن عبد الله والسمرقندي); TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 80:1/q08/p2 The move from third person to direct address sharpens the censure. — Kashshāf and Rāzī compare it to a public complaint followed by confrontation; Ibn ʿAṭiyya sees disregard in third-person speech. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says direct address intensifies accountability; Asad says third-person forms especially sharpen the rebuke; Elmalılı says the shift exposes inward meaning. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 80:1/q08/p3 The unnamed subject creates suspense until later details identify him. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the two verbs lack an antecedent and later references clarify the event. Iṣlāḥī says readers wonder who is meant; the later 'you know' and direct address identify the Prophet, while avoiding an initial impression that the issue was simple rudeness. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 80:1/q09 Who was the blind visitor?  [* · reports]
- 80:1/q09/p1 He was Ibn Umm Maktūm, identified as ʿAbd Allāh in most accounts. — Māwardī transmits Qatāda's 'ʿAbd Allāh ibn Zāʾida of Banū Fihr'; Kashshāf, Ṭabrisī, and Rāzī give ʿAbd Allāh ibn Shurayḥ ibn Mālik; Muqātil gives ʿAbd Allāh ibn Abī Musraḥ and also ʿAmr ibn Qays. Farrāʾ calls Umm Maktūm his paternal grandmother, whereas Kashshāf calls her his mother. Jishumī attributes the identity to Ibn ʿAbbās, Ḥasan, Mujāhid, Qatāda, Ḍaḥḥāk, and Ibn Zayd. [22 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(عائشة); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-ASBAB(عائشة); WAHIDI-BASIT(جماعة المفسرين); JISHUMI(ابن عباس والحسن ومجاهد وقتادة والضحاك وابن زيد,الحسن وجماعة); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); IBNKATHIR-FULL(غير واحد من المفسرين); DURR-FULL(عائشة,مسروق); SUYUTI-LUBAB(أنس,عائشة); TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); STUDYQURAN(ʿĀʾishah)]

## 80:1/q10 Why did the blind man approach the Prophet?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on whether his request was for teaching, repentance, or entry into Islam.
- 80:1/q10/p1 He sought instruction, recitation, or guidance in religion. — Reports describe repeated requests while the Prophet was addressing Quraysh; Farrāʾ calls them useful questions. Māwardī and Wāḥidī specify Qurʾān teaching. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Mālik's mursal report via Hishām and ʿUrwa that he asked to be brought near; al-Tirmidhī's ʿĀʾisha route is similar and rated ḥasan gharīb. A report in Ibn Kathīr says the notables saw no fault in the Prophet's words. [34 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(عائشة); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-ASBAB(عائشة); WAHIDI-BASIT(جماعة المفسرين); JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,عائشة,غير واحد من المفسرين); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,عائشة); SUYUTI-LUBAB(عائشة); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(مالك عن هشام بن عروة عن أبيه); TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some narratives); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(ʿĀʾishah)]
- 80:1/q10/p2 He sought repentance. — Muqātil tells of the blind man hearing two angels in the Sacred Mosque, including a remark about Abū Ṭālib, then repeatedly seeking repentance; he left despairing before revelation. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 80:1/q10/p3 He came to embrace Islam. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this as the account of Muqātil and others. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل)]

## 80:1/q11 Which leaders was the Prophet addressing in the encounter?  [* · reports]
turns on: Different occasion reports name different individuals and groups; Ibn Kathīr questions the Ibn ʿAbbās route's chain.
- 80:1/q11/p1 Quraysh or polytheist leaders are left unnamed. — The reports say the Prophet hoped their conversion would attract others; Ibn ʿĀshūr says most or all Quraysh might follow. Jishumī's occasion report and Ibn ʿĀshūr's account name no leader. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(عائشة); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-ASBAB(عائشة); WAHIDI-BASIT(جماعة المفسرين); JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(مالك عن هشام بن عروة عن أبيه); TABATABAI(طرق أهل السنة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some narratives); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(ʿĀʾishah)]
- 80:1/q11/p2 He was speaking with Umayya ibn Khalaf. — Māwardī attributes this to Qatāda; Qurṭubī reports Qatāda naming Umayya or, in another report, Ubayy. Abū Mālik in Durr also names Umayya. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(أبو مالك)]
- 80:1/q11/p3 He was speaking with Ubayy ibn Khalaf. — Qatāda through ʿAbd al-Razzāq and Anas through Ibn Kathīr and Durr name Ubayy; Qurṭubī gives it as Qatāda's alternate report. [4 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس); DURR-FULL(أنس)]
- 80:1/q11/p4 He was speaking with al-ʿAbbās and Umayya ibn Khalaf. — Muqātil says the Prophet turned back to these two; Qushayrī reports the same pair. [2 notes: MUQATIL; QUSHAYRI]
- 80:1/q11/p5 He was speaking with ʿUtba and Shayba, sons of Rabīʿa. — Māwardī attributes this pair to Mujāhid; Durr cites a report from ʿĀʾisha through Masrūq authenticated by al-Ḥākim. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مسروق)]
- 80:1/q11/p6 He was speaking with ʿUtba, Shayba, and Ubayy. — Qurṭubī attributes this three-person list to Mujāhid. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 80:1/q11/p7 He was speaking with ʿUtba alone. — Qurṭubī attributes this to ʿAṭāʾ. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عطاء)]
- 80:1/q11/p8 He was speaking with al-ʿAbbās alone. — Qurṭubī attributes this to Sufyān al-Thawrī. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(سفيان الثوري)]
- 80:1/q11/p9 He was speaking with al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra. — Qurṭubī cites Ibn al-ʿArabī's report of a Mālikī identification and gives al-Walīd's kunya as Abū ʿAbd Shams. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)]
- 80:1/q11/p10 He was speaking with ʿUtba, Abū Jahl, al-ʿAbbās, Ubayy, and Umayya. — Bagḥawī and Ṭabrisī report this five-person list; Ṭabrisī's account says the Prophet feared the leaders would scorn blind or enslaved followers, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmits it from Majmaʿ al-Bayān. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); TABATABAI(مجمع البيان)]
- 80:1/q11/p11 He was speaking with ʿUtba, Abū Jahl, Umayya, and Ubayy. — Ibn al-Jawzī's commentators give this group, identifying Umayya and Ubayy as sons of Khalaf. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون)]
- 80:1/q11/p12 He was speaking with ʿUtba, Shayba, Abū Jahl, al-ʿAbbās, Umayya, and al-Walīd. — Rāzī gives this list; Qurṭubī attributes the same list to Zamakhsharī, adding hope that others would follow the leaders. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 80:1/q11/p13 He was speaking with ʿUtba, Abū Jahl, and al-ʿAbbās. — Ibn Kathīr transmits this from Ibn ʿAbbās through al-ʿAwfī but calls its chain criticized and the report strange and objectionable. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 80:1/q11/p14 He was speaking with ʿUtba, al-ʿAbbās, and Abū Jahl. — Durr transmits this list from Ibn ʿAbbās; it is the same trio as the Ibn Kathīr report, with the order varied. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 80:1/q11/p15 He was speaking with Abū Jahl and ʿUtba among Quraysh leaders. — Durr gives this in an account from ʿĀʾisha. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(عائشة)]

## 80:1/q12 What is reported of Ibn Umm Maktūm and the Prophet afterward?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports concern different subsequent actions and biographical events, rather than mutually exclusive accounts.
- 80:1/q12/p1 The Prophet honored him, welcomed him as the person for whom God reproached him, and sometimes appointed him over Medina. — Reports variously say he asked after his needs and appointed him twice. Ālūsī cites Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr for thirteen prayer deputations; Kur'an Yolu reports Zamakhsharī's two prayer appointments. Study Quran adds that Ibn Umm Maktūm was an early convert, one of the first two migrants to Medina, a Quran teacher, and shared the call to prayer with Bilāl; 4:95 is said to concern his blindness and campaign exemption. [20 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-ASBAB; JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس,ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(أنس,ابن عباس,الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 80:1/q12/p2 Al-Ṣādiq reports that the Prophet warmly welcomed him, saying God would never rebuke him on his account. — Ṭabrisī says the Prophet treated him so kindly that Ibn Umm Maktūm restrained himself for the Prophet; this wording differs from reports that the Prophet welcomed the man for whom his Lord had reproached him. [1 notes: TABRISI(الصادق)]
- 80:1/q12/p3 The Prophet followed him and sent someone to find him. — Qushayrī reports this in a tafsīr account. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 80:1/q12/p4 He never again frowned at or turned from poor people, or courted the rich. — Qushayrī and Kashshāf report the change; Durr cites al-Ḥakam that he was never seen favoring a rich person or turning away from a poor one. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحكم)]
- 80:1/q12/p5 Ibn Umm Maktūm later appeared at al-Qādisiyya armed and carrying a black standard. — Anas reports seeing him armored with a black banner. Study Quran says that, despite exemption, he insisted on a battlefield role and was found dead holding the standard after the battle. [6 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(أنس بن مالك); JISHUMI(أنس); KASHSHAF-FULL(أنس); TABRISI(أنس بن مالك); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس بن مالك); STUDYQURAN]

## 80:1/q13 How strong are the occasion reports?  [* · reports]
turns on: The extent to which solitary occasion reports and their individual chains establish the verse's historical referents.
- 80:1/q13/p1 The report identifies the Prophet and Ibn Umm Maktūm, though some transmissions have chain variants. — Ibn Kathīr says al-Tirmidhī notes Hishām ibn ʿUrwa's route omitting ʿĀʾisha, as in the Muwaṭṭaʾ. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls Mālik's route mursal and notes al-Tirmidhī's similar ʿĀʾisha route graded ḥasan gharīb. Durr says al-Ḥākim authenticated Masrūq's report from ʿĀʾisha. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(هشام بن عروة); IBNASHUR-FULL(مالك عن هشام بن عروة عن أبيه); DURR-FULL(مسروق)]
- 80:1/q13/p2 The reports are solitary and do not establish that the verse concerns the Messenger. — Jishumī says the verse itself does not establish the identification and the reports are not mass-transmitted. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 80:1/q13/p3 A particular Ibn ʿAbbās report is strange and objectionable, with a criticized chain. — Ibn Kathīr gives this judgment of the al-ʿAwfī route that names ʿUtba, Abū Jahl, and al-ʿAbbās. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 80:1/q14 Was the Prophet's conduct sinful?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether a divinely reproved action was sinful, permissible but less fitting, or justified by missionary priorities at the time.
- 80:1/q14/p1 The frown was not a sin, though God trained him toward higher manners. — Jishumī and Ṭabrisī reason that a blind man could not see the expression; Ṭabrisī adds preference for guiding a believer. Rāzī says the Prophet's action served the situation and at most left caution or what was preferable. Al-Jubbāʾī says a later prohibition does not make the earlier act sinful. [4 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI(الجبائي); RAZI-FULL+]
- 80:1/q14/p2 Divine reproach shows that the Prophet sinned. — Rāzī attributes this inference to those who allow prophets to sin; Asad calls what would be minor discourtesy in an ordinary person a major sin for a prophet. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(القائلون بصدور الذنب عن الأنبياء); ASAD-NOTES; RAZI-FULL-]
- 80:1/q14/p3 He was authorized to discipline companions, and the interruption could make his action required. — Rāzī voices the objection that the seeker knowingly interrupted, endangered the larger good of conversion, and could be compared with the censured callers of 49:4; another objection appeals to the Prophet's discretion to teach and discipline. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 80:1/q15 What was God teaching through the rebuke?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the rebuke chiefly teaches social and spiritual priority, refinement of the Prophet's visible conduct, limits on his missionary burden, or fuller assessment of benefits.
- 80:1/q15/p1 Give receptive, sincere believers priority and do not favor rank or wealth. — Zajjāj says not to neglect a Muslim for another; Jishumī says to uphold the guided believer and prevent alienation. Kashshāf reports Sufyān's poor-led gatherings as etiquette learned from this verse. Ibn Kathīr extends equal warning across status, gender, and age, since God guides whom He wills. Qurtubī and Study Quran compare 6:52 and 18:28; Study Quran cites 28:56 for the Prophet's limited power to guide. Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts rich and poor; Iṣlāḥī stresses willing companions over unresponsive chiefs. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ; SULAMI(بعضهم); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL(سفيان الثوري); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 80:1/q15/p2 The Prophet was being refined in manners, with the apparent class preference corrected. — Jishumī says the act must not become habitual; Ṭabrisī says it could wrongly imply poverty caused dismissal. Rāzī says the scene seemed to break poor believers' hearts and put worldly standing before religion, comparing 6:52. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a lapse in focus; Kur'an Yolu compares another prophetic correction at 9:43 and calls this tone especially stern. [7 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 80:1/q15/p3 The affectionate correction limits the Prophet's burden and indirectly rebukes the arrogant leaders. — Iṣlāḥī says the chiefs wanted poor companions excluded; he reads the apparent reproach as an indirect condemnation of them, using Farāhī's shepherd and lost sheep analogy. The Prophet's zeal led him to assume more responsibility than assigned; Iṣlāḥī compares Moses' eagerness at Ṭūr and 26:3. [7 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Far āhī)]
- 80:1/q15/p4 The lesson is to weigh hidden benefits as well as obvious missionary gains. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the sūra teaches investigation of unseen factors and comparison of equal or greater benefits; he likens it to legal theorists requiring a mujtahid to seek conflicting evidence. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 80:1/q16 Why is the visitor called blind in the following verse?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the physical description is a slight or a reason for greater care.
- 80:1/q16/p1 His blindness should have increased compassion and welcome, so naming it sharpens the reproach. — Kashshāf and Rāzī say the description is no insult; the condition creates a stronger claim to care. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 80:1/q16/p2 Calling him blind seems degrading amid an otherwise honorific rebuke. — Rāzī raises this as an objection to the passage's description. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 80:1/q17 What does this self-reproach show about revelation?  [* · theology]
- 80:1/q17/p1 The Prophet conveyed God's criticism of himself, showing faithful, objective transmission. — Ibn Zayd's saying in Thaʿlabī, Jishumī, and Durr says he would have concealed this passage if he concealed anything. Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds the Zayd and Zaynab verses. Asad links public transmission to 53:3. Kur'an Yolu sees sincerity, love of truth, and evidence that the Prophet conveyed God's word rather than his wishes; it also rejects treating him as incapable of error. [7 notes: THALABI(ابن زيد); JISHUMI(ابن زيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(كثير من العلماء، ابن زيد، عائشة); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 80:1/q18 What is this sūra called?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The titles are drawn from the opening, distinctive later words, or the story's central person.
- 80:1/q18/p1 Its standard title is Sūrat ʿAbasa. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says al-Suyūṭī's list omits alternative titles and lists it as ʿAbasa; Biqāʿī says this title points to the lesson and contrasts other names evoking the destructive blast. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 80:1/q18/p2 It is also called al-Ṣākhkhah, al-Safarah, or al-Aʿmā. — Ālūsī reports these alternate titles. Ibn ʿĀshūr attributes al-Ṣākhkhah to Khafājī and al-Safarah or al-Aʿmā to ʿAynī; Study Quran locates their distinctive words in verses 33 and 15. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الخفاجي والعيني); STUDYQURAN]
- 80:1/q18/p3 Ibn al-ʿArabī also called it Sūrat Ibn Umm Maktūm. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says he found this title nowhere else. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 80:1/q19 Where was this sūra revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether consensus on Meccan revelation resolves Ibn al-ʿArabī's broader chronological uncertainty.
- 80:1/q19/p1 It was revealed in Mecca. — Māwardī, Ālūsī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr claim agreement or consensus; Durr transmits Meccan reports from Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn al-Zubayr. Study Quran calls it early Makkan. Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects Ibn al-ʿArabī's uncertainty on this point. [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; BAYDAWI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن الزبير,ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 80:1/q19/p2 The Meccan or Medinan classification and the date of Ibn Umm Maktūm's conversion were not established. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Ibn al-ʿArabī's uncertainty but rejects it for this sūra because exegetes agree it is Meccan. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن العربي); IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 80:1/q20 How many verses are counted in this sūra?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Regional verse counting yields 40, 41, or 42; Jishumī gives 45.
- 80:1/q20/p1 Forty-two verses. — Bayḍāwī and Durr state 42; Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr assign it to the Hijazi or Meccan, Kufan, and some Medinan counts. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; DURR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 80:1/q20/p2 Forty-one verses. — Bursevī lists it as one option; Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr attribute it to Basra. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 80:1/q20/p3 Forty verses. — Bursevī lists it as one option; Ālūsī attributes it to the Syrian and first Medinan counts, and Ibn ʿĀshūr to Syria. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 80:1/q20/p4 Forty-five verses. — Jishumī gives this count. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 80:1/q21 Where does the sūra fall in revelation and Qurʾānic division?  [* · sciences]
- 80:1/q21/p1 It is twenty-fourth in revelation, between al-Najm and al-Qadr, and begins the middle Mufaṣṣal. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives the revelation sequence and section placement. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 80:1/q22 What virtue is reported for reciting the sūra?  [* · sciences]
- 80:1/q22/p1 Its reciter will arrive on Resurrection Day with a smiling, delighted face. — Jishumī reports a transmission from Ubayy from the Prophet. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبي، النبي)]

## 80:1/q23 May reciters add their own words to the sūra?  [* · reports]
- 80:1/q23/p1 No; the Prophet stopped additions and said the sūra's own wording was sufficient or complete. — Durr transmits Abū Wāʾil's account of a Banū Asad delegate adding a line about God creating a child, and Anas's account of al-ʿAlāʾ ibn Yazīd adding a line; the Prophet corrected each. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو وائل,أنس)]

## 80:1/q24 How does the opening connect to the preceding sūra?  [* · links]
- 80:1/q24/p1 It applies al-Nāziʿāt 79:45's warning to those who fear Judgment by distinguishing receptive seekers from indifferent leaders. — Jishumī and Ālūsī connect the closing warning to this opening; Biqāʿī extends the theme to resurrection, human creation, and food as proofs. Iṣlāḥī says the two sūras pair in theme, style, and reasoning, with different topic order; Study Quran reports the same link. [6 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(some)]

## 80:1/q25 How does this opening fit the rest of the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the intra-sūra link is the sustained contrast of receptive and resistant hearers or the repeated imagery of turning and flight.
- 80:1/q25/p1 The sūra contrasts resistant chiefs with believers seeking instruction, then expands to revelation, human dependence, and resurrection. — Ibn ʿĀshūr describes praise of the Quran and its teaching, followed by Quraysh resistance that had occupied the Prophet. Ṭabāṭabāʾī traces poor versus wealthy, human ingratitude, and final recompense. Biqāʿī reads the basmala as God's universal creation and guidance and particular favor to allies; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes that his occasion discussion follows at 80:1. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 80:1/q25/p2 The opening's turning away anticipates the later scene of people fleeing one another. — Sāmarrāʾī links turning away, inward flight in the frown, and distraction from the seeker with the flight scene at 80:34, also citing 80:10. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 80:1/q26 What inward lesson is drawn from the opening?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the inward emphasis falls on a guide's treatment of seekers or the Prophet's divine education and relation to God.
- 80:1/q26/p1 A spiritual guide should judge readiness for faith rather than outward status and attend to a prepared weak seeker. — Qāshānī says wealthy appearance can veil inward readiness: the poor man may perfect himself and guide others, while the rich man may remain arrogant and unbelieving. Sulamī reports that the gentle rebuke honors poverty and the sincere poor. [6 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); QASHANI]
- 80:1/q26/p2 The Prophet's self briefly veiled him from truth as God educated him toward spiritual steadiness. — Qāshānī cites 'My Lord disciplined me and perfected my discipline'; he describes adoption of divine qualities after arrival, annihilation, and abiding. Qushayrī says God's noble name opens generosity to believers while His mighty name eludes seekers, and that God is known and remembered only through Himself, beyond time and place. [5 notes: QASHANI(النبي); QUSHAYRI]
