# 20:17: 17 questions

## 20:17/q01 What does the interrogative mā ask Moses to tell?  [وَمَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether mā seeks the object's name, its qualities and uses, or both; Tabatabai invokes the feminine demonstrative and Moses' detailed reply.
- 20:17/q01/p1 It asks him to identify the object in his hand as his staff. — Tabari, Wahidi al-Wajiz, Razi reporting al-Zajjaj and al-Farra, Asad, and Wahb transmitted by Suyuti present the question as identification. Zarkashi says interrogative mā asks about the objects, kinds, or qualities of non-rational things and can also ask the kinds or qualities of rational beings (2:68, 2:69). Bursawi says it asks the thing's essence, as asking what Zayd is and answering that he is a human. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج,الفراء); BURHAN; DURR-FULL(وهب بن منبه); BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:17/q01/p2 It asks about the known staff's qualities and uses, rather than merely its name. — Jishumi compares asking about Dhu al-Qarnayn to elicit his story (18:83); Razi and Alusi say Moses then learns uses beyond human reason's grasp (20:18). Tabatabai argues the feminine تلك points to the already known staff: an unknown thing would have been indicated with masculine ذلك; he prefers asking for its qualities, explaining why Moses lists uses. [6 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 20:17/q01/p3 It invites both identification of the staff and a description of its ordinary benefits. — Baydawi says Moses understood both parts of the invitation and named the staff and its uses before its transformation. Tabatabai says the reply establishes that it was lifeless matter. Zarkashi says Moses' answer matches the question and adds information. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; TABATABAI; BURHAN]

## 20:17/q02 What does تلك point to or mean?  [تِلْكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the demonstrative is read as near pointing or as a relative expression; grammarians dispute whether a demonstrative can serve as a relative pronoun.
- 20:17/q02/p1 It points to Moses' familiar staff or piece of wood. — Muqatil and Tabari identify the object; Tabatabai says the feminine form suits the known staff or piece of wood. Razi notes that the pointing singles out the staff, alongside the separately mentioned hand. Biqai says Moses identifies it both outwardly and inwardly. [6 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:17/q02/p2 It has the near demonstrative sense ‘this.’ — Al-Farra gives this gloss; Qurtubi adds that mā dhālika would likewise ask what that thing is. [5 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); NASAFI-FULL]
- 20:17/q02/p3 It functions like a relative pronoun, ‘that which is in your right hand.’ — Zajjaj and Abu Ishaq compare it to allatī; al-Farra cites an Arabic verse about the thing carried. Zamakhshari, Ibn Atiyya as reported by later grammarians, and the Kufans permit this usage. Basran grammarians restrict relative use of demonstratives to dhā under conditions; Abu al-Abbas rejects relative use of demonstratives altogether. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); TABRISI(الزجاج,الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري، ابن عطية,الكوفيون); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية); NAHHAS(أبو العباس)-; SAMIN-DURR(البصريون)-; TABRISI(بعض المتأخرين)-]

## 20:17/q03 How is mā tilka bi-yamīnika parsed?  [وَمَا تِلْكَ بِيَمِينِكَ · grammar]
turns on: The subject-predicate order and whether bi-yamīnika is circumstantial or a relative complement; the Kufan and Basran rules for demonstratives divide the latter analyses.
- 20:17/q03/p1 Mā is the interrogative subject, tilka its predicate, and bi-yamīnika is circumstantial or governed by the demonstrative's pointing. — Nahhas calls it a subject-predicate sentence. Kashshaf compares the circumstantial pointing with 11:72; Tabarsi's later grammarian says the circumstantial phrase specifies the demonstrative and avoids modifying it by an indefinite clause. Baydawi also allows this attachment; Nasafi says the staff rests in or is held by the hand. Abu Hayyan and Samin explicitly assign subject and predicate. Alusi explains an omitted circumstantial predicate and says this is one permissible ordering. [11 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(بعض المتأخرين); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 20:17/q03/p2 Tilka is the subject and interrogative mā its predicate. — Bursawi says the predicate is the question about the thing's essence, and this order better fits Moses' answer; Alusi likewise considers both orders and finds this one better suited to the meaning and reply. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL+; BURSEVI+]
- 20:17/q03/p3 Tilka functions as a relative expression completed by bi-yamīnika, ‘the thing in your right hand.’ — Al-Farra cites a line by Yazid ibn Mufarrigh or Yazid ibn Rabi'a, using a demonstrative for ‘the one you carry’; his account explains ʿadas as a mule-shooing cry. Tabari says demonstratives join complements as alladhī does. Zajjaj calls tilka an indefinite demonstrative-like noun behaving as allatī. Nahhas says Abu Ishaq leaned toward al-Farra. Alusi describes Ibn Atiyya's omitted relative clause. Abu Hayyan identifies this as Kufan rather than Basran; Samin states the schools' opposing rules. Abu al-Abbas objects that demonstratives cannot be relatives, and Tabarsi's later grammarian objects that an indefinite clause cannot describe an ambiguous demonstrative. [19 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,الفراء); JISHUMI(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج,الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري، ابن عطية,الكوفيون); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية); NAHHAS(أبو العباس)-; SAMIN-DURR(البصريون)-; TABRISI(بعض المتأخرين)-]
- 20:17/q03/p4 Mā itself is a relative noun linked to bi-yamīnika. — Qurtubi attributes this analysis to Zajjaj and al-Farra and glosses it as ‘what is the thing in your right hand?’ This differs in which word is treated as relative. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج والفراء)]

## 20:17/q04 What does yamīn mean here?  [بِيَمِينِكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether yamīn specifically names the right hand or can denote either hand in Arabic usage.
- 20:17/q04/p1 It means Moses' right hand. — Ibn al-Jawzi gives this as a possible sense of yamīn and compares 69:19. Nasafi describes the staff as resting in or held by it. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); NASAFI-FULL]
- 20:17/q04/p2 It means a hand, whether right or left. — Islahi appeals to classical Arabic usage. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:17/q05 Why specify Moses' hand as bi-yamīnika?  [بِيَمِينِكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes explain the precision through possible multiple objects, Moses' confusion, or the coming sign involving the hand itself.
- 20:17/q05/p1 It identifies exactly which object is being asked about. — Bursawi suggests Moses could have held a ring or another item in the left hand, making an unspecific ‘in your hand’ unclear. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 20:17/q05/p2 It helps a bewildered Moses distinguish right from left. — Jishumi reports that the wonders had left him confused enough to need this specification. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 20:17/q05/p3 It sets Moses' hand alongside the staff as a separate sign. — Razi says those two things are separately indicated and each becomes an overpowering sign, with inert matter elevated to a miraculous state. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 20:17/q06 Why address Moses again by name?  [يَٰمُوسَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 20:17/q06/p1 Repeating his name increases familiarity and draws his attention. — Baydawi gives both effects of the direct address. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 20:17/q07 Why does God ask about the staff when He already knows it?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The question's proposed function: certify the staff before the miracle, secure Moses' admission, soothe his fear, teach unknown uses, focus attention, or invite extended speech.
- 20:17/q07/p1 To establish Moses' certainty about the ordinary staff before its miraculous transformation. — Muqatil and Tabari connect the question with the coming serpent and its sign for Pharaoh. Wahidi's authorities, Ibn Atiyya, Tabarsi, Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, Biqai, Bursawi, Tabatabai and Study Quran stress Moses' recognition of dry or harmless wood before the change. Kashshaf and Bursawi compare a goldsmith displaying iron before making armor. Razi says this displays power over lowly things and makes the miracle unmistakable; Biqai also associates the swift transformation with a sign of the Hour. Qushayri says repeated questions about the staff reflect its future great miracle. Zarkashi reports that prior identification prevents surprise. Moses' response was to be knowledge in the heart and verbal acknowledgement, says Baghawi. [28 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض المفسرين); BURHAN(بعضهم); BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+]
- 20:17/q07/p2 To obtain Moses' own admission of the staff's identity as proof against later denial. — Zajjaj illustrates eliciting an admission with dyed water; Wahidi's Abu Ishaq compares making a person name water before changing it. Ibn al-Jawzi reports Zajjaj's same proof argument. Qurtubi notes that God's prior knowledge is no obstacle to seeking an admission. Ibn Qutayba compares confirmation questions to those addressed to Jesus and communities by messengers. [7 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); ALUSI-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 20:17/q07/p3 To ease Moses' awe and fear by letting him answer a familiar, easy question. — Sulami reports Faris and Abu Bakr ibn Tahir: divine address nearly overwhelmed Moses, so the question returned him to created things and steadied him. Wasiti says direct speech had carried him beyond ordinary discernment. Qushayri warns that sustained awe would prevent understanding or bearing the encounter. Jishumi says ordinary human speech calmed him. Abu Sulayman al-Dimashqi, Nasafi, Qashani, Ibn Kathir, Zarkashi, Biqai, Suyuti, and Alusi likewise give reassurance; Alusi says a correct answer restores composure. Islahi calls the tone affectionate like a parent asking a child a known answer. [17 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر,الواسطي,فارس); QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض المفسرين); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL+; BURHAN+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; QASHANI+]
- 20:17/q07/p4 To ask after the staff's uses and reveal uses beyond Moses' knowledge. — Jishumi says God indicated miraculous uses after Moses gave an ordinary one, and compares a question about Dhu al-Qarnayn (18:83). Razi says the question teaches the limits of human reason even about an object in one's hand (20:18); Alusi repeats the contrast between divine perfection and human limitation. Zarkashi reports Ibn Faris calling the question an imparting of unknown understanding. [5 notes: JISHUMI; BURHAN(ابن فارس); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL+]
- 20:17/q07/p5 To prepare Moses' attention for the important revelation and miracles ahead. — Nahhas identifies an alerting force; Jishumi says the prompt signals that something important is coming. Ibn Atiyya says Moses is readied to receive what follows; the divine knowledge of the object is eternal. Wahidi's authorities and Baydawi likewise speak of focused reflection and awakening to wonders. [5 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 20:17/q07/p6 To give Moses room to speak at length in the encounter. — Abu Bakr ibn Tahir says God wanted Moses to speak expansively so he would not feel constrained when asking. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر)]

## 20:17/q08 Why did Moses answer beyond the apparent request?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether his fuller answer reflects delight and awe, expectation of a miracle, or the scope of the question itself.
- 20:17/q08/p1 The delight or awe of being addressed by God led him to extend his answer. — Sulami's Faris says Moses, almost overwhelmed by unprecedented speech, claimed the staff as his and said more than asked; Wasiti says pleasure in direct address impaired discernment, while the stick to Moses was already a moving serpent in divine knowledge. Qushayri says divine address itself was the greatest benefit. Suyuti says Moses added uses from delight in the conversation. [4 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي,فارس); QUSHAYRI; ITQAN]
- 20:17/q08/p2 He sensed a great event involving the staff and described its ordinary uses first. — Zarkashi says Moses anticipated something significant and therefore added ordinary uses. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 20:17/q08/p3 The question invited both the staff's identity and its uses. — Baydawi says Moses understood the invitation this way. Biqai says he first listed needs the staff served for himself, then those of his flock; Alusi suggests leaning came first because it directly answered what was in his right hand. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:17/q09 What are the staff's ordinary features and uses in these reports?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports contribute different physical details and practical uses rather than explicitly disputing one another.
- 20:17/q09/p1 It had two prongs and two points at its base, and was called nabʿa. — Thalabi attributes this description and name to Muqatil. [1 notes: THALABI(مقاتل)]
- 20:17/q09/p2 Moses used it to lean on, tend sheep, carry and shelter things, get water, and defend his flock. — Wahb's report via Suyuti names leaning, beating leaves for sheep, and further uses; another part describes the fork and crook moving branches and carrying gear, building shade, reaching distant water, and warding off predators. Biqai adds relying on it in fatigue, slippery places, descents, climbs, leaps, and darkness; he divides uses for Moses from uses for the flock. [4 notes: DURR-FULL(وهب بن منبه); BIQAI-FULL]

## 20:17/q10 What happened around the divine address to Moses?  [* · reports]
turns on: The transmitted accounts emphasize different stages and details of the same encounter.
- 20:17/q10/p1 The address followed Moses' removal of his sandals in the sacred valley. — Bursawi reports Kashifi's placement of the event. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 20:17/q10/p2 Moses approached a luminous tree trembling and heard God identify Himself. — Wahb's account in Suyuti says the tree's light rose into a blazing column; Moses heard the call, approached afraid, and was told God was nearer than himself. It continues with the question and Moses' answer about the staff. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]
- 20:17/q10/p3 Moses clutched the staff and struggled toward the tree, speechless with fear, before the question. — Alusi transmits this account from Ahmad: Moses trembled, crept toward the tree, and was asked what he held after God called him near. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أحمد)]

## 20:17/q11 What sign did the staff question begin, and for whom?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the staff sign was needed to certify Moses' prophethood to himself, or his earlier encounter at the tree already sufficed and the signs served the people.
- 20:17/q11/p1 The staff's transformation is a sign of God's power and a proof for Moses' mission to his people and Pharaoh. — Muqatil and Tabari link the question to the serpent and Pharaoh. Jishumi says the passage introduces miracles and the order to convey the message. Ibn Kathir calls the transformation a manifest breach of ordinary nature achievable only by God through a sent prophet. Razi says the change of inert dry wood into living, powerful matter displays divine greatness. Biqai says conviction is strengthened and recipients of the mission confirmed; Islahi calls the staff the source of Moses' miracles. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:17/q11/p2 The staff and hand signs also establish Moses' prophethood for Moses himself. — Qurtubi reports that the question was revelation because he had been commanded to listen to revelation, and a prophet needs a sign by which to know his own prophethood; the staff's effects supply it. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 20:17/q11/p3 The tree's sign already established Moses' prophethood; staff and hand confirmed it for his people. — Qurtubi reports the tree sufficed for Moses personally. Jishumi says a miracle need not be specified in advance: Moses likely knew his prophethood through God's speech and the signs at the tree before the staff and hand were given to his people; others call the further sign kindness to Moses or protection against fear before Pharaoh. [3 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 20:17/q12 How does this question connect to the surrounding revelation and mission?  [* · links]
- 20:17/q12/p1 It begins the revelation of Moses' mission after instructions directed to Moses himself. — Tabatabai says the three preceding verses completed the revelation of prophethood and this question begins the mission. Alusi says the narrative turns from personal commands to a task concerning others. Jishumi says the passage introduces miracles and the command to convey the message. Razi counts six demanding charges in the sequence: oneness, worship and prayer, the Hereafter, wisdom in God's acts, great signs, and the mission to the most defiant people (20:14, 20:15, 20:17, 20:23). [4 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 20:17/q13 What spiritual reading is drawn from the staff and its change?  [* · inward]
turns on: The readings draw different inward correspondences: hidden reality, self under intellect, or moral change of the heart.
- 20:17/q13/p1 God knows hidden realities behind the staff's outward form and reveals them to Moses. — Ibn Ata says Moses knew only its appearance while God knew its transformation. Junayd says Moses knew its name and uses; disclosure of its hidden serpent reality prepared him against fear and directed him back to God (20:67). Al-Husayn says Moses' verified description and the change exposed his incapacity and furnished a communal sign. [3 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الجنيد,الحسين)]
- 20:17/q13/p2 The staff signifies the self held in the intellect's right hand. — Qashani says this hand receives divine gifts and restrains the self. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 20:17/q13/p3 The staff's change mirrors God's power to transform the believer's heart. — Razi compares inert wood made living with a disobedient heart illuminated by knowledge; the serpent swallowing magicians' sorcery with a heart swallowing the commanding soul's magic; and Moses' blessed right hand with God's fingers turning a heart from sin's darkness to service's light. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 20:17/q14 What analogy is made between Moses' question and the believer after death?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:17/q14/p1 A believer may be asked about familiar faith to ease awe in the afterlife. — Razi says the familiar answer is monotheism, known without error in life, so the question lifts bewilderment and shyness. Alusi reports the corresponding explanation that recalling worldly faith removes the awe of arriving in God's presence. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 20:17/q15 Does God's direct speech to Moses prove him superior to Muhammad?  [* · theology]
- 20:17/q15/p1 No; Muhammad was also addressed by God, and his community has forms of divine address. — Razi rejects the inference: 53:10 indicates divine address to Muhammad though its content remained secret; Muhammad's community addresses God repeatedly in prayer and believers receive greeting and honor in the next life (36:58). [2 notes: RAZI-FULL+]

## 20:17/q16 What is said about parsing the verse where no answer survives?  [* · grammar]
- 20:17/q16/p1 Razi poses a grammatical parsing question, but this excerpt ends before his answer. — The note records only the opening of Razi's third question, with no proposed iʿrāb to compare. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 20:17/q17 What do the additional cited poetic images illustrate?  [* · rhetoric]
- 20:17/q17/p1 Poets animate natural changes through human motives, motion, laughter, and reversal. — Jurjani describes darkness as an enemy routed by a sword it drew; a similar poem makes dawn an ambusher and night wary, while al-Khalidi recasts dawn drawing swords and night fleeing. Ibn al-Muʿtazz makes a rose laugh in victory over a fading narcissus, reversing Ibn al-Rumi's roles; his gray hair laughs at a man's ill-fitting youthful behavior, giving a motive beyond Daʿbal's bare reversal and making the image more vivid. These notes contain no explicit link to the verse. [4 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
