# 26:84: 17 questions

## 26:84/q01 What does lisān mean in this expression?  [لِّسَانَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether lisān names speech by its organ or the resulting public mention and reputation.
- 26:84/q01/p1 Speech, a spoken report, or the means by which remembrance spreads. — Ibn Qutayba and al-Qutaybi explain substitution of the speech organ for speech; al-Thaʿlabi and al-Tabrisi add Arabic usage for language. An unnamed poet and al-Aʿsha's line use lisān for news or a message, including news of a brother's killing; al-Jawhari explains the line's ʿulū vocalizations and feminine agreement. Al-Bursawi stresses the means by which praise spreads; al-Alusi calls this cause-for-effect metonymy. [17 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(شاعر); THALABI(الأعشى الباهلي,القتيبي); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(القتيبي); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الأعشى,الجوهري,القتبي); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); STUDYQURAN]
- 26:84/q01/p2 Good mention, reputation, or renown. — Al-Damghani and Ibn al-Jawzi give favorable mention as a lexical sense; Islahi specifies everlasting fond remembrance. Study Quran renders the expression 'renown,' while observing the literal sense 'tongue.' [4 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 26:84/q02 What does ṣidq add to the expression?  [صِدْقٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the genitive chiefly conveys truthfulness and sincerity or an idiom for praiseworthy quality.
- 26:84/q02/p1 It marks something true or sincere. — Study Quran gives 'sincere' and 'true'; al-Tabatabai takes the possessive construction to indicate a tongue speaking only what is in its owner's heart. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI]
- 26:84/q02/p2 It marks praiseworthy goodness or excellence. — Al-Wahidi cites the Arab idiom of attaching ṣidq to a praised thing and compares 'qadam ṣidq' in 10:2. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 26:84/q03 What does Abraham ask God to give him by a 'tongue of truth'?  [وَٱجْعَل لِّى لِسَانَ صِدْقٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the truthful tongue is Abraham's future renown, communities' recognition, surviving teaching or descendants, Muhammad specifically, prayer for him, his own words or qualities, protection from misrepresentation, or a second life; critics of the Muhammad reading question its fit with the wording.
- 26:84/q03/p1 Lasting good praise, remembrance, reputation, and honored acceptance among later people. — Muqatil, Yahya, al-Tabari, al-Zajjaj, al-Wahidi, and many others give this sense; Ibn Kathir adds remembrance after death and following Abraham's example, citing 37:108–110. Yahya cites 37:78; al-Razi cites 37:108; Mujahid relates it to worldly good in 16:122 and 29:27. The broad acceptance extends across religious communities, who honor Abraham; Ibn ʿAtiyya says they honor his monotheism as upheld by Muhammad. Al-Biqaʿi ties the praise to the good God made Abraham display. Al-Alusi says good works and approved practices earn truthful praise. Ibn ʿAshur says praise prompts later prayer for him. Study Quran calls it sincere words on his behalf; Asad points to 19:50. Ibn ʿAta says Muhammad's community is given tongues to praise and testify for Abraham as accepted witnesses. [47 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد,مجاهد); SULAMI(ابن عطاء,سهل); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس، مجاهد، سفيان، السدي، مقاتل، الكلبي، المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية,مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد,مجاهد وقتادة); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن عطاء,سهل); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 26:84/q03/p2 People of every religious community should believe in, acknowledge, or rally around Abraham. — Ikrima says later nations would not deny him; Jews acknowledged Moses but not Jesus, Christians Jesus but not Muhammad, yet both claimed Abraham. He notes Jewish claims of kinship, while 3:67–68 denies Abraham was Jew or Christian and assigns him to Muhammad and believers. Ibn ʿAbbas, Layth, and Ibn Zayd describe agreement, belief, or recognition across religious communities; 29:27 and 16:122 are cited as worldly reward. The notes vary between belief, love, alliance, and universal honor. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(عكرمة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,ليث بن أبي سليم); MAWARDI-FULL(ليث بن أبي سليم); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ليث بن أبي سليم); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN]
- 26:84/q03/p3 Later descendants or generations should preserve and transmit Abraham's true religion and call to monotheism. — ʿAli ibn ʿIsa says descendants who speak truth; al-Tabatabai says they continue Abraham's mission and cites 37:108 and 19:50 for preserving the call by later messengers. Islahi says Israelite prophets carried the message and Muhammad made it global; Kuran Yolu cites 16:123, 3:95, and 4:125 for prophetic descent and following Abraham's religion. Study Quran describes descendants upholding truth until the end of time. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(علي بن عيسى); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI+]
- 26:84/q03/p4 A truthful later descendant or caller should arise, specifically Muhammad, to renew Abraham's religion. — Makkī, al-Tabrisi, al-Razi, al-Baydawi, Elmalılı, and others identify Muhammad; al-Alusi connects this to 2:129 and the report 'I am Abraham's prayer.' Al-Tabatabai cites 2:128–129 and that report; Study Quran attributes this reading to Q and R. Ibn ʿAtiyya and Abu Hayyan call the idea good but say the wording requires forcing the text. Al-Alusi reports a proposed ellipsis, 'companion of a truthful tongue,' or part-for-whole metonymy and says his own account requires neither. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(مكي); TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل,يحتمل); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(Q, R); IBNATIYYA-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL(مكي)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مكي)-]
- 26:84/q03/p5 A good supplication should continue to be made for him until Resurrection. — Al-Qushayri says everyone seeks increased reward; al-Qurtubi explains prayer as supplication for mercy, and Study Quran reports this as one sense. Ibn ʿAshur says lasting praise moves people to pray for Abraham after death. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري); STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 26:84/q03/p6 His own words should carry true, real, elevated meanings. — Kuran Yolu attributes this to exegetes who understand lisān as speech or its transmitters. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler)]
- 26:84/q03/p7 He should receive qualities worthy of remembrance. — Study Quran attributes this to R; al-Alusi says the request implicitly seeks success in good works, its real object, and may seek the praise that indicates divine approval. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(R); ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:84/q03/p8 Later generations should not lie about Abraham. — Study Quran attributes this reading to Ṭ. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 26:84/q03/p9 He seeks a second life. — Al-Qurtubi transmits this gloss from al-Layth ibn Sulayman without further explanation. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الليث بن سليمان)]

## 26:84/q04 Who are 'the later ones'?  [ٱلْءَاخِرِينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the expression is universal across later generations or specifically the final community.
- 26:84/q04/p1 People and generations after Abraham, extending to the end of time or Resurrection. — Al-Wahidi explicitly identifies those after Abraham; Ibn al-Jawzi says through the Day of Resurrection. Abū Isḥāq, al-Qushayri, al-Baydawi, and al-Alusi likewise stress endurance until the end. Ibn Abi Hatim transmits Ibn Zayd's gloss 'later people' with an unclear added phrase 'min al-ism.' [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:84/q04/p2 The final community, among whom Abraham's religion is renewed through Muhammad. — Al-Alusi offers this alternative and cites 2:129 and the report 'I am Abraham's prayer.' [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(يحتمل)]

## 26:84/q05 How does the wording express the request?  [لِّى لِسَانَ صِدْقٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical force of the lām and genitive, and whether interpreting a future person requires ellipsis or part-for-whole reference.
- 26:84/q05/p1 The lām marks benefit, and lisān is a cause-for-effect metonym for lasting mention. — Al-Alusi gives both grammatical and rhetorical points; al-Qashani says mention stands for the thing remembered. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; QASHANI]
- 26:84/q05/p2 On the Muhammad reading, an omitted 'companion of' or part-for-whole use of 'tongue' supplies a person. — Al-Alusi reports these two proposed constructions for the alternative interpretation and says his own interpretation requires neither. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 26:84/q05/p3 The possessive phrase makes the tongue wholly truthful, speaking what its owner inwardly holds. — Al-Tabatabai bases this on the construction 'tongue of truth.' [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 26:84/q06 Why seek an enduring good name?  [لِّسَانَ صِدْقٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes give complementary reasons: reward and influence, evidence of divine love, and the spiritual value of enduring honor.
- 26:84/q06/p1 Praise for virtue can bring reward and inspire later people to practice the same good. — Al-Razi says gathered human attention may increase perfection and public praise encourages imitation. Al-Biqaʿi invokes the saying that initiating a good practice earns its reward and followers' reward until Resurrection; al-Bursawi links excellent deeds, character, and gentle speech to followers' reward. Al-Alusi's unnamed scholars say posthumous praise redirects effort toward divine nearness and imitation; Ibn al-ʿArabi reports Sufi elders' encouragement to righteous deeds. Prophetic reports describe enduring reward for deeds, planting, and border duty. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض الآجلة)]
- 26:84/q06/p2 Good reputation signals God's pleasure and love, and creation's love follows His love. — Al-Qurtubi compares Moses' divinely given love in 20:39 and the promised affection for believers in 19:96; al-Qashani says God's love brings creation's love. Al-Bursawi says even fish and birds are made to love the beloved servant. Al-Alusi calls divine love and approval the ultimate aim. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:84/q06/p3 Lasting honorable mention is an inward or spiritual perfection worth asking for. — Al-Razi contrasts inward virtue with outward appearance and spiritual honor and lasting remembrance with wealth in Abraham's sequence after knowledge. Ibn ʿAshur calls the request lasting perfection. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:84/q07 Is it permissible or desirable to pray for righteous praise?  [وَٱجْعَل لِّى لِسَانَ صِدْقٍۢ · theology]
- 26:84/q07/p1 Yes, when sought for God's pleasure and grounded in good deeds. — Malik says one may wish to be seen among doers of good if intending God's pleasure; Ibn al-ʿArabi transmits his restriction to a good reputation among the righteous. Al-Jishumi approves prayer for descendants and honorable later repute. Al-Bursawi ties genuine remembrance to deeds, character, and speech; al-Alusi says the prayer implicitly asks for success in good works. [6 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL(مالك); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(مالك); IBNASHUR-FULL(مالك)]

## 26:84/q08 How was this prayer answered or evidenced?  [وَٱجْعَل لِّى لِسَانَ صِدْقٍۢ فِى ٱلْءَاخِرِينَ · reports]
turns on: The notes cite broad public praise, prophetic descendants and scriptures, and recurring liturgical blessings as forms of fulfillment.
- 26:84/q08/p1 Abraham receives lasting praise and acceptance among religious communities. — Muqatil, Yahya, al-Thaʿlabi, al-Baghawi, al-Nasafi, and others say people of every religion praise or love him; al-Tabari's Ikrima calls it worldly good or reward and cites 29:27 and 16:122. Ibn ʿAbbas cites 37:108. Kuran Yolu says Muslims, Jews, and Christians honor him, and Muslim prayers name Abraham with Muhammad. Study Quran points to 19:50; Kuran Yolu reports proofs that all Abraham's requests except the one for his father were granted. [23 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(عكرمة); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ليث بن أبي سليم,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); STUDYQURAN]
- 26:84/q08/p2 His descendants include prophets, especially Muhammad, who renew his remembrance and religion. — Al-Biqaʿi cites Abraham's prophetic descendants and the prayer formula blessing Muhammad as Abraham was blessed; Ibn ʿAshur says later scriptures preserve Abraham's name. Islahi says Israelite prophets and Muhammad made his message endure and spread. Kuran Yolu adds the command to follow Abraham's religion, citing 16:123, 3:95, and 4:125; Study Quran attributes fulfillment in Muhammad to Q and R. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(Q, R)]
- 26:84/q08/p3 Blessings on Muhammad include blessings on Abraham in prayers and sermons. — Al-Qurtubi points to prayers and pulpits; al-Biqaʿi and Kuran Yolu cite the blessing formula naming both prophets. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 26:84/q09 What merit is reported for reciting Abraham's prayer after ablution?  [* · reports]
- 26:84/q09/p1 A report promises guidance, provision, healing, forgiveness, truthful renown, martyrdom, and Paradise benefits to one reciting it before obligatory prayer. — Al-Durr transmits the Prophetic report promising Paradise food, drink and homes, expiation, blessed life, wisdom, and other benefits; al-Hasan adds a prayer for parents. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 26:84/q10 How does Abraham's request fit the sequence of his prayers and the surrounding narrative?  [* · links]
turns on: These notes address different links: the prayer sequence, Abraham's life setting, and the sura's narrative arrangement.
- 26:84/q10/p1 It follows requests for wisdom and righteousness and seeks a lasting, spiritual perfection. — Al-Razi reads knowledge first, then inward virtue and lasting honor rather than outward appearance or wealth. Al-Tabatabai says essential righteousness completes the gift of wisdom and cites 16:122; Ibn ʿAshur says the earlier 'righteous' request sums up prophets, martyrs, and the righteous. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب)]
- 26:84/q10/p2 The prayer accompanies Abraham's break with his people and migration. — Islahi locates it at the sensitive point of declaring acquittal and migrating. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:84/q10/p3 The Abraham narrative advances the sura's preaching pattern and bridges the interval between Noah and Moses. — Islahi says it shares the preceding anecdote's purpose and recurring closing verses, and fills the prior sura's gap because Abraham was the most prominent messenger between Noah and Moses. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:84/q11 How does this request connect to other Qur'anic passages?  [* · links]
turns on: The links differ by parallel: fulfillment in remembrance verses, the later messenger prayer, or divinely granted love.
- 26:84/q11/p1 Other verses speak of Abraham's enduring good remembrance and worldly good. — Yahya points to 37:78; Ibn ʿAbbas via al-Razi to 37:108; Ibn Kathir to 37:108–110; Mujahid to 29:27 and 16:122; al-Tabatabai to 37:108 and 19:50, and Study Quran to 19:50. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(عكرمة); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 26:84/q11/p2 The prayer resembles the request for a later messenger and community in 2:128–129. — Al-Alusi cites 2:129 and the hadith 'I am Abraham's prayer' for the final community and Muhammad; al-Tabatabai reports 2:128–129 and that hadith but calls its equation with this verse unclear. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(يحتمل); TABATABAI(بعضهم); TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 26:84/q11/p3 God's placing love for Moses and believers among people illuminates Abraham's request. — Al-Qurtubi cites 20:39 and 19:96; Ibn ʿAshur also connects Moses' divinely given love in 20:39 with Abraham's desired remembrance and notes the earlier family prayer in 25:74. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:84/q12 What does the adjacent request for wisdom and inclusion among the righteous mean?  [* · interpretation]
- 26:84/q12/p1 It asks for essential righteousness completing wisdom and full readiness for divine mercy. — Al-Raghib contrasts ṣalāḥ with corruption as preservation of original nature and its proper effects. Al-Tabatabai says soundness of essence, beyond deeds alone, leaves no corrupt belief or action obstructing mercy; he cites 7:58 and 16:122. Ibn ʿAshur says 'the righteous' includes prophets and messengers, with the broad category placed last as a summation, and reads the petition as seeking ranks including Noah, Hud, Salih, martyrs, and the righteous. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب)]

## 26:84/q13 What does Abraham's wider speech teach about religious reasoning and worship?  [* · theology]
turns on: The notes draw distinct lessons about proof, the object of worship, and the order of prayer.
- 26:84/q13/p1 Truth in religion is established by proof, and imitation is condemned. — Al-Jishumi explicitly affirms religious argument and criticizes imitation in the surrounding passage. [2 notes: JISHUMI]
- 26:84/q13/p2 Worship belongs to the one who originates basic blessings and controls benefit and harm. — Al-Jishumi draws this criterion from Abraham's argument. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 26:84/q13/p3 Supplication should begin with monotheism, following the prophets' method. — Al-Jishumi derives this order from Abraham's speech and prayer. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 26:84/q14 How does the wider speech explain provision and healing?  [* · theology]
- 26:84/q14/p1 God creates provision and cure, while medicine and other natural factors are causes within habits He establishes. — Against natural philosophers, al-Jishumi compares medicine with parents and food: God creates offspring, satiety, and quenching. Natural causes would yield uniform immediate effects, but nature does not reason and drugs have no known feature that itself heals. God made some things nourish, poison, or heal and could have established opposite habits. [4 notes: JISHUMI]

## 26:84/q15 Why did Abraham pray for his father in the following verse?  [* · interpretation]
- 26:84/q15/p1 He relied on a promise or rational obligation until the prayer was forbidden. — Al-Jishumi gives these alternatives concerning 26:86; Kuran Yolu notes that exegetes except the father prayer from the requests granted. [2 notes: JISHUMI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler)]

## 26:84/q16 What inward reading is given to this passage?  [* · inward]
- 26:84/q16/p1 The prophets symbolize spirit or heart, while their peoples' denial symbolizes the soul resisting discipline and perfected morals. — Al-Qashani extends this allegory across the chapter: fearing God is avoiding vices; a trustworthy messenger conveys certain received truths without illusion or fantasy. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 26:84/q17 Why does Ibn Qutayba cite this prayer?  [* · interpretation]
- 26:84/q17/p1 It exemplifies a truthful legacy while he argues for scholarly investigation of hidden meanings in transmitted language. — Ibn Qutayba discusses proverbs, idioms, and reports that need inquiry to uncover their meanings. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]
