# 16:68: 20 questions

## 16:68/q01 What does God's revelation to the bee mean?  [وَأَوْحَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether wahy here names an inwardly implanted capacity, subjection, a command, or dispatch; the notes distinguish animal inspiration from prophetic revelation.
- 16:68/q01/p1 God inwardly inspires and instructs the bees, implanting knowledge, guidance, or instinct for their behavior. — Muqatil, Yahya, al-Farra, al-Kalbi, Mujahid, Ibn Abbas, al-Hasan and many later commentators call it inspiration or something cast into the bee's nature, heart, hearing, or inner self; no messenger is sent (al-Farra, Mujahid in al-Durr). Al-Zajjaj, al-Wahidi, al-Qurtubi, Bursevi and Kur'an Yolu say God guides creatures to benefit and away from harm; al-Suddi extends inspiration to every animal. Al-Sulami's Ibn Ata adds the bee's clean placement of honey. Al-Qushayri links obedience and lawful eating with wholesome healing honey. Al-Razi, Zamakhshari, Ibn al-Arabi, al-Baydawi, Ibn Kathir, Alusi, Asad and Kur'an Yolu cite the precise, gapless hexagonal comb: geometry avoids wasted space, and humans need tools. Al-Razi and Alusi add a large leader and bees summoned home by drums/music. Elmalili distinguishes ordinary ilham, which does not compel certainty, from the bee's necessary and certain craft, a likeness to prophetic revelation. The bee's actual inward mechanism is hidden from people (Zamakhshari, Abu Hayyan, Alusi). Al-Tabatabai calls it instinct in the bee's constitution. The word wahy broadly denotes discreet communication: Ibn Qutayba gives speech, writing, gesture and message (4:163, 6:19, 42:51); al-Tabrisi cites Abu Ubayda, Mujahid, al-Dahhak and al-Ajjaj on secret signs and 19:11; al-Raghib calls it rapid indication. Al-Razi distinguishes prophetic revelation (42:51), saints/disciple inspiration (5:111), Moses' mother (28:7), and animal inspiration; al-Qurtubi also cites the soul (91:7–8), earth (99:4–5) and Ibrahim al-Harbi on inanimate awareness without messengers. Al-Tabatabai adds dreams and satanic whispering (6:121), and reserves unqualified 'revelation' in religious usage for prophets. Study Quran cites 20:38, 28:7, 5:111, 99:5 and 41:12. Al-Biqa'i quotes al-Razi's hierarchy of guidance to inanimate beings, bees, silkworms, spiders, angels and humans; Bursevi gives the raven guiding Cain, with the Mathnawi. [89 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(الكلبي); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(أصحاب معمر,مجاهد,معمر); ZAJJAJ; SULAMI(ابن عطاء); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد,الحسن); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(أبو عبيدة,ابن عباس,الحسن,الضحاك,العجاج,مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(إبراهيم الحربي,ابن العربي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الرازي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(الراغب); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī,some commentators); ELMALILI(müfessirîn)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 16:68/q01/p2 God subjected the bee to its task and the behavior follows that subjection. — Ibn Qutayba gives subjection as one Arabic sense of wahy; al-Mawardi reports this alternative from him. Al-Alusi reports it as a rival explanation. [3 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن قتيبة); ALUSI-FULL]
- 16:68/q01/p3 It means a command addressed to the bees. — Al-Awfi reports this from Ibn Abbas in Ibn al-Jawzi; Ibn Atiyya notes command is a possible sense of wahy, and al-Tabari's Ibn Abbas says God commanded bees to eat fruit and follow the paths. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(العوفي عن ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 16:68/q01/p4 It means God dispatched the bee to its work. — Ibn Mujahid reports this from his father. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مجاهد عن أبيه)]

## 16:68/q02 Why does the verse say 'your Lord' and address the Prophet?  [رَبُّكَ · rhetoric]
- 16:68/q02/p1 It recalls God's beneficence and highlights the bee's remarkable creation as a sign of purposeful power. — Al-Biqa'i points to honey provided even in remote wilderness. Al-Tabatabai suggests the shift to 'your Lord' responds to the bee's remarkable social life and craft. Elmalili hears the divine source of certain knowledge in the phrase. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI]

## 16:68/q03 What does al-naḥl denote?  [ٱلنَّحْلِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the point is ordinary designation or an etymology from the honey given.
- 16:68/q03/p1 It denotes honeybees as a collective; one bee is naḥla. — Al-Tha'labi, al-Wahidi and al-Baghawi call them honey-producing wasps; Ibn al-Jawzi calls them honeybees. Al-Jawhari says naḥl and naḥla can denote either sex and gives yaʿsub as another name. Abu Hayyan and Alusi explicitly describe the collective and singular. [7 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(الجوهري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 16:68/q03/p2 The bee's name comes from God's gift of honey to people. — Al-Zajjaj links naḥl to niḥla, gift, as transmitted by al-Wahidi, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi and Bursevi. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BURSEVI(الزجاج)]

## 16:68/q04 How is al-naḥl pronounced in the variant reading?  [ٱلنَّحْلِ · readings]
- 16:68/q04/p1 Yaḥyā ibn Waththāb reads it with the ḥāʾ opened, giving two fatḥas. — Ibn Atiyya, al-Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi report the reading; al-Samin and Alusi explain it as a dialectal form or vowel assimilation/harmony. Al-Zamakhshari notes its noun remains masculine while the feminine imperative follows sense. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(يحيى بن وثاب); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(يحيى بن وثاب); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن وثاب); ALUSI-FULL(ابن وثاب)]

## 16:68/q05 Why is the command to al-naḥl feminine?  [ٱلنَّحْلِ ٱتَّخِذِى · grammar]
- 16:68/q05/p1 The collective noun is feminine in Hijazi usage or may take either gender, so the imperative follows its feminine sense. — Al-Akhfash, al-Wahidi, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi cite Hijazi feminine use and the collective's singular differentiated by tāʾ; other Arabs use masculine. Al-Nahhas notes the feminine and a diminutive naḥīl without tāʾ to avoid resembling the singular. Al-Zamakhshari and al-Baydawi explain feminine agreement by meaning even when the noun is grammatically masculine. Al-Nasafi, al-Samin and Bursevi say either gender is possible. [15 notes: AKHFASH; NAHHAS(الأخفش); WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL(يحيى بن وثاب); RAZI-FULL(غيره); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 16:68/q06 Can the bee receive literal divine address or possess reason?  [وَأَوْحَىٰ ٱلنَّحْلِ ٱتَّخِذِى · theology]
turns on: Whether animal skill implies rational comprehension and whether prophetic revelation can apply to animals.
- 16:68/q06/p1 The address can be understood literally, with animals capable of intelligence or meaningful divine communication. — Al-Razi reports some holding animals have reason and receive commands and prohibitions, referring to the ant in 27:18. Study Quran cites creatures as communities that praise and prostrate to God (6:38, 17:44, 16:48–50 and other verses), and reports al-Razi's evidence of hexagonal craft and an obeyed leader. Al-Alusi reports Illuminationist philosophers' rational animal souls and nearly concedes that claim. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعض الناس); STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī); ALUSI-FULL(بعض حكماء الإشراق); STUDYQURAN+; RAZI-FULL(آخرون)-; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]
- 16:68/q06/p2 Bee behavior comes from divinely created instinct; verbal command is a figurative extension suited to its nature. — Al-Razi reports the alternative that dispositions necessitate animal conduct (27:18). Al-Tabrisi says command language follows the phrasing of what is inspired despite bees' inability to understand speech. Al-Alusi reports that some reserve literal revelation for rational beings, while noting the bee's seeming intelligence. Al-Razi refers the detailed discussion to 27:18. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(آخرون); TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); STUDYQURAN-]
- 16:68/q06/p3 Animals have messengers and prophets and can receive revelation literally. — Al-Alusi attributes this to Sufis as reported by al-Sha'rani and says revealed law rejects it. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الصوفية)]

## 16:68/q07 How does an introduce the instruction 'take'?  [أَنِ ٱتَّخِذِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether the sense of wahy here retains a speech implication despite its meaning inspiration.
- 16:68/q07/p1 It is explanatory: revelation carries the sense of speaking, and the clause states its content. — Al-Zamakhshari, al-Razi, al-Nasafi, al-Biqa'i and al-Alusi give this analysis. Al-Samin answers the objection by saying each thing's speech suits its kind; al-Alusi replies that the familiar sense of revelation implies speech. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي)-; ITQAN(الزمخشري)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-]
- 16:68/q07/p2 It is infinitival, with an implied preposition, yielding God's inspiration of the bee's taking homes. — Al-Baydawi and al-Alusi offer an omitted bi; al-Samin gives the infinitival alternative. Abu Hayyan and al-Itqan reject the explanatory analysis because the unanimously accepted sense here is inspiration without speech. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ITQAN(الزمخشري)+; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 16:68/q08 What are the bees instructed to take or make?  [ٱتَّخِذِى بُيُوتًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'houses' focuses on the bees' own comb construction or on the natural and prepared sites sheltering it.
- 16:68/q08/p1 They establish dwellings in mountains, trees, and human-made places. — Muqatil, Yahya, al-Tabari, al-Wahidi, Ibn Kathir, al-Biqa'i and Study Quran state the three settings. Ibn Abbas in al-Wahidi says bees use mountains when places have no human owners; al-Wahidi and al-Razi distinguish wild bees and tended bees. Al-Tabari's Ma'mar and companions describe inner instruction for mountain homes. Al-Baghawi says people prepare shelter among the settings. Al-Biqa'i adds suitable trees in groves, mountains and deserts. [18 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(أصحاب معمر); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 16:68/q08/p2 The 'houses' are the bees' own precisely built nests or combs. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Damghani and Ibn al-Jawzi give bayt as a nest or hive, comparing the spider's home at 29:41. Al-Mawardi says the homes have sound division and strong defense. Al-Zamakhshari says their craft warrants calling them houses; al-Baydawi and al-Alusi call this a metaphor based on dwelling and proportioned human construction. Al-Biqa'i places homes first because their form and workmanship amaze. Kur'an Yolu says wax-built hexagonal-prism combs stand in tree hollows or prepared hives. [12 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 16:68/q08/p3 The sites include existing mountain crevices, hollow trees, hives, and wall cavities. — Ibn Atiyya, al-Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi's report of him give these as the physical homes or shelters; this encompasses wild and tended bees. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]

## 16:68/q09 Why use min before mountains, trees, and what people raise?  [مِنَ ٱلْجِبَالِ وَمِنَ ٱلشَّجَرِ وَمِمَّا يَعْرِشُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether one occurrence of min can express partitivity of both objects and locations within them.
- 16:68/q09/p1 Min is partitive: bees occupy only some mountains, trees, and built places. — Al-Zamakhshari, al-Razi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin, Bursevi and Alusi cite the fact that bees do not nest in every member of those classes. Al-Alusi extends this to only some places within each member, and mentions Ibn Kamal's treatise on the issue. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]
- 16:68/q09/p2 Min marks only some individual mountains, trees, or trellises; selection of locations within them comes from context. — Al-Alusi reports a grammarian's objection that a single min cannot carry both individual and locational partitivity at once. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 16:68/q10 What is the reading and analysis of buyūtan?  [بُيُوتًۭا · readings]
turns on: Whether a kasra before the following yāʾ is an acceptable dialectal plural vowel.
- 16:68/q10/p1 The bāʾ takes ḍamma, buyūtan, following the regular fuʿūl plural pattern. — Al-Zajjaj calls it regular; al-Nahhas compares fulūs; al-Alusi calls it the usual plural of faʿl. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; ALUSI-FULL]
- 16:68/q10/p2 The bāʾ takes kasra, biyūtan, influenced by the following yāʾ; the meaning is unchanged. — Al-Zajjaj says Basran grammarians reject this pattern; al-Nahhas calls substitution of kasra for ḍamma remote. Al-Zamakhshari, al-Razi, al-Baydawi and al-Alusi report it; Qiraat-Er calls it dialectal with no semantic difference. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]

## 16:68/q11 What does yaʿrishūn mean in Arabic?  [يَعْرِشُونَ · meaning]
- 16:68/q11/p1 It means people raise, arrange, trellis, or roof a structure. — Abu Ubayda's Majaz and al-Wahidi say both yaʿrush and yaʿrish are used; Ibn Qutayba extends ʿarsh/maʿrūsh to raised vine, plant or roof. Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi describe arranging branches and wood into shade and cite the shelter built for the Prophet at Badr. Al-Tabrisi derives it from ʿarsh, roof; Ibn al-Jawzi says make a trellis. Al-Farra, al-Damghani and al-Razi emphasize building or roofing. [14 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT; DAMGHANI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 16:68/q12 How is yaʿrishūn read?  [يَعْرِشُونَ · readings]
turns on: The two accepted Form I vowel patterns of the verb, without a semantic difference.
- 16:68/q12/p1 The rāʾ is read with kasra, yaʿrishūn. — Al-Qurtubi says other readers use kasra while reports vary about Asim. Al-Majaz, al-Wahidi, al-Zamakhshari, Ibn Atiyya, al-Tabrisi, al-Razi and Qiraat-Er treat the two Form I vowel patterns as equivalent. Al-Samin refers to the related reading at 7:137. [11 notes: MAJAZ; WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الكلبي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عامر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; QIRAAT-ER]
- 16:68/q12/p2 The rāʾ is read with ḍamma, yaʿrushūn. — Ibn Amir and Abu Bakr are named by Ibn al-Jawzi and al-Baydawi; al-Qurtubi names Ibn Amir and notes disagreement about Asim; al-Nasafi says Shami and Abu Bakr. The lexical meaning is the same (Qiraat-Er). [14 notes: MAJAZ; WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عامر); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(شامي وأبو بكر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; QIRAAT-ER]

## 16:68/q13 What does 'what they raise' refer to?  [وَمِمَّا يَعْرِشُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the context of bee habitation restricts raised structures to hives and buildings, allows vineyards, or leaves the class broad.
- 16:68/q13/p1 Human-made bee shelters, especially hives, roofs, walls, and other buildings. — Al-Farra, al-Tabari as reported by al-Mawardi, Ibn al-Jawzi and Alusi, al-Wahidi, al-Razi, al-Nasafi and Study Quran refer it to people's building and roofing. Al-Wahidi's Ibn Abbas identifies hives; al-Wahidi rejects Ibn Zayd's vines because bees do not inhabit them, and reports the observation that divine subjection makes bees accept human hives. Ibn al-Jawzi reports structures where bees deposit honey. Al-Baghawi and al-Biqa'i include human shelter. Al-Nasafi and al-Zamakhshari explicitly identify the pronoun as people. Al-Tabari notes that he explained the human-raised phrase elsewhere and does not restate it here. [34 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(أبو جعفر الطبري); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء,قول); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,الطبري); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 16:68/q13/p2 They are trellised grapevines or vineyards. — Ibn Zayd is reported by al-Tabari, al-Tha'labi, al-Mawardi, al-Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and Alusi; al-Tabrisi attributes the option to al-Kalbi. Al-Wahidi objects that bees do not live in vines; Ibn Atiyya finds this and the roof gloss imprecise. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن زيد); TABRISI(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد)-; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد)-]
- 16:68/q13/p3 The phrase broadly includes vines, roofs, walls, hives, or other raised constructions. — Ibn Qutayba's lexical category spans vines, plants and roofs. Al-Tabrisi's al-Kalbi gives grape arbors or hives; Bursevi and Alusi transmit inclusive readings. Al-Ibn Atiyya finds the narrow vine and roof explanations imprecise. [6 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(بعض)]

## 16:68/q14 What happened when bees received this inspiration?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports describe different events and practices.
- 16:68/q14/p1 A report says the bee was inspired in honey-making on Eid al-Fiṭr, the Day of Mercy. — Bursevi attributes the report to ʿAjāʾib al-Makhlūqāt. [1 notes: BURSEVI(عجائب المخلوقات)]
- 16:68/q14/p2 People move bees to pasture, travel with them, and the bees follow. — Al-Durr cites this account alongside livestock made subject to humans at 36:71–72. [1 notes: DURR-FULL]

## 16:68/q15 What reports describe the bee's special standing?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports concern distinct claims.
- 16:68/q15/p1 A report says all flies are in the Fire except bees. — Al-Qurtubi cites Abu Hurayra's Prophetic report recorded by al-Tirmidhi al-Hakim in Nawadir al-Usul; Bursevi repeats the saying as a mark of the honeybee's distinction. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI]
- 16:68/q15/p2 A Prophetic report forbids killing bees along with ants, hoopoes, and shrikes. — Al-Qurtubi gives Ibn Abbas as transmitter, Abu Dawud as recorder, and says the issue recurs in the ant passage. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 16:68/q16 What rulings are reported about eating and killing bees?  [* · law]
- 16:68/q16/p1 Eating bees is forbidden though honey is lawful; killing bees is disliked. — Bursevi cites Hayat al-Hayawan and compares lawful human milk with forbidden human flesh; al-Qurtubi cites the Prophetic prohibition on killing bees. [2 notes: BURSEVI(حياة الحيوان); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 16:68/q17 May bees be sold?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether bees can be treated as saleable property, with visibility or exposure conditions.
- 16:68/q17/p1 Sale is valid when all bees in the hive are visible, and valid if sold exposed. — Bursevi attributes these rulings to al-Tatimma. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التتمة)]
- 16:68/q17/p2 Sale of exposed bees is invalid. — Bursevi says al-Tahdhib gives the opposite ruling from al-Tatimma on exposed bees. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التتمة)]
- 16:68/q17/p3 Sale of bees is invalid, like sale of wasps and other insects. — Bursevi attributes this to Abu Hanifa, who nevertheless allows sale of silkworms for silk. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أبو حنيفة)]

## 16:68/q18 How does the bee passage connect to surrounding verses and the sūra?  [* · links]
- 16:68/q18/p1 It follows food and drink signs as a culminating proof of God's power, wisdom, and purposeful creation. — Al-Razi links milk and the drinks from dates and grapes in 16:66–67 with honey. Al-Biqa'i says the bee is a more striking and precious third example, placed later because people esteem it less, and 'revelation' signals its marvel. Asad says the sūra's bee title points to divine creativity, with purposeful activity leading to the ethical injunctions of verse 90. Study Quran says the sūra is named for this account. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]

## 16:68/q19 What is meant by the Lord's easy paths and how does the instruction continue?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the following verse speaks of routes, honey-making procedure, or access to prescribed fruits.
- 16:68/q19/p1 The paths are easy or docile routes made smooth for the bee. — Al-Jishumi derives dhulul as the plural of dhalul, opposed to difficult travel; al-Tabatabai's al-Majma' explains docile or submissive for an animal or human. Al-Tabari's Ibn Abbas says bees eat fruits and follow the Lord's paths gently. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABATABAI(المجمع); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 16:68/q19/p2 The paths are the procedure God taught bees for making honey. — Study Quran reports this understanding; al-Tabatabai adds that eating from all fruits includes flower material before fruit ripens. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(some commentators); TABATABAI]
- 16:68/q19/p3 The bee follows paths to the particular fruits God ordained it to eat. — Study Quran reports this alternative. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]

## 16:68/q20 Is the bee read as a symbol of ʿAlī and the Prophet's family?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether Ali's title yaʿsub warrants taking the bee and its drink as symbols of his family and teachings.
- 16:68/q20/p1 Yes: the bee is ʿAlī and the family, and its diverse drink is their Quranic knowledge and wisdom that heals spiritually. — Study Quran reports this from some Shiite commentators; al-Tabrisi traces it to Ali's saying he was the believers' yaʿsub, a word for both male bee and leader. It reports al-Qurtubi and al-Zamakhshari rejecting the interpretation. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭabrisī,some Shiite commentators); STUDYQURAN(al-Qurṭubī and al-Zamakhsharī)-]
- 16:68/q20/p2 No: this Shiite symbolic reading is unsound. — Study Quran reports al-Qurtubi and al-Zamakhshari's rejection. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(al-Qurṭubī and al-Zamakhsharī); STUDYQURAN(some Shiite commentators)-]
