# 35:36: 27 questions

## 35:36/q01 Who are the disbelievers here?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Ordinary disbelief or an inward state of distance.
- 35:36/q01/p1 People who deny God, His oneness, His Book, or His messengers. — Muqātil specifies denial of God's oneness; Bursawī includes denial of God's existence. Biqāʿī describes concealing rationally evident signs and arrogantly rejecting God's allies and callers. Islāḥī relates the warning to the Book's original deniers. [4 notes: MUQATIL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 35:36/q01/p2 People veiled from the addressee, lacking any spiritual closeness or inheritance of the Book. — Qāshānī gives an inward reading: denial leaves them truly distant from him. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 35:36/q02 What does assigning Hellfire to them convey?  [لَهُمْ نَارُ جَهَنَّمَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the lām denotes desert or exclusive and enduring assignment.
- 35:36/q02/p1 Hell is the deserved recompense for their disbelief and deeds. — Ṭabarī says they have no share of Paradise or its bliss; Bursawī calls disbelief the greatest sin and contrasts this fire with ordinary fire. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the lām marks desert and cites 2:24 and 3:131; he distinguishes the fire of sinful believers. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 35:36/q02/p2 Hell is assigned exclusively and inseparably to them. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the lām as exclusive assignment of their recompense. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 35:36/q03 Why is لَهُمْ placed before the mention of Hell?  [لَهُمْ نَارُ جَهَنَّمَ · rhetoric]
- 35:36/q03/p1 It creates anticipation, then lets the completed statement settle in listeners' minds. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies this effect of the fronted phrase. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:36/q04 What is the fire in the inward reading?  [نَارُ جَهَنَّمَ · inward]
- 35:36/q04/p1 It is the hell of nature, with unending deprivation and pain. — Qāshānī says its sufferers find neither death nor easing. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 35:36/q05 What does the denial of death mean?  [لَا يُقْضَىٰ عَلَيْهِمْ فَيَمُوتُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether qaḍāʾ is a death decree, an act of destruction, or a figure for death's pains.
- 35:36/q05/p1 No death is decreed for them, so they remain alive in torment without death's relief. — Al-Suddī says death never descends; Qatāda and Ibn ʿAbbās say dying would grant rest. Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Bursawī and Ālūsī specify no second death. Ālūsī favors a denied sentence over a redundant bare denial of dying. Ibn ʿĀshūr links the literal sense to their plea in 43:77. Qurtubī and Study Quran cite 20:74, 'neither die nor live'; Ibn Kathīr cites a sound Muslim report and 20:74, 43:74–77, 17:97 and 78:30. Biqāʿī contrasts ordinary fire, which finishes its victims. Rāzī says they wish for death but are denied it (43:77). [29 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); TAB-FULL(أبو السوداء عن قتادة,أبو سعيد الخدري عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عثمان المازني); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 35:36/q05/p2 They are not killed or destroyed, and therefore cannot find relief. — Wāḥidī offers this as an alternative needing no omitted word, citing Moses' killing in 28:15. Ibn al-Jawzī cites the same usage; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says they are not killed off. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 35:36/q05/p3 Figuratively, they undergo the pains of death without its release. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says renewed skins keep them tasting torment, citing 4:56. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:36/q06 How is فَيَمُوتُوا۟ read and construed?  [فَيَمُوتُوا۟ · readings]
turns on: Whether the fāʾ introduces a subjunctive result or a coordinated indicative with retained nūn.
- 35:36/q06/p1 The common reading drops nūn: the subjunctive follows negation through an implicit an and makes death its denied result. — Zajjāj, Abū Isḥāq, Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Bursawī and Ālūsī explain the implicit an. Abū Ḥayyān says this denies both sentence and resulting death, not affirmative arrival without dying. Burhān contrasts deletion and retention of nūn. Samīn rejects the alternative affirmative-result sense as impossible here. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(العامة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BURHAN]
- 35:36/q06/p2 The raised reading retains nūn and coordinates dying with the negated decree: neither happens. — Reported from al-Ḥasan, ʿĪsā and al-Thaqafī. Thaʿlabī, Kashshāf, Qurtubī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn and Ālūsī compare 77:36. Al-Kisāʾī attributes its written nūn to verse-final position and says either construction works in either clause. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls this reading weak; Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn report that judgment. [13 notes: THALABI(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن البصري والثقفي); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن,الكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عثمان المازني,عيسى، والحسن); SAMIN-DURR(عيسى والحسن); ALUSI-FULL(عيسى والحسن)]
- 35:36/q06/p3 A hypothetical subjunctive construction affirms the first event while denying its sequel, but that sense is impossible here. — Samīn gives arrival without conversation as the analogous sense and rejects it for this verse. [0 notes: SAMIN-DURR-]

## 35:36/q07 What is the force of qaḍāʾ here?  [لَا يُقْضَىٰ عَلَيْهِمْ · meaning]
turns on: A decree or the completed act of death.
- 35:36/q07/p1 It is judging or decreeing, here a death sentence. — Jishumī links qaḍāʾ with ḥukm; Ibn ʿĀshūr allows literal judgment or God's decree. [2 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 35:36/q07/p2 It can mean causing death or completing a life. — Jishumī says it can mean finishing something completely or death as life's completion; Ibn ʿĀshūr allows causing death. [2 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:36/q08 How does the death clause relate to the preceding Hell statement?  [لَا يُقْضَىٰ عَلَيْهِمْ · rhetoric]
- 35:36/q08/p1 It is an explicative substitute, spelling out their condition in Hell. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the clause as an explicative badal. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:36/q09 What does the sequence of withheld death and unrelieved torment stress?  [لَا يُقْضَىٰ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا يُخَفَّفُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Absence of an ending and absence of adaptation or decrease.
- 35:36/q09/p1 Their torment has neither an end nor a decrease. — Rāzī says death would be its strongest possible end, and the damned wish for it (43:77). He contrasts this lack of decrease with the blessed receiving an increase from God's bounty (35:30). [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 35:36/q09/p2 Afterlife torment never becomes harmless through bodily familiarity. — Rāzī contrasts worldly pain: it neither kills them nor becomes so familiar that they cease to feel it. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 35:36/q10 How is the passive لَا يُقْضَىٰ عَلَيْهِمْ parsed?  [لَا يُقْضَىٰ عَلَيْهِمْ · grammar]
- 35:36/q10/p1 عَلَيْهِمْ serves as the passive verb's deputy subject. — Samīn gives this analysis alongside the corresponding parse of ʿanhum after yukhafaf. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 35:36/q11 What does the denial of lighter punishment cover?  [وَلَا يُخَفَّفُ عَنْهُم مِّنْ عَذَابِهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause covers all mitigation or especially death as escape; changing flames need not ease pain.
- 35:36/q11/p1 Their felt punishment is never relieved, reduced, or interrupted. — Wāḥidī and Bursawī say not even for an eye blink or instant. Jishumī distinguishes less amount, less severity, and interruption, finding reduction likeliest because eternity already implies continuity. Qurtubī invokes renewed skins in 4:56. Rāzī says agony remains severe at every moment. Study Quran says worldly respite and repeated chances to repent preceded final sentencing, citing 2:162, 3:88, 2:86, 16:85, 43:74–75. [21 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عثمان المازني); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 35:36/q11/p2 The clause particularly denies relief through death, though flames may abate and intensify. — Ṭabarī reconciles it with 17:97: this kind of torment is not eased by death. Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes enduring punishment from temporary subsiding. Jishumī, Bursawī, Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī say renewed blazing prevents relief of pain; Yaḥyā links it with increase in 78:30. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; YAHYA-SALLAM; JISHUMI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 35:36/q12 How is يُخَفَّفُ read?  [يُخَفَّفُ · readings]
- 35:36/q12/p1 One transmission from Abū ʿAmr stills the fāʾ. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya through Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī name ʿAbd al-Wārith from Abū ʿAmr. Samīn treats the separated form like a connected one and cites poetic أشرب; Ibn ʿAṭiyya also cites poetry. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(عبدالوارث عن أبي عمرو)]

## 35:36/q13 How are عَنْهُم and مِّنْ عَذَابِهَا parsed?  [عَنْهُم مِّنْ عَذَابِهَا · grammar]
turns on: Which phrase supplies the passive deputy subject, and whether min is additional.
- 35:36/q13/p1 عَنْهُم is the deputy subject and مِّنْ عَذَابِهَا fills the other argument. — Samīn, Bursawī and Ālūsī present this analysis. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 35:36/q13/p2 مِّنْ عَذَابِهَا is the deputy subject and عَنْهُم fills the other argument. — Samīn, Bursawī and Ālūsī give this alternative. Samīn attributes to al-Akhfash the possibility that min is extra; Abū al-Baqāʾ says if so its genitive object must be raised as deputy subject. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]

## 35:36/q14 What does the pronoun in عَذَابِهَا refer to and encompass?  [عَذَابِهَا · referent]
- 35:36/q14/p1 It refers to Hell's punishment, including fire and reported intense cold. — Ibn ʿĀshūr names zamharīr; Ālūsī says permanence of torment need not exclude cold or other forms. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 35:36/q15 Can another person's greater punishment make one's own feel lighter?  [عَذَابِهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether comparison itself is excluded or each person's perception explains the lack of comfort.
- 35:36/q15/p1 No; sufferers are wholly consumed by their own pain, so comparisons cannot relieve it. — Jishumī reports the qāḍī's rejection of comparative easing and explicitly prefers that view. [2 notes: JISHUMI(القاضي); JISHUMI+]
- 35:36/q15/p2 Each sufferer believes his own punishment exceeds the others'. — Jishumī reports this alternative account of why another's suffering affords no comfort. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]

## 35:36/q16 What does the verse imply about sinful believers and permanent inhabitants of Hell?  [لَا يُقْضَىٰ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا يُخَفَّفُ عَنْهُم مِّنْ عَذَابِهَا · theology]
- 35:36/q16/p1 Permanent inhabitants neither die nor live; sinful believers can die in the Fire, receive intercession, and be revived in Paradise. — Ṭabarī transmits the Prophet's report comparing revival to a seed after floodwater. Ibn Kathīr refers to a sound Muslim report. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the denial of easing hints that sinful believers' fire is lighter than polytheists' fire, and distinguishes their fire in discussing the lām. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:36/q17 What does كَذَٰلِكَ mean and how does it function?  [كَذَٰلِكَ · grammar]
turns on: A backward reference or emphasis on the punishment's full severity.
- 35:36/q17/p1 It points back to this same kind of recompense. — Kashshāf says 'with that kind of recompense'; Nasafī says the same punishment. Samīn allows omitted 'the matter is thus' (nominative) or 'like that recompense' (accusative). Ibn ʿĀshūr takes it as an absolute object of najzī, 'such a requital', and calls the following phrase a concluding generalization, comparing 2:143. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 35:36/q17/p2 It introduces the pattern of dreadful punishment without a lesser recompense. — Bursawī and Ālūsī emphasize that no lighter recompense is intended for intense disbelief or ingratitude. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 35:36/q18 How is the recompense verb read?  [نَجْزِى · readings]
turns on: Voice, verb form, and the resulting case of كُلّ.
- 35:36/q18/p1 The majority read active نَجْزِى, with كُلَّ accusative and God as agent. — Al-Fārisī cites alignment with 35:37. Thaʿlabī describes first-person nūn and accusative lām; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr report the majority. [12 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(الباقون); THALABI(العامة); JISHUMI(أبو عمرو); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمرو، الباقون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); NASHR(أبو عمرو); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 35:36/q18/p2 Abū ʿAmr reads passive يُجْزَىٰ, with كُلُّ nominative as deputy subject. — Some lists add Khalaf or a Nāfiʿ transmission via Abū Ḥātim. Al-Fārisī says preceding passive 'punishment is not lightened' supports it and active and passive mean the same; he compares 34:23 and yujāzā/nujāzī. Lists differ on whether Abū ʿAmr is sole reader. Qirāʾāt ER states the shift. [16 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو); THALABI(أبو عمرو); JISHUMI(أبو عمرو); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمرو، الباقون); BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو عمرو); NASAFI-FULL(أبو عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); NASHR(أبو عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(أبو عمرو وأبو حاتم عن نافع); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو عمرو); QIRAAT-ER(Imp|Pss|CE)]
- 35:36/q18/p3 Another variant reads يُجَازَىٰ, a passive extended form. — Kashshāf and Bayḍāwī report it; al-Fārisī compares the active extended form. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو عمرو); FARISI-HUJJA]
- 35:36/q18/p4 Another variant reads نُجَازِي, the first-person active extended form. — Kashshāf reports nujzī variants; Ālūsī specifies ḍamma on nūn and alif after jīm for nujāzī. Al-Fārisī compares active and passive extended forms. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; FARISI-HUJJA]

## 35:36/q19 Who is included in كُلَّ كَفُورٍۢ?  [كُلَّ كَفُورٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Any unbeliever, an especially persistent denier, denial of blessings, or specifically a polytheist.
- 35:36/q19/p1 Every disbeliever or denier of God and the truth. — Qatāda says everyone disbelieving in his Lord; Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil gloss every disbeliever. Ṭabrisī mentions denial of God and prophets; Qurtubī names God and His messenger; Ibn Kathīr names their Lord and the truth. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 35:36/q19/p2 An intense or persistent unbeliever or ingrate. — Bayḍāwī describes excess; Ṭabāṭabāʾī allows extreme or repeated denial. Bursawī and Ālūsī say the form is emphatic and no lesser recompense is meant. Nasafī calls him an ingrate. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 35:36/q19/p3 A denier of God's blessings requited for worldly sins. — Ṭabarī explicitly links denial of blessings and the sins committed in this life. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]
- 35:36/q19/p4 Specifically, an intensely unbelieving polytheist. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the emphatic kāfūr with a polytheist. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:36/q20 Does this verse speak of repentance from only some sins?  [كَفُورٍۢ · theology]
turns on: Whether partial repentance is assessed by curse or deserved punishment.
- 35:36/q20/p1 The question concerns whether such repentance averts a curse. — Jishumī reports this as one framing of repentance from one sin while persisting in another. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 35:36/q20/p2 The question concerns whether such repentance averts deserved punishment. — Jishumī reports this as the other framing of the same disputed validity. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]

## 35:36/q21 How severe was this verse considered by an early transmitter?  [* · reports]
- 35:36/q21/p1 ʿAbd Allāh b. ʿAmr called it the most severe verse concerning Hell's people. — Qatāda transmits this saying through Yaḥyā b. Sallām. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(عبد الله بن عمرو)]

## 35:36/q22 How does this warning connect with the preceding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: The immediately preceding inheriting groups or the earlier promise to reciters.
- 35:36/q22/p1 It turns from believers' reward to disbelievers' fate and confirms that all three inheriting groups enter Paradise. — Wāḥidī says Muḥammad's monotheist community has just been described. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, Kaʿb and Ibn ʿĀshūr use the contrast to support all three groups' entry to Paradise; Ibn ʿĀshūr says rewards differ by time and place and links the clause to 35:33. Qurtubī describes the shift to Hell's people and their speech. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(كعب الأحبار); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 35:36/q22/p2 It is conjoined with the promise to those who recite God's Book. — Rāzī traces the intervening discussion back to the reciters of 35:29 and notes one view that the gardens of Eden in 35:33 refer back to them. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 35:36/q23 How do the condemned respond when they face punishment?  [* · interpretation]
- 35:36/q23/p1 They plead for another chance after ignoring worldly warnings, but their cries bring no relief. — Kur'an Yolu compares their futile plea with 23:107–108; Qurtubī says this passage proceeds to their speech. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 35:36/q24 What broader contrast frames this section of the sūra?  [* · links]
- 35:36/q24/p1 The extended comparison of believers and disbelievers in verses 29–39 leads to God's power versus idols and then the outcomes of disbelief. — Study Quran gives this outline. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 35:36/q25 What does the nearby discussion of provision say about divine favor?  [* · links]
- 35:36/q25/p1 Wealth and children do not prove favor or unworthiness; provision tests gratitude or patience, and success appears in the Hereafter. — Islāḥī says the repeated opening has different force: verse 36 stresses God's control, so people should neither boast nor despair. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 35:36/q26 Does ḍiʿf in the nearby discussion mean exactly double reward?  [* · meaning]
- 35:36/q26/p1 No; the Arabic word should not be restricted to a double reward. — Islāḥī explicitly rejects that restriction. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 35:36/q27 Does perpetual negation require lan rather than lā?  [* · grammar]
- 35:36/q27/p1 No; lā can also express perpetual negation. — Burhān cites 2:255 and 7:40 to reject giving eternal force uniquely to lan. [1 notes: BURHAN]
