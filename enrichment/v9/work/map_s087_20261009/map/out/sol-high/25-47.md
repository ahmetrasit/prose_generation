# 25:47: 25 questions

## 25:47/q01 What does the verse say about who governs night and day?  [وَهُوَ ٱلَّذِى ٱلَّيْلَ ٱلنَّهَارَ · theology]
- 25:47/q01/p1 God alone brings about night and day; the act cannot be shared by other gods. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reads the defined clauses as exclusive agency: Quraysh conceded God's creation, while their partners cannot share an indivisible divine control. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 25:47/q02 How does the wording direct the address and benefit of God's making?  [وَهُوَ ٱلَّذِى جَعَلَ لَكُمُ · grammar]
turns on: The notes discuss two different features of the same opening construction, rather than competing analyses.
- 25:47/q02/p1 The address turns to people, and lakum depends on jaʿala to foreground their benefit. — Al-Ālūsī says the beneficiary phrase precedes both objects to emphasize that the benefits are for people. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it names one purpose, not the only purpose, and notes other ends of night in 25:62 and the turning earth's side away from sunlight. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 25:47/q02/p2 The discourse shifts from first person to third person by iltifāt. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the person shift. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 25:47/q03 Why is night called a garment?  [ٱلَّيْلَ لِبَاسًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the comparison rests on darkness concealing, night permitting rest, or actual bedtime clothing.
- 25:47/q03/p1 Its darkness covers and conceals people or things as clothing covers a wearer. — Ibn Qutayba's Mushkil cites Dhū al-Rummah's night dyeing stones; al-Wāḥidī's Basīṭ cites his فلما لبسن الليل and the Arabists' enveloping darkness. Al-Bursawī relates labs to covering and compares spouses and piety as garments in 2:187 and 7:26. Ibn Kathīr cites 92:1 and 91:4. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a direct metaphor that hides people from one another for private needs. Elmalılı calls night the earth's shadow, shielding from the sun and watchful eyes. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Māwardī, al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī repeat the covering analogy; al-Suddī and al-Jishumī add settled rest. [26 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل العربية والمعاني,ابن عباس,ذو الرمة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 25:47/q03/p2 Night is likened to a garment because it gives repose and stops work. — Muqātil explains night as a time of rest; al-Suddī says creation settles in it; Ibn ʿAbbās says the darkness clothes people so they can rest and cease movement. Al-Wāḥidī cites 10:67 for settling at night; al-Ṭabrisī cites 28:73. [5 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); TABRISI]
- 25:47/q03/p3 The comparison invokes the garments people wear when they sleep. — Al-Jishumī reports the undergarments and outer garments worn at bedtime as another explanation. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 25:47/q04 Does darkness itself satisfy the prayer requirement to cover nakedness?  [ٱلَّيْلَ لِبَاسًۭا · law]
turns on: Whether the requirement concerns being unseen or an act of worship requiring physical covering.
- 25:47/q04/p1 No; bodily covering remains required in darkness. — Ibn al-ʿArabī, reported by al-Qurṭubī, says covering in prayer is worship, not a concession dependent on being unseen. Al-Bursawī says the requirement applies in darkness and light. [2 notes: BURSEVI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)+]
- 25:47/q04/p2 Praying naked in darkness is valid because night covers the body. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this claim only as the view Ibn al-ʿArabī rejects. [0 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)-]

## 25:47/q05 What does subāt mean when sleep is made subāt?  [ٱلنَّوْمَ سُبَاتًۭا · meaning]
turns on: The root's suggested cut, stretch, stillness or swoon, and whether parallel nushūr requires a death sense. Abū Ḥayyān says al-Zamakhsharī's objection to rest works only if nushūr must mean life.
- 25:47/q05/p1 Rest and cessation of movement or work. — Al-Ṭabarī calls it bodily rest and quiet limbs. Ibn Qutayba derives rest from stretching out; Ibn ʿĪsā, al-Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Anbārī, al-Baghawī, al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī derive cessation from a root meaning to cut, with shaving as another use; Sabbath names stopped work, while Ibn al-Jawzī adds the report that creation ended Friday and Israelites were told to rest. Al-Zajjāj specifies stopped movement with the spirit remaining in the body. Abū Muslim cites Sabbath rest and a patient relieved from illness; al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī report him. Al-Qurṭubī cites let-down hair and a lank body for stretching, and Sabbath labor for cutting. Ibn Kathīr says nighttime rest follows tiring daytime activity and rests body and spirit. Al-Bursawī gives Sabbath as stopped work or the end of six creation days. Elmalılı allows both rest and death from suspended activity. Al-Ālūsī reports a claim that God created nothing on Saturday. [35 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري,الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غيره); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI(Ebû Müslim); TABATABAI; KASHSHAF-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)-]
- 25:47/q05/p2 Death, or a deathlike severing of life and awareness. — Muqātil compares unconsciousness to death; al-Naqqāsh says the sleeper cannot reason and is like the dead. Al-Kashshāf calls masbūt one whose life is cut off, citing 6:60; al-Rāzī prefers this because nushūr is its opposite, as does al-Kashshāf. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı preserve the alternative, citing 6:60; al-Ālūsī adds 39:42. Al-Bursawī distinguishes full separation of spirit's light in death from separation only from the body's exterior in sleep; masbūt may mean dead or unconscious. [14 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(النقاش); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري,قيل); ELMALILI; ELMALILI(Sâhib-i Keşşâf)+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 25:47/q05/p3 A swoon or coma to which sleep is likened. — Al-Layth defines it as sleep resembling a swoon. Ibn ʿAṭiyya observes that illness may cause sabat in one awake; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī relay this analogy. Yaḥyā ibn Sallām calls the sleeper inactive and unaware. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT(الليث); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]
- 25:47/q05/p4 Stillness or remaining in one place. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya connects sabt with staying put and settled repose; al-Qurṭubī likewise describes freedom from disturbance and motion. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī report the same lexical connection; al-Biqāʿī includes stillness alongside rest. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]
- 25:47/q05/p5 Deep sleep that allows complete bodily recovery. — Al-Khalīl, as reported by al-Qurṭubī, says God made sleep heavy so the body can fully recover and rest. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الخليل)]

## 25:47/q06 Is sleep a distinct entity or only a condition?  [ٱلنَّوْمَ · theology]
turns on: Whether the wording's apparent treatment of sleep as a thing is ontologically literal.
- 25:47/q06/p1 The surface wording suggests sleep is a distinct entity or meaning. — Al-Jishumī reports this implication of the verse's surface expression. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 25:47/q06/p2 Sleep is forgetfulness and torpor, with rest attached, rather than an entity in itself. — Al-Jishumī reports this as the view of most of his teachers. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أكثر مشايخنا)]

## 25:47/q07 Does nushūr rule out taking subāt as rest?  [ٱلنَّوْمَ سُبَاتًۭا ٱلنَّهَارَ نُشُورًۭا · interpretation]
- 25:47/q07/p1 The objection works only if nushūr must mean life. — Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī's death reading and appeal to 6:60, then limits his argument against rest: it depends on fixing nushūr as life rather than daytime spreading. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]

## 25:47/q08 What physical process or condition is sleep here?  [ٱلنَّوْمَ · interpretation]
turns on: The proposed physical cause and al-Ālūsī's assessment of its reliability.
- 25:47/q08/p1 Sleep is a bodily relaxation and suspension of waking faculties. — Al-Bursawī attributes relaxation of brain nerves to rising moist vapor; al-Ālūsī says vapors usually overpower the faculties and judges the proposed breeze from beneath the Throne scarcely sound. Al-Biqāʿī says it suspends sensation and awareness, a minor death; al-Bursawī distinguishes sleep from death by spirit's light remaining in the body's interior. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 25:47/q08/p2 Sleep comes from a breeze beneath the Throne. — Al-Ālūsī records the proposed cause but considers it scarcely sound. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 25:47/q09 What does sleep do for different kinds of spiritual seekers?  [ٱلنَّوْمَ سُبَاتًۭا · inward]
turns on: The sleeper's spiritual condition and whether sleep, dreams or wakeful discipline best opens perception.
- 25:47/q09/p1 Night gives rest to the heedless, while lovers may stay awake from joy in union or grief in separation. — Al-Qushayrī distinguishes intimacy that prevents sleep from distress that prevents it; their vigil may express either joy or sorrow. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 25:47/q09/p2 A lover may seek sleep to encounter the beloved or a theophany in dreams. — Al-Qushayrī reports a theophany unavailable while awake; one anonymous lover seeks sleep without drowsiness to meet the beloved, another loves sleep after a dream of his heart's joy. Al-Bursawī reports Shāh Shujāʿ avoided sleep for thirty years but then dreamed of God and afterward carried a pillow. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI(قال قائلهم); BURSEVI]
- 25:47/q09/p3 Sleep punishes the heedless but mercifully rests diligent strivers from spiritual effort. — Al-Qushayrī explicitly gives the opposed effects according to the sleeper's spiritual condition. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 25:47/q09/p4 Sleep shuts outward senses, freeing the soul or inner senses to receive unseen meanings. — Al-Bursawī's elders say the heart's senses open when outward senses close. They describe the rational soul as imprisoned in bodily maintenance while awake, returning among spirits in sleep and receiving unseen meanings through images, the basis of dream interpretation. [3 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الكبار)]
- 25:47/q09/p5 Abandoning sleep in spiritual striving can unveil the realm of spirits. — Al-Bursawī's elders say bodily rest opposes spiritual disciplines; sleepless striving dissolves the four elements and opens the heart to the dominion of spirits and longing for God. [2 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الكبار)]

## 25:47/q10 How much sleep does the verse permit?  [ٱلنَّوْمَ سُبَاتًۭا · law]
- 25:47/q10/p1 Only enough to relieve bodily fatigue. — Al-Bursawī derives this limit from sleep being rest for the body. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 25:47/q11 What is the extent and character of daytime here?  [ٱلنَّهَارَ · meaning]
turns on: One note defines the day's bounds; the report counts and describes its hours.
- 25:47/q11/p1 Daylight is spreading light; its ordinary span is sunrise to sunset, and its legal span is dawn to sunset. — Al-Bursawī distinguishes the basic from the legal extent. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 25:47/q11/p2 Daytime unfolds through twelve hours from dawn through morning, noon, afternoon and the last hours before night. — Al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas, reported by al-Durr, associates noon prayer with the sun's decline and cites 17:78. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس)]

## 25:47/q12 What does nushūr mean for daytime?  [ٱلنَّهَارَ نُشُورًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether nushūr names outward dispersal for work, rising from the deathlike sleep, or the spirit's spreading upon waking; the sleep/death parallel and Qurʾānic usage are cited for rising.
- 25:47/q12/p1 People spread out or go about by day to seek provision and attend their needs. — Mujāhid gives spreading out, while Qatāda adds livelihoods, needs and varied tasks; al-Suddī, Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil specify provision. Al-Jishumī contrasts nashr, spreading, with folding and cites al-Aʿshā's dead الناشر for the related rising sense. Al-Wāḥidī says it also denotes the dead person's renewed movement. Abū Muslim, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān allow dispersal for bounty; Ibn Kathīr and al-Bursawī cite 28:73, and al-Ālūsī compares 78:11. [31 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو مسلم,ابن عطية); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 25:47/q12/p2 Day is waking, renewed life or resurrection after sleep. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers waking: the contrast with sleep and root's use for rising from death outweigh mere dispersal; he cites al-Aʿshā and 25:3, and accepts Mujāhid's dispersal as plausible. Ibn ʿAṭiyya connects it with sleep's taking of the soul; al-Nasafī and al-Biqāʿī connect renewed senses with being raised. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Bursawī, Elmalılı and al-Iṣlāḥī make the resurrection analogy; Elmalılı cites 25:3 and 6:60. Al-Ālūsī reports al-Zamakhsharī's view that Qurʾānic nushūr rarely means ordinary spreading, and al-Kashf says the context combines bounty, power and evidence of return. [20 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(الأعشى); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري,الكشف,قيل); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TAB-FULL+]
- 25:47/q12/p3 The soul or spirit spreads through the body upon waking. — Al-Māwardī reports it as an echo of spreading and resurrection; al-Ṭabrisī explicitly says the spirit spreads through the body; Ibn al-Jawzī relays al-Māwardī. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي)]

## 25:47/q13 How is calling day itself nushūr grammatically understood?  [ٱلنَّهَارَ نُشُورًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the intensified attribution is ellipsis with substitution or figurative attribution of an action or participle to day.
- 25:47/q13/p1 An elided genitive and replacement intensify the identification of day with resurrection. — Al-Bursawī gives this analysis for the resurrection reading. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 25:47/q13/p2 Day is identified with its action for emphasis, or nushūr is an active participle figuratively attributed to day. — Al-Ālūsī supplies both alternatives, instead of merely calling day a time for the action. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 25:47/q14 Why is jaʿala repeated and what is omitted in the three clauses?  [جَعَلَ لَكُمُ وَجَعَلَ ٱلنَّهَارَ · grammar]
turns on: The notes explain different grammatical and rhetorical effects of the repeated verb and paired predicates.
- 25:47/q14/p1 Jaʿala takes two objects, and its repetition reinforces the construction. — Al-Naḥḥās says wa-l-nahāra nushūran would be possible outside this Qurʾānic wording. [1 notes: NAHHAS]
- 25:47/q14/p2 The first two benefits form one contrast to daytime activity, so repeating jaʿala suits visible emergence or resurrection. — Al-Ālūsī contrasts the formulation with Sūra 78's phrasing of night and day, citing 78:10–11. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 25:47/q14/p3 Reciprocal implication omits what can be inferred: rest implies movement, and resurrection implies preceding stillness. — Al-Biqāʿī describes this as ellipsis, with each named state implying its counterpart. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 25:47/q15 Why does concealment precede sleep in this verse?  [ٱلَّيْلَ لِبَاسًۭا ٱلنَّوْمَ سُبَاتًۭا · rhetoric]
- 25:47/q15/p1 Night's concealment comes first to continue the preceding shadow image. — Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts 78:8–10, where sleep comes first because it matters more there and follows the creation of spouses. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 25:47/q16 How do night and day in this verse connect with nearby verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes give complementary links with preceding shadow imagery and the broader appeal to reason.
- 25:47/q16/p1 The move from shadow to night and day develops one continuous sign of God's power and favor. — Al-Biqāʿī says the preceding shadow sign leads to explicit proof and favor here, then the discourse passes from meanings of creation, removal, death and life to visible things and remote causes. Al-Ālūsī calls night earth's shadow. Ibn ʿĀshūr says shadow and night both follow sunlight and that the earlier shrinking shadow figures unbelief's darkness contracting before guidance's sun. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī matches covering, pause and renewed activity with shadow's extension, solar guide and withdrawal in 25:45–46. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 25:47/q16/p2 The passage calls people to reason from nature's order to God and guidance. — Kur'an Yolu says the appeal follows condemnation of those who deify desire and abandon reason; it invokes nature and divine laws. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 25:47/q17 Which other verses use comparable night, sleep and day imagery?  [* · links]
- 25:47/q17/p1 Other verses describe night as rest or covering and day as active life. — Al-Wāḥidī's Muqātil report cites 10:67 for settling at night; al-Ṭabrisī cites 28:73 for repose; Ibn Kathīr cites 92:1 and 91:4 for night enveloping existence and 28:73 for activity; the Study Quran cites 78:9–11 as a parallel triad. Al-Ālūsī compares 78:10–11's different arrangement; al-Bursawī compares 28:73 for livelihood. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 25:47/q18 What does the alternation of night, sleep and day teach?  [* · interpretation]
- 25:47/q18/p1 It displays God's power, mercy, wise provision and blessings for human life. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī stress night concealment's many religious and worldly benefits; al-Nasafī calls life a lesson. Abū Ḥayyān cites a poet on darkness's favors against Manichaean dualism. Al-Biqāʿī calls sleep a minor death with countless benefits. Al-Ālūsī says the direct address heightens the reminder of favor. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites earth's rotation and sunlight, rest, vigor, concealment, work and sight. Al-Iṣlāḥī names providence, mercy, power, wisdom, monotheism and the hereafter, linking 25:82–87. Al-Wāḥidī notes restful sleep and useful daylight. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشاعر); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 25:47/q19 How does sleep and waking point to death and resurrection?  [ٱلنَّوْمَ سُبَاتًۭا ٱلنَّهَارَ نُشُورًۭا · interpretation]
- 25:47/q19/p1 Sleep resembles death and waking resembles resurrection, making the daily cycle a sign of the return to life. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Bursawī and al-Iṣlāḥī use the analogy. Al-Rāzī cites 6:60 and 39:42 for the taking of souls; the Study Quran adds the proverb that sleep is death's brother. Al-Ṭabarī's preferred nushūr reading rests on sleep's resemblance to death; al-Wāḥidī says night interrupts the sleeper like death. Elmalılı says morning resumes stopped activity and connects this with 25:3 and 6:60; he adds that those who do not hope for resurrection do not desire renewed life. Al-Ālūsī's al-Kashf says the passage couples favor and power with evidence for return. [16 notes: TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(المثنوي); ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(some)]

## 25:47/q20 What saying of Luqmān illustrates the sleep and resurrection analogy?  [ٱلنَّوْمَ سُبَاتًۭا ٱلنَّهَارَ نُشُورًۭا · reports]
- 25:47/q20/p1 He tells his son that as one sleeps and awakens, one dies and is raised. — The report is cited by al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī and al-Bursawī; wording varies slightly between sleep reminding of death and waking illustrating resurrection. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(لقمان); RAZI-FULL(لقمان); BAYDAWI-FULL(لقمان); NASAFI-FULL(لقمان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(لقمان); BIQAI-FULL(لقمان); BURSEVI(لقمان عليه السلام)]

## 25:47/q21 What waking prayer is cited for this verse?  [ٱلنَّهَارَ نُشُورًۭا · reports]
- 25:47/q21/p1 The Prophet praised God for reviving people after causing them to die and affirmed that resurrection is to Him. — Al-Ṭabarī calls the prayer soundly transmitted; al-Qurṭubī also reports that the Prophet said it on waking. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي)]

## 25:47/q22 What does the verse mean in an inward reading of night, sleep and day?  [* · inward]
- 25:47/q22/p1 Night is the soul's heedless darkness, sleep is separation from true life, and day is spirit's light reviving the heart. — Al-Qāshānī says night veils people from witnessing the Real, attributes and Essence; the awakened heart spreads into holiness after sensory sleep. He cites the saying attributed to the Prophet that people are asleep and awaken when they die. [4 notes: QASHANI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 25:47/q23 What do the cited literary examples teach about recognizing subtle expression?  [* · rhetoric]
- 25:47/q23/p1 Fine verbal effects require receptive taste and perceptive judgment to recognize and explain. — Al-Jurjānī praises poetic deletion and indefiniteness in a line about a gaze and greetings; al-Buḥturī's لي عليك دموع works through word order and indefiniteness; he also praises a star image's craft. He says sensitivity is rare, a writer may succeed without realizing it or miss failures, and an unqualified confident critic is dangerous while one aware of limits is rational. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports his view that rhetorical merit can be hidden and spiritual, illustrated with poets al-Buḥturī, Abū Nuwās and Ismāʿīl ibn Yasār rather than Qurʾānic citations. [9 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL(البحتري); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(عبد القاهر الجرجاني)]

## 25:47/q24 How does one expression pair night and day with devotional acts?  [ٱلَّيْلَ ٱلنَّهَارَ · rhetoric]
- 25:47/q24/p1 It pairs a sleeping night with a fasting day. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives the expression; Abū Ḥayyān repeats the pairing in his discussion of night and day. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 25:47/q25 What revelation placement is reported for this sūra?  [* · sciences]
- 25:47/q25/p1 A minority calls most of the sūra Madinan but verses 68–70 Makkan. — The Study Quran records the minority classification. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(A minority)]
