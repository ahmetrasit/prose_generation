# 81:10: 13 questions

## 81:10/q01 What are the ṣuḥuf?  [ٱلصُّحُفُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pages are deeds records, separate pages announcing recompense, a metaphor for enumeration, or inward pages of the faculties and souls.
- 81:10/q01/p1 The records of people's deeds, written by angels and opened for reckoning. — Muqātil calls each person's record a book delivered by guardian angels. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Māwardī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Biqāʿī specify good and evil deeds; al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī say angels wrote them. Al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī call them the records of Adam's children. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows actual pages unlike familiar pages. Al-Iṣlāḥī cites 81:14; Study Quran cites 18:49, 17:71, 50:17–18, 69:19–29 and 82:11–12 for the books and their contents. [33 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 81:10/q01/p2 Pages of recompense flying to their recipients, distinguished from the scrolls of deeds. — Abū Ḥayyān explicitly distinguishes these pages from deeds scrolls. Marthad ibn Wadāʿah, as reported by al-Kashshāf and al-Ālūsī, says they fly from beneath the Throne; the believer's page describes a high garden and the unbeliever's scorching wind and hot water. Al-Qurṭubī transmits the flight and the believer's receipt. Ibn ʿAṭiyya records the right and left according to recompense; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī give distribution as an alternative. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(مرثد بن وداعة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مرثد بن وداعة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(مرثد بن وداعة)]
- 81:10/q01/p3 A metaphor for whatever enumerates people's deeds. — Ibn ʿĀshūr presents this alongside the possibility of actual, unfamiliar pages. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 81:10/q01/p4 The pages of the faculties and souls that contain deeds. — Al-Qāshānī says they are folded at death when the spirit's sun is rolled up and opened at resurrection and return to the body. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 81:10/q02 What does nushirat describe happening to the scrolls?  [نُشِرَتْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether nashr is the opening of folded records for reckoning or their distribution and flight to recipients.
- 81:10/q02/p1 Records folded at death are spread open on Resurrection Day for their owners to read and for judgment. — Qatāda says the filled or dictated record is folded then opened; Muqātil, Ibn Jurayj, al-Māwardī, al-Qurṭubī and others locate folding at death and opening at resurrection or reckoning. Al-Farrāʾ and al-Baghawī compare the spread pages of 74:52; Ibn ʿĀshūr defines nashr as undoing a scroll's folds to read its writing and compares 74:52 and 17:13. Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī cite 18:49 for each owner's seeing the contents. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk says each receives a record in the right or left hand; Muqātil and al-Zajjāj likewise mention guardian angels or the hand corresponding to deeds. Al-Biqāʿī says the blessed receive theirs before them in the right hand, the wretched behind their backs in the left. Küran Yolu adds that all good and evil deeds are recalled and judged, citing 69:19–28, 17:13–14 and 18:49. [33 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الضحاك,قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 81:10/q02/p2 The pages are distributed or fly to their recipients, right or left according to their recompense. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī report distribution as an alternative. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports pages flying right and left according to recompense; Abū Ḥayyān calls these distinct from deed scrolls. Marthad's report in al-Kashshāf, al-Qurṭubī and al-Ālūsī places their flight from beneath the Throne, with the believer receiving a page of high garden and, in the fuller versions, the unbeliever one of scorching wind and hot water. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(مرثد بن وداعة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مرثد بن وداعة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(مرثد بن وداعة)]

## 81:10/q03 How is nushirat read, and what does each form suggest?  [نُشِرَتْ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted vocalizations, the force of 74:52's munashshara, plural morphology, and whether doubling conveys repetition, plurality or intensity; al-Naḥḥās insists both forms are sound.
- 81:10/q03/p1 With a light shīn, nushirat. — Al-Ṭabarī names most Medinans and some Kufans; al-Thaʿlabī names Medina, Syria and Basra except Abū ʿAmr; al-Māwardī says the other readers. Later lists include Nāfiʿ, ʿĀṣim, Ibn ʿĀmir, Abū Jaʿfar, Shayba, al-Aʿraj, al-Ḥasan, Abū Rajāʾ, Qatāda, Yaʿqūb and Sahl, with differences in attribution: al-Jishumī reports Abū ʿAmr, Nāfiʿ, Ibn ʿĀmir and ʿĀṣim here and an alternative report involving Yaʿqūb; Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim leaves this verb undoubled while doubling sajjirat; al-Jishumī's other report puts Ibn Kathīr and Abū ʿAmr in the doubled group. Al-Farrāʾ says ordinary Arabic manshūra and plural usage permit the light form; al-Naḥḥās says grammarians allow it for few or many and rejects making 74:52's munashshara decisive against it. Al-Māwardī takes the light form as unfolding once. [18 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(قراء المدينة وبعض الكوفيين); NAHHAS(أبو جعفر، شيبة، نافع؛ أبو عمرو، ابن كثير، يحيى، الأعمش، حمزة، الكسائي); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الباقون); JISHUMI(أبو بكر عن عاصم,أبو عمرو ونافع وابن عامر وعاصم,يعقوب); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المدينة والشام وعاصم ويعقوب); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع، ابن عامر، عاصم، أبو جعفر، شيبة، الأعرج، الحسن، أبو رجاء، قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(نافع وعاصم وأبو جعفر وابن عامر ويعقوب); NASAFI-FULL(مدنى وشامى وعاصم وسهل ويعقوب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو رجاء وقتادة والحسن والأعرج وشيبة وأبو جعفر ونافع وابن عامر وعاصم); SAMIN-DURR(الأخوان وابن كثير وأبو عمرو); NASHR(المدنيان وابن عامر ويعقوب وعاصم); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 81:10/q03/p2 With a doubled shīn, nushshirat. — Al-Ṭabarī names some Meccans and most Kufans; al-Māwardī names Ḥamzah and al-Kisāʾī; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Samīn, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī also name Ibn Kathīr and Abū ʿAmr. Al-Jishumī reports Ibn Kathīr and Abū ʿAmr doubling this verb while leaving the neighbouring sajjirat and suʿʿirat undoubled, though his reports differ. Al-Farrāʾ and al-Ṭabarī invoke 74:52's munashshara and the plural's capacity for doubled forms; al-Ṭabarī contrasts singular usage. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the doubling emphasis. Al-Māwardī takes it as repeated unfolding; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī say emphasis, many pages or forceful dispersal; Ibn ʿĀshūr says many scrolls; Qiraat-ER gives thorough, repeated or successive spreading as possible Form II senses. [21 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(بعض قراء مكة وعامة قراء الكوفة); NAHHAS(أبو جعفر، شيبة، نافع؛ أبو عمرو، ابن كثير، يحيى، الأعمش، حمزة، الكسائي); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); JISHUMI(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو,يعقوب); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المدينة والشام وعاصم ويعقوب); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير، أبو عمرو، حمزة، الكسائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(نافع وعاصم وأبو جعفر وابن عامر ويعقوب); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي); NASAFI-FULL(مدنى وشامى وعاصم وسهل ويعقوب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو رجاء وقتادة والحسن والأعرج وشيبة وأبو جعفر ونافع وابن عامر وعاصم); SAMIN-DURR(الأخوان وابن كثير وأبو عمرو); NASHR(المدنيان وابن عامر ويعقوب وعاصم); ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي); IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER(Vr); TAB-FULL+]

## 81:10/q04 What does the opened record reveal or demand of its owner?  [ٱلصُّحُفُ نُشِرَتْ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes give complementary functions of disclosure, proof, repeated review or emotional effect, and moral warning; al-Māwardī ties the alternatives to once versus repeated unfolding.
- 81:10/q04/p1 It makes every deed visible and establishes the basis of reckoning and proof. — Al-Māwardī says a single unfolding establishes proof and cites 18:49 for the owner seeing the contents; al-Qurṭubī cites the same verse. The Prophet's answer to Umm Salamah describes records containing dust- and mustard-seed weights. Küran Yolu says people see and recall good and evil before judgment, with 69:19–28, 17:13–14 and 18:49; Study Quran says no deed is hidden and cites 18:49 and other record passages. Al-Iṣlāḥī connects the display of every deed to 81:14. Al-Biqāʿī says the record will save or ruin its owner. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; THALABI(أم سلمة); KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 81:10/q04/p2 Repeated unfolding intensifies rebuke for sinners and glad tidings for the obedient. — Al-Māwardī offers this as one explanation of the doubled reading's repeated unfolding. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 81:10/q04/p3 Repeated unfolding lets the person and angel witnesses review the record repeatedly. — Al-Māwardī offers this as a second explanation of repeated unfolding. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 81:10/q04/p4 The impending record calls people to guard what they write in it. — Qatāda, reported by al-Kashshāf and Ibn Kathīr, tells each person to watch what is written. Al-Biqāʿī says to put only good words, deeds and beliefs into the record because it will save or ruin its owner. ʿUmar says on reciting the verse that the matter is carried to God, son of Adam. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(عمر,قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL]

## 81:10/q05 What did the Prophet tell Umm Salamah about the Resurrection and the records?  [ٱلصُّحُفُ نُشِرَتْ · reports]
- 81:10/q05/p1 People rise barefoot and naked, but are too occupied by the spread records, containing even minute deeds, to attend to women. — Al-Thaʿlabī transmits the resurrection statement and Umm Salamah's question separately; al-Kashshāf gives the combined exchange. The records include dust- and mustard-seed weights. [3 notes: THALABI(أم سلمة,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي)]

## 81:10/q06 How does the opening of the records fit the sequence and message of the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes connect the sign either to the immediately preceding questioning or to the larger arc from cosmic signs and the previous sūra to final judgment.
- 81:10/q06/p1 It begins the signs of each person's coming bliss or punishment after the preceding universal questioning. — Al-Biqāʿī explicitly places the opening of records after the universal questioning. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the listed events descriptions of the Day of Resurrection. Al-Iṣlāḥī places exposed records among the post-resurrection sorting, justice for the oppressed, reddening sky, Hell, Paradise and each person's seeing what was prepared. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 81:10/q06/p2 The sequence announces the Hour and culminates in each soul knowing its deeds at final judgment. — Al-Biqāʿī relates the cosmic signs here to the flight from relatives in 80:33–34 and says the sūra answers when the Hour will come. He also relates the warning to ingratitude in 80:17 and denial of the Qur'an, described as a reminder on honored, pure pages borne by trusted angels. Elmalılı reports Abū ʿĀliya's division into six worldly signs from sun through seas and six afterlife signs from paired souls through approaching Paradise; Elmalılı says their culmination is the great resurrection. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI(Ebû Âliye)]

## 81:10/q07 Why does each sign have its own conditional opening?  [وَإِذَا · rhetoric]
- 81:10/q07/p1 Each repeated condition draws attention to a distinct awesome event, while one answer belongs to the conditions together. — Elmalılı says a single opening condition could have sufficed, but repetition gives each event its own force and the answer is attached collectively. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 81:10/q08 Can the signs also describe a person's lesser resurrection at death?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether applying the sequence to death is a compatible secondary analogy or an illicit replacement of its apparent account of the great resurrection.
- 81:10/q08/p1 They can yield a secondary analogy to death while their primary subject remains the great resurrection. — Elmalılı reports interpreters applying the events to the lesser resurrection and Nīsābūrī presenting the apparent sense first. He says the details of resurrection remain unknown, so possible metaphors help imagine the great event through the certain lesser one and strengthen faith; such reflections do not deny the final resurrection and culminate in it. He cites 3:7 and 59:2. Al-Qāshānī specifically reads the books as faculties' and souls' pages, folded at death when the spirit's sun is rolled up and opened when the body is restored. [6 notes: QASHANI; ELMALILI(Nizâmüddîn Nîsâbûrî,أهل التأويل); ELMALILI+]
- 81:10/q08/p2 The signs allegorize the human being and circumstances of death, with the sun as self and stars as senses. — Elmalılı attributes this view to a group cited by Ibn ʿAṭiyya; the transmitted examples concern the sequence of signs. [1 notes: ELMALILI(قوم)]
- 81:10/q08/p3 Such symbolic treatment is a Bāṭinī or extreme Sufi departure from clear Arabic exegesis. — Elmalılı reports Abū Ḥayyān's charge that the group's symbolic reading is a cover for covert rejection of Islam, and his broader denial of inner symbols. Elmalılı rejects the severity of the polemic in separate notes. [2 notes: ELMALILI(Ebû Hayyân); ELMALILI-]

## 81:10/q09 What bounds should govern inward or symbolic interpretation of this passage?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether clear Arabic excludes all inner indications, permits those consistent with the apparent text, or is displaced by interpretations that annul decisive meanings and rulings.
- 81:10/q09/p1 Keep the clear Arabic and apparent sense primary, while accepting compatible inward indications and insights. — Elmalılı says the text is clear Arabic, normally read in its apparent sense unless context prevents it; it also has difficult and ambiguous passages and figurative, implicit and rhetorical forms. He affirms outward and inward meaning, tafsīr and taʾwīl, citing reports of outward, inward, limit and ascent. Their agreement follows from 4:82; he compares their meeting to the bounded seas of 55:19–20 and the inexhaustibility of insights to 18:109. Qur'anic address suits differing capacities, with 6:99, 13:4, 2:230, 10:24, 6:126, 6:98, 2:118, 10:6 and 3:190; 3:18 supports learned justice. Compatible Sufi commentaries can retain outward rulings, and attention to intellectual and moral levels serves illumination. [22 notes: ELMALILI(آثار); ELMALILI+]
- 81:10/q09/p2 Clear Arabic admits no inner symbols, riddles or philosophical and naturalist allusions in exegesis. — Abū Ḥayyān, as quoted by Elmalılı, cites 26:195 for clear Arabic; he censures al-Rāzī's use of philosophers, astrologers and astronomers and Sufi statements called 'truths' in Taḥrīr and Taḥbīr. He calls the symbolic reading Bāṭinī or extreme Sufi. Elmalılı calls that polemic excessive. [4 notes: ELMALILI(Ebû Hayyân); ELMALILI-]
- 81:10/q09/p3 Distortive esotericism abandons decisive passages and cancels their apparent meanings and rulings. — Elmalılı rejects the method associated with Ḥasan Ṣabbāḥ and extreme Imāmite books, distinguishing al-Qāshānī and ʿArāʾis from it. He cites 3:7 against pursuing ambiguous passages for discord. His claim that extreme literalism and esotericism both harm interpretation also invokes 3:18. [4 notes: ELMALILI]

## 81:10/q10 What distinctions does al-Jurjānī make among compound similes?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The first likeness depends on a full arrangement of components; the second on a familiar relation between paired things.
- 81:10/q10/p1 One kind requires all components in a particular arrangement for the likeness to hold. — Al-Jurjānī says detailed similes have many common patterns and their finest distinctions resist complete enumeration. His example likens narcissus to pearl bowls containing agate; removing a component or changing its relation destroys the intended likeness. These notes state a general rhetorical principle and do not explicitly apply it to this verse. [3 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 81:10/q10/p2 Another kind compares the configuration of paired things rather than either thing alone. — Al-Jurjānī compares dawn beneath night to a gray horse beneath its covering. The paired configuration is observed and familiar, not merely imagined. These notes do not explicitly identify such a simile in this verse. [2 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]

## 81:10/q11 What does the adjacent sign about the sky say?  [* · links]
- 81:10/q11/p1 The sky is uncovered and stripped from what lies above it. — Al-Rāzī explains kushitat as removal like stripping an animal's skin or taking away a cover, exposing Paradise and God's Throne. He reports Ibn Masʿūd's qushitat; al-Farrāʾ glosses it as stripping then folding and notes the common k/q alternation. These notes concern the neighbouring sky sign. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 81:10/q12 What does al-Jurjānī say enables someone to judge Qur'anic rhetoric and inimitability?  [* · rhetoric]
- 81:10/q12/p1 A receptive taste and willingness to follow an argument make its rhetorical force intelligible. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports al-Jurjānī's al-Risāla al-Shāfiya: a person without the faculty may wrongly assume competence, as with judging poetry; attentive listeners can move from resistance to acceptance. These notes concern rhetorical judgment generally and do not explicitly apply it to this verse. [3 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(عبد القاهر الجرجاني)]

## 81:10/q13 What challenge to earlier writing on Qur'anic inimitability is reported?  [* · reports]
- 81:10/q13/p1 Ibn Ḥazm challenged earlier writers and sharply criticized al-Bāqillānī. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ places Ibn Ḥazm's challenge in the fifth century; the note does not connect it directly to this verse. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن حزم الظاهري)]
