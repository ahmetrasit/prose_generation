# 74:42: 16 questions

## 74:42/q01 What does سلككم mean here?  [سَلَكَكُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether salakakum is explained as entry into Saqar or, in the setting of the question, continued detention there.
- 74:42/q01/p1 It means brought, admitted, or inserted you into Saqar, the Fire. — Ibn ʿAtiyya cites Abū Wajza al-Saʿdī's verse about limbs entering a hide. Al-Qurṭubī and Bursevī compare threading something into something else, especially a needle; Bursevī derives it from aslaka, not sulūk meaning walking. Ibn ʿĀshūr says its original sense is insertion among other parts, like threading beads, and its use here is metaphorical; he cites 15:12 and 72:17. Ibn al-Jawzī's Nuzha lists entry as a Qurʾānic sense. Al-Biqāʿī stresses insertion through a narrow opening. Elmalılı specifies the offense that caused entry. [15 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:42/q01/p2 It asks what detained or kept them in Saqar. — Muqātil gives this as the force of the question. Al-Rāzī uses the same force while describing Saqar as a depth of the Fire and the question as a rebuke. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL]

## 74:42/q02 How is the sound sequence in سلككم recited?  [سَلَكَكُمْ · readings]
turns on: Assimilation of the adjacent kāfs versus separate pronunciation.
- 74:42/q02/p1 Abū ʿAmr assimilates the first kāf into the next. — Al-Samīn compares the assimilation with manāsikakum in 2:200; Bursevī specifies the first kāf as the assimilated consonant. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); BURSEVI]
- 74:42/q02/p2 The other readers pronounce both kāfs distinctly. — Bursevī explicitly contrasts their pronunciation with Abū ʿAmr's assimilation. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 74:42/q03 How unusual is the adjacent kāf sequence in سلككم?  [سَلَكَكُمْ · grammar]
- 74:42/q03/p1 A word has two adjacent kāfs without an intervening letter only here and in مناسككم. — Al-Burhān and al-Itqān both identify these two forms as the only cases they cite. [2 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN]

## 74:42/q04 How does the direct address ما سلككم fit the preceding account of asking about criminals?  [مَا سَلَكَكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the second-person clause is a direct question, reported speech with omitted words, or an explanation of reciprocal or collective asking; Ibn ʿĀshūr also gives two attachments for the preceding garden phrase.
- 74:42/q04/p1 The people of the right ask the criminals directly; ʿan in the preceding phrase may be extra, or this clause directly states their question. — Al-Rāzī and the analysis reported by al-Nasafī treat ʿan as extra. Al-Ālūsī allows the question to explain the asking without an implied 'saying.' Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the second-person address to imply the sinners were asked, not a grammatical shift. Elmalılı, following Kashshāf, says an exchange among the righteous would use 'them,' not 'you.' [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI(Keşşâf)+]
- 74:42/q04/p2 The clause quotes what the questioners said to the criminals, with an unspoken saying or exchange supplied from context. — Al-Kashshāf as reported by al-Rāzī, al-Samīn and al-Nasafī says the sinners recount the question and their reply. Al-Samīn and Bursevī make an omitted 'saying' circumstantial; al-Ālūsī offers that parse. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows some of the questioned sinners to tell companions what they answered. Al-Rāzī says such omitted elements are frequent in Qurʾānic expression. Al-Bayḍāwī says the question introduces the criminals' narrated answer. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI(Keşşâf); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 74:42/q04/p3 They first asked where the criminals were, then addressed them on seeing them. — Al-Rāzī supplies the intervening inquiry and encounter as a further possible ellipsis. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 74:42/q04/p4 The reciprocal form can mean the people of the right ask one another about the sinners, before the second-person question is explained. — Ibn ʿĀshūr accepts a genuinely reciprocal use. Al-Ālūsī finds this less strained than making the wording quoted speech; Elmalılı's Kashshāf argument objects that their mutual question would say 'them,' not 'you.' [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; ELMALILI(Keşşâf)-]
- 74:42/q04/p5 The reciprocal-looking plural can instead mean multiple or repeated questioners acting from one side. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the object is omitted and understood from 'criminals': they ask why the criminals entered Saqar. His report of al-Zamakhsharī compares collective 'we called' with singular 'I called,' and analogous expressions for throwing and moon sighting; 4:1 is cited. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 74:42/q04/p6 The words report what angels asked the criminals, as related by the blessed. — Al-Ālūsī says the developed answer makes the reported encounter more convincing. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 74:42/q04/p7 The preceding 'in gardens' can attach to 'ask one another,' with asking as a circumstantial clause. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says 'in gardens' is fronted for emphasis and 'asking' describes the people of the right. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:42/q04/p8 Alternatively, 'in gardens' completes an omitted 'they are in gardens'; the asking is circumstantial to its omitted pronoun. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this an explanatory resumption. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:42/q05 Who are the criminals addressed in the question?  [سَلَكَكُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the condemned are identified as unbelievers/polytheists or as believers who failed to act on revelation.
- 74:42/q05/p1 They are polytheists or unbelievers in Saqar. — Al-Baghawī identifies the criminals as polytheists. Muqātil says believers question the unbelievers left in the Fire after monotheists have been removed. The angel-mediated account reported by al-Qurṭubī also calls them polytheists. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 74:42/q05/p2 They are fellow believers who failed to follow revelation and face this predicament. — The Study Quran attributes this interpretation to JJ. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(JJ)]

## 74:42/q06 Who asks the criminals what put them in Saqar?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the saved themselves, sinless children in their number, or angels in a mediated exchange address the people in Saqar.
- 74:42/q06/p1 The people of Paradise or the people of the right ask those in Hell directly. — Al-Ṭabrisī calls the question a rebuke. Al-Qurṭubī reports al-Kalbī's more specific picture of one person in Paradise addressing a named person in Hell. Islahi says the damned then openly confess; the Study Quran reports Q and Ṭ for direct questioning. Elmalılı allows a direct question or one through an intermediary. [7 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي); BURSEVI(يروى); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ)]
- 74:42/q06/p2 The questioners are sinless children among the people of the right. — Al-Farrāʾ prefers a report attributed to ʿAlī: children had no deeds to bind them, and their question about sinners supports the identification. Al-Naḥḥās says the speakers did not sin; al-Qurṭubī reports al-Farrāʾ's inference that children do not know sins. [3 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); FARRA(علي)+]
- 74:42/q06/p3 Believers ask angels about relatives, and the angels ask the polytheists. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this as an alternative mediated exchange; al-Ālūsī likewise reports angels as the immediate questioners whose words the blessed relate. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 74:42/q07 What additional words are reported in readings of the question?  [* · readings]
turns on: Different transmitted explanatory additions and their attribution; Ibn al-Anbārī excludes the 'so-and-so' gloss from Qurʾān text.
- 74:42/q07/p1 A reading inserts an explanatory 'so-and-so' before asking what put that person in Saqar. — Al-Naḥḥās says Ibn ʿUyayna's transmission through ʿAmr ibn Dīnār from Ibn al-Zubayr is sound. Al-Qurṭubī and al-Ālūsī also name ʿUmar alongside Ibn al-Zubayr. Al-Qurṭubī quotes Ibn al-Anbārī that this explanatory gloss is not Qurʾān. [3 notes: NAHHAS(ابن الزبير); QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن الزبير وعمر)]
- 74:42/q07/p2 Ibn Masʿūd is reported to have read an address, 'O disbelievers,' before the question. — Al-Ālūsī transmits the wording. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 74:42/q08 What is the question meant to do to its hearers?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes give rebuke, astonishment, and eliciting a confession for gratitude and warning as possible functions; these can overlap.
- 74:42/q08/p1 It rebukes and humiliates the damned, deepening their remorse. — Al-Ṭabrisī calls it a rebuke from Paradise to Hell. Al-Rāzī says it adds humiliation; Bursevī says it intensifies regret. Al-Ālūsī says the askers likely know the cause already because all truth will be disclosed that day. [5 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 74:42/q08/p2 It can express astonishment at their presence in Saqar. — Al-Biqāʿī offers astonishment as another force of the question. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 74:42/q08/p3 It elicits the damned person's own account, increasing the saved person's gratitude and warning listeners. — Al-Biqāʿī says the saved learn the account from the condemned themselves. Bursevī also calls it a warning to hearers; al-Bayḍāwī says it introduces the narrated answer, and Islahi says the damned openly confess. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:42/q09 How do the saved come to see and speak with the damned?  [* · reports]
- 74:42/q09/p1 God reveals the damned burning in Hell to the saved, who then ask them. — Al-Biqāʿī says believers initially do not know the damned person's state because each is preoccupied with himself; God shows their enemies to increase their joy, citing 37:51 and 83:34. Bursevī transmits a report that God shows Paradise's inhabitants the people of Hell while they are there, prompting the question. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(يروى)]
- 74:42/q09/p2 The question is an instance of communication between inhabitants of Garden and Fire. — The Study Quran says such communication occurs at least sometimes, and cites 7:44–50 and 57:13–15. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 74:42/q10 How is the sinners' answer about what brought them to Saqar explained?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The positions explain different parts of the reply rather than dispute a single reading; al-Rāzī specifies legal obligations, while Ibn Kathīr states the broader religious and social failures.
- 74:42/q10/p1 Their failure to pray and feed the poor means omission of obligatory prayer and zakat. — Al-Rāzī reasons that punishment cannot attach to omitting merely voluntary acts. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 74:42/q10/p2 Their failure to pray and feed the poor means they neither worshipped God nor did good to people. — Ibn Kathīr sets the two omissions alongside duties to God and fellow humans. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 74:42/q10/p3 Joining those who delved means engaging in falsehood. — Al-Rāzī explains the third confession this way. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 74:42/q10/p4 The coming of 'certainty' means death, until which they denied resurrection. — Al-Rāzī cites 15:99 for the sense of certainty as death. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 74:42/q11 Does the reply assign all four confessed faults to each condemned person?  [* · interpretation]
- 74:42/q11/p1 Its apparent wording assigns all four faults to each member of the condemned groups. — Al-Rāzī explicitly draws this inference from the wording. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 74:42/q12 Why is denial of judgment placed last among the offenses?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the ordering chiefly stresses persistence in denial or a progression from neglected practices to disbelief.
- 74:42/q12/p1 Its last place stresses that the criminals persisted in this worst fault after the preceding three. — Al-Rāzī calls denial of judgment the worst fault and cites 90:17. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 74:42/q12/p2 The sequence moves from lack of Muslim practice to the graver sin of disbelief and denial of Judgment. — Abū Ḥayyān compares the progression with 90:11 and 90:17. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 74:42/q13 Are unbelievers punished for neglecting subsidiary religious duties?  [* · law]
- 74:42/q13/p1 Yes; the verse supports their punishment for omitting subsidiary rules of religious law. — Al-Rāzī reports his school's argument and refers detailed treatment to al-Maḥṣūl; his interpretation of prayer and feeding as obligations supports it. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا)]

## 74:42/q14 What does the passage teach about whose deeds can save or condemn a soul?  [* · theology]
- 74:42/q14/p1 Each soul is held for its own deeds; no other's deeds or intercession free it or avert its Hell. — Islahi draws this from the passage's contrast between the people of the right and those held for their crimes. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:42/q15 How does this exchange serve the sura's warning about the Hereafter?  [* · links]
- 74:42/q15/p1 The Qurʾān warns people to prepare for the Hereafter; evaders have only stubbornness as an excuse. — Islahi says manifestations of nature argue for the Hereafter and expose heedlessness, while the warning gives people a chance to prepare. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:42/q16 What does Anas's report say about the condemned person's lost chance to avoid Hell?  [* · reports]
- 74:42/q16/p1 The lowest-ranking person in Hell would ransom himself with the earth's weight in gold, though he had failed to ask God for much easier help and forgiveness. — In Anas's prophetic report, God recalls those earlier requests that the person never made or feared; the report says no one is worse than him and relates to 74:42–48. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
