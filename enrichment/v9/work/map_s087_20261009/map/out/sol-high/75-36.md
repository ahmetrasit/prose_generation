# 75:36: 13 questions

## 75:36/q01 How is أيحسب read?  [أَيَحْسَبُ · readings]
- 75:36/q01/p1 The imperfect stem is read with i rather than a, without changing the meaning of ḥ-s-b. — QIRAAT-ER records the vw vowel variant as a living recitation pattern. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(vw)]

## 75:36/q02 What force does the question أيحسب have?  [أَيَحْسَبُ · rhetoric]
- 75:36/q02/p1 It repudiates and rebukes the assumption that people can be left without purpose. — Jishumi calls the initial alif an interrogative of disapproval and denial; Ibn ʿAtiyya calls the question a rebuke. Biqaʿi says it exposes deficient reasoning. Al-Alusi calls it repudiating; Ibn ʿAshur compares the denial-form question at 75:3; Tabatabaʾi calls it rebuking. [7 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 75:36/q03 Who is الإنسان here?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the passage targets Abu Jahl in particular or states a general rebuke, as Jishumi explicitly weighs.
- 75:36/q03/p1 It refers to Abu Jahl ibn Hisham. — Muqatil applies the passage through the end of the sura to Abu Jahl. Al-Wahidi attributes this identification to the commentators; Jishumi attributes it to Ibn ʿAbbas and Mujahid but favors the general sense. Ibn ʿAtiyya attributes it to most interpreters; Tabrisī attributes it to Ibn ʿAbbas and Mujahid. [6 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(جماعة المفسرين); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور المتأولين); TABRISI(ابن عباس ومجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 75:36/q03/p2 It refers to humankind generally, or to the disbelieving person as a representative case. — Jishumi expressly calls the general reference stronger. Tabari and Nasafi speak of the disbeliever; Tabrisī presents a general rebuke to an unbeliever denying resurrection. Biqaʿi portrays the denier acting as though he had no master or duties. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; JISHUMI+]

## 75:36/q04 What does يُترك mean in this context?  [يُتْرَكَ · meaning]
turns on: Ibn ʿAshur distinguishes the verb's ordinary metaphor of neglect from the contextual prospect of remaining unrevived.
- 75:36/q04/p1 It means being neglected or abandoned without care, extending the ordinary sense of leaving someone. — Ibn ʿAshur says the basic verb is voluntarily parting from something, used metaphorically here for neglect without attention. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 75:36/q04/p2 It means being left in nonexistence without revival. — Ibn ʿAshur derives this contextual sense from 75:3 and 75:13 and says the question resumes the opening concern with gathering human bones. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 75:36/q05 Why is يُترك passive?  [يُتْرَكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The sources offer different rhetorical purposes for leaving the agent unspoken.
- 75:36/q05/p1 The unnamed agent focuses attention on the imagined abandonment itself. — Biqaʿi says the point is being left wholly without purpose or care, regardless of who leaves one. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 75:36/q05/p2 God's agency is already clear, so the passive is concise. — Ibn ʿAshur makes this point while explaining the prospect of being left without revival. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 75:36/q05/p3 The omitted agent continues a sequence of passives and suits the sura's urgent tone. — Samarraʾi compares the subsequent verbs for being created, fashioned, and made with the preceding passive. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:36/q06 How does أن يترك function in the construction?  [أَن يُتْرَكَ · grammar]
- 75:36/q06/p1 The verb phrase occupies the place of the two objects of يحسب. — Al-Alusi states this alongside his analysis of sudā as a circumstantial accusative and the question as repudiation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 75:36/q07 What does سدى mean here?  [سُدًى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether abandonment concerns duties during life, reckoning and repayment, remaining unrevived after death, or all these stages; al-Wahidi appeals to 75:37 for the resurrection sense, while Ibn Kathir combines the senses.
- 75:36/q07/p1 Left neglected, idle, unattended, or without purpose. — Ibn ʿAbbas, Qatada, and others give the bare gloss neglected; al-Suddi compares untended camels grazing wherever they wish. Ibn Qutayba, al-Wahidi, al-Alusi, and Bursevi cite asdaytu for neglecting something or an unfinished need; Abu ʿUbayd transmits Abu Zayd's camel expression and calls sudā its verbal noun. Tabrisī, Itqan, and Elmalili stress purposelessness. Qurtubi, Samīn, and al-Alusi cite poetry saying God created nothing neglected; Samīn also gives asdaytu hajati and explains asda ilayhi maʿrufan as not reminding a recipient of a favor. Ibn ʿAshur notes the form can serve singular and plural, ḍamma on sīn is commoner than fatḥa, its alif derives from wāw, and neither al-Kashshaf nor al-Raghib explains it. [19 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيد عن أبي زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشاعر); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(شاعر); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 75:36/q07/p2 One is not left without commands, prohibitions, work, or religious duties in this life. — Tabari transmits Mujahid's commands and prohibitions and Ibn Zayd's imposed duty and work; al-Mawardi reports Ibn Zayd on deeds and Mujahid on commands. Al-Suyuti transmits Mujahid's explanation of commands and prohibitions. Ibn ʿAshur reports al-Shafiʿi's statement that Quran scholars agreed, to his knowledge, on neither commanded nor forbidden. Tabrisī says these duties reform and benefit people in this life and the next. Ibn Qutayba and Ibn al-Jawzī add punishment to the duties; Thalabī cites a neglected need and unattended grazing camels. Qurtubi also cites camels without a herder. [23 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد,مجاهد); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن عباس، ومجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس ومجاهد,قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد والشافعي وعبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الشافعي)]
- 75:36/q07/p3 One is not left without reckoning, moral accountability, punishment, or recompense for deeds. — Muqatil applies accountability to Abu Jahl through the sura's end. Al-Mawardi reports a separate reckoning-and-punishment view and cites the poet saying God leaves nothing neglected. Jishumi compares camels ranging freely; al-Wahidi adds admonition and reckoning. Ibn ʿAshur calls sudā a metonym for no recompense, since commands and prohibitions lead to repayment in the Hereafter. Al-Qummi's report says judged, punished, and questioned; Asad stresses responsibility for one's acts. [10 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI(القمي); ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 75:36/q07/p4 One is not left in the grave, or after death, without resurrection. — Al-Mawardi reports this as a distinct view. Al-Wahidi prefers it because the next verse argues from human origins (75:37). Al-Suddi gives it in Ibn Kathir; Qurtubi and Bursevi also report it. Ibn ʿAshur points to 75:3 and 75:13; Tabatabaʾi describes the question as rebuking disbelief in revival. Study Quran notes that Ibn Kathir relates aimlessness to doubters of resurrection. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(IK); WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 75:36/q07/p5 The verse includes both worldly duties and later resurrection or recompense. — Ibn Kathir explicitly prefers both: no neglect without commands in life and no abandonment in the grave; the verse answers deniers of the afterlife. Nasafi combines commands, resurrection, and recompense; Baydawi combines duties and requital; Bursevi gives duties and recompense. Al-Alusi presents duties/recompense and an alternative grave reading. Tabatabaʾi says neglect would entail neither obligation nor recompense. Ibn ʿAshur says sudā inserts the purpose-and-recompense argument and explains why resurrection follows. Study Quran's JJ combines revealed obligations and accountability; Biqaʿi includes obligation, reward, judgment, and gratitude for gifts. [10 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(JJ); IBNKATHIR-FULL+]

## 75:36/q08 What is the grammatical form and role of سدى?  [سُدًى · grammar]
- 75:36/q08/p1 It is an accusative circumstantial expression describing the subject of the passive يُترك. — Nahhas calls it a circumstantial accusative. Samīn specifies that it describes the subject of yutraka, as in camels left unattended. Al-Alusi calls it a circumstantial complement of the pronoun in yutraka; Ibn ʿAshur gives the same analysis. [4 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 75:36/q09 How is سدى read at a pause?  [سُدًى · readings]
turns on: The transmitted pausal vowel quality differs by route, especially the Iraqi and Egyptian/Maghrebi transmissions.
- 75:36/q09/p1 The pause may use imāla. — Nashr reports imāla among variant transmissions from Abu Bakr and among Egyptian and Maghrebi transmitters from Shuʿayb, including those who use imāla elsewhere; the report covers both سوى and سدى. [2 notes: NASHR(أبو بكر,المصريون والمغاربة)]
- 75:36/q09/p2 The pause may use fatḥ. — Nashr says both fatḥ and imāla are valid in reports from Abu Bakr, while Iraqi transmitters know only fatḥ. [1 notes: NASHR(أبو بكر)]

## 75:36/q10 What argument does the verse make from human purpose and accountability?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether moral wisdom supplies a rational proof of resurrection; al-Alusi disputes the proof's force. The reported permanence claim addresses human purpose directly.
- 75:36/q10/p1 Divine wisdom requires duties and eventual resurrection for just recompense. — Razi argues that granting power, instruments, and reason without commands would imply approval of evil; the gracious God's imposition of duties requires an afterlife of reward. He cites 20:15 and 38:28 on accountability and the contrast of righteous and wicked. Baydawi says commands and prohibitions entail recompense, which may not occur in this life. Biqaʿi observes oppressors and victims die without their due, and a wise maker does not create merely to waste; he calls humans weak, needy created servants and says faith in God, angels, scriptures, messengers, and the Last Day leads to prayer, good works, fearful obedience, and glad tidings. Bursevi says capacities without duties would imply divine approval of evil, while this cramped world cannot fully repay deeds. Ibn ʿAshur invokes humans' careful formation and reason, the difference between righteous and corrupt, and God's power to return them; he distinguishes the wisdom argument here from the material proof following it. Al-Alusi presents the moral argument but questions its force as rational proof. [17 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 75:36/q10/p2 The moral-accountability reasoning is debatable as a rational proof of resurrection. — Al-Alusi reports the proposed rational proof and expressly questions whether it establishes resurrection. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 75:36/q10/p3 Humans were created for permanence and movement between abodes, not extinction. — Tabatabaʾi transmits a report through Musʿada ibn Ziyad: when a man says humans were made for extinction, Jaʿfar al-Sadiq replies that Paradise does not perish and Hell does not go out; people move from one abode to another. [1 notes: TABATABAI(جعفر بن محمد عليه السلام)]

## 75:36/q11 How does this question connect with the surrounding sura and subsequent verses?  [* · links]
- 75:36/q11/p1 It resumes the sura's resurrection question and leads into proof from human creation and God's power to revive. — Zajjaj moves from rejecting an aimless life to God's power to initiate creation at 75:37. Ibn ʿAtiyya says reflection on human beginnings establishes possibility of resurrection; Ibn al-Jawzī locates the proof at the drop of semen in 75:37. Razi connects it to gathering bones in 75:3 and calls this the first proof. Baydawi calls it a renewed denial of resurrection. Bursevi and al-Alusi call it a repeated rebuttal of denial; al-Alusi cites 75:3. Ibn ʿAshur says 75:3 argues for resurrection while 75:36 argues from wisdom; its repeated question follows Judgment and prepares for the material proof in 75:37, like 23:115. He says 75:40 explains revival without separately detailing recompense, since resurrection serves recompense. Samarraʾi connects it to the oath (75:1), gathering bones (75:3), record of deeds (75:13), threats (75:34-35), and closing proof of divine power (75:40). [14 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:36/q12 What is reported about Abu Jahl in connection with this passage?  [* · reports]
- 75:36/q12/p1 The Prophet warned Abu Jahl at al-Baṭḥāʾ; he boasted of safety, then was struck at Badr and finished by Ibn Masʿud. — Bursevi says the Prophet seized and shook him; the sons of ʿAfraʾ felled him at Badr. Bursevi distinguishes aqaṣahu, killing on the spot, from ajhaza, finishing a near-dead wounded person. [2 notes: BURSEVI]
- 75:36/q12/p2 The Prophet called Abu Jahl this community's Pharaoh. — Bursevi reports the saying that every community has a Pharaoh. [1 notes: BURSEVI(رسول الله)]

## 75:36/q13 What inward counsel is drawn from the verse's promise of later recompense?  [* · inward]
- 75:36/q13/p1 One should not demand immediate results of deeds or spiritual states in this world. — Bursevi transmits a great Sufi's warning that doing so is bad adab because this world's nature does not promise those results. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الكبار)]
