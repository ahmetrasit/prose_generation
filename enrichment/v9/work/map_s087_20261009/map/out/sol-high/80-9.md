# 80:9: 13 questions

## 80:9/q01 Who is the person described as fearing?  [وَهُوَ · referent]
- 80:9/q01/p1 He is Ibn Umm Maktum, the blind seeker who came to the Prophet. — Muqatil identifies him as the visitor who came striving in the heat. Tabrisi says he strove for good; the report he transmits also calls him Abdullah ibn Shurayh ibn Malik ibn Rabiʿa al-Fihri. Ibn al-Jawzi attributes the interpretation of striving as doing good to a second view. Jishumi and the report in Tabrisi place his arrival amid the Prophet's address to Quraysh leaders. [7 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; DURR-FULL(عائشة)]

## 80:9/q02 How is the clause 'while he fears' constructed?  [وَهُوَ يَخْشَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause describes the circumstances of his striving or supplies another predicate.
- 80:9/q02/p1 It is a circumstantial clause describing the seeker as he strives. — Al-Nahhas allows the verbal clauses in verses 8 and 9 as circumstantial accusatives. Al-Alusi takes this clause as circumstantial from the subject of yasʿā, itself circumstantial from the subject of jāʾaka. Ibn Ashur says the object of 'fears' is omitted because scriptural usage makes fear of God understood. [4 notes: NAHHAS; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 80:9/q02/p2 It can be another predicate. — Al-Nahhas gives this as an alternative construction for the clause in verse 9. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 80:9/q03 What does the seeker fear?  [يَخْشَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: The verb leaves the feared object unstated; the sources supply God, hostile disbelievers, or a physical hazard.
- 80:9/q03/p1 He fears God. — Muqatil says he fears his Lord; Razi includes concern about failing to attend properly to religious duties. Ibn Kathir links this fear to seeking guidance from the Prophet's teaching. Ibn Ashur says God is the understood, omitted object in scriptural usage. Tabatabai calls such fear a sign of taking the Qur'an's reminder to heart, citing 20:2–3 and 87:10. The Study Quran specifies God and the Day of Judgment. Kashshaf, Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi and Alusi give this alongside other possibilities; Biqai holds all three together. [23 notes: MUQATIL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الوجه الأول); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 80:9/q03/p2 He fears harm from the disbelievers as he comes to the Prophet. — Razi gives this as his second possibility. Kashshaf, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi and Alusi also report it; Biqai describes the harm as persecution for visiting the Prophet. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الوجه الثاني); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 80:9/q03/p3 Being blind and without a guide, he fears stumbling on the road. — Razi gives this as his third possibility. Kashshaf, Baydawi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi also mention lack of a guide; Nasafi calls such wariness customary for blind people. Biqai includes the road hazard alongside the other fears. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الوجه الثالث); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 80:9/q04 How does the seeker's coming and fear contrast with the self-sufficient person?  [وَهُوَ يَخْشَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the paired descriptions require a formal ellipsis by ihtibāk; al-Alusi disputes that need.
- 80:9/q04/p1 The two people form an opposing pair: the seeker comes and fears, while the self-sufficient person does neither. — Biqai contrasts the seeker's arrival and fear with the rich man's failure to come and lack of fear, making favoritism especially pointed. Ibn Ashur says the clause about the comer is coordinated with the self-sufficient person and completes a division by opposites. Corpus Coranicum identifies piety in 80:9 as the positive counterpart to istaghnā in 80:5, noting the antonymy of istaghnā and ittaqā in 92:5–8 and their proximity in 96:7. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 80:9/q04/p2 The contrast works by ihtibāk ellipsis: each side implies qualities stated only on the other side. — Saʿdi al-Mufti, as reported by Bursevi, says the earlier mention of the rich implies the later seeker's poverty, while his coming and fear imply their opposites in the first person. Al-Alusi reports the proposal but says the device is unnecessary in light of his prior explanation. [1 notes: BURSEVI(سعدي المفتي); ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأفاضل)-]

## 80:9/q05 Why was attention given to the leaders instead of the seeker?  [وَهُوَ يَخْشَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether their social standing implies a material motive; al-Wahidi says the verse provides no such basis.
- 80:9/q05/p1 The Prophet was trying to win the Quraysh leaders to Islam and feared they would scorn followers who were blind or enslaved. — Jishumi's report describes Ibn Umm Maktum interrupting the effort to convert Meccan leaders. Tabrisi's report names ʿUtba ibn Rabiʿa, Abu Jahl, al-ʿAbbas, Ubayy and Umayya ibn Khalaf, and says Ibn Umm Maktum repeatedly asked to be taught. It gives the Prophet's concern that the leaders might scorn a following of blind people and slaves. Aʿisha's account in al-Durr also places the Prophet with Quraysh chiefs, including Abu Jahl and ʿUtba. [4 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABRISI(قيل); DURR-FULL(عائشة)]
- 80:9/q05/p2 Seeking the leaders' riches is not established as his motive. — Al-Wahidi rejects that suggestion because the verse describes the other man's striving and fear, not his poverty. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT+]

## 80:9/q06 Who is reproached for turning away from the fearful seeker?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the unnamed frowner can be identified as the Prophet from the reported incident despite the lack of an explicit name and the character objection.
- 80:9/q06/p1 The Prophet is addressed and reproached for turning from the seeker. — Muqatil says God admonished him to welcome the fearful visitor rather than attend to the self-sufficient. Qushayri calls the passage a reproach to the Prophet on behalf of the poor. Jishumi, the report in Tabrisi, and Aʿisha's account in al-Durr describe him turning back to the leaders. Tabatabai describes the rebuked pattern as attention to wealthy people who scorn truth while neglecting a seeker of purification. Al-Murtada, quoted by Tabrisi, argues against identifying the Prophet as the subject. [8 notes: MUQATIL; QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABRISI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,قيل); DURR-FULL(عائشة); TABATABAI; TABRISI(المرتضى علم الهدى)-]
- 80:9/q06/p2 The passage does not identify the Prophet as the one who frowned. — Al-Murtada says the text does not name him and that frowning at a believing seeker or preferring the rich to the poor would conflict with his noble character. [1 notes: TABRISI(المرتضى علم الهدى)+]

## 80:9/q07 What is reported to have happened when the seeker came?  [* · reports]
- 80:9/q07/p1 Ibn Umm Maktum asked for instruction while the Prophet addressed Quraysh leaders; the Prophet turned away and the passage was revealed as a rebuke. — Jishumi attributes the account to al-Hasan and others. Tabrisi reports that Ibn Umm Maktum came during the invitation of Quraysh chiefs, repeatedly requested teaching, and that the Prophet turned away; its list of chiefs includes ʿUtba, Abu Jahl, al-ʿAbbas, Ubayy and Umayya ibn Khalaf. Aʿisha's account in al-Durr mentions Abu Jahl and ʿUtba and quotes verses 5–10. Qushayri says the eager seeker was neglected while the Prophet occupied himself with others; Ibn Kathir says the Prophet was busy with another person as the blind man sought guidance. [6 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABRISI(قيل); DURR-FULL(عائشة); QUSHAYRI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 80:9/q08 What is reported after the revelation?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports describe different later actions and events, rather than opposing versions of one event.
- 80:9/q08/p1 The Prophet welcomed and honored Ibn Umm Maktum, asked after his needs, and appointed him deputy over Medina twice. — Muqatil reports the honoring and two appointments during military expeditions. Jishumi reports the Prophet greeting him as the man for whom his Lord had reproached him and asking his needs; Tabrisi reports the greeting, care, and two appointments. [3 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABRISI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 80:9/q08/p2 Ibn Umm Maktum was later seen at al-Qadisiyya in armor, carrying a black banner. — Anas gives the eyewitness account in Jishumi and in Tabrisi; the latter calls the banner a black standard. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أنس); TABRISI(أنس بن مالك)]
- 80:9/q08/p3 Thereafter the Prophet did not frown at poor people or seek out rich people, and the poor had an honored place in his gathering. — Bursevi transmits this as a report about his subsequent conduct. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 80:9/q09 What lesson does the incident teach about attention to people?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw complementary lessons about equal warning, honoring poor devotees, and the measure of human worth.
- 80:9/q09/p1 Give the sincere seeker full attention without favoring people for rank or wealth. — Muqatil says the Prophet was told to welcome the one fearing his Lord rather than attend to the self-sufficient. Ibn Kathir says warning should be equal across eminent and weak, rich and poor, masters and slaves, men and women, adults and children, since God guides whom He wills. Biqai says the contrast warns against favoring the wealthy and slighting the sincere. Bursevi applies it to scholars, judges and rulers, who should bring the weak seeker near and prefer him over a prominent person lacking good. Tabatabai says the seeker wants purification and fears God, while the wealthy scorn truth and their purification is disregarded. [5 notes: MUQATIL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]
- 80:9/q09/p2 The passage gives special honor to the poor and to companionship with sincere, detached devotees. — Qushayri sees a reproach on behalf of the poor. Bursevi reports the view that poverty and its people are honored while worldly status is devalued and that company with sincere detached poor people benefits. He invokes 18:28 as the higher instruction to stay with those calling on God morning and evening, and a divine saying about people who failed to feed the hungry or give drink to the thirsty. His cited hadith says favoring a rich person by wronging a poor person destroys a third of one's religion; he explains taḥāmala as either enduring difficulty or treating another unjustly. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(الحديث,الله تعالى,بعضهم)]
- 80:9/q09/p3 Human worth lies in faith and spiritual state rather than outward appearance or wealth. — Bursevi says faith ennobles the socially lowly and unbelief makes the socially esteemed wretched. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 80:9/q10 How is the Prophet's attention to the chiefs read inwardly?  [* · inward]
- 80:9/q10/p1 He honored the Quraysh chiefs as manifestations of divine majesty, while the higher station sees no distinction among people under God's absolute reality. — Bursevi attributes this explanation to some great Sufis. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الأكابر)]

## 80:9/q11 What does the revelation of this rebuke show about the Prophet's transmission of revelation?  [* · interpretation]
- 80:9/q11/p1 He did not conceal revelation, even when it rebuked him. — Ibn Zayd, as reported by Jishumi, says that if the Messenger had concealed any revelation, he would have concealed this passage. [1 notes: JISHUMI(ابن زيد)]

## 80:9/q12 Does the switch to direct address soften the rebuke?  [* · rhetoric]
- 80:9/q12/p1 No; the following direct address intensifies the rebuke and shows no conciliatory intimacy. — Tabatabai rejects an honorific reading of the switch from third to second person because the direct address that follows is stronger reproof. [1 notes: TABATABAI+]

## 80:9/q13 How does the fearful seeker fit the surah's themes and its link to al-Naziat?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources emphasize different aspects of the link: warning those who fear or the broader pairing of the two surahs.
- 80:9/q13/p1 The surah elaborates the call to warn those who fear by showing how resurrection can purify receptive hearts. — Biqai connects this to 79:45. He says human creation and the cycle of food prove God's power, making rejection of resurrection astonishing. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 80:9/q13/p2 Abasa pairs with al-Naziat through a shared central theme, discourse style, and reasoning, with a distinctive sequence of topics. — Islahi points to 79:45 and says the changed topic order gives this surah its distinctive majesty. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
