# 26:74: 14 questions

## 26:74/q01 What does بَلْ signal in their reply?  [بَلْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the particle is read as a corrective turn away from the prior questions or as an expression of astonishment.
- 26:74/q01/p1 It turns from the idols' supposed powers to the actual basis of their worship, inherited practice, exposing their failed answer. — Al-Ṭabarī treats it as a corrective turn, like 'it was not so, but rather this.' Al-Nasafī says they retract a claim that the idols hear, benefit, or harm. Abū Ḥayyān says a direct claim would be an obvious lie or error and that the diversion admits argumentative helplessness. Al-Ālūsī likewise says they concede what cannot be denied. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:74/q01/p2 It expresses astonishment. — Asad identifies the initial particle's force as astonishment. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 26:74/q02 What did they find their fathers doing?  [وَجَدْنَآ ءَابَآءَنَا كَذَٰلِكَ يَفْعَلُونَ · interpretation]
- 26:74/q02/p1 Their fathers worshipped and served the idols, and they themselves repeat that practice. — Muqātil says this is their defense and that Abraham then questions the idols worshipped by them and their earliest ancestors. Al-Ṭabarī says they follow their forebears' way; Qatāda calls it their fathers' religion. Al-Ālūsī explains that 'likewise' means the fathers worshipped the same way. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses imitation of ritual forms without understanding beyond those forms. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 26:74/q03 Why do they invoke their fathers as a basis for worship?  [ءَابَآءَنَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the ancestral appeal is only unsupported repetition or rests on an inference from the forebears' supposed wisdom.
- 26:74/q03/p1 They have no proof for idol worship and rely on inherited practice as their sole support. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām says they had no argument. Al-Wāḥidī says they abandon proof and reasoning despite knowing the idols neither hear nor help nor harm; al-Jishumī and al-Baghawī likewise say custom replaces argument. Al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān call their reply a retreat or diversion; Ibn Kathīr, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Bursawī, al-Ālūsī, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī connect it to their inability or refusal to defend the idols' powers. Asad says they stress the practice's antiquity. Iṣlāḥī says they cannot answer Abraham's questions and will not abandon the tradition. [21 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:74/q03/p2 Their forebears must have seen some good in the practice because they were wiser and more experienced. — Al-Biqāʿī reports this as the worshippers' own justification, without endorsing it. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(العبدة)]

## 26:74/q04 What evidentiary value does ancestral practice have in matters of faith?  [ءَابَآءَنَا · theology]
turns on: Whether inherited practice itself, or presumed ancestral wisdom, can count as evidence without independent proof.
- 26:74/q04/p1 Antiquity and inherited authority do not establish truth; faith requires proof and reasoning rather than blind imitation. — Al-Thaʿlabī says religion must rest on proof; al-Wāḥidī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Rāzī contrast this reply with evidence. Al-Kashshāf says chronological precedence cannot make falsehood true; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls this imitation especially bad in an obvious, weighty matter. Al-Rāzī argues that praising imitation and condemning reasoning would praise the unbelievers' condemned way and condemn Abraham's praised way. Bursawī cites a Persian couplet warning truth-seekers against following an imitator's path to the Kaaba. Asad reports Zamakhsharī's argument against antiquity as proof and Rāzī's indictment of taqlīd on a scholar's or leader's authority; Study Quran likewise reports Rāzī's case for sound reasoning. Al-Biqāʿī notes that religious custom can sustain false imitation even though visible physical harm would cause a practice to be abandoned. [14 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES(Razi,Zamakhshari); STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī); BAGHAWI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; THALABI+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 26:74/q04/p2 The wisdom and experience of earlier generations make their practice seem a warrant for continuing it. — Al-Biqāʿī reports the worshippers' inference that their ancestors must have recognized some good. The cited critics reject antiquity or imitation as proof; none of them adopts the worshippers' inference. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(العبدة); THALABI-; BAGHAWI-FULL-; KASHSHAF-FULL-; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; RAZI-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL-; ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari)-]

## 26:74/q05 How is كَذَٰلِكَ يَفْعَلُونَ parsed?  [كَذَٰلِكَ يَفْعَلُونَ · grammar]
- 26:74/q05/p1 كَذَٰلِكَ is an accusative object governed by يَفْعَلُونَ, and the verbal clause is the second object of وَجَدْنَا. — Abū Ḥayyān says the kāf points to the same act of idol worship. Al-Samīn says it means that the fathers do as the speakers do and explicitly makes yafʿalūn the second object of wajadnā. Bursawī also gives yafʿalūn as governor and the clause as wajadnā's second object. Al-Ālūsī calls the fronted element an absolute object. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 26:74/q06 Why does كَذَٰلِكَ precede يَفْعَلُونَ?  [كَذَٰلِكَ يَفْعَلُونَ · rhetoric]
- 26:74/q06/p1 The object is fronted to preserve the verse ending's rhyme. — Al-Ālūsī gives the fronting and its rhyme function twice, once while explaining that the fathers performed the same idol worship. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 26:74/q07 How does the reply address Abraham's question about the idols' powers?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the indirect ancestral reply counts as conceding impotence or avoids making that concession explicitly.
- 26:74/q07/p1 Their switch to ancestral custom amounts to a concession that the idols cannot hear, benefit, or harm. — Al-Wāḥidī says they acknowledge the idols' incapacity; al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, Ibn Kathīr, Bursawī, and Study Quran's IK also call it a concession. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī say they cease claiming such powers; al-Ālūsī says they concede what cannot be denied. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 26:74/q07/p2 They avoid a direct negative answer about the idols' powers and evade the question through the ancestral appeal. — Abū Ḥayyān says a direct assertion of power would expose an obvious falsehood and calls their diversion evidence of helplessness. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says they avoid answering negatively and expressly conceding impotence. Asad says they evade Abraham's criticism by stressing antiquity; Yaḥyā ibn Sallām and al-Qurṭubī say they have no argument. [6 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 26:74/q08 Why can a false inherited religious practice persist?  [ءَابَآءَنَا · interpretation]
- 26:74/q08/p1 Religious custom allows imitation to continue even where people would abandon a practice that caused visible physical harm. — Al-Biqāʿī contrasts the ready detection of physical injury with the ease of maintaining a false religious custom over time. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 26:74/q09 How does Abraham pursue their appeal to ancestors in the following exchange?  [ءَابَآءَنَا · links]
- 26:74/q09/p1 He presses the point back to the earliest ancestors and asks about their idols; the age of a practice gives it no authority. — Muqātil says Abraham asks about the idols worshipped by his hearers and their earliest ancestors. Al-Kashshāf explains the move: chronological priority is no proof and aged falsehood remains false. [2 notes: MUQATIL; KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 26:74/q10 How did Burayda apply their words to later people?  [* · reports]
- 26:74/q10/p1 He saw people imitating one another and warned that they would soon claim, 'We found our fathers doing the same.' — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports Burayda's warning in response to observed imitation. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(بريدة)]

## 26:74/q11 What does Ibn Isḥāq report about Abraham's early treatment of idols?  [* · reports]
- 26:74/q11/p1 Abraham's father made idols for him to sell; Abraham mocked their uselessness, tipped their heads into a river, and told them to drink. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits Ibn Isḥāq's account. Abraham described the idols as harmful and useless; news of the ridicule spread locally but had not reached Nimrod. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق)]

## 26:74/q12 When does Iṣlāḥī place the reported confrontation?  [* · reports]
- 26:74/q12/p1 After Abraham had conclusively conveyed the truth through scriptural and intellectual arguments. — Iṣlāḥī locates the reported events after that completed argument. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:74/q13 How does the Abraham narrative fit the sūra's sequence and purpose?  [* · links]
- 26:74/q13/p1 It serves the same preaching purpose as the preceding account, closes with the recurring verses, and fills the interval between Noah and Moses. — Iṣlāḥī says Abraham was the most prominent messenger between Noah and Moses, explaining this account's placement after the previous sūra's treatment of Noah. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:74/q14 Which other verses echo the rejection of ancestral imitation?  [* · links]
- 26:74/q14/p1 The same rejection is linked to 5:104 and similar injunctions in 7:28, 10:78, 28:36, 31:21, and 43:24. — Study Quran supplies these six cross-references. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
