# 19:48: 13 questions

## 19:48/q01 What does Abraham's withdrawal involve?  [وَأَعْتَزِلُكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether iʿtizāl describes an outward departure as well as a break in belief, or only an inward break.
- 19:48/q01/p1 He separates from his people and their idolatry, including an outward departure. — Jishumi and Qurtubi explain iʿtizāl as moving away or separation, comparing the Cave youths (18:16). Razi specifies geographic and religious separation. Islahi says the plural includes father, companions, associates and family; his kindness does not prevent formal disavowal. Biqaʿi says the break lasts while they persist in their religion. Alusi says outward migration is the apparent, transmitted sense. [24 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭabarī); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)+]
- 19:48/q01/p2 He separates inwardly in heart and belief. — Alusi reports this spiritual reading but calls it contrary to the apparent and transmitted sense. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]

## 19:48/q02 Where does Abraham go when he leaves?  [وَأَعْتَزِلُكُمْ · reports]
turns on: The transmitted destination: Holy Land, Syria, or nearby Harran.
- 19:48/q02/p1 He leaves Kutha for the Holy Land. — Muqatil gives Kutha as the point of departure; Thaʿlabi and Baghawi transmit him. Study Quran reports that some understand the departure as toward the Holy Land. [4 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); STUDYQURAN(Ṭabarī)]
- 19:48/q02/p2 He migrates to Syria. — Kashshaf says Syria; Nasafi specifies Babylon to Syria; Alusi reports Syria while placing his people in Kutha. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 19:48/q02/p3 He migrates to nearby Harran. — Alusi reports this alternative while placing his people in Kutha. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 19:48/q03 Why does Abraham withdraw from his people?  [وَأَعْتَزِلُكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes stress either his religious commitment or the failure of his counsel as the immediate motive.
- 19:48/q03/p1 He gives priority to faith and disavows his family's religion despite social pressure. — Jishumi contrasts religion with helping his father and emigration with homeland. Razi contrasts the creating, beneficent Lord with harmful idols. Islahi says he trusts God, disregards public opinion, and stays firm despite kindness and threats. [5 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 19:48/q03/p2 His counsel has failed, so he leaves. — Bursevi says his advice failed; Alusi says the counsel had no effect and takes this promise as coordinated with the prior promise to seek forgiveness. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:48/q04 How is mā in 'what you call upon' construed?  [وَمَا تَدْعُونَ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ · grammar]
- 19:48/q04/p1 Mā is accusative through coordination. — Nahhas says the coordinated object is what Abraham will leave: what they invoke besides God. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 19:48/q05 What does 'what you call upon besides God' mean?  [وَمَا تَدْعُونَ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether tadʿūna describes worship directed to the idols or the act of naming them gods.
- 19:48/q05/p1 It means the idols they worship or invoke. — Yahya identifies the objects as idols. Jishumi reports both the idol referent and 'calling' as worship. Ibn Atiyya explicitly glosses tadʿūn as worship. Ibn Jawzi also reports worship as one sense. Wahidi, Ibn Jawzi and Razi add that idols neither help, answer nor hear. [19 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(غير مسمى); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭabarī)]
- 19:48/q05/p2 It means the idols they call gods. — Ibn Jawzi reports this alternative sense of tadʿūna. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 19:48/q06 What does 'I call upon my Lord' mean?  [وَأَدْعُوا۟ رَبِّى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether duʿāʾ is broad worship, or a particular act of supplication; the hadith and parallel wording support the broader sense.
- 19:48/q06/p1 He worships his Lord alone. — Kashshaf treats prayer as a part and means of worship and points to 19:49; Kashshaf and Abu Hayyan cite the prophetic saying that supplication is worship. Abu Hayyan notes the parallel 'I worship my Lord.' Alusi says 'worship' answers his father's complaint in 19:46, while 'call' emphasizes Abraham's positive turn to God. [14 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(الرسول صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحديث); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:48/q06/p2 He supplicates to God and hopes for an answer. — Farrah takes the clause as calling upon God without being made miserable by that prayer. Kashshaf, Abu Hayyan and Alusi allow an actual supplication; its specific requests are treated below. [11 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Quran commentators,Ṭabarī)]

## 19:48/q07 What does Abraham ask God for in this prayer?  [وَأَدْعُوا۟ رَبِّى · interpretation]
turns on: Which prayer is intended: the earlier promise about his father, the prayers in al-Shuʿaraʾ, a request for a child, or supplication in general.
- 19:48/q07/p1 He asks for forgiveness for his father. — Muqatil and Tabari connect the prayer to his earlier promise to ask God to forgive his father; Tabari explains forgiveness as concealment of sins and sparing their punishment. [2 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL]
- 19:48/q07/p2 He asks for wisdom and to join the righteous. — Kashshaf identifies the supplication in 26:83–89; Abu Hayyan cites 26:83 and its requests for wisdom and joining the righteous; Alusi mentions the same prayers, citing 26:83–84. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 19:48/q07/p3 He asks for family or a righteous child to ease his loneliness. — Qurtubi prefers family and children to comfort him after separation. Alusi cites the request for a righteous child in 37:100. Study Quran says Isaac and Jacob answer this prayer in 19:49 and ties this to the surah's blessed-children theme. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN(Quran commentators); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 19:48/q07/p4 He asks for his father's guidance. — Qurtubi reports this alternative, with ʿasā marking doubt about whether it will be answered, but prefers the family reading. Study Quran rejects it because the Quran says the guidance did not occur. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)-]
- 19:48/q07/p5 He supplicates without a specified request. — Alusi lists general supplication as an alternative to identifying a particular request. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 19:48/q08 What does 'perhaps' convey here?  [عَسَىٰٓ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether ʿasā reflects genuine uncertainty about acceptance or the future, or voices a sure outcome with humility.
- 19:48/q08/p1 It expresses humility and proper courtesy toward God. — Jishumi combines certainty with humble speech; Razi compares Abraham's 'I hope' in 26:82. Bursevi and Alusi explicitly call this good manners. Baydawi, Alusi and Tabatabai say reward is God's gift rather than an obligation; its outcome is unseen. Alusi adds that intimate friendship requires exceptional courtesy. [14 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 19:48/q08/p2 It expresses hope mixed with fear or uncertainty. — Ibn Atiyya says the hope contains deep apprehension; Abu Hayyan adds intense fear and self-effacement. Tabrisi says believers combine hope with fear. Baydawi, Alusi and Tabatabai cite the unseen outcome; Biqaʿi allows for worldly trials. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 19:48/q08/p3 It expresses certainty of the hoped-for outcome. — Jishumi says ʿasā here indicates certainty even though Abraham speaks humbly. Ibn Kathir says the hope is sure to be fulfilled because Abraham is the greatest prophet after Muhammad. [2 notes: JISHUMI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 19:48/q08/p4 It teaches that one cannot know one's future state of knowledge with certainty. — Qurtubi reports this separate explanation of ʿasā. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 19:48/q09 What does Abraham hope 'not to be wretched' means?  [أَلَّآ أَكُونَ بِدُعَآءِ رَبِّى شَقِيًّۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether shaqiyyan names disappointed prayer, rejected worship, the misery of idolatry, lack of family, or final perdition.
- 19:48/q09/p1 God will answer his prayer, so he will not be disappointed. — Thaʿlabi and Baghawi report this alternative; Qurtubi prefers answered prayer for family and children. Alusi glosses shaqiyy as disappointed and unsuccessful. Study Quran's reported Tabari reading prefers answered supplications, comparing Zachariah's wording in 19:4. [9 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; QURTUBI-FULL+; STUDYQURAN(Ṭabarī)+]
- 19:48/q09/p2 God will accept and reward his worship. — Wahidi Wajiz explicitly gives acceptance and reward; Tabrisi says God accepts instead of rejects him. Tabatabai says any reward is freely bestowed. The hope is worship without divine rejection. [5 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 19:48/q09/p3 He will not suffer or fail through worship as idolaters do. — Thaʿlabi gives this as an alternative to answered prayer. Baghawi, Ibn Jawzi, Nasafi and Tabrisi compare the idolaters' misery. Wahidi Basit says idols neither help nor answer; Biqaʿi says they neither answer nor harm. [11 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:48/q09/p4 Calling upon God will bring happiness rather than misery. — Yahya contrasts happiness with wretchedness; Farrah says calling will not make him miserable. [2 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA]
- 19:48/q09/p5 Prayer will bring him family and children after separation. — Qurtubi calls this the most apparent interpretation; Study Quran reports Isaac and Jacob as its fulfillment in 19:49. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(Quran commentators); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 19:48/q09/p6 He will avoid perdition in the Hereafter. — Study Quran mentions this possible meaning of shaqiyyan but prefers answered supplication in this context. [0 notes: STUDYQURAN(Ṭabarī)-]

## 19:48/q10 How does his prayer contrast with the people's invocation?  [وَمَا تَدْعُونَ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ وَأَدْعُوا۟ رَبِّى · interpretation]
- 19:48/q10/p1 His Lord can hear, benefit, and answer; their idols cannot, so their invocation leaves them wretched. — Razi recalls 19:42, where the idol cannot hear or benefit. Wahidi Basit and Ibn Jawzi say idols bring misery by neither helping nor answering. Kashshaf, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi identify an indirect contrast in Abraham's humble hope. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:48/q11 How does the declaration connect with its surrounding passage?  [* · links]
- 19:48/q11/p1 It follows Abraham's courteous reply to his father's threat and promise to seek forgiveness. — Tabari explains 'peace upon you' as refusing retaliation and not repeating what angered his father. He interprets the forgiveness promise as asking God to cover sins and spare punishment. Alusi coordinates 'I will leave you' with 'I will seek forgiveness.' [3 notes: TAB-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:48/q11/p2 The ensuing gift of Isaac and Jacob answers his request and fits the surah's theme of blessed children. — Study Quran cites 19:49 and the surah's children theme; Qurtubi's preferred reading asks for family after separation. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(Quran commentators); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 19:48/q11/p3 The next verse's wording confirms that 'calling' here means worship. — Kashshaf cites 19:49 for the worship sense of duʿāʾ. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 19:48/q12 What ethical or inward lessons are drawn from the passage?  [* · inward]
- 19:48/q12/p1 Leave bad companions and seek God's help to do so. — Al-Qasim says this secures outward and inward safety here and hereafter; a person follows those he loves, as companions follow each other. [1 notes: SULAMI(القاسم)]
- 19:48/q12/p2 Bad company causes suspicion of good people. — Abu Turab al-Nakhshabi says this in Sulami and Alusi; Alusi adds that scripture and experience show bad company hardens the heart and discourages good. [2 notes: SULAMI(أبو تراب النخشبي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو تراب النخشبي)]
- 19:48/q12/p3 Approach prayer with shame, awe, and awareness of one's unworthiness. — ʿAbd al-ʿAziz al-Makki says Abraham feared his tongue was unworthy of remembering God and hoped God would excuse him. [1 notes: SULAMI(عبد العزيز المكي)]
- 19:48/q12/p4 Devotion to God should be wholly sincere. — Qushayri says neither blame nor self-serving temptation turned Abraham from God. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 19:48/q12/p5 Religious counsel combines respect for family with firmness and trust in God. — Islahi says Abraham's disavowal disregards public opinion; a preacher combines softness and firmness. Kuranyolu notes respectful address to his father Azar despite threats and says a younger person may counsel an elder; guidance comes from revelation. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 19:48/q12/p6 Estrangement is deepest when one lacks like-minded companions. — Biqaʿi reports al-Khattabi's distinction between lack of kindred companions and mere distance from home. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو سليمان الخطابي)]

## 19:48/q13 What illustration of an extra kāna appears in these notes?  [* · grammar]
- 19:48/q13/p1 Kāna may be extra and have no syntactic effect. — Majaz also says kāna in 'God was knowing, wise' can span past, present and future (48:4), and cites poetic lines and Qays ibn Ghalib. Qays says 'no kāna like them' means no people like Fatima bint al-Kharshab's sons were found. [2 notes: MAJAZ(قيس بن غالب)]
