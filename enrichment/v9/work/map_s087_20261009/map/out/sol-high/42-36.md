# 42:36: 21 questions

## 42:36/q01 To whom is the address directed?  [أُوتِيتُم · referent]
turns on: Whether the second-person address continues the address to polytheists in 42:30–31 or has its apparent general reach.
- 42:36/q01/p1 People generally, including believers and unbelievers. — Abu Hayyan calls this the apparent sense while reporting the alternative of polytheists; Tabataba'i says the address is general. Ibn Ashur allows the whole community as an alternative. [3 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 42:36/q01/p2 The polytheists are addressed directly, with believers included by implication. — Qurtubi names polytheists; Ibn Ashur connects the address to 42:30–31 and says its ruling reaches believers by implication. Abu Hayyan reports this as another view. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 42:36/q02 What is encompassed by whatever people are given?  [فَمَآ أُوتِيتُم مِّن شَىْءٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether the scope includes only worldly provision and blessings or also a person's outward acts of worship.
- 42:36/q02/p1 Worldly provision and blessings, including wealth, children, livelihood, safety, and other visible gifts. — Tabari specifies wealth and children; Baghawi calls them worldly adornments; Bursevi includes things desired and competed for, including livelihood; Ibn Ashur includes safety at sea. Biqa'i stresses that visible blessings are gifts beyond human control; Tabataba'i says all received blessings and provision. Qurtubi calls them wealth and abundance; Kuran Yolu says to see them as gifts rather than one's own skill. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 42:36/q02/p2 Even visible deeds and worship count among gifts, and cannot by themselves secure salvation. — Sulami reports some saying that deeds and worship are lesser than blessings such as hearing and sight; blessings are not earned by deserving them. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]

## 42:36/q03 What does the opening فاء convey?  [فَمَآ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes address either its local consequence or its contrast with the waw of the parallel verse.
- 42:36/q03/p1 It marks this statement as a consequence of the preceding discourse. — Ibn Ashur calls it a consequential fa and links the address with 42:30. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 42:36/q03/p2 It is the fa used here where the parallel in al-Qasas uses waw. — Burhan cites the Shura and Qasas wording as an example of one particle occurring in place of another. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 42:36/q04 How is the first ما and its result clause parsed?  [فَمَآ أُوتِيتُم مِّن شَىْءٍۢ فَمَتَٰعُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether ما is formally relative with conditional force or formally conditional; both account for the answer's فاء.
- 42:36/q04/p1 ما is relative, with conditional force that licenses the فاء in its predicate or answer. — Baydawi and Bursevi say receiving causes the enjoyment, hence the fa; Nasafi contrasts the second ma. Alusi adds an omitted object pronoun. Bursevi supplies an omitted subject to complete the result and says the causal phrasing encourages gratitude. Ibn Ashur says the statement remains informative rather than a strict condition. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 42:36/q04/p2 ما is conditional, the fronted second object of أوتيتم, and فمتاع answers the condition. — Samin says the addressee pronoun is first object and deputy subject, the second object is fronted for clause-initial position, من شيء explains the ambiguous ما, and متاع predicates an omitted فهو. Biqa'i says the condition makes every gift a provision of lowly life. Bursevi gives this as an alternative; Alusi reports Abu Hayyan's analysis. [6 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]

## 42:36/q05 Why are these gifts called enjoyment of worldly life?  [فَمَتَٰعُ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا · interpretation]
- 42:36/q05/p1 They give brief, limited benefit in this life and then vanish, so they should not become one's ultimate goal. — Tabari says they are neither Hereafter provision nor lasting benefit at the return; Tabrisi says death ends them and they may vanish earlier. Qushayri and Bursevi add that worldly ease has troubles and defects. Razi says متاع signals baseness and الدنيا its end, and reason favors lasting good. Qurtubi warns against boasting; Ibn Kathir warns against deception. Tabataba'i says enjoyment lasts a few days; Study Quran compares 3:185 and 57:20's delusive enjoyment. Bursevi invokes a Persian maxim to consider the end at the outset; Kuran Yolu stresses their temporary gifted character. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 42:36/q06 What is meant by what is with God?  [وَمَا عِندَ ٱللَّهِ · referent]
- 42:36/q06/p1 The reward and bliss God has prepared and preserved in the Hereafter for believers and obedient people. — Tabari places it in the Hereafter and cites 28:60; Jishumi also frames it as preserved pay for work done for God rather than worldly gain. Bursevi says the afterlife reward was mentioned just before, and includes hidden favors, lofty stations, and gifts surpassing both worlds. Biqa'i adds lasting righteous deeds leading to afterlife bliss. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 42:36/q07 Why is what is with God better and more lasting?  [خَيْرٌۭ وَأَبْقَىٰ · interpretation]
- 42:36/q07/p1 Its benefit is purer and superior, and it endures when imperfect worldly pleasures and possessions perish. — Tabari says worldly gifts end but Paradise's bliss does not, citing 28:60. Baydawi, Bursevi, Alusi, Elmalili, and Tabataba'i specify pure benefit without evil, pain, or distress. Biqa'i says it remains beyond death when possessions are removed. Qushayri contrasts troubled, short-lived ease; Ibn Kathir says not to choose what perishes. Study Quran lists 28:60, 29:64, 3:14, 4:77, 9:38, 13:26, 20:131, 40:39 and 43:35 as parallels. [24 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 42:36/q08 How are ما عند الله, خير, and أبقى construed?  [وَمَا عِندَ ٱللَّهِ خَيْرٌۭ وَأَبْقَىٰ · grammar]
- 42:36/q08/p1 The second ما is relative and the subject; خير is its predicate, with أبقى coordinated as a further predicate or description. — Nahhas identifies خير as predicate and أبقى as coordinated; Samin explicitly makes ما عند الله a relative subject. Bursevi calls خير the predicate and أبقى a statement of duration. Alusi notes the predicate has no fa because superiority follows from being with God, unlike the first construction. [7 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]

## 42:36/q09 How does للذين آمنوا attach and what does its لام mean?  [لِلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the لام phrase is governed by أبقى or separately identifies who possesses or receives the reward.
- 42:36/q09/p1 The phrase attaches to أبقى, relating the enduring good to those who believe. — Samin makes الذين governed by أبقى; Bursevi calls the phrase attached to أبقى, and Alusi gives this parse. Tabataba'i mentions it as another analysis; Nahhas notes the genitive from لام. [5 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 42:36/q09/p2 The لام is explanatory or possessive, specifying the believers as owners or recipients of the blessing, with a loose phrase or omitted predicate. — Bursevi reports the Sa'diyya marginalia's explanatory لام and Abu al-Layth's specification of the recipient. Alusi allows an explanatory لام with omitted predicate. Tabataba'i prefers the possessive reading and loose adverbial phrase as clearer than attachment to أبقى. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الحواشي السعدية); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI+]

## 42:36/q10 Who receives the enduring reward?  [لِلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَلَىٰ رَبِّهِمْ يَتَوَكَّلُونَ · interpretation]
- 42:36/q10/p1 Believers who sincerely trust and obey their Lord receive it; disbelievers do not share this afterlife reward. — Tabari says they rely on God in affairs and needs and cites 5:82. Baghawi and Bursevi distinguish shared worldly gifts from believers' afterlife reward; Bursevi adds the virtues named afterward. Biqa'i calls theirs realized faith; Bursevi calls it sincere. Razi points to the verse's contrast with disbelievers; Ibn Jawzi says punishment awaits them. Elmalili adds trusting God's power, promises, and warnings while doing duties and seeking His pleasure. [20 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]

## 42:36/q11 Why call God their Lord here?  [رَبِّهِمْ · rhetoric]
- 42:36/q11/p1 Lord suits the mention of beneficence because believers recognize Him alone as the source of their nurture and every kindness. — Biqa'i relates the divine title to the surrounding discussion of gifts. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 42:36/q12 What does their reliance on the Lord entail?  [وَعَلَىٰ رَبِّهِمْ يَتَوَكَّلُونَ · interpretation]
- 42:36/q12/p1 They entrust their affairs and needs to God alone, trust His ordering and provision, and seek His aid in obedience and adversity. — Jishumi stresses confidence that provision continually comes from Him; Tabrisi says He orders events best and they turn to prayer. Biqa'i compares entrusting a burden to a capable bearer and says this reliance excludes hidden association, as faith excludes open association. Bursevi and Alusi stress exclusivity; Ibn Kathir specifies aid to perform duties and avoid prohibitions. Razi says reliance is on divine favor, not a claim that one's obedience obligates reward. Elmalili includes trust in God's power, promises, and warnings; Islahi makes reliance the provision for the Hereafter's journey and for leaving obstacles behind. Biqa'i says they receive sufficiency and blessings. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 42:36/q13 What do على and the imperfect verb add to the portrayal of reliance?  [وَعَلَىٰ رَبِّهِمْ يَتَوَكَّلُونَ · rhetoric]
- 42:36/q13/p1 على pictures placing a burden on God, while the imperfect depicts reliance renewed with each concern. — Biqa'i says this imagery conveys ease and freedom from worry. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 42:36/q14 What occasion of revelation is reported?  [* · reports]
- 42:36/q14/p1 Abu Bakr gave away all his wealth for God, was reproached, and the verse was revealed. — Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi, and Elmalili attribute the report to Ali; Abu Hayyan and Alusi say both Muslims and disbelievers criticized him, whereas Bursevi says all Muslims and other reports say people or a group. Qurtubi adds a hadith saying he spent eighty thousand. Alusi states no grading. [8 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(علي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(علي); ELMALILI(Hazret-i Ali)]

## 42:36/q15 What example is told of leaving worldly attachment?  [* · reports]
- 42:36/q15/p1 Harun al-Rashid's young son chose ascetic worship and left worldly status behind. — Bursevi says the sixteen-year-old son's bird obeyed him but not Harun, and the son said his father's worldliness shamed him before God's friends. Abu Amir al-Wa'iz al-Basri says the prince departed with a ring and Quran, earned a dirham and daniq by Saturday labor in Basra, worked tenfold when hired, and was later found ill in a ruin. [2 notes: BURSEVI(أبو عامر الواعظ البصري)]

## 42:36/q16 What conduct does the contrast between worldly goods and God's reward urge?  [* · interpretation]
- 42:36/q16/p1 Give worldly goods their limited place, pursue God's enduring reward through faith, reliance, obedience, and detachment. — Ibn Atiyya says the verse diminishes worldly life and urges lasting blessings and high rank. Jishumi reports an exhortation to work for God rather than worldly gain. Ibn Kathir says not to choose the perishing and mentions patiently leaving pleasures. Bursevi says to consider the outcome from the outset, cites a Persian maxim, and calls the choice God's gift to whom He wills. Ibn Ashur says worldly safety or wealth should not be a final goal. Kuran Yolu calls means divine gifts; Islahi says believers should desire the future reward. [11 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 42:36/q17 How does verse 36 follow the preceding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Which preceding theme chiefly supplies the transition: changing blessings, provision and sea danger, or affliction and patience.
- 42:36/q17/p1 The signs of rain and creatures show worldly blessings can disappear and change, preparing the contrast with lasting good. — Biqa'i draws the transition from the preceding natural signs. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 42:36/q17/p2 It extends the earlier discussion of expanded and restricted provision and of God's pardon amid sea-voyage danger. — Ibn Ashur traces it to 42:27 and 42:34, and includes safety at sea among passing worldly goods. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 42:36/q17/p3 It sits in the sūra's treatment of affliction, patience, and adversity under God's will. — Samarrai links the surrounding passages at 42:30, 42:43, and 42:48 to the contrast between passing provision and lasting good. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 42:36/q18 How do later passages develop this contrast?  [* · links]
- 42:36/q18/p1 They describe believers' qualities and wrongdoers' fate, then return to the contrast of temporary worldly life and lasting reward. — Tabataba'i says the verse divides provision between worldly gifts shared by believers and unbelievers and afterlife reward for believers before listing virtues and wrongdoers' fate. Islahi says 42:44–50 resumes this contrast, giving oppressed Muslims immediate glad tidings and foreshadowing their success. [2 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 42:36/q19 Why does the parallel in al-Qasas mention adornment?  [فَمَتَٰعُ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا · links]
- 42:36/q19/p1 Al-Qasas contains Qarun's public display of adornment, while al-Shura contains no comparable worldly scene. — Samarrai relates the added word to 28:79; he also reports Ma'tarak al-aqran making the same contextual connection. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR(معترك الأقران)]

## 42:36/q20 What broader themes of the sūra are connected with this verse and its believers' qualities?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes connect the passage with different overarching themes: two harvests, faith embodied in conduct, or communal consultation.
- 42:36/q20/p1 The sūra contrasts people pursuing this world's harvest with those seeking the Hereafter's harvest. — Study Quran states this as a theme of the sūra. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 42:36/q20/p2 Faith must become conduct that reshapes individual and communal life, including duties to God and relations with others. — Kuran Yolu says the Quran aims to transform the Jahiliyya order and form an exemplary generation; Elmalili calls faith and reliance foundational to religious and communal success. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI]
- 42:36/q20/p3 Consultation among believers is a basic principle of their community and gives the sūra its title. — Asad's introduction notes the title from consultation among believers in this passage. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 42:36/q21 How is the following verse's expression for major sin or sins read?  [* · readings]
turns on: Whether the reading uses a collective singular or an explicit plural; Tabrisi supplies linguistic and Quranic support for each.
- 42:36/q21/p1 Kufan readers except Asim use the singular, which can have collective sense. — Tabrisi reports the singular here and in al-Najm; he says singular wording can denote a collective, comparing Quranic references to God's blessing and Arabic usage, with 16:18. [2 notes: TABRISI(أهل الكوفة غير عاصم والباقون)]
- 42:36/q21/p2 The remaining readers use the plural, supported by the Quranic phrase major sins. — Tabrisi reports this reading here and in al-Najm and cites 4:31 in support. [2 notes: TABRISI(أهل الكوفة غير عاصم والباقون)]
