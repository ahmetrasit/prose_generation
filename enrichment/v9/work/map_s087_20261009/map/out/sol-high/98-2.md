# 98:2: 26 questions

## 98:2/q01 What does rasūl mean?  [رَسُولٌۭ · meaning]
- 98:2/q01/p1 An emissary sent by God. — Al-Qurṭubī glosses it as one sent by God. [1 notes: QURTUBI]

## 98:2/q02 Who is the messenger in this verse?  [رَسُولٌۭ · referent]
turns on: Whether the verse identifies the historical Prophet, Gabriel or the angel demanded by opponents, or speaks generally of messengers.
- 98:2/q02/p1 Muhammad. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Māwardī, al-Nasafī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and others identify him. Al-Ālūsī prefers Muhammad as transmitted from Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil and objects that the angelic interpretation makes the pages into revelation weakly. Ibn ʿĀshūr says earlier scriptures expected a law-bearing messenger like Moses or Jesus; al-Ghazālī points to his miraculous character and many miracles. [23 notes: NAHHAS(الضحاك); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR(حجة الإسلام); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Bg, IK, Q, Ṭ); ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI(بعض)+]
- 98:2/q02/p2 Gabriel, reciting angelic copies of the Preserved Tablet. — Al-Bayḍāwī reports Gabriel; al-Ālūsī reports and rejects an elaboration with angelic copies, calling the resulting sense of pages as revelation weak. [3 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ); ALUSI(بعض)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 98:2/q02/p3 An angel directly sent by God to read written pages, as the opponents demanded. — Iṣlāḥī says the opponents demanded an angel and written messages read aloud, rather than a human claiming revelation. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 98:2/q02/p4 The messengers sent to every community. — Study Quran cites 35:24, that every community had a warner, and extends the phrase to messengers and their revelations generally. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 98:2/q03 How is rasūl constructed after al-bayyina?  [رَسُولٌۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the indefinite nominative continues the definite bayyina through apposition or begins a new sentence; some analyses supply an omitted noun.
- 98:2/q03/p1 It is nominative apposition explaining the clear proof, directly or through an understood noun. — Al-Zajjāj and the majority make it appositional. Al-Samīn allows inclusion or emphatic whole-for-whole apposition; an omitted noun can mean the Messenger's proof. Al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī allow an understood proof, revelation, miracle or book when bayyina is the Qur'an. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this compressed appositional style and says it presents the expected proof as already arrived. [25 notes: FARRA; TAB; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF; TABRISI(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(قتادة وابن زيد); QURTUBI(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 98:2/q03/p2 It starts an explanatory nominal sentence with an omitted subject: it or he is a messenger. — Al-Farrāʾ treats the indefinite noun as a fresh nominative after the definite proof; al-Ṭabarī compares 85:15–16. Al-Akhfash supplies 'it is.' Ibn ʿĀshūr allows a stop after 'until the proof comes,' then a parenthetic explanation before 98:4. Al-Ālūsī also allows rasūl as predicate of an omitted subject. [17 notes: FARRA; TAB; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(الأخفش سعيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); TABRISI(الفراء); QURTUBI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR(الفراء)]
- 98:2/q03/p3 It is an independent subject, with yatlū as its predicate, or a sentence inserted to explain or praise the proof. — Al-Ālūsī reports these further analyses, including a modifier of bayyina when that word means Qur'an. [2 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 98:2/q04 How is rasūl read and parsed in the reported variants?  [رَسُولٌۭ · readings]
turns on: The nominative common text versus the accusative attributed to Ubayy and Ibn Masʿūd, with competing parses of the accusative.
- 98:2/q04/p1 The common reading is nominative rasūlun. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān attribute the nominative to the majority; it is commonly parsed as apposition to bayyina. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; JISHUMI]
- 98:2/q04/p2 Ubayy and/or Ibn Masʿūd read accusative rasūlan, parsed as a circumstantial, detached or specification accusative. — Al-Farrāʾ names Ubayy; al-Jishumī names Ibn Masʿūd; al-Qurṭubī and others report both. Al-Samīn says the circumstantial parse retains the same hyperbole or ellipsis as the nominative analysis. [12 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(أبي); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA(أبي); QURTUBI(أبي وابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(أبيّ وعبد الله); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(عبد الله، أبي); ALUSI(أبي وعبد الله); ALUSI-FULL(أبي وعبد الله)]

## 98:2/q05 What does min Allāh modify?  [مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the prepositional phrase qualifies the messenger or the scrolls.
- 98:2/q05/p1 It modifies rasūl, describing the messenger as sent from God. — Al-Samīn and al-Bursawī take it as a modifier; al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say it is adjectival rather than a genitive construction. [6 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 98:2/q05/p2 It can be circumstantial to the scrolls, meaning they descended from God. — Abū al-Baqāʾ allows this alternative as transmitted by al-Samīn. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]

## 98:2/q06 Why say 'a messenger from God' in this form?  [رَسُولٌۭ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the form signals eminence, the kind of awaited proof, or an ellipsis in the source phrase.
- 98:2/q06/p1 The indefinite noun magnifies the messenger, and 'from God' adds honor and grandeur. — Al-Biqāʿī describes an exceptionally eminent messenger; al-Ālūsī says the indefinite and divine source reinforce each other. Ibn ʿĀshūr says avoiding iḍāfa permits the magnifying tanwīn. Al-Bursawī calls the source an honor. [8 notes: BIQAI; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 98:2/q06/p2 The indefinite presents a type of awaited messenger, making the proof easier to recognize. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 'numbered days' in 2:184 and the transition to 'a Book' in 7:1–2. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]
- 98:2/q06/p3 The wording can omit an understood ʿind, 'from the presence of God'. — Al-Zarkashī's Burhān compares 2:147, 19:45 and 5:15, with 'from God' making the ellipsis apparent. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 98:2/q07 What does yatlū mean?  [يَتْلُوا۟ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb is given its ordinary recitation sense or Qatāda's explanatory sense of fine remembrance and praise.
- 98:2/q07/p1 To recite or read aloud. — Al-Jishumī and al-Qurṭubī give the basic gloss; Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies repeating speech without addition or omission, from writing or memory, so the verb indicates the revealed wording is unchanged. Al-Biqāʿī calls the recitation mass-transmitted and taught by God. [6 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 98:2/q07/p2 To present and praise the Qur'an in the finest terms. — Qatāda's recurrent gloss treats the messenger's tilāwa as excellent recollection and praise of the Qur'an; Ibn Abī Ḥātim adds that the purified pages are the Qur'an. [13 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); MAWARDI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 98:2/q08 How does the yatlū clause attach grammatically?  [يَتْلُوا۟ صُحُفًۭا مُّطَهَّرَةًۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether rasūl already has a predicate and yatlū qualifies it, supplies a circumstance, or itself predicates the subject.
- 98:2/q08/p1 It is another adjective describing the messenger. — Al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī, al-Samīn and Ibn ʿĀshūr give this parse; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it also inserts praise of the Qur'an, a description not apparently in earlier promises. [7 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BAYDAWI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 98:2/q08/p2 It is a circumstantial clause attached to the pronoun in the preceding modifier. — Al-Samīn conditions this on min Allāh being an adjective of rasūl; al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr also allow it. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 98:2/q08/p3 It can be the predicate of rasūl. — Al-Bayḍāwī allows this when rasūl is treated as a nominal subject; al-Ālūsī reports the same analysis. [3 notes: BAYDAWI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 98:2/q09 How can an unlettered messenger recite pages?  [يَتْلُوا۟ صُحُفًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether 'pages' is metonymy for the text memorized by Muhammad or points figuratively to earlier sheets confirmed by his recitation.
- 98:2/q09/p1 He recites the pages' written contents from memory; 'pages' stands for their contents. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr invoke his unlettered state. Al-Ālūsī calls it metonymy and reports an omitted 'like pages' or a reader metaphor; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 29:48. Al-Rāzī adds a book attributed to Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq suggesting he could read, but not write, perhaps miraculously. [20 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 98:2/q09/p2 He recites the Qur'an that confirms the earlier sheets' fundamental laws, so its recited content is treated as those sheets. — Al-Bursawī calls the attribution figurative because the Qur'an confirms earlier sheets in the foundations of laws and rulings. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 98:2/q10 What does ṣuḥuf mean literally?  [صُحُفًۭا · meaning]
- 98:2/q10/p1 Sheets, pages or surfaces that hold writing; the plural of ṣaḥīfa. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī give the plural and material sense; al-Ālūsī relates it to spreading out. Ibn ʿĀshūr lists parchment, leather and cloth. ʿAyn al-Maʿānī says sheets are laid one over another; Iṣlāḥī stresses that the demanded message had to be written and read aloud. [22 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(عين المعاني); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 98:2/q11 Which pages does the messenger recite?  [صُحُفًۭا · referent]
turns on: Whether ṣuḥuf points to the recited Qur'an, its heavenly source, angelic copies, earlier scripture, or an inward celestial reality.
- 98:2/q11/p1 The Qur'an, its written contents or revealed portions. — Muqātil says any writing may be called pages and the Qur'an has many kinds of qualities. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Qatāda specify its written sheets. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls them revealed portions, citing 80:13–16; al-Kāshifī says the term honors the Qur'an, which gathers all scriptures' secrets. [36 notes: MUQATIL; TAB; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); NAHHAS(الضحاك); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA(الضحاك وقتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Bg, IK, Q, Ṭ)]
- 98:2/q11/p2 Heavenly pages or the Mother of the Book, from which revealed books descend. — Al-Ḥasan places them in heaven; al-Ṭabrisī adds that purified angels alone touch them. Al-Qurṭubī relates them to the heavenly original and 85:21–22; Ibn Kathīr connects purified heavenly sheets to 80:13–16. Study Quran names the Preserved Tablet and 85:22 and 97:1–2. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك); TABRISI(الحسن والجبائي); QURTUBI(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Sh)]
- 98:2/q11/p3 Angelic copies from the Preserved Tablet recited by Gabriel. — Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative but rejects its treatment of the pages as revelation as weak. [0 notes: ALUSI(بعض)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 98:2/q11/p4 Tablets of celestial intellects and souls. — In al-Qāshānī's inward reading they are reached by detachment from natural, material and human corruption and contain enduring just foundations of true religion. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 98:2/q11/p5 Earlier prophetic scriptures, whose foundations the Qur'an confirms. — Al-Māwardī offers a connection between Muhammad's mission and scriptures sent to earlier prophets; al-Bursawī says Qur'anic recitation figuratively counts as reciting those earlier sheets because it confirms their laws. [3 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 98:2/q12 Why is the revelation called pages?  [صُحُفًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the designation depends on current contents, future writing, honor, or the physical form of sheets.
- 98:2/q12/p1 The written surface names its contents by close association. — Al-Biqāʿī says the physical sheets stand for their contents; al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr make the same metonymic point, Ibn ʿĀshūr citing 29:48. [6 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 98:2/q12/p2 The revelation is called pages in anticipation of its being written. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says God ordered it written, compares Joseph's 'pressing wine' in 12:36, and names sheets, sheep shoulder blades, scraps and stones; 29:51 supports calling the recited revelation a Book. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 98:2/q12/p3 The term honors the Qur'an as containing the secrets of all scriptures. — Al-Kāshifī gives this explanation through al-Bursawī; Muqātil adds that it contains many kinds of qualities. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الكاشفي); MUQATIL]
- 98:2/q12/p4 Sheets are named for being laid one upon another. — ʿAyn al-Maʿānī, as quoted by al-Bursawī, explains the name by their physical arrangement. [1 notes: BURSEVI(عين المعاني)]

## 98:2/q13 In what sense are the pages purified?  [مُّطَهَّرَةًۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Purity is explained by the text's truth, its praised status, restrictions on touching, divine origin, or an inward celestial state.
- 98:2/q13/p1 They and their contents are free of falsehood, unbelief, shirk, error, distortion and misleading material. — Ibn ʿAbbās lists lies, doubt, hypocrisy and error; Muqātil and ʿIkrima add shirk; al-Zajjāj cites 41:42 and 80:14. Al-Qurṭubī treats the varying lists as one sense. Al-Qushayrī stresses protection from disbelievers' alteration; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī from devils' intrusion. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees clear, unambiguous meaning and an allusion to corruption in some earlier scriptures. [41 notes: MUQATIL; TAB; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI(عكرمة); MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس,مقاتل); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(ابن عباس وقتادة وغيرهما); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 98:2/q13/p2 They are honored with the finest praise and commended judgments. — Qatāda's gloss ties purity to excellent remembrance and judgment; al-Rāzī says the Qur'an is free of blameworthy mention and receives the best praise. [4 notes: MAWARDI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 98:2/q13/p3 Only purified people or angels may touch them. — Al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Biqāʿī cite or echo 56:79; al-Ṭabrisī places the pages in heaven with purified angels. Iṣlāḥī says no human or jinn has touched them, only God and angels. Al-Bursawī and Elmalılı combine this with freedom from falsehood. [15 notes: TABRISI(الحسن والجبائي); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 98:2/q13/p4 Their origin from God makes them pure. — Al-Māwardī gives divine descent as a further possibility. [2 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL]
- 98:2/q13/p5 They are free of material and human corruption through spiritual detachment. — Al-Qāshānī applies purity to tablets of celestial intellects and souls. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 98:2/q14 What exactly does the adjective 'purified' describe?  [صُحُفًۭا مُّطَهَّرَةًۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the adjective is transferred to sheets from their textual contents or from those who may touch them.
- 98:2/q14/p1 It grammatically describes the sheets but in meaning describes the Qur'an contained in them. — Al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī explicitly distinguish grammar from intended content; al-Qurṭubī compares the honored purified sheets of 80:13–14. Al-Ālūsī calls freedom from falsehood a metaphor or transferred description. [6 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 98:2/q14/p2 It describes the pages themselves as ritually accessible only to the purified. — Al-Ālūsī allows a figurative transfer to the pages from the people permitted to touch them; al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī relate this to 56:79. [6 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 98:2/q15 What is the clear proof identified by this description?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether bayyina denotes the Messenger, the Qur'an, both together, or the evident disclosure of truth.
- 98:2/q15/p1 Muhammad, the messenger whose person and signs clarify truth. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Māwardī, al-Baghawī and others identify the proof with the Messenger. Al-Wāḥidī calls bayyina manifest evidence and allows a prophet or just witness; Muqātil says he exposed error and shirk. Al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr invoke al-Ghazālī's view of the Prophet's miraculous character and numerous miracles; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 65:10–11. [25 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-QT; BAGHAWI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR(حجة الإسلام); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TAB-FULL+; TAB+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 98:2/q15/p2 The Qur'an as clarifying and miraculous revelation. — Qatāda and Ibn Zayd cite 20:133; al-Ālūsī says it clarifies truth and proves the claim by miracle. On this reading rasūl can have an omitted link such as revelation or book of a messenger. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); RAZI-FULL(قتادة وابن زيد); ALUSI(قتادة وابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة وابن زيد); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 98:2/q15/p3 Muhammad together with the Qur'an he recites. — Ibn Kathīr identifies both together, with the revelation inscribed in the highest assembly and connected to 80:13–16. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 98:2/q15/p4 The disclosure of truth and appearance of proofs. — Al-Māwardī gives this as a third possible identification; al-Wāḥidī's general gloss allows any evidence that distinguishes truth from falsehood. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 98:2/q16 How does the arrival of this proof relate to the disbelievers' state and division?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: What state munfakkīn describes and what changed when the proof arrived, given that 98:4 describes division after its arrival.
- 98:2/q16/p1 They stayed in disbelief until the Messenger came; afterward some believed and others denied. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers this temporal division and says they had previously agreed he was a prophet. Al-Wāḥidī confines God's favor to those from the groups who believed. Al-Rāzī's fourth solution says the whole group ceased to be uniformly disbelieving. Al-Wāḥidī and Elmalılı note the difficulty that a straightforward endpoint might imply all converted, contrary to 98:4. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); ALUSI(الواحدي); ALUSI-FULL(الواحدي); ELMALILI(Fahr-ı Râzî); TAB-FULL+; TAB+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 98:2/q16/p2 They had pledged to remain in their religions until the promised Prophet arrived, but then broke their pledge and divided. — Al-Kashshāf's solution is cited by Elmalılı as subtler; al-Rāzī illustrates broken expectation with a sinner becoming richer yet increasing sin. Abū al-Suʿūd says the understood prior state was their resolve to follow the final Messenger; People of the Book had prayed for victory through him and threatened opponents. Elmalılı objects that extending the pledge to polytheists is awkward. [6 notes: ELMALILI(Ebussuud,Fahr-ı Râzî,Sâhib-Keşşâf); ELMALILI(Fahr-ı Râzî)+; ELMALILI-]
- 98:2/q16/p3 Before his arrival they mentioned the coming Messenger's virtues; after he came they disagreed. — Al-Rāzī's third solution, transmitted by Elmalılı, cites 2:89. [1 notes: ELMALILI(Fahr-ı Râzî)]
- 98:2/q16/p4 His mission loosened their commitment to inherited religions, though only some converted and others resisted. — Al-Rāzī's fifth solution distinguishes infikāk, detachment from what was clung to, from tafarruq, dispersion; Elmalılı says some recognized the weakness of prior religions yet divided through stubbornness. [2 notes: ELMALILI(Fahr-ı Râzî)]
- 98:2/q16/p5 Before decisive proof they could be excused in a confused interval; its coming clarified duty and made subsequent refusal accountable. — Elmalılı prefers this explanation, citing 5:19 for the interval, 17:15 for no punishment before a messenger, and the previous sūra's discussion of revelation. He says the People of the Book especially lost excuse after proof. [6 notes: ELMALILI; ELMALILI+]

## 98:2/q17 How does the messenger answer earlier expectations or demands for proof?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the verse describes the actual fulfillment of earlier promise, answers demands for a sign, or states the opponents' demanded form of proof.
- 98:2/q17/p1 He is the awaited, law-bearing messenger who confirms earlier scripture and defeats the claim that the promised proof has not arrived. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites earlier scripture's promises, the Torah's words in the prophet's mouth, the Gospel's promise of Jesus' words recalled, and Qur'anic confirmation in 5:48; 2:89 recalls earlier Jewish rejection. Elmalılı reports that people asked the People of the Book whether Muhammad was the promised figure. [8 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI(Ebussuud)]
- 98:2/q17/p2 The reply to polytheists' escalating demands is that a human messenger is the valid sign. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares demands for impossible signs and a written book in 17:90 and the affirmation of a human messenger in 17:94. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]
- 98:2/q17/p3 The opponents demanded a directly sent angel reading written pure pages. — Iṣlāḥī says they rejected a human who claimed revelation and insisted on an angel who would read actual written messages; he calls their demand a pretext rooted in conceit. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 98:2/q18 How do these pages relate to earlier revealed books?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the sheets are Qur'anic content that gathers earlier revelation or the earlier scriptures themselves.
- 98:2/q18/p1 The Qur'an confirms, preserves and gathers the uncorrupted substance of earlier scriptures. — Al-Bursawī says it confirms fundamental laws and rulings; al-Rāghib says earlier laws were partly superseded and partly completed by Muhammad's law. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 2:97 and 87:18–19, calls the Qur'an their gathered fruit, and says 'books' for their yield is a part-for-whole metaphor; his full commentary says the Qur'an adds further merit. [8 notes: BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ITQAN(الراغب); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 98:2/q18/p2 The phrase can point to the purified scriptures sent to earlier prophets. — Al-Māwardī gives this alternative connection to Muhammad's prophethood. [2 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL]

## 98:2/q19 How does this description connect to the neighboring sūra?  [* · links]
- 98:2/q19/p1 Al-Bayyina follows al-Qadr by explaining the exalted Qur'an and the rejection of its proof. — Iṣlāḥī says the People of the Book and Makkan idolaters rejected from conceit and used a demand for a sign as pretext. Elmalılı says the previous sūra explains revelation's wisdom while this one explains the interval before Messenger and Qur'an and subsequent accountability. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI]

## 98:2/q20 What report describes the Prophet reciting this sūra to Ubayy, and why?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the reported command names this sūra specifically or the Qur'an generally.
- 98:2/q20/p1 God ordered the Prophet to recite this sūra or its opening to Ubayy, and Ubayy wept when told he was named. — Anas transmits the command and Ubayy's tears; Ubayy's report has the opening followed by upright non-polytheist religion and reward for good. Ibn Kathīr says the recitation communicated, confirmed and warned; he conjectures it reassured Ubayy after doubt over Ibn Masʿūd's differing recitation and the seven authorized modes. [7 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(أنس بن مالك); MUSNAD-AHMAD(أبي بن كعب); DURR-FULL(أبي بن كعب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبي بن كعب)]
- 98:2/q20/p2 In Qatāda's variant the command was to recite the Qur'an to Ubayy. — Al-Baghawī records the broader wording of the command. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة)]

## 98:2/q21 What additional wording was transmitted alongside recitation of this sūra?  [* · reports]
- 98:2/q21/p1 Reports append upright monotheism, reward for good, human desire for more wealth, and repentance. — Ubayy's versions in Aḥmad and al-Suyūṭī differ in length; one includes the best and worst of creation and recompense. Ibn ʿAbbās separately recalls the two-valley saying: only dust fills the belly and God accepts repentance. Ubayy told ʿUmar he would not forbid writing the disputed wording, while remaining unsure whether it was revelation or Prophetic speech. [6 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(أبي بن كعب); DURR-FULL(أبي بن كعب,ابن عباس)]

## 98:2/q22 What merit is reported for reciting this sūra?  [* · reports]
- 98:2/q22/p1 Its reciter will be with the best of creation at the evening resting place on Judgment Day. — Al-Bayḍāwī reports this from the Prophet. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 98:2/q23 How is Muhammad's revelation defended against denial?  [* · links]
turns on: The denials elicited either comparison with prior revelation or direct divine and angelic testimony.
- 98:2/q23/p1 God revealed to Muhammad as to earlier prophets, sending messengers with warning and good news so people lack an argument against Him. — Ibn Isḥāq reports this reply to Sakeen and ʿAdī ibn Zayd's assertion that no revelation followed Moses, citing 4:163–165. [1 notes: IBNHISHAM; IBNHISHAM(سكين وعدي بن زيد)-]
- 98:2/q23/p2 God and the angels witness that the revelation to Muhammad was sent with God's knowledge. — Ibn Isḥāq reports a group's refusal to acknowledge the Prophet despite his saying they knew; the response in 4:166 invokes divine and angelic testimony. [2 notes: IBNHISHAM(جماعة منهم)]

## 98:2/q24 Which neighboring wording was reported with this description?  [* · readings]
turns on: The reported codex variants alter different parts of the preceding wording.
- 98:2/q24/p1 Ubayy's variant says the disbelievers had not ceased to be separated from the proof. — Al-Māwardī reports this reading of the preceding verse. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبي بن كعب)]
- 98:2/q24/p2 Ibn Masʿūd's variant puts the polytheists before the People of the Book. — Al-Māwardī reports this order in the preceding phrase. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 98:2/q25 What are the 'books' within the pages?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether kitāb denotes writing itself or a written decree and judgment.
- 98:2/q25/p1 Written Qur'anic verses or portions, including its sūras. — Al-Rāzī gives written verses; Ibn ʿĀshūr explains kutub as plural of kitāb, meaning what is written, and takes these books as portions or sūras of the Qur'an. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR]
- 98:2/q25/p2 Divine rulings. — Al-Rāzī reports Ṣāḥib al-Naẓm's argument from 'God has decreed I shall prevail' in 58:21 and a hadith using God's Book for His judgment. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 98:2/q26 What does 'upright' say of the books within the pages?  [* · meaning]
turns on: Whether qayyima stresses moral straightness and clarification or independent establishment as proof.
- 98:2/q26/p1 They have no deviation and clarify truth from falsehood. — Al-Zajjāj explains qayyima through uprightness and the root's sense of standing or establishment. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الزجاج)]
- 98:2/q26/p2 They stand established and independently serve as proof. — Al-Rāzī reports an interpretation drawing on the idiom of carrying a matter through. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
