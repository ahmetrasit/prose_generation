# 24:21: 31 questions

## 24:21/q01 Who is addressed by the call to believers?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the address is read as general exhortation, immediate reference to the slanderers, or a report about all believer-addresses.
- 24:21/q01/p1 Believers are addressed, but the ban extends to every morally accountable person. — Rāzī says the conditional form implies these believers have not yet followed Satan; unbelievers already have. The warning follows the warning to slanderers. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 24:21/q01/p2 The address particularly concerns participants in the slander, including Ḥassān, Misṭaḥ and Ḥamna. — Rāzī transmits Ibn ʿAbbās's three names but allows general application; Wāḥidī reports a restriction to the slanderers. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(آخرون); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 24:21/q01/p3 ʿAlī is foremost in every Qurʾanic address to believers. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits Ibn ʿAbbās's claim that other Companions were censured in the Qurʾan, whereas ʿAlī was not. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس)]

## 24:21/q02 What are Satan's steps here?  [خُطُوَٰتِ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase is explained as a general path, acts of disobedience, the immediate slander, or a gradual method of temptation.
- 24:21/q02/p1 His ways, paths, traces and promptings that people follow into wrongdoing. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī connect the image to actual footsteps; Rāzī applies it to hearing and spreading slander; Nasafī and Bursevī include whispered suggestions, and Ālūsī calls it metonymy for obedience in acts and omissions. Ibn Kathīr includes what Satan commands; Ikrima calls them impulses. Abū Ḥayyān refers back to 2:168 and Ṭabāṭabāʾī to 2:208. [15 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(بعضهم); IBNABIHATIM(عكرمة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 24:21/q02/p2 Every sin or disobedient deed is a step of Satan. — Ibn ʿAbbās says his deeds or everything God dislikes; Qatāda says every disobedience. Muqātil and al-Kalbī include acts unrecognized by law or prophetic practice; Māwardī pictures movement from one sin to the next. [10 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل، الكلبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(علي بن أبي طلحة عن ابن عباس,قتادة); BURSEVI]
- 24:21/q02/p3 Here they are Satan's embellishment of the false accusation against ʿĀʾisha and its circulation. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and Abū Mālik transmit this application; Ṭabarī says believers must avoid repeating the source's report, and Bayḍāwī identifies the steps with spreading indecency. Iṣlāḥī applies it to passing on claims without proof. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(أبو مالك,سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; DURR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 24:21/q02/p4 They are Satan's gradual movement from lawful to forbidden acts and from obedience to disobedience. — Ibn ʿĪsā describes that progression; Biqāʿī says Satan aims first at the worst deviation and descends to lesser evil if thwarted. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); BIQAI-FULL]

## 24:21/q03 How is khuṭuwāt read?  [خُطُوَٰتِ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted vowel or hamza on the word; transmitters' lists differ.
- 24:21/q03/p1 The ṭāʾ has ḍamma, the majority reading. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī name the majority; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it an ordinary permissible form. Rāzī records the vowelled option. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور وعاصم والأعمش); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 24:21/q03/p2 The ṭāʾ has sukūn. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī name ʿĀṣim and al-Aʿmash; Bayḍāwī, Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr also list Nāfiʿ, al-Bazzī, Abū ʿAmr, Abū Bakr and Ḥamza, with transmission details varying. Qirāʾāt ER notes the same variant at both occurrences and suggests its consonant cluster may evoke a footfall. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عاصم والأعمش); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور وعاصم والأعمش); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(نافع والبزي برواية ابن ربيعة وأبو عمرو وأبو بكر وحمزة); IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER(skn)]
- 24:21/q03/p3 The ṭāʾ has fatḥa. — Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī record this additional vocalization. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(نافع والبزي برواية ابن ربيعة وأبو عمرو وأبو بكر وحمزة)]
- 24:21/q03/p4 A hamzated form, khaṭuʾāt, is read. — Ibn Jinnī names ʿAlī, al-Aʿraj, ʿAmr ibn ʿUbayd and Sallām, and separately cites Abū al-Sammāl's form, referring to an earlier discussion. [1 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(علي والأعرج وعمرو بن عبيد وسلام وأبو السمال)]

## 24:21/q04 What is the word khuṭuwāt derived from?  [خُطُوَٰتِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the underlying singular denotes a footstep or a misdeed with a softened hamza.
- 24:21/q04/p1 It is the plural of khuṭwa, an actual step or distance between the feet. — Qurṭubī distinguishes khuṭwa, the distance, from khaṭwa, the verbal noun; cites a report on crossing Friday prayer rows. Bursevī varies the vowel distinction, calling the fatḥa form one stride. Ālūsī says the medial vowel distinguishes noun from adjective; Rāzī describes singular and plural vowels. Ibn ʿĀshūr describes moving the rear foot past the other and compares 2:168. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 24:21/q04/p2 It may be the plural of khaṭaʾ, a misdeed, with its hamza eased. — Mundhir ibn Saʿīd offers this alternative derivation. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(منذر بن سعيد)]

## 24:21/q05 What does the image and repeated wording of following Satan do?  [لَا تَتَّبِعُوا۟ خُطُوَٰتِ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنِ وَمَن يَتَّبِعْ خُطُوَٰتِ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنِ · rhetoric]
- 24:21/q05/p1 The walking image makes an unseen temptation visible and the repeated explicit phrase strengthens the warning. — Ibn ʿĀshūr depicts whispering as Satan walking and obedience as following; Biqāʿī and Ālūsī say naming Satan and his steps again heightens aversion. Biqāʿī says the verb implies deliberate resolve and suggests pardon for inadvertent slips; Ibn Kathīr praises the warning's clarity and force. [6 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 24:21/q06 What do vows called Satan's steps entail?  [خُطُوَٰتِ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنِ · law]
- 24:21/q06/p1 Vows to commit sin are Satan's steps; they are not fulfilled, and an oath expiation may be required. — Abū Majliz includes sinful vows; the Prophet's reported ruling concerns anger vows and oath expiation. Ibn Masʿūd orders a man abstaining from udder or food to eat and expiate. Abū Rāfiʿ's wife threatened to become Jewish or Christian and free slaves unless divorced; Ibn ʿUmar, Zaynab and ʿĀṣim call this satanic, not grounds for divorce. Masrūq or al-Shaʿbī directs one who vowed to sacrifice his son to sacrifice a ram. Bursevī excludes vows to obey, including prayer and fasting. [12 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(أبو مجلز,النبي); IBNABIHATIM(أبو رافع,أبو مجلز,عبد الله بن مسعود,مسروق); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو مجلز); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو رافع,أبو مجلز,ابن مسعود,الشعبي); BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 24:21/q07 Why must one not follow Satan's steps?  [وَمَن يَتَّبِعْ خُطُوَٰتِ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنِ فَإِنَّهُۥ يَأْمُرُ بِٱلْفَحْشَآءِ وَٱلْمُنكَرِ · interpretation]
- 24:21/q07/p1 Satan commands indecency and wrong, and following him draws the follower into them. — Bayḍāwī says this clause explains the prohibition. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the follower falls into both because the command's general addressee includes him. Study Quran compares Satan's commands in 2:169 and 2:268 and the warning elsewhere in 2:168, 2:208 and 6:142. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 24:21/q08 To whom does the pronoun in fa-innahu refer?  [فَإِنَّهُۥ يَأْمُرُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the conditional response requires a pronoun returning to man, or permits an implicit link or ellipsis.
- 24:21/q08/p1 It refers to Satan, who commands the follower. — Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies the omitted general object of 'commands,' which includes the follower. Samīn says this parse, like the matter-pronoun parse, requires allowing a conditional answer without a pronoun returning to the conditional noun. Ālūsī also discusses an omitted answer supplied by the following clause, answering the objection based on a poetic line about past conditions. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 24:21/q08/p2 It refers to whoever follows Satan, who becomes a leader commanding others to evil. — Abū Ḥayyān's analysis needs no ellipsis and makes the conditional answer contain a returning pronoun; Samīn reports the same syntactic reference. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]
- 24:21/q08/p3 It is a pronoun of the matter, introducing the ensuing statement. — Abū al-Baqāʾ gives this parse; Samīn notes its dependence on allowing no resumptive pronoun in the conditional answer. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL]

## 24:21/q09 How does man yatbaʿ work grammatically?  [وَمَن يَتَّبِعْ · grammar]
- 24:21/q09/p1 Man is conditional, yatbaʿ is jussive, and fa-innahu introduces the response. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says all readers agree on the jussive; his analysis links the response through the general, unexpressed object of 'commands.' [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 24:21/q10 What is al-faḥshāʾ here?  [بِٱلْفَحْشَآءِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word names all extreme ugliness, sin broadly, or the particular sexual offense.
- 24:21/q10/p1 Extreme ugliness or vile action, including ugly speech. — Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī and Ālūsī stress extremity; Bursevī includes custom and reason and both word and deed. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 2:169. [9 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 24:21/q10/p2 It means sins generally. — Abū Mālik and Suyūṭī's Durr tie this to the accusation against ʿĀʾisha. [3 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(أبو مالك); DURR-FULL]
- 24:21/q10/p3 It means fornication. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan and ʿIkrima on this gloss; Ṭabarī pairs it with evil speech as munkar. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس، الحسن، عكرمة)]

## 24:21/q11 What is al-munkar here?  [وَٱلْمُنكَرِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether rejection is defined by law, human moral recognition, a particular utterance, or shirk.
- 24:21/q11/p1 What religious law, scripture or prophetic practice rejects. — Baghawī says what God dislikes; Bayḍāwī, Bursevī and Ālūsī invoke religious law; Abū Layth specifies revealed law and Sunna. Al-Mufradāt includes what sound reason finds ugly or cannot assess when the Law condemns it. Ibn ʿĀshūr also includes people of virtue and compares 3:104; Asad refers to 16:90. [8 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(أبو الليث,المفردات); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 24:21/q11/p2 What people or sound minds fail to recognize and recoil from. — Rāzī and Nasafī describe the soul's rejection; Abū Ḥayyān speaks of sound minds. Muqātil's unrecognized thing is applied by Abū Mālik and Durr to the accusation. [6 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(أبو مالك); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL]
- 24:21/q11/p3 It means evil speech, particularly slander. — Ṭabarī pairs evil speech with fornication as the preceding faḥshāʾ. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]
- 24:21/q11/p4 It means shirk. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan and ʿIkrima, paired with fornication as faḥshāʾ. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس، الحسن، عكرمة)]

## 24:21/q12 Why is Satan said to command?  [يَأْمُرُ بِٱلْفَحْشَآءِ وَٱلْمُنكَرِ · rhetoric]
- 24:21/q12/p1 Calling his embellishment and goading a command disparages those who obey him. — Bursevī says their compliance makes a temptation function like an order. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 24:21/q13 What does God's favor and mercy provide here?  [فَضْلُ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْكُمْ وَرَحْمَتُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether favor is specified by repentance and guidance, delayed punishment in the scandal, or the source of all good works.
- 24:21/q13/p1 Divine bounty, guidance, forgiveness and the opportunity for cleansing repentance. — Ibn Isḥāq calls favor a bestowal and Saʿīd a blessing; Bayḍāwī and Bursevī include explained guidance, enabling repentance and legal penalties as expiation. Ālūsī adds clear signs and excludes apostasy from expiatory penalties according to a group. Biqāʿī distinguishes favor's uplift from mercy's repentance. Kuranyolu says repentance remains open through life. Ibn ʿĀshūr links guidance and forgiveness to resisting Satan's formidable trial, citing 38:82–83. [17 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير,محمد بن إسحاق); THALABI; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الحديث); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 24:21/q13/p2 God's favor forestalls swift punishment or destruction for the slander. — Ṭabarī names compassion and delayed punishment; Rāzī says God preserved them, delayed punishment and enabled repentance, and mercy continues to benefit even one who wrongs himself. Bayḍāwī says repetition underscores the offense's gravity. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 24:21/q13/p3 Grace is the source of even abundant worship and all human good. — Al-Siyārī contrasts grace with worship, prayer and jihad; Ibn ʿAbbās says no one finds benefit or avoids harm unaided. Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 3:26, 4:79 and an allusion in 11:108 for goodness originating with God. [5 notes: SULAMI(السياري); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); TABATABAI]

## 24:21/q14 What is the answer to the earlier lawlā clause in this passage?  [وَلَوْلَا فَضْلُ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْكُمْ وَرَحْمَتُهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether intervening speech prevents the later purification clause from serving as the omitted answer.
- 24:21/q14/p1 Its apodosis is omitted, understood as destruction or punishment averted by grace. — Ṭabarī infers it from the warning; Zamakhsharī says repeated omission intensifies emphasis as with earlier divine names in 24:10 and 24:20. Rāzī prefers omission because intervening speech separates the later clause, and reports 'perished,' 'punished' or 'destroyed' as reconstructions. Bayḍāwī says it was already stated. Burhān says negative lawlā answers are omitted in Qurʾanic usage, against Ibn ʿAṣfūr's preference for a lām with negative mā. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURHAN; RAZI-FULL+]
- 24:21/q14/p2 The following 'none of you would have been purified' is its answer. — Rāzī reports this analysis; al-Kisāʾī treats the intervening prohibition as parenthetical and the later clause as answering both it and the earlier lawlā. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي); RAZI-FULL-]
- 24:21/q14/p3 Without grace indecency would spread and its harm grow. — Rāzī reports Abū Muslim's proposed completion. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم)]

## 24:21/q15 What does lawlā contribute grammatically?  [وَلَوْلَا · grammar]
- 24:21/q15/p1 Here it is an exception-preventing particle followed by a nominal clause; a positive answer takes lām and a negative answer does not. — Itqān adds that a following pronoun should be nominative. It also records lawlā as halā for exhortation or offer and as rebuke or regret elsewhere; al-Harawī's suggested interrogative examples are better read as halā. The majority reject al-Harawī's negation analysis of another verse, supported by Ubayy's halā reading. [4 notes: ITQAN; ITQAN(الجمهور,الهروي)+]

## 24:21/q16 Why is favor and mercy repeated?  [فَضْلُ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْكُمْ وَرَحْمَتُهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 24:21/q16/p1 The repetition recalls delayed punishment and emphasizes the gravity of the slander and dependence on God. — Yaḥyā refers to the earlier explanation at 24:20; Zamakhsharī notes repeated omitted answers, Bayḍāwī the delayed punishment, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī the now explicit answer about purification. Asad sends readers to 24:10 and its note. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 24:21/q17 Whom does 'none of you' concern?  [مَا زَكَىٰ مِنكُم مِّنْ أَحَدٍ · referent]
turns on: Whether the statement is universal or locally addressed to the people implicated in the slander.
- 24:21/q17/p1 All people or all the addressees: none could be guided or made righteous unaided. — Ahl al-maʿānī and some exegetes give the general reading. Ibn ʿAbbās says no one can secure good or avert harm alone; Ṭabrisī mentions Satan's whispering. Ibn ʿĀshūr connects it to the danger of Satan's trial; Kuranyolu names ego and Satan as life's obstacles. [14 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); BAGHAWI-FULL(بعض المفسرين); TABRISI(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL; ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 24:21/q17/p2 It particularly concerns those who took part in the slander; none would be cleansed of that offense without accepted repentance. — Ibn ʿAbbās via ʿAṭāʾ says their repentance would not have been accepted; Wāḥidī explains repair after their sin. Baghawī reports this group, while Nasafī stresses expiatory repentance and Wāḥidī's Wajīz specifies forgiveness. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(آخرون,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس عن عطاء,قوم); JISHUMI; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 24:21/q18 What does zakā mean here?  [زَكَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the outcome is described as cleansing, moral soundness, or guidance and Islam.
- 24:21/q18/p1 To become pure or cleansed from sin. — Ibn Qutayba gives purity; Ṭabarī specifies sins and shirk; Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī the stain of sin. Rāzī likens a zakī person's attained obedience to a mature crop; one is called pure only when truly so. Biqāʿī includes growth in the word's sense. [8 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 24:21/q18/p2 To become sound, upright or righteous. — Muqātil and Abū Zayd give soundness; Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Q and later summaries use ṣāliḥ. [8 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل، أبو زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 24:21/q18/p3 To be guided, enter Islam or recognize right guidance. — Ibn Zayd generalizes that every Qurʾanic zakā or tazakkā means Islam; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī associate this sense with the majority's light reading. Study Quran reports guidance and Islam separately. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن زيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور); ITQAN; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]

## 24:21/q19 How is mā zakā read?  [مَا زَكَىٰ · readings]
turns on: Gemination of kāf and treatment of the final alif; transmitted reader lists and imāla vary.
- 24:21/q19/p1 A light kāf, the common reading, with the verb describing becoming pure or guided. — Jishumī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya explain the sense; Samīn derives it from zakā yazkū and records imāla or its absence on the final alif. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the familiar reading has no imāla; Abu Ḥayyān reports that some do apply it. Ibn Mujāhid names Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī among forms left without imāla. [8 notes: IBNMUJAHID(حمزة، الكسائي); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 24:21/q19/p2 A doubled kāf, mā zakkā, making purification God's action on the person. — Thaʿlabī cites the later yuzakkī in support and names Ibn Muḥayṣin and Yaʿqūb; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī name Abū Ḥaywa and al-Ḥasan; Samīn names al-Aʿmash and Abū Jaʿfar. Ālūsī reports gemination with imāla from Rūḥ and al-Aʿmash. The reader lists vary. [8 notes: THALABI(ابن محيص ويعقوب); JISHUMI(ابن محيصن ويعقوب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حيوة والحسن); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن وأبو حيوة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(الأعمش وأبو جعفر); ALUSI-FULL(روح والأعمش)]
- 24:21/q19/p3 An imāla of the alif is transmitted. — Ibn Jinnī names Abū Jaʿfar, Shayba, two ʿĪsās, and reports from ʿĀṣim and al-Aʿmash. He explains that imāla is a kind of inflection and verbs admit it more readily than nouns, contrasting al-ʿafā and al-sanā; reports differ on whether this accompanies gemination. [6 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(أبو جعفر وشيبة وعيسى الهمداني وعيسى الثقفي وعاصم والأعمش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL(روح والأعمش)]

## 24:21/q20 Why is zakā written with a yāʾ-shaped alif?  [زَكَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the written yāʾ shape is explained by imāla or analogy, or treated as orthography independent of oral transmission.
- 24:21/q20/p1 The spelling can reflect imāla or analogy with the doubled form despite the wāw root. — Ibn Jinnī derives the verb from zakawtu yazkū and uses that to account for imāla; Samīn and Ālūsī offer imāla or the doubled reading for the orthography, the latter citing Abū Ḥayyān. [3 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; SAMIN-DURR(الأعمش وأبو جعفر); ALUSI-FULL]
- 24:21/q20/p2 The expected spelling is plain alif, but the muṣḥaf's yāʾ shape does not change the spoken reading. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the familiar light verb has a wāw root and no imāla; transmission safeguards pronunciation, and only altering pronounced letters would conflict with the muṣḥaf. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 24:21/q21 How are minkum min aḥadin parsed?  [مِنكُم مِّنْ أَحَدٍ · grammar]
- 24:21/q21/p1 The first min explains the group; the second is extra for emphasis, and aḥad is the subject with light zakā or object with doubled zakkā. — Bursevī gives the subject parse; Samīn and Ālūsī contrast both readings and make God the subject of doubled zakkā. Biqāʿī says repeated min excludes every single person. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 24:21/q22 What force does abadan have?  [أَبَدًۭا · meaning]
- 24:21/q22/p1 It extends the denial through all time, without an endpoint. — Biqāʿī connects it with emphatic min, while Bursevī and Ālūsī gloss it as forever. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 24:21/q23 How does God purify whom He wills?  [وَلَٰكِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُزَكِّى مَن يَشَآءُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether purification is stated as divine making-righteous, specified through repentance, or conditioned on preparedness and a request.
- 24:21/q23/p1 He makes the chosen person pure or righteous by grace. — Muqātil, Yaḥyā, Saʿīd and al-Kalbī give righteousness; Ibn Qutayba gives purification. Ṭabrisī describes favor that makes a person righteous before God. Ibn Kathīr adds different degrees of removal of shirk, immorality and bad traits, and divine choice in guidance or misguidance; Biqāʿī mentions soul and heart diseases and protection from the slander. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says good works reveal prior destiny for felicity. [12 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL]
- 24:21/q23/p2 He guides people to repentance and accepts it, cleansing their sins. — Wāḥidī relates Ibn ʿAbbās's will to accept the slanderers' repentance; Nasafī specifies sincere repentance. Bayḍāwī, Bursevī and Ālūsī include both enabling and accepting repentance, the last limiting the slanderers to those free of hypocrisy. Abū Ḥayyān allows this alongside prior felicity. [10 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,غيره); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 24:21/q23/p3 He purifies those prepared for it who request it through their state of readiness. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God hears their request and knows their preparedness. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 24:21/q24 Does divine purification create a person's purity or describe grace and judgment?  [ٱللَّهَ يُزَكِّى مَن يَشَآءُ · theology]
turns on: Whether tazkiya means producing purity, providing enabling grace, or judging purity, and how divine will relates to evil acts.
- 24:21/q24/p1 God produces purity in the person; purification depends on His will and enabling. — Rāzī's school compares tazkiya to making something black or red. Rāzī says the verse explicitly supports its account; the will clause and 'favor and mercy' make purification discretionary. Bursevī says the verse refutes Qadariyya claims of purification through worship alone. Ibn ʿAṭiyya treats deeds as signs of prior felicity. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا); IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; RAZI-FULL+]
- 24:21/q24/p2 God performs gracious acts, luṭf, that help the servant become pure. — Rāzī attributes this to Muʿtazilites, then argues: ineffectual luṭf is irrelevant; an impossible effect deters, a merely possible effect lacks a determinant, and a necessary effect makes God creator of the act. He adds that obligatory luṭf cannot be the contingent favor and mercy described here. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); RAZI-FULL-]
- 24:21/q24/p3 God judges or declares that a servant is pure. — Rāzī attributes this second interpretation to Muʿtazilites and objects that God must declare the truth and cannot make it depend on will without falsehood. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); RAZI-FULL-]
- 24:21/q24/p4 God does not will the indecency Satan wills; He acts for morally responsible people's good. — Ṭabrisī argues that if God created and willed the indecency He condemns, He would merit greater blame than its commander, which is impossible for Him; His hearing and knowledge inform beneficial action. [2 notes: TABRISI]

## 24:21/q25 What spiritual means help a person receive purification?  [ٱللَّهَ يُزَكِّى مَن يَشَآءُ · inward]
- 24:21/q25/p1 God enables obedience by grace, while a teacher or spiritual guide helps a seeker learn purification. — Bursevī calls the Prophet the greatest means; ʿAbd Allāh al-Anṣārī credits Abū al-Ḥasan al-Kharaqānī rather than his many hadith and law teachers for teaching him the spiritual path. Guides and the perfected person's companionship open certainty. [3 notes: BURSEVI(عبد الله الأنصاري)]

## 24:21/q26 What does 'Hearing, Knowing' encompass here?  [وَٱللَّهُ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌۭ · interpretation]
- 24:21/q26/p1 God hears speech, including slander and repentance, and knows inner states, intentions and truth. — Muqātil specifies speech about ʿĀʾisha; Wāḥidī and Nasafī include remorse and sincere repentance; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says God distinguishes true from false without error. Biqāʿī says He knows suitability for purification; Ibn Kathīr who deserves guidance or misguidance. Ibn ʿĀshūr joins threat for rumor-spreaders with reward for its opponents, and Iṣlāḥī warns against self-indulgence. Ṭabāṭabāʾī applies hearing to the request for purification and knowledge to readiness. [15 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 24:21/q27 Why repeat God's name in the closing statement?  [وَٱللَّهُ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 24:21/q27/p1 The explicit divine name reinforces hearing and knowledge and gives the close the force of an independent maxim. — Ālūsī says the repeated name evokes divinity and urges sincere repentance; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the sentence stands like a proverb. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 24:21/q28 How does this warning relate to the slander passage and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connection is to the immediate slander narrative or wider parallel teachings; both may apply.
- 24:21/q28/p1 It identifies slander, credulous rumor-spreading and desire for scandal as Satan's path in the surrounding account. — Ibn ʿĀshūr places it after ten slander verses; Iṣlāḥī locates 11–26 in the hypocrites' false accusation campaign and says some Muslims unwittingly amplified it. He connects the warning with the preceding directive and demands proof before relaying claims. Study Quran names the false accusation against ʿĀʾisha and community response. Asad warns accusers against forgetting human weakness. [9 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES]
- 24:21/q28/p2 The verse's warning parallels other Qurʾanic statements about Satan and divine goodness. — Abū Ḥayyān points to 2:168, Ṭabāṭabāʾī to 2:208, Study Quran to 2:168, 2:208, 6:142, 2:169 and 2:268; Ṭabāṭabāʾī links divine goodness with 3:26, 4:79 and 11:108. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares faḥshāʾ with 2:169 and munkar with 3:104. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 24:21/q29 What does this passage teach about willing or loving grave sin?  [* · theology]
turns on: The moral weight assigned to a resolve compared with the intended act.
- 24:21/q29/p1 Inward love, intention or approval can itself incur blame; willing disbelief is disbelief. — Jishumī infers accountability from the threat attached to love and reports agreement that intending, desiring, approving or loving disbelief counts as disbelief, all being acts of the heart. [2 notes: JISHUMI]
- 24:21/q29/p2 Resolving to commit a grave sin is as grave as the intended deed. — Jishumī attributes this to Abū ʿAlī. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي)]
- 24:21/q29/p3 Resolve is less grave than the intended sin, although it can itself become grave. — Jishumī attributes this to Abū Hāshim. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو هاشم)]

## 24:21/q30 Do legal penalties expiate the sins they punish?  [فَضْلُ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْكُمْ وَرَحْمَتُهُۥ · law]
- 24:21/q30/p1 Prescribed penalties can expiate sins, except apostasy according to one group. — Bayḍāwī and Bursevī count these penalties among God's favor. Ālūsī reports a group's exclusion of apostasy and says they answer an earlier Abū Hurayra report by dating it before revelation of this rule. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(الحديث); ALUSI-FULL(جمع)]

## 24:21/q31 What conduct does the warning call for?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes offer complementary practical counsels.
- 24:21/q31/p1 Reject rumors and unfounded accusations; repent sincerely and guard one's speech. — Abū al-Dardāʾ reports the Prophet's warnings against partisanship without knowledge, thwarting a divine limit and falsely damaging a Muslim, tying these to 24:19. Bursevī cites hadith that speech usually counts against its speaker, lying to a trusting brother is treachery, and one's own faults should occupy one. Ālūsī urges sincerity in repentance; Iṣlāḥī requires investigation. [7 notes: THALABI(أبو الدرداء); BURSEVI(الحديث); ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 24:21/q31/p2 Support purification through lawful livelihood and wise company while avoiding sinful company. — Bursevī advises lawful spending, the company of jurists and the wise, and avoidance of the ignorant and sinful. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
