# 43:75: 17 questions

## 43:75/q01 What does 'lā yufattaru ʿanhum' say about the punishment?  [لَا يُفَتَّرُ عَنْهُمْ · meaning]
- 43:75/q01/p1 Their punishment is never eased, reduced, suspended, or allowed to slacken, even briefly. — Muqātil and the Study Quran's IK specify not even an instant; the latter cites 25:65. Al-Ṭabarī says fatūr has the root sense of weakness; al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Ālūsī compare a fever subsiding and losing heat. Bursawī cites al-Rāghib's definition: stillness after intensity, softness after severity, and weakness after strength. Ibn ʿĀshūr says no one lessens or calms it, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī glosses tafṭīr as mitigation. Iṣlāḥī says no part of the all-encompassing torment is removed. The Study Quran connects its inescapability and lack of security to 25:65 and 70:28. Al-Farrāʾ specifies that it is the punishment they are in. [21 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(IK)]

## 43:75/q02 How is the clause 'lā yufattaru ʿanhum' construed?  [لَا يُفَتَّرُ عَنْهُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause is treated as circumstantial or as an additional predicate.
- 43:75/q02/p1 It is a circumstantial clause about their punishment. — Al-Samīn calls it a circumstantial clause. Ibn ʿĀshūr relates it to Hellfire punishment and adds that yufattaru is the doubled, transitively used form of fatara. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 43:75/q02/p2 It is another predicate, saying the punishment is neither eased nor diminished. — Al-Nasafī expressly calls it another predicate. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 43:75/q03 Who are the people referred to by 'ʿanhum'?  [عَنْهُمْ · referent]
- 43:75/q03/p1 They are the disbelieving criminals condemned to Hell, particularly polytheist unbelievers. — Al-Qushayrī specifies polytheist unbelievers who remain there forever. Ibn ʿAṭiyya identifies the disbelievers contrasted with Paradise's people; Bursawī contrasts unbelievers with believers who retain hope. Kur'an Yolu says unbelievers and sinners open the road to Hell by misusing the means given them. Iṣlāḥī and the Study Quran describe the corresponding fate of those who deny the true call. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 43:75/q04 What does 'fīhi' refer to?  [فِيهِ · referent]
turns on: The masculine received pronoun points to punishment; the feminine variant points to Hell or the Fire.
- 43:75/q04/p1 In the received wording, it refers to the punishment in which they remain. — Al-Ṭabarī says this locates them in Hell while they despair of rescue and submit to torment. Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Naḥḥās reporting al-Farrāʾ, and al-Ṭabarī contrast this masculine pronoun with the feminine alternative. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله); NAHHAS(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 43:75/q04/p2 In the reading 'fīhā,' the feminine pronoun refers to Hell or the Fire. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās citing al-Farrāʾ, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī report the reading attributed to ʿAbd Allāh; al-Samīn says the mention of punishment indicates the Fire. [5 notes: FARRA(عبد الله); TAB-FULL(عبد الله); NAHHAS(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(عبد الله); ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله)]

## 43:75/q05 How is the phrase containing 'fīhi' read?  [وَهُمْ فِيهِ مُبْلِسُونَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the phrase contains the masculine pronoun, a feminine pronoun for Hell, or no expressed locative pronoun.
- 43:75/q05/p1 The received wording has 'fīhi,' with a masculine pronoun referring to punishment. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Naḥḥās distinguish it from the reading with fīhā. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله); NAHHAS(الفراء)]
- 43:75/q05/p2 ʿAbd Allāh reads 'fīhā,' with a feminine pronoun referring to Hell or the Fire. — Reported by al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās citing al-Farrāʾ, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī; al-Samīn says the prior mention of punishment indicates the Fire. [5 notes: FARRA(عبد الله); TAB-FULL(عبد الله); NAHHAS(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(عبد الله); ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله)]
- 43:75/q05/p3 Ibn Masʿūd reads 'wa-hum mublisūn,' omitting 'fīhi.' — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says this reading clarifies that they are in Hell. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 43:75/q06 What does 'mublisūn' mean here?  [مُبْلِسُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether iblās names despair itself, surrender, sorrow, a changed condition, or silence; Ibn ʿĀshūr specifically separates silence as an effect of despair from its lexical definition.
- 43:75/q06/p1 They despair of mercy, rescue, relief, escape, or every good outcome. — Muqātil adds certainty of punishment, tidings of evil, blue eyes, and black faces. Ibn Qutayba and Ibn al-Jawzī specify God's mercy; Ibn al-Jawzī points to his earlier discussion at 6:44. Al-Farrāʾ and al-Ṭabarī specify salvation, the latter also describing submission to torment. Al-Qushayrī glosses iblās as disappointment; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Qatāda and others saying cast away from every good outcome. Al-Bayḍāwī specifies escape; al-Nasafī relief and bewilderment; Bursawī escape, rest, and any lighter punishment. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds humiliation but treats silence as a consequence rather than part of the definition. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says mercy or escape from Fire; Iṣlāḥī says even the remote hope of relief or release that consoles sufferers is gone. Several authorities pair despair with silence, surrender, sorrow, or changing condition, represented separately below. [26 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA(عبد الله); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(قتادة); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة وغيره); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(المفردات,تاج المصادر); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 43:75/q06/p2 They are silent or dumbstruck in despair, unable to argue or speak in their own defense. — Al-Wāḥidī and Abū Ḥayyān describe silence with despair. Al-Qurṭubī reports this as another view. Bursawī cites al-Mufradāt: the despairing lose their argument and fall silent. Al-Ālūsī says they forget what to say. The Study Quran reports Iṣ's gloss of regret and speechlessness, mentioning 6:42–44 and 30:49. Ibn ʿĀshūr accepts silence as an implication of despair while denying that it belongs to the definition itself. [6 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(المفردات); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Iṣ); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 43:75/q06/p3 They are surrendered, resigned, and subdued under the punishment. — ʿAbd al-Razzāq reports Qatāda pairing surrender with despair. Al-Ṭabarī reports Qatāda's separate gloss of surrender and also describes submission to torment himself. Al-Durr reports Qatāda as calling them resigned and subdued. [5 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 43:75/q06/p4 Iblās conveys sorrow, brokenness, or grief from intense distress. — Bursawī cites Tāj al-Maṣādir for despair, brokenness, and sorrow, and al-Mufradāt for sorrow caused by intense despair and the derivation of Iblīs's name. Al-Ālūsī reports al-Rāghib's account of grief from severe distress and one derivation of Iblīs's name. The Study Quran reports Iṣ's nuance of regret. [4 notes: BURSEVI(المفردات,تاج المصادر); ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); STUDYQURAN(Iṣ)]
- 43:75/q06/p5 Their condition has changed. — This is al-Suddī's reported gloss in al-Ṭabarī. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي)]

## 43:75/q07 How is 'wa-hum fīhi mublisūn' attached grammatically?  [وَهُمْ فِيهِ مُبْلِسُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether its syntactic role is identified as circumstantial or by its coordination with the preceding statement of abiding.
- 43:75/q07/p1 It is a circumstantial clause describing their abiding state. — Al-Samīn calls the phrase circumstantial. Al-Ālūsī notes that the circumstantial clause remains tied to their ongoing abiding state even when discussing the coordinating wāw. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 43:75/q07/p2 It is coordinated with the statement that the criminals abide in Hell's punishment. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the preceding statement of abiding as the clause's point of coordination. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:75/q08 How can their despair coexist with their later call for help?  [مُبْلِسُونَ · links]
turns on: Whether the call and despair occur in alternating periods, or the conjunction makes no claim about their temporal order; the continuous despair clause is raised against alternation.
- 43:75/q08/p1 They fall silent in despair at some times and cry out at others during their long punishment. — Al-Ālūsī offers this reconciliation, while reporting an objection that alternating states may not suit the continuous wording 'they are despairing.' [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 43:75/q08/p2 The coordinating wāw does not impose a time sequence, so their call need not follow their despair. — Al-Ālūsī reports this reply from others and notes that the circumstantial description remains connected with their abiding state. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 43:75/q09 How does this punishment passage connect with the surrounding passage and the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Ibn ʿĀshūr offers either completion of an earlier interrupted warning or a resumption prompted by the newly described believers; other sources stress the adjacent contrast.
- 43:75/q09/p1 It places the deniers' fate after the believers' Paradise to contrast the two outcomes and make the warning vivid. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the turn after Paradise makes the warning clear; Kur'an Yolu says juxtaposition makes the contrast clearer and more vivid. Iṣlāḥī says the closing passage gives the respective fates of accepters and deniers, then assures the Prophet about mockers and closes with tawḥīd and rejection of intercession as rescue; he describes the whole sūra as a harmonious unit governed by its central theme. The Study Quran likewise notes an extended Hereafter contrast. Ibn ʿĀshūr's resumptive explanation answers the implied question about the opposite of the believers just described; on either of his accounts, the sentence has no syntactic position in the larger construction. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 43:75/q09/p2 It completes the warning begun at 43:65 and developed in 43:66–67 after the believers' promise interrupted it. — Ibn ʿĀshūr offers this as one function and says the sentence has no syntactic position in the larger construction on either account. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:75/q10 Does this unending despair apply to monotheist believers who enter Hell?  [* · theology]
- 43:75/q10/p1 No; unbelievers remain and despair, while monotheists who enter Hell retain hope and eventually leave. — Al-Qushayrī distinguishes everlasting punishment for polytheist unbelievers from temporary punishment of monotheists, and infers from iblās as disappointment that believers remain hopeful until their reckoning ends. Bursawī reports al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya that monotheists do not remain and their punishment is eased, and holds that believers in affliction retain hope. Al-Qushayrī reports the shaykhs, and Bursawī some Sufi elders, saying a believer's Hell ordeal can be easier on the heart than worldly life because rescue is assured, unlike worldly fear of destruction. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI(الشيوخ); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,قال بعض الشيوخ)]

## 43:75/q11 What do the reports say about death among people of the Fire before release?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on whether people awaiting removal from the Fire remain alive and able to suffer or die and cease feeling pain.
- 43:75/q11/p1 God does not let the people of the Fire die before taking them out. — Al-Qushayrī calls this report sound and explains that dead people cannot feel or suffer. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI(خبر صحيح)]
- 43:75/q11/p2 God causes monotheists in Hell to die until they are brought out. — Bursawī reports this account and says the dead feel no pain. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 43:75/q12 What account describes how a criminal is confined in the Fire?  [* · reports]
- 43:75/q12/p1 A criminal is enclosed in a coffin of fire and entombed there forever, unseen and unable to see. — Bursawī introduces this as one reported account, without naming its transmitter. [1 notes: BURSEVI(قيل)]

## 43:75/q13 What does the passage teach about divine justice and human responsibility for Hell?  [* · theology]
- 43:75/q13/p1 God wrongs no one; people bring punishment on themselves through their own wrongdoing and misuse of the means God gave them. — Al-Baghawī says God did not wrong them because they wronged themselves. Kur'an Yolu argues that imposing duties without giving ability and means would be unjust, that God has provided both, and that unbelievers and sinners misuse those means and thereby open the road to Hell. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 43:75/q14 What causes faulty recitation and how is accurate pronunciation learned?  [* · readings]
- 43:75/q14/p1 Habitual overemphasis and excessive thickening without sound teachers cause error; practice, precision, training, and rules correct it. — Ibn al-Jazarī describes faulty recitation in his region and says strong and weak, emphatic and light letters affect one another in connected speech, making strenuous practice necessary. [2 notes: NASHR]

## 43:75/q15 Which letters are light or emphatic in connected recitation?  [* · readings]
- 43:75/q15/p1 Low-tongue letters are light except specified divine-name lām cases, some emphatic letters in certain transmissions, and rāʾ; elevated letters are emphatic. — Ibn al-Jazarī adds that adjacent strong, weak, light, and emphatic sounds influence one another in connected speech. [2 notes: NASHR]

## 43:75/q16 Does alif have an inherent light or emphatic quality?  [* · readings]
turns on: Whether alif has its own emphatic quality or merely takes the quality of the preceding letter.
- 43:75/q16/p1 No; alif follows the preceding letter in lightness or emphasis. — Ibn al-Jazarī says earlier warnings about lightening alif opposed exaggeration that made it resemble wāw. [1 notes: NASHR; NASHR(أبو عبد الله محمد بن بصخان)-]
- 43:75/q16/p2 There is an emphatic alif, and denying it is mistaken. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports Ibn Baṣkhān's treatise, which attributes denial to ignorance, poor natural disposition, or lack of understanding. [1 notes: NASHR(أبو عبد الله محمد بن بصخان); NASHR-]

## 43:75/q17 What distinction is drawn between sīn and ṣād in the cited verbs?  [* · rhetoric]
- 43:75/q17/p1 Sīn in 'they are dragged' conveys pulling, while ṣād in 'they are accompanied' conveys companionship. — Al-Burhān states the distinction between the two cited verbs without relating it to a specific word in this verse. [1 notes: BURHAN]
