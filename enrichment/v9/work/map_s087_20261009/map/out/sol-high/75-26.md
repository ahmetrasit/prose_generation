# 75:26: 16 questions

## 75:26/q01 What does kallā mean here?  [كَلَّآ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether kallā rebukes disbelief and worldly attachment or affirms the certainty of death; the rebuke is variously tied to prior denial or preference for the immediate life.
- 75:26/q01/p1 It rebukes and warns people, especially for preferring this world and denying what lies beyond it. — Al-Zajjāj calls it a warning to desist from deeds leading to punishment; al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr relate it to preferring immediate life. Ibn ʿAṭiyya names Quraysh; al-Qurṭubī notes a clause begins after kallā. Ibn Kathīr and Study Quran say death reveals what deniers could previously deny. Al-Biqāʿī says death forcibly breaks attachment to this life. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this a fresh warning linked to 75:20. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects the warning with being driven to the Lord. [20 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK); JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; ELMALILI+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; TABATABAI+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 75:26/q01/p2 It denies the disbelievers' refusal to believe resurrection or requital. — Muqātil says they disbelieve the preceding account of Resurrection. Ibn Jarīr locates the pause at kallā and says it refutes the polytheists' belief that shirk and disobedience go unrecompensed. Al-Rāzī reports that the unbeliever may deny resurrection but cannot deny death and its pains; al-Qurṭubī likewise relates the rebuke to disbelief. [8 notes: NAHHAS(محمد بن جرير); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL; JISHUMI; MUQATIL+; TABRISI+]
- 75:26/q01/p3 It affirms emphatically: truly or indeed. — Al-Naḥḥās says kalla can mean 'indeed' and can serve as a subject in this construction. Al-Rāzī's reported view makes the certainty of death and its bitterness the point; al-Qurṭubī links the affirmation to the journey to God. Ibn Kathīr says on this reading the soul leaves the body and reaches the collarbones. Study Quran records both negative and affirmative readings. [8 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI; TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL(آخرون); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK)]

## 75:26/q02 What governs the conditional idhā?  [إِذَا بَلَغَتِ · grammar]
turns on: Which part of the surrounding passage completes or governs the time clause.
- 75:26/q02/p1 Its governor may be baṣīra. — Al-Naḥḥās names baṣīra as one possible governor. [1 notes: NAHHAS]
- 75:26/q02/p2 Its governor may be balaghat; the answer comes later. — Al-Naḥḥās explicitly gives this alternative and says the answer then occurs later in the passage. [1 notes: NAHHAS]
- 75:26/q02/p3 It attaches to the implied predicate 'the journey is to your Lord.' — Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies this later predicate as the construction to which 'when it reaches' attaches. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 75:26/q03 What reaches the tarāqī?  [بَلَغَتِ ٱلتَّرَاقِىَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the unstated feminine referent is the soul or the dying person's final breath.
- 75:26/q03/p1 The soul or spirit reaches them. — Ibn ʿAbbās specifies the disbeliever's soul; al-Bursawī calls it the rational soul or human spirit, and Elmalılı allows the soul that loves the world or the spirit. The verse's feminine verb and death context support the inference; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and others compare 56:83. Muqātil and Ibn ʿĀshūr describe reaching the throat as the same scene. [54 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(دريد بن الصمة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,المبرد,مقاتل); JISHUMI(دريد بن الصمة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,دريد بن الصمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN(الكسائي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(حاتم,دريد بن الصمة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(القمي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Farāhī); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT+; TABATABAI+; TABRISI+; THALABI+; WAHIDI-WAJIZ+]
- 75:26/q03/p2 The last breath reaches the throat. — Asad describes the last breath rising, as bystanders ask whether a wizard can save the dying person. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES+]

## 75:26/q04 How is the unspoken subject of balaghat supplied?  [بَلَغَتِ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical difference between an implicit subject and a deleted agent.
- 75:26/q04/p1 The soul is an implicit, contextually understood subject. — Al-Wāḥidī compares the unnamed sun in 38:32; al-Ṭabrisī and Iṣlāḥī compare the implied earth in 35:45; al-Qurṭubī, Farāhī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cite 56:83. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr compare Arabic 'it rained' or 'I sent' without naming the sky and Ḥātim's verse with an implicit feminine soul. Al-Samīn says al-tarāqī is the object of balaghat and the human context indicates its subject. Al-Burhān insists the agent is implicit, not actually deleted. [27 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(قيل); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(حاتم); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Farāhī); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; BURHAN+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 75:26/q04/p2 The agent is deleted wherever context identifies it. — Al-Kisāʾī groups this verse with the sun disappearing, punishment descending and a messenger coming to Solomon. The author of al-Burhān objects that these agents are implicit rather than deleted. [1 notes: BURHAN(الكسائي); BURHAN-]

## 75:26/q05 Why leave the subject unspoken?  [بَلَغَتِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the notes explain the omission solely by contextual recoverability or also by the sūra's theme of haste.
- 75:26/q05/p1 The context already makes the soul evident. — Al-Wāḥidī says listeners understand the soul without naming it, comparing the sun in 38:32; al-Ṭabrisī compares the earth in 35:45. Al-Iṭqān says the collarbones entail the soul just as a veil entails the sun. Al-Biqāʿī says the soul is inferred from the one who loves present life. Al-Samīn reports that the reference to the human being supplies it. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(قيل); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 75:26/q05/p2 The omission contributes to the sūra's pattern of haste. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares the omitted answer to the oath, governor of qādirīn, and antecedent of the tongue pronoun in 75:1, 75:4 and 75:16. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT+]

## 75:26/q06 What are the tarāqī?  [ٱلتَّرَاقِىَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term names the paired bones beside the throat, the throat itself, or chest bones/ribs; some descriptions overlap anatomically.
- 75:26/q06/p1 They are the paired collarbones bordering the hollow at the base of the neck. — Al-Layth and al-Rāghib describe each bone as joining the throat hollow to a shoulder. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Qurṭubī and others place one on either side; some call them upper chest bones or the upper front of the chest. Al-Ṭabrisī says breath or vapor rises there and cites Dhū al-Rumma for the death rattle. Al-Samīn rejects the Shaykh's description of the collarbone simply as a chest bone. [27 notes: THALABI(دريد بن الصمة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الليث,المبرد); DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(دريد بن الصمة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الراغب,الزمخشري,الشيخ); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; TABATABAI+; THALABI+]
- 75:26/q06/p2 They refer to the throat or its upper front. — Al-Tustarī defines al-tarāqī as the throat; Ibn Jurayj does likewise. Muqātil says the soul reaches the throat, and al-Jishumī also reports an upper-front-of-throat description. [6 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); JISHUMI; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); IBNASHUR-FULL; TUSTARI+]
- 75:26/q06/p3 They can be equated with chest ribs or breast bones. — Al-Dāmghānī equates the chest ribs of 86:7, al-tarāʾib, with the tarāqī. Al-Māwardī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Biqāʿī locate the bones in the upper chest; al-Samīn rejects the Shaykh's chest-bone definition as unrecognized. [4 notes: DAMGHANI; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]

## 75:26/q07 What is the form of tarāqī?  [ٱلتَّرَاقِىَ · grammar]
- 75:26/q07/p1 It is the plural of tarquwa, a collarbone. — Al-Samīn derives an underlying tarāqū whose wāw changes to yāʾ after kasrah; he assigns the pattern faʿluwa with original tāʾ and added wāw, agreeing with Abū al-Baqāʾ's rejection of tafʿila because raqū is unattested. Al-Iṭqān notes an open first vowel in tarquwa. [13 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ITQAN; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMIN-DURR+]

## 75:26/q08 Why use a plural for paired collarbones?  [ٱلتَّرَاقِىَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the plural follows a generic referent, an Arabic body-part convention, ease of expression, or the scene's imagery.
- 75:26/q08/p1 The soul or person is spoken of as a generic class. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the plural follows al-nafs as a generic class. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 75:26/q08/p2 Arabic can use a plural for a paired body part. — Al-Samīn reports al-Zamakhsharī's comparison with descriptions such as thick brows or broad shoulders; each person has two collarbones. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)]
- 75:26/q08/p3 The plural avoids the heaviness of the dual. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the plural 'your hearts' at 66:4. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 75:26/q08/p4 It suggests the soul's desperate movement into a narrow exit. — Al-Biqāʿī imagines movement from the body's remotest regions toward the narrowing throat. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL+]

## 75:26/q09 What does the article in al-tarāqī supply?  [ٱلتَّرَاقِىَ · grammar]
- 75:26/q09/p1 It stands in for the omitted possessor: the person's collarbones. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the similarly understood possessor in 'the journey.' [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 75:26/q10 How is the final yāʾ of al-tarāqī read or paused on?  [ٱلتَّرَاقِىَ · readings]
turns on: One note records a vowel reading; the other reports a pausal practice.
- 75:26/q10/p1 One reading gives the yāʾ sukūn. — Al-Samīn compares Zayd's reading of ahālīkum in 5:89 and says its justification was discussed earlier. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 75:26/q10/p2 Ibn Mujāhid paused on its yāʾ in dawn prayer. — Ibn Khālawayh reports this pause and says the following yāʾ in min maniyyin yumnā at 75:37 makes the letter clear. [1 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA(أبو بكر بن مجاهد)]

## 75:26/q11 What does the soul's reaching the tarāqī signify?  [بَلَغَتِ ٱلتَّرَاقِىَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether reaching is an idiom for approaching death or a literal stage after the soul has left the heart.
- 75:26/q11/p1 It signifies the near, severe moment of death and transition to the afterlife. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī cite Durayd ibn al-Ṣimma's idiom for lives reaching the collarbones. Al-Rāzī compares 33:10; Ibn Kathīr and others compare the soul at the throat in 56:83–85. Al-Zajjāj calls this the first hardship of the Hereafter; al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Biqāʿī and al-Bursawī mention the death rattle or choking. Ḥātim's verse, cited by al-Zamakhsharī and others, portrays constriction and the uselessness of wealth. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the final breaths the last stage of earthly life and derives 'breaths' from nafas with open fāʾ. Study Quran says the soul is about to depart. [39 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(دريد بن الصمة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); JISHUMI(دريد بن الصمة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(دريد بن الصمة); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(حاتم الطائي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(حاتم,دريد بن الصمة); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(القمي); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; BAGHAWI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 75:26/q11/p2 On a literal reading, reaching the collarbones means death has already happened. — Al-Rāzī reports a critic's argument: the soul would have left the heart before reaching the collarbones, yet later verses still describe signs of life. Al-Rāzī answers that the expression means nearing this state, so the objection does not follow. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]

## 75:26/q12 Does the death idiom imply life persists after death?  [بَلَغَتِ ٱلتَّرَاقِىَ · theology]
turns on: Whether 'reaches' describes completed passage of the soul from the heart or an approach to death.
- 75:26/q12/p1 No: it describes nearing that state, before death is complete. — Al-Rāzī answers the critic by taking 'reaches' as nearness rather than completed arrival. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+]
- 75:26/q12/p2 A literal arrival would mean death has already occurred despite later signs of life. — The critic cited by al-Rāzī reasons that the soul must leave the heart first. Al-Rāzī rejects this inference through the idiomatic reading. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]

## 75:26/q13 How does this death scene connect to the surrounding sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connection emphasized is the worldly preference and ensuing personal death or the disbelievers' denial of preceding Resurrection discourse.
- 75:26/q13/p1 It turns the warning against worldly preference to each person's inevitable death and journey to God. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī say immediate life ends as the everlasting afterlife begins. Al-Biqāʿī says death breaks the world's hold; Elmalılı describes a turn from cosmic end and afterlife outcomes to the individual's final crisis. Ibn ʿĀshūr links the warning to 75:20, the journey to God to 75:6, and cites the saying that each person's resurrection is death; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise describes being driven to the Lord. Iṣlāḥī stresses that no one escapes the agony. [17 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 75:26/q13/p2 It recalls the preceding account of Resurrection that disbelievers refuse to accept. — Muqātil makes the repudiation refer to disbelief in what has already been said about Resurrection; al-Rāzī says death and its pains still cannot be denied. [4 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(مقاتل); TABRISI]

## 75:26/q14 What do reports say happens as the soul reaches this point?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ over the speaker seeking help and include distinct accounts of the angel's question and the body's farewell.
- 75:26/q14/p1 Bystanders ask whether a healer can save the dying person. — Al-Farrāʾ describes the bystanders' question and the dying person's knowledge of separation. Kur'an Yolu reports the same scene; Asad says they ask whether a wizard can save him. [3 notes: FARRA; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES]
- 75:26/q14/p2 The dying person asks for a doctor or someone to recite over him. — Al-Qummī's report allows treatment or recitation and says he recognizes separation and the journey to the Lord. In al-Kāfī, Abū Jaʿfar al-Bāqir tells Jābir the dying person asks for a doctor, realizes separation from loved ones, and the entwined legs mark the world and afterlife meeting. [2 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر,القمي)]
- 75:26/q14/p3 The angel of death asks which angels will take the soul. — Kur'an Yolu reports the alternative of angels of mercy or punishment. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 75:26/q14/p4 A dying servant's joints bid each other farewell until Resurrection. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports the servant grappling with death's pangs as the joints take leave. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 75:26/q15 What reports link a person's death with resurrection?  [* · reports]
- 75:26/q15/p1 A person's own Resurrection begins at death. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites al-Mughīra's saying that each person's resurrection is death. He also reports ʿAlqama saying after a burial that the deceased person's resurrection had begun. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(المغيرة بن شعبة,علقمة)]

## 75:26/q16 How do different people experience this death?  [* · interpretation]
- 75:26/q16/p1 Worldly attachment makes it painful separation; devotion to the afterlife makes it joyful release. — Elmalılı describes endless exile for lovers of this world and joyful union for those devoted to the afterlife. [1 notes: ELMALILI+]
