# 14:38: 29 questions

## 14:38/q01 Why does Abraham repeat 'Our Lord' here?  [رَبَّنَآ · rhetoric]
- 14:38/q01/p1 The renewed address intensifies humble entreaty, dependence, and the appeal for an answer. — Al-Kashshaf, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, and Abu Hayyan call it pleading or reliance. Al-Biqa'i connects it to sincerity after descendants opposed Abraham's aims. Al-Alusi calls it humble insistence. Islahi counts seven Rabbi or Rabbana addresses and says repetition seeks God's attention. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 14:38/q02 Does 'Our Lord' differ from 'my Lord' in this prayer?  [رَبَّنَآ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the singular and plural address carry distinct grounds or scope for the appeal.
- 14:38/q02/p1 The two forms have no apparent difference here. — Abu Hayyan explicitly sees no apparent distinction between addressing God as my Lord and our Lord. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 14:38/q02/p2 The plural address is apt because it encompasses a wider circle of hidden matters. — Al-Alusi says the plural reaches all hidden things in God's kingdom and dominion, as the following clause explains, and objects to Abu Hayyan's denial of a difference. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 14:38/q02/p3 Rabbi invokes a favor personally experienced; Rabbana invokes one witnessed by all God's creatures. — Islahi makes the difference depend on the reach of the favor cited as grounds for a plea. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 14:38/q03 What are the things Abraham says God knows they hide and disclose?  [مَا نُخْفِى وَمَا نُعْلِنُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the words cover all affairs or identify a particular inward and outward pair, and which episode or inward state supplies that pair.
- 14:38/q03/p1 All their private and public affairs, including needs, states, and desires. — Al-Tabari includes the family's motives and sincere worship (14:35); al-Kashshaf, al-Baydawi, al-Biqa'i, and al-Alusi include needs and interests; Ibn Atiyya includes guidance and care for children. Abu Hayyan explicitly prefers the general scope only indirectly through his reading of the following expansion; al-Alusi explicitly prefers the general reading and says it includes states unknown even to Abraham. Al-Tabataba'i calls the meaning plain. [16 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 14:38/q03/p2 His concealed intent is God's approval and obedient, sincere worship by his descendants. — Al-Tabari links the motive to the prayer for the town in 14:35; Ibn Kathir transmits Ibn Jarir's explanation that the prayer for its people seeks God's pleasure alone. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير)]
- 14:38/q03/p3 He hid distress or grief over Ishmael in the barren valley and openly said he had settled his descendants there. — Muqatil specifies Ishmael's foreign place without water or livelihood and identifies the valley as Mecca; a report in al-Jishumi likewise contrasts hidden grief with the spoken statement. [3 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI(الأصم)]
- 14:38/q03/p4 He concealed grief over Ishmael and made his apprehension public. — Al-Jishumi reports this wording from Ibn Abbas. [1 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عباس)]
- 14:38/q03/p5 His longing or affection for Ishmael and Hajar is among what is hidden and revealed. — Al-Tha'labi, al-Baghawi, and al-Qurtubi attribute this to Ibn Abbas and Muqatil after the family was settled in the barren valley; they do not assign the feeling exclusively to either side of the pair. [3 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس ومقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس ومقاتل)]
- 14:38/q03/p6 He hid grief at separation and openly wept, prayed, or relied on God. — Al-Tabari's Ibn Abbas report places their grief after Abraham left Hajar and Ishmael in Mecca. Al-Kashshaf, al-Razi, Abu Hayyan, and al-Alusi report public crying and prayer; al-Baydawi adds reliance on God. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 14:38/q03/p7 He hid sorrow at parting and publicly entrusted Hajar and Ishmael to God in their farewell exchange. — Al-Kashshaf, al-Razi, Abu Hayyan, and al-Alusi recount Hajar asking whom he left them to; Abraham answered God. In the fuller forms she asks whether God commanded it and accepts that they need not fear or will be safe. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 14:38/q03/p8 He concealed anguish at leaving Ishmael but openly displayed love for him. — Abu Salih transmits this from Ibn Abbas in Ibn al-Jawzi; the commentators place the speech when Ishmael had been settled in the sanctuary and Abraham was about to leave. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس)]
- 14:38/q03/p9 He hid love for Ishmael and Hajar but outwardly showed harshness or estrangement toward them. — Al-Durr reports hidden love and outward harshness from Ibn Abbas; al-Alusi transmits from Ibrahim al-Nakha'i through Ibn Abi Hatim that he openly showed Sarah estrangement. The formulations of the outward display differ. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي)]
- 14:38/q03/p10 Their hidden and public matter is monotheism. — Al-Jishumi reports this identification from al-Asamm. [1 notes: JISHUMI(الأصم)]
- 14:38/q03/p11 The hidden is love of God and the declared is remembrance of Him. — Al-Sulami reports al-Khawwas's inward reading. [1 notes: SULAMI(الخواص)]
- 14:38/q03/p12 The hidden is spiritual states and the manifest is proper devotional conduct. — Al-Sulami reports Ibn Ata's inward reading. [1 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
- 14:38/q03/p13 The hidden is companionship with God and the declared is ecstatic love. — Al-Sulami reports al-Husayn's inward reading. [1 notes: SULAMI(الحسين)]
- 14:38/q03/p14 The hidden is potential within us and the declared is perfections brought into actuality. — Al-Qashani pairs these with God's knowledge of the earth of capacity and heaven of spirit. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 14:38/q04 How does ma function in 'what we hide and what we disclose'?  [مَا نُخْفِى وَمَا نُعْلِنُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether ma names things concealed and disclosed or turns the verbs into verbal nouns.
- 14:38/q04/p1 Ma is a relative pronoun referring to concealed and disclosed things. — Al-Alusi says the reported specific explanations construe ma as relative, with an omitted antecedent. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 14:38/q04/p2 Ma can be a verbal noun, yielding 'our secrecy as our publicity.' — Al-Alusi gives this as another grammatical analysis. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 14:38/q05 Why is the hidden mentioned before the public?  [نُخْفِى نُعْلِنُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the order emphasizes God's reach into secrecy or follows the order from secrecy to disclosure.
- 14:38/q05/p1 Hiddenness comes first to stress that God's knowledge reaches it as fully as the manifest. — Al-Alusi says the less visible side receives priority to establish equal divine knowledge. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 14:38/q05/p2 Secrecy precedes public disclosure, so knowledge of it is mentioned first. — Al-Alusi also offers this sequence of events as an explanation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 14:38/q06 Why voice needs to God when He already knows them?  [إِنَّكَ تَعْلَمُ مَا نُخْفِى وَمَا نُعْلِنُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the stated knowledge chiefly entrusts unspoken needs to God or attests the petitioner's motive.
- 14:38/q06/p1 Prayer entrusts needs and unspoken desires to the One who knows them best, while displaying servitude, humility, dependence, and hope for mercy. — Al-Kashshaf and al-Baydawi say God knows needs better and is more merciful; al-Kashshaf cites a petitioner who speaks from need, not to remind a forgetful patron. Al-Razi says Abraham cannot know future outcomes but God knows interests and harms. Ibn Atiyya says his compact plea entrusts guidance and care for his children to God. Al-Bursevi and al-Alusi say articulation manifests need and eagerness for gifts; al-Alusi cites al-Suhrawardi's verse that God knows a complaint before it is spoken. Al-Biqa'i says prayer supplies no information. Study Quran adds 3:29 and says supplication hastens response. Islahi adds what the petitioner cannot express or is shy to say. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(بعضهم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(السهروردي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 14:38/q06/p2 The appeal attests Abraham's pure motive in praying for his descendants and the town. — Al-Tabari says he wants his child among obedient, sincere worshippers and seeks God's approval (14:35); Ibn Kathir reports Ibn Jarir that his prayer for the town's people seeks God's pleasure alone. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير)]

## 14:38/q07 Who says that nothing on earth or in heaven is hidden from God?  [وَمَا يَخْفَىٰ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ مِن شَىْءٍۢ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَلَا فِى ٱلسَّمَآءِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the universal clause is Abraham's own expansion or a divine parenthesis confirming him.
- 14:38/q07/p1 Abraham continues speaking, expanding from his family's hidden matters to everything in earth and heaven. — Al-Kashshaf and al-Razi offer the continuation as an assertion about the Knower of the unseen. Abu Hayyan prefers it for the natural expansion from family to all things. Al-Alusi reports that most commentators take it as Abraham's speech. Al-Tabataba'i says the divine name then explains the earlier knowledge. [8 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو علي الجبائي); TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 14:38/q07/p2 God interjects a confirming statement before Abraham's praise resumes. — Al-Tha'labi calls it a parenthesis; al-Tabrisi attributes it to al-Jubba'i. Al-Kashshaf, Abu Hayyan, and al-Alusi compare God's parenthetical 'and so they do' in 27:34; al-Razi cites 16:34 as his comparison. Al-Baghawi reports this as the majority view in his presentation, while al-Alusi reports a majority for Abraham's speech. [11 notes: THALABI(الآخرون,بعضهم); BAGHAWI-FULL(الأكثرون); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو علي الجبائي); TABATABAI]

## 14:38/q08 How extensive is God's knowledge in the final clause?  [وَمَا يَخْفَىٰ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ مِن شَىْءٍۢ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَلَا فِى ٱلسَّمَآءِ · theology]
- 14:38/q08/p1 It includes every thing and every hidden or public state in full detail, without any temporal or creaturely limit. — Muqatil, al-Tha'labi, al-Qushayri, al-Tabrisi, al-Qurtubi, al-Nasafi, al-Biqa'i, al-Tabataba'i, and Asad state the universal scope. Ibn Atiyya stresses fully detailed knowledge. Al-Baydawi and al-Alusi ground it in God's essential knowledge, equally related to every object; al-Bursevi adds that past, present, and future do not apply to Him and contrasts acquired human or angelic knowledge. Al-Tabari says all creation is manifest to its Creator and governor. Al-Kashshaf and al-Razi say no single thing is excepted. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI(الآخرون); QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 14:38/q09 Why phrase universal knowledge as 'nothing is hidden'?  [وَمَا يَخْفَىٰ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ مِن شَىْءٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 14:38/q09/p1 The negative wording emphatically excludes every trace of obscurity from divine knowledge. — Al-Alusi contrasts God's knowledge with creaturely knowledge, where obscurity exists. Al-Biqa'i notes the generic 'anything' and repeated negation across earth and heaven. Ibn Ashur calls the closing clause a comprehensive maxim. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 14:38/q10 What does min contribute to 'nothing'?  [مِن شَىْءٍۢ · grammar]
- 14:38/q10/p1 It makes the negation exhaustive: not a single thing is hidden. — Al-Kashshaf, al-Razi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, and al-Bursevi expressly give this force; al-Biqa'i also notes the generic 'anything' in the emphatic universal wording. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 14:38/q11 What does 'in earth and in heaven' grammatically modify?  [فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَلَا فِى ٱلسَّمَآءِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the location qualifies the thing or the act of being hidden.
- 14:38/q11/p1 It describes the thing: no thing located in earth or heaven is hidden. — Al-Bursevi allows this attachment. Al-Alusi considers it most natural as an omitted adjective of shay', whether the thing resides there or is part of them. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 14:38/q11/p2 It modifies 'is hidden': nothing is hidden in earth or heaven. — Al-Bursevi allows this attachment; al-Alusi calls it possible but strained. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 14:38/q12 What do 'earth' and 'heaven' encompass here?  [ٱلْأَرْضِ ٱلسَّمَآءِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the two realms are spatial, directional, perceptual, or inward images.
- 14:38/q12/p1 Earth and all the heavens are the literal lower and upper realms. — Al-Alusi says the singular al-sama' includes all heavens and that earth is the nearer realm. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 14:38/q12/p2 They may mean the lower and upper directions. — Al-Alusi offers this alternative to the literal account of earth and heaven. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 14:38/q12/p3 Earth may mean what humans perceive and heaven what is hidden from human perception. — Al-Tabataba'i proposes this perceptual distinction. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 14:38/q12/p4 Earth is capacity and heaven is spirit. — Al-Qashani uses this inward correspondence for God's knowledge of potential and actual perfections. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 14:38/q13 Why is earth named before heaven?  [ٱلْأَرْضِ ٱلسَّمَآءِ · rhetoric]
- 14:38/q13/p1 Earth is nearer to us, so it is mentioned first. — Al-Alusi gives proximity as the reason for the order. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 14:38/q14 Why does the final clause use the name Allah after addressing God directly?  [ٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
- 14:38/q14/p1 The divine name makes the statement an independent, universal rule that explains His knowledge of all hidden and public things. — Al-Alusi says the shift shows the rule's reason and reach beyond Abraham's family. Ibn Ashur says the name lets the tailpiece stand as a comprehensive maxim. Al-Tabataba'i says that if Abraham speaks, the name explains his preceding claim. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 14:38/q15 How does this statement lead into praise and the next petition?  [* · links]
- 14:38/q15/p1 Knowledge of the family's needs leads to thanks for God's gifts and then a further request. — Al-Tha'labi says God's assertion leads into praise for granting Ishmael and Isaac in old age. Ibn Atiyya says Abraham turns from entrusting desires to praising complete knowledge and thanking God. Ibn al-Jazari says Abraham's prayer models praise before asking, moving from knowledge and gifts to a request that he and his descendants establish prayer. [3 notes: THALABI(الآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASHR]

## 14:38/q16 When were the prayers around this verse uttered?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the surrounding prayers belong to one later occasion or include the earlier departure before Isaac's birth.
- 14:38/q16/p1 The prayers in these verses were made after Mecca became a settlement and Abraham and Ishmael built the Kaaba. — Kuranyolu reports this view of commentators. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 14:38/q16/p2 The reported prayers were uttered at different times; praise for both sons came after the departure for Mecca. — Abu Hayyan argues that Isaac had not yet been born when Hajar and Ishmael were left there. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 14:38/q17 How does this prayer gather up the preceding account?  [* · links]
- 14:38/q17/p1 It gathers the earlier account of misguidance, settlement, faith, provision, gratitude, prayer, and concern for descendants into a renewed appeal. — Ibn Ashur lists misguidance and responses, settling sons by the House, and prayer and gratitude. Al-Biqa'i says Abraham has prayed for pure faith and provision that permits gratitude, yet descendants have opposed his aims. Al-Razi ties the words to his request for their provision. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]

## 14:38/q18 What should Quraysh learn from Abraham's prayer?  [* · links]
- 14:38/q18/p1 Their prosperity comes from Abraham's prayer and its monotheistic aims; Muhammad's followers fulfill those aims while Quraysh oppose them. — Islahi says the prayer concerns Makkah and Abraham's children there; its retelling reminds Quraysh why they were settled, the source of their blessings, and their error in crediting alleged deities. Kuranyolu presents Abraham's grateful devotion as an example of servitude and salvation through his monotheistic way. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 14:38/q19 What place does Abraham's prayer have in this surah?  [* · links]
- 14:38/q19/p1 The surah is named for Abraham's episode and prayer for Makkah, his progeny, faithful worship, and forgiveness. — Study Quran lists security for Makkah, protection from idolatry, prosperity for progeny (14:35–41), and forgiveness for his parents and all believers on the Last Day. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 14:38/q20 What limit on prophetic knowledge does the verse illustrate?  [* · theology]
- 14:38/q20/p1 Knowledge of tomorrow belongs to Allah, not to the Prophet. — In Ibn Hibban, Aisha calls claims that Muhammad saw his Lord, concealed revelation, or knew tomorrow grave lies; the report contrasts prophetic limits with Allah's knowledge here. [1 notes: IBNHIBBAN(عائشة)]

## 14:38/q21 What inward response follows from knowing that God sees all states?  [* · inward]
turns on: The notes give distinct inward consequences of the same universal knowledge.
- 14:38/q21/p1 Purify the secret and correct hidden affairs. — Al-Sulami reports Abu Uthman urging cultivation of the inner self because nothing is hidden from God. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان)]
- 14:38/q21/p2 Rest from anxious worldly intrusions and distraction. — Al-Qushayri says certainty of God's knowledge brings relief from translating anxious thoughts and preoccupation with events. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 14:38/q21/p3 Remain watchful in every state and make intentions sincere. — Ibn Ashur says Abraham teaches his household and followers this consequence of universal knowledge. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 14:38/q22 What does God do with hidden realities?  [* · inward]
- 14:38/q22/p1 God alone originates what is absent and brings hidden realities of accord and opposition into view. — Al-Sulami reports this inward account from an unnamed interpreter. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]

## 14:38/q23 What virtue of supplication is reported?  [* · reports]
- 14:38/q23/p1 Nothing is more honored before Allah than supplication. — Ibn Hibban transmits Abu Hurayra's report of the Prophet's saying, pertinent to Abraham's prayer. [1 notes: IBNHIBBAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 14:38/q24 Why should a supplicant humble himself?  [* · interpretation]
- 14:38/q24/p1 Even a petitioner to a king welcomes humbling himself to obtain a need. — Ibn al-Jazari cites Muslim ibn Yasar's comparison, transmitted by Ibn Abi Shayba, to illuminate humility in prayer. [1 notes: NASHR(مسلم بن يسار)]

## 14:38/q25 How should rhyme be treated in supplication?  [* · rhetoric]
- 14:38/q25/p1 Avoid forced rhyme, though effortless balanced wording in transmitted prayers is acceptable. — Ibn al-Jazari cites a Bukhari report from Ibn Abbas; al-Ghazali explains that affected rhyme conflicts with humility. [1 notes: NASHR(ابن عباس)]

## 14:38/q26 How should praise frame a supplication?  [* · interpretation]
- 14:38/q26/p1 Begin and end by praising God, then bless the Prophet; Abraham's prayer models praise before petition. — Ibn al-Jazari traces the movement from divine knowledge and gifts to the request that Abraham and his descendants establish prayer. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 14:38/q27 How should one speak of evil while praising God?  [* · theology]
- 14:38/q27/p1 As etiquette, attribute beautiful things to God and avoid ascribing low or ugly things to Him, while affirming that He creates and governs both good and evil. — Ibn al-Jazari reports al-Khattabi's explanation of 'evil is not from You' and explicitly says it does not deny that all things issue from God's creation and power. [2 notes: NASHR(الخطابي)]

## 14:38/q28 Why do complete stops matter to the meaning?  [* · readings]
- 14:38/q28/p1 They keep speakers and clauses distinct, preventing a divine reply from being assigned to opponents or an unrelated ma from being joined to a preceding list. — Ibn al-Jazari supplies several examples of stops that protect meaning, including false conjunctions or adjectival attachments. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 14:38/q29 What contrast with false prophetic speech is reported?  [* · reports]
- 14:38/q29/p1 Qur'anic speech was recognized as beyond human composition, whereas Musaylima's rhymes were judged false, base, and devoid of comparable meaning or wisdom. — Al-Khattabi reports Abu Bakr's response to the speech. Amr ibn al-As recounts Musaylima claiming a mission over trifles, reciting absurd rhymes, and giving a rhymed palm-tree judgment. Al-Khattabi endorses Amr's verdict, compares later comic nonsense favorably to Musaylima, and adds al-Asma'i's anecdote of Abu Amr ibn al-Ala' likening bad verse to another poet's work. [5 notes: KHATTABI(أبو بكر,عمرو بن العاص)]
