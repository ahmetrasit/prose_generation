# 11:116: 28 questions

## 11:116/q01 What force does فَلَوْلَا have here?  [فَلَوْلَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the particle is read as reproach with an implied negative, a wish, or its broader conditional use; the universal hallā attribution is tested against other Qur'anic instances.
- 11:116/q01/p1 It asks reproachfully why earlier communities had no people forbidding corruption; the question implies their scarcity or absence. — Ibn Qutayba and Burhan say lawlā without an answer means hallā, with a past verb expressing reproach; Ibn Qutayba cites 9:122, 6:43, 56:86 and a poet. Al-Farrāʾ and Ibn ʿAbbās take its implication as 'there were not,' comparing 10:98. Al-Rummānī allows either reproach or question. Burhan prefers exhortation because Ubayy reads fahlā; its reproach still implies nonoccurrence. Al-Ṭabarī points to unheeded signs, proofs and consequences. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī hear grief or lament, comparing 36:30. Al-Biqāʿī says the living audience is urged by the destroyed peoples' fate. Asad renders it 'alas.' [36 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس والفراء); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,ابن قتيبة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس والفراء,ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(ابن فارس والهروي); BIQAI-FULL(الرماني,الزمخشري); DURR-FULL(ابن أبي مالك); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭūsī); BURHAN+]
- 11:116/q01/p2 It is a wish that earlier people of virtue had existed. — Al-Kashshāf gives 'would that there had been'; al-Jishumī reports analysis as law of wishing joined to lā, by analogy with hallā in censure. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 11:116/q01/p3 Every Qur'anic lawlā means hallā except its occurrence in al-Ṣāffāt. — Attributed to al-Khalīl by al-Rāzī; al-Kashshāf, al-Samīn and al-Rāzī reject the universal claim, citing contrary examples at 68:49, 48:25 and 17:74; al-Samīn identifies 37:143 as the purported exception. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الخليل); KASHSHAF-FULL-; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف)-; SAMIN-DURR(الخليل)-]
- 11:116/q01/p4 In its original conditional use, lawlā means 'if it were not for' an existing impediment. — Ibn al-Jawzī illustrates it with disobedience preventing good treatment; this explains the particle's broader range rather than making that conditional sense the verse's local reading. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 11:116/q02 How is the opening read in the report from Ubayy?  [فَلَوْلَا · readings]
- 11:116/q02/p1 Ubayy's transmitted reading opens 'Had there been among the generations before you.' — Al-Durr reports Ubayy saying the Prophet taught him this wording; Burhan also invokes Ubayy's fahlā wording to support the exhortative force. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(أبي بن كعب); BURHAN]

## 11:116/q03 How do the opening words fit grammatically?  [كَانَ مِنَ ٱلْقُرُونِ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ يَنْهَوْنَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether kāna is complete or deficient, and whether the focus falls on the existence of virtuous people or their act of forbidding.
- 11:116/q03/p1 Kāna is complete, with ūlū baqiyya its subject; the prepositional phrases locate it among former generations. — Al-Samīn says min al-qurūn may attach to complete kāna or be circumstantial to ūlū, while min qablikum qualifies al-qurūn. Al-Ālūsī reports partitive min and a circumstance or adjective for min qablikum; he notes objection that this parse focuses on existence rather than forbidding, while usage can focus on the qualification. His lute simile illustrates that added qualification. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 11:116/q03/p2 Kāna can be incomplete, with yanhawna its predicate and min al-qurūn an implied circumstantial phrase. — Al-Samīn calls it semantically weaker and says most grammarians do not let deficient kāna govern the prepositional phrase here. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 11:116/q04 Who are the earlier قُرُون?  [ٱلْقُرُونِ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ · referent]
- 11:116/q04/p1 They are earlier communities, especially the destroyed peoples previously narrated, including Noah's people, ʿĀd and Thamūd. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān name Noah's people, ʿĀd and Thamūd; al-Biqāʿī calls them once powerful destroyed peoples. Ibn ʿĀshūr glosses qurūn as nations or communities and points to his discussion at 6:1. Asad points to his note at 6:5. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 11:116/q05 What span does a qarn denote?  [ٱلْقُرُونِ · meaning]
turns on: The reported span of a generation; Ibn ʿAṭiyya favors the century estimate using a Prophetic report.
- 11:116/q05/p1 A people sharing a long period, reckoned here as a hundred years. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers a hundred years on the Prophetic report that no one alive on a given night would remain after a century; Ibn ʿUmar says this marks the end of that generation. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عمر,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 11:116/q05/p2 A generation may be reckoned as eighty years. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this estimate among alternatives. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 11:116/q05/p3 A generation may be reckoned as thirty years. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this estimate among alternatives. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 11:116/q06 What are أُو۟لُوا۟ بَقِيَّةٍ?  [أُو۟لُوا۟ بَقِيَّةٍ · meaning]
turns on: Whether baqiyya emphasizes retained virtue, discernment, religious obedience and self-preservation, a small remnant, or lasting benefit; many sources combine these senses.
- 11:116/q06/p1 People of virtue, goodness and praiseworthy excellence, the best remaining members of a community. — Al-Zajjāj cites the idiom 'a person has baqiyya'; al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr explain that people keep their best possessions. Al-Kashshāf, Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite a Ḥamāsa line and the proverb about hidden things and human remnants. Al-Jishumī relates it to what remains and preservation; al-Biqāʿī adds gathering, strength, endurance, guarding and quality. Ibn al-Jawzī distinguishes baqāʾ enduring successively from baqiyya remaining after companions vanish, and reports Arabs using baqā for baqiya. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 2:248 and his discussion of enduring good in Shuʿayb's speech. [36 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,القتبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(القتبي,قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(رويَشد بن كثير الطائي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 11:116/q06/p2 People of discernment, reason, resolve and religious firmness who heed God's proofs. — Al-Ṭabarī grounds the sense in reflection on God's admonitions and the stakes of belief; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān call them steadfast survivors as institutions weaken; al-Biqāʿī adds vigilance for benefit. Al-Ṭabrisī stresses God's gifts of reason, messengers and proofs. [19 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:116/q06/p3 People possessing religion, obedience and fear of God. — Ibn ʿAbbās says 'religion'; Ibn Qutayba adds moral firmness. Al-Zajjāj also offers obedience; al-Asamm and Abū Muslim describe fearing punishment and guarding against Hell. Al-Zamakhsharī and followers derive baqiyya from baqwā, akin to taqiyya/taqwā, preserving oneself from divine anger. [19 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,القتبي); JISHUMI(الأصم وأبو مسلم,قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:116/q06/p4 A small surviving number of people. — Al-Damghānī and Ibn al-Jawzī report this sense; al-Jishumī transmits Abū ʿAlī's more specific 'remnant of the elders,' asking why none persisted despite numerous messengers and proofs. [3 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); JISHUMI(أبو علي)]
- 11:116/q06/p5 People blessed with enduring good or benefit. — Al-Ṭabrisī includes blessing among glosses. Ibn ʿĀshūr's linked discussion of Shuʿayb contrasts lasting divine reward with transient gains (19:76), cites 2:248 for preserved precious good, and explains that rightful earnings protect social order while wrongful seizure and ingratitude invite loss; he cites the Farewell Pilgrimage's inviolability of blood and property and Ibn ʿAṭāʾ Allāh on gratitude preserving blessings. [9 notes: TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطاء الله,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 11:116/q06/p6 A virtuous community among earlier Mosaic and Christian adherents, serving what endures and helping others. — Elmalılı gives this specific historical identification. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 11:116/q07 How is بَقِيَّةٍ read and derived?  [بَقِيَّةٍ · readings]
turns on: The vowels, yāʾ weight and derivation, with some forms tied to baqiya 'remain' and another to baqāhu 'watch or await.'
- 11:116/q07/p1 The familiar baqiyya has a geminated yāʾ and can be an intensive adjective or a noun of preservation. — Al-Jishumī relates it to preserving; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn describe an intensive feminine active adjective for select people. Al-Bayḍāwī also reports a verbal-noun analysis. [4 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 11:116/q07/p2 A light-yāʾ baqiya or biqya is read as a remaining act or group, or as a feminine active participle. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya's group treats faʿīla as faʿla; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī compare shajiya; al-Samīn calls it a remaining group. Ibn Jammāz from Abū Jaʿfar has kasra on bāʾ, still qāf and light yāʾ; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it an unattested dialect form, perhaps suggesting dignity. Qiraat-ER attributes biqya to Ḥamza and suggests a shift to an act of remaining. These transmissions differ in attribution and vowel. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جماز عن أبي جعفر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فرقة); SAMIN-DURR(فرقة); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن جماز عن أبي جعفر); QIRAAT-ER(Ḥamza)]
- 11:116/q07/p3 Abū Jaʿfar and Shayba read buqya with ḍamma on bāʾ and still qāf. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it the fuʿla pattern; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī report the same vowels. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو جعفر وشيبة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو جعفر وشيبة); SAMIN-DURR(أبو جعفر وشيبة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو جعفر وشيبة)]
- 11:116/q07/p4 A faʿla-pattern reading derives it from baqāhu, 'watch or await,' making the people vigilant for punishment. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī give this derivation; Ibn al-Jawzī says yubqī can mean observe and cites a hadith where the Companions waited for the Prophet; al-Ālūsī cites Muʿādh's delayed-night-prayer report. Al-Samīn also reports an interpretation as a single verbal-noun occurrence. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(معاذ بن جبل); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 11:116/q08 What does their forbidding corruption entail?  [يَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ ٱلْفَسَادِ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'corruption' is specified chiefly as unbelief and sins or also as damaged social conditions.
- 11:116/q08/p1 They warn against unbelief, idolatry, disobedience and accompanying sins. — Al-Ṭabarī specifies warnings to sinners and disbelievers; Ibn ʿAbbās includes shirk, violation of God's rights and disobedience. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī identify unbelief and associated sins; Muqātil and Ibn al-Jawzī specify idolatry and sins. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:116/q08/p2 They restrain wider moral and social corruption, repairing communal conditions. — Ibn ʿĀshūr includes sins and disrupted social conditions, comparing the Israelites' decline when admonition ceased; Ibn Kathīr says evils and abominations; al-Biqāʿī hears renewed prohibition in the imperfect verb because corrupters were numerous. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 11:116/q09 How is يَنْهَوْنَ related to أُو۟لُوا۟ بَقِيَّةٍ?  [أُو۟لُوا۟ بَقِيَّةٍ يَنْهَوْنَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the verb qualifies the people, predicates what they should do, or answers the opening particle.
- 11:116/q09/p1 It is an adjective describing the people of remnant. — Al-Samīn prefers the adjective because annexation specifies the noun. Al-Kashf, as reported by al-Ālūsī, says this parse laments the absence of virtuous forbiders and heightens blame, suggesting no genuinely virtuous people. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم,صاحب الكشف); ALUSI-FULL(صاحب الكشف)+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 11:116/q09/p2 It is a circumstantial clause describing the people of remnant. — Al-Samīn reports this possible parse. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 11:116/q09/p3 It is the predicate of deficient kāna. — Al-Samīn says this requires min al-qurūn as an implied circumstance but finds the meaning weaker; al-Kashf finds a connected exception incoherent with this parse. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(صاحب الكشف)]
- 11:116/q09/p4 It is the answer to lawlā. — Al-Jishumī attributes this analysis to Abū Muslim. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم)]

## 11:116/q10 What does فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ attach to?  [ٱلْفَسَادِ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the location is governed by the verbal noun or is an implicit circumstantial qualification.
- 11:116/q10/p1 It attaches to the verbal noun 'corruption.' — Al-Samīn notes that an article-marked verbal noun can govern objects, so it can govern an adverbial phrase. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 11:116/q10/p2 It is an omitted circumstantial modifier of 'corruption.' — Al-Samīn offers this alternative. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 11:116/q11 How does إِلَّا قَلِيلًۭا function?  [إِلَّا قَلِيلًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether lawlā retains affirmative exhortation or implies negation; the nominative can be analyzed as a substitute or as an adjective depending on the construction.
- 11:116/q11/p1 It is a disconnected exception: nevertheless a few of those rescued did forbid corruption. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Ṭabarī compare Jonah's people at 10:98; al-Ṭabarī also mentions 2:83. Sībawayh, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ṭabrisī point to the affirmative wording. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī say a connected exception under literal exhortation would wrongly exempt the few from forbidding. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the accusative marks disconnection and corrects an overgeneralization about earlier nations. [27 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سيبويه); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري,بعضهم,قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش)-; SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش)-; ALUSI-FULL(صاحب الكشف)-]
- 11:116/q11/p2 It can be connected if exhortation is understood as a negation: there were no such forbiders except a few. — Al-Farrāʾ says the question contains a shade of negation; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports other grammarians reading it so. Al-Zamakhsharī allows accusative ordinary exception but calls nominative substitution more eloquent; Zayd ibn ʿAlī's nominative is compared to 4:66. Al-Akhfash is reported as rejecting disconnection. Al-Quṭb says requests can exclude an already compliant person, as in 'strike the people except Zayd.' Al-Kashf's proposed predicate parse and objections are reported by al-Ālūsī. [16 notes: AKHFASH; FARRA; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(غيره); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش,الزمخشري,الفراء,زيد بن علي); SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش,الزمخشري,الفراء,زيد بن علي); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري,القطب,صاحب الكشف); KASHSHAF-FULL-; RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)-; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)-]
- 11:116/q11/p3 A nominative qalīl is permissible as an adjective after a disconnected exception. — Al-Akhfash gives this construction; al-Mubarrad and al-Jarmī, cited in Burhān, also deem nominative valid. This differs from Zayd's nominative substitute after implicit negation. [2 notes: AKHFASH; BURHAN(المبرد والجرمي)]

## 11:116/q12 What does مِّمَّنْ أَنجَيْنَا مِنْهُمْ mean grammatically?  [مِّمَّنْ أَنجَيْنَا مِنْهُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the preposition identifies all the saved with the forbiders or selects a subset of the saved.
- 11:116/q12/p1 Min explains the few: the rescued people were precisely those who forbade corruption. — Al-Zamakhsharī cites 7:165, followed by al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī. Al-Samīn says it can be an appositional 'I mean'; al-Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr identify the few with rescued followers of messengers. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 11:116/q12/p2 Min is partitive: a few among the rescued forbade corruption. — Al-Samīn favors or reports the partitive parse, with the phrase modifying qalīlan; al-Ālūsī reports a form in which some saved others failed to forbid. Al-Zamakhsharī objects that 7:165 limits salvation to forbiders. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL-; NASAFI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)-]

## 11:116/q13 Who were the few saved forbiders?  [إِلَّا قَلِيلًۭا مِّمَّنْ أَنجَيْنَا مِنْهُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the few are prophetic followers generally, prophets as well, or specifically Jonah's people.
- 11:116/q13/p1 The small number of prophetic followers, believers and people of truth in earlier nations. — Al-Ḥasan names Noah, Hūd and Ṣāliḥ with their believers, citing 11:48; Ibn Jurayj and Qatāda stress the few in every people. Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies followers of messengers, not messengers themselves; he says their admonition led to repentance, abandonment of sin and escape from the Fire. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats the rescue as clarifying that some earlier nations did possess virtue. [35 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,ابن زيد,الحسن,قتادة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن جريج); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم,قيل); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭūsī)]
- 11:116/q13/p2 They were the prophets together with righteous believers. — Al-Ṭabrisī explicitly includes prophets, whereas Ibn ʿĀshūr limits the people of remnant to followers rather than the messengers. [1 notes: TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 11:116/q13/p3 They were Jonah's people. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this identification as an alternative to prophetic followers and people of truth; 10:98 is cited. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]

## 11:116/q14 Why does the verse say أَنجَيْنَا?  [أَنجَيْنَا · rhetoric]
- 11:116/q14/p1 It highlights beneficial rescue that encourages forbidding corruption. — Al-Biqāʿī says najjaynā would suggest gradual deliverance and obscure the point; Ibn ʿĀshūr links admonition, repentance and salvation. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 11:116/q15 Who are ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا۟?  [ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether wrongdoing here points narrowly to failure to forbid evil or broadly to the active corrupting majority.
- 11:116/q15/p1 Those who abandoned the duty to forbid corruption, thus wronging themselves. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī call this a central religious duty; al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī tie the relative phrase to the preceding few. Al-Biqāʿī says the unqualified wording broadens the blame. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:116/q15/p2 They include active corrupters and people who failed to stop them. — Al-Bursevī includes direct offenders and lax people; al-Ālūsī reports the wider reference while noting slight tension with the preceding exhortation. Elmalılı says both groups wrong themselves and cause destruction. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ELMALILI]
- 11:116/q15/p3 They are the disbelieving majority or those who harmed themselves through sins. — Al-Ṭabarī calls them people who acquired unbelief; al-Jishumī says sinners harming themselves; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī identifies the majority left after the saved exception. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; TABATABAI]

## 11:116/q16 How is وَٱتَّبَعَ read?  [وَٱتَّبَعَ · readings]
turns on: The active or passive morphology, with the passive requiring an understood recompense.
- 11:116/q16/p1 The common reading is active ittabaʿa, 'the wrongdoers followed.' — Ibn Mujāhid says Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ and Abū ʿAmr double the tāʾ, as do ʿĀṣim, Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī here though they use cut or hamzated forms for other cited verbs. Al-Fārisī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya confirm the contrast; comparisons include 26:60, 7:175, 37:10 and 15:18. [6 notes: IBNMUJAHID; FARISI-HUJJA; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 11:116/q16/p2 A passive utbiʿa or utbiʿū is transmitted: they were made to follow the recompense of their luxury. — Al-Juʿfī transmits it from Abū ʿAmr; Ibn ʿAṭiyya also names Ḥafṣ ibn Muḥammad, Abū Ḥayyān names Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad and al-ʿAlāʾ ibn Sayyāba, while al-Ālūsī specifies hamza, still tāʾ and kasra on bāʾ. Al-Rāzī's report calls the reading ittabaʿa but explains unlawful indulgence; the other sources explain passive consequence. [10 notes: IBNMUJAHID; FARISI-HUJJA; KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو عمرو); IBNATIYYA-FULL(حفص بن محمد); RAZI-FULL(أبو عمرو في رواية الجعفي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(جعفر بن محمد والعلاء بن سيابة وأبو عمر); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(أبو جعفر والعلاء بن سيابة وأبو عمرو,قيل)]

## 11:116/q17 How does وَٱتَّبَعَ connect to what precedes?  [وَٱتَّبَعَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause advances the narrative by coordination, gives a simultaneous circumstance, or stands independently.
- 11:116/q17/p1 It coordinates with an implied statement that the few forbade corruption while the others failed and followed desires. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Samīn supply the implied clause; al-Ālūsī notes a linkage problem if coordinated directly with nahaw and reports fixes such as supplying 'their counterparts' or making nahaw independent. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the explicit consequence of an exception in 2:34. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري,بعض المحققين,قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 11:116/q17/p2 The waw is circumstantial: the few were saved while wrongdoers followed or received luxury's consequence. — Al-Kashshāf relates it to the preceding rescue and destruction; al-Ālūsī says the common active reading can carry this sense, with mā as subject or inverted word order in a proposed variant. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 11:116/q17/p3 The clause is parenthetical, supplying a judgment on the wrongdoers. — Al-Bayḍāwī allows this alternative. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 11:116/q18 What is مَآ أُتْرِفُوا۟ فِيهِ that the wrongdoers followed?  [مَآ أُتْرِفُوا۟ فِيهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether mā names enjoyed goods and desires, the consequence of that indulgence, or a reported alternative gloss.
- 11:116/q18/p1 Worldly ease, wealth, power, pleasures and status, preferred to duty and the afterlife. — Al-Farrāʾ stresses familiar pleasure and preference for this life; al-Ṭabarī synthesizes pleasure, arrogance, disbelief, rebellion and obstructing God's path. Al-Zajjāj says they resisted anything that might diminish comfort through work or earning. Mujāhid includes kingship and tyranny; al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān include leadership and wealth. Qatāda says worldly indulgence ensnared most; Ibn ʿĀshūr says 'followed' pictures exclusive devotion and lost gratitude. Al-Ṭabarī's poem of wealthy oppressors supports his discussion. The phrase may include bestowed goods and respite, as Ibn ʿAbbās, Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān and Ibn Ḥayyān gloss it. [49 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(ابن حيان,ابن عباس,الفراء,مجاهد,مقاتل بن سليمان); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,عطاء عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن عباس,الفراء,مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(الفراء,مقاتل بن حيان); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TAB-FULL+]
- 11:116/q18/p2 The consequence or punishment of their indulgence, which they followed to destruction. — Al-Farrāʾ reports sins and evil deeds leading to Fire; al-Zamakhsharī says the previous rescue implies the others perished, permitting an active 'followed recompense' and a circumstantial waw. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says luxury diverted them from truth until perishing. Passive readers require an omitted 'recompense.' Al-Māwardī reports seizure amid luxury. [14 notes: FARRA; MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو عمرو); IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,جعفر بن محمد والعلاء بن سيابة وأبو عمر); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 11:116/q18/p3 Their arrogance, tyranny, disbelief or sins under the luxury bestowed on them. — Abū ʿUbayda cites poetry calling affluent people arrogant; Mujāhid says rule became tyrannical and truth abandoned; Ibn ʿAbbās is also reported to say comfort made them insolent. Al-Ālūsī says deriving utrifū from blessings causing insolence fits context but departs from the familiar meaning. [7 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI(ابن عباس,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); JISHUMI(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 11:116/q18/p4 They made excuses. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports al-Ḍaḥḥāk's brief gloss without further support. [1 notes: THALABI(الضحاك)]
- 11:116/q18/p5 They examined or considered it. — Al-Māwardī and al-Durr transmit distinct reports from Ibn ʿAbbās with this gloss. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 11:116/q19 What does the root of أُتْرِفُوا۟ convey?  [أُتْرِفُوا۟ · meaning]
turns on: Whether insolence belongs to the word's lexical sense or is an effect of luxury in context.
- 11:116/q19/p1 Luxury, blessing, ease and expansion in prosperity; a mutraf is pampered or made indulgent by comfort. — Al-Ṭabarī defines the person as raised in luxury and fed pleasures; al-Wāḥidī cites a comfortable child; Ibn ʿArafa says a mutraf is left free to do as he wishes. Al-Farrāʾ derives utrifa from blessing; al-Ṭabrisī says habituated again to luxury. Al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Ālūsī and Asad give the prosperous-life sense, sometimes noting resulting insolence. [14 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(ابن عرفة); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); ASAD-NOTES]
- 11:116/q19/p2 It specifically conveys blessings that made people insolent. — Al-Ālūsī reports this derivation but rejects it as a departure from the common lexical meaning, despite its contextual fit. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 11:116/q20 How is مَآ أُتْرِفُوا۟ فِيهِ parsed?  [مَآ أُتْرِفُوا۟ فِيهِ · grammar]
turns on: The antecedent of fīhi and whether mā denotes a thing, an action, an omitted recompense, or a cause.
- 11:116/q20/p1 Mā is relative, referring to the luxuries or blessings in which they were indulged. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Taqrīb argue that fīhi refers back to mā; al-Samīn finds this more apparent on the passive reading. Al-Ālūsī reports that relative mā is needed unless the pronoun refers to ẓulamū. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(صاحب التقريب); SAMIN-DURR+]
- 11:116/q20/p2 Mā can be a verbal noun, 'their being indulged,' or represent an omitted noun such as its recompense. — Al-Samīn offers both alternatives; al-Ālūsī says a verbal noun is possible if fīhi returns to ẓulamū; a passive reading requires supplying the recompense. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(بعض,قيل); ALUSI-FULL(صاحب التقريب)-]
- 11:116/q20/p3 Mā may be causal or metaphorically locative if comfort made them insolent. — Al-Ālūsī reports the analysis but prefers the ordinary luxury sense. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 11:116/q21 What does وَكَانُوا۟ مُجْرِمِينَ say about them?  [وَكَانُوا۟ مُجْرِمِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether criminality is specified as disbelief or as wider persistent wrongdoing, including ingratitude.
- 11:116/q21/p1 They were unbelievers or polytheists. — Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī explicitly gloss criminals as unbelievers; al-Jishumī reports al-Aṣamm's polytheists; al-Ālūsī notes this is the graver offense. [4 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI(author والأصم); BAGHAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض)]
- 11:116/q21/p2 They persisted in sins, neglecting correction and gratitude, beyond the pursuit of luxury. — Al-Kashshāf says crime can attach to indulgence as ingratitude or to desire-following; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī report additional crimes beyond failure to forbid. Al-Biqāʿī calls criminality entrenched severance of God's bond; Ibn ʿĀshūr says luxury without gratitude becomes criminal and explains why punishment is due. Ibn Kathīr says they ignored objectors and punishment overtook them. [14 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI(author والأصم); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,بعض); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 11:116/q22 How does وَكَانُوا۟ مُجْرِمِينَ connect grammatically?  [وَكَانُوا۟ مُجْرِمِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Which earlier clause receives the coordination, and whether the final clause is a grammatical parenthesis, circumstance, or explanatory judgment.
- 11:116/q22/p1 It coordinates with 'followed,' stating a further or resultant action. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī give this ordinary parse; al-Bursevī says the two clauses together explain the destroyed nations' crime. Al-Ālūsī also reports coordination with an implied 'they did not forbid.' [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 11:116/q22/p2 It coordinates with 'they were indulged,' making criminality part of what they followed. — Al-Samīn gives this if mā is a verbal noun; al-Ālūsī reports it with desire's sin or failure of gratitude, while al-Taqrīb objects that fīhi favors relative mā. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(بعض); ALUSI-FULL(صاحب التقريب)-]
- 11:116/q22/p3 It is an independent or circumstantial judgment that they were criminals. — Al-Zamakhsharī calls it parenthetical; al-Nasafī follows him. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn object that a clause closing the verse is not technically a parenthesis between dependent elements; al-Ālūsī says rhetoricians permit a terminal parenthesis, or the clause can give the reason the rest perished. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,بعض); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)-]
- 11:116/q22/p4 It explains that following indulgence itself was their criminal act. — Al-Ālūsī reports this explanatory coordination but calls it remote. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض)-]

## 11:116/q23 What duty does the verse establish?  [* · law]
- 11:116/q23/p1 People should enjoin good, forbid corruption and respond to wrongdoing; neglect can expose a whole community to punishment. — Al-Ṭabrisī infers the duty from censure and rescue. Ibn Kathīr cites 3:104 and a prophetic warning that failure to change seen wrongdoing may bring inclusive punishment; al-Bursevī transmits a report that able bystanders and elites are punished together when wrong is tolerated. Saʿdī's verses urge useful speech even if unaccepted, and courageous resolve when hand and tongue cannot act. Al-Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya address living Muslims; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes the Companions' calling to faith (3:110) and warning for sinful Muslims. [17 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(السعدي); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 11:116/q24 Why were earlier peoples destroyed, and who is spared?  [* · interpretation]
- 11:116/q24/p1 Widespread unbelief and neglected correction, together with indulgence and other crimes, brought ruin; reforming believers were spared. — Al-Rāzī distinguishes absence of forbiders as first cause and indulgence as second. Al-Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Bayḍāwī and Elmalılı likewise link disbelief, neglect and luxury; al-Qushayrī says God did not destroy reformers. Ibn Kathīr says punishment arrived suddenly; al-Biqāʿī says destruction follows entrenched crimes, not injustice. Kuran Yolu says prophetic warnings were rejected and reform would prevent destruction. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees saved and criminal classes, developed at 11:118. [17 notes: QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭūsī)]

## 11:116/q25 How does this verse connect to its context and later audience?  [* · links]
- 11:116/q25/p1 It draws the lesson from the destroyed peoples' stories and urges the present community to maintain reform. — Al-Biqāʿī connects it to 11:115's patience and the difficulty of persuading entrenched opponents, including imitators in worship and those disputing Moses' guidance. Asad notes good-doers' recompense at 11:115 and each person's due at 11:111. Ibn ʿĀshūr says 'before you' offers hope Muslims will differ, praises the Companions' mission and says the closing ellipsis prepares 11:117. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī links the two classes to 11:118. [11 notes: TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 11:116/q26 What do the cited poetic forms mean?  [مَآ أُتْرِفُوا۟ فِيهِ · meaning]
turns on: The derivation of a poetic word cited while explaining affluent people.
- 11:116/q26/p1 Al-mumtād is the person asked for help; māda-hu means 'he asked him.' — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ṭabrisī gloss the poetic form this way; al-Ṭabrisī's line about spoiled leaders' heads is cited in discussion of luxury. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI]
- 11:116/q26/p2 Al-mumtād derives from māda yamīdu. — Abū ʿUbayda gives this alternative derivation for the cited verse. [1 notes: MAJAZ]

## 11:116/q27 What separate theological discussion occurs in a source excerpt?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether immaterial or subtle beings can perceive and act on particulars; these excerpted notes do not interpret 11:116.
- 11:116/q27/p1 Celestial spirits could guide human souls and enable extraordinary acts. — Al-Rāzī reports proponents of immaterial jinn saying human souls receive dreams or waking inspiration from higher spirits, and that a strong connection can produce supernatural acts. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(مثبتو الجن والشياطين)]
- 11:116/q27/p2 Immaterial beings cannot perceive particulars or cause particular acts. — Al-Rāzī reports philosophers' objection; he replies that a human soul judging one individual as human perceives particulars, that bodily instruments could permit perception, and that no proof excludes such beings. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(قوم من الفلاسفة); RAZI-FULL-]
- 11:116/q27/p3 Subtle embodied jinn are possible and could affect humans. — Al-Rāzī says bodies may share magnitude yet differ in nature, so subtle bodies could live, think and change shape; wind need not tear them, just as lightning fire penetrates stone or iron. They might enter and affect humans until an appointed term; unknown individual encounters defeat the social-contact objection. He also denies that angels or jinn necessarily undermine prophecy. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]

## 11:116/q28 How do these sources summarize the verse's contrast?  [* · interpretation]
- 11:116/q28/p1 Only a few preserved religion and forbade corruption, while most lost virtue and pursued indulgence. — Al-Baghawī says the people of virtue would undertake prohibition, yet such people were absent among the many. Al-Jishumī adds that preserving and guarding oneself means protection from harm. Al-Qushayrī says the few obeyed their prophets and kept religion. Al-Ṭabrisī says the condemned majority chose familiar pleasures over the afterlife and persisted in wrongdoing. [4 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; JISHUMI; QUSHAYRI; TABRISI]
