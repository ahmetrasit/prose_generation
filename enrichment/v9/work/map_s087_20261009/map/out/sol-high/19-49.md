# 19:49: 16 questions

## 19:49/q01 What did Abraham withdraw from?  [ٱعْتَزَلَهُمْ وَمَا يَعْبُدُونَ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ · referent]
- 19:49/q01/p1 He left his father or people and their idols, separating from their unbelief and departing their land. — Yahya identifies what they worshiped as idols. Razi calls the separation both religious and physical; Ibn ʿAtiyya specifies his father's town, and Ibn Kathir his father and people. Islahi says the announced disavowal was followed by actual migration. [23 notes: JISHUMI(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); BURSEVI(الشيخ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; MUQATIL+; NASAFI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+; TABATABAI+; TABRISI+; THALABI+; WAHIDI-BASIT+; WAHIDI-WAJIZ+; YAHYA-SALLAM+]

## 19:49/q02 Where did Abraham migrate, and what route is reported?  [ٱعْتَزَلَهُمْ · reports]
turns on: The reports supply different levels of detail about Kutha, Harran, Egypt and the encounter with a ruler.
- 19:49/q02/p1 He migrated to Syria or the Holy Land, also identified as Palestine. — Muqatil, cited by Jishumi, says the Holy Land; Wahidi, Ibn al-Jawzi's commentators and Baydawi say Syria. The sheikh cited by Bursevi names Kutha as his starting point. [9 notes: JISHUMI(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); BURSEVI(الشيخ); BAYDAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; MUQATIL+; TABRISI+; WAHIDI-BASIT+; WAHIDI-WAJIZ+]
- 19:49/q02/p2 He left Kutha for Syria and on the journey met a ruler who gave Hajar to Sarah. — Ibn ʿAtiyya transmits this as a report. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 19:49/q02/p3 He first reached Harran and married Sarah there; a nearer account places Isaac's birth there. — Baydawi says it is reported that he first arrived in Harran and married Sarah. Alusi presents the Harran account as nearer: Isaac was born to Sarah and Jacob to Isaac. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 19:49/q02/p4 He passed through Harran, Palestine and Egypt, then returned to Palestine with Sarah, Lot and Lot's wife. — The commentators and historians cited by Kur'an Yolu add that Lot settled in Sodom and Gomorrah and Abraham remained in Palestine. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(tefsirci ve tarihçiler)]

## 19:49/q03 What does Abraham's withdrawal show about the word iʿtizāl?  [ٱعْتَزَلَهُمْ · meaning]
- 19:49/q03/p1 Iʿtizāl can properly describe withdrawal from evil. — Jishumi draws this lexical usage from Abraham's withdrawal in the verse. [1 notes: JISHUMI+]

## 19:49/q04 How does this withdrawal continue Abraham's earlier declaration?  [فَلَمَّا ٱعْتَزَلَهُمْ · links]
- 19:49/q04/p1 His stated resolve or disavowal was fulfilled by actual separation, after which the narrative names God's reward. — Ibn ʿAshur says the text leaves the completed departure implicit after the stated resolve. Islahi says Abraham left his people after proclaiming disavowal. Tabatabai summarizes the preceding argument with his father, separation and subsequent gifts. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; TABATABAI+]

## 19:49/q05 What did God grant Abraham in Isaac and Jacob, and why?  [وَهَبْنَا لَهُۥٓ إِسْحَٰقَ وَيَعْقُوبَ · interpretation]
turns on: The explanations emphasize consolation, elevated status and the later prophetic lineage; the notes do not treat these as mutually exclusive.
- 19:49/q05/p1 Isaac and his son Jacob, Abraham's grandson, were a consoling replacement for lost kin or company. — Tabari calls them better people, more honored by God; Qushayri stresses pious descendants, prophethood and enduring good remembrance. Wahidi, Baghawi, Ibn ʿAtiyya, Tabarsi, Ibn al-Jawzi's commentators, Ibn Abbas cited by Qurtubi, Nasafi, Ibn Kathir, Alusi and Ibn ʿAshur speak of relief from loneliness or replacement of family and people. Nasafi calls Jacob an extra gift for companionship. Ibn Kathir cites 21:72 and 11:71; Study Quran cites 6:84, 21:72, 29:27, 14:39, 11:71 and 37:101 as related gift or glad-tidings passages. Kur'an Yolu notes elderly Abraham and Sarah received Isaac, then Isaac's son Jacob. [26 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; JISHUMI+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; MUQATIL+; NASAFI-FULL+; QUSHAYRI+; STUDYQURAN(Ṭabarī)+; TAB-FULL+; TABATABAI+; TABRISI+; THALABI+; WAHIDI-BASIT+; WAHIDI-WAJIZ+]
- 19:49/q05/p2 The gift raised Abraham's rank and established a prophetic line to follow in religion. — Jishumi explicitly connects emigration with raised rank and says the three prophets are religious models. Razi says Abraham lost nothing in religion or worldly life and notes the sons' prophethood with reference to 19:48. Alusi notes that Isaac and Jacob became ancestors of many eminent prophets; Kur'an Yolu identifies later Israelite prophets among their descendants. [7 notes: ALUSI-FULL+; JISHUMI+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; RAZI-FULL+; TABATABAI+]

## 19:49/q06 When were Isaac and Jacob given relative to migration and Ishmael?  [وَهَبْنَا لَهُۥٓ إِسْحَٰقَ وَيَعْقُوبَ · reports]
- 19:49/q06/p1 The births followed migration, but Isaac and Jacob did not arrive immediately; Ishmael came first. — Abu Sulayman cited by Ibn al-Jawzi says Isaac and Jacob came after Ishmael. Ibn ʿAtiyya's report and Abu Hayyan say Sarah conceived Isaac after Hajar conceived Ishmael and Sarah became jealous. Bursevi's sheikh identifies the nearby promised child in 37:100 as Ishmael. Alusi calls Ishmael's priority the better-known chronology, citing 37:100–101, and says Hajar came before Sarah's jealousy and Isaac's birth. Ibn ʿAshur also identifies the promised gentle boy of 37:99–101 as Ishmael; Kur'an Yolu calls him Abraham's first son by Hagar and ancestor of the Hijaz Arabs. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان); BURSEVI(الشيخ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]

## 19:49/q07 Why are Isaac and Jacob named here while Ishmael is absent?  [إِسْحَٰقَ وَيَعْقُوبَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The sources offer several compatible emphases: prophetic lineage, separate mention, Sarah's participation, geographic companionship and the verse's selection of generations.
- 19:49/q07/p1 They foreground the prophetic line descending from Isaac and Jacob. — Baydawi and Bursevi say they may be singled out as prophetic lineages; Tabatabai says Isaac introduces the Israelite prophets and Jacob follows because many prophets descend from him. Alusi stresses their numerous eminent prophetic descendants. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 19:49/q07/p2 Ishmael has his own separate mention and merit in the surrounding account. — Baydawi and Bursevi mention his separate treatment; Alusi says he is singled out afterward for his own merit. Ibn ʿAshur points to 38:48 alongside 38:45. Islahi says the separately named ancestor of the Arabian idolaters receives their focused attention; he notes that Ishmael fathered the Ishmaelites, including Muhammad. Farahi, as reported by Islahi, calls Ishmael the greatest blessing from Abraham's migration. [7 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Imām Farāhī); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 19:49/q07/p3 Isaac and Jacob remained with Abraham and succeeded him where he lived, while Ishmael lived apart near the Sacred Mosque. — Biqai says God personally raised Ishmael after he was moved there as an infant. Ibn ʿAshur says Ishmael's proximity to God's House exceeded proximity to his father and brother. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 19:49/q07/p4 Isaac and Jacob were a shared gift to Abraham and Sarah, who had also left her people, and were close companions. — Ibn ʿAshur says the shared companionship matched Sarah's sacrifice. Biqai notes Isaac's birth through Abraham's barren, postmenopausal wife. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 19:49/q07/p5 Naming Jacob beside Isaac displays successive prophetic generations: Abraham, son and grandson. — Islahi says adding Joseph makes this an unmatched prophetic family. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 19:49/q07/p6 Ishmael was also granted after the departure, so the named pair does not exhaust Abraham's reward. — Ibn ʿAshur expressly includes Ishmael among the gifts after departure. Islahi calls his omission striking in view of his descendants; Farahi calls him the greatest migratory blessing. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Imām Farāhī)+]

## 19:49/q08 Who is included in 'each' whom God made a prophet?  [وَكُلًّۭا جَعَلْنَا نَبِيًّۭا · referent]
turns on: The antecedent of kull may be the immediately named pair, the sons collectively, or Abraham together with Isaac and Jacob.
- 19:49/q08/p1 Isaac and Jacob are each meant. — Baghawi, Tabarsi, Nasafi, Biqai and Tabatabai expressly name the two. Ibn al-Jawzi reports this as one reading; Baydawi permits it, and Alusi calls it the most natural reference. The statement accords with Jacob being Isaac's son. [9 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; TABATABAI+; TABRISI+]
- 19:49/q08/p2 Abraham too is included, so all three are prophets. — Yahya, Muqatil, Thalabi, Jishumi and Tabari name Abraham with Isaac and Jacob. Ibn al-Jawzi attributes this extension to Muqatil; Study Quran attributes it to Tabari and cites 6:84–89. Biqai says Isaac and Jacob received high prophetic rank as Abraham had. Alusi allows this reference. [10 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI+; MUQATIL+; STUDYQURAN(Ṭabarī)+; TAB-FULL+; THALABI+; YAHYA-SALLAM+]
- 19:49/q08/p3 The phrase may refer to Abraham's sons collectively. — Baydawi gives this as another possible scope; Alusi also allows the two sons together. The notes leave the exact extent of this collective phrasing open. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:49/q09 How does the construction 'each We made a prophet' work?  [وَكُلًّۭا جَعَلْنَا نَبِيًّۭا · grammar]
- 19:49/q09/p1 Kullan is the fronted first object of jaʿalnā and nabiyyan its second object; the singular predicate follows singular kull. — Samin al-Halabi supplies the two-object parsing. Tabari explains the singular predicate by kull's singular form. Bursevi and Alusi say fronting kull emphasizes that every one, rather than only some, was made a prophet. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; SAMIN-DURR+; TAB-FULL+]

## 19:49/q10 Was Jacob a prophet during Abraham's lifetime?  [وَيَعْقُوبَ نَبِيًّۭا · interpretation]
- 19:49/q10/p1 Yes; Jacob's prophethood and the prophetic succession were a joy Abraham could witness. — Ibn Kathir argues that naming Jacob rather than his also-prophetic son Joseph requires Jacob's prophethood within Abraham's life. He cites 2:133 for the descent and transmits the sound agreed-upon report calling Joseph a prophet son of Jacob, Isaac and Abraham, or a noble son of noble lineage. Islahi likewise stresses the three successive prophets and Joseph's addition. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 19:49/q11 What general lesson does Abraham's loss and divine gift teach?  [* · interpretation]
- 19:49/q11/p1 Giving up people or worldly means for God brings a better divine replacement and no ultimate loss. — Kashshaf applies this to leaving unbelieving, wicked people and receiving believing prophetic children. Baladhuri as cited by Sulami states the broader rule about worldly means. Razi says migration cost Abraham neither religion nor worldly life, and his opposition to creatures for God led to divine friendship (26:77; 4:125). Jishumi calls the rise in status God's custom; Nasafi specifies believing prophetic children in exchange for wicked unbelievers. [9 notes: SULAMI(أبو محمد البلاذري); ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+; TABRISI+]

## 19:49/q12 How do spiritual readings extend the pattern of separation and reward?  [* · inward]
turns on: The reported analogies differ in whether the recompense is described as inner understanding or as gifts and assurances granted to other prophets.
- 19:49/q12/p1 Divine recompense is proportioned to spiritual struggle and may be inner understanding. — Al-Wasiti, reported by Sulami, speaks of leaders receiving understanding of recitation in legal rulings and of spiritual truth in afflictions. Baladhuri, also cited by Sulami, states the wider rule of receiving something better for what one relinquishes for God. [2 notes: SULAMI(أبو محمد البلاذري,الواسطي)]
- 19:49/q12/p2 Abraham's gift has parallels in Moses and Muhammad. — Sulami pairs Abraham's Isaac and Jacob with Moses' Aaron (19:53) and Muhammad's steadfast vision and exalted character (53:17; 68:4), also citing the pledge in 48:10. Alusi reports a mystical analogy: Muhammad's withdrawal from the whole cosmos was compensated by the declaration that pledges to him are pledges to God (48:10). [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; SULAMI+]

## 19:49/q13 What other rewards to Abraham illustrate this divine pattern?  [* · links]
- 19:49/q13/p1 Several episodes of Abraham's service received corresponding divine honors. — Razi says disavowing his father brought the title father of the Muslims (9:114; 22:78); laying down his son for sacrifice brought a great ransom (37:103,107); surrender to God brought a cool, safe fire (2:131; 21:69); care and prayer for this community brought inclusion in the five daily prayers (2:129); faithfulness concerning Sarah brought a blessed prayer place at his footprint (53:37; 2:125); opposition to creatures brought divine friendship (26:77; 4:125). He calls messengership requiring people's obedience a supreme worldly and afterlife blessing. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL+]

## 19:49/q14 How is Abraham's account used within the sūra's argument?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes give two related roles for the account: correcting religious claims about Abraham and placing miraculous births in the sūra's sequence.
- 19:49/q14/p1 It presents the earlier prophets as teaching Muhammad's message and Abraham's true religion, addressing Christians and warning Quraysh. — Islahi says Quraysh's current religion differs from Abraham's and Ishmael's. Farahi's tract, as reported by Islahi, exposes distortions and interpolations in the histories of Abraham and the other celebrated figure discussed with him. Tabatabai summarizes Abraham's dispute with his father, withdrawal and enduring good name for descendants. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Ḥamīd al-Dīn Farāhī); ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; TABATABAI+]
- 19:49/q14/p2 Abraham's childbearing in old age supplies another miraculous-birth example after Zacharias, preparing the discussion of Jesus. — Islahi says the comparison prevents miraculous birth from being treated as proof of divinity; Zacharias' narrative also prepares for Jesus. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 19:49/q15 How is Abraham's disavowal connected with divine guidance and dominion?  [* · links]
- 19:49/q15/p1 Vision of God's dominion involves trials, and guidance follows disavowal by God's gift. — Burhan links this with the saying that disbelief's head is East and cites Abraham's declaration in 37:99 together with 19:49. [1 notes: BURHAN+]

## 19:49/q16 What might the following gift of mercy to Isaac and Jacob mean?  [* · interpretation]
- 19:49/q16/p1 It may refer to imamate. — Tabatabai links the following gift of mercy with the account of Isaac and Jacob in 21:72–73. [1 notes: TABATABAI+]
