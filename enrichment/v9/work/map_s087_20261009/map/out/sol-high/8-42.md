# 8:42: 37 questions

## 8:42/q01 How does the opening 'when' connect grammatically to the preceding verse?  [إِذْ · grammar]
turns on: Its governor may be an understood command, an earlier time or verb, the Criterion, or God's power.
- 8:42/q01/p1 It is appositive to the Day of Criterion or the day the armies met. — Kashshaf, Baydawi, Ibn Ashur and Tabatabai connect it to 8:41; Ibn Ashur also relates it to 'We sent down'. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 8:42/q01/p2 An omitted 'remember' governs it. — Wahidi compares 8:26; Zajjaj and Qurtubi paraphrase 'recall when you were there'. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج); ALUSI-FULL]
- 8:42/q01/p3 The earlier 'met' governs it. — Ibn Atiyya attaches it to iltaqa in the preceding context. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 8:42/q01/p4 It attaches to al-furqan, the distinction between truth and falsehood. — Wahidi and Samin give this alternative. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; SAMIN-DURR]

## 8:42/q02 What does al-ʿudwa mean?  [ٱلْعُدْوَةِ · meaning]
- 8:42/q02/p1 The side, lip, or bank of a valley. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan derive it from preventing passage or water crossing; Mawardi places the lip above the bottom and below the top; Layth specifies firm bank ground. Ibn al-Sikkit, Tabarsi and Nahhas give plurals ʿudan, ʿudiyy or ʿudā; Biqai explores related root senses. Poems using ʿadatanī and ʿaddatnī attest restraint. ## 8:42/q08 Why does quṣwā retain wāw, and how else is it said?  [ٱلْقُصْوَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Morphologists give opposite general rules for nouns and adjectives, but agree the yāʾ form is analogical and the wāw form common.
- 8:42/q08/p1 Quṣwā is feminine of aqṣā, 'farthest'; quṣyā or qaṣiyā is the analogical alternative. — Hijaz retains wāw and Tamim uses yāʾ. Khalil and Ibn al-Sikkit call retained wāw irregular under the usual change, yet Samin notes it is widely transmitted. Kashshaf likens it to qawd; Alusi links retention to its adjective status. Zayd ibn Ali reads the yāʾ form. [30 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن السكيت عن الحراني,الخليل عن الليث); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زيد بن علي); SAMIN-DURR(زيد بن علي); ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن علي); BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 8:42/q08/p2 Under a reversed morphological rule, adjectives normally change wāw to yāʾ and nouns retain it. — Samin cites ḥazwā in poetry and says this account too makes quṣwā analogically irregular, though common in usage; Alusi reports some morphologists who hold it. [6 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الشاعر); ALUSI-FULL(من أهل التصريف)]

## 8:42/q10 What does al-rakb denote?  [وَٱلرَّكْبُ · referent]

## 8:42/q11 How many riders were in Abu Sufyan's caravan?  [وَٱلرَّكْبُ · reports]

## 8:42/q12 What does rakb mean as an Arabic collective?  [ٱلرَّكْبُ · meaning]
turns on: Sources differ over whether it is a collective noun or broken plural, and over a numerical threshold.
- 8:42/q12/p1 A collective noun for riders, especially riders on camels. — Ibn al-Sikkit and Ibn Faris exclude horse riders; Ibn Atiyya says it can pluralize like ṣāḥib/ṣuḥb and is not a very large group. Samin cites diminutive rukayb as proof it is a singular collective, rejecting Akhfash's plural analysis. [9 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن السكيت); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن السكيت وابن فارس); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش); ALUSI-FULL]
- 8:42/q12/p2 It denotes at least ten or about ten riders. — Wahidi reports Ibn al-Sikkit; Ibn Atiyya rejects al-Qutaybi's threshold, citing the Prophet's use for three people. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن السكيت); IBNATIYYA-FULL(القتبي)-]
- 8:42/q12/p3 It is a broken plural of rider. — Samin reports and rejects al-Akhfash's analysis on the evidence of diminutive rukayb. [0 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش)-]

## 8:42/q13 How is 'asfala minkum' parsed and read?  [أَسْفَلَ مِنكُمْ · grammar]

- 8:42/q13/p1 Accusative locative adverb or implied 'in a lower place', functioning as predicate. — Farra, Akhfash, Zajjaj, Sibawayh, Abu Hatim, Kashshaf, Samin, Alusi and Ibn Ashur discuss the omitted place and predicate. ## 8:42/q14 How do the clauses about the three groups connect?  [وَهُم وَٱلرَّكْبُ · grammar]

## 8:42/q16 What does the hypothetical failure to keep an appointment mean?  [وَلَوْ تَوَاعَدتُّمْ لَٱخْتَلَفْتُمْ فِى ٱلْمِيعَٰدِ · interpretation]
turns on: Explanations turn on fear of relative forces, ordinary timing failures, obstacles, distrust, or the force of the hypothetical itself.
- 8:42/q16/p1 The Muslims would shrink from facing Quraysh after learning the imbalance. — Tabari and Ibn Ishaq cite fewer Muslims versus numerous enemy. Zamakhshari adds Meccan fear of the Prophet and believers. Alusi rejects this uniform pronoun reading, and Ibn Ashur objects that fear predicts nonattendance rather than different arrival times. [25 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري وغيره); TABRISI(ابن إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن إسحاق); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,الطبري وغيره); IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL(بعض المفسرين)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 8:42/q16/p2 They would differ unintentionally in exact timing or place. — Mawardi mentions advance, delay, increase or decrease; Abu Sulayman says the camps would not coincide. Ibn Ashur argues approximate daytime appointments could not reliably bring both armies together at once; timely arrival determined battle prospects according to Islahi. [10 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان,الماوردي); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABRISI(الشاعر)]
- 8:42/q16/p3 Obstacles and contingencies would prevent the arranged meeting without divine facilitation. — Wahidi, Mawardi, Mahdawi and Tabarsi give impediments. Ibn Atiyya rejects mere obstacles and emphasizes God's facilitation; Abu Hayyan reports Ibn Atiyya as preferring the Mahdawi explanation, a transmission at odds with Ibn Atiyya's own note. Ibn Ashur calls contingencies closest yet incomplete. - 8:42/q16/p4 God alone could bring this meeting about despite human plans and effort. — Ibn Atiyya stresses divine favor and power, Qushayri recoil, and Tabatabai special will. ## 8:42/q17 Who is meant by 'you' in the meeting conditional?  [تَوَاعَدتُّمْ لَٱخْتَلَفْتُمْ · referent]
turns on: Alusi restricts the second pronoun to Muslims, whereas Zamakhshari and Ibn Ashur include both armies.
- 8:42/q17/p1 Both armies are included in both pronouns. — Zamakhshari cites fear on both sides; Ibn Ashur explains grammatical predominance of the addressees; Alusi rejects Zamakhshari's uniform reference as unsuitable. - 8:42/q17/p2 The first pronoun includes both armies, but the second points to the Muslims. — Alusi says the shift fits the stress on Muslim weakness and divine aid. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 8:42/q18 What grammatical force does the conditional have?  [وَلَوْ تَوَاعَدتُّمْ · grammar]
- 8:42/q18/p1 It is a circumstantial, elliptical counterfactual about an arranged battle. — Ibn Ashur relates it to the two armies' meeting; Asad expands the implied words, and says commentators accept the ellipsis. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(all commentators)]
- 8:42/q18/p2 Its apodosis can stay true even when its condition is merely imagined. — Ibn Ashur calls this the Suhaybiyya use of law and cites other Quranic cases and Tufayl's poem. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(طفيل)]

## 8:42/q19 What is the matter God brought about?  [وَلَٰكِن لِّيَقْضِىَ ٱللَّهُ أَمْرًۭا · interpretation]
- 8:42/q19/p1 The divinely decreed Badr victory: Islam and its people raised, idolatry and its people humbled. — Tabari specifies killing and capture, Thalabi and Ibn Ishaq vengeance and favor, Ibn Abbas the promised joy, and Ibn Ashur a great rather than ordinary matter. ## 8:42/q20 What does 'was accomplished' mean?  [كَانَ مَفْعُولًۭا · interpretation]

## 8:42/q21 Why is amran indefinite?  [أَمْرًۭا · rhetoric]
- 8:42/q21/p1 Its indefiniteness magnifies the momentous affair. — Ibn Ashur says it means a great matter rather than an ordinary command and compares 19:21 and 33:38. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 8:42/q22 What does the purpose lām in liyaqḍiya attach to?  [لِّيَقْضِىَ · grammar]

## 8:42/q36 What other reports and neighboring discussions accompany the Badr account?  [* · reports]

## 8:42/q37 What adjacent-verse or technical notes appear with this verse's sources?  [* · links]
- 8:42/q37/p1 Adjacent Badr wording concerns revelation, spoils, and the Day of Criterion. — Abu Hayyan says what was sent down comprised verses, angels and victory; reports Zayd's reading of ʿabdinā and two parses of yawm al-furqān. Ibn Atiyya likes Zajjaj's sense but finds attachment hard; Abu Hayyan rejects it for syntactic separation. Islahi identifies the address as especially to weak Muslims. Burhan defines iḥtirās as closing a distant unwanted sense. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزجاج,زيد بن علي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURHAN]
- 8:42/q37/p2 Ibn Jinni treats readings in nearby verses about honey and water, sharridh and fajnuḥ. — He defends generic coordinated nouns with a supporting poem, explains dhāl as substitution for dāl, and calls the ḍamma imperfect of the intransitive verb analogical.
