# 26:81: 19 questions

## 26:81/q01 What does God causing Abraham to die and then live mean?  [يُمِيتُنِى يُحْيِينِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the language denotes bodily death and resurrection, deferred reward, or a specific inward contrast; al-Mawardi and al-Qurtubi dispute treating allegories as the verse's intended meaning.
- 26:81/q01/p1 God causes actual death in this world and restores life at resurrection in the next. — Muqatil locates death in this world and life in the next. Yahya al-Sallam identifies revival as resurrection. Ibn Abbas and the commentators, reported by al-Wahidi, give the same sequence. Al-Tabrisi places resurrection on its Day. Al-Bursevi adds repayment of deeds. Al-Tabataba'i calls death a decreed transition between abodes, not annihilation, citing 21:35. Ibn Kathir says the One who originated creation alone can restore it. [12 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 26:81/q01/p2 God causes death through sin and life through obedience. — Reported by al-Sulami, al-Tha'labi, al-Qurtubi, al-Bursevi and The Study Quran. Al-Mawardi rejects this as speculative embellishment departing from the apparent, reasonable meaning; al-Qurtubi rejects taking obscure inward symbols as the intended sense for an uninformed audience. [5 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); THALABI(أهل اللسان والإشارة); QURTUBI-FULL(أهل الإشارات); BURSEVI(الثعلبي); STUDYQURAN(Al-Thaʿlabī); MAWARDI-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 26:81/q01/p3 God causes death through justice and life through grace. — Al-Tha'labi's language-and-allusion authorities, al-Qurtubi's allusionists, al-Bursevi's report from al-Tha'labi, and The Study Quran's report all give this pairing. Al-Qurtubi rejects these allegories as the intended sense. [4 notes: THALABI(أهل اللسان والإشارة); QURTUBI-FULL(أهل الإشارات); BURSEVI(الثعلبي); STUDYQURAN(Al-Thaʿlabī); QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 26:81/q01/p4 God causes death through separation and life through reunion or meeting Him. — Al-Tha'labi, al-Qurtubi, al-Bursevi reporting al-Tha'labi, and The Study Quran give union, reunion, or meeting God as the corresponding life. Al-Qurtubi objects to making such symbols the intended sense. [4 notes: THALABI(أهل اللسان والإشارة); QURTUBI-FULL(أهل الإشارات); BURSEVI(الثعلبي); STUDYQURAN(Al-Thaʿlabī); QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 26:81/q01/p5 God causes death through ignorance and life through knowledge or understanding. — Al-Tha'labi says knowledge; al-Qurtubi says reason; al-Bursevi's report from al-Tha'labi says intellect; The Study Quran reports knowledge. Al-Qurtubi rejects these allegories as the intended sense. [4 notes: THALABI(أهل اللسان والإشارة); QURTUBI-FULL(أهل الإشارات); BURSEVI(الثعلبي); STUDYQURAN(Al-Thaʿlabī); QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 26:81/q01/p6 God causes death through heedlessness or forgetting Him and life through remembrance. — Al-Tustari holds this. Al-Sulami transmits it from al-Junayd and Sahl, and The Study Quran reports the forgetting-and-remembering form. [4 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(الجنيد,سهل); STUDYQURAN(Su)]
- 26:81/q01/p7 God causes death through fear of Him and life through hope in Him. — Al-Sulami transmits Abu Uthman's reading and al-Qurtubi reports it among the allusions. Al-Mawardi calls this speculative embellishment; al-Qurtubi denies that the obscure allegory is the intended sense. [2 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان); QURTUBI-FULL(أهل الإشارات); MAWARDI-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 26:81/q01/p8 God causes death through greed and life through contentment. — Al-Sulami attributes this to Muhammad ibn Hamid; al-Qurtubi records it as an allegory. Al-Mawardi rejects it as an embellishment; al-Qurtubi rejects such allegories as the intended sense. [2 notes: SULAMI(محمد بن حامد); QURTUBI-FULL(أهل الإشارات); MAWARDI-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 26:81/q01/p9 Death occurs outwardly while life persists inwardly. — Al-Sulami reports this from some authorities; The Study Quran reports the same outward and inward contrast. [2 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(Su)]
- 26:81/q01/p10 God kills the self or its ego and gives it life through Himself or divine guidance. — Al-Sulami reports self-effacement and life through God from Dhu al-Nun, and independence of self and life through God from al-Kharraz. Al-Bursevi cites Salmi's H in which guidance is true life. The Study Quran describes death of the passionate ego and enlivening of the spirit. [4 notes: SULAMI(الخراز,ذو النون); BURSEVI(سلمى); STUDYQURAN]
- 26:81/q01/p11 Death releases one from worldly afflictions and revival brings recompense. — Al-Razi links dying to release from worldly afflictions and punishments and restored life to recompense. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 26:81/q01/p12 God postpones Abraham until the time of recompense. — Al-Jishumi makes the death-and-life expression point toward deferred recompense. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 26:81/q01/p13 God causes death by veiling and life by manifestation. — Al-Sulami reports this from al-Wasiti. [1 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي)]
- 26:81/q01/p14 God kills the heart's attachment to this world and revives it by nearness to Him. — Al-Sulami reports this from Dhu al-Nun. [1 notes: SULAMI(ذو النون)]
- 26:81/q01/p15 God causes death through need of Him and life through independence from Him. — Al-Sulami reports this paradoxical contrast from al-Junayd. [1 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد)]
- 26:81/q01/p16 God causes death away from Him and life through Him. — Al-Sulami reports this from Ibn Ata. Al-Bursevi's Persian couplet says one whose joy and life is God need not grieve or fear or attach the heart even to the next world. [2 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
- 26:81/q01/p17 God causes death through trials and life through blessings. — Al-Sulami reports this from an unnamed authority. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 26:81/q01/p18 God causes death through abandonment and life through success. — Al-Tha'labi transmits this among the language-and-allusion pairings. [1 notes: THALABI(أهل اللسان والإشارة)]
- 26:81/q01/p19 God causes death and life through richness. — Al-Tha'labi includes richness on both sides of this allusive contrast. [1 notes: THALABI(أهل اللسان والإشارة)]
- 26:81/q01/p20 God causes death through greed and life through piety. — Al-Bursevi reports this version from al-Tha'labi, differing from the greed-and-contentment wording elsewhere. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الثعلبي)]
- 26:81/q01/p21 God causes both bodily death and spiritual death, then restores bodily and spiritual life. — Al-Biqa'i says efforts cannot avert bodily death or the spiritual death of ignorance; God restores bodily life by spirit and spiritual life by guidance, for recompense in the afterlife. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 26:81/q02 Why does Abraham attribute his death to God?  [يُمِيتُنِى · rhetoric]
turns on: The explanations appeal either to death's eventual benefit or to its universal inevitability and the manners of attributing affliction.
- 26:81/q02/p1 Death is a divine favor, since it leads to eternal life and relief from suffering or sin. — Al-Nasafi says the wording 'He causes me to die' rather than 'when I die' presents passage from affliction and perishing to abiding life and hoped-for meeting with God. Al-Bursevi says perfected people gain eternal life and freedom from trials. Al-Alusi prefers the further explanation that perfected people value eternal good above worldly life and sinners cease further sin. Ibn Ashur says death appears a blessing on reflection and prepares spiritual perfection. [4 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)+]
- 26:81/q02/p2 Death is a universal, inevitable decree that can fittingly be attributed to God. — Ibn al-Munir, reported by al-Alusi, says sharing death with everyone lessens its sting, unlike particular illness, which good manners ascribe to one's own proximate cause. Al-Alusi faults al-Zamakhshari for overlooking the distinction: even illness-related death can follow human negligence, yet Abraham attributes death generally to God. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير)]

## 26:81/q03 What did Abraham's people accept or deny about death and resurrection?  [يُمِيتُنِى يُحْيِينِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether their accepted knowledge of death included God's role in causing it.
- 26:81/q03/p1 They accepted death, assigned it to another cause, and denied resurrection. — Al-Wahidi's compositional analyst says they assigned death a cause other than God. Ibn al-Jawzi says they denied God's decree as its cause; al-Qurtubi says they ascribed death to causes. Ibn Ashur names time or nature, while allowing that believers in divine creation and resurrection might have attributed it to God. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 26:81/q03/p2 They accepted God's causing death but denied resurrection. — Al-Burhan says the disputed claim was resurrection, not God's role in death. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 26:81/q04 Why is the death clause stated without restriction or added emphasis?  [يُمِيتُنِى · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether lack of restriction follows from God's obvious prerogative or from the audience's actual beliefs.
- 26:81/q04/p1 Only God can do this, so an exclusive or emphatic claim was unnecessary. — Abu Hayyan calls Nimrod's claim to give life and death mere imposture and insolence. Al-Biqa'i says Abraham visibly showed his inability to create a form, much less give or remove its spirit; he notes that only God reports Nimrod's claim in 2:258. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 26:81/q04/p2 The people did not attribute death to idols, so the clause needs no restriction. — Ibn Ashur says they instead attributed it to time or nature, or to God if they acknowledged creation and resurrection. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:81/q05 How does Abraham's mention of death and resurrection argue his case?  [يُمِيتُنِى يُحْيِينِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the argument's immediate conclusion is resurrection from accepted premises or the sole worthiness of God for worship.
- 26:81/q05/p1 The accepted facts about death and God's favors imply the disputed resurrection. — Al-Mawardi calls this an inference rather than a direct argument, since the audience disputed death and resurrection; accepted favors entail what they disputed. Ibn al-Jawzi says their acceptance of death supports the resurrection they deny. Al-Islahi says recreation is no harder than creation, and such providence cannot end without consequence. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:81/q05/p2 These acts show that only their performer deserves divinity and worship. — Al-Tabrisi says Abraham argues against his people for God's sole divinity. The Study Quran says only a being capable of the listed acts merits worship; Ibn Kathir says God alone gives life and death because He originates and returns creation. [3 notes: TABRISI; STUDYQURAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 26:81/q06 How does the death-and-life pair fit the sequence of divine acts?  [يُمِيتُنِى يُحْيِينِ · links]
- 26:81/q06/p1 The sequence traces the whole course of life and prepares for afterlife and spiritual perfection. — Ibn Ashur says five divine acts span bodily and intellectual creation, food and water, illness and healing, death and resurrection; physical and second life are gifts. He says reflection reveals death's blessing and the descriptions prepare spiritual perfection. Al-Islahi says recreation follows naturally from creation, sustenance and guidance, since providence requires consequence. Al-Alusi reports that pairing death and revival reflects God's defining prerogatives and the affairs of the afterlife. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة)]

## 26:81/q07 What does 'then' indicate between death and life?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
- 26:81/q07/p1 Resurrection comes after an interval following death. — Al-Tha'labi and al-Wahidi identify separation or interval. Al-Baghawi places death in this world and life in the Hereafter. Al-Nasafi contrasts immediate fa for guidance and healing with thumma here. Al-Burhan, al-Bursevi, al-Alusi and al-Samarrai also mark temporal delay; al-Alusi says future events remain near despite it. Al-Biqa'i adds the long barzakh interval and resurrection's greatness. [9 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 26:81/q08 How is the final first-person yāʾ treated in the verse ending?  [يُحْيِينِ · readings]
turns on: Which reciter or written form retains the extra first-person yāʾ, especially at a pause.
- 26:81/q08/p1 The yāʾ is omitted in the verse-ending form. — Al-Tabrisi says final yāʾ letters are omitted because they end verses. Ibn Ashur says four verbs omit first-person yāʾ for lightness and pause rhyme, since most endings of this surah have a still nun, mentioning 26:14. Qiraat-ER contrasts Hafs's deleted yāʾ with an explicit suffix and says meaning is unaffected. [3 notes: TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER(Yz)]
- 26:81/q08/p2 The final yāʾ is retained or affirmed. — Al-Samin reports Ibn Abi Ishaq, reportedly followed by Asim, retaining it in yasqin, yashfin and yuhyin. Ibn al-Jazari reports Yaqub affirming sixteen extra yāʾs, the eight quoted forms plus atīʿūn in eight places, in both connected and pause forms. Qiraat-ER records the explicit suffix as orthographic and without change of meaning. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(ابن أبي إسحاق); NASHR(يعقوب); QIRAAT-ER(Yz)]

## 26:81/q09 What is resurrection like in al-Junayd's inward comparison?  [يُحْيِينِ · inward]
- 26:81/q09/p1 It entails nakedness, hunger and thirst, except for those clothed in reverence and given gnosis and love. — The Study Quran reports al-Junayd's comparison. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Junayd)]

## 26:81/q10 How does the death clause connect to Abraham's later hope?  [وَٱلَّذِى يُمِيتُنِى · grammar]
- 26:81/q10/p1 The expression is glossed with 'I hope,' completing the sense of the following clause. — Al-Wahidi reports this gloss from Muqatil. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)]

## 26:81/q11 Why repeat the relative pronoun in this series?  [وَٱلَّذِى · rhetoric]
- 26:81/q11/p1 It emphasizes the possessor of three magnificent divine attributes. — Ibn Ashur contrasts the repeated relative pronoun with direct coordination of all clauses. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:81/q12 What rhetorical pattern do Abraham's successive descriptions exemplify?  [* · rhetoric]
- 26:81/q12/p1 They exemplify al-tafwit: varied meanings in separate sentences of equal measure. — Al-Suyuti defines the form as praise and description in matched long, medium, or short sentences, then gives Abraham's statements about guidance, provision, healing, death and revival as a long-sentence example. [2 notes: ITQAN]

## 26:81/q13 How does this description lead into Abraham's prayer?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the link is a rhetorical transition to petition or an implied phrase completing the next clause.
- 26:81/q13/p1 The speech moves gracefully from God's attributes into a fitting request. — Al-Tibi, reported by al-Suyuti, cites the move to 'My Lord, grant me judgment and join me with the righteous.' [1 notes: ITQAN(الطيبي)]
- 26:81/q13/p2 Muqatil supplies an implied 'I hope' that carries the sense into the next clause. — Al-Wahidi reports Muqatil's gloss of 'the One who causes me to die' as 'I hope.' [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)]

## 26:81/q14 How does the Abraham account fit the sūra's surrounding narratives?  [* · links]
- 26:81/q14/p1 It continues the sura's preaching pattern and fills the chronological gap between Noah and Moses. — Al-Islahi says it has the preceding anecdote's preaching purpose and recurring ending verses, and fills the gap after Noah in the previous surah because Abraham was the most prominent messenger between Noah and Moses. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:81/q15 Why does Abraham present these fundamentals as personal impressions?  [* · rhetoric]
- 26:81/q15/p1 During acquittal and migration, intellectual persuasion is futile, but this logical, subtle style may still stir hearts. — Al-Islahi describes the ordered statements of Abraham's preaching this way. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:81/q16 What account is given of Abraham's illness in the surrounding passage?  [* · reports]
- 26:81/q16/p1 It was a kind of feigned illness, like a scholar pretending sickness to receive visitors. — Al-Bursevi reports this from Kashf al-Asrar and says his illness was not known at the time. [1 notes: BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار)]

## 26:81/q17 What inward healing is alluded to in the surrounding passage?  [* · inward]
- 26:81/q17/p1 Gabriel's divinely commissioned visits healed Abraham's heart. — Al-Bursevi says Gabriel sometimes visited to ask after him, and Abraham inwardly rejoiced at being asked. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 26:81/q18 What anecdote illustrates reliance on God as the source of sickness?  [* · reports]
- 26:81/q18/p1 A pale sick man declined a doctor's help because the doctor himself had made him ill. — Al-Bursevi reports the unnamed man's reply as an expression of reliance on God. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 26:81/q19 What is promised for reciting Abraham's prayer after ablution on the way to obligatory prayer?  [* · reports]
- 26:81/q19/p1 A prophetic report promises divine gifts spanning guidance, sustenance, healing, forgiveness, renown and Paradise. — Al-Suyuti reports guidance; Paradise food and drink; healing and expiation; blessed life and martyrdom; forgiveness, wisdom and truthful renown; and Paradise homes. Al-Hasan adds a prayer for the parents. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
