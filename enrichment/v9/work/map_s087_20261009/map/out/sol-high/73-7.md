# 73:7: 7 questions

## 73:7/q01 Who is addressed by 'you'?  [لَكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the second-person address is specific to the Prophet or general to night worshippers.
- 73:7/q01/p1 The Prophet is addressed directly; the command may extend to his community. — Al-Tabari explicitly names Muhammad and his community. Al-Biqāʿī says addressing the Prophet strengthens the duty for the community; Kur'an Yolu extends his daytime responsibilities to it. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 73:7/q01/p2 The address can apply to any person who worships at night. — Al-Biqāʿī gives this as one possibility alongside a specific address to the Prophet. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 73:7/q02 What is the long daytime sabḥ?  [سَبْحًۭا طَوِيلًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether sabḥ denotes motion and consuming duties, available time and rest, devotional activity, or daytime compensation; the movement and glorification readings dispute lexical usage and the force of ṭawīlan.
- 73:7/q02/p1 It is extended movement through work, errands, livelihood, and other daytime duties, leaving night better suited to concentrated worship. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Mubarrad, al-Zamakhshari, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāghib, and others ground sabḥ in swimming, running, swift movement, a fast horse, or moving back and forth; al-Qurṭubī cites Imruʾ al-Qays, al-Thaʿlabī an unnamed poet, and al-Rāghib cites 21:33 and 79:3. Al-Samīn calls it a verbal noun and a metaphor for moving through affairs like water. Ibn Zayd says use day for needs and night for religion. Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, and Study Quran say duties prevent full concentration by day. Al-Ṭabrisī lists preaching, teaching, and provision for family; Kur'an Yolu adds earning livelihood and conveying the Qur'an. Al-Biqāʿī says earning supports duties and dependents without lethargy or excess. Ibn Kathīr endorses Ibn Zayd's historical reading: night prayer was then obligatory before 73:20 eased it, with 17:79 also cited. Al-Ālūsī prefers this explanation on the root sense and context; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers it on context. Islāhī objects that unqualified sabḥ seldom means busyness, that tawīlan suits glorification better, and that the night is inherently suitable for prayer. [54 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(قراءة العامة); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي وابن زيد); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأعرابي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قراءة العامة); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين,علي بن عبيد الله); RAZI-FULL(المبرد); QURTUBI-FULL(قراءة العامة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL(البغوي,الرازي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(الراغب,بعض التفاسير,تاج المصادر); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Some exegetes); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(JJ); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن زيد)+; TABATABAI+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 73:7/q02/p2 It is a long span of free time for needs, sleep, rest, or general use, so night can be devoted to worship. — Ibn ʿAbbās and ʿAṭāʾ explicitly say sleep and rest; Mujāhid calls it enjoyment or use, Qatāda, Abū Muʿādh, Abū al-ʿĀliya and others free time. Muqātil says exclusive night prayer grew difficult for some. Al-Ṭabarī finds sabḥ and sabkh close here in giving ample room; al-Naṣafī allows both activity and rest. Al-Ālūsī says on this reading daytime rest completes the rationale for night worship. Study Quran describes worldly matters occupying the day and a share of night for prayer. [37 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(أبو معاذ,ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(قراءة العامة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس وعطاء); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(قراءة العامة); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حاتم); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وعطاء); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وعطاء); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو العالية، ومجاهد، وابن مالك، والضحاك، والحسن، وقتادة، والربيع بن أنس، وسفيان الثوري,ابن عباس، وعكرمة، وعطاء بن أبي مسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو مالك والربيع,ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ABUDAWUD(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 73:7/q02/p3 It means daytime supplication, glorification, or worship alongside night prayer. — Al-Suddī calls it abundant supplication or voluntary worship. Ibn Ṭāhir describes service, turning toward God, and awaiting revelation. Ibn al-Jawzī reports ʿAlī b. ʿUbayd Allāh's derivation of tasbīḥ as running in glorification, and Ibn Fāris's definition as exalting God above evil. Islāhī holds that the Prophet remembered God throughout daily activities and that transmitted supplications turn routines into worship; he argues tawīlan fits glorification and rejects the busyness gloss. [9 notes: SULAMI(ابن طاهر); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي وابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن فارس,علي بن عبيد الله); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 73:7/q02/p4 It is time during the day to make up a missed portion of night worship. — Some scholars in Ibn ʿAṭiyya specify sleep or an excuse. Al-Zajjāj says missed sleep or rest may be recouped in the day; al-Qurṭubī and al-Rāzī report daytime replacement for what was missed at night, and al-Kashshāf reports making up missed prayer. Al-Bursawī says this protects communion with God; al-Ālūsī relates it to 25:62 and says the need to make it up urges keeping the night vigil; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also cites 25:62. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم من أهل العلم); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BURSEVI(بعض التفاسير); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 73:7/q02/p5 It promises a long period of purification after the commanded night vigil. — Elmalılı presents this as a future-oriented glad tiding and compares the dawn image in 74:34. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 73:7/q02/p6 Sabḥ can also denote stretching oneself. — Al-Qurṭubī attributes tasabbuḥ in this sense to al-Khalīl. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 73:7/q02/p7 The phrase gives leisure to the moon. — Al-Layth's gloss is transmitted by al-Wāḥidī without further explanation. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الليث)]

## 73:7/q03 How is sabḥan read?  [سَبْحًۭا · readings]
turns on: The consonant is ḥāʾ in the common reading and khāʾ in the transmitted variant.
- 73:7/q03/p1 The common reading has sabḥan with ḥāʾ. — Al-Zajjāj explicitly prefers this reading. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Jishumī, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Samīn call it the common form; its derivation from swift movement, swimming, and a fast horse underlies the busy-day interpretation. [5 notes: THALABI(قراءة العامة); JISHUMI(قراءة العامة); QURTUBI-FULL(قراءة العامة); SAMIN-DURR; ZAJJAJ+]
- 73:7/q03/p2 A variant reads sabkhan with khāʾ. — Transmitters vary: Yaḥyā ibn Yaʿmar, ʿIkrima, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Abū Wāʾil, ʿAlī, Ibn Masʿūd, Abū ʿImrān, and Ibn Abī ʿAbla are named in different reports. Al-Zajjāj says it is sound Arabic with a similar broad sense. Reported glosses include respite, sleep, lightening, spreading, and a heart scattered like carded wool. Al-Kashshāf describes the substitution inconsistently as khāʾ for sīn. [18 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(يحيى بن يعمر); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(يحيى بن يعمر); THALABI(يحيى بن يعمر); JISHUMI(يحيى بن يعمر); BAGHAWI-FULL(يحيى بن يعمر); KASHSHAF-FULL(قراءة غير مسماة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(يحيى بن يعمر وعكرمة); TABRISI(يحيى ابن يعمر والضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(علي وابن مسعود وأبو عمران وابن أبي عبلة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(يحيى بن يعمر وأبو وائل); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو الفضل الرازي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن يعمر وعكرمة,ابن يعمر وعكرمة وابن أبيعبلة)]

## 73:7/q04 What does the variant sabkhan convey?  [سَبْحًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the khāʾ root evokes relief and rest, the dispersal of concerns, or physical spreading and spaciousness; al-Samīn and al-Rāzī differ over whether the readers' sleep gloss restricts its sense.
- 73:7/q04/p1 It conveys easing, spacious respite, rest, or sleep. — Al-Farrāʾ records Abū Ziyād al-Kilābī's Bedouin phrase asking God to ease illness or a sting. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Jishumī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Samīn cite the Prophet's instruction to ʿĀʾisha not to lighten a thief's burden through her supplication; al-Baghawī also reports it. Al-Aṣmaʿī uses sabbakha of fever easing; a poet uses it for relief, and Abū ʿAmr glosses it as sleep or free time. Thaʿlab distinguishes sabḥ's motion from sabkh's stillness, with the fever-cooling report as evidence; al-Qurṭubī also transmits a variant account in which sabkh can mean agitation or stillness. Al-Rāzī reports the readers' gloss as daytime sleep aiding night prayer and says it bounds interpretation; al-Samīn rejects that restriction, and al-Ālūsī treats sleep as a consequence, not the direct sense. [32 notes: FARRA(أبو زياد الكلابي); TAB-FULL(يحيى بن يعمر); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(يحيى بن يعمر); THALABI(تغلب,يحيى بن يعمر); JISHUMI(ثعلب,يحيى بن يعمر); BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,يحيى بن يعمر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حاتم,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,يحيى بن يعمر وعكرمة); TABRISI(ثعلب,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,يحيى ابن يعمر والضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(علي وابن مسعود وأبو عمران وابن أبي عبلة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو,الأصمعي,النبي,ثعلب,يحيى بن يعمر وأبو وائل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأصمعي); SAMIN-DURR(أبو الفضل الرازي,الأصمعي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن يعمر وعكرمة,غير واحد)]
- 73:7/q04/p2 It evokes daytime concerns scattering the heart, like fibers teased apart. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī use carded or spread wool as the metaphor. Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Samīn describe fluffing cotton, wool, or flax; al-Akhṭal's line compares scattered dust to cotton tufts. Al-Zajjāj also notes sabkha as a cotton tuft. This lexical image explains dispersal of worries and concerns. [14 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL(قراءة غير مسماة); RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخطل); SAMIN-DURR(الأخطل,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(ابن يعمر وعكرمة وابن أبيعبلة,الأخطل)]
- 73:7/q04/p3 It conveys spreading or expansion and ample daytime scope. — Al-Zajjāj calls it sound Arabic and similar to sabḥ in spaciousness, deriving the image from fluffing cotton. Al-Ṭabrisī reports the reading as expansion or spreading and cites the carding usage. Al-Samīn transmits the command to stretch cotton; Abū Ḥayyān similarly gives stretching or spreading, and al-Ālūsī records cotton pieces called sabāʾikh. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; TABRISI(يحيى ابن يعمر والضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(علي وابن مسعود وأبو عمران وابن أبي عبلة); QURTUBI-FULL(يحيى بن يعمر وأبو وائل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأصمعي); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 73:7/q05 How does the verse's sentence function grammatically?  [* · grammar]
- 73:7/q05/p1 It is an uncoordinated sentence, without an explicit prescription of daytime prayer. — Ibn ʿĀshūr notes that the five daily prayers had not yet been prescribed according to the common view. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 73:7/q06 What should one do about a missed night devotion?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether restoring a missed voluntary portion is a formal legal make-up or a devotional discipline; reports also specify the daytime reward window.
- 73:7/q06/p1 Use daytime to make up a missed night portion. — The scholars reported by Ibn ʿAṭiyya mention missing it through sleep or an excuse. Al-Bursawī reports a Prophetic saying that one who recites a missed portion between dawn prayer and noon receives its night reward. Some shaykhs recommend restoring a missed litany even a month later to train the self against laziness. Al-Ṭabrisī argues that teaching and learning do not excuse omission because the Prophet had heavier teaching duties yet kept his night portion. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم من أهل العلم); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BURSEVI(النبي,بعض التفاسير,بعض المشايخ); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 73:7/q06/p2 A missed voluntary litany is not legally made up as an obligatory prayer is; doing an equivalent later is discipline. — Al-Bursawī quotes Qūt al-qulūb: perform an equivalent when remembered to train resolve and prevent habitual concessions, but it has no compulsory make-up status. [1 notes: BURSEVI(صاحب قوت القلوب)]

## 73:7/q07 How does this verse relate to the surrounding night-vigil command?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the day contrasts with night's solitude, provides compensating rest or make-up time, or joins a continuous prophetic mission.
- 73:7/q07/p1 Daytime duties explain why night offers the solitude and collected heart needed for worship. — Al-Zamakhsharī contrasts calm, quiet night recitation with scattered daytime concerns. Al-Ṭabrisī lists the Prophet's teaching, preaching, religious instruction, and family provision. Ibn ʿĀshūr says this may explain why night was chosen after 73:6: prayer then has deeper effect and firmer recitation. Al-Ālūsī calls it the external motive after the earlier internal motive. Islāhī rejects this linkage on the ground that night is intrinsically fitting for prayer. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QUSHAYRI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Some exegetes); TABATABAI+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 73:7/q07/p2 Daytime room for sleep and necessary tasks eases the burden of the night vigil. — Al-Wāḥidī says daytime rest and needs leave night for prayer; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this a gentle justification for its burden. Al-Ālūsī says the free-time interpretation completes the rationale by allowing rest. Al-Ṭabarī gives day to needs and night to religion. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وعطاء); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 73:7/q07/p3 The day's free span is a chance to replace a missed vigil, recalling the alternation of night and day. — Al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cite 25:62. Al-Ālūsī says the need for daytime replacement can urge preserving night worship in the first place. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 73:7/q07/p4 The opening call may mean taking on the prophetic mission by night and day, except necessary sleep. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports ʿIkrima's figurative reading of 'wrapped up' and places it alongside 73:7. He sketches a possible sūra chronology: 96:1, the accusation of madness and reassurance in 68:2, then al-Muddaththir after the angel at Ḥirāʾ; this depends on al-Muzzammil preceding al-Muddaththir. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(عكرمة)]
