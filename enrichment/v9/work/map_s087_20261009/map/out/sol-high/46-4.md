# 46:4: 38 questions

## 46:4/q01 Who is addressed by the command to say this?  [قُلْ · referent]
- 46:4/q01/p1 Muhammad addresses the people of Mecca, the unbelieving idolaters. — Muqatil names the Meccans; Bursevi and Alusi describe the address as rebuke and confutation. [3 notes: MUQATIL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 46:4/q02 What does أَرَءَيْتُم mean here?  [أَرَءَيْتُم · meaning]
- 46:4/q02/p1 Tell me or inform me. — Nasafi, Bursevi, Alusi, and Tabatabai use this gloss; Biqai says the question probes the idols' status, already visibly apparent. [5 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 46:4/q03 How is أَرَءَيْتُم read?  [أَرَءَيْتُم · readings]
turns on: How the hamza is retained, softened, deleted, or supplemented with kaf.
- 46:4/q03/p1 A reading adds kaf: araʾaytkum or araytkum. — Farra reports Abdullah's practice also at 107:1; Jishumi identifies Ibn Masud's codex; Alusi records the variant. [3 notes: FARRA(عبد الله); JISHUMI(عبد الله بن مسعود); ALUSI-FULL]
- 46:4/q03/p2 The common reading is araʾaytum. — Jishumi cites regional codices; Nahhas calls araytum a familiar Arabic reduction of original araʾaytum and notes another dialect softens the second hamza. [2 notes: JISHUMI(قراءة العامة); NAHHAS]
- 46:4/q03/p3 A reading deletes the hamza. — Zajjaj records omitted alif; Qiraat-ER calls deletion a mutawatir Hijazi simplification from raʾa to raytum. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ; QIRAAT-ER(Cs)]
- 46:4/q03/p4 A mutawatir reading softens the hamza to an intermediate sound. — Qiraat-ER distinguishes this from deletion. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(hmz)]
- 46:4/q03/p5 A mutawatir reading lengthens the vowel before the hamza. — Qiraat-ER notes its deliberative prosody. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(madd)]

## 46:4/q04 How do أَرَءَيْتُم, أَرُونِى, and the questions fit together syntactically?  [أَرَءَيْتُم أَرُونِى مَاذَا خَلَقُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the first verb takes objects, is an attention-getter, or shares government with the second.
- 46:4/q04/p1 أَرَءَيْتُم is transitive: ما تدعون is its first object and the creation question its second; أَرُونِى reinforces 'tell me'. — Bursevi says both objects ask the alleged gods' status; Samin and Alusi give the emphatic analysis; Abu Hayyan notes an alternative with omitted second object. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 46:4/q04/p2 أَرَءَيْتُم is an intransitive attention-getter and ما تدعون begins a rebuking question. — Ibn Atiyya allows this; Samin attributes it to Akhfash and compares 18:63; Alusi repeats it. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 46:4/q04/p3 The two verbs compete for the creation question, with the later governing and an object omitted from the first. — Abu Hayyan prefers this; Samin reports the shared-object construction; Alusi records Abu Hayyan's preference. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)+; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)+]
- 46:4/q04/p4 أَرُونِى begins a fresh challenge about the idols' status. — Biqai argues the idols can already be seen, so 'show me' asks their status rather than mere sight. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 46:4/q04/p5 أَرُونِى is an appositive of inclusion to أَرَءَيْتُم. — Alusi records this unnamed analysis. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(غير مسمى)]

## 46:4/q05 What beings are called upon besides God?  [مَّا تَدْعُونَ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the referents are idols, angels, celestial bodies, jinn, or all claimed deities.
- 46:4/q05/p1 Idols or false gods worshipped by the polytheists. — Zajjaj calls them claimed deities; Muqatil names al-Lat, al-Uzza, Manat; Razi, Qurtubi, Nasafi, and Tabatabai say idols; Alusi prefers the broader class of all false deities; Study Quran includes jinn. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 46:4/q05/p2 Angels worshipped by the Meccans. — Muqatil identifies them as the deities addressed. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 46:4/q05/p3 Idols, celestial bodies, and other worshipped objects. — Bursevi explicitly broadens the referents. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 46:4/q05/p4 All beings to which people assign independent existence or causal force. — Qashani gives this inwardly inflected referent and asks what they independently or jointly affect. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 46:4/q06 Why does the verse use plural personal verbs or the reading من for these objects?  [مَّا تَدْعُونَ خَلَقُوا۟ لَهُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether their imagined personhood or ordinary nonrational agreement governs.
- 46:4/q06/p1 Personal plural agreement reflects the worshippers' treatment of the idols as rational, honored agents. — Farra says they were imagined speaking and honored as rulers; Nahhas contrasts ordinary ما with Ibn Masud's personal من; Tabatabai says worshippers attribute rational acts. Nahhas records unanimous masculine plural خلقوا and لهم. [4 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(الفراء,القراء); TABATABAI]
- 46:4/q06/p2 Ibn Masud reads من تدعون or من تعبدون in place of ما تدعون. — Farra gives من تعبدون; Nahhas and Ibn Atiyya report من تدعون; the personal form follows their imagined status. [3 notes: FARRA(عبد الله بن مسعود); NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفراء)]

## 46:4/q07 What does تَدْعُونَ mean here?  [تَدْعُونَ · meaning]
- 46:4/q07/p1 Worship or invoke as an object of worship. — Ibn Atiyya glosses it as worship; Alusi agrees. Qashani extends calling to naming and assigning causal power. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; QASHANI]

## 46:4/q08 What does مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ convey?  [مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ · meaning]
- 46:4/q08/p1 Apart from God, beneath the supreme King who has no equal. — Biqai stresses the disparity of rank. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 46:4/q09 How is مَاذَا خَلَقُوا۟ parsed?  [مَاذَا خَلَقُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether ذا is extra, or ما and ذا form one interrogative or a relative construction.
- 46:4/q09/p1 ما is interrogative and ذا is extra; the whole phrase is the object of خلقوا. — Tabatabai gives this analysis; Alusi includes it among three parsings. [3 notes: TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 46:4/q09/p2 ماذا is one interrogative object. — Alusi says on this parse خلقوا supplies the second object of أرأيتم. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 46:4/q09/p3 ما is interrogative and ذا relative, with خلقوا as its clause. — Alusi says the whole ماذا خلقوا clause then functions as the second object of أرأيتم. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 46:4/q10 What is the challenge about earthly creation?  [مَاذَا خَلَقُوا۟ مِنَ ٱلْأَرْضِ · interpretation]
- 46:4/q10/p1 Show any earthly thing or part independently created by the alleged gods; they created nothing and so do not merit worship. — Muqatil compares God's creation; Tabari says God created the entire earth from nothing; Razi says reason excludes even the smallest thing; Tabarsi and Ibn Kathir link creation to gratitude; Islahi notes idolaters already accepted God as Creator; Kuranyolu says influence presupposes creation. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QASHANI; QUSHAYRI]

## 46:4/q11 What does مِنَ ٱلْأَرْضِ specify?  [مِنَ ٱلْأَرْضِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether it marks a part, things on earth, lower realms, or location.
- 46:4/q11/p1 It is partitive: any portion of earth or what is on its surface. — Ibn Atiyya says even animals come from earth; Abu Hayyan allows earth's parts or an omitted noun for surface things; Alusi lists parts and regions. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 46:4/q11/p2 It clarifies the otherwise ambiguous ماذا خلقوا. — Samin and Alusi identify the specifying grammatical function; Tabatabai attaches it to the interrogative. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 46:4/q11/p3 It refers to what is in or on the earth; من can mean في. — Damghani cites 2:223 and 35:40; Burhan allows this or a genus use, and compares the Friday call. [2 notes: DAMGHANI; BURHAN]
- 46:4/q11/p4 It refers broadly to the lower worlds. — Alusi prefers this over parts of earth or its surface creatures. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]

## 46:4/q12 What share in the heavens is denied?  [أَمْ لَهُمْ شِرْكٌۭ فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether partnership means creation alone, dominion/governance, or any participation.
- 46:4/q12/p1 A share in creating the heavens with God, which they have none. — Zajjaj says such creation would justify worship; Qurtubi, Alusi, Tabatabai and others read the question this way. Tabatabai argues governance entails creation, citing 39:38 and 43:87. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 46:4/q12/p2 Any share in heavenly dominion, ownership, control, governance, benefit, harm, or creation. — Muqatil links 34:22; Qushayri asks about power to help or harm; Biqai includes naturalists' governance; Bursevi includes possession; Ibn Kathir assigns all ownership and control to God. [6 notes: MUQATIL; QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; RAZI-FULL]
- 46:4/q12/p3 They share nothing in the heavens or earth. — Tabari says the supposed share would warrant service, but creation belongs only to God. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]

## 46:4/q13 How is أَمْ joined to the first challenge?  [أَمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether أم is disconnected or connected with an omitted counterpart.
- 46:4/q13/p1 It is connected to an omitted matching clause: a share in earth or a share in heaven. — Bursevi prefers this analysis; Alusi records it; Biqai describes the paired omissions as iḥtibāk, with creation supplied for heaven and partnership for earth. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(غير مسمى); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI+]
- 46:4/q13/p2 It is a disconnected أم introducing a fresh question. — Samin states this; Alusi allows it. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 46:4/q14 Why contrast creation on earth with partnership in the heavens?  [مِنَ ٱلْأَرْضِ فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ · rhetoric]
turns on: What the upper and lower language brings out.
- 46:4/q14/p1 The pair excludes independent lower-world creation and shared upper-world creation, covering all grounds of worship. — Biqai identifies reciprocal ellipsis; Alusi says either denial removes their claim; Bursevi names heaven's elevated unsupported structure as conspicuous evidence. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 46:4/q14/p2 The heavens guard against a theory of celestial intermediaries governing earthly events. — Baydawi notes this possible inference; Bursevi repeats it; Kuranyolu says the unseen heaven receives a thought-provoking question rather than a demand to show it. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 46:4/q14/p3 Heavenly creation is obvious common ground; agency in lower events is disputed. — Alusi says everyone concedes the first, while apparent human agency makes the second contested. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 46:4/q15 What kind of book is demanded, and what must it show?  [ٱئْتُونِى بِكِتَٰبٍۢ مِّن قَبْلِ هَٰذَآ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether it must validate idol worship, partnership in creation, or affirm divine oneness.
- 46:4/q15/p1 An earlier revealed scripture that validates worship of idols or their partnership with God. — Muqatil includes the claim God has a partner; Tabari says proof of creation or a heavenly share; Razi says an alleged divine command requires revelation. Tabatabai names the Torah; Biqai adds Gospel and Psalms, which actually attest oneness; Ibn Ashur says 'come' metaphorically means produce evidence. [26 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(Q, R, Ṭ,Ṭ)]
- 46:4/q15/p2 A scripture predating the Quran affirming God's oneness. — Baydawi words the requested scripture this way. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 46:4/q16 What does مِن قَبْلِ هَٰذَآ modify or identify?  [مِّن قَبْلِ هَٰذَآ · grammar]
turns on: Whether it describes the book itself or an implied general proof.
- 46:4/q16/p1 It qualifies كتاب: a revealed book before this Quran. — Abu al-Baqa gives the adjective parse; Qurtubi and Ibn Ashur identify 'this' as Quran. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 46:4/q16/p2 It qualifies an implied more general item: something existing before this. — Samin prefers the broader ellipsis. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR+]

## 46:4/q17 Why require the book to predate the Quran?  [مِّن قَبْلِ هَٰذَآ · rhetoric]
- 46:4/q17/p1 It prevents the opponents from composing a text for the challenge and confronts them with earlier revelation's monotheism. — Ibn Ashur relates their boast at 8:31; Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi, and Razi say all earlier scriptures reject idolatry, Razi calling that widely transmitted knowledge. [7 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 46:4/q18 How is أَثَٰرَةٍ read and formed?  [أَثَٰرَةٍۢ · readings]
turns on: The presence of alif, hamza vowel, thāʾ vowel, and singular or verbal-noun patterns.
- 46:4/q18/p1 Without alif and with moving thāʾ: atharah or atharah, a singular trace or special knowledge. — Sulami, Ibn Abbas, Ikrima, Qatada, Ali and others are reported; Ibn Jinni and Samin call it singular of athar. Qurtubi and Wahidi associate a vocalization with knowledge specially favored; JZ records Ibn Masud, Abu Razin, Ayyub, Yaqub. Reports vary on specific readers. [20 notes: FARRA(أبو عبد الرحمن وبعض القراء); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي,الفراء); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(ابن عباس وعكرمة وقتادة وعمرو بن ميمون والأعمش); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن وطائفة معه); WAHIDI-BASIT(السلمي والحسن); JISHUMI(علي بن أبي طالب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الرحمن السلمي); TABRISI(علي وأبو عبد الرحمن السلمي وابن عباس وعكرمة وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود وأبو رزين وأيوب السختياني ويعقوب); QURTUBI-FULL(السلمي والحسن وأبو رجاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي، وابن عباس، وزيد بن علي، وعكرمة، وقتادة، والحسن، والسلمي، والأعمش، وعمرو بن ميمون); SAMIN-DURR(علي وابن عباس وزيد بن علي وعكرمة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(آخرون); ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن علي، عكرمة، قتادة، الحسن، السلمي، الأعمش، عمرو بن ميمون,علي، ابن عباس)]
- 46:4/q18/p2 The common or majority reading has an alif: athārah, a verbal noun on faʿālah. — Farra compares samāhah; Tabari compares samājah and qabāhah; Ibn Atiyya, Samin and Alusi give verbal-noun formation. Tabari says this is the established Arabic remainder sense. [17 notes: FARRA(أبو عبد الرحمن وبعض القراء); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(قراء الحجاز والعراق); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى); JISHUMI(قراءة العامة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(علي وأبو عبد الرحمن السلمي وابن عباس وعكرمة وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج,قراءة العامة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; TAB-FULL+]
- 46:4/q18/p3 Without alif and with silent thāʾ: athrah, a single transmitted report or trace. — Farra analogizes rajfah and khaṭfah; Ibn Jinni says demanding even one account strengthens the challenge; reported for Ali, Sulami, Qatada and others. Traditions differ on whether the hamza has fatḥa, ḍamma, or kasra. [14 notes: FARRA(أبو عبد الرحمن وبعض القراء); TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(علي وأبو عبد الرحمن السلمي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبي بن كعب وأبو عبد الرحمن السلمي والحسن وقتادة والضحاك وابن يعمر); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن وعكرمة,قراءة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي، والسلمي، وقتادة); SAMIN-DURR(قتادة والسلمي); ALUSI-FULL(الكسائي)]
- 46:4/q18/p4 Other hamza vowels include uthurah and itharah, sometimes with silent thāʾ. — Kisa'i reports the damma form and is attributed kasra too; Nahhas and Ibn Atiyya preserve variants; Baydawi and Alusi associate itharah with stirring meanings. [9 notes: NAHHAS(الكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكسائي); SAMIN-DURR(الكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الكسائي)]
- 46:4/q18/p5 Another reported reading is 'an inheritance of knowledge' (mīrāth min ʿilm). — Ikrima is cited by Ibn Atiyya through Thalabi. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عكرمة)]

## 46:4/q19 What does أَثَٰرَةٍ مِّنْ عِلْمٍ mean?  [أَثَٰرَةٍۢ مِّنْ عِلْمٍ · meaning]
turns on: Whether it is a remnant, transmitted account, special knowledge, a sign, extracted knowledge, writing, divination, or reasoned argument.
- 46:4/q19/p1 A surviving remnant or trace of earlier knowledge. — Abu Ubayda, Farra, Ibn Qutayba, Kalbi, Abu Bakr b. Ayyash and many others give this sense. Camel fat and inherited nobility illustrate it in al-Raʿi's poem; Tabari prefers established Arabic usage; Tabatabai notes it remains close to inherited learning. [47 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(أبو بكر بن عياش,راعي الإبل,قراء الحجاز والعراق); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الفراء); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; MAWARDI-FULL(أبو بكر بن عياش,عطية); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,أهل اللغة,الحسن,الكلبي); JISHUMI(الراعي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الراعي); TABRISI(ابن جني); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة والفراء وأبو عبيدة,الفراء,اليزيدي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس والكلبي وأبو بكر بن عياش,قراءة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور,الراعي); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو بكر بن عياش); BIQAI-FULL(ابن برجان); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(Q, R, Ṭ); TAB-FULL+]
- 46:4/q19/p2 A transmitted report, chain, or inherited teaching from earlier people or prophets. — Farra allows recovery from earlier books; Mujahid asks for a transmitter, Qurazi an isnad, Muqatil a prophetic report of partnership. Aʿsha's line, Umar's āthiran saying and maʾthūr usage support the sense; Tabatabai prefers Raghib's derivation from following a trace. [49 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; MAJAZ; FARRA; NAHHAS(الفراء,الكسائي); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد,يحيى); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل اللغة,الفراء,المبرد,عطاء عن ابن عباس,مقاتل); JISHUMI(الأعشى); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي,مجاهد وعكرمة ومقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(القرظي,عمر,مجاهد); TABRISI(عكرمة ومقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء,اليزيدي,مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(الأعشى,القرظي,عكرمة ومقاتل,مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القرطبي,مجاهد); SAMIN-DURR(عمر); IBNKATHIR-FULL(آخرون,مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL(ابن برجان,البغوي); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(القرطبي); TABATABAI(الراغب); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(R); TABATABAI+]
- 46:4/q19/p3 Written marks, handwriting, or divinatory lines in earth. — Ibn Abbas and some Prophetic attributions give writing or ground lines; Abu Bakr b. Ayyash calls it divination; Abu Said calls it good handwriting; Sufyan doubts whether Ibn Abbas heard his report from the Prophet. Itqan also reports astrology/divination. ## 46:4/q20 What does مِّنْ عِلْمٍ add?  [مِّنْ عِلْمٍ · grammar]
- 46:4/q20/p1 It qualifies أثارة as actual knowledge, even a small amount, and excludes conjecture. — Samin makes it an adjective; Alusi says tanwin reduces quantity; Biqai accepts a fragment from necessity, experiment, observation, another source or even conjecture, while Islahi excludes fancy and conjecture. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 46:4/q21 What kinds of proof does the verse demand, and what follows if none is produced?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: How rational, written, oral, and divine testimony support or refute the claim.
- 46:4/q21/p1 It challenges idolatry with rational evidence from creation and transmitted evidence from scripture or prior learning; absent both, the claim fails. — Tabarsi distinguishes reason, scripture, report; Qurtubi distinguishes rational and transmitted proof; Razi argues divine command needs revelation and says earlier scripture and prophetic teaching reject idols; Ibn Ashur says the breadth of allowed evidence makes the rebuttal stronger, citing 28:50. Study Quran relates other challenge verses. [41 notes: TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 46:4/q21/p2 A rational proof based on sound knowledge could also serve as the requested أثارة. — Qashani opens the offered evidence to reason; Biqai admits even a fragment; Mawardi mentions argument. [3 notes: QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL]
- 46:4/q21/p3 God's testimony through revelation or faithfully transmitted prophetic teaching is needed to establish divine partners. — Islahi says conjecture cannot justify the claim; Razi rejects any supposed command to worship idols in Muhammad's revelation and denies one in prior prophets. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; RAZI-FULL]

## 46:4/q22 What is the point of إِن كُنتُمْ صَٰدِقِينَ?  [إِن كُنتُمْ صَٰدِقِينَ · rhetoric]
- 46:4/q22/p1 It puts the claimants' truthfulness to a test: failure to produce evidence exposes a baseless or false claim. — Tabari says unsupported claims benefit no one, comparing 2:23; Biqai notes their aversion to lying; Tabatabai demands proof of a share in creation. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 46:4/q23 What does the report of a prophet drawing lines say?  [أَثَٰرَةٍۢ مِّنْ عِلْمٍ · reports]
- 46:4/q23/p1 A prophet knew line drawing; one who matched his line would know that prophet's knowledge. — Abd al-Razzaq, Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, Durr and Alusi transmit the report. Qurtubi says the line report is established through Muawiya b. al-Hakam in Muslim and another sound route, while Ibn al-Arabi and Qurtubi elsewhere call particular Ibn Abbas or matching-line routes unsound. [9 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(النبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة)]

## 46:4/q24 Does the line-drawing report permit geomancy?  [أَثَٰرَةٍۢ مِّنْ عِلْمٍ · law]
turns on: Whether matching the prophetic line is possible and whether claims to unseen knowledge are lawful.
- 46:4/q24/p1 A genuinely matching line would be valid, or the prophetic precedent permits the practice. — Ibn Atiyya says many scholars take the apparent conditional this way; Ibn al-Arabi reports a permissive view. Makki describes the prophet's two-finger method; Ibn Abbas details paired erasure and outcomes in Arab al-asham. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,ابن عباس,مكي)]
- 46:4/q24/p2 The practice is prohibited: the prophetic technique has ceased or cannot be verified, and ordinary geomancy claims unseen knowledge. — Ibn Atiyya reports this view; Khattabi calls the line a past prophetic sign; Qadi Iyad says coincidence cannot be known and law forbids divination; Qurtubi prefers prohibition, describing planetary figures and intent-dependent sin or unbelief. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,الخطابي,القاضي عياض); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 46:4/q24/p3 The verse's mention of sand writing is a taunt, not a validation of its proof. — Abu Hayyan says the gloss either has no evidential force or mocks the idolaters; Alusi relays this conditional reading. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(البحر)]

## 46:4/q25 How does the discussion distinguish omens, dreams, and knowledge of the unseen?  [أَثَٰرَةٍۢ مِّنْ عِلْمٍ · law]
turns on: Which signs can guide action and whether they give certainty.
- 46:4/q25/p1 Good words and dreams may encourage; bad omens and divinatory augury are forbidden, and God alone knows the unseen. — Ibn al-Arabi allows dreams as part of prophethood and describes faʾl as proceeding cheerfully after good words while ignoring bad ones. Qurtubi rejects a poet's condemnation of faʾl; the Prophet's prayer denies independent omens. Qurtubi says customary signs can fail, as with date palms. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,النبي)]

## 46:4/q26 Can handwriting itself serve as legal evidence?  [أَثَٰرَةٍۢ مِّنْ عِلْمٍ · law]
turns on: The reliability of authenticated writing after forgery spread.
- 46:4/q26/p1 Recognized handwriting once supported judgments, and authenticated letters, wills, and admissions can be accepted. — Ibn Khuwayz Mindad reports Malik's early practice and his school's rules when witnesses recognize a judge's letter, will, or debt admission. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن خويز منداد)]
- 46:4/q26/p2 Malik later abandoned judgment by handwriting when fraud and forgery spread. — Malik says changed conduct brings changed rulings. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مالك)]

## 46:4/q27 How does the root أثر enter the separate discussion of oaths?  [أَثَٰرَةٍۢ · meaning]
- 46:4/q27/p1 آثرا means transmitting another person's oath, rather than personally uttering it; swearing by other than God usurps God's supreme reverence. — Nahhas cites the sound report forbidding oaths by fathers, Umar's resolve, and the distinction from ذاكرا; he interprets kufr as concealing God's due reverence. He also records root uses for raised dust, soft underfoot, and mīthara with waw changed to ya. [7 notes: NAHHAS(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الأعشى)]

## 46:4/q31 What inward meanings are drawn from the verse?  [* · inward]
- 46:4/q31/p1 Ego and Satan cannot act in the self or spirit; God alone creates and turns hearts. — Bursevi maps earth to self and heavens to spirit. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 46:4/q31/p2 Sincere worship seeks neither paradise as reward nor escape from Hell. — Bursevi says even reward- or fear-driven worship regards something besides God; he attributes agreement to outer and inner scholars. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

- 46:4/q32/p1 Readers quiesce nine possessive yāʾs, including the one in Yusuf's يدعونني إليه. — Nashr records suggested difficulty from many successive letters and notes other explanations. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 46:4/q33 How does al-Khuli's hermeneutic approach understand Quran interpretation?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether interpretation receives a fixed meaning or is shaped by the interpreter's horizon.
- 46:4/q33/p1 Interpretation is active dialogue shaped by the reader's worldview, culture, expectations, and assumptions; tafsir should use literary and human sciences. — Rifai-Khuli says al-Khuli criticized sectarian instrumental tafsir, explored sociology and psychology, directed students toward the method, and formed al-Umana with Khalafallah, Bint al-Shati, and al-Alai. Later Arabic writers mainly used it in literary criticism; some experts feared clerical hostility to applying it to scripture. [11 notes: RIFAI-KHULI(أمين الخولي)]
- 46:4/q33/p2 Religious thought should be rebuilt first by clergy familiar with the tradition. — Rifai-Khuli says their knowledge and concern equip them for the task and compares Tahtawi's clerical mediation of Western ideas. [2 notes: RIFAI-KHULI]

## 46:4/q34 What intellectual history does the al-Khuli note give for modern hermeneutics?  [* · reports]
turns on: What influence on al-Khuli can be documented versus inferred.
- 46:4/q34/p1 German modern hermeneutics runs from Schleiermacher through Dilthey and Heidegger to Gadamer; al-Khuli may have encountered it during his stay in Germany. — Rifai-Khuli recounts al-Khuli's imam posts in Rome and Berlin, his return in 1927, German philosophy's influence, and Shabestari's later Hamburg path. [9 notes: RIFAI-KHULI(السيد أحمد خليل)]

## 46:4/q35 How does مَّا refer to multiple worshipped objects?  [مَّا تَدْعُونَ · grammar]

## 46:4/q36 Do Jesus's extraordinary acts amount to independent divine creation?  [خَلَقُوا۟ · theology]
- 46:4/q36/p1 No; Jesus revived the dead and made a bird only through God's power and permission. — Bursevi says those miracles do not remove Jesus's own incapacity. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

- 46:4/q37/p2 Inherited knowledge. — Mawardi transmits Ikrima's gloss without specifying its content. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة)]
