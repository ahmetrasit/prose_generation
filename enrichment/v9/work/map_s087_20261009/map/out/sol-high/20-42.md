# 20:42: 17 questions

## 20:42/q01 What does the command to go mean here, and why is it renewed?  [ٱذْهَبْ أَنتَ وَأَخُوكَ · links]
- 20:42/q01/p1 Moses and Aaron are commissioned together to go to Pharaoh, renewing the earlier command after Aaron was granted as Moses requested. — Ṭabarī says they carry proofs and deliver God's message to rebellious Pharaoh (2:41); Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes Moses is addressed honorifically, though Aaron may also have received angelic revelation. Biqāʿī, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Iṣlāḥī link the renewal to Moses' request and the earlier order (20:24); Ālūsī calls it a new sentence explaining God's special favor. Bursevī explains dhahā as physical or figurative departure (37:99; 11:74). [7 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURSEVI]

## 20:42/q02 How can Aaron be included in this command when he was absent?  [أَنتَ وَأَخُوكَ · interpretation]
- 20:42/q02/p1 The command anticipates Moses meeting Aaron and passing on the instruction; a dual prohibition can address both while one is absent. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says it is not an immediate journey; Aaron was absent at the first address and would meet Moses in Egypt. Bursevī says Aaron's going is implicit because he was away. Ālūsī invokes precedence of the present addressee for the dual prohibition. Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows separate angelic revelation to Aaron. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 20:42/q03 How is وأخوك connected grammatically to اذهب?  [ٱذْهَبْ أَنتَ وَأَخُوكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the nominative noun joins the implicit imperative subject or stands after an omitted imperative.
- 20:42/q03/p1 أخوك is coordinated with the implicit subject of اذهب, reinforced by أنت. — Abū Ḥayyān compares اذهب أنت وربك in 5:24. Ālūsī reports this analysis but notes a concern that a form valid only as a dependent cannot stand independently. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:42/q03/p2 أخوك is the subject of an omitted command: let your brother go too. — Bursevī says the ellipsis accommodates Aaron's absence; Ālūsī relates it to Moses' request. Abū Ḥayyān reports the parallel analysis of وربك in 5:24 as subject of an omitted verb. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 20:42/q04 What does أخوك mean and how does it behave in annexation?  [وَأَخُوكَ · grammar]
- 20:42/q04/p1 أخ remains أخوك in annexation even when used as a personal name; brotherhood may also be literal or figurative. — Sībawayh says naming does not alter أبوك or أخوك. His comparison distinguishes فمك from فوك, ذواك from ذوك, and named adverbs لداك, إلاك, علاك from ordinary لديك, إليك, عليك; al-Khalīl records analogous Arab usage. He compares oblique كليهما to the often annexed adverbs, while noting كلا is always dual and analogy need not cover every feature. Bursevī lists full, half and nursing brothers and figurative ties of qibla, religion, craft, dealings or affection. [10 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل); BURSEVI]

## 20:42/q05 What are God's signs with which the brothers go?  [بِـَٔايَٰتِى · referent]
turns on: Whether the signs are limited to those already shown, include revelation or the speech cure, or promise later miracles; arguments appeal to the plural, immediate context and accompaniment.
- 20:42/q05/p1 The staff and the hand. — Muqātil names both. Ibn al-Anbārī allows a plural for two; Ibn al-Jawzī prefers this view. Rāzī cites their presentation before Pharaoh and 26:31–33, 28:32. Abū Ḥayyān cites 28:32. Ālūsī prefers them because only these are named as signs before this order, and the speech knot is not called a sign there (20:22); Pharaoh requests a sign and Moses presents two proofs. The staff and hand each unfold into further signs according to Rāzī and Ālūsī. [13 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL(أصحاب القول الأول,غير مسمى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 20:42/q05/p2 The nine signs sent to Moses. — Ibn ʿAbbās is cited repeatedly; Bursevī notes that some of the nine had yet to occur. Study Quran cites 17:101. Ālūsī objects that accompaniment suggests signs already present, whereas most of the nine were future. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:42/q05/p3 God's proofs, evidences and miracles, stated generally. — The commentaries use proofs, demonstrations or miracles without fixing a number; Bayḍāwī calls them Moses' miracles and Wāḥidī says miracles given to both. Bursevī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say they equip the brothers to prove their mission before Pharaoh. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(آخرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:42/q05/p4 The staff, hand and removal of Moses' speech knot. — Ibn al-Anbārī notes that Pharaoh and his people knew the knot; Rāzī counts three miracles. Ālūsī objects that the knot is not called a sign in the preceding account. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL(غير مسمى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:42/q05/p5 All divine miracles and revelation supplied to Moses, including commands and prohibitions. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes the Torah; Abū Ḥayyān reports all miracles and revelation. Biqāʿī speaks of signs already shown and further ones displayed through Moses. Ālūsī reports the broad view while favoring the staff and hand. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:42/q05/p6 Signs God would provide as needed, beyond the two already given. — Rāzī says future signs would remove Pharaoh's and his people's excuses. Ṭabāṭabāʾī takes the plural as a promise of further divine support; Biqāʿī also mentions further signs. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(غير مسمى); BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI+]

## 20:42/q06 Why is signs plural if the staff and hand are meant?  [بِـَٔايَٰتِى · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether Arabic plural covers a pair, the miracles contain multiple proofs, or the plural refers to additional signs. Bursevī defers fuller explanation elsewhere.
- 20:42/q06/p1 Arabic can use a plural for two signs. — Ibn al-Anbārī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī cite the usage, with 28:32; Rāzī reports the view that two are the minimum plural; Bursevī attributes it to al-Khalīl and alternatively calls the plural honorific. Ibn al-Jawzī uses this to prefer staff and hand. Bursevī had deferred his explanation of the plural to a later discussion. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL(من الناس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 20:42/q06/p2 Each of the two miracles contains many signs. — Rāzī lists the staff's transformations and harmlessness and the hand's whiteness, radiance and disappearance (27:10); even one staff proves divine power, knowledge, wisdom, prophethood and resurrection (3:96–97). Abū Ḥayyān adds the staff's changes in size and safe mouth; Ālūsī adds speed, submission and return. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحاب القول الأول); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 20:42/q06/p3 The plural anticipates additional signs that God will provide. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says only two had yet been given; Rāzī says further signs would remove Pharaoh's excuses. Biqāʿī speaks of signs yet displayed. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(غير مسمى); BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 20:42/q06/p4 The plural includes a third sign, the healing of Moses' speech. — Ibn al-Anbārī, Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī report the addition of the tongue knot; Ālūsī counters that 20:22 does not name it a sign. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL(غير مسمى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 20:42/q07 What does the bāʾ in بآياتي express?  [بِـَٔايَٰتِى · grammar]
- 20:42/q07/p1 Accompaniment: the brothers go equipped with God's signs. — Rāzī says the signs make an obligation to believe possible and refute blind imitation. Bursevī and Ālūsī say the signs accompany the brothers to perform the mission rather than being delivered as objects; Ālūsī infers the signs were already present, contrary to identifying all nine. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the proofs reassure Moses before Pharaoh. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:42/q08 How did Aaron come to meet Moses?  [أَنتَ وَأَخُوكَ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ over whether Aaron received revelation or inspiration, or heard news of Moses' approach.
- 20:42/q08/p1 God revealed or instructed Aaron in Egypt to meet Moses. — Muqātil places Aaron in Egypt and the meeting before Pharaoh. Al-Kalbī says Moses then told Aaron of the command and his request that Aaron be sent; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says an angel may have brought Aaron revelation. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the command anticipated a later meeting in Egypt. [6 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:42/q08/p2 Aaron heard that Moses was approaching. — Reported as another account by Zamakhsharī and Abū Ḥayyān. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 20:42/q08/p3 Aaron was inspired to meet Moses. — Al-Kalbī relates inspiration while Aaron was in Egypt; Zamakhsharī and Abū Ḥayyān report it as an account. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 20:42/q09 What does ولا تنيا mean?  [وَلَا تَنِيَا · meaning]
turns on: The root is glossed by weakness or slackness, slowness, and in Abān's poetic evidence by cessation.
- 20:42/q09/p1 Do not weaken, flag, tire, slacken or fall short. — Mujāhid, Qatāda, Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Suddī, Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb and many commentators give these close glosses. Ibn Qutayba and Sāmīn give waniya yanī and related forms; Zajjāj adds tawānā. Ṭabarī, Wāḥidī, Jishumī, Māwardī and Qurṭubī cite al-ʿAjjāj's line of Muḥammad not flagging; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī cite horse poetry, and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ another poetic line. Abū Ḥayyān and Sāmīn gloss al-wānī as one who slackens. Ibn Zayd adds inattentive or negligent; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī include fatigue in people and animals. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes this root and form occur only here in the Qur'an. [53 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL(ابن مسعود); YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(العجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي,محمد بن كعب); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس,السدي,محمد بن كعب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,امرؤ القيس,قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد عن ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:42/q09/p2 Do not delay or be slow. — Ibn ʿAbbās gives this gloss in several transmissions. Ibn ʿAṭiyya combines weakening and delay; Itqān equates not slackening with not being slow. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(علي ابن أبي طلحة عن ابن عباس); ITQAN]
- 20:42/q09/p3 Do not cease. — Abān cites Ṭarafa's line about cauldrons that never stop boiling. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبان); QURTUBI-FULL(أبان)]

## 20:42/q10 How does the verb ونى work grammatically and morphologically?  [تَنِيَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the poetic example requires a defective verb or permits the ordinary intransitive verb with an omitted preposition.
- 20:42/q10/p1 It is normally intransitive; a prepositional complement such as في, عن or من specifies its sphere. — Abū Ḥayyān lists min or fī; Sāmīn says the verse uses fī and also mentions ʿan; Ālūsī lists fī or ʿan. Sāmīn gives ونى يني ونيا on the pattern وعد يعد وعدا. Abū Ḥayyān and Sāmīn note anāh for a woman slow to rise, originally wanāh with irregular hamza for wāw; Sāmīn also cites a hadith praising clemency and deliberateness. [9 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:42/q10/p2 It can act as a defective verb like زال under negation, meaning continue or not cease. — Some Baghdadi grammarians and Ibn Mālik adopt the analysis; Abū Ḥayyān cites a lover poem, Sāmīn gives ما ونى زيد قائما, and explains Ibn Mālik's لا يني with ḥubb as love or lover according to vowel. Ālūsī mentions the lexical explanation لا يني يفعل, 'keeps doing'. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعض البغداديين); SAMIN-DURR(ابن مالك); ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 20:42/q10/p3 The poem need not make ونى defective: a preposition has been omitted. — Sāmīn reports the opponents' analysis: love does not slacken in a lover's character, with fī or ʿan understood. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 20:42/q11 How is ولا تنيا read?  [وَلَا تَنِيَا · readings]
turns on: The reported variations concern the prefix vowel or an alternate codex wording; Jishumī distinguishes the common transmitted reading from Ibn Masʿūd's report.
- 20:42/q11/p1 The widespread reading is لا تنيا with the usual tāʾ vowel. — Jishumī accepts only the widely transmitted reading against Ibn Masʿūd's reported variant. [1 notes: JISHUMI(ابن مسعود)+]
- 20:42/q11/p2 The tāʾ of تنيا is read with kasra. — Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Sāmīn and Ālūsī report it; Abū Ḥayyān and Sāmīn name Ibn or Yaḥyā ibn Waththāb and explain vowel assimilation to the following nūn. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قُرئ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن وثاب); SAMIN-DURR(يحيى بن وثاب); ALUSI-FULL(ابن وثاب)]
- 20:42/q11/p3 Ibn Masʿūd or ʿAbd Allāh's codex reads ولا تهنا. — The reported wording means do not weaken, soften or yield, from hayyin and layyin. Qurṭubī also associates it with praise, glorification and conveying the message. Jishumī rejects its validity as a reading because it lacks wide transmission. [5 notes: JISHUMI(ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله); ALUSI-FULL(ابن وثاب)]

## 20:42/q12 How is the first-person yāʾ in ذكري read?  [ذِكْرِى · readings]
turns on: The named readers differ on opening or stilling the first-person yāʾ; their other exceptions belong to broader reading lists.
- 20:42/q12/p1 The yāʾ is opened with fatḥa. — Ibn Mujāhid's lists have Nāfiʿ and Abū ʿAmr opening the relevant yāʾ; he distinguishes their exceptions at ولي فيها, أخي اشدد and حشرتني أعمى. Ibn Kathīr opens the remaining listed yāʾs while using sukūn in specified other phrases including لذكري إن and على عيني إذ. Baghawī names Abū ʿAmr and Hijaz readers and compares لنفسي and 25:30, 61:6, where Abū Bakr agrees on the latter. Nashr names Nāfiʿ, Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr and Abū Jaʿfar, the two Medinese among its five listed yāʾs (20:10, 12, 14, 41, 42). [6 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو,ابن كثير,نافع); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عمرو وأهل الحجاز); NASHR(المدنيان وابن كثير وأبو عمرو)]
- 20:42/q12/p2 The yāʾ is quiescent with sukūn. — Ibn Mujāhid lists Ḥamza, Kisāʾī, Ibn ʿĀmir and ʿĀṣim through Abū Bakr as using sukūn on all his listed yāʾs; Baghawī says the remaining readers use sukūn. Sāmīn notes the quiescent yāʾ alongside Ibn Waththāb's kasra on the tāʾ. [3 notes: IBNMUJAHID(حمزة، الكسائي، ابن عامر، عاصم عن أبي بكر); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عمرو وأهل الحجاز); SAMIN-DURR(يحيى بن وثاب)]

## 20:42/q13 What is meant by My remembrance here?  [فِى ذِكْرِى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether remembrance denotes their own sustained recollection and worship, the public mission and its message, or particular subjects of that message.
- 20:42/q13/p1 Continual remembrance of God by heart and tongue, including praise, glorification and awareness of Him wherever they go. — Ṭabarī links it to God's blessings; al-Farrāʾ equates fī and min and calls for gratitude for divine kindness. Zamakhsharī and Bayḍāwī say not to forget God anywhere. Qurṭubī reports Ibn Masʿūd's praise and glorification. Bursevī asks for heart and tongue in every state; Ālūsī includes divine attributes and worthy acts while carrying the message. Rāzī says majesty and gratitude strengthen obedience; Ibn Kathīr relates a hadith of one remembering God while facing an opponent. [18 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(غير مسمى); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:42/q13/p2 Conveying God's message and carrying out His mission to Pharaoh. — Muqātil and Ibn Masʿūd apply the prohibition to delivering the message. Qatāda and Ibn ʿAbbās refer to God's mission; Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī say dhikr can cover worship generally and message delivery is its greatest form. Biqāʿī makes it the whole mission. Ṭabāṭabāʾī expressly excludes merely inward or verbal recollection in this context. [21 notes: MUQATIL(ابن مسعود); YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس,السدي,محمد بن كعب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(غير مسمى); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:42/q13/p3 Call people to God and proclaim His oneness. — Al-Ḥasan gives calling and conveying; Damghānī lists divine oneness as a meaning of dhikr with 43:36. Bayḍāwī says proclaim remembrance publicly. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads a call to belief in God alone, and Bursevī reports the Jelveti master's advice to begin exhortation with oneness. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); DAMGHANI; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI; BURSEVI(حضرة الهدايي)]
- 20:42/q13/p4 Observe religious duties, glorify God and declare His oneness. — Ibn al-Jawzī gives this as the alternative to the mission to Pharaoh; it extends remembrance across worship. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 20:42/q13/p5 Remind Pharaoh and his people of God's rejection of unbelief and warn of reward and punishment. — Rāzī reports this as a distinct interpretation directed at the audience of the mission. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(غير مسمى)]
- 20:42/q13/p6 Recount God's favors and kindnesses to Pharaoh. — Rāzī reports this as a fourth interpretation. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(غير مسمى)]
- 20:42/q13/p7 Prayer together with preaching. — Iṣlāḥī connects prayer with 20:14: prayer draws strength from God for true preaching; losing that connection drains the mission or turns it into Satan's tool. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:42/q14 What does sustained remembrance accomplish for the mission?  [فِى ذِكْرِى · interpretation]
- 20:42/q14/p1 It strengthens resolve, overcomes fear and makes the mission effective. — Ṭabarī says recalling God's countless blessings steadies them. Muqātil says it emboldens them against Pharaoh. Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Nasafī and Ālūsī describe divine aid; awareness of majesty makes others seem small, while gratitude supports obedience. Ibn Kathīr cites a hadith of a servant remembering God while facing an opponent. Biqāʿī urges full attention; Bursevī says neglect of other affairs follows neglect of remembrance. Iṣlāḥī makes prayer the source of true preaching. Bursevī's Jelveti report says beginning with oneness makes listeners attentive and admonition effective. [12 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(غير مسمى); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم,حضرة الهدايي); ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:42/q15 Why is Aaron's partnership useful to Moses' mission and remembrance?  [أَنتَ وَأَخُوكَ · interpretation]
- 20:42/q15/p1 Working together multiplies the call, helps withstand Pharaoh and frees more time for preaching and remembrance. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says Moses expected Pharaoh's tyranny to obstruct the people and try the preachers. Aaron's appointment spurs him to preach; their teaching of God's attributes, commands, faith and piety multiplies remembrance. Sharing daily necessities saves time for the mission. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:42/q16 How does this command answer Moses' earlier prayer?  [فِى ذِكْرِى · links]
- 20:42/q16/p1 It fulfills Moses' request that the brothers glorify and remember God abundantly. — Biqāʿī says Moses made remembrance the goal of his prayer (20:34); Bursevī cites 20:33–34. Ibn ʿĀshūr says delivering divine commands and prohibitions increases the glorification Moses sought. Study Quran's spiritual reading also cites the earlier prayer. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(spiritual interpretation)]

## 20:42/q17 What inward warning is drawn from remembrance?  [فِى ذِكْرِى · inward]
turns on: The inward readings stress either distracted verbal invocation or absorption in divine work at the expense of witnessing.
- 20:42/q17/p1 Abundant spoken remembrance while the heart is inattentive fails the command. — Tustarī and Sahl warn against tiring the tongue while neglecting the heart's watchfulness; Study Quran reports the warning against invoking God without attention to the One invoked, citing 20:33–34. [3 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل); STUDYQURAN(spiritual interpretation)]
- 20:42/q17/p2 Service to God must not displace direct awareness of Him. — Bursevī reports al-ʿArāʾis: being occupied with God's work can make a servant absent from divine witnessing and fatigued away from God. [1 notes: BURSEVI(العرائس)]
