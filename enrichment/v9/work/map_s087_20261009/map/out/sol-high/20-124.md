# 20:124: 17 questions

## 20:124/q01 What does turning away from My remembrance mean?  [أَعْرَضَ ذِكْرِى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the turning is explained as rejection of revelation or as neglect of inward remembrance.
- 20:124/q01/p1 Rejecting or neglecting God's revelation, especially the Qur'an, by neither believing in nor following it. — Muqatil says refusal to believe; Yahya adds failure to follow guidance; Tabari adds refusal to respond, take heed, and desist; Tabrisi includes refusal to consider revealed proofs; Ibn Kathir includes turning to other guidance and neglecting recitation, retention, and care of the Qur'an, though the latter is only part of the intent; Kuranyolu includes denial and disregard of admonition. [12 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler)]
- 20:124/q01/p2 Turning from inward remembrance toward desire and the lower world. — Sulami says it darkens time and unsettles provision; Ja'far says true knowledge of God would prevent it; al-Wasiti distinguishes one's own remembrance from God's true remembrance; Qushayri traces failure, scattered hearts, whispers, loneliness, and harmful companionship to neglect of remembrance; Qashani and Alusi trace attachment to goods and miserliness to this turn. [15 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي,جعفر); QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL(بعض المتصوفة); NASAFI-FULL(بعض المتصوفة); QASHANI(بعض الصوفية); ALUSI-FULL(some interpreters); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 20:124/q02 What does “My remembrance” refer to?  [ذِكْرِى · referent]
turns on: How broad dhikr is: a specific scripture or admonition, all guidance and proof, or remembrance itself.
- 20:124/q02/p1 The Qur'an. — Al-Kalbi, Ibn al-Sa'ib, and others specify the book that one neither believes nor follows; Itqan lists this sense of dhikr; Tabatabai also allows all revealed books. [10 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب); NASAFI-FULL; ITQAN; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:124/q02/p2 God's admonition. — Ibn Abbas and Ata give the gloss; Wajiz identifies the admonition with the Qur'an. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطاء); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler)]
- 20:124/q02/p3 God's guidance, revealed proof, and call to worship, including scriptures beyond the Qur'an. — Baydawi and Alusi say guidance produces remembrance of God; Razi and Abu Hayyan include other revealed books; Qurtubi also includes religion, recitation, deeds, proofs, and the Messenger; Bursevi includes the messenger; Tabatabai includes the true call and Alusi prefers the general sense. [12 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:124/q02/p4 The act of remembering God, including monotheistic recognition and remembrance in the heart. — Ibn al-Jawzi's lexical collection cites 43:36 for monotheistic recognition; Tabatabai explicitly allows the act of remembering; Qushayri and the inward commentators stress continual remembrance. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); TABATABAI]
- 20:124/q02/p5 The Prophet's household's wilaya is an application of this reminder. — Tabatabai calls reports applying the phrase to wilaya examples of Qur'anic application rather than lexical explanation. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:124/q03 What does “a constricted life” mean, and where does it occur?  [مَعِيشَةًۭ ضَنكًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: The period meant by ma'isha, whether outward provision or inward experience determines its narrowness, and the strength of grave reports against the sequence with 20:127.
- 20:124/q03/p1 Narrow, difficult, severe or miserable living; ḍank denotes constriction in Arabic. — Tabari, Tha'labi, Wahidi, Mawardi, Ibn Atiyya, and others cite Antara's cramped dwelling; Ibn Abbas and other poets illustrate severe hardship and a tight pass; the root describes narrowness, including a fleshy woman whose skin is tight; ḍīq covers physical, emotional, and punitive distress, while Bint al-Shati judges ḍank more intense and notes its sole Qur'anic use here. Mujahid also glosses ma'isha as provision; al-Raghib defines ma'isha as what sustains animal life. [57 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,عنترة,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(ابن عباس,عنترة,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,ابن عباس); JISHUMI(مجاهد، وقتادة، وأبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(عنترة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,مجاهد); ITQAN(غير مسمى); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(عنترة); TABATABAI(الراغب); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس,الفراء); STUDYQURAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ+]
- 20:124/q03/p2 Worldly inner distress, greed, anxiety, and loss of contentment even amid wealth. — Tabari and Ibn Abbas stress fear that God will not replace expenditure; Said ibn Jubayr says contentment is removed; Kashshaf and Qurtubi contrast submission, trust, and 16:97 with greed; Baydawi cites 2:61, 5:66, 7:96 for provision; Ibn Kathir and Islahi distinguish outward luxury from inward doubt; Biqai cites the gold-valley report and reports on worldly versus afterlife concerns; Tabatabai rejects comparing rich with poor, comparing believers with forgetful unbelievers; Alusi conditionally prefers this if grave reports fail. [55 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون,ابن عباس); THALABI(سعيد بن جبير,عطية عن ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); TABRISI(ابن عباس,مجاهد، قتادة، الجبائي); RAZI-FULL(جمع من المفسرين,عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(ابن جبير); QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جبير,الجوهري,عطاء); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(أنس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(some interpreters,ابن عباس,بعضهم,عطاء وابن جبير); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(al-Zamakhsharī); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(العوفي عن ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL+; TAB-FULL-]
- 20:124/q03/p3 Worldly life spent in disobedience or sustained by unlawful earnings. — Muqatil calls it evil because of disobedience; Ikrima, Qays, and Dahhak specify forbidden provision and deeds; Ibn Abbas says lawful avenues close; Malik ibn Dinar says it is eaten until death then punished; Alusi says the worldly cause can be named for its Judgment Day effect. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(آخرون,الضحاك,عكرمة,قيس بن أبي حازم); THALABI(الضحاك,عكرمة,قيس بن أبي حازم,مقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن,الضحاك,عكرمة); TABRISI(عكرمة، الضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فرقة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الضحاك، عكرمة، مالك بن دينار); DURR-FULL(الضحاك,عكرمة,مالك بن دينار); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 20:124/q03/p4 Punishment and pressure in the grave or intermediate state. — Prophetic and Companion reports identify the grave; Abu Said, Abu Hurayra, and Ibn Abbas describe displaced or interlocking ribs, while reports differ over ninety-nine dragons or snakes, seven heads, and their effect. Yahya and Durr contrast believer's protected, lit, expansive grave and correct answers with the unbeliever's punishment, citing 14:27. Tabari prefers this because 20:127 makes the hereafter more severe and worldly unbelievers may prosper; Wahidi agrees. Qurtubi calls it correct. Ibn Kathir grades one Abu Hurayra chain good but calls another raised version very munkar and prefers a stopped Abu Said version. Alusi follows sound grave reports conditionally. - 20:124/q03/p5 Punishment and harsh food in Hell. — Hasan, Qatada, Ibn Zayd and others name fiery thorn, zaqqum, ghislin, and dari'; Razi and Abu Hayyan add painful drink and a condition neither death nor life. Ibn Zayd cites 89:24 to argue true life is only the afterlife. Zajjaj favors this setting; Tabari argues 20:127 places the severe afterlife after the narrow life; Tabatabai objects that the first clause is unrestricted while the gathering is specifically dated. - 20:124/q03/p6 A condition spanning this world, the grave, and the Fire. — Qushayri makes unbelief's loneliness and the closing of affairs its inward expression in every stage; Tabatabai permits grave punishment as one instance of a broad first clause; Ibn al-Jawzi lists the three settings without adopting one. - 20:124/q03/p7 An inward life cut off from guidance, good, and divine intimacy. — Ibn Abbas says doors of good close; Shibli calls being left to oneself the worst affliction; Asad describes sterility and lack of purpose; Bursevi's Persian couplet calls remembrance the soul's key and saints' food. ## 20:124/q04 How does ḍank agree with the feminine maʿīsha?  [مَعِيشَةًۭ ضَنكًۭا · grammar]
- 20:124/q04/p1 Ḍank is a verbal noun used adjectivally and therefore stays the same for feminine and masculine, singular and plural. — Tabari and Tha'labi specify singular, dual and plural; Wahidi derives it from the verbal noun; Jishumi calls it indeclinable; Razi paraphrases 'a life possessing constriction'; Ibn Ashur derives it from ḍanuka; Alusi notes ma'isha retains its original feminine gender. [19 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الفراء)]

## 20:124/q05 Why use the feminine maʿīsha and the form ḍank here?  [مَعِيشَةًۭ ضَنكًۭا · rhetoric]
- 20:124/q05/p1 The feminine maʿīsha belittles this life, while the invariant verbal noun ḍank intensifies its harshness. — Biqai gives this contrast; Bint al-Shati distinguishes the more intense bottleneck and choking quality of ḍank from the wider uses of ḍīq. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 20:124/q06 How is ḍank read?  [ضَنكًۭا · readings]
turns on: Whether the final alif is a feminine form or a pause-like realization of tanwin.
- 20:124/q06/p1 Ḍankan with tanwin and an inflected ending. — Samin calls this the general reading; tanwin becomes alif at pause. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن)]
- 20:124/q06/p2 Ḍankā or ḍankī with a final feminine alif, without tanwin. — Kashshaf, Qurtubi and Baydawi report the fa'la/sukra pattern; Abu Hayyan and Alusi attribute imala to Hasan; Samin allows either a feminine verbal-noun form or a pause-like replacement of tanwin during connected recitation. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); SAMIN-DURR(فرقة); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]

## 20:124/q07 About whom was this warning revealed?  [وَمَنْ أَعْرَضَ · reports]
- 20:124/q07/p1 Al-Aswad ibn Abd al-Asad al-Makhzumi. — Muqatil calls him al-Aswad ibn Abd al-Aswad and says Hamza killed him at Badr by the watering place; reports from Ibn Abbas connect him with the grave's crushing of the ribs. [4 notes: MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 20:124/q08 How is the gathering verb read?  [وَنَحْشُرُهُۥ · readings]
turns on: Person of the verb, mood or lightening of rāʾ, and treatment of the pronoun hāʾ in connected recitation.
- 20:124/q08/p1 Naḥshuruhu with first-person nūn and indicative rāʾ. — Samin calls this the general reading and a new sentence; Ibn Atiyya contrasts it with yāʾ. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(العامة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]
- 20:124/q08/p2 Naḥshurhu with a quiescent rāʾ, jussive or lightened indicative. — Aban ibn Taghlib reads it thus; Ibn Jinni, Kashshaf and Tabrisi coordinate it with the syntactic position of the conditional answer, comparing the jussive of 63:10; Samin also permits mere lightening and cites 7:186 and 2:67. [8 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(أبان بن تغلب); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(أبان بن تغلب); SAMIN-DURR(أبان ابن تغلب); ALUSI-FULL(أبان بن تغلب)]
- 20:124/q08/p3 Yaḥshuruhu with third-person yāʾ. — Samin gives God or the angel as the understood subject; Ibn Atiyya and Alusi report the variant. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); SAMIN-DURR(فرقة); ALUSI-FULL(فرقة)]
- 20:124/q08/p4 Naḥshurh with a quiescent pronoun hāʾ in connected speech. — Kashshaf calls it pause-like; Samin and Alusi attribute it to Aban and explain it by Banu Uqayl and Banu Kilab usage or treating connection as pause. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبان بن تغلب); ALUSI-FULL(أبان بن تغلب)]

## 20:124/q09 How does the conditional construction govern the gathering verb?  [وَمَنْ فَإِنَّ لَهُۥ وَنَحْشُرُهُۥ · grammar]
- 20:124/q09/p1 The jussive gathering verb may coordinate with the jussive syntactic position of the conditional answer. — Ibn Jinni cites Aban's reading and 63:10; Tabrisi and Kashshaf explain the same construction. Razi's cited Imru al-Qays line illustrates that coordinated verbs need not share an object when that would make the conditional self-contradictory. [7 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(أبان بن تغلب); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR(أبان ابن تغلب); BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]

## 20:124/q10 Does ḥashr here mean resurrection or gathering?  [وَنَحْشُرُهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Which sense of ḥashr is intended in this setting.
- 20:124/q10/p1 Resurrection. — Bahr al-Ulum says ḥashr can have either sense but this is intended here. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بحر العلوم)]
- 20:124/q10/p2 Gathering. — Bahr al-Ulum acknowledges this alternate lexical sense. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بحر العلوم)]

## 20:124/q11 How is aʿmā read here and in the following verse?  [أَعْمَىٰ · readings]
turns on: Transmission routes and whether the word is a verse ending, with authorities differing in both-place versus first-place inclination.
- 20:124/q11/p1 Both occurrences with fatḥ or open vowels. — Hafs from Asim, Ibn Kathir and Ibn Amir are listed; Farisi also lists Abu Amr with fatḥ; Ibn al-Jawzi describes fatḥ on both mīms. Farisi accepts both imala and its absence. [4 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(حفص عن عاصم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وحفص عن عاصم); NASHR(أبو عمرو)]
- 20:124/q11/p2 Both occurrences with imāla or inclined vowels. — Abu Bakr from Asim, Hamza, Kisai, and Khalaf are listed; Alusi grounds the inclination in the defective-ya form; Nashr details Abu Bakr routes and exceptional transmissions. [6 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو بكر عن عاصم وحمزة والكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(حمزة والكسائي وأبو بكر عن عاصم); NASHR(أبو بكر); ALUSI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وخلف)]
- 20:124/q11/p3 A pronunciation between fatḥ and imāla. — Nafi is reported between the two in both places; Nashr distinguishes Warsh routes for non-final forms written with ya. [3 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(حفص عن عاصم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(نافع); NASHR(الأزرق عن ورش)]
- 20:124/q11/p4 Imāla at the first occurrence only. — Nashr names Abu Amr and Yaqub on some routes; Alusi says Abu Amr treats the first as a changeable verse ending and stopping point. [2 notes: NASHR(أبو بكر); ALUSI-FULL(أبو عمرو)]

## 20:124/q12 What does being gathered blind mean?  [أَعْمَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether aʿmā names lost eyesight, lack of a case before God, loss of guidance, or the continuation of an inward state; 20:125 and other resurrection descriptions are key evidence.
- 20:124/q12/p1 Blind in physical sight. — Ibn Abbas and others read it literally; Tabarsi cites the plain sense and 20:125, while Wahidi notes an unfinished appeal to the next passage. Ibn Atiyya says prior blindness of insight would not explain fear of being raised blind; Nasafi, Bursevi and Alusi cite descriptions of blind gathering in 17:97. Zajjaj and Ibn Atiyya compare 20:102's blue-eyed criminals; Ibn Atiyya separates groups or scenes. [22 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(غيره,قيل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; TABRISI+]
- 20:124/q12/p2 Blind to proof, argument, or insight. — Mujahid, Muqatil and others say no argument remains; Yahya cites 23:117 and Zajjaj cites 6:149, since God warned through messengers; Abu Ishaq distinguishes having no proof from being unable to see one. Alusi cites 22:46 and 20:125 for the heart's blindness; Razi replies to the Qadi that ignorant souls stay ignorant after death, painfully. Ibn Atiyya and the Qadi object on different grounds. ## 20:124/q14 When is sight lost or regained in the resurrection sequence?  [وَنَحْشُرُهُۥ أَعْمَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: How 20:125 and 19:38 fit a physically blind gathering.
- 20:124/q14/p1 The person emerges from the grave sighted and becomes blind on the way to the gathering. — Farra and reports from Ibn Abbas say this; Razi cites 17:97 and the blue-eyed description; Alusi says 20:125 can refer to sight at resurrection's beginning. [8 notes: FARRA; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 20:124/q14/p2 Blindness occurs in one scene, but sight is restored in another. — Mawardi and Qurtubi allow shifting states; Alusi cites 19:38, 'How clearly they hear and see,' and says blindness later ends; Biqai uses that verse to favor literal blindness of only some misguided people; Study Quran notes they can see rejected realities clearly. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة); STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL+]

## 20:124/q15 Why does turning from remembrance lead to blindness and constricted life?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the clauses describe distinct periods or broad consequences, and how 20:126-127 order those periods.
- 20:124/q15/p1 The punishment mirrors abandoned signs and guidance. — Kashshaf cites 17:97; Tabatabai says the wording replaces 'does not follow My guidance' to expose the cause and prepare 20:126's forgetting; Qushayri says people are repaid by their deeds and should heed the signs; Asad states the paired consequence. Biqai cites a sound report that wrongdoing becomes darkness on Judgment Day. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 20:124/q15/p2 The verse pairs worldly hardship with the later gathering, while the grave can be attached to worldly life. — Razi states the worldly reading's sequence; Tabatabai calls barzakh an appendage of worldly life; Alusi says the resurrection clause favors worldly hardship if grave reports are not sound; Ibn Atiyya instead regards narrow life and blindness as afterlife punishments and invokes 20:127. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 20:124/q15/p3 The later 20:127's more severe afterlife punishment places the constricted life before it. — Tabari and Wahidi use this to favor the grave; Tabari explicitly excludes Hell. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 20:124/q16 What do these verses teach about human acts, decree, and delayed judgment?  [* · theology]
turns on: How responsibility, divine decree, and respite are understood from the warning.
- 20:124/q16/p1 Human acts originate from human agents, against compulsion. — Jishumi draws this lesson from the passage. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 20:124/q16/p2 Happiness and misery are decreed, and striving and effort belong to that decree. — Qushayri affirms God's comprehensive prior knowledge together with human effort. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 20:124/q16/p3 God delays this community's punishment for a fixed term while future saints remain among its descendants. — Qushayri says delay does not mean neglect. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 20:124/q17 What does the verse imply about reciting the Qur'an for gain?  [ذِكْرِى · law]
- 20:124/q17/p1 Using Qur'an recitation to solicit payment or favor is blameworthy. — Itqan says earning through recitation is disliked; an ungraded Prophetic report warns against asking people through recitation, and a report with a sound chain curses reciting to gain favor before an unjust ruler ten times per letter. [3 notes: ITQAN(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
