# 16:96: 25 questions

## 16:96/q01 How does mā function in 'what you have'?  [مَا عِندَكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the question concerns the verse's clause syntax or the range of noun uses of mā.
- 16:96/q01/p1 It is a relative noun, 'that which,' with the following clause as its content. — Abū Zakariyyā gives this analysis; Ibn Qutayba contrasts mā for things with man for people. Al-Burhān says relative mā does not inflect for gender or number; its referent controls translation. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أبو زكريا,ابن قتيبة); BURHAN]
- 16:96/q01/p2 The two mā phrases are subjects; yanfadu and bāqin are their respective predicates. — Al-Naḥḥās gives each subject–predicate pair, and al-Ṭabrisī adds that the plural pronoun follows man according to sense. [4 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR]
- 16:96/q01/p3 As a noun, mā can also be interrogative, conditional, or an indefinite noun qualified by what follows. — Abū Zakariyyā lists these wider grammatical possibilities, without choosing them for the verse. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أبو زكريا)]

## 16:96/q02 Whom does 'what you have' address?  [عِندَكُمْ · referent]
- 16:96/q02/p1 It addresses people generally, including those who promise and those offered promises. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the statement proverb-like: even the warned believers, who possess nothing, are included by the general address. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 16:96/q03 What do 'what you have' and 'what is with God' denote?  [مَا عِندَكُمْ وَمَا عِندَ ٱللَّهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the contrast concerns material goods, deeds and recompense, or human and divine spiritual qualities.
- 16:96/q03/p1 People's worldly possessions or enjoyment perish; God's lasting reward, afterlife, or Paradise endures. — Muqātil mentions wealth and afterlife reward; Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes an owner may die before goods expire. Al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr and Ibn Jubayr specify Garden or Hereafter reward; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Kashshāf and Bursevī speak of God's inexhaustible stores of mercy. Al-Rāzī says sense proves worldly loss and reason the afterlife's duration. Al-Qurṭubī cites poems comparing wealth to vanishing shade and warning that sins remain. [31 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن جبير); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 16:96/q03/p2 What is with God includes mercies in this world as well as the next. — Al-Ālūsī names both stores of mercy and says worldly blessings become lasting good deeds through their connection to the afterlife. Ibn ʿĀshūr says God's worldly promise already surpasses the polytheists' offer, while His afterlife gift exceeds all else; he cites 16:97. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 16:96/q03/p3 Human acts of obedience end, but God's recompense for them remains. — Al-Sulamī's first report contrasts passing acts with everlasting recompense; his second says human obedience is flawed while divine recompense endures. Al-Māwardī offers this as an alternative to world and afterlife. [3 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); MAWARDI-FULL]
- 16:96/q03/p4 Human attributes and originated deeds perish; divine attributes or spiritual states endure. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ asks the servant to relinquish self-attributes and abide with God. Jaʿfar identifies people's side with obligatory and extra deeds, God's side with His pre-eternal attributes. [2 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,جعفر)]
- 16:96/q03/p5 Human gnoses and beloved experiences pass in successive forms, although their effects remain; God's mercy, love, and constancy are eternal. — Al-Qushayrī's reported formulations distinguish changing created experiences and enduring divine qualities. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال)]
- 16:96/q03/p6 Human longing to meet God may end, whereas God's welcome and favor never do. — Al-Qushayrī quotes poetry in which the righteous long for God and God longs still more to meet them. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال)]

## 16:96/q04 What does yanfadu mean?  [يَنفَدُ · meaning]
- 16:96/q04/p1 It means to run out, be depleted, perish, or go away. — Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī cite anfada for provisions running out and nāfada for exhausting an opponent's proof; al-Jishumī cites a hadith, while al-Ṭabrisī notes another transmission. Al-Samīn distinguishes نفد with dāl from نفذ with dotted dhāl; al-Ālūsī says the latter means pass through with a different vowel pattern. [9 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 16:96/q05 What does bāqin mean?  [بَاقٍۢ · meaning]
- 16:96/q05/p1 It means enduring or continuing to exist, opposite to perishing. — Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī allow uninterrupted existence or persistence simply; Ibn ʿĀshūr defines baqāʾ as not ceasing. Al-Dāmghānī cites 28:60 as another use. [5 notes: DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 16:96/q06 How is bāqin read at a pause?  [بَاقٍۢ · readings]
turns on: Whether the original final yāʾ is retained at pause for form or dropped for ease.
- 16:96/q06/p1 Ibn Kathīr and Yaʿqūb retain the final yāʾ at a pause; connected speech has tanwīn. — Al-Jishumī says yāʾ retention follows the original form. The Yz witness also writes bāqī explicitly without changing the meaning. [3 notes: JISHUMI(ابن كثير ويعقوب); QIRAAT-ER(Yz)]
- 16:96/q06/p2 Other readers drop the yāʾ at a pause; connected speech has tanwīn. — Al-Jishumī says the omission eases pronunciation; al-Samīn points to earlier discussion under Sūrat al-Raʿd. [3 notes: JISHUMI(ابن كثير ويعقوب); SAMIN-DURR]

## 16:96/q07 Does Paradise's bliss end?  [وَمَا عِندَ ٱللَّهِ بَاقٍۢ · theology]
turns on: Whether 'what is with God remains' includes the continued existence of Paradise's pleasures.
- 16:96/q07/p1 No; God's reward and Paradise's bliss endure without end. — Ibn ʿAbbās says what is consumed is replaced, directly refuting an end to Paradise's pleasures. Al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī and al-Ālūsī use the verse against Jahm or the Jahmiyya. Al-Bayḍāwī and Ibn Kathīr state its enduring provision. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(جهم بن صفوان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(الجهمية); ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 16:96/q07/p2 Yes; Paradise's bliss eventually ends. — Attributed to Jahm ibn Ṣafwān by al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī, and to the Jahmiyya by Bursevī; each invokes the verse against it. [0 notes: RAZI-FULL(جهم بن صفوان)-; BURSEVI(الجهمية)-; ALUSI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 16:96/q08 Does a persistent thing need a distinct attribute of persistence?  [بَاقٍۢ · theology]
turns on: Whether persistence is bare continued existence or an added quality.
- 16:96/q08/p1 No; persistence is continued existence itself. — Abū ʿAlī and Abū Hāshim say no added quality is required; al-Ṭabrisī attributes the same answer to most theologians. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي وأبو هاشم); TABRISI(الأكثرون)]
- 16:96/q08/p2 Yes; a thing persists through a distinct attribute of baqāʾ. — Al-Jishumī attributes this to Abū al-Qāsim; al-Ṭabrisī attributes it to al-Balkhī. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أبو القاسم); TABRISI(البلخي)]

## 16:96/q09 Does substance persist?  [بَاقٍۢ · theology]
turns on: Whether persistence applies to all substances or only some.
- 16:96/q09/p1 Substance persists, and in one formulation all substance does. — Al-Jishumī reports the first from most exegetes and the universal claim from the Ḥashwiyya. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أكثر المفسرين,الحشوية)]
- 16:96/q09/p2 Some substance persists and some does not. — Al-Jishumī reports his teachers' position, adding that they differ over details. [1 notes: JISHUMI(مشايخنا)]
- 16:96/q09/p3 Not all substance persists. — Al-Jishumī reports Abū Hāshim's denial of universality. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو هاشم)]

## 16:96/q10 Why use the nominal form bāqin?  [بَاقٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 16:96/q10/p1 The noun form signals permanence more strongly than a present-tense verb. — Al-Ālūsī draws the implication from the wording. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 16:96/q11 How is the reward verb read?  [وَلَنَجْزِيَنَّ · readings]
turns on: Whether the promise turns to direct divine speech or continues reference to God in the third person.
- 16:96/q11/p1 With first-person plural nūn: 'We shall reward,' a majestic divine self-reference and shift to direct speech. — Ibn Kathīr and ʿĀṣim recur across the sources; some include Abū Jaʿfar, Ibn Dhakwān, or Yaʿqūb, and reports from Abū ʿAmr differ. Al-Fārisī says it has the same meaning as yāʾ and notes unanimous nūn in 16:97. [16 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وعاصم,علي بن نصر عن أبي عمرو); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر وابن كثير وعاصم); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جعفر وابن كثير وعاصم والباقون); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير وعاصم); TABRISI(أبو جعفر وابن كثير وعاصم وعياش عن أبي عمرو); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن كثير وعاصم); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن كثير، عاصم، ابن ذكوان); BIQAI-FULL(الجماعة، ابن كثير، عاصم); ALUSI-FULL(عاصم وابن كثير); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن كثير وعاصم وابن ذكوان عن ابن عمر وأبو جعفر); QIRAAT-ER(Imp)]
- 16:96/q11/p2 With third-person yāʾ: 'He shall reward,' referring back to God. — Al-Fārisī and al-Ṭabrisī connect the pronoun to 'what is with God remains'; Ibn ʿĀshūr links it to God as the one forbidding, promising, and rewarding. The majority or remaining readers have this form, with reported differences over named readers. [14 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(نافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي); JISHUMI(الباقون); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جعفر وابن كثير وعاصم والباقون); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الباقون); TABRISI(الباقون); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الباقون); BIQAI-FULL(الجماعة، ابن كثير، عاصم); ALUSI-FULL(عاصم وابن كثير); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); QIRAAT-ER(Imp)]

## 16:96/q12 What makes the reward promise emphatic?  [وَلَنَجْزِيَنَّ · rhetoric]
- 16:96/q12/p1 The oath lām and emphatic nūn make it a solemn divine promise. — Ibn Kathīr calls it God's oath. Ibn ʿĀshūr names both markers; al-Ālūsī says the oath-like repetition encourages covenant steadfastness. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 16:96/q13 Who are 'those who were patient,' and what did they endure?  [ٱلَّذِينَ صَبَرُوٓا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the patience names covenant fidelity and obedience, endurance of persecution, or the discipline underlying every duty.
- 16:96/q13/p1 They remain faithful to God and His covenant, obey commands, and avoid prohibitions despite hardship or temptation. — Muqātil, al-Baghawī and al-Ṭabrisī emphasize covenant keeping; Ibn ʿAṭiyya specifies desire for prohibited earnings. Ibn ʿAbbās mentions religion and prohibitions; al-Rāzī adds steadfast Islam and legal duties. Abū Ḥayyān includes lawful earning. Ṭabāṭabāʾī requires sincerity and covers obedience, abstention, and affliction. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 16:96/q13/p2 They endure unbelievers' persecution, poverty, deprivation, and the hardships of Islam. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Nasafī, al-Bayḍāwī and Bursevī mention abuse; al-Bayḍāwī alternatively names religious duties. Al-Ālūsī includes covenant keeping under threats of breach. Ibn ʿĀshūr ties patience to forgoing the polytheists' immediate offer. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 16:96/q13/p3 They bear adversity or persevere in truth despite opposition. — Asad describes adversity; Iṣlāḥī promises the best reward in this world and next for persistence in truth; Kuranyolu speaks of hardship. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 16:96/q13/p4 Patience is the inward discipline common to all duties. — Abū Ḥayyān says every obligation needs it and treats it as the root of obligations; al-Ālūsī reports this view. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]

## 16:96/q14 What special reward is promised for patience?  [صَبَرُوٓا۟ أَجْرَهُم · interpretation]
- 16:96/q14/p1 Patient people receive divine recompense, with their bad deeds overlooked or forgiven. — Muqātil and al-Ṭabarī mention pardon; Ibn Kathīr speaks of overlooking worse deeds. Ibn Jubayr says best worldly deeds are rewarded and bad deeds forgiven. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says 39:10 promises reward without reckoning over deficiencies. [6 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); TABATABAI]
- 16:96/q14/p2 The reward includes God's contentment and the desired spiritual outcome. — Al-Sulamī transmits Abū ʿUthmān's claim that God grants contentment, with best reward in both lives. Al-Qushayrī says outcomes vary: hardship for God receives immense recompense (39:10), restraining desire receives the chamber (25:75), and acceptance of decree receives divine companionship (2:153). [5 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان); QUSHAYRI]
- 16:96/q14/p3 Patience leads to ample reward and divine presence and love. — Study Quran cites 2:177, 33:35, 2:153, 2:249, 8:46, 3:146, 25:75, 28:54, and 39:10 for patience as integral to piety and abundantly rewarded. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 16:96/q15 What does ajr mean here?  [أَجْرَهُم · meaning]
turns on: Whether ajr is used for divine recompense here or in its wider lexical senses.
- 16:96/q15/p1 It is reward or recompense for obedience and patience. — Al-Dāmghānī also glosses the Prophet's reward as thawāb at 10:72; Ibn al-Jawzī lists reward for obedience as a Qurʾānic sense. Al-Ālūsī specifies reward corresponding to this patience. [3 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); ALUSI-FULL]
- 16:96/q15/p2 The root also denotes a contracted wage, setting a bone, and certain roof terms. — Ibn al-Jawzī explains a wage for use of benefits or an act; he also lists setting a bone, ijār for an unenclosed roof, plurals ajājīr and ajājirah, and variant injār. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 16:96/q16 How does ajrahum function grammatically?  [أَجْرَهُم · grammar]
turns on: Whether jazā is parsed as taking one direct object here or as a two-object verb of giving.
- 16:96/q16/p1 It is the direct object of the rewarding verb. — Al-Ālūsī calls it the direct object of lanajziyanna. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 16:96/q16/p2 It is the second object of yujziyan when that verb means 'to give.' — Ibn ʿĀshūr analyzes the two-object construction. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 16:96/q17 What does the bāʾ in bi-aḥsani express?  [بِأَحْسَنِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the bāʾ denotes the basis of exchange or the cause of reward.
- 16:96/q17/p1 It marks recompense or equivalence: reward in exchange for deeds. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares 'I sold this for that.' Ibn ʿAṭiyya says reward is in proportion to the best works. [2 notes: TABATABAI; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 16:96/q17/p2 It marks cause: reward because of the exceptionally good deed. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links it to remaining Muslim under persecution and cites 12:33. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 16:96/q18 What does 'the best of what they used to do' mean for the reward?  [بِأَحْسَنِ مَا كَانُوا۟ يَعْمَلُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'best' identifies a class of deeds, a comparative scale of reward, patience itself, or an emphatic way to promise full recompense.
- 16:96/q18/p1 It refers to obedient acts, especially obligations and recommended works, rather than merely permitted acts. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Rāzī and al-Bayḍāwī say merely permissible acts earn no promised reward. Al-Ṭabrisī takes the comparative as evidence that some good is better than other good. Al-Ālūsī reports an extension to avoiding prohibited and disliked acts, but records al-Irshād's objection that this constricts mercy; Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that permissible good acts are unrelated to this patience. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI(آخرون)-]
- 16:96/q18/p2 It is patience itself, the best act needed by every obligation. — Abū Ḥayyān prefers this because patience underlies all duties. Al-Ālūsī reports an implied genitive: reward for their patience. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 16:96/q18/p3 God gives every deed the reward of its best instance, elevating even lesser instances. — Al-Biqāʿī says divine generosity raises lesser deeds to the level of best deeds. Al-Ālūsī reports that even a lowest-ranked act gets the highest reward, absorbing momentary distress into beautiful patience. Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers best-instance recompense for each kind of deed, even if the performed act was not its best instance; he rejects sorting out and discarding merely good works. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 16:96/q18/p4 The reward is better than the deeds themselves, multiplied by God. — Al-Bayḍāwī offers this alternative; al-Ālūsī reports a parse with an omitted reward qualified by aḥsan and a comparative genitive. Kuranyolu also says reward exceeds the deeds. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 16:96/q18/p5 The best works are voluntary acts, chosen freely rather than imposed. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this reason. Al-Ālūsī adds that rewarding them would entail obligatory works, but calls the interpretation entirely unsound; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says obligations have greater merit. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI-]
- 16:96/q18/p6 Aḥsan keeps its comparative force: reward of the best implies reward of lesser good deeds. — Al-Samīn prefers this implication by agreement and rejects denial of comparison as a mistaken attempt to avoid it. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR+; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 16:96/q18/p7 Aḥsan means good without comparative force, or praises excellence without limiting reward. — Abū Ḥayyān reports the noncomparative reading. Bursevī calls it completeness of goodness, citing 3:148. Al-Ālūsī reports al-Irshād's view that the superlative encourages and praises reward, with a parallel in 16:89. Al-Samīn rejects a noncomparative analysis motivated by limiting reward. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(إرشاد العقل السليم); SAMIN-DURR-]
- 16:96/q18/p8 The mention of the best encourages action although reward covers both good and better deeds. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this distinct exhortative reading. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 16:96/q18/p9 Aḥsan intensifies the especially good act of remaining Muslim under persecution. — Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the superlative intensively, with causal bāʾ, and cites 12:33. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 16:96/q18/p10 Only each person's best deeds are rewarded, while merely good deeds are discarded. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī attributes and rejects this proposal because context, reward verses, and divine mercy oppose it. [0 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]

## 16:96/q19 What incident is reported around these verses?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in names, property claimed, timing, and what Imruʾ al-Qays did afterward.
- 16:96/q19/p1 A Kindah–Hadramawt land dispute led to a demanded oath and revelation as Imruʾ al-Qays prepared to swear. — Yaḥyā reports the claimant lacked proof, the Prophet ordered an oath and warned of God's anger for seizing Muslim property falsely; al-Ashʿath assigned the verses to the two men. Imruʾ al-Qays then acknowledged that his property perishes and his opponent gains reward. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,امرؤ القيس,يحيى)]
- 16:96/q19/p2 The verse was revealed concerning Imruʾ al-Qays ibn ʿAbbās al-Kindī and ʿAbdān ibn Ashrāʾ al-Ḥaḍramī's judgment of a claim to land and a house. — Muqātil gives these names and the land-and-house claim. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 16:96/q19/p3 It concerned Imruʾ al-Qays ibn ʿĀbis and ʿAydān ibn Ashwaʿ over land; as he prepared to swear, he wept and admitted the other's right. — Al-Wāḥidī reports this from al-Kalbī. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي)]
- 16:96/q19/p4 After the covenant verses were recited, Imruʾ al-Qays admitted taking land and offered it back with compensation for its fruit; 16:97 then descended about him. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports the sequel and its link to the next verse. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 16:96/q20 How does the contrast explain the preceding promise?  [* · links]
- 16:96/q20/p1 It explains 16:95: God's promised good is better because enduring divine gifts outlast human gain. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a concluding restatement, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī an explanation of the earlier claim. Al-Bayḍāwī says the inexhaustible mercy explains the prior judgment; Study Quran compares 20:131, 28:60, and 42:36. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also cites 28:60 elsewhere. [7 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; TABRISI]

## 16:96/q21 Why does the passage move from covenant keeping to patient people?  [* · links]
- 16:96/q21/p1 Keeping a covenant under pressure requires patience, so the promise expands to patient believers generally. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī explains the shift; Ibn ʿĀshūr says refusing the polytheists' immediate offer calls for endurance. Al-Ālūsī says the emphasized clause repeats the earlier promise to encourage steadfastness. [4 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 16:96/q22 What practical choice does the verse urge?  [* · interpretation]
- 16:96/q22/p1 Choose lasting divine reward over worldly gain, and keep faith, covenants, and oaths. — Al-Ṭabarī urges work for what lasts; Abū Mūsā reports the Prophet saying love of either world harms the other, so prefer the enduring. Al-Rāzī says awareness of loss spoils even noble worldly enjoyment. Ibn ʿĀshūr urges reliance on God's promise instead of human offers. Al-Wāḥidī warns against false oaths. Bursevī says faithfulness benefits both lives. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو موسى الأشعري); WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 16:96/q22/p2 The contrast is a general, exceptionless principle with many applications. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses absolute wording, and Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the address proverb-like. [2 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 16:96/q23 What inward lesson is drawn from choosing divine over worldly gifts?  [* · inward]
- 16:96/q23/p1 A seeker can forfeit extraordinary gifts by choosing a worldly benefit, even teaching, and must shed status and outward identity. — Bursevī relates two ascetics' vow not to eat anything made by a creature. Their companion left the cave for Tarsus to teach; one said he broke their pact and lost powers of instant travel, walking on water, and self-concealment. [2 notes: BURSEVI(بعض أهل العلم)]

## 16:96/q24 Which prophetic stories illustrate the patience promised here?  [صَبَرُوٓا۟ · links]
turns on: Which prophet's form of endurance is used as an example.
- 16:96/q24/p1 Jacob endures Joseph's absence with patience. — Study Quran cites 12:18 and 12:83–86 as recurrent examples and reward. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 16:96/q24/p2 Job faithfully endures affliction. — Study Quran cites 21:83–84 and 38:41, 44, identifying Job with prophetic steadfastness. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 16:96/q25 How do these verses fit the sūrah and its revelation?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The notes range from revelation classification to the sūrah's theme and nearby wording.
- 16:96/q25/p1 Verses 95–97, with verse 112, are classified as Madinan by some authorities. — Study Quran attributes the classification to Āl and IJ. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Āl, IJ)]
- 16:96/q25/p2 The sūrah answers objections meant to unsettle the public about the Qurʾān and the Prophet. — Iṣlāḥī gives this larger purpose. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 16:96/q25/p3 Nearby glad tidings fit the promise of a good life and full reward. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī relates the mention of bushrā to the nearby promise and distinguishes grouped wording in the verse about the Book's clarification, citing 16:89. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 16:96/q25/p4 The prior warning's singular indefinite 'foot' heightens danger: one person's slip implies the danger of many. — Al-Kashshāf connects the nearby foot image with slipping from truth after firmness. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]
