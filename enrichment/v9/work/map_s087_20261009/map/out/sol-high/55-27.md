# 55:27: 27 questions

## 55:27/q01 What remains when those on earth perish?  [وَيَبْقَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether perishing is read as future death or as the present dependence of created existence.
- 55:27/q01/p1 God alone remains eternally, while every creature passes away. — Muqatil compares 28:88; Tabari specifies jinn and humans; Abu Hayyan says every created thing is extinguished; Islahi links creation, provision, wisdom and sovereignty to its end and subsequent judgment; Ibn Ashur says mortality points to reckoning; Kuranyolu draws a call to live for God's pleasure. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 55:27/q01/p2 The universe is already perishing in its dependence, even while it appears to exist. — Bursevi reports that dependent things lack self-subsisting existence; only the pre-eternal is real. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 55:27/q02 What does 'the face of your Lord' denote here?  [وَجْهُ رَبِّكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether face refers directly to God, an attribute or manifestation, or the Godward aspect and deeds of creation.
- 55:27/q02/p1 God Himself, His enduring Essence or Reality. — Muqatil cites 28:88. Nahhas compares 'face of the earth'; Tabarsi cites the face as the person's manifestation through proofs and Arabic idioms for the essence of an opinion or plan, including al-A'sha's poetry. Razi says seeing a face lets one know the whole person; Qurtubi cites Ibn Abbas, Ibn Furak and Abu al-Ma'ali; Biqai says the face, the noblest part, cannot endure apart from its possessor. Bursevi calls it a borrowing from the chief seat of the senses; Asad, Ibn Ashur and Islahi cite ordinary metonymy. Baydawi and Bursevi also describe the God-facing aspect of otherwise finite things. Elmalili says nominative dhu supports the Essence and excludes created relations. [33 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو المعالي,ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(القاضي); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI(Kādî Beyzâvî); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI+; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن فورك وأبو المعالي)+]
- 55:27/q02/p2 God as manifest through His signs and proofs. — Wahidi's meaning scholars liken signs to a human face making a person apparent; Tabarsi combines this with reference to God Himself; Qurtubi reports it as an alternative. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 55:27/q02/p3 The Godward direction, aim, or aspect of beings, including the means of drawing near to Him. — Tabrisi cites poetry joining God's face to deeds; Qurtubi reports the approach to God; Bursevi's Ibn al-Shaykh links direction with 'in God's side' (39:56), while Sa'di al-Mufti explains the aim as a genitive. Baydawi's Godward aspect is reported by Alusi and Elmalili; Shihab calls direction an alternative to essence. Tabatabai says this can encompass prophets, saints, religion, reward and nearness. Alusi objects that a deed reading does not fit universal perishing. [8 notes: TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ,سعدي المفتي); ALUSI-FULL(البيضاوي); ELMALILI(Kādî Beyzâvî,Şihâb); TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-; ELMALILI-]
- 55:27/q02/p4 Righteous deeds directed to God, or their enduring reward. — Bursevi's Ibn al-Shaykh says only God-directed works escape passing; Elmalili says a deed is kept until its reward, which remains in its place. Alusi reports this but objects to its fit with universal perishing. [3 notes: BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-; ELMALILI-]
- 55:27/q02/p5 A distinct divine attribute by which God turns toward those He honors. — Qushayri reports it as an additional, unqualified attribute. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري)]
- 55:27/q02/p6 God's noble attributes or sacred Name, which convey His blessings to creation. — Tabatabai defines face as what a thing presents to or receives from another; creating, governing, mercy and provision reach creation through the attributes. The name finally designates the sacred Essence and remains unchanged by creatures' death. [4 notes: TABATABAI]
- 55:27/q02/p7 God's sustaining care for creatures, an attribute of His Essence. — Elmalili reports some shaykhs' reading and says it too stresses essential permanence through lordship. [1 notes: ELMALILI(Meşâyıhımızdan bazısı)]
- 55:27/q02/p8 The divine regard or bestowed existence through which contingent things exist. — Alusi says God's attending and bestowing aspect remains while things perish continually; his metaphysical account makes face the relation to God, or existence bestowed on possible beings. Makdisi and Elmalili say possible beings otherwise lack existence. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI(Makdisî); ELMALILI-]
- 55:27/q02/p9 The special relation in which possible beings manifest divine presence. — Alusi ascribes this to the theistic philosophers' metaphorical existence account; their possible beings mirror the self-subsistent Necessary, with reflections differing by relation. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(المتألهون من الحكماء)]
- 55:27/q02/p10 Divine affairs and manifestations of one Essence, apart from which created things have no independent being. — Alusi distinguishes the realized Sufis' one-Essence account from philosophers' mirrors and says the two are often treated alike; see 6:91. Bursevi reads the abiding face as essential unity after relative plurality of Names passes. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(المحققون من الصوفية); BURSEVI]

## 55:27/q03 Can the divine 'face' be understood as a bodily organ?  [وَجْهُ · theology]
turns on: Whether to leave the nonbodily expression unprobed or explain its figurative meaning.
- 55:27/q03/p1 No; divine transcendence rules out a corporeal face. — Razi argues from 28:88 that if face were an organ, other divine parts would perish; he says essence includes attributes, as a remaining garment includes color and size. Abu Hayyan excludes bodily limbs; Biqai invokes God's incomparability and freedom from need; Ibn Ashur says listeners know a head's bodily face is impossible. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 55:27/q03/p2 Affirm the revealed expression without asking how or assigning an interpretation, while denying likeness to creatures. — Suyuti reports the majority of early Sunnis and hadith scholars, Muhammad ibn al-Hasan's jurist consensus, and al-Tirmidhi's imams; al-Juwayni reportedly returned to this early method. Elmalili and Ibn Ashur describe the same restraint; Ibn Ashur accepts it. [6 notes: ITQAN(جمهور أهل السنة,سفيان الثوري ومالك وابن المبارك وابن عيينة ووكيع,محمد بن الحسن); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(السلف); ITQAN(إمام الحرمين)+]
- 55:27/q03/p3 Interpret attribute expressions figuratively in a manner fitting divine majesty. — Suyuti reports a later Sunni method; Alusi identifies essence interpretation as later theologians' ta'wil; Ibn Ashur says successors and later scholars clarified it through rhetoric and regards that as sound. [3 notes: ITQAN(طائفة من أهل السنة); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL(علماء التابعين ومن بعدهم)]

## 55:27/q04 Do God's attributes remain with His Essence?  [وَجْهُ · theology]
turns on: Whether the attributes belong with the Essence in what the verse calls enduring.
- 55:27/q04/p1 Yes; the enduring Essence includes its attributes. — Razi's garment analogy says the thing that remains retains color and dimensions; Qashani expressly includes all attributes; Ibn Ashur says the majesty-and-generosity qualification embraces transcendence, perfection, beauty and beneficence. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 55:27/q04/p2 Only the divine Essence remains; distinct attributes pass away. — Study Quran reports the inference made by theologians separating Essence from Attributes, and reports Razi's objection that such a claim would require the wording 'nothing remains except the Face of thy Lord.' [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(some theologians); STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)-]

## 55:27/q05 To whom does 'your Lord' address the verse?  [رَبِّكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular address names the Prophet in particular or distributes the warning to each listener.
- 55:27/q05/p1 Every hearer is addressed. — Razi says each person must attend to God alone; the singular prevents a pair of addressees from exempting each other. Bursevi extends it especially to humans and jinn; Ibn Ashur includes all hearers as the intended audience. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 55:27/q05/p2 The Prophet is the immediate addressee, to honor him. — Razi allows this; Abu Hayyan and Alusi prefer it; Ibn Ashur says the ultimate audience nevertheless includes everyone who hears the Qur'an. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 55:27/q06 What does the word 'Lord' convey here?  [رَبِّكَ · meaning]
- 55:27/q06/p1 The one who nurtures and sustains. — Razi reports rabb as a verbal noun for upbringing, by comparison with verbs of raising; alternatively it is an agent-like adjective from that noun. Biqai says God nurtures the Prophet by revelation toward unbounded knowledge. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 55:27/q07 Why say 'the face of your Lord' rather than simply 'your Lord' or another divine name?  [وَجْهُ رَبِّكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Which force of the chosen expression the commentator emphasizes.
- 55:27/q07/p1 The wording asserts God's absolute permanence without implying that a subject remains under His lordship. — Razi says Lord, Creator and Provider are agent names that could suggest continuing objects; even Allah may be explained as the worshipped one. Elmalili says face stresses the Essence's permanence as its attributes and lordship manifest. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 55:27/q07/p2 The face, as the noblest part, more eloquently conveys the whole enduring person. — Biqai says a face cannot continue apart from its possessor and its mention is more eloquent than mentioning the possessor directly. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 55:27/q07/p3 The wording evokes care, gentleness and the sūrah's blessings. — Razi contrasts the gentleness of direct address to the Lord with the might of abiding; Study Quran reports that 'your Lord' suits the recurring blessing and bounty theme. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 55:27/q07/p4 This passage's lordly sustaining favors suit 'your Lord'; other passages use 'face of Allah' for worship or charity. — Razi compares 2:115 and 30:38. Ibn Ashur likewise notes direction and charitable intention in 2:115 and 76:9. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 55:27/q08 How is 'rabb' ordinarily constructed in Arabic?  [رَبِّكَ · grammar]
- 55:27/q08/p1 It is ordinarily annexed; when unannexed it is accompanied by another description. — Razi cites 44:8, 1:2, 34:15 and 36:58. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 55:27/q09 Is 'rabb' originally a verbal noun or a derived description?  [رَبِّكَ · grammar]
turns on: Its proposed morphological origin.
- 55:27/q09/p1 It may be a verbal noun meaning upbringing. — Razi compares verbs meaning to raise or nurture. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 55:27/q09/p2 It may be a derived adjective meaning one who nurtures. — Razi compares agent names and qualities formed from verbal nouns. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 55:27/q10 How is ذُو read, and what does it modify?  [ذُو · readings]
turns on: The case ending and whether the epithet follows face, Lord, or an implied predicate.
- 55:27/q10/p1 The common nominative ذُو modifies وَجْهُ. — Tabari, Nahhas, Wahidi, Jishumi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, Nasafi, Elmalili and Study Quran attach the adjective to face; Wahidi says a Lord adjunct would require ذِي. Ibn al-Jazari says every codex agrees on wāw here, so it cannot be an insertion. Alusi connects this to God's uninjured self-sufficiency and future generosity. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(عبد الله); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; NASHR; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 55:27/q10/p2 The reported genitive ذِي modifies رَبِّكَ. — Attributed to Abdullah/Ibn Mas'ud and Ubayy by Tabari, Nahhas, Jishumi, Abu Hayyan, Samin and Alusi; it makes the Lord the grammatical bearer of the epithet. Ibn al-Jazari distinguishes the canonical text's unanimous wāw. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله); NAHHAS(ابن مسعود); JISHUMI(عبد الله); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبي وعبد الله)]
- 55:27/q10/p3 The nominative may be detached for praise, with an implied 'He is'; it then describes the Lord. — Tabatabai proposes an elided predicate; Alusi says this parse is used particularly if face means action or direction. [2 notes: TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(أبي وعبد الله)]

## 55:27/q11 What further recitational variants affect the epithet?  [ذُو ٱلْإِكْرَامِ · readings]
turns on: Which occurrence or pronunciation the report concerns.
- 55:27/q11/p1 At the sūrah's ending the seven readers differ in the corresponding phrase. — Al-Samin notes the contrast with this occurrence. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 55:27/q11/p2 Reports differ on Ibn Dhakwan's imāla in الإكرام. — Ibn al-Jazari says al-Tajrid records only imāla through al-Akhfash and several transmitters, alongside عمران (3:36) and إكراههن (24:33). [1 notes: NASHR]

## 55:27/q12 What does 'majesty' mean here?  [ٱلْجَلَٰلِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether majesty is glossed chiefly as greatness, independence, or awe-inspiring transcendence.
- 55:27/q12/p1 Divine greatness, exaltation, sovereignty and deservingness of praise. — Wahidi and Qurtubi derive jalal from jalla, to be great; Tabarsi adds supreme beneficence as the source of favors; Ibn Abbas is reported in Ibn Kathir and Durr as glossing it greatness and pride. Qashani describes veils and overpowering dominion; Elmalili speaks of greatness that overwhelms all. [15 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 55:27/q12/p2 Absolute independence and freedom from every need or unworthy quality. — Baydawi joins self-sufficiency with bounty; Bursevi and Alusi report the same; Biqai says it excludes what is unworthy of God. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين); ELMALILI]
- 55:27/q12/p3 God's awe-inspiring transcendence, which monotheists affirm. — Abu Hayyan lists transcendence above likeness to creation and awe before God; Alusi distinguishes intrinsic perfection beyond comprehension from felt awe. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 55:27/q13 Is 'jalāl' used only of God?  [ٱلْجَلَٰلِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the claim concerns all historical usage or the proper divine title.
- 55:27/q13/p1 Only God deserves or is described by jalāl. — Wahidi reports al-Asma'i's usage rule; Abu Ali allows that al-Asma'i may mean exclusive deservingness after Islam. Raghib says the full title uniquely belongs to God. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأصمعي); ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); ELMALILI(Râgıb)]
- 55:27/q13/p2 Arabic poetry also applies jalāl to someone other than God. — Wahidi's Abu Ali cites a poem, possibly pre-Islamic, qualifying al-Asma'i's claim. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي)]

## 55:27/q14 What does 'honor' or 'generosity' mean here?  [ٱلْإِكْرَامِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ikram names God's gifts, the servant's duty to honor Him, or God's own supreme honor.
- 55:27/q14/p1 God honors and benefits His servants through grace, pardon and bounty. — Wahidi specifies prophets through grace and, through Hasan and al-Kalbi, adherents of God's religion through forgiveness; Baghawi and Tabarsi name prophets and friends; Ibn Jawzi says God raises devotees' ranks; Nasafi says pardon and beneficence. Biqai makes it divine action, and Elmalili extends care even to the perishing. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]
- 55:27/q14/p2 Servants must honor God through worship, praise, reverence and avoiding disbelief or shirk. — Wahidi mentions worship and praise for favors; al-Khattabi calls both terms verbal nouns deserving human reverence and honor, without denial; Qurtubi requires declaring God free of partners; Ibn Kathir says to revere and obey without defiance. Ibn Jawzi compares the distribution of God and servant actions in 74:56. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الخطابي); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 55:27/q14/p3 God's incomparable honor: none is more honorable or greater. — Wahidi reports Ibn Abbas's gloss. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)]
- 55:27/q14/p4 God grants nearness through manifestations of gentleness and mercy. — Qashani connects generosity to attributes appearing and the Essence manifesting in mercy. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 55:27/q15 What does the combined title 'Possessor of Majesty and Honor' express?  [ذُو ٱلْجَلَٰلِ وَٱلْإِكْرَامِ · interpretation]
turns on: How the two qualities divide between transcendence, positive perfection, God's action, and human response.
- 55:27/q15/p1 It joins God's transcendent greatness with generous beneficence. — Baydawi calls these absolute self-sufficiency and general bounty; Bursevi and Alusi pair independence with complete favor; Ibn Ashur says the pair covers transcendence, perfection, beauty and service to creatures; Study Quran relates the pair to Majesty and Beauty. [10 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 55:27/q15/p2 The title can mark God's worthiness of reverence and His honoring of servants respectively. — Ibn Jawzi allows one noun to name a divine attribute and the other a servant's act, comparing 74:56; Abu Hayyan reports God as the giver of majesty and honor to sincere servants. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 55:27/q15/p3 Majesty denotes negated limitations; generosity or beauty denotes affirmed perfections. — Alusi reports al-Kirmani's examples of no partner versus life and knowledge, but says the division needs examination. Study Quran reports a Shiite scheme of Beauty's inherent positive attributes and Majesty's denial of bodily limits. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الكرماني); STUDYQURAN(many Shiites)]
- 55:27/q15/p4 The name ultimately denotes the sacred Essence. — Tabatabai says the paired names of majesty and beauty name God Himself, comparing 55:78. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 55:27/q15/p5 It is among God's uniquely exalted names. — Alusi and Elmalili report Raghib's description of the full title as unique to God and one of His greatest descriptions. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); ELMALILI(Râgıb)]

## 55:27/q16 What is reported about invoking God by this title?  [ذُو ٱلْجَلَٰلِ وَٱلْإِكْرَامِ · reports]
turns on: The reports concern distinct exhortations, answered prayers and transmitted formulas.
- 55:27/q16/p1 The Prophet urges persistent repetition of 'O Possessor of Majesty and Honor' in supplication. — Qurtubi transmits Anas and an Ibn Mas'ud attribution; Abu Ubayd explains ilzaz as adhering and insistent prayer. Nasafi, Bursevi, Alusi and Elmalili report the same exhortation, with Alusi and Elmalili also naming Rabi'a ibn Amir. [7 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد,ابن مسعود,النبي); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام); ALUSI-FULL(أنس وربيعة بن عامر); ELMALILI(Enes, Rebî‘a b. Âmir)]
- 55:27/q16/p2 The Prophet told a man who invoked this name that his prayer had been answered. — Nasafi and Bursevi transmit the incident; Bursevi draws the prospect of acceptance. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام)]
- 55:27/q16/p3 A man invoking this name heard that his request had been heard. — Qurtubi transmits Sa'id al-Maqburi's report. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد المقبري)]
- 55:27/q16/p4 Invocation including the title is a supplication by God's Greatest Name, which draws an answer. — Alusi reports Anas's account of a man; Elmalili specifies praise, generosity, majesty, life and self-subsistence in the prayer. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أنس); ELMALILI(Resûlullah sallallâhu aleyhi ve sellem)]
- 55:27/q16/p5 A transmitted supplication joins this title to God's living, sustaining and creating power. — Ibn Kathir cites the request for mercy, rectitude and protection from dependence on self or creation. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 55:27/q16/p6 A man prayed by God's generous face; another wording called it the abiding beautiful face. — Durr reports the man and al-Bayhaqi's variant wording. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(رجل)]

## 55:27/q17 How does the verse relate to the death of creatures?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the point is the coming end and judgment or why that end counts as a favor.
- 55:27/q17/p1 The verse states a universal end and anticipates reckoning and reward. — Ibn Kathir reports Qatada that God tells of creation and then its passing; Ibn Ashur says the reminder of death looks ahead to recompense; Islahi says none can challenge God's judgment or intercede without leave. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 55:27/q17/p2 Death is a favor because it leads from earthly trial to recompense. — Tabrisi says death equalizes creatures and warns of life's end; immediate reward would compel action and deprive it of merit. Tabatabai calls worldly life a preparation for the afterlife; Kuranyolu points to lasting happiness through a life pleasing God. [3 notes: TABRISI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 55:27/q18 What did the angels conclude when the verse was revealed?  [* · reports]
turns on: The report differs on the later verse and on what exactly perishes.
- 55:27/q18/p1 They first inferred the earthly beings had perished, then learned their own mortality from 28:88. — Muqatil says they wondered how life could benefit people and were reassured that all die; Wahidi attributes the report to al-Kalbi and Muqatil. [2 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي ومقاتل)]
- 55:27/q18/p2 They first thought Adam's children had perished, then learned their own mortality from 'every soul tastes death' (3:185). — Bursevi adds that angels' subtle bodies and attached spirits perish but lofty immaterial spirits do not. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الملائكة)]

## 55:27/q19 Where should a reciter pause across verses 26–27?  [* · readings]
- 55:27/q19/p1 Continue past 'all on earth perish' until God's abiding face has been recited. — Wahidi, Ibn Kathir, Ibn al-Jazari, Durr and Suyuti transmit al-Sha'bi's direction; Suyuti cites Ibn Abi Hatim. Burhan says the two statements do not conflict. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الشعبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الشعبي); NASHR(الشعبي); DURR-FULL(الشعبي); ITQAN(الشعبي); BURHAN]

## 55:27/q20 How does this verse connect to other Qur'anic passages?  [* · links]
turns on: Which passage supplies the comparison: 28:88 or the sūrah's own ending.
- 55:27/q20/p1 It parallels 28:88: all things perish except God's face. — Muqatil, Razi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, Elmalili, Asad and Study Quran invoke the parallel; Ibn Kathir also connects seeking the face to 18:28 and 76:9. [7 notes: MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 55:27/q20/p2 The closing title in 55:78 echoes 55:27 and confirms God's exclusive exaltation. — Islahi says no partner shares majesty and bounty; Study Quran says the echo places God beyond earthly and heavenly blessings; Tabatabai also compares 55:78. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI]

## 55:27/q21 What role does this verse play in the sūrah's movement?  [* · links]
turns on: Which structural connection within the sūrah is emphasized.
- 55:27/q21/p1 It closes the worldly blessings and turns toward the Hereafter. — Study Quran locates the turn after vv. 10–25; Abu Hayyan contrasts the passing world and enduring face with the sūrah's later lasting Paradise and blessed name; Bursevi says generosity after death includes resurrection and eternal bliss. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 55:27/q21/p2 It sets God's permanence against creation's transient dualities and grounds the repeated favor refrain. — Study Quran contrasts God with what passes; Ibn Ashur says the ensuing question about favors follows because His great and generous treatment never ceases; Islahi traces the passage to the earth since verse 10. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 55:27/q22 What rhetorical effect joins human death to divine permanence?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes identify distinct rhetorical effects of the same juxtaposition.
- 55:27/q22/p1 The sequence blends consolation of creatures and praise of God's enduring majesty and generosity. — Suyuti calls the ten-word turn iftinan, combining two modes, consolation and praise. [1 notes: ITQAN]
- 55:27/q22/p2 The verse names a part, 'face,' for the whole divine Self. — Suyuti compares body-wide states expressed through faces and hands and actions of prayer naming whole prayer, citing 2:144, 88:8, 88:3, 2:286, 2:184, 17:78, 76:26, 2:43 and 5:95. [1 notes: ITQAN]
- 55:27/q22/p3 God's majesty and generosity signal that kindness continues beyond creatures' deaths. — Bursevi says resurrection to lasting bliss is the greatest blessing. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 55:27/q23 What inward insight does the enduring face convey?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the inward lesson concerns veiling, witnessing, or the paired manifestations.
- 55:27/q23/p1 Divine disclosure and veiling govern what creatures can witness. — Sulami's al-Wasiti says God inwardly discloses in this life while veiling by wonders; in the next He gives outwardly but shields creatures according to their capacity. [1 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي)]
- 55:27/q23/p2 The knower witnesses God's abiding presence through creation. — Elmalili reports realized knowers who see nothing apart from God; Ibn Ata calls creation darkness lit only by divine manifestation and says overlooking God in, before, after or alongside it veils knowledge. [2 notes: ELMALILI(Muhakkikler,İbn Atâ)]
- 55:27/q23/p3 Majesty and generosity manifest as overpowering veils and merciful nearness. — Qashani describes luminous and dark veils, dominion, attributes, gentleness and mercy. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 55:27/q24 Does 'face' derive from confrontation?  [وَجْهُ · meaning]
turns on: Which sense is taken as the lexical starting point.
- 55:27/q24/p1 No; the organ name preceded terms for facing or confrontation. — Razi rejects the reverse derivation found in some legal books and treats later related forms as transferred from the earlier organ word. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 55:27/q24/p2 The original sense may be direction, with essence a later allusion. — Alusi reports this alternative origin account. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 55:27/q25 How does the title's majesty-and-generosity description identify the face?  [وَجْهُ ذُو ٱلْجَلَٰلِ وَٱلْإِكْرَامِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the modifier attaches semantically to the Essence directly or to a divine name or attribute.
- 55:27/q25/p1 The title identifies God's Essence, which bears these attributes. — Ibn Ashur says the epithet embraces transcendence, perfection and beneficence; Elmalili argues that created God-facing relations cannot bear it, so face means Essence. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 55:27/q25/p2 The title can describe a noble divine Name or attribute and thereby ultimately the Essence. — Tabatabai says applying the name to the name ultimately names God; creation's passing leaves the name unchanged. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 55:27/q26 How do different accounts of existence frame this verse?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether possible beings possess actual existence or are described as existent through divine manifestation.
- 55:27/q26/p1 Possible beings have actual existence inhering in them. — Alusi identifies this as the view of most philosophers and theologians, in contrast with metaphorical-existence accounts. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 55:27/q26/p2 Created existence is metaphorical, explained either as divine manifestation or as affairs of one Essence. — Alusi distinguishes the theistic philosophers' mirror model from realized Sufis' one-Essence model; his note says the two are often assimilated. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 55:27/q27 How is this verse's 'face' compared with other divine attribute expressions?  [* · links]
- 55:27/q27/p1 It serves as a comparison for treating an apparent body-part term as emphatic. — Suyuti reports Mujahid's comparison of the 'hand' in 38:75 with 'the Face of your Lord' here; al-Baghawi rejects the hand-as-addition reading because it would erase Adam's distinction from Satan. [1 notes: ITQAN(مجاهد)]
