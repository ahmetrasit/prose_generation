# 3:64: 28 questions

## 3:64/q01 Who is addressed as the People of the Book?  [يَٰٓأَهْلَ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ · referent]
turns on: Reports name Najran Christians, Medina Jews, both communities, and an Abyssinian audience; several sources distinguish occasion from general scope.
- 3:64/q01/p1 Both Jews and Christians, and by extension all recipients of scripture, are addressed. — Al-Tabari argues from the unqualified wording and shared duty; al-Tabrisi and Kuran Yolu prefer the general scope. Ibn Kathir includes comparable scriptural peoples; Heraclius received the verse. [24 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(عمر بن عبد العزيز); WAHIDI-BASIT(بعضهم); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو علي الجبائي); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; TABRISI(أبو علي الجبائي)+]
- 3:64/q01/p2 The Jews of Medina are the immediate audience. — Qatada, al-Rabi and Ibn Jurayj connect their invitation with the Abraham dispute; some report refusal, fighting and jizya. [20 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,الربيع,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن جريج); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة، الربيع، ابن جريج); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة، والربيع، وابن جريج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة، ابن جريج، الربيع بن أنس); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة والربيع وابن جريج); TABRISI(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة وابن جريج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة والربيع وابن جريج); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,الربيع,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 3:64/q01/p3 The Najran Christians are the immediate audience. — Al-Razi prefers Najran because the passage follows proof and imprecation; Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan favor this occasion but allow wider use. Reports cite al-Hasan, al-Suddi, Ibn Zayd and Muhammad ibn Jafar. [24 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,السدي,محمد بن جعفر بن الزبير); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن، السدي، ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن، والسدي، وابن زيد، ومحمد بن جعفر بن الزبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي، مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد,محمد بن جعفر بن الزبير والسدي); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن وابن زيد والسدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد,الحسن والسدي ومحمد بن جعفر بن الزبير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد,الحسن,السدي,محمد بن جعفر بن الزبير); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some); IBNATIYYA-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 3:64/q01/p4 Priests and monks in Abyssinia were an audience of its recitation. — Ibn Abbas says the Prophet sent it to Jafar, who recited it before the Negus and Abyssinian leaders. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BURHAN]

## 3:64/q02 Why use the title People of the Book?  [يَٰٓأَهْلَ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ · rhetoric]
- 3:64/q02/p1 The title invokes scriptural knowledge and opens a respectful hearing. — Al-Razi says it honors and soothes former opponents; Abu Hayyan calls them to follow their scriptures; Biqai and Kuran Yolu stress tact and respectful dialogue. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 3:64/q03 What does kitāb denote here?  [ٱلْكِتَٰبِ · meaning]
- 3:64/q03/p1 Scripture, chiefly Torah and Gospel, though the Gospel can be singled out. — Ibn al-Jawzi traces kitāb to gathered written speech; Ibn Qutayba treats the act of writing as naming its product, like a prince's struck coin. [7 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن قتيبة,بعض المفسرين)]

## 3:64/q04 What does taʿālaw mean?  [تَعَالَوْا۟ · meaning]
turns on: Some explain approach to an agreement and Biqai gives a moral ascent.
- 3:64/q04/p1 Approach and attend to the proposed agreement, without physical movement. — Al-Tabari relates the form to rising; Razi and Bursevi explain the extended usage. Ibn Ashur likens the word to a meeting place; Tabatabai emphasizes acting on its meaning. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMIN-DURR]
- 3:64/q04/p2 Rise out of lesser and greater polytheism. — Biqai interprets the invitation morally. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 3:64/q05 What is meant by a singular kalima?  [كَلِمَةٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: It can name a proposition, a full discourse, plural words collectively, or a connected formula.
- 3:64/q05/p1 A meaningful utterance or whole discourse. — Zajjaj, Wahidi and others cite an ode called a 'word'; Hassan's report names al-Huwaydira's poem. Ibn Ashur uses the ordinary brief-utterance sense, and Asad notes philosophical 'tenet'. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج وغيره); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 3:64/q05/p2 A singular stands for multiple words or a whole poem. — Ibn al-Anbari cites Alqama's hides, and al-Khansa and an anonymous poem use 'rhyme' for an ode; Abu Hayyan notes singular for plural. [9 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,الخنساء,الزجاج وغيره); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR(بعضهم,شاعر غير مسمى)]
- 3:64/q05/p3 Connected words form one indivisible statement. — Abu Hayyan and al-Samin say removing any part of the tawhid formula breaks its exclusive meaning. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR]

## 3:64/q06 How is kalima read?  [كَلِمَةٍۢ · readings]
turns on: The variants alter kāf or lām vowels and stillness.
- 3:64/q06/p1 The lām is still, with fatḥa or kasra on kāf. — Abu al-Sammal has both attributions; Qu'nub and Alusi mention shifting the lām's vowel, as with kabid/kabd. Abu Hayyan compares sidra and darba. [8 notes: NAHHAS(قعنب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو السمال); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو السمال); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور); QURTUBI-FULL(قعنب); ALUSI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 3:64/q06/p2 The common form has fatḥa on kāf and kasra on lām. — Ibn Atiyya and al-Samin call it the basic form. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور)]

## 3:64/q07 What is the common word's content?  [كَلِمَةٍۢ أَلَّا نَعْبُدَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهَ · interpretation]
turns on: Most take the ensuing clauses as its content; some compress it to the tawhid confession or read it inwardly.
- 3:64/q07/p1 It is worshipping God alone, associating no partner, and making no human lord. — The following clauses explain it; Baydawi, Nasafi and others say prophets and scriptures agree. Ibn Hisham says the appeal removes their argument. [31 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور المفسرين); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(others)]
- 3:64/q07/p2 It is the declaration 'there is no god but God'. — Abu al-Aliya and Mujahid name the formula; Damghani and others repeat it. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو العالية); IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية); NAHHAS(أبو العالية); DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو العالية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو العالية); DURR-FULL(أبو العالية,مجاهد)]
- 3:64/q07/p3 It is inward servanthood and realized divine unity. — Al-Wasiti invokes servanthood before divine self-sufficiency; Ibn Ata realization of unity; Qushayri God as sole originator. [3 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الواسطي); QUSHAYRI]

## 3:64/q08 What does sawāʾ mean?  [سَوَآءٍۭ · meaning]
turns on: Glosses emphasize justice, balanced middle, shared standing or shared scriptural agreement.
- 3:64/q08/p1 Justice, fairness, and equitable balance. — Zuhayr's poetry, Ibn Masud's 'word of justice,' and other Quranic uses support this. Bint al-Shati identifies equality as the root sense and distinguishes it from legal ʿadl. [59 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(الربيع,قتادة); ZAJJAJ(زهير بن أبي سلمى); IBNABIHATIM(الربيع); NAHHAS(الفراء,قتادة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة,الزجاج); DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن قتيبة,الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج,زهير بن أبي سلمى); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء,زهير,عبد الله,قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيدة,ابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس,الجمهور); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الربيع,قتادة,محمد بن جعفر بن الزبير); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Al-Rāzī,some)]
- 3:64/q08/p2 The balanced middle or right course. — Ibn Qutayba says the middle is fairest; commentators cite 37:55, 20:58, 38:22, 41:10 and 28:22. Ibn Atiyya calls right direction close to justice. [16 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة); JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن قتيبة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المفسرين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ibn Masʿūd)]
- 3:64/q08/p3 What Torah, Gospel and Quran agree on. — Kashshaf and later commentators take equality as shared scriptural meaning; Tabatabai says the following clause corrects distorted understandings. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); STUDYQURAN(others); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 3:64/q08/p4 Equal standing of all persons in a shared truth. — Ibn Atiyya prefers parity of status: a captive's choice of conversion or death may be just yet unequal, whereas faith with freedom and an equal share invites as peers. Abu Hayyan dismisses the extension; Ibn Ashur approves it. Tabatabai emphasizes equal acceptance and action. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية); TABATABAI; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 3:64/q09 What is sawāʾ's grammatical form and reading?  [سَوَآءٍۭ · grammar]
turns on: The common genitive adjective competes with a verbal noun analysis and al-Hasan's accusative.
- 3:64/q09/p1 Genitive adjective of kalima, often an adjectival verbal noun. — Basran grammarians, Zajjaj and the majority give this parse; a verbal noun need not agree in gender or number. Ibn Abi al-Rabi discusses its ability to raise a coordinated noun. [22 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي البصرة,بعض نحويي الكوفة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); JISHUMI(الزجاج,قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور,زهير); BURHAN(ابن أبي الربيع); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 3:64/q09/p2 A noun or verbal noun of equality, with an estimated 'possessing' or adverbial sense. — The Kufan grammarian and Tabrisi mention a nominal parse; sawā/suwā are shortened alternate forms, illustrated by 20:58. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي الكوفة); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; JISHUMI; TABRISI(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء)]
- 3:64/q09/p3 Al-Hasan's accusative is a cognate accusative or circumstantial modifier. — Al-Hawfi and Zamakhshari supply istawat istiwāʾ; al-Shaykh objects to an implied governing verb. Sibawayh permits a circumstance from an indefinite noun, which al-Samin accepts. [9 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الحسن,الزمخشري,الشيخ,سيبويه); ALUSI-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج)]

## 3:64/q10 What does 'between us and you' refer to and depend on?  [بَيْنَنَا وَبَيْنَكُمْ · grammar]
- 3:64/q10/p1 It denotes the Prophet and Muslims opposite the invitees, and is governed by sawāʾ. — Ibn Ashur infers 'us' from the final command; Zuhayr's verse supports the construction of sawāʾ with bayna. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(زهير); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 3:64/q11 How is an lā naʿbuda parsed?  [أَلَّا نَعْبُدَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهَ · grammar]
turns on: Analyses differ over apposition, an implied subject or preposition, explanatory an, and predicate relations.
- 3:64/q11/p1 Genitive apposition to kalima, explaining the common word. — Al-Tabari, Zajjaj, Wahidi and others adopt it; Ibn Ashur favors it under grammatical convention. [18 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 3:64/q11/p2 Nominative predicate after an implicit 'it is'. — Zajjaj treats it as the answer to 'What is the word?' Tabrisi discusses an lightened from emphatic an, citing 20:89 and 21:44. [17 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); NAHHAS; THALABI(الزجاج); JISHUMI(الزجاج,قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]
- 3:64/q11/p3 Explanatory an, with prohibitive jussive or indicative verbs. — Zajjaj compares 43:23; Sibawayh licenses jussive or raised verbs. Al-Kisai and al-Farra invoke attraction to a supposed opening an; al-Nahhas rejects that account. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الكسائي والفراء); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي والفراء,سيبويه); ALUSI-FULL; NAHHAS-]
- 3:64/q11/p4 An omitted preposition yields an accusative clause. — Al-Thalabi and al-Jishumi supply bi-an; Baghawi also gives the analysis. [3 notes: THALABI(قيل); JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 3:64/q11/p5 Nominative subject with baynanā as predicate. — Abu al-Baqa proposes a stop at sawāʾ; al-Akhfash and Kufans allow an unsupported adverb to govern, unlike Basrans. Abu Hayyan and al-Samin fault a missing link. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 3:64/q11/p6 Apposition to sawāʾ. — Abu al-Baqa allows it; al-Samin calls it unclear. Ibn Hisham and Ibn Ashur reject its adjective-like result, while al-Damamini disputes their objection. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL(الدماميني وغيره,جماعة); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن هشام)-]
- 3:64/q11/p7 Subject governed by sawāʾ after an implied link. — Ali ibn Isa and al-Zamani estimate an omitted fiha; Abu Hayyan and al-Shaykh call that omission weak. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الزماني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي بن عيسى)-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]

## 3:64/q12 What does worshipping God alone entail?  [أَلَّا نَعْبُدَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهَ · theology]
- 3:64/q12/p1 Sincere exclusive devotion to the one God. — Tabatabai says the clause denies other objects of worship, not God's existence, and understands except-God appositionally. Razi argues worship belonged only to God before Jesus; Tabatabai grounds one Lord in one Creator. [28 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 3:64/q13 What does associating nothing with God prohibit?  [وَلَا نُشْرِكَ بِهِۦ شَيْـًۭٔا · theology]
turns on: Some treat this as a distinct foundation and some as emphatic expansion.
- 3:64/q13/p1 No being can share God's worthiness of worship or divine status. — Wahidi explains bi as 'with'; Ibn Kathir includes idols, crosses, tyrants and fire (21:25; 16:36). Biqai excludes even an unworshipped supposed partner; Razi argues three independent hypostases entail shirk. [20 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 3:64/q13/p2 The added prohibition reinforces exclusive worship. — Abu Hayyan says all three negatives overlap to address deeply rooted practice; Alusi mentions emphasis but prefers an independent foundation. Tabatabai notes worship of one supposed partner remains shirk. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 3:64/q14 How does 'anything' work grammatically?  [شَيْـًۭٔا · grammar]
- 3:64/q14/p1 It may be object or verbal noun, with unrestricted negation. — Abu Hayyan extends the negative over object, act, time, place and manner; Alusi rejects narrowing it to 'forms of idolatry'. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 3:64/q15 What is meant by taking one another as lords?  [وَلَا يَتَّخِذَ بَعْضُنَا بَعْضًا أَرْبَابًۭا مِّن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
turns on: The sources distinguish deification, unauthorized obedience, bodily homage, idols, indwelling, and absolute human rule.
- 3:64/q15/p1 Obeying clergy or leaders against God's command, especially in lawful and forbidden rulings. — 9:31 and Adi ibn Hatim's report say accepting clergy's reversals is worship without ritual devotion. Ibn Jurayj includes chiefs outside worship; al-Fudayl likens it to prayer away from the qibla. [51 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); IBNABIHATIM(ابن جريج); NAHHAS; THALABI(قيل); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج); WAHIDI-BASIT(الخبر,بعضهم); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(الفضيل,عدي بن حاتم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جريج); TABRISI(أبي عبد الله,النبي,عدي بن حاتم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جريج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(عدي بن حاتم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جريج,الطبري,عدي بن حاتم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جريج); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); BURSEVI(الفضيل); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جريج,عدي بن حاتم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hz. Peygamber); STUDYQURAN(some); ELMALILI(Resûlullah)]
- 3:64/q15/p2 Treating Jesus, Ezra, another human, or angels as divine or God's child. — Muqatil singles out Jesus; Ibn Abbas includes Uzayr. Zajjaj stresses Jesus' humanity; Asad mentions saints and Talmudic scholars; Elmalili argues 'teacher' does not remove the Trinity issue (3:48). [29 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; IBNABIHATIM(عكررمة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,عطاء عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل، الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عكرمة); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل والزجاج وعكرمة); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 3:64/q15/p3 Bowing or prostrating to one another. — Ikrima gives this sense; Qurtubi cites Muadh's proposed prostration and Anas' handshaking report. Razi includes prostration to clergy. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(عكرمة); IBNABIHATIM(عكرمة); THALABI(عكرمة); MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة); BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عكرمة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أنس بن مالك,عكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جريج); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة); DURR-FULL(عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة); STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 3:64/q15/p4 Attributing divine indwelling or independent agency to perfected or powerful humans. — Abu Muslim reports ascetics attributing revival and healing to spiritually perfected people; Biqai rejects independent created agency; Qushayri and Bursevi reject reliance on people for provision. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); BIQAI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI]
- 3:64/q15/p5 One person's absolute domination over another violates equal human standing. — Tabatabai reasons from shared humanity and exclusive divine lordship; Elmalili grounds rights and legitimate allegiance in God's rule. [21 notes: TABATABAI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi)]
- 3:64/q15/p6 Idols are the forbidden lords. — Ikrima is reported with this referent. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عكررمة)]

## 3:64/q16 Why say 'some of us, others' and 'besides God'?  [بَعْضُنَا بَعْضًا مِّن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
- 3:64/q16/p1 The reciprocal wording stresses shared humanity and the impossibility of human divinity. — Abu Hayyan compares objections to human prophets at 14:10-11 and 23:47. Alusi limits 'our' to humans and reads 'besides' as apart from God alone; Qurtubi glosses dun as other than. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 3:64/q17 What legal implications follow from rejecting human lords?  [أَرْبَابًۭا · law]
turns on: The issue is whether religious judgment rests on proof.
- 3:64/q17/p1 Bare legal preference and an unproved imam's binding ruling are invalid. — Al-Kiya criticizes unsupported estimates; Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan reject obedience without a stated legal basis. Kuran Yolu says the target is arbitrary inference. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الكيا الطبري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Kurtubî)]
- 3:64/q17/p2 Istihsan grounded in stronger evidence or principles can be valid. — Kuran Yolu distinguishes benefit, fairness and honesty from arbitrary judgment and says most scholars use grounded preference despite terminological differences. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 3:64/q18 What does the closing declaration demand?  [فَإِن تَوَلَّوْا۟ فَقُولُوا۟ ٱشْهَدُوا۟ بِأَنَّا مُسْلِمُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Explanations vary between testimony to Muslim submission, proof, reproach, indirect accusation and steadfastness.
- 3:64/q18/p1 Profess sincere submission and exclusive monotheism despite refusal. — Al-Tabari specifies inward and outward submission; Zajjaj includes prior prophets; Qurtubi rejects Jesus, Uzayr, angels and monks as lords; Tabatabai calls Islam God's approved religion. [22 notes: MUQATIL; IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 3:64/q18/p2 Demand acknowledgement that proof binds them and Muslims are right. — Kashshaf likens it to a defeated opponent conceding; Baydawi, Nasafi and Bursevi repeat the proof-based challenge. Tabrisi says truth binds regardless of how many accept. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 3:64/q18/p3 Publicly confront and rebuke the rejecters, warning of consequences. — Ibn Atiyya reads a public announcement and threat; Abu Hayyan stresses the contrast of refusal and Muslim obedience; Biqai hears impending confrontation. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); BIQAI-FULL]
- 3:64/q18/p4 Indirectly make them admit unbelief or rejection of their own scripture. — Kashshaf gives an indirect challenge; Baydawi invokes their scriptures and messengers; Alusi reports verifiers preferring the implication. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين)]
- 3:64/q18/p5 Remain steadfast, openly professing Islam without fear. — Tabrisi names perseverance; Alusi reports this interpretation but says hiding from fear ill fits openly professing opponents. [2 notes: TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 3:64/q18/p6 Their testimony will stand against them at the Resurrection. — Bursevi compares the hadith that every hearer of the prayer call testifies for its caller. [2 notes: BURSEVI(رسول الله)]

## 3:64/q19 How do tawallaw and the conditional work?  [فَإِن تَوَلَّوْا۟ فَقُولُوا۟ · grammar]
- 3:64/q19/p1 Tawallaw is past tense about opponents; faqulu addresses believers. — Al-Samin and Alusi reject tatwallaw because it mismatches the addressees and lacks a valid apodosis. Ibn Ashur and Biqai say 'if' portrays refusal as unlikely after the evidence. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 3:64/q20 What encounter prompted or applied the invitation?  [* · reports]
turns on: Accounts place it after Najran's imprecation, among Medina Jews, in a mixed Abraham dispute, or in Abyssinia.
- 3:64/q20/p1 Jews and Christians disputed Abraham and alleged Muhammad wanted to be worshipped. — The Prophet said Abraham was a hanif; Abu Rafi and a Najranian ask whether he sought status like Jesus, and he denies it. [11 notes: THALABI(المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو رافع القرظي,ابن عباس); ELMALILI; KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 3:64/q20/p2 Najran's Christians declined imprecation and received an easier common appeal. — Ibn Zayd names the sequence; Razi and Biqai say the Prophet continued seeking their faith after proof and tribute. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد,محمد بن جعفر بن الزبير والسدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد,الحسن والسدي ومحمد بن جعفر بن الزبير); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي,محمد بن جعفر بن الزبير)]
- 3:64/q20/p3 Medina Jews disputed Abraham's religion and were invited. — Qatada cites their claim that Abraham died a Jew and 3:65; Ibn Jurayj reports refusal, fighting and jizya. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,الربيع,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن جريج); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة والربيع وابن جريج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة والربيع وابن جريج); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,الربيع,قتادة); TABATABAI(بعض الروايات)]
- 3:64/q20/p4 Jafar recited the invitation before the Negus. — Ibn Abbas connects it with priests and monks; Burhan says the Negus affirmed Abraham's faith. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BURHAN; ITQAN]

## 3:64/q21 How was the verse used in messages to rulers?  [* · reports]
turns on: The accounts name Heraclius, other rulers, and different responses.
- 3:64/q21/p1 The Prophet quoted it to Heraclius in a letter inviting Islam. — Abu Sufyan recounts the emperor's questions and his reading of prophetic signs; the letter promises double reward and warns of subjects' sins. Roman dignitaries protested. Bukhari, Muslim and other sources transmit it. [19 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(أبو سفيان); IBNABIHATIM(أبو سفيان بن حرب); THALABI(النبي ﷺ); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو سفيان بن حرب,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو سفيان); DURR-FULL(أبو سفيان); BURSEVI; TABATABAI(أبو سفيان); BUKHARI(أبو سفيان,ابن الناظور,عبد الله بن عباس,محمد); MUSLIM(أبو سفيان)]
- 3:64/q21/p2 The invitation also went to other unbelievers or rulers. — Ibn Abbas reports letters to unbelievers; Tabatabai mentions Muqawqis and a circulated Kufic manuscript; Itqan says first Abyssinia then Rome. Al-Izz cites the letter for Quranic wording outside recitation. [5 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(ابن عباس,قيل); ITQAN; BURHAN(الشيخ عز الدين)]
- 3:64/q21/p3 Heraclius recognized signs but did not openly convert. — Bursevi says he feared losing his crown and wrote that he believed Muhammad a prophet but could not leave Jesus' religion; Bukhari preserves his failed test of Roman allegiance. [6 notes: BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام,هرقل); BUKHARI(أبو سفيان,ابن الناظور)]
- 3:64/q21/p4 Khosrow tore up the Prophet's letter and lost his rule. — Bursevi says the envoy escaped an attempt on his life and the Prophet prayed for Khosrow's dominion to be torn apart. [1 notes: BURSEVI(رسول الله)]

## 3:64/q22 What happened when Najran refused mutual imprecation?  [* · reports]
- 3:64/q22/p1 The parties made a tribute pact after al-Aqib warned against the imprecation. — Muqatil gives one thousand garments in each of Safar and Rajab plus thirty old iron coats. The Prophet said imprecation would have destroyed the liars within a year; when Umar asked whose hands he would take, he named Ali, Fatima, Hasan, Husayn, Hafsa and Aisha. [8 notes: MUQATIL(العاقب,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عمر); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الزهري)]

## 3:64/q23 When could this verse have been revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The pre-conquest Heraclius letter conflicts with some late dates for Najran and jizya.
- 3:64/q23/p1 A year-nine or year-ten Najran visit implies a later revelation if it occasioned the verse. — Historians cited by Tabatabai give those dates; Ibn Kathir flags tension with the earlier Heraclius letter. [3 notes: TABATABAI(أبو الفداء وآخرون,الطبري وابن الأثير والمقريزي); IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 3:64/q23/p2 It was revealed twice. — Ibn Kathir proposes before Hudaybiyya and after the conquest; Ibn Hajar transmits the possibility. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI(ابن حجر)]
- 3:64/q23/p3 It was early, perhaps by year six or before year nine. — Tabatabai cites year-six letters and passage continuity; he argues Najran would not have been ignored while distant rulers were contacted. [3 notes: TABATABAI(المؤرخون)]
- 3:64/q23/p4 The verse was already revealed before the wider Najran passage. — Ibn Kathir questions the report that over eighty opening verses descended with Najran, given Abu Sufyan's earlier letter account. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 3:64/q23/p5 Najran came before Hudaybiyya and its payment was a settlement, not jizya. — Ibn Kathir compares later legislation confirming a precedent with the shares ruling after Abdullah ibn Jahsh's expedition. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 3:64/q23/p6 The Prophet used the wording before its revelation. — Ibn Kathir compares revelation later agreeing with Umar on the veil, captives, hypocrites' funeral prayer and Abraham's station. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 3:64/q23/p7 A reported letter before al-Naml implies a very early date, but is rejected. — Al-Bayhaqi transmits the letter; Tabatabai objects that al-Naml is Meccan and the jizya/war language is unexplained. [1 notes: TABATABAI(البيهقي); TABATABAI-]

## 3:64/q24 What details accompany the Abyssinian recitation?  [* · reports]
- 3:64/q24/p1 Jafar defended Muslim migrants while the Negus heard both sides. — Al-Khazin's transmitted account dates Quraysh's mission after Badr: Amr and Amara brought gifts and alleged a false prophet. Jafar sought fair hearing; Muslims said they prostrated only to God and used salam. The Negus recognized their account in scripture and heard each side. [9 notes: TABATABAI(النجاشي,جعفر بن أبي طالب,عمرو بن العاص,قريش); BURHAN]

## 3:64/q25 How does this verse connect to its surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: It follows proofs and imprecation and leads into the Abraham debate.
- 3:64/q25/p1 After proofs and refused imprecation, it returns to a gentle common appeal. — Razi, Baydawi, Biqai, Abu Hayyan and Ibn Ashur cite the Prophet's concern for their faith; Ibn Ashur explains the repeated address and lack of conjunction after 3:61. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 3:64/q25/p2 The Muslim declaration invokes Abraham's submission and prepares the next dispute. — Biqai cites 2:131-32 and 2:135; Ibn Ashur links Islam to Abraham's hanif faith through 16:123 and 3:67; Tabatabai calls it a new phase of address. [8 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 3:64/q26 What inward lesson is drawn?  [* · inward]
- 3:64/q26/p1 Turn intention, inner attention and reliance away from creatures and self toward God. — Tustari joins tawhid with lawful food, avoiding harm and obedient acts, compares its profession to water in 21:30, calls an unresponsive person dead, and narrates a crucified confessor turning to qibla. Qushayri cites Labid that all besides God passes; Sulami and Bursevi reject inward dependence on people. [18 notes: TUSTARI(الثوري); SULAMI(أبو عثمان,ابن عطاء,الواسطي); QUSHAYRI(لبيد); BURSEVI]

## 3:64/q27 What broader human or religious order does the verse teach?  [* · theology]
turns on: The sources stress human equality, social reform, and common ground.
- 3:64/q27/p1 Divine lordship grounds equal human standing, rights and resistance to domination. — Elmalili makes God's command the measure of allegiance, freedom and justice; Tabatabai reasons from shared human nature and illustrates prophetic rejection of oppression through Noah, Hud, Salih, Abraham, Moses and Jesus. [34 notes: ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi); TABATABAI(إبراهيم,ابن نوح,صالح,عيسى,محمد صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم,موسى,هود)]
- 3:64/q27/p2 Sound monotheistic belief begins moral reform and joins purified souls with lawful action. — Ibn Ashur says false belief breeds fear while sound belief supports self-respect and free thought, citing 16:97; Tabatabai says these clauses express the prophetic mission. [12 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 3:64/q27/p3 The shared principle invites universal, respectful dialogue and self-examination. — Kuran Yolu cites approved People of the Book (3:110, 3:113, 3:199), rejoicing at Christian victory (30:3-4), and the best manner of argument (29:46). It says safeguards against clerical domination help, but hostility to religion harms. [24 notes: ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi,Hz. Peygamber)]
