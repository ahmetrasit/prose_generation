# 80:27: 15 questions

## 80:27/q01 How does growing grain follow the splitting of the earth?  [فَأَنۢبَتْنَا · links]
turns on: What the preceding earth-splitting denotes and how its order after rain and before grain works.
- 80:27/q01/p1 The earth splits as plants grow through it, and the fāʾ marks grain as the ensuing stage of growth. — Bursevi says fīhā is the earth split by plants and the fāʾ marks succession. Al-Ālūsī says splitting widens progressively until grain forms; human plowing would interrupt the account of divine gifts. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows the interval required by the actual process. Ṭabāṭabāʾī traces rain, plant growth, and grain through 'then' and 'so'. Al-Ālūsī adds that the paired verbs and verbal nouns emphasize extraordinary divine provision. [6 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 80:27/q01/p2 The earlier splitting may be plowing or digging before the grain grows. — A view reported by al-Ālūsī defends plowing: the conjunction need not carry every restriction on the first action over to the second; plowing may be illustrative, or the word may mean digging, since plowing does not fit grapes, olives, and palms. Al-Ālūsī objects that intervening human plowing disrupts the sequence of divine gifts. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 80:27/q01/p3 The earlier splitting is the emergence of springs, with rivers flowing from the rain before crops grow. — Al-Ālūsī reports this interpretation, then objects that 'then' places splitting after the poured water. He also argues that crops following both rain and spring water fits poorly with 78:14–15, where rain alone brings forth grain. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 80:27/q02 Why are growing and the preceding acts attributed to God?  [فَأَنۢبَتْنَا · rhetoric]
- 80:27/q02/p1 The attribution expresses God's governance of the causes that produce water and plants. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the attribution of pouring, splitting, and growing a rational metaphor, though usage makes pouring and planting almost literal; God orders effective causes, sets their laws, and inspires people to use them. Al-Ālūsī says the paired verbs and verbal nouns convey an extraordinary divine provision beyond familiar custom and maintain the focus on God's act. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 80:27/q03 What does 'in it' refer to?  [فِيهَا · referent]
- 80:27/q03/p1 It refers to the earth in which God grows the grain. — Bursevi specifies the earth split by the emerging plants; Ṭabāṭabāʾī expressly identifies the pronoun as Earth. Al-Wāḥidī says God grows grain in the earth. [3 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 80:27/q04 What does 'grain' include here?  [حَبًّۭا · meaning]
- 80:27/q04/p1 It is grain generally: harvested crops and cereals used as food, especially staple grains. — Muqātil and Ibn Kathīr include all kinds. Al-Farrāʾ, al-Zajjāj, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī give wheat and barley; al-Ālūsī adds maize, Abū Ḥayyān adds corn and lentils, and al-Qurṭubī adds sult. Al-Zajjāj and al-Zamakhsharī stress what is harvested; al-Qurṭubī and al-Ālūsī add storage. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Nasafī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr stress human nourishment or staples. Al-Biqāʿī includes seeds eaten by both people and animals and says connected growth enables nourishment and development. Bursevī says the collective term may denote a little or a lot. Ibn ʿĀshūr relates the wording to the grain with seven ears in 2:261. [20 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 80:27/q05 Why is grain named first among the foods?  [حَبًّۭا · rhetoric]
- 80:27/q05/p1 Grain is the chief or foundational nourishment, with the other produce supplementing it. — Bursevi calls it the basic foundation of food; Iṣlāḥī calls it the chief nourishment and the remaining produce supplements. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 80:27/q06 What inward growth does this verse suggest?  [فَأَنۢبَتْنَا حَبًّۭا · inward]
- 80:27/q06/p1 God waters hearts with gnosis, bringing forth the flowers of unity and the lights of spiritual stripping. — This is al-Qushayrī's inward reading of the water and growth. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 80:27/q07 What is qadab in the surrounding food list, and who eats it?  [* · meaning]
turns on: Whether the cut growth is counted as human food or livestock feed.
- 80:27/q07/p1 It is greens cut after growing, eaten by people. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports a view that calls it greens because they are cut, and a view that people eat it and that it fattens women. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 80:27/q07/p2 It is cut fodder or herbage for livestock. — Al-Māwardī derives the name from cutting after it appears. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports a division assigning both qadab and abb to livestock. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 80:27/q08 How are the seven provisions apportioned between people and livestock?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether qadab is human food or livestock feed, and whether livestock itself counts as the seventh human provision.
- 80:27/q08/p1 Seven listed foods, including qadab, serve people; abb serves animals. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this division and notes that qadab fattens women; another reported view says qadab is cut greens eaten by people. Al-Qurṭubī identifies the seven provisions as crops through fruit and reserves abb for animals; he illustrates the seven stages of creation with 22:5. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 80:27/q08/p2 Qadab and abb feed livestock; six other foods feed people, and livestock itself is their seventh major provision. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this alternative count. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 80:27/q09 What sevenfold saying is reported from the Prophet alongside this verse?  [* · reports]
- 80:27/q09/p1 People were created from seven, provided for from seven, and directed to prostrate on seven parts. — Muqātil reports creation, provision, and being brought forth 'upon seven'; al-Qurṭubī reports seven creation stages, seven provisions, and prostration on seven parts. Their wording of the final seven varies. Al-Qurṭubī illustrates the creation stages with 22:5 and identifies the seven provisions as crops through fruit, with abb for animals. [3 notes: MUQATIL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 80:27/q10 What cautionary reports accompany interpretation of the food list?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports concern different speakers and incidents of restraint in interpretation.
- 80:27/q10/p1 ʿUmar declined to identify abb, treating speculation beyond the clear wording as needless strain. — Al-Durr reports through Anas that ʿUmar recognized the other foods, urged following what is clear and leaving the unknown to God, and cites al-Ḥākim's authentication. Ibn Kathīr explains that Abū Bakr and ʿUmar knew abb was a plant from the earth, as 80:28 and 80:31 show, but sought its precise nature. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(عمر)]
- 80:27/q10/p2 Ibn ʿAbbās refused to interpret a verse that others might have answered. — Ibn Kathīr reports the incident and grades its chain authentic. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 80:27/q10/p3 Jundub ibn ʿAbd Allāh rebuked Ṭulq ibn Ḥabīb for asking about a Quranic verse. — Ibn Kathīr reports that Jundub adjured Ṭulq, if Muslim, to leave or stop sitting with him. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(جندب بن عبد الله)]

## 80:27/q11 How is the vegetation across verses 27–30 described?  [* · interpretation]
- 80:27/q11/p1 The growth is entwined or closely interwoven. — Al-Naḥḥās gives this gloss for the description across verses 27–30. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 80:27/q12 How does the opening reading 'anā' frame the food account?  [* · readings]
- 80:27/q12/p1 It opens a detailed account of God's provision that remains necessarily incomplete. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says ordinary explanation cannot encompass the entire food system and its cosmic relations. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 80:27/q13 How is the produce in this part of the list arranged for observation?  [* · interpretation]
- 80:27/q13/p1 It begins with produce visible at ground level: grain, grapes, and vegetables. — Iṣlāḥī says seeing these items does not require looking upward. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 80:27/q14 What does the growth and food passage teach or invite?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw different lessons from the same visible provision: resurrection, gratitude and obedience, or reflection on creation and revelation.
- 80:27/q14/p1 Crop growth demonstrates resurrection and God's power to bring life after decay. — Al-Qurṭubī compares growth after decay with emergence from graves and also recalls divine favor. Al-Biqāʿī says human creation and the food cycle prove resurrection and God's power, making rejection astonishing. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 80:27/q14/p2 The sustaining blessings display God's favor and power, calling for faith, gratitude, worship, and obedience. — Al-Qurṭubī says crops recall God's favor. Kur'an Yolu says the passage recalls external blessings sustaining human life after the favors of creation in verses 18–20 and aims to strengthen faith and grateful obedience. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 80:27/q14/p3 Natural provisions invite reflection on divine wisdom and the connection of creation with revelation. — The Study Quran says creation is true and glorifies God; it cites 14:19, 6:73, 59:1, 61:1, 62:1, 64:1, 57:1, 59:24, 3:191, and 45:3, and notes an echo of 79:33. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 80:27/q15 How does this food passage connect with the sūra and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes connect the passage to different contexts: the sūra's resurrection warning, its prior account of human creation, other creation verses, or a grain parable.
- 80:27/q15/p1 It advances the sūra's call to warn receptive people by using creation and food as evidence of resurrection. — Al-Biqāʿī relates the warning of those who fear to 79:45 and says resurrection purifies receptive hearts while disbelief despite these signs is astonishing. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 80:27/q15/p2 It moves from the favors of human existence in verses 18–20 to outside blessings that sustain life. — Kur'an Yolu gives this sequence for the passage starting at 80:24. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 80:27/q15/p3 Its provisions echo 79:33 and the Quran's wider teaching that creation is true, glorifies God, and invites reflection. — The Study Quran cites 14:19, 6:73, 59:1, 61:1, 62:1, 64:1, 57:1, 59:24, 3:191, and 45:3. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 80:27/q15/p4 The word grain recalls the parable of a grain yielding seven ears in 2:261. — Ibn ʿĀshūr makes this verbal link in both notes. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
