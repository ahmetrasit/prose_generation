# 80:26: 10 questions

## 80:26/q01 What sequence does 'then' mark?  [ثُمَّ · links]
- 80:26/q01/p1 The earth's splitting follows the pouring of rain, and growth follows the splitting. — Bursevi explicitly takes thumma as placing the split after the pouring. Tabatabai says 'then' and the subsequent 'so' trace rain, growth, and grain. Alusi invokes this order against both plowing and the view that the split is the emergence of springs. Elmalılı describes rain stirring and swelling lifeless earth before it cracks and grows plants, citing 22:5. [5 notes: BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI]

## 80:26/q02 What does shaqq mean?  [شَقَقْنَا · meaning]
- 80:26/q02/p1 It means splitting or opening: separating what had been joined. — Jishumi treats shaqq, ṣadʿ, faṭr, and farj as synonymous terms for splitting or opening. Ibn Ashur defines shaqq as separating joined parts. [2 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 80:26/q03 What physical splitting of the earth is described?  [شَقَقْنَا ٱلْأَرْضَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the opening is made by emerging vegetation, agricultural work, conditions that prepare soil for rain, or the emergence of springs; the sequence after poured water is raised against some alternatives.
- 80:26/q03/p1 Plants emerge and open the earth above them. — Muqatil, Zajjaj, Thalabi, Mawardi, the two Wahidi works, Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Qurtubi, Nasafi, Durr's Ibn Abbas, and others identify the split with vegetation growing through the ground. Jishumi says the fragile plant roots and sends up its shoot; his alternative accounts differ on whether God directly parts the soil or forces in the plant push it aside. Ibn Kathir details water entering deep soil and permeating seeds before they sprout and rise. Biqai stresses fragile plants piercing even hard earth in diverse regions after rain. Bursevi says the split follows rain. Alusi transmits Ibn Abbas's account of a wondrous opening matched to each plant's size, shape, form, and appearance. Elmalılı says rain makes dry earth stir, swell, crack, and produce plants, citing 22:5. Tabatabai reads the sequence as rain, growth, then grain. Study Quran points to comparable vegetation and revived-earth passages at 6:99, 29:63, 30:24, 31:10, 35:27, and 39:21. Kashshaf and Baydawi give this alongside a plowing interpretation. [24 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 80:26/q03/p2 The earth is opened in plowing or digging. — Kashshaf specifically mentions furrows cut by ox-drawn plows, while Baydawi allows plowing beside plant growth. Alusi reports this interpretation and notes that thumma's order counts against it; a reported defense says the conjunction need not transfer every limitation to the following action, and that plowing may be illustrative or mean digging, since it does not suit grapes, olives, or palms. Ibn Ashur includes a plow or hoe among things that break the surface to let later rain enter it. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 80:26/q03/p3 The soil surface opens through water or summer sun, allowing autumn and winter rain to enter. — Ibn Ashur gives water and summer sun alongside plow and hoe as ways the joined surface is separated; on this account the opening prepares the ground for later rain. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 80:26/q03/p4 The split is the opening through which springs and rivers flow. — Alusi reports a proposal taking the poured water as rain and the rivers as its flow. A reported defense says spring water itself comes from rain rather than underground stores of vapor. Alusi objects that thumma puts the splitting after the water and calls the defense far-fetched and contrived. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]

## 80:26/q04 How is the ground parted as the plant emerges?  [شَقَقْنَا ٱلْأَرْضَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether God parts the soil directly or the plant's growth is the intermediary cause.
- 80:26/q04/p1 God directly separates the earth's parts and brings out the crop without an intermediary cause. — Jishumi reports this as the first account and explicitly contrasts an unmediated divine act with an act through a cause. [2 notes: JISHUMI]
- 80:26/q04/p2 Forces in the growing plant push soil aside as an intermediary cause. — Jishumi gives this as the second account and distinguishes it from direct separation. Ibn Kathir describes water penetrating embedded seeds, after which they sprout, rise, and emerge; Biqai notes that even fragile plants break through very hard earth. [4 notes: JISHUMI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 80:26/q05 Why is the splitting attributed to God?  [شَقَقْنَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether producing or governing a cause warrants attribution of its action, or proper attribution requires the action to subsist in the named agent.
- 80:26/q05/p1 God is the ultimate cause or true creator of the effective causes, so the action can be ascribed to Him figuratively. — Kashshaf attributes either plant openings or plow furrows to God as ultimate cause; Baydawi likewise calls Him their cause. Alusi's reported plowing view calls the attribution figurative while saying God is the true creator. Ibn Ashur calls the attribution of pouring, splitting, and growing a rational metaphor: God orders the causes, establishes their laws, and inspires human use of them; conventional usage has made the attribution of pouring and planting almost literal. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 80:26/q05/p2 Properly, an action belongs to the agent in whom that action subsists, rather than to one producing it in someone else. — Alusi appeals to the derivation of an agent's active participle from the action, challenging the account that makes an agent of another's action its proper subject. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 80:26/q06 What does the inward reading make of the earth's splitting?  [ٱلْأَرْضَ شَقَقْنَا · inward]
turns on: Which spiritual growth the earth-and-water image is used to explain: blossoming inner qualities or a hard heart softened for repentance.
- 80:26/q06/p1 God opens the heart so spirit, intellect, faith, and gnosis blossom within it. — Tustari identifies the earth with the heart. He also reports the Prophet comparing the Qur'an to a blossom in hearts, faith to rain planting richness, and greed to dew producing hypocrisy like grass. [2 notes: TUSTARI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 80:26/q06/p2 God's rain of mercy softens hardened hearts for repentance. — Qushayri makes the rain mercy and the formerly hard earth the heart rendered receptive to repentance. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 80:26/q07 What does the accusative shaqqan convey?  [شَقًّۭا · grammar]
- 80:26/q07/p1 It is a cognate accusative emphasizing the splitting, with indefiniteness conveying its wondrous magnitude. — Ibn Ashur treats shaqqan with ṣabban as an emphatic cognate accusative; their indefinite forms invite magnification and wonder appropriate to each act. Bursevi and the Ibn Abbas report transmitted by Alusi describe the wonder as an opening fitted to the varied sizes, shapes, forms, and appearances of plants. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 80:26/q08 How is 'wal-arḍa shaqqan' pronounced at the junction?  [ٱلْأَرْضَ شَقًّۭا · readings]
turns on: The isolated report of assimilation versus the majority pronunciation, with Nashr noting that the sequence of sukūn and fatḥa makes this junction light.
- 80:26/q08/p1 The majority practice is the light pronunciation without the isolated assimilation. — Nashr distinguishes this junction from the first phrase: the first needs care to avoid a repeated sound, whereas this one is light because a fatḥa follows a sukūn. He says practice follows the majority. [1 notes: NASHR+]
- 80:26/q08/p2 An isolated chain reports assimilation at this junction. — Nashr records the lone chain but contrasts it with majority practice. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 80:26/q09 What Prophetic report connects human creation, provision, and emergence?  [* · reports]
- 80:26/q09/p1 People were created from seven, provided for from seven, and brought forth upon seven. — Muqatil reports this wording from the Prophet without further specification in the note. [1 notes: MUQATIL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 80:26/q10 How does the split earth serve the passage's wider argument?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes emphasize either the provision that calls for gratitude or its role as evidence for resurrection and accountability; these functions can coexist.
- 80:26/q10/p1 The ordered provision of food displays God's power and mercy and calls for faith, gratitude, worship, and obedience. — Tabatabai says the open reading anā introduces a necessarily incomplete account of God's provisioning because ordinary explanation cannot encompass its cosmic relations. Islahi says rain and opened earth pores are conditions no one else can provide. Kuranyolu says verses 18–20 discussed favors in human existence and nature, while this passage turns to external blessings sustaining life; its reminders strengthen faith and move people to grateful worship and obedience. Study Quran cites related descriptions of vegetation and water-revived earth at 6:99, 29:63, 30:24, 31:10, 35:27, and 39:21. [5 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 80:26/q10/p2 The food cycle, alongside human creation, demonstrates the possibility of resurrection and points toward accountability. — Biqai relates the call to warn those who fear at 79:45 to resurrection and receptive hearts: human creation and food make rejection astonishing. Islahi says the food argument parallels the creation argument, both showing resurrection possible through providential signs and ending in reward or punishment; he cites 21:30. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
