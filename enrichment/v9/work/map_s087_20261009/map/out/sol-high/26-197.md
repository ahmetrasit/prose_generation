# 26:197: 22 questions

## 26:197/q01 What does the opening interrogative and conjunction convey?  [أَوَلَمْ · rhetoric]
- 26:197/q01/p1 The question rebukes denial or seeks acknowledgment of an overlooked sign; the waw connects it to an implicit contextual clause. — Bursevi says the hamza negates a negative and the waw coordinates with an omitted clause. Al-Alusi allows confirmation or denial and negation, suggesting an implied question about overlooking the sign. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 26:197/q02 Who are 'them' for whom this is a sign?  [لَّهُمْ · referent]
- 26:197/q02/p1 The Meccan polytheists, Quraysh, or more generally deniers of Muhammad's prophethood. — Muqatil, al-Tha'labi and others address Meccans; Bursevi specifies Quraysh. Asad broadens the description to disbelievers in Muhammad's prophethood. [6 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES; ELMALILI]

## 26:197/q03 How does 'for them' function in the sentence?  [لَّهُمْ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical analysis of kāna and its subject and predicate.
- 26:197/q03/p1 It attaches circumstantially to 'sign'. — Bursevi calls it circumstantial; al-Samin attaches it as a circumstantial element to āya in the majority reading. [2 notes: BURSEVI; SAMIN-DURR]
- 26:197/q03/p2 It attaches to the verb of being and is advanced before the subject to foreground the sign. — Al-Alusi says the advanced phrase emphasizes that descriptions in their books make the scholars' knowledge a sign. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:197/q03/p3 On some feminine parses it is the predicate, or attaches to a complete verb of being. — Al-Baydawi allows lahum as predicate with āya subject; al-Nasafi and al-Samin give fronted-predicate and complete-verb analyses. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 26:197/q04 What is the sign in this verse?  [ءَايَةً · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'sign' names the scholars' recognition, the Qur'an or Prophet recognized, or a particular scholar's belief.
- 26:197/q04/p1 Israelite scholars' knowledge or recognition is the sign confirming the Qur'an's truth and Muhammad's prophethood. — Al-Tabari says their knowledge of the Qur'an's truth confirms Muhammad as messenger. Al-Zajjaj cites his description in Torah and Gospel. Al-Qurtubi and al-Biqa'i say Quraysh trusted the People of the Book for religious reports. Al-Biqa'i adds Torah, Gospel, Psalms and other books; their alteration did not cancel their evidentiary value for adherents. Ibn Ashur calls this a third commendation of the Qur'an; al-Tabataba'i says Jews used their predictions to claim advantage over Arabs. Islahi says opponents thereby lack a further excuse and describes their selective trust in supportive unbelievers. The sign can attest either Qur'an or prophethood, as al-Baydawi explicitly says. [24 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:197/q04/p2 The Qur'an itself is the sign. — Mubashshir ibn Ubayd gives this identification in both Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Durr. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مبشر بن عبيد); DURR-FULL(مبشر بن عبيد القرشي)]
- 26:197/q04/p3 Muhammad himself is the sign, recognized from the earlier scriptures. — Qatada says the Prophet was the sign and that Israelite scholars recognized his scriptural description. [2 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة)]
- 26:197/q04/p4 Abd Allah ibn Salam's belief in Muhammad's Book exemplifies the intended sign. — Ibn Abbas calls him one of Israel's best scholars; his acceptance of the Book witnesses to its truth. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 26:197/q06 How is the clause involving 'was a sign' parsed?  [يَكُن ءَايَةً أَن يَعْلَمَهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether kāna is complete, which expression supplies its subject and predicate, and whether feminine agreement follows grammar or meaning.
- 26:197/q06/p1 With masculine yakun and accusative āyatan, the infinitive clause is the subject and āyatan the advanced predicate. — Ibn Khalawayh says the scholars' knowledge is definite; al-Samin says lahum is circumstantial to āya. Bursevi says fronting emphasizes the sign. Al-Burhan gives a pronoun-of-affair explanation for the cited reading. [11 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; JISHUMI(الباقون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:197/q06/p2 With feminine takun and nominative āya, āya is subject and the scholars' knowledge clause is predicate. — Ibn Khalawayh, al-Zajjaj, al-Qurtubi, al-Biqa'i and others give this direct analysis. Al-Zamakhshari, as reported by Abu Hayyan, finds an indefinite subject before a definite predicate awkward; al-Samin says this is unnecessary and rejects its defense as poetic necessity. Al-Alusi calls the order weak. [8 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; JISHUMI(ابن عامر); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عامر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عامر); NASAFI-FULL(الشامي); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عامر); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عامر والجحدري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عامر والجحدري)-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 26:197/q06/p3 A feminine verb can contain an implicit story or affair pronoun, with the following clause explaining it. — Al-Farisi cites 21:97 and 22:46 for feminine agreement with a feminine expression within the explanatory clause. Al-Wahidi and al-Tabrisi report the implicit story; al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi and al-Samin give affair-pronoun versions. Al-Samin varies the placement of lahum and āya. [9 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI(أبو علي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عامر والجحدري); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عامر والجحدري)]
- 26:197/q06/p4 The infinitive clause itself is raised by feminine takun because its meaning is the feminine 'sign'. — Al-Farisi, al-Wahidi and Abu Ali in al-Tabrisi cite semantic agreement; they compare 6:160 and a reading of 6:23. [3 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI(أبو علي)]
- 26:197/q06/p5 Feminine takun can be a complete verb, with āya its subject and the knowledge clause apposition or an omitted subject's predicate. — Al-Nasafi mentions complete kāna; al-Samin says lahum attaches to it. Al-Baydawi and al-Samin also give an incomplete-verb parse with lahum predicate, āya subject, and the clause appositive or supplied predicate. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 26:197/q06/p6 Feminine takun with accusative āyatan uses agreement by meaning, with a feminine predicate or understood verbal noun. — Al-Zajjaj compares 6:23; Abu Hayyan allows agreement with the predicate or construed feminine noun; al-Samin cites 6:23 and poetry; al-Alusi cites Labid's wild-ass verse and rejects deriving agreement from the genitive noun because the usual condition is absent. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,لبيد)]

## 26:197/q07 What does the object pronoun in 'know it' refer to?  [يَعْلَمَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Which nearby discourse item the singular object pronoun resumes.
- 26:197/q07/p1 The Qur'an, its mention, or its described qualities in prior scripture. — Al-Alusi explicitly prefers the Qur'an; Ibn Ashur says it may mean knowing its mention. Al-Tabari and al-Baydawi relate knowledge to the Qur'an's truth or descriptions. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; MUJAHID(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 26:197/q07/p2 Muhammad, whose attributes and prophethood were foretold. — Qatada takes this object; several explanations say Israelite scholars knew him from the Torah and Gospel. [6 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 26:197/q07/p3 The preceding assertion that the Qur'an is revelation brought by the faithful Spirit, or that it occurs in earlier writings. — Al-Alusi calls the full preceding-statement referent remote. Ibn Ashur permits the preceding claim of its presence in earlier writings, referring to 26:196. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]

## 26:197/q08 What did the Israelite scholars know?  [يَعْلَمَهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the knowledge centers on Muhammad's predicted attributes, the Qur'an, or both.
- 26:197/q08/p1 Their scriptures described Muhammad, his time, mission, and prophetic qualities. — Qatada says Torah and Gospel name him as God's Messenger; reports from Jews say the Torah describes him and his time. Ibn Ashur adds verification that the bearer actually matches those qualities. Islahi says most concealed these predictions. Al-Tabataba'i says Jewish communities used them for glad tidings and advantage over Arabs. [11 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:197/q08/p2 Their books foretold the Qur'an's advent or contained its described qualities and teachings. — Al-Tabari says the earlier-scripture wording is general in form but concerns some books revealed to some messengers. Al-Nasafi quotes the scholars' affirmation at 28:53. Ibn Ashur also includes knowledge of contents drawn from their scriptures. Islahi says earlier scriptures predicted the Qur'an; Kuran Yolu says some recognized agreement between Quranic accounts and Torah, citing 29:47 for People of the Book who believed. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 26:197/q10 Who are the learned among the Children of Israel?  [عُلَمَٰٓؤُا۟ بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · referent]

Asad adds converts he names as Abd Allah ibn Salim and Ka'b ibn Malik in the Prophet's lifetime, Ka'b al-Ahbar and Yemenite peers under Umar, and later converts. Study Quran notes Ibn Salam's high standing but objects that his conversion followed migration while the sura is Meccan. - 26:197/q10/p3 All contemporary scholars of Jewish scripture, whether they converted or not. — Al-Qurtubi says the wording covers any knowledgeable person. Al-Alusi and Kuran Yolu prefer this scope; Kuran Yolu points to Meccan-era acknowledgment of Torah and Quranic accounts and 29:47. [7 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 26:197/q10/p4 Israelite prophets who foretold Muhammad's traits, time and place. — Abu Hayyan reports this alternative. Al-Alusi reports it but says it is not apparent from the wording. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]

## 26:197/q11 Why are Israelite scholars singled out?  [عُلَمَٰٓؤُا۟ بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · interpretation]
- 26:197/q11/p1 They were recognized scriptural authorities whom Arabs consulted, so their knowledge had force for Quraysh. — Al-Qurtubi and al-Biqa'i say the polytheists relied on them for religious knowledge. Islahi says Arabs considered People of the Book religiously superior. Asad says Jewish scripture survives, though corrupted, while Jesus's original revelation was lost and so could not attest the common fundamentals. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES]

## 26:197/q12 Why is 'scholars' written with an extra waw?  [عُلَمَٰٓؤُا۟ · grammar]
- 26:197/q12/p1 The rasm records a dialectal inclination of final alif toward waw. — Abu Hayyan notes the Medinan codex and parallels in prayer, alms and usury. Al-Samin and al-Alusi likewise compare spellings of prayer and alms. Al-Nasafi records the spelling without explaining it. [4 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 26:197/q13 What consultation of Jewish scholars is reported behind this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the consulted rabbis plainly confirmed Muhammad or gave a confused answer.
- 26:197/q13/p1 Meccans asked Jews in Medina or Yathrib about Muhammad; they said his time had come and the Torah described him, confirming his truth. — Ibn Abbas is the recurring transmitter; Kuran Yolu says emissaries were sent and Study Quran reports the answer from at least some People of the Book. Bursevi describes the question as about his mission. [8 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما); BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Kurtubî, Ebû Hayyân); STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 26:197/q13/p2 Meccans asked Yathrib's rabbis, who described the Prophet's time and qualities but became confused about Muhammad; the verse followed. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan trace this form to al-Tha'labi from Ibn Abbas; al-Alusi likewise says their answer became mixed up and the verse was revealed. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 26:197/q14 What effect did Israelite testimony have among the Arabs?  [* · reports]
- 26:197/q14/p1 Their scriptural reports and predictions helped bring the Aws and Khazraj to Islam. — Al-Tabrisi reports this with Ibn Abbas's identification of Ibn Salam and companions, and says Israelites told Arabs of the Qur'an's mention and foretold the Prophet. [2 notes: TABRISI(ابن عباس)]

## 26:197/q15 Was this verse revealed in Mecca or Medina?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether mention of Israelite scholars, especially later converts, requires a Medinan setting or fits the Meccan context and reported consultation.
- 26:197/q15/p1 It is Meccan, in line with the sura's setting and a prior Jewish consultation report. — Ibn Atiyya prefers Meccan and says the Torah already described the unlettered Prophet; Abu Hayyan prefers the Meccan setting for the occasion report; al-Alusi says context and that report support it. Asad rejects Suyuti's Medina inference from the mention of Israelite scholars, since Meccan revelations make such references too. Ibn Atiyya reports the majority date the whole sura Meccan, while Muqatil excepts the poets verse 26:224. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); ALUSI-FULL(مقاتل); ASAD-NOTES; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(القاضي أبو محمد)+]
- 26:197/q15/p2 Verse 197 is Medinan. — Muqatil is cited by Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi; Study Quran also reports Āl's Medinan classification. Asad attributes a Medina dating to Suyuti and rejects its argument from mention of Israelite scholars. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Āl); IBNATIYYA-FULL(القاضي أبو محمد)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(مقاتل)-; ASAD-NOTES-]

## 26:197/q16 How does this verse connect with the preceding passage and sura?  [* · links]
- 26:197/q16/p1 It confirms the prior statement that earlier writings attest the Qur'an and adds a fresh commendation of it. — Ibn Ashur calls it the third commendation and says the conjunction signals a new commendation as well as supporting evidence; if it were merely evidence, coordination would be unexpected. Islahi links the ending to the opening warnings and prophetic histories, and says his reading has parallels in earlier and later suras. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:197/q17 Can this passage justify reciting the Qur'an in a non-Arabic language?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether translated meaning counts as the original Qur'an and preserves its eloquence.
- 26:197/q17/p1 Some attribute to jurists a deduction allowing non-Arabic recitation. — Islahi reports the attribution without naming the jurists; he suggests it may rest on misunderstanding. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some people); ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 26:197/q17/p2 A translation cannot replace Arabic Qur'anic recitation. — Islahi says no translated verse or sura is the Qur'an; even an excellent translator cannot carry all its meaning and eloquence. He says translations displaced original scriptures when their languages died out, and alleges Saint Paul manipulated translations. He calls the contrary deduction groundless and gravely consequential. [6 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 26:197/q18 How should readers determine grammatical meaning in a text?  [* · grammar]
- 26:197/q18/p1 Words become intelligible through assigned syntactic relations and the intended rhetorical aim. — Al-Jurjani says thought first relates verbs and nouns as governor, subject, object, predicate or modifier. Rearranging qifā nabki to block those links makes interpretation impossible. Lexical senses may be compared after roles are assigned; Bishr's simile illustrates annexation, attachment, coordination, predication and a descriptive night clause. [4 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 26:197/q19 How should correspondence between verses and suras be studied?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether event-based revelation limits the search for textual links despite the deliberately arranged mushaf.
- 26:197/q19/p1 Ask whether a verse completes its predecessor or stands independently, then examine its connection; apply this to suras. — Al-Burhan credits Abu Bakr al-Naysaburi with publicizing such questions in Baghdad. Al-Burhan and al-Itqan give the method; al-Razi calls Quranic arrangement miraculous alongside eloquence and meaning and criticizes exegetes who overlook it. [4 notes: BURHAN(أبو الحسن الشهرابانى); ITQAN(الرازي)]
- 26:197/q19/p2 Correspondence is useful for one connected discourse but strained across revelations with different causes and rulings. — Ibn Abd al-Salam gives this limitation. [1 notes: BURHAN(عز الدين بن عبد السلام)]
- 26:197/q19/p3 The wise, prophetically instructed order of the mushaf warrants seeking correspondences despite event-based revelation. — Exacting scholars in al-Burhan distinguish event order from mushaf order, compare 11:1, and call arrangement part of inimitability; al-Itqan repeats the heavenly-archetype and prophetic-instruction account. [2 notes: BURHAN(بعض مشايخنا المحققين); ITQAN]

## 26:197/q20 What are the biographical reports about early reciters here?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports concern different people and incidents.
- 26:197/q20/p1 Abu Ja'far and Shayba were linked through Medina recitation teaching and Shayba's marriage to Abu Ja'far's daughter. — Ibn Mujahid dates al-Harra fifty-three years after the Prophet's death. He says Shayba, Umm Salama's client, met her and A'isha; Ya'qub says Shayba and Abu Ja'far taught in the Prophet's Mosque before al-Harra and A'isha prayed for his learning. Sulayman reports Umm Salama blessed him as a child. Abu Ja'far valued his Qur'an over wealthier suitors, and people predicted a mushaf would be born from the marriage. Al-Mufaddal says Shayba led Sukayna's funeral prayer. [7 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو جعفر,المفضل بن غسان,الناس,سليمان بن مسلم,يعقوب بن جعفر)]
- 26:197/q20/p2 A mark seen on Abu Ja'far after death was taken for the light of the Qur'an. — Nafi reports a page-like mark between his neck and heart observed by those washing him. [1 notes: IBNMUJAHID(نافع بن أبي نعيم)]
- 26:197/q20/p3 Muslim ibn Jundab was an eloquent Medinan preacher who reported from al-Zubayr and Ibn Umar. — Ibn Mujahid supplies these biographical details. [1 notes: IBNMUJAHID]

## 26:197/q21 How did recitation teachers test students for authorization?  [* · reports]
- 26:197/q21/p1 Teachers marked difficult passages and tested them individually; failure required repeating the entire reading and test. — Al-Nashr says the practice transmitted knowledge carefully and encouraged progress.
