# 39:18: 19 questions

## 39:18/q01 How is the word before الذين read and joined to it?  [ٱلَّذِينَ · readings]
turns on: Whether and how the possessive yāʾ is pronounced.
- 39:18/q01/p1 عبادي with possessive yāʾ and fatḥa in connection. — Al-Fārisī reports Abū ʿAmr, Ibn Kathīr and the Meccans through ʿUbayd, Shibl and al-Qaṭʿī; ʿUbayd pauses at the verse ending but connects عبادي الذين otherwise. [2 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو,ابن كثير وأهل مكة)]
- 39:18/q01/p2 عباد الذين without possessive yāʾ. — Al-Fārisī reports Qunbul through al-Nabbāl and Ibn Kathīr's companions with kasra on dāl; the remaining readers also omit yāʾ. [2 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(الباقون,قنبل)]
- 39:18/q01/p3 Both leaving yāʾ quiescent and vocalizing it are acceptable. — Al-Fārisī permits both pronunciations. [1 notes: FARISI-HUJJA]

## 39:18/q02 How does الذين يستمعون connect to the preceding verse?  [ٱلَّذِينَ يَسْتَمِعُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the relative clause attaches backward or begins after a pause.
- 39:18/q02/p1 It is an adjective of the preceding 'My servants'. — Abū Ḥayyān allows it; al-Samīn calls it apparent; al-Ālūsī prefers continuity because it completes the preceding thought and clarifies who follows the best. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 39:18/q02/p2 It is a substitute or explanatory apposition to 'My servants'. — Al-Samīn allows badal or ʿaṭf bayān. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 39:18/q02/p3 It begins a new subject, with أولئك الذين هداهم الله as predicate. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī report a pause after عبادي; Ibn ʿĀshūr treats the clause as a fresh sentence. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:18/q03 Who are the listening servants?  [ٱلَّذِينَ يَسْتَمِعُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the description is general, continues 39:17, or concerns particular reported recipients.
- 39:18/q03/p1 They are those who shunned false worship and turned to God in 39:17. — Al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī read the description as identifying the recipients of glad tidings; al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī require both devotion and discernment. Kur'an Yolu adds escape from misleading pressure. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:18/q03/p2 They are generally anyone who hears, weighs and follows the best speech. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya favors a broad description encompassing narrower examples. Al-Rāzī reports a view avoiding exclusion of most people from mercy. Bursevī includes any speech; Ṭabāṭabāʾ rejects unsupported restrictions. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(منهم); BURSEVI; TABATABAI(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 39:18/q03/p3 They are Zayd ibn ʿAmr, Abū Dharr and Salmān, pre-Islamic monotheists. — Ibn Zayd and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd report that the three avoided idols, affirmed God's oneness, and followed the best teaching or reason available without scripture or prophet. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد); THALABI(ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن زيد); TABRISI(عبد الرحمن بن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن زيد)]
- 39:18/q03/p4 They are Abū Bakr's early converts: ʿUthmān, ʿAbd al-Raḥmān, Ṭalḥa, al-Zubayr, Saʿīd and Saʿd. — ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās includes Abū Bakr as first believer; other reports list the six, who heard his profession and believed. [5 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB(عطاء عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(المهدوي,كشف الأسرار); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 39:18/q03/p5 They include an Anṣārī who freed seven slaves after hearing of Hell's seven gates. — Jābir reports that after 15:44 he freed one for each gate and received this verse as good news. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله)]

## 39:18/q04 Why describe the listeners explicitly rather than use a pronoun?  [ٱلَّذِينَ يَسْتَمِعُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The rhetorical force assigned to the explicit description.
- 39:18/q04/p1 It identifies the promised servants and highlights attentive discernment. — Al-Rāzī links wholehearted devotion to listening and following; al-Bayḍāwī stresses discerning truth, al-Nasafī the combination of repentance and judgment, Abū Ḥayyān careful hearing. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 39:18/q04/p2 It singles out people of exceptional standing. — Al-Biqāʿī calls them chosen people of high aspiration; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the fresh sentence draws attention to their deserving divine care. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:18/q05 What speech do they hear?  [ٱلْقَوْل · referent]
turns on: Whether al-qawl is scripture, a broader field of utterance, or a historically specific hearing.
- 39:18/q05/p1 The Qur'an or God's Book. — Muqātil, Muqātil and Yaḥyā as reported by al-Māwardī, al-Baghawī and al-Wāḥidī identify the saying as scripture; Abū Ḥayyān reports this with Qur'anic pardon and forbearance. [5 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل ويحيى بن سلام); BAGHAWI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 39:18/q05/p2 Speech broadly, including guidance and error from any speaker. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya praises weighing all kinds. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the definite noun generic, including Qur'an, prophetic instruction and fabrication. Al-Biqāʿī urges hearing every speaker. Bursevī includes God, angels, humans, Satan and the self; human speech varies in truth and goodness. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(بحر الحقائق); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:18/q05/p3 The Qur'an alongside other speech. — Al-Zajjāj and many later interpreters use this comparison; Ṭabāṭabāʾ rejects making it an exclusive restriction. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج); TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 39:18/q05/p4 God's commands and competing lawful courses, sometimes also prophetic instruction. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Kashshāf compare pardon and retaliation; al-Qāshānī includes duties, recommendations, strictness and dispensations from God or others; al-Qurṭubī includes the Messenger's statements and clearer rulings. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; QASHANI; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 39:18/q05/p5 Earlier communities' sayings heard by people without divine scripture. — Ibn Zayd is reported by al-Māwardī. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد)]
- 39:18/q05/p6 Speech connected with action, whose best form surely guides and counsels. — Ṭabāṭabāʾ infers the action connection from 'they follow'. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 39:18/q06 How do they listen?  [يَسْتَمِعُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether their listening is characterized chiefly as receptivity or critical comparison.
- 39:18/q06/p1 Attentively and openly, seeking truth before judgment. — Al-Biqāʿī contrasts wholehearted hearing with stubborn dismissal; Ṭabāṭabāʾ says they fear missing truth and reflect instead of obeying desire; Iṣlāḥī contrasts careful hearing with aggression; Abū Ḥayyān stresses attentive listening. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 39:18/q06/p2 They hear rival claims for rational comparison. — Al-Rāzī says hearing is common to all while reason distinguishes the soundest; Kur'an Yolu reports his appeal to rational proof. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]

## 39:18/q07 What does أَحْسَنَهُ mean as a comparison?  [أَحْسَنَهُۥٓ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the form grades good alternatives or serves as simple or emphatic praise.
- 39:18/q07/p1 A truly better, more fitting option among good ones. — Al-Qushayrī contrasts permissible good with more fitting best; Ṭabāṭabāʾ says love of goodness moves one from good to better; al-Ṭabrisī stresses worthiness of acceptance and action. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; TABATABAI; TABRISI]
- 39:18/q07/p2 Simply good. — Al-Qushayrī reports this sense of 'best'. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 39:18/q07/p3 An intensified praise, without ranking two good sayings. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Joseph's 'prison is dearer' at 12:33. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:18/q08 What do they follow as the best speech or course?  [فَيَتَّبِعُونَ أَحْسَنَهُۥٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'best' picks true speech, scripture, obedience, a superior lawful course or a specific confession.
- 39:18/q08/p1 Whatever is truest, most guiding and best supported. — Al-Ṭabarī stresses oneness and obedience; al-Thaʿlabī and al-Ṭabrisī, truth-guiding speech; Ibn ʿAṭiyya favors broad discernment, encompassing the examples, as does al-Baghawī's conclusion that all given explanations are good. Al-Biqāʿī contrasts reason with desire; al-Rāghib says furthest from doubt; Ṭabāṭabāʾ includes truth over falsehood and greater over lesser guidance. [19 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Z); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 39:18/q08/p2 The Qur'an, preferred to other discourse. — Al-Zajjāj and many others give this reading. Al-Biqāʿī says the Qur'an surpasses other good revealed books in expression, meaning and arrangement; Bursevī cites 39:23. Ṭabāṭabāʾ rejects restricting the verse to it. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج); BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 39:18/q08/p3 Obedience to God rather than prohibited conduct. — Muqātil cites 39:55. Qatāda is transmitted by al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Māwardī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Durr; al-Ḍaḥḥāk specifies obedience commanded to prophets. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(الضحاك,قتادة)]
- 39:18/q08/p4 The best of what they are commanded, understood and acted upon. — Al-Suddī is reported by al-Ṭabarī, al-Māwardī, al-Baghawī and al-Ṭabrisī; al-Thaʿlabī speaks of hoped-for action. Ibn Kathīr and Study Quran compare Moses' command to take the Torah's best at 7:145. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); THALABI(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); TABRISI(السدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 39:18/q08/p5 Pardon, forbearance, patience and discreet charity over lawful alternatives. — Al-Zajjāj cites 42:43 and 42:41; al-Kashshāf adds 2:237 and 2:271. Al-Rāzī contrasts retaliation, blood money and pardon; Bursevī contrasts kindness with matching injury. Ṭabāṭabāʾ rejects restriction to such choices. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI(بعضهم); TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 39:18/q08/p6 Obligations or strict duties over recommendations or dispensations, and recommendations over the merely permitted. — Al-Māwardī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Qāshānī mention duties or strictness; al-Kashshāf, al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī include the more rewarding recommendation. Al-Rāzī's fuller prayer has opening intention, Fātiḥa, tranquility, tashahhud and salutation; Bursevī gives washing thrice and glorification. [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(Z)]
- 39:18/q08/p7 The best report from a gathering, withholding its bad parts. — Ibn ʿAbbās is transmitted by al-Māwardī, al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān; al-Nasafī gives the conversational use. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL]
- 39:18/q08/p8 The confession that there is no god but God. — Ibn Zayd and al-Qurṭubī apply this to the three early monotheists; Bursevī's Kashf al-Asrār applies it to Abū Bakr's listeners. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار)]
- 39:18/q08/p9 Islam after hearing Muslims and polytheists. — Al-Naqqāsh, reported by al-Māwardī, names this choice. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(النقاش)]
- 39:18/q08/p10 Revelation understood and put into practice. — Ibn Kathīr and Study Quran invoke Moses and 7:145; Iṣlāḥī says they follow every sound thing; Study Quran connects understanding with acting on revelation, citing 39:9 and 5:100. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:18/q08/p11 Speech or action devoted solely to God, especially pure remembrance. — Al-Qushayrī gives both possibilities. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 39:18/q08/p12 The best evidenced legal view or strongest proof in each science. — Al-Kashshāf applies examination to schools of law; Ibn ʿĀshūr and an attributed saying apply it to soundest argument and each field. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل)]

## 39:18/q09 Can Qur'anic passages be intrinsically better than one another?  [أَحْسَنَهُۥٓ · theology]
turns on: Whether superiority concerns divine speech itself, its topic, its reward or its application.
- 39:18/q09/p1 Yes, passages about God are superior by their subject. — Bursevī prefers the investigators' view: al-Ikhlāṣ names divine oneness and attributes while al-Masad's merit is God's speech. He cites abundant special virtue reports and al-Ghazālī's Prophetic reports on Yā Sīn, al-Fātiḥa, the Throne Verse and al-Ikhlāṣ. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الغزالي); BURSEVI(المحققون)+]
- 39:18/q09/p2 No, the eternal divine speech is equal; reward or practical fit can differ. — Bursevī reports al-Ashʿarī's fear that ranking implies inferiority elsewhere and explains 'best surah' by differing reward. Another reported view makes Qur'anic pardon, forgiveness or patience better depending on the actor. [3 notes: BURSEVI(أبو الحسن الأشعري,بعضهم)]

## 39:18/q10 What does God's guidance of them consist in?  [هَدَىٰهُمُ ٱللَّهُ · theology]
turns on: How divine agency, human receptivity and human action relate.
- 39:18/q10/p1 God guides them to religion, truth and what pleases Him. — Muqātil and al-Bayḍāwī say His religion; al-Ṭabrisī says they follow guidance to truth; al-Qurṭubī says what pleases Him; Ibn Kathīr extends it through this world and the next; Iṣlāḥī links a truth-loving attitude to God's gift. [6 notes: MUQATIL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:18/q10/p2 God causes guidance; the soul receives it but cannot originate the decisive preference. — Al-Rāzī argues equal receptivity to true and false belief cannot itself select one, like a body receptive to motion and rest; appeal to will repeats the problem. Al-Ālūsī reports the Ashʿarī divine-act-and-acceptance position; al-Bayḍāwī also names both sides. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:18/q10/p3 God creates souls receptive to the Prophet's guidance, beyond sending commands available to everyone. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says even rejecters received commands; these listeners sought God's approval and rescue. Their sound intellects are God-given and receptive; he rejects a merely command-address interpretation. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 39:18/q10/p4 Guidance includes their active seeking and following of truth. — Al-Ṭabrisī says they followed God's guidance; al-Jishumī makes piety and truth-following servants' acts; Ṭabāṭabāʾ makes readiness to follow truth the general basis of detailed guidance; al-Qāshānī describes original guidance and receptive liberated inner cores. [4 notes: TABRISI; JISHUMI; TABATABAI; QASHANI]

## 39:18/q11 Why does the first closing clause name God and use a demonstrative?  [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ ٱلَّذِينَ هَدَىٰهُمُ ٱللَّهُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether emphasis falls on the recipients or on the divine guide.
- 39:18/q11/p1 The demonstrative and defined terms single out deserving recipients of guidance. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says demonstratives after descriptions emphasize a judgment, citing 38:55; the two definite terms restrict this additional guidance compared with the losers of 39:15. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:18/q11/p2 Naming God magnifies the guidance and shows access to Him is through Him. — Al-Biqāʿī compares 6:90; here the object of guidance is explicit and the relative clause repeats it. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 39:18/q12 What makes them people of understanding?  [أُو۟لُوا۟ ٱلْأَلْبَٰبِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether intellect is identified by action, freedom from distortion or inward receptivity.
- 39:18/q12/p1 They possess and use sound intellect to follow truth and divine commands. — Al-Ṭabarī glosses understanding; Muqātil joins it to obeying commands and avoiding prohibitions; al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Nasafī stress using reason. Ibn Kathīr adds upright disposition; Ṭabāṭabāʾ calls following truth its sign; Study Quran cites 39:9 and 5:100 for acting on revelation. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 39:18/q12/p2 Their intellect is free from fancy, habit, delusion and caprice. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī describe freedom from these influences. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Khadīja, Abū Bakr and ʿAlī believing at the first call; Kur'an Yolu reports al-Rāzī that strong intellect is needed to assemble true knowledge. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]
- 39:18/q12/p3 Their liberated inner cores discern promptings and receive verified meanings. — Al-Qāshānī speaks of original guidance and liberated cores; al-Qushayrī distinguishes impulses of self, Satan, angel and the Real, each calling for a different response. [2 notes: QASHANI; QUSHAYRI]

## 39:18/q13 What do the repeated demonstrative and pronoun add?  [وَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ هُمْ أُو۟لُوا۟ ٱلْأَلْبَٰبِ · rhetoric]
- 39:18/q13/p1 They honor these people and specify their fully rooted understanding. — Al-Biqāʿī says repetition and 'they' intensify honor and restrict true intellect to clear minds. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds the separating pronoun, possessive form and definite 'intellects': all have some reason, these have matured understanding. The second clause shares the first's restriction. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:18/q14 What does the verse teach about inquiry and imitation?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The scope of reasoned choice and the duties inferred from it.
- 39:18/q14/p1 It praises reasoned testing of claims and condemns blind imitation. — Al-Jishumī says imitation blocks knowing the best; al-Kashshāf and al-Ālūsī cite the tethered-camel poem. Al-Rāzī offers detailed proof or initial comparison; Asad reports him and al-Ṭabarī similarly. Ibn ʿĀshūr urges exposing sophistry; Kur'an Yolu reports al-Rāzī's rational proof across faith, worship and law. [10 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Razi,Tabari); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]
- 39:18/q14/p2 Initial rational comparison favors one living, knowing, powerful, wise and merciful God. — Al-Rāzī adds sovereignty, absence of composition, independence of space and time, and possible forgiveness. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 39:18/q14/p3 Individual inquiry to sound belief is obligatory according to capacity; deeper legal and social inquiry is a collective duty. — Ibn ʿĀshūr includes fiqh, law's purposes, worship, transactions, social etiquette and replies to deceivers and extremists. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:18/q14/p4 One should take the best legal evidence, argument and knowledge from every science. — Al-Kashshāf calls for examining schools of law; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls for refining sciences and books; a reported saying and Kur'an Yolu's account of al-Rāzī extend this broadly. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]
- 39:18/q14/p5 The description promises reward for following truth. — Al-Jishumī takes the praise as a promise. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 39:18/q15 What inward or prophetic significance is given to the best address?  [ٱلْقَوْل أَحْسَنَهُۥٓ · inward]
turns on: Whether excellence concerns prophetic rank or spiritually helpful company.
- 39:18/q15/p1 Muhammad has the best prophetic address and primordial spiritual precedence. — Al-Sulamī says all prophets teach good but Muhammad leads each rank; a prophetic report calls his community last in worldly time but first in primordial address and the holy station. [2 notes: SULAMI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 39:18/q15/p2 The best companion recalls God by appearance and the afterlife by deeds. — Al-Sulamī transmits Jesus' description. [1 notes: SULAMI(عيسى عليه السلام)]

## 39:18/q16 What reports illustrate selecting excellent speech?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports have different speakers and events.
- 39:18/q16/p1 Abū al-Dardāʾ prized thirst in heat, night prostration and companions selecting speech like choice dates. — Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Ṭabrisī transmit this triad. [2 notes: THALABI(أبو الدرداء); TABRISI(أبو الدرداء)]
- 39:18/q16/p2 Al-Kalbī prized prostration, companions choosing good words like fruit and striving for God. — Al-Durr transmits this differing triad. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(الكلبي)]
- 39:18/q16/p3 A herald promised Paradise to those dying without shirk, then ʿUmar recalled him for fear of complacency. — Abū Saʿīd reports the Prophet contrasting hope in mercy and fear of punishment. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو سعيد)]
- 39:18/q16/p4 The verse followed an unidentified group's statement. — Al-Durr's excerpt preserves neither their identity nor their words. [1 notes: DURR-FULL]

## 39:18/q17 How does the verse connect to its neighbors and earlier scripture?  [* · links]
turns on: Which nearby promise or scriptural parallel explains this statement.
- 39:18/q17/p1 It completes glad tidings to those who reject false worship, unlike people bound for Hell. — Al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī preserve continuity with 39:17; Iṣlāḥī contrasts the believers with those beyond the Prophet's responsibility. Bursevī says the Messenger gives worldly news and angels afterlife news, citing 10:64. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURSEVI]
- 39:18/q17/p2 It echoes commands to take the best revelation and Moses' instruction about the Torah. — Muqātil cites 39:55; Ibn Kathīr and Study Quran cite 7:145; Bursevī cites both. [4 notes: MUQATIL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK); BURSEVI]
- 39:18/q17/p3 Its people of understanding recall 39:9 and the reminder in Sūrah Ṣād. — Iṣlāḥī cites 39:9 and 38:29; Study Quran also cites 39:9 and 5:100. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 39:18/q18 How do Arabic comparison forms treat qualities of mind?  [أَحْسَنَهُۥٓ ٱلْأَلْبَٰبِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether a quality describes mental character or bodily form.
- 39:18/q18/p1 Mental traits admit exclamatory comparison because they concern character and discernment. — Sībawayh permits ما أحمقه, ما أرعنه, ما أنوكه, ما ألده and ما أهوجه and compares these to foolishness and courage. [3 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 39:18/q18/p2 Colors and bodily defects do not form these Afʿal comparisons and exclamations. — Sībawayh uses ما أشد حمرته and extends restriction to أفعل به and أفعل منه; al-Khalīl likens such traits to body parts. Sībawayh says مفعال and فعول intensify action or character, not bodily quality, citing ضروب and محسان. [4 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]

## 39:18/q19 How are reports distinguished from an author's linguistic analysis?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether a source transmits a report or endorses its analysis.
- 39:18/q19/p1 Ibn Jinnī marks where Abū ʿAlī's report ends and his own analysis starts. — He uses formulas introducing his independent position. [1 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB]
- 39:18/q19/p2 Ibn Jinnī trusts Ibn Mujāhid's transmission while opposing his linguistic judgment. — The note separates reports and readings from their interpreter's reasoning. [1 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB]
