# 23:1: 26 questions

## 23:1/q01 What does قد contribute here?  [قَدْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether qad is heard chiefly as confirmation of awaited news or as bringing the perfect near the present; many sources combine both.
- 23:1/q01/p1 It confirms that the believers' expected success has occurred. — Al-Layth calls qad affirmation of occurrence; al-Zamakhshari contrasts lamma's denial of an expected event, and al-Razi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Biqai, Bursevi and al-Zarkashi relate it to believers awaiting glad tidings. Al-Khalil compares the awaited start of prayer; al-Zarkashi compares 58:1 and discusses 7:59. [23 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الليث); TABRISI(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURHAN(الخليل,الزمخشري); BAGHAWI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; BURHAN+; BURSEVI+; ITQAN+; JISHUMI+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT+; THALABI+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 23:1/q01/p2 It brings a past event near the present: success has been attained and remains true now. — Grammarians cite qad qamat al-salah; Wahidi cites a poem about a crawling or toddling child. Al-Farra, Ibn Jawzi and Tabarsi compare the prayer announcement. Ibn Malik favors nearness over expectation, while al-Zarkashi allows both. The perfect also makes fulfillment certain for Bursevi and Islahi. [16 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الشاعر,النحويون); BAGHAWI-FULL(المحققون); TABRISI(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; BURHAN(ابن مالك)+; BURSEVI+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; ITQAN+; JISHUMI+; THALABI+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]

## 23:1/q02 What grammatical environments does قد have?  [قَدْ · grammar]
- 23:1/q02/p1 It enters an affirmative independent finite verb and can accompany a perfect in circumstantial or oath constructions. — Wahidi attributes to al-Farra the need for expressed or implied qad in a past circumstantial clause, citing 4:90. Al-Zarkashi cites 6:119, an argument over supplying qad at 12:65, and Ibn Asfur on qad and lam in oath answers. He lists five general uses and excludes governed or future-marked imperfects. Suyuti says it enters affirmative finite ungoverned verbs. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); BURHAN(ابن عصفور,المبرد والفراء); BURHAN+; ITQAN+]

## 23:1/q03 What does أَفْلَحَ mean for the believers?  [أَفْلَحَ · meaning]
turns on: The notes give overlapping lexical senses: attainment, felicity, enduring good, and the root image; many sources accept more than one.
- 23:1/q03/p1 They attain their aim and escape what they fear. — Mawardi cites a poem about Mulaib al-Rimah; Ibn Abbas adds escape from evil. Qushayri speaks of attaining one's intended goal. Nasafi, Biqai, Bursevi, Elmalili and Bint al-Shati give attainment; Study Quran observes Quranic success and failure in 3:104, 6:21, 10:17, 30:38 and 2:5. [15 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب); BINTSHATI-IJAZ+; BIQAI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; ELMALILI+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; JISHUMI+; NASAFI-FULL+; QUSHAYRI+; STUDYQURAN+; TABATABAI+]
- 23:1/q03/p2 They become happy, blessed, victorious or prosperous. — Muqatil and Yahya gloss success as happiness. Mawardi and Tabarsi cite Labid's line; Ibn Abbas and Bint al-Shati cite Labid for winning and happiness. Damghani, Suyuti and others give Arab usage; Qashani calls it the greatest victory. [19 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(قيل); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,لبيد); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL+; BINTSHATI-IJAZ+; DAMGHANI+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; ITQAN+; JISHUMI+; MUQATIL+; QASHANI+; STUDYQURAN+; THALABI+; WAHIDI-WAJIZ+; YAHYA-SALLAM+]
- 23:1/q03/p3 They or their good deeds endure in goodness, especially in Paradise. — Mawardi cites the call to prayer and poetry for lasting good; al-Zajjaj speaks of lasting continuance. Wahidi attributes everlasting life to Abu Ishaq and Paradise to Ibn Abbas. Tabarsi applies endurance to deeds; Bagawi speaks of salvation and enduring life. [19 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; ELMALILI+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; JISHUMI+; TAB-FULL+; THALABI+; WAHIDI-WAJIZ+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 23:1/q03/p4 The word evokes cultivation or splitting through an obstacle to reach a goal. — Al-Raghib traces falh to cleaving, illustrated by iron splitting iron. Tabatabai says falah pictures breaking through an obstacle and revealing the desired goal; Study Quran notes the cultivation association. [3 notes: TABATABAI(الراغب); STUDYQURAN+; TABATABAI+]

## 23:1/q04 How does Qur'anic usage distribute forms of أَفْلَحَ?  [أَفْلَحَ · meaning]
- 23:1/q04/p1 The past occurs four times, the present twenty-three times, and the active participle twice. — Bint al-Shati says the Quran affirms success for faithful groups and denies it to their opponents. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ+]

## 23:1/q05 What is the force of the أَفْلَحَ verb form?  [أَفْلَحَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the form is read as entry into a state or as causing entry into it.
- 23:1/q05/p1 It means entering or attaining success. — Zamakhshari compares abshara, entering glad tidings. Razi, Bursevi and Elmalili repeat the analysis; Elmalili says this is the Quran's usual use. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI+; ELMALILI+]
- 23:1/q05/p2 It can be causative: making someone attain success. — Zamakhshari, Razi, Bursevi and Elmalili present this alongside the inchoative sense; al-Samin notes that aflaha can mean bringing another into felicity. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(طلحة بن مصرف وعمرو بن عبيد); BURSEVI; ELMALILI]

## 23:1/q06 How is قَدْ أَفْلَحَ read?  [قَدْ أَفْلَحَ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted vowel, hamza treatment, voice and plural marker differ; grammatical objections concern the plural marker and the explanation for omitted waw.
- 23:1/q06/p1 The common reading has a still dal, distinct hamza, and active past verb with open hamza and ha. — Jishumi describes the other readers and the common active form; al-Samin gives the general reading. Asim is separately reported with nasb by Suyuti, without further explanation. [4 notes: JISHUMI(الباقون,قراءة العامة); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); DURR-FULL(عاصم)]
- 23:1/q06/p2 Warsh transfers the hamza's vowel to the dal and drops the hamza. — Ibn Mujahid says Warsh follows this rule within and between words, also citing 28:71. Al-Nahhas says the dal takes the vowel to avoid adjacent still letters. Jishumi, Baydawi and al-Samin report the same transfer. [5 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ورش عن نافع); NAHHAS; JISHUMI(ورش عن نافع); BAYDAWI-FULL(ورش عن نافع); SAMIN-DURR(ورش)]
- 23:1/q06/p3 Hamza pauses at the dal before pronouncing the hamza separately, with variant pause forms. — Jishumi names Hamza, Abu Bakr from Asim and Qutayba from Kisai for a brief pause, adding that Hamza and al-Asha lengthen it while others do not pause. Al-Samin reports one Hamza pause form like Warsh and another like the general reading; he rejects Abu al-Baqa's proposed alif and deletion analysis as an unnecessary weaker dialect. [2 notes: JISHUMI(حمزة وأبو بكر عن عاصم وقتيبة عن الكسائي); SAMIN-DURR(حمزة); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-]
- 23:1/q06/p4 A passive verb says the believers were brought into success or preserved in reward. — Talha is named by Thalabi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and al-Samin; Ibn Jawzi also names Ubayy, Ikrima and Asim al-Jahdari, while Abu Hayyan names Amr ibn Ubayd. Abu Hayyan allows derivation from falaha or aflaha. Jishumi gives an unusual gloss 'cast into the earth' for Talha's form. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI(طلحة بن مصرف); JISHUMI(قراءة العامة); KASHSHAF-FULL(طلحة بن مصرف); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبي بن كعب وعكرمة وعاصم الجحدري وطلحة بن مصرف); RAZI-FULL(طلحة بن مصرف); QURTUBI-FULL(طلحة بن مصرف); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(طلحة بن مصرف وعمرو بن عبيد); SAMIN-DURR(طلحة بن مصرف وعمرو بن عبيد); BURSEVI]
- 23:1/q06/p5 Talha has a plural-marked aflaḥū before the expressed subject المؤمنون. — Zamakhshari and Razi allow the akaluni al-baraghith dialect or an anticipatory unexplained subject later clarified. Isa ibn Umar challenged Talha, who said he followed his companions; Abu Hayyan and al-Samin treat this as fidelity to transmission. Ibn Khalawayh writes waw; al-Lawami says connected recitation drops it before another still sound and writing follows pronunciation, comparing 42:24, 96:18 and 37:163. Al-Samin cites analogous accepted readings at 5:71 and 21:3. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(طلحة بن مصرف); RAZI-FULL(طلحة بن مصرف); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,عيسى بن عمر); SAMIN-DURR(ابن خالويه,اللوامح,طلحة بن مصرف,عيسى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(طلحة بن مصرف)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)-]
- 23:1/q06/p6 Talha has a form with damma on ha and no written waw. — Zamakhshari treats the vowel as a substitute for waw and cites poetry. Abu Hayyan and al-Samin reject the analogy: deletion in connected speech avoids two still letters, whereas the poem omits waw through poetic necessity. Suyuti reports Talha's raf reading. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(طلحة بن مصرف); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,طلحة بن مصرف); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); DURR-FULL(طلحة بن مصرف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 23:1/q06/p7 Talha is also reported with damma on hamza and kasra on lam. — Ibn Atiyya reports this additional vocalization without an explanation. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(طلحة بن مصرف)]

## 23:1/q07 Who are ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ?  [ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the designation is simple assent or profession, the commendatory description of lived faith, or a foremost historical audience.
- 23:1/q07/p1 They affirm God, His oneness, His messengers and the revealed essentials. — Muqatil and Said ibn Jubayr identify monotheists; Zajjaj specifies affirming revelation, God's oneness and Muhammad's prophethood. Tabarsi includes messengers. Samarrai reports a view including bodily resurrection and recompense. Tabatabai adds general obedience to these affirmations; Razi points back to al-Baqara. [11 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(روح المعاني); BURSEVI+; ELMALILI+; MUQATIL+; RAZI-FULL+; TABATABAI+; TABRISI+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 23:1/q07/p2 In basic Arabic, a believer is one who assents or confirms. — Zamakhshari and Nasafi give this lexical sense. Qushayri describes inner affirmation reaching the heart's core. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; QUSHAYRI+]
- 23:1/q07/p3 In religious usage, a believer professes both testimonies with heart and tongue agreeing. — Zamakhshari reports a legal view and Nasafi states it. Jishumi reports another view that believer is a name fixed by religious law. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL+]
- 23:1/q07/p4 The praised believers have rooted, active faith shown by the listed virtues; sinners do not share this commendatory name. — Jishumi says the traits identify believers and argues against applying the praise to a sinner deserving punishment. Kashshaf reports this stricter view. Qushayri describes settled inner realization; Tabatabai distinguishes committed faith from mere knowledge and allows some disobedience without severing faith from all action. Islahi rejects outward profession without inward roots; Bursevi requires all faith's conditions; Asad points to humble prayer and purity. Qashani emphasizes certainty. [18 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر,بعضهم); QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; ASAD-NOTES+; BIQAI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; JISHUMI+; QASHANI+; TABATABAI+]
- 23:1/q07/p5 Foremost are the Meccan emigrants preparing for jihad. — Islahi takes these believers as the initial audience, while including a wider promise of the Hereafter and establishment of religion in Arabia. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 23:1/q08 Why use the noun ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ here?  [ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ · rhetoric]
- 23:1/q08/p1 The fixed noun presents faith as a settled quality after believers answered earlier commands. — Biqai links the name to prior commands and 59:9; Samarrai contrasts the verbal address in al-Hajj with the settled nominal description here. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL+; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT+]

## 23:1/q09 Where and how does the promised success occur?  [قَدْ أَفْلَحَ ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the promised reward is chiefly Paradise, includes present worldly flourishing, or is described as inward nearness.
- 23:1/q09/p1 It is chiefly salvation, reward and enduring happiness in Paradise. — Tabari promises lasting Gardens for belief and the listed acts; Yahya calls the happy people Paradise's people. Wahidi, Bagawi, Tabarsi and Ibn Kathir speak of Paradise and reward. Kur'an Yolu cites the sura's rebuttal of afterlife deniers and the hadith on entering Paradise. Islahi includes establishment of religion in Arabia beneath the principal afterlife promise. [11 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(hadis); IBNKATHIR-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; STUDYQURAN+; TAB-FULL+; TABRISI+; WAHIDI-WAJIZ+; YAHYA-SALLAM+]
- 23:1/q09/p2 It includes flourishing in this world as well as in the Hereafter. — Tabatabai names worldly long life, wealth and honor and otherworldly permanence without poverty, disgrace or ignorance; al-Raghib distinguishes worldly success. Kur'an Yolu says right belief and conduct yield worldly well-being by testimony and experience. Study Quran recognizes both, especially the Hereafter. [5 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; STUDYQURAN+; TABATABAI+]
- 23:1/q09/p3 In an inward reading, success is nearness to God and affirmation of His promise. — Ibn Ata calls it the highest station of nearness and happiness. Sulami also transmits portraits of the believer whose treasure is the Lord and whose outward and inward life is trustworthy and merciful. [4 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر,أحمد بن عاصم الأنطاكي,ابن عطاء,بعضهم)]

## 23:1/q10 What conditions make the believers successful?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The sources give both the listed practices and an account of the active faith that generates them.
- 23:1/q10/p1 Faith must bear the worship and ethical traits listed in the opening passage. — Tabari names belief in God and Messenger, acceptance of revelation and these deeds. Razi and Elmalili count seven qualities; Ibn Ashur says each relative clause presents a sign of rooted faith, without exhausting every cause, and compares the condemned in 74:42–46. Islahi and Samarrai list prayer, zakah, chastity, trust and restraint. Tabatabai cites 16:97 and 13:29 for faith with good deeds. Asad stresses humility and inner purity. [20 notes: ASAD-NOTES+; BIQAI-FULL+; ELMALILI+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; JISHUMI+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; RAZI-FULL+; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT+; STUDYQURAN+; TAB-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 23:1/q10/p2 Faith is an active commitment shaped by beliefs about God, the afterlife and human life. — Tabatabai contrasts three social outlooks: believers order life for lasting afterlife happiness, worshippers of other deities seek their favor and worldly goods, and materialists seek finite enjoyment. He distinguishes practical faith from theoretical knowledge and treats the listed traits as effects of living faith. Bint al-Shati contrasts believers' worldly khushu in 17:109, 3:199, 21:90, 2:45, 33:35 and 57:16 with sinners' involuntary afterlife humility in 88:1–4, 79:8–12, 70:43–44, 42:44–45 and 54:6–7. [9 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ+; TABATABAI+]

## 23:1/q11 Does this promise include a grave sinner called a believer?  [ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether the promise follows profession of faith alone or requires the enumerated faithful conduct.
- 23:1/q11/p1 No; the praised believers' reward is reserved for those with the stated traits. — Jishumi explicitly disputes the Murjiite claim that a grave sinner obtains Paradise, calls believer a commendatory name, and cites the traits as the test. Kashshaf reports a righteous-only legal view; Bursevi says bare assent is insufficient. Tabatabai qualifies that some disobedience can coexist with faith, but faith cannot be wholly severed from action. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABATABAI; BURSEVI+; JISHUMI+]
- 23:1/q11/p2 A grave sinner who has faith may attain the believers' Paradise. — Jishumi attributes this to the Murjiites and argues against it. [0 notes: JISHUMI-]

## 23:1/q12 What was said when the Garden was created?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ about whether the Garden, God, or the tree uttered the words, and on the objects made by God's hand and details of Paradise.
- 23:1/q12/p1 The Garden of Eden or Paradise spoke the verse at God's command. — Ka'b variously names three handiworks Adam, Torah and Eden, while Maysara names four including Tablets; Mujahid's version says God planted Eden and viewed it. Ibn Abbas has Firdaws adorned and speaking, three repetitions in another version, and exclusion of misers and show-offs. Reports give gold and silver bricks, musk mortar, precious materials, fruit and rivers, scent at the gate and angels calling it a dwelling for kings. Abu Sa'id's raised report is questioned by al-Bazzar because Adi ibn al-Fadl was not a memorizer; al-Tabarani notes Baqiyya's weakness in the Ibn Abbas report. Muqatil's extended report identifies Muhammad's monotheists as eternal heirs, with gate, Pool and ablution-light details. [31 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL(ابن عباس); YAHYA-SALLAM(المعلى بن هلال,كعب); ABDURRAZZAQ(كعب); TAB-FULL(كعب,ميسرة); ZAJJAJ(كعب الحبر); THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(رسول الله,كعب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(كعب الأحبار); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سعيد الخدري عن رسول الله); RAZI-FULL(رسول الله); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي); NASAFI-FULL(رسول الله); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري,أنس,ابن عباس,كعب الأحبار، مجاهد، أبو العالية); DURR-FULL(أنس,ابن عباس,غير مسمى,كعب); BURSEVI]
- 23:1/q12/p2 God Himself declared the verse on creating or viewing the Garden. — Mujahid and Abu al-Aliya transmit this version; Abu al-Aliya says God then revealed the declaration in the Quran. Study Quran distinguishes God's declaration at planting Eden and upon creating the Garden. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو العالية,مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(كعب الأحبار، مجاهد، أبو العالية); DURR-FULL(أبو العالية,مجاهد); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 23:1/q12/p3 The tree Tuba was commanded to speak and uttered the verse. — Razi transmits this variant from Ka'b: God personally made Adam, wrote Torah and planted Tuba before commanding it to speak. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(كعب)]

## 23:1/q13 How is the Garden's speech and God's hand understood?  [* · theology]
- 23:1/q13/p1 The Garden's speech signifies that it has been prepared for believers, and God's hand signifies His direct creation. — Razi compares the personified response of heaven and earth at 41:11 and says God undertook Eden's creation personally. He treats prayer's praising and interceding for a good worshipper, or condemning a neglectful one, as figurative because movements cannot literally speak; he compares saying a benefactor's kindness speaks gratitude. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(رسول الله); RAZI-FULL+]

## 23:1/q14 What happened when the opening ten verses were revealed?  [* · reports]
- 23:1/q14/p1 The Prophet heard revelation like buzzing bees, prayed for divine favor, then said whoever upholds the ten verses enters Paradise. — Umar's reports vary in prayer wording: increase, honor, giving, divine approval and contentment; Ahmad, Ali ibn al-Madini and others add contentment. He faced the qibla and raised his hands in some versions. Al-Nahhas glosses upholding as observing without violating the teachings; Qurtubi adds later ablution and pilgrimage obligations. Ibn al-Arabi authenticated the report, while al-Tirmidhi called it munkar because only unknown Yunus ibn Sulaym transmits it; al-Nasai and al-Hakim also transmitted it. Kur'an Yolu cites it for the afterlife sense. [14 notes: THALABI(عمر بن الخطاب); MAWARDI-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); WAHIDI-ASBAB(عمر بن الخطاب); JISHUMI(عمر); BAGHAWI-FULL(أحمد بن حنبل وعلي بن المديني وجماعة,عمر بن الخطاب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عمر بن الخطاب عن رسول الله); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحاكم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); DURR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(hadis); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس)+; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الترمذي)-]

## 23:1/q15 How did Aisha describe the Prophet's character through this passage?  [* · reports]
- 23:1/q15/p1 She recited the opening through those guarding prayer and said these traits were his character. — Mawardi, Ibn Kathir and Suyuti report her broader statement that his character was the Quran. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبو عمران الجوني); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عائشة); DURR-FULL(عائشة)]

## 23:1/q16 What is reported about the Prophet reciting this sūra in prayer?  [* · reports]
- 23:1/q16/p1 At Mecca's conquest he began the sūra while praying at the Kaaba, coughed near the mention of Moses and Jesus or Aaron, then bowed. — Qurtubi cites al-Nasai and Muslim from Abd Allah ibn al-Saib; Ibn Ashur gives al-Nasai's version and Abu Dawud's dawn-prayer variant, where he cut the recitation short. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله بن السائب); IBNASHUR-FULL(عبد الله بن السائب)]

## 23:1/q17 What other reports connect this passage to revelation or recitation?  [* · reports]
turns on: These are distinct transmitted claims about the passage.
- 23:1/q17/p1 Reciting this sūra brings angels' glad tidings of comfort at death. — Jishumi transmits it from Ubayy ibn Ka'b from the Prophet. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبي بن كعب)]
- 23:1/q17/p2 The qualities were revealed also to Abraham, and only Abraham and Muhammad fulfilled them. — Abu Umama connects passages of 9:112, 23:1, 33:35 and 70:23 in the report cited by Suyuti. [1 notes: ITQAN(أبو أمامة)]

## 23:1/q18 What was the sūra's place and manner of revelation?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The sources address both location and batching of revelation.
- 23:1/q18/p1 It was revealed in Mecca. — Zajjaj, Jishumi's al-Qadi, Abu Hayyan, Baydawi, Suyuti's Ibn Abbas and Study Quran affirm it; Ibn Ashur calls it consensus and rejects an inference of Medinan revelation from zakah. Asad says most classical commentators place it late in Mecca; some call it the final Meccan revelation, which he finds inconclusive. [8 notes: JISHUMI(القاضي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ASAD-NOTES(classical commentators; Suyuti); STUDYQURAN(Q); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 23:1/q18/p2 The opening was revealed as one of the Quran's batches of verses according to need. — Suyuti uses the opening alongside the Ifk passage, a single phrase and part of another verse to illustrate revelation in groups of varying length. [1 notes: ITQAN+]

## 23:1/q19 How many verses does the sūra contain?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The reported regional verse counts differ, especially the Basran count.
- 23:1/q19/p1 It has 118 verses by Kufan count and 117 by Basran count. — Jishumi transmits this from al-Qadi. [1 notes: JISHUMI(القاضي)]
- 23:1/q19/p2 It has 118 verses by Kufan count and 119 by Basran count. — Baydawi gives these counts. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL+]
- 23:1/q19/p3 It has 118 verses. — Suyuti states this without a regional distinction. [1 notes: DURR-FULL+]
- 23:1/q19/p4 It has 118 verses by Kufan count and 110 by Basran count. — Bursevi gives these counts. [1 notes: BURSEVI+]

## 23:1/q20 Why is the sūra called al-Mu'minun, and what other names has it?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The title can name the opening believers, quote its wording, or use the success word.
- 23:1/q20/p1 Its title comes from the believers and the success announced at its opening. — Ibn Ashur gives both an association with its theme and an alternative that the title quotes the exact opening word; Biqai says the title plainly states the sūra's purpose. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 23:1/q20/p2 It is also called Qad Aflaha or Surat al-Falah. — Ibn Ashur cites Ibn al-Qasim's report about Malik's grandfather's Uthman-era mushaf notation, contrasted with a reading at 23:85. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن القاسم)]

## 23:1/q21 What is the sūra's overall argument?  [* · interpretation]
- 23:1/q21/p1 It assigns success to believers, describes their virtues, and warns the denying or powerful opponents. — Biqai makes believers' special success the purpose; Tabatabai describes a Meccan call to God and the Last Day, believers' service, unbelievers' vices and warnings through the histories from Noah to Jesus. Study Quran observes repeated opposition by the affluent and powerful, comparing 7:59–62 and 7:136. Asad makes belief in a conscious Creator imply afterlife belief; Islahi sees the promised Paradise subject to conditions. [7 notes: STUDYQURAN(Q); ASAD-NOTES+; BIQAI-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; STUDYQURAN+; TABATABAI+]

## 23:1/q22 How does the opening connect to the end of al-Hajj?  [* · links]
- 23:1/q22/p1 It announces the success hoped for after al-Hajj's commands to worship and do good. — Jishumi sees the opening detailing al-Hajj's final commands. Abu Hayyan cites 22:77. Biqai lists prayer, zakah and divine protection as duties yielding good in both lives. Islahi calls the sūras counterparts on the community's duty to witness true religion, with different styles and temporary trials. Samarrai matches the commands at 22:77–78 to the traits at 23:1–4, and says qad confirms their quick fulfillment. [11 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; JISHUMI+; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(البحر المحيط)+]

## 23:1/q23 How does the sūra's ending answer its opening?  [* · links]
- 23:1/q23/p1 Its closing denial of unbelievers' success contrasts with the opening affirmation for believers. — Zamakhshari, al-Kirmani, Islahi and Samarrai cite the close at 23:117; Islahi notes that detailed believer traits and unbeliever humiliation give the two ends force. [6 notes: BURHAN(الزمخشري); ITQAN(الزمخشري); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; SAMARRAI-ASRAR+; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT+]

## 23:1/q24 What other passages or sūras connect with this opening?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes point to different cross-sūra relationships.
- 23:1/q24/p1 The next sūra, al-Nur, develops faith's light in individual and collective life. — Islahi says al-Nur supplements this sūra by showing faith's inward illumination and the darkness of its absence. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 23:1/q24/p2 Al-Mu'minun describes faith generally, while al-Ma'arij specifies belief in Judgment. — Samarrai says this sūra's account is thus more comprehensive. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT+]
- 23:1/q24/p3 The believers' reward can be described as inheritance or exchange. — Qurtubi refers to an exchange discussed at 64:9 and to inheritance under al-Mu'minun's opening. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL+]

## 23:1/q25 What related notes are given on the basmala and the divine name at this opening?  [* · inward]
turns on: The sources comment on different aspects of the opening formula.
- 23:1/q25/p1 The name conveys primordial loftiness and knowing Bismillah raises aspiration and brings unexpected heart knowledge. — Qushayri derives the name from elevation and links it to the Real's primordial loftiness. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI+]
- 23:1/q25/p2 The basmala presents sovereign mercy given generally through explanation and particularly through faith. — Biqai distinguishes al-Rahman as comprehensive mercy and al-Rahim as singling out those chosen for faith. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL+]

## 23:1/q26 Where does the opening passage allow a necessary stop in recitation?  [* · sciences]
- 23:1/q26/p1 Its story endings are among places where necessity licenses stopping. — Suyuti transmits Ibn al-Jazari's preferred examples, including paired directions, prophets, prayer and almsgiving, and story endings in this sūra. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن الجزري)]
