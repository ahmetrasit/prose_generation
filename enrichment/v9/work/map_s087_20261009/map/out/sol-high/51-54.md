# 51:54: 9 questions

## 51:54/q01 Who is addressed by 'فَتَوَلَّ'?  [فَتَوَلَّ · referent]
- 51:54/q01/p1 Muhammad, the Prophet, is the addressee. — Mujahid explicitly identifies Muhammad as the addressee, both directly and as transmitted by al-Tabari. [2 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 51:54/q02 Who are 'عَنْهُمْ' from whom he must turn?  [عَنْهُمْ · referent]
- 51:54/q02/p1 The stubborn rejecters among Quraysh's polytheists, particularly their conceited leaders. — Al-Tabari names Quraysh's polytheists; al-Wahidi calls them polytheists. Al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, al-Alusi, Elmalılı, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī stress their repeated refusal and arrogance. Asad calls them rejecters. Iṣlāḥī identifies hostile people and, more specifically, Quraysh leaders unwilling to listen; he says the Prophet had pursued those leaders because their conversion might strengthen the new faith and draw others. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 51:54/q03 What does 'فَتَوَلَّ عَنْهُمْ' direct him to do?  [فَتَوَلَّ عَنْهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether turning away concerns dealings with persistent rejecters, consuming concern for them, their eternal decree, or the Prophet's inward orientation to God.
- 51:54/q03/p1 Turn away or leave the resistant people after conveying the warning, ending futile pursuit or dispute with them. — Abu ʿUbayda glosses the verb as leaving; he cites Ḥuṣayn ibn Ḍamḍam's horseman departing his companions, and al-Ṭabarī cites the same verse for a party turning away. Al-Ṭabarī also explains its aʿwar as one whose need was unmet or aim unachieved; Mujahid simply glosses the verb as turn away. Al-Ṭabarī says the withdrawal lasts until God's command concerning them; al-Naḥḥās specifies the eventual order to fight. Muqātil, Tustarī, Sahl, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, and Ibn al-Jawzī tie withdrawal to completed delivery. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abu Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī, Bursawī, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı, Ṭabāṭabāʾī, and Iṣlāḥī stress repeated calls met by stubborn refusal; al-Biqāʿī specifies leaving further disputation and harsh confrontation, Bursawī also glosses turning from their requital until a command to fight, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the proof had been established. Iṣlāḥī says previous nations treated their messengers similarly, counsels patient continuation and reminder of receptive people, and says the leaders had become immovable. Ibn Kathīr, Asad, and Abū Jaʿfar ibn al-Zubayr state the basic command to turn away. [29 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ(حصين بن ضمضم); TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(حصين بن ضمضم,مجاهد); NAHHAS(مجاهد); SULAMI(سهل); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 51:54/q03/p2 Turn away from excessive grief over them or from exhausting efforts to convert them. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the Prophet had delivered the message and is not their controller. Bursawī attributes the reading about consuming concern and grief to Fatḥ al-Raḥmān. Al-Rāzī explains the Prophet's intense prior warning by his noble habit of blaming himself for their unbelief. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI(فتح الرحمن); RAZI-FULL]
- 51:54/q03/p3 Leave them to the felicity or misery decreed for them in pre-eternity. — Al-Wāsiṭī, reported by al-Sulamī and Bursawī, gives this inward reading; al-Sulamī adds that the Prophet counselled, strove, and suffered, so blame is removed. [2 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); BURSEVI(الواسطي)]
- 51:54/q03/p4 Return or journey toward God while conveying the message outwardly. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ, as reported by al-Sulamī, hears an invitation for the Prophet to return to God and reassurance that he has not fallen short. Bursawī reports an inward reading in which he stays present to the divine Essence without being veiled while conveying the means of salvation. [2 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 51:54/q04 What does 'فَمَآ أَنتَ بِمَلُومٍۢ' affirm?  [فَمَآ أَنتَ بِمَلُومٍۢ · interpretation]
- 51:54/q04/p1 The Prophet incurs no censure for withdrawing or for their rejection, because he fulfilled his duty to warn and convey; their guidance and response are not his responsibility. — Bursawī glosses lawm and malāma as reproach or censure, including a Persian gloss of blame. Al-Ṭabarī and al-Naḥḥās say there is no reproach for neglecting warning because he did warn; Ibn Zayd adds that no one can blame him after he fulfilled the command. Al-Rāzī says the Prophet blamed himself and intensified warning from noble character, but God reassures him that the stubborn rejecters themselves are culpable; al-Ṭabrisī and Iṣlāḥī likewise assign blame to rejecters. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abu Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī, Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī emphasize that he exhausted or exceeded every customary effort. Bursawī reports spiritual elders who say he can convey but cannot guide even those he loves, citing 28:56. Study Quran says he is not accountable for their guidance or punishment, citing 2:272. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says he has not fallen short; al-Wāsiṭī adds his counsel, striving, and suffering. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says he established the proof and completed the case. [32 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الواسطي,سهل); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); BURSEVI(بعض الكبار); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 51:54/q05 What reaction followed the command to withdraw?  [* · reports]
- 51:54/q05/p1 The Prophet and/or his Companions grieved or feared imminent punishment and an end to revelation; the subsequent reminder command reassured them. — Muqātil and Ibn ʿAbbās describe the Prophet fearing punishment for his people; al-Wāḥidī says Muqātil and Qatāda agree. ʿAlī's reports emphasize the Companions' sorrow or fear of destruction and divine anger; Qatāda says they feared revelation had ceased and punishment was near. Al-Baghawī's exegetes and al-Ṭabrisī mention both Prophet and believers. Al-Durr says the later reminder is with the Quran. The reports vary in whose fear they foreground, but all connect relief to the command to remind in 51:55. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(علي,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(علي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(المفسرون); TABRISI; DURR-FULL(علي,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(علي)]

## 51:54/q06 How does the following command to remind relate to this withdrawal?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the reminder cancels withdrawal or directs it toward rejecters while admonition continues for receptive people.
- 51:54/q06/p1 It supersedes the order to withdraw and resumes preaching. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk says the subsequent reminder command supersedes withdrawal because Muhammad was later told to preach. Ibn ʿAbbās, as reported by al-Ālūsī, says God ordered withdrawal so rejecters would be punished and excused the Prophet, then 51:55 abrogated that order. Ibn al-Jawzī reports 51:55 as one of two proposed abrogators. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 51:54/q06/p2 He withdraws from resistant people while continuing to remind those who can benefit, especially believers. — Mujahid, as reported by al-Durr, says the Prophet was first told to turn away and then to admonish because reminders benefit believers. Iṣlāḥī says to turn from wrongdoers, remind the responsive, and trust God's support for the Prophet and Companions. Study Quran says his continuing responsibility is to remind believers and cites 2:272. The reported fear at withdrawal was relieved by the next command, as ʿAlī and Qatāda recount. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 51:54/q07 Is the withdrawal command abrogated, and by what?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether 51:55 itself supersedes withdrawal, or the later sword verse does; Ibn ʿAṭiyya makes the latter conditional on reading withdrawal as a truce.
- 51:54/q07/p1 It is abrogated by the following command to remind in 51:55. — Ibn al-Jawzī says most exegetes consider the verse abrogated and records the next verse as one proposed abrogator. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk says the later order to preach superseded withdrawal. Ibn ʿAbbās, reported by al-Ālūsī, says the reminder command abrogated the withdrawal ordered before punishment of rejecters. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أكثر المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 51:54/q07/p2 It is superseded by the sword verse or later order to fight. — Ibn al-Jawzī gives the sword verse as the other proposed abrogator. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says that if withdrawal implies a truce, the sword verse abrogates such truces. Al-Naḥḥās explains withdrawal as lasting until God's order about them, namely fighting them; Bursawī's Persian gloss likewise says until the order to fight. Ibn al-Jawzī says most exegetes regard the verse as abrogated without specifying which abrogator they choose. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أكثر المفسرين); IBNATIYYA-FULL; NAHHAS(مجاهد); BURSEVI]

## 51:54/q08 How does the preceding account of the deniers explain this command?  [* · links]
- 51:54/q08/p1 Their shared arrogance and darkened hearts explain repeated disbelief, so the Prophet can withdraw without blame. — Abū Jaʿfar ibn al-Zubayr, reported by al-Biqāʿī, says the question in 51:53 whether deniers taught each other or are simply transgressive points to that common cause; he cites 32:13 for God's ability to guide every soul. He then notes that the Prophet is told to turn away without blame. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير)]

## 51:54/q09 What lesson does Bursawī's aside draw about youth and enjoyment?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Bursawī gives an exhortation about using worldly gifts and also cites a Persian couplet praising a different combination of youthful pleasures.
- 51:54/q09/p1 Youth, free time, and wealth can ruin a person, so these gifts should serve the true aim. — Bursawī urges spending one's age, youth, and riches on that aim. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 51:54/q09/p2 Love, youth, and revelry together are desired and enable eloquent expression. — Bursawī reports this as the point of a cited Persian couplet. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
