# 14:40: 14 questions

## 14:40/q01 Why does the prayer use 'my Lord' and 'our Lord,' and repeat the address?  [رَبِّ رَبَّنَا · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes explain different functions of the address and repetition, without setting them against each other.
- 14:40/q01/p1 The singular addresses Abraham's own experience or precedence; the plural voices a request shared with his descendants or a favor witnessed by all. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes requests specific to Abraham's precedence and imamate from shared requests. Biqāʿī, Bursawī, and Ālūsī connect the plural to descendants included in the petition. Iṣlāḥī says rabbī invokes a personally experienced favor, whereas rabbanā invokes a favor witnessed by all creatures. [6 notes: TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 14:40/q01/p2 Repeated Lord-addresses are part of humble, insistent supplication. — Iṣlāḥī counts rabbī or rabbanā seven times and says repetition repeatedly seeks God's attention. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the acceptance request further humble pleading; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it reinforces and intensifies the earlier request, like the explanation about idols in 14:35–36. Biqāʿī portrays eagerness for all his prayer to be accepted. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 14:40/q02 What does asking God to make Abraham establish prayer imply about human action?  [ٱجْعَلْنِى مُقِيمَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةِ · theology]
turns on: How God's making the act relates to the servant's own undertaking: creation, enabling grace, or divine permission alongside human action.
- 14:40/q02/p1 God creates or alone brings about the commanded act of prayer. — Al-Qushayrī argues that making prayer for Abraham is creating prayer for him and hence human acts are created. Rāzī reports his associates' inference that God alone brings commanded acts about and that all acts are from God. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا)]
- 14:40/q02/p2 God grants enabling grace for prayer and adherence to religion. — Al-Ṭabrisī calls the requested help luṭf and extends it to the Muslim group of descendants. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 14:40/q02/p3 Prayer is attributed to God through permission and will and to the servant through undertaking and action. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares Abraham's request to be kept from idolatry in 14:35. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 14:40/q03 What does it mean to be a maintainer of prayer here?  [مُقِيمَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether prayer names sustained ritual observance, broader steadfast devotion, or an inward state.
- 14:40/q03/p1 To perform prayer soundly and persist in its regular observance. — Ṭabarī identifies the prayer God obligated and worshipful obedience. Baghawī specifies its pillars and maintenance; Bayḍāwī proper regular performance; Ibn Kathīr its limits; Bursawī straightening, keeping it flourishing like a market, or performing it; Ālūsī likens making it upright to straightening a crooked stick and notes regular observance and constancy; Ibn ʿĀshūr explains iqāma as steadfast maintenance, comparing 2:3. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān say the already praying Abraham sought continuation; Ibn ʿĀshūr reads ajʿalnī as bringing him into that future state. Wāḥidī, Asad, and Study Quran also describe constancy or persistent actual practice. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL]
- 14:40/q03/p2 To remain firm in religion or wholly devoted to God. — Qurṭubī glosses it as steadfastness in Islam and its rulings. Study Quran reports firmness in religion as one possible sense. Asad calls establishing prayer a metonym for utter devotion to God. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES]
- 14:40/q03/p3 In an inward sense, to sustain the prayer of witnessing or an ascent through God. — Qāshānī assigns the speaker the prayer of witnessing and each descendant a prayer appropriate to them. Bursawī calls prayer the believer's miʿrāj and reads its establishment as continuous ascent in and through God. [2 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 14:40/q04 Why say 'one who establishes prayer' rather than use a verb?  [مُقِيمَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةِ · rhetoric]
- 14:40/q04/p1 The adjectival or participial form asks for an enduring state of prayer keeping. — Al-Wāḥidī says the adjective is more enduring and emphatic than asking to perform an action, with a link to 17:29. Bursawī says the active participle conveys continuity and persistence. Ālūsī reports a scholar who takes the nominal form to add regularity alongside constancy without positing two metaphors. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 14:40/q05 Why does Abraham single out prayer in his request?  [ٱلصَّلَوٰةِ · interpretation]
- 14:40/q05/p1 Prayer is central to worship and to the purpose for which Abraham settled his descendants. — Biqāʿī calls prayer the greatest pillar after faith and says Abraham requests the worship that motivated settling his descendants; establishing it is difficult for the soul without God's help. Iṣlāḥī connects the descendants' prayer to the purpose for which Abraham built the House. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 14:40/q06 Who is sought in 'from my descendants,' and what does min indicate?  [وَمِن ذُرِّيَّتِى · referent]
turns on: Whether min limits the requested descendants or marks their origin; the Meccan setting also affects which descendants a source identifies.
- 14:40/q06/p1 Some descendants are included, since others would disbelieve or fail to pray. — Muqātil, Ṭabarī, Zajjāj, Thaʿlabī, Qushayrī, Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Kashshāf, Rāzī, Qurṭubī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn, Biqāʿī, Bursawī, Ālūsī, al-Ṭabrisī, and Asad explain the selective request. Qushayrī, Kashshāf, Rāzī, and Asad cite 2:124 about wrongdoers not receiving God's covenant; Asad says descent confers no chosenness, including Israelite and Ishmaelite branches, Quraysh, and unrighteous descendants of Muhammad. Wāḥidī compares the partitive min of 14:37 and 14:34. Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī add inference from earlier peoples as an alternative to revelation; Abū Ḥayyān includes believing descendants who neglect prayer. Bursawī relates it to the lasting word in 43:28. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says 'my sons' in 14:35 likewise means some descendants. [26 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABRISI; ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 14:40/q06/p2 Min marks the beginning or source among Abraham's descendants, allowing the fullest good to be asked for all of them. — Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers initial min because Abraham asks the fullest good for himself and his offspring. He rejects the partitive explanation as weak: if revelation already established which descendants would pray, the prayer would seek an accomplished result. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 14:40/q06/p3 In the setting of the Meccan settlement, the phrase points to Ishmael rather than every descendant of Abraham. — Iṣlāḥī says only Ishmael among Abraham's descendants was settled there. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 14:40/q07 How is 'and from my descendants' construed with 'make me'?  [ٱجْعَلْنِى وَمِن ذُرِّيَّتِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether the ellipsis is an object noun coordinated with 'me' or a repeated verb supplied from the first clause.
- 14:40/q07/p1 It coordinates an unspoken 'some of my descendants' with the object 'me.' — Abū ʿUbayda supplies an omitted pronoun. Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī, Samīn, and Abū al-Baqāʾ as reported by Ālūsī coordinate with the first object. Samīn and the Shihāb marginalia explicitly supply an omitted baʿḍ or noun; the latter says the construction is awkward without it. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the phrase an adjective of an omitted noun coordinated with the first-person suffix. [7 notes: MAJAZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء,الحواشي الشهابية); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 14:40/q07/p2 The second clause omits its verb, understood from the preceding 'make.' — Al-Ṭabrisī supplies the previous verb to yield 'make some descendants establish prayer.' [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 14:40/q08 How is the alternative 'for my children' reading assessed?  [وَمِن ذُرِّيَّتِى · readings]
- 14:40/q08/p1 A reported reading says 'for my children,' specifying Ishmael and Isaac. — Al-Aṣamm reports the reading but rejects it as impermissible because it conflicts with the widespread reading. [0 notes: JISHUMI(الأصم)-]

## 14:40/q09 Why does Abraham mention himself first and use a singular prayer request?  [ٱجْعَلْنِى وَمِن ذُرِّيَّتِى · rhetoric]
- 14:40/q09/p1 He presents himself as the exemplar whose descendants should follow or join him in excellence. — Ālūsī says the singular marks Abraham as exemplar and the descendants as followers. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares his wish for lasting good mention in 26:84 and his imamate and descendants after trial in 2:124. Biqāʿī says he uses the singular while appealing through blessings uniquely bestowed on him in his time. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 14:40/q10 What does 'accept my prayer' ask God to accept?  [وَتَقَبَّلْ دُعَآءِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether duʿāʾ means worship or petition; Ālūsī appeals to the plural address to prefer the petition, while other sources cite prophetic sayings and Quranic usage for worship.
- 14:40/q10/p1 Accept his worship, deeds, and acts of obedience, with acceptance carrying recompense. — Ṭabarī says deeds and worship; Ibn ʿAbbās is cited by Wāḥidī and Rāzī, the latter comparing 19:48. Thaʿlabī, Baghawī, and Qurṭubī cite the saying that supplication is the core or marrow of worship; Thaʿlabī and Qurṭubī also cite 40:60, and Ṭabarī reports the Prophet saying supplication is worship with 40:60. Al-Ṭabrisī distinguishes accepting obedience as rewarding it; Jishumī says taqabbul includes recompense. Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, and Study Quran give worship as one alternative. Ālūsī reports it but objects that worship shared with descendants should more aptly be called 'our worship.' [15 notes: TAB-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI(المفسرون,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الحديث); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABRISI; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]
- 14:40/q10/p2 Answer this supplication for Abraham and his descendants, or all the requests he has made. — Muqātil explicitly identifies the prayer for Abraham and descendants to establish prayer; Bursawī adds firmness and avoidance of idolatry. Ibn Kathīr says everything Abraham has requested. Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, and Study Quran give answering the supplication as a sense; Study Quran specifies this verse. Ālūsī calls this the apparent meaning and explains the plural address through the descendants' inclusion. Qāshānī gives an inward request for complete annihilation in God. [9 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABRISI; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 14:40/q10/p3 In an inward reading, accept the request for complete annihilation in God. — Qāshānī interprets the accepted prayer in terms of fanāʾ. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 14:40/q11 How is the final yāʾ of 'my prayer' read in connection and at pause?  [دُعَآءِ · readings]
turns on: Reader and transmission, and whether connection or pause preserves the possessive yāʾ; analogy favors preservation in connection, while verse ending and ease favor omission.
- 14:40/q11/p1 Retain the possessive yāʾ in connected recitation and at pause in some transmissions. — Ibn Mujāhid, Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī, Ṭabrisī, Nasafī, Samīn, and Ālūsī report al-Bazzī from Ibn Kathīr in both states; Ibn Mujāhid also reports specific Nāfiʿ routes in connection. Nashr lists Yaʿqūb and al-Bazzī in both states, with Qunbul varying. Zajjāj calls retention in connection best; al-Fārisī says full retention follows regular analogy. Qirāʾāt-ER's Yz pronounces the first-person yāʾ overtly. [14 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير عن البزي، نافع عن الأصمعي، ورش,ابن كثير، حمزة، أبو عمرو، حفص عن عاصم); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وحمزة وهبيرة عن حفص عن عاصم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو وابن كثير); TABRISI(البزي عن ابن كثير); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(طلحة); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو وحمزة وورش); NASHR(أبو جعفر وأبو عمرو وحمزة وورش ويعقوب والبزي وقنبل); ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وحمزة وهبيرة عن حفص); QIRAAT-ER(Yz); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 14:40/q11/p2 Retain or hint at yāʾ in connection but omit it at pause. — Ibn Mujāhid, al-Fārisī, Ṭabrisī, and Ālūsī name Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr, Ḥamza, and a Ḥafṣ route, with Qunbul hinting at the sound then stopping on alif. Ibn Mujāhid, al-Fārisī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, and Abū Ḥayyān report Warsh or other Nāfiʿ routes retaining it in connection. Samīn includes Abū ʿAmr, Ḥamza, and Warsh. Nashr adds Abū Jaʿfar and Yaʿqūb's both-state retention separately. Ṭabrisī and al-Fārisī say the kasra can signal an omitted or hinted yāʾ; both consider deletion more natural at pause, citing al-Aʿshā's verse. Qirāʾāt-ER's Yz distinguishes pause without possession from connection with 'my calling.' [22 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير عن البزي، نافع عن الأصمعي، ورش,ابن كثير، حمزة، أبو عمرو، حفص عن عاصم,عاصم عن أبي عمارة، قنبل عن ابن كثير، نافع عن غير الأصمعي); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وحمزة وهبيرة عن حفص عن عاصم,نافع); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو وابن كثير,ورش عن نافع); TABRISI(أبو علي,ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وحمزة وهبيرة عن حفص,البزي عن ابن كثير); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(طلحة); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو وحمزة وورش); NASHR(أبو جعفر وأبو عمرو وحمزة وورش ويعقوب والبزي وقنبل); ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وحمزة وهبيرة عن حفص); IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER(Yz)]
- 14:40/q11/p3 Omit the yāʾ in both connected recitation and pause, reading duʿāʾ. — Ibn Mujāhid names al-Kisāʾī, Ibn ʿĀmir, Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim, Abū ʿUmāra's ʿĀṣim route, and other Nāfiʿ routes. Al-Fārisī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ṭabrisī, and Ālūsī report omissions with differing reader lists: Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes Nāfiʿ, Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamza, and al-Kisāʾī, and separately Ṭalḥa and al-Aʿmash. Ālūsī and Nashr's fuller accounts distinguish retained routes. Al-Fārisī permits deletion at verse endings like poetic rhyme and cites al-Aʿshā. Ibn ʿĀshūr attributes most readers' omission to lightness. Zajjāj permits omission in connection and says pause lacks yāʾ. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNMUJAHID(الكسائي، ابن عامر، عاصم عن أبي بكر,عاصم عن أبي عمارة، قنبل عن ابن كثير، نافع عن غير الأصمعي); FARISI-HUJJA(الباقون,نافع); IBNATIYYA-FULL(طلحة والأعمش,نافع وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي); TABRISI(الباقون); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(طلحة); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو وحمزة وورش); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 14:40/q12 How is Warsh's lengthening in 'accept my prayer' analyzed?  [وَتَقَبَّلْ دُعَآءِ · readings]
turns on: Whether incidental opening or orthographic loss affects the length, or the underlying form governs it.
- 14:40/q12/p1 Treat it by Warsh's usual lengthening pattern according to the underlying form. — Ibn al-Jazarī groups wa-taqabbal duʿāʾ with millat ābāʾī Ibrāhīm and fa-lam yazidhum duʿāʾī illā, disregarding incidental opening or written deletion; he says this is his analogy learned in performance for Abraham's duʿāʾ. [1 notes: NASHR]
- 14:40/q12/p2 In the discussed incidental case, shortening is more likely, though lengthening is possible under the general rule. — Ibn al-Jazarī reasons that both hamzat al-waṣl and initial position are incidental, so they should not govern length. [1 notes: NASHR+]

## 14:40/q13 What reports describe the effect of Abraham's prayer on his descendants?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports describe different outcomes of the prayer rather than competing counts or persons.
- 14:40/q13/p1 God answered Abraham's requests, including descendants' prayer, avoidance of idols, and other blessings. — Kashshāf reports Mujāhid: no descendant worshipped an idol afterward; the land became safe and fruitful; Abraham became an imam; some descendants established prayer; God taught him the rites and accepted his repentance. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 14:40/q13/p2 Some descendants remain on the natural faith, worshipping God until the Hour. — Nasafī reports Ibn ʿAbbās; Durr reports Ibn Jurayj, who explicitly says they worship God. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج)]

## 14:40/q14 How does this prayer fit the surrounding passage and its address to Quraysh?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources emphasize either the prayer's place in Abraham's sequence of requests or its lesson for Quraysh.
- 14:40/q14/p1 It continues a sequence of shared requests for protection from idolatry, prayer, and forgiveness. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls prayer the second shared request after 14:35 and before forgiveness in 14:41. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this a new sentence continuing Abraham's prayer. Nashr says Abraham models supplication beginning and ending with praise, moving from God's knowledge and gifts to his request for prayer. Study Quran says the sūrah is named from Abraham's episode, whose prayers seek Meccan security, protection from idolatry, prosperity, and forgiveness for his parents and believers on the Last Day. [5 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; NASHR; STUDYQURAN]
- 14:40/q14/p2 The retelling reminds Quraysh of their settlement's purpose and Abraham's prayer behind its blessings. — Iṣlāḥī says Quraysh's lives oppose Abraham's hopes while Muhammad and his followers fulfill them; he says Quraysh wrongly credit alleged deities for blessings from Abraham's supplication. The account concerns Makkah and its settled children. Kur'an Yolu reports commentators who place the prayer after Mecca had become a settlement and Abraham and Ishmael had built the Kaaba; its authors present Abraham's gratitude and monotheistic way as exemplary servitude and salvation. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
