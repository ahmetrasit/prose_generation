# 29:48: 23 questions

## 29:48/q01 What does tatlu mean here?  [تَتْلُوا۟ · meaning]
- 29:48/q01/p1 It means to read or recite, here a book. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām glosses it as reading; Jishumī allows recitation or reading; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says tilāwa may be from memory or a written text, while the context here indicates a book. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; JISHUMI; TABATABAI]

## 29:48/q02 Why say tatlu rather than taqra?  [تَتْلُوا۟ · rhetoric]
- 29:48/q02/p1 Tatlu is lighter in pronunciation than taqra. — Al-Bārizī points to the hamza that makes taqra heavier. [1 notes: ITQAN(البارزي)]

## 29:48/q03 What does 'before it' refer to?  [مِن قَبْلِهِۦ · referent]
- 29:48/q03/p1 Before the Qur'an or its revelation. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām, Ibn al-Jawzī and Qurṭubī identify the pronoun with the Qur'an; the passage concerns the time before its arrival. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 29:48/q04 How does min kitāb work grammatically?  [مِن كِتَٰبٍۢ · grammar]
- 29:48/q04/p1 Kitāb is the object of tatlu and min reinforces negation. — Abū ʿUbayda, as reported by Ibn al-Jawzī, and Abū Ḥayyān call min extra under negation; Majāz compares 69:47. Samīn says min qablihi can qualify kitāb circumstantially or attach to tatlu. Biqāʿī says the repeated min excludes every book and any amount of prior reading, little or much. [6 notes: MAJAZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL]

## 29:48/q05 What earlier reading or writing does the verse deny?  [وَمَا كُنتَ تَتْلُوا۟ مِن قَبْلِهِۦ مِن كِتَٰبٍۢ وَلَا تَخُطُّهُۥ بِيَمِينِكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the negation describes actual habitual conduct, ability, or a prohibition; the verb's mood and the verse's period delimit the readings.
- 29:48/q05/p1 Muhammad had read no earlier book and written none; he was unlettered and unable to read or write. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatāda explicitly describe inability, with Qatāda defining ummī as one who does not write. Muqātil, Zajjāj, Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī and others cite the Torah or Gospel description; Ibn Kathīr says his people had long known his condition. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the two clauses exclude both memorization and copying, since either alone would not establish illiteracy. Muqātil and Majāz clarify that he had written no book. The Study Quran cites 7:157; Kuran Yolu cites 7:157–158. [40 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد,يحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود,الضحاك,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 29:48/q05/p2 The verse denies an established practice of reading or writing, without settling bare ability. — Biqāʿī says he had never regularly read or mastered writing; Bursawī says he had not customarily recited a revealed book or written one. Ālūsī allows inability or lack of custom. Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers the habitual reading as fitting the proof and cites his lifetime among his people in 10:16. The actual lack of a visible course of study matters to Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr. [8 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 29:48/q05/p3 The verse denies the ability to read and write before revelation. — Reported as an alternative by Ṭabāṭabāʾī, who prefers the habitual-practice reading; Ṭabrisī says he did not know how to read then. Al-Murtaḍā likewise limits the negation to the time before prophethood and says Gabriel could later have taught him. [4 notes: TABATABAI(قيل); TABRISI(الشريف الأجل المرتضى)]
- 29:48/q05/p4 The writing clause forbids him to write after revelation and does not deny his ability before it. — Bursawī reports this Shīʿī construction. Jishumī says the verb is indicative coordinated with the negation; al-Asʾila al-Muqḥima argues a prohibition would need subjunctive ṭāʾ or an intensified verb. Kashf al-Asrār records a rare fatḥa reading as a prohibition but endorses the denial of writing. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الشيعة); JISHUMI-; BURSEVI(الأسئلة المقحمة,كشف الأسرار)-]

## 29:48/q06 How is 'nor do you write it' construed?  [وَلَا تَخُطُّهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: The mood and reading of the writing verb: indicative negation versus a prohibitive form.
- 29:48/q06/p1 It is an indicative negation coordinated with the earlier negation. — Jishumī says a prohibition would take the subjunctive; al-Asʾila al-Muqḥima adds that the verb could be intensified as lā tukhaṭṭiṭhu. Kashf al-Asrār reports a rare reading with fatḥa on ṭāʾ but prefers the negative sense. [3 notes: JISHUMI; BURSEVI(الأسئلة المقحمة); BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار)+]
- 29:48/q06/p2 It can be read as a prohibition. — The Shīʿī reading reported by Bursawī takes it as a command not to write; Kashf al-Asrār notes an uncommon fatḥa on ṭāʾ. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الشيعة,كشف الأسرار); JISHUMI-; BURSEVI(الأسئلة المقحمة)-]

## 29:48/q07 What does khaṭṭ mean?  [تَخُطُّهُۥ · meaning]
- 29:48/q07/p1 To write or draw marks. — Jishumī gives khaṭṭa-yakhuṭṭu and its imperative and negative forms; Bursawī says the root denotes drawing something long and is applied to writing; Ṭabāṭabāʾī glosses it as writing. [3 notes: JISHUMI; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 29:48/q08 Did Muhammad later read or write, and how do the reported acts fit this verse?  [مِن قَبْلِهِۦ وَلَا تَخُطُّهُۥ · reports]
turns on: The time limit of 'before it', the force of hadiths saying 'he wrote', their reliability, and whether later writing would conflict with or add to the miracle of illiteracy.
- 29:48/q08/p1 He never personally read or wrote; reports of writing mean that he ordered a scribe, and reports of literacy are weak. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya rejects the reports of al-Shaʿbī, Abū Kabsha and al-Bājī as weak. Qurṭubī prefers that he wrote no letter or read or spelled anything and notes twenty-six revelation scribes; Qāḍī ʿIyāḍ cites Muʿāwiya receiving letter-form instructions while writing for him. A later scholar argues writing would cancel the miracle of illiteracy. Most scholars, according to Abū Ḥayyān, treat 'he wrote' as ordinary agency, like a ruler writing through a scribe. Ibn Kathīr compares the Bukhārī and other Hudaybiyya wordings, says the Prophet remained unable to write a line, and calls the report of learned writing baseless. Al-Baghawī's Tahdhīb chooses this as sounder. Qurṭubī answers the Dajjāl objection with Ḥudhayfa's report that any believer can read its mark. Ālūsī says 'before it' preceding the writing negation nearly indicates he never wrote, though he calls the figurative account contrary to the hadith's apparent wording. [17 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية,بعض المتأخرين,حذيفة,معاوية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أكثر العلماء); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة); ALUSI-FULL(البغوي)+; QURTUBI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(القاضي عياض)-]
- 29:48/q08/p2 He personally wrote, or learned to read and write, after his earlier unlettered state. — Al-Shaʿbī is reported to say he wrote before death; Abū Kabsha reports that he read and explained ʿUyayna's document. Al-Barāʾ and Bukhārī's Hudaybiyya reports say he erased 'Messenger of God' and wrote 'son of ʿAbd Allāh'; one Bukhārī route also says he did not know writing. Al-Sumnānī, Abū Dharr, al-Bājī and others accept personal writing; al-Bājī was denounced and later defended his view before scholars. Ālūsī cites Qāḍī ʿIyāḍ via Nawawī that 'he could not write, then wrote' is nearly explicit. Some argue 'before it' scopes both negations and implies later literacy, but Ālūsī says that inference relies on an implication their advocate rejects. Abū al-ʿAbbās Aḥmad ibn ʿUmar says the reports are solitary and nondefinitive, so proponents should not be charged with unbelief. Ālūsī observes that the unlettered-community hadith need only describe early conditions and ultimately leaves the dispute to God. [22 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو كبشة السلولي,الشعبي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو العباس أحمد بن عمر,البخاري,البراء,السمناني,الشعبي); NASAFI-FULL(مجاهد والشعبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو ذر الهروي والباجي,أبو كبشة السلولي,الشعبي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو الوليد الباجي,أبو ذر عبد بن أحمد الهروي وأبو الفتح النيسابوري وأبو الوليد الباجي,ابن أبي شيبة وغيره,الشعبي,القاضي عياض,بعض الأجلة,بعضهم,رسول الله صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية)-; IBNKATHIR-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 29:48/q08/p3 A personally written mark could have been a new miracle without learned literacy. — Al-Sumnānī says God could move the Prophet's hand and pen to make intelligible marks without study, like knowledge given without learning; supporters cite the hadith that the community neither writes nor calculates. Ibn Kathīr suggests al-Bājī may have meant miraculous Hudaybiyya writing and compares the Dajjāl's written kafir mark, also pronounced as three letters. Ālūsī says learning writing after being unlettered would not erase the first miracle, while untrained writing would itself be another. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(السمناني); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(بعض المتأخرين)-]
- 29:48/q08/p4 The verse leaves post-revelation literacy open; God might have taught him later. — Al-Murtaḍā restricts the explicit negation to before prophethood and says either later literacy or continued illiteracy is possible, even through Gabriel's teaching. Qāḍī ʿIyāḍ allows granted knowledge of writing while he was kept from actually reading and writing; he doubts the report of personal writing. Ālūsī says the unlettered-community hadith does not explicitly establish lifelong inability. [4 notes: TABRISI(الشريف الأجل المرتضى); QURTUBI-FULL(القاضي عياض); ALUSI-FULL]
- 29:48/q08/p5 He could miraculously perceive written meaning without ordinary reading. — Ālūsī says God could grant reading without writing to account for the Night Journey report of the inscription at Paradise's gate promising charity tenfold and a loan eighteenfold; alternatively the report may omit his asking its meaning. An unidentified book says letters told him their names, as the poisoned foreleg spoke, but Ālūsī rejects this without a sound report. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض الكتب,رسول الله صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 29:48/q09 Why specify the right hand?  [بِيَمِينِكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the expression primarily stresses the normal physical instrument or uses it as a metonym for personal action.
- 29:48/q09/p1 It vividly and literally emphasizes writing with the usual instrument, the right hand. — Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī say naming the hand strengthens the denial and excludes figurative attribution. Nasafī, Bursawī and Ibn ʿĀshūr invoke ordinary right-handed writing; Bursawī says this does not rule out rare left-handed writing. Biqāʿī points to the stronger hand; Ibn Kathīr and Ibn ʿĀshūr compare a bird flying with its wings at 6:38. Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares 'I saw with my eyes.' Abū Ḥayyān says the mention reinforces the proof. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 29:48/q09/p2 Right hand can stand for one's own hand. — Asad calls yamīn, literally right hand, a metonym for personal writing. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 29:48/q10 How is idhan and the consequence construed?  [إِذًۭا لَّٱرْتَابَ · grammar]
- 29:48/q10/p1 It answers an implicit condition: had he read or written before, the falsifiers would have doubted. — Farrāʾ, Nasafī, Burhān and Ibn ʿĀshūr reconstruct an omitted law condition. Majāz expands the compressed clause with a pronoun: 'if you had read the book and written it.' Samīn takes the same counterfactual sense. Burhān compares an implicit conditional about supposed other gods. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Qurayẓ al-ʿAnbarī's camel-raiding poem, al-Marzūqī's imagined question, and another possible second answer to law; he compares the saying 'if free, I would condemn slaves' deeds; then I would admire free men's deeds.' [9 notes: MAJAZ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL(المرزوقي)]

## 29:48/q11 What does irtiyāb mean here?  [لَّٱرْتَابَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the counterfactual describes first doubt or stronger doubt among those already suspicious.
- 29:48/q11/p1 Doubt or the occurrence of suspicion. — Muqātil and Mukhtār gloss the root as doubt; Jishumī adds suspicion; Ibn ʿĀshūr describes doubt arising in the mind, and distinguishes preliminary suspicion from justified denial. Al-Rāghib, quoted by Bursawī, contrasts rayb as a suspicion exposed as false with irāba as one not removed and treats irtiyāb like irāba, citing 74:31. [6 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI; BURSEVI(الراغب,المختار); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 29:48/q11/p2 For opponents already in doubt, it means their suspicion would increase. — Farrāʾ gives this explanation as reported by Wāḥidī; Wāḥidī applies it especially to Quraysh, whose existing suspicion would grow. [3 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)]

## 29:48/q12 Who are al-mubṭilūn in this verse?  [ٱلْمُبْطِلُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the relevant doubt is an accusation of copying from Meccan opponents or a mismatch with the scriptural description known to the People of the Book.
- 29:48/q12/p1 The Quraysh or Meccan polytheists. — Mujāhid repeatedly names Quraysh; Qatāda and others are also cited. Their hypothetical accusation is that Muhammad learned from someone or copied earlier books. Ālūsī prefers this identification. Study Quran transmits it alongside the People-of-the-Book view. [17 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ(قيل); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد وقتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة ومجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Z); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد)+]
- 29:48/q12/p2 Opposing People of the Book, especially Jewish unbelievers. — Muqātil, Suddi and others identify Jews; Farrāʾ names Christians familiar with the description. Wāḥidī prefers the People of the Book because they knew the unlettered prophet from scripture yet denied him. Qatāda is cited by Ibn ʿAṭiyya and others for Jews or the People of the Book. Some say the Torah or Gospel would contradict a literate claimant. [18 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي,بعضهم); FARRA; MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة ومجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Z); WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 29:48/q12/p3 Deniers among both the People of the Book and the Arabs, or opponents generally. — Biqāʿī explicitly includes both groups. Wāḥidī reports Ibn ʿAbbās's generic 'unbelievers'; Elmali describes unfair deniers seeking a pretext rather than fair-minded seekers. Farrāʾ also allows liars in Mecca and elsewhere. [4 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 29:48/q13 What does mubṭilūn mean, and why call them that?  [ٱلْمُبْطِلُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the label points simply to false claims or specifically to how remaining evidence makes their denial baseless even under the hypothetical.
- 29:48/q13/p1 People who assert falsehood, deny truth, or try to invalidate it. — Wāḥidī says a mubṭil lies or claims contrary to truth and applies it to adherents of other religions; Ibn al-Jawzī says they bring falsehood; Bursawī contrasts muḥiqq and quotes Mufradāt that ibṭāl removes or destroys, citing 8:8. Ṭabāṭabāʾī favors those who call truth false; Asad cites lexicons allowing false claims, attempts to nullify, and judgments that something is unfounded, regardless of the target's actual truth. Nasafī, Ālūsī, Elmali and Ibn ʿĀshūr apply it to denial of prophethood and revelation; Ibn ʿĀshūr links the label with disbelievers in 29:47. [14 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(المفردات); ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 29:48/q13/p2 The label marks their denial as baseless even in the counterfactual of a literate Prophet. — Kashshāf and Ālūsī say illiteracy leaves no actual ground for doubt. Yet Kashshāf, Ālūsī, and Elmali point to other prophets' miracles and the Qur'an's inimitability: literacy would remove one evidential advantage, not excuse rejection. Al-Kashf calls the conditional illustrative of the miracle's role. Ibn ʿĀshūr says such doubt might be preliminary but certainty of denial remains unwarranted after examining the Qur'an's style and meaning. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 29:48/q14 What doubt or accusation would earlier literacy have invited?  [إِذًۭا لَّٱرْتَابَ ٱلْمُبْطِلُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Which audience's reason to doubt is in view: a possible human source of the Qur'an or a mismatch with the prophet described in earlier scriptures.
- 29:48/q14/p1 Opponents would allege that he learned, copied, compiled, or authored the Qur'an from previous books or a teacher. — Muqātil distinguishes the charge that he composed it from reading and wrote it from writing. Ṭabarī also lists rhymed soothsaying and ancient tales; Qatāda says they would allege that he learned and wrote material himself. Jishumī says writing while claiming not to would heighten suspicion. Ālūsī says a detailed book would require long, visible study; Kur'an Yolu says reading and writing are ways to learn from others, and notes that allegations persisted during the thirteen Meccan years of the twenty-three-year mission. Ibn Kathīr and Study Quran cite the 'written down and dictated' charge at 25:5–6 and its parallels 6:25, 8:31, 16:24, 23:83, 27:68, 46:17, 68:15 and 83:13. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the book's style and content answer preliminary suspicion. [28 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة,يحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 29:48/q14/p2 People acquainted with prior scriptures would doubt that he matched their foretold unlettered Prophet. — Muqātil, Farrāʾ, Wāḥidī, Kashshāf, Ibn al-Jawzī and others refer to Torah and Gospel descriptions of a prophet who neither reads nor writes. Wāḥidī says they denied after knowing his attributes with certainty. Mujāhid says the verse was revealed in accord with the People of the Book's scriptural description. [22 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT+]

## 29:48/q15 What does the verse establish about the Qur'an's origin and Muhammad's mission?  [* · interpretation]
- 29:48/q15/p1 His unlettered condition together with the Qur'an's content shows revelation from God and supports his prophethood. — Bāqillānī says he had no historical teachers or scriptures yet told history from Adam onward, citing 6:105. Ibn ʿAṭiyya stresses the Qur'an's length, inimitability and unseen reports. Ṭabrisī points to people who watched him and saw no teacher. Qurṭubī and Naḥḥās add he did not mingle with People of the Book in Mecca yet conveyed earlier prophetic accounts; Bayḍāwī cites its noble sciences. Rāzī calls the unlettered delivery itself a miracle, links 2:23's 'someone like him' to his inability, and invokes 2:1. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds 42:52 and 10:16; Asad says this should persuade an unbiased reader. Study Quran calls non-authorship a belief shared across Islamic schools and cites rejection of the poet charge at 26:224–227 and 36:69. Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr and others say illiteracy removes a human-source pretext, though Rāzī, Kashshāf and Elmali say literacy would not defeat the Qur'an's independent miracle. [28 notes: BAQILLANI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 29:48/q16 How does this verse advance the argument of the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Which nearby passage or broader Qur'anic feature is being connected to this verse.
- 29:48/q16/p1 It adds the Prophet's unlettered delivery to the preceding proof that the Qur'an is revealed. — Rāzī says 29:47 had argued by analogy from the miracle proving earlier books revealed; this verse supplies another miracle. Ibn ʿĀshūr parallels the 'disbelievers' in 29:47 with the 'falsifiers' here. Burhān associates the disjoint opening letters with his lack of writing training; it counts fourteen of an alphabet it regards as twenty-nine, explains the extra letter and the alif/hamza naming, one omitted letter for concision, and twenty-nine opening-letter sūras with some forms avoided for difficult pronunciation. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN]
- 29:48/q16/p2 The context of 29:50–51 concerns requested signs, so 29:51 is a poor heading for the hadith on melodic Qur'an recitation. — Ibn Kathīr rejects Bukhārī's chapter placement on the basis of 29:48–51's argumentative context. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 29:48/q17 Can an unlettered person read without writing?  [* · meaning]
- 29:48/q17/p1 Yes; unlettered can describe someone who reads but cannot write. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports this definition from Ibrāhīm, differing from Qatāda's definition of one who does not write and the accounts that the Prophet could neither read nor write. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(إبراهيم)]

## 29:48/q18 How did scholars receive al-Bājī's claim of the Prophet's writing?  [* · reports]
- 29:48/q18/p1 Jurists strongly condemned the claim. — Ibn Kathīr says jurists in East and West denounced those who followed al-Bājī and disassociated themselves from that position. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 29:48/q19 What apparent objection from the Dajjāl report was raised against lifelong illiteracy?  [* · reports]
- 29:48/q19/p1 The Prophet reportedly spelled the letters of kafir inscribed between the Dajjāl's eyes. — Qurṭubī records this as an objection, then invokes Ḥudhayfa's report that every believer, whether literate or not, can read the mark. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله)]

## 29:48/q20 What further conclusions are drawn from preventing suspicion?  [* · theology]
turns on: These are separate inferences from the same preventive rationale, not competing accounts of the verse.
- 29:48/q20/p1 A messenger's protection from acts that repel acceptance implies still stronger protection from major sins and transgression. — Jishumī says God prevented reading and writing to secure acceptance of the caller, lawgiver and explainer; avoidance of major sins and fisq follows even more strongly. [2 notes: JISHUMI]
- 29:48/q20/p2 The conditional variation in doubt shows doubt is a human act rather than an act created by God. — Jishumī argues that if God created doubt it would persist independent of the Prophet's literacy; he uses this against the Mujbira's account of creation and capacity. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 29:48/q21 What inward significance is given to his inability to read?  [* · inward]
- 29:48/q21/p1 Divine love and election left him free of otherness so revelation could address him. — Abū Saʿīd al-Kharrāz, reported by Sulamī, says love erased customary forms and natural traces; when first commanded to read he answered that he could not, then found ease in the command to read in his Lord's name at 96:1. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو سعيد الخراز)]

## 29:48/q22 What do related reports say about consulting the People of the Book?  [* · reports]
- 29:48/q22/p1 Muslims should not seek guidance from them, given the risk of accepting falsehood or rejecting truth. — Jābir reports the Prophet warning against questions to them and saying even Moses would follow him; Zayd ibn Aslam transmits a warning that they have gone astray; Ibn Masʿūd says to avoid asking, or if unavoidable, accept what agrees with the Qur'an and leave what conflicts. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,جابر بن عبد الله,زيد بن أسلم)]

## 29:48/q23 How is the related hadith's taghannī with the Qur'an understood?  [* · reports]
turns on: The hadith's apparent recitational sense and Shāfiʿī's argument about the preposition needed for 'sufficiency'.
- 29:48/q23/p1 It means audible, beautiful, plaintive or melodic recitation. — Ibn Kathīr prefers this apparent sense; Ibn Wahb says chanting melodiously, and Muzanī and Rabīʿ transmit it from Shāfiʿī. Shāfiʿī says recitation is with sorrow and melody and argues that sufficiency would require yataghanā bihi. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن وهب,الشافعي); IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 29:48/q23/p2 It means finding sufficiency in the Qur'an, possibly dispensing with worldly things. — Sufyān ibn ʿUyayna gives this reading and Abū ʿUbayd and others follow it in the worldly-sufficiency sense; Ibn Kathīr and Shāfiʿī reject that account of the hadith. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(سفيان بن عيينة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الشافعي)-]
