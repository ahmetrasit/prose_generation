# 75:4: 18 questions

## 75:4/q01 What does balā affirm in reply to the preceding question?  [بَلَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the particle continues the negative question or starts a new utterance.
- 75:4/q01/p1 It reverses the preceding denial: God will certainly gather the bones and resurrect the person. — Al-Akhfash treats it as completing the preceding question; al-Nasafi and al-Samin explain its response to a negative interrogative. Elmalılı contrasts balā with naʿam and lā, which can leave the negative standing. Al-Burhān says it rebukes denial; Bursawī says reflective reason confirms divine power. The implied answer is 'We gather them.' [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الأخفش); BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(الجمهور); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 75:4/q01/p2 Balā begins a fresh emphatic exclamation after the prior statement has ended. — Al-Māwardī transmits this as the second construction. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 75:4/q02 Where is the answer to the opening oath?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the following resurrection passage serves as an expressed oath response or the response is understood.
- 75:4/q02/p1 The answer is the passage from the question about gathering bones through the assertion of capability. — Al-Wāḥidī places it from 75:3 through 75:4. Al-Jishumī reports an analysis supplying 'We shall surely gather them, able.' [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI]
- 75:4/q02/p2 The oath's answer is omitted: the implied sworn claim is that people will be resurrected. — Al-Wāḥidī reports al-Naḥḥās; al-Jishumī reports this alongside the explicit-answer analysis. Al-Ālūsī and al-Sāmarrāʾī link the omission to the sūra's force or haste. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو جعفر النحاس); JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:4/q03 How is the accusative qādirīn construed?  [قَٰدِرِينَ · grammar]
turns on: The governing ellipsis and whether case comes from a circumstantial relation, a new reckoning, or substitution for a finite verb.
- 75:4/q03/p1 It is a circumstantial accusative describing the subject of an implied 'We gather them.' — Sībawayh cites Yūnus for the ellipsis. Al-Ṭabarī prefers the Basran analysis as sound Arabic; al-Naḥḥās derives al-Farrāʾ's account from Sībawayh. Al-Ṭabrisī adduces 2:239 for ellipsis. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the omitted verb also conveys haste. [22 notes: SIBAWAYH; AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(بعض نحوي البصرة); NAHHAS(سيبويه); WAHIDI-BASIT; QUSHAYRI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(سيبويه); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور وابن أبي عبلة وابن السميفع); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN(سيبويه); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(سيبويه); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; BURHAN+; SAMIN-DURR(العامة)+; TAB-FULL+]
- 75:4/q03/p2 It breaks from the gathering clause to assert capacity for even more than bone gathering. — Al-Farrāʾ supplies an omitted expression, 'Yes, we are able'; a Kufan grammarian contrasts this with the participle-for-verb account. Al-Farazdaq's accusative khārijan is cited under an implied vow; al-Wāḥidī spells out the pledge not to curse or depart. [9 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(الفرزدق,بعض نحوي الكوفة); NAHHAS(الفراء); THALABI(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 75:4/q03/p3 It replaces a finite 'we are able' with an accusative active participle. — Al-Ṭabarī reports the proposal. A Kufan grammarian and al-Farrāʾ object that changing a verb to a participle cannot itself license the case; al-Naḥḥās says the constructions have different syntax, and instead coordinates the poem's 'departing' with the syntactic position of 'I do not curse.' Al-Burhān says no recognized rule licenses the accusative merely for standing in a verb's place. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم); NAHHAS(الفرزدق); TAB-FULL(بعض نحوي الكوفة)-; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)-; NAHHAS-; BURHAN-]
- 75:4/q03/p4 It is accusative under an implied 'we reckon ourselves capable.' — Ibn al-Jawzī presents repetition of balā; al-Qurṭubī reports al-Farrāʾ's repeated assertion. Al-Burhān reports al-Farrāʾ's supplied ḥasibnā/naḥsib and a defense that it means firm belief; it also reports an objection that explicit ḥisāb suggests conjecture, and prefers Sībawayh because balā answers denial of gathering rather than a question about human supposition. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); BURHAN(الفراء); BURHAN-]
- 75:4/q03/p5 It is the predicate of an omitted kāna, 'we were capable from the beginning.' — Al-Qurṭubī reports that this original capacity was conceded even by polytheists. Al-Samīn finds the parse less clear and al-Ālūsī dismisses the resulting question about initial versus renewed capacity as untenable. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 75:4/q03/p6 It is accusative because it occupies the place of naqdِr, 'we are able.' — Al-Burhān reports the estimate balā naqdِr qādirīn. Al-Ālūsī relays Makkī's account and the objection that it would also license a circumstantial accusative after a noun. [2 notes: BURHAN(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(مكي)]
- 75:4/q03/p7 The implied reply is 'then We shall surely resurrect them, able.' — Al-Burhān reports al-Farrāʾ's estimate but calls it weak because it does not match the question's grammatical pattern. [1 notes: BURHAN(الفراء)]

## 75:4/q04 How is qādirīn read?  [قَٰدِرِينَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the participle is the accusative circumstantial form or a nominative predicate in a new clause.
- 75:4/q04/p1 The common reading has accusative qādirīn. — Al-Samīn and Abū Ḥayyān identify this as the majority reading, commonly analyzed as a circumstantial phrase; al-Naḥḥās explicitly notes its accusative case. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(العامة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور وابن أبي عبلة وابن السميفع); NAHHAS]
- 75:4/q04/p2 Ibn Abī ʿAbla and others read nominative qādirūn, 'we are capable.' — Ibn Abī ʿAbla is named by al-Jishumī and al-Thaʿlabī; al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī add Ibn al-Sumayqa/Ibn al-Sumayfi with spelling variants. The nominative is an independent clause with omitted 'we'; al-Farrāʾ also allows that construction. [8 notes: FARRA; THALABI; JISHUMI(ابن أبي عبلة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة وابن السميقع); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور وابن أبي عبلة وابن السميفع); SAMIN-DURR(ابن أبي عبلة وابن السميفع); ALUSI-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة وابن السمقيع)]

## 75:4/q05 Where may one pause around balā?  [بَلَىٰ قَٰدِرِينَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the response particle is a sufficient stopping point or syntactically tied to what follows.
- 75:4/q05/p1 A good pause follows balā before qādirīn begins the next clause. — Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī mark the pause there; Ibn al-Jawzī still supplies the sense 'We will assemble them, fully able.' [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 75:4/q05/p2 A stop at balā is impermissible because its following words complete the answer. — Al-Burhān treats this as one of seven connected uses of balā. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 75:4/q06 What does banān denote?  [بَنَانَهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the expression names all digits, their tips, or their fine sections; some authorities stress hands, others include feet.
- 75:4/q06/p1 It denotes fingers or their tips; its singular is banāna. — Al-Akhfash, al-Naḥḥās, al-Qurṭubī and al-Ālūsī give the singular; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ records plural or collective use and a lexicon's allowance of one finger. Al-Qāmūs and Ibn al-Athīr permit fingers or tips. Ibn ʿAbbās cites ʿAntara's reins line; al-Qurṭubī cites al-Nābigha's delicate tips and ʿAntara's hand on a sword. Ibn al-Jawzī points to 8:12 for his gloss. [13 notes: AKHFASH; NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL(عنترة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI(القاموس); ALUSI-FULL(القاموس); TABATABAI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن الأثير,ابن عباس)]
- 75:4/q06/p2 It specifically denotes the fingertips or small finger sections. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī gives fingertips as his gloss and reports fingers as another. Islahi takes it as each finger section. Bursawī calls it collective in sense though singular in form, like tamr, and says the tips are the final, smallest parts to form. Al-Zamakhsharī calls them the body's extremities and last formed parts. [4 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURSEVI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الزمخشري)]
- 75:4/q06/p3 The word includes the digits of feet as well as hands in this interpretation. — Mujāhid specifically identifies the feet; al-Ḍaḥḥāk allows feet or both hands and feet. Reports from al-Qushayrī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī include fingers and toes. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(الضحاك,مجاهد); QUSHAYRI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة ومجاهد وعكرمة والضحاك); TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 75:4/q06/p4 The name points to what fingers enable a person to arrange, indicate, or accomplish. — Bursawī cites al-Rāghib for ordering one's affairs; al-Ālūsī reports al-Rāghib's link to indicating intentions. Bursawī also cites the command to strike every fingertip at 8:12 as evidence of their role in fighting and defense. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL(الراغب)]

## 75:4/q07 What is meant by God's ability to make the banān even?  [نُّسَوِّىَ بَنَانَهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether taswiya describes exact restoration at resurrection, a possible loss of articulated digits, mere smoothing, or a changed whole-body form.
- 75:4/q07/p1 God can restore the original, distinct fingertips and their smallest joints exactly at resurrection. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Zajjāj, al-Qubaysī, al-Ṭabrisī and others reason from the tiny decayed phalanges to the larger bones; Ibn Qutayba illustrates the a fortiori challenge with bitter gourd and mustard. Al-Ṭabarī favors the Basran grammar; al-Zajjāj, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī favor this sense. Al-Ālūsī says the pieces may have dispersed anywhere; al-Nasafī stresses no loss or difference. [35 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; FARRA; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(القبيسي); MAWARDI-FULL(جرير بن عبد العزيز); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو علي الفارسي,ابن قتيبة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL(الزجاج وابن قتيبة); TABRISI(الزجاج والجبائي وأبو مسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة والزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(القتبي والزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(أبو حيان,الزمخشري); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس)+; QURTUBI-FULL+; TABATABAI+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 75:4/q07/p2 God could instead fuse fingers into a hoof-like or undivided form, depriving people of their dexterity. — Reports compare a camel's pad or hoof, a donkey's hoof, a pig's cloven hoof, an animal's paw, or a sheep's cloven hoof; these are variants of the same answer. The hand would not grasp, eat, write, sew or perform fine work. Al-Ḥasan contrasts opening and curling fingers with walking on palms; Qatāda imagines eating by mouth. Ibn Jarīr and Abū Ḥayyān place this power in earthly life; al-Ālūsī says that moves away from the apparent resurrection context. [45 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,الضحاك,عكرمة,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ(جاء في التفسير); NAHHAS(ابن عباس,الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي,أحمد بن يحيى,ابن عباس,المبرد,قتادة وعكرمة والحسن,مقاتل والكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JURJANI-ASRAR; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجمهور); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وعامة المفسرين,الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس والجمهور); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير,ابن عباس وسعيد بن جبير والعوفي ومجاهد وعكرمة والحسن وقتادة والضحاك وابن جرير); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,الضحاك,عكرمة,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة ومجاهد وعكرمة والضحاك); TABATABAI(قيل); BINTSHATI-IJAZ; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-; TABATABAI-; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس)-]
- 75:4/q07/p3 God can make the fingertips level and smooth. — Ibn Kathīr reports this from Ibn Qutayba and al-Zajjāj; his own description pairs gathering bones with making the tips level. The note does not specify whether 'smooth' entails their original articulation. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن قتيبة والزجاج)]
- 75:4/q07/p4 God could restore the person in an animal's form, showing He can restore the original human form. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this further alternative and compares 56:60–61. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]

## 75:4/q08 Why mention the fingertips in the proof of resurrection?  [بَنَانَهُۥ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the detail is singled out for smallness, usefulness, individual features, or representation of the whole.
- 75:4/q08/p1 Their tiny, delicate bones make a stronger proof: restoring these entails restoring larger bones. — Al-Qurṭubī says mention of the smallest bones draws attention to all remaining parts. Al-Ālūsī prefers the a fortiori argument over a mere statement of complete gathering. Al-Bayḍāwī gives either joining tiny joints or naming tips a fortiori. Elmalılı stresses the subtlety of their construction. [9 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; THALABI(القبيسي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 75:4/q08/p2 The fingers' dexterity and human usefulness display the care involved in creation. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Ḥasan and others contrast human grasping, eating, writing, sewing and defense with hooves. Aḥmad ibn Yaḥyā quotes the oath 'By the One who split them five from one'; al-Mubarrad contrasts the proverb that fingers in palms are not equal. Bursawī cites al-Rāghib on practical function and 8:12 on combat. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي,أحمد بن يحيى,المبرد); JURJANI-ASRAR; TABRISI(ابن عباس وقتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وعامة المفسرين,الحسن); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن); BURSEVI(الراغب); ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 75:4/q08/p3 The fingers' detailed features and markings express complete, individually precise recreation. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī names intricate form, number and markings as enabling countless crafts. Elmalılı describes fingertip sensitivity with an example of distinguishing materials in a dark shop by cane vibration. Kuran Yolu suggests palm and fingertip patterns may serve as a personal identity code. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports Jābir recognizing his martyred father at Uḥud only by the fingertips. [5 notes: TABATABAI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(جابر بن عبد الله بن عمرو الأنصاري)]
- 75:4/q08/p4 The fingers stand for the complete body and its full reconstruction. — Al-Ālūsī offers complete bodily gathering as an alternative emphasis; Kuran Yolu calls the fingers an eloquent part standing for the whole. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 75:4/q09 What does taswiya convey in this context?  [نُّسَوِّىَ · meaning]
- 75:4/q09/p1 It conveys complete forming and proportioning of the fingers, beyond simply putting their bones together. — Kuran Yolu cites 82:7 and 87:2, where taswiya gives a person bodily features during creation, then applies that usage to every feature of the recreated fingers. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 75:4/q10 What does the verse teach against denial of resurrection?  [* · interpretation]
- 75:4/q10/p1 God can and will reassemble the decayed body, down to its finest details. — Al-Māwardī calls this the reply to denial of bodily resurrection. Al-Ālūsī describes scattered bones in seas and deserts. Kuran Yolu says the polytheists' question after decay was mockery; the verses decisively censure it. The Study Quran compares first creation with renewed creation and cites 36:78–79 and 17:49–51. Ibn Qutayba illustrates the argument with bitter gourd and mustard. [10 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 75:4/q11 About whom was this passage revealed?  [* · reports]
- 75:4/q11/p1 It concerns ʿAdī ibn Rabīʿa and al-Akhnaṣ ibn Sharīq. — Muqātil names both. Al-Baghawī describes ʿAdī questioning the Prophet about resurrection and refusing belief even if he saw it; he also reports that the Prophet prayed for protection from the two bad neighbors, identified as ʿAdī and al-Akhnaṣ. [3 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 75:4/q12 Whom does the surrounding address to 'man' target?  [* · referent]
- 75:4/q12/p1 Its universal phrasing addresses Quraysh deniers of Judgment. — Islahi says the general address expresses disgust at their attitude. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 75:4/q13 How does this verse connect with other resurrection passages and the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: These explain different links: verbal parallels, the sūra's argument, the other use of banān, and the preceding bone image.
- 75:4/q13/p1 The objection about bones echoes other Qurʾānic resurrection challenges. — Al-Baghawī points to the denier's question about decayed bones at 36:78. The Study Quran cites its answer at 36:78–79 and related questions at 17:49–51. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 75:4/q13/p2 Specific shaping of fingertips here is paired with general creation and reviving the dead later in the sūra. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares this detail with general shaping at 75:38, direct affirmation here with interrogative affirmation of reviving the dead, and partial shaping with unrestricted creation. He notes the plural forms 'We are able/gather/shape' beside singular 'He created/proportioned.' [4 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:4/q13/p3 The sūra moves from Judgment and conscience through death to an argument from God's originating power. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes this arc and says the context marks the sūra Meccan. Islahi says verses 1–6 cite the reproaching soul against deniers who demand haste, likening them to a brazen thief before a judge. [2 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 75:4/q13/p4 The Qurʾān uses banān here and in the battle instruction at 8:12. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ identifies these as its two occurrences; Bursawī cites 8:12 for fingers' defensive use. [2 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ; BURSEVI(الراغب)]
- 75:4/q13/p5 Bones stand for the person because they frame the soul and bodily form. — Al-Baghawī reports this explanation of the preceding verse's reference to bones. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL]

## 75:4/q14 What role does conscience have in the argument for Judgment?  [* · links]
- 75:4/q14/p1 The reproaching soul is an inward witness to the greater Judgment and rebukes evil despite excuses. — Islahi contrasts the greater Judgment with this lesser one, connects it to 75:14–15, and says deniers' doubts about gathered bones contradict their own conscience. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 75:4/q15 What rhetorical effect does the omitted verb have here?  [قَٰدِرِينَ · rhetoric]
- 75:4/q15/p1 Ellipsis gives the reply a sense of haste and joins this verse to other rapid omissions in the sūra. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī lists the oath's response, the governing verb of qādirīn, the tongue pronoun's antecedent, and the unstated subject of balaghat (75:1, 4, 16, 26). He contrasts the concise affirmation of shaping fingers with the complete emphatic question about reviving the dead. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:4/q16 What related report is given about human joints?  [* · reports]
- 75:4/q16/p1 Every human joint owes daily charity, through speech, action, or wealth. — Bursawī cites a prophetic hadith saying this obligation returns each day the sun rises. [1 notes: BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام)]

## 75:4/q17 What is salāmā in the explanation of the finger joints?  [بَنَانَهُۥ · meaning]
- 75:4/q17/p1 It is a plural for the small bones of the hand and foot. — Bursawī says its morphological pattern is like ḥabara. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 75:4/q18 What does the tiny original particle of the body illustrate?  [* · theology]
- 75:4/q18/p1 God can gather all human characteristics within a single cell or tiny remnant. — Elmalılı likens this to the report that a tiny tailbone particle contains a person's features. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
