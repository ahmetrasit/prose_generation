# 43:43: 16 questions

## 43:43/q01 How does the command follow the preceding warnings about the rejecters?  [فَٱسْتَمْسِكْ · links]
turns on: Which feature of the preceding passage supplies the immediate rationale: the uncertain timing of punishment, the Prophet's distress at rejection, or the whole argument for revelation.
- 43:43/q01/p1 The Prophet should hold fast regardless of when or how God deals with the rejecters. — Abū Ḥayyān links it to the alternatives of the Prophet's death or witnessing punishment. Al-Biqāʿī says these leave fear and hope, with unseen outcomes and harm and benefit in God's hands. Bursawī says enemies may be punished during or after the Prophet's life, through him at Badr or without him under Abū Bakr; the uncertainty reserves knowledge of the unseen to God. He cites Jāmī's Persian warning against demanding foreknowledge and says to keep the Qurʾān's rulings whether the outcome comes now or in the afterlife. Al-Ālūsī takes the fāʾ as answering an implied condition: since one of the two outcomes must occur, hold fast. Iṣlāḥī says God may judge opponents but grants respite by His established practice. Kur'an Yolu says God will repay all and cannot be resisted. [9 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(جامي); ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 43:43/q01/p2 The command steadies the Prophet amid rejection and assures him that his mission remains sound. — Al-Qurṭubī mentions deniers, yet says the Qurʾān leads him to God, divine approval, and reward. Ibn ʿĀshūr connects it to easing distress at unbelief and promising victory; the Prophet must not lose resolve. Iṣlāḥī traces rebellion to affluence and Satan's blinding influence, says the opponents have no argument and will be defeated, and says the Prophet must persist in preaching the religion all prophets confirm. Kur'an Yolu says conditioning can block hearing, sight, and thought, making persuasion hard; preachers should be patient rather than despair at rejection or delayed punishment. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 43:43/q01/p3 The command follows the passage's accumulated case for revelation and the certainty of judgment. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says revelation and prophethood are God's established way, the Book is true, the Prophet a clear messenger, the Godfearing respond, and rejection meets punishment. The preceding material therefore warrants holding fast to the straight path. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 43:43/q02 What does holding fast to the revelation require here?  [فَٱسْتَمْسِكْ · interpretation]
- 43:43/q02/p1 Continue to cling firmly to revelation and put it into practice, including its commands and prohibitions. — Muqātil, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, Ibn Kathīr, and Study Quran speak of firm adherence to the Qurʾān. Al-Ṭabarī adds its commands; al-Dāmghānī and Ibn al-Jawzī classify this use as action upon revelation. Al-Ṭabrisī specifies proper recitation, obedience, and avoiding prohibitions. Al-Bayḍāwī specifies revealed verses and laws. Al-Qushayrī adds striving without falling short, trust without slackening, stopping at commands, and confidence in the path. Al-Biqāʿī extends diligence from the start of prophethood to dealings with opponents and everything else. Bursawī specifies observing rulings despite delayed outcomes. Al-Ālūsī says the direction may address the Prophet or his community. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the augmented letters in istimsāk intensify holding tightly. Iṣlāḥī applies persistence to preaching monotheism and the Book; Study Quran compares the straight path of 1:6. [19 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 43:43/q03 What range of meanings and related forms of the root m-s-k is cited?  [فَٱسْتَمْسِكْ · meaning]
- 43:43/q03/p1 The root denotes holding or restraint and takes several contextual senses and related noun forms. — Ibn al-Jawzī defines imsāk as restraint, the opposite of release, and also stinginess. He distinguishes mask (leather), misk (perfume), and masaka (bracelets of dhabal, an ivory-like material). Reported exegetes use it for marital retention or taking a wife back (2:229; 65:2), confinement in houses (4:15), stinginess from fear of spending (17:100), preserving the heavens or birds (22:65; 35:41; 67:19), withholding mercy (35:2; 39:38), and grasping a firm handhold (2:256). These are contextual usages rather than rival meanings assigned to this verse. [8 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين)]

## 43:43/q04 Why command the Prophet to hold fast when he already does so?  [فَٱسْتَمْسِكْ · rhetoric]
- 43:43/q04/p1 It commands continued steadfastness and teaches the community to make the same effort. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says an imperative can require persistence in an existing state; he compares Arabic prayers to remain honored and the prayer for guidance in 1:6. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ, reported by al-Sulamī, says the Prophet was already the exemplar of religion; his exalted status explains the address, which teaches hearers effort, struggle, following, and steadfastness. Al-Ālūsī allows the direction to be to the Prophet or his community. [3 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:43/q05 What is the revelation to which he must hold fast?  [بِٱلَّذِىٓ أُوحِىَ إِلَيْكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the relative phrase denotes the Qurʾān specifically or the full scope of what God revealed, including non-recited revelation.
- 43:43/q05/p1 It is the Qurʾān revealed to the Prophet. — Muqātil, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, Ibn Kathīr, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Study Quran identify the Book. Ibn Kathīr says it was revealed to his heart; Study Quran says it keeps one on the path and cites 1:6. Al-Ṭabarī identifies what the Qurʾān commands as what God revealed; Bursawī calls for observing its rulings. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 43:43/q05/p2 It embraces everything God revealed to him, including revelation beyond the recited Qurʾān. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya explicitly includes both recited revelation and otherwise. Al-Biqāʿī says all revelation from the beginning of prophethood and in every sphere. Al-Bayḍāwī speaks of revealed verses and laws without specifying whether those laws are non-recited. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 43:43/q06 How is أُوحِيَ read, and who is its subject?  [أُوحِىَ · readings]
turns on: Passive versus active voice, and the pronunciation of the passive ending in the reported Syrian variant.
- 43:43/q06/p1 The common reading is passive, ūḥiya. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes it to most readers; Abū Ḥayyān calls it the majority reading and also notes a Syrian variant. Al-Samīn specifies fatḥa on the yāʾ in the common reading. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]
- 43:43/q06/p2 Al-Ḍaḥḥāk reads the verb actively, awḥā, with God as subject. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī transmit this reading; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Samīn explicitly name God as the revealer. Al-Bayḍāwī reports the same active reading without naming its reader. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 43:43/q06/p3 Some Syrian readers make a consonant quiescent in the passive form. — Abū Ḥayyān reports a Syrian variant. Al-Samīn specifies sukūn on the yāʾ for ease; al-Ālūsī describes a quiescent lām. The notes vary in which consonant they name. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(العامة); ALUSI-FULL(بعض قراء الشام)]

## 43:43/q07 What does the preposition عَلَىٰ convey here?  [عَلَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 43:43/q07/p1 It portrays the Prophet as firmly established upon and master of guidance. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this figurative elevation and compares the description of the guided in 2:5. Saying the Prophet is on the path rather than merely that the path is straight depicts confidence, without hesitation or fear of straying; he compares 27:79. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:43/q08 What readings of صِرَٰطٍ are noted?  [صِرَٰطٍ · readings]
turns on: Articulation of the initial consonant; the noted alternatives preserve the meaning.
- 43:43/q08/p1 A reading uses sīn instead of emphatic ṣād. — Qiraat-ER says this softens the onset while preserving the path sense. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
- 43:43/q08/p2 A reading uses ishmām, blending ṣād with a zāy-like sound. — Qiraat-ER says this changes articulation without changing the path sense. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 43:43/q09 What is the straight path on which the Prophet stands?  [صِرَٰطٍۢ مُّسْتَقِيمٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase is identified by the religion's name or by the conduct and destination of following revelation.
- 43:43/q09/p1 It is Islam, the true religion of monotheism. — Al-Ṭabarī identifies God's religion as Islam and reports Qatāda's identification; al-Ṭabrisī and the report in al-Durr likewise name Islam. Muqātil calls it a straight religion, al-Nasafī an unbending religion, and Bursawī the path of monotheism and Islam. Iṣlāḥī calls it the true religion confirmed by all prophets. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 43:43/q09/p2 It is the way of acting on revelation and following its guidance. — Ibn ʿĀshūr defines the path as acting on revelation and compares 27:79. Study Quran says adherence to the Qurʾān keeps one on the straight path and cites 1:6. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it the road. Ibn Kathīr says the revealed truth guides toward God's straight path, Paradise, and lasting good. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 43:43/q10 What does the path's straightness imply?  [صِرَٰطٍۢ مُّسْتَقِيمٍۢ · meaning]
- 43:43/q10/p1 It is a clear, sound, level route with no crookedness. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya glosses ṣirāṭ as a road; al-Ṭabarī calls the way straight and sound. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī deny any bend, Bursawī calls it level and without crookedness, and al-Biqāʿī calls revelation a broad clear straight route to the goal. Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī repeat the characterization without a further gloss. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 43:43/q11 What does the clause إِنَّكَ عَلَىٰ صِرَٰطٍۢ مُّسْتَقِيمٍۢ explain?  [إِنَّكَ عَلَىٰ صِرَٰطٍۢ مُّسْتَقِيمٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the explanatory clause is attached to the imperative speech act or to the steadfast adherence it enjoins.
- 43:43/q11/p1 It gives the reason for the command to hold fast. — Al-Ālūsī gives this as one possible attachment of the explanatory clause. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it confirms and explains the command and calls being on the path the reason for the command. Iṣlāḥī likewise makes the true religion he preaches the reason to persist. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 43:43/q11/p2 It gives the reason for holding fast itself. — Al-Ālūsī offers this as the other possible attachment, alongside explaining the command. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 43:43/q12 What assurance and praise does being on the straight path convey?  [إِنَّكَ عَلَىٰ صِرَٰطٍۢ مُّسْتَقِيمٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: These are different effects of the same assurance; the notes do not set them against each other.
- 43:43/q12/p1 It assures the Prophet of his guidance and steadies believers and the mission. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Prophet has not strayed from his commission and believers are steadied; his wording portrays guidance without hesitation or fear of straying. Al-Biqāʿī says steadfast adherence leaves opponents unable to harm him. Ibn Kathīr says revealed truth leads to God's path, Paradise, and lasting good. Iṣlāḥī says opponents lack an argument against his rightly guided call. Al-Qurṭubī says the Qurʾān leads to God, approval, and reward despite deniers. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 43:43/q12/p2 The clause is a sixth praise of the Qurʾān. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly counts this clause as the sixth praise of the Qurʾān. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 43:43/q12/p3 The clause also praises and supports the Messenger. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it the Messenger's share of praise and support as well as the rationale for holding fast. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:43/q13 What inward meaning is given to holding to the revealed Qurʾān?  [فَٱسْتَمْسِكْ بِٱلَّذِىٓ أُوحِىَ إِلَيْكَ · inward]
- 43:43/q13/p1 The Qurʾān is God's firm rope; embody its character and follow it toward the divine presence. — Bursawī reports al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya: follow where the Qurʾān leads, stop where it commands, and trust it to bring one to the divine presence. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 43:43/q14 How does this revelation contrast with the idolaters' claim?  [* · links]
- 43:43/q14/p1 The Prophet has revelation to hold to, whereas the idolaters had no earlier book to follow. — Study Quran contrasts this verse with the idolaters' lack of a prior book in 43:21. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 43:43/q15 How does this passage fit the sūrah's theme?  [* · links]
- 43:43/q15/p1 It belongs to a unified argument running through all of Sūrah al-Zukhruf. — Iṣlāḥī says every part relates deeply to the central theme and the whole sūrah is harmonious from beginning to end. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 43:43/q16 What is notable about the verse's compact expression?  [* · rhetoric]
- 43:43/q16/p1 It expresses ten meanings in a line's space, with emphasis, allusion, and verbal ornament. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares its ten meanings with six in a celebrated concise line by Imruʾ al-Qays. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
