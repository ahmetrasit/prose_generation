# 20:114: 23 questions

## 20:114/q01 What do God's exaltation, kingship and truth mean?  [فَتَعَٰلَى ٱللَّهُ ٱلْمَلِكُ ٱلْحَقُّ · meaning]
- 20:114/q01/p1 God is exalted above creation and false descriptions; His sovereignty and truth are absolute. — Yahya and Jishumi derive taʿālā from height, the latter naming the tafaʿala pattern. Muqatil contrasts false gods. Tabari, Wahidi, Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi and Biqai reject polytheist descriptions. Nasafi excludes bodily likeness; Razi says majesty exceeds imagination and intellect. Tabrisi and Baydawi contrast divine and created power, knowledge and speech. Bursevi argues from necessary cause to contingent effect. Qashani says His rule gives each its due. Asad renders Ultimate Sovereign and Ultimate Truth. [22 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 20:114/q02 What does al-Malik convey?  [ٱلْمَلِكُ · meaning]
- 20:114/q02/p1 God alone holds complete, effective and enduring sovereignty; other kings depend on Him and lose their rule. — Ibn Abbas, cited by Wahidi, places reward and punishment in His hand. Qushayri says malik intensifies mālik and true kingship is unique creative power. Tabrisi says God owns both worlds. Razi says His rule never changes or derives from another. Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi cite effective commands, promises and threats. Ibn Ashur invokes 25:26 and a hadith challenging earthly kings, comparing Muawiya's address to Medina's scholars; Allah paired with King denotes all perfections. Asad renders Ultimate Sovereign. [20 notes: MUQATIL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الله,معاوية); ASAD-NOTES]

## 20:114/q03 What does al-Haqq mean here?  [ٱلْحَقُّ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the title describes God's intrinsic reality or His rightful dominion; several sources allow both.
- 20:114/q03/p1 God is intrinsically existent and true in essence and attributes, unlike false gods and changing creation. — Yahya calls al-Haqq a divine name. Qushayri compares the evil eye being true, meaning existent. Nasafi says God truly deserves divinity. Ibn Kathir extends truth to God's promise, threat, messengers, Paradise and Hell. Asad stresses eternal, immutable reality. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; QUSHAYRI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:114/q03/p2 God possesses or establishes the right and truly deserves kingship. — Qushayri offers both possession and establishment and accepts all senses. Wahidi and Qurtubi say possessor of truth or right. Al-Raghib, cited by Alusi, says God creates according to wisdom. Ibn Ashur says His sovereignty admits no incapacity or subjection. [9 notes: QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:114/q04 Why is God glorified at this point?  [فَتَعَٰلَى ٱللَّهُ ٱلْمَلِكُ ٱلْحَقُّ · links]
turns on: Whether the praise closes the immediate judgment and Quran discussion or the larger Moses account.
- 20:114/q04/p1 The opening concludes or responds to the preceding account of revelation, divine power, judgment and guidance. — Ibn Atiyya sees a close to the judgment passage. Razi says the Quran is magnified before God. Biqai sees the book and preceding eloquence, knowledge, power and justice as grounds for praise. Ibn Ashur calls it an interjection thanking God for reforming guidance. Alusi says it evokes wonder. Tabatabai prefers the resurrection and judgment context. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 20:114/q04/p2 It may close the entire Moses account. — Tabatabai presents this as an alternative to his preferred immediate connection. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:114/q05 How does the prohibition connect to the preceding praise?  [وَلَا تَعْجَلْ بِٱلْقُرْءَانِ · links]
turns on: How widely to draw the preceding discourse.
- 20:114/q05/p1 The praise of God and the Quran leads to instruction in attentive, reverent reception of revelation. — Baydawi and Nasafi call it an aside after mention of revelation. Biqai contrasts not turning away with rushing ahead. Alusi cites al-Tibi: the Sender's and Book's grandeur call for understanding without haste. Ibn Ashur links it to the Quran's reforming message. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:114/q05/p2 The earlier discourse about the mountains ends here and a new address begins. — Razi reports Abu Muslim locating its start at 20:105; Alusi reports an excursus interpretation. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 20:114/q05/p3 It connects to an earlier command to make the earth dust. — Jishumi reports Abu Muslim's connection. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم)]

## 20:114/q06 What haste with the Quran is prohibited?  [وَلَا تَعْجَلْ بِٱلْقُرْءَانِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the haste concerns recitation during delivery, transmission or interpretation before clarification, desire for quicker descent, or later reading of the whole Quran.
- 20:114/q06/p1 The Prophet must not recite along with or ahead of Gabriel; he should listen until the current passage ends, then recite. — Ibn Abbas, al-Suddi and others say he repeated the opening or each word for fear of forgetting. Razi and Ibn Kathir say God would preserve and explain it; Biqai says attention improves retention, and Bursevi and Alusi say speaking could hide the next words. Baydawi calls this primary. Tabatabai accepts the recitation explanation but rejects fear of forgetting on infallibility grounds. Islahi accepts natural eagerness but rejects it as the warning's reason. [57 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,السدي,المفضل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI(ابن عباس، والحسن، وجماعة); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(ابن عباس، الحسن، الجبائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(مقاتل والسدي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي); SUYUTI-LUBAB(السدي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(السدي,القمي); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Our exegetes); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); STUDYQURAN; BAYDAWI-FULL+; SUYUTI-LUBAB+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 20:114/q06/p2 He should not recite, dictate, convey or interpret for others until the passage's meaning is clarified. — Mujahid, Qatada, Ibn Abbas and Atiyya report the clarification gloss. Tabari locates it in dictation to a scribe. Ibn Atiyya reports waiting to write. Razi and Abu Muslim mention a later exception, condition or other specification; Jishumi cites Abu Ali on general and ambiguous wording. Alusi says early conveyance is lawful and rejects this reading; Tabatabai finds no support in the verse. [36 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة,مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(مجاهد وقتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(عطية); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مجاهد، قتادة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(مجاهد، قتادة، عطية، أبو مسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد وقتادة); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل,مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو مسلم,قيل); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-; TABATABAI-]
- 20:114/q06/p3 He should not ask or press for further revelation before God's appointed time. — Dahhak and others report Meccans and the bishop of Najran demanded answers in three days and rumors spread when revelation delayed. Ibn Ashur says zeal to reform his community led to yearning for quicker revelation but mere wishing is allowed. Islahi compares the Prophet's waiting for Quranic aid to a soldier awaiting supplies, citing 19:64-65. Tabatabai rejects the wording fit; Alusi finds the Najran account unconvincing. [13 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(بعض أهل التفسير); TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); RAZI-FULL(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الماوردي); IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL(الماوردي)-; TABATABAI-]
- 20:114/q06/p4 Readers should avoid conclusions from isolated verses until the coordinated Quranic message is considered. — Asad extends the first address to every reader and invokes 25:32 and 75:16-19; Kuranyolu reports this. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(M. Esed)]

## 20:114/q07 What occasion is reported for the prohibition?  [وَلَا تَعْجَلْ بِٱلْقُرْءَانِ · reports]
turns on: Which reported event explains the admonition and whether the account fits its wording and prophetic protection.
- 20:114/q07/p1 The Prophet repeated Gabriel's words from concern he would forget the revelation. — Versions describe repeating the opening or every word, and fearing Gabriel's departure or loss from memory. Ibn Kathir calls Ibn Abbas's account authentic. Suyuti prefers al-Suddi's account over a cause cited in Surat al-Nisa. Tabatabai rejects fear of forgetting as incompatible with infallibility; Islahi rejects it as the reason for the warning. [22 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,السدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن عباس، والحسن، وجماعة); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(مقاتل والسدي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(السدي); SUYUTI-LUBAB(السدي); ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Our exegetes); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); SUYUTI-LUBAB+; TABATABAI(السدي)-; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 20:114/q07/p2 After a man struck his wife, she sought retaliation; the Prophet first ordered it, then waited until 4:34. — Some specify a slap to the face. Wahidi says the story does not fit except perhaps the prayer for knowledge; Razi calls it remote. Alusi and Tabatabai reject the connection, the latter saying neither verse fits. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن البصري); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT-; RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)-; TABATABAI(الحسن)-]
- 20:114/q07/p3 Delayed answers to Meccans and the bishop of Najran stirred rumors and a wish for faster revelation. — Dahhak describes a three-day deadline and a rumor of defeat by Jews. Abu Hayyan attributes the report to Mawardi. Alusi reports it but finds it unconvincing. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(الضحاك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الماوردي); ALUSI-FULL(الماوردي)-]

## 20:114/q08 What must be completed before the Prophet proceeds?  [مِن قَبْلِ أَن يُقْضَىٰٓ إِلَيْكَ وَحْيُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether completion is verbal delivery or subsequent clarification of meaning and rulings.
- 20:114/q08/p1 The recitation or delivery of the current portion by Gabriel must finish. — Wahidi reports al-Mufaddal's delivery gloss. Ibn Ashur specifies the portion then descending. Biqai says revelation arrives in measured stages, and Bursevi compares completion of a term in 10:11. [14 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفضل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:114/q08/p2 God's clarification of the text and its legal or interpretive meaning must arrive. — Qatada glosses completion as explanation. Al-Hasan includes obligations, limits, rulings, lawful and forbidden matters; Yahya says following the Quran means acting on these, citing 75:18. Razi allows textual completion or explanatory revelation because later exceptions and conditions may alter the opening. Jishumi says the ending may change earlier meaning. [16 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن,قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(مجاهد وقتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد، قتادة); JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة)]

## 20:114/q09 What does qaḍā mean here?  [يُقْضَىٰٓ · meaning]
- 20:114/q09/p1 It means to complete, finish or fully deliver revelation. — Damghani says qaḍā can complete a period or the revelation sent to someone. Bursevi compares 10:11; Ibn Ashur specifies the portion then descending. [4 notes: DAMGHANI; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:114/q10 How is the completion verb read and parsed?  [يُقْضَىٰٓ وَحْيُهُۥ · readings]
turns on: The verb's person and voice determine whether revelation is passive subject or object of God's direct act; al-Aʿmash further differs in final vowel.
- 20:114/q10/p1 The common reading has passive yuqḍā and nominative waḥyuhu as passive subject. — Ibn Atiyya calls this the majority reading. Jishumi, Abu Hayyan, Samin and Nashr explain its syntax. Durr reports al-Hasan reading this form. Biqai says the passive focuses on completion rather than naming the revealer. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); NASHR(الباقون); DURR-FULL(الحسن); BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:114/q10/p2 Another reading has active first-person plural naqḍiya, with God as agent and waḥyahu accusative. — Yaqub, Ibn Masud, al-Hasan, al-Jahdari, Abu Haywa and others are named, in varying lists. Thalabi specifies Yaqub's vowel pattern; Baghawi and Ibn al-Jawzi give kasra on dad and fatha on final ya. Qiraat-Er notes direct divine agency. Zajjaj says taqḍī is not read. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; JISHUMI(يعقوب); BAGHAWI-FULL(يعقوب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود والحسن ويعقوب); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود وغيره); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(الجحدري وأبو حيوة والحسن وعبد الله); NASHR(يعقوب); ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله والجحدري والحسن وأبو حيوة وسلام ويعقوب والزعفراني وابن مقسم); QIRAAT-ER(Imp|CE)]
- 20:114/q10/p3 Al-Aʿmash reads active first-person plural with final yāʾ vowelless. — Abu Hayyan and al-Lawami, quoted by Alusi, cite a dialect leaving final ya closed after kasra. Samin says this avoids a heavy movement on a weak letter and mentions 5:89. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأعمش); SAMIN-DURR(الأعمش); ALUSI-FULL(الأعمش؛ صاحب اللوامح)]

## 20:114/q11 What variant pronunciation of al-Qurʾān is noted?  [بِٱلْقُرْءَانِ · readings]
- 20:114/q11/p1 Qurān without hamza simplifies qurʾān phonologically without changing meaning. — Qiraat-Er reports the omission. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(hmz)]

## 20:114/q12 What lessons about judgment and action follow from waiting?  [وَلَا تَعْجَلْ بِٱلْقُرْءَانِ · law]
turns on: Whether the verse imposes a rule on transmission and judgment or chiefly counsels receptive deliberation.
- 20:114/q12/p1 Withhold conveyance, belief or judgment until the text and any explanation or qualification are complete. — Jishumi cites Abu Ali on specification of general wording and explanation of ambiguity; an ending can change prior sense. Razi and Abu Muslim list private reading, delivery, apparent interpretation and public explanation, noting later exceptions and conditions. Qushayri advises testing outward evidence against foundations but applying general wording if no specific proof appears, against those who suspend judgment. Alusi rejects a ban on conveyance as unlawful and calls a writing ban weaker. [13 notes: QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو مسلم); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]
- 20:114/q12/p2 More broadly, deliberate, listen and reflect; understanding helps preserve learning. — Qushayri extends the lesson to all matters. Biqai says full understanding aids retention. Bursevi's inward reading calls for stillness and contemplation; Kuranyolu says safeguarding the trust is not condemned. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 20:114/q13 Was the Prophet's haste culpable?  [وَلَا تَعْجَلْ بِٱلْقُرْءَانِ · theology]
turns on: Whether fear of losing revelation can be attributed to the Prophet.
- 20:114/q13/p1 It arose from care for revelation and perhaps personal judgment, for which restraint was preferable. — Razi proposes ijtihad and contrasts the Prophet's zeal with Adam's neglect. The Adam story reassures him his motive was devotion. Kuranyolu says the following prayer honors rather than rebukes him. Islahi accepts eagerness as natural. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Our exegetes)]
- 20:114/q13/p2 Fear of forgetting revelation conflicts with prophetic infallibility. — Tabatabai rejects al-Suddi's account on this ground. [1 notes: TABATABAI(السدي)]

## 20:114/q14 What knowledge is requested?  [رَّبِّ زِدْنِى عِلْمًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether knowledge is Quranic, general, one specified domain, practical faith or inward realization.
- 20:114/q14/p1 Knowledge of the Quran and its meanings, growing as revelation and understanding increase. — Muqatil and al-Kalbi say more Quran; Thalabi, Baghawi and Nasafi stress understanding. Mawardi says each portion increases knowledge. Biqai includes understanding and further knowledge drawing followers; Alusi says every word contains secrets and sciences. Bursevi's inward reading includes Quranic realities and character. [18 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الثعلبي,مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل,مقاتل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:114/q14/p2 More knowledge generally, including what has not yet been taught and what benefits life and religion. — Wahidi says teach me what I do not know. Mawardi adds religious instruction cannot be withheld until requested. Baydawi says revelation will arrive, so ask God. Kuranyolu explicitly includes matters beyond revelation. Razi mentions asking God for knowledge, provision and protection. [14 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 20:114/q14/p3 Better memorization or retention. — Thalabi, Ibn al-Jawzi and Abu Hayyan report this alternative. [3 notes: THALABI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الثعلبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]
- 20:114/q14/p4 Knowledge of prophetic stories and the stations of God's friends. — Mawardi and Tabrisi give this domain. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل)]
- 20:114/q14/p5 Knowledge of the community's condition and future after the Prophet. — Mawardi reports this explanation. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 20:114/q14/p6 Patience in obedience and fighting enemies, eased by knowledge. — Mawardi states the causal link. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 20:114/q14/p7 Knowledge of God alone and detachment from all else. — Sulami reports this as true increase; Bursevi cites Abu Yazid on taking knowledge from the Living rather than the dead. [2 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); BURSEVI(أبو يزيد البسطامي)]
- 20:114/q14/p8 Knowledge of one's hidden evils, schemes and excuses, for treatment with God's help. — Sulami and Bursevi report Muhammad ibn al-Fadl; the lists vary slightly between excuses and treachery. [2 notes: SULAMI(محمد بن الفضل); BURSEVI(محمد بن الفضل)]
- 20:114/q14/p9 Increased faith and certainty in God. — Ibn Masud's practice includes faith, certainty, understanding and knowledge. Bursevi prefers this as the finest interpretation because single-hearted certainty is difficult. [5 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود); BURSEVI(ابن مسعود); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود); BURSEVI+]
- 20:114/q14/p10 Direct divine knowledge reached by spiritual readiness. — Qashani says higher knowledge follows purification, ascent and adornment rather than premature asking. Alusi reports this as an allusion. [3 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL(some interpreters)]

## 20:114/q15 Why ask for more knowledge, and who should do so?  [وَقُل رَّبِّ زِدْنِى عِلْمًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the prayer emphasizes the Prophet's dependence, knowledge's special merit, direct instruction, or a practice shared with the community.
- 20:114/q15/p1 It teaches humility, dependence and gratitude, and the continuing merit of knowledge. — Qushayri cites 4:113: even the most learned asks more, and neediness is the servant's noblest quality. Kashshaf and Abu Hayyan cite gratitude for refined instruction. Jishumi and Alusi infer knowledge's merit. Ibn Uyayna says the Prophet learned until death; Ibn Kathir reports revelation continued until that day. Kuranyolu says the prayer honors him. [12 notes: QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عيينة); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:114/q15/p2 God singled out knowledge as what His Messenger was commanded to seek in increase. — Kashshaf, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi and Alusi report the maxim. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:114/q15/p3 Muhammad seeks added knowledge from God Himself, unlike Moses directed to al-Khidr. — Qushayri contrasts 18:66, 18:72 and 18:78; Bursevi reports Kashifi citing Qushayri and mentions 4:113. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 20:114/q15/p4 The prayer also belongs to the community, who should seek knowledge from God. — Study Quran quotes Ibn Arabi: exclusivity to the Prophet would be explicit; it notes use by Muslims, especially students and Sufis, and argues a fortiori. [4 notes: STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿArabī)]

## 20:114/q16 What related prayers or sayings are reported?  [وَقُل رَّبِّ زِدْنِى عِلْمًۭا · reports]
turns on: The reports give different prayers; Tabatabai specifically contests the no-blessing saying.
- 20:114/q16/p1 The Prophet prayed for beneficial knowledge, increase in knowledge and praise in every condition. — Abu Hurayra's report is attributed to Ibn Majah, al-Tirmidhi and al-Bazzar; al-Tirmidhi calls one route unusual. Biqai and Ibn Kathir include al-Bazzar's plea for refuge from the state of Hell's people. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة); BIQAI-FULL(أبو هريرة); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة؛ رسول الله)]
- 20:114/q16/p2 He reportedly wished no blessing on a day without knowledge drawing him nearer to God. — Tabrisi and Study Quran report it. Tabatabai questions it because the Prophet would not curse himself over what he cannot control and transmission by meaning may have distorted it. [2 notes: TABRISI(النبي); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet); TABATABAI-]

## 20:114/q18 How does Adam's following story connect to the warning?  [* · links]
- 20:114/q18/p1 Adam's forgetting parallels the warning and illustrates divine forbearance, repentance and the danger of human lapse. — Jishumi says even the Prophet need not marvel at forgetting since Adam forgot. ## 20:114/q19 What broader theological claims are drawn from this passage?  [* · theology]
turns on: These are separate implications rather than directly competing explanations.
- 20:114/q19/p1 The Quran is sufficient proof of unity and prophecy, and scholars should explain its inimitability. — Baqillani likens denying its evident force to doubting observation and responds to claims of magic, poetry and ancient tales. [2 notes: BAQILLANI]
- 20:114/q19/p2 The Prophet's ability to hasten recitation supports a complete single descent alongside gradual revelation. — Tabatabai reasons that he could not otherwise recite what was not yet revealed. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 20:114/q19/p3 God's just rule warns before punishment and guides by revelation for human welfare. — Ibn Kathir says warning removes excuse. Ibn Ashur calls the Quran the law of God's kingdom, securing welfare in both worlds. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:114/q19/p4 Claims of seeing God are rejected by Quranic statements of His invisibility and unlikeness. — Tabatabai reports al-Rida answering Abu Qurra with 6:103, 20:110, 42:11 and Muslim consensus. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو الحسن عليه السلام)]

## 20:114/q20 Whom does the prohibition address?  [وَلَا تَعْجَلْ بِٱلْقُرْءَانِ · referent]
turns on: Whether Muhammad's initial address is extended to later readers or inwardly to Moses.
- 20:114/q20/p1 It first addresses Muhammad receiving revelation. — Asad calls this very probable and attributes it to most classical commentators; Kuranyolu locates the ban in reception. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 20:114/q20/p2 It also addresses Quran readers of every age. — Asad extends it because the Quran is a coordinated whole; Kuranyolu reports this view. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(M. Esed)]
- 20:114/q20/p3 In an inward reading it addresses Moses longing for direct knowledge at union. — Qashani says knowledge comes in stages of spiritual receptivity. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:114/q21 What inward practice is taught by listening and praying?  [وَلَا تَعْجَلْ بِٱلْقُرْءَانِ رَّبِّ زِدْنِى عِلْمًۭا · inward]
