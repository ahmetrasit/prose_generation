# 4:49: 21 questions

## 4:49/q01 What does أَلَمْ تَرَ convey here?  [أَلَمْ تَرَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the opening is explained as epistemic perception or as a summons to wonder at the claim.
- 4:49/q01/p1 It calls on the Prophet to know or be informed of their case, rather than literally see it. — Abu Ubayda (MAJAZ) denies literal eyesight; al-Zajjaj's language scholars say the question conveys information and commands knowledge of their story; Ibn Qutayba and al-Zajjaj give the informed and known glosses; al-Tabari makes this perception of the heart; Tabrisi combines both glosses. Jishumi and Tabrisi distinguish looking or turning attention from achieved perception, including bodily, mental, merciful, hostile, expectant and disputational uses; Tabrisi says God may be called seeing but not looking. [12 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ(أهل اللغة,بعضهم); JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR]
- 4:49/q01/p2 It directs attention to their conduct and expresses astonishment at their claim. — Muqatil says to look at their action; al-Nahhas and Bursevi stress the Prophet's amazement, the latter despite their disbelief and grave sins; al-Alusi gives either their purity claim or supposed daily erasure of sins as the object of wonder. [4 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:49/q02 Is there a variant reading of أَلَمْ تَرَ?  [أَلَمْ تَرَ · readings]
- 4:49/q02/p1 Al-Sulami reads the rāʾ still after loss of the jussive letter. — Al-Thaʿlabi says this reading of alam tarh occurs throughout the Quran, illustrates the dialect's added sukūn with an anonymous poem about divine guidance and misguidance; Jishumi calls the open form more elevated. [3 notes: THALABI(السلمي); JISHUMI(السلمي)]

## 4:49/q03 Who are ٱلَّذِينَ يُزَكُّونَ أَنفُسَهُم?  [ٱلَّذِينَ يُزَكُّونَ أَنفُسَهُم · referent]
turns on: Whether contextual reports restrict the referent to Jews, include Christians or the People of the Book, or allow the general wording to reach later self-praisers.
- 4:49/q03/p1 Jews, particularly those making claims of purity and special status. — Muqatil names Bahri ibn Amr and Marhab ibn Zayd; the majority according to Abu Hayyan, and all interpreters according to Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi, identify Jews. Tabatabai prefers Jews or some of them in context and cites 5:18, 2:80, 62:6. Ibn Ashur points to claims in 5:18 and 2:111. Study Quran specifies some Madinan Jews as a reported narrower identification. [20 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(السدي,الضحاك,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(المتأولون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس، مجاهد، قتادة، مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور والحسن وابن زيد); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(a group of Madinan Jews); TAB-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 4:49/q03/p2 Jews and Christians, or the People of the Book generally. — Al-Hasan cites the claims of divine sonship, love and exclusive Paradise (5:18, 2:111); Ibn Zayd includes both groups' claims of obedience. Ibn Jurayj applies the children's prayer account to both. Bagha wi also attributes this to al-Dahhak, Qatada and Muqatil; Tabrisi adds a report from al-Baqir. Study Quran prefers the wider People of the Book reference over Madinan Jews. Biqai includes claims of guidance, brief fire, unearned praise and others following their desires (2:80, 2:111, 3:188, 4:27). [22 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,الحسن,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن والضحاك وقتادة ومقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن، ابن زيد,الحسن، قتادة); TABRISI(الضحاك والحسن وقتادة والسدي وأبو جعفر); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة والحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة والحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن زيد,الحسن وقتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); STUDYQURAN(most commentators); STUDYQURAN+]
- 4:49/q03/p3 The rebuke extends to anyone who claims purity or exceptional piety. — Kashshaf, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi, Alusi and Study Quran apply the general wording beyond the initial community; Asad includes Jews and Christians under deeper shirk and also Muslims who venerate saints as divine. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES]

## 4:49/q04 What did their self-purification consist of?  [يُزَكُّونَ أَنفُسَهُم · interpretation]
turns on: The rival reports identify different purity claims and acts; Tabari questions proof for the prayer account, Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi question its fit, and Qurtubi favors mutual praise.
- 4:49/q04/p1 They claimed divine sonship and love, exclusive Paradise, election, or privileged exemption from punishment. — Al-Hasan cites 5:18 and 2:111; Ibn Zayd adds claimed obedience. Razi adds brief punishment (2:80) and ancestral intercession; Biqai adds unearned praise and demands that believers follow their desires (3:188, 4:27); Tabatabai adds chosen friendship (62:6), and Kuranyolu says such praise confers no status. Asad adds Christian reliance on Jesus' vicarious atonement. [30 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,الحسن,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة، الحسن); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن والضحاك وقتادة ومقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة والحسن); TABRISI(الضحاك والحسن وقتادة والسدي وأبو جعفر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن، قتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة والحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة والحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن زيد,الحسن وقتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(most commentators); TAB-FULL+]
- 4:49/q04/p2 They declared themselves sinless, including the claim that daytime and nighttime sins were alternately forgiven. — Al-Dahhak likens them to newborns, while Ibn Abi Hatim reports a variant likening them to sinless ancestors; al-Suddi says boys learned Torah young and that adults' daytime sins were erased at night. Study Quran observes that the Quran does not explicitly attest these reported claims. [23 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA(اليهود); TAB-FULL(السدي,الضحاك); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); WAHIDI-ASBAB(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك والسدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس,ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك والسدي); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك والسدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(السدي); ALUSI-FULL(الكلبي); STUDYQURAN(a group of Madinan Jews)]
- 4:49/q04/p3 They put children forward as prayer leaders, relying on the children's alleged innocence to cleanse the adults. — Mujahid speaks of children leading prayer; Ibn Jurayj adds supplication and extends the group to Christians; Ibn Abbas adds offerings or sacrifices; Abu Hayyan says the adult worshipers sought forgiveness behind them. Tabari finds no authoritative report establishing this account; Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi call it remote from the verse's aim. Ibn Kathir and Ibn Abbas reject the premise that another innocent person purifies a sinner. [21 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(آخرون,أبو مالك,عكرمة,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(مجاهد وعكرمة); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد، عكرمة); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد وعكرمة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وأبو مالك وعكرمة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عكرمة، مجاهد، أبو مالك); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد وأبو مالك وعكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عكرمة ومجاهد وأبو مالك); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد، عكرمة، أبو مالك، السدي، الضحاك); DURR-FULL(أبو مالك,ابن عباس,عكرمة,مجاهد); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس، عكرمة، مجاهد، أبو مالك); TAB-FULL(الذين قالوا)-; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 4:49/q04/p4 They claimed dead children or forebears would intercede with God and purify them. — Ibn Abbas' reports variously say deceased children or ancestors, and call the children a means of nearness to God; Razi mentions prophetic forebears. Study Quran records mutual absolution through intercession as an additional variant. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(عطية عن ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطية عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(a group of Madinan Jews)]
- 4:49/q04/p5 They praised, vouched for, or declared one another pure, sometimes for worldly advantage. — Ibn Masud's account concerns mutual praise; Mawardi specifies Jews seeking worldly gain; the man in Abdullah's report flatters someone unable to help or harm him, loses religion or incurs God's anger, and gains nothing. Qurtubi prefers this as the apparent meaning. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون,عبد الله); THALABI(ابن مسعود,عبد الله); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود); BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); TABRISI(ابن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود,قيل); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود)+]
- 4:49/q04/p6 They verbally certified their own deeds, piety, obedience or nearness to God. — Tabrisi gives the direct declaration 'we are pure'; Razi compares testimony to a witness's uprightness. Ibn Atiyya says words cannot confer the purity established by good deeds and God; generalizing authors include anyone making such claims. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 4:49/q05 What happened when Jews brought children to the Prophet?  [ٱلَّذِينَ يُزَكُّونَ أَنفُسَهُم · reports]
turns on: Different narrated occasions and their named actors; Tabari disputes sufficient transmission for the prayer account.
- 4:49/q05/p1 Jewish men asked whether their children had sins and, after the Prophet said no, claimed their own sins were erased day and night. — Names vary: Muqatil has Bahri ibn Amr and Marhab ibn Zayd; Baghawi adds al-Numan ibn Awfa; Ibn Jawzi has Bahri ibn Awn; Abu Hayyan has Bahri ibn Amr and Marhab. Farra, Kalbi, Ibn Abbas and others report the alternation of day and night erasure; the reports say the verse answered their claim. Study Quran notes the Quran itself does not explicitly attest these details. [15 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA(اليهود); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-ASBAB(الكلبي); JISHUMI(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك والسدي); ALUSI-FULL(الكلبي); STUDYQURAN(a group of Madinan Jews)]
- 4:49/q05/p2 Some report that Jews made sinless children lead worship or give offerings, prompting revelation. — Mujahid reports prayer leadership; Ibn Abbas adds offerings; Ikrima specifies prepubescent boys. Abu Malik and others explicitly call it the occasion of revelation. [13 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(أبو مالك,عكرمة,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(مجاهد وعكرمة); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد وعكرمة); DURR-FULL(أبو مالك,ابن عباس,عكرمة,مجاهد); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس، عكرمة، مجاهد، أبو مالك); TAB-FULL(الذين قالوا)-; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد وأبو مالك وعكرمة)-]
- 4:49/q05/p3 Another report connects the passage with a Jewish delegation's alliance with Quraysh and Kaʿb's judgment in favor of idolatry. — Elmalılı names Huyay ibn Akhtab and Ka'b ibn Ashraf with seventy Jewish horsemen, their journey to Mecca to break a pact, Quraysh's demand that they prostrate to idols, their compliance, and Abu Sufyan's question after hearing Muhammad's monotheist call; Ka'b commended Quraysh's services and preferred them to the believers. [5 notes: ELMALILI]

## 4:49/q06 What does يُزَكُّونَ mean, and what is its root sense?  [يُزَكُّونَ · meaning]
- 4:49/q06/p1 It is purifying or declaring someone pure and free of faults or sins; its root also means sound growth or increase in goodness. — Al-Zajjaj, Ibn Jawzi and Tabrisi connect zakā to growth; Tabrisi uses sound crops; al-Raghib says growth by God's blessing. Jishumi says description itself can count as tazkiya; Baydawi and Alusi stress removing blameworthy conduct or speech, with 91:9 and 9:103; Damghani gives vindication or acquittal (2:174, 18:74). Kuranyolu distinguishes character improvement and moral education through acts from speech attesting honesty or freedom from grave faults; al-Raghib gives the same deed and speech distinction (87:14, 53:32). [14 notes: ZAJJAJ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الراغب)]

## 4:49/q07 Why does the verse say أَنفُسَهُم in the plural for mutual praise?  [أَنفُسَهُم · rhetoric]
- 4:49/q07/p1 Members of one religion count as one self, so their praising one another is called praising themselves. — Tabrisi explains the shared religious identity behind the plural. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 4:49/q08 What does the verse teach about praising oneself or another person?  [يُزَكُّونَ أَنفُسَهُم · law]
turns on: Whether praise is truthful and useful, whether it claims knowledge of the inward state, and whether any human virtue is treated as independent of God.
- 4:49/q08/p1 Verbal self-praise, self-certification and vanity are condemned; real standing belongs to God and good deeds. — Qurtubi joins 53:32 and notes the renaming of Barrah as Zaynab in Muslim; Egyptian titles implying purity can mask vice. Ibn Kathir reports Umar warning that declaring oneself a believer, learned or Paradise-bound reveals the opposite, and that self-admiration is feared. Joseph refuses to acquit his soul (12:53). Sulami and Sufis say the carnal soul is unfit as a site of purity and self-satisfaction extinguishes certainty; Study Quran adds ignorance and disobedience. Tabatabai traces admiration into arrogance, exploitation and injury to life, honor and property, especially when social custom (3:75). [21 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عمر,عمر بن الخطاب); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI(الراغب); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Aj,Qu,Su,Sufis)]
- 4:49/q08/p2 Praise of others is permissible when true, measured, and useful; false or excessive flattery is condemned. — Qurtubi cites Bukhari's warning that praise can cut a man's neck and the instruction to say 'I think him so,' leaving judgment to God; Muslim and Ibn Majah reports warn against lavish praise, even throwing dust at eulogists. Scholars distinguish exploitative praise from honest encouragement by intention. False praise breeds vanity and neglected deeds, while the Prophet accepted praise by Abu Talib, al-Abbas, Hassan and Ka'b and praised Companions. He forbade exaggerated praise of himself like Christians' claims about Jesus; Qurtubi says elevating anyone beyond their true rank is sinful. Al-Raghib permits certification only in a specified circumstance; Kuranyolu says accurate testimony may be appropriate or necessary, and Tabatabai says religion determines which virtues count. [13 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(العلماء,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,معاوية عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الراغب); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 4:49/q08/p3 A person divinely certified in a role may state that role for others' benefit, or mention God's favor for a sound purpose. — Biqai gives prophethood as an example; Alusi permits legitimate mention of God's favor; Zamakhshari and Abu Hayyan explain the Prophet's statement that he was trustworthy in heaven and earth as a response to hypocrites' accusation of unfair distribution, while Razi adds God's authentication through a miracle. God's spoken praise of the prophets, listed by Tabatabai with verses for Adam, Noah, Abraham, Idris, Jacob, Joseph, Moses, Jesus, Solomon, Job and Muhammad, is authoritative. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 4:49/q08/p4 Practical purification by good deeds and moral improvement is encouraged, while boasting of it is forbidden. — Al-Raghib distinguishes acts that show virtue from speech that claims it (87:14, 53:32); Kuranyolu describes moral education and escape from bad traits; Tabatabai says acknowledging real learning and honoring scholars follows truth (22:32, 39:9), though scholars must not boast. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الراغب); TABATABAI(الراغب); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 4:49/q08/p5 Self-reliance is counted a virtue by some researchers. — Tabatabai attributes this view to fellow researchers following Maghrebi thinkers; he rejects it, citing Quranic dependence on God and deriving strength and honor from Him (3:173, 2:165, 10:65). [1 notes: TABATABAI(بعض باحثينا); TABATABAI-]
- 4:49/q08/p6 Reliance on God, rather than independent self-reliance, is the virtue. — Tabatabai invokes 3:173, 2:165 and 10:65; people cannot own virtue independently because God owns their possessions. [3 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]

## 4:49/q09 How does بَلِ correct the self-praisers?  [بَلِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the preceding denial is understood directly from bal or supplied as an omitted clause.
- 4:49/q09/p1 It turns from and repudiates their claim, making God's purification the only effective certification. — Abu Hayyan says they are unqualified to certify themselves; Ibn Ashur says bal must expressly repudiate them, for without it they might still expect inclusion among those God purifies. Alusi says the correction concerns their false self-purification, not miserliness and envy. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 4:49/q09/p2 It is joined to an implicit statement that they err or cannot truly purify themselves. — Abu al-Baqa reconstructs 'they erred; rather, God purifies'; Alusi supplies 'they do not truly purify themselves' before bal. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:49/q10 What does God's يُزَكِّى مَن يَشَآءُ mean?  [ٱللَّهُ يُزَكِّى مَن يَشَآءُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether purification here denotes moral cleansing, God's true testimony, acceptance of deeds, creation of faith, or inward transformation.
- 4:49/q10/p1 God cleanses from sin, reforms and makes pure whom He wills, enabling obedience. — Muqatil calls it reform; Tabari specifies divine enabling to abandon disobedience and obey, rather than effective self-praise; Thalabi, Zajjaj, Baghawi and Tabrisi stress cleansing, acquittal and setting right. Wahidi identifies monotheists and increase in righteousness; Ibn Kathir says God alone knows hidden realities. Biqai adds divine knowledge, power, wisdom and justice and created qualities producing righteous acts. [15 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 4:49/q10/p2 God judges, attests to and verbally describes as pure those He knows and approves. — Kashshaf, Baydawi, Nasafi and Abu Hayyan ground this in God's knowledge of hidden virtue; Razi says only God knows inward piety (53:32). Tabatabai distinguishes practical favor from verbal testimony and says verbal praise is meant here; he lists God's praise of named prophets. Ibn Ashur calls it God's testimony in place of self-certification. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 4:49/q10/p3 God accepts a person's deeds, thereby purifying that person. — Tabrisi reports this alternative and says God does not so purify the Jews but punishes them. [1 notes: TABRISI(قيل)]
- 4:49/q10/p4 God creates believers' faith, the highest form of purification. — Razi argues from faith's rank as the greatest and noblest purification and God's choice of whom to purify. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 4:49/q10/p5 In an inward reading, God replaces the ego's transient qualities with His own enduring attributes. — Qashani says inherent ego qualities cannot purify themselves and invokes 59:9 on greed; a Prophetic saying about resurrection overtaking the living depicts one who learns tawhid before the ego dies by annihilation, then becomes a libertine heretic. God erases ego qualities and grants lasting divine attributes without withholding their due. [6 notes: QASHANI(النبي)]

## 4:49/q11 What does وَلَا يُظْلَمُونَ فَتِيلًا assure?  [وَلَا يُظْلَمُونَ فَتِيلًا · interpretation]
turns on: Which people the pronoun covers and whether the promise is applied to punishment, reward, or both.
- 4:49/q11/p1 God wrongs no one by even the least amount, withholding no due good or imposing no undeserved evil. — Tabari includes both allocation of purification and rights, while affirming that He grants success or leaves the disobedient in failure by choice. Muqatil and Wahidi stress no loss of deeds or reward; Ibn Abbas in Durr includes both good and evil. Tabrisi, Biqai and Abu Hayyan reason that if no tiny injustice occurs, none greater occurs, comparing 4:40; Ibn Qutayba compares naqir. Bursevi defines wrong as extra punishment or reduced reward; Ibn Ashur says God knows deserts and guides truly. Bint Shati says all three Quranic occurrences concern God's reckoning. [25 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس، مجاهد، عكرمة، عطاء، الحسن، قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الفراء وابن قتيبة والطبري والقرطبي والراغب); SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN]
- 4:49/q11/p2 The self-praisers receive exactly the punishment or blame earned by their false claim. — Kashshaf, Razi and Nasafi give this as one reading; Tabrisi, Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi apply it to their punishment. Alusi prefers this warning-context reading and joins the clause to an implied statement that they will be punished. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)+]
- 4:49/q11/p3 Those God purifies receive the entire reward of their purity or obedience. — Kashshaf, Razi and Nasafi offer this alternative, Razi comparing 4:40; Sam in says they lose none of their purification; Alusi calls it a circumstantial reading referring to the Resurrection. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 4:49/q11/p4 Both the self-praisers' just punishment and the divinely purified people's reward are fully measured. — Samin reports this two-group reading; Qurtubi and Ibn Atiyya include both antecedents; Abu al-Baqa allows it with an independent sentence, and Alusi reports it but prefers the warning reading. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,غير مسمى); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 4:49/q12 To whom does the pronoun in يُظْلَمُونَ refer?  [يُظْلَمُونَ · referent]
turns on: The nearest grammatical antecedent versus the warning context and an inclusive reading of both groups.
- 4:49/q12/p1 The preceding self-praisers. — Ibn Atiyya, Samin and Abu Hayyan allow this; Alusi prefers it in the warning context, and connects the clause with an omitted statement of their punishment. Bursevi and Baydawi likewise speak of the claimants' punishment. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)+]
- 4:49/q12/p2 Those whom God chooses to purify. — Abu Hayyan and Samin prefer the nearest antecedent man yasha, with Abu Hayyan noting the reference can follow sense; Samin says bal begins a disconnected correction. Alusi says they obtain full reward. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 4:49/q12/p3 Both the claimants and those God purifies. — Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi say justice toward others is known independently; Samin reports the two-group reading and Abu al-Baqa's independent-clause account, while allowing the antecedent also with a circumstantial clause; Alusi reports but does not prefer it. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,غير مسمى); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 4:49/q13 How is وَلَا يُظْلَمُونَ attached grammatically?  [وَلَا يُظْلَمُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause describes the preceding action's circumstance or starts a new assertion, with a possible implied antecedent statement.
- 4:49/q13/p1 It is a circumstantial clause related to what precedes. — Samin allows this, including with both groups as antecedent; Alusi gives a circumstantial reading for those God purifies, who receive their full reward. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL]
- 4:49/q13/p2 It is an independent sentence, possibly connected to an omitted statement of punishment. — Abu al-Baqa assigns both groups as referent in an independent clause; Alusi attaches it to an omitted statement that their heinous claim will receive just punishment. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 4:49/q14 How is يُظْلَمُونَ read?  [يُظْلَمُونَ · readings]
turns on: Whether Ibn Atiyya's attributed tāʾ reading is admitted alongside the unanimously reported canonical yāʾ reading.
- 4:49/q14/p1 The received reading uses third-person yāʾ. — Ibn Mujahid says all agree on it at 4:49, although several readers use tāʾ at 4:77; Ibn al-Jazari likewise reports unanimity here and explains that the third-person man yasha governs the following clause, unlike other occurrences where Rawh has varying routes. [4 notes: IBNMUJAHID(نافع، أبو عمرو، عاصم، ابن عامر؛ القراء في 4:49); NASHR]
- 4:49/q14/p2 An attributed group reads second-person tāʾ, 'you are not wronged.' — Ibn Atiyya reports this group, against later reports of unanimous third-person recitation. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(طائفة)]

## 4:49/q15 What physical thing is فَتِيلًا?  [فَتِيلًا · meaning]
turns on: Which tiny object supplies the image; several sources treat the alternatives as equivalent evidence for negligible amount.
- 4:49/q15/p1 The minute fiber or thread in a date stone's groove or cleft. — This is attributed to Ibn Abbas, Ata, Mujahid, Qatada, Dahhak, Hasan and others by multiple collectors. Some call it the groove itself, a line inside the stone or the thread on its back where the palm sprouts; Ibn Kathir says the alternatives are close. Authors distinguish naqir as a back dimple or dot and qitmir as husk, skin or wrapping; Mujahid also compares an egg membrane and reed skin. Samin lists thafruq as a fourth minute date-stone object, the first three appearing in scripture. Ibn Qutayba says these images are figurative for minute justice; Study Quran links 4:77, 4:124, 17:71 and 19:60. ## 4:49/q16 How is فَتِيل morphologically derived?  [فَتِيلًا · grammar]
- 4:49/q16/p1 It comes from twisting and has faʿīl with the passive sense of mafʿūl: a twisted thing. — Tabari compares sariʿ from masruʿ and dahin from madhun; Ibn Jawzi cites him. Jishumi cites fatila, a wick; Tabrisi adds the twisted-looking camel forearm. Thalabi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Samin apply the passive sense to the finger residue, with Abu Hayyan calling the form intensive. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وأبو مالك والسدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 4:49/q17 What is the accusative function of فَتِيلًا?  [فَتِيلًا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the accusative is governed as an object, a specification of amount, or an implicit absolute object.
- 4:49/q17/p1 It is the second object of 'wrong,' the first having become the passive subject. — Ibn Atiyya states this; Ali ibn Isa is cited by Tabrisi and Alusi; Samin explains the passive promotion. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(علي بن عيسى); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(علي بن عيسى)]
- 4:49/q17/p2 It is an accusative of specification or a measure standing for 'a fatil's amount.' — Jishumi describes the measured object and an alternative specification; Ali ibn Isa permits specification, compared by Alusi to 'I sweated sweat.' [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI(علي بن عيسى); ALUSI-FULL(علي بن عيسى)]
- 4:49/q17/p3 It substitutes for an absolute object of injustice, or modifies an omitted verbal noun. — Ibn Ashur says the comparison particle is omitted and compares 4:40; Samin supplies the omitted verbal noun and likewise compares 4:40; Samarrai says the expression conveys both physical thread and smallest injustice. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 4:49/q18 How does this verse connect with its surrounding passage and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes emphasize different links in the passage: the preceding sins, the following accusation, or Quranic parallels.
- 4:49/q18/p1 It rebukes the People of the Book after discussion of unbelief, shirk and scriptural distortion, and their self-praise helps explain the following charge of lying. — Tabrisi places it after unbelief and distortion; Razi places the privileged-status claims after the shirk threat of 4:48; Tabatabai sees Jewish arrogance, refusal to believe and the divine curse as consequences of self-admiration. Asad says deeper shirk by Christians' elevation of Jesus and saints and Jews' reliance on Talmudic lawmaking authority, compared with 9:31, links this to 4:48; he also includes Muslims worshipping saints. - 4:49/q18/p2 It parallels other warnings against self-praise and images of minute divine justice. — Kashshaf, Razi, Qurtubi, Nasafi and Study Quran connect 53:32; Qurtubi compares naqir at 4:124, Abu Hayyan and Ibn Ashur compare the atom's weight at 4:40; Study Quran also cites 4:77, 17:71 and 19:60; Bint Shati notes all three fatil occurrences concern reckoning.
