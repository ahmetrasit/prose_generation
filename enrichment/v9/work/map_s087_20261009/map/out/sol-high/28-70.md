# 28:70: 26 questions

## 28:70/q01 To whom does the opening 'He' refer?  [وَهُوَ ٱللَّهُ · referent]
- 28:70/q01/p1 It refers to the God already described in the preceding discourse. — Ibn ʿĀshūr traces the antecedent to the One described from 28:58 onward; Ṭabāṭabāʾī identifies the nearer 'your Lord' in the preceding verse. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 28:70/q02 How is the opening pronoun and divine name constructed?  [وَهُوَ ٱللَّهُ · grammar]
- 28:70/q02/p1 The divine name is the predicate of the opening pronoun. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the explicit name prevents confusion or attribution of its qualities to another; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the definite name alludes to a description. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 28:70/q03 What does 'no god but He' establish?  [لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ · theology]
- 28:70/q03/p1 God alone possesses divinity and deserves worship; He has no equal, partner, or independent rival. — Al-Qushayrī stresses unmatched lordship; al-Bayḍāwī and Ibn Kathīr say only He is worshipped, with Ibn Kathīr linking this to His sole creation and choice. Al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī say the negation reinforces the first clause, like saying the Kaaba alone is the qibla. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Iṣlāḥī read it against idols and polytheists, with Ibn ʿĀshūr citing 28:64 and 10:32. Ṭabāṭabāʾī grounds exclusive worship in God's knowledge, all perfection and blessing, sole ownership of creation and legislation, and final accounting; he says worship is praise by word or deed, and ownership cannot be shared, transferred, or delegated, citing 12:40, 3:26, 42:49, and 67:1–3. Al-Qāshānī adds that God has no partner in existence. [22 notes: QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 28:70/q04 What do 'the first' and 'the last' designate here?  [ٱلْأُولَىٰ وَٱلْءَاخِرَةِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the paired temporal words mark the two abodes or time's beginning and end.
- 28:70/q04/p1 This world and the Hereafter. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām states the identification directly. Muqātil, as reported by al-Wāḥidī, and al-Baghawī distinguish praise by God's allies here from praise by Paradise's people there. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies al-ūlā as this world; Study Quran notes that al-ākhirah means 'the last' and is commonly the Hereafter, especially against al-dunyā, 'lower' or 'nearer,' while al-ūlā means 'first.' Asad offers the rendering 'present life' and 'life to come.' Kuran Yolu reports this interpretation from commentators. [25 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); STUDYQURAN]
- 28:70/q04/p2 The beginning and end of time, or 'before and after.' — Study Quran allows the beginning and end of time alongside the two abodes; Kuran Yolu renders the expression 'before and after.' [2 notes: STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 28:70/q05 What does the praise mentioned here signify?  [ٱلْحَمْدُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether praise is glossed as an act directed to God or depicted by al-Ḍaḥḥāk's cloak metaphor.
- 28:70/q05/p1 It is praise directed to God. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports Kaʿb's direct gloss. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(كعب)]
- 28:70/q05/p2 Praise is the cloak of the All-Merciful. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports this metaphor from al-Ḍaḥḥāk. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك)]

## 28:70/q06 How exclusive and deserved is God's praise in both abodes?  [لَهُ ٱلْحَمْدُ فِى ٱلْأُولَىٰ وَٱلْءَاخِرَةِ · theology]
turns on: Whether reward is a debt or grace, whether worldly blessings still require gratitude in Hell, and whether exclusivity applies to each abode or only to both together.
- 28:70/q06/p1 All praise ultimately belongs to God in each abode, for His gifts, attributes, just acts, and every created virtue. — Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī, Ṭabāṭabāʾī, and Iṣlāḥī ground it in blessings from God in both worlds; al-Ālūsī says other agents are means and 28:71 emphasizes response to blessings. Al-Qushayrī distinguishes praise for gifts and thanks for favor; Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites divine attributes; Ibn Kathīr cites the justice and wisdom of all divine acts; al-Rāzī says reward is grace, not a debt, citing 35:34, 39:74, and 10:10. Al-Qāshānī sees all manifest and hidden perfection, including worldly beauty, wealth, strength, and honor and afterlife knowledge and gnosis, as God's manifestations. Ibn ʿĀshūr argues that even polytheists praise God, that the lām and fronting make true exclusivity and generic al-ḥamd covers all praise; worldly praise of others is figurative, with 1:1 and 17:52 cited. Al-Ālūsī replies that even virtues in others are God's creation while the Prophet is also praised at the great intercession. Biqāʿī says belief or disbelief does not change God's possession of praise. [21 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 28:70/q06/p2 Exclusive praise applies to the two worlds together: others receive worldly praise, while only God receives it in the Hereafter. — Al-Ālūsī reports unnamed interpreters who distinguish broader praise from gratitude. He objects that created virtues ultimately belong to God and that the Prophet is praised in the Hereafter at the great intercession. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 28:70/q06/p3 Paradise's people owe no praise for reward because it is owed, and Hell's people received no favor requiring praise. — Al-Rāzī attributes this to the Muʿtazilites; his own grace argument counters the claim about reward. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); RAZI-FULL-]
- 28:70/q06/p4 Even Hell's people owe God praise for worldly enablement, facilitation, kindness, and other blessings. — Al-Qāḍī, as reported by al-Rāzī, says wrongdoing does not erase those gifts. Al-Rāzī objects that afterlife dwellers would then know repentance must be accepted and gratitude rewarded; able to act on that knowledge, they would repent and thank God, ending punishment. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); RAZI-FULL-]

## 28:70/q07 Who praises God in the Hereafter, and how?  [لَهُ ٱلْحَمْدُ فِى ٱلْأُولَىٰ وَٱلْءَاخِرَةِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the response in the next life is described as praise or specifically as thanks.
- 28:70/q07/p1 Paradise's people praise God with joy and delight, as a spontaneous pleasure rather than a burdensome duty. — Muqātil, al-Baghawī, and Ibn al-Jawzī identify God's allies here and Paradise's people there. Al-Nasafī says the saved praise Him for removal of grief, fulfilled promise, and His being Lord of the worlds, citing 35:34, 39:74–75. Al-Bayḍāwī says believers rejoice in His favor and praise, citing 35:34 and 39:74; Abū Ḥayyān mentions several formulas and cites the same verses. Al-Ālūsī reports commentators citing 39:74, 35:34, and 10:10, and reports Jābir's hadith in Muslim and Abū Dāwūd: Paradise's people are inspired to glorify and proclaim oneness as naturally as breathing. Abū Ḥayyān also reports a hadith of inspired glorification and sanctification but gives no transmitter or grading. Al-Bursawī and Iṣlāḥī also cite Paradise praise, the latter pointing to Sūrah Yūnus. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(جابر,غير واحد); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:70/q07/p2 God is praised in this life and thanked in the next. — Al-Qushayrī relates praise to divine gifts and thanks to divine favor. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 28:70/q08 Why are 'the first' and 'the last' given in that order?  [ٱلْأُولَىٰ وَٱلْءَاخِرَةِ · rhetoric]
- 28:70/q08/p1 Their order follows their sequence in meaning. — Al-Itqān compares the ordering of first and last with before and after and earlier and later groups. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 28:70/q09 What rhetorical purpose does the praise clause carry?  [لَهُ ٱلْحَمْدُ فِى ٱلْأُولَىٰ وَٱلْءَاخِرَةِ · rhetoric]
- 28:70/q09/p1 Exclusive praise is a literally true assertion that also points ahead to resurrection and recompense. — Ibn al-Iṣbaʿ, reported by al-Itqān, defines embedding as concealing one purpose in another and says seeming hyperbole about God's praise is literal truth. Al-Itqān's author prefers this embedding of the final-life purpose. Ibn ʿĀshūr says possession, fronting, and generic al-ḥamd establish true exclusivity. [3 notes: ITQAN(ابن الإصبع); IBNASHUR-FULL; ITQAN+]

## 28:70/q10 What is God's 'judgment' in this clause?  [وَلَهُ ٱلْحُكْمُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ḥukm covers God's governing decree across both worlds, derivative authority in this world, or final adjudication.
- 28:70/q10/p1 His effective decree, rule, and adjudication over all creatures and affairs, in this life and the next. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām calls it decree; al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and al-Nasafī call it decision among creatures. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says deciding and resolving affairs, Abū Ḥayyān joins decree with deciding between servants, and Study Quran notes ḥukm can mean judgment, decision power, dominion, rule, or wisdom. Al-Ṭabrisī says it distinguishes truth from falsehood; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī call it effective decree over everything, the latter denying any partner. Ibn Kathīr says none can overturn it because of God's might, dominance, wisdom, and mercy. Biqāʿī says God alone executes it and could compel belief. Al-Bursawī applies it to creation, choice, honor, abasement, life, and death; Kashf al-Asrār extends it over both worlds. Al-Qāshānī applies divine will even to worldly ugliness, poverty, weakness, and lowliness and to the forsaking and captivity of the veiled in the next life. Ibn ʿĀshūr defines judgment as assigning benefit or harm in both abodes; Study Quran says rightful worldly power operates through God's power, while He judges directly in the next, citing 4:141, 10:93, 22:56, and 27:78. [22 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 28:70/q10/p2 It refers specifically to worldly authority, under which every creature's ruling only takes effect through God's ruling. — Al-Rāzī includes masters, husbands, parents, rulers, and the Messenger as holders of derivative worldly rule. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 28:70/q10/p3 It refers specifically to God's final judgment among servants, including justice for the oppressed. — Al-Rāzī gives this as the alternative to worldly authority: God secures the oppressed against oppressors. Study Quran likewise says final judgment is direct; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it belongs to God alone there. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:70/q11 What judgment awaits the obedient and disobedient?  [وَلَهُ ٱلْحُكْمُ · interpretation]
- 28:70/q11/p1 God grants the obedient forgiveness and favor and the disobedient misery and woe, while restoring rights without injustice. — Ibn ʿAbbās is cited by al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī; their wordings vary between forgiveness and mercy or favor, and misery or woe. Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports from ʿAzīz that God judges without injustice, restores rights between weak and powerful, and reveals contrasting eternal outcomes to those assembled. Ibn Kathīr says every good and evil deed is repaid and none is hidden. Biqāʿī says the passage fortifies the obedient through God's knowledge, freedom from defects, and just recompense. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عزيز); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 28:70/q12 How does the wording make judgment God's alone?  [وَلَهُ ٱلْحُكْمُ · rhetoric]
- 28:70/q12/p1 The possessive construction and fronting assert exclusive judgment. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the unstated object includes both abodes, that other worldly judgment is figurative, and that sole final judgment refutes idols and their claimed intercession, citing 10:18. Ibn ʿĀshūr also makes this the main point of verse 70, reached from God's creation and choice in 28:68. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:70/q13 What does returning to God mean?  [وَإِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether return is read as the universal final resurrection alone, an inward annihilation, or as both voluntary spiritual and involuntary final returns.
- 28:70/q13/p1 All people are resurrected and brought to God's judgment and recompense, with God as their final destination of authority. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām places it on Resurrection Day and mentions 28:88; Abū al-ʿĀliya says it follows life. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Bursawī, and Biqāʿī mention resurrection, Biqāʿī also the trumpet blast. Al-Ṭabrisī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī add recompense or accounting, and Ibn Kathīr says no deed escapes God. Al-Rāzī explains return as arrival at the realm of God's judgment and decree, not a spatial destination, since God transcends place and direction. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the ending singles out afterlife return after general praise and judgment and obliges affirmation of resurrection; Iṣlāḥī and al-Bursawī stress return to God alone. Kashf al-Asrār says all ultimately return to His final judgment; Biqāʿī adds that present decisions too depend on His enactment or reversal. [16 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 28:70/q13/p2 Return is annihilation in God's existence, acts, attributes, or Essence. — Al-Qāshānī offers these levels in an inward reading. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 28:70/q13/p3 Return can be voluntary spiritual journey to the Divine Presence or involuntary death, resurrection, judgment, and recompense. — The Najmiyya reading, reported by al-Bursawī, reserves the voluntary journey, following, and arrival for humans. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 28:70/q14 What does the preposition in 'to Him you return' imply?  [إِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ · grammar]
- 28:70/q14/p1 It marks the endpoint of return as God's jurisdiction, without implying that God occupies a place or direction. — Al-Rāzī identifies the endpoint as the realm of God's judgment and decree and explicitly denies a spatial direction for God. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 28:70/q15 Why is 'to Him' placed before the return verb?  [وَإِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ · rhetoric]
- 28:70/q15/p1 Its fronting serves the verse ending's rhyme and emphasizes arrival at God's judgment. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives both purposes. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:70/q16 How is the verb 'you return' read?  [تُرْجَعُونَ · readings]
- 28:70/q16/p1 Yaʿqūb's active reading opens the tāʾ and puts kasra on the jīm, making humans initiate the return. — Al-Nasafī reports Yaʿqūb's vocalization; Qiraat-Er contrasts its self-initiated meaning with the passively imposed return of the other form. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]

## 28:70/q17 How does this verse conclude the preceding argument?  [* · links]
- 28:70/q17/p1 It sums up God's sole divinity, choice, knowledge, praise, judgment, and the defeat of idolatrous claims. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says verse 70 continues creation and choice from 28:68, makes exclusive judgment its main point, and embeds divine majesty. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it completes the case for exclusive worship after God's knowledge of hidden and visible states. Iṣlāḥī says it summarizes the discussion and refutes Arabs who acknowledged a supreme God yet gave Him partners; his wider passage exposes Quraysh's baseless idols and intercessors, which they regarded as sources of success and feared abandoning. Al-Qurṭubī says God praises Himself as knower of hidden and visible, uniquely divine, sole recipient of praise and ruler, and final recipient of all return. Biqāʿī says these verses strengthen the obedient with knowledge of God's omniscience, perfection, and just recompense. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 28:70/q18 What does the nearby statement of God's choice establish?  [* · links]
- 28:70/q18/p1 God's will alone makes choices effective; people cannot independently bring about their preferred outcomes. — Biqāʿī applies 28:68 to the demand for a messenger with Moses' signs and says a contrary event would imply a defect in God; independent rival wills would imply weakness. He explains al-khiyara as choosing or the chosen object, a result named for its cause: without God's choice, human choice is void. He reports al-Rāzī's use of it against independent human freedom. He adds that the contented obey promptly, endure affliction, accept honor and abasement, and want only what God wills; the cited verses illustrate acceptance of blame out of love. Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects God's choice to knowing inward and outward sins and purifying people through wisdom. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الرازي); TABATABAI]

## 28:70/q19 How does God's knowledge in the preceding verse support this verse?  [* · links]
- 28:70/q19/p1 God knows hidden and disclosed states, so His choice and judgment are informed by what people truly believe and do. — Biqāʿī mentions hidden thoughts, possible response to Moses-like signs, sincerity of professed belief, and outward disclosure in 28:69. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says knowledge of inward and outward sins informs God's wise choice and purification; al-Qurṭubī also connects hidden and visible knowledge to God's sole divinity, praise, rule, and final return. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 28:70/q20 What does 'subḥānahu wa-taʿālā' in the adjacent passage mean?  [* · meaning]
- 28:70/q20/p1 It declares God transcendent, holy, and glorified above the partners ascribed to Him. — Al-Qurṭubī distinguishes subḥāna as declaring transcendence and taʿālā as exaltation above their associations. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 28:70/q21 What variant of 'takun' is reported in the adjacent passage?  [* · readings]
- 28:70/q21/p1 Ibn Muḥayṣin and Ḥumayd read it with fatḥa on tāʾ and ḍamma on kāf. — Al-Qurṭubī reports the reading and mentions 27:74. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن محيصن وحميد)]

## 28:70/q22 What does 'araytum' in the surrounding passage mean?  [* · meaning]
- 28:70/q22/p1 It means 'tell me.' — Abū Ḥayyān gives this gloss alongside his comment on divine judgment. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 28:70/q23 What inward account of divine gifts and spiritual states is given around this verse?  [* · inward]
- 28:70/q23/p1 All good and changes of spiritual state come from God, whose continued favor sustains communion. — Al-Qushayrī traces beneficence to sovereignty, blessing to mercy, and victory to power. He says only God gives dawn after spiritual nights: repentance after lapse, existence after seeking, expansion after constriction, and union after separation; He also gives restful seclusion at night. While communion's daylight persists, detractors cannot spoil its joy, and its continuation depends on God. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 28:70/q24 What is reported about the revelation setting of al-Qaṣaṣ?  [* · sciences]
- 28:70/q24/p1 The whole sūra is Meccan. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this from al-Ḥasan, ʿAṭāʾ, and ʿIkrima. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وعطاء وعكرمة)]

## 28:70/q25 What historical continuation does al-Bursawī mention in this passage?  [* · reports]
- 28:70/q25/p1 The Ottoman sultans are the final dynasty and continue until the awaited Mahdī. — Al-Bursawī says major scholars established this as sound. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 28:70/q26 What scholarly path does al-Bursawī commend here?  [* · inward]
- 28:70/q26/p1 Scholars should unite outward and inward knowledge and remain steadfast on the path of realization. — Al-Bursawī favors such scholars and prays for steadfastness. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
