# 80:20: 14 questions

## 80:20/q01 What does thumma convey here?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
- 80:20/q01/p1 It marks a rise in rank, stressing the greater wonder and favor of easing human action through intellect. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says this is a distinction in rank rather than mere temporal delay: intellect is humanity's greatest endowment, and the facilitated path of action follows from it. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 80:20/q02 Which path is made easy?  [ٱلسَّبِيلَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether al-sabīl belongs to the surrounding account of bodily creation and birth, human moral guidance and choice, the path of conduct during life, or each person's divinely decreed course; sources appeal to surrounding verses, 76:3 and 90:10, and accounts of capacity and birth.
- 80:20/q02/p1 The child's passage out of the mother's womb is eased. — Al-Ṭabarī and the Kuran Yolu report of his view favor this from the context of creation and bodily change. Al-Qushayrī says the infant emerges headfirst and inverted; al-Ṭabrisī and al-Rāzī describe the turn at delivery and the narrow passage; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Bursawī add the womb's opening; al-Ālūsī adds nerves extending along the passage. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it one of the two passages and connects emergence with the following death and burial. Al-Biqāʿī also extends the route to Paradise or Hell. Iṣlāḥī accepts delivery for mother and child but extends providential easing through nourishment and later activity. [39 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(أبو صالح,ابن عباس,السدي,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة والضحاك); QUSHAYRI(ويقال); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي، ومقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي ومقاتل); RAZI-FULL(القول الأول); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وعطاء وقتادة والسدي ومقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو صالح,ابن عباس,الضحاك,عكرمة,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(IK, Q); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî)+; NAHHAS(عبد الله بن الزبير)+; TAB-FULL+]
- 80:20/q02/p2 God opens and facilitates the ways of good and evil, guidance and error. — Mujāhid and others compare 76:3; al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and others also cite 90:10. Al-Rāzī's Abū Muslim includes worldly and religious choices, enabled by capacity, instruction, reason, prophets and books; al-Ṭabrisī says this enables good and avoidance of evil. Al-Bursawī similarly lists capacity, benefit and harm, reason, prophets and books. Ibn Kathīr calls this the stronger view. Kuran Yolu prefers it as a memorable gift of human choice, distinguishing humans and bringing responsibility. Al-Biqāʿī and Iṣlāḥī include both material and spiritual routes; Iṣlāḥī cites 87:2–3 and 91:7–10. Asad emphasizes intellect and earthly opportunity. The speakers named by individual sources vary, including Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, al-Ḥasan and ʿAṭāʾ. [31 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); FARRA; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن، ومجاهد، وعطاء، والكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي ومقاتل والحسن ومجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن ومجاهد); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، الحسن، عطاء، ابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد والحسن وعطاء); TABATABAI(قيل); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(IK); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد والحسن وابن زيد)+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 80:20/q02/p3 The way is the two outcomes of happiness and misery, or felicity and wretchedness. — Reports from Mujāhid express the two alternatives in terms of their outcomes and link them to 76:3, where a person is grateful or ungrateful. Al-Māwardī likewise reports this wording. [5 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 80:20/q02/p4 God eases the particular course or function decreed for each person or created thing. — Abū Bakr ibn Ṭāhir and other reports link ease to what God created and decreed for each person. Al-Qurṭubī and Study Quran cite the Prophet's saying that each person is facilitated toward what they were created for; Study Quran broadens the formulation to each thing's function. [7 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو بكر بن طاهر,النبي); BURSEVI(أبو بكر بن طاهر); STUDYQURAN(Some)]
- 80:20/q02/p5 The path is sound reflection leading to faith, eased by human reason. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports al-Ḥasan; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī instead transmit it from Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda, Abū Ṣāliḥ and al-Suddī. They make the granting of reason and capacity for thought the means of facilitation. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس، قتادة، أبو صالح، السدي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة وأبو صالح والسدي)]
- 80:20/q02/p6 The way is the path of good, obedience, religion or Islam leading to salvation. — Al-Ḥasan calls it the path of good; Ibn Zayd says God taught and eased Islam; al-Rāzī reports a restriction to religion, arguing that al-sabīl suggests worldly conditions rather than what is attained in the afterlife. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ speaks of the person destined for success seeking right guidance and salvation. Al-Jishumī infers enabled obedience and prior capacity even for an unbeliever. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ prefers the unqualified path as obedience and attainment of good and happiness in context. [9 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,الحسن); SULAMI(ابن عطاء); JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(القول الثالث); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); BURSEVI(ابن عطاء); TABATABAI+]
- 80:20/q02/p7 The path is a person's deeds and conduct, likened to the road a traveler walks. — Ibn ʿĀshūr states the ordinary sense of sabīl is a road and reads it metaphorically of human acts and conduct. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 80:20/q02/p8 The verse covers God's providential easing of life from birth through growth, nourishment and activity, both material and spiritual. — Elmalılı includes development from birth to mature intellect and reason. Iṣlāḥī broadens the common birth reading to nourishment and human activity, then distinguishes following the guided path from misuse of free will. [3 notes: ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 80:20/q02/p9 God makes accessible the paths of a person's benefits and harms. — Al-Māwardī offers this as a further possibility. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 80:20/q03 Why is the path expressed with the definite article?  [ٱلسَّبِيلَ · rhetoric]
- 80:20/q03/p1 The definite article presents the path as general and shared rather than peculiar to one person. — Al-Bayḍāwī contrasts the definite article with a possessive construction. Al-Bursawī applies its breadth to humans and jinn on the moral reading and to animals too on the birth reading. Al-Ālūsī reports that 'his path' might imply each person has a private route, though some restrict the observation to guidance and error. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 80:20/q04 How is al-sabīl parsed and what does the object pronoun refer to?  [ٱلسَّبِيلَ يَسَّرَهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the suffix in yassarah returns to the fronted path or denotes the human, and whether the accusative follows an omitted governing verb, an adverbial use, or a second-object construction.
- 80:20/q04/p1 Al-sabīl is accusative through an omitted verb explained by yassarah, and the pronoun resumes the path. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr describe the ishtighāl construction. Al-Samīn cites 20:50 and 76:3; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 54:17 and supplies 'He made the path easy for him.' Al-Ālūsī says the separation before the returning pronoun causes no obscurity. Al-Jishumī simply calls al-sabīl the object of yassara. [10 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 80:20/q04/p2 The pronoun refers to the human, with al-sabīl read as an adverbial path or a second object. — Al-Samīn gives the adverbial parse for the good and evil road, citing 90:10. He reports Abū al-Baqāʾ's second-object parse, 'guided him to the path,' but says it requires a causative 'give' sense or an omitted preposition. Al-Naḥḥās also supplies an omitted lām in 'for the path' and describes a two-object construction. [3 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]

## 80:20/q05 Why is al-sabīl placed before its verb?  [ٱلسَّبِيلَ يَسَّرَهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 80:20/q05/p1 Fronting draws attention to the lesson of the eased path and preserves the verse endings. — Ibn ʿĀshūr applies this to both metaphorical senses of path, conduct and birth, and notes the verse-final sound. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 80:20/q06 What does yassarah mean here?  [يَسَّرَهُۥ · meaning]
- 80:20/q06/p1 God made the way easy or accessible for the human. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī, Ibn al-Jawzī reporting al-Farrāʾ, Abū Ḥayyān and Asad gloss the phrase directly this way. Ibn ʿĀshūr defines taysīr as making something easy and compares the Qur'an made easy for remembrance in 54:17. Al-Akhfash illustrates the person-path relation with hadāhu al-ṭarīq; al-Zajjāj expresses guidance along the way, whether grateful or ungrateful. [9 notes: FARRA; AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 80:20/q07 What force does the implicit and repeated verb add?  [ٱلسَّبِيلَ يَسَّرَهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 80:20/q07/p1 The explanatory repetition intensifies and fixes the sense of ease. — Al-Bayḍāwī says the verb governing the accusative is explained by the following verb, intensifying ease; al-Ālūsī says repetition reinforces it in the mind. Al-Bursawī says an explicating fāʾ, on either interpretation, details action already contained in the implied meaning. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 80:20/q08 What does facilitation imply about human choice and divine decree?  [ٱلسَّبِيلَ يَسَّرَهُۥ · theology]
- 80:20/q08/p1 God gives people capacity and guidance to choose their acts, so divine decree does not remove responsibility. — Al-Jishumī infers prior capacity for obedience, including an unbeliever's ability to believe. Al-Zamakhsharī calls moral paths accessible through ability and empowerment. Abū Muslim, reported by al-Rāzī, includes instruction, reason, prophets and books. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says God's will concerns acts humans perform by their own will: acts are decreed and chosen, and humans remain accountable; his preferred reading counters compulsory-action claims. Kuran Yolu says the gift of choice carries freedom and responsibility; Iṣlāḥī says misuse of free will leads to ruin. [8 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 80:20/q09 How can access to an evil path be counted a blessing?  [ٱلسَّبِيلَ يَسَّرَهُۥ · theology]
turns on: Whether goodness lies in capacity for either path, in praiseworthy refusal of an accessible evil, or in inability to harm; the notes also dispute whether abstention earns reward without ability.
- 80:20/q09/p1 The capacity to take either course is itself a manifest blessing, whatever course is chosen. — Al-Ālūsī makes empowerment itself the benefit, without depending on the moral direction selected. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 80:20/q09/p2 The accessible evil path makes abstention from it worthy of praise and reward. — Al-Ālūsī reports this answer and its assumption that a person unable to sin is not rewarded for abstaining. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 80:20/q09/p3 Resolving not to sin can be praised and rewarded even if the person lacks the ability to sin. — Al-Ālūsī reports this counterview while explaining the dispute over whether reward for abstention needs the ability to do the evil deed. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 80:20/q09/p4 Being unable to do evil is itself a blessing. — Al-Ālūsī reports some who cite a Persian couplet thanking God for lacking the power to harm people. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 80:20/q10 What does the path image suggest about worldly life's destination?  [ٱلسَّبِيلَ · interpretation]
- 80:20/q10/p1 Worldly life is a passage whose true destination is beyond it, ending in reward or punishment. — Al-Bayḍāwī says this is hinted by the moral-path reading. Al-Biqāʿī calls the present world a passage indicating the next. Al-Ālūsī reports the reward-or-punishment implication but calls it obscure. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 80:20/q11 How does this verse fit the surrounding sequence and the sūra's argument?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the sequence is read primarily as stages of the body, as divine favors supporting the sūra's warning, or as a recurrent event expressed in the past tense.
- 80:20/q11/p1 It continues the account of bodily creation and birth before death, burial and resurrection. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers the birth view because neighboring verses concern creation, bodily development and changing states. Al-Rāzī moves from proportioning to facilitation and calls death, burial and resurrection the final stage after this middle stage, citing 80:21–22. Ibn ʿĀshūr pairs emergence from the womb with subsequent death and burial as opposed movements onto and into the earth. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TAB-FULL+]
- 80:20/q11/p2 The sequence of favors and life events exposes human arrogance and points toward resurrection and judgment. — Al-Biqāʿī connects the sūra's warning to receptive hearts with resurrection, human creation and food as proofs of God's power, making rejection astonishing, and cites 79:45. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the inference and continuation weave divine favor into the argument. Iṣlāḥī says reminders of creation, sources of life, death and resurrection expose pride's transience and point to Judgment. Muqātil urges reflection, God's oneness and gratitude for blessing and good creation. [4 notes: MUQATIL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 80:20/q11/p3 The past-tense account describes a recurring human phenomenon. — Asad explicitly applies this observation to verses 18–22. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 80:20/q12 What recitation style accords with the ease God gives?  [يَسَّرَهُۥ · readings]
- 80:20/q12/p1 Ordinary recitation should be smooth and easy, with careful training for beginners but without excess. — Ibn Qutayba invokes the Prophet, early Muslims, Successors and learned reciters for smooth recitation. He permits a beginner's deliberate training to loosen the tongue and remove impediments, without excessive lengthening, hamza or assimilation. He faults an unnamed reader for inconsistency across analogous words and rules, strained defenses of readings, departure from Arab and Hijazi usage through excessive lengthening, hamza, imāla and assimilation, and making difficult what God made easy; he says later non-Arab readers' errors do not discredit the Qur'an. [5 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL+]

## 80:20/q13 Why did common people admire the difficult recitation?  [يَسَّرَهُۥ · reports]
- 80:20/q13/p1 Its difficulty, lengthy training, strained expression and sweat looked to them like mastery. — Ibn Qutayba gives this explanation of the unnamed reader's popularity. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]

## 80:20/q14 What ruling was reported for prayer using that reader's recitation?  [يَسَّرَهُۥ · law]
- 80:20/q14/p1 A worshipper reciting in that reader's mode, or praying behind it, must repeat the prayer. — Ibn Qutayba reports this from Ibn ʿUyayna and says Bishr ibn al-Ḥārith, Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal and many distinguished Muslims agreed. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(بشر بن الحارث وأحمد بن حنبل,سفيان بن عيينة)]
