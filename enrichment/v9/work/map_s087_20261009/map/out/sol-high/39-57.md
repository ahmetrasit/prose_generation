# 39:57: 16 questions

## 39:57/q01 How does this saying join the other sayings, and who says them?  [أَوْ تَقُولَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the alternatives distribute speeches among groups or follow one condemned person's sequence of thoughts; Bursawī gives both possibilities.
- 39:57/q01/p1 The sayings are alternatives voiced by different groups of condemned people. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the later aw taqūlu clauses coordinate with the first an taqūla. Qatāda, as reported by al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Durr, assigns neglect, the guidance plea and the return wish to first, second and third groups respectively. Elmalılı calls this speaker another soul. Bursawī offers different groups as an alternative. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI; BURSEVI]
- 39:57/q01/p2 The three sayings trace the condemned person's unfolding thought: regret, excuse about guidance, then wish to return. — Al-Ṭabarī connects the warnings and commands to these three regrets. Al-Zamakhsharī, reported by Abū Ḥayyān, retains this order. Ibn ʿĀshūr says changing it would lose the natural sequence of thought at judgment and compares the return wish in 23:99–100. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the sayings follow the order of utterance and that moving the reply beside its target would spoil the arrangement. Bursawī describes bewilderment, excuses and useless regret. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BURSEVI]

## 39:57/q02 When and by whom is this plea spoken?  [تَقُولَ · referent]
- 39:57/q02/p1 A lost or condemned soul says it in the Hereafter. — Al-Ṭabarī presents the speaker as imagining an afterlife plea; al-Baghawī and al-Thaʿlabī call it the soul's claim, and al-Ālūsī explicitly locates it in the Hereafter. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī puts it after reckoning and separation from the pious, comparing 36:59. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the speakers as polytheists who retain their earlier argument at judgment, citing 43:20. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:57/q03 How does the construction لو أن work here?  [لَوْ أَنَّ · grammar]
- 39:57/q03/p1 After لو, a nominal clause normally enters through أن rather than beginning with a bare noun. — Sībawayh contrasts لو with لولا, which can be followed by nouns, and cites 17:100. His wider discussion shows that an أن-clause can depend on what precedes: أحقا and أكبر ظنك support a raised construction, with a possible reversed analysis; Yūnus transmits the Arabs' recitation of al-Aswad ibn Yaʿfur, and al-Khalīl compares its threat with الرحيل بعد غد. Al-ʿAbdī, ʿUmar ibn Abī Rabīʿa and al-Nābigha al-Jaʿdī supply further poetic attestations. In لا محالة أنك, he supplies an omitted من. أما licenses إن through its meaning مهما يكن من شيء; his examples distinguish report from location, allow أن after جهد رأيي, and permit a preceding temporal phrase such as اليوم or بعد. شد ما and عز ما أنك mean حقا أنك and may be analyzed like نعم ما. In كما أنه the kāf governs أن while extra ما distinguishes it from كأن; poetry can omit ما. His separate singular فريق and قعيد analogy illustrates number usage in the same grammatical discussion. [16 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل,يونس)]

## 39:57/q04 How can a conditional clause supply what a wish leaves unspoken?  [لَوْ أَنَّ · rhetoric]
- 39:57/q04/p1 An omitted object of wishing or intending can be recovered from a following لو answer when that answer supplies it. — Al-Jurjānī distinguishes a poetic case where the object must be stated because wishing to weep and producing reflection are separate acts. In al-Buḥturī's line, لك مثلا is omitted from قد طلبنا because لم نجد لك مثلا supplies it; he says omission gains force when the explicit مثلا bears the denial of existence, whereas reversing the clauses would leave the denial on a pronoun. [4 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL(البحتري)]

## 39:57/q05 Why does the speaker name God explicitly?  [ٱللَّهَ · rhetoric]
- 39:57/q05/p1 Naming God instead of using a pronoun expresses reverence and delight in His name. — Al-Biqāʿī gives both reverence and delight as motives and glosses 'guided me' here as 'showed me the way.' [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 39:57/q06 What guidance does the speaker wish God had given?  [هَدَىٰنِى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether هدى means showing an accessible path, granting effective success, restoring the speaker to worldly obligation, or compelling belief; the timing and the force of divine willing also differ.
- 39:57/q06/p1 Direction to truth, God's religion and the path to faith, available in worldly life. — Al-Ṭabarī says truth and right direction; Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī say God's religion; al-Bayḍāwī and Bursawī say truth. Al-Biqāʿī glosses it as showing the way. Al-Ālūsī reports that many read it as showing the way to faith, fitting the conditional and the reply bala. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise says showing and directing. Al-Naḥḥās reports the worldly-life view. Al-Ṭabrisī's view about neglected proofs and Qurʾān presupposes guidance was disclosed. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 39:57/q06/p2 Effective enabling guidance or divine success that would bring about faith and piety. — Abū Manṣūr, reported by al-Nasafī, says God withheld success because He knew the unbeliever would choose error; special لطف protects and enables its recipient to be guided, while the one denied it strays despite having power to attain truth. Al-Ālūsī reports Abū Ḥayyān's alternative gloss as creating the act of guidance. The Study Quran reports commentators who cite 2:213, 24:46, 2:142 and 10:25 for God's guiding whomever He wills. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); NASAFI-FULL(أبو منصور)+]
- 39:57/q06/p3 Guidance to safety from the Fire through a return to earthly life and obligation. — Al-Naḥḥās reports a view that the speaker wishes to return and avoid sins. Al-Jubbāʾī, reported by al-Ṭabrisī, says they mean salvation through restored worldly accountability even though they know at judgment that God had guided them. [2 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI(الجبائي)]
- 39:57/q06/p4 God's willing to guide the person, thereby causing fear of punishment and avoidance of sin. — Al-Ṭabrisī casts the plea as 'if God had wanted to guide me'; the Study Quran reports the broader view that God guides whom He wills. [2 notes: TABRISI; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 39:57/q06/p5 Compelling the sinner to be guided. — Abū Ḥayyān describes this proposed plea but calls compulsion outside divine wisdom; he mentions as other evasions the claim that the sinner was unfit for grace or had ignored revelation. [0 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 39:57/q07 What would being among the God-fearing have meant?  [لَكُنتُ مِنَ ٱلْمُتَّقِينَ · interpretation]
- 39:57/q07/p1 The speaker claims guidance would have made him obedient, avoiding idolatry and sins and following what pleases God. — Al-Ṭabarī adds obedience and pursuit of God's pleasure; Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Nasafī stress avoidance of shirk, and al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, Bursawī and al-Ālūsī add sins. Al-Ṭabrisī mentions fear of punishment; al-Biqāʿī adds persistence among people who act only with guiding evidence. Al-Baghawī, Muqātil and al-Thaʿlabī state the general imagined outcome. [12 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:57/q08 Why say 'among the God-fearing' in this form?  [مِنَ ٱلْمُتَّقِينَ · rhetoric]
- 39:57/q08/p1 The phrase repeats the previous verse's emphatic pattern 'among the mockers.' — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the construction with 39:56. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:57/q09 Is the appeal to divine guidance a valid excuse for the speaker?  [* · theology]
turns on: How to distinguish disclosed signs from effective divine لطف or created guidance, and whether the true dependence on God's will excuses refusal of available proof.
- 39:57/q09/p1 The conditional can be true, but its use as an excuse is false or evasive. — Al-Qurṭubī calls the statement true yet compares it to the polytheists' claim in 6:148, a true phrase used for a false purpose; his report of ʿAlī's judgment on the Khārijites' slogan makes the same distinction. The Study Quran reports that God guides whom He wills while also emphasizing responsibility for refused signs. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the polytheists reused their old plea from 43:20 to silence the Prophet. Abū Ḥayyān compares their evasion with blaming leaders and devils in 14:21; al-Biqāʿī calls the excuse a lie when only truth could help. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(علي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 39:57/q09/p2 God disclosed sufficient guidance and proof, so refusing it leaves the speaker responsible and defeats the excuse. — Al-Zajjāj says God made the ways plain and a living discerning person has proof. Al-Ṭabrisī says worldly distraction led people to neglect proofs and Qurʾān; the signs rebut their presumption. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, al-Iṣlāḥī and the Study Quran say signs and revelation came but were denied, ignored or turned away from for worldly pursuits. Abū Ḥayyān rejects the appeal to compulsion as outside divine wisdom. Al-Biqāʿī calls the excuse false. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 39:57/q09/p3 The unbeliever correctly recognizes that effective divine لطف was not granted, though he still chose error and wasted the truth. — Abū Manṣūr, transmitted by al-Nasafī, says God knew the person's choice of error and withheld the success that would have enabled him to guide others. He holds that special grace protects and leads its recipient to guidance while people retain power to attain truth. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL(أبو منصور)+]
- 39:57/q09/p4 God had granted guidance and enabling grace, but the people did not follow it. — Al-Nasafī records this as the Muʿtazilī account and says Abū Manṣūr rejects it. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL(المعتزلة); NASAFI-FULL(أبو منصور)-]

## 39:57/q10 How does God's following reply answer this plea and the neighboring sayings?  [* · links]
turns on: Which implied negative balā cancels and how the reply's clauses distribute over the three preceding sayings.
- 39:57/q10/p1 Balā answers the plea's implied denial that God guided the speaker: His signs had come, but the speaker denied them. — Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Zajjāj, as reported by al-Qurṭubī, say balā answers an unstated negative. Ibn ʿĀshūr explains that balā cancels the implied negative and that the reply pairs the guidance excuse with arriving signs, neglect with arrogance, and the wish for piety with disbelief. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the reply specifically answers the second saying by naming the speaker's denial, arrogance and disbelief. Al-Ālūsī notes that reading guidance as direction fits both the conditional and balā; al-Zamakhsharī, as reported by Abū Ḥayyān, says the reply selects the statement needing an answer. Al-Zajjāj also says clear guidance and proof were already given. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 39:57/q10/p2 The reply deliberately answers the principal excuse first, then the other claims, rather than following their order. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a scrambled distribution after a summary: guidance is answered first, neglect second and the return wish third; mockery needs no further answer because it already admits guilt. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says moving the reply to the second saying would disrupt the natural sequence of the three speeches. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 39:57/q11 Would a return to worldly life make the condemned righteous?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The difference between what the condemned wish or claim and what God discloses they would actually do if returned.
- 39:57/q11/p1 The condemned person wishes for a return to do good or avoid sins. — Al-Naḥḥās reports the wish to regain earthly obligation and avoid sins. Al-Thaʿlabī and Ibn Kathīr recount the imagined return and virtuous conduct; al-Ṭabarī identifies it as the third afterlife regret. Kur'an Yolu says the wish follows the sight of punishment and receives no answer favorable to the speaker. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; THALABI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:57/q11/p2 Even if returned, they would repeat their former disobedience, so the claim of reform is false. — Ibn Kathīr cites 6:28; Ibn ʿAbbās, as transmitted by al-Durr, cites 6:28 and 6:110 and says they still could not attain guidance. Kur'an Yolu points to the following verse as the reason the chance is refused. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 39:57/q12 What does the reported vision of Paradise and Hell say about this lament?  [* · reports]
- 39:57/q12/p1 Each person in Hell sees the Paradise place they lost and regrets not being guided; each person in Paradise sees a Hell place and thanks God for guidance. — Abū Hurayra's report appears in Aḥmad and, according to Ibn Kathīr, al-Nasāʾī. Al-Itqān reports the Hell portion, including the verse's words; Bursawī transmits that nobody enters Hell without first seeing the assigned Paradise place and wishing for guidance. [4 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(أبو هريرة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ITQAN(رسول الله ﷺ); BURSEVI(الخبر)]

## 39:57/q13 What reports about decree and accountability are brought to bear on the plea?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports give distinct illustrations of divine decree and just accountability, rather than competing accounts of one event.
- 39:57/q13/p1 Reports from Zayd and other Companions teach belief in God's decree: events cannot miss their appointed recipients, and God could justly punish creation. — Aḥmad reports Ubayy, Ḥudhayfa, Ibn Masʿūd and Zayd answering Ibn al-Daylamī; Zayd attributes the teaching to the Prophet. One version adds that God's mercy exceeds deeds. Ibn Mājah's version adds that even immense charity is not accepted without belief in decree. [4 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNMAJAH(رسول الله)]
- 39:57/q13/p2 The Prophet says God could justly punish even him and Jesus for their sins, teaching humble self-accountability. — Ibn Ḥibbān transmits Abū Hurayra's report and frames it as a call to blame oneself for deficiencies in obedience despite much effort. [2 notes: IBNHIBBAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 39:57/q14 What does divine foreknowledge add to these future speeches?  [* · theology]
- 39:57/q14/p1 God disclosed what people would say and do before they said or did it. — Ibn ʿAbbās, transmitted by Ibn Kathīr and al-Durr, connects this disclosure with 35:14; al-Durr's version says it precedes even their own knowledge. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 39:57/q15 How does this plea fit the passage's call and warning?  [* · links]
- 39:57/q15/p1 The passage urges repentance, submission and following revelation before afterlife regret becomes useless. — Al-Ṭabarī says the commands aim to forestall three regrets. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads seven connected verses: mercy and the ban on despair prepare the calls to repent, submit and act; the discourse warns against worldly arrogance and contrasts pious salvation with unbelievers' loss. Al-Iṣlāḥī urges turning to the forgiving God rather than other deities or intercessors, accepting His Book before judgment; he says polytheism and intercession hasten ruin. The Study Quran says 54–67 leads into the final contrast between believers' and disbelievers' ends. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 39:57/q16 What occasion of revelation is reported for this passage?  [* · reports]
- 39:57/q16/p1 One reported view says verses 53–59 were revealed concerning Waḥshī. — The Study Quran attributes this to other commentators. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Other commentators)]
