# 73:4: 13 questions

## 73:4/q01 How much night may be added, and what are the available portions?  [أَوْ زِدْ عَلَيْهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'half' is the central measure with permitted reductions and increases, or an alternative syntax constrains the increments and yields smaller portions; a further explanation relates the choices to seasonal night length.
- 73:4/q01/p1 The worshipper may stand about half the night, reduce it toward one third, or increase it toward two thirds. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Wāḥidī, al-Nasafī and the exegetes cited by al-Ṭabrisī explicitly name one third and two thirds; al-Ṭabarī says the choice was given when night prayer was prescribed. Ibn al-Jawzī says flexibility answered the companions' difficulty in measuring the night, which sometimes led them to stand all night. Al-Qāshānī allocates thirds to rest, necessities and devotion for the weak. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ālūsī also describe the increments more generally as small changes around half. [15 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI(المفسرون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 73:4/q01/p2 The commanded portion remains below half, between one third and half; both increase and decrease remain inside that interval. — Al-Kashshāf's construction as reported by al-Ālūsī keeps either movement from reaching a neighboring named fraction. Al-Kashf argues that if the endpoints were meant the fractions would be named plainly; al-Ālūsī finds this strained. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ALUSI-FULL(الكشف)-]
- 73:4/q01/p3 An alternative rearrangement yields half, one third, or one quarter of the night. — Al-Ālūsī reports a fronting-and-postponement parse in which half substitutes for part of the night, and decrease or increase is measured from an amount below half. Some regard the named fractions as examples, not an exhaustive list. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 73:4/q01/p4 The choices are half, one quarter, or three eighths of the night. — A further proposal, recorded by al-Ālūsī, takes the second 'little' as one quarter and the increase as an eighth: for a sixteen-hour night the options are eight, four, or six hours. Al-Ālūsī objects that unrestricted 'increase' does not limit the increment to another little. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 73:4/q01/p5 The three portions vary with the seasons: half at equinox, less on short nights, more on long nights. — Al-Būrsawī reports this explanation from some interpreters. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 73:4/q02 What do the conjunction, ellipsis and pronoun in 'or increase it' connect to?  [أَوْ زِدْ عَلَيْهِ · grammar]
turns on: The attachment of 'half' and 'a little,' the referent of the pronouns, and whether a conjunction or earlier element is understood or transposed.
- 73:4/q02/p1 The pronoun refers to the stated half, and 'or' offers half, somewhat less, or somewhat more. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya refers both pronouns in the surrounding construction to half. Al-Ālūsī prefers measuring the change by the half spent in prayer, although the unprayed half is equal; he says the conjunction and pronoun work with either of the two main parses. Al-Nasafī makes half appositive to 'a little' in a three-way choice; he says half is little relative to the whole, though ordinary 'little' is under half. Al-Naḥḥās says the preceding wāw of 'or decrease' takes ḍamma to separate sukūns or kasra by its original pattern. Al-Ālūsī judges his first interpretation clearest and least contrived. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; NAHHAS; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 73:4/q02/p2 An omitted 'or' before 'half' supplies an explicit three-way disjunction. — Al-Akhfash says Arabic can leave out an 'or' in such a series, comparing an offer of one, two, or three dirhams. [1 notes: AKHFASH]
- 73:4/q02/p3 Half substitutes for the remaining night after the exception, and both pronouns refer to that remainder. — Al-Ālūsī reports this parse from some. Abū Ḥayyān objects that referring the pronoun to the unspecified 'little' would yield an unintelligible exception, while referring it to night makes the first exception redundant. Replies recorded by al-Ālūsī say the exception eases the vigil, contrasts worship and leisure, and clarifies after initial ambiguity; al-Samīn says an unspecified part may be excepted from the known night. Al-Ālūsī ultimately favors his earlier parse. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(السمين,بعض الأجلة,بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-]
- 73:4/q02/p4 A fronted construction makes half a partitive substitute or a fixed amount below half, with decrease and increase read against it. — Al-Ālūsī records variants: both pronouns may refer to half, giving a definite amount below half plus a decrease or increase; a further parse repeats 'or' as in 'either Zayd or ʿAmr.' He calls the latter strained because an exception normally precedes a substitute and the pronoun should return to the nearer half. Al-Tabrīzī instead excepts from a commanded two thirds and makes half substitute for 'little'; al-Ālūsī calls this implausible. One defense treats the lower amount as original obligatory baseline, while a counterexample questions whether decrease is truly a concession. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(التبريزي)-]

## 73:4/q03 What was the legal status of the night vigil associated with this command?  [أَوْ زِدْ عَلَيْهِ · law]
turns on: Whether the later easing removed the original duty generally or left a specific obligation for the Prophet.
- 73:4/q03/p1 The early obligation was eased or abrogated for the community, while night prayer remained obligatory for the Prophet. — Al-Qushayrī says the five daily prayers ended the community's original duty but not the Prophet's. Kur'an Yolu cites 17:79 and reports most commentators retained the duty for him, while calling the community's tahajjud sunnah. Al-Ḥasan calls additional prayer voluntary after an obligation. ʿĀʾisha's report in Ibn Kathīr says eight months of intense vigil ended when God restored the obligatory prayers; she also says the Prophet feared the followers' night prayer would become obligatory and advised sustainable deeds. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; THALABI(الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عائشة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler)]
- 73:4/q03/p2 The earlier night-vigil obligation was abrogated by the later command to recite what is easy. — Al-Māwardī cites 73:20 for the easing without specifying a continuing Prophetic exception. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 73:4/q04 Is this command for recitation within night prayer, and whom does it address?  [وَرَتِّلِ ٱلْقُرْءَانَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the command is read as part of the ordered vigil, as an independent act, or as a general instruction that also guides the Prophet's public delivery.
- 73:4/q04/p1 It directs the Prophet to recite during night prayer, while its manner also applies beyond that prayer. — Al-Biqāʿī says the command sets a general mode and makes recitation the animating core of prayer. Elmalılı says standing here ordinarily means prayer, although recitation and teaching extend beyond it; the lengthy practice trained worship and application when little revelation had yet arrived. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī coordinates it with the order to stand and cites the Qur'an's similar use of recitation for prayer at 17:78. Islahi also places slow recitation during prayer. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI+]
- 73:4/q04/p2 The command requires Qur'an recitation apart from prayer. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this alternative. [1 notes: TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 73:4/q04/p3 It may address all Muslims, or especially the Prophet in delivering revelation to others. — The Study Quran reports both scope alternatives; it notes that these are the earliest revealed verses explicitly enjoining Qur'an recitation as worship and cites 18:27, 27:92, and 29:45 as further commands. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 73:4/q05 How is the word 'Qur'an' read here?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانَ · readings]
- 73:4/q05/p1 The Hamza reading smooths the intervocalic hamza, pronouncing qurān rather than qurʾān. — Qiraat-ER says this phonetic reduction does not change the meaning. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(hmz)]

## 73:4/q06 What does tartīl mean and require in reciting the Qur'an?  [وَرَتِّلِ تَرْتِيلًا · meaning]
turns on: Whether tartīl chiefly describes audible clarity and measured spacing, unaltered textual sequence, intelligible explanation, gentle tone, or inward engagement; several sources combine these features.
- 73:4/q06/p1 Recite deliberately, clearly and in order, giving letters their full articulation and appropriate pauses so words and meanings remain intelligible. — Al-Ḥasan, Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda and many authors gloss it as clear, unhurried delivery; Mujāhid describes successive portions. Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq say haste prevents every letter and its length from receiving its due. Al-Aṣmaʿī, al-Mubarrad, al-Layth, al-Nāḥḥās, Ibn Kaysān and later authors compare separated, well-aligned teeth. Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ḥasan give groups of three to five verses. ʿAlī adds knowledge of where to pause; al-Naḥḥās prefers the established sense of separating letters. Elmalılı includes correct sounds, arrangement and meanings; Asad cites Jawharī, Bayḍāwī, Lisān al-ʿArab and Qāmūs for distinct, orderly, calm delivery. Ibn Qutayba refers to his discussion at 17:106, and Ibn al-Jawzī to 25:32. [64 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(الأصمعي); THALABI(الحسن,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس وزيد بن أسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن الأعرابي,ابن عباس,الضحاك,الليث,المبرد,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كيسان); TABRISI(ابن عباس,الزجاج,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج,الليث,المبرد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; NASHR(علي); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن,علي,قتادة,مجاهد); ITQAN(الزركشي,علي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; NAHHAS+; TABRISI+]
- 73:4/q06/p2 Read in the revealed sequence, without changing words or moving later material forward. — Ibn Baḥr and an alternative cited by al-Ṭabrisī ground the sense in even arrangement of teeth; al-Ṭabrisī describes them as gap-free and well ordered. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); TABRISI]
- 73:4/q06/p3 Explain and understand the Qur'an as it is recited. — Ibn Jubayr and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr gloss tartīl as interpretation or clear explanation; Ibn Kaysān says understanding should accompany recitation. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جبير); THALABI(ابن كيسان); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كيسان); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 73:4/q06/p4 The root suggests gentleness; the delivery should be soft, and on one reading sorrowful. — Al-Nāḥḥās records derivation from weakness and gentleness as a reason not to rush. Quṭrub, cited by al-Ṭabrisī, takes ratl as softness and asks for a sorrowful tone. [2 notes: NAHHAS(قيل); TABRISI(قطرب)]
- 73:4/q06/p5 It calls for inward contemplation, obedience, and bringing latent spiritual meanings into awareness. — Abū Bakr ibn Ṭāhir assigns the self to observe rulings, the heart to understand meanings, and the innermost self to attend to the address's subtleties. Al-Qushayrī pairs inner dwelling with slow speech; al-Qāshānī interprets distinct recitation as releasing meanings hidden in primordial potential through purification. [4 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر); THALABI(أبو بكر ابن طاهر); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI]
- 73:4/q06/p6 It means al-nabdh, casting or placing something down. — Ibn Jurayj transmits this gloss from ʿAṭāʾ without further explanation. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن جريج)]
- 73:4/q06/p7 It is a melodic, beautified recitation that lingers over verses. — Abū Baṣīr reports Abū ʿAbd Allāh's explanation of lingering and beautifying the voice; another Abū Baṣīr report similarly links taking time with improved voice. [2 notes: TABRISI(أبو بصير); TABATABAI(أبو بصير)]

## 73:4/q07 How should pauses and changes of meaning shape the actual recitation?  [وَرَتِّلِ ٱلْقُرْءَانَ تَرْتِيلًا · interpretation]
turns on: The degree of pause and slowness required to preserve letters and meaning, and whether embellishment serves or distorts the text.
- 73:4/q07/p1 Pause at verse endings and wonders, reflect, move the heart, and respond to mercy or warning with fitting prayer and voice. — Ibn Masʿūd and ʿAlī reject scattering like dates or sand and rushing like poetry; the Prophet paired similar short sūras rather than racing through al-Mufaṣṣal; Ibn Kathīr gives the twenty paired short sūras as ten pairs. Abū ʿAbd Allāh directs asking for Paradise and seeking refuge at Hell; al-Burhān and al-Zarkashī match vocal delivery to threat and reverence. Al-Itqān cites Ibn ʿUmar on learning lawful and forbidden content with stopping points, al-Naḥḥās on Companion agreement, Ibn al-Anbārī on complete Qur'anic knowledge, al-Nakzāwī on meaning and law, and Ibn al-Jazarī on breath, sound restarting and preserving meaning; later authorities require mastery for certification. Islahi describes the Prophet's melodic pauses, repetition, refuge, thanks and prostration. Elmalılı lists tajwīd contrasts of force, softness, length, nasalization, clarity, concealment, assimilation, connection and pause, all serving meaning; he cautions against articulation so exaggerated that it obscures meaning. [35 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو عبد الله,أمير المؤمنين,ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو وائل,ابن مسعود); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL(ابن مسعود رضي الله عنه); DURR-FULL(الحسن,علي); ITQAN(ابن الأنباري,ابن الجزري,ابن مسعود,الزركشي,النكزاوي,عبد الله بن عمر,علي); ALUSI-FULL(علي); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله,علي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 73:4/q07/p2 Precise articulation remains necessary even in a hurried reading; the fullest tartīl pauses naturally without dragging. — Al-Burhān reports this graded minimum and maximum. Elmalılı allows different tempos when letters and vowels remain sound, but rejects arbitrary song that stretches and bends sounds like an instrument. [4 notes: BURHAN(قيل); ELMALILI; ELMALILI-]

## 73:4/q08 How did the Prophet and early reciters perform measured recitation?  [وَرَتِّلِ ٱلْقُرْءَانَ تَرْتِيلًا · reports]
turns on: The reports illustrate different degrees of measured practice: ordinary lengthening and pauses, prolonged repetition, and a preference for fewer verses with deliberation.
- 73:4/q08/p1 The Prophet read slowly with audible lengthening, clear letters and pauses at verse endings. — Qatāda's received report says most Prophetic recitation was lengthened. Anas demonstrates lengthening each word or syllable of the basmalah; ʿĀʾisha says a shorter sūra could take longer because of measured pacing. Umm Salama and a wife of the Prophet describe verse-by-verse pauses or such measured pace that listeners could not reproduce it; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says listeners could count the letters. Ibn Kathīr names Aḥmad, Abū Dāwūd and al-Tirmidhī as transmitters of Umm Salama's example. [17 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أنس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(رسول الله); TABRISI(أم سلمة,أنس); QURTUBI-FULL(أنس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أم سلمة,أنس,عائشة); DURR-FULL(بعض أزواج النبي); ITQAN(أم سلمة,أنس); TABATABAI(أم سلمة,أنس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Kesîr); STUDYQURAN(Umm Salamah)]
- 73:4/q08/p2 A deliberate reading may linger on or repeat a single verse through the night. — Al-Biqāʿī cites al-Tirmidhī's report from ʿĀʾisha that the Prophet repeated 5:118 all night. Al-Durr reports a Companion identifying a weeping man repeating a verse as an example of commanded tartīl. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(عائشة رضي الله عنها); DURR-FULL(الحسن)]
- 73:4/q08/p3 Ibn ʿAbbās favored a measured reading of al-Baqara over finishing the whole Qur'an. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports his answer to Abū Ḥamza. [1 notes: TABRISI(ابن عباس)]

## 73:4/q09 What force does the repeated verbal noun 'tartīlan' add?  [تَرْتِيلًا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the emphasis chiefly establishes a binding level of performance or marks the practice's special importance.
- 73:4/q09/p1 It intensifies the command and indicates that proper measured recitation is necessary. — Al-Rāzī calls the verbal noun proof that the command binds every reciter; al-Nasafī likewise says it makes tartīl necessary. Elmalılı takes it to require the finest degree, fully giving letters and meanings their due. Al-Burhān contrasts its abundant measured reading with 36:69, which denies even teaching poetry. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ELMALILI; BURHAN]
- 73:4/q09/p2 The repetition emphasizes the honor and importance of measured Qur'an recitation. — Al-Biqāʿī explains the emphatic verbal noun in terms of the recitation's rank. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 73:4/q10 Is tartīl obligatory, and how does slower reading compare with faster reading?  [وَرَتِّلِ ٱلْقُرْءَانَ تَرْتِيلًا · law]
turns on: Whether the essential manner is a binding minimum, a recommended excellence, or both at different levels; the comparative reward also depends on quality versus quantity.
- 73:4/q10/p1 Every reciter must preserve the essential tartīl of clear letters, vowels, breathing and meaning, even at a faster tempo. — Al-Burhān makes the minimum obligatory for every Muslim. Al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī derive necessity from the emphatic verbal noun. Elmalılı says all tempos require tartīl and tajwīd; he distinguishes slow taḥqīq, moderate tadwīr and fast ḥadr by elongation, associating ʿĀṣim, Ḥamza and Warsh with the first, Ibn ʿĀmir and al-Kisāʾī with the second and the rest with the third. He says 73:20 permits the faster modes while preserving full letters and vowels. Al-Ṭabrisī calls fast ḥadr good although tartīl is encouraged here. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURHAN; ELMALILI; TABRISI]
- 73:4/q10/p2 Measured tartīl is recommended; a smaller amount read well is better than more read hastily. — Al-Itqān reports agreement in Sharḥ al-Muhadhdhab that excessive haste is disliked, cites scholars favoring one portion with tartīl over two without, and says tartīl aids reflection and reverence even for one who does not understand Arabic. Al-Ṭabrisī encourages tartīl though ḥadr is also good; Elmalılı says poorly performed reading can spoil a beautiful text. [6 notes: ITQAN(أصحاب شرح المهذب); TABRISI; ELMALILI]
- 73:4/q10/p3 Slow tartīl has greater reward in quality, while faster, longer reading gains more reward by the number of letters. — Ibn al-Jazarī, as reported by al-Itqān, gives this reconciliation of differing authorities; it invokes tenfold reward per letter. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن الجزري)]

## 73:4/q11 What does measured recitation do for understanding, spiritual response and perseverance?  [وَرَتِّلِ ٱلْقُرْءَانَ تَرْتِيلًا · interpretation]
turns on: The notes give complementary effects of deliberate reading: understanding and inner transformation, and visibly reverent delivery.
- 73:4/q11/p1 Its unhurried pace makes reflection possible, softens and illuminates the heart, and supports attentive response to revelation. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya names understanding, softness, light and mercy. Al-Rāzī says the mind can consider each verse's truths and subtleties, feel God's majesty, and respond to promise and threat; haste betrays inattentiveness, while reflection yields presence of heart and knowledge of God. Al-Biqāʿī says measured articulation reveals difficult meanings and hidden secrets; he connects steadfast service with divine aid and purification. Kur'an Yolu says measured reading better supports comprehension; Islahi says night prayer relieves anxiety and renews faith and resolve. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the vigil prepares the Prophet for weighty revelation and endurance of opponents. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]
- 73:4/q11/p2 The best recitation manifests reverent fear of God to its listener. — Al-Durr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmit the Prophetic description. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(طاووس); TABATABAI(رسول الله)]

## 73:4/q12 What promises and warnings about Qur'an recitation are connected with this verse?  [وَرَتِّلِ ٱلْقُرْءَانَ تَرْتِيلًا · reports]
turns on: These are different reported promises and warnings, rather than rival versions of one outcome.
- 73:4/q12/p1 The Qur'an's companion will be told to recite and ascend; the final rank matches the last verse recited. — The report appears through the Prophet in al-Thaʿlabī, Ibn ʿUmar in al-Ṭabrisī and al-Itqān, Abū Dāwūd in al-Qurṭubī, ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr in Ibn Kathīr, and Abū Hurayra or Abū Saʿīd in al-Durr. Ibn Kathīr says al-Tirmidhī grades it ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ. [6 notes: THALABI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI(ابن عمر); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو داود); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة أو أبو سعيد); ITQAN(ابن عمر)]
- 73:4/q12/p2 The Qur'an intercedes for one who kept vigil and restrained desire, bringing divine pleasure and ascent by verses. — Mujāhid's report in al-Durr adds a reward for each verse. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 73:4/q12/p3 Angels assist a deceased believer's ascent through Paradise to the extent of his Qur'anic knowledge. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk ibn Qays pairs this report with an exhortation to teach children and families. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(الضحاك بن قيس)]
- 73:4/q12/p4 Fluent reciters join noble angelic scribes, while struggling reciters receive double reward. — The Study Quran cites this hadith. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(aḥādīth)]
- 73:4/q12/p5 A gathering that recites and contemplates scripture receives peace, mercy, angels and divine remembrance. — The Study Quran reports the promise for a group gathered in a house of God. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(aḥādīth)]
- 73:4/q12/p6 Recitation changes the believer's comparison from a sweet date to fragrant sweet citrus; hypocrites receive parallel bitter comparisons. — The Study Quran gives the report's contrasts of reciting and nonreciting believers and hypocrites. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(a famous ḥadīth)]
- 73:4/q12/p7 The Prophet urged beautifying and chanting Qur'an with the voice. — The Study Quran cites his injunction to adorn the Qur'an with voices and the saying that one who does not chant it is not among his followers; Ibn Kathīr recalls praise of Abū Mūsā's Davidic-sounding voice and Abū Mūsā's wish to embellish it further. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN(the Prophet); IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 73:4/q12/p8 The Prophet warned against technically polished but hasty recitation that fails to reach the heart. — Sahl ibn Saʿd reports his warning that people would perfect letters like an arrow yet have the Qur'an remain at their throats and hasten to its end. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(سهل بن سعد الساعدي)]

## 73:4/q13 How does this command connect with other verses and the sūra's setting?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes connect the command either to parallel recitation passages or to the sūra's unfolding purpose and early revelatory setting.
- 73:4/q13/p1 It echoes God's own measured delivery of revelation and other Qur'anic instructions about recitation. — The Study Quran compares the Prophet's measured reading with God's description at 25:32; Asad says the phrase at 25:32 differs somewhat because it describes revelation rather than human reading. Ibn Qutayba refers to 17:106 and Ibn al-Jawzī to 25:32 for earlier discussion; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 17:78 for prayer named by recitation. The Study Quran cites 18:27, 27:92 and 29:45 as repeated recitation commands. [6 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 73:4/q13/p2 Night prayer and recitation train the Prophet for the weighty revelation and his mission, with later easing of the vigil. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes the sūra's sequence: prepare for revelation, bear abuse, leave opponents graciously, warn unbelievers, extend the ruling to believers, then ease it. He calls the sūra Meccan and among the earliest, reportedly second or third. A report from the Prophet describes revelation as a difficult bell-like sound or an angel in human form; ʿĀʾisha describes bodily effects. Islahi calls night prayer a remedy for anxiety and a source of renewed resolve. [4 notes: TABATABAI(رسول الله); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
