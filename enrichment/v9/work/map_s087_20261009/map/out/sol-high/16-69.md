# 16:69: 34 questions

## 16:69/q01 Why does the command to eat follow the command to make homes?  [ثُمَّ كُلِى · rhetoric]
- 16:69/q01/p1 Eating follows home-making in the bee's divinely ordered life. — Ibn Atiyya says the inspired command comes after home-making. Abu Hayyan says thumma marks the delay before gathering honey; Biqai and Bursevi say food follows rest as the next great need and the ease of provision is wondrous. Nasafi and Kashshaf also note the sequence. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; NASAFI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 16:69/q02 What does 'from all fruits' allow or describe?  [مِن كُلِّ ٱلثَّمَرَٰتِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether kull is contextually restricted, how thamarat names bee forage, and whether the gathered substance is plant matter or deposited dew.
- 16:69/q02/p1 The bee takes from the fruits or plants it customarily visits; 'all' need not mean every fruit. — Ibn Qutayba, Thalabi, Wahidi, Baghawi and Ibn Jawzi compare 46:25 and 27:23, where 'everything' is limited by context. Kashshaf, Nasafi, Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi say whatever fruits bees desire, including sweet, sour and bitter; Alusi says universal permission is also possible without obliging bees to eat each kind. Burhan says fronting kull stresses the class and avoids limiting it to dates and grapes at 16:67. Abu Hayyan reports Zamakhshari taking bee homes as hexagonal cells and the fruit as whichever bees desire. [16 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BURHAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 16:69/q02/p2 The intended fruits are blossoms, flowers, leaves or plant produce on which bees feed. — Damghani identifies thamarat as blossoms and flowers; Qurtubi says tree blossoms; Ibn Atiyya and Tabarsi say blossoms or different plants, and Abu Hayyan reports Ibn Atiyya. Alusi records fruit as tree or plant, but calls 'fruit' for tree an unfamiliar metaphor; a trusted observer saw bees carry flower leaves. Biqai reports direct eating of leaves and flowers. Kuranyolu distinguishes nectar and pollen, and notes bees pollinate crops. [12 notes: DAMGHANI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية); BIQAI-FULL(بعضهم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABRISI]
- 16:69/q02/p3 The fruit phrase can denote subtle honeyed particles or dew settling on plants, which bees gather. — Razi cites medical books for night dew in scattered or visible form, naming accumulated taranjabin on tamarisk, and favors this account from resemblance and observation; bees eat some and store some. Abu Hayyan and Alusi transmit it; Biqai likewise reports particles on leaves. Alusi disputes the inference from taranjabin and notes different medical temperaments of honey and taranjabin. [12 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); BIQAI-FULL(قالوا); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,بعض أجلة الأطباء); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)+; RAZI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 16:69/q02/p4 The bee is commanded to eat its allotted provision, an image of obedient acceptance. — Ibn Abbas reports the divine command; Sulami says food is God's provision. Muqatil has bees feed on paths through mountains and trees; Zajjaj, through Wahidi and Ibn Jawzi, stresses that God turns sour, bitter and unpalatable forage into honey. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); SULAMI; MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج)]

## 16:69/q03 What does min contribute to 'eat from all fruits'?  [مِن · grammar]
turns on: Whether bees eat part of plant produce or gather a substance originating on plants.
- 16:69/q03/p1 Min is partitive: bees take some from the named fruits or flowers. — Ibn Atiyya says bees eat only some flowers; Abu Hayyan calls this the natural sense, and Samin prefers it. Razi allows it; Alusi retains it even if kull is broad. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 16:69/q03/p2 Min marks origin or source rather than a part eaten from fruit. — Razi uses this in his preferred dew account; Abu Hayyan reports it. Samin calls the associated manna-like substance theory very remote. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); RAZI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 16:69/q04 What are the Lord's paths that the bee follows?  [سُبُلَ رَبِّكِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether subul denotes travel, the craft of honey-making, internal transformation, or routes to food.
- 16:69/q04/p1 They are the bee's travel routes, especially its reliably found routes back to the hive. — Ibn Abbas says bees seek pasture; Ibn Atiyya says paths of flight and travel created by God; Qurtubi says mountain and tree routes to food. Kashshaf, Razi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Bursevi, Biqai, Alusi, Tabatabai and Kuranyolu describe long foraging journeys and accurate return, sometimes when local food fails. Abu Hayyan prefers flight after eating because of the fa. Bursevi compares the quta bird. Ibn Abd al-Salam objects that air has no trodden path; Alusi questions that inference. [21 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عبد السلام)-]
- 16:69/q04/p2 They are inspired methods or courses by which the bee makes honey. — Kashshaf, Razi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Samin and Alusi report this account; Samin takes subul as the object of 'follow'. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 16:69/q04/p3 They are channels or processes through which food becomes honey inside the bee. — Kashshaf and Baydawi describe the body's channels. Samin makes subul adverbial, with the bee following what it ate; Alusi calls the image metaphorical, compares threading a needle, and says voluntarily entering the channels allows an imperative despite involuntary transformation. Ibn Abd al-Salam favors food passages because air is not a trodden path. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عبد السلام)]
- 16:69/q04/p4 They are routes to seek the fruits, with 'eat' understood as setting out for food. — Kashshaf and Razi report this ordering; Alusi makes eat mean seek food. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 16:69/q05 Why are they called 'your Lord's' paths?  [رَبِّكِ · rhetoric]
- 16:69/q05/p1 God creates, prepares and directs the bee along these paths. — Mawardi identifies the Lord as God; Ibn Atiyya attributes flight routes to God's creation; Bursevi, Biqai and Alusi say the possessive points to divine care enabling sure navigation; Ibn Ashur says the bee's ordered path is constrained by God, and Tabatabai relates it to inspiration. Asad notes the literal wording 'thy Sustainer's paths'. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 16:69/q06 What does dhululan describe?  [ذُلُلًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the circumstantial plural attaches to nearby 'paths' or to the commanded bees.
- 16:69/q06/p1 The paths are made easy, smooth and passable for the bee. — Mujahid says no terrain is rugged; Tabari says it is a circumstantial description of nearby subul and prefers it for proximity, while acknowledging both. Zajjaj via Wahidi and Ibn Jawzi, Wahidi, Ibn Kathir, Abu Hayyan and Samin favor or explain this; Kashshaf and Razi compare 67:15. Tabari supplies an understood 'for you'. The word is plural of dhalul according to Tabari, Akhfash and others. [33 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); FARRA; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو جعفر الطبري,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(unnamed view,مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج)+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; TAB-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 16:69/q06/p2 The bees themselves are docile, obedient or subjected to God and their keeper. — Qatada supplies obedience; Ibn Zayd says bees follow keepers like subdued livestock (36:72); Ibn Qutayba favors God's subjection of bees. Qurtubi makes it a circumstantial accusative of the bee; Samin of the subject of usluki. Kashshaf says unresisting compliance, and Wahidi Wajiz combines obedient foraging with honey's benefit. Alusi also reports Qatada's obedient-bee explanation. [31 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(unnamed view); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة، وعبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة)+]

## 16:69/q07 What form and basic sense is dhululan?  [ذُلُلًۭا · meaning]
- 16:69/q07/p1 It is the plural of dhalul, meaning subdued, yielding, tractable or gentle. — Akhfash, Ibn Qutayba, Tabari, Wahidi, Qurtubi, Samin and Ibn Ashur give the morphology; Wahidi compares a compliant horse, Damghani 76:14's subdued fruit clusters, and Ibn Ashur 2:71. [8 notes: AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; DAMGHANI; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 16:69/q08 How is 'follow the paths' construed grammatically?  [فَٱسْلُكِى سُبُلَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether suluk is intransitive passage along routes or transitive following of specified paths.
- 16:69/q08/p1 Subul is an adverbial route or an accusative after an omitted preposition with intransitive suluk. — Samin explains adverbial passage through pathways; Ibn Ashur says suluk means passing through and contrasts transitive 15:12, with subul accusative by removal of a preposition. Abu Hayyan notes the fa makes following immediate after eating. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 16:69/q08/p2 Subul is the direct object: paths shown to the bee for honey-making. — Samin explicitly makes the word a direct object; Alusi also describes transitive salk and an omitted object in the internal-process metaphor. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 16:69/q09 Why does the address to the bee become a third-person statement?  [يَخْرُجُ مِنۢ بُطُونِهَا · rhetoric]
- 16:69/q09/p1 The speech turns to people so they can see the beneficial result and reflect on God's governance. — Wahidi and Samin note the person shift, Samin observing that continued address would say 'your bellies'. Razi says the marvel becomes an argument for God's power, wisdom and governance; Qurtubi calls it a counted blessing; Baydawi says the bee serves people's benefit; Alusi says a new sentence addresses people. Biqai adds that the bee did not intend to make honey. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 16:69/q10 How is honey made from what bees gather?  [يَخْرُجُ مِنۢ بُطُونِهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether honey is transformed inside bees, gathered as ready-made sweet particles, or drawn from two sources.
- 16:69/q10/p1 Bees eat plant matter that God transforms into honey inside their bodies, then regurgitate or release it. — Zajjaj says bitter and sour food changes in the belly; Razi reports flowers and leaves eaten then vomited, though he prefers the dew account. Baydawi, Bursevi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi describe internal formation. Alusi cites the plain 'eat' and distinct medical qualities from taranjabin against the dew theory, plus Ali's description of regurgitation, al-Ma'arri and al-Hariri. Kuranyolu describes nectar in a separate honey stomach, transfer by a young bee and airing/sealing in combs; Ibn Ashur notes seasonal thickening by evaporation. Qurtubi stresses divine transformation of harmful forage into sweet healing. [25 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(بعض الناس); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(ظهير الفاريابي); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,بعض أجلة الأطباء,بعضهم,علي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(المحققون من الحكماء)+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 16:69/q10/p2 Bees collect pre-existing fine, sweet dew-like particles from plants and store them as honey. — Razi's medical-book account describes airborne particles falling on leaves, like taranjabin; some are eaten and some carried home. He prefers its resemblance and says bees eat honey. Baydawi and Abu Hayyan transmit it. Alusi gives the taranjabin Persian etymology but objects that observed feeding on honey proves little, the command says 'eat,' and honey and taranjabin differ medically. [14 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)+; RAZI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]
- 16:69/q10/p3 Bees may gather both flowers and dew, with one yielding honey and the other poison. — Alusi reports this mixed account, while noting that the proposed assignments of flower to honey and dew to poison could rationally be reversed. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 16:69/q11 Where does honey leave the bee?  [مِنۢ بُطُونِهَا · referent]
turns on: Whether observations and Ali's figurative language locate the outlet at the mouth, rear, or nowhere definite.
- 16:69/q11/p1 It leaves through the mouth, though formed or held within the bee. — Zajjaj compares human saliva. Most people according to Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi take the mouth view; Tabarsi agrees. Al-Hasan calls it bee saliva, and Abu Hayyan cites al-Hariri's honey-licking/wasp-vomit line. Alusi says some find mouth emergence a stronger display of power; Bursevi cites a Persian verse depicting regurgitation. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(جمهور الناس); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); BURSEVI(ظهير الفاريابي); ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 16:69/q11/p2 It exits from the bee's rear or lower body. — Ibn Atiyya says Ali's world-disparaging remark suggests this; Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan quote Ali's 'excretion' or 'residue' language, while Alusi says a minority takes it literally. Bursevi says this wording disparages its source despite honey's pleasant taste. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي); BURSEVI(علي رضي الله عنه); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم,علي)]
- 16:69/q11/p3 The verse does not settle the exit, so its precise location is left undecided. — Qurtubi says the exit is unknown and notes the warmth of bee breath; Alusi reports suspension of judgment. A glass-hive story about Aristotle, expanded to Solomon and Alexander, says bees coated the glass with clay so observers could not see. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الغزنوي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(آخرون)]

## 16:69/q12 Why does the verse say 'from their bellies'?  [بُطُونِهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'belly' is the digestive organ or can name an interior mouth cavity.
- 16:69/q12/p1 Belly means the internal organ where food is changed into honey. — Zajjaj says food is transformed there before honey leaves through the mouth; Qurtubi agrees. Razi's ordinary regurgitation account reads the phrase straightforwardly. Alusi prefers the ordinary organ and says the command to eat supports stomach involvement. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(المحققون من الحكماء)+]
- 16:69/q12/p2 Belly can include the mouth as an inner cavity, fitting a theory of collected dew. — Razi says any bodily cavity may be a belly, comparing brain cavities; Abu Hayyan and Alusi report the mouth-as-belly account. Baydawi similarly equates bellies and mouths on the particle-gathering view. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 16:69/q13 What drink emerges from the bees?  [شَرَابٌۭ · referent]
- 16:69/q13/p1 It is honey. — Muqatil, Yahya, Mawardi, Thalabi, Ibn Jawzi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Ibn Kathir, Durr, Bursevi, Tabatabai and Study Quran identify it. Damghani gives sharab as a word for honey; Muqatil and Yahya link it to bee feeding and the cure. [20 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي,مجاهد); BURSEVI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 16:69/q14 Why is honey called a drink?  [شَرَابٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 16:69/q14/p1 It is consumed as a liquid, alone or in other beverages, and the wording foregrounds its benefit. — Kashshaf and Baydawi say it is drunk; Razi adds honey beverages. Biqai calls it both noble food and drink; Alusi says Arabic says 'drink honey'. Ibn Ashur says it is swallowed without chewing, prepares the healing claim and recalls drink at 16:10; Kuranyolu remarks on the syrup term. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 16:69/q15 What explains honey's different colors?  [مُّخْتَلِفٌ أَلْوَٰنُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether color follows food, age, season, or direct divine variation.
- 16:69/q15/p1 Colors vary with the bees' food, flowers, grazing or plant sources. — Mawardi, Tabarsi, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, Bursevi, Nasafi and Alusi cite differing forage; Zaynab's remark about arfat and maghafir illustrates effects on scent. Reported colors include white, yellow, red, black, as well as solid and liquid varieties. [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(زينب); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); TABATABAI]
- 16:69/q15/p2 Colors vary with the bees' age or temperament. — Baydawi and Nasafi report age; Abu Hayyan adds temperament. Bursevi repeats life-stage variation. Alusi says proof is lacking but trusted observers saw age as the strongest apparent cause, favoring internally formed honey. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 16:69/q15/p3 Colors vary by season or by changes as floral moisture dries. — Baydawi and Alusi mention season; Ibn Ashur says thin comb honey thickens as floral moisture evaporates, white in spring and brown in summer. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 16:69/q15/p4 The differing colors are directly governed by a choosing Creator. — Razi lists red, white and yellow and compares 35:27's mountains; Biqai says the varied honey shows free divine choice. Alusi reports direct divine will among explanations, and Ibn Ashur compares 13:4's diverse produce from shared water. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 16:69/q15/p5 The verse simply identifies observable white, yellow, red and sometimes black varieties. — Ibn Abbas gives red, white and yellow through Wahidi; Kashshaf adds black. Muqatil, Baghawi, Ibn Jawzi, Qurtubi, Bursevi and Tabatabai likewise list varieties, without a single exclusive cause. [9 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 16:69/q16 How do the drink's emergence and healing function in the sentence?  [يَخْرُجُ شَرَابٌۭ فِيهِ شِفَآءٌۭ · grammar]
- 16:69/q16/p1 The new statement gives the result of the bee's inspired acts; its present tense depicts recurrent emergence. — Abu Hayyan relates it to nesting, eating and routes and to earlier milk and fruit drinks. Ibn Ashur calls it a new explanatory sentence answering why bees act thus, a blessing and lesson; yakhruj imperfect marks repeated production. Alusi notes omission of an explicit divine agent after God's inspiration already established causation. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 16:69/q17 What does 'in it is healing' refer to?  [فِيهِ شِفَآءٌۭ لِّلنَّاسِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the antecedent is the nearby honey or an unmentioned Quran, or the narrated signs themselves.
- 16:69/q17/p1 The pronoun refers to the drink, honey, whose medicinal benefit is in view. — Ibn Abbas, Qatada, Ibn Masud and many commentators support honey; Tabari prefers the nearby antecedent, as do Baghawi, Tabarsi, Razi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, Ibn Kathir and others. Razi and Ibn al-Arabi object that the Quran is not mentioned here; Tabarsi says the Quran antecedent is absent. Zajjaj answers harmful cases by water analogy. Reports of the brother cured by honey support this reading. [53 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); ZAJJAJ(قيل); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود وقتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أكثر المفسرين,ابن مسعود,الزجاج,السدي,الفراء والزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); TABRISI(الحسن,السدي,قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود وقتادة,العوفي عن ابن عباس وابن مسعود); RAZI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري,ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,جمهور الناس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن العربي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,السدي,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(القاضي أبو بكر بن العربي); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+; TAB-FULL+; TABRISI+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 16:69/q17/p2 The pronoun refers to the Quran, whose guidance heals hearts, doubts, ignorance or unbelief. — Mujahid gives the reading; Farra, Zajjaj, Tabarsi, Razi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi transmit it, with reported attributions to Ibn Abbas, Hasan, Dahhak and Ibn Kaysan; al-Nahhas approves. Zajjaj and Wahidi explain that the bee narrative proves oneness, clarifies lawful and unlawful matters and removes doubt; 10:57 and 17:82 speak of Quranic healing. Razi says this takes the honey story as ended. Ibn al-Arabi challenges both context and attribution; Ibn Kathir accepts Quranic healing generally but rejects it here. ## 16:69/q33 What inward lessons are drawn from the bee's food and honey?  [* · inward]
turns on: The inward notes apply different parts of the bee's work to distinct stages, conduct, and divine inspiration.
- 16:69/q33/p1 Honey heals souls, while the heart and spirit require deeper disciplines and witnessing. — Sulami distinguishes soul healing from heart nourishment through attention, humility and retreat, and spirit nourishment through witnessing and hearing God without created distractions. [1 notes: SULAMI]
- 16:69/q33/p2 The obedient believer becomes a source of healing to others like the bee. — Abu Bakr al-Warraq says guarding the inward secret and turning to God make sight, speech and company healing. Al-Tirmidhi, cited by Biqai, says surrendering preference despite sweet and bitter food makes the servant's speech heal sick hearts. Study Quran reports Sufi commentators similarly; Bursevi says reflective insight cures ignorance as honey cures outward illness. [5 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر الوراق); BIQAI-FULL(محمد بن علي الترمذي); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); STUDYQURAN(Sufi commentators)]
- 16:69/q33/p3 The bee's output images the separation of sincere deeds from ostentatious ones. — Ibn Ata says fire separates mixed bee output into healing honey and burning wax, as purified sincerity is saved while work mixed with ostentation or association is fit for fire; Bursevi also compares bee poison, honey and wax with fear and hope in believers. [2 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); BURSEVI(حياة الحيوان)]
- 16:69/q33/p4 The bee's retreat, purity and fruit eating map the spiritual traveler's stages. — Bursevi compares mountain retreat with the Prophet at Hira, and bees' cleanliness with avoidance of polluted food. Body fruits are righteous deeds; soul fruits discipline, heart fruits renunciation, secret fruits witnessing and nearness. The command parallels 23:51's wholesome food and righteous action. [3 notes: BURSEVI]
- 16:69/q33/p5 God's inspiration, rather than a creature's nature, orders animal actions and teaches surrender. — Bursevi cites Najmi that varied animal actions come from divine instruction, wisdom and eternal will. Al-Harali, cited by Biqai, says surrender to the Lord opens one to the unseen. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]
- 16:69/q33/p6 The Lord hides valued knowledge and love in imperfect people, as honey in bees. — Qushayri's examples include silk in worms, pearls in shells and gems in stone; Bursevi repeats the comparison and a verse warning against discounting the apparently insignificant. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(القشيري)]
- 16:69/q33/p7 Reflective thought has lasting sweetness like honey. — Bursevi's Persian verses say its sweetness reaches and remains in the soul; al-Harali maps the bee sign to the higher intellect and heart's core. [2 notes: BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]
