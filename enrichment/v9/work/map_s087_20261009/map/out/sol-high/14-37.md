# 14:37: 41 questions

## 14:37/q01 Why is Abraham's invocation repeated and phrased as 'Our Lord'?  [رَّبَّنَآ رَبَّنَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the plural points to these descendants or to earlier sons, and how the intervening address functions.
- 14:37/q01/p1 It renews humble supplication and draws attention to the request. — Abū Ḥayyān says repetition seeks an answer; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a new prayer; Bursevī and Iṣlāḥī stress repeated humble appeal. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 14:37/q01/p2 The plural includes the family for whom Abraham prays. — Bursevī and Ibn ʿĀshūr include the sons; Ālūsī prefers the descendants in this request over al-Baḥr's link to sons in 14:35, since 14:36 then lacks a plural. [3 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 14:37/q01/p3 The repeated address emphasizes prayer as the settlement's purpose. — Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī highlight prayer; al-Kashf infers an omitted verb after the vocative, which Ālūsī rejects. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 14:37/q01/p4 The plural follows the sons mentioned earlier. — Al-Baḥr links it to 14:35; Ālūsī objects by comparison with 14:36. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(البحر); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 14:37/q01/p5 Rabbi invokes a personally experienced favor; Rabbana a favor witnessed by all. — Iṣlāḥī distinguishes these forms of address. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 14:37/q02 How is the yāʾ of 'innī' read?  [إِنِّىٓ · readings]

- 14:37/q03/p1 It is making a dwelling for someone to live in when desired. — Jishumī and Biqāʿī give this lexical explanation. [2 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 14:37/q04 How does 'min dhurriyyatī' work grammatically?  [مِن ذُرِّيَّتِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether min apportions the offspring and licenses ellipsis, or only reinforces the phrase.
- 14:37/q04/p1 Min is partitive; an object such as people, a child, or a group is expressed or understood. — Al-Farrāʾ, Akhfash, Ṭabarī, Naḥḥās and others cite ellipsis and 7:50; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān note Isaac remained in Syria. Burhān offers substitution by baʿḍ. Ālūsī permits direct object or adjective of an omitted object. [22 notes: FARRA; AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الفراء); JISHUMI(أبو القاسم); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(البلخي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأخفش والفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي)+]
- 14:37/q04/p2 Min is emphatic or extra, so the sense is 'my descendants'. — Abū Bakr cites Dhū al-Rumma; Ibn al-Anbārī and others report it. Ālūsī rejects this as unsound. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 14:37/q04/p3 Dhurriyya denotes offspring collectively. — Jishumī relates its name to tiny particles or God's bringing creatures forth. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 14:37/q07 What occurred when Abraham settled Hagar and Ishmael?  [أَسْكَنتُ مِن ذُرِّيَّتِى بِوَادٍ · reports]
turns on: Narrative stages and the theological classification of Zamzam.
- 14:37/q07/p1 At God's command he left mother and child with dates and water; Hagar trusted God. — Ibn ʿAbbās's narrative gives the empty valley, her questioning, his assent and his prayer after leaving sight. Other narrations add Sarah's jealousy, concealment of tracks, a Buraq journey from Syria and later visits. Al-Qāḍī doubts abandonment without divine assurance; Qurtubī denies general license to do so. [27 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(غير مسمى); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(القاضي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL(رُوي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(سعد); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 14:37/q07/p2 Hagar searched seven times between Ṣafā and Marwa; Zamzam appeared and Jurhum settled. — Reports give the seven trips as origin of saʿy, angelic excavation, Hagar gathering water and Jurhum's arrival. Details include a bird circling above the water, milk for access and Hagar welcoming company. [18 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الملك); BAYDAWI-FULL(رُوي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 14:37/q07/p3 Abraham later visited Ishmael's wives and built the House with him. — A first wife complained and was divorced at Abraham's coded advice; the second praised meat and water, which he blessed. The Prophet says he would have blessed grain had they had it. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 14:37/q07/p4 Hagar's haste kept Zamzam from remaining a flowing spring. — The Prophet's statement appears through Ibn ʿAbbās, sometimes specifying flow until the Last Day. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(محمد); THALABI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 14:37/q07/p5 Zamzam's emergence was a foretoken for Ishmael or a miracle for Abraham. — Rāzī permits irhāṣ for Ishmael; the Muʿtazila classify it as Abraham's miracle. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 14:37/q10 What does 'without cultivation' imply about the valley?  [غَيْرِ ذِى زَرْعٍ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether infertility implies lack of water or deliberately leaves future water open.
- 14:37/q10/p1 It emphatically excludes cultivation and describes land unfit for crops. — Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Bursevī, Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī compare 39:28. Ibn ʿĀshūr says dhū conveys capacity for a quality; Abū Ḥayyān objects to Zamakhsharī's adverb qaṭṭ with a nonpast verb. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 14:37/q10/p2 Lack of crops implies that the valley then had no water. — Ṭabarī infers this from farming; Jishumī adds no livestock; Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī report the inference. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]
- 14:37/q10/p3 Abraham omitted 'without water' because he knew God would provide it. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān say God would not abandon them; Ālūsī adds a view that stored floodwater was possible and fruit was the concern. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية,بعضهم)]
- 14:37/q10/p4 Barrenness underscores reliance and freedom for worship. — Biqāʿī contrasts worldly crops with otherworldly benefit; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says comfort would distract; Iṣlāḥī sees leaving a fertile region. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 14:37/q11 What is meant by 'Your Sacred House' and why is it attributed to God?  [بَيْتِكَ ٱلْمُحَرَّمِ · referent]

- 14:37/q11/p1 The Kaaba or sanctuary, belonging to or honored by God. — Māwardī calls it the qibla; Ṭabrisī, Qurtubī and Jishumī say God alone owns it, Abū ʿAlī says the attribution commands honor, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī links ownership to exclusive worship. ## 14:37/q12 When did Abraham say 'at Your Sacred House' relative to its building?  [عِندَ بَيْتِكَ ٱلْمُحَرَّمِ · reports]
turns on: Whether this was a later prayer or the House had an earlier or anticipated existence.
- 14:37/q12/p1 Before construction, the future House was already known or decreed. — Ṭabarī, Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Jishumī and others explain the proleptic name by divine knowledge or disclosure. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 14:37/q12/p2 After construction, in a later prayer. — Abū Ḥayyān takes the House as present; Ibn Kathīr distinguishes an earlier prayer; Bursevī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī cite 14:39 and settled Mecca. Ālūsī's narrative gives an earlier mound. [11 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI+]
- 14:37/q12/p3 Before construction, an earlier House or site already existed. — Ṭabarī, Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī and others mention a pre-Flood House, its removal, destruction or mound; Bursevī says rebuildings are certain but counts differ. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب,ابن جرير); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد,الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-]

## 14:37/q13 Why is the House called 'al-muharram'?  [ٱلْمُحَرَّمِ · meaning]
turns on: The prohibition, protective status, honor, Flood history or primordial sanctity named by the adjective.
- 14:37/q13/p1 God prohibited violating it and made its precinct sacred. — Qatāda forbids disrespect; others mention fighting, hunting, blood and filth. ʿUmar says Ṭasm and Jurhum perished for violation; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites aggressors and the Elephant. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب,قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); JISHUMI(أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة وغيره); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب,قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 14:37/q13/p2 It has always been protected and feared by tyrants. — Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Nasafī and others describe its impregnable status; Jishumī adds no tyrant owns it. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; JISHUMI(القاضي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة وغيره); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 14:37/q13/p3 The Flood did not overtake it. — Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī and others compare al-ʿAtīq, freed from the Flood. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 14:37/q13/p4 Visitors must enter consecration and abstain from otherwise lawful acts. — Māwardī mentions sex; Jishumī and Ṭabrisī mention impurity and iḥrām; Ālūsī rejects this derivation. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو علي); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 14:37/q13/p5 It is greatly honored and revered. — Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Nasafī and Ṭabrisī give this sense. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 14:37/q13/p6 Its sacred status dates to creation. — Ibn ʿAbbās says seven angels surrounded it; Ibn al-Sāʾib dates sanctification to creation; Rāzī mentions Adam's heavenly counterpart. [3 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب); RAZI-FULL]

## 14:37/q14 How do the location phrases attach syntactically?  [بِوَادٍ عِندَ بَيْتِكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the House phrase modifies settling or the valley.
- 14:37/q14/p1 The valley phrase locates settlement, and 'at Your House' modifies 'I settled'. — Akhfash and al-Samīn gloss bi-wādin as 'in a valley'; Bursevī, Ālūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī make the House phrase adverbial. [5 notes: AKHFASH; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 14:37/q14/p2 'At Your House' describes or apposes 'valley'. — Abū al-Baqāʾ suggests adjective or partitive apposition; al-Samīn objects that ʿinda is noninflecting, and Ālūsī prefers adverbial attachment; Ibn ʿĀshūr allows a second adjective or circumstantial phrase. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 14:37/q15 What rank does prayer at the Sacred House have?  [عِندَ بَيْتِكَ ٱلْمُحَرَّمِ لِيُقِيمُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · law]
turns on: Competing preferences and prayer reports.
- 14:37/q15/p1 Prayer in the Sacred Mosque is superior to the Prophet's Mosque and other places. — Qurtubī cites Ibn al-Zubayr, Ibn ʿUmar and Jābir reports, including hundredfold and hundred thousandfold figures. Abū ʿUmar judges transmitters reliable. ʿUmar's sermon says more than a hundredfold. Companions and al-Shāfiʿī favor Mecca; Eid prayer there is held in the Sacred Mosque. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمر,أهل الأثر,ابن حبيب,ابن عمر,حكيم بن سيف,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عمر وعلي وابن مسعود وأبو الدرداء وجابر,مالك,مالك وسائر العلماء); DURR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمر)+]
- 14:37/q15/p2 Medina is preferred. — Qurtubī reports the well-known view of Mālik and Medinans; Basrans and Baghdadis varied. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مالك وأهل المدينة)]

## 14:37/q16 To what does 'so that they establish prayer' attach?  [لِيُقِيمُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · grammar]
turns on: Purpose lām versus imperative lām, and which action supplies its purpose.
- 14:37/q16/p1 It is a purpose clause attached to 'I settled'. — Zamakhsharī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ṭabrisī, Rāzī, Qurtubī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ālūsī and others hold this; Ṭabrisī gives a poetic parallel for the intervening vocative. Ālūsī derives exclusivity from barrenness and the House, not an omitted verb. [20 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 14:37/q16/p2 The lām is imperative, asking God to enable prayer. — Māwardī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurtubī, Bayḍāwī and Abū Ḥayyān report this; Ālūsī finds it less plausible. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 14:37/q16/p3 It attaches to the earlier request to keep descendants from idols. — Muqātil and Ibn al-Jawzī offer this; Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī call it remote. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الجوزي)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 14:37/q16/p4 It depends on an implied repeated verb of settling. — Al-Kashf argues the vocative blocks attachment to the explicit verb; Ālūsī says it is parenthetical. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 14:37/q16/p5 It attaches to the House's inviolability. — Ibn Kathīr reports Ibn Jarīr's explanation: it was made sacred so inhabitants could pray. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير)]

## 14:37/q17 What makes prayer the exclusive purpose of settlement?  [لِيُقِيمُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether exclusivity comes from the purpose lām, context, or an implied verb.
- 14:37/q17/p1 A purpose clause itself implies exclusivity. — Mālik argues from 16:8 that 'so that you may ride them' excludes eating the animals. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(مالك)]
- 14:37/q17/p2 The surrounding sequence implies it: no cultivation, a House for worship, then prayer. — Ālūsī prefers this contextual explanation and needs no omitted verb. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]
- 14:37/q17/p3 An implied repeated verb states settlement solely for prayer. — Al-Kashf infers an omitted 'I settled them in this settlement'; Ālūsī rejects it because the vocative is parenthetical. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 14:37/q18 What is meant by establishing prayer there?  [لِيُقِيمُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · interpretation]
turns on: Ritual prayer, wider worship, qibla practice or an inward practice.
- 14:37/q18/p1 They should pray and worship God at the House as its abiding purpose. — Ṭabarī speaks of obligations; Wāḥidī glosses worship; Zamakhsharī adds remembrance, residence, circumambulation, bowing and prostration. Ṭabrisī insists on proper conditions. Iṣlāḥī includes calling and arranging prayer. [18 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 14:37/q18/p2 Prayer is singled out for its superior rank and as the core of religion. — Qurtubī calls it God's covenant and cites five prescribed prayers; Abū Ḥayyān says best worship or source of good; Bursevī and Ālūsī call it a pillar. Study Quran lets prayer represent religion (42:13). [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 14:37/q18/p3 It includes praying toward the Kaaba as qibla. — Māwardī and Jishumī mention facing it; Ibn al-Jawzī reports this reading. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي)]
- 14:37/q18/p4 Its inward sense is intimate communion and unveiling. — Qāshānī's allegory treats prayer of the faculties thus. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 14:37/q19 Why is 'they establish' plural?  [لِيُقِيمُوا۟ · rhetoric]
- 14:37/q19/p1 It signals Ishmael's future descendants there. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, Ālūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī infer that God informed Abraham of the child's posterity. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البحر); TABATABAI]

## 14:37/q20 What does the fa before 'make' express?  [فَٱجْعَلْ · grammar]
- 14:37/q20/p1 It makes the request follow from the preceding circumstances. — Ālūsī says those circumstances were beginnings of the answer to Abraham's prayer. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 14:37/q21 What does 'afʾida' denote?  [أَفْـِٔدَةًۭ · meaning]
turns on: Whether it denotes hearts or delegations; the inward reading gives senses.
- 14:37/q21/p1 Hearts, the plural of fuʾād, including feeling or intellect. — Māwardī, Baghawī, Qurtubī, Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr give the plural; poetry cited by Māwardī, Ibn al-Jawzī and Qurtubī uses fuʾād for heart. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān relate it to burning; Ibn al-Anbārī to proximity to the core. [18 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(آخر,ابن الأنباري,امرؤ القيس); QURTUBI-FULL(الشاعر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(العامة); BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 14:37/q21/p2 Delegations or groups of people. — Māwardī and Qurtubī derive it from wafd; Muʾarrij attributes a group sense to Quraysh, adopted by Ibn Baḥr; Ālūsī notes the alternate syntax. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مؤرج); ALUSI-FULL(ابن بحر)]
- 14:37/q21/p3 Human senses supplying inner faculties. — Qāshānī's inward reading connects senses with bodily pleasure. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 14:37/q22 How is 'afʾida' read?  [أَفْـِٔدَةًۭ · readings]
turns on: Transmission of the added yāʾ and derivations of alternate forms.
- 14:37/q22/p1 Some routes add a yāʾ after hamza. — Ibn ʿĀmir/Hishām through al-Ḥulwānī is reported widely. Al-Ḥulwānī calls it received or Arabic lengthening; poets lengthen vowels. [14 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عامر بخلاف); BAYDAWI-FULL(هشام); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عمرو الداني,هشام); SAMIN-DURR(هشام عن ابن عامر); NASHR(الحافظ الداني,الحلواني,الحلواني عن هشام، والعباس بن الوليد عن أصحاب ابن عامر); ALUSI-FULL(الحافظ أبو عمرو الداني,قالوا,هشام); QIRAAT-ER(Lvw); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(هشام بن عمار)]
- 14:37/q22/p2 Āfida is a long-alif form from metathesis or a participle for approaching groups. — Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and Ālūsī offer these derivations. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قُرئ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 14:37/q22/p3 Afida without lengthening is reduced by hamza deletion or derives from afada. — The same grammarians discuss transfer, deletion and weak between-between softening. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قُرئ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الاولون وصاحب النشر)]

- 14:37/q22/p5 Ifāda is read by Zayd ibn ʿAlī. — Hamza may replace wāw in a word for visitors, or the form may mean useful people. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زيد بن علي); SAMIN-DURR(زيد بن علي); ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن علي)]
- 14:37/q22/p6 The usual afʾida has no added yāʾ. — Al-Samīn gives the plural pattern; Ibn al-Jazarī says most Hishām routes and all other readers omit yāʾ. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(العامة); NASHR(الداجوني وسائر أصحاب هشام)]
- 14:37/q22/p7 The added yāʾ is a mistaken description of hamza softening. — Some suggest a transmitter described easing as an added letter; al-Dānī and Ibn al-Jazarī reject this because transmitters knew the forms and this hamza eases by transfer. ## 14:37/q35 What does 'so that they may give thanks' entail?  [لَعَلَّهُمْ يَشْكُرُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Material gratitude and worship versus inward use of knowledge.
- 14:37/q35/p1 Thank God for provision through worship, prayer and acknowledgment of His oneness. — Māwardī glosses 'thank You'; Wāḥidī says affirm oneness and exalt Him; Bursevī and Ālūsī specify rites. Ibn ʿĀshūr says gratitude completes the plea and removes distracting hardship. [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 14:37/q35/p2 Use knowledge and perception to pursue perfection. — Qāshānī's inward gloss. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 14:37/q36 Why does security precede these requests in Abraham's prayer?  [* · interpretation]
- 14:37/q36/p1 Security is the greatest blessing and makes religious and worldly life possible. — Rāzī reads the request order this way; a scholar compares a sheep that can recover from injury with one too afraid of a wolf to graze. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعض العلماء)]

## 14:37/q37 What does the verse teach about reliance and worship?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The degree of reliance and its relation to material means.
- 14:37/q37/p1 Abraham entrusted his family to God so they could worship despite deprivation. — Qushayrī stresses reliance over comforts; Jishumī says God turned hearts to sustain them; Rāzī makes provision serve obligations. Iṣlāḥī says worship can require sacrifice; Qurtubī limits the story to Abraham's divine command. [15 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال); JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 14:37/q37/p2 The barren valley symbolizes leaving created supports for God. — Sulamī's Ibn ʿAṭāʾ and others describe freedom from convention and material causes; Qushayrī says inner selves attach to no worldly other. [4 notes: SULAMI(آخر,ابن عطاء,بعضهم); QUSHAYRI(ويقال)]
- 14:37/q37/p3 The family is honored insofar as God cares for them. — Qushayrī says their honor and weakness depend on God's decree. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 14:37/q38 How does the prayer connect with other verses and the sūrah?  [* · links]
turns on: Parallel prayers and the sūrah's contrast of gratitude with ingratitude.
- 14:37/q38/p1 It parallels 2:126 and God's answer in 28:57. — Wāḥidī cites the parallel request; Burhān contrasts baladan and al-balad syntax; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the city is unnamed for suspense or familiarity. Iṣlāḥī and Kuran Yolu connect security and produce to the prayer. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; THALABI]
- 14:37/q38/p2 It confronts Quraysh's idolatry and ingratitude through their ancestor. — Abū Ḥayyān contrasts Abraham's faith with Quraysh's idols; Ālūsī sees an aside inviting Meccan rejecters. Biqāʿī and Iṣlāḥī say Quraysh misattributed answered provision to idols. [11 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(إرشاد العقل السليم); BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 14:37/q38/p3 The Abraham episode includes security, protection from idols, prosperity and forgiveness. — Study Quran summarizes the sūrah and God's control of guidance; Ṭabāṭabāʾī recalls 9:114 on Abraham's father's forgiveness. [4 notes: STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI]
- 14:37/q38/p4 The House's first role was prayer before pilgrimage and sacrifice. — Iṣlāḥī describes this development. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 14:37/q39 What analogies about Arabic forms appear with the verse's grammatical notes?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Different general form rules cited in associated notes.
- 14:37/q39/p1 Deleted radicals can reappear in nisba forms. — Sībawayh gives dam, yad, ghad, thab, shaf and ḥar, with poetry and plurals; doubled forms avoid an awkward geminate. [4 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 14:37/q39/p2 A stopped hāʾ can transfer its vowel to a preceding consonant. — Sībawayh gives poetry and Banū ʿAdī's kasra examples, which do not mark case. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH(بعض بني تميم من بني عدي)]
- 14:37/q39/p3 Nominal patterns differ in declension and diptosis by alteration, definiteness and diminutive. — Sībawayh surveys ḥuṭam, distributives, ʿUmar, Zufar, Zuhal, Jamʿ, Kutaʿ, Akhar, Qāl/Qīl and reduced names, citing 35:1 and poetry. [16 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 14:37/q39/p4 A quiescent wāw after kasra may become yāʾ, with morphological exceptions. — Sībawayh contrasts mīzān/miʿād with mawʿid/mawqif and discusses vocalized wāw, nouns, verbs, masdars and feminine compensation. [6 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 14:37/q39/p5 A disputed temporal particle usage lacks attestation in Arabic. — Al-Ṣaffār says Arabic uses madh or mundhu instead. [1 notes: BURHAN(الصفار)]

## 14:37/q40 What wider recitation issues appear with this verse's reading notes?  [* · readings]
turns on: Broader recitation debates included in the source excerpts.
- 14:37/q40/p1 An anomalous reading is not necessarily forbidden or outside the seven aḥruf. — Ibn al-Jazarī favors the majority and argues prohibition would condemn Companions and undermine prayers; theologians require certainty of rejection, while a middle view distinguishes obligatory Fātiḥa. Ibn Daqīq al-ʿĪd notes aggregate transmission. [6 notes: NASHR(أبو الفتح محمد بن علي بن دقيق العيد,بعض أهل الكلام,بعضهم); NASHR(الجمهور)+]
- 14:37/q40/p2 The basmala's verse status follows a reading's sūrah separation practice. — Ibn al-Jazarī reconciles competing claims thus. [1 notes: NASHR]
- 14:37/q40/p3 At pause after a prior kasra, rāʾ may remain light or become heavy. — Ibn al-Jazarī prefers heavy under sukūn but finds Egyptian routes favor light for Warsh; Makkī, al-Ḥuṣrī and others give analogical or transmitted grounds, including incidental and transferred kasra. [9 notes: NASHR(أبو الحسن الحصري,أبو عبد الله بن شريح وأبو علي بن بليمة,قوم عن ورش وابن بليمة,مكي); NASHR+]

## 14:37/q41 What broader claim about Qur'anic knowledge appears in associated notes?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The reach of Qur'anic knowledge and derivation of Sunnah.
- 14:37/q41/p1 The Qur'an contains foundations or guidance for all religious knowledge and rulings. — Al-Suyūṭī cites 6:38 and 16:89, reports from Ibn Masʿūd, al-Ḥasan and al-Shāfiʿī, and the command to follow the Prophet. Ibn Barjān and Ibn al-Faḍl extend the claim to near and remote roots; the wasp example and ʿUmar's ruling illustrate derivation. [17 notes: ITQAN(ابن الفضل المرسي,ابن برجان,ابن مسعود,الحسن,الشافعي,النبي,سعيد بن جبير,عمر بن الخطاب)]
- 14:37/q41/p2 The Prophet's statements and judgments have Qur'anic confirmation. — Al-Shāfiʿī, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and Ibn Masʿūd say Sunnah and hadith find grounds in scripture; al-Suyūṭī cites al-Umm and a report on following Abū Bakr and ʿUmar. [8 notes: ITQAN(ابن مسعود,الشافعي,النبي,سعيد بن جبير)]
