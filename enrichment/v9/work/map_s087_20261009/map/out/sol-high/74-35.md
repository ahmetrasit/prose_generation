# 74:35: 13 questions

## 74:35/q01 What does the pronoun in إنها refer to?  [إِنَّهَا · referent]
turns on: Whether an antecedent is the named Saqar, a warning or afterlife event, the Quran or verse under attack, or an implicit matter; the Resurrection proposal faces unstated-antecedent and gender objections.
- 74:35/q01/p1 Saqar, Hell, or the Fire. — Al-Farra, al-Wahidi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Razi, al-Alusi and Ibn Ashur connect the pronoun to the previously named Saqar (Ibn Ashur cites 74:26); Qatada, Mujahid, Abu Razin, Ibn Zayd, al-Dahhak and Ibn Abbas identify it as Fire or Hell. Abu Hayyan prefers this as the apparent antecedent; al-Tabataba'i reports it as the usual view. Ibn al-Jawzi transmits Hasan's remark that nothing warned of is frailer than Saqar. [37 notes: FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(أبو رزين,ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة,مجاهد); NAHHAS(أبو رزين); FARISI-HUJJA(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة وأبو رزين وغيره); TABRISI(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة والضحاك); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري,في البحر); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 74:35/q01/p2 The warning. — Ibn Atiyya proposes this alongside the affair of the afterlife; Abu Hayyan, al-Samin and al-Alusi also report it as an alternative antecedent. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(في البحر)]
- 74:35/q01/p3 The affair of the afterlife. — Ibn Atiyya compares 38:67; Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi report this alternative. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(في البحر)]
- 74:35/q01/p4 The Resurrection or rising of the Hour. — Al-Mawardi calls it one of the great calamities and punishments; al-Qurtubi transmits this from Ibn Abbas. Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi note that the antecedent is unstated; al-Samin adds an objection about gender. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(في البحر)]
- 74:35/q01/p5 The denial of Muhammad's prophethood, a grave sin. — Al-Mawardi and al-Qurtubi report Ibn Abbas; Study Quran reports the same alternative through Q and Ṭ and again through Q. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Q,Q, Ṭ)]
- 74:35/q01/p6 The Quran, one of God's greatest signs and a universal warning. — Al-Tabataba'i prefers this because the rebuke answers the accusation that the Quran is magic or human speech; its warning releases followers from deeds binding them to Saqar. Al-Tabrisi offers Quranic warnings as the alternative to Saqar. [3 notes: TABRISI(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة); TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 74:35/q01/p7 This verse, one of the great signs. — Al-Mawardi reports Ibn Isa's proposal. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى)]
- 74:35/q01/p8 A pronoun of the matter, with no specific antecedent. — Al-Samin lists this grammatical possibility. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 74:35/q02 How is the hamza of لإحدى read and explained?  [لَإِحْدَى · readings]
turns on: The transmitted pronunciation and whether ordinary hamza easing licenses complete deletion after the attached lam.
- 74:35/q02/p1 The hamza is fully pronounced and retained in connected speech. — Al-Farisi reports Qunbul from Ibn Kathir and Abu Amr with hamza; Ibn Atiyya, al-Qurtubi and al-Samin call this the common reading. Al-Wahidi and al-Razi call its alif hamzat qatʿ, retained in connection. [6 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(قنبل عن ابن كثير); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير ونصر بن عاصم); QURTUBI-FULL(جرير بن حازم عن ابن كثير); RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); SAMIN-DURR(نصر بن عاصم، ابن محيصن)]
- 74:35/q02/p2 The hamza is omitted: la-hda. — A report from Ibn Kathir is given by al-Farisi, al-Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, al-Tabrisi, al-Qurtubi and al-Razi; Ibn Atiyya and al-Samin add Nasr ibn Asim; Abu Hayyan, al-Samin and al-Alusi add Ibn Muhaysin, and Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi name Wahb ibn Jarir's report from Ibn Kathir. Al-Wahidi cites Abu al-Aswad, Ahmad ibn Yahya and al-Farazdaq's poetry; al-Farisi cites al-Farazdaq's إثم and 6:74, 14:35, 43:26, and 22:58. Ibn Atiyya and al-Tabrisi cite Abu al-Aswad and Thaʿlab; al-Tabrisi gives Abu al-Aswad's address to Ziyad, 'father of al-Mughira,' and al-Farazdaq's line on Atiyya's sin. Al-Farisi and al-Wahidi object that deletion is non-analogical and leaves an initial consonant cluster, but the attached lam makes deletion word-internal; al-Wahidi compares lahu and ʿuḍudan. Al-Samin proposes hamza to alif followed by loss of alif when two vowelless letters meet. Al-Alusi calls the deletion light but irregular; al-Samin says it cannot found a productive analogy. Al-Razi compares waylamma. [19 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(قنبل عن ابن كثير); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن كثير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو الأسود الدؤلي وثعلب,أبو علي,ابن كثير ونصر بن عاصم); TABRISI(أبو الأسود لزياد,الفرزدق,بعضهم عن ابن كثير); QURTUBI-FULL(جرير بن حازم عن ابن كثير); RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(نصر بن عاصم وابن محيصن ووهب بن جرير عن ابن كثير); SAMIN-DURR(نصر بن عاصم، ابن محيصن); ALUSI-FULL(نصر بن عاصم وابن محيصن ووهب بن جرير عن ابن كثير)]
- 74:35/q02/p3 The hamza is eased to an intermediate sound between hamza and vowel. — Al-Farisi, al-Wahidi, Abu Ali as reported by Ibn Atiyya, al-Tabrisi, al-Razi, Abu Hayyan and al-Samin say this is the regular analogical easing, in contrast to complete omission. [7 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو علي); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(نصر بن عاصم وابن محيصن ووهب بن جرير عن ابن كثير); SAMIN-DURR]

## 74:35/q03 How is the feminine form إحدى built?  [إِحْدَى · grammar]
- 74:35/q03/p1 It is an original feminine formation rather than a form derived from a masculine base. — Al-Wahidi compares ʿasa and ukhra. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 74:35/q04 What force does calling it إحدى الكبر have?  [لَإِحْدَى ٱلْكُبَرِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether 'one of' counts an item among many great calamities or is an idiom for singular prominence.
- 74:35/q04/p1 It is one among many great calamities, with more tribulations implied. — Al-Alusi reports al-Zamakhshari's reading and prefers it as fitting the context and hinting at further tribulations. Study Quran reports an inference that such calamities will be endless. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); STUDYQURAN(Āl); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 74:35/q04/p2 It is uniquely prominent among the greatest, unequalled in magnitude. — Ibn Ashur says the sentence works like a proverb, comparing an outstanding man among notable men and إحدى حظيات لقمان, and refers to 74:11. Al-Nasafi and Abu Hayyan stress that its greatness is without equal; Bursevi gives this nuance if the expression is understood as definite. Al-Alusi reports a reading of unique greatness alongside al-Zamakhshari's alternative. [6 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:35/q05 What is the singular and formation of الكبر?  [ٱلْكُبَرِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the attested plural is formed from kubrā or kabīra; three later authors reject or suspect a copying error in the latter attribution.
- 74:35/q05/p1 It is the plural of كبرى, a feminine form. — Ibn Qutayba compares ūlā/awwal and sughrā/sughr; al-Wahidi compares sughrā/sughar; al-Jishumi adds ukhrā/ukhar; Ibn al-Jawzi compares ṣighar/ṣughrā; al-Qurtubi adds ʿuẓmā/ʿuẓam and says the feminine is built directly, not from a masculine base. Al-Kashshaf, reported by al-Razi, treats the feminine ending like tāʾ marbūṭa and compares plurals for dust and a burrowing animal. Al-Baydawi says the plural follows an analogy to its verb; Abu Hayyan says the feminine alif drops. Ibn Ashur calls it an irregular broken plural that treats kubrā as a noun for a calamity rather than an adjective, unlike ʿuẓmā. Al-Tabrisi, al-Nahhas and others gloss the plural as great matters. [16 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; NAHHAS(أبو رزين); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:35/q05/p2 It is the plural of كبيرة. — Ibn Atiyya states this. Abu Hayyan and al-Samin suspect a copyist's error; al-Alusi calls the claim mistaken. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية)-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 74:35/q06 What are the الكبر counted here?  [ٱلْكُبَرِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the plural names calamities or punishments, Hell's seven divisions, Hell itself, or divine signs; the proposed antecedent and surrounding dispute affect the choice.
- 74:35/q06/p1 Great calamities, disasters, horrors, or severe punishments. — Ibn Qutayba and al-Mubarrad gloss the expression as one of the disasters; al-Qurtubi cites a rajaz line for kubar as a major calamity, Abu Hayyan cites another rajaz line for devastating misfortune, and al-Samin cites Ibn al-Muʿalla's verse. Al-Wahidi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, al-Samin and others apply this sense to Hell. Al-Farra's analysis connects the next verse's nadhīran to 'one of the great calamities.' [20 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد وابن قتيبة); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الراجز); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ)]
- 74:35/q06/p2 The seven gates or levels of Hell, with Saqar one of them. — Muqatil, al-Kalbi and Ibn al-Sa'ib are transmitted as listing Jahannam, Laza, Hutama, Saʿir, Saqar, Jahim and Hawiya; al-Biqa'i repeats the seven names. Al-Razi and al-Alusi report the interpretation. Al-Alusi says the seven-level account is unpersuasive. [6 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل والكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل والكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب ومقاتل); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(صاحب التيسير)-]
- 74:35/q06/p3 A name of Hell. — Al-Qurtubi reports Muqatil's use of al-kubar as one of Hell's names. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل)]
- 74:35/q06/p4 Great divine signs, including the Quranic warning or Saqar as proof of power. — Al-Mawardi reports Ibn Isa's verse-as-sign interpretation; al-Tabrisi offers the Quran's warnings as signs; al-Tabataba'i calls the Quran one of God's greatest signs. Bursevi says Saqar is a great sign if the objection concerns signs, or proof that God subdues sinners from Adam to the Resurrection with only a few guards if it concerns their number. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); TABRISI(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة); BURSEVI; TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]

## 74:35/q07 What grammatical constraint governs the feminine superlative in الكبر?  [ٱلْكُبَرِ · grammar]
- 74:35/q07/p1 A feminine فعلى superlative is used with من, in a construct, or with the definite article. — Al-Burhan compares its restriction with that of afʿal and cites definite feminine forms; al-Nahhas says al-kubar retains the article and so is diptote. [2 notes: BURHAN; NAHHAS(أبو رزين)]

## 74:35/q08 How does this clause relate to the oath and the preceding كلا?  [* · links]
turns on: Where the oath's answer is placed and whether كلا begins a rebuke explained by the clause; al-Samin objects that the parenthetical analysis strains the apparent syntax.
- 74:35/q08/p1 The clause answers the oath by the moon, night, and dawn. — Al-Tabrisi identifies Saqar's greatness as the oath's answer; al-Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Ibn Ashur take it as the apparent or straightforward syntax; al-Razi, al-Baydawi, al-Alusi and al-Tabataba'i report it. Ibn Ashur says the oath begins a new statement about Saqar, citing 74:26 and 74:31. Islahi says the Quranic warning verses complete the oaths by portraying Hell as a grave upheaval rather than a jest. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:35/q08/p2 It explains the rebuke كلا; the intervening oath emphasizes the rebuke or has an implicit answer. — Al-Razi, al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin and al-Tabataba'i report this analysis, associated with al-Zamakhshari; al-Samin rejects it as forced and contrary to apparent syntax. Al-Alusi says kalla rebukes a denier and the oath is parenthetical or has an implied answer. Ibn Ashur says the oath can conclude rejection of the claim about Hell's guards (74:26), with this clause explaining that rejection. Al-Tabataba'i notes another possible function for kalla beyond two readings without specifying it in this note. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)-]
- 74:35/q08/p3 The earlier كلا rejects the preceding claim about the parable; intervening material is parenthetical and the oath starts a new statement. — Ibn Ashur says this permits a pause after kalla. He describes kalla as a rebuke and rejection of a claim, citing 26:61–62 and 19:77. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:35/q09 How is the following نذيرا للبشر connected grammatically to this clause?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Whether the following warning is an accusative associated with this clause or a nominative singular in Ubayy's reading.
- 74:35/q09/p1 نذيرا is accusative, linked to 'one of the great calamities' as an indefinite after a definite expression or as a warning verbal noun. — Al-Farra prefers this attachment and offers the two grammatical analyses. [1 notes: FARRA+]
- 74:35/q09/p2 نذيرٌ للبشر is nominative singular, 'a warner to humankind.' — Al-Farra reports Ubayy's reading and says both readings are sound. [1 notes: FARRA(أبي)]

## 74:35/q10 What do the nearby warning and oath signs teach?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the note explains the Quranic warning's aim or the imagery and emphatic force of the three oaths.
- 74:35/q10/p1 The Quran warns humanity of Hell and the Hereafter so people may prepare and escape its consequences. — Al-Tabataba'i says the Quran clears the accusation against it and frees followers from deeds tying them to Saqar. Islahi says natural manifestations argue for the Hereafter and expose heedlessness; those evading the Quran's warning retreat into stubbornness. His other note says Hell's warning is a grave upheaval, not a jest, and mockers are unfortunate. Kuran Yolu says the oaths draw attention to divine signs and the warning's importance. [4 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 74:35/q10/p2 The moon, departing night and brightening dawn point to divine power and the passage from darkness or disbelief to light and guidance. — Ibn Ashur says three oath clauses give conventional threefold emphasis and invoke a great created object and two signs of God's power. Their light in darkness evokes guidance, misguidance and Quranic light. Night departs as dawn brightens; the adverbs إذ and إذا are circumstantial, and the sequence recalls Islam removing disbelief, as at 14:1. Kuran Yolu likewise sees divine signs in the oaths. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 74:35/q11 What do the two readings of the preceding night-oath say?  [* · readings]
turns on: The time adverb and verb form for the night’s departure; Ibn Ashur regards both as sound.
- 74:35/q11/p1 إذ أدبر uses إذ with a past-tense form, swearing by the recurring moment when night has departed. — Ibn Ashur attributes it to Nafi, Hamza, Hafs, Yaqub and Khalaf. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وحمزة وحفص ويعقوب وخلف)]
- 74:35/q11/p2 إذا دبر uses the future-time adverb and a plain verb meaning departed, parallel to إذا أسفر. — Ibn Ashur attributes it to Ibn Kathir, Ibn Amir, Abu Amr, Abu Bakr from Asim, al-Kisa'i and Abu Ja'far; he says both readings are sound. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن كثير وابن عامر وأبو عمرو وأبو بكر عن عاصم والكسائي وأبو جعفر)]

## 74:35/q12 How do inward readings understand the great warning?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the spiritual danger is renewed injustice and darkness, unreflective conformity, or descent into nature and bodily desire.
- 74:35/q12/p1 It warns against a return to injustice or inward darkness after illumination. — Al-Sulami says warning to humanity confirms the matter for those granted direct illumination; al-Qushayri fears darkness returning to hearts after reality is disclosed. [2 notes: SULAMI; QUSHAYRI]
- 74:35/q12/p2 Prophetic warning returns those absorbed in conformity to spiritual witnessing, though conformity has no significance at direct vision. — Al-Sulami reports al-Wasiti: lights appear to the spirits of the unifiers, and warning calls them back to their witness. [1 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي)]
- 74:35/q12/p3 Saqar signifies nature or the lower self's hell; its warning benefits receptive souls who can rise toward heart and spirit. — Al-Qashani contrasts receptive people who attain virtues with others descending into bodily desire. Bursevi connects the hell of the lower self to the preceding moon, night and dawn imagery. [2 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 74:35/q13 What other rhetorical effects are noted around this verse?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes identify separate rhetorical devices in the surrounding discussion: emphasis, paronomasia, and added nuance from arrangement.
- 74:35/q13/p1 Repetition lends emphasis. — Abu Ubayda illustrates this with repeated 'I saw,' awlā laka, three plus seven restated as ten, and the reiterated curse of Abu Lahab. [1 notes: MAJAZ]
- 74:35/q13/p2 The two occurrences of البشر form complete paronomasia. — Ibn Ashur compares the occurrence in the passage with لواحة للبشر at 74:29. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:35/q13/p3 Rhetorical merit comes from a phrase's arrangement and the extra nuance it conveys. — Al-Jurjani compares كأن زيدا الأسد with زيد كالأسد: the former makes Zayd's bravery seem indistinguishable from a lion's. [1 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
