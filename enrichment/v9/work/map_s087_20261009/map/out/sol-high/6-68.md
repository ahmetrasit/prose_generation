# 6:68: 35 questions

## 6:68/q01 Who is addressed by the command?  [وَإِذَا رَأَيْتَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular address is confined to Muhammad, includes believers by shared cause, or addresses them through him.
- 6:68/q01/p1 The Prophet and all believers are included. — Ibn ʿAtiyya, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Kuranyolu cite the shared danger of hearing mockery. Ibn Kathir applies it to every community member; Islahi cites 6:69 and 4:140. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 6:68/q01/p2 The Prophet is addressed formally, but believers are intended. — Biqaʿi says addressing him deters others. Tabatabaʾi says prophetic impeccability excludes his forgetting a divine rule; 4:140 repeats the rule for believers. Some compare 39:65; Ibn al-ʿArabi rejects that analogy because prophetic forgetting is possible. [8 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين); IBNASHUR-FULL(بعض العلماء); TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)-; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو بكر بن العربي)-]
- 6:68/q01/p3 The Prophet alone is intended. — Ibn Jarir explains that his visible departure rebukes and troubles the polytheists; believers' departure would not. [6 notes: MUQATIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جرير); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(بعض المفسرين); TABRISI]
- 6:68/q01/p4 Any listener is directly addressed. — Razi and Abu Hayyan transmit this alternative; Kuranyolu records Muslims other than the Prophet as addressees. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 6:68/q02 What does 'see' mean and how is its clause construed?  [رَأَيْتَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether seeing is physical perception or knowledge, and whether the active verb already supplies the circumstantial state.
- 6:68/q02/p1 It means physically seeing them engaged in mockery. — Abu Hayyan treats it as a one-object verb with an omitted circumstantial state. Samin says yakhudun is already circumstantial, so another state is redundant; Ibn Ashur describes seeing active polemic. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 6:68/q02/p2 It means hearing their talk. — Muqatil addresses this explanation to Muhammad. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 6:68/q02/p3 It could mean mental knowledge, requiring an omitted second object. — Abu Hayyan calls this remote, citing grammarians who forbid or rarely allow such omission; Samin records the Sheikh's objection and stricter grammatical opinions. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 6:68/q02/p4 A separate omitted circumstantial state must be supplied. — The Sheikh reads seeing them while engaged; Samin rejects the extra ellipsis because yakhudun or the active-participle force of the relative clause suffices. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); SAMIN-DURR-]

## 6:68/q03 How do the temporal clauses and responses work?  [وَإِذَا رَأَيْتَ وَإِمَّا · grammar]
turns on: Whether emphatic nun is obligatory with conditional imma, and whether idha itself makes the rule iterative.
- 6:68/q03/p1 Imma is conditional in plus added ma and normally takes emphatic nun, though omission occurs. — Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi and Samin cite a poetic line beginning imma yusibka ʿadu; Bursevi explains assimilation of in's nun; Tabatabaʾi calls ma emphatic or mitigating; Alusi cites Ibn Durayd. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن دريد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 6:68/q03/p2 The first time clause is governed by 'turn away'; imperative responses have general force. — Samin and Bursevi assign idha accusative force through fa-aʿrid; Burhan illustrates generality with two idha examples. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; BURHAN]
- 6:68/q03/p3 Emphatic nun is strictly required after this conditional. — Samin attributes this to al-Zajjaj and rejects it on poetic and grammatical grounds; no one requires specifically the heavy form. [0 notes: SAMIN-DURR-]
- 6:68/q03/p4 Idha itself makes the ban recur whenever mockery resumes. — Some scholars infer this; Alusi says recurrence instead follows from tying the rule to its occasion. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض العلماء)-]

## 6:68/q04 Why is 'when' used first and 'if' for forgetting?  [وَإِذَا رَأَيْتَ وَإِمَّا · rhetoric]
- 6:68/q04/p1 Mockery is expected, whereas forgetting is merely possible. — Abu Hayyan, Samin and Alusi contrast idha with in; Samin adds prophetic protection. Bursevi says the wording does not establish an actual lapse. Biqaʿi and Ibn Ashur see emphasis on the order. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:68/q05 Who are those engaging in the signs?  [ٱلَّذِينَ يَخُوضُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the historical mockers were polytheists, People of the Book, or sectarian disputants, and whether the description extends by conduct.
- 6:68/q05/p1 They are Meccan polytheists, especially Quraysh. — Tabari, Suddi, Kashshaf, Nasafi, Alusi and Elmalili identify them; Ibn Ashur limits withdrawal to fierce public attackers, since shunning every denier would halt preaching. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(أبو مالك، السدي,السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,ابن عباس وسعيد بن جبير والسدي ومقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو مالك، سعيد بن جبير,ابن جريج,السدي,مقاتل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:68/q05/p2 They are sectarian innovators or people of disputation. — Muhammad or Ibn Sirin associates revelation with people of passions; Abu Jaʿfar and Muhammad ibn ʿAli apply it to disputants. Tabatabaʾi says reports generalize the underlying cause. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو جعفر); IBNABIHATIM(محمد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو جعفر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جعفر محمد بن علي); DURR-FULL(أبو جعفر,محمد بن سيرين,محمد بن علي); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر,محمد بن على); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 6:68/q05/p3 They are People of the Book or Jews speaking falsely about the Qur'an. — Mujahid and later transmitters specify People of the Book; Ibn Jawzi specifies Jews. Alusi says their habit resembles the polytheists'. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 6:68/q05/p4 The description applies to anyone who mocks signs, whatever their affiliation. — Ibn Khuwayz Mindad includes believer and unbeliever; Tabatabaʾi extends the rule by rationale. Some reports link it with believers' divisive debate in 6:65. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن خويز منداد); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(some reports)]
- 6:68/q05/p5 In an inward reading, they project the self's knowledge and power onto divine signs. — Qashani calls them veiled associators, whose companionship risks veiling. [4 notes: QASHANI]

## 6:68/q06 What are the signs at issue?  [ءَايَٰتِنَا · referent]
- 6:68/q06/p1 They are the Qur'an and its revelations. — Muqatil ibn Hayyan, Ibn Jawzi and Bursevi state this; Tabari adds insulting the Messenger. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 6:68/q06/p2 They are God's religion or signs more generally. — Hasan and Saʿid describe denial of God's signs and religion; Tabatabaʾi speaks of God's true signs. [2 notes: TABRISI(الحسن وسعيد بن جبير); TABATABAI]

## 6:68/q07 What does khawd mean in this verse?  [يَخُوضُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether it is disparaging talk, denial, or theological debate.
- 6:68/q07/p1 It is mocking, denying, insulting, or falsely disputing revelation. — Mujahid stresses mockery, Suddi denial, Tabari both. Ibn Abbas includes denial of the Prophet; Razi cites 23:110. Raghib, Islahi and Study Quran cite Quranic parallels including 9:65, 4:140 and 6:91. [46 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(أبو مالك، سعيد بن جبير,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو مالك، سعيد بن جبير,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الطبرسي); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الراغب); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB]
- 6:68/q07/p2 It includes contentious religious debate. — Abu Jaʿfar and Ibn Abbas warn against disputants and division; a Tabatabaʾi report adds storytellers. Literalists and Hashwiyya ban theological debate, which Razi, Alusi, Elmalili, Kuranyolu and Study Quran reject as confusing mockery with sincere inquiry. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو جعفر,ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,محمد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو جعفر); RAZI-FULL(الحشوية); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جعفر محمد بن علي); DURR-FULL(أبو جعفر,ابن عباس,محمد بن سيرين,محمد بن علي); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر,محمد بن على); STUDYQURAN(some literalists,some reports); RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(الحشوية)-; ELMALILI(Haşviyye)-; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)-]
- 6:68/q07/p3 It is confused, playful, excessive speech without insight. — Tabrisi cites Hasan and Saʿid; Biqaʿi likens blind desire to wading. Elmalili and Kuranyolu reject using this to ban proof-based inquiry; Islahi includes hair-splitting. [6 notes: TABRISI(الحسن وسعيد بن جبير); BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:68/q07/p4 It means denying God's signs. — This is the narrower gloss of Mujahid, Abu Malik, Saʿid, Suddi and Qatada in transmitted reports. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو مالك، سعيد بن جبير,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); DURR-FULL(أبو مالك، سعيد بن جبير,قتادة)]
- 6:68/q07/p5 In an inward reading, it means asserting the self's powers and knowledge as its own. — Qashani treats that as veiling divine qualities. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 6:68/q08 What is the literal origin and image of khawd?  [يَخُوضُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the underlying image is wading in water or mixing.
- 6:68/q08/p1 It originally means wading through water, then metaphorically difficult or muddled engagement. — Jishumi, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Alusi, Ibn Ashur, Raghib, Tabatabaʾi and Islahi give variants. Rummani says the metaphor makes entanglement visible; Burhan says every Qur'anic use is borrowed from water. Ibn Ashur distinguishes walking from swimming. [11 notes: JISHUMI; RUMMANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 6:68/q08/p2 Its derivation is from mixing things, like water with honey. — Qurtubi offers this alternative. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 6:68/q09 Why is a relative clause used for the mockers?  [ٱلَّذِينَ يَخُوضُونَ · rhetoric]
- 6:68/q09/p1 Their defining conduct supplies the reason and endpoint for withdrawal. — Ibn Ashur says it singles out the abusive subset after 6:66 and ends the ban when they cease. He favors Qutb al-Shirazi's reading of Sakkaki against Taftazani. Tabatabaʾi says the wording avoids repetition. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 6:68/q10 What event prompted the withdrawal order?  [ٱلَّذِينَ يَخُوضُونَ فَأَعْرِضْ عَنْهُمْ · reports]
turns on: Reports differ in who was present and how the mockery arose.
- 6:68/q10/p1 Polytheists insulted or mocked the Prophet and Qur'an while sitting with believers. — Suddi, Ibn Abbas, Saʿid and Muqatil transmit this; Muqatil says believers feared staying implied assent. Nahhas and Qurtubi say the Prophet had sat to admonish them. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس وسعيد بن جبير والسدي ومقاتل); JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي,مقاتل)]
- 6:68/q10/p2 Muslims feared leaving would prevent sitting or circumambulating in the Sacred Mosque. — Abu Jaʿfar says 6:69 then permitted reminding and teaching as possible; Baydawi, Kashshaf and Bursevi report this concern. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(المسلمون); TABRISI(أبو جعفر); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 6:68/q10/p3 Polytheists came to hear the Prophet, then mocked. — Ibn Jurayj says revelation followed; another report says his rising frightened them into warning one another to stop. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج)]

## 6:68/q11 What action does 'turn away' require?  [فَأَعْرِضْ عَنْهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether withdrawal must be physical departure or may be another clear display of disapproval.
- 6:68/q11/p1 Leave the gathering and do not remain seated or listen. — Jishumi glosses iʿrad as turning away, near sadd and opposite iqbal. Ibn Atiyya and Razi infer literal departure from the later sitting ban; Abu Hayyan rejects merely inward disapproval. Qushayri asks gracious withdrawal without extended response. [31 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); NAHHAS; JISHUMI; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الذين يتمسكوا بظواهر الألفاظ); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:68/q11/p2 Any act that clearly ends association or publicly shows disapproval can fulfill it. — Razi reports a purpose-based approach; Biqaʿi and Tabatabaʾi allow a clear equivalent to leaving. Kuranyolu cites this alternative. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(الذين يقولون المعنى هو المعتبر); BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Fahreddin er-Râzî)]
- 6:68/q11/p3 In an inward reading, one turns away from the self-veiled. — Qashani says their self-assertion associates others with God. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 6:68/q12 When may one sit with them again?  [حَتَّىٰ يَخُوضُوا۟ فِى حَدِيثٍ غَيْرِهِۦ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether any change of topic suffices or permissible non-mocking talk is required.
- 6:68/q12/p1 When discussion changes away from mockery or signs, the temporary ban ends. — Muqatil says other than God's matter and remembrance; Kashshaf explicitly permits sitting again; Nasafi specifies permissible talk. Tabatabaʾi says sound-purpose meetings remain possible; Kuranyolu permits association with people of incorrect beliefs who do not insult values. [20 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 6:68/q12/p2 Even their other talk remains undisciplined rambling. — Biqaʿi says it mixes trivial and weighty matters without religious ordering. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 6:68/q13 What does the pronoun in 'other than it' refer to?  [غَيْرِهِۦ · grammar]
turns on: Whether its antecedent is the signs as discourse, or the understood act of khawd or mockery.
- 6:68/q13/p1 It returns to the signs understood as Qur'an, speech or discourse. — Baydawi, Abu al-Baqa, Bursevi and Alusi explain why masculine singular can stand for plural signs. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:68/q13/p2 It returns to khawd, the act of engaging in talk. — Samin derives the noun from the preceding verb and cites a parallel where safah is understood from foolish; Alusi prefers this antecedent, and Tabatabaʾi calls it the prohibited discourse. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR(قيل); TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 6:68/q13/p3 It returns specifically to mockery. — Al-Hawfi cites a poem about a fool running toward folly when warned away. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي)]

## 6:68/q14 Why withdraw from the mockery?  [فَأَعْرِضْ عَنْهُمْ · interpretation]
- 6:68/q14/p1 Withdrawal publicly repudiates abuse and avoids fruitless argument or complicity. — Tabrisi says argument in that state misplaces proof; Qurtubi says leave when advice cannot be accepted. Ibn Ashur warns avoiding every denier would stop preaching. Islahi says debate fuels frenzy, silence dishonors truth and risks hypocrisy; Tabatabaʾi cites 4:140, where sitting makes one like them. [18 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جرير); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الذين يقولون المعنى هو المعتبر); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:68/q14/p2 Withdrawal protects inward communion from veiling. — Qashani warns that company may cause a shift into their veil; Qushayri urges the realized to keep intimate communion with God. [2 notes: QASHANI; QUSHAYRI]

## 6:68/q15 How broadly does the rule apply to other harmful gatherings?  [فَأَعْرِضْ عَنْهُمْ ٱلْقَوْمِ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · law]
turns on: Whether the ban extends beyond mockers to sinners, innovators or other harmful speech.
- 6:68/q15/p1 It extends by its rationale to innovators, open wrongdoers and hostile speech. — Nahhas and Qurtubi reject associating with sinners while endorsing their views as dissimulation. Ibn al-Arabi includes major sinners; Ibn Khuwayz Mindad includes innovators, with reports of Nakhaʿi and Ayyub refusing to hear them. Al-Fudayl warns against loving, marrying into, or sitting with innovators; ʿAisha's reported hadith condemns honoring one. Kuranyolu includes backbiting and other hostility; a report in Tabatabaʾi includes insulting an imam or backbiting a Muslim. Study Quran points to false adultery gossip in 24:14–16 as a cause of injustice, division and mistrust. [24 notes: NAHHAS; IBNABIHATIM(إبراهيم التيمي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو جعفر); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جعفر محمد بن علي,أيوب السختياني,إبراهيم النخعي,ابن العربي,ابن خويز منداد,الفضيل بن عياض,عائشة); DURR-FULL(أبو وائل، إبراهيم النخعي); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر,عبد الأعلى بن أعين,محمد بن على); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:68/q15/p2 The ban lasts for the time and conduct of mockery, allowing a sound purpose otherwise. — Tabatabaʾi says it is not a permanent ban on every gathering; Kuranyolu permits ordinary association when values are not insulted. [4 notes: TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:68/q15/p3 In severe fear, a person other than the Messenger may be excused; the Messenger must leave. — Razi argues excusing the Messenger would undermine trust in obligations he conveyed, while another person's lapse would not. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 6:68/q15/p4 One should leave despite fear of ridicule for abandoning debate. — Islahi likens tolerating abuse of God to tolerating abuse of parents; prolonged debate can serve ego. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 6:68/q16 How does the command relate to 4:140 and surrounding verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether 4:140 repeats, explains, changes or supersedes the rule.
- 6:68/q16/p1 Verse 4:140 repeats or clarifies the rule against sitting with mockers. — Tabari links 4:140 and 4:63; Ibn Kathir and Tabatabaʾi say shared sitting resembles approval. Elmalili and Islahi use 4:140 to clarify the general address and sense of khawd. Razi and Ibn Ashur connect the command to denial in 6:66. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:68/q16/p2 Verse 6:69 gave a concession to stay and admonish mockers when leaving the Mosque was impractical. — Abu Jaʿfar, Baydawi, Kashshaf and Bursevi report concern over worship in the Sacred Mosque. [4 notes: TABRISI(أبو جعفر); BAYDAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(المسلمون); BURSEVI]
- 6:68/q16/p3 The passage warns against divisive religious disputation and urges unity. — Ibn Abbas links 6:159, 3:105 and 42:13; earlier communities perished through dispute. Study Quran notes possible links to factions in 6:65. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(some reports)]
- 6:68/q16/p4 Verse 4:140 abrogated this early command; a fighting command followed. — Balkhi prefers abrogation when Muslims were few; Suddi adds 9:5 after 4:140. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(السدي); TABRISI(البلخي)+]
- 6:68/q16/p5 The Prophet's role remains warning through revelation rather than forcing belief. — Islahi says he should disregard new demands and preach at a receptive time. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 6:68/q17 Which readings of yunsiyannaka are reported?  [يُنسِيَنَّكَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the verb uses the light causative form or geminated intensive form, and what supports each.
- 6:68/q17/p1 Ibn ʿAmir uses an open first nun and doubled sin; some also report Ibn ʿAbbas. — Farisi says Form II causativizes like Form IV; Ibn Khalawayh distinguishes forgetting from being made to forget and cites a prophetic practice report. Others say forms interchange, comparing 89:17, 86:17 and 74:17. Ibn Atiyya and Qiraat-ER call it more intensive or repeated. Thalabi describes a doubled nun; others specify sin. [28 notes: NAHHAS(عبد الله بن عامر); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA(النبي); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن عامر); THALABI(ابن عباس وابن عامر); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عامر والعامة); JISHUMI(ابن عامر); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عامر والآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عامر); TABRISI(ابن عامر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عامر); RAZI-FULL(ابن عامر); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن عامر); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن عامر); NASAFI-FULL(شامى); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عامر); NASHR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عامر,ابن عطية); ELMALILI(İbn Âmir); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عامر); QIRAAT-ER]
- 6:68/q17/p2 Most readers use a silent first nun and light sin, the causative ansā. — Farisi, Wahidi and Razi support it with 18:63; Samin adds 12:42. The related forms can share a sense. [12 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن عامر); JISHUMI(ابن عامر); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عامر والآخرون); TABRISI(الباقون); NASAFI-FULL(شامى); SAMIN-DURR(قراءة العامة); NASHR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); RAZI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عامر والعامة)+]
- 6:68/q17/p3 The two causative patterns can carry the same sense. — Ibn Khalawayh cites 9:67 for forgetting as leaving obedience and 18:24 for remembering after disobeying; Qurtubi adduces two poetic lines. Wahidi cites 86:17 and Ibn Jawzi compares garrama and aghrama. [8 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن عامر); NASAFI-FULL(شامى)]

## 6:68/q18 What object is omitted from 'make you forget'?  [يُنسِيَنَّكَ · grammar]
- 6:68/q18/p1 The omitted object is the commanded action: leaving them when reminded. — Samin prefers this over supplying remembrance or truth; both readings omit the second object. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR+]
- 6:68/q18/p2 The omitted object could be remembrance or truth. — Samin records these alternatives but prefers the commanded action. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 6:68/q19 What does Satan make one forget?  [يُنسِيَنَّكَ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the forgotten thing is the revealed prohibition or alleged rational aversion before revelation.
- 6:68/q19/p1 The prohibition on sitting with mockers or withdrawing. — Kashshaf, Tabrisi, Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi and Alusi say Satan distracts from the command, so one remains seated. Study Quran adds missing when talk turns disparaging; Tabatabaʾi says the condition intensifies the order. [13 notes: MUQATIL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:68/q19/p2 Before revelation, one might forget that reason itself finds such sitting ugly. — Kashshaf gives this alternative; Abu Hayyan objects that the condition follows the command and describes a future event. Alusi rejects intrinsic rational good and evil as its premise and denies universal rational condemnation. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير)-]

## 6:68/q20 Who is Satan here, and how can forgetting be ascribed to him?  [ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ · theology]
turns on: Whether Satan causes a human lapse indirectly or cannot influence the Prophet at all.
- 6:68/q20/p1 Satan can indirectly cause natural forgetfulness without controlling revelation or causing sin. — Tabrisi attributes forgetfulness to God but says neglected thought and whispers provide an occasion, like death from exposing someone to cold. Ibn Ashur compares Job's affliction in 38:41, Bilal's sleep, a quarrel before the Night of Decree and bodily symptoms; he distinguishes absence through distraction from positive whispering. Study Quran cites 12:42, 58:19 and 22:52. [13 notes: TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL(أيوب عليه السلام,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عياض); STUDYQURAN(Āl)]
- 6:68/q20/p2 The Prophet cannot be distracted by Satan; the condition is hypothetical. — Alusi excludes whispering and passing thoughts; Ibn Ashur records protection even from permissible symptoms and cites Gabriel's removal of Satan's share. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(جبريل)]
- 6:68/q20/p3 It may mean Iblis or a chief among his troops. — Bursevi says the Prophet's personal companion became Muslim and urged only good; he cites a report comparing Adam's Satan and wife. [2 notes: BURSEVI]
- 6:68/q20/p4 In an inward reading, the self's whisper and seductive falsehoods incline one toward veiled company. — Qashani says showing one's own qualities makes one resemble the veiled. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 6:68/q21 Can the Prophet forget, and in what matters?  [يُنسِيَنَّكَ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ · theology]
turns on: Whether lapse is possible in ordinary actions, worship or law, and whether conveying revelation is protected.
- 6:68/q21/p1 He can forget in action, worship or legal practice, but God corrects him. — Qurtubi reports Adam's forgetting, the Prophet's saying he forgets as a human, and recitation that reminded him of a verse. Ibn Ashur calls reports of an interrupted four-rakʿa prayer and forgotten verses authentic. The Sunni majority allows lapses outside conveyed revelation; Nawawi says they teach rulings for the forgetful. Bursevi claims scholarly agreement. [19 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA(النبي); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عامة العلماء والأئمة النظار); ALUSI-FULL(القاضي,جمهور العلماء); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(جمهور علماء السنة من الأشاعرة وغيرهم,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Resûlullah); STUDYQURAN(Āl); ALUSI-FULL(النووي)+]
- 6:68/q21/p2 He cannot forget in conveying revelation, but may lapse elsewhere if religion remains intact. — Tabrisi states the Imami rule, comparing sleep and fainting. Alusi distinguishes authoritative Rafidi books from broad attributions; Qadi Iyad excludes intentional error and verbal revelation. [5 notes: TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL(القاضي); IBNASHUR-FULL(جمهور علماء السنة من الأشاعرة وغيرهم)]
- 6:68/q21/p3 He cannot forget in religious acts or delivery; apparent reports need reinterpretation. — Abu Ishaq and others take this stricter view; Alusi says verbal delivery is unanimously protected. Some cite 87:6 for total protection. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الأستاذ أبو إسحاق); ALUSI-FULL(أبو إسحاق الإسفراييني,الرافضة); IBNASHUR-FULL(جمهور علماء السنة من الأشاعرة وغيرهم)]
- 6:68/q21/p4 The address is intended for the community because prophetic lapse against a divine rule is excluded. — Tabatabaʾi's impeccability argument uses 4:140; Ibn al-ʿArabi rejects community-only address if meant to rule out any prophetic forgetfulness. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(بعض العلماء); TABATABAI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)-; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو بكر بن العربي)-]
- 6:68/q21/p5 He cannot forget at all, but deliberately appears to forget to teach a practice. — Batinites and some spiritual authorities report this; Qurtubi rejects al-Isfarayini's formulation because deliberately forgetting and not forgetting conflict. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الباطنية وطائفة من أرباب علم القلوب); QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 6:68/q22 When must God correct a prophetic lapse?  [يُنسِيَنَّكَ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ · theology]
turns on: Whether correction must be immediate or may occur later in his lifetime.
- 6:68/q22/p1 Correction must accompany the event immediately. — Qadi Abu Bakr and most scholars require it; Alusi reports the same majority, and Nawawi favors prompt correction to prevent harm. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(القاضي أبو بكر وأكثر العلماء); ALUSI-FULL(الأكثرون); ALUSI-FULL(النووي)+]
- 6:68/q22/p2 Correction may be delayed, but before his life and transmission end. — Abu al-Maʿali and another group permit this interval. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو المعالي); ALUSI-FULL(الأكثرون)]

## 6:68/q23 Can prophets err in ordinary speech or worldly beliefs?  [يُنسِيَنَّكَ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ · theology]
turns on: Whether protection from error extends beyond revelation to all reports and ordinary beliefs.
- 6:68/q23/p1 A prophet may forget ordinary speech unrelated to revelation, law or eschatological report. — Alusi reports a group arguing no religious harm results. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قوم)]
- 6:68/q23/p2 Prophets cannot err in any report, in any state. — Qadi Iyad argues their speech was preserved, and no verbal correction is transmitted, unlike corrections in prayer, date-palm advice and Badr's position. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(القاضي)+]
- 6:68/q23/p3 Inadvertence in worldly beliefs is possible. — Qadi Iyad does not rule it out. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(القاضي)]

## 6:68/q24 How is the conditional mention of forgetting emphatic?  [وَإِمَّا يُنسِيَنَّكَ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ · rhetoric]
- 6:68/q24/p1 The hypothetical case reinforces the importance of prompt withdrawal. — Biqaʿi highlights the emphatic wording; Ibn Ashur says adding forgetfulness underscores the order; Tabatabaʾi says even forgetfulness cannot justify remaining after recollection. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMIN-DURR]

## 6:68/q25 What does dhikra mean?  [ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether it denotes recollection, a reminder, or reminding the mockers.
- 6:68/q25/p1 It means remembering or a reminder, equivalent to dhikr. — Majaz and Ibn Atiyya equate the forms; Layth calls it a noun for reminder; Farra allows remembrance or admonition and cites 6:69. Wahidi refers to 11:114; Ibn Ashur contrasts it with forgetting. [11 notes: MAJAZ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,الليث); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الفراء,الليث); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:68/q25/p2 It means reminding the mockers by inviting them to faith. — Abu Muslim takes withdrawal to follow attempted admonition while hope remains; Alusi rejects this. [1 notes: TABRISI(أبو مسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم)-]

## 6:68/q26 How is dhikra parsed?  [ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether it is accusative or nominative, and how its form is classified.
- 6:68/q26/p1 It is a verbal noun on the rare fuʿlā pattern. — Samin says it is the only such verbal noun; Bursevi repeats this. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]
- 6:68/q26/p2 It may be accusative by an implied command to remind, or nominative in 'a reminder is upon you'. — Tabrisi cites 42:48 for the predicative possibility. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 6:68/q27 What does 'after the reminder' mean?  [بَعْدَ ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether it means recollecting the ban, God's reminding of rational ugliness, or admonishing offenders.
- 6:68/q27/p1 After remembering God's prohibition and duty to withdraw. — Abu Malik and Jubbaʾi state this; Baydawi, Alusi's majority and Ibn Ashur concur. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو مالك); TABRISI(الجبائي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(جمهور المفسرين); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:68/q27/p2 After God reminds one that sitting with mockers is ugly. — Zamakhshari ties this to pre-revelation rational prohibition; Alusi calls it remote. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 6:68/q27/p3 After reminding them by inviting them to faith. — Abu Muslim says withdraw when further appeal is hopeless; Alusi rejects it. [1 notes: TABRISI(أبو مسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم)-]

## 6:68/q28 What must one do on remembering after forgetfully sitting there?  [فَلَا تَقْعُدْ بَعْدَ ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · law]
- 6:68/q28/p1 Leave promptly; do not continue sitting with them. — Ibn Abbas, Qatada, Mujahid, Abu Malik, Saʿid and Muqatil repeatedly transmit this. Qushayri urges vigilance; Islahi warns of Satan's trick. Ibn Ashur says leaving follows from the duty to withdraw. [37 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(أبو مالك، السدي,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(أبو مالك وسعيد بن جبير,مقاتل بن حيان); THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي عن أبي مالك وسعيد بن جبير ومقاتل بن حيان); DURR-FULL(أبو مالك، سعيد بن جبير,قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:68/q28/p2 In an inward reading, divine remembrance restores alertness and separation from the veiled. — Qashani says not to sit with those who attribute divine qualities to themselves; Qushayri ties recovery to remembrance. [2 notes: QASHANI; QUSHAYRI]

## 6:68/q29 Is a forgetful person responsible while unaware?  [فَلَا تَقْعُدْ بَعْدَ ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · law]
turns on: Whether obligation can attach to the heedless, and how mere lapse differs from a chosen cause such as drunkenness.
- 6:68/q29/p1 Forgetfulness lifts accountability until recollection. — Razi and Ibn Ashur infer this from the resumed command. Ibn Kathir cites a report lifting error, forgetfulness and coercion. Alusi considers non-obligation correct because a heedless command brings no benefit; Kuranyolu calls it legitimate excuse. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:68/q29/p2 An act may remain due even though a heedless person is not presently addressable. — Ibn Burhan contrasts jurists' remaining duty with theologians' denial of an inconceivable command. Alusi distinguishes chosen drunkenness and records responses to the knowledge-of-God objection. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن برهان)]
- 6:68/q29/p3 Ignorance or incapacity excludes impossible duties, including a command to unbelievers to believe. — Jubbaʾi reasons that if ignorance excuses, inability does more strongly and capacity must precede action. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الجبائي)]

## 6:68/q30 Who are the wrongdoing people?  [ٱلْقَوْمِ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether they are specifically polytheists or all open deniers and mockers.
- 6:68/q30/p1 They are polytheists or idolaters in this setting. — Muqatil, Ibn Abbas, Suddi, Razi and Qurtubi identify them. [11 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(أبو مالك، السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي,مقاتل بن حيان); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو مالك، سعيد بن جبير)]
- 6:68/q30/p2 They are gatherings of unbelievers and immoral people who openly deny or mock revelation. — Saʿid and Suddi give this broader identification; Ibn Ashur points to polemicists in the clause and Tabatabaʾi extends the rationale. [5 notes: TABRISI(سعيد بن جبير والسدي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:68/q30/p3 In an inward reading they put their own qualities in God's place. — Qashani says this veils the divine. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 6:68/q31 Why are they explicitly called wrongdoers?  [ٱلْقَوْمِ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · rhetoric]
- 6:68/q31/p1 The noun marks denial and mockery as injustice rather than using a neutral pronoun. — Ibn Atiyya says they put things out of place; Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi specify substituting denial for belief and mockery for reverence. Abu Hayyan, Samin and Biqaʿi say it records their cause or quality. Ibn Ashur calls their polemic stubborn denial; Kuranyolu says it is neither fair nor evidence-based. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 6:68/q32 What does the verse imply about dissimulation by religious authorities?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether the prohibition rules out taqiyya altogether or only when no independent proof safeguards the ruling.
- 6:68/q32/p1 It refutes permission for prophets or imams to associate with sinners and endorse their views as taqiyya. — Jubbaʾi argues this against the Imamis; Nahhas and Qurtubi likewise use the verse against such claimed permission. [3 notes: TABRISI(الجبائي); NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI-]
- 6:68/q32/p2 An imam may use taqiyya only where decisive independent proof preserves the duty; prophets may omit repeating a ruling already explained. — Tabrisi rejects Jubbaʾi's accusation, says rulings known only through an imam cannot be concealed, and cites the report that the Prophet answered ʿUmar's kalala question by saying the sword verse sufficed; the note mentions 9:5. [4 notes: TABRISI(عمر بن الخطاب)]

## 6:68/q33 What spiritual reflections are drawn from this passage?  [* · inward]
- 6:68/q33/p1 Conveyance is the Messenger's duty; realization of divine connection belongs to God's will, and the realized should not return to veiled company. — Qushayri says remembrance restores alertness; the pure are spared wrongdoers' Resurrection pains, divine stratagems break their delusion, guidance cannot be traded for error or Paradise for Hell, and no new purpose obstructs God's power. Qashani describes self-assertion and Satanic attraction as veiling. [14 notes: QUSHAYRI; QASHANI]

## 6:68/q34 What wider account of Qur'anic guidance and reform accompanies these notes?  [* · interpretation]
- 6:68/q34/p1 The Qur'an aims at individual, collective and civilizational reform through sound belief and reflective reading. — Ibn Ashur describes moral purification, worship, social rules, shared welfare and correction of false belief, citing 11:101 and 38:29. He notes dispute over knowing God's full intent but requires inquiry as ability permits. Arabic eloquence and the first Arab audience serve a universal, lasting law; 11:49, 6:156 and 6:157 illustrate Arab-specific addresses. [11 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:68/q35 What rhetorical discussions use this passage as an example?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether eloquence rests in ease of sound or meaning and arrangement, and how brevity compares with expansion.
- 6:68/q35/p1 Eloquence includes meaning, construction, and appropriate brevity or expansion. — Jurjani disputes an account that ranks inimitability by smooth articulation alone, citing clarity, allusion, simile, omission, joining and order. Suyuti reviews equality, brevity and expansion, says equality is rare in the Qur'an, and cites 35:43 as omitting a modifier of alladhina. [8 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL; ITQAN(ابن الأثير وجماعة,السكاكي وجماعة,القزويني,الكشاف)]
- 6:68/q35/p2 Ease of articulation measures eloquence and inimitability. — Jurjani reports this opposing account after al-Jahiz's example of phonetic difficulty in Ibn Yusayr's poetry, then rejects a sound-only standard. [2 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL(الْجَاحِظُ,ممن يزعم); JURJANI-DALAIL-]
