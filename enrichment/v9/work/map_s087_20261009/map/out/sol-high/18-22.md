# 18:22: 25 questions

## 18:22/q01 Who are the people predicted to give these counts?  [سَيَقُولُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether future speech is the Prophet's contemporaries' dispute, a retrospective report of the cave community, or continuing public discussion; reports assign denominations differently.
- 18:22/q01/p1 People of Scripture in the Prophet's time, variously Jews or Christian groups, voiced the counts. — Yahya, Ibn Hisham and Ibn Atiyya connect the Jews with questions sent through Meccans. Muqatil, Tha'labi, Wahidi and others recount Najran's Jacobite Sayyid saying three, Nestorian Aqib five, and Muslims seven; Ibn Jawzi also reports a different Melkite/Jacobite/Nestorian assignment. Baydawi and Suddi assign three to Jews and five to Christians. Alusi doubts the exclusively Jewish account. [42 notes: MUQATIL(نصارى نجران); YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNHISHAM; FARRA; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(المسلمون,النسطورية,اليعقوبية); BAGHAWI-FULL(السيد,العاقب,المسلمون); KASHSHAF-FULL(السيد والعاقب); TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(السيد والعاقب); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(النصارى,اليهود); NASAFI-FULL(السيد,العاقب,المسلمون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عطية,الزمخشري); BURSEVI(المسلمون,النصارى,اليهود); DURR-FULL(النصارى,اليهود); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية,السدي)]
- 18:22/q01/p2 People of the youths' own city disputed before their discovery, or people around the cave later disputed. — Mawardi proposes people of Medina before disclosure or People of Scripture after a long time. Alusi finds the early historical reading unlikely in view of future tense; another possibility is groups who discovered the cave and obtained a count from the returning youth. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الماوردي); ALUSI-FULL(الماوردي وأبو حيان)]
- 18:22/q01/p3 The unintroduced subject can be people in general, including Muslims, who continue discussing the number. — Ibn Ashur says discussion itself is permissible but unsupported assertions lack proof; Qashani says surface readers among Jews and Muslims speculate. Alusi rejects the suggestion that God speaks the third count in the majestic plural. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 18:22/q02 What does the future wording imply about the dispute?  [سَيَقُولُونَ وَيَقُولُونَ · grammar]
turns on: The scope of the initial future particle and the timing of the reported debate.
- 18:22/q02/p1 The first future marker extends to the later coordinated statements, which need no repeated marker. — Zamakhshari and Nasafi allow the later imperfects themselves future force; Abu Hayyan, Razi, Baydawi and Alusi take coordination to carry the initial future sense. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 18:22/q02/p2 It predicts a later exchange after the Prophet recounts the story or asks the number. — Abu Hayyan and Samin supply an omitted exchange; Wahidi notes the sura is Meccan while Najran came after the Hijra. Alusi envisages speech after telling or questioning; Tabatabai allows near-future circulation relative to revelation or event. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMIN-DURR]
- 18:22/q02/p3 The future signals continuing, ethically irrelevant speculation about a legendary narrative. — Asad reads the tense as stressing the parable's ethical purpose; Ibn Ashur observes debate over number and duration continued after revelation. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:22/q03 How are the numbers and dog clauses parsed?  [ثَلَٰثَةٌۭ رَّابِعُهُمْ كَلْبُهُمْ خَمْسَةٌۭ سَادِسُهُمْ كَلْبُهُمْ سَبْعَةٌۭ وَثَامِنُهُمْ كَلْبُهُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether an omitted subject, pronoun link, participle, or explicit waw licenses each following clause.
- 18:22/q03/p1 Each number is a nominative predicate of omitted 'they'; the dog clause describes the number or serves as another predicate. — Farra and Zajjaj reject accusative object syntax. Nahhas supplies subjects. Zamakhshari, Nasafi and Samin make dog clauses adjectival; Ibn Ashur also permits successive predicates. Abu Hayyan and Samin supply 'persons', not 'men', because the dog makes the total four. Razi notes omitted 'they'; Burhan compares 3:13. Alusi reports Ibn al-Hajib's successive-predicate analysis. Tabatabai transmits Zamakhshari's parse. [18 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(النحويون); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL(ابن الحاجب); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)+]
- 18:22/q03/p2 The dog clause is a circumstantial clause attached to the count. — Tha'lab calls the last waw circumstantial; Sahib al-Nazm takes the first clause as a circumstance of three. Abu al-Fath objects that 'they are three' offers no governor. Ibn Ashur makes the last clause circumstantial. [3 notes: MUQATIL(أبو العباس ثعلب); WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); IBNASHUR-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو الفتح الموصلي)-; TABRISI-]
- 18:22/q03/p3 'Fourth' adjectivally modifies 'three' and governs 'their dog' as an active participle. — One grammatical proposal uses participial government. Abu al-Fath and Abu al-Baqa object that a past participle cannot work this way; Samin answers that the past scene is depicted as present, like basit. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(آخرون); SAMIN-DURR; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو الفتح)-; TABRISI-; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-]
- 18:22/q03/p4 The dog clause describes the group as an independent subject-predicate sentence. — The example 'these are three, their servant fourth' supports it; Abu al-Fath objects that the final conjunction requires parallel coordination, with earlier conjunctions omitted through pronominal linkage. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(بعضهم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو الفتح)-]
- 18:22/q03/p5 The dog clauses are coordinated sentences whose conjunction can be understood where absent. — Abu al-Fath and Abu Ali say pronominal reference links earlier clauses without an overt conjunction; Wahidi makes 'fourth' coordinate with the initial predicate. Tabrisi says shared reference permits either overt or omitted conjunction, but denies treating the last clause as a mere description. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو الفتح,أبو علي); TABRISI(أبو علي الفارسي); BURHAN(الواحدي)]

## 18:22/q04 How is the three-count pronounced?  [ثَلَٰثَةٌۭ رَّابِعُهُمْ · readings]
- 18:22/q04/p1 Ibn Muhaysin assimilates the final thāʾ of 'three' to the following tāʾ. — Ibn Jinni, Abu Hayyan, Samin and Alusi cite proximity of articulation, common voicelessness, and the preceding alif or weak letter; Kashshaf and Ibn Atiyya report it too. [6 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(ابن محيصن); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن محيصن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن محيصن); SAMIN-DURR(ابن محيصن); ALUSI-FULL(ابن محيصن)]

## 18:22/q05 How is the five-count pronounced?  [خَمْسَةٌۭ سَادِسُهُمْ · readings]
turns on: Transmitted vowel and assimilation variants and the strength of their attribution.
- 18:22/q05/p1 A report from Ibn Kathir opens the mīm of khamsa with fatḥa. — Ibn Jinni, Abu Hayyan, Samin and Alusi call it transmitted dialectal usage and compare ʿashara; Ibn Jinni says poetic necessity is not required and compares variant place-name forms. [5 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(ابن كثير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(شبل بن عباد); SAMIN-DURR(ابن كثير); ALUSI-FULL(شبل بن عباد عن ابن كثير)]
- 18:22/q05/p2 Ibn Muhaysin is reported with kasra on khāʾ and mīm and assimilation into the following sīn. — Abu Hayyan and Alusi add a variant assimilating tanwīn without nasalization. Samin calls the reading very heavy and suspects mistaken attribution. Ibn Atiyya reports vowel variation. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن محيصن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن محيصن); SAMIN-DURR(ابن محيصن); ALUSI-FULL(ابن محيصن)]

## 18:22/q06 How many youths were in the cave?  [ثَلَٰثَةٌۭ خَمْسَةٌۭ سَبْعَةٌۭ وَثَامِنُهُمْ كَلْبُهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the uncriticized final count and waw establish seven, whether 'dog' conceals an eighth man, and whether the divine reservation counsels withholding a precise answer.
- 18:22/q06/p1 Seven youths, with the dog eighth. — Ibn Abbas repeatedly counts himself among the few and says seven; Abu Masud and others concur. Tha'labi, Kashshaf, Baydawi and Nasafi say Muslims learned this from the Prophet through Gabriel. Many argue that only three and five are labeled conjecture; Razi adds the emphatic waw, the following 'few know', likelihood that one reported count is true, and revelation to the Prophet. Ibn Kathir calls Ibn Abbas chains sound. Ali's reported seven include six royal advisers and a shepherd; transmitted name spellings vary. Alusi questions the Ali attribution and the inference that few early hearers perceived the linguistic clue. Tabatabai adds the earlier dialogue's implication of at least seven. [63 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن عباس); FARRA(ابن عباس); ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); IBNHISHAM; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(ابن عباس,المسلمون); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,محمد بن إسحاق); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,عطاء); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(أبو مسعود); BURSEVI(المسلمون); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(unnamed view,İbn Abbas); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL(المسلمون)+; RAZI-FULL+; TABATABAI+; THALABI+; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 18:22/q06/p2 Eight youths, with the eighth understood as the dog's owner or companion. — Ibn Jurayj and Ibn Ishaq construe 'eighth' with an implied owner or companion; the noncanonical kalbihim or kalbihim reading is taken as 'their herder' or dog's owner. Ibn Kathir cautions that an eight-name report from Mujahid may come from Ibn Ishaq or an intermediary; Bagawi calls seven correct. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج ومحمد بن إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جريج وابن اسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج ومحمد بن إسحاق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:22/q06/p3 Three youths, with the dog fourth. — Najran Jacobite or Melkite speakers, or Jews in another report, make this first claim. The Quran labels it conjecture. A discrepant Suddi report has Ibn Abbas tentatively guess three despite many reports of his seven. [9 notes: MUQATIL(نصارى نجران); YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(اليعقوبية); BAGHAWI-FULL(السيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL(السيد); BURSEVI(اليهود); DURR-FULL(اليهود); QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 18:22/q06/p4 Five youths, with the dog sixth. — Reports variously assign it to Najran Nestorians or Jacobites, or Christians generally; it is explicitly called conjecture. [8 notes: MUQATIL(نصارى نجران); YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(النسطورية); BAGHAWI-FULL(العاقب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL(العاقب); BURSEVI(النصارى); DURR-FULL(النصارى); QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 18:22/q06/p5 The revealed wording leaves the exact count with God rather than establishing a count for readers. — Islahi calls the seven inference plausible but prefers the verse's reserve. Ibn Ashur says seven and eight do not settle the count; Kuranyolu stresses that names and number are deliberately unspecified, and knowing them does not advance the lesson. Qushayri says human observation cannot access the hidden number. [8 notes: QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]

## 18:22/q07 What do reports say about the youths' identities and setting?  [سَبْعَةٌۭ · reports]
turns on: The value and reliability of transmitted names and geographical traditions.
- 18:22/q07/p1 Lists name seven youths, often six royal advisers and a seventh shepherd, in or near Ephesus. — Tha'labi, Ibn Abbas, Ali and Hushaym give names with unstable foreign spellings; some call Yamlikha the food purchaser. Ali's report situates advisers with Daqyanus and a shepherd. Alusi and Ibn Kathir warn names are weak or variably transmitted; Kashifi prefers Martush for the shepherd. Muqatil's quoted account calls the youths Roman kings Banū al-Raknā who embraced Islam and withdrew. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(علي); TABRISI(الضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(هشيم); RAZI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); BAYDAWI-FULL(علي); NASAFI-FULL(علي); QASHANI(أمير المؤمنين عليه السلام); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما); BURSEVI(علي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,علي); BURSEVI(الكاشفي)+]
- 18:22/q07/p2 The youths' religious identity and exact place are not established by Quran or hadith. — Kuranyolu says many countries claim a cave, including Tarsus, Ephesus and Afşin, Christian sources usually placing it at Ephesus; a pre-Jesus date is inferred from Jewish questioners and an etymology of raqim, but is not settled. Its emphasis is resurrection and believers' ultimate victory. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

- 18:22/q09/p1 It was called Qitmir or Qatmur, with variant spelling. — Ibn Abbas, Ali and others report the name; Tha'labi and Qurtubi describe it as brindled and between Qalti and Kurdish breeds. ## 18:22/q10 What does 'rajman bil-ghayb' mean?  [رَجْمًۢا بِٱلْغَيْبِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the phrase's scope includes both preceding claims and whether the unqualified seven count is thereby endorsed.
- 18:22/q10/p1 It means guessing or speaking conjecturally about an unseen matter without knowledge. — Qatada and Suddi gloss it thus. The root literally throws stones, hence a blind shot at an unknown target or an idiom for supposition; Zuhayr's line on war and al-hadith al-murajjam, plus other poetry, attest conjectural speech. Kashshaf cites 34:53; Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan mention association with translation. Wahidi says the ba marks speaking while absent from events. Tabatabai prefers the blind-throw image to a flat gloss. Rajm elsewhere can mean expulsion, cursing, killing, or insult according to Burhan and Ibn Faris. [50 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ(زهير); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(زهير,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(زهير); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين,مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(زهير); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زهير); SAMIN-DURR(زهير); BURHAN; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ITQAN(ابن فارس); BURSEVI(النصارى); ALUSI-FULL(الكشف,زهير); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 18:22/q10/p2 It specifically discredits both the three and five counts, while the third is left without this label. — Tabatabai says its grammatical position reaches both earlier counts; restricting it to five would require different order. Ibn Kathir, Ibn Atiyya, Razi and others take the omission after seven as a clue to its truth; Ibn Ashur refuses an inferred definitive count. [7 notes: IBNHISHAM; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(unnamed view)]

## 18:22/q11 What is the grammatical role of 'rajman'?  [رَجْمًۢا بِٱلْغَيْبِ · grammar]
turns on: Which implicit governing verb or adverbial relation accounts for the accusative.
- 18:22/q11/p1 It is an accusative cognate verbal noun governed by an implied conjecturing verb or the sense of 'they say'. — Jishumi supplies an implied verb. Abu Hayyan and Samin allow this or the speech verb's conjectural sense; Alusi and Bursevi also report the verbal-noun analysis. [5 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI(النصارى); ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:22/q11/p2 It is circumstantial: they speak while guessing. — Samin, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi and Alusi report this parse. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI(النصارى); ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:22/q11/p3 It is an accusative of purpose explaining why they speak. — Abu Hayyan and Samin allow it; Alusi calls it weak. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:22/q12 Why is there a waw before 'eighth' but not before 'fourth' or 'sixth'?  [وَثَامِنُهُمْ كَلْبُهُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether waw merely links clauses, confirms the final count, emphatically attaches a description, or follows a special counting idiom; the grammatical legitimacy of adjective waw and the cited number lists are disputed.
- 18:22/q12/p1 It is ordinary conjunction or resumption; overt and omitted waw can have equivalent effect. — Zajjaj and Abu Ishaq say inclusion makes no essential semantic change. Abu Ali and Abu al-Fath say earlier clauses link through pronouns and can omit conjunction. Ibn Atiyya rejects a special eight rule; Qurtubi's grammarians say it separates the final report. Tabrisi says shared reference supplies linkage, and Qushayri warns against strained hidden causes. [20 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(قولان); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو الفتح,أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(أبو علي الفارسي,علي بن عيسى); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو نصر,الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري,النحويون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(الواحدي); ITQAN(جماعة); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 18:22/q12/p2 It closes the reported guesses and confirms that the dog was eighth, validating seven youths. — Nahhas, Tha'labi, Ibn Abbas, Abu Muslim, Bagawi and Ibn Jawzi describe a waw of judgment or completion. Some take the dog clause as a divine aside rather than quoted human speech; Alusi reports Ibn al-Hajib's proposal but rejects adopting it. Ibn Abbas says the count ends where waw appears. [18 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(إبراهيم بن السري,قولان); THALABI(بعضهم); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس); JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج ومقاتل بن سليمان); QURTUBI-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن الحاجب,ابن عباس); TABATABAI(ابن عباس)]
- 18:22/q12/p3 It introduces an adjective or circumstance with emphatic attachment, marking the final count as firm knowledge. — Zamakhshari's adjectival account is followed by Razi, Nasafi, Biqai and others; 15:4 is cited. Taybi defends a metaphorical relation like ba, with Sibawayh's usage; Alusi rejects Ibn Malik's separation objection. Abu Hayyan, Ibn Malik, Sakkaki, Farāʾid, Miftah, Zarkashi and Ibn Ashur dispute adjectival waw as ungrammatical, preferring circumstance or coordination. Samarrai treats it as circumstantial confirmation and says semantically distant descriptions invite waw, comparing 57:3 and 68:11. [29 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURHAN(الأزهري,الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء,السكاكي,الطيبي,سيبويه); TABATABAI(الزمخشري); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(المفسرون); ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير,الزمخشري,السيد المحقق,الطيبي)+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; BURHAN(جمال الدين بن مالك وأثير الدين)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,ابن مالك,صاحب الفرائد,صاحب المفتاح)-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 18:22/q12/p4 It is a conventional 'waw of eight' following a completed sequence of seven. — Tha'labi, Ibn Khalawayh, Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash and Qaffal cite 9:112, 39:73 and 66:5; some explain the eight Paradise gates against seven Hell gates. Ibn Atiyya says those examples need ordinary conjunction; Qushayri and Qaffal's critics cite the unmarked eighth divine name in 59:23. Suyuti and Abu Ali reject a special rule. [15 notes: THALABI(بعضهم); JISHUMI(علي بن عيسى,قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن خالويه); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو إسحاق الثعلبي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو بكر بن عياش,ابن خالويه,القفال); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو بكر بن عياش وابن خالويه); SAMIN-DURR(ابن خالويه); BURHAN(ابن خالويه); ITQAN(جماعة); IBNATIYYA-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري)-; ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير)-; TABATABAI(أبو علي الفارسي)-]

## 18:22/q13 How is 'rabbi' read?  [رَّبِّىٓ · readings]
- 18:22/q13/p1 Ibn Kathir, Nafi and Abu Amr open the possessive yāʾ; other readers leave it quiescent. — Ibn Jawzi records the split; the recitation note says fatḥa lengthens pronunciation without changing meaning. Ibn Mujahid's wider list of extra possessive yāʾs supplies the comparative reading context. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو); QIRAAT-ER; IBNMUJAHID]

## 18:22/q14 What does 'my Lord knows best their number' assert?  [قُل رَّبِّىٓ أَعْلَمُ بِعِدَّتِهِم · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the verse's reservation simply states divine epistemic priority or also withholds any definitive human count.
- 18:22/q14/p1 God has complete and certain knowledge of the count; humans know only what He discloses. — Damghani glosses idda as number or count. Qushayri says hidden states and hearts too are known to God, and observation cannot establish the count. Abu Hayyan reconciles divine complete knowledge with limited human knowledge. Ibn Ashur says the command answers the audience's request for certainty. Tabatabai compares the youths' answer about their sleep duration; Razi says past and future detail is known only to God or those informed. Bursevi's Sa'di Mufti says 'best' compares degrees of certainty, not God's knowledge to ignorant guessers. [10 notes: DAMGHANI; QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(سعدي المفتي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 18:22/q14/p2 It instructs readers to suspend a useless, unproved exact count and leave it to God. — Ibn Kathir says unknown details lacking reliable revelation need not be pursued. Ibn Ashur says inquiry yields no religious or human benefit; Elmalili, Islahi and Kuranyolu emphasize the story's lesson over a fixed figure. Asad links future speculation to the ethical parable. [7 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]

## 18:22/q15 Who are the few who know their number?  [مَّا يَعْلَمُهُمْ إِلَّا قَلِيلٌۭ · referent]
turns on: Whether the exception is unrestricted among people or especially the learned People of Scripture; both can include Ibn Abbas in later reports.
- 18:22/q15/p1 A small number of people, including Ibn Abbas and perhaps the Prophet, know through divine information. — Qatada and Tabari give a few of God's creatures; Akhfash and Nahhas supply people as the understood subject and number as understood object. Numerous Ibn Abbas reports say he is among them; Zajjaj qualifies the report if authentic, Razi's Qadi requires transmission from the Prophet, and Tabrisi suggests that source. Alusi cites sound-chain Ibn Abbas and parallel Ibn Masud reports; Wahb says 'few' elsewhere means fewer than ten, whereas Alusi says it need not. Ibn Ashur says the Prophet must be included; Bursevi says they followed the available indications. [24 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن عباس); FARRA(ابن عباس); ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); ZAJJAJ(ابن عباس); NAHHAS; THALABI(ابن عباس,قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس,قتادة); DURR-FULL(وهب بن منبه); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI]
- 18:22/q15/p2 A few knowledgeable People of Scripture are the exception. — Ataa and Ataa al-Khurasani identify them; Kashshaf and Nasafi stress that most scripturalists still guess. Ibn Atiyya includes Ibn Abbas among those who know. Tabatabai takes this as likely. [13 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(آخرون); THALABI(عطاء); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء الخراساني); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(عطاء); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL(عطاء); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]

## 18:22/q16 What does 'mirāʾ' mean?  [فَلَا تُمَارِ · meaning]
- 18:22/q16/p1 It means disputing or arguing about something doubtful. — Wahidi, Damghani and Jishumi gloss dispute; Ibn al-Anbari, Raghib and Ibn Ashur relate the root to drawing milk by rubbing a she-camel's udder, as an argument draws out contention. Tabatabai's Raghib distinguishes hesitation from general doubt. Islahi says habitual polemicists shift issues. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 18:22/q17 What sort of argument is permitted about the sleepers?  [فَلَا تُمَارِ فِيهِمْ إِلَّا مِرَآءًۭ ظَٰهِرًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ẓāhir means the revealed surface, a clear proof, a defeating reply, or an exchange visible to witnesses.
- 18:22/q17/p1 Give only the plain revealed account, briefly and without prolonged probing or humiliation. — Hasan, Qatada, Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Dahhak and Sufyan say revelation suffices. Kashshaf, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi add courteous public speech, citing 16:125 or 29:46; Ibn Kathir and Ibn Ashur say exact knowledge has little benefit. Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi call 'argument' figurative for a clear reply rather than license for pointless disputation. Asad limits discussion to the ethical lesson. Islahi says politely divert polemicists. The 18:22-24 passage is parenthetical in Islahi's account. [55 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNHISHAM; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس، قتادة، الضحاك,السدي,سفيان); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,ابن عباس وقتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الأنباري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن الأنباري); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 18:22/q17/p2 Tell opposing claimants plainly that their proposed count is wrong and unsupported. — Ibn Zayd says deny their claim and say only a few know. Razi counsels suspending judgment on an exact count while denying their evidence. Tabrisi says ask disputants for proof of claims that could be true or false. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد)]
- 18:22/q17/p3 Argue only with manifest proof and a truthful, certain report. — Ali ibn Isa, Mawardi and Ibn al-Anbari demand clear proof and certainty rather than guesswork; Tabatabai cites a verse of poetry for an argument that removes the opponent's case. [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(علي بن عيسى); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,الماوردي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الأنباري,التبريزي,الماوردي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن الأنباري,التبريزي,الماوردي); TABATABAI]
- 18:22/q17/p4 Make the exchange public before witnesses, or testify to the story. — Ibn Bahr and Tabrisi say public debate prevents opponents later claiming private prior knowledge; Mawardi mentions testimony before people. Nasafi says publicity displays the Prophet's truth. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن بحر); ALUSI-FULL(ابن بحر)]

## 18:22/q18 What does 'istiftāʾ' mean here?  [وَلَا تَسْتَفْتِ · meaning]
- 18:22/q18/p1 It means asking for authoritative information or a judgment about the youths' unknown affairs. — Jishumi glosses seeking information; Ibn Ashur says seeking an authoritative answer on what is not commonly known. [2 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:22/q19 Whom do 'about them' and 'from them' designate?  [فِيهِمْ مِّنْهُمْ · referent]
turns on: The antecedent of the two pronouns, the Meccan setting, and whether the addressed Prophet stands for the wider community.
- 18:22/q19/p1 'About them' refers to the Cave Companions; 'from them' refers to Jews or other People of Scripture being questioned. — Mujahid and Yahya name Jews; Tabari, Ibn Abbas and others broaden to People of the Book. Tha'lab and Mubarrad note the deliberate pronoun shift, despite ordinary expectation of a common antecedent. Qurtubi and Ibn Atiyya say the omitted topic is their number; some identify Najran Christians specifically. [21 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN(ثعلب والمبرد); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN(ثعلب); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 18:22/q19/p2 The prohibition addresses the Prophet in form but is meant for his community. — Mawardi proposes this; Tabrisi reasons the Prophet already trusted God's report and would not turn to Jewish questioning. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI]
- 18:22/q19/p3 'From them' cannot mean People of Scripture in the Meccan setting; it refers to the local challengers. — Ibn Ashur argues that commentators and transmitters agree the sura is Meccan. He takes the prohibition as implying either that Meccan questioners did not know the details or that the Prophet already had divine knowledge and need not satisfy them, preferring the latter. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 18:22/q20 Why forbid consulting them about the youths?  [وَلَا تَسْتَفْتِ فِيهِم مِّنْهُمْ أَحَدًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the prohibition covers every disputant and whether it rests on ignorance, sufficiency of revelation, or conversational manners.
- 18:22/q20/p1 Revelation supplies what matters, while disputants lack reliable knowledge; neither seeking instruction nor trying to trap them is needed. — Tabari says their counts are guesses; Zajjaj, Tha'labi and Bagawi say the account suffices. Kashshaf and Abu Hayyan add that baiting them conflicts with gracious debate; Ibn Kathir says divine truth judges prior books. Ibn Ashur prefers the inference that the Prophet already knows from God and need not satisfy challengers. Alusi says do not adopt even the correct count merely on a disputant's authority. [25 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNHISHAM; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 18:22/q20/p2 The mistaken factions alone are not to be consulted. — Alusi reports this restriction but finds it contrary to the apparent wording. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:22/q21 What wider rule about knowledge or legal opinion is drawn from the prohibition?  [رَجْمًۢا بِٱلْغَيْبِ وَلَا تَسْتَفْتِ · law]
turns on: Whether the verse's rebuke of unsupported claims applies generally to transmitted knowledge or conjectural legal reasoning.
- 18:22/q21/p1 Some infer Muslims should not seek religious rulings or knowledge from Jews and Christians. — Wahidi's associates infer a prohibition on religious rulings; Jishumi says reports of unbelievers are unreliable; Qurtubi sees the Najran story as proof against consultation on knowledge. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أصحابنا); JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 18:22/q21/p2 Opponents of analogical legal reasoning cite condemnation of guessing as forbidding conjectural fatwas. — Razi reports their use of 'rajman bil-ghayb' and a Kashshaf poetry line where rajm means mere supposition; he presents it as their argument, not his own verdict. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف)]

## 18:22/q22 What inward lesson is drawn from the youths and their dog?  [سَبْعَةٌۭ وَثَامِنُهُمْ كَلْبُهُمْ مَّا يَعْلَمُهُمْ إِلَّا قَلِيلٌۭ · inward]
turns on: Which elements of the surface story serve as symbols: cognitive faculties, saints, companionship, or divine knowledge.
- 18:22/q22/p1 The seven youths represent faculties of cognition and action; the dog represents the lower self. — Qashani maps seven to two intellects, thought, estimation, imagination, memory and common sense; a related royal-adviser report maps the king to the commanding self, right advisers to intellect and thought, left advisers to imagination, estimation and memory, and shepherd to common sense. The three and five counts select progressively fewer faculties. [3 notes: QASHANI]
- 18:22/q22/p2 The youths are seven abdal sustaining seven climes; the cave is retreat and the dog the animal soul. — Bursevi reports Kashifi's esoteric explanation. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 18:22/q22/p3 God hides His saints, and only intimate knowers can recognize or speak truly of their state. — Qushayri says God's protective jealousy conceals them; Sufi masters liken their company to one household; a heart without love cannot remember them or be consulted about them. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI(شيوخ هذه الطائفة)]
- 18:22/q22/p4 The dog's mention exemplifies the fortune of accompanying the beloved youths. — Qushayri notes that it is repeatedly mentioned and counted with them; Taybi says even the dog is rhetorically joined to their blessed company, but Alusi rejects its elevation to rational beings as poetic fantasy. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 18:22/q22/p5 The rival three and five counts contrast with God's certain presence and knowledge among groups of that size. — Qushayri connects this verse with God's being fourth of three and sixth of five in 58:7. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 18:22/q23 What other events around the cave and the challenge are reported?  [* · reports]
turns on: Different narrative reports and the reliability or benefit of later name traditions.
- 18:22/q23/p1 Jewish scholars supplied Meccan challengers with questions about the youths, a world traveler, and the spirit. — Abu Hayyan names al-Nadr and Uqba and gives competing formulations of the three-question test, connected with 18:83 and 17:85. Kuranyolu uses this occasion as tentative evidence for a pre-Christian dating. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 18:22/q23/p2 At discovery, people debated the youths' condition and whether to build a structure or mosque. — Tabrisi reports the ruler and people finding the youths dead, polytheists wanting a concealing structure, uncertainty over whether they slept or died, and rival identifications of those who prevailed. The mosque signifies believers' victory or, for Hasan, a place for the youths if they awoke. Another report has God restore their former state and divert seekers from the cave. [9 notes: TABRISI(ابن عباس,الجبائي,الحسن,مشركو ذلك الوقت); TABRISI+]
- 18:22/q23/p3 The youths' names were written down in historical accounts and later assigned protective uses. — Mawardi says their people recorded the names and later inscribed them at the cave. Abu Abd al-Rahman reports a fire-calming use; Bursevi transmits purported benefits for healing, travel, childbirth, crops and access to rulers. Alusi calls such claims unfounded money-making fabrications; Suyuti says the dog's name, color and similar details have no practical use. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو عبد الرحمن); BURSEVI(النيسابوري عن ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN]

## 18:22/q24 How does this passage connect to the sura's message and adjacent verses?  [* · links]
- 18:22/q24/p1 The cave account directs attention to revelation's guidance and resurrection rather than unprofitable detail. — Abu Hayyan connects the sura's opening praise and upright warning to the prior sura's true revelation at 17:105. Asad links outward discussion to 18:21's ethical lesson; Kuranyolu emphasizes resurrection and believers' eventual victory. Islahi calls 18:22-24 a parenthetical instruction and says the text then resumes the earlier discussion. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 18:22/q25 What other recitation and textual observations accompany this verse?  [* · readings]
turns on: Different ancillary observations about recitation transmission, rhythm and numerical order.
- 18:22/q25/p1 The word 'tumāri' has transmitted imāla and fatḥ routes. — Nashr records Abu Uthman al-Darir's imāla routes, consistent in report and performance, and Jafar al-Nasibi's fatḥ route without reported variation. [2 notes: NASHR(أبو عثمان الضرير,جعفر بن محمد النصيبي)]
- 18:22/q25/p2 Ibn Masud's reading dominated early Kufa before Uthman's standardized reading was taught there. — Ibn Mujahid cites Ibrahim's list of teachers, Masruq on Ibn Masud teaching in the mosque, A'mash on later reversal of rarity, and Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami's forty years teaching the standardized reading until Bishr ibn Marwan's governorship, dated 73 AH. [7 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو إسحاق,إبراهيم,الأعمش,مسروق)]
- 18:22/q25/p3 The verse can appear as metrical coincidence within Quranic prose. — Zarkashi groups its dispute phrase with examples on night worship, charity, flood, Abu Lahab, victory, conversion and Qarun. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 18:22/q25/p4 The numeric sequences move naturally from a rank to its next higher rank. — Zarkashi compares three/four and five/six here with 4:3 and 58:7. [1 notes: BURHAN]
