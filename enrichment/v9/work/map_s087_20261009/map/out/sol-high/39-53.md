# 39:53: 30 questions

## 39:53/q01 How does this verse turn from the surrounding warnings to its invitation?  [قُلْ · links]
- 39:53/q01/p1 After warnings of death, loss, and punishment, God turns through the Prophet to a tender invitation that restores hope while retaining the call to repent and submit. — Biqaʿi says prolonged threats risk alienation; Abu Hayyan says the preceding threat and possible ransom prepare hope and fear; Ibn ʿAshur calls the vocative a fresh opening; al-Tabatabaʾi reads seven connected verses calling to Islam, submission and righteous action; Study Quran points to the desperate death passage; Islahi says the section opposes reliance on idols and intercessors and urges turning before regret. [9 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 39:53/q02 How is the final yāʾ of يَٰعِبَادِىَ read and written?  [يَٰعِبَادِىَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the possessive yāʾ is vocalized with fatḥa, sukūn, or omitted at pause; transmission lists vary.
- 39:53/q02/p1 The possessive yāʾ is pronounced with fatḥa in the connected reading. — Ibn Mujahid names Ibn Kathir, Nafiʿ, ʿAsim and Ibn ʿAmir, with an Abu ʿAmr transmission; Jishumi includes Abu Jaʿfar. Al-Nashr names the Medinans, Ibn Kathir, Abu ʿAmr and ʿAsim for this phrase. Transmission lists differ. Samarraʾi connects the full form to the large number of sinners, citing 12:103, 34:13 and 6:116. [10 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير,ابن كثير، نافع، ابن عامر، عاصم، أبو زيد عن أبي عمرو، حمزة، الكسائي، أبو عمرو,ابن كثير، نافع، عاصم، ابن عامر، أبو عمرو، حمزة، الكسائي,عاصم وابن عامر,نافع); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ونافع وابن كثير وابن عامر وعاصم); RAZI-FULL(نافع وابن كثير وابن عامر وعاصم); NASHR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 39:53/q02/p2 The yāʾ is quiescent in the connected reading. — Ibn Mujahid names Abu ʿAmr, Hamza and al-Kisaʾi, with differing Abu ʿAmr transmissions; Hamza opens none of his five listed yāʾs. Jishumi adds Yaʿqub and some ʿAsim transmissions; Nasafi names the Basran, Hamza and ʿAli readings. Al-Nashr also records Ibn ʿAmir, al-Kisaʾi and Rawh for a related phrase and compares 29:56; Qiraat-ER notes a different pause and cadence without changed reference. [11 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير، نافع، ابن عامر، عاصم، أبو زيد عن أبي عمرو، حمزة، الكسائي، أبو عمرو,ابن كثير، نافع، عاصم، ابن عامر، أبو عمرو، حمزة، الكسائي,حمزة); JISHUMI(أبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي ويعقوب); RAZI-FULL(نافع وابن كثير وابن عامر وعاصم); NASAFI-FULL(بصرى وحمزة وعلى); NASHR; QIRAAT-ER(Y-iḍ)]
- 39:53/q02/p3 The yāʾ is normally retained in pause, but Abu Bakr from ʿAsim omits it; vocative usage also permits omission in writing. — Jishumi says all retain the written yāʾ in pause except Abu Bakr; Razi repeats this. Nahhas and Qurtubi explain deletion as a property of vocatives; Zarkashi surveys omission, sukūn, fatḥa and substitution by alif. [5 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN]

## 39:53/q03 Who are the servants addressed by يَٰعِبَادِىَ?  [يَٰعِبَادِىَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the possessive ʿibād conventionally confines the address to believers, and whether the following verses show an initial call to polytheists or allow a universal address.
- 39:53/q03/p1 The address extends to every sinner, believer or polytheist; reported occasions do not limit its general wording. — Tabari expressly prefers the general wording over particular occasions; Ibn ʿAtiyya extends it to Resurrection; Razi, Baydawi and Bursevi distinguish general text from particular occasion. Bursevi cites 17:5 against a believers-only rule. Tabatabaʾi points to the following denial and arrogance at 39:59; Study Quran says all humanity. Al-Qurazi says all people. The Prophet is reported to have said the Wahshi promise applies generally; Tabatabaʾi regards application to the Imams' Shiʿa as an instance, not a restriction. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(القرظي); THALABI(وحشي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI(أئمة أهل البيت); STUDYQURAN; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; TAB-FULL+]
- 39:53/q03/p2 The first addressees are polytheists, especially Meccans with pre-Islamic sins. — Zayd b. Aslam and Ibn ʿAbbas specify Meccan idolaters; Ibn ʿAshur invokes the next call to submit and later references to denial and arrogance (39:54, 56, 59), and judges other occasion reports weak or unknown. Tabatabaʾi finds the polytheist reading more plausible than believer-only; al-Burhan says the gentle call encourages embracing Islam. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض أهل التأويل,زيد بن أسلم); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN; STUDYQURAN(many commentators); TABATABAI(ابن عباس)+]
- 39:53/q03/p3 The possessive address refers to sinful believers. — Baydawi, Burhan and reported views in Bursevi, Alusi and Tabatabaʾi invoke Qurʾanic use of God's possessive ʿibād, including 76:6. Razi says the address answers believers' prayer at 3:193 and 40:60, whereas unbelievers did not pray; Tabari says obligations also address believers. Reports connect it to persecuted Muslims and to people who thought major sinners damned. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURHAN; BURSEVI(البيضاوي ومن تبعه); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(جمع); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(a third opinion); BURSEVI-; TABATABAI(ابن عباس)-]

## 39:53/q04 What does calling sinners 'My servants' accomplish?  [يَٰعِبَادِىَ · rhetoric]
- 39:53/q04/p1 The intimate possessive and direct call honor sinners, invite them toward God, and awaken hope and compassion. — Razi says servanthood evokes need and poverty and possession reassures; Baydawi and Abu Hayyan connect it to self-inflicted harm; Samīn notes direct attention and honor; Biqaʿi calls it the sweetness of belonging. Qushayri says it lifts sinners' heads and lowers the self-assured obedient. Kashf al-Asrar contrasts human love with angelic servitude and says God veils particular faults. Ibn ʿAshur says it prepares acceptance; Suyuti calls the mode tender; Tabatabaʾi recalls lordship and the duty of obedience. Burhan gives an inward progression from Islam and obedience to excellence, comparing 29:56. [16 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(القشيري); BURSEVI(القشيري); IBNASHUR-FULL; ITQAN; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL; BURHAN]

## 39:53/q05 What can عِبَاد mean?  [عِبَادِىَ · meaning]
- 39:53/q05/p1 It denotes servants or slaves, including God's servants and household slaves. — Damghani compares 43:15 and 24:32; Junayd defines true servanthood as turning from distractions to worship. [2 notes: DAMGHANI; SULAMI(الجنيد)]

## 39:53/q06 What does أَسْرَفُوا۟ mean here?  [أَسْرَفُوا۟ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term is explained by its general sense of exceeding limits or specified by the serious sins at issue.
- 39:53/q06/p1 It means crossing the proper limit in any action, here excessive sin and wrongdoing. — Al-Raghib, cited by Bursevi and Tabatabaʾi, says excessive spending is the common use but the root applies to any action; Alusi approves that breadth while reporting a two-step figurative derivation from spending; Elmalili and Damghani agree. Biqaʿi says transgressors put things out of place and burden themselves. Tabatabaʾi includes shirk and major and minor sins; Study Quran notes pre-Islamic killing and cites 3:154, 5:50, 33:33, 48:26 and 10:12. [14 notes: DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(الراغب); BIQAI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABRISI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:53/q06/p2 It especially denotes grave sins and indecencies. — Muqatil names shirk, murder and adultery, calling shirk the greatest excess; Mujahid specifies pre-Islamic killing. Mawrdi separately allows sins while retaining faith. Thalabi, Wahidi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Ibn ʿUmar and Bursevi name major sins or shameful acts. [10 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عمر); BURSEVI]

## 39:53/q07 How does عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ work grammatically?  [عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ · grammar]
turns on: Which implied verbal sense governs ʿalā: doing injury or bearing a burden.
- 39:53/q07/p1 The preposition ʿalā supplies the sense of committing an offense against oneself. — Bursevi, Alusi and Elmalili explain the verb's construction by an incorporated sense of injury; Alusi says this need not be literal and also reports the alternative sense of bearing a burden. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 39:53/q07/p2 The construction can evoke bearing a burden upon oneself. — Alusi reports this as another explanation of the preposition. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:53/q08 Why say that sinners transgressed against themselves?  [عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the self-directed wording only evokes compassion or also limits the promise where another person's right is involved.
- 39:53/q08/p1 Their sins injure and burden their own souls, a reminder that invites divine compassion. — Razi and Alusi argue self-inflicted harm is already sufficient injury; Baydawi links the wording to compassion; Abu Hayyan and Kuran Yolu say sin first soils the sinner; Biqaʿi describes removal from divine presence. Qushayri hears renewed hope even for a repeated returner. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(القشيري); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:53/q08/p2 Wrongs against other people require discharge of the injured person's claim before forgiveness. — Biqaʿi infers this from the wording's restriction to transgression against oneself. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 39:53/q09 How is the nūn of تَقْنَطُوا۟ read?  [تَقْنَطُوا۟ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted stem vowel and dialect pattern of qaniṭa; the prohibition's sense remains the same.
- 39:53/q09/p1 It is read with fatḥa: taqnaṭū. — Thalabi says the other readers use it; Jishumi, Ibn ʿAtiyya and Razi call it the majority reading and a dialect form. Ibn ʿAtiyya reports Abu Hatim's objection that its principle would predict an unattested kasra in 42:28. Qurtubi compares 15:56. [6 notes: THALABI(الآخرون); JISHUMI(الباقون); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(أبو عمرو والكسائي); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 39:53/q09/p2 It is read with kasra: taqniṭū. — Thalabi names Abu ʿAmr, al-Aʿmash, Yahya b. Waththab, ʿIsa, al-Kisaʾi and Yaʿqub; Jishumi names Abu ʿAmr, al-Kisaʾi and Yaʿqub; Ibn ʿAtiyya names Abu ʿAmr and Ibn Waththab; Nasafi names ʿAli and Basrans. Jishumi gives qaniṭa yaqniṭu beside qaniṭa yaqnaṭu; Qiraat-ER says the vowel changes sound, not sense. [10 notes: THALABI(أبو عمرو والأعمش ويحيى بن وثاب وعيسى والكسائي ويعقوب); JISHUMI(أبو عمرو والكسائي ويعقوب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو وابن وثاب); RAZI-FULL(أبو عمرو والكسائي); NASAFI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QIRAAT-ER(vw)]
- 39:53/q09/p3 It is read with ḍamma: taqnuṭū. — Ashhab al-ʿUqayli is named by Thalabi, Jishumi and Ibn ʿAtiyya; Kashshaf also records the three vowels. [4 notes: THALABI(أشهب العقيلي); JISHUMI(أشهب العقيلي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأشهب العقيلي); KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 39:53/q10 What does تَقْنَطُوا۟ mean?  [تَقْنَطُوا۟ · meaning]
- 39:53/q10/p1 It means to despair or lose hope of good, especially God's mercy. — Ibn ʿAbbas glosses it as losing hope; Zajjaj, Mawrdi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Jishumi, Nasafi, Biqaʿi, Tabatabaʾi and al-Mufradat agree. Ibn ʿAtiyya and Bursevi call qunūṭ the severest despair; Jishumi contrasts despair's certainty with hope's possibility. [12 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(المفردات); TABATABAI]

## 39:53/q11 What does the ban on despair teach about responding to sin?  [لَا تَقْنَطُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: The first position addresses hope and repentance; the second diagnoses despair's role in seeking intermediaries.
- 39:53/q11/p1 Even severe sinners should hope in God, repent promptly, and avoid preaching that makes others despair. — Razi and Abu Hayyan infer a command to hope; Biqaʿi says despair blocks repentance. Ibn Masʿud repeatedly rebukes a preacher dwelling on Hell; Ali defines a sound jurist as neither causing despair nor excusing sin, and says learning requires reflection. ʿAʾisha warns preachers, ʿAtaʾ threatens those who cause despair, and Zayd's devout man is condemned for doing so. Ibn ʿAbbas says despairing people of repentance denies God's Book yet repentance needs God's enabling. Elmalili says hope urges immediate repentance rather than more sin; the report about a despairing believer is reflected in prophetic warnings. [24 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TAB-FULL(عبد الله); THALABI(ابن مسعود,زيد بن أسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن عمر,ابن مسعود,زيد بن أسلم,عائشة,عطاء بن يسار,علي بن أبي طالب); BURSEVI(ابن مسعود); ELMALILI; MUSNAD-AHMAD(رسول الله); IBNHIBBAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:53/q11/p2 Despair and distrust of mercy can lead people to seek idols or alleged intercessors instead of turning directly to God. — Islahi identifies bad assumptions about God as a cause of polytheism and innovation; imagined divine favorites arise from believing ordinary people cannot approach God. He urges sincere turning to God alone before the chance passes. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 39:53/q12 What is the mercy of God from which they must not despair?  [رَّحْمَةِ ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether forgiveness is contained in mercy or supplied through a strained implied parallel clause.
- 39:53/q12/p1 It includes forgiveness first, then God's further beneficence and favor. — Baydawi and Bursevi place forgiveness before further favor; Bursevi says forgiveness shields from punishment and mercy then gives. Alusi says forgiveness is included in or necessarily implied by mercy; Tabatabaʾi calls it afterlife mercy, immediately forgiveness for repentant sinners. Tustari stresses grace even near the final breath. Bursevi cites the hundred portions of mercy report, including the mare sparing her foal and ninety-nine portions retained by God; a report of God rescuing a man who invoked Him as Compassionate illustrates its reach. [8 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(أبو هريرة رضي الله عنه); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; TUSTARI; DURR-FULL]
- 39:53/q12/p2 A proposed ellipsis coordinates mercy and forgiveness in both the ban and the reason for it. — Alusi reports a proposed omitted-symmetry analysis, 'do not despair of mercy and forgiveness, for God forgives and shows mercy,' but considers it strained. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:53/q13 Why say رَّحْمَةِ ٱللَّهِ rather than 'My mercy'?  [رَّحْمَةِ ٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
- 39:53/q13/p1 The shift to God's name magnifies the scope and grounds of mercy. — Razi, Abu Hayyan, Biqaʿi and Alusi invoke the supreme name and its encompassing attributes; Samīn notes the shift from first to third person and the explicit name. Tabatabaʾi links it to the beautiful names: He forgives because He is God. Alusi treats the name as an indication that even unrepentant sinners can be covered. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 39:53/q14 What does forgiveness mean in the promise?  [يَغْفِرُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the note explains the act of pardon itself or its possible timing.
- 39:53/q14/p1 It is overlooking sin and its punishment, either concealing it or erasing it from the record. — Alusi reports both analyses. Biqaʿi says God can erase sins and traces without blame or punishment and then honor the forgiven; Bursevi distinguishes pardon that erases sin, citing 11:114, from forgiveness that can turn bad deeds into good, citing 25:70. Gabriel tells Abraham a forgiven sin can become a good deed. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الشيخ الكبير); TUSTARI(جبريل)]
- 39:53/q14/p2 Forgiveness can occur at once or after punishment and remains God's choice. — Razi says sinners cannot know that punishment is entirely removed; Baydawi says pardon can follow delay; Bursevi says even innumerable sins may be forgiven by pardon or after punishment, as God wills. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 39:53/q15 Which sins and sinners can receive the forgiveness promised here?  [إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَغْفِرُ ٱلذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًا · theology]
turns on: Whether repentance is required or merely one route to forgiveness, how 4:48/4:116 qualifies this verse, and whether pardon of the penitent is certain.
- 39:53/q15/p1 All sins, including shirk, are forgiven when their doer repents and turns to God; unrepented shirk remains excluded. — Muqatil cites 25:68–70; Tabari cites 4:48 and 19:60 and allows other sins by will. Zamakhshari treats the Qur'an as one coherent discourse whose stated repentance condition governs unstated cases; Abu Hayyan identifies and disputes this Muʿtazilite restriction. Ibn Kathir cites 9:104, 4:110, 4:145–46, 5:73–74 and 85:10, and says dying in shirk rules out an unconditioned reading; Qurtubi cites 20:82. Tabatabaʾi reads the seven verses together, rejects intercession here because it excludes shirk, and distinguishes 4:48 as a different case. Asad cites 6:54 and 4:110; Study Quran cites 4:48 and 4:116. Thawban's inclusive answer is reported repeatedly, although Ahmad 22362 and Tabatabaʾi 39:53–61 transmit the opposite answer. [32 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ(التفسير); NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جعفر النحاس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; MUSNAD-AHMAD(ثوبان); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; BAYDAWI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Ehl-i sünnet âlimleri)-]
- 39:53/q15/p2 Apart from unrepented shirk, God may forgive even unrepentant sins at His discretion. — Baydawi and Alusi say restricting the apparent promise to repentance narrows its wording; 4:48 reserves lesser sins to God's will. Tabari distinguishes grace and just requital; Ibn ʿAtiyya calls the restriction consensus; Bursevi cites concealed sins forgiven on Judgment Day, reads 4:17 as excluding last-gasp repentance and says idolatry without repentance violates wisdom. Alusi gives seventeen textual indications: servant address and possession, self-harm, unlimited ban on despair, divine name and its repetition, causal inna, definite sins and all, final cause, intensive and objectless ghafur, exclusivity and mercy after forgiveness. Kuran Yolu makes 'if He wills' explicit in translation and warns against an unrestricted guarantee for every person. [39 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Ehl-i sünnet âlimleri); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 39:53/q15/p3 All sins are forgiven through repentance alone; an unrepentant grave sinner receives no such pardon. — Zamakhshari and the Muʿtazilites argue that the Qur'an is a coherent discourse, so repentance conditions stated elsewhere qualify this verse; Alusi says they deny forgiveness of grave sins without repentance and see no possibility of abrogation. Abu Hayyan identifies this as their disputed doctrine. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(المعتزلة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 39:53/q15/p4 The promise concerns minor sins when major sins are avoided, or repentants and people with minor but no major sins. — Mawrdi reports forgiveness of minor sins upon avoiding major ones; Nahhas and Qurtubi identify repentants or minor sinners, citing the next command in 39:54. Tabatabaʾi reports a different restriction to minor sins after excluding threatened major sins and shirk. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جعفر النحاس); TABATABAI(قوم)]
- 39:53/q15/p5 Every sin is eligible for cause-free forgiveness except shirk. — Tabatabaʾi reports this inference from 4:116, but argues forgiveness has causes such as repentance or intercession and that this context requires repentance. Kuran Yolu rejects a guaranteed pardon irrespective of faith or turning as encouraging moral laxity. [1 notes: TABATABAI(آخرون); TABATABAI-; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 39:53/q15/p6 God's pardon is guaranteed to the penitent. — One Sunni group in Ibn ʿAtiyya reads the apparent Qur'anic promise as certain; Qurtubi cites 20:82. A different reported group leaves even the penitent subject to divine will, albeit with predominant hope. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة من أهل السنة); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 39:53/q15/p7 Even a penitent's forgiveness remains subject to God's will, though hope predominates. — Ibn ʿAtiyya reports this alternative and contrasts fear for the unrepentant sinner. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]

## 39:53/q16 How is جَمِيعًا construed?  [جَمِيعًا · grammar]
- 39:53/q16/p1 It is accusative as a circumstantial expression. — Ibn ʿAtiyya gives the iʿrāb. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 39:53/q17 Why add جَمِيعًا to 'the sins'?  [ٱلذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًا · rhetoric]
- 39:53/q17/p1 The definite noun and 'all' emphasize comprehensive forgiveness, however many or grave the sins. — Razi, Baydawi, Biqaʿi and Bursevi stress the reinforced totality; Bursevi notes the definite article; Ibn ʿAshur says the closing divine names remove the objection from sheer number. Alusi reads the definite noun and all as evidence for including unrepented sins; al-Akhfash, cited by Zarkashi, allows an affirmative extra min in another wording to preserve the same 'all sins' sense. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURHAN(الأخفش)]

## 39:53/q18 What variant additions to the forgiveness clause are reported?  [إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَغْفِرُ ٱلذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًا · readings]
turns on: The wording and attribution of supplementary recitations, and whether they function as explanatory glosses.
- 39:53/q18/p1 A reading adds 'for whom He wills' (لمن يشاء). — Farra attributes it to Ibn ʿAbbas in ʿAbd Allah's codex; Tabari, Nahhas, Thalabi, Ibn ʿAtiyya, Tabrisi and Qurtubi associate it with Ibn Masʿud/ʿAbd Allah; Razi and Kashshaf also name Ibn ʿAbbas. Nahhas calls it an explanatory gloss; Zamakhshari interprets the will as the will to forgive penitents under wisdom and justice. ## 39:53/q27 How do the unattached grammatical notes bear on the verse's wording?  [* · grammar]
- 39:53/q27/p1 The notes give general analyses of ellipsis, exception particles, predication, rebuke, and lamentation without stating an application to this verse. — Sibawayh analyzes implicit baʿḍ and adjectival versus verbal exception structures, including laysa, lā yakūn, ʿadā, khalā, mā, ḥāshā and sawāk; al-Khalil, Yunus and others differ. Another passage analyzes noun-like comparatives and ḥasbuk, nominative and genitive patterns, emphatic bāʾ, and Arab usage. A third covers accusative elliptical rebukes, a Banu Asad warning, poetry, verb replacement and nominative predicates, with ʿIsa allowing a present-state reading in Farazdaq's oath. A fourth covers the lamentation alif on adjectives, constructs and compounds: al-Khalil rejects Yunus's adjective ending. None of these notes supplies a grammatical claim about the present verse. [31 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل,بعض العرب,عيسى,يونس)]
