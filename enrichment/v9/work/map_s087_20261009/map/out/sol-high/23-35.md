# 23:35: 14 questions

## 23:35/q01 What does the chiefs' question convey?  [أَيَعِدُكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 23:35/q01/p1 It voices astonishment and scorn at a resurrection promise they consider impossible. — Yaḥyā says they mock resurrection after decay; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it a challenge presenting the promise as implausible. Rāzī says their subsequent 'how far-fetched' intensifies the denial. Ibn Kathīr includes their denial of meeting God and bodily resurrection; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the question shifts their attack from the messenger's claim to his message. Elmâlılı reports their claim that worldly life alone exists and generations replace one another. [10 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 23:35/q02 Who voices the question?  [أَيَعِدُكُمْ · referent]
- 23:35/q02/p1 Some chiefs address one another; they belong to the affluent opponents of the prophet. — Yaḥyā identifies the exchange among chiefs. Kur'an Yolu connects opposition to prophets with wealthy elites content with an advantageous social order and afraid of afterlife accountability and universal rights; Study Quran notes recurring affluent opposition throughout the surah, comparing 7:59–62 and 7:136. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 23:35/q03 Who is the one promising them?  [أَيَعِدُكُمْ · referent]
- 23:35/q03/p1 Their messenger, the prophet, promises resurrection. — Muqātil names Hūd; Ṭabrisī says the messenger. Bursevî says they ask whether the prophet makes this promise, and Asad says they dismiss the messenger as merely human while rejecting it. [4 notes: MUQATIL; TABRISI; BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 23:35/q04 How is the promised content governed by يَعِدُكُمْ?  [يَعِدُكُمْ أَنَّكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether وعد requires an understood bāʾ before its second object, or takes that object directly.
- 23:35/q04/p1 The first أنكم is an object of وعد, directly in the accusative. — Abū ʿAlī argues وعد takes two direct objects, citing 48:20, 20:80, 7:142, 48:29, and 9:114; therefore no omitted preposition is needed. Sibawayh's analysis as reported by Qurṭubī and Ṭabrisī also makes this clause the promised object. Samīn notes accusative use without a preposition is permitted. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(سيبويه); SAMIN-DURR]
- 23:35/q04/p2 The object is construed with an understood preposition, بأنكم, which may be omitted. — Abū Isḥāq conventionally supplies bāʾ; Ālūsī likewise allows an implicit preposition. Samīn says the clause may be genitive after deletion of its original preposition. Abū ʿAlī counters that Qurʾānic uses of وعد make the preposition unnecessary. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي)-]

## 23:35/q05 Can the particles after يَعِدُكُمْ be read with kasra?  [يَعِدُكُمْ أَنَّكُمْ · readings]
- 23:35/q05/p1 Both particles may be read إن, with kasra, if يَعِدُكُمْ means 'does he say to you.' — Qurṭubī reports Abū Isḥāq's allowance of this reading on the basis of the verb's possible sense 'say.' [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو إسحاق)]

## 23:35/q06 How is the mīm of مِتُّمْ read?  [مِتُّمْ · readings]
turns on: The vocalization of the mīm and corresponding verbal form.
- 23:35/q06/p1 It is read with kasra: mittum. — Nasafī assigns kasra to Nāfiʿ, Ḥamza, ʿAlī, and Ḥafṣ. Bursevî and Ālūsī connect it with māta–yamātu; Qiraat-Er calls it the standard canonical Form I perfect of m-w-t. [4 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER(canonical)]
- 23:35/q06/p2 It is read with ḍamma: muttum. — Nasafī assigns ḍamma to the other readers; Bursevî and Ālūsī derive it from māta–yamūtu. Qiraat-Er calls it an accepted canonical alternative from the same root. [4 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER(vw)]

## 23:35/q07 What physical condition do تُرَابًا وَعِظَامًا describe?  [تُرَابًا وَعِظَامًا · interpretation]
- 23:35/q07/p1 After death the body decays into dust and exposed bones stripped of flesh. — Thaʿlabī says flesh disappears; Bayḍāwī mentions flesh and sinews. Biqāʿī first describes souls leaving bodies, then flesh becoming dust and bare bones remaining. Bursevî and Ālūsī likewise describe bare or decayed bones. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the deniers regard these states as incompatible with restoration of bodily life. [6 notes: THALABI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:35/q08 Why are dust and bones mentioned in that order?  [تُرَابًا وَعِظَامًا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the order reflects difficulty of restoration, generations at different stages, or no chronological force in wāw.
- 23:35/q08/p1 Dust comes first because restoration from dust seems especially implausible; visible flesh becomes dust while bones remain. — Bursevî and Ālūsī give this explanation; Ālūsī specifies that flesh and similar visible parts turn to dust while others remain bare bones. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 23:35/q08/p2 Earlier generations are dust, while later generations are bones. — Bursevî and Ālūsī offer this as another reason for dust's precedence. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 23:35/q08/p3 They become bones and then dust; wāw does not impose a chronological order. — Bursevî explicitly prefers this sequence and argues that the conjunction merely joins the two states. [1 notes: BURSEVI+]

## 23:35/q09 How does the second أَنَّكُم relate syntactically to the first?  [أَنَّكُمْ إِذَا مِتُّمْ وَكُنتُمْ تُرَابًا وَعِظَامًا أَنَّكُم مُّخْرَجُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the second clause substitutes for or emphasizes the first, depends on إذا, forms a nominal predicate, or requires an omitted event or predicate; critics dispute whether the first clause can stand before its predicate and what may govern إذا.
- 23:35/q09/p1 The second أنكم is an appositional substitute for the first, whose omitted predicate concerns extraction or resurrection. — Sibawayh says the first clause is advanced to announce the event before resurrection; parallel prose and Ibn Muqbil's line put a conditional before the second أن, which supplies the promised action. He says إن cannot independently start here. Wāḥidī reports an implied resurrection predicate; defenders supply إخراجكم so a time phrase predicates an event, not persons. Ṭabrisī compares 9:63 and 6:54; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Samīn, Ālūsī, and Biqāʿī explain the implied predicate. Burhān says an omitted 'intending' would complete the first clause. Abū ʿAlī and al-Mubarrad object that the first أن has no complete, stated predicate before إذا; Abū ʿAlī adds that a time clause cannot predicate the addressed people, and 9:63 has the same difficulty. [17 notes: SIBAWAYH(ابن مقبل); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي,سيبويه); IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(سيبويه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سيبويه); SAMIN-DURR(سيبويه); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(سيبويه); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي)-; IBNATIYYA-FULL(المبرد)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المبرد)-; ALUSI-FULL(المبرد)-]
- 23:35/q09/p2 The second أنكم repeats the first for emphasis after a long intervening time phrase; مخرجون predicates the first. — ʿAbd Allāh says separation by إذا makes repetition well formed; Zajjāj, Thaʿlabī, Jishumī (comparing 4:14), Baghawī (comparing 9:63), and Ibn al-Jawzī cite length. Abū al-ʿAbbās calls it the best account; al-Farrāʾ, al-Jarmī, and al-Mubarrad adopt it. Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Nasafī, and others make مخرجون the predicate; Ālūsī prefers this as best suited to word order and reports al-Sakhāwī's answer to Abū Nizār: attaching إذا to مخرجون need not put emergence at the instant of death. Burhān says repetition guards against forgetting and cites repeated pronouns and clauses; al-Ṣaffār answers Kūfan opposition with transmitted pronouns, لما, and أنكم (41:7, 11:108, 2:89). Itqān lists repetition of nouns, clauses, verbs, imperative nouns, and particles. Abū ʿAlī objects that أن cannot be repeated without its clause, although he finds this closer than apposition; Abū Ḥayyān disputes making the conditional an apodosis without initial fāʾ under Basran grammar, while al-Farrāʾ allows it. [33 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو العباس,أبو علي الفارسي,عبد الله); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه); TABRISI(أبو عمرو الجرمي، المبرد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء والجرمي وأبو العباس المبرد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء والجرمي والمبرد,المبرد); SAMIN-DURR(الجرمي والمبرد والفراء); BURHAN(الكوفيون); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الفراء والجرمي والمبرد); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجرمي والمبرد); ALUSI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي)-]
- 23:35/q09/p3 The second أنكم is raised by إذا; the adverbial construction predicates the first أنكم. — Abū al-Ḥasan proposes this and Abū ʿAlī chooses it against apposition and repetition. Abū ʿAlī says the clause from إذا through مخرجون is the first clause's predicate, expressing resurrection at death; Ṭabrisī develops an implicit event such as 'happens' or 'is,' so 'when you die, your extraction happens.' Extraction itself cannot govern إذا without leaving the sentence incomplete; Ṭabrisī compares al-Farazdaq's line, analyzed by Abū ʿUthmān with an implicit adverb governing 'their like,' and points to 9:63 and 6:54. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو الحسن,أبو علي الفارسي); TABRISI(أبو عثمان); TABRISI(أبو الحسن)+; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي)+]
- 23:35/q09/p4 The second أنكم مخرجون is a nominal subject with إذا as its fronted predicate; the entire clause predicates the first أنكم. — Bayḍāwī includes the nominal-clause parse; Samīn states it explicitly. Abū Ḥayyān prefers al-Zamakhsharī's version as straightforward and free of strain. Ālūsī says al-Baḥr calls it easy and al-Sakhāwī attributes it to al-Mubarrad; Ibn ʿĀshūr also allows it. Samīn says مخرجون itself cannot govern the preceding إذا because what is governed by أن cannot govern material before it, nor can متم as a genitive complement of a muḍāf. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 23:35/q09/p5 The second أنكم مخرجون is a verbal noun raised by an omitted verb, which serves as the conditional answer; the conditional construction predicates the first أنكم. — Jishumī reports an understood إخراجكم. Ṭabrisī permits مخرجون to occupy the conditional answer position with an implicit event governing إذا. Al-Akhfash's omitted 'occurs' appears in Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Ālūsī; al-Zamakhsharī proposes the same outcome. Samīn distinguishes this as his third analysis. [8 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الأخفش)]
- 23:35/q09/p6 An omitted verb raises the second أنكم مخرجون and itself predicates the first أنكم while governing إذا. — Al-Akhfash's alternative, reported by Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī, makes the omitted verb both predicate and governor of the adverb; Samīn records it as his fourth analysis. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الأخفش)]
- 23:35/q09/p7 An omitted noun annexed to the address pronoun underlies the first أنكم; its predicate is the إذا clause, and the repeated particle clarifies the omission. — Samīn gives this as his first analysis, distinguishing it from the account with an omitted predicate. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 23:35/q09/p8 The second أن may be an uncounted extra particle. — Abū ʿAlī lists this theoretical analysis but says no one endorses treating the second particle as extra or disregarding it. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي)-]
- 23:35/q09/p9 The second clause can explain the first, or stand as the conditional answer, apart from the main repetition analysis. — Bayḍāwī offers explanatory and conditional-answer alternatives; al-Zamakhsharī also lists a conditional-result parse. Abū Ḥayyān objects to placing إذا in the apodosis: between إنكم and its predicate the clause answers an omitted oath, and a conditional answer of this kind would need fāʾ under Basran grammar, though al-Farrāʾ permits omission. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 23:35/q10 What alternative wording is reported for the promise?  [أَنَّكُمْ إِذَا مِتُّمْ وَكُنتُمْ تُرَابًا وَعِظَامًا أَنَّكُم مُّخْرَجُونَ · readings]
turns on: Which part of the standard wording the report says is absent: the first particle, repeated particle, or intervening description.
- 23:35/q10/p1 A reading attributed to ʿAbd Allāh or Ibn Masʿūd omits the first أنكم and begins the content with إذا متم. — Wāḥidī says this reading has only the أن after إذا; al-Farrāʾ compares 'I think, if you leave, that you will regret it.' Kashshāf, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, and Ālūsī also report the omission. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عبد الله); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله)]
- 23:35/q10/p2 A reading attributed to ʿAbd Allāh omits the repeated أن after the intervening clause. — Baghawī reports the omission and compares 9:63, the verse about one who opposes God and His Messenger. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله)]
- 23:35/q10/p3 A reading attributed to Ibn Masʿūd omits the intervening words about death and becoming dust and bones. — Rāzī reports this shortened wording. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 23:35/q11 What does being مُّخْرَجُونَ mean?  [مُّخْرَجُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'brought out' denotes emergence from graves or re-creation from nonexistence.
- 23:35/q11/p1 They will be raised alive from the earth or graves, restored after death and bodily decay. — Muqātil says Hūd promises emergence from earth alive; Thaʿlabī and Jishumī specify graves after flesh decays. Bayḍāwī presents resurrection from graves as one sense. Biqāʿī and Bursevî say they return to life in their former bodies; Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies complete human form. Study Quran glosses the literal 'caused to leave it' as leaving the earth on the Last Day. Nasafī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī add questioning, reckoning, reward, and punishment; Rāzī mentions recompense. [18 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 23:35/q11/p2 It can mean being recreated from nonexistence into existence. — Bayḍāwī gives this as an alternative meaning of being brought out. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 23:35/q12 What reasons support resurrection against their objection?  [* · theology]
turns on: The notes give complementary grounds: God's power and knowledge, and justice through recompense.
- 23:35/q12/p1 God's omnipotence and knowledge make resurrection possible. — Rāzī says God leaves the objection unanswered here because its error is evident in light of divine power and knowledge. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 23:35/q12/p2 Divine wisdom and justice require recompense after death. — Rāzī says without resurrection the strong could oppress the weak without redress; he cites 20:15 on each soul being recompensed for its striving. Kur'an Yolu says the privileged fear that accountability would threaten their status. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 23:35/q13 How does this question connect with the surrounding passage and surah?  [* · links]
- 23:35/q13/p1 It continues the story of prophetic rejection and leads into the deniers' intensified dismissal and warned fate. — Study Quran says the surah recounts Noah's struggle after worldly blessings and repeatedly depicts affluent opposition. Ālūsī calls this question a resumption of the preceding rebuke. Rāzī says the next expression intensifies denial; Ibn ʿĀshūr says هيهات explains the promise in a separate sentence. Kur'an Yolu says this passage warns of their painful fate while answers to denials occur elsewhere. Samarrahī sees resurrection as the natural last link in life's development (compare 39:31). [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; STUDYQURAN]

## 23:35/q14 Where else does the Quran present the objection about reviving dust and bones?  [* · links]
- 23:35/q14/p1 The same objection recurs in several verses, with other passages addressing revival of dead bones. — Study Quran cites 17:49, 17:98, 23:82, 37:16, 37:53, and 56:47 for the recurring objection, and 36:78 and 79:11 for dead bones being revived. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
