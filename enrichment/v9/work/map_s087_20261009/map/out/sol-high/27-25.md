# 27:25: 21 questions

## 27:25/q01 How is أَلَّا يَسْجُدُوا۟ read?  [أَلَّا يَسْجُدُوا۟ · readings]
turns on: Whether the letters combine an with lā and keep third-person narrative syntax, or introduce an alert and imperative; transmitters, written yāʾ, and continuity of the story are cited.
- 27:25/q01/p1 With a doubled lām, as an lā yasjudū: they do not prostrate. — Al-Ṭabarī reports the Medinan, Kufan and Basran majority; al-Naḥḥās names Abū ʿAmr, ʿĀṣim, Nāfiʿ and Ḥamza; al-Fārisī says all except al-Kisāʾī. Others likewise call it the majority. Abū ʿUbayd, Abū Ḥātim, al-Fārisī and al-Wāḥidī favor its continuity with the Sheba story; al-Fārisī and al-Ṭabrisī cite the written yāʾ, whereas al-Naḥḥās says the light form is remote and strains spelling. Al-Ṭabarī accepts both regional readings. Al-Samīn explains nūn assimilation, and al-Nashr notes joined versus separate writing. Al-Itqān also calls the stressed form exhortative. [31 notes: TAB-FULL(قراء المدينة والكوفة والبصرة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو عمرو وعاصم ونافع وحمزة); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(الكسائي); MAWARDI-FULL(الكسائي); JISHUMI(الباقون); BAGHAWI-FULL(الآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأكثرون); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو ونافع وعاصم وحمزة,النحاس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(قيل); NASHR; TABATABAI; ITQAN; QIRAAT-ER(ḥf); STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); FARISI-HUJJA+; JISHUMI(أبو عبيد)+; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حاتم وأبو عبيدة)+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 27:25/q01/p2 With a light lām: alā yā usjudū, an attention opening followed by an imperative to prostrate. — Authorities named across sources include al-Kisāʾī, Abū Jaʿfar, al-Zuhrī, al-Ḥasan, Ḥumayd, Yaʿqūb and Ruways; al-Kisāʾī heard elders use it. Reciters stop after alā yā and resume at usjudū; Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites a prophetic report calling this a prostration place. Al-Ṭabarī accepts both readings; Sībawayh reports a softened pronunciation transmitted by ʿĪsā through hamza deletion. Al-Samīn says the two analyses can coincide in sound and script; the written hamzat al-waṣl drops in connection, per al-Nashr. Al-Naḥḥās objects to interruption and written form. [32 notes: SIBAWAYH; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(بعض المكيين وبعض المدنيين والكوفيين); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الزهري وأبو جعفر وأبو عبد الرحمن وحميد وطلحة والكسائي); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(الكسائي); MAWARDI-FULL(الكسائي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); JISHUMI(السلمي والحسن وأبو جعفر وحميد والكسائي ويعقوب); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جعفر والكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس، أبو جعفر، الزهري، أبو عبد الرحمن، الحسن، الكسائي، حميد,الكسائي); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وأبو عبد الرحمن السلمي والحسن والزهري وقتادة وأبو العالية وحميد الأعرج والأعمش وابن أبي عبلة والكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(الزهري والكسائي,الكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL(الكسائي ويعقوب); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الكسائي); BURHAN(الكسائي); NASHR(أبو جعفر والكسائي ورويس); BIQAI-FULL(الكسائي وأبو جعفر); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); QIRAAT-ER(al-Kisāʾī,ḥf); STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); NAHHAS-; JISHUMI(أبو عبيد)-; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس)-]
- 27:25/q01/p3 Exhortative or interrogative variants ask or urge whether they will prostrate. — ʿAbd Allāh is quoted with hallā yasjudūn, alā hal tasjudūn, and addressed tasjudūn with indicative nūn; al-Aʿmash has hal lā yasjudūn or hallā. Ubayy is transmitted with alā hal tasjudū or hal yasjudū, with variation in person. Al-Samīn explains indicative nūn as exhortation with either light or stressed alā; al-Burhān records regret or grief as well as an alerting sense, and even delight in another poetic use. Al-Rāzī's investigators infer a command from the divine attributes that follow. [18 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA(عبد الله); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبي بن كعب,ابن مسعود,الأعمش); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود وأبي); RAZI-FULL(أبي,أهل التحقيق,عبدالله,عبدالله، الأعمش); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله وأبي); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الأعمش,عبد الله); BURHAN(ابن فارس)]

## 27:25/q02 How does the doubled أَلَّا يَسْجُدُوا۟ connect grammatically to what precedes?  [أَلَّا يَسْجُدُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Which earlier expression governs or is restated by an, whether an omitted purpose lām is supplied, and whether lā negates or is extra.
- 27:25/q02/p1 It expresses Satan's purpose or result in turning them from the path, so that they would not prostrate. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, al-Samīn, al-Nasafī and Bursawī supply a purpose lām after faṣaddahum, treating an as accusative or sometimes genitive. Al-Kisāʾī links it to their being turned away. Al-Ṭabarī gives the accusative sense li-allā; al-Qurṭubī says the sense cannot stop at lā yahtadūn. Al-Samīn prefers ordinary negating lā in this analysis. Yaḥyā calls the order advanced; al-Biqāʿī also describes lack of prostration as connected to their blindness and Satanic control. [21 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الأخفش,الكسائي); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأكثرون); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الأنباري,الكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 27:25/q02/p2 It expresses Satan's purpose in beautifying their deeds, keeping them from prostrating. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Akhfash and others connect the supplied purpose lām to zayyana rather than faṣaddahum; an can be accusative or genitive. Al-Fārisī allows either antecedent; al-Biqāʿī calls the beautification intentional. [8 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 27:25/q02/p3 An lā is an accusative apposition to 'their deeds': their failure to prostrate is what Satan adorned. — ʿAlī ibn Sulaymān and al-Yazīdī offer this analysis; al-Samīn says it requires a parenthetical interruption. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ reads it as the common reading's explanatory apposition. [5 notes: NAHHAS(علي بن سليمان); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); QURTUBI-FULL(اليزيدي وعلي بن سليمان); SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI]
- 27:25/q02/p4 An lā is a genitive apposition to 'the path': the path is prostration to God. — ʿAlī ibn Sulaymān also allows substitution for al-sabīl, as do Abū ʿAmr and Ibn ʿAṭiyya; al-Samīn makes lā extra in this parse. [4 notes: NAHHAS(علي بن سليمان); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو); SAMIN-DURR]
- 27:25/q02/p5 It depends on 'they are not guided': they fail to find the way to prostration, with an extra lā. — Al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī transmit this alternative. Al-Qurṭubī relates it to knowing prostration is obligatory and cites 7:12. Al-Samīn describes an omitted preposition and compares 57:29, while also recording a variant where Satan expects or fears their prostration if lā is extra. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 27:25/q02/p6 It is the predicate of an omitted subject referring to their deeds or the path. — Al-Samīn distinguishes an omitted subject referring to aʿmālahum, with negative lā, from one referring to al-sabīl, with extra lā; this analysis permits a stop after yahtadūn, unlike the prior governed or appositional analyses. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 27:25/q03 How is the light أَلَّا يَسْجُدُوا۟ construction understood?  [أَلَّا يَسْجُدُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether yā is a vocative requiring an understood addressee or a second attention particle.
- 27:25/q03/p1 Alā alerts the hearer, yā is vocative with an omitted addressee, and usjudū is the imperative. — Ibn Qutayba expands it as alā yā hāʾulāʾ usjudū. Al-Ṭabarī quotes Basran and Kufan accounts of elision; the Kufan says either yā or its addressee can stand for the other. Poems of al-Akhṭal, Dhū al-Rummah, al-ʿAjjāj, Abū Zayd and unnamed poets, and spoken alā yā arḥamūnī, supply parallels. Al-Naḥḥās finds it unusual; al-Zajjāj says yā without direct address is rare. Al-Fārisī also allows omitted qawm or muslimūn. Al-Biqāʿī ties omission to urgency. Al-Ṭabarī calls the imperative jussive and alā syntactically independent. Al-Itqān lists alerting, astonishment and regret among figurative vocative functions, citing this verse with 11:73, 36:30 and 78:40. [55 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الشاعر); TAB-FULL(الأخطل,بعض المكيين وبعض المدنيين والكوفيين,بعض نحويي البصرة,بعض نحويي الكوفة); ZAJJAJ(الأخطل,العجاج,ذو الرمة); NAHHAS(الزهري وأبو جعفر وأبو عبد الرحمن وحميد وطلحة والكسائي); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو زيد,الفراء); JISHUMI(السلمي والحسن وأبو جعفر وحميد والكسائي ويعقوب); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو زيد,الأخطل,ذو الرمة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض القراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وأبو عبد الرحمن السلمي والحسن والزهري وقتادة وأبو العالية وحميد الأعرج والأعمش وابن أبي عبلة والكسائي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزهري والكسائي,سيبويه); BAYDAWI-FULL(الكسائي ويعقوب); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الكسائي); BURHAN(الكسائي); BIQAI-FULL; NASHR(أبو جعفر والكسائي ورويس); QIRAAT-ER(al-Kisāʾī); ITQAN]
- 27:25/q03/p2 Yā itself is another attention particle; the two particles jointly alert the listener without an omitted vocative. — Al-Samīn prefers this because it avoids extensive ellipsis; paired attention particles are defensible, with Arabic parallels and 4:73. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī allow a warning yā without a true vocative; al-Burhān offers two alerts. Al-Samīn cites imperatives in poetry and prose and a curse line whose yā could alternatively be vocative. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR(الكسائي); BURHAN(الكسائي); SAMIN-DURR+]

## 27:25/q04 Where may a reciter pause in أَلَّا يَسْجُدُوا۟?  [أَلَّا يَسْجُدُوا۟ · readings]
turns on: Whether the phrase starts an independent imperative or remains grammatically attached to the preceding narration.
- 27:25/q04/p1 With the light reading one can stop before the new command, or at alā yā for testing, then begin usjudū. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, al-Nasafī and al-Nashr describe the pause at alā yā. Al-Samīn allows a complete stop at yahtadūn and test stops at alā yā, alā, or yā, but says the latter are poor chosen stops because the particles need what follows. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; FARISI-HUJJA(الكسائي); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جعفر والكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; NASHR(أبو جعفر والكسائي ورويس)]
- 27:25/q04/p2 With the doubled, connected reading the clause continues the earlier narration, so the stop comes later. — Al-Nasafī places the stop only at the mighty throne. Al-Samīn disallows stopping after yahtadūn under governed or appositional analyses; his omitted-subject analysis does allow it. Reciters pause on allā as one written word in a test, whereas grammarians can split an and lā for explanation; nūn assimilates to lām. [7 notes: JISHUMI(الباقون); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(القراء,النحويون)]

## 27:25/q05 Whose speech is the prostration clause?  [أَلَّا يَسْجُدُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: The doubled versus light reading, whether the hoopoe can issue a religious command, and whether the passage breaks reported speech.
- 27:25/q05/p1 It is the hoopoe's speech within its report to Solomon. — Muqātil says the hoopoe urges prostration. Ibn Zayd and Ibn Isḥāq assign the reported speech to it, and Abū Ḥayyān calls this apparent; al-Samīn likewise favors the hoopoe throughout. The doubled reading and third-person verbs support this attribution for Ibn ʿAṭiyya, though he objects that the bird lacks legal accountability. Al-Māwardī offers that it either addressed Sheba on seeing their worship or spoke in disapproval to Solomon on return. He explains recognition through inspiration to obey Solomon or analogy to a near-mature child. [19 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد,الجمهور); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 27:25/q05/p2 It is God's parenthetical command or comment, interrupting the hoopoe's report. — Al-Māwardī reports a divine command to all creatures, and Abū ʿUbayda calls the light reading a new divine command. Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers God's inserted speech after reflection, since a nonaccountable bird is unlikely to deliver a legal command; the light reading favors this. Islāḥī takes the verses as God's insertion linked to Arabia at revelation because a lapwing would not say God knows what you conceal. Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Baghawī allow a divine insertion, and second-person forms may address Muḥammad's community. [17 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جعفر والكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الكسائي وعاصم في رواية حفص); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 27:25/q05/p3 It is a new command spoken by Solomon. — Al-Baghawī permits Solomon as the speaker of the intervening phrase; al-Ṭabrisī and al-Bayḍāwī also allow a Solomonic command with the light reading. Al-Ālūsī says independent speech can be God or Solomon. [4 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 27:25/q06 Who is addressed by the command to prostrate?  [يَسْجُدُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Who speaks the light-reading command and whether its second person remains within the ancient story or addresses the Qur'an's audience.
- 27:25/q06/p1 People generally, including the listeners, believers, or all creatures. — Al-Māwardī reports God's address to all creatures. The light reading supplies 'O people,' 'O Muslims,' 'O rational ones,' or 'O commanded ones' in Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Fārisī and al-Wāḥidī; al-Ṭabrisī allows both believers and unbelievers already mentioned. Al-Ālūsī lists people generally. Al-Biqāʿī omits the address because the command is urgent. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); BIQAI-FULL]
- 27:25/q06/p2 The people of Sheba or Bilqis are addressed after their sun worship. — Al-Māwardī describes the hoopoe addressing them upon finding their contrary worship; al-Ālūsī includes Bilqis's people among the possibilities. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 27:25/q06/p3 Solomon and his attendants, or his people, are addressed. — Al-Māwardī says the hoopoe could have spoken to Solomon on return; Abū Ḥayyān allows the attendants because it had already claimed to know what Solomon did not. Al-Ālūsī also lists Solomon's people. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكسائي وحفص); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 27:25/q06/p4 Muḥammad's community is addressed by God. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya's and Abū Ḥayyān's accounts connect second-person wording with a divine address to this community; Islāḥī links the insertion to Arabia at the Qur'an's revelation. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الكسائي وعاصم في رواية حفص); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 27:25/q07 What does prostration mean and why is it urged here?  [يَسْجُدُوا۟ لِلَّهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether sujūd is explained as bodily worship in opposition to sun worship or as inward actualization.
- 27:25/q07/p1 Literal worship and submission to God alone, the proper response to His power and knowledge. — Ibn al-Jawzī defines sujūd as placing the forehead on the ground. Ibn Kathīr says Satan kept them from worshipping God rather than created stars, citing 41:37. Al-Biqāʿī calls prostration the greatest act of nearness, prayer's restraint of wrongdoing, and the remedy to their lack of guidance. Al-Rāzī contrasts God's universal power and knowledge with the finite sun's inability to bring hidden things forth, know secrets, confer benefits or avert harm, citing 27:24 and 19:42. Al-Bayḍāwī says only the Necessary Existent can create from possibility and nonexistence. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ adds that God created even the sun and governs it while it is unaware of worshippers. Al-Ṭabrisī objects to al-Jubbāʾī that the hoopoe's distinction of right and wrong worship implies knowledge of God and divine justice. [14 notes: TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; RAZI-FULL(أهل التحقيق); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 27:25/q07/p2 In an inward reading, prostration is submission that brings latent perfections into actuality in the intellect. — Al-Qāshānī says Satan's obstruction prevents this inner realization. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 27:25/q08 Does this verse call for prostration of recitation?  [يَسْجُدُوا۟ · law]
turns on: Whether the doubled wording's censure has the force of a command, whether the light wording is divine speech, and what the Sunnah establishes.
- 27:25/q08/p1 The light imperative calls for it, while the doubled narrative reading does not. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Zajjāj, al-Qurṭubī and reports in other sources distinguish a direct order from a narrative description of refusal; Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites al-Kisāʾī's prophetic report identifying this location as a prostration place. Al-Bayḍāwī says both readings imply prostration generally but not necessarily upon recitation. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الكسائي); TABRISI(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج); TABRISI-; NASAFI-FULL(الزجاج)-; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)-]
- 27:25/q08/p2 Both readings require prostration: one commands it and the other censures its omission. — Al-Ṭabrisī rejects al-Farrāʾ and compares the censure in 25:60. Al-Nasafī rejects al-Zajjāj on the same ground, as does al-Zamakhsharī as quoted by al-Samīn. Bursawī says the censure explains prostration at the end of these verses. [5 notes: NASAFI-FULL(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; NASAFI-FULL+; TABRISI+]
- 27:25/q08/p3 Prostration here is recommended rather than obligatory. — Al-Samīn appeals to the Sunnah and says a divine command would imply obligation absent contrary evidence, whereas a hoopoe's command has uncertain authority; the hoopoe seems the speaker to him. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 27:25/q09 What does ٱلْخَبْءَ mean as an Arabic word?  [ٱلْخَبْءَ · meaning]
- 27:25/q09/p1 A concealed or unseen thing, named from the verbal noun for concealing. — Ibn Qutayba derives it from khabaʾtu al-shayʾ; al-Māwardī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn say the verbal noun names the concealed passive object. Yaḥyā notes its singular form. Al-Zajjāj says anything hidden is khabʾ; al-Wāḥidī adds stored for future need. Al-Ṭabrisī describes concealment behind a barrier preventing perception, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ transmits this. Al-Bayḍāwī describes what is inside another. Bursawī says stored and concealed; al-Biqāʿī includes actual underground concealment and potential, not-yet-existent things. Al-Samīn compares conventionally unhamzated khābiya, bariyya and dhurriyya. [23 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة,يحيى); FARRA; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 27:25/q10 What hidden thing does God bring forth?  [يُخْرِجُ ٱلْخَبْءَ فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ · referent]
turns on: Whether khabʾ is unrestricted, exemplified by rain and growth, narrowed to one material thing, or extended to creation and inward potential; the following knowledge clause favors the general sense for some.
- 27:25/q10/p1 Every concealed or unseen thing in heaven and earth. — Qatāda, ʿIkrima, Mujāhid, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and Ibn ʿAbbās are transmitted with secret, unseen or everything concealed; a report from Ḥakīm ibn Jābir says God knows every hidden thing. Al-Zajjāj prefers this general sense because the next knowledge clause supports it; al-Ālūsī also prefers it. Al-Ṭabrisī includes whatever perception cannot reach; al-Ṭabarī includes rain and vegetation as examples. Al-Burhān says the general wording also embraces the dead emerging alive in 99:2 and hadith treasures and minerals. Study Quran reports mysteries generally. Ibn Kathīr transmits Ibn ʿAbbās through ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥa, with ʿIkrima, Mujāhid, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and Qatāda, for everything hidden. - 27:25/q10/p2 Rain from heaven and vegetation, fruits, treasures or water from earth. — The common Arabic saying and many exegetes identify paired heavenly rain and earthly plants. Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds earthly treasures; al-Bayḍāwī adds stars and broader creation; Bursawī adds snow and water. Islāḥī includes sun, moon, Venus and Mercury above, and seas and springs below. - 27:25/q10/p3 Rain or water itself is the hidden thing. — Mujāhid and Muqātil identify rain; Ḥakīm ibn Jābir is also transmitted with rain, despite another report about all hidden things. Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib says water. The variation between rain and water is noted; this position does not specify a matching earthly plant. [12 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(حكيم بن جابر,سعيد بن المسيب,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد ومقاتل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب); DURR-FULL(حكيم بن جابر,سعيد بن المسيب); ALUSI-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]

Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ makes the bringing out a metaphor for creating from nonexistence and compares the title Originator of heaven and earth; he allows literal extraction but says the place of hiding would need explanation. Asad connects hidden and manifest phenomena to the appearing and disappearing sun and other celestial bodies worshipped by ancient Semites, citing 6:74. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 27:25/q10/p7 In an inward reading, it is possible perfection hidden in the heavens of spirits and earth of the body. — Al-Qāshānī relates the hidden treasure to possible human perfections. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 27:25/q11 How is ٱلْخَبْءَ pronounced and explained in variant readings?  [ٱلْخَبْءَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the hamza is kept, its vowel transferred before deletion, or replaced by alif; the dispute cites dialect, connected versus pausal usage, and transmitted recitation.
- 27:25/q11/p1 The majority keep a still bāʾ and the hamza. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān give the majority form. Al-Naḥḥās describes the still hamza at pause, possible ḍamma or imāla in the nominative, and rules out gemination. Al-Nashr calls vowel transfer at pause the standard regular treatment; Qiraat-ER describes the pausal sukūn and consonantal skeleton. [5 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور وأبي بن كعب وعكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; NASHR; QIRAAT-ER(waqf)]
- 27:25/q11/p2 Ubayy and ʿĪsā transfer the hamza's vowel to bāʾ and omit hamza, yielding al-khab. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn report this, comparing raʾaytu al-ab. Sībawayh records such transfer for some Tamīm and Asad speakers; al-Naḥḥās quotes broader dialectal hamza substitution after sukūn, with forms al-khabu, al-khabi and al-khaba. [6 notes: NAHHAS(سيبويه); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور وأبي بن كعب وعكرمة,سيبويه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سيبويه); SAMIN-DURR(أبي وعيسى)]
- 27:25/q11/p3 ʿIkrima and others read al-khabā with alif in place of the hamza. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it shortened alif; Abū Ḥayyān associates ʿIkrima, ʿAbd Allāh and Mālik ibn Dīnār and balances dialectal change against carrying a pause form into connected speech. Al-Samīn instead favors a shifted vowel with retained hamza, comparing al-marʾa and al-kamʾa. Al-Naḥḥās rebuts Abū Ḥātim using Sībawayh's account of replacement by alif, wāw or yāʾ according to vowel. Abū Ḥātim calls al-khabā non-Arabic, arguing deletion would yield al-khab and replacement al-khabī; al-Mubarrad's criticism of his grammar is reported. Al-Nashr reports Abū al-ʿAlāʾ's possible explanation by vowel assimilation at pause. [11 notes: NAHHAS(سيبويه,محمد بن يزيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور وأبي بن كعب وعكرمة,سيبويه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المبرد,عكرمة، عبد الله، مالك بن دينار); SAMIN-DURR(عبد الله وعكرمة ومالك بن دينار); NASHR(الحافظ أبو العلاء); NAHHAS+; SAMIN-DURR+; NAHHAS(عكرمة)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حاتم)-; SAMIN-DURR(أبو حاتم)-]
- 27:25/q11/p4 At pause, regular hamza easing allows transfer and specified assimilations, while arbitrary deletion and weakly transmitted forms are unsound. — Ibn al-Jazarī gives transfer as standard after a sound consonant; for kasra hamza rawm is possible, as with milʾ, difʾ and yanzur al-marʾ. After original long vowels he allows transfer or idghām for open hamza; kasra adds rawm, and ḍamma adds ishmām for six forms. He rejects al-Hudhalī's idghām for juzʾ as untransmitted from Ḥamza, and rejects arbitrary deletion, extended preceding vowels, and Ibn Ghalbūn's bayna bayn. [8 notes: NASHR(الحافظ أبو العلاء,الهذلي,الهذلي وابن غلبون)]

## 27:25/q12 How does فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ relate to 'brings forth'?  [يُخْرِجُ ٱلْخَبْءَ فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ · grammar]
turns on: The attachment of the prepositional phrase, and whether fī can carry the extraction sense of min.
- 27:25/q12/p1 The phrase modifies the hidden thing: it is what lies in heaven and earth. — Abū Ḥayyān calls this the apparent syntax, and al-Ālūsī prefers it. Al-Samīn permits it alongside the other attachment. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 27:25/q12/p2 The phrase modifies 'brings forth,' with fī used in the sense of min, 'from heaven and earth.' — Al-Farrāʾ points to ʿAbd Allāh's reading with min and says the two prepositions can alternate or one be dropped without changed meaning. Al-Ṭabarī and al-Wāḥidī give Arabic extraction idioms, including drawing knowledge from a person; al-Ṭabarī, al-Baghawī and Ibn al-Jawzī report the reading. Al-Samīn allows this parse. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī report al-Farrāʾ but prefer the other attachment. [9 notes: FARRA(عبد الله); TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,عبد الله); BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 27:25/q13 How is ٱلَّذِى construed?  [ٱلَّذِى · grammar]
- 27:25/q13/p1 It may be genitive as adjective, apposition or explanatory substitute for God; accusative as praise; or nominative after an omitted subject. — Al-Samīn lists these case possibilities without preferring one. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 27:25/q14 Why describe God as bringing forth hidden things here?  [ٱلَّذِى يُخْرِجُ ٱلْخَبْءَ فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the choice is explained by a special trait of the hoopoe or by the story's disclosure and the contrast with sun worship.
- 27:25/q14/p1 It suits the hoopoe's knowledge of hidden water and its way of speaking. — Abū Ḥayyān says God taught the hoopoe to detect water; a report says each servant is clothed with the mantle of his work. Ibn Kathīr cites Ibn ʿAbbās and others that the hoopoe sees water deep underground. Al-Ālūsī reports the proposed connection but rejects it for lack of a reliable report and because it fails on the light reading as divine or Solomonic speech. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 27:25/q14/p2 The attribute fits the disclosure of Bilqis and her affairs, and names God's power over the visible and hidden world. — Al-Ālūsī relates revelation of hidden realities to the narrative discovery; al-Biqāʿī says heaven and earth are the limits of human observation and lists clouds, rain, plants, celestial bodies, weather, motion and rest. Al-Ālūsī says God brings hidden states to light for recompense; al-Rāzī says the verse moves from power to knowledge. Al-Rāzī also says the order differs from Abraham's and Moses' arguments because the debate concerns sun worship; setting and rising show the sun under a ruler, citing 2:258, 6:76 and 26:28. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 27:25/q16 Whose concealment and disclosure does the final clause describe?  [مَا تُخْفُونَ وَمَا تُعْلِنُونَ · referent]
turns on: The third-person versus second-person recitation and the identity of the intervening speaker.
- 27:25/q16/p1 The people whose deeds Satan adorned, the queen and her people. — Al-Ṭabarī connects the third-person reading with those creatures; Abū Ḥayyān names the queen and her people. Their secret and public actions remain known to God. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحرميان والجمهور); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 27:25/q17 What do concealing and declaring include?  [مَا تُخْفُونَ وَمَا تُعْلِنُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause is explained through ordinary human secrets and public conduct or inward potential and moral states.
- 27:25/q17/p1 God knows every private and public word, act and state. — Muqātil says hearts versus tongues, as does al-Wāḥidī; Yaḥyā says breasts. Ibn ʿAbbās says night and day, and al-Ḥasan adds dark night and inside homes. Ibn Kathīr includes words and deeds and cites 13:10. Bursawī adds limbs and a Persian couplet in which not a grain escapes knowledge. Al-Ṭabrisī calls it secret and public. Al-Ālūsī says hidden states are exposed for requital and mentioning open conduct stresses equal divine knowledge. Al-Biqāʿī says even noisy, overlapping public speech is known. Islāḥī includes this knowledge in the divine insertion. [17 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 27:25/q17/p2 In an inward reading, God knows latent perfections and the dark dispositions or harmful morals people display. — Al-Qāshānī says veiling actions block possible perfections from the intellect, while ruinous qualities become manifest. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 27:25/q18 Why mention what is concealed before what is declared, and mention both?  [مَا تُخْفُونَ وَمَا تُعْلِنُونَ · rhetoric]
- 27:25/q18/p1 The order echoes the preceding hidden thing and emphasizes God's equal knowledge of secret and public matters. — Al-Ālūsī gives the thematic echo, says heaven's hidden thing comes first because it fits the context better, and says the manifest is added to broaden the scope or underscore equality. Bursawī likewise says the two terms broaden the scope; al-Biqāʿī notes that even overlapping public utterances are fully known. Al-Ālūsī starts another coordination point, but the surviving excerpt stops before the explanation. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 27:25/q19 How does this verse connect to the surrounding account and other scriptural arguments?  [* · links]
turns on: Which feature of the surrounding Solomon and Sheba narrative supplies the connection.
- 27:25/q19/p1 The narrative turns from Sheba's sun worship to a call to worship the God who controls the sun and knows the hidden. — Al-Burhān identifies the transition. Al-Rāzī links God's power and knowledge to refuting sun worship and says the argument's order reverses Abraham's and Moses' because this is specifically about the sun, citing 2:258, 6:76, 26:28 and 27:24. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ likewise infer sole worship from divine creation and knowledge. Ibn Kathīr cites 41:37 against worship of created stars. Islāḥī sees an insertion connecting the ancient scene to Arabia at revelation. Study Quran says the account ends in the queen's acceptance of the One God. Asad reads Satan as immoral impulse evading divine accountability and sun worship as worship of perceptible natural phenomena; he points to Sheba background at 34:15. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 27:25/q19/p2 The hoopoe episode also shows that a humble creature can know what Solomon does not and warns learned people against conceit. — Asad reports al-Rāzī's and al-Zamakhsharī's interpretations of the earlier scene; Abū Ḥayyān notes the hoopoe's claim to know what Solomon did not. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Razi,Zamakhshari); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 27:25/q20 What report about the hoopoe accompanies this verse?  [* · reports]
- 27:25/q20/p1 The Prophet prohibited killing the hoopoe among four creatures because it called people to worship God alone. — Ibn Kathīr reports Abū Hurayra's narration naming the ant, bee, hoopoe and shrike and grades its chain sound. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 27:25/q21 What ancillary grammatical analogy is recorded with the light reading?  [أَلَّا يَسْجُدُوا۟ · grammar]
- 27:25/q21/p1 The permissibility of omitting a circumstantial element depends on attested usage versus analogy. — Al-Burhān reports Abū ʿAlī: Sībawayh restricts the omission to attested examples, while al-Akhfash and al-Mubarrad allow it by analogy. The source records this in discussing the lightened construction. [1 notes: BURHAN(أبو علي)]
