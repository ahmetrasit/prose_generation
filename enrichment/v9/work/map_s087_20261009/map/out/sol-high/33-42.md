# 33:42: 14 questions

## 33:42/q01 What does the command to glorify Him designate?  [وَسَبِّحُوهُ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes differ over whether tasbīḥ retains its verbal and theological sense, denotes prayer or supplication, or serves as a name for particular timed prayers; prayer identifications differ over which prayers the two time words include.
- 33:42/q01/p1 Perform the dawn and afternoon prayers. — Reported from Qatāda, Abū al-ʿĀliya, Muqātil in one transmission, and al-Ṭabarī; al-Ṭabarī himself takes this view. Kashf al-asrār urges people to keep these prayers despite work; Bursevī cites a hadith against being distracted from the prayers before sunrise and sunset, 17:78 for angelic witness to dawn, and 2:238 for afternoon as the middle prayer. Biqāʿī says observing them amid rest and work indicates love of worship and encourages other acts. Alūsī finds this identification more plausible than dawn and night. [20 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة,مقاتل); JISHUMI(قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو العالية وقتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة والطبري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)+]
- 33:42/q01/p2 Perform prayer, without specifying particular prayers. — Māwardī, Baghawī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī and Qurṭubī report prayer as a sense of tasbīḥ. Ibn Ḥayyān says to pray morning and evening. Abū ʿAlī argues prayer has appointed times and required remembrance, and is called glorification for its tasbīḥ. Alūsī calls prayer a possible vehicle for literal tasbīḥ. Elmalılı includes prayer in remembrance and glorification; Kuran Yolu considers the prayers probable. Islahi reads prayer as the specific within general remembrance, with set times (17:78, 20:130). [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن حيان); JISHUMI(أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; JISHUMI(القاضي)-]
- 33:42/q01/p3 Declare God transcendent above every defect or unsuitable attribution. — Abū Muslim and the judge take the plain wording as declaring transcendence, a duty at all times (Jishumī). Rāzī says to declare Him free of every evil. Biqāʿī adds contradiction of revelation, after affirming perfection. Bursevī cites al-Mufradāt: sabḥ is rapid motion in water or air, whence rapid worship and then worship by speech, deed, or intention. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the declaration need not be verbal; phrases such as subḥān Allāh are one form. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the formula rejects imperfections and can repudiate the hypocrites' unsuitable claims about the Prophet, comparing 24:16; reports of the Prophet and Hind bint ʿUtba exemplify such repudiating use. [15 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,هند بنت عتبة); TABATABAI; JISHUMI(القاضي)+]
- 33:42/q01/p4 Perform dawn and the four later obligatory prayers. — Al-Kalbī identifies bukra with dawn and aṣīl with noon, afternoon, sunset, and night; Ibn al-Sāʾib gives the same evening set. Al-Nasafī also allows dawn and later prayers. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); TABRISI(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب); NASAFI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ)]
- 33:42/q01/p5 Recite formulas of transcendence, praise, oneness, greatness, and reliance on God. — Mujāhid lists subḥān Allāh, al-ḥamdu lillāh, lā ilāha illā Allāh, Allāhu akbar, and lā ḥawla wa-lā quwwata illā bi-llāh; Baghawī says tasbīḥ stands for its companion formulas. Nasafī attributes the list to Qatāda. Qurṭubī urges frequent utterance of glorification, oneness, praise, and magnification. [6 notes: THALABI(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل,مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 33:42/q01/p6 Perform dawn and the two evening prayers. — Kashshāf says these prayers are singled out because they are harder to perform and observe; Nasafī gives the pair as a possible prayer reading. Qurṭubī names dawn, sunset and night and says they merit encouragement at the edges of night; al-Wāḥidī's Wajīz also names dawn, afternoon and two evening prayers, a broader variant. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
- 33:42/q01/p7 Perform dawn, noon, and afternoon prayers. — Muqātil and Ibn ʿAbbās in Yaḥyā ibn Sallām identify morning as dawn and evening as noon and afternoon; Ibn al-Jawzī also reports Muqātil's evening pair. [3 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل)]
- 33:42/q01/p8 Perform dawn and the night prayer. — Ṭabrisī reports this pair, singled out because day and night angels meet then. Ibn ʿAbbās is reported with this pair by Abū Ḥayyān and Alūsī; the latter calls prayer a synecdoche for tasbīḥ and prefers the dawn and afternoon pair over it. [3 notes: TABRISI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)-]
- 33:42/q01/p9 Perform all prayers or acts of worship. — Kashshāf says every obedience and good deed may count as remembrance, with all prayers as especially excellent glorification. Abū Ḥayyān likewise encompasses every obedience and singles out prayer. Alūsī reports an extension to all obedience and prayer at all times but calls it remote. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 33:42/q01/p10 Pray or invoke God in the morning and evening without naming a specific rite. — Ibn Ḥayyān's gloss is praying to God at these times; al-Durr reports Muqātil as dawn and dusk prayer. Study Quran reports prayer and invocation from dawn to sunrise and from sunset to night prayer. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن حيان); DURR-FULL(مقاتل); STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 33:42/q01/p11 Perform supererogatory prayer or devotions, including night vigil. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says optional prayer is possible, which changes the relation between this command and general remembrance. Study Quran reports optional devotions such as night vigil alongside prayer identifications. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ)]
- 33:42/q01/p12 Call upon God in supplication. — Māwardī cites Jarīr's verse about Yūnus calling on God and being rescued as evidence that sabbaha can mean supplication or prayer. Qurṭubī gives a variant poetic example recalling Yūsuf calling on his Lord. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(جرير); QURTUBI-FULL(جرير,قيل)]
- 33:42/q01/p13 Perform afternoon and the two evening prayers. — Thaʿlabī reports this attribution to Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس)]
- 33:42/q01/p14 Glorify through the acts, attributes, and Essence in an inward sense. — Qāshānī's inward reading calls for transcendence of acts, attributes, and Essence. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 33:42/q02 What do bukra and aṣīl mean as times of day?  [بُكْرَةًۭ وَأَصِيلًا · meaning]
turns on: The issue is whether the terms are ordinary day periods, prayer designations, or a particular lexical form of aṣīl.
- 33:42/q02/p1 Aṣīl is the late afternoon or evening from ʿaṣr until night. — Abū ʿUbayda, al-Akhfash and Abū Ḥayyān define this span; Majāz gives late afternoon until night for the phrase. Muḥammad ibn Yazīd calls aṣīl al-ʿashī; he gives plural aṣāʾil and says aṣl is a synonym with plural āṣāl. Qurṭubī repeats al-Mubarrad's account. [7 notes: MAJAZ; NAHHAS(محمد بن يزيد); MAWARDI-FULL(الأخفش); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); QURTUBI-FULL(المبرد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 33:42/q02/p2 The beginning and end of the day. — Nasafī, Biqāʿī, Bursevī, Alūsī, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī define the pair this way; Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes the later part as after ʿAṣr. Ibn ʿĀshūr says al-bukra is daybreak and al-aṣīl the late afternoon after ʿaṣr. [6 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 33:42/q02/p3 Aṣl is a plural of aṣīl. — Another grammarian compares the singular ragīf and plural rughuf; this differs from al-Mubarrad's account of aṣl as a synonym. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 33:42/q02/p4 On the prayer reading, bukra is Fajr. — Ibn al-Jawzī says the prayer interpreters agree that morning is Fajr; Baghawī also identifies it. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون)]

## 33:42/q03 Do the two time words limit the command or imply a wider span?  [بُكْرَةًۭ وَأَصِيلًا · rhetoric]
turns on: The sources either take the endpoints as a figure for continuous practice or emphasize the stated periods themselves; several combine both.
- 33:42/q03/p1 They name the two ends to imply remembrance and glorification through the whole day or at all times. — Kashshāf and Abū Ḥayyān treat the ends as all time. Rāzī illustrates the convention with a Prophetic mention of first and last that embraces the middle; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites a verse of Ṭarafa. Biqāʿī includes both busy and resting periods, Bursevī stresses the endpoints and their special honor, and Elmalılı compares a Turkish expression. Ibn ʿAbbās says remembrance has no set limit or ordinary excuse save loss of reason; al-Ṭabarī adds remembrance in every condition and consequent blessings from God and angels. Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares 41:38; Study Quran points to 6:52, 18:28, 19:11, 24:36 and 48:9. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 33:42/q03/p2 They direct believers to actual morning and evening remembrance. — Ṭabrisī describes early morning and evening practice; Ibn Kathīr compares 30:17–18, which names evening, morning and other parts of the day. Qushayrī says two terms keep remembrance from becoming tiresome. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 33:42/q04 Why are morning and evening especially mentioned?  [بُكْرَةًۭ وَأَصِيلًا · rhetoric]
turns on: The reasons offered are angelic witnessing, the times' practical difficulty or honor, their symbolism, and the rhetorical value of spanning different hours.
- 33:42/q04/p1 They are virtuous times witnessed by the changing angels of night and day. — Bayḍāwī calls the times excellent and witnessed; Nasafī and Alūsī say the angels meet then. Bursevī cites a hadith that angels alternate by night and day. Ṭabrisī ties the angelic meeting specifically to dawn and night prayers. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 33:42/q04/p2 The prayers at these times require special effort amid rest or work. — Kashshāf says dawn and the evening pair are difficult to perform and observe; Abū Ḥayyān speaks of dawn and night. Biqāʿī says dawn and afternoon kept despite competing rest and work show love of worship. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 33:42/q04/p3 They encompass different states of activity and encourage remembrance in both. — Biqāʿī notes that the day's beginning and end encompass busy and resting periods. Ibn ʿĀshūr says naming both fills the day's portions as far as possible. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 33:42/q04/p4 They are familiar, honored hours for devotion. — Elmalılı says these are well-known times; Bursevī says God distributes provision, sends blessings and answers prayers between dawn and sunrise, so people should attend to that hour. [2 notes: ELMALILI; BURSEVI]
- 33:42/q04/p5 The two terms vary the wording so that remembrance stays fresh. — Qushayrī explains the double expression this way. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 33:42/q04/p6 Dusk recalls the world's end and dawn the resurrection. — Biqāʿī says both times are witnessed by angels and also signify the Hour. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 33:42/q04/p7 The periods suit glorifying God beyond changing conditions and defects. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects the changing day to divine transcendence of change and passing defects. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 33:42/q05 How do the time adverbs relate grammatically to the two commands?  [بُكْرَةًۭ وَأَصِيلًا · grammar]
turns on: The disagreement is whether the time phrase governs both coordinated imperatives or only the second, whose predecessor already demands frequent remembrance.
- 33:42/q05/p1 They modify both the command to remember and the command to glorify. — Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī and Alūsī compare 'fast and pray on Friday'; Ibn ʿĀshūr says both words are adverbial with both commands. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 33:42/q05/p2 They are accusative adverbs of time. — Jishumī identifies the case and adverbial function. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 33:42/q05/p3 They attach to glorification while abundant remembrance already covers most times. — Alūsī reasons that if much remembrance in 33:41 means remembrance through most times, the first command does not need the two adverbs. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 33:42/q06 Why is tasbīḥ singled out after remembrance?  [وَسَبِّحُوهُ · rhetoric]
turns on: The distinction depends on whether tasbīḥ is a special form of spoken remembrance, an especially important prayer, or optional prayer.
- 33:42/q06/p1 It is a central or especially excellent form of remembrance. — Kashshāf says singling it out marks special merit; Bayḍāwī calls it the core of remembrance. Nasafī compares Gabriel and Michael singled out among angels and links its merit to denying unworthy attributes. Bursevī calls it the central adornment; Elmalılı likewise calls it central. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the formula comprehensively praises God and denies imperfections. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 33:42/q06/p2 Prayer is singled out as the specially timed form of general remembrance. — Kashshāf says all good deeds may be dhikr, with prayers excelling them. Abū Ḥayyān singles prayer out of all obedience, while Islahi says constant remembrance is general and set prayer times the specific. Kuran Yolu finds the second command probably refers to prayers. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 33:42/q06/p3 If it means optional prayer, it is not a specific instance within general remembrance. — Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes this possible reading from the verbal formula reading. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 33:42/q07 Who may recite these remembrances, and in what state?  [وَسَبِّحُوهُ · law]
- 33:42/q07/p1 People in ritual purity, minor impurity, or major impurity may recite the formulas. — Mujāhid includes all three states. Baghawī and Qurṭubī repeat the view. Bursevī cites a hadith urging even the person in major impurity not to omit transcendence, praise, oneness and greatness; he infers the same even more strongly for minor impurity. Purity and ablution remain proper etiquette. [5 notes: THALABI(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI]

## 33:42/q08 What reported benefits or practices accompany morning, evening, or repeated glorification?  [وَسَبِّحُوهُ بُكْرَةًۭ وَأَصِيلًا · reports]
- 33:42/q08/p1 Those who remember and glorify God can have the angels pray for them. — Sulaym ibn ʿĀmir reports a man's dream of angels praying at every movement; Abū Umāma cites 33:41–42 as the means. Ibn Kathīr points to 33:43 for God's and the angels' blessings following remembrance; al-Ṭabarī reports Ibn ʿAbbās on the same consequence. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(سليم بن عامر); IBNKATHIR-FULL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 33:42/q08/p2 An hour's remembrance after dawn and afternoon brings divine sufficiency until the next period. — Abū Hurayra reports God's instruction and promise. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة)]
- 33:42/q08/p3 Sitting in remembrance until sunrise or sunset is better than freeing descendants of Ishmael. — Abū Umāma says the dawn session is worth more than freeing two or more, and the afternoon session more than freeing four. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو أمامة)]
- 33:42/q08/p4 One hundred morning repetitions of 'Glory and praise belong to God' earn a thousand good deeds. — Abū al-Dardāʾ reports the Prophet saying that the day's sins do not match these merits and further good deeds remain. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو الدرداء)]
- 33:42/q08/p5 Saying Subḥān Allāh al-ʿAẓīm plants a tree or palm in Paradise. — Al-Durr reports this from Muʿādh ibn Anas, Ibn Mardawayh and Jābir. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 33:42/q08/p6 The Prophet commended the accessible formula Subḥān Allāh wa-bi-ḥamdihī. — The report describes the expression as two near or readily accessible words, as excerpted. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 33:42/q08/p7 A hundred daily repetitions of Subḥān Allāh wa-bi-ḥamdihī erase sins even as numerous as sea foam. — Al-Durr reports the Prophetic statement. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 33:42/q08/p8 One hundred glorifications earn a thousand good deeds and erase a thousand sins. — The Prophet gives this answer to a question about earning a thousand good deeds in one day. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 33:42/q08/p9 The phrase 'Allāhu akbar kabīran wa-subḥānallāhi bukratan wa-aṣīlā' can replace counting glorifications with pebbles or seeds. — ʿAbdallāh advises a Hamdānī woman who counted her recitations with such objects. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(عبد الله)]
- 33:42/q08/p10 Dawn prayer followed by remembrance until sunrise and two rakʿahs earns a complete ḥajj and ʿumra reward three times over. — Bursevī reports the hadith. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 33:42/q08/p11 Sleeping before sunrise and certain grave acts make the earth cry out. — Bursevī reports a tradition coupling sleeping then with unlawful bloodshed and bathing after fornication. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 33:42/q08/p12 Sufi practitioners gather for remembrance after dawn until sunrise prayer. — Bursevī says this remembrance acts powerfully on the soul and is preferred to recitation at that time. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 33:42/q08/p13 Blessings on the Prophet, supplication and tasbīḥ are preferred to recitation while prayer is prohibited. — Bursevī quotes al-Qunya for this preference. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 33:42/q09 How does this command relate to the surrounding passage and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes variously connect the command to its preceding controversy, the following promise, and parallel verses.
- 33:42/q09/p1 Other verses name or prescribe comparable times of glorification and prayer. — Ibn Kathīr cites 30:17–18 for evening, morning and other day periods; Study Quran cites the same pair and 6:52, 18:28, 19:11, 24:36, 48:9 for persistent devotion. Islahi points to 17:78 and 20:130 for prayer times and 2:45 for prayer's tie to perseverance. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 33:42/q09/p2 It redirects believers from conflict with hypocrites over the Prophet to remembrance. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says replacing argument or insult over rumors about the Prophet's marriage to Zaynab avoids civic strife; he compares remembrance replacing tribal boasting after pilgrimage (2:200) and restraint from provoking retaliation against God (6:108). It follows the instruction to the Prophet to leave harm aside (33:48); continued dispute would mark disobedient hypocrites. The new address remains contextually connected. Islahi likewise urges disregarding the hypocrites' uproar and praising God by night and day. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 33:42/q09/p3 The next verse promises divine and angelic blessings to those who remember. — Ibn Kathīr connects the practice to 33:43; Ibn ʿAbbās, as reported by al-Ṭabarī, says God and the angels then pray for those who remember in every condition. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 33:42/q10 What does the remembrance urged here accomplish for the believer?  [* · interpretation]
- 33:42/q10/p1 It establishes servitude and sustains the believer's awareness of God. — Abū al-Ḥusayn ibn Hind says God calls and selects believers through the Prophet, directs them to unity and commands servitude; remembrance establishes that servitude. Kuran Yolu calls tongue and heart remembrance, reflection and awareness the energy and lifeline of servitude. [2 notes: SULAMI(أبو الحسين بن هند); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 33:42/q10/p2 It brings God's and the angels' blessings. — Ibn Kathīr cites the following verse 33:43; Ibn ʿAbbās says remembrance has no fixed limit and its practitioners receive these blessings. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 33:42/q10/p3 It shields believers and supports perseverance against enemies. — Islahi calls remembrance a shield against Satan and links calls to persevere with prayer, referring to the discussion of 2:45. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 33:42/q10/p4 It gives the soul a powerful response to devotion at dawn. — Bursevī says Sufi remembrance after dawn until the sunrise prayer strongly affects the soul. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 33:42/q11 What inward images are attached to remembering or glorifying God here?  [* · inward]
- 33:42/q11/p1 Remembrance can be the unbroken awareness of one who never forgets God. — Sulamī cites an anonymous poem whose speaker says remembrance is unnecessary because God is never forgotten. [1 notes: SULAMI]
- 33:42/q11/p2 Love's remembrance is death and renewed life through longing and hope. — ʿAlī ibn ʿAbd al-Raḥīm's verses make love life through hope and death through longing; inexhaustible love never forgets the beloved. [1 notes: SULAMI(على بن عبد الرحيم)]
- 33:42/q11/p3 The heart's dawn begins as the lower self's darkness recedes, leading to enduring annihilation. — Qāshānī depicts a heart lit at morning and the spirit's sun setting in annihilation of Essence; remembrance continues to everlasting annihilation. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 33:42/q11/p4 Spiritual attraction reveals divine light even as created lamps fade. — Bursevī's Persian lines say the inwardly perceptive see an eternal sun in an atom and a sea in a drop; another couplet urges familiarity with love's sea and purity like a shell. He prays for spiritual acts that bring blessings and meet needs. [3 notes: BURSEVI]

## 33:42/q12 Why is morning named before aṣīl here, while 30:17 begins with evening?  [بُكْرَةًۭ وَأَصِيلًا · rhetoric]
- 33:42/q12/p1 Morning naturally precedes afternoon; 30:17 follows a different calendrical starting point. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says this verse simply follows the day's order with no special foregrounding of aṣīl. In 30:17 evening includes early night, which begins a day in the Arab and Islamic reckoning of a month. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 33:42/q13 Does this verse support an early prescription of only two daily prayers?  [* · sciences]
- 33:42/q13/p1 No; the verse is Medinan and the claimed two-prayer report is weak. — Qurṭubī rejects using this verse for the claim that prayer was first prescribed only at the two ends of the day and says to disregard that report. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 33:42/q14 Where is the fuller lexical discussion of aṣīl in al-Wāḥidī's commentary?  [وَأَصِيلًا · links]
- 33:42/q14/p1 Under the phrase bi-l-ghuduww wa-l-āṣāl. — Al-Wāḥidī refers the reader there. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]
