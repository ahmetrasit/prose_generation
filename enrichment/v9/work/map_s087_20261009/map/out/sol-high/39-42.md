# 39:42: 26 questions

## 39:42/q01 Why is God named as the taker of souls?  [ٱللَّهُ يَتَوَفَّى · theology]
- 39:42/q01/p1 God alone truly takes souls and governs life and death; angels act at His command as delegated causes. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Qurṭubī infer exclusive divinity. Rāzī reconciles 32:11 and 6:61 through the Angel of Death and assistants; al-Baghawī's account says angels extract, their chief orders, and God creates death. Bursevī says God commands even what angels cannot extract alone. Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Ālūsī see exclusive force in fronting Allah. Elmalılı stresses the soul's return to God and distinguishes God's direct taking of the rational soul from the Angel's taking of bodily life; he notes 15:29, 6:62 and 32:11. Bursevī relates exceptional direct taking of Fāṭima and Dhū al-Nūn's request. [17 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURHAN(البغوي); BURSEVI(ذو النون المصري,زهرة الرياض); ELMALILI(Fahreddin Râzî); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; ELMALILI+]

## 39:42/q02 What does yatawaffā mean?  [يَتَوَفَّى · meaning]
turns on: Whether complete taking names death specifically or also other forms of divine taking.
- 39:42/q02/p1 It means to take or receive something fully; death is so called from this complete taking. — Ibn Qutayba derives it from istīfāʾ and compares doubled forms. Jishumī and Ṭabrisī use collecting one's full due or debt; al-Majmaʿ agrees. Elmalılı compares delivering a trust in full. Asad calls the death sense tropical because vital impulses are withdrawn. Al-Qāmūs glosses it as taking the spirit. [7 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; JISHUMI; TABRISI; BURSEVI(القاموس); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(المجمع); ASAD-NOTES]
- 39:42/q02/p2 Across Qur'anic use tawaffī can signify death, sleep, or being raised; taking need not itself mean dying. — Sahl lists three senses; al-Farrāʾ takes 3:55 as Jesus being taken from earth without death. [2 notes: TUSTARI; JISHUMI(الفراء)]

## 39:42/q03 What does nafs mean in Arabic usage?  [ٱلْأَنفُسَ · meaning]
- 39:42/q03/p1 Nafs can name blood, breath, and the soul through their association with breathing and life. — Ibn Qutayba explains the naming of blood by association with breath; lexicographers cite nafisat and nafisāʾ for menstrual bleeding, and Ibrāhīm's rule uses nafs for flowing blood. Nasama derives from nafas; an unnamed hadith uses it for breath. Rabw is called nafs because of breathing; ḥatfa nafsihi and ḥatfa anfihi refer to dying in bed as breath leaves through nose and mouth. [7 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(أصحاب اللغة,إبراهيم,بعض الحديث)]

## 39:42/q04 What does al-anfus refer to here?  [ٱلْأَنفُسَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the predicates death and sleep require the whole person, or apply to a soul whose relation to the body changes.
- 39:42/q04/p1 It refers to souls or spirits attached to bodies. — Al-Baghawī identifies the spirits taken after provision and lifespan end. Abū Ḥayyān calls them spirits; Bursevī calls the speaking, sovereign human spirit nafs by bodily attachment and rūḥ by its immaterial return. Ṭabāṭabāʾī limits the reference to spirits attached to bodies, not all spirits. Elmalılı calls it the self-aware person and says nafs and rūḥ may name aspects of one substance; al-Qurṭubī cites death reports where sight follows spirit or soul. Bursevī locates animal spirit in the brain and human spirit in the heart without treating either as contained there; he reports Sunni spatial location of human spirits. [20 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة,أم سلمة,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 39:42/q04/p2 It means whole living persons, whose death is the loss of bodily life, sensation and perception. — Al-Zamakhsharī argues that death and sleep are predicated of the same whole being rather than of faculties; al-Nasafī follows the whole-self account. Ālūsī reports and challenges al-Kashf's full-removal rationale with hadith about souls corresponding to bodies. Asad rejects 'souls' because a soul survives bodily death. Elmalılı records al-Kashshāf's view. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري,الكشف); ELMALILI(Sâhib-i Keşşâf); ASAD-NOTES]
- 39:42/q04/p3 It denotes the rational soul whose governing attachment to the body is severed or restored. — Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī distinguish complete inward and outward severance at death from outward severance during sleep. Elmalılı cites other commentators and treats rational selves as retrievable without destruction; his sun-and-ray comparison does not require different substances. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(diğer müfessirîn); TABATABAI]

## 39:42/q05 Are the nafs and rūḥ distinct, and which is taken in sleep?  [ٱلْأَنفُسَ فِى مَنَامِهَا · theology]
turns on: Whether the sleeping being's continuing breath requires a second life-spirit, and whether death and sleep in the verse apply to one identical subject.
- 39:42/q05/p1 They are distinct: sleep takes the discerning nafs while the life-giving, breathing rūḥ remains; death takes both. — Ibn ʿAbbās compares their link to sunlight and its ray, assigning thought to nafs and motion to rūḥ. Al-Zajjāj argues breathing continues in sleep. Ibn Jurayj adds that a soul marked for death does not return and its spirit is taken. Bursevī distinguishes human from animal spirit and Ālūsī reports a hierarchy of rational soul, animal spirit and heart vapor. Al-Ṭabrisī distinguishes sleep's loss of waking perception from death's loss of life, with the living spirit remaining only in sleep. [33 notes: TUSTARI; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جريج,ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس,قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الأكثرون,قيل); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(قالوا); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,بعض الحكماء المتألهين); ELMALILI(İbn Abbas); KASHSHAF-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري أبو نصر)-; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)-]
- 39:42/q05/p2 Nafs and rūḥ are names for one soul; the same soul undergoes sleep and death in different degrees. — Al-Qurṭubī appeals to sound reports using both terms, including the valley report and the dying person's gaze. Abū Ḥayyān cites hadith and Arabic usage; Ibn al-Ṣalāḥ finds this closest to Scripture and Sunna. Zamakhsharī and Abū Naṣr al-Qushayrī object to distinct faculties because the verse speaks of one subject. Biqāʿī identifies the sleeper's and dying person's soul as the spirit. [12 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري أبو نصر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جبير); BIQAI-FULL(ابن الصلاح); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جبير,الزمخشري); ELMALILI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; TUSTARI(سهل)-]
- 39:42/q05/p3 Spirit and soul are distinct but connected so neither subsists without the other. — Al-Mubarrad says this; Sahl rejects it, citing primordial divine address to spirit, intellect and heart perception before bodily nature. [1 notes: TUSTARI(المبرد النحوي); TUSTARI(سهل)-]
- 39:42/q05/p4 The spirit can subsist by its own subtle nature without dense bodily nature. — Sahl cites the primordial address before bodies existed; he says the subtle natural self lives by spirit-light while spirit lives by remembrance, with 3:169 for the living dead. [5 notes: TUSTARI(سهل); SULAMI(سهل)]
- 39:42/q05/p5 Some distinguish a discriminating soul from an imaginative soul. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls such detailed divisions conjectural, as 17:85 withholds the spirit's exact reality; Abū Ḥayyān reports the division. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فرقة)]
- 39:42/q05/p6 The exact nature and distinctions of soul and spirit remain hidden. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya invokes 17:85 and finds speculation burdensome; al-Qurṭubī also invokes the spirit being God's command. Ibn al-Jawzī records two views without resolving them; Ālūsī says ʿAlī's heaven report needs interpretation if the soul is immaterial. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 39:42/q06 What happens when God takes a soul at death?  [حِينَ مَوْتِهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the death is predicated directly of the soul or through an understood reference to its body.
- 39:42/q06/p1 Death completely severs bodily life or the soul's bodily control; the soul is not restored to worldly life. — Muqātil ties it to the appointed decree. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī explain loss of bodily health and perception; Bayḍāwī, Rāzī, Bursevī and Ālūsī describe complete withdrawal of the soul or its light. Bursevī says the body becomes like dry wood, while the human spirit bears reason, faith and knowledge. Kur'an Yolu calls the soul the source of life and consciousness. [19 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]
- 39:42/q06/p2 God takes souls when bodies die; 'bodies' is understood in the wording. — Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Wasīṭ supply the omitted genitive; Bursevī uses 3:185, 'every soul tastes death,' to explain the separation. Ālūsī reports either ellipsis or figurative attribution of bodily states to souls. [7 notes: TABRISI; BURSEVI(الوسيط); ALUSI-FULL(الطبرسي,قيل); TABATABAI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 39:42/q07 What happens when God takes a person during sleep?  [وَٱلَّتِى لَمْ تَمُتْ فِى مَنَامِهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether taking in sleep means loss of action, a partial soul departure, or an actual soul journey, and when a sleeping person's final death occurs.
- 39:42/q07/p1 Sleep partially withdraws the soul or its bodily control and perception while bodily life remains. — Al-Farrāʾ prefers sleep itself as the taking, citing 6:60. Ibn ʿĪsā describes suspended action with spirit remaining; Zamakhsharī and Nasafī compare the sleeper's inability to discern or act to death. Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī limit severance to outward control; Rāzī calls it partial withdrawal of the soul's light. Ibn ʿĀshūr says ordinary speech does not call a sleeper dead and organs remain ready to resume. [28 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء وابن الأنباري); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عيسى); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R); FARRA+]
- 39:42/q07/p2 The soul or spirit leaves the body in sleep but a connected ray remains until it returns on waking. — ʿAlī likens the link to a ray and says return is faster than an instant; Muʿtamir likens it to wool drawn from a skein while most remains secretly connected. [5 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(علي); NASAFI-FULL(علي); BURSEVI(علي رضي الله عنه); QURTUBI-FULL(معتمر); TABRISI(أبو جعفر)]
- 39:42/q07/p3 God takes all souls nightly and chooses some for death that night. — Farqad says God asks each soul about its day and orders the Angel of Death to take those decreed to die; Ibn ʿAbbās describes a course cut off when death falls. Al-Asʾila al-Muqḥima says a sleeper's spirit is retained if its death coincides with sleep. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,فرقد); BURSEVI(الأسئلة المقحمة)]
- 39:42/q07/p4 A person's term ends while asleep, and that is when the sleeping person is taken. — Al-Farrāʾ reports this as the other account and prefers sleep itself as taking; al-Qurṭubī repeats both. [2 notes: FARRA; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء)]

## 39:42/q08 How are sleep and death related?  [حِينَ مَوْتِهَا فِى مَنَامِهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'death' describes sleep literally as a lesser instance or by comparison.
- 39:42/q08/p1 Sleep is a lesser or partial death; death is the complete, lasting form of the same taking. — Ibn Zayd says both are deaths; al-Qurṭubī cites ʿUmar's 'sibling of death,' a prophetic sleep/resurrection analogy and a report that Paradise has no sleep because it has no death. Bursevī says light death and deep sleep are valid descriptions from different views. Ibn Kathīr contrasts this verse's order with 6:60–61. [18 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,جابر بن عبد الله,عمر); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(aḥādīth)]
- 39:42/q08/p2 Sleep and death share loss of perception, but are distinct divine acts with different outcomes. — Biqāʿī says paired wording makes the distinction clear and that death, like sleep, will end. Ibn ʿĀshūr says death removes life and blocks return while sleep partially removes it and restores it repeatedly; he calls death language for sleep a comparison. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 39:42/q09 How are the clause about undying souls and 'in their sleep' construed?  [وَٱلَّتِى لَمْ تَمُتْ فِى مَنَامِهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the sleeping clause directly shares the first verb, repeats an understood verb, or is read by transposition.
- 39:42/q09/p1 'Those that did not die' coordinates with 'souls' as a second object of 'takes'; 'in their sleep' qualifies that taking. — Al-Samīn attaches the phrase to yatawaffā. Ālūsī permits manām as time noun or verbal noun; Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Ibn ʿĀshūr take it as temporal. Iṣlāḥī supplies the repeated yatawaffā in translation. [6 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:42/q09/p2 'In their sleep' attaches to an understood second occurrence of yatawaffā. — Bursevī supplies the verb after the coordinated clause; Iṣlāḥī likewise supplies it in translation. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:42/q09/p3 The clauses are reordered in sense: God takes and holds souls at death, while sleepers live to their term. — Jishumī records this alternative sequence. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]

## 39:42/q10 Why does the verse say 'at its death' but 'in its sleep'?  [حِينَ مَوْتِهَا فِى مَنَامِهَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the prepositions signal stability versus degree, or sharpen the distinction between the paired states.
- 39:42/q10/p1 Death is stable from its onset, whereas taking during sleep varies in intensity. — Ālūsī reports this distinction between ḥīn and fī. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:42/q10/p2 The paired wording distinguishes the two signs rather than repeating the same preposition for each. — Biqāʿī says 'by its death and by its sleep' would obscure their difference. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 39:42/q11 What does 'decreed death upon it' mean?  [قَضَىٰ عَلَيْهَا ٱلْمَوْتَ · meaning]
- 39:42/q11/p1 God decisively ordained its death at the end of its allotted life. — Biqāʿī glosses qaḍā as concluding and settling; Bursevī glosses it as decreeing. Muqātil and Jishumī link it to the finished term and removal of life and spirit. [4 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 39:42/q12 How is qaḍā ʿalayhā al-mawt read?  [قَضَىٰ عَلَيْهَا ٱلْمَوْتَ · readings]
turns on: Voice of qaḍā and case of al-mawt; some favor the active wording because it matches Allah as subject and active yursilu.
- 39:42/q12/p1 Active qaḍā with al-mawt accusative: God decreed death upon it. — The majority read it so. Abū ʿUbayd and Abū Ḥātim favor God's explicit agency; al-Naḥḥās, Abū ʿAlī and al-Qurṭubī favor agreement with active yursilu. Al-Qurṭubī also cites Allah as the preceding subject. Al-Naḥḥās and Abū ʿAlī say the passive means the same. [16 notes: FARRA; IBNMUJAHID(حمزة، الكسائي); FARISI-HUJJA(الباقون); NAHHAS(يحيى بن وثاب); JISHUMI(الباقون); BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي والآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أهل الكوفة غير عاصم وقتيبة؛ والباقون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); NASHR; IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); FARISI-HUJJA+; JISHUMI(أبو عبيد وأبو حاتم)+; QURTUBI-FULL+; TABRISI(أبو علي)+; THALABI(أبو عبيد وأبو حاتم)+]
- 39:42/q12/p2 Passive quḍiya with al-mawt nominative: death was decreed upon it. — Reported for Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī and, in various lists, Khalaf, al-Aʿmash, Ibn Waththāb, Ṭalḥa, ʿĪsā and other Kufans. Ibn ʿĀshūr analyzes the nominative as arising from dropped bi- or qaḍā meaning 'ordained'; al-Samīn calls al-mawt the passive subject. Qiraat-ER notes the shift of grammatical focus. Biqāʿī says it clarifies the settled decree. Al-Naḥḥās and Abū ʿAlī say the sense remains the same. [20 notes: FARRA; IBNMUJAHID(حمزة، الكسائي); NAHHAS(يحيى بن وثاب); FARISI-HUJJA(حمزة والكسائي); JISHUMI(حمزة والكسائي والأعمش); BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي والآخرون); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أهل الكوفة غير عاصم وقتيبة؛ والباقون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(حمزة والكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(الأعمش ويحيى بن وثاب وحمزة والكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); NASAFI-FULL(حمزة وعلى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(الأخوان); NASHR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(حمزة); IBNASHUR-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وخلف); QIRAAT-ER(Pss)]

## 39:42/q13 Which soul does God hold, and for how long?  [فَيُمْسِكُ ٱلَّتِى قَضَىٰ عَلَيْهَا ٱلْمَوْتَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the retained soul is described generally after death or specifically as one seized during sleep when death arrives.
- 39:42/q13/p1 He holds the soul decreed to die and prevents its return to the body; it remains until resurrection. — Jishumī, Ṭabrisī, Biqāʿī and Ālūsī specify resurrection. Ibn ʿĀshūr reads imsak as holding fast, figuratively maintaining the death state even if family wish revival. Bursevī distinguishes the later barzakh from the imaginal sleep barzakh and says earthly deeds shape its forms. [26 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى,سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:42/q13/p2 The holding concerns a sleeper whose death is decreed during that sleep; the other sleeper awakens. — Al-Asʾila al-Muqḥima states this explicitly; Bursevī says the bedtime prayer supports taking the holding clause as following the sleep clause. Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise says the sleeping soul decreed dead is kept. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الأسئلة المقحمة,النبي عليه السلام); TABATABAI]

## 39:42/q14 Do living and dead souls meet during sleep?  [فِى مَنَامِهَا · reports]
- 39:42/q14/p1 Yes; they meet, recognize and sometimes converse, then the dead are held and the living return. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, al-Suddī and Ibn ʿAbbās report the meeting. Ibn Kathīr places it in the highest assembly and cites a prophetic report through Ibn Mandah. Al-Haythamī says transmitters of the Ibn ʿAbbās version are Ṣaḥīḥ narrators. Ibn ʿAbbās's Juwaybir report adds a cord between heaven and earth; the reports say God returns the right souls without error. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي,سعيد بن جبير); THALABI(المفسرون,سعيد بن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); NASAFI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,بعض السلف); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(سعيد بن جبير)]

## 39:42/q15 Where does a sleeper's soul go and what is its connection to the body?  [فِى مَنَامِهَا · reports]
turns on: How literally heavenly ascent and a continuing bodily link should be understood.
- 39:42/q15/p1 It ascends toward heaven while a life-giving spirit or ray remains connected to the body. — Abū Jaʿfar likens the link to a sunbeam and says soul and spirit answer one another by God's permission. ʿAlī and Muʿtamir describe a ray or extended wool returning rapidly. A report says pure believers' spirits may prostrate beneath the Throne; Bursevī connects ablution to true dreams. [7 notes: TABRISI(أبو جعفر); BAGHAWI-FULL(علي); NASAFI-FULL(رُوي,علي); QURTUBI-FULL(معتمر); BURSEVI(علي رضي الله عنه)]
- 39:42/q15/p2 The soul travels through an imaginal realm during sleep and descends to bodily life on waking. — Bursevī distinguishes the bounded earlier imaginal realm from the later barzakh, describes its luminous measurable forms and knowledge taking form, and compares spiritual shedding to sleep. Ālūsī says the celestial soul turns to a world of light during bodily sleep. [9 notes: BURSEVI(قال بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:42/q16 Why are some dreams true and others false?  [فِى مَنَامِهَا · reports]
turns on: Whether to take the heavenly journey literally or as experience in imaginal or spiritual realms.
- 39:42/q16/p1 Visions seen while the soul is with God in heaven are true; devils mix falsehood into visions as it returns. — ʿAlī answers ʿUmar's question; ʿUmar marvels. Abū Jaʿfar says heavenly visions have interpretation and those between heaven and earth are satanic. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls these greater and lesser imaginal realms and refers to his Sūrat Yūsuf discussion. Ālūsī separates the report's heavenly and returning stages. [12 notes: IBNABIHATIM(علي بن أبي طالب); MAWARDI-FULL(علي); TABRISI(أبو جعفر); QURTUBI-FULL(علي); NASAFI-FULL(علي); DURR-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); ALUSI-FULL(علي); TABATABAI(علي بن أبي طالب,عمر بن الخطاب)]
- 39:42/q16/p2 True dreams arise when the soul receives from the perfected world of light; false ones reflect its return to worldly delusion. — Ālūsī gives this interpretation of the report and says persons vary in readiness and insight. Bursevī recommends sleeping in ablution for true dreams and divine converse. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 39:42/q17 What prayer and practice are reported for bedtime in connection with the verse?  [فَيُمْسِكُ وَيُرْسِلُ · reports]
turns on: The transmitted formulas emphasize either divine retention and release or sleep and waking as death and revival.
- 39:42/q17/p1 The Prophet taught prayer for mercy or forgiveness if the soul is retained, and protection if it is released. — Abū Hurayra versions add dusting bedding with its inner edge and lying on the right side. Al-Qurṭubī notes Bukhārī and Muslim, wording variants in Ibn Mājah and Tirmidhī, and a waking thanksgiving prayer. Abū Ayyūb asks forgiveness if taken and protection if deferred; the prayer's wording underlies Bursevī's clause reading and his lesson that life serves righteousness. [13 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو أيوب); THALABI(أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو أيوب,أبو هريرة); BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]
- 39:42/q17/p2 The Prophet said at night 'In Your name I die and live' and on waking praised revival after death and recalled resurrection. — Hudhayfa transmits it in Bukhārī; Iṣlāḥī cites the believer's morning supplication as an echo of daily resurrection. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 39:42/q18 What happened when the Prophet's party slept past sunrise?  [فِى مَنَامِهَا · reports]
- 39:42/q18/p1 He said God had taken their souls or spirits during sleep and returned them when He willed. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls Bilāl's valley report authentic. Abū Qatāda, Abū Juḥayfa and Anas describe waking after sunrise; Anas calls souls entrusted in bodies. Abū Umāma says the Prophet recited this verse after the delayed prayer. Ālūsī names Aḥmad, Bukhārī, Abū Dāwūd, Nasāʾī and Ibn Abī Shayba for Abū Qatāda. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(حديث بلال); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو أمامة,أبو جحيفة,أبو قتادة,أنس بن مالك); ALUSI-FULL(أبو قتادة,أنس بن مالك)]

## 39:42/q19 What does God send back when the sleeper wakes?  [وَيُرْسِلُ ٱلْأُخْرَىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether irsāl names the return of the soul or more broadly the person's preservation and reprieve.
- 39:42/q19/p1 He returns the other, living sleeper's soul to its body or restores its bodily control. — Ibn ʿĀshūr glosses irsāl as release and permission to return and says it restores full life. Al-Qurṭubī says restraint is removed so sensation returns. Ālūsī describes inward and outward governing relation restored. Reports stress that God does not confuse living with dead souls. [21 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(السدي); THALABI(سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:42/q19/p2 Sending also means preserving the other soul from actual death until its term. — Ālūsī reports this further sense. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 39:42/q19/p3 The wording means a person is returned until the term without that soul itself being taken. — Abū Jubayr describes the living soul's return 'without taking them' in his report. [1 notes: MUQATIL(أبو جبير)]

## 39:42/q20 What is the appointed term?  [إِلَىٰٓ أَجَلٍۢ مُّسَمًّى · referent]
turns on: Whether the stated limit is final bodily death, the next awakening, or Judgment.
- 39:42/q20/p1 It is the person's divinely fixed death or end of lifespan; repeated waking continues until then. — Bursevī and Ālūsī say the limit applies to the general series of sendings, avoiding the implication that one awakening prevents later sleep. Ibn ʿĀshūr attaches the phrase to yursilu and glosses tasmiyah as designation, citing 2:282. Study Quran cites other verses that give every creature a term. [17 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 39:42/q20/p2 It can refer to the Day of Judgment, when all return to God. — Study Quran gives this as another possible referent. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 39:42/q20/p3 It is the next waking return to the body, a reprieve before death and reckoning. — Study Quran attributes this reading to R and cites 35:45. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 39:42/q21 How does the verse's expression arrange and emphasize its claims?  [ٱللَّهُ فَيُمْسِكُ وَيُرْسِلُ ٱلْأُخْرَىٰٓ إِنَّ فِى ذَٰلِكَ لَءَايَٰتٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Which feature of the wording carries the emphasis: sequence, fronting, number, deixis, or particles.
- 39:42/q21/p1 It first gathers all souls under God's taking, then separates the retained and released kinds. — Al-Ṭībī calls this jamʿ wa-tafrīq; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the fāʾ in 'so He retains' explicative of an inference from taking. [2 notes: ITQAN(الطيبي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:42/q21/p2 Fronting 'Allah' emphasizes or restricts the true taking to Him. — Ālūsī favors exclusivity from context; Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects it to angels as intermediate causes. Biqāʿī says the named subject fits the claim that only God guards people. [3 notes: TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 39:42/q21/p3 The plural 'souls' treats many as though one, while keeping literal plurality clear. — Biqāʿī calls it a plural of paucity used for many. Al-Burhān separately observes that plurals can cover few or many and cites the generic article in al-ghurufāt at 38:24. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN]
- 39:42/q21/p4 The distal 'that,' indefinite plural 'signs,' and emphatic conclusion elevate and multiply the evidence. — Ālūsī gives recency, starting point and exalted rank as reasons for the demonstrative's form, and abundance and greatness for nunation. Ibn ʿĀshūr says 'indeed' presses a neglected implication. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:42/q22 What signs does reflection on this verse reveal?  [إِنَّ فِى ذَٰلِكَ لَءَايَٰتٍۢ لِّقَوْمٍۢ يَتَفَكَّرُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the primary lesson is divine uniqueness, resurrection, or conduct flowing from both.
- 39:42/q22/p1 God's exact control over death, sleep, retention and return proves His unique power, knowledge, wisdom and right to worship. — Al-Baghawī notes that God never errs in holding or returning. Rāzī points to ordered soul-body relations; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ṭabrisī infer oneness against idols. Bayḍāwī and Bursevī add mercy and the soul's persistence and fate. Ibn ʿĀshūr says tafakkur is repeated, effortful thought, and the signs are two distinct acts of divine craftsmanship encountered daily. [26 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABRISI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:42/q22/p2 The daily restoration from sleep is evidence that God can resurrect the dead. — Muqātil and Ibn al-Jawzī make resurrection the reflective point. Biqāʿī reasons that the agent ending sleep's minor death can end greater death; al-Kawāshī and al-Kāshifī invoke death as sleep and resurrection as waking, also mentioning the Torah. Iṣlāḥī says the daily cycle rehearses renewed life. [8 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكواشي والكاشفي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:42/q22/p3 The signs teach reliance on God and make the universe an educator in truths needed for salvation. — Iṣlāḥī draws exclusive trust from exclusive divine control and calls creation a lesson in Qur'anic truths; Biqāʿī says recognition eases affliction and consoles the Prophet. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 39:42/q23 What does the verse imply about the soul after bodily death?  [فَيُمْسِكُ ٱلَّتِى قَضَىٰ عَلَيْهَا ٱلْمَوْتَ · theology]
turns on: How retention after death relates to survival, consciousness, and punishment before resurrection.
- 39:42/q23/p1 The soul survives separately from the body and is held for resurrection and reckoning. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī infers distinct existence from separation. Al-Qurṭubī describes a subtle body capable of being drawn out, wrapped, carried, seeing and smelling; 56:83 depicts its exit at the throat. Bursevī says the human spirit is questioned and rewarded or punished in the grave; Elmalılı says death is detention, cites 36:52 and 40:11, and favors ʿAlī's couplet that the dead will be questioned. His contrast with al-Kashshāf addresses whether separated disbelievers escape punishment. [11 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ELMALILI(Hazret-i Ali)+]
- 39:42/q23/p2 The rational soul is not itself subject to death. — Elmalılı reports al-Kashshāf's answer to the problem of punishment after sleep-like separation. [1 notes: ELMALILI(Sâhib-i Keşşâf)]
- 39:42/q23/p3 Souls are created and dependent on God; their consciousness can be withdrawn without destroying them. — Al-Qurṭubī calls the subtle soul beginningful but not perishing. Elmalılı cites 8:24 for divine control over a self's consciousness. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 39:42/q24 How does this verse connect with its surrounding passage and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Which neighboring claim the verse is read as developing: guardianship, divine sufficiency, guidance, or the sūra's broader death theme.
- 39:42/q24/p1 It follows 'you are not their guardian': God alone guards, takes, restores, and governs people. — Jishumī, Ṭabrisī, Biqāʿī and Elmalılı link 39:41 to God's continuous guardianship; Elmalılı notes 39:62. Biqāʿī says an absent, sleeping or dead deputy cannot preserve guidance. [5 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 39:42/q24/p2 It elaborates God's sufficiency and the Creator's sole claim to worship against powerless idols. — Abū Muslim connects 39:36 to God's sufficiency for the Prophet; another Jishumī report links 39:38 to the Creator's ownership of life and death. Biqāʿī says the next rejection of intercessors follows from exclusive divine power. [4 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم,قيل); TABRISI(قيل); BIQAI-FULL]
- 39:42/q24/p3 Death and sleep illustrate guidance and misguidance in the preceding discussion. — Biqāʿī pairs guidance with life and waking; Elmalılı and Rāzī as reported by Asad make the comparison, with temporary straying like sleep. Elmalılı also ties 39:30–31 and 10:108 to each person's own guidance or error. Kur'an Yolu says the argument turns from human choice in 39:41 to divine governance and then 39:43. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES(Razi); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:42/q24/p4 The passage joins other Qur'anic death and life texts. — Ibn Kathīr contrasts the order of 6:60–61; al-Sāmarrāʾī links the sūra's 'struck down' with death and sleep; Study Quran notes that 39:53 later opens mercy even to squandered souls. Iṣlāḥī says the next discussion puts life and death under God's authority. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 39:42/q25 What further spiritual lessons are drawn from death and sleep?  [* · inward]
turns on: Which aspect of the soul's separation yields the lesson: remembrance, bodily instrument, unseen travel, or divine encounter.
- 39:42/q25/p1 The knower makes natural life and spirit live by remembrance and striving. — Sahl says dense nature lives on food, drink and pleasure, while spirit lives by remembrance; he cites the living dead of 3:169. [2 notes: TUSTARI]
- 39:42/q25/p2 The body is a temporary instrument for the soul's knowledge; death can relieve its burden after spiritual harvest. — Bursevī cites a Mathnawī couplet, compares the body to a hunter's net, and explains the Prophet's 'death is a believer's gift'; if it fails before the harvest, 23:99–100 describes regret. A Persian line calls death a door all pass. [5 notes: BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام)]
- 39:42/q25/p3 Sleep and spiritual shedding let the spirit visit unseen realms; wakeful shedding can bring stronger witnessing. — Bursevī says subtle spirits can travel beyond dense bodies, renew witnessing and longing. He explains sleep's pleasure as merciful care and death's pain as the Angel's hand. He identifies brain vapors and relaxed nerves as sleep's bodily process. [4 notes: BURSEVI(قال بعضهم)]
- 39:42/q25/p4 The believer's death is a divine meeting for which God prepares the soul. — Bursevī cites a sacred report of divine hesitation over taking a believer: he denies perplexity and reads it as showing prepared blessings until the servant longs for death. Sharḥ al-Sunna instead says God repeatedly sends harms but delays their fatal effect; dislike of death concerns its severity. A report says none sees God before death, and Persian verse speaks of dying before death for vision and security. [5 notes: BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام,شرح السنة)]

## 39:42/q26 What other claims are attached to this discussion?  [* · theology]
turns on: These are distinct accompanying claims rather than competing explanations of one phrase.
- 39:42/q26/p1 Life and death are alternating accidents that only God can produce. — Jishumī reports this kalām account. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 39:42/q26/p2 Life is an accident, and a loss of necessary knowledge can occur while life and spirit remain; death's seizure excludes life. — Jishumī reports this distinct analysis. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 39:42/q26/p3 Guidance and misguidance are human acts, while God intended the revealed book to guide and to command action. — Jishumī also says religious argumentation is valid and worship belongs to the Creator of blessings rather than one unable to benefit or harm. [4 notes: JISHUMI]
- 39:42/q26/p4 Traditional formulaic accounts of rhetorical inimitability fail to convince modern Arabic readers. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ criticizes rigid templates and inflated language, including al-Khaṭṭābī's account. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
