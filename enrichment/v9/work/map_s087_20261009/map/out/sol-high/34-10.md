# 34:10: 18 questions

## 34:10/q01 Who is David, and what is said of his name?  [دَاوُۥدَ · referent]
- 34:10/q01/p1 His foreign name may be linked to the phrase 'he healed his wound.' — Qushayri gives both accounts; Bursevi reads the absence of connecting letters in the name as a sign of detachment from the world, hidden and manifest grace, and divinely bestowed names. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI]
- 34:10/q01/p2 David is the Israelite prophet. — Kur’an Yolu identifies him from the Qur'an and Bible, citing 2:251 and 27:15. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 34:10/q02 What is the favor given to David?  [فَضْلًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether faḍl identifies a particular gift, gathers his gifts, or describes a distinction in rank.
- 34:10/q02/p1 Prophethood, often together with the Psalms or revelation. — Muqatil cites 4:113; Wahidi reports Muqatil's prophecy and scripture and Kalbi's prophecy plus worldly gifts. Study Quran cites 4:163. Tabrisī adds decisive judgment and miracles. [11 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN(JJ); TABRISI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 34:10/q02/p2 A comprehensive divine gift, exceptionally great but not necessarily superiority in every respect. — Ibn ʿAbbas says none before or after got the like. Baydawi considers superiority over prophets or all people; Alusi reports rankings over earlier prophets, Israelite prophets, all except Muhammad, or all prophets, and questions uniqueness of kingship and voice. Bursevi cites 2:253 and allows advantage in one respect amid inferiority in another. Kur'an Yolu permits a simple 'favor' with no claim of superiority. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 34:10/q02/p3 His beautiful voice, sweet prayer, good character, and intimacy. — Sulami's Nahrajuri stresses sweet intimate prayer. Qushayri lists voice, prayer, character and justice. Qurtubi cites 35:1, the Prophet's praise of Abu Musa's 'flute of David,' and the scholars' explanation of mizmar as voice. [13 notes: SULAMI(النهرجوري); MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI-FULL(العلماء,النبي,قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو عثمان النهدي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ELMALILI(Resûl-i Ekrem sallallâhu aleyhi ve sellem); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 34:10/q02/p4 Subjection and response of mountains and birds. — Mawardi lists this favor; Qushayri says their response consoled David in grief; Qurtubi also mentions people. - 34:10/q02/p5 Just judgment, discernment, intelligence, or knowledge. — Qurtubi cites 27:15 for knowledge and 38:26 for just rule; Study Quran cites 21:79 and 27:15. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 34:10/q02/p6 Strength, kingship, soft iron, armor craft and other worldly gifts. — Qurtubi cites 38:17 for strength and separately lists iron; Wahidi reports Kalbi's worldly gifts. Ibn Ashur enumerates courage, reform, self-support, long life and more; Kur'an Yolu adds reconciliation. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 34:10/q02/p7 David's repentance and return to God. — Qurtubi cites 38:25. Qushayri says humility and apology followed his lapse; Sulami's account presents return after a stumble. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; SULAMI; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 34:10/q02/p8 The Psalms themselves. — Mawardi and Qurtubi give this separately; Abu Hayyan lists them among David's gifts. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 34:10/q02/p9 Reliance on God and awareness of utter need. — Sulami's Ibn ʿAta says the servant has no good apart from God in obedience or sin; Jaʿfar says trust and reliance; Ibn Khallal says knowing one's measure and returning. Qushayri reports David's awareness of need. [5 notes: SULAMI(ابن خلال,ابن عطاء,جعفر); QUSHAYRI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 34:10/q02/p10 Mercy for the weak and love for the poor. — Sulami transmits ʿAbd al-ʿAziz; Mawardi lists mercy. [2 notes: SULAMI(عبد العزيز); MAWARDI-FULL]

## 34:10/q03 What does 'from Us' and the indefinite 'favor' convey?  [مِنَّا فَضْلًۭا · rhetoric]
- 34:10/q03/p1 The favor originates directly from God and its indefinite form magnifies it. — Razi says 'from Us' marks special prophetic rank despite general favors, citing 9:21. Biqai denies any independent created source. Alusi says beneficence is magnified and fronted for focus and anticipation, citing 18:65. Ibn Ashur says min denotes origin governed by ātaynā, citing 28:57; he lists David's gifts. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 34:10/q03/p2 Ātaynā simply means 'We gave'; faḍl is a gift or increase. — Qurtubi says faḍl distinguishes David above others; Tabatabai says gift; Bursevi says increase. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI; BURSEVI]

## 34:10/q04 How is the call to the mountains connected syntactically to the gift?  [يَٰجِبَالُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the quoted call apposes the gift or giving, or begins with an implied saying.
- 34:10/q04/p1 An omitted 'We said' introduces the quoted command, explaining the favor. — Yahya says God called the mountains. Kashshaf, Razi, Baydawi, Nasafi and Abu Hayyan allow apposition to faḍlan or ātaynā; Ibn Ashur calls omission of the speech verb common and sees the clause as explanation. Wahidi reports Abu Ubayda and Zajjaj's ellipsis. [10 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الزمخشري); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة والزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 34:10/q05 What does 'awwibī with him' ask the mountains to do?  [أَوِّبِى مَعَهُۥ · meaning]

Wahidi reports exegetical consensus and Ibn Qutayba's alternative. 38:18–19 and 21:79 support praise. Alusi prefers Arabic taʾwīb; Ibn Kathir prefers this contextual sense. ## 34:10/q06 How is 'awwibī' read and formed?  [أَوِّبِى · readings]
turns on: Doubled wāw from taʾwīb versus light wāw from awb; whether doubling is intensive or causative.
- 34:10/q06/p1 A light wāw with ḍamma on hamza reads 'return with him' from awb. — Hasan, Qatada, Ibn Abi Ishaq, Ibn Abbas and others are named; Zajjaj allows return to praise. Tabari rejects an alternative as contrary to authoritative readers. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم); ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن وقتادة وابن أبي إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحلبي عن عبد الوارث); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة وغيرهما); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس، والحسن، وقتادة، وابن أبي إسحاق); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن وقتادة وابن أبي إسحاق); RAZI-FULL; TAB-FULL-]
- 34:10/q06/p2 The common reading doubles wāw, from taʾwīb, expressing repeated or caused return of praise. — Abu Hayyan argues that doubling makes intransitive āba transitive, against Ibn ʿAtiyya's intensive analysis. Razi and Kashshaf list both forms. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]

## 34:10/q07 Why is the imperative to the mountains feminine singular?  [يَٰجِبَالُ أَوِّبِى · grammar]
- 34:10/q07/p1 A nonrational plural regularly takes feminine singular agreement. — Ibn ʿAtiyya compares a call to horses and 20:18; Abu Hayyan cites poetic horse calls and notes rare extension to rational feminine plurals; Alusi adds a poetic command to women. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL]

## 34:10/q08 How did the mountains' response occur?  [يَٰجِبَالُ أَوِّبِى مَعَهُۥ · reports]
turns on: A created audible voice versus natural echo, distinct glorification, or another mechanism.
- 34:10/q08/p1 God enabled real, audible glorification by the mountains as David glorified. — Kashshaf compares speech created in the tree; Tabrisī allows created praise and understanding; Qurtubi calls it a miracle. Alusi adduces pebbles heard in the hands of the Prophet and Abu Bakr. Biqai adds pebbles, food, greeting stone, moaning trunk, lizard and camel reports, and argues that birds' own speech excludes mere echo. Abu Hayyan notes the Muʿtazilite implication of the tree analogy. [16 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazı müfessirler)]
- 34:10/q08/p2 The mountain sound was an echo of David's chanting. — Thaʿlabi and Ibn ʿAtiyya report this as the origin of mountain echoes; Baghawi and Wahidi also describe birds gathering overhead. Wahb says their response supported David when he faltered. [6 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(وهب); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(وهب بن منبه); QURTUBI-FULL(وهب بن منبه); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Diğer bir yorum); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-; BIQAI-FULL-]
- 34:10/q08/p3 God caused mountains to move physically with David. — Jubba'i considers it a miracle; Alusi denies any report and cites mountains as earth's pegs. Tabatabai cites 38:18–19 against motion. [3 notes: TABRISI(الجبائي); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 34:10/q08/p4 Their praise refreshed or consoled David. — A report says God made David hear them when tired; another says they comforted him after he pledged exceptional worship and an angel reminded him God hears a worm. Qushayri says the response consoled grief. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); THALABI(ويقال); QUSHAYRI]
- 34:10/q08/p5 The mountains retained their own distinct praise while joining David. — Bursevi says true unveiling hears a separate remembrance from each thing; their cooperation need not erase that. Kur'an Yolu reports distinctive glorification. [3 notes: BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazı müfessirler)]

## 34:10/q09 Why address mountains and birds as though they could understand?  [يَٰجِبَالُ وَٱلطَّيْرَ · rhetoric]
- 34:10/q09/p1 The vivid command displays God's sovereignty and David's stature by portraying even hard mountains and fleeing birds as obedient. — Kashshaf says this is more majestic than plain narration; Razi says stone and birds make the miracle striking and hardened human hearts worse. Biqai sees earthly and heavenly signs in hardest mountains and light birds; Ibn Ashur calls the address a creative command and notes the compact majesty of 'with him.' [17 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 34:10/q10 How is 'the birds' read and parsed?  [وَٱلطَّيْرَ · readings]

- 34:10/q10/p1 The accusative coordinates with the vocative's underlying syntactic position: birds too are called. — Sibawayh, Zajjaj, Tabari and others invoke the accusative position of vocatives; ʿIsa ibn ʿUmar reads it so. Yunus, Abu Amr and others prefer accusative in such coordination; Jishumi's Ali ibn Isa prefers another surface-based analysis. ## 34:10/q11 What did the birds do with David?  [وَٱلطَّيْرَ · reports]
- 34:10/q11/p1 They joined his glorification and answered him, sometimes gathering or circling above. — Ibn Zayd and Ibn Abbas report praise; Ibn Jawzi adds listeners weeping. Wahb has David call mountains to glorify, birds to answer, then chant Psalms; Ibn Kathir says birds stopped and answered in varied languages. Qurtubi says wild animals gathered and water stopped to listen; Wahb as transmitted by Ibn Kathir mentions seventy vocal tones and imitators' instruments. [23 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNABIHATIM(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,وهب بن منبه); QURTUBI-FULL(وهب بن منبه); IBNKATHIR-FULL(وهب بن منبه); BIQAI-FULL(وهب بن منبه); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد,وهب); ASAD-NOTES; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 34:10/q11/p2 In one reported aftermath, the birds dispersed when David's state changed after repentance. — Qushayri says his voice was familiar but birds left because his lapse had caused estrangement, whereas earlier obedience brought intimacy; he says the spiritual meaning belonged to David, not the creatures. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 34:10/q12 What does softening the iron mean?  [وَأَلَنَّا لَهُ ٱلْحَدِيدَ · interpretation]
turns on: Physical pliability, David's strength or skill, ordinary smelting, symbolic sharpness, or inward allegory.
- 34:10/q12/p1 God made iron physically pliable in David's hand like wax, dough, wet clay, or threads. — Muqatil says he plaited it; Yahya says three fingers sufficed. Qatada, Hasan and others say no heating or hammering. Farra says it flowed. Ibn Ashur specifies rings and rivets; he also describes ordinary heated iron. Razi argues fire already melts iron, making the miracle conceivable. Elmalili rejects reduction to ordinary discovery. Asad allows literal iron as one reading. [47 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); NAHHAS(الحسن); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الأعمش,الحسن,قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,السدي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السلمي، وابن هرمز، وأبو يحيى، وأبو نوفل، ويعقوب، وابن أبي عبلة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن البصري، وقتادة، والأعمش وغيرهم); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(السدي وغيره); ELMALILI(Fahreddin Râzî); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî; İbn Atıyye; Râzî)]
- 34:10/q12/p2 Iron was easy for David to bend because God gave him extraordinary strength. — Mawardi says it kept its nature, comparing pliability in human dealings; Baydawi permits either mechanism. Bursevi cites 38:17 and says David was strong but not bulky. Kur'an Yolu prefers adequate strength and special skill in light of 34:11. [10 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI(ويقال); MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 34:10/q12/p3 The phrase refers to David's discovery or development of ironworking and armor craft. — Elmalili reports and rejects a reduction to extraction and melting with tools, yet allows David to have refined known smelting into thin armor. Kur'an Yolu prefers divine direction, strength and skill; Islahi stresses light protective mail. [3 notes: ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; ELMALILI-]
- 34:10/q12/p4 Inwardly, softened iron is David's softened heart or elemental bodily nature. — Qashani identifies bodily nature; Bursevi transmits Najmi's image of the softened heart and armor as profound judgments voiced from it. [2 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 34:10/q12/p5 Al-ḥadīd can mean sharpness, whose softening alludes to David's gentle aesthetic and moral character. — Asad cites 50:22 and idioms of sharp intellect, sight and odor; definite al-ḥadīd permits sharpness or iron. His interpretation points to Psalms, goodness and humility. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 34:10/q13 Why was iron softened and how did David use it?  [وَأَلَنَّا لَهُ ٱلْحَدِيدَ · reports]
- 34:10/q13/p1 He made coats of linked mail, becoming the first to do so rather than using older iron plates. — Zajjaj and Qatada contrast mail with earlier cuirasses or plates. Muqatil says a suit took part of a day or night and fetched 1,000 dirhams; another report says a suit fetched 4,000; another says one daily fetched 6,000, with 2,000 for family and 4,000 for Israelite poor. Ibn Ashur describes rings and rivets. 21:80 says armor protects people in battle and, for Elmalili, its address suggests transmission to Muhammad's community. Razi says protective armor preserves life, better than swords or arrows; Abu Hayyan contrasts David's armor with Solomon's peaceful buildings. Qurtubi notes dirʿ is feminine for war armor and masculine for a woman’s breastplate. [23 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(الأخبار,قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل,مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 34:10/q13/p2 He sought honest income independent of the public treasury and sold the armor. — Thaʿlabi and Baghawi say an angel in disguise criticized treasury support; Ibn ʿAtiyya has a kinglike stranger and a suit worth 1,000 daily, with a third for public needs. Qurtubi transmits both a daily 1,000 and a 4,000 suit. Razi says David prayed for independence; Qushayri calls the craft a miracle and relief from burdening followers. The Prophet is reported to have said David ate from his own hands' work. [15 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الأخبار,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,قيل); QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(النبي,قيل); BURSEVI]

## 34:10/q14 What lessons are drawn from David's gifts and response?  [* · interpretation]
- 34:10/q14/p1 Divine grace, repentance, gratitude and sincere work shape a servant's response. — Sulami contrasts grace governing judgment and justice decree. Qushayri reports David's trial, lapse, humility, God's preference for repentant sighs, and his changed prayer for sinners. Islahi says David and Solomon stayed grateful in kingship; Kur'an Yolu emphasizes God's oversight and useful works; Study Quran relates vicegerency to creation's praise. [11 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); QUSHAYRI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 34:10/q14/p2 Beautiful, meaningful recitation is approved, while licentious melody is censured. — Qurtubi reports jurists approving melodically varied Qur'an recitation. Elmalili says voice should preserve pronunciation, rhetoric and tajwid; music affects nerves through air vibration and has spiritual value when carrying meaning. Kur'an Yolu reports his view that music is not categorically forbidden. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Elmalılı)]
- 34:10/q14/p3 Tasbīḥ can be conveyed in words or states and declares God above imperfection. — Ibn Qutayba cites 17:44 that everything glorifies though humans do not grasp it. Kur'an Yolu explains the concept; an esoteric generalization of all praise is reported critically by Abu Hayyan. [3 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعض الباطنية أو من يشبههم)]
- 34:10/q14/p4 Even people of excellence may learn crafts and earn lawfully. — Qurtubi says work adds humility, independence and lawful earnings; the Prophet's report calls earnings from one's labor best. Baghawi also quotes that David ate from his own work. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 34:10/q15 How does this account connect to its surrounding verses and other scriptural passages?  [* · links]
turns on: The returning servant, resurrection and prophetic precedent each provide a proposed link.
- 34:10/q15/p1 The passage summarizes earlier accounts and leads toward gratitude and righteous works. — Tabatabai names David's mountains, birds and iron and Solomon's wind and jinn. Islahi points to Anbiya and Naml. Ibn Ashur recalls 4:163 and 6:84; Study Quran calls the sura Makkan and says these accomplishments follow critique of disbelievers. Yasalam, Asad, and Islahi cite 21:79 for joint praise. [8 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; YAHYA-SALLAM; ASAD-NOTES]
- 34:10/q15/p2 David exemplifies the servant who turns back to God in 34:9. — Alusi cites 34:9 and 34:12; Abu Hayyan cites 38:24; Tibi invokes 38:17 and sees David's repentance turning a shepherd into a prophet and king. Asad connects 34:9 with 38:24; Ibn Ashur prefers calling the passage a digression or parenthesis over Tibi's transition, since 34:20 returns to the polytheists. [10 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الطيبي); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazı müfessirler,Şevkânî)]
- 34:10/q15/p3 Earlier miracles answer denial of resurrection and show God's power over future judgment. — Abu Hayyan cites traditions and poetry attesting singing mountains, soft iron, wind, copper and jinn. Tabatabai connects gratitude and ingratitude in this world to distinction in the next; Islahi addresses affluent Quraysh with David and Solomon versus Sheba and worldly reward as precedent. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 34:10/q15/p4 The favors to David and Solomon show that favors and prophethood for Muhammad are possible. — Ibn ʿAtiyya calls this precedent; Qurtubi says prior messengers had miracles and opponents were punished. Abu Hayyan and Alusi say the discourse later returns to Sheba; Tibi also sees a hint of future honor for Muhammad. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية,الطيبي)]

## 34:10/q16 What inward readings are drawn from David, mountains, birds and iron?  [* · inward]
- 34:10/q16/p1 David represents the spirit; mountains the obedient bodily members; birds spiritual powers; iron bodily nature. — Qashani describes witnessing in love, worship, reflection, scientific and practical perfections; bodily members glorify through motion and rest, and spiritual powers through remembrance, reasoning and illumination. Jami in Bursevi says David's focused spirit transmitted praise through his faculties to mountains and birds and their reward returned to him. [5 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]
- 34:10/q16/p2 Every sound can be heard as from the Beloved, leading the listener to inner silence. — Bursevi cites a Persian couplet; he also distinguishes true unveiling, which hears each thing's own praise. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 34:10/q17 How are 'We softened iron' and the following instruction joined?  [وَأَلَنَّا لَهُ ٱلْحَدِيدَ · grammar]
- 34:10/q17/p1 The clause coordinates with the implied 'We said' or with 'We gave him favor'; the following 'an' explains the gift. — Razi gives both coordination possibilities. Ibn Ashur says the explanatory 'an' introduces God's inspiration to make full coats of mail. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 34:10/q18 What is the root's broader usage?  [أَوِّبِى · meaning]
- 34:10/q18/p1 Awb and taʾwīb involve return or resumed movement. — Tabari adduces returning home at night and a poet; Bint al-Shati quotes lines about an absent traveler returning unlike the dead, and counts Qur'anic iyāb, maʾāb, awʾiba and awwāb forms. Raghib distinguishes awb for willing animals from rujuʿ for living and inanimate things; Razi mentions a proposed movement sense of sabāḥah in 21:79. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(الشاعر); BINTSHATI-IJAZ; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); RAZI-FULL]
