# 2:280: 34 questions

## 2:280/q01 Who is the person in hardship?  [ذُو عُسْرَةٍۢ · referent]
- 2:280/q01/p1 A debtor pursued for payment, especially one whose principal remains due after usury is cancelled. — Ibn ʿAbbās identifies the person sought for payment; Jishumī supplies 'among your debtors' as the understood complement. Ibn ʿĀshūr says dhū can be annexed to an unfavorable quality. [10 notes: MUJAHID(شريح); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:280/q02 What does hardship mean here?  [عُسْرَةٍۢ · meaning]
- 2:280/q02/p1 Poverty or restricted access to money that prevents paying a debt. — Thaʿlabī calls it poverty, constraint and hardship; Rāzī stresses inability to find available money; Jishumī defines ʿusra and iʿsār as little money. Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites 9:117. [9 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI]

## 2:280/q03 How is ʿusra read?  [عُسْرَةٍۢ · readings]
turns on: The sīn's vowel differs by Arabic dialect without changing the basic sense.
- 2:280/q03/p1 With ḍamma on sīn, attributed chiefly to Abū Jaʿfar. — Thaʿlabī, Baghawī, Jishumī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and al-Nashr transmit it; al-Nashr also connects Abū ʿAmr's ḍamma of related ʿusr/yusr forms. [9 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI(أبو جعفر); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جعفر); TABRISI(أبو جعفر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASHR(أبو جعفر,أبو عمرو); QIRAAT-ER]
- 2:280/q03/p2 With sukūn on sīn, the common reading. — Jishumī and Ṭabrisī say both vocalizations are Arabic; Qiraat-ER says the vowel difference leaves the sense intact. [5 notes: JISHUMI(أبو جعفر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI(أبو جعفر); NASHR(أبو عمرو); QIRAAT-ER]

## 2:280/q04 How does kāna govern dhū ʿusratin?  [وَإِن كَانَ ذُو عُسْرَةٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether kāna itself supplies existence or requires an understood predicate; the implications claimed for scope and insolvency.
- 2:280/q04/p1 Kāna is complete, meaning an insolvent person exists, occurs, or appears; dhū is its nominative subject. — Sībawayh's verse and poetry about a cloudy day or arriving winter illustrate existential kāna. Naḥḥās says this gives general scope; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān say the construction presents insolvency as a state needing proof. Rāzī prefers it because a buyer-specific parse would narrow the rule. Samarrai cites poetry for occurrence and existence. [23 notes: SIBAWAYH; AKHFASH(some); TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(سيبويه); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه وأبو علي وغيرهما); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; QURTUBI-FULL(سيبويه,سيبويه وأبو علي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; NAHHAS+; RAZI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 2:280/q04/p2 Kāna is defective with an unspoken predicate such as 'one of your debtors' or 'owing you a right.' — Al-Ṭabarī says Arabic may omit a predicate with an indefinite subject; Jishumī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ṭabrisī and al-Samīn supply creditor–debtor relations. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn report the Basran objection that kāna's predicate cannot be suppressed; al-Samīn distinguishes laysa because it resembles lā. [15 notes: AKHFASH(some); TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; NAHHAS; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض الكوفيين); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(عثمان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعض الكوفيين); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,أبو علي); ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 2:280/q04/p3 In Arabic more generally kāna may mark becoming, time or continuity, while Rāzī says both complete and defective uses retain an event sense. — Rāzī reports a Khwarazm debate about whether an incomplete verb remains a verb; he explains becoming as acquiring a new description, and also reports a poetic redundant use. Al-Suyūṭī lists continuous divine attributes and Abū Bakr al-Rāzī's five temporal senses. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; ITQAN(أبو بكر الرازي)]

## 2:280/q05 What alternative readings recast the insolvent person?  [وَإِن كَانَ ذُو عُسْرَةٍۢ · readings]
turns on: Which codex or transmitter supplies the wording and whether its implied debtor affects scope.
- 2:280/q05/p1 Accusative dhā ʿusratin implies a debtor as kāna's subject. — Attributed variously to Ubayy, Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿUthmān and ʿAbd Allāh; Abān adds 'man' or 'wa-man'; a report adds 'for this crop.' Naḥḥās and al-Zajjāj say it is grammatical, though al-Ṭabarī rejects its recitation against the muṣḥafs. Some infer a narrower usury context, which Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn dispute. [26 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI(آبان بن عثمان,أبي وابن مسعود وابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(أبي); JISHUMI(أبان بن عثمان,أبي بن كعب,ابن مسعود وابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو الداني عن أحمد بن موسى,الطبري,مكي والنقاش); TABRISI(أبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(المهدوي والنحاس,النحاس ومكي والنقاش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبان بن عثمان,أبي وابن مسعود وعثمان وابن عباس,بعضهم); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور,الشيخ,الواحدي,عبد الله وأبي وعثمان); ALUSI-FULL(عثمان); BAYDAWI-FULL; TAB-FULL(أبي بن كعب)-; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 2:280/q05/p2 A reading says 'if he is insolvent' with muʿsiran. — Al-Aʿmash transmits it; al-Dānī reports a codex route. Thaʿlabī says it supports the received meaning. The implicit pronoun points back to the debtor. [8 notes: THALABI(الأعمش); JISHUMI(الأعمش); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعمش); QURTUBI-FULL(الأعمش وأبو عمرو الداني عن أحمد بن موسى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبي وابن مسعود وعثمان وابن عباس,الداني عن أحمد بن موسى); SAMIN-DURR(الأعمش,الشيخ)]
- 2:280/q05/p3 Some codex reports substitute or add conditional particles, including wa-man and fa-in. — Abān is reported with wa-man; al-Mahdawī reports an ʿUthmānic fa-in. These variants variously identify an individual debtor. [6 notes: THALABI(آبان بن عثمان); JISHUMI(أبان بن عثمان); IBNATIYYA-FULL(المهدوي); QURTUBI-FULL(المهدوي والنحاس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبان بن عثمان); ALUSI-FULL(عثمان)]
- 2:280/q05/p4 The received nominative dhū ʿusratin reads an indefinite person in hardship. — Al-Ṭabarī refuses Ubayy's accusative as recitation because it conflicts with written codices; al-Samīn says the majority retain nominative and Makkī takes it generally. Some commentators disagree that these readings must differ in legal scope. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(أبي بن كعب); ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مكي والنقاش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعضهم); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ,الواحدي); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور)+]
- 2:280/q05/p5 Anomalous forms may explain the verse without functioning as valid recitation. — Jishumī explicitly treats them as explanatory paraphrases even when Arabic permits them. [2 notes: JISHUMI]

## 2:280/q06 What event prompted this verse?  [وَإِن كَانَ ذُو عُسْرَةٍۢ · reports]
turns on: Whether an occasion is stated broadly as debt or tied to the particular usury claim.
- 2:280/q06/p1 After the usury ban, Banu ʿAmr ibn ʿUmayr or Thaqīf demanded principal from Banu al-Mughīra; they sought delay until harvest, were refused, and this verse came. — Muqātil says the Prophet sent the earlier verse to ʿAttāb, who read it to Banu ʿAmr, and they repented. Rāzī identifies four brothers and the threat of war; reports vary in names and detail. Some notes distinguish the request for respite from the rule it occasioned. [10 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-ASBAB(الكلبي); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب، مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(مفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الكلبي)]
- 2:280/q06/p2 The verse was revealed concerning usury. — Ibn ʿAbbās says so in several transmitted routes; the fuller Banu al-Mughīra report explains the usury context. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 2:280/q06/p3 The verse was revealed concerning debt in general. — A report from Ibn ʿAbbās gives debt as its occasion without specifying usury. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 2:280/q07 What must a creditor do when the debtor cannot pay?  [فَنَظِرَةٌ إِلَىٰ مَيْسَرَةٍۢ · law]
turns on: Whether hardship is complete inability to pay or strain from realizing assets; when solvency begins.
- 2:280/q07/p1 Grant time until the debtor can pay; collection then becomes due. — Al-Ṭabarī and Qatāda specify principal; Mujāhid and al-Suddī forbid increasing the debt for time, replacing the pre-Islamic increase-or-pay demand. Several sources explain the statement as a binding command. Ibn ʿĀshūr makes obligation certain for complete insolvency; al-Study Quran instead calls the respite encouragement. [40 notes: MUQATIL; AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(الحسن,السدي,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(أبان بن عثمان وعمر بن عبد العزيز,السدى); THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي,الضحاك); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; JISHUMI+]
- 2:280/q07/p2 A usury debtor's principal remains payable once able; a solvent debtor must not delay. — Al-Ṭabarī says the legal debt attaches to property; Qurtubī cites 2:279 for a right to principal and seizure of property. Thaʿlabī cites reports that solvent delay is injustice, and Bursevī urges swift repayment even before the deadline. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI(أبو هريرة,ابن عباس); JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 2:280/q07/p3 For a debtor in financial strain whose assets could be sold, some require delay and Mālik and the majority recommend it. — Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes absolute insolvency from hardship caused by an immediate sale that would damage livelihood; later Cordoban judges used discretionary respite to avert harm. The Study Quran speaks of encouragement while retaining principal. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(مالك والجمهور,متأخرو قرطبة); STUDYQURAN]

## 2:280/q08 Which debts receive obligatory respite?  [وَإِن كَانَ ذُو عُسْرَةٍۢ فَنَظِرَةٌ · law]
turns on: Whether occasion limits wording, and whether generality is textual or analogical.
- 2:280/q08/p1 Every insolvent debtor's debt, whether born of usury, sale, loan, or trust, is covered. — Al-Ṭabarī distinguishes the usury occasion from the verse's general ruling, with debt owed from property rather than the body. ʿAṭāʾ, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and others rely on unrestricted language; Rāzī cites the shared inability to pay; Ibn ʿĀshūr says annulled usury principal has become ordinary debt. Naḥḥās prefers the comprehensive reading. [23 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون,الضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(عطاء); MAWARDI-FULL(عطاء، الضحاك); JISHUMI(ابن عباس والحسن والضحاك); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور العلماء); TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(القاضي,مجاهد وجماعة من المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة والحسن وعامة الفقهاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو هريرة والحسن وعطاء والضحاك والربيع بن خثيم وعامة الفقهاء); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن والضحاك وأئمة أهل البيت); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); TABATABAI(ابن عباس والضحاك والحسن); DURR-FULL(الضحاك); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; JISHUMI+; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس وعطاء والضحاك والربيع بن خثيم)+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 2:280/q08/p2 The verse's obligatory respite is specific to a usury debt; ordinary trusts and other debts are to be repaid. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Shurayḥ and Ibrāhīm invoke the usury occasion and 4:58's duty to restore trusts. Shurayḥ even detained a claimant of insolvency; Rāzī's Qāḍī says context and 2:279 favor this reading. Ibn ʿAṭiyya concedes manifest poverty still requires delay. Sources differ on whether al-Ḍaḥḥāk belongs here. [21 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(شريح); TAB-FULL(إبراهيم,ابن عباس,شريح); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس، شريح); JISHUMI(شريح وإبراهيم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وشريح); TABRISI(شريح); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس وشريح والضحاك والسدي وإبراهيم,شريح); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وشريح); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وشريح); ALUSI-FULL(شريح وإبراهيم النخعي وابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(شريح); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,شريح); RAZI-FULL(القاضي)+]
- 2:280/q08/p3 The text directly rules on usury debt and the identical rule extends to other debts by analogy. — Al-Māwardī reports this reconciliation; Jishumī prefers it because inability to pay is common to both kinds of debt. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the cancelled interest leaves ordinary principal. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(القاضي); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); JISHUMI+]

## 2:280/q09 When may an insolvent debtor be imprisoned or pursued?  [فَنَظِرَةٌ · law]
turns on: Whether insolvency is proven and whether the debt falls under the verse.
- 2:280/q09/p1 Once insolvency is known, the creditor may neither imprison, press, nor continually restrain the debtor. — Al-Ṭabarī says no payable asset is being withheld and a debt cannot be collected from the debtor's person; al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Qushayrī exclude prison after insolvency is established. Abū Hurayra and Abū Saʿīd reports are cited; Qurtubī says Abū Qatāda's report bars demand even on credible suspicion without a judge's finding. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); THALABI(أبو سعيد الخدري,أبو هريرة); QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري,مالك والشافعي وأبو حنيفة وغيرهم); DURR-FULL(الضحاك,سعيد بن جبير); ELMALILI]
- 2:280/q09/p2 On a usury-only construction, an ordinary debtor may be held until payment. — Shurayḥ cites 4:58 and argues divine commands cannot conflict; the reported usury-only view permits prison for other claims. [4 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(شريح); TAB-FULL(شريح); RAZI-FULL(شريح); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وشريح)]
- 2:280/q09/p3 A debtor may be detained while a doubtful claim of insolvency is investigated, but released once genuine insolvency is proven. — Abū Ḥanīfa suggests two or three months and private inquiry. Mālik, Shāfiʿī and Ḥanafī jurists allow detention pending proof, while Mālik reserves it for suspected concealment or obstinacy; Rāzī likewise allows detention when status is unclear. [3 notes: THALABI(أبو حنيفة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مالك والشافعي وأبو حنيفة وغيرهم)]

## 2:280/q10 What qualifies a debtor as insolvent?  [ذُو عُسْرَةٍۢ · law]
turns on: Which assets and necessities count as available means.
- 2:280/q10/p1 The debtor lacks resources beyond moderate necessities for self and dependents. — Abān and ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz still count a person with only a house and no surplus as insolvent. Al-Ṣādiq and Ṭabrisī use economical subsistence; al-Jubbāʾī includes stagnant goods or lack of usable resources. Jishumī includes inability to sell. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبان بن عثمان وعمر بن عبد العزيز); TABRISI(أبو عبد الله,أبو علي الجبائي); JISHUMI; TABATABAI(جعفر الصادق); BURSEVI]
- 2:280/q10/p2 Any saleable assets must first be used, while only necessary food and clothing are kept. — Rāzī allows sale of a house and spare clothing and requires distressed sale of unsalable goods. Qurtubī gives rules for clothing, disputes over a wife's clothes and a scholar's books, and says homes, servants and Friday clothes can be sold unless of little value; Mālik's transmitted minimum is a covering garment. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 2:280/q10/p3 Jurists disagree whether an able-bodied insolvent person must work or accept another's payment. — Rāzī records both unresolved disputes without preferring either. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 2:280/q11 Who must prove or verify insolvency?  [ذُو عُسْرَةٍۢ · law]
turns on: Whether the debtor received property in the transaction, affecting the starting presumption.
- 2:280/q11/p1 For a sale or loan with value received, the debtor must prove loss or insolvency. — Thaʿlabī says receipt creates a presumption of means; Rāzī requires two upright witnesses that the consideration was lost. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān connect the existential construction to treating solvency as the ordinary state. [4 notes: THALABI; RAZI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه وأبو علي وغيرهما); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:280/q11/p2 For an obligation without value received, the creditor must prove solvency; an oath or debtor's statement can establish poverty. — Dowry, guarantee and damages supply the examples; the original presumption is poverty. Al-Ḥasan accepts a debtor's sworn claim unless creditors produce evidence or see assets. [3 notes: THALABI(الحسن); RAZI-FULL]

## 2:280/q12 Did this verse end selling an insolvent debtor for debt?  [فَنَظِرَةٌ · sciences]
turns on: Whether a valid prior Islamic authorization, rather than merely pre-Islamic custom, can be shown.
- 2:280/q12/p1 It abrogated an earlier practice of selling or enslaving a debtor unable to pay. — Some scholars identify a pre-Islamic practice; al-Ṭaḥāwī says it also existed early in Islam. A report describes the Prophet selling a debtor, but Qurtubī notes unreliable transmitters. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares ancient Egyptian and Roman law and reports the early Islamic claim as unestablished. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض العلماء,مكي); QURTUBI-FULL(الطحاوي,بعض العلماء ومكي وابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:280/q12/p2 It counts as abrogation only if the Prophet's earlier authorization of debtor sale is established. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya makes the historical proof decisive; Abū Ḥayyān repeats the conditional conclusion, and Qurtubī reports weakness in the supporting report. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(بعض العلماء ومكي وابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:280/q13 What does naẓira mean?  [فَنَظِرَةٌ · meaning]
- 2:280/q13/p1 Waiting, postponement, or granting a debtor respite. — Ibn Qutayba, Zajjāj and others cite deferred-sale Arabic usage; Rāzī and Qurtubī cite 15:36–38 and Ṭabarī cites 2:196. Ḥarallī calls it a delay whose end is awaited. [18 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 2:280/q14 How does the noun 'then respite' issue a command?  [فَنَظِرَةٌ · grammar]
- 2:280/q14/p1 It is a nominative declarative clause serving as the conditional answer and implying an obligation to defer. — Thaʿlabī and Ṭabrisī say the declaration carries imperative force; al-Samīn compares 2:233. Analyses supply an omitted subject ('the ruling is respite'), an omitted predicate ('respite is due'), or an implied verb; Thaʿlabī adds an accusative command would be possible. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:280/q15 How is fa-naẓira read?  [فَنَظِرَةٌ · readings]
turns on: Whether the form is a normal noun, a dialectal contraction, an attributive noun, or an imperative.
- 2:280/q15/p1 Nāẓirahu or nāẓirūhu commands 'defer him,' with a pronoun for the debtor. — Reported from ʿAṭāʾ, Mujāhid and ʿAbd Allāh; Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares sāmiḥahu. Some forms carry a connected hāʾ. Al-Samīn counts six transmitted forms in all. [15 notes: AKHFASH(some); NAHHAS(مجاهد وعطاء); THALABI(عبد الله); KASHSHAF-FULL(عطاء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عطاء بن أبي رياح ومجاهد); RAZI-FULL(عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد وعطاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عطاء,مجاهد); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,عبد الله); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(عطاء)]
- 2:280/q15/p2 Naẓra with still ẓāʾ is a Tamīmī dialect form. — Transmitted from Mujāhid, Abū Rajāʾ, al-Ḥasan, Qatāda, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and ʿAṭāʾ with varying attributions; Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares other Tamīmī consonant clusters. Thaʿlabī says it may be a verbal noun of looking or waiting. [9 notes: NAHHAS(الحسن وأبو رجاء); THALABI(عطاء بن أبي رباح); JISHUMI(عطاء); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وأبو رجاء والحسن والضحاك); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد وأبو رجاء والحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن ومجاهد وأبو رجاء)]
- 2:280/q15/p3 Nāẓira may be a verbal noun on the fāʿila pattern meaning waiting. — Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq cite kādhiba, fāqira, and khāʾinat al-aʿyun in 56:2, 75:25, and 40:19. Abū Ḥātim rejects it here, reserving the form for a woman in 27:35 or 27:32. ## 2:280/q16 What does 'until ease' mark?  [إِلَىٰ مَيْسَرَةٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ease denotes restored resources, death, or payment through public alms.
- 2:280/q16/p1 Until the debtor gains sufficient wealth or means to repay. — Most commentators derive maysara from yusr, the opposite of hardship. Al-Suddī and the majority stress solvency and payment of principal; some say God provides it. Jishumī connects it to ease of transactions and needs. [20 notes: MUQATIL; AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(أبان بن عثمان وعمر بن عبد العزيز,السدى); THALABI(عبد الله); JISHUMI; MAWARDI-FULL(الأكثرون); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ]

- 2:280/q18/p1 No; postponement ends when solvency begins. — Al-Suyūṭī says including it would defeat the creditor's right. ## 2:280/q19 How is 'that you give charity is better' constructed?  [وَأَن تَصَدَّقُوا۟ خَيْرٌۭ لَّكُمْ · grammar]

## 2:280/q22 Why is remitting debt better for creditors?  [خَيْرٌۭ لَّكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Which benefit the sources foreground: personal reward, social effects, or both.
- 2:280/q22/p1 It brings praise or blessing now and lasting, greater reward in the next life. — Ṭabarī says knowledgeable people recognize its merit; Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān name worldly praise and abundant reward. Bayḍāwī contrasts multiplied lasting reward with money that passes; Biqāʿī adds compensation and blessing. Jishumī says praise here and reward hereafter. - 2:280/q22/p2 It turns the abandoned usurious increase into a real increase through charity. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī ties the verse to 2:276, 30:39 and 3:130: usury takes without compensation, charity gives without it and spreads mercy, maintains the poor, grows wealth, and stabilizes society. ## 2:280/q23 What does 'if you know' require its audience to know?  [إِن كُنتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the object of knowledge is the act's value, God's command, or the debtor's condition.
- 2:280/q23/p1 Knowledge must become action: recognizing divine guidance, then remitting or easing the debt. — Abū ʿAlī makes it an instruction to recognize and act; Abū Muslim says reasoners grasp God's inducement. Zamakhsharī and Nasafī treat unused knowledge as no knowledge; Biqāʿī makes it a rebuke of professed knowledge. Al-Samīn and Ālūsī supply an omitted answer: those who know will act. - 2:280/q23/p2 Know that charity's reward is better than delay, collection, or the money taken. — Jishumī lists divine reward and superiority over delay or collection; Rāzī gives it as a second reading. Ṭabarī frames the knowledgeable as knowing charity's merit. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]
- 2:280/q23/p3 Know that God's command benefits people more than their own desires. — Jishumī lists this alternative; Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān repeat it. [3 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:280/q23/p4 Know that this particular debtor truly is insolvent. — Al-Ṣādiq's report makes that knowledge the condition for forgiving; Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes it differs from his own earlier explanation. [2 notes: TABATABAI(جعفر الصادق)]

## 2:280/q25 What ancillary rulings and warnings accompany debt in these sources?  [* · law]
turns on: The sources extend the verse to adjacent debt ethics and disputed court consequences.
- 2:280/q25/p1 Borrowers should intend repayment and pay promptly; wrongful delay or borrowing to default is condemned. — Thaʿlabī transmits theft for a loan with no intention to repay and injustice for solvent delay. Bursevī says repayment should be hastened, cites guardian angels for intending repayment, and commends borrowing for God’s cause, burial shrouds, or chaste marriage; early Muslims sometimes borrowed while relying on the angel report. [9 notes: THALABI(أبو هريرة,ابن عباس); BURSEVI(جماعة السلف,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); ALUSI-FULL(أبو المسعود)]
- 2:280/q25/p2 A lawful creditor retains a claim, and God remains with the creditor until payment unless the debt funds what God dislikes. — ʿAbd Allāh ibn Jaʿfar directed his treasurer to collect after hearing this report. Qushayrī likewise says the established claim persists despite respite. [3 notes: THALABI(عبد الله بن جعفر); QUSHAYRI; STUDYQURAN]
- 2:280/q25/p3 Debt remains morally serious even after major good deeds. — Thaʿlabī reports that the Prophet did not pray over a debtor until Abū Qatāda guaranteed seventeen or eighteen dirhams, likened debt to disbelief in gravity, and warned of anxiety, humiliation, divine humbling and severe posthumous sin. Bursevī reports that even martyrdom does not expiate debt, repeated three times. [6 notes: THALABI(أبو سعيد الخدري,أبو قتادة,أبو هريرة,أنس بن مالك,عائشة); BURSEVI(جبريل عليه السلام)]
- 2:280/q25/p4 A wife may not obtain separation merely because her husband is poor, according to one legal view. — ʿAṭāʾ, al-Zuhrī and Kufan jurists analogize her claim to a deferred court debt and cite this verse and 24:32. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عطاء والزهري)]
- 2:280/q25/p5 A judge may sell a debtor's assets, but jurists differ on who bears loss before proceeds reach creditors. — Qurtubī says loss after the judge receives proceeds is the creditors' and discharges the debtor; Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd al-Ḥakam keeps liability on the debtor until creditors receive them. Loss before sale and delivery remains debtor's risk. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن عبد الحكم)]
- 2:280/q25/p6 A loan may itself be superior to alms when it reaches a needy borrower. — Bursevī transmits Umāma's dream of an eighteenfold loan versus tenfold charity; the explanation says charity may reach a rich person whereas a borrower is needy. A separate report promises Paradise entry for lending, pardoning a killer, or repeated recitation of al-Ikhlāṣ. [2 notes: BURSEVI(أمامة الباهلي,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 2:280/q25/p7 Loans were ideally charitable; credit sales and leases offered investment uses of money over time. — Study Quran distinguishes charitable loans from investment and financing forms. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 2:280/q26 How does this command connect to the surrounding usury passage?  [* · links]
turns on: How immediate principal recovery, usury prohibition, and charity frame the conditional.
- 2:280/q26/p1 After canceling interest while preserving principal, the passage addresses an insolvent debtor and replaces pay-or-increase with delay or remission. — Ṭabarī locates the immediate context among converts owed pre-Islamic usury. Ṭabrisī, Jishumī and Ibn ʿĀshūr explain why the conditional follows principal repayment. Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts the pre-Islamic increase on delayed payment. Kuran Yolu distinguishes enforceable principal from moral excellence, and Biqāʿī explains the reciprocal prohibition of wronging. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 2:280/q26/p2 The warning of war against usurers intensifies the surrounding passage; some will persist despite prohibition. — Ḥarallī calls war forceful resistance, and says the Messenger of mercy becomes an adversary to a usurer; Biqāʿī says pairing God and Messenger magnifies the threat and predicts some disobedience. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]
- 2:280/q26/p3 The usury passages are set against neighboring charity material: taking without compensation versus giving without it. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī links 2:276, 2:280, 30:39 and 3:130, arguing opposite social and spiritual consequences. [2 notes: TABATABAI]
- 2:280/q26/p4 Related usury reports prohibit delayed currency exchange and unequal exchange of like goods. — Al-Durr transmits hand-to-hand currency exchange, the fresh-versus-dried date prohibition because fresh dates shrink, a warning on surplus gold or silver exchange, and Abū Bakra's report of a prohibition two months before the Prophet's death. [4 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو بكر الصديق,أبو بكرة,البراء بن عازب وزيد بن أرقم,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 2:280/q27 When were the usury verses revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Which report identifies the last verse or set of verses.
- 2:280/q27/p1 The usury verse or verses were among the last or the final revelation. — ʿUmar says the Prophet died before explaining them and cautions against doubtful usury; Ibn ʿAbbās calls the usury verse last. Other reports give the last material as the verses of usury and debt. ʿĀʾisha says the Prophet recited the final verses in the mosque then forbade wine trading. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,عمر,عمر بن الخطاب); MAWARDI-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,عمر بن الخطاب); TABRISI(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(عمر وابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(ابن شهاب,ابن عباس رضي الله تعالى عنهما,عائشة رضي الله تعالى عنها)]
- 2:280/q27/p2 The last revealed verse was the following warning of return to God. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya records this identification while describing al-Baqara as Medinan and revealed at different times. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 2:280/q28 What does Qushayrī draw inwardly from the debt and respite language?  [* · inward]
turns on: The movement from an outward debtor to human spiritual need and present accountability.
- 2:280/q28/p1 Divine mercy grants needy people time, even while God knows their incapacity and sincere dependence on Him. — Qushayrī contrasts an indebted person without assets or business capital with one destitute in livelihood who has only the Master; he also retains the outward creditor's claim and respite. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 2:280/q28/p2 Restraint of desire and reliance on God's promise bring present respite and blessing; returning to blameworthy states brings erasure or punishment. — Qushayrī contrasts deeds undertaken by God's leave with acts driven by caprice and says persistent wrong has no standing with God. [7 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 2:280/q28/p3 The return and reckoning occur at resurrection and inwardly through the heart's accounting with every breath. — Qushayrī says the present demand is more pressing than the promised future reckoning. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 2:280/q29 What source traditions frame interpretation of this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: The different transmission backgrounds supplied as source prefaces.
- 2:280/q29/p1 Mujāhid's full verse-by-verse study with Ibn ʿAbbās gives his interpretation special authority. — Ibn Masʿūd praises Ibn ʿAbbās; Ibn Abī Mulayka saw Mujāhid ask and write; Mujāhid says he presented the codex three times; Sufyān al-Thawrī says his tafsīr suffices. Reports also question al-Ḍaḥḥāk's direct contact with Ibn ʿAbbās and Abū Ṣāliḥ Bādhān's qualification. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن أبي مليكة,الشعبي,سفيان الثوري,عبد الله بن مسعود,عبد الملك بن ميسرة,مجاهد,مشاش)]
- 2:280/q29/p2 Reports about Ibn Kathīr's Meccan reading identify its transmitters, local acceptance and chronology. — Ibn Mujāhid names Shibl, Ismāʿīl b. ʿAbd Allāh and Maʿrūf as transmitters and contrasts others with only a few readings; Abū al-Ḥasan calls the reading locally agreed, Shibl equates his reading with Ibn Kathīr's, and Ibn ʿUyayna dates the death to 120 AH, when he was thirteen. [6 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو الحسن,ابن عيينة,شبل بن عباد)]

## 2:280/q30 What general method and limits do the cited tafsīr prefaces claim?  [* · sciences]
turns on: How authoritative explanation can be developed when few direct Prophetic glosses survive.
- 2:280/q30/p1 Definitive divine intent is usually unavailable without Prophetic transmission, so interpreters infer from signs and proofs; God nevertheless invites reflection. — Al-Khūʾī says only a few verses have transmitted Prophetic explanation and cites the Prophet's approval of multiple interpreters; al-Suyūṭī repeats both the difficulty and permission to reflect. [3 notes: BURHAN(القاضي شمس الدين الخويى); ITQAN(الخويي)]
- 2:280/q30/p2 Tafsīr is needed to unfold concise wording and weigh lexicon, grammar, context, eloquence and outward and inward senses. — Al-Zarkashī says later readers lack early direct language command and need a systematic method. [2 notes: BURHAN]
- 2:280/q30/p3 Exegetes should read predecessors before judging their own or others' work. — Al-Khūʾī criticizes boasting of independence from earlier books and connects sound work with study of the righteous. [1 notes: BURHAN(القاضي شمس الدين الخويى)]

## 2:280/q31 What dialect or language is the Qurʾān said to use?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether Quraysh names an exclusive dialect, a predominant one, or a wider collection.
- 2:280/q31/p1 Seven languages, five from lower Hawāzin, or words from every Arab dialect and language. — Al-Kalbī reports Ibn ʿAbbās's seven-language account and Abū ʿUbayd names tribes; Abū Maysara says every language. Al-Suyūṭī prefers Abū Maysara's authentic report. [3 notes: BURHAN(أبو ميسرة,الكلبى عن أبى صالح عن ابن عباس); ITQAN+]
- 2:280/q31/p2 Only a prophet can comprehend all Arabic or speak the Arab groups' collective language. — Al-Shāfiʿī is explained by al-Ṣayrafī as referring to a Prophet addressing all Arab groups; a report of ʿUmar and the Prophet's divinely taught speech has an unknown chain. [4 notes: BURHAN(الشافعى,الصيرفى,عمر); ITQAN(الشافعي)]
- 2:280/q31/p3 Predominantly Quraysh speech, selected for its eloquence, while dialect features from others can occur. — Al-Farrāʾ says Quraysh selected fine forms and criticizes named features of Tamīm, Rabīʿa and Qays. Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr treats 'Quraysh' as predominant, noting non-Quraysh hamza and a seven-aḥruf report. [3 notes: BURHAN(أبو عمر بن عبد البر,الفراء)]
- 2:280/q31/p4 Speech of the two Kaʿbs, the neighboring Quraysh and Khuzāʿa communities. — Abū al-Aswad and Ibn ʿAbbās transmit the formula; Abū ʿUbayd says Khuzāʿa adopted Quraysh speech, and Khālid explains their shared home. [2 notes: BURHAN(أبو الأسود الدؤلى,ابن عباس)]

## 2:280/q32 Can Qurʾānic words be foreign in origin?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether earlier foreign origin survives once a term is assimilated into Arabic.
- 2:280/q32/p1 The Qurʾān contains no foreign vocabulary: its words are Arabic or shared across languages. — Most authorities cite the Qurʾān's description as Arabic; Abū ʿUbayda objects to calling a word Nabataean, Ibn Jarīr explains multilingual glosses as shared terms, and another view invokes Arabic's vastness. Al-Shāfiʿī says only a prophet comprehends it; Abū al-Maʿālī suggests Arabic may have used the terms first. [6 notes: ITQAN(آخرون,أبو المعالي عزيزي بن عبد الملك,أبو عبيدة,ابن جرير,الشافعي,الشافعي وابن جرير وأبو عبيدة والقاضي أبو بكر وابن فارس)]
- 2:280/q32/p2 A few terms originated elsewhere but were borrowed, adapted and made eloquent Arabic before revelation. — Proponents say a small number does not make the whole Qurʾān non-Arabic and read 41:44 as contrasting an unfamiliar discourse and an Arab audience; al-Suyūṭī prefers Abū Maysara's report of words from every language. [3 notes: ITQAN(آخرون); ITQAN+]

## 2:280/q33 What introductory claims are made about al-Baqara?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Different introductory descriptions, merits and counts of the sūra.
- 2:280/q33/p1 It is a Medinan sūra revealed at different times, called the Qurʾān's tent for its stature, beauty, laws and exhortations. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya also reports Ibn ʿUmar's eight years studying its legal sciences, a count of five hundred rulings and fifteen parables, and a claim of 360 meanings of mercy, hope and punishment. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن عمر)]
- 2:280/q33/p2 Reports call al-Baqara the best or summit sūra, with Āyat al-Kursī its leading verse. — Other reports say Satan avoids a house in which it is recited, it and Āl ʿImrān will defend reciters as clouds or birds, and its last two verses suffice at night. A report traces it and other sūras to earlier scripture, Moses's tablets or beneath the Throne. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 2:280/q33/p3 Its verse count is given as 285, with alternative counts of 286 and 287. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives all three counts, favoring 285 in the note. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 2:280/q34 What does the ancillary word ghamm mean?  [* · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term stresses covering sorrow or its short duration.
- 2:280/q34/p1 Sorrow that covers the heart, with its root sense of covering. — Ibn al-Jawzī relates the root to a covering over neck and forehead and to cloud or a veiled moon; he distinguishes fear before an event, ghamm after it, and recurring worry. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 2:280/q34/p2 A quickly passing distress of the soul. — Ibn al-Jawzī transmits this as another definition. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعضهم)]
