# 20:7: 16 questions

## 20:7/q01 What does it mean to speak aloud?  [تَجْهَرْ · meaning]
- 20:7/q01/p1 It means raising the voice so speech is audible, as opposed to hushed speech. — Jishumi contrasts voiced sound with whispering; Tabrisi supplies the verb and participial forms; Ibn al-Jawzi applies it to Moses' effort; al-Alusi applies it to speech and al-Tabataba'i contrasts jahr with israr. [6 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 20:7/q02 Whom does the conditional address?  [تَجْهَرْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the second person singles out the Prophet or is indefinite and general.
- 20:7/q02/p1 It addresses Muhammad, with the teaching extending to his community or all people. — Al-Tabari includes the Prophet's private and public speech; Ibn Atiyya extends Muhammad's address to human speech to others or idols; Abu Hayyan calls the Prophet the apparent addressee; al-Bahr, cited by Alusi, includes his community; Ibn Ashur includes others. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البحر); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:7/q02/p2 It addresses humanity without singling out a particular person. — Alusi allows a general human address, and Ibn Ashur says an indefinite second person reaches everyone. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(البحر); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:7/q03 What speech does al-qawl include here?  [ٱلْقَوْلِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the definite noun takes all speech or the contextual act of addressing God.
- 20:7/q03/p1 Speech broadly, including recitation, supplication, remembrance, and ordinary conversation. — Alusi reports a broad sense; Ibn Ashur defines qawl as a verbal noun for utterance and says recitation is particularly relevant after 20:2; Ibn Atiyya includes speech addressed to other people or idols. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 20:7/q03/p2 Remembrance or invocation addressed to God, especially loud recitation. — Al-Mufaddal, Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi and Ibn Ashur relate it to reciting without strain; Zamakhshari, Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi treat voiced remembrance and supplication; Alusi reports the contextual definite article argument. Razi says the loud invocation or other voiced mention is unnecessary for God's hearing. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(المفضل); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL]

## 20:7/q04 How does the conditional clause work?  [وَإِن تَجْهَرْ بِٱلْقَوْلِ فَإِنَّهُۥ يَعْلَمُ ٱلسِّرَّ وَأَخْفَى · grammar]
turns on: Whether an unspoken alternative or answer is supplied, or the stated answer itself asserts universal knowledge.
- 20:7/q04/p1 The jussive condition has 'He knows the secret and more hidden' as its answer, expressing an invariable fact, not knowledge caused by speech. — Al-Nahhas identifies the jussive and answer; Alusi denies temporal contingency; Ibn Ashur adduces conditionals by Abu Kabir, Bashama ibn Hazn, Ibrahim ibn Kunayf, and al-Qutami to show universal force. [7 notes: NAHHAS; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(إبراهيم بن كنيف النبهاني,القطامي,بشامة بن حزن النهشلي)]
- 20:7/q04/p2 An omitted parallel clause yields 'whether you speak aloud or not, He knows the secret and more hidden.' — The Nazm author, Tabrisi, and Islahi make the omitted alternative explicit; Wahidi notes why the stated contrast initially seems unusual. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); TABRISI(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:7/q04/p3 An omitted answer means 'do not strain yourself'; the stated knowledge supplies its reason. — Ibn Ashur supplies the apodosis from the knowledge assertion and the Prophet's effort to recite aloud. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:7/q04/p4 The condition implies 'know that God knows your loud speech as well as the hidden.' — Alusi gives this expansion; Ibn Ashur says loudness adds nothing to God's prior knowledge. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:7/q05 Why mention loud speech when God knows the secret?  [وَإِن تَجْهَرْ بِٱلْقَوْلِ فَإِنَّهُۥ يَعْلَمُ ٱلسِّرَّ وَأَخْفَى · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the conditional chiefly argues from the hidden, guides the Prophet's vocal effort, cautions speakers, or recalls a dispute about God's hearing.
- 20:7/q05/p1 Knowing what is most hidden proves all the more that God knows audible speech; volume adds nothing to His hearing. — Mawardi frames 'why speak aloud?' as a question; the Nazm author, Wahidi, Razi, Abu Hayyan, Tabataba'i, and Study Quran use inference from hidden to audible; Bagawi and Tabrisi stress the greater hidden degree; Ibn Ashur notes secret speech is especially hard for humans to know. [13 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī); RAZI-FULL+]
- 20:7/q05/p2 It reassures or counsels the Prophet not to strain in loud recitation or supplication, since God hears without it. — Al-Mufaddal, Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Biqai and Ibn Ashur apply it to recitation; Islahi says God hears the Prophet's pleas in every state and will guide him for his benefit, with 19:64. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(المفضل,صاحب النظم); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:7/q05/p3 It can warn speakers against evil public speech, since God hears it. — Alusi says public speech is singled out because most conversation is aloud. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:7/q05/p4 It alludes to a dispute over whether God hears quiet speech and answers the doubters. — Ibn Ashur relates the Ibn Masud report near the Kaaba, links 41:22 and 11:5, and says this context explains the seemingly unusual choice of loud speech. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(عبدالله بن مسعود)]

## 20:7/q06 What guidance does the verse give about audible remembrance or supplication?  [تَجْهَرْ بِٱلْقَوْلِ · law]
turns on: How 7:205 and the restraint reports relate to sound reports of aloud remembrance, and whether loudness is moderate, excessive, or serves a purpose.
- 20:7/q06/p1 Aloud remembrance is allowed or recommended when useful and free of harm; excessive volume is discouraged. — Baydawi and Alusi give imprint, focus, humility and protection from distraction; Biqai allows volume to make others hear. Bursevi adds awakening others, blessing, witnesses, Sufi resistance to ego and Satan, and enemy concealment; the Khaybar/Abu Musa valley report orders gentleness before the near, hearing God. Alusi cites over twenty sound reports, the Prophet's voiced prayer formula for teaching, Shafi'i preference with apparent Hanbali and Maliki support, the two Hanafi companions' Eid takbir, and al-Kurani's defense. He treats funeral dislike as contextual, the Ibn Masud group-dhikr report as unsound or about excess, and Abu Hanifa's reports as different degrees of volume. Ibn Ashur says Sunna establishes loud prayer and remembrance though it is only an implication here; he raises possible supersession by 15:94 of an earlier recitation restraint. [26 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(إنسان العيون,النبي عليه السلام); ALUSI-FULL(أبو موسى الأشعري,أبو يوسف ومحمد وأبو حنيفة,إبراهيم الكوراني,ابن مسعود,النووي,عبد الله بن الزبير,قاضيخان); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين)+]
- 20:7/q06/p2 Loud remembrance is discouraged or prohibited; inward remembrance is preferable. — Zamakhshari, Razi and Bursevi cite 7:205 as a possible restraint. Abu Hanifa, as Ibn al-Humam quotes him, calls it an innovation except prescribed Eid al-Adha loudness; a ninth-century Hanafi notable argued similarly. Alusi says several scholars prefer silent supplication, and the sound hadith favors hidden remembrance when ostentation or self-admiration is feared. Alusi reports objections to a blanket ban. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حنيفة,إبراهيم الكوراني,السيوطي); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:7/q06/p3 The volume best for remembrance varies with the person, situation and time. — Alusi allows both modes under their conditions, favors quiet when ostentation is feared, and rejects an unqualified ban; Bursevi limits restraint to excess or a need such as concealment from enemies. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI(إنسان العيون); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:7/q06/p4 The inward-remembrance command at 7:205 was later superseded. — Alusi reports Suyuti's first reply: the command was Meccan and ceased to restrict after the Hijra; Ibn Ashur proposes possible supersession by 15:94. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(السيوطي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:7/q06/p5 The inward-remembrance command concerns remembrance during Qur'an recitation. — Suyuti, Abd al-Rahman ibn Zayd and Ibn Jarir cite the following context at 7:204–205. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم وابن جرير)]
- 20:7/q06/p6 The inward-remembrance command is specific to the Prophet; others troubled by whispers may recite aloud. — Alusi reports this Sufi answer but questions it. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض الصوفية)-]
- 20:7/q06/p7 Rulers should speak loudly for awe and stronger effect on listeners. — Bursevi reports the philosophers' recommendation. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الحكماء)]

## 20:7/q07 How loud must speech be to count as audible or quiet remembrance?  [تَجْهَرْ · law]
turns on: Whether hearing oneself marks the audible or quiet threshold, and whether soundless articulation can be recitation.
- 20:7/q07/p1 Audible speech reaches another person; quiet speech is heard by oneself. — Ibn Hajar al-Haytami gives this measure; al-Muhit's sounder position, held by most and preferred by Hinduwani and Fadli, applies it to recitation. Ibn al-Humam says articulation without sound is neither letters nor recitation. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن الهمام,ابن حجر الهيتمي); ALUSI-FULL(الشيخان الهندواني والفضلي)+]
- 20:7/q07/p2 Audible recitation is heard by oneself; quiet recitation requires only articulated letters. — Alusi reports this Hanafi narration and attributes it also to al-Karkhi; Ibn al-Humam's argument against soundless letters challenges its lower threshold. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(رواية عن الحنفية); ALUSI-FULL(ابن الهمام)-]

## 20:7/q08 What are 'the secret' and 'what is more hidden'?  [ٱلسِّرَّ وَأَخْفَى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether concealment is measured against other people, one's present awareness, future unknown acts or thoughts, forgotten thoughts, or God's own undisclosed knowledge.
- 20:7/q08/p1 The secret is a present inward thought or concealment; the more hidden is future thought, action, or event unknown to the person now. — Sa'id, Qatada and Ibn Abbas distinguish what one knows now from what has not entered the mind or occurred; Ibn Abbas says one divine knowledge encompasses past and future and cites 31:28. Ibn Kathir reports al-Dahhak and Mujahid too; Wahidi says this is the majority view. Tustari specifies a thought arriving in sleep. Sulami's Fudayl says what one will know tomorrow; al-Wasiti says what God has not commanded to exist. Razi permits future divine acts; Tabari's Ibn Abbas report distinguishes completed deed from an unacted prompting; Durr cites al-Hakim's authentication of one Ibn Abbas report. Kuranyolu attributes to Tabari a preference for possible things God has not created or disclosed, whereas Qurtubi's attribution to Tabari favors future human thought. [53 notes: MUJAHID(سعيد بن جبير); YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); SULAMI(الفضيل بن عياض,الواسطي); THALABI(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي,قتادة وسعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT(أكثرهم,ابن عباس,مجاهد وقتادة وسفيان والضحاك وسعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس عن علي بن أبي طلحة,ابن عباس، سعيد بن جبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(قتادة وسعيد بن جبير وابن زيد,قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(جماعة عن ابن عباس والضحاك,سعيد بن جبير); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الطبري,قتادة وغيره); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,سعيد بن جبير,مجاهد وسعيد بن جبير); BURHAN(ابن عباس وغيره); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,سعيد بن جبير); ALUSI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); ELMALILI; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî)+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 20:7/q08/p2 The secret is something confided quietly to another; the more hidden is a thought or matter kept within oneself. — Al-Hasan, Ibn Abbas, Ikrima and al-Farra distinguish private speech from inner thought; Zamakhshari and Nasafi add a possible later inward concealment. Ibn Ashur locates the comparison in inner speech quieter than a whisper. Tabataba'i accepts the whispered report as speech but says a true secret is kept within oneself. [19 notes: THALABI(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن,عكرمة,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن، عكرمة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazı müfessirler); TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 20:7/q08/p3 The secret is a deed concealed from people; the more hidden is inner whispering. — Mujahid's explanation recurs through Tabari, Thalabi, Mawardi, Bagawi, Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Ibn Kathir and Durr; Thalabi also reports Ibn Abbas identifying the most hidden with self-talk. [11 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI(ابن عباس,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 20:7/q08/p4 God knows creatures' secrets and has hidden His own secret from them. — Zayd ibn Aslam and Ibn Zayd are repeatedly cited. Zamakhshari rejects this, Ibn Atiyya calls its past-verb construction weak, Qurtubi reports Tabari's rejection, and Alusi and Tabataba'i reject or disfavor it. Razi compares 2:255 and Samīn compares 20:110; al-Tabrisi gives the past-verb construction. Kuranyolu's attribution to Tabari concerns uncreated possibilities and differs from Qurtubi's attribution. [14 notes: THALABI(زيد بن أسلم); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(زيد بن أسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); TABRISI(زيد بن أسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(زيد بن أسلم وابنه); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); SAMIN-DURR; DURR-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); ELMALILI; KASHSHAF-FULL-; IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض السلف)-; QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري وابن عطية)-; ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 20:7/q08/p5 The secret is an inward intention or settled resolve; the more hidden is a subtler prompting not yet resolved or formed. — Mawardi and Bagawi distinguish resolve from impulse; Razi offers both formed resolve versus unformed notion and unconceived thought; Abu Hayyan reports a settled intention versus what has not entered the heart. Burhan places promptings beyond imagined whispers. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(unnamed view); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN]
- 20:7/q08/p6 The secret is inwardly concealed thought; the more hidden is a thought that arose and was forgotten. — Al-Baqir and al-Sadiq are cited by Tabrisi, Alusi and Tabataba'i; Tabataba'i also gives Abu Abd Allah's report through Muhammad ibn Muslim. [4 notes: TABRISI(الباقر والصادق); ALUSI-FULL(الباقر والصادق); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عن محمد بن مسلم,السيدان الباقر والصادق)]
- 20:7/q08/p7 The secret is an unspoken conscious thought; the more hidden reaches the subconscious or unconscious. — Asad gives conscious versus subconscious; Kuranyolu cites Esed on the same distinction. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(M. Esed)]
- 20:7/q08/p8 The secret is anything the human mind can contain; the more hidden is divine knowledge beyond human access. — Ibn Atiyya reports a group including possible future thoughts within the human side; Zajjaj says the further unseen is known to God alone. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]
- 20:7/q08/p9 The secret is an inspiration latent in the heart's core; the more hidden remains concealed until its manifestation begins. — Sulami places the inspiration beyond senses, reason, angels and devils, unfolding through will and decree; an explanation reported by Alusi compares it to fire latent in damp wood, stirred by will. [3 notes: SULAMI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 20:7/q08/p10 The secret and the more hidden are God's nested secrets within a person. — Sulami reports Junayd's formulation. [1 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد)]
- 20:7/q08/p11 The secret is what a person does not disclose; the more hidden is what only that person has said to themself. — Ibn Qutayba defines undisclosed sirr and self-confided akhfa; Majaz makes akhfa self-confiding kept from everyone and cites the poet's awhad for a non-comparative elative. Bursevi includes unspoken and future thoughts as other possibilities. [4 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; MAJAZ; BURSEVI]
- 20:7/q08/p12 God knows audible speech, what people conceal within, and deeds they do without realizing He knows them. — Muqatil distinguishes the person's concealment from their unawareness of God's knowledge. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 20:7/q09 How is akhfā construed grammatically?  [وَأَخْفَى · grammar]
turns on: Whether the form is an elative with an omitted comparison, a non-comparative elative, or a past verb; transmitted explanations and poetic usage are weighed.
- 20:7/q09/p1 It is a comparative or superlative adjective, 'more hidden than the secret,' with the comparison understood. — Al-Nahhas and Tabrisi supply 'than it'; Burhan and Itqan discuss omitted comparands and af'al outside predicate position; Razi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, Ibn Ashur, and Tabataba'i take the elative. Alusi and Tabataba'i say indefiniteness intensifies hiddenness; Wahidi distinguishes its comparative derivation. The Majaz work also reports an elliptical comparison. [16 notes: MAJAZ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ITQAN; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 20:7/q09/p2 It is a past-tense verb: God has concealed something from His servants. — Zayd's reported reading takes God as agent. Razi cites 2:255; Samīn and Zamakhshari compare 20:110, and Alusi cites Abu al-Shaykh's transmission. Ibn Atiyya calls it weak; Zamakhshari, Alusi and Tabataba'i reject or disfavor it. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI(زيد بن أسلم); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); ELMALILI; KASHSHAF-FULL-; IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض السلف)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري وابن عطية)-; ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 20:7/q09/p3 It is an elative used without comparison, meaning simply 'hidden.' — Majaz and Wahidi cite a poet's awhad meaning 'one'; Abu Ubayda cites al-Farazdaq's 'more noble and taller' for plain noble and tall. Ibn al-Anbari rejects this as remote without necessity and contrary to transmitted exegesis; Wahidi prefers the commentators' readings. [4 notes: MAJAZ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,الشاعر,قوم); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري)-]

## 20:7/q10 Why place 'more hidden' after 'the secret'?  [ٱلسِّرَّ وَأَخْفَى · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the order is explained by semantic emphasis or the verse ending; Burhan records both.
- 20:7/q10/p1 The ascending degree of hiddenness emphatically displays God's penetrating knowledge. — Burhan calls the wording more emphatic; Tabataba'i says movement from audible to secret to most hidden proves knowledge of all, even an intention hidden from its thinker; Kuranyolu says the pair conveys penetration of every detail; Razi divides the three levels. [4 notes: BURHAN; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 20:7/q10/p2 The order preserves the verse ending or rhyme. — Burhan offers the verse ending alongside the comparative analysis and notes reversal of an expected order; Itqan gives the same reason. [3 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN]

## 20:7/q11 What does the verse teach about God's knowledge?  [* · theology]
turns on: The notes develop the verse's omniscience as a doctrine of God's timeless attribute, moral accountability, or the source of human knowledge.
- 20:7/q11/p1 God knows all open, hidden, past and future things without limitation, even what people cannot know of themselves. — Qashani and Asad include outward and inward states; Ibn Abbas reports compare all creation to one soul under one knowledge, citing 31:28. Razi says knowledge is essential, unchanging and neither originated nor contingent; his sun analogy says imparting knowledge does not reduce God's. Bursevi says time changes objects, not divine knowledge. Tabataba'i compares 57:4; Kuranyolu takes universal knowledge as evidence of sovereignty. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; RAZI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:7/q11/p2 Knowledge of inward and outward deeds warns against sin and encourages obedience. — Razi prefers applying hidden levels to acts liable to reward and punishment; he includes inward and outward conduct. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+]
- 20:7/q11/p3 Human knowledge is tiny and derives from God's teaching, while divine knowledge remains undiminished. — Razi cites 4:166 and 67:14 for God teaching and knowing, likens imparted knowledge to sunlight that never diminishes, and sees detailed awareness of every particle in nature. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 20:7/q12 How do these words describe inward spiritual faculties?  [ٱلسِّرَّ وَأَخْفَى · inward]
turns on: How the two scriptural terms are mapped onto the ordered inner faculties.
- 20:7/q12/p1 Sirr is a subtle faculty between heart and spirit; khafī is a subtler faculty between spirit and divine presence receiving divine lights. — The Najmi commentary says sirr exists from first creation, while khafi appears in the second; monotheists alone host its unveiling and inspired knowledge. It links the following mention of God to divine attributes manifesting there and to Adam's reality. Alusi reports a matching Sufi account; Kuranyolu reports the broader heart-secret-spirit-hidden-most-hidden sequence from Qur'anic terms. [6 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL(بعض الصوفية); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Tasavvufçular)]
- 20:7/q12/p2 Sirr is the heart as the locus of awareness of God; akhfā is a deeper dimension of that reality. — Study Quran reports this as a common Sufi psychological teaching. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(a common Sufi psychological teaching)]

## 20:7/q13 How does this verse connect to its surrounding passage and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connection is the progression from dominion to knowledge, the source of revelation, or the larger Moses narrative.
- 20:7/q13/p1 After God's dominion and power, the verse establishes His comprehensive knowledge; together they establish sovereignty and lordship. — Razi and Alusi connect 20:5–6's power to 20:7's knowledge; Baydawi links complete attributes to their implications. Ibn Ashur places it between 20:6 and 20:8, calling the condition parenthetical and its proof-like assertion madhhab kalami. Biqai says human rulers' ignorance of distant domains disrupts rule, unlike God's ordered dominion. Tabataba'i says knowledge implies permission, will and governance, joining 20:6's ownership and comparing 57:4. Study Quran compares 2:284 and 2:255, 6:3, 13:10, 21:110. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:7/q13/p2 God's knowledge shows that the revelation comes from the Creator of hidden things. — Ibn Kathir invokes 25:6. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 20:7/q13/p3 The Prophet's situation is illuminated by Moses' experience and by the sura's account of Moses. — Biqai links Moses' forgetting, guidance, plea for eloquence and helper to the Prophet's prayer and God's aid through Umar, citing 20:115, 20:122 and 20:27. He says the Moses narrative rebukes Jews who coached Quraysh on the Spirit by giving unknown details: festival day, magicians' faith, threatened crucifixion and the Samaritan's trace. ## 20:7/q14 What is the ruling on sound at the recitation boundary discussed here?  [تَجْهَرْ · readings]
- 20:7/q14/p1 On connection, retain length before the following tāʾ after madd or layn; other preceding sukūn, including tanwīn, may join when Arabic usage and transmission allow. — Ibn al-Jazari states both the phonetic condition and the requirement of transmission. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 20:7/q15 How reliable is the unusual report preceding Ibn Kathir's explanation?  [* · reports]
- 20:7/q15/p1 The report is exceptionally strange, unique to al-Qasim ibn Abd al-Rahman, and apparently muddled or interpolated. — Ibn Kathir says its material combines other reports and may have been deliberately mixed or inserted. He cites Yahya ibn Ma'in calling al-Qasim worthless, Abu Hatim weakening him, and Ibn Adi calling him unknown. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو حاتم الرازي,ابن عدي,يحيى بن معين)]

## 20:7/q16 How is the Qur'an's inimitability known?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whose knowledge is at issue, and whether recognition is immediate or follows inference from rhetorical incapacity.
- 20:7/q16/p1 Its miraculous status is established by inference, although its appearance through the Prophet is known necessarily. — Baqillani and Itqan report al-Ash'ari's distinction; Baqillani contrasts a directly seen miracle such as the sea parting with the premises and comparison of styles needed here. [3 notes: BAQILLANI(أبو الحسن الأشعري); ITQAN(أبو الحسن الأشعري)]
- 20:7/q16/p2 A rhetoric expert knows necessarily his own inability to match it, then infers the inability of others; nonexperts know only by inference. — Baqillani explains that Qur'anic style has no counterpart in customary speech and differs from ordinary poetic skill. Itqan reports al-Qadi saying experts sense their own and others' incapacity, whereas Baqillani distinguishes necessary personal recognition from inference about others. [3 notes: BAQILLANI; ITQAN(القاضي)]
- 20:7/q16/p3 The Prophet knew its inimitability immediately at revelation, directly or from being told it proved his prophethood. — Baqillani places this knowledge before the Prophet recited it to others. [1 notes: BAQILLANI]
