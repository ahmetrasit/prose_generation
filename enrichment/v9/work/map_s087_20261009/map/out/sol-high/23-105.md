# 23:105: 16 questions

## 23:105/q01 What does the question do rhetorically?  [أَلَمْ · rhetoric]
- 23:105/q01/p1 It reproaches the condemned and reminds them that they denied recited signs, exposing the reason for their punishment and denying their excuse. — Al-Ṭabarī places the rejected recitation in worldly life. Al-Rāzī says the clear signs made their painful punishment deserved. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī call denial the reason for punishment. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the question also denies their excuse. Al-Biqāʿī stresses that they persisted in denial. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī state the confrontation or question plainly; Ibn Kathīr calls it a rebuke for disbelief, sins, forbidden acts, and grave offenses. [9 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 23:105/q02 Why does the nūn remain in تَكُنْ?  [تَكُنْ · grammar]
- 23:105/q02/p1 The nūn remains because the signs' recitation is treated as a completed, fully realized state. — Al-Burhān says the act of recitation has reached completion. Al-Zarkashī, as reported by al-Sāmarrāʾī, contrasts this with incomplete beginnings and compares the earth's already realized spaciousness. Al-Sāmarrāʾī likewise says each state is fully specified and its meaning complete. [3 notes: BURHAN; SAMARRAI-ASRAR(الزركشي); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 23:105/q03 What are 'My signs' here?  [ءَايَٰتِى · referent]
turns on: Whether 'signs' names the Qur'an specifically, divine books or messages more broadly, warnings, or God's proofs and arguments.
- 23:105/q03/p1 They are the Qur'an or its verses recited to the people. — Muqātil says the Qur'an told them about the Day and its events. Al-Ṭabarī locates its recitation in worldly life. Al-Wāḥidī, reporting Muqātil and al-Kalbī, identifies the signs in the next verse as the Qur'an; his own explanation adds denial of the Qur'an and Muhammad concerning the Fire. Al-Baghawī says the Qur'an warned them. Al-Ṭabrisī offers the Qur'an as one reading. Al-Nasafī says they claimed it was not from God. Ibn ʿĀshūr points to the ordinary senses of 'recited to you' and 'you denied them.' [11 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل والكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:105/q03/p2 They are God's books or conveyed messages. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports some exegetes saying God's books are recited to people. Asad calls them God's messages conveyed to the condemned. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); ASAD-NOTES]
- 23:105/q03/p3 They are warnings given to the people. — Al-Wāḥidī reports this explanation from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)]
- 23:105/q03/p4 They are God's proofs and arguments. — Al-Ṭabrisī offers this alongside the Qur'an as an explanation of the recited signs. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 23:105/q04 Why are the signs attributed to God?  [ءَايَٰتِى · rhetoric]
- 23:105/q04/p1 Attributing the signs to God presents them as possessing the highest rank. — Al-Biqāʿī grounds their elevated description in their attribution to God. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 23:105/q05 What does 'recited' mean?  [تُتْلَىٰ · meaning]
- 23:105/q05/p1 It means read or recited. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites earlier uses of tilāwa in 2:102 and 8:2. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:105/q06 What does the passive 'were recited' emphasize?  [تُتْلَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 23:105/q06/p1 The mere recitation of the signs should have sufficed to bring about belief. — Al-Biqāʿī draws this point from the passive construction. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 23:105/q07 What did their denial involve?  [بِهَا تُكَذِّبُونَ · interpretation]
- 23:105/q07/p1 They rejected the recited revelation in worldly life, persistently treating it as false. — Muqātil specifies denial of what the Qur'an foretold about the Day. Al-Wāḥidī specifies denial of the Qur'an and Muhammad regarding the Fire and its punishment. Al-Nasafī says they asserted it was not from God. Al-Biqāʿī stresses persistence. Asad says they habitually denied the conveyed messages. Al-Qurʾān Yolu calls this denial of recited revelation. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 23:105/q08 Why is 'in them' placed before 'you denied'?  [بِهَا تُكَذِّبُونَ · rhetoric]
- 23:105/q08/p1 The order emphasizes their persistent rejection of signs that should have led them to faith. — Al-Biqāʿī reads the advance placement of 'in them' this way. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 23:105/q09 What introductory speech is understood before the question?  [أَلَمْ تَكُنْ ءَايَٰتِى تُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْكُمْ فَكُنتُم بِهَا تُكَذِّبُونَ · grammar]
- 23:105/q09/p1 An omitted expression such as 'it is said to them' introduces the question addressed to the condemned. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya identifies an omitted introduction. Al-Bayḍāwī specifies an implied speech verb. Al-Ālūsī supplies يقال لهم. Ibn ʿĀshūr places the omitted speech on that Day. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:105/q10 Who is addressed, and who delivers the question?  [* · referent]
- 23:105/q10/p1 God addresses the condemned; He can put the question to them through whichever angels He wills. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām places the addressees in Hell. Abū Ḥayyān gives the angelic means of questioning. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Asad state that God asks them; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls them the condemned. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 23:105/q11 When does the confrontation occur relative to the recitation?  [* · interpretation]
- 23:105/q11/p1 The signs were recited and denied in worldly life; the condemned face this question in Hell or at the afterlife judgment. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām says the question is put to people in Hell. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī place recitation or denial in worldly life. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the question is addressed on that Day. Al-Qurʾān Yolu places the reminder at afterlife judgment. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 23:105/q12 Why does the rebuke leave the condemned without an excuse?  [* · interpretation]
- 23:105/q12/p1 They had received God's signs and warnings but denied them, making their punishment deserved; denial underlay their other evils. — Al-Rāzī cites the clear signs and their rejection as grounds for painful punishment. Ibn Kathīr says God sent messengers and books and removed objections; he cites 4:165 and 17:15 on messengers before punishment, and 67:8–11 on Hell's inmates admitting rejected warning. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī call denial the reason for punishment. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the question denies their excuse. Al-Qurʾān Yolu says their other evils began with rejection. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 23:105/q13 What does punishment for denial imply about human acts and desert?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether a punishable act can be voluntary and deserved if God creates human acts, and what causes the person's motivation.
- 23:105/q13/p1 The verse assigns punishment to bad human deeds; if God created those acts, punishing people for them would be unjust. — Al-Rāzī reports this as the Muʿtazilite argument from the verse. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); RAZI-FULL-]
- 23:105/q13/p2 The argument from punishment cannot establish uncreated voluntary acts: an unmotivated sin is accidental, while a motivated act ultimately depends on a cause outside the person's own acts. — Al-Rāzī argues that an accidental sin could not deserve punishment, and that treating the motivating cause as the person's act produces an infinite regress; the resulting compelled act would not deserve reward. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 23:105/q14 How do the condemned respond after this question?  [* · links]
- 23:105/q14/p1 They offer explanations, including that decreed wretchedness overcame them, and seek release when their plea comes too late. — Al-Rāzī says their answer supplies two explanations, without detailing them in this note. Al-Ṭabarī names their claim of decreed wretchedness in the Hell scene and mentions 40:49. Al-Qurʾān Yolu says their request to be brought out goes unanswered because they should have made it during worldly life. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 23:105/q15 How does the sūrah's wider narrative frame this scene?  [* · links]
- 23:105/q15/p1 The sūrah repeatedly depicts opposition to prophets and closes by contrasting the disbelievers' too-late wish to return with the believers' attained salvation. — The Study Quran notes recurrent opposition by affluent and powerful people to prophets, also mentioning 7:59–62 and 7:136; it says the conclusion shows doomed disbelievers wishing to return and do good too late. Al-Qurʾān Yolu says disbelievers mocked sincere Muslims who prayed and prepared for salvation, while those believers have now attained the happy outcome. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 23:105/q16 How is this verse invoked in explaining an omitted fa after idhā elsewhere?  [* · links]
- 23:105/q16/p1 Al-Burhān relates the clause 'when [signs] are recited' to three explanations of a missing fa: temporal idhā, emphatic hum, or omission because the verb is past. — Al-Burhān says the temporal explanation also accounts for the clause beginning 'and when [signs] are recited'; its note mentions 23:105 as a comparison. [1 notes: BURHAN]
