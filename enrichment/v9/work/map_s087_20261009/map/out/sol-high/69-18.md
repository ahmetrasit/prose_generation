# 69:18: 24 questions

## 69:18/q01 When is 'that Day' of presentation?  [يَوْمَئِذٍۢ · interpretation]
- 69:18/q01/p1 It is the Day of Resurrection and judgment, broad enough to include the trumpet blasts, swoon, resurrection, reckoning, and final destinations. — Al-Māwardī names Resurrection. Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursawī say the presentation follows the second blast but the day spans the entire sequence. Abū Ḥayyān allows the day to cover events after the first blast or to mark the second directly. Biqāʿī points back to the events described; al-Ālūsī supplies the implied trumpet blowing for the tanwīn. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 69:18/q02 How does 'that Day you are presented' connect grammatically to the preceding trumpet clause?  [يَوْمَئِذٍۢ تُعْرَضُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Which clause answers the earlier conditional and whether this time clause is new or a substitution.
- 69:18/q02/p1 The presentation clause answers the preceding conditional about the trumpet. — The Shaykh says tuʿraḍūn answers idhā in fa-idhā nufikha. Abū Ḥayyān likewise allows the phrase to answer the first conditional; Bursawī says 'that day' is governed by 'you are presented'. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 69:18/q02/p2 The earlier 'the event occurs' answers the conditional, while 'you are presented' begins a new sentence. — Al-Samīn explicitly rejects making presentation the conditional's answer and identifies waqaʿat al-wāqiʿa as the answer. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR+; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 69:18/q02/p3 The time phrase substitutes for or repeats the earlier time setting. — Abū Ḥayyān allows the timing clauses as substitution in the Resurrection setting. Al-Ālūsī makes yawmʾidhin tuʿraḍūn a substitution for the preceding time phrase. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the first yawmʾidhin repeats the idhā of the trumpet clause. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:18/q03 Why is 'that Day' repeated?  [يَوْمَئِذٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 69:18/q03/p1 Its repetition magnifies the terror of the Day. — Ibn ʿĀshūr counts five mentions of the day overall: the first echoes idhā in the trumpet clause and four follow it; the events begin with the trumpet blast. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:18/q04 Who is addressed by 'you will be presented'?  [تُعْرَضُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the universal address is described as all creation or specifically those accountable.
- 69:18/q04/p1 All people or all creation are addressed. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the address encompasses all creation; Ibn ʿĀshūr points to context and following details. Bursawī says everyone is addressed by predominance. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 69:18/q04/p2 It addresses all who are morally responsible and subject to reckoning. — Al-Ṭabrisī names all morally responsible people; Abū Ḥayyān specifies those subject to reckoning. [2 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 69:18/q05 What does being presented mean?  [تُعْرَضُونَ · interpretation]
- 69:18/q05/p1 People are brought before God for reckoning, questioning, judgment, and recompense. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, and others state presentation before the Lord. Al-Qushayrī adds rebuke or punishment as due; Tustarī adds that everything was already recorded in God's prior knowledge. Al-Qurṭubī cites 18:48. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Bursawī, al-Ālūsī, and Elmalılı liken it to a ruler inspecting troops; Biqāʿī adds selection of the good for honor and the corrupt for punishment. Ibn ʿĀshūr gives the lexical sense of showing goods to a buyer or troops to a commander, allows a literal display, and says presentation alludes to reckoning. Ṭabāṭabāʾī uses the seller/buyer likeness and cites 18:48. Study Quran cites 11:18 for 'brought forth.' Al-Iṣlāḥī says no place or object remains to hide someone after the heavens and earth are destroyed. Ibn Kathīr stresses God's knowledge of inner and outer states. [24 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 69:18/q05/p2 The presentation includes people's inward forms of their deeds and actions. — Al-Qāshānī explicitly specifies these inward forms. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 69:18/q06 Why are people presented when God already knows them?  [تُعْرَضُونَ لَا تَخْفَىٰ مِنكُمْ خَافِيَةٌۭ · theology]
- 69:18/q06/p1 The display reveals people's condition, establishes proof, and makes divine justice visible; it does not give God new knowledge. — Al-Ṭabrisī says God knows deeds by His essence and exposes them for creation to see. Al-Qurṭubī denies that presentation informs God and cites 18:48. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī say it manifests justice; the latter adds proof and God's prior knowledge. Bursawī compares 82:9. Elmalılı contrasts this complete disclosure with worldly reviews and cites 14:48. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God's dominion and knowledge are always true but become unmistakable on that Day. [7 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]

## 69:18/q07 How many presentations occur, and what happens at each?  [تُعْرَضُونَ · reports]
- 69:18/q07/p1 There are three: two for disputes, excuses, argument or accounting, then records fly into people's hands, right or left. — Qatāda transmits this, sometimes tentatively or as a mursal report. Reports are attributed to Abū Mūsā, Ibn Masʿūd or ʿAbd Allāh, and the Prophet. Abū Ḥayyān's ʿAbd Allāh report frames Moses as seeing them and includes reprimands. Al-Nasafī calls right-hand recipients saved and left-hand recipients doomed; al-Durr records a prayer for right-hand recipients. Ibn Kathīr says al-Tirmidhī gives Abū Hurayra rather than Abū Mūsā as Companion narrator. Al-Qurṭubī says Tirmidhī deemed the Abū Hurayra route unsound because al-Ḥasan did not hear from him. Al-Ālūsī gives no grading. [27 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(أبو موسى الأشعري,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله); IBNABIHATIM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(النبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو موسى); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو موسى الأشعري وابن مسعود); TABRISI(ابن مسعود وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو موسى); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); NASAFI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو موسى,عبد الله); BIQAI-FULL(أبو موسى رضي الله عنه); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم); ELMALILI(Ebû Mûsâ el-Eş‘arî); STUDYQURAN(IK, Q, R, Ṭ)]

## 69:18/q08 When do people fail to remember their families at Judgment?  [تُعْرَضُونَ · reports]
- 69:18/q08/p1 At the scales, the bridge, and the flying of records into hands. — ʿAbd al-Razzāq reports Qatāda tentatively transmitting a wife's question and the Prophet's answer. [1 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 69:18/q09 How exacting can a person's questioning be?  [تُعْرَضُونَ · inward]
turns on: The examples differ in subject and reported duration; the notes do not argue one against the other.
- 69:18/q09/p1 God asks even about favors and the time and gatherings devoted to Him. — Tustarī gives recognition of granted hours and gatherings and an answered request for a wife as examples, then asks how much more severe questioning about disobedience will be. [1 notes: TUSTARI]
- 69:18/q09/p2 A believer may be held before God for a single sin for a hundred years. — Tustarī reports this statement from ʿUtba the ascetic. [1 notes: TUSTARI(عتبة الغلام)]

## 69:18/q10 How should one prepare for the great presentation?  [تُعْرَضُونَ · inward]
- 69:18/q10/p1 Audit and amend oneself, weigh one's deeds before judgment, and persistently seek God over past failings. — ʿUmar urges self-accounting and weighing oneself before the final account. Muḥammad ibn Ḥāmid calls failure to prepare, amend the self, and keep supplicating heedlessness, especially when limbs will testify. [4 notes: SULAMI(محمد بن حامد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عمر بن الخطاب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); DURR-FULL(عمر)]

## 69:18/q11 How will the Prophet recognize his community at the presentation?  [تُعْرَضُونَ · reports]
- 69:18/q11/p1 By the marks of ablution, records in their right hands, and light ahead of them. — Al-Durr transmits the Prophet's report. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 69:18/q12 Why is 'you are presented' passive?  [تُعْرَضُونَ · rhetoric]
- 69:18/q12/p1 The presentation itself is terrifying, and the statement comes from the Almighty. — Biqāʿī gives both grounds for the passive wording. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 69:18/q13 How should the Throne imagery associated with the presentation be understood?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether presentation and Throne language requires bodily presence or is figurative or left uninterpreted.
- 69:18/q13/p1 Presentation and the bearing of the Throne imply that God is on the Throne and carried with it. — Al-Rāzī reports the anthropomorphists arguing that bearing the Throne would otherwise be pointless. Bursawī reports their claim that God is carried and present there. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(المشبهة); BURSEVI(المشبهة); RAZI-FULL(أهل التوحيد)-; BURSEVI-]
- 69:18/q13/p2 The scene uses familiar royal imagery to show divine majesty, judgment, dominion, or power without bodily location or need. — Al-Rāzī's people of divine oneness argue literal sitting would make God dependent on angels and imply their greater power; they compare God's house, the stone called His right hand, and record-keeping angels, none of which imply human limitations. He says the Throne, attendants, and assembly follow the custom of kings judging officials. Bursawī interprets coming amid clouds as the coming of God's command and decree; he reads a transformation report as manifestation in an attribute-rank and the vision of a beardless youth as dreamlike, because God lacks bodily qualities. Kur'an Yolu reports theologians reading the Throne as dominion and its bearers as angels administering the universe, with divine rule continuing after the material world's collapse; it also reports Ibn ʿĀshūr and Asad taking its bearing as decisive manifestation of power, citing 40:7. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(أهل التوحيد); BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Cehmiye, Mu‘tezile ve Sünnî ulemânın önemli bir kısmı,İbn Âşûr ve Esed); BURSEVI+; RAZI-FULL(أهل التوحيد)+; RAZI-FULL(المشبهة)-; BURSEVI(المشبهة)-]
- 69:18/q13/p3 Accept the wording of these ambiguous verses while leaving their meaning unknown. — Kur'an Yolu attributes this to Abū Ḥanīfa, al-Ashʿarī, and al-Māturīdī. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İmam Ebû Hanîfe, Eş‘arî ve Mâtüridî)]

## 69:18/q14 What does 'ʿarsh' in the preceding scene refer back to?  [* · links]
- 69:18/q14/p1 Its earlier Qurʾanic discussion, especially al-Aʿrāf 7:54. — Kur'an Yolu points to that earlier discussion without adding a new definition here. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 69:18/q15 Who are the eight bearers of the Throne in the preceding scene?  [* · referent]
turns on: The Qurʾan does not explain who the eight are; the alternatives are contextual inferences.
- 69:18/q15/p1 Eight angels or Throne-bearers. — Kur'an Yolu says the Qurʾan does not identify them and commentators infer this from context; its translation favors angels. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 69:18/q15/p2 Eight persons. — Kur'an Yolu reports this alternative and says al-Rāzī prefers it. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 69:18/q15/p3 Eight ranks. — Kur'an Yolu reports this alternative without further grounds. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 69:18/q15/p4 Eighty angels. — Kur'an Yolu reports this alternative without further grounds. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]

## 69:18/q16 How is the trumpet scene before the presentation described?  [* · links]
- 69:18/q16/p1 A horn-shaped trumpet is blown twice by Isrāfī: the first blast kills the living, and the second raises them. — Kur'an Yolu gives this as traditional belief and cites 6:73; the scene leads to the presentation after the destruction of the present heavens and earth. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 69:18/q17 How is this presentation linked to the surrounding verses and sūra?  [* · links]
- 69:18/q17/p1 The catastrophe gives way to universal presentation and then the distinct outcomes of the righteous and wicked. — Biqāʿī moves from warning about the Day of Calling to the general scene, then the particular division. Ibn ʿĀshūr says reckoning and recompense introduce the later detailed outcomes. Al-Iṣlāḥī says 13–18 portray Judgment's terrors. Kur'an Yolu says 13–18 explain 1–3 and describe Resurrection and the end of worldly life, citing 18:47. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 69:18/q18 Which verb reading occurs in 'lā takhfā'?  [لَا تَخْفَىٰ · readings]
turns on: Agreement with a grammatically feminine but nonnaturally feminine and separated subject, plus differing reader attributions.
- 69:18/q18/p1 The verb is feminine, lā takhfā, agreeing with khāfiyah. — Al-Farrāʾ calls both readings sound. The general or majority reading is feminine; Abū Ḥātim prefers it because the subject is feminine. Al-Samīn calls overt feminine marking the default. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes that khāfiyah is separated from the verb and not naturally feminine. Reports vary in assigning the masculine reading: al-Thaʿlabī and al-Qurṭubī say Kufans except ʿĀṣim, while Jishumī includes ʿĀṣim. [15 notes: FARRA; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(قراءة العامة); JISHUMI(عاصم والكسائي); BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الباقون); TABRISI(حمزة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(حمزة والكسائي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; NASHR(حمزة، والكسائي، وخلف); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); QURTUBI-FULL(الباقون)+]
- 69:18/q18/p2 The verb is masculine, lā yakhfā. — Al-Farrāʾ attributes it to Yaḥyā ibn Waththāb and considers both forms sound, comparing paired feminine and masculine verbs for 'the cry seized.' Al-Naḥḥās says a nonliteral feminine subject can take masculine agreement when separated. Abū ʿUbayd prefers yāʾ because it can serve masculine or feminine while tāʾ only feminine, and minkum intervenes. Al-Samīn and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite nonnatural femininity and separation. Readers named across sources include Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, Khalaf, ʿAlī, Ibn Waththāb, Ṭalḥa, al-Aʿmash, ʿĪsā, and Ibn Miqsam from ʿĀṣim; assignments vary, with Jishumī naming ʿĀṣim and al-Kisāʾī but others excluding ʿĀṣim. [18 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(الكوفيون); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; THALABI; JISHUMI(عاصم والكسائي); BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وعلي بن أبي طالب وابن وثاب وطلحة والأعمش وعيسى); TABRISI(حمزة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(حمزة والكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(الكوفيون إلا عاصما); BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; NASHR(حمزة، والكسائي، وخلف); ALUSI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وابن وثاب وطلحة والأعمش وابن مقسم عن عاصم); IBNASHUR-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وخلف); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيد)+]
- 69:18/q18/p3 An imperative masculine yakhfī can make God the implicit subject: 'He will hide no secret from you.' — Qiraat-er calls this the Imp form and gives its resulting subject and sense. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(Imp)]

## 69:18/q19 What is the grammatical relation of 'no hidden thing is concealed' to 'you are presented'?  [لَا تَخْفَىٰ مِنكُمْ خَافِيَةٌۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause functions as circumstance or as an independent, explanatory, or substitutive clause.
- 69:18/q19/p1 It is a circumstantial clause describing people as they are presented. — Bursawī says it is a ḥāl of 'you are presented'; al-Ālūsī specifies its subject. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 69:18/q19/p2 It can be independent, explain the preceding event, or be a substitution of inclusion. — Ibn ʿĀshūr offers all three possibilities. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:18/q20 Why and how is 'minkum' placed before 'khāfiyah'?  [مِنكُمْ خَافِيَةٌۭ · grammar]
- 69:18/q20/p1 It qualifies khāfiyah but is fronted, and its position makes it circumstantial. — Bursawī says the fronting serves the rhyme and the phrase syntactically reaches both preceding and following wording. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a fronted qualifier that becomes a circumstantial phrase. [2 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:18/q21 To whom is no hidden thing concealed?  [لَا تَخْفَىٰ مِنكُمْ خَافِيَةٌۭ · referent]
turns on: Whether the implicit observer is God, people generally, or God with His angels.
- 69:18/q21/p1 Nothing is concealed from God. — Muqātil and others say neither person nor deed escapes Him. God already knows all acts; al-Wāḥidī's Ibn ʿAbbās includes hidden traits. Al-Kalbī includes deeds and persons. Al-Ṭabarīsī allows the phrase's hidden thing to be a person, act, or verbal noun. Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 86:9 and 40:16 for secrets becoming public; Study Quran cites 40:16. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الكلبي,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن شجرة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 69:18/q21/p2 Nothing is concealed from people: hidden selves, deeds, and secrets become public. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī cite 86:9 about secrets tested. Biqāʿī says graves and hearts disclose all, magnifying the saved person's joy and the wicked person's misery. Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes unseen things becoming witnessed; Study Quran reports exposure of inner states causing believers' joy and disbelievers' grief. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 69:18/q21/p3 Nothing is concealed from God or His angels. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly includes the angels alongside God. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:18/q22 What is the 'khāfiyah' that cannot remain hidden?  [خَافِيَةٌۭ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the concealed item is an act, person, inner state, nakedness, or a person's moral and religious standing.
- 69:18/q22/p1 It is a hidden deed or act. — Ibn Shajara specifies deeds people used to conceal; Ibn ʿAbbās includes hidden acts and traits. Al-Kalbī says no deed is hidden; Ibn ʿĀshūr says a feminine implicit act can be understood. Asad stresses even the most hidden deed. Other sources also allow a person as an alternative. [10 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن شجرة); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 69:18/q22/p2 It is a hidden person or soul: no one evades judgment. — Muqātil specifies neither righteous nor wicked person is concealed. Al-Iṣlāḥī says nothing remains in the destroyed heavens and earth to hide a person. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that no confusion remains between believer and unbeliever or virtuous and wicked. Study Quran reports Q's translation 'None among you shall be hidden.' [12 notes: MUQATIL; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,مقاتل); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 69:18/q22/p3 It is any inner secret, thought, belief, state, or concealed thing. — Jishumī derives khāfiyah from concealment, the opposite of public disclosure. Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives an inner thought or conviction; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Nasafī mention formerly hidden states. Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes beliefs and deeds and cites 86:9 and 40:16. Study Quran's R says disclosure brings believers joy and disbelievers grief. Al-Ṭabrisī also permits the verbal noun 'anything hidden.' [9 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 69:18/q22/p4 It may mean exposed nakedness: no private parts remain covered. — Al-Māwardī quotes the Prophet that people are gathered barefoot and naked; al-Qurṭubī gives the same report as the basis for this interpretation. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 69:18/q22/p5 It entails the visible distinction of believers from unbelievers and the righteous from the wicked. — ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr gives the distinction; Ibn ʿĀshūr says no confusion between the groups remains. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:18/q23 What does the adjacent description of a weak heaven mean?  [* · meaning]
- 69:18/q23/p1 Wāhiyah means weak; wahā is to weaken, as with a torn water skin. — Al-Samīn cites a poem as evidence for the verb's usage. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 69:18/q24 What does 'at its edges' in the adjacent verse mean?  [* · meaning]
- 69:18/q24/p1 Arjāʾihā means its sides or regions. — Al-Samīn says singular rajā has a short alif written against the pattern of ramā, supported by rajawān and cited poetry. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
