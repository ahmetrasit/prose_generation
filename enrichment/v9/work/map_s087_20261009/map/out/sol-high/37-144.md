# 37:144: 15 questions

## 37:144/q01 What kept Jonah from remaining in the fish?  [لَلَبِثَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the saving tasbih is prior devotion, prayer in the fish, or both.
- 37:144/q01/p1 His established worship, glorification, remembrance, and righteous deeds brought divine rescue. — Muqatil, Tabari, Wahidi, Qatada, and others locate the saving devotion before the trial; Tabari and Ibn Kathir cite God's remembrance of earlier worship, and Ibn Kathir says an Anas report supports Ibn Jarir's preference if sound. Al-Rabi's maxim says good deeds raise one after a stumble (Wahidi, Qurtubi, Durr); Samarrai reads the descriptive form as an established habit. Ibn Atiyya adds God's prior decree. Al-Dahhak contrasts Pharaoh's too-late profession at drowning (10:90–91). [34 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(الضحاك بن قيس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الربيع بن أنس,الضحاك بن قيس,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس بن مالك); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس,الضحاك,سعيد بن أبي الحسن,قتادة); BURSEVI(الوسيط); ALUSI-FULL(أنس); ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; STUDYQURAN(IK, Ṭ,the Prophet Muhammad); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير)+]
- 37:144/q01/p2 His prayer, confession, and glorification when distressed brought his release. — Nahas attributes the expulsion to prayer after confinement; Abu Hurayra and Anas reports describe angels hearing or recognizing his faint, familiar voice and interceding (Wahidi, Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, Alusi). Islahi identifies the tasbih with the confession of 21:87 and says Jonah recognized punishment and turned wholeheartedly; Hamid says he invoked God before expulsion. Al-Hasan expressly says Jonah did not pray inside the fish. [11 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو هريرة عن النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس بن مالك); DURR-FULL(حميد بن هلال,عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(أنس); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن)-]
- 37:144/q01/p3 God delivered him after his previous good deeds, though he did not pray inside the fish. — Al-Hasan says he had done good earlier, stayed only briefly, and was cast out well after being swallowed. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو هريرة عن النبي)-; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة)-; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس بن مالك)-; ALUSI-FULL(أنس)-]

## 37:144/q02 How does the conditional form make the counterfactual work?  [لَلَبِثَ · grammar]
turns on: How the exception after negation and the consequent of لولا are parsed.
- 37:144/q02/p1 It is the consequent of an exceptional لولا conditional: without Jonah's prior praise, he would have remained. — Ibn al-Jawzi calls this an original conditional use of لولا. Burhan answers Ibn al-Shajari's objection from the accusative exception after negation in 37:143: the negative sense is implicit even if substitution would normally be stronger. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); BURHAN(ابن الشجري); ELMALILI]
- 37:144/q02/p2 The exception's accusative suggests a positive construction, though negation can be understood implicitly. — Ibn al-Shajari argues substitution is strongest after negation, although the accusative is allowed; Burhan reports and answers him through implicit negation in 37:143–144. [1 notes: BURHAN(ابن الشجري)]
- 37:144/q02/p3 Al-Khalil reportedly treated Qur'anic لولا as هلا except in Jonah's case; the exception is questioned. — Ibn Barjan quotes al-Khalil on the unusual consequent here; Burhan questions the alleged exception. [1 notes: BURHAN(الخليل)]

## 37:144/q03 What would remaining in the fish until resurrection entail?  [لَلَبِثَ فِى بَطْنِهِۦٓ إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ يُبْعَثُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the conditional is literal, what survives within it, and how the resurrection endpoint is understood.
- 37:144/q03/p1 The fish's belly would have become Jonah's grave until resurrection. — Qatada is cited by Tabari, Mawardi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Durr, and Alusi; Bagha wi, Tabrisi, Qurtubi, Study Quran, and Kuranyolu also give the grave or death reading. Kuranyolu says he would die inside. Alusi says the fish's body could endure intact without affliction; Biqai allows Jonah to become the fish's food and mix with it. Ibn Ashur envisages fish and Jonah dead at the sea bottom. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Aj, Ṭ); NASAFI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 37:144/q03/p2 Jonah would have remained alive inside the fish until resurrection. — Nasafi calls this the apparent sense; Abu Hayyan and Alusi also read it literally, while Aj in Study Quran says he would live until human resurrection. Biqai and Bursevi allow the fish and Jonah both to survive, or the fish to die while Jonah remained. Alusi counters the objection that no living creature survives the first trumpet by making the trumpet a precursor of resurrection. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Aj); ALUSI-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 37:144/q03/p3 The wording leaves alive or dead unspecified; it conveys continued confinement until raising. — Baydawi allows alive or dead; Bursevi says the verb permits either, and lists three survival/death combinations from Kashf al-Asrar. Tabatabai compares confinement until raising to a grave and cites 20:55, but says the verse does not establish life, death, fish-body survival, or trumpet timing. Elmalili leaves these mechanics open. [8 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; MUQATIL]
- 37:144/q03/p4 The conditional endpoint can express an exceedingly long period. — Alusi offers this response if reports that all living things perish at the first trumpet are accepted; Tabatabai says the verse itself does not establish this idiom. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 37:144/q03/p5 Jonah could have remained on the sea bottom because the fish died or was trapped underwater. — Ibn Ashur offers a dead fish resting on the sea bottom, or a fish caught underwater and never surfacing; the latter need not specify Jonah's life or death. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 37:144/q04 How is فِى بَطْنِهِۦٓ attached syntactically?  [فِى بَطْنِهِۦٓ · grammar]
turns on: Direct verbal attachment versus a circumstantial phrase.
- 37:144/q04/p1 It attaches directly to لَلَبِثَ. — Samin calls this the apparent construction. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR+]
- 37:144/q04/p2 It is circumstantial, meaning Jonah was settled there. — Samin reports this alternative parsing. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(قيل)]

## 37:144/q05 What creature does the belly belong to?  [بَطْنِهِۦٓ · referent]
turns on: The creature's specific identification.
- 37:144/q05/p1 The fish or whale that swallowed Jonah. — The sources routinely call the creature a fish or whale; Bursevi's report says God entrusted Jonah to it and forbade injury to bone or joint. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 37:144/q05/p2 Probably a shark. — Islahi proposes this identification in recounting Jonah's swallowing and rescue. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 37:144/q06 How long did Jonah actually stay inside the fish?  [لَلَبِثَ فِى بَطْنِهِۦٓ · reports]
turns on: Conflicting transmitted estimates, from an hour to forty days.
- 37:144/q06/p1 Forty days, or forty days and nights. — Abu Malik, al-Suddi, Ibn Jurayj, al-Kalbi, Anas, Ka'b, Ibn Abbas, Ikrima, Sa'id b. Abi al-Hasan, and others transmit forty. Bursevi gives forty nights and infers the stay from Jonah's sick condition on landing; Kashifi calls forty better known than three or seven. Some reports add repentance and a gourd after release. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو مالك); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو مالك); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن جريج والسدي عن أبي مالك); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو مالك والسدي وابن جريج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أنس بن مالك وكعب وأبو مالك وابن جريج والسدي); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي والكلبي ومقاتل بن سليمان); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(أبو مالك,ابن جريج,ابن عباس,سعيد بن أبي الحسن,عكرمة); BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 37:144/q06/p2 Seven days. — Ja'far, Ata, Sa'id b. Jubayr, and others give seven; Durr says the fish carried Jonah through all seas and deposited him by the Tigris. Kashifi also transmits seven as a lesser known duration. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(جعفر); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عطاء بن أبي رباح); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير وعطاء); QURTUBI-FULL(عطاء); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 37:144/q06/p3 Three days. — Qatada, Muqatil b. Hayyan, Mujahid, and other reports give three; Kashifi records it as less well known than forty. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل بن حيان); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مقاتل بن حيان); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد وقتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل بن حيان); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 37:144/q06/p4 Less than a day, from morning or midmorning until evening. — Al-Sha'bi is reported by Mawardi, Ibn al-Jawzi, and Nasafi; Ibn al-Jawzi specifies expulsion before sunset. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الشعبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الشعبي); NASAFI-FULL(الشعبي)]
- 37:144/q06/p5 Twenty days. — Al-Dahhak is cited by Wahidi, Ibn al-Jawzi, and Qurtubi. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 37:144/q06/p6 About one hour of daylight. — Ibn Atiyya reports a group giving an hour; Qurtubi reports an hour and leaves the true duration to God. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 37:144/q06/p7 Seven hours. — Ibn Atiyya reports another group's estimate. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]
- 37:144/q06/p8 Fourteen days. — Ibn Atiyya reports another group's estimate. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]
- 37:144/q06/p9 Only a short time, without a number. — Al-Hasan says Jonah did earlier good and was expelled some time after being swallowed. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن)]
- 37:144/q06/p10 The reports conflict, so no definite duration is adopted. — Abu Hayyan declines to repeat the contradictory accounts; Qurtubi leaves the matter to God even while noting an hour report. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 37:144/q07 Where did the fish carry Jonah, and where was he released?  [فِى بَطْنِهِۦٓ · reports]
turns on: Different itineraries in the transmitted accounts.
- 37:144/q07/p1 Past Eilat, then by the Tigris to Nineveh. — Mawardi gives this as an unnamed report. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(قيل)]
- 37:144/q07/p2 Through all seas, then onto the Tigris bank. — Sa'id b. Jubayr's seven-day account in Durr gives this itinerary. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 37:144/q07/p3 Across seven seas, where Jonah could see their wonders. — Kashifi says Jonah's flesh and skin became transparent while he remembered God; Bursevi transmits the account. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]

## 37:144/q08 What happened when Jonah prayed and was expelled?  [فِى بَطْنِهِۦٓ · reports]
turns on: Different reported details of prayer, angelic intercession, and recovery ashore.
- 37:144/q08/p1 God ordered the fish to cast him out after angels recognized his prayer and interceded. — Abu Hurayra's report has faint glorification, past deeds, and angels' intercession (Wahidi); Qurtubi adds the fish was forbidden to hurt bone or flesh and Jonah heard sea creatures praise God before reaching shore sick. Anas's report describes the familiar voice from a distant place and previously accepted prayers; Ibn Kathir says its attribution to the Prophet is tentative, while Alusi calls a version marfu without grading. Ikrima says Jonah recounted his confinement and asked what good deed he could offer; the angels recognized him. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو هريرة عن النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس بن مالك); ALUSI-FULL(أنس); DURR-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 37:144/q08/p2 Prayer caused the fish to eject him, with no angelic intercession specified. — Sa'id b. Jubayr in Nahhas reports expulsion after prayer; Hamid says Jonah invoked God and was cast ashore. [2 notes: NAHHAS; DURR-FULL(حميد بن هلال)]
- 37:144/q08/p3 He emerged sick and was sustained under a gourd before the plant withered. — Tawus says God rebuked Jonah for mourning the plant but not a hundred thousand people; Ikrima says its shade, fruit, and root sustained him; Hamid says it fed him while his wasted body recovered. Bursevi and Qurtubi also mention his sickness on shore. [5 notes: DURR-FULL(حميد بن هلال,طاوس,عكرمة); BURSEVI; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة)]

## 37:144/q09 What does the endpoint إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ يُبْعَثُونَ mean?  [إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ يُبْعَثُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the endpoint is read literally or as a long-period expression.
- 37:144/q09/p1 The Day of Resurrection, when creatures are raised from their graves. — Qatada explicitly identifies the endpoint; Bursevi states the same. Tabatabai compares being brought out of the fish then to rising from a grave and cites 20:55. [4 notes: DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; TABATABAI]
- 37:144/q09/p2 It may mark a very long period in the counterfactual, rather than a literal survival claim. — Alusi offers this if the first-trumpet mortality reports are accepted; Tabatabai cautions that the verse alone does not establish such an idiom. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 37:144/q10 Who are 'they' in يُبْعَثُونَ?  [يُبْعَثُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the implicit plural is understood as people or all creatures.
- 37:144/q10/p1 Those raised at resurrection: people, or all creatures. — Bursevi speaks of creatures rising from graves, and Study Quran's Aj says human beings. Biqai says the plural allows all creatures to be included. Alusi observes that the plural has no explicit antecedent. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
- 37:144/q10/p2 The plural has no explicit antecedent in the wording. — Alusi makes this syntactic observation without naming a narrower referent. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 37:144/q11 Why is resurrection expressed with a plural verb?  [يُبْعَثُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The plural's scope and its unexpressed antecedent.
- 37:144/q11/p1 The plural makes the resurrection universal. — Biqai argues a singular might exclude animals, while a dual might imply only Jonah and the fish rise. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 37:144/q11/p2 The plural is implicit without an expressed antecedent. — Alusi points to the absence of an explicit antecedent. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 37:144/q12 What spiritual lesson is drawn from Jonah's rescue?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Which aspect of Jonah's rescue the commentators make exemplary.
- 37:144/q12/p1 Practice remembrance and righteous action in ease, so God helps in hardship. — Al-Dahhak's sermon contrasts Jonah with Pharaoh's rejected drowning confession (10:90–91); Tabari, Baydawi, Biqai, Bursevi, and Study Quran urge habitual praise. Al-Rabi's maxim says good deeds raise one after a fall; the Anas reports make the angels appeal to earlier accepted worship. Biqai calls the rescue a sign of divine greatness. [25 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك بن قيس); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو هريرة عن النبي,الربيع بن أنس,الضحاك بن قيس,مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس بن مالك,ابن جرير); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس,الضحاك,سعيد بن أبي الحسن,قتادة); BURSEVI(الوسيط); ALUSI-FULL(أنس); STUDYQURAN(IK, Ṭ,the Prophet Muhammad); SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 37:144/q12/p2 Confess folly and seek forgiveness wholeheartedly when calamity reveals a fault. — Islahi says Jonah recognized punishment immediately and reads his prayer in 21:87 as an ideal confession and plea for forgiveness. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 37:144/q12/p3 The prophet stories together teach divine mercy, support for the messenger, and reward for good deeds. — Islahi says this refrain summarizes the narratives. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 37:144/q13 How does this episode fit the surrounding prophet stories?  [* · links]
turns on: How the account relates to other verses and the order of prophet narratives.
- 37:144/q13/p1 The Jonah story was already told in Sura al-Anbiya. — Ibn Kathir points to 21:87–88. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 37:144/q13/p2 The prophets are ordered chronologically through Aaron, then by their attributes. — Islahi uses this to explain the sequence around Jonah. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 37:144/q13/p3 These verses condense Jonah's ordeal to foreground its instructive lesson. — Kuranyolu says the Qur'an supplies a brief report after Jonah left home rather than bare history; Islahi identifies a shared prophetic refrain. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 37:144/q14 What fuller narrative background is reported for Jonah?  [* · reports]
turns on: The degree of detail supplied by Biblical and Qur'anic accounts.
- 37:144/q14/p1 A Biblical account describes Jonah's mission to Nineveh, flight by ship toward Tarshish, storm, casting overboard, swallowing, prayer, return, recommission, and Nineveh's repentance. — Kuranyolu says the fuller Biblical account broadly matches the Qur'an; Elmalili, through Alusi, adds departure via Jaffa and Jonah's distress at Nineveh's wickedness. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Kitâb-ı Mukaddes); ELMALILI(Âlûsî)]
- 37:144/q14/p2 The Qur'anic passage briefly recounts Jonah's departure and instructive ordeal. — Kuranyolu describes this as the emphasis of these verses. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 37:144/q15 What unrelated material appears in one source excerpt attached to this verse?  [* · interpretation]
- 37:144/q15/p1 Razi's excerpt turns to jinn, devils, their origin, naming, and classification. — Razi first notes a clipped report about divine help until a person's conversion and many related hadiths (r1). He cites 15:27 and 7:12 for creation from fire (r2), then physicians' account of the hot heart, Galen's monkey dissection, and innate heat to argue life in fire is plausible (r3); some imagine spiritual beings in a sphere of fire (r4). Concealment etymology is supported by janna, al-janna, majnun, janin, and jinnah (63:2), potentially including angels until usage narrows the term (r5); a second derivation calls jinn Garden custodians (r6), which Razi judges weaker (r7). He lists angels, humans, jinn, devils as accountable classes (r8), reports devils as a separate genus (r9), and reports evil jinn called devils alongside good jinn (r10). None of these claims explains the Jonah verse. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL+]
