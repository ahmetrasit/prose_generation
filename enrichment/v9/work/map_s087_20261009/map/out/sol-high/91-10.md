# 91:10: 17 questions

## 91:10/q01 What does repeating qad before the failure statement accomplish?  [وَقَدْ · rhetoric]
- 91:10/q01/p1 It confirms the certainty of failure and makes the oath apply to this assertion as well. — Al-Ālūsī explicitly gives both functions for the repeated particle. [3 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 91:10/q02 What does khāba say the loser has lost?  [خَابَ · meaning]
- 91:10/q02/p1 The person fails to attain the sought good or aim, ultimately lasting good and reward in the hereafter. — Al-Naḥḥās glosses missing the goal; al-Qāmūs, quoted by Bursawī, adds loss and deprivation. Biqāʿī says the prepared afterlife reward is lost and effort is vain. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes durable benefit from immediate gain that ends in loss; Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise contrasts success in reaching a goal with failure. Elmalılı adds hopeless regret and cites 89:24. Corpus Coranicum observes that this sūra leaves afterlife recompense implicit, though 91:12 may suggest it. [9 notes: NAHHAS; BURSEVI(القاموس); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 91:10/q03 Who is the agent that buries or corrupts the soul?  [مَن دَسَّىٰهَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the subject of the verbs resumes man or is God; grammatical pronominal return, the Prophetic prayer, transmitted glosses and the relation of human action to divine decree are cited.
- 91:10/q03/p1 The human being acts upon his or her own soul. — Al-Farrāʾ contrasts purification through obedience and charity with neglect. Al-Ḥasan, Qatāda, al-Rabīʿ and others contrast obedience and good works with self-ruin through sin. Al-Zamakhsharī appeals to the surrounding inspiration and choice; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī say the apparent subject is man, with hā referring to the soul. Al-Naḥḥās finds a God-subject relative clause syntactically awkward; al-Samīn says the needed pronominal return through nafs and its possessor is awkward. Al-Ālūsī calls this the immediate reading while conceding the reports do not settle it. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats actor and soul as one being represented twice for choice and action; Jishumī finds the soul the natural antecedent. [32 notes: FARRA; THALABI(الحسن); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI(قتادة وغيره); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; DURR(الحسن,الربيع); DURR-FULL(الحسن,الربيع); ALUSI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+; JISHUMI+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 91:10/q03/p2 God debases, hides, corrupts or misguides the soul, and the person thereby fails. — Mujāhid, Ibn ʿAbbās, Ikrima, al-Kalbī and others are transmitted for this reading. Al-Farrāʾ allows it. Al-Wāḥidī and Rāzī use the oath and prior decree to deny self-directed purification independent of God; Ṣāḥib al-Intiṣāf cites transmitted reports, consistent divine subjects in the oath, and parallel tazakkā. Ibn ʿAṭiyya invokes the Prophet's prayer to God as purifier. Al-Naḥḥās raises the relative-clause objection. Al-Zamakhsharī rejects this as attributing reprehensible destiny to God; al-Ālūsī says that charge is not established by the grammar or reports. - 91:10/q03/p3 The wording permits human involvement under God's creating and decreeing of acts. — Bursawī says ordinary speech attributes a blow to Zayd although God creates the act; al-Rāghib distributes purification among servant, God, charity and worship. Al-Ālūsī says grammatical attribution requires human participation, not independent creation or power. Study Quran cites 35:18, 76:30, 4:49, 24:21 and 13:11 for the two sides. Qutb locates accountable choice within God's encompassing will and decree. [9 notes: BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; QUTB-ZILAL]

## 91:10/q04 What does the suffix hā refer to?  [دَسَّىٰهَا · grammar]
- 91:10/q04/p1 It refers to the soul mentioned in verse 7. — Jishumī calls this the most plausible antecedent; al-Ālūsī pairs the suffix with a subject pronoun referring to man. Corpus Coranicum states the syntactic reference explicitly. Al-Samīn describes the return through nafs and its possessor as the difficulty for the God-subject analysis. [5 notes: ALUSI; CORPUSCORANICUM; JISHUMI+; SAMIN-DURR+]

## 91:10/q05 What does dassāhā mean here?  [دَسَّىٰهَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word's concealment image is applied to sin, divine misguidance, specific moral states, hypocrisy or bodily matter; Corpus Coranicum disputes deriving the contextual sense from concealment.
- 91:10/q05/p1 To bury, hide, lower or diminish the soul through sin, neglect, ignorance or miserliness. — Ibn Qutayba says the sinner hides and loses dignity; benefactors' conspicuous hilltop homes and fires versus misers' recesses illustrate the image, also developed by al-Muʿtazila, al-Qurṭubī, Biqāʿī and Ibn al-Jawzī. The unnamed poet praises visible hospitality; another line about ʿAmr illustrates burial or ruin. Al-Wāḥidī invokes 16:59 for concealment and says obedience is left unmanifest. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Ālūsī contrast growth/elevation with diminishment. Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses prevention of good; al-Rāghib describes insertion of one thing into another. Elmalılı adds stunted virtue, vile bodily aims, ostentation and obscured divine light; Qutb speaks of weakening the conscious faculty. Ṭabāṭabāʾī likens the lost growth to a plant deformed by covert insertion. [70 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الشاعر); TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI(ابن عيسى); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(الشاعر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,الفراء); RAZI(المعتزلة); RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); QURTUBI(قتادة وغيره); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI(الشاعر,الكشاف); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ELMALILI(الشاعر); QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(IK,Sh, Z); CORPUSCORANICUM-]
- 91:10/q05/p2 To misguide or seduce the soul into error. — Mujāhid, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and Ibn ʿAbbās are reported with closely related active and passive phrasings. Al-Qurṭubī cites a poem about ʿAmr's widowed wives; Durr's Ibn ʿAbbās survey glosses this term as leading astray. Some accounts assign misguidance to God, others to the person. - 91:10/q05/p3 To make the soul sinful, wicked, impure or corrupt. — Qatāda gives sin and wickedness; al-Lisān calls the term the antonym of zakā, impure. Corpus Coranicum prefers a separate poetic example for ruin/corruption to a sense derived only from Qurʾānic antithesis. Elmalılı links this to repeated deceit and spoiling. Biqāʿī specifies corrupt beliefs, acts, intentions and states. ## 91:10/q06 How is the form dassāhā derived?  [دَسَّىٰهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the contextual sense follows the doubled d-s-s concealment derivation or a contamination image; Corpus Coranicum says a hide/insert derivation makes little contextual sense.
- 91:10/q06/p1 It comes from the doubled d-s-s root, with one repeated sīn changed to a weak letter, normally yāʾ and then alif. — Al-Farrāʾ, Abū ʿUbayda and the grammarians compare dassastu/dassasahā with qaṣaytu, taẓannaytu and taqaḍḍā; al-ʿAjjāj's hawk line and other poets illustrate substitution. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn describe replacement of the third repeated consonant; al-Ālūsī specifies yāʾ becoming alif after fatḥa and an intensified hiding sense. Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes both dasā-yadsū and doubled dassā-yudassī. Al-Majāz gives mulibb from albabtu and a verse by al-Muḍarrib ibn Kaʿb for settled labīb. Elmalılı relates Turkish desise and the varied senses of desis and dessas to this root. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the substitution eases articulation. [58 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء، أبو عبيدة، أهل المعاني); BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,الفراء); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(أهل اللغة); QURTUBI-FULL(أهل اللغة); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(القاموس); ALUSI(الشاعر,بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Sh, Z); CORPUSCORANICUM(Lisān); CORPUSCORANICUM-]
- 91:10/q06/p2 Its contamination sense can be explained from wrapping or putting something into dirt, with an intensifying transformation. — Islāḥī gives this derivation, stressing the intensified sense of contamination. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 91:10/q07 How does the verse relate human choice to God's decree?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the attribution to a human requires independent efficacy, whether divine misguidance may be attributed to God, and how the supplication and reported glosses bear on the subject.
- 91:10/q07/p1 People are responsible for the path they choose, while their capacity and acts remain within God's will and guidance. — Jishumī says God disclosed both paths and enabled both choices. Bursawī says God creates acts but they are attributed to their human performer. Al-Ālūsī denies that agency entails independent creation. Qutb joins freedom to accountability, divine measures in revelation and signs, and decree realized through conduct, citing 13:11. Study Quran combines human striving and God's will with 35:18, 76:30, 4:49, 24:21 and 13:11. Islāḥī likens heedlessness to closing one's eyes and falling; using divine gifts properly is gratitude, abusing them self-destruction. [19 notes: JISHUMI; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 91:10/q07/p2 The oath locates success and failure in God's prior purification, abandonment or misguidance, not independent self-purification. — Al-Wāḥidī, quoted by Rāzī, says the oath by noble creatures guards against supposing people independently reform or ruin themselves. Rāzī's school gives God the misguiding act. Ibn ʿAṭiyya finds support in the Prophet's supplication; al-Zamakhsharī attacks this attribution, while al-Ālūsī says its transmitted basis defeats his charge of Sunni inversion. [11 notes: RAZI(أصحابنا,الواحدي); RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا,الواحدي); IBNATIYYA; ALUSI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(صاحب الانتصاف)+; KASHSHAF-; KASHSHAF-FULL-]
- 91:10/q07/p3 The human being's own purification or corruption determines the outcome. — Al-Zamakhsharī sees the human action in the inspiration context and rejects the God-agent reading as attributing evil destiny to God. The reported Muʿtazilite glosses locate burial in the person's choices: immersion in sin, sinful company, neglected obedience or hiding among the righteous. [13 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI(المعتزلة); RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة)]

## 91:10/q08 What prayer is reported when the Prophet recited this passage?  [* · reports]
- 91:10/q08/p1 He asked God to grant his soul piety and purify it as its guardian and best purifier. — Al-Thaʿlabī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya connect the prayer to recitation. ʿĀʾisha says she heard it at night. Zayd ibn Arqam's longer report adds refuge from incapacity, laziness, miserliness, cowardice, senility or worry, grave punishment, useless knowledge, an unsated soul, an unhumble heart and unanswered supplication; the accounts vary in these additions. Ibn ʿAṭiyya uses the prayer to argue for God as purifier. [5 notes: THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(عائشة); BAGHAWI(زيد بن أرقم); BAGHAWI-FULL(زيد بن أرقم); IBNATIYYA(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 91:10/q09 How is the recitation of this sūra valued in a report?  [* · reports]
- 91:10/q09/p1 A report likens reciting Sūrat al-Shams to giving charity to everything the sun and moon reach. — Jishumī transmits the report through Ubayy ascribed to the Prophet. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبي)]

## 91:10/q10 How are the verse endings pronounced in the reported readings?  [دَسَّىٰهَا · readings]
turns on: Degree of inclination of the final vowel and whether pronunciation follows the original form or ease of articulation.
- 91:10/q10/p1 Ibn Kathīr, Ibn ʿĀmir and ʿĀṣim use fatḥ and tafkhīm. — Jishumī says fatḥ is the original pronunciation. [2 notes: JISHUMI(ابن كثير وابن عامر وعاصم)]
- 91:10/q10/p2 Al-Kisāʾī uses imāla throughout. — Jishumī explains imāla as easing pronunciation. [2 notes: JISHUMI(ابن كثير وابن عامر وعاصم)]
- 91:10/q10/p3 Other readers use intermediate or root-conditioned patterns. — Jishumī says the intermediate sound both eases pronunciation and indicates the original form. [2 notes: JISHUMI(ابن كثير وابن عامر وعاصم)]

## 91:10/q11 Do the success and failure statements answer the preceding oath?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Whether a missing oath lām or an entire answer is supplied, and whether the statements are integral to the oath, parenthetical, or fronted.
- 91:10/q11/p1 Yes: the pair is the oath's answer or complement. — Elmalılı says most commentators take this view, with the lām of laqad omitted because the oath is long, and that it summarizes the sūra. Corpus Coranicum sees the eight-verse oath ending in opposed moral outcomes. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the pair the answer; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares Sūrat al-Layl at 92:4–5. Al-Ālūsī argues omission of lām is common, especially after length, and lighter than omitting a whole clause; he invokes al-Zamakhsharī's treatment of 23:1 and says purification is a central goal. [13 notes: ELMALILI; CORPUSCORANICUM; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 91:10/q11/p2 No: they interrupt the oath, whose omitted answer threatens deniers with destruction like Thamūd. — Al-Zamakhsharī identifies Meccan deniers of Muḥammad and the parallel denial of Ṣāliḥ by Thamūd. Qāshānī elaborates that veiled deniers reject inspiration, let the dark self dominate and perish through transgression. Islāḥī endorses the suppressed-complement analysis but says omission lets the whole evidence of the oaths count; destruction of rebellious peoples is one prominent implication. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows an interruption explaining divinely indicated piety. Al-Ālūsī counters that deleting the lām is easier than omitting a whole clause and denies that purification is a mere preliminary. [9 notes: KASHSHAF; QASHANI; ALUSI(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 91:10/q11/p3 The oath's answer is omitted: 'you will surely be resurrected.' — Al-Qurṭubī reports this alternative reconstruction. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 91:10/q11/p4 The statements are advanced before the oath by inversion, without an omitted answer. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this word-order analysis. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 91:10/q12 How does this failure statement connect to its surrounding verses and sūras?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the verse is read chiefly as the culmination of the oath's contrasts, as a lesson embodied by Thamūd, or in relation to adjoining sūras; these links can coexist.
- 91:10/q12/p1 It completes a chain of contrasts between nature, moral capacity, purification and failure. — Ibn ʿĀshūr lists sun/moon, day/night, disclosure/covering, heaven/earth, building/spreading, vice/piety and success/failure. Corpus Coranicum says the pre-Islamic oath genre is adapted with biblical creation ideas in verses 5–7, then proceeds to the moral pair. Abū Ḥayyān notes taqwā followed by success and failure followed by the next example, though the notes vary in describing that counterpoint. Elmalılı maps purification to daylight/sky and burial to night/earth. Jishumī says the signs reveal divine power and blessing. [9 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; JISHUMI; STUDYQURAN]
- 91:10/q12/p2 Thamūd's coming denial and punishment exemplify the failure caused by corrupting the soul. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links 91:11 to arrogance and the failure claim; Abū Ḥayyān says God names a people after the general warning so others learn. Al-Zamakhsharī and Qāshānī read the parallel as the oath's suppressed threat to Meccan deniers. [7 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KASHSHAF; QASHANI]
- 91:10/q12/p3 It carries forward Sūrat al-Balad's two paths and anticipates al-Layl's account of moral divergence. — Jishumī says obedience here is called purification where the prior sūra called it the steep path. Biqāʿī says al-Balad's warning about rebellion at 90:11 spurs reform, while al-Layl explores differing paths despite shared desires; night's darkness and sleep, sibling of death, thwart aims. Al-Ālūsī links the previous right/left groups and 90:10's two paths to this summary. Islāḥī says al-Layl identifies corruption and protection of the soul. Study Quran also cites 90:10. [7 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 91:10/q13 What practical or spiritual lesson is drawn from the failure?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw both a moral warning and an inference about divine attributes, without presenting them as mutually exclusive.
- 91:10/q13/p1 Sin and neglect of obedience cause loss, so the verse warns people to cultivate piety, good works and vigilance. — Jishumī reads the pair as encouragement and warning; al-Rabīʿ connects good and evil acts to the two outcomes. Al-Ṭabarī describes lost guidance, sin and abandoned obedience. Islāḥī says awareness of good and evil requires choice, and denying accountability contradicts the soul's witness; misuse of gifts destroys oneself. Qutb stresses responsibility for fate and the need to check desire against God's revelation and signs; purification remains an ongoing task. Study Quran notes that some regarded soul-purification as the Qurʾān's whole purpose. [23 notes: FARRA; TAB; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI(قتادة وغيره); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); DURR(الربيع); DURR-FULL(الربيع); QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 91:10/q13/p2 The oath-backed promise and warning also testify to God's knowledge, necessary existence, perfect attributes and grace. — Al-Ālūsī says the sworn signs and the paired outcomes disclose God's attributes and beneficence. [3 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 91:10/q14 What inward readings are given to burying the soul?  [* · inward]
turns on: The inward interpreters apply concealment to watchfulness, entanglement in matter, or an allegory of the preceding cosmic signs.
- 91:10/q14/p1 Failure is neglect of inward watchfulness, divine demands and orientation to God. — Abū ʿUthmān al-Maghribī contrasts inspecting one's food with neglect; Abū Bakr ibn Ṭāhir contrasts guarding the inner secret from the world with occupying it by the world. Other voices contrast knowing God's demands with neglect and turning toward God with turning away. Tustarī says the astray soul fails by disregarding its afterlife. [5 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر,أبو عثمان المغربي,بعضهم); TUSTARI]
- 91:10/q14/p2 The soul is buried in bodily matter, appetites and darkness, away from divine light. — Qāshānī pictures bodily soil; al-Ālūsī reports material pollution; Bursawī adds unleashed natural appetites. Elmalılı says sin, ostentation and dark feelings bury the divine light in the breathed spirit. [7 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]
- 91:10/q14/p3 The cosmic images map the soul's advancement or corruption: prophecy, sainthood and gnosis versus desire and estrangement. — Biqāʿī identifies sun with prophethood, dawning light with the mission, moon with sainthood, day with gnosis, and night with estrangement; sky represents dignity above passion and satanic steps, earth humility and benefiting believers. He quotes Abū Ḥanīfa and al-Shāfiʿī, as transmitted by al-Khaṭīb, that acting scholars are God's friends. [6 notes: BIQAI(العلماء العاملون); BIQAI-FULL(أبو حنيفة والشافعي)]

## 91:10/q15 What grammatical difficulty arises if God is the subject of the paired verbs?  [* · grammar]
- 91:10/q15/p1 The relative pronoun cannot straightforwardly resume an element within its own clause, though man may be construed as the soul or as a group. — Al-Naḥḥās supplies the objection and the two possible grammatical workarounds. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 91:10/q16 What report describes God's fashioning of the soul?  [* · reports]
- 91:10/q16/p1 God fashioned the soul's creation completely, leaving nothing out. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports this gloss from Mujāhid. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد)]

## 91:10/q17 How is purification set against the failed soul?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes contrast rising purity and goodness with debasement, without treating these as rival accounts.
- 91:10/q17/p1 Purification grows and cleanses the soul through righteous action, piety and removal of desire, preparing it for lasting life. — Qatāda, as quoted by al-Naḥḥās, links purification to righteous acts. Al-Naḥḥās says God's purification enables obedience and compares zakā of wealth as growth by alms or cleansing by the poor's share; zakiyya and zakī mean pure. Al-Qurṭubī points to crop growth and a judge's validation of a witness for the root sense of increase. A prophetic saying quoted by Bursawī links cleansing desire from the self to freeing the heart from attachment to anything besides God. Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 2:197 for piety as provision for the soul's enduring life. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the oath is prepared by stating purification leads to success while failure to reform leads to vice and loss. [7 notes: NAHHAS(قتادة); QURTUBI; BURSEVI(عبد الله بن عباس رضي الله عنهما); TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 91:10/q17/p2 Defilement makes the soul low and base. — Ibn al-Jawzī transmits this gloss from al-Zajjāj. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج)]
