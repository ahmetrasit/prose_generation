# 71:18: 9 questions

## 71:18/q01 What does ثم convey before the return to earth?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether ثم orders events by time or orders the argument by importance.
- 71:18/q01/p1 It marks the long interval from creation to death and burial. — Alusi says that people live under obligations and earn recompense during this interval. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 71:18/q01/p2 It marks a rise in importance to the warning of resurrection, with burial preparing for that main point. — Ibn Ashur says the resurrection warning matters more than the prior proof from creation, so the ordering is by rank rather than elapsed time. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 71:18/q02 What is God's returning people into the earth?  [يُعِيدُكُمْ فِيهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the return is physical burial or an inward attachment to the material world.
- 71:18/q02/p1 People die and are buried in the earth, their bodies returning to it. — Mawardi and Tabrisi specify corpses in graves; Ibn Atiyya calls burial customary human practice. Biqai says the return is gradual through death and burial, however long lives last, and death is undisputed. Tabatabai cites 7:25. Ibn Kathir compares their eventual raising with initial creation. Several sources couple this return with later emergence. [19 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 71:18/q02/p2 It is an inward turn to earthly pleasures, desires, bodily dispositions and material veils. — Qashani reads the earth as the condition of attachment from which a person may later emerge through voluntary death. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 71:18/q03 What is God's bringing people out of the earth?  [وَيُخْرِجُكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether emergence denotes the final bodily resurrection or an inward resurrection of the heart.
- 71:18/q03/p1 God raises people alive from the earth for resurrection, gathering, judgment and recompense. — Muqatil places it at the final blast and says they return to God. Ibn Atiyya names gathering, judgment and recompense; Baydawi and Alusi name the gathering. Bursevi names reward for God's friends and reckoning for His enemies. Tabatabai cites 7:25; Ibn Kathir relates resurrection to initial creation. Asad calls it a final bringing-forth. [20 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 71:18/q03/p2 It is emergence from earthly attachment at the station of the heart through voluntary death. — Qashani interprets this as a resurrection from the earth of bodily dispositions and material veils. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 71:18/q04 Why does و join emergence to return?  [وَيُخْرِجُكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 71:18/q04/p1 Burial and emergence are linked as one certain sequence, with return preceding emergence. — Bursevi says neither part can fail. Alusi says the states of the barzakh and afterlife form one unit even though the whole sequence follows origination after a delay. Tabatabai says the waw, rather than ثم, hints at one process. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 71:18/q05 What is the grammatical form of إخراجا?  [إِخْرَاجًۭا · grammar]
- 71:18/q05/p1 It is the regular إفعال verbal noun of an أفعل verb, within the system of augmented verb forms. — Sibawayh gives يخرجكم إخراجا as the example and says أفعل always takes إفعال. For comparison he gives افتعل–افتعال, with a connected initial alif, such as احتبست احتباسا and انطلقت انطلاقا; استفعل–استفعال, such as استخرجت استخراجا and استصعبت استصعابا; فعل–تفعيل, whose initial ت replaces the extra عين and whose ي corresponds to the alif of إفعال; تفعل–التفعل, retaining letters and damma on عين to avoid confusion with تفعيل; and تفاعل–تفاعل, parallel to تفعل and with damma to avoid a plural-like form. He says speakers of كذابا also say تحملت تحمالا, extending an إفعال-like alif. The fixed فاعل source is مفاعلة, whose م and ة compensate for two alifs, as in جالسته مجالسة and قاعدته مقاعدة, though forms such as قيتالا, مراء and قتالا also occur; فعال is common while مفاعلة is regular. [9 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 71:18/q06 Why is إخراجا added after the verb?  [إِخْرَاجًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: Which feature of the wording is being explained: emphatic verbal noun, bodily identity, or matching verse endings.
- 71:18/q06/p1 The verbal noun emphatically confirms that God will bring people out and that resurrection is certain. — Tabrisi says it emphasizes the act; Razi intensifies the assertion; Baydawi makes the promised return as certain as initial creation; Nasafi and Abu Hayyan stress certainty. Biqai and Ibn Ashur connect the force to deniers of resurrection. Islahi says the passage's verbal nouns emphasize their verbs by different modes that are difficult to translate. [9 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 71:18/q06/p2 It specifies that the very people returned to earth will emerge again, with bodies. — Burhan says the phrasing counters the claim that resurrection involves disembodied souls alone. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 71:18/q06/p3 إخراجا is chosen over خروجا to match the endings of the preceding verses. — Ibn Ashur calls preservation of the verse endings a mark of excellence. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 71:18/q07 What does this sequence establish or demand?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw different lessons from the same sequence: resurrection, oneness, worship, accountability and the nature of the next abode.
- 71:18/q07/p1 God's power to create and revive makes resurrection credible and promises life after death. — Razi says initial creation proves capacity to resurrect. Ibn Ashur says humans are formed from earth's elements like plants, with a promise of life after death. Islahi compares humans with vegetation that grows, dies and returns to earth: God's revival of dead vegetation shows His power to raise people at will. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 71:18/q07/p2 The cycle is proof of God's oneness. — Razi calls growth from earth, return and emergence the third proof of divine oneness. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 71:18/q07/p3 God's creation and gifts call people to worship Him alone and not answer His blessings with denial. — Tabrisi says God alone knows people's interests and governs wisely. Ibn Kathir says God alone creates and provides, with no peer, equal, partner, spouse, child, minister or adviser; Noah invokes His creative power, the sky as a structure, earth as a resting place and expanded provision. [3 notes: TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 71:18/q07/p4 Return and emergence remind people of their responsibility and final reckoning. — Kuranyolu stresses afterlife responsibility; Bursevi specifies reward for God's friends and reckoning for His enemies. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BURSEVI]
- 71:18/q07/p5 In return and emergence, people occupy the abode of truth. — Tabatabai contrasts these states with worldly life, the abode of delusion. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 71:18/q08 How does this verse fit the surrounding passage and sura?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes emphasize different transitions and scales of context; Islahi alone records a choice over who speaks in verses 15–20.
- 71:18/q08/p1 The passage moves from human beginnings to death and future emergence. — Elmalili says the earlier stages give way to humanity's end. Study Quran says that if verse 14 covers only life's stages, this verse extends them through death and resurrection, as in 2:28. [2 notes: ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 71:18/q08/p2 The passage moves from heavenly signs to earthly signs, placing humanity first among earth's creatures. — Islahi describes humanity as earth's noblest creatures; Ibn Kathir includes Noah's invocation of heavenly and earthly benefits. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 71:18/q08/p3 Verses 15–20 are divine insertions before Noah's prayer resumes. — Islahi prefers this over reading all six verses as Noah's speech because verse 21 renews 'Noah said, My Lord.' [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 71:18/q08/p4 Verses 15–20 can be read as part of Noah's speech. — Islahi acknowledges this reading but prefers divine insertion on the evidence of verse 21. [0 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 71:18/q08/p5 The sura presents Noah's preaching to his erring people. — Asad calls the Meccan sura wholly devoted to this subject and mentions 11:25. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 71:18/q08/p6 Noah's story symbolizes every conscious believer's struggle against blind materialism and loss of spiritual values. — Asad gives this reading of the story and mentions 11:25. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 71:18/q08/p7 The sura's aim is to establish God's power to destroy and replace rejecters and to resurrect them. — Biqai says Noah's people's destruction establishes the warning to those who deny resurrection. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 71:18/q08/p8 The basmala frames the sura with God's majesty, general outward gifts and lasting blessing for obedient allies. — Biqai assigns majesty and generosity to God, universal gifts to al-Rahman and enduring blessing to al-Rahim. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 71:18/q09 What details of the neighboring wording are noted with this verse?  [* · links]
turns on: These notes concern different nearby expressions and do not record a common dispute.
- 71:18/q09/p1 فجاج denotes broad branching roads, while a فج can be a mountain pass. — Jishumi gives both senses for the neighboring wording. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 71:18/q09/p2 ديار derives from دوران, with an original waw turned to ya and assimilated. — Tabrisi reports al-Zajjaj's explanation that ما بالدار ديار means no one is moving about there. [1 notes: TABRISI(الزجاج)]
- 71:18/q09/p3 A poet used connected ديار where separated wording was expected, by poetic necessity. — Tabrisi cites the line as an example of unusual connected wording. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 71:18/q09/p4 The object of أسررت is omitted and understood as supplication, warning or the like. — Burhan says a source-like form can coexist with the ellipsis because it need not convey literal emphasis. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 71:18/q09/p5 إسرارا can fit the omitted object because it serves rhyme, or because أسررت becomes intransitive once its object is disregarded. — Burhan compares the second explanation with 'he gives and withholds'; the two explanations are alternatives in the note. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 71:18/q09/p6 A synonym rather than a verb's own source can supply emphasis. — Burhan gives جهارا as a kind of الدعاء, ليا as a kind of distortion, and perhaps بهتانا as a kind of wrongdoing. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 71:18/q09/p7 The second verbal construction can convey ease. — Islahi prefers this sense while allowing other interpretations; the note does not name those alternatives. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
