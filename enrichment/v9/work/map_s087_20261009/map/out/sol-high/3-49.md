# 3:49: 78 questions

## 3:49/q01 How is 'a messenger' connected syntactically to the preceding words?  [وَرَسُولًا · grammar]
turns on: Whether a governing verb is supplied, the phrase coordinates with earlier predicates or circumstances, or rasūl is taken as a verbal noun.
- 3:49/q01/p1 An omitted verb such as 'He makes/sends him' governs the accusative. — Al-Ṭabarī cites poetic ellipsis; Abū Ḥayyān prefers this as the least strained parse; al-Samīn illustrates implied verbs with poetry and 59:9. Versions supply makes, sends, or recites. [19 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(قول أول); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); IBNASHUR-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 3:49/q01/p2 It means that Jesus speaks to the people as a messenger. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq prefer this because the following first-person declaration is his speech; the Kufans let rasūl carry the sense of saying. [12 notes: NAHHAS(قول ثان); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج)+; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق)+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 3:49/q01/p3 It coordinates with earlier circumstantial descriptions or the clause 'He teaches him'. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya connects it with Jesus's honor or speech as child/adult; Ibn ʿĀshūr treats yaʿlamuhu as a single circumstantial clause. Abū Ḥayyān objects to long separation in some parses. Al-Samīn reports the Sheikh rejecting the long-link analysis as built on another weak coordination. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية,الزمخشري وابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 3:49/q01/p4 It is circumstantial after 'He teaches him', treating wāw as extra. — Al-Akhfash proposes an extra wāw; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn call this unsupported or weak. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش); RAZI-FULL(الأخفش); SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش)-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 3:49/q01/p5 It is governed by an implied 'I was sent' inside Jesus's quoted speech. — Al-Zamakhsharī supplies both speech and verb to resolve the person shift; Abū Ḥayyān says the double omission is needless, while al-Samīn explains its logic. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(اليزيدي); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; ALUSI-FULL(البحر,التفتازاني)-]
- 3:49/q01/p6 As a verbal noun 'message', it is another object of 'teach him'. — Al-Ḥūfī and Abū al-Baqāʾ propose this; al-Samīn adduces poetry. Al-Ālūsī objects that Arabic does not use 'teach a message' this way. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 3:49/q02 What does rasūlan mean here?  [وَرَسُولًا · meaning]
turns on: Whether rasūl names a sent person or a message.
- 3:49/q02/p1 A sent messenger, Jesus. — Al-Samīn derives the adjective on faʿūl and compares ṣabūr and shakūr; al-Ālūsī says this is the immediate sense. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; TABRISI; QASHANI; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(العنانية); ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 3:49/q02/p2 A message, as a verbal noun. — Al-Samīn cites two anonymous poetic lines; Abū al-Baqāʾ allows it, but al-Ālūsī rejects it as less natural. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 3:49/q03 How is rasūlan read?  [وَرَسُولًا · readings]
turns on: The case ending changes its syntactic link.
- 3:49/q03/p1 The usual accusative rasūlan. — The accusative supports an omitted governing verb or circumstantial parse. [21 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(قول أول,قول ثان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الأخفش,الحوفي,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء,أبو حيان,البحر,التفتازاني,بعضهم)]
- 3:49/q03/p2 A variant reads rasūl in the genitive, coordinated with 'a word'. — Al-Yazīdī is named; Abū Ḥayyān finds the long separation weak, and al-Samīn awkward but finds no clearer parse. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(اليزيدي); ALUSI-FULL(اليزيدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(اليزيدي والزمخشري)-]

## 3:49/q04 When did Jesus become a messenger?  [وَرَسُولًا · reports]
turns on: The reports give incompatible ages and durations.
- 3:49/q04/p1 Revelation or commission came in adulthood at thirty, followed by three years of prophecy. — Wahb and al-Baḥr report age thirty; al-Ālūsī calls that account famous. One report instead gives three months and three days before his raising. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البحر,قيل)]
- 3:49/q04/p2 He was commissioned in childhood, in one account at age three. — Al-Baghawī reports the childhood view; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī points to Jesus speaking as an infant and 19:29–30. [4 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI]
- 3:49/q04/p3 His preaching lasted thirty-three years. — Al-Ṣādiq's report gives thirty-three years; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī suggests the figure may refer to his lifespan. [2 notes: TABATABAI(الصادق عليه السلام)]

## 3:49/q05 To whom was Jesus sent, and how broad was his mission?  [إِلَىٰ بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · referent]
turns on: Whether all Israel, only a subset, or a particular mission within wider prophethood.
- 3:49/q05/p1 His commission was to all the Children of Israel. — Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī, Rāzī and Kur'an Yolu explicitly oppose the claim that he addressed only a particular Israelite group. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; JISHUMI+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; RAZI-FULL+; TABRISI+]
- 3:49/q05/p2 He was sent only to a particular Israelite group. — Some Jews are said to claim this; Bayḍāwī gives a limited-commission possibility. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعض اليهود); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bazı yahudiler); JISHUMI-; TABRISI-; RAZI-FULL-; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 3:49/q05/p3 The wording rebuts a claim that Jesus was sent to non-Israelites. — Bayḍāwī and an unattributed report offer this; al-Ālūsī doubts the alleged Jewish claim, finding it absent from familiar Jewish books. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أكثر اليهود,العنانية,العيسوية,قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 3:49/q05/p4 His special messenger mission was to Israel, while prophethood could reach other peoples. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes particular messengership from conveying religion generally; he cites Moses's address to Pharaoh and Jesus's acceptance among Romans, eastern peoples, and Najrān. [3 notes: TABATABAI]

## 3:49/q06 How does Jesus's Israelite mission relate to Torah law?  [إِلَىٰ بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · law]
turns on: Retaining earlier law with some abrogation.
- 3:49/q06/p1 He confirmed and taught Torah law while making some prohibitions lawful. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya mentions certain foods, camel meat, fish and birds; al-Qushayrī and Jishumī say some rulings were retained and some abrogated. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 3:49/q07 Who was the first and last prophet of Israel in the reports?  [بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · reports]
turns on: Reports differ between Joseph and Moses for the first.
- 3:49/q07/p1 Joseph was first and Jesus last. — Al-Baghawī asserts this; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī report it. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 3:49/q07/p2 Moses was first and Jesus last. — A report attributed to the Prophet in Jishumī says this; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī also note Moses as an alternative. [3 notes: JISHUMI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 3:49/q08 What minor vocal or sound variants occur in the opening words?  [بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ أَنِّى · readings]
- 3:49/q08/p1 The hamza of Isrāʾīl and the first-person suffix have dialectal variants without changed sense. — Qirāʾāt ER records both the base pronoun vocalization and a dialectal suffix variant, plus a change in the glottal stop of Isrāʾīl. [3 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 3:49/q09 Whose speech is 'I have come to you' and how does it enter the narrative?  [أَنِّى قَدْ جِئْتُكُم · rhetoric]
turns on: Where angelic announcement ends and Jesus's later speech begins.
- 3:49/q09/p1 The angels tell Mary what Jesus will later say to Israel. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān carry the announcement onward; the transition to Jesus's speech is understood after 'Israel'. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 3:49/q09/p2 The narrative shifts to Jesus's later direct address to Israel. — Asad prefers this on the narrative movement from 3:52; al-Ṭabrisī says Jesus addressed them when sent. [6 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES+]

## 3:49/q10 How is the first annī in 'I have come' read and governed?  [أَنِّى قَدْ جِئْتُكُم · grammar]
turns on: Open hamza attaches the clause; broken hamza resumes or quotes speech.
- 3:49/q10/p1 With fatḥa, it depends on messengerhood or an implied bi or li. — Al-Kisāʾī relates it to sending; Sībawayh and al-Farrāʾ delete bi; al-Naḥḥās treats anna as accusative; Ibn ʿĀshūr hears the declaration accompanying the mission. - 3:49/q10/p2 A kasra reading begins direct speech or a fresh assertion. — Al-Zajjāj and Ibn Khālawayh allow an implied saying or new sentence; Qirāʾāt ER describes the independent assertion. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; THALABI(نافع والباقون); JISHUMI(نافع); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الكوفيون,بعض القراء); NASHR(المدنيان); IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وأبو جعفر); QIRAAT-ER]

## 3:49/q11 What does 'a sign' denote here, and why is it singular?  [بِـَٔايَةٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether it names bird-making specifically or a class/unity of multiple miracles.
- 3:49/q11/p1 It is proof of Jesus's prophethood and truthful mission, embracing the listed miracles. — Al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Isḥāq, Rāzī and others call it evidence; al-Baghawī says many signs establish one truth; al-Ālūsī says indefiniteness magnifies it; Rāzī treats the singular as generic. [31 notes: MUQATIL; IBNHISHAM; TAB-FULL(محمد بن جعفر بن الزبير); IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Some opinions); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 3:49/q11/p2 The singular sign is specifically the clay bird; later cures form a new sentence. — Al-Wāḥidī explicitly parses the bird miracle as the sign and the other wonders as subsequent discourse. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TAB-FULL]

## 3:49/q12 Is 'sign' read in the singular or plural?  [بِـَٔايَةٍۢ · readings]
turns on: The canonical singular is generic; Ibn Masʿūd's plural expresses the number of acts.
- 3:49/q12/p1 The common text has singular āya in both occurrences. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn take singular as a collective or generic designation. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود,جمهور الناس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن مسعود,الجمهور وعبد الله); SAMIN-DURR(ابن مسعود); BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL]
- 3:49/q12/p2 Ibn Masʿūd or ʿAbd Allāh reads plural āyāt in both places. — This directly reflects the multiple signs. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود,جمهور الناس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن مسعود,الجمهور وعبد الله); SAMIN-DURR(ابن مسعود,عبد الله); ALUSI-FULL]

## 3:49/q13 How does 'with a sign' attach to 'I have come'?  [بِـَٔايَةٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether a direct complement or a circumstantial accompaniment.
- 3:49/q13/p1 It attaches to 'I have come', with bāʾ marking accompaniment or transitivity. — Al-Ālūsī gives both uses; Ibn ʿĀshūr emphasizes Jesus's status as messenger while taking the bāʾ as accompaniment. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 3:49/q13/p2 It is a circumstantial phrase: Jesus came bearing a sign. — Al-Samīn allows an omitted general predicate, but rejects Abū al-Baqāʾ's specific implied 'advancing'. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 3:49/q14 How does the following 'to make lawful' clause relate to 'I came with a sign'?  [بِـَٔايَةٍۢ · links]
- 3:49/q14/p1 It is coordinated with Jesus's coming: he came with a sign and to make some things lawful. — Al-Kashshāf links the following wa-li-uḥilla back to bi-āyatin min rabbikum. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 3:49/q15 What does 'from your Lord' modify, and why say 'your Lord'?  [مِّن رَّبِّكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether it qualifies the sign or the coming.
- 3:49/q15/p1 It attaches to 'I have come': Jesus came from God with the sign. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī allow this; al-Biqāʿī and al-Ālūsī say 'your Lord' evokes beneficence and obedience. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 3:49/q15/p2 It qualifies the sign as having its figurative origin with God. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn posit an omitted relational phrase; al-Ālūsī calls min figurative origin. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 3:49/q16 Why does the speech shift between singular and plural address?  [لَكُم رَّبِّكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 3:49/q16/p1 The shift shows the sign is witnessed and beneficial both individually and collectively. — Al-Biqāʿī says it answers doubts that only a group could witness it or that it benefits just one person. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 3:49/q18 What does 'I create' mean in Jesus's claim?  [أَخْلُقُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether khalq here is shaping measured material or origination from nonexistence.
- 3:49/q18/p1 Jesus measures and shapes existing clay into a bird likeness. — Al-Ṭabarī's successors cite craft usage, Zuhayr and other poetry, a measured sandal, and 29:17 and 23:14 for nonabsolute uses. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls proportioning the root sense and distinguishes originating an animal. [38 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو علي عن أبي الحسن); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو علي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 3:49/q18/p2 Khalq can also mean bringing an entity from nonexistence, a sense reserved for God. — Abū Ḥayyān distinguishes the two senses; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says bodily origination properly belongs to God, while figurative khalq covers fabricating falsehood at 29:17. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 3:49/q19 Can a human be called a creator or originator on the evidence of this verse?  [أَخْلُقُ · theology]
turns on: The scope of human agency and the divine name al-Khāliq.
- 3:49/q19/p1 A servant can be called creator only in a qualified sense of forming or originating an act; God alone is Creator absolutely. — Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī use the verse against the Mujbira; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 'best of creators' at 23:14. Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes Jesus's shaping from God's origination of life. [8 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 3:49/q19/p2 The divine name al-Khāliq belongs only to God. — Rāzī reports his school's appeal to 13:16 and 35:3, while he rejects treating 35:3 alone as decisive because its negation includes providing from heaven. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا)]
- 3:49/q19/p3 Al-Khāliq is not literally a divine name if it implies conjectural measuring. — Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Baṣrī advances this objection; Rāzī's school answers that measuring can mean knowledge without conjecture. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو عبد الله البصري); RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا)-]

## 3:49/q20 Was the bird miracle requested by challengers?  [أَخْلُقُ لَكُم مِّنَ ٱلطِّينِ · reports]
turns on: Public demand versus an offered sign at school.
- 3:49/q20/p1 The Israelites challenged Jesus to make a bat. — Reports say they called other miracles magic, selected the hardest bird, then still called him a sorcerer. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,محمد بن إسحاق); IBNABIHATIM(ابن جريج); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك عن ابن عباس,المعظم)]
- 3:49/q20/p2 He offered the sign himself while a schoolboy; it was not demanded. — Ibn Isḥāq says the boys doubted his offer and spread the news to their teacher. Abū Ḥayyān finds no demand apparent from the verse. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن إسحاق); ALUSI-FULL(ابن إسحق); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)+]

## 3:49/q21 When did Jesus first animate the clay bird?  [أَخْلُقُ لَكُم مِّنَ ٱلطِّينِ · reports]
turns on: Schoolboy story versus challenge upon claiming prophethood.
- 3:49/q21/p1 As a boy in a scribal school. — Ibn Isḥāq says the other boys witnessed it, publicized it, and Mary later fled. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); DURR-FULL(ابن إسحاق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن إسحق,بعضهم)]
- 3:49/q21/p2 After he claimed prophethood, as a challenge from the Israelites. — Rāzī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī report the request for a bat. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(المعظم)]

## 3:49/q22 What other grammatical scope and ordering details are noted in the miracle clauses?  [أَخْلُقُ وَأُبْرِئُ وَأُحْىِ وَأُنَبِّئُكُم · rhetoric]
- 3:49/q22/p1 The coordinated healing and revival clauses remain within the scope of annī and its predicates. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī state their syntactic dependence. [4 notes: THALABI; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 3:49/q22/p2 The imperfect verbs portray repeated acts and are ordered from the greatest creative sign through healing, revival, and disclosure. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn emphasize recurring action; Abū Ḥayyān identifies creation as the foremost miracle. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 3:49/q23 Why does Jesus say 'for you'?  [لَكُم · rhetoric]
- 3:49/q23/p1 The shaping serves the audience's benefit by inviting faith and ending denial. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says it qualifies his limited shaping; al-Samīn calls the lām purposive, and al-Bursawī specifies faith as the purpose. ## 3:49/q24 What is the clay and what does 'from clay' contribute?  [مِّنَ ٱلطِّينِ · meaning]

- 3:49/q24/p1 It is ordinary clay, water and earth that can receive a shape; min marks its starting material. — Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī cite sealing a document or coating a house; al-Ḥarālī describes matured water and earth. ## 3:49/q25 What does the clay-bird miracle show beyond Jesus's truthfulness?  [مِّنَ ٱلطِّينِ فَيَكُونُ طَيْرًۢا · interpretation]
turns on: The notes give distinct emphases on divine life, resurrection, Adam, and Jesus's birth.
- 3:49/q25/p1 It displays God's power to create life and resurrect whatever He wills. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits Ibn Isḥāq; al-Biqāʿī says power over some dead proves power over all and compares Adam. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN; JISHUMI(أبو هاشم)]
- 3:49/q25/p2 It parallels Jesus's own unusual birth, which should not suggest his divinity. — Al-Ālūsī links it to the fatherless birth; al-Biqāʿī says Adam's dust creation is still more astonishing. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 3:49/q26 What minor lexical and grammatical details accompany the bird narrative?  [مِّنَ ٱلطِّينِ فَيَكُونُ طَيْرًۢا · grammar]
- 3:49/q26/p1 The result becomes a living bird, while the clay term has related Arabic forms and names. — Abū Ḥayyān notes the transmitter nicknamed Muṭīn; al-Samīn says ilā Banī Isrāʾīl can depend on rasūl or be its omitted adjective. ## 3:49/q27 How does the comparison 'like the form' work syntactically?  [كَهَيْـَٔةِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether kāf is a noun object, modifies an omitted object, or modifies an omitted verbal noun.
- 3:49/q27/p1 Kāf or its phrase modifies an omitted object: Jesus makes something shaped like a bird. — Most grammarians and al-Samīn favor this; Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the object from 'I create'. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو الحسن والجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو الحسن,الجمهور); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 3:49/q27/p2 Kāf itself is a noun meaning 'likeness' and direct object. — Al-Akhfash and Abū al-Ḥasan permit this; al-Samīn says other grammarians restrict a noun-like kāf to necessity. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو الحسن والجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(أبو الحسن)]
- 3:49/q27/p3 Kāf modifies an omitted verbal noun: I create a creation like a bird's form. — Abū ʿAlī proposes this; al-Samīn objects that the challenge concerns the created result, unless the verbal noun means its object. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي); SAMIN-DURR(الواحدي); SAMIN-DURR-]

## 3:49/q28 What does hayʾa mean here?  [كَهَيْـَٔةِ · meaning]

- 3:49/q55/p3 Specific personal facts stand for all kinds of unseen information. — Al-Ālūsī reports this extension; he also says nourishment and reserves command attention and are easily checked. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 3:49/q56 How does mā work in 'what you eat and what you store'?  [بِمَا تَأْكُلُونَ وَمَا تَدَّخِرُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Relative noun, verbal-noun particle, or indefinite noun described by a clause.
- 3:49/q56/p1 Both instances are relative pronouns: the things eaten and stored. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Ṭabrisī and Abū Ḥayyān prefer this; the omitted resumptive pronouns are understood. [9 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزهري); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; TABRISI+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 3:49/q56/p2 Both can be verbal-noun particles: your eating and storing. — Al-Naḥḥās, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Samīn allow this; most grammarians say this option requires no resumptive pronoun. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزهري); TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 3:49/q56/p3 Both can be indefinite nouns with following adjectival clauses. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī allow this, with an omitted resumptive pronoun. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 3:49/q57 What does 'you store' mean?  [تَدَّخِرُونَ · meaning]
- 3:49/q57/p1 You hide or retain something for a later need, especially food. — Muqātil glosses next-day savings; Jishumī gives future need; al-Ḥarālī distinguishes private storing from ihtikār for trade profit. Al-Samīn cites poetry where dhukhr denotes the thing stored. [21 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA(بعض بني أسد,بعض بني عقيل,بعضهم); TAB-FULL; THALABI(الكسائي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور,الزمخشري وابن عطية); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL]

## 3:49/q58 How is taddakhirūn formed, and how is it read?  [تَدَّخِرُونَ · readings]
turns on: Assimilated dāl, simple dhāl, doubled dhāl, and unassimilated dhāl-dāl.
- 3:49/q58/p1 The common taddakhirūn has doubled dāl from iftiʿāl of dhakhara. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Zajjāj, al-Khalīl, Sībawayh and later grammarians explain voicing t to d beside voiced dhāl and assimilation. Al-Naḥḥās prefers this to al-Farrāʾ's reverse account; parallels include izdajara, iḍṭarra, iṣṭabara and Zuhayr's ẓ/ṭ line. [32 notes: FARRA(بعض بني أسد,بعض بني عقيل,بعضهم); TAB-FULL(زهير); ZAJJAJ(الخليل,بعض النحويين); NAHHAS(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الخليل); JISHUMI(العامة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو جعفر بن القعقاع,الجمهور,زهير); TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,الجمهور); DURR-FULL(عاصم بن أبي النجود); ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; NAHHAS(الخليل وسيبويه)+]
- 3:49/q58/p2 A light tadhkharūn uses the simple verb dhakhara, without doubling. — Al-Farrāʾ reports it; Mujāhid, al-Zuhrī, Ayyūb and Abū al-Sammāl are named. Al-Zajjāj answers an objection of ambiguity. [15 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(مجاهد والزهري وأيوب السختياني); THALABI(الكسائي,مجاهد وأيوب السختياني); JISHUMI(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزهري ومجاهد وأيوب السختياني وأبو السمال); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد والزهري والسختياني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); SAMIN-DURR(الزهري ومجاهد وأبو السمال وأيوب السختياني); ALUSI-FULL]
- 3:49/q58/p3 Another assimilation yields doubled dhāl or tadhakhirūn. — Al-Farrāʾ describes speakers who let dhāl dominate; al-Zajjāj reports dāl/dhāl alternatives; Abū Ḥayyān allows this though favoring the majority. [4 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 3:49/q58/p4 A less assimilated tadhdakhirūn keeps dhāl before dāl. — Abū Shuʿayb al-Sūsī is cited; al-Samīn calls it permissible but says assimilation is more eloquent. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو شعيب السوسي); SAMIN-DURR(السوسي)]
- 3:49/q58/p5 One variant replaces second-person tāʾ with third-person yāʾ. — Jishumī attributes it to Mujāhid; the ordinary reading addresses 'you'. [2 notes: JISHUMI(العامة,مجاهد)]

## 3:49/q59 Why mention the house and what does 'in your houses' attach to?  [فِى بُيُوتِكُمْ · grammar]
- 3:49/q59/p1 It attaches to 'you store' and locates what they hid privately. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī attach it to the latter verb, with no competing governance; al-Biqāʿī says a house is the most concealing personal place. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL]

## 3:49/q60 How is buyūtikum vocalized?  [بُيُوتِكُمْ · readings]
turns on: Ḍamma versus kasra on bāʾ.
- 3:49/q60/p1 The base reading has ḍamma on bāʾ, buyūtikum. — Qirāʾāt ER calls this canonical base. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
- 3:49/q60/p2 A dialectal variant has kasra, biyūtikum. — Qirāʾāt ER says the vowel shift leaves meaning unchanged. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 3:49/q61 Who speaks the closing statement?  [إِنَّ فِى ذَٰلِكَ لَءَايَةًۭ · rhetoric]
turns on: Jesus's continuous speech versus God's independent rebuke.
- 3:49/q61/p1 It continues Jesus's speech to the Israelites. — Abū Ḥayyān prefers this because the clause is enclosed by Jesus's words; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī allow it. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 3:49/q61/p2 It is God's independent statement, reporting the proof and rebuking deniers. — Abū Ḥayyān reports the rebuke analysis; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī also allow God as speaker. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 3:49/q62 How is the hamza in the closing inna read?  [إِنَّ · readings]
- 3:49/q62/p1 It has kasra, opening an independent assertion. — Abū Mālik specifies kasra and adds an unexplained 'if not' phrase. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو مالك)]

## 3:49/q63 What does 'that' refer to in the closing statement?  [ذَٰلِكَ · referent]
- 3:49/q63/p1 All the preceding miracles, taken collectively as one set of signs. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn list bird-making, healing, revival and disclosure; Abū Mālik glosses 'that' as 'this'. [10 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو مالك); THALABI(الزهري وأبو جعفر); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 3:49/q64 How does 'if you are believers' qualify the sign?  [إِن كُنتُم مُّؤْمِنِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Future believers benefit, or existing believers are exhorted to sincerity and action.
- 3:49/q64/p1 The sign benefits those who believe or are receptive; disbelief blocks benefit, not its status as a sign. — Al-Ṭabrisī says knowledge of God as sender precedes recognizing the messenger; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the signs remain signs either way. Al-Samīn supplies an omitted result: they benefit and reflect. [17 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير,محمد بن إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(جميع القراء); STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL]
- 3:49/q64/p2 It exhorts people who already claim to believe to act as true believers. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares an exhortation 'if you are truly a man'; Abū Ḥayyān compares a son told to obey; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls for sincerity. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 3:49/q65 What does 'believers' mean in the final condition?  [مُّؤْمِنِينَ · meaning]
- 3:49/q65/p1 Those who affirm the truth of God's messenger and accept his proof. — Saʿīd b. Jubayr glosses them as truth-affirmers; Muqātil says assent to Jesus's messengership. [4 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); JISHUMI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 3:49/q66 How do inward readings understand Jesus's signs?  [* · inward]
turns on: Literal acts versus spiritual education and regeneration.
- 3:49/q66/p1 The signs describe spiritual training: clay is an unformed soul, the bird its aspiration, breath divine knowledge, and cures purification. — Al-Qāshānī addresses prepared descendants of Jacob, reads blind as veiled, leprous as stained by vice and desire, and resurrection as knowledge. Asad reads a destiny shaped from humble lives and faith, with spiritual healing and reviving. [13 notes: QASHANI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 3:49/q66/p2 A person effaced in self and sustained by lordly attributes gives life only through God. — Al-Sulamī reports this obscure gloss and immediately stresses that miracles occur by God's leave through bodily instruments. [2 notes: SULAMI(قيل)]

## 3:49/q67 What do adjacent notes say God taught Jesus as Book and wisdom?  [* · links]
turns on: Writing, another scripture, or broader knowledge and sound action.
- 3:49/q67/p1 Book may mean handwriting; a report assigns Jesus nine of ten parts of writing. — Ibn Jurayj gives writing by hand; Jishumī reports the numerical division without a named authority. [2 notes: JISHUMI(ابن جريج)]
- 3:49/q67/p2 Book may be another scripture besides Torah and Gospel, such as Psalms. — Abū ʿAlī prefers this as closer to the wording and prophethood. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي)+]
- 3:49/q67/p3 Wisdom is knowledge or soundness of speech and action, including prophetic knowledge. — Jishumī reports both glosses and says Jesus knew Torah, Gospel and wisdom. [2 notes: JISHUMI]

## 3:49/q68 How does this verse join the account of Mary and Jesus?  [* · links]
- 3:49/q68/p1 Mary's birth story pauses; the commentary turns to Jesus's mission, with the remainder of Mary's story in Sūrat Maryam. — Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī give this narrative division. [4 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; QUSHAYRI; TABATABAI]
- 3:49/q68/p2 The angelic promise was certain and fulfilled in Jesus's whole mission. — Elmalılı cites God's unbroken promise, the angels' truth, minhu and 'Be'; Jesus's life expressed the announced identity and call. [3 notes: ELMALILI]

## 3:49/q69 Why did Jesus's signs match medicine, and how do other prophets' signs compare?  [* · interpretation]
- 3:49/q69/p1 God challenged each audience in its leading skill: Jesus's medicine, Moses's magic, Muḥammad's eloquence. — Jishumī says a miracle is conclusive when it defeats experts in their own field; Ibn Kathīr adds the Qur'an's inimitability before humans and jinn. [8 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(كثير من العلماء); ALUSI-FULL]
- 3:49/q69/p2 Moses's confrontation of Pharaoh and Jesus's Israelite challenge used visible signs before or apart from a new scripture. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī notes Moses's tablets came after Egypt, so the staff and hand answered Pharaoh; Jesus likewise came with signs. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 3:49/q70 How does the verse answer later claims about Jesus?  [* · theology]
- 3:49/q70/p1 His miracles and teaching point to worship of God alone, opposing a claim that Jesus or Mary is divine. — Elmalılı says the whole mission called people to fear and obey their shared Lord; Kur'an Yolu reads the address for Najrān and cites 5:110–118; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Najrān Christians argued from miracles. [5 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(أهل السير); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 3:49/q71 What additional signs and stories are attached to Jesus's mission?  [* · reports]
- 3:49/q71/p1 Reports add garments in requested colors and a royal bowl that did not diminish, gaining disciples. — Al-Thaʿlabī narrates both, with a disciple identifying divine origin in the cloth sign. [2 notes: THALABI(ابن عون)]
- 3:49/q71/p2 Reports also attribute revival and healing signs to others by God's leave. — Al-Biqāʿī cites 2:260, the Prophet restoring Qatāda's eye and Uways's cured baras, and a report of the Prophet calling a dead daughter. The reports have no grading there. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 3:49/q71/p3 A moral story tells of three men dying through murder and poisoned food over money. — Al-Durr gives the continuation without named attribution; Khalid al-Ḥadhdhāʾ separately recounts Jesus instructing messengers sent to raise the dead. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(خالد الحذاء)]

## 3:49/q72 What do surrounding reports say about the disciples and Jesus's fate?  [* · reports]
turns on: Etymology of ḥawāriyyūn and later accounts differ.
- 3:49/q72/p1 The disciples were called ḥawāriyyūn for laundering and whitening clothes. — Al-Riḍā reports the popular explanation and a link to ḥiwār bread. [1 notes: TABATABAI(الرضا عليه السلام)]
- 3:49/q72/p2 They were sincere and purified others from sin through exhortation. — Al-Riḍā favors this Imāmī interpretation and reports twelve disciples, Luke being most knowledgeable. [2 notes: TABATABAI(الرضا عليه السلام); TABATABAI(الرضا عليه السلام)+]
- 3:49/q72/p3 A report says Jews claimed to torture, kill and crucify Jesus, though God gave them no actual power over him. — Al-Ṣādiq says it only appeared so; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī glosses a related transformation into devils as affecting a wicked subgroup. [2 notes: TABATABAI(الصادق عليه السلام)]

## 3:49/q73 How can someone receive angelic communication without being a prophet?  [* · theology]
- 3:49/q73/p1 A muhaddath hears an angel with the heart while remaining distinct from a prophet or messenger. — Reports name ʿAlī and Salmān; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the hearing is real to the recipient, whose divinely granted knowledge is immediate and certain. He describes prophetic dreaming as a swoon-like perception. [6 notes: TABATABAI(أبو بصير,حمران بن أعين)]
- 3:49/q73/p2 Divine light and inward calm distinguish angelic speech from Satanic suggestion. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī links God's light to 6:122 and calm to remembrance at 13:28, while Satanic prompting causes agitation. [3 notes: TABATABAI]

## 3:49/q74 Must a recipient of angelic communication see an angel?  [* · theology]
turns on: Reports deny a requirement; proposed limits concern essence or lawgiving revelation.
- 3:49/q74/p1 Sight is not required, though an angel may appear in human form. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites Mary's encounter at 19:17–19 and Abraham's household at 11:69–73. [3 notes: TABATABAI]
- 3:49/q74/p2 Denied vision may mean seeing an angel's actual essence rather than its appearance. — A proposed reconciliation; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī observes the cited verses establish appearances only. [1 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم)]
- 3:49/q74/p3 Denied vision may apply specifically to legislative revelation. — Another proposal preserves the lawgiving role of prophets; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī considers it somewhat remote. [1 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم)]

## 3:49/q75 What broader stylistic points do sources draw from this passage?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether its cadence is conventional sajʿ, plus repetition, collective singulars, antithesis and verb shortening.
- 3:49/q75/p1 Shortening a verb can imply only part of an action. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī gives this rule in two discussions; he compares easier yafʿal for ordinary conception with harder yakhluq for Jesus's fatherless birth. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 3:49/q75/p2 Qur'anic cadence here is not ordinary sajʿ, despite surface resemblance. — Al-Bāqillānī argues familiar sajʿ could not have baffled hearers or supported the challenge; unbalanced cadence would be disorder, impossible of Qur'anic eloquence. [3 notes: BAQILLANI; BAQILLANI+]
- 3:49/q75/p3 The passage repeats key phrases, uses singular for a collective, and juxtaposes opposites. — Abū Ḥayyān lists repeated 'I came', bird, permission, Lord and mā; he notes collective singulars and antithesis between life and death and later lawful/forbidden. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 3:49/q75/p4 True synonyms within a single speaker's language are doubtful; variant forms may come from dialect contact. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ endorses this; Ibn Jinnī says rarer synonyms may be borrowed tribal forms. [2 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن جني)]

## 3:49/q76 What related grammatical examples do the notes cite outside this wording?  [* · grammar]
- 3:49/q76/p1 Fa-idhā in a related example is a place adverb or particle with an omitted predicate; a time adverb cannot predicate of a person. — Al-Burhān uses this as a syntactic illustration. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 3:49/q76/p2 A future circumstantial state is allowed when its future sense is clear. — Al-Samīn compares a falcon example and 39:73, though the normal circumstance is simultaneous. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 3:49/q77 How is this verse linked to other Qur'anic narratives?  [* · links]
- 3:49/q77/p1 The miracles recur in 5:110, with further Gospel or Qur'anic comparisons. — Study Quran notes 5:110 and says the clay bird is absent from the New Testament; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Matthew 17's Moses and Elijah scene. Al-Qushayrī says Muḥammad knows the prior account by revelation, not inference. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI]
- 3:49/q77/p2 Jesus's signs led toward one message: worship of God alone and the straight path. — Elmalılı treats all miracles as one call, linking the following verse. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 3:49/q78 Did Jesus actually perform the listed signs?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Actual acts versus a merely hypothetical offer.
- 3:49/q78/p1 The verses present accomplished acts, not hypothetical capabilities. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says hypothetical performance would require an 'if you asked' condition; Jishumī and Elmalılı assert miracles occurred. [4 notes: JISHUMI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(بعضهم)+]
- 3:49/q78/p2 The verse only states Jesus could perform them if requested. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports and rejects this limitation. [0 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
