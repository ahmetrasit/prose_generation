# 6:128: 37 questions

## 6:128/q01 What is the gathering mentioned here?  [يَحْشُرُهُمْ · meaning]
- 6:128/q01/p1 The gathering of creatures for judgment at the Resurrection. — Muqatil includes disbelieving humans, devils, and jinn; Mawardi, Wahidi, Baghawi, Elmalili, and others locate it at the resurrection assembly. Jishumi gives the lexical sense of hasḥr as gathering; Mujahid's gloss of death is distinct. [10 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ELMALILI; BURSEVI; JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(التبريزي,الزمخشري)]
- 6:128/q01/p2 The gathering is death. — Ibn Abi Hatim reports Mujahid's terse gloss. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد)]

## 6:128/q02 Who is included in 'He gathers them all'?  [جَمِيعًۭا · referent]
turns on: The scope of the pronoun and the force of 'all.'
- 6:128/q02/p1 Jinn and humans, including their allies, are gathered. — Muqatil specifies human disbelievers, devils and jinn. Ibn Atiyya includes the impure and those granted the Abode of Peace, citing jami'an. Ibn Kathir identifies the human allies by worship, refuge and obedience. Islahi connects the wrongdoers of 6:123–124 and their human and jinn tempters. [15 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(التبريزي,الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:128/q02/p2 All creatures, including beings beyond jinn and humans, are gathered. — Kashshaf and Razi extend the pronoun broadly; Biqai says every ally and enemy is included, and Qurtubi calls jami'an comprehensive. Tabarsi gives this as its own broad reading. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI]
- 6:128/q02/p3 Only the unbelievers and their misleading devils are meant. — Razi links the pronoun to the devils of 6:112; Abu Sulayman specifies the polytheists who disputed carrion; Tabarsi and Alusi report restricted readings. Kuranyolu focuses on polytheists and unbelievers. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان); TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:128/q02/p4 The pronoun refers only to the believers whose deeds were mentioned before. — Abu Hayyan rejects attaching yawm to the deeds of 6:127 because it would force this restrictive antecedent. [0 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 6:128/q03 How is 'He gathers them' read?  [يَحْشُرُهُمْ · readings]
turns on: Third-person yāʾ versus divine first-person plural nūn.
- 6:128/q03/p1 Yaḥshuruhum, 'He gathers them,' with yāʾ. — Hafs from Asim is named by Ibn Mujahid and others; Tabarsi and Alusi add Rawh from Yaqub. Al-Farisi links this to preceding third-person context; Samin says it refers to rabbihim. Ibn Atiyya deems both readings valid. [11 notes: IBNMUJAHID(حفص عن عاصم); FARISI-HUJJA(حفص عن عاصم); BAGHAWI-FULL(حفص); IBNATIYYA-FULL(حفص عن عاصم); TABRISI(حفص وروح); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(حفص عن عاصم); BAYDAWI-FULL(حفص عن عاصم وروح عن يعقوب); NASAFI-FULL(حفص); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(حفص); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(حفص عن عاصم وروح عن يعقوب)]
- 6:128/q03/p2 Naḥshuruhum, 'We gather them,' with nūn. — Other readers use nūn; al-Farisi cites 18:47 and 20:124. Tabarsi cites 20:124; Alusi hears a majestic change of person, and Qiraat-ER sees the shift to direct divine speech. [7 notes: IBNMUJAHID(حفص عن عاصم); FARISI-HUJJA(حفص عن عاصم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(حفص عن عاصم); TABRISI(حفص وروح); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(حفص); ALUSI-FULL(حفص عن عاصم وروح عن يعقوب); QIRAAT-ER]

## 6:128/q04 What governs the accusative 'day'?  [وَيَوْمَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the governing expression is omitted remembrance, speech, an earlier verb, or an unspoken dreadful event.
- 6:128/q04/p1 An implied speech verb governs it: on the day they are gathered, God says or it is said. — Al-Farisi, Nahhas, Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan parse the temporal phrase under saying; Abu Hayyan prefers it as retaining adverbial force and objects to a double omission. [12 notes: NAHHAS; FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:128/q04/p2 An omitted 'remember' governs it. — Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Razi, Baydawi, Samin and Alusi give udhkur as possible; Ibn Kathir makes it a command to Muhammad to recall the gathering as warning. Abu Hayyan objects that treating yawm as the object loses adverbial force. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-]
- 6:128/q04/p3 It depends on preceding wording, especially 'He is their guardian' in 6:127. — Ibn Atiyya and Samin allow preceding wali, coordinated with the earlier account of deeds. Kashshaf allows attachment to the earlier worship of jinn; Abu Hayyan rejects a deeds antecedent because it points only to believers. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 6:128/q04/p4 An omitted momentous event governs it: on that day occurred what is too terrible to describe. — Kashshaf and Razi evoke the terrible unsaid event. Abu Hayyan and the Shaykh object that al-Zamakhshari's solution requires omitting two clauses. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 6:128/q04/p5 It is coordinated with the gathering and address themselves. — Kashshaf and Razi allow the gathering and saying to supply the construction. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL]

## 6:128/q05 How does 'all together' function grammatically?  [جَمِيعًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Circumstantial accusative or emphatic expression.
- 6:128/q05/p1 It is a circumstantial accusative. — Nahhas and Qurtubi parse jami'an as a ḥāl; Qurtubi connects it to the gathering of all creation. [3 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(بعض النحويين)]
- 6:128/q05/p2 It is an emphatic expression. — Samin reports this alternative from some grammarians. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(بعض النحويين)]

## 6:128/q06 Whom does 'company of jinn' address?  [يَٰمَعْشَرَ ٱلْجِنِّ · referent]
turns on: Literal jinn devils versus their close human associates.
- 6:128/q06/p1 The devils or evil jinn who misled humans. — Muqatil, Baghawi, Kashshaf, Baydawi and many later authors identify the addressed jinn as devils. Biqai calls them concealed rebellious powers; Tabatabai says the context points to whispering devils. Islahi calls them Iblis's followers and recalls his defiance and threats in 17:62 and 7:16–17; Asad reports this as the majority view. [17 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(آخرون,بعض الفضلاء); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(most commentators); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI]
- 6:128/q06/p2 The address is to misguided humans closely associated with invisible evil beings. — Asad bases maʿshar on intimate consorting, invokes the half-truths of 6:112, and cites 6:130, where apostles are human; he prefers this reading. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES+]

## 6:128/q07 What does maʿshar mean here?  [يَٰمَعْشَرَ ٱلْجِنِّ · meaning]
turns on: Ordinary group or a complete multitude derived from ten.
- 6:128/q07/p1 A group or class held together by a common bond. — Ibn al-Jawzi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, Bursevi and Alusi give this sense and plural maʿāshir; Abu Hayyan and Samin cite the prophetic phrase about the prophets and poems by al-Afwah or al-Awdi. Asad emphasizes close associates or family from intimate consorting. [10 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأفوه); SAMIN-DURR(الأودي,النبي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 6:128/q07/p2 A full community or multitude, derived from ten as a complete number. — Bursevi reports the derivation; Alusi attributes a complete-community definition to Tabarsi. [2 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(الطبرسي)]

## 6:128/q08 What implicit speech introduces the address, and who speaks?  [يَٰمَعْشَرَ ٱلْجِنِّ · grammar]
turns on: Direct divine speech versus passive 'it is said.'
- 6:128/q08/p1 God says the address after gathering them. — Razi supplies 'then God says' and treats it as rebuke; Ibn Atiyya and Alusi supply a saying verb. Samin notes the vocative is accusative under omitted yaqūl or qulnā; Biqai allows God to address through troops He wills. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 6:128/q08/p2 It is said to them, with a passive speech verb. — Al-Zajjaj takes direct divine speech to unbelievers as unlikely in view of 2:174; Abu Hayyan and Samin transmit this, Samin citing 3:77. Samin notes maʿshar would then be nominative as deputy subject. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج، الشيخ)]

## 6:128/q09 Why is the jinn assembly addressed publicly?  [يَٰمَعْشَرَ ٱلْجِنِّ · rhetoric]
- 6:128/q09/p1 The call reproaches and exposes the misleaders before all creatures. — Razi says they must admit guilt after defiance; Qurtubi calls it a public reproach of leaders and followers; Abu Hayyan says it brings notoriety; Biqai stresses humiliation when the devils cannot defend themselves. Alusi observes maʿshar suits their cooperative seduction. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 6:128/q09/p2 The paired wording exploits what is unsaid about jinn and humans. — Biqai says hidden jinn imply visible humans, while sociable humans imply eerie and averse jinn. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 6:128/q10 What does 'you have taken many from humans' mean?  [قَدِ ٱسْتَكْثَرْتُم مِّنَ ٱلْإِنسِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether it emphasizes misguidance, followers, or the resulting number of Hell's people.
- 6:128/q10/p1 The jinn misled many humans and made them their followers. — Muqatil, al-Farra, Qatada, Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Zajjaj, Wahidi and others give this core gloss. Kashshaf, Nasafi, Baydawi and Abu Hayyan compare amassing soldiers or partisans. Razi and Tabatabai rule out literal creation or physical aggregation; God creates and gathers humans. Ibn Kathir cites 36:60–62, and Study Quran cites whispering in 6:112 and 114:4–6 plus human response in 14:22. Islahi recalls Iblis's threats. [40 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس، الحسن، قتادة، مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس والحسن وقتادة,ابن عباس والفراء,غيره,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد والحسن وقتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد والزجاج); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:128/q10/p2 The phrase points especially to the numerous people of Hell produced by the jinn's influence. — Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Durr transmit al-Hasan's gloss that God made Hell's people numerous on Judgment Day. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); DURR-FULL(الحسن)]

## 6:128/q11 How is 'taken many from humans' construed?  [قَدِ ٱسْتَكْثَرْتُم مِّنَ ٱلْإِنسِ · grammar]
turns on: Misleading action implied versus abundance of followers or enjoyment.
- 6:128/q11/p1 It can mean amassing human followers, as a commander amasses troops. — Kashshaf, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, and Alusi use the troop or partisan analogy. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:128/q11/p2 Istakthara min means doing much of an action, with misguidance understood. — Tabatabai gives the lexical construction and rejects a physical gathering. Alusi reports an ellipsis for leading astray; Razi says jinn cannot create humans. [3 notes: TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد والزجاج); RAZI-FULL]
- 6:128/q11/p3 The clause can mean taking much enjoyment from humans. — Qurtubi analyzes an annexed verbal noun with its object elided and reads the later mutual-enjoyment statement as confirmation. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 6:128/q12 Who are the jinn's human allies?  [أَوْلِيَآؤُهُم مِّنَ ٱلْإِنسِ · referent]
- 6:128/q12/p1 Humans who obeyed, followed, or were misled by the jinn. — Wahidi, Baghawi, Kashshaf, Ibn al-Jawzi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Biqai and others identify the allies as obedient or misled people. Abu Hayyan specifies human unbelievers; Elmalili renders them henchmen. Ibn Kathir adds worship, refuge and mutual deceptive inspiration. [15 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 6:128/q12/p2 They are the polytheists and devils involved in the prior carrion dispute. — Abu Sulayman links them with the argument about carrion; Razi links the devils to 6:112. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان); RAZI-FULL]

## 6:128/q13 How does 'from humans' attach to 'their allies'?  [أَوْلِيَآؤُهُم مِّنَ ٱلْإِنسِ · grammar]
turns on: Circumstantial qualifier versus identification of kind.
- 6:128/q13/p1 It is a circumstantial qualifier: their allies were human. — Samin reports this; Bursevi holds that min al-ins modifies awliyāʾuhum as ḥāl. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]
- 6:128/q13/p2 It specifies the human kind among allies who include jinn as well. — Samin offers specification of kind; Alusi allows identification of kind or an omitted descriptive phrase. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 6:128/q14 What does istimtāʿ mean as a word?  [ٱسْتَمْتَعَ بَعْضُنَا بِبَعْضٍۢ · meaning]
- 6:128/q14/p1 Taking a benefit from something; matāʿ is what one uses or profits from. — Ibn Qutayba says each side took a share. Jishumi illustrates the root with divorce gift when no dowry or intercourse occurred, pilgrimage mutʿa, temporary marriage, 4:24, worldly goods, and related amtaʿtu phrases. He reports ʿUmar's ban and his own view that marriage mutʿa and a form of pilgrimage mutʿa were abrogated; al-Hadi agrees on marriage. [9 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; JISHUMI(عمر)]

## 6:128/q15 What mutual enjoyment do the humans confess?  [ٱسْتَمْتَعَ بَعْضُنَا بِبَعْضٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: The nature and participants of the reciprocal benefit.
- 6:128/q15/p1 Humans gained desires, pleasure, or means to sin; jinn gained obedience, influence, and help in seduction. — Zajjaj and Wahidi prefer this because the verse speaks of many human followers, whereas valley refuge was rare. Hasan says jinn commanded and humans acted; Muhammad ibn Kaʿb says their worldly association. Others specify fornication, wine, magic, soothsaying, lies, fraud, offerings, vows, and corrupted rules. Tabatabai and Elmalili stress voluntary reciprocity; Abu Hayyan treats examples as nonexclusive. [48 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن,محمد بن كعب); NAHHAS(قيل); THALABI(محمد بن كعب وعبد العزيز بن يحيى); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس عن عطاء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(محمد بن كعب); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(محمد بن كعب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطاء عن ابن عباس، محمد بن كعب، الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن,محمد بن كعب); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن,محمد بن كعب); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(محمد بن كعب); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; NAHHAS+; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج)+; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج)+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 6:128/q15/p2 Travelers sought a valley jinn's protection; humans felt safe, and jinn gained prestige from their dependence. — Muqatil, Farra, al-Kalbi and many others describe fearful travelers or hunters calling on the valley chief, citing 72:6. Jinn boasted of ruling humans and jinn. Study Quran calls the assurance false and the jinn's attributed power unreal. Zajjaj objects that few people followed this custom, so it cannot be the main sense. [28 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(الكلبي); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن، ابن جريج); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن وابن جريج والكلبي وعكرمة); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن وابن جريج والزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس، مقاتل، الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الحسن وعكرمة والكلبي وابن جريج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جريج); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن وابن جريج والزجاج وغيرهم); BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ZAJJAJ-; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج)-; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج)-]
- 6:128/q15/p3 Jinn supplied rumors, magic, soothsaying, and charms; humans enjoyed these practices while jinn enjoyed enticing them. — Thaʿlabi, Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi and Qurtubi give this variant of the sinful mutual-benefit account. Islahi expands it to poetic inspiration, travel, settlement, war, and claims about the Prophet's jinn companions. [7 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI]
- 6:128/q15/p4 It was companionship, mutual aid, support or belief in each other's useful power. — Mawardi reports companionship and mutual support or reciprocal beliefs of benefit; Muhammad ibn Kaʿb glosses worldly company. Ibn Atiyya allows other worldly benefits beyond refuge. Abu Hayyan cites beliefs about jinn occupying land, afflicting unprotected people, riding hunted animals, eating bones, and using dung, while humans used charms and service. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن كعب); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكرماني,قوم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن كعب); DURR-FULL(محمد بن كعب)]
- 6:128/q15/p5 The members of each species enjoyed others of their own species, with no necessary cross-species exchange. — Mawardi, Tabarsi, Razi and Abu Hayyan report this intra-species reading; al-Balkhi restricts enjoyment to humans in Tabarsi. Abu Hayyan prefers mutual benefit across species. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(البلخي); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 6:128/q15/p6 The enjoyment belonged only to humans who benefited from jinn. — Al-Balkhi's version in Alusi allows one-sided human benefit. Qurtubi rejects any reading that denies reciprocal enjoyment. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(البلخي); TABRISI(البلخي); QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 6:128/q15/p7 Inwardly, psychic powers and bodily faculties benefited through a shared form. — Qashani reads the jinn as lower-self powers, with physical senses and limbs or human forms drawn by worldly trinkets and pleasures. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 6:128/q16 How does 'some of us by others' work grammatically?  [ٱسْتَمْتَعَ بَعْضُنَا بِبَعْضٍۢ · grammar]
- 6:128/q16/p1 The reciprocal wording supplies an understood pronoun, 'some of us by some of us.' — Nahhas notes the Arabic ellipsis and restores the reciprocal pronoun. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 6:128/q17 What is the point of the human allies' confession?  [وَقَالَ أَوْلِيَآؤُهُم مِّنَ ٱلْإِنسِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether confession is excuse, regret, shared guilt, or an interrupted plea.
- 6:128/q17/p1 They confess shared, voluntary guilt and regret after their worldly alliance. — Razi says jinn invited and humans accepted; Kashshaf, Baydawi, Nasafi and Alusi name obedience, desire and denial of resurrection. Elmalili says both share responsibility; Tabatabai says divine decree did not compel them. Kuranyolu says the admission is too late. [18 notes: RAZI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:128/q17/p2 They speak as an excuse, invoking their enjoyment or God's decree. — Ibn Atiyya calls it an excuse. Biqai says the mention of the term shifts responsibility to destiny but fails; Abu Hayyan reports the view that the limit of enjoyment does this. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قوم)]
- 6:128/q17/p3 The human speakers are quoted because the jinn tempters are dumbstruck. — Bursevi and Alusi give this reason for the absence of a jinn answer; Elmalili observes their silence. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 6:128/q17/p4 They start to plead for forgiveness, but God cuts them off with a final verdict. — Islahi says the shameful confession precedes an unfinished apology; God's immediate answer closes it. Elmalili says they step forward to excuse the silent jinn. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI]

## 6:128/q18 How is the speech and judgment narrated as past?  [وَقَالَ أَوْلِيَآؤُهُم مِّنَ ٱلْإِنسِ · rhetoric]
- 6:128/q18/p1 The past tense presents the future judgment as certain. — Ibn Atiyya calls this eloquent Qur'anic usage; Biqai says God's promise cannot fail; Kuranyolu describes the gathering as if already past. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:128/q18/p2 The human response presumes an omitted response from the jinn. — Biqai reconstructs it from the following sūra's dialogue at 7:38. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 6:128/q19 What is omitted before 'our Lord'?  [رَبَّنَا · grammar]
- 6:128/q19/p1 The vocative particle is omitted before rabbanā. — Samin al-Halabi notes the suppressed yāʾ. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 6:128/q20 What appointed term had they reached?  [وَبَلَغْنَآ أَجَلَنَا ٱلَّذِىٓ أَجَّلْتَ لَنَا · interpretation]
turns on: Death, resurrection, the limit of enjoyment or respite, or an acquired spiritual state.
- 6:128/q20/p1 Death, sometimes with the grave, is the appointed term. — Muqatil, Ibn Qutayba, al-Hasan, al-Suddi, Muhammad ibn Kaʿb and a majority in Abu Hayyan give this gloss. Qurtubi adds the grave and regret; Biqai sees their appeal to destiny. Wahidi Wajiz also offers a resurrection appearance. [21 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNABIHATIM(السدي,محمد بن كعب); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن، السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن والسدي وأكثر المفسرين); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; THALABI; TABRISI(الحسن والسدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن، السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,محمد بن كعب); BIQAI-FULL; QASHANI; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:128/q20/p2 Resurrection, gathering, or the Day of Judgment is the term. — Thaʿlabi combines death and resurrection; Mawardi, Baghawi, Tabarsi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Kashshaf, Baydawi, Nasafi, Bursevi, Elmalili, and others identify resurrection as the endpoint for requital. Alusi expressly prefers it. Elmalili links the appointed term to 6:2. [18 notes: THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي); TABRISI(قول غير مسمى); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); RAZI-FULL(قوم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; QASHANI; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 6:128/q20/p3 The term is the end of their mutual enjoyment, worldly respite, or period of freedom. — Ibn Atiyya reports the endpoint of benefit; Razi records release and empowerment; Abu Hayyan says the limit of enjoyment and an appeal to decree. Elmalili and Kuranyolu call it the end of their respite. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي); RAZI-FULL(آخرون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قوم); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:128/q20/p4 The term is the attained limit of their own deeds and sinful existence, not the end of lifespans. — Tabatabai reads the attained degree of being formed by chosen mutual enjoyment and unbelief. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 6:128/q21 What does ajal mean as a word?  [أَجَلَنَا · meaning]
- 6:128/q21/p1 It is the duration or appointed limit of a thing. — Jishumi contrasts ājil with the immediate or present and gives the lexical time sense. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 6:128/q22 Does reaching the term imply one fixed lifespan?  [أَجَلَنَا · theology]
turns on: Whether the confession disproves separate terms for natural death, killing, or resurrection.
- 6:128/q22/p1 Yes: everyone, including one murdered, dies at the appointed term. — Al-Jubba'i argues that otherwise a murdered person could not say 'we reached our term'; Razi reports this inference, and Abu Hayyan uses the admission against a doctrine of two terms. [3 notes: TABRISI(الجبائي); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 6:128/q22/p2 No: the verse does not rule out different terms. — Ali ibn Isa and other Baghdadi theologians allow death and resurrection terms or actual death versus a possible full lifespan. Tabarsi reports their rejection of al-Jubba'i's inference. [1 notes: TABRISI(علي بن عيسى وغيره من البغداديين)]

## 6:128/q23 How is 'our term which You appointed' read and parsed?  [وَبَلَغْنَآ أَجَلَنَا ٱلَّذِىٓ أَجَّلْتَ لَنَا · readings]
turns on: Singular ajal versus plural ājāl, with a singular relative.
- 6:128/q23/p1 A variant reads plural ājālanā with singular masculine alladhī. — Abu Ali treats the plural as generic or alladhī as standing for a feminine relative; Abu Hayyan instead calls it apposition to implied waqt, since it is not an agreeing adjective. Samin's Shaykh prefers the appositive analysis. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو علي); SAMIN-DURR(أبو علي); ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)+]
- 6:128/q23/p2 The majority read singular ajalanā, matching singular alladhī. — Samin explicitly states this reading and agreement. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 6:128/q24 What is the grammatical relation of 'which You appointed'?  [ٱلَّذِىٓ · grammar]
turns on: Generic relative versus apposition after an implied 'time.'
- 6:128/q24/p1 Alladhī is generic, allowing a masculine singular relative for a plural term. — Abu Ali gives this explanation of the plural variant; Samin and Alusi report it. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو علي); SAMIN-DURR(أبو علي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:128/q24/p2 It is appositive to an implied 'time which,' not an adjective of the plural. — Abu Hayyan and Samin's Shaykh prefer this because plural and singular forms do not agree for an adjectival construction. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)+]

## 6:128/q25 What does mathwā mean?  [مَثْوَىٰكُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Place of residence versus verbal noun of remaining.
- 6:128/q25/p1 A dwelling, settled abode, or final place of residence. — Zajjaj, Mawardi, Ibn Abbas, Jishumi, Baghawi and many others gloss the place noun. Mawardi and Jishumi cite poetic uses of thawā; Tabatabai derives it from staying and says the subsequent abiding reinforces permanence. Study Quran contrasts dār in 6:127. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي,ابن عباس,الزجاج); JISHUMI(الشاعر); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج وأبو علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:128/q25/p2 A verbal noun meaning the act of remaining or settled stay. — Abu Ali argues a place noun cannot govern the following ḥāl; poetry where maghār takes an ʿalā object supports event-noun force. Ibn Atiyya and Samin report this; Baydawi and Alusi allow both analyses. Wahidi notes Arabic temporal mā and deleted time nouns by analogy with 5:117. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج وأبو علي); TABRISI(أبو علي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو علي); SAMIN-DURR(الفارسي); ALUSI-FULL]

## 6:128/q26 How are 'the Fire is your abode' and 'abiding therein' parsed?  [قَالَ ٱلنَّارُ مَثْوَىٰكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: What governs the circumstantial accusative khālidīn.
- 6:128/q26/p1 The Fire is subject, your abode predicate, and khālidīn an accusative circumstance. — Nahhas and Zajjaj provide this parse; Qurtubi and Tabarsi agree. Razi notes the added circumstance bars an inference that an ordinary abode could be left by death. [7 notes: NAHHAS; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 6:128/q26/p2 Mathwā as a verbal noun governs khālidīn. — Abu Ali, al-Farisi, Tabarsi, Abu Hayyan and Samin say the event noun retains verbal force; Samin records al-Farisi's poetry evidence. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج وأبو علي); TABRISI(أبو علي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو علي); SAMIN-DURR(الفارسي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:128/q26/p3 If mathwā is a place noun, an implied verb of dwelling governs khālidīn. — Abu Hayyan supplies 'they remain'; Samin gives yathwūn, and Alusi offers tabawwaʾūn. Baydawi alternatively invokes a possessive analysis; Samin rejects Abu al-Baqa's claim that idāfa meaning itself governs the ḥāl. [7 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]

## 6:128/q27 Whose home is the Fire, and what follows from the verdict?  [قَالَ ٱلنَّارُ مَثْوَىٰكُمْ · interpretation]
- 6:128/q27/p1 It is the common, lasting home of the disbelieving jinn and their human allies. — Muqatil, Tabarsi, Ibn Kathir and Biqai apply the verdict to both. Elmalili says the shared confession merits a shared judgment; Kuranyolu says the belated admission cannot avert it. Biqai adds that a settled intention to persist in disbelief merits endless recompense. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:128/q27/p2 The Fire signifies inward deprivation of pleasures and painful experience. — Qashani's inward reading takes the punishment in terms of acquired soul dispositions. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 6:128/q28 To what does the exception attach?  [إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · grammar]
turns on: The Fire-and-abiding clause versus the earlier appointed-term clause or a disconnected punishment clause.
- 6:128/q28/p1 It is disconnected and concerns extra kinds or degrees of punishment. — Nahhas separates the exception from the first clause; Farra takes illā as 'besides,' and Sibawayh as corrective 'but.' Al-Munir explains extremity beyond the ordinary name punishment, with Abu al-Tayyib's poetic idiom; Alusi calls this good. [16 notes: NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء,سيبويه); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء,غيره); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ,الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير,الزجاج); ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير)+]
- 6:128/q28/p2 It is an ironic exception that intensifies the threat without real reprieve. — Kashshaf likens it to an avenger pretending to offer relief; Abu Hayyan and Samin explain the analogy. Biqai says God's contingent wording dangles hope but He will not end punishment; Alusi calls the unchanged will an intensification. Samin rejects figurative force as unnecessary. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); SAMIN-DURR-]
- 6:128/q28/p3 It qualifies the immediately preceding judgment about abiding in the Fire. — Abu Hayyan and Samin call this the apparent or majority attachment; Bursevi reports mā as an event noun with an omitted preposition. Samin says connected people-exceptions may use mā for rational classes, as at 4:3. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور); BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
- 6:128/q28/p4 It qualifies the earlier statement 'we reached our appointed term.' — Abu Muslim exempts people destroyed before the term they might have had; Razi cites Noah's people, ʿĀd and Thamud and 6:6, but rejects it as against word order. Abu Hayyan and Samin object that it disrupts syntax and implies two terms; Samin adds that the pronoun should be 'You willed.' [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); SAMIN-DURR(أبو مسلم); RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو مسلم)-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 6:128/q28/p5 It is an ordinary literal exception. — Mawardi reports this as the majority; Samin prefers a genuine rather than figurative exception. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR+]

## 6:128/q29 Who, if anyone, is excepted from perpetual Fire?  [إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · theology]
turns on: Sinners later released, future believers never entering, or broader mercy.
- 6:128/q29/p1 Sinful monotheists may enter the Fire but are released by God's mercy and intercession. — Muqatil and reports of Ibn Masud, Anas and others invoke the exception. Hadith collections describe release of those with even a trace of faith and repeated prophetic intercession, leaving only those whom the Qur'an binds to perpetual punishment; Qatada explains that boundary. Tabarsi allows punishment or gracious pardon for sinful Muslims. [33 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قوم); QASHANI; MUSNAD-AHMAD(أبو هريرة,أنس بن مالك عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,رسول الله,نبي الله); IBNHIBBAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BUKHARI(أبو هريرة,أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,أنس,أنس عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة); IBNMAJAH(رسول الله); MUSLIM(رسول الله,قتادة); ABUDAWUD(أبو مجلز); SAMIN-DURR+]
- 6:128/q29/p2 People God knows will believe, repent, or accept Islam in this world are excluded. — Ibn Abbas and Ata make mā mean man; Ata mentions believers before and after the conquest. Ibn Atiyya prefers a present address to the Prophet and community, supported by the Wise/Knowing close; Abu Hayyan calls that good. Bursevi reports repentance. Samin and Alusi raise objections from time mismatch, uncommon rational mā, and the future scene; Islahi rejects the Arabic of mā-as-man. [20 notes: THALABI(عطاء); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(عطاء); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قوم); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); BURSEVI(صاحب التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; ALUSI-FULL-; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some exegetes)-]
- 6:128/q29/p3 No person can be assigned a final fate by human judgment; God alone knows the exception. — Ibn Abbas cautions against pronouncing any creature destined to Paradise or Hell. Mawardi adds that amount and duration remain with God's will; Qurtubi construes the caution as concerning people still alive who may believe. Ibn Atiyya rejects an open final outcome for unbelievers as against consensus. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري عن ابن عباس)-]
- 6:128/q29/p4 God may spare even those threatened here through mercy; some theologians infer Hell's punishment is finite. — Asad cites 6:12, 11:107 and prophetic reports and distinguishes Paradise's endless bliss. Kuranyolu reports possible finite Hell or selected unbelievers' release, but prefers divine freedom rather than those implications. Islahi rejects making 'eternal' merely a long period. [4 notes: ASAD-NOTES(some of the great Muslim theologians); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some exegetes)-; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 6:128/q29/p5 The exception is for people of Islam already foreknown to God, so the unbeliever threat was later made definite by 4:48 and 4:116. — Tabarsi transmits Ibn Abbas's account of the initially open warning and its later specification about shirk. [1 notes: TABRISI(ابن عباس)]
- 6:128/q29/p6 Angels are destined to Paradise, devils to Hell, while humans and jinn may reach either. — Bursevi transmits Ibn Abbas's classification of creatures, which limits who can be covered by the exception. [1 notes: BURSEVI(ابن عباس)]

## 6:128/q30 Which time or experience does 'except what God wills' exclude?  [إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Different proposed intervals and modes of torment.
- 6:128/q30/p1 The interval after resurrection and before entry into the Fire. — Zajjaj and Tabari exclude emergence, gathering and reckoning; Razi says Zajjaj prefers it because the verse names that Day. Abu Hayyan says 'abode in the Fire' need not refer only to future time. Alusi reports an objection that connected exceptions must match in time and a reply via disconnected exception and 44:56. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفراء); TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الطبري,قوم); SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج,الطبري); BURSEVI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج)+; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-]
- 6:128/q30/p2 Additional kinds or intensity of punishment beyond fire. — Zajjaj, Farra and others allow added punishment; some frame it as exemption from particular modes of fire-punishment while residence continues. Al-Munir explains the ultimate intensity and cites idiom and 11:107. [16 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفراء); TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء,غيره); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ,الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير,الزجاج)]
- 6:128/q30/p3 Intervals transferred from Fire to freezing cold. — Kashshaf and Razi transmit a report that the cold tears limbs apart and the damned beg to return; Nasafi, Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, Bursevi, Alusi and Elmalili repeat versions. Elmalili points to mā rather than man to favor time periods. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(غير مسمى); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 6:128/q30/p4 The time of earthly life before punishment. — Thaʿlabi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Qurtubi, al-Hasan in Abu Hayyan and Samin, and Ibn Kathir report this. Abu Hayyan and Samin object to the temporal distance from the immediately preceding future judgment. [4 notes: THALABI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن)-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 6:128/q30/p5 Forever: God's will does not actually end their punishment. — Al-Kalbi glosses what God wills as forever. Biqai says the conditional wording offers no prospect of ending punishment; Islahi maintains eternal punishment once judgment begins. [8 notes: THALABI(الكلبي); BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some exegetes)]
- 6:128/q30/p6 The time in graves or the barzakh before resurrection. — Ibn Kathir reports the barzakh view; Samin mentions time in graves and Zajjaj's combined grave-and-preentry interval. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم); SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج,الطبري)]
- 6:128/q30/p7 An apparent opening to Paradise before their final exclusion. — Bursevi reports a door opening and shutting on the damned; Alusi mentions a report of being near enough to see and smell Paradise, then being ordered away, and doubts a post-entry reprieve in light of 83:34. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:128/q30/p8 Periods outside the Fire while drinking boiling water. — Bursevi transmits this explanation from al-Jalalayn. [1 notes: BURSEVI(تفسير الجلالين)]
- 6:128/q30/p9 The excluded time is whatever respite God wills. — Qashani reads a possible respite inwardly, alongside rescue of those whose shirk was not entrenched. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 6:128/q31 What does the exception teach about divine will and eternal punishment?  [إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · theology]
turns on: Whether God retains discretionary power, whether mercy may end punishment, and how definite eternity is.
- 6:128/q31/p1 Eternal residence begins after a specified excluded interval, without weakening its permanence. — Zajjaj, Tabari and other time-interval interpreters exclude reckoning, graves, worldly life or transfer to cold. Qurtubi compares 11:106; Baghawi compares 11:107. [14 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الطبري); SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج,الزمخشري,الطبري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 6:128/q31/p2 God remains able to end punishment, though He does not will to do so. — Tabatabai says the exception preserves divine power; Biqai says God is bound by no obligation. Alusi prefers this against the claim that wisdom makes eternal punishment rationally obligatory; Kuranyolu prefers it because other verses rule out finite Hell or unbeliever exceptions. Islahi says only God's undisclosed will remains and warns against ascribing further inferences to the Qur'an. [7 notes: TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 6:128/q31/p3 God may end or lessen punishment through mercy. — Asad invokes the merciful exception and reports theologians who infer finite punishment for sinners. Abū Mijlaz says even intentional murder's Hell penalty may be pardoned. [5 notes: ASAD-NOTES(some of the great Muslim theologians); ABUDAWUD(أبو مجلز); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:128/q31/p4 Once the final punishment starts, there is no repentance, intercession or release for those sentenced eternally. — Islahi says the verdict closes apology and hope and treats its undisclosed exception as a limit on what people know. Bukhari and Muslim report that the Qur'an retains some eternally while monotheist sinners are released. [7 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some exegetes); BUKHARI(قتادة); MUSLIM(قتادة)]

## 6:128/q32 Why end with 'Wise, Knowing'?  [إِنَّ رَبَّكَ حَكِيمٌ عَلِيمٌۭ · interpretation]
- 6:128/q32/p1 God assigns recompense wisely and knows all deeds, states and deserts. — Muqatil, Zajjaj, Nahhas, Kashshaf, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Bursevi, Alusi and Elmalili explain the closing names through just measure of punishment and all-encompassing knowledge. Tabarsi allows specific knowledge of who merits reward or punishment. Qashani applies wisdom to acquired soul traits and temporary or endless punishment. Tabatabai says it explains the verdict to Muhammad. [15 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 6:128/q32/p2 The names explain divine foreknowledge of who will later believe and be excepted. — Wahidi and Baghawi cite decreed repentance, truthful faith and piety of heart; Ibn Atiyya makes the ending evidence for a present warning that excludes future believers. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 6:128/q32/p3 The divine name emphasizes majesty in a judgment beyond objection. — Biqai says the greatest name manifests majesty and guards the divine station. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 6:128/q33 How does this scene connect with nearby verses and the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Different contextual emphases.
- 6:128/q33/p1 It develops jinn and human leader-follower exchanges elsewhere. — Biqai compares later blame among followers at 7:38 and the weak followers' admission at 7:75; Ibn Kathir links Satan's many followers to 36:60–62. Qurtubi and Baghawi compare the exception in 11:106–107. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 6:128/q33/p2 It contrasts the promised Garden for guided people with warning of the Fire. — Razi describes promise followed by threat; Kuranyolu similarly moves from guided believers' Paradise to disbelievers' gathering. Biqai says the gathering day is a central theme veiled at the sūra's opening and disclosed in later passages. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 6:128/q33/p3 It explains how wrongdoers become guardians of one another without injustice. — Tabatabai relates it to earlier verses and stresses their free response to warning; Islahi links the wrongdoers and tempters of 6:123–124. [4 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 6:128/q33/p4 It warns humans against the jinn's enticing of evil, since both groups are paired in Fire. — Wahidi cites the people of meaning; Kuranyolu describes a lasting voluntary alliance across generations. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 6:128/q34 What is Qashani's inward reading of the verse?  [* · inward]
- 6:128/q34/p1 The jinn are lower-self powers that master senses, limbs or human forms and receive punishment matching acquired traits. — Qashani places the absolute unitive gathering against psychic powers and their bodily instruments. Worldly goods and delights induce their obedience; the appointed term may be death or ugly bodily resurrection, and the Fire is painful deprivation with possible respite or rescue where shirk is not ingrained. [7 notes: QASHANI]

## 6:128/q35 What separate reports illuminate the judgment, mercy, and intercession scene?  [* · reports]
turns on: Different reported events and their scope.
- 6:128/q35/p1 Repeated intercession releases believers from Hell; those bound by the Qur'an remain. — Anas's reports in Ahmad, Ibn Hibban, Bukhari, Ibn Majah and Muslim include God-set limits and faith as small as an atom or grain; Qatada identifies those eternally detained and connects the praised station to 17:79. Ibn Masud describes mercy after a divinely willed stay. [13 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(أنس بن مالك عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,رسول الله); IBNHIBBAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BUKHARI(أنس,أنس عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة); IBNMAJAH(رسول الله); MUSLIM(رسول الله,قتادة)]
- 6:128/q35/p2 People follow their worshiped objects, cross Hell's bridge, and some believers are rescued. — Abu Hurayra's accounts in Ahmad, Bukhari and Muslim describe the last entrant to Paradise; Abu Sa'id corrects the final gift to ten times the reported reward. One Ahmad report mentions 45:28. [11 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(أبو سعيد,أبو سعيد الخدري,أبو هريرة,رسول الله); BUKHARI(أبو هريرة); MUSLIM(رسول الله)]
- 6:128/q35/p3 Muhammad reserves his answered supplication for intercession on Judgment Day. — Ahmad, Bukhari and Muslim transmit versions through Abu Hurayra; Ka'b asks whether he heard it directly, and Abu Hurayra confirms. [7 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(رسول الله,نبي الله); BUKHARI(أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); MUSLIM(رسول الله)]
- 6:128/q35/p4 Believers are privately reckoned and forgiven, while unbelievers and hypocrites are exposed. — Ibn Hibban and Ibn Majah report this distinction with reference to 11:18. [2 notes: IBNHIBBAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNMAJAH(رسول الله)]
- 6:128/q35/p5 A prophetic dream details punishments for neglecting Qur'an and prayer, lying, adultery and usury, and depicts children born on fitra and possible pardon for mixed deeds. — Ibn Hibban and Bukhari report Samura's dream; the children include those of polytheists. [2 notes: IBNHIBBAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BUKHARI(سمرة بن جندب)]
- 6:128/q35/p6 Hell asks for more until filled, while Paradise receives new creation. — Abu Sa'id transmits this account twice in Ahmad, saying Paradise remains as long as God wills. [2 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(رسول الله)]
- 6:128/q35/p7 A believer who meets God affirming both testimonies is shielded from Hell. — Ibn Hibban relates the saying after an army's food multiplies. [1 notes: IBNHIBBAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 6:128/q35/p8 The Prophet warned women about Hell, urged charity, and explained menstruation and testimony rules. — Ahmad reports Abu Hurayra's narration involving Zaynab and Ibn Masud's family. [1 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(أبو هريرة)]
- 6:128/q35/p9 The Prophet contrasted the Muslim's lesser eating with the unbeliever's seven intestines. — Ibn Hisham reports the saying after Thumama accepted Islam; the report also describes his capture, kind treatment, release and pledge. [2 notes: IBNHISHAM(أبو هريرة,رسول الله)]
- 6:128/q35/p10 Worldly poverty may accompany a Hereafter granted to believers. — Ibn Majah reports Umar's recollection of the Prophet's contrast with Persian and Byzantine rulers. [1 notes: IBNMAJAH(عمر بن الخطاب)]

## 6:128/q36 What ancillary linguistic, historical, and recitation material is preserved with these notes?  [* · reports]
turns on: The distinct ancillary reports concern Arabic forms, Meccan history, prophetic biography, and recitation practice.
- 6:128/q36/p1 Ishmael's descendants and Jurhum successively held the Kaaba, while conflicts named Meccan sites. — Ibn Hisham gives Nabit then Mudad; Azraqi gives Ishmael's marriages, twelve sons, 130-year life, burial, Jurhum and Qatura settlement, kingship, fighting and site names Qayqaʿan, Ajyad, Fadih and al-Matabikh. Umar warns that profaning the sanctuary loses custody. [16 notes: IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); AZRAQI(ابن عباس,عمر بن الخطاب,محمد بن إسحاق)]
- 6:128/q36/p2 Arabic can preserve an original vowel pattern in lightened forms. — Sibawayh discusses shahida/laʿiba, niʿma/biʾsa, and ghuzī, citing al-Akhtal's line; these notes offer linguistic analogy without a direct gloss of a 6:128 word. [4 notes: SIBAWAYH(الأخطل)]
- 6:128/q36/p3 Hagar found Zamzam and Abraham with Ishmael built the House; later Jurhum lost it and Abd al-Muttalib rediscovered the well. — Azraqi reports Hagar's search as the origin of saʿy, Gabriel's opening of Zamzam, Jurhum settlement, Abraham's visits and the Maqam, varied accounts of the sakina, Jurhum's profanation, Khuzaʿa's conquest, and Zamzam's concealment and recovery. [14 notes: AZRAQI(ابن جريج,ابن عباس,بعض أهل العلم,رسول الله,سعيد بن جبير,عمرو بن الحارث بن مضاض,مجاهد)]
- 6:128/q36/p4 Salman's route to Medina and accounts of prophetic signs and miracles are narrated. — Ibn Hisham recounts Salman's enslavement and recognition of Medina, pre-revelation greetings from stones and trees, Muhammad and Ali's prayer with Abu Talib's response, the trench rock, and the Tabuk spring. [7 notes: IBNHISHAM(أهل العلم,ابن إسحاق,بعض أهل العلم,جابر بن عبد الله,سلمان)]
- 6:128/q36/p5 Correct recitation depends on oral training and natural performance, with examples of distinguished reciters. — Ibn al-Jazari reports conversions after Sibt al-Khayyat's recitation, names Badr al-Din and Ibrahim al-Hakari, cites al-Dani on practice, objects to affected mouth and voice manipulation, and laments a decline in the highest mastery. [6 notes: NASHR(أبو عمرو الداني)]
- 6:128/q36/p6 A hidden spiritual realization about the exception is reserved for experienced wayfarers. — Bursevi attributes this advice to Najm al-Din, without specifying its content; the source also prays for a sound-hearted end. [2 notes: BURSEVI(حضرة الشيخ نجم الدين)]
- 6:128/q36/p7 A Persian admonition says to wake before death ends the opportunity to act. — Bursevi quotes verses about a sleeper who did no work before time passed. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
