# 24:37: 27 questions

## 24:37/q01 How is the preceding verb of glorification read, and how does that affect “men”?   [رِجَالٌۭ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted active and passive vocalizations of the preceding verb govern the pause and the syntax of رجال.
- 24:37/q01/p1 Active: the men glorify God; “men” is the expressed subject. — Al-Tabari prefers it because the preceding report about the houses is complete. Al-Wahidi calls it the majority reading; Jishumi says the remaining readers have kasra. Ibn Kathir says the pause belongs after the subject. [11 notes: JISHUMI(الباقون); WAHIDI-BASIT; THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الفراء); TAB-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 24:37/q01/p2 Passive: God is glorified in the houses; “men” begins or supplies a new clause. — Al-Tha'labi, Razi, Ibn Kathir, and Nasafi place a pause at “evenings.” Ibn Kathir notes fath on the bāʾ. Sibawayh's and Nahshal's poetic example of the mourner for Yazid illustrates supplying a subject after an implied question. [16 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الشاعر); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الشاعر); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 24:37/q02 How is “men” connected grammatically with the preceding houses and time phrases?  [رِجَالٌۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the preceding verb is active or passive and where a pause falls; the notes also offer ellipsis and an independent nominal sentence.
- 24:37/q02/p1 It is the subject of the active verb, delayed after the house and time phrases. — Muqatil and al-Farra describe fronting and delay. Al-Alusi says the long description of the men would disrupt the order if placed earlier. He treats the repeated “in them” as emphasis and says fronting marks importance, not restriction; he disputes analyzing a pronoun as emphasis of explicit wording. [11 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; RAZI-FULL(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 24:37/q02/p2 After the passive verb, it is raised by an omitted active verb answering who glorifies. — Sibawayh and later grammarians cite the poetic “who weeps for Yazid? A suppliant.” Al-Samin notes disagreement over the construction; Abd al-Qahir says an implicit question normally receives an explicit verb. Al-Alusi permits ellipsis, while rejecting the different passive-agent-as-specification analysis. [13 notes: RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الشاعر); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ITQAN(عبد القاهر); WAHIDI-BASIT(الشاعر)]
- 24:37/q02/p3 It is the predicate of an omitted subject: those glorifying are men. — Abu Hayyan and al-Samin give this parse, with a pause at “evenings”; al-Alusi accepts it. Abu Hayyan also reports a disputed analogy to “ḍuribat Hind Zayd.” [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 24:37/q02/p4 “In houses” is the fronted predicate and “men” its subject. — Yahya offers a separate statement beginning after “evenings”; Qurtubi gives the following verb as circumstantial, without a pause. Ibn Ashur says this answers who receives the light of 24:35. Burhan records the independent construction. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 24:37/q03 Who are the “men” described here?  [رِجَالٌۭ · referent]

- 24:37/q03/p1 Working merchants and market people who leave business when prayer calls. — Ibn Umar says shopkeepers shut their shops and entered the mosque when the call sounded; Salim and Ibn Masud observed sellers doing likewise. Matar says one laid down a scale mid-transaction; another report adds a swordsmith dropping his hammer. Abu al-Darda says lawful trading for 300 dinars daily can coexist with mosque prayer. Ibn Abbas describes busy traders. Razi prefers this because only an actual trader can be undistracted by trade; al-Alusi appeals to the apparent wording. Al-Alusi uses the report to support the importing sense of trade. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); DURR-FULL(النبي ﷺ); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة)]

- 24:37/q03/p5 People of complete faith, including exemplary prophets, successors, saints and the Prophet's purified household. — Tabataba'i says the description is not universal to every believer; it identifies those guided by God's will in 24:35. [10 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,النصرآباذي,بعضهم,جعفر); QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI(علي عليه السلام)]

## 24:37/q04 Why does the verse say “men,” and does that include women?  [رِجَالٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether رجال is a gender-specific reference linked to mosque attendance or generic masculine usage.
- 24:37/q04/p1 Men are named because mosque congregation and Friday prayer are especially their obligation or usual practice; women's home prayer is better. — Tha'labi and Baghawi cite women's exemption; Qurtubi infers no share in mosque attendance. Biqai says women's attendance remains an established sunna but home prayer is better. Al-Alusi cites Umm Salama; Ibn Ashur says men predominate in mosques. [6 notes: THALABI(قائل); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 24:37/q04/p2 The masculine plural includes women through generic usage, or describes the usual practice. — Kuranyolu invokes 33:35, which praises worshipping men and women together. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 24:37/q04/p3 Men are specified because women do not engage in commerce or congregational gatherings. — This is al-Razi's rationale. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 24:37/q04/p4 The indefinite “men” magnifies their high aspiration and devotion. — Ibn Kathir mentions lofty intentions and 33:23; Islahi sees intensified praise. Al-Alusi says tanwin in every reading conveys magnificence and the following adjective reinforces it. Burhan explains repeating an explicit noun after intervening words and at a new verse opening. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL; BURHAN]

## 24:37/q05 What is the relation between “trade” and “sale” here?  [تِجَٰرَةٌۭ بَيْعٌ · meaning]
turns on: Whether تجارة is a general category, buying, imported commerce, or an ongoing occupation determines how بيع relates to it.
- 24:37/q05/p1 Trade is buying and selling for profit, while sale is a named instance of it. — Kashshaf calls it the trader's craft; Bursevi cites al-Mufradat's capital-for-profit definition and its note that the Arabic word uniquely has tāʾ followed by jīm. Wahidi prefers the broad sense over restricting trade to buying. Razi and Bursevi distinguish cash received in sale from goods acquired in purchase; Asad extends bayʿ to worldly gain. [15 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(المفردات); ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 24:37/q05/p2 Trade means buying; sale is the other half of commerce. — Muqatil, al-Waqidi and others infer the restriction from sale being named separately and cite 62:11. Al-Alusi calls buying the beginning of trade. [12 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI(الواقدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل، الواقدي); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الواقدي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الواقدي)]
- 24:37/q05/p3 Trade means importing or traveling to bring goods; sale is direct or local selling. — Al-Farra contrasts caravan commerce with selling by hand; al-Kalbi contrasts traveling merchants and resident sellers. Kashshaf notes itʿajara for bringing goods; al-Alusi cites the report of travel seeking bounty. [14 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA(التفسير); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(الفراء); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 24:37/q05/p4 Trade is sustained earning; sale is an individual transaction, including occasional selling outside a trader's profession. — Tabataba'i says both continuous and occasional distraction are negated. Ibn Ashur defines trade as acquisition for resale, whereas anyone can sell for a price. Biqai includes sales of possessions to meet need; bayʿ may mean acquiring or disposing. [3 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 24:37/q06 Why name sale separately and repeat the negation?  [تِجَٰرَةٌۭ وَلَا بَيْعٌ · rhetoric]
turns on: The explanation depends on whether sale is a subset of trade, a distinct kind of transaction, or the occasion of surer profit.
- 24:37/q06/p1 A profitable sale gives certain immediate gain and is the strongest commercial distraction. — Kashshaf, Razi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi and others contrast realized cash from selling with uncertain future gain from buying. Al-Alusi says the general negation might not clearly cover this case. Tabataba'i calls the explanation good. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 24:37/q06/p2 The two terms cover both a trader's continuing occupation and discrete or necessary sales. — Tabataba'i pairs both commercial rhythms with continuing remembrance and worship at set times. Biqai says someone may sell possessions for necessities; Ibn Ashur says sale can be done by anyone. [4 notes: TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 24:37/q06/p3 Sale is a specific instance after general trade, or the phrase intensifies and expands the negation. — Baydawi and Abu Hayyan give general-then-specific; Islahi calls it that rhetorical pattern. Al-Alusi says repeated lā reinforces denial and reports the reverse scope, profitable trade then all exchange. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 24:37/q07 Why does trade receive special mention as a possible distraction?  [تِجَٰرَةٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 24:37/q07/p1 It is the greatest or most widespread ordinary distraction from prayer and devotion. — Tha'labi's People of Meaning, Wahidi, Baghawi, Qurtubi and Bursevi make this point; Bursevi says no kind of trade can distract them. [5 notes: THALABI(أهل المعاني); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 24:37/q07/p2 The wording tests whether worldly gains and pleasures displace what lasts. — Ibn Kathir says they prefer God's lasting reward to perishable possessions. Kuranyolu says neglect of timely prayer or underpaid zakat is a failure of wealth's test; good servants balance world and afterlife. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 24:37/q09 What is “remembrance of God” here?  [ذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the following explicit mention of prayer repeats and specifies remembrance or shows remembrance has another sense.
- 24:37/q09/p1 The obligatory prayer, especially attending it when due. — Muqatil, Ibn Abbas, Ata, the two Muqatils, al-Suddi and others gloss it as the five prayers; Baghawi connects it to mosque attendance. Wahidi and Ibn Jawzi say the following clause then emphasizes keeping its time. [17 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,بعضهم); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(المقاتلان,عطاء); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وعطاء); RAZI-FULL(آخرون); QURTUBI-FULL(عطاء وابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 24:37/q10 Why is establishing prayer named after remembrance?  [ذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ وَإِقَامِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether ذكر الله is identified with prayer or distinguished as inward or verbal remembrance.
- 24:37/q10/p1 If remembrance means prayer, the repetition stresses performing it at its appointed time and fulfilling its requirements. — Wahidi, Baghawi and Ibn Jawzi name timing; Biqai says even minimally adequate performance counts and the wording stresses the requirements, without demanding highest perfection. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BIQAI-FULL]
- 24:37/q10/p2 Prayer is separate from remembrance, so naming both avoids redundancy. — Qurtubi argues explicitly for this; Tabataba'i calls remembrance inward knowledge opposite heedlessness and sees prayer as one outward duty. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 24:37/q10/p3 Prayer and zakat show worship at fixed times alongside continuous remembrance. — Tabataba'i pairs sustained and single sales with continuous inward remembrance and time-bound acts. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 24:37/q11 What does “establishing prayer” require, and which prayers does it include?  [إِقَامِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةِ · interpretation]
turns on: The scope of إقامة ranges from due time and proper form to congregational performance or the religion as a whole; Razi distinguishes obligatory from voluntary prayer.
- 24:37/q11/p1 Performing prescribed prayer at its appointed time, without delay. — Ibrahim, Tabari, Ibn Abbas, Baghawi and others focus on timely attendance; Baghawi says delayed prayer is not properly established. Muqatil ibn Hayyan adds attending and guarding God's trust. [13 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(إبراهيم); TAB-FULL(رجل نسي عوف اسمه); IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية,مقاتل بن حيان); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن أبي الحسن والضحاك,مقاتل بن حيان); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 24:37/q11/p2 Completing prayer according to its due form and conditions. — Al-Kalbi says complete it. Razi invokes all its due rights under 2:3; Ibn al-Shaykh requires pillars, conditions, sunnas and etiquettes, considering any omission a failure to establish. Ibn Jawzi joins completeness with due time. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان)]
- 24:37/q11/p3 The obligatory prayer in particular. — Abu al-Aliya names the obligation; Razi prefers obligatory prayer as the precise established legal term. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية); RAZI-FULL(قوم); RAZI-FULL+]
- 24:37/q11/p4 Both obligatory and voluntary prayer. — Razi reports Ibn Abbas's ḍuḥā example. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 24:37/q11/p5 Prayer in congregation. — Al-Suddi says this directly; Ibn Kathir reports his congregational gloss of remembrance. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي)]
- 24:37/q11/p6 Establishing the religion. — Zayd ibn Aslam gives this broader gloss. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(زيد بن أسلم)]

## 24:37/q12 How is the form “iqām” derived and why is its ending absent?  [إِقَامِ · grammar]
turns on: Grammarians differ on whether annexation licenses loss of a compensatory ending, whether it can disappear independently, and whether the cited poem proves the form.
- 24:37/q12/p1 It is an ifʿāl verbal noun from weak-root aqwām; sound change deletes the weak letter, compensatory tāʾ appears in iqāma, and annexation allows its omission. — Sibawayh compares istiʿāna and irāʾa; Farra compares ijāra, ijāba and ʿidda. Tabari, Zajjaj, Nahhas, Tha'labi and others describe vowel transfer, adjacent alifs and the governed noun substituting for tāʾ; al-Aʿsha's “ʿad al-amr” illustrates deletion in poetry. Tha'labi calls the ending hāʾ. Razi says grammarians agree; Ibn Ashur calls the form exceptional among weak-middle verbal nouns. [28 notes: SIBAWAYH; FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الفراء); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,الشاعر,الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج,الفراء); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 24:37/q12/p2 The shortened verbal noun can omit the compensatory tāʾ even without annexation. — Sibawayh notes irāʾ alongside irāʾa and iqām, and uses ikhtiyār without feminine tāʾ; he contrasts taʿziya and related forms where deletion is unavailable. Al-Alusi reports Sibawayh's permission and notes that Khalid ibn Kulthum reads the poetic ʿadā as the plural of ʿidwa, “sides,” weakening the annexation proof. Abu Hayyan says Basrans deny that annexation itself causes omission. [6 notes: SIBAWAYH; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء,خالد بن كلثوم); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء,سيبويه)]
- 24:37/q12/p3 Iqām is itself a verbal noun used in construct phrases. — Ibn Atiyya reports al-Rummani's analysis: Arabs do not say “aqamtuhu iqāman” independently. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الرماني)]
- 24:37/q12/p4 The final tāʾ is omitted for ease of pronunciation. — Tabataba'i gives this as the direct reason. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 24:37/q13 What does giving zakat mean here?  [وَإِيتَآءِ ٱلزَّكَوٰةِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether “giving” fixes الزكاة as a property payment or permits a broader sense of sincere obedience and righteous action.
- 24:37/q13/p1 Paying the obligatory alms from property when due. — Al-Hasan and many authors give this legal sense; Ibn Abbas is also reported saying believers do not withhold it. Razi argues the verb “give” points to property and rejects the broader gloss; Al-Alusi calls that gloss remote. Baydawi and Bursevi specify rightful recipients. [15 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI(الحسن); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); ASAD-NOTES; RAZI-FULL+]
- 24:37/q13/p2 Sincere obedience to God, extending the verse to believers without taxable wealth. — Ibn Abbas gives this gloss; Razi cites 19:55, 24:21, and 9:103 for broader usage. Wahidi prefers it since many believers are poor, especially the Suffa; Qurtubi gives the same poverty reason. Razi rejects it because of “giving,” and Al-Alusi calls it remote. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT+; RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)-]
- 24:37/q13/p3 Righteous works generally. — Baghawi reports this alternate interpretation. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل)]
- 24:37/q13/p4 Spiritual guidance and completion during subsistence. — Qashani pairs this inward zakat with prayer as witnessing in annihilation. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 24:37/q14 Why are prayer and zakat paired here?  [وَإِقَامِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةِ وَإِيتَآءِ ٱلزَّكَوٰةِ · interpretation]
- 24:37/q14/p1 They represent the central duties to God and to people, encompassing commanded good works. — Tabataba'i calls each a pillar, prayer duties to God and zakat duties to creation; Islahi says the pair encompasses the whole religion. [2 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 24:37/q14/p2 They purify spirit and body respectively. — Biqai calls zakat purification and growth of bodies, complementing prayer's purification of spirits. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 24:37/q14/p3 Zakat commonly accompanies prayer and shows their good deeds extend outside mosques. — Bursevi and Al-Alusi note that zakat is not performed in mosques; Al-Alusi says the mention widens the account of their deeds. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 24:37/q15 How does the fear clause attach grammatically, and what is “a day” syntactically?  [يَخَافُونَ يَوْمًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause modifies رجال, describes their state, or explains it, and whether يومًا functions as feared object or time adverb.
- 24:37/q15/p1 “They fear” is another adjective describing the men. — Abu Hayyan treats it like “trade does not distract them”; Nasafi and al-Samin also allow it. Ibn Ashur calls both clauses descriptions of the men; al-Alusi adds that their fear is not limited to the mosque. [5 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 24:37/q15/p2 “They fear” is a circumstantial clause from the object pronoun in “distracts them.” — Nasafi, al-Samin and al-Alusi give this parse. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 24:37/q15/p3 The fear clause stands independently as an explanation. — Al-Alusi offers this third parse. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 24:37/q15/p4 “A day” is the direct object of fearing, with terrors or punishment understood. — Al-Samin calls this the stronger parse and makes “turning” an adjective of the day. Bursevi and Ibn Ashur agree; al-Alusi rejects temporal-adverb analyses because they fear in this life. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 24:37/q15/p5 “A day” is a time adverb with an omitted object. — Al-Alusi reports the possibility but calls it remote, and rejects making it an adverb modifying the time of fear. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 24:37/q16 Why do these worshippers fear the day, and what is fear here?  [يَخَافُونَ يَوْمًۭا · interpretation]
- 24:37/q16/p1 They fear the Resurrection's terror despite worship, hoping for safety and aware of their shortcomings. — Razi says they know they have not worshipped God as deserved; Baydawi stresses fear during obedience. Jishumi says fear heightens worship; Ibn Ashur says it prompts action leading to success in 24:38. Bursevi defines fear as anticipating the disliked on probable or certain evidence, contrasting hope and safety. [10 notes: TUSTARI; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 24:37/q16/p2 Fear and hope must coexist without despair or false security. — Tustari quotes Luqman's counsel through Awn, saying a believer has a heart for each; al-Hasan's wish to be the person eventually released from Hell after a thousand years exemplifies hope. Ibn Masud's recitation after two fasting men refused a drink is also transmitted. [3 notes: TUSTARI(الحسن,عون بن عبد الله); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الله بن مسعود)]
- 24:37/q16/p3 For realized people the feared day is immediate in each present breath. — Qushayri contrasts ordinary future fear with continual anticipation of punishment now. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 24:37/q16/p4 They fear losing divine light and honor when perception changes; ultimately they fear themselves. — Tabataba'i says deprivation would lead to enduring misery and punishment. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 24:37/q17 Which day is meant?  [يَوْمًۭا · referent]
- 24:37/q17/p1 The Day of Resurrection and reckoning. — Zayd ibn Aslam, Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and others identify it. Jishumi stresses its severity; al-Durr's report describes all people gathered where a caller and sight reach everyone. [19 notes: TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); IBNABIHATIM(زيد بن أسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي); JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(النبي ﷺ,زيد بن أسلم); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]

## 24:37/q18 What happens to the hearts and eyes that “turn”?  [تَتَقَلَّبُ فِيهِ ٱلْقُلُوبُ وَٱلْأَبْصَٰرُ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes distinguish bodily terror, oscillating hopes, transformed knowledge, punishment in Fire, and inward change; critics test whether a view fits the day's terror and timing.
- 24:37/q18/p1 They convulse, stare or scan in fear as the Day's horrors unfold. — Ibn Atiyya prefers this as fitting the day's terror, cites Arab battle language and poets' bird-wing and bouncing-ball images, and rejects accounts that miss that terror. Abu Hayyan prefers it over Hellfire or cognitive change, citing 33:10. Razi, Kashshaf, Ibn Kathir and Study Quran compare 33:10 or 14:42–43; Ibn Ashur compares 6:110. Al-Sulami reports ordinary people's fear that deeds will be rejected, linked to 39:47. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفقيه الإمام القاضي); RAZI-FULL(القول الأول); BAYDAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); SULAMI(الواسطى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفقيه الإمام القاضي)+]
- 24:37/q18/p2 Hearts alternate between hope of rescue and fear of ruin, while eyes search where people or their records will go. — Tabari says eyes look right or left as people are led and await books from either side. Razi reports the Mu'tazilite objection: the saved have no fear and the condemned no hope, which he says he has refuted. Abu Hayyan's phrasing moves from hope to hope and fear to ruin. [14 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفقيه الإمام القاضي); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); RAZI-FULL(القول الثالث); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة)-]
- 24:37/q18/p3 Hearts rise or lodge at the throats, while eyes turn blue, become blind, or stare fixedly. — Muqatil puts hearts at the larynx; Yahya says they can neither return nor emerge and cites 40:18. Al-Dahhak describes the unbeliever's keen sight turning blue then blind. Baghawi has fixed eyes; Bursevi cites 33:10 and 14:42. [16 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك,مقاتل بن سليمان); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك); BURSEVI]
- 24:37/q18/p4 Former doubt, disbelief or sealed perception changes to certain knowledge and direct sight. — Ibn Qutayba cites the removed veil of 50:22. Farra and Zajjaj distinguish doubters' certainty from believers' increased insight. Ma-w ardi says an unbeliever sees guidance after error; Qurtubi says it cannot then benefit him. Tabataba'i describes believers seeing generosity and disbelievers becoming blind, citing 50:22, 39:47, 39:69, 57:12, 17:72, 75:22–23 and 83:15. [19 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة,الفراء، الزجاج، ابن قتيبة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(القول الثاني); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفقيه الإمام القاضي)-]
- 24:37/q18/p5 Hearts and eyes are turned over Hell's embers or stones. — Nahhas and Mawardi transmit the image; Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan object that this occurs after Resurrection rather than on the day named. Al-Alusi calls it unsound. [5 notes: NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); RAZI-FULL(الجبائي); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفقيه الإمام القاضي)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 24:37/q18/p6 Fire successively scorches, roasts or burns hearts and eyes, altering their states. — Nahhas and Mawardi give fiery changes; al-Jubba'i links changed appearance to punishment and cites 6:110. Al-Alusi rejects this with the ember account. [4 notes: NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي); RAZI-FULL(الجبائي); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 24:37/q18/p7 Their states pass continually without settling. — Tustari says believers fear this at Resurrection. Al-Nasrabadhi contrasts the transfer of souls with the turning of hearts. [2 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(النصرآباذي)]
- 24:37/q18/p8 Hearts and eyes undergo spiritual unveiling and inner transformation. — Al-Husayn says God changes and veils them, then tears veils with light, lifts them through remembrance and opens locks through nearness; this calls for self-watchfulness. Qashani speaks of secrets, inner perceptions and annihilation in the Real, citing the divine-hearing-and-sight report. Tabataba'i identifies hearts and eyes with souls and inward insights. [4 notes: SULAMI(الحسين); QASHANI; TABATABAI]

## 24:37/q19 Whose hearts and eyes are turning?  [ٱلْقُلُوبُ وَٱلْأَبْصَٰرُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the definite plural is restricted to disbelievers or includes both communities.
- 24:37/q19/p1 The disbelievers' hearts and eyes. — Yahya and Qurtubi make this explicit, describing hearts in throats and blue or blind eyes; al-Dahhak specifies the unbeliever. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; QURTUBI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك); RAZI-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 24:37/q19/p2 Both believers and disbelievers. — Tabataba'i says the definite plural covers both groups, with differing outcomes. Zajjaj contrasts believers' increased insight with others' recognition; Farra similarly distinguishes them. [5 notes: TABATABAI; ZAJJAJ; FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء، الزجاج، ابن قتيبة)]

## 24:37/q20 Is there a variant pronunciation of “turn”?  [تَتَقَلَّبُ · readings]
- 24:37/q20/p1 Ibn Muhaysin assimilates the two tāʾs or silences the second tāʾ. — Abu Hayyan describes assimilation; Al-Alusi reports sukūn of the second tāʾ. Zajjaj says a form taqallab is sound Arabic but rejects it as Qur'anic recitation because readings must be transmitted. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن محيصن); ALUSI-FULL(ابن محيصن); ZAJJAJ]

## 24:37/q21 What rhetorical force lies in applying “turn” to both hearts and eyes?  [تَتَقَلَّبُ فِيهِ ٱلْقُلُوبُ وَٱلْأَبْصَٰرُ · rhetoric]
- 24:37/q21/p1 The paired root suits hearts changing among thoughts and eyes among sights. — Al-Rummani calls this rhetorical homogeneity, distinguishing paired wording and semantic correspondence. Burhan groups words sharing a root or origin. [3 notes: RUMMANI; BURHAN]
- 24:37/q21/p2 The doubled written tāʾ visually intensifies the turning. — Biqai says the writing depicts the hearts' and eyes' intense movement. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 24:37/q22 Why does the phrase include “in it” after “a day”?  [فِيهِ · rhetoric]
- 24:37/q22/p1 It confines the turning to the specific Day of Resurrection and reckoning. — Samarrai compares 31:33, where omission of fīhi broadens the effect beyond that day, with 2:281 and 24:37, where it remains for events specifically on Judgment Day. He says Qur'anic word placement is deliberate. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 24:37/q23 How do these men relate to the houses and the parable of God's light?  [رِجَالٌۭ · links]
turns on: Whether the houses are read as literal mosques or as inward houses, and how the men exemplify the preceding light.
- 24:37/q23/p1 They pray in the houses, identified as mosques, and embody guidance to God's light. — Tabari prefers mosques because the men pray there; al-Hasan says God authorized the houses for morning and evening worship. Ibn Atiyya agrees. Ibn Abbas connects the niche and lamp of 24:35 to these undistracted traders. Tabataba'i says “whomever He wills” identifies fully faithful men, and Islahi says the faith parable's cause is followed by lived conduct. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; TAB-FULL+]
- 24:37/q23/p2 The houses can symbolize hearts that may be turned away from remembrance. — Study Quran reports the Sufi symbolic continuation. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Sufis)]

## 24:37/q24 How does the verse connect with nearby contrasts and other Qur'anic exhortations?  [* · links]
- 24:37/q24/p1 It displays faithful guidance before the contrasting image of disbelievers' darkness. — Jishumi says the opposite examples encourage faith and warn against disbelief; Abu Hayyan connects 24:39 with who can receive guidance. Ibn Ashur says the clauses implicitly contrast the men with hypocrites. Islahi contrasts shining believing hearts with unbelievers' darkness. [4 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 24:37/q24/p2 It parallels warnings against wealth distracting from remembrance and commands to leave trade for Friday prayer. — Ibn Kathir and Study Quran cite 63:9 and 62:9. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 24:37/q24/p3 The fearful imagery recalls other descriptions of the Last Day. — Ibn Kathir cites 40:18, 14:42 and 76:8–12; Study Quran cites 14:42–43. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 24:37/q25 What rulings and reports concerning women's prayer accompany this verse?  [رِجَالٌۭ · law]
- 24:37/q25/p1 A woman's prayer in the more private parts of her home is preferable to public mosque prayer. — Yahya transmits Ibn Masud's home exception for the Sacred and Prophet's Mosques, Umm Humayd's ranking from private room to the Prophet's Mosque, Ka'b's porch comparison, and Qatada's concealment principle; a report warns about Israelite women in mosques and markets. Qurtubi gives a home-chamber ranking. Biqai cites Ibn Masud, Umm Salama and Umm Humayd, who built a prayer place deep in her home; no grading is given for two reports. Al-Durr cites Umm Salama, another Prophetic ranking and Ibn Masud's exception for the Sacred Mosque. [15 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن مسعود,النبي,كعب); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله); BIQAI-FULL(أم حميدة,أم سلمة,عبد الله بن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,النبي ﷺ); ALUSI-FULL]

## 24:37/q26 What other mosque reports or teachings do the sources attach to the men?  [رِجَالٌۭ · reports]
- 24:37/q26/p1 Visiting a mosque for prayer, learning or teaching earns exceptional reward. — Tha'labi cites Sahl: the learner or teacher resembles a victorious fighter, unlike a visitor with another aim. Qurtubi reports a purified worshipper earning pilgrim reward, Duha earning Umrah reward, successive prayers recorded in Illiyyin, light for those walking in darkness, a paradise welcome for each morning or evening visit, forgiven sins and raised degrees at each step, superior congregational prayer and angels' supplication. He calls Tha'labi's extra host analogy unauthentic. Ibn Kathir cites both Sahih collections for twenty-fivefold congregational reward and the walking and angel reports. Kuranyolu cites tranquility, mercy and divine mention for those studying scripture in a house of God. [9 notes: THALABI(سهل بن سعد الساعدي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو أمامة,أبو هريرة,بريدة,سعيد بن المسيب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hz. Peygamber)]
- 24:37/q26/p2 Mosques and their worshippers will be honored at the Resurrection. — Qurtubi transmits Anas's report of radiant mounts led by callers and imams, carrying worshippers across the plain like lightning. Al-Durr transmits a herald calling generous people, night worshippers, undistracted traders and praisers, citing 32:16. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أنس); DURR-FULL(النبي ﷺ)]
- 24:37/q26/p3 Mosques can be built yet empty of remembrance when scholars fail to practice. — Qurtubi transmits Ali's warning that Islam can remain in name and the Quran in script while scholars spread turmoil. Jishumi instead draws the positive lesson of maintaining and frequenting mosques. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); JISHUMI]

## 24:37/q27 How does the clause “trade does not distract them” attach to “men”?  [لَّا تُلْهِيهِمْ تِجَٰرَةٌۭ وَلَا بَيْعٌ · grammar]
- 24:37/q27/p1 It is a nominative adjective qualifying “men.” — Al-Samin states this grammatical relation. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
