# 14:36: 20 questions

## 14:36/q01 Why does Abraham renew the address 'My Lord' here?  [رَبِّ · rhetoric]
- 14:36/q01/p1 It intensifies a personal appeal for compassion and for protection against idolatry. — Abu Hayyan says Abraham had seen his father and people worship idols; al-Biqa'i notes omission of the vocative particle and consistent singular possessive. Al-Alusi links it to the preceding request and hope of acceptance; Tabataba'i says it invokes mercy and explains protection for Abraham and his descendants. Islahi counts seven Rabbi or Rabbana appeals, treating repetition as supplication's humility; Rabbi invokes a favour personally experienced, Rabbana a favour witnessed by all. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 14:36/q02 What does 'they' refer to?  [إِنَّهُنَّ · referent]
turns on: Whether the objects named in the preceding prayer are cult images or wealth.
- 14:36/q02/p1 The idols, images or objects worshipped instead of God. — Qatada calls them both awthan and asnam. Mujahid distinguishes a human-shaped carved sanam from an otherwise shaped wathan. Al-Samin identifies the pronouns in innahun and adlalna as referring to idols. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL]
- 14:36/q02/p2 Gold and silver coins. — Ibn Atiyya reports this as another interpretation of the idols. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل)]

## 14:36/q03 How can idols be said to lead people astray?  [أَضْلَلْنَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether misguidance is purely figurative causation or is also mediated by devils inhabiting idols.
- 14:36/q03/p1 The attribution is figurative or causal: people stray by worshipping or attaching themselves to lifeless idols. — Ibn Qutayba offers both 'caused to stray' and 'strayed through them.' Zajjaj and Abu Ishaq say idols cannot reason or act and compare a house captivating someone; Tabrisī compares a woman who beguiles through infatuation and explains a poet's wandering camel similarly. Tha'labi and Baghawi compare Satan's frightening through allies (3:175); Baghawi calls this an inversion. Kashshaf, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Baydawi, Bursevi, and others compare worldly life deluding people (6:70). Razi says all theological schools agree the attribution is metaphorical; Ibn Atiyya and Alusi add that God actually creates misguidance. Tabataba'i says causal effects need not involve a conscious cause. Study Quran invokes 71:24. Islahi says living-being terms express idols' profound influence. [25 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 14:36/q03/p2 Devils may have spoken through idols and actively enticed worshippers. — Bursevi reports a story of a devil in Abu Jahl's idol slandering the Prophet; the idol later professed Islam and warned worshippers, whereupon Abu Jahl smashed it and claimed sorcery. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 14:36/q04 Why are feminine pronouns used of the idols?  [إِنَّهُنَّ أَضْلَلْنَ · grammar]
- 14:36/q04/p1 The nonrational broken plural is treated as feminine singular in agreement. — Al-Samin identifies both pronouns; Abu Hayyan contrasts the figurative masculine plural agreement in fa-qad dallu. Islahi additionally reads living-being diction as stressing the idols' influence. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 14:36/q05 What does following Abraham and being 'of him' mean?  [فَمَن تَبِعَنِى فَإِنَّهُۥ مِنِّى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the affiliation is described as Abraham's religious community or as an inward relation to the spirit.
- 14:36/q05/p1 Following his monotheistic religion, teaching, and way makes one a member of his religious community, closely attached to him. — Tabari specifies worship of God alone and abandonment of idols; Ibn Abbas adds knowledge of God. Ibn al-Anbari cites a poem where not being 'of' someone means not adhering to his covenant. Kashshaf, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, and Bursevi stress closeness like an inseparable part; Kashshaf and Abu Hayyan compare the saying that a cheat is not 'of us,' a grave departure from believers' traits. Al-Samin glosses 'of me' as follower or party. Al-Biqa'i connects this to avoiding idols and hoped-for success. Tabataba'i insists on the whole path of belief and practice, rather than bare affirmation of oneness; the path image answers the preceding straying. Al-Alusi includes the call to monotheism and Islam. Kur'an Yolu includes salvation for protected believers. Razi treats religious belief as making a follower almost a part of Abraham, while disobedience outside religion remains subject to mercy. Bursevi calls min partitive; Alusi allows partitive simile or connective affiliation; al-Sharif and al-Suyuti explain the specific implied relation. [30 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري,ابن عباس,الشاعر); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الجلال السيوطي,الشريفي); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 14:36/q05/p2 Inwardly, following the spirit on the path of tawhid makes one belong to it. — Qashani describes spiritual affiliation through following the spirit. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 14:36/q06 Who counts as Abraham's descendants or true heirs in this prayer?  [فَمَن تَبِعَنِى فَإِنَّهُۥ مِنِّى · referent]
turns on: The scope of spiritual kinship beyond biological descent.
- 14:36/q06/p1 Spiritual allegiance admits later followers and can exclude disobedient biological descendants. — Tabataba'i says this interprets 'my sons'; like 2:126 it broadens blessing to followers and limits protected descendants. He invokes 3:68 for the Prophet and believers, and Noah's son excluded from his family in 11:46. He reports two nuances: Abraham may disown all disobedient people, or merely be silent about their affiliation. His earlier discussion takes that silence as disowning and as evidence for a faithful line. Razi also uses Noah's son to confine the prayer to believing descendants. Asad notes Abraham accepts God's verdict on sinful descendants in 2:124. [8 notes: TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 14:36/q06/p2 Abraham's words also support including devoted followers of the Imams among the Prophet's family. — In two al-Ayyashi reports, al-Sadiq applies 'of me' to lovers of the Imams, and to those who fear God and act righteously; he and Umar ibn Yazid also cite 3:68. [2 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]

## 14:36/q07 What does 'disobeys me' denote?  [وَمَنْ عَصَانِى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the contrast with following and the prior idolatry make disobedience unbelief, or whether lesser sins are included or intended.
- 14:36/q07/p1 Failure to follow Abraham's path, including idolatry or unbelief. — Qushayri says opposition to Abraham is disobedience to God. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan infer unbelief from the contrast with following; Qurtubi and a report from Muqatil specify persistence in shirk. Al-Biqa'i says such a person has strayed through idols and deserves Hell. Bursevi and Alusi explain the antithesis with following; Alusi says the wording presupposes Abraham kept preaching, so failure was disobedience rather than lack of access. Tabataba'i allows abandoning the whole or part of the path; Islahi speaks of descendants who turn to polytheism. [11 notes: MUQATIL; QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)+]
- 14:36/q07/p2 Disobedience in matters below associating partners with God, including violations of religious law by a monotheist. — Muqatil ibn Hayyan gives the restriction; Abu Bakr specifies violations of some laws while affirming monotheism. Razi takes the disobedient as sinners inside Abraham's religious community. Alusi and Tabataba'i object that the contrast and unrestricted wording do not supply this qualifier. [10 notes: THALABI(مقاتل بن حيان); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل بن حيان); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل بن حيان); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل بن حيان); NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); ALUSI-FULL(مقاتل)-; TABATABAI-]

## 14:36/q08 How does the conditional sentence work?  [وَمَنْ عَصَانِى فَإِنَّكَ غَفُورٌۭ رَّحِيمٌۭ · grammar]
turns on: Which unstated element, if any, is supplied to connect the conditional subject to its answer.
- 14:36/q08/p1 Man is a conditional nominative subject; 'You are forgiving, merciful' is its answer with an omitted 'to him'. — Al-Samin supplies the omitted resumptive lahu. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 14:36/q08/p2 An omitted clause says Abraham will not pray against whoever disobeys him, before the appeal to divine mercy. — Al-Alusi reports this proposed ellipsis. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 14:36/q09 Why say 'disobeys me' and set it against 'follows me'?  [فَمَن تَبِعَنِى وَمَنْ عَصَانِى · rhetoric]
- 14:36/q09/p1 The parallel makes following equivalent to obedience; 'me' keeps Abraham's appeal courteous and merciful. — Abu Hayyan and al-Bahr identify semantic antithesis; Bursevi compares disbelief set against gratitude. Qushayri says Abraham asks mercy for infringement of his own right rather than invoking a violation of God's right; al-Ta'wilat al-Najmiyya likewise sees adab and extraordinary generosity. Alusi says the phrasing implies an ongoing call and culpable nonresponse. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL(البحر)]

## 14:36/q10 How should the appeal to God's forgiveness for a disobedient person be understood?  [وَمَنْ عَصَانِى فَإِنَّكَ غَفُورٌۭ رَّحِيمٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether disobedience includes shirk, and whether the divine names request actual pardon, conditional pardon, conversion, delay, or leave the outcome to God.
- 14:36/q10/p1 God forgives the person after repentance, faith, or a return to obedience, including repentance from unbelief. — Muqatil says God may lead a disbeliever to repent and monotheism (33:24); Zajjaj invokes 9:114; al-Suddi and others require repentance. Ibn Atiyya says unbelief is meant but forgiveness accepts later belief. Kashshaf speaks of past disobedience after new obedience. Abu Hayyan prefers Ibn Atiyya's reading. Tabataba'i objects that no qualifier appears and the repentant person re-enters the followers' side; Razi rejects restricting the verse to repentance. [16 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي,مقاتل بن سليمان); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(السدي); TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)+; RAZI-FULL(السدي)-; TABATABAI-]
- 14:36/q10/p2 The plea concerns sins below shirk, which God may pardon for a monotheist. — Abu Bakr specifies violations of law while affirming tawhid; Razi says the wording also embraces major sins before repentance, not merely minor or repented sins. Tabataba'i cites 4:48 and 4:116 and says no forgiveness of shirk; Study Quran agrees. Alusi and Tabataba'i object to restricting the original wording to lesser sins without a qualifier. [14 notes: THALABI(مقاتل بن حيان); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل بن حيان); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل بن حيان); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل بن حيان); NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL(مقاتل)-; TABATABAI-]
- 14:36/q10/p3 Abraham spoke before learning that God would not forgive shirk. — Abu Bakr and Ibn al-Anbari compare Abraham's later disavowal of his parents or father; Alusi says a prophet need not know every revealed proof at once and reason allows the possibility. Study Quran and Tabataba'i cite 4:48. Razi says context already excludes intercession for unbelief; Tabataba'i invokes Abraham's mature doctrine and 9:114 to reject ignorance. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); RAZI-FULL(قيل)-; TABATABAI-]
- 14:36/q10/p4 Forgiveness means guiding a person still in unbelief into Islam or monotheism. — Abu Bakr and a report from Muqatil take pardon as transfer from kufr to faith. Razi objects that forgiveness and mercy imply removal of punishment, not conversion; Tabataba'i objects that this leaves the apparent request for the persisting disobedient. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر); ALUSI-FULL(مقاتل); TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); RAZI-FULL(قيل)-; TABATABAI-]
- 14:36/q10/p5 Forgiveness means worldly respite or concealment while idolatry persists, delaying punishment so repentance remains possible. — Alusi cites 13:6. Razi objects that contemporary unbelievers are not immediately punished yet are not called forgiven; Tabataba'i says forgiveness normally removes consequences, especially in the afterlife. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); RAZI-FULL(قيل)-; TABATABAI-]
- 14:36/q10/p6 God has discretion to forgive even the idolatrous or persistently disobedient person; Abraham entrusts that fate to Him. — Tabari says God pardons whom He wills, even rejecters who associate partners; Baydawi allows forgiveness from the outset or after repentance and notes a scriptural threat. Bursevi distinguishes rational possibility under Ash'ari theology from 4:116's scriptural bar. Ibn Kathir says entrusting the case to God does not assert that forgiveness will occur, comparing Jesus in 5:118. Al-Biqa'i says the idolater deserves Hell yet remains God's servant; punishment or forgiveness rests with God. Islahi denies this is a prayer on behalf of polytheists and leaves judgment to God. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI(المفسرون)-]
- 14:36/q10/p7 The prayer intercedes for major sinners among Abraham's believers before repentance, removing their punishment. — Razi argues the unrestricted wording is an explicit plea, while Abraham's disavowal of idolaters excludes unbelievers and minor sins or repented major sins would already be forgiven on opponents' premises. He extends Abraham's station to Muhammad by parity and 6:90 and 16:123. Alusi reports the argument but rejects its force and refers to an earlier reply. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 14:36/q10/p8 Shirk could be forgiven under an earlier dispensation although it is barred in the present dispensation. — Alusi attributes this to Nawawi. Tabataba'i reports it then rejects it for lack of evidence: warnings to Adam's community and Jesus' dispensation, Qur'anic judgment, intercession, and prophetic prayers show no rescue from shirk save repentance before death (2:39; 5:72). [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); TABATABAI-]
- 14:36/q10/p9 The wording conveys hope or an implicit plea without fixing the disobedient person's outcome. — Qushayri says the divine names allusively request mercy; his alternative says Abraham avoids a definite request as proper supplicatory etiquette. Sulami says he does not curse and claims no ownership over God's servants. Tabrisī describes God's covering of sins and mercy in all states. Al-Biqa'i says hope for pardon does not diminish divine majesty or wisdom and compares Jesus. [5 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); QUSHAYRI(ويقال); TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 14:36/q11 What do 'Forgiving, Merciful' express?  [غَفُورٌۭ رَّحِيمٌۭ · meaning]
- 14:36/q11/p1 God abundantly covers sins, then bestows mercy, honour, and favour. — Tabrisī says God covers servants' sins and favours them in all states. Al-Biqa'i distinguishes abundant covering by Ghafur from abundant honour afterward by Rahim, with emphatic wording showing heightened hope. Qashani reads the covering inwardly as divine light over a dark state, then perfection. Yahya ibn Mu'adh hopes for mercy as a sinner and even reward despite lacking obedience; Bursevi's Persian couplet calls sinners fitting recipients of generosity. Bursevi's herald report says God pardons His claims against Muhammad's community, while people must pardon each other's outstanding claims; he glosses taba'at as rights owed to another. [7 notes: TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI(الحديث,يحيى بن معاذ الرازي)]

## 14:36/q12 What merciful conduct does Abraham's wording model?  [وَمَنْ عَصَانِى فَإِنَّكَ غَفُورٌۭ رَّحِيمٌۭ · interpretation]
- 14:36/q12/p1 He avoids cursing opponents and speaks graciously, leaving room for God's mercy. — Qatada condemns prophets' being abusive cursers and compares Jesus at 5:118; Ibn Atiyya calls this beautiful speech and refined courtesy. Qushayri says Abraham seeks mercy for violations of his own right; Kur'an Yolu calls it compassion for opponents. Qushayri says Muhammad's direct 'forgive my people, for they do not know' is fuller in pardon; the Prophet's words are also separately reported. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); SULAMI(بعضهم); QUSHAYRI(النبي); DURR-FULL(قتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 14:36/q13 What is reported about the Prophet's use of this prayer?  [فَمَن تَبِعَنِى فَإِنَّكَ غَفُورٌۭ رَّحِيمٌۭ · reports]
turns on: These are distinct reports and uses of the prayer, rather than competing versions of one event.
- 14:36/q13/p1 He recited Abraham's plea with Jesus' plea, wept and prayed for his community, and received God's promise of consolation. — Tabari, Ibn Hibban, Tha'labi, Ibn Kathir and Ibn Atiyya transmit forms of the report through Abd Allah ibn Amr (Ibn Atiyya says Ibn Umar). Some give raised hands, repetition of 'my community' three times, Gabriel as messenger, and God's promise to satisfy him and not grieve him. Jesus' comparison is 5:118. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(محمد); IBNHIBBAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص,عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص عن النبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن عمر عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 14:36/q13/p2 The Prophet compared Abu Bakr's merciful disposition to Abraham's prayer. — Musnad Ahmad reports the comparison, citing Abraham's words about followers and disobedient people. [1 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(إبراهيم)]
- 14:36/q13/p3 The Prophet prayed forgiveness for a sincere believing Arab during his lifetime. — Al-Durr al-Manthur transmits this distinct prayer for one who affirms meeting God. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 14:36/q14 How does this prayer connect to its context and Abraham's other prayers?  [* · links]
turns on: The setting and fulfilment of Abraham's prayer, especially the tension between the reported answer and idolatry among descendants.
- 14:36/q14/p1 It contrasts Abraham's monotheistic hopes for Mecca and his descendants with later Quraysh idolatry, while presenting faithful followers as their fulfilment. — Abu Hayyan connects the condemnation of Quraysh's exchanged favour with their ancestor's plea for Mecca's safety, freedom from idols and prayer at the House. Islahi says Quraysh inhabit blessings from Abraham's prayer but wrongly credit deities, while Muhammad and believers fulfil his hopes; Kur'an Yolu sees grateful servitude and salvation through Abraham's way. Study Quran says the surah is named for the episode (14:35–41) and notes Abraham's prayer for his parents and believers on the Last Day. Kur'an Yolu refers Abraham's prayer for polytheist parents to 9:114. Tabataba'i says the clause about followers explains the preceding protection request and resolves exegetical difficulties; he parallels the provision prayer in 2:126. Asad connects it with the verdict at 2:124. [11 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 14:36/q14/p2 Abraham prayed after Mecca became a settlement and he and Ishmael built the Kaaba. — Kur'an Yolu reports this timing from commentators. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 14:36/q14/p3 God answered the prayer: Abraham's descendants avoided idols, and the town received safety, provision, leadership and religious guidance. — Al-Durr al-Manthur reports this from Mujahid; Baghawi preserves the objection that many descendants worshipped idols despite Abraham's prayer and prophetic protection. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL-]

## 14:36/q15 What does the verse imply about human misguidance and choice?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the causal account of idols also makes God the actual agent of misguidance.
- 14:36/q15/p1 Following and disobeying are acts of the servant, and misguidance should not be attributed to God. — Jishumi appeals to the preceding denial that misguidance comes from God and to this verse's division into followers and disobedient persons. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 14:36/q15/p2 The idols are only figurative causes; God is the actual creator of misguidance. — Ibn Atiyya and Alusi make this qualification while agreeing that idols cannot act. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 14:36/q16 What is the inward significance of idols here?  [أَضْلَلْنَ · inward]
- 14:36/q16/p1 Attachment to idols veils people from unity; the idol of pride must be broken. — Qashani describes attraction and attachment as the veil. Bursevi quotes Kamal Khujandi's couplet that breaking pride's idol in lovers' religion surpasses a hundred acts of worship. [2 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(الكمال الخجندي)]

## 14:36/q17 Who established the order of the suras in which this prayer appears?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether sura order came by explicit revelation or Prophetic instruction, an implied pattern allowing judgment, or Companion arrangement.
- 14:36/q17/p1 The order was divinely or prophetically fixed before codex collection. — Al-Kirmani places it in the Preserved Tablet and Gabriel's annual reviews, twice in the last year; al-Tibi adds descent to the lowest heaven and later compilation in that order. Ibn al-Hassar says revelation fixed suras and verses. Al-Nahhas prefers Prophetic arrangement and says it preceded codex collection, with al-Anfal separate from Bara'a; he cites the seven long suras and Quranic divisions. Ibn Hajar says most was fixed by Prophetic instruction, citing Aws al-Thaqafi's groupings. Al-Bayhaqi excepts al-Anfal and Bara'a. Reports cited by Gharnati, Burhan, Itqan and Suyuti include the two bright suras in Muslim, seven long suras in one rak'a and Mufassal grouped in Ibn Abi Shayba, Ibn Masud's early sequence in Bukhari, and the Prophet's bedtime sequence of the three short suras in Bukhari; variants give Sa'id or Ma'bad ibn Khalid. Gharnati says coherent connection holds regardless of origin and disputes by unqualified readers are unsurprising. [31 notes: GHARNATI(النبي,عائشة,عبد الله بن مسعود,معبد بن خالد); BURHAN(أبو جعفر النحاس,ابن مسعود,الكرماني,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,سعيد بن خالد); ITQAN(أبو جعفر بن الزبير,ابن مسعود,البخاري,البيهقي,الطيبي); SUYUTI-TANASUQ(أبو جعفر النحاس,أبو جعفر بن الزبير,ابن الحصار,ابن حجر,ابن مسعود,البيهقي,الكرماني,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,سعيد بن خالد); BURHAN(أبو جعفر النحاس)+; ITQAN(أبو جعفر النحاس)+]
- 14:36/q17/p2 Many suras had a settled Prophetic order, while some remaining order may have been left to the community. — Ibn Atiyya names the seven long suras, Hawamim and Mufassal. Abu Muhammad leaves the second class undecided. Gharnati and Ibn al-Zubayr say reports establish more than that proposal allows, with only a small disputed remainder. Itqan records Ibn al-Zubayr's rejection of the broader undecided remainder. [5 notes: GHARNATI(القاضي أبو محمد); ITQAN(ابن عطية); SUYUTI-TANASUQ(أبو جعفر بن الزبير,ابن عطية); ITQAN(أبو جعفر بن الزبير)-]
- 14:36/q17/p3 The Companions arranged suras; the Prophet fixed verse order and sequencing within them. — Ibn Faris distinguishes the two tasks, saying Gabriel conveyed God's instruction for verse order. [1 notes: BURHAN(ابن فارس)]
- 14:36/q17/p4 The disagreement can be verbal: Prophetic indications guided placement while some ordering still allowed Companion judgment. — Al-Zarkashi contrasts explicit command with implied direction allowing ijtihad and cites Malik's account of compilation from what Companions heard. [1 notes: ITQAN(الزركشي)]

## 14:36/q18 What does irdaaf mean as a rhetorical device?  [* · rhetoric]
- 14:36/q18/p1 It conveys a meaning through a synonymous expression instead of direct wording or mere allusion. — Al-Tibi defines the device; the note supplies no specific application to 14:36. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 14:36/q19 Why did Abraham ask protection against idol worship even for himself?  [أَضْلَلْنَ · interpretation]
- 14:36/q19/p1 His inclusion of himself underscores the grave danger of polytheism, since idols had drawn many astray. — Islahi stresses the breadth of that danger despite Abraham's standing. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 14:36/q20 How is the preceding prayer for distance from idols read?  [* · readings]
- 14:36/q20/p1 Some read wa-ajnibni with hamza; both janibni and ajnibni ask God to turn Abraham and his children away from idol worship. — Ibn Jinni reports al-Jahdari, al-Thaqafi and Abu al-Hajhaj for the hamza reading; he says it can additionally suggest making them devoted to God. [1 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(الجحدري، الثقفي، أبو الهجهاج)]
