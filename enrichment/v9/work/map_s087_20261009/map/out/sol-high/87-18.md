# 87:18: 24 questions

## 87:18/q01 What does “this” refer to?  [هَٰذَا · referent]
turns on: Which earlier material the demonstrative reaches: the nearest statement, the adjacent passage, the whole sūra, or the Qurʾān; proximity and the reported sayings are offered as tests.
- 87:18/q01/p1 The teaching in verses 14–17: purification, remembrance, prayer, worldly preference, and the better, lasting afterlife. — Al-Ṭabarī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Ibn ʿAṭiyya favor proximity; Ibn Kathīr calls this a strong choice. Rāzī reads the sequence as purification of belief and morals, knowledge of God, bodily obedience, and release from worldly attachment. Abū Dharr's report is cited for the span; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the opening through verse 13 is not confirmed here and cannot grade that report. ## 87:18/q02 In what sense is this teaching “in” the earlier scriptures?  [لَفِى ٱلصُّحُفِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether being written earlier entails shared teaching or actual transcription and verbal correspondence.
- 87:18/q02/p1 Its meaning or counterpart is there, though the wording and language may differ. — Ibn Qutayba excludes an exact verbal identity; al-Kashshāf, Nasafī, al-Ālūsī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr specify shared meaning. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes scrolls as a container metonymy, comparing 38:16. [9 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI+]
- 87:18/q02/p2 The sūra was copied or transcribed from earlier scrolls, even appearing as revealed to Muhammad. — Ibn ʿAbbās's reported wording says copied; al-Suddī makes the stronger claim of correspondence as revealed. Another report extends this to the end of Sūra 53. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,السدي); IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك); DURR(ابن عباس,السدي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB-]

## 87:18/q03 What does ṣuḥuf mean?  [ٱلصُّحُفِ · meaning]
- 87:18/q03/p1 Written sheets or scrolls, plural of ṣaḥīfa; by extension, books of revelation. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Thaʿlabī give the plural. Bursawī distinguishes a muṣḥaf as a collection of sheets. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls ṣuḥuf an eloquent irregular plural, like sufun from safīna; Kuran Yolu says it became synonymous with book. Corpus Coranicum suggests Old South Arabian borrowing and notes the early Qurʾānic revelatory sense, later largely replaced by kitāb, alongside zubur. [8 notes: TAB; THALABI; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 87:18/q04 How is the ḥāʾ of ṣuḥuf read?  [ٱلصُّحُفِ · readings]
turns on: The vowel of the ḥāʾ, with sukūn identified as a Tamīm dialect.
- 87:18/q04/p1 With ḍamma on the ḥāʾ. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān assign this to the majority. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 87:18/q04/p2 With sukūn on the ḥāʾ, here and sometimes in the next verse. — Reported from al-Aʿmash and from Abū ʿAmr through Hārūn and ʿIṣmah; Abū Ḥayyān's al-Lawāmiʿ and al-Ālūsī call it Tamīmī usage. Al-Samīn says the form is straightforward. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN(الأعمش وهرون وعصمة عن أبي عمرو,كتاب اللوامح العبقلي عن أبي عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(الأعمش وهرون وعصمة عن أبي عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(الأعمش وهارون وعصمة عن أبي عمرو)]

## 87:18/q05 Which scriptures are meant by the earlier scrolls?  [ٱلصُّحُفِ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether the named prophets possessed scrolls, exemplify all revelation, or evoke biblical writings.
- 87:18/q05/p1 Abraham's scrolls and Moses's books, especially the Torah. — Al-Ṭabarī names the two; Muqātil says Abraham's scrolls have been taken away. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Moses had many scrolls comprising Torah books. Kuran Yolu treats them as examples of prophetic revelation, not its limits. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB; TAB-FULL; THALABI(الضحاك); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 87:18/q05/p2 All of God's earlier revealed books. — Al-Ḥasan says the teaching occurs in all God's books; al-Māwardī gives this as a third interpretation. The claim is about the reach of the plural, beyond the two named exemplars. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); DURR(الحسن); DURR-FULL(الحسن)]
- 87:18/q05/p3 A broad evocation of biblical scripture through Abraham and Moses. — Ahrens calls it a rough label for the Old Testament or whole Bible, citing 53:36 onward. Corpus Coranicum prefers a biblical summary through prominent figures and finds specific apocryphal Abrahamic books unlikely; it notes the Old Testament has no revealed Abrahamic book. Heck reads writings about the figures through an objective genitive, which Corpus Coranicum does not adopt. [3 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM(Ahrens,Heck); CORPUSCORANICUM+]
- 87:18/q05/p4 Revealed writings about Moses and Abraham. — Corpus Coranicum reports Heck's objective genitive analysis, but its author prefers a broad biblical evocation. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM(Heck)]

## 87:18/q06 How many scrolls or revealed books were given, and to whom?  [ٱلصُّحُفِ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in their totals and in whether Adam or Moses receives ten preliminary scrolls, and whether Abraham receives ten or twenty.
- 87:18/q06/p1 104 books: ten sheets to Adam, fifty to Seth, thirty to Idrīs, ten to Abraham, plus four named books. — Abū Dharr's account in al-Thaʿlabī and al-Kashshāf gives this allocation; al-Thaʿlabī adds that Idrīs first wrote with a pen. Kuran Yolu repeats the ten, fifty, thirty, ten distribution. [3 notes: THALABI(أبو ذر); KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو ذر); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 87:18/q06/p2 104 books: fifty to Seth, thirty to Idrīs, ten to Abraham, ten to Moses before the Torah, then four named books. — Abū Dharr's prophetic account gives this distribution in al-Durr; Study Quran reports it and notes Shiite variants. [3 notes: DURR(أبو ذر); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]
- 87:18/q06/p3 105 scrolls and four books: thirty-five to Seth, fifty to Idrīs, twenty to Abraham. — Wahb ibn Munabbih names the four later books for Moses, David, Jesus, and Muhammad; Study Quran says some reports assign Abraham twenty scrolls. [4 notes: MAWARDI(وهب بن منبه); MAWARDI-FULL(وهب بن منبه); STUDYQURAN(Shiite sources)]
- 87:18/q06/p4 A total of 104 revealed books, without an allocation in this note. — Jishumī says the books were apportioned among a list of prophets; Shiite sources have a slightly different narration according to Study Quran. [2 notes: JISHUMI; STUDYQURAN(Shiite sources)]

## 87:18/q07 When were these and other scriptures revealed?  [ٱلصُّحُفِ · reports]
- 87:18/q07/p1 Abraham's scrolls on Ramadan's first night; Torah on the sixth, Psalms on the twelfth, Gospel on the eighteenth, and Furqān on the twenty-fourth. — Abū al-Khald gives this sequence in both Ṭabarī versions. [2 notes: TAB(أبو الخلد); TAB-FULL(أبو الخلد)]

## 87:18/q08 What do reports say Abraham's scrolls taught?  [ٱلصُّحُفِ · reports]
- 87:18/q08/p1 Practical maxims for wise people and rulers. — The sayings counsel guarding tongue and time, attending to one's affairs, worship, self-examination, lawful needs and livelihood, and answering the oppressed even when unbelieving; Abū Dharr's report also warns rulers against hoarding. [6 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 87:18/q09 What do reports say Moses's scrolls taught?  [ٱلصُّحُفِ · reports]
- 87:18/q09/p1 Admonitions about mortality, worldly change, destiny, and judgment. — Abū Dharr's prophetic account challenges joy despite death, trust amid change, striving as though destiny were unknown, and failure to act despite certain judgment. [2 notes: DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 87:18/q10 What is said about the survival of Abraham's and Moses's teachings?  [ٱلصُّحُفِ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the earlier teachings survived as scrolls, in later writings, or as enduring principles across laws.
- 87:18/q10/p1 The central teachings persisted through every revealed law without abrogation. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān apply this to purification and the afterlife; Rāzī says reason and transmission show these truths do not vary; al-Biqāʿī says the aim of all laws cannot be abrogated. The prophetic saying about acting without shame is cited as inherited from the first prophecy. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI(بعض المفسرين); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 87:18/q10/p2 Mosaic and Israelite writings survive in the Bible despite alteration, retaining monotheism and the afterlife. — Islāhī says the surviving teaching can revive faith. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:18/q10/p3 Abraham left oral exhortations later written down by Israelite descendants, and later revived for the Ishmaelites. — Islāhī says Israelite prophets renewed the teaching, while unlettered Ishmaelites kept it orally before neglect and innovations; Muhammad revived and completed it. Muqātil says Abraham's scrolls were later taken away. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; MUQATIL]

## 87:18/q11 What does al-ūlā add to ṣuḥuf?  [ٱلْأُولَىٰ · meaning]
- 87:18/q11/p1 It marks the scrolls as earlier or ancient and can magnify their standing. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports that calling them ancient before naming Abraham and Moses honors them; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the concluding claim dignifies enduring teaching. [3 notes: TABATABAI(قيل); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 87:18/q12 How is al-ūlā formed and related grammatically to ṣuḥuf?  [ٱلْأُولَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: The three reported analyses differ over the root and the status of the initial hamza.
- 87:18/q12/p1 It is a feminine adjective agreeing with the broken plural ṣuḥuf, treated as feminine. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives this agreement. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]
- 87:18/q12/p2 The related awwal derives from hamza-wāw-lām. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this as one of three root analyses; ūlā's derivation follows the analysis. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]
- 87:18/q12/p3 The related awwal derives from wāw-wāw-lām with an added hamza. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this as another root analysis. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]
- 87:18/q12/p4 The related awwal derives from wāw-hamza-lām with metathesis. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this as a third root analysis. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]

## 87:18/q13 Why is the statement phrased and emphasized this way?  [إِنَّ لَفِى ٱلْأُولَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 87:18/q13/p1 It dignifies enduring guidance and confirms it against deniers. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the emphatic particles as directed at deniers familiar with Abraham and Moses. Al-Biqāʿī says ancient acceptance makes hearers more receptive and confirms Muhammad's continuity with earlier prophets; Corpus Coranicum says the ending authorizes the preceding rebuke and its psalm-like hymn reinforces the claim. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 87:18/q13/p2 The wording announces one divine source for prophetic truth, with changing details and shared core principles. — Quṭb says the creed is one though eras differ; Islāhī says the Prophet's teaching is not new and includes the afterlife and individual responsibility. Kuran Yolu invokes 26:196 for shared faith, worship, and morality; Islāhī says worldly preference prevents the message taking root. [7 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 87:18/q13/p3 The general mention of earlier scrolls is specified by Abraham and Moses in the next verse. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it summary followed by detail and apposition; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the preliminary antiquity magnifies the scrolls. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]

## 87:18/q15 How does this statement function at the sūra's close?  [* · links]

## 87:18/q18 What recitation practices and merits are reported for Sūrat al-Aʿlā?  [* · reports]
- 87:18/q18/p1 The Prophet recited it in the two rakʿas before witr, with al-Kāfirūn; then al-Ikhlāṣ, al-Falaq, and al-Nās in witr. — ʿĀʾisha gives this sequence. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(عائشة)]
- 87:18/q18/p2 He recited it in the first of three witr rakʿas, followed by al-Kāfirūn and al-Ikhlāṣ. — The report is from ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Abī Shabrah. [1 notes: DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:18/q18/p3 He recited it in the first Maghrib rakʿa and al-Kāfirūn in the second. — ʿAbd Allāh ibn al-Ḥārith reports this. [1 notes: DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:18/q18/p4 He used it at Eid and Friday prayers with al-Ghāshiya, sometimes both on one day. — Quṭb also says al-Aʿlā is Meccan, has nineteen verses, and was loved by the Prophet. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]
- 87:18/q18/p5 Reciting it merits ten rewards for each letter revealed to Abraham, Moses, and Muhammad. — Al-Kashshāf transmits this as a prophetic report. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:18/q18/p6 On reciting it, the Prophet said “Glory to my Lord the Most High.” — The saying is also ascribed to ʿAlī and Ibn ʿAbbās; al-Kashshāf reports the Prophet loved the sūra and says Michael first uttered the formula. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(علي وابن عباس)]

## 87:18/q19 How does the older-scripture claim bear on Persian recitation in prayer?  [* · law]
- 87:18/q19/p1 It supports permissibility of reciting the Qurʾān in Persian during prayer. — Nasafī argues that Qurʾānic meaning was present in earlier scriptures despite differences in wording and language. [2 notes: NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL]

## 87:18/q20 What is said about loss or abrogation of recited scripture?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether solitary reports establish Qurʾānic wording, and whether ʿUmar's account shows continued recitation.
- 87:18/q20/p1 God can erase script and recitation by causing forgetfulness and commanding their abandonment, as with earlier scriptures; Qurʾānic abrogation ends with the Prophet's death. — Abū Bakr al-Rāzī gives this explanation in al-Burhān and al-Itqān. [2 notes: BURHAN(أبو بكر الرازي); ITQAN(أبو بكر الرازي)]
- 87:18/q20/p2 Solitary reports cannot establish Qurʾānic text and its subsequent abrogation. — Al-Itqān reports this objection and Ibn Ẓafar's view that the stoning text is al-munsaʾ rather than abrogated recitation, though its wording may be known. [3 notes: ITQAN(ابن ظفر,قوم)]
- 87:18/q20/p3 ʿUmar's stoning report does not securely establish that its recitation remained. — Al-Burhān infers writability from ʿUmar's statement; a counterargument says criticism would not have stopped him writing a still-recited verse. Al-Suyūṭī finds the continued-recitation inference problematic but rejects calling ʿUmar's transmission solitary, since he received it from the Prophet. Kathīr ibn al-Ṣalt reports Zayd quoting the wording during codex writing and ʿUmar explaining lashes versus stoning. [5 notes: ITQAN(البرهان,قول,كثير بن الصامت)]

## 87:18/q21 What reading of the sūra's moral drama is tied to this verse?  [* · inward]
- 87:18/q21/p1 Its truths describe receptivity to reminders, purification, and the ruin of seekers of sensory life, preserved on luminous tablets disclosed to the two prophets. — Qāshānī gives this inward account of the ancient scriptures. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 87:18/q22 What does the verse imply about the Qurʾān's distinctive scope?  [* · interpretation]
- 87:18/q22/p1 Its eloquence and range exceed poets and soothsayers while continuing the prophets' message. — Al-Biqāʿī cites praise, love verse, heroic boasting, satire, exhortation, warning, narrative, law, and unseen past and future information. [2 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 87:18/q23 Why are Abraham and Moses named in this order?  [* · rhetoric]
- 87:18/q23/p1 The order follows revelation chronology. — Al-Itqān compares placing Torah and Gospel before the Furqān. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 87:18/q24 How is the name Abraham read in the following verse?  [* · readings]
- 87:18/q24/p1 The majority read Ibrāhīm; reported variants include forms without alif and yāʾ and Ibrāhām. — Abū Ḥayyān attributes the shortened form to Abū Rajāʾ and Ibrāhām to Abū Mūsā and Ibn al-Zubayr. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور)]
