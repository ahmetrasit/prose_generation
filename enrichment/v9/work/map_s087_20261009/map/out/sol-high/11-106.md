# 11:106: 17 questions

## 11:106/q01 What does it mean that these people became wretched?  [شَقُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether wretchedness names an already decreed destiny, a temporary condition, or an inward orientation.
- 11:106/q01/p1 Their wretchedness was decreed and leads, through their evil deeds, to their being sentenced to Hell. — Al-Ṭabrisī says they are called wretched before entering because their present course leads there; his prophetic report about wretchedness in the womb describes foreknown evil deeds, like calling a child an orphan in anticipation. Al-Biqāʿī stresses the judgment to enter; Bursevī and al-Ālūsī stress prior decree. Al-Qurʾān Yolu connects the division to worldly faith and deeds. [7 notes: TABRISI(محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 11:106/q01/p2 Wretchedness can be a temporary state of deprivation or punishment, distinct from an enduring final state. — Al-Qushayrī contrasts present deprivation with faith in the final state and says misery and happiness can each be temporary or lasting. Ibn ʿAbbās calls punished Muslim sinners wretched while in Hell, then released through intercession. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس)]
- 11:106/q01/p3 The spiritually wretched attribute obedience to themselves and remain bound to their own planning rather than witnessing divine decree. — Al-Qushayrī contrasts them with the happy, who know events belong to God; divine success excludes abandonment and realization excludes deprivation. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI(قيل,ويقال)]

## 11:106/q02 Who are the wretched destined for the Fire?  [ٱلَّذِينَ شَقُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the paired groups include everyone gathered, and whether wretchedness describes temporary punishment or only final destiny.
- 11:106/q02/p1 All who are punished in Hell, including unbelievers and sinful believers. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya ties the scope to the following exception; al-Jishumī says sinners are included against the Murjiʾa. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī connect sinful believers' temporary residence to the exception while unbelievers remain. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the two groups must exhaust all gathered people. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة والضحاك وأبو سنان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة والضحاك); TABATABAI; TAB-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 11:106/q02/p2 The eternally wretched are unbelievers, who remain under Hell's threat. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya makes this depend on whether the exception excludes temporarily punished people. Al-Qushayrī contrasts their fate with the happy promised whatever they wish and more (50:35); al-Qurʾān Yolu identifies those persisting in unbelief. Abū Jaʿfar excludes enemies of ʿAlī from intercession; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites Imami reports of disbelievers' eternal punishment. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QUSHAYRI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر)]
- 11:106/q02/p3 The wretched in this passage are specifically sinful monotheists who enter Hell and later leave it. — The sixth view transmitted from Ibn ʿAbbās, Jābir, Abū Saʿīd and Successors makes the same people wretched in Hell and happy in Paradise. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that this restricts an exhaustive division of all gathered people and leaves doubled subjects and exceptions unexplained. [2 notes: TABATABAI(ابن عباس وجابر بن عبد الله وأبو سعيد الخدري وجماعة من التابعين); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI-]

## 11:106/q03 How is شقوا read and understood?  [شَقُوا۟ · readings]
turns on: Whether the verb is intransitive with fatḥa or transitive with ḍamma.
- 11:106/q03/p1 The common reading has fatḥa on shīn, from intransitive shaqiya, 'they became wretched'. — Al-Zamakhsharī calls it common and al-Samīn calls it the majority reading. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور)]
- 11:106/q03/p2 Al-Ḥasan reads ḍamma on shīn, with the transitive sense 'God made them wretched'. — Al-Zamakhsharī compares the form with saʿidū; al-Samīn likens it to ashqāhum. Al-Ālūsī repeats the reading. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قُرئ); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]

## 11:106/q04 What is the grammatical structure of the wretched people's clause?  [فَأَمَّا ٱلَّذِينَ شَقُوا۟ فَفِى ٱلنَّارِ · grammar]
- 11:106/q04/p1 The wretched are the subject and 'in the Fire' is the predicate; the sounds clause continues the predication. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī give the subject-predicate analysis. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 11:106/q05 Why does the passage use ammā to introduce the wretched?  [فَأَمَّا · rhetoric]
- 11:106/q05/p1 It unfolds the previously summarized gathering into the fates of two groups. — Al-Burhān says ammā can elaborate a summary or restrict an assertion to an attribute; al-Qurʾān Yolu links the fates to the gathering of 11:103 and worldly faith and deeds. [2 notes: BURHAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 11:106/q06 Why are the wretched mentioned first?  [فَأَمَّا ٱلَّذِينَ شَقُوا۟ · rhetoric]
- 11:106/q06/p1 Their precedence strengthens the warning, since most people perish. — Al-Biqāʿī gives both the prevalence of loss and the threatening purpose of the passage. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 11:106/q07 What is meant by their being in the Fire?  [فَفِى ٱلنَّارِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'Fire' is explained as the afterlife abode or as an inward punishment of the soul.
- 11:106/q07/p1 They enter actual Hell as recompense for evil deeds and dwell there. — Al-Jishumī says this follows the division into wretched and happy; al-Ṭabrisī calls the Fire punishment for evil deeds. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the clause establishes settled residence, parallel to the Garden's inhabitants in 11:108. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; TABATABAI]
- 11:106/q07/p2 The Fire also signifies the soul's painful deprivation of what it sought and the consequences of its sins. — Al-Qāshānī contrasts the self's Fire with a garden of attained desires, while al-Ālūsī describes the deprivation and pains as punishment of the soul. [2 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 11:106/q08 What do zafīr and shahīq mean or depict here?  [زَفِيرٌۭ وَشَهِيقٌ · meaning]
turns on: The reports variously distinguish breath direction, place in the body, loudness, braying sequence, distressed vocal quality, or movement in Hell.
- 11:106/q08/p1 Zafīr is the first donkey bray and shahīq its final sound. — Al-Ṭabarī adduces Ruʾba's verse for the final sound in the chest; al-Qurṭubī likewise cites a braylike poetic breath. Qatāda applies the sequence to the disbeliever's voice; al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Muqātil say the final sound returns into throat or belly. Al-Shammākh's line places zafīr before shahīq. Al-Bayḍāwī notes either term can denote either end; Elmalılı calls them inhalation and exhalation respectively. Ibn ʿAbbās compares zafīr alone to a donkey's bray in his answer to Nāfiʿ ibn al-Azraq. [33 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ(أهل اللغة من البصريين والكوفيين); NAHHAS(أهل اللغة من البصريين والكوفيين); THALABI(الضحاك ومقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك ومقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قتادة,مقاتل والضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك ومقاتل); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL(أهل اللغة من الكوفيين والبصريين,الضحاك ومقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أهل اللغة من الكوفيين والبصريين,الشماخ,الضحاك ومقاتل والفراء وابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أهل اللغة من الكوفية والبصرية,الشماخ,رؤبة); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(المجمع); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 11:106/q08/p2 Zafīr expels breath; shahīq draws it back in. — Al-Layth and other lexicographers describe the breath cycle; Ibn Fāris calls them opposites and connects zafīr with strain under a heavy load. Al-Shammākh's verse illustrates the sequence. Al-Qāmūs says exhalation follows inhalation, while Ibn Kathīr calls them breath's exit and entry. Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies forceful expiration under pressure followed by urgent strained inspiration. Al-Rāghib describes repeated breathing swelling the ribs; 25:12 and 67:7 are cited. [22 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الليث); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL(الشماخ); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن فارس); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن فارس); SAMIN-DURR(ابن فارس,الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL(أبو الحسن الرماني,ابن كثير); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن فارس,الراغب); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب,الزمخشري)]
- 11:106/q08/p3 Zafīr is a loud sound and shahīq a faint or weak one. — Ibn ʿAbbās is repeatedly transmitted with this contrast; al-Wāḥidī finds it close to lexicographers' and exegetes' accounts. Al-Rāzī says zafīr suggests strength and shahīq weakness; a Durr report locates the first in the throat and second in the chest. Al-Biqāʿī's Ibn ʿAbbās citation breaks off before the second gloss. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(قوم); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس)]
- 11:106/q08/p4 Zafīr is in the throat and shahīq in the chest. — Abū al-ʿĀliya, al-Rabīʿ and al-Ḍaḥḥāk are transmitted this way; Ruʾba's verse is used to illustrate the chest sound. Ibn Kathīr calls the two exhalation and inhalation reflecting torment; Ibn ʿAbbās is also reported with this ordering. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو العالية); IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك,الضحاك، الربيع بن أنس); THALABI(أبو العالية); MAWARDI-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العالية); JISHUMI(أبو العالية); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو العالية); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(أبو العالية); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو العالية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو العالية والربيع بن أنس,رؤبة); SAMIN-DURR(أبو العالية والربيع بن أنس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 11:106/q08/p5 Zafīr comes from the chest or whole body; shahīq comes from the throat or chest. — Thaʿlab gives whole body versus chest, whereas Ibn ʿArafa and one report from Abū al-ʿĀliya give chest versus throat. Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers chest and cavity expansion for zafīr and a final gasp from throat for shahīq, likening it to dying or fainting. Al-Layth's reported bodily account varies. [10 notes: MUQATIL(أبو العباس ثعلب); NAHHAS(أبو العالية); JISHUMI(ابن عرفة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو العالية وقتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو العالية); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 11:106/q08/p6 They are harsh groans and distressed cries expressing pain, grief and anguish. — Abū Isḥāq and al-Zajjāj call the first ugly or severe and the second especially loud or high; Ibn ʿAṭiyya likens them to weeping. Ibn ʿAbbās' ʿAṭāʾ report speaks of regret, loud breathing and endless tears; Abū Muslim links sobbing to interrupted breathing and even fainting or death. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī describe heat gripping the heart and constricting breath; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees a metaphorical braylike depiction. The Study Quran includes incurable grief. [22 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس (رواية عطاء)); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم,ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; TABRISI(ابن عباس,الزجاج)]
- 11:106/q08/p7 Zafīr is repeated, labored breathing from grief; shahīq is a prolonged breath or deep sound. — Ibn ʿĪsā connects zafīr to bearing a heavy load and shahīq to a lofty mountain; al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī add swelling ribs, intensity, binding, roaring fire and water carriers. Al-Rummānī and al-Rāghib stress strain and length. Al-Jaʿdī's horse with a large belly, al-Rāʿī's mare folded over its breaths, and al-Farrāʾ's large-bellied horse illustrate the bodily root. Elmalılı calls ordinary usage audible distressed breathing. [18 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن السكيت,الجعدي); JISHUMI(علي بن عيسى); TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(غيره); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(أبو الحسن الرماني); ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); ELMALILI]
- 11:106/q08/p8 Zafīr is drawing in breath and shahīq is expelling it. — Al-Layth describes a trapped chest breath released as shahīq; al-Rāzī's physiological account says grief heats the heart, leads to cooling inhalation and difficult exhalation, matching physicians' terms. Elmalılı distinguishes medical terminology from his broader lexical gloss. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn transmit variant al-Layth wording. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(الليث); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الليث); ELMALILI]
- 11:106/q08/p9 Zafīr is a donkey's sound and shahīq a mule's sound. — Ibn al-Sāʾib is cited for this unusual species distinction by Ibn al-Jawzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن السائب); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(السائب)]
- 11:106/q08/p10 Zafīr is a sharp sound and shahīq a heavy sound. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives this distinct Ibn ʿAbbās report. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 11:106/q08/p11 Together they denote a loud sound. — Ibn ʿAbbās is transmitted with this collective gloss. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس)]
- 11:106/q08/p12 Zafīr is the damned rising on swelling Hellfire; shahīq is their descent when angels strike them back. — Al-Ḥasan describes hope of escape followed by iron maces and cites 22:22. Elmalılı explains the fire's rise and fall and relates forced return to 32:20. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(الحسن); ELMALILI(حسن)]

## 11:106/q09 What inward meaning is drawn from the two sounds?  [زَفِيرٌۭ وَشَهِيقٌ · inward]
- 11:106/q09/p1 Zafīr evokes strong attachment to worldly pleasures; shahīq evokes inability to reach spiritual bliss and divine lights. — Al-Rāzī offers this spiritual reading alongside his lexical accounts. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 11:106/q10 How does the sounds clause attach grammatically?  [لَهُمْ فِيهَا زَفِيرٌۭ وَشَهِيقٌ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause stands as a new predication or describes a circumstance of their being in Hell.
- 11:106/q10/p1 It is a separate sentence answering an implied question about their condition. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī present this analysis; al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī also treat it as continued predication. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 11:106/q10/p2 It is circumstantial, linked to the implicit existential in 'in the Fire,' its pronoun, or the Fire itself. — Al-Nasafī identifies the implicit existential; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī give the pronoun or Fire as possible anchors. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 11:106/q11 Why are zafīr and shahīq left indefinite and singled out?  [زَفِيرٌۭ وَشَهِيقٌ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the wording emphasizes the ghastliness of the sounds or their warning effect.
- 11:106/q11/p1 The indefinite nouns magnify the unprecedented horror of their gasps and sobs. — Elmalılı says the forms intensify the scene; al-Qāshānī says, alternatively, indefiniteness may indicate kinds of sounds. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 11:106/q11/p2 Naming their disfiguring breaths makes the warning more frightening than simply naming pain. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the sounds deter people from the causes of damnation; Bursevī compares them to ugly cries and bodily changes in worldly torture, with the afterlife worse. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 11:106/q12 How does the following exception affect the wretched people's stay in Hell?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The attachment and force of the exception: residence, sounds, added forms of punishment, pre-entry time, divine will, or a later repeal; reports differ on who leaves.
- 11:106/q12/p1 Sinful monotheists are punished for a time and then removed to Paradise; unbelievers remain eternally. — Qatāda leaves God's exception to divine knowledge but reports scorched sinners' admission to Paradise. Al-Ṭabarī invokes transmitted reports and doctrine against denying their entry to or exit from Hell. Ibn ʿAbbās says intercession releases them; Anas and Abū Hurayra transmit prophetic removal reports, opposed to Ḥarūriyya or Khārijite perpetual punishment of all entrants. Al-Rāzī calls the argument strong. Abū Jaʿfar describes charcoal bodies restored at Paradise's spring or river, the name Jahannamiyyūn later removed; a righteous servant attributes release to pardon. Jābir's prophetic report says God can remove whom He wills. [23 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(أنس بن مالك,الضحاك بن مزاحم,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة والضحاك وأبو سنان); NASAFI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة والضحاك); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر,أنس بن مالك,ابن عباس وجابر بن عبد الله وأبو سعيد الخدري وجماعة من التابعين,جابر بن عبد الله,عبد صالح,قتادة); RAZI-FULL(قوم)+; TAB-FULL+; TABRISI+]
- 11:106/q12/p2 Eventually all inhabitants leave Hell, leaving it empty. — A report from ʿUmar allows even residence as long as the dunes of ʿĀlij before release; Abū Hurayra recites this verse for an empty Hell, Ibn Masʿūd says its doors flap, and al-Shaʿbī says it empties quickly. Ibrāhīm calls the exception Hell's greatest hope. Al-Qāshānī cites an unattributed report of watercress growing at Hell's bottom and another of empty, clapping doors. [10 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة,إبراهيم,ابن مسعود,الشعبي,عمر); QASHANI; TABATABAI(أبو هريرة,ابن مسعود,عمر بن الخطاب); TABATABAI-]
- 11:106/q12/p3 The exception concerns other forms of torment beyond the mentioned sounds, while Hell's duration continues. — Al-Zajjāj pairs extra punishments with unmentioned Paradise delights and the unbroken gift in 11:108. Ibn al-Anbārī and another proposal attach it to the sounds; al-Rāzī rejects that as departing from the apparent meaning. One variant has the sounds cease when the damned become motionless. [5 notes: TABRISI(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الأنباري); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ZAJJAJ+; RAZI-FULL-]
- 11:106/q12/p4 The exception adds punishment or bliss beyond the stated span; illā means 'apart from' or 'besides'. — Al-Ṭabrisī attributes this to al-Zajjāj, al-Farrāʾ and ʿAlī ibn ʿĪsā, making the added span more torment or bliss. [1 notes: TABRISI(الزجاج والفراء وعلي بن عيسى)]
- 11:106/q12/p5 The excepted period is before entry, during gathering, judgment, or the barzakh. — Al-Māzinī and al-Balkhī give gathering, judgment or barzakh, and al-Balkhī prefers it. Abū Naḍra's report from Jābir or Abū Saʿīd makes the exception a delay before entering Fire. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes preliminary grave questioning and punishment from final Resurrection reward and cites 40:46 and 40:71–72. The Study Quran reports al-Bayḍāwī's afterlife setting rather than physical-world time, with 14:48. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو نضرة عن جابر أو أبي سعيد الخدري); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(al-Bayḍāwī); TABRISI(المازني والبلخي)+]
- 11:106/q12/p6 The exception affirms permanence subject only to divine will; God does not will their departure. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this seventh view as guarding endless residence against any cause other than God; al-Qāshānī uses 11:108's unsevered gift and opposition between the groups to support permanent punishment. [2 notes: TABATABAI; QASHANI]
- 11:106/q12/p7 The exception marks staggered entry of successive groups. — Salām ibn al-Mustanīr cites the processions in 39:71 and 39:73; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that the wording concerns the endpoint of residence and all begin settling on Judgment Day. [1 notes: TABATABAI(سلام بن المستنير البصري); TABATABAI-]
- 11:106/q12/p8 The exception covers an interval when worldly heavens and earth end and grave torment pauses before resurrection. — Abū Jaʿfar al-Ṭūsī describes Hell residence including graves until heaven and earth end; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this inherits objections to reading illā as 'other than'. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر الطوسي); TABATABAI-]
- 11:106/q12/p9 The first exception excludes people pardoned before Hell, while the Paradise exception concerns former Hell inmates. — Abū Mijlaz proposes distinct referents. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that this breaks the two verses' unity and that pardon by good deeds or intercession makes the pardoned happy from the outset. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو مجلز); TABATABAI-]
- 11:106/q12/p10 The exception was abrogated by later statements of endless punishment. — Al-Suddī cites 4:168 to end hope of Hell's release and 4:57 for Paradise's permanence. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects this for lack of rational or transmitted proof, conflict with explicit Paradise wording, and other Meccan declarations of both groups' abiding. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(السدي); TABATABAI(السدي); TABATABAI-]
- 11:106/q12/p11 Illā functions as 'and,' appending whatever God wills. — Al-Farrāʾ reads a poem about cold ashes guarded from wind this way; al-Ṭabrisī says grammarians versed in language weakened the analysis. [1 notes: TABRISI; TABRISI(الفراء)-]
- 11:106/q12/p12 Inwardly, the wretched pass to worse fires or between levels of fire, and may come to enjoy what suits them there. — Al-Qāshānī distinguishes fires of self, heart and spirit; his deeper account says desire drives the soul through Hell's levels and can make the fire pleasurable. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 11:106/q12/p13 If the indefinite sounds denote kinds, purification can lead the wretched from Hell to Paradise. — Al-Qāshānī makes departure depend on removal of dark traits and consequences of sin, so they are not wretched forever. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 11:106/q13 What broader teaching is drawn from the verse's fate division?  [* · inward]
- 11:106/q13/p1 Appointed lifespans and spiritual arrival follow divine knowledge and will, beyond human efforts to advance or delay them. — Al-Qushayrī says today is the fleeting witnessed moment to use well; he applies fixed timing to death and spiritual union. His cited poem says safety entails fear of calamity while trial may lead to joy. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال)]

## 11:106/q14 How do these fates connect with the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
- 11:106/q14/p1 The two outcomes explain the gathering of 11:103 and the universal division of 11:105; the Fire and Garden clauses run in parallel. — Al-Qurʾān Yolu relates destinations to worldly faith and deeds. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says plural groups fit the collective gathering after the singular indefinite nafs and that the next verse confirms settled residence. Al-Burhān says repeating an exception first generally and then in detail emphasizes its placement and distinction. [5 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI; BURHAN]

## 11:106/q15 What grammatical and lexical points in the adjoining duration clause affect this verse?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Whether permanence is a future state or already present; how mā expresses duration.
- 11:106/q15/p1 Khālidīn is an anticipatory circumstantial expression from the locative's implied pronoun. — Abū Ḥayyān calls the state future; Bursevī uses this analysis if 'in the Fire' means coming to be there. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 11:106/q15/p2 No anticipatory circumstantial expression is needed, since the people are already abiding in Hell. — Bursevī contrasts 39:73, where permanence follows entry. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
- 11:106/q15/p3 Mā is a temporal verbal noun meaning 'for the duration that the heavens and earth endure'. — Abū Ḥayyān gives the adverbial parse; al-Rummānī glosses khālid as perpetual residence and dāʾim as continued duration. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو الحسن الرماني)]

## 11:106/q16 What does 'as long as the heavens and earth endure' mean for Hell's duration?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase uses ordinary Arabic permanence language or describes the afterlife's enduring setting.
- 11:106/q16/p1 It is an Arabic idiom for perpetuity, not a physical time limit. — Al-Ṭabrisī cites conventional formulas about night, day, sky and earth; poetry by ʿAmr ibn Maʿdīkarib and Zuhayr uses stars, mountains and lasting days. Abū Ḥayyān cites formulas about Mount Thabīr and a shining star. [4 notes: TABRISI(الحسن,عمرو بن معد يكرب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 11:106/q16/p2 It refers to enduring heavens and earth of the afterlife. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and al-Jubbāʾī say transformed skies and earth are recreated and do not perish; another view means Paradise's and Hell's own sky above and earth below. Abū Ḥayyān cites 14:48 and 39:74; another argument requires a covering and shade. Ibn ʿAbbās says heavens and earth return to originating light under the Throne; al-Bayḍāwī's reported account calls it afterlife topography. [6 notes: TABRISI(الضحاك والجبائي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(al-Bayḍāwī)]

## 11:106/q17 How is the adjoining verb سعدوا read and defended?  [* · readings]
turns on: Whether dialectal transitive سعده الله and the passive مسعود justify the ḍamma reading.
- 11:106/q17/p1 Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī and Ḥafṣ read سعدوا with ḍamma, supported by dialectal transitive سعده الله. — Al-Farrāʾ attributes it to Hudhayl; al-Jawharī and Ibn al-Qushayrī give سعد, مسعود and أسعد forms; al-Kisāʾī calls فعل and أفعل dialect variants. Abū ʿAmr reports سعد الرجل and a dialectal سعده. Al-Kisāʾī cites مسعود, though an objection derives it from مكان مسعود فيه with فيه omitted; al-Samīn disputes ʿAlī ibn Sulaymān's astonishment at the reading. [9 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو بن العلاء,ابن القشيري,الجوهري,الفراء,الكسائي,حمزة والكسائي وحفص,علي بن سليمان); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,المهدوي,مكي)-]
- 11:106/q17/p2 The other readers have fatḥa on سعدوا; the ḍamma reading is rare or grammatically remote. — Al-Mahdawī says مسعود is uncommon passive because ordinary usage is أسعده الله; Makkī derives it from أسعد with augment removed, like مجنون from أجنه. Abū al-Baqāʾ says neither attestation nor analogy supports the bare form. ʿAlī ibn Sulaymān criticizes al-Kisāʾī's reading. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,المهدوي,حمزة والكسائي وحفص,علي بن سليمان,مكي)]
