# 33:41: 14 questions

## 33:41/q01 What kind of remembrance of God is commanded?  [ٱذْكُرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether dhikr specifies speech, heart awareness, prayers, formulas, conduct or all fitting forms.
- 33:41/q01/p1 Verbal mention of God with the tongue. — Muqātil, al-Suddī and al-Kalbī specify speech. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the context favors it. Jishumī reports invoking beautiful names and attributes verbally in every place and time, privately and openly. Kashshāf transmits a report with 'tongue,' also given as 'heart.' Damghānī gives the lexical verbal sense. [10 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,مقاتل); DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 33:41/q01/p2 Inward remembrance and sustained awareness. — Māwardī says constant heart awareness brings obedience and avoidance of sin. Qushayrī and Ibn ʿAjībah say heart remembrance can continue when speech cannot; Qushayrī adds that love entails frequent mention. Qurtubī and Study Quran contrast sincere heart remembrance with hypocritical tongue speech. Jishumī and Kashshāf give heart alternatives. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAjībah,Q)]
- 33:41/q01/p3 Remembrance can be inward or spoken. — Bursevī defines dhikr as bringing something to mind in heart or speech, distinguishing ordinary recovery after forgetting from the elite's sustained presence. Ṭabāṭabāʾī defines it as directed perception, opposite to forgetfulness; uttering names is one instance. Kuran Yolu combines tongue, heart, reflection and awareness as servitude's lifeline. [3 notes: BURSEVI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 33:41/q01/p4 Praise and invocation with God's fitting names, attributes and formulas. — Ibn Ḥayyān lists glorification, praise, unity and magnification. Qatāda adds lā ḥawla wa-lā quwwata illā bi-llāh. Maqātil and Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān prescribe the four formulas in all circumstances; Durr adds thanks. Ṭabrisī and Ālūsī also name beautiful names and exalted attributes. Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Bursevī and Elmālili embrace varied fitting praise. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the phrases are included but do not exhaust remembrance. [16 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن حيان); KASHSHAF-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(قيل,مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل بن حيان); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مقاتل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم,مقاتل); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); BIQAI-FULL]
- 33:41/q01/p5 The five daily prayers. — Al-Kalbī and Ibn al-Sāʾib identify remembrance with the five prayers. Elmālili says remembrance and tasbīḥ also include prayer. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب); ELMALILI]
- 33:41/q01/p6 Supplication and entreaty. — Māwardī attributes this answer to Ibn Jubayr. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جبير)]
- 33:41/q01/p7 Acknowledging God's lordship and one's servitude. — Māwardī reports this interpretation. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 33:41/q01/p8 Belief in divine oneness and justice. — Jishumī reports its obligation in all states except forgetfulness and sleep. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 33:41/q01/p9 Obedience, worship and good works generally. — Māwardī's heart remembrance induces obedience; Jishumī says perpetual obedience without sin. Kashshāf includes every good work. Bursevī applies it to sincerity, repentance, gratitude and patience. Al-Ṣādiq says to recall God at what He permits and forbids so one observes His limits. Study Quran calls it the core of worship. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); STUDYQURAN]
- 33:41/q01/p10 Turning to God in hope and fear and seeking protection. — Jishumī reports this explanation. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 33:41/q01/p11 Every fitting form of remembrance without a restricted subject or form. — Kuran Yolu makes the unrestricted scope explicit; Bayḍāwī and Elmālili include all fitting kinds. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BAYDAWI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 33:41/q02 How is 'much' remembrance understood or measured?  [ذِكْرًۭا كَثِيرًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether abundance concerns continuity, sincerity, a prescribed count or formula, custom, or no fixed cap.
- 33:41/q02/p1 Never forgetting God. — Mujāhid's gloss recurs in Wāḥidī, Jishumī, Baghawī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī and Ālūsī; some attribute it also to Ibn ʿAbbās. Wāḥidī says never forgetting in any circumstance. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
- 33:41/q02/p2 Remembering through most or all times and circumstances. — Baghawī names night and day, land and sea, health and illness, private and public. Ibn Kathīr adds travel and residence, wealth and poverty, citing 4:103. Jishumī, Ālūsī, Bayḍāwī, Elmālili and Bursevī give all or most times and conditions. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(ابن عباس,قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد); ELMALILI]
- 33:41/q02/p3 Thirty recitations of the four formulas. — Ṭabrisī reports this from the Imams; Ālūsī attributes it to the Prophet's family. [2 notes: TABRISI(أئمتنا); ALUSI-FULL(العترة الطاهرة)]
- 33:41/q02/p4 The Tasbīḥ of Fāṭima al-Zahrāʾ counts as much remembrance. — Ṭabrisī cites Zurāra and Ḥumrān from Abū ʿAbd Allāh. [1 notes: TABRISI(زرارة وحمران عن أبي عبد الله)]
- 33:41/q02/p5 Gabriel's expanded counted formula counts as much remembrance. — Ṭabrisī cites al-Wāḥidī via al-Ḍaḥḥāk from Ibn ʿAbbās; Ālūsī says it is counted by God's knowledge, weight and fullness. Six promises are registration among frequent rememberers, precedence over ordinary day-and-night dhikr, a Paradise tree, falling sins, God's regard and safety from punishment. [3 notes: TABRISI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 33:41/q02/p6 Sincere inward remembrance, unlike hypocritical public tongue speech. — Qurtubī and Study Quran distinguish sincere abundance from scant hypocritical speech. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports private remembrance as abundant, cites 4:142 for hypocrites' public-only practice and calls the inference subtle. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN(Q); TABATABAI(أمير المؤمنين عليه السلام)]
- 33:41/q02/p7 An amount judged abundant by customary usage. — Ālūsī reports this view without a numerical threshold. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 33:41/q02/p8 An abundance without a set numerical end. — Ibn ʿAbbās contrasts remembrance with fixed-limit duties. Qurtubī says it is easy and greatly rewarded. Al-Rāzī says nothing impedes it. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL]

## 33:41/q03 What time limits, duties and excuses govern remembrance?  [ٱذْكُرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ ذِكْرًۭا كَثِيرًۭا · law]
turns on: Whether the command is voluntary or obligatory and which incapacity excuses neglect.
- 33:41/q03/p1 No fixed limit; all conditions are included, with loss of reason the stated excuse. — Ibn ʿAbbās distinguishes other duties' fixed limits and excuses. Ṭabarī adds God's and angels' prayer in response. Baghawī cites 4:103, standing, sitting and lying. Qurtubī says remembrance is easy and highly rewarded. Ibn Kathīr includes travel and residence, wealth and poverty, privacy and publicity. Sūlamī urges unbroken heart witnessing; Durr and Bursevī restate the exception. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); SULAMI(النصرآباذي); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 33:41/q03/p2 No fixed time, but voluntary. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām describes it so. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM]
- 33:41/q03/p3 Obligatory in all states except forgetfulness and sleep. — Jishumī reports this specifically for belief in unity and justice. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 33:41/q03/p4 No limit or excuse except oppressive coercion forcing neglect. — Study Quran attributes this version to Ibn ʿAbbās, unlike versions naming loss of reason. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAbbās)]

## 33:41/q04 Can the remembrance formulas be recited during ritual impurity?  [ٱذْكُرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ · law]
- 33:41/q04/p1 Yes, in major or minor ritual impurity. — Wāḥidī's received report permits them after relieving oneself too. Baghawī reports pure, sexually impure and minor-impure persons; Kashshāf transmits Mujāhid's allowance for pure and major-impure persons. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 33:41/q05 What stages and qualities of inward remembrance are described?  [ٱذْكُرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ · inward]
- 33:41/q05/p1 It ascends from speech to heart presence, intimate converse, witnessing, union and annihilation. — Qāshānī locates these successively in soul, heart, secret, spirit, hidden and Essence. Bursevī says the lower tongue form lacks thought, heart presence, intimacy, witnessing divine lights and inward self-annihilation; people's states vary with their forms. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]
- 33:41/q05/p2 Love of God prompts frequent remembrance, especially inwardly. — Qushayrī reasons that people mention what they love and heart remembrance can continue when ceaseless speech is difficult. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 33:41/q06 How is 'dhikran kathiran' constructed grammatically?  [ذِكْرًۭا كَثِيرًۭا · grammar]
- 33:41/q06/p1 Dhikran is a cognate accusative modified by kathiran. — Jishumī parses it directly. Burhān says a cognate accusative of kind normally takes a descriptive adjective, also possible through an annexed phrase, citing 3:102. Itqān calls this usual for an emphatic accusative and compares gracious release of women. [3 notes: JISHUMI; BURHAN; ITQAN]

## 33:41/q07 What force does the cognate accusative give the command?  [ذِكْرًۭا كَثِيرًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: Lightness of the noun versus continuity suggested by the verb.
- 33:41/q07/p1 The noun is a light way to express emphasis. — Burhān reports this scholarly side of the comparison. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 33:41/q07/p2 A verb may suggest continuity more strongly. — Burhān reports the other scholarly side. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 33:41/q08 Why is remembrance described as 'much'?  [كَثِيرًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes appeal to ease, human forgetfulness, or the scale of gifts and reward.
- 33:41/q08/p1 Nothing impedes remembrance. — Al-Rāzī says that makes 'much' apt. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 33:41/q08/p2 God has many blessings and remembrance brings great reward. — Ibn Kathīr gives the divine gifts, great reward and good return. Qurtubī stresses ease and great reward. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 33:41/q08/p3 Believers may forget God and need a continuing reminder. — Al-Rāzī contrasts the Prophet, who does not forget yet is told in 33:1 to fear God because closeness can diminish fear even among the elect. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 33:41/q09 How does remembrance compare with other deeds?  [ٱذْكُرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ ذِكْرًۭا كَثِيرًۭا · reports]
turns on: The reports make separate comparisons of merit, reward, difficulty and divine love.
- 33:41/q09/p1 It is the best and purest deed, raises ranks and surpasses wealth and battle. — Abū al-Dardāʾ's report ranks it above gold and silver or fighting and dying. Ibn Kathīr cites the prophetic version, Tirmidhī's note that some transmit it as mursal, and a similar Muʿādh route. Ṭabāṭabāʾī repeats it. Muʿādh also calls it the strongest protection from punishment. [6 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(أبو الدرداء,معاذ بن جبل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الترمذي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,معاذ بن جبل); TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]
- 33:41/q09/p2 Frequent rememberers outrank a bloodied fighter whose sword breaks. — Abū Saʿīd's report appears in Durr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī, who names Aḥmad, Tirmidhī and Bayhaqī. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]
- 33:41/q09/p3 The most frequent rememberers earn the greatest reward among fighters and other worshippers. — Muʿādh's report names fasting, prayer, alms, pilgrimage and charity; Abū Bakr tells ʿUmar they have taken all good and the Prophet agrees. Another prophetic answer names them best in a mosque. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(معاذ); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet); TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]
- 33:41/q09/p4 It is among the believer's most severe tests. — Al-Ṣādiq lists it with giving wealth and fairness to oneself. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 33:41/q09/p5 It is the deed most loved by God. — The Prophet tells Umm Anas to leave sins as the best migration, keep obligations as best striving and increase remembrance as most beloved. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أم أنس)]
- 33:41/q09/p6 It is the core of worship and sustains servitude. — Study Quran calls it quintessential prayer; Kuran Yolu calls remembrance and reflection the indispensable energy of servitude. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 33:41/q10 What specific rewards, warnings and responses attach to remembrance?  [ٱذْكُرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ ذِكْرًۭا كَثِيرًۭا · reports]
turns on: The reports give distinct rewards, responses and warnings.
- 33:41/q10/p1 God and angels pray for people who remember Him. — Ṭabarī's Ibn ʿAbbās and Durr connect the command to divine and angelic blessings. Abū Umāma tells a dreamer that angels may pray for them on entering, leaving, standing and sitting if they remember and glorify God, citing 33:41–42. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(سليم بن عامر)]
- 33:41/q10/p2 A dedicated remembrance gathering rises forgiven, with sins made good deeds. — Anas transmits the Prophet's heavenly call. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(أنس بن مالك)]
- 33:41/q10/p3 God is with a servant who remembers Him and moves his lips. — Abū Hurayra reports the Prophet's quotation of God. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو هريرة)]
- 33:41/q10/p4 Remembrance saves from punishment. — Muʿādh says no deed saves more; Gabriel's reported formula promises safety to one God regards. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(معاذ بن جبل); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس)]
- 33:41/q10/p5 The frequent rememberers are the 'solitary ones' and foremost devoted. — Abū Hurayra reports the first designation; Durr's opening report calls to the foremost and likens abundant remembrance to grazing in Paradise's gardens. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة)]
- 33:41/q10/p6 Subḥān Allāh al-ʿAẓīm plants a tree or palm in Paradise. — Durr gathers reports from Muʿādh ibn Anas, Ibn Mardawayh and Jābir; tree and palm vary. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 33:41/q10/p7 Subḥān Allāh wa-bi-ḥamdihi is easy and expiates sins. — Durr quotes the Prophet calling these two words near or accessible; a hundred daily recitations erase sins like sea foam. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 33:41/q10/p8 A hundred glorifications earn a thousand good deeds and erase a thousand sins. — Durr gives the Prophet's answer to whether one can gain a thousand deeds in a day. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 33:41/q10/p9 A gathering without remembrance brings regret on Resurrection Day. — Ibn Kathīr cites the prophetic warning. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 33:41/q10/p10 Failure to remember abundantly disavows faith. — Abū Hurayra transmits the prophetic report in Durr. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة)]
- 33:41/q10/p11 A remembering tongue gains good in this world and the next. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the saying. [1 notes: TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]
- 33:41/q10/p12 Remembrance shields from Satan and supports perseverance. — Iṣlāḥī connects it to prayer and endurance against enemies, citing 2:45. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 33:41/q11 What prophetic advice urges frequent remembrance in practice?  [ٱذْكُرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ ذِكْرًۭا كَثِيرًۭا · reports]
turns on: The reports offer distinct exhortations, circumstances and spoken formulas.
- 33:41/q11/p1 Remember until people call you mad. — The exhortation appears in Thaʿlabī, Qurtubī, Ibn Kathīr, Durr and Study Quran, including Abū Saʿīd routes. [5 notes: THALABI(محمد); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سعيد والنبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]
- 33:41/q11/p2 Remember even if hypocrites accuse you of showing off. — Ibn Kathīr and Durr give Ibn ʿAbbās versions; Durr also gives Abū al-Jawzāʾ's account. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو الجوزاء,ابن عباس)]
- 33:41/q11/p3 Keep the tongue moist with remembrance amid many duties. — Ibn Kathīr's ʿAbd Allāh ibn Busr report begins with the best person being long-lived with good deeds; Study Quran also cites the counsel. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن بسر); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]
- 33:41/q11/p4 Increase remembrance if unable to keep vigil, fight or spend. — Māwardī and Ṭabrisī transmit the Prophet's advice through Ibn ʿAbbās. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI(ابن عباس)]
- 33:41/q11/p5 Ask to be grateful, receptive to counsel, remembering and protective of God's charge. — Ibn Kathīr transmits Abū Hurayra's prayer; Tirmidhī calls it gharīb and Aḥmad has a route through Abū Saʿīd al-Madanī. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو سعيد المدني,أبو هريرة,الترمذي)]
- 33:41/q11/p6 Replace pebble-counted glorifications with 'Allāhu akbar kabīran wa-subḥān Allāhi bukratan wa-aṣīlā'. — Durr reports ʿAbd Allāh's advice to a woman from Hamdān who counted with pebbles or seeds. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(عبد الله)]

## 33:41/q12 Why does the command occur here in the sura?  [* · links]
turns on: The links stress gratitude, a shift of address or the response to the Zaynab dispute.
- 33:41/q12/p1 The preceding Messenger declaration and sura blessings call for thanks. — Jishumī links it to 33:40 and the blessings from the sura's start. Iṣlāḥī says the Prophet's advent is mercy leading from polytheism and unbelief to faith. Biqāʿī ties praise to honoring the truthful Messenger and promised forgiveness. [4 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 33:41/q12/p2 It continues the sura's discipline of the Prophet and now addresses believers. — Al-Rāzī traces duties toward God and family, citing 33:28 and 33:1, then God's command to believers as He commands messengers. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 33:41/q12/p3 It turns believers away from quarrels over the marriage to Zaynab. — Ibn ʿĀshūr urges remembrance instead of insulting hypocrites, avoiding civic strife and retaliation; he compares 2:200 and 6:108, connects the Prophet's instruction in 33:48 and says continued dispute marks disobedient hypocrites. The new address stays contextually connected. Iṣlāḥī says to disregard the uproar and praise God. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 33:41/q12/p4 God remembers needy people despite His independence, so they should remember His favors. — Jishumī includes the blessing of prayer. Ibn Kathīr points to many divine blessings and reward; Qurtubī adds thanks. [3 notes: JISHUMI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 33:41/q13 How does this command relate to the next glorification?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the next command highlights tasbīḥ's excellence or points specifically to prayer.
- 33:41/q13/p1 Both apply morning and evening, with glorification singled out for merit. — Kashshāf compares 'fast and pray on Friday': both share the time. Tasbīḥ frees God's essence from impermissible attributes and acts, with a merit like moral purity. Jishumī defines tasbīḥ and al-Sabūḥ as transcendence, noting two initial vowelings. [4 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 33:41/q13/p2 The next glorification likely includes or signifies prayers. — Kashshāf allows morning-evening glorification to mean all prayers, superior to other deeds. Elmālili says both terms include prayer; Kuran Yolu thinks the following direction probably means prayers. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 33:41/q14 What adjacent contextual details do the sources record?  [* · links]
- 33:41/q14/p1 Zaynab's marriage underscores Muhammad's finality. — Biqāʿī says no man of the community would be a prophet whose son-wife would thereby be forbidden, citing 33:37. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 33:41/q14/p2 Repeated testimony about the Messenger strengthens public announcement. — Burhān relates repetition to God as sufficient witness, citing 4:79, and says it signals abundance. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 33:41/q14/p3 One should not regard one's good work for God as abundant. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports an unattributed gloss of 'do not give in order to receive more.' [1 notes: TABATABAI(غير مسمى)]
- 33:41/q14/p4 Practical advice on eating and cupping precedes the discussion. — Bursevī says fullness causes illness and recommends only a taste if food before cupping is necessary. He advises avoiding intercourse a day and night before and after, and bathing that day. Citing Bustān al-ʿĀrifīn, he advises avoiding salt, head meat and dairy after cupping, recommending vinegar, broth and sweets; he closes by invoking God as healer and sufficiency. [4 notes: BURSEVI]
- 33:41/q14/p5 Other verses discuss remembrance. — Study Quran points to 13:28 and 29:45c; Ibn Kathīr notes many verses, reports and traditions that urge it. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
