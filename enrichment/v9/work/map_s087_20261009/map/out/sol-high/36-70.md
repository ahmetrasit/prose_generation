# 36:70: 18 questions

## 36:70/q01 What does the purpose lām attach to?  [لِّيُنذِرَ · grammar]
turns on: Which preceding clause or implied verb supplies the purpose.
- 36:70/q01/p1 It attaches to the preceding clear Qur’an or reminder. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya attaches the lām to mubīn; Bursawī relates it to Qur’an. Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects it to the reminder and clear Qur’an in 36:69. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الباقون); BURSEVI; TABATABAI]
- 36:70/q01/p2 It depends on an implied verb: the reminder and Qur’an were revealed to warn. — Bursawī supplies the omitted verb of revelation. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 36:70/q01/p3 It attaches to God’s not teaching the Prophet poetry. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this and the attachment to reminder and clear Qur’an have the same upshot. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 36:70/q02 How is لِّيُنذِرَ read?  [لِّيُنذِرَ · readings]
turns on: The verb’s consonant, voice, and vowels, and the transmission of each form.
- 36:70/q02/p1 With third-person yāʾ: li-yundhira, 'that he or it may warn.' — Ibn Mujāhid says all but Nāfiʿ and Ibn ʿĀmir; other lists vary: Ibn ʿAṭiyya places Ibn Kathīr among tāʾ readers, while Ibn al-Jawzī places him among yāʾ readers. The subject may be Qur’an, Prophet, or God. Ibn al-Jazarī also records a nearby yāʾ reading of يقدر by Ruways, with Rawḥ agreeing in al-Aḥqāf; this is contextual and concerns a different verb. [18 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قارئو الياء); IBNMUJAHID(نافع وابن عامر); NAHHAS(أبو عمرو,محمد بن السميفع اليماني); FARISI-HUJJA(نافع); BAGHAWI-FULL(الآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الباقون); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير وعاصم وأبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الباقون); NASAFI-FULL(مدني وشامي وسهل ويعقوب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(نافع وابن عامر وباقي السبعة); SAMIN-DURR(نافع وابن عامر); NASHR(المدنيان وابن عامر ويعقوب,رويس وروح)]
- 36:70/q02/p2 With second-person tāʾ: li-tundhira, addressing Muhammad. — Nāfiʿ and Ibn ʿĀmir are repeatedly named; some lists add Yaʿqūb or the two Medinans, and Ibn ʿAṭiyya names Ibn Kathīr. Abū ʿUbayd prefers it, citing 13:7. Al-Biqāʿī says it marks the Prophet’s unmatched grasp of the Qur’an; Kuran Yolu says the basic sense remains. [22 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قارئو التاء); ZAJJAJ; IBNMUJAHID(نافع وابن عامر); FARISI-HUJJA(نافع); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المدينة والشام ويعقوب); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع وابن كثير); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(نافع وابن عامر ويعقوب); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر); BAYDAWI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر ويعقوب); NASAFI-FULL(مدني وشامي وسهل ويعقوب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(نافع وابن عامر وباقي السبعة); SAMIN-DURR(نافع وابن عامر); NASHR(المدنيان وابن عامر ويعقوب); BIQAI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر ويعقوب); ALUSI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QIRAAT-ER(Imp); NAHHAS(أبو عبيد)+]
- 36:70/q02/p3 A passive form is reported, meaning that a person be warned and take a share of warning. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Muḥammad al-Yamānī with ḍamma on yāʾ and fatḥa on dhāl; Abū Ḥātim allows a passive with two fatḥas. Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī also attribute passive readings to al-Yamānī and al-Jaḥdarī. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(محمد اليماني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(اليماني والجحدري); SAMIN-DURR(الجحدري واليماني); ALUSI-FULL(اليماني)]
- 36:70/q02/p4 A form from nadhira, 'to learn or become aware,' is read with fatḥa on yāʾ and dhāl. — Abū al-Sammāl and al-Yamānī are named; al-Samīn makes man the subject. Al-Qurṭubī reports Ibn al-Sumayqa; Ibn al-Jawzī names Abū al-Mutawakkil, Abū al-Jawzāʾ, and Ibn al-Sumayfaʿ, with fatḥa on dhāl and rāʾ. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(محمد اليماني); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو المتوكل وأبو الجوزاء وابن السميفع); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن السميقع); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو السمال واليماني); SAMIN-DURR(الجحدري واليماني); ALUSI-FULL(أبو السمال واليماني)]

## 36:70/q03 Who is the warner in لِّيُنذِرَ?  [لِّيُنذِرَ · referent]
turns on: The reading and the antecedent chosen for its subject.
- 36:70/q03/p1 The Qur’an or reminder is the warner. — The nearest antecedent favors Qur’an for al-Rāzī, although he favors the Prophet on meaning. Al-Fārisī and al-Wāḥidī treat the yāʾ reading this way. The Qur’an’s warning reaches its hearers. [23 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قارئو الياء); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو عمرو); FARISI-HUJJA; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(الآخرون); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الباقون); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير وعاصم وأبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الباقون); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(مدني وشامي وسهل ويعقوب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(نافع وابن عامر وباقي السبعة); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 36:70/q03/p2 Muhammad is the warner, by direct address or as third-person subject. — The tāʾ reading addresses him. Al-Rāzī prefers his return as yāʾ subject on contextual meaning (36:69). Al-Biqāʿī says the Qur’an forms him and his state conveys it; Muqātil and al-Wāḥidī name his Qur’anic threats. [31 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قارئو التاء); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو عبيد,أبو عمرو); FARISI-HUJJA(نافع); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الباقون,نافع وابن كثير); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(نافع وابن عامر ويعقوب); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الباقون,نافع وابن عامر); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(مدني وشامي وسهل ويعقوب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(نافع وابن عامر وباقي السبعة); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر ويعقوب); ALUSI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QIRAAT-ER(Imp); RAZI-FULL+]
- 36:70/q03/p3 God is the third-person subject. — Al-Naḥḥās, al-Ṭabrisī, and al-Qurṭubī include God among possible subjects of the yāʾ form. [3 notes: NAHHAS(أبو عمرو); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الباقون)]

## 36:70/q04 What does the verb convey here?  [لِّيُنذِرَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the ordinary warning sense or an attested informational use of the root is intended.
- 36:70/q04/p1 It means to warn of divine punishment, especially the Fire. — Yaḥyā identifies the Fire; Muqātil names Qur’anic threats. Al-Ṭabrisī says believers are warned against disobedience. Al-Rāzī specifies punishment for sins and reward for obedience. [5 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL]
- 36:70/q04/p2 It can mean to inform or become aware. — Al-Zajjāj cites nadhartu bi-kadhā like ʿalimtu; al-Naḥḥās reports nadhartu bi-l-qawm, knowing a people and preparing; al-Kashshāf likewise gives 'know.' Variant readings from nadhira support this sense. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو السمال واليماني); SAMIN-DURR(الجحدري واليماني); ALUSI-FULL(أبو السمال واليماني)]

## 36:70/q05 Who is the 'living' person?  [مَن كَانَ حَيًّۭا · referent]
turns on: Whether life is physical, intellectual, faith-filled, foreknown, receptive, or inwardly bestowed.
- 36:70/q05/p1 One with reason, understanding, and discernment, capable of receiving the address. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and a report from ʿAlī support reason; al-Ṭabarī contrasts the understanding heart with dullness. Al-Zajjāj says unbelievers do not reflect or recognize the message; Kuran Yolu includes emotional openness and no deliberate barrier to faith. [24 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(غير مسمى); TAB-FULL(الضحاك); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(الضحاك); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(علي عليه السلام); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 36:70/q05/p2 A believer, whose faith is life and whose heart responds. — Yaḥyā, Ibn ʿAbbās, and others give believer; al-Fārisī cites 16:21 and 6:122, al-Wāḥidī cites 16:26 and 6:122. Al-Baghawī explains that unbelief blocks reflection; al-Ṭabrisī says disbelief makes one worse than dead. [16 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; NAHHAS(الضحاك); FARISI-HUJJA; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي,ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(يحيى بن سلام); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 36:70/q05/p3 Someone God knows will be guided or believe, whether that is outwardly visible yet or not. — Muqātil and al-Suddī frame guidance in divine knowledge; al-Suddī adds that this person accepts the Prophet’s warning. Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursawī say faith causes eternal life; Ibn al-Jawzī connects benefit from warning with 35:18. [15 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي,مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
- 36:70/q05/p4 One alive in heart, sight, and spiritual receptivity. — Qatāda repeatedly says heart and sight; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the contrast metaphorical. Asad and Study Quran describe a heart able to hear and soften; the latter cites 39:23. Bursawī says God’s light turns such a heart toward the afterlife. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 36:70/q05/p5 A heedless person who is to be awakened. — Al-Māwardī attributes this to al-Ḍaḥḥāk. Abū Ḥayyān reports the same but says heedlessness resembles death, and he understands the target as those decreed to faith; Elmalılı speaks of awakening from heedlessness. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); ELMALILI]
- 36:70/q05/p6 Every physically living human being or hearer of the revelation. — Ibn Kathīr cites 6:19 and 11:17 for all living on earth; Study Quran reports the Quran’s reach to all who hear it or receive it, citing 6:19. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 36:70/q05/p7 One given inward life by God, beyond bodily persistence. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says God enables seeing, understanding, hearing, and receiving His greeting; Jaʿfar says the message reaches recipients of His prior gift. Junayd says life through the Creator reaches its origin at death, whereas life through self alone is death. [7 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الجنيد,جعفر); BURSEVI(ابن عطاء,الجنيد); BURHAN]

## 36:70/q06 Why single out the living for warning?  [لِّيُنذِرَ مَن كَانَ حَيًّۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'living' limits delivery of the warning or its effective reception.
- 36:70/q06/p1 The warning benefits those who can receive and answer it. — Jishumī distinguishes listeners from those who turn away. Ibn al-Jawzī invokes those who fear God (35:18). Al-Ālūsī and al-Bayḍāwī identify beneficiaries; Islahi cites 36:11 and 35:22. Bursawī says the warning addresses living and dead hearts, though only living hearts benefit. [9 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 36:70/q06/p2 The Prophet is reassured that he can awaken the morally living while the dead-hearted reject the warning. — Islahi treats this as the verse’s address to the Prophet and a recurring theme; the opening and closing themes of monotheism and Hereafter frame the reassurance. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 36:70/q06/p3 The warning has a universal addressee even though its saving effect is limited. — Ibn Kathīr cites 6:19 and 11:17 for every living being, and Bursawī distinguishes delivery from benefit; Study Quran says it reaches all hearers. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(IK)]

## 36:70/q07 How does 'living' contrast rhetorically with disbelievers?  [حَيًّۭا ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Which faculty or response counts as 'life,' and how the contrast figures disbelief.
- 36:70/q07/p1 Life is a metaphor for intellect, faith, or responsiveness; disbelievers are as dead because they lack reflection or benefit. — Al-Zajjāj says failure to reflect keeps unbelievers from recognizing the Prophet. Al-Ālūsī distinguishes explicit metaphor for reason or faith and a hidden metaphor signaled by another; al-Bayḍāwī calls the disbelievers truly dead for lack of proof and reflection. [27 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو علي الفارسي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 36:70/q07/p2 The beloved's speech itself gives life to a responsive heart. — Al-Burhān links this verse with the call to what gives life: the hearer lives by responding to the speech. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 36:70/q08 What does the past tense of كَانَ signify?  [كَانَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the past form marks divine certainty, future sense, or a future event cast as past.
- 36:70/q08/p1 It expresses what is already certain in God’s knowledge. — Al-Ālūsī favors this construal. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]
- 36:70/q08/p2 It can stand for future time or present a prospective event as though past. — Al-Ālūsī records both alternatives but dismisses them. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 36:70/q09 Why is the living person singular and the disbelievers plural?  [مَن كَانَ حَيًّۭا ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ · rhetoric]
- 36:70/q09/p1 The singular follows man’s form and suggests the few fortunate; the plural follows its sense and indicates the many wretched. — Al-Biqāʿī supplies both grammatical and numerical implications. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 36:70/q10 What is 'the word' that becomes due?  [ٱلْقَوْلُ · referent]
turns on: Whether qawl denotes a penal sentence, established proof, wrath, foundational truths, or the earlier promise.
- 36:70/q10/p1 God’s sentence or decree of punishment, including the threat of Hell. — Muqātil links it to rejecting oneness; al-Rāzī cites 32:13, 39:71, and 17:15 and notes judgment after a messenger. Bursawī cites the decree to fill Hell (32:13). Asad ties its fulfillment on Judgment Day to 36:7. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 36:70/q10/p2 The proof or case established against disbelievers. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī understand the due word as a binding proof; al-Naḥḥās presents this as another parse. Ibn Kathīr says the Qur’an is mercy to believers and conclusive proof to unbelievers; Islahi says rejecters have no excuse. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 36:70/q10/p3 The wrath of God. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām glosses qawl as wrath. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM]
- 36:70/q10/p4 The doctrinal statements of divine oneness, prophethood, resurrection, and other foundations, supported by the Qur’an’s proofs. — Al-Rāzī gives this alternative to the punishment sentence. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 36:70/q10/p5 God’s earlier promise to Adam’s progeny to provide guidance and punish followers of Satan. — Islahi identifies this promise as the conclusive word. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 36:70/q11 Why does the word become due against disbelievers?  [وَيَحِقَّ ٱلْقَوْلُ عَلَى ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause focuses on the guilty conduct, the completed proof, or both.
- 36:70/q11/p1 They reject God, His messenger, and the guidance despite warning; their deeds and persistent unbelief warrant punishment. — Al-Ṭabarī names turning away and rejection; Qatāda cites deeds. Al-Rāzī relates punishment to a messenger’s arrival (17:15); Ibn Kathīr cites 11:17. Bursawī and Kuran Yolu stress obstinacy once excuses are removed. [18 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن الحاجب); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 36:70/q11/p2 The Qur’an establishes proof against them, leaving them without excuse. — Al-Zajjāj describes proof established; al-Wāḥidī says the Qur’an makes it binding. Ibn Kathīr contrasts mercy for believers with proof against disbelievers; Islahi says the truth is conclusively conveyed. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 36:70/q12 What reciprocal omissions shape the two clauses?  [مَن كَانَ حَيًّۭا ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ · rhetoric]
- 36:70/q12/p1 Faith is understood with the living from the opposing unbelievers, and death with unbelievers from the opposing living. — Al-Biqāʿī calls this ellipsis by reciprocal implication. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 36:70/q13 How does the verse connect with the sūra and nearby verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Which earlier or neighboring passage supplies the operative connection.
- 36:70/q13/p1 Its warning returns to the sūra’s opening aim and verdict. — Al-Ālūsī cites 36:6; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the receptive and rejecters echo the opening. Asad and Study Quran direct the due word back to 36:7. Islahi says the close resumes monotheism and the Hereafter. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 36:70/q13/p2 The closing passage moves from who benefits from the Book to providential signs, gratitude, and entrusting mockery to God. — Islahi describes the sequence of the closing section. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 36:70/q13/p3 The transition from resurrection to Qur’an and warning is especially fitting. — Al-Ālūsī calls this turn strikingly apt. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 36:70/q13/p4 Earlier descriptions of responsive hearers clarify the living. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites 35:18; Islahi cites 36:11 and 35:22. Al-Fārisī cites 16:21 and 6:122; al-Wāḥidī has 16:26 and 6:122. [5 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(يحيى بن سلام); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 36:70/q14 What does the verse show about the purpose of Qur’anic warning as a whole?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the emphasis is the universal reach or the differing outcomes for recipients.
- 36:70/q14/p1 Revelation warns receptive people and establishes the case or punishment against rejecters. — Al-Wāḥidī and Ibn Kathīr present the two outcomes together. Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees the same dual aim echoed at the sūra’s opening; Islahi says conveying truth removes excuses. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 36:70/q14/p2 The Qur’an’s warning is universal in reach. — Ibn Kathīr cites 6:19 and 11:17; Study Quran reports the same universal reading. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK)]

## 36:70/q15 How do cited discussions distinguish Qur’anic expression from poetry and explain figurative language?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the point concerns fidelity to meaning, figurative transfer, or wording within composition.
- 36:70/q15/p1 Qur’anic expression follows serious intended meaning; rhyme and meter can force weak or false expression. — Al-Biqāʿī contrasts revelation with poetry and says the Prophet never chooses meter over meaning. The Ḥassān and Saʿd episode illustrates a name forced by rhyme; Ibn al-Ḥājib says poetry by the Prophet would invite suspicion about revelation. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL(حسان بن ثابت); ALUSI-FULL(ابن الحاجب); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة)]
- 36:70/q15/p2 Metaphor changes the expressed judgment through an unstated conceptual analogy, while literalness depends on expression, not truth. — Al-Jurjānī uses 'spring fashioned/wove' to distinguish metaphor from a spoken simile; a false assertion may remain literal. He invokes God’s creation and the fall from heaven in 22:31 as examples of firm truth and denial. [6 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 36:70/q15/p3 Rhetorical merit lies in wording that most precisely and powerfully conveys its intended meaning. — Al-Jurjānī says arrangement attracts the hearer; outside composition isolated words differ chiefly in familiarity and sound, illustrated by layth and asad. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 36:70/q16 What principle governs acceptance of transmitted readings when grammar seems weak?  [* · readings]
- 36:70/q16/p1 A widely transmitted early reading remains valid despite a weaker parse or analogy. — Ibn Jinnī notes grammatically weak readings among the Seven, citing 10:5 and 6:137; he says habitual reception outweighs a rival reading’s grammatical advantage. [2 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB]

## 36:70/q17 What contextual reports accompany discussions of this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: The different reports concern separate illustrations or transmission contexts.
- 36:70/q17/p1 Muhammad’s letter to Chosroes invited him to Islam using the warning to the living and judgment against disbelievers. — Al-Bāqillānī reproduces the letter. [1 notes: BAQILLANI]
- 36:70/q17/p2 Ḥassān’s rhyme at al-Ghāba forced a name and angered Saʿd ibn Zayd al-Ashhalī. — Al-Biqāʿī reports that Saʿd gave his horses to al-Miqdād and vowed not to speak to Ḥassān, who explained the rhyme. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(حسان بن ثابت)]
- 36:70/q17/p3 Abū ʿAlī had intended a book on readings but upheaval prevented it. — Ibn Jinnī relates the abandoned plan and contrasts Abū ʿAlī’s freedom from worry with his own burdens and gratitude. [2 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB]

## 36:70/q18 How do the cited authors describe their handling of readings and adjacent spiritual instruction?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes supply different ancillary reflections rather than a direct contest.
- 36:70/q18/p1 An argument about readings should remain accessible while recognizing harder grammatical treatments. — Ibn Jinnī says his account is for readers of qirāʾāt and contrasts Abū ʿAlī’s al-Ḥujja, which exceeds many readers’ needs. [1 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB]
- 36:70/q18/p2 Waking, ablution, and prayer can figure inner vigilance, repentance, and turning toward God. — Bursawī reports his shaykh’s sequence: waking is first heart awakening, ablution repentance, prayer entry to the spiritual realm, and bodily movements carry blessings. [1 notes: BURSEVI(حضرة شيخي وسندي)]
