# 94:3: 18 questions

## 94:3/q01 What does anqaḍa mean?  [أَنقَضَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb names the weight and weakness, its audible effect, or the underlying giving way.
- 94:3/q01/p1 It made the back heavy, weak, thin, or exhausted under a load. — Mujāhid, Qatāda, Ibn Zayd and the exegetes gloss it as heaviness. Al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Zayd, Jishumī, and Elmalılı cite a camel worn down by travel; Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds thinning or impairment. Jishumī compares naqḍ with dismantling a structure. [23 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB(ابن زيد); NAHHAS(أهل التفسير); MAWARDI(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); JISHUMI(الحسن ومجاهد وقتادة وابن زيد); QURTUBI(السدي); IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR(غير واحد من السلف); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI(Bazıları,İbn Cerîr); WAHIDI-QT; BINTSHATI+]
- 94:3/q01/p2 It made an audible creak or crack under strain. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Farrāʾ, al-Zajjāj, and others describe the back or its load making a faint sound. Al-Wāḥidī cites grief creaking the ribs, a cupping glass, a nestling, and Dhū al-Rumma's chicks. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn cite ʿAbbās ibn Mirdās; al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn cite Jamīl's creaking ropes. Others compare a camel, saddle, ship, pot, or joints. [46 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ذو الرمة); JISHUMI(الفراء); BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA(عباس بن مرداس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عباس بن مرداس); TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI(علماء اللغة); RAZI-FULL(علماء اللغة); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL(جميل); BAYDAWI; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(أهل اللغة,جميل,عباس بن مرداس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أهل اللغة,عباس بن مرداس وجميل); SAMIN-DURR(العباس بن مرداس,جميل); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Bazıları,Râzî); BINTSHATI(أبو حيان,الزمخشري); STUDYQURAN(al-Bayḍāwī); BINTSHATI(الراغب)-]
- 94:3/q01/p3 Its primary sense is a structure or back giving way, loosening, or splitting; the sound follows from that. — Al-Rāghib distinguishes the underlying collapse from the noise. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers this sense using Qurʾānic naqḍ for undoing in 16:92, 2:27, 8:56, 13:20, 13:25, 4:150, 5:13, and 16:91. Jishumī pairs naqḍ with hadm. [3 notes: BINTSHATI(الراغب); JISHUMI; BINTSHATI+]

## 94:3/q02 How does anqaḍa govern 'your back' and attribute the effect to the burden?  [أَنقَضَ ظَهْرَكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the creak is assigned syntactically to the back through a causative verb or to the load at the location of the back.
- 94:3/q02/p1 The verb is causative and transitive; the burden is its figurative agent and the back its object. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the base verb is intransitive and hamza makes it transitive. Al-Ālūsī calls attribution of the creak to the burden an attribution of an effect to its cause. Ibn ʿĀshūr pictures the overburdened carrier's bones creaking. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 94:3/q02/p2 The back is a locative with an understood fī; the burden itself creaks on the Prophet's back. — Elmalılı prefers this parsing as more natural than assigning the sound to the back and rejects forcing the verb into a transitive sense. [2 notes: ELMALILI+]

## 94:3/q03 Why portray the burden as making the back creak?  [ٱلَّذِىٓ أَنقَضَ ظَهْرَكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the picture is taken as a figure for crushing distress or emphasizes the actual weight and consequences of an obligation.
- 94:3/q03/p1 The back-crushing image makes the burden's exceptional weight felt through an extended metaphor or comparison. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Farrāʾ, al-Zajjāj, Ibn al-Jawzī and others call the sound image figurative. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it an elaborate compound comparison, with separately analyzable elements, and says the relative description completes the image of immensity. Al-Ālūsī treats setting down the burden as an extension of the illustrative metaphor; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the paired images intensify oppression. [15 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI(الفراء); TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI(الشيخ محمد عبده); ELMALILI]
- 94:3/q03/p2 The wording points to an actual burden of responsibility and its possible legal consequence. — Biqāʿī reads the weight literally and also as what would follow from neglecting an onerous obligation; he says God protected the Prophet from that. [1 notes: BIQAI]

## 94:3/q04 What does ẓahraka contribute to the image?  [ظَهْرَكَ · meaning]
- 94:3/q04/p1 The back is the body's support, whose joints or bones seem to creak under strain. — Biqāʿī calls the back the body's support and compares a loaded new saddle. Ibn ʿĀshūr imagines the overburdened carrier's bones creaking. [2 notes: BIQAI; IBNASHUR]

## 94:3/q05 How should the adjacent ḍād and ẓāʾ be pronounced?  [أَنقَضَ ظَهْرَكَ · readings]
- 94:3/q05/p1 Preserve distinct ḍād and ẓāʾ sounds, despite the slight weight of their adjacency. — Ibn al-Jazarī says ḍād lengthens along its articulation and must not become ẓāʾ, mixed dhāl, emphatic lām, or zāy; this verse is a key adjacency example. Ibn ʿĀshūr says required adjacency remains eloquent, cites 76:26 and 25:2, and notes prayer validity disputes over faulty pronunciation. [5 notes: IBNASHUR; NASHR+]
- 94:3/q05/p2 The claim that the Prophet was the most eloquent speaker of ḍād is unsound. — Ibn al-Jazarī says the popular saying has no basis. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 94:3/q06 Which reading variants are reported for the verse's surrounding burden expression?  [* · readings]
turns on: The transmitted substitute wording and its attributed reader.
- 94:3/q06/p1 Ibn Masʿūd or ʿAbd Allāh is reported to read 'we relieved/lifted your burden from you.' — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports Mujāhid's attribution to ʿAbd Allāh; al-Qurṭubī names Ibn Masʿūd. Al-Ālūsī gives the wording wa-ḥalalnā ʿanka wiqraka. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); QURTUBI(عبد الله بن مسعود); ALUSI(ابن مسعود); ALUSI-FULL]
- 94:3/q06/p2 Anas is reported to read ḥaṭaṭnā or ḥalalnā instead of waḍaʿnā. — Al-Ālūsī reports both verb substitutions. [2 notes: ALUSI(أنس); ALUSI-FULL]

## 94:3/q07 What was the burden that weighed on the Prophet?  [ٱلَّذِىٓ أَنقَضَ ظَهْرَكَ · referent]
turns on: Which experience is understood as the oppressive load, and whether it denotes actual sin, protected innocence, the mission, or distress about the Prophet and his people.
- 94:3/q07/p1 His own sins or earlier lapses, removed by forgiveness. — Al-Kalbī, Qatāda, Mujāhid, al-Ḥasan and al-Ḍaḥḥāk report sins; al-Ṭabarī connects forgiveness with days of ignorance. Ibn Zayd places them before prophethood; al-Wāḥidī, Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Ālūsī and Asad specify pre-mission acts or mistakes. Some qualify them as minor sins, errors, or inadvertence; al-Ḥusayn ibn al-Mufaḍḍal specifies the latter. Elmalılı cites 6:31 and 48:2 for this reading, but rejects its fit with prophetic minor lapses. [36 notes: FARRA(الكلبي); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB(ابن زيد,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); QURTUBI(الحسين بن المفضل,قتادة والحسن والضحاك); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI(أصحاب جواز الصغائر,قتادة); DURR(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Bir kısım müfessirîn); ASAD-NOTES(Tabari); BINTSHATI(الطبري,قتادة,مفسرون); ELMALILI-]
- 94:3/q07/p2 The responsibilities of prophethood, revelation, warning, and conveying the message. — Al-Māwardī says the message weighed until delivery; al-Kisāʾī, Juwaybir from al-Ḍaḥḥāk, ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz ibn Yaḥyā and Abū ʿUbayda describe duties, preservation and rights made manageable. Biqāʿī stresses warning people who resented criticism of their faith and ancestors; 5:67 promises protection. Qutb says God opened hearts and sent clarifying revelation. Elmalılı adds fear of failure, 35:28, Gabriel's awe in 53:5–6, hostility, and eventual habituation. Qāshānī gives an inward reading: return from exclusive witnessing to prophetic commands nearly broke the heart's veil, until abiding empowered unity amid multiplicity. [30 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكسائي); BAGHAWI(عبد العزيز بن يحيى وأبو عبيدة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عبد العزيز بن يحيى); RAZI; QURTUBI(عبد العزيز بن يحيى وأبو عبيدة); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN(some); BINTSHATI(مفسرون); ELMALILI+]
- 94:3/q07/p3 His bewilderment and lack of a clear path before revelation. — Rāzī and Elmalılı describe a conscience troubled by God's unrepayable gifts without knowledge of how to serve Him; service through prophethood eased the shame. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī mention perplexity, including the mission's reception. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ agrees with ʿAbduh, linking confusion to 93:7. Iṣlāḥī adds unanswered truth-seeking and delayed revelation. [12 notes: RAZI; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI(الشيخ محمد عبده,مفسرون); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BINTSHATI+]
- 94:3/q07/p4 Distress over his people's error, idolatry, and refusal of guidance. — Rāzī cites 16:123: Abraham's religion was being changed before Muhammad had power to intervene. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī describe inability to guide the resistant; al-Ālūsī names Ḥamza and ʿUmar's conversion as relief. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ corrects al-Ḍaḥḥāk's 'shirk' to the people's shirk; ʿAbduh calls the concern psychologically crushing. Iṣlāḥī cites 20:1–3 about preaching not intended to distress; Kur'an Yolu favors relief through revelation and social support. [13 notes: RAZI; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI(الشيخ محمد عبده,الضحاك,مفسرون); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 94:3/q07/p5 Persecution, insults, and threats directed at him by his people. — Rāzī says God strengthened him to bear even a bloodied face, when he prayed for their guidance. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī mention persistent abuse. Biqāʿī recounts fears of his head being split, relatives restraining his preaching, and divine support under 5:67. Elmalılı cites 5:67 and 93:4–5 for protection or hope. [12 notes: RAZI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 94:3/q07/p6 The sins of his community, borne through concern for them. — Rāzī says reassurance came through 8:33 and promised intercession. Al-Qurṭubī says their sins occupied his heart. Al-Ālūsī reports removal as withholding immediate punishment while the Prophet remained or they sought forgiveness, but calls the interpretation remote. [3 notes: RAZI; QURTUBI; ALUSI; ALUSI(بعض)-; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]
- 94:3/q07/p7 He was protected from sin and pre-revelation impurity, so no actual sin burden existed. — Al-Muḥāsibī says the burden would have weighed had God not pardoned him. Rāzī illustrates protection by the wedding story: God put him to sleep before he could join music. Al-Qurṭubī says he was pure for forty years; Abū Ḥayyān calls the apparent removal a hyperbolic figure for purification. Al-Rāghib says he was exempted from pre-Islamic customs; al-Ālūsī reports Abū Ḥayyān's view. [9 notes: QURTUBI(المحاسبي); RAZI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI(أبو حيان); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); BINTSHATI(أبو حيان,الراغب)]
- 94:3/q07/p8 His ignorance of revealed rulings before God taught them. — Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Ālūsī, and later survey notes distinguish this absence of legal knowledge from uncertainty about the mission; revelation removed it. [5 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI(مفسرون)]
- 94:3/q07/p9 The initial terror and weight of Gabriel's appearance or reception of revelation. — Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī describe trembling and fear at the first encounter, even a near leap from a mountain; Gabriel's appearance relieved fear that his reason had changed. Elmalılı invokes the mighty angel in 53:5–6, then gradual adaptation. [8 notes: RAZI; QURTUBI; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 94:3/q07/p10 Grief after the deaths of Abū Ṭālib and Khadīja. — Rāzī and al-Ālūsī condition this reading on the sūra's revelation after their deaths; ascension, angels' greeting, raised renown, and beholding the Lord are described as consolation. [4 notes: RAZI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 94:3/q07/p11 Blessings that required gratitude. — Al-Māwardī lists gifts as a possible weight until the Prophet gave thanks. Elmalılı notes the shame of enjoying gifts without a way to serve the giver. [5 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI; ELMALILI]
- 94:3/q07/p12 The pause in revelation and the resulting loneliness and mockery. — Elmalılı says interruption increased worldly and otherworldly anxiety; resumed revelation and al-Ḍuḥā brought relief. [2 notes: ELMALILI]
- 94:3/q07/p13 Practical insecurity and livelihood that distracted from reform. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says God removed part of the burden through guidance to truth and provision for livelihood, citing 93:7–8. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 94:3/q08 Why could forgiven sins still weigh so heavily?  [أَنقَضَ ظَهْرَكَ · theology]
turns on: Whether the continuing weight comes from remorse, uncertain completion of repentance, or the cost in reward.
- 94:3/q08/p1 The Prophet and the righteous deeply regret and grieve even over forgiven lapses. — Al-Naḥḥās, al-Wāḥidī, al-Muḥāsibī and al-Qurṭubī stress continuing sorrow. Rāzī attributes intense remorse to those who allow minor prophetic sins. Elmalılı reports the alternative that exceptional sensitivity makes even a small lapse heavy. [7 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI(أصحاب جواز الصغائر); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL(المحاسبي); ELMALILI]
- 94:3/q08/p2 The righteous fear they have not yet fulfilled every duty of complete repentance. — Al-Naḥḥās offers this as another explanation for the burden's persistence. [1 notes: NAHHAS]
- 94:3/q08/p3 Even a minor sin could forfeit a great reward. — Rāzī records this separate defense of calling the lapse weighty. [1 notes: RAZI]

## 94:3/q09 Does the burden wording establish a view on prophetic impeccability?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the image presupposes committed sin or is a rhetorical way to describe protection.
- 94:3/q09/p1 It may describe actual minor sin and forgiveness, which is a divine favor. — Rāzī reports the permissive view but objects to the Qāḍī's claim that forgiveness of minor sins is obligatory for God: a mandatory pardon could not be praised as a favor. Elmalılı argues the emphatic crushing image is disproportionate to minor prophetic lapses. [2 notes: RAZI(أصحاب جواز الصغائر,القاضي); ELMALILI-]
- 94:3/q09/p2 The metaphor can describe divine prevention of sin without prior guilt; the wording alone settles no impeccability doctrine. — Abū Ḥayyān treats removal as a hyperbolic statement of protection and purification. Al-Ālūsī reports this and explicitly says the verse establishes no position on impeccability or proof of earlier lack of protection. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI(أبو حيان); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]

## 94:3/q10 How was the burden lifted or made bearable?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The answer follows which burden is identified.
- 94:3/q10/p1 God forgave prior sins or prevented a burden of sin. — Al-Ṭabarī, Qatāda, Ibn Zayd and others identify forgiveness; al-Muḥāsibī and Abū Ḥayyān frame relief as pardon or prior protection. The wedding report has God make the Prophet sleep. [7 notes: TAB(ابن زيد); QURTUBI(المحاسبي); RAZI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 94:3/q10/p2 God eased the prophetic mission through revelation, growing familiarity, opened hearts, and strength to endure. — Al-Wāḥidī says duties and rights became manageable; Qutb names opened hearts, revelation's truth, and gentler delivery. Elmalılı describes habituation, insight, spiritual strength and al-Ḍuḥā; al-Ālūsī notes practice. Biqāʿī invokes signs, miracles and 5:67; his account of Gabriel cleansing the black clot and filling the heart with knowledge, faith and wisdom explains capacity. Qāshānī's inward account names empowerment in abiding. [15 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكسائي); QUTB-ZILAL; ELMALILI; ALUSI; BIQAI(ابن عباس); QASHANI]
- 94:3/q10/p3 God taught religious law and gave direction for service. — Al-Ālūsī says revealed knowledge ended its absence. Rāzī and Elmalılı say revelation and duty answered the Prophet's question of how to repay blessings and follow Abraham, citing 16:123. [5 notes: ALUSI; RAZI; ELMALILI]
- 94:3/q10/p4 God guided some of his people and supported the establishment of monotheism and virtue. — Al-Ālūsī names Ḥamza and ʿUmar's conversion. Kur'an Yolu mentions justice, honesty, mercy and cooperation; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 93:7–8 for guidance and provision. [4 notes: ALUSI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 94:3/q10/p5 He was consoled by ascension and the raising of his renown. — Rāzī and al-Ālūsī connect this conditionally to the deaths of Abū Ṭālib and Khadīja; the angels greeted him, and al-Ālūsī adds beholding his Lord. [3 notes: RAZI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 94:3/q10/p6 God reassured him about the community and withheld immediate punishment. — Rāzī mentions later intercession and 8:33. Al-Ālūsī says no punishment while he remains among them or they seek forgiveness, but judges this interpretation remote. [4 notes: RAZI; ALUSI(بعض); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 94:3/q11 What do the nearby words for removing a burden and the burden itself convey?  [* · meaning]
- 94:3/q11/p1 Waḍʿ evokes putting down a heavy load and thus relief. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ compares war's burdens, weapons, shackles and this verse, citing 47:4, 4:102 and 7:157. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 94:3/q11/p2 Wizr can be mountain, refuge, shared load, physical weight, or moral sin. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ traces wazīr as a load-sharer and cites 75:11, 20:29, 25:35, 20:87, 47:4, 6:31, 6:164, 35:18, 39:7, 16:25, 20:100, 17:15 and 53:38. Al-Qurṭubī glosses burden as a heavy load and explains bawānī al-zawr as chest foundations. [2 notes: BINTSHATI; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 94:3/q12 How does this verse relate to the sūra's nearby favors and to al-Ḍuḥā?  [* · links]
turns on: Which earlier and later gifts the commentator sees the burden removal completing.
- 94:3/q12/p1 The relief here belongs to a sequence of chest expansion, burden removal, raised renown, and assurance after hardship. — Al-Ḥasan pairs an expanded breast of forbearance and knowledge with the heavy burden and raised remembrance. Al-Ālūsī notices the conjunction and added laka in raising renown after burden removal. Elmalılı says the Qurʾān raised the Prophet's standing; Iṣlāḥī tracks expansion, relief, mission completion and disciplined success. [4 notes: DURR-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 94:3/q12/p2 Al-Sharḥ continues al-Ḍuḥā's enumeration of favors, including a move from external or acquired blessings to spiritual or original gifts. — Ibn al-Zubayr says the chapters separately enumerate blessings that could seem earned before gifts preceding existence; Biqāʿī likens it to a parent describing present care then earlier preparation, and compares Zechariah in 19:7,9. Asad says revelation followed nearly at once. Study Quran distinguishes external needs in 93 from spiritual relief in 94; Iṣlāḥī sees earlier shelter in 93:6 and blessings after Islam spread, with predictions now materializing. [9 notes: BIQAI(الأستاذ أبو جعفر ابن الزبير); BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 94:3/q12/p3 The relief can be read alongside 93:7–8 as guidance after bewilderment and provision after need. — Asad and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ link the former to mistakes or confusion before prophethood; Ibn ʿĀshūr points to guidance and livelihood as relief. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES; BINTSHATI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 94:3/q13 Was al-Sharḥ a separate sūra, and when and for whom was it revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The transmission about its unity with sūra 93 and competing chronological reports.
- 94:3/q13/p1 It follows al-Ḍuḥā closely and addresses the Prophet first, then Qurʾān followers. — Asad dates it almost immediately after al-Ḍuḥā and extends the address through the Prophet to true followers. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 94:3/q13/p2 The two chapters were treated as one by some reciters. — Asad attributes this to Ṭāwūs and ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz, citing Rāzī; Study Quran says some Companions recited both in one prayer cycle without a second basmala. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Ta'us ibn Kaysan and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz); STUDYQURAN]
- 94:3/q13/p3 Most call it Makkan after sūra 93; Ibn ʿAbbās calls it Madinan, and some assign only verses 5–6 to Madinah. — Study Quran reports the competing place assignments. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(most commentators)]

## 94:3/q14 What reports or comparisons are cited to illuminate the Prophet's preparation and the sūra's favors?  [* · reports]
- 94:3/q14/p1 Gabriel opened and washed the Prophet's chest, then filled it with knowledge, faith and wisdom. — Biqāʿī reports Ibn ʿAbbās's black-clot account and infers that it equipped the Prophet to bear what others could not. [1 notes: BIQAI(ابن عباس)]
- 94:3/q14/p2 A report likens reciting the sūra to visiting the distressed Prophet and relieving him. — Bayḍāwī reports this statement attributed to the Prophet. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 94:3/q14/p3 A report in which the Prophet asks why others got special favors is criticized. — The Qāḍī says prophets ask only by permission and that God's reply in the report sounds reproachful. [0 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي)-]

## 94:3/q15 How do the neighboring opening expressions explain the Prophet's inward relief?  [* · interpretation]
- 94:3/q15/p1 The chest is expanded when Satan's whispers and constricting emotions give way to good impulses. — Rāzī locates whispering in the chest and cites 114:5. Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī al-Tirmidhī says Satan brings worry, grief and greed into the chest, the heart's guard; early resistance restores ease. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(محمد بن علي الترمذي)]
- 94:3/q15/p2 The phrase 'for you' may signal reciprocity of worship and divine favor or the Prophet's own gain from his mission. — Rāzī reports both explanations. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 94:3/q15/p3 The divine 'We' either magnifies the gift or includes angelic aid. — Rāzī gives both senses, with angels strengthening the Prophet and frightening opponents. Corpus Coranicum notes the direct divine plural as in 97:1 and 108:1, and possible Psalm 4:2 imagery of room made in distress. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 94:3/q16 What other recitation and linguistic examples accompany discussion of this verse?  [* · readings]
- 94:3/q16/p1 In a sākin ṭāʾ followed by tāʾ, assimilation remains incomplete to preserve emphasis. — Ibn al-Jazarī calls ṭāʾ the strongest emphatic letter. [1 notes: NASHR]
- 94:3/q16/p2 A sākin ẓāʾ before tāʾ is normally clear, though Ibn Muḥayṣin assimilated it while retaining emphasis. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports the imams' agreement on clarity and Ibn Muḥayṣin's variant. [2 notes: NASHR(ابن محيصن)]
- 94:3/q16/p3 Preserve ʿayn before a voiceless letter and distinguish it from ghayn; keep ghayn clear, especially near qāf. — Ibn al-Jazarī warns against emphatic ʿayn and against moving ghayn in lā tuzigh qulūbanā. [2 notes: NASHR]
- 94:3/q16/p4 The poetic expression qaḍḍihā bi-qaḍīḍihā denotes a mass rush and illustrates Arabic case analogy. — Sībawayh cites al-Shammākh and notes some Arabs inflect qaḍḍihim like kullihim. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 94:3/q17 What does the cited Arabic analogy say about waḥdahu and similar accusatives?  [* · grammar]
- 94:3/q17/p1 Hijazi Arabic uses the accusative for exclusive waḥdahu and group forms, whereas Banū Tamīm may match the preceding noun's case. — Sībawayh distinguishes dialects. Al-Khalīl says accusative thalāthatuhum restricts to those three, while genitive can generalize like kulluhum. He models waḥdahu and khamstahum on the verbal noun ifrādan, which Sībawayh calls analogy rather than attested speech; Sībawayh derives waḥdahu from tafarrud and distinguishes exclusive accusative from inclusive genitive. [4 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]

## 94:3/q18 What broader claims appear alongside this verse's discussion?  [* · interpretation]
- 94:3/q18/p1 The Qurʾān's unanswered challenge demonstrates its inimitability. — Al-Suyūṭī argues Quraysh's eloquent speakers and poets could have answered with a short sūra more cheaply than war but did not for twenty-three years. [1 notes: ITQAN]
