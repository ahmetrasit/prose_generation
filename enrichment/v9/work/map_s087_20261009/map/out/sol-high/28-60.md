# 28:60: 24 questions

## 28:60/q01 How does the opening conjunction compare with the parallel wording in al-Shura?  [وَمَآ · rhetoric]
- 28:60/q01/p1 Al-Qasas opens with waw, whereas the parallel in al-Shura opens with fa. — Al-Burhan cites this as an example of one particle being substituted for another. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 28:60/q02 Who is addressed, and what are they given?  [وَمَآ أُوتِيتُم مِّن شَىْءٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether the general address is stated as applying to all recipients or specified by the surrounding address to Meccan opponents.
- 28:60/q02/p1 The address concerns people generally and the beneficial worldly means, wealth, children, and pleasant goods they receive. — Al-Tabari names wealth and children; al-Baydawi says worldly means; Ibn Ashur says the context restricts the general wording to beneficial things because giving normally means granting a benefit. Al-Tabatabai glosses ita as giving, and Abu Hayyan specifies pleasant gifts in which people boast. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 28:60/q02/p2 The direct addressees are Meccan unbelievers, especially Quraysh and its leaders, and the worldly goods in which they take pride. — Muqatil and al-Wahidi specify Meccan unbelievers and wealth or good that ends with their lives; Ibn Atiyya names wealth, sons, and strength; al-Qurtubi says either the goods or their owners depart; al-Wasit as reported by Bursevi calls the gifts worldly means. Islahi identifies the leaders, and Kuranyolu calls them Meccan polytheists. [7 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي ومقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(الوسيط); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 28:60/q03 What does min shay add to the opening expression?  [مِّن شَىْءٍۢ · grammar]
- 28:60/q03/p1 It clarifies the general ma and extends the expression to everything given in the context. — Ibn Ashur says the context concerns beneficial things; al-Tabatabai says min shay clarifies ma and generalizes it. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 28:60/q04 How do the first ma and the fa in its predicate work?  [وَمَآ أُوتِيتُم مِّن شَىْءٍۢ فَمَتَٰعُ · grammar]
- 28:60/q04/p1 The first ma is a subject with conditional force, so its predicate takes fa; the later ma is different. — Bursevi explicitly distinguishes the two instances of ma. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 28:60/q05 Why does the wording turn to direct address?  [أُوتِيتُم · rhetoric]
- 28:60/q05/p1 The prior third-person discussion becomes direct address to reproach the opponents face to face. — Ibn Ashur connects the turn to the preceding speech and 28:57. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:60/q06 What does mataʿ mean here?  [مَتَٰعُ · meaning]
- 28:60/q06/p1 It is something used or enjoyed for a time and then lost. — Ibn Ashur defines it by temporary enjoyment; al-Tabatabai calls it what one enjoys; Bursevi likens it to household goods and says its name signals that it does not last. Al-Alusi says the noun signals brevity, reinforced by the contrast with abqa. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 28:60/q07 How is mataʿ al-hayat al-dunya read and parsed?  [فَمَتَٰعُ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا · readings]
turns on: The nominative predicate versus the reported accusative verbal noun and temporal accusative.
- 28:60/q07/p1 The nominative mataʿ is the predicate: it is an enjoyment of worldly life. — Al-Samin supplies the implied pronoun huwa before mataʿ. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 28:60/q07/p2 A variant accusative mataʿan treats it as a verbal noun and life as the time during which they enjoy it. — Abu Hayyan reports the variant with al-hayata as a temporal accusative; al-Samin and al-Alusi likewise parse mataʿan as a verbal noun and life as a time expression. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 28:60/q08 What is said about the name al-dunya?  [ٱلدُّنْيَا · meaning]
- 28:60/q08/p1 This life is near and low, short and finite beside the other life. — Sufyan says it is called dunya because it is near. Al-Biqai says the description as near and low implies the afterlife; al-Alusi says it suggests lowliness and short duration; al-Tabatabai contrasts its nearness and finite span with the eternal afterlife. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سفيان); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 28:60/q09 What does zina mean?  [وَزِينَتُهَا · meaning]
- 28:60/q09/p1 It is an added beauty or ornament of bodies and other things. — Ibn Ashur says it beautifies bodies or things; al-Tabatabai calls it an addition that makes something beautiful. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 28:60/q10 Why does this verse mention adornment in the parallel expression absent from al-Shura?  [وَزِينَتُهَا · rhetoric]
- 28:60/q10/p1 Adornments fit al-Qasas because Qarun later appears before his people in his finery; al-Shura has no comparable scene. — Al-Samarrai and the Maʿtarak al-aqran report both point to Qarun's appearance at 28:79. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR(معترك الأقران)]

## 28:60/q11 What does the verse say about worldly enjoyment and adornment?  [فَمَتَٰعُ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا وَزِينَتُهَا · interpretation]
- 28:60/q11/p1 Worldly goods give temporary enjoyment and beauty during mortal life, then perish or cease to benefit their owners. — Muqatil and al-Wahidi say Meccan gifts end with their lives; al-Tabari says wealth and children cannot help at the return to God; al-Baghawi, al-Zamakhshari, al-Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, al-Biqai, Bursevi, al-Alusi, and al-Tabatabai emphasize their finite duration. Al-Qurtubi notes that either the goods or the owner depart. Ibn Atiyya says the address belittles Quraysh's pride in wealth, sons, and strength. Ibn Kathir calls the adornment lowly and fleeting; Ibn Ashur says even the quality of the blessings is inferior. Al-Biqai adds that the world and its adornments were neither eternal in the past nor will last in the future. Study Quran connects worldly ornament to 33:28. [24 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 28:60/q12 How are people divided in their use of this world's enjoyment and adornment?  [مَتَٰعُ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا وَزِينَتُهَا · interpretation]
- 28:60/q12/p1 Believers provision themselves for the next life, hypocrites adorn themselves, and disbelievers merely enjoy this life. — Al-Zamakhshari and al-Nasafi both transmit this threefold division from Ibn Abbas. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 28:60/q13 What is meant by what is with God?  [وَمَا عِندَ ٱللَّهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase points to God's promised reward or, in the inward reading, to God Himself beyond the blessings.
- 28:60/q13/p1 It is God's reward, Paradise, or the blessed afterlife prepared for believers and obedient people. — Yahya, al-Qurtubi, al-Alusi, and al-Tabatabai identify Paradise or the afterlife; al-Tabari specifies the reward God keeps for those who obey and are loyal; al-Wahidi says it is bliss for believers. Al-Biqai calls it the reward promised for obedience, and Ibn Kathir says it is prepared for the righteous. [24 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 28:60/q13/p2 For the elect, God Himself is better and more enduring than created blessings, even Paradise. — Al-Sulami distinguishes the ordinary hearers of the first statement from the elect who hear that God is better; al-Nasrabadhi says most worship blessings, while the rare worship the Benefactor, and separation by anything, even obedience or Paradise, cheats a person. [2 notes: SULAMI(النصرآباذي)]

## 28:60/q14 In what sense is what is with God better?  [خَيْرٌۭ · interpretation]
- 28:60/q14/p1 Its reward and delight surpass worldly benefits in degree and quality, and sound judgment should prefer it. — Ibn Ishaq calls it better recompense; al-Razi says its benefits are greater and unmixed with worldly harms, which predominate; al-Baydawi calls it complete delight and pleasure; al-Biqai says it is more pleasant, beautiful, and satisfying even if worldly pleasure has some good; Bursevi and al-Alusi say its pure pleasure lacks pain or worry; Ibn Ashur stresses the quality and completeness of its kinds of blessing. The Prophet's reported image in Ibn Kathir compares this world with a finger dipped into the sea and what it brings back; Study Quran reports R's speck-of-dust beside the sea comparison. [21 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 28:60/q15 In what sense is what is with God more lasting?  [وَأَبْقَىٰٓ · interpretation]
- 28:60/q15/p1 The divine reward and afterlife endure forever without depletion, while every worldly benefit ends. — Al-Tabari explicitly says the reward does not run out; Ibn Ishaq calls it more enduring; Ibn al-Jawzi's lexicon gives baqiyyah the sense of permanence; al-Razi compares infinite afterlife with each person's tiny finite share of the world; al-Biqai says the afterlife is everlasting although it was not eternal in the past. Al-Tabatabai similarly contrasts finite nearer life with eternal afterlife; Study Quran reports R's assertion that this world ends while the Hereafter continues. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 28:60/q16 How secure is the promised reward with God?  [وَمَا عِندَ ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
- 28:60/q16/p1 God's promise of reward can be trusted completely. — Al-Biqai expressly calls the promise wholly trustworthy. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 28:60/q17 What does the closing appeal to reason demand?  [أَفَلَا تَعْقِلُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: The general force of the rebuke and the identification of its immediate addressees; the notes do not present these as mutually exclusive.
- 28:60/q17/p1 It rebukes the hearers for failing to reflect, distinguish the lasting good from fleeting goods, and choose faith, obedience, and the better afterlife. — Muqatil, Ibn Abbas and Muqatil as transmitted by al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, al-Tabrisi, al-Nasafi, Ibn al-Jawzi, and al-Qurtubi state that what lasts is preferable. Al-Tabari asks them to distinguish good and evil; Ibn Zayd glosses reason as reflection. Al-Razi calls preference for worldly benefit a grave failure of reason and cites al-Shafi'i's view that the most intelligent are those devoted to obedience, exchanging little for much. Al-Baydawi urges exchanging lower for better; Ibn Kathir says they prefer the present world; al-Biqai compares the peril of obedience with the greater peril of disobedience; Bursevi contrasts disbelief's misery with the happiness of faith; al-Alusi addresses fear of losing worldly possessions; Ibn Ashur treats their minds as if impaired. [19 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن زيد); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:60/q17/p2 The admonition is directed particularly to the polytheists. — Yahya ibn Sallam identifies the addressees as the polytheists. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM]

## 28:60/q18 How is the final verb read, and whom does each reading address?  [أَفَلَا تَعْقِلُونَ · readings]
turns on: Second-person continuity with the direct address versus a third-person shift that speaks about the opponents; reports also differ on Abu Amr and al-Susi's transmissions.
- 28:60/q18/p1 Most readers use second-person taʿqilun, continuing direct address and rebuke. — Al-Farisi says it matches the preceding wa-ma utitum; al-Qurtubi prefers it for that reason. Al-Thaʿlabi, al-Baghawi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin, Ibn Ashur, and al-Nashr give it to the other or majority readers. Ibn Atiyya records reports assigning it to Nafi, al-Aʿraj, al-Hasan, and Isa, and also a report assigning it to Abu Amr; al-Baghawi and Ibn Mujahid allow Abu Amr both forms. In al-Nashr, Ibn Sawwar and Abu al-Ala fix it for al-Susi, other authorities allow both transmissions equally, and the author uses both for al-Susi. [14 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو); THALABI(الباقون); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عمرو); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو علي وأبو حاتم,الجمهور وأبو عمرو); NASAFI-FULL(أبو عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عمرو والجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); NASHR(أبو طاهر بن سوار وأبو العلاء); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور; أبو عمرو ويعقوب); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو)+]
- 28:60/q18/p2 The third-person yaʿqilun speaks of the opponents as absent and makes the admonition more pointed. — Abu Amr is named by Ibn Mujahid, al-Farisi, al-Thaʿlabi, al-Wahidi, al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin, al-Nashr, and al-Alusi; Ibn Ashur also names Yaʿqub. Ibn Atiyya instead reports the majority with ya and notes Abu Ali's concern that the Abu Amr report conflicts with Abu Hatim and others. Al-Wahidi and al-Farisi say Muhammad is asked whether they understand. Al-Zamakhshari, al-Baydawi, and al-Alusi say the shift heightens the warning, the latter saying they seem unworthy of direct address. Abu Hayyan says it exposes poor judgment. Ibn Ashur says it may invite believers' wonder or treat the opponents as too distant for address; Qiraat-ER calls it a move from confrontation to observation. Al-Nashr says al-Duri reads it; many fix it for al-Susi, and al-Dani and others prefer it. Al-Nashr's author accepts both transmitted forms but calls this one more famous. [21 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو); THALABI(أبو عمرو); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عمرو); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عمرو); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو علي وأبو حاتم,الجمهور وأبو عمرو); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو); BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو عمرو); NASAFI-FULL(أبو عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عمرو والجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); NASHR(الدوري عن أبي عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(أبو عمرو); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور; أبو عمرو ويعقوب); QIRAAT-ER(Imp); NASHR+]

## 28:60/q19 When does taking worldly goods affect one's better share?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether one takes worldly goods from desire or to meet necessity and duty.
- 28:60/q19/p1 Taking goods from desire costs a person better goods in this life and the next. — Al-Tustari identifies the present loss as worship's pleasure and love of God, and the future loss as the highest ranks. He reports Amir ibn Abd Qays saying one is even less content if one accepts the world as one's share of the hereafter. [2 notes: TUSTARI(عامر بن عبد قيس)]
- 28:60/q19/p2 Taking what necessity or an obligation requires does not deprive a person of the better share. — Al-Tustari expressly makes this exception for personal compulsion or a duty one must fulfill. [1 notes: TUSTARI]

## 28:60/q20 Where should a person place worldly provision for the hereafter?  [* · interpretation]
- 28:60/q20/p1 One should send provision ahead or deposit treasure with God, where it is safe and will be found when needed in the next life. — Abd Allah says treasure there cannot be eaten by moth or reached by thieves; Kaʿb reports a Torah promise that what is deposited with God survives flood and fire and returns on Resurrection Day when most needed; Abd al-Rahman ibn Zayd says people will find in the afterlife what they sent from God's provision. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن اسلم,عبد الله,كعب)]

## 28:60/q21 How does this verse answer the surrounding objection to guidance?  [* · links]
- 28:60/q21/p1 It answers Quraysh's fear of losing worldly security, wealth, and influence by setting that possible loss beside the lasting reward of guidance. — Al-Razi calls it the third answer to the objection that following religion would cost worldly life; al-Tabatabai calls it a third answer to fear of being seized from their land in 28:57, even conceding the risk. Ibn Ashur follows the reminder of Meccan security and provision in 28:57 and says faith should be sought even at worldly cost. Islahi says the passage reassures the Prophet and answers Quraysh's fear for political and economic influence, and its warning leaves Quraysh unable to plead ignorance if punishment follows. Ibn Ashur adds that the fa introduces a contrast between polytheists' flaunted luxury, poor Muslims' present position, believers' lasting reward, and disbelievers' punishment; he cites 28:61, 83:31, 73:11, 11:116, and 21:13. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 28:60/q22 How do the following verses continue this comparison?  [* · links]
- 28:60/q22/p1 Verse 61 concludes the comparison with a connective fa, and verse 62 compares the groups who receive the differing outcomes. — Al-Baydawi says the following verse concludes this one and joins it with fa; Kuranyolu points to 28:62 for the comparison of groups. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 28:60/q23 What other Quranic passages make the same contrast or mention worldly ornament?  [* · links]
- 28:60/q23/p1 Other passages contrast fleeting worldly goods with what endures with God, and one also calls worldly goods ornament. — Ibn Kathir cites 16:96, 3:198, 13:26, and 87:16–17; al-Tabatabai cites 16:96 in explaining the contrast; Study Quran cites 4:77, 7:169, 12:57, 16:30, and 87:17, and names 33:28 for worldly ornament. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 28:60/q24 Where was this verse revealed according to the reported view of the sura?  [* · sciences]
- 28:60/q24/p1 It belongs to a wholly Meccan sura. — Ibn al-Jawzi reports al-Hasan, Ata, and Ikrima holding that all of al-Qasas is Meccan. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وعطاء وعكرمة)]
