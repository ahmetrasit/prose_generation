# 93:5: 17 questions

## 93:5/q01 How is the lām before sawfa construed?  [وَلَسَوْفَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether lām is introductory or an oath response, and whether emphasis can coexist with a suppressed subject and future marker.
- 93:5/q01/p1 It is an emphatic introductory lām, with an implicit subject. — Al-Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī and others supply 'you'; an oath lām with an imperfect ordinarily needs emphatic nūn. Abū Ḥayyān compares 93:4. Ālūsī answers Ibn al-Ḥājib that the emphasis bears on the sentence and the omission is syntactic. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ objects to imposing a reconstructed wording on Qurʾānic usage. [26 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI(صاحب الكشاف); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI(صاحب الكشاف); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR(صاحب الكشاف); IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري); BINTSHATI(أبو حيان,الزمخشري)]
- 93:5/q01/p2 It is the lām of an oath response. — Ṭabrisī says sawfa distinguishes it without nūn. Nasafī reports this as another analysis. Ālūsī finds it attractive; grammarians permit nūn's absence with sawfa, an intervening object or coordination. Ibn al-Ḥājib and Ibn Hishām agree; Elmalılı calls this stronger. Ibn ʿĀshūr questions whether sawfa forces this parsing. [11 notes: TABRISI; NASAFI(صاحب الكشاف); ALUSI(النحاة,بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR(ابن الحاجب وابن هشام); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن الحاجب); ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+; ELMALILI+; KASHSHAF-; KASHSHAF-FULL-; BAYDAWI-; NASAFI-FULL-; BIQAI-; BURSEVI-; ALUSI(المعترض)-; IBNASHUR-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 93:5/q01/p3 An emphatic subject cannot be suppressed. — Ibn al-Ḥājib compares qad before verbs and inna before nouns. Ālūsī disputes the objection by locating the emphasis in the whole clause. [2 notes: ALUSI(ابن الحاجب); ALUSI-FULL(ابن الحاجب); ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 93:5/q01/p4 Each lām implies present time while sawfa reinforces assertion. — Al-Kalnabawī likens sawfa to an emphatic nūn; Ālūsī says emphasis need not force present time and future certainty can be described as present. [1 notes: ALUSI(الفاضل الكلنبوي); ALUSI-]

## 93:5/q02 What force does sawfa and the paired emphasis give the promise?  [وَلَسَوْفَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether sawfa marks temporal distance, continuity, simple futurity, or a special emphatic construction.
- 93:5/q02/p1 The promised gift is certain even though wisely delayed. — Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Biqāʿī and Bursawī say the emphatic lām and sawfa join certainty to postponement; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ calls future emphasis ordinary Arabic. Elmalılı stresses certain realization. [17 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI(صاحب الكشاف); NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI(مفسرون); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BINTSHATI+]
- 93:5/q02/p2 Sawfa points to an extended, continuing future promise. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 12:98 and 92:21 for continuous future force. Abduh connects the delay to the twenty-year completion of religion in 5:3; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ sees reassurance of care across the Prophet's life. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI(الشيخ محمد عبده)]
- 93:5/q02/p3 Sawfa implies that the Prophet would live some time longer. — Rāzī says his death was not near and the future promise counters polytheists' expectation of his death, alongside 93:3's denial of abandonment. [4 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 93:5/q02/p4 Sawfa ranges farther in time than sa-. — The Basran argument is that its extra letters signal a larger meaning. [1 notes: ITQAN(البصريين)]
- 93:5/q02/p5 Sawfa and sa- are synonymous. — Other grammarians make no temporal distinction. [1 notes: ITQAN(غيرهم)]
- 93:5/q02/p6 Sawfa admits prefixed lām and can serve promises though often used for threats. — Al-Suyūṭī contrasts its lām with sa-; Abū Ḥayyān attributes the restriction on sa- to successive vowel movements and analogy. Ibn Bābshādh says either future marker can serve promise or threat. [4 notes: BURHAN(ابن بابشاذ); ITQAN(أبو حيان,ابن بابشاذ)]
- 93:5/q02/p7 Sa-yuʿṭīka is a colloquial contraction with the same sense. — Al-Farrāʾ cites a poet's shortened kayfa as analogy; al-Naḥḥās reports Sībawayh's semantic equivalence. [3 notes: FARRA(شاعر); NAHHAS]

## 93:5/q03 What variant form is reported for the future verb?  [وَلَسَوْفَ يُعْطِيكَ · readings]
- 93:5/q03/p1 Ibn Masʿūd or ʿAbd Allāh read a sa-yuʿṭīka form. — Al-Farrāʾ says wa-la-sa-yuʿṭīka retains the common meaning; al-Naḥḥās also records wasi-yuʿṭīka and fa-sayuʿṭīka. Ibn ʿAṭiyya's reports vary between la-sa-yuʿṭīka and wa-la-sawfa yuʿṭīka. [4 notes: FARRA(عبد الله); NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA(ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 93:5/q04 What is the unexpressed object of 'give'?  [يُعْطِيكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the omitted object is a specific prior promise or an open range of gifts.
- 93:5/q04/p1 It is deliberately omitted to leave every hoped-for good open. — Al-Naḥḥās compares saying 'I gave Zayd.' Ibn ʿĀshūr extends it to goods for the Prophet and community and links 93:4. Islahi identifies 93:4's promised good, veiled because triumphs remained future. [3 notes: NAHHAS; IBNASHUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 93:5/q04/p2 The promise is deliberately unrestricted in gift and resulting satisfaction. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ resists fixing it to interpreters' favored goods or measured palaces; Sāmarrāʾī says personal address and unnamed gift magnify its scope. Ṭabāṭabāʾī relates its breadth to 93:4. [5 notes: BINTSHATI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI+]

## 93:5/q05 Why does the verse say yuʿṭīka rather than use ītāʾ?  [يُعْطِيكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether giving requires recipient acceptance and disposal, or the two verbs are near synonyms.
- 93:5/q05/p1 Iʿṭāʾ suggests repeated giving and the recipient's acceptance or free use. — Al-Khuwayyī contrasts ītāʾ's stronger assertion and weighty grants such as dominion, wisdom and seven verses with iʿṭāʾ's recipient-side counterpart; he cites Kawthar (108:1), recurrent creation (20:50), and jizya (9:29). Sāmarrāʾī says gifts are at the recipient's disposal while ītāʾ may be withdrawn. [6 notes: ITQAN(الخويي); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 93:5/q05/p2 The two verbs are scarcely distinct in common lexical accounts. — Al-Khuwayyī reports this view before arguing for a distinction. [1 notes: ITQAN(الخويي)]

## 93:5/q06 Why say 'your Lord' explicitly?  [رَبُّكَ · rhetoric]
- 93:5/q06/p1 The repeated, personal title conveys tenderness, care and honor. — Ālūsī, Elmalılı and Ibn ʿĀshūr link the possessive Rabb to tenderness; Elmalılı notes that a pronoun could have sufficed. Sāmarrāʾī includes direct address among the promise's honors. [5 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 93:5/q07 What does fa-tarḍā convey about the result of giving?  [فَتَرْضَىٰٓ · interpretation]
- 93:5/q07/p1 Giving brings complete, immediate satisfaction. — Ibn ʿĀshūr takes fa as immediate benefit without waiting. Elmalılı describes enduring contentment beyond grief or waiting and above the station of being merely content and approved (17:79). Biqāʿī lists four possibilities of receiving or denial with contentment or discontentment; this promise joins giving to satisfaction. Sāmarrāʾī calls contentment a great blessing bringing stability. [7 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 93:5/q07/p2 Riḍā carries an eschatological resonance but can express a worldly wish. — Corpus Coranicum compares Psalm 20:5's wish for granted desires and fulfilled plans. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 93:5/q08 What gifts does the promise include, and when?  [يُعْطِيكَ رَبُّكَ فَتَرْضَىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the giving is limited to the next life, worldly success, a named honor, or the full open range.
- 93:5/q08/p1 It embraces worldly and afterlife gifts. — Zamakhsharī lists Badr, Mecca, conversions, Jewish campaigns, Arab and caliphal conquests, Persian spoils, Islam's spread and unseen reward. Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān prefer breadth. Bayḍāwī adds character and public standing; Biqāʿī adds knowledge, lawful spoils, Praiseworthy Station, Pool and intercession. Bursawī, Elmalılı, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ibn al-Qayyim add mission, guidance, renown and later rewards; Abduh stresses continuing revelation. Ālūsī says intercession need not limit breadth. - 93:5/q08/p2 Its chief or exclusive fulfillment is in the Hereafter. — Ṭabarī names abundant favors; Qatāda names Paradise on Resurrection Day. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān cite the majority; Ālūsī repeats it. Rāzī reads it as 93:4's measure: all the Prophet desires, beyond worldly capacity. ## 93:5/q09 Whose fate is included in the Prophet's satisfaction?  [فَتَرْضَىٰٓ · referent]
turns on: Whether family, whole community, monotheists, or the Prophet's own honors define the scope.
- 93:5/q09/p1 No member of his household enters the Fire, or his family enters Paradise. — Ibn ʿAbbās through al-Suddī repeatedly gives the household reading; Zayd b. ʿAlī says admitting the family to Paradise. - 93:5/q09/p2 His whole community enters Paradise. — Ibn ʿAbbās is reported through Saʿīd b. Jubayr; Elmalılı also cites the report. Another chain says neither Muhammad nor anyone of his community is left in the Fire. [7 notes: DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI(ابن عباس)]

## 93:5/q10 What limits, if any, govern intercession and forgiveness?  [يُعْطِيكَ رَبُّكَ فَتَرْضَىٰٓ · theology]
turns on: Whether the community reports entail universal deliverance or are bounded by permission, repentance and faith.
- 93:5/q10/p1 Intercession requires God's permission and rightly directed speech; faith and following determine its benefit. — Elmalılı cites 78:38, 2:255, 39:7 and 49:7. He reads 39:53 with 39:54 as a call to repent and submit, not a license for sin; the unrepentant unbeliever receives no afterlife share. He says followers belong to Muhammad (14:36) and major-sinner intercession values faith. [9 notes: ELMALILI]
- 93:5/q10/p2 Intercession or satisfaction may reach every community member left in the Fire. — Reports quote the Prophet's refusal to be pleased with anyone left; Rāzī argues from 47:19 and pardon reports. Ibn al-Qayyim objects that this oversteps God's judgment. [6 notes: RAZI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب وابن عباس); BINTSHATI(إبن القيم)-; ELMALILI-]
- 93:5/q10/p3 Discussions of intercession and the household should not be imported into this verse. — Muḥammad ʿAbduh calls them foreign to its religious spirit; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports his criticism. [1 notes: BINTSHATI(الشيخ محمد عبده)]

## 93:5/q11 What events are reported around the verse's revelation?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which occasion report or pause duration is accepted; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ disputes the value of specifying either.
- 93:5/q11/p1 Shown his community's future conquests, the Prophet rejoiced and received the promise. — Ibn ʿAbbās reports successive openings, sometimes village by village, followed by pearl-palace detail; Ibn al-Jawzī instead describes him as grieving at the sight. - 93:5/q11/p2 The Prophet consoled Fāṭima amid grinding and nursing, promising afterlife sweetness; the verse was recited or revealed. — Jaʿfar's report says he cited it; Jābir's versions say it was revealed. [7 notes: THALABI(جعفر بن محمد); TABRISI(الصادق); DURR(جابر بن عبد الله); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI(جابر بن عبد الله); ALUSI-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله); TABATABAI(جابر بن عبد الله)]

- 93:5/q11/p6 The pause lasted fifteen days. — Ibn ʿAbbās ties it to pagans' claim that the Lord had abandoned and hated Muhammad. [1 notes: TABRISI(ابن عباس)]
- 93:5/q11/p7 The pause lasted forty days. — Muqātil gives this duration. [1 notes: TABRISI(مقاتل)]
- 93:5/q11/p8 Gabriel's delayed visit prompted questions about hygiene and divine command. — A report has the Prophet blame untrimmed nails and unclean knuckles; Gabriel says he too longed for him but descends only by God's order. [2 notes: TABRISI(جبريل)]
- 93:5/q11/p9 The Qurʾān supplies no cause or length for the pause. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ regards proposed explanations and counts of twelve to forty days as irrelevant; she calls the interval natural, like night following dawn. [2 notes: BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI+]
- 93:5/q11/p10 The sūra came early in Mecca, years before the Hijra. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ uses its early setting in discussing the reassurance. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 93:5/q11/p11 On revelation the Prophet said his Lord favors him and is worthy to favor him. — Al-Durr preserves the Ibn ʿAbbās report. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 93:5/q11/p12 Ibn ʿAbbās reports the Prophet asked a favor and God recalled earlier blessings. — Study Quran connects the answer to orphan shelter, guidance, enrichment, expanded breast and lifted burden (94:1–2). [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAbbās)]

## 93:5/q12 Why do some call this the Qurʾān's most hopeful verse?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the strongest hope lies in this verse's satisfaction and intercession or 39:53's forgiveness.
- 93:5/q12/p1 The Prophet's household associates it with his concern and intercession for his community. — Reports contrast Iraqis' choice of 39:53 with the household's choice of 93:5; some cite no community member left in the Fire, others intercession for monotheists. [19 notes: THALABI(علي بن أبي طالب عن النبي); BAGHAWI(محمد بن علي); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جعفر محمد بن علي); IBNATIYYA(بعضهم من أهل البيت); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعضهم من أهل البيت); TABRISI(أهل البيت,محمد بن علي ابن الحنفية عن الحارث بن شريح); RAZI(الباقر); RAZI-FULL(الباقر); QURTUBI(علي); QURTUBI-FULL(أهل البيت); BIQAI(علي); BIQAI-FULL(علي); DURR(محمد بن علي بن الحسين); ITQAN(علي بن أبي طالب); ALUSI(محمد بن الحنفية عن علي عن النبي ﷺ); ALUSI-FULL(محمد بن الحنفية); ELMALILI(علي)]

## 93:5/q13 How does the verse connect to the rest of the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: How the promise relates to the prior assurance, the larger sequence of favors and duties, or other scriptural warnings.
- 93:5/q13/p1 It confirms 93:3–4 and completes reassurance that the Prophet's later state will improve. — Rāzī says the afterlife's superiority is specified by unlimited giving; Bayḍāwī contrasts present honor and greater reward; Nasafī says past favors strengthen patience. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the clause an independent continuation of the opening oath, not its answer. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ links past non-abandonment to better future; Islahi sees prior deliverance followed by duties. [24 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 93:5/q13/p2 The preceding denial is circumstantial, and the promise means God will give the Hereafter. — ʿIṣām al-Dīn says 93:4 explains why God will not abandon the Prophet; Ālūsī objects that the paired lām clauses follow an oath and the paraphrase is strained. [2 notes: ALUSI(عصام الدين); ALUSI-FULL(عصام الدين); ALUSI-]
- 93:5/q13/p3 The consoling pattern extends to all faithful servants. — Biqāʿī links predestination's fears to favor in both lives, cites good news (10:64), discernment (8:29), light (57:28; 6:122), and divides paths into faith or disbelief and Paradise or Fire (64:2; 42:7; 92:3; 51:49–50). Piety, reflection and deeds bring insight; the Prophet urged Ḥāritha and Abū Bakr to stand firm. [8 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير,النبي)]
- 93:5/q13/p4 The earlier sūra's Fire warning bounds the consolation. — Elmalılı cites 92:8–10, 14–16: those denying good are eased to hardship and Fire, while this sūra singles out good for the Prophet. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 93:5/q14 Who conveys the sūra's promise to the Prophet?  [* · reports]
- 93:5/q14/p1 Gabriel addresses him throughout the sūra. — Rāzī says Gabriel longed for the Prophet and God granted him the joy of conveying the good news. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 93:5/q15 What ethical or spiritual lessons are drawn from the promise?  [* · interpretation]
- 93:5/q15/p1 The Prophet prioritizes his community and religion above his own comfort. — Rāzī contrasts his prayer for attackers who wounded him with his prayer against those who obstructed prayer, and asserts a sharper judgment for harming his person than for long prayer abandonment. The 'my community' cry accompanies his joy at followers' reward. [5 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 93:5/q15/p2 Worldly benefits may reach unbelievers, while afterlife benefit requires faith. — Elmalılı links the promise's reach to belief and following, citing Abraham's statement (14:36) and warning that unrepentant unbelief ends in loss. [3 notes: ELMALILI]
- 93:5/q15/p3 The promise invites contentment and reflection on divine generosity. — Sāmarrāʾī says contentment yields peace even where wealth lacks it; his wider account of Qurʾānic inimitability spans language, law, society, history and human sciences, whose inexhaustible treasures reward contemplation. [5 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 93:5/q16 What inward interpretation is given of the gift and improvement?  [* · inward]
- 93:5/q16/p1 After annihilation God gives the Prophet true divine existence to guide creation. — Qāshānī makes restored existence the ground of satisfaction. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 93:5/q16/p2 The Prophet's inward states progress to completion without severing spiritual flow. — Bursawī reports the Najmī interpretation of continuing inward prophethood, messengership and sainthood, and cites completion of religion (5:3) for later states' excellence. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 93:5/q17 What poetic or literary parallels are quoted in the discussion?  [* · rhetoric]
- 93:5/q17/p1 Cited poems illustrate expressive pairings of condition, generosity, courage, proof, lineage and honor. — Al-Jurjānī quotes sleepless night versus sleep-bringing sword; kindness as bond; Sayf al-Dawla's illness as earth's, valor's and generosity's illness; spontaneous giving versus tardy excuses; steadfast generosity amid battle; warrior composure; dawn-like evident virtue; speech and deeds as proof of lineage; place honored by the aim it yields; and hostile witnesses acknowledging merit. [10 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
