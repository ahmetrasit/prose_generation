# 4:56: 30 questions

## 4:56/q01 Who are the disbelievers threatened here?  [ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the preceding discussion restricts the address or introduces a general threat.
- 4:56/q01/p1 The warning covers all disbelievers, including rejecters of Muhammad or any prophet. — Tabari includes Jews of Israel and other persistent deniers; Ibn Atiyya extends the warning from rebellious Israelites to all who share their unbelief; Ibn Ashur calls it broader than the preceding passage. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 4:56/q01/p2 They are the Jews. — Muqatil identifies them as Jews. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 4:56/q02 What constitutes disbelief in the signs?  [كَفَرُوا۟ بِـَٔايَٰتِنَا · interpretation]
turns on: The outward acts of rejecting evidence versus an inward veil.
- 4:56/q02/p1 It includes denial, neglect, doubt, and knowing rejection. — Razi lists denying evidentiary status, neglecting consideration, raising doubts, and obstinacy or envy despite knowledge, referring to 2:6; Biqai says deniers conceal what intellect disclosed. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 4:56/q02/p2 It is being veiled from divine manifestations. — Qashani connects this veil to God's attributes and acts. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 4:56/q03 What are God's signs here?  [بِـَٔايَٰتِنَا · referent]
turns on: Whether signs means scripture specifically, all proofs, or divine manifestations.
- 4:56/q03/p1 They are divine proofs, including revelation and prophetic signs. — Tabari emphasizes revealed proofs of Muhammad's truth; Tabrisi includes oneness and the Prophet; Razi includes signs of God's essence, acts, attributes and names, plus angels, books and messengers; Bursevi includes Quran and miracles. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 4:56/q03/p2 They are the Qur'an. — Muqatil specifies the Qur'an. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 4:56/q03/p3 They are God's manifestations in attributes and acts. — Qashani relates them to knowledge, wisdom and sovereignty in Abraham's household. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 4:56/q04 How is the opening clause parsed?  [إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ بِـَٔايَٰتِنَا سَوْفَ نُصْلِيهِمْ نَارًۭا · grammar]
- 4:56/q04/p1 Alladhina is inna's subject and sawfa nuslihim naran its predicate. — Nahhas states the subject and predicate analysis. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 4:56/q05 What does sawfa convey here?  [سَوْفَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the particle chiefly marks postponement and threat or consequential confirmation.
- 4:56/q05/p1 It marks a future threat or warning. — Al-Hasan calls it a threat; Sibawayh calls it respite, threat and warning and allows saw without fa. Wahidi's other source derives it from postponement, with sin as substitute and 74:26 and 93:5 as evidence. Razi and Alusi say it also promises; Bursevi says it adds emphasis; Biqai says the threat is certain after reprieve. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(سيبويه,غيره); RAZI-FULL(سيبويه); TABRISI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(سيبويه); BIQAI-FULL]
- 4:56/q05/p2 It can mark consequence, confirmation, or deferred fulfillment. — Wahidi reports this alternative, citing 12:98 and Joseph's deferral of supplication until dawn to secure fulfillment. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(بعضهم)]

## 4:56/q06 How is nuslihim read?  [نُصْلِيهِمْ · readings]
turns on: Vocalization of initial nun and pronoun suffix.
- 4:56/q06/p1 Humayd reads with fatha on initial nun, from triliteral salaya, meaning roast. — Tha'labi, Jishumi, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Samin report it; Ibn Atiyya cites the roasted sheep hadith; Tha'labi also gives a gloss of putting into fire. [6 notes: THALABI(حميد بن قيس); JISHUMI(حميد ابن قيس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(حميد); QURTUBI-FULL(حميد بن قيس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(حميد); SAMIN-DURR(حميد)]
- 4:56/q06/p2 The common reading has damma on initial nun, from asla. — Jishumi says aslaytuhu means cast into fire; Ibn Atiyya says bring near and cast in, equivalent to doubled sallaytu; Ibn Ashur derives it from if'al. Ibn Jinni reports Humayd's text without further analysis. [5 notes: JISHUMI(قراءة العامة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(حميد بن قيس)]
- 4:56/q06/p3 Sallam and Yaqub read the pronoun ha with damma. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan record it; Samin calls it a Hijazi dialect form; Qiraat-ER says -him/-hum after kasra has no semantic effect. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(سلام ويعقوب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سلام ويعقوب); SAMIN-DURR(سلام ويعقوب); QIRAAT-ER]

## 4:56/q07 What does nuslihim naran mean?  [نُصْلِيهِمْ نَارًۭا · meaning]
turns on: The verb's causative admission and roasting senses.
- 4:56/q07/p1 God will cast or admit them into fire. — Tabari says this entails scorching; Tha'labi, Baghawi, Nasafi, Tabrisi, Jishumi and Bursevi explain admission; Ibn Kathir says fire envelops every part. Wahidi refers to 4:10. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 4:56/q07/p2 God will roast or cook them in fire. — Majaz, Zajjaj, Ibn Jawzi and Ibn Ashur cite maṣliyya roasted sheep, including the report of a Jewish woman gifting one to the Prophet; Tabrisi distinguishes ṣalā, roast, from aṣlā, cast, and cites the idiom of suffering another's evil; Razi includes both admission and roasting. [9 notes: MAJAZ; ZAJJAJ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 4:56/q07/p3 The deniers themselves become fuel for the fire. — Jishumi reports Abu Muslim's explanation. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم)]

## 4:56/q08 How is naran governed?  [نَارًۭا · grammar]
turns on: The governing verb changes with the reading.
- 4:56/q08/p1 On Humayd's fatha reading, an omitted preposition governs its sense. — Tha'labi understands bi-nar; Jishumi says roast with fire; Qurtubi understands in a fire. [3 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 4:56/q08/p2 On the common reading, naran is a direct object. — Jishumi identifies the object on damma; Ibn Ashur calls it a second object on the pattern of a'ta, citing 2:61. [2 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 4:56/q09 Why is fire indefinite?  [نَارًۭا · rhetoric]
- 4:56/q09/p1 Indefiniteness magnifies the fire as formidable. — Alusi presents an immense, dreadful fire. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:56/q10 How does kullama govern and express repetition?  [كُلَّمَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether ma itself or the full construction gives universality, and where the clause attaches.
- 4:56/q10/p1 It is a temporal adverb of each occurrence, governed by baddalnahum. — Nahhas and Alusi call it a temporal adverb; Samin makes its clause circumstantial from the object of nuslihim; Abu Hayyan calls ma temporal-source, its clause conditional in force and also relates it to nuslihim. Zarkashi says ma and clause stand for time, with kull accusative as adverb; bare ma is not itself a time adverb. Zarkashi contrasts repeated marriage and divorce with bare kull, citing 2:25. [9 notes: NAHHAS; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; BURHAN(بعض الأصوليين)]
- 4:56/q10/p2 Its repetition arises from temporal ma's own universality, with kull reinforcing it. — Abu Hayyan's account is reported by Zarkashi, who objects that source ma has no independent universality. [1 notes: BURHAN(أبو حيان); BURHAN-]
- 4:56/q10/p3 Its repetition arises from the whole kullama construction. — Zarkashi rejects assigning generality to ma alone; some legal theorists distinguish intentional generalization of acts with kullama from intentional generalization of nouns with bare kull. [4 notes: BURHAN(بعض الأصوليين)]
- 4:56/q10/p4 The kullama clause qualifies naran. — Samin records this possible analysis with an omitted resumptive pronoun and calls it weak. [0 notes: SAMIN-DURR-]

## 4:56/q11 What does nadijat say happened to the skins?  [نَضِجَتْ جُلُودُهُم · meaning]
turns on: Whether ripening reaches destruction or denotes an earlier heated state.
- 4:56/q11/p1 They are burned or cooked through. — Muqatil, Ibn Umar, Baghawi, Qurtubi, Bursevi, Alusi and Ibn Ashur give burning or full cooking; Qurtubi notes nadij al-ra'y for sound judgment, and Alusi compares ripened fruit or meat. Al-Dahhak says fire strips the skin to bone before replacement, forever for denial of the Messenger and God's signs. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عمر); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 4:56/q11/p2 They soften from heat before being burned through. — Wahidi says full destruction would interrupt sensation and ease punishment, contrary to 2:162. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 4:56/q11/p3 They begin to decay and separate. — Abu Hayyan compares meat and fruit ripening. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 4:56/q11/p4 They lose sensation when cooked. — Biqai compares cooked meat and dead flesh in a wound. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 4:56/q12 How is the ta of nadijat pronounced before jim?  [نَضِجَتْ جُلُودُهُم · readings]
- 4:56/q12/p1 Clear pronunciation is preferable, though assimilation is allowed. — Zajjaj and Nahhas prefer showing ta to avoid too many jim sounds; Zajjaj explains assimilation by tongue articulation and voiceless ta. Nashr records both Ibn Dhakwan routes, Hisham's assimilation and Ibn Dhakwan's showing in Abu al-Fath, challenges Shatibi's route report, lists those showing ta before six letters and exceptions from Ruways and Rawh, citing 22:36. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; NASHR(أبو الفتح فارس)]

## 4:56/q13 What are the replacement skins?  [بَدَّلْنَٰهُمْ جُلُودًا غَيْرَهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ghayraha changes the underlying skin, replaces its material, names clothing, or figuratively depicts duration.
- 4:56/q13/p1 The very same skin is restored with changed form or quality. — Tabari and Zajjaj compare remaking a ring; Zajjaj adds that resurrection itself recreated decayed skin. Nahhas compares rekindling fire at 17:97. Tha'labi cites a changed person, 14:48's earth, and Labid's verse; Qurtubi adds the changed brother, dust and a poem about people and a house. Wahidi cites Zajjaj, Ibn Kaysan, Ibn al-Anbari, Farra's ring-to-hoop, Abu al-Abbas's tabdil versus ibdal distinction, and Mubarrad's form-change view supported by Ibn Abbas. Baghawi uses the ring and changed brother; Baydawi permits renewal of the burned surface. Ibn Ashur notes ghayraha reinforces replacement. [36 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI(قيل,لبيد); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العباس أحمد بن يحيى,الفراء,جماعة من أهل المعاني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(فضيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(الزجاج والبلخي وأبو علي الجبائي,المغربي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الشاعر,بعضهم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفضيل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد)+]
- 4:56/q13/p2 God creates genuinely different skins after the old ones burn. — Tabari's plain paraphrase and Ibn Umar's white-paper simile support this. Jishumi calls it strongest; Tabrisi reports Ali ibn Isa's choice; Abu Hayyan prefers the apparent sense. Wahidi says here ghayr marks incompatible difference, with Mubarrad's 25:70 and Abu al-Najm's prince line showing exchange; Tabrisi cites the prince line and 14:48. Ibn Kathir calls it continuous literal punishment; Alusi says it is possible by God's power. [30 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عمر,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عمر); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو النجم,أبو علي الفارسي,ابن عباس,المبرد,جماعة); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عمر,فرقة); TABRISI(قتادة وجماعة من أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عمر,الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; JISHUMI(قتادة وجماعة من المفسرين)+]
- 4:56/q13/p3 The skins are clinging tar garments replaced as they burn. — Tabari cites 14:50 and says bodily skin's disappearance would mean death and relief. Mawardi likewise says the damned neither die nor gain relief. Wahidi cites 14:50, an adjacent-skin idiom and the Taim garments poem; Qurtubi cites 14:49-50, Ibn Umar's close-attachment poem and the Taim poem. Tabrisi, Razi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir and Alusi object that this abandons the apparent sense; Razi's Qadi adds garments burn but do not ripen. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الشاعر,بعضهم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عمر,الشاعر,بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(other commentators); TABRISI(قيل)-; RAZI-FULL(القاضي)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 4:56/q13/p4 New skin grows from the unbeliever's own flesh. — Al-Suddi says burned skin turns to flesh and fresh skin emerges from flesh, avoiding an innocent external skin. Razi finds finite flesh would run out without further explanation. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي); RAZI-FULL(السدي)-]
- 4:56/q13/p5 Skin renewal is an allegory of endlessly renewed torment or life. — Razi says each apparent approach to death is followed by fresh life; Asad attributes an allegory of long afterlife suffering to Razi. Abu Hayyan calls the metaphor far-fetched. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Razi); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 4:56/q14 How does tabdil describe the replacement?  [بَدَّلْنَٰهُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether lexical form fixes material continuity.
- 4:56/q14/p1 It can mean changing form while preserving substance. — Farra's recast ring, Abu al-Abbas's tabdil/ibdal distinction, Mubarrad's restored skin, al-Maghribi's cloak-to-shirt example and Damghani's renewed earth at 14:48 illustrate this sense. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العباس أحمد بن يحيى,الفراء,المبرد); DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; TABRISI(المغربي); BURSEVI]
- 4:56/q14/p2 It can mean exchanging one entity for another. — Farra also allows a new object; Mubarrad says Arabs use baddala for abdala, as in 25:70. Abu al-Najm's prince line and Tabrisi's 14:48 illustrate substitution; Kashshaf explains exchanging skins. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو النجم,الفراء,المبرد); TABRISI]
- 4:56/q14/p3 Baddala and abdala cannot be reliably distinguished. — Abu Ali al-Farisi cites 16:101, where a verse may remain recited or be lifted, and 34:16, where both gardens remain. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي)]

## 4:56/q15 How is ghayraha functioning?  [غَيْرَهَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether 'other' marks altered qualities or a distinct thing.
- 4:56/q15/p1 It can mean a changed state without a new underlying object. — Tha'labi, Baghawi, Qurtubi and Wahidi compare the same person, ring or earth in altered form. [5 notes: THALABI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(بعضهم); WAHIDI-BASIT(جماعة من أهل المعاني)]
- 4:56/q15/p2 It reinforces the difference already implied by replacement. — Ibn Ashur says ghayraha restates what baddalnahum entails. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 4:56/q15/p3 It can signal incompatible difference of material. — Wahidi reports the literal-new-skin view using this sense. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(جماعة)]

## 4:56/q16 How often are skins consumed or replaced?  [كُلَّمَا نَضِجَتْ جُلُودُهُم بَدَّلْنَٰهُمْ جُلُودًا غَيْرَهَا · reports]
turns on: Different transmitted counts and whether a fixed number is asserted.
- 4:56/q16/p1 Seventy thousand times each day. — Tabari, Ibn Abi Hatim, Tha'labi, Baghawi, Ibn Jawzi, Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi, Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, Durr and Alusi report al-Hasan; variants count roasting, skin consumption, or flesh consumption; several say the consumed return when commanded. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن); IBNABIHATIM(الحسن,فضيل بن هشام); THALABI(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن بن أبي الحسن); TABRISI(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن البصري); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن); DURR-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 4:56/q16/p2 One hundred times in an hour. — Ibn Abi Hatim, Tha'labi, Wahidi, Baghawi, Ibn Kathir and Durr transmit Mu'adh's explanation confirmed by Umar as Prophetic; Itqan calls its Tabarani route weak. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(معاذ بن جبل); THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(معاذ بن جبل); BAGHAWI-FULL(عمر); IBNKATHIR-FULL(معاذ بن جبل); DURR-FULL(معاذ); ITQAN(معاذ)]
- 4:56/q16/p3 Seven times each day. — Muqatil specifies days measured as worldly days; Kashshaf attributes this to a Prophetic report without grading; Qurtubi reports Muqatil and Abu Hayyan records the figure. [4 notes: MUQATIL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن)]
- 4:56/q16/p4 Seventy times each day or each cycle. — Kashshaf and Abu Hayyan attribute seventy replacements to al-Hasan, with white-paper skins; Bursevi reports seventy daily and return on command. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); BURSEVI(الحسن)]
- 4:56/q16/p5 One hundred twenty times in an hour. — Ibn Kathir, Durr and Alusi give Ka'b's pre-Islamic reading affirmed by Umar as something heard from the Prophet; Alusi gives Ibn Umar's route. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عمر); DURR-FULL(كعب); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عمر)]
- 4:56/q16/p6 The replacement may recur many times in an hour. — Alusi allows this without a fixed figure. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 4:56/q16/p7 The tar garments are replaced a hundred or a hundred thousand times daily. — Abu Hayyan reports the proposal while calling it far-fetched. [0 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 4:56/q17 What color or form are the replacement skins?  [جُلُودًا غَيْرَهَا · reports]
turns on: Different reported descriptions.
- 4:56/q17/p1 They are white like sheets of paper. — Ibn Umar and Ibn Abbas transmit this comparison through Tabari, Ibn Abi Hatim, Tha'labi, Wahidi, Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, Abu Hayyan and Durr; Kashshaf links white skins to al-Hasan's seventy replacements. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عمر); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عمر); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عمر); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عمر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عمر); DURR-FULL(ابن عمر)]
- 4:56/q17/p2 There are one hundred skins with differently colored torments between each pair. — Yahya ibn Yazid al-Hadrami gives this in Ibn Abi Hatim, Ibn Kathir and Durr. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(يحيى بن يزيد الحضرمي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(يحيى بن يزيد الحضرمي); DURR-FULL(يحيى بن يزيد الحضرمي)]
- 4:56/q17/p3 The skin is stretched like a wild ass's hide, with worms between skin, flesh and blood. — Tha'labi reports Mujahid's description. [1 notes: THALABI(مجاهد)]

## 4:56/q18 What size and bodily features are reported for the damned?  [جُلُودُهُم · reports]
turns on: Different reported measurements and features.
- 4:56/q18/p1 An earlier book gives skin as forty cubits and a belly able to hold a mountain. — Al-Rabi's versions vary: Tabari gives height seventy cubits; Ibn Abi Hatim gives a ninety-cubit measure for san; Ibn Kathir gives tooth ninety cubits; Durr gives teeth seventy cubits. Each says the consumed skins are replaced. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(الربيع); IBNABIHATIM(الربيع بن أنس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); DURR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس)]
- 4:56/q18/p2 Skin is forty-two cubits thick and a molar is like Mount Uhud. — Tha'labi transmits Abu Hurayra from the Prophet. [1 notes: THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 4:56/q18/p3 Skin is forty cubits; tooth like Uhud; upper lip to navel, with worms, snakes and scorpions. — Bursevi's attributed Prophetic report describes camel-neck snakes and mule-size scorpions between flesh and skin. [1 notes: BURSEVI(النبي)]
- 4:56/q18/p4 Skin is forty cubits thick and fire consumes seventy thousand skins daily. — Tabari reports al-Hasan and says only God knows which cubit is meant. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن)]
- 4:56/q18/p5 Skin is seventy cubits thick, tooth like Uhud, and earlobe to shoulder seven hundred years. — Ibn Kathir gives Ibn Umar's report and says Ahmad alone transmits it by that route. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 4:56/q18/p6 Skin thickness is a three-day journey and a tooth or tusk like Uhud. — Baghawi's Abu Hurayra report attributes this to the Prophet. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 4:56/q18/p7 The distance between shoulders is a three-day journey for a swift rider. — Baghawi reports Abu Hurayra. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة)]

## 4:56/q19 How can replacement skin be punished justly?  [بَدَّلْنَٰهُمْ جُلُودًا غَيْرَهَا · theology]
turns on: Whether the skin is a moral subject and whether identity depends on the same material.
- 4:56/q19/p1 The continuing person or soul suffers; skin conveys pain but is not the guilty subject. — Tabari says skin and flesh may be insentient; Zajjaj rejects treating skin as sinner; Tha'labi says spirits mediate pain; Mawardi, Wahidi, Jishumi, Baghawi, Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Alusi, Ibn Ashur and Kuranyolu distinguish person from instrument. Alusi compares a killer's hand or sword, changing bodily parts, oversized afterlife bodies, and an iron body; Ibn Ashur compares continuity from seed to palm and invokes coccygeal regrowth; Kuranyolu adds body-to-brain pain signaling. Alusi rejects the parable of a seeing cripple and blind man jointly taking fruit, because the body as instrument has no choice or awareness; he allows only unknown consciousness as a possible qualification. [30 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر,جماعة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(بعضهم,عبد العزيز بن يحيى); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(علي بن عيسى); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد العزيز بن يحيى); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(السفيري); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL-]
- 4:56/q19/p2 The original skin is restored, so the same sensitive skin that sinned suffers. — Zajjaj, Baghawi, Tabrisi and Bursevi answer the innocent-new-skin objection by material identity; Razi's changed attribute keeps the subject identical. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج والبلخي وأبو علي الجبائي); RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; NASAFI-FULL]
- 4:56/q19/p3 God has authority to create bodily pain in fire without being questioned. — Razi says God could inflict immense pain without fire yet puts them in it. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 4:56/q19/p4 The replacement of an innocent new skin appears unjust. — Razi raises the objection; Qurtubi and Kuranyolu reject it, while Zajjaj, Baghawi and Bursevi answer it by identity. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL; ZAJJAJ-; QURTUBI-FULL-; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]

## 4:56/q20 How does li-yadhuqu attach grammatically?  [لِيَذُوقُوا۟ · grammar]
- 4:56/q20/p1 The purposive lam attaches to baddalnahum and makes the verb subjunctive. — Nahhas says it stands for an; Samin gives baddalnahum as its governor. [2 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR]

## 4:56/q21 What does the purpose clause li-yadhuqu express?  [لِيَذُوقُوا۟ ٱلْعَذَابَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause foregrounds each new painful onset, continued duration, or their renewed state.
- 4:56/q21/p1 Renewal makes them feel fresh pain repeatedly, without relief or habituation. — Muqatil says fresh punishment; Tabari stresses pain and distress for worldly rejection; Wahidi says severe pain never removes feeling; Tabrisi and Abu Hayyan say new skin restarts sensation before habituation; Biqai connects repeated restoration to repeated denial and avoiding bodily perishing; Bursevi and Alusi stress acute inner sensation and bitter tasting. Kuranyolu cites skin and membranes carrying pain through nerves to the brain, and replacement preventing escape. [20 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الإسلام,قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 4:56/q21/p2 Their experience of punishment continues without interruption. — Kashshaf compares a wish for lasting and increasing honor; Razi, Nasafi, Bursevi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi repeat the analogy. Study Quran says consumption and replacement show suffering without end; Wahidi-Wajiz glosses tasting as undergoing it. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
- 4:56/q21/p3 They taste punishment in their renewed state. — Razi says they were already tasting it continuously, so the clause may specify the renewed condition. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 4:56/q22 What does tasting mean here?  [يَذُوقُوا۟ · meaning]
- 4:56/q22/p1 It means perceiving or experiencing punishment. — Wahidi-Wajiz glosses undergo; Wahidi-Basit says dhawq is taste, with dhawāq and madhāq denoting verbal nouns or flavor; Ibn al-A'rabi says perception can occur in mouth or elsewhere. Tabrisi says feel pain; Alusi adds bitterness and inward penetration. [5 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأعرابي); TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:56/q23 What does al-Aziz convey?  [عَزِيزًا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the epithet emphasizes irresistible power generally, fulfillment of promises, or inward subjugation.
- 4:56/q23/p1 God is invincible, unopposed, and able to exact punishment. — Muqatil and Abu al-Aliya emphasize vengeance; Muhammad ibn Ishaq says God can support against unbelievers when He wills. Zajjaj, Nahhas, Wahidi, Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Biqai, Bursevi, Alusi and Ibn Ashur say none can stop, overcome or elude Him. [19 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية,محمد بن إسحاق); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 4:56/q23/p2 It means God can fulfill whatever He threatens or promises. — Tabrisi and Alusi report this alternate gloss. [2 notes: TABRISI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 4:56/q23/p3 Inwardly it is the might that subdues by the soul's own qualities. — Qashani describes humiliation through longing for inaccessible perfection. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 4:56/q24 What does al-Hakim convey?  [حَكِيمًۭا · meaning]
turns on: The outward justice of the punishment or its inward correspondence to the soul.
- 4:56/q24/p1 God governs and apportions punishment wisely and justly. — Muqatil relates wisdom to decreeing Hell; Zajjaj, Nahhas, Wahidi, Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Biqai, Bursevi, Alusi and Ibn Ashur explain wisdom in governance, deserving punishment or proportioning it to sins; Biqai adds that sinners intended lifelong persistence. [18 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 4:56/q24/p2 Inwardly wisdom matches punishment to chosen appetites and angry impulses. — Qashani says their attraction to bodily pleasure exchanges one dark veil for another. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 4:56/q25 What force does kana have in the final divine description?  [كَانَ عَزِيزًا حَكِيمًۭا · grammar]
- 4:56/q25/p1 It does not confine God's attributes to the past; He always was and remains so. — Durr reports Ibn Abbas applying this to mighty, wise, knowing and powerful. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 4:56/q26 Why does the verse end with God's might and wisdom?  [إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ كَانَ عَزِيزًا حَكِيمًۭا · rhetoric]
- 4:56/q26/p1 The names assure execution of a wise and just threat. — Zajjaj answers skeptics by pointing to wisdom in all God's acts; Ibn Atiyya says no rival overcomes God and He judges rightly; Alusi says the clause explains casting into fire and skin renewal, with God's name repeated to ground the ruling; Ibn Ashur relates might to complete punishment and wisdom to its particular form. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 4:56/q27 How does this verse connect with its context?  [* · links]
turns on: How the general threat follows the prior discussion.
- 4:56/q27/p1 It broadens and explains the preceding warning, then sets threat against promised reward. — Tabari directs the warning to Israelite Jews and other persistent unbelievers; Jishumi and Tabrisi say discussion of believers and unbelievers leads to corresponding promise and threat; Ibn Atiyya and Razi say a specific threat expands to all unbelievers; Baydawi calls it confirmation; Abu Hayyan contrasts ensuing believers' reward and cites 3:106; Ibn Ashur calls the verse a set-apart coda. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 4:56/q27/p2 It resonates with other fire and renewal verses. — Nahhas and Qurtubi cite 17:97's rekindled fire; Wahidi cites 2:162 against a respite; Ibn Ashur recalls 4:10 and 4:30; the skin-as-garment reading invokes 14:49-50. [5 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL(بعضهم); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 4:56/q28 What does the passage disclose in an inward reading?  [* · inward]
turns on: Different inner referents of fire and skins.
- 4:56/q28/p1 God manifests clarification to the elect. — Sulami reports this gloss. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 4:56/q28/p2 The fire is longing for perfection while the veil persists. — Qashani says nature and capacity demand perfection, but a rooted veil bars it; God's might humiliates through unfulfilled longing. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 4:56/q28/p3 The fire is coercion from God's overpowering attributes. — Qashani says this takes a form suited to their states. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 4:56/q28/p4 The fire is their evil selves craving denied habitual pleasures. — Qashani links deprivation to their own dispositions. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 4:56/q28/p5 Renewed skins are renewed veils of the body. — Qashani says as bodily coverings fall, new veils replace them, prolonging the fires of deprivation; divine wisdom matches what they chose through appetitive and angry impulses. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 4:56/q29 What does the verse imply about bodily resurrection and continuity?  [بَدَّلْنَٰهُمْ جُلُودًا غَيْرَهَا · theology]
turns on: Whether personal continuity requires the original bodily matter and how literal bodily punishment is maintained.
- 4:56/q29/p1 The same person can persist through restored or similar bodily matter. — Zajjaj compares recreating skin after resurrection; Qurtubi compares a soul recreated from dust; Alusi says scriptural evidence varies between restoring annihilated bodies and creating similar bodies, both consistent with bodily resurrection, and mentions testimony of tongues, hands and feet in 24:24. Ibn Ashur invokes coccygeal regrowth and palm from seed; Kuranyolu locates continuity in self, soul and consciousness. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 4:56/q29/p2 Purely spiritual recompense is rationally possible, though doctrine establishes bodily resurrection. — Alusi explicitly marks bodily resurrection as necessary religious doctrine. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 4:56/q29/p3 Some take afterlife pleasures and torments literally. — Study Quran reports this reading against spiritual metaphorical readings. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]

## 4:56/q30 What miscellaneous literary or lexical examples occur alongside this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: These excerpts preserve independent illustrations rather than rival explanations of this verse.
- 4:56/q30/p1 A report introduces Aisha's citation of Labid without preserving the answer. — Tha'labi's excerpt ends before Labid's lines or Ibn Abbas's reply; Qurtubi preserves Ibn Abbas's answer about Ad and a later found spear bearing a verse about changed conditions. [2 notes: THALABI(الشعبي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 4:56/q30/p2 Abu Tammam's ornate style is criticized, while al-Buhturi is judged smoother. — Baqillani reports critics' charges that Abu Tammam's badi' drained natural force and Ahmad ibn Ubayd Allah ibn Ammar disparaged him; Baqillani cites grotesque imagery, strained comparisons, repeated antithesis and paronomasia, while preferring al-Buhturi's more graceful restraint. [4 notes: BAQILLANI(من الأدباء); BAQILLANI+]
- 4:56/q30/p3 Shade and canopy receive distinct lexical descriptions. — Tabrisi reports Ru'ya distinguishing departing-sun shade from wider zill, including night shelter; he defines zulla as covering and zalil as sheltered. [2 notes: TABRISI(رؤية)]
- 4:56/q30/p4 Bare kull can function as an intensifying adjective. — Zarkashi cites Sibawayh's al-rajul kull al-rajul; al-Saffar reads it as complete greatness and allows this emphatic use only after a preceding adjective-like word, not a bare name; a Prophetic falcon saying illustrates inclusive greatness. [4 notes: BURHAN(الصفار,النبي,سيبويه)]
