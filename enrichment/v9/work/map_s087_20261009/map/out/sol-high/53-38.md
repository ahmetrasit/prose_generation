# 53:38: 17 questions

## 53:38/q01 How is أَلَّا تَزِرُ parsed and attached to the preceding report of the scrolls?  [أَلَّا تَزِرُ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical force of أن and whether the clause substitutes for earlier material, supplies an omitted predicate, takes an implied verb, or explains the written report.
- 53:38/q01/p1 أن is lightened from أنَّ, with an implicit subject, and its clause is genitive as an apposition to ما or the reported contents of the scrolls. — Akhfash, Zajjaj, Wahidi, Tabrisī, Rāzī, and Samīn identify the preceding ما as the appositional base; Tabrisī calls it relative. Rāzī says lightening is required before a verb or particle governing one. Samīn makes لا تزر the predicate of an omitted pronoun of شأن and says negation licenses the finite verbal predicate without قد; Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites 73:20 for an intervening element while the verb remains indicative. Samīn expressly prefers the genitive case. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it specification of the scriptures' general contents. [20 notes: AKHFASH; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 53:38/q01/p2 The clause is nominative, supplying the predicate of an omitted هو or ذلك or independently clarifying what the scrolls contain. — Zajjāj and Wahidi phrase it as an answer to 'What is in the scrolls?' Samīn gives an omitted هو or ذلك. Naḥḥās allows a new nominative clause. The lightened أن and implicit matter-pronoun remain possible in this parse, according to the sources that give both analyses. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 53:38/q01/p3 The clause is accusative, either appositional to ما or governed by an omitted أعني. — Naḥḥās gives the accusative apposition; Samīn permits omitted أعني and notes that the verb is omitted with this sort of implicit subject. [2 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR]
- 53:38/q01/p4 أن is explanatory, introducing an explanation of the meaningful speech conveyed by the written scriptures. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says written material can convey speech in meaning, allowing explanatory أن. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:38/q02 What does تَزِرُ mean?  [تَزِرُ · meaning]
- 53:38/q02/p1 It means to incur or bear a burden of sin, that is, wrongdoing. — Zajjāj derives it from وزر يزر; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it the imperfect of وزر. Nasafī glosses the root as incurring the burden of wrongdoing. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:38/q03 What does the feminine وَازِرَةٌ designate?  [وَازِرَةٌ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the feminine ending reflects an understood نفس or an intensive form.
- 53:38/q03/p1 It designates a soul, so the feminine form agrees in sense with نفس. — Jishumī and Ibn ʿĀshūr supply نفس; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the feminine forms under negation make the rule universal. Rāzī and Bursevī specify a soul capable of bearing a burden, not one already loaded; Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise calls it one disposed to carry sin. [6 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 53:38/q03/p2 The feminine form is intensive, like ʿallāma and nassāba. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives this as an alternative to supplying نفس. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 53:38/q04 What does وِزْرَ mean here?  [وِزْرَ · meaning]
- 53:38/q04/p1 A burden or weight, used here for sin and the punishment it entails. — Jishumī says sin is called a burden because it weighs on its bearer. Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives burden or weight; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the word is commonly used for sin. Elmalılı includes bearing its afterlife punishment; Naḥḥās glosses the root as carrying a burden. [5 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; NAHHAS]

## 53:38/q05 What responsibility does the verse assign for another person's sin?  [أَلَّا تَزِرُ وَازِرَةٌۭ وِزْرَ أُخْرَىٰ · interpretation]
- 53:38/q05/p1 Each soul answers for its own wrongdoing; no one can take over another's guilt or punishment to release that person. — Muqātil states the principle directly. Ṭabarī, Wāḥidī, and Baghawī apply it to the offered transfer of al-Walīd's liability. Abū Rimtha reports the Prophet applying it between father and son. Ibn Kathīr includes disbelief and other sins and cites 35:18. Biqāʿī includes guilt for turning away and what follows. Qāshānī grounds reward and punishment in settled dispositions formed by repeated acts, while allotted immediate fortunes also depend on decree and proximate causes. Elmalılı formulates a rule that punishment cannot be delegated; Study Quran says no one is forced to endure another's earned punishment. Qurtubī points to his discussion at 6:164. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(أبو رمتة); WAHIDI-BASIT(عكرمة عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 53:38/q06 Does the rule govern worldly liability, afterlife liability, or both?  [أَلَّا تَزِرُ وَازِرَةٌۭ وِزْرَ أُخْرَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the unqualified burden extends to worldly punishment or the passage chiefly addresses judgment after death.
- 53:38/q06/p1 It applies in worldly life and the afterlife. — Wāḥidī explicitly applies the teaching in both realms. Ibn ʿĀshūr argues that the unqualified word 'burden' covers both; he adduces the Torah rule against killing parents for children or children for parents and Moses' plea in 7:155 that Israel not be destroyed for its fools. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:38/q06/p2 Its central intent is the afterlife rule that no one pays another's punishment. — Elmalılı says the sin and its penalty here concern the afterlife and identifies that as the central intent. [2 notes: ELMALILI]

## 53:38/q07 What earlier practice did Abraham's teaching end?  [أَلَّا تَزِرُ وَازِرَةٌۭ وِزْرَ أُخْرَىٰ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in whom they say was liable for whose wrong and whether the wrong was specifically a killing; some give only the general practice.
- 53:38/q07/p1 People had been held for someone else's sin until Abraham conveyed the rule. — ʿAmr ibn Aws reports the earlier practice generally. Hudhayl ibn Shurayhil dates it from Noah to Abraham; Wāḥidī likewise reports that span. The reports vary in describing the persons punished, detailed in the other positions. [5 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(عمرو بن أوس); WAHIDI-BASIT; DURR-FULL(عمرو بن أوس,هذيل بن شرحبيل)]
- 53:38/q07/p2 A relative, including a brother or son, could be punished for another relative's sin. — Ibn ʿAbbās, as cited by Qurtubī and Durr, speaks of relatives; Wāḥidī specifies brother or son, and Jishumī mentions kin and neighbors. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(ابن عباس ومجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 53:38/q07/p3 For another's killing, a parent, child, sibling, wife, or slave could be killed in their place. — Baghawī transmits Ibn ʿAbbās's specified list and says Abraham prohibited that practice. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة، ابن عباس)]
- 53:38/q07/p4 An ally could be held for another's killing. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya transmits this from Ibn ʿAbbās and says Abraham fulfilled and conveyed the corrective rule. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 53:38/q08 How did Abraham fulfill his duty in relation to this teaching?  [أَلَّا تَزِرُ وَازِرَةٌۭ وِزْرَ أُخْرَىٰ · reports]
turns on: Which act is taken as Abraham's fulfillment: conveying this rule, action after his dream about his son, or seeking forgiveness for his father.
- 53:38/q08/p1 He fulfilled his task by conveying or establishing the rule that none bears another's burden. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid in Jishumī, Mujāhid and ʿIkrima in Durr, Abū al-ʿĀliya, and Ibn ʿAbbās in Qurtubī make delivery of the rule the fulfillment. Ibn ʿAbbās also says Abraham prohibited transferring sins among kin and neighbors; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports an ally held for a killing. Durr transmits that Abraham fulfilled this principle through the passage reaching the ancient scrolls. [9 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عباس ومجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(أبو العالية,ابن عباس,عمرو بن أوس,مجاهد وعكرمة,هذيل بن شرحبيل)]
- 53:38/q08/p2 He completed obedience in his action concerning his son after the dream. — Ibn ʿAbbās gives this report in Ṭabarī and Durr; both also associate the burden rule with Moses' scrolls. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 53:38/q08/p3 He fulfilled his promise to seek forgiveness for his father, but God's refusal taught that each receives only the fruit of their own effort. — Rāzī links Abraham's fulfilled promise, the refusal, and the lessons of 53:38–39; he mentions 12:98. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:38/q09 What part of this passage is said to be in Abraham's and Moses' scrolls?  [أَلَّا تَزِرُ وَازِرَةٌۭ وِزْرَ أُخْرَىٰ · reports]
turns on: The reported starting and ending verses, and whether the attribution expressly names both scrolls or Moses' scrolls.
- 53:38/q09/p1 The rule beginning at 53:38 and the following material through 53:56 were in both scrolls. — Abū Mālik al-Ghifārī gives this extent in Ṭabarī and Qurtubī. Ṭabarī says the statement is cited from the scrolls rather than restricting the meaning of Abraham's fulfillment. Rāzī prefers the rule as the most apparent content but allows that the scrolls contain more. Ibn Jawzī states that the scrolls teach the rule. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو مالك الغفاري); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو مالك الغفاري); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL+]
- 53:38/q09/p2 Everything from 53:36 through 53:56 was in the two scriptures. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites al-Suddī and Abū Ṣāliḥ through Qurtubī with this earlier starting point. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(السدي وأبو صالح)]
- 53:38/q09/p3 This verse and the following clauses introduced by أن are teachings in the scrolls, part of a seventeen-verse set. — Ṭabrisī treats the following anna clauses as continued reported contents. Ṭabāṭabāʾī counts seventeen verses drawn from the two scriptures. Durr reports Ibn ʿAbbās extending the principle to the ancient scrolls without a numbered endpoint. [3 notes: TABRISI; TABATABAI; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 53:38/q09/p4 The no-burden statement is in Moses' scrolls. — Ibn ʿAbbās in Ṭabarī and Durr explicitly assigns the statement to Moses' scrolls while discussing Abraham's completion of obedience. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 53:38/q10 What claim about al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra does the rule refute?  [أَلَّا تَزِرُ وَازِرَةٌۭ وِزْرَ أُخْرَىٰ · reports]
- 53:38/q10/p1 It refutes a promise that someone could be paid to bear al-Walīd's sin or punishment for him. — Ṭabarī reports the promised transfer of punishment; Wāḥidī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya specify payment. Baghawī and Bursevī invoke the same claim, and Ibn ʿĀshūr says the universal wording rejects it. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:38/q11 How can the rule coexist with statements that misleaders bear the burdens of followers?  [أَلَّا تَزِرُ وَازِرَةٌۭ وِزْرَ أُخْرَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the additional burden is one's own liability for causing others' wrongdoing or an unwilling bearing of their burdens.
- 53:38/q11/p1 The leader bears an added burden for their own act of causing or encouraging wrongdoing, while followers retain responsibility for their acts. — Wāḥidī and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite 16:25; Bayḍāwī and Bursevī address 5:32 on killing one resembling killing all. Bursevī calls later murders consequences of the killer's prompting. Elmalılı cites 29:13 for an added burden and a hadith about instituting a bad practice until Judgment Day; Bursevī and Study Quran also give the hadith. Ibn ʿĀshūr gives the first son of Adam's share in every unjust killing because he initiated murder. Study Quran cites 7:38–39 and 33:68 for doubled punishment of those who lead others astray. Ibn ʿĀshūr reads the Torah warning about punishment visiting descendants for three generations as worldly causal effects rather than transferred guilt. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 53:38/q11/p2 One does not voluntarily assume another's sin, whereas another's burden may be borne unwillingly. — Al-Ḥusayn ibn al-Faḍl uses the voluntary versus unwilling distinction to reconcile this verse with 29:13. [1 notes: THALABI(الحسين بن الفضل)]

## 53:38/q12 What inherited-guilt or redemption claims are excluded by this rule?  [أَلَّا تَزِرُ وَازِرَةٌۭ وِزْرَ أُخْرَىٰ · theology]
turns on: Which proposed route for transferring guilt or gaining another's saving merit is being addressed.
- 53:38/q12/p1 It excludes inherited original sin and human guilt. — Asad applies the individual-responsibility principle against the Christian doctrine of original sin. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 53:38/q12/p2 It excludes vicarious atonement through a saint's or prophet's sacrifice, including Christian and Mithraic redemption. — Asad names Christian vicarious atonement and Persian redemption by Mithras as examples. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 53:38/q12/p3 It rules out mediation or baseless intercession between a sinner and God. — Asad draws the implication about mediation. Iṣlāḥī says Quraysh and the People of the Book expect Paradise through alleged intercession, while this scriptural rule refutes it and addresses a subject of the sūrah. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:38/q13 How does the verse challenge reliance on descent from Abraham or Moses?  [أَلَّا تَزِرُ وَازِرَةٌۭ وِزْرَ أُخْرَىٰ · interpretation]
- 53:38/q13/p1 Association with the prophets cannot secure Paradise or rank while one withholds charity; each faces the fruits of personal deeds. — Iṣlāḥī contrasts boasts of Abrahamic or Mosaic association with the prophets' teachings and sacrifices, particularly amid withholding charity. He says the Torah and Injīl repeatedly teach the rule and finds it surprising that their recipients were led into polytheism. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:38/q14 How does this rule connect to the nearby verses and the sūrah's argument?  [* · links]
turns on: Which neighboring passage or thematic progression the commentator uses to explain this rule.
- 53:38/q14/p1 It explains recompense in 53:30–31 and the accountability of the man discussed just before the scrolls passage. — Rāzī aligns the scrolls passage with 53:30–31 because both explain recompense. He reports as one possible object of يرى the man's mistaken belief that another can carry his burden. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:38/q14/p2 It begins a set of enduring scriptural truths following reflection on creation. — Study Quran describes the third section's move from creation to truths attested in Moses' and Abraham's scriptures. Iṣlāḥī says the rule refutes baseless intercession, a subject of the sūrah. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 53:38/q14/p3 The next verse develops the personal-effort principle through different kinds of striving and their outcomes. — Qushayrī distinguishes losing worldly pursuit, profitable pursuit of Paradise, self-discipline reaching God's approval, and thanked spiritual aspiration guided to Him. He says the repentant sinner who seeks forgiveness and regrets a darkened record receives reward, nearness, honor, and intimacy. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 53:38/q15 What other scriptural passages echo or qualify this rule?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the parallel is another Qur'anic statement of the maxim or a related Abrahamic and Mosaic narrative.
- 53:38/q15/p1 Other Qur'anic passages repeat the individual-burden principle. — Asad and Study Quran list 6:164, 17:15, 35:18, and 39:7; Ibn Kathīr also cites 35:18, and Qurtubī points to 6:164. [4 notes: ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 53:38/q15/p2 Abraham's prayer and Torah narratives likewise distinguish the righteous person's fate from another's wrongdoing. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links Abraham's plea in 26:87–89 to avoid shame when wealth and children cannot help, the Torah account of his plea for Lot's people, the Torah ban on killing parents for children or vice versa, and Moses' plea at 7:155 for Israel's fools. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:38/q16 Where does this axiom first appear in revelation chronology?  [* · sciences]
- 53:38/q16/p1 This occurrence is the earliest Qur'anic statement of the axiom in revelation order. — Asad and Study Quran identify four later Qur'anic occurrences: 6:164, 17:15, 35:18, and 39:7; Asad counts five occurrences including this one. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]

## 53:38/q17 What incidental report is preserved among these notes?  [* · reports]
- 53:38/q17/p1 Ibn ʿAbbās says the Kursī is the place of the two feet and the Throne cannot be measured. — ʿAbd al-Razzāq transmits this report from Ibn ʿAbbās; the note gives no connection to the verse. [1 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس)]
