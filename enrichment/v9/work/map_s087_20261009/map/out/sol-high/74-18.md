# 74:18: 13 questions

## 74:18/q01 Who is the man referred to by 'he'?  [إِنَّهُۥ · referent]
- 74:18/q01/p1 Al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra al-Makhzūmī, a Quraysh leader. — Mujāhid locates his deliberation at Dār al-Nadwa (Ṭabarī); al-Naḥḥās says there is no disagreement over the identification and that he repeatedly sought to refute God's signs. Jishumī, citing Ibn ʿĀmir, calls him wealthy, father of sons, and al-Wāḥid among his people. Ibn Kathīr calls him a Quraysh leader. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); JISHUMI; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; NAHHAS+; QURTUBI-FULL+]

## 74:18/q02 What does fakkara mean?  [فَكَّرَ · meaning]
- 74:18/q02/p1 He thought, reflected, considered, or deliberated over a matter. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī gloss it as examining or deliberating over a matter; the lexicon cited by Bursawī gives thinking or reflecting. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls fikr thinking. Elmalılı describes mental thought and estimate. [5 notes: BURSEVI(تاج المصادر); ELMALILI+; RAZI-FULL+; TABATABAI+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]

## 74:18/q03 What was his thinking directed toward?  [فَكَّرَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the object of his calculation was a verbal accusation or the broader conduct of his hostility.
- 74:18/q03/p1 He considered the Qurʾān and Muhammad to devise a plausible accusation against them. — Muqātil says he reckoned Muhammad separated people; Ibn Qutayba lists poet, magician, and soothsayer as accusations. Muqātil via al-Wāḥidī says he privately calculated what to say about Muhammad, while Ibn ʿAbbās there says he thought about the Qurʾān. Al-Ṭabrisī says thought intended to contrive falsehood, unlike praiseworthy thought seeking guidance. Al-Biqāʿī says he repeatedly weighed attacks under desire to choose the most plausible; Ṭabāṭabāʾī lists poetry, soothsaying, madness, and fables before magic. Al-Nasafī and al-Qurṭubī describe preparing words; Ibn ʿĀshūr says he knew the rejected labels were false. Asad describes his effort to disprove the messages, followed by frowning and arrogance. [17 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ASAD-NOTES+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; MUQATIL+; NASAFI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; TABATABAI+; TABRISI+]
- 74:18/q03/p2 He considered his hostility and calculated how to wage it. — Al-Māwardī expressly allows this reading as the object of al-Walīd's reflection and calculation. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL+]

## 74:18/q04 Why did he delay his response after hearing the Qurʾān?  [فَكَّرَ · interpretation]
- 74:18/q04/p1 He wanted onlookers to think their leader was considering it seriously. — Iṣlāḥī says the pause was staged before those watching him. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 74:18/q05 What does qaddara mean here?  [وَقَدَّرَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb stresses weighing an estimate or arranging the words that result; both readings describe his inner deliberation.
- 74:18/q05/p1 He estimated, calculated, or weighed a judgment in his mind. — Al-Wāḥidī glosses it as estimating or calculating. Elmalılı speaks of mental measuring and guessing. Al-Biqāʿī says he weighed possible attacks for plausibility; Iṣlāḥī says he carefully weighed an opinion so listeners would find it credible. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL+; ELMALILI+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 74:18/q05/p2 He arranged and prepared what he would say. — Al-Rāzī says reflection came first, then arranging and preparing words in his heart. Al-Qurṭubī cites Arabic qaddartu for 'I prepared something'; al-Nasafī likewise describes devising words. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says taqdīr mentally orders meanings and qualities by precedence, affirmation, and negation to reach a desired conclusion. [4 notes: NASAFI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; TABATABAI+]

## 74:18/q06 What accusation did he settle on?  [وَقَدَّرَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the reported final dismissal is a charge of sorcery or a disdainful reduction of the Qurʾān to the prophet's eloquence.
- 74:18/q06/p1 He called Muhammad a sorcerer and the Qurʾān magic, often said to be transmitted from others. — Al-Walīd first rejected madness, poetry, soothsaying, and sometimes lying: al-Zajjāj says conversation disproved madness, Arabs knew poetry, and Muhammad said 'if God wills'; al-Baghawī recalls his prior title al-Amīn. Qatāda and others report al-Walīd praising the Qurʾān's sweetness, beauty, fruitfulness, supremacy, and force before the magic charge; Ibn ʿAbbās even reports he initially called it God's speech (Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr). Muqātil, Jishumī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, and al-Bayḍāwī give its alleged division of families and allies as his rationale. The provenance varies: al-Zajjāj names Musaylima and Babylon; Ibn Zayd, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn Kathīr, and the Qurʾān Yolu report describe inherited, learned, or transmitted magic. Qurʾān Yolu adds the accusation of human speech. Al-Ṭabarī's Qatāda places his frowning after the judgment; al-Durr says the condemnation through his sullenness was then revealed. [31 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة); ZAJJAJ(الوليد بن المغيرة); NAHHAS(قتادة); WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن عباس,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,مقاتل وعكرمة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الوليد بن المغيرة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزهري وجماعة); TABRISI(أبو جهل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي,عكرمة); DURR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد عن ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة)+; MUQATIL+; TABATABAI+]
- 74:18/q06/p2 He treated the Qurʾān as the prophet's stirring eloquence and dismissed its warning with disdain. — Iṣlāḥī says the arrogant leader's excuse ignored that the Qurʾān's statements were realities he would soon encounter. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 74:18/q07 How does this clause relate to the preceding threat and description of obstinacy?  [إِنَّهُۥ فَكَّرَ وَقَدَّرَ · grammar]
turns on: Which earlier statement the clause explains, and whether explanation is an independent sentence or a grammatical substitution.
- 74:18/q07/p1 It explains why he deserves the preceding threat. — Al-Samīn calls it an independent explanatory sentence for 'I shall burden him'; Abū Ḥayyān reports that explanation. Al-Bayḍāwī and Elmalılı allow a causal relation. Al-Ālūsī says it may explain his deserving the threat. Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī tie the threat to his extreme defiance and magic accusation, followed by poverty, humiliation, and later torment. [7 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KASHSHAF-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+]
- 74:18/q07/p2 It explains and completes his obstinacy toward God's signs without syntactic substitution. — Al-Bayḍāwī and Elmalılı give this as an alternative relation. Al-Ālūsī says this explanatory reading has no syntactic position. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the threat is parenthetical between the obstinacy and this explanation, bringing early warning for him and comfort for the Prophet (74:16). [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 74:18/q07/p3 It is an appositional substitute for the statement that he was obstinate toward God's signs. — Al-Kashshāf proposes substitution for 'he was obstinate toward Our signs'; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī report the same parsing. Al-Bayḍāwī's alternative says the clause explains his defiance without specifying substitution. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 74:18/q08 What encounter led to al-Walīd's deliberation, according to the reports?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on the setting and recited passage: mosque recitation of Ghāfir, a Quraysh council, an unspecified hearing, prayer, a personal summons, or Ḥā Mīm al-Sajda.
- 74:18/q08/p1 He heard the Prophet recite the opening of Ghāfir in the mosque and praised it before the later accusation. — Al-Thaʿlabī cites 40:1–3; al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, and al-Ṭabrisī say the Prophet repeated it on noticing al-Walīd listening. He praised its sweetness, elegance, force, and superiority to human or jinn speech. Al-Qurṭubī says Quraysh then heard those verses from al-Walīd and feared his acceptance. [5 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل وعكرمة); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI+]
- 74:18/q08/p2 Quraysh consulted at a council or pilgrimage gathering about what to tell people concerning Muhammad. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Wāḥidī's al-Kalbī place the issue before pilgrims; al-Zajjāj has al-Walīd ask leaders how to answer growing interest. Ibn Kathīr's al-Suddī says the council sought to deter pilgrims; Mujāhid via al-Wāḥidī and Ibn ʿAbbās via al-Durr report competing labels. Ṭabarī's Mujāhid names Dār al-Nadwa; Study Quran says al-Walīd convened the leaders, while Qurʾān Yolu says the polytheists asked him. [9 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ(الوليد بن المغيرة); WAHIDI-ASBAB(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي); DURR-FULL(مجاهد عن ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 74:18/q08/p3 He heard and admired the Qurʾān, then faced pressure from Abū Jahl before denouncing it; the recitation setting is unspecified. — Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Ṭabarī says he initially called it God's speech; al-Wāḥidī's Ibn ʿAbbās says its language was sweet and unlike poetry. Ibn ʿAṭiyya's exegetes say he nearly converted and visited Abū Bakr; Ibn Kathīr's ʿIkrima says he softened. Ibn Isḥāq and other historians are reported by Ibn Kathīr to give a similar account. Qatāda's versions in al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās, al-Māwardī, and al-Durr preserve his praise of sweetness and superiority. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); NAHHAS(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزهري وجماعة,جمهور المفسرين); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة,محمد بن إسحاق); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 74:18/q08/p4 He heard the Prophet recite while praying, found the speech sweet and gripping, and considered a charge of earlier magic. — Ibn Zayd in al-Ṭabarī specifies the prayer setting and says al-Walīd ruled out poetry and soothsaying. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد)]
- 74:18/q08/p5 The Prophet personally invited or summoned him, and he thought before giving his judgment. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk in al-Ṭabarī says Muhammad invited him to Islam and he delayed before frowning and turning away; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the Prophet told him to look and think. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك); IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 74:18/q08/p6 He heard the Prophet recite Ḥā Mīm al-Sajda, praised its eloquence, then denounced it after Abū Jahl's provocation. — Al-Bayḍāwī gives this chapter in the report and says Quraysh rejoiced at the magic charge; he states no grading. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 74:18/q09 How did Abū Jahl press al-Walīd to denounce the message?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the reproach was chiefly his apparent conversion or the more specific allegation that he praised Muhammad for material gain.
- 74:18/q09/p1 He warned that Quraysh believed al-Walīd had converted or apostatized and pressed him to satisfy them. — Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Ṭabarī says Abū Jahl shamed him over feared conversion; al-Wāḥidī's Muqātil and ʿIkrima say Quraysh said he converted; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says Quraysh reproached him. Al-Baghawī reports Quraysh feared the tribe would follow; al-Ṭabrisī says Abū Jahl urged dismissal. Al-Wāḥidī's Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Kathīr's ʿIkrima likewise report pressure without specifying the money allegation. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل وعكرمة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قريش); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور المفسرين); TABRISI(أبو جهل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 74:18/q09/p2 He alleged that al-Walīd praised Muhammad for food or money, provoking a denial of need. — Al-Zajjāj reports Abū Jahl's claim that people spoke of apostasy and would collect wealth for him; al-Walīd answered that he was among the richest. Al-Wāḥidī's al-Kalbī says Abū Jahl accused him of converting for food. Al-Baghawī says Muhammad's companions lacked spare food; al-Qurṭubī likewise gives the food accusation and al-Walīd's denial. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ(أبو جهل); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جهل); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 74:18/q10 Why is his deliberation condemned, and what does it bring?  [* · interpretation]
- 74:18/q10/p1 He knowingly contrived falsehood against the signs, turning others from faith and bringing punishment on himself. — Al-Ṭabrisī contrasts this aim with praiseworthy thought seeking guidance. Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī connect his magic charge to the threat: loss after wealth, humiliation after honor, and severe afterlife torment. Ibn Kathīr links it to distance from faith; al-Biqāʿī says he barred others from faith and lost beneficial life in both worlds. Ibn ʿĀshūr says he knew the charges of magic, poetry, or soothsaying were false (74:24–25). Asad traces his attempt to disprove the messages through searching for arguments, frowning, and arrogant withdrawal. [7 notes: ASAD-NOTES+; BIQAI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; TABRISI+]

## 74:18/q11 Does the condemnation extend beyond al-Walīd?  [* · interpretation]
- 74:18/q11/p1 Verses 18–25 also condemn anyone who adopts al-Walīd's unbelieving posture toward the Qurʾān. — Qurʾān Yolu makes the general application explicit. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]

## 74:18/q12 What does the repeated curse following this verse convey?  [* · links]
- 74:18/q12/p1 The second denunciation is stronger than the first. — Al-Zamakhsharī, as cited by al-Burhān, reads the second thumma in the repeated curse as increasing its emphasis. [1 notes: BURHAN(الزمخشري)]

## 74:18/q13 How does the sūra warn about the Hereafter's arrival?  [* · links]
- 74:18/q13/p1 Its exact time is known only to God, but its signs show it may arrive suddenly; ignoring it because the time is unknown is folly. — Iṣlāḥī says its burden may split the sky and disclose the Day without warning while people are heedless, citing 7:187 on its burden on heaven and earth and sudden coming. He likens the visible signs to a pregnancy near delivery and calls indifference to the Hereafter unfortunate. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
