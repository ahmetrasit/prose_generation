# 23:33: 16 questions

## 23:33/q01 Why is the chiefs’ speech introduced with wāw here?  [وَقَالَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the conjunction marks coordination of opposed speech, delayed public response, or the narrative parallel; Ālūsī questions whether the proposed contrasts determine the form.
- 23:33/q01/p1 The wāw coordinates their false speech with the messenger’s true call and makes their contrast present together. — Jishumī calls it a link between successive statements. Kashshāf, Rāzī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursawī, and al-Kashf as reported by Ālūsī contrast the asyndetic, implied-question replies in al-Aʿrāf and Hūd (7:66; 11:27 or 11:53); Nasafī contrasts Noah’s immediate fa (23:24). Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this coordination with the preceding account rather than a separate dialogue turn. Ālūsī finds the contrast account insufficient to explain why the passages chose different forms. [9 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 23:33/q01/p2 The wāw marks that the chiefs’ words were not an immediate reply to the messenger. — Bayḍāwī proposes delayed speech rather than direct succession, assuming an implicit question. Bursawī and a proposal reported by Ālūsī compare Noah’s immediate fa (23:24). Ibn ʿĀshūr says the messenger repeated his call before chiefs addressed the public; the preceding call uses explanatory أن after the sending, not direct quotation (23:32). [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:33/q01/p3 The conjunction reinforces consolation through the parallel with Noah’s people. — Biqāʿī says the pattern of unbelief and failure to fear God recurs, making the account stronger consolation for the Prophet. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 23:33/q01/p4 No specific explanation for the differing connective forms is established. — Ālūsī objects that exchanging wāw and asyndeton could also create variation, so the proposed contrast account does not decide the choice. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 23:33/q02 Who are the malāʾ?  [ٱلْمَلَأُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term is glossed by social rank or by a united, awe-inspiring group.
- 23:33/q02/p1 They are the prominent chiefs, nobles, and leading figures of the community. — Muqātil, Jishumī, Qurṭubī, Ālūsī, and others identify the leaders. Biqāʿī adds that their appearance fills onlookers’ chests; Bursawī says the description of them as unbelievers censures them. [6 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 23:33/q02/p2 The term denotes a group united in opinion, impressive in appearance. — Al-Rāghib, reported by Bursawī, says their presence fills eyes with awe and souls with grandeur. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الراغب)]

## 23:33/q03 Why does 'of his people' precede 'who disbelieved'?  [مِن قَوْمِهِ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the relative clause modifies the chiefs or their people, and whether its length creates ambiguity.
- 23:33/q03/p1 The order identifies the chiefs clearly before the long description and avoids awkward or misleading attachment. — Al-Sakkākī as reported in Burhān says delay could make the phrase appear to describe worldly life. Burhān says it clarifies the speakers; Biqāʿī says it specifies consolation. Burhān al-Qurʾān as reported by Bursawī compares the shorter Noah clause (23:24), where ordinary order works. A proposal reported by Ālūsī avoids separating noun and identifying phrase. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the order preserves the sequence 'disbelieved, denied, indulged' and avoids excessive length. [6 notes: BURHAN(السكاكي); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(برهان القرآن); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI]
- 23:33/q03/p2 The order keeps the following coordinated descriptions attached to 'who disbelieved'. — Ibn ʿĀshūr takes الذين كفروا as a second adjective of الملأ, parallel to Noah’s elite (23:24), and says its later place lets the following descriptions coordinate with it. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:33/q03/p3 No special ordering explanation is necessary if 'who disbelieved' describes 'his people'. — Ālūsī reports an objection that the relative clause can plainly modify قومه. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 23:33/q04 Whom does 'who disbelieved' describe, and what does the description do?  [ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical antecedent of الذين: الملأ or قومه.
- 23:33/q04/p1 It describes the chiefs, censuring or distinguishing these particular nobles. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a second adjective of الملأ, comparing 23:24. Ālūsī says it disparages them and may distinguish them if other nobles believed; Bursawī also sees censure. Nasafī allows this attachment. [5 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:33/q04/p2 It describes the people from whom the chiefs came. — Nasafī allows the description to cover the whole people. Ālūsī reports the objection that it can directly modify قومه, obviating an order problem. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 23:33/q05 What is their disbelief?  [كَفَرُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: The scope of the unbelief identified in the chiefs’ description.
- 23:33/q05/p1 They rejected divine oneness or the Creator, covering up known proofs. — Muqātil and Ṭabarī connect disbelief with denying God’s oneness; Biqāʿī says they concealed proofs of oneness and idolaters’ punishment. Rāzī lists disbelief in the Creator alongside denial of the Hereafter and indulgence. [4 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 23:33/q05/p2 Their unbelief includes rejection of monotheism, prophecy, and resurrection. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī links all three denials to the chiefs’ later arguments and luxurious life. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 23:33/q06 What does 'meeting the Hereafter' mean?  [بِلِقَآءِ ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the encounter is resurrection into the next life, its consequences, or meeting God there.
- 23:33/q06/p1 Resurrection or arrival in a second life, with judgment or recompense. — Muqātil says deeds are repaid upon resurrection; Jishumī gives resurrection and judgment as an alternative; Baghawī calls it return to the afterlife. Ṭabrisī, Qurṭubī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Biqāʿī, Bursawī, and Ālūsī mention resurrection, gathering, judgment, or the second life. [10 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 23:33/q06/p2 Encountering the Hereafter’s reckoning, reward, and punishment or promised recompense. — Jishumī offers the promised recompense as an alternative. Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursawī, and Ālūsī specify the afterlife’s contents, often reckoning, reward, and punishment. [7 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 23:33/q06/p3 Meeting God in the next life for reckoning. — Ṭabarī says the encounter is with God; Ibn ʿĀshūr explains لقاء as one person’s presence with another and construes its attachment to الآخرة as 'in the Hereafter,' citing 2:223 and 8:45. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:33/q07 What does 'We made them affluent' mean?  [وَأَتْرَفْنَٰهُمْ · meaning]
- 23:33/q07/p1 God gave them ease, wealth, abundant provision, comfort, and worldly luxury. — Yaḥyā says provision expanded; Ibn Qutayba links turfa to tuḥfa, gift; Ṭabarī cites the rajaz 'وقد أراني بالديار مترفا' for affluence. Thaʿlabī calls tarf blessing; Jishumī says indulgence and freedom without restraint. Bayḍāwī and Nasafī specify wealth and children; Ibn ʿĀshūr defines it as causing a life of luxury, refers to 21:13. Asad transmits Ṭabarī’s ease and plenty and points to 11:116. Study Quran compares 11:116, 17:16, 21:13. Several add that abundance could breed insolence. [24 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Tabari); STUDYQURAN]

## 23:33/q08 How is 'We made them affluent' joined to the description?  [وَأَتْرَفْنَٰهُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause is another relative description or a ḥāl of the chiefs/denial.
- 23:33/q08/p1 It is coordinated into the relative description of the disbelieving chiefs. — Abū Ḥayyān allows this and reads their denial despite divine favor as its cause. Ālūsī calls conjunction grammatically apparent; a reply he reports favors it because chiefs are customarily affluent. Ibn ʿĀshūr’s analysis of the delayed relative clause accommodates the following coordinated descriptions; Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes the ordered sequence of three descriptions. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 23:33/q08/p2 It is a circumstantial clause describing the chiefs’ state when they denied the messenger. — Abū Ḥayyān allows this with the same ingratitude implication. Ālūsī says the circumstance can attach to the chiefs, with or without قد, and rejects restricting affluence to chiefs; he prefers it as more eloquent because it heightens blame for mistreating the benefactor. The reported reply also allows a circumstance from كذبوا. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 23:33/q09 How did worldly luxury affect their rejection?  [وَأَتْرَفْنَٰهُمْ فِى ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا · interpretation]
turns on: The notes connect luxury either to moral arrogance and loss of accountability, or to the chiefs’ social and political interests.
- 23:33/q09/p1 Ease fostered arrogance, rebellion, ingratitude, and heedlessness of accountability. — Ṭabarī says plenty made them insolent and rebellious; Qurṭubī and Bursawī link luxury to arrogance. Biqāʿī says mistaken worldly happiness blocks afterlife felicity and gratitude. Ibn ʿĀshūr says denial removes fear of accountability, while wealth strengthens arrogance and attachment to rule (73:11). Asad says ease and plenty corrupted them. Rāzī lists the three traits together. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 23:33/q09/p2 Wealth and power gave elites a stake in the existing order, making prophetic reform threatening. — Kur’an Yolu says the wealthy feared accountability and a society conscious of universal rights and justice would threaten their status. Ṭabāṭabāʾī links their objections to indulgent worldly life. Study Quran notes Noah’s notables feared he wanted power (23:24), as a nearby example. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 23:33/q10 Whom did the chiefs address, and why?  [وَقَالَ ٱلْمَلَأُ · interpretation]
- 23:33/q10/p1 They spoke among their own people or descendants to divert them from the messenger. — Bursawī says they misled their descendants, aided by wealth and children. Ibn ʿĀshūr says they chose to stop the call influencing the public rather than reply to the messenger; after repeated calls they turned to their people. Al-Kashf, reported by Ālūsī, also describes talk among themselves. [4 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف)]

## 23:33/q11 Who is the messenger denoted by 'this', and which people are meant?  [مِن قَوْمِهِ هَٰذَآ · referent]
turns on: The narrative identification differs: Hūd and his people, Ṣāliḥ and Thamūd, or an unnamed messenger from among them.
- 23:33/q11/p1 The messenger is Hūd, speaking to his own people. — Muqātil and Bursawī name Hūd; Nasafī says the elite are of Hūd’s people and answer his call. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes direct replies to Hūd elsewhere (11:53–54). [6 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:33/q11/p2 The messenger is Ṣāliḥ and his people are Thamūd. — Ṭabarī explicitly identifies the prophet after Noah as Ṣāliḥ and the community as Thamūd; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes a direct answer to Ṣāliḥ elsewhere (11:62). [2 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:33/q11/p3 He is one of their own people, the messenger sent to call them to worship God alone. — Ibn Kathīr describes the sending and the call with 23:32–33 but does not name him. Nasafī says 'this' refers to the prophet. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]

## 23:33/q12 What is the force of calling him an ordinary human who eats and drinks like them?  [مَا هَٰذَآ إِلَّا بَشَرٌۭ مِّثْلُكُمْ يَأْكُلُ مِمَّا تَأْكُلُونَ مِنْهُ وَيَشْرَبُ مِمَّا تَشْرَبُونَ · interpretation]
- 23:33/q12/p1 The chiefs belittle the messenger and infer that shared humanity and bodily needs exclude any superior claim to prophethood. — Muqātil, Ṭabrisī, Qurṭubī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Biqāʿī, Bursawī, Ālūsī and others describe the claimed lack of distinction; Biqāʿī and Ālūsī stress the belittling force of 'this.' Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the humanity claim metonymy for denying prophecy: the supposed premise is stated and the conclusion intended. Food and drink supply the evidence; Bursawī’s Persian gloss expects an angel-like prophet needing neither. Rāzī adds that they call obeying such a man loss because it gives no return, despite not deeming idol worship loss. Yaḥyā, Thaʿlabī, and Jishumī report the warning that followers lose. Kur’an Yolu says the tactic diverts attention from the religious and moral message. Study Quran compares the complaint about Muhammad eating and walking in markets (25:7); Asad notes the same opponents also reject resurrection after dust and bones. [26 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 23:33/q13 How is the second 'what you drink' construed without a stated 'from it'?  [مِمَّا تَشْرَبُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā is relative or a verbal noun; if relative, whether a genitive partitive link or direct object is omitted.
- 23:33/q13/p1 The relative mā has an omitted genitive resumptive and its preposition, understood from the parallel eating clause. — Kashshāf and Nasafī supply 'from your drink' or 'from it.' Farrāʾ, reported by Qurṭubī, Samīn, and Ālūsī, posits omitted min. Bayḍāwī allows the preposition understood from the first clause. Samīn says the deletion meets matching governor/preposition and other syntactic conditions and rejects a blanket Basran prohibition. Ālūsī says the clause-ending rhyme helps; he criticizes the alternatives for losing parallelism. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL(البصريون)-; ALUSI-FULL(التحرير)-]
- 23:33/q13/p2 The relative mā has an omitted direct object pronoun, with drink taking its object directly. — Qurṭubī’s Basran account, Bayḍāwī, and al-Taḥrīr as reported by Ālūsī allow this; Samīn details conditions for object-pronoun deletion. Ibn ʿĀshūr says its equivalent in the previous relative clause licenses omission. Burhān rejects this reading because 'drink' already takes a direct object; Ālūsī says it loses the partitive parallel and still requires interpreting the drink as the same kind rather than the very same liquid. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(البصريون); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(التحرير); IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 23:33/q13/p3 Mā is a verbal noun, so no resumptive pronoun or deletion is needed. — Qurṭubī’s Basran alternative, Samīn, and al-Taḥrīr as reported by Ālūsī give this analysis: the verbal noun fills the object place, with the sense of what they drink. Ālūsī criticizes its weaker parallelism and the need to construe different portions of one kind of drink. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(البصريون); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(التحرير); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 23:33/q14 What proof and obligation are implied by the rejection of the messenger?  [مَا هَٰذَآ إِلَّا بَشَرٌۭ مِّثْلُكُمْ · theology]
- 23:33/q14/p1 A sign establishing his claim is implied, and following becomes obligatory once proof reaches its intended audience. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says a miracle was shown and denied although the passage leaves its identity unspecified. Punishment follows abandonment of an obligation only after proof is established. He compares the Qurʾān as proof for Arabs, Jesus’ miracles for physicians, and Moses’ signs for magicians. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 23:33/q15 What report about eating is cited in connection with the verse?  [يَأْكُلُ · reports]
- 23:33/q15/p1 The Prophet said that a believer eats in one intestine and an unbeliever in seven. — Bursawī reports the prophetic saying. [1 notes: BURSEVI(النبي ﷺ)]

## 23:33/q16 How does this verse fit the surrounding account and sūrah?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes locate the verse in different recurring strands: Noah’s story, wealthy opposition, the afterlife theme, and the account’s warning.
- 23:33/q16/p1 It repeats the pattern of elite rejection seen in Noah’s story and elsewhere. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares the objection to Noah’s people. Biqāʿī says the parallel strengthens consolation for the Prophet. Elmalılı says this account especially highlights denial of the Hereafter and luxury, seen also among contemporary unbelievers. Study Quran says Noah’s struggle follows mention of worldly blessings, recalls the notables’ fear he sought rule (23:24), and notes recurring affluent opposition across the sūrah and 7:59–62, 7:136. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 23:33/q16/p2 Its afterlife denial belongs to the sūrah’s recurring sequence of death, resurrection, and return to God. — Sāmarrāʾī calls resurrection the natural final link in life’s development (39:31) and cites 23:16, 35–37, 60, 74, 80, 82–83 for the repeated theme. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 23:33/q16/p3 The passage warns of the rejecters’ painful fate while fuller replies to the denials appear elsewhere. — Kur’an Yolu says the Qurʾān answers objections to the prophet’s humanity and to the Hereafter in other places; this account concentrates on the outcome. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
