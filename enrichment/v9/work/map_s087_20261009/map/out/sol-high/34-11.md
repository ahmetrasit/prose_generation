# 34:11: 28 questions

## 34:11/q01 How does an introduce the command to make armor?  [أَنِ ٱعْمَلْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the preceding softening of iron implies a speech act, or an omitted preposition expresses purpose.
- 34:11/q01/p1 It explains an implied divine command to David. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq supply 'We said to him'; an explanatory an can introduce an imperative. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the pliability of iron implies divine inspiration to use it. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي وغيره)-; SAMIN-DURR(الحوفي)-; ALUSI-FULL(الحوفي وغيره)-]
- 34:11/q01/p2 It is infinitival or purposive after an omitted preposition: iron was softened so David could make armor. — Al-Zajjāj also allows purpose; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī prefer the omitted lām analysis. They object that explanatory an needs an earlier expression of speech; inventing and deleting 'He commanded him' is unnecessary or unattested. Al-Ālūsī also mentions estimating 'He commanded him to make.' [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]

## 34:11/q02 What are the sābighāt David is told to make?  [سَٰبِغَٰتٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the adjective names full armor, as the overwhelming lexical and report tradition says, or is applied to deeds.
- 34:11/q02/p1 Complete, ample coats of mail, often long enough to cover the wearer fully. — Muqātil, al-Layth, al-Qurṭubī and others explain sabagha by fullness, breadth, and extension, even to the ground. Al-Māwardī, al-Hudhalī, ʿAbd Allāh b. al-Zubayr, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī cite poetic uses; the latter notes that armor became the conventional referent. Ibn Zayd and Ibn ʿAbbās specify iron. [47 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس,الليث,الهذلي,عبد الله بن الزبير,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(شاعر); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الشاعر); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(المعري); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; MAWARDI-FULL]
- 34:11/q02/p2 Abundant, perfect deeds. — Asad reads the adjective substantively, appealing to the related asbagha at 31:20 for abundance and perfection. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 34:11/q03 Does sābighāt stand for an omitted noun?  [سَٰبِغَٰتٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether sābighāt is parsed as an adjective of suppressed 'coats' or as an established noun.
- 34:11/q03/p1 Yes; it is an accusative adjective for omitted coats of mail. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Naḥḥās, Abū Isḥāq, Ibn al-Jawzī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say the noun is understood from the distinctive adjective. Al-Naḥḥās and Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguish feminine iron armor from masculine women's armor. Al-Itqān compares other omitted nouns before adjectives. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNASHUR-FULL; ITQAN]
- 34:11/q03/p2 No omitted noun is needed; sābighāt itself conventionally names mail. — Al-Ālūsī says this usage does not challenge the grammar rule limiting deletion of a noun before an adjective. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 34:11/q04 Is there a variant reading of sābighāt?  [سَٰبِغَٰتٍۢ · readings]
- 34:11/q04/p1 Yes: ṣābighāt, with ṣād in place of sīn. — Al-Zamakhsharī reports an accusative plural form. Abū Ḥayyān calls ṣād a dialect form and al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī explain accommodation to the following ghayn; they relate it to asbagha in 31:20. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قارئ)]

## 34:11/q05 What kind of armor did David introduce?  [ٱعْمَلْ سَٰبِغَٰتٍۢ · reports]
turns on: Whether 'first armor' refers specifically to linked mail or to armor generally.
- 34:11/q05/p1 He first made linked mail; earlier armor was iron sheets or plates. — Muqātil contrasts hammered plates with nails and rings made without fire or hammering. Qatāda and many later sources repeat the transition; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī call plates heavy. Kur'an Yolu describes David's armor as shirt shaped. [19 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 34:11/q05/p2 David was the first to make armor in any form. — Al-Ṭabrisī says 'first to make armor'; Ibn Kathīr's transmitted Qatāda report says 'first human to make armor,' while its comparison to earlier sheets suggests the distinctive invention was linked mail. [2 notes: TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 34:11/q06 How was the iron made workable for David?  [ٱعْمَلْ سَٰبِغَٰتٍۢ · reports]
turns on: Whether softening describes transformed iron, a power in David, or taught metallurgy.
- 34:11/q06/p1 God made the iron soft and pliable like wax, dough, or wet clay, without fire or hammering. — Qatāda, al-Ḥasan, al-Suddī and Ibn ʿAbbās supply the similes; Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Durr describe effortless shaping. Al-Ṭabrisī connects this gift to David's wish to earn by his work. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة,الحسن,السدي); DURR-FULL(الحسن,قتادة)]
- 34:11/q06/p2 David was given a special power to soften iron. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this as another account. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]
- 34:11/q06/p3 God revealed knowledge of iron smelting and armor construction to David. — Islāḥī presents divine instruction in the craft as the enabling gift and says the invention advanced defensive weaponry. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 34:11/q06/p4 The softening means ordinary extraction of iron with fire and tools. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this from certain Bāṭinī interpreters or those like them, who similarly explain flowing copper by ordinary extraction. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعض الباطنية أو من يشبههم)]

## 34:11/q07 Why did David seek armor making as a livelihood?  [ٱعْمَلْ سَٰبِغَٰتٍۢ · reports]
turns on: Whether the report includes the angel's reproach or simply David's request.
- 34:11/q07/p1 After criticism for supporting his family from the public treasury, David asked God for an independent trade and was taught armor making. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, Muqātil via Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī, and Kur'an Yolu recount a disguised angel; some say David had gone incognito to ask about himself. Al-Ṣādiq's account says he wept forty mornings. Ibn Kathīr notes criticism of Isḥāq b. Bishr in Wahb's transmission. [12 notes: THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; TABRISI(الصادق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(وهب بن منبه); ALUSI-FULL(الصادق,مقاتل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Zemahşerî; İbn Atıyye)]
- 34:11/q07/p2 David prayed to support himself by work, and God granted softened iron and the craft. — Wahb's report in Ibn Kathīr gives the prayer and gift; al-Ṭabrisī says he wanted to live from his work without supplying the angel episode. [2 notes: TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]

## 34:11/q08 How much did David sell his armor for and how were the proceeds used?  [ٱعْمَلْ سَٰبِغَٰتٍۢ · reports]
turns on: The transmitted sale amounts and allocations differ.
- 34:11/q08/p1 One suit sold for 6,000 dirhams, with 2,000 for his family and 4,000 for feeding Israelites. — Damra or Ibn Shawdhab's report names the allocation; al-Māwardī says hawari bread and al-Ālūsī fine white bread. Al-Būrsawī preserves four or six thousand. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن شوذب); DURR-FULL(ابن شوذب); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن شوذب); MAWARDI-FULL(ضمرة بن شوذب)]
- 34:11/q08/p2 A suit sold for 4,000, supporting David's household and giving alms to the poor. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī report this price; al-Būrsawī also lists it. [5 notes: THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 34:11/q08/p3 A suit was worth 1,000 dirhams. — Muqātil via Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī gives this price; Abū Ḥayyān says he made one in part of a day or night, and spent a third of his wealth on public needs. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل); ALUSI-FULL(مقاتل)]
- 34:11/q08/p4 David made 360 suits and sold them for 360,000 dirhams. — Al-Ṣādiq via al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ālūsī says this made him independent of the public treasury. [2 notes: TABRISI(الصادق); ALUSI-FULL(الصادق)]
- 34:11/q08/p5 He sold armor for a high price, lived from it, and gave charity, without a fixed sum. — Ibn al-Jawzī says he shaped iron like dough and made a coat within part of a day. Al-Ṭabrisī and Wahb through Ibn Kathīr mention support and charity; Wahb describes portions of the sales. [3 notes: TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); IBNKATHIR-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]

## 34:11/q09 What does qaddir mean here?  [وَقَدِّرْ · meaning]
- 34:11/q09/p1 Set the work to a suitable, moderate proportion. — Al-Zajjāj says the measure must fit the need; Ibn ʿĀshūr defines qaddir as setting a determinate proportion. Al-Baghawī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Islāḥī stress accurate sizing of the construction. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 34:11/q10 What part of the mail must David proportion?  [وَقَدِّرْ فِى ٱلسَّرْدِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the measured element is rivet fit, ring or weave design, or the amount of time spent on the craft.
- 34:11/q10/p1 Proportion the rivets to the rings or holes so they neither slip loose nor split the mail. — Mujāhid, Ibn ʿAbbās, Muqātil and many others give the thin/thick and wide/narrow tests. Al-Ḥakam adds that over-hammering can split a ring; al-Ṭabrisī notes variant explanations of what thin rivets damage. Al-Wāḥidī prefers this as the exegetes' common fit with sard as nails. [29 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(الحكم,مجاهد); NAHHAS(مجاهد); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد، والحكم بن عتيبة، وقتادة وغيرهم); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة ومجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة)+; BAYDAWI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 34:11/q10/p2 Make the linked rings or weave proportionate, avoiding gaps or weakness and preserving protection and mobility. — Ibn Zayd says small rings weaken and large rings expose the wearer; Qatāda says not to widen them. Bayḍāwī prefers proportionate weaving because he thinks David's mail lacked rivets. Al-Biqāʿī adds resistance to arrows and swords and ease of movement in heat and cold. Kur'an Yolu prefers measured weave from context. [24 notes: ZAJJAJ(التفسير); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî; İbn Atıyye); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 34:11/q10/p3 Make the craft only to the extent needed for livelihood, leaving time for worship. — Al-Būrsawī urges economical work; al-Ālūsī reports the same gloss and says the following moral command supports it. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 34:11/q11 What is the implied object of 'measure'?  [وَقَدِّرْ · grammar]
- 34:11/q11/p1 The omitted object is the mail's rings and rivets. — Al-Ṭabrisī makes this explicit; the detailed sizing reports supply the practical referent. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 34:11/q12 What does sard denote?  [ٱلسَّرْدِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the immediate reference is the assembled mail, its rings, rivets or holes, and how the craft sense extends from ordinary joining and succession.
- 34:11/q12/p1 Linked armor making or weaving, with each element fastened to the next. — Al-Zajjāj, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī derive this from orderly succession; Ibn Qutayba and others call the maker sarād/sarrād or zarād/zarrād, with s/z alternation. Abū Dhuʾayb, al-Hudhalī, al-Aʿshā, Labīd, al-Shammākh and other poets attest masrūd mail and linked construction; Maʿarrī evokes David's armor. Islāḥī calls it construction; Asad stresses consecutive continuation. [52 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العلم بكلام العرب); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,ابن قتيبة,الزجاج,الليث,المبرد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو ذؤيب,لبيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشماخ); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الزجاج); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(البحر,الراغب,الشاعر,الشماخ); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو ذؤيب الهذلي,المعري); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; MAWARDI-FULL(الهذلي)]
- 34:11/q12/p2 The nails or rivets fastening the rings. — Qatāda and al-Mubarrad define sard by fastenings; al-Wāḥidī prefers Ibn Qutayba's nail gloss because sizing reports concern rivets. Al-Aʿshā's verse supports secured nails. [9 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة ومجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة)+]
- 34:11/q12/p3 The iron rings or links themselves. — Ibn Zayd cites a poem about widening and perfecting rings; Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Abī Ḥātim identify the iron links as rings. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 34:11/q12/p4 The holes or perforation of the mail, including closing gaps. — Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Ṭabarī and al-Māwardī gives this sense; al-Māwardī cites Labīd's description of David's interwoven armor. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 34:11/q12/p5 Stitching, piercing, or sewing thick material; by extension interlacing iron. — Ibn Qutayba cites al-Shammākh for sewing and names a misrad/awl; al-Qurṭubī adds the thong used in sewing. Abū ʿUbayda compares ship fastening with fiber, while al-Biqāʿī likens linking metal to leather stitching. Rāghib calls thick-material stitching the original sense. [9 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; QURTUBI-FULL(الشماخ); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب,الشماخ)]
- 34:11/q12/p6 An ordered, continuous sequence, as in speech, reading, fasting, or connected things. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Shammākh provide sequential speech and threading; the Prophet's three consecutive sacred months and ʿĀʾisha's report of his distinct speech exemplify use and its limit. Sībawayh relates sarandī, bold, to pressing forward. Abū Ḥayyān adds rapid Qurʾān recitation; Asad says repeated stages. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ(سيبويه); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(سيبويه,عائشة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشماخ); BIQAI-FULL(الزجاج); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(البحر,الشماخ); ASAD-NOTES; MAWARDI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 34:11/q12/p7 The making or construction of an object generally. — Islāḥī supplies this broad gloss. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 34:11/q13 Were David's mail rings fastened with rivets?  [ٱلسَّرْدِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether pliable iron made rivets unnecessary; the transmitted rivet instructions and the unidentified observer's claim are contested.
- 34:11/q13/p1 Yes, the ring ends were nailed or riveted together. — Muqātil, al-Mubarrad, Abū ʿUbayda and several others describe nailed rings; reports on rivet sizing presume this construction. Abū Dhuʾayb's masrūdatān is cited for nailed or linked mail. [8 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,الليث,المبرد); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 34:11/q13/p2 Probably no: pliable iron allowed ring ends to join without rivets. — Bayḍāwī rejects rivet sizing on this ground. Al-Biqāʿī says rivets would make the softening less useful and invokes an observer; al-Ālūsī thinks fusing like wax could make a stronger join, but doubts the unidentified observer and allows the rivet reports if sound. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(البقاعي)-]

## 34:11/q14 What does Luqman's encounter with David's first armor report?  [ٱعْمَلْ سَٰبِغَٰتٍۢ · reports]
- 34:11/q14/p1 He watched silently, then praised the completed armor as war protection and called silence a wisdom few practice. — Yaḥyā b. Sallām and al-Farrāʾ report his curiosity without asking; al-Māwardī and al-Ṭabrisī add his praise when David wore it. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL(لقمان,يحيى بن سلام والفراء); TABRISI(لقمان)]

## 34:11/q15 What benefit or ethic does the armor instruction teach?  [ٱعْمَلْ سَٰبِغَٰتٍۢ وَقَدِّرْ فِى ٱلسَّرْدِ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw distinct lessons from protection, responsible invention, and independent labor.
- 34:11/q15/p1 Armor protects human life in war, so defensive craft and careful design serve people. — Elmalılı and Kur'an Yolu link 21:80 to protection from people; Rāzī favors defensive armor over offensive weapons. Shirt mail improves mobility over plates. Biqāʿī specifies resisting arrows and swords without hindering movement. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]
- 34:11/q15/p2 Skill and invention are divine trusts whose use must avoid disorder and remain accountable to God. — Islāḥī says God guided the invention; technical progress can become destructive weaponry when its moral limit and divine observation are ignored. Kur'an Yolu adds attention to useful work and continual improvement. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 34:11/q15/p3 Honorable people should work with their own hands and learn crafts. — Al-Būrsawī cites the hadith that David ate from his own earnings and a hadith praising labor-earned food; Saʿdī's poetry says a craft outlasts a purse and preserves dignity. Kur'an Yolu quotes the Prophet on David's own handiwork. [5 notes: BURSEVI(الشيخ سعدي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Resûlullah)]

## 34:11/q16 Whom does the plural 'do righteous work' address?  [وَٱعْمَلُوا۟ صَٰلِحًا · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural stays within David's context, honors David individually, or turns to Muhammad's community.
- 34:11/q16/p1 David and his household or people. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī and others infer the family from context; al-Qurṭubī compares 34:13 and Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 20:132. Biqāʿī extends it to all who obey David; Islāḥī includes followers. [19 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل وابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الخفاجي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI-]
- 34:11/q16/p2 David alone, addressed in an honorific plural. — Al-Ālūsī reports this option; Ibn ʿĀshūr also permits it, though al-Ālūsī insists the work is general righteousness, not merely flawless armor. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 34:11/q16/p3 Muhammad's community, as an appended lesson. — Elmalılı prefers this against the household view and argues from the address 'you' in 21:80, which indicates the craft was passed to that community. [1 notes: ELMALILI+]

## 34:11/q17 What righteous work is commanded?  [وَٱعْمَلُوا۟ صَٰلِحًا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether righteousness is described broadly as obedience and thanks, specified as remembrance, or qualified by sincerity and inner faculties.
- 34:11/q17/p1 All righteous acts of obedience, especially gratitude for God's gifts. — Al-Māwardī states all obedience; al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn Kathīr, Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī connect it to thanks. Ibn ʿĀshūr says gratitude is acts pleasing to the benefactor. Al-Ālūsī rejects reducing it to flawless armor; Abū Ḥayyān warns against absorption in worldly adornment. [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل وابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 34:11/q17/p2 Specific remembrance: tasbīḥ, praise, tawḥīd and takbīr, or thanking God in words. — Muqātil glosses the instruction as saying 'Praise be to God'; Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Māwardī names four forms of remembrance, while his report in al-Wāḥidī says to thank God as He deserves. [3 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل وابن عباس)]
- 34:11/q17/p3 Sincere deeds without ulterior motives. — Al-Būrsawī stresses purity of intention; al-Nasafī says sincerity brings acceptance. [2 notes: BURSEVI; NASAFI-FULL]
- 34:11/q17/p4 Each outward and inward faculty should perform its proper servitude. — Al-Būrsawī reports the Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya interpretation. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 34:11/q18 Why is righteous work commanded after armor making?  [وَٱعْمَلُوا۟ صَٰلِحًا · rhetoric]
turns on: The commentators draw different emphases from the order of craft and moral commands.
- 34:11/q18/p1 The extraordinary gift calls for gratitude expressed in deeds. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says thanks are acts pleasing to a benefactor; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees the favors to David and Solomon as a frame for this command. [5 notes: TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 34:11/q18/p2 The sequence prevents worldly craft from displacing obedience. — Abū Ḥayyān reads the successive commands to make armor, do good and give thanks this way. Al-Būrsawī and al-Ālūsī limit craft to livelihood, leaving time for worship. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 34:11/q18/p3 The instruction keeps a divine gift from being used for disorder. — Islāḥī says invention must stay within moral limits because God observes its use. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 34:11/q19 What does 'I see what you do' mean?  [إِنِّى بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرٌۭ · theology]
- 34:11/q19/p1 God knows and observes every deed, including inward and outward acts, and will recompense them. — Al-Māwardī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya specify good and evil; al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn Kathīr say nothing is hidden, including words. Al-Biqāʿī sees encouragement and warning; al-Nasafī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Būrsawī, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı and Ibn ʿĀshūr make recompense explicit. [19 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; THALABI; TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 34:11/q19/p2 The divine name al-Baṣīr means direct sight of every existent and calls for constant self-monitoring. — Al-Būrsawī applies the name to each movement and stillness, avoiding prohibitions and fulfilling commands. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 34:11/q20 How does 'indeed I see' function in the sentence?  [إِنِّى بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 34:11/q20/p1 It gives the reason for the command and motivates obedience through reward and warning. — Al-Būrsawī and al-Ālūsī say divine sight explains either the command or its binding force; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares causal inna to Bashshār's line about success and rising early. Al-Biqāʿī connects the emphatic warning to denials of resurrection and divine attributes. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 34:11/q21 What inward meanings are drawn from the armor and its measured links?  [ٱعْمَلْ سَٰبِغَٰتٍۢ وَقَدِّرْ فِى ٱلسَّرْدِ · inward]
turns on: Whether the symbolic measure governs inner moral craft or the delivery of wisdom.
- 34:11/q21/p1 The armor is scrupulous piety and its linked construction is practical wisdom guarding against the soul and Satan. — Al-Qāshānī reads the protective garment as piety, the arrows as hostile impulses, and measured linking as skilled rational and religious practice that purifies deeds. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 34:11/q21/p2 Measured linking means speaking wisdom in amounts listeners can grasp. — Al-Būrsawī transmits Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya and cites a Persian couplet warning against scattering jewels of wisdom before a donkey. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 34:11/q22 Who is addressed in the inward reading of 'do good'?  [وَٱعْمَلُوا۟ صَٰلِحًا · inward]
turns on: The inward readings differ in symbolic referent and emphasis.
- 34:11/q22/p1 David signifies the spirit and its faculties; those working for God ascend through righteous deeds. — Al-Qāshānī says the workers gather from the lower realm toward the upper, preparing for holy lights. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 34:11/q22/p2 Every outward and inward faculty must perform its created act of servitude. — Al-Būrsawī reports the Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya reading. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 34:11/q23 What special effect is ascribed to reciting the name al-Baṣīr?  [بَصِيرٌۭ · inward]
- 34:11/q23/p1 Reciting it one hundred times before Friday prayer opens inward sight and guides speech and action. — Al-Būrsawī reports this special effect of the divine name, while acknowledging human fallibility. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 34:11/q24 What story about David's lapse is told here?  [إِنِّى بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرٌۭ · reports]
turns on: The two reports focus on different consequences of David's lapse.
- 34:11/q24/p1 After his own lapse, David changed from asking that wrongdoers not be forgiven to praying for their forgiveness. — Al-Būrsawī transmits the saying as a lesson about zeal, fault and mercy. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 34:11/q24/p2 After David repented, birds heard his voice but withdrew because his lapse had altered their intimacy with him. — Al-Būrsawī reports God explaining their estrangement as the effect of disobedience. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 34:11/q25 How is gratitude construed in the nearby command to work?  [وَٱعْمَلُوا۟ صَٰلِحًا · grammar]
turns on: Whether shukran intensifies the act of thanking or states the purpose of obedient work.
- 34:11/q25/p1 Shukran is a cognate accusative: 'give thanks, thanking.' — Al-Ṭabrisī gives this analysis of the following gratitude wording. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 34:11/q25/p2 Shukran expresses purpose, with obedience understood as the object of 'do.' — Al-Ṭabrisī offers this as the alternative parse of the following gratitude wording. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 34:11/q26 How is David's softened iron compared with the Prophet's miracles?  [* · links]
- 34:11/q26/p1 It is set beside transformations of a trench rock, wooden objects into swords, and a severed hand restored. — Al-Biqāʿī cites the trench rock's breaking in one account and becoming loose sand with water in another; Salmān's report has three illuminating blows and announced conquests. He also cites reports on ʿAbd Allāh b. Jaḥsh, ʿUkāsha, Salama b. Aslam, and Muʿawwidh b. ʿAfrāʾ. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الواقدي,سلمان,عبد الله بن جحش,عكاشة بن محصن,معوذ بن عفراء)]

## 34:11/q27 How does the armor instruction relate to other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes connect the verse to protective craft, the surrounding account of favors, and the sūrah's warning.
- 34:11/q27/p1 Qur'an 21:80 describes armor making as protection and extends its benefit to Muhammad's community. — Elmalılı and Kur'an Yolu use that verse to explain defense and the community's inherited craft. [4 notes: ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 34:11/q27/p2 The command belongs to the sequence of favors to David and Solomon and the call to grateful work. — Al-Biqāʿī sees armor making as the purpose of softened iron and as work devoted to God; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī lists mountains, birds, iron, wind and obedient jinn. Study Quran says this follows criticism of disbelievers. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 34:11/q27/p3 David and Solomon's grateful rule contrasts with affluent Quraysh and Sheba, and points from worldly reward to resurrection. — Islāḥī says David and Solomon remained grateful, unlike arrogant Quraysh; the Sheba example shows wrath, and present reward and punishment challenge denial of the next life. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
