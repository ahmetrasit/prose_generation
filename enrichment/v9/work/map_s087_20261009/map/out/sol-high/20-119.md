# 20:119: 18 questions

## 20:119/q01 How is وَأَنَّكَ read and construed?  [وَأَنَّكَ · readings]
turns on: The hamza's vowel determines whether the clause is a coordinated constituent of the prior promise or a complete assertion; sources differ over reader attribution and syntactic attachment.
- 20:119/q01/p1 With fatḥa, wa-annaka is coordinated with the earlier promise that Adam will not hunger. — Sibawayh attaches the open form to inna laka; al-Tabari, Abu Ishaq, al-Tabrisi, al-Razi, al-Samin, al-Alusi and Ibn Ashur relate it to an la tajua, as a nominalized clause or subject of inna, with laka preceding. Abu Ali in Ibn al-Jawzi instead links it to anna laka. Abu Ishaq and al-Qurtubi allow nominative coordination by syntactic position. Al-Tabrisi, al-Razi, al-Baydawi and al-Samin explain why waw may precede anna despite the apparent doubling of particles; al-Alusi says the two assertions govern different relations and rejects the weaker claim that laka alone permits waw as a surrogate anna. Readers listed vary by source: Ibn Kathir, Abu Amr, Ibn Amir, Hamza, al-Kisai and Hafs from Asim in al-Farisi and Ibn al-Jawzi; al-Qurtubi specifies Abu Amr and Kufans except Abu Bakr's Asim; al-Alusi calls it the majority. Al-Tabari prefers it while allowing kasra. Bursawi says separation from hunger makes distinct blessings. Ibn Ashur says both readings affirm the same four protections. [29 notes: SIBAWAYH; TAB-FULL(بعض قراء المدينة وعامة قراء الكوفة والبصرة); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي وحفص عن عاصم); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI(نافع وأبو بكر عن عاصم); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الباقون وحفص عن عاصم,فرقة); TABRISI(نافع، أبو بكر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو علي,ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي وحفص عن عاصم); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو والكوفيون إلا عاصما في رواية أبي بكر); BAYDAWI-FULL(نافع وأبو بكر); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الباقون,الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(شيبه ونافع وحفص وابن سعدان); IBNASHUR-FULL(الباقون); TAB-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]
- 20:119/q01/p2 With kasra, wa-innaka opens or coordinates a complete emphatic sentence with the earlier inna laka. — Sibawayh reports the independent sentence; al-Tabari calls it plausible. Nafi and Abu Bakr from Asim are repeatedly named; al-Alusi instead lists Shayba, Nafi, Hafs and Ibn Sadan. Al-Qurtubi says the remaining readers use kasra, differing in attribution from the other lists. Al-Samin and Ibn Ashur allow coordination with the whole preceding inna clause. QIRAAT-ER describes an independent emphatic assertion. [23 notes: SIBAWAYH(بعضهم); TAB-FULL(بعض قراء المدينة والكوفة); FARISI-HUJJA(نافع وعاصم برواية أبي بكر); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(نافع وأبو بكر عن عاصم); BAGHAWI-FULL(نافع وأبو بكر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة,نافع وعاصم في رواية أبي بكر); TABRISI(نافع، أبو بكر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو علي,نافع وأبو بكر عن عاصم); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الباقون); BAYDAWI-FULL(نافع وأبو بكر); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(نافع وأبو بكر); ALUSI-FULL(شيبه ونافع وحفص وابن سعدان); IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وأبو بكر عن عاصم); QIRAAT-ER(ḥf)]

## 20:119/q02 What does لَا تَظْمَؤُا۟ promise?  [لَا تَظْمَؤُا۟ · meaning]
- 20:119/q02/p1 Adam will not become thirsty in Paradise. — Muqatil, Yahya, Ikrima through Abd al-Razzaq, al-Tabari, al-Thalabi, al-Baghawi, Ibn Abbas in several transmissions, al-Itqan and others give the direct gloss. Al-Tabari says it lasts as long as Adam remains there; al-Thalabi attributes protection to the Garden. Al-Nasafi points to available drinks; Bursawi to continuously flowing springs and rivers. Jishumi distinguishes hamzated zama (thirst), zam (the interval between drinks), and unhamzated zama (thin gums); al-Raghib in Bursawi defines thirst arising between drinks. Ibn al-Jawzi cites ordinary verb use. Ibn Atiyya adds completeness and continuity of provision. [23 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ABDURRAZZAQ(عكرمة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(ابن عباس، وسعيد بن جبير، وعكرمة، وقتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس، سعيد بن جبير، قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; BURSEVI(الراغب); ASAD-NOTES]

## 20:119/q03 What does وَلَا تَضْحَىٰ mean?  [وَلَا تَضْحَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb itself names exposure to sun or its bodily effect of sweating; the latter is treated as an approximate gloss by Bint al-Shati.
- 20:119/q03/p1 Adam will not be exposed to the sun or harmed by its heat. — Muqatil, Qatada, Ikrima, Ibn Abbas and many commentators give the gloss. Al-Tabari, al-Thalabi, al-Zajjaj through al-Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, al-Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin, al-Alusi and Ibn Abbas cite poetry using yadḥā for a man in midday sun, sometimes chilled by evening. Al-Mubarrad glosses the poem as appearing in sun; Bint al-Shati and al-Farra prefer exposure over mere perspiration. Al-Nadr, Ibn al-Arabi and Shammar in al-Wahidi record differing perfect, imperfect and verbal-noun forms; Jishumi links the root to what is open or visible, morning, exposed road or district, open action and forenoon sacrifice. Al-Farra allows masculine or feminine aḍḥā; Ibn Arafa defines ḍāḥ as exposed; Ibn al-Arabi explains scorched, drought-stricken land. Abu Zayd describes a sun-exposed road. Ibn Umar's ihram instruction and al-Asmai's correction to iḍḥa support exposure. Ibn Ashur specifies forenoon heat beginning to intensify; Bint al-Shati notes this verb is unique in the Quran while related forms occur at 93:1, 20:59, 7:98, 79:46, 79:49 and 91:1. Ibn al-Jawzi's lexicon compares 91:1. Al-Tabatabai says avoiding sun exposure removes need for shelter from heat or cold. [64 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(عكرمة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن أبي ربيعة,ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير,قتادة); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; THALABI(عكرمة,عمر بن أبي ربيعة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأعرابي,ابن عباس,الزجاج,النضر,شمر); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن الأعرابي,ابن عباس، وسعيد بن جبير، وعكرمة، وقتادة,ابن عرفة,الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس، سعيد بن جبير، قتادة); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو زيد,ابن عمر,الأصمعي,عمر بن أبي ربيعة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم,عكرمة,عمرو بن أبي ربيعة); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(المبرد); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الفراء)+]
- 20:119/q03/p2 Adam will not perspire from intense sun heat. — Ibn Abbas in the Ibn al-Azraq exchange explains it as freedom from sweating and cites a poem about sun exposure and evening chill; al-Itqan and Bint al-Shati report the same gloss. Al-Qurtubi records sweating as a possible sense of the verb. Bint al-Shati calls this an approximation; she and al-Farra prefer protection from sun exposure. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الفراء)-]

## 20:119/q04 What condition of Paradise explains the absence of harmful sun exposure?  [فِيهَا وَلَا تَضْحَىٰ · interpretation]
- 20:119/q04/p1 Paradise has no harmful sun; its people have extended shade and light. — Al-Wahidi, Ikrima in al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi, al-Nasafi and Bursawi say it has no sun and extended shade. Jishumi and al-Tabrisi also report only light or brightness. Abu al-Aliyah in al-Qurtubi describes its daylight as lasting about the time of the dawn prayer; al-Qurtubi locates the shade between dawn and sunrise. Ibn al-Jawzi likewise says there is no sun. These descriptions vary in how they characterize its light. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(ابن عباس، وسعيد بن جبير، وعكرمة، وقتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة); TABRISI(ابن عباس، سعيد بن جبير، قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو العالية); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 20:119/q05 What comforts does the verse promise together with the previous verse?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:119/q05/p1 The Garden secures the four basic comforts: food, clothing, drink and shelter, with lasting freedom from their corresponding hardships. — Ibn Atiyya says provision is complete and lasting; al-Razi calls satiety, drinking, clothing and shade the four pillars of well-being, supplied without earning. Al-Baydawi adds no effort and no fear of loss. Al-Alusi says the negations mean afflictions never reach Adam before relief arrives. Ibn Ashur says all four protect bodily constitution, with freedom from sun implying freedom from cold. Asad and Study Quran describe the Garden's ease; Qushayri says Adam had every comfort but only later appreciated safety. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; QUSHAYRI]

## 20:119/q06 Why are hunger, nakedness, thirst and sun exposure paired and ordered this way?  [لَا تَظْمَؤُا۟ وَلَا تَضْحَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Sources offer different organizing principles: inner and outer affliction, separated matching items, seasonal weather, rhyme, or rank; Islahi and al-Alusi record explicit objections to some explanations.
- 20:119/q06/p1 The pairs match inner and outer conditions: hunger with nakedness, then thirst with sun heat. — Al-Tabrisi pairs hunger and nakedness as inner and outer deprivation, and thirst and sun as heat linked causally. Ibn Kathir, al-Biqai, al-Burhan, al-Itqan, al-Alusi and Ibn Ashur distinguish inner thirst or emptiness from exterior sun or bodily exposure. Abu Hayyan says the Eden pairings share semantic features; al-Burhan adds an anecdote about al-Mutanabbi and Sayf al-Dawla. Al-Biqai says no inner or outer burning afflicts Adam. Ibn Atiyya notes the conventional pairs are dispersed; Ibn Ashur adduces Imru al-Qays and Abu al-Tayyib for striking antithesis. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:119/q06/p2 Separating corresponding items lets listeners restore the usual pairs and makes each blessing count separately. — Ibn Atiyya and al-Tabrisi invoke Imru al-Qays's separated riding and embracing imagery; al-Tabrisi says Arabic trusts listeners to match semantic counterparts, while another cited poet offers more fitting direct pairs. Bursawi and al-Intisaf in al-Alusi say separation keeps two blessings from seeming one. Al-Alusi reports two Kindi poems and Sayf al-Dawla's reproach and reply, but questions whether riding and urging horses, or woman and wine, are actually separated semantic counterparts. Ibn Ashur finds such separation can sharpen antithesis. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(امرؤ القيس); TABRISI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الانتصاف,الكندي الآخر,الكندي الأول,سيف الدولة); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:119/q06/p3 The two pairs evoke the seasons: hunger and need for shelter in cold, thirst and need for shade in heat. — Islahi says Arabic literary pairing uses cold and hot weather conditions. Some readers object that hunger should go with thirst and sun heat with shelter; Islahi rejects their objection as missing the literary usage. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some people)-]
- 20:119/q06/p4 The apparently more fitting arrangement would pair hunger with thirst and sun heat with shelter. — Islahi reports this objection from some readers and rejects it because the given pairs evoke hot and cold weather. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some people); ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 20:119/q06/p5 The four comforts form an ordered wrap-and-unfold sequence serving the rhyme. — Al-Tabatabai says the unmarked order would be hunger, thirst, nakedness, sun exposure. Al-Alusi questions whether rhyme requires precisely this arrangement, since another order might also meet the cadence. [1 notes: TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:119/q06/p6 The order moves from primary gifts of satiety and clothing to their complements, drink and shade, and ranks the four by importance. — Al-Kashf, reported by al-Alusi, calls drink and shade complements that clarify the favor and distinguishes inna laka from innaka. Al-Tibi says the sequence ranks the items and repeated fiha and negation evoke worldly hardship. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي,الكشف)]

## 20:119/q07 Why are the comforts expressed as the absence of hardships?  [لَا تَظْمَؤُا۟ وَلَا تَضْحَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 20:119/q07/p1 Naming hardships warns Adam what disobedience and expulsion could make him suffer. — Al-Razi, al-Baydawi, al-Samin citing al-Zamakhshari, al-Alusi and Ibn Ashur say hearing the names of misery makes Adam wary of its cause. Al-Alusi says the positive permission to enjoy the Garden, except the tree, was already stated at 2:35; the negations add that hardship never reaches him at all. Al-Tibi observes that repeated fiha and negation suggest worldly hardship. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:119/q08 How does this promise explain the warning and the account of the fall?  [* · links]
- 20:119/q08/p1 Its four protections explain the misery threatened if Adam leaves the Garden, contrasting its ease with later worldly hardship. — Al-Razi links the four absences to the prior warning; al-Alusi says complete comfort gives Adam reason to avoid expulsion at 20:117. Al-Tibi says the sequence expands the warning. Study Quran contrasts Garden ease with hardship after Satan's deception and cites 2:36 and 7:24-25; it also points to the warning, deception, fall and remorse at 2:35-37 and 7:19-23. Its discussion of 7:11 notes the Quranic and biblical connection between disobedience, exposed nakedness and subsequent clothing verses, including 20:118-121. Ibn Ashur says this sura's wording is distinctive relative to the earlier Adam accounts in al-Baqara and al-Araf. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:119/q09 Why is Adam singled out in the address?  [وَأَنَّكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular address follows the preceding address or reflects a reported question from Adam; al-Alusi doubts the report.
- 20:119/q09/p1 He is addressed for the same reason as in the earlier warning. — Al-Alusi refers back to his explanation of the singular address at 20:117 without restating it in this note. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:119/q09/p2 Adam was the one who asked about these needs. — Al-Alusi records this as another suggestion, connected to the report that Adam asked about food, clothing, drink and shade; he doubts that report's authenticity. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 20:119/q10 Did Adam ask about the Garden's basic provisions?  [* · reports]
- 20:119/q10/p1 A report says Adam asked about food, clothing, drink and shade, and God answered with four assurances. — Al-Alusi transmits the report anonymously and doubts its authenticity; he also notes the suggestion that Adam is singled out because he asked. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 20:119/q11 What happened when Adam encountered worldly hardship after Paradise?  [* · reports]
- 20:119/q11/p1 Adam came to appreciate Paradise after hunger, thirst and affliction, weeping at each new trial when Gabriel asked why. — Qushayri says Gabriel conveyed God's greeting and reminded Adam of his former freedom from thirst, heat and the other hardships. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 20:119/q12 How did Adam know hardships he had not experienced?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:119/q12/p1 He learned their realities through God's teaching him all the names. — Ibn Ashur says Adam had not directly experienced hunger, nakedness, thirst or sun exposure, and cites 2:31. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:119/q13 What do the four absences foreshadow for humanity?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:119/q13/p1 They identify basic human needs whose provision will occupy people's later lives. — Ibn Ashur says circumstances at a being's creation signal its defining components. He compares the Isra report: the Prophet chose milk rather than wine and was told wine would have led his community astray. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 20:119/q14 What does the Ibn al-Azraq exchange about this word report?  [وَلَا تَضْحَىٰ · reports]
- 20:119/q14/p1 Ibn Abbas explained it as no sweating from intense heat and cited a poem contrasting sun exposure with evening chill. — Al-Durr and Bint al-Shati preserve the gloss and poetic evidence. In al-Mubarrad's account Ibn Abbas corrects Ibn al-Azraq's recitation from yakhsar to yakhsur; Ibn al-Azraq had asked about listening to a Qurayshi youth. Bint al-Shati says al-Mubarrad alone among her available transmissions preserves this report and uses the Quranic verse to explain rare words in a literary recital rather than analyze the Quran. Ibn al-Anbari's different account has Nafi reproach Ibn Abbas for answering tafsir questions at the Meccan water basin. [8 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن الأنباري,ابن عباس,المبرد)]

## 20:119/q15 What is the inward reading of these Garden comforts?  [* · inward]
- 20:119/q15/p1 The abiding Garden, though God's neighborhood, also serves the animal self as a pasture of food, drink, clothing and partners. — Bursawi reports this from al-Tawilat al-Najmiyya, comparing these enjoyments to those of worldly life. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 20:119/q16 What is the force of the emphatic wording in the thirst clause?  [وَأَنَّكَ · rhetoric]
- 20:119/q16/p1 It assures Adam emphatically that thirst and sun exposure will be absent. — Al-Alusi says waw itself is not emphatic, so it does not duplicate assertion within one domain; he nonetheless leaves open why this clause receives an extra assurance compared with the preceding clause. Ibn Ashur says both readings retain emphatic protection while varying syntax. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:119/q17 What literary principles are recorded in the discussions associated with this verse?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: These are separate literary observations in the extracted notes, rather than rival interpretations of the verse.
- 20:119/q17/p1 Calling one man 'all people' gathers virtues, while calling him uniquely 'the brave' or 'the generous' implies no rival deserves the name. — Al-Jurjani uses cited poems to distinguish summed qualities from hyperbolic exclusive excellence. [2 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 20:119/q17/p2 Present tears cannot be taken to guarantee lasting union and future tearlessness. — Al-Jurjani rejects the proposed poetic reading because it would seek tears to become like an eye unable to weep; dryness is not an established sign of joy. [0 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL-]
- 20:119/q17/p3 Sound composition conveys meaning as words are heard; faulty or excessively obscure composition delays or consumes meaning. — Al-Jurjani contrasts immediate understanding with verbal complexity. [1 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 20:119/q17/p4 Practiced poets have authority in judging poetry. — Al-Buhturi preferred Abu Nuwas over Muslim and said practicing poets judge poetry better than nonproducing scholars. [1 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL(البحتري)]
- 20:119/q17/p5 Eloquence and brevity depend on meaning, including indirect meaning conveyed in few words. — Al-Jurjani rejects eloquence in bare wording and says concise expression uses one sense to point to another that would need many words directly. He warns that prestige, repetition and inherited consensus can conceal errors in the wording-only theory. [1 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL; JURJANI-DALAIL-]

## 20:119/q18 How does a separate comparison align worldly desires with afterlife blessings?  [* · links]
- 20:119/q18/p1 Six worldly desires are matched to six afterlife blessings, with desire and sustenance at the ends, spouses placed in the afterlife sequence and God's approval last. — Al-Burhan compares 3:14 and 3:15 and says the order culminates in divine approval. [1 notes: BURHAN]
