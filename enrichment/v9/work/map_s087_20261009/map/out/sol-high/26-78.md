# 26:78: 18 questions

## 26:78/q01 How is الذي attached syntactically?  [ٱلَّذِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether the relative continues the description of the Lord or starts a new sentence, and whether a specific, completed relative clause licenses a predicate فاء: Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn object; al-Rāzī says generality is usual, not required, and al-Akhfash allows an added فاء.
- 26:78/q01/p1 It describes or stands in apposition to the preceding Lord of the worlds. — Al-Samīn also allows appositional substitution or explanatory apposition; Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers description of رب العالمين, with فهو يهدين continuing the relative clause. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn give the adjectival accusative analysis; Bursawī and Ālūsī identify the described Lord. Ibn ʿĀshūr says either major parsing still supports exclusive devotion, comparing 6:79. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 26:78/q01/p2 It is accusative after an implied 'I mean.' — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn supply an implicit أعني. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 26:78/q01/p3 It is nominative as predicate of an omitted 'He.' — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn give this as an alternative. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 26:78/q01/p4 It begins an independent nominal sentence as subject, with فهو يهدين as predicate. — Al-Ḥawfī invokes a conditional sense to explain the predicate's فاء; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports the same independent analysis. Al-Samīn also lists الذي as subject and the following nominal clause as predicate. Ālūsī reports al-Ḥawfī and others. Ibn ʿĀshūr says this parsing too links creation to Abraham's exclusive devotion, comparing 6:79. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(الحوفي وغيره); IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الحوفي)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-; TABATABAI-]

## 26:78/q02 What does 'created me' say about Abraham's origin and purpose?  [خَلَقَنِى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'created me' is explained as the act of bringing Abraham into being, its intended purpose, or its status as the first blessing.
- 26:78/q02/p1 God brought Abraham into existence and bodily life. — Al-Ṭabrisī and Bursawī say from nonexistence; al-Zamakhsharī mentions life breathed into him; al-Nasafī places creation in the secure womb. Al-Rāzī traces embodiment through semen, menstrual blood, food, four elements, temperament, then plant powers of nutrition, growth and reproduction, animal powers of sense, memory, appetite and movement, and human intellectual and practical powers. He contrasts creation with guidance as benefit seeking and harm avoidance. Al-Biqāʿī and al-Rāzī say bodily formation precedes spiritual reception, citing 15:29, 38:72 and 23:12–14; al-Rāzī describes the spirit as a luminous subtlety of the realm of command. [16 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TABRISI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:78/q02/p2 God created Abraham for servitude, worship, obedience or service. — Al-Tustarī and al-Sulamī's anonymous speaker pair servanthood with nearness; al-Māwardī pairs worship with Paradise and also reports creation by grace with guidance to obedience; al-Ṭabrisī pairs obedience with Paradise; al-Nasafī pairs divine service with manners of intimacy. The late speaker in al-Sulamī says God enslaved and employed Abraham and eased this for him. [7 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(بعض المتأخرين,بعضهم); MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL]
- 26:78/q02/p3 God created Abraham to summon His creatures. — An anonymous speaker in al-Sulamī pairs this commission with guidance to the manners of intimacy. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 26:78/q02/p4 Creation is God's first blessing to a servant. — Qatāda's saying is transmitted by Ibn Abī Ḥātim and al-Suyūṭī; al-Jishumī says guidance completes all blessings after creation, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls religious guidance the best blessing after existence on one reading. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); JISHUMI; TABATABAI]

## 26:78/q03 What does 'He guides me' include?  [يَهْدِينِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether guidance is unrestricted direction toward all benefits, specifically religious direction, a final leading to Paradise, nearness to God, or divine aid.
- 26:78/q03/p1 God continually directs Abraham or creatures toward every beneficial end in worldly and afterlife life. — Al-Zamakhsharī includes nourishment and infant skills; Abū Ḥayyān reports his womb, breast and suckling examples. Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursawī trace guidance from fetal absorption of blood to Paradise, citing 87:3 in al-Bayḍāwī. Al-Rāzī details powers of sense, reason, attraction of fitting things and repulsion of harms; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī treats food, drink and healing as instances, links 20:50 and the plant sign 26:7, and says guidance continues forever. Ālūsī includes nature and choice. Al-Nasafī names worldly paths and religious interests; Bursawī both abodes and renewed spirit. Ibn Kathīr says each creature is guided to its determined course; al-Islāḥī notes God gives abilities, noble nature and discriminating intellect, citing 87:2–3. Ibn ʿĀshūr specifically includes paths to knowledge and protection of reason from false suggestions, comparing 90:10. [19 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:78/q03/p2 God guides Abraham to religion, obedience, right speech and action, truth or the path of salvation. — Ibn ʿAbbās glosses guidance as direction; al-Kalbī names religion. Al-Ṭabarī names sound speech and action; al-Jishumī the path of goodness; al-Baghawī salvation; Ibn al-Jawzī right guidance against idols. Abū Ḥayyān reports obedience as one gloss. Al-Rāzī says reason distinguishes truth and good; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts this religious sense with universal guidance. Al-Islāḥī says intellect alone cannot reach the highest human status, so God sends prophets and messengers for spiritual needs. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الكلبي); JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:78/q03/p3 God guides Abraham to Paradise after worship or obedience in this world. — The people of language reported by al-Thaʿlabī distinguish worldly creation upon innate nature from afterlife guidance to the Garden. Al-Māwardī, al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī pair worship or obedience here with Paradise hereafter; Abū Ḥayyān reports Paradise as an alternative gloss. [5 notes: THALABI(أهل اللسان); MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]
- 26:78/q03/p4 God guides Abraham toward nearness, intimacy and what pleases Him. — Al-Tustarī and al-Sulamī's anonymous speaker pair servanthood with nearness; another speaker says God eases Abraham's service and guides him toward what pleases God in him. A third pairs his summons to creatures with the manners of intimacy; al-Nasafī reports a similar pairing of service and intimacy. [5 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(بعض المتأخرين,بعضهم); NASAFI-FULL]
- 26:78/q03/p5 Guidance is God's gracious aid to Abraham. — Al-Jishumī reports this as another explanation. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]

## 26:78/q04 How does the فاء relate creation to guidance?  [فَهُوَ يَهْدِينِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the فاء expresses causal sequence, simple coordination, a conditional predicate, or an allowed extra predicate marker; and how الذي is parsed.
- 26:78/q04/p1 The فاء makes guidance follow from creation as its consequence, response or immediate next act. — The author of al-Naẓm contrasts فاء with a mere wāw; al-Bayḍāwī calls it causal if الذي is a subject. Al-Burhān calls guidance the answer to creation; Bursawī says the فاء and present mark renewal. Ibn ʿĀshūr says created intellect makes guidance possible, citing 20:50; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says gradual bodily creation and governance cannot separate; al-Islāḥī argues leaving a created human to wander would violate mercy and wisdom and make creation futile and unjust. Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts immediate فاء here with delayed ثم between death and resurrection. Ālūsī reports the view that creation causes guidance through divine wisdom. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 26:78/q04/p2 The فاء is conjunctive, linking creation and guidance without asserting that creation causes guidance. — Al-Bayḍāwī gives this if the relative modifies رب العالمين; creation is complete while guidance continues. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 26:78/q04/p3 The فاء is licensed by conditional force in the relative subject. — Al-Ḥawfī, followed possibly by Abū al-Baqāʾ, and those reported by Ālūsī use this syntax. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn object that الذي is specific and the creation clause is not renewed. Ālūsī reports al-Rāzī's reply that a general relative is customary but not mandatory; another reply treats creation abstractly as recurring. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(الحوفي وغيره,الرازي); IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الحوفي)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-]
- 26:78/q04/p4 The فاء may be an added predicate فاء without conditional force. — Al-Samīn says al-Akhfash allows this generally, with the example Zayd fa-ḍribhu; Ālūsī reports the same explanation. Al-Samīn says it could be Abū al-Baqāʾ's unstated basis. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,الأخفش); ALUSI-FULL(الأخفش)]

## 26:78/q05 What does the explicit هو contribute?  [فَهُوَ · rhetoric]
- 26:78/q05/p1 It restricts guidance to God alone, answering the idolaters' claim that their gods guide. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says fronting هو before the verbal predicate gives this restriction and denies that it is a pronoun of separation after a conjunction. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Burhān connect exclusive guidance with the idolaters' contrary claim; al-Thaʿlabī says there is no guide besides the Creator. [4 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:78/q06 Why is creation past tense and guidance present tense?  [خَلَقَنِى يَهْدِينِ · rhetoric]
- 26:78/q06/p1 Creation was completed once; guidance is renewed and continuous. — Al-Rāzī contrasts one creation with recurrent worldly and religious direction; al-Biqāʿī says creation does not recur in this world. Al-Subkī in al-Itqān applies the same contrast also to feeding, drink and healing, while observing that a past formulation need not imply a divine attribute began in the past. Al-Itqān's author gives a general past/present rule and cites al-Zamakhsharī on 2:15. Al-Burhān, Bursawī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise stress continuous guidance. Al-Bayḍāwī says creation is complete while guidance continues; al-Nasafī's future-oriented gloss suggests guidance toward what is most necessary, best and most complete. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(بهاء الدين السبكي); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 26:78/q07 How is the first-person yāʾ read and written at the verse ending?  [يَهْدِينِ · readings]
turns on: Whether the first-person suffix yāʾ is elided for verse ending rhythm or retained as a pronounced pronoun; reports name Ibn Abī Isḥāq, Nāfiʿ and Yaʿqūb.
- 26:78/q07/p1 The first-person yāʾ is omitted or shortened in the verse ending. — Al-Ṭabrisī attributes omission to verse endings; al-Qurṭubī and Ibn ʿĀshūr to matching rhyme and ease at pause. Ibn ʿĀshūr says four verb endings omit it, most sūra endings have a still nūn, and cites 26:14. The Qirāʾāt entry separately notes a variant fully vocalizing the pronoun after خَلَقَنِي. [4 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]
- 26:78/q07/p2 The first-person yāʾ is retained in يهديني and following forms. — Ibn Abī Isḥāq reads with yāʾ because it is a pronoun and the nūn has a purpose; Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī also report this from Nāfiʿ. Al-Nashr says Yaʿqūb retains sixteen listed extra yāʾs in connection and at pause, including eight cited forms and أطيعون in eight places. The Qirāʾāt entry notes the extended object suffix vowel after يهدِي. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق); NASHR(يعقوب); ALUSI-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق); QIRAAT-ER]

## 26:78/q08 Why does Abraham cite these acts of God?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the focus is the obligation to worship the sole benefactor or the range and sequence of the benefits displayed; several notes make both points.
- 26:78/q08/p1 The acts show that only their divine performer deserves worship and obedience, excluding idols. — Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī say the benefactor must be obeyed; al-Māwardī notes creation and guidance are common to all yet Abraham models that duty. Al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī and Study Quran make divinity depend on the listed acts. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the human-relevant actions courteous and intelligible, all from God; al-Biqāʿī says the Creator alone knows and rules the creature's inward state and can benefit or harm. Ālūsī reports that special blessings warrant sole worship and reliance. Al-Islāḥī contrasts the source of blessings with deaf, blind, powerless stone idols; Kuran Yolu lists creation, guidance, provision, healing, death, life and forgiveness against fabricated gods. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects treating creation as an insignificant new passage: creation implies divine governance; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares Abraham's devotion with 6:79. [14 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 26:78/q08/p2 The acts display the scope and order of God's beneficence through bodily, intellectual and final life. — Al-Rāzī says creation and guidance supply all benefits and begins four attributes after the Lord of the worlds; al-Jishumī says guidance completes blessings. Ibn ʿĀshūr traces bodily and intellectual creation, food, drink, illness, healing, death and resurrection as gifts, with death recognized as a blessing after reflection and later life as plainly one for its seeker. Ālūsī says the named blessings especially warrant worship and reliance. [6 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:78/q09 How does Abraham's speech function as preaching?  [* · rhetoric]
- 26:78/q09/p1 He uses evident personal divine acts to teach true religion and move idolaters toward faith. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says human-relevant examples let people understand their Lord; al-Rāzī says Abraham rejected false gods, praised God, then prayed, modelling religious instruction. Ibn ʿĀshūr says sensible acts visible to everyone can lead seekers of guidance to faith; al-Islāḥī says the fundamentals come in logical order as personal impressions during acquittal and migration, when intellectual persuasion is useless but a subtle style may stir hearts. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:78/q10 Why use attributes and repeated relative clauses here?  [ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَنِى فَهُوَ يَهْدِينِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Different notes explain distinct stylistic choices in the descriptive sequence.
- 26:78/q10/p1 Abraham speaks of the Beloved by attribute because intimacy overwhelms direct naming. — Al-Wāsiṭī in al-Sulamī contrasts Abraham's earlier direct call to his Lord, before full absorption in attraction, with this indirect naming. [1 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي)]
- 26:78/q10/p2 Repeating the relative pronoun emphasizes the possessor of three magnificent divine attributes. — Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts repetition with simply coordinating the clauses. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:78/q11 What is the theological force of attributing guidance to God?  [يَهْدِينِ · theology]
turns on: The notes present divine determination broadly or apply it specifically to spiritual ranks and reliance on causes.
- 26:78/q11/p1 God Himself determines and gives guidance, guiding or misguiding whomever He wills. — Ibn Kathīr says God creates and determines each creature's course and guides it, while guiding some and leading others astray by His will. Al-Rāzī invokes Abraham's attribution here and his prayer in 14:35 as clear support for his theological position. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 26:78/q11/p2 Prophethood, sainthood and intimacy are gifts from primordial divine election, not earned by human means. — Bursawī says their gradual appearance can fool veiled observers into crediting acquisition; his cited couplet contrasts striving for union with entrusting it to fixed decree. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 26:78/q12 What inward relation between created things and the heart is drawn from the verse?  [* · inward]
- 26:78/q12/p1 Created things mediate outward creation, while God reserves the heart's bond with Him for Himself. — Junayd in al-Sulamī calls the heart God's treasury and the earthly place of His secret. [1 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد)]

## 26:78/q13 What does the reported cave episode say Abraham declared?  [خَلَقَنِى · reports]
- 26:78/q13/p1 Born in a cave after his mother fled there in labor, Abraham declared his creator, provider, feeder and giver of drink his sole Lord. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits this account from Ibn Isḥāq. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن اسحاق)]

## 26:78/q14 What reward is reported for reciting Abraham's prayer after ablution?  [* · reports]
- 26:78/q14/p1 A report promises guidance and multiple worldly and afterlife gifts to one who recites it while leaving for obligatory prayer. — Al-Suyūṭī transmits a report from the Prophet promising Paradise food and drink, healing, expiation, blessed life, martyrdom, forgiveness, wisdom, truthful renown and Paradise homes; al-Ḥasan adds a prayer for parents. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 26:78/q15 How is this verse linked to other passages and the sūra's sequence?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes point to separate links: repeated scriptural wording, narrative placement, and transition to prayer.
- 26:78/q15/p1 The creation-and-guidance pairing recurs in other descriptions of God. — Al-Rāzī and al-Islāḥī cite al-Aʿlā 87:2–3; al-Bayḍāwī cites 87:3; Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cite 20:50. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also connects universal creaturely guidance to the plant sign in 26:7. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:78/q15/p2 Abraham's account continues the sūra's preaching sequence and fills the earlier narrative gap between Noah and Moses. — Al-Islāḥī says it shares the preceding anecdote's preaching aim and recurring ending verses; he calls Abraham the most prominent messenger between Noah and Moses and says his account fills a gap left by the previous sūra. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:78/q15/p3 The sequence of divine attributes leads gracefully to Abraham's prayer for judgment and righteous company. — Al-Ṭībī in al-Itqān calls the move to 'My Lord, grant me judgment and join me with the righteous' a graceful transition and good request. [1 notes: ITQAN(الطيبي)]

## 26:78/q16 What rhetorical figure appears in Abraham's successive statements?  [* · rhetoric]
- 26:78/q16/p1 They exemplify al-tafwīt: varied meanings in successive sentences of equal measure, here in long sentences. — Al-Itqān defines the figure as praise and description arranged in equal long, medium or short sentences and identifies guidance, provision, healing, death and revival here as a long-sentence instance. It reports Ibn Abī al-Iṣbaʿ's claim that the Qur'an has no short-sentence example. [3 notes: ITQAN(ابن أبي الإصبع)]

## 26:78/q17 How does Abraham assign divine acts and illness in the wider speech?  [* · rhetoric]
- 26:78/q17/p1 He attributes creation, guidance, food, drink and healing to God, but phrases illness as 'when I am ill.' — Al-Sāmarrāʾī notes this difference in attribution across the sequence. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 26:78/q18 What inward meanings are given to the later acts named in Abraham's speech?  [* · inward]
turns on: The reported inward readings explain different later acts and images in the same speech.
- 26:78/q18/p1 The acts symbolize nourishment and healing of the soul and spiritual life through death of the passionate ego. — Study Quran gives this symbolic reading. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 26:78/q18/p2 Spiritual death and life can be justice and grace, disobedience and obedience, separation and meeting, or ignorance and knowledge. — Study Quran reports these pairings from al-Thaʿlabī. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Al-Thaʿlabī)]
- 26:78/q18/p3 People can die outwardly yet live inwardly, or die by forgetting God and live by remembering Him. — Study Quran reports these alternative formulations from others. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Su)]
- 26:78/q18/p4 The resurrected are naked, hungry and thirsty except those clothed in reverence and granted gnosis and love. — Study Quran reports Junayd's comparison. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Junayd)]
