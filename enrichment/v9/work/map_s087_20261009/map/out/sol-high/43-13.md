# 43:13: 30 questions

## 43:13/q01 What does istawā mean here?  [لِتَسْتَوُۥا۟ ٱسْتَوَيْتُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether istiwāʾ describes taking one's place on the vehicle or mastery over it.
- 43:13/q01/p1 To settle, sit, or mount a conveyance. — Muqātil glosses it as sitting; al-Māwardī as riding, al-Dāmghānī as riding or boarding. Al-Biqāʿī adds upright balance, steadiness, security and ease. Ibn ʿĀshūr says climbing aboard; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says settling on it. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; DAMGHANI; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 43:13/q01/p2 To gain mastery over the conveyance. — Asad cites al-Jawharī, al-Rāghib, Lisān al-ʿArab, and Lane for the connotation of mastery. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 43:13/q02 What is the force of the lām in litastawū?  [لِتَسْتَوُۥا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause gives God's end, the ensuing result, or a second-person imperative; the rarity of the last construction is disputed.
- 43:13/q02/p1 It is a lām of purpose: conveyances were made so people might ride. — Abū Ḥayyān calls this most natural; al-Samīn calls it apparent and attaches it to jaʿala; al-Ālūsī prefers it and reports Abū Ḥayyān's account of the majority view. Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows it. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 43:13/q02/p2 It may be a lām of consequence or result. — Al-Ḥūfī allows this category; al-Samīn also allows it and attaches it to jaʿala; al-Ālūsī reports the allowance. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 43:13/q02/p3 It may be a command lām: 'let you mount.' — Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows this; al-Zajjāj regards the second-person form as good Arabic. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn call it far-fetched because lām imperatives rarely take second-person tāʾ. The anomalous 10:58 reading, a poem with لتقم, and a Prophetic phrase are cited; the Prophetic wording may have been transmitted by meaning. Al-Ālūsī repeats those examples. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية,الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية)-]

## 43:13/q03 What conveyances are meant by the backs?  [ظُهُورِهِۦ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'backs' extends naturally to ships' upper surfaces or primarily names animal backs.
- 43:13/q03/p1 The backs or upper surfaces of what people ride, including animals and ships. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, and Asad explicitly include both. Ibn ʿĀshūr says ship decks or interiors can be spoken of by the shared sense of backs and upper surfaces, citing 23:28; large ships shelter riders. Al-Ālūsī says 'settling' especially suits animals while wording covers all. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(classical commentators); ALUSI-FULL]
- 43:13/q03/p2 Animal mounts, especially camels, are in view. — Muqātil specifies male and female camels. Mujāhid lists camels, horses, mules and donkeys. Al-Qurṭubī argues ships are boarded by their undersides, though he allows their submerged outside to count as inside; Bursawī says backs literally suit livestock, which gives them semantic priority. [6 notes: MUQATIL; MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI]

## 43:13/q04 Why are 'backs' plural while its pronoun is singular masculine?  [ظُهُورِهِۦ عَلَيْهِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether agreement is explained through relative mā, a generic singular, or the collective noun al-fulk.
- 43:13/q04/p1 The pronoun follows singular masculine mā, while plural 'backs' follows its collective meaning. — Abū ʿUbayda, al-Naḥḥās, al-Baghawī and others trace the pronoun to mā; al-Farrāʾ compares collective army or troop. The Basran grammarian says mā tarkabūn is masculine and notes anʿām can take either gender (16:66, 16:69). Al-Biqāʿī adds that ʿalayhi follows form and masculine wording may signal strength. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the pronoun covers ships and livestock. [21 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي البصرة,بعض نحويي الكوفة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد,الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيدة,الفراء); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABRISI; NAHHAS+]
- 43:13/q04/p2 The singular pronoun may follow the generic singular noun or collective ship noun. — Al-Farrāʾ compares 'the dirham became numerous'; another grammarian says al-fulk is collective, permitting plural backs with a singular pronoun and singular or plural actions. [2 notes: NAHHAS(الفراء); TAB-FULL(آخر منهم)]

## 43:13/q05 Which blessing should a rider recall?  [نِعْمَةَ رَبِّكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the blessing is the immediate transport, Islam, or divine gifts more broadly.
- 43:13/q05/p1 God's subjection and provision of the conveyance for land or sea travel. — Muqātil specifies the mount and praise; al-Kalbī says thank God for carrying the rider; Ibn ʿAbbās calls conveyances an honor to Adam's children, citing 17:70. Al-Rāzī includes sea, wind and ship, which humans did not devise by themselves. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes transport of loads (14:32, 16:7); Study Quran catalogs other cattle uses and signs. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; BURSEVI]
- 43:13/q05/p2 The blessing especially includes Islam, Muhammad, and belonging to the best community. — Shahr names Islam; the reports of Abū Mijlaz and the Prophet's family prescribe thanks for Islam, Muhammad, and the community before the riding formula. The names differ between al-Ḥasan and al-Ḥusayn in the transmitted reports. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو مجلز); KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن بن علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو مجلز); RAZI-FULL(الحسن بن علي); DURR-FULL(أبو مجلز,شهر بن حوشب); ALUSI-FULL(أبو مجلز,شهر بن حوشب)]
- 43:13/q05/p3 The words may recall God's blessings generally, beyond this ride. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī offers this move from the vehicle to God Himself; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says praise can concern blessing in every circumstance. [2 notes: TABATABAI; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 43:13/q06 How is the blessing to be remembered and thanked?  [تَذْكُرُوا۟ نِعْمَةَ رَبِّكُمْ وَتَقُولُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'remember' requires verbal thanks as well as inward acknowledgment, and whether the following tasbīḥ is itself that thanks.
- 43:13/q06/p1 Recognize the blessing inwardly and express gratitude aloud. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī distinguish heart awareness from spoken glorification. Al-Ḥasan's correction of a rider who only glorified God illustrates explicit praise. Al-Biqāʿī adds gratitude of limbs. Bursawī says heart remembrance explains the verb choice and cites the report that God looks to hearts and intentions. Ibn ʿĀshūr says riding's experienced benefit makes recollection timely and effective, while the wording counters polytheists thanking other gods; he compares taught praise and supplication at 1:2 and 2:286. Al-Ālūsī finds the joint inward and verbal account sound. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmits al-Ḥasan ibn ʿAlī asking a man who said the riding glorification whether he had been commanded to say it. [18 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن بن علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو مجلز); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري,بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الحسن بن علي عليهما السلام)]
- 43:13/q06/p2 Inward recognition can suffice for the remembrance before glorification. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says a rider may begin with glorification if the blessing of the mount and other blessings are recognized inwardly. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 43:13/q06/p3 Remembering means saying praise for the mount. — Muqātil states the spoken phrase 'Praise be to God'; al-Ṭabrisī combines remembering, thanking, and declaring God unlike creatures. [2 notes: MUQATIL; TABRISI]
- 43:13/q06/p4 Gratitude for the blessing is distinct from the ensuing glorification. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says remembering a blessing in use normally means thanking its giver; the subsequent tasbīḥ has another function, and variant reports include both praise and glorification. [3 notes: TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم وأئمة أهل البيت عليهم السلام)]

## 43:13/q07 Why does thumma place remembrance after mounting?  [ثُمَّ إِذَا ٱسْتَوَيْتُمْ · rhetoric]
- 43:13/q07/p1 Remembrance is a higher aim after the transport benefit, and should arise when the benefit is felt. — Al-Biqāʿī sees delay as marking a lofty goal and the Lord's nurturing kindness. Ibn ʿĀshūr says gratitude while experiencing the ride is more affecting and less apt to lapse; al-Biqāʿī calls familiar stability extraordinary beside unrideable animals and uncaring sea objects. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:13/q08 How does the 'when' clause time the commanded remembrance?  [إِذَا ٱسْتَوَيْتُمْ · grammar]
- 43:13/q08/p1 A command with 'when' can be performed before, at, or after the condition, depending on its action. — Al-Zarkashī illustrates the possibilities with washing before prayer, measured recitation at its occasion, and hunting or Friday prayer after their conditions. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 43:13/q09 What riding invocation is reported from ʿAlī and the Prophet?  [وَتَقُولُوا۟ سُبْحَٰنَ ٱلَّذِى سَخَّرَ لَنَا هَٰذَا · reports]
turns on: The transmitted practices differ by reporter, sequence, added phrases, and grading.
- 43:13/q09/p1 At the stirrup invoke God's name; once mounted, praise Him, recite the verse, magnify Him, and seek forgiveness. — ʿAlī says the Prophet acted likewise. Reports vary in whether they specify three repetitions of praise, takbīr, tasbīḥ or tahlīl. The Prophet marvels that the penitent knows only God forgives. Al-Tirmidhī's grading is cited as ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ; al-Ālūsī calls it authentic. Ibn Ḥibbān says the expanded words permit adding phrases. Al-Qurṭubī names Abū Dāwūd al-Ṭayālisī and Ibn Khuwayzimindād as transmitters; al-Thaʿlabī gives no grading. [24 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي بن أبي طالب); MUSNAD-AHMAD(رسول الله); IBNHIBBAN(علي بن ربيعة الأسدي); THALABI(علي); BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); RAZI-FULL(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب,علي بن ربيعة); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASAFI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي); ALUSI-FULL(علي); ELMALILI(Resûl-i Ekrem)]
- 43:13/q09/p2 ʿAlqama invoked God's name before mounting, praised Him once seated, and recited the formula when the animal rose. — Ibrāhīm transmits ʿAlqama ibn Qays's practice. [1 notes: MUJAHID(إبراهيم)]
- 43:13/q09/p3 Ṭāwūs or his father acknowledged the ride as God's bounty, praised Him, then recited the verse. — ʿAbd al-Razzāq attributes the practice to Ṭāwūs's father; al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, and al-Durr report Ṭāwūs himself. The wordings name favor, grace, or gift. [4 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(طاوس عن أبيه); TAB-FULL(طاوس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(طاوس); DURR-FULL(طاوس)]
- 43:13/q09/p4 The Prophet recited the riding formula and then prayed for a pious, easy and protected journey and a thankful return. — Ibn ʿUmar's reports add three opening takbīrs, righteousness, accepted deeds, divine company and care for family; on return he says they come back repentant, worshipping and praising God. Al-Tirmidhī grades one route ḥasan gharīb. Ibn Kathīr lists Muslim, Abū Dāwūd, Nasāʾī, Tirmidhī through Abū al-Zubayr; Ibn Ḥibbān adduces another transmission against the claim Ḥammād b. Salama alone carried that report. Abū Dāwūd adds the troops' takbīr uphill and tasbīḥ downhill. [13 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(رسول الله); IBNHIBBAN(ابن عمر,عبد الله بن عمر); RAZI-FULL(رسول الله); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمر); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عمر); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عمر); ELMALILI(Resûlullah); ABUDAWUD(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); MUSLIM(ابن عمر); TIRMIDHI(النبي)]
- 43:13/q09/p5 The Prophet magnified, praised, glorified and declared God one, then reclined and laughed. — Ibn ʿAbbās reports that God smiles toward a Muslim rider who does likewise. Aḥmad alone transmits it according to Ibn Kathīr. The reports vary over the threefold phrases and mention a single tahlīl. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 43:13/q10 What should be said on boarding a ship or disembarking?  [وَتَقُولُوا۟ · reports]
turns on: Whether the present formula covers ships or the separate scriptural boarding formula governs sea travel.
- 43:13/q10/p1 On ships say 11:41, 'In God's name is its course and anchorage'; on disembarking ask for a blessed landing (23:29). — Qatāda and several commentators distinguish this from the animal mounting formula. Al-Rāzī sets beside them a prayer for entering a dwelling. One report has al-Ḥasan ibn ʿAlī recite 11:41 and 39:67 aboard. Study Quran describes the land and sea practice. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(الحسن بن علي رضي الله عنهما); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 43:13/q10/p2 The remembrance and glorification in this passage apply to ships as well as animals. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ālūsī extend the formula to sea travel; al-Thaʿlabī's report has Qatāda teach words for both ships and livestock. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI(قتادة); TABRISI(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL]

## 43:13/q11 What happens in reports when riders omit the remembrance?  [تَذْكُرُوا۟ وَتَقُولُوا۟ · reports]
- 43:13/q11/p1 Satan urges an unremembering rider to sing or waste time in fantasies. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes this to the Prophet; al-Qurṭubī reports Mujāhid's version and says al-Naḥḥās transmitted it. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 43:13/q12 What do reports say about a devil on a camel?  [سَخَّرَ لَنَا هَٰذَا · reports]
- 43:13/q12/p1 A devil is on each camel's hump; invoke God and ride or tame it humbly, since God carries the rider. — Al-Qurṭubī also transmits Abū Jaʿfar's account through ʿAmr ibn Dīnār. Al-Durr preserves versions from Muḥammad b. Ḥamza, Abū Hurayra and Abū Lās. Al-Ālūsī says Aḥmad and others transmit a version. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عمرو بن دينار); DURR-FULL(أبو لاس الخزاعي,أبو هريرة,محمد بن حمزة بن عمر الأسلمي); ALUSI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 43:13/q13 What happened to the rider who boasted he could master his camel?  [وَمَا كُنَّا لَهُۥ مُقْرِنِينَ · reports]
- 43:13/q13/p1 The camel bucked or ran, threw the boasting rider, and broke his neck. — Sulaymān ibn Yasār's versions describe an emaciated, still or stubborn camel, while Ibn al-ʿArabī calls the rider a Bedouin; al-Ālūsī has a powerful camel. These details vary across the transmission. Al-Qurṭubī explains al-jawr baʿd al-kawr in the added travel prayer as affairs scattering after they were ordered. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(سليمان بن يسار); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أعرابي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,سليمان بن يسار); NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(سليمان بن يسار); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(سليمان بن يسار)]

## 43:13/q14 What conduct does this riding remembrance require?  [تَذْكُرُوا۟ وَتَقُولُوا۟ · law]
- 43:13/q14/p1 Recall the formula when mounting, especially for travel, while spoken recitation is not obligatory. — Al-Qurṭubī recommends adding prayers for divine company, safety from travel hardship and bad return; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls praise for Islam, Muhammad, or all circumstances the rider's sunnah. Ibn Ḥibbān permits adding phrases to the supplication. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNHIBBAN]
- 43:13/q14/p2 Riding should be for need, God's path, or a licit purpose, without heedless indulgence. — Abū Bakr b. Ṭāhir excludes amusement and pride. Al-Qurṭubī censures wine and instruments on pleasure trips, citing al-Zamakhsharī's story of a ruler drunk through a month's ride, and contrasts this with commanded remembrance. Elmalılı says journeying should recall return to God. [5 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر); QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري); ELMALILI]

## 43:13/q15 May someone ritually impure say the riding words?  [وَتَقُولُوا۟ · law]
- 43:13/q15/p1 Yes, if the intention is supplication rather than Qur'anic recitation. — Al-Nawawī gives the travel words among examples of Qur'anic phrases used with another intention. [1 notes: BURHAN(النووي)]

## 43:13/q16 Is the verse's instruction to say the formula a statement or a command?  [وَتَقُولُوا۟ · rhetoric]
- 43:13/q16/p1 Its declarative form carries an instruction to say the words. — The author of al-Naẓm reasons that if it were mere report, all people would actually do it; 51:56 similarly means they were created to be commanded to worship. Al-Ālūsī says the utterance's declarative wording implies a request. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); ALUSI-FULL]

## 43:13/q17 What does subḥāna express in the formula?  [سُبْحَٰنَ · interpretation]
- 43:13/q17/p1 It exalts God above defect and partners, with wonder at His power to subject the vehicle. — Al-Ṭabarī rejects polytheists' partners and descriptions; al-Ṭabrisī says God is unlike created things. Ibn ʿĀshūr says tasbīḥ explicitly negates defects and implicitly affirms perfections, gathering praise. Bursawī locates wonder in the mount's subjection. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses transcendence above a partner in lordship or divinity and distinguishes this from thanks. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 43:13/q18 Is alladhī read differently?  [ٱلَّذِى · readings]
- 43:13/q18/p1 ʿAlī is reported to have read man in place of alladhī. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this reading from ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب)]

## 43:13/q19 What does sakhkhara mean, and how was the conveyance subjected?  [سَخَّرَ · meaning]
- 43:13/q19/p1 God made the vehicle compliant and usable by riders. — Muqātil, al-Māwardī, and al-Baghawī stress its docility. Al-Rāzī describes a strong yet obedient animal with four legs that support secure seating, inspiring wonder at divine wisdom. Ibn ʿĀshūr extends this to navigable sea, wind, sails, oars, human ingenuity, and trainable animals. Al-Biqāʿī and Bursawī credit divine knowledge, power and wisdom. [15 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:13/q20 What does hādhā point to, and why use it?  [هَٰذَا · referent]
- 43:13/q20/p1 It points to the particular animal or ship being ridden. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the present conveyance; al-Ālūsī says the demonstrative pictures the situation and heightens wonder rather than belittling the gift. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 43:13/q21 What does muqrinīn mean?  [مُقْرِنِينَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the lexical emphasis is ability to match and control, companionship or coupling, ownership, or an attested contrary usage.
- 43:13/q21/p1 Able or strong enough to master, control, or match the conveyance. — Al-Kumayt, Ibn Hurmā and ʿAmr b. Maʿdīkarib furnish poetic evidence for capacity; Abū ʿUbayda, al-Akhfash and others gloss control. Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda, al-Suddī and Ibn Zayd explain lack of ability or strength, by hand or without God; Qatāda also mentions worship. Al-Zajjāj, Ibn Qutayba, al-Kashshāf and others derive the sense from being a peer or equal in strength. Ibn Qutayba distinguishes qarn with fatḥa as an age peer. Qutrub's poem is repeatedly cited. Ibn al-Sikkīt also derives it from binding and mastering with a tether; al-Qāmūs attests ability against weakness. The riding animal can be stronger than the human; God grants control. [70 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(غير مسمى); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,السدي,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس والكلبي,الأخفش,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الليث,المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن هرمة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة,ابن عباس ومجاهد,ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL(أبو عبيدة,الواحدي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن السكيت,ابن عباس والكلبي,الأخفش وأبو عبيدة,قطرب); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيد,ابن هرمة,قطرب); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس، قتادة، السدي، وابن زيد); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); ITQAN; BURSEVI(القاموس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن هرمة,قطرب); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 43:13/q21/p2 Its root may describe pairing or joining things for travel. — Al-Māwardī offers muqārana as an alternative derivation; Ibn al-Sikkīt also derives it from pairing during travel. Abū Ḥayyān notes qarn as a rope coupling things, and al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī cite a young camel tethered beside mature camels. These notes give an etymology rather than a different practical incapacity. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن السكيت); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 43:13/q21/p3 It can mean owners or masters. — Al-Dāmghānī says owners or those able to control; al-Baghawī gives mastery alongside capability. [2 notes: DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 43:13/q21/p4 It can denote a person overwhelmed by livestock or watering camels without a herder. — Al-Qurṭubī transmits both alternative uses of muqrin. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 43:13/q22 Are there variant readings of muqrinīn?  [مُقْرِنِينَ · readings]
turns on: The variant's consonants and vocalization.
- 43:13/q22/p1 A doubled rāʾ reading, muqarrinīn or muqirrinīn, has the same meaning. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Bayḍāwī report doubling; al-Ālūsī says the doubled rāʾ has fatḥa or kasra, both equivalent to the ordinary reading. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 43:13/q22/p2 A noncanonical reading has muqtarinīn, with an added tāʾ. — Abū Ḥayyān calls it the active participle of iqtaran; al-Samīn specifies the extra tāʾ before rāʾ. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(قارئ شاذ)]

## 43:13/q23 How does lahū attach grammatically?  [لَهُۥ مُقْرِنِينَ · grammar]
- 43:13/q23/p1 Lahū depends on muqrinīn and is fronted to maintain the verse endings. — Al-Samīn gives this syntactic and rhythmic explanation. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 43:13/q24 What does the admission that humans could not master it teach?  [وَمَا كُنَّا لَهُۥ مُقْرِنِينَ · interpretation]
- 43:13/q24/p1 Human strength alone cannot control the stronger mount or the ship; divine subjection supplies the capacity. — Ibn Zayd says people would lack power without God. Al-Rāzī points to the animal's strength and docility as signs of divine knowledge, wisdom and power. Al-Biqāʿī emphasizes dependence and secure travel; Bursawī says admitting incapacity is needed to value the blessing and benefactor. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the clause as circumstantial to lanā, describing divine subjection in place of bodily strength; Study Quran connects it with divine aid (31:20). [13 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]

## 43:13/q25 What does the purpose clause imply about divine intention and human acts?  [لِتَسْتَوُۥا۟ ثُمَّ تَذْكُرُوا۟ · theology]
- 43:13/q25/p1 God intends riding and remembrance, acts toward ends, and seeks servants' thanks. — Al-Kashshāf's three arguments, quoted by al-Rāzī, challenge the Mujabbira view that God intended unbelief and persistent denial. If human utterances were God's own acts, creating animals merely to create thanks on a tongue would be needless, since He could create the utterance directly. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف)]

## 43:13/q26 How does mounting connect with the return to God?  [إِذَا ٱسْتَوَيْتُمْ · links]
- 43:13/q26/p1 A journey and its dangers remind riders of death, return to God, and preparation for that meeting. — Al-Qurṭubī stresses accidental death and reforming oneself. Al-Bayḍāwī says the greatest journey is return to God; alternatively travel recalls death. Elmalılı sees every journey as change of state that prompts reflection on the greater journey. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 43:13/q27 What inward lesson is drawn from remembering blessings?  [نِعْمَةَ رَبِّكُمْ · inward]
- 43:13/q27/p1 A spiritually attentive person notices God's gifts beyond food, drink and transport, even in each breath and motion. — Sahl says prophets and some truthful servants were given awareness of blessings before they vanished and of God's forbearance or decree; Tustarī says limiting blessings to material provision belittles them. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says ordinary people miss gifts in every breath, blink, motion and stillness. [4 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء,بعضهم,سهل)]

## 43:13/q28 Do the second-person pronouns here mark an address shift?  [لِتَسْتَوُۥا۟ رَبِّكُمْ ٱسْتَوَيْتُمْ · rhetoric]
- 43:13/q28/p1 No; they continue the expected address. — Ibn ʿĀshūr counts eleven second-person pronouns across the four verses and denies that they are iltifāt. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:13/q29 What neighbouring verses or sūra themes frame the riding blessing?  [* · links]
turns on: Which element of the surrounding context is taken as framing the ride.
- 43:13/q29/p1 It continues earlier mention of transport and creation's gifts within the sūra's challenge to idolatry. — Ibn al-Jawzī refers explanation of this material to Yā-Sīn 36:42. Study Quran lists seafaring and cattle's many benefits with 14:32, 17:66, 22:65, 30:46, 6:142, 16:5, 16:66, 16:79, 23:21–22, 36:72 and 40:79; it describes the sūra as answering Makkan idolaters. Iṣlāḥī says every part of the sūra forms a harmonious unit around its central theme. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 43:13/q29/p2 The preceding pairs show creation's relative multiplicity against the Creator's transcendence. — Study Quran records views that pairs mean all animal species, all opposites, or male and female members of kinds, citing 36:36. It reports al-Rāzī's argument that the Absolute Creator transcends duality. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī,some commentators)]

## 43:13/q30 How is the passage used in discussion of poetry and expressive precision?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes supply two different rhetorical uses or contexts rather than a shared explanation of one verse word.
- 43:13/q30/p1 Its subjugation wording can be made metrical by adding words. — Al-Bāqillānī says a poet can expand the verse's wording until it scans. [1 notes: BAQILLANI]
- 43:13/q30/p2 Eloquence and compound simile depend on exact arrangement and detailed fit. — Al-Jurjānī says general reference to word arrangement is insufficient; the features of excellence must be specified, like brocade weaving. In his compound simile discussion he contrasts precise musk placed at an agate cup's bottom with wording suggesting it fills the cup; moist residual ghāliya better resembles a plant's thin dark stain than dry musk. The dawn and crow image maps darkness onto crows, pale feathers onto the fringe of light, and makes dawn seem to drive darkness away. These notes give rhetorical context without a specific claim about a word of 43:13. [7 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR; JURJANI-DALAIL]
