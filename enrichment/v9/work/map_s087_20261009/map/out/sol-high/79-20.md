# 79:20: 11 questions

## 79:20/q01 How does the fāʾ connect the showing to the preceding command?  [فَأَرَىٰهُ · grammar]
- 79:20/q01/p1 It follows an omitted account of Moses going to Pharaoh before showing the sign. — Al-Rāzī and al-Zamakhsharī supply 'he went' after the command; both compare the omitted striking before the rock split in 2:60. Al-Ālūsī says the fuller narrative allows more intervening events than al-Zamakhsharī supplies. Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies that Moses went, called Pharaoh, was rejected, and showed the sign; Ṭabāṭabāʾī supplies his coming and calling him. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī call the fāʾ explanatory. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 79:20/q02 What does Moses' showing of the sign entail?  [فَأَرَىٰهُ · interpretation]
- 79:20/q02/p1 He made Pharaoh see the sign or acquainted him with it. — Al-Ālūsī gives both senses for the verb and says Pharaoh recognized the sign on seeing it. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 79:20/q03 Why are the signs attributed to Moses as the one who showed them?  [فَأَرَىٰهُ · theology]
- 79:20/q03/p1 Moses is their apparent agent, while God is their true source. — Bursevī contrasts the outward attribution here with God's attribution of the signs to Himself in 20:56. Al-Ālūsī makes the same distinction and cites God's 'We showed him Our signs' in 20:42. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 79:20/q04 What does āya mean in this verse?  [ٱلْءَايَةَ · meaning]
- 79:20/q04/p1 It is a miraculous sign and proof that Moses was God's messenger. — Al-Qurṭubī calls it the supreme miracle; al-Ṭabarī a great proof of prophethood; Ibn Kathīr powerful and clear evidence of a message from God; al-Biqāʿī a sign proving Moses' message after Pharaoh demanded proof. Ibn ʿĀshūr traces āya from sign or marker to evidence establishing truth and then a messenger's miracle. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says religious usage can prove God's existence, greatness, unity, or power or support a chosen messenger, and here supports Moses' mission. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI]

## 79:20/q05 Why is āya used with the definite article?  [ٱلْءَايَةَ · grammar]
- 79:20/q05/p1 The noun must be definite here; an indefinite form is not permissible. — Al-Naḥḥās says the term cannot occur here without the article or as an indefinite noun. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 79:20/q06 What was the great sign shown to Pharaoh?  [ٱلْءَايَةَ ٱلْكُبْرَىٰ · referent]
turns on: The accounts and comparative force of Moses' miracles: some single out the staff or hand, some join them or all the miracles as one proof; others read the sign beyond those miracles. Al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī appeal to the narrative sequence against the collective reading.
- 79:20/q06/p1 Moses' staff, especially its transformation into a serpent. — Ibn Zayd calls it the staff and serpent. ʿAṭāʾ says the hand changed color, whereas the staff became alive, grew, swallowed objects, then returned to its former lifeless form. Al-Bayḍāwī calls the serpent the first and central miracle; Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn ʿAbbās as cited by al-Ālūsī, and al-Zamakhsharī make the hand secondary. Bursevī says the sequence makes it first and the other miracles lesser. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī favors the detailed account; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Pharaoh's demand and the ensuing staff miracle in 26:30–32. Elmalılı sees proof of resurrection and the creative command 'Be.' Iṣlāḥī says the other miracles, apart from the white hand, were contained in and manifested through the staff. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports an argument that its change into a living, powerful thing surpasses a color change. The Study Quran cites 7:107, 20:66–67, and 26:32, 44–45. The wordings 'staff,' 'staff and serpent,' and 'staff becoming a serpent' describe this same identification. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(الزمخشري,مفسرون); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some); TABATABAI+]
- 79:20/q06/p2 Moses' staff and shining white hand together count as the great sign. — Al-Ḥasan, Qatāda, Mujāhid, and the majority of commentators are reported for both signs. Al-Ṭabarī describes the white hand and staff becoming a manifest serpent; Qatāda calls them two signs. Muqātil says the hand shone like the sun without leprosy, dazzling the eye. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says they were displayed in challenge and defeated the false party. Al-Rāzī reports Mujāhid's sequence, staff first and then hand. Al-Nasafī says the pair counts as one sign; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī, and al-Zamakhsharī as reported by Bint al-Shāṭiʾ make the hand subsidiary, with their combined proof counting as one. Al-Ālūsī notes that the two are called plural in 20:42 because each contains multiple clear wonders for rational people. [20 notes: MUJAHID(الحسن); MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(الحسن,قتادة,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وغيره); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(جمهور المفسرين); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); BINTSHATI(أبو حيان,الزمخشري)]
- 79:20/q06/p3 Moses' shining white hand alone was the great sign. — Al-Qu­shayrī and al-Ṭabrisī compare its brilliance to the sun; al-Qu­shayrī reports that Pharaoh then consulted Haman. Muqātil says it was more wondrous than the staff, dazzling yet free of leprosy. Al-Rāzī reports Maqātil and al-Kalbī citing 27:12 and 20:23; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports exegetes' appeal to 20:23 calling it one of the greatest signs. The Study Quran cites 7:108, 26:33, and 27:12. [10 notes: MUQATIL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(مقاتل والكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BINTSHATI(مفسرون); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 79:20/q06/p4 All of Moses' miracles collectively were one great sign. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī say their shared evidentiary force permits a singular sign; al-Ālūsī also mentions referring to the group through its first instance. Kur'an Yolu specifies all nine and cites 7:107–108, 7:133–136, and 17:101. Al-Ālūsī argues against this reading: the magicians were gathered only after the staff and hand, while the remaining nine signs came after their defeat over roughly twenty years (7:133). Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it remote on the detailed account. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(some); ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 79:20/q06/p5 The sign was the parting of the sea. — The Study Quran cites 7:136, 10:90, 20:77–78, and 26:63–66; al-Qurṭubī and Kur'an Yolu also report this identification. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 79:20/q06/p6 The verse leaves the great sign unidentified. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers the Qur'an's choice to call it 'the great sign' and stresses its proof of Moses and God's power. The Study Quran notes that the account of nine clear signs in 17:101 does not select a greatest one. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN; BINTSHATI+]
- 79:20/q06/p7 It refers to Paradise and Hell. — Al-Māwardī reports this from al-Suddī. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(السدي)]
- 79:20/q06/p8 It was God's speech to Moses from the tree. — Al-Māwardī offers this as a third possibility. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 79:20/q06/p9 It was the true identity disclosed by intellectual unity and divine guidance. — Al-Qāshānī says Pharaoh's veil and delusion prevented recognition of a higher station. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 79:20/q06/p10 It was God's guidance offered even to Pharaoh. — Asad calls it measureless grace offered to even the most recalcitrant sinner. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 79:20/q06/p11 It was ʿAlī appearing to Moses in a transformed spiritual state. — Al-Ālūsī attributes this claim to extremist Shīʿīs and rejects it as irrational and unsupported by transmitted tradition. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(غلاة الشيعة)-]

## 79:20/q07 How does the feminine superlative al-kubrā function grammatically?  [ٱلْكُبْرَىٰ · grammar]
- 79:20/q07/p1 Though originally adjectival, this form can function as a noun. — Al-Burhān compares al-quṣwā and al-kubrā and gives al-abṭaḥ, al-ajraʿ, and al-adham as other adjectives used substantively. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 79:20/q08 Why is this sign called great or greatest?  [ٱلْكُبْرَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes compare the hand's brilliance and 20:23 with the staff's greater transformation, or take 'great' as a description of the sign's power as proof or of a collective miracle.
- 79:20/q08/p1 The word magnifies the force of the proof for Moses' mission. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the epithet strengthens the sign's evidentiary support and turns to Moses' call in Ṭā Hā, citing 20:12 and 20:17–18. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 79:20/q08/p2 The staff was greatest because its transformations surpassed the hand's color change. — ʿAṭāʾ says it came alive, grew, swallowed objects, then lost life and form; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports the same comparison in terms of life and power in an inanimate thing. Al-Bayḍāwī calls it the first and central miracle; Bursevī contrasts it with lesser later signs. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(عطاء); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; BINTSHATI(مفسرون)]
- 79:20/q08/p3 The hand was greater for its dazzling radiance or the Qur'anic description of it. — Muqātil says the hand shone like the sun, dazzled the eye, and was more wondrous than the staff without being leprous. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports appeal to 20:23, where the hand is called one of the greatest signs. [2 notes: MUQATIL; BINTSHATI(مفسرون)]
- 79:20/q08/p4 The collective miracle's greatness may be relative to earlier prophets' miracles or absolute. — Al-Ālūsī gives both senses for the proposal that all Moses' miracles are one sign, but considers the absolute sense less plausible. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 79:20/q09 How did Pharaoh respond after seeing the sign?  [* · reports]
turns on: These reports describe different parts of Pharaoh's response, rather than conflicting alternatives.
- 79:20/q09/p1 He recognized the sign but called it magic out of stubborn bravado. — Bursevī says recognition preceded his later claim of magic; al-Ālūsī says the claim feigned bravado. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 79:20/q09/p2 He consulted Haman, who warned submission would cost him kingship. — Al-Qushayrī relates this after the shining white hand appeared. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 79:20/q10 What does the demonstration teach about proof and guidance?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes either draw a conclusion about miraculous evidence establishing the messenger's claim or about the offer of divine guidance.
- 79:20/q10/p1 A miracle makes the proof binding. — Al-Jishumī states this explicitly. Al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Bint al-Shāṭiʾ, and al-Biqāʿī describe the sign as evidence for Moses' divine mission; al-Biqāʿī says Pharaoh had demanded such proof despite raising Moses and doubting his exceptional status. Elmalılı says Moses moved to a physical sign when verbal evidence failed to persuade Pharaoh. [8 notes: JISHUMI; TAB-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI]
- 79:20/q10/p2 God's guidance remains graciously offered even to the most resistant sinner. — Asad applies this to Pharaoh, identifying the great sign as God's guidance. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 79:20/q11 How does this verse serve the sūra's theme?  [* · links]
- 79:20/q11/p1 Its sign contributes to the sūra's proof of resurrection and final gathering. — Al-Biqāʿī says the sūra establishes resurrection, gathering, and humanity's final fate after preceding sūras' exposition, with its oaths reaching a decisive climax. Elmalılı takes the staff's transformation into a serpent as evidence of resurrection and God's creative command 'Be.' [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]
