# 94:6: 18 questions

## 94:6/q01 What does ʿusr mean here?  [ٱلْعُسْرِ · meaning]
- 94:6/q01/p1 Severe constriction and difficulty. — Qurṭubī glosses it as constriction and difficulty. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ emphasizes intense struggle, citing Arabic use and 54:8, 74:9, 25:26, 92:10, 65:6–7, 9:117, and 18:73. [2 notes: QURTUBI; BINTSHATI]

## 94:6/q02 Which hardship is meant?  [ٱلْعُسْرِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the definite noun points to the Prophet's known distress or hardship as a general kind; specific accounts identify struggle, poverty, or expulsion.
- 94:6/q02/p1 The Prophet's struggle against polytheists. — Ṭabarī and al-Baghawī name the struggle and harm of the polytheists; al-Nasafī names their abuse. Ibn ʿAṭiyya points to harm witnessed by the Prophet. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ identifies his distress before entrenched idolatry. [8 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(قيل); BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI; IBNATIYYA; BINTSHATI+]
- 94:6/q02/p2 The Prophet's poverty and the mockery it brought. — Al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī say mockery of his poverty distressed him. Al-Nasafī and al-Kashshāf as cited by Bursawī add that the believers were mocked, and the Prophet feared poverty might turn them from Islam. The asbāb report locates this in Mecca. [6 notes: RAZI; NASAFI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(الكشاف); SUYUTI-LUBAB; QURTUBI+]
- 94:6/q02/p3 The Prophet's expulsion from Mecca. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this as another interpretation, pairing it with his honorable return at the conquest. [1 notes: QURTUBI]
- 94:6/q02/p4 Worldly adversity and the trials of believers generally. — Al-Qushayrī says all believers undergo worldly trials. Al-Māwardī allows worldly hardship or the hardship of a tested person. Al-Ālūsī notes the generic article can include constricted breast, burden and obscurity; Bursawī calls this world a familiar abode of hardship. [29 notes: SULAMI(القاسم); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF; BURSEVI; ALUSI(بعض الأجلة); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(قسم من المفسرين); ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+]
- 94:6/q02/p5 The acute trials of the Prophet and believers in Mecca. — Kuranyolu specifies escalating persecution and torture, grieving them; Quṭb says the Prophet's weighty concern must have been immense. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 94:6/q02/p6 Two spiritual veils: creation from God, then God from creation. — Al-Qāshānī relates these to disclosure of the Essence and divinely granted expansion. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 94:6/q03 What does maʿa say about the timing of ease?  [مَعَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether maʿa is temporal nearness, actual co-presence, or an inward transformation of hardship.
- 94:6/q03/p1 Ease follows hardship so soon that it is portrayed as accompanying it. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursawī, al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı treat accompaniment as close succession; Elmalılı cites 65:7. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nīsābūrī and ʿAbduh on this consoling nearness. Mujāhid simply says ease follows hardship. Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts the specific future relief in 65:7 with this verse's general wording. [17 notes: TAB(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF; RAZI; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN; DURR(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI(الزمخشري,الشيخ محمد عبده,النيسابوري); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BINTSHATI-]
- 94:6/q03/p2 Ease is truly present with hardship. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ favors literal companionship for the Prophet. Elmalılı says one event can be hard and easy in different aspects. Bursawī reports the elite reading and Ibn al-ʿArabī's intermixture: ease prevents ruin within hardship, while hardship gives ease contrast. Al-Ālūsī reports that God may avert a worse harm. [8 notes: BURSEVI(الشيخ الأكبر); ALUSI(بعض الأفاضل); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI+; ALUSI(أنس بن مالك)-; ALUSI-FULL(أنس بن مالك)-]
- 94:6/q03/p3 The lover experiences even the Beloved's hardship as ease. — Bursawī reports Ibn al-ʿArabī's spiritual account: hardship polishes the elect's hearts for disclosure and mercy precedes wrath. Al-Ālūsī reports Ibn al-Fāriḍ and a Persian poet on sweet harsh treatment but dismisses this construal as strained. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الشيخ الأكبر); ALUSI(بعضهم)-; ALUSI-FULL(ابن الفارض)-]

## 94:6/q04 What does yusr mean here?  [يُسْرًۭا · meaning]
- 94:6/q04/p1 Relief, spaciousness, or an untroubled means of provision. — Qurṭubī contrasts hardship with spaciousness and wealth. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says Arabic uses yusr for wealth and for what is prepared or achieved without difficulty, citing 65:7, 2:185, 2:280, 74:9, 92:7, 92:10, 2:196, 73:20, 54:17, and 44:58. [2 notes: QURTUBI; BINTSHATI]

## 94:6/q05 What concrete ease is promised?  [يُسْرًۭا · referent]
turns on: Whether the two occurrences designate distinct worldly events, this life and the next, a broad kind of relief, or a spiritual counterpart.
- 94:6/q05/p1 Victory and relief from the polytheists. — Ṭabarī and al-Baghawī expect the Prophet's eventual triumph and their submission to truth, willingly or under compulsion. Al-Qurṭubī describes the conquests of the Hijaz and Yemen, wealth, generous gifts, and family provisions before the Prophet's death. Al-Nasafī says God made him prevail. [15 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(قيل); BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI; JISHUMI; QURTUBI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KASHSHAF; RAZI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 94:6/q05/p2 Return to Mecca at its conquest. — Al-Qurṭubī reports that the ease countering expulsion was the Prophet's honorable return with ten thousand men. [1 notes: QURTUBI]
- 94:6/q05/p3 Worldly relief followed by enduring afterlife ease. — Al-Thalabī, al-Qushayrī, al-Jurjānī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī and others distinguish worldly prosperity or victory from Paradise and recompense. Al-Kashshāf compares the two good outcomes of 9:52; al-Rāzī names opened lands and Paradise. Al-Bayḍāwī likens them to the fasting person's two joys. Some identify the first as the Prophet's favor and the second as for all believers. [42 notes: THALABI(أبو بكر الوراق,القاسم,قيل); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الجرجاني صاحب النظم); BAGHAWI(أبو علي الحسن بن يحيى بن نصر الجرجاني); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو علي الحسن بن يحيى بن نصر الجرجاني); KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA(بعض الناس); TABRISI; RAZI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI; IBNASHUR(من المفسرين); STUDYQURAN; QURTUBI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 94:6/q05/p4 Ease in the afterlife, as reward for worldly toil or patient restraint. — Al-Warrāq names Paradise as reward for worldly hardship; al-Jawzajānī names the dignity of reliance after avoiding forbidden and doubtful things. Al-Qāsim contrasts the fortunate's worldly prison and future pleasure with the wretched's confinement in both states; al-Māwardī includes contentment or provision for the patient. [11 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر الوراق,الجوزجاني,القاسم); THALABI(أبو بكر الوراق,القاسم); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 94:6/q05/p5 Two episodes of worldly relief or victories. — Jishumī reports two worldly eases; al-Kashshāf names victories under the Prophet and caliphs. Al-Ālūsī also allows caliphal relief. [4 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 94:6/q05/p6 God's favors outnumber ordeals. — Al-Naḥḥās reports this reading. Jishumī says Abū ʿAlī equates ease with blessings and hardship with ordeals; al-Ālūsī links the earlier gifts of expanded breast, lifted burden and raised mention with great relief. [4 notes: NAHHAS(قيل); JISHUMI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 94:6/q05/p7 Some real ease exists within each hardship, at least the avoidance of something worse. — Al-Ālūsī reports this as a literal-accompaniment answer to hard cases; the ease may also be immediate assurance followed by customary later relief. [3 notes: ALUSI(بعض الأفاضل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 94:6/q05/p8 Spiritual disclosure and expansion. — Al-Qāshānī pairs two veils with disclosure of the divine Essence through sainthood and divinely given expansion in prophethood. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 94:6/q06 Why is yusr indefinite?  [يُسْرًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the indefinite form conveys magnitude, unspecified scope, or numerical difference on repetition.
- 94:6/q06/p1 It magnifies the promised ease. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī call it immense and extraordinary; al-Ālūsī also allows the form to suggest a fresh promise. [5 notes: KASHSHAF; RAZI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 94:6/q06/p2 It leaves the relief open, without setting its limits. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ contrasts the known hardship of the Prophet with an unspecified ease. [1 notes: BINTSHATI+]
- 94:6/q06/p3 It signals a second distinct ease in the repeated clause. — Al-Qushayrī, al-Bayḍāwī and many others apply the ordinary treatment of a repeated indefinite; Biqāʿī insists this is only a default, not an absolute rule. [23 notes: THALABI(العلماء); MAWARDI(ثعلب); MAWARDI-FULL(ثعلب); QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI(المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI(الفراء والزجاج); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI(أبو معاذ); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ITQAN; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL(سعد الدين التفتازاني); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI+]

## 94:6/q07 Why is the assurance repeated?  [إِنَّ مَعَ ٱلْعُسْرِ يُسْرًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether repetition itself adds a distinct promise or instead reinforces the first, and whether the sequence describes recurring cycles.
- 94:6/q07/p1 It confirms one promise and strengthens hope. — Ibn Qutayba cites 102:3–4, 75:34–35 and 82:17–18; al-Māwardī transmits al-Farrāʾ's repeated answers and al-Khansāʾ's poem. Al-Kashshāf compares 52:11; al-Rāzī compares 77:15. Al-Burhān says repeating a sentence need not create a second object, like repeating 'Zayd has a book.' Quṭb takes the repetition as fitting the Prophet's severe burden; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says it establishes inward certainty. Al-Itqān observes that a repeated sentence is ideally introduced by thumma, as in repeated Judgment Day warnings. [44 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; BAQILLANI; THALABI; MAWARDI(الفراء); MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA; RAZI; QURTUBI(الفراء); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI; BURSEVI; ALUSI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(most commentators); ITQAN; BINTSHATI+; BURHAN+; ALUSI-FULL-; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 94:6/q07/p2 It establishes a fresh promise of another ease. — Al-Thalabī, al-Jurjānī and al-Qurṭubī see the absence of a conjunction as a new statement for believers, with afterlife ease. Al-Kashshāf and al-Bayḍāwī allow another ease; al-Ālūsī prefers fresh establishment as more eloquent in consolation and says simple syntactic resumption explains the missing waw. ʿIṣām al-Dīn calls separation artful because the second resembles repetition. [29 notes: THALABI(قيل); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF; BAGHAWI(أبو علي الحسن بن يحيى بن نصر الجرجاني); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو علي الحسن بن يحيى بن نصر الجرجاني); TABRISI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI(عصام الدين); ALUSI-FULL(عصام الدين); IBNASHUR(من المفسرين); TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+; QURTUBI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 94:6/q07/p3 It marks recurring cycles of hardship, success, and renewed trial. — Iṣlāḥī says the mission proceeds through further struggles after each success, requiring steady work; each hardship and ease coexist in the divine pattern. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 94:6/q07/p4 It strengthens the idea of ease without deciding whether there are two separate eases. — Al-Kashshāf says the saying may reflect the promise's fullest hopeful sense; al-Ālūsī says one magnified ease can cover both realms and Ibn Masʿūd's codex has the clause once. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats the second sentence as pure confirmation and the dual as abundance. [12 notes: KASHSHAF; ALUSI(ابن مسعود,بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 94:6/q08 Do the repeated nouns establish one hardship and two eases?  [ٱلْعُسْرِ يُسْرًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether Arabic definiteness alone counts referents, or whether sentence repetition and context can override an ordinary default.
- 94:6/q08/p1 Yes: the repeated definite hardship is one, and the repeated indefinite ease is different. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Zajjāj, Thaʿlab and many commentators apply the identity and difference rule; al-Baghawī illustrates with 'the dirham' and 'a dirham,' al-Nasafī cites Abū Muʿādh, and al-Tanūkhī calls the hardship a unique generic kind. Biqāʿī accepts the rule as a contextual default. The saying of one hardship and two eases supports this reading for Jishumī. [45 notes: THALABI(العلماء); MAWARDI(ثعلب); MAWARDI-FULL(ثعلب); QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI(المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA(كثير من العلماء); RAZI(ابن عباس,الفراء والزجاج); QURTUBI(ابن عباس,ثعلب); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI(أبو معاذ); ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN(التنوخي); BIQAI(الكشاف,سعد الدين التفتازاني); BIQAI-FULL(سعد الدين التفتازاني); ITQAN; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR(شاع بين أهل العلم); BINTSHATI(أبو حيان,الفراء والزجاج,النيسابوري); BIQAI+; JISHUMI(النبي)+; THALABI(الحسن بن يحيى بن نصر الجرجاني)-; BAGHAWI(أبو علي الحسن بن يحيى بن نصر الجرجاني)-; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو علي الحسن بن يحيى بن نصر الجرجاني)-; RAZI(الجرجاني)-; QURTUBI(الجرجاني)-; IBNASHUR(أبو علي الحسين الجرجاني,صاحب الكشاف وابن هشام)-; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو علي الحسين الجرجاني)-; BINTSHATI(الجرجاني)-]
- 94:6/q08/p2 No: the forms do not force a count of two eases. — Al-Jurjānī's repeated horse-and-sword example would not force one horse and two swords. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the relevant anaphoric rule concerns an indefinite followed by a definite in separate sentences; here the article is generic and the sentence repeats, citing 73:15–16. He reports that al-Kashshāf and Ibn Hishām also object. Al-Burhān compares repeated 'Zayd has a book.' Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects multiplying grammatical scenarios. [20 notes: THALABI(الحسن بن يحيى بن نصر الجرجاني); JISHUMI(الجرجاني صاحب النظم); BAGHAWI(أبو علي الحسن بن يحيى بن نصر الجرجاني); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو علي الحسن بن يحيى بن نصر الجرجاني); RAZI(الجرجاني); QURTUBI(الجرجاني); BURHAN; IBNASHUR(أبو علي الحسين الجرجاني,صاحب الكشاف وابن هشام); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو علي الحسين الجرجاني); TABATABAI; BINTSHATI(الجرجاني,النيسابوري); BURHAN+; JISHUMI-]
- 94:6/q08/p3 The rule is a defeasible default, and context decides here. — Biqāʿī cites al-Taftāzānī's four combinations but rejects an absolute rule. Repeated definites may differ (5:48, 3:26, 55:60, 5:45); repeated indefinites may stay identical (43:84, 6:37, 30:54); shifts also occur in 6:154–55, 2:36, 6:165, 4:153, 6:156, 4:128, 16:88, 18:110. He still applies the ordinary rule here. Ṭabāṭabāʾī also calls the rule non-universal. [16 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI+]

## 94:6/q09 What is meant by the saying that one hardship cannot overcome two eases?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'two' counts distinct promised instances or expresses the predominance of ease, and whether worldly and afterlife relief are separate.
- 94:6/q09/p1 One hardship is outweighed by two actual eases. — Ibn ʿAbbās says God made one hardship between two eases. The widely reported maxim is tied to repeated definite hardship and indefinite ease; some identify the two with this life and the next, or with two worldly victories. Al-Thalabī compares two feast months not both being diminished by one event. Peygamber); STUDYQURAN; BIQAI+; JISHUMI(النبي)+]
- 94:6/q09/p2 The afterlife ease cannot be overcome even if worldly ease fails. — Al-Thalabī, al-Jurjānī and al-Qurṭubī say the single worldly hardship may beat worldly ease, but the enduring afterlife promise is secure; the second uncoordinated clause extends to believers. [9 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI(الجرجاني صاحب النظم); BAGHAWI(أبو علي الحسن بن يحيى بن نصر الجرجاني); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 94:6/q09/p3 The dual is symbolic of ease's repeated abundance and dominance. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says two can be an intensifying dual, as in repeated looking at 67:4 and labbayk. The second clause makes ease preponderant over hardship without counting two distinct referents. [6 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 94:6/q09/p4 A single magnified ease can span both worlds despite one written occurrence. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Ālūsī say the maxim does not compel a new second promise; Ibn Masʿūd's codex reportedly has the clause once, yet the saying is attributed to him. Al-Ālūsī rejects the strained explanation that hardship cannot overcome ease simply because it is said twice. [6 notes: KASHSHAF; ALUSI(ابن مسعود); ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI(بعضهم)-]

## 94:6/q10 For whom and under what conditions does the promise hold?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether a specific consolation can be generalized, and how apparently unrelieved hardship affects the breadth of the promise.
- 94:6/q10/p1 It chiefly addresses the Prophet's known hardship. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ stresses the direct context; al-Ālūsī says a particular known hardship avoids counterexamples though the apparent rhetoric favors generality. Al-Sāmarrāʾī reports confinement to the Prophet because this and al-Ḍuḥā address him. [6 notes: BINTSHATI; ALUSI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(قسم من المفسرين); ALUSI-FULL+; BINTSHATI+]
- 94:6/q10/p2 It extends to every believer, at least through afterlife relief. — Al-Thalabī, al-Jurjānī and al-Qurṭubī take the second clause as for all believers. Al-Qushayrī says believers share worldly trials and two eases. Al-Nasafī and Kuranyolu take it as consolation for the community. Asad says the surah addresses followers through the Prophet. [17 notes: THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI(أبو علي الحسن بن يحيى بن نصر الجرجاني); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو علي الحسن بن يحيى بن نصر الجرجاني); TABRISI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR-FULL(الحسن)]
- 94:6/q10/p3 Every decreed hardship has a corresponding ease. — Al-Māwardī offers relief with adversity, patience with expansion and misery with happiness; al-Nīsābūrī's general reading is reported by Bint al-Shāṭiʾ. Al-Sāmarrāʾī reports the general promise that ease accompanies and overcomes decreed hardship. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a regular universal fulfillment. [15 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA(كثير من العلماء); BURSEVI; ALUSI(بعض الأجلة); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI(النيسابوري); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(قسم من المفسرين); ALUSI-]
- 94:6/q10/p4 It is conditioned by patience, faith, hope, reliance, or God's will. — Al-Māwardī limits reward for the tested to patience and seeking reward; Elmalılı cites 92:5–7 for faith and endurance, placing tranquility in a believer's heart. Al-Ālūsī raises lasting illness, poverty, the disbeliever and the impatient believer as counterexamples, then reports customary scope, patient believers, God's will, and trust and hope. Qatāda's version adds 'God willing.' Iṣlāḥī says the steadfast receive renewed ease. [22 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; SULAMI(الجوزجاني); ALUSI(بعض,زيد بن أسلم,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI(Zeyd b. Eslem، Ebû Ubeyde، Ömer b. Hattâb); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 94:6/q10/p5 God disposes hardship and ease as He wills. — Al-Māwardī states this as an independent possibility and allows worldly hardship with future ease or contentment and provision for the patient. [4 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL]

## 94:6/q11 What practical teaching is drawn from the promise?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the main exhortation is patient expectation of relief or sustained effort through recurring trials.
- 94:6/q11/p1 Endure hardship with hope, patience, and trust in God. — Al-Māwardī and Thaʿlab say the doubled assurance strengthens hope and patience. Al-Thalabī cites poems by an unnamed poet, al-Karkhī, Zayd al-ʿAlawī, and al-Raqqī: calamities end, winter yields to spring, and relief comes at the peak of distress. Ibn Kathīr cites al-Shāfiʿī, Abū Ḥātim al-Sijistānī and anonymous poems on patient trust and near deliverance. The Study Quran cites 65:7 and 2:185; Bursawī cites 40:60 on persistent prayer. [34 notes: MAJAZ; THALABI(زيد بن محمد العلوي,سليمان بن أحمد الرقي,شاعر غير مسمى,محمد بن سليمان بن معاذ الكرخي); MAWARDI(ثعلب); MAWARDI-FULL(ثعلب); KASHSHAF; IBNKATHIR(آخر,أبو حاتم السجستاني,أبو هريرة,الشافعي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن دريد,الشافعي,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI; BURSEVI(رسول الله); NASAFI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 94:6/q11/p2 Continue the struggle because new trials follow each success. — Iṣlāḥī reads the sequence as a pattern through the mission: relief enables further work, and trial distinguishes the righteous from hypocrites and disbelievers, making recompense just. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 94:6/q12 How is the sīn vocalized?  [ٱلْعُسْرِ يُسْرًۭا · readings]
turns on: Whether the dialectal medial sīn is still or has ḍamma.
- 94:6/q12/p1 With sukūn in the common reading. — Al-Thalabī and Jishumī name the common reading; Abū Ḥayyān identifies it as the majority reading. [4 notes: THALABI(قراءة العامة); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر); ABUHAYYAN(ابن وثاب وأبو جعفر وعيسى والجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن وثاب وأبو جعفر وعيسى)]
- 94:6/q12/p2 With ḍamma in the reported reading, without a change in meaning. — Al-Naḥḥās names ʿĪsā; al-Thalabī names Abū Jaʿfar and ʿĪsā; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and later sources add Ibn Waththāb. Some say both al-ʿusr and yusr, others specify yusr. Qirāʾāt ER describes the full vocalization as a dialect variant parallel to al-ʿusuri. [10 notes: NAHHAS(عيسى بن عمر); THALABI(أبو جعفر وعيسى); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر); IBNATIYYA(عيسى ويحيى بن وثاب وأبو جعفر); ABUHAYYAN(ابن وثاب وأبو جعفر وعيسى والجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن وثاب وأبو جعفر وعيسى); ALUSI(ابن وثاب وأبو جعفر وعيسى); ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]

## 94:6/q13 Is the ease clause repeated in Ibn Masʿūd's reading?  [إِنَّ مَعَ ٱلْعُسْرِ يُسْرًۭا · readings]
turns on: Whether the reported single occurrence is accepted as evidence despite its isolated transmission.
- 94:6/q13/p1 His attributed codex or reading has the clause only once. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Thalabī, al-Kashshāf, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Burhān report it. Al-Ālūsī observes that the maxim of two eases is still transmitted from Ibn Masʿūd; al-Kashshāf allows one magnified ease to cover both realms. [8 notes: FARRA(عبد الله); THALABI(قراءة عبد الله); JISHUMI(ابن مسعود); KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA(ابن مسعود); BURHAN; ALUSI(ابن مسعود)]
- 94:6/q13/p2 The isolated report of a single clause cannot be relied upon. — Jishumī explicitly rejects the Ibn Masʿūd attribution because it is an isolated report. [0 notes: JISHUMI(ابن مسعود)-]

## 94:6/q14 What did the Prophet reportedly say or do when this promise came?  [* · reports]

- 94:6/q14/p1 He joyfully announced that ease had arrived and one hardship would not overcome two eases. — Al-Ḥasan's routes include revelation, the Prophet coming out smiling or laughing, and reciting both verses; Qatāda also relays the glad tidings. Ibn Kathīr calls al-Ḥasan's versions mursal, mentions routes through ʿAwf and Yūnus ibn ʿUbayd, and gives Qatāda's report without a connected route. Biqāʿī notes a weak connected route via Ibn Mardawayh. Other reports attribute the saying to Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd, ʿUmar, or early people rather than the Prophet. - 94:6/q14/p2 He said ease would pursue hardship even into a stone or burrow and drive it out. — Anas's version says the verse then descended; one version places it at Baqīʿ al-Farqad. ## 94:6/q15 What did ʿUmar say in connection with the verse?  [* · reports]
- 94:6/q15/p1 He reassured Abū ʿUbayda that a believer's distress is followed by relief and one hardship cannot defeat two eases. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites the Muwaṭṭaʾ letter; al-Qurṭubī adds Abū ʿUbayda's fear of Roman forces and 3:200. Biqāʿī reports conflicting direction of the letter in summaries but says Ibn Ḥajar deemed ʿUmar's route the soundest among these reports. Al-Ālūsī says this case does not settle all hardships; Elmalılı stresses relief within a believer's heart. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA(عمر); QURTUBI(عمر بن الخطاب); BIQAI(ابن حجر,عمر); BIQAI-FULL(عمر); ALUSI(زيد بن أسلم); ELMALILI(Zeyd b. Eslem، Ebû Ubeyde، Ömer b. Hattâb); IBNASHUR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب)]

## 94:6/q16 How does this promise follow the preceding favors and connect to the surah?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the immediate fā draws an inference from 94's favors, or the link is read across the neighboring surahs.
- 94:6/q16/p1 Past divine favors ground the promise of future relief. — Al-Rāzī links the fā to the Prophet's distress after recalling 94:1–2. Al-Nasafī says the earlier gifts prevent despair; Abū Ḥayyān says they imply victory. Bursawī and al-Ālūsī see the fā as causal or inferential, and al-Ālūsī names expanded breast, lifted burden and raised remembrance as matching relief. Ibn ʿĀshūr says ease cancels hardship's effect and God's past care will continue; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ adds inna and repetition against doubt. [15 notes: RAZI; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(الكشاف); ALUSI(بعض الأجلة,بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 94:6/q16/p2 The surah's favors are grace and signs of divine love. — Ibn al-Zubayr relates this surah to the preceding two: human potential diverges by response to God's call, and 32:13 underscores the appointed ends; he urges sincerity and gratitude. Biqāʿī says the Prophet's favors were freely given and show God had not forsaken him. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير)]
- 94:6/q16/p3 Sūrah 94 continues Sūrah 93's promise and develops it toward mission success. — Asad says it followed al-Ḍuḥā closely and speaks to followers through the Prophet. Iṣlāḥī contrasts earlier shelter and external favors with blessings after Islam spread, then a bright future becoming realized. The Study Quran contrasts external needs in 93 with spiritual relief in 94 and its final command to exert oneself and turn to God. [6 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 94:6/q17 What is reported about this surah's revelation and its relation to Sūrah 93?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the two surahs were revealed and recited as separate units, and which chronology is accepted.
- 94:6/q17/p1 Most call it Meccan and place it just after Sūrah 93. — The Study Quran reports the majority chronology; Asad says revelation followed al-Ḍuḥā almost immediately. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(most commentators); ASAD-NOTES]
- 94:6/q17/p2 Ibn ʿAbbās calls it Medinan, or some assign only verses 5–6 to Medina. — The Study Quran records these minority chronology reports. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(most commentators)]
- 94:6/q17/p3 Some treated Sūrahs 93 and 94 as one and recited them without a second basmala. — Asad names Ṭāwūs ibn Kaysān and ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz, citing al-Rāzī; the Study Quran similarly reports Companion practice in one prayer cycle. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Ta'us ibn Kaysan and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz); STUDYQURAN]

## 94:6/q18 What inward reading is given to the hardship and ease?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether ease is future recompense and reliance or present unveiling within spiritual trial.
- 94:6/q18/p1 Worldly restraint or confinement becomes reliance on God and Paradise. — Al-Warrāq pairs worldly effort with Paradise; al-Jawzajānī pairs abstaining from forbidden and doubtful things with the dignity of reliance; al-Qāsim contrasts the fortunate's move from worldly prison to afterlife delight with the wretched's continuing constraint. [5 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر الوراق,الجوزجاني,القاسم); THALABI(أبو بكر الوراق,القاسم)]
- 94:6/q18/p2 Hardship and ease are stages of spiritual unveiling. — Al-Qāshānī's two veils, by creation from God and by God from creation, give way to disclosure through sainthood and expansion through prophethood. Ibn al-ʿArabī, cited by Bursawī, says hardship polishes the elect's hearts and mercy precedes wrath. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(الشيخ الأكبر)]
