# 80:8: 20 questions

## 80:8/q01 Who is the person who comes?  [مَن · referent]
- 80:8/q01/p1 Ibn Umm Maktum, the blind seeker. — Muqatil identifies him as Ibn Umm Maktum, and Tabrisi and Ibn Ashur likewise connect him to the opening account. Tabrisi's reported genealogy calls him Abdullah ibn Shurayh ibn Malik ibn Rabiʿa al-Fihri; Biqai adds that he was the Prophet's muezzin. [10 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); DURR-FULL(عائشة)]

## 80:8/q02 Who is addressed and rebuked by the passage?  [جَآءَكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the unnamed subject can be identified with the Prophet, given the narrative reports and al-Murtada's character argument.
- 80:8/q02/p1 The Prophet is the addressee and recipient of the rebuke. — Muqatil, Qushayri, Jishumi, Biqai and the reported occasion all describe the Prophet turning from Ibn Umm Maktum while addressing others; Ibn Ashur says this confirms the opening account. [11 notes: MUQATIL; QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABRISI(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; DURR-FULL(عائشة); TABRISI(المرتضى علم الهدى)-]
- 80:8/q02/p2 The rebuked subject is not the Prophet. — Al-Murtada, reported by Tabrisi, argues that the text does not name the Prophet and that frowning at a believing seeker or preferring rich people over the poor conflicts with his noble character. [1 notes: TABRISI(المرتضى علم الهدى)]

## 80:8/q03 Why was attention given to the Quraysh leaders?  [جَآءَكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the preference reflected hope for conversions or regard for wealth; Wahidi challenges the latter from the verse's actual description.
- 80:8/q03/p1 The Prophet hoped their conversion would benefit them and their people. — Biqai says the Prophet addressed a notable in hope of saving him and his kin; Tabrisi's report says he feared the leaders might scorn a following of blind people and slaves. Jishumi says he was trying to convert Meccan leaders. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة)]
- 80:8/q03/p2 It was not for their wealth. — Wahidi rejects a wealth motive because the verse describes the other man by striving and fear, not poverty. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 80:8/q03/p3 It was for their wealth. — Wahidi reports this suggestion only to reject it; his objection is that the verse does not call the seeker poor. [0 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT-]

## 80:8/q04 How do the clauses about coming and striving work grammatically?  [جَآءَكَ يَسْعَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the following fear clause is circumstantial or predicative, and, if circumstantial, how it attaches.
- 80:8/q04/p1 The striving clause is circumstantial to the subject of 'came'; the following fear clause is another circumstance. — Nahhas says the verbal clauses of verses 8 and 9 can be circumstantial accusatives. Samin specifies that 'while he fears' is a circumstance of the subject of 'striving', nested within the first circumstance; he calls treating it as a second coordinated circumstance weak. [2 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 80:8/q04/p2 The clause in verse 9 can instead be another predicate. — Nahhas offers this as an alternative to treating both verbal clauses as circumstantial. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 80:8/q05 What does 'yasʿā' mean here?  [يَسْعَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether saʿy denotes actual brisk movement, active pursuit of religious good, or uses movement to express that pursuit.
- 80:8/q05/p1 He eagerly pursues religious good, guidance, or knowledge. — Ibn Abbas, al-Kalbi and Muqatil, as reported by Wahidi, explain it as seeking God's pleasure and good deeds; Ibn Jawzi reports doing good as one of two views. Kashshaf, Razi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi and Alusi describe pursuit of good, religion, guidance or knowledge. Razi and Islahi cite 62:9; Islahi says zeal need not mean running, likening it to thirst for a fountain. Tabatabai relates the literal quick movement to remembrance, purification and learning; Ibn Kathir says he sought guidance. [16 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، والكلبي، ومقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QUSHAYRI]
- 80:8/q05/p2 He walks or moves briskly toward the Prophet, expressing eagerness to meet him. — Damghani gives quick movement and cites 36:20, 20:20 and 28:20. Ibn Jawzi reports walking as one view; Ibn Atiyya allows walking as well as striving in affairs and religious practice. Ibn Ashur calls it brisk walking used for eagerness and sets it against the self-sufficient man's aversion; Tabatabai gives quick walking as its literal sense. Biqai depicts an urgent approach through the crowd. [8 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 80:8/q06 What does the verbal picture of the man's striving emphasize?  [يَسْعَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 80:8/q06/p1 His urgency and eagerness strengthen his claim to the Prophet's attention. — Biqai says the verbal form depicts someone coming urgently for good; pressing through a crowd to reach the Prophet gives him a strong claim. Ibn Ashur says brisk walking conveys eagerness to meet, in contrast to the self-sufficient man's disdain. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 80:8/q07 How does this clause contrast with the self-sufficient person of verse 5?  [* · links]
- 80:8/q07/p1 It completes an opposition: the sincere man comes eagerly and fears God, while the other avoids the call and lacks fear. — Ibn Ashur says the coordination completes the division into opposites and reinforces the opening account (80:1–2, 80:5). Biqai says the wealthy man's failure to come and fear is implied by the seeker's arrival and fear; the contrast cautions against slighting the latter. Islahi contrasts worldly absorption with fear of meeting God. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 80:8/q08 What is left implicit in the contrast between the two men?  [* · rhetoric]
- 80:8/q08/p1 It uses ihtibāk: the rich man's opposite is poor, and the seeker's coming and fear imply the other's failure to come and fear. — Biqai gives this reciprocal ellipsis, as does Saʿdi al-Mufti in Bursevi. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(سعدي المفتي)]

## 80:8/q09 What event prompted this admonition?  [* · reports]
- 80:8/q09/p1 Ibn Umm Maktum interrupted the Prophet while he addressed Quraysh leaders; the Prophet turned back to them and the rebuke was revealed. — Jishumi reports al-Hasan and a group; Tabrisi reports the seeker repeatedly asked to be taught and lists ʿUtba ibn Rabiʿa, Abu Jahl, al-ʿAbbas, Ubayy and Umayya ibn Khalaf. Aisha's account in Durr names Abu Jahl and ʿUtba among the leaders and quotes verses 5–10. Biqai says the Prophet hoped a Quraysh notable's conversion would save him and his kin. The reports vary in which leaders they name. [6 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABRISI(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عائشة)]

## 80:8/q10 How did the Prophet treat Ibn Umm Maktum afterward?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the reports describe his treatment of Ibn Umm Maktum specifically or a broader change toward poor people.
- 80:8/q10/p1 He welcomed and honored him, asked about his needs, and appointed him deputy over Medina twice. — Muqatil and Tabrisi give the two appointments. Jishumi and Tabrisi report that the Prophet greeted him as the man over whom his Lord reproached him and asked what he needed. [3 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABRISI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 80:8/q10/p2 Thereafter he never frowned at a poor person or sought out a rich person, and the poor were honored in his gathering. — Bursevi reports this broader account of his later conduct. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 80:8/q11 What later report concerns Ibn Umm Maktum?  [* · reports]
- 80:8/q11/p1 Anas saw him at al-Qadisiyya wearing armor and carrying a black banner. — Jishumi reports Anas's account; Tabrisi reports the same observation from Anas ibn Malik, calling it a black standard. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أنس); TABRISI(أنس بن مالك)]

## 80:8/q12 What does a report say this rebuke shows about revelation?  [* · reports]
- 80:8/q12/p1 If the Messenger had concealed revelation, he would have concealed this passage. — Jishumi reports this saying from Ibn Zayd. [1 notes: JISHUMI(ابن زيد)]

## 80:8/q13 What conduct does the episode teach toward sincere seekers?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: How far the rebuke is generalized: priority for the receptive seeker, equal warning for all, or the basis of human worth.
- 80:8/q13/p1 Give the sincere weak seeker attention and priority over prominent people who lack goodness. — Muqatil describes the admonition to welcome him. Ibn Atiyya applies it to scholars; Bursevi extends it to scholars, judges and rulers. Qushayri calls it a reproach on behalf of the poor. Biqai invokes 18:28 and says visible readiness exceeds speculative benefit from elites; Tabatabai describes neglect of the seeker as the rebuked pattern. Islahi contrasts the indifferent and the receptive. [11 notes: MUQATIL; QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 80:8/q13/p2 Address warning to everyone without social preference; God guides whom He wills. — Ibn Kathir names eminent and weak, rich and poor, masters and slaves, men and women, adults and children. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 80:8/q13/p3 Spiritual worth rests on faith and inner state, not wealth or outward rank. — Bursevi says faith ennobles the socially lowly while unbelief diminishes the esteemed. A reported view honors detached poverty; a hadith warns that favoring the rich by wronging the poor destroys a third of one's religion. Bursevi explains the hadith's taḥāmala as either enduring hardship or treating another unjustly and cites the divine saying about failing to feed or give drink to the hungry and thirsty. Ibn al-Zubayr and the report of a dusty overlooked person likewise stress hidden rank. [7 notes: BURSEVI(الحديث,الله تعالى,بعضهم); BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 80:8/q14 Does the episode's lesson extend beyond its original people?  [* · interpretation]
- 80:8/q14/p1 Yes; it applies to anyone with the same qualities. — Ibn Atiyya identifies Quraysh's disbelievers and Abdullah ibn Umm Maktum as the immediate parties, then extends the lesson to everyone sharing their qualities. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 80:8/q15 What inward lesson is drawn from the treatment of the Quraysh chiefs?  [* · inward]
- 80:8/q15/p1 The Prophet honored them as manifestations of divine majesty; a higher station sees no human distinction beneath God's absolute reality. — Bursevi reports this explanation from some great Sufis. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الأكابر)]

## 80:8/q16 How does this episode connect with other Quranic instructions about seekers?  [* · links]
- 80:8/q16/p1 It exemplifies the call to stay with those who seek God and the charge to warn those who fear. — Biqai cites 18:28 for keeping company with those who call on God and also 39:65, 38:88 and 31:18 among related admonitions; he says 18:28 follows the pattern of Ibn Umm Maktum's case. Bursevi calls remaining with such people the higher instruction in 18:28. Biqai applies the description of the warner for those who fear in 79:45 to Ibn Umm Maktum. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 80:8/q17 How does the episode fit this surah's larger argument?  [* · links]
- 80:8/q17/p1 The surah's call to warn the fearful and its proofs of resurrection address receptive hearts. — Biqai says human creation and the food cycle prove divine power and expose the strangeness of rejection; he links the call to 79:45. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 80:8/q18 How is this surah related to al-Naziʿat?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the link is framed as a shared call concerning the humble or as the paired surahs' broader thematic and formal relationship.
- 80:8/q18/p1 It continues the earlier call to heed and fear, especially honoring humble people overlooked by the world. — Ibn al-Zubayr, reported by Biqai, cites 79:26 and 79:45. The saying of the disheveled person whose oath God would fulfill illustrates their hidden rank. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 80:8/q18/p2 The paired surahs share theme, style and reasoning, while this surah changes the sequence of topics. — Islahi cites 79:45 and says the changed order gives ʿAbasa its distinct majesty. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 80:8/q19 What does the shift to direct address imply?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the second-person turn conveys honor or intensifies the rebuke.
- 80:8/q19/p1 It is a stronger, unsoftened rebuke. — Tabatabai rejects an honorific account of the shift from third to second person: the following direct address intensifies reproach without conciliatory intimacy. [1 notes: TABATABAI+]
- 80:8/q19/p2 It is honorific. — Tabatabai mentions this proposed reading only to reject it on the grounds that the ensuing address is direct and severe. [0 notes: TABATABAI-]

## 80:8/q20 What does the dropped ta in the later verb convey?  [* · rhetoric]
- 80:8/q20/p1 The Prophet's distraction was slight. — Biqai says the omitted ta marks the small degree of his distraction. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
