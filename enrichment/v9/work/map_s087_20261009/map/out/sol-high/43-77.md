# 43:77: 18 questions

## 43:77/q01 Who is Mālik?  [يَٰمَٰلِكُ · referent]
turns on: Literal keeper or reported inward identification.
- 43:77/q01/p1 He is the angelic keeper or chief attendant of Hell. — Muqātil, Ibn ʿAbbās, and the exegetes identify him. Al-Qurṭubī says he was created for wrath and roars until the Fire devours itself. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls him the appointed angel; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites Sunni and Shia reports and calls him its king. [12 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; ITQAN; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; NASAFI-FULL]
- 43:77/q01/p2 In an inward reading he is bodily nature, dark matter, or the universal animal soul governing bodies. — Al-Qāshānī reports this alternative along with a maxim about contentment with God's decree. [1 notes: QASHANI(قيل)]

## 43:77/q02 Why is Hell's keeper called Mālik?  [يَٰمَٰلِكُ · inward]
- 43:77/q02/p1 He serves those who owned and preferred the worldly life. — Al-Qāshānī cites 79:37–39. He contrasts Riḍwān, keeper of the garden, associated with God's pleasure and those pleased with Him. Fire agrees with Mālik's substance and is a garden to him but torments those whose essences conflict with it. [3 notes: QASHANI]

## 43:77/q03 Why do the condemned call Mālik rather than God?  [وَنَادَوْا۟ يَٰمَٰلِكُ · interpretation]
turns on: Mediation, veiling, or estrangement.
- 43:77/q03/p1 They ask Hell's keeper to carry their plea to God. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls Mālik their intended intercessor. Al-ʿĀlūsī says 'your Lord' appeals for help rather than rejecting God; Iṣlāḥī calls him the jailer. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; MAWARDI-FULL]
- 43:77/q03/p2 They cannot reach God because they are veiled from Him. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 83:15 and 23:108; al-Qāshānī adds that their wishes were devoted to Mālik. [2 notes: TABATABAI; QASHANI]
- 43:77/q03/p3 Estrangement keeps their speech from an answer. — Al-Qushayrī says a true address to Mālik could have brought them nearer an answer. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 43:77/q04 What leads to their call, and how does it relate to other pleas from Hell?  [وَنَادَوْا۟ · links]
turns on: Different reported preceding conditions and sequences.
- 43:77/q04/p1 They call in despair as punishment becomes unbearable. — Al-Nasafī says they have despaired of respite; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Bursawī call it a cry of suffering. An ungraded report in al-ʿĀlūsī says hunger equals their other torment before the call. [4 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض الآثار)]
- 43:77/q04/p2 They call after seeking lesser relief from Hell's keepers and being refused. — Al-Māwardī says they had tasted the punishment. Al-Qurazī describes a request for one day's relief in 40:49–50, refused before they call Mālik in the middle of Hell. Al-Qurṭubī reports an initial cry to the keepers; Kuran Yolu calls this the last request. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 43:77/q04/p3 The call is a plea for help like the Fire's inhabitants asking for water. — Al-Dāmaghānī cites 7:50 for nidāʾ in this sense. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]
- 43:77/q04/p4 A prophetic report has them turn to God after Mālik does not answer. — Al-Baghawī reports forty silent years, a further plea to God, silence twice the world's span, then an order to be quiet and make donkey-like gasps. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 43:77/q05 How is Mālik's name read in the address?  [يَٰمَٰلِكُ · readings]
turns on: Full codex form, truncation grammar, and transmission status.
- 43:77/q05/p1 Some read shortened yā Māl, usually with kasra, by vocative truncation. — Reported from Ibn Masʿūd, ʿAlī, Ibn Yaʿmar, Yaḥyā, Ibn Waththāb, al-Aʿmash, and through Abū al-Dardāʾ from the Prophet. Al-Naḥḥās says Arabic often shortens Mālik and ʿĀmir and cites Ḥārith as Ḥār; he calls yā Māl eloquent. The lām can anticipate the lost ending. Al-Zajjāj rejects its conflict with the codex; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it is not canonical. [18 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن مسعود); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(بعض المتقدمين); NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود ويحيى والأعمش); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(علي بن أبي طالب وابن مسعود وابن يعمر); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الدرداء وابن مسعود,علي وابن مسعود); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(ابن مسعود); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(علي بن أبي طالب وعبد الله وابن وثاب والأعمش); BURHAN(بعضهم); ITQAN(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(علي وابن مسعود وابن وثاب والأعمش); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية,ابن مسعود); NAHHAS+; ZAJJAJ-; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج)-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 43:77/q05/p2 The full yā Mālik retains the kāf and is the canonical reading. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it the majority form. Yaʿlā's pulpit report is transmitted by ʿAbd al-Razzāq, al-Qurṭubī, al-Durr, and Ibn ʿĀshūr; al-Durr also reports ʿAlī and Ibn Masʿūd. Ibn ʿĀshūr says only this form is mutawātir, whereas the shortened form is singly transmitted and not Qur'an. [11 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,علي,يعلى بن أمية); IBNASHUR-FULL(يعلى بن أمية); IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 43:77/q05/p3 Abū al-Sawwār or al-Sarrār reads yā Māl with final ḍamma. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-ʿĀlūsī say this dialect treats the shortened remainder as an independent name rather than anticipating the omitted ending. Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī mention this grammatical option. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو السرار الغنوي); SAMIN-DURR(أبو السوار الغنوي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو السوار); NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 43:77/q05/p4 Mujāhid says Ibn Masʿūd's yā Māl clarified the name or reading. — Al-Naḥḥās says they did not understand yā Mālik until ʿAbd Allāh's reading. Al-Qurṭubī compares Mujāhid's learning that zukhruf was a house of gold and says the codex resolved Mālik versus Malak; Abū Bakr al-Anbārī rejects this disconnected report as unsuitable as a prophetic narration. [2 notes: NAHHAS(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو بكر الأنباري)-]

## 43:77/q06 What does the shortened call convey?  [يَٰمَٰلِكُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Involuntary broken speech or implausible wordplay.
- 43:77/q06/p1 Distress, weakness, and humiliation prevent them finishing the name. — Al-Bayḍāwī suggests weakness. Ibn Jinnī through al-ʿĀlūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr says collapsed strength and humbled souls make truncation apt; al-ʿĀlūsī rejects deliberate artistry. Al-Burhān and al-Itqān report this explanation. [7 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURHAN(بعضهم); ITQAN(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جني,ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن جني)]
- 43:77/q06/p2 Hell's people have more pressing concerns than deliberate name shortening. — Ibn ʿAbbās objects in al-Nasafī, al-ʿĀlūsī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr; a Salaf objection appears in al-Burhān and al-Itqān. Al-ʿĀlūsī replies that incapacity forces the abbreviation. [5 notes: NASAFI-FULL(ابن مسعود); BURHAN(بعضهم); ITQAN(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 43:77/q07 How does the lām in li-yaqḍi function?  [لِيَقْضِ · grammar]
- 43:77/q07/p1 It makes a supplicatory request or wish. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the imperative a plea; Abū Ḥayyān says request or wish. Al-Zarkashī lists this among forms of the lām of command, which may also express self-instruction, threat, or declaration. Ibn ʿĀshūr explains an absent addressee addressed for conveyance, or a present one treated as absent for reverence. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:77/q08 What does yaqḍi ʿalaynā mean?  [لِيَقْضِ عَلَيْنَا · meaning]
- 43:77/q08/p1 It means to kill or put an end to their lives. — Al-Dāmaghānī cites Pharaoh's people's request and Moses' fatal blow; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Nasafī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite Moses killing a man in 28:15. Al-Bursawī calls qaḍā ʿalayhi an idiom for killing; Elmalılı adds finishing their case. [8 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 43:77/q09 What do they ask their Lord to do, and why?  [لِيَقْضِ عَلَيْنَا رَبُّكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Expressed request for death or inward analysis of what ceasing means.
- 43:77/q09/p1 They ask for death or annihilation to escape and rest from torment. — Muqātil, al-Māwardī, al-Kalbī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and others explain relief from pain. Abū Ḥayyān says one death would prevent recurrence; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it ends awareness. The Abū al-Dardāʾ prophetic report gives this plea. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says they carry their worldly belief that death annihilates, though they have already died and learned it is transition. Study Quran compares 78:40 and 25:13. [25 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 43:77/q09/p2 Inwardly, they wish painful forms or sensation to cease. — Al-Qāshānī says they may wish to lose capacity and innate disposition, ending painful forms, or bodily sensation amid torment. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 43:77/q10 Why do they say 'your Lord'?  [رَبُّكَ · rhetoric]
- 43:77/q10/p1 They appeal to Mālik for help and mediation. — Al-ʿĀlūsī says the possessive is no rejection of God; Ibn ʿĀshūr says Mālik conveys their plea. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:77/q11 How can their call coexist with the despair described nearby?  [وَنَادَوْا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Timing of the call and the syntax of its link with 43:75.
- 43:77/q11/p1 A desperate wail for death can continue despite despair. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Bursawī describe a cry from severe torment. Abū Ḥayyān cites al-Zamakhsharī's examples of confused pleas despite knowing the outcome, including 23:107 and this verse. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 43:77/q11/p2 Silence and calling occur at different times over prolonged punishment. — Abū Ḥayyān says the states do not conflict. Al-ʿĀlūsī notes an objection: alternation seems ill suited to the continuous clause 'they are despairing.' [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 43:77/q11/p3 The wāw need not mark sequence, so calling may precede despair. — Al-ʿĀlūsī reports this reply but notes that the circumstantial clause still attaches to their abiding state. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 43:77/q11/p4 The call is circumstantial to despair or coordinated with it. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the first construal puts despair after call and answer; on coordination, past tense presents a future event as certain, as in 27:87 and 39:68. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:77/q12 What does Mālik's answer mean?  [قَالَ إِنَّكُم مَّٰكِثُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Plain finality or finality sharpened by a mocking suggestion.
- 43:77/q12/p1 They remain in Hell's punishment without death, respite, or release. — Al-Māwardī specifies continued life. Muqātil, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and others gloss lasting torment; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Bursawī, al-ʿĀlūsī, Elmalılı, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Iṣlāḥī rule out death or exit. Al-Bursawī defines mākith as staying while awaiting and mentions donkey-like wails. Iṣlāḥī calls death's withholding the greatest deprivation. Study Quran connects 35:36. [20 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,محمد بن كعب القرظي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK,JJ, Q); MUQATIL]
- 43:77/q12/p2 'Remaining' may mockingly suggest a finite stay or waiting although their stay has no end. — Al-ʿĀlūsī reports this from unnamed authorities; a delayed reply heightens the effect. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة)]

## 43:77/q13 What duration of silence is reported before Mālik answers?  [قَالَ · reports]
turns on: The reports give differing intervals; one places a millennium before the call.
- 43:77/q13/p1 One thousand years. — Reported from Ibn ʿAbbās by ʿAbd al-Razzāq, al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Durr, al-ʿĀlūsī, and Elmalılı; al-Baghawī adopts it. Al-Qurṭubī has al-Aʿmash hear the same through al-Tirmidhī. Ibn Abī Ḥātim places a thousand years before the call, not after it. Elmalılı takes figures as a great span. [15 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الأعمش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابنعباس); ELMALILI(İbn Abbas); STUDYQURAN(JJ, Q); BAGHAWI-FULL+]
- 43:77/q13/p2 Forty years. — Muqātil and al-Kalbī report it; al-Māwardī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursawī, al-ʿĀlūsī, and Elmalılı cite it too. Al-Baghawī's prophetic report places a further petition to God after this wait. Elmalılı treats figures as figurative. [11 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن عمر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عبد الله بن عمرو ومقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(غير مسمى); ELMALILI(İbn Amr)]
- 43:77/q13/p3 One hundred years. — Reported from Nawf, Mujāhid with Nawf, and Kaʿb; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursawī, al-ʿĀlūsī, and Elmalılı list it. Elmalılı treats numbers as hyperbole. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(نوف); IBNATIYYA-FULL(نوف); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(كعب); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد ونوف البكالي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(نوف); ELMALILI(bazıları)]
- 43:77/q13/p4 Eighty years. — Reported by al-Suddī and al-Qurazī, and anonymously elsewhere. Al-Wāḥidī makes each day a thousand human years; al-Qurṭubī defines a year as 360 days and a month as thirty days. Abū Ḥayyān and al-ʿĀlūsī also list it. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(القرظي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(غير مسمى)]
- 43:77/q13/p5 Thirty years. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this from Anas. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أنس)]
- 43:77/q13/p6 Forty days. — Al-Bursawī attributes it to al-Tibyān. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 43:77/q13/p7 An unspecified delay until God orders Mālik to answer. — Muqātil reports this through Ibn al-Jawzī. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل)]

## 43:77/q14 What purpose or effect is ascribed to the delayed answer?  [قَالَ · interpretation]
turns on: Effect of delay or command that ends it.
- 43:77/q14/p1 It increases humiliation, grief, or torment. — Al-Māwardī says humiliation; Ibn al-Jawzī reports disgrace; al-Bursawī says grief; al-ʿĀlūsī says it may sharpen the mockery in 'remaining.' [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة)]
- 43:77/q14/p2 Mālik replies only when God commands it. — Muqātil reports this through Ibn al-Jawzī, and in his own account says it follows forty years. [2 notes: MUQATIL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل)]

## 43:77/q15 What determines how long the condemned remain?  [مَّٰكِثُونَ · inward]
turns on: Conditional inward account or eternal punishment of deniers.
- 43:77/q15/p1 If sound belief and capacity persist, rooted dispositions and sins set the duration; otherwise it is eternal. — Al-Qāshānī distinguishes duration from eternity and sinner from primordial wretch. Thus 43:74 can describe a figuratively long stay or eternal rejection. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 43:77/q15/p2 Associating partners and denying God and the afterlife bring eternal punishment. — Kuran Yolu says Mālik's answer fulfills the prophets' worldly warning. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 43:77/q16 What moral explanation is given for their remaining?  [مَّٰكِثُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Rejected counsel or denial of faith and warning.
- 43:77/q16/p1 They refused counsel and hated the truth. — Al-Qushayrī repeats this and says estrangement delayed an answer. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 43:77/q16/p2 They denied God, the afterlife, and the prophets' warning. — Kuran Yolu relates this to their eternal punishment. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 43:77/q17 What broader figurative-usage example appears in these notes?  [* · rhetoric]
- 43:77/q17/p1 Familiar figurative 'hand' can mean power with a more specific and honorific force. — Al-Zarkashī says neither early believers nor disbelievers objected to 'hand' as a bodily limb, implying the figurative usage was familiar. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 43:77/q18 How does this scene fit the surrounding passage and sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Whole closing section or immediate link with 43:75.
- 43:77/q18/p1 It belongs to the closing contrast between believers' and deniers' fates. — Iṣlāḥī says the close presents their fates, reassures the Prophet against mockers, then restates tawḥīd and rejects intercession as rescue. Study Quran notes the extended contrast; Iṣlāḥī says the sūra is a harmonious unit serving one theme. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 43:77/q18/p2 Mālik's refusal completes the portrayal of despair in 43:75. — Iṣlāḥī says death, their last support, is withheld; Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the call's grammatical relation to despair. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
