# 7:25: 15 questions

## 7:25/q01 Why is قال repeated without a conjunction?  [قَالَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the second utterance explains the first, begins a new or emphasized speech, reflects a discourse shift, follows omitted speech, or simply follows accepted narrative usage.
- 7:25/q01/p1 The new sentence explains the preceding statement, so Arabic leaves it uncoordinated. — Abū Ḥayyān says successive sentences normally take coordination unless the second interprets the first. He identifies the earlier statement as the earthly settlement and provision of 7:24. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 7:25/q01/p2 The repeated قال marks a fresh utterance or emphasizes the earthly-life decree. — Al-Ālūsī gives renewed speech or emphasis. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Abū al-Suʿūd's emphasis explanation. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو السعود)]
- 7:25/q01/p3 The absence of a conjunction is permissible Arabic narration, though it departs from expected coordination. — Al-Qurṭubī says adding wāw would also be grammatical, illustrating this with Zayd speaking to ʿAmr. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes that the same speaker and purpose would lead one to expect coordination; he says many leading exegetes, including al-Zamakhsharī, leave the absence unexplained, perhaps as a storytelling convention. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(جمهور الحذاق من المفسرين)]
- 7:25/q01/p4 A change in the kind of discourse can account for repeated قال. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Ibn ʿArafa's explanation of the analogous 7:140 after 7:138: first comes rejection of idolatry, then an argument against it. Al-Khafājī gives the same explanation without attribution. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عرفة التونسي)]
- 7:25/q01/p5 Unreported intervening speech separates the two utterances. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this suggestion from Abū al-Suʿūd, who compares 15:56–57 and 17:61–62. Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects the inference because a report of speech need not be exhaustive or continuous. [0 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو السعود)-]

## 7:25/q02 Who receives this decree?  [قَالَ · referent]
- 7:25/q02/p1 Adam, his wife, and their descendants, extending the address to humanity. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls this probable because a new قال introduces the decree. Al-Biqāʿī and Elmalılı discuss its force for human beings or the human species generally. [3 notes: TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 7:25/q03 What do فيها and منها refer to?  [فِيهَا وَمِنْهَا · referent]
- 7:25/q03/p1 They refer to the earth of this world, already named as humanity's abode in 7:24. — Ibn ʿAbbās specifies this world's earth for living. Al-Wāḥidī derives the pronoun from the preceding earthly abode in 7:24. Ibn al-Jawzī identifies the referent of فيها as earth. Al-Qurṭubī extends the earth referent to all the pronouns. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 7:25/q04 What is meant by living فيها?  [فِيهَا تَحْيَوْنَ · interpretation]
- 7:25/q04/p1 People live their worldly lives on earth until death, within the earthly settlement God decreed. — Muqātil and al-Ṭabarī specify the span of each lifespan. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the decree irrevocable. Abū Ḥayyān connects it to 7:24's settlement and provision. Al-Biqāʿī adds outward and inward life of bodies and souls, first and again on this earth, excluding another earthly realm. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes each person or community's lifespan here from humanity's wider span of earthly provision, ending at extinction or resurrection. [18 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:25/q05 What is meant by dying فيها?  [وَفِيهَا تَمُوتُونَ · interpretation]
- 7:25/q05/p1 People die on earth and are buried in it when their earthly lives end. — Muqātil ties death to the end of one's appointed lifespan. Al-Kalbī, al-Baghawī, Ibn Kathīr, and Bursawī explicitly mention burial. Al-Biqāʿī says death concerns the person as a whole but the soul retains a connection to the body, allowing answers to grave angels and bodily pleasure or pain. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:25/q06 What relation does death have to life?  [تَحْيَوْنَ تَمُوتُونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether death is understood as nonbeing or as a contrary to life.
- 7:25/q06/p1 Life and death are contraries. — Al-Burhān calls this the sounder account. [1 notes: BURHAN+]
- 7:25/q06/p2 If death is nonbeing, death and life are privation and possession. — Al-Burhān states this conditional account while preferring the contrary account. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 7:25/q07 Why does life precede death in this verse?  [تَحْيَوْنَ تَمُوتُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The addressees' lived sequence versus the meanings served by a death-first order in other contexts.
- 7:25/q07/p1 The addressees, whether Adam and Eve or living people generally, live before they die. — Al-Burhān gives both scopes of address. He explicitly raises this ordering question; his discussion of the contrary sequence in the command to Mary has no answer in the excerpt. [2 notes: BURHAN]
- 7:25/q07/p2 A death-first sequence elsewhere can emphasize prior nonexistence or the world's transient destination. — Al-Burhān says death can be prior as nonexistence or the final destination of worldly life; such ordering can disparage the transient world and direct desire beyond death, citing 23:15. This explains the alternate order rather than replacing the life-first explanation here. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 7:25/q08 How is تُخْرَجُونَ read, and how does the reading express agency?  [تُخْرَجُونَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the verb is active with fatḥa on the prefix and ḍamma on rāʾ, or passive with ḍamma on the prefix and fatḥa on rāʾ; reader assignments and related passages provide the evidence.
- 7:25/q08/p1 The active form means 'you come out,' making the people the grammatical subject. — Al-Fārisī cites the agreed active 30:25, the people as subject of ينسلون in 36:51, and 7:29's return imagery. Al-Jishumī says the action is attributed to people; QIRAAT-ER takes this as autonomous emergence rather than divine extraction. Reports assign the active here to Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī, also Khalaf or Kūfans other than ʿĀṣim; Ibn ʿĀmir, Ibn Dhakwān, and Yaʿqūb are reported to agree here. Reports differ in detail about corresponding forms in al-Rūm, al-Zukhruf, and al-Jāthiya: al-Fārisī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and Ibn al-Jazarī specify differing scopes and exceptions, including 30:19 or 30:25, 43:11, 45:35, and 70:43. [19 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن عامر,حمزة والكسائي); JISHUMI(ابن عامر,حمزة والكسائي,يعقوب); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي ويعقوب والآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عامر,حمزة والكسائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عامر,حمزة، الكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وابن ذكوان); NASAFI-FULL(حمزة وعلى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخوان وابن ذكوان); SAMIN-DURR(الأخوان وابن ذكوان); NASHR(حمزة، والكسائي، وخلف); ALUSI-FULL(أهل الكوفة غير عاصم); QIRAAT-ER]
- 7:25/q08/p2 The passive form means 'you are brought out,' leaving the grammatical agent unnamed. — Al-Fārisī supports it with مخرجون in 23:35 and God's bringing out the dead in 7:57. Al-Jishumī contrasts its unnamed agent with the active form. Reports assign it here to Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr, Nāfiʿ, ʿĀṣim, or the remaining Seven; al-Samīn says both readings are clear. Reports give related passive patterns and exceptions at 30:19, 30:25, 43:11, 45:35, and 70:43; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ibn al-Jawzī call the active forms at 30:25 and 70:43 agreed exceptions. [9 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو ونافع وعاصم); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي ويعقوب والآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير ونافع وعاصم وأبو عمرو); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير، عاصم، أبو عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(باقي السبعة); SAMIN-DURR(الباقون)]

## 7:25/q09 What does emergence from the earth entail?  [وَمِنْهَا تُخْرَجُونَ · interpretation]
- 7:25/q09/p1 People are raised from earth or its graves, alive for the resurrection, gathering, judgment, and recompense. — Muqātil and al-Ṭabarī place it on Resurrection Day; al-Ṭabarī explicitly says God brings and gathers people alive. Al-Kalbī and al-Baghawī specify graves. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it God's irrevocable decree and says He restores first creation. Al-Bayḍāwī says judgment; al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān say reward or punishment, the latter comparing 20:55. Ibn Kathīr says first and last are gathered and repaid for deeds. Al-Biqāʿī adds bodily and spiritual return from dust and restoration of rights as God's justice. Bursawī says Adam inferred eventual return to Paradise and was comforted. Elmalılı says this establishes the human species on earth; Iṣlāḥī says God then shows people their gains, losses, and success. [18 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:25/q10 From which earth are people raised, and where are they taken afterward?  [وَمِنْهَا تُخْرَجُونَ · interpretation]
- 7:25/q10/p1 They rise from this same earth; God then destroys it and moves them by a blast to al-Sāhira. — Al-Jubbāʾī cites 79:14 for the subsequent move to the land called al-Sāhira. [2 notes: TABRISI(الجبائي)]

## 7:25/q11 Does resurrection restore bodily existence after death?  [تَمُوتُونَ تُخْرَجُونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether souls retain bodily connection and return bodily, or leave bodies permanently for celestial existence.
- 7:25/q11/p1 Yes; body and soul remain connected in the grave and emerge together from the earth for requital. — Al-Biqāʿī says the dead answer grave angels and feel pleasure or pain, then return from dust as body and soul; restoration of rights manifests divine justice. He rejects the philosophers' denial of later bodily attachment. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 7:25/q11/p2 No; souls leave bodies for celestial beings or realms, without later bodily attachment or resurrection. — Al-Biqāʿī attributes this to transmigrationist philosophers and rejects it. [0 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الفلاسفة التناسخية)-]

## 7:25/q12 What is the inward reading of living, dying, and emerging?  [فِيهَا تَحْيَوْنَ وَفِيهَا تَمُوتُونَ وَمِنْهَا تُخْرَجُونَ · inward]
turns on: Whether the stages signify gnosis, ignorance, and decree, or the seeker's movement through love and spiritual discipline to reality.
- 7:25/q12/p1 One lives by gnosis, dies by ignorance, and emerges into prior decrees and governing judgments. — Al-Sulamī reports this from an unnamed interpreter. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 7:25/q12/p2 One lives in love and sincere, patient servitude, dies while seeking the Real through Sharīʿa and ṭarīqa, then emerges into reality. — Bursawī urges steadfast waiting for relief. He cites a Prophetic report that people die as they live and are raised as they die. His Persian verses commend striving for love and portray night prayer and dawn weeping as guidance through darkness. [4 notes: BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام)]

## 7:25/q13 How does the decree connect with the surrounding Adam narrative?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connection stressed is the preceding earthly settlement or the narrative's return to the afterlife.
- 7:25/q13/p1 It spells out the earlier earthly settlement and provision in 7:24. — Al-Wāḥidī links the pronoun to the prior earthly abode. Abū Ḥayyān says earthly life lasts until death. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes each person's or community's life and death from humanity's wider period of provision; he treats the new قال as introducing the decree. Elmalılı and Kur'an Yolu describe the establishment and spread of the human species on earth. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 7:25/q13/p2 Its resurrection promise closes Adam's story by returning to the afterlife and humanity's ultimate fate. — Al-Biqāʿī says the story ends where its afterlife message began. Study Quran describes 7:11–27 as Adam's creation, temptation, and expulsion and says the life–death–resurrection sequence recalls ultimate fate. Bursawī says Adam took comfort in an inferred return to Paradise. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; BURSEVI]

## 7:25/q14 What does Adam's fall in this account teach about sin, decree, and guidance?  [* · theology]
turns on: Which moral or theological implication is drawn from the fall: the timing of Adam's sin, warning under decree, need for guidance, or the emergence of accountable freedom.
- 7:25/q14/p1 Adam's grave sin occurred before his prophethood. — Al-Rāzī says the account, as explained in al-Baqara, implies grave sin and resolves the difficulty by placing it before prophethood. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 7:25/q14/p2 Even an honored person can lose ease after one sin; divine decree governs prophets and saints alike. — Bursawī reports al-Qushayrī's warning from Adam's lost garments and ease: many sins are more alarming. He also reports Ḥāfiẓ's couplet about longing for union while destiny binds the soul to separation, and himself states that decree governs all. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الحافظ,القشيري)]
- 7:25/q14/p3 Adam's temptation illustrates human weakness and the continuing need for prophetic guidance. — Asad connects appetite, vanity, and false self-interest to human susceptibility, citing the temptation and fall at 7:16, 7:18, 7:19, and 7:25. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 7:25/q14/p4 The move after the first sin marks humanity's rise to free moral accountability and conscious choice of good. — Kur'an Yolu says humans can err and repent; it describes the move as moral rise rather than moral decline. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 7:25/q15 What ancillary observations appear in these notes without an explicit application to 7:25?  [* · links]
- 7:25/q15/p1 The source excerpts include a lexical note on riyāshan and a separate note about an omitted vocative at 30:23. — Abū ʿUbayda equates riyāshan and rīshan and gives visible clothing, adornment, equipment including saddle trappings, and abundance or livelihood. Al-Samīn reports Makkī's point that omitting yā in the prayer at 30:23 exalts God and removes the imperative-like force of the vocative. Neither note states an application to the wording of 7:25. [2 notes: MAJAZ; SAMIN-DURR(مكي)]
