# 20:55: 20 questions

## 20:55/q01 What does “from it” refer to?  [مِنْهَا · referent]
- 20:55/q01/p1 The earth mentioned earlier, also described as dust. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī connect it with earth made a cradle in 20:53; al-Naḥḥās cites Abū Isḥāq and Adam's creation. Study Quran says dust can also mean earth and cites 43:11. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 20:55/q02 How were people created from the earth?  [مِنْهَا خَلَقْنَٰكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether earthly origin works through Adam, nutrition, grave soil joined to semen, or inward formation.
- 20:55/q02/p1 Through Adam's creation from earth, as humanity's father and origin. — Al-Ḥasan specifies Adam; al-Qushayrī adds descent from his loins; al-Rāzī cites 3:59 while noting sperm creation; al-Ālūsī says Adam's formation prototyped the species. Al-Bayḍāwī calls earth the original material of bodies. Some simply state earthly origin. Bursevī says all except Adam and Eve come from semen and relays Najmiyya's account of Azrael gathering soil across earth. [31 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج وغيره); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:55/q02/p2 Each person is formed from semen or an embryo joined to soil from the future burial place. — ʿAṭāʾ al-Khurāsānī describes an angel sprinkling that soil; Ibn Masʿūd adds recording lifespan, provision, and burial place, citing 3:6. Al-Qurṭubī notes Abū Nuʿaym's rare newborn report through trustworthy Abū ʿĀṣim and refers to 6:2. Versions vary between semen, embryo, newborn, and created body. [14 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; NAHHAS(غير أبي إسحاق); THALABI(عطاء الخراساني); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء الخراساني); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو نعيم,عطاء الخراساني); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عطاء الخراساني); DURR-FULL(عطاء الخراساني); ALUSI-FULL(عطاء الخرسانى)]
- 20:55/q02/p3 Human bodies come from food rooted in earth, which produces reproductive fluid. — Al-Rāzī traces food and reproductive fluid through plants, water, and soil, including animal food, without denying sperm creation. Abū Ḥayyān mentions earthly humors. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:55/q02/p4 Every human seed is itself created from soil. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī report this as an alternative suggested by the apparent Qurʾanic wording. [2 notes: NAHHAS(غير أبي إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 20:55/q02/p5 In an inward reading, earthly creation forms bodily organs with differing temperaments and faculties. — Al-Qāshānī reads earth through bodily temperaments and their manifested faculties. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:55/q03 What does “created” imply about making a human from matter?  [خَلَقْنَٰكُمْ · theology]
- 20:55/q03/p1 Creation from existing matter need not mean origination from absolute nothing. — Al-Rāzī says fāṭir alone does not prove creation from nothing: heaven came from smoke and people from clay; changing a substance's attribute is possible. Bursevī gives khalq as proportionate formation, origination without a model, or making one thing from another, citing 6:1. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 20:55/q04 What does returning people into the earth mean?  [وَفِيهَا نُعِيدُكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether return is bodily death and burial or inward discipline.
- 20:55/q04/p1 At death their bodies go into earth by burial or dispersal and may decay there. — Al-Ṭabarī says people become dust. Al-Rāzī calls graves earth's place for the dead and allows those raised to heaven to return later. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī describe bodily disintegration; Abū Ḥayyān includes dispersal on earth. Al-Ālūsī says this is general, since some bodies, including prophets', do not decay. Bursevī says they are buried where their soil was taken. [24 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:55/q04/p2 Inwardly, discipline returns each faculty to its place and effaces its self-attributes. — Al-Qāshānī says faculties cease to exceed bounds or act independently. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:55/q05 Why does the verse say “in it” for the return?  [وَفِيهَا · rhetoric]
- 20:55/q05/p1 The preposition emphasizes settled and lasting residence within the earth. — Bursevī reads fī as settling there; al-Ālūsī contrasts it with merely saying 'to' the earth. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:55/q06 What does the return to earth imply for treatment of the dead?  [وَفِيهَا نُعِيدُكُمْ · law]
- 20:55/q06/p1 Burial in earth is prescribed, whether in a trench or niche. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says burning, drowning, and aboveground coffins conflict with divine custom and human nature. He cites 5:31, where a crow teaches Cain to cover Abel's body, and says divine laws then required burial in earth. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:55/q07 What does the reported return of a soul after death involve?  [وَفِيهَا نُعِيدُكُمْ · reports]
- 20:55/q07/p1 After ascent, the soul is commanded back to earth or body; grave questioning follows. — ʿAlī's report in al-Thaʿlabī has the believer's soul ascending, returning by God's command, and hearing footsteps. Al-Barāʾ's account in al-Rāzī describes grave punishment, questioning, and return, without grading; al-Qurṭubī's version names ʿIlliyyīn and cites his Tadhkira and al-Thaʿlabī. Al-Biqāʿī calls sitting and answering two angels soundly established. [5 notes: THALABI(علي); RAZI-FULL(البراء); QURTUBI-FULL(البراء,الثعلبي); BIQAI-FULL]

## 20:55/q08 What does God bring out of earth another time?  [وَمِنْهَا نُخْرِجُكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether emergence names resurrection directly, an intervening dust-and-clay stage, or inward true life.
- 20:55/q08/p1 People rise alive for resurrection, gathering, judgment, and recompense. — Al-Rāzī prefers resurrection and gathering. Al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, and al-Ālūsī describe reassembly of scattered parts, former form, and restored soul; al-Kashshāf cites swift emergence in 70:43. Al-Qurṭubī names reckoning and Bursevī recompense. Ibn Kathīr cites 17:52 and 7:25. Ibn ʿĀshūr describes recreation within earth, citing 21:104. Al-Biqāʿī compares plants; Bursevī quotes Firdawsī's dust-and-return poem. [28 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL+]
- 20:55/q08/p2 People first emerge as dust and clay and are then revived. — Al-Rāzī reports this from some accounts. Al-Wāḥidī cites al-Zajjāj's comparison of resurrection from dust with Adam's creation from dust. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعض الأخبار); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج)]
- 20:55/q08/p3 Inwardly, emergence is true life through subsistence and balanced faculties. — Al-Qāshānī says the faculties' movements become balanced and their dispositions prevail. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:55/q09 Does this verse describe reincarnation?  [وَمِنْهَا نُخْرِجُكُمْ تَارَةً أُخْرَىٰ · theology]
turns on: Whether the later emergence is resurrection or a new earthly life.
- 20:55/q09/p1 The emergence is resurrection after death. — Kur'an Yolu rejects reincarnation readings and cites 2:28. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 20:55/q09/p2 The verse supports reincarnation. — Kur'an Yolu reports attempts to use this verse for the claim but rejects them, citing 2:28. [0 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]

## 20:55/q10 What does tārah mean?  [تَارَةً · meaning]
- 20:55/q10/p1 One time, occasion, or turn in a repeated sequence; here, once again. — Al-Ṭabarī reports Qatāda and Ibn Zayd, who calls it later creation; he and al-Wāḥidī refer to 17:69. Bursevī derives usage from a turn or movement to a repeated occurrence; al-Ālūsī likens it to marra. Ibn ʿĀshūr gives plural tārāt. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:55/q11 What is the origin of the word tārah?  [تَارَةً · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word is underived or retains an earlier hamza.
- 20:55/q11/p1 It is an underived, fixed noun. — Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers this. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 20:55/q11/p2 Its alif comes from a lost hamza. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Ibn al-Aʿrābī's proposal; hamza in plural taʾr is offered as evidence. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:55/q12 What earlier event makes the emergence “another time”?  [تَارَةً أُخْرَىٰ · grammar]
- 20:55/q12/p1 It parallels first creation from earth, understood as earlier emergence, rather than return into earth. — Al-Farrāʾ says ukhrā calls for an act of like kind; al-Qurṭubī uses a camel-house-camel analogy. Al-Zajjāj compares emergence as soil with Adam's creation; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī agree. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 21:104. [9 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:55/q13 Why are “from it” and “in it” placed first?  [مِنْهَا فِيهَا وَمِنْهَا · rhetoric]
- 20:55/q13/p1 The order emphasizes earth as origin of both creations and makes the middle phrase parallel. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explains fronting min-hā and matching placement of fī-hā. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:55/q14 How does the verse connect earth's benefits to human fate and resurrection?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the focus is earth's benefits or the full return-to-God cycle.
- 20:55/q14/p1 Earth's worldly benefits lead to its role as human origin and grave, pointing toward afterlife. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, and al-Nasafī list bed or cradle, routes, plants for food and fodder, origin or mother, and shroud or container. Al-Rāzī says worldly benefits serve the afterlife. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares humans and plants and cites 71:17–18; al-Ālūsī sees delicate bodies emerging from dense soil. Al-Biqāʿī also compares plants. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:55/q14/p2 The cycle through earth completes people's return to God. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes people becoming part of earth, emerging, and returning to God in a completed guidance cycle. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:55/q15 What do creation, return, and emergence establish about God's power?  [* · theology]
- 20:55/q15/p1 The God who created from earth can revive the dead for judgment. — Al-Biqāʿī says the same power creates and raises life from lifeless matter, proving divine choice rather than nature and that none escapes God. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees first creation as evidence. Iṣlāḥī says resurrection is easy for the Creator and providence entails reward for obligations and punishment for disorder and misuse of favors. Asad adds life's ephemerality and cites 3:59 and 15:26. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:55/q16 Does the verse include the soul in bodily resurrection?  [خَلَقْنَٰكُمْ نُعِيدُكُمْ نُخْرِجُكُمْ · theology]
turns on: Whether the resurrected body necessarily contains the same soul.
- 20:55/q16/p1 The plural addresses body and soul together, related through death and resurrection. — Al-Biqāʿī answers philosophers who call spirit neither subtle body, force, nor form but governor of body, and some who deny its return to its former body; even in ʿIlliyyīn it remains tied to a body experiencing pain and pleasure. Al-Qushayrī distinguishes earthen bodies from spirits entrusted from nearness, assigning worship to bodies and gnosis to spirits. Al-Ālūsī likens bodies to cages of spirit nightingales from the realm of dominion, citing 39:69. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:55/q16/p2 Some accounts separate spirit's fate from return to its former body. — Al-Biqāʿī reports philosophers who take spirit as governor rather than bodily form and place it with intellects and angels or a celestial body; some resurrection-affirmers deny return to the former body. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الفلاسفة)]

## 20:55/q17 Who speaks these lines in the surrounding passage?  [* · rhetoric]
- 20:55/q17/p1 God inserts His speech into the Moses account to address Quraysh. — Iṣlāḥī says the first-person shift marks four verses as God's own speech and calls present Quraysh to recognize and obey their Lord. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:55/q18 What does earth's role invite people to value inwardly?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether earth is valued as path to nearness or body as vehicle of spirit.
- 20:55/q18/p1 Earth merits affection as human mother, provision, return, and path to God. — Sulamī reports Yaḥyā ibn Muʿādh: the righteous cross earth toward nearness. Al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī quote the Prophet urging contact or kindness to earth; al-Kashshāf gives no grading. Bursevī favors earth over sky because bodily instruments enable ascent; prayer is bodily in form yet heavenly in heart and direction. He cites the Prophet's love of perfume, women, and prayer. [5 notes: SULAMI(يحيى بن معاذ); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL(النبي عليه السلام); BURSEVI(رسول الله)]
- 20:55/q18/p2 The body is adorned by grace while the entrusted spirit lives through unveiling and gnosis. — Al-Qushayrī assigns bodies to worship and spirits to enduring gnosis, unveiling majesty and beauty. Al-Ālūsī speaks of luminous spirits in bodily cages, citing 39:69. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:55/q19 What reports describe creation and burial at life's beginning and end?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the report concerns prenatal decree generally or future grave soil.
- 20:55/q19/p1 A womb report describes three forty-day stages, angelic recording, and destiny overriding apparent conduct. — Yaḥyā reports the Prophet saying an angel writes provision, deeds, lifespan, and final destiny after three forty-day womb stages; decree can prevail shortly before death. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 20:55/q19/p2 An angel joins soil from the future burial site to a developing person. — Yaḥyā reports soil mixed with or scattered on a created body. ʿAṭāʾ specifies semen or embryo; Ibn Masʿūd adds recording lifespan, provision, and burial site, citing 3:6. Abū Nuʿaym calls the newborn-dust version rare from ʿAwn and known through trustworthy Abū ʿĀṣim; al-Qurṭubī points to Ibn Masʿūd at 6:2. [12 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI(عطاء الخراساني); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء الخراساني); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو نعيم,عطاء الخراساني); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عطاء الخراساني); DURR-FULL(عطاء الخراساني); ALUSI-FULL(عطاء الخرسانى)]

## 20:55/q20 How is the verse used at funerals?  [* · reports]
- 20:55/q20/p1 It is recited during burial to recall earthly creation, return, and resurrection. — Ibn Kathīr reports the Prophet tossing soil into a grave and reciting the three phrases. Al-Durr reports recitation at Umm Kulthūm's burial with 'In God's name, in God's path, and upon the way of God's Messenger.' Study Quran says Muslims often recite it at funerals. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN]
