# 23:72: 14 questions

## 23:72/q01 How does this question connect to the surrounding passage?  [أَمْ · links]
turns on: The notes emphasize different parts of the passage's sequence and wider theme.
- 23:72/q01/p1 It follows the accusation of madness as another rebuke and removes a possible excuse for rejecting the Messenger. — Al-Bayḍāwī, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr connect أم to 23:70. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī counts this as the fourth rejected excuse: the first two concern the Qurʾān and religion's novelty, the third the Prophet's alleged madness, the fourth his conduct. Al-Biqāʿī says they already know he takes no fee. [7 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 23:72/q01/p2 The series of questions exhausts excuses and leaves the rejecters responsible for resisting a known Messenger and clear truth. — Al-Bayḍāwī connects 23:70–73, leaving hatred of truth or lack of discernment. Abū Ḥayyān and the Kashshāf as quoted by Ibn ʿĀshūr add that the Messenger is known, seeks no wealth, calls to Islam, and has signs; opponents rely on inherited religion and groundless charges. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the earlier questions probed their motives, while this rules out a cause in the Messenger. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(صاحب الكشاف)]
- 23:72/q01/p3 The passage rebukes careless opponents, reassures the Prophet that he is on the right path, and exposes their contradictory rejection. — Iṣlāḥī says they acknowledge truth but utter its opposite; they have strayed and will see their fate. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 23:72/q01/p4 The opposition of the affluent and powerful to prophets is a recurring theme of the sūra. — Study Quran compares 7:59–62 and 7:136. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 23:72/q02 What does the question about asking them for payment mean?  [تَسْـَٔلُهُمْ خَرْجًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the imagined human contribution is paraphrased as a mission fee or as provision.
- 23:72/q02/p1 It denies that the Prophet charges people for conveying the Qurʾān, faith, counsel or guidance, so a fee cannot excuse their rejection. — Muqātil specifies belief in the Qurʾān; Qatāda, al-Zajjāj, al-Ḥasan and others specify what he brought or his mission. Al-Ṭabarī cites 42:23 and says they recoiled proudly. Al-Ṭabrisī says charging could arouse suspicion or burden acceptance. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān describe their imagined resentment; al-Rāzī says suspicion is refuted. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 34:47, 68:46 and 42:23, including sarcastic formulations and the hypothetical return of any payment. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares 11:51. Ibn Kathīr and Study Quran list further no-wage parallels; Iṣlāḥī says recipients deprive themselves, not the Prophet, by rejecting his freely given favor. Kuran Yolu says his sole aim is calling to Islam. [31 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل، أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن وغيره); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 23:72/q02/p2 The question can phrase the imagined demand as provision from them, while affirming that God's provision is better. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī use rizq, provision, in their paraphrases; both affirm that no one provides or bestows favor like God. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 23:72/q03 Who are the people addressed by 'them'?  [هُمْ · referent]
- 23:72/q03/p1 They are the Quraysh unbelievers. — Mujāhid says this discussion and its preceding context concern them. Al-Qurṭubī reports that Quraysh offered to make the Prophet their richest man, and he refused. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن)]

## 23:72/q04 What is the خرج the Prophet is imagined to ask for?  [خَرْجًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term is read as the fee itself or more generally as a demand.
- 23:72/q04/p1 It is a payment, wage, grant or property given to him for the message or guidance. — Al-Ḥasan, Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās and many commentators gloss it as wage, reward, gift or money. Ibn Qutayba notes that such a levy would burden them. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a designated contribution on persons or land. Abū ʿUbayda says a servant's yield or rent, a subject's payment to a ruler, and even rain from a cloud can be called kharj; al-Hudhalī supplies the rain explanation. Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī cites al-ʿAjjāj's يوم خراج and 18:94 for the broad payment sense, and says the offered sum in 18:95 was a gift rather than a land tax. Al-Ālūsī derives it as what goes out, opposite dakh­l. [24 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(الحسن); FARRA; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عبيدة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); BIQAI-FULL(البغوي); DURR-FULL(الحسن); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 23:72/q04/p2 It can mean an affair or demand. — Al-Māwardī gives this gloss for the first word. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 23:72/q05 How are خرج and خراج distinguished?  [خَرْجًۭا خَرَاجُ · meaning]
turns on: The notes differ over synonymy, the breadth of the two terms, and whether the contrast is obligation, payer, timing, grammatical form, or kind of revenue.
- 23:72/q05/p1 They are synonymous or share the basic sense of assessed revenue, produce or recompense. — Al-Akhfash says variation of wording is more elegant. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī say both are verbal nouns without plurals, with al-Jishumī deriving them from assessed collection. Al-ʿAṣmaʿī's distinction is usage rather than fundamental meaning according to Ibn ʿAṭiyya; Asad also calls them broadly synonymous. Abū al-Ḥasan could not decide which form is more common. [11 notes: NAHHAS(الأخفش); TAB-FULL; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو الحسن); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأصمعي); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:72/q05/p2 Kharj is a voluntary payment; kharāj is an obligatory due. — Al-Naḍr ibn Shumayl transmits this from Abū ʿAmr ibn al-ʿAlāʾ; al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Biqāʿī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and others report it. [8 notes: THALABI(النضر بن شميل); JISHUMI(النضر بن شميل); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL(أبو عمرو بن العلاء); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو بن العلاء); BIQAI-FULL(الأصبهاني); IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 23:72/q05/p3 Kharj is the narrower human payment, while kharāj is broader or more ample. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī argue that extra letters suit greater meaning and the mixed reading. Al-Nasafī compares a village's revenue with Kūfa's levy. Al-Bayḍāwī, Bursevī and al-Ālūsī say land-tax usage evokes abundant recurring income. Qirāʾāt ER contrasts a specific payment with systematic revenue. Asad marks worldly recompense for people but leaves God's unrestricted. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers a broad/narrow distinction over synonymy, though he reports that the wording attributed to al-Zamakhsharī was corrected in al-Farāʾid as quoted by al-Ṭībī. [12 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري); ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari); QIRAAT-ER(Lvw); IBNASHUR-FULL+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+]
- 23:72/q05/p4 Kharj is the broader term for anything paid out, while kharāj specifically denotes land tax. — Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī cites al-ʿAjjāj and a poem about land tax; he connects the people's offered gift in 18:94–95 with the general kharj sense. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports an argument attributed to al-Zamakhsharī in this direction and a correction of its reversed wording. [5 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]
- 23:72/q05/p5 Kharj is paid by persons; kharāj comes from land. — Abū ʿAmr ibn al-ʿAlāʾ is reported by al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī; Ibn al-Aʿrābī by Ibn ʿĀshūr. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبو عمرو بن العلاء); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو بن العلاء); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن الأعرابي)]
- 23:72/q05/p6 Kharj is a fee, while kharāj is a gift or allowance. — Abū Ḥātim gives this distinction in al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī. [2 notes: NAHHAS(أبو حاتم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حاتم)]
- 23:72/q05/p7 Kharj is a verbal noun, while kharāj is a substantive noun. — Muḥammad ibn Yazīd or al-Mubarrad is reported in al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī. [2 notes: NAHHAS(محمد بن يزيد); QURTUBI-FULL(المبرد)]
- 23:72/q05/p8 Kharj is a one-time payment; kharāj is periodically recurring. — Al-Aṣmaʿī gives the distinction; Ibn ʿAṭiyya treats it as usage while saying the underlying senses coincide. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأصمعي)]
- 23:72/q05/p9 Kharāj is fayʾ, whereas kharj is a tax or jizya. — Al-Biqāʿī transmits the distinction from al-Zajjāj. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الزجاج)]
- 23:72/q05/p10 Both terms can refer to land dues, a worker's wage or stipend, and assessed revenue. — Al-Thaʿlabī mentions dues on slave or land; Ibn Maktūm, quoted by al-Biqāʿī, includes annual measured levies on communal wealth and the earnings of a slave or bondwoman. The Kashshāf allows land dues to rulers and workers' pay for either form. [3 notes: THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(ابن مكتوم)]

## 23:72/q06 How are the two payment words read?  [خَرْجًۭا خَرَاجُ · readings]
turns on: The written or recited presence of alif in each word, and whether matching or contrasting forms convey a nuance.
- 23:72/q06/p1 The first is read without alif and the second with alif: kharjan fa-kharāj. — Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, ʿĀṣim and Abū ʿAmr are repeatedly named; al-Jishumī adds Abū Jaʿfar and Yaʿqūb, Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it the majority. Al-Ḥasan is also reported to recite it, and Qirāʾāt ER says it contrasts a specific fee with systematic revenue. Al-Zajjāj reports the alternative with alif in the first word. The Nasafī gives a divergent grouping of readers. Al-Nashr refers back to earlier discussion without restating forms. Al-Naḥḥās and Ibn ʿĀshūr say variation avoids inelegant repetition; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares a more distant repetition in 34:47. Al-Nasafī explicitly prefers the mixed reading for its greater second term. [20 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNMUJAHID; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وعاصم); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ونافع وابن كثير وعاصم وأبو عمرو ويعقوب); BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وابن عامر); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وعاصم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وعاصم وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي والأعمش ويحيى بن وثاب وابن عامر وأبو حيوة); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن,عاصم); NASHR; IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); QIRAAT-ER(Lvw); NASAFI-FULL+]
- 23:72/q06/p2 Both are read without alif: kharjan fa-kharj. — Ibn ʿĀmir is repeatedly named; al-Qurṭubī adds Abū Ḥaywa for the second form. Al-Nasafī instead attributes the second unlengthened form to ʿAlī and Ḥamza. Qirāʾāt ER says this removes the specific-versus-systematic contrast. Ibn ʿĀshūr says repeating a form suits synonymy and paired symmetry. [15 notes: IBNMUJAHID; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن عامر); JISHUMI(ابن عامر); BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وابن عامر); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عامر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وعاصم وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي والأعمش ويحيى بن وثاب وابن عامر وأبو حيوة); NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن عامر); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي والحسن وعيسى); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عامر); QIRAAT-ER(Lvw)]
- 23:72/q06/p3 Both are read with alif: kharājan fa-kharāj. — Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī are repeatedly named; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds Khalaf, and al-Qurṭubī adds al-Aʿmash and Yaḥyā ibn Waththāb for the first word. Al-Bayḍāwī cites matched sound patterns. Qirāʾāt ER says both words suggest systematic extraction; Ibn ʿĀshūr says repetition suits synonymy. [16 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNMUJAHID; FARISI-HUJJA(حمزة والكسائي); JISHUMI(حمزة والكسائي); BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وابن عامر); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وعاصم وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي والأعمش ويحيى بن وثاب وابن عامر وأبو حيوة); NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي والحسن وعيسى); IBNASHUR-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وخلف); QIRAAT-ER(Lvw)]
- 23:72/q06/p4 A reported reversal reads kharājan fa-kharj. — Al-Ālūsī attributes it to al-Ḥasan and ʿĪsā; he says the first larger term can stress entrenched disbelief and the second, smaller term that even a little from God surpasses much from people. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي والحسن وعيسى)]

## 23:72/q07 What is meant by 'your Lord's kharāj'?  [خَرَاجُ رَبِّكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether kharāj here is worldly sustenance, later reward, both, unrestricted recompense, or imposed dues.
- 23:72/q07/p1 It is God's reward or recompense in the Hereafter for the Prophet's obedience and calling. — Muqātil, al-Ḥasan and many others contrast it with worldly wages; al-Qurṭubī reports Quraysh offered to make the Prophet their richest man and he declined. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says reward is called compensation in context. Iṣlāḥī says God supports messengers and righteous successors who preach truth. [19 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل، author); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; TABRISI(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 23:72/q07/p2 It is God's worldly sustenance or gift, better than any payment from people. — Ibn ʿAbbās calls it His gift; al-Kalbī specifies worldly sustenance. Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites the following 'best provider' as evidence. Al-Bayḍāwī says its breadth and permanence leave the Messenger without need for fees. Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī stress that none grants favor like God. [11 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 23:72/q07/p3 It includes provision in this life and reward in the next, both greater and more lasting than a human fee. — Al-Baghawī, Bursevī and al-Ālūsī expressly combine the two. Bursevī says this makes the Messenger independent of gifts; al-Ālūsī adds freedom from obligation to people. [4 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; THALABI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 23:72/q07/p4 In the mixed reading, God's kharāj can point to land dues and capitation He has imposed. — Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī invokes 25:57 and 12:104, where the Prophet asks no wage, to explain the contrast. [1 notes: FARISI-HUJJA]
- 23:72/q07/p5 It is God's unrestricted recompense, without limiting it to this world or the next. — Asad's translation leaves God's recompense open while marking the human side as worldly. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari)]

## 23:72/q08 Why is God's kharāj better than theirs?  [فَخَرَاجُ رَبِّكَ خَيْرٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: The usual explanation stresses abundance and permanence; one explanation makes the contrast striking even if God's gift is phrased as small.
- 23:72/q08/p1 God gives more, more reliably and for longer, so the Prophet has no need for their payment. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān contrast even a small human fee with the Creator's greater gift. Al-Bayḍāwī says God's provision is broad and lasting; al-Biqāʿī says His reward is never cut off or withheld even from sinners and 'due' hints at a pledge to provide. Bursevī says His gift never ends or becomes clouded; al-Ālūsī adds freedom from human obligation. Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ālūsī say the final provider clause reinforces this superiority. [15 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل، author); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(تفسير المناسبات); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:72/q08/p2 Even a little from God is better than much from them. — Al-Ālūsī offers this explanation for the reported kharājan fa-kharj reading, alongside a stress on the rejecters' entrenched disbelief. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 23:72/q09 Why does the verse say 'your Lord'?  [رَبِّكَ · rhetoric]
- 23:72/q09/p1 It strengthens the argument and honors the Prophet. — Al-Ālūsī explains the address in this way. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 23:72/q10 What does calling God 'the best of providers' imply?  [وَهُوَ خَيْرُ ٱلرَّٰزِقِينَ · theology]
turns on: Whether 'best' bears only on the quality of giving or also yields a rule about what counts as provision.
- 23:72/q10/p1 God gives and rewards best, while created people may provide for one another through His permission and without equalling Him. — Al-Suddī says God allots one person's provision through another's hands; the 'people of meanings,' al-Jubbāʾī and al-Jishumī argue that the comparative presupposes other providers. Al-Wāḥidī cites Arabic usage about a ruler providing for soldiers. Al-Ṭabrisī expressly qualifies human provision by God's permission. Al-Biqāʿī says God knows each recipient's benefit and harm and provides without being asked. Al-Qurṭubī says none bestows favor as He does; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the clause parenthetical praise of His abundant favor. [19 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الجبائي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الجبائي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:72/q10/p2 Unlawful things do not count as God's provision. — Al-Jishumī draws this inference from saying that God's provision is best. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 23:72/q11 What reports illustrate accepting or declining provision from people?  [وَهُوَ خَيْرُ ٱلرَّٰزِقِينَ · reports]
turns on: The reports give different responses to human offers and different illustrations of divine provision.
- 23:72/q11/p1 Umm al-Dardāʾ says provision comes through people; accept it, use what is needed and pass surplus to needy brethren. — She says God does not send coins from heaven and warns against rejecting provision He sends. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(أم الدرداء)]
- 23:72/q11/p2 The Prophet told ʿUmar to accept an unsolicited gift, then use it or give it to the needy. — The gift must be neither sought nor pursued. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(النبي)]
- 23:72/q11/p3 ʿUmar gave Qurayshī ʿAbd Allāh ibn Saʿd a thousand dinars and urged him to accept it. — Qubayṣa ibn Dhuʾayb reports that ʿUmar cited the Prophet's instruction about unsolicited gifts. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قبيصة بن ذؤيب)]
- 23:72/q11/p4 Bahlūl refused Hārūn's offer to pay his debts or grant a pension. — He reasoned that debt cannot be discharged with debt and that God would not remember Hārūn while forgetting another servant; Bursevī links this to unexpected divine provision being better than a specified human source. [2 notes: BURSEVI(بهلول)]

## 23:72/q12 What practice does the verse suggest for preaching or seeking wages?  [تَسْـَٔلُهُمْ خَرْجًۭا · law]
turns on: Whether the Prophet's example requires abstention or permits wages while preferring abstention.
- 23:72/q12/p1 A preacher may lawfully take wages, but abstaining is better; prophets chose God's reward. — Bursevī reports Ibn al-ʿArabī's view that preaching wages are among the most lawful earnings. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الشيخ الأكبر)]
- 23:72/q12/p2 One should preach without seeking payment or trading knowledge for livelihood. — Bursevī quotes a Persian couplet warning that an exegete loses by selling knowledge and skill for bread. Iṣlāḥī says the Prophet freely shared what he had freely received; the opponent's refusal harms only the opponent. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 23:72/q13 What inward counsel is drawn from the verse?  [* · inward]
- 23:72/q13/p1 Those firmly grounded in knowing God should call people through God without staining their hearts with worldly or otherworldly desires. — Bursevī quotes the Taʾwīlāt Najmiyya. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 23:72/q14 How did the Meccan idolaters respond to the Prophet's call?  [* · interpretation]
- 23:72/q14/p1 They strayed from right belief and conduct despite the Prophet's efforts. — Kuran Yolu describes the path he called them to as Islam and says they went astray despite his work. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
