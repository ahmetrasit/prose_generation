# 79:27: 14 questions

## 79:27/q01 How is the opening hamza of ءَأَنتُمْ performed?  [ءَأَنتُمْ · readings]
- 79:27/q01/p1 The HzT performance eases or alters the hamza at the opening boundary. — QIRAAT-ER says the pronoun, comparison, and word identity remain the same. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(HzT)]

## 79:27/q02 Who is addressed by أَنتُمْ?  [أَنتُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the direct plural is God's immediate address to deniers or a question conveyed through Muhammad; within direct address, how broadly its audience extends.
- 79:27/q02/p1 The direct address confronts the deniers of resurrection, especially the Meccans or Quraysh. — Muqatil calls them Arabs; al-Farra, al-Qurtubi, Bursevi, and Alusi specify Meccans, while al-Tabari specifies Quraysh. Abu Hayyan says the form is general but targets deniers; Ibn Ashur says the direct second person replaces the prior third-person report of their objection in 79:10 and 79:14. Bint al-Shati reports Abu Hayyan's explanation that they deem decayed bodies impossible to restore. Al-Biqai says the shift from addressing the Prophet to all people makes the proof accessible and reproaches their weakness. [17 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI(أبو حيان)]
- 79:27/q02/p2 All people may be addressed, with unbelievers particularly in view. — Ibn Atiyya gives this as one possibility for the scope of the address. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 79:27/q02/p3 Muhammad may be commanded to tell the people this question. — Ibn Atiyya gives this as another possibility. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 79:27/q03 What does the comparison in أَشَدُّ خَلْقًا seek to establish?  [أَشَدُّ خَلْقًا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the comparison answers denial of resurrection, concerns workmanship, or establishes the original creation of humans; al-Wahidi and al-Razi argue against the latter alternatives.
- 79:27/q03/p1 Creating the greater heaven shows that God can recreate people after death; the comparison measures difficulty only from the human viewpoint. — Muqatil asks weaker humans to consider their fate and invokes the sky's splitting in 82:1 and 84:1. Al-Tabari invokes the builders' power and 79:12; al-Thalabi, al-Tabrisi, Ibn Kathir, al-Qurtubi, Ibn Ashur, and Kuranyolu cite 40:57, while al-Wahidi, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, and Study Quran invoke 36:81. Al-Wahidi and al-Baghawi say both acts are equally easy for God; Ibn al-Jawzi reports the same distinction between people's estimate and God's power. Al-Razi prefers this reading because humans are small beside heaven and the sura opens with resurrection. Al-Baydawi glosses ashadd khalqan as harder to create. Abu Hayyan adduces heaven's permanence and resistance to change; al-Biqai its size, height, and benefits; Elmali its encompassing size, assembled materials and celestial bodies; Alusi its vastness and intricate marvels. Al-Jishumi adds that the power preserving created substances can restore them after destruction. Ibn Kathir notes that this is recreation after first creation. Ibn Ashur says habit led opponents to deem unfamiliar resurrection impossible despite the greater familiar creation. Asad connects 40:56-57 to humanity's smallness before cosmic scale and complexity. Bint al-Shati says reflection on 79:28 lets deniers answer themselves. Islahi adds that the ordered sky, night, day, sustenance, and providence show both power and the wisdom of accountability; Kuranyolu cites pillarless heavens in 13:2 and night and day in 2:164 and 78:10-11. Study Quran notes that some opponents already accepted sky's creation. Al-Tabatabai says the rebuke removes the perceived difficulty of resurrection. [48 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 79:27/q03/p2 It compares whose workmanship is more perfect. — Al-Wahidi reports al-Kalbi's explanation but calls it remote. [0 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي)-]
- 79:27/q03/p3 It proves that human beings are created. — Al-Razi reports this alternative but objects that someone denying human creation could deny heaven's creation too, and that the sura began with resurrection. [0 notes: RAZI-FULL-]

## 79:27/q04 What is the grammatical function of خَلْقًا?  [خَلْقًا · grammar]
- 79:27/q04/p1 It is an accusative specification, recast from the predicate: is your creation harder? — Bursevi parses antum as subject and ashadd as predicate; Alusi says the underlying sense is your creation after death. Both attach the specification to the comparative question. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 79:27/q05 How is أَمِ ٱلسَّمَآءُ joined to the first half of the question?  [أَمِ ٱلسَّمَآءُ · grammar]
- 79:27/q05/p1 The sky is coordinated with you, with the second predicate understood: or is the sky harder to create? — Al-Samin explicitly coordinates al-sama with antum; al-Nasafi and Bursevi supply the omitted predicate. Bursevi treats am as a connected alternative. Al-Wahidi reports al-Zajjaj's expansion of the same question; al-Zajjaj and Ibn al-Jawzi also report that heaven, rather than a relative clause, completes the comparison. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قوم); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]

## 79:27/q06 Why is the comparison phrased as a direct question?  [ءَأَنتُمْ أَشَدُّ خَلْقًا أَمِ ٱلسَّمَآءُ · rhetoric]
- 79:27/q06/p1 It rebukes deniers and elicits their own admission that heaven's creation is greater, so resurrection is possible. — Al-Qurtubi calls it reproach and rebuke. Bursevi says their concession about the harder sky forces admission of the easier resurrection. Ibn Ashur calls the question confirmatory. Bint al-Shati says the signs are cast as a question so the deniers answer themselves; al-Tabatabai says the rebuke dispels their doubt. Al-Biqai says the plural address exposes human weakness. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 79:27/q07 What does ٱلسَّمَآءُ denote here?  [ٱلسَّمَآءُ · referent]
- 79:27/q07/p1 Heaven denotes the cosmic system by metonymy. — Asad compares his note on 2:29 and elsewhere in the Quran; his link to 40:56-57 stresses the cosmic system's vastness and complexity relative to humanity. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 79:27/q08 What does بَنَىٰهَا say God did to heaven?  [بَنَىٰهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether building emphasizes elevation, assembly, even formation, or fitness for angels; some sources combine these descriptions rather than oppose them.
- 79:27/q08/p1 He raised the heaven above earth as a lofty roof or building. — Al-Tabari calls it a roof for earth; al-Thalabi, al-Qurtubi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Tabrisi, and al-Biqai likewise emphasize height or shelter. Ibn al-Jawzi says anything elevated above another thing is called a construction. Ibn Abbas, reported by al-Durr, glosses raising its height as building it. Al-Kashshaf gives the height as a journey of five hundred years upward. Ibn Atiyya says samk is the distance from the lower to upper surface; al-Tabrisi says samk is height opposed to depth and cites Ali and al-Farazdaq. Al-Wahidi reports commentators calling samk the roof or ceiling. Al-Biqai adds that the immense roof proves power to recreate lesser humans. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد)]
- 79:27/q08/p2 He assembled separate components into an ordered celestial whole. — Elmali defines building as joining components; he describes subtle and dense materials and celestial bodies integrated through various forces. Ibn Kathir describes raising, extending, leveling, and adorning the sky with stars. [3 notes: ELMALILI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 79:27/q08/p3 He made heaven even, sound, and without cracks or unevenness. — Al-Wahidi says it has no cracks or fissures. Al-Tabrisi includes smoothness and absence of fissures or unevenness. Ibn Atiyya gives smooth and even or perfectly constructed as interpretations of the following taswiya, but says God knows its exact nature; al-Tabrisi defines taswiya as making measures equal in reality or judgment. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 79:27/q08/p4 He made heaven fit for angels. — Al-Tabrisi reports this as another explanation of the following description of heaven's making. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 79:27/q09 Why call heaven a building?  [بَنَىٰهَا · rhetoric]
- 79:27/q09/p1 The word highlights its stability against disorder or collapse. — Bursevi observes that building usually names lower structures, yet the sky is called a building to stress its resistance to disintegration. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 79:27/q10 Does بَنَىٰهَا start a new sentence or modify ٱلسَّمَآءُ?  [ٱلسَّمَآءُ بَنَىٰهَا · grammar]
turns on: The pause after heaven or after built it, the possibility of an omitted relative, and whether a relative description implies an unbuilt heaven or avoids unjoined descriptions.
- 79:27/q10/p1 The question ends at heaven; He built it starts a new sentence explaining how heaven was made. — Al-Wahidi reports al-Kisai's pause, al-Farra's exposition, and al-Zajjaj's expanded comparison; he prefers the pause because heaven is not a relative antecedent. Al-Razi reports al-Kisai, al-Farra, and al-Zajjaj together. Al-Farra objects that the relative construal suggests another heaven not built by God. Al-Samin compares 43:58; Bursevi says the next clause resumes after the connected alternative. Ibn Ashur says syntax preserves this division even with continuous recitation; he notes that pause placement affects sense and that Quranic verse endings have deliberate cadence like poetic rhyme or rhymed prose. He also says the basic sense was intelligible to Arabic hearers while rhetorical inimitability challenged their eloquent leaders. Al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, al-Tabatabai, Alusi, and al-Razi say the subsequent words detail the sky's construction; Alusi adds that leaving the agent unstated both makes God self-evident and magnifies Him. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,الفراء,الكسائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قوم); RAZI-FULL(الفراء,الكسائي والفراء والزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو حاتم)+]
- 79:27/q10/p2 He built it is an adjectival or omitted-relative clause: the heaven which He built; the pause follows built it. — Al-Zajjaj and Ibn al-Jawzi report grammarians who attach the clause to heaven. Al-Wahidi reports Abu Hatim's pause at banaha. Al-Razi reports Abu Hatim's omitted relative and al-Qaffal's valid Arabic example; al-Qaffal argues that otherwise built it and raised its height are successive unrelated descriptions needing a conjunction. Al-Wahidi objects that heaven is not a relative antecedent; al-Farra, reported by al-Razi, objects that the reading implies an unbuilt heaven. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(بعض النحويين); RAZI-FULL(أبو حاتم,القفال); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو حاتم)-; RAZI-FULL(الفراء)-]

## 79:27/q11 How can heaven's builder be known to be God?  [بَنَىٰهَا · theology]
turns on: Whether reason can identify God's direct agency or only a first maker, given the proposed possibility of an intermediary with power to create bodies.
- 79:27/q11/p1 Reason establishes that heaven is originated and requires a maker, God. — Al-Razi's first proof says every body originates: an eternal body cannot move, since motion follows a prior state; eternal rest is also impossible because heaven moves now, its rest is removable and contingent, and neither necessary nor contingent causes can sustain it without regress or a necessary endpoint. A free agent cannot intend an already existing state. His second proof says all besides the Necessary Existent is possible and caused; two necessary beings would be composite and dependent. His third says heaven's precise size, which could be larger or smaller, requires a determiner. Al-Nasafi says the omitted subject of built it is Allah. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 79:27/q11/p2 Reason proves an originated heaven and an ultimate eternal God; revelation specifies that God directly built it. — Al-Razi reports some scholars distinguishing rational proof of origin from revelation's identification of the actual builder, leaving open the question of an intermediary. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعض العلماء)]
- 79:27/q11/p3 Reason excludes an intermediary builder and establishes God's direct agency. — Al-Razi reports scholars arguing that all possibilities are equally within God's power and one effect cannot issue from two independent agents; he considers this sound for those who deny any effective agent besides the One. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعض العلماء); RAZI-FULL+]
- 79:27/q11/p4 A being created by God might have power to make bodies and thus have built heaven. — Al-Razi presents this as a proposed objection to identifying the immediate builder through reason alone. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(سائل); RAZI-FULL(بعض العلماء)-]

## 79:27/q12 How does heaven's building relate to the earth's creation and spreading?  [بَنَىٰهَا · links]
turns on: Whether earth's initial creation and its later spreading are distinguished when comparing 41:9-12 with 79:27-30.
- 79:27/q12/p1 The passages appear to give conflicting orders: Fussilat creates earth before heaven, while al-Naziat builds heaven before spreading earth. — Ibn Qutayba reports the objection, citing 41:9, 41:12, 79:27-28, and 79:30. Al-Itqan reports Saʿid ibn Jubayr's questioner presenting this among four apparent discrepancies to Ibn Abbas, alongside afterlife denial and questioning and the wording was Allah. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(قالوا); ITQAN(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 79:27/q12/p2 Earth was created first, heaven was made next, and earth was then spread, anchored, and planted; the stages total six days. — Ibn Qutayba assigns two days to earth's creation, two to the heavens from smoke, and two to spreading and anchoring the gathered earth and growing its plants. Al-Itqan reports Ibn Abbas resolving apparent conflicts by differing circumstances or meanings; al-Hakim authenticated the full report, and Ibn Hajar says its original appears in an authenticated collection and classifies four discrepancies. [3 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; ITQAN(ابن عباس,الحاكم)]

## 79:27/q13 How does this verse connect to the sura's argument before and after it?  [* · links]
- 79:27/q13/p1 It follows Pharaoh's warning and the mockers' doubt with a creation proof, then leads to certainty of resurrection and judgment. — Al-Wahidi says the discourse turns after Pharaoh to resurrection deniers; al-Biqai says Pharaoh's story proves divine power, unbelief gathers evil while belief gathers good, and the sura's oaths advance its central concern with gathering and final fate. Elmali likewise connects Moses' story to lordship, ease of resurrection, and punishment. Ibn Ashur says the passage answers the decayed-bones objection in 79:10. Bursevi links it to resurrection requiring just one blast in 79:13. Al-Razi says the proof of God's ability precedes the announced event in 79:34. Al-Tabatabai says cosmic order also establishes lordship, prophecy, obligation, and repayment of deeds from 79:34 onward. Islahi says providential order warrants accountability, answering 79:11-12. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 79:27/q14 What admonition is drawn from the verse's warning?  [* · interpretation]
- 79:27/q14/p1 Turn from punishment toward pardon and leave heedlessness before shame comes. — Bursevi conveys this in a Persian admonitory poem. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
