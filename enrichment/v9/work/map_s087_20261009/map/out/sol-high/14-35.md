# 14:35: 28 questions

## 14:35/q01 How is the opening 'when Abraham said' construed?  [وَإِذْ قَالَ إِبْرَٰهِيمُ · grammar]
- 14:35/q01/p1 An omitted verb means 'remember when Abraham said.' — Jishumī supplies the omitted governing verb; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Nasafī gloss the summons as remembrance; Biqāʿī adds 'remember for them' God's dealings with Abraham; Ālūsī calls idh the object of the omitted verb; Ibn ʿĀshūr parses it as an accusative time noun and links its wonder to 14:28. [6 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 14:35/q02 How is Abraham's name read here?  [إِبْرَٰهِيمُ · readings]
- 14:35/q02/p1 A canonical reading has Ibrāhām, with fatḥa on hāʾ and a long ā before mīm. — Qirāʾāt ER attributes the form to Aramaic or Syriac phonology and notes its inclusion among the Seven. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 14:35/q03 What does the address 'my Lord' do and how is it formed?  [رَبِّ · rhetoric]
- 14:35/q03/p1 The repeated Lord-address expresses dependence and solicits divine attention. — Ibn ʿĀshūr parses rabb as a vocative with omitted particle and dropped first-person yāʾ; Abū Ḥayyān says repetition appeals for compassion. Iṣlāḥī counts seven Rabbi/Rabbana invocations: Rabbi grounds a plea in personally experienced favor and Rabbana in a favor witnessed by all. Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes requests specific to Abraham's precedence from shared ones. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]

## 14:35/q04 When was this prayer made in relation to the prayer in 2:126?  [ٱجْعَلْ هَٰذَا ٱلْبَلَدَ ءَامِنًۭا · reports]
turns on: Whether the two wordings mark successive occasions, a single retold supplication, or a chronology not fixed by narrative order.
- 14:35/q04/p1 This was a later prayer, after Mecca became a settlement or the House was built. — Kashshāf, Rāzī, Biqāʿī, Itqān, Ibn Kathīr, Ṭabāṭabāʾī, Samarrāʾī and Kuran Yolu contrast earlier indefinite baladan with the established al-balad. Biqāʿī places the first prayer in an empty valley; Itqān names Jurhum; Ibn Kathīr cites Ishmael's infancy, his thirteen-year seniority to Isaac, and 14:39; Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites differing provision beneficiaries. Ṭabrisī reports a post-Kaʿba setting. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); TABRISI; BURHAN]
- 14:35/q04/p2 The prayer was made while Abraham settled Hagar and Ishmael in an insecure Mecca. — Iṣlāḥī places the family in barren land and says Abraham left fertility to found a monotheistic worship center; Study Quran reports Sarah's jealousy and the move from Syria. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 14:35/q04/p3 The two passages may recount one prayer with different omissions or emphasis. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reconstructs 'make this town a secure town' with town or its descriptor omitted; Ālūsī reports the one-request view and prefers that 14:37 retells the wish for settlement as Ibn ʿAbbās understood hearts inclining to the family. [3 notes: TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 14:35/q04/p4 The Qur'an's narrative order need not match the chronology of the prayers. — Burhān answers the chronological objection by noting nonchronological reporting and some Meccan revelation after the Hijra or conquest. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 14:35/q05 Which place is 'this town'?  [هَٰذَا ٱلْبَلَدَ · referent]
- 14:35/q05/p1 It is Mecca, including its sacred precinct and the place of the Kaʿba. — Muqātil recalls pre-Islamic safety; Baghawī and Kashshāf call it the sacred city or precinct; Biqāʿī notes the opponents sought to expel the Messenger from it; Asad locates the Kaʿba there; Study Quran connects Hagar and Ishmael with it. [17 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 14:35/q06 What does al-balad denote grammatically and lexically?  [ٱلْبَلَدَ · grammar]
- 14:35/q06/p1 It is the specified tract or town, in apposition to 'this.' — Ibn ʿĀshūr says balad may denote land or town and the article marks a known place. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 14:35/q07 How does 'make this town secure' relate to 'make this a secure town' in 2:126?  [ٱجْعَلْ هَٰذَا ٱلْبَلَدَ ءَامِنًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: The force of definite versus indefinite balad, the objects of jaʿala, and whether the passages record one occasion or two.
- 14:35/q07/p1 The indefinite wording asks for a town to be established; the definite wording requests security for the established town. — Kashshāf, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī and Nasafī distinguish becoming a town among secure towns from changing an existing fearful town into a secure one. Ṭabrisī notes definite repetition as in 24:35. Ibn Kathīr, Biqāʿī, Itqān, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Samarrāʾī give the early-valley/later-settlement sequence. Burhān parses the two object structures, with 14:37's barren valley in view. [15 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); TABRISI; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ITQAN; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 14:35/q07/p2 Here the definite wording asks that this town join lands whose inhabitants are safe; the indefinite wording asks to change a fearful site into a secure one. — Abū Ḥayyān reports Zamakhsharī's contrast; Ālūsī explains the second object's informational role with 'make this a good ring' versus 'make this ring good,' and says one prayer requests townhood plus safety, the other safety alone. Ālūsī objects that the first then appears unanswered and chronologically prior. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 14:35/q07/p3 The two wordings can be one request retold, or requests for continuing or differently scoped safety. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī posits ellipsis in a single prayer; Burhān reports a request for security to endure. Kashf offers continuing habitability, relief from occasional danger, lasting worldly or otherworldly safety, a second request before the first was answered, and emphatic progression amid fear; Ālūsī finds both townhood and safety intended and links 14:37 to settlement. [7 notes: TABATABAI; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); ALUSI-FULL+]

## 14:35/q08 What is the construction of 'make this town secure'?  [ٱجْعَلْ هَٰذَا ٱلْبَلَدَ ءَامِنًۭا · grammar]
- 14:35/q08/p1 Causative jaʿala takes two objects: al-balad and āminan. — Samīn expressly parses them thus. Burhān contrasts 2:126, where baladan is the second object and āminan its adjective, with this verse's first object al-balad and second object āminan. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN]

## 14:35/q09 What kind of safety does 'secure' request?  [ءَامِنًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether safety describes the place or its residents, a legal sanctity or physical protection, and whether later attacks limit the latter.
- 14:35/q09/p1 Safety for the town's inhabitants, their lives, property and worship. — Ṭabarī says the sanctuary and residents are secure; Baghawī glosses a safe place for people. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls attribution to a place figurative, citing a stormy day and a poet's sleepless camel-night; Rāzī cites 'ask the town' (12:82) and safe refugees, enemies and wild animals. Ālūsī allows a possessive adjective like labin/tāmir or figurative place-attribution. Biqāʿī adds rest from danger; Abū Ḥayyān stresses worship without fear. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الشاعر); RAZI-FULL(أكثر المفسرين); ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 14:35/q09/p2 The requested safety is a divine legal designation of the sanctuary. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it is legislative rather than physical. Rāzī reports a command and judgment certainly fulfilled; Iṣlāḥī says even harming an animal became sinful. The answer also secured travel during four sacred months and Quraysh's caravan passage, according to Iṣlāḥī. [4 notes: TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 14:35/q09/p3 The prayer asks physical protection and God granted or increased it. — Kashshāf says God protected Mecca from aggressors; Mujāhid counts security and provision among answered petitions. Ṭabrisī reports a father's killer left unassailed in the precinct and animals approaching safely; Ibn Kathīr cites 29:67 and 3:96–97; Kuran Yolu adds incoming produce and pilgrimage trade, with 28:57. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls its recorded and unrecorded blessings immense. [12 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 14:35/q09/p4 An objection says the city was later raided and the Kaʿba destroyed. — Rāzī raises these events against the claim of unqualified physical security. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 14:35/q10 Why ask for the town's safety before protection from idolatry?  [ٱجْعَلْ هَٰذَا ٱلْبَلَدَ ءَامِنًۭا وَٱجْنُبْنِى · interpretation]
- 14:35/q10/p1 Physical safety makes peaceful worship and daily life possible; religious safety then follows. — Abū Ḥayyān calls it a safe place of worship; Biqāʿī contrasts protection of bodies/property with protection of religion. Kuran Yolu says religious and worldly duties require secure lives and property. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 14:35/q11 What does 'keep me apart' mean and how do its verb forms work?  [وَٱجْنُبْنِى · meaning]
- 14:35/q11/p1 It means distance, turn away, or protect from idol worship; related light, doubled and hamzated forms share that sense. — Abū ʿUbayda cites anonymous and Dhū al-Rumma lines; Ṭabarī and Thaʿlabī cite jannabtu and Umayya ibn al-Ashkar; Wāḥidī repeats Umayya. Farrāʾ/Kisāʾī distinguish Hijazi light/doubled usage and Najdi light or hamzated usage; Samīn traces jānib and an analogy of leading a horse aside; Rāzī and Ālūsī explain the root image of putting on another side. Naḥḥās calls doubled jannaba intensive and ajnaba available, with two objects. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the request refuge from idols' misguidance. [36 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI(أمية بن الأشكر الليثي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,الزجاج,الكسائي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الفراء,بعضهم,علي بن عيسى); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL]

## 14:35/q12 Which readings of 'keep me apart' are reported?  [وَٱجْنُبْنِى · readings]
turns on: Reported reader pronunciation and regional verb patterns.
- 14:35/q12/p1 Readers use the form with a pronounced initial hamza, ajnibnī. — Kashshāf records wa-ajnibnī; Ibn ʿAṭiyya names Jaḥdarī and Thaqafī with kasra on nūn, Ṭabrisī adds Abū al-Jaḥjāḥ, and Qurṭubī names ʿĪsā. Samīn calls its hamza qaṭʿ and Ālūsī likens its pattern to akramanī. Farrāʾ had not heard a reader use the hamzated form in his day; the forms share a sense. [12 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI(بعضهم); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجحدري والثقفي); TABRISI(الجحدري والثقفي وأبو الجحجاح); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الجحدري وعيسى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجحدري وعيسى الثقفي); SAMIN-DURR(الجحدري وعيسى الثقفي); ALUSI-FULL(الجحدري وعيسى الثقفي)]
- 14:35/q12/p2 The light or doubled nonhamzated forms are also used. — Farrāʾ and al-Kisāʾī report regional use; Kashshāf, Samīn and Abū Ḥayyān describe Hijazi and Najdi forms. [7 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكسائي); JISHUMI(الفراء); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 14:35/q13 Why does Abraham ask to be kept from idolatry despite prophetic protection?  [وَٱجْنُبْنِى · theology]
turns on: Whether the prayer requests persistence, inward purity, enabling grace, or protection despite a supposed necessity of prophetic infallibility.
- 14:35/q13/p1 He asks for steadfast, continuing, or increased protection already granted. — Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq compare 'make us Muslims' (2:128); Baghawī says increase; Kashshāf, Nasafī and Ibn al-Jawzī say continual avoidance. Rāzī replies that assured prophetic protection still calls for explanation; Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues necessary infallibility remains necessary and rejects continuation as sufficient. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج); NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); RAZI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 14:35/q13/p2 He humbly acknowledges perpetual need for God's grace and asks for the enabling that sustains protection. — Rāzī says known protection does not remove humility; Jishumī's Abū al-Qāsim allows devotional prayer for what is known. Ālūsī says prophetic protection comes from enabling grace, not an innate trait, and cites a report to Moses about not feeling secure before crossing the bridge; Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds that bestowed gifts never make the recipient independent. He criticizes a premise of essential infallibility used against this answer. [8 notes: JISHUMI(أبو القاسم); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); ALUSI-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 14:35/q13/p3 He asks protection from subtle inward association with causes rather than overt idol worship. — Rāzī reports Sufis' distinction between manifest and hidden shirk; Ālūsī and Asad list attachment to intermediaries, wealth, power, luck or people's favor. Ālūsī objects that prophets' protection from this too renews the question; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the next verse's idols misleading many rules out this reinterpretation. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(الصوفية); ALUSI-FULL(الصوفية); TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); ASAD-NOTES(Razi); ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 14:35/q13/p4 He seeks divine لطف that enables avoidance without coercing choice. — Ṭabrisī describes لطف; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says coercion would remove merit and Abraham asks protection as persons become receptive. Rāzī rejects the Muʿtazilite لطف explanation as departing from the apparent wording. [3 notes: TABRISI; TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL-]
- 14:35/q13/p5 The prayer voices fear of trial even for God's intimate friend and teaches others to seek a good end. — Ibrāhīm al-Taymī asks who can feel secure; Ibn ʿAṭiyya urges emulation. Qurṭubī notes Abraham's father and people worshipped idols; Abū Ḥayyān makes that experience the motive; Iṣlāḥī says many succumbed. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي); THALABI(إبراهيم التيمي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي); QURTUBI-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); DURR-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 14:35/q13/p6 An objection says an infallible prophet cannot meaningfully ask for protection from idol worship. — Baghawī and Rāzī report it; Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes the same difficulty and rejects answers based on necessary protection. [4 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI(المفسرون)]

## 14:35/q14 Who are Abraham's 'sons' in this prayer?  [وَبَنِىَّ · referent]
turns on: Whether bani means immediate children, living or believing descendants, the broader lineage, or a contextually selected line.
- 14:35/q14/p1 His immediate biological sons are meant. — Wāḥidī reports direct sons; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān say their prayer was answered. Qurṭubī says Abraham had eight and none worshipped idols; Ālūsī favors the apparent direct-offspring sense. Biqāʿī says sons are male descendants and reads omission of daughters as indicating dependence, while males act independently. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين)]
- 14:35/q14/p2 Sons includes the wider line of descendants. — Muqātil says Abraham knew descendants would differ in monotheism; Kashshāf expressly includes offspring and descendants. Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 'father Abraham' (22:78) and repeated 'children of Israel' for distant progeny; Iṣlāḥī points to the following term for progeny. [5 notes: MUQATIL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 14:35/q14/p3 Only descendants already alive when he prayed are intended. — Rāzī says the prayer was answered for children and grandchildren then living; Ālūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī report the same limit. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين)]
- 14:35/q14/p4 The intended descendants are believers or those God meant to guide. — Wāḥidī says prophetic prayers are answered and some descendants became idolaters; Baghawī and Qurṭubī likewise restrict the petition; Ṭabrisī says God knew and permitted the prayer for believers. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 14:35/q14/p5 The words include all descendants but the prayer benefits only those receptive to guidance. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says its wording is broad and its effect reaches those prepared to receive it, not the stubborn. [2 notes: TABATABAI]
- 14:35/q14/p6 The context may limit 'sons' to Ishmael's descendants in the Hijaz. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī gives this as a possible contextual restriction, excluding Isaac's line; Iṣlāḥī's discussion concerns children settled at Mecca. [2 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 14:35/q14/p7 The prayer ultimately concerns the Prophet and his purified progeny. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says avoidance of all misguidance and sin amounts to infallibility, so broad descendants must identify those purified recipients. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 14:35/q15 How is the prayer for the sons answered despite idolatrous descendants?  [وَبَنِىَّ أَن نَّعْبُدَ ٱلْأَصْنَامَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether descendants are restricted, the prayer has selected beneficiaries, or image-idols are distinguished from other acts of worship.
- 14:35/q15/p1 It was answered for Abraham's direct sons, none of whom worshipped idols. — Baghawī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya say immediate sons; Qurṭubī counts eight. Ālūsī notes Quraysh does not disprove this. Rāzī reports the answer but raises the parallel prophetic-protection objection; Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects this restriction as contrary to the wording. [8 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); RAZI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 14:35/q15/p2 It was answered for believers, guided descendants, or those receptive to it. — Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ṭabrisī and Qurṭubī limit its beneficiaries; Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes all addressed from the receptive recipients and preserves choice. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI]
- 14:35/q15/p3 It was answered for descendants alive at the time. — Rāzī, Ālūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī say later idolatry leaves the answered prayer intact. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); TABATABAI-]
- 14:35/q15/p4 God answered a general prayer for some descendants, though not all. — Rāzī cites 2:124, where wrongdoers are excluded from the covenant; Ālūsī reports him. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says partial fulfillment does not meet the objection of why an unfulfilled prayer is recited without express rejection. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); TABATABAI(المفسرون)-]
- 14:35/q15/p5 No descendant worshipped a figured image-idol; some worshipped unfigured stones or trees. — Mujāhid distinguishes ṣanam from wathan, and Ibn ʿUyayna says Ishmael's descendants installed substitute stones called al-dawwār and circled them. Kashshāf prefers 'circumambulated the House' to 'circled the House'; Ālūsī records the verbal scruple. Rāzī says both kinds equally violate the prayer; Ālūsī cites Qur'anic condemnation of Quraysh; Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the distinction and doubts a related report's authenticity. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عيينة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري عن مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن عيينة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سفيان بن عيينة); DURR-FULL(سفيان بن عيينة,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(سفيان بن عيينة,مجاهد); TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)-; TABATABAI-]
- 14:35/q15/p6 The later polytheists treated idols as intercessors rather than worshipping them. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports and rejects this distinction: intercessory veneration still involves association and the reports look fabricated. [1 notes: TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); TABATABAI-]
- 14:35/q15/p7 Mujāhid reports that no descendant worshipped an image-idol after the prayer. — His broader report says God also made Abraham a leader, secured and provisioned Mecca, and guided descendants to prayer and rites; later exegetes debate the image/unfigured distinction. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 14:35/q16 How does 'that we worship' attach syntactically?  [أَن نَّعْبُدَ · grammar]
- 14:35/q16/p1 The infinitival phrase is accusative, with an understood 'from' before worshipping. — Naḥḥās calls it accusative and glosses 'from worshipping idols'; Samīn explicitly supplies ʿan before an naʿbuda. Ibn Qutayba notes the children as understood object of the plea. [3 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB]

## 14:35/q17 What are aṣnām?  [ٱلْأَصْنَامَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ṣanam is specifically a figured statue or names every object of worship and, inwardly, every attachment.
- 14:35/q17/p1 The plural of ṣanam: a carved, figured, worshipped image or statue. — Ṭabarī cites Ruʾba using ṣanam for a woman's image; Thaʿlabī cites poetry. Wāḥidī says a worshipped image; Jishumī and Mujāhid distinguish a sculpted image, especially human-shaped in Ibn ʿAṭiyya's report, from unfigured awthān. Rāzī and Ālūsī object that unfigured cult objects are equivalent for worship. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري عن مجاهد); QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)-]
- 14:35/q17/p2 An idol is any object or supposed power worshipped besides God, even without a physical image. — Qushayrī cites desire taken as a god (45:23) and lists wealth, children, rank, obedience and worship as distractions. Asad rejects restriction to concrete false-deity images; Kuran Yolu includes anything credited with divine power and its human inventors. Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects making every orientation to something else into worship. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI-]
- 14:35/q17/p3 In inward interpretation, idols are passions, the self, attachments, spiritual states, or the multiplicity of sensory and bodily desire. — Sulamī reports Siyyārī on passion; Dīnawarī lists self, children, wealth, trade, spouse and spiritual state and prescribes disavowal without self-satisfaction. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ calls desire-driven self worst, mentions intrusive heedlessness, and says only divinely purified selves lack this idol. Qāshānī speaks of idols of multiplicity. [7 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الدينوري,السياري); QASHANI]

## 14:35/q18 What is sought by refusing idol worship?  [أَن نَّعْبُدَ ٱلْأَصْنَامَ · interpretation]
- 14:35/q18/p1 Abraham seeks sustained monotheism and freedom from association. — Rāzī calls this the second request; Biqāʿī warns that idolatry merits the Fire. Asad identifies God's existence, oneness and unique agency as the path to righteousness; Kuran Yolu finds his grateful devotion and monotheistic way exemplary. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the following explanation, 'they led many astray,' to include all deviation and sin. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]
- 14:35/q18/p2 The idols' misguidance makes divine protection necessary. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the prayer refuge from misguidance; Iṣlāḥī says the danger is great since many succumbed; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the context concerns manifest idolatry. [3 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 14:35/q19 Who grants avoidance of idolatry, and how does human choice relate?  [وَٱجْنُبْنِى وَبَنِىَّ أَن نَّعْبُدَ ٱلْأَصْنَامَ · theology]
turns on: Whether God grants the very avoidance, or enables free agents through receptive choice, and how far the plea proves a school doctrine.
- 14:35/q19/p1 God is the source of protection and guidance; servants receive the capacity by His grant. — Rāzī cites Abraham's attribution of guidance here and at 26:78 as Ashʿarite proof, echoed by his associates. Bayḍāwī says prophets' protection depends on God; Ālūsī reports the inference but finds it problematic. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says good and abstention are first God's gifts, then the servant's deeds, while evil is first attributed to the servant; reports of the Imams speak of enabling good and avoiding evil. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض أصحابنا); TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL+]
- 14:35/q19/p2 Divine protection works through لطف and receptive, free human agency. — Ṭabrisī calls the request enabling لطف; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says coerced avoidance lacks merit and God guides recipients who make themselves receptive. Rāzī rejects لطف as against the apparent plea. [4 notes: TABRISI; TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL-]

## 14:35/q20 What inward readings are given for the town and Abraham's prayer?  [هَٰذَا ٱلْبَلَدَ ءَامِنًۭا وَٱجْنُبْنِى · inward]
turns on: Whether the city symbolizes the heart or body, and whether the prayer addresses attachment to self and spiritual acts.
- 14:35/q20/p1 The secure town is an inward abode free from separation and filled only with God. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ asks for a heart secure from veiling; Qushayrī sees Mecca's safety mirrored by a heart holding none besides God. [2 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); QUSHAYRI]
- 14:35/q20/p2 The town is the bodily realm, whose safety is release from unruly soul qualities. — Qāshānī treats Abraham as spirit turning toward direct witnessing; his sons are theoretical and practical intellects, thought, intuition, remembrance and other faculties endangered by sensory multiplicity. [3 notes: QASHANI]
- 14:35/q20/p3 The prayer seeks release from self-observation and reliance on anything besides God. — Junayd says not to approach God by anything other than Him. Jaʿfar says not to return to witnessing friendship or prophethood as one's possession. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ's Kaʿba account rebukes Abraham's foregrounding of his own deed (2:127); Qushayrī reports freeing him from noticing self and deeds. Other accounts contrast witnessing divine generosity or others' condition with felt poverty in prayers for his father (26:86) and against idolatry. [7 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الجنيد,جعفر); QUSHAYRI(ويقال)]

## 14:35/q21 What reports describe events linked with the recitation of this prayer?  [* · reports]
turns on: The distinct narrated episodes.
- 14:35/q21/p1 The Prophet recited these verses and prayed for his community, receiving good news. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya transmits the report through al-Ṭabarī from ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿUmar. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن عمر عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 14:35/q21/p2 At al-ʿAqaba the Prophet recited Abraham's prayer to six Anṣār, who accepted his invitation. — Al-Durr reports ʿAqīl ibn Abī Ṭālib's account; Ṭabāṭabāʾī repeats it and says the men were moved. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(عقيل بن أبي طالب); TABATABAI(عقيل بن أبي طالب)]
- 14:35/q21/p3 The Prophet called himself the answer to Abraham's prayer and foretold his banner of praise. — Al-Durr reports his statement that Arabs will be nearest that banner on Judgment Day. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 14:35/q22 How does this passage connect with its context and other verses?  [* · links]
- 14:35/q22/p1 It rebukes Meccan idolatry and ingratitude by contrasting Quraysh with Abraham's monotheism and answered prayer. — Ṭabrisī connects it to the idol ban and command to worship, with alternatives involving Moses or granted requests; Rāzī follows proofs of God's sole worthiness. Abū Ḥayyān, Biqāʿī, Ibn Kathīr, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Iṣlāḥī and Kuran Yolu contrast God's general favors with special Meccan blessings and ancestral claims. Asad links gratitude in 14:34 with 14:42; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it a second reminder after Moses in 14:6. Ālūsī's Irshād account stresses their disobedience and threatened ruin. [16 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(إرشاد العقل السليم); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 14:35/q22/p2 The earlier Baqara account supplies another aspect of the same supplication. — Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī and Qurṭubī direct readers to 2:126; Study Quran points to 2:126–129. Kashf and Ālūsī use 14:37 to discuss residence and the prayer's relation to that earlier account. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL]

## 14:35/q23 What broader supplication practice does the verse model?  [* · interpretation]
- 14:35/q23/p1 A supplicant should pray for self, parents and descendants while acknowledging dependence on God. — Ibn Kathīr draws this rule. Kuran Yolu presents Abraham as a model of grateful devotion; Iṣlāḥī says the repeated Lord-address sustains humble supplication. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 14:35/q23/p2 Even one promised a good end may keep asking God for it. — Ālūsī cites people promised Paradise and the warning to Moses not to feel secure from divine plan until crossing the bridge. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)]

## 14:35/q24 What is reported about the sura's title and revelation setting?  [* · sciences]
- 14:35/q24/p1 It is a late Meccan sura titled for Abraham's prayer. — Asad says all authorities place it late Meccan and accepts Itqān's position immediately after sura 71. Study Quran says the title reflects the episode's requests for safety, purity, prosperity and forgiveness through 14:41. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]

## 14:35/q25 What formal difference between the town prayers is observed?  [هَٰذَا ٱلْبَلَدَ · rhetoric]
- 14:35/q25/p1 This prayer uses the definite town, whereas 2:126 uses an indefinite form. — Abū Ḥayyān notes the difference in expression. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 14:35/q26 Why is security emphasized in the rebuke?  [ءَامِنًۭا · rhetoric]
- 14:35/q26/p1 Security is an especially fitting favor to mention when censuring ingratitude. — Ālūsī reports this contextual explanation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 14:35/q27 What warning to Moses is invoked to explain continued requests for protection?  [وَٱجْنُبْنِى · reports]
- 14:35/q27/p1 God warned Moses not to feel secure from divine plan until he crossed the bridge. — Ālūsī offers the report as a possible reason to seek protection even when assurance is expected. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الله سبحانه)]

## 14:35/q28 What objection arises from Quraysh's idolatry?  [وَبَنِىَّ أَن نَّعْبُدَ ٱلْأَصْنَامَ · interpretation]
- 14:35/q28/p1 Abraham's prayer appears unanswered because Quraysh, among his descendants, worshipped idols. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this objection from commentators. [1 notes: TABATABAI(المفسرون)]
