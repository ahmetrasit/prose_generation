# 24:36: 40 questions

## 24:36/q01 How does “in houses” connect syntactically to the surrounding verses?  [فِى بُيُوتٍ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the phrase completes the lamp parable, introduces the worshippers, or resumes an earlier passage; the chosen reading of يسبح and the pause at 24:35 also matter.
- 24:36/q01/p1 It qualifies the niche, lamp, glass, or their location in the preceding light parable. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Zajjāj, Abū Ḥātim, al-Wāḥidī, al-Samīn and others offer the link to مشكاة or مصباح; al-Samīn also lists زجاجة. This keeps the parable continuous and locates its exemplar in worship houses. Ibn ʿĀshūr says these are generic nouns; Biqāʿī likewise treats the singular fittings as a type. Rāzī notes many investigators supply the implied niche; Kur’an Yolu summarizes the possible antecedents. [28 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; ZAJJAJ(التفسير); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج، الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حاتم); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الأنباري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الحوفي); IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL(كثير من المحققين); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bazı müfessirler); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Al-Zamakhsharī)+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 24:36/q01/p2 It attaches to the preceding verb for kindling: the lamp is lit in houses. — Al-Ṭabarī permits attachment to تُوقَد; al-Rummānī and later grammarians state this parse. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes it locates the lamp, while al-Bayḍāwī says it gives the example a fine setting. [14 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(غيرهما); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الرماني); QURTUBI-FULL(الرماني); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الرماني); IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN; BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 24:36/q01/p3 It attaches forward to يسبح, with فيها repeated for emphasis: worshippers glorify God in these houses. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Farrāʾ, al-Wāḥidī and others accept fronting; Rāzī defends repetition as preferable to ellipsis. Al-Samīn cites 11:108; al-Ālūsī says fronting signals importance rather than exclusivity; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it raises anticipation. The pause at عليم is possible. Al-Khafājī disputes al-Ālūsī's interruption objection, locating the parable's end earlier; al-Samīn corrects an enumeration of parses. [24 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج، الفراء); JISHUMI(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حاتم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الأنباري); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الخفاجي); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 24:36/q01/p4 A fresh sentence supplies an omitted command or verb, such as “pray” or “glorify in houses.” — Al-Jubbāʾī supplies a command to pray; al-Kashshāf and al-Samīn cite 27:12 for an omitted verb. Al-Wāḥidī favors a fresh description because plural houses do not extend the singular niche; the pause at عليم follows. [9 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الجبائي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 24:36/q01/p5 It reaches back to the examples of earlier people in 24:34, understood as prophets and righteous people whose houses were mosques. — Abū Muslim cites prophetic prayer chambers, including the intruders in 38:21; he says intervening text does not bar return to the earlier topic. Al-Qāḍī objects that 24:34 concerns deniers and slave-girl coercion and that 24:35 intervenes. [4 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم بن بحر الأصفهاني); RAZI-FULL(القاضي)-]
- 24:36/q01/p6 It is a settled circumstantial phrase modifying “His light” in the preceding verse, portraying Qur’an received and studied in gatherings. — Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers this reading, supported by the report on people gathering in a house of God for recitation and study. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 24:36/q01/p7 It is a fronted predicate with “men” as subject, answering who receives the preceding light. — Ibn ʿĀshūr offers this as another possible fresh construction. Al-Wāḥidī says the passive reading prevents forward attachment to يسبح, an argument for a fresh parse. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 24:36/q01/p8 It attaches to “His name be remembered” as an adverbial expression. — Kur’an Yolu states this; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Samīn reject direct attachment because يذكر is within أن and cannot govern a preceding phrase. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BAYDAWI-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 24:36/q01/p9 It begins a statement placing God in the houses. — Al-Tirmidhī al-Ḥakīm cites a report on a mosque sitter being in the company of his Lord and a Torah report about divine hospitality. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الترمذي الحكيم محمد بن علي)]

## 24:36/q02 How can a singular niche and lamp appear in plural houses?  [فِى بُيُوتٍ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the parable depicts an individual object, a generic type, or a shift in address.
- 24:36/q02/p1 The singular denotes a type, with an instance in each house. — Al-Rāzī says matching lamps illuminate each worship house at night; al-Bayḍāwī and Ibn ʿĀshūr call the objects generic; al-Biqāʿī says the singular fittings convey the light's rarity. [7 notes: THALABI(بعضهم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم بن بحر الأصفهاني)-]
- 24:36/q02/p2 It is a shift from singular to plural address. — Al-Thaʿlabī cites 33:1; al-Qurṭubī cites 65:1. [3 notes: THALABI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 24:36/q02/p3 A plural setting can contain a singular object in one member, just as the moon is said to be in the heavens. — Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Qurṭubī cite 71:16. [2 notes: THALABI(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 24:36/q02/p4 The mismatch is an objection to placing the lamp in the houses. — Abū Muslim says one lamp cannot occupy every house; he also says a mosque setting adds no brightness. Rāzī counters that the glass lamp in a mosque is grander and completes the parable. [3 notes: THALABI(قائل); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم بن بحر الأصفهاني); RAZI-FULL-]

## 24:36/q03 What does بُيُوتٍ mean as an Arabic word?  [بُيُوتٍ · meaning]
- 24:36/q03/p1 It means dwellings or places in which something resides. — Al-Rāghib says bayt originally designated a person's night dwelling, then any dwelling; buyūt is for houses, abyāt for poetry, and the word can extend to any locus. [1 notes: BURSEVI(المفردات)]

## 24:36/q04 How is the first consonant of بُيُوتٍ read?  [بُيُوتٍ · readings]
turns on: The variant is dialectal and has no difference of sense.
- 24:36/q04/p1 With ḍamma on the bāʾ. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī report this alongside kasra. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 24:36/q04/p2 With kasra on the bāʾ. — Qirāʾāt Encyclopedia calls it dialectal, without semantic change. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER(vw)]

## 24:36/q05 Which houses does the verse mean?  [بُيُوتٍ · referent]

- 24:36/q05/p1 Mosques generally. — Mujāhid, Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda and many others identify mosques. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān prefer this because men pray and remember God there. Rāzī objects to including houses not authorized for such use. Several reports call mosques God's houses and compare their light for heaven's people to stars. Kur’an Yolu clarifies that calling the Kaʿba God's house is metaphorical: God needs no place, and prayer and circumambulation mark its dedication. - 24:36/q05/p12 The believer's prayers or body, figured as a mosque. — Al-Bayḍāwī and Abū Ḥayyān allow this symbol; al-Ālūsī finds it unpersuasive. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]
- 24:36/q05/p13 Three particular mosques, with the indefinite plural indicating exaltation. — Al-Bayḍāwī reports this without naming all three. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 24:36/q06 What does God’s أَذِنَ convey here?  [أَذِنَ ٱللَّهُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether permission is command, mere allowance and enablement, or inspiration.
- 24:36/q06/p1 God commanded or decreed it. — Muqātil, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Kashshāf, al-Qurṭubī and others give command; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī add that the root includes knowledge and enabling without prohibition. Al-Ālūsī says calling command permission suggests an already eager addressee. [9 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 24:36/q06/p2 God made it permissible, informed people of that permission, and enabled it. — Bursevī defines izin as granting leave; Biqāʿī includes permission, enablement, recommendation and obligation. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 24:36/q06/p3 God inspired builders to erect them for worship. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says monasteries were not commanded in original Christianity but were not forbidden, and good intent made their construction pleasing to God; he cites 22:40 and 57:27. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 24:36/q07 What does it mean for the houses to be raised?  [أَن تُرْفَعَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether رُفع is physical building or spiritual honor, and whether the wording presupposes existing buildings.
- 24:36/q07/p1 They are built or physically elevated. — Mujāhid and others compare Abraham raising the House's foundations in 2:127; al-Kashshāf adds 79:28. Al-Ṭabarī prefers common architectural usage. Ibn ʿĀshūr argues lofty monastic cells suit this sense and rejects spiritual elevation as remote from the passage. ## 24:36/q09 What does remembering God’s name in the houses comprise?  [وَيُذْكَرَ فِيهَا ٱسْمُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the wording is general or specifies Qur’an, monotheism, divine names, or prayer.
- 24:36/q09/p1 Every kind of remembrance and religious worship or study. — Al-Ṭabarī, Rāzī, al-Kashshāf, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī favor the unrestricted wording; Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes speech and action, al-Bayḍāwī God's acts and rulings, Iṣlāḥī acts to raise religion's banner. Bursevī lists tawḥīd, Qur’an, teaching, adhān and iqāma. - 24:36/q09/p2 Reciting God's Book. — Ibn ʿAbbās repeatedly specifies recitation; al-Ṭabarī treats it as an instance of the general wording. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 24:36/q09/p3 Affirming God's oneness, especially “there is no god but God.” — Muqātil, al-Kalbī and Ibn ʿAbbās give this specification. [6 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 24:36/q09/p4 Mentioning God's beautiful names. — Ibn Jarīr and later sources give this specification; Bursevī notes a view that divine names need transmitted warrant. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جرير); TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI(بعض العلماء)]
- 24:36/q09/p5 Performing prayer. — Abū Rawq and others interpret name-remembrance here as prayer. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو روق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 24:36/q09/p6 Avoiding improper or obscene speech. — Al-Wāḥidī and Rāzī include guarding the houses from unsuitable utterances. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL]

## 24:36/q10 How is the coordination of raising houses and remembering God's name understood?  [أَن تُرْفَعَ وَيُذْكَرَ فِيهَا ٱسْمُهُۥ · grammar]

## 24:36/q18 What does the verse teach about constructing and maintaining mosques?  [بُيُوتٍ أَن تُرْفَعَ · law]
- 24:36/q18/p1 Mosques should be established, maintained, cleaned, perfumed and lit for worship. — ʿĀʾisha and Samura report a command to build neighborhood mosques and clean and perfume them. ## 24:36/q20 May mosques be decorated or built with lavish ornament?  [أَن تُرْفَعَ · law]
turns on: Whether decoration honors the house or distracts worshippers and encourages rivalry.
- 24:36/q20/p1 Decoration is permitted as an act of honoring mosques. — Al-Qurṭubī records ʿUthmān's teak building, Abū Ḥanīfa's allowance of gold wash, ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz's work, al-Walīd's Damascus expenditure and Solomon's Jerusalem ornament. - 24:36/q20/p2 Ornament that distracts, encourages boasting, or copies other communities should be avoided. — ʿUmar asks for shelter without red or yellow distraction; reports say the Prophet was not ordered to embellish mosques, and Anas and Ibn ʿAbbās warn of competitive ornament. ## 24:36/q21 What cleanliness and protection are required inside mosques?  [أَن تُرْفَعَ · law]
- 24:36/q21/p1 Keep out filth, spit, bad smells, bodily harm and actions that soil or endanger worshippers. — Al-Qurṭubī cites garlic, onion and leek reports; angels are harmed by human odors. Spitting is called sin, with covering or burying its expiation; al-Ālūsī adds scraping and perfuming the qibla wall, excluding louse remains and bodily waste, and cautions against bloodletting and ablution inside. Ibn Kathīr explains weapons, raw meat and penalties by injury or impurity risks. [33 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو ذر,أبو سعيد الخدري,أبو موسى,أنس,ابن عمر,جابر بن عبد الله,واثلة بن الأسقع); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عمر,واثلة بن الأسقع); DURR-FULL(النبي ﷺ); ALUSI-FULL(أنس,الشعبي,رجل من الأنصار,مالك,واثلة بن الأسقع)]
- 24:36/q21/p2 Keep ritually impure persons from entry or transit where the mosque would be dishonored. — Al-Ālūsī reports jurists prohibiting menstruating, postpartum and major-impurity entry even in transit; Ibn Kathīr warns against passage when soiling is feared. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 24:36/q22 What speech and activity belong in mosques?  [وَيُذْكَرَ فِيهَا ٱسْمُهُۥ · law]
turns on: Whether ordinary conversation and worldly affairs fit houses designated for remembrance.
- 24:36/q22/p1 Prayer, Qur’an, praise, learning and reverent conversation are the proper uses; worldly chatter and disruption are discouraged or barred. — The Prophet tells Muʿāwiya that mosques are for glorification, magnification and Qur’an; he answers the lost-camel caller that mosques were built for their purpose. Qurtubī and Ibn Kathīr extend this to trade, disputes, shouting, penalties and ordinary talk. Abū ʿUthmān urges entrants to clear other concerns; a report says worldly mosque talk consumes good deeds. [33 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان); QURTUBI-FULL(أنس,ابن المسيب,خلف بن أيوب,رسول الله,سليمان بن بريدة عن أبيه,عمر بن الخطاب,معاوية بن الحكم السلمي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن عمر,بريدة,عمر بن الخطاب,كثير من العلماء,واثلة بن الأسقع); BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(النبي ﷺ); ALUSI-FULL(ثوبان,عبد الله بن عمر,واثلة بن الأسقع)]
- 24:36/q22/p2 Some buying and selling in a mosque is allowed. — Al-Tirmidhī reports this view from some Successors; it contrasts with Aḥmad and Isḥāq's disapproval. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(بعض أهل العلم من التابعين)]

## 24:36/q23 Is poetry allowed in a mosque?  [وَيُذْكَرَ فِيهَا ٱسْمُهُۥ · law]
turns on: Whether the prohibition on poetry is absolute or depends on its content.
- 24:36/q23/p1 Poetry in mosques is forbidden or discouraged. — The report of ʿAmr b. Shuʿayb forbids poetry with trade; Thawbān's report prescribes a repeated rebuke. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عمرو بن شعيب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عمرو بن شعيب عن أبيه عن جده); DURR-FULL(النبي ﷺ); ALUSI-FULL(ثوبان)]
- 24:36/q23/p2 Good poetry is allowed, including praise, defense of faith, exhortation and wisdom; obscene or frivolous verse is barred. — Al-Qurṭubī cites Prophetic permission for poetry and the principle that good poetry is good speech, while rejecting obscenity and empty frivolity; he notes Shāfiʿī attribution of a Prophetic saying. Al-Ālūsī similarly distinguishes blameworthy satire, erotic and wine verse from edifying verse. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله); ALUSI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL+]

[9 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة,خلف بن أيوب,عمر بن الخطاب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(إبراهيم بن عبد الرحمن بن عوف,السائب بن يزيد الكندي,كثير من العلماء); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 24:36/q24/p2 Instruction and judicial disputes may justify raised voices. — Abū Ḥanīfa, his companions and Muḥammad b. Maslama allow this; al-Qurṭubī rejects necessity. [0 notes: QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 24:36/q25 May people sleep or take refuge in a mosque?  [بُيُوتٍ · law]
- 24:36/q25/p1 Needy travelers and homeless men or women may sleep there. — Al-Qurṭubī cites the Suffa, Ibn ʿUmar as a bachelor, a woman sheltered there, and ʿAṭāʾ reportedly dwelling in a mosque for forty years. A report forbids denying residents or guests a midday rest. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عائشة); DURR-FULL(النبي ﷺ)]

## 24:36/q26 What should a person say on entering and leaving a mosque?  [بُيُوتٍ · reports]
- 24:36/q26/p1 Ask for God's mercy on entry and bounty on exit, with reported additions of forgiveness, blessings on the Prophet, greetings and refuge from Satan. — Reports from Abū Ḥumayd or Abū Usayd, Fāṭima, Abū Hurayra and ʿAbd Allāh b. ʿAmr vary in their wording; Ibn Kathīr notes the Fāṭima chain is disconnected. Satan reportedly says one seeking refuge is protected for the day. [12 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حميد أو أبو أسيد,أبو داود,أبو هريرة,عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص,فاطمة بنت رسول الله); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو حميد أو أبو أسيد,أبو هريرة,عبد الله بن عمرو,فاطمة بنت رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); DURR-FULL(فاطمة بنت رسول الله)]

## 24:36/q27 Should a mosque entrant pray two rakʿas before sitting?  [بُيُوتٍ · law]
turns on: Whether the entry prayer is recommended or obligatory.
- 24:36/q27/p1 The two-rakʿa greeting is recommended. — Abū Qatāda reports the Prophet's command and example; most scholars and al-Qurṭubī take it as recommendation. Al-Qurṭubī rejects obligation because that would bar an unabluted entrant; he also rejects an unsupported addition requiring the same prayer at home. [7 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو قتادة,رسول الله); DURR-FULL(النبي ﷺ); ALUSI-FULL(أبو قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 24:36/q27/p2 The two-rakʿa entry prayer is obligatory. — Dāwūd and followers hold this; al-Qurṭubī rejects the consequence for entrants without ablution. [0 notes: QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 24:36/q28 May women attend mosques, and where is their prayer preferable?  [بُيُوتٍ · law]
- 24:36/q28/p1 Women's prayer is better in their homes, especially an innermost room. — Ibn Kathīr cites reports from Ibn Masʿūd, Umm Salama and Umm Ḥumayd, including her private home prayer place. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أحمد وأبو داود,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 24:36/q28/p2 Women may attend mosque congregations without adornment or perfume that harms others. — The Prophet says not to prevent God's female servants; reports prohibit perfume, and ʿĀʾisha recalls women attending dawn wrapped and unrecognized. She also says later innovations would have changed the Prophet's judgment. [6 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أحمد وأبو داود,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عائشة)]

## 24:36/q29 What virtues and signs attach to regular mosque attendance?  [بُيُوتٍ · reports]
- 24:36/q29/p1 Attendance marks faith and brings divine protection, light, relief and reward. — Reports cite 9:18, promise light on Judgment Day for dark walks, liken mosque regulars to the pious at home, and picture mosques at the gathering as luminous mounts. Another report promises pilgrim-like reward for walking to obligatory or forenoon prayer. [13 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو إدريس الخولاني,أبو الدرداء,أبو صادق الأزدي,أنس بن مالك,رسول الله,مالك بن دينار); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الدارقطني,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو أمامة); DURR-FULL(النبي ﷺ)]

## 24:36/q30 What special reports describe a mosque's light, lamps and visitors?  [بُيُوتٍ · reports]
- 24:36/q30/p1 Mosques shine to heaven's people and their lamps or attendants receive reported rewards. — Ibn ʿAbbās likens them to stars; an Anas report promises angelic prayers for a lamp lighter and reward for sweeping; Tamīm al-Dārī is reported as first lighting the Prophet's mosque with Syrian lamps, though Ibn ʿĀshūr dates that after the verse. Reports also describe praise of the visitor after ablution. [6 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري,أبو هند,أنس); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 24:36/q31 What other conduct is forbidden or disputed in mosques?  [بُيُوتٍ · law]
- 24:36/q31/p1 Keep out marketplaces, lost-item calls, weapons, punishments, unsafe arrows, idle passage, disruptive children, crafts and misuse of mosque property. — Prophetic reports and later jurists give these rules. Al-Qurṭubī calls the craftsman report unpreserved because Muḥammad b. Mujīb is abandoned but approves its sense. Ibn Kathīr says passage without need is disliked, raw meat can drip, and judges' arguments make court sessions unsuitable; a cluster of reports warns that removed mosque pebbles complain, cry or curse. [40 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو موسى,أحمد وإسحاق,أنس,بعض أصحابنا,سليمان بن بريدة عن أبيه,علي بن أبي طالب,عمرو بن شعيب,عيسى ابن مريم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن عمر,بعض العلماء,عمر بن الخطاب,كثير من العلماء,واثلة بن الأسقع); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود,النبي ﷺ,سعيد بن جبير,سليمان بن يسار,كعب,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمر,عبد الله بن مسعود,واثلة بن الأسقع)]

## 24:36/q32 How does the houses passage develop the preceding parable and the following verses?  [* · links]
- 24:36/q32/p1 The illuminated believer of 24:35 is made visible in mosque worship and conduct, contrasted with darkness or distracted unbelievers. — Ibn Qutayba says the parable depicts the believer's heart lit by faith and Qur’an; Ibn Kathīr and Elmalılı place that light in mosques. Iṣlāḥī says faith's cause is followed by lived effect, opposed to unbelievers' darkness; Ibn ʿĀshūr says recitation and study show Qur’anic light in believers. The Day of Resurrection of the following passage is named by Zayd b. Aslam. [13 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(أبي,المفسرون); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,زيد بن أسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 24:36/q32/p2 The houses also recall other places where God's name is mentioned, and mosque law elsewhere. — Study Quran cites 22:40's monasteries, churches, synagogues and mosques; Ibn Kathīr links dress, sincere worship and not calling on others at mosques to 7:31, 7:29 and 72:18. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 24:36/q33 What inward reading is offered for the house and light image?  [* · inward]
- 24:36/q33/p1 Hearts and spiritual stations are houses of gnosis, will, love and witnessing, raised through God-guided practice. — Qushayrī distinguishes worship leading to reward from aspiration reaching God; Qāshānī describes soul, heart, secret and spirit through remembrance. Bursevī says absolute Light is ultimately known only by God. [7 notes: QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; BURSEVI]
- 24:36/q33/p2 Mosques are niches, men the glass, hearts lamps and their meanings radiance; another report applies niche, glass, lamp, tree and oil to Muhammad and Abraham. — Biqāʿī records both the inward analogy and an Ibn ʿUmar report without grading. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(ابن عمر)]

## 24:36/q34 What rhetorical or grammatical principles do the cited comparanda supply?  [* · rhetoric]
- 24:36/q34/p1 A parable may be enhanced by setting, vivid detail and an indirect semantic step. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the verse with Ṭarafa and Kaʿb, says it combines tajrīd and explanation, and invokes a Prophetic thorn image. ʿAbd al-Qāhir argues figurative expression conveys an extra semantic step, unlike a dictionary gloss, and says repetition can make originally ingenious comparison feel ordinary. [13 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(كعب بن زهير); JURJANI-ASRAR; JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 24:36/q34/p2 An apposition or fresh predication can resolve what is meant by a following noun or an implied question. — Sībawayh illustrates definite-for-indefinite apposition, correction and fresh predication; ʿAbd al-Qāhir says implicit questions often need a repeated verb, though Burhān finds the passive reading here a challenge. Al-Itqān discusses which omitted clause is preferable. [11 notes: SIBAWAYH; BURHAN(عبد القاهر); ITQAN(عبد القاهر)]

## 24:36/q35 What other cited discussions accompany this verse in the collected sources?  [* · interpretation]
- 24:36/q35/p1 Several excerpts discuss general Qur’anic grammar, recitation practice and sura naming rather than an additional claim about 24:36. — Rāzī explains why even brief formulae require many grammatical and theological inquiries; Burhān compares analyses of ʿasā with 5:52, 17:79 and 2:216; al-Nashr debates takbīr at completion and tasbīḥ prayer reports; al-Itqān reconciles loud and quiet Qur’an recitation, favoring quiet where others are disturbed. Ibn ʿĀshūr details how sura titles arose, were transmitted and written, and reports competing gradings of a prohibition on saying “Sura of X.” [39 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURHAN(الكوفيون); NASHR(القاضي حسين); ITQAN(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,النووي,بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL(أحمد بن حنبل,أنس,ابن الجوزي,ابن حجر,ابن عباس,ابن عمر,ابن مسعود,الحجاج بن يوسف,الذين صححوا حديث أنس,المازري,عائشة)]

- 24:36/q36/p1 They are people who watch God's commands and seek His approval, undistracted by worldly affairs. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this description in connection with prayer and remembrance and the following verse. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 24:36/q37 How do other verses complete the prayer times mentioned here?  [بِٱلْغُدُوِّ وَٱلْءَاصَالِ · links]

## 24:36/q39 How does the light image portray a group studying the Qur’an?  [* · rhetoric]
- 24:36/q39/p1 The arrangement of people, objects and relations portrays a gathering that becomes illuminated through reciting and reflecting on revelation. — Kur’an Yolu reports Ibn ʿĀshūr's preferred image and cites the Prophetic report that such a gathering receives tranquility, mercy and divine mention.
