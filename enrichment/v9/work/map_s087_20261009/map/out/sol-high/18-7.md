# 18:7: 20 questions

## 18:7/q01 How does jaʿalnā govern mā and zīnatan?  [جَعَلْنَا مَا عَلَى ٱلْأَرْضِ زِينَةًۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether jaʿala means making something into something or creating it, and whether zīna expresses result, purpose or circumstance.
- 18:7/q01/p1 Jaʿalnā means 'made'; mā and zīnatan are its two objects. — Al-Naḥḥās reports this analysis. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān distinguish it from the 'created' analysis; al-Qurṭubī adopts it. Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī say transformation gives zīna the second-object role. [6 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:7/q01/p2 Jaʿalnā means 'created'; mā is its object and zīnatan states the purpose of creation. — Abū Jaʿfar al-Naḥḥās calls this a good view. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī make its syntax depend on the creation sense of jaʿala. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; NAHHAS+]
- 18:7/q01/p3 If jaʿalnā means 'created,' zīnatan can be a circumstantial accusative. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn give this alternative alongside the purpose accusative. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 18:7/q02 What does mā ʿalā l-arḍ include as the earth's adornment?  [مَا عَلَى ٱلْأَرْضِ زِينَةًۭ · referent]
turns on: How general mā is; whether humans belong to what is adorned or to those tested; and whether adornment means all creation, selected attractive goods, or spiritually distinguished people.
- 18:7/q02/p1 Everything on earth is its adornment. — Mujāhid's inclusive reading is repeated by al-Ṭabarī, al-Māwardī and others. Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī prefer its breadth: creation and design point to the Maker, so even ugly or harmful things may be called adornment. Al-Ālūsī answers objections that everything has some benefit, at least as evidence of the Maker. Al-Wāḥidī lists seas, mountains, plants and water; al-Baghawī includes snakes, scorpions and devils as signs of divine unity. [19 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; QURTUBI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 18:7/q02/p2 The broad sense embraces earthly animals, plants and minerals, with humans included or the noblest among them. — Al-Rāzī classifies earthly kinds as mineral, plant and animal and calls humans the noblest animals. Al-Ṭabrisī also includes inanimate life. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Burṣawī and the Study Quran list these kinds; al-Biqāʿī stresses visual and inward attraction. Al-Ālūsī allows humans as adornment in one respect and tested subjects in another, citing 18:46 against al-Qāḍī's exclusion. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 18:7/q02/p3 It broadly denotes nonrational things on earth, including animals, plants and minerals, while tested humans are excluded. — Abū Ḥayyān calls nonrational mā the apparent sense. Al-Ālūsī calls this apparent too. Al-Qāḍī excludes morally responsible humans because they are the tested subjects, but includes useful plants and animals; al-Ālūsī contests that exclusion with 18:46. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القاضي)+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 18:7/q02/p4 It refers especially to vegetation, trees, rivers and renewed growth. — Muqātil stresses annual renewal; al-Ḍaḥḥāk flourishing plants; al-Baghawī cites 10:24. Reports vary between vegetation alone, trees and rivers, and flowering plants. Al-Rāzī also reports adding minerals, gold and silver to a plant reading. [8 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:7/q02/p5 It denotes attractive worldly goods and useful things such as wealth, clothing, fruit, water and livestock. — Reports vary in their lists: trees, fruits, metals, vessels, animals and livestock in al-Jishumī; blessings, clothes, fruit, greenery and water in Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān; wealth and children in al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Kur'an Yolu. Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī stress luxuries pleasing inhabitants. Al-Ālūsī rejects such a list as exhaustive of the general wording. [13 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); RAZI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,قيل); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 18:7/q02/p6 It means people or men, who beautify the earth. — Al-Kalbī, Mujāhid and reports through Saʿīd ibn Jubayr from Ibn ʿAbbās give this reading; al-Rāzī notes people as the noblest earthly animal. Ibn al-Anbārī says mā stands for man on this reading. Al-Ālūsī finds a solely rational referent unlikely and suggests people can exemplify the whole. [14 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); JISHUMI(الضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جبير عن ابن عباس ومجاهد); TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جبير عن ابن عباس ومجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جبير والحسن)]
- 18:7/q02/p7 The earth's adornment is its prophets, scholars, saints or devout worshippers. — Al-Qāsim, Sahl and others identify spiritual people; al-Qushayrī says their value is perceived by insight, compares inhabitants' value to their home, and calls God's friends a source of safety. Reports extend the list to Qur'an preservers, God's knowers and lovers, and monotheists whose inward light brightens the horizons. Some versions include caliphs and rulers. [20 notes: SULAMI(الحسين,القاسم); MAWARDI-FULL(القاسم); QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس عن ابن أبي نجيح عن مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القاسم بن محمد); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جبير والحسن,بعضهم)]
- 18:7/q02/p8 It means rulers, caliphs, scholars and commanders. — ʿIkrima's report from Ibn ʿAbbās varies between caliphs, scholars and rulers or rulers, scholars and commanders; Ibn al-Anbārī makes mā stand for man on this personal reading. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عكرمة عن ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس عن عكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جبير والحسن)]
- 18:7/q02/p9 It is earthly desire and the things made attractive to human nature, including the body's powers and pleasures. — Al-Māwardī calls it desires appealing to inhabitants; al-Qāshānī includes the body's self, perception and powers; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes goods, children and status. These become attachments and a test. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QASHANI; TABATABAI]
- 18:7/q02/p10 It is what appears yellow in sunlight on earth. — Al-Awzāʿī supplies this visual gloss. [1 notes: ABUDAWUD(أبو عمرو الأوزاعي)]

## 18:7/q03 To whom does lahā assign the adornment?  [زِينَةًۭ لَّهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether lahā identifies the adorned earth or includes its inhabitants, and how its lām attaches syntactically.
- 18:7/q03/p1 It assigns adornment to the earth itself. — Al-Rāzī compares stars adorning the sky; al-Sāmarrāʾī compares flowers and growth to the adornment of the sky in 15:16 and 37:6, and cites 27:60. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 18:7/q03/p2 It is adornment for the earth and its inhabitants. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Burṣawī explicitly include inhabitants; Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī's formulation. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:7/q03/p3 The first lām can express the purpose of zīna, be extra, or modify an omitted adjective describing zīna. — Al-Samīn lists the three syntactic possibilities; al-Ālūsī allows attachment to zīna or a description of it. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:7/q04 What does the lām of linabluwahum attach to?  [جَعَلْنَا لِنَبْلُوَهُمْ · grammar]
- 18:7/q04/p1 It attaches to jaʿalnā whether that verb means 'created' or 'made.' — Al-Samīn gives the attachment under both senses; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī also attach the testing lām to jaʿalnā. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:7/q05 To whom do the pronouns in linabluwahum and ayyuhum refer?  [لِنَبْلُوَهُمْ أَيُّهُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether mā itself denotes people or the tested inhabitants must be supplied from context.
- 18:7/q05/p1 They refer to the earth's inhabitants understood from context. — Ibn al-Anbārī says the observers are intended if mā denotes plants. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn infer inhabitants from the context; al-Samīn also reports exegetical identifications as shepherds or scholars, righteous people and caliphs. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:7/q05/p2 They return to mā when it means a collective of rational beings. — Ibn al-Anbārī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī allow this on the human reading of mā. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:7/q06 How does God test people through earthly adornment?  [زِينَةًۭ لِنَبْلُوَهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the emphasis falls on manifest deeds under commands, disciplined use of benefits, grateful reflection, or response to spiritually distinguished people.
- 18:7/q06/p1 Adornment is the setting for moral responsibility; commands and prohibitions reveal obedience or disobedience for fitting recompense. — Ibn ʿAbbās says the trial is intelligible to creatures. Ibn Isḥāq and al-Ṭabrisī stress obedience; al-Jishumī says known deeds become manifest. Al-Biqāʿī contrasts sincere service and pleasure pursued in defiance. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says attractive attachments draw souls into earthly life before judgment. Al-Elmalılı emphasizes reward and punishment. [22 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(القاضي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن إسحاق); BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 18:7/q06/p2 Attractive benefits test whether people master desire, avoid prohibited use, and use lawful goods gratefully and rightly. — Abū Muslim says prohibited desire invites punishment or reward and permissible goods require gratitude and proper spending. ʿUmar prayed for help spending adornment rightly. Al-Ālūsī combines lawful use, thanks and reflection. Asad sees moral character in attitudes to material benefits; Iṣlāḥī contrasts reason directed to the Hereafter with desire for this world. [11 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي,أبو مسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(عمر); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 18:7/q06/p3 The trial distinguishes gratitude from unbelief and reflection and faith from rejection. — Al-Jishumī says blessings distinguish the grateful although God knows outcomes. Al-Qurṭubī connects reflection and belief to recompense at resurrection. Al-Biqāʿī says worldly desire conceals the call to infer the Creator's knowledge and power. [4 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:7/q06/p4 The adornment tests people's response to prophets and scholars who themselves adorn the earth. — Al-Qāsim ibn Muḥammad says the test is which recipients or imitators respond and accept best. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القاسم بن محمد)]

## 18:7/q07 Does God's testing mean He acquires knowledge of people's deeds?  [لِنَبْلُوَهُمْ · theology]
turns on: Whether divine foreknowledge is compatible with a real human test and alternate human action.
- 18:7/q07/p1 No; God already knows, and testing manifests deeds or treats people as an examiner would for just recompense. — Yaḥyā, al-Jishumī and al-Qāḍī say the outcomes are known beforehand. The majority reported by al-Rāzī affirm eternal knowledge of particulars; al-Ālūsī adds universals and rejects Hishām's contrary view. Ibn al-Jawzī describes God's dealing with creation as one who tests. Al-Ālūsī says reward then fits each person. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(القاضي,جمهور علماء الإسلام); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(هشام بن الحكم)-]
- 18:7/q07/p2 Yes; God learns events as they occur, since foreknown outcomes would prevent alternate action and make testing unreal. — Al-Rāzī reports Hishām ibn al-Ḥakam's argument. The majority and al-Ālūsī reject it on the ground of God's eternal knowledge. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(هشام بن الحكم); RAZI-FULL(جمهور علماء الإسلام)-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 18:7/q08 Does the purpose lām mean God's acts are directed toward ends?  [لِنَبْلُوَهُمْ · theology]
turns on: Whether a stated purpose implies a need or intermediary incompatible with divine power.
- 18:7/q08/p1 Yes; the lām of testing supports purposive divine action. — Al-Rāzī reports the Muʿtazilite appeal to the purpose-connoting lām. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); RAZI-FULL-]
- 18:7/q08/p2 No; divine acts cannot be caused by purposes. — Al-Rāzī's school argues that requiring an intermediary to attain a purpose would imply divine incapacity. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 18:7/q09 What does the test imply about human action and God's will?  [لِنَبْلُوَهُمْ أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًۭا · theology]
turns on: Whether meaningful testing entails human origination of deeds and how that relates to divine control of faith.
- 18:7/q09/p1 Human deeds originate with people, and God wills good action; otherwise testing and accountability would fail. — Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī use the verse against compulsion or determinism; Kur'an Yolu adds human will and moral discernment. Al-Qāḍī says duties were imposed for obedience, opposing the claim that some were created for Hell. [5 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(القاضي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 18:7/q09/p2 God alone places faith in hearts. — Al-Biqāʿī puts this alongside the attractiveness of earthly animals, minerals and plants. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 18:7/q10 What counts as the best deed in the test?  [أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether excellence is measured chiefly by detachment, obedient and lawful use, inward intention, intellectual restraint, reflection, or a specific spiritual discipline.
- 18:7/q10/p1 Renouncing worldly attachment and leaving its adornment without being deceived by it. — Abū ʿĀṣim, al-Ḥasan, al-Thawrī, Ibn ʿAṭāʾ and others variously stress abandoning adornment, detachment and resisting illusion; Tustarī adds humility, stillness, trust and turning to God. Al-Qurṭubī's asceticism reports refine its inward sense. Al-Ālūsī includes lawful use and reflection. The Study Quran reports al-Ghazzālī on detachment. [30 notes: TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(أبو عاصم العسقلاني); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الحسين,سهل); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عطاء); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن وأبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عاصم العسقلاني,سفيان الثوري); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عصام العسقلاني,سفيان الثوري); NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عاصم العسقلاني,الثوري,الزمخشري); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); DURR-FULL(الحسن,سفيان الثوري); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطاء,الحسن); STUDYQURAN(Abū Ḥāmid al-Ghazzālī)]
- 18:7/q10/p2 Following God's commands and doing the most obedient work. — Ibn Isḥāq defines it as following command; al-Ṭabrisī stresses productive obedience; al-Kalbī and others give obedience directly. Al-Jishumī says deeds become manifest for recompense. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); NAHHAS; JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن إسحاق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن إسحاق,الكلبي)]
- 18:7/q10/p3 Using one's allotted worldly goods lawfully and well, with faith, duties, restraint and voluntary good works. — Muqātil stresses wealth's best use. Ibn ʿAṭiyya's father specifies taking one's due, spending rightly, faith, obligations, avoiding prohibitions and recommended acts; al-Qurṭubī praises this and cites 'believe in God and remain upright.' Al-Bayḍāwī adds contentment. Al-Ālūsī combines renunciation, lawful use, gratitude and reflection. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو محمد); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو ابن عطية); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو بكر غالب بن عطية); ALUSI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 18:7/q10/p4 Showing the greatest trust and reliance on God amid worldly adornment. — Sahl gives this reading through al-Sulamī, al-Māwardī and Abū Ḥayyān; Tustarī lists trust among the qualities of withdrawal. [4 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل); MAWARDI-FULL(سهل بن عبد الله); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سهل)]
- 18:7/q10/p5 Having a pure heart, true intention, clear insight and rightly guided conduct. — Fāris stresses truth, purpose and intention; al-Qāsim stresses an empty heart, keen perception and guided comportment. Al-Qushayrī stresses intention and inward purity; al-Māwardī and Abū Ḥayyān report purity and conduct. [5 notes: SULAMI(القاسم,فارس); QUSHAYRI; MAWARDI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]
- 18:7/q10/p6 Possessing sound or complete intellect, avoiding God's prohibitions, and hastening to obedience. — A report attributed to the Prophet gives the three traits; Qatāda singles out complete intellect. Al-Ālūsī says the Prophetic answer is best; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites the same report through al-Durr. [5 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(النبي); TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 18:7/q10/p7 Avoiding what is forbidden among worldly adornments. — Al-Māwardī reports this criterion; Abū Muslim says resisting prohibited desire earns reward and indulging it punishment. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم)]
- 18:7/q10/p8 Learning the most from worldly adornment as a sign of its Maker. — Al-Māwardī gives the criterion; al-Biqāʿī says desire hides the invitation to infer divine knowledge and power; al-Ālūsī makes worldly beauty a mirror for divine majesty and beauty. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:7/q10/p9 Remaining steadfast on the Sunnah. — Sahl gives this measure in al-Sulamī, and al-Jishumī reports it as another definition. [2 notes: SULAMI(سهل); JISHUMI]
- 18:7/q10/p10 Counting one's own deed small and deficient before God's due, rather than relying on its reward. — Al-Qushayrī reports reward expectation as one measure but prefers the higher standard of minimizing one's act, supported by a cited poem. Al-Sulamī reports forgetting self and deed, annihilation in the Real, and refraining from adorning oneself with deeds. [3 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); QUSHAYRI+]
- 18:7/q10/p11 Doing the deed while expecting and counting on its reward. — Al-Qushayrī reports this measure, then ranks awareness of one's deed's deficiency still higher. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 18:7/q10/p12 Affirming servanthood in both speech and action. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ gives this definition. [1 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
- 18:7/q10/p13 Engaging worldly adornment without becoming preoccupied by it. — Al-Junayd gives this inward reading. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الجنيد)]
- 18:7/q10/p14 Revering God's knowers and lovers, who adorn the earth. — An anonymous inward reading reported by al-Ālūsī combines their status as adornment with the proper response to them. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 18:7/q11 What constitutes asceticism toward the world?  [أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether renunciation is brevity of hopes, an inward preference, outward withdrawal, freedom from praise, or readiness for death.
- 18:7/q11/p1 Short hopes and contentment with sufficient provision. — Sufyān al-Thawrī says it need not mean rough food or clothing; al-Qurṭubī says short hopes foster sufficiency. Al-Ālūsī ranks contentment with a traveler's provision highest. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(سفيان الثوري); ALUSI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 18:7/q11/p2 Renunciation in the heart, so leaving the world is dearer than acquiring it. — Ibrāhīm ibn Adham requires preference for leaving; Ibn al-Mubārak locates renunciation inwardly. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(إبراهيم بن أدهم,ابن المبارك)]
- 18:7/q11/p3 Abandoning the whole world whether one likes doing so or not. — Al-Fuḍayl gives this criterion. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(فضيل)]
- 18:7/q11/p4 Disliking praise and commendation. — Al-Awzāʿī defines renunciation this way. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الأوزاعي)]
- 18:7/q11/p5 Avoiding association with people. — Bishr ibn al-Ḥārith equates love of the world with love of meeting people; al-Fuḍayl calls withdrawal a sign. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(بشر بن الحارث)]
- 18:7/q11/p6 Loving death. — An unnamed group gives this definition; al-Qurṭubī says short hopes encompasses it. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(فرقة)]

## 18:7/q12 How is the interrogative ayyuhum parsed after linabluwahum?  [لِنَبْلُوَهُمْ أَيُّهُمْ أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether ayy is an interrogative opening a suspended question or a relative pronoun with an omitted relative-clause subject.
- 18:7/q12/p1 Ayyuhum is an interrogative nominative subject, aḥsanu its predicate, and the whole question is suspended as the testing verb's object. — Al-Zajjāj says the question retains initial position. Al-Jishumī cites 18:12: an interrogative is not governed by preceding material. Al-Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī explain that testing evokes knowing, asking or consideration. Al-Burṣawī adds ʿamalan as specification. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:7/q12/p2 Ayyuhum is a relative pronoun meaning 'the one who'; aḥsanu predicates an omitted huwa, and the relative phrase substitutes for the tested object. — Abū Ḥayyān reports Sībawayh's analysis. Al-Samīn says its nominative can be built on ḍamma when the relative clause's head is omitted, citing 19:69, or inflected, as an anomalous Maryam reading shows; a poem about greeting the best of Banū Mālik attests nominative ayyuhum. Al-Ālūsī notes that Sībawayh permits rather than requires building and finds the point difficult. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سيبويه); SAMIN-DURR(سيبويه); ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:7/q13 Why say 'best' when the trial includes bad deeds?  [أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًۭا · rhetoric]
- 18:7/q13/p1 Excellence is the primary aim, so 'best' is apt even though the trial also discloses bad deeds. — Al-Ālūsī makes the aim of revealing the excellent decisive. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:7/q14 Is aḥsanu a true comparative?  [أَحْسَنُ · grammar]
- 18:7/q14/p1 Yes; the comparison is omitted: better than people whose deeds are less good. — Al-Ālūsī supplies the implicit comparison. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:7/q15 What follows from knowing God personally manages affairs?  [أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًۭا · inward]
- 18:7/q15/p1 The wise see their true condition, disdain the world, become content and turn away from it. — Abū ʿAlī al-Rūdhbārī links this response to knowledge of God's personal governance of affairs. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو علي الروذباري)]

## 18:7/q16 How does this verse relate to the removal of adornment in 18:8?  [* · links]
- 18:7/q16/p1 The next verse shows that earthly beauty is temporary: God will strip it away and leave barren ground. — Al-Wāḥidī cites 18:8. Al-Ṭabrisī glosses ṣaʿīd as earth's surface or a plantless road and juruz as barren, consuming plants; al-Jishumī adds soil or level ground. Al-Ṭabrisī cites Arabic jaraz/ajrāz, al-jazar for a drought year, and a rajaz line. Ibn Kathīr contrasts transience with permanent abode; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says attachments are removed before judgment. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 18:7/q17 How does the verse console or guide the Prophet amid rejection?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the consolation is framed around the audience's trial or the Prophet's inward response to rejection.
- 18:7/q17/p1 Worldly attractiveness is a test, so rejection by some does not show a failure of his message and he should continue calling them. — Al-Qāḍī in al-Rāzī compares God's continued provision for disbelievers with Muḥammad's continued preaching. Al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī and Abū Ḥayyān say some will believe and some refuse. Iṣlāḥī says unbelief is neither a defect in the Book nor a shortcoming in preaching; Asad links refusal to worldly pride, citing 16:22. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 18:7/q17/p2 The Prophet's grief led him to blame his own spiritual state, and the verse consoles that compassionate self-scrutiny. — Al-Qāshānī says he sought to subdue the self from compassion and reverence when people rejected the Qur'an. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 18:7/q18 How does 18:7 connect to other passages of al-Kahf or other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Which later narrative or verse brings the earlier claim about earthly adornment into view.
- 18:7/q18/p1 The cave sleepers mirror humanity's awakening from a long seeming day of absorption in worldly adornment. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī invokes 23:114 and 46:35 for the short seeming duration. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 18:7/q18/p2 The command to recite after the cave story resumes the earlier argument about earthly adornment. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects 18:27 to the earlier statement, followed by unchanged divine words and absence of refuge. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 18:7/q18/p3 God's enduring reality makes Him better in reward and outcome than vanishing worldly adornment. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects 18:42–44 to 18:7–8: people need something to rely on, and their Lord endures. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 18:7/q18/p4 The verse's themes are also discussed under 11:7. — Ibn al-Jawzī points readers to four earlier views there without detailing them in this note. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 18:7/q19 What reports illustrate the lure of the earth and the worth of deeds?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports illustrate different outcomes or tests: temptation, afterlife reward, refused faith, greed, neglected alms and the state of creation.
- 18:7/q19/p1 The world is green or lush and sweet; God makes people successors in it to test their action and warns against worldly temptation, especially women. — Qatāda and Abū Saʿīd transmit versions of the Prophetic report; Ibn Kathīr adds that women's temptation first tested Israel. Al-Qurṭubī notes two Abū Saʿīd reports in Muslim about the dangers of worldly blossoms and blessings. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة عن النبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(رسول الله); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو سعيد)]
- 18:7/q19/p2 Paradise's people would not return for all earthly goods, except a martyr who would return to be killed again for the honor seen. — The reports vary: some say the martyr would return and be killed ten times; others speak of the righteous dead generally. Ibn Ḥibbān and Muslim frame the report as the greater worth of reward than all earthly possessions. [8 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNHIBBAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BUKHARI(أنس بن مالك عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); MUSLIM(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 18:7/q19/p3 A person in Hell would offer everything on earth as ransom despite having refused the lesser pledge not to associate partners with God. — The Musnad report sets all earthly wealth against a refused demand of faith. [1 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 18:7/q19/p4 Greedy accumulation is insatiable and dangerous, while sufficient provision and contentment bring success. — Al-Qurṭubī reports the Prophet's comparison with a person who eats without becoming full. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 18:7/q19/p5 Livestock owners who die without paying zakat leave an obligation unmet. — Muslim's report names camels, cattle and sheep as earthly wealth subject to conduct. [1 notes: MUSLIM(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 18:7/q19/p6 The Creator can make harmful animals harmless and visibly beautiful in an age of universal obedience. — Al-Biqāʿī says children will play with them in Jesus's time. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 18:7/q20 How does earthly adornment serve al-Kahf's larger argument?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the sūra's frame is its parables generally or its Makkan conflict and exhortation.
- 18:7/q20/p1 The sūra's parables contrast faith with undue attachment to worldly life, especially the story of the rich and poor men. — Asad calls earthly beauty as test the sūra's central idea. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 18:7/q20/p2 The passage addresses late Makkan conflict: Quraysh's worldly success blinds them, while the Prophet and companions should persevere. — Iṣlāḥī situates al-Kahf after a sūra exposing Jews and alongside Maryam exposing Christians; Quraysh, indirectly backed by Jews and Christians, risk rejecting clear truth and punishment. He also sees hints of migration and victories. Worldly pleasure and status are immediate, while pains are hidden and the Hereafter unseen, making reason and patience essential. [9 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
