# 2:28: 34 questions

## 2:28/q01 Who is addressed by the challenge to disbelief?  [تَكْفُرُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether address is limited to deniers or extends through admonition.
- 2:28/q01/p1 All people, including believers, can be warned. — Zajjaj invites believers to marvel; Razi counts four shared blessings, Baydawi permits a mixed or believers-only audience, and Elmalili includes sinners. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 2:28/q01/p2 Disbelievers, especially polytheists. — Muqatil and Tha'labi identify polytheists; Ibn Ashur excludes Jews, who affirm God and resurrection. Baydawi and Abu Hayyan note the turn to direct address. [5 notes: THALABI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:28/q01/p3 Hypocrites and People of the Book as well. — Tabari addresses professed faith masking denial and Jewish scholars; Qurtubi says rejecting Muhammad and the Quran is denial of God. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 2:28/q01/p4 Prophets, saints, or covenanting believers in inward readings. — Najmiyya applies the address to prophets, saints and Adamic particles answering the covenant. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 2:28/q02 What does the opening question do?  [كَيْفَ تَكْفُرُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the interrogative is rebuke, wonder, alert or information seeking.
- 2:28/q02/p1 It rebukes disbelief and denies that it has an excusable state. — Farra compares 81:26; Ibn al-Anbari cites al-Ajjaj; Zamakhshari and later grammarians argue that denying every state entails denying the act. Ibn Ashur asks the disbelievers for an excuse; the imperfect censures persistence. [38 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 2:28/q02/p2 It expresses wonder and asks listeners to marvel. — Zajjaj, Qushayri and others stress disbelief despite manifest signs; Burhan and Itqan treat kayfa as an indirect astonishment expression. Divine wonder can mean causing hearers to wonder. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة والزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(أبو الليث); ALUSI-FULL; BURHAN(الثمانيني); ITQAN]
- 2:28/q02/p3 It alerts or prompts reflection. — Raghib calls God's kayfa admonitory inquiry; Burhan reports alert rather than wonder. [4 notes: BURHAN(الراغب,بعضهم,غيره); ITQAN]
- 2:28/q02/p4 It asks for an account of their state. — Al-Qadi reports this alternative. [1 notes: BURSEVI(القاضي)]

## 2:28/q03 How is kayfa parsed?  [كَيْفَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether it is termed adverbial or circumstantial.
- 2:28/q03/p1 It asks about a state and is accusative under takfurun. — Ibn Qutayba, Wahidi, Jishumi, Tabarsi and Raghib contrast state with number, kind, place or essence. Nahhas explains its fatḥa; Sibawayh calls it adverbial and Akhfash circumstantial. Samin discusses substitution, omitted verb and governing construction. [17 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; BURHAN(سيبويه); ITQAN(الراغب); IBNASHUR-FULL; NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش,سيبويه); ALUSI-FULL(سيبويه)]
- 2:28/q03/p2 It is not strictly an adverb of place or time. — Ibn Malik rejects that label; Ibn Hisham allows it figuratively. Akhfash allows nominative with a subject, and Alusi discusses rare conditional use and rejected jussive or coordinating use. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن مالك,الأخفش); ITQAN]
- 2:28/q03/p3 It should retain interrogative force rather than assert obstinacy. — Samin objects to Abu al-Baqa's gloss mu'anidin. [2 notes: TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]

## 2:28/q04 How does kafara govern its object?  [تَكْفُرُونَ بِٱللَّهِ · grammar]
- 2:28/q04/p1 It normally takes bi, though direct government occurs when it means deny. — Samin cites 3:70, 41:41 and 11:68. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:28/q05 What is the force and scope of the waw clause?  [وَكُنتُمْ أَمْوَٰتًۭا فَأَحْيَٰكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the circumstantial scope includes future events.
- 2:28/q05/p1 It is circumstantial, with qad understood before the past verb. — Farra, Tabari and Zajjaj reconstruct wa-qad; examples include 4:90, 12:27, 12:26, 12:45 and 11:42. Wahidi reports Farra's rule for completed past states. [17 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:28/q05/p2 The circumstance spans the whole history through return. — Zamakhshari makes present knowledge of past and future the state; Baydawi says proofs confer effective knowledge, and Alusi reports this as an alternative. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:28/q05/p3 Only the first life clause is circumstantial; later clauses resume. — Abu Hayyan rejects forcing all future clauses into a present state; Samin's Sheikh calls the alternative strained, citing differing forms and conjunctions. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)+]
- 2:28/q05/p4 It may attach to an implied command to remember. — Biqai proposes this construction. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:28/q06 How does kana work here?  [وَكُنتُمْ أَمْوَٰتًۭا · grammar]
turns on: How the underlying verb and predicate are explained.
- 2:28/q06/p1 Kana is incomplete with amwatan as accusative predicate. — Nahhas and Qurtubi parse it; Wahidi and Jishumi also set out complete, extra and implicit-subject uses, comparing 19:29. It marks time in a nominal predication. [10 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 2:28/q06/p2 The noun kaynuna has competing derivations. — Farra treats it as an analogical exception among waw-root verbs; Khalil derives kaywanuna by assimilation and lightening. Farra prefers analogy. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الخليل); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)+]

## 2:28/q07 What was the initial dead state?  [أَمْوَٰتًۭا · referent]
turns on: Which pre-life, grave, or primordial event the adjective identifies.
- 2:28/q07/p1 Lifeless sperm or embryos before ensoulment. — Muqatil, Farra, Qatada and many others locate it in loins or womb; some include clot and morsel. Abu Hayyan prefers womb stages for immediate fa; Ibn Ashur traces elements, food, gametes, clot and morsel. Ibn Masud's Prophetic report gives successive forty-day periods before the spirit. A variant counts semen's departure from the man. Akhfash illustrates predication of former material with a garment as cotton or yarn and an unripe date. [44 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(غيره,قتادة); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وقتادة ومقاتل والفراء وثعلب والزجاج وابن قتيبة وابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,قتادة); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الساليكوتي); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; AKHFASH; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; TAB-FULL-]
- 2:28/q07/p2 People were nonexistent or nothing before creation. — Companion reports and Ibn Atiyya count nonexistence as first death; Ibn Atiyya and Samin prefer it because disbelievers accept the first three stages yet deny resurrection. Alusi contrasts 'you were dead' with God's later causing death. [32 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو العالية,أبو مالك,أصحاب النبي,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الله بن مسعود); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود ومجاهد); TABRISI(ابن عباس وابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,ابن مسعود، وابن عباس، ومجاهد); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس، ابن مسعود، مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي وابن مسعود وابن عباس وغيرهم,عبد الله بن مسعود); DURR-FULL(أبو العالية,ابن مسعود وناس من الصحابة,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; SAMIN-DURR+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 2:28/q07/p3 Dust or primal earthly matter. — Ibn Abbas through Dahhak traces people to Adam's dust; Razi distinguishes Adam from semen-derived descendants except Jesus. Baydawi and Bursevi include elements and food. [11 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(صاحب المنتخب); STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 2:28/q07/p4 Obscurity or being unremembered. — Tabari adduces Arabic usage and Abu Nukhayla's poem, while others cite 76:1 or a verse attributed to Abu Bujayla or al-Mukhabbal. One Ibn Abbas report specifies remembrance through Islam and the Prophet. Tabari prefers obscure embryos later known as humans. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(أبو نخيلة السعدي); RAZI-FULL(القفال,المخبل السعدي); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); TAB-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 2:28/q07/p5 The first dead state is ordinary death before grave questioning. — Abu Salih's sequence has life in the grave, another death, then resurrection. Tabari objects that a postmortem audience could not repent; Alusi calls the past-for-future reading remote. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو صالح); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو صالح); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وأبو صالح); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وأبو صالح,قائل); DURR-FULL(أبو صالح); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو صالح عن السدي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Ebü’l-Muîn en-Nesefî); TAB-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:28/q07/p6 It belongs to primordial covenant and extracted Adamic particles. — Ibn Zayd invokes 7:172–173, 4:1, 39:6, 4:154 and 5:7; Qurtubi gives expanded counts. Tabari objects that this creates three deaths and lives without an alleged death at extraction; Ibn Kathir calls it unusual. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد)-; IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 2:28/q07/p7 The first death is semen leaving the man's body. — A reported chronology starts with departure from the body, then ensoulment and ordinary death. Tabari says it lacks a trusted interpretive authority. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم); MAWARDI-FULL; TAB-FULL-]
- 2:28/q07/p8 Adam was lifeless clay before receiving spirit. — An attributed account begins at Adam's own formation and extends his life to descendants. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:28/q07/p9 It is unmanifest existence within divine knowledge. — Ibn Arabi distinguishes this from ordinary nothingness. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿArabī)]

## 2:28/q08 Is dead literal for matter without prior life?  [أَمْوَٰتًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether dead requires former life, present capacity, or mere absence.
- 2:28/q08/p1 Yes; dead can mean devoid of life. — Farra and Ibn Qutayba count separated sperm, hair and nails dead; Zamakhshari and Nasafi cite dead town and earth. Abu Hayyan cites 36:33, 41:39, 22:5 and 21:30; Qatada is reported to take it literally. [10 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(قتادة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الساليكوتي); BURSEVI(الكشاف); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:28/q08/p2 No; for inert pre-life matter it is figurative. — Most cited by Razi and lexicographers require previous life or capacity for life; Razi distinguishes mawāt from mayyit. Zamakhshari compares shared absence of spirit; Ibn Kathir compares dead idols and earth. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الأكثرون,القفال,المخبل السعدي); IBNASHUR-FULL(أئمة اللغة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الساليكوتي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL+]
- 2:28/q08/p3 A developing body capable of life can be called dead properly. — Ibn Ashur distinguishes the embryo from permanently inert stuff; Alusi says the decision depends on the definition of death. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(أئمة اللغة); ALUSI-FULL(الساليكوتي)]

## 2:28/q09 How is amwatan formed?  [أَمْوَٰتًۭا · grammar]
- 2:28/q09/p1 It is a broken plural of mayyit or its lightened form. — Zamakhshari and Nasafi compare aqwal from qil; Abu Hayyan also includes mayyita and notes expected fa'a'il. Samin prefers the lightened-form analysis; Burhan records accusative parallels and plural-of-paucity morphology. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; SAMIN-DURR+]

## 2:28/q10 What are life and death as states?  [أَمْوَٰتًۭا فَأَحْيَٰكُمْ يُمِيتُكُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether death is a positive entity and which level of life is intended.
- 2:28/q10/p1 Life is sensory or animate power; death is its absence or opposite. — Ibn Jawzi connects life with sensation and movement; Baydawi and Ibn Ashur discuss spirit, balanced temperament and embodied faculties. Alusi surveys sensation and equilibrium definitions, noting paralysis and distinct organs; Jishumi calls life an accident only God creates. [11 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; JISHUMI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 2:28/q10/p2 Life can describe growth, knowledge, faith or renown. — Baydawi, Alusi, Qushayri and Tabari describe figurative senses and corresponding deaths; 57:17 and 6:122 illustrate. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:28/q10/p3 Death is absence or privation, not a positive accident. — Abu Hashim denies a death accident; Ibn Ashur says death is nonexistence while life is positive, both created by God. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أبو هاشم); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:28/q10/p4 Divine life denotes God's perfection in knowledge and power. — Baydawi allows either predication through associated qualities or a real essential attribute; Alusi speaks of divine perfection. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:28/q10/p5 Death is a created positive accident opposed to life. — Jishumi reports the majority citing 67:2. [1 notes: JISHUMI(الأكثر)]

## 2:28/q11 What is the first act of giving life?  [فَأَحْيَٰكُمْ · interpretation]
- 2:28/q11/p1 God creates and ensouls people for womb and worldly life. — Qushayri describes differentiation into bone, flesh, hair and skin. Damghani says ahya can mean initial creation; Ibn Jawzi compares 3:27, 22:66, 40:11 and 45:26. [17 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:28/q11/p2 God makes what was nothing into living humans. — Companion reports stress bringing into being; Abu Hayyan calls it the greatest gift with continued provision and Tabari adds proof of oneness. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو العالية,أبو مالك,أصحاب النبي,مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود ومجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 2:28/q12 How are the object suffixes parsed?  [فَأَحْيَٰكُمْ يُمِيتُكُمْ يُحْيِيكُمْ · grammar]
- 2:28/q12/p1 Each -kum is an accusative object suffix. — Nahhas parses the suffixes of the three life-and-death verbs as objects. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 2:28/q13 How is the first life verb read?  [فَأَحْيَٰكُمْ · readings]
turns on: Whether imala depends on reader or preceding particle.
- 2:28/q13/p1 Al-Kisa'i inclines it by imala, with or without preceding fa or waw. — Ibn Mujahid connects alif-prefixed waw roots and compares 91:1 and 93:1; Ibn Khalawayh argues identical pronunciation warrants consistent treatment. [4 notes: IBNMUJAHID(الكسائي); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA]
- 2:28/q13/p2 Ibn Amir reads the forms open. — Ibn Mujahid reports complete opening. [1 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن عامر)]
- 2:28/q13/p3 Hamza inclines comparables after waw but not fa. — Ibn Khalawayh attributes this to ease and heaviness of connected contexts. [1 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA(حمزة)]

## 2:28/q14 Why fa for the first revival and thumma thereafter?  [فَأَحْيَٰكُمْ ثُمَّ يُمِيتُكُمْ ثُمَّ يُحْيِيكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 2:28/q14/p1 Fa marks close succession; thumma marks intervals. — Zamakhshari, Razi, Nasafi and Samarrai distinguish quick first life from later death, revival and judgment. Abu Hayyan prefers a chronology preserving fa; Bursevi allows embryonic stages between. Samarrai distinguishes thumma with ḍamma from locative thamma with fatḥa in 26:64. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 2:28/q14/p2 Death comes before life in wording to humble creatures and mark life's emergence. — Burhan gives humility, life's tendency to death, and the prior dead state; Biqai comments on 67:2. [3 notes: BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:28/q15 When does God cause the stated death?  [ثُمَّ يُمِيتُكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Which life ends at this stage.
- 2:28/q15/p1 At the end of worldly life or appointed term. — Tabari describes departure of soul, Qushayri reduction to bones and dust, and Baghawi and Baydawi the appointed lifespan. [14 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QUSHAYRI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 2:28/q15/p2 Again in the grave after questioning. — Abu Salih reports revival and another death; Qurtubi and Kuran Yolu allow this alternative. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو صالح); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو صالح); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وأبو صالح); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وأبو صالح); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو صالح عن السدي); DURR-FULL(أبو صالح); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Ebü’l-Muîn en-Nesefî)]
- 2:28/q15/p3 It is inward death of self or private witnessing. — Sulami, Qushayri and Qashani describe this spiritual stage. [5 notes: SULAMI(بعض البغداديين,فارس); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI]

## 2:28/q16 What is the second life?  [ثُمَّ يُحْيِيكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether renewed life occurs in grave, at resurrection or inwardly.
- 2:28/q16/p1 Bodily resurrection for judgment. — Early transmitters place it at the trumpet or Judgment Day; Tabari says restored souls and bodies recognize one another, Biqai says movement is restored, and Razi depicts trumpet, graves and awed silence. Qurtubi invokes 21:104. [48 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(أبو العالية,أصحاب النبي,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود,قتادة); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود ومجاهد); TABRISI(ابن عباس وابن مسعود); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وقتادة ومقاتل والفراء وثعلب والزجاج وابن قتيبة وابن الأنباري); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وأبو صالح,ابن مسعود، وابن عباس، ومجاهد,قتادة); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود وناس من الصحابة,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL]
- 2:28/q16/p2 Life in the grave for questioning or punishment. — Abu Salih and Ibn Abbas reports place life there. Jishumi says Paradise life would make return incoherent; Bursevi cites hearing departing footsteps and questioning, and says thumma excludes resurrection here. Baydawi, Zamakhshari and Nasafi permit both readings. [18 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو صالح); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو صالح); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وأبو صالح); TABRISI; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وأبو صالح,الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو صالح عن السدي); DURR-FULL(أبو صالح); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Ebü’l-Muîn en-Nesefî); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:28/q16/p3 True life after annihilation of self. — Sulami, Qushayri and Qashani describe life through God, knowledge, unveiled secrets or abiding through the Real. [10 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الشبلي,بعض البغداديين,بعضهم,فارس,قيل); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI]
- 2:28/q16/p4 Its realm may be earth, heaven, grave or resurrection. — Elmalili says humans cannot know the conditions, though revelation secures its occurrence. [2 notes: ELMALILI]

## 2:28/q17 Does the sequence settle life and punishment in the grave?  [ثُمَّ يُمِيتُكُمْ ثُمَّ يُحْيِيكُمْ · theology]
turns on: Whether the stated count exhausts all stages and how 40:11 relates.
- 2:28/q17/p1 Silence about grave life does not exclude it; grave punishment is affirmed. — Razi and Abu Hayyan answer the argument from silence. Abu Hayyan cites abundant sound reports: Sunnis say an unbeliever is revived for punishment and a sinner may be punished, while Karramiyya allow punishment while dead. Biqai allows awareness in barzakh; Bursevi's Taysir affirms punishment and comfort. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أهل السنة والكرامية); ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(التيسير); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 2:28/q17/p2 Grave life may be counted within worldly life, or not counted. — Qurtubi reports both practices; Hasan, Razi and Abu Hayyan cite exceptional repeated deaths and revivals, including 2:259, 2:243, 2:55, 2:73 and 2:260. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن)]
- 2:28/q17/p3 Two lives and deaths exclude intermediate grave life or punishment. — Razi and Abu Hayyan report this inference. Some deny that 40:11 proves otherwise because it is disbelievers' speech and might include a pre-world covenant; Zamakhshari's school denies grave questioning and punishment. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,قوم); RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 2:28/q17/p4 The verses positively support intermediate barzakh. — Tabatabai says 2:28 begins with a dead state but 40:11 has two acts of causing death; a barzakh life intervenes. He rejects the deniers' equation of the passages. [2 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+; TABATABAI(بعض المنكرين للبرزخ)-]

## 2:28/q18 How does this sequence relate to 40:11?  [أَمْوَٰتًۭا فَأَحْيَٰكُمْ يُمِيتُكُمْ يُحْيِيكُمْ · links]
turns on: Whether the two passages count the same episodes.
- 2:28/q18/p1 It gives the same two-death and two-life history. — Ibn Masud, Ibn Abbas, Mujahid and others align 40:11 with pre-world state, first life, worldly death and resurrection. Ibn Kathir compares 45:26, and Kuran Yolu prefers this reconciliation. [25 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(أبو مالك,ابن عباس,عبد الله,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,عبد الله بن مسعود); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود,قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود ومجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس، ابن مسعود، مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي وابن مسعود وابن عباس وغيرهم,الضحاك عن ابن عباس,عبد الله بن مسعود); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 2:28/q18/p2 The primordial covenant supplies another comparison. — Ibn Zayd links extraction and pledge to 7:172–173 and other verses; Tabari rejects the extra death it requires. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد)-]
- 2:28/q18/p3 The count does not exhaust exceptional cases. — Hasan, Razi, Abu Hayyan and Qurtubi mention people with more deaths and revivals. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن)]
- 2:28/q18/p4 The passages count different stages. — Tabatabai contrasts an initial dead condition here with two acts of causing death in 40:11, supporting barzakh; Razi notes the latter is disbelievers' speech. [3 notes: TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI+; TABATABAI(بعض المنكرين للبرزخ)-]

## 2:28/q19 What does 'to Him you return' mean?  [إِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the destination is judgment, a physical place, resurrection or inward encounter.
- 2:28/q19/p1 Return to God's judgment and recompense under His authority. — Tabari cites 36:51, Zajjaj 54:8 and 70:43, and Tabarsi compares an affair returning to its governor, excluding spatial movement. Qushayri adds prior decree; Wahidi says divine knowledge and decree determine outcome. [35 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو العالية); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 2:28/q19/p2 Arrival at the place of divine adjudication. — Mawardi, Abu Hayyan and Samin report this gloss. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:28/q19/p3 Resurrection or renewed life itself. — Qurtubi compares renewed creation with 21:104; Abu Hayyan reports reference to the earlier revival and helplessness. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:28/q19/p4 Return to decree, divine grasp or spiritual witnessing. — Qushayri speaks of decree and grasp; Qashani distinguishes witnessing attributes and vision of essence; Najmiyya gives prophets ascension and saints attraction. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 2:28/q19/p5 Physical movement to a place occupied by God. — Razi names the corporealist reading; he, Abu Hayyan and Alusi reject it because judgment explains the phrase. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(المجسمة); RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 2:28/q20 What is the antecedent of ilayhi?  [إِلَيْهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun points to God or implied first life.
- 2:28/q20/p1 God. — Ibn Atiyya prefers God over resurrection; Abu Hayyan and Samin cite the earlier divine pronouns and supply recompense as implied destination. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 2:28/q20/p2 The earlier revival or its implied helpless state. — Abu Hayyan and Samin report the alternative. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:28/q21 What does preposing ilayhi accomplish?  [إِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ · rhetoric]
- 2:28/q21/p1 Return is to God alone. — Ibn Ashur says idols cannot help the deniers at resurrection; Tabarsi names exclusive divine jurisdiction. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABRISI]

## 2:28/q22 How is turja'un read?  [تُرْجَعُونَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the verb makes people the agent or leaves God as understood agent.
- 2:28/q22/p1 Active: you return. — Ya'qub, with readers listed by Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan, uses intransitive raja'a; Bursevi suggests chosen return, while Ibn Ashur leaves voluntariness unstated. Abu Hayyan says the active loses agency parallelism. [12 notes: THALABI(يعقوب); JISHUMI(يعقوب); BAGHAWI-FULL(يعقوب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق); TABRISI(يعقوب); QURTUBI-FULL(يحيى بن يعمر وابن أبي إسحاق ومجاهد وابن محيصن وسلام بن يعقوب); BAYDAWI-FULL(يعقوب); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI(يعقوب); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد ويحيى بن يعمر وابن أبي إسحاق وابن محيصن والفياض بن غزوان وسلام ويعقوب)-]
- 2:28/q22/p2 Passive: you are returned. — The majority reading, preferred by Abu Hayyan, Samin and Alusi, preserves God's agency and verse-end cadence; Ibn Ashur says it depicts return despite denial. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); TABRISI(يعقوب); QURTUBI-FULL(يحيى بن يعمر وابن أبي إسحاق ومجاهد وابن محيصن وسلام بن يعقوب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER; JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور)+; ALUSI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]

## 2:28/q23 What other recitational detail occurs?  [تَكْفُرُونَ ثُمَّ يُمِيتُكُمْ · readings]
- 2:28/q23/p1 Ya'qub keeps a final ya at pause and continuation in comparable forms. — Nashr lists farhabun, fattaqun and takfurun. [1 notes: NASHR(يعقوب)]
- 2:28/q23/p2 A complete pause follows fa-ahyakum thumma yumitukum. — Qurtubi reports Abu Hatim's judgment. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حاتم)]

## 2:28/q24 What does the verse establish about God and resurrection?  [* · theology]
- 2:28/q24/p1 Creation, life and death show divine oneness and power to resurrect. — Tabari, Jishumi, Razi, Abu Hayyan and Ibn Kathir reason from first creation to restoration. Razi rejects stars, elements and temperaments as independent sources of life. Elmalili invokes repeatability in nature and human inability to create life; Kuran Yolu compares the inference of an agent from a moving stone or growing plant. Ibn Kathir cites 52:35–36 and 76:1; Biqai cites 36:81. [32 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; QUSHAYRI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:28/q24/p2 Available signs remove ignorance as an excuse. — Zamakhshari, Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi say rational and revealed proofs give effective knowledge even of future events. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TAB-FULL]
- 2:28/q24/p3 Justice and wisdom also require final reckoning. — Ibn Ashur says the resurrection clause may teach a conclusion or follow from a Creator who would not leave deeds unrewarded; prophets teach accountability. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 2:28/q25 Does the rebuke imply human creation of disbelief or divine determination?  [تَكْفُرُونَ · theology]
turns on: How divine causation and human blame fit together.
- 2:28/q25/p1 People produce their disbelief; God does not create or intend it. — Tabarsi infers ownership of the act from its attribution. Razi reports Mu'tazilite arguments: God-created unbelief would make rebuke and command incoherent like blame for illness; they cite 84:20 and 74:49. Other objections cite blame of Satan, Pharaoh and the Samaritan and prayers for protection. [9 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة,قالوا); JISHUMI]
- 2:28/q25/p2 Foreknowledge and a divine determinant can necessitate acts without voiding rebuke. — Razi argues foreknowledge makes its contrary impossible; a capacity for both outcomes still needs a determinant, and a servant-supplied one regresses. He mentions both compulsion and human acquisition. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 2:28/q26 Are life and death divine favors?  [فَأَحْيَٰكُمْ ثُمَّ يُمِيتُكُمْ ثُمَّ يُحْيِيكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether death and worldly life count as gifts for every addressee.
- 2:28/q26/p1 Life and the whole sequence are gifts calling for gratitude. — Razi compares duty to a nurturing parent; Jishumi insists worldly gifts to unbelievers remain gifts. Zamakhshari and Nasafi find signs and blessings; Baydawi says death leads to true life in 29:64, Tabarsi says it ends obligation and opens lasting reward. [18 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 2:28/q26/p2 The point is proof, and death need not count as a favor. — Tabarsi reports this view; Ibn Ashur says people welcome life but not the two deaths, so proof is the aim. [2 notes: TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:28/q26/p3 No favor is given to unbelievers destined for punishment. — Jishumi attributes this to Mujbira and rejects it; Razi reports the Mu'tazilite poisoned-food comparison. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); JISHUMI-]

## 2:28/q27 What practical warning follows?  [* · interpretation]
- 2:28/q27/p1 Remember mortality, repent, worship and prepare for judgment. — Tabari urges repentance; Razi depicts grave loneliness and Yaḥyā ibn Mu'ādh's plea for mercy. Abu Hayyan says return awakens fear and hope. Elmalili rejects clinging to pleasure, urges lawful gratitude, preparation and prayer for a good end. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم,يحيى بن معاذ الرازي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 2:28/q27/p2 Humanity was made for lasting life beyond death. — Biqai says death and life are paired, cites a sound report of death slaughtered and a report of Paradise's unending bliss, and sees death as passing while life endures. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 2:28/q28 How does the verse connect with its surah and nearby verses?  [* · links]
- 2:28/q28/p1 It resumes the worship call and creation argument. — Biqai links 2:21–22 to creation and prohibition of partners; Ibn Ashur says it returns to 2:21 after 2:25–27 and prepares 2:29, rejecting a local shift from 2:26. Tabatabai sees a second return to the opening call; Elmalili relates earth and heaven as habitation. [10 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI]
- 2:28/q28/p2 Parallel verses trace the life-and-return pattern. — Burhan lists amwatan in 3:169 and 77:26 and other formula variants. Ibn Kathir invokes 45:26; Tabatabai compares 32:7–11, 23:14 and 20:55. [6 notes: BURHAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 2:28/q28/p3 The Adam account warns and verifies prophethood. — Tabari says Adam and Iblis show repentance and pride, while Muhammad's inaccessible scriptural knowledge evidences mission; Muqatil says polytheists objected to resurrection after dust, prompting the next verse. [3 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL]
- 2:28/q28/p4 The account includes human reason and nature only insofar as they bear on guidance. — Tabatabai says the Quran selects journey details for guidance, happiness and misery, while human intellect and the subjection of creation frame the account. [3 notes: TABATABAI]
- 2:28/q28/p5 A later return verse, 2:281, is reported as last revealed. — Biqai describes 2:281 as the final comprehensive address to those facing judgment. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:28/q29 What inward states do death and life represent?  [أَمْوَٰتًۭا فَأَحْيَٰكُمْ ثُمَّ يُمِيتُكُمْ ثُمَّ يُحْيِيكُمْ · inward]
turns on: Which inward condition each temporal stage symbolizes.
- 2:28/q29/p1 Shirk or ignorance gives way to tawhid or knowledge. — Sulami and Abu Hayyan transmit movement from association to unity, ignorance to knowledge and discord to harmony; Qashani calls disbelief veiling. [6 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم,قيل); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أهل الحقائق)]
- 2:28/q29/p2 Ego-life dies and heart or secrets come alive. — Sulami's Baghdadi and Ibn Ata, Qushayri and Alusi describe ego-death, unveiling and the end of servant traits. Qushayri says realized people alternate subsistence and extinction. [7 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,بعض البغداديين); QUSHAYRI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطاء); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطاء)]
- 2:28/q29/p3 Own witnessing dies and divine witnessing begins. — Faris describes successive loss of private witnessing; Qashani speaks of annihilation in unity and abiding through the Real. [4 notes: SULAMI(فارس); QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فارس); ALUSI-FULL(فارس)]
- 2:28/q29/p4 Distance from God is death and being through Him life. — Al-Shibli's reading appears in Sulami and Abu Hayyan. [2 notes: SULAMI(الشبلي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشبلي)]
- 2:28/q29/p5 The rebuke is strongest because inert things do not contest God. — Al-Wasiti locates opposition in spiritual forms. [2 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); QURTUBI-FULL(الواسطي)]

## 2:28/q30 How is inward return understood?  [إِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ · inward]
turns on: Whether final inward return is witnessing, grasp, or ascent.
- 2:28/q30/p1 Return from personal witnessing to God and His sacred law. — Faris describes total return; Qushayri describes enactment through the Real and the servant in God's grasp. [5 notes: SULAMI(فارس); QUSHAYRI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فارس); ALUSI-FULL(فارس)]
- 2:28/q30/p2 Witnessing of attributes or vision of essence. — Qashani distinguishes the two unities. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 2:28/q30/p3 Prophets ascend and saints are drawn. — Najmiyya cites 89:28 and traces hidden existence, spirit formation, sense-bound death, revelation or faith and guidance by law. [6 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 2:28/q31 Does the verse allow reincarnation?  [أَمْوَٰتًۭا فَأَحْيَٰكُمْ يُمِيتُكُمْ يُحْيِيكُمْ · theology]
turns on: Whether initial dead implies a previous embodied life.
- 2:28/q31/p1 No; the sequence culminates in resurrection and recompense. — Kuran Yolu rejects scriptural, rational and scientific support for repeated embodiment. The two-of-each count and lack of conscious prior-body memory count against it; hypnosis reports may involve dreams, jinn, collective consciousness or memory errors. [7 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 2:28/q31/p2 Some infer prior embodied life from the initial word dead. — Kuran Yolu reports this inference but rejects it. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bazı kimseler); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]

## 2:28/q32 What postmortem report is cited?  [ثُمَّ يُمِيتُكُمْ ثُمَّ يُحْيِيكُمْ · reports]
turns on: Whether death in the cited report is literal.
- 2:28/q32/p1 Sinful believers in the Fire truly die, then revive after intercession. — Qurtubi cites Muslim's Abu Sa'id report: they become charcoal, then grow by Paradise's rivers like flood-borne seeds. He favors literal death because an emphatic verbal noun enforces literal sense, citing 4:164. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 2:28/q32/p2 The death is sleep-like concealment from pain. — Qurtubi reports this alternative but prefers the literal sense. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 2:28/q33 How is the rhetorical force of wonder classified?  [كَيْفَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Which rhetorical category explains the question.
- 2:28/q33/p1 Wonder is a secondary force of question or report. — Burhan and Itqan discuss taxonomies of two through ten speech kinds; Akhfash lists six. Ibn Faris, Ibn al-Da'i or al-Sa'igh, Zamakhshari and Rummani define wonder through exceptionality or obscure cause. Indirect forms include kayfa; Rummani also discusses ni'm and generic magnification. [18 notes: BURHAN(أبو الحسن الأخفش,ابن الضائع,ابن فارس,الثمانيني,الرماني,الزمخشري,قوم,قيل); ITQAN(ابن الصائغ,ابن فارس,الرماني,الزمخشري)]
- 2:28/q33/p2 Wonder or alert need not seek unknown information. — Burhan contrasts these functions; Alusi says divine questioning can make the addressee understand without divine ignorance. [6 notes: BURHAN(الراغب,بعضهم,غيره); ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:28/q34 What larger human journey does the verse describe?  [* · interpretation]
- 2:28/q34/p1 Human dependence leads to accountability and different final outcomes. — Elmalili says people cannot own or remake life and are judged by acquired deeds under 99:7–8. Tabatabai traces material origin to creative command and distinguishes happiness rising from misery descending; Biqai says voluntary return through knowledge precedes compelled return. [9 notes: ELMALILI; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); TABATABAI]
- 2:28/q34/p2 Mortality counters attachment to worldly pleasure. — Razi pictures possessions and body abandoned in the grave with 23:100; Elmalili recommends lawful gratitude and sincerity. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI]
