# 50:3: 22 questions

## 50:3/q01 What answer to the opening oath is understood here?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the oath's implied answer states resurrection alone or embraces the ensuing message about prophethood as well.
- 50:3/q01/p1 They will certainly be raised after death. — Al-Farrāʾ says the oath supplies the assertion the deniers contest; Ibn Qutayba cites omission of an oath's answer when later words reveal it, comparing 79:1 and 79:6. Al-Wāḥidī reports al-Farrāʾ and al-Zajjāj choosing this explanation. Al-Qushayrī gives it as a proposed answer. [5 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 50:3/q01/p2 The ensuing message establishes both prophethood and resurrection. — Ibn Kathīr prefers the answer implicit in what follows and compares the Qur'anic oath in 38:1–2. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL+]

## 50:3/q02 What do the unbelievers find astonishing before this objection, and how does 50:3 follow it?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the earlier 'this is strange' points chiefly to the arriving warner or also to his warning of resurrection.
- 50:3/q02/p1 They marvel at a human warner; their resurrection objection then dismisses his warning. — Rāzī says Ṣād's wonder follows the demand to make the gods one, whereas Qāf's follows the warner's arrival; taking both expressions as wonder at resurrection would repeat the point. He distinguishes the nearby 'this' (the present warner) from distant 'that' (future return), cites 11:73 and 34:43, and notes their accusations against the messenger. Elmalılı makes the connective explanatory and 'this' the warning. Iṣlāḥī says resistance to accepting a messenger from their own community drives the objection. Al-Biqāʿī says astonishment rather than rejection of the Qur'an's glory or the Prophet's truth is foregrounded. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the earlier 'this' points to the Prophet's call to believe in resurrection, which 50:3 spells out. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 50:3/q02/p2 Their astonishment also concerns the resurrection announced by the warner. — Al-Zamakhsharī, as reported by Rāzī, takes 'this is strange' as a second expression of wonder at resurrection and its warning. Al-Ṭabrisī mentions both a messenger from their people and resurrection. Elmalılı makes the connective coordinate the two wonders; Ṭabāṭabāʾī permits either the human warner or true Resurrection after the turn from the omitted oath answer. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(الزمخشري); TABRISI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL-]

## 50:3/q03 Why does the preceding wording name them 'the disbelievers'?  [* · rhetoric]
- 50:3/q03/p1 Replacing a pronoun with 'the disbelievers' marks the gravity of their rejection of resurrection. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī identify the explicit noun as a rebuke of their unbelieving claim. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]

## 50:3/q04 How is أَءِذَا read?  [أَءِذَا · readings]
turns on: Whether the single-hamza form removes the question or merely suppresses its interrogative particle.
- 50:3/q04/p1 It has two hamzas and is interrogative. — Al-Samīn calls this the common reading; its question has an implied verb such as 'shall we be raised?' or 'shall we return?'. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]
- 50:3/q04/p2 It has one hamza and is declarative. — Ibn Jinnī names Yaḥyā, al-Aʿraj, Shayba, Abū Jaʿfar, and Ṣafwān ibn ʿAmr; Ibn ʿAṭiyya names al-Aʿraj, Shayba, and Abū Jaʿfar. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī add Ibn Waththāb, al-Aʿmash, and Ibn ʿUtba; al-Samīn also mentions a transmission from Ibn ʿĀmir. Ibn Jinnī understands the return as implausible to them, not temporally distant. [7 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعرج وشيبة وأبو جعفر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأعرج وشيبة وأبو جعفر وابن وثاب والأعمش وابن عتبة); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); ALUSI-FULL(الأعرج، شيبة، أبو جعفر، ابن وثاب، الأعمش، ابن عتبة عن ابن عامر)]
- 50:3/q04/p3 The one-hamza form can remain interrogative if the interrogative hamza is omitted. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī report this possibility. Ibn Jinnī recalls having judged the deletion explanation weak. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأعرج وشيبة وأبو جعفر وابن وثاب والأعمش وابن عتبة); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); ALUSI-FULL(الأعرج، شيبة، أبو جعفر، ابن وثاب، الأعمش، ابن عتبة عن ابن عامر); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB-]

## 50:3/q05 What completes and governs the construction beginning with أَءِذَا?  [أَءِذَا مِتْنَا وَكُنَّا تُرَابًۭا · grammar]
turns on: The reading of idhā and whether the final clause belongs to the deniers or to God's answer determine its omitted completion and governor.
- 50:3/q05/p1 An implied verb or predicate asks whether they will be raised or returned when dead and dust. — Al-Zajjāj says idhā needs a governing verb. Al-Akhfash treats the question as their response to an implied 'you will return'; al-Thalabī and al-Baghawī say context supplies resurrection. Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī supply 'will we be raised/returned?' Al-Qurṭubī also describes an omitted repetition of their objection and says the warner's warning and the Qur'an's unity suffice without an immediately preceding mention of resurrection. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Ibn Jinnī's 'when we die, are we then returned?' Al-Wāḥidī calls it an interrogative denial with omitted answer. [18 notes: ZAJJAJ; AKHFASH; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جني); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأخفش); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 50:3/q05/p2 On the declarative reading, the answer after idhā is omitted: they will return. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī supply the omitted response. Ibn Jinnī and al-Zamakhsharī also construe the one-hamza form as a statement of return after death and dust. [4 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 50:3/q05/p3 On the declarative reading, 'that is a distant return' itself answers idhā. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says it is the predicate. Al-Samīn reports deletion of the expected fāʾ; al-Ālūsī attributes permission for it to the author of al-Lawāmiḥ. Both say the majority of grammarians allow such deletion only in poetry. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعرج وشيبة وأبو جعفر); SAMIN-DURR(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(صاحب اللوامح)]
- 50:3/q05/p4 If the final words are God's reply, idhā is governed by resurrection implied in the warner's warning. — Al-Zamakhsharī says the implied resurrection matter governs the time adverb. Al-Samīn repeats this; al-Ālūsī says the deniers' answer itself proves that they were warned of resurrection, meeting the objection that idhā has no governor. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(غير مسمى)]

## 50:3/q06 What is the force of their question about death and dust?  [أَءِذَا مِتْنَا وَكُنَّا تُرَابًۭا · interpretation]
- 50:3/q06/p1 It is astonished denial of bodily resurrection, not a request for information. — Muqātil, al-Farrāʾ, Ibn Qutayba, al-Qushayrī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Nasafī, al-Rāzī, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Asad, and Study Quran describe rejection or incredulity. Rāzī says the warner's threat of lasting punishment entails resurrection. Ibn Jarīr, reported by Ibn al-Jawzī, links their amazement at the warning to their denial of Muhammad and says they would learn the outcome on being raised. Ibn Jurayj reports that they asked who could restore them. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the omitted question makes the claim seem barely worth uttering; he compares 32:10. Study Quran compares 17:49, 36:78, 37:16–17, 37:53, 56:47, and 79:11. [16 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]

## 50:3/q07 How is مِتْنَا pronounced?  [مِتْنَا · readings]
turns on: The alternative stem patterns of māta, and the resulting vowel on the mīm.
- 50:3/q07/p1 Mitnā has kasra on the mīm. — The Qirāʾāt note calls it the alternative a-stem form of the verb. Al-Nasafī reports a vowel form from Nāfiʿ, ʿAlī, Ḥamza, and Ḥafṣ without spelling that form in the extracted note. [2 notes: QIRAAT-ER(vw); NASAFI-FULL(نافع وعلى وحمزة وحفص)]
- 50:3/q07/p2 Mutnā has ḍamma on the mīm. — The Qirāʾāt note derives it from the u-stem māta/yamūtu pattern and calls it standard in Ḥijāz. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(vw)]

## 50:3/q08 What does becoming تُرَابًۭا describe?  [تُرَابًۭا · meaning]
- 50:3/q08/p1 Their bodies decay into dust or indistinguishable earthly remains. — Al-Dāmghānī glosses dust as decayed remains. Ibn Kathīr adds severed limbs and loss of former bodily form; al-Biqāʿī and Bursevī distinguish separated souls from bodies mingled with earth. Ibn ʿĀshūr says this bodily decay is the timing on which they base their wonder; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it appears to erase their identity without trace. [6 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 50:3/q09 What does ذَٰلِكَ رَجْعٌ mean, and whose judgment does the clause give?  [ذَٰلِكَ رَجْعٌ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether rajʿ denotes the promised return or a returned reply, and whether the sentence is the deniers' speech or God's assessment.
- 50:3/q09/p1 The deniers call the resurrection or return to life a distant return. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, al-Ālūsī, and al-Rāzī take 'that' as the warned-of resurrection. Abū ʿUbayda, al-Wāḥidī, al-Jishumī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr gloss rajʿ or related forms as return; al-Thalabī cites 9:83, and al-Wāḥidī the same usage. Al-Rāzī distinguishes transitive rajʿ from intransitive rujuʿ and rujʿā, while allowing intransitive rajʿ; he cites 96:8 and 79:10 for return or raising. Al-Bayḍāwī also allows 'the returned thing'. [23 notes: MAJAZ; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(these disbelievers)]
- 50:3/q09/p2 God calls their reply denying resurrection itself outlandish or distant. — Al-Zamakhsharī proposes rajʿ as a returned answer; al-Nasafī allows response and says a pause after 'dust' is good. Al-Samīn reports al-Zamakhsharī's 'thing returned' as resurrection in a divine answer and calls for a stop before it. Al-Ālūsī reports the usage 'reply to your letter'; Elmalılı also notes irjāʿ can mean reply. Abū Ḥayyān calls the interpretation astonishing and unintelligible to Arabs; al-Samīn reports the Shaykh's criticism, and al-Ālūsī deems rajʿ as 'reply' remote. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(غير مسمى); ELMALILI; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 50:3/q10 What does بَعِيدٌ mean in their verdict?  [بَعِيدٌ · meaning]
- 50:3/q10/p1 They consider resurrection far beyond experience or imagination, and therefore impossible. — Al-Farrāʾ, cited by al-Naḥḥās, says baʿīd can mean it will not happen, a recognized Arabic usage; al-Farrāʾ compares being far from the truth in an answer. Ibn Qutayba, al-Zajjāj, al-Qushayrī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Zamakhsharī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī variously stress implausibility, custom, thought, reason, or impossibility. Ibn Jinnī explicitly denies that 'distant' means far away in time, since they denied resurrection itself. [21 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 50:3/q11 Why does their verdict state the denial after the question?  [ذَٰلِكَ رَجْعٌۢ بَعِيدٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 50:3/q11/p1 The direct statement confirms what the elliptical question had already implied. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says explicit confirmation makes the denial clearer after the question conveyed it indirectly. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 50:3/q12 What underlies the deniers' resistance to resurrection?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the notes explain the objection by imagined bodily impossibility or by resistance to the warner.
- 50:3/q12/p1 They find restoration after bodily decay inconceivable. — Ibn Kathīr describes the loss of bodily form and severed limbs; al-Biqāʿī and Bursevī picture the soul separated and the body indistinguishable from earth. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the timing after death and decay drives their wonder. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says they imagine no trace of identity remains; Asad summarizes the question as how return after dust is possible. [6 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 50:3/q12/p2 Pride and reluctance to accept a messenger from their own people drive the objection. — Iṣlāḥī says accusations of poetry and soothsaying pretextually dismiss the Qur'an despite its grandeur and divine origin; arrogance prevents recognizing revelation and its arguments. He says this group's resurrection objections are excuses for opposition to the Prophet. Al-Biqāʿī calls their amazement inconsistent with their acceptance of stone gods and their own first emergence from dust. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL]

## 50:3/q13 How does the passage answer the resurrection objection?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The evidence invoked ranges from divine knowledge and observed creation to the need for recompense and judgment.
- 50:3/q13/p1 God's knowledge of bodies and His creative power make restoration possible. — Al-Jishumī invokes God's power over bodies and knowledge of their parts; Ibn ʿAṭiyya mentions what earth consumes and its preserved record. Al-Biqāʿī adduces first life from dust, recurring day and night, revived land, animals, plants, trees, and fruit. Ibn al-Zubayr, reported in al-Burhān, traces the move from denial to the visible sky and revived land (50:6, 50:9); al-Burhān says 'Thus is the emergence' concludes from these undeniable facts. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says encompassing knowledge and the Preserved Book answer the loss-of-identity objection. Kur'an Yolu adds creation from nothing, knowledge of every remaining or decayed body part, and return of the soul. Iṣlāḥī says God records deeds and words and calls the denied reality obvious. [12 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURHAN(ابن الزبير); BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 50:3/q13/p2 Recompense and judgment require a resurrection. — Al-Zamakhsharī says the deniers admit God's first creation, yet reason requires recompense; al-Jishumī's rational scope of resurrection is those entitled to reward or compensation. Rāzī says the warner's threat of enduring punishment entails a future raising. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL]

## 50:3/q14 Who is restored at resurrection?  [* · theology]
turns on: Al-Jishumī distinguishes the scope proved by reason from the scope reported by revelation.
- 50:3/q14/p1 Reason establishes resurrection for those owed reward or compensation. — Al-Jishumī distinguishes what reason establishes from what revelation adds. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 50:3/q14/p2 Revelation says all living beings will be restored. — Al-Jishumī gives this as the wider revealed scope beyond the rational argument. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 50:3/q15 About whom was this objection revealed?  [* · reports]
- 50:3/q15/p1 It concerns named Quraysh deniers who asked how scattered dust could live again. — Muqātil names Abū ibn Khalaf, Abū al-Ashaddayn (Usayda ibn Kalda), and the brothers Nabh and Munabbih, sons of al-Ḥajjāj. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 50:3/q16 What virtue of reciting Sūrat Qāf is reported?  [* · sciences]
- 50:3/q16/p1 Its reciter will have death's agonies eased. — Al-Jishumī reports Ubayy's report from the Prophet; the note gives no grading. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبي)]

## 50:3/q17 How does Sūrat Qāf relate to neighboring or comparable sūras?  [* · links]
turns on: The compared sūras and the features chosen for comparison.
- 50:3/q17/p1 It follows al-Ḥujurāt's close on faith by opening with resurrection, the Qur'an, and God's oneness. — Al-Jishumī makes this transition explicit. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 50:3/q17/p2 It parallels Sūrat Ṣād in form but foregrounds resurrection where Ṣād foregrounds God's unity. — Rāzī compares isolated letters, oaths by the Qur'an, 'bal', wonder, and Qur'anic reminders at both ends; he contrasts Ṣād's Adam and unity passages with Qāf's closing gathering, citing 38:1, 38:5, 38:71, 38:87, 50:44–45. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 50:3/q18 How does this objection fit the sūra's unfolding argument?  [* · links]
- 50:3/q18/p1 It introduces the resurrection theme that the sūra then answers, illustrates, and warns about. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the opening gives a brief denial, response, and threat that later passages detail; he cites 50:22, 50:30, and 50:35 as resurrection scenes. Ibn al-Zubayr and al-Burhān link the objection to visible signs and 'Thus is the emergence' in 50:6 and 50:9. Kur'an Yolu describes monotheism, prophethood, and afterlife as the passage's subjects. Iṣlāḥī says this group addresses objections used against the Prophet. [7 notes: TABATABAI; BURHAN(ابن الزبير); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 50:3/q19 Why is Sūrat Qāf recited in Eid prayer?  [* · sciences]
- 50:3/q19/p1 Its resurrection and gathering reminders turn festivity toward judgment and away from heedlessness and sin. — Rāzī says the Qur'anic reminder suits Eid and cites 50:11, 50:42, and 50:44–45. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 50:3/q20 Where was Sūrat Qāf revealed?  [* · sciences]
- 50:3/q20/p1 It is Meccan throughout, as its context indicates. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says proposed exceptions for the verse or verses on creating the heavens and earth lack textual evidence. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 50:3/q21 What does ḥāda mean in the surrounding passage?  [* · meaning]
- 50:3/q21/p1 It means to turn or incline away from something. — Abū Ḥayyān gives the verbal nouns ḥuyūdan, ḥīdatan, and ḥaydūdatan. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 50:3/q22 What does added min after negation do in a cited comparison?  [* · grammar]
- 50:3/q22/p1 It emphasizes the negation or its equivalent. — Al-Burhān compares passages about a falling leaf, creation, and God having a child or partner; the extracted note mentions 50:3 and 6:100. [1 notes: BURHAN]
