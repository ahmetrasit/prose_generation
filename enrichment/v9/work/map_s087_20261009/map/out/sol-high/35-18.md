# 35:18: 40 questions

## 35:18/q01 What does wizr mean?  [وِزْرَ · meaning]
- 35:18/q01/p1 A heavy load, here sin and its liability or punishment. — Jishumī gives the plural awzār and calls sins heavy; Kashshāf, Nasafī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr compare wiqr; Māwardī connects wazīr with bearing a ruler's burden; al-Rāghib distinguishes wazr refuge from wizr burden; Elmalılı stresses punishment. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب)]
- 35:18/q01/p2 The root verb means to carry a load. — Bursevī gives wazara-yaziru and wazara-yūzaru; Ibn ʿAṭiyya glosses taziru as carrying. [2 notes: BURSEVI; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 35:18/q02 What does the first clause teach?  [وَلَا تَزِرُ وَازِرَةٌۭ وِزْرَ أُخْرَىٰ · interpretation]
- 35:18/q02/p1 Each soul bears only its own sin and is not punished for another's deed. — Ṭabarī and many others state divine justice; Zamakhsharī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī, and Biqāʿī contrast worldly tyrants. Abū Rimtha reports the Prophet applying it to father and son; ʿAmr ibn al-Aḥwaṣ reports his Farewell Pilgrimage statement. [44 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة وابن عباس ومجاهد); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو رمثة,عمرو بن الأحوص); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Aj,Aj, IK, R, Ṭ)]
- 35:18/q02/p2 It covers duties as well as sins. — Study Quran reports Aj, IK, R, and Ṭ with this broader expression. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Aj, IK, R, Ṭ)]

## 35:18/q03 Why use wāzirah rather than simply nafs?  [وَازِرَةٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 35:18/q03/p1 It depicts a soul already carrying its own burden and makes the negated rule general. — Rāzī says a bare 'soul' could imply a soul with no burden; Nasafī and Biqāʿī stress its existing load. A reported scholar says the participle denotes an established quality, indefinite under negation. Ālūsī rejects a need for grammatical tajrīd. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأفاضل)]
- 35:18/q03/p2 It foregrounds the soul as the one that earns and bears its deeds. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 6:164 and 74:38. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:18/q04 What is the grammar of wāzirah and muthqalah?  [وَازِرَةٌۭ مُثْقَلَةٌ · grammar]
- 35:18/q04/p1 Both feminine adjectives imply nafs, which can mean a male or female person. — Ṭabarī and al-Farrāʾ cite 3:185; Naḥḥās mentions dābba and al-Akhfash's insān; Islahi notes common ellipsis; Abū Ḥayyān explains the later masculine pronoun by the sense 'person'. [20 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(الأخفش,الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش,الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 35:18/q05 How is taziru formed?  [تَزِرُ · grammar]
- 35:18/q05/p1 Its original initial wāw was deleted in the pattern of yaziru. — Naḥḥās and Qurṭubī give tawzaru as the underlying form. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 35:18/q05/p2 Its indicative form states a prohibition. — Jishumī says declarative syntax has prohibitive force. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 35:18/q06 What prompted the burden rule according to the reported occasion?  [وَلَا تَزِرُ وَازِرَةٌۭ وِزْرَ أُخْرَىٰ · reports]
- 35:18/q06/p1 Al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra urged believers to reject Muhammad and promised to bear their sins. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya states the occasion; Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī report it; Zamakhsharī cites the promise in 29:12. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الوليد بن المغيرة); KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 35:18/q07 How does this rule apply to misleaders' added burdens?  [وِزْرَ أُخْرَىٰ · theology]
turns on: Whether extra burdens are the person's own causal sin or another burden imposed unwillingly.
- 35:18/q07/p1 The added load is their own sin of misleading, while followers remain liable for their own choice. — Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī, Ālūsī, Elmalılı, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Kuran Yolu, and Study Quran reconcile 29:12–13. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Elmalılı cite the hadith on originating practices. [17 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Commentators)]
- 35:18/q07/p2 Additional burdens are imposed unwillingly; this verse denies carrying another's sin willingly. — Al-Ḥusayn ibn al-Faḍl offers this reconciliation. [1 notes: THALABI(الحسين بن الفضل)]

## 35:18/q08 Can a relative be punished for someone's crime in worldly law?  [وِزْرَ أُخْرَىٰ · law]
- 35:18/q08/p1 Only if the relative aided, supported, knew of, or approved the crime and so shared responsibility. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya states this; Abū Ḥayyān reports it and condemns punishment of innocent associates. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 35:18/q08/p2 The rule supplies a maxim of personal legal responsibility. — Kuran Yolu says early Muslim jurists used it and compares Western law's later adoption. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 35:18/q09 What is a muthqalah?  [مُثْقَلَةٌ · meaning]
- 35:18/q09/p1 A soul weighed down by sins. — Mujāhid, al-Farrāʾ, al-Zajjāj, and later exegetes give this sense; Bursevī cites al-Rāghib on literal heaviness extended to sin and debt; Biqāʿī includes disbelief. [12 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 35:18/q09/p2 The feminine wording heightens the expectation of mercy. — Jishumī says women are seen as weaker and likelier to receive help, yet none is given. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 35:18/q10 What happens when a burdened soul asks for help?  [وَإِن تَدْعُ مُثْقَلَةٌ إِلَىٰ حِمْلِهَا لَا يُحْمَلْ مِنْهُ شَىْءٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ilā ḥimlihā names the load itself or the burden of carrying it.
- 35:18/q10/p1 No one takes any part of its sins, even after a plea, whether kin or stranger. — Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, ʿAṭāʾ, Qatāda, and many later authors say the refusal is total. Rāzī says even a severe load and plea win no relief; Biqāʿī and Elmalılı include voluntary and forced transfer; Islahi says the righteous did not share the wrongdoing. [59 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,عطاء الخراساني,قتادة,مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عطاء الخراساني,قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Zemahşerî)]
- 35:18/q10/p2 The call may be construed as asking someone to bear the burden of its load. — Ālūsī transmits this alternative sense of ilā ḥimlihā. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 35:18/q11 Why is the call for help so pointed?  [تَدْعُ مُثْقَلَةٌ إِلَىٰ حِمْلِهَا · rhetoric]
- 35:18/q11/p1 It evokes a desperate rescue call that would normally stir sympathy. — Rāzī says an overwhelmed person finally asks after silent endurance; Ibn ʿĀshūr invokes Arab rescue custom, Waddāk's verse, and a proverb about generosity even to death. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(وداك بن ثميل المازني)]
- 35:18/q11/p2 It restates the first rule in a more urgent scene. — Mujāhid explicitly equates the clauses; Elmalılı reads a hint at attempts to shift sin. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ELMALILI]

## 35:18/q12 What does ḥiml mean and what does its pronoun refer to?  [حِمْلِهَا · meaning]
- 35:18/q12/p1 A physical load carried on the back, metaphorically the soul's sins. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares hands metaphorically earning deeds; al-Kisāʾī and Ibn al-Sikkīt distinguish pronunciations for back load, pregnancy, and palm fruit; al-Mufradāt distinguishes external ḥiml from internal ḥaml. [6 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن السكيت,الكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(المفردات)]
- 35:18/q12/p2 The pronoun in 'none of it' points to the soul's burden. — Wāḥidī specifies its antecedent. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 35:18/q13 What is omitted in the call's construction?  [تَدْعُ إِلَىٰ حِمْلِهَا · grammar]
- 35:18/q13/p1 The object called upon is implicit, making the statement cover any possible helper. — Al-Akhfash and Zamakhsharī supply the person; Ibn ʿĀshūr also supplies an implied request for carrying as the aim of ilā; Islahi notes an omitted governing noun. [8 notes: NAHHAS(الأخفش); QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 35:18/q13/p2 The feminine verb's subject is the implied soul. — Bursevī points out its feminine singular form. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 35:18/q14 What are the readings of lā yuḥmal and shayʾ?  [لَا يُحْمَلْ مِنْهُ شَىْءٌۭ · readings]
turns on: Passive versus active verb and nominative versus accusative shayʾ.
- 35:18/q14/p1 The common passive has shayʾ as deputy subject. — Al-Samīn records it. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]
- 35:18/q14/p2 An active reading has the summoned person as subject and shayʾan as object. — Al-Samīn attributes it to Abū al-Simmāl, Ṭalḥa, and al-Kisāʾī; Ālūsī specifies tāʾ and kasra on mīm in reports. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو السمال وطلحة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو السمال عن طلحة وإبراهيم عن الكسائي)]

## 35:18/q15 Who is the close relative?  [ذَا قُرْبَىٰٓ · referent]
turns on: Whether kāna's implicit subject is the helper or the caller.
- 35:18/q15/p1 The summoned helper is kin to the burdened soul. — Al-Akhfash, Ṭabarī, Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Ālūsī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr favor or use this reading; examples are parent, child, sibling, or spouse. Rāzī says kinship makes refusal more striking. [18 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(الأخفش); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 35:18/q15/p2 The caller is kin to the person asked. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives this alternative; Abū Ḥayyān defends its coherence; al-Samīn and Biqāʿī allow both. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية)]

## 35:18/q16 How does kāna work in 'even if a close relative'?  [كَانَ ذَا قُرْبَىٰٓ · grammar]
turns on: Which implied subject and case are read, and whether kāna is complete.
- 35:18/q16/p1 Kāna is incomplete, with an omitted person as subject and dhā qurbā as predicate. — Al-Akhfash and Ṭabarī parse the predicate; Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, and Ālūsī prefer the summoned helper as subject because the syntax and sense hold together. [6 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; KASHSHAF-FULL+]
- 35:18/q16/p2 The omitted subject is instead the caller. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives this parse; Abū Ḥayyān finds it coherent. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 35:18/q16/p3 With nominative dhū qurbā, kāna may be complete or have an omitted predicate. — Al-Farrāʾ and Sībawayh permit it, citing a hardship verse and an elliptical reward example; al-Samīn cites 2:280. Zamakhsharī and Bayḍāwī object to the complete-verb analysis. [7 notes: NAHHAS(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء وسيبويه,سيبويه); KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL-; BAYDAWI-FULL-]

## 35:18/q17 What scenes of family refusal are reported?  [وَلَوْ كَانَ ذَا قُرْبَىٰٓ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in who asks, what is asked, and any limited relief.
- 35:18/q17/p1 A parent asks a child to bear sins; the child refuses because of its own burden. — Ibn ʿAbbās is transmitted by Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ṭabrisī, and Bursevī; Bursevī adds a husband's request for a speck of merit from his wife. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(ابن عباس)]
- 35:18/q17/p2 A mother invokes pregnancy, nursing, and care to ask a son to bear one sin; he refuses. — Al-Fuḍayl ibn ʿIyāḍ is reported by Thaʿlabī and Qurṭubī. [2 notes: THALABI(الفضيل بن عياض); QURTUBI-FULL(الفضيل بن عياض)]
- 35:18/q17/p3 Neighbors, Jews or Christians, parents, children, and spouses plead at Judgment; each fears for their own fate. — ʿIkrima's accounts differ in speakers and requests; some include a believer's appeal that reduces an unbeliever's place in Hell, without transfer of sin. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عكرمة); NAHHAS(عكرمة); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة); DURR-FULL(عكرمة)]

## 35:18/q18 Does the refusal rule exclude every intercession?  [وَلَوْ كَانَ ذَا قُرْبَىٰٓ · theology]
turns on: Whether God-permitted mitigation amounts to transferring another's sin.
- 35:18/q18/p1 It excludes family or patronage as independent rescue and excludes transferring personal guilt. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says polytheists imagined autonomous intercessors; Kuran Yolu says they projected worldly patronage; Elmalılı cites 2:123 and 14:31. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI]
- 35:18/q18/p2 Intercession by God's permission or divinely granted expiation can occur. — Ibn ʿĀshūr names Muhammad's intercession and believing children for bereaved mothers, citing 34:23; ʿIkrima reports partial easing of an unbeliever's punishment. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(عكرمة); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة)]

## 35:18/q19 How do the two burden clauses differ?  [وَلَا تَزِرُ وَازِرَةٌۭ وِزْرَ أُخْرَىٰ وَإِن تَدْعُ مُثْقَلَةٌ إِلَىٰ حِمْلِهَا لَا يُحْمَلْ مِنْهُ شَىْءٌۭ · rhetoric]
turns on: Request, quantity, coercion, or the absence of refuge.
- 35:18/q19/p1 First: no assigned liability for another's sin. Second: no relief from one's own after asking. — Zamakhsharī, Nasafī, Biqāʿī, Kuran Yolu, and Ālūsī use this distinction; Ālūsī prefers the broad second clause covering willing and forced carrying. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Zemahşerî); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 35:18/q19/p2 First: no transfer of an entire load. Second: no transfer of even part. — Ālūsī transmits this alternative. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 35:18/q19/p3 First: no forced bearing. Second: no voluntary bearing. — Al-Irshād gives it; Ālūsī objects that the wording should reverse the cases and mention coercion. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 35:18/q19/p4 First: complete justice. Second: no refuge from the day's terror. — Ālūsī reports this distinction but prefers his broader reading. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 35:18/q20 Who benefits from the Prophet's warning?  [إِنَّمَا تُنذِرُ ٱلَّذِينَ يَخْشَوْنَ رَبَّهُم بِٱلْغَيْبِ · interpretation]
- 35:18/q20/p1 The Prophet warns all, but only receptive God-fearing people heed and benefit. — Naḥḥās, Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, and others distinguish delivery from effect; Wāḥidī cites 79:45; Qurṭubī cites 36:11. Rāzī says all possible guidance has arrived; Ibn ʿĀshūr says warning here means its achieved effect. [36 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الأخفش); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Zemahşerî); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 35:18/q20/p2 God selectively grants the capacity to hear and respond. — Biqāʿī reads the later passage as confirming this and consoling the Prophet. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 35:18/q21 What is warning?  [تُنذِرُ · meaning]
turns on: Delivery of a warning versus its effective result.
- 35:18/q21/p1 Alerting someone to danger, with fear attached to the message. — Qushayrī, Biqāʿī, and Bursevī give these definitions. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL(القشيري); BURSEVI]
- 35:18/q21/p2 Achieving abstention or belief as the warning's effect. — Ibn ʿĀshūr argues the Prophet had already delivered threats to the polytheists repeatedly. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 35:18/q22 What does innamā convey?  [إِنَّمَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Absolute versus context-relative restriction.
- 35:18/q22/p1 It restricts effective warning to the receptive and indirectly reproaches the obstinate. — Jurjānī says innamā has more force than merely making inna inoperative; Burhān compares the unreceptive to people without hearing or reason; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the restriction qasr qalb. [4 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL; BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 35:18/q22/p2 Literal exclusivity is also possible. — Ibn ʿĀshūr acknowledges it but favors a context-relative correction. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:18/q23 Who are those described as fearing and praying?  [ٱلَّذِينَ يَخْشَوْنَ رَبَّهُم بِٱلْغَيْبِ وَأَقَامُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · referent]
turns on: General believers or particular early recipients.
- 35:18/q23/p1 Sincere believers or Muslims who receive warning. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says description instead of a group name praises their belief and practice; Qatāda contrasts living believers with dead-hearted unbelievers. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 35:18/q23/p2 The Prophet's Companions in particular. — Zamakhsharī identifies their lasting fear and prayer. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 35:18/q23/p3 God's allies who fear unseen punishment. — Ibn ʿAbbās is reported by Wāḥidī. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)]

## 35:18/q24 What does bil-ghayb refer to?  [بِٱلْغَيْبِ · referent]
turns on: What is unseen: God, observers, punishment, the Prophet, or unseen reality.
- 35:18/q24/p1 God, whom they fear without seeing. — Muqātil, Thaʿlabī, Jishumī, Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and Elmalılı give this sense. [7 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ELMALILI]
- 35:18/q24/p2 Their private state: they fear unseen by other people. — Yaḥyā, Māwardī, Jishumī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and others emphasize inward sincerity; Biqāʿī invokes Khadīja and al-Ṣiddīq. [14 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام); JISHUMI(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 35:18/q24/p3 God's punishment, the Fire, reckoning, or afterlife, still unseen to them. — Ṭabarī and Ibn ʿAbbās name unseen judgment; Qatāda names Fire and reckoning; Ibn ʿĪsā and Jishumī stress afterlife belief. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 35:18/q24/p4 The absent Prophet who communicates by a message. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives this reading. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 35:18/q24/p5 Unseen realities in general. — Asad cites 2:3, 27:80–81 and 30:52–53. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 35:18/q25 How is bil-ghayb parsed?  [بِٱلْغَيْبِ · grammar]
- 35:18/q25/p1 It may be circumstantial of the fearer or of what is feared. — Zamakhsharī, Nasafī, al-Samīn, Bursevī, and Ālūsī set out both attachments. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 35:18/q26 What does establishing prayer require?  [وَأَقَامُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · interpretation]
- 35:18/q26/p1 Maintaining prescribed prayer properly, with its times and conditions. — Muqātil and Yaḥyā specify the obligatory prayer; Ṭabrisī its conditions; Nasafī its times; Biqāʿī the five prayers and sunnas; Ibn ʿĀshūr says none is neglected. [11 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; JISHUMI; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 35:18/q26/p2 Making prayer an enduring raised standard. — Zamakhsharī, Bursevī, and Ālūsī use this image. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 35:18/q27 Why pair fear with prayer and use different verb forms?  [يَخْشَوْنَ رَبَّهُم بِٱلْغَيْبِ وَأَقَامُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Verbal aspect and prayer's thematic prominence.
- 35:18/q27/p1 Fear is continuous, while prayer has fixed recurring times. — Ṭabrisī, Biqāʿī, and Kashf al-Asrār via Bursevī explain present versus past this way; Bayḍāwī sees enduring practice; Ālūsī refers to 35:9. [6 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار); ALUSI-FULL]
- 35:18/q27/p2 Prayer is the foremost outward sign of inward fear and deserves particular honor. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says it is singled out for honor; Rāzī compares inward faith with outward deeds; Biqāʿī calls prayer noblest worship; Tabāṭabāʾī and Islahi stress its centrality. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 35:18/q28 What follows from the emphasis on prayer?  [وَأَقَامُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · theology]
- 35:18/q28/p1 A profession of faith without prayer or practice does not benefit a person. — Jishumī argues against the Murjiʾa; Bursevī cites a hadith on abandoning prayer and unbelief; Islahi says neglect betrays heedlessness. [3 notes: JISHUMI; BURSEVI(النبي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 35:18/q29 What is tazakkā?  [وَمَن تَزَكَّىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether purification is glossed as good deeds, cleansing sin, or finding guidance.
- 35:18/q29/p1 Reforming oneself through obedience and righteous works. — Yaḥyā, Qatāda, Muqātil, Baghawī, Ibn Kathīr, and others include doing good; Ṭabrisī includes zakat and duties; Biqāʿī stresses effort against the soul's pull; Kuran Yolu includes avoiding prohibitions. [17 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 35:18/q29/p2 Cleansing disbelief, sins, idolatry, or indecency through repentance and faith. — Ṭabarī names repentance, faith and obedience; Ibn al-Jawzī adds idolatry; Bayḍāwī calls sin a stain; Ālūsī says warning prompts purification. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 35:18/q29/p3 Finding guidance. — Ibn ʿAbbās via Wāḥidī and Qurṭubī gives this gloss. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 35:18/q30 How is the purification clause read?  [وَمَن تَزَكَّىٰ فَإِنَّمَا يَتَزَكَّىٰ لِنَفْسِهِۦ · readings]
turns on: Retained versus assimilated tāʾ and variant initial hamza.
- 35:18/q30/p1 The common wording retains the tāʾ of tafaʿʿul. — Al-Samīn and Abū Ḥayyān identify the majority reading. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(العامة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 35:18/q30/p2 Other readings assimilate tāʾ into zāy, some with an initial connecting hamza. — Abū ʿAmr, Ibn Masʿūd and Ṭalḥa are named by al-Samīn, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī; Kashshāf, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Bayḍāwī and Qurṭubī give related forms. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(طلحة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو,ابن مسعود وطلحة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود وطلحة,العباس عن أبي عمرو)]

## 35:18/q31 Who benefits from purification?  [فَإِنَّمَا يَتَزَكَّىٰ لِنَفْسِهِۦ · interpretation]
- 35:18/q31/p1 The benefit and reward return to the person who purifies, while sin harms its doer. — Ṭabarī names God's pleasure, Paradise and escape from punishment; Biqāʿī and Ālūsī draw the reverse implication for sin; Tabāṭabāʾī and Islahi say God needs no worship; Ibn ʿĀshūr says accepting warning benefits its hearer. [27 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(IK); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 35:18/q31/p2 Reward and punishment follow human agents' own deeds. — Jishumī argues against the Mujbira and the claim that human acts are created. [2 notes: JISHUMI]

## 35:18/q32 How does the purification maxim serve the passage?  [وَمَن تَزَكَّىٰ فَإِنَّمَا يَتَزَكَّىٰ لِنَفْسِهِۦ · rhetoric]
- 35:18/q32/p1 It generalizes fear and prayer as purification and encourages it with personal reward. — Rāzī links it to nontransferable sin; Ibn ʿAṭiyya sees strong hope; Bayḍāwī, Ālūsī, Tabāṭabāʾī and Ibn ʿĀshūr include the preceding traits; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it proverbial tadhyīl. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 35:18/q32/p2 It balances the personal stain of sin against the personal growth of virtue. — Biqāʿī supplies the implicit contrast; Bursevī likens obedience to light at the crossing that another cannot share. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 35:18/q33 What does the return to God mean?  [وَإِلَى ٱللَّهِ ٱلْمَصِيرُ · interpretation]
- 35:18/q33/p1 All return to God for judgment and recompense of their deeds. — Rāzī says hidden consequences become visible then; Ibn ʿĀshūr says fronting 'to God' draws attention to divine justice and generosity and serves the cadence; Bursevī includes Paradise and divine beauty. [21 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 35:18/q33/p2 It warns the unpurified and promises reward to those purified. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya stresses warning; Kashshāf and Nasafī stress reward. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]

## 35:18/q34 How is personal accountability read inwardly?  [وَلَا تَزِرُ وَازِرَةٌۭ وِزْرَ أُخْرَىٰ · inward]
- 35:18/q34/p1 Each has an individual divine address, record, and inward relation that no proxy can replace. — Qushayrī says saints cannot supply another's missed prayer or gnosis; Bursevī compares each secret to a seed yielding only its own fruit and quotes Saʿdī. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(سعدي)]

## 35:18/q35 How does the burden rule connect to the preceding threat?  [* · links]
turns on: Reassurance, fresh opening, or continuation of the prior threat.
- 35:18/q35/p1 It reassures believers they will not bear unbelievers' sins or collective annihilation. — Tabāṭabāʾī sees an answer to an implied objection; Ibn ʿĀshūr considers afterlife and worldly rescue, citing 19:72, 8:25, 12:110 and 8:33; he prefers the broader collective-deliverance reading. Umm Salama's hadith limits worldly immunity when evil spreads. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 35:18/q35/p2 It opens a fresh discussion. — Naḥḥās, Qurṭubī and Study Quran's Q call the clause detached. [3 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 35:18/q35/p3 It follows proofs and threats with resurrection's terrors and effective warning. — Zamakhsharī gives two sequence accounts; Rāzī invokes a messenger's own accountability; Study Quran's R connects human poverty at 35:15. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 35:18/q36 What later contrast does this warning prepare?  [* · links]
- 35:18/q36/p1 It anticipates blind and seeing, darkness and light, shade and heat, living and dead. — Tabāṭabāʾī states this; Qatāda depicts dead-hearted unbelievers; Biqāʿī connects receptive hearing. [3 notes: TABATABAI; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL]
- 35:18/q36/p2 It recalls parallel burden and warning passages. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites 14:48 and 6:164; Asad cites 6:164 and 53:38; Qurṭubī cites 36:11; Tabāṭabāʾī compares timing with 12:36. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ASAD-NOTES; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 35:18/q37 What doctrinal applications are drawn from this verse?  [* · theology]
- 35:18/q37/p1 It rejects inherited original sin and vicarious atonement through Jesus. — Asad assigns those denials to the first and second clauses; Kuran Yolu gives a similar application. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 35:18/q37/p2 It teaches that good and evil acts affect their own agents and God has no need of worship. — Study Quran calls this moral reflexivity; Tabāṭabāʾī, Islahi and Bursevī draw divine self-sufficiency. [4 notes: STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 35:18/q38 What separate poetic or lexical material appears among the notes?  [* · rhetoric]

## 35:18/q39 What other illustrative reports appear?  [* · reports]
