# 23:4: 12 questions

## 23:4/q01 What does zakāh mean here?  [ٱلزَّكَوٰةِ · interpretation]
turns on: The financial sense may mean the prescribed amount or broader charity; other interpreters rely on zakāh's senses of purity, good works, and growth, and on whether its pairing with prayer or the Meccan date is decisive.
- 23:4/q01/p1 The obligatory alms due from wealth. — Muqātil, Yaḥyā, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī's Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Kalbī, and others identify the due payment; Ibn ʿAbbās adds seeking God's pleasure. Al-Naḥḥās says even payment of only the due earns praise, whereas hoarders omit it. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls this sense clear. Ibn Kathīr calls it the majority interpretation despite the Meccan setting. Al-Biqāʿī calls payment the strongest outward proof of faith; al-Durr transmits Saʿīd ibn Jubayr's 'wealth.' Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the fixed-amount reading but prefers broader giving. Al-Sāmarrāʾī notes the paid amount reaches a rightful recipient. [26 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس,الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري وغيره); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(بعض من حمل الزكاة على هذا المعنى); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(ابن كثير); IBNKATHIR-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 23:4/q01/p2 Financial giving or charity, without specifying the later fixed levy. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reads charity in this Meccan passage, cites Meccan 41:6–7 and Ismāʿīl's earlier command in 19:54–55, and explains that zakāh names the money given by an effect-for-cause extension from purification; 9:103 links charity and purification. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites its pairing with prayer. Iṣlāḥī calls it spending for God's cause across religions despite varied legal forms. Kur'an Yolu cites interchangeable zakāh/ṣadaqa, 9:60, and Meccan exhortations to feed and aid. Study Quran notes the general charity sense and later yearly levy on accumulated liquid wealth. Al-Biqāʿī and Elmalılı describe wealth purified through due giving. [12 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI+]
- 23:4/q01/p3 Purifying the soul or pursuing inner purity. — Ibn Kathīr cites 91:9–10 and 41:6–7 for cleansing shirk and impurity. Al-Rāghib, through al-Ālūsī and al-Sāmarrāʾī, makes purity the aim of worship; al-Ṭībī cites 87:14–15 and 91:9, saying the Qurʾān interprets itself. Al-Ālūsī reports the Meccan dating argument for this sense but also a scholar's objection that pairing with prayer signals financial worship. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports cleansing reprehensible traits yet prefers spending. Asad reports al-Zamakhsharī and Abū Muslim on active inner purity. Elmalılı reports refinement. [12 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب,الطيبي); ELMALILI(bazıları); TABATABAI(بعضهم); ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الراغب,روح المعاني,صاحب الكشاف); TABATABAI-; ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة)-]
- 23:4/q01/p4 Righteous works and the purest course in one's actions. — Al-Khaṭṭābī and al-Baghawī report this alternative. Ibn ʿAṭiyya expands it to seeking virtue and the best course in each act; he, Abū Muslim as reported by Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī, and al-Sāmarrāʾī cite 18:81. Al-Sāmarrāʾī also connects good deeds to the emphatic or purposive lām. [8 notes: KHATTABI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو مسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(أبو مسلم)]
- 23:4/q01/p5 Both financial alms and purification of the soul are intended. — Ibn Kathīr says the complete believer does both. Al-Biqāʿī allows payment and purification, joining heart, body, and property and saying payment purifies while withholding wastes. Elmalılı includes self, body, and wealth. Al-Ālūsī says the purification wording indirectly entails actual payment. Al-Sāmarrāʾī repeats the complete-believer reading and cites 91:9–10. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 23:4/q01/p6 The wording can encompass financial giving, righteous deeds, purification, and growth together. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī identifies the paid amount, the giving act, good work, purification from idolatry and defilement, and growth. He cites 18:81, 87:14–15 and 91:9–10; the two possible lām functions help the compact phrase carry these senses. [5 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 23:4/q01/p7 Zakāh denotes growth or increase gained by doing good. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Sāmarrāʾī transmit a view taking the lām as causal: they act for growth. Al-Qushayrī also starts from the lexical sense of growth but directs it inward, to diminishing self-witness. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes that wealth spending can be called zakāh because it increases the donor's wealth. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(قيل); QUSHAYRI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:4/q02 How does mention of zakāh fit the sūra's Meccan setting?  [ٱلزَّكَوٰةِ · sciences]
turns on: The notes distinguish the early duty or general charity from Medina's assessed levy; one argument instead takes the Meccan date as support for inner purity.
- 23:4/q02/p1 The basic obligation existed in Mecca; Medina later prescribed assessed thresholds. — Ibn Kathīr dates the fixed amounts to the second Hijrī year, citing 6:141 for an earlier duty. Al-Biqāʿī prefers this distinction, cites 6:141 and links payment to purification in 9:103. Al-Sāmarrāʾī transmits Ibn Kathīr's majority financial reading and its dating distinction. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL(ابن كثير); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(ابن كثير); BIQAI-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 23:4/q02/p2 The verse speaks of charity or financial aid before the later fixed levy. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Meccan Fussilat 41:6–7 and the earlier command to Ismāʿīl's household in 19:54–55. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī takes prayer's pairing with zakāh as evidence for giving. Iṣlāḥī says its legal form varies across religions. Kur'an Yolu says the Medinan prescription followed the Meccan sūra, while Meccan verses already urged spending, beneficence, and feeding; 9:60 calls obligatory recipients' allocation ṣadaqa. Study Quran notes a later technical yearly sense. [8 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI+]
- 23:4/q02/p3 The Meccan date supports reading zakāh as inner purification. — Al-Ālūsī reports that this reading avoids explaining how a later legal obligation appears here; al-Rāghib's purposive reading and al-Ṭībī's purification verses supply its sense. A scholar reported by al-Ālūsī calls the financial pairing with prayer stronger. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الراغب,الطيبي); ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة)-]

## 23:4/q03 What duty and social purpose does this trait convey?  [ٱلزَّكَوٰةِ · law]
turns on: The notes draw both the legal duty of wealth giving and its effects on faith and community.
- 23:4/q03/p1 Believers owe zakāh as worship and a condition of faith, subject to its legal conditions. — Al-Jishumī names the ownership threshold and a full year. Al-Naḥḥās says paying only the due still earns praise and nonpayers are the hoarders of gold and silver. Iṣlāḥī calls prayer and zakāh the two primary directives and grounds Abū Bakr's campaign against withholding zakāh in rejecting a foundational duty. [5 notes: JISHUMI; NAHHAS; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 23:4/q03/p2 Giving fulfills the needs and rights of others and helps make communal life fairer. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says laws answer human needs; necessities closest to nature deserve priority and neglecting them harms more than neglecting luxuries. Faith-driven giving helps people receive rights and share life's benefits. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls alms proper conduct toward those in need and an ethic of wealth. Iṣlāḥī says prayer connects one to God and zakāh to people; gratitude drives prayer, spending, and sacrifice. [5 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 23:4/q03/p3 The trait describes active, sustained faith leading to success. — Al-Jishumī says the specified traits remove ambiguity about who is a believer. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says living faith produces effects toward success. Asad connects humility in prayer and active inner purity with a happy state. Iṣlāḥī's summary proceeds from inward faith through prayer, zakāh, restraint, fidelity, and guarded prayer to success. [4 notes: JISHUMI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 23:4/q04 What does the lām before zakāh do?  [لِلزَّكَوٰةِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether zakāh is the participle's object or the goal of otherwise implicit good deeds.
- 23:4/q04/p1 It strengthens the active participle's government of its fronted object. — Abū Ḥayyān says a derived active participle has weaker verbal force: lām can reinforce its object before or after it, though fronting is commoner, and an accusative would also be Arabic. Al-Samīn calls it an added object marker; al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise connect reinforcement to the weak, following participle. Al-Sāmarrāʾī calls it lām al-taqwiya and compares 2:91. [9 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 23:4/q04/p2 It expresses purpose or cause: they act to attain purification or growth. — Abū Ḥayyān reports the growth reading with omitted good deeds. Al-Rāghib, through al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Sāmarrāʾī, says they worship so God purifies them or they purify themselves; the object of 'do' is omitted. Al-Ṭībī and al-Kashshāf support self-purification with 87:14–15 and 91:9. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the omission permits general good deeds. [9 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(الراغب,الطيبي); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو مسلم والراغب); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الراغب,صاحب الكشاف,قيل)]

## 23:4/q05 How do 'doers' and zakāh fit together grammatically?  [ٱلزَّكَوٰةِ فَٰعِلُونَ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical issue is whether zakāh names an act or an amount, and whether payment must be supplied or is already conveyed by the verb.
- 23:4/q05/p1 Zakāh is the act of giving or purification, which an agent can perform directly. — Al-Nasafī prefers the act of purification over the paid amount. Al-Zamakhsharī in al-Samīn, Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Sāmarrāʾī compares doing a blow or killing. Al-Ālūsī calls the verbal-noun sense apparent and says purification implies payment by metonymy. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī find an act natural with 'doers'; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that money itself is not an act. Al-Samīn notes both verbal noun and concrete amount senses. [15 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الكشاف); TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(البحر المحيط,الكشاف,روح المعاني,فتح القدير); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 23:4/q05/p2 Zakāh is the paid amount; an omitted 'performance' or 'payment' makes it the object of doing. — Al-Bayḍāwī allows the concrete amount after an elided genitive noun. Al-Nasafī, al-Zamakhsharī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī, al-Ṭabarīzī and al-Sāmarrāʾī supply adāʾ. Al-Zamakhsharī cites Umayya's plural zakawāt as paid amounts, claiming the verbal noun cannot be plural; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn's Shaykh and al-Ālūsī object that verbal nouns can be pluralized when kinds differ. Al-Samīn says the monetary reading is grammatically possible although humans do not create the amounts. Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects the needless ellipsis. [17 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(أمية بن أبي الصلت,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(التبريزي,الزمخشري); TABATABAI(بعض من حمل الزكاة على هذا المعنى); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(البحر المحيط,التبريزي,الكشاف,فتح القدير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 23:4/q05/p3 'Doers' itself means those who give or deliver zakāh. — Abū Isḥāq glosses it muʾtūn. Al-Tabrīzī, reported by Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī and al-Sāmarrāʾī, offers payers (muʾaddūn); al-Ālūsī notes the objection that faʿaltu al-zakāh is not idiomatic for paying. Al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn al-Jawzī explain that paying is an act. Al-Qurṭubī calls this eloquent usage. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(التبريزي); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(البحر المحيط,التبريزي,فتح القدير)]
- 23:4/q05/p4 Zakāh is the given object itself, and 'doing' means giving it without an omitted noun. — Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the verbal noun as naming its resulting object and cites 9:103; he argues that ordinary Arabic cannot make a verb govern its own cognate verbal noun as object. He equates the implied action with giving as in 5:55 and rejects al-Kashshāf's formally motivated act-or-ellipsis analysis. Al-Sāmarrāʾī likewise allows the concrete wealth under a reinforced object construction. [8 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 23:4/q06 Why call them 'doers' of zakāh?  [فَٰعِلُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether fāʿilūn emphasizes habitual action, prompt action, semantic breadth, or idiomatic payment; Arabic usage and Umayya's line are debated.
- 23:4/q06/p1 It presents giving as a continuing, defining practice. — Al-Khaṭṭābī prefers this as more complete and forceful than verbs merely recording a payment. Al-Nasafī says 'doers' suggests continuity more clearly than 'payers.' Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the expression stresses care and readiness, comparing a person's 'I will do it' when asked to drink water. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says nominal descriptions turn commands into stable traits. [4 notes: NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; KHATTABI+]
- 23:4/q06/p2 The broad verb evokes good action or carries financial and inward senses together. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the root is common in doing good and cites Muḥammad ibn Bashīr al-Khārijī's poem about spending and noble action. Al-Ālūsī says purification indirectly conveys the payment, more eloquently than 'they purify.' Al-Sāmarrāʾī says muʾtūn would narrow the phrase to payment, while omitting lām would lose purpose; the present wording gathers all senses. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(محمد بن بشير الخارجي); ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 23:4/q06/p3 The phrase is sound, eloquent Arabic for paying zakāh. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī cite Umayya ibn Abī al-Ṣalt's zakawāt line; Abū Ḥayyān says eloquence scholars accepted it, and al-Ālūsī notes hostile Arabs never faulted this wording. Al-Khaṭṭābī reports the objection that Arabic usually says give, pay or purify wealth; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī reject a non-Arab critic's demand for muʾaddūn as unsupported. Ibn ʿĀshūr doubts the poem's attribution and reports Ibn Qutayba's refusal to use Umayya against the Qurʾān. [9 notes: THALABI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أمية بن أبي الصلت); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(أمية بن أبي الصلت,ابن قتيبة); KHATTABI-]
- 23:4/q06/p4 The expression is questionable because ordinary Arabic uses 'give' or 'pay' zakāh. — Al-Khaṭṭābī records the objection without adopting it. Al-Ālūsī reports the narrower objection to al-Ṭabrīzī's gloss, and both al-Ālūsī and Abū Ḥayyān reject the non-Arab critic's proposed muʾaddūn. The poetry's attribution is doubted by Ibn ʿĀshūr. [1 notes: KHATTABI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-; THALABI-; QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 23:4/q06/p5 The verb faʿala suggests prompt action to meet the poor's needs. — Al-Burhān contrasts ʿamila for sustained good work with faʿala for hastening to do good, citing fa-stabiqū al-khayrāt. Al-Itqān likewise says ʿamila commonly denotes extended work, while faʿala marks quick action. [2 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN]

## 23:4/q07 What effect do the fronting and repeated relative construction have?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ لِلزَّكَوٰةِ فَٰعِلُونَ · rhetoric]
- 23:4/q07/p1 Fronting zakāh marks emphasis, care, or restriction to proper action. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says moving fāʿilūn before zakāh or altering lām changes the layered force; fronting can imply doing only good, including zakāh. Al-Ālūsī allows focus without restriction, or contrast with improper spending; he extends earlier explanations for 'turning away' from idle talk rather than mere nonparticipation. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the fronting and repeated relative follow the same construction as earlier traits. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 23:4/q08 How does this trait relate to prayer and the neighboring traits?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ ٱلزَّكَوٰةِ · links]
turns on: The notes connect zakāh to prayer as complementary duties and to the wider list of traits; one comparison distinguishes what completes each duty.
- 23:4/q08/p1 Prayer and zakāh pair bodily worship with financial worship. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī say this shows comprehensive devotion; al-Ālūsī says avoiding idle talk lies between them because it connects closely with prayer, although the Qurʾān often pairs the duties. Ibn ʿĀshūr also notes their frequent pairing and an earlier explained thematic reason for the interruption. Iṣlāḥī calls them faith's two arms: prayer joins one to God and zakāh to people; gratitude makes each complement the other. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 23:4/q08/p2 The traits collectively cover duties and identify successful believers. — Al-Jishumī says duties omitted by name fall under trusts and covenants, while sins fall under avoidance of laghw; the list clearly identifies believers. Iṣlāḥī traces faith through prayer, zakāh, restraint, fidelity and guarded prayer to lasting success, and summarizes the opening similarly. Study Quran describes the opening as believers' virtues and righteous acts. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says living faith works toward success. [6 notes: JISHUMI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI]
- 23:4/q08/p3 The prescribed payment completes zakāh, while prayer needs inward humility to complete its outward acts. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī distinguishes the two obligations: prayer also requires reflection, presence of heart and proper manners. He cites a Prophetic saying that one receives from prayer only what one consciously understands; Iṣlāḥī says earlier discussion of humble, fearful prayer preventing sin leads to further benefits. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 23:4/q09 How does the verse connect with other Qurʾānic passages or the wider sūra?  [* · links]
- 23:4/q09/p1 Al-Ḥajj's commands become the stable believer qualities listed here. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares bowing, prostration, doing good, establishing prayer and giving zakāh in 22:77–78 with 23:1–4; imperative actions become nominal descriptions of humility, avoiding idle talk and giving. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 23:4/q09/p2 This verse's zakāh is broader than al-Maʿārij's specified share for the asker and deprived. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares 70:24–25: this wording reaches financial worship across eight recipient classes and purification of the self as well as the specified share. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 23:4/q09/p3 Al-Nūr supplements al-Muʾminūn's opening account of faithful life. — Iṣlāḥī says faith illuminating the inner self brightens individual and collective life in the next sūra, while lack of faith darkens both. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 23:4/q09/p4 The sura's broader narrative repeatedly shows powerful and affluent opponents of prophets. — Study Quran cites parallels in 7:59–62 and 7:136. Al-Qurṭubī says the meanings of idle talk and zakāh were already treated at al-Baqara, mentioning 2:219. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 23:4/q10 What inward meaning is given to zakāh?  [ٱلزَّكَوٰةِ · inward]
- 23:4/q10/p1 It is detachment from one's own traits and self-witness. — Al-Qāshānī calls their zakāh detachment from their own traits. Al-Qushayrī takes zakāh as growth but says spiritual growth needs diminishing one's witness of self; complete servitude follows the dissolution of that witness. [2 notes: QASHANI; QUSHAYRI]

## 23:4/q11 What is reported about the first ten verses?  [* · reports]
- 23:4/q11/p1 Whoever upholds them enters Paradise. — Al-Wāḥidī reports ʿUmar describing the bee-like sound of revelation and the Prophet's supplication before relating this promise. [1 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB(عمر بن الخطاب)]

## 23:4/q12 When does wealth become zakāh?  [ٱلزَّكَوٰةِ · meaning]
- 23:4/q12/p1 Only when it reaches the poor recipient. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this view of when the paid wealth acquires the name zakāh. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]
