# 2:200: 36 questions

## 2:200/q01 What does qaḍaytum mean here?  [قَضَيْتُم · meaning]
turns on: Whether qaḍāʾ describes completing one's own act or beginning it.
- 2:200/q01/p1 To perform and bring the rites to completion. — Wahidi explains discharge of what was due with 2:117 and 62:10; Razi distinguishes completion of one's act from imposing another's and informing; Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan distinguish later performance of missed worship; Ibn Qutayba and Zajjaj derive sealing and finishing, citing 4:103 and 46:29. [15 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,ابن قتيبة,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ITQAN]

## 2:200/q02 What are the manāsik completed here?  [مَّنَٰسِكَكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the word denotes all Hajj acts, the sacrifice in particular, or ritual places with their acts understood.
- 2:200/q02/p1 All the rites and acts of worship of Hajj. — Zajjaj, Hasan, Ata and Ibn Atiyya take the whole pilgrimage; Ibn Atiyya prefers this and says Arafat marks a common completion point. Wahidi, Razi, Tabrisi and Ibn Ashur explain mansak as a verbal noun plural because worship has many acts and utterances; 2:128 is a parallel. The Prophet's instruction to learn his rites supports it for Qurtubi. [29 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(عطاء); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن البصري); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(بعض المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(عطاء,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 2:200/q02/p2 The sacrificial animals or slaughter and shedding of blood. — Tabari argues nasaka can describe slaughter and mansak is patterned like mashriq and maghrib; Tha'labi and Baghawi repeat the lexical point. Mujahid consistently glosses shedding blood; the sacrifice is the end of Hajj in Study Quran. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); JISHUMI(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); STUDYQURAN]
- 2:200/q02/p3 The ritual places, with the acts performed there understood. — Wahidi, Razi, Tabrisi, Abu Hayyan and Ibn Ashur supply an omitted noun, 'acts of the places'; mansak may name a place of worship or slaughter. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:200/q03 How is manāsikakum pronounced and formed?  [مَّنَٰسِكَكُمْ · readings]
turns on: Whether adjacent kāfs are pronounced separately or assimilated.
- 2:200/q03/p1 The two kāfs are pronounced distinctly by most readers. — Nahhas compares their separability with mandatory assimilation in 4:78; Samin says the inflectional vowel is normally preserved. Burhan and Itqan say the adjacent kāf pair without another letter occurs only here and in mā salakakum. [7 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); BURHAN; ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:200/q03/p2 Abu Amr, especially al-Susi, assimilates the first kāf into the second. — Tha'labi and Qurtubi give identity of consonants as the reason; Samin says the inflectional vowel is treated as a stem vowel and removed, unlike examples in 9:35 and 3:106. [5 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI(أبو عمرو); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:200/q04 When does the commanded remembrance occur relative to Hajj?  [فَإِذَا قَضَيْتُم مَّنَٰسِكَكُمْ فَٱذْكُرُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether qaḍaytum and the following fa put remembrance after completion or allow it during the rites.
- 2:200/q04/p1 After the rites are finished, especially at Mina after the Aqaba stoning. — Razi says qaḍāʾ signals completion and fa makes it consequent; Abu Hayyan adds the preceding fa. Ibn Ashur places it after departure from Muzdalifa and describes Aqaba stoning, sacrifice and ifada; Itqan identifies the later remembrance of 2:203 with stoning on Tashriq days. Baghawi locates the pilgrim at Mina. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ITQAN; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 2:200/q04/p2 The command can apply during the rites, at Arafat, the Sacred Monument, circumambulation, and saʿy. — Jishumi reports this alongside after-completion; Razi rejects it because qaḍāʾ denotes completion; Abu Hayyan rejects the beginning-of-Hajj reading because of completion and fa. [3 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعض المفسرين); RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 2:200/q05 Which Hajj practices are connected with the close of the rites?  [مَّنَٰسِكَكُمْ · law]
- 2:200/q05/p1 A pilgrim stones Aqaba on the Day of Sacrifice, then sacrifices and performs ifada; only sex remains forbidden. — Ibn Ashur places Aqaba stoning between sunrise and noon on the tenth and reports scholarly agreement on the sequence. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:200/q05/p2 The talbiya ceases at Mina on reaching and casting the first pebble; other stopping points are also reported. — Jishumi reports Malik's alternatives for a resident: after Arafat, touching the Black Stone, or seeing the House. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 2:200/q05/p3 Arafah is a day of eating, drinking and takbir, so pilgrims should not fast. — Durr transmits Ibn Abbas's advice to men and women. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 2:200/q06 What kind of remembrance does the command specify?  [فَٱذْكُرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether it is a particular pilgrimage formula, supplication, or continuing worship and praise.
- 2:200/q06/p1 Broad remembrance of God: praise, thanks, magnification, monotheism, obedience and worship. — Tabari prefers obedience and humility and links takbir in 2:203; Zajjaj calls for tawhid and recounting blessings; Ibn Ashur says worship in deed, speech and belief. Razi says these readings converge on constant remembrance and seeking needs from God. [21 notes: TAB-FULL(أنس); ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); BAGHAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+]
- 2:200/q06/p2 Takbir on the days of Mina or after prayers on Sacrifice and Tashriq days. — Tabari links it to 2:203 as the newly prescribed remembrance; Razi says no other specific formula follows Hajj and notes disagreement on its timing; Itqan identifies Aqaba stoning and 2:203's Tashriq stoning. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ITQAN; ELMALILI]
- 2:200/q06/p3 All supplications at the sacred stations. — Mawardi, Wahidi, Jishumi and Tabrisi report this; Tabrisi says prayer there is better than elsewhere. Razi links it to humility, forgiveness after hardship, travel and expense, as with prayer after ṣalāt. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); TAB-FULL]
- 2:200/q06/p4 Invocation over the sacrificial animal. — Razi and Abu Hayyan report this as a specific reading tied to the completed sacrifice. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:200/q07 Why is remembrance commanded after the rites?  [فَإِذَا قَضَيْتُم مَّنَٰسِكَكُمْ فَٱذْكُرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ · rhetoric]
- 2:200/q07/p1 To displace idle ancestral boasting and sustain gratitude and worship when worshippers might relax after Hajj. — Biqai and Tabatabai note the risk of forgetfulness after completion; Ibn Ashur links the renewed command with forgiveness and prevention of argument; Elmalılı says Hajj's awakening should continue and expose human frailty and worldly transience. [10 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 2:200/q07/p2 To move from bodily service in the rites to lifelong heartful love and remembrance. — Qushayri reads the transition inwardly as the body's duty followed by the heart's. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 2:200/q08 What pre-Islamic practice occasioned the comparison with fathers?  [كَذِكْرِكُمْ ءَابَآءَكُمْ · reports]

Muqatil and Farra place them between mosque and mountain; Qatada describes circles at Mina; Ibn Abbas describes all-day recitation. Many sources relate the same custom. Al-Qaffal's report of the Prophet at the conquest condemns tribal pride and cites 49:13. Nasafi compares Quraysh's ancestral remembrance, or that of a group surpassing them. - 2:200/q09/p3 Remember God as fathers remember their children. — Ikrima and Dahhak say parents are entrusted with their child; al-Qurazi's reading makes fathers the ones doing the remembering. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عكرمة، الضحاك); THALABI(القرظي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:200/q10 Why does the verse name fathers rather than mothers?  [ءَابَآءَكُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether fathers denote reverence specifically or stand for both parents.
- 2:200/q10/p1 Fathers are remembered with reverence; mothers with compassion. — Qushayri says God is revered and shows mercy rather than receiving mercy. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 2:200/q10/p2 Fathers stand for both parents in ordinary speech. — Wahidi, Razi and others compare 16:81, where clothing protecting from heat implies protection from cold; the child calls both father and mother. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الربيع والضحاك); RAZI-FULL(الضحاك والربيع); THALABI(ابن عباس وعطاء والربيع والضحاك); ASAD-NOTES(Ad-Dahhak, Ar-Rabi and Abu Muslim)]

## 2:200/q11 Why should remembrance of God exceed remembrance of fathers?  [أَوْ أَشَدَّ ذِكْرًۭا · interpretation]
- 2:200/q11/p1 God is the ultimate source of the parents' virtues and every blessing, and has the greater claim. — Muqatil, Farra, Zajjaj and others trace ancestral good to God. Razi contrasts limited ancestral merits with God's endless perfections; Qushayri says parents raised one but God's favor is more complete. Biqai notes parents may also mislead. Tabatabai highlights divine guidance. [22 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); THALABI(أكثر المفسرين); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI(أنس والحسن وقتادة); RAZI-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); BURSEVI(السعدي); TABATABAI]

## 2:200/q12 What force has 'or stronger remembrance'?  [أَوْ أَشَدَّ ذِكْرًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether it means more intense, more frequent, or both.
- 2:200/q12/p1 Remember God at least as intensely, indeed more intensely, than fathers. — Muqatil and Wahidi take the comparative literally. Razi and Qaffal say Arabic advances from an initial comparison to the stronger one without uncertainty. Ibn Kathir compares 2:74, 4:77, 37:147 and 53:9. Ibn Ashur calls it upward transition; Tabatabai allows intensity in quality. [15 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القفال); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي وابن جنى); TABATABAI]
- 2:200/q12/p2 The comparative particularly calls for more frequent and abundant remembrance. — Tha'labi, Baghawi and Nasafi gloss ashadd as more abundant; Abu Hayyan and Ibn Ashur stress repeated remembrance filling free time. [6 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 2:200/q13 What does 'aw' mean in the comparison?  [أَوْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether it shifts to a stronger claim, permits either measure, or gives a choice.
- 2:200/q13/p1 It means 'rather' or marks an upward shift to stronger remembrance. — Ibn Jawzi reports bal; Razi's Qaffal says this is ordinary Arabic advancement; Ibn Ashur cites Abu Ali and Ibn Jinni and 37:147; Tabatabai calls it correction. [8 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(القفال); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي وابن جنى); TABATABAI]
- 2:200/q13/p2 It can act like 'and'. — Ibn Jawzi and Jishumi record this equivalence. [2 notes: JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 2:200/q13/p3 It allows either of the two degrees of remembrance. — Abu Hayyan and Samin report a permissive sense. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:200/q13/p4 It offers a choice between two alternatives. — Abu Hayyan and Samin record this analysis. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:200/q14 What does dhikr mean in this context and in Arabic usage?  [ذِكْرًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether dhikr denotes mental remembering, spoken mention, or disparaging mention.
- 2:200/q14/p1 Remembering, as opposed to forgetting, including inward presence. — Wahidi cites Moses's companion at 18:63; Tabatabai says spoken words disclose the heart's presence and cites remembrance in all postures at 3:191. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABATABAI]
- 2:200/q14/p2 Spoken mention and remembrance by the tongue. — Damghani and Itqan gloss verbal mention; Wahidi notes nondisparaging speech and 2:203. [3 notes: DAMGHANI; ITQAN; WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 2:200/q14/p3 Mention can also mean disparagement. — Wahidi cites a report about idols at 21:60 and Adi ibn Hatim's poetic yadhkurukum. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 2:200/q15 How does the kāf comparison work grammatically?  [كَذِكْرِكُمْ · grammar]
- 2:200/q15/p1 The kāf phrase modifies an understood accusative verbal noun: remember with remembrance like yours. — Nahhas says it can be a verbal-noun modifier or circumstantial; Zajjaj and Qurtubi make it accusative; Ibn Ashur says it describes manner. Samin compares the kāf in 2:198. [5 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:200/q16 How is 'your fathers' read and construed?  [ءَابَآءَكُمْ · readings]
turns on: Whether fathers are the object of the people's remembrance or the agent remembering children.
- 2:200/q16/p1 The majority read accusative ābāʾakum: people remember their fathers. — Samin says the verbal noun is annexed to its agent, the addressees. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:200/q16/p2 Muhammad ibn Kaʿb reads nominative ābāʾukum: fathers remember their children. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan describe annexation of the verbal noun to its object; Abu Hayyan says a parent repeatedly mentions a child. Samin explains the syntax but its note glosses the child's remembrance. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي); SAMIN-DURR(محمد بن كعب)]
- 2:200/q16/p3 A singular reading abākum is also attributed to Muhammad ibn Kaʿb. — Abu Hayyan says the plural audience gives a collective sense; Samin says the singular generically names father and follows the majority construction; nominative under a shortened-noun dialect is remote. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(محمد بن كعب); SAMIN-DURR(محمد بن كعب)]

## 2:200/q17 How are ashadda and dhikran parsed?  [أَشَدَّ ذِكْرًۭا · grammar]
turns on: The competing parses choose genitive coordination, accusative ellipsis, or a fronted circumstance, with dispute over synonymous tamyīz.
- 2:200/q17/p1 Ashadda is genitive by coordination with dhikrikum under the kāf; dhikran is a specification. — Zajjaj, Nahhas and others note the diptote afʿal adjective has fatḥa for genitive. Abu Hayyan objects that this figuratively gives remembrance a remembrance; Bursawi defends personification. Ibn Ashur permits synonym tamyīz, citing Sibawayh and Jarir, while Samin questions its normal grammar. Abu Hayyan notes that a same-kind comparison normally takes the genitive, which motivated competing analyses. Ibn Arafa calls the syntax exceptionally difficult and praises al-Tayyibi's clarification, relaying Ibn Abd al-Salam's approval. [25 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI(الزجاج); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج وابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN(الجمهور); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عرفة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 2:200/q17/p2 Ashadda is accusative under an implied 'remember Him more intensely' or 'be more intense'. — Al-Akhfash, Makkī and Abu al-Baqa supply a verb; Abu al-Baqa prefers this to figurative personification. Zajjaj and Ibn Atiyya allow the same sense. [14 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI(الأخفش); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,مكي); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 2:200/q17/p3 Ashadda is accusative as a circumstance modifying the delayed dhikran. — Abu Hayyan prefers the direct order dhikran ashadda and cites the Layla poem; an intervening circumstance can separate a conjunct. Samin reports his two analyses of dhikran, as governed by fadhkurū or coordinated by syntactic position. Alusi reports an objection that this yields remembering while strong rather than strong remembrance; Ibn Ashur stresses cadence and separation of repeated dhikr. [13 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL(البحر); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:200/q17/p4 Ashadda coordinates with the genitive pronoun in dhikrikum without repeating its governor. — Zamakhshari's sense is remembrance by people stronger than yours. Samin notes the Basran ban; Ibn Ashur cites Kufians, Ibn Malik, Hamza's reading at 4:1, and Ibn al-Hajib's restriction to annexation. [8 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن الحاجب,الزمخشري,الكوفيون وابن مالك,حمزة)]
- 2:200/q17/p5 Ashadda coordinates with the accusative ābāʾakum, so God is more mentioned than fathers or stronger rememberers are implied. — Zamakhshari and Baydawi offer this; Abu Hayyan calls the resulting agent and object relation awkward. Alusi takes remembrance passively; Ibn Ashur rejects Zamakhshari's strained alternatives. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; IBNASHUR-FULL(صاحب الكشاف)-]
- 2:200/q17/p6 Ashadda coordinates with the syntactic position of the kāf, modifying an omitted verbal noun. — Abu Ali and Ibn Jinni treat remembrance figuratively as a rememberer; Ibn Ashur calls their version still more strained. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو علي وابن جني); SAMIN-DURR(أبو علي); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن جني)-]

## 2:200/q18 Why is the comparative phrase delayed?  [أَوْ أَشَدَّ ذِكْرًۭا · rhetoric]
- 2:200/q18/p1 Delay highlights intensity, preserves the pause cadence, and avoids repeating dhikr immediately after fadhkurū. — Abu Hayyan and Ibn Ashur explain the fronted circumstance and later noun; Al-Bahr is quoted similarly by Alusi. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البحر); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:200/q19 How does the worldly-prayer clause connect to the command?  [فَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ · links]
turns on: Whether it divides present pilgrims or compares different historical believers.
- 2:200/q19/p1 It divides the addressed pilgrims or supplicants by what they seek. — The fa marks a division for Ibn Ashur; Abu Hayyan says supplication, unlike praise, admits a worldly-only and a two-world form, and rejects a Sufi third group that makes no request. Ibn Kathir says prayer follows remembrance at a responsive time. The groups include Muslims and polytheists before 9:28. Abu Hayyan says the shift from direct address to third person excludes people whose worldly-only prayer is unworthy of a rational addressee. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 2:200/q19/p2 It contrasts those remembering God before the Prophet's mission with believers after it. — Abu Hayyan reports this historical reading but prefers a division among pilgrims. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:200/q20 How is min al-nās man yaqūlu parsed?  [مِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ مَن يَقُولُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether man is subject, adjective or agent.
- 2:200/q20/p1 Man is the subject, with the preceding prepositional phrase as predicate; yaqūlu is its relative clause. — Nahhas and Qurtubi permit subject status; Samin calls it strongest. [4 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:200/q20/p2 Man is an adjectival or indefinite noun qualified by the following clause. — Nahhas, Qurtubi and Samin record this analysis. [3 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:200/q20/p3 Man can function as an agent. — Samin attributes the option to al-Akhfash. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش)]

## 2:200/q21 Why does the supplication use plural 'us' with singular man?  [مَن يَقُولُ رَبَّنَآ ءَاتِنَا · grammar]
- 2:200/q21/p1 Man is singular in form but plural in meaning, so a group says 'grant us'. — Abu Hayyan says singular prayer might suggest few worldly seekers; Samin says the clause follows meaning rather than form. Biqai says the later singular pronoun follows form and suggests few of this community will be lost. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:200/q22 Who are the people who ask only for this world?  [فَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ مَن يَقُولُ رَبَّنَآ ءَاتِنَا فِى ٱلدُّنْيَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the historical referent is polytheists specifically or the warning also includes believers.
- 2:200/q22/p1 Historically they are polytheists or disbelieving pre-Islamic pilgrims. — Zajjaj, Wahidi and Ibn Ashur say disbelief in resurrection explains their sole worldly concern; Ibn Zayd distinguishes the Messenger, unbelievers and hypocrites. Ibn Abbas describes Bedouin requests at the pilgrimage standing. [24 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA(أهل الجاهلية); TAB-FULL(أبو بكر بن عياش,أبو وائل,ابن زيد,السدي); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو وائل والسدي وابن زيد); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو وائل والسدي وابن زيد); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,عبد الله بن الزبير); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 2:200/q22/p2 The warning can also apply to a believer who confines supplication and effort to worldly aims. — Qurtubi says such a believer lacks a share like the one who seeks the afterlife; Qatada describes a servant working for this world; Tabatabai speaks of generic people, including unbelievers. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; QASHANI]

## 2:200/q23 What do the worldly-only petitioners ask for?  [رَبَّنَآ ءَاتِنَا فِى ٱلدُّنْيَا · interpretation]
- 2:200/q23/p1 Worldly goods such as provision, wealth, livestock, servants, status, rain, victory, safe travel and offspring. — Mujahid names victory and provision; Anas reports naked circumambulators asking for rain, victory and safe return; Ibn Abbas lists fertile years and children. Tha'labi notes that the object is omitted, so the examples do not limit it. Elmalılı reports Razi's wider classes of bodily, external and even spiritual goods sought for worldly status. [35 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; FARRA(أهل الجاهلية); TAB-FULL(أبو بكر بن عياش,أبو وائل,أنس,السدي,قتادة,مجاهد); THALABI(أنس,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو وائل والسدي وابن زيد); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو وائل والسدي وابن زيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(أنس بن مالك,ابن عباس,عبد الله بن الزبير); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI(İmam Fahreddin Râzî); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 2:200/q24 How is the object of ātinā understood?  [ءَاتِنَا فِى ٱلدُّنْيَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether a second object is omitted or fī is altered.
- 2:200/q24/p1 The second object is omitted: grant us whatever worldly benefit we seek. — Wahidi, Tha'labi, Abu Hayyan, Samin and Biqai say the request is left general or understood. Kuran Yolu explains the omission as indiscriminate desire; Zajjaj pauses at ātinā because it is supplication. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 2:200/q24/p2 Fī means min, making the request 'give us from the world'. — Abu Hayyan and Samin reject this alternative because it improperly omits both objects. [0 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 2:200/q24/p3 Fī is extra, making the world the direct object. — Abu Hayyan and Samin reject the parse; Abu Hayyan says only one object can be suppressed. [0 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 2:200/q24/p4 An implied continuation says God grants what He chooses rather than necessarily the request. — Biqai records this alternative to a simple unstated object. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:200/q25 Is 'says' literal speech or an inward disposition?  [يَقُولُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the prayer is spoken or represents a person's state.
- 2:200/q25/p1 It depicts a person's inward aim, whether or not the words are spoken. — Tabatabai says the worldly seeker treats desire as good, while the seeker of God asks only for genuine good. [2 notes: TABATABAI]
- 2:200/q25/p2 It recounts actual prayers made at Hajj. — Anas, Ibn Abbas and Ibn al-Zubayr relate spoken requests for rain, offspring, camels and sheep. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو بكر بن عياش,أنس); DURR-FULL(أنس بن مالك,ابن عباس,عبد الله بن الزبير); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس)]

## 2:200/q26 What does 'no share in the Hereafter' mean?  [وَمَا لَهُۥ فِى ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ مِنْ خَلَٰقٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether it denies a reward there or describes a refusal to seek one.
- 2:200/q26/p1 They receive no share of good, divine reward or Paradise there. — Tabari says they traded the afterlife for worldly adornment; Baydawi says their concern is confined here; Tabatabai infers their works are nullified, contrasting 2:201–202 and citing 25:23, 46:20 and 18:105. Ibn Kathir says the warning deters imitation. [24 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو وائل والسدي وابن زيد); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:200/q26/p2 They have no desire to seek an afterlife share. — Abu Hayyan records this as an alternative sense; Biqai says they neither desire nor strive for it, and Elmalılı says they ask for none. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 2:200/q27 What does khalāq mean?  [خَلَٰقٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether it denotes an allotted share, an abundant share, or a share sought or merited.
- 2:200/q27/p1 An allotted share or portion of good. — Muqatil cites 9:69; Tabrisi derives a measured entitlement and also relates it to khuluq, noble character; Ibn Abbas cites a line by Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt and parallel verses. Harali relates the share to created nature and character. [14 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA(أهل الجاهلية); TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس)]
- 2:200/q27/p2 A substantial or abundant share of good. — Zajjaj and Tabrisi specify abundance, explaining the force of denying khalāq. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ; TABRISI]
- 2:200/q27/p3 A sought share or allotment. — Baydawi records this alternate nuance. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 2:200/q28 What is the syntax of min khalāq and fī l-ākhirah?  [فِى ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ مِنْ خَلَٰقٍۢ · grammar]
- 2:200/q28/p1 Min before khalāq is extra after negation, making the denial comprehensive. — Nahhas, Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi and Qurtubi describe the construction. [4 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 2:200/q28/p2 Khalāq is the subject, lahu its predicate, and fī l-ākhirah attaches to the predicate or is circumstantial. — Tabrisi gives both attachments. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 2:200/q29 What follows for prayer and life from the contrast with 2:201?  [فَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ مَن يَقُولُ رَبَّنَآ ءَاتِنَا فِى ٱلدُّنْيَا · links]
- 2:200/q29/p1 Seek good in both lives, not solely this world's benefits. — Tabari says ask God's pleasure in both; Wahidi, Durr and Ibn Zayd contrast the two prayers. Ibn Kathir treats the censure as a deterrent. Qushayri calls preservation of faith and forgiveness comprehensive goods, and transmits an inward reading of renouncing the world and separation's fire. Bursawi mentions health and sufficiency. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 2:200/q29/p2 The worldly-only seeker and the God-oriented seeker differ by what they count as good. — Tabatabai says desire defines good for the first, while the second asks for what God approves; Qashani says low aspiration and dark attachment block the higher. [3 notes: TABATABAI; QASHANI]

## 2:200/q30 What worldly good is named in interpretations of the next verse's prayer?  [فِى ٱلدُّنْيَا · links]
- 2:200/q30/p1 Worldly good can be righteous family, health and sufficiency, knowledge or worship, wealth, abundant provision, blessing, contentment, success, protection, or children. — Abu Hayyan lists Ali's wife, Qatada's health and money, Hasan's knowledge or worship, Suddi/Abu Wa'il/Ibn Zayd's wealth, Muqatil's provision, Ibn Qutayba's blessing, and other suggestions. He also calls hasanah unrestricted. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن قتيبة,الحسن,السدي وأبو وائل وابن زيد,علي,قتادة,مقاتل)]

## 2:200/q31 How does this passage connect with neighboring verses?  [* · links]
- 2:200/q31/p1 The remembrance and supplicant contrast continues into 2:201–204 and later seekers of God's pleasure. — Ibn Zayd identifies a third group in 2:204; Abu Hayyan says 2:204–207 contrasts sweet speech with a person who gives himself for God, so outward speech should match belief; Tabatabai says God swiftly grants according to the servant's aim. Itqan connects 2:198's repeated remembrance and ifada to this verse. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; ITQAN]

## 2:200/q32 What inward lessons are drawn from the pilgrimage and its remembrance?  [* · inward]
- 2:200/q32/p1 Rites subdue the lower self; remembrance illuminates the heart and demands continual love, purity and freedom from pride. — Razi describes negating all but the Real and then affirming Him; Qushayri contrasts bodily service with the heart's love and urges pride in God, reverent intimacy and tireless remembrance. Qashani urges study with companions and guidance of others; Elmalılı sees Hajj as renouncing shirk, pride and sensual drives. [15 notes: RAZI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; ELMALILI]

## 2:200/q33 What do the cited digressions say about jinn and devils?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether jinn are airy bodies, immaterial spirits, or unreal.
- 2:200/q33/p1 They are airy rational bodies able to take forms and perform difficult acts. — Razi reports this as one account. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القول الأول)]
- 2:200/q33/p2 They are immaterial entities with ranks of spirits, from angels to governing spirits. — Razi reports many holders; he describes associations with Throne, Kursi, spheres and natural things, citing 21:19, 69:17 and 39:75. He says luminous good spirits are righteous jinn and dark evil ones devils. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(كثير من الناس)]
- 2:200/q33/p3 Their existence is denied on perceptual and physical grounds. — Deniers argue dense bodies would be visible, subtle bodies could not endure winds or labor, and long contact should yield observed effects; Razi says jinn and devils are not ordinary dense bodies. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(المنكرون لوجود الجن والشياطين)]

## 2:200/q34 What does the cited negative-noun poetry illustrate?  [* · grammar]
- 2:200/q34/p1 Arabic poetry mixes nominative and accusative indefinite nouns after negation, sometimes under implicit negation. — Farra cites Umayya's 'no idle speech nor sin' and another poet; Abu al-Jarrah's line has nominative kilāhumā with an implicit 'not one of two,' and another line similarly supplies an unstated negation. Farra says this is poetic usage, not a canonical reading. [5 notes: FARRA(أبو الجراح,أمية,الآخر,الشاعر)]

## 2:200/q35 What do the cited digressions say about verse placement and compilation?  [* · sciences]
- 2:200/q35/p1 Placement of verses within sūras was fixed by prophetic instruction; compilation proceeded in stages. — Burhan quotes Abu Bakr al-Qadi, Makki and others on Gabriel's instruction, basmala and al-Tawba; Zayd's report describes compilation around the Prophet, graded sound by Hakim. Burhan distinguishes later collection under Abu Bakr and sura order under Uthman, and reports preference for Zayd's reading over Ubayy's. [8 notes: BURHAN(البيهقي عن زيد بن ثابت,الحاكم,القاضي أبو بكر,مكي وغيره)]

## 2:200/q36 What distinction is proposed between sīn and sawfa?  [* · grammar]
- 2:200/q36/p1 One objection says sīn may promise this-worldly fulfillment and sawfa the afterlife. — Burhan reports this proposal. [1 notes: BURHAN]
