# 58:6: 23 questions

## 58:6/q01 How does this resurrection scene continue the preceding warning?  [يَوْمَ يَبْعَثُهُمُ ٱللَّهُ جَمِيعًۭا · links]
- 58:6/q01/p1 It specifies the day of the humiliating punishment threatened in the preceding verse and completes the warning with resurrection and reckoning. — Al-Wahidi, al-Razi, and Tabataba'i attach the punishment to this day (58:5); al-Razi says it completes worldly abasement with punishment in the afterlife. Biqa'i moves from God's limits, implied promise and threat to punishment and reassurance of believers' victory. Islahi calls worldly punishment a prelude to the afterlife and says opponents cannot prevail; Tabataba'i prefers reading verses 5–6 as explaining the preceding prohibition, while allowing that they could begin a new discussion. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI+]

## 58:6/q02 What governs the accusative yawm?  [يَوْمَ · grammar]
turns on: Which preceding expression or supplied verb supplies the time phrase's syntactic governor; the later aḥṣāhu and al-kāfirīn attachments draw express criticism.
- 58:6/q02/p1 The preceding humiliating punishment, or its qualifying word or implied predicate, governs it. — Al-Zajjaj and al-Nahhas attach it to the punishment of 58:5; Ibn Atiyya and al-Nasafi specify mahīn, while Ibn 'Ashur allows either mahīn or an implied predicate of the unbelievers' punishment. Al-Alusi allows the constituent attached to the preceding lām or mahīn as transmitted in his note. Al-Wahidi says that the preceding punishment happens on this day. Al-Samin reports its temporal attachment to 'adhāb mahīn. Al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Ibn Atiyya, Ibn 'Ashur, Study Quran, and Islahi also mention an omitted 'remember' as an alternative; Islahi prefers that alternative. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 58:6/q02/p2 An omitted command to remember or mention the Day governs it. — Al-Zamakhshari, as reported by al-Samin, uses this ellipsis to magnify the Day; al-Qurtubi, al-Nasafi, al-Bursevi and al-Alusi also connect it with awe. Ibn 'Ashur calls it a familiar Quranic construction and compares 2:30. Islahi prefers it on standard Arabic principles. Study Quran takes it as an invitation to recall the gathering for judgment on an open barren plain, citing 18:47, 20:106, 39:69–70 and 79:14. Ibn Atiyya and al-Baydawi offer it alongside attachment to the preceding warning. [13 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 58:6/q02/p3 An omitted passive verb or statement of their state governs it. — Abu al-Baqa, reported by al-Samin, supplies 'they are humiliated' or 'they are punished,' or takes their state as established on that day. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]
- 58:6/q02/p4 The preceding 'for them' governs it through its predicate's implied state. — Al-Samin reports this as al-Zamakhshari's additional connection. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)]
- 58:6/q02/p5 A supplied 'it will be' answers when their punishment occurs. — Al-Alusi reports an omitted yakūn as the answer: it will be on the day of resurrection. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 58:6/q02/p6 The later 'He counted it' governs yawm. — Al-Samin reports Abu al-Baqa's attachment to aḥṣāhu but finds it awkward because the pronoun in aḥṣāhu refers to what they did. [0 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-]
- 58:6/q02/p7 The preceding 'unbelievers' governs yawm. — Al-Alusi calls this proposed parse baseless. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 58:6/q03 Is yawm inflected or built here?  [يَوْمَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether a time noun before a future verb can be built, as the Kufans infer from its meaning of idhā, or must remain inflected under the Basran restriction.
- 58:6/q03/p1 It is an inflected accusative governed by the preceding punishment. — Al-Nahhas says Basran grammarians reject building yawm before a future verb; they permit that only before a past verb or an indeclinable form. [1 notes: NAHHAS]
- 58:6/q03/p2 It is built because it has the sense of idhā. — Al-Nahhas reports this as the Kufan analysis. [1 notes: NAHHAS(الكوفيون); NAHHAS-]

## 58:6/q04 Who are the people raised and addressed?  [يَبْعَثُهُمُ فَيُنَبِّئُهُم · referent]
turns on: How far the plural pronouns extend beyond the immediate opponents and secret plotters.
- 58:6/q04/p1 The hypocrites involved in secret talk are in view. — Muqatil connects the verse with hypocrites and their secret conversations. Islahi says they whispered evil suggestions, maligned Islam, disrespected the Prophet, mistook divine forbearance for approval, and were exposed by God's knowledge of every whisper. [4 notes: MUQATIL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 58:6/q04/p2 Hypocrites and Jews in Medina who plotted against believers are in view. — Ibn Atiyya says the verses through the end of the private-talk passage were revealed about people who harassed believers, plotted, wished them harm, and whispered. Islahi describes apparent Muslims nurtured by Jews and operating as their agents. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 58:6/q04/p3 The pronouns refer to opponents of God and His Messenger, including opponents mentioned before them. — Ibn 'Ashur explicitly extends the plural pronouns to both sets. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 58:6/q04/p4 Disbelievers are explicit, and believers are included implicitly in the resurrection. — Biqa'i says God restores both groups to life together. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 58:6/q05 What resurrection scene is described?  [يَوْمَ يَبْعَثُهُمُ ٱللَّهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the scene is stated as raising from graves for public judgment or described through the bodies' resting places.
- 58:6/q05/p1 God raises the dead from their graves on the Day of Judgment and assembles them for recompense. — Al-Nahhas, Ibn al-Jawzi and al-Qurtubi mention graves; al-Qurtubi specifies men and women. Al-Bursevi says they are revived for recompense. Ibn Kathir places the first and last people together on one plain. Study Quran describes an open barren plain and cites 18:47, 20:106, 39:69–70 and 79:14. Muqatil includes all earlier and later people. [7 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN]
- 58:6/q05/p2 Their raising is from the resting places of their bodies. — Al-Qashani gives this inward account of resurrection. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 58:6/q06 What does jamīʿan add?  [جَمِيعًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether jamīʿan emphasizes the completeness of those resurrected, their collective assembly, or the complete assembly of the conspiracies' elements.
- 58:6/q06/p1 No one is left out of resurrection. — Al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, al-Bursevi and al-Alusi give the universal emphasis. Muqatil and Ibn Kathir include first and last people; al-Qurtubi explicitly includes men and women. Al-Bursevi treats it as emphasis on the pronoun. [7 notes: MUQATIL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 58:6/q06/p2 They are gathered together at one time or place. — Al-Zajjaj specifies one time; al-Nahhas makes jamīʿan a circumstantial accusative; Ibn 'Ashur attaches that circumstance to the plural pronoun. Al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi and al-Bursevi offer assembled together as an alternative to universal emphasis; al-Alusi specifies one plain. Al-Qurtubi says all at once; Ibn Kathir says first and last on one plain, and Biqa'i describes all gathered together. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 58:6/q06/p3 Every element and motive of the conspiracies is assembled and attributed to its proposer or follower. — Islahi extends jamīʿan to the full disclosure of each person's part in the schemes. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 58:6/q07 What does God's informing them of their deeds do?  [فَيُنَبِّئُهُم بِمَا عَمِلُوٓا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the wording foregrounds factual disclosure and proof, public disgrace, manifested deed-forms, or the consequences of the deeds.
- 58:6/q07/p1 He tells them all their deeds, including hidden acts, to establish the proof against them. — Al-Zajjaj and al-Wahidi stress their recognition of binding proof. Al-Nahhas includes concealed acts; Ibn al-Jawzi includes sins and neglected duties, public and secret. Ibn Kathir includes good and evil, while al-Qurtubi says worldly acts. Biqa'i says the disclosure is full and exhaustive; Tabataba'i calls it the full reality of all they did. Al-Tabari and al-Baghawi place the disclosure at resurrection. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 58:6/q07/p2 He exposes and rebukes them publicly before witnesses, increasing shame and punishment. — Al-Baydawi says exposure establishes punishment; al-Nasafi adds that disgrace makes them wish to be hurried to Hell. Al-Bursevi says mere information would serve no purpose. Elmalili stresses humiliation; Ibn 'Ashur says the threat exposes hypocrisy. Biqa'i says disclosure exposes them. Al-Qushayri says recalled sins evoke shame and regret eclipsing every punishment, as Biqa'i also reports. [8 notes: QUSHAYRI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(القشيري); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 58:6/q07/p3 Their deeds themselves appear in terrifying forms suited to the afterlife. — Al-Bursevi and al-Alusi offer manifestation in forms before witnesses as a means of terror, shame and intensified punishment; al-Bursevi also allows simply stating that the deeds issued from them. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 58:6/q07/p4 The deeds are disclosed through forms impressed on the tablets of their souls. — Al-Qashani gives the impression of deed-forms on souls as the reason for disclosure. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 58:6/q07/p5 Informing them includes showing their deeds' consequences and the basis of requital. — Islahi says they will face what they did; Ibn 'Ashur calls the wording a euphemism for requital. Al-Alusi reports that showing forgotten deeds makes clear that the punishment they witness follows from them, deepening reproach and regret. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 58:6/q08 What does God's iḥṣāʾ of the deeds mean?  [أَحْصَىٰهُ ٱللَّهُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word denotes exact enumeration alone or enumeration that also entails comprehensive control.
- 58:6/q08/p1 God exactly enumerated, knew, recorded and preserved every deed without omission. — Muqatil, al-Tabari, al-Nahhas, al-Wahidi and others contrast God's preservation with human forgetting. Al-Qurtubi and Elmalili specify written records; al-Qurtubi says their presentation strengthens proof. Biqa'i includes number, quality, time and place; al-Alusi says exhaustive enumeration answers how passing deeds can be reported. Ibn 'Ashur says God knew details all along and compares the Book containing every small and great deed in 18:49; Study Quran cites 18:49, 6:59 and 17:13–14 for the registry. Al-Qashani describes recording in four books. Al-Raghib, reported by al-Bursevi and Tabataba'i, derives the term from counting with pebbles, with fingers used for comparison; the reports differ in which counted with pebbles and which with fingers. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 58:6/q08/p2 Iḥṣāʾ is counting with complete control, a narrower sense than ordinary counting. — Al-Bursevi reports this anonymous gloss, which makes comprehensive command part of the word's meaning. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 58:6/q09 How is the clause 'and they forgot it' grammatically related?  [أَحْصَىٰهُ ٱللَّهُ وَنَسُوهُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether wa nasūhu describes the circumstances of the deeds or advances the sequence leading to disclosure and reproach.
- 58:6/q09/p1 The counting and forgetting form a circumstantial clause attached to 'what they did.' — Ibn 'Ashur says its point lies particularly in the forgetting; al-Alusi allows the wāw clause as circumstantial, with or without an implicit qad. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 58:6/q09/p2 The forgetting is a subsequent premise for God's disclosure of their deeds. — Al-Alusi reports the alternative sequence: they forgot the deeds, so God shows them what caused their punishment, increasing regret. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 58:6/q10 Why did they forget their deeds?  [وَنَسُوهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether forgetfulness stems from the volume of deeds, indifference to sins, or absorption in bodily pleasures.
- 58:6/q10/p1 The deeds were too numerous to retain in detail. — Al-Baydawi and al-Bursevi give their abundance as one cause; Biqa'i calls the total immense. Ibn Atiyya says an unbeliever literally does not retain the details. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 58:6/q10/p2 They were heedless of their sins and treated them as unimportant. — Al-Nasafi says people remember only what matters to them. Biqa'i calls their disregard of forgotten sins audacity toward God. Al-Bursevi adds that they did not believe those acts would matter. Ibn 'Ashur sees their forgetting during life as evidence of heedlessness and delusion, making the record astonishing. Al-Sulami reports that forgetting offenses without weeping, repentance or seeking forgiveness wastes life. [7 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 58:6/q10/p3 Sensual pleasures and bodily preoccupations absorbed them. — Al-Qashani names these distractions as the cause of forgetting. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 58:6/q11 What does the contrast between God's record and their forgetting teach?  [أَحْصَىٰهُ ٱللَّهُ وَنَسُوهُ · interpretation]
- 58:6/q11/p1 Their forgetting cannot erase the record or avert the day when each deed is shown and recompensed. — Al-Tabari and al-Wahidi stress that God's preservation outlasts human memory. Al-Qurtubi says the presented books strengthen proof. Ibn 'Ashur says the contrast exposes heedlessness and astonishes them; 18:49 describes small and great deeds found in the Book. Study Quran cites 18:49, 6:59 and 17:13–14. Islahi says God fixed a day for each person's reward and punishment. Al-Sulami warns that repentance and excuses will no longer help at the great assembly; al-Alusi says confronting forgotten deeds deepens reproach and regret. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; SULAMI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 58:6/q12 What does it mean that God is witness over all things?  [وَٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍۢ شَهِيدٌ · theology]
turns on: How to express divine presence: universal knowing and observation, with al-Bursevi expressly denying bodily presence.
- 58:6/q12/p1 God knows, observes and encompasses everything, so no deed is hidden or forgotten. — Al-Tabari says nothing in creation escapes divine knowledge. Al-Qurtubi says He is fully aware and observing; al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi and Ibn Kathir say nothing is absent. Biqa'i adds preserving and overpowering witness. Ibn 'Ashur glosses witness as knowledge of observable matters. Study Quran links this testimony to 4:33, 5:117, 22:17, 33:55, 34:47, 41:53, 85:9 and 3:98. Al-Alusi says 58:7 confirms its scope over heaven and earth. [19 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 58:6/q12/p2 God is present with everything and watches over it; this presence is through knowledge. — Al-Qashani calls God present with everything; Islahi says nowhere is outside His presence and all events in heaven and earth happen before Him. Al-Bursevi explicitly rules out bodily presence and says language of divine presence in 58:7 does not justify declaring its speaker an unbeliever. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 58:6/q13 Why does the verse close with God's universal witness?  [وَٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍۢ شَهِيدٌ · rhetoric]
- 58:6/q13/p1 The closing assertion confirms that God could enumerate and preserve every deed. — Al-Alusi calls it a parenthetical conclusion confirming exhaustive recording; Ibn 'Ashur calls it a summation; Tabataba'i says it explains comprehensive enumeration; Islahi says it substantiates that God did not forget. Ahmad ibn Hanbal, reported by Ibn Kathir, observes that the passage opens and closes with knowledge. Al-Alusi points to 58:7 as evidence of the witness's unlimited scope; Study Quran lists other witness verses. [7 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أحمد بن حنبل); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 58:6/q14 What response should awareness of this judgment prompt?  [أَحْصَىٰهُ ٱللَّهُ وَنَسُوهُ وَٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍۢ شَهِيدٌ · interpretation]
- 58:6/q14/p1 Remember sins, avoid disobedience, repent, weep and seek forgiveness before public exposure. — Al-Sulami says forgetting offenses without repentance wastes life because excuses fail at the great assembly. Al-Qushayri says recalled lapses cause shame and regret exceeding punishment, and urges earnest prayer after a lapse; Biqa'i transmits the same counsel. Al-Bursevi draws repentance and weeping from God's witness before pleas cease to help. [5 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL(القشيري); BURSEVI]

## 58:6/q15 What false claim do they make at resurrection?  [يَوْمَ يَبْعَثُهُمُ ٱللَّهُ · reports]
- 58:6/q15/p1 They swear to God that they were not polytheists, repeating their worldly habit of lying. — Abu Hayyan compares their oath to their oaths to believers and cites 6:23; they imagine falsehood will benefit them even in the Hereafter. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 58:6/q16 How does the sūra's theme frame this warning?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes offer complementary scales of thematic framing: the local shift to opponents and the sūra's comprehensive warning.
- 58:6/q16/p1 The passage turns from earlier rulings to opponents, secret plots, their humiliation and God's knowledge. — Study Quran says verses 1–4 address ẓihār and verses 5–6 introduce opponents, the rest of the sūra's theme. Islahi says it checks clandestine anti-Islamic propaganda, rebukes hypocrites and directs Muslims; it models bringing a difficulty sincerely to the Prophet and trusting God. He says those who use difficulties as propaganda face humiliation, and God knows their whispers. Asad describes the sūra's movement through faith, its absence, social effects, hypocrisy and believers' posture toward nonbelievers. Ibn Atiyya links these verses through the private-talk passage to hypocrites and Jews in Medina. Tabataba'i prefers continuity with the preceding prohibition. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI+]
- 58:6/q16/p2 The sūra warns that opposing God and His Messenger brings severe punishment; complete knowledge accompanies complete power. — Biqa'i gives this as the sūra's encompassing aim, linking divine knowledge, power and perfection. Islahi says such opponents cannot overcome God's and the prophets' cause. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 58:6/q17 What do the preceding words about opposition and humiliation mean?  [* · meaning]
- 58:6/q17/p1 Muḥāddah is opposition or contention, and kabt is humiliation and disgrace. — Mujahid, reported by al-Durr, glosses yuḥāddūn as opposing or contending; Tabataba'i gives muḥāddah as resistance and opposition and kabt as humiliation and disgrace. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI]

## 58:6/q18 What does Biqa'i draw from the sūra's title and divine names?  [* · links]
- 58:6/q18/p1 The title and repeated comprehensive divine name bind the opening story to the ending and join divine majesty with perfection. — Biqa'i calls the name's recurrence throughout the story and every verse unusually pervasive. He says it presents erring believers under majesty and perfection, condemns certain acts even by believers without legalizing desire, and contrasts Ramadan-night permissions for food and intercourse. He uses this reading to support his explanation of why al-Waqi'ah, al-Rahman and al-Qamar lack the divine name. In the basmala, he reads Allah's knowledge as completing power and attributes, al-Rahman as generosity in creation and guidance, and al-Rahim as special approval of the chosen. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 58:6/q19 How does Ibn al-Zubayr situate this sūra in the surrounding Qur'anic sequence?  [* · links]
- 58:6/q19/p1 The sequence moves from creation's praise and earlier warnings to believers' address, duties, recompense and salvation. — Biqa'i reports Ibn al-Zubayr: the world glorifies God by its state and shows dependence on a wise maker unlike creation. The sequence moves from 57:1 to believers at 57:7, comparable to the shift from classifying people to inviting humanity and addressing the Prophet in 2:21 and 2:30. It traces rebuke of Arab polytheists through al-Qamar and al-Rahman, the afterlife in al-Waqi'ah, then transcendence and believers in al-Hadid. Once past peoples, creation, afterlife, duties and recompense have been covered, later discourse chiefly turns to believers' rulings and salvation. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير)]

## 58:6/q20 What aims are ascribed to narratives about the Israelites?  [* · links]
- 58:6/q20/p1 They prove prophethood, recall favors, explain rejection of prophets, and warn later hearers of analogous punishment. — Al-Qaffal, reported by al-Burhan, says knowledge available only by revelation proves Muhammad's prophethood; the accounts recall favors to Israel, show their ancestors' rejection and foreshadow descendants' treatment of Muhammad, and warn contemporaries by earlier punishments. [1 notes: BURHAN(القفال)]

## 58:6/q21 What does the Itqān author say about his work and the recording of conduct?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The author's two separate remarks concern his book's method and limits, and a moral appeal to the recording of conduct.
- 58:6/q21/p1 He synthesizes dispersed Qur'anic sciences and tafsirs while acknowledging inevitable human deficiency. — Al-Suyuti describes extracting and organizing benefits from many works and disclaims any promise of flawless completeness. [2 notes: ITQAN]
- 58:6/q21/p2 Those who malign scholars should remember that guardians record their words and deeds. — Al-Suyuti rebukes ignorant, envious rivals who neglect religious law for philosophy and act as though no guardians record them. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 58:6/q22 How is remembering contrasted with forgetting?  [وَنَسُوهُ · meaning]
- 58:6/q22/p1 Remembering is recalling something after it has been forgotten. — Bint al-Shati reports al-Zamakhshari's explanation and his comparison with this Day, when God recounts deeds people had forgotten. [1 notes: BINTSHATI(الزمخشري)]

## 58:6/q23 How does the warning turn to Muhammad and the community after this declaration?  [وَٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍۢ شَهِيدٌ · links]
- 58:6/q23/p1 God directly warns Muhammad in wording shared by the whole Muslim community. — Ibn Atiyya places this direct warning after the declaration that God witnesses everything. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
