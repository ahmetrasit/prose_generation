# 80:24: 10 questions

## 80:24/q01 Who is the human told to look?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the 'man' of this command continues the particular target of the rebuke or addresses people generally; Alusi and Tabatabai explicitly favor the general sense.
- 80:24/q01/p1 Humankind generally; everyone is invited to reflect. — Alusi says this is the apparent sense, as with the earlier 'man' in 80:17; he reports al-Isami's allowance that the commanded observer can be distinct from the disbeliever whose failure was rebuked. Tabatabai contrasts everyone here with the extreme disbeliever in 80:17 and notes the explicit repetition of the noun instead of a pronoun. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 80:24/q01/p2 Utba ibn Abi Lahab. — Muqatil identifies him; Damghani connects the referent with the earlier rebuke 'Perish man'; Ibn al-Jawzi's Nuzha identifies him directly, and his Zad transmits Muqatil's identification. [4 notes: MUQATIL; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل)]

## 80:24/q02 What does looking at one's food mean?  [فَلْيَنظُرِ طَعَامِهِۦٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the object of reflection is the food and its divine production, or its bodily transformation and eventual waste.
- 80:24/q02/p1 Examine the food one eats and lives by, including its creation, provision, and growth under God's care. — Muqatil glosses food as provision; Yahya in Mawardi says it is whatever one eats and lives by. Zajjaj and Wahidi Basit stress God's creation of food as the cause of human and animal life; Wahidi Wajiz, Baghawi, Kashshaf, Nasafi, Bursevi, and Tabatabai stress its measured arrangement. Ibn al-Jawzi Zad and Qurtubi mention its creation; Abu Hayyan adds the processes making it edible. Biqai calls for sight and insight into God's craft and human dependence; Elmalili adds its gradual growth and inner and outer blessings. Alusi takes food as consumables of every kind. StudyQuran reports reflection on food as a sign of power and mercy. [23 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 80:24/q02/p2 Consider food's passage into the body and its exit as waste, or look at the waste itself. — Baghawi reports Mujahid's entry and exit; Qurtubi reports al-Hasan and Mujahid, and Durr reports Ibn al-Zubayr and Mujahid, in similar terms. Durr transmits Ibn Abbas's gloss of food as human excrement. Mawardi says to consider what emerges after food preserved life while the body dies. Abu Hayyan records this as the alternative to the literal food reading; StudyQuran links eating and elimination to the world's fate. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن ومجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عبد الله بن الزبير,مجاهد); STUDYQURAN(Others)]

## 80:24/q03 What does reflection on food teach?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw distinct lessons from provision, renewed plant life, and the transformation of food into waste; they do not say these lessons exclude each other.
- 80:24/q03/p1 It reveals God's power, care, and human dependence, calling for gratitude and obedience. — Biqai says without divine provision people perish; Tabatabai sees precise, comprehensive planning for human welfare. Elmalili calls for thanks and proper use of blessings. Kuranyolu says these signs strengthen faith and lead to grateful worship and obedience. StudyQuran's reported view names power and mercy; Zajjaj includes the food of all animals as a means of life. Wahidi Basit says its coming into existence invites reflection. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 80:24/q03/p2 It displays resurrection through God's revival of vegetation and points to accountability and preparation for the afterlife. — Ibn al-Jawzi Zad infers resurrection from plant growth; Ibn Kathir compares plants from dead earth to bodies raised from decayed bones and scattered dust. Biqai treats creation and the food cycle as proof of divine power to resurrect. Qurtubi directs preparation for the afterlife. Islahi says food and its sustaining rain show resurrection easy for God, and providential favor entails answering for duties toward it. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 80:24/q03/p3 Food's end as waste reveals worldly transience and the person's lowliness, discouraging arrogance and forgetfulness. — Mawardi says the body's output is filth and reflection checks arrogance; its transformation also reminds one of bodily transformation and prevents forgetfulness. Bursevi reports Ibn Abbas saying the sight teaches low status and short life. Abu Hayyan and StudyQuran connect waste with worldly transience. The prophetic reports through Tha'labi, Qurtubi, and Bursevi call food's end a parable of worldly life. [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن عباس,رسول الله); STUDYQURAN(Others)]

## 80:24/q04 What reports illustrate the command to look at food?  [فَلْيَنظُرِ طَعَامِهِۦٓ · reports]
turns on: These are distinct reported sayings and episodes, with differing speakers and details, rather than rival versions of one event.
- 80:24/q04/p1 An angel bends a person's neck in the latrine so the person sees what comes out. — Ibn Abi Hatim, Mawardi, and Durr transmit this from al-Hasan; the accounts vary slightly between turning and bending the neck. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); DURR-FULL(الحسن)]
- 80:24/q04/p2 The Prophet asked al-Dahhak what he ate and what it became, making excrement a parable of worldly life. — Tha'labi and Qurtubi transmit the exchange; Qurtubi names al-Dahhak ibn Sufyan al-Kilabi and specifies meat and milk. [2 notes: THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 80:24/q04/p3 The Prophet said human food represents the world: however well seasoned, one should consider its eventual end. — Tha'labi transmits the report through Ubayy ibn Ka'b; Qurtubi reports the prophetic statement; Bursevi says it remains true after spices and salt are added. Bursevi explains qazzaha as adding spices, qizah as seasoning, and malaha as adding salt. [4 notes: THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(رسول الله)]
- 80:24/q04/p4 Ibn Umar related an angel's rebuke to someone looking at excrement: see what the thing hoarded became. — Tha'labi transmits Ibn Umar's account; Qurtubi says Abu al-Walid asked Ibn Umar about looking into the privy and reports the angel's question. [2 notes: THALABI(ابن عمر); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عمر)]
- 80:24/q04/p5 The Prophet said people were created from seven, provided for from seven, and brought forth upon seven. — Muqatil reports this prophetic saying in the passage. [1 notes: MUQATIL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 80:24/q04/p6 A Torah admonition asks one to look at what one hoarded and what it became. — Durr transmits Abu Qilaba's quotation, pointing to food's transformation into waste. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو قلابة)]
- 80:24/q04/p7 Bashir ibn Ka'b took companions to a refuse heap to see what became of honey, ghee, and food. — Durr reports Bashir's question about things formerly in people's bellies. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(بشير بن كعب)]
- 80:24/q04/p8 A scriptural admonition recounts God's creation and care, then calls the disobedient person to invoke Him. — Durr transmits Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi quoting it as from the Torah or Abraham's scripture; it calls God near, responsive, forgiving, and merciful. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي)]

## 80:24/q05 Why is food singled out rather than drink?  [طَعَامِهِۦٓ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether food is singled out for its richer signs or drink is implicitly included; Alusi rejects the latter explanation.
- 80:24/q05/p1 Food displays more signs of divine power, so drink is left unmentioned. — Alusi offers this as the explanation for the omission. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]
- 80:24/q05/p2 Drink is included under the word food. — Alusi records this explanation but finds it unconvincing. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 80:24/q06 Why is 'the human' stated explicitly again?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · rhetoric]
- 80:24/q06/p1 The explicit noun marks the general human audience here, distinct from the extreme disbeliever in the earlier rebuke. — Tabatabai observes that the noun is repeated rather than replaced by a pronoun and contrasts its universal referent with 80:17. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 80:24/q07 How does the call to consider food connect to the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes trace different links: the shift from creation to provision, the force of the preceding rebuke, and the sura's argument for resurrection.
- 80:24/q07/p1 The passage turns from human creation and inherent blessings to external provision that sustains life. — Baghawi and Ibn al-Jawzi Zad follow creation with sustenance; Kashshaf twice describes a shift from blessings in the person to necessities of life. Baydawi and Abu Hayyan contrast intrinsic favors with external ones. Bursevi contrasts origination with life-preserving gifts; Kuranyolu points to verses 18–20. Alusi records versions of this contrast as internal/external and special/general, but questions a strict origination/continued-life distinction. Qurtubi adds that the arrangement of life's necessities directs preparation for the afterlife. Biqai says the order recalls first creation and dependence on the whole world. [11 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 80:24/q07/p2 The earlier rebuke for failing to obey leads to a fresh command to reflect, which may prompt obedience. — Alusi connects the command to 80:23: despite blessings, people have not fulfilled what was commanded. He reports al-Isami saying the rebuke strongly stirs obedience to the next command and assumes they abandon their present course. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(العصامية)]
- 80:24/q07/p3 The food cycle supports the sura's warning by proving resurrection and making rejection astonishing. — Biqai links the warning to receptive hearts and resurrection, mentioning 79:45. Islahi says reflection on food and rain strengthens earlier proofs of resurrection and divine accountability. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 80:24/q08 How is verse 24 joined to the next verse?  [* · grammar]
turns on: The recitational stopping point: Medinan readers and Abu Amr stop, while Kufans continue with a causal connection.
- 80:24/q08/p1 The sentence ends at verse 24. — Nahhas attributes this stopping point to Medinan readers and Abu Amr. [1 notes: NAHHAS]
- 80:24/q08/p2 Verse 24 connects causally to what follows. — Nahhas attributes this reading of the connection to Kufan readers, who do not stop at the verse end. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 80:24/q09 What does the following water refer to, and why begin the food account with it?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes answer complementary aspects of the following water: its referent and the reason it begins the account.
- 80:24/q09/p1 The poured water is rain from clouds, though the wording can include other waters God brings forth. — Jishumi gives rain as the immediate referent and allows the wider wording; Kashshaf twice identifies the poured water as rain. [3 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 80:24/q09/p2 The account begins with sky and water because they are exalted and water sustains life. — Biqai says this order also recalls first creation and human dependence on the whole world. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 80:24/q10 How does the later list of plants serve this food passage?  [* · links]
turns on: These notes address different features of the later list: its order and the contextual sense of al-abb.
- 80:24/q10/p1 The list gives food for people before fodder for livestock, matching the final order of people then animals. — Samarrai contrasts the order in Abasa with 32:27, observing that grains and fruit precede animal fodder here. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 80:24/q10/p2 The context establishes al-abb as a plant provided for people or livestock without requiring its exact botanical identity. — Kashshaf makes this point twice and says general understanding suffices until the exact referent is clear; the passage thanks God for provisions. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]
