# 26:76: 13 questions

## 26:76/q01 What did Abraham's people and their forefathers worship?  [أَنتُمْ وَءَابَآؤُكُمُ ٱلْأَقْدَمُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the objects are identified generally as idols or more specifically as heavenly bodies and their earthly representations.
- 26:76/q01/p1 They worshipped idols, as their earlier ancestors had done. — Muqātil says they defended their worship by citing their fathers' practice, which Abraham then challenged. Al-Ṭabarī says earlier generations before those addressed also worshipped idols. Al-Ṭabrisī says Abraham's challenge includes the idols worshipped by both present and earlier generations. [3 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; TABRISI]
- 26:76/q01/p2 They worshipped the moon, sun, and stars, or earthly idols representing them. — KURANYOLU-TEFSIR gives this as the passage's surface meaning and reports Ibn ʿĀshūr's detail that the sun was their greatest heavenly god and Baal its greatest earthly representative idol. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr)]

## 26:76/q02 Why mention the fathers alongside those addressed?  [أَنتُمْ وَءَابَآؤُكُمُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the addition chiefly rebuts ancestral precedent or displays Abraham's fearlessness; Ibn ʿĀshūr gives both effects.
- 26:76/q02/p1 To confront reliance on ancestral worship and deny that precedent proves the idols worthy of worship. — Muqātil reports the people's defense that they found their fathers worshipping; Abraham's question extends his challenge to those fathers. Al-Biqāʿī says he pushes their imitation to its limit. Ibn ʿĀshūr says adding the fathers invalidates inherited worship as proof. [3 notes: MUQATIL; BIQAI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 26:76/q02/p2 To show Abraham's lack of concern for idols even when generations have worshipped them. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly gives this force to adding the fathers. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:76/q03 What does 'the most ancient' mean?  [ٱلْأَقْدَمُونَ · meaning]
- 26:76/q03/p1 The earlier, first ancestors from the distant past. — Al-Ṭabarī specifies the earlier generations before Abraham's addressees; al-Wāḥidī calls them people of the distant past. Al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Nasafī gloss the word as the earlier ancestors or earlier ones. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]

## 26:76/q04 Why call the ancestors 'the most ancient'?  [ٱلْأَقْدَمُونَ · rhetoric]
- 26:76/q04/p1 Their great age stresses the longstanding practice and sharpens the rebuke of relying on ancestral precedent. — Abū Ḥayyān says the wording traces persistent idol worship back to Noah's time. Ibn ʿĀshūr says older ancestors normally count as stronger precedent, so this intensifies the rebuke of imitation. Al-Ālūsī says the error's antiquity only makes its longstanding falsehood clearer. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 26:76/q05 Why does the surrounding statement use kāna?  [* · grammar]
- 26:76/q05/p1 It combines worship by those now addressed with worship by their ancestors in the past. — Al-Ṭabrisī explains the verb by the combination of present and past worshippers in Abraham's challenge. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 26:76/q06 Does inherited, longstanding practice justify idol worship?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ancestral practice and its great age count as evidence for the worship's correctness.
- 26:76/q06/p1 No; falsehood does not become true through age, number of followers, or ancestral authority. — Al-Rāzī says neither old or new practice nor many or few adherents determines truth. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Biqāʿī deny that age or precedence proves correctness. Al-Ālūsī calls the ancient worship longstanding falsehood. Ibn ʿĀshūr says including fathers invalidates ancestral worship as proof, and that older ancestors would normally seem a stronger precedent. STUDYQURAN reports Q and R on entrenched error remaining wrong regardless of invoked authority. [7 notes: ALUSI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; STUDYQURAN(Q, R)+]
- 26:76/q06/p2 The people invoked their fathers' worship as a defense of their own worship. — Muqātil reports their appeal to having found their fathers practicing it; this is the rationale Abraham challenges. [1 notes: MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL-; BAYDAWI-FULL-; BIQAI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-; IBNASHUR-FULL-; STUDYQURAN(Q, R)-]

## 26:76/q07 Who are the enemies in Abraham's declaration?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the declaration's enemy refers to the worshipped idols or to the people and their ancestors; al-Ḥasan's reading excepts ancestors who worshipped God.
- 26:76/q07/p1 The idols: Abraham opposes them, and they would oppose him on Judgment Day if he worshipped them. — Al-Ṭabarī says false gods will disown and oppose worshippers on Judgment Day, citing 19:81. Ibn Kathīr says Abraham declares himself the idols' enemy and challenges them to harm him if they can, showing no concern for them. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 26:76/q07/p2 Abraham's people and their ancestors are his enemies, except ancestors who worshipped the Lord of the worlds. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām reports this reading from al-Ḥasan, with the exception for worshipping ancestors. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن)]

## 26:76/q08 What attitude to the idols does Abraham's address convey?  [* · interpretation]
- 26:76/q08/p1 It expresses rejection and disgust at baseless idols, with no fear of their power. — Al-Iṣlāḥī reads the question as amazement and distaste. Al-Ālūsī calls the address a rebuke declaring the idols and their worship false. Ibn Kathīr says Abraham challenges them to harm him if they can, declares himself their enemy, and is unconcerned. Ibn ʿĀshūr says including the fathers underlines that lack of concern. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:76/q09 What did the people believe about idols and cosmic events?  [* · interpretation]
- 26:76/q09/p1 They believed idols governed human life and time caused creation and destruction. — KURANYOLU-TEFSIR reports this account from Ibn ʿĀshūr. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr)]

## 26:76/q10 What role does Satan play behind idol worship?  [* · interpretation]
- 26:76/q10/p1 Idols are Satan's lures; he is mankind's perpetual enemy and seeks to divert people from the straight path. — Al-Iṣlāḥī gives this explanation of the idols. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:76/q11 Which other verses echo the rejection of ancestral imitation?  [* · links]
- 26:76/q11/p1 It connects to 5:104 and similar injunctions in 7:28, 10:78, 28:36, 31:21, and 43:24. — STUDYQURAN supplies these cross-references for the rejection of ancestral imitation. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 26:76/q12 How does the Abraham narrative relate to the surrounding accounts?  [* · links]
- 26:76/q12/p1 It serves the same preaching purpose as the preceding account and ends with the recurring verses. — Al-Iṣlāḥī compares its purpose and ending with the preceding anecdote. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:76/q13 Why does the Abraham account follow Noah in the sequence?  [* · links]
- 26:76/q13/p1 It fills the gap after Noah in the previous sūrah, since Abraham was the most prominent messenger between Noah and Moses. — Al-Iṣlāḥī explains the narrative placement this way. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
