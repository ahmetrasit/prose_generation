# 9:102: 24 questions

## 9:102/q01 Who are the “others”?  [وَءَاخَرُونَ · referent]
turns on: Their religious status and the antecedent of 'others' in the surrounding verses.
- 9:102/q01/p1 Believers who stayed behind from Tabuk through weakness, then admitted their sins. — Al-Tabari invokes the plural and exegetical agreement that Abu Lubaba was among a group; Ibn Kathir contrasts their laziness with the preceding hypocrites' denial; Ibn Ashur cites plural sins and mixed deeds. Alusi prefers this over repentant hypocrites. [22 notes: TAB-FULL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل التفسير); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة عظيمة); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(rivayetler); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; TAB-FULL+]
- 9:102/q01/p2 Bedouins who confessed their sins. — Ibn Abbas, Abu Uthman and other reported interpreters identify Bedouins; Tabatabai calls them sinful Bedouins distinct from hypocrites. Some sources allow either Medinans or Bedouins. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,بعضهم); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وأبو عثمان); QURTUBI-FULL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; TABATABAI]
- 9:102/q01/p3 Hypocrites who were enabled to repent. — Razi reports an argument from coordination with the preceding hypocritical Bedouins: the former persisted, whereas these repented. Qurtubi allows hypocrites not entrenched in hypocrisy; Alusi reports this as an alternative. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(قائلون بالقول الأول); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 9:102/q01/p4 Wavering people between firm faith and hypocrisy, or believers who sin and dislike their sin. — Asad describes hesitation between truth and falsehood; Kuran Yolu describes sincere believers whose conduct varies. Reports from al-Baqir say believers or sinners who commit disliked sins. [5 notes: ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام)]

## 9:102/q02 How is “others” connected syntactically?  [وَءَاخَرُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause is coordinated with the preceding verse or starts an independent nominal sentence.
- 9:102/q02/p1 It coordinates with the earlier mention of hypocrites or those around Medina. — Tabrisi allows a further 'others deferred' to coordinate there too, or an understood minhum; Samīn and Alusi cite the earlier منافقون, and Ibn Ashur relates it to 'those around you' in 9:101. [5 notes: TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(المحققون)+; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 9:102/q02/p2 It is a subject qualified by 'confessed', with 'mixed' as predicate. — Samīn gives this analysis as one of three. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 9:102/q02/p3 It is a subject whose predicate is 'perhaps God will turn to them'. — Then 'they mixed' is circumstantial, with qad understood, according to Samīn and a report in Alusi. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 9:102/q03 What occasion is reported for the verse?  [وَءَاخَرُونَ بِذُنُوبِهِمْ · reports]
turns on: Whether the plural refers to Tabuk penitents or is applied to Abu Lubaba's Qurayza incident.
- 9:102/q03/p1 A group missed Tabuk, admitted wrongdoing and bound themselves in the mosque until revelation and the Prophet's release. — Muqatil names three Ansar; reports differ on totals and names. The Prophet generally waited for God's command. Ibn Abbas distinguishes the unbound three later deferred under 9:118; Qatada says these were not the three. Al-Tabari prefers the group account because of the plural. [81 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(الزهري,قتادة); TAB-FULL(آخرون,ابن عباس,الزهري,الضحاك,زيد بن أسلم,سعيد,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,زيد,قتادة); THALABI(ابن عباس,الحسن وقتادة,الزهري,سعيد بن جبير وزيد بن أسلم أبو منية,عطية,قتادة والضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل التفسير); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عباس وفرقة,زيد بن أسلم,فرقة عظيمة,قتادة); TABRISI(أبو حمزة الثمالي,ابن عباس,الزهري,سعيد بن جبير، زيد بن أسلم,قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,الزهري,سعيد بن جبير ومجاهد وزيد بن أسلم,قتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الجمهور,زيد بن أسلم,قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,جابر بن عبد الله,قتادة); ITQAN(ابن عباس,قتادة); SUYUTI-LUBAB(أم سلمة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عباس وزيد); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو حمزة الثمالي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(rivayetler); STUDYQURAN(the most common account); TAB-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة)-]
- 9:102/q03/p2 The verse concerned Abu Lubaba alone after his warning to Banu Qurayza that surrender meant slaughter. — Mujahid reports the throat gesture, linked by Tha'labi to 8:27; al-Baqir and others give the same occasion. He then tied himself to a pillar. Al-Tabari and Abu Hayyan object that 'others' is plural; Ibn Atiyya says Ibn Ishaq's biography is fuller. [20 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); IBNHISHAM(بعض أهل العلم); TAB-FULL(آخرون,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); TABRISI(أبو جعفر الباقر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب,مجاهد); TAB-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 9:102/q04 How many people were in the Tabuk group?  [وَءَاخَرُونَ · reports]
turns on: The differing transmitted counts, and whether a count describes all absentees or only those who bound themselves.
- 9:102/q04/p1 Ten, often with seven bound to pillars. — Ibn Abbas's versions distinguish three unbound men; Durr says those three were deferred until 9:117 and the passage on the three. Ibn Kathir gives five, seven or nine companions in addition to Abu Lubaba. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 9:102/q04/p2 Eight. — Zayd names Kardam, Mirdas and Abu Lubaba; some lists add Hilal and Abu Qays. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); IBNABIHATIM(زيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); TABRISI(سعيد بن جبير، زيد بن أسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير ومجاهد وزيد بن أسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وزيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 9:102/q04/p3 Seven. — Qatada names Jadd/Jad, Abu Lubaba, Hiram/Khidham/Jutham and Aws among them; reports vary on the names. Ibn Atiyya disputes inclusion of al-Jadd, saying his departure was not repentance. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة والضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ITQAN(ابن عباس,قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 9:102/q04/p4 Six, with three bound and three unbound. — Ibn Abbas and Jabir each report six; Jabir names Abu Lubaba, Aws ibn Judham, Tha'laba ibn Wadi'a, Ka'b, Murara and Hilal. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون,ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 9:102/q04/p5 Five. — Reported from Ibn Abbas through Atiyya and by other accounts; Abu Lubaba is included. [6 notes: THALABI(عطية); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وفرقة); TABRISI(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وزيد)]
- 9:102/q04/p6 Three. — Muqatil and others name Abu Lubaba, Aws and Wadi'a, though their patronymics vary. Abu Hamza instead names Abu Kinana, Tha'laba ibn Wadi'a and Aws ibn Hidham. Qatada rejects reducing the group to three. [11 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(أبو حمزة الثمالي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وزيد); TABATABAI(أبو حمزة الثمالي); TAB-FULL(قتادة)-; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة)-]

## 9:102/q05 What did the penitents do before and after revelation?  [ٱعْتَرَفُوا۟ أَن يَتُوبَ عَلَيْهِمْ · reports]
turns on: Differences among transmitted durations, locations, and details of Abu Lubaba's penance.
- 9:102/q05/p1 They tied themselves to mosque pillars, awaited the Prophet's release after divine command, then offered wealth as charity. — Muqatil says they vowed not to untie; the Prophet vowed to await God's command. Reports add that he prayed first, released and excused them after revelation, initially declined the offered property, and 9:103 then ordered charity. Some identify the wealth as what held them back. [27 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل التفسير); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو حمزة الثمالي); QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(أبو حمزة الثمالي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(the most common account)]
- 9:102/q05/p2 Abu Lubaba bound himself, fasted about seven days, then offered his property; the Prophet allowed one third. — Zuhri says he fainted after seven days. Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib says he was shunned after Tabuk and abstained from food and water; Umm Salama heard the dawn announcement of pardon, though Suyuti notes al-Waqidi in its chain. Some versions say he offered to leave his home. [11 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الزهري); TAB-FULL(الزهري,مجاهد); THALABI(الزهري); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو لبابة,الزهري); QURTUBI-FULL(أشهب عن مالك); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب); SUYUTI-LUBAB(أم سلمة)]
- 9:102/q05/p3 Abu Lubaba remained tied to a tree trunk for six nights, with his wife untying him for prayer. — Ibn Hisham attributes this version to some learned authorities. [1 notes: IBNHISHAM(بعض أهل العلم)]

## 9:102/q06 What does “confessed” mean?  [ٱعْتَرَفُوا۟ · meaning]
- 9:102/q06/p1 They knowingly admitted their wrongdoing instead of denying or inventing excuses. — The Arabic root concerns knowledge; Wahidi, Jishumi, Tabrisi, Ibn Jawzi, Razi and Ibn Ashur explain acknowledgment on knowledge. Jishumi contrasts iqrār from fixity and quotes poetry for iʿtarafa as seeking information. Ibn Ashur sees intensified knowing; confession shows humility and self-blame. [17 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أصحاب العربية); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES]

## 9:102/q07 To whom did they confess?  [ٱعْتَرَفُوا۟ بِذُنُوبِهِمْ · interpretation]
- 9:102/q07/p1 To God, and also to His Messenger. — Tha'labi explicitly says both; Ibn Kathir names God. Qushayri distinguishes legal confession to people, which confirms rights, from confession to God, which elicits generous pardon and is likened in poetry to an apology compensating a wrong. [4 notes: THALABI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; QUSHAYRI]

## 9:102/q08 Does confession itself amount to repentance?  [ٱعْتَرَفُوا۟ بِذُنُوبِهِمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether acknowledgment by itself supplies the regret and resolve required for repentance.
- 9:102/q08/p1 The confession indicates or accompanies their repentance. — Hasan explains acknowledgment as repentance; Zamakhshari and Nasafi say no separate mention is needed. Alusi reports customarily linked confession and repentance; Shihab requires regret and resolve. Ibn Ashur says past sin confession implies both; Qashani sees heart insight and readiness. A Persian couplet cited by Bursevi warns refusal to confess. [12 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الشهاب,غير واحد); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 9:102/q08/p2 Confession alone does not establish repentance. — Razi requires regret for offending God's prohibition and future resolve; the past confession and future divine turning are distinct. Alusi objects that general wording cannot imply Shihab's particular conditions by recognized modes of indication. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 9:102/q09 What is the righteous deed?  [عَمَلًۭا صَٰلِحًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the verse names earlier merit, present repentance, all good conduct, or a sequence of repentance and relapse.
- 9:102/q09/p1 Their confession, remorse and repentance. — Tabari, Tha'labi and others pair it with missing Tabuk; some include binding themselves to pillars. Alusi prefers confession as foremost. Ibn Abbas in Wahidi instead specifies sincere intention and rejection of hypocrisy. Variants join earlier good works and present remorse. [25 notes: FARRA(يقال); TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(بعض التابعين); QUSHAYRI(ويقال); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الفراء,الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن والكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري وغيره); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الأول); QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري وغيره,قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الطبري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الكلبي); ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 9:102/q09/p2 Their earlier participation in jihad, especially campaigns with the Prophet. — Farra reports Badr, citing 3:123; Muqatil and others contrast prior campaigns with staying behind at Tabuk. Qurtubi also reports a composite account including pillar penance. [21 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA(يقال); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن,الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); RAZI-FULL(الثاني); QURTUBI-FULL(فرقة,قيل); NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI(الحدادي); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن والسدي); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 9:102/q09/p3 Their good works generally. — Razi calls these the good works Muslims had done; Alusi allows all righteous conduct; Tabrisi says commendable acts without limiting the kind. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(الثالث); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 9:102/q09/p4 Repentance followed by a later relapse. — Qushayri reports this particular reading: the bad deed is undoing repentance through the abandoned sin, yet divine gracious acceptance can remain. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال)]

## 9:102/q10 What is the evil deed?  [سَيِّئًا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the sin is tied to the Tabuk occasion, the Qurayza incident, or general moral conduct.
- 9:102/q10/p1 Staying behind from the Tabuk expedition and neglecting jihad. — Some add failing to fund the army, siding with hypocrites, or choosing comfort. The report of Abu Lubaba alone shifts the scene, but the Tabuk interpreters agree on this sin. [24 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(السدي); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن,الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة عظيمة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي,الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الأول,الثاني); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الطبري); BURSEVI(الحدادي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 9:102/q10/p2 Sin or evil conduct generally. — Mawardi pairs sin with repentance; Alusi and Tabrisi allow all bad works. Bursevi includes earlier and later misconduct. Hasan's rhymed contrast calls it a sin and a lash, with no abundance or disappearance. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن,بعض التابعين); QUSHAYRI(ويقال); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الثالث); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 9:102/q10/p3 Abu Lubaba's disclosure to Banu Qurayza. — Mujahid says he signaled at his throat that submission to the Prophet's or Sa'd's judgment meant slaughter; this is the single-person occasion. [9 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب,مجاهد)]
- 9:102/q10/p4 Returning to the sin after repentance. — Qushayri reports this reading alongside the assurance that God's prior acceptance remains. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال)]

## 9:102/q11 What does mixing the deeds signify?  [خَلَطُوا۟ · meaning]
turns on: Whether mixing is mere combination, reciprocal blending, or two temporal sequences.
- 9:102/q11/p1 Bringing good and bad deeds together while each retains its character. — Tabrisi compares mixed coins; Razi says the mixture need not alter either item's quality, unlike water and milk. Alusi calls it gathering and reports Ibn al-Munir's paraphrase 'they did' both. [5 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير); BURSEVI(الحدادي)]
- 9:102/q11/p2 Mutual blending, with each deed mixed into the other. — Zamakhshari contrasts the reciprocal waw with water mixed into milk; Biqai reads an equal balance with neither predominant. Alusi distinguishes mixing one into another from inherent reciprocity but accepts reciprocity from context. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 9:102/q11/p3 Alternating sequences of good followed by bad, then bad followed by good. — Alusi favors al-Sakkaki's two sequences; Burhan and Itqan reconstruct the omitted reciprocal complements. Alusi opposes making two acts appear identical. [5 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 9:102/q12 How does “and another bad deed” work grammatically?  [وَءَاخَرَ سَيِّئًا · grammar]
turns on: Whether waw substitutes for a preposition, coordinates two reciprocal objects, or relies on ellipsis; the reversibility of the comparison idiom.
- 9:102/q12/p1 The waw can express 'with' or stand for bi, yielding a good deed mixed with a bad one. — Akhfash and Basran grammarians allow the omitted bi; Tabari prefers the water-and-milk analogy over the water-and-plank idiom because mixing allows reversal. Thalabi, Bagha​wi, Ibn Atiyya, Ibn Jawzi, Qurtubi and others give the same construction. [14 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي البصرة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); QURTUBI-FULL(الكوفيون,قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن الحاجب,سيبويه)]
- 9:102/q12/p2 The waw coordinates both deeds equally and conveys two-sided mixing. — Wahidi prefers waw for combining deeds rather than physical mixing. Zamakhshari and others contrast it with directional bi. Ibn Ashur calls the wording clearer and more eloquent; Samīn says the sheep-and-dirham example paraphrases meaning without making waw literally bi. [16 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,الزمخشري); BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 9:102/q12/p3 An omitted complement supplies each reciprocal direction of mixing. — Burhan and Itqan supply 'good with bad, and bad with good'. Biqai calls the compact expression ihtibak. Alusi rejects the ellipsis as contrary to apparent wording. [6 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 9:102/q12/p4 The waw may mean accompaniment, like water level with a plank. — Akhfash and Tabrisi cite the idiom; other grammarians and Kufans object that the plank cannot precede the water, whereas mixed objects can exchange order. [4 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي البصرة); TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); TAB-FULL(آخرون)-; QURTUBI-FULL(الكوفيون)-]

## 9:102/q13 Does the bad deed cancel reward for the good deed?  [عَمَلًۭا صَٰلِحًۭا سَيِّئًا · theology]
turns on: Whether the verse's simultaneous description of both deeds allows annulment.
- 9:102/q13/p1 No; both remain attributable, so obedience retains its reward despite sin. — Qushayri says it would not still be called righteous if canceled; Tabrisi says 'mixed' would lose sense. Razi adds that praise and blame need their surviving subjects. Alusi rejects cancellation as Mu'tazilite. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 9:102/q13/p2 A later good deed can erase a bad one. — Alusi cites an unnamed saying urging a good act after a bad one; Biqai describes divine favor replacing an evil deed with a good one. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 9:102/q14 How should God’s “perhaps” be understood?  [عَسَى ٱللَّهُ · interpretation]
turns on: How certain divine ʿasā is, and how its wording preserves hope, fear, and divine freedom.
- 9:102/q14/p1 It is an assured divine promise that He accepts their repentance. — Farra, Tabari, Ibn Abbas and many others say God's ʿasā is certain. Wahidi and Razi cite 5:52's fulfilled victory; Razi compares a great ruler's gracious promise. Ibn Ashur calls it metonymy for fulfillment while retaining divine choice. Biqai and Qushayri stress grace rather than debt. [20 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس، الضحاك، الحسن، أبو مالك، السدي); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس والمفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(المفسرون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 9:102/q14/p2 Its tentative wording keeps sinners between hope and fear and discourages complacency or despair. — Wahidi, Tabrisi, Ibn Jawzi, Razi, Bursevi and Tabatabai describe this moral effect; Abu Hayyan combines it with certainty of divine promise. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); TABRISI(المفسرون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(أدى); TABATABAI]
- 9:102/q14/p3 It expresses God's freedom: forgiveness is possible but no creature can claim it as owed. — Qushayri says God may forgive or not and any promise is fulfilled by truth, not obligation. Biqai and Alusi likewise reject a debt upon God; Asad takes the expression as possible acceptance. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 9:102/q15 What is God’s turning toward them?  [أَن يَتُوبَ عَلَيْهِمْ · theology]
turns on: Whether God's tawbah names His creating repentance or accepting a servant's repentance.
- 9:102/q15/p1 God accepts the servant’s repentance and pardons. — The Mu'tazilites explicitly give acceptance; Bursevi and Ibn Ashur do too, the latter referring to 2:37. Alusi relates acceptance to God's generous promise, but Razi rejects limiting divine repentance to this sense. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]
- 9:102/q15/p2 God creates the servant’s repentance, including regret. — Razi's school argues regret cannot simply be chosen: willing it would require a further will, and desire or regret cannot be dismissed at will. Razi rejects the Mu'tazilite departure from apparent sense without compelling proof. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا)]

## 9:102/q16 What do “forgiving, merciful” add?  [غَفُورٌۭ رَّحِيمٌ · interpretation]
- 9:102/q16/p1 God covers and overlooks repentant sins and grants mercy instead of punishment. — Tabari cites 2:173; Baydawi and Bursevi describe grace toward the penitent. Qashani adds success in good works; Tabrisi and Ibn Ashur say the ending explains or suits acceptance. Tabatabai says it strengthens hope; Biqai says divine forgiveness remains characteristic. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 9:102/q17 How does the verse relate to its neighbors?  [* · links]
turns on: Which preceding group the verse contrasts with and how its penitents connect to the ensuing alms and deferred-three passages.
- 9:102/q17/p1 It contrasts confessed, repentant sinners with nearby hardened hypocrites. — Ibn Ishaq places them after 9:101 and before 9:103; Ibn Kathir contrasts laziness with disbelief. Biqai calls them the gentle counterpart to the harsh preceding group; Ibn Ashur makes them Medinans alongside nearby Bedouin hypocrites. [5 notes: IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABRISI]
- 9:102/q17/p2 Their offered property leads to the command to take alms in 9:103. — Tha'labi, Bagha​wi, Kashshaf, Nasafi and Durr report that the Prophet first declined their wealth for lack of instruction; then 9:103 ordered collection, purification and prayer. Malik as reported by Ashhab places Abu Lubaba and companions among the deferred group. [10 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(أبو حمزة الثمالي); QURTUBI-FULL(أشهب عن مالك); NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); TABATABAI(أبو حمزة الثمالي)]
- 9:102/q17/p3 The unbound three are distinguished from the three later mentioned in 9:118. — Ibn Abbas's ten-person account has seven bound and three unbound; Qatada says this group was not the three. Durr links the deferred men to 9:117 and the ensuing passage. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(الحسن وقتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 9:102/q18 How far does the verse’s promise apply?  [* · interpretation]
- 9:102/q18/p1 It applies beyond its original occasion to all sinners who combine good and bad and sincerely acknowledge wrong. — Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi extend it to the Resurrection; Ibn Kathir, Abu Hayyan and Asad likewise generalize. Abu Uthman calls it the community's most hopeful verse; Matruf and Ahnaf apply it to themselves. Alusi argues 39:53 is still more hopeful and says hope need not rest on prior repentance. [20 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو عثمان); IBNABIHATIM(الأحنف بن قيس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عثمان,ابن عباس وأبو عثمان); TABRISI(بعض التابعين); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عثمان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عثمان); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو عثمان النهدي,الأحنف بن قيس,مطرف); ITQAN(أبو عثمان النهدي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو عثمان النهدي,مطرف); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 9:102/q19 What visions illustrate people who mixed good and bad?  [* · reports]
turns on: Different visionary reports, one a dream from Samura and another tied to the Night Journey.
- 9:102/q19/p1 The Prophet saw mixed-looking people become wholly beautiful after washing in a river. — The Samura report appears in Bukhari in abbreviated form; Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir and Biqai identify them as mixed-deed people whom God pardoned. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سمرة بن جندب); BIQAI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 9:102/q19/p2 In an Ascension report, grey or blemished people who mixed deeds were cleansed near Abraham. — Abu Sa'id reports grey clothing versus paper-white; Abu Hurayra's Bayhaqi version has Abraham explain their repentance and three rivers of mercy, favor and pure drink. Abu Hayyan recounts color cleansing. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو سعيد الخدري); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 9:102/q20 What inward state can confession and mixed deeds represent?  [* · inward]
- 9:102/q20/p1 A self-reproaching soul still able to recognize sin, wavering between heart-light and veiling impulses. — Qashani says good prevails through angelic thoughts and righteous companionship, whereas repeated sin and corrupt company extinguish preparedness. Sulami pictures penitents remembering sin, forgetting obedience and beseeching God. Bursevi distinguishes animal, bodily and satanic traits and cites the prophetic signs of hypocrisy: lying, betrayal, broken promises and treachery. [7 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 9:102/q21 Is a variant reading reported for the two deed descriptions?  [عَمَلًۭا صَٰلِحًۭا سَيِّئًا · readings]
- 9:102/q21/p1 A reported form lightens the good-deed expression and geminates the evil-deed expression. — Tabrisi reports the form without further argument. [1 notes: TABRISI(قيل)]

## 9:102/q22 What other reports apply the verse or its language?  [* · reports]
- 9:102/q22/p1 Abu Bakr invoked hope of repentance when urging mercy to Badr captives. — The Musnad's Ibn Mas'ud report has Abu Bakr urging that the captives be spared in hope that God accepts their repentance. [1 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(أبو بكر)]
- 9:102/q22/p2 Hasan ibn Ali used a moral example of worldly service damaging religion. — Alusi cites Aswad ibn Qays's account of Hasan admonishing Habib ibn Maslama over serving Mu'awiya for worldly gain. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الأسود بن قيس)]
- 9:102/q22/p3 An incomplete sermon report survives without an identifiable verse explanation. — The Durr fragment urges asking God for well-being and has Umar rebuke an allied man after asking about an unnamed Prophetic sermon. [1 notes: DURR-FULL]

## 9:102/q23 What did their confession involve?  [ٱعْتَرَفُوا۟ · interpretation]
- 9:102/q23/p1 They deliberately named their sins in repentance, with regret, cessation and resolve. — Biqai contrasts this with false excuses and identifies them as those who stayed behind. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 9:102/q24 What noun is understood after “another”?  [وَءَاخَرَ سَيِّئًا · grammar]
- 9:102/q24/p1 A second noun meaning deed is implied: another, bad deed. — Tabrisi reconstructs the phrase as mixing one good deed with another bad deed. [1 notes: TABRISI]
