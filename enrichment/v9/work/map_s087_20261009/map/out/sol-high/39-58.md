# 39:58: 15 questions

## 39:58/q01 How does this 'or says' attach to the earlier speech?  [أَوْ تَقُولَ · grammar]
- 39:58/q01/p1 It coordinates with the first 'that it should say' clause. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya applies this to both later occurrences of aw taqūla. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 39:58/q02 Do the three regret statements belong to one speaker or different groups?  [أَوْ تَقُولَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the conjunction marks successive sayings in one person's bewilderment or sayings distributed among distinct classes.
- 39:58/q02/p1 Different classes of people voice the three regrets. — Qatāda assigns neglect to the first class, the complaint about guidance to the second, and the wish to return to the third. Bursevī reports this as an alternative account of the conjunction. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI]
- 39:58/q02/p2 The sayings voice a person's bewilderment, excuses, and useless regret. — Bursevī gives this account of the conjunction. Al-Bayḍāwī describes the alternatives as futile vacillation and excuses. [2 notes: BURSEVI; BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 39:58/q03 What does seeing the punishment involve?  [حِينَ تَرَى ٱلْعَذَابَ · interpretation]
- 39:58/q03/p1 The soul sees the punishment plainly and directly. — Al-Baghawī says it is face to face; Bursevī likewise says plainly and directly. Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes people brought to the Fire and seeing it, comparing 6:27 and 23:107. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 39:58/q04 What force does 'law' have here?  [لَوْ · grammar]
- 39:58/q04/p1 It introduces an unqualified wish for another chance. — Bursevī glosses it 'if only'; al-Ālūsī says it expresses wishing; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a particle of wishing and the wish unqualified; Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise reads it as wishing. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 39:58/q05 What is the 'return' wished for?  [كَرَّةًۭ · meaning]
- 39:58/q05/p1 A further turn back to worldly life, the place where one can act. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls karra the verbal noun of karra-yakurr, 'to return.' Bursevī adds kara ʿalayhi ('turn toward him'), kara ʿanhu ('return from him'), and karra as 'one time.' Al-Biqāʿī calls this world the abode of deeds. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the speaker wants to do good there and points to 26:102. Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares the return wish at the Fire in 6:27 and 23:107. The sources variously say another return, another chance, or return to earthly life. [21 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 39:58/q06 How is 'so I might be' put in the subjunctive?  [فَأَكُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether becoming good is the wish's consequence, part of the wish coordinated with the verbal noun karra, or coordinated with the wish's response; the analyses differ on implied an and what is wished.
- 39:58/q06/p1 It is the consequence or answer of the wish expressed by law. — Al-Ṭabarī also calls it the apodosis of 'if only I had a return.' Al-Samīn says an implicit an governs it. Abū Ḥayyān says that an is obligatorily hidden in this parse and becoming good follows the wished return; he and al-Ālūsī distinguish this from wishing for goodness itself. Al-Nahhās and Abū Ḥayyān cite poetry for the analyses. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the wish unqualified. [11 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(البحر); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:58/q06/p2 It coordinates with karra understood as a verbal noun or as 'that I return.' — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī, al-Nahhās, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Baḥr give this analysis. Ibn ʿAṭiyya supplies implicit an and a verb construed as a verbal noun; his report of al-Ṭabarī spells out 'could return and then become,' as a wish. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Baḥr say an may be expressed or omitted and becoming good is itself wished, unlike the consequence parse. Al-Ṭabarī and al-Farrāʾ cite the line coordinating 'you ask' with 'remembrance'; al-Qurṭubī also gives the abaya-and-comfort line; al-Baḥr cites two poetic parallels. Al-Nahhās and al-Samīn also cite poetry. [14 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(البحر)]
- 39:58/q06/p3 It coordinates with the response to the wish. — Al-Biqāʿī prefers this analysis because it best fits the following verses. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL+]
- 39:58/q06/p4 Its accusative has two analyses, left unspecified in the excerpt. — Al-Thaʿlabī notes the existence of two analyses but supplies neither in the available note. [1 notes: THALABI]

## 39:58/q07 Who are the 'good-doers' the speaker hopes to join?  [ٱلْمُحْسِنِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Which aspect of becoming a good-doer is made explicit: God's oneness, belief and obedient action, guidance, or gratitude.
- 39:58/q07/p1 Those who affirm God's oneness. — Muqātil glosses doing good as becoming a monotheist; al-Baghawī identifies the good-doers as affirmers of oneness. Al-Ṭabrisī joins monotheism with obedience. Al-Nasafī speaks of people of faith and good works, identifying them as monotheists. [4 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL]
- 39:58/q07/p2 Those righteous in obedience, belief, and deeds. — Al-Ṭabarī specifies obedience to God and the messengers' commands. Al-Thaʿlabī calls them virtuous; al-Ṭabrisī says monotheist and obedient; al-Bayḍāwī and Bursevī specify both belief and conduct; al-Nasafī says faith and good works; al-Ālūsī says creed and deeds. Ibn Kathīr, al-Biqāʿī, Elmalılı, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Kur'an Yolu describe doing good or becoming righteous. Al-Ṭabarī's report of the wish says the speaker would return and become a doer of good. [14 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:58/q07/p3 The guided ones. — Al-Itqān gives this gloss of al-muḥsinīn. [1 notes: ITQAN]
- 39:58/q07/p4 Those grateful to God. — Iṣlāḥī gives gratitude as what the person wishes to attain on returning to worldly life. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 39:58/q08 What does this wish reveal about the speaker's earlier conduct?  [* · interpretation]
- 39:58/q08/p1 It acknowledges earlier wrongdoing and seeks another chance after recognizing loss. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says wanting to do good admits prior evil. Study Quran contrasts this admission of some culpability with the earlier blame of God and compares the guilty in 32:12. Al-Biqāʿī says the wish follows admission of falsehood and fabrication and increases loss. Al-Thaʿlabī depicts a lost person first imagining divine guidance would have made him God-fearing. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL; THALABI]

## 39:58/q09 Would the plea for a return be granted or lead to reform?  [* · interpretation]
- 39:58/q09/p1 The plea is futile; if returned, the condemned would repeat their former conduct. — Ibn ʿAbbās calls their professed future guidance false: God would again prevent their guidance as before; al-Ṭabarī's report cites 35:14, 6:28 and 6:110. Ibn Kathīr says they would resume forbidden conduct and cites 6:28; al-Durr's Ibn ʿAbbās report also cites 6:28 and 6:110. Study Quran calls the pleas insincere and useless: revelation and respite lasted until death, and 6:28 calls them liars. Kur'an Yolu says the following verse explains why the wish goes unanswered. Al-Biqāʿī calls it a futile wish increasing loss; al-Bayḍāwī calls the alternatives futile excuses. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 39:58/q10 What did God disclose beforehand about these speakers?  [* · reports]
- 39:58/q10/p1 God revealed what they would say and do before they said or did it. — Ibn ʿAbbās is reported with this claim by al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr and al-Durr; the reports connect it to 35:14. Al-Ṭabarī's version adds that the claim of reform upon return is false, pointing to 6:28 and 6:110. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 39:58/q11 Why do the three sayings occur in this order?  [* · rhetoric]
- 39:58/q11/p1 They move from regret over neglect, through an excuse about guidance, to wishing for a return. — Al-Ṭabarī lists the three regrets; al-Zamakhsharī, as reported by Abū Ḥayyān, preserves this sequence and says the reply targets the statement requiring an answer. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it the natural movement of thought at judgment, with a different order losing that signal, comparing 23:99–100. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it follows the criminals' order of utterance, so moving the reply alongside its target would disrupt the arrangement. Bursevī describes bewilderment, excuses and useless regret; al-Bayḍāwī calls them vacillation and excuses. Al-Biqāʿī places the final wish after the soul's admission of falsehood and fabrication. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BURSEVI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 39:58/q12 How does the subsequent reply address these sayings?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the subsequent reply distributes answers across the substantive claims or directly addresses only the guidance excuse.
- 39:58/q12/p1 It answers the substantive sayings in a changed order after summarizing them. — Ibn ʿĀshūr pairs the guidance excuse with signs that came and were denied, neglect with arrogance, and the wish to be God-fearing with being a disbeliever; he cites 39:56–58 and says Quranic guidance left no excuse. He says the reply first disposes of the main excuse, then neglect, then the return wish; the mockery claim is passed over because it was already an admission. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:58/q12/p2 It answers the guidance excuse while leaving the other sayings unanswered. — Al-Zamakhsharī, reported by Abū Ḥayyān, says the reply answers the one saying that requires an answer. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God does not answer the mockery and return sayings because He rebuffs the criminals and forbids them to speak, citing 23:106–111. Kur'an Yolu says the return wish goes unanswered and the following verse gives the reason. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 39:58/q13 How does the warning about this regret fit the surrounding call to repent?  [* · links]
- 39:58/q13/p1 The warning urges repentance, submission, and following revelation before regret becomes too late. — Al-Ṭabarī says the commands avert three afterlife regrets: neglect, a claim of missing guidance, and the wish to return. Al-Zajjāj says the paths of guidance were made clear and a discerning living person has proof. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads seven verses as one discourse: the ban on despair prepares the calls to repent, submit and act righteously; he also notes mercy, worldly arrogance, and the contrasting ends of the pious and unbelievers. Iṣlāḥī urges turning to the merciful, forgiving God rather than despair or seek deities and intercessors; belief in the revealed Book must come before seizure. He says polytheism and intercession hasten ruin and later regret is useless. Study Quran says the call in 39:54–67 leads into the extended contrast of the two ends. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 39:58/q14 Which other verses parallel this wish for another chance?  [* · links]
- 39:58/q14/p1 Other passages likewise depict regret and a wish to return. — Asad cites 2:167, 26:102 and 6:27–28. Ibn ʿĀshūr mentions 26:102 and, for the movement from regret to wish, 23:99–100. Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 6:27 and 23:107 for seeing the Fire and wishing to return. Study Quran compares the guilty in 32:12, while its discussion of the false plea cites 6:28. [6 notes: ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 39:58/q15 Concerning whom was this passage revealed?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reported addressee and occasion of revelation.
- 39:58/q15/p1 People condemned in pre-Islamic times who feared that God and His Messenger would not accept them. — Ibn Zayd reports that they considered following Muhammad but feared rejection; the revelation continued through 39:58. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد)]
- 39:58/q15/p2 Waḥshī. — Study Quran reports other commentators' view that verses 53–59 were all revealed concerning him. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Other commentators)]
