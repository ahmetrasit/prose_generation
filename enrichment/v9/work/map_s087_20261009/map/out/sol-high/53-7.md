# 53:7: 12 questions

## 53:7/q01 Who is 'he' at the highest horizon?  [وَهُوَ · referent]
turns on: Which antecedent governs istawā and huwa, whether a second subject can be coordinated, and whether the scene is Gabriel's appearance, Muhammad's elevation, or divine istiwā.
- 53:7/q01/p1 Gabriel is the one at the horizon, appearing in his created form to Muhammad. — Al-Suddī, al-Zajjāj, al-Qurṭubī and others identify Gabriel as the mighty teacher of the preceding verse; al-Zajjāj and al-Qurṭubī say he normally appeared as a man and filled the east at Muhammad's request. Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers Gabriel because 53:13 calls the later vision another descent and no report puts a vision of God before the Night Journey; seeing God with the heart remains possible. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Gabriel was poised before descending. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ allows this referent without insisting on east. Al-Biqāʿī calls it the main reading. Al-Rāzī, as quoted by Elmalılı, reports this common view while preferring Muhammad. [26 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض أهل التأويل); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الربيع والزجاج,الطبري والفراء); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الفراء، الطبري); ELMALILI(Fahreddin Râzî); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(JJ,many commentators,Ṭ); IBNATIYYA-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 53:7/q01/p2 Gabriel and Muhammad are both at the horizon, with 'he' naming one beside the subject of istawā. — Ibn Qutayba and al-Ṭabarī describe the pair together, the latter on the Night Journey at the upper eastern rising; al-Farrāʾ and al-Rabīʿ put them at the lowest heaven during the ascent. Al-Farrāʾ cites poetry for coordination without an overt reinforced pronoun, and al-Baghawī and Ibn Kathīr cite 27:67 as an analogue. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Samīn record both assignments: istawā may concern Gabriel and huwa Muhammad, or the reverse. Ibn Kathīr accepts possible grammar but says the first true-form vision preceded the Night Journey, so the scene cannot be their joint station there. Al-Zajjāj, Abū Jaʿfar, al-Wāḥidī, al-Qurṭubī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya object to the unreinforced hidden subject or to the eastern restriction. [22 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ(أهل اللغة); NAHHAS(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفراء); TABRISI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الربيع بن أنس والفراء,الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(الفراء والطبري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير,الفراء عن بعض العرب); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الفراء، الطبري); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî); ZAJJAJ-; NAHHAS-; WAHIDI-BASIT-; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL-; IBNKATHIR-FULL-; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Atıyye)-]
- 53:7/q01/p3 Muhammad is at the horizon, his elevation being prophetic knowledge or spiritual rank. — ʿIkrima says Muhammad saw Gabriel there. Elmalılı reads the teaching as followed by Muhammad's settled knowledge and prophethood; al-Rāzī in his report treats elevation as rank rather than a bodily location. Kur'an Yolu repeats Elmalılı's ascent in prophetic knowledge. Study Quran reports a reading placing him there during the Night Journey and Ascension. Al-Samīn and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ allow Muhammad as the pronoun's referent. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة); SAMIN-DURR; ELMALILI(Fahreddin Râzî); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI+]
- 53:7/q01/p4 On a divine reading of the preceding istawā, the horizon expresses God's majesty and dominion. — Al-Ḥasan takes the mighty one and subject of istawā as God. Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares this reading to God's istiwā over the Throne in 20:5, while preferring Gabriel for this passage. Al-Samīn reports al-Ḥasan's coordinated-pronoun treatment of the divine reading. Study Quran reports wider interpretations of the opening as vision of God by heart, or by eye and heart, on the Night Journey. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن); STUDYQURAN(many others); IBNATIYYA-FULL-]

## 53:7/q02 How is 'and he was at the highest horizon' parsed?  [وَهُوَ بِٱلْأُفُقِ ٱلْأَعْلَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether wāw introduces a circumstantial or fresh subject-predicate clause, or coordinates huwa with the hidden subject of istawā; the last parse depends on disputed coordination of an unreinforced pronoun.
- 53:7/q02/p1 Huwa is a subject with bil-ufuq its predicate; the clause gives the circumstance of the preceding istawā. — Abū Jaʿfar prefers the circumstantial account of Gabriel rising high. Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, Bursawī and al-Ālūsī call the clause a ḥāl; Makkī and al-Samīn make it a circumstantial subject-predicate clause. Al-Wāḥidī says its nominative predicate contains a pronoun returning to the subject, and the wāw is circumstantial; istawā takes one subject, as in 28:14 and 20:5. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ likewise links the circumstance to becoming upright. Al-Jishumī lists the independent subject-predicate parse. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(مكي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; NAHHAS+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 53:7/q02/p2 Huwa is coordinated with the concealed subject of istawā, so both are subjects. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Ṭabarī apply this to Gabriel and Muhammad. Al-Jishumī lists coordination with the fāʾ in fastawā. Kufan grammar permits an unreinforced connected nominative pronoun to take a coordinated noun; al-Farrāʾ cites a line about nabʿ and kharūʿ and analogy with 27:67. Al-Ālūsī says the prepositional phrase then attaches to istawā, though most reject this sense. Al-Zajjāj says an explicit ana in istawaytu ana wa-Zayd is acceptable but omission is ugly outside poetry. Abū Jaʿfar says a bare hidden nominative needs emphasis or expanded wording, and the cited Qurʾanic example has a longer context and explicit plural pronoun; q umnā wa-Zayd is easier than a singular hidden pronoun. Ibn Kathīr grants grammatical plausibility but disputes fit with the event. [15 notes: NAHHAS(الفراء); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفراء); TABRISI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن,الفراء والطبري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير,الفراء عن بعض العرب); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء، الطبري); ZAJJAJ-; NAHHAS-; WAHIDI-BASIT-; QURTUBI-FULL-; IBNKATHIR-FULL-; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Atıyye)-]
- 53:7/q02/p3 The subject-predicate clause is a fresh statement by God rather than a circumstantial clause. — Al-Samīn records this as the other way to construe the independent clause after istawā. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 53:7/q03 What does ufuq mean in Arabic?  [ٱلْأُفُقِ · meaning]
turns on: The notes distinguish the verse's sky-region sense from a separate poetic use of the same word for a horse.
- 53:7/q03/p1 It is a side, region, or visible boundary of the sky, plural āfāq; by extension it can name earthly regions. — Al-Jishumī cites a poem using āfāq for horizons. Al-Ṭabrisī cites an unnamed poet for heavenly horizons and Imruʾ al-Qays for earthly regions. Al-Qurṭubī records variant vowelings of ufuq. Bursawī defines the visible and unseen celestial boundary; al-Ālūsī notes astronomers distinguish true and conventional horizons. Elmalılı explains astronomical horizons as technical forms of the sky-earth boundary. Ibn ʿĀshūr describes where the remote earth seems to meet the blue dome and notes east and west directions; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ gives the general side or region sense. [11 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI(امرؤ القيس,شاعر غير مسمى); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 53:7/q03/p2 Afaq can also describe an excellent horse. — Al-Qurṭubī cites a poetic phrase about an afaq chestnut horse for this separate Arabic usage. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 53:7/q04 Where is the highest horizon in this verse?  [ٱلْأُفُقِ ٱلْأَعْلَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the qualifier selects the sunrise side of the earthly horizon, a higher heavenly region, or an elevation of rank; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ dispute the evidence for east.
- 53:7/q04/p1 It is the eastern horizon, the place of sunrise or daybreak. — Muqātil says the direction of sunrise; al-Ḥasan calls it the higher of two eastern horizons. Qatāda and others say the source of daylight; Mujāhid and Ibn ʿAbbās say sunrise. Al-Wāḥidī says exegetes commonly place it above the east. Al-Ṭabrisī says east is higher than west on the earth's plain, not a point in the air; Bursawī similarly contrasts the upper sunrise and lower western horizon. Al-Ṭabarī couples the eastern rising to the night journey. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says there is no evidence for restricting it to sunrise; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ dismisses the east-higher-than-west rationale. [21 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن); TAB-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل والكلبي); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة وسفيان ومجاهد); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن، قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة,قتادة، وابن زيد وغيرهما,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن); ELMALILI(Fahreddin Râzî); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî); STUDYQURAN(JJ); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن وقتادة)-; TABATABAI(قيل)-; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Atıyye)-]
- 53:7/q04/p2 It is a high region of heaven or the upper sky, without a necessary eastern specification. — Al-Rabīʿ locates Gabriel in the highest heaven; al-Bayḍāwī says the upper sky, and Study Quran reports the highest Heaven. Ibn Zayd gives a side of the sky. Ibn ʿĀshūr says highest marks a heavenly region and anticipates Gabriel's approach. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says it is simply an elevated sky horizon and rejects restriction to east. Al-Biqāʿī calls it heaven's highest region. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(الربيع); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 53:7/q04/p3 The height is Muhammad's exalted status in knowledge and prophethood. — Elmalılı places Muhammad at the upper horizon after Gabriel's instruction; his report of al-Rāzī explicitly interprets elevation as rank, not bodily position. Kur'an Yolu repeats the interpretation as spiritual elevation. [5 notes: ELMALILI(Fahreddin Râzî); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI+]
- 53:7/q04/p4 The word aʿlā refers to the crown of the head and what accompanies it. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives this gloss for aʿlā. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 53:7/q04/p5 The horizon expresses God's greatness, power and dominion, as does His istiwā over the Throne. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya sets out this implication of the divine reading and compares 20:5. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 53:7/q05 How is al-aʿlā pronounced in the readings?  [ٱلْأَعْلَىٰ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted degree of inclination of the final alif, including a special Abū ʿAmr transmission.
- 53:7/q05/p1 The final alif is read open with fatḥ. — Al-Fārisī reports Ibn Kathīr, ʿĀṣim, Ibn ʿĀmir and Ḥafṣ from ʿĀṣim opening the endings throughout the sūra. Ibn ʿAṭiyya likewise reports Ibn Kathīr, ʿĀṣim and Ibn ʿĀmir; he adds that ʿĀṣim through Abū Bakr inclines raʾā. Al-Fārisī accepts the uninclined alif. [3 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وعاصم وابن عامر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير وعاصم وابن عامر ونافع وأبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي)]
- 53:7/q05/p2 The final alif is pronounced with imāla. — Al-Fārisī reports Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī inclining throughout the sūra; al-Qaṭʿī from ʿUbayd reports Abū ʿAmr inclining al-aʿlā and tadallā, while wa-laʿalā in 23:91 remains open. Ibn ʿAṭiyya also reports the Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī pattern and Abū ʿAmr's ʿUbayd transmission. Al-Fārisī accepts imāla. [4 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو,حمزة والكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير وعاصم وابن عامر ونافع وأبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي)]
- 53:7/q05/p3 The alif has an intermediate inclination between fatḥ and kasr. — Al-Fārisī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya report this for Nāfiʿ and Abū ʿAmr; al-Fārisī calls the mild inclination good. [3 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو ونافع); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير وعاصم وابن عامر ونافع وأبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي)]

## 53:7/q06 Which appearance or ascent does the horizon scene recall?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which of Gabriel's two true-form visions the verse evokes, whether it instead describes a shared Night Journey station, or whether the ascent is Muhammad's spiritual elevation.
- 53:7/q06/p1 Gabriel's first true-form appearance to Muhammad on earth, at Ḥirāʾ or nearby al-Abṭaḥ. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī say Muhammad requested the angel's original form; Gabriel appeared from the east, filled the horizon from east to west and Muhammad fainted. Al-Wāḥidī links 81:23 and describes a second vision at the Lote Tree. Al-Ṭabrisī says Gabriel then embraced him in human form. Ibn Kathīr places the first appearance after al-ʿAlaq's first verses and a pause in revelation, at al-Abṭaḥ, where the six-hundred-winged Gabriel reassured him and conveyed God's command; this chronology underlies his objection to the joint Night Journey interpretation. Al-Biqāʿī pictures Gabriel seated on a chair between heaven and earth. Asad likewise places the first sight after the pause. [14 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الربيع والزجاج); TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(JJ,many commentators); QURTUBI-FULL+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 53:7/q06/p2 The scene refers to the later sighting of Gabriel during Muhammad's ascent, at or near the Lote Tree. — Ibn Masʿūd says Gabriel's created form was seen twice and relates this verse to the later ascent; Ibn Abī Ḥātim and Ibn Kathīr preserve this report. Another version, tentatively attributed to Ibn Masʿūd and transmitted by Aḥmad, says Gabriel returned to his form and prostrated; Ibn Kathīr calls it strange. Al-Baghawī and al-Wāḥidī identify the second sight at the Lote Tree; al-Baghawī says no other prophet saw Gabriel in that form. Ibn ʿAṭiyya describes a majestic second descent there. Asad cites 53:13 and 53:18 for the two visions. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن مسعود); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الربيع والزجاج); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن مسعود (على ظن محمد),عبد الله بن مسعود); ASAD-NOTES]
- 53:7/q06/p3 Gabriel and Muhammad stood together at the horizon on the Night Journey and Ascension. — Al-Ṭabarī places both at the upper eastern rising; al-Farrāʾ and al-Rabīʿ locate their shared height at the lowest heaven. Al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr and Kur'an Yolu transmit this interpretation. Ibn Kathīr rejects its timing: the first true-form appearance was before the Journey and the second at the Lote Tree during it. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الربيع بن أنس والفراء); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 53:7/q06/p4 The scene expresses Muhammad's ascent in prophetic knowledge and rank after Gabriel taught him. — Elmalılı prefers this reading and Kur'an Yolu repeats his account; al-Rāzī as reported by Elmalılı calls it a rise of status rather than place. [4 notes: ELMALILI(Fahreddin Râzî); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI+]
- 53:7/q06/p5 Gabriel is poised in the upper heaven before descending to the earthly world. — Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the pronoun as certainly Gabriel and treats the high station as his readiness before descent; al-Sāmarrāʾī also emphasizes preparation before he draws near. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 53:7/q07 What is reported about Gabriel's form and Muhammad's sight of it?  [* · reports]
- 53:7/q07/p1 Muhammad saw Gabriel's original angelic form twice, unlike his usual human appearance. — ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās reports six hundred wings, with al-Kalbī giving a similar account. Al-Wāḥidī's exegetes, al-Baghawī, Ibn Masʿūd as reported by Ibn Abī Ḥātim and Ibn Kathīr, Asad and Study Quran describe two visions: an earthly appearance filling the horizon and a heavenly one at the Lote Tree on the ascent. Al-Baghawī says no other prophet saw that form. Ibn Kathīr calls one Aḥmad report attributed uncertainly to Ibn Masʿūd strange; it adds Gabriel's prostration and says the great signs were his creation. Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes the six hundred wings at Ḥirāʾ and a second majestic descent. [13 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن مسعود); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون,عطاء عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الربيع والزجاج); TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن مسعود (على ظن محمد),عبد الله بن مسعود); ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(many commentators)]

## 53:7/q08 How can Gabriel have wings and a visible angelic form?  [* · theology]
- 53:7/q08/p1 An angel can unite spiritual power with wings suited to its realm, whether birdlike or otherwise. — Bursawī says sensory form and rational or spiritual power can coexist according to divine wisdom and power. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 53:7/q09 What inward hierarchy does the ascent express?  [* · inward]
- 53:7/q09/p1 Muhammad's ascent passes beyond Gabriel's station: seven stages of annihilation lead toward higher subsistence and prophetic power. — Bursawī makes night signify fanāʾ and day baqāʾ. Seven night stages correspond to seven divine names ending in al-Qayyūm and al-Qahhār; Mecca's seven sanctuary minarets signify them. Five remaining names, ending al-Aḥad al-Ṣamad, give five subsistence stations signified by the Prophet's sanctuary's five minarets. Seven names at one hundred each yield seven hundred: Gabriel has six hundred wings in image and meaning and stops at the Lote Tree and al-Qayyūm, while Muhammad has seven hundred spiritual wings, proceeds to the Throne and subdues that station. Gabriel remains the Spirit of Holiness who conveys life-giving revelation; his power derives from Muhammad. Bursawī cites 48:10, God's hand above their hands, for this hierarchy. [7 notes: BURSEVI]

## 53:7/q10 How does the horizon scene lead into Gabriel's approach and teaching?  [* · links]
- 53:7/q10/p1 Gabriel's poised height prepares for his descent and approach to Muhammad, stressing the importance of revelation and Muhammad's station. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the fāʾ in the preceding standing elaborates Gabriel's teaching by the angel of strong powers; highest sets up the later approach, and thumma marks a rise in rank because approaching for revelation matters more. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says Gabriel prepares at the highest horizon before drawing near, honoring the Prophet and his great mission. Islahi pictures affectionate, close instruction through which Muhammad fully grasped what was taught rather than hearing unclear words from afar. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:7/q11 How does the opening scene answer the sūra's wider concern?  [* · links]
- 53:7/q11/p1 The opening defends the revelation against the charge that Muhammad invented it. — Study Quran connects the opening to the disbelievers' claim in 52:33, and notes the opening's rhetorical force and its disputed interpretations, including visions of Gabriel or of God. [4 notes: STUDYQURAN(many commentators,many others)]

## 53:7/q12 Why is the horizon called highest here but clear in al-Takwīr?  [ٱلْأُفُقِ ٱلْأَعْلَىٰ · links]
- 53:7/q12/p1 Highest suits Muhammad's ascent in al-Najm; clear suits al-Takwīr's denial that he conceals the unseen. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says al-Najm's horizon is the destination of the Prophet's ascent, while al-Takwīr's clear horizon matches its description of a clear, open Messenger who is not niggardly with unseen knowledge. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
