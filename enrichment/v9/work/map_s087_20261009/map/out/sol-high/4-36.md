# 4:36: 42 questions

## 4:36/q01 What does worshipping God require?  [وَٱعْبُدُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether worship here is glossed by its core of tawḥīd, by all obedient acts, or by inward self-effacement.
- 4:36/q01/p1 Submit humbly to God by obeying His commands and avoiding His prohibitions in heart and body. — Al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Biqāʿī, and Ibn al-Jawzī connect ʿibāda with humble obedience; Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares a marked road and branded camel. Rāzī and Bursawī expressly extend it beyond monotheism to every commanded act or omission, including inward acts. Qushayrī calls it accepting commands and prohibitions; Ibn Kathīr says God's creative and providential gifts ground the duty. [18 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(معاذ بن جبل عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 4:36/q01/p2 Affirm God's oneness and worship Him alone. — Ibn ʿAbbās is repeatedly reported for tawḥīd (Ibn Abī Ḥātim, Ibn al-Jawzī, Rāzī); Muqātil contrasts this with the People of Scripture's insincere worship. Al-Ṭabrisī grounds exclusive worship in God alone giving foundational blessings. [7 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); DAMGHANI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 4:36/q01/p3 Turn wholly toward God and become annihilated in Him. — Qāshānī and Ālūsī give an inward reading: exclusive orientation to God is the utmost humility. [2 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:36/q02 How does the form and clause structure carry the commands?  [وَٱعْبُدُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ وَلَا تُشْرِكُوا۟ بِهِۦ شَيْـًۭٔا · grammar]
- 4:36/q02/p1 The first verb is an imperative with omitted plural nūn, the second a prohibition; the opening wāw coordinates clauses. — Naḥḥās parses the forms and Ibn ʿAṭiyya the initial conjunction; Sībawayh's general examples explain that a verb needs a subject and a nominal start its predicate. [8 notes: SIBAWAYH; NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 4:36/q03 What inward meanings are given to worship and nonassociation?  [وَٱعْبُدُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ وَلَا تُشْرِكُوا۟ بِهِۦ شَيْـًۭٔا · inward]
- 4:36/q03/p1 Servanthood is self-surrender through sincerity, humility, love, reverence, fear, and relinquishing self-will. — Sulamī reports Abū ʿUthmān on cutting attachments, Junayd on relinquishing will, Ibn ʿAṭāʾ on accepting limits, and a six-station account. Qushayrī opposes hidden self-regard and worldly praise to unity. Qāshānī and Ālūsī speak of annihilation in God; Bursawī's poem uses lā ilāha to sever other attachments and connects beneficence with divine rather than autonomous human agency, citing 32:7, 12:53 and 4:79. Reports say hidden shirk can be subtler than an ant on a dark stone; deeds without God's face become dust per 25:23. [27 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان,ابن عطاء,الجنيد,الطيب البصري,الواسطي,بعضهم,يحيى بن معاذ); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; BURSEVI(الله,بعض العارفين,رسول الله); ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:36/q04 What forms of association with God does the prohibition cover?  [وَلَا تُشْرِكُوا۟ بِهِۦ شَيْـًۭٔا · theology]
turns on: Whether the text is explained through doctrinal partners, subtle motives, or both.
- 4:36/q04/p1 No created thing may share God's divinity, worship, or independent creative power. — Al-Ṭabarī excludes partners in lordship and worship; al-Zajjāj says worship beside another corrupts devotion. Qurṭubī distinguishes open divine partners from claiming another independently creates, his second degree directed against Qadariyya. Asad says shayʾan includes quasi-divine powers attributed to saints or objects. Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports the Prophet prohibiting shirk even under extreme torture. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 4:36/q04/p2 The ban also includes hidden or practical association: showing off, desire, reliance on another, or mixed motives. — Qushayrī details ostentation, flattery, self-admiration, and praise-seeking; Sulamī reports Ibn ʿAṭāʾ and al-Wāsiṭī on relying on other than God. Qurṭubī's third degree is ostentation and his reports say God accepts only deeds intended for Him; Sahl distinguishes acting for an audience, gaining energy when seen, and delight in praise, while Qurṭubī exempts gratitude when publicity is unbidden. Ṭabāṭabāʾ reads nearby verses 4:37–39 with 38:26 and 45:23 as practical monotheism versus worship of desire. Bursawī, Kur'an Yolu, and others relate arrogance, miserliness, and praise-seeking to hidden shirk without necessarily making one a polytheist. [31 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري,أبو سعيد بن أبي فضالة الأنصاري,أبو هريرة,أنس بن مالك,أيوب السختياني,الضحاك بن قيس الفهري,المحاسبي,النبي ﷺ,بعض علمائنا,سهل بن عبد الله التستري,شداد بن أوس,علماؤنا,لقمان); BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 4:36/q05 How is shayʾan parsed, and what scope follows?  [شَيْـًۭٔا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the accusative names a possible partner or the act of association.
- 4:36/q05/p1 It is the direct object: no object at all may be associated with God. — Ālūsī says the indefinite generalizes the ban, while ʿIṣām al-Dīn also reads the tanwīn as belittling even the smallest rival to the Necessary. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 72:2. [8 notes: ALUSI-FULL(عصام الدين); IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 4:36/q05/p2 It substitutes for an absolute object: no degree or form of shirk is allowed. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 5:42. Ālūsī applies it to open and secret shirk. Sammarrāʾī accepts both parses together, gives concrete-noun and instrument examples such as stabbing 'a knife,' and contrasts the more specified 18:110. [8 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 4:36/q06 Who are the parents in inward readings?  [وَبِٱلْوَٰلِدَيْنِ · inward]
turns on: Whether parentage is symbolic of faculties, guidance by the Prophet and Imam, or training by shaykhs.
- 4:36/q06/p1 They are spirit and soul, from which the heart is born. — Qāshānī and Ālūsī say to receive from the spirit and purify and restrain the soul's appetites. Qushayrī links their outward priority to upbringing and spiritual growth. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 4:36/q06/p2 They include the Prophet and Ali as spiritual fathers of the community. — Ṭabāṭabāʾ transmits ungraded reports from al-Bāqir and al-Ṣādiq that the verse concerned the Prophet and Ali and a report naming them two fathers; he says a guide who perfects the soul is a truer father inwardly, while bodily parents retain their outward sense. [3 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر وأبو عبد الله,النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]
- 4:36/q06/p3 They can signify spiritual teachers or shaykhs. — Ālūsī reports that the disciple honors them by obedience because they know hearts; he cites Junayd giving al-Sarī's command precedence as a blessing. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الجنيد,قيل)]

## 4:36/q07 What kindness is owed to parents, and why are they named first?  [وَبِٱلْوَٰلِدَيْنِ إِحْسَٰنًۭا · interpretation]
- 4:36/q07/p1 Honor both parents in speech, service, support, and gentle conduct, with special priority after God's right. — Ibn ʿAbbās (Wāḥidī) describes lowered voice and gaze and gentle response, invoking 17:24. Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes required honor, protection and needy support from recommended gracious speech; the Prophet names the mother three times before the father. Rāzī cites 17:23 and 31:15, including kindness to unbelieving parents without obeying a call to shirk, and a Yemenite turned back from jihad to serve his parents. Biqāʿī, Ibn Kathīr and Rāzī ground their priority in their role as cause of one's life; Qurṭubī adduces 31:14 and a report joining divine pleasure to parental pleasure. Ibn ʿAshūr calls the ordering emphatic; Sammarrāʾī notes the recurring word wālidayn in such commands. [36 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(بعضهم); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(أبو بكر الرازي,أبو سعيد الخدري,النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN]

## 4:36/q08 How is iḥsānan construed with the parents?  [وَبِٱلْوَٰلِدَيْنِ إِحْسَٰنًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the accusative is an omitted imperative's verbal noun or an exhortative construction.
- 4:36/q08/p1 It is an accusative verbal noun standing for an omitted command to do good. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Zajjāj's grammarians, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Rāzī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ṭabāṭabāʾ give versions of the ellipsis; Rāzī compares 47:4. Wāḥidī reports the omitted-command analyses of al-Zajjāj and al-Farrāʾ. Ibn ʿĀshūr says fronting the object emphasizes parents without restricting goodness and takes bāʾ as respectful birr rather than financial ilā; Ālūsī lists bāʾ, ilā, and lām. Sībawayh's cited case and complementation examples distinguish accusative circumstances, predicates, and required complements. [24 notes: SIBAWAYH; FARRA; TAB-FULL(بعضهم); ZAJJAJ(بعض النحويين); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(كثير); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 4:36/q08/p2 A nominative iḥsān would be possible with the prepositional phrase as predicate. — Al-Farrāʾ permits the nominative if the verb is not expressed; Naḥḥās transmits the analysis. [2 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS]
- 4:36/q08/p3 It is an accusative of exhortation or an implied injunction to enjoin kindness. — Ṭabarī calls it an exhortative accusative and finds 'enjoin yourselves' close to his reading. Ibn ʿAṭiyya disagrees with the exhortative parse and requires a governed verbal noun. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم); IBNATIYYA-FULL-]

## 4:36/q09 How is iḥsān read?  [إِحْسَٰنًۭا · readings]
turns on: Accusative command form versus nominative declarative command.
- 4:36/q09/p1 Ibn Abī ʿAbla reads nominative iḥsānun, a declarative form with imperative force. — Jishumī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn report it; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn parse it as subject with the preceding prepositional predicate, and al-Samīn compares 12:18. [4 notes: JISHUMI(ابن أبي عبلة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة); SAMIN-DURR(ابن أبي عبلة)]
- 4:36/q09/p2 The common reading has accusative iḥsānan, an injunction to kindness. — Jishumī names it the common reading. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قراءة العامة)]

## 4:36/q10 Who are the near kin and what does care for them involve?  [وَبِذِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ · interpretation]
- 4:36/q10/p1 Blood relatives on either parent's side are owed maintained ties, affection, and aid. — Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya include maternal and paternal kin; Wāḥidī gives visits and affection. Rāzī and Biqāʿī connect this duty to 4:1. Ibn Kathīr says charity to poor kin combines charity and kinship. Bursawī names siblings, uncles and maternal uncles, with spending when needy. Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes parents are distinguished for their special closeness; Wāḥidī treats qurbā as a verbal noun akin to qarāba. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 4:36/q11 How are the listed people syntactically joined to the command?  [وَبِذِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ وَٱلْجَارِ ذِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ وَمَا مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَٰنُكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the construction is ordinary coordination or requires ellipsis for a minority referent.
- 4:36/q11/p1 The apparent definite-article objection to 'neighbor of one's relative' can be resolved by an omitted appositive neighbor. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ṭabarī reject Maymūn's construal as bad Arabic; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn posit an omitted noun and cite poetic and ordinary examples. Ibn al-Jawzī transmits Abū ʿAlī's 'neighbor of the side' analysis. [6 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو علي); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 4:36/q11/p2 They are genitive by coordination after bāʾ, with mā joining the same kindness list. — Naḥḥās parses dhī al-qurbā and later nouns as coordinated; al-Zajjāj and Ṭabrisī specifically join mā. A Kufan codex wording, says Naḥḥās, would require an accusative neighbor and following terms under an implied verb. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; TABRISI; TABATABAI]

## 4:36/q12 Why does this verse repeat bāʾ before the kin where 2:83 does not?  [وَبِذِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 4:36/q12/p1 The repeated preposition emphasizes kinship and the later recipients, especially in a sūra centrally concerned with kin. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and Ālūsī contrast 2:83's Israelite context with this community's special honor. Sammarrāʾī attributes the difference to al-Nisāʾ's sustained kinship theme and al-Baqara's brevity. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 4:36/q13 How are the listed recipients interpreted inwardly?  [وَبِذِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ وَٱلْيَتَٰمَىٰ وَٱلْمَسَٰكِينِ وَٱلْجَارِ ذِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ وَٱلْجَارِ ٱلْجُنُبِ وَٱلصَّاحِبِ بِٱلْجَنۢبِ وَٱبْنِ ٱلسَّبِيلِ وَمَا مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَٰنُكُمْ · inward]
turns on: Which inner correspondence the literal recipients evoke.
- 4:36/q13/p1 The near neighbor is the heart, distant neighbor the self, companion law-guided reason, and wayfarer obedient limbs. — Sahl al-Tustarī's scheme is transmitted by Tustarī, Sulamī and Abū Ḥayyān. [3 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سهل التستري)]
- 4:36/q13/p2 The list can map spiritual affinities, faculties, stations, and acquired virtues. — Qāshānī and Ālūsī identify kin with spiritual affinity, orphans with faculties cut off from spirit, needy with those lacking gnosis, neighbors by station, companion by shared station, wayfarer as truth-seeker, and owned servants as affiliated people. An alternative maps kin to immaterial spirits, orphans to spiritual faculties, needy to psychic faculties, near neighbor to intellect, distant to imagination, companion to will, wayfarer to thought, and possessions to acquired virtues. [15 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض العارفين)]
- 4:36/q13/p3 The neighbor image obliges care for the heart, spirit, and secret. — Qushayrī says a house neighbor's right points inwardly to guarding the heart from corrupt thoughts, then the spirit and secret from unsuitable occupants and absence from witnessing; company with poor and orphans prepares for divine company. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 4:36/q13/p4 The figures may signify the celestial spirit, body, heart or commanding self, and a perfected saint. — Ālūsī describes freeing the spirit from appetite, weaning the bodily form from desire, guiding the heart's longing, restraining the self, and guarding and obeying the saint. A report calls the self between one's sides one's worst enemy. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:36/q14 Who are the orphans and why and how should they be cared for?  [وَٱلْيَتَٰمَىٰ · interpretation]
- 4:36/q14/p1 A fatherless child before maturity deserves gentle care, support, and protection of property. — Al-Ṭabarī, Māwardī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya define the orphan by loss of the father before puberty; Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports both a puberty limit and Ibn ʿAbbās's sound-judgment criterion from 4:6. Ibn ʿAbbās urges closeness, stroking the head, upbringing, and guarding assets. Rāzī, Biqāʿī and Ibn Kathīr stress youth and lack of a provider; reports in Baghawī and Durr promise closeness to the Prophet in Paradise for caring for an orphan. Ṭabrisī and Bursawī specify property management and spending in the child's best interest. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 4:36/q15 Who are the needy and what kindness is due to them?  [وَٱلْمَسَٰكِينِ · interpretation]
- 4:36/q15/p1 People humbled by unmet needs deserve provision, charity, or a kind answer. — Al-Ṭabarī and Māwardī connect miskīn with the abasement of need; Ibn Kathīr requires help until hardship is relieved. Wāḥidī, Rāzī, Bursawī and Wajīz allow a modest gift or courteous refusal, citing 93:10. Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports that the truly poor may refrain from asking and go unnoticed, while Ibn ʿAṭiyya specifies indigent Muslims visibly asking and eligible for zakat. Rāzī contrasts an adult's ability to ask with an orphan's helplessness, explaining the order. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 4:36/q16 Who is the near neighbor?  [وَٱلْجَارِ ذِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether qurbā marks kinship, residential proximity, religious or communal nearness, or a particular grammatical annexation.
- 4:36/q16/p1 A neighbor who is also a blood relative. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Qatāda and many others are reported for this sense by Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, Ibn Kathīr and later commentators. Qatāda gives that person both kinship and neighborhood rights; Wāḥidī adds Islamic rights. Wāḥidī prefers this majority interpretation, and Ṭabarī argues ordinary Arabic qurbā denotes blood kinship. - 4:36/q16/p2 A neighbor whose home is physically near or who is familiar to you. — Al-Zajjāj stresses mutual acquaintance; Wāḥidī says the contrast with the distant neighbor supports proximity despite preferring blood kinship. Spatial interpreters cite ʿĀʾisha's nearer-door gift report. Ibn ʿAshūr rejects this because he says qurbā does not denote spatial proximity; Ibn ʿAṭiyya rejects Maymūn's particular grammatical construction. Some sources give both spatial and kinship options. [21 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI(بعضهم); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(جماعة من العلماء); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فرقة,ميمون بن مهران); SAMIN-DURR(ميمون بن مهران); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL(بعضهم)-]
- 4:36/q16/p3 A Muslim neighbor, near through religion. — Nawf al-Shāmī contrasts Muslim with Jewish or Christian neighbor; other commentators report it. Ṭabarī argues against reading religious nearness because ordinary Arabic usage points to blood relationship. ## 4:36/q17 What duties are owed to neighbors?  [وَٱلْجَارِ ذِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ وَٱلْجَارِ ٱلْجُنُبِ · law]
- 4:36/q17/p1 Care for every neighbor, share help and food, and protect them from harm, whatever their faith. — The Prophet's Gabriel report, transmitted in Baghawī, Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr and Durr, says the urging was so strong he thought neighbors would inherit. Other reports tie faith to a neighbor's safety, rank adultery or theft against a neighbor especially grave, and urge broth sharing. Thaʿlabī, Rāzī, Qurṭubī and Bursawī list aid, lending, invitations, visiting illness, condolences, funerals and avoiding harmful building. Qurṭubī notes the comprehensive rights report is ḥasan despite a disapproved transmitter. Reports grant Muslim kin three rights, Muslim nonkin two, and non-Muslims neighborliness; Ālūsī calls that route weak and Kur'an Yolu uncertain, while broader sound reports have no religious restriction. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees the duty as perfecting an Arab virtue; Kur'an Yolu ties it to equal civic treatment, invoking 5:8. ## 4:36/q18 How far does neighborliness extend?  [وَٱلْجَارِ ذِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ وَٱلْجَارِ ٱلْجُنُبِ · law]
turns on: Whether a report fixes a measure, communal proximity defines it, or custom does.
- 4:36/q18/p1 Forty houses in each direction. — Al-Awzāʿī, Ibn Shihāb, al-Zuhrī and al-Ḥasan are cited for this; reports ascribe a forty-house announcement to the Prophet. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kur'an Yolu note doubtful boundary reports. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأوزاعي وفرقة); RAZI-FULL(الزهري,النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(الأوزاعي وابن شهاب,النبي ﷺ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن شهاب والأوزاعي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Evzâî)]
- 4:36/q18/p2 Forty cubits. — Ṭabrisī and Ālūsī report it; Ṭabāṭabāʾ suggests forty cubits and forty houses might correspond to near and distant neighbors. [4 notes: TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 4:36/q18/p3 Whoever can hear the call to prayer or the iqāma. — ʿAlī and groups of jurists are reported for hearing the call; Abū Ḥayyān includes a narrower iqāma criterion. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأوزاعي وفرقة); QURTUBI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب,فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 4:36/q18/p4 Anyone sharing a district or city. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān report this broad criterion; Qurṭubī cites 33:60 and adds that relations still have degrees, with a spouse nearest. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); QURTUBI-FULL(فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 4:36/q18/p5 Local custom determines the scope. — Ālūsī states this; Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers it because Mālik transmitted no fixed limit, and Kur'an Yolu says sound texts set no clear boundary. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(مالك); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(âlimler); IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 4:36/q19 Which neighbor should receive a gift first?  [وَٱلْجَارِ ذِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ وَٱلْجَارِ ٱلْجُنُبِ · law]
- 4:36/q19/p1 The neighbor whose door is closer. — ʿĀʾisha's question receives this answer in Bukhārī as cited by Baghawī, Ibn Kathīr, Qurṭubī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and others. Qurṭubī explains that proximity makes needs visible and timely help easier; he says the report prioritizes gifts without settling a property preemption right. [12 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عائشة والنبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(جماعة من العلماء,عائشة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عائشة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(عائشة); IBNASHUR-FULL(عائشة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 4:36/q20 Must an owner allow a neighbor to place a beam or watercourse through the property?  [وَٱلْجَارِ ذِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ وَٱلْجَارِ ٱلْجُنُبِ · law]
turns on: Whether the Prophetic beam command creates an enforceable amenity or recommends generosity subject to property consent.
- 4:36/q20/p1 Yes, a harmless neighbor amenity is obligatory. — Qurṭubī reports al-Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad, Isḥāq, Abū Thawr, Dāwūd and hadith scholars taking the beam command as binding; Abū Hurayra insists on it. ʿUmar forced passage of a watercourse, and al-Shāfiʿī argues no Companion opposed him. Earlier jurists distinguish use of an amenity from transfer or consumption of property; a Medinan judge reportedly ruled for this right. [9 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة,أنس,الأولون,الشافعي,الشافعي وأحمد بن حنبل وإسحاق وأبو ثور وداود بن علي,النبي ﷺ,عمر بن الخطاب,مالك)]
- 4:36/q20/p2 No, it is recommended kindness and the owner's consent remains decisive. — Mālik and Abū Ḥanīfa appeal to inviolability of property. Abū ʿUmar says Muḥammad b. Maslama and others disputed ʿUmar's ruling, restoring the consent principle. The narration that a youth may have withheld harmless things is qualified by Abū ʿUmar because al-Aʿmash's hearing from Anas is unestablished. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمر,أبو هريرة,أنس,مالك وأبو حنيفة وأصحابهما)]

## 4:36/q21 Do neighbor rights confer property preemption or determine bequests?  [وَٱلْجَارِ ذِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ وَٱلْجَارِ ٱلْجُنُبِ · law]
turns on: Whether neighborliness implies a proprietary claim, and how broadly 'neighbor' in a bequest is construed.
- 4:36/q21/p1 A neighbor may have a preemption claim from proximity. — Qurṭubī reports scholars drawing on the nearer-door gift report and a report of a neighbor's claim to adjoining property. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(جماعة من العلماء); QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 4:36/q21/p2 The gift report establishes priority in giving, not preemption. — Qurṭubī rejects the inference because ʿĀʾisha asked only whom to gift first; Abū Ḥanīfa denies preemption without shared wall or route if the adjoining neighbor waives it. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة)]
- 4:36/q21/p3 A bequest to neighbors includes adjoining and other nearby neighbors. — Qurṭubī reports this as the majority view. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عوام العلماء)]
- 4:36/q21/p4 A bequest to neighbors reaches only the adjoining neighbor. — Qurṭubī attributes this restriction to Abū Ḥanīfa. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عوام العلماء)]

## 4:36/q22 How is the near neighbor read and parsed?  [وَٱلْجَارِ ذِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ · readings]

## 4:36/q25 How is honoring a guest connected with the verse?  [وَٱبْنِ ٱلسَّبِيلِ وَٱلْجَارِ ٱلْجُنُبِ · links]
- 4:36/q25/p1 Hospitality is part of the same moral care enjoined toward neighbors, companions, and travelers. — Burhān connects the saying to honor guests with Abraham's honored guests and this verse, citing 51:24; it adds guarding secrets, preventing harm, speaking well or remaining silent, and neighborly kindness. ## 4:36/q40 What does Jishumī's surrounding discussion say about Ali's arbitration?  [* · reports]

- 4:36/q41/p1 Recitation in minor impurity is allowed, though ablution is recommended; major impurity and menstruation receive stricter limits. — Burhān and Itqān report al-Juwaynī allowing recitation without ablution and the Prophet's practice. Itqān recommends ablution, pauses during passing wind, and notes prohibition on voiced recitation during major impurity or menstruation with looking or inward recitation still possible. Burhān reports al-Shāfiʿī allowing a menstruating woman to recite to avoid forgetting and Abū al-Layth allowing fragments for instruction. Burhān dislikes recitation while passing wind; Itqān reports disagreement over reciting with an impure mouth. [8 notes: BURHAN(أبو الليث,إمام الحرمين وغيره,الشافعى); ITQAN(إمام الحرمين,شرح المهذب)]
- 4:36/q41/p2 Reciters should review what is memorized, be clean and presentable, and honor the setting of recitation. — Burhān warns that memorized Qur'an escapes faster than tethered camels; it advises toothstick, clean mouth and body, scent and proper clothes, and facing the qibla. Saʿīd b. al-Musayyib's upright posture when reporting hadith is given as an analogy. [3 notes: BURHAN]

## 4:36/q42 How does believers' unity inform kindness?  [* · inward]
- 4:36/q42/p1 People of truth are one soul despite distinct bodies and numbers. — Bursawī grounds their solidarity in undiminished unity of faith. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
