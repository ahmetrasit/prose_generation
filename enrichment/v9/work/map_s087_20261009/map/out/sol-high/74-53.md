# 74:53: 7 questions

## 74:53/q01 What does kallā do here?  [كَلَّا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether kallā refutes the preceding demand for individual scrolls or serves as an emphatic assertion; al-Qurtubi prefers the former, while the latter is said to suit verse 54 better.
- 74:53/q01/p1 It rebukes and refuses their demand that each person receive a heavenly scroll or further special signs. — Muqatil as reported by al-Wahidi says they will not be given the pages. Al-Baghawi and al-Qurtubi say their wish will not be granted; al-Qurtubi prefers this refusal over 'indeed.' Al-Razi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Bursawi and al-Alusi say it checks their proposals for more signs; Bursawi calls the demand defiance rather than a search for guidance. Ibn Ashur says even a document would not win their belief, exposing evasion. Tabatabai says the Prophet's proof is already complete for all, so separate pages are unnecessary; prophethood also requires purity and fitness, as God's answer about knowing where to place His message in 6:124 indicates. Al-Wahidi's Wajiz and Ibn Atiyya connect the refusal with their demand for spread-out books; al-Qurtubi adds that worldly life deludes them. [15 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 74:53/q01/p2 It can be an emphatic 'truly' or 'indeed.' — Al-Baghawi allows 'truly' wherever kallā occurs. The Study Quran reports this alternative but says it suits verse 54 better than verse 53; al-Qurtubi explicitly prefers the refutation reading here. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q); QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 74:53/q02 How are the adjacent lām sounds in bal lā treated?  [بَل لَّا · grammar]
- 74:53/q02/p1 The first lām assimilates into the second. — Al-Nahhas says assimilation is required because the first consonant is vowelless. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 74:53/q03 What does bal identify as the real reason for their demands and rejection?  [بَل لَّا يَخَافُونَ ٱلْءَاخِرَةَ · interpretation]
- 74:53/q03/p1 Their lack of fear or belief in the Hereafter drives their refusal of the reminder and obstinate demands; receiving individual scrolls would not remedy it. — Al-Tabari says their proposed scrolls would not create belief: they disregard divine punishment and deny resurrection, reward and punishment. Qatada, reported by al-Tabari and al-Durr, traces their corruption, laughter and ruin to this lack of faith and fear; al-Hasan says it exposed them. Al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, al-Tabrisi and Ibn al-Jawzi argue that fear of the Fire would prevent demanding more signs after clear proofs and miracles. Al-Zamakhshari, Ibn Atiyya, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Bursawi and al-Alusi explicitly reject withheld pages as the cause of turning away; al-Alusi also rejects their claim that granting the proposal would resolve it. Al-Razi says ample miracles already proved prophethood, making more demands obstinate. Al-Qurtubi cites delusion by worldly life; Bursawi cites consuming love of this world and defiance. Ibn Kathir and Ibn Ashur identify denial of the Hereafter or resurrection; Ibn Ashur calls absence of fear a metonym for that denial and says even a document would not persuade them. Al-Biqa'i likewise calls lack of fear the real reason. Islahi says even open scrolls would only draw another excuse. Kuranyolu connects denial of accountability with pursuit of desire, fearlessness about sin and reckoning, and a wish to evade responsibility. [26 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 74:53/q04 Is yakhāfūn read as 'they fear' or 'you fear'?  [يَخَافُونَ · readings]
turns on: The reported consonant changes the addressees from the absent people to the listeners; sources disagree specifically over the attribution of the second-person reading to Ibn Amir.
- 74:53/q04/p1 With third-person yāʾ: 'they do not fear.' — Ibn Khalawayh carries the subject forward from 'rather, each of them wants' in the preceding verse. Al-Jishumi says this is everyone's reading and that the sound reading of Ibn Amir agrees with it; he calls the contrary attribution to Ibn Amir erroneous. [2 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; JISHUMI+]
- 74:53/q04/p2 With second-person tāʾ: 'you do not fear,' directly addressing the listeners. — Ibn Khalawayh explains the direct address. Ibn Atiyya and al-Alusi report the reading from Abu Haywa; Ibn Atiyya and the Study Quran also report it from Ibn Amir. The Study Quran takes it as a warning to the reader or listener. Al-Jishumi rejects the attribution to Ibn Amir and says his sound reading matches the third-person reading. [4 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حيوة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيوة); STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿĀmir); JISHUMI-]

## 74:53/q05 What sense does yakhāfūn carry here?  [يَخَافُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether yakhāfūn retains the fear sense used by most explanations or takes the sense yarjūn, 'expect,' in Islahi's gloss.
- 74:53/q05/p1 It means to fear the Hereafter and its punishment. — Muqatil, al-Baghawi and Ibn al-Jawzi specify punishment in the next life, particularly the Fire; al-Wahidi, al-Tabrisi and others reason that such fear would stop demands for further signs once proof was established. Ibn Ashur says the lack of fear also stands metonymically for disbelief in the Hereafter. [24 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 74:53/q05/p2 It means 'expect' or 'hope for'; they neither fear nor expect the Hereafter. — Islahi explicitly glosses yakhāfūn as yarjūn and says the disbelievers' lack of fear and expectation makes signs ineffective. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:53/q06 What does al-ākhirah signify in this warning?  [ٱلْءَاخِرَةَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase is explained as the whole future life and its reckoning or, more specifically, as the Fire and punishment within it.
- 74:53/q06/p1 The life to come, including resurrection, recompense, and accountability for deeds. — Al-Tabari names resurrection, reward and punishment; al-Tabrisi and Ibn Kathir say the people deny its reality or occurrence. Ibn Ashur says denial of resurrection underlies rejection of the Qur'an. Kuranyolu stresses that all will answer for their deeds, which would require responsible living. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 74:53/q06/p2 The Fire and its punishment in the next life. — Ata explicitly glosses al-ākhirah as the Fire and its punishment. Muqatil, al-Baghawi and Ibn al-Jawzi specify afterlife punishment; al-Wahidi and al-Tabrisi argue that fear of the Fire would end their demands for further signs. [6 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 74:53/q07 What merit is reported for reciting al-Muddaththir?  [* · sciences]
- 74:53/q07/p1 A report promises ten good deeds for every Meccan who believed in Muhammad or disbelieved in him. — Al-Zamakhshari attributes this promise to the Prophet for whoever recites the sura; no grading of the report is given. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله)]
