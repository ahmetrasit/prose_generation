# 19:12: 33 questions

## 19:12/q01 Who addressed Yahya, and when?  [يَٰيَحْيَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether the address is divine or paternal, and whether it came at birth or after Yahya could understand.
- 19:12/q01/p1 God addressed Yahya after birth when he could understand, commonly through an angel. — Razi and Asad infer an age fit for address. Jishumi allows maturity or sound childhood reason. Abu Hayyan prefers an angel because Yahya received wisdom in childhood; Alusi says angelic address is usual. Kashifi places the call after Zakariya recovered, gestation, and Yahya's youthful worship. Ibn Ashur says the words are Quranic narration, not necessarily spoken to Zakariya. [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Razi); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 19:12/q01/p2 God addressed Yahya immediately after his birth. — Qurtubi supplies the omitted birth; Study Quran reports this timing from Qurtubi and Tabari. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ)]
- 19:12/q01/p3 Zakariya addressed Yahya after he grew up. — Mawardi gives this possibility. Abu Hayyan and Alusi attribute it to Tabrizi but call it implausible; Alusi argues that the next clause gives Yahya judgment as a child. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(التبريزي)-; ALUSI-FULL(التبريزي)-]

## 19:12/q02 How does the account move from Zakariya's promise to Yahya's address?  [يَٰيَحْيَىٰ وَءَاتَيْنَٰهُ · rhetoric]
turns on: The amount of unspoken time and action between promise and address.
- 19:12/q02/p1 The narrative omits Yahya's grant, birth and growth before God's speech to him. — Zajjaj, Wahidi, Baghawi, Ibn Jawzi, Bursevi and Tabatabai supply 'God gave Yahya and said.' Abu Hayyan, Burhan, Alusi and Islahi supply birth and growth; Tabrisi also supplies understanding and reason. Qurtubi supplies birth, and Razi says context explains the omission. Alusi says the compression hastens fulfillment and coordinates 'We gave judgment' with omitted 'We said.' Ibn Ashur sees a transition to prophethood. [15 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 19:12/q02/p2 The childhood command is extraordinary compression beyond ordinary human speech. — Abu Ali, as reported by Jishumi, characterizes it this way. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي)]

## 19:12/q03 How is 'Ya Yahya' read?  [يَٰيَحْيَىٰ · readings]
- 19:12/q03/p1 The Prophet recited it with imala. — Safwan ibn Assal says that, when told this was not Quraysh speech, the Prophet called it the language of his maternal kin, Banu Sad. [1 notes: ITQAN(صفوان بن عسال)]

## 19:12/q04 What follows from Yahya being addressed by his announced name?  [يَٰيَحْيَىٰ · theology]
- 19:12/q04/p1 The name and named person are one. — Al-Asila al-Muqhama, reported by Bursevi, cites the announcement of Yahya's name followed by address by that name in answering a question about a Mutazilite position. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الأسئلة المقحمة)]

## 19:12/q05 How is the form خُذِ explained?  [خُذِ · grammar]
- 19:12/q05/p1 It derives from أخذ with the second hamza dropped and a kasra on ذ. — Nahhas attributes loss of the hamza to frequency or adjoining gutturals, and kasra to meeting silent letters. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 19:12/q06 Which book was Yahya told to take?  [ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the definite Book is Torah alone, scripture generally, or a distinct revelation.
- 19:12/q06/p1 The Torah. — Ibn Atiyya says Yahya preceded Jesus and the Gospel. Qurtubi claims no disagreement; Abu Hayyan and Alusi reject consensus, and Alusi reports Ibn Atiyya's definite-article argument. Razi prefers Israel's familiar scripture to a special book. Ibn Kathir says Israel's prophets and scholars studied and judged by it. Islahi says Zakariya received no separate book, Kuran Yolu says Yahya received none. Tabatabai points to then-current Torah law but allows earlier books. [27 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الجلالين); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ,Ṭ); RAZI-FULL+]
- 19:12/q06/p2 Scripture generally, including all God's books or earlier prophetic books. — Ibn al-Anbari says accept all God's books and apply their rulings; Abu Hayyan and Alusi list scripture generally; Tabatabai allows Torah with earlier prophetic books. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 19:12/q06/p3 Abraham's scrolls. — Mawardi gives this possibility; Abu Hayyan and Alusi list it against Torah-only consensus. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:12/q06/p4 A distinct revealed book for Yahya. — Qushayri infers Yahya had a revealed book; Abu Hayyan and Alusi list a special scripture. Razi considers but disfavors this in favor of Torah. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]
- 19:12/q06/p5 The Torah and Gospel. — Hasan, reported by Abu Hayyan, says Yahya learned both, was sent to Israel, and prayed, fasted and called people to God vigorously as a child. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن)]

## 19:12/q07 What does taking the Book with strength require?  [خُذِ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ بِقُوَّةٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether strength emphasizes earnest resolve, concrete observance, or divinely supplied power.
- 19:12/q07/p1 Earnest, diligent and persevering commitment. — Mujahid, Qatada and Suddi emphasize effort and resolve. Razi adds patience in prophetic duties beyond physical lifting. Zajjaj, Zamakhshari, Baydawi, Nasafi and Bursevi include God's aid. Ibn Ashur adds leading the community while Torah practice weakened. Ibn Jawzi points to 2:63; Tabatabai cites 7:145, 7:171 and 2:93. [32 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ,Ṭ); IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 19:12/q07/p2 Learn or memorize the Book and obey its commands while avoiding prohibitions. — Ibn Zayd, Zayd ibn Aslam and Said ibn Jubayr stress observance. Ibn Atiyya adds learning, memorizing and keeping obligations. Ibn Abbas, Wahidi Wajiz and Jalalayn say God enabled memory and practice. Qurtubi cites 2:63; Ibn Ashur adds understanding and leadership. [14 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BURSEVI(الجلالين); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 19:12/q07/p3 Use divinely supplied strength and confidence of heart. — Qushayri calls heart-strength a prophetic gift, not physical force. Wasiti rejects dependence on prostration, tears or pleading and says God honors or abases without a cause. Ibn Abbas specifies strength to memorize and act; Jishumi says God-given capacity. Jami says the unseen power enabling Zakariya and his wife passed to Yahya. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ; SULAMI(الواسطي); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(المولى الجامي); ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:12/q08 What is the grammatical role of بِقُوَّةٍۢ?  [بِقُوَّةٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the phrase describes the taker or the Book.
- 19:12/q08/p1 It is circumstantial of Yahya, the subject. — Tabrisi says bi marks his earnest state in taking the Book; Samin gives the subject as one possible possessor of the state. [2 notes: TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR]
- 19:12/q08/p2 It is circumstantial of the Book, the object. — Samin gives the object as the other possible possessor of the state. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 19:12/q09 What does the command imply about human ability and action?  [خُذِ بِقُوَّةٍۢ · theology]
- 19:12/q09/p1 Capacity precedes Yahya's act, and taking the Book is his own act. — Jishumi says command presupposes prior capacity, against determinists; the imperative and need for strength oppose the view that God creates human actions. [2 notes: JISHUMI]

## 19:12/q10 What is the judgment granted to Yahya?  [ٱلْحُكْمَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether hukm names wisdom, prophecy, intellect, judicial knowledge, service, or hidden insight; sources cite poetry, Quranic usage and passage context.
- 19:12/q10/p1 Wisdom and understanding or knowledge of scripture and religion. — Mujahid and Muqatil stress understanding the Book; Ibn Abbas adds religious jurisprudence. Zamakhshari cites a poetic ahkamu; Razi cites poetry about judging doves. Wahidi says hukm prevents ignorance and error. Baghawi notes Yahya read scripture young; Ibn Kathir adds seriousness and devotion. Ibn Ashur cites 2:269 and 12:22. Shawkani lists knowledge and action. Tabatabai rejects unsupported alternative senses but takes Baqir's wisdom report as supporting his account. - 19:12/q10/p2 Prophethood in childhood. — Ibn Abbas is cited for age three; others say two or seven. Zamakhshari says God matured Yahya's mind and revealed to him. Razi prefers prophecy as the greatest listed honor and because hukm can mean judicial authority. Hasan in Ibn Atiyya takes 'child' figuratively or as an enduring description. Ibn Ashur calls childhood prophecy unlikely; Tabatabai says 45:16 and 6:89 distinguish judgment from prophecy. [17 notes: QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî); STUDYQURAN(R); RAZI-FULL+; TABATABAI-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 19:12/q10/p3 Reason or intellect. — Suddi says understanding and reason; Hasan, Ikrima and Mamar give intellect, illustrated by the play report. Baydawi says God matured his mind. Islahi says the faculty distinguishes right and wrong, usually peaking at forty, citing 3:79. Tabatabai reports but rejects unsupported proposed senses. [12 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL(معمر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وعكرمة); RAZI-FULL(معمر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî); TABATABAI-]
- 19:12/q10/p4 Sound judicial judgment and knowledge of rulings. — Mawardi gives the rulings and knowledge of them; Qushayri says judging rightly or acting as commanded. Ibn Atiyya's group combines law and knowledge; Qurtubi adds sound application. Wahidi says the root averts error. Shawkani includes religious rulings. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî); TABATABAI-]

- 19:12/q10/p6 Divinely granted manners of service. — Jafar says divine success in practicing proper service; Husayn invokes the lights of witnessing and a self formed in servitude and striving. Abu Hayyan and Alusi list devotional manners. [4 notes: SULAMI(الحسين,جعفر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-]

## 19:12/q11 How old was Yahya when judgment was given?  [صَبِيًّۭا · reports]
turns on: Whether sabiyyan marks a reported small-child age, generic boyhood, or youth.
- 19:12/q11/p1 Three years old. — Muqatil and reports from Ibn Abbas give three; Ibn Jawzi pairs Qatada and Muqatil. Mawardi links Muqatil's age to the play report; Bursevi also allows seven. [10 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(معمر ومقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:12/q11/p2 Seven years old. — Ibn Jawzi reports this from Ibn Abbas. Abu Hayyan cites a Prophet-attributed report through Ibn Abbas; Durr and Alusi report understanding and worship at seven. Bursevi allows either three or seven. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس عن النبي); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 19:12/q11/p3 Boyhood before puberty, without a precise age. — Tabari stresses before adulthood; Tabatabai says before puberty. Ibn Ashur says exceptional boyhood judgment and allows prophecy before forty, perhaps hastened by early martyrdom. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 19:12/q11/p4 Two or three years old. — Qatada in Qurtubi gives either age; Abu Hayyan reports both; Alusi also lists two. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:12/q11/p5 A young man before middle age, not necessarily a small child. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan report this meaning of sabiyyan. Ibn Atiyya also reports Hasan using 'child' figuratively or as a lasting description for prophecy. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن,فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]

## 19:12/q12 What is the grammatical role of صَبِيًّۭا?  [صَبِيًّۭا · grammar]
- 19:12/q12/p1 It is an accusative circumstance describing Yahya at the grant. — Nahhas says it marks his age; Qurtubi and Samin attach it to the pronoun in 'We gave him.' [3 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 19:12/q13 What happened when children invited Yahya to play?  [ٱلْحُكْمَ صَبِيًّۭا · reports]
turns on: Whether the report only gives Yahya's refusal or also an instruction to pray.
- 19:12/q13/p1 He refused, saying he or they had not been created for play. — The wording varies between 'I' and 'we/they.' Mamar, Dahhak and Qatada transmit it as childhood wisdom; Abd al-Razzaq and Tabari tie it to the grant. Qatada in Durr calls it the occasion for this description. Durr gives a Prophet-attributed chain through Muadh. Bursevi also attributes it to the Prophet; Kashifi warns against wasting life, citing 47:36. [20 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; ABDURRAZZAQ; TAB-FULL(معمر); THALABI(معمر); MAWARDI-FULL(معمر ومقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(معمر); JISHUMI(معمر); KASHSHAF-FULL(الضحاك); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(معمر); QURTUBI-FULL(معمر); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن المبارك); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة,معمر بن راشد); BURSEVI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI(معمر); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 19:12/q13/p2 He refused play and told the boys to pray. — Durr gives this fuller report; Alusi gives a Prophet-attributed report through Ibn Abbas. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 19:12/q14 What does childhood judgment imply for someone reciting the Quran before puberty?  [ٱلْحُكْمَ صَبِيًّۭا · interpretation]
- 19:12/q14/p1 Such a reciter is among those given judgment as a child. — Ibn Abbas says this in Ibn Atiyya, Ibn Jawzi and Qurtubi; Baghawi attributes it to early authorities. [5 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 19:12/q15 Can a child receive prophetic wisdom or prophethood?  [وَءَاتَيْنَٰهُ ٱلْحُكْمَ صَبِيًّۭا · theology]
turns on: Whether the extraordinary childhood gift includes the prophetic office.
- 19:12/q15/p1 Yes; extraordinary childhood intellect or prophecy is possible by miracle. — Razi says objection requires denial of miracles. Biqai says pre-maturity prophecy is exceptional because the charge ordinarily requires developed reason and strength. Ibn Ashur accepts Yahya's prophecy well before forty, perhaps due to early martyrdom. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 19:12/q15/p2 The boyhood grant was exceptional judgment, but childhood prophecy is unlikely. — Ibn Ashur reads sound judgment and perception rather than a prophetic office in boyhood. Tabatabai excludes prophecy from hukm because the Quran distinguishes the two. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 19:12/q16 What does Yahya's childhood grant show about imamate?  [وَءَاتَيْنَٰهُ ٱلْحُكْمَ صَبِيًّۭا · theology]
- 19:12/q16/p1 God can grant imamate in childhood. — Ali ibn Asbat reports the five-year-old Baqir citing Yahya's gift. Tabatabai adds Baqir's comparison with full strength at forty in 46:15, showing wisdom can come in childhood or adulthood; he takes the report as supporting his view. [3 notes: TABRISI(علي بن أسباط عن أبي جعفر); TABATABAI(علي بن أسباط)]

## 19:12/q17 What inward meaning is drawn from taking the Book?  [خُذِ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ بِقُوَّةٍۢ · inward]
turns on: Whether the reading stresses confidence in taking scripture or transformation by divine effusion.
- 19:12/q17/p1 The living heart receives divine effusion by God's power and is transformed. — The Najmi interpretations reported by Bursevi contrast God's power with weak human power and list knowledge, wisdom, mercy, purity from other-than-God and piety. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 19:12/q17/p2 It calls for strength of heart and confidence in God. — Wasiti excludes reliance on prostration, tears and pleading and says God may honor or abase without cause. Qushayri calls heart-strength a prophetic gift. [2 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); QUSHAYRI]

## 19:12/q18 May a ritually impure person utter this Quranic phrase?  [خُذِ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ بِقُوَّةٍۢ · law]
- 19:12/q18/p1 Yes, when intending something other than Quranic recitation. — Nawawi, reported by Burhan, gives 'take the Scripture firmly' and a travel supplication as examples. [1 notes: BURHAN(النووي)]

## 19:12/q19 What does childhood wisdom add to Yahya's distinctive standing?  [وَءَاتَيْنَٰهُ ٱلْحُكْمَ صَبِيًّۭا · interpretation]
- 19:12/q19/p1 It is among his distinctive honors and signs. — Kuran Yolu combines elderly parents, unprecedented name and childhood wisdom, meaning prophecy or scripture grasp. Tabatabai lists unique naming, childhood wisdom and peace at birth, death and resurrection; 3:39, 3:45 and 21:90 show leadership, chastity and righteousness, with Jesus sharing some traits later. Biqai says Muhammad's people lacked a prior prophetic community whose denial would contradict its own history; Muhammad's truthfulness and enduring Quranic miracle served that role. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 19:12/q20 How does this account connect to the preceding surah?  [* · links]
- 19:12/q20/p1 Both surahs recount astonishing events. — Abu Hayyan compares the Cave, Moses and Khidr, and Dhul Qarnayn in surah 18 (18:9, 18:60, 18:83) with John's birth to an old man and barren woman and Jesus' fatherless birth. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 19:12/q21 Where was this surah revealed?  [* · sciences]
- 19:12/q21/p1 It is Meccan, with a reported exception for its prostration verse. — Abu Hayyan calls it Meccan like the prior surah; Muqatil puts the prostration verse in Medina after migration to Abyssinia. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 19:12/q22 How does Zakariya's story serve the surah's argument?  [* · links]
- 19:12/q22/p1 It introduces Mary and rebuts the argument that Jesus' miraculous birth proves divinity. — Islahi says the earlier miraculous birth prepares Mary's account and counters the Christian inference. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 19:12/q23 What broader pattern does the surah draw from the prophetic line?  [* · interpretation]
- 19:12/q23/p1 Prophets preached monotheism, prayer and zakat, but followers abandoned them. — Islahi names Adam's, Noah's and Abraham's lines, then later polytheism, neglected rites, innovations and desires. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 19:12/q24 What encouragement does the surah give the Prophet?  [* · interpretation]
- 19:12/q24/p1 Persevere through hostility and expect success. — Islahi says the Prophet is urged to persist and receives glad tidings. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 19:12/q25 How does the surah explain opponents' worldly success?  [* · interpretation]
- 19:12/q25/p1 It is respite, not proof of their truth. — Islahi says the truth must be fully conveyed so they have no excuse when seized. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 19:12/q26 Why are Mary and Jesus prominent in the surah?  [* · links]
- 19:12/q26/p1 Their account exposes the People of the Book and their support for Quraysh. — Islahi says Jews and Christians secretly backed Quraysh and cannot sustain an untrue cause. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 19:12/q27 When did Zakariya emerge from the prayer niche?  [* · reports]
- 19:12/q27/p1 The morning after his wife conceived. — Ibn Jawzi refers the preceding account to 3:39. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 19:12/q28 How did Zakariya communicate to his people?  [* · interpretation]

- 19:12/q30/p1 Compassion from the Lord toward Yahya. — Mujahid glosses the next verse's hananan this way. [1 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد)]

## 19:12/q31 What is reported about Yahya's later life?  [* · reports]
- 19:12/q31/p1 He lived ascetically, preached repentance and piety, and was martyred. — Tabatabai describes birth to an elderly father and barren mother, worship from childhood, lifelong unmarried state and contemporaneity with Jesus whom he affirmed. His people loved him and gathered to hear him. Islahi says guarding God's Book against Satan eventually cost his life. [3 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 19:12/q33 How did Jesus and Yahya compare one another?  [* · reports]
- 19:12/q33/p1 Each asked the other to seek forgiveness and called the other better. — Hasan's report in Ibn Kathir says Jesus cited God's peace upon Yahya as evidence of merit. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن)]
