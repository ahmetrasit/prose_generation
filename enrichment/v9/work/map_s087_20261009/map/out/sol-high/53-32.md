# 53:32: 32 questions

## 53:32/q01 How does “those who avoid” attach to the preceding verse?  [ٱلَّذِينَ يَجْتَنِبُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the relative phrase continues the description of good-doers or starts a new predication.
- 53:32/q01/p1 It qualifies or substitutes for the previous “those who do good.” — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it an adjective; Rāzī prefers a badal, as avoiding major sins identifies the good-doers. Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī allow apposition or clarification; Ibn ʿĀshūr makes it a defining modifier. [13 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI(سعدي المفتي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 53:32/q01/p2 It begins a new sentence or is raised as praise. — Rāzī says the new sentence promises forgiveness to avoiders and distinguishes them from good-doers and unaccountable children. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī allow an omitted subject, 'they are those who.' [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 53:32/q01/p3 Making “those who” subject and “vast in forgiveness” its predicate is rejected. — Al-Ālūsī rejects the omitted-link analysis; Saʿdī al-Muftī objects that one approach leaves the earlier phrase idle. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI(سعدي المفتي)]
- 53:32/q01/p4 Yajtanibūn means to keep away or leave aside. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya supplies the lexical gloss, fitting the description of doers of good. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 53:32/q02 What does the present tense of “avoid” convey?  [يَجْتَنِبُونَ · rhetoric]
- 53:32/q02/p1 It indicates continuing, habitual avoidance of varied sins. — Rāzī contrasts the past tense for once-for-all repudiation of idols at 39:17 and the present here and at 42:37; Bursevī says purification precedes adornment and repeated restraint merits Paradise; al-Ālūsī concurs. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI+]

## 53:32/q03 What makes a sin a major sin here?  [كَبَٰٓئِرَ ٱلْإِثْمِ · interpretation]
turns on: Which mark of gravity is decisive: explicit threat, legal penalty, religious contempt, repentance, or shirk.
- 53:32/q03/p1 A specific severe threat of punishment marks major sin. — Muqātil and Ibn ʿAbbās specify the Fire; Bayḍāwī, Biqāʿī, Bursawī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and others use explicit grave warning. Al-Ālūsī prefers threat over hadd alone because it captures slander, tale-bearing, and filial disobedience. [18 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(فقهاء); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الرواية); STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL(البغوي)+; RAZI-FULL-]
- 53:32/q03/p2 It is shirk or unbelief, the supreme great sin. — Al-Ṭabarī is reported by Māwardī; Zayd b. Aslam explains singular kabīr as kufr; Ibn ʿAbbās explains the singular as shirk; Qushayrī calls shirk greatest of all. [9 notes: FARRA(يحيى وأصحاب عبد الله); TAB-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); MAWARDI-FULL(الطبري); QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(الأعمش ويحيى بن وثاب وحمزة والكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وخلف); ALUSI-FULL(حمزة، الكسائي، خلف)]
- 53:32/q03/p3 A sin not expiated without repentance is major. — Ibn ʿĪsā, the Muʿtazila as Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān report, and Zamakhsharī in Study Quran use this criterion. Rāzī rejects it as unclear; Abū Ḥayyān calls it a Muʿtazilite tendency. Abū Ḥayyān reiterates that Zamakhsharī's repentance-only account reflects Muʿtazilite doctrine. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); STUDYQURAN(Z); RAZI-FULL-]
- 53:32/q03/p4 A sin becomes major through persistence; repentance removes that status. — Ibn ʿAbbās says no major sin remains with seeking forgiveness and no minor with persistence; Ibn Jubayr says no repentance makes a sin major; Biqāʿī cites 24:15 for treating minor wrongdoing lightly. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن جبير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 53:32/q03/p5 It is a sin with a prescribed worldly penalty. — Jurists in Māwardī, Bayḍāwī, and Ālūsī give the hadd criterion; al-Rāfiʿī leans to it. The position differs from the view that legal penalties define fawāḥish. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(بعض الفقهاء); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البغوي); RAZI-FULL-]
- 53:32/q03/p6 It bears a hadd, Hell threat, curse, comparable harm, or evident contempt for religion. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers a broad warning/penalty/curse test; Māwardī says hadd or threat. Al-Barīzī's broader test, including apparent wrongful killing or adultery in mistaken belief, is preferred by al-Ālūsī. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الماوردي,شيخ الإسلام البارزي); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الإسلام البارزي)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 53:32/q03/p7 A sin shows disregard for religion or weak religious commitment. — Rāzī makes social judgment of reverence contextual: a scholar's conduct, a peddler's conduct, and the time of prayer can change gravity. Imām al-Ḥaramayn defines grave offenses by contempt; al-Adhraʿī relates this to probity. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا); ALUSI-FULL(إمام الحرمين,القاضي حسين، الحليمي); IBNASHUR-FULL(إمام الحرمين)]
- 53:32/q03/p8 Its boundary is intentionally unknown or only approximate. — Al-Wāḥidī says concealment discourages people from treating unlisted acts lightly, likening it to the hidden greatest name, middle prayer, Night of Decree, and response hour. Ibn Ḥajar says proposed tests approximate. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن حجر الهيتمي,الواحدي); STUDYQURAN]
- 53:32/q03/p9 The Prophet identified shirk, killing a child for fear of food, and adultery with a neighbor's wife. — Māwardī transmits Ibn Masʿūd's report of the Prophet's examples; the neighbor aggravation also appears in Qāḍī Ḥusayn's view. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(القاضي حسين، الحليمي)]
- 53:32/q03/p10 Ithm is wrongdoing that impedes reward, including oppression and usurping rights. — Rāghib's root gloss is reported by Ṭabāṭabāʾī; Islahi uses the term for rights, injustice and oppression. [2 notes: TABATABAI(الراغب); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 53:32/q03/p11 An explicit Quranic prohibition identifies major sins. — An unnamed definition in Ālūsī also counts hadd, missed immediate obligation, false testimony and perjury; another lists carrion, pork, orphan property and fleeing battle, but critics reject a restricted count. Harawī and Shurayḥ add opposing consensus. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الهروي، شريح,غير مسمى)]
- 53:32/q03/p12 Deeming the act lawful would amount to unbelief. — Rāzī reports this criterion but rejects it with other definitions as clarifying an unclear term by an equally unclear one. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL-]
- 53:32/q03/p13 Kabāʾir is a feminine adjective modifying implicit sinful acts. — Rāzī supplies the omitted noun and distinguishes quantity of grave acts from the quality of fawāḥish. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:32/q04 How many major sins are there?  [كَبَٰٓئِرَ ٱلْإِثْمِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the reports enumerate a fixed prophetic set, a much larger range, or only examples.
- 53:32/q04/p1 Seven destructive sins are singled out. — The hadith names shirk, magic, murder, orphan property, usury, desertion, and slander of chaste believing women; the majority in Ibn ʿAṭiyya and ʿAlī, ʿAṭāʾ and ʿUbayd in Ālūsī cite seven. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL(علي، عطاء، عبيد بن عمير)]
- 53:32/q04/p2 They are nearer seventy than seven. — Ibn ʿAbbās gives this estimate, with Bursevī also quoting it. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI]
- 53:32/q04/p3 They are nearer seven hundred than seven. — Ibn ʿAbbās is also reported with this larger count; Māwardī and Ālūsī add his maxim about repentance and persistence. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 53:32/q04/p4 Seventeen are counted by bodily or spiritual organ. — Abū Ṭālib al-Makkī counts four of the heart, four tongue, three belly, two sexual, two hand, flight from battle, and filial disobedience; Ālūsī notes flaws. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو طالب المكي)]
- 53:32/q04/p5 Twenty-five explicit hadith cases are counted. — Al-ʿAlāʾī gives twenty-five, but Ibn Ḥajar criticizes omissions. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الإسلام العلائي)]
- 53:32/q04/p6 Other counts are three, four, ten, fourteen, or fifteen. — Ālūsī reports these alternatives, including three from Ibn Masʿūd. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(غير مسمى)]
- 53:32/q04/p7 Ibn ʿAbbās counts sins named through Qurʾān 4:31. — The report covers the opening of al-Nisāʾ through its major-sins verse. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 53:32/q05 Does the verse distinguish major from minor sins?  [كَبَٰٓئِرَ ٱلْإِثْمِ · theology]
turns on: Whether 'minor' is an absolute category or a relative description before God, and what avoiding major sins guarantees.
- 53:32/q05/p1 Sins divide into major and minor, with lesser sins pardoned on avoiding major ones. — Ṭabarī, Naḥḥās, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Kathīr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī cite 4:31. Jishumī uses the distinction against Khārijites; al-Ghazālī says denying it is inappropriate; the majority says both sides recognize differences in moral probity. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور,الغزالي); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 53:32/q05/p2 Every sin is major in absolute relation to God's command; lesser is relative. — Qushayrī, Rāzī, Asʾila al-Muqḥima, and scholars named by Ālūsī argue that defying God's gifts makes any sin weighty, though shirk is greatest. Ibn Furak attributes this to Ashʿarites; Ibn Masʿūd reports every prohibition as major. Error and forgetfulness receive grace. [11 notes: QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(الأسئلة المقحمة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن فورك,ابن مسعود,الأستاذ أبو إسحاق الأسفرايني، القاضي أبو بكر الباقلاني، إمام الحرمين، تقي الدين السبكي، ابن القريشي,القاضي عبد الوهاب,المعتزلة)]
- 53:32/q05/p3 Avoiding major sins merits forgiveness as a divine promise. — Jishumī says pardon is deserved and supports the doctrine of divine threat; Ṭabarī cites 4:31, whereas Bayḍāwī and Nasafī emphasize God's freedom to forgive as He wills. [2 notes: JISHUMI; TAB-FULL]

## 53:32/q06 How is “major sins” read?  [كَبَٰٓئِرَ ٱلْإِثْمِ · readings]
turns on: Whether the singular denotes a collective genus or a single supreme sin; readers and grammatical precedents vary.
- 53:32/q06/p1 The singular kabīr al-ithm is read, expressing a genus, multiple sins, or shirk. — Farrāʾ names Yaḥyā and Ibn Masʿūd's companions; Ḥamza, Kisāʾī, and Khalaf recur. Al-Fārisī argues faʿīl can be collective like faʿūl, citing 4:92, 6:112, 26:100–101, 4:69, 50:17 and poetry; the abstract ithm is collective. Sources differ on exact reader lists. [26 notes: FARRA(يحيى وأصحاب عبد الله); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(حمزة والكسائي); THALABI(يحيى بن رئاب وحمزة والكسائي وخلف); WAHIDI-BASIT(حمزة والكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن وثاب وطلحة والأعمش وعيسى وحمزة والكسائي); TABRISI(قارئ); QURTUBI-FULL(الأعمش ويحيى بن وثاب وحمزة والكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وخلف); NASAFI-FULL(حمزة وعلى); NASHR(حمزة والكسائي وخلف); ALUSI-FULL(حمزة، الكسائي، خلف); IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER(Lvw)]
- 53:32/q06/p2 The plural kabāʾir is the majority reading. — It directly expresses multiple grave sins; al-Wāḥidī notes singular ithm can still denote a mass or genus. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the singular/plural alternation compatible with a collective noun. [7 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(حمزة والكسائي); THALABI(يحيى بن رئاب وحمزة والكسائي وخلف); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء والناس); IBNASHUR-FULL; NASHR(حمزة والكسائي وخلف)]

## 53:32/q07 What are the fawāḥish?  [ٱلْفَوَٰحِشَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word denotes all ugly sins, a legally punished class, or sexual offenses in particular.
- 53:32/q07/p1 They are especially ugly or manifestly shameful grave sins. — Ṭabrisī calls them the ugliest; Rāzī derives manifest repugnance from Arabic and camel usage; Rāghib and Ibn al-Jawzī attest the root's excess and foul speech. Ibn ʿĀshūr gives adultery, theft and ambush killing; Ṭabāṭabāʾī adduces adultery and sodomy. [17 notes: TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(الحديث); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Z)]
- 53:32/q07/p2 They are acts carrying a prescribed worldly penalty. — Muqātil and Ibn ʿAbbās distinguish Hell-marked major sins from legally punished indecencies; later sources repeat this. The Nabhan chronology raises a Meccan/Medinan problem. [9 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURHAN; ITQAN]
- 53:32/q07/p3 They mean adultery or illicit intercourse. — Zayd b. Aslam, Māwardī, Qurṭubī and Islahi identify zina or open sexual transgression; Ibn al-Jawzī supplies 3:135, 4:15, 7:33 and 33:30 as usage. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Z)]
- 53:32/q07/p4 They mean all sin or disobedience. — Māwardī and Qushayrī report the broad gloss; Ibn al-Jawzī cites 7:28 for sin. Zayd b. Thābit emphasizes both outward and inward indecencies. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(زيد بن ثابت); MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); DURR-FULL(زيد بن ثابت)]
- 53:32/q07/p5 The term can designate sodomy or a wife's marital misconduct elsewhere. — Ibn al-Jawzī adduces Lot's people at 29:28 for the former, and 4:19 and 65:1 for the latter; these are Quranic usages of the word. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 53:32/q07/p6 These are the sins already mentioned in the surrounding description. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya ties them to the prior discussion rather than listing individual acts. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 53:32/q08 Why mention fawāḥish after major sins?  [كَبَٰٓئِرَ ٱلْإِثْمِ ٱلْفَوَٰحِشَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether coordination selects a heinous subset, marks overlapping categories, or repeats a synonym.
- 53:32/q08/p1 Fawāḥish are a specially heinous subset of major sins, named after the general category. — Abū Ḥayyān and Bursawī call it specific after general; Bayḍāwī and Nasafī say particularly obscene members; Rāzī distinguishes magnitude from conspicuous ugliness, explaining why only kabāʾir needs 'of sin.' [10 notes: NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:32/q08/p2 They are coordinated, overlapping but distinguishable categories. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the conjunction marks distinct categories despite overlap; Rāzī contrasts quantitative greatness and qualitative ugliness. [3 notes: NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:32/q08/p3 They may be synonyms for grave sins. — Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 53:32/q09 What does al-lamam denote in this verse?  [ٱللَّمَمَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the lapse is defined by smallness, lack of penalty, brief occurrence and repentance, pre-Islamic time, or mere intention.
- 53:32/q09/p1 Minor sins or slight lapses, outside major sins and indecencies. — Ṭabarī prefers this with 4:31 and a disconnected exception; Naḥḥās calls it most inclusive. A glance, kiss, touch, wink or remark exemplifies it, but sources vary over whether intention, repetition, or repentance is needed. Five prayers, Fridays and Ramadans expiate intervening lesser sins when major ones are avoided. [64 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العلم بكلام العرب); ZAJJAJ(قيل); THALABI(أبو هريرة عن النبي,ابن مسعود ومسروق والشعبي وأبو سعيد الخدري وحذيفة وابن عباس,الفراء,الكلبي); MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI(تكلموا فيه وقالوا,ويقال); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,الكلبي عن ابن عباس,عطاء عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو هريرة وابن عباس والشعبي وغيرهم); TABRISI(ابن مسعود وأبو هريرة والشعبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود وأبو هريرة والشعبي ومسروق); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الجوهري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو هريرة وابن عباس والشعبي والكلبي,الخدري); SAMIN-DURR(جماعة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري,ابن عباس); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(قيل); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(JJ); NAHHAS+; QUSHAYRI+; TAB-FULL+]
- 53:32/q09/p2 An occasional sin, even a grave indecency, followed by repentance and no persistence. — Reports from Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Ḥasan, Abū Ṣāliḥ and others give zina, theft or drinking once, then stopping. Abū Ṣāliḥ's answer was praised by Ibn ʿAbbās. Zajjāj prefers it from the root's brief-contact sense and the breadth of forgiveness; Ṭabāṭabāʾī favors nonhabitual lapses, citing 3:135. Ibn Kathīr questions the Prophetic attribution of a supporting verse. [113 notes: MUJAHID(الحسن); ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن); TAB-FULL(أبو صالح,أبو هريرة,أصحاب النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,ابن عباس,الحسن,بعض أهل التأويل,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن القاسم,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ(قيل); THALABI(أبو هريرة ومجاهد والحسن وأبو صالح وابن عباس,الأعشى,الفراء,الكلبي,النبي,عطاء بن أبي رياح); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن ومجاهد,بعض التابعين); QUSHAYRI(بعض السلف,ويقال); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو عبيدة,ابن عباس,الحسن,السدي عن أبي صالح,المبرد,الناس,النبي,جرير,قوم); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو صالح، ابن عباس,أبو هريرة، مجاهد، الحسن، ابن عباس,ابن عباس,رسول الله); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره,الحسن عند الطبري,النبي,نفطويه); TABRISI(أمية,ابن عباس,الأعشى,الحسن والسدي,الفراء,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس والحسن والسدي,زيد بن ثابت); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الزجاج,الفراء,الكلبي,النحاس,عطاء بن أبي رباح,مجاهد والحسن والزهري,نفطويه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,نفطويه); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة,أصحاب رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,ابن عباس,السدي، وابن عباس,شاعر,مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL(أبو صالح,ابن عباس,البغوي); DURR-FULL(أبو صالح,أبو هريرة,ابن عباس,الحسن,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(ابن عباس,الأسئلة المقحمة,بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(أئمة أهل البيت عليهم السلام); ASAD-NOTES(Baghawi, Razi, Ibn Kathir); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Mujāhid and Ibn ‘Abbās (rta)); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI+; TABRISI(الزجاج)+; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق)+; ZAJJAJ+]

Naḥḥās says al-lamam is accusative as a heterogeneous exception. Wāḥidī prefers it because a connected exception might imply permission. Abū ʿUbayd permits a pause before it. [28 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(بعض أهل التأويل,بعض أهل العلم بكلام العرب); NAHHAS; QUSHAYRI(تكلموا فيه وقالوا); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,المبرد); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(جماعة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN(أبو عبيد); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); QUSHAYRI+; TAB-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]

[12 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض أهل التأويل); THALABI(أبو هريرة ومجاهد والحسن وأبو صالح وابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(قوم); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة، مجاهد، الحسن، ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(البغوي); BURSEVI(ابن عباس)]

## 53:32/q12 What is encompassed by God's vast forgiveness?  [وَٰسِعُ ٱلْمَغْفِرَةِ · theology]
turns on: Whether forgiveness is promised through avoidance of major sins, conditioned on repentance, or left to God's will.
- 53:32/q12/p1 Minor sins are pardoned for those avoiding major sins. — Ṭabarī and others use 4:31; Ibn ʿAbbās says prayers expiate lapses below the two punishments. Bursevī cites five prayers, Fridays, Ramadans and 11:114. Biqāʿī excludes habitual minor wrongs. Ṭabarī says God does not reinstate punishment for what He forgave. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says this promise consoles believers, who commonly lapse. [24 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الزبير، وعكرمة، وقتادة، والضحاك، ومقاتل,العوفي والحكم عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Z); IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 53:32/q12/p2 God forgives repentant grave sinners as well as lesser lapses. — Ibn ʿAbbās applies vast forgiveness to a repentant lapse; Ibn Kathīr cites 39:53; Rāzī says the breadth embraces repentant major sinners but persistent sinners lie outside the stated pardon. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the promise encourages repentance. [11 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); TABATABAI]

- 53:32/q12/p4 Forgiveness covers the failings of every creature before God's favors. — Rāzī derives maghfira from covering: even a person who gives a benefactor only a dirham fails to repay unstinting favor. Ṭaʿlabī cites 7:156, 'My mercy encompasses everything.' [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; THALABI]

## 53:32/q13 Why call the forgiveness “vast” and God “your Lord”?  [إِنَّ رَبَّكَ وَٰسِعُ ٱلْمَغْفِرَةِ · rhetoric]
- 53:32/q13/p1 “Vast” metaphorically conveys abundance, and “your Lord” conveys care. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 40:7, notes the Prophet is addressed as the channel of care for followers, and links the breadth to allowing lapses among righteous people. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:32/q14 What does God's knowing people from their origin establish?  [هُوَ أَعْلَمُ بِكُمْ · interpretation]

- 53:32/q14/p5 The verse answers unbelievers who doubted God knew them. — Rāzī gives this as one possible addressee, while another makes the address universal. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:32/q15 Is aʿlam comparative here?  [أَعْلَمُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether comparison with human knowledge is retained or aʿlam simply means knowing.
- 53:32/q15/p1 Aʿlam means truly more knowing than anyone else. — Farrāʾ and Ibn ʿAṭiyya use literal comparison; Abū Ḥayyān says there is no need to abandon it; Ālūsī says God could inform angels, so sole knowledge is not required. [7 notes: FARRA; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور أهل المعاني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مكي); ALUSI-FULL(مكي); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 53:32/q15/p2 Aʿlam can mean simply “knowing,” without comparison. — Naḥḥās and Makkī allow this; Burhān says comparison ceases when none shares divine knowledge. Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī resist abandoning the comparative. [3 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مكي بن أبي طالب); BURHAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مكي)-; ALUSI-FULL(مكي)-]

## 53:32/q16 What governs the temporal phrase “when He created you”?  [إِذْ أَنشَأَكُم · grammar]
turns on: Whether the adverb modifies divine knowing or an implied command to remember.
- 53:32/q16/p1 The temporal phrase depends on aʿlam or its implied knowing. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers direct attachment: knowledge of embryos entails knowledge of adults. Rāzī allows the verb implied by aʿlam; al-Ālūsī retains ordinary comparison. Ṭabrisī also allows attachment to ajinna or an omitted descriptive clause. [5 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 53:32/q16/p2 An omitted “remember” governs the temporal phrase. — Some grammarians and Rāzī offer this; al-Ālūsī rejects it as needless. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض النحاة); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]

## 53:32/q17 How were people created “from the earth”?  [أَنشَأَكُم مِّنَ ٱلْأَرْضِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural address is through Adam, each person's material origin, or location on earth.
- 53:32/q17/p1 It means God created humanity's father Adam from dust. — Farrāʾ and many exegetes read a plural address through the common ancestor; Ibn Qutayba cites analogous shifts in addressee. Ṭabarī says people then arose from Adam; Ibn Kathīr describes offspring extracted from his loins. [31 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(من الناس); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ, IK, Q,JJ, Q); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 53:32/q17/p2 It means each person derives from earth through food, bodily substances, and semen. — Rāzī prefers this and rebuts an Adam-only objection; Balkhī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī explain the process through food and parental substances. Tirmidhī gives a shared soil model; Elmalılı includes first human cell, and Kuran Yolu adds ovum and sperm. Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites al-Rāghib's nashʾ as bringing into being and nurturing. [15 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(البلخي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الترمذي أبو عبد الله); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(الراغب); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL+]
- 53:32/q17/p3 It means creating humans upon the earth. — Kuran Yolu records this spatial explanation. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 53:32/q17/p4 It may allude to each person's burial soil. — Qurṭubī recalls the view under 6:2 that people are created from the soil where they will be buried. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 53:32/q18 What does ajinna mean?  [أَجِنَّةٌۭ · meaning]
- 53:32/q18/p1 Ajnna is the plural of janīn, a fetus concealed in the womb. — Abū ʿUbayda compares sarīr/asirra; Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī and others derive the name from hiddenness. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes a passive pattern and three dark layers. ʿAmr's verse uses janīn for someone concealed in a grave; Ruʾba's line uses hidden embryos. After birth, a different child or miscarriage term applies. ## 53:32/q19 Why specify embryos in their mothers' wombs?  [أَجِنَّةٌۭ فِى بُطُونِ أُمَّهَٰتِكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 53:32/q19/p1 The seemingly redundant womb phrase intensifies proof of God's hidden knowledge and power. — Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān and Bursawī point to darkness; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it explanatory and recalls development from conception to birth. - 53:32/q20/p1 Dialectal forms have initial kasra and varied vowels on doubled mīm. — Qiraat-ER reports kasra on the initial alif with either kasra or fatḥa on doubled mīm; two tagged variants also describe kasra on alif and mīm or fatḥa on mīm. All retain 'mothers.' [4 notes: QIRAAT-ER(vw)]

Aslam emphasize innocence claims; Rāzī warns against certitude about final salvation and supports 'I am a believer, God willing.' - 53:32/q21/p5 It rebukes trust in lineage, sanctuary service, intercessors, or inherited holiness. — Islahi says Quraysh and People of the Book imagined exemptions from judgment; he compares 9:19, cites Jesus' answer about Abrahamic descent, and rejects similar Muslim assumptions about families and graves. ## 53:32/q22 When may a person praise or commend someone?  [فَلَا تُزَكُّوٓا۟ أَنفُسَكُمْ · law]
turns on: How certainty, audience, motive, and legal or public need qualify praise.
- 53:32/q22/p1 Truthful, qualified commendation for a public or legal need is allowed. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya permits commending a model and assessing witnesses; Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī allow testimony establishing rights. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows tested trustworthiness and technical narrator taʿdīl; Joseph's self-description at 12:55 is a public-benefit example. The hadith formula says 'I think him such, but God knows.' [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN]
- 53:32/q22/p2 Mentioning one's obedience as gratitude or joy is allowed without vanity. — Nasafī and Ālūsī distinguish gratitude from ostentation; Bursawī makes the reported boasting occasion blameworthy only when driven by vanity. One may not presume on the deed's final acceptance. [4 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 53:32/q22/p3 Face-to-face flattery is forbidden; discreet true praise may be allowed. — Abū al-Layth distinguishes praise to a face, absent praise expected to reach a person, and private truthful praise. Bursawī reports literal or figurative dust, withholding gifts, or even paying a flatterer to silence him; posthumous praise is allowed within bounds. The Mathnawī warns that praise inflates even virtuous people. [8 notes: THALABI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو بكرة,المقداد بن الأسود); BURSEVI(أبو الليث,المثنوي)]

## 53:32/q23 How does the verse bear on names implying purity?  [فَلَا تُزَكُّوٓا۟ أَنفُسَكُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether a name explicitly certifies purity or merely has an auspicious sense.
- 53:32/q23/p1 Names that ascribe purity should be changed or avoided. — The Prophet changed Barra to Zaynab, saying God knows who is pious; Ibn Kathīr cites Muslim, and Ālūsī cites several transmitters and recommends changing such names. Ālūsī limits dislike to strongly explicit praise names and excludes Saʿīd or Ḥasan. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(آخرون,النبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(زينب بنت أبي سلمة); DURR-FULL(زينب بنت أبي سلمة); ALUSI-FULL(زينب بنت أبي سلمة); IBNASHUR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 53:32/q23/p2 Some other auspicious names are discouraged because they invite ill omen. — Ālūsī mentions Baraka, Yasār, Nāfiʿ and Aflaḥ, notes Nawawī's stricter reading, and reports ʿUmar's uncertain renaming of ʿĀṣiya as Jamīla. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 53:32/q24 Who is the person God knows as pious?  [هُوَ أَعْلَمُ بِمَنِ ٱتَّقَىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether piety is specified as avoiding shirk, active sincere obedience, or an ultimate state only God can assess.
- 53:32/q24/p1 The pious do good, turn from evil, obey, and make their deeds sincere. — ʿAlī's formulation is doing good and leaving wrong; Ḥasan emphasizes sincerity. Naḥḥās adds obligations and fear, while Wāḥidī says righteousness and obedience. - 53:32/q24/p2 Only God can finally assess piety, whose degree and outcome may be hidden. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls piety the true basis of commendation; Biqāʿī cites a hadith of a person's deeds changing near death. - 53:32/q24/p3 The pious avoid shirk and major sins and believe. — Thaʿlabī, Ṭabrisī and Ibn al-Jawzī emphasize avoiding idolatry; Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī report shirk alone as a gloss. [5 notes: THALABI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الثعلبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 53:32/q25 How does this verse connect to its context and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Which nearby statement or wider polemic the verse explains.
- 53:32/q25/p1 The verse defines the good-doers rewarded in 53:31. — Rāzī and Ibn ʿĀshūr take avoidance as a mark of iḥsān; Ibn Kathīr cites 4:31 for pardon of lesser acts. - 53:32/q25/p2 God's earlier knowledge reinforces His knowledge of guidance and error. — Mujāhid links the creation claim to 28:56 in one transmission and 6:117 in Ṭabarī; Rāzī relates it to divine decree of guided and misguided persons. - 53:32/q25/p3 God's knowledge and forgiveness lead directly to the ban on self-praise. — Rāzī notes 53:30 and 53:31 as alternate contexts; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the knowledge clause an answer to why God grants easy forgiveness, and the fāʾ a parenthetical warning before 53:33. - 53:32/q25/p4 The verse answers claims of automatic rank through lineage or sanctuary service. — Islahi cites 9:19, 70:38–39, and claims of Abrahamic descent among Quraysh and People of the Book; he says only faith, deeds and God's assessment establish rank. [9 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:32/q29 Is this passage Meccan or Medinan?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the legal and narrative setting outweighs the sura's Meccan classification.
- 53:32/q29/p1 This passage is Medinan although al-Najm is classified as Meccan. — Itqān lists 53:32 as an exception; its second report argues that fixed legal punishments and campaigns presuppose Medina. Burhān notes Mecca had no such penalties or campaigns and connects Nabhan's story. [4 notes: ITQAN; BURHAN; STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 53:32/q29/p2 The verse is Meccan; a later Jewish report cannot be its original occasion. — Ibn ʿĀshūr argues the Jewish debate was in Medina and the Ibn Lahīʿa report is weak; the Prophet may have recited the already-revealed verse there. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن معين ووكيع ويحيى القطان وابن مهدي,الذهبي,ثابت بن حارث الأنصاري); IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 53:32/q30 What inward readings are given for this verse?  [* · inward]
turns on: How inward humility and surrender are read, and whether mystical claims of intrinsic purity are accepted.
- 53:32/q30/p1 Self-praise reveals a veiled, arrogant ego; humility follows awareness of origin. — Abū ʿUthmān says the commanding self should see servitude's deficiency; Qushayrī says witnessing the Lord displaces self-praise and one should consider other Muslims better. [5 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان); QUSHAYRI(ويقال); BIQAI-FULL(القشيري)]
- 53:32/q30/p2 The gnostic treats even mentioning indecency as weighty. — Dhū al-Nūn's maxim, transmitted by Sulamī and Biqāʿī, measures the knower's speech against another person's act. [2 notes: SULAMI(ذو النون); BIQAI-FULL(ذو النون)]
- 53:32/q30/p3 True piety attributes knowledge and good action wholly to God. — Bursawī describes taking God as protection from all else and annihilating limited human knowledge before absolute divine knowledge; Study Quran cites Zamakhsharī that good deeds need God's support. [3 notes: BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(Z)]
- 53:32/q30/p4 Forgiveness opens to the person who seeks it and acknowledges failure. — Sulamī makes recognition of failure a condition of seeking expansive mercy. [1 notes: SULAMI]
- 53:32/q30/p5 Major sins are attachments to self, desire, and world; lamam is a slight necessary inclination. — Najmī Taʾwīlāt names these three roots and their obscene outcomes, but permits bodily rest and worldly rights without egoism. Its forgiveness is annihilation of metaphorical self and subsistence in divine reality; another elder distinguishes legitimate worldly duties from egoic gratification. [4 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,بعض الكبار)]
- 53:32/q30/p6 Claims that humans are parts of God or intrinsically flawless contradict this rebuke. — Islahi challenges waḥdat al-wujūd from the verse's lowly account of human origins, rejects Sufi self-exaltation, and attributes the creed to Bāṭinī, extremist Shiʿī and Brahman influences. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Sufis)]

## 53:32/q31 What other reports are related to this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports relate different illustrative events rather than competing versions of one event.
- 53:32/q31/p1 People die at every age, so no later age guarantees safety. — Makḥūl lists death in the womb, infancy, youth and old age; Māwardī, Qurṭubī and Ibn Kathīr cite the reflection. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مكحول); QURTUBI-FULL(مكحول); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مكحول)]
- 53:32/q31/p2 A dream depicts God's forgiveness of men who fought one another. — Abū Maysara sees Paradise pavilions for Dhū al-Kilāʿ and Ḥawshab, and learns that ʿAmmār and companions found God vast in forgiveness despite mutual fighting; Qurṭubī adds a report that Dhū al-Kilāʿ freed twelve thousand girls. [3 notes: THALABI(أبو مسيرة عمرو بن شرحبيل); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو خالد,أبو ميسرة عمرو بن شرحبيل)]
- 53:32/q31/p3 The Prophet was shown generations before and after his time. — Qurṭubī reports that some were represented in clay while still in loins or wombs, and the Prophet recognized them as Adam had learned the names. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي)]
- 53:32/q31/p4 A hadith qudsi promises unforeseen reward for the righteous. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the saying that no eye has seen, ear heard or heart imagined what God has prepared, to illustrate divine knowledge exceeding human forecasts. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الله تعالى)]
- 53:32/q31/p5 The Prophet is reported to have said God created Adam in His image. — Bursawī cites this report and leaves another version unfinished in the excerpt. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 53:32/q32 What broader teachings do the sources draw from the verse?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The sources draw distinct ethical lessons from the verse.
- 53:32/q32/p1 Avoidance of grave sins and sincere good deeds complete the rank of the good-doer. — Biqāʿī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kuran Yolu stress moral restraint; Islahi adds that minor lapses should be repaired promptly rather than allowed to dominate. The forgiven sinner remains answerable to God. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 53:32/q32/p2 Teachers point to further discussions of major sins and self-praise. — Qurṭubī refers to 4:49 for self-purification; Ālūsī directs readers to Ibn Ḥajar's al-Zawājir for major sins; Bursevī refers to 42:37. These are cross-references rather than added definitions. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 53:32/q32/p3 A firm claim that God has honored a particular deceased person exceeds human knowledge. — The Prophet questioned Umm ʿAṭiyya's praise of ʿUthmān b. Maẓʿūn and expressed hope rather than certainty; companions thereafter limited commendation to what they knew. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(أم عطية)]
