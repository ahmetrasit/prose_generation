# 37:142: 17 questions

## 37:142/q01 What does “fa-ltaqamahu” mean?  [فَٱلْتَقَمَهُ · meaning]
- 37:142/q01/p1 The fish swallowed Jonah as a morsel, often in one gulp. — Al-Ṭabarī derives the iftiʿāl form from laqm; al-Wāḥidī and al-Ālūsī connect it to a mouthful. Al-Ṭabrisī gives laqimahu, iltaqamahu, and talaqqamahu as equivalent. Al-Bayḍāwī specifies one gulp; Ibn ʿĀshūr says a huge fish swallowed without biting. [20 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 37:142/q02 How did Jonah come to be swallowed?  [فَٱلْتَقَمَهُ · reports]
turns on: The accounts differ on whether Jonah leapt, was cast in, or was caught before reaching the water.
- 37:142/q02/p1 He jumped into the sea and the waiting fish swallowed him. — Yaḥyā says the fish opened its mouth at every side of the ship; Saʿīd ibn Jubayr says it waited for God's command. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports a stalled ship and three lots naming Jonah, then a sea creature watching from all sides. Ṭāwūs likewise has three lots. Ḥamīd says Jonah asked to be tipped feet first; a larger fish followed. Al-Ālūsī's Ibn Masʿūd report describes floating arrows as the lot's method and Jonah calling himself the runaway servant. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the laden ship escaped. [11 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT(سعيد بن جبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود,قائل غير مسمى); DURR-FULL(حميد بن هلال,طاوس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود); TABATABAI; BURSEVI]
- 37:142/q02/p2 The fish caught him before he reached the water. — Al-Ālūsī transmits Qatāda: Najm raised its head about three cubits and pursued Jonah around the ship. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 37:142/q02/p3 The sailors threw him into the sea after a storm and lot, then the fish swallowed him. — The biblical account quoted by al-Biqāʿī, Elmalılı, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, and Kur'an Yolu says the sailors first tried to return to shore; the sea calmed after Jonah was cast in. It recounts his flight from Nineveh toward Tarshish via Jaffa. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL(مترجمه); ELMALILI(Âlûsî); TABATABAI(الآلوسي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Kitâb-ı Mukaddes)]

## 37:142/q03 What kind of creature is “al-ḥūt”?  [ٱلْحُوتُ · referent]
turns on: The reports variously identify its kind and name; Asad gives a symbolic interpretation of its interior.
- 37:142/q03/p1 A whale or enormous familiar fish. — Abū Ḥayyān takes ḥūt in its familiar sense; al-Biqāʿī calls it the largest familiar whale. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it was enormous and reportedly called al-bālīn in European usage. Ibn Kathīr places it in the Green Sea. Kur'an Yolu calls a whale probable and the rescue miraculous. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 37:142/q03/p2 A large fish, probably a shark. — Iṣlāḥī says ḥūt covers large fish, such as shark or whale, capable of swallowing a person whole; he favors a shark here. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 37:142/q03/p3 Al-lakhm, a fish of the Green Sea. — Al-Māwardī reports Ibn ʿAbbās naming al-lakhm and saying God ordered it to hold Jonah as prisoner. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 37:142/q03/p4 It was named Najm. — Qatāda supplies the name in Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Durr, and al-Ālūsī; other Qatāda reports add its journey and duration. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 37:142/q03/p5 The fish's interior symbolizes spiritual distress at fleeing the prophetic mission and God's presence. — Asad relates the familiar legend to an allegorical reading of 21:87. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 37:142/q04 Why is “al-ḥūt” definite?  [ٱلْحُوتُ · rhetoric]
- 37:142/q04/p1 It evokes the familiar great fish as though there were no other. — Al-Biqāʿī singles out the largest whale. Asad notes the definite article in all three Qur'anic references, including 21:87 and 68:48, and says a familiar legend may explain it. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 37:142/q05 Where did the fish come from or carry Jonah?  [ٱلْحُوتُ · reports]
turns on: The transmitted routes differ; Ibn ʿĀshūr disputes a Tigris location for Jonah's drowning.
- 37:142/q05/p1 Najm carried Jonah through the Sea of Rome, the Nile, Persia, and the Tigris. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports this from Qatāda. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة)]
- 37:142/q05/p2 It took him past Eilat and returned by the Tigris to Nineveh. — Al-Māwardī reports this without a named authority. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(قيل)]
- 37:142/q05/p3 It toured all seas and cast him on the Tigris bank. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports Saʿīd ibn Jubayr's seven-day journey. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 37:142/q05/p4 It came from Yemen and carried him to the lowest earth. — Bursevī cites Kashf al-Asrār; Jonah heard stones glorifying God there. [1 notes: BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار)]
- 37:142/q05/p5 It came from the far end of the seas to the passing ship. — Bursevī cites Kāshifī: God inspired it to open its mouth. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 37:142/q05/p6 Jonah's drowning took place in the Mediterranean Sea of Rome. — Ibn ʿĀshūr locates the event there and rejects some exegetes' claim of the Tigris. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(بعض المفسرين)]
- 37:142/q05/p7 Jonah's drowning took place in the Tigris. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this as a view of some exegetes and rejects it; other reports have the fish carry him to the Tigris. [0 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(بعض المفسرين)-]

## 37:142/q06 What was the fish ordered to do with Jonah?  [فَٱلْتَقَمَهُ ٱلْحُوتُ · reports]
- 37:142/q06/p1 Swallow and shelter him without eating or injuring him; its belly was a prison, refuge, or place of worship. — Yaḥyā says God prohibited eating and drinking him. Al-Māwardī's Ibn ʿAbbās report says no scratched skin or broken bone. Al-Wāḥidī attributes the no-injury order to the Prophet without a grading. Al-Ṭabrisī calls him a guest; Shahr calls the belly a mosque. Al-Qurṭubī's Abu Hurayra report includes sea creatures' praise and angelic intercession; Ibn Kathīr says no bruising. [11 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(النبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الله تعالى); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(شهر بن حوشب)]

## 37:142/q07 How long was Jonah in the fish?  [ٱلْحُوتُ · reports]
turns on: Reported spans range from part of a day to forty days; Ibn Kathīr does not decide.
- 37:142/q07/p1 Part of a day, morning or midday to evening, with no night. — Al-Shaʿbī is reported by Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Māwardī, al-Baghawī, al-Durr, and Ibn Kathīr through Mujālid; Ibn Kathīr says God knows. Al-Ṭabarī's Saʿīd ibn Jubayr report has ejection at sunset after prayer. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الشعبي); MAWARDI-FULL(الشعبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الشعبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الشعبي); DURR-FULL(الشعبي); TAB-FULL(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 37:142/q07/p2 One hour of daylight. — One group in Ibn ʿAṭiyya. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]
- 37:142/q07/p3 Seven hours. — Another group in Ibn ʿAṭiyya. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]
- 37:142/q07/p4 Three days, or three days and nights. — Qatāda and Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān give three days; the biblical report gives days and nights, relayed by al-Biqāʿī, Elmalılı, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل بن حيان); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مقاتل بن حيان); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة وجعفر الصادق وأبو مالك); BIQAI-FULL(مترجمه); ELMALILI(Âlûsî); TABATABAI(الآلوسي)]
- 37:142/q07/p5 Seven days or a week. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Jaʿfar, ʿAṭāʾ, and Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq are reported; Saʿīd adds a tour of all seas and ejection at the Tigris. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL(جعفر); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عطاء بن أبي رباح); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة وجعفر الصادق وأبو مالك)]
- 37:142/q07/p6 Fourteen days. — A group in Ibn ʿAṭiyya. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]
- 37:142/q07/p7 Twenty days. — Al-Baghawī reports al-Ḍaḥḥāk. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 37:142/q07/p8 Forty days or nights. — Yaḥyā says nights; Abū Mālik, al-Suddī, al-Kalbī, Muqātil ibn Sulaymān, and Ibn Jurayj are reported for forty days. [6 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNABIHATIM(أبو مالك); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو مالك); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي والكلبي ومقاتل بن سليمان); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو مالك والسدي وابن جريج); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة وجعفر الصادق وأبو مالك)]
- 37:142/q07/p9 Days and nights, without an exact count. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says he was preserved alive and well; Umayya ibn Abī al-Ṣalt's verse speaks of nights in the fish. [2 notes: TABATABAI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أمية بن أبي الصلت)]

## 37:142/q08 What did Jonah do in the fish, and how was he rescued?  [فَٱلْتَقَمَهُ ٱلْحُوتُ · reports]
- 37:142/q08/p1 He prayed, God answered, and the fish cast him out. — Yaḥyā cites 21:87–88 and identifies the three darknesses as night, sea, and belly. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr says he was cast out at sunset after prayer. Ibn Kathīr says Jonah thought he had died, then stood praying in the unprecedented place of worship. Al-Qurṭubī's Abu Hurayra report adds creatures' glorification, angels' intercession, and Jonah's sick ejection (37:145). The biblical account has him released on dry land. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI(الآلوسي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Kitâb-ı Mukaddes)]
- 37:142/q08/p2 His earlier abundant prayer and glorification prevented the belly from becoming his grave until Resurrection. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports Qatāda's conditional explanation. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة)]

## 37:142/q09 What does “mulīm” mean?  [مُلِيمٌۭ · meaning]
turns on: The senses differ on culpable action, external blame, self-blame, defect, and surrender.
- 37:142/q09/p1 He had done wrong or something deserving blame: a sinner or blameworthy person. — Mujāhid, Qatāda, Ibn Zayd, Ibn ʿAbbās and others say sinner or wrongdoer. Al-Farrāʾ, Ibn Qutayba, al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās and others define alāma as doing what merits blame. Labīd's ghayr mulīm and Umayya's poetic line witness usage. Al-Ṭabarī distinguishes mulīm, whose act merits blame even without spoken reproach, from malūm, verbally blamed whether deserved or not; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites him and al-Qurṭubī. [49 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL(الفراء); MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(إسماعيل بن أبي خالد,الكلبي,قتادة عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(لبيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس,الطبري,الفراء,القرطبي,مجاهد); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 37:142/q09/p2 He surrendered himself to his Lord. — Muqātil gives this and returns to it after citing al-Farrāʾ; al-Wāḥidī reports the same gloss. [2 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)]
- 37:142/q09/p3 He blamed himself for what he did. — Al-Māwardī reports Qatāda; al-Bayḍāwī, Bursevī and al-Ālūsī give this sense. Bursevī treats the hamza as causative; al-Ālūsī says its object is omitted. Elmalılı adds regret. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 37:142/q09/p4 He was blamed, whether or not he deserved it. — Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursevī include this sense; al-Samīn quotes an anonymous poem using mulīm for one blamed without sin. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; SAMIN-DURR]
- 37:142/q09/p5 He was blemished or made defective by his act. — Al-Qurṭubī offers blemished and says lāma can describe making oneself defective through an act; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites him. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(القرطبي)]

## 37:142/q10 What is the form of “mulīm,” and why use it?  [مُلِيمٌۭ · grammar]
turns on: How the active form and hamza convey culpability or self-blame.
- 37:142/q10/p1 It is the active form of alāma, showing Jonah as agent of the blameworthy cause. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it an active participle. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ contrasts mulīm with malūm and surveys cognates in 51:54, 17:29, 17:39, 70:30, 23:6, 12:32, 14:22, 68:30, 5:54, and 75:2. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; NAHHAS; TAB-FULL]
- 37:142/q10/p2 Alāma can mark entering blame, becoming blameworthy, or causatively blaming oneself. — Al-Ālūsī assigns these functions to the three glosses; Bursevī reserves the causative hamza for self-blame. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 37:142/q11 Is there a variant reading of “mulīm”?  [مُلِيمٌۭ · readings]
turns on: The first vowel and whether the form is active or an exceptional passive of a wāw-root.
- 37:142/q11/p1 The usual mulīm has ḍamma and is an active form from alāma. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly calls it active; al-Naḥḥās and al-Samīn derive it from alāma. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR]
- 37:142/q11/p2 An anomalous malīm has fatḥa on its first mīm and is understood as passive. — Al-Bayḍāwī derives it from līma. Al-Samīn calls it very anomalous: malūm is expected from a wāw-root, as maqūl and maṣūn are; he compares mashīb and muddaʿī with expected mashūb and madʿū. Abū Ḥayyān compares alīm; al-Ālūsī also explains it via līma. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(قارئ شاذ); ALUSI-FULL(قارئون)]

## 37:142/q12 How does “wa-huwa mulīm” function in the sentence?  [وَهُوَ مُلِيمٌۭ · grammar]
- 37:142/q12/p1 It is a circumstantial clause: Jonah was blameworthy when swallowed. — Al-Jishumī calls it accusative as ḥāl; al-Samīn agrees. Al-Nasafī and Elmalılı render the simultaneous state. [4 notes: JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR; NASAFI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 37:142/q13 What made Jonah blameworthy?  [مُلِيمٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the fault was unauthorized departure, neglected recommendation, minor sin, anger at Nineveh's reprieve, or the reported refusal of wilayah.
- 37:142/q13/p1 He left or fled his people and mission without God's authorization. — Al-Wāḥidī's scholars say the departure warranted discipline. Al-Ṭabrisī also says he left without command. Iṣlāḥī says his motive was pious but the act unauthorized and severely chastised in proportion to status. Study Quran names flight from Nineveh; its Kisāʾī report says the Ninevites beat Jonah, dismissed forty days' preaching, and called him mad. Asad connects flight with spiritual distress. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); TABRISI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Kisāʾī, Qiṣaṣ al-anbiyāʾ); ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI]
- 37:142/q13/p2 He omitted a recommended act, which may still merit blame. — Al-Ṭabrisī prefers this account and treats Jonah's experience as admonition. [1 notes: TABRISI+]
- 37:142/q13/p3 He committed an expiated minor sin. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports this from those who permit minor sins for prophets. [1 notes: TABRISI(قيل)]
- 37:142/q13/p4 He left angry that Nineveh was spared, thinking God had no power over him. — Study Quran says the Ninevites repented when Jonah watched for punishment; some explain his flight through anger that rejecters escaped it, citing 21:87. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(Some)]
- 37:142/q13/p5 He rejected divine wilayah, then affirmed it inside the fish. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites Ḥabba al-ʿUranī's report from ʿAlī and notes similar reports. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أمير المؤمنين)]

## 37:142/q14 What does Jonah's blame and trial teach about prophets?  [وَهُوَ مُلِيمٌۭ · theology]
turns on: How prophetic fault relates to admonition, training, and human weakness.
- 37:142/q14/p1 Blame can be corrective admonition and training that raises a prophet's rank. — Al-Ṭabrisī says admonition rather than punishment; al-Wāḥidī's scholars say discipline refines him. Iṣlāḥī ties its severity to rank. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says trials complete God's intimates, sometimes with reproach, and cites a proverb linking intimacy and trial. Kur'an Yolu says extraordinary events prepare prophets for monotheistic struggles. [6 notes: TABRISI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 37:142/q14/p2 The trial made Jonah confess his wrong and God's purity from willing it. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says Jonah's conduct displeased God; universal divine wilayah means God guides a servant to will only what He wills through servitude and election. [2 notes: TABATABAI]
- 37:142/q14/p3 Even prophets are susceptible to human failings. — Asad cites the human weakness of 4:28. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 37:142/q15 How does this event connect to Jonah's other Qur'anic passages and the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes stress both Jonah's prayer and the sūra's larger prophetic pattern.
- 37:142/q15/p1 Jonah prayed at 21:87, God rescued him at 21:88, and likewise saves believers. — Yaḥyā identifies three darknesses as night, sea, and fish belly and quotes the promise for believers. Qatāda says earlier glorification saved Jonah from a grave-like stay until Resurrection. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 37:142/q15/p2 The sūra selects parts of Jonah's story within a pattern of mercy, mission, and reward. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī lists mission, departure, ship, fish, rescue, renewed mission, and belief (37:133, 140, 142, 148). Kur'an Yolu says the lesson takes priority over bare history. Iṣlāḥī gives the narratives' refrain of peace, mercy, support, and reward; after Aaron the prophets follow attributes rather than chronological order. [4 notes: TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 37:142/q16 How do expanded accounts relate the swallowing to Jonah's mission?  [* · reports]
turns on: How much detail outside the Qur'anic telling can be relied on.
- 37:142/q16/p1 The biblical account gives a fuller sequence of Nineveh, flight to Tarshish, storm, fish, prayer, shore, renewed mission, and repentance. — Al-Biqāʿī quotes a translator's Book of Jonah; Elmalılı and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī relay al-Ālūsī's account, adding Jaffa and three days and nights. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds Jonah son of Amittai and Nineveh's size. Kur'an Yolu says the Bible broadly matches the Qur'an. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL(مترجمه); ELMALILI(Âlûsî); TABATABAI(الآلوسي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Kitâb-ı Mukaddes)]
- 37:142/q16/p2 Long embellishments of the story are unreliable and should be abridged. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says he deliberately shortened people's expanded storytelling. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 37:142/q17 What preceding context do commentators bring to the swallowing?  [* · links]
turns on: The cited notes explain different preceding words or the narrative gap.
- 37:142/q17/p1 The narrative omits intervening events for concision. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya supplies a stalled ship, other ships passing, blame of a sinner, and lots. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 37:142/q17/p2 The prior lot's idḥāḍ uses slipping as a metaphor for defeat or loss. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explains its literal and contextual senses. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 37:142/q17/p3 A preceding lexical discussion contrasts injustice by inclination with constriction and burden. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ explains mayl and ʿawl in the preceding fragment. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
