# 23:31: 11 questions

## 23:31/q01 What does أَنشَأْنَا mean here?  [أَنشَأْنَا · meaning]
- 23:31/q01/p1 God created and brought the next people into being. — Al-Jishumī gives two explanations of إنشاء: bringing into being without a prior model, or making without a cause. Al-Nasafī glosses it as creating. Al-Biqāʿī adds that God gave the people life and nurtured them. [3 notes: JISHUMI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 23:31/q02 Why is مِنۢ used before بَعْدِهِمْ?  [مِنۢ بَعْدِهِمْ · rhetoric]
- 23:31/q02/p1 It indicates that the next generation did not fill the whole interval after its predecessors. — Al-Biqāʿī explains the force of the preposition in terms of the interval between the peoples. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 23:31/q03 After whom was the new generation brought into being?  [بَعْدِهِمْ · referent]
- 23:31/q03/p1 After Noah's people had been destroyed. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Ālūsī explicitly place the new generation after the earlier people's destruction. Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī describe the same transition from Noah's people; al-Nasafī explicitly identifies the pronoun's antecedent as Noah's people. [7 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 23:31/q04 What does قَرْنًا mean?  [قَرْنًا · meaning]
- 23:31/q04/p1 A generation: people living in the same era. — Al-Jishumī says they are called a qarn because they coexist. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Būrsawī describe people sharing a period. Asad gives the literal sense 'generation' and points to 6:5 for a wider sense of the term, without specifying that wider sense here. [4 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 23:31/q05 Who are the later people and their messenger?  [قَرْنًا ءَاخَرِينَ · referent]
turns on: The order after Noah and Hūd's succession statement favor ʿĀd; the later punishment by a cry favors Thamūd and allows Midian. Some readers take the lack of a name as deliberate generalization or as a reason to leave the people unidentified.
- 23:31/q05/p1 ʿĀd, whose messenger was Hūd. — Ibn ʿAbbās is reported by al-Zamakhsharī and al-Biqāʿī. Al-Qurṭubī says no nation arose immediately after Noah except ʿĀd. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Būrsawī invoke Hūd's reminder in 7:69 that ʿĀd succeeded Noah's people; al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān also cite the order of stories in al-Aʿrāf, Hūd and al-Shuʿarāʾ, with Abū Ḥayyān mentioning 11:50 and 26:123. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says most reports imply ʿĀd came earlier, while noting that ʿĀd were not destroyed by a cry and leaving the many possibilities to God. Abū Ḥayyān and Kuran Yolu describe this as the majority view. Al-Biqāʿī additionally points to ʿĀd's bodily strength and size. Elmalılı calls it likely rather than certain. [23 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(çoğunluk); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; ELMALILI+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 23:31/q05/p2 Thamūd, whose messenger was Ṣāliḥ. — Al-Ṭabarī is reported by Ibn ʿAṭiyya, and Abū Sulaymān al-Dimashqī and al-Ṭabarī by Abū Ḥayyān; al-Jubbāʾī is reported by al-Ṭabrisī. Their destruction by the cry or blast is the main evidence, cited by al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī (11:67), Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Kathīr (23:41) and al-Biqāʿī. Al-Biqāʿī also cites parallels with descriptions in al-Shuʿarāʾ and al-Qamar and their objection to a human messenger. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); TABRISI(الجبائي); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو سليمان الدمشقي والطبري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(others); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Eski müfessirlerin bir kısmı); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 23:31/q05/p3 The people and prophet are left unnamed, without a firm identification. — Elmalılı and the Study Quran note the absence of an explicit name; Elmalılı still considers ʿĀd and Hūd likely. Al-Biqāʿī says the people are left unnamed so that the account applies to every oppressive, self-indulgent nation, while himself favoring ʿĀd. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(al-Qurṭubī)]
- 23:31/q05/p4 The passage refers generally to recurring experiences of prophets and peoples, without one particular pair in view. — Asad speaks of new generations after earlier peoples, each receiving a messenger who calls them to worship God alone and be conscious of Him. Kuran Yolu reports Asad's general reading and explicitly agrees with it. Al-Biqāʿī gives the unnamed people a wider application to oppressive, self-indulgent nations, while considering ʿĀd the likely historical identification. [4 notes: ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Muhammed Esed); BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 23:31/q05/p5 The people of Midian, whose messenger was Shuʿayb, could be intended. — Al-Qurṭubī observes that Midian too were seized by the cry. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]

## 23:31/q06 Why call them another generation?  [قَرْنًا ءَاخَرِينَ · rhetoric]
- 23:31/q06/p1 It marks them as newly created people, not survivors of the earlier people's destruction. — Al-Biqāʿī says the wording distinguishes the new generation from remnants of the destroyed people. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 23:31/q07 Why are the later people left unnamed?  [قَرْنًا ءَاخَرِينَ · rhetoric]
- 23:31/q07/p1 So the story can apply to every oppressive, self-indulgent nation. — Al-Biqāʿī gives this reason even though he favors identifying the people as ʿĀd. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 23:31/q08 How does this verse connect to Noah's preceding story?  [* · links]
- 23:31/q08/p1 It turns from Noah's struggle and his people's destruction to the next community God raised. — Al-Jishumī describes the turn after the previous people were destroyed; al-Ṭabrisī says God joins the two accounts. The Study Quran's introduction recalls Noah's people's denial and rebuke, the blessings mentioned earlier, and Noah's journey in the ark before this transition. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; STUDYQURAN]

## 23:31/q09 How does the verse fit the wider pattern of generations and prophets?  [* · links]
- 23:31/q09/p1 Generations succeed one another, receive messengers, and repeatedly show patterns seen in earlier prophetic stories. — Asad says God sends each generation a messenger from among them to call them to worship Him alone and be conscious of Him; Kuran Yolu reports and adopts his general reading. The Study Quran cites 6:6, 10:13, 11:116, 19:74, 19:98, 20:128, 25:38, 28:43, 28:45, 28:78, 32:26, 36:31, 38:3, 46:17 and 50:36 for successive generations, and says affluent and powerful opponents repeatedly resist prophets in this sūra and elsewhere, citing 7:59–62 and 7:136. Al-Biqāʿī says the later people repeated and exceeded earlier people's conduct. [6 notes: ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Muhammed Esed); STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL]

## 23:31/q10 What lesson is drawn from the succession of peoples?  [* · interpretation]
- 23:31/q10/p1 Later peoples failed to learn from earlier destruction and repeated or exceeded earlier wrongdoing. — Al-Biqāʿī says their repeated and greater misbehavior occurred by God's will. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 23:31/q10/p2 Past generations show the world's fleeting nature and God's power to destroy those who ignore His message. — The Study Quran points to 17:17, which specifically mentions Noah. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 23:31/q11 What inward application does Bursevi draw from the prophetic accounts?  [* · inward]
- 23:31/q11/p1 Prophets and perfected saints often migrated because life among unjust people offered no security. — Bursevi says reflection on their lives reveals that most migrated. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 23:31/q11/p2 Migration and trial can become occasions for gratitude for divine rescue. — Bursevi thanks God for his repeated migrations, Bursa as a good and forgiving home, and rescue from unjust opponents; he describes his trial becoming deliverance, subjugation becoming kindness, and majesty becoming beauty. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
