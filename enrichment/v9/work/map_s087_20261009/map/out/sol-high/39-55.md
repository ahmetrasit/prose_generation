# 39:55: 15 questions

## 39:55/q01 Whom does the command to follow address?  [وَٱتَّبِعُوٓا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the address continues the earlier address to God's servants and whether following the Qur'an is chiefly a new demand on polytheists; the jinn reading departs from the apparent sense according to al-Alusi.
- 39:55/q01/p1 Both believers and unbelievers are addressed, each according to their situation. — Al-Alusi connects the address with the earlier 'servants' and says the Qur'an came through Muhammad for all. Ibn Ashur says polytheists must embrace monotheism and Islam, whereas sinning believers must repent and increase good works. Al-Tabataba'i says revelation was sent to both groups. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 39:55/q01/p2 The directive is chiefly for polytheists. — Ibn Ashur says Muslims already follow the Qur'an, as indicated by the depiction of God's servants in 39:17–18. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:55/q01/p3 The addressees could be jinn. — Al-Alusi calls this less apparent; it takes the revealed material as the heavenly books and the Qur'an as their best. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:55/q02 What is the 'best of what was sent down' to follow?  [أَحْسَنَ مَآ أُنزِلَ إِلَيْكُم · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'best' names the whole Qur'an, a kind of revealed material, a narrower class of rulings, the safest conduct, or the most transformative inward and moral guidance. Al-Tabataba'i objects that restricting it to reported subsets repeats the preceding command to submit; Ali ibn Isa contests the abrogation gloss.
- 39:55/q02/p1 The Qur'an itself is the best revealed book and is to be followed as a whole. — Al-Zajjaj, al-Wahidi, al-Razi, Bursevi, Islahi and Study Quran cite 39:23's 'best discourse'; al-Qurtubi and al-Baghawi say all the Qur'an is good. Ibn Zayd and al-Qurtubi contrast it with Torah, Gospel and Psalms and its authority over abrogated earlier books. Al-Biqa'i adds its best teachings. Islahi calls it final, complete, protected from alteration, suited to natural religion without earlier scriptures' harsh directives, unmatched lucid Arabic for Arabs, doubly beneficial in its mutashabih and mathani form, and familiar through Abraham's religion; these gifts warrant wholehearted acceptance. Al-Alusi gives the less apparent jinn and heavenly-books version. Most exegetes reported by Kuran Yolu identify the revealed thing as Qur'an. [26 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 39:55/q02/p2 Follow God's commands in revelation and avoid disobedience and prohibited acts. — Al-Hasan says revelation also names evil to shun and lesser or permissible things one need not desire; al-Tabari includes its commands, prohibitions, reports, parables, stories, arguments, promises and warnings. Ibn Atiyya adds clear beliefs, promises, threats and forbearance, with understanding followed by action. Study Quran says revelation achieves its purpose when its injunctions are practiced. Al-Tabataba'i's reported version includes obligatory and recommended acts, avoidance of forbidden and disliked acts, and no requirement concerning the permitted. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); BURSEVI(الحسن); TABATABAI(قيل); STUDYQURAN; THALABI(الحسن)+; TABATABAI-]
- 39:55/q02/p3 Follow what God commands in His Book. — This is al-Suddi's recurring gloss; it singles out commands without separately explaining prohibitions. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); THALABI(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); TABATABAI-]
- 39:55/q02/p4 Follow the abrogating rulings rather than the abrogated ones. — Ibn Isa, al-Jubba'i and others give this interpretation; al-Razi cites 2:106, promising a better or equivalent replacement. Al-Alusi says the comparative allows two analyses here. Ali ibn Isa objects that an abrogated ruling can still be good, though following its replacement is better. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); TABRISI(الجبائي); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); TABRISI(علي بن عيسى)-; TABATABAI-]
- 39:55/q02/p5 Follow binding or strict duties rather than dispensations. — Al-Mawardi proposes strict duties over dispensations; al-Baydawi calls these decisive rulings; Bursevi says binding duties; al-Alusi reports decisive obligations and says the comparative is literal in that formulation. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 39:55/q02/p6 Choose the safest course that brings salvation, including repentance and steady obedience. — Al-Baydawi and those following him give this reading; Bursevi names repentance and sustained obedience. Al-Alusi says the comparative retains its ordinary force. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(البيضاوي ومن تبعه); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 39:55/q02/p7 Follow the decisive verses and leave knowledge of ambiguous verses to God. — Ibn Zayd gives this distinction as reported by al-Tha'labi and al-Qurtubi. [2 notes: THALABI(ابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); TABATABAI-]
- 39:55/q02/p8 The best is obedience in lawful and unlawful matters. — Muqatil frames it as the Qur'an's teachings in these matters; Ibn Ziyad gives the same practical scope. [2 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زياد); TABATABAI-]
- 39:55/q02/p9 Follow revealed legal rulings concerning lawful and unlawful matters, excluding narratives. — Al-Tabataba'i reports this restricted view of revealed material. [1 notes: TABATABAI(قيل); TABATABAI-]
- 39:55/q02/p10 Follow obligatory and supererogatory obedience, excluding merely permissible acts. — This is a view reported by al-Tabrisi; al-Tabataba'i reports a close formulation that also distinguishes forbidden and disliked acts. [2 notes: TABRISI(قيل); TABATABAI(قيل); TABATABAI-]
- 39:55/q02/p11 Fulfill the obligations. — Zayd ibn Ali gives this gloss; al-Alusi also lists obligations as a possible sense and says the comparative allows two analyses. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(زيد بن علي); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 39:55/q02/p12 Follow firm obligations and prohibitions. — Al-Tabataba'i reports this narrower view separately from a view including recommended and disliked acts. [1 notes: TABATABAI(قيل); TABATABAI-]
- 39:55/q02/p13 Follow Quranic guidance to the good of this life and the next, excluding stories and similar material. — Al-Alusi says the comparative is literal on this reading. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 39:55/q02/p14 Practice the better moral teachings, especially pardon, forgiveness, patience, and generous response to wrongs. — Al-Qurtubi reports pardon. Bursevi reports pardon, forgiveness and patience, saying what is best depends on the actor. Al-Biqa'i specifies reconnecting with one who severs ties, giving to one who withholds, and doing good to one who wrongs you. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم); BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 39:55/q02/p15 Follow what God taught the Prophet outside the Qur'an, with Qur'anic revelation as the best. — Al-Qurtubi reports this as one explanation, alongside other interpretations. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 39:55/q02/p16 The best is the accounts of earlier peoples. — Al-Qurtubi reports this among other explanations. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 39:55/q02/p17 Follow revelation that shows how to fulfill servanthood through inward belief and action. — Al-Tabataba'i names remembrance, love, true piety and sincere religion; these revive life, faith, deeds and divine guardianship. He says prior submission already encompasses the narrower legal views, making them repetitive without cause. The Najmiyya interpretation cited by Bursevi describes revealed good as what calls people to God, citing 33:46. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); TABATABAI+]
- 39:55/q02/p18 In worship the highest course is to act as though seeing God. — Al-Biqa'i ranks this above merely remembering that God sees the worshipper. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 39:55/q03 Does 'best' rank parts of the Qur'an, and how does its comparative form work?  [أَحْسَنَ · theology]
turns on: Whether God's speech admits intrinsic differences in excellence, whether virtue reports concern content or reward, and whether the comparative here compares Qur'anic parts or expresses complete goodness.
- 39:55/q03/p1 It does not rank Qur'anic parts; all are good, and 'best' can mean complete goodness. — Al-Tabari says the Qur'an's commands, prohibitions, reports, parables, stories, arguments, promises and warnings are all good. Ibn Atiyya relates goodness to human conduct and its consequences. Al-Ash'ari and others, reported by Bursevi, reject intrinsic ranking because all is God's speech and ranking implies inferiority. Ibn Ashur calls ahsan a superlative of complete goodness and compares Joseph preferring prison in 12:33. Kuran Yolu follows him and rejects a translation implying some passages are better. Study Quran and Abu Hayyan compare 39:18. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(أبو الحسن الأشعري); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 39:55/q03/p2 Passages about God's essence, unity and attributes are intrinsically better than passages about other subjects. — Bursevi's preferred view of the verifying scholars ranks al-Ikhlas above al-Masad because the former describes God, whereas the latter's merit is simply being God's speech. Bursevi cites abundant reports of particular chapters' virtues; al-Ghazali's Jawhar al-Qur'an cites Prophetic praise of Yasin, al-Fatiha, the Throne Verse and al-Ikhlas. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الغزالي); BURSEVI(المحققون)+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 39:55/q03/p3 Differences in a passage's reported excellence can concern its reward while God's eternal speech remains equal in merit. — Bursevi offers this reconciliation for those reluctant to rank verses, including Prophetic expressions such as 'best surah.' [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 39:55/q03/p4 An abrogated ruling can be good even when acting on its replacement is better. — Ali ibn Isa uses this to reject an interpretation that treats 'best' simply as abrogating rather than abrogated revelation. [1 notes: TABRISI(علي بن عيسى)]

## 39:55/q04 How is 'best of what was sent down' constructed grammatically?  [أَحْسَنَ مَآ أُنزِلَ · grammar]
- 39:55/q04/p1 'Best' is an adjective annexed to the noun it describes. — Ibn Ashur identifies the annexation of ahsan to ma unzila as this construction. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:55/q05 Which other passages illuminate following the best revelation?  [وَٱتَّبِعُوٓا۟ أَحْسَنَ مَآ أُنزِلَ · links]
turns on: Which cited scriptural parallel supplies the explanatory context.
- 39:55/q05/p1 It parallels those who listen and follow the best in 39:18. — Al-Nasafi explicitly draws this parallel; Abu Hayyan and Study Quran also mention 39:18 while discussing the scope of 'best.' [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 39:55/q05/p2 It is explained by the instruction in 7:145 to take the best of the revealed material. — Ibn al-Jawzi invokes that verse's matching wording. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 39:55/q06 Why say that revelation comes 'from your Lord'?  [مِّن رَّبِّكُم · rhetoric]
- 39:55/q06/p1 Naming the Lord encourages obedience by recalling the Benefactor. — Al-Biqa'i says the explicit repeated name encourages even those continuing in grave wrongs; al-Alusi says mentioning the Lord urges obedience to what He sent down. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:55/q07 Why repeat 'before the punishment comes' with its preposition?  [مِن قَبْلِ أَن يَأْتِيَكُمُ ٱلْعَذَابُ · rhetoric]
- 39:55/q07/p1 The repetition and retained preposition express God's gentleness to defenseless people. — Al-Biqa'i explains the repeated warning and preposition in these terms. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 39:55/q08 What punishment is threatened?  [ٱلْعَذَابُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the threatened event is a general chastisement, historical defeat, death, or both worldly and resurrection punishment.
- 39:55/q08/p1 God's affliction and chastisement are threatened without a narrower identification. — Bursevi describes the coming punishment in these general terms. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 39:55/q08/p2 It is worldly defeat of the polytheists by Muslims. — Kuran Yolu attributes this to most exegetes. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 39:55/q08/p3 It includes both previously mentioned worldly and afterlife punishment. — Ibn Ashur identifies the worldly instance as punishment by the sword at Badr and the later one as punishment at resurrection; both arrive without warning. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:55/q08/p4 It may be death, which opens onto and accompanies afterlife punishment. — Bursevi gives this possibility because death initiates the subsequent punishment. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 39:55/q09 What does 'suddenly' mean and add to the warning?  [بَغْتَةًۭ · meaning]
- 39:55/q09/p1 Punishment arrives unexpectedly, without an appointment or forewarning, intensifying its force and urging action now. — Al-Zajjaj, Jishumi and Ibn Atiyya gloss baghtatan as sudden surprise; al-Raghib, cited by Bursevi, says it comes from an unanticipated direction. Al-Tabari says it overtakes the unaware; al-Razi says the description threatens and frightens; al-Biqa'i and Abu Hayyan say surprise makes it harder to bear. Al-Baydawi says one should act while recovery is possible; al-Alusi says there is no chance to avert it. Muqatil, al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, Elmalili and Ibn Ashur also join following revelation to this sudden deadline. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:55/q10 What is the grammatical function of 'suddenly'?  [بَغْتَةًۭ · grammar]
- 39:55/q10/p1 It is accusative as a circumstantial expression (hal). — Jishumi gives the grammatical analysis. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 39:55/q11 What does 'while you do not perceive' imply?  [وَأَنتُمْ لَا تَشْعُرُونَ · meaning]
- 39:55/q11/p1 Heedlessness prevents people from sensing punishment's approach and leaves no time to prepare or make amends. — Al-Nasafi describes extreme inattention as acting as if nothing is feared; al-Biqa'i says neither arrival nor approach is perceived, contrary to settled expectations. Bursevi says no chance remains for amends; al-Alusi says no countermeasure is possible. Al-Tabrisi, al-Baydawi and Ibn Kathir also stress the unknown time. Al-Biqa'i adds that imagining regret in advance would allow preparation and avoiding its causes. [8 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:55/q12 What is the derivation of 'you perceive'?  [تَشْعُرُونَ · meaning]
- 39:55/q12/p1 It derives from shi'ar. — Ibn Atiyya states the derivation without further elaboration. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 39:55/q13 What reports explain the revelation of this passage?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ about those concerned and whether the Wahshi report covers three verses or verses 53–59.
- 39:55/q13/p1 After the Prophet reached Medina, verses 39:53–55 were revealed about persecuted Muslims who had apostatized and despaired of repentance. — Umar says they and others believed God would accept neither repentance nor expiation. In Ibn Hisham and Ibn Kathir, Umar copied the revealed passage and sent it to Hisham ibn al-As; Hisham repeatedly read it at Dhu Tuwa, prayed for understanding, recognized it concerned his group's self-talk, and went to join the Prophet in Medina. [4 notes: IBNHISHAM(عمر,عمر بن الخطاب,هشام بن العاصي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عمر)]
- 39:55/q13/p2 Three verses beginning with 'My servants who transgressed' were revealed in Medina about Wahshi and his companions. — Ibn Ishaq transmits this from Ata ibn Yasar in al-Tabari; al-Durr also reports Ata. Study Quran says many commentators connect verses 53–55 with Wahshi, who gained freedom by killing Hamzah at Uhud. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(عطاء بن يسار); DURR-FULL(عطاء بن يسار); STUDYQURAN(Many commentators)]
- 39:55/q13/p3 Verses 39:53–59 were all revealed concerning Wahshi. — Study Quran attributes this wider span to other commentators. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Other commentators)]

## 39:55/q14 How does this verse fit the surrounding call to repentance?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes trace both the immediate link to repentance in 39:53–54 and the later consequences of accepting or rejecting that call.
- 39:55/q14/p1 It elaborates the preceding call to turn to God, joining hope and the ban on despair to prompt repentance, submission and action. — Ibn Ashur reads the conjunction and imperative as pressing prompt action after 39:53; Islahi says verse 55 develops 39:54. Al-Tabataba'i calls the seven verses one discourse: the ban on despair prepares the later commands, so the first verse is no isolated promise. He describes the whole passage as calling to Islam and revelation, warning against arrogance and regret, and showing mercy. Islahi urges turning to the forgiving God rather than despair or seek deities and intercessors. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:55/q14/p2 The command and warning prepare the later contrast between believers' salvation and disbelievers' loss and regret. — Study Quran connects verses 54–67 to the final contrast in the hereafter. Islahi says idolaters' reliance on polytheism and intercession hastens ruin; they must turn sincerely and follow the Book before later regret and wishes to believe become useless. Al-Tabataba'i likewise notes the contrast and warning against excess and arrogance. [4 notes: STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]

## 39:55/q15 What practical urgency does the warning teach?  [* · interpretation]
- 39:55/q15/p1 Submit, repent and put revelation into practice before punishment removes the chance and regret becomes useless. — Muqatil and al-Tabari urge obedience before surprise; Ibn Kathir and Elmalili urge following the Qur'an now. Al-Baydawi stresses remaining time to recover, while al-Biqa'i says anticipated regrets would help one avoid their causes. Al-Tabataba'i says people delay what they regard lightly, and, citing 8:24, that the warning especially fits reform of the heart beyond outward bodily acts. Islahi urges belief before seizure, and says the Qur'an's qualities warrant wholehearted acceptance lest denial bring unimaginable punishment. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
