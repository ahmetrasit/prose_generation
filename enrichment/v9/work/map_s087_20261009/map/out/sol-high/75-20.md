# 75:20: 11 questions

## 75:20/q01 What does kallā reject or affirm?  [كَلَّا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether kallā resumes a rebuke of worldly preference, addresses the Prophet's haste, rejects earlier denial or excuses, or functions as an affirmative particle.
- 75:20/q01/p1 It rebukes attachment to the immediate world and neglect of the Hereafter. — Jishumi calls it a deterrent against worldly love and desire. Bursevi says it summons people to remember death, which severs worldly immediacy. Baydawi also frames it as a rebuke of humanity deceived by present life. Abu Hayyan reports Zamakhshari's identification of kallā as a rebuke. [4 notes: JISHUMI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 75:20/q01/p2 It rebukes the Prophet's haste in receiving revelation. — Al-Kashshaf urges patience and deliberation; Razi reports that account. Nasafi and Qashani likewise connect the rebuke to preceding haste. Alusi says God's loving guidance urges the Messenger to wait, and Elmalili allows this reading. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 75:20/q01/p3 It rebukes denial of resurrection. — Ibn Jarir says love of the immediate world led deniers to claim resurrection would not happen. Nasafi and Tabatabai link the denial to the supposition at 75:3; Kuranyolu says the denial springs from worldly attachment. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 75:20/q01/p4 It rejects people's excuses for knowingly doing wrong. — Biqai says people know their ugly deeds despite subsequent excuses; he invokes 75:14–19, the recording of deeds and the Quran's witness. Al-Tibi connects the rejection to 75:15 and their continuing to sin while expecting no judgment. Ibn Ashur says the excuses are false because worldly desire displaces the Hereafter. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 75:20/q01/p5 It says Abu Jahl will not believe the Quran's explanation. — Ibn Abbas through Ata supplies this identification; Ibn Jawzi reports Ata and Qurtubi reports Ibn Abbas. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 75:20/q01/p6 It says the Meccan unbelievers do not pray or purify themselves. — Wahidi transmits Muqatil's 'do not pray or give alms'; Qurtubi reports the alternative as 'do not pray or purify themselves.' [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 75:20/q01/p7 It means 'indeed' or 'truly,' affirming that people love the present life. — Jishumi ties the assertion to failure to reflect; Razi reports other commentators explaining that people work for this world and turn away from the next. Elmalili also allows 'indeed.' [3 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(سائر المفسرين); ELMALILI]

## 75:20/q02 How are tuḥibbūna and the following verb read?  [تُحِبُّونَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the imperfect verbs use second-person tāʾ or third-person yāʾ; the sources differ in some attributions to readers.
- 75:20/q02/p1 Both verbs are read with second-person tāʾ: 'you love' and 'you leave.' — Farra reports this from Ali and notes the competing yāʾ. Farisi attributes it to Ibn Kathir and Abu Amr; Thalabi, Baghawi, Tabrisi, Qurtubi, and Nasafi associate it with Medinan and Kufan readers. Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, and Ibn Ashur call it the majority reading; Ibn Atiyya instead places Ibn Kathir and Abu Amr among yāʾ readers. Durr reports it from Mujahid and Asim. Abu Ubayd selects it. Qurtubi and Ibn Ashur say direct confrontation intensifies blame. Farra compares shifts of address at 10:22; Abu Ali explains an implied 'tell them.' Alusi notes the shift draws the listener into the reproach. [26 notes: FARRA(علي بن أبي طالب); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي,الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المدينة والكوفة، والآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); TABRISI(أبو علي,أهل المدينة والكوفة); RAZI-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي,الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(مدني وكوفي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; DURR-FULL(عاصم,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد)+]
- 75:20/q02/p2 Both verbs are read with third-person yāʾ: 'they love' and 'they leave.' — Farra calls this the reading of many, while Farisi calls it that of other readers. Ibn Khalawayh, Abu Ali, Abu Hatim, Samin, and Ibn Ashur connect yāʾ to the earlier generic al-insān; parallels given are 75:13–14 and 70:19–22. Qurtubi cites 76:27; Abu Hatim prefers this reading. Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, Samin, Alusi and Ibn Jawzi attribute it to Ibn Kathir and Abu Amr, contrary to Farisi's attribution. Ibn Ashur adds Ibn Amir and Yaqub; Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and Alusi add Hasan, Mujahid, Jahdari and Qatada. Alusi prefers its gentler turning of the Prophet out of direct reproach. The Imp reading changes tu- to yu-; Study Quran calls it a reading of some schools. [21 notes: FARRA(علي بن أبي طالب); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المدينة والكوفة، والآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); TABRISI(أبو علي,أهل المدينة والكوفة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); RAZI-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي,الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد والحسن وقتادة والجحدري وابن كثير وأبو عمرو); SAMIN-DURR(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن كثير وابن عامر وأبو عمرو ويعقوب); QIRAAT-ER(Imp); STUDYQURAN(some other schools of recitation); ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو ومجاهد والحسن وقتادة والجحدري)+; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حاتم)+]

## 75:20/q03 Whom does the address or implied subject identify?  [تُحِبُّونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural is a direct rebuke of Quraysh or a general statement about human nature, with the preceding address to the Prophet still in view.
- 75:20/q03/p1 It addresses the Meccan or Quraysh unbelievers and deniers. — Muqatil says they choose worldly life over an afterlife they do not seek. Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Samin identify the Meccan or Quraysh audience; Ibn Atiyya says their desires defeated reflection on the law. Abu Hayyan says the discourse returns to the earlier denier after addressing the Prophet. Ibn Ashur says the polytheists are admonished more forcefully by the shift to direct address. Islahi says the discourse resumes addressing deniers after the aside. [10 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 75:20/q03/p2 It addresses or describes humanity generally. — Abu Ali connects third-person wording to collective al-insān, comparing 70:19–22; Ibn Khalawayh cites 75:13, and Ibn Ashur cites 75:14. Kashshaf and Nasafi describe Adam's children as hasty; Qashani says even the preceding addressee shares this human tendency. Tabatabai says the plural address concerns people generally rather than continuing the address to the Prophet. Ibn Ashur calls the audience generic and unspecified; Study Quran notes the move from singular Prophet to plural humanity. Durr's Qatada says most people choose the immediate world, apart from those God protects. [18 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي,صاحب الكشاف); NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; SAMIN-DURR; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن كثير وابن عامر وأبو عمرو ويعقوب); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الزمخشري); STUDYQURAN]
- 75:20/q03/p3 The rebuke can include the Prophet as a person sharing humanity's tendency to haste. — Qashani says the preceding addressee shares the restless soul's natural condition. Alusi says even the Prophet should resist the human impulse to hurry. [2 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 75:20/q04 What does al-ʿājilah denote?  [ٱلْعَاجِلَةَ · meaning]
- 75:20/q04/p1 The immediate, fleeting worldly life or present world. — Nahhas offers life at hand or worldly life. Wahidi refers to 17:18 for the word; Qurtubi, Baydawi, Bursevi, Elmalili, Tabatabai and al-Qummi identify this world or its life. Bursevi stresses its swift passing. The notes of Asad and Study Quran call it fleeting or ephemeral. [10 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(القمي); ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(some other schools of recitation)]

## 75:20/q05 What do people love and choose over the Hereafter?  [تُحِبُّونَ ٱلْعَاجِلَةَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the contrast concerns worldly life broadly, the rewards of each life, or action directed to each life.
- 75:20/q05/p1 They prefer present worldly life and its pleasures to the Hereafter, neglecting or abandoning the latter. — Muqatil says Meccan unbelievers do not seek the Hereafter. Thalabi and Qurtubi cite the parallel at 76:27; Muqatil also points to that warning. Wahidi says they work for this world and abandon work for the next. Baghawi says both readings convey this preference. Ibn Kathir says it underlies denial of resurrection and opposition to revelation. Durr reports Ibn Masud that worldly luster was displayed while the Hereafter was hidden. Biqai likens the two lives to rival co-wives: love of one distances a person from the other and blinds and deafens. Ibn Ashur says blame lies in combining preference for the world with abandonment of the Hereafter. [25 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المدينة والكوفة، والآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:20/q05/p2 They love this world's reward and abandon reward in the next. — Mawardi transmits Muqatil's specific reward-based explanation. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل)]
- 75:20/q05/p3 They love worldly action and abandon action for the Hereafter. — Mawardi reports this as another view, locating the contrast in deeds rather than rewards. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 75:20/q06 Why do people prefer the immediate life?  [تُحِبُّونَ ٱلْعَاجِلَةَ · interpretation]
turns on: The explanations emphasize innate haste, faulty judgment, deliberate desire despite evidence, or the unequal visibility of the two lives.
- 75:20/q06/p1 Human beings are disposed to haste and rush toward what is present. — Kashshaf, Nasafi, Qashani, Itqan, Alusi and Samarrai connect worldly love to human haste. Samarrai cites 21:37, the human created in haste; Qashani emphasizes the restless soul. Alusi also mentions delayed repentance, false hope and indulgence in pleasures. Alusi says even the Prophet should not follow the impulse. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الألوسي,الزمخشري)]
- 75:20/q06/p2 Ignorance, poor judgment and failure to reflect let worldly desire govern the choice. — Jishumi says they choose perishing goods over pure lasting reward; their choice is astonishing. Tabrisi says they neglect Quranic guidance through ignorance and poor choice. Ibn Atiyya says desire overcame Quraysh and their reflection on the law. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 75:20/q06/p3 They knowingly follow worldly desire despite evidence, conscience or their own excuses. — Biqai says they eagerly pursue immediate life and knowingly do wrong; 75:14–19 establishes self-witness and the Quran's clear testimony. Islahi says conscience gives strong testimony to judgment, but deniers will not forgo luxuries and present pleasure. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 75:20/q06/p4 The visible world's luster attracts people while the Hereafter is hidden. — Ibn Masud, reported by Durr, says worldly good was given to people and the Hereafter was concealed. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 75:20/q07 What moral or theological point follows from this preference?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether censure falls on worldly pleasure itself or on preference that abandons the Hereafter; Ibn Ashur also draws a doctrinal inference from 'love'.
- 75:20/q07/p1 One must choose the Hereafter over the perishing world; worldly love that abandons it is blameworthy. — Jishumi explicitly calls that choice obligatory and contrasts perishing goods with lasting pure reward. Junayd says love, pursuit and turning toward the world cost one's share of the Hereafter. Biqai says attachment to one life moves conduct away from the other. Durr's Qatada exempts those God has mercifully protected. [4 notes: JISHUMI; SULAMI(الجنيد); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 75:20/q07/p2 Worldly enjoyment itself is permissible if joined to obedience and care for the Hereafter. — Ibn Ashur cites 28:77: blame attaches to preferring desires while forsaking the Hereafter. Kuranyolu cites 7:31–33: blessings were created for people, and the censure is for abandoning the afterlife because of love for them. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 75:20/q07/p3 The verb 'love' illustrates acquisition (kasb) as inclination toward doing or refraining. — Ibn Ashur sees the wording as an indication of al-Ashari's account of kasb. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 75:20/q08 How does the wording make its rebuke effective?  [كَلَّا بَلْ تُحِبُّونَ ٱلْعَاجِلَةَ · rhetoric]
- 75:20/q08/p1 The direct second-person address confronts listeners more forcefully. — Qurtubi says it faces unbelievers with blame; Ibn Ashur calls the move from absent to direct address a stronger admonition. Alusi says it includes the listener, while third person gently removes the Prophet from direct reproach. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو ومجاهد والحسن وقتادة والجحدري)]
- 75:20/q08/p2 The paired verses use iḥtibāk, each stated act implying its unstated opposite. — Biqai says loving the present implies hating the Hereafter, and leaving the Hereafter implies turning to the present. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 75:20/q08/p3 Reproach is made more severe by exposing the audience's resistance to admonition. — Alusi invokes the proverb that one reproaches a hide only while its surface remains sound: worldly love has made admonition ineffective. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 75:20/q09 How does this verse connect to the preceding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Where the resumed discourse begins, whether the Prophet's haste supplies an analogy, and whether the interruption fits the surrounding argument.
- 75:20/q09/p1 It resumes the main discourse about deniers after the intervening instruction to the Prophet. — Kashshaf describes a move from recitation instruction back to rebuking worldly love. Biqai says the Prophet's formerly hurried recitation had been corrected by 75:16–19 and he returned to his tranquil nature. Bursevi says kallā resumes the earlier discourse. Itqan, Islahi and Kuranyolu likewise call the instruction parenthetical or an aside. Tabatabai says the instruction is unrelated to the verses on either side. Alusi rejects the claim that the tongue instruction is irrelevant and proves alteration, saying scholars answered it. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ITQAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 75:20/q09/p2 It extends the theme of haste in receiving recitation to humanity's haste for worldly life. — Kashshaf and Razi tie the Prophet's haste to the human tendency to rush. Alusi argues that if haste was forbidden even for receiving healing revelation, haste toward ruin deserves stronger blame; Samarrai says both passages prefer immediacy. Alusi says third-person wording gently turns the Prophet out of direct reproach. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الزمخشري)]
- 75:20/q09/p3 It resumes and exposes the denial and excuses already discussed at 75:3–15. — Jishumi and Alusi connect worldly love with the desire to sin ahead at 75:5; Alusi says intervening 75:16–19 delays the explicit charge and intensifies it. Biqai recalls recorded deeds, self-witness and Quranic testimony at 75:14 and 75:19. Al-Tibi and Ibn Ashur connect kallā bal to false excuses at 75:15. Tabatabai says the transition replaces the supposition at 75:3 with the real motive, paralleling 75:5. Samarrai says this love accounts for denial and turning away. [8 notes: JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:20/q09/p4 It contrasts the Prophet's reproaching soul, which hastens to good, with deniers' souls, which hasten toward the world. — Biqai says the Prophet's soul urges prompt obedience while theirs incites attachment to what passes and abandonment of lasting life. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 75:20/q09/p5 It directly resumes a recitation interrupted when Gabriel corrected the Prophet's haste. — Al-Tibi says Gabriel inspired the instruction as the Prophet hurried ahead while hearing 75:15; revelation resumed at 75:20 like a teacher pausing a pupil. Alusi calls this interruption account far-fetched and unsupported by the sound majority report that 75:16–19 addressed the Prophet. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 75:20/q09/p6 Abu Hayyan's proposed contrast between the denier and the Prophet explains the placement of the tongue instruction. — Abu Hayyan says the discourse returns from addressing the Prophet to the earlier denier. Alusi objects that the tongue instruction occurs in the middle of the denier discussion, before that discussion is complete. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 75:20/q09/p7 The tongue instruction is irrelevant to this context and indicates textual alteration. — Alusi attributes this contention to some heretics and early extremist Rafidis, and says scholars answered it. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:20/q10 What wider links does the verse make within the sūra and beyond it?  [* · links]
- 75:20/q10/p1 The verse parallels the warning in 76:27 about loving the present and leaving the Hereafter. — Muqatil, Thalabi, Qurtubi and Study Quran draw this verbal parallel. [4 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 75:20/q10/p2 It leads to the death scene and the contrasting outcomes of present choices. — Alusi says the warning is reinforced at 75:26 by death and the good or evil ends of those preferences. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 75:20/q10/p3 It joins the sūra's recurring pattern of haste, self-reproach and opposing pairs. — Samarrai connects haste to 75:5, the tongue instruction, and present-life preference; says the soul reproaches both rash acts and omissions; identifies the two groups who work for this world or the Hereafter as fulfilling the opening oath; and lists paired motifs: resurrection/reproaching soul, sun/moon, deeds advanced/delayed, self-knowledge/excuses, and present/afterlife. [6 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:20/q10/p4 The preceding claim that God encompasses and counts all things supports that deniers' deeds are preserved. — Biqai cites 72:27–28 for divine encompassing and counting, connecting it to the recording of deeds before this verse. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 75:20/q10/p5 A later change in address is apt because the plural 'you love' here has a generic, unspecified audience. — Ibn Ashur explains the subsequent shift away from the plural audience in these terms. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 75:20/q11 What inward significance is drawn from the verse and its context?  [* · inward]
- 75:20/q11/p1 Love of worldly sensual pleasure causes neglect of the Hereafter's blessing of obscurity. — Bursevi reports this reading from al-Tawilat al-Najmiyya. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 75:20/q11/p2 The Prophet's relation to the Quran spans outward obedience and inward realities beyond plain speech. — Bursevi says plain outward language cannot contain the inward secrets, while the Prophet's comprehensive station joins both realms. He follows the Quran outwardly in the created dominion and inwardly in the unseen; Bursevi prays believers do so in every age. [2 notes: BURSEVI]
