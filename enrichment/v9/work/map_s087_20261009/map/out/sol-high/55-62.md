# 55:62: 13 questions

## 55:62/q01 What does مِن دُونِهِمَا mean here?  [مِن دُونِهِمَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether dūn marks lesser rank, addition, proximity, or being before the first pair; comparisons of the garden descriptions inform these readings.
- 55:62/q01/p1 Below the first pair in rank, degree, merit, or standing. — Ibn ʿAbbās says lower in degree or rank (al-Naḥḥās, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Biqāʿī). Ibn Zayd and most commentators say lower in merit (al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān). Al-Samīn calls this the apparent sense; Ibn Kathīr says the wording and earlier mention of the first pair indicate it. Al-Biqāʿī says below both spatially and in rank. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says lower in grace and honor but similar in blessings. Al-Rāzī reports al-Zamakhsharī's comparison of foliage, fountains, fruit, and furnishings at 55:48, 50, 52, 54, 68, 76. Elmalılı cites the first pair's more complete branching qualities and highest iḥsān. Kur'an Yolu notes that the sense depends on dūn and the reports; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Kur'an Yolu regard rank deductions as uncertain. [41 notes: NAHHAS(ابن زيد,ابن عباس); THALABI(أبو معاذ الفضل بن يحيى,ابن زيد,ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أكثر المفسرين,ابن جريج ومقاتل,ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد); TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد ومقاتل,ابن عباس,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد والأكثرون); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; QURTUBI-FULL+; TABATABAI+; RAZI-FULL-]
- 55:62/q01/p2 Besides, other than, or beyond the first two: two further gardens are introduced. — Al-Zajjāj and the commentators who say 'two further gardens' take the construction as an addition. Abū Muʿādh says 'other than them' is intended because the gardens are below the first pair. Al-Qāshānī compares 'besides God' in 21:98. Elmalılı renders the pair beyond the preceding one; Iṣlāḥī allows 'besides' and 'in contrast with'; Kur'an Yolu allows two more gardens beyond them. Al-Rāzī describes the further pair as a reward and increase, citing 10:26. [14 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI(أبو معاذ الفضل بن يحيى); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; QASHANI; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 55:62/q01/p3 In a nearer or lower physical place relative to the first gardens or their beneficiaries. — Al-Māwardī allows nearness to the first pair. Al-Ṭabrisī defines dūn as a locative term: one place is nearer than another more distant place; nearby gardens allow joyful movement by the palace. Al-Rāzī reports a view in which dwellers look down onto two gardens from above, like the chambers of 39:20. Al-Qāshānī says nearby beyond them, not in front of their occupants. Ibn Barrajān makes 'nearer' relative to human beings and identifies an intermediate realm. A report from Ibn ʿAbbās makes the latter gardens nearer their beneficiaries; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports an alternative nearness to the first two. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(ابن برجان); TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 55:62/q01/p4 In front of or before the first pair; on one version, nearer the Throne. — Al-Kisāʾī cites poetic usage for 'in front/before' (al-Thaʿlabī) and al-Wāḥidī points to discussion at 2:23. Al-Baghawī ties this to al-Ḍaḥḥāk's assignment of gold and silver to the first pair and ruby to the latter pair. Al-Tirmidhī al-Ḥakīm says the latter pair is in front of the first, closer to the Throne. [4 notes: THALABI(الكسائي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكسائي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبد الله الترمذي الحكيم)]
- 55:62/q01/p5 Further on than the first pair, with the later gardens described more favorably. — Elmalılı reports this alternative based on the later gardens' more praiseworthy qualities. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 55:62/q02 Who receives the two gardens mentioned here?  [جَنَّتَانِ · referent]
turns on: Whether all four are assigned to the same God-fearers, distributed among ranks, or associated with other followers or rescued people.
- 55:62/q02/p1 The people of the right or followers receive the latter pair; the foremost or brought-near receive the first pair. — Ibn Zayd, Ibn Jurayj, al-Ḥasan, Abū Mūsā, and others give this division. Muqātil's report in al-Wāḥidī also assigns the later pair to the people of the right and their followers. Al-Māwardī says the first golden pair belongs to the brought-near and the latter silver pair to the right; al-Qurṭubī, citing Saʿīd b. Jubayr from Ibn ʿAbbās and Abū Mūsā, relates the division to 55:46 and 55:64, then endorses it: all four recipients fear God, but to different degrees. Ibn Jurayj cites the second description of fruit at 55:68. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī identifies the latter recipients as believers below the sincere God-fearing; Iṣlāḥī notes the sūrah distinguishes the two groups. [32 notes: NAHHAS(ابن زيد); THALABI(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد,الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن جريج ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو موسى الأشعري,ابن جريج); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو موسى الأشعري,ابن زيد,الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج,ابن زيد,الماوردي,سعيد بن جبير عن ابن عباس وأبو موسى الأشعري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد والأكثرون,الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو موسى,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو موسى,ابن زيد والأكثرون,الحسن); ELMALILI(Hasan,İbn Zeyd); TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QURTUBI-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 55:62/q02/p2 The same people who fear standing before their Lord receive all four gardens. — Al-Zajjāj says the person who fears the Lord's station has two gardens in addition to the first two. Ibn ʿAbbās, as reported by al-Māwardī and by al-Rummānī in Ibn ʿAṭiyya, assigns all four to whoever fears their Lord (55:46). Al-Ṭabrisī likewise adds two besides those promised to such people. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the alternative that nearness to the first gardens means the later pair also belongs to their inhabitants. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 55:62/q02/p3 Descendants or lower-ranked followers joined to the first pair's occupants receive them. — Al-Māwardī suggests followers of lesser station and assigns one garden to houris and another to immortal youths. Al-Rāzī proposes descendants and followers whom God joins to the first pair, whose occupants may invite whomever they wish. Al-Ālūsī says no supporting transmitted evidence for the descendant view was found. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]
- 55:62/q02/p4 The latter gardens house a rescued group below God's close servants. — Imam al-Ṣādiq cites 55:62, with gardens and fires at differing levels, in answer to whether people leaving Hell join God's friends; another account says they do not reside alongside the close servants. Al-Ṭabrisī says that, if the report is sound, these people were not among the best believers. [3 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله,أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); TABRISI]
- 55:62/q02/p5 The gardens belong to the foremost and brought-near in an inward sense. — Al-Qāshānī interprets them as the garden of spirit and the garden of essence, with witnessing of the essence beyond witnessing at the station of spirit. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 55:62/q03 How do the later gardens compare in rank and excellence with the first pair?  [مِن دُونِهِمَا جَنَّتَانِ · interpretation]
turns on: The force of dūn, the sequence of promised gifts, and how the two sets of foliage, springs, fruit, furnishings, and materials compare.
- 55:62/q03/p1 The later pair is lower in rank, beauty, merit, or degree. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Zayd say lower rank or merit in numerous reports; Ibn Zayd, Ibn Jurayj, al-Ḥasan, and Abū Mūsā connect the ranking with the foremost versus people of the right. Ibn ʿAbbās also links the lower pair to hidden comfort in 32:17 (al-Naḥḥās). A prophetic report describes a golden pair and a silver pair, with vessels and contents of the respective metals; al-Ḥākim authenticated a Companion version of Abū Mūsā's statement (al-Ālūsī). Al-Kashshāf's comparisons, cited by al-Rāzī, concern foliage, fountains, fruit, and furnishings at 55:48, 50, 52, 54, 68, 76. Abū Ḥayyān reports arguments from flowing rather than gushing springs, more abundant fruit, brocade-lined couches rather than canopies, finer silk, and ruby-and-coral imagery. Ibn Kathīr says flowing is stronger than gushing (55:50, 66), and that the first pair's earlier mention and the verse's wording signal superiority. Elmalılı says the first pair's branching qualities are fuller and embody the highest iḥsān. Al-Samīn calls this the apparent interpretation; al-Biqāʿī adds a difference in place and standing; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes similar blessings despite lower grace and honor. [62 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS(ابن زيد,ابن عباس); THALABI(أبو معاذ الفضل بن يحيى,ابن زيد,ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد,الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(أكثر المفسرين,ابن جريج ومقاتل,ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو موسى الأشعري,ابن زيد,ابن عباس,رسول الله); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو موسى الأشعري,ابن زيد,الحسن); TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو موسى,ابن زيد ومقاتل,ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج,ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الماوردي,سعيد بن جبير عن ابن عباس وأبو موسى الأشعري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد والأكثرون,الحسن); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو موسى,ابن زيد,ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(أبو موسى,ابن زيد والأكثرون,الحسن); ELMALILI(Hasan,İbn Zeyd); TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; QURTUBI-FULL+; TABATABAI+; RAZI-FULL-]
- 55:62/q03/p2 The later pair is superior to the first pair. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk says the first pair is gold and silver, the later pair ruby and emerald, and the latter is finer. Ibn ʿAbbās is reported to favor the later pair because its springs gush and it is intensely green, against the earlier flowing springs and branches; another report says its descriptions are more laudatory and its gardens nearer beneficiaries. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn report al-Zamakhsharī favoring this reading. Al-Rāzī challenges the lower-rank argument: successive divine gifts lead recipients to expect each to equal or surpass its predecessor. Elmalılı and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī report the more laudatory-descriptions claim. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(بعضهم); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)+]
- 55:62/q03/p3 The rank comparison is uncertain. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the majority's lower-rank interpretation has nondecisive supporting arguments. Kur'an Yolu repeats that characterization and permits either lower rank or two more gardens. Al-Rāzī explicitly calls the lower-rank reasoning weak in light of expectations raised by successive gifts. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL]

## 55:62/q04 What does جَنَّة mean?  [جَنَّتَانِ · meaning]
- 55:62/q04/p1 A garden or orchard. — Al-Zajjāj gives this Arabic lexical sense. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]

## 55:62/q05 Does the dual جَنَّتَانِ denote individual gardens or categories?  [جَنَّتَانِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the dual counts separate gardens or garden categories.
- 55:62/q05/p1 It denotes two further gardens, bringing the described total to four. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, and Asad describe an added pair. Ibn Jurayj explicitly says four gardens are intended; Ibn Barrajān distributes four between two groups. The prophetic gold-and-silver reports also count four. [8 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن جريج); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(ابن برجان); ASAD-NOTES]
- 55:62/q05/p2 The dual may denote two categories of gardens. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the later mention of two gardens beneath the first pair supports reading the earlier dual as two categories. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 55:62/q06 What are the first and later pairs called?  [جَنَّتَانِ · referent]
- 55:62/q06/p1 The first pair is Eden and Bliss; the later pair is Firdaws and the Garden of Refuge. — Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī transmit this from Muqātil; al-Māwardī adds that each pair includes many gardens. Muqātil also places Firdaws and Refuge below the gardens of the brought-near, truthful, and martyrs. [3 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل)]

## 55:62/q07 What are the pairs made of?  [جَنَّتَانِ · reports]
turns on: Whether the gold-and-silver reports divide the pairs by rank or describe both materials in the first pair.
- 55:62/q07/p1 The first pair is gold and the later pair silver, including their vessels and contents. — Ibn Zayd, Abū Mūsā, and a prophetic report give this contrast. The Prophet's version in al-Baghawī says only the veil of majesty separates people from seeing their Lord in the Garden of Eden. Al-Ālūsī notes that al-Ḥākim authenticated a version transmitted as Abū Mūsā's Companion statement. Ibn Barrajān interprets the gold-and-silver report in his distribution between two groups. [13 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو موسى الأشعري,رسول الله); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو موسى الأشعري); TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو موسى); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو موسى,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL(ابن برجان); ALUSI-FULL(أبو موسى); TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]
- 55:62/q07/p2 The first pair is gold and silver; the later pair is ruby and emerald. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk says ruby and emerald are finer, reversing the material ranking. Al-Kisāʾī's 'before them' interpretation is associated with this view in al-Baghawī. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك)]

## 55:62/q08 How are the two pairs' plants, springs, and furnishings described?  [جَنَّتَانِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether flowing or gushing springs and the distinct plant and furnishing descriptions indicate a superior pair.
- 55:62/q08/p1 The first pair has palms and trees; the later pair has crops and spreading plants. — Ibn ʿAbbās gives this distinction through al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī. Al-Ṭabrisī explains the later gardens' dahmah as blackness produced when a field is thoroughly watered; the same word can name a calamity because of its darkness. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI]
- 55:62/q08/p2 The first pair has flowing springs and richer features; the later pair has gushing springs and lesser features. — Ibn Jurayj associates flowing springs and paired fruits with the first pair, and 55:68's fruit with the second. Al-Rāzī reports al-Zamakhsharī's contrasts at 55:48, 50, 52, 54, 68, 76. Abū Ḥayyān lists more abundant fruit, brocade-lined couches rather than canopies, finer silk, and ruby-and-coral description for the first. Ibn Kathīr argues flowing is stronger than gushing (55:50, 66). [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 55:62/q08/p3 The later pair has gushing springs and intense greenery, favoring it over the earlier flowing springs and branches. — A second interpretation attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās through Ibn ʿAṭiyya uses these details to argue superiority. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 55:62/q08/p4 The later pair has flowing springs. — Al-Ḥasan says these two gardens below the first pair have flowing springs, as transmitted by al-Suyūṭī. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(الحسن)]

## 55:62/q09 Why does the verse introduce a further pair of gardens?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the added pair marks reward, enjoyment of multiple nearby gardens, or a numerical allusion.
- 55:62/q09/p1 It extends the promised reward with a like gift or increase. — Al-Rāzī compares it with the promise of the best reward and an increase at 10:26. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 55:62/q09/p2 Nearby further gardens give the blessed joyful movement and prevent boredom. — Al-Ṭabrisī places them near the palace so their occupants can move between gardens. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 55:62/q09/p3 The four gardens allude to the four seasons. — Ibn Barrajān distributes them between two groups and interprets the gold-and-silver report accordingly. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(ابن برجان)]

## 55:62/q10 How do Paradise's ranks affect spouses and visits?  [* · reports]
- 55:62/q10/p1 Paradise has multiple deed-based ranks; spouses may choose and higher inhabitants may visit lower ones. — Abū ʿAbd Allāh says God lets the higher-ranked spouse choose, but lets the lower-ranked spouse choose if that spouse is better. He says rank reflects deeds (6:165); higher inhabitants may descend to visit, lower ones cannot ascend, and loved ones meet on couches when they wish. [3 notes: TABRISI(أبو عبد الله)]

## 55:62/q11 Where do people who leave Hell stand in relation to God's close servants?  [* · theology]
turns on: How reports apply the verse's lower gardens to those leaving Hell and whether they can join the closest believers.
- 55:62/q11/p1 They do not live among God's close servants; Paradise and Hell have differing levels. — Imam al-Ṣādiq answers Ḥumrān's objection with 55:62 and 11:106, 108; al-ʿAlāʾ b. Sayāba reports a similar answer. Al-Ṭabrisī says that, if sound, the report means these people were not among the best believers. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds that the Imam mentioned an unexplained station between groups and says the Qāʾim will begin with scoffers as retribution. [4 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله,أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); TABRISI]
- 55:62/q11/p2 People released from Hell should not be placed among God's close friends. — Abū ʿAbd Allāh rejects that placement: they were neither unbelievers, who cannot enter Paradise, nor believers, who would not enter Hell. [1 notes: TABRISI(أبو عبد الله)]

## 55:62/q12 What is the verse's inward reading?  [* · inward]
- 55:62/q12/p1 It describes gardens of spirit and essence for the foremost and brought-near. — Al-Qāshānī links the garden of essence to unity of witnessing the essence, beyond witnessing in the station of spirit. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 55:62/q13 How does this verse connect the sūrah's descriptions of Paradise?  [* · links]
- 55:62/q13/p1 It opens a second description, distinguished from the earlier gardens for the brought-near. — Iṣlāḥī says the sūrah distinguishes the traits of Paradise for the muqarrabūn and aṣḥāb al-yamīn; its refrain signals further gardens and favors. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the higher level is presented first and the second explicitly called lower. The Study Quran directs readers to 55:48 to compare the gardens of 55:62–76 with those of 55:48–60. Ibn Kathīr treats the earlier mention as a sign of special concern and superiority. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; STUDYQURAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
