# 94:8: 22 questions

## 94:8/q01 Who is “your Lord” here, and why seek Him?  [رَبِّكَ · referent]
- 94:8/q01/p1 God, the giver of the favors recalled in these two sūras and the sole source of effective help. — Biqāʿī identifies the Lord with the one who nurtured the Prophet through the favors already named, and says all creaturely power comes from Him. Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī say only He can grant a request. [7 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 94:8/q02 What does raghaba mean in this wording?  [فَٱرْغَب · meaning]
turns on: The preposition changes whether desire moves toward or away from its object.
- 94:8/q02/p1 To desire, incline strongly toward, and earnestly seek what is loved. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes the Qurʾānic use for strong inclination outside religious contexts. Ibn ʿĀshūr defines raghbah as seeking attainment of a loved thing. Bursevī says raghaba fīhi or ilayhi is desiring and earnestly seeking. [4 notes: BINTSHATI; IBNASHUR; BURSEVI; SAMIN-DURR]
- 94:8/q02/p2 With ʿan, raghaba means to turn desire away. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 4:127 and ʿĀʾisha's explanation, which supplies an omitted ʿan; Bursevī contrasts raghaba ʿanhu with the positive constructions. [2 notes: IBNASHUR(عائشة أم المؤمنين); BURSEVI]

## 94:8/q03 Why does the verb take ilā here?  [إِلَىٰ فَٱرْغَب · grammar]
- 94:8/q03/p1 It incorporates a sense of turning or approaching the one who possesses the desired thing. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the person sought usually takes the direct object and the desired thing fī; ilā evokes travel toward a destination, with Abraham's words in 37:99 as a comparison. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 94:8/q04 Why is “to your Lord” placed before the verb?  [إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ فَٱرْغَب · rhetoric]
- 94:8/q04/p1 The fronting restricts desire to God alone. — Elmalılı says this makes hope exclusively divine and effort continuous. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls exclusivity fitting for a messenger of the highest rank. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ also reads it as restriction. [4 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI]

## 94:8/q05 What is left unspoken as the object of desire?  [فَٱرْغَب · rhetoric]
- 94:8/q05/p1 The omitted object embraces everything the Prophet may seek. — Ibn ʿĀshūr names spiritual perfection, the spread of religion, and Muslim victory; al-Rāzī similarly includes religious and worldly needs and victory. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 94:8/q06 How does the fāʾ of farghab connect this command to what precedes?  [فَٱرْغَب · grammar]
turns on: Whether the fronted complements supply a conditional sense for the fāʾ.
- 94:8/q06/p1 The fronted complements create a conditional sense to which the fāʾ links the actions. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 39:66, 74:3–5, 83:26, and 10:58; the Prophet's instruction to a prospective fighter to strive in service of his parents illustrates a preposed phrase treated conditionally. He says this removes al-Ṭībī's difficulty. [6 notes: IBNASHUR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL(الطيبي,محمد); IBNASHUR(الطيبي)-]
- 94:8/q06/p2 The meeting of wāw and fāʾ is syntactically puzzling. — Al-Ṭībī, citing al-Sayyid's Amālī or Ibn al-Shajarī, notes that fāʾ ordinarily serves coordination, answer, or a relative clause. Ibn ʿĀshūr disputes the puzzle once restriction is understood as conditional. [2 notes: IBNASHUR(الطيبي); IBNASHUR-FULL(الطيبي); IBNASHUR-]

## 94:8/q07 How is farghab read, and what does the variant command?  [فَٱرْغَب · readings]
turns on: Whether the verb is the simple imperative or the geminated causative imperative.
- 94:8/q07/p1 The majority read an imperative of the simple verb raghiba, directing desire to God. — Abū Ḥayyān and his full version identify the majority reading and its simple triliteral form. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 94:8/q07/p2 A geminated ghayn reading commands the Prophet to make people desire what God has. — Ibn Abī ʿAbla, and in later reports Zayd ibn ʿAlī too, read an opened rāʾ and doubled ghayn with kasra. Sources variously write the variant farghab, farghiba, faraghib, or faraghghib and gloss the goal as God's bounty or reward. [14 notes: KASHSHAF(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(ابن أبي عبلة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(زيد بن علي وابن أبي عبلة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زيد بن علي وابن أبي عبلة); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن علي، ابن أبي عبلة)]

## 94:8/q08 What does the command ask the Prophet or believer to do?  [إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ فَٱرْغَب · interpretation]
turns on: The notes vary in whether they emphasize exclusive desire, intention, supplication, assistance, eschatological hope, or completion of the mission.
- 94:8/q08/p1 Direct all longing, hope, and requests to God alone in every state. — Al-Ṭabarī contrasts the idolaters' appeals to rivals; al-Zajjāj and others stress exclusivity. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī and Ibn ʿAṭiyyah include reliance and seeking only God's bounty; al-Nasafī cites 14:11 or 3:122. Bayḍāwī, Bursevī, Ālūsī and Biqāʿī ground this in God's sole ability to help. Elmalılı urges seeing the Benefactor beyond secondary causes. Qutb says the missionary should be free even of preoccupation with the people served, taking God as provision for the hard road. Study Quran contrasts desire for God with desire for the world; Kur'an Yolu stresses God's approval in work and worship. [38 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); BAGHAWI(الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL(الزجاج,قيل); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI(الطبري,النيسابوري); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭabarī)]
- 94:8/q08/p2 Turn to God with sincere intention and wholehearted worship. — Mujāhid's transmitted glosses say intention as well as desire; al-Thawrī agrees. Ibn Kathīr adds energetic worship with distractions cleared from the mind. [14 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); MAWARDI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR(الثوري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الثوري); DURR(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 94:8/q08/p3 Turn to God in supplication and ask Him for one's needs. — Ibn Masʿūd equates turning to the Lord with asking. Al-Farrāʾ, Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda and al-Ḍaḥḥāk place supplication after prayer; Ibn ʿĀshūr sees the command as requesting continued increase of the blessings after thanking God, citing 14:7. [16 notes: FARRA; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI(ابن مسعود); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود); DURR(ابن مسعود,الضحاك,قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود,الضحاك,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNASHUR]
- 94:8/q08/p4 Seek God's assistance, including victory over enemies and needs of both worlds. — Al-Māwardī first gives general assistance, then victory over enemies; al-Rāzī widens the requests to all religious and worldly needs. Al-Nīshābūrī mentions the good of both worlds. [7 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BINTSHATI(النيسابوري)]
- 94:8/q08/p5 Implore God while fearing Hell and hoping for Paradise. — ʿAṭāʾ's account through Ibn ʿAbbās has humble calling; al-Baghawī and al-Ṭabrisī repeat fear of Fire and hope for Paradise. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI(عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء); TABRISI(عطاء)]
- 94:8/q08/p6 Seek intercession from the Lord after conveying the message. — Al-Qushayrī reports this alternate interpretation. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 94:8/q08/p7 Seek recompense for one's deeds from God alone. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports Muḥammad ʿAbduh's explanation. [1 notes: BINTSHATI(الشيخ محمد عبده)]
- 94:8/q08/p8 Seek God more fervently and prepare for the final journey after completing the mission. — Iṣlāḥī links completion and victory to more energetic worship; Biqāʿī treats aspiration to the better afterlife as a hint of the Prophet's approaching death, compared with Sūrat al-Naṣr. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 94:8/q09 When is the turn to God enjoined?  [فَٱرْغَب · interpretation]
turns on: Which preceding state or task is meant by finishing in 94:7, or whether the command is continuous.
- 94:8/q09/p1 After worldly or personal affairs, turning from distraction to prayer and worship. — Mujāhid speaks of finishing personal affairs and standing for prayer. Ibn Kathīr adds setting aside worldly attachments and worshipping with an unoccupied mind. Al-Thaʿlabī reports emptying the heart for the afterlife. [11 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI(قيل); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 94:8/q09/p2 After an obligatory prayer, turning to supplication, sometimes while still seated. — Ibn ʿAbbās specifies prayer; Ibn Masʿūd's report says bowing and prostration are complete and the request can follow while seated. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Qatāda specify the obligatory prayer. Al-Farrāʾ glosses the preceding exertion as supplication; al-Wāḥidī also reports night prayer after obligatory prayers. [18 notes: FARRA; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود); WAHIDI-BASIT(عبد الله); IBNKATHIR(ابن مسعود); DURR(ابن مسعود,الضحاك,قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود,الضحاك,قتادة,مجاهد)]
- 94:8/q09/p3 After the campaign or jihad against the Arabs has ended, turning more fully to worship. — Ibn Zayd transmits this from his father; al-Ḥasan, Zayd ibn Aslam, and al-Ḍaḥḥāk give related reports. Biqāʿī dates the end of fighting against Arabian unbelievers to eight years after the Hijra. Iṣlāḥī describes obstacles overcome, divine help and the reconquest of Makkah before the mission's completion. [11 notes: TAB(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); IBNKATHIR(زيد بن أسلم والضحاك); IBNKATHIR-FULL(زيد بن أسلم، الضحاك); BIQAI; DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(الحسن); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 94:8/q09/p4 After release from inner confusion and constriction, dedicating oneself to the mission's burdens. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers this specification and cites the relief and elevation in 94:1–4. [1 notes: BINTSHATI+]
- 94:8/q09/p5 In every circumstance, with longing to God as a constant mark. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Qushayrī, al-Baghawī and Biqāʿī make the direction of desire continuous, rather than tied to a single completed task. [7 notes: THALABI; QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); BIQAI; ELMALILI]

## 94:8/q10 What inward orientation does this command call for?  [إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ فَٱرْغَب · inward]
turns on: Whether exclusive longing removes interest in reward, other attachments, or confidence in one's own worship.
- 94:8/q10/p1 Desire God's Essence alone, even while inviting others, without reward or self-interest. — Al-Qāshānī says seeking reward or another motive makes the caller act from self and deviate. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 94:8/q10/p2 Direct desire both in God and toward Him. — Al-Qāsim's formulation is transmitted by al-Sulamī. [1 notes: SULAMI(القاسم)]
- 94:8/q10/p3 Remember the Lord once free of everything besides Him. — Al-Thaʿlabī transmits Jaʿfar's explanation. [1 notes: THALABI(جعفر)]
- 94:8/q10/p4 After worship, turn from confidence in the act, fear its rejection, and seek forgiveness in shame. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports this alternative, which makes desire for God follow self-criticism of worship. [1 notes: THALABI(قيل)]

## 94:8/q11 What does the verse imply about recreation and continuous work?  [فَٱرْغَب · law]
turns on: Whether the command excludes lawful recreation and requires uninterrupted work.
- 94:8/q11/p1 Every interval of leisure should be used for obligation and worship. — Al-Farrāʾ reports Shurayḥ rebuking two wrestlers; al-Qurṭubī reports his rebuke of Eid play as contrary to the lawgiver's instruction. [2 notes: FARRA(شريح); QURTUBI(شريح)]
- 94:8/q11/p2 Continuous work is neither obligatory nor desirable for ordinary people; Eid play is allowed. — Ibn al-ʿArabī counters Shurayḥ with the Prophet watching Abyssinians play with shields and spears in the mosque, and the Prophet permitting two Anṣārī girls to sing at ʿĀʾisha's home on Eid despite Abū Bakr's objection. Al-Qurṭubī says sustained application is disliked for humans. [4 notes: QURTUBI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; QURTUBI(شريح)-; FARRA(شريح)-]

## 94:8/q12 May one ask God while still seated after completing the prayer movements?  [فَٱرْغَب · law]
- 94:8/q12/p1 Yes, after bowing and prostration are complete. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk transmits Ibn Masʿūd's view that even ritual impurity at this point leaves the prayer complete; the person may then make a request while seated. Other reports simply say to turn to God after prayer and tashahhud. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن مسعود); IBNKATHIR(ابن مسعود); DURR(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود)]

## 94:8/q13 What recitation practice is reported at the close of this sūra?  [* · reports]
- 94:8/q13/p1 Pronounce takbīr at the end of each sūra beginning with al-Ḍuḥā. — Al-Thaʿlabī transmits through Ubayy a report that the Prophet instructed this practice. [1 notes: THALABI(النبي)]

## 94:8/q14 What merit is reported for reciting Sūrat al-Sharḥ?  [* · reports]
- 94:8/q14/p1 Its reciter is likened to someone who visited the distressed Prophet and relieved him. — Jishumī transmits a prophetic report; al-Zamakhsharī and al-Bayḍāwī repeat a closing report with this comparison. [4 notes: JISHUMI(زر بن حبيش); KASHSHAF(النبي); BAYDAWI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 94:8/q15 How does this sūra relate to al-Ḍuḥā?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether thematic continuity or joint recitation implies a single sūra.
- 94:8/q15/p1 It follows and completes al-Ḍuḥā's account of divine favor. — Jishumī says it recalls blessings like those in al-Ḍuḥā. Asad says revelation was almost immediate. Iṣlāḥī distinguishes al-Ḍuḥā's favor before and just after prophethood from later blessings as Islam spread, and its promised bright future from predictions already materializing. Elmalılı traces blessing to its Giver before al-Tīn. Qutb sees tenderness and care shared by both; Study Quran distinguishes external needs in 93 and spiritual relief in 94. [7 notes: JISHUMI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN]
- 94:8/q15/p2 The two sūras were treated by some as one unit in prayer. — Tāwūs and ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz recited them together in one obligatory prayer unit without a division or second basmala; Asad cites al-Rāzī, and Study Quran mentions Companions. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(طاوس); ASAD-NOTES(Ta'us ibn Kaysan and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz); STUDYQURAN]
- 94:8/q15/p3 They are separate sūras according to the codex divisions. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the joined practice anomalous and contrary to the community's agreement on the codex. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(طاوس)-; ASAD-NOTES(Ta'us ibn Kaysan and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz)-; STUDYQURAN-]

## 94:8/q16 How does the command complete this sūra's movement?  [* · links]
- 94:8/q16/p1 It moves from recalled relief and promised ease through exertion to seeking God's help and continued blessing. — Biqāʿī says the ending rejoins the opening as effect to cause. Ibn ʿĀshūr recalls removal of grief, elevation, and promised ease amid the mission's burdens; he compares thanks followed by a request for more to 14:7. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ ties exclusive desire to prior relief and promises. Iṣlāḥī moves from inner expansion and hardship with relief to mission completion and disciplined use of success. Study Quran moves from blessings and reassurance to exertion and Godwardness. [7 notes: BIQAI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 94:8/q16/p2 The ending also fits the following sūra and other closing calls to worship. — Biqāʿī notes sequence with what follows. Elmalılı says al-Tīn affirms afterlife and religion. Corpus Coranicum compares the calls at 96:19 and 69:52, 93:11's proclamation of grace, and diction at 68:32, with 95:8 for rabb. [3 notes: BIQAI; ELMALILI; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 94:8/q17 How does the instruction connect with Sūrat al-Naṣr?  [* · links]
- 94:8/q17/p1 Victory and completion of the mission lead to worship, gratitude, and preparation for the end of life. — Biqāʿī takes aspiration to the better hereafter as a sign of approaching death, like Sūrat al-Naṣr. Iṣlāḥī calls 110:1–3 an extended treatment after victory and mass entry into Islam. [3 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 94:8/q18 To whom does the sūra's instruction extend?  [* · interpretation]
- 94:8/q18/p1 It addresses the Prophet first and, through him, every true follower. — Asad states this scope; the Study Quran applies its call to prayer and deeds for God's pleasure generally. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]

## 94:8/q19 What is reported about the sūra's place and order of revelation?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the entire sūra or only verses 5–6 were revealed in Madinah.
- 94:8/q19/p1 Most call it Makkan and place it just after Sūrah 93. — Study Quran reports this majority position; Asad says it was revealed almost immediately after al-Ḍuḥā. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(most commentators); ASAD-NOTES]
- 94:8/q19/p2 Ibn ʿAbbās calls the sūra Madinan. — Study Quran reports this attribution. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(most commentators)]
- 94:8/q19/p3 Some assign only verses 5–6 to Madinah. — Study Quran reports this partial dating. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(most commentators)]

## 94:8/q20 What is reported about the Prophet's worship after his forgiveness?  [* · reports]
- 94:8/q20/p1 He labored in worship to be a grateful servant. — Iṣlāḥī reports that when questioned about such effort despite forgiven sins, the Prophet answered that he should be grateful. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 94:8/q21 What promise concerning the Prophet's community is drawn from the verse?  [* · interpretation]
- 94:8/q21/p1 God will give the Prophet what delights his eyes concerning his community. — Al-Sulamī transmits this from Ibn ʿAṭāʾ. [1 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]

## 94:8/q22 Which Qurʾānic verse is cited to support reliance on God here?  [* · links]
- 94:8/q22/p1 The reliance urged here is connected with 14:11. — Al-Nasafī explicitly connects reliance on God with 14:11. [1 notes: NASAFI]
