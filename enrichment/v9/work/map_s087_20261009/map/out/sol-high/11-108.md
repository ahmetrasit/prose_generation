# 11:108: 19 questions

## 11:108/q01 How does the division introduced by وَأَمَّا present the two groups and their fates?  [وَأَمَّا · links]
- 11:108/q01/p1 It elaborates the gathering by dividing all souls into the wretched and fortunate, then setting out their destinations. — Al-Biqāʿī says the final judgment strengthens the Prophet for his mission; al-Ālūsī cites the successive fa particles and al-Sharīf al-Qayrawānī’s poem for the gathering-to-classification pattern; Kuranyolu links 11:103 and worldly faith and deeds. [7 notes: BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 11:108/q01/p2 The division also signals that felicity and misery were settled beforehand. — Al-Ālūsī infers this from the sequence of fa particles. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 11:108/q02 Who are ٱلَّذِينَ سُعِدُوا۟?  [ٱلَّذِينَ سُعِدُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the label names all believers by their final destiny, those transferred from Hell, or graded states of happiness.
- 11:108/q02/p1 They are the faithful and obedient, especially followers of the messengers, who receive Paradise. — Al-Ṭabrisī names obedience and avoidance of sin; Ibn Kathīr names followers of the messengers; Elmalılı contrasts believers who humble themselves with seekers of worldly life in 11:15–16, 23; Kuranyolu ties destiny to faith and deeds. [7 notes: TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 11:108/q02/p2 They include monotheists who first suffer in Hell and later become fortunate in Paradise. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk via Ibn ʿAbbās says they become happy after earlier misery; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī explains that the same persons can be wretched in Hell and happy in Paradise; his objection is that the verse's two groups are exhaustive, so neither can be reduced to former sinners alone. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(الضحاك); TABATABAI(ابن عباس وجابر بن عبد الله وأبو سعيد الخدري وجماعة من التابعين); TABATABAI-]
- 11:108/q02/p3 The happy have ranks: some remain within Paradise's grades, while the happier ascend to divine proximity. — Bursevī's Najmiyya account grounds the higher stations in knowledge, piety, and love, citing 54:54–55; a hadith likens the people of ʿIlliyyīn to bright stars and names Abū Bakr and ʿUmar. [5 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الكبار)]

## 11:108/q03 What does سُعِدُوا۟ mean?  [سُعِدُوا۟ · meaning]
turns on: Active saʿidū versus passive suʿidū affects whether divine causation is overt.
- 11:108/q03/p1 The passive expression says they were granted or made happy by God. — Al-Ṭabarī notes passive majnūn and maḥbūb; al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, and al-Bayḍāwī give this meaning; al-Ālūsī sees a call to gratitude, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts God-given good with self-caused evil in 24:21. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI(أهل الكوفة، أبو عبيد، أبو حاتم); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الثعلبي); BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وحفص); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 11:108/q03/p2 It denotes felicity, the opposite of wretchedness, with success and ease. — Al-Wāḥidī gives saʿāda, saʿd, saʿīd; al-Ṭabrisī contrasts shaqāʾ and traces the yāʾ of shaqī to wāw; al-Jawharī compares salima; al-Biqāʿī glosses attaining aims and ease. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الجوهري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجوهري); BIQAI-FULL]

## 11:108/q04 How is سُعِدُوا۟ read, and what supports each vocalization?  [سُعِدُوا۟ · readings]
turns on: Whether a transitive saʿada is valid or an augment can drop from asʿada, versus the ordinary intransitive saʿida and parallel shaqū.
- 11:108/q04/p1 With ḍamma on sīn, suʿidū, a passive reading. — Reported for Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, Ḥafṣ, Khalaf, al-Aʿmash and early readers, with lists varying. Support includes Hudhayl and Tamīm transitive saʿadahu, dialectal pairs, masʿūd, or loss of the augment from asʿada. Al-Ṭabarī accepts it; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī defend its transmission. Al-Naḥḥās, Abū ʿAlī, al-Mahdawī and others object that ordinary saʿida is intransitive and masʿūd is inconclusive; poetry on luwāqiḥ and ghāḍī illustrates disputed augment loss. [60 notes: TAB-FULL(جماعة من قراء الكوفة); NAHHAS(الأعمش وحمزة والكسائي,الكسائي,علي بن سليمان); FARISI-HUJJA(حمزة والكسائي وحفص عن عاصم); THALABI(أهل الكوفة، أبو عبيد، أبو حاتم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل الكوفة,الزجاج والفارابي والأزهري,الفراء,الكسائي,المحققون من أهل اللغة,سيبويه والمحققون من أهل اللغة); JISHUMI(حمزة والكسائي وحفص عن عاصم); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود وطلحة بن مصرف وابن وثاب والأعمش,الفراء,حمزة والكسائي وعاصم عن حفص,قيل); TABRISI(أبو علي,أهل الكوفة غير أبي بكر); RAZI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وحفص عن عاصم); QURTUBI-FULL(الأعمش وحفص وحمزة والكسائي,الثعلبي,القشيري أبو نصر عبد الرحيم,المهدوي,النحاس); BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وحفص); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو نصر عبد الرحيم القشيري,ابن مسعود,الثعلبي,الجوهري,الفراء,الكسائي,المهدوي,علي بن سليمان); NASHR(حمزة والكسائي وخلف وحفص والباقون); BURSEVI(الكسائي); ALUSI-FULL(الجوهري,الفراء,القشيري); IBNASHUR-FULL(حمزة، والكسائي، وحفص عن عاصم، وخلف); TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+; NAHHAS-; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 11:108/q04/p2 With fatḥa on sīn, saʿidū, the active intransitive form. — Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, ʿĀṣim via Abū Bakr, Ibn ʿĀmir and most other readers are listed. Abū ʿAmr argues from the preceding shaqū; Abū ʿUbayd and Abū Ḥātim prefer it. Sibawayh and Abū ʿAlī cite intransitive Arabic usage; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers it. Al-Ṭabarī accepts both. ## 11:108/q05 How does خَٰلِدِينَ work grammatically?  [خَٰلِدِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether Paradise residence follows the stated placement or is already established; whether a circumstantial qualifier can confirm a fixed state.
- 11:108/q05/p1 It is an accusative circumstantial participle qualifying those in Paradise; some call the state anticipatory. — Sībawayh compares participles in 51:15–16 and 52:18 and explains how a locative licenses a circumstantial accusative, with adjectival alternatives for indefinites. Abū Ḥayyān calls it a future state; Bursevī gives an anticipatory analysis; Ibn Jinnī supplies an omitted belief in abiding. [7 notes: SIBAWAYH; JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(ابن جنى); BURSEVI]
- 11:108/q05/p2 It is a confirmatory circumstantial participle describing a settled state, with no anticipatory analysis required. — Al-Samīn contrasts 39:73, where residence follows entry; Bursevī reports the same point. Al-Burhān says the rule requiring changing circumstances applies to non-confirmatory qualifiers only. [3 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم); BURHAN+; SAMIN-DURR+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 11:108/q06 What does خَٰلِدِينَ convey?  [خَٰلِدِينَ · meaning]
- 11:108/q06/p1 It denotes enduring or perpetual residence. — Al-Ṭabrisī distinguishes God as dāʾim rather than khālid; al-Rummānī compares khālid and dāʾim; al-Rāghib traces khulūd from unchanged long continuance to permanent existence, citing 26:129, 2:82, 2:39, and 4:93. [3 notes: TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL(أبو الحسن الرماني); TABATABAI(الراغب)]

## 11:108/q07 What are the ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتُ وَٱلْأَرْضُ whose duration is mentioned?  [ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتُ وَٱلْأَرْضُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the wording is idiom, refers to transformed afterlife creation, or measures a worldly term; objections concern literal perishability, audience familiarity, and the plural heavens.
- 11:108/q07/p1 The phrase is a familiar Arabic idiom for indefinite or everlasting duration. — Ibn Qutayba lists night and day, rising seas, mountains, heaven and earth; al-Ṭabrisī cites ʿAmr ibn Maʿdīkarib and Zuhayr; al-Kashshāf cites Taʿār, Thabīr and stars. Abū Ḥayyān says the idiom works without asking whether those things literally last. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that God declares the worldly heavens perishable (46:3), so their names cannot set eternity; al-Rāzī says the comparison still conveys immense duration. [19 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI(أهل المعاني); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(عمرو بن معد يكرب); RAZI-FULL(قالوا); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(مفسرون); JISHUMI; MUQATIL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 11:108/q07/p2 They are the transformed, enduring heavens and earth of the Hereafter or of Paradise and Hell. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and al-Jubbāʾī read them literally; 14:48 and 39:74 describe a changed earth inherited by Garden dwellers. Al-Kashshāf and Abū Ḥayyān add that support and shade can be called earth and heaven. Ibn ʿAbbās says each Garden has its own and that the heavenly substance returns to Throne-light; al-Ḥasan describes replacement and cleansing. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī defends distinct afterlife heavens with 13:22 and 16:96, but rejects the support-and-shade analogy for reversing the duration relation and failing to explain plural heavens. [26 notes: THALABI(الضحاك); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(الضحاك والجبائي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,السدي); BURSEVI(حضرت شيخ قدس سره); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الآلوسي,الزمخشري,القاضي); STUDYQURAN(al-Bayḍāwī); TABATABAI+; TABATABAI-]
- 11:108/q07/p3 They are the world's heavens and earth, whose term is followed by an added period. — Ibn Qutayba cites 14:48 and 21:104 for their change; Ibn ʿAbbās defines their span from creation to end. Al-Tirmidhī al-Ḥakīm says the worldly term ends with the covenant transaction, followed by unbounded outcome. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects a finite-plus-infinite division as requiring unstated premises. [6 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; TABATABAI(مفسرون); JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL(الترمذي الحكيم أبو عبد الله محمد بن علي)+; TABATABAI-]
- 11:108/q07/p4 The comparison means the Hereafter lasts as long as the world's heavens and earth lasted, without claiming those bodies remain. — Al-Ḥusayn and al-Ḥasan offer this analogy. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that a simile on its face entails termination. [3 notes: THALABI(الحسين); TABRISI(الحسن); TABATABAI(مفسرون); TABATABAI-]
- 11:108/q07/p5 The existing heavens and earth endure in substance though their form changes. — Bursevī attributes this distinction to his shaykh in the Futūḥāt; it reconciles transformation with permanence. [1 notes: BURSEVI(حضرت شيخ قدس سره)]

## 11:108/q08 How is مَا دَامَتِ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتُ وَٱلْأَرْضُ parsed?  [مَا دَامَتِ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتُ وَٱلْأَرْضُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the duration is treated as conventional perpetuity or analyzed as a condition whose converse does not follow.
- 11:108/q08/p1 Mā forms a temporal verbal noun: for the duration that the heavens and earth remain; dāma is complete. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, Bursevī and al-Burhān explain this temporal maṣdariyya; al-Burhān contrasts non-temporal mā and distinguishes relative mā elsewhere. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; BURHAN; ITQAN]
- 11:108/q08/p2 The phrase measures an immense span without logically proving what follows if that condition ends. — Al-Rāzī reasons that denying the condition does not deny its consequent, just as a nonhuman can still be an animal; he also answers a critic of the idiom argument. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(قائل); RAZI-FULL+]

## 11:108/q09 What grammatical scope and force can إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ رَبُّكَ have?  [إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ رَبُّكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether illā excludes actual time or persons, adds something, qualifies a promise, or speaks of kinds of bliss; the ordinary force of exception and the uncut gift are decisive evidence in the notes.
- 11:108/q09/p1 Illā means ‘besides’ or ‘other than,’ a disconnected exception that adds duration or reward. — Al-Farrāʾ prefers it; Ibn Qutayba compares adding time to a promised year in a house. A grammarian cited by al-Ṭabarī argues God does not break promises and the uncut gift confirms addition. Al-Ṭabrisī attributes it to al-Zajjāj, al-Farrāʾ and ʿAlī ibn ʿĪsā; Rāzī rejects departure from the apparent exception; Sibawayh instead paraphrases disconnected illā as ‘but.’ [18 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية); THALABI(الزجاج); MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفراء,سيبويه); TABRISI(الزجاج والفراء وعلي بن عيسى); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الترمذي الحكيم أبو عبد الله محمد بن علي,الزجاج عن أهل المعاني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); TABATABAI(مفسرون); FARRA+; RAZI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 11:108/q09/p2 It can be a hypothetical exception expressing God's continuing power and will, even though He never removes Paradise's people. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes God's unrestricted power from actual exit; Ibn ʿĀshūr treats it as a true condition without enacted consequence. Rāzī objects that exception wording entails an actual willed period. [16 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية); THALABI(الزجاج); JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(ابن قتيبة وابن الأنباري والفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr); TABATABAI+; RAZI-FULL-]
- 11:108/q09/p3 It may attach to the described bliss and punishment rather than to duration. — Al-Zajjāj links the Hell side to the sounds in 11:106 and Paradise to other delights; al-Kashshāf expands this to cold and divine pleasure. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn reject the Paradise application because added gifts do not end residence; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it disrupts parallel syntax. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); THALABI(أهل المعاني); JISHUMI(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); ZAJJAJ+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; TABATABAI-; RAZI-FULL-]
- 11:108/q09/p4 Illā functions like ‘and,’ adding what God wills to the stated duration. — Al-Farrāʾ cites Abū Tharwān’s poetry and 2:150; al-Ṭabrisī says linguists weakened this and Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the poem only proves it under a particular belief about perishability. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the usage is unestablished. [12 notes: THALABI(الفراء); MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; TABATABAI-]
- 11:108/q09/p5 It is an ordinary exception from the time of residence, with mā referring to a period. — Al-Ṭabarī says illā normally marks a real exception. Abū Ḥayyān prefers the apparent syntax, and Ibn ʿĀshūr says temporal mā suits its usual nonrational force; Rāzī rejects moving it outside the stay itself. The temporal exception may mark a starting interval. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; TAB-FULL+]
- 11:108/q09/p6 It is a conventional will-qualified condition attached to a settled promise, without a real withdrawal. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū ʿUbayd and al-Qurṭubī compare 48:27, the promise of secure entry to the Sacred Mosque. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the exception would occur only if God willed it, and the promise shows He will not. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 11:108/q09/p7 It excepts persons from the implied pronoun of ‘in it’ or ‘abiding’; mā can mean ‘who.’ — Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite rational mā, the latter comparing 4:3; al-Ṭabrisī says mā may equal man for those removed from Hell. Al-Wāḥidī objects to treating sinful believers as the wretched, which is why the verse says mā, not man. [5 notes: TABRISI(ابن عباس وجابر بن عبد الله وأبو سعيد الخدري وقتادة والسدي والضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT-]
- 11:108/q09/p8 Illā can mean ‘as,’ yielding ‘as God willed.’ — Al-Farrāʾ cites 4:22 for the idiom; al-Qurṭubī reports the same. [2 notes: THALABI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]

## 11:108/q10 What does the exception إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ رَبُّكَ exclude for Paradise's people?  [إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ رَبُّكَ · interpretation]
turns on: The dispute turns on whether exception changes the start or end of residence, adds duration or quality, states a hypothetical divine prerogative, or leaves its content undisclosed.
- 11:108/q10/p1 The time sinful monotheists spend in Hell before being brought into Paradise. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Ḥasan, Ibn ʿAbbās and others transmit this. Al-Ṭabarī prefers it because illā ordinarily marks a real exception; al-Ṭabrisī selects it. Abū Ḥayyān says late entrants did not begin with first entrants. A sound Anas report describes the Jahannamiyyūn brought out like charcoal; Abū Hurayra’s prophetic report applies both exceptions to them. Al-Samīn objects that Hell departure alone does not explain the blessed side; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects to reducing the exhaustive groups to sinners, while offering a general formulation involving the same people. - 11:108/q10/p2 They receive higher bliss beyond ordinary Paradise: divine pleasure, vision, nearness, or other gifts. — Al-Kashshāf cites divine approval and gifts beyond recompense; al-Bayḍāwī names vision and proximity; al-Nasafī calls them greater than Garden pleasures; Rāzī suggests elevation to the Throne or stations known only to God, citing 9:72. - 11:108/q15/p2 No: the verse already secures Paradise, and there is no sound basis for abrogation. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the claim presumes an end absent from the wording and conflicts with other Meccan permanence statements; al-Ālūsī says decisive proofs of unbelievers' punishment make abrogation unnecessary. ## 11:108/q16 What inward meanings are given to the Garden, its heavens and earth, and the exception?  [ٱلْجَنَّةِ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتُ وَٱلْأَرْضُ إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ رَبُّكَ · inward]

- 11:108/q16/p1 The Garden is the soul's enjoyment of fulfilled desires; higher gardens of heart and spirit disclose God. — Qāshānī pairs the self's deprivation and gratification with later states of heart and spirit, then describes annihilation in divine unity and selfless witnessing. [10 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعض الكبار); QUSHAYRI]
- 11:108/q16/p2 Garden and Fire can signify God's and Satan's authority embodied in bliss and torment, with movement governed by faith and conduct. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī accepts this as an inward meaning but rejects it as the apparent sense of a passage about Resurrection's distinctive terrors. - 11:108/q16/p3 The heavens mean spirits and hearts, while earth means souls and embodied humanity. — Bursevī reports this from the people of inward interpretation. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أهل التأويل)]
- 11:108/q16/p4 The exception can point to a station beyond Paradise's ordinary entry and exit, in which the secret of eternity appears. — Bursevī's oral teaching describes a final disclosure unlike earlier states, likens it to saints' annihilation, and relates the people of beauty and majesty and the perfected knowers across worldly life, barzakh and afterlife. ## 11:108/q17 How does this verse connect the final judgment to the preceding and following passages?  [* · links]
- 11:108/q17/p1 It contrasts God's freedom to punish or relieve in 11:107 with the assured gift to Paradise's people here. — Ibn Kathīr links God's justice, wisdom and will to 21:23; al-Burhān says the paired endings explain gracious rescue and uninterrupted gift; al-Ālūsī notes the Hell passage's warning aim. - 11:108/q17/p2 It completes the sequence of gathering, division, then lasting destinations. — Al-Ālūsī and Kuranyolu relate it to the gathering in 11:103 and two groups' faith and deeds; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī treats the preceding Hell duration and this Garden duration as parallel settled residences. - 11:108/q17/p3 The contrast connects worldly choices to the destinations: seekers of this life meet Fire; humble believers meet Paradise. — Elmalılı cites 11:15–16 and 11:23, while Kuranyolu reads outcomes according to faith and deeds. [4 notes: ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 11:108/q17/p4 The passage's afterlife garden is final reward, unlike preliminary garden or fire in the grave. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 3:169–170 and 23:99–100 for the barzakh's intermediate state and contrasts it with the final accounting and everlasting reward. [4 notes: TABATABAI(مفسرون)]

## 11:108/q18 How do the exception here and the preceding exception for Hell compare?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the paired clauses share a single referent or require different effects because Paradise's gift is explicitly unbroken.
- 11:108/q18/p1 They need different outcomes: no one leaves Paradise once admitted, while the Hell exception may still permit release. — Rāzī invokes consensus that no Paradise entrant later leaves for Hell. Al-Ṭabrisī extends the earlier exception proposals only where compatible with permanent Paradise. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the Paradise gift promises no removal, unlike the Hell clause of unfettered will; he objects to Abu Majliz's separate explanation as breaking contextual unity and treating pardoned persons as wretched. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABRISI]
- 11:108/q18/p2 Both clauses preserve God's sovereignty; the closing phrases show what He actually decrees for each group. — The uncut gift assures Paradise, while ‘He does what He wills’ in 11:107 can include mercy. Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes God's power; Abū Jaʿfar's report points to the different endings. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر)]
- 11:108/q18/p3 The Hell exception can refer to other punishments or to a pause after worldly and grave torment, while Paradise remains assured. — Al-Kashshāf speaks of extreme cold; Abū Jaʿfar al-Ṭūsī's reported view puts Hell during the world's span including graves, with a pause until resurrection. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the latter repeats a strained ‘other than’ analysis. [2 notes: BURHAN(الزمخشري); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر الطوسي); TABATABAI-]
- 11:108/q18/p4 They can concern the same sinful monotheists: release from Hell there and their earlier absence from Paradise here. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī formulates this for the exhaustive final groups; al-Burhān keeps the Hell exception's ordinary sense as deliverance. Abū Majliz instead allows pardon before Hell and a different Paradise interval. [1 notes: BURHAN+]
- 11:108/q18/p5 Abū Majliz takes the first exception as those pardoned before Hell and the second as sinners later entering Paradise. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this disrupts the two verses' unity and that those pardoned through deeds or intercession were happy from the outset. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو مجلز); TABATABAI-]

## 11:108/q19 How does repeated wording in the Paradise clause work grammatically?  [فَفِى ٱلْجَنَّةِ فِيهَا · grammar]
- 11:108/q19/p1 Repetition in a genitive or prepositional construction can confirm the earlier phrase, even when separated. — Al-Burhān cites transmitted instances including pronouns, repeated lammā and repeated annakum; al-Ṣaffār rejects the Kūfan rule against separating an emphatic element. Al-Itqān also notes emphasis by synonyms. [3 notes: BURHAN(الكوفيون); ITQAN]
