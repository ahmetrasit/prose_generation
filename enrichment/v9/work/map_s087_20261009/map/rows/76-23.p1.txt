[ELMALILI:76:23-24/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The doubled “We” stresses that God alone revealed the Qur’an to the Prophet.
[ELMALILI:76:23-24/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Elmalılı describes the Qur’an as guiding humanity to the right path and offering the delight of the pure drink.
[ELMALILI:76:23-24/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The Qur’an came in installments over twenty-three years, not all at once; its gradual development parallels the stages of the first human’s creation.
[ELMALILI:76:23-24/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | This revelation will bring about many things not previously mentioned, and the promised events will certainly occur.
[TABATABAI:76:1-22#5/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The commands to be patient and not obey a sinner or unbeliever occur in a later, self-contained section that may have been revealed in Mecca, while the opening section concerning the Prophet's household was Medinan.
[ASAD-NOTES:76:23#n21/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | The verbal form nazzalna implies that Qur'anic revelation came gradually.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p87/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The Prophet should persevere despite opponents, trust God, and seek patience through prayer and remembrance.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p87/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Disbelievers' real ailment is unwillingness to sacrifice immediate gains for the Hereafter; they fabricate objections despite its certainty, though recreating them is easy for God.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:76:23/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The preceding verses describe the afterlife outcomes people reach according to the value of their worldly beliefs and deeds.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:76:23/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | These verses remind the Prophet of revelation’s value and urge him to remain steadfast in worship and carrying out God’s commands, strengthening his resolve against the deniers.
[QIRAAT-ER:76:23:5#1/r1] QIRAAT-ER · hmz | reports | The hmz canonical reading elides hamza in qurʾān to yield Qurān, simplifying the glottal stop without altering meaning.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p345/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | Three emphasis markers in the revelation verse name sender, message, and recipient; greater emphasis is warranted because Quraysh strongly denied revelation but did not deny God as creator, and revelation, the worship-giving book, is creation's purpose. | mentions 31:25, 39:38, 51:56
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p346/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | He likens the doubled emphasis here to Yā Sīn: the apostles’ stronger rejection after a third messenger is answered with stronger emphasis and the oath ربنا يعلم. | mentions 36:14, 36:16
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p346/r2] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | The phrase عليك suits the following commands addressed to the Prophet; al-Ḥijr instead centers on the Qur’an and has no surrounding address to him. | mentions 15:9, 76:24, 76:25, 76:26
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p347/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | The surah names the revelation Quran only here; using dhikr in al-Hijr answers Quraysh's own label and better fits the preceding verse. | mentions 15:6, 15:9
[STUDYQURAN:76:23/r1] STUDYQURAN · JJ, Ṭs | reports | JJ and Ṭs understand the verse to describe revelation in periodic installments rather than the Quran descending all at once.
