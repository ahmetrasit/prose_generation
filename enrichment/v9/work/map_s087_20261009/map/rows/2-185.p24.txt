[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#2/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj says شهر names the moon itself, called so for its visibility; a cited saying compares it to a fingernail clipping.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ramadan is the generic proper name for this specific month.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#3/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The hamzated word derives from قرأ, 'to gather', because the Quran gathers suras, verses, rulings, and admonitions.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#3/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن كثير | holds | Ibn Kathir reads القرآن without hamza; the author gives two derivations for this form.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#3/r12] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The more evident analysis is نقل: like Warsh, Ibn Kathir transfers the hamza vowel to the preceding consonant and deletes it, a special choice due to frequent use and combining two forms. | mentions 23:1
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#3/r13] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The alternative derives it from قرنت, 'joined two things', on pattern فعال because the Quran joins its suras, verses, rulings, and admonitions.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#3/r14] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | Derivation from قريت الماء في الحوض ('collected water in a basin') is wrong because the two roots are distinct.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#3/r15] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | In أنزل فيه القرآن, القرآن is نائب الفاعل.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#3/r16] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | أنزل فيه القرآن may mean revelation occurred in Ramadan, some specifying its twenty-fourth day; another view takes فيه as concerning Ramadan's status and merit.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | One etymology says Ramadan arrived during the intense summer heat الرمضاء, like Rabi and Jumada named for their seasons.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#3/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Other proposed derivations say Ramadan burns sins away and hearts burn from admonition.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#3/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A blade etymology describes thinning a blade between stones; a sharpened blade is called رميض or مرموض.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#3/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · المفضل | reports | The month was called ناتق in pre-Islamic times; al-Mufaddal's poem uses that name.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#3/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari derives رمضان as a verbal noun from رمض إذا احترق; the author says this requires attestation because فعَلان is not usually a verbal noun of an intransitive verb.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#3/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Another etymology connects the name to pre-autumn rain that cleans dust from earth, analogously cleansing hearts of sins.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#3/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | القرآن was originally the verbal noun of قرأت and later became the name for what lies between the covers.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#3/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · حسان | reports | Hassan's verse about Uthman uses قرآنا as a verbal noun, evidence for القرآن's original مصدر meaning.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#4/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | هدى is an accusative حال from القرآن, governed by أنزل; as a مصدر it may mean ذو هدى, function as هاديا, or intensively be guidance itself.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#4/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | للناس either attaches to هدى if it means هاد, or is an omitted phrase adjectivally qualifying the preceding indefinite noun.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#4/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | هدى cannot predicate omitted هو because بينات is overtly accusative; بينات is a second necessary حال and a specific kind of general guidance, which includes hidden and manifest matters.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#5/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | من الهدى والفرقان qualifies هدى وبينات in the accusative and depends on an omitted phrase: the Quran's guidance and proofs are part of God's guidance and proofs.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#5/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | الفرقان replaces البينات because it adds the sense of distinguishing truth from falsehood and echoes the preceding verse endings.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#5/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · بعضهم | reports | Some say the first هدى denotes the foundations of religion and the second its branches.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#5/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn Atiyya treats the second الهدى as anaphoric to the first, citing the definite repetition pattern, Pharaoh's messenger, and Ibn Abbas's saying about one hardship and two eases. | mentions 73:15, 73:16
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#5/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The author rejects Ibn Atiyya's anaphoric reading: since هدى and بينات are حال, من الهدى cannot modify either or both without circularly qualifying the Quran by a part identical to the whole.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#6/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | من الهدى cannot attach to بينات as a qualifier either: replacing الهدى by منه is invalid; the author therefore takes الهدى and الفرقان generally, with هدى وبينات a subset.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#6/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · السلمي وأبو حيوة | reports | Al-Sulami, Abu Haywa, and others preserve the original kasra of the imperative لام throughout the Quran.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#6/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الفراء | reports | Farra reports that opening the imperative لام is a Sulaym dialect; some restrict it to cases where the following ya is open.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#6/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | من in فمن شهد may be relative or conditional; the conditional parse is more apparent.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#6/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | منكم is a حال of the implicit subject of شهد, governed by an omitted كائنا.

[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#6/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | One parse makes الشهر an adverb: شهد means 'was present', with an omitted object such as the city.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#6/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The preferred direct-object parse estimates دخول الشهر; هلال الشهر is weak because Arabic says شاهدت الهلال, and it would wrongly require everyone seeing the crescent to fast.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#6/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari parses الشهر and the pronoun in فليصمه as adverbs because residents and travelers both witness the month; the author objects that verbs do not govern adverb pronouns without في unless broadened to object-case.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#6/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | فاء may mark the conditional answer or be extra in a predicate after relative من; the لام is imperative.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#6/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الجمهور | holds | The majority read the imperative لام with sukun, analogizing its position after واو or فاء to the connected form كتف.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:185#7/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The definite article in الشهر is anaphoric; a pronoun could replace it, but the noun is expressed overtly to honor the month.
