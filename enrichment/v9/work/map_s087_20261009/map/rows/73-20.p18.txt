[ALUSI-FULL:v29p111/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · مقاتل وابن كيسان | reports | Muqatil and Ibn Kaysan say tahajjud was obligatory in Mecca before the five prayers, then abrogated by them except for voluntary prayer; al-Bukhari and Muslim report this from Jabir.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p111/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · عائشة | reports | In a report through Saʿd ibn Hisham, ʿAʾisha says the Prophet's character was the Qur'an; vigil was made obligatory at the sūra's opening, observed for a year by him and his Companions, then eased at its end and became voluntary.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p111/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · عائشة | reports | A variant report from ʿAʾisha says the obligatory practice lasted eight months.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p111/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · قتادة | reports | Qatada says the obligatory vigil lasted a year or two.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p111/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · بعضهم | reports | Some say the duty remained obligatory while the amount was optional, then was abrogated after ten years; al-Kalbi says a man might pray until dawn for fear of misjudging the interval.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p111/r8] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · غير واحد | reports | Some hold the vigil was voluntary, citing the choice of amounts and 17:79, which calls night prayer a special additional devotion for the Prophet. | mentions 17:79
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p111/r9] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · صاحب الكشف | prefers | Al-Kashf says the voluntary view relies on precedent: obligatory prayers have fixed times rather than timing left to each worshipper, a sound argument.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p112#2/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الشافعي ومالك | reports | The Shāfiʿī-Mālikī proof from “no prayer except with al-Fātiḥa” takes the negation as invalidity; opponents suggest it means no complete prayer.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p112#2/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The response argues that if al-Fātiḥa is part of prayer’s essence, omitting it permits negating prayer itself; even if literal negation is impossible, invalidity is a nearer metaphor than incompleteness, and consensus supports it.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p112#2/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The response further cites consensus, the closeness of an invalid prayer to nonexistence, the continuing duty, and greater caution as reasons to read the Fātiḥa.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p112#2/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الشافعي ومالك | reports | Another argument says omitting al-Fātiḥa without necessity forfeits an agreed extra merit and is therefore religiously blameworthy, based on the report that Muslims’ good and bad judgments count with God.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p112#2/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الشافعي ومالك | reports | A further argument says reading al-Fātiḥa certainly discharges the duty and avoids doubt, while leaving it creates fear of liability; the practical precaution is to read it.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p112/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The phrase that God relented supports the earlier view that night prayer was initially obligatory for the Prophet and the community; many reports support it.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p112/r10] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · ابن جبير وجماعة | reports | Ibn Jubayr and a group hold that some night prayer is unavoidably obligatory, even if only fifty verses are recited.

[ALUSI-FULL:v29p112/r13] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The early pious Muslims kept vigil as consistently as they performed Islam’s obligatory duties, cherishing private intimacy with God.
