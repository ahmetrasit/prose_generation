[IBNASHUR:94:4/r4] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Ibn Ashur suggests the Prophet first grieved over idolatrous society and sought a path of truth in retreat at Hira; revelation clarified the truth and commissioned him to rescue his people.

[IBNASHUR:94:4/r8] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Raising mention is a metaphor for inspiring people to praise him and creating its causes; elevation suggests noble fame beyond ordinary reach, grounded in rare virtues that earned him the title al-Amin.

[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p412/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Raising the Prophet's mention is a metaphor for causing people to remember him well by creating the means of his reputation; he was known for exceptional virtues and called al-Amin.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p412/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | His name's pairing with God's name in the profession of faith is a supreme instance of raised mention; Qur'an 81:19–21 may also refer to Muhammad. | mentions 81:19, 81:20, 81:21
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p412/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | Abu Sa'id al-Khudri transmits a Prophetic interpretation of raised mention; al-Suyuti grades its Ibn Hibban and Abu Ya'la chain fair, while Qadi Iyad cites it without a chain.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p412/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The addition of لك with 'raised' parallels 'expanded for you'; عنك with 'removed your burden' also signals care while supplying the needed complement and a vague-then-specific effect.

[TABATABAI:94:2-5/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | God raised the Prophet's mention above others, pairing his name with God's in the two testimonies and requiring every Muslim to mention him in each of the five daily prayers; the lift follows the burden poetically.

[ASAD-NOTES:94:intro/r3] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | The surah addresses the Prophet first and, through him, every true follower of the Qur'an.

[BINTSHATI:v1p63/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | Ordinary Arabic expressions show that these pronouns are purposeful rather than redundant.

[BINTSHATI:v1p67/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الشيخ محمد عبده | reports | Abduh emphasizes that Muhammad rescued many nations from superstition and corrupt ideas and restored them to sound human nature.
