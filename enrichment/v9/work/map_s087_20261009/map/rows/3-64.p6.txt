[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p194/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives سواء a contextual sense of equal standing: a shared truth in which young and old alike are equal, answering the audience's practice of making some people lords over others; people naturally welcome such equality. He distinguishes this from justice, which could describe coercively offering a captive conversion or death, and says the word still includes justice under every reading.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p194/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | Abū Ḥayyān dismisses Ibn ʿAṭiyya's extended discussion as verbose and without useful result.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p194/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The الظرف in the phrase is apparently governed by سواء.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p194/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | One parse makes ألا نعبد genitive as an appositive replacing كلمة; another makes it the predicate of an omitted subject, meaning “it is that we worship only God.”
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p195#2/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The reciprocal “some of us, others” subtly denies divinity: people share human nature, so one like oneself cannot be God; claiming divinity for humans is even less plausible than objecting to following prophets who are human, as the cited verses show. | mentions 14:10, 14:11, 23:47
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p195#2/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The three negative clauses overlap and reinforce God's exclusive worship, an emphatic expansion suited to people deeply attached to worshipping others; Christians combined all three by worshipping Jesus, calling God “third of three,” and making clerics lords through obedience and prostration.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p195#2/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الطبري | reports | Al-Ṭabarī says taking people as lords means treating them as God's equals by accepting their rulings that forbid or permit what God did not.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p195#2/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Abū Ḥayyān says this supports rejecting free-standing legal preference without revealed evidence and the Rawāfiḍ claim that an imam's ruling must be accepted without proof.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p195#2/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | If they turn away from the common word, Muslims should make them witness their own adherence to it; the wording sharply contrasts their refusal with Muslims' acceptance and obedience.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p195#2/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Using “bear witness” for knowledge intensifies the point by presenting an inward understanding as though it were sensibly witnessed and present.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p195#2/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the command publicly announces disagreement and confronts the rejecters as a reproach and threat, warning them to see the consequence of turning away.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p195/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The invitation means that worship is reserved for God alone, with no partner attributed to Him.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p195/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | شيئا can be a direct object or a verbal noun meaning any act of association; because the verb is negated, the negation extends to all its complements, including object, action, time, place, and manner.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p195/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · مقاتل والزجاج وعكرمة | reports | They explain taking one another as lords as believing in one another's divinity and worshipping them, as with Ezra and Jesus.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p195/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن جريج | reports | Ibn Jurayj interprets the phrase as people prostrating to one another or obeying bishops and leaders in unbelief and sin, treating their commands as religious law; the parallel is 9:31. | mentions 9:31
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p195/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · عدي بن حاتم | reports | ʿAdī ibn Ḥātim told the Prophet they had not worshipped their clerics; the Prophet asked whether the clerics had made things lawful and forbidden and they had followed, and ʿAdī agreed, to which the Prophet replied, “That was it.”
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p203/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The passage uses warning and pointing, paired emphatic particles, separation, and focus in “knowing the corrupters” and “guardian of the believers”; it also uses singular for a plural in كلمة and a generic designation in أهل الكتاب when understood as Jews.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p203/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Other rhetorical devices include repeated إلا الله, إن الله, the People of the Book address, and Abraham; أربابا is a metaphor likening obedience in lawmaking to the rightful Lord, and أولى/ولي form paronomasia.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p203/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The broad address “People of the Book” is an example of concise general address, comparable to Abraham's repeated “Father” and the cited poems.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64#2/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | ألا نعبد may be a full appositive to كلمة, an appositive to سواء (Abu al-Baqa’s less convincing choice), or an implied subject’s predicate answering what the word is; in these parses it is genitive, and بين is governed by سواء.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64#2/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | rejects | Al-Shaykh says al-Zamani’s analysis requires a weak, omitted linking expression فيها.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64#2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqa permits ألا نعبد as appositive to سواء, but the author finds this unclear because the described noun, not its adjective, is the main referent.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Under the first three parses, بين is an adverb governed by سواء, describing where the equality occurs; Zuhayr’s line is evidence, with a full stop at من دون الله.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64#2/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · زهير | reports | Zuhayr’s line أرونا خطة لا غيب فيها يسوي بيننا فيها السواء witnesses the construction of سواء with بين.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64#2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A fourth parse makes أن and its clause a nominative subject, with the preceding adverbial phrase as predicate.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64#2/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqa also proposes أن نعبد as the subject governed by the preceding adverbial, a parse available only under al-Akhfash and semantically strained.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64#2/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The fifth parse makes the sentence an adjective of كلمة without a clear linking pronoun; its proposed stop after سواء and restart as a modifier are inconsistent.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64#2/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqa alternatively says the statement ends at سواء and begins anew with بيننا وبينكم ألا نعبد, meaning tawhid between both parties; on this syntax it is a subject-predicate sentence.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64#2/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزماني | reports | Al-Zamani takes ألا نعبد as the subject of سواء, estimating that shared equality between the parties is their not worshipping anyone but God.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | تولوا is past tense; making it present تتولوا would mismatch the addressees, since فقولوا addresses believers while the conditional addresses the polytheists, and would leave the condition without an answer. The implied command is say to them.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | إلى كلمة attaches to تعالوا, whose object is stated here; an earlier تعالوا focused only on coming, or its destination was omitted as understood to be the mutual imprecation. | mentions 3:61
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan reads سواء in the accusative, either as a verbal noun meaning استوت استواء or as a circumstantial modifier from an indefinite noun.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari and al-Hawfi take the accusative as a verbal noun, estimating استوت استواء.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64/r12] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · سيبويه | reports | Sibawayh explicitly allows a circumstantial modifier to come from an indefinite noun; the author and al-Shaykh accept that analysis here.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64/r13] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | reports | Al-Shaykh objects that the verbal-noun reading needs an implied governing verb and an interpretation of سواء as استواء; مستو as an active participle is the more familiar sense.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64/r14] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas glosses سواء as مستوية, supporting the ordinary active-participle sense of equitable.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الجمهور | reports | The common reading has كلمة with fatḥa on kāf and kasra on lām, its basic form; Abu al-Sammal reads it on the pattern of sidra or ḍarba.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | كلمة is explained by ألا نعبد, using a part for the whole: the single word denotes a longer statement, as قصيدة may be called قافية.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · شاعر غير مسمى | reports | An anonymous poem uses قافية for the whole poem, not merely one rhyme, illustrating part-for-whole naming.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The phrase كلمة الشهادة names the whole testimony, and the Prophet’s report about Labid’s most truthful word refers to his line that everything besides God is false.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · بعضهم | reports | Some say the singular كلمة stands for a plural, as in a poem describing many exhausted animals and their bones and hides.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Another explanation uses singular كلمة for connected words: the tawhid statement functions as one whole because its intended relation fails if a part is missing.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:64/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الجمهور | reports | The common reading makes سواء genitive as an adjective of كلمة, meaning equitable; Abd Allah’s إلى كلمة عدل explains it but is not a reading.
