[ELMALILI:2:185#2/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The phrase can mean a person residing in Ramadan, knowing it with certainty, or seeing its crescent; because time cannot literally be seen, witnessing can mean certain knowledge or sight of the crescent.
[ELMALILI:2:185#3/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Sound hadiths explain the legally valid grounds for establishing the month's sighting, in agreement with 2:189; the matter leaves no room for independent legal opinion. | mentions 2:189
[ELMALILI:2:185#3/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Hanafi jurists require widely transmitted, knowledge-producing reports for matters everyone needs to know, rather than solitary reports.
[ELMALILI:2:185#3/r11] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | When an obstruction clouds the sky, outward circumstances no longer contradict the accepted testimony: one upright witness suffices for Ramadan and two for Eid.
[ELMALILI:2:185#3/r12] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | The apparent contradiction applies only when believers collectively search; later jurists facing lax searches set aside the large-crowd condition and accepted two upright witnesses unconditionally, as other schools did.
[ELMALILI:2:185#3/r13] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Because fasting is an individual duty for all, the month is established for all through possible communal moon sighting, or completion of thirty days; searching on the twenty-ninth is at least a communal obligation.

[ELMALILI:2:185#3/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Once one believer's sighting establishes fasting as obligatory, it applies by implication to others; private testimony binds its hearers, while court-established public testimony binds everyone.
[ELMALILI:2:185#3/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Fasting is solely God's right, while breaking the fast and Eid concern human rights; human-rights cases require at least two upright witnesses, but a solitary upright report can establish Ramadan.
[ELMALILI:2:185#3/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · eimme-i Hanefiyye | holds | The Hanafi imams accept one upright witness for Ramadan and two for Shawwal or Dhul Hijjah when the sky is obstructed; with clear skies, they require a numerous testimony that yields knowledge.
[ELMALILI:2:185#3/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | On the twenty-ninth, believers must search for the new moon; if many search under a clear sky, a few witnesses contradicted by the healthy observers' failure to see it are ordinarily implausible.
[ELMALILI:2:185#3/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | A clear sky and a proper communal search can discredit a few claims as error, illusion, or deliberate falsehood; this protects a commonly needed religious matter from confusion caused by solitary reports.
[ELMALILI:2:185#4/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Astronomical months between conjunctions or new moons are fractional, averaging twenty-nine and a half days, while fasting is measured in whole days; this calculation is not the legal month standard.
[ELMALILI:2:185#4/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | When mass testimony is absent, astronomical calculation should serve as an apparent circumstance for assessing witnesses in the present age, while Muslims retain their duty to search for the moon.
[ELMALILI:2:185#4/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Astronomical determination of the new moon is inferential knowledge, which may count in inferential matters but not where the law requires direct sighting.
[ELMALILI:2:185#4/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Making astronomy the criterion would force lay Muslims to rely on experts for fasting and deprive them of direct observation; making astronomy basic obligatory learning for everyone conflicts with Islam's universal ease.
[ELMALILI:2:185#4/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The conjunction is hidden and relative, while the new moon's sighting is its outward sign; in religious matters, an outward sign must stand in for what is hidden.
[ELMALILI:2:185#4/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | If calculation were the standard, the criterion would be the conjunction's occurrence and completion rather than the moon's visibility, leaving sighting without scientific or legal meaning.
[ELMALILI:2:185#4/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The absence of a text validating astronomical calculation for fasting reflects its unsuitability as a universal standard, not a judgment that astronomy is worthless.
[ELMALILI:2:185#4/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Instead of making astronomy obligatory, Muslims should build observatories that help everyone observe the new moon on sacred days.
[ELMALILI:2:185#4/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Valid testimony conveys direct observational knowledge and must not be contradicted by outward circumstances; astronomical calculation can be a better safeguard than trusting a few potentially corrupt witnesses.
