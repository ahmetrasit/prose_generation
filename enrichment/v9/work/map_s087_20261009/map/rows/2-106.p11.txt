[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#3/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · طوائف من اليهود | rejects | Groups of Jews also deny abrogation; al-Qurtubi answers that their own Torah, by their account, describes changed divine commands.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#3/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Examples include God permitting Noah and his descendants all animals except blood, later restrictions for Moses and Israel, sibling marriage for Adam’s children but not later generations, Abraham’s son first designated for sacrifice then spared, the command to kill calf-worshippers later lifted, and Moses’ law becoming binding after his commissioning.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#3/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Abrogation is not badaʾ; it transfers people from one act of worship or ruling to another for a change in interest, displaying God’s wisdom and sovereignty.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#3/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | All rational people agree that prophetic laws aim at religious and worldly interests; changed commands track changed interests, like a physician adapting treatment to a patient’s state.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#3/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Badaʾ would imply ignorance of outcomes; God’s address may change with circumstances, but His knowledge and will do not, since change in them is impossible.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#3/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Al-Qurtubi says Jews erred by treating abrogation and badaʾ as one thing.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#4/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · النحاس | reports | Al-Nahhas distinguishes abrogation as transferring a legal obligation from permitted to forbidden or the reverse; badaʾ is abandoning a prior resolve, a human defect caused by imperfection.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#4/r10] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | Another view permits abrogating a report when it contains a legal ruling, with the example from 16:67 continued in the next segment. | mentions 16:67
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#4/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · النحاس | reports | Al-Nahhas illustrates badaʾ with deciding that someone should visit today, then changing one’s mind and saying not to go, or changing one’s planting plan.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#4/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | God is the true abrogator; the legal address is called abrogating figuratively because abrogation occurs through it, and the ruling being removed can also be called the abrogator by metonymy.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#4/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The abrogated item is the removed ruling; the one abrogated from is the person who had been charged with that ruling.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#4/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · القاضي عبد الوهاب والقاضي أبو بكر | reports | The precise Sunni definition is removing an established legal ruling by a later-arriving legal address; absent the later address, the earlier ruling would remain.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#4/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The definition’s terms exclude rational judgments, include textual, apparent, and implied forms of evidence, exclude analogy and consensus as abrogating means, and require temporal delay so abrogation is not mere explanation of a ruling’s endpoint.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#4/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أهل السنة | reports | Sunnis hold that the abrogated item is the very ruling previously established, while the Muʿtazila say the earlier address declares that an equivalent future ruling will end.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#4/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · المعتزلة | reports | The Muʿtazila define the abrogated matter as an address stating that a future instance of the same ruling is removed, a position al-Qurtubi says follows from their doctrines about divine commands and goodness.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#4/r9] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الجمهور | reports | The majority restrict abrogation to commands and prohibitions because a report cannot be abrogated without implying that God’s report was false.

[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#5/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Legal rulings may be abrogated from heavier to lighter, as standing for ten enemies becomes standing for two, or from lighter to heavier, as the fast of ʿAshura and counted days gives way to Ramadan.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#5/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Like-for-like rulings may replace one another, as with the direction of prayer; a ruling may also be removed without a substitute, as with the alms before private consultation.
