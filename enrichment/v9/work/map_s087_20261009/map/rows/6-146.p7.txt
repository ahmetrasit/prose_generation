[SAMIN-DURR:6:146#4/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن الأعرابي | reports | Ibn al-Aʿrābī gives حوية and حاوية as singulars of الحوايا, without mentioning حاوياء.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146#4/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن السكيت | reports | Ibn al-Sikkīt lists حاوية, حوية, and حاوياء as possible singulars of حوايا.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146#4/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الفارسي | reports | Al-Fārisī allows حوايا to be the plural of each of the three proposed singular forms.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146#4/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Some speakers also derive حوايا from حوية by analogy with the saddle pad placed on a camel’s back; the line by Jarīr is introduced as evidence for حاوياء.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146#5/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Jarīr’s line uses حاوياء as a singular behind حوايا; Abū Bakr Ibn al-Anbārī recites a second line containing حاويائه.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146#5/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The line recited by Abū Bakr Ibn al-Anbārī attests the form حاويائه.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146#5/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | If the singular is حاوية or حاوياء, حوايا has pattern فواعل from حواوي, with hamza substitution and subsequent vowel changes. | mentions 2:58
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146#5/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | If the singular is حوية, حوايا has pattern فعائل from حوائي; hamza and weak-final changes yield the same surface form by a different derivation.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146#5/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ coordinates this phrase with the excepted ما and identifies it as the tail fat; another sense is fat in the flank, eye, ear, or limbs.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146#6/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الحوفي ومكي وأبو البقاء | reports | One parsing supplies an omitted subject: the matter is that. The author understands Ibn ʿAṭiyya’s unspecified nominative analysis this way because the alternative is weak.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146#6/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Another parsing makes ذلك the subject and the following clause its predicate, with an omitted object resuming it; the author calls this weak.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146#6/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī takes ذلك as an accusative cognate object meaning that punishment.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146#6/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن مالك | reports | Ibn Mālik objects that a demonstrative pointing to a verbal noun should be followed by that noun, as in ضربت ذلك الضرب.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146#6/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن مالك | reports | Ibn Mālik rejects the defense of al-Mutanabbī’s هذي برزت as a demonstrative for a source, since the source noun does not follow it.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146#6/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The author rejects Ibn Mālik’s rule, citing a poet’s ذاك as a demonstrative for the source of خال without a following source noun.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146#6/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء ومكي | reports | Abū al-Baqāʾ and Makkī also parse ذلك as an accusative governed by جزيناهم, without saying whether it is a second object or cognate object.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146#7/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The omitted complement may mean that God is truthful in completing their requital in the afterlife, alluding to the Jews’ false claim of following Israel’s prohibitions.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146#7/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Alternatively, God is truthful in informing about them, or no complement is supplied because truthfulness is intrinsic to God.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Fronting على الذين هادوا before حرمنا marks restriction for some grammarians, including al-Zamakhshari and al-Razi; al-Razi explicitly notes the governed phrase precedes its verb.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | rejects | Abu al-Baqa forbids attaching من البقر to the second verb; the Shaykh rejects that restriction, distinguishing its position after the verb from an alleged required position after the object and allowing either order.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | reports | The Shaykh supports the order with examples ضرب غلام المرأة أبوها and غلام المرأة ضرب أبوها: a prepositional phrase and object can exchange order without violating grammatical rank.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الجمهور | reports | The common recitation uses ظفر, which the author calls the highest of five Arabic forms for the word.

[SAMIN-DURR:6:146/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو السمال | reports | A form with kasra on both ظاء and فاء is attributed by al-Wahidi to Abu al-Sammal.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الحسن | reports | Another form has kasra on ظاء and sukūn on فاء, easing the kasra; readers also attribute this recitation to al-Hasan.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | أظفور is a fifth lexical form, though the author knows of no recitation using it; a poetic line attests the word.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:146/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The triliteral plural is أظفار; أظافير is the regular plural of أظفور, while أظافر without long ا is irregular. | mentions 6:59
