[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-7-english:p474/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The word for burden includes what winds carry, such as dust and pebbles, but conventionally refers to clouds, as 7:57 shows. | mentions 7:57
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-7-english:p475/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The varied effects of winds and clouds bring mercy to one people and storms as punishment to another.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-7-english:p481/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Two oaths about wind effects and striped clouds substantiate two claims: the threatened punishment is certain, and the Hereafter's reward and punishment will occur.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:51:1/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | After oaths by four groups of beings or events, verses 5–6 insist that the Day of Judgment is certain.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:51:1/r12] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Zemahşerî ve Râzî | reports | Zamakhshari and Razi allow that the first four oath objects may be separate things or different qualities of one thing.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:51:1/r13] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Hz. Ali | reports | A saying transmitted from Ali supports the separate-entities interpretation: winds, clouds, ships, and angels distributing provision, respectively.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:51:1/r14] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Râzî | prefers | Razi regards the interpretation of all four terms as different wind qualities as stronger: winds form clouds, carry water-vapor clouds heavier than mountains, drive those clouds, and distribute rain.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:51:1/r15] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | The author surveys oath scholarship: besides Ibn Qayyim’s dedicated work, few older scholars wrote separately on Quranic oaths; contemporary Indian scholar Abd al-Hamid al-Farahi wrote a substantial introduction using that work and Razi.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:51:1/r16] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Abdülhamîd el-Ferâhî | reports | Farahi’s Im’an fi Aqsam al-Qur’an studies general principles, analyzes Ibn Qayyim and Razi, discusses the history and human need for oaths with examples, and notes Arab oaths can honor the object or addressee, elevate the swearer, or draw on a belief that perjurers are cursed.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:51:1/r17] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Abdülhamîd el-Ferâhî | reports | Farahi says the essence and purpose of oaths is indication of meanings; glorification is not their primary Quranic purpose, though some oaths imply it.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:51:1/r18] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Abdülhamîd el-Ferâhî | reports | Farahi says the oath need not name an object sworn by; no unstated object must be supplied, and interpretation need not assume the object is being glorified.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:51:1/r19] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Abdülhamîd el-Ferâhî | reports | Farahi identifies the main purpose of an oath as reinforcing the speaker’s statement and showing firm resolve to do or refrain from something.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:51:1/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The plural participles used for the oath objects have prompted several different interpretations.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:51:1/r20] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The oaths draw the audience’s attention and invite reflection, emphasizing the seriousness of the subject.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:51:1/r21] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Beyzâvî | reports | The One capable of establishing extraordinary order and balance is also capable of fulfilling the promised resurrection of people after death.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:51:1/r22] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · İbn Âşûr | reports | Swearing by important qualities without naming what bears them is an elegant concision that allows readers to imagine many possible referents.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:51:1/r23] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Because this surah repeatedly stresses earth and sky and says in verse 22 that provision and promises are in heaven, the first four oaths may point to life-critical laws, especially atmospheric events, biosphere changes, and angels assigned to enact them by divine will. | mentions 51:7, 51:20, 51:23, 51:47, 51:48, 51:22
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:51:1/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Hamilat literally means carriers bearing a load or weight.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:51:1/r7] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · müfessirler | reports | Given the wind interpretation in verse 1, commentators understand verse 2 as rain-laden clouds or winds carrying clouds.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:51:1/r8] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · İbn Abbas | reports | Ibn Abbas and some other scholars interpret the carriers as ships loaded with people and goods.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:51:1/r9] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Bir grup âlim | reports | A group of scholars also thinks the verse includes pregnant females.
[STUDYQURAN:51:2/r1] STUDYQURAN · Bḍ, JJ, Q, Ṭ | reports | One interpretation identifies those bearing a burden as clouds carrying moisture.
[STUDYQURAN:51:2/r2] STUDYQURAN · Bḍ, Q | reports | Another interpretation identifies them as pregnant women.
