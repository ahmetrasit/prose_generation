[TABRISI:2:180#2/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · علي | reports | ʿAlī says that dying without a bequest for close relatives who do not inherit ends one's deeds in disobedience.
[TABRISI:2:180#2/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · النبي | reports | A report attributed to the Prophet says that one who dies without a will dies a death of the pre-Islamic age.
[TABRISI:2:180#2/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · النبي | reports | The Prophet is reported to call a person's failure to make a sound will before death a deficiency in character and intellect.
[TABRISI:2:180#2/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو عبد الله | reports | Abu ʿAbd Allāh says a Muslim should not go to sleep without keeping his will beneath his head.
[TABRISI:2:180/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Maʿrūf is just conduct that cannot rightly be condemned and contains no unfairness or injustice.
[TABRISI:2:180/r10] TABRISI, d. 548 · الزهري | reports | Al-Zuhrī says the bequest is due for any amount of property, little or much.
[TABRISI:2:180/r11] TABRISI, d. 548 · إبراهيم النخعي | reports | Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī sets the relevant property threshold at one thousand to five hundred dirhams.
[TABRISI:2:180/r12] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās sets the threshold at eight hundred dirhams.
[TABRISI:2:180/r13] TABRISI, d. 548 · علي | prefers | ʿAlī visited a sick client with six or seven hundred dirhams, advised him not to make a will because he did not have much wealth, and the author says this is the adopted position among his school.
[TABRISI:2:180/r14] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Fair dealing may refer to a proportionate bequest, since a wealthy person’s bequest of one dirham is not fair, or to choosing recipients justly; both readings apply.
[TABRISI:2:180/r15] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Fair distribution also requires not preferring the rich over the poor or a more distant relative over a closer one.
[TABRISI:2:180/r16] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The phrase ‘a duty upon the God-fearing’ emphasizes that the bequest is obligatory for those who choose piety.
[TABRISI:2:180/r17] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Three positions are reported: the verse is abrogated, it is abrogated only for heirs but remains for non-heirs, or it is not abrogated; the author endorses the last view.
[TABRISI:2:180/r18] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | prefers | The author prefers that the verse remains in force: inheritance and bequest rulings do not conflict, and abrogation requires that the two rulings cannot both be followed.
[TABRISI:2:180/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Ḥuḍūr means that something is present in a way that can be perceived.
[TABRISI:2:180/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Ḥaqq can mean an act that cannot be denied, or anything known to be sound as a statement, act, or belief; it is the verbal noun of ḥaqqa.
[TABRISI:2:180/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Kutiba ʿalaykum means ‘and it was prescribed’; the conjunction is omitted because the prior account is complete and this account connects through its subject matter.
[TABRISI:2:180/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Al-waṣiyya is nominative either as the passive subject of kutiba or as a subject whose predicate is li-l-wālidayn; in the latter parse the statement is quoted as what was prescribed.
[TABRISI:2:180/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | The time phrase may attach to kutiba, placing the duty at the onset of illness, or follow al-Zajjāj’s parse: make a will while healthy for implementation when death comes.
[TABRISI:2:180/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Ḥaqqan can be an emphatic verbal noun, a circumstantial adjective meaning ‘rightful’, or a verbal noun from kataba meaning ‘a written obligation’.
[TABRISI:2:180/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The verse establishes the law of bequests; ‘when death comes’ means its causes, such as illness or old age, not the moment when death is directly witnessed and the dying person is too occupied to dictate a will.
[TABRISI:2:180/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · قيل | reports | Another view makes the bequest obligatory while a person is healthy, with instructions for what should happen when death arrives.
