[IBNKATHIR:93:4/r1] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · author | holds | The Hereafter is better for the Prophet than this world, as shown by his exceptional asceticism and his choosing what is with God over worldly life when offered the choice near death.
[IBNKATHIR:93:4/r2] IBNKATHIR, d. 774 · عبد الله بن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masʿūd reports that after the Prophet's rough mat marked his side, he compared himself and the world to a rider who rests under a tree then leaves it; al-Tirmidhī graded the report ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ.
[BIQAI:93:4/r1] BIQAI, d. 885 · أنس | reports | Anas reports hearing the Prophet say that he was delighted by what would be opened for his community after him, land after land.
[BIQAI:93:4/r2] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | The hereafter is the higher and greater realm: even its lowest entrant receives what no eye has seen, ear heard, or heart imagined, so the Prophet's share is beyond comparison.
[BIQAI:93:4/r3] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | Al-ākhirah can mean the lasting, untroubled hereafter, the true end of existence, or the Prophet's later condition; the latter sense implies endless ascent from one perfection to another.
[BIQAI:93:4/r4] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | The qualification “for you” matters because this upward progression is not true of everyone; the fleeting world offers no unalloyed joy and is less noble than what follows.
[BIQAI:93:4/r5] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | People fall into four groups: obedient wealthy people have good in both realms; poor unbelievers bad in both; wealthy unbelievers apparent worldly good but afterlife harm; poor believers worldly hardship but afterlife good. He illustrates this with a four-part poem.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p453/r6] BIQAI-FULL, d. 885 · أبو جعفر بن الزبير | reports | Ibn al-Zubayr says the predestination discussion could cause fear, so God reassured His dearest servant by recounting his favor and the good of both lives.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p455#2/r1] BIQAI-FULL, d. 885 · author | reports | The surah was revealed after a pause in revelation when disbelievers said God had abandoned Muḥammad; it announces favor and glad tidings.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p455#2/r2] BIQAI-FULL, d. 885 · author | reports | Anas reports that the Prophet said he was shown the successive gifts that would be opened to his community after him, and this pleased him.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p455#2/r3] BIQAI-FULL, d. 885 · author | holds | The Hereafter is better for the Prophet than the first life and implies his continual ascent from one high state to a higher one without end.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p455#2/r4] BIQAI-FULL, d. 885 · author | holds | People fall into four worldly and afterlife patterns: obedient rich prosper in both, poor unbelievers fare badly in both, wealthy unbelievers have only worldly good, and poor believers only afterlife good.

[ALUSI:93:4/r1] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | The afterlife is better because it is everlasting and wholly free of worldly defects; even prophetic distinction in this life has incidental hindrances, while the afterlife brings precedence, the praiseworthy station, the community's witness, and believers' elevation through his intercession.
[ALUSI:93:4/r10] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | reports | One proposed link says that, after the denial itself bestowed great honor, God reassured the Prophet that this honor is surpassed by what awaits him in the afterlife.
[ALUSI:93:4/r11] ALUSI, d. 1270 · قيل | reports | A weaker proposal says that if revelation ceased, it would signal death rather than dismissal from prophethood, and death would be better because his afterlife reward exceeds this world's.
[ALUSI:93:4/r12] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | rejects | Al-Ālūsī judges the death-and-revelation-cessation proposal much weaker than the preceding explanation.
[ALUSI:93:4/r2] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | The afterlife gifts prepared for him include precedence over all prophets and messengers, the praiseworthy station, his community witnessing for other nations, and believers raised through his intercession.
[ALUSI:93:4/r3] ALUSI, d. 1270 · قيل | reports | The lām may mark a relative distinction: the afterlife's goodness is particular to the Prophet rather than those who harmed him and mocked the revelation delay.
[ALUSI:93:4/r4] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | The lām need not restrict the promise to the Prophet alone: all successful people share in afterlife good, though his reward is necessarily greater and the praiseworthy station uniquely his.
[ALUSI:93:4/r5] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبو إسحاق وغيره | reports | The apparent and transmitted meaning takes the Afterlife and the First as the next life and this worldly life, respectively.
[ALUSI:93:4/r6] ALUSI, d. 1270 · ابن عطية وجماعة | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya and others allow the terms to mean the end and beginning of the Prophet's mission: its ending will steadily grow stronger and higher.
[ALUSI:93:4/r7] ALUSI, d. 1270 · ابن عطية وجماعة | reports | On the beginning-and-end interpretation, the definite articles refer back or stand in place of a possessive construction.

[ALUSI:93:4/r9] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | prefers | Al-Ālūsī prefers linking the promise to the preceding denial: after the assurance that God had not abandoned him, the Prophet is told that his future reward exceeds the honor already given. | mentions 93:3

[ALUSI-FULL:v30p158/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | His afterlife honors—precedence over all prophets, his community's witness over others, and believers' elevation through his intercession—vastly surpass worldly beginnings.
