v15p250/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | A traveler from the Maghrib hears two Baghdad vegetable sellers debate delayed exceptions: one argues that Job would have been told to except if Ibn ʿAbbās were right, since the remedy in the verse about his oath came after a year. | mentions 38:44
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p250/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | The traveler returns home impressed that even a street seller could challenge Ibn ʿAbbās, and infers how learned Baghdad's scholars must be.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p250/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | rejects | Al-Ālūsī says such an objection is excessive even from a vegetable seller and cautions that God knows whether the anecdote is authentic.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p250/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | rejects | The apparent verse and report may seem to support Ibn ʿAbbās, but the required recovery is only a renewed declaration of dependence on God, not a delayed change to legal effect; the passage does not address legal rulings.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p250/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · ابن عباس | reports | A report from Ibn ʿAbbās says that one who forgot the exception should make it when remembering, but that this privilege belongs only to the Prophet; others may except only as part of an oath.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p250/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · بعضهم | rejects | An alternative says the later words attach to an implied recurring statement: whenever one forgets God, remember Him upon recollection, or say if God wills when announcing a future act; al-Ālūsī finds this far from the apparent sense.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p251/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الجبائي | reports | Al-Jubbāʾī links the guidance prayer to remembering God: ask God to remind the Prophet of a forgotten matter, and if not, to guide him to something nearer and more beneficial; al-Ālūsī finds this closer syntactically but distant in other respects.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p251/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · بعضهم | reports | Another proposal links it to the preceding commands and prohibitions: hope for guidance to what is more beneficial than their instruction; al-Ālūsī judges this strained.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p251/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · ابن الأنباري | reports | Ibn al-Anbārī says God may reveal answers to the questioners before the promised time and hasten guidance; al-Ālūsī says the meaning cannot be derived and would make the Quran like a riddle.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p251/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · رجل من أهل الكوفة | reports | An unnamed Kufan, transmitted through Muʿtamir ibn Sulaymān and his father and attributed by Abū Ḥayyān to Muḥammad al-Kūfī, says repentance for forgetting the exception is to recite the guidance prayer; al-Ālūsī says it has no evidence.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p261/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · بعضهم | reports | An esoteric interpretation says to remember God after forgetting the whole universe, including oneself, because remembrance becomes pure only then.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p261/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الجنيد | reports | Al-Junayd reads the command as remembering after forgetting remembrance itself; true remembrance is annihilation in the Remembered apart from the act of remembering.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p261/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الجنيد | reports | Al-Junayd says a station above remembrance is nearer to guidance: God describes and remembers the servant before the servant remembers Him.
[ELMALILI:18:23/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | If one forgets to say “if God wills,” one should remember God when recalling it, by saying it then or by glorification and seeking forgiveness; this expiates the lapse even if it does not change the statement's effect.
[ELMALILI:18:23/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | An alternative reading is that remembering God after forgetting anything acknowledges human weakness and may help one recall the forgotten thing.
[ELMALILI:18:24-26/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The prayer asks that the Lord bring the Prophet to success more directly than the Cave companions' success.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p295#2/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī takes ‘unless God wills’ as an exception from the prohibition itself: one may make the statement only if God permits it, with the omitted object supplied from context.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p295#2/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · الكسائي والأخفش والفراء | reports | Al-Kisāʾī, al-Akhfash, and al-Farrāʾ take the exception from ‘I will do that tomorrow’: the promise is allowed when accompanied by saying ‘if God wills.’
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p295#2/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The verse honors the Prophet by answering his question, teaching prophetic etiquette, and postponing the rebuke until after answering so he would not mistake correction for refusal.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p295#2/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · حكيم بن حزام | reports | In a report, the Prophet gave Ḥakīm repeatedly when he asked, then advised him about taking wealth freely; Ḥakīm understood the counsel was ethical and vowed not to take from anyone else.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p295/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · ابن إسحاق والطبري والواحدي | reports | Reports say the polytheists asked about the sleepers and Dhū al-Qarnayn, and the Prophet promised an answer the next day without saying ‘if God wills’; revelation was delayed fifteen days, or three in another report.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p295/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Ibn Ashur treats the delayed revelation as a subtle reproach, followed by a direct prohibition against promising future action without qualifying it by God’s will.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p295/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · البخاري | reports | Bukhari’s report about Solomon says he vowed to visit one hundred women in a night so each would bear a fighter, but only one gave birth to half a boy.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p295/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The Prophet forgot to say ‘if God wills’ when promising an answer; after relating the story, God forbade unqualified promises, as with Solomon’s forgotten qualification.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p297/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The lām in li-shayʾ is causal, meaning ‘for something you intend to do’; dhālika refers to that intended action, including answering their question.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p297/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Ghadā is a figurative term for future time, not necessarily the next day, as in Zuhayr’s contrast among today, yesterday, and tomorrow.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p297/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | Although the apparent scope is future plans, most jurists say adding ‘if God wills’ releases an oath without expiation; they extend the broad wording to vows.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p297/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · ابن القاسم وأشهب وابن عبد الحكم عن مالك | reports | These transmit from Mālik that ‘remember your Lord when you forget’ concerns remembering God after forgetfulness, not oath exceptions; the oath ruling comes from Sunna, as also held by Abū Ḥanīfa and al-Shāfiʿī.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p297/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · عمر بن الخطاب | reports | ʿUmar says the best remembrance of God is to remember Him when encountering His commands and prohibitions, supporting the meaning of remembrance as compliance.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p298/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Addressing God as ‘your Lord’ rather than using the divine name carries a special gentleness; the object of forgetting is the prohibition just mentioned.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p298/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · ابن عباس | reports | Some jurists read ‘remember your Lord when you forget’ as permission to add an oath exception upon remembering, without a time limit; Ibn ʿAbbās permits even a long delay.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p298/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | The majority reject using this verse to permit delayed oath exceptions, arguing that the Sunna states otherwise.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p298/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | After fulfilling the promised answer about the sleepers, God rebukes the Prophet for engaging questions beyond the mission without permission and directs him to seek more important guidance in revealed law.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p298/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | ‘Say, perhaps my Lord will guide me’ may continue the command not to dispute their number, or be a prayer joined to remembering God after forgetting. | mentions 18:22
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p298/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Rushdan either specifies the comparative relation in ‘closer than this’ or serves as an absolute object describing the kind of guidance sought.

[TABATABAI:18:9-26#25/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | When one remembers the omission, append the exception before finishing, repeat the statement afterward, or hold the intention and add it later; any remembrance of God’s authority is suitable.
