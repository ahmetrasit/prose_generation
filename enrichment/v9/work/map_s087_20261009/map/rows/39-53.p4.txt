[RAZI-FULL:v25p73#2/r8] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The servant address followed believers’ prayer: they invoked God in faith, and He answered by calling them His servants; unbelievers did not supplicate and were not included. | mentions 40:60, 3:193, 39:53
[RAZI-FULL:v27p4/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Repentance remains obligatory and fear of punishment remains because sinners cannot know punishment is wholly removed: God may forgive outright or forgive after a period in Hell.
[RAZI-FULL:v27p4/r10] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The emphatic pronoun yields exclusivity: only God is truly forgiving and merciful, displaying perfection in both attributes.
[RAZI-FULL:v27p4/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Calling sinners servants evokes their need, humility, and poverty, qualities that invite a generous Lord to give mercy.
[RAZI-FULL:v27p4/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | God’s possessive attribution of the sinners to Himself honors them and reassures them against punishment.
[RAZI-FULL:v27p4/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The damage of sins returns to the sinners themselves, so they need no additional harm imposed on them.
[RAZI-FULL:v27p4/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Forbidding despair commands hope, and a generous Lord’s command to hope is a promise of generosity.
[RAZI-FULL:v27p4/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The verse says “God’s mercy” rather than “My mercy,” invoking God’s greatest name and therefore the greatest kind of mercy and favor.
[RAZI-FULL:v27p4/r7] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Repeating God’s name and adding the emphatic particle إن intensifies the promise of mercy.
[RAZI-FULL:v27p4/r8] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The word “all” reinforces the comprehensive promise of forgiveness.
[RAZI-FULL:v27p4/r9] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | “The Forgiving” signals removal of grounds for punishment, while “the Merciful” signals the granting of grounds for mercy and reward.
[RAZI-FULL:v27p5/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · أهل مكة | reports | One reported occasion is that Meccans, having worshipped idols and killed people, wondered how they could accept Islam if Muhammad said such sins would not be forgiven.
[RAZI-FULL:v27p5/r10] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · ابن عباس وابن مسعود | reports | A reading attributed to Ibn Abbas and Ibn Masud adds “for whom He wills” after the promise to forgive all sins.

[RAZI-FULL:v27p5/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · عياش بن أبي ربيعة والوليد بن الوليد | reports | The verse was also said to concern Ayyash, al-Walid, and other Muslims who apostatized under persecution; Umar sent them the verses, after which they returned to Islam and emigrated.
[RAZI-FULL:v27p5/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Although reports give particular occasions, the general wording governs and the verse is not restricted to those cases.

[RAZI-FULL:v27p5/r8] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | All readers retain the ya in pausal form except Abu Bakr’s transmission from Asim, which pauses without it.
