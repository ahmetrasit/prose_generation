Qatāda sets the amount of wealth that triggers the bequest rule at one thousand dirhams or more.
[TAB-FULL:v3p137/r1] TAB-FULL, d. 310 · علي بن أبي طالب | reports | In reports about sick relatives with roughly six hundred to nine hundred dirhams, ʿAlī advises against a bequest because they had not left much property.
[TAB-FULL:v3p137/r2] TAB-FULL, d. 310 · عائشة | reports | ʿĀʾisha sees no surplus for a man with many children who wanted to bequeath when he had four hundred dinars.
[TAB-FULL:v3p137/r3] TAB-FULL, d. 310 · إبراهيم النخعي | reports | Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī places the threshold at five hundred to one thousand dirhams.
[TAB-FULL:v3p137/r4] TAB-FULL, d. 310 · الزهري | reports | Al-Zuhrī says the bequest is obligatory regardless of whether the property is little or much.
[TAB-FULL:v3p137/r5] TAB-FULL, d. 310 · author | prefers | Al-Ṭabarī prefers al-Zuhrī's unrestricted view: small and large property both count as khayr, God set no threshold, and every dying person with property must make a proper bequest to non-inheriting parents and kin.
[ZAJJAJ:v1p249/r3] ZAJJAJ, d. 311 · author | holds | The second ‘prescribed for you’ omits the conjunction waw, which long discourse can leave understood; the sense is that both retaliation and bequest were prescribed.
[ZAJJAJ:v1p249/r4] ZAJJAJ, d. 311 · author | holds | The bequest requirement was abrogated by the inheritance verses in Sūrat al-Nisāʾ; Zajjāj explains its original wisdom because God prescribes with profound wisdom.
[ZAJJAJ:v1p249/r5] ZAJJAJ, d. 311 · author | holds | The original prescription meant that one who left wealth was to bequeath fairly to parents and relatives.
[ZAJJAJ:v1p249/r6] ZAJJAJ, d. 311 · author | holds | Al-waṣiyya may be the passive subject in ‘the bequest was prescribed,’ or a nominative topic with li-l-wālidayn as predicate in the sense of a reported instruction.
[ZAJJAJ:v1p249/r7] ZAJJAJ, d. 311 · author | holds | The command addressed favoritism toward distant outsiders for display and reputation by directing a fair bequest to parents and close relatives.
[ZAJJAJ:v1p249/r8] ZAJJAJ, d. 311 · author | holds | The verse does not require making a will while death is actually present, when a person is occupied; it requires arranging it while still able, in anticipation of death.
[ZAJJAJ:v1p249/r9] ZAJJAJ, d. 311 · author | holds | Bi-l-maʿrūf means what a discerning person recognizes as free of bias and injustice.
[ZAJJAJ:v1p250/r1] ZAJJAJ, d. 311 · قوم | reports | Some say the bequest command remains for one third; al-Zajjāj rejects this as an obligatory command, since Muslims agree one may bequeath a third or leave it, and the verse’s prescription was abrogated.
[ZAJJAJ:v1p250/r2] ZAJJAJ, d. 311 · author | holds | Ḥaqqan is accusative as ‘that is truly required of you’; outside Qur’anic wording, nominative would also be grammatical.
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p298/r1] IBNABIHATIM, d. 327 · الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan says every Muslim must make a proper, nonwrongful bequest when death approaches.
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p298/r2] IBNABIHATIM, d. 327 · علي | reports | Ali advised a man who asked about making a bequest that, since he had little, he should leave it to his children.
