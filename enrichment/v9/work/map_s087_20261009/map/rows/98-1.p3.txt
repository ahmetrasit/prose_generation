[IBNATIYYA:98:1/r9] IBNATIYYA, d. 542 · الفراء وغيره | reports | Al-Farrāʾ and others say they did not cease recognizing the truth of Muhammad’s prophethood and attending to his cause until the sign came, whereupon they split.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p507#3/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · أبي بن كعب | reports | Ubayy’s reading begins with ‘Those who disbelieved among the People of the Book and the polytheists were not…’ using kana instead of yakun.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p507#3/r2] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · عبد الله بن مسعود | reports | Ibn Mas‘ud’s reading places the polytheists before the People of the Book.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p507#3/r3] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | Munfakkin means separated or divided; it is not the defective verb ma انفك, which belongs to the sisters of kana.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p507#3/r4] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · مجاهد | reports | Mujahid and others say the disbelievers would not leave unbelief and error until the clear proof came; the future verb is used in place of the past because the rest of the verse describes what had not yet happened.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p507#3/r5] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farra’ and others say they remained aware of Muhammad’s prophethood and awaiting his appearance, then divided after the proof came.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p507#3/r6] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | reports | Some grammarians treat the negation plus munfakkin as the kana construction, supplying a predicate such as ‘knowing Muhammad’s affair.’
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p507#3/r7] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | prefers | The author prefers a third sense: the people remained under God’s care and power until He sent a warning messenger, establishing proof and completing the blessing for believers, as in other Qur’anic passages.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p507#3/r8] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | reports | Some recite al-mushrikun in the nominative, while the majority read al-mushrikin in the genitive; the author says their meanings are clear.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p507#4/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | The clear proof means the evident account, namely Muhammad; most readers put rasul in the nominative, while Ubayy reads rasulan in the accusative as a circumstantial phrase.
[TABRISI:98:1-4/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · قيل | reports | Alternatively, they could not leave unbelief until God's proof came and established the argument against them.
[TABRISI:98:1/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Infikak means separation after strong attachment and is used mostly with negation, as in a line by Dhu al-Rumma about camels that only stop to kneel or be driven to a remote land.
[TABRISI:98:1/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Bayyina is a clear proof distinguishing truth from falsehood; its root carries the sense of separation. The Prophet, fair testimony, and every proof are called bayyina.
[TABRISI:98:1/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The verse addresses Jews, Christians, and idol-worshipping polytheists from Arabs and others; munfakkin can mean separating or stopping from disbelief and worship of others, an interpretation attributed to Ibn Abbas through Ata and al-Kalbi.
[TABRISI:98:1/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The future-tense phrase until the proof came refers to the past, as in the statement about what demons recited in 2:102. | mentions 2:102
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p194/r6] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · الجمهور | reports | The majority say Sura al-Bayyina is Medinan.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p194/r7] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن عباس | reports | Another view says the surah is Meccan, reported by Abu Salih from Ibn Abbas and chosen by Yahya ibn Salam.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p196/r1] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | The people of the Book are Jews and Christians, and the polytheists are idol worshippers; munfakkīn means separated or departed.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p196/r2] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | Although the verse uses a future form, it means that they did not leave their disbelief until the proof came, namely Muhammad, who clarified their error; this rescued believers among both groups.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p196/r3] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · بعض المفسرين | reports | Some exegetes say the groups did not disagree about God sending a prophet until He sent one, after which they split.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p196/r4] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · بعضهم | reports | Another explanation is that they would not leave their state without God's proofs until the clear proof had been established against them.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p196/r5] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | prefers | The author prefers the first interpretation: they remained in disbelief until the Messenger came.
[RAZI:98:1/r1] RAZI, d. 606 · الواحدي | reports | Al-Wāḥidī calls this one of the Qur'an's hardest verses in structure and interpretation, saying major scholars struggled with it.
[RAZI:98:1/r10] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | A fourth solution says the collective group did not leave disbelief before the Messenger, but afterward it changed because some believed and others persisted in disbelief.
[RAZI:98:1/r11] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | A fifth solution says they had been certain of their inherited religions before Muhammad, but his coming unsettled them into doubt and confusion; munfakkīn suggests separation from certainty. | mentions 2:213
[RAZI:98:1/r12] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | The unbelievers are two groups: People of Scripture, Jews and Christians who introduced unbelief into their religion, and polytheists who had no scripture. | mentions 9:30
[RAZI:98:1/r13] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | One solution to the apparent partitive sense of min is to read it as explanatory, as in the command to shun the abomination of idols in 22:30. | mentions 22:30
[RAZI:98:1/r14] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Alternatively, min identifies the two sources of those who disbelieved in Muhammad: some were People of Scripture and some polytheists.
[RAZI:98:1/r15] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | A third analysis treats al-mushrikīn as a second descriptor of the People of Scripture: Christians are trinitarian and most Jews anthropomorphists, both forms of shirk.
[RAZI:98:1/r16] RAZI, d. 606 · بعض العلماء | reports | Some scholars include the Magians among the People of Scripture, citing the Prophetic instruction to treat them by the rules applied to that community.
[RAZI:98:1/r17] RAZI, d. 606 · الآخرون | reports | Others exclude the Magians, arguing that the verse concerns the Arab communities and that 6:156 identifies only Jews and Christians as the two earlier scriptural groups. | mentions 6:156
[RAZI:98:1/r18] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Although conjunction does not establish order, the People of Scripture are placed first because the sura is Medinan, they knew scripture and were more culpable for rejecting Muhammad, others followed them, and they held higher status.
[RAZI:98:1/r19] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Calling them People of Scripture rather than naming Jews and Christians can honor them by their learning, or highlight that their learning makes their unbelief more blameworthy and punishable.
[RAZI:98:1/r2] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | The apparent problem is that 98:1 seems to say they left disbelief when the Messenger came, while 98:4 says the People of Scripture divided only after the proof arrived. | mentions 98:4
[RAZI:98:1/r20] RAZI, d. 606 · العلماء | reports | Some jurists infer from naming both groups under 'those who disbelieved' that unbelief is one community, so a polytheist may inherit from a Jew and vice versa.
[RAZI:98:1/r21] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Others take the coordination to distinguish the groups: a protected non-Muslim is not a polytheist, supported by a Prophetic statement about their women and slaughtered food.
[RAZI:98:1/r22] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Mention of learned People of Scripture warns Muslims not to be impressed by scholars: people of the Qur'an can repeat the failings of earlier religious communities.
[RAZI:98:1/r23] RAZI, d. 606 · القفال | reports | Al-Qaffāl derives infikāk from opening or loosening: unsealing a book, releasing a pledge or captive, or separating something joined, like a bone from its joint.
[RAZI:98:1/r24] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | The image means they clung tightly to their religion and would not detach from it until the clear proof arrived.
[RAZI:98:1/r3] RAZI, d. 606 · author | prefers | Preferring al-Kashshāf's solution, al-Rāzī reads the first clause as the unbelievers' claim that they would not leave their religions until the promised Prophet, Muhammad, came; the next clause reports that his coming instead divided them from truth. | mentions 98:4
[RAZI:98:1/r4] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | The poor sinner analogy shows how a preacher can quote someone's earlier condition as a rebuke: he claimed he would reform when wealthy, then grew more sinful after gaining wealth.
[RAZI:98:1/r5] RAZI, d. 606 · القاضي | rejects | Al-Qāḍī construes the clause as saying they would not leave disbelief even if the proof came; al-Rāzī rejects this because hatta does not mean 'even if' in Arabic.
[RAZI:98:1/r6] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | Another proposed reading takes munfakkīn as continuing to praise and mention Muhammad's virtues until the proof came; after his arrival they split and spoke badly of him.
[RAZI:98:1/r7] RAZI, d. 606 · ابن عرفة | reports | Ibn ʿArafa says the wording uses the present form but has past meaning, until the proof came, as in 2:102. | mentions 2:102
[RAZI:98:1/r8] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | The praise-then-rejection reading parallels 2:89, where people previously sought victory through revelation but rejected what they recognized when it came. | mentions 2:89
[RAZI:98:1/r9] RAZI, d. 606 · author | prefers | Al-Rāzī explicitly selects the first explanation: the clause reports what the unbelievers claimed, whereas the following clause reports what actually happened. |
