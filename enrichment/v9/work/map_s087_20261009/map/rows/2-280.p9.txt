tataṣaddaqū.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:280#4/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The object of taṣaddaqū is omitted as understood; one interpretation is to grant more time by forgoing the debt, another to remit the principal to the debtor.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:280#4/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The answer to in kuntum taʿlamūn is omitted, while an taṣaddaqū is a verbal-noun clause serving as subject of khayrun lakum.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:280/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The author prefers kāna as a complete verb meaning ‘occurred’ or ‘came to exist,’ taking the indefinite dhū ʿusratin as its subject; it often appears this way with an indefinite subject.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:280/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | reports | The Shaykh says the accusative reading or muʿsiran may be specific to usury debtors, while the nominative is general to all debtors.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:280/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The author rejects the suggestion that the ruling is limited to usury debtors: even if the passage arose in that context, its legal scope is not restricted to them.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:280/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | A second analysis makes kāna defective with an omitted predicate, such as ‘a debtor who owes you a right’; some Kufans specify that the person is one of the creditors’ debtors.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:280/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | rejects | The Shaykh says Basran grammarians do not allow an omitted predicate of kāna, because its predicate is doubly required as both a predicate and a complement of the preceding verb.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:280/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | holds | The Shaykh answers the counterexample from laysa by saying it alone permits omission because it resembles lā; the predicate of other kāna-type verbs is doubly demanded and cannot be deleted.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:280/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · عبد الله وأبي وعثمان | reports | The readings attributed to ʿAbd Allāh, Ubayy, and ʿUthmān use the accusative dhā ʿusratin, supporting the Kufan parse in which the debtor is an implicit subject.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:280/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو علي | reports | Abū ʿAlī supplies an implicit huwa as kāna’s subject, understood as the debtor from the prior discourse about a lender and borrower.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:280/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الأعمش | reports | Al-Aʿmash reads in kāna muʿsiran; al-Dānī reports from Aḥmad ibn Mūsā that this wording is in ʿAbd Allāh’s codex.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:280/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الجمهور | prefers | The majority retain the familiar nominative reading; Makkī says it applies to any insolvent person, whereas accusative dhā would identify a particular debtor or purchaser.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:280/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الواحدي | reports | Al-Wāḥidī explains that nominative dhū ʿusratin means any insolvent person; the accusative would make the respite specific to the purchaser, though anyone in debt is entitled to it.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p717#2/r1] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | God commands creditors to give an insolvent debtor time until he can pay, unlike the pre-Islamic creditor's demand to pay at once or increase the debt through usury.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p717#2/r2] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | The verse encourages a creditor to waive the entire principal and promises great good and reward for doing so.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p717#2/r3] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · رسول الله | reports | Abu Umama reports that whoever wants God's shade on the day without shade should ease an insolvent debtor's burden or waive the debt.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p717#2/r4] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · رسول الله | reports | Burayda reports that before a debt comes due, each day of delay earns charity equal to the debt; after it falls due, each day of delay earns double that amount.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p717#2/r5] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · رسول الله | reports | Abu Qatada found his debtor hiding because he was insolvent; he wept and reported the Prophet's promise of the Throne's shade for one who relieves or erases a debtor's obligation. Muslim records it.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p718/r1] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · رسول الله | reports | A man who eased solvent buyers and delayed insolvent ones is told by God that He is more entitled to ease and is admitted to Paradise; Bukhari, Muslim, and Ibn Majah transmit it, with Muslim adding Uqba ibn Amir and Abu Masud to the report.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p718/r2] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · رسول الله | reports | A merchant instructed his servants to overlook insolvent buyers in hope that God would overlook him, and God forgave him.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p718/r3] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · رسول الله | reports | Sahl ibn Hunayf reports that helping a fighter, an insolvent debtor, or an enslaved person buying freedom earns God's shade on the day without shade; al-Hakim calls its chain sound and says the two collectors did not include it.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p718/r4] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | reports | Al-Hakim grades the Sahl ibn Hunayf report's chain as sound and notes that Bukhari and Muslim did not record it.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p718/r5] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · رسول الله | reports | Ibn Umar reports that someone seeking an answered prayer and relief from distress should ease an insolvent debtor; Ahmad alone transmits the report.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p718/r6] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | reports | Ibn Kathir notes that Ahmad alone transmits the Ibn Umar report.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p719/r1] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · رسول الله | reports | Abu Masud reports a man who could name no good deed except easing solvent buyers and delaying insolvent ones; God says He is more entitled to do so and forgives him. Muslim also transmits this through Abu Malik Saad ibn Tariq.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p719/r2] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | reports | Ibn Kathir says the route for the Imran ibn Husayn report is unusual and notes that a similar report from Burayda was given earlier.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p719/r3] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · رسول الله | reports | Imran ibn Husayn reports that each day a creditor delays a debt owed by someone earns charity equal to the debt.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p719/r4] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · رسول الله | reports | Abu al-Yusr reports that delaying an insolvent debtor or waiving the debt earns God's shade on the day without shade; Muslim transmits another route through Ubada ibn al-Walid.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p719/r5] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · أبو اليسر | reports | When his debtor admitted insolvency and explained he feared lying or breaking a promise, Abu al-Yusr erased the debt record and released him if unable to pay, then testified that he heard the Prophet promise divine shade for delaying or waiving a debt.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p720/r1] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · رسول الله | reports | Uthman reports that God shades a person who delays an insolvent debtor or releases a debtor from what is owed.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p720/r2] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · رسول الله | reports | Ibn Abbas reports promises for delaying or waiving an insolvent person's debt, including protection from Hell; the report also says Paradise's deeds are arduous, Hell's deeds easy, the fortunate are protected from trials, and restraining anger fills the heart with faith.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p720/r3] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · رسول الله | reports | A second Ibn Abbas report says that God will grant respite regarding a debtor's sin until the debtor repents if the creditor delays payment until the debtor can pay.
[BURHAN:v1p15/r1] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | holds | Because early authorities’ full interpretation was not transmitted, later readers need tafsīr even more: they lack direct command of the language and must learn it.
[BURHAN:v1p15/r2] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | holds | Tafsīr expands concise wording, uncovers meanings, and weighs possible readings in light of eloquence; it therefore needs a general method covering vocabulary, constructions, context, and outward and inward senses.
[BURHAN:v1p15/r3] BURHAN, d. 794 · القاضي شمس الدين الخويى | reports | Al-Khūʾī calls tafsīr difficult because definitive knowledge of divine intent normally requires hearing it from the Prophet, which is unavailable except for a few verses; interpreters infer by signs and proofs.
[BURHAN:v1p15/r4] BURHAN, d. 794 · القاضي شمس الدين الخويى | reports | Al-Khūʾī says God intended people to reflect on the Qur’an rather than receive every meaning explicitly; the Prophet’s approval of several exegetes proves interpretation without direct transmission permissible.
[BURHAN:v1p15/r5] BURHAN, d. 794 · القاضي شمس الدين الخويى | reports | Al-Khūʾī criticizes writers who boast of not reading earlier books: without comparison they cannot judge the merit of their own or others’ work; good comes through God and study of the righteous, while weakness comes from the self.
[BURHAN:v1p283/r1] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | reports | The familiar position is that the Qur’an was revealed in the Quraysh dialect.
[BURHAN:v1p283/r10] BURHAN, d. 794 · أبو عمر بن عبد البر | reports | Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr reads “in Quraysh dialect” as predominant usage, since other dialect features such as hamza appear in the Qur’an although Quraysh do not pronounce it; he also cites Ibn ʿAbbās on seven aḥruf, five from Hawāzin.
[BURHAN:v1p283/r2] BURHAN, d. 794 · أبو الأسود الدؤلى | reports | Abū al-Aswad says it was revealed in the speech of the two Kaʿbs, ancestors of Quraysh and Khuzāʿa; Khālid ibn Salama instead names both tribes because they shared a home.
[BURHAN:v1p283/r3] BURHAN, d. 794 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says it came in the language of the two Kaʿbs; Abū ʿUbayd explains that Khuzāʿa, as Quraysh’s neighbors, adopted their language.
[BURHAN:v1p283/r4] BURHAN, d. 794 · الكلبى عن أبى صالح عن ابن عباس | reports | Al-Kalbī reports from Ibn ʿAbbās that the Qur’an was revealed in seven languages, five of them from lower Hawāzin; Abū ʿUbayd identifies those tribes and Abū ʿAmr calls upper Hawāzin and lower Tamīm the most eloquent.
