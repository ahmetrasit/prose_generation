[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p356/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | For brevity, the oath’s answer states only the reassurance the Prophet most needed: his present hardship does not mean God is indifferent or displeased.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p357/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The Prophet feared that his own mistakes had intensified Quraysh hostility and angered God; waiting for revelation could deepen the worry, though revelation comes according to divine wisdom. | mentions 94:2, 94:3
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p357/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | This assurance addresses the Prophet’s private anxiety about his mission and revelation, a concern also discussed in Sūrah Ṭāhā and Sūrah al-Qiyāmah.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p357/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | rejects | Islahi rejects the claim that the verse answered a disbelievers’ taunt: they denied revelation and could not know of any pause in it, while the Prophet never stopped preaching.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:93:1/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The oath refutes the polytheists' claim that revelation had stopped and God had abandoned the Prophet.
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH:p91/r1] SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH · author | holds | In 93:3, directly naming the Prophet in ما ودعك honors him, while omitting him as the object of قلى avoids directly associating him with hatred; mention and omission both honor him.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p1098/r3] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | Mentioning the object in ما ودعك honors the Prophet because farewell is between beloved people; omitting it after وما قلى avoids directly naming him as the object of hatred.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p411/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | The object of “forsake” is explicit, but the object of “despise” is omitted as a courtesy: speakers avoid even negating hurtful conduct toward someone they honor.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p411/r2] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | Naming the object with the favorable act of farewell and omitting it with the negative act teaches Muslims respectful speech; the verse honors the Prophet twice.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p411/r3] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | Calling God “your Lord” reassures the Prophet: a Lord nurtures and cares for his servant, and the term conveys the Prophet's particular relationship and protection.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p541/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | God names the Prophet in denying the loving act of farewell, honoring him, but omits the object after قلى so the beloved Prophet is not directly cast as hated.
[STUDYQURAN:93:3/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | This verse is the response to the oath.
[STUDYQURAN:93:3/r2] STUDYQURAN · most | reports | Most supply “thee” as the implied object of “despise,” though the Arabic omits it; the clause can instead generalize that despising is not in God's nature.
[STUDYQURAN:93:intro/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | Al-Ḍuḥā is early Makkan and ranked fourth by many, seventh by others; it may be the first sūrah revealed whole, though others give that distinction to al-Fātiḥah.
[STUDYQURAN:93:intro/r3] STUDYQURAN · several commentators | reports | One occasion account says revelation paused for twelve, forty, or fifty days, after which an idolater taunted Muhammad that his satan had forsaken him, prompting reassurance.
[STUDYQURAN:93:intro/r4] STUDYQURAN · others | reports | Another account says idolaters declared that Muhammad's Lord had forsaken and despised him.
[STUDYQURAN:93:intro/r5] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | A further account says illness kept the Prophet from night vigil for two or three nights; Umm Jamīl, Abū Lahab's wife, taunted him, and vv. 1–3 were revealed. | mentions 111:1, 93:1, 93:2, 93:3
[STUDYQURAN:93:intro/r6] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The sūrah consoles the Prophet: it assures him God will not abandon him, recalls God's favors, and ends by urging care for the downtrodden and preaching the message.
[STUDYQURAN:94:intro/r3] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | Some say Sūrahs 93 and 94 were originally one, since Companions recited them together in one prayer cycle without a basmalah separating them. | mentions 1:1
[STUDYQURAN:94:intro/r5] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Both sūrahs present God's bounties as relief: Sūrah 93 addresses external needs, Sūrah 94 spiritual relief; 94 recalls blessings, reassures, then commands exertion and turning to God.
