[TABATABAI:93:1-11/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · علي | reports | Through Muhammad ibn al-Hanafiyya, ʿAli reports the Prophet affirming that the intercession discussed by the Iraqis is true; the excerpt breaks before the rest of the report.
[TABATABAI:93:3-7/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The promise of a gift that will satisfy him reaffirms that the afterlife is better; it promises an unrestricted gift followed by unrestricted satisfaction.
[TABATABAI:93:3-7/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | Another view says the promise looks to both lives rather than only the afterlife.
[BINTSHATI:v1p36/r1] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | The surah is among the earliest revelations and came in Mecca years before the Hijra.
[BINTSHATI:v1p36/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | The apparent pause in revelation was natural, like night settling after the dawn's brightness.
[BINTSHATI:v1p36/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | rejects | The Quran gives neither a cause nor duration for the pause; proposed explanations and counts of twelve to forty days add nothing to its central meaning.
[BINTSHATI:v1p36/r5] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | The promised good confirms that God has not abandoned or hated the Prophet and comforts him after the pause.
[BINTSHATI:v1p38/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | Verse 5 completes the reassurance: God did not leave the Prophet in the past, and his future is better.
[BINTSHATI:v1p38/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | prefers | The promised gift is deliberately left unrestricted; the Prophet needs only the gift that satisfies him, and commentators should not define it by their own preferences.
[BINTSHATI:v1p38/r5] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · ابن عباس | reports | Al-Tabari transmits from Ibn Abbas that the promised gift is a palace in Paradise with spouses and servants; later exegetes add material details, which the author declines to impose.
[BINTSHATI:v1p39/r1] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · ابن عباس | reports | A reported interpretation specifies a thousand pearl palaces whose earth is musk, with another report describing them as white pearls.
[BINTSHATI:v1p39/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | rejects | Fixing the gift at a thousand or a million palaces, of any jewel or soil, constrains the Quran's promise, whose wording extends until complete satisfaction.
[BINTSHATI:v1p39/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · آخرون | reports | Others read the gift as victory over enemies, the conquest of Mecca, mass conversion, and major conquests by the caliphs.
[BINTSHATI:v1p39/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · قيل | reports | Another view identifies the gift as intercession and forgiveness for the Prophet's followers, supported by reports about intercession and his satisfaction when their requests are answered.
[BINTSHATI:v1p39/r5] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · إبن القيم | rejects | Ibn al-Qayyim rejects the claim that the Prophet cannot be satisfied while any follower remains in Hell: he accepts God's judgment and intercedes only with divine permission.
[BINTSHATI:v1p39/r6] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · إبن القيم | prefers | Ibn al-Qayyim instead takes the gift broadly to include Quran, guidance, followers, renown, victory, and what Muhammad receives after death.
[BINTSHATI:v1p40/r1] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الشيخ محمد عبده | rejects | Muhammad Abduh condemns importing discussions of intercession and the Prophet's family into this tafsir as alien to the Quran's religious spirit.
[BINTSHATI:v1p40/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الشيخ محمد عبده | reports | Abduh explains the gift as continuing revelation, guidance for Muhammad and his people, the spread of Islam, and their elevation in both worlds.
[BINTSHATI:v1p40/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | prefers | Specifying the gift does the verse an injustice; the grandeur of the promise is best preserved by leaving satisfaction beyond every limit and description.
[BINTSHATI:v1p40/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الزمخشري | reports | To solve the grammatical objection to the laam before sawfa, al-Zamakhshari posits an omitted subject: the original construction would say that you will be given.
[BINTSHATI:v1p40/r5] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · أبو حيان | reports | Abu Hayyan likewise calls the laam an introductory emphatic particle and supplies an omitted subject, أنت.
[BINTSHATI:v1p41/r1] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | rejects | Grammatical rules should be tested against the Quranic expression as the primary witness, rather than forcing an imagined original wording onto it.
[BINTSHATI:v1p41/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · مفسرون | reports | Some commentators ask how emphatic laam can accompany the future-marking sawfa, then answer that the gift is certain even if delayed for a benefit.
[BINTSHATI:v1p41/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الشيخ محمد عبده | reports | Abduh links sawfa to the fact that completion of the religion took twenty years, citing the verse announcing that Islam was completed. | mentions 5:3
[BINTSHATI:v1p41/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | prefers | Emphasis on the future is ordinary Arabic; sawfa reassures Muhammad that God's care would continue through his past and future, his earlier and later life.
[CORPUSCORANICUM:93:5:anmerkung/r1] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | holds | The root r-ḍ-y has eschatological connotations, while the verse also resembles Psalm 20:5’s non-eschatological wish that God grant one’s desire and fulfill one’s plans.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p353/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The discourse moves from day and night as models of developmental trials, to reassurance, promised better times, examples of past divine help, and duties arising from those favours.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p354/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The future success promised to the Prophet is followed by reminders of earlier deliverance and spiritual blessing, then guidance to fulfil the duties those favours impose.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p358/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The unstated object of “give” is the promised good of verse 4; it is suppressed because the triumphs were still future, while the wording promises the Prophet’s pleasure.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:93:4/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | prefers | The author prefers the first reading: against claims that revelation had stopped and Muhammad's cause would fail, God promises his future prophetic work will be spiritually and practically more fruitful.
