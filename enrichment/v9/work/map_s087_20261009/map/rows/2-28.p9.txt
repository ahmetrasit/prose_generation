[ALUSI-FULL:v1p212/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · ابن مالك | reports | Ibn Mālik says no one calls كيف an adverb because it denotes neither place nor time; Ibn Hishām approves calling it one metaphorically because it means “in what state.”
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p212/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | كيف is mostly interrogative and only rarely conditional; jussive use is unattested, though Kufans and Quṭrub allow it by analogy, and Ibn Mawhab’s claim that it can coordinate is dismissed.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p212/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Here كيف combines a question with denial, astonishment, and rebuke, asking how they can disbelieve whether ignorant of God or aware of Him.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p212/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | If they know the account of their origin and end, they know a perfect Creator; acting despite this powerful deterrent is astonishing, and the wording charges them with obstinate disbelief.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p212/r8] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | كيف intensifies the rebuke beyond a plain “you disbelieve”: denying any state that must accompany existence implies, by proof, denial of the disbeliever’s existence; it can also negate every conceivable state of disbelief.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p212/r9] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Divine questioning need not imply ignorance: a request for information may alert or rebuke the hearer; the interrogative can seek that understanding occur in someone who lacks it, without combining literal and figurative meanings.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p213/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The clause before ثم is a circumstantial clause attached to the subject of تكفرون, with قد necessarily understood; it means “how can you disbelieve when He created you?”
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p213/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Creation is a state that should prevent disbelief because human nature and reason are disposed to recognize that God alone creates.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p213/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | The clauses after the first may be resumptive rather than part of the circumstantial clause, since their conjunctions and forms differ; a stronger alternative treats the whole account as the state known to the addressees.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p213/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | Life is defined as a power arising from species-level equilibrium that gives rise to other powers; another definition is sensory capacity, though paralysis shows absent sensation can result from an impediment rather than absent life.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p213/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | Those defining life by sensation likely mean touch: different senses belong to different organs, some animals lack particular senses, and identifying life with each would multiply or compose life in one individual.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p213/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Life is also used figuratively for growth and for human perfections such as intellect, knowledge, and faith; death is the opposite at each level, while divine life denotes God’s perfection in knowledge and power.
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p213/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · ابن عباس | reports | A report from Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd, and Mujāhid takes the first death as prior nonexistence, the first life as creation, the second death as ordinary death, and the final life as resurrection.

[ALUSI-FULL:v1p213/r9] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | Another view takes “dead” as the embryonic stages until ensoulment, the first life as breathing of the spirit after those stages, and the later death and resurrection in their ordinary senses.

[ALUSI-FULL:v1p214#2/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · فارس | reports | Fāris reads the stages as moving from the servants’ witnessing to God’s witnessing, then their own witnessing dies away until they return wholly to God.
