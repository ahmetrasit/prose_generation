[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r3#3/r13] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · author | holds | The verse pairs the form V verb tabattal with the form II verbal noun tabtīlan rather than the expected form V verbal noun tabattulan.
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r3#3/r14] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · author | holds | Form V conveys gradual, effortful progress, while form II conveys multiplication and intensity; using both captures both meanings concisely.
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r3#3/r15] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · author | holds | Examples such as kassar versus kasara and qattaʿa versus qataʿa show that form II can mark repeated or intensive action, unlike the basic form.
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r3#3/r16] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · التفسير القيم | reports | Tafsīr al-Qayyim explains that the verb supplies gradual effort and the verbal noun supplies intensification, so both commands are understood together.
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r3#3/r17] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · author | holds | The sequence is pedagogical: begin gradually in devotion, then build toward abundance; the verb conveys this developing stage and the verbal noun the enduring devotional state.
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH:p114/r1] SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH · author | holds | Instead of the expected verbal noun تبتل, the verse uses تبتيلاً from another form: تفعّل conveys gradual effort, while فعّل conveys multiplication and abundance.
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH:p114/r2] SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH · author | holds | The wording teaches worshippers to begin with gradual, manageable devotion and then increase it, since steady progression leads to abundant worship.
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH:p114/r3] SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH · author | holds | The verb form marks renewal and occurrence, while the nominal form marks stability, adding another deliberate rhetorical contrast.
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH:p114/r4] SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH · author | holds | Arabs grasped such eloquence naturally but could not produce this Qur'anic formulation, which the author presents as challenge and inimitability.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p563/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | Although the expected verbal noun is تبتّلاً, the verse pairs the fifth-form verb with a second-form verbal noun to combine the implications of both patterns.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p563/r2] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | The fifth form conveys gradualness and effort; the second form conveys multiplication, as in breaking or cutting something into repeated pieces, so تبتيلاً adds intensification.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p563/r3] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | The wording teaches a gradual path in worship: begin with manageable devotion and increase toward abundant worship instead of taking on too much immediately.
