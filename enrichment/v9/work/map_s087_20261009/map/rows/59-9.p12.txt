[ELMALILI:59:9#2/r16] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Câbir b. Abdillah | reports | A Prophetic report from Jabir warns against injustice and shuhh: shuhh destroyed earlier peoples by driving them to bloodshed and treating forbidden things as lawful.
[ELMALILI:59:9#2/r17] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Protection from shuhh does not require a person to be generous in every matter.
[ELMALILI:59:9#2/r18] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Mücemmi‘ b. Yahyâ | reports | A Prophetic report says one is free of shuhh by paying zakat, hosting a guest, and helping during a calamity.
[ELMALILI:59:9#2/r19] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Hazret-i Ali | reports | Ali says that whoever pays zakat on his wealth is protected from the shuhh of his soul.
[ELMALILI:59:9#2/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The cited occasions are only examples; the Ansar and Companions displayed many such acts, including men with two wives giving one up so an unmarried Muhajir could marry.
[ELMALILI:59:9#2/r20] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Ebû Heyyâc-ı Esedî | reports | Abu Hayyaj al-Asadi reports meeting a man at the Kaaba who only prayed for protection from shuhh, explaining that such protection would keep him from theft, adultery, and wrongdoing; the man was Abdurrahman ibn Awf.
[ELMALILI:59:9#2/r21] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İbn Zeyd | reports | Ibn Zayd says one is protected from shuhh by neither taking what God forbids nor letting it prevent one from fulfilling what God commands; such a person is successful.
[ELMALILI:59:9#2/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | At Yarmuk, dying wounded martyrs passed even a sip of water to a groaning companion; Akif's Safahat beautifully depicts the sacred historical episode.
[ELMALILI:59:9#2/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Those protected from the soul's shuhh—greed, envy, and stinginess—are the truly successful, escaping every harm and attaining their aims; this is both an ethical principle and glad tidings for the Ansar and their imitators.
[ELMALILI:59:9#2/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Shuhh is an ingrained disposition of greed, envy, and miserliness; buhl is its practical manifestation in withholding.
[ELMALILI:59:9#2/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Râgıb | reports | Raghib defines shuhh as greed and miserliness that have become habitual.
[ELMALILI:59:9#2/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Hasan | reports | Hasan distinguishes buhl as begrudging what one owns from shuhh as begrudging what anyone else owns.
[ELMALILI:59:9#2/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İbn Mes‘ûd | reports | Ibn Masud tells a man who fears his stinginess means doom that this is buhl, not Quranic shuhh; shuhh is unjustly consuming one's brother's property.
[ELMALILI:59:9#2/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İbn Ömer | reports | Ibn Umar says shuhh is not withholding one's own property but coveting what does not belong to oneself.
