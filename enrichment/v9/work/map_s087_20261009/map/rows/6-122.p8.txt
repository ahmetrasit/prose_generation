[TABATABAI:6:122-123/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The unbelievers' deeds have been adorned to attract and imprison them in darkness, preventing escape into happiness and light; God does not guide wrongdoers.
[TABATABAI:6:122-123/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | rejects | A reported reading compares these unbelievers, whose disbelief was adorned, with the earlier believers whose faith was adorned; al-Tabataba'i finds it far from context.
[TABATABAI:6:122-123/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | On this reading, adornment explains why their deeds cannot free them from darkness, while the following verse explains that their plotting harms themselves rather than others.
[TABATABAI:6:122-123/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | reports | Another view says God made the disbelievers' deeds attractive and town criminals plot just as He made believers bear light; believers chose guidance, while criminals chose error and were enabled to plot.
[TABATABAI:6:122-127#2/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · أبو جعفر عليه السلام | reports | In al-Kafi, Zayd says Imam al-Baqir glossed the dead person as one who knows nothing, the light as an imam followed, and the person in darkness as one who does not know the Imam.
[TABATABAI:6:122-127#2/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Al-Tabataba'i treats the Imam gloss as an application to a particular case; the context requires life to mean faith and light to mean divine guidance to true belief and righteous action.
[TABATABAI:6:122-127#2/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · زيد بن أسلم | reports | Al-Durr al-Manthur cites Zayd ibn Aslam as saying the verse was revealed about Ammar ibn Yasir; no grading is stated.
[TABATABAI:6:122-127#2/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · ابن عباس وزيد بن أسلم | rejects | Al-Durr al-Manthur reports from Ibn Abbas and Zayd ibn Aslam that the verse concerned Umar ibn al-Khattab and Abu Jahl; al-Tabataba'i says the context rules out restricting it to them.
[TABATABAI:6:122-127/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Guidance is of two kinds: creational direction of each kind and individual toward its created perfection, acts, appointed course, and lifespan; the Qur'an describes God as giving each thing its form and then guiding it. | mentions 20:50, 87:2, 87:3
[TABATABAI:6:122-127/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Legislative guidance concerns true beliefs and righteous acts prescribed through commands and prohibitions, with reward promised for obedience and punishment threatened for neglect.
[TABATABAI:6:122-127/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Legislative guidance can show the road, as in 76:3, or bring someone to the destination, as in 7:176. | mentions 76:3, 7:176
[TABATABAI:6:122/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | At the plain level, the verse compares believer and unbeliever: before divine guidance a person is like the dead, while accepted faith revives and gives a light that distinguishes good from evil and benefit from harm.
[TABATABAI:6:122/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The unbeliever resembles someone trapped without escape in death’s darkness and in ignorance about distinguishing good from evil and benefit from harm.
[TABATABAI:6:122/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | For ordinary hearers, death represents misguidance, life faith or guidance, revival guidance to faith, light insight into good deeds, and darkness ignorance; the comparison uses familiar animal life as its frame.
[TABATABAI:6:122/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Verse 6:36 parallels the contrast between those who hear and the dead whom God will raise. | mentions 6:36
[TABATABAI:6:122/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Verse 16:97 similarly promises a good life to a believing man or woman who does righteous deeds. | mentions 16:97
[TABATABAI:6:122/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | On a deeper Qurʾānic reading, the divinely guided person has an everlasting life beyond worldly death, protected by God, free of fatigue and hardship, immersed in divine love, secure and endlessly happy.
[TABATABAI:6:122/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Because of this higher life, the believer sees, hears, understands, and wills what others do not; inward awareness and volition exceed theirs even when outward actions look alike.
[ASAD-NOTES:6:122#n108/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · all commentators | reports | Commentators agree that the one who was dead is metaphorical: a person made spiritually alive through faith who can then navigate life without error.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p187/r1] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · author | holds | The bāʾ in a negated predicate of laysa gives a different, firm denial from bare laysa, which may be uttered without full conviction; the passage lists Quranic examples of the bāʾ construction. | mentions 3:182, 8:51, 22:10, 41:46, 5:116, 6:66, 6:89, 15:20, 46:32, 58:10

[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-3-surah-anam-06:p115/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The parable contrasts believers given faith and scripture with people wandering in superstition; it encourages believers to ignore human and jinn devils’ clamour and carry the message of life forward.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-3-surah-anam-06:p115/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Those given God’s light must seek guidance from it and present it publicly so others can benefit; light is meant to illuminate people’s path, not be hidden.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-3-surah-anam-06:p115/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The statement that disbelievers’ deeds seem fair reassures the Prophet not to despair over those who choose darkness; people are surrounded by what they like, a law discussed further at 6:125 and the beginning of al-Baqarah. | mentions 6:125, 2:7
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:6:122/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | After 6:121 describes polytheists fighting believers under satanic prompting, this verse contrasts the two groups through a vivid parable. | mentions 6:121

[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:6:122/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The person granted Islam is revived and gains light in heart and mind to distinguish truth from falsehood and benefit from harm, which also lights the way.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:6:122/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | For one fixed in disbelief, there is no hope of escaping darkness while disbelief continues.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:6:122/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | As 6:121 indicates, satanic suggestions undermine disbelievers’ reasoning and evaluation until their bad deeds appear attractive to them. | mentions 6:121
