[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p339/r10] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن الأنباري | reports | A further explanation is that God changes what He places in hearts, turning fear into security or security into fear.

[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p339/r2] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās, through al-ʿAwfī, and al-Ḍaḥḥāk and al-Farrāʾ say God can separate a believer from disobedience and an unbeliever from obedience.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p339/r3] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid says God comes between a person and his heart so that he cannot reason; Ibn al-Anbārī urges prompt action because reason may disappear before one can secure the fruits of deeds.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p339/r4] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · قتادة | reports | Qatāda understands the clause to mean God is near to a person and knows every secret, as in 50:16. | mentions 50:16

[RAZI-FULL:v15p117#2/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · أكثر الفقهاء | holds | Most jurists hold that a command’s apparent force is obligation; al-Razi argues a merely recommendatory reading makes the command redundant, while the following threat confirms obligation.
[RAZI-FULL:v15p117#2/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · أبو هريرة | reports | Abu Hurayra reports that the Prophet called Ubayy ibn Kaʿb while he prayed; after Ubayy delayed, the Prophet reproached him with this verse, and Ubayy promised to answer future calls.
[RAZI-FULL:v15p117#2/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Al-Razi treats the Prophet’s rebuke of Ubayy as evidence that the verse requires answering; the command’s force is a probabilistic practical question, so a solitary report can support it.
[RAZI-FULL:v15p118#2/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · ابن عباس والضحاك | reports | As related by al-Wahidi, Ibn Abbas and al-Dahhak say God bars an unbeliever from obedience and a believer from sin; hearts are under God’s control, making people blessed or wretched as He wills.
[RAZI-FULL:v15p118#2/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Al-Razi argues that knowledge cannot be intentionally acquired without already knowing it is knowledge and its object, producing circular dependence; ignorance likewise requires prior ignorance.
[RAZI-FULL:v15p118#2/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Wills and motives cannot arise without a cause; if the human were their cause, each intention would require another intention, so God must create beliefs, wills, and motives.
[RAZI-FULL:v15p118#2/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | prefers | Al-Razi concludes that both Qur’anic wording and rational proofs show that the states of hearts come from God, affirming the determinist account.
[RAZI-FULL:v15p118#2/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · الجبائي | reports | Al-Jubba’i argues that if God prevented someone from believing, that person would be incapable and the command absurd, like commanding someone to climb the sky or a disabled person to pray standing; he invokes the capacity rule. | mentions 2:286, 58:4
[RAZI-FULL:v15p118/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The life-giving call cannot mean restoring already living people; since every divine command carries reward, the phrase applies generally to all commands.
[RAZI-FULL:v15p118/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · السدي | reports | Al-Suddi identifies the life-giving call with faith and Islam: faith revives the heart, whereas unbelief is its death; he adduces the verse about bringing the living from the dead. | mentions 30:19
[RAZI-FULL:v15p118/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · قتادة | reports | Qatada identifies the call with the Qur’an, which brings life, rescue, and protection because it causes knowledge, and knowledge is life.
[RAZI-FULL:v15p118/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · الأكثرون | reports | Most commentators identify it with jihad: weakening one enemy strengthens the Muslims; martyrdom grants lasting life; fighting can lead to the afterlife, the realm of enduring life. | mentions 3:169, 29:64
[RAZI-FULL:v15p118/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | A fourth interpretation takes the phrase as every truth and right, thus including Qur’an, faith, jihad, and all obedience; it means good, everlasting life promised to righteous workers. | mentions 16:97

[RAZI-FULL:v15p119/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · القائلون بالقدر | reports | One interpretation is that God interposes death between a person and the use of the heart; obey and repent before death arrives. Al-Qadi says the following mention of gathering supports this reading.

[RAZI-FULL:v15p119/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · القائلون بالقدر | reports | A further interpretation addresses Muslims’ fear at Badr: they should not refuse obedience because of weakness or cowardice, since God can replace those states with strength and courage as the turner of hearts.
