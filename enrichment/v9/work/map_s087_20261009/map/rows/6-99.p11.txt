[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#10/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The distinction offered for al-Kahf—ḍamma for wealth and fatḥ for edible fruit—cannot explain the readings in al-Anʿām and Yāsīn. | mentions 18:34, 36:35
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#10/r12] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | As a parallel reading example, Abu ʿAmr, al-Kisaʾi, and Qunbul read خشب, while the others use ḍamma; the author calls the former reading analogous to the one just discussed.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#10/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الأخوان، author | reports | The Brothers' double-ḍamma reading ثمر admits four analyses: a singular noun, a plural of plurals, a plural of ثمر, or a plural of ثمرة.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#10/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | One analysis treats ثمر as a singular noun, like طنب or عنق.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#10/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Another analysis makes ثمر a plural of plurals: ثمر is plural of ثمار, itself plural of ثمرة, comparable to أكم and إكام.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#10/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A third analysis treats ثمر as a plural of ثمر, by analogy with the two plural forms أسد and أسد.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#10/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الفارسي | prefers | Al-Farisi prefers reading ثمر as the plural of ثمرة, like خشبة/خشب, أكمة/أكم, and the defective pairs لابة/لوب, ناقة/نوق, ساحة/سوح.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#10/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The majority's fatḥ reading الثمر is a collective noun whose singular is ثمرة, like شجر/شجرة, بقر/بقرة, and جزر/جزرة.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#10/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The rare reading with a still second radical either eases the double-ḍamma reading or is a plural of ثمرة; with the latter analysis, ثمر parallels سمر and its broken plural is أثمار.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#10/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو عمرو | reports | Abu ʿAmr reads the Kahf occurrence with ḍamma and a still م; that is eloquent, though consistency would suggest using it here too, since recitation follows transmission. | mentions 18:34
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#11/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | إذا أثمر is a time adverb for انظروا, either purely temporal or conditional with an omitted or advanced response: when it fruits, look at it.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#11/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The majority read ينعه with fatḥa on ي and sukūn on ن; Ibn Muhaysin, also reported from Qatada and al-Dahhak, uses ḍamma, while Ibrahim ibn Abi ʿAbla and al-Yamani read يانعة.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#11/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | الينع with fatḥa or ḍamma is the verbal noun of ripening; fatḥa is Hijazi and ḍamma is used by some Najdis, with variants ينَع and ينوع.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#11/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Alternatively الينع is a plural of يانع, like تاجر/تجر and صاحب/صحب; ينعت and أينعت both mean that fruit ripened, in triliteral and quadriliteral forms.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#11/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الحجاج | reports | Al-Hajjaj's phrase رؤوسا قد أينعت وحان قطافها describes heads as ripe and ready to be harvested.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#11/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farraʾ says أينعت means the fruit turned red; the hadith phrase compares a red-born child to الينعة, a red bead said to be carnelian or a kind of it.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#11/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو عبيد | reports | Abu ʿUbayd says ينعت تينع has fatḥa in the past and kasra in the present, citing a line about olives ripening; al-Layth reverses the vowels.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#11/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The close addresses believers because the signs show one God's power to create varied products from one soil and water; only reflective believers benefit from them.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#2/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The apparent antecedent of منه is نبات; Zamakhshari gives only this option. من then marks part or point of origin.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqa also allows منه to refer to ماء, with من expressing cause.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | If منه refers to water, Abu al-Baqa treats the second فأخرجنا clause as a semantic replacement; al-Samin says strict grammatical بدل is not evident and prefers coordination.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#2/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | Al-Samin corrects the Shaykh: Abu al-Baqa’s بدل analysis depends on منه referring to water and cannot be attributed to the plant reading.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو إسحاق | reports | Abu Ishaq says خضر and أخضر both mean green, like عور and أعور.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#2/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Green lies between white and black but closer to black; thus black can denote green and vice versa, as in سواد العراق for its tree-covered greenness.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#2/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | مدهامتان in 55:64 means intensely black because of the gardens’ abundant moisture and greenness. | mentions 55:64
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#2/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | المخاضرة is selling green fruit before ripening; الخضيرة is a palm whose unripe dates fall while green.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#2/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · النبي | reports | The Prophet explains خضراء الدمن as a beautiful woman from a bad upbringing; الدمن are refuse heaps where attractive plants may grow.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الجمهور وابن محيصن والأعمش | reports | The majority read نخرج active in first-person plural; Ibn Muhaysin and al-Aʿmash read passive يخرج, with حب as its agent.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | On either reading, the clause is most naturally an adjective of خضرا, though it may be independent.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#3/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | متراكبا can be nominative or accusative as an adjective of حب, following the two case parses.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#3/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | Best parse: من النخل is a fronted predicate, من طلعها a partitive بدل with repeated preposition, and قنوان a delayed topic; the nominal clause coordinates with the prior verbal clause.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#3/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | On al-Akhfash’s alternative, قنوان is raised as subject by من النخل, with من طلعها as apposition.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#3/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A تنازع parse has both prepositional phrases seeking قنوان as subject: Basrans prefer the second governor and insert a pronoun in the first, Kufans the reverse.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#3/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqa’s other parse makes قنوان the subject governed by من طلعها, with a pronoun in من النخل; al-Samin assigns governance to the second phrase.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#3/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A further parse makes قنوان a topic, من طلعها its predicate, and posits an understood شيء or ثمر with من النخل; al-Samin rejects it because the predicate cannot also be بدل.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#3/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | If من طلعها is already the predicate, it cannot also be بدل; taking من النخل as predicate repeats the earlier parse.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#4/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | rejects | The Shaykh calls the preceding grammatical account confused.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#4/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الواحدي | reports | Al-Wahidi compares the new final ḍamma to the distinction between منصور and the vocative حار in يا حار.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#4/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الجمهور والأعمش والحباب والأعرج | reports | The majority use qaf kasra, called Hijazi; al-Aʿmash, al-Habbab from Abu ʿAmr, and al-Aʿraj use ḍamma. Al-Farra assigns it to Qays and says kasra is more common; Ibn ʿAtiyya reverses the Hijazi label.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#4/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Zamakhshari makes قنوان a topic with its predicate omitted and inferred from أخرجنا; the Shaykh and al-Samin find this unnecessary because the clause already informs independently.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#4/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAtiyya links من النخل to an omitted verb and makes من طلعها قنوان a nominal clause functioning as the object of نخرج.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#4/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | rejects | A verb taking one object cannot take a whole clause unless it licenses clausal complements and a blocker prevents governance within it; نخرج has neither condition.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#4/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | holds | If the verb governed a word inside the nominal clause, قنوان would have to be accusative as its object.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#4/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | reports | With passive يخرج, the palm clause may coordinate with the passive clause; the Shaykh prefers the alternative topic-predicate parse.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#4/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | قنوان is the broken plural of قنو, like صنوان from صنو; قنو is a date cluster or stalk, also called كباسة.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#4/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Imruʾ al-Qays uses قنو النخلة المتعثكل in a foliage simile and elsewhere uses قنوانا for red dates.
