لكتاب means the Torah’s rule that a grave sinner is forgiven only through repentance, أن لا يقولوا is a purpose clause meaning so that they not say.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169#3/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | أن may explain the covenant as a statement; on that analysis لا is prohibitive and makes the verb jussive, while the other analyses take لا as negative and أن as a subjunctive particle.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169#3/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | الحق may be a direct object or cognate accusative; ميثاق is annexed to الكتاب because the covenant is mentioned in the book.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169#3/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | prefers | The most apparent parsing coordinates درسوا with ألم يؤخذ: the rhetorical question asserts that they took the covenant and studied, like 26:18. | mentions 26:18
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169#3/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Another analysis coordinates درسوا with ورثوا, with ألم يؤخذ as an intervening parenthesis; al-Baqāʾ reports that earlier authorities including al-Ṭabarī held it.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169#3/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A third analysis supplies قد before درسوا, making it circumstantial: they claim forgiveness while studying the book’s ban on bribes, or take the gain while studying it.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169#4/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Under either preceding syntactic analysis, the interrogative is parenthetical.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169#4/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الجحدري | reports | Al-Jahdari reads the clause with second-person address; the author calls this a pleasing shift in person.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169#4/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · علي وأبو عبد الرحمن السلمي | reports | Ali and Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami read وادارسوا with a doubled dal, derived from تدارسوا like فادارأتم. | mentions 2:72
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169#4/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عامر ونافع وحفص | reports | Ibn Amir, Nafi, and Hafs read تعملون in address; the other readers use third-person الغيبة.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169#4/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The address reading may shift from third person while retaining the same referents for the pronouns.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169#4/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Alternatively, the address is to this community: do you not understand and marvel at the earlier people's condition?
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169#4/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | reports | The Shaykh assigns the third-person reading to Abu Amr and the Meccans, and the address reading to the remaining readers.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | ورثوا is a nominative adjective describing خلف, while يأخذون is a circumstantial clause describing the subject of ورثوا.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الحسن البصري | reports | Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī reads ورثوا passively with a damma on wāw and doubled rāʾ; يأخذون may then begin a new sentence.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ distinguishes خلف with quiescent lām for a succeeding generation from خلف with open lām for one appointed as successor.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjāj says خلف denotes a generation that comes after another.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ثعلب | reports | Thaʿlab reports common usage: خلف صدق is a good successor and خلف سوء a bad one; the anonymous verse with في خلف witnesses the latter.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A proverb and anonymous verse use خلف with open lām for a successor; other cited lines show disagreement over the vowels for good and bad successors.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · حسان | reports | Ḥassān’s line uses خلف with quiescent lām for a good successor, contrary to the distinction that reserves that form for the bad.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · النضر | reports | Al-Naḍr allows both vowels for a bad successor but says the good takes open lām; most linguists agree, unlike al-Farrāʾ and Abū ʿUbayd, who allow quiescent lām for good.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن الأنباري | reports | The quiescent form may be a verbal noun, or a collective noun meaning خالف like ركب for راكب; the author says the latter is challenged because it inflects like a singular.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:169/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The noun may derive from succession or from wine going bad; the same root describes changed mouth odor, as in the fasting hadith.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p498#2/r1] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · السدي | reports | Al-Suddi says Israelite judges repeatedly took bribes despite a mutual covenant; each justified himself by saying God would forgive, while later appointees repeated the same practice.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p498#2/r2] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | Ibn Kathir links the rebuke to the covenant requiring the People of the Book to make scripture clear and not conceal it, as in 3:187. | mentions 3:187
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p498#2/r3] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas explains their false claim that God would forgive as asserting forgiveness for sins they repeatedly commit without repenting.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p498/r4] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | A later, bad generation inherited study of the Torah after the mixed earlier generation; Mujāhid identifies them as Christians, though Ibn Kathīr allows that the description may be broader.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p498/r5] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | They traded conveying the truth for worldly gain, indulged themselves, and promised themselves repentance, then repeated the same wrongdoing when an equivalent opportunity came.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p498/r6] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · سعيد بن جبير | reports | Saʿīd ibn Jubayr says they commit a sin, ask God to forgive it, then commit it again when the same opportunity presents itself.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p498/r7] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid says they took any worldly gain that came their way, lawful or unlawful, while hoping for forgiveness, and took it again if offered another time.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p498/r8] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · قتادة | reports | Qatāda calls them a wicked generation that inherited scripture after the prophets and messengers, although God entrusted it to them; he links them to those who neglected prayer and followed desires. | mentions 19:59
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p499/r1] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | The afterlife and God’s reward are better for those who avoid prohibitions, resist their desires, and obey their Lord; the verse both encourages reward and warns of punishment.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p499/r2] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | The rebuke asks whether those trading the afterlife for worldly gain lack reason to restrain their foolishness and waste.
[BURHAN:v1p98/r1] BURHAN, d. 794 · أم سلمة | reports | Abū Dāwūd transmits from Umm Salama that the Prophet recited verse by verse and paused at each, including the basmala, to teach the verse endings.
[BURHAN:v1p98/r2] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | holds | Zarkashī rejects calling this simply a Prophetic Sunnah pause: consistent pauses establish an ending and consistent linkage excludes one, while occasional pauses may teach endpoints, mark a full stop, or allow rest.
[BURHAN:v1p98/r3] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | holds | A second method analogically assigns plausible verse boundaries not explicitly transmitted; it changes only pausing or linking, neither adding to nor subtracting from the Qur'an.
[BURHAN:v1p98/r4] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | holds | Verse endings resemble prose rhyme and poetic line endings: ordinary rhyme defects do not apply, and endings may shift rhyme types in Qur'an, rhymed prose, and rajaz unlike the ode.
