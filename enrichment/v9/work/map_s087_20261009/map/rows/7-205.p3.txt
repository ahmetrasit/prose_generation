[RAZI-FULL:v15p86/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Remembrance in the prescribed inward, humble manner best completes its benefit because it is closer to sincerity and entreaty.
[RAZI-FULL:v15p86/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Remembering God inwardly means knowing the meanings of spoken invocations and bringing to mind His perfection, might, exaltedness, majesty, and greatness.
[RAZI-FULL:v15p86/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Verbal remembrance without awareness in the heart is fruitless, just as uttering 'I sold' or 'I bought' without understanding those words does not effect a sale.
[RAZI-FULL:v15p86/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | Rāzī recounts a spiritual master's method: after forty days of retreat and purification, the disciple hears the ninety-nine names and identifies which most moves the heart; steady repetition of that name may open disclosures.
[RAZI-FULL:v15p87/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Entreaty is an essential condition of remembrance, supported by the Qur'anic description of people calling on God in distress humbly and privately. | mentions 6:63
[RAZI-FULL:v15p87/r10] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · أبو بكر الواسطي | reports | Abū Bakr al-Wāsiṭī says gratitude is shirk; Rāzī suggests he means that claiming to repay God's gifts with thanks makes the servant seem an independent giver, whereas humble gratitude reveals servitude.
[RAZI-FULL:v15p87/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Human completion requires awareness of divine lordship, expressed by inward remembrance, and witnessing one's servile lowliness, expressed by entreaty; movement between them resembles descent and ascent on a spiritual ascent.
[RAZI-FULL:v15p87/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | One answer to why these are separately commanded is that knowledge does not always entail humility and fear: someone may reason that God will not punish because punishment harms another without benefiting Him.
[RAZI-FULL:v15p87/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | Another answer distinguishes fear of punishment, characteristic of beginners, from fear of divine majesty, characteristic of realized knowers and intensified by greater knowledge.
[RAZI-FULL:v15p87/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | A further answer says those granted unveilings experience both beauty and majesty: beauty sustains them, majesty overwhelms them, so remembrance must honor both aspects.
