[TABRISI:2:185#6/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · النبي | reports | In a sermon reported by al-Bāqir through al-Ḥasan ibn Maḥbūb, Abū Ayyūb, and Abū al-Ward, the Prophet describes Ramadan's night better than a thousand months, obligatory fast, and voluntary night prayer worth seventy months' prayers; good deeds equal obligatory deeds elsewhere and an obligatory deed equals seventy. He calls it a month of patience, mutual aid, and increased provision; feeding a faster brings emancipation and forgiveness, even a sip of milk, water, or a few dates suffices, and easing a servant's work eases one's reckoning. He describes its beginning as mercy, middle as forgiveness, and end as answered prayer and release from fire; he urges testimony, asking for needs and Paradise, and seeking refuge from Hell.
[TABRISI:2:185#6/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · سلمان الفارسي | reports | Salman al-Fārisī's version urges four practices: the testimony of faith and seeking forgiveness please God, while asking for Paradise and refuge from Hell are indispensable.
