God inspires bees to follow in making honey, or their easy return routes from distant feeding grounds without getting lost.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p262#3/r3] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | reports | The word dhululan may describe the pathways, which God made easy, or the bee, which is submissive and unresisting in obedience.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p262#3/r4] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | The drink issuing from the bees is honey, which they emit from their mouths.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p262#3/r5] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | reports | Honey’s white, yellow, and red colors may reflect the bee’s age or the colors of its foods.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p262#3/r6] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | Honey is one of the beneficial medicines and is cited by physicians as an ingredient in remedies; the verse does not claim it cures every patient.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p262#3/r7] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | reports | The indefinite word healing either magnifies the healing in honey or limits it to some healing, since an indefinite noun in an affirmative clause is specific.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p262#3/r8] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · عليه السلام | reports | A man complained that his brother had severe diarrhea; after honey was prescribed twice and the complaint worsened, the speaker said God tells the truth and the brother’s belly lies, and the man recovered after giving honey again.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p262#3/r9] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · ابن مسعود رضي الله عنه | reports | Ibn Masʿūd says honey heals every illness and the Qur’an heals what is in hearts, so people should use both cures.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p559/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī explicitly takes بيوتا as the hexagonal cells built by bees and construes eating from every fruit as from those they desire.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p559/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The delay conveyed by ثم fits honey gathering after nest-building; كلي is an imperative coordinated with اتخذي, and كل الثمرات is a general expression restricted to customary fruits. | mentions 27:18
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p560/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the bee eats blossoms from trees.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p560/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · أبو عبد الله الرازي | reports | Al-Rāzī proposes that honey forms from subtle particles arising in the air like ترنجبين, which bees collect from flowers and leaves and store as food; on this account من marks origin, not a part.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p560/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | من كل الثمرات most naturally means a portion: the bee takes from good trees and fragrant leaves, producing honey within itself.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p560/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | prefers | The fāʾ in فاسلكي naturally places flight along the bee's routes immediately after eating; bees may migrate far for forage and return home. Other readings take the paths as inspired honey-making methods or the divine processes that transform food into honey.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p560/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid says ذللا means the routes are easy and not rugged for the bee; it is then an adverbial state of the Lord's paths. Qatāda instead says obedient and compliant, and Ibn Zayd says the bees follow beekeepers as they move them. | mentions 20:53
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p560/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The mention of a drink from the bees' bellies recounts the result of nest-making, eating, and following the routes, just as milk and fruit drinks were benefits of livestock and trees.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p561#2/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan, Mujāhid, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Farrāʾ, and Ibn Kaysān reportedly make the pronoun in فيه refer to the Qur'an as healing; al-Naḥḥās approves, while Ibn al-ʿArabī rejects the report as contextually and rationally unsound.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p561#2/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن العربي | rejects | Ibn al-ʿArabī doubts this attribution and rejects the Qur'anic interpretation: the whole context concerns honey, with no mention of Qur'an.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p561#2/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The remarkable cells, diverse sour, bitter, and harmful flowers and leaves the bee eats, and its obedience to leader and keeper invite contemplation, fitting the verse's conclusion.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p561/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Most commentators say honey exits the bee's mouth, the endpoint of its route; al-Ḥarīrī's line calls praise 'honey-licking' and blame 'wasp vomit,' both terms implying a mouth.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p561/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · علي | reports | ʿAlī belittles worldly life by saying humanity's finest clothing is a worm's saliva and finest drink is a bee's residue; another saying of his calls honey fly vomit, implying an exit other than the mouth.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p561/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | A tale says Solomon, Alexander, and Aristotle built glass hives to watch where honey emerged, but the bees coated the glass with mud and blocked the view.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p561/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan calls honey wheat's pulp with the bee's saliva and thus treats it as mouth secretion; another view calls the mouth a belly because it is hidden within.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p561/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Honey's colors and taste vary with bees' temperaments and forage; a hadith mentions bees feeding on ʿurfut, and another view links colors to young, middle-aged, and mature bees.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p561/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | prefers | The pronoun in فيه returns to honey, a familiar medicine, though it does not cure every illness; شفاء is indefinite either for magnification ('remarkable healing') or generality ('some healing').
[SAMIN-DURR:16:69/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | ذللا is the plural of ذلول.
[SAMIN-DURR:16:69/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | It may be a circumstantial accusative of the paths, which God made subservient, as in 67:15. | mentions 67:15
[SAMIN-DURR:16:69/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Alternatively, it is a circumstantial accusative of the bee as subject of اسلكي, meaning obedient and compliant; both interpretations occur in tafsir.
[SAMIN-DURR:16:69/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | سبل may be adverbial: the bee follows what it ate through the Lord's pathways where divine power transforms it into honey.
[SAMIN-DURR:16:69/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Alternatively, سبل is the direct object: paths God taught and showed the bee for making honey.
[SAMIN-DURR:16:69/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | من كل الثمرات may be partitive; its alternative means the bee eats a manna-like substance descending onto leaves, not the fruit itself, which he calls very remote.
[SAMIN-DURR:16:69/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | يخرج من بطونها shifts person; continuing the earlier address would have said من بطونك.
[SAMIN-DURR:16:69/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The pronoun in فيه most naturally refers to شراب; another view refers it to the Qur'an.
