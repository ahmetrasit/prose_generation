[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:106/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Naskh lexically means annul, remove, eliminate, or transfer; as a religious term it is repeal or replacement of a ruling by a later ruling.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:106/r10] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Şevkânî | reports | Shawkani says the second interpretation, allowing repeal, is the one most scholars of language and rational inquiry accept.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:106/r11] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | The phrase may also refer to divine messages in earlier scriptures being made to be forgotten, so they did not pass into later books and reports.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:106/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Abrogation applies only to commands and prohibitions; the repealed ruling is mansukh and the repealing ruling is nasikh.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:106/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Scholars agree abrogation is rationally possible and occurred in earlier religions: the Gospel repealed some Torah rules, and the Qur'an changed or removed some rulings in both earlier scriptures.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:106/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Some earlier Islamic rulings also changed as Islam developed; the commentary gives the Prophet's hadiths on visiting graves as an example.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:106/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Whether the Qur'an contains abrogated verses is disputed: most scholars accept abrogation by later verses or hadiths, some reject it, and proposed counts range from five to two hundred.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:106/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | prefers | The author favors the later scholars' view: unless final replacement is explicit, both differing rulings remain valid in their own circumstances and the applicable circumstance determines which governs.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:106/r7] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | prefers | On this view, if the circumstances for one ruling never recur, it becomes impossible to apply it in practice, without requiring that its text or ruling was wholly annulled.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:106/r8] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Şevkânî | reports | Shawkani reports that those reading the verb as nansa(hā) understand it to mean delaying the abrogation.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:106/r9] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | prefers | The authors prefer the nunsi(hā) reading, which they explain as leaving the verse unchanged and unabrogated or allowing it to be abrogated.
