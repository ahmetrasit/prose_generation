[TABRISI:18:22-24/r10] TABRISI, d. 548 · الجبائي | reports | Al-Jubbaʾi explains the rule by the risk that a person may be prevented by illness, incapacity, or a change of intention; saying ‘if God wills’ avoids a false report because the act did not occur only if God did not will it.
[TABRISI:18:22-24/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · الأخفش | reports | One reading of the exception supplies an implied verb: do not promise tomorrow unless you say ‘if God wills’; this teaches people to qualify future promises and avoid a lie or broken oath if prevented.
[TABRISI:18:22-24/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas associates the exception with teaching people to qualify what they report they will do, so an obstacle does not make the report false or an oath broken.
[TABRISI:18:22-24/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · الفراء | prefers | Al-Farraʾ reads the exception literally as ‘only by God's will’; this fits the surface wording without supplying omitted speech, and can mean that one should promise only acts God wills, namely obedience.
[TABRISI:18:22-24/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | On al-Farraʾ's reading, the exception does not bar reports about permissible acts, since the prohibition is discouragement rather than a ban: omitting it is not sinful by consensus.
[TABRISI:18:24#2/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · السيد المرتضى | holds | The verse also covers every act that is not evil, by Muslim consensus on the propriety of making the exception in such acts.
[TABRISI:18:24#2/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · السيد المرتضى | reports | For permissible acts, an exception can mean facilitation, ability, release, or continuation in one's current condition; another possible sense is simply declaring dependence on God without changing the statement's truth or legal effect.
[TABRISI:18:24#2/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · السيد المرتضى | rejects | Al-Murtaḍā answers the opponents of divine justice: if God's willing only obedience made a promise to pay an unfulfilled debt false despite the exception, it would entail a broken oath and expiation contrary to consensus.
[TABRISI:18:24#2/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · الزجاج | reports | The hope is that God will grant signs and proofs of prophethood clearer and more guiding than the story of the Cave Companions; the author says God then gave knowledge of earlier messengers and their histories.
[TABRISI:18:24#2/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · الجبائي | reports | Al-Jubbaʾi alternatively reads the hope as asking God to remind the Prophet of what he forgot, or to give him something more beneficial if the forgotten matter is not recalled.
[TABRISI:18:24/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | When asked about the Cave Companions and Dhu al-Qarnayn, the Prophet promised to answer the next day without making an exception; revelation was delayed for days, troubling him, and this verse then commanded the exception.
[TABRISI:18:24/r10] TABRISI, d. 548 · السيد المرتضى | reports | Al-Murtaḍā distinguishes an exception that suspends legal force in oaths, divorce, emancipation, contracts, and reports from an exception in sins, where such surrender to God is inappropriate.
[TABRISI:18:24/r11] TABRISI, d. 548 · السيد المرتضى | reports | An exception may also express reliance on divine kindness and facilitation, especially in obedience; if the act does not occur, its absence shows that God did not provide that favor, so the speaker is neither perjured nor false.

[TABRISI:18:24/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The author says a late exception may earn the reward of making the exception but cannot retroactively change the completed statement or invalidate an oath and its expiation; this best captures Ibn Abbas's intent.
