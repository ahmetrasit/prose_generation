[IBNASHUR-FULL:v24p181/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Du‘a can mean calling, which acknowledges the one called, or requesting; asking God for a need is its common literal meaning.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v24p181/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Du‘a can also stand figuratively for worship because worship involves calling the one worshipped with reverence and supplication; this usage is less common. Quranic ‘worship’ means singling God out and acknowledging His oneness.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v24p181/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Istijaba commonly means granting a request; it can also mean accepting worship by forgiving prior shirk and rewarding acts of faith.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v24p181/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | If the command means asking for needs, the response depends on God’s will or fulfillment of the request’s conditions; He may grant the request, something better in this life, or its recompense in the next.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v24p181/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | If the command means exclusive worship, God’s response is acceptance of repentance from shirk, which is assured.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v24p181/r7] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The pairing of the two terms, followed by the reference to worship, signals an ellipsis-like combination: du‘a means both asking and worship, while response means answering and accepting worship’s effects.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v24p181/r8] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Ud‘uni is used for both meanings through the full scope of a shared homonym.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v24p182/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Astajib is used both literally and figuratively; the context supplies the cue, making the expression concise and comprehensive.
