<<87:4 notes, part 1 of 0..1>>
Bringing out the pasture means bringing it forth from the earth by making it grow.
[IBNASHUR:87:4/r3] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Al-marʿā is vegetation grazed by livestock; it can be a place-name for grazing or a verbal noun used for the grazed object.
[IBNASHUR:87:4/r4] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Choosing pasture rather than the general word plant evokes livestock’s benefit to people and preserves the verse ending.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p276/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Repeating the relative pronoun before measuring and producing pasture highlights each attribute; فهدى then marks guidance as the effect of measure.
[TABATABAI:87:1-19#2/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Al-marʿā is grazing fodder brought forth by God.
[TABATABAI:87:1-19#2/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Pasture growth and its change into dark dry refuse exemplify divine governance.
[CORPUSCORANICUM:87:2-5:anmerkung/r1] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | holds | The perfects are aspectual rather than inherently past; verses 4–5 show creation as ongoing world-sustaining activity through the pasture’s recurring growth and decay. | mentions 53:43, 53:44, 53:45, 53:46, 53:47, 53:48, 53:49, 96:1, 96:2, 96:3, 96:4, 96:5, 95:4, 95:5
[CORPUSCORANICUM:87:4:anmerkung/r1] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | holds | The wording parallels Q 79:31, where God brings its water and pasture out. | mentions 79:31
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p270/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The preceding sūrah urges the Prophet to wait: persistent deniers receive only temporary respite under God's practice, and cannot escape His grasp.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p270/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | This sūrah shifts its primary address from stubborn Quraysh to the Prophet and, over at least ten following sūrahs, dispels his preaching fears in varied ways.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p270/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Verses 1–5 direct the Prophet to glorify God, whose attributes show that creation and human physical and intellectual capacities mature gradually.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p275/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | rejects | He rejects the common decay-based readings of black litter or waste: aḥwā describes reddish or greenish blackness produced by freshness, fertility, and luxuriant growth.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p275/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Through Imām al-Farāhī, the author cites Qaṭṭāmī's valley couplet depicting dense, layered undergrowth as evidence that ghuthāʾ can mean fertile luxuriant vegetation.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p276/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | prefers | Because aḥwā does not denote decay-blackness and qualifies ghuthāʾ, the phrase means God brings out vegetation and makes it luxuriant and blackish green.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:87:1/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | The verses may describe spring growth turning into black plant remains, or metaphorically living things receiving life and then dying.
[STUDYQURAN:87:4/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The pasture and its blackened stubble illustrate God’s power over the creation and destruction of living things; marʿā means all forms of vegetation.
