[TABRISI:11:105#2/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | prefers | The stronger parse makes يوم يأتي an adverb governed directly by لا تكلم; this avoids the ellipsis required by alternatives, and time nouns naturally attach to verbs.
[TABRISI:11:105#3/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | لا تكلم means لا تتكلم, with one of the two tāʾs elided, as in the cited poetic example.
[TABRISI:11:105#3/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | God delays the Day only to a counted term because continued obligation benefits creatures until then; the counted limit signals that the Day is near.
[TABRISI:11:105#3/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The wording لأجل signals a wise purpose for delaying the Day, whereas إلى أجل would not express that purpose.
[TABRISI:11:105#3/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The Day’s arrival is the coming of resurrection and recompense.
[TABRISI:11:105#3/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · الجبائي | reports | Al-Jubbāʾī says no one speaks except with God’s permission and command; people are compelled to leave sins, so only permitted good speech occurs.
[TABRISI:11:105#3/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | prefers | The more apparent meaning is that no useful speech, such as intercession or mediation, occurs in the Hereafter except by God’s permission.
[TABRISI:11:105#3/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan reconciles differing statements by saying the Resurrection has stations: speech is permitted at some and forbidden at others. | mentions 77:36, 55:39, 37:24
[TABRISI:11:105#3/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Another explanation takes “no speech” to mean no speech that supplies an argument: sinners may confess, blame one another, and shift guilt, yet such empty talk counts as no speech. | mentions 77:36, 55:39, 37:24
[TABRISI:11:105#4/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Calling people deaf, dumb, and blind explains that argumentless speech counts as no speech: although they hear, speak, and see, they do not accept or consider truth. | mentions 2:18, 2:171
[TABRISI:11:105#4/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Not being asked about sin means no inquiry to discover what God already knows; questioning instead serves rebuke, reproach, and proof, as in 37:24. | mentions 37:24
[TABRISI:11:105#4/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | People divide into those deserving punishment and reward: the wretched through disobedience, the happy through obedience; misery and happiness are strength of causes of harm and blessing.
[TABRISI:11:105#4/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | The pronoun منهم may refer to the people gathered on the Day in 11:103, or to نفس in this verse, since نفس is generic. | mentions 11:103
