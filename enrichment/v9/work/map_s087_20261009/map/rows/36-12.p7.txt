[TABATABAI:36:1-12/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · بعضهم | reports | Some say the Preserved Tablet records events only through the Resurrection, since a finite physical body cannot contain an infinite series of events.
[TABATABAI:36:1-12/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · بعضهم | reports | Their argument is that the Tablet is a body with finite dimensions, so recording everything about infinite events in it at once is impossible; they accordingly limit everything to events through the Resurrection.
[TABATABAI:36:12#2/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | The author rejects mapping the passage piecemeal onto the stories of Abu Jahl’s group and the distant Ansar homes: doing so disrupts the unified flow describing two responses to the call.
[TABATABAI:36:12#2/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | He argues that the verses came down together as one coherent passage: people divide into two groups when the call and warning reach them. This does not preclude an actual episode in which the Prophet was concealed from enemies.
[TABATABAI:36:12#2/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · أبو سعيد الخدري | reports | Abū Saʿīd reports that Banu Salama wanted to move nearer the mosque; the Prophet called them, said their footsteps would be recorded, recited the verse, and they stayed. Al-Tirmidhī graded it ḥasan and al-Ḥākim authenticated it.
[TABATABAI:36:12#2/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās reports that the distant homes of the Anṣār led them to consider moving nearer the mosque; the verse came down and they chose to remain. No grading is stated.
[TABATABAI:36:12#2/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | The author says the two relocation reports have the same contextual difficulty as the earlier reports tying separate verses to the concealment story.

[TABATABAI:36:12#3/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · تفسير القمي | reports | Al-Qummī’s commentary identifies the clear Imam as a clear, firmly established book.
[TABATABAI:36:12#3/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · أمير المؤمنين | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās reports ʿAlī saying, ‘By God, I am the clear Imam’: he clarifies truth from falsehood and inherited it from the Prophet.
[TABATABAI:36:12#3/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم | reports | A report in Maʿānī al-akhbār, through Abū al-Jārūd from Abū Jaʿfar and his forefathers, says of ʿAlī that he is the Imam in whom God gathered knowledge of everything.
[TABATABAI:36:12#3/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | If sound, the two reports about ʿAlī are inner Qur’anic allusions rather than exoteric tafsir; God may grant His uniquely devoted servant knowledge of the clear Book, and ʿAlī is the foremost monotheist after the Prophet.
[TABATABAI:36:12/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Reviving the dead refers to bringing them back to life for recompense.
[TABATABAI:36:12/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | What people send ahead is the work they performed before death.
[TABATABAI:36:12/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Their traces are good or bad effects left after death: beneficial knowledge, a mosque, or ablution facility, and innovations or places of vice used for disobedience.
[TABATABAI:36:12/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | rejects | A reported alternative makes what was sent ahead the intentions and the traces their resulting actions; the author considers this far from the context.
[TABATABAI:36:12/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Writing deeds and traces means recording them in individual deed-books through the angel scribes, distinct from their enumeration in the clear Imam.
[TABATABAI:36:12/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | The author rejects identifying the writing of deeds and traces with their enumeration in the clear book: scripture distinguishes the universal register, each nation’s book, and each person’s record, and here contrasts writing with enumeration. | mentions 6:59, 45:28, 17:13
[TABATABAI:36:12/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The clear Imam is the Preserved Tablet, unchanged, containing the detail of God’s decree for creation and enumerating everything; its several scriptural names each reflect a distinct aspect.
[TABATABAI:36:12/r9] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | It is called an Imam because its inexorable decree is followed by creation; deed-books are copied from it.
