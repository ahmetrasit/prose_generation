[ALUSI-FULL:v18p175#2/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · عبد الله بن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masud reports that making a mosque a thoroughfare is one of the signs of the Hour; habitually doing so is sinful and makes a person publicly disobedient, while a genuine excuse removes the dislike.
[ALUSI-FULL:v18p175#2/r10] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | prefers | Some interpret raising as constructing high buildings, citing Abraham’s raising the foundations of the House; al-Alusi prefers honoring the mosques generally, with lofty construction included among its instances. | mentions 2:127
[ALUSI-FULL:v18p175#2/r11] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | rejects | The suggestions that raising means needs being presented to God there or voices being raised in remembrance are unsound.
[ALUSI-FULL:v18p175#2/r12] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Calling God’s command permission hints that the person being commanded is already inclined to the commanded act, as though seeking permission to carry it out.
[ALUSI-FULL:v18p175#2/r13] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | prefers | The remembrance of God includes all forms of remembrance and religious learning; Ibn Abbas narrows it to monotheism or Qur’an recitation, and another view takes it as God’s beautiful names.
[ALUSI-FULL:v18p175#2/r14] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas is reported to interpret remembrance as affirming divine unity, specifically saying “There is no god but God.”
[ALUSI-FULL:v18p175#2/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · زيد بن أسلم | reports | Zayd ibn Aslam reports that mosques were sprinkled and swept in the Prophet’s time.
[ALUSI-FULL:v18p175#2/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · يعقوب بن زيد | reports | Yaqub ibn Zayd reports that the Prophet removed mosque dust with a palm frond.
[ALUSI-FULL:v18p175#2/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Mosque members may furnish a mosque with brick, mats, or lamps from their own money, not mosque funds unless the ruler authorizes it, except where the endower specified this use.
[ALUSI-FULL:v18p175#2/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Lavish lighting on set nights that draws children and idlers into noise and disrespect is a reprehensible innovation; patterned carpets that distract worshippers and diminish concentration should likewise be avoided.
[ALUSI-FULL:v18p175#2/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | Some authorities recommend entering with the right foot, leaving with the left, and praying two rakahs before sitting when entering for a purpose other than prayer.
[ALUSI-FULL:v18p175#2/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · أبو قتادة | reports | Abu Qatada reports the Prophet’s instruction to give mosques their due, explaining that this means praying two rakahs before sitting.
[ALUSI-FULL:v18p175#2/r8] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Mosques may be built high, unlike ordinary houses, but should not be decorated in ways that distract worshippers.
[ALUSI-FULL:v18p175#2/r9] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · جبير بن مطعم | reports | A report from Jubayr ibn Mutim says mosques should not be built with images or adorned with glass.

[ALUSI-FULL:v18p175/r10] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Sexual intercourse and relieving oneself in or atop a mosque are forbidden.
[ALUSI-FULL:v18p175/r11] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | People with odor from garlic, onions, leeks, radishes, chronic bad breath, strong body odor, or foul-smelling clothes should not enter; garlic-smelling men were led to al-Baqiʿ in the Prophet’s time.

[ALUSI-FULL:v18p175/r13] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Eating garlic in a mosque is prohibited for anyone not in retreat; sitting there for condolences or worldly conversation is prohibited, and using a mosque as a passage is disliked or forbidden.
[ALUSI-FULL:v18p175/r14] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · عبد الله بن عمر | reports | A report from Ibn Umar traces the prohibition on passing through mosques to the Prophet.

[ALUSI-FULL:v18p175/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Spitting and bodily mucus must be kept out of mosques; ablution there is disliked as naturally repugnant, so mosques must be kept clean.
