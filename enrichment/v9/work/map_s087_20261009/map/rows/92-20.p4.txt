[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p343/r7] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Verses 15–21 explain the traits of those cast into Hell and those shielded from it, together with their reward.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p351/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Spending that purifies wealth matters to God when done to please Him, without seeking repayment or gratitude, as 76:9 also says. | mentions 76:9
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p351/r5] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · some people | reports | Some render the verse as saying that the recipient had done no favour that obliges the giver to repay it.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p352/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | rejects | Even if that rendering is linguistically possible, it cannot mean that repaying a benefactor is not God-pleasing; reciprocal kindness remains virtuous.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p352/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | prefers | Islahi prefers his own translation because it is linguistically sound and avoids the mistaken implication that reciprocal kindness cannot please God.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:92:17/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Müfessirler | reports | Some say verses 19–21 were revealed about Abu Bakr, who bought and freed enslaved people tortured by polytheists.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:92:17/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Polytheists alleged that Abu Bakr freed people in exchange for reciprocal kindness or some benefit.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:92:17/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | A pious believer need not receive a favor or gain before helping; all blessings come from God, and good should be done for God's approval alone.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:92:17/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The passage rejects the polytheists' self-interested accusation and honors believers like Abu Bakr, whose hearts are adorned with love for people and generosity.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:92:17/r7] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | After warning of the blazing fire in verse 14, verses 17–20 describe giving for God's sake without owing anyone, spiritual purification, and being kept from the fire.
[STUDYQURAN:92:15/r1] STUDYQURAN · several commentators | reports | Commentators tell of a Medinan palm owner who snatched fallen dates from a poor family's children and refused the Prophet's offer of a Paradise palm; another man paid forty palms, donated the tree, and the Prophet gave it to the family. The refusal maps to vv. 15–16, the gift to vv. 18–21.
[STUDYQURAN:92:15/r2] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | If this is an early Makkan sūrah, the palm-tree story likely is not its occasion; the verses instead generally condemn those who rejected Muhammad as prophet.
[STUDYQURAN:92:15/r4] STUDYQURAN · others | reports | Others say vv. 17–21 praise Abū Bakr, who bought and freed weak slaves; when his father asked why he did not buy stronger defenders, he said he sought no defense.
[STUDYQURAN:92:15/r5] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | One revelation account says Bilāl was tortured after desecrating the Kaʿbah idols, kept testifying to God's oneness, and the Prophet assured him that the One would save him.
[STUDYQURAN:92:15/r6] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | In that account Abū Bakr bought Bilāl for gold after the Prophet told him Bilāl was tortured for God; the idolaters alleged he acted to repay a favor, prompting vv. 19–21.
[STUDYQURAN:92:15/r8] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The verse literally says no one has a favor with him to repay: he neither repays past favors nor expects future recompense, but gives only to seek God's will.
