[ASAD-NOTES:16:68#n77/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | Awha highlights bee instinct: it builds an exceptionally ordered, economical honeycomb from proportioned hexagonal prismatic wax cells.
[ASAD-NOTES:16:intro/r2] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | The title points to the bee as an instance of divine creativity, while evidence of God's purposeful activity culminates in the ethical injunctions of verse 90.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:16:68/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Here waḥy, rendered “inspired,” means God creates in creatures the skills needed to seek benefit, avoid harm, and sustain themselves; psychology calls this instinct, and Ibn al-Arabi is cited for the gloss.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:16:68/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The bees’ “houses” are combs with hexagonal cells, made from their own products in suitable natural places such as tree hollows or in hives prepared by people.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:16:68/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | By instinct, honeybees make their own nests from beeswax and arrange precisely measured hexagonal-prism cells within the comb.
[QIRAAT-ER:16:68:10#1/r1] QIRAAT-ER · author | holds | Kasra on bāʾ yields biyūt rather than buyūt, a dialectal plural vowel with no semantic change.
[QIRAAT-ER:16:68:16#1/r1] QIRAAT-ER · author | holds | The rāʾ may take ḍamma rather than kasra; both Form I patterns have the same meaning.
[STUDYQURAN:16:68/r1] STUDYQURAN · some commentators | reports | The bee's revelation can mean God inspiring it or placing in its heart knowledge unique to the creature.
[STUDYQURAN:16:68/r10] STUDYQURAN · some commentators | reports | Alternatively, the bee is to follow paths to the fruits God ordained it to eat.
[STUDYQURAN:16:68/r2] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Awḥā can denote formal scriptural revelation or other divine communication and inspiration, as in several other verses. | mentions 20:38, 28:7, 5:111, 99:5, 41:12
[STUDYQURAN:16:68/r20] STUDYQURAN · some Shiite commentators | reports | Some Shiite readings take the bee as ʿAlī and the Prophet's family, and its diverse drink as Quranic knowledge and wisdom in their teachings that heals spiritually.
[STUDYQURAN:16:68/r21] STUDYQURAN · al-Qurṭubī and al-Zamakhsharī | rejects | Al-Qurṭubī and al-Zamakhsharī note this Shiite interpretation but reject its soundness.
[STUDYQURAN:16:68/r22] STUDYQURAN · al-Ṭabrisī | reports | Al-Ṭabrisī traces the interpretation to ʿAlī saying he was the believers' yaʿsūb, a term meaning both male bee and leader or chieftain.
[STUDYQURAN:16:68/r3] STUDYQURAN · some commentators | reports | The bee's revelation may metaphorically describe the instinctive behaviors God implanted in it.
[STUDYQURAN:16:68/r4] STUDYQURAN · al-Rāzī | reports | Al-Rāzī allows that bees may possess intelligence, shown by their intricate hexagonal hives and social organization under an obeyed leader.
[STUDYQURAN:16:68/r5] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | A literal reading fits descriptions of creatures as religious communities, praising God, and prostrating to Him. | mentions 6:38, 17:44, 57:1, 59:1, 59:24, 60:1, 61:1, 62:1, 64:1, 16:48, 16:49, 16:50

[STUDYQURAN:16:68/r7] STUDYQURAN · some commentators | reports | That which people build can refer to human structures and their roofs.
