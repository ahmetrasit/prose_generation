YYAN-FULL:v2p308/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The delayed wording makes a fitting cadence and avoids repeating ذكر in فاذكروا ذكرا كذكركم; this explains why the phrase comes late.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p308/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The objection that أو separates a conjunction from its coordinated word fails: an intervening circumstantial modifier is semantically like an adverbial and can be treated similarly.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p308/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | Abu Hayyan favors the circumstantial parse over strained analyses that make ذكرًا a specification after the comparative and force figurative readings.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p308/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | Some commentators say the verse contrasts those remembering God before the Prophet's mission with believers after it, who know reward and punishment.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p308/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | prefers | Abu Hayyan instead sees a division among pilgrims commanded to remember God: some ask only for worldly goods, while others ask for good in this life and the next.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p309/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The shift to third person excludes worldly-only petitioners from the direct address, since limiting prayer to this life is unworthy of a rational addressee.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p309/r10] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · علي | reports | Ali interprets worldly good as a righteous wife.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p309/r11] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · قتادة | reports | Qatada interprets worldly good as health and enough money.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p309/r12] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan interprets worldly good as knowledge or worship.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p309/r13] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · السدي وأبو وائل وابن زيد | reports | Al-Suddi, Abu Wa'il, and Ibn Zayd interpret worldly good as wealth.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p309/r14] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · مقاتل | reports | Muqatil interprets it as abundant provision.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p309/r15] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن قتيبة | reports | Ibn Qutayba interprets it as blessing in this world.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p309/r16] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | Other suggested senses are contentment with provision, success and protection from error, or righteous children.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p309/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | This is an elegant exhaustive division into two groups; Abu Hayyan rejects the Sufis' proposed third group, people content with God's decree who make no supplication.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p309/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The second object of آتنا is omitted; possible estimates include what you wish to grant or our request.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p309/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | Abu Hayyan rejects treating في as redundant and الدنيا as the second object, or changing في to mean من and omitting both objects; only one object may be omitted with this verb.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p309/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | The clause can mean that the worldly-only petitioner has no afterlife share because he sought only this world.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p309/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | Alternatively, it says the petitioner has no desire to seek an afterlife share, reinforcing his exclusive worldly request.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p309/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Abu Hayyan points back to his explanation of the same phrase in the discussion of 2:102. | mentions 2:102
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p309/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Although من is singular, the petition is plural because many people seek only worldly aims; singular wording might suggest few do so.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p309/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | حسنة is unrestricted: the petitioners ask God for a good condition in this world.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p314#2/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Pilgrims' aims divide into those seeking only this world and those seeking worldly and afterlife good; the first group has no afterlife share.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p314/r11] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | After the command to seek forgiveness, the passage commands remembrance after completing the rites.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p314/r12] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | People treated recalling and praising their fathers' noble deeds as the height of remembrance; God uses that familiar intensity as a comparison and commands even more remembrance so they never forget Him.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p325/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Following the two kinds of supplicants in 2:200–201, the passage contrasts a sweet-speaking worldly seeker with one who seeks God's pleasure and gives himself for it; the first comes first as in the earlier division. [SAMIN-DURR:2:200#2/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزجاج | reports | One genitive parse coordinates أشد with the genitive ذكركم under the comparison kāf, estimating like a more intense remembrance; it figuratively makes remembrance itself remember. Abū al-Baqāʾ and Ibn ʿAṭiyya follow it.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:200#2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Another genitive parse coordinates أشد with the genitive pronoun in ذكركم: more intense remembrance by you. Its meaning avoids personifying remembrance, but Basran grammarians prohibit coordination on a genitive pronoun without repeating the preposition.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:200#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | An accusative parse coordinates أشد with آباءكم, estimating people more intense in remembrance than your fathers; ذكرا is their distinguishing measure, so remembrance is not personified.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:200#2/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو علي | reports | Abū ʿAlī and Ibn Jinnī coordinate أشد with the syntactic position of the kāf, understood as an adjective of an omitted verbal noun; this figuratively treats remembrance as a rememberer.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:200#2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · مكي | reports | Makkī supplies an implicit verb: remember Him with remembrance more intense than your remembrance of your fathers; the verbal noun is an accusative circumstantial description.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:200#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abū al-Baqāʾ supplies an implicit be: be more intense in remembering God than you are in remembering your fathers, which he considers easier than the figurative parse.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:200#3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | reports | A fifth accusative parse makes أشد a circumstantial modifier of ذكرا, as though it were a delayed adjective; the Shaykh illustrates fronted adjective becoming circumstantial with a line about a desolate trace of Laylā.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:200#3/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | prefers | The Shaykh says the direct sense is to remember God with remembrance matching or surpassing remembrance of fathers; the five preceding parses are all weak.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:200#3/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | prefers | For the preferred reading, ذكرا may coordinate with the syntactic position of the kāf, though أو is separated from its conjunct by circumstantial أشد. Grammarians permit separation only when the conjunction exceeds one letter and the intervening word is an oath, adverb, or prepositional phrase; the Shaykh treats the circumstance as estimated with a preposition, like an adverb.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:200#3/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | prefers | Alternatively, ذكرا is the verbal noun governed by فاذكروا; كذكركم is a fronted circumstance originally adjectival to it, and أشد coordinates with that circumstance.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:200#4/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The usual analysis makes ذكرا a specification of degree, but the accusative is puzzling because remembrance is the same kind as the comparison term and would normally be genitive. Earlier parses resolve this by personifying remembrance, making أشد describe persons or a circumstance, or supplying a verb.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:200#4/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | One analysis takes أو as permissive, allowing either way of remembering.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:200#4/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Another analysis takes أو as a choice between alternatives.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:200#4/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A third analysis takes أو in the sense of بل, rather.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:200#5/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The strongest parse makes من the subject, with its predicate in the preceding prepositional phrase.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:200#5/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash allows من to function as an agent.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:200#5/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A further analysis treats من as an indefinite noun modified by a descriptive clause.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:200#5/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The phrasing shifts from the preceding pattern: it would have said among you, but من is treated by meaning as plural in our Lord, grant us; agreement with its singular form would be Lord, grant me.
