[TABATABAI:23:4-7/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Taking zakah as an act of spending makes its attachment to 'doers' natural; treating it as the extracted money does not, since money is not an action performed by an agent.
[TABATABAI:23:4-7/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Saying they are active doers of zakah, rather than merely payers, stresses their care for it, like saying 'I will do it' when asked to drink water.
[TABATABAI:23:4-7/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Faith should prompt giving because happiness requires a community where people receive their rights and enjoy life's benefits more equally; aid to poor people is a strong means toward it.
[ASAD-NOTES:23:1#n1/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Zamakhshari | reports | Zakah here means working for or actively promoting inner purity, an interpretation also advanced by Abu Muslim.
[ASAD-NOTES:23:1/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | Believers attain a happy state through humility in prayer and active pursuit of inner purity.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-5-english:p240/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Zakāh is the second pillar and, though its legal form varied across religions, broadly means spending for God's cause; in this Makkan sūrah it means charity, not purification as a verbal noun.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-5-english:p240/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Prayer joins a person to God and zakāh to fellow people; together they make one rightly connected to Creator and community worthy of success in both worlds.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-5-english:p240/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Prayer and zakāh are two arms of faith grounded in gratitude: gratitude moves believers to pray, spend, and sacrifice, so zakāh manifests and complements prayer and each necessitates the other.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-5-english:p240/r5] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The earlier point about humble, fearful prayer preventing sin leads into the additional benefits of prayer discussed next.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-5-english:p243/r11] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The next sūrah, al-Nūr, supplements this one: faith illuminating the inner self brightens individual and collective life, while its absence darkens both.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-5-english:p243/r13] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Islahi summarizes the earlier passage as a progression from inward faith through prayer, zakāh, restraint, fidelity, and guarded prayer to lasting success.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-5-english:p243/r5] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Zakāh is faith's second manifestation and prayer's second arm; together they are religion's two primary directives and the basis of all sharīʿah.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-5-english:p243/r6] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Islahi says Abū Bakr's campaign against those withholding zakāh followed from the principle that rejecting either primary directive undermines adherence to both.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-5-english:p287/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Al-Mu'minūn's opening describes success through humble prayer, avoiding frivolity, zakāh, chastity and restraint, trustworthiness, and keeping promises. | mentions 23:1
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:23:4/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Because obligatory zakat was prescribed in Medina after this Meccan sūra, zakat here must be used broadly for charity (ṣadaqa).
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:23:4/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The Qur’an sometimes uses zakat and ṣadaqa interchangeably; 9:60 calls the recipients’ obligatory zakat ṣadaqa. | mentions 9:60
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:23:4/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Although obligatory zakat came later, Meccan revelations already strongly encouraged financial aid through ṣadaqa, zakat, spending, beneficence, and feeding.

[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p128/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | The commands to bow, prostrate, worship, do good, establish prayer and give zakat match the opening of al-Muʾminun and its listed believer qualities. | mentions 22:77, 22:78, 23:1, 23:2, 23:4

[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p138/r3] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | الزكاة can mean righteous action and purification of the self from idolatry and defilement, as the cited verses use it for purity and purification. | mentions 18:81, 87:14, 87:15, 91:9, 91:10

[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p138/r6] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | On another parse, الزكاة is the object of فاعلون, fronted for exclusivity; the lām reinforces the relation and is called لام التقوية, as in 2:91. | mentions 2:91
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p138/r7] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | With either grammatical analysis, الزكاة denotes the concrete wealth paid to its rightful recipient.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p139/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | Zakat can mean the act of purification or giving alms, so فاعلون means they perform the act, like someone who performs striking or killing.
