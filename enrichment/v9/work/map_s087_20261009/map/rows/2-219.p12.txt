[TABRISI:2:219-220#3/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The governing word for “in this world and the hereafter” is يبين; it may instead depend on تتفكرون.
[TABRISI:2:219-220#3/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · جماعة من الصحابة | reports | A group of Companions asked the Prophet about wine and gambling because they destroy reason and strip away wealth; this verse was revealed in response.
[TABRISI:2:219-220#3/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The apparent sense in Imami reports, also adopted by al-Shafiʿi, is any intoxicating drink that covers the mind; even a little is forbidden if much intoxicates.
[TABRISI:2:219-220#3/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو حنيفة | reports | Abu Hanifa restricts khamr to grape juice once it ferments and becomes strong.
[TABRISI:2:219-220#4/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Wine is called ithm in Arabic, as the cited poem shows; the verse’s description of it as a great sin also supports prohibition.
[TABRISI:2:219-220#4/r10] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو جعفر الباقر | reports | Abu Jaʿfar al-Baqir says al-ʿafw is what remains after a year’s provisions; he says the zakat verse abrogated this, as al-Suddi also held.
[TABRISI:2:219-220#4/r11] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | A fourth, unattributed interpretation takes al-ʿafw to mean the finest and best property.
[TABRISI:2:219-220#4/r12] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The demonstrative’s kāf is singular because the address is to the Prophet, while including the community.
[TABRISI:2:219-220#4/r13] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Another construal makes the address “thus, people of this group.”
[TABRISI:2:219-220#4/r14] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The signs are proofs concerning spending, wine, and gambling.
[TABRISI:2:219-220#4/r15] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Another explanation takes the signs to mean all the rulings of Islam.
[TABRISI:2:219-220#4/r16] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Here “this world and the hereafter” means their affairs.
[TABRISI:2:219-220#4/r17] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Pondering worldly and afterlife affairs shows that this life is trial, hardship, and passing, while the afterlife is recompense and permanence, prompting renunciation of one and desire for the other.
[TABRISI:2:219-220#4/r18] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Alternatively, “in this world and the hereafter” attaches to “He makes clear”: God explains signs in those matters so people ponder, showing He intends reflection whether or not they actually reflect.
[TABRISI:2:219-220#4/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Calling the sin great is itself evidence of prohibition, since a major sin is prohibited without disagreement.
[TABRISI:2:219-220#4/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak reads the comparison across time: after prohibition, their sin is greater than their benefit before prohibition.
[TABRISI:2:219-220#4/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · سعيد بن جبير | reports | Saʿid ibn Jubayr says both refer to the pre-prohibition period: the sin caused by them exceeds their benefit.
[TABRISI:2:219-220#4/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · قتادة | reports | Qatada says this verse does not establish prohibition; al-Maʾida 5:90 does. | mentions 5:90
[TABRISI:2:219-220#4/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The question means, “What thing should they spend?”
[TABRISI:2:219-220#4/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · عمرو بن الجموح | reports | ʿAmr ibn al-Jamuh asked what should be spent in jihad; another report says the question concerned charity.
[TABRISI:2:219-220#4/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن عباس وقتادة | reports | Ibn Abbas and Qatada interpret al-ʿafw as what remains beyond family needs and sufficiency.
[TABRISI:2:219-220#4/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · الحسن وعطاء | reports | Al-Hasan and ʿAtaʾ take al-ʿafw as the moderate amount, avoiding extravagance and stinginess; this is also transmitted from Abu Abd Allah.
[TABRISI:2:219-220/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The Kufans count two verses across 2:219–220 and treat تتفكرون as a verse ending; the other regional count has one verse and does not count that word as a verse ending.
[TABRISI:2:219-220/r10] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | The single-expression analysis is illustrated by the idiom عما ذا تسأل and a poem asking ماذا بال نسوتكم, meaning ما بال نسوتكم.
[TABRISI:2:219-220/r11] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | For nominative العفو, ذا means الذي, so the question means what is it that they spend; العفو answers as the predicate of an omitted subject.
[TABRISI:2:219-220/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · أهل الكوفة غير عاصم | reports | Kufan readers other than ʿĀṣim read إثم كثير with thāʾ, while the other readers use bāʾ and read إثم كبير.
[TABRISI:2:219-220/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو عمرو | reports | Abū ʿAmr alone reads العفو in the nominative; the other readers use the accusative.
[TABRISI:2:219-220/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو علي | reports | Abū ʿAlī supports كبير because كبير contrasts with صغير, while كبير denotes a grave sin; Qur'anic examples use صغير and كبير and call grave sins كبائر. The comparison أكبر also supports كبير rather than كثير.
[TABRISI:2:219-220/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | The كبير reading is supported by Qur'anic wording about every small and great matter being recorded and by references to major sins. | mentions 54:53, 4:31, 42:37
[TABRISI:2:219-220/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | The كثير reading is supported by the following verse's account of Satan causing enmity and obstructing prayer and remembrance through wine and gambling, and a report that the Prophet cursed ten involved with wine.
[TABRISI:2:219-220/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | The argument for كثير cites the Qur'anic description of Satan's effects through wine and gambling. | mentions 5:91
[TABRISI:2:219-220/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · النبي | reports | A Prophetic report curses ten wine-related parties: buyer and recipient, presser and commissioner, server and requester, carrier and recipient, and the consumer of its price.
[TABRISI:2:219-220/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | For accusative العفو, ما ذا is one interrogative expression and ماذا ينفقون functions like ما ينفقون, asking what they spend; the answer is accusative.
[IBNJAWZI-NUZHA:v1p154/r8] IBNJAWZI-NUZHA, d. 597 · بعض المفسرين | reports | Āyāt can mean commands and prohibitions that God makes clear.
[IBNJAWZI-NUZHA:v1p436/r1] IBNJAWZI-NUZHA, d. 597 · author | holds | ʿAfw can mean pardon, as in God’s forgiving a servant.
[IBNJAWZI-NUZHA:v1p436/r2] IBNJAWZI-NUZHA, d. 597 · author | holds | ʿAfw can mean disappearance of a trace, as when dust covers and hides a dwelling’s remains.
[IBNJAWZI-NUZHA:v1p436/r3] IBNJAWZI-NUZHA, d. 597 · ابن فارس | reports | Ibn Fāris says ʿafw is lawful, good wealth; ʿafāt are petitioners, and ʿafw of wealth is what remains beyond expenses.
[IBNJAWZI-NUZHA:v1p436/r6] IBNJAWZI-NUZHA, d. 597 · أهل التفسير | reports | ʿAfw can mean the surplus wealth available after one’s needs. | mentions 7:199
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p239#2/r1] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · عمر بن الخطاب | reports | One occasion report says ʿUmar asked God to give a decisive clarification about wine, after which this verse was revealed.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p239#2/r2] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · الأنصار وعمر ومعاذ | reports | Another occasion report says a group of Anṣār, including ʿUmar and Muʿādh, asked the Prophet for a ruling because wine removes reason and takes away wealth; this verse was revealed.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p239#2/r3] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · محمد بن القاسم | reports | Three etymologies are reported: wine is called khamr because it mixes with the intellect, covers it, or veils it.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p239#2/r4] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjāj says khamr is what covers the intellect; khimār can mean a crowd in which one is hidden, and a woman’s head covering is named for covering.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p239#2/r5] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjāj says the named wine is the agreed-upon kind, and by analogy every drink that acts like it shares its prohibition.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p239#2/r6] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | Scholars agree all gambling is forbidden; the verse names maysir as an example, though strictly maysir applies to gambling over camels.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p239#2/r7] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن عباس وابن عمر والحسن وسعيد بن جبير ومجاهد وقتادة | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn ʿUmar, al-Hasan, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Mujāhid, Qatāda, and others define maysir as gambling.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p239#2/r8] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن قتيبة | reports | Ibn Qutayba derives the word from yāsara, drawing lots with arrows; the person who draws is called yāsir, yāsirūn, yusr, or aysār.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p239#2/r9] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | In winter hardship, wealthy men slaughtered a camel and divided it into shares by drawing arrows; winners gave shares to the needy, the benefit named in the verse, and people praised participation and condemned refusal.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p240/r1] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | reports | Most reciters read kabīr with bāʾ; Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī read kathīr with thāʾ.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p240/r10] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · سعيد بن جبير، الضحاك، مقاتل | reports | One reading compares post-prohibition sin with pre-prohibition benefit.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p240/r11] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن جبير | reports | Another reading compares sin and benefit before prohibition: the sins arising from their causes already outweighed their benefits.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p240/r12] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن جبير | reports | Ibn Jubayr says wine had been permitted under the verse about drink made from date palms and grapes. | mentions 16:67
