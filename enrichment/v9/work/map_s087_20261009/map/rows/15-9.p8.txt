[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:15:9/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Dhikr refers to the Qur'an and Book named in the surah's opening verse, both terms used especially for the revelation addressed to Muhammad; the commentary translates it as revelation. | mentions 15:1
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:15:9/r10] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · bazı müfessirler | reports | Some interpreters take the one promised protection for to be the Prophet himself; God shielded him through severe opposition and enabled an unprecedented success in spreading Islam.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:15:9/r11] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Since protecting the Prophet also indirectly protects revelation, the author says the Prophet-protection and revelation-protection interpretations can be combined.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:15:9/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Verse 9 directly rejects the mockery in verse 6 that Muhammad received no revelation and was mad, with words from jinn or meaningless ravings rather than God. | mentions 15:6
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:15:9/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | In context, dhikr is revelation and preservation means shielding it during revelation from outside influence that could compromise its divine character; the verse rejects claims of nondivine origin or additions.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:15:9/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Taberî | reports | Tabari interprets the promise as preventing false words or errors from entering the Qur'an and preventing omissions from its rulings, limits, and obligations.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:15:9/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Most commentators and scholars adopt preservation against later intervention: unlike earlier scriptures, the Qur'an will not be diminished, altered, corrupted, or lost, but remain intact until the Resurrection by God's certain promise.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:15:9/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Muslims treated preservation as a sacred duty and transmitted the Qur'an in its original form, first through memorization and later through both memorization and writing.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:15:9/r7] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The development of writing tools, especially digital media, makes preserving the Qur'anic text easier today and in the future.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:15:9/r8] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Râzî | reports | Some infer that the basmala at the start of each surah is part of that surah and its first verse, since a later addition would conflict with this preservation promise.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:15:9/r9] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | rejects | The author rejects that basmala inference as an unsound interpretation arising from excessive concern about Qur'anic preservation.
[STUDYQURAN:15:9/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | As in 15:6, the Reminder here refers to the Quran. | mentions 15:6
[STUDYQURAN:15:9/r2] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The promise to preserve the Quran means God protects its text from alteration or additions; 41:42 and 4:82 support this reading. | mentions 41:42, 4:82, 18:1, 75:17
[STUDYQURAN:15:9/r3] STUDYQURAN · Muslims | reports | Muslims hold that the Quran cannot be falsified because believers widely memorize, study, and transmit it.
[STUDYQURAN:15:9/r4] STUDYQURAN · another interpretation | reports | Another interpretation says divine protection includes the Quran's miraculous arrangement, like walls fortifying and protecting a city.
[STUDYQURAN:15:9/r5] STUDYQURAN · another interpretation | reports | Another interpretation says God preserves the Quran through its traditional reciters, whose hearts are treasuries of His Book.
