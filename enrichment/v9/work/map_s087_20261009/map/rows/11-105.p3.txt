[QURTUBI-FULL:11:105/r11] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The pronoun refers either to the souls or to the people previously said to be gathered; the wretched and fortunate are those decreed for wretchedness or felicity.

[QURTUBI-FULL:11:105/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjāj considers retaining yāʾ grammatically better and favors following the codex and the readers’ consensus, since recitation is transmitted practice and the form occurs in Arabic.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:105/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Takallamu is originally tatakallamu; one of the two tāʾs is omitted for ease.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:105/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | An implicit “in it” is understood: souls speak only with permission, and the permitted speech is good speech, since they are compelled to abandon evil speech.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:105/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · قيل | reports | Another explanation is that no argument or intercession is spoken without God’s permission.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:105/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · قيل | reports | A further view says there are times during the gathering when people are barred from speaking except by God’s permission.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:105/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The apparent conflict with verses describing speech, questioning, and argument on Judgment Day is resolved by distinguishing futile speech from speech that could serve as a proof for them. | mentions 77:35, 77:36, 37:27, 16:111, 37:24, 55:39
