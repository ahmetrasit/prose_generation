[NAHHAS:v3p206/r5] NAHHAS, d. 338 · الزهري وأبو جعفر وأبو عبد الرحمن وحميد وطلحة والكسائي | reports | Al-Zuhrī, Abū Jaʿfar, Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān, Ḥumayd, Ṭalḥa, and al-Kisāʾī read ألا يا اسجدوا, addressing “these people.”
[NAHHAS:v3p206/r6] NAHHAS, d. 338 · author | reports | The second reading means “O people, prostrate”; two poetic examples support vocative yā, though al-Naḥḥās calls the construction unusual and the reading remote because it interrupts the syntax and differs from the written consonantal text.
[NAHHAS:v3p206/r7] NAHHAS, d. 338 · author | holds | At pause the hamza is still; in nominative it may have ḍamma or imālah, but it cannot be geminated.
[NAHHAS:v3p206/r8] NAHHAS, d. 338 · عكرمة | reports | ʿIkrima is reported to read الخبا with an alif and no hamza; Abū Ḥātim says this is not valid Arabic, arguing that easing hamza would produce الخب or الخبي.
[NAHHAS:v3p206/r9] NAHHAS, d. 338 · author | rejects | Al-Naḥḥās rejects Abū Ḥātim’s claim: Sībawayh records hamza changing to alif, wāw, or yāʾ after a still consonant according to its vowel, including in الخبء.
[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p270#2/r1] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · author | holds | With shadda, ألا is أن plus prohibitive لا, governing the verb and dropping its nūn; it means Satan made them not prostrate to God.
[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p270#2/r2] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · author | holds | Without shadda, ألا opens the utterance; an omitted vocative yields ألا يا هؤلاء اسجدوا, as Arabic commonly does.
[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p270#2/r3] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · عبد الله | reports | The reading هلا يسجدون is cited as evidence for the exhortative sense, since هلا commonly urges prostration.
[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p270#2/r4] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · author | holds | ويعلم ما تخفون وما تعلنون is read with either yāʾ or tāʾ; the source says its rationale was explained earlier.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v5p383/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · الكسائي | reports | All readers except al-Kisai geminate the lām in allā yasjudū; al-Kisai lightens it, treats it without أن, pauses after alā-yā, then begins asjudū.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v5p383/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | prefers | With gemination, the phrase means Satan turned them aside so that they would not prostrate, or that he adorned their deeds so they would not prostrate; the purpose lām keeps the story's sequence intact.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v5p383/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | holds | The geminated form can also be heard as an appeal, ألا يا قوم أو يا مسلمون اسجدوا, contrasting the unbelievers' failure to worship with a call to believers to praise God. | mentions 27:24
[FARISI-HUJJA:v5p384/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | holds | In the appeal reading, ألا can address an omitted audience, or an audience after يا can be elided; Abu Zayd's poem uses ألا يا before a direct imperative.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v5p384/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو زيد | reports | Abu Zayd's line ألا يا اسمع demonstrates the attention particle with an omitted or understood addressee before a command.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v5p384/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | holds | The written yāʾ in yasjudū supports the geminated أن لا reading: a light ألا would lead to asjudū without the yāʾ, whereas the verb after أن remains subjunctive with its prefix.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v5p385/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · عاصم برواية حفص والكسائي | reports | Hafs from Asim and al-Kisai read the verbs with second-person tāʾ in both places.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v5p385/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · الباقون وأبو بكر عن عاصم | reports | The other readers, including Abu Bakr from Asim, read both verbs with third-person yāʾ.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v5p385/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | holds | The yāʾ reading continues third-person narration about those led astray and God's knowledge of what they hide and reveal; the tāʾ reading follows a shift to direct address in اسجدوا لله.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v5p385/r4] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | holds | Even after ألا يسجدوا, the verbs may address the believers and unbelievers already mentioned in third person; Abu Bakr's yāʾ best matches the third-person reading of the preceding clause.

[MAWARDI-FULL:v4p203/r27] MAWARDI-FULL, d. 450 · author | reports | On the hoopoe-speech account, it may have addressed Bilqis’s people after finding them prostrating to other than God.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v4p203/r28] MAWARDI-FULL, d. 450 · author | reports | Alternatively, the hoopoe may have said this to Solomon on its return, expressing disapproval of what it found.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v4p203/r29] MAWARDI-FULL, d. 450 · author | reports | One rationale says the hoopoe understood from the inspiration to obey Solomon that he was a prophet whose words and actions must be obeyed, even if the bird was not legally accountable.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v4p203/r30] MAWARDI-FULL, d. 450 · author | reports | Another rationale compares the hoopoe to a child nearing maturity who sees people worship God and recognizes contrary worship as false.
