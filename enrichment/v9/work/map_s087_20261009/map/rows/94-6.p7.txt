[ASAD-NOTES:94:intro/r3] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | The surah addresses the Prophet first and, through him, every true follower of the Qur'an.
[BINTSHATI:v1p68/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | The fa links ease causally to the prior gifts of expanded breast, removed burden, and raised mention; inna and repetition reinforce reassurance and remove doubt.
[BINTSHATI:v1p68/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | Repetition in short surahs need not be rhetorical excess when the occasion calls for reinforcing assurance.
[BINTSHATI:v1p69/r1] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari says ma'a makes relief follow hardship soon, so that the coming ease is treated as if accompanying it for comfort.
[BINTSHATI:v1p69/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · النيسابوري | reports | Al-Nisaburi likewise says nearness makes the time of ease seem continuous with hardship and strengthens hope.
[BINTSHATI:v1p69/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الشيخ محمد عبده | reports | Abduh says ma'a secures hope that relief must come, as though already with hardship.
[BINTSHATI:v1p69/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | prefers | The author agrees that ma'a signals accompaniment but rejects the simile and the generalization to believers: the context chiefly reassures the Prophet, and the ease is joined to hardship.
[BINTSHATI:v1p69/r5] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الفراء والزجاج | reports | They argue that repeated definite al-usr refers to one known hardship, while the indefinite yusr presents two distinct eases; a report from the Prophet says one hardship cannot overcome two eases.
[BINTSHATI:v1p70/r1] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · أبو حيان | reports | Al-Bahr reports the view that one hardship comes with two eases because hardship is definite and ease indefinite.
[BINTSHATI:v1p70/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الجرجاني | rejects | Al-Jurjani warns that repeating a sentence about a horse with a sword does not prove the horse has two swords.
[BINTSHATI:v1p70/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · النيسابوري | reports | Al-Nisaburi explores generic or definite hardship and repeated or new sentences, concluding that a sound hadith would require one hardship and two distinct eases; without it, several readings remain possible.
[BINTSHATI:v1p70/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | rejects | The author finds the competing grammatical scenarios needlessly complex; some commentators also identify the two eases as worldly and afterlife relief or as conquests and afterlife reward.
[BINTSHATI:v1p71/r1] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | prefers | The second verse confirms the first to strengthen the Prophet's inward certainty in God's gifts, rather than introduce a separate promise.
[BINTSHATI:v1p71/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | prefers | Al-usr is definite by reference to the Prophet's distress at facing entrenched idolatry, while indefinite yusr leaves the relief open rather than setting limits on it.
[BINTSHATI:v1p71/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | Usr denotes the most severe hardship and struggle, with Arabic usage for intense physical constriction and Qur'anic descriptions of the Day's hardship and other acute trials. | mentions 54:8, 74:9, 25:26, 92:10, 65:6, 65:7, 9:117, 18:73
[BINTSHATI:v1p72/r1] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | Yusr is the opposite of hardship; Arabic also uses it for wealth and for something prepared or achieved without difficulty, conveying relief and release here. | mentions 65:7, 2:185, 2:280, 74:9, 92:7, 92:10, 2:196, 73:20, 54:17, 44:58

[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p363/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The prior sūrah predicts a bright future; this one points to predictions already beginning to materialize and assures the Prophet that hardship yields to success if faced courageously.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p363/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The discourse proceeds from inner expansion, through the recurring law of hardship with relief, to completion of the mission and disciplined use of its success.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p366/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | These verses teach the Prophet to endure affliction patiently: earlier hardship was followed by relief, and the same divine pattern will continue through the mission.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p366/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · most commentators | reports | Most commentators take the repeated statement as mere emphasis.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p366/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | rejects | Islahi rejects mere emphatic repetition: hardship and ease coexist and recur in cycles, so each success is followed by further trials requiring continued struggle on the path of truth.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p367/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Those who remain steadfast through trials receive renewed ease after each difficulty, helping them continue toward their ultimate destination.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p367/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Trials distinguish the righteous from hypocrites and believers from disbelievers, so reward and punishment follow deeds without grounds for a complaint of injustice.
