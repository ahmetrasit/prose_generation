[BIQAI:91:8/r1] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | Human wrongdoing is astonishing: people knowingly pursue what harms them and fight anyone who restrains them, whereas animals avoid visible harm; reason and revelation condemn vice, while desire makes it alluring and piety difficult.

[BIQAI:91:8/r3] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | Fujūr is the soul's rush to follow the promptings of desire without fear, breaking the boundary of the Sacred Law.
[BIQAI:91:8/r4] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | Taqwā is fear that stills the soul and makes it protect itself with the safeguards of the Sacred Law.
[BIQAI:91:8/r5] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | The verse uses iḥtibāk: mentioning vice first signals its opposite, stillness, later; mentioning piety later signals its opposite, absence of fear, earlier.
[BIQAI:91:8/r6] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | Attributing both dispositions to the soul makes them its responsibility, though God creates and decrees them: He gives it power and choice for either path, explains both through scripture and messengers, and thereby establishes proof while concealing destiny and outcome.
