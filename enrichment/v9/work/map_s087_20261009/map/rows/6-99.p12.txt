[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#5/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · تميم وربيعة | reports | Two further dialectal forms use ya rather than waw: قنيان with qaf ḍamma or kasra; both are Tamim and Rabiʿa forms.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#5/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | These dialects retain waw in the singular and never use ya there, so the plural unexpectedly differs from the singular stem.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#5/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The view that قنو means a palm’s heart or pith is nearly mistaken: the pith is inside the palm, unlike the nearby fruit; the established meaning is a cluster.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#5/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو عبيد | reports | Abu ʿUbayd says the dual of قنو is قنوان with kasra on nun and the plural matches it, like صنو/صنوان, an otherwise unmatched form.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#5/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | In pause, dual and plural قنوان are homonyms; in connected speech, case marking distinguishes them.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#6/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib compares قناة to قنو because both resemble branches; a water channel is explained by its straight extension or as a place that stores water.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#6/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | جنات may instead coordinate with خضرا.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#6/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | prefers | Zamakhshari prefers taking accusative جنات as اختصاص, singling out these kinds like والمقيمي الصلاة; al-Samin says his wording implies he treats nominative as famous and accusative as anomalous.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#6/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib also derives قناة from قاناه, ‘mixed it’; Imruʾ al-Qays’s مقاناة البياض بصفرة supports mixture.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#6/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الراغب | reports | Nasal قنا, the curvature of a nose, is named by resemblance in shape; a man is أقنى and a woman قنواء.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#6/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | طلع is the first growth to emerge from the palm within its enclosing sheaths.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#6/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو عبيد | reports | Abu ʿUbayd defines الطلع as the kafara before it splits from agrīḍ, also called طلع; أطلعت النخلة describes the palm producing it while طلع الطلع describes the growth’s emergence.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#6/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | An unnamed poem lists palm-fruit stages: طلع, خلال, بلح, بسر, رطب, then تمر.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#6/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الجمهور | reports | The majority read جنات accusative, coordinating it with نبات: the water brings forth plants generally and gardens specifically.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#6/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | This is a specific-after-general construction honoring these kinds, like naming Gabriel and Michael after the angels and messengers. | mentions 2:98
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#6/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | On this parse, the palm clause is parenthetical and formed as a topic-predicate sentence to magnify the blessing of dates, a major Arab food combining enjoyment and sustenance.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#7/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Al-Aʿmash, Muhammad ibn Abi Layla, and Abu Bakr in a report from ʿAsim read وجنات in the nominative.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#7/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | As a nominative subject with omitted predicate, al-Zamakhshari supplies ثم, while Abu al-Baqa supplies من الكرم, matching ومن النخل.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#7/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Al-Nahhas estimates ولهم and Ibn ʿAtiyya ولكم; the omitted-predicate construction also parallels وحور عين, whose understood predicate is لهم, and is accepted by Sibawayh, al-Kisaʾi, and al-Farraʾ. | mentions 56:22
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#7/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A delayed omitted predicate can be أخرجناها, inferred from the earlier فأخرجنا; Ibn al-Anbari had already analyzed the phrase this way.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#7/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Al-Farazdaq's line raises الخمر although it is the object, understood as والخمر أحلها الطعنة, illustrating delayed coordination with an omitted predicate.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#7/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن الأنباري | rejects | Ibn al-Anbari also allows coordination on قنوان by proximity: eyes follow brows though they are not plucked, just as grape gardens do not come from palm flowers; the author calls this defense weak and characteristic of adjectival attraction.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#7/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Coordination on قنوان is also possible if من النخل does not scope over the gardens: palm clusters and separately produced grape gardens; al-Tabari gives this analysis, Ibn ʿAtiyya weakens it, and Abu al-Baqa objects that grapes do not grow from palms.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#7/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | من أعناب is an adjectival phrase modifying جنات; it is nominative with the nominative reading and accusative with the other reading, and depends on an omitted element.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#8/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | All readers have الزيتون and الرمان in the accusative; they may coordinate with جنات or with نبات, as al-Zamakhshari and Abu al-Baqa explain.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#8/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Ibn ʿAtiyya instead coordinates these two nouns with حبا; a later conjunct may attach either to the immediately preceding item or to the first item in the series.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#8/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | زيتون follows the pattern فيعول, with ت extra and ن original, supported by زتنة for land rich in olives and by the pattern قيصوم; فعلول is absent or rare.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#8/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | زيت and زيتون are distinct roots despite oil being extracted from olives; زات الطعام means adding oil, زات الرأس means anointing it, and ازدات reflects ت changing to د after ز.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#8/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | رمان has the pattern فعال with original ن, like عناب and حماض; رمنة describes land rich in pomegranates.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#9/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | مشتبها is a circumstantial accusative most naturally referring to the nearer الرمان, with an omitted matching state for الزيتون; resemblance concerns color and difference concerns taste.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#9/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari instead applies both states to both olives and pomegranates, with omitted repeated predicates; he compares the ellipsis with 9:62 and a poetic line. | mentions 9:62
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#9/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | rejects | The cited poem does not prove omitted predicates: بريئا can predicate of singular, dual, or plural, and can be shared by the pronoun and its conjunct; as two circumstantial states it would need dual agreement.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#9/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqa says the state applies to the pomegranate and to all the fruits; that is semantically sound if ellipsis is understood, but not as literal syntactic agreement.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99#9/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الجمهور | reports | The majority read singular مشتبها; an anomalous reading has متشابها وغير متشابه, with the same sense, since افتعال and تفاعل often share meanings.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | فأخرجنا shifts from third-person reference to the first-person plural of majesty.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The باء in به expresses causation.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | One interpretation takes نبات كل شيء to mean everything linguistically called a plant.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farra interprets the phrase as the provision or food suitable to nourish each thing, restricting it to what feeds.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الطبري | reports | Al-Tabari includes all growing things among animals, plants, and minerals, since all are nourished by water.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | On the non-Farra interpretation, the annexation resembles an adjective and described noun: نبات means المنبت, not a verbal noun as in 71:17. | mentions 71:17
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | On al-Farra’s food/provision reading, the annexation joins distinct things: the food or provision of every thing.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:99/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farra also allows نبات to be annexed to كل شيء while intending كل شيء as نبات, like حق اليقين; al-Samin notes this additional view was omitted by the Shaykh. | mentions 56:95
