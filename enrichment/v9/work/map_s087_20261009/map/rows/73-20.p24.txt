[TABATABAI:73:20/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The verse eases the earlier night-prayer command by allowing what is manageable from the Quran, without abrogating the original instruction to stand the specified portions.
[TABATABAI:73:20/r10] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Adna is a comparative from nearness, conventionally meaning just less than an amount: a count near ten is nine, not eleven.
[TABATABAI:73:20/r11] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The waw is additive: on some nights the Prophet stands just under two-thirds, on others half, and on others a third.
[TABATABAI:73:20/r12] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Those with the Prophet means his fellow believers, and min is partitive; some of them also kept night vigil.
[TABATABAI:73:20/r13] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | reports | Another parsing takes min as explanatory; the author dismisses this reading.
[TABATABAI:73:20/r14] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | God knows because He is the creator and determiner; determining night and day entails determining their thirds, halves, and two-thirds.
[TABATABAI:73:20/r15] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The verse names Allah in connection with measurement because determining measures is a matter of creation, and all creation belongs to Him.
[TABATABAI:73:20/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | Reports place this Meccan verse eight months, a year, or ten years after the sura's opening verses.
[TABATABAI:73:20/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | prefers | Prayer followed by obligatory zakat and distinguished from voluntary charity suggests zakat here is obligatory, which was instituted in Medina after the Hijra.
[TABATABAI:73:20/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | reports | Some say zakat was imposed in Mecca without specified thresholds, while Medina later specified its thresholds.
[TABATABAI:73:20/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | The claim that Meccan zakat was imposed without specifying thresholds is unsupported.
[TABATABAI:73:20/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | reports | Some propose that the verse was revealed earlier but its legal ruling took effect later; the author calls this unsupported.
[TABATABAI:73:20/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | The delayed-ruling proposal is also conjecture without evidence.
[TABATABAI:73:20/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | prefers | Mentioning fighting would not suit the early mission's circumstances, so the verse is apparently Medinan; some others also favor this dating.
[TABATABAI:73:20/r9] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Addressing God as your Lord intimates divine mercy and care; noting that He knows the Prophet's vigil also suggests gratitude for his effort, as in 17:19. | mentions 17:19
