[NASHR:v1p268/r1] NASHR, d. 833 · author | holds | Optional recitation forms authorize the reader to use any one of them; they do not require combining every form at the same location, including pause forms and alternatives at two silent letters.
[NASHR:v1p268/r2] NASHR, d. 833 · author | reports | Some verifiers use only the strongest form and regard other forms as permitted; others let the reader choose, while some vary choices across locations to demonstrate the full oral transmission.
[NASHR:v1p268/r3] NASHR, d. 833 · author | holds | Combining every variant at every location is an uninformed strain; combining Hamza’s rare stop forms can train beginners, but is not required of proficient readers.
[NASHR:v1p268/r4] NASHR, d. 833 · مشايخ الأندلس | reports | Some Andalusian teachers use only one of Qālūn’s two plural-mīm forms, either sukūn or ṣila, following the clear wording that leaves him a choice.
[NASHR:v2p195/r1] NASHR, d. 833 · author | holds | Traditional teachers allowed combined readings only after a student separately completed each reading and mastered its routes; combining too early was driven by impatience and a wish to advance quickly.
[NASHR:v2p195/r2] NASHR, d. 833 · الكمال الضرير | reports | Al-Kamāl al-Ḍarīr read al-Shāṭibī’s seven readings over nineteen full cycles, separating al-Bazzī and Qunbul before combining them; al-Shāṭibī later directed him to combine Abū al-Ḥārith with al-Dūrī.
[NASHR:v2p195/r3] NASHR, d. 833 · author | holds | The author says his teachers required twenty-one separate cycles of the seven readings before combination, and likewise for the ten.
[NASHR:v2p195/r4] NASHR, d. 833 · عبد الوهاب القروي الإسكندري | reports | ʿAbd al-Wahhāb al-Qarawī read the seven readings according to al-Iʿlān in forty full cycles with al-Qawṣī.
[NASHR:v2p195/r5] NASHR, d. 833 · author | reports | More lenient teachers allowed one cycle per reader, except that Nāfiʿ’s Qālūn and Warsh transmissions and Ḥamza’s Khalaf and Khallād transmissions each required a separate cycle.
[NASHR:v2p195/r6] NASHR, d. 833 · author | reports | The author recounts that he had completed Abū ʿAmr and Ḥamza separately with Ibn al-Sallār, but was denied general combination because he had not individually studied all readings; only Nāfiʿ and Ibn Kathīr could be combined.
[NASHR:v2p195/r7] NASHR, d. 833 · author | holds | Once a student has individually mastered and combined readings with a recognized teacher and been authorized, no further individual cycles are required before combining in one cycle; al-Qalānisī read al-Kāmil in one cycle with al-Hudhalī.
[NASHR:v2p27/r1] NASHR, d. 833 · author | reports | Some reciters call assimilation with retained ghunna into wāw/yāʾ, and in some transmissions into lām/rāʾ, incomplete; without ghunna they call it complete.
[NASHR:v2p27/r2] NASHR, d. 833 · أبو الحسن السخاوي | reports | Al-Sakhāwī argues that what is called assimilation while ghunna remains is actually concealment, with only a slight doubling.
[NASHR:v2p27/r3] NASHR, d. 833 · author | prefers | The author prefers incomplete assimilation: retained ghunna is like the remaining emphatic sound in «أحطت» and «بسطت», and the audible shadda proves assimilation because concealment does not take shadda.
[NASHR:v2p27/r4] NASHR, d. 833 · الحافظ أبو عمرو | reports | Abū ʿAmr defines complete assimilation as leaving no trace of the merged letter; retained nūn or tanwīn ghunna therefore resembles concealment, whereas removing it changes the letter fully into the following sound.
[NASHR:v2p27/r5] NASHR, d. 833 · أبو عمرو الداني | prefers | Al-Dānī restricts retained ghunna before lām to cases where the nūn remains in the written form; he omits it when the muṣḥaf omits that nūn, as in the cited Hūd and al-Kahf examples.
[NASHR:v2p438/r1] NASHR, d. 833 · author | holds | When connecting the final sūra to the tahlīl, retain the final word’s original vowel or sukūn; assimilate tanwīn, as in «لخبير لا إله إلا الله».
[NASHR:v2p438/r2] NASHR, d. 833 · author | reports | Lengthening «لا إله إلا الله» is allowed as a devotional magnification; some teachers always lengthened it as remembrance, while most readers shortened it in keeping with their rule for separated madd, and both are accepted.
[NASHR:v2p438/r3] NASHR, d. 833 · author | reports | If takbīr belongs to the end of a sūra, pronounce it before stopping and begin the next sūra with basmala alone; if it belongs to the beginning, stop without it and pronounce it at the next sūra’s start.
[NASHR:v2p438/r4] NASHR, d. 833 · author | holds | That end-versus-beginning distinction also governs the extra takbīr at prostration in the final sūra of al-ʿAlaq and at the transition to bowing in prayer.
[NASHR:v2p438/r5] NASHR, d. 833 · author | holds | If using Ḥamza’s takbīr between sūras according to those who permit it, the reader must include basmala by intending to stop at the previous sūra and begin a new one.
