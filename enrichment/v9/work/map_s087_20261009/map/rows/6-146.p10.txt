[ELMALILI:6:143-147#3/r15] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The broad reading fits the contrast: Jews held that animals both cud-chewing and split-hoofed were lawful, leaving other kinds forbidden to them.
[ELMALILI:6:143-147#3/r16] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | That Jewish criterion would also make fish forbidden, although fish were not prohibited to them, showing their own account of the restrictions was inaccurate.
[ELMALILI:6:143-147#3/r17] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The Qur'an's contrast excludes fish yet signals that the restriction included swine, predators, single-hoofed animals, camels, and all birds, intensifying the Jews' dietary constraint.
[ELMALILI:6:143-147#3/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Kāmûs | reports | The lexicon says mihlab is the claw of predatory beasts and hooked claw of predatory birds; one view reserves zufur for non-hunting birds, while the famous view makes it broader than mihlab.
[ELMALILI:6:143-147#3/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Sparrows and domestic hens unquestionably have zufur; hawks and falcons unquestionably have mihlab, while their inclusion among zufur is disputed.
