[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p495/r5] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | reports | Another view keeps them between hope and apprehension, which discourages heedlessness and neglect.
[RAZI-FULL:v16p139#2/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Confession alone is not repentance; repentance combines regret for the past and resolve to stop in future, with regret because God prohibited the act.
[RAZI-FULL:v16p139#2/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · المفسرون | reports | Razi says the commentators take God’s use of ʿasā as indicating that their repentance will certainly be accepted.
[RAZI-FULL:v16p139#2/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · الأول | reports | One interpretation makes the good deed their confession, regret, and repentance, and the bad deed their failure to join the expedition.
[RAZI-FULL:v16p139#2/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · الثاني | reports | A second interpretation takes their good deeds as joining the Messenger on other campaigns and the bad deed as missing Tabūk.
[RAZI-FULL:v16p139#2/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · الثالث | reports | A third view says the verse concerns Muslims, and the good deed is the good works they had performed.
[RAZI-FULL:v16p139#2/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Here mixing means simple combination, not intermixture that changes each item’s original quality, as in mixing water and milk.
[RAZI-FULL:v16p139#2/r7] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | rejects | Razi uses the wording to reject cancellation of deeds: obedience retains its praise and reward, sin its blame and punishment; because both are described as mixed, both must remain present, since an attribute cannot exist without its subject.
[RAZI-FULL:v16p139/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | Two views identify the people who confessed: repentant hypocrites, or Muslims who missed Tabūk through laziness rather than unbelief or hypocrisy, then regretted and repented.
[RAZI-FULL:v16p139/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · قائلون بالقول الأول | reports | Those favoring the first view cite the conjunction with the nearby mention of hypocritical Bedouins: the first group persisted in hypocrisy, while this group was enabled to repent.
[RAZI-FULL:v16p139/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | A report names three men, while another says ten, seven of whom bound themselves in the mosque. The Prophet initially refused to release them without revelation, then freed and excused them; their request that he take their wealth preceded 9:103.
[RAZI-FULL:v16p139/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Linguistically, confession is acknowledging something with knowledge; its nuance is that they did not invent excuses but admitted wrongdoing, regretted it, and blamed themselves for staying behind.
[RAZI-FULL:v16p140#2/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Their confession occurred in the past, while the hoped-for repentance is future; confession therefore preceded and was not itself repentance.
[RAZI-FULL:v16p140/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · المفسرون | reports | The commentators say divine ʿasā means certainty, citing 5:52 where the promised victory occurred; human speech conventions let a great ruler use ʿasā to signal that his acts are favor, not an obligation, while still guaranteeing the answer. | mentions 5:52
[RAZI-FULL:v16p140/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Another answer is that ʿasā keeps the person morally responsible in hope and apprehension, discouraging denial and neglect.
[RAZI-FULL:v16p140/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · أصحابنا | holds | Razi’s school reads this as repentance being created by God: regret is not chosen by the servant, since willing it would require another will and people cannot dismiss either desire or regret at will.
[RAZI-FULL:v16p140/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · المعتزلة | reports | The Muʿtazilites interpret God’s repentance as His accepting the servant’s repentance.
[RAZI-FULL:v16p140/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | rejects | Razi rejects the Muʿtazilite interpretation: departing from the apparent meaning is warranted only when proof makes it impossible, whereas reason here supports the apparent reading.
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:102#2/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · قيل | reports | One grammatical interpretation takes the conjunction wāw as meaning bi- (with), so the phrase means mixing a good deed with a bad one.
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:102#2/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · قيل | reports | Another interpretation takes wāw as maʿa (with), like the expression that water is level with the wood.
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:102#2/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الكوفيون | reports | The Kufan grammarians reject the maʿa interpretation: wood cannot precede water, whereas the verse permits the second object to precede the first; they parse it like mixing water with milk.
