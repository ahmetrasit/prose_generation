[TABATABAI:7:161-171#3/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · أبو عبد الله عليه السلام | reports | Al-Majmaʿ reports from Abu Abdillah that both offending factions perished while the faction that forbade evil was saved.
[TABATABAI:7:161-171#3/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · أبو عبد الله عليه السلام | reports | A report in al-Kafi divides the people into those who obeyed and enjoined good and were saved, those who obeyed but did not enjoin and were transformed, and those who did neither and perished.
[TABATABAI:7:161-171#3/r9] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · أبو جعفر عليه السلام | reports | Al-Saduq transmits through Talha the same three-part account: those who obeyed and enjoined were saved, those who obeyed without enjoining were transformed, and those who did neither perished.
[TABATABAI:7:164-166/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Their forgetting means the reminder ceased to affect them, not that they literally forgot it; divine punishment follows disdain for God's command and turning away from remembrance.
[TABATABAI:7:164-166/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Repeated sin first causes inner pain, then repetition strengthens the sinful image and weakens the reminder until it has no effect, as though forgotten.
[TABATABAI:7:164-166/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Only those who forbade evil were saved; God seized both the Sabbath transgressors and those who asked why they should be admonished.
[TABATABAI:7:164-166/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The questioners shared in the transgressors' wrongdoing because they abandoned admonition and did not separate themselves from them.
[TABATABAI:7:164-166/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | God's general rule is that failing to restrain wrongdoing, or to admonish or shun wrongdoers when restraint is impossible or ineffective, makes one complicit and liable to severe punishment.
[TABATABAI:7:170-171/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · أبو جعفر عليه السلام | reports | Al-Bāqir, citing ʿAlī's book, says Sabbath fish were sent visibly; some exploited a loophole to catch them that day and eat later, while one faction warned and withdrew, another stayed silent, and the warners ultimately left town fearing shared punishment.
[ASAD-NOTES:7:165#n132/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | The saved group is described literally as those who were forbidding evil.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:165/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Those who tried to stop evil were saved, persistent violators were punished for their wrongdoing, and the verse leaves the fate of the third group unstated.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:165/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · bir görüş | reports | One reported view says those who failed to oppose evil share the wrongdoers’ fate and punishment.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:165/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Abdullah b. Abbas | reports | Ibn Abbas is reported either to have withheld judgment about the non-admonishers or, in another report, to have said they too would be punished.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:165/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · başka bir rivayet | reports | A separate report attributed to Ibn Abbas says the non-admonishers would also be punished.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:165/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | prefers | The preferred view, also attributed to Hasan al-Basri, is that non-admonishers were saved because they recognized the offenders’ conduct as evil.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:165/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Râzî | reports | Al-Razi supports that view by treating the duty to oppose wrongdoing, including through force as well as verbal or written criticism, as a collective obligation.
[QIRAAT-ER:7:165:18#1/r1] QIRAAT-ER · author | holds | Eliding hamza changes baʾīs to bīs, simplifying articulation without semantic change.
[QIRAAT-ER:7:165:18#2/r1] QIRAAT-ER · author | holds | A compressed short-vowel form may evoke biʾs as an abstract noun of wretchedness rather than an adjective.
[QIRAAT-ER:7:165:18#3/r1] QIRAAT-ER · author | holds | The bayʾas variant metathesizes hamza in an archaic morphological pattern while preserving the root consonants.
