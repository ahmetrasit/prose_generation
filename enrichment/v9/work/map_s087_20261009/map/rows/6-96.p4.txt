[RAZI-FULL:v13p78/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | A body receives light only when it faces the luminous source, a premise philosophers and mathematicians accept; since the sun is below the horizon at dawn, it cannot directly illuminate the ground, so God creates dawn's light.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p78/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · أبو علي بن الهيثم | reports | Ibn al-Haytham proposes that the sun below earth lights the opposite air, which successively illuminates adjacent air until the light reaches the atmosphere above us.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p78/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | rejects | Razi rejects this: transparent, colorless air cannot receive light or color in its substance; if it did, sight would stop at its surface and fail to see what lies beyond, so it could not reflect light onward.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p78/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · القائلون | reports | Another objection posits dense vapors and smoke that absorb sunlight and illuminate neighboring air; Razi rejects it because more vapor should strengthen dawn, whereas observation shows the opposite.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p79#2/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Since bodies equally admit every property, the sun’s distinctive illumination requires specification by a voluntary agent.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p79#2/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Darkness is deprivation and light pure existence; night brings fear, sleep, and cessation, while dawn restores wakefulness, perception, and movement.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p79#2/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Because light enables life, sensation, and movement, creating it is among God’s greatest blessings and proofs of power, mercy, and generosity.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p79#2/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | He also reads the dawn splitting figuratively: God brings creation from nonexistence, life and reason from inertness, guidance from ignorance, and spiritual illumination from worldly darkness.
