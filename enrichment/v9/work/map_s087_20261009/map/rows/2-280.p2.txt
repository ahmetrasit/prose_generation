[THALABI:v2p285/r16] THALABI, d. 427 · أبي وابن مسعود وابن عباس | reports | Ubayy, Ibn Masud, and Ibn Abbas read dhā ʿusratin, with an implied subject such as the creditor or debtor.

[THALABI:v2p286#2/r1] THALABI, d. 427 · author | holds | An insolvent debtor must be granted time until solvent; the creditor may not demand payment before then, and the debt must be paid in full once solvency appears.
[THALABI:v2p286#2/r2] THALABI, d. 427 · author | holds | For debts incurred in exchange for money received, such as a loan or purchase, a debtor claiming insolvency must prove it because receipt establishes an initial presumption of means.
[THALABI:v2p286#2/r3] THALABI, d. 427 · author | holds | For obligations without money received, such as dowry or guarantee, the creditor must prove the debtor is solvent, since the presumption is that people are poor; absent evidence of prior means, retain the original presumption.
[THALABI:v2p286#2/r4] THALABI, d. 427 · الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan says a debtor who declares himself indigent is believed on oath, while creditors must establish his assets by evidence or direct observation.
[THALABI:v2p286#2/r5] THALABI, d. 427 · أبو حنيفة | reports | Abu Hanifa would detain the debtor for two or three months, inquire privately, then release him if insolvency was confirmed.
[THALABI:v2p286#2/r6] THALABI, d. 427 · أبو سعيد الخدري | reports | A report from Abu Said al-Khudri supports not imprisoning an insolvent debtor: after a man lost his fruit crop and his debt grew, the Prophet said to take whatever was found, and nothing else was owed.
[THALABI:v2p286#2/r7] THALABI, d. 427 · أبو هريرة | reports | As judge in Medina, Abu Hurayra refused to jail a debtor with no visible property or assets to sell; he should earn for himself and his family and repay when solvent.
[THALABI:v2p286#2/r8] THALABI, d. 427 · ابن عباس | reports | The Prophet promised that a person who goes to a debtor to collect his right receives prayers from earthly creatures, a tree in Paradise and forgiveness for every step; a debtor who delays is transgressive.
[THALABI:v2p286#2/r9] THALABI, d. 427 · أبو هريرة | reports | The Prophet calls a solvent person’s delay in repayment injustice and instructs creditors to accept transfer to a solvent payer.
[THALABI:v2p286/r1] THALABI, d. 427 · author | holds | ʿUsrah means poverty, constraint, or hardship; Abu Jaʿfar reads the word with a damma on the sin, another dialect form.

[THALABI:v2p286/r11] THALABI, d. 427 · author | holds | Giving the poor debtor charity means remitting the principal owed; it is better for creditors if they know.
[THALABI:v2p286/r12] THALABI, d. 427 · عاصم | reports | Asim reads taṣṣaddaqū with a light ṣad; the other readers pronounce it doubled.
[THALABI:v2p286/r2] THALABI, d. 427 · author | holds | Fa-naẓirat is an imperative in the form of a declarative; the conditional fa introduces it, and its meaning is that deferment is obligatory.
