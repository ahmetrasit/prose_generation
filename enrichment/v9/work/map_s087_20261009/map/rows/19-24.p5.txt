[ALUSI-FULL:v16p82#2/r9] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · البراء؛ الحاكم | reports | Al-Ḥākim transmits from al-Barāʾ that sari means a stream and grades the report sound according to the two Ṣaḥīḥ collections; al-Bukhārī cites it suspended, while connected versions are mawqūf, not soundly raised to the Prophet.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p86/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The one calling Mary is the baby she carried; the phrase من تحتها can mean from beneath her, or use من as a relative pronoun for the boy below her. The latter reading makes the newborn's speech a sign of Jesus and an honor to Mary. | mentions 19:22
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p86/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | Readers differ in parsing من تحتها: Nafi, Hamza, al-Kisai, Hafs, Abu Jafar, Khalaf, and Ruh read من as a preposition; Ibn Kathir, Abu Amr, Ibn Amir, Abu Bakr, and Ruways read it as a relative pronoun for the child.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p86/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Specifying that the baby called from beneath her depicts him speaking as soon as he was born, before she lifted him, to comfort her at once and portray the intimate moment of birth.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p86/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | أنلا is explanatory of the call; the statement about the stream explains why Mary should not grieve, since her circumstances merit joy as a divine honor.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p86/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | سريا means a flowing stream; God provides pleasant food and drink as a visible honor and evidence of her innocence to Yusuf the carpenter and any witnesses. Water nearby and fresh dates out of season from a dead palm would be extraordinary; fresh fruit is more appealing than dried dates.
[TABATABAI:19:21-24/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | prefers | The one calling from beneath Mary is Jesus, since the phrase fits a newborn at his mother's delivery and nearby pronouns refer to him.
[TABATABAI:19:21-24/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | The alternative says the spirit stood beneath a hill where Mary delivered, but the wording provides no evidence for that scenario.
[TABATABAI:19:21-24/r9] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The sequence suggests Mary spoke her wish at or just after delivery and Jesus immediately comforted her; her fear of dishonor was especially grave because she was a chaste, defenseless virgin from a respected family.
[TABATABAI:19:24-26/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | prefers | Sari means a stream, which fits the following command to eat and drink; the alternate meaning “noble, elevated person,” identified with Jesus, is not supported by context.
[TABATABAI:19:40/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | Reports differ over whether Gabriel or Jesus caused a spring to appear by striking the ground; the report from al-Bāqir favors Jesus.
[TABATABAI:19:40/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · النبي | reports | The Prophet identifies the sariyy beneath Mary as a river; another report through Ibn ʿUmar says God made it flow for her to drink.

[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p433/r2] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · الحسن البصري | reports | Al-Hasan recited the verse and said Maryam’s child was a sariy; Khalid ibn Safwan clarified that Arabs call a stream sariy, and al-Hasan agreed.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p433/r3] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · author | holds | The form is unique in the Quran; related forms occur in the night-travel verb, the Prophet’s Night Journey, and commands to Lot and Moses to travel by night. | mentions 89:4, 17:1, 11:81, 15:65, 20:77, 26:52, 44:23

[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p434/r3] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · الحسن وغيره | reports | Al-Ḥasan and others take sariyy to mean Jesus himself; they argue that a river would be beside Mary, not underneath her.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p434/r4] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · author | prefers | The author favors a stream: the poetic evidence clearly supports that sense, and the wording 'under her' gives it a subtle, hidden quality.
