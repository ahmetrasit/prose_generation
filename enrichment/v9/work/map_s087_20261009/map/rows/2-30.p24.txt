[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#2/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | للملائكة attaches to قال, and ل is the particle of communication.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · بعضهم | reports | One etymology makes ملك a form فعَل from الملك with original mīm; on this account only its plural فعائلة is irregular.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · بعضهم | reports | Another original-mīm derivation starts from ملأك with an extra hamza like شمأل; its vowel shifts to the lām and the hamza drops, while the plural preserves the original addition.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#2/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · بعضهم | reports | A third derivation makes mīm extra and derives the word from ألك, to send. Poems with مألكة, بألوك and مألك attest the message sense; the derivation involves metathesis and hamza deletion, with the plural restoring hamza.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · بعضهم | reports | A fourth analysis derives it from لأك, also meaning to send; the poem فلست لإنسي ولكن لملأك witnesses the form, and the plural restores hamza.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#2/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · بعضهم | reports | A fifth derivation connects ملك to لاكه يلوكه, to turn something over, since an angel turns a message in the mouth; it derives singular ملوك and plural ملاوكة with vowel change, deletion and hamza formation.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#2/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · النضر بن شميل | reports | Al-Nadr ibn Shumayl says ملك has no derivation in Arab usage.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The ة in ملائكة marks a feminine plural as in صلادمة, or could be an intensifier as in علامة and نسابة; the author rejects that latter view. Poetry attests rare deletion of the suffix in الملائك.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | إني جاعل في الأرض خليفة is the quoted content and direct object of قال; إن is closed because it follows قال without a meaning of supposition.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#3/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | When قول means supposition, إن may be opened or closed; a poem closes it despite its sense, apparently following the surface form of قول.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#3/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | General rule: open إن when its clause can replace a verbal noun; close it when it cannot, such as after قول, initially, in a relative clause or circumstantial clause. Both are possible when substitution is ambiguous, such as after فاء الجزاء or إذا الفجائية.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#3/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | If جاعل means خالق, خليفة is its object and في الأرض either attaches to جاعل or is an omitted phrase circumstantially modifying the following indefinite noun.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#3/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari takes جاعل to mean صير, making خليفة the first object and في الأرض the fronted second object.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#3/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | خليفة can mean an active successor, succeeding the people addressed or the jinn who preceded them; this is stronger because its feminine suffix is regular for the active sense.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#3/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Alternatively, خليفة means one succeeded by each later generation; the feminine suffix is not regular for this passive sense unless the word has become noun-like, as in النطيحة and الذبيحة.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#3/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The singular خليفة can intend Adam and his descendants collectively, using the ancestor as with tribal names Mudar and Rabi'a; another view understands خليفة as a generic.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#4/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A reading substitutes خليقة with qāf for the verse’s خليفة.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#4/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The second فيها is governed by يفسد, and يسفك coordinates with يفسد under either parsing.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#4/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الجمهور | reports | The majority read يسفك in the indicative; another reading makes it subjunctive as an answer to the question with أن understood after wāw.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#4/r12] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | An anonymous poem beginning أتبيت ريان illustrates the subjunctive after an understood أن.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#4/r13] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the subjunctive governed by واو الصرف, a Kufan term for wāw redirecting inflection to the subjunctive.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#4/r14] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Readings vary between kasra and ḍamma on fāʾ, a ḍamma on the imperfect prefix from أسفك, and a doubled form for intensity.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#4/r15] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | سفك means pouring and is normally used only of blood; Ibn Fāris and al-Jawharī also allow tears, while al-Mahdawī adds scattered speech.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#4/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | خليفة is accusative as the object of جاعل; active participles govern like their verbs and may be annexed to their object for lightness if nothing intervenes.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#4/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | قالوا governs إذ قال ربك, which al-Samīn identifies as the preferred parsing.

[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#4/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | من may be a relative pronoun, leaving the following clause without an independent syntactic position; as an indefinite it makes the clause accusative.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:30#5/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | دماء is the plural of دم; a declinable noun cannot consist of two letters, so the third radical, its final letter, is omitted.
