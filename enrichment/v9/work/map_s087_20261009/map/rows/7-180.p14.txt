KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Hz. Peygamber | reports | The Prophet promises Paradise to those who memorize Allah’s ninety-nine names and internalize the beliefs their meanings teach.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:180/r11] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Tirmidhī and Ibn Mājah give a list of ninety-nine names after the hadith, but some listed names do not occur in the Qur’an.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:180/r12] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · bazı âlimler | reports | Some scholars argue that the lists in Tirmidhī and Ibn Mājah were a narrator’s personal additions, not part of the original hadith.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:180/r13] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · İbn Hacer el-Askalânî | reports | Ibn Hajar compiled a new list of ninety-nine beautiful names from names found in the Qur’an.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:180/r14] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | People should invoke Allah by His beautiful names alone, never assign them to another or treat anything else as His equal or divine.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:180/r15] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Ilḥād means departing from truth and moderation; one form is using names reserved for Allah, with their divine meanings, for another.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:180/r16] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | A second kind of deviation is describing God with names and attributes exclusive to created beings, such as father or son.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:180/r17] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | A third kind of deviation is denying God’s names outright or interpreting and distorting them in a way that amounts to denial.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:180/r18] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Those who distort the beautiful names from their true meanings are condemned and warned that their ill-intentioned conduct will be punished.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:180/r19] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The verse also warns against people who flatter rulers, leaders, heroes, or sheikhs so excessively that their words place the praised person in an almost divine position.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:180/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The Qur’an gives Allah more than one hundred names, hadiths attribute others, and the technical term is commonly taken to mean the ninety-nine names collected in a hadith.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:180/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Ebû Bekir İbnü’l-Arabî | reports | He says these names express Allah’s transcendence and greatness and awaken reverence in people. | mentions 17:110, 20:8, 59:24
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:180/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Ebû Bekir İbnü’l-Arabî | reports | He says invoking these names in remembrance and supplication helps prayers be accepted and earns reward. | mentions 17:110, 20:8, 59:24
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:180/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Ebû Bekir İbnü’l-Arabî | reports | He says the names bring peace to hearts and instill hope for mercy. | mentions 17:110, 20:8, 59:24
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:180/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Ebû Bekir İbnü’l-Arabî | reports | He says understanding and reciting the names of the highest being also elevates the reciter. | mentions 17:110, 20:8, 59:24
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:180/r7] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Ebû Bekir İbnü’l-Arabî | reports | He says the names express what is necessary, possible, and impossible regarding Allah, so knowing them helps form sound belief. | mentions 17:110, 20:8, 59:24
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:180/r8] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Names and attributes are reminders and partial introductions to their referent, not the referent itself; human understanding cannot encompass Allah, though the words intend Him.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:180/r9] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Because the Qur’an and hadith invoke Allah by these names, pronouncing them carries the worship value of remembrance, glorification, and supplication.
[QIRAAT-ER:7:180:12#1/r1] QIRAAT-ER · author | holds | Form I yalḥadūna means incline or dig laterally, while Form IV yulḥidūna means cause deviation or commit ilḥād; the first foregrounds physical action and the second causative abstraction.
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Following the heedless of verse 179, the verse tells people to call upon God by His Most Beautiful Names; commentators connect the verb with supplication and remembrance of God. | mentions 7:179
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r10] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Islamic metaphysics regards the cosmos as reflections or theophanies of the Divine Names and Qualities.
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r11] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The Names and Qualities are major concerns in theology, philosophy, and Sufism; the science of Divine Names includes al-jafr, symbolic numerology of Names and their letters.
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r12] STUDYQURAN · most Islamic thinkers | reports | They identify God's Names as Attributes, usually dividing them into Attributes of Essence and of action, while traditional sources differ on classification and on whether Attributes are distinct from or identical with Essence.
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r13] STUDYQURAN · Islamic esoterism | reports | It regards God and His Names as one; God is present in the sanctified Names, each a ladder leading to Him.
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r14] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Names are also divided into those of Beauty, such as Compassionate and Merciful, and Majesty, such as Judge and Compeller.
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r15] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | Some Names can be manifested in humans while others belong to God alone; human names may use the former but not the latter, and some Names fit both categories.
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r16] STUDYQURAN · some commentators | reports | One view says all God's Names are Most Beautiful; another narrows the phrase to Names of Beauty, encouraging invocation for mercy while discouraging invocation of Names of Majesty or Wrath.
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r17] STUDYQURAN · other commentators | reports | Others allow calling God by any Name or description found in the Quran, citing expressions such as completer of His Light and best of plotters. | mentions 61:8, 3:54, 58:7
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r18] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | God's supreme Name is Allāh, said to encompass all His other Names.
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r19] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Sufis commonly repeat Allāh or other Names prescribed by a shaykh to cultivate virtues and treat spiritual ailments, figuratively transmuting the soul from lead into gold.
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r2] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The verse is a scriptural basis for dhikr in Islamic spirituality, especially Sufism.
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r20] STUDYQURAN · Sufi commentators | reports | They view Quranic Names and Attributes as limits of human knowledge of God and as gates to His infinite reality, enabling knowledge beyond what is humanly accessible.
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r21] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | Those who deviate may use specifically human attributes such as father or son for God, attribute evil actions or intentions to Him, or anthropomorphize Him with ignorant or unworthy descriptions.
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r22] STUDYQURAN · several commentators | reports | They say God should be named only with descriptions in the Quran or Sunnah, even when another description is synonymous with a scriptural Name.
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r23] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Ritual invocation requires Arabic, but private supplication outside canonical prayer or Sufi dhikr may use non-Arabic names such as Persian Khudā or English God.
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r24] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | Deviation can mean using God's Names or their derivatives for false gods; the pagan Makkans worshipped Allāt, apparently a feminine form of Allāh.
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r3] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | God's Most Beautiful Names also appear in 20:8 and 59:24, while 17:110 similarly commands invocation by them. | mentions 20:8, 59:24, 17:110
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r4] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | The term al-ḥusnā relates to goodness and virtue, and the Names are beautiful both to the ear and to the heart.
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r5] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | A widely reported hadith underlies the traditional teaching that God has ninety-nine Beautiful Names. | mentions 59:24
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r6] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Lists of the Names vary and may exceed ninety-nine; they are grounded in Quranic descriptions, and some derive from divine actions described there.
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r7] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The Names do not compromise God's Oneness or divide Him into parts; they express qualities of His undifferentiated Essence that become differentiated through self-manifestation and perceived by the caller.
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r8] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The Names reveal both essential divine attributes and the different ways God relates to creation.
[STUDYQURAN:7:180/r9] STUDYQURAN · al-Ṭabrisī | reports | Each Name has a real Divine Presence or Quality; there are no empty titles for God.
