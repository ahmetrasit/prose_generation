[ELMALILI:98:1-4#2/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Fahr-ı Râzî | reports | A fifth proposal says the Prophet’s mission shook their prior certainty in inherited religions; some converted, while others divided through stubbornness despite recognizing their old religions’ weakness.
[ELMALILI:98:1-4#2/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Fahr-ı Râzî | reports | This proposal distinguishes infikak as detachment from something once clung to from tafarruq as dispersion, showing how the post-Prophet condition differed from the prior one.
[ELMALILI:98:1-4#3/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | prefers | The author finds his earlier explanation most apparent: before the decisive proof, people were in a state where they could not leave their inherited condition; after it, the People of the Book should have believed.
[ELMALILI:98:1-4#3/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The pledge reading is awkward when extended to polytheists; reading the object as their religions or disbelief would imply they should depart from them, though the proof was sent for that purpose.
[ELMALILI:98:1-4#3/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Before Muhammad’s mission was a period of interruption when religions were corrupted, pagans and non-pagans mixed, and truth and falsehood could not be distinguished. | mentions 5:19
[ELMALILI:98:1-4#3/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Given the rule that punishment does not come before a messenger, the interval is an excuse period without responsibility; before decisive proof, the default is to leave people in their existing state. | mentions 17:15
[ELMALILI:98:1-4#3/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Prophets are sent to illuminate truth and duty during such periods, distinguishing believers and disbelievers so accountability can apply.
[ELMALILI:98:1-4#3/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Following the previous surah’s account of revelation’s wisdom, this surah explains the pre-Prophetic interval: before Qur’an and Messenger the disbelievers could be excused, but after proof, especially the People of the Book’s rejection and division, they deserved punishment. | mentions 17:15
[ELMALILI:98:1-4/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Min can be partitive or explanatory, attached to an omitted phrase and describing the disbelievers; the syntax permits several ways of dividing the two groups.
[ELMALILI:98:1-4/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Munfakkin is the predicate of lam yakun, and hatta attaches to it: the disbelievers would not leave their condition before the clear proof came.
[ELMALILI:98:1-4/r11] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Bayyina denotes an evident proof that makes another matter clear, such as a witness, articulate proof, or miracle; here it is a decisive proof establishing truth.
[ELMALILI:98:1-4/r12] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The bayyina is the Messenger from God, either by apposition or as an explanation of bayyina through an omitted subject and predicate.
[ELMALILI:98:1-4/r15] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Before a messenger, people could not fully know or unite upon true monotheism and could be excused; after the proof came, the People of the Book had no excuse for persisting in disbelief. | mentions 17:15
[ELMALILI:98:1-4/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The wording can mean some disbelievers from each group, disbelievers as two groups, or disbelievers who belong to both the People of the Book and the polytheists.
[ELMALILI:98:1-4/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | prefers | The reading that includes People of the Book who also held explicit or implicit shirk best fits verse 3, which names only them. | mentions 98:3
[ELMALILI:98:1-4/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The People of the Book are Jews and Christians; naming them this way suggests their rejection of Muhammad and the Qur’an also amounts to rejecting their own Torah and Gospel.
[ELMALILI:98:1-4/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Polytheists include all who assign divinity to anything besides Allah, broader than idol worshippers; it includes polytheists and those making their desires gods. | mentions 79:24, 28:38
[ELMALILI:98:1-4/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Since Allah alone is truly worthy of worship, assigning worship to another independently or in partnership is shirk; multiplicity of attributes with unity of essence is not.
[ELMALILI:98:1-4/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The Trinity is shirk and self-contradictory because it claims both plurality and unity of divine essence.
[ELMALILI:98:1-4/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | prefers | Lam makes the category general, so polytheists include all kinds; some instead restrict the term to the familiar Arab idolaters of Mecca and Medina.
[ELMALILI:98:1-4/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | rejects | He rejects restricting the phrase to known Arab idolaters, since the prior definite phrase already identifies the known disbelievers as some within both broader groups.
[QUTB-ZILAL:98:1/r1] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | reports | The surah is Madinan according to most reports, though some say Meccan; Qutb favors Madinan on the basis of reports and its declarative style but does not rule out a Meccan origin.
[QUTB-ZILAL:98:1/r2] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | Mention of the People of the Book and zakah does not exclude a Meccan origin: some People of the Book lived in Mecca, Najran Christians visited and believed, and zakah appears in Meccan surahs.
[QUTB-ZILAL:98:1/r3] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | The surah's four central truths are that the final Messenger's mission was necessary, the People of the Book divided after proof, religion's core is simple and one, and the disbelievers and believers face different fates. | mentions 98:2, 98:4, 98:5, 98:6, 98:7, 98:8
[QUTB-ZILAL:98:1/r4] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | The earth had become deeply corrupt, with both scriptural communities having distorted earlier religions and polytheism widespread; only a new message, method, and movement could bring reform.
[QUTB-ZILAL:98:1/r5] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | Those disbelievers would not abandon their condition without the new message and a Messenger who himself served as a decisive, clear proof.
[IBNASHUR:98:1/r1] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أنس بن مالك | reports | Al-Bukhārī and Muslim report from Anas that the Prophet said God had commanded him to recite the sūrah to Ubayy; Ubayy asked whether he had been named and, told yes, wept. Its opening clause names the whole sūrah.
[IBNASHUR:98:1/r10] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya argues for a Medinan setting on the ground that the Prophet only confronted the People of the Book there.
[IBNASHUR:98:1/r11] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | It ranks 101st in revelation, after al-Ṭalāq and before al-Ḥashr; since Banū al-Naḍīr was in Rabīʿ I, year four, it was revealed in late year three or early year four. | mentions 65:1, 59:1
[IBNASHUR:98:1/r12] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Most authorities count eight verses in the sūrah, while the people of Basra count nine.
[IBNASHUR:98:1/r13] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The sūrah rebukes the polytheists and People of the Book for rejecting the Qurʾān and the Messenger, and exposes the contradiction of awaiting the clear proof and then rejecting it.
[IBNASHUR:98:1/r14] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Among the sūrah’s aims are to refute their claim that God required them to remain with their existing religions, warn them of the afterlife, and mark them as the worst creatures.
[IBNASHUR:98:1/r15] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The sūrah praises believers who do good and promises them everlasting bliss, God’s approval, and whatever will satisfy them.
[IBNASHUR:98:1/r16] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | It also extols the Qurʾān above earlier scripture because it contains what the Messenger brought in previous divine books and adds further merit.
[IBNASHUR:98:1/r17] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · الفخر | reports | Al-Wāḥidī, quoted by Fakhr al-Dīn from his al-Tafsīr al-Basīṭ, calls this verse among the Qurʾān’s hardest in construction and interpretation, saying even leading scholars were baffled; Fakhr says he did not clarify the problem.
[IBNASHUR:98:1/r18] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · الفخر | reports | Fakhr reads the omitted complement of “detached” as their disbelief and “the clear proof” as the Messenger; “until” then implies they leave disbelief at his coming, apparently conflicting with 98:4, where division follows the proof’s arrival. | mentions 98:4
[IBNASHUR:98:1/r19] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that actual history shows neither group ever left disbelief, while the subjunctive verb after ḥattā signals a future event even though the Messenger had arrived years earlier and both groups persisted.
[IBNASHUR:98:1/r2] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The common title in tafsīr and hadith books is Sūrat Lam yakun, shortened to its first word; it is also the familiar name among Tunisian Qurʾān-school students.
[IBNASHUR:98:1/r20] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Given the apparent contradictions, the wording cannot be understood by its surface sense; interpreters either recast the whole statement or figuratively interpret one or more of its terms. | mentions 98:4
