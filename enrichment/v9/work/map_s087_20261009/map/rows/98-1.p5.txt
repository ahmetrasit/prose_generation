[QURTUBI:98:1/r3] QURTUBI, d. 671 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | In the sound report from Anas, God ordered the Prophet to recite this sura to Ubayy ibn Ka'b; when Ubayy asked whether God had named him, the Prophet said yes and Ubayy wept. Bukhari and Muslim transmit it.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r4] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Qurtubi draws a legal lesson from the report: a teacher may recite to a learner.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r5] QURTUBI, d. 671 · بعضهم | reports | Some say the Prophet recited to Ubayy to teach humility, so no one would disdain learning from or reading to someone lower in rank.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r6] QURTUBI, d. 671 · بعضهم | reports | Another view says Ubayy learned the Prophet's wording especially quickly, so the Prophet recited to him for exact transmission and to enable him to teach others.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r7] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبي بن كعب | reports | A statement about Adam's greed for wealth, death filling his belly, and God's acceptance of repentance was attributed to Ubayy's reading of this sura.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r8] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبو بكر الأنباري | rejects | Abu Bakr al-Anbari says this addition is false as Qur'an: the connected readings of Ibn Kathir and Abu Amr, both traced to Ubayy, omit it; it is known as the Prophet's speech, not God's Qur'an, and consensus outweighs one contrary report.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r9] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | The public reading and the consonantal text of the codex have lam yakun alladhina kafaru.
[QURTUBI-FULL:98:1-2/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | One explanation counts the People of the Book among the polytheists: Christians are Trinitarian and most Jews anthropomorphists, so both groups share shirk.
[QURTUBI-FULL:98:1-2/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | Another view takes kufr as rejection of Muhammad: the People of the Book are rejecting Jews and Christians, while the polytheists are idolaters without a scripture.
[QURTUBI-FULL:98:1-2/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · القشيري | rejects | Al-Qushayri finds that interpretation unlikely because the proof is a messenger, apparently Muhammad, so it would be odd to say Muhammad-deniers persisted until Muhammad came; he allows that some formerly revered him and believed when his signs came.
[QURTUBI-FULL:98:1-2/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | Al-A'mash and Ibrahim read al-mushrikun in the nominative; Qurtubi prefers the common reading because nominative coordination makes both groups seem outside the People of the Book.
[QURTUBI-FULL:98:1-2/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | Ubayy's codex reads fa-ma kana and Ibn Masud's codex reads the explanatory ordering, both recorded as alternate written forms.
[QURTUBI-FULL:98:1-2/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The proof may be read as having come; it is Muhammad, the messenger sent by God.
[QURTUBI-FULL:98:1/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The common reading matches the written codex; Ibn Masud's wording is an explanatory reading, not recitation.
[QURTUBI-FULL:98:1/r10] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ذو الرمة | reports | Dhu al-Rumma's verse uses the verb for camels that do not cease to be stationed, with illa added.
[QURTUBI-FULL:98:1/r11] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | Some gloss munfakkin as departing: they would not leave this world until the proof came.
[QURTUBI-FULL:98:1/r12] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن كيسان | reports | Ibn Kaysan says the People of the Book kept Muhammad's description in their scriptures until he was sent, then envied and denied him, as in 2:89; the polytheists likewise praised him as trustworthy until the proof came and they became hostile. | mentions 2:89
[QURTUBI-FULL:98:1/r13] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | Some linguists derive munfakkin from a childbirth separation that fails to heal and causes death, so the verse means people are not punished or destroyed until messengers and scriptures establish proof.
[QURTUBI-FULL:98:1/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن العربي | holds | Ibn al-Arabi allows Ibn Masud's wording as clarification but not liturgical recitation, since recitation follows the codex; he cites a Prophetic explanatory rendering of divorce verse 1. | mentions 65:1
[QURTUBI-FULL:98:1/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | People of the Book means Jews and Christians; the word for polytheists is grammatically genitive, coordinated with People of the Book.
[QURTUBI-FULL:98:1/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas identifies the People of the Book as the Jews of Yathrib, especially Qurayza, Nadir, and Qaynuqa, and the polytheists as the Meccan and surrounding idolaters, including Quraysh.
[QURTUBI-FULL:98:1/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Munfakkin can mean ceasing and turning away from disbelief.
[QURTUBI-FULL:98:1/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | Another interpretation takes the phrase to mean reaching their life's end and dying before the proof came; on this reading infikak means reaching the limit.
[QURTUBI-FULL:98:1/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | A further interpretation makes munfakkin mean vanishing: their appointed duration would not disappear before a messenger came.
[QURTUBI-FULL:98:1/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Arabic usage ma انفككت أفعل كذا and ma انفك قائما means continuing to do something or remaining standing; the root fakk means opening, as when opening a book or unfastening a bracelet.
[QURTUBI-FULL:98:1/r9] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · طرفة | reports | Tarafa's verse uses infikak for not ceasing, with a sword described as clinging at the speaker's waist.

[BAYDAWI-FULL:98:1-6/r2] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | reports | Munfakkin may mean that they would not abandon their religion, or that they would not abandon their pledge to follow the truth when the Messenger came.
