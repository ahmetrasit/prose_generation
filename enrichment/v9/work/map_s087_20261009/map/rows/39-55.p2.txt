[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-6-2:p522/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Rather than despairing of God or seeking deities and intercessors, people should turn to the forgiving and merciful God in every circumstance.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-6-2:p522/r5] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | People should turn to God and believe in His revealed Book before He seizes them; later regret and a wish to believe will be too late.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-6-2:p564/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The next section warns that polytheism and intercession will not save the idolaters but hasten ruin; success requires sincere turning to God and following His revealed Book, or their later regret will be useless.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-6-2:p567/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Verse 55 elaborates the previous verse’s call to turn to God: the “best thing” is the Qur’an, called the “best word” in 39:23. | mentions 39:23, 39:54
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-6-2:p567/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The Qur’an is God’s final and most complete Book.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-6-2:p567/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The Qur’an is protected from alteration.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-6-2:p567/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | It calls people to the natural religion and lacks the stern, harsh directives found in previous scriptures.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-6-2:p567/r5] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | For Arabs, the Qur’an’s lucid, eloquent Arabic is unmatched, and its Arabic revelation is a great divine favor to them.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-6-2:p567/r6] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Its mutashābih and mathānī form doubles its benefit compared with other divine books, as discussed at 39:23. | mentions 39:23
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-6-2:p567/r7] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The Qur’an calls Arabs to Abraham’s religion, a message already familiar to them.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-6-2:p567/r8] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Given these qualities, people should wholeheartedly welcome and absorb the Qur’an; denying it brings an unimaginable punishment.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:39:54/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Müfessirler | reports | Most exegetes take “punishment” here as worldly defeat of the polytheists by Muslims and “what was sent down” as the Qur’an.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:39:54/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | rejects | Translating this as “follow the best of what was revealed” could imply that some Qur’anic passages are better than others, so the authors reject that rendering.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:39:54/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | prefers | Following Ibn Ashur, the authors understand ahsan as “perfectly good,” encompassing all Qur’anic rulings, rather than as a comparative “better.”
[STUDYQURAN:39:55/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | As at 39:18, following the most beautiful revelation does not rank one part above another; it means acting according to revelation through obedience and avoidance of disobedience. | mentions 39:18
[STUDYQURAN:39:55/r2] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Another formulation is to practice what God enjoins and avoid what He forbids; revelation achieves its purpose when its injunctions are put into practice.
[STUDYQURAN:39:55/r3] STUDYQURAN · some commentators | reports | Some understand the phrase to mean the Quran is the best or most beautiful of God's revealed books, as in 39:23. | mentions 39:23
[STUDYQURAN:39:intro/r12] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The call to repent in verses 54–67 leads into the final extended contrast between the believers’ and disbelievers’ ends in the Hereafter.
[STUDYQURAN:39:intro/r2] STUDYQURAN · Many commentators | reports | Many commentators hold verses 53–55 to be Madinan and say they were revealed concerning Waḥshī, who won freedom by killing Ḥamzah at Uḥud.
[STUDYQURAN:39:intro/r4] STUDYQURAN · Other commentators | reports | A different reported view says verses 53–59 were all revealed concerning Waḥshī.
