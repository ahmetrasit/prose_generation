[ELMALILI:15:8-9/r13] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Fahreddin Râzî | reports | Razi treats the preservation foretold in this verse, already borne out over nearly six centuries by his time, as a decisive report of the unseen and a compelling miracle.
[ELMALILI:15:8-9/r14] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Elmalılı notes that by Hijri 1349, over thirteen and a half centuries after revelation, the promised preservation had become a witnessed intellectual and practical miracle.
[ELMALILI:15:8-9/r15] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Even without this explicit prediction, the Qur'an's uniquely long preservation would itself be a practical miracle; the verse's emphatic foretelling makes it an intellectual miracle too.
[ELMALILI:15:8-9/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · cumhur | prefers | The majority says the pronoun in the preservation promise refers to the Reminder, that is, the Qur'an; Elmalılı considers this the more apparent reading.
[ELMALILI:15:8-9/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Ferrâ ve İbnü’l- Enbârî | reports | Ferrā and Ibn al-Anbārī take the pronoun to refer to the Messenger, whom God guards from jinn, Satan, and enemies; Elmalılı accepts this as sound but less apparent.
[ELMALILI:15:8-9/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | prefers | The Messenger reading is sound, but the wording most readily indicates that the pronoun refers to the Qur'an.
[ELMALILI:15:8-9/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | God undertakes to protect the Qur'an from additions, omissions, distortion, and alteration, guaranteeing its preservation.
[ELMALILI:15:8-9/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The Companions' collection of the Qur'an into a codex does not conflict with the promise; their effort was one of God's means of preservation and was enabled by Him.
[ELMALILI:15:8-9/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Elmalılı reports exegetes' first account: God's miraculous preservation makes additions or omissions impossible, since altering the Qur'an changes its inimitable composition and the change is recognized.
[ELMALILI:15:8-9/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Elmalılı reports exegetes' second account: God preserves the Qur'an by giving no one the power to produce a counter-imitation, a view close to the first.

[TABATABAI:15:1-9#10/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | Consensus cannot establish absence of additions: it gives probable, not certain, belief, since accumulating agreeing opinions strengthens probability without turning it into certainty.
[TABATABAI:15:1-9#11/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | For Sunnis consensus relies on a Prophetic report against communal error; for Shia it depends on an infallible Imam's inclusion or being inferred. Both depend on prophethood, whose living proof is the Qur'an, so possible distortion undermines the proof and consensus. Historical survival of a Qur'an in general does not prove each cited verse untouched.
[TABATABAI:15:1-9#11/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | Treating distortion reports as authoritative traditions is circular for the same reason as consensus; as historical evidence they are isolated, conflicting, not mutawatir or decisive, and few are both sound in chain and conclusive in meaning.
