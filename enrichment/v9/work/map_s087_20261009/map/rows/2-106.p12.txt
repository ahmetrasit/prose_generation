BI-FULL, d. 671 · حذاق الأئمة | reports | The leading jurists allow a ruling to be abrogated before it is carried out; examples are Abraham’s sacrifice test and the fifty prayers being reduced by five before performance.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#7/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Other signs of abrogation are a narrator’s chronology, an explicit statement that one ruling replaced another, or consensus identifying the abrogating ruling; legal theory treats the subject at length.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#7/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الجمهور | reports | The majority read ما ننسخ with an open initial nun, from نسخ: removing a verse’s ruling while leaving its recitation, though it can also mean removing both.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#7/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن عامر | reports | Ibn ʿAmir reads ننسخ with a ḍamma on the initial nun, deriving it from أنسخت, “found it abrogated”; Abu Hatim calls this wrong, while Abu ʿAli says it works only in that “found” sense and converges in meaning with the majority reading.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#7/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · مكي | reports | Makkī rejects treating the hamza as causative, since “whatever verse We send down or make you forget, We bring a better one” would make the whole Qur’an abrogated, though only a little was abrogated.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#7/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو عمرو وابن كثير | reports | Abu ʿAmr and Ibn Kathir read ننسأها with open nun and sin and hamza, also transmitted from ʿUmar, Ibn ʿAbbas, ʿAtaʾ, Mujahid, Ubayy, ʿUbayd ibn ʿUmayr, al-Nakhaʿi, and Ibn Muhaysin.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#7/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · عطاء | reports | ʿAtaʾ understands ننسأها as postponing the abrogation of the verse’s wording, leaving it in the Mother of the Book so it does not cease to be.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#7/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | Others take ننسأها to mean deferring the verse from abrogation until a known time; the root نسأ means to delay, as in asking God to extend someone’s life.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#8/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | A further view takes the hamza reading as removing the verse from people’s memory so it is neither read nor recalled; the wording also allows delaying revelation or abrogation.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#8/r10] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو علي | reports | Abu ʿAli and others consider the causative interpretation sound: God makes the Prophet leave the verse.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#8/r11] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | Another interpretation takes forgetting literally: God makes Muhammad forget the verse; hamza makes the verb take two objects, with the Prophet omitted and the verse explicit.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#8/r12] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | خير is comparative in benefit: a lighter replacement benefits people sooner in this life, a heavier one in the afterlife, and an equal one as much as its predecessor.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#8/r13] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · مالك | reports | Malik explains خير منها as “more firmly established in place of what was abrogated.”
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#8/r14] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | Another view says خير is not comparative, since God’s speech does not vary in excellence; as in 27:89, it means a benefit or reward, while “or like it” supports the first interpretation. | mentions 27:89
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#8/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن عباس والسدي | reports | The majority read ننسها with a ḍamma on the initial nun, from “leave”: God leaves the verse without replacing or abrogating it; Ibn ʿAbbas and al-Suddi give this meaning.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#8/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | In 9:67, “they forgot God, so He forgot them” means they abandoned worship and God left them in punishment. | mentions 9:67
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#8/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو عبيد وأبو حاتم | prefers | Abu ʿUbayd and Abu Hatim prefer the reading with ḍamma, ننسها.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#8/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو عبيد | reports | Abu ʿUbayd says Abu Nuʿaym told of reciting Abu ʿAmr’s reading to the Prophet in a dream; the Prophet corrected two letters, and Abu ʿUbayd guessed one was ننسأها corrected to ننسها.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#8/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الأزهري | reports | Al-Azhari says ننسها can mean “we command you to leave it,” distinguishing أنسيته, “I ordered him to leave it,” from نسيته, “I left it.”
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#8/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | A poet’s line uses منسيها for one not commanded to be left behind; it supports the causative sense “make someone leave.”
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#8/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الزجاج | rejects | Al-Zajjaj rejects the “leave” sense for the ḍamma reading, saying أنسى is not used to mean ترك; he also rejects the attributed Ibn ʿAbbas explanation as unreliable.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106#8/r9] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Al-Qurtubi suggests Ibn ʿAbbas may have meant “leave it without replacing it,” but his wording was not carefully preserved; most linguists and scholars accept “make you leave it.”
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | ننسها is coordinated with ننسخ, with its final yāʾ omitted because the verb is jussive; in the hamza reading ننسأها, the hamza’s ḍamma is omitted for the same reason.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | نأت is the apodosis of the conditional construction.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Al-Qurtubi calls this a tremendous verse concerning legal rulings and says knowledge of abrogation is essential for scholars to understand legal cases and distinguish lawful from unlawful.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · علي | reports | When ʿAli found a preacher frightening people, he asked whether the man knew abrogating and abrogated rulings; after the man said no, ʿAli expelled him from the mosque.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · علي | reports | In another version, ʿAli asks whether the preacher knows abrogating and abrogated rulings, then says he has ruined himself and misled others.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | Al-Qurtubi says a similar report about knowledge of abrogation is also transmitted from Ibn ʿAbbas.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | In Arabic naskh can mean transferring or copying, as with a book; the Qur’an was transferred from the Preserved Tablet to the lowest heaven, but that sense is not meant here.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The expression نستنسخ in 45:29 means that God orders the recording and preservation of deeds. | mentions 45:29
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:106/r9] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The other Arabic sense is cancellation or removal; one kind removes something and installs another in its place, like the sun removing shade, and is the sense intended in the verse.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:106/r1] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | The verse was revealed after polytheists or Jews said Muhammad orders his companions to do something, then forbids it and orders its opposite.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:106/r10] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · أبو عمرو | reports | Abū ʿAmr reads the hamza as an alif.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:106/r11] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | God’s power encompasses abrogating a verse and bringing a replacement equal to or better than the abrogated one.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:106/r12] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | The verse establishes the permissibility of abrogation and delaying revelation: rulings and verses serve people’s welfare and moral completion, which vary with times and persons, as livelihoods do.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:106/r13] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | reports | Some use the verse to prohibit abrogation without a substitute, with a heavier substitute, or of the Qur’an by Sunna, since Sunna is not the replacement; al-Bayḍāwī calls all these arguments weak.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:106/r14] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | rejects | A missing ruling or a heavier one can be more beneficial; an abrogation can be known by something other than scripture, and Sunna too is brought by God. ‘Better or equal’ concerns benefit or reward, not wording.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:106/r15] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · المعتزلة | reports | The Muʿtazilites argue that the Qur’an is originated because change and variation are among its properties.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:106/r16] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | rejects | Al-Bayḍāwī answers that change and variation affect things related to the eternal divine essence’s meaning, not that essential meaning itself.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:106/r2] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | In language, abrogation is removing a form from one thing and establishing it elsewhere, as a shadow replaces the sun or as copying transfers text; usage then covers either removal, as wind erasing traces, or copying a book.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:106/r3] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | Abrogating a verse means ending the ritual obligation of its recitation, its ruling, or both.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:106/r4] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | Causing a verse to be forgotten means removing it from people’s memories.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:106/r5] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | ما is a conditional particle that makes ننسخ jussive and is itself its object.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:106/r6] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · ابن عامر | reports | Ibn ʿĀmir reads ما ننسخ from أنسخ: ‘We command you or Gabriel to abrogate it,’ or ‘We find it abrogated.’
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:106/r7] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · ابن كثير وأبو عمرو | reports | Ibn Kathīr and Abū ʿAmr read ننسأها, meaning ‘We defer it,’ from النسء.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:106/r8] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | reports | Other readings mean ‘We cause someone to forget it,’ ‘We cause you to forget it,’ passive ‘you are made to forget it,’ or ‘We cause you to forget it’ with two implied objects.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:106/r9] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | A replacement is better for servants in benefit or reward, or equal in reward.
