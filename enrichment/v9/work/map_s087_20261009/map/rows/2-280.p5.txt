[TABRISI:2:280#2/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · بريدة | reports | Bُrayda reports from the Prophet that every day of deferment for an insolvent debtor earns the creditor a day's charity.
[TABRISI:2:280#2/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | When a creditor knows his debtor is insolvent, it is forbidden to imprison, pursue, or demand payment from him; the creditor must wait for solvency.
[TABRISI:2:280#2/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Forgiving the principal as charity to an insolvent debtor is better than waiting for him to become solvent.
[TABRISI:2:280#2/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn ʿUmrān report that the usury verses were the last part of the Qurʾān revealed.

[TABRISI:2:280/r10] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبي | reports | An anomalous reading attributed to Ubayy has accusative dhā: “if the person who owes you is in hardship.”
[TABRISI:2:280/r11] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Naẓira is nominative as the predicate of an omitted subject, “then what you deal with him is deferment”; the fāʾ marks the consequence.
[TABRISI:2:280/r12] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The phrase wa-an taṣaddaqū occupies subject position and khayrun lakum is its predicate.
[TABRISI:2:280/r13] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | After requiring repayment of principal from a solvent debtor, God explains what to do when a debtor is insolvent.
[TABRISI:2:280/r14] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The phrase means that if one among your debtors is in hardship, what you owe him is delay until he becomes solvent.
[TABRISI:2:280/r15] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The clause is phrased as a statement, but its legal force is a command to grant the debtor time until ease.
[TABRISI:2:280/r16] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو عبد الله | reports | Abū ʿAbd Allāh defines insolvency as lacking funds beyond what suffices for the debtor and family at an economical level.
[TABRISI:2:280/r17] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو علي الجبائي | reports | Abū ʿAlī al-Jubbāʾī says insolvency is inability caused by having no resources, stagnant merchandise, or the like.
[TABRISI:2:280/r18] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Ḥasan, and the two Imams al-Bāqir and al-Ṣādiq hold that granting time is obligatory for every debt.
[TABRISI:2:280/r19] TABRISI, d. 548 · شريح | reports | Shurayḥ and Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī restrict the duty to deferment to usury debts.
[TABRISI:2:280/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · زيد عن يعقوب | reports | Zayd reports Yaʿqūb's reading maysura with a ḍamma on sīn; the same reading is also transmitted from Mujāhid.
[TABRISI:2:280/r20] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | A third position makes deferment obligatory for usury debts by the verse and for other debts by analogy.
[TABRISI:2:280/r21] TABRISI, d. 548 · الباقر | reports | Al-Bāqir interprets “until ease” as until the debtor's case reaches the imām, who pays him from the debtors' share if the loan was spent on a recognized good.
[TABRISI:2:280/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · عاصم | reports | ʿĀṣim reads taṣaddaqū with a light ṣād; the others double it. Both derive from tataṣaddaqū, by deleting one tā or assimilating the tāʾs.
[TABRISI:2:280/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Naẓira means postponement and is a noun used in place of inẓār; ukhrah and the cited phrases likewise indicate delay or lateness.
[TABRISI:2:280/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Maysara and maysūr both mean wealth, affluence, or abundance.
[TABRISI:2:280/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Basran grammarians reject the reported form maysura without tāʾ marbūṭa because mafʿil does not occur as a singular without tāʾ, although it can occur in plurals; poetic attestations include maʿūn and maʾlak.
[TABRISI:2:280/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · سيبويه | reports | The author says Sibawayh does not rely on a form reported only once or twice; he may state a categorical rule despite such rare attestations.
[TABRISI:2:280/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Kāna may be complete, requiring only its subject, with the sense “if a debtor in hardship occurs among you.”
