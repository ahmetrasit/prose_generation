59:1-10/r10] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | An unattributed hadith cited in al-Majmaʿ says miserliness cannot coexist with faith in a Muslim’s heart, nor dust from God’s cause with Hell’s smoke inside him.
[TABATABAI:59:1-10/r11] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · أبو عبد الله عليه السلام | reports | Al-Faḍl ibn Abī Qurrah reports Imam al-Ṣādiq’s view that shuh is worse than miserliness: it covets both one’s own and others’ property, even by unlawful means, and is never content with God’s provision.
[TABATABAI:59:1-10/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · أبو عبد الله عليه السلام | reports | A report from Imam al-Ṣādiq says faith is a house, as are Islam and disbelief.

[TABATABAI:59:8-10/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | prefers | Given that poor emigrants exemplify spending in God’s cause, the Anṣār and later generations are best read as coordinated with the emigrants, not as fresh independent clauses.
[TABATABAI:59:8-10/r10] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | This is stronger praise than the preceding trait: they not only refrain from wanting the emigrants’ possessions but give them their own despite need.
[TABATABAI:59:8-10/r11] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · الراغب | reports | Al-Rāghib explains that a gap in a house is called khaṣāṣ and by extension poverty is a deficiency left unfilled, like khallah.
[TABATABAI:59:8-10/r12] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · الراغب | reports | Al-Rāghib defines shuh as miserliness coupled with acquisitiveness in what is customary.
[TABATABAI:59:8-10/r13] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Yūq is a passive imperfect of protection: whoever God protects from inner constriction about giving or distress at others’ possessions succeeds.
[TABATABAI:59:8-10/r14] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Those whom God protects from the constriction of their souls are the successful.
[TABATABAI:59:8-10/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The report that the Prophet gave fayʾ to three Anṣār supports their inclusion; if only poor emigrants had a share, no Anṣār should have received any, while many Anṣār were poor and in need.
[TABATABAI:59:8-10/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The pronoun refers to the emigrants, and means the Anṣār settled there before the emigrants arrived in Medina.
[TABATABAI:59:8-10/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The Anṣār love those who migrated to them because they left the abode of disbelief for the abode of faith and the Muslim community.
[TABATABAI:59:8-10/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The pronouns in they find and their hearts refer to the Anṣār, while the pronoun in they were given refers to the emigrants; min is partitive.
[TABATABAI:59:8-10/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The Anṣār feel no desire for the emigrants’ allotment and are not resentful or envious of the fayʾ being distributed to them.
[TABATABAI:59:8-10/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | reports | Another view understands ḥājah as the anger or resentment that need can produce.
[TABATABAI:59:8-10/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Īthār means choosing and giving precedence to something over another.
[TABATABAI:59:8-10/r9] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | They put the emigrants ahead of themselves even while poor and in need.
[TABATABAI:59:8-9/r10] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Settling faith means cultivating it in practice and removing impediments, so its acts of obedience and devotion can be performed freely, as they could not in Mecca.
[TABATABAI:59:8-9/r11] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | Another possible parsing coordinates faith with the verb they settled, supplying an omitted governing verb meaning they preferred faith.
[TABATABAI:59:8-9/r12] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | reports | Another view coordinates the Anṣār with the emigrants, making the Anṣār participants in the fayʾ.
[TABATABAI:59:8-9/r13] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | prefers | The report that the Prophet gave some fayʾ to three poor Anṣār supports their participation: if they could receive none, not even three could have received it; the Prophet chose the allocation pattern.
[TABATABAI:59:8-9/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | reports | One view treats the verse as a fresh sentence praising the Anṣār and consoling them for not receiving the fayʾ; the relative clause is subject and their love of emigrants is its predicate.
[TABATABAI:59:8-9/r9] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Settling in the abode is a figurative way to describe building a religious community where believers can take refuge.
[ASAD-NOTES:59:9#n12/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | The earlier settlers preceded those who left the domain of evil, as explained in the following note.
[ASAD-NOTES:59:9#n13/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | Historically these are Medina's Ansar, who embraced Islam before the Prophet and Meccan followers arrived, then shared homes and possessions generously with the refugees.
[ASAD-NOTES:59:9#n13/r2] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | More broadly, this describes believers in every age living securely in Islam who welcome those forced to leave home to follow their faith.
[ASAD-NOTES:59:9#n14/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | Greed, niggardliness and covetousness are chief barriers to happiness in this life and the hereafter; compare surah 102. | mentions 102:1
[ASAD-NOTES:59:9/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | The believers who settled in the realm of faith love those seeking refuge and bear no resentment over what the newcomers receive.
[ASAD-NOTES:59:9/r2] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | They give refugees preference even when they themselves are poor; those saved from covetousness attain happiness.
