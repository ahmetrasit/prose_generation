[TABATABAI:43:11-14/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | ‘What you ride’ means what you ride upon; the verb takes a direct object for animals but takes fī with ships, so the wording favors the animal construction.
[ASAD-NOTES:43:11#n10/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | Asad gives the literal sense of azwaj as 'all pairs'.
[ASAD-NOTES:43:11#n10/r2] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Baghawi, Zamakhsharl, Baydawi, Ibn Kathir | reports | These commentators understand azwaj as kinds of things, beings, and phenomena.
[ASAD-NOTES:43:11#n10/r3] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Tabari | reports | Tabari reads the phrase as referring to polarity evident throughout creation.
[ASAD-NOTES:43:11#n10/r4] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Ibn 'Abbas | reports | Ibn Abbas, as quoted by Razi, takes the phrase to denote opposites generally, such as sweet and sour or male and female.
[ASAD-NOTES:43:11#n10/r5] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Razi | reports | Razi expands the idea to complementary pairs throughout creation, while God alone has no opposite, likeness, or complement.
[ASAD-NOTES:43:11#n10/r6] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | Asad connects this account of pairs to the statement that nothing is comparable to God. | mentions 112:4
