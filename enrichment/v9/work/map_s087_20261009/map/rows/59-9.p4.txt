ULL, d. 542 · author | prefers | Ibn Atiyya calls shuḥḥ an inner poverty that wealth cannot cure, but instead increases and exhausts.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p288/r7] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · عبد الرحمن بن عوف | reports | Abd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf used to pray while circumambulating only that God protect him from the greed of his own soul; if protected, he said, he would do no wrong.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p288/r8] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · ابن زيد وابن جبير وجماعة | reports | Ibn Zayd, Ibn Jubayr, and others say a person avoids shuḥḥ by taking nothing God forbids and not withholding obligatory zakat.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p288/r9] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masʿūd defines shuḥḥ as consuming another's property unlawfully; refusing to give one's own is bukhl, blameworthy but distinct.
[TABRISI:59:9-10/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The Ansar settled in Medina, the abode of the Hijra, before the emigrants; the wording's estimate is that they settled the abode and embraced faith before them.
[TABRISI:59:9-10/r10] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd says one is spared avarice by not taking what God forbids and not withholding what God commands one to give.
[TABRISI:59:9-10/r11] TABRISI, d. 548 · سعيد بن جبير | reports | Saʿid ibn Jubayr identifies avarice as taking unlawful wealth and withholding alms.
[TABRISI:59:9-10/r12] TABRISI, d. 548 · الحديث | reports | A hadith says avarice does not coexist with faith in a Muslim's heart, nor dust from God's path with Hell's smoke in his body.
[TABRISI:59:9-10/r13] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | One parsing makes the Ansar an initial subject whose predicate is that they love the emigrants; the Prophet gave them little of the Fay, with reports differing between two and three men.
[TABRISI:59:9-10/r14] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Alternatively, the phrase is genitive, coordinated with the poor emigrants, and the following description of the Ansar is a circumstantial clause.
[TABRISI:59:9-10/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Faith is coordinated with abode in wording, not sense, because faith is not a place one can settle; the intended sense is that they chose faith.
[TABRISI:59:9-10/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · قيل | reports | One interpretation takes before them to mean before the emigrants arrived in Medina.
[TABRISI:59:9-10/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · قيل | reports | Another interpretation takes it as before the emigrants believed and identifies the group as the seventy men at Aqaba who pledged to fight all peoples.
[TABRISI:59:9-10/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The Ansar loved the emigrants because they welcomed them into their homes and shared their property with them.
[TABRISI:59:9-10/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | They felt no envy, resentment, or anger about the Banu Nadir wealth given to the emigrants instead of to them.
[TABRISI:59:9-10/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | They preferred the emigrants, putting them ahead of themselves in their wealth and homes, even though they themselves were in need; this makes their reward greater.
[TABRISI:59:9-10/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · أنس بن مالك | reports | Anas ibn Malik reportedly swore that there were no misers among the Ansar and recited this verse.
[TABRISI:59:9-10/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | To be protected from the soul's avarice is to be kept from its miserliness; such people succeed and win God's reward and Paradise.
[TABRISI:59:9/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · رسول الله | reports | On the day of Banu Nadir, the Prophet offered the Ansar a choice: share homes and property with the emigrants and share the booty, or keep their property and receive none of it.
[TABRISI:59:9/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · الأنصار | reports | The Ansar chose to share their wealth and homes and give the emigrants all the booty without taking a share themselves; the verse was revealed about this.
[TABRISI:59:9/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · قيل | reports | One reported occasion is seven thirsty men at Uhud who passed a cup of water along; all died without drinking, and God praised them.
[TABRISI:59:9/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · قيل | reports | Another occasion is an Ansari who hosted a hungry visitor of the Prophet, serving the children's food while the couple pretended to eat and went to bed hungry; the Prophet recited this verse in the morning.
[TABRISI:59:9/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو هريرة | reports | In the account with a sound chain from Abu Hurayra, the host who hid the lack of food and put out the lamp was Ali and Fatima.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p212/r1] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | Al-dar is the abode of emigration, Medina; the phrase about faith is fronted and delayed, referring to the Ansar’s faith before the emigrants arrived.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p212/r2] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | On the rearranged reading, faith is formally coordinated with abode but not semantically, since faith is not a place one can inhabit; the sense is that they preferred faith.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p212/r3] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | The emigrants accepted Islam before the Ansar, while the Ansar inhabited Medina before the emigrants; an alternative retains the wording’s surface sense of settling in abode and faith before migration.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p212/r4] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | The Ansar loved the emigrants and shared their homes and property with them.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p212/r5] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | The need or grievance in their hearts means envy and anger over what the emigrants received.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p212/r6] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · الحسن | reports | Hasan says what the emigrants received was the fay; the Prophet distributed Banu al-Nadir’s property among them, giving it to only three Ansar.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p212/r7] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · الماوردي | reports | The other interpretation is that what the emigrants received was distinction and precedence.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p212/r8] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | The Ansar preferred the emigrants in wealth and homes despite their own poverty and need, so their generosity did not come from abundance.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p212/r9] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · أبو هريرة | reports | A report in both Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim tells of an Ansari host who fed a starving guest by pretending to eat while his family went hungry; the Prophet said God marveled at their deed. Some versions identify the guest as one of the People of the Bench.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p214/r1] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن عمر | reports | Ibn Umar reports that a sheep’s head was passed among seven households, each preferring another family, before returning to the first; the verse was revealed about this.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p214/r2] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · أنس بن مالك | reports | Anas reports a similar account: a roasted sheep’s head passed through nine needy people and returned to its first recipient, occasioning the verse.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p214/r3] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن السميفع وأبو رجاء | reports | Ibn al-Sumayfa and Abu Rajaʾ read yuwaqqa with a doubled qaf.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p214/r4] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · المفسرون | reports | The commentators explain protection from miserliness as neither taking what God forbids nor withholding what He commands one to give; the Ansar showed it by freely leaving the fay to the emigrants.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p214/r5] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن جرير | reports | Ibn Jarir defines shuh in Arabic as withholding surplus wealth.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p214/r6] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · أبو سليمان الخطابي | reports | Al-Khattabi says shuh is more intense than bukhl: shuh is a broad, inborn disposition, while bukhl usually concerns particular things.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p214/r7] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · بعضهم | reports | An authority quoted by al-Khattabi distinguishes bukhl as stinginess with one’s money and shuh as stinginess with both money and generosity or good offices.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p214/r8] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masud tells a man who feared damnation because he rarely gave that Quranic shuh is consuming another’s wealth unjustly; mere stinginess is bukhl and a bad trait.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p214/r9] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · رسول الله | reports | The Prophet says one is free of shuh by paying zakat, honoring a guest, and giving in adversity; Anas ibn Malik transmits the report.
