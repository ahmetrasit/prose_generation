[RAZI-FULL:v26p92/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · قوم | reports | One reported interpretation is that poetry did not come naturally to Muhammad.

[RAZI-FULL:v26p92/r11] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Meaning is the heart and spirit of wording, while wording is its form; a sage's metrical speech remains wisdom, and a poet's admonition may convey wisdom, so meter does not negate the message's substance.
[RAZI-FULL:v26p92/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · آخرون | reports | Another interpretation is that poetry did not come easily to him, so any verse he attempted would have defective meter.
[RAZI-FULL:v26p92/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report says that when Muhammad recited the line “And news will come to you from one you did not provide for,” its meter was defective, offered as evidence that poetry was not easy for him.
[RAZI-FULL:v26p92/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | prefers | Al-Razi prefers the plain sense that poetry does not befit Muhammad: a lawgiver's wording follows the intended meaning, whereas a poet's meaning follows diction chosen first to satisfy meter or rhyme.
[RAZI-FULL:v26p92/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Poetry is metered speech whose meter is intended primarily; if a speaker aims first at meaning and the result happens to be metered and rhymed, it is not poetry.
[RAZI-FULL:v26p92/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Qur'an 3:92 is not poetry although it can be scanned in a poetic meter: the wise person seeks the meaning first, while a poet selects words first to fit the meter and makes meaning follow them. | mentions 3:92
[RAZI-FULL:v26p92/r7] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Muhammad's occasional utterance of a metrical line, including “I am the Prophet, no lie; I am the son of Abd al-Muttalib,” does not make him a poet because he did not intend meter and rhyme first; even much accidentally metrical speech would not do so.
[RAZI-FULL:v26p92/r8] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Ordinary market speech can scan as a poetic meter without being poetry, since its speakers did not first intend the wording for meter or rhyme.
[RAZI-FULL:v26p92/r9] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The contrast is between the Qur'an as reminder and admonition aimed at meaning and poetry as speech embellished by rhyme and meter.
