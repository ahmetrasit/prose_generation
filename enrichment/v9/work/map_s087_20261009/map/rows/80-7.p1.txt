[ALUSI-FULL:v30p41/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Ma is negative and the clause is circumstantial from the subject of taṣaddā; alternatively it is an interrogative repudiation meaning the same thing: what harm is it if he does not purify himself?
[ELMALILI:80:3-42/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The Prophet bears no responsibility if the self-sufficient person refuses purification or Islam, but ignoring a Muslim who seeks instruction entails responsibility; guidance efforts toward unbelievers remain permissible in principle.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p108/r8] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Mā is negative; ʿalayka is the fronted predicate and allā yazakkā is the subject.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p108/r9] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The Prophet is not responsible for the man’s failure to purify himself and need not exert himself beyond what God requires; this is divine gentleness toward His Messenger.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p138#7/r8] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Mā is negative; ʿalayka is the fronted predicate, and the clause “that he be purified” is the delayed subject.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p138#7/r9] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The man’s failure to be guided is not the Prophet’s responsibility; he is not accountable for it and need not press beyond what God charged him with. This reassures him.
[TABATABAI:80:5-12/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | The proposed honorific reading of the switch from third to second person does not fit the direct address that follows, whose rebuke is stronger and has no conciliatory intimacy.
[TABATABAI:80:5-12/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The rebuked pattern is that the Prophet attends to wealthy people who scorn the truth, unconcerned whether they purify themselves, while neglecting the person earnestly seeking purification and fearing God.
[TABATABAI:80:5-12/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | reports | One reading takes mā as negative: it is no fault of yours if the rich man does not purify himself, so eagerness for his conversion should not divert you from one who already accepted Islam.
[TABATABAI:80:5-12/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | reports | A second reading takes mā as an incredulous question: what obligation falls on you if he does not cleanse himself of disbelief and sin? You are only a messenger responsible for conveying.
[TABATABAI:80:5-12/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | prefers | The most contextually apt reading is that the Prophet should not concern himself with the rich man's failure to purify himself; the interrogative reading comes next, and the negative reading is least apt.
[ASAD-NOTES:80:3#n3/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | Asad's literal gloss says it is not upon the Prophet if the man does not attain purity.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p169/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | ʿAbasa pairs with al-Nāziʿāt and shares its central theme, discourse style, and reasoning; the changed sequence of topics gives this sūrah its distinct majesty. | mentions 79:45
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p176/r5] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The Prophet’s duty is to warn of the Hereafter and communicate Islam, not to ensure that people become Muslims or to overburden himself over their refusal.
