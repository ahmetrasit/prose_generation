[IBNABIHATIM:v2p392/r6] IBNABIHATIM, d. 327 · طاوس عن مجاهد | reports | A report through Mujahid from Tawus defines al-afw as the easy or readily available portion of everything.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p392/r7] IBNABIHATIM, d. 327 · الربيع بن أنس | reports | Al-Rabi explains al-afw as the good portion of one's wealth, its best and finest part.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p393/r1] IBNABIHATIM, d. 327 · مجاهد عن قيس المكي | reports | A report through Qays al-Makki says Mujahid defined al-afw as the obligatory charity.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p393/r2] IBNABIHATIM, d. 327 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says al-afw is surplus wealth and that this instruction came before charity was prescribed.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p393/r3] IBNABIHATIM, d. 327 · السدي، عطاء الخراساني، أبو محمد | reports | Al-Suddi interprets al-afw as surplus wealth and says the command was abrogated by zakat; Ata al-Khurasani is reported similarly.
[NAHHAS:v1p308/r10] NAHHAS, d. 338 · الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan takes the instruction to mean: spend the surplus.
[NAHHAS:v1p308/r11] NAHHAS, d. 338 · author | holds | The reflection invited here concerns both worldly life and the Hereafter.
[NAHHAS:v1p308/r6] NAHHAS, d. 338 · author | prefers | Al-Ḥaramayn and Abū ʿAmr read kabīr, while the Kufans read kathīr; kabīr is preferable, matching ṣaghīr/kabīr and the established pair kabāʾir/ṣaghāʾir. | mentions 4:2
[NAHHAS:v1p308/r7] NAHHAS, d. 338 · author | prefers | The Ḥaramayn and Kufans read al-ʿafw accusative, while Abū ʿAmr, ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar, and Ibn Abī Isḥāq read nominative; both are grammatical, but the verse’s interpretation favors accusative.
[NAHHAS:v1p308/r8] NAHHAS, d. 338 · author | holds | If dhā means “that which,” nominative is preferred but accusative is allowed; if mādhā is one word, accusative is preferred but nominative is allowed. Both occur in the question ماذا تعلمت أنحوا أم شعرا.
[NAHHAS:v1p308/r9] NAHHAS, d. 338 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās explains al-ʿafw as the surplus.
[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p96/r1] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · author | holds | The reading with bāʾ, kabīr, is supported by the next phrase's akbar rather than akthar.
[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p96/r2] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · author | holds | The reading with thāʾ, kathīr, suits the counted items of consumed wine and gambling; a verse elsewhere uses akthar for degree or amount. | mentions 6:59
[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p96/r3] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · author | holds | With rafʿ, mā and dhā are separate: dhā is relative, and al-ʿafw is the predicate answering what they should spend.
[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p96/r4] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · author | holds | With naṣb, mādhā is one interrogative word and al-ʿafw is the object of yanfiqūn.
[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p96/r5] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · author | holds | Mā and dhā combine because both are general and ambiguous, covering rational and non-rational things; man is restricted to rational beings, so man and dhā do not combine.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v2p307/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | reports | Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī read kathīr with thāʾ; the rest read kabīr with bāʾ.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v2p307/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · سعيد عن قتادة | reports | Qatāda says the verse condemned wine without prohibiting it while it was lawful; the prayer and later al-Māʾida verses then prohibited every amount. | mentions 4:43
[FARISI-HUJJA:v2p307/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أهل النظر | reports | Some scholars infer prohibition because God forbids sin in 7:33 and this verse calls wine and gambling a great sin. | mentions 7:33
[FARISI-HUJJA:v2p307/r4] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | holds | Al-Fārisī also reads the sin as arising in deeming wine and gambling lawful; only some qualities of otherwise ordinary things are forbidden.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v2p307/r5] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · أبو حنيفة | reports | Abū Ḥanīfa says looking at wine for sensual enjoyment is itself forbidden.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v2p307/r6] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | holds | The sin refers to sinful acts committed through wine and gambling and the causes leading to them.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v2p307/r7] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · بعض نقلة الآثار | reports | Transmitters report that this verse descended without prohibiting wine; reports differ on whether prohibition came here or in al-Māʾida.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v2p309/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | holds | Al-Fārisī says ithm can denote appropriated property; ‘sin or offense’ distinguishes a wrong against God from a wrong against people, and the verse arose over theft. | mentions 4:112, 5:107

[FARISI-HUJJA:v2p312/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | holds | Labīd’s h-j-d forms contrast sleeping with staying awake; the Qurʾān uses tahajjud for night prayer, illustrating the specialized sense. | mentions 17:79
[FARISI-HUJJA:v2p312/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | prefers | Al-Fārisī favors kabīr: great sin contrasts with small sin, matching kabāʾir and akbar; great sin is also ʿaẓīm, while kathīr would contrast with qalīl. | mentions 54:53, 53:32, 4:31, 31:13, 4:48
[FARISI-HUJJA:v2p312/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | holds | The thāʾ reading is supported by the warning that wine and gambling produce enmity and obstruct remembrance and prayer. | mentions 5:91

[FARISI-HUJJA:v2p314/r4] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | holds | Since sin is balanced against benefits, kathīr naturally means many harms, like the Qurʾānic description of numerous livestock benefits. | mentions 23:21
[FARISI-HUJJA:v2p314/r5] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | holds | Wine is not linguistically identical to nabīdh; primary names are not assigned by analogy, as Abū al-Aswad’s poem calls wine its sister.
