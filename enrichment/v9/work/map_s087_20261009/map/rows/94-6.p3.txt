[BURHAN:v4p93#2/r1] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | holds | When the same definite noun is repeated, the second usually refers to the first; examples include hardship in 94:5–6, the path in the Fatiha, and celestial objects in 41:37.
[BURHAN:v4p93#2/r2] BURHAN, d. 794 · التنوخي | reports | Al-Tanukhi explains that the hardship is one because the definite article denotes the unique generic kind, which is neither individually one nor numerically many.
[BURHAN:v4p97/r2] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | reports | A common account says the repeated indefinite “ease” is distinct from the first, whereas the definite “hardship” refers back; some explain the indefinite as generic and the definite as already known.
[BURHAN:v4p97/r3] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | prefers | The author concludes that the second sentence emphasizes the first rather than promising two separate eases: explicit repetition implants the point, just as repeating “Zayd has a book” does not imply two books.
[BURHAN:v4p97/r6] BURHAN, d. 794 · author | reports | The same repetition question appears in legal theory: repeating “Pray two units” may be a second command for a second act or merely emphasis; jurists hold both views.

[BIQAI:94:6/r17] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | rejects | Biqāʿī objects that definiteness need not be generic: anaphoric reference is the default, and prior mention does not force a following indefinite to identify the same thing.
