YAN-FULL, d. 745 · مكي | reports | Makkī treats الآيات as indefinite, making the two prepositional phrases descriptions of signs existing in both abodes; Abū Ḥayyān calls this a semantic paraphrase.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p410/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | Abū Ḥayyān rejects saying the phrase syntactically attaches to الآيات: a noun cannot govern the prepositional phrase; it must modify the noun through an implicit participle or state.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p415/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | As wine among drinks and gambling’s food are prohibited, polytheist spouses are prohibited among those one may marry.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p417/r11] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | As with wine and gambling, some benefit from a forbidden thing does not establish permissibility.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p431/r11] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The believers asked the Messenger about wine and gambling as God had foretold; the answer that both involve grave sin was meant to lead them to abstain and indicates their prohibition.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p431/r12] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | The sin is described as great in one reading and numerous in another; Abū Ḥayyān relates the wording to the Qurʾānic category of major sins. | mentions 4:31
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p431/r13] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Benefits include money from trading in wine, gambling, and other gains; even prohibited things have some benefit, especially when people naturally desire them or grow accustomed to them.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p431/r14] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The sin's harm, which leads to Hell, outweighs the benefit that ends with its occasion; a rational person should avoid lasting punishment for temporary gain.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p432/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Asked what to spend, believers are told to give whatever is easy for them to part with; this reflects that religion imposes no undue hardship.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p432/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | God explains the signs as He has clarified wine, gambling, and spending, so that reflection on this world and the next may lead people to prefer the afterlife.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p434/r10] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Abū Ḥayyān praises the passage's eloquence, its joined clauses and elegant structure, and its movement from warning about disobedience at the opening to glad tidings for believers at the end.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219#2/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The draw used ten lots, or eleven in one account: seven bore one through seven marked shares (الفذ، التوءم، الرقيب، الحلس، النافس، المسبل، المعلى); three blanks were المنيح، السفيح، الوغد, with an optional fourth blank called المضعف.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219#2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | They added blank lots to prevent the impartial dealer (الحرضة) from favoring anyone; he knelt, wrapped himself in a cloth, exposed his head, shook the lots in a bag, and drew one for each named person.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | A marked lot won its assigned share; a blank meant the person paid toward the camel. This was done in winter scarcity, with the meat given to the poor, not eaten by participants; they took pride in it and called nonparticipants البرم.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219#2/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Most divided the camel into ten portions, one per lot.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219#2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الأصمعي | reports | Al-Aṣmaʿī says the camel was divided by the count of marked lines, producing twenty-eight portions.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219#2/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls al-Aṣmaʿī's division mistaken.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219#2/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The author calls that correction surprising: Arabs may have divided the camel by ten lots on one occasion and by twenty-eight marked shares on another.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | A noun is omitted after عن: the question concerns the ruling on wine and gambling, including permissibility or prohibition, not the substances themselves; the answer matches that understood question.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219#3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | فيهما is the fronted predicate and إثم the delayed subject. Fronting is optional because indefiniteness is also licensed by description or coordination; a noun meaning their use is omitted because sin is not inherent in the substances.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219#3/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī read كثير; the others read كبير. The majority form suits sin described as grave, drinking and gambling as major sins, and the seven readers' ب in وإثمهما أكبر. | mentions 4:2, 42:37
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219#3/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The much-sin reading may describe the many individual sins of drinkers and gamblers, or the repeated and multiplied punishment resulting from their acts.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219#3/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | It can also encompass the bad words and deeds produced by drinking, and all involved with wine from grape to consumption; the Prophet cursed wine and ten parties connected with it.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219#3/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The plural benefits contrasts with singular sin, so describing the sin as many matches the collective sense. The author says each reading should be supported without weakening the other, as in the earlier ملك and مالك discussion.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219#3/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abū al-Baqāʾ calls the ب reading better: grave sins are called كبائر and lesser ones صغائر. He still regards the ث reading as sound, since abundance is a kind of greatness just as smallness can mean lowliness or scarcity.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Khamr properly denotes grape juice after fermentation and frothing; by metaphor it also covers any other liquid that ferments and froths.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عطية | reports | Another derivation links maysir to yasar, slaughtering or cutting: a yāsir is the butcher who divides a camel. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the camel staked for lots was called maysir as the source of gain, then the arrows took that name by proximity.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The famous derivation connects khamr with covering: it veils the mind like a woman's veil. خامري حضاجر is an admonition to a fool to hide; the segment also cites the hadith about covering vessels and poetry using خمر الطريق for concealing vegetation.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A second derivation says wine is covered until it ferments and strengthens, illustrated by the hadith about covering vessels.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن الأنباري | reports | Ibn al-Anbārī derives khamr from its mingling with the mind, as in the phrase the disease mixed with him.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A fourth derivation connects khamr with leaving something until it matures, as dough is left to rise or an opinion until its sound course becomes clear. On all four accounts it is originally a verbal noun used for an active or passive participle.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Maysir means gambling and is formed on mifʿal from yusr/yasara-yaysiru. Alqama's line uses ييسرون and another poet's uses ييسرونني as usage evidence.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The participant in drawing lots is called yusr and pluralized as أيسار; another account treats يسر as a plural of ياسر, like حرس from حارس.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | One derivation takes maysir from ease, because winnings are easy to obtain; another derives it from wealth, since gambling strips a person of wealth.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:219/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · مجاهد | reports | Al-Ṭabarī attributes to Mujāhid a derivation from يسر لي كذا, meaning something became due to me; Ibn ʿAṭiyya rejects it.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p578/r1] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · عمر | reports | In a report from ʿUmar, he repeatedly asks God for a clear ruling on wine: first this verse is revealed, then 4:43 bars approaching prayer while drunk, and finally 5:90–91 commands abstention, to which ʿUmar replies that they desist. | mentions 4:43, 5:90, 5:91
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p578/r2] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | The report is transmitted by Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi, and al-Nasa’i through Israel from Abu Ishaq, and by Ibn Abi Hatim and Ibn Mardawayh through al-Thawri; Abu Zurʿa says Abu Maysara did not hear from ʿUmar, while Ibn al-Madini calls the chain sound and al-Tirmidhi authenticates it.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p578/r3] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · ابن أبي حاتم | reports | Ibn Abi Hatim adds ʿUmar’s explanation that wine makes wealth and reason disappear.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p578/r4] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · عمر بن الخطاب | reports | ʿUmar defines wine as anything that clouds the intellect; Ibn Kathir says the definition will be explained further in the commentary on al-Ma’ida. | mentions 5:90
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p578/r5] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | Maysir means gambling.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p579#2/r1] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · author | holds | The word al-afw is read in both the accusative and nominative; Ibn Kathir regards both readings as sound, apt, and close in meaning.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p579#2/r10] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet says the best charity is given from a position of means, the upper hand is better than the lower, and one should begin with dependents.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p579#2/r2] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · معاذ بن جبل وثعلبة | reports | A report reaching Yahya says Muadh and Thaʿlaba asked how much to spend given their dependents and servants, and this question occasioned the verse.
