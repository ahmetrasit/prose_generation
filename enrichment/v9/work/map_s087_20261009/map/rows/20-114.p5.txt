[ALUSI-FULL:v16p268/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الماوردي | reports | Al-Māwardī says Meccans and the Najrān bishop gave the Prophet three days to answer questions; when revelation was delayed and Jews spread rumors, he became distressed and hurried it. Al-Ālūsī finds this and the preceding proposal unconvincing.
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p268/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · عبد الله والجحدري والحسن وأبو حيوة وسلام ويعقوب والزعفراني وابن مقسم | reports | These readers say نقضى with the first-person plural, fatḥa on its final yāʾ, and وحيه in the accusative.
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p268/r8] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الأعمش؛ صاحب اللوامح | reports | Al-Aʿmash reads the same construction but with a quiescent final yāʾ; al-Lawāmiʿ attributes this to a dialect that does not open final yāʾ after a kasra.
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p268/r9] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The command to seek more knowledge demonstrates its virtue; some say the Prophet was commanded to seek increase in no other thing.
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p269/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masʿūd used to pray for increased faith, understanding, certainty, and knowledge; Saʿīd ibn Manṣūr and ʿAbd ibn Ḥumayd transmit it.
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p269/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Al-Ālūsī says this further reflects knowledge’s great merit and prays for increased knowledge and the ability to act upon it.
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p289/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · some interpreters | reports | Some take the prayer for increased knowledge as an allusion to knowledge bestowed directly by God.
[ELMALILI:20:105-115/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | He renders God as the true, exalted sovereign and preserves the prayer asking God to increase the Prophet’s knowledge.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p315/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The praise is an interjection between the statement about Quranic revelation and the instruction not to hasten; it teaches thanks for the Quran’s means of reform, exhortation, and eloquent guidance.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p315/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The revelation and its reforming means flow from God’s exaltation and greatness as the true King, who governs His subjects with complete perfection and effective rule.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p315/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Calling God the true King implies that other figures called kings possess imperfect sovereignty. | mentions 25:26
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p315/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · الله | reports | A hadith depicts God saying, I am the King; where are the kings of the earth? It challenges anyone to find a rival to His sovereignty.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p315/r7] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · معاوية | reports | Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the hadith’s challenge to Muʿāwiya’s address to the people of Medina, asking where their scholars were.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p315/r8] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Pairing the name Allah with the title King shows that God deserves reverence in Himself: the comprehensive divine name signifies all perfections and exclusive, complete divinity.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p316/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | حق means sovereignty without any trace of incapacity or submission to another; the description implies that other kingship is false.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p316/r10] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Under either interpretation, completing the revelation means finishing the portion then in the process of descending.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p316/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | As the true King, God’s actions plainly follow wisdom; the Quran is the law of His kingdom and the perfect policy securing the welfare of its followers in both worlds.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p316/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The instruction not to hasten follows the preceding praise of the Quran and its extensive means for reforming people.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p316/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The Prophet’s concern for his community’s reform and salvation may have led him, after hearing these verses, to desire more Quran and its speedy delivery; God told him to leave the timing to divine knowledge of their needs.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p316/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The phrase means before the revelation decreed for you has been completed, that is, before the portion slated for sending has finished being revealed.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p316/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The prohibition targets asking or inwardly yearning intensely for faster revelation, as if it were delayed; it does not forbid simply wishing for more Quran.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p316/r7] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet said he wished Moses had been patient with al-Khiḍr so that God could tell them more of their story, an example of wanting further revelation without demanding haste.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p316/r8] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · ابن عباس | reports | Another interpretation takes hastening with the Quran as reciting while Gabriel delivers it: Ibn ʿAbbās says the Prophet read ahead before Gabriel finished from eagerness to memorize and fear of forgetting.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v16p316/r9] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās gives the same explanation for the command not to move the tongue hastily in the Quran, as cited in Sahih al-Bukhari. | mentions 75:16
