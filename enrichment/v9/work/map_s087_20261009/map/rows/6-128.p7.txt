[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#2/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | يا معشر is accusative under the omitted يقول or قلنا; with al-Zajjaj's passive صيغة, it is nominative as نائب الفاعل.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#2/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | prefers | The Shaykh instead parses الذي as an appositive, implicitly الوقت الذي; this makes it generic without a mismatch that would prevent an adjectival analysis.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#2/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الفارسي | prefers | Al-Farisi makes خالدين a circumstantial accusative governed by مثواكم, which here is a verbal noun meaning your remaining, not a place noun; he rejects al-Zajjaj's locative gloss.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#2/r12] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Al-Farisi's poetic evidence uses مغار ابن همام as a verbal event; its على complement fits an action rather than a place, supporting his verbal-noun reading of مثوى.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | معشر means a group; an Arabic line uses it for the people from whom the speaker defends others.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · النبي | reports | The Prophet's saying نحن معاشر الأنبياء uses معاشر for a class or group, and states that prophets do not leave inheritance.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#2/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الأودي | reports | Al-Awdi's verse uses معاشر as the plural of معشر, for a group who restore what their people have spoiled.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | من الإنس may be a circumstantial phrase: their allies were human.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#2/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Alternatively من identifies the kind, since their allies included humans and jinn, with the phrase specifying the human allies.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#2/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | ربنا has an omitted vocative particle.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#2/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The majority read أجلنا in the singular, matching الذي; an alternative has آجالنا plural while الذي remains singular and masculine.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#2/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو علي | reports | Abu ʿAli treats الذي as generic, with the masculine singular standing for a feminine relative form.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A second possible governor is an omitted verb, يثوون فيها خالدين, with مثواكم understood as the place of remaining.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | rejects | Abu al-Baqa proposes the meaning of the possessive construction as the governor; the author rejects this because the meaning of an iḍāfa cannot govern.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#3/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الجمهور | reports | The majority take the exception from the immediately preceding clause, النار مثواكم خالدين فيها.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#3/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو مسلم | reports | Abu Muslim instead makes the exception from their statement that they reached the appointed term: it excludes those whom God seized before that term for disbelief and error.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#3/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The author rejects Abu Muslim's antecedent: the wording should then be شئت to match أجلت, and the intervening judgment about the fire improperly separates exception from its antecedent.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#3/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The author also rejects Abu Muslim's interpretation because it assumes two terms, an early term of seizure and a separately appointed term, which he says is disproved elsewhere.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#3/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | Makkī and Abu al-Baqa in one view call this a disconnected exception: the fire is their abode, except those who believed in worldly life, by analogy with 44:56; the author finds this remote. | mentions 44:56
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#3/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Others regard the exception as connected to the addressed pronoun in مثواكم: those among the group who believed in worldly life are excluded; ما can refer to a rational kind or class. | mentions 4:3
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#4/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | For this connected reading, ما can apply to a rational class, as in the Qur'anic phrase فانكحوا ما طاب لكم من النساء. | mentions 4:3
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#4/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Al-Hasan al-Basri says the exception is the time they spent in the world before punishment; the author considers this even farther from the preceding readings.

[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#4/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj takes the exception as the combined duration in the graves and the period after resurrection before entering Hell.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#4/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari says the exception marks intervals when the damned are moved from Hell to a valley of extreme cold that tears their limbs; they howl and ask to return.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#4/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Some identify the exception as monotheist sinners who enter Hell but do not remain there forever; ما applies to a category of rational people, and the author says the exception is real and apparent.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:128#4/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The author says this is a genuine exception: the apparent meaning is clear and should not be replaced with a figurative threat that presents an unreal event as real.
