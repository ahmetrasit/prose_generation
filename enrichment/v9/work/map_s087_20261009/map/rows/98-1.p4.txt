mentions 98:4
[RAZI-FULL:v32p37#2/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · صاحب الكشاف | prefers | Al-Zamakhsharī's explanation, preferred by Razi, resolves the apparent contradiction: verse 98:1 reports disbelievers saying they would leave their religion only when the promised Prophet came, while 98:4 reports that his arrival actually divided them from truth.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p37#2/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · القاضي | reports | Al-Qāḍī removes the conflict by reading the sense as they would not abandon disbelief even when the proof came; Razi rejects this because حتى does not mean that in Arabic.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p37#2/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · ابن عرفة | reports | Ibn ʿArafa reads انفكاك as ceasing to praise Muhammad: before his coming they mentioned his virtues, but after he came they split into hostile claims; the imperfect can carry past meaning, as in 2:102 and 2:89. | mentions 2:102, 2:89
[RAZI-FULL:v32p37#2/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | Another explanation says the whole group remained in disbelief until the Messenger came; afterward the group changed because some believed and others remained disbelievers, satisfying حتى.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p37#2/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | A further view says they were previously certain of their inherited religions, but Muhammad's coming unsettled their convictions and left them doubtful about their own and other religions; Razi compares this to humanity before prophetic missions. | mentions 2:213
[RAZI-FULL:v32p38/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The disbelievers comprise two groups: scriptural communities, including Jews and Christians who altered their religion and scripture, and polytheists with no scripture.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p38/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | To explain من, one view takes it as explanatory, like the phrase about avoiding the abomination of idols in 22:30. | mentions 22:30
[RAZI-FULL:v32p38/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | Another view says those rejecting Muhammad include some People of the Book: Christians' trinitarianism and Jews' anthropomorphism are forms of shirk; several coordinated descriptions can name one group, as in 9:112. | mentions 9:112
[RAZI-FULL:v32p38/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | Some scholars include Zoroastrians among scriptural communities, citing a prophetic instruction to treat them by the People of the Book's rule; others exclude them because the verse concerns Arabian Jews and Christians, as in 6:156. | mentions 6:156
[RAZI-FULL:v32p39/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Though واو gives no sequence, People of the Book come first because the surah is Medinan, they knew scripture and could best recognize Muhammad, their persistence was more blameworthy, and others followed their example.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p39/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Calling them People of the Book rather than Jews and Christians highlights their learning, which can honor them or intensify their culpability and punishment for disbelief.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p39/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | Some jurists infer that all disbelief is one religion, so a polytheist may inherit from a Jew and vice versa, because the verse places both under disbelief.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p39/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | Others infer from the conjunction that the categories differ: a protected scripturalist is not a polytheist, consistent with the reported distinction concerning marriage and slaughtered food.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p39/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Naming People of the Book warns against trusting learned people, since Quran scholars can repeat the failures of earlier communities.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p39/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · القفال | reports | Al-Qaffāl derives انفكاك from opening or removing a bond: release follows close attachment; thus disbelievers clung strongly to their religion until the evident proof came, and bayyina is proof separating truth from falsehood.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p40#2/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · قتادة وابن زيد | reports | Qatāda and Ibn Zayd take the proof as the Quran, citing 20:133; رسول من الله then has an omitted noun, meaning that proof is revelation and the Messenger recites purified sheets. | mentions 20:133
[RAZI-FULL:v32p40/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | Those identifying the proof as Muhammad say his extraordinary seriousness in proclaiming prophethood rules out fabrication and mental deficiency, while the totality of his qualities reaches miraculous perfection, as al-Jāḥiẓ and al-Ghazālī argued.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p40/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | A further argument is that Muhammad's numerous, manifest miracles make him himself a proof; God accordingly calls him a shining lamp in 33:46. | mentions 33:46
[RAZI-FULL:v32p40/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | The next phrase رسول من الله is grammatically taken as an appositive to البينة; ʿAbd Allāh reads رسولا as a circumstantial accusative.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p40/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | The definite article in البينة identifies the Prophet foretold in the Torah and Gospel, or magnifies him as the ultimate proof; the indefinite رسول then adds emphasis by naming him as a messenger of exceptional stature.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p40/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · أبو مسلم | reports | Abū Muslim says the proof means messengers from God who recite purified sheets, as in the request for a heavenly book and for unfolded pages. | mentions 4:153, 74:52
[QURTUBI:98:1/r1] QURTUBI, d. 671 · يحيي بن سلام وابن عباس والجمهور | reports | Yahya ibn Sallam calls the sura Meccan, while Ibn Abbas and most scholars call it Medinan; it has nine verses.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r10] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masud's wording, the polytheists and People of the Book did not cease, is an explanatory reading rather than a recitation; Ibn al-Arabi allows it for exposition only, since recitation is what the codex contains.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r11] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Ahl al-Kitab means Jews and Christians; al-mushrikin is genitive, coordinated with Ahl al-Kitab.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r12] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas identifies the People of the Book as the Jews of Yathrib—Qurayza, Nadir, and Banu Qaynuqa—and the polytheists as Quraysh in Mecca and its vicinity.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r13] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Munfakkin means ceasing from disbelief or turning away from it.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r14] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Qurtubi explains the wording until the clear proof comes as until the clear proof came, referring to Muhammad.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r15] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | reports | One explanation takes infikak as reaching an endpoint: they would not reach the end of their lives and die until the clear proof came.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r16] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | reports | Another gloss is vanishing: their allotted time would not expire until a messenger came.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r17] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | The root f-k-k carries the sense of opening or separating; Arab idiom ما انفككت أفعل and ما انفك فلان قائما means continuing or remaining.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r18] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | reports | Another gloss is departing: they would not leave and pass from the world until the clear proof came.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r19] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن كيسان | reports | Ibn Kaysan says the People of the Book retained descriptions of Muhammad in their scriptures until his mission, then envied and denied him, as in 2:89 and 98:4. | mentions 2:89, 98:4
[QURTUBI:98:1/r2] QURTUBI, d. 671 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report promising immense rewards for learning this sura says people would abandon family and wealth to learn it, that angels recite it continually, and that reciters receive angelic protection and prayers; Qurtubi says the report is unsound, and Ibn al-Arabi calls its version false.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r20] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن كيسان | reports | On Ibn Kaysan's reading, the Meccan polytheists called Muhammad al-Amin and did not speak ill of him until the clear proof came and they opposed him.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r21] QURTUBI, d. 671 · بعض اللغويين | reports | Some linguists derive munfakkin from a woman's pelvic bone separating during childbirth and failing to reunite; the verse then means they are not punished or destroyed until messengers and books establish proof.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r22] QURTUBI, d. 671 · قوم | reports | One view includes the polytheists among the People of the Book, citing Jews who called Ezra God's son and Christians who called Jesus God, His son, or one of three.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r23] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | reports | Another view distinguishes them by history: the People of the Book believed before disbelieving after their prophets, while polytheists began in natural disposition and disbelieved upon maturity.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r24] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | reports | Another view takes polytheists as a description of the People of the Book too: they abandoned monotheism and their scriptures, since Christians are trinitarian and most Jews anthropomorphists; all are shirk.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r25] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | reports | Another interpretation restricts disbelief to rejection of Muhammad: the People of the Book are Jews and Christians, while the polytheists are idol worshippers without scripture.
[QURTUBI:98:1/r26] QURTUBI, d. 671 · القشيري | reports | Al-Qushayri finds that interpretation strained because the following phrase identifies the clear proof as Muhammad; it can work only if it means they persisted in present disbelief until Muhammad came, explained the signs, and some then believed.
