[IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB:v1p24/r2] IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB, d. 276 · author | holds | Arabs call blood nafs because it is connected to breath, following their practice of naming things for what is associated, adjacent, or causative.
[IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB:v1p24/r3] IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB, d. 276 · أصحاب اللغة | reports | The Arabs use nafisat al-marʾa for menstruation, as if she bled; lexicographers explain nafisāʾ as named for the flow of blood.
[IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB:v1p24/r4] IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB, d. 276 · إبراهيم | reports | Ibrahim says anything without flowing blood does not render water impure if it falls into it; here nafs means flowing blood.
[IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB:v1p24/r5] IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB, d. 276 · author | holds | The Arabs also call the soul nasama, whose root is nafas.
[IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB:v1p24/r6] IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB, d. 276 · بعض الحديث | reports | An unnamed hadith says to avoid dust because nasama comes from it; Ibn Qutayba explains nasama here as breath.
[IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB:v1p24/r7] IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB, d. 276 · author | holds | Rabw is called nafs because it arises from breathing.
[IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB:v1p24/r8] IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB, d. 276 · author | holds | The idioms ḥatfa nafsihi and ḥatfa anfihi mean dying in bed: breathing continues until death, when the soul leaves through the nose and mouth.
[TUSTARI:39:42/r1] TUSTARI, d. 283 · author | holds | Tawaffī in the Qur'an has three senses: death, sleep, and being raised; at death God separates the luminous spirit's subtle self from the dense natural self that enables cognition and visions of the unseen.
[TUSTARI:39:42/r2] TUSTARI, d. 283 · author | holds | In sleep, God withdraws the subtle self of dense nature but not the luminous spirit; the sleeper can awaken because that spirit remains, while its departure leaves a being without movement, dead.
[TUSTARI:39:42/r3] TUSTARI, d. 283 · author | holds | The subtle natural self lives by the light of the spirit, while the spirit's own life is remembrance; the dead are called alive with their Lord because they are given remembrance through the luminous spirit. | mentions 3:169
[TUSTARI:39:42/r4] TUSTARI, d. 283 · author | holds | Dense nature lives through food, drink, and pleasure; a true knower reconciles nature and spirit so that both live by remembrance and striving in it.
[TUSTARI:39:42/r5] TUSTARI, d. 283 · المبرد النحوي | reports | Al-Mubarrad the grammarian says spirit and soul are two connected things, neither of which can subsist without the other.
