[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:73:20/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Şevkânî | reports | Some hold that night prayer was initially obligatory for both the Prophet and the community, then became voluntary for the community while remaining obligatory for the Prophet.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:73:20/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · müfessirler | reports | One interpretation takes “recite what is easy” to mean praying as much tahajjud as is easy during the night.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:73:20/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · müfessirler | reports | A second interpretation takes it to mean reciting as much of the Qur'an, and whichever verses, as are easy during night prayer.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:73:20/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Ebû Bekir İbnü’l-Arabi | reports | Ibn al-Arabi reports both that “recitation” stands for prayer and that the passage means recitation itself.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:73:20/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Illness, travel for livelihood or other purposes, and jihad for defense, freedom, or independence bring divine ease; night prayer becomes recommended rather than obligatory.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:73:20/r7] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The closing section commands proper prayer, zakat, good works, charity, and compassionate help for the poor, promising multiplied reward with God in the afterlife.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:73:20/r8] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · müfessirler | reports | Commentators usually identify the prayer here as the five daily prayers and zakat as obligatory alms; the authors reject that dating because both formal duties became obligatory years later.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:73:20/r9] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | prefers | Instead, the verse aims to cultivate awareness of the duties of prayer and zakat and begin their earliest practices.
[QIRAAT-ER:73:20:11#1/r1] QIRAAT-ER · CE | reports | The CE genitive niṣfihi is coordinated with thuluthayī, so both are governed by min: 'less than two-thirds and its half and its third.'
[QIRAAT-ER:73:20:13#1/r1] QIRAAT-ER · CE | reports | The CE genitive thuluthihi parallels the niṣfihi variant; both are governed by min through thuluthayī.
[QIRAAT-ER:73:20:37#1/r1] QIRAAT-ER · hmz | reports | The hmz canonical variant drops hamza in Qurāni for Qurʾāni, removing the glottal stop.
[QIRAAT-ER:73:20:8#1/r1] QIRAAT-ER · skn | reports | The skn reading places sukūn on lām for a lighter pronunciation; the meaning of the fraction is unchanged.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p562/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | Although يقرض is quadriliteral and its verbal noun is إقراض, the Quran uses the triliteral noun قرض, which can also mean money.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p562/r2] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | If قرض means the act of lending, it is a cognate accusative; if it means money, it is a direct object.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p562/r3] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | قرضا حسنا combines lending sincerely for God's reward with giving good, lawful money.

[STUDYQURAN:73:20/r14] STUDYQURAN · some | reports | Some interpret zakāh here as the obligatory alms.
[STUDYQURAN:73:20/r15] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Because zakāh’s specific form was not yet institutionalized, the verse may refer to charity generally. | mentions 23:4
[STUDYQURAN:73:20/r16] STUDYQURAN · some | reports | Some say payment of alms was required in the Makkan period, while its detailed rules were established only in the Madinan period.
