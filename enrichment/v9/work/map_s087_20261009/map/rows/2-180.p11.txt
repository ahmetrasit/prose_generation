[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p162/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | Ibn ʿAṭiyya's parse cannot take the earlier spoken kataba as an omitted answer indicated by what precedes; that would make the estimated answer identical to the already uttered word. [ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p162/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari calls al-waṣiyya the faʿil of kataba, his term for the passive subject, and explains the masculine verb by separation and by the sense of the verbal phrase to make a will.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p162/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · قول غير مسمى | reports | Some grammarians make al-waṣiyya a subject in a conditional answer, with an omitted fa or an implied predicate such as it is due; the passive subject would be an implicit yuktabu explained by the wording that follows.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p162/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | Abū Ḥayyān again finds Ibn ʿAṭiyya's analysis inconsistent: if idha is governed as a temporal adverb it is not a condition, and an omitted passive subject or unspoken verbal noun cannot serve as the proposed governor under the stated Basran rules.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p163#2/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | prefers | The prepositional phrase serving as passive subject is the best analysis and requires fewer assumptions than positing an implicit verbal noun and its pronoun.
